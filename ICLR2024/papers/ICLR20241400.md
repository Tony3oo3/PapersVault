## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models can silently reproduce training images verbatim when given certain prompts, raising legal and privacy concerns. Existing detection methods (e.g., generation-density approaches) require 16–32+ generations per prompt and over 40 seconds of computation, making them impractical for real-time service deployment. No prior work provides a single-generation, first-step detection signal, an explainable per-token attribution of memorization triggers, or a unified mitigation strategy spanning both inference and training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (encoded via a pre-trained CLIP text encoder into a prompt embedding e_p) and the diffusion model's noise predictions at each denoising step in latent space—specifically the difference ε_θ(x_t, e_p) − ε_θ(x_t, e_∅) between conditional and unconditional predictions. It produces three outputs: (1) a scalar detection metric (mean L2 norm of text-conditional predictions over T steps) compared against a tunable threshold γ, (2) per-token significance scores computed as the mean L2 norm of the gradient of the detection loss with respect to each token embedding, and (3) a perturbed prompt embedding e* obtained by minimizing the text-conditional prediction magnitude at t=0. Crucially, all computations occur in latent space without decoding to pixel space, and detection is valid from the very first denoising step with a single generation.

## Method

The core insight is that memorized prompts cause the text condition to overwhelmingly dominate the denoising trajectory regardless of initialization, manifesting as abnormally large text-conditional noise prediction magnitudes (ε_θ(x_t, e_p) − ε_θ(x_t, e_∅)). For detection, the paper averages the L2 norm of this quantity across all T sampling steps and flags prompts exceeding a threshold γ, achieving AUC 0.960 and TPR@1%FPR 0.760 from the first step alone in 0.2 s with a single generation. For explainability, it computes the gradient of the detection loss with respect to each token embedding, averaged over steps, yielding a significance score that identifies trigger tokens whose modification neutralizes memorization. For inference-time mitigation, it optimizes the prompt embedding at t=0 to minimize the text-conditional prediction magnitude, with early stopping at a target loss l_target to preserve semantic meaning. For training-time mitigation, it excludes samples from the mini-batch whenever their text-conditional prediction magnitude exceeds a threshold τ, adding roughly 10% training overhead from an extra unconditional forward pass.

## Results

On 500 memorized prompts vs. 2,000 non-memorized prompts for Stable Diffusion v1, the proposed method achieves AUC 0.960 and TPR@1%FPR 0.760 using only a single generation at the very first denoising step, completing in 0.199 seconds. With 4 generations, it reaches AUC 0.990 and TPR@1%FPR 0.912 at the first step (0.794 s), and with 32 generations, AUC 0.996 and TPR@1%FPR 0.954 (1.606 s). For mitigation, the inference-time strategy (minimizing text-conditional prediction magnitude) and training-time strategy (filtering high-magnitude samples) both reduce SSCD similarity while maintaining higher CLIP scores than the Random Token Addition baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Carlini et al. (2023) density-based baselines (Density_l2 and Density_SSCD), the proposed method dominates on every metric. At the first step with a single generation, the proposed method achieves AUC 0.960 and TPR@1%FPR 0.760 in 0.199 s, whereas the best density baseline (Density_SSCD, n=32) reaches only AUC 0.940 and TPR@1%FPR 0.530 at the final step after 79.47 s — a 400× speedup with substantially higher detection accuracy. Even matching the baseline's 32 generations, the proposed method at the first step (AUC 0.996, TPR@1%FPR 0.954, 1.606 s) outperforms the baseline at the last step (AUC 0.940, TPR@1%FPR 0.530, 79.47 s). For mitigation, the proposed inference-time and training-time strategies outperform the Random Token Addition (RTA) baseline from Somepalli et al. (2023b) on the CLIP-score-vs-SSCD-similarity trade-off: RTA often fails to suppress memorization and introduces undesirable visual artifacts, while the proposed methods reduce memorization while preserving prompt-image alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classifier-free guidance mechanism—standard in text-to-image diffusion models—as a memorization detection signal, observing that memorized prompts produce disproportionately large text-conditional noise prediction magnitudes. It departs from prior detection approaches that require access to the training dataset (Somepalli et al., 2022) or that generate many images to measure output density (Carlini et al., 2023), instead extracting the signal from a single forward pass within the existing sampling loop. The token-level significance scoring extends the trigger-token identification of Somepalli et al. (2023b) from manual n-gram probing to an automated gradient-based method, and the two mitigation strategies (inference-time embedding perturbation and training-time sample filtering) are novel applications of the same magnitude signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for model operators: detection in under 0.2 seconds with a single generation (vs. 39+ seconds for baselines) enables real-time intervention before serving potentially infringing outputs, and the token-level explainability gives users actionable feedback to rephrase prompts. Limitations include the reliance on a hand-tuned threshold without principled confidence scores, and the training-time mitigation adding ~10% overhead. Open questions include whether the magnitude signal generalizes to non-CLIP text encoders, video diffusion, or models trained on non-duplicated data, and whether the detection threshold can be made adaptive across model scales.
