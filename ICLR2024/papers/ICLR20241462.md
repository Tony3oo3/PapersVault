## Problem

Existing opponent modeling approaches rely on online learning, requiring continuous interaction with the competitive environment to build and update opponent models. This is impractical in real-world settings where opponent access is limited or policies switch between episodes, making thousands of rollouts prohibitively expensive. The paper formalizes Offline Opponent Modeling (OOM), which pre-trains an opponent-aware adaptive policy from pre-collected offline datasets and deploys it to adapt to unknown opponent policies without gradient updates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241462/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During offline training, the method consumes paired trajectories from K opponent policies: the controlled agent's trajectory (observations, actions, rewards-to-go) and the opponent's trajectory, from which C trajectories are sampled and H consecutive fragments are stitched together as in-context data. At deployment, the model is fully frozen; adaptation occurs solely by accumulating the most recent C opponent trajectories in an Opponent Context Window (OCW), from which the same fragment-sampling procedure constructs the in-context prompt. The output is the controlled agent's next action, autoregressively generated by a causal Transformer conditioned on its own trajectory and the opponent's in-context embedding.

## Method

TAO is a three-stage Transformer pipeline. Stage 1 (Policy Embedding Learning) trains a GPT2-based Opponent Policy Encoder with a combined generative loss (conditional imitation: predict opponent actions given observations plus the average embedding of a different trajectory from the same policy) and a discriminative contrastive loss (pull same-policy trajectory embeddings together, push different-policy embeddings apart). Stage 2 (Opponent-aware Response Policy Training) trains a Decision-Transformer-style In-context Control Decoder via supervised pre-training to predict near-optimal actions conditioned on the controlled agent's trajectory (with Reward-To-Go) and the OPE-encoded opponent in-context data, using cross-attention to link opponent policy representations to appropriate responses. Stage 3 (Deployment) freezes all parameters and adapts to unknown opponents purely through in-context learning by updating the OCW with new opponent trajectories. Theoretically, TAO is proven equivalent to Posterior Sampling in Opponent Modeling (PSOM), inheriting PS's convergence guarantees for opponent policy recognition.

## Results

On Markov Soccer (sparse-reward, 2-player zero-sum), TAO achieves an average return of 0.51 (seen), 0.55 (unseen), and 0.43 (mix) under Configuration 1, outperforming the strongest baseline LIAM (0.43/0.47/0.32) by 8.1%, 17.0%, and 34.4% respectively. On Particleworld Adversary (dense-reward, 3-player non-zero-sum), TAO reaches 105.6 (seen), 109.5 (unseen), and 101.6 (mix), surpassing the best baseline DRON-MoE (94.2/20.5/60.9) by 12.1%, 434%, and 66.5%. The advantage is most pronounced on unseen opponent policies, where TAO maintains high returns while most baselines degrade sharply or produce negative returns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAO outperforms all six baselines across both environments and all three test settings (seen, unseen, mix). The gap is most dramatic on unseen opponent policies: on PA, TAO achieves 109.5 while the next-best LIAM reaches only 60.5, and DRON-concat and Prompt-DT produce negative returns (−40.0 and −10.1 respectively), indicating complete failure to adapt. On MS unseen, TAO (0.55) leads LIAM (0.47) by 17% and DRON-MoE (0.38) by 45%. Among baselines, LIAM and MeLIBA (autoencoder/VAE-based) consistently outperform DRON variants (hand-crafted feature encoding), suggesting reconstruction-based opponent modeling is more effective than direct feature encoding. Prompt-DT is competitive on seen settings (0.48 on MS, close to TAO w/o PEL's 0.46) but collapses on unseen (0.36 on MS, −10.1 on PA), confirming that relying solely on the controlled agent's trajectory without explicit opponent information is insufficient for generalization. TAO w/o PEL remains competitive with baselines, validating that the in-context learning-based supervised pre-training is the core mechanism, while PEL provides additional gains of 5–36% depending on setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAO builds directly on the Decision Transformer architecture (Chen et al., 2021) for its In-context Control Decoder and on the recent finding that supervised pre-training of Transformers yields robust in-context RL capabilities (Lee et al., 2023), repurposing that mechanism for the multi-agent competitive setting. It departs from the dominant online opponent modeling paradigm (DRON, LIAM, MeLIBA) by formalizing an offline variant where no environment interaction is available during training, and from offline meta-RL (e.g., Prompt-DT) by explicitly encoding opponent trajectory information rather than relying solely on the controlled agent's own history. The three-stage pipeline—generative/discriminative policy embedding, in-context supervised pre-training, and frozen-parameter in-context adaptation at deployment—is a novel composition of these ingredients tailored to the OOM problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing OOM as a distinct problem and providing a theoretical equivalence to Bayesian posterior sampling, the paper gives practitioners a principled, gradient-free deployment mechanism for adapting to non-stationary opponents using only pre-collected data—a practical advantage in domains like e-commerce or sports analytics where online interaction is costly or unavailable. However, experiments are confined to two small-scale benchmarks (Markov Soccer, Particleworld Adversary) with at most three agents, leaving open whether the in-context adaptation mechanism scales to high-dimensional continuous games, larger agent populations, or partially observable settings with richer state spaces. The reliance on a fixed set of pre-trained opponent policies during offline training also limits applicability to truly open-ended opponent distributions.
