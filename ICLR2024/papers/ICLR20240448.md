## Problem

Existing interactive 3D segmentation methods (e.g., InterObject3D) formulate the task as binary classification and segment objects sequentially, one at a time. This sequential approach ignores synergies between objects: a positive click for one object is by definition a negative click for nearby objects, and adjacent objects directly compete for their shared boundary. The paper introduces the interactive multi-object 3D segmentation task and an attention-based architecture that segments multiple objects simultaneously with fewer clicks and faster inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N points with xyz coordinates, optionally rgb) and an iteratively growing sequence of user clicks, where each click carries 3D coordinates and a region index (0 for background, 1–M for target objects). The output is a single holistic segmentation mask assigning every point to exactly one of M+1 regions via a softmax, guaranteeing globally non-overlapping masks without post-processing. Crucially, the backbone features are pre-computed once per scene (~0.05 s), and each iterative update only runs a lightweight decoder (~0.02 s), making the system 2× faster than baselines that re-run the full network per click.

## Method

AGILE3D disentangles scene encoding from click processing: a sparse convolutional U-Net (Minkowski Res16UNet34C) extracts per-point features from the point cloud alone, while user clicks are encoded as spatial-temporal queries combining backbone content features, Fourier spatial encodings, and sin/cos temporal encodings. A click attention module with three attention types—click-to-scene (C2S) cross-attention, click-to-click (C2C) self-attention, and scene-to-click (S2C) cross-attention—enables explicit information exchange between clicks and the 3D scene across L decoder layers. A query fusion module aggregates per-click mask logits via a per-point max over clicks sharing the same region index, then applies a softmax over M+1 classes so that regions directly compete for space. Training uses a novel iterative multi-object strategy that simulates realistic user behavior by sampling clicks from the top error regions across all target objects simultaneously, with backpropagation restricted to the final iteration to keep computational cost manageable.

## Results

On the single-object task, AGILE3D achieves IoU@10 of 82.2 on ScanNet40 (vs. 77.6 for InterObject3D), 89.2 on S3DIS-A5 (vs. 79.9), and 40.6 on KITTI-360 (vs. 26.3), requiring fewer clicks to reach target IoU (e.g., NoC@85 of 6.7 on S3DIS vs. 11.2). On the novel multi-object task, AGILE3D reaches IoU@10 of 85.0 on ScanNet40 and 88.3 on S3DIS-A5, needing only 6.3 and 3.4 clicks respectively to reach 80% average IoU, compared to 10.2 and 6.8 for the adapted InterObject3D baseline. In the low-click regime (≤3 clicks), a single click already yields ~60 IoU on ScanNet and S3DIS, surpassing the 3-click performance of InterObject3D. Inference is 2× faster than baselines (0.5s vs. 1.2s at 5 clicks per object) due to the lightweight decoder design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InterObject3D (Kontogianni et al., 2023), the primary interactive 3D segmentation baseline, AGILE3D wins on all metrics across all four train→eval settings, with the largest gains on domain-shifted data: IoU@5 improves from 14.3 to 44.4 on KITTI-360 (3×) and from 72.4 to 83.5 on S3DIS. Against the enhanced InterObject3D++ (which incorporates AGILE3D's iterative training but retains the single-object architecture), AGILE3D still leads by 1.5–4.0 IoU points, confirming that the multi-object attention mechanism itself provides additional benefit beyond training strategy. Against the fully-supervised Mask3D (Schult et al., 2023), AGILE3D with just 3 clicks achieves AP of 70.3 on seen classes and 45.5 on unseen classes, surpassing Mask3D's zero-click AP of 51.5 and 5.3 respectively, demonstrating that minimal human feedback can outperform full supervision especially for novel categories. For iterative training strategies, AGILE3D's multi-object iterative scheme outperforms both ITIS (Mahadevan et al., 2018; IoU@10=79.9) and RITM (Sofiiuk et al., 2022; IoU@10=81.4) in the single-object setting (IoU@10=82.9), despite those methods being specifically designed for that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGILE3D builds directly on InterObject3D (Kontogianni et al., 2023), the state-of-the-art for interactive 3D point cloud segmentation, reusing its Minkowski sparse U-Net backbone but fundamentally rethinking how user clicks are processed. Rather than encoding clicks as binary masks concatenated with point coordinates (as in InterObject3D), AGILE3D introduces a transformer-style click-as-query module with spatial-temporal positional encodings and a multi-head click attention module (C2S, C2C, S2C) that enables explicit interaction between clicks and between clicks and scene features. The iterative training strategy extends ITIS (Mahadevan et al., 2018) and RITM (Sofiiuk et al., 2022) from single-object 2D image segmentation to the multi-object 3D setting, sampling clicks from top error regions across all target objects simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formulating interactive multi-object 3D segmentation as a holistic task where objects compete for space, AGILE3D addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D annotation workflows: sequential single-object processing wastes clicks and ignores boundary synergies between adjacent objects. The 2× inference speedup from decoupling the backbone (pre-computed once) from a lightweight per-iteration decoder makes real-time interactive annotation feasible, and the strong cross-domain generalization (indoor ScanNet → outdoor KITTI-360 LiDAR) suggests the approach could accelerate annotation of new sensor modalities. Open questions include semantic label prediction alongside masks, part-level segmentation (where user intent is ambiguous), and whether the attention-based query mechanism scales to scenes with dozens of target objects.
