## Problem

Existing GNN defense methods face two simultaneous limitations: they suffer an emergent, severe drop in robustness once attack intensity crosses a threshold (e.g., error rates surge >50% beyond 300 injected nodes), and many rely on dense adjacency-matrix operations (SVD, eigen-decomposition) that cause out-of-memory failures on medium-to-large graphs. No prior method addresses both the anti-degradation requirement and the scalability constraint together.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attacked attributed graph G'=(V',E',X') containing injected malicious nodes with adversarially chosen features and edges, alongside the original node feature matrix and adjacency structure. It produces two outputs: a reconstructed subgraph G_r with malicious edges pruned (via DMGAN edge decoding), and per-node class labels from the DPMoE GNN classifier. The framework operates without ever materializing a full N×N adjacency matrix, keeping memory linear in N+E.

## Method

DRAGON combines two modules. First, a Denoise Masked Graph Auto-Encoder (DMGAN) uses random-walk path masking, a GCN encoder, and an MLP decoder trained self-supervised (binary cross-entropy for link prediction + L2 for degree regression) on clean graphs; at inference it decodes the attacked graph's edges and retains only those with high reconstruction confidence, thereby severing malicious edges attached to injected nodes. Second, each GNN convolution layer is replaced by a Differentially-Private Mixture-of-Experts (DPMoE) layer: the linear transformation is split into N expert sub-networks, each injecting Gaussian DP noise with a distinct scaling coefficient μ_i that increases linearly with expert index, and a softmax gating network (with added Gaussian randomness) selects the top-k experts per node. This lets the model apply minimal noise on clean inputs and progressively stronger noise as attack intensity grows, while a theoretical proposition (Proposition 1) certifies robustness when the expected correct-class output exceeds a DP-derived margin over all other classes. The overall design avoids O(N²) adjacency operations, yielding O(N+E) time and memory complexity.

## Results

On the GRB benchmark with FGSM node-injection attacks at five intensities, DRAGON maintains near-clean accuracy across all five datasets: 83.3% on Cora, 75.3% on Citeseer, 48.8% on Flickr, 95.8% on Reddit, and 49.7% on AMiner at the highest intensity (V), compared to clean accuracies of 84.1%, 75.9%, 52.7%, 96.2%, and 64.3% respectively. In contrast, most baselines suffer severe degradation (e.g., RGCN drops from 84.0% to 11.9% on Cora; GCN from 84.2% to 12.4%), while GCNSVD and GATGuard encounter out-of-memory failures on all medium and large datasets (Flickr, Reddit, AMiner). DRAGON outperforms the strongest baseline GAME+AT by up to 12.5 percentage points on Citeseer at intensity V and 10.4 points on Flickr at intensity V.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRAGON outperforms all 12 baselines on the primary FGSM injection-attack evaluation. Against the most competitive baseline GAME+AT (Zhang et al., ICLR 2023), DRAGON leads by 3.2 pp on Citeseer at intensity I (75.6 vs. 72.4) and by 12.5 pp at intensity V (75.3 vs. 62.8); on Flickr the gap widens from 6.4 pp (I) to 10.4 pp (V). GCNSVD (Entezari, WSDM 2020) and GATGuard (Zhang & Zitnik, NeurIPS 2020) are the only baselines that cannot run on medium/large datasets due to O(N²) adjacency-matrix operations, while DRAGON scales linearly. RGCN (Zhu, KDD 2019) and EvenNet (Lei, NeurIPS 2022) both suffer emergent degradation, dropping below 25% on Cora at intensity V, whereas DRAGON retains 83.3%. Under graph-modification attacks (GR-BCD/PR-BCD, Appendix E), DRAGON (78.8/78.4) surpasses Soft-Medoid GDC (77.5/76.1) and SVD GCN (75.5/72.4). Under adaptive attacks (Appendix F), DRAGON (80.3/79.9) outperforms GARNET (77.7/75.3) and GNNGuard (74.8/72.5). Structure-only defenses (Jaccard GCN, SoftMedian, GARNET) and anomaly-detection-based removal (CoLA) all degrade sharply under injection attacks, confirming that DRAGON's combined denoising + DP-MoE design is necessary for robustness against attacks that modify both edges and node features.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRAGON sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) graph adversarial defense methods like GCN-SVD, GNNGuard, RGCN, and GAME that address node injection attacks but suffer from O(N²) adjacency matrix operations or emergent robustness collapse at high attack intensities; (2) masked graph autoencoders (inspired by MAE and graph-specific variants) repurposed here as a denoising preprocessor to remove malicious edges; and (3) Mixture-of-Experts architectures (Liu et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023c) modified so that each expert carries a differentially-private Gaussian noise module with a distinct scaling coefficient. The key conceptual departure is the DPMoE design—splitting a DP-GConv layer into multiple experts with linearly increasing DP noise magnitudes—combined with a non-differentiable denoising autoencoder, yielding a framework with O(N+E) complexity that avoids the heavy spectral or adjacency-matrix computations of prior defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and names a practical failure mode—emergent robustness degradation at high attack intensities—that prior defenses (RGCN, GCN-SVD, EvenNet) exhibit but rarely address, and provides a scalable solution that maintains performance across five datasets from 2.7K to 660K nodes without OOM. However, the approach shows notably weaker gains on AMiner (low feature dimension, 100), the DMGAN's non-differentiability limits defense against adaptive attacks, and the theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) is a standard DP-based bound rather than a tight certified radius. Open questions include whether the DPMoE noise-scheduling strategy generalizes to modification attacks or multi-hop poisoning, and whether the linear-complexity advantage persists when the number of experts grows with graph size.
