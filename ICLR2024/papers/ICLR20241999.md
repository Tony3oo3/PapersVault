## Problem

Bayesian neural networks provide principled uncertainty quantification but incur substantially higher training and inference costs than deterministic networks. Existing sparse BNN methods either gradually introduce sparsity during training or prune after obtaining a dense BNN, leaving the dominant training cost largely unaffected. The paper asks how to build a fully sparse BNN framework that maintains high sparsity throughout both training and inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100) and outputs class labels along with uncertainty estimates obtained by drawing 5 Monte Carlo samples from the sparse posterior at inference time. Because the model operates within a fixed-dimensional sparse subspace (e.g., 90–95% of weights are zero) from initialization onward, the effective parameter count and per-step FLOPs are constant and drastically reduced compared to a dense BNN, with no post-training pruning step required.

## Method

SSVI confines the mean-field variational posterior q_φ(θ) to a sparse subspace S ⊂ R^d of fixed dimension s, so that only s weights carry non-zero (μ, σ²) parameters while all others are pinned to zero. Training alternates between two steps: (1) updating φ via M steps of standard VI gradient descent (using the local reparameterization trick for efficient forward passes) within the current subspace, and (2) updating the subspace indicator γ via a removal-and-addition strategy. Removal selects the least important active weights using novel criteria derived from the weight distribution statistics—most notably the signal-to-noise ratio of |θ|, which combines magnitude and uncertainty in a theoretically grounded way that bounds the influence of σ. Addition reintroduces weights by gradient magnitude using one-step Monte Carlo. A key technical detail is that newly activated σ parameters must be initialized to the mean of surviving σ values in the same layer, because the LRT gradient for σ is linearly dependent on σ itself and would otherwise remain zero.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SSVI achieves 93.74% test accuracy with 95% sparsity and only 5.7×10¹⁵ training FLOPs, outperforming the closest competitor mBNN (93.14%, 22.8×10¹⁵ FLOPs) while using 4× fewer FLOPs. On CIFAR-100, SSVI reaches 75.81% accuracy at 90% sparsity with 11.4×10¹⁵ FLOPs, surpassing mBNN (73.42%, 25.9×10¹⁵ FLOPs). Compared to dense BNNs, SSVI achieves 10–20× model size compression and training FLOPs reduction with under 3% accuracy drop and minimal ECE increase (≤0.002).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSVI dominates all five baselines on every reported metric (accuracy, loss, ECE, and training FLOPs) across both datasets. On CIFAR-10, SSVI beats the strongest prior method mBNN by 0.6% accuracy (93.74 vs. 93.14) while using 4× fewer training FLOPs (5.7 vs. 22.8×10¹⁵); it also achieves lower ECE (0.006 vs. 0.008). On CIFAR-100, the gap widens: SSVI outperforms mBNN by 2.4% accuracy (75.81 vs. 73.42) with 2.3× fewer FLOPs (11.4 vs. 25.9×10¹⁵). The older VI-based methods (VD, SBP) are far behind in both accuracy and sparsity, achieving only 58–81% sparsity with 67.1×10¹⁵ FLOPs. AEB, while reaching 90% sparsity, requires 383.4×10¹⁵ FLOPs—over 67× more than SSVI—due to its MCMC-based inference. RigL (Evci et al., 2020), a sparse DNN training method, is compared qualitatively in Figure 2: SSVI follows a similar accuracy-vs-sparsity trajectory but shows greater robustness, with a smaller accuracy decline as sparsity increases from 50% to 99%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSVI sits at the intersection of two research threads: sparse BNNs (which typically rely on sparse-promoting priors like log-uniform or spike-and-slab to gradually induce sparsity during training, or post-hoc pruning of dense BNNs) and fully sparse DNN training (exemplified by RigL, which maintains a fixed sparsity level throughout training via removal-and-addition). The paper departs from the sparse-BNN line by rejecting the need for intricately designed priors, instead confining the variational posterior to a fixed-dimension sparse subspace from initialization and co-optimizing the subspace basis with the variational parameters via alternating optimization. The closest prior work, mBNN (Kong et al., 2023), also uses a birth-and-death mechanism but requires a complex hierarchical sparse prior and MCMC inference, whereas SSVI operates entirely within standard VI with simple Gaussian distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSVI addresses a practical bottleneck in BNN deployment: the training cost of dense VI remains prohibitive even when inference is sparsified. By maintaining constant sparsity throughout training, SSVI achieves 10–20× FLOPs reduction with under 3% accuracy drop, making BNNs more accessible for resource-constrained settings. The finding that a sparse BNN can occasionally outperform its dense VI counterpart—largely due to reduced sensitivity to the σ initialization hyperparameter—challenges the implicit assumption that more parameters always yield better uncertainty estimates. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale models and deeper architectures, whether the SNR(|θ|) removal criterion generalizes beyond ResNet-18, and how the method interacts with structured (rather than unstructured) sparsity patterns.
