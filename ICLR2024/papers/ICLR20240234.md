## Problem

Existing distributed multimodal learning assumes every client possesses all modalities, which is unrealistic when multimodal data are expensive and sparse. This paper introduces 'patchwork learning,' where each client holds only a subset of modalities, and asks how to impute all missing modalities across clients without accessing raw data, while simultaneously handling statistical heterogeneity (non-i.i.d. local distributions) and modality combination heterogeneity (different clients observe different modality subsets).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240234/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client provides only its locally observed modalities (e.g., client 1 has {M1, M3, M4} while client 2 has {M4, M5}); the missing modalities are never available during training. The method outputs imputed values for every missing modality at every client, completing the full K-modality patchwork, and additionally yields a shared joint latent representation usable for downstream classification. Training follows a federated protocol in which only aggregated gradients are exchanged between server and clients, so raw data never leaves the local site.

## Method

CLAP consists of two synergistic components. A Modality VAE learns one encoder-decoder pair per modality, trained with a vertical Pareto min-max objective that minimizes the worst-client KL divergence to enforce a uniform latent space across heterogeneous clients. A Client-Adaptation VAE (CA-VAE) shares the modality encoders but adds a separate decoder; for each pair of clients it identifies the intersection of their observed modalities and trains a novel ELBO (Eq. 10) that approximates the KL between the PoE-aggregated posterior from the intersection and the true posterior over the full observed set, thereby learning only the dependencies actually needed for cross-client imputation. A horizontal Pareto min-max objective then balances these per-client ELBOs so that no single modality combination dominates the learned transformations. At inference, the maximal intersection combination is used to aggregate representations via Product-of-Experts and decode all missing modalities.

## Results

On PolyMNIST (5 modalities, 5 clients), CLAP achieves the best imputation coherence accuracy across all modalities, reaching 45.2% on M1, 79.3% on M4, and 50.5% on M5, compared to MoPoE (38.5%, 75.7%, 45.6%) and MVAE (22.1%, 68.7%, 19.6%). For latent representation quality, CLAP attains 91.9% average linear classification accuracy, surpassing MoPoE (85.7%), MMVAE (83.0%), and MVAE (77.9%). On the real-world eICU clinical dataset (13 modalities), CLAP achieves 62.3% AUC for mortality prediction and 0.0653 MSE, outperforming MVAE (56.3% AUC, 0.0661 MSE) and MMVAE (53.0% AUC, 0.0681 MSE); MoPoE cannot be applied due to the exponential 2^13 modality subsets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAP consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and metric. Against MVAE (Wu & Goodman, 2018), CLAP achieves substantially higher imputation coherence (e.g., 45.2% vs. 22.1% on PolyMNIST M1) because MVAE fails to model inter-modality dependencies, and higher latent classification accuracy (91.9% vs. 77.9% average on PolyMNIST). Against MMVAE (Shi et al., 2019), CLAP wins on both coherence and representation quality, with the gap widening on harder modalities (e.g., 50.5% vs. 34.4% on PolyMNIST M5). Against MoPoE (Sutter et al., 2020), CLAP achieves better coherence on most modalities (e.g., 45.2% vs. 38.5% on M1, 79.3% vs. 75.7% on M4) and superior latent classification (91.9% vs. 85.7% average), while MoPoE is entirely infeasible on the 13-modality eICU dataset due to its exponential 2^13 subset complexity. On the real-world eICU dataset, CLAP improves AUC by 6.0 points over MVAE and 9.3 points over MMVAE, demonstrating practical advantage in a clinical setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multimodal variational autoencoders (MVAE, MMVAE, MoPoE-VAE) and federated learning, but departs from both in a key way: prior distributed multimodal learning methods (Zhao et al., 2021; Xiong et al., 2022) assume every client observes all modalities, while patchwork learning explicitly handles clients with heterogeneous modality subsets. The technical machinery draws on Product-of-Experts from MVAE for representation aggregation and on Pareto min-max optimization from Cui et al. (2021) for balancing across clients, but the novel architectural contribution is the Client-Adaptation VAE (CA-VAE), which identifies the maximal intersection of observed modalities between client pairs as the optimal dependency to learn, and the dual vertical/horizontal Pareto min-max formulation that simultaneously addresses statistical heterogeneity and modality-combination vulnerability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: in real distributed settings (e.g., multi-hospital clinical networks), different sites rarely collect the same set of modalities, yet existing federated multimodal methods assume complete modality coverage at every node. The eICU validation demonstrates that the approach is feasible on 13-modality tabular clinical data where MoPoE becomes intractable (2^13 subsets). Open questions include how to integrate differential privacy into the gradient-sharing protocol (acknowledged but not addressed), whether the intersection-based dependency identification scales gracefully to very large modality spaces, and how the method performs in fully unsupervised or large-model multimodal settings.
