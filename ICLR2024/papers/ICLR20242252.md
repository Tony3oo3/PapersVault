## Problem

Standard adversarial training is formulated as a two-player zero-sum game where both the defender and adversary optimize the same surrogate loss (e.g., cross-entropy) in place of the discontinuous 0-1 classification error. The paper proves that this surrogate-based relaxation voids all robustness guarantees: maximizing a surrogate upper bound on classification error provides no guarantee that the classification error actually increases, yielding weak adversaries and ineffective training. This misalignment is identified as the root cause of robust overfitting and the plateau in adversarial robustness on standard benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The BETA attack takes an image-label pair (x, y) with pixel values in [0,1]^d, a perturbation budget ε (e.g., 8/255 for CIFAR-10 32×32 images), and a trained model f_θ, and produces a single adversarial perturbation η* by solving K−1 parallel margin-maximization subproblems (one per non-true class) and selecting the perturbation that achieves the largest negative margin. BETA-AT consumes a full dataset of such pairs and outputs a robust classifier f_θ* trained by iteratively computing BETA perturbations on mini-batches and updating θ via gradient descent on the surrogate loss evaluated at the perturbed inputs.

## Method

The core contribution is a non-zero-sum bilevel formulation of adversarial training that decouples the attacker and defender objectives. The attacker maximizes the negative margin M_θ(X,Y)_j = f_θ(X)_j − f_θ(X)_Y for each class j ≠ Y, which Proposition 1 shows is an exact differentiable reformulation of the 0-1 misclassification indicator (not a relaxation), so the adversary directly optimizes the true classification error. The defender minimizes a standard surrogate loss (e.g., cross-entropy) on the perturbed data, preserving the usual guarantee that surrogate minimization decreases the 0-1 loss. Algorithmically, BETA runs K−1 parallel gradient-based optimizations (e.g., RMSprop) with random initializations within the ℓ∞ ball, projects onto the valid perturbation set after each step, and returns the perturbation with the largest margin; BETA-AT wraps this in a stochastic training loop. A smooth variant (SBETA-AT) replaces the hard argmax over classes with a softmax-weighted combination of all class-specific perturbations, making the entire bilevel problem end-to-end differentiable.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ε=8/255), BETA-AT²⁰ achieves 49.96% robust test accuracy under PGD¹⁰ and 45.27% under APGD (best checkpoint), with last-iterate accuracy within 1.3% of best, demonstrating the elimination of robust overfitting. BETA-AT¹⁰ reaches 45.67% under PGD¹⁰ and 44.32% under APGD. As an attack, 30-step BETA matches AutoAttack (100 iterations, 5 restarts) within 0.03–0.17 percentage points on top RobustBench models, while being 5.11× faster on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BETA-AT²⁰ outperforms all 10-step baselines on last-iterate robust accuracy: it exceeds PGD¹⁰ (48.67% vs. 39.90% under PGD¹⁰), TRADES¹⁰ (48.67% vs. 42.31%), and MART¹⁰ (48.67% vs. 41.12%) by 6–9 percentage points, with the gap driven primarily by the absence of robust overfitting (Best–Last gap of ≤1.3% for BETA-AT vs. 6.8–8.0% for baselines). FGSM-trained models collapse to near-zero robust accuracy on last iterate (1.49% under PGD¹⁰), making them non-competitive. As an attack, BETA (30 steps, single restart) matches AutoAttack (100 iterations, 5 restarts) within 0.03–0.17 percentage points across nine top RobustBench models (e.g., 70.78% vs. 70.69% on WRN-70-16), while running 5.11× faster. BETA also matches APGD-Targeted (30 steps) within 0.01–0.13 percentage points, confirming that the margin-based objective captures the same adversarial signal as the more complex ensemble-based AutoAttack without heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard zero-sum minimax formulation of adversarial training (Madry et al., 2018) by arguing that replacing the 0-1 loss with a surrogate (e.g., cross-entropy) for both the attacker and defender simultaneously voids all robustness guarantees. It builds on the surrogate loss theory of Bartlett et al. (2006) for the defender's side, while introducing a negative-margin objective for the attacker that directly characterizes misclassification. The multi-targeted attack idea from Gowal et al. (2019) is extended from linear models to nonlinear DNNs, and the bilevel structure differs from Zhang et al. (2022), which was designed specifically to justify FastAT iterates rather than arising from principled relaxations of the classification error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete theoretical flaw in the dominant AT paradigm and shows that a clean bilevel reformulation eliminates robust overfitting without any heuristics, regularization tricks, or ad-hoc schedules — a result that reframes robust overfitting as an artifact of misaligned objectives rather than an inherent property of adversarial training. BETA matching AutoAttack's robustness estimates while being 5× faster and using an off-the-shelf optimizer is practically significant for evaluation. Open questions include whether the bilevel formulation scales to ImageNet or larger perturbation budgets, whether theoretical consistency/calibration guarantees can be established for the negative-margin objective, and whether the approach extends to other threat models (e.g., ℓ₂, semantic perturbations).
