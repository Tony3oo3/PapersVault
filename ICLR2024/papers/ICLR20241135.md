## Problem

Federated learning involves two coupled learning rates (global on the server, local on clients) whose optimal values shift with data heterogeneity, model architecture, and training stage, yet existing schedulers (FedAdam, FedAdagrad, FedExp) primarily adjust only the global rate and fail to remain robust when initial learning rates are suboptimal. No prior method simultaneously schedules both global and local rates while guaranteeing convergence under arbitrary initializations.

## Input / Output

FEDHYPER operates as a drop-in scheduler on top of FedAvg: it receives the standard FedAvg inputs (initial global weights, a user-specified initial global rate α and local rate β, and per-client local data batches) and outputs the trained global model alongside dynamically adapted α^(t) and β^(t,k) at every round and local step. Both rates are clipped to fixed bounds [1/γ_α, γ_α] and [1/γ_β, γ_β] (γ_α=3, γ_β=10) to guarantee convergence, and the scheduler is composable with existing FL optimizers such as server momentum or FedAdam without modifying their internal update rules.

## Method

FEDHYPER applies hypergradient descent to adapt learning rates by computing the inner product of consecutive model updates: a positive inner product (aligned gradients) increases the rate to accelerate progress, while a negative one (diverged gradients) decreases it to suppress oscillation. It comprises three schedulers: FEDHYPER-G updates the global rate using the inner product of consecutive aggregated client updates (treated as pseudo-global gradients); FEDHYPER-SL applies the same hypergradient to synchronously adjust all clients' local rates from the server; and FEDHYPER-CL performs fine-grained per-batch local rate updates on each client using the inner product of consecutive local gradients, augmented by a normalization term (1/K)·g_m(w_m^(t,k))·Δ^(t−1) that anchors local updates to the global direction and prevents individual clients from diverging. The convergence proof builds on the FedNova framework, showing that under standard smoothness, bounded-variance, and bounded-dissimilarity assumptions, the optimization error is O(P/√(MkT) + Q/(kT)) when learning rates are bounded. Experiments on FMNIST, CIFAR-10, and Shakespeare show 1.1–3× faster convergence than FedAvg and up to 15% accuracy improvement under suboptimal initial learning rates.

## Results

FEDHYPER converges 1.1–3× faster than FedAvg and competing baselines while achieving superior final accuracy across all three benchmarks. Specifically, FEDHYPER-G improves accuracy by 0.45% (FMNIST), 0.58% (CIFAR-10), and 0.66% (Shakespeare) over the best baseline, while FEDHYPER-CL outperforms FedAvg+SGD by 0.44%, 1.18%, and 1.33% respectively. Under suboptimal initial learning rates, FEDHYPER augments accuracy by up to 15% over FedAvg (e.g., 30% → 45.77% on Shakespeare with initial local LR = 0.001 and global LR = 0.5). The computational overhead of FEDHYPER-CL is less than 5% compared to FedAvg(SGD) (45,780 s vs. 44,031 s for 200 rounds on CIFAR-10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), FEDHYPER wins on all three datasets: FEDHYPER-CL achieves +0.44% (FMNIST), +1.18% (CIFAR-10), and +1.33% (Shakespeare) accuracy gains, while also converging up to 3× faster. Against FedAdam and FedAdagrad (Reddi et al., 2020), FEDHYPER-G consistently outperforms both as a global scheduler, with the gap being most pronounced in the early training stages. Against FedExp (Jhunjhunwala et al., 2023), FEDHYPER-G shows comparable performance on FMNIST and CIFAR-10 but clearly outperforms it on Shakespeare, where FedExp exhibits unstable accuracy in late training. Against server momentum (Liu et al., 2020), FEDHYPER-G + server momentum surpasses FedAvg + server momentum, demonstrating that FEDHYPER acts as a plug-in enhancement for existing optimizers. Overall, FEDHYPER dominates or matches all baselines, with the largest margins appearing under suboptimal initial learning rate configurations where FedAvg degrades significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDHYPER adapts the hypergradient descent framework from centralized learning (Baydin et al., 2017; Chen et al., 2022) to the two-level structure of federated learning, where both a global server learning rate and per-client local learning rates must be scheduled simultaneously. It departs from prior FL optimizers (FedAdam, FedAdagrad, FedExp) that primarily adjust only the global learning rate, by introducing a three-tier scheduler (global, server-side local, client-side local) that jointly coordinates server-client learning rate dynamics. The convergence analysis is built on the FedNova unified framework (Wang et al., 2020b), extended to accommodate dynamic learning rates bounded by clipping constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the expensive trial-and-error process of tuning initial learning rates in FL, where each experiment incurs substantial communication and computation costs. The demonstrated robustness—up to 15% accuracy recovery under severely suboptimal initial rates—addresses a real deployment bottleneck. However, the experimental scope is limited to three relatively small datasets with 100 clients and modest architectures (CNN, ResNet-18, BiLSTM), leaving open questions about scalability to larger models, more clients, and more complex FL variants (personalized FL, asynchronous settings). Whether the hypergradient mechanism generalizes to other hyperparameters (batch size, momentum) or to non-convex deep network training at scale remains unexplored.
