## Problem

Sampling from pre-trained flow or diffusion models requires solving high-dimensional ODEs, and off-the-shelf solvers need hundreds of function evaluations (NFE) for high-quality samples. Existing remedies—distillation (costly to train, can degrade quality) and hand-designed dedicated solvers (still require large NFE)—do not exploit the fact that each trained model has unique sampling-path characteristics that lead to model-specific truncation errors. The paper asks whether a lightweight, model-specific solver can be learned to dramatically reduce NFE while preserving consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240492/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained velocity field u_t (from ε-VP, FM-OT, or FM/v-CS models), a noise sample x_0 ~ N(0,I), and a small set of learned solver parameters θ (e.g., 80 parameters for a 10-step RK2-Bespoke solver). It outputs a single generated image x_n after n solver steps. A key practical detail is that a separate θ must be trained for each (model, target NFE, guidance weight) combination, and the training requires only ~1% of the original model's GPU time using a naive implementation that re-samples the model at each iteration.

## Method

The core idea is to define a parametric family of consistent ODE solvers by applying a learnable scale-time transformation (time reparametrization t_r and positive scaling s_r) to the sampling paths before feeding them through a standard base solver (RK1 or RK2), then transforming back. The transformed vector field has an explicit closed form (Proposition 2.1), and Theorem 2.2 guarantees the resulting solver retains the base solver's order, ensuring consistency as NFE→∞. Training minimizes an RMSE-Bound loss that upper-bounds the global truncation error via a product of per-step Lipschitz constants, enabling parallel computation over all n steps and avoiding the memory cost of backpropagating through the full n-step chain. Theorem 2.3 further shows that the scale-time transformation space is equivalent to the space of all Gaussian Path schedulers, so the learned solver effectively searches over all possible noise-schedule reparameterizations. In practice, the solver is trained with Adam (lr=2e-3) over 6k iterations on 48k–72k GT trajectories computed with an adaptive RK45 solver, using a stop-gradient linearization trick to handle gradients with respect to the learned time steps.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, RK2-Bespoke with FM-OT achieves FID 2.73 at 10 NFE and 2.59 at 20 NFE, reaching within 1% of the ground-truth FID (2.57) with only 20 NFE. On the more challenging ImageNet-64×64, RK2-Bespoke with FM/v-CS achieves FID 2.20 at 10 NFE and 1.71 at 20 NFE (within 2% of GT FID 1.68). On ImageNet-128×128, RK2-Bespoke FM-OT reaches FID 2.45 at 20 NFE (GT FID 2.30). Training a Bespoke solver requires only ~1% of the GPU time needed to train the original pre-trained model, with as few as 80 learnable parameters for a 10-step solver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bespoke solvers decisively outperform all dedicated and learned solvers at low NFE. At 10 NFE on CIFAR-10, RK2-Bespoke FM-OT (FID 2.73) improves over the best dedicated solver DEIS (FID 4.17) by 34%, over DPM-Solver (FID 4.70) by 42%, over GENIE (FID 5.28) by 48%, and over DDSS (FID 7.86) by 65%. Against the EDM baseline (Karras et al., 2022) on the pre-trained CIFAR-10 model, RK2-Bespoke achieves FID 2.99 at 20 NFE versus EDM's FID 3.05 at 35 NFE, a 42% reduction in NFE. The only method that achieves a lower FID is progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022) at 8 NFE (FID 2.57), but distillation requires GPU training time comparable to the original model, whereas Bespoke training takes only ~1% of that time. On ImageNet-64, RK2-Bespoke FM/v-CS reaches FID 1.71 at 20 NFE (within 2% of GT), substantially outperforming DPM-2nd order and other baselines shown in Figure 5. The paper also demonstrates that Bespoke solvers are consistent (Theorem 2.2), meaning they converge to the pre-trained model's distribution as NFE increases, a property that distillation methods lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scale-time transformation concept introduced by Karras et al. (EDM, 2022), which hand-designed specific transformations to improve sampling efficiency, by making the transformation fully learnable and optimizing it per pre-trained model. It builds on the flow matching velocity field formulation (Lipman et al., 2022) and positions itself within the dedicated ODE solver literature (DPM-Solver, DEIS, GENIE), but departs from both hand-crafted solvers and prior learned solvers (DDSS, Duan et al.) by guaranteeing consistency by construction and minimizing a tractable upper bound on global truncation error. Theorem 2.3 provides a unifying theoretical lens, showing that all Gaussian Path schedulers in the diffusion/flow literature are related by scale-time transformations, thereby subsuming prior heuristic scheduler choices as special cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a solver with as few as 80 learnable parameters, trained in ~1% of the original model's GPU time, achieves near-ground-truth FID at 10–20 NFE across CIFAR-10, ImageNet-64/128, and AFHQ-256, offering a lightweight path to fast inference without distillation's training cost or quality degradation. Key limitations include the need for separate solver training per target NFE and guidance weight, and the fact that it still does not match the best distillation methods at very low NFE. Open questions include whether a continuous or NFE-general representation of the transformation can be learned, whether the solver can be conditioned on guidance weight, and whether more expressive (non-scale) transformations yield further gains.
