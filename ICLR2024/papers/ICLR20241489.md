## Problem

GNN uncertainty estimates are known to deteriorate under distribution shifts, yet existing post-hoc calibration methods only improve in-distribution calibration and have not been validated on OOD data. The Δ-UQ stochastic centering framework, which produces reliable intrinsic uncertainty from a single model, was designed for vision and cannot be directly applied to graphs due to their variable size, discrete structure, and iterative message-passing dynamics. This paper asks how to adapt anchoring-based uncertainty estimation to GNNs and whether full network stochasticity is truly required.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard graph inputs—node feature matrices X⁰ ∈ ℝ^{N×d} and adjacency matrices A ∈ [0,1]^{N×N}—and produces class predictions (node-level or graph-level) along with epistemic uncertainty estimates. At inference, the single model is evaluated K times (typically K=10) with different randomly sampled anchors; the mean prediction μ and variance σ across these K runs yield a calibrated output μ_calib = μ(1−σ). A key design constraint is that anchors must be sampled from a distribution compatible with variable-sized graphs, which rules out direct pairwise subtraction of adjacency matrices or arbitrary node-feature subsets.

## Method

G-ΔUQ adapts stochastic data centering to GNNs through three graph-specific anchoring strategies that trade off stochasticity against anchor semantic expressivity. Node Feature Anchoring (NFA) fits a d-dimensional Gaussian over training node features and creates anchored representations [X⁰−C‖X⁰] per node, inducing a fully stochastic GNN analogous to the original Δ-UQ. Hidden Layer Anchoring performs anchoring at an intermediate MPNN layer r by shuffling node representations across the batch to form anchors, making layers 1…r−1 deterministic while the remaining layers and classifier remain stochastic. READOUT Anchoring pushes anchoring to the graph-level readout, so only the final classifier head is stochastic, which also makes the method compatible with pretrained GNN backbones (only the anchored classifier is trained). The central empirical finding is that full stochasticity is unnecessary: partially stochastic GNNs with deeper, more semantically expressive anchors achieve equal or better calibration and OOD detection than fully stochastic variants, because the functional diversity of sampled hypotheses matters more than raw stochasticity.

## Results

On node classification (GOODCora, degree shift), G-ΔUQ without any post-hoc calibration reduces ECE from 0.307 to 0.130 (concept) and 0.348 to 0.141 (covariate) while also improving accuracy (0.581→0.595, 0.470→0.518). On graph classification (GOODMotif, basis shift), G-ΔUQ achieves ECE 0.078 vs 0.095 for vanilla under concept shift. On the RotMNIST structural shift benchmark, G-ΔUQ reduces average ECE from 0.038 to 0.018 for GatedGCN, and combining G-ΔUQ with Deep Ensembles further lowers it to 0.014 (vs 0.026 for basic ensembles). Across all 8 node-classification evaluation sets, G-ΔUQ matches or beats vanilla accuracy (8/8) and improves ECE (7/8) without any post-hoc calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the vanilla GNN (no G-ΔUQ), which G-ΔUQ outperforms on ECE in 7/8 node-classification benchmarks without any post-hoc calibration (e.g., ECE drops from 0.307 to 0.130 on GOODCora concept shift). Against Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), single-model G-ΔUQ is competitive or superior on most datasets: on GOODCora, G-ΔUQ (ECE 0.130) dramatically outperforms Deep Ensembles (ECE 0.437) under concept shift, while on GOODMotif and GOODSST2, Deep Ensembles retain a slight ECE edge (0.047 vs 0.078; 0.248 vs 0.277). However, combining G-ΔUQ with ensembling (anchored ensembles) consistently outperforms basic ensembles: on RotMNIST, GatedGCN+G-ΔUQ+Ens. achieves average ECE 0.014 vs 0.026 for GatedGCN+Ens., and GPS+G-ΔUQ+Ens. achieves 0.014 vs 0.016 for GPS+Ens. The paper also shows that G-ΔUQ can be combined with post-hoc calibration methods (Spline, IRM, Dirichlet, etc.) to further improve ECE relative to applying the same post-hoc method to a vanilla model, e.g., on WebKB concept shift, G-ΔUQ+Spline achieves ECE 0.386 vs 0.436 for vanilla+Spline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Δ-UQ stochastic data centering framework (Thiagarajan et al., 2022) from vision models to graph neural networks, requiring novel graph-specific anchoring strategies to handle variable-sized, discrete, structured data where simple input-space subtraction is inapplicable. It departs from the Δ-UQ assumption that fully stochastic networks are necessary by introducing partially stochastic variants (hidden-layer and READOUT anchoring) that trade stochasticity for semantic expressivity of anchors. The work also positions itself against post-hoc calibration methods (temperature scaling, Dirichlet, etc.) and Deep Ensembles, arguing that intrinsic training-time uncertainty estimation provides more robust OOD estimates than validation-set-fitted rescaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As GNNs are increasingly deployed in safety-critical domains (drug discovery, brain data classification) where test-time distribution shifts are expected, reliable intrinsic uncertainty estimates are essential for model trustworthiness. The practical finding that READOUT anchoring on a frozen pretrained backbone is competitive with or superior to end-to-end training offers a lightweight, low-compute path to safer GNNs. Open questions include automatic selection of the optimal anchoring layer, understanding why pretrained G-ΔUQ sometimes outperforms end-to-end variants, and quantifying how much calibration improvement translates to real-world safety gains in deployed systems.
