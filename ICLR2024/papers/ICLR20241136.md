## Problem

During adversarial training, accuracy disparity between classes (the robust fairness problem) is amplified, with the worst-performing class suffering disproportionately. Existing methods address this by uniformly restricting easy classes to boost hard ones, but the paper identifies that hard classes are primarily misclassified toward *similar* neighboring classes rather than easy ones, meaning the relevant factor is inter-class distance, not just class difficulty. This paper provides theoretical and empirical evidence that robust fairness deteriorates as inter-class distance shrinks, and proposes a distance-aware solution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 96×96 resized to 64×64 for STL-10) and outputs a class label via a ResNet18 (or PreActResNet18 / WideResNet-28) CNN. A distinguishing detail is that during a 70-epoch warm-up phase, the model's softmax output probabilities on adversarial examples are collected to compute per-class weights; these weights are then fixed for the remainder of training and modulate both the cross-entropy loss coefficient and the per-class adversarial margin ε, so the same network architecture produces class-differentiated training dynamics without any architectural change.

## Method

DAFA builds on the TRADES adversarial training loss and introduces two class-wise modifications driven by inter-class similarity. After a warm-up phase, the average class-wise softmax probability p_i(j) is computed for every pair of classes, capturing how much class i's samples are confused with class j. A per-class weight W_i is then assigned via Eq. 8: for each pair (i, j), if class i is harder (lower p_i(i)), its weight is increased by λ·p_i(j)·p_j(j) while the easier class's weight is decreased by the analogous term, so that the magnitude of adjustment is proportional to the inter-class confusion probability. These weights multiply both the cross-entropy loss and the adversarial margin ε in the TRADES objective (Eq. 9), effectively shifting the decision boundary away from the hard class's center toward its similar neighbors while leaving boundaries with distant classes largely unaffected. The paper supports this design with two theorems showing that both per-class error and the accuracy gap decrease monotonically as inter-class distance grows, and with empirical ablations confirming that the loss-weight and margin components each contribute additively to worst-class robust accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DAFA achieves 30.53% worst robust accuracy (vs. 21.31% for TRADES baseline) while maintaining 49.05% average robust accuracy (vs. 49.80% baseline), yielding the highest ρ_rob of 0.42 among all methods. On CIFAR-100, DAFA reaches 2.33% worst robust accuracy (vs. 1.33% baseline) with 25.08% average robust accuracy and ρ_rob of 0.73. On STL-10, DAFA attains 10.73% worst robust accuracy (vs. 7.73% baseline) with 29.98% average robust accuracy and ρ_rob of 0.34, consistently outperforming all five competing robust fairness methods on worst-class robust accuracy across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAFA outperforms all five baselines on worst robust accuracy across all three datasets: on CIFAR-10 it achieves 30.53% vs. 28.55% (WAT), 27.62% (CFA), 25.84% (FRL), 21.31% (TRADES), and 17.82% (BAT). Crucially, DAFA is the only method that simultaneously improves worst robust accuracy by >9% over TRADES while keeping average robust accuracy within 0.75% of the baseline (49.05% vs. 49.80%), whereas FRL, BAT, and WAT sacrifice 3–5% average robust accuracy. On CIFAR-100, where worst robust accuracy is extremely low, DAFA achieves 2.33% (ρ_rob = 0.73) compared to 1.87% (WAT), 1.80% (FRL), 1.67% (CFA), 1.33% (TRADES), and 0.20% (BAT), again with minimal loss in average robust accuracy (25.08% vs. 25.60%). On STL-10, DAFA reaches 10.73% worst robust accuracy, surpassing CFA (7.69%), FRL (7.94%), TRADES (7.73%), WAT (6.72%), and BAT (3.22%), while maintaining 29.98% average robust accuracy close to the 31.36% baseline. BAT consistently underperforms, showing negative ρ_rob on all datasets, indicating it degrades average robustness more than it improves worst-class performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAFA builds on the robust fairness line of work initiated by Benz et al. (2021) and formalized by Xu et al. (2021), which drew an analogy between long-tailed classification and adversarial training fairness. Prior methods (FRL, CFA, WAT, BAT) all attribute the accuracy disparity to class-wise variance or training difficulty, applying stronger regularization to easy classes to free capacity for hard ones. DAFA departs from this variance-centric framing by identifying inter-class distance (similarity) as the operative factor: it shows that hard-class misclassifications concentrate on neighboring classes rather than easy ones, and that constraining similar classes—rather than merely easy classes—is the more effective lever for shifting decision boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual reframing from variance to inter-class distance offers a more mechanistic explanation for robust fairness and a more targeted intervention (class-specific loss weights and adversarial margins derived from softmax confusion patterns). Practically, DAFA improves worst-class robust accuracy by ~9 percentage points on CIFAR-10 while preserving average accuracy, outperforming all prior fairness methods on the ρ metric across three datasets. Limitations include reliance on a single static weight computation after warm-up (no adaptive re-weighting), evaluation restricted to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, STL-10) and shallow CNNs, and the method's coupling to the TRADES loss structure. Open questions include whether the distance-based weighting generalizes to larger architectures (e.g., ViTs), larger datasets (ImageNet), and whether dynamic re-computation of class-wise probabilities during training would yield further gains.
