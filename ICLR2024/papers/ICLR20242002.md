## Problem

Active learning for GNNs faces two intertwined challenges: existing methods either require costly iterative model retraining or depend heavily on node features that may be noisy or unavailable, and the positioning of labeled nodes introduces a label position bias that causes unfair performance disparities across graph regions. The paper asks whether graph structure alone can serve as a reliable criterion for selecting representative labeling nodes that simultaneously improve GNN accuracy and mitigate this structural fairness issue.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph adjacency matrix (from which a sparse Personalized PageRank matrix P is computed via PPRGo with top-k entries per row), an optional node feature matrix, and an integer annotation budget b. It outputs a binary labeling indicator vector t ∈ {0,1}^n with exactly b ones, obtained by multinomial sampling from the optimized continuous solution and selecting the best candidate among R=3000 random trials based on the overall loss. The method is one-step and model-agnostic: no GNN is trained during the selection process, and the same t can be used with any downstream GNN backbone.

## Method

SCARCE formulates one-step active learning as a combinatorial optimization over a binary selection vector t, relaxed to its convex hull and solved via projected gradient descent. The objective jointly minimizes the LPS variance (‖Pt − c·1‖², promoting fairness by equalizing label proximity across nodes) and maximizes the Structure Inertia Score (Σᵢ maxⱼ Pᵢⱼtⱼ, promoting representativeness by ensuring every node is close to at least one selected node). A gradient balance mechanism normalizes each term's gradient to unit L1-norm and scales by the minimum gradient magnitude, preventing any single criterion from dominating the optimization trajectory without requiring hyperparameter tuning. Optionally, a Feature Inertia Score (FIS) based on propagated feature similarity is added as a third term, yielding SCARCE-ALL. The entire procedure requires only the PPR matrix and runs in O(nk) per gradient step, making it scalable to graphs with millions of nodes.

## Results

On the Computers dataset (Z=5, GCN), SCARCE-Structure achieves 79.51% accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (Random at 72.94%) by 6.6 points and FeatProp (64.68%) by 14.8 points. On Photo (Z=5, GCN), SCARCE-Structure reaches 87.65%, surpassing FeatProp (74.48%) by 13.2 points. On citation datasets, SCARCE-ALL achieves the best results: 80.58% on Cora, 66.03% on CiteSeer, and 74.01% on PubMed (all Z=5, GCN). The method also scales to OGB Products (2.4M nodes), where SCARCE achieves 74.43% (GCN) and 76.57% (APPNP) at 20C budget, while GraphPart times out (>24 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCARCE-Structure decisively outperforms FeatProp (Wu et al., 2019) on the Amazon co-purchase datasets: 79.51% vs. 64.68% on Computers and 87.65% vs. 74.48% on Photo (Z=5, GCN), a gap of 13–15 points. Against GraphPart (Ma et al., 2022), SCARCE-Structure wins by 15.4 points on Computers and 16.5 points on Photo, while SCARCE-ALL edges out GraphPart on citation datasets (e.g., 80.58% vs. 78.59% on Cora, 66.03% vs. 63.71% on CiteSeer). The general AL baselines (Uncertainty from Settles et al., 2008; CoreSet from Sener & Savarese, 2018) are consistently outperformed across all datasets, with CoreSet showing particularly high variance (e.g., 56.12±14.37 on Computers). Structure-only baselines from Cai et al. (2017) — Degree, PageRank, and AGE — are also surpassed: SCARCE-Structure exceeds PageRank by 6.1 points on Computers and 6.6 points on Photo. Notably, on the large-scale OGB Products dataset (2.4M nodes), GraphPart times out (>24 hours) while SCARCE completes and achieves 74.43% (GCN) and 76.57% (APPNP) at 20C budget, demonstrating both accuracy and scalability advantages.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCARCE builds on two key prior works: the label position bias framework from Han et al. (2023), which introduces the Label Proximal Score (LPS) as a graph-specific fairness metric, and the convex relaxation technique from Xu et al. (2019) for combinatorial graph optimization. It departs from the dominant feature-propagation paradigm (FeatProp, GraphPart) by demonstrating that original node features can be uninformative or even harmful for active learning, and instead grounds node selection entirely in graph structure via Personalized PageRank. The unified optimization framework with gradient balance is a methodological contribution that unifies multiple selection criteria (LPS variance for fairness, Structure Inertia Score for representativeness) into a single differentiable objective, distinguishing it from prior methods that apply criteria sequentially or heuristically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work is significant because it identifies and addresses a graph-specific fairness issue (label position bias) that is distinct from traditional demographic bias, showing that structural fairness and overall GNN performance are aligned rather than in tension. The negative result—that feature-based AL methods like FeatProp can underperform random selection on co-purchase graphs—challenges a widely held assumption in the GNN-AL literature and motivates structure-first approaches. Limitations include the restriction to one-step active learning (no iterative retraining), reliance on PPR matrix computation (mitigated by sparse approximation), and evaluation limited to semi-supervised node classification. Open questions include whether the structural criteria generalize to multi-step AL, link prediction, or other graph tasks, and whether the fairness-performance alignment holds under adversarial or distributional shifts in graph structure.
