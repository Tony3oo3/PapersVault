## Problem

Shannon and Weaver's communication theory identifies three levels—technical, semantic, and effectiveness—and while LLMs have made progress on the first two, the effectiveness level (predicting and optimizing content for desired receiver behaviors like clicks, likes, and purchases) remains largely unsolved. The root cause is that standard LLM training corpora strip out receiver behavior data (e.g., Enron emails lack open/reply metadata; CommonCrawl forums remove like counts), so no existing model has been exposed to the joint content-behavior space needed to simulate or optimize for human responses.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single concatenated text sequence that interleaves verbalized visual tokens (from EVA-CLIP + Q-Former), scene-level ASR transcripts and BLIP captions, channel/account metadata, and numeric behavior metrics (replay values, view counts, like ratios, CTR) all rendered as text tokens. Outputs are also text: predicted behavior values (e.g., 'Replay: 60%', 'CTR: 0.037%'), masked content segments chosen from 25 options, generated tweet text, or free-form sentiment explanations. The key design choice is that behavior is encoded as discrete text tokens within the same autoregressive sequence as content, rather than as a separate regression or classification head.

## Method

LCBM builds on Vicuna-13B (Llama-based) with an EVA-CLIP visual encoder, a Global Multi-Head Relation Aggregator (GMHRA) from Uniformer for temporal aggregation across video frames, and a Q-Former (from BLIP-2) with extra query tokens to project visual tokens into the LLM's language space. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: first, standard vision-language alignment on WebVid, COCO, Visual Genome, CC3M, and CC12M; second, Behavior Instruction Fine-Tuning (BFT), where the model is instruction-tuned end-to-end on verbalized content-behavior pairs to learn both forward prediction (behavior given content) and reverse prediction (content given behavior). The core insight is to treat receiver behavior as a new modality in the text-to-text framework—exactly analogous to how vision was grafted onto LLMs—so that the same autoregressive objective and decoding process handles both content and behavior. During BFT, the visual encoder is frozen while the LLM weights are updated, and the model is trained with varying context-window masking strategies (3, 5, 7, 11 masked scenes) to encourage generalization. This yields a 13B model that outperforms GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 (10–12× larger) on all behavior-related tasks while also showing unexpected zero-shot behavior domain adaptation across YouTube, Twitter, and email marketing domains.

## Results

On behavior simulation (replay value prediction), LCBM-13B (3-BFT) achieves RMSE 8.12 and 55.10% accuracy in the Future setting, and RMSE 8.55 / 61.41% in the Random-10 setting, substantially outperforming GPT-4 (RMSE 34.45 / 20.55% Future) and GPT-3.5 (RMSE 34.10 / 19.06% Future) while being at least 10× smaller. On likes/views ratio prediction, LCBM achieves R²=0.87 versus R²=−0.01 for GPT-4 and R²=−256.96 for GPT-3.5. On content simulation, LCBM reaches 48.68% accuracy vs. 34.98% for GPT-3.5 and 19.30% for Vicuna-13B. On behavior understanding, LCBM scores 72.73% sentiment accuracy and 4.00/5 reasoning score, beating both GPT-3.5 (61.62%, 1.67) and Vicuna (65.66%, 2.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCBM-13B decisively outperforms GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 on all behavior-related tasks despite being 10–12× smaller: on replay value prediction it achieves ~4× lower RMSE and ~2–3× higher accuracy; on likes/views ratio it reaches R²=0.87 while GPT-4 is at R²≈0 and GPT-3.5 is catastrophically negative (R²=−257). On content simulation (25-way scene ASR selection), LCBM (48.68%) beats GPT-3.5 (34.98%) by 14 points and Vicuna-13B (19.30%) by nearly 30 points. On behavior understanding (sentiment + reasoning), LCBM leads GPT-3.5 by 11 points in sentiment accuracy and by 2.33/5 in human-rated reasoning quality. On content understanding (Table 5), GPT-3.5 still leads LCBM on topic (51.6% vs. 42.2%) and persuasion (79.7% vs. 58.8%), but LCBM surpasses both Vicuna and VideoChat on all sub-tasks, confirming that behavior tokens do not degrade content understanding. On the LVU benchmark (zero-shot like-ratio prediction), LCBM achieves MSE 0.14, beating all trained baselines (best: Object Transformers at 0.23), though GPT-3.5 surprisingly achieves lower MSE (0.03) on the smaller test split. On Twitter like prediction, LCBM trained on both Twitter and YouTube (76.87% time-separated, 92.19% brand-separated) outperforms GPT-3.5 (58.84%, 64.19%) and also shows domain-adaptation gains over Twitter-only LCBM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-text paradigm of T5 and the visual instruction tuning framework of LLaVA/BLIP-2 by introducing a new modality—receiver behavior tokens (replay values, likes, views, CTR)—into the LLM training corpus. Architecturally, it follows the standard VLM recipe (EVA-CLIP visual encoder + QFormer + Vicuna-13B LLM), but departs from prior behavior-prediction work (regression, CTR models, popularity prediction networks) by casting behavior simulation, content simulation, and behavior explanation as unified instruction-tuning tasks within a single autoregressive language model. The conceptual lineage traces back to Shannon & Weaver's Level C (effectiveness) problem, which the authors argue has been neglected because standard LLM training corpora (Common Crawl, Enron, etc.) strip out receiver behavior before training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that behavior tokens can be integrated into LLMs to enable behavior simulation, content simulation, and cross-behavior domain adaptation within a single model, opening a path toward content optimization for desired receiver responses. The released Content Behavior Corpus (40K+ YouTube videos, 168M Twitter posts) and the joint content-behavior benchmark are valuable resources for a nascent research direction. Key limitations include the 13B model scale, reliance on simplified behavior proxies (replay values, binary high/low likes), and the explicitly preliminary nature of the experiments; open questions include whether the approach scales to more nuanced behavioral signals, whether true content optimization (generating content that maximizes a target behavior) is achievable, and whether the domain-adaptation gains hold with larger, more diverse behavior corpora.
