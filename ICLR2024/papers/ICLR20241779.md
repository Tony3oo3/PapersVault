## Problem

The only existing zeroth-order hard-thresholding algorithm (SZOHT) imposes strict lower bounds on the number of random directions q needed for gradient estimation, arising from an inherent conflict between ZO gradient deviation and the non-expansiveness of the hard-thresholding operator. This makes SZOHT impractical for many real-world problems where q must be small. The paper asks whether a ZO hard-thresholding algorithm can converge without such restrictions on q.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241779/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only black-box function evaluations f_i(θ) (no gradient access), a sparsity level k, a smoothing radius μ, and q random unit vectors with support size s₂ for the ZO gradient estimator. It outputs a k-sparse solution vector θ* minimizing the empirical risk under the ℓ₀ constraint. In the adversarial attack setting, the output is a sparse perturbation θ ∈ ℝ^d (with ‖θ‖₀ ≤ k) applied to 32×32×3 CIFAR-10 images to flip predicted classes, evaluated via the Carlini-Wagner loss.

## Method

The key insight is that the conflict between ZO gradient error and hard-thresholding expansivity is fully encapsulated in the gradient-squared term of the convergence inequality, which for unbiased estimators reduces to the variance of the gradient estimate. The paper introduces pM-SZHT, a generalized p-Memorization framework that maintains a table of historical ZO gradient estimates and updates a random subset of p entries per iteration, yielding a variance-reduced gradient estimate ĝ^(r) = ∇̂f_{i_r}(θ) − â_{i_r} + (1/n)Σâ_j. This eliminates the dependence of convergence on q: under RSC/RSS assumptions, the algorithm converges for any q > 0 (though q still affects speed). Specific instantiations include VR-SZHT (SVRG-style with inner loop of m iterations), SAGA-SZHT, and SARAH-SZHT, achieving ZO query complexity O([n + κ³/(κ²+1)] log(1/ε)) versus O((k + d/s₂)κ² log(1/ε)) for SZOHT.

## Results

On the ridge regression task (n=10, d=5, k=3, q=200), all variance-reduced methods (VR-SZHT, SAGA-SZHT, SARAH-SZHT) converge to lower function values than SZOHT within 80,000 IZO calls, while remaining cheaper than the full-gradient FGZOHT. On the few-pixel universal adversarial attack task (CIFAR-10, 10 airplane images, k=60, q=10, 600 IZO budget), SARAH-SZHT achieves the highest attack success rate at 7/10 images, and SAGA-SZHT attains the lowest final Carlini-Wagner loss. Theoretically, VR-SZHT reduces the hard-thresholding query complexity from O(κ² log(1/ε)) (SZOHT) to O(log(1/ε)), and the ZO query complexity from O((k + d/s₂)κ² log(1/ε)) to O([n + κ³/(κ²+1)] log(1/ε)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SZOHT (de Vazelhes et al., 2022), the only prior zeroth-order hard-thresholding algorithm. On the ridge regression task, all three variance-reduced variants (VR-SZHT, SAGA-SZHT, SARAH-SZHT) outperform SZOHT in final function value within the same IZO budget, while remaining substantially cheaper than the full-gradient FGZOHT baseline. On the CIFAR-10 adversarial attack task, SARAH-SZHT achieves the highest attack success rate (7/10 vs. 4/10 for SZOHT), and SAGA-SZHT reaches the lowest final Carlini-Wagner loss. The key theoretical advantage is that variance reduction removes SZOHT's restrictive lower bound on the number of random directions q (which required q ≥ O(dκ²) for s₂ > 1), making the algorithms applicable with any q > 0, though q still influences convergence speed. FGZOHT, while achieving competitive function values on ridge regression, requires full gradient evaluations and is infeasible in true black-box settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SZOHT (de Vazelhes et al., 2022), the sole prior algorithm for ℓ₀-constrained optimization with zeroth-order gradients, by identifying that SZOHT's restrictive lower bound on the number of random directions q arises from an incomplete variance reduction strategy. The authors reframe the conflict between ZO gradient deviation and hard-thresholding expansivity as fundamentally a variance problem, then apply the p-Memorization framework (Hofmann et al., 2015) to incorporate historical gradient information, yielding a unified pM-SZHT framework that subsumes SVRG-, SAGA-, and SARAH-style variance reduction in the ZO hard-thresholding setting. The analysis builds on the restricted strong convexity/smoothness assumptions standard in sparse learning (Nguyen et al., 2017b; Shen and Li, 2017) and the ZO gradient estimator of de Vazelhes et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is eliminating the restrictive q lower bound that made SZOHT difficult to deploy, broadening the applicability of ZO hard-thresholding to settings where many random directions are infeasible. However, the paper acknowledges that variance reduction mitigates rather than fully resolves the conflict—q still influences convergence speed—and the theoretical guarantees rely on RSC/RSS assumptions that may not hold in all sparse learning regimes. Empirical validation is limited to small-scale problems (n=10, d=5 ridge regression; 10 CIFAR-10 images), leaving open whether the theoretical advantages persist at scale or in more complex black-box attack scenarios. A natural open question is whether the pM-SZHT framework can be combined with adaptive step-size schemes or momentum to further close the gap to full-gradient methods.
