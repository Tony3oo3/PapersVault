## Problem

Posterior collapse in VAEs—where the learned posterior converges to the prior, rendering latent variables uninformative—has been theoretically characterized only for the simplest linear standard VAE with a single latent. The causes and precise conditions for collapse in two widely used but less-studied architectures, conditional VAE (CVAE) and Markovian hierarchical VAE (MHVAE), remain largely unexplored, leaving practitioners without principled guidance on when and why collapse occurs in these more expressive models.

## Input / Output

For CVAE experiments, the input is a partial MNIST digit (the bottom-left 14×14 quadrant, 196-dim) serving as the conditioning variable x, and the model reconstructs the remaining three quadrants (588-dim) as output y through a 16-dim latent z. For MHVAE experiments, the full 784-dim MNIST image or 3072-dim CIFAR-10 image is encoded through two sequential latent layers (each 16- or 64-dim) and decoded back to the original image. The paper's analytical output is a set of closed-form singular values and rank conditions for the encoder/decoder matrices that precisely determine how many latent dimensions collapse, which is then validated against nonlinear ReLU, CNN, and ResNet-18 variants.

## Method

The paper derives the global minima of the negative ELBO for linear CVAE and linear two-latent MHVAE by reducing the matrix optimization to singular-value relations via SVD decompositions of data second-moment matrices. For CVAE, the key finding is that the rank of the decoder map U₁—and hence the collapse level—is governed by the singular values of a matrix E that encodes the correlation between input condition x and output y: higher correlation forces more latent dimensions to collapse. For MHVAE, the analysis reveals that a learnable encoder variance at the second latent level, combined with the KL-regularization between the two latents (the 'consistency term'), drives collapse of z₂, while an unlearnable isotropic variance at z₁ can prevent collapse even under low-rank encoders. The paper further proves that learnable encoder variance is a necessary cause of collapse in the standard VAE setting, contradicting a prior claim by Wang & Ziyin (2022). Extensive nonlinear experiments with ReLU MLPs, CNNs, and ResNet-18 on MNIST and CIFAR-10 confirm that the linear-theory predictions (e.g., smaller β₂ or η_dec mitigates collapse, larger β₁ helps in MHVAE, decorrelating x and y reduces CVAE collapse) transfer to practical architectures.

## Results

The paper's headline empirical finding is that fixing the encoder variance (unlearnable Σ=I) yields 100% active units (AU) and a test log-likelihood of −743.63 on MNIST, compared to only 69% AU and −744.99 log-likelihood with a learnable Σ, directly confirming the theoretical prediction that learnable encoder variance is a cause of posterior collapse. For ReLU CVAE, decreasing β from 3.0 to 0.25 increases AU from 25% to 56%, and decreasing η_dec from 2.0 to 0.25 increases AU from 0% to 50%. For two-latent ReLU MHVAE, increasing β1 from 0.25 to 3.0 raises AU from 34% to 100%, while increasing β2 from 0.25 to 3.0 drops AU from 100% to 40%, matching Theorem 3's predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without traditional method-vs-method baselines; instead, it validates its theorems by comparing hyperparameter configurations. The key internal comparison is learnable versus unlearnable encoder variance: the unlearnable setting achieves 100% AU (all 16 latent dimensions active) versus 69% for the learnable setting, with a marginally better log-likelihood (−743.63 vs −744.99), confirming that learnable variance is a causal factor in collapse. For CVAE, the paper shows that the correlation between input condition x and output y is the decisive factor: when x=y (perfect correlation), AU drops to 0.1% with KL≈0.01, whereas independent x yields 64.7% AU. For MHVAE, the separate β1 and β2 hyperparameters have opposite effects: larger β1 mitigates collapse of the second latent (100% AU at β1=3.0) while larger β2 induces it (40% AU at β2=3.0), a non-intuitive result confirmed both theoretically (Theorem 3) and empirically on MNIST and CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear VAE posterior collapse analysis of Lucas et al. (2019) and Wang & Ziyin (2022), which were restricted to the simplest standard VAE with a single latent variable, to two practically important architectures: Conditional VAE (Sohn et al., 2015) and Markovian Hierarchical VAE (Luo, 2022). It also revisits the standard VAE case to correct a claim in Wang & Ziyin (2022) that learnable encoder variance is not a cause of posterior collapse, showing instead that it is a necessary condition for collapse in the low-rank encoder regime. The β-VAE parameterization (Higgins et al., 2016) provides the unified training objective across all three model families analyzed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first closed-form characterization of posterior collapse conditions for CVAE and MHVAE, identifying concrete causes (input-output correlation in CVAE; learnable encoder variance and the β₁/β₂ ratio in MHVAE) that translate directly into actionable hyperparameter guidance. A key limitation is that all theoretical results are for linear models, and the nonlinear validation, while extensive, remains empirical rather than rigorous. Open questions include the case where both encoder variances are learnable in MHVAE, the role of training dynamics in reaching the characterized global minima, and whether the correlation-driven collapse mechanism generalizes to deeper hierarchical or diffusion-model settings.
