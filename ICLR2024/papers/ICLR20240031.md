## Problem

Language models underperform diffusion models on image and video generation benchmarks, with the best LM trailing diffusion by 48% FID on ImageNet. The paper identifies the visual tokenizer as the primary bottleneck: existing VQ-VAE-based tokenizers are limited to small vocabularies (1–8k) because enlarging the codebook improves reconstruction but degrades LM generation quality. This paper demonstrates that a well-designed tokenizer can close and reverse this gap, providing the first evidence that an LM beats diffusion on ImageNet under matched data, model size, and training budget.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer accepts 17-frame video clips at 128×128 or single RGB images and produces a compact discrete token grid of 5×16×16 (video) or 16×16 (image) drawn from a shared vocabulary of 2^18 ≈ 262K tokens. These tokens are flattened via raster scan and fed into a ~307M-parameter masked language model transformer, which generates new token sequences decoded back to pixels at 256×256 or 512×512 for images, or 17×128×128 clips for video. For compression, the token sequence itself serves as the compressed representation; for action recognition, tokens act as either prediction targets (BEVT-style) or detokenized inputs to a frozen ViViT backbone.

## Method

The core contribution is Lookup-Free Quantization (LFQ), which eliminates the VQ-VAE codebook embedding entirely by reducing the quantization dimension to zero: the latent space is decomposed into log₂K independent binary dimensions, and each dimension is quantized via a simple sign function, yielding a token index through binary-to-integer conversion. An entropy penalty encourages uniform codebook utilization, and this design allows the vocabulary to scale to 2^18 while both reconstruction and LM generation quality improve monotonically—unlike standard VQ where large vocabularies hurt generation. The encoder uses a temporally causal 3D CNN (padding only before, not after, in the time axis) so the first frame is frame-independent, enabling a single shared vocabulary for both images and videos. Additional architectural changes include strided convolutions replacing average pooling, depth-to-space upsampling, deferred temporal downsampling to later encoder blocks, 3D blur pooling in the discriminator, and adaptive group normalization in the decoder. For efficient LM prediction over the large vocabulary, tokens are factorized into two 2^9 subspaces with separate prediction heads and weight tying between embedding and softmax layers.

## Results

On ImageNet 512×512 class-conditional generation, MAGVIT-v2 (MLM+LFQ, 307M params, 64 steps) achieves FID 1.91 with classifier-free guidance, outperforming the best diffusion model VDM++ (FID 2.65, 2B params, 512 steps) by 28%. On Kinetics-600 frame prediction, it achieves FVD 5.2 (12 steps) vs. MAGVIT's 9.9 and Video Diffusion's 16.2 (256 steps). For video compression at 0.0384 bpp, MAGVIT-v2 attains LPIPS 0.104, surpassing VVC (0.153) and HEVC (0.199). On action recognition, it reaches 75.34% on SSv2 and 77.93% on K400, exceeding MAGVIT (72.29% and 74.65% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGVIT-v2 decisively outperforms all diffusion-based baselines on ImageNet 512×512: with guidance, its FID of 1.91 beats VDM++ (2.65), simple diffusion (3.02), DiT-XL/2 (3.04), and ADM+Upsample (3.85), while using only 307M parameters and 64 sampling steps versus 2B parameters and 512 steps for the strongest diffusion models. Among prior MLM+VQ methods, it surpasses MaskGIT (FID 7.32), DPC+Upsample (3.62), and Contextual RQ-Transformer (3.41 at 256×256) by a wide margin. In video generation, MAGVIT-v2 halves the FVD of MAGVIT on Kinetics-600 (5.2 vs. 9.9) and UCF-101 (58 vs. 76), and substantially outperforms Video Diffusion (16.2), RIN (10.8), Phenaki (36.4), and TATS (332). For video compression, it achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.104) among all tested methods including VVC (0.153) and HEVC (0.199), and user studies confirm perceptual preference over both codecs. On action recognition, it improves over MAGVIT by 3.05 points on SSv2 (75.34% vs. 72.29%) and 3.28 points on K400 (77.93% vs. 74.65%), approaching raw-pixel performance (76.13% and 78.92%) while operating on discrete tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MAGVIT (Yu et al., 2023a), the prior state-of-the-art video tokenizer built on the VQ-VAE framework, by introducing two key departures: (1) Lookup-Free Quantization (LFQ), which eliminates the learned d-dimensional codebook entirely and replaces it with independent binary sign-based quantization, enabling a 262K vocabulary without the generation-quality degradation that plagues standard VQ at large codebook sizes; and (2) a temporally causal 3D CNN architecture that allows a single tokenizer to handle both images and videos with a shared vocabulary. The paper also draws on StyleGAN's adaptive normalization for the decoder and BEVT-style pre-training for the video understanding experiments, but its core conceptual contribution is the argument that the visual tokenizer—not the LM architecture—is the primary bottleneck limiting language models in visual generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first credible demonstration that a masked language model can outperform the best diffusion models on ImageNet class-conditional generation under matched data and comparable compute, challenging the prevailing narrative that diffusion is inherently superior for visual synthesis. The LFQ technique is a clean, minimal architectural change (replacing a 256-dim codebook lookup with a sign function) that is likely to be widely adopted in future visual tokenizers. Key limitations temper the impact: the generation comparison is on class-conditional benchmarks rather than the more practically relevant text-to-image/video setting, the compression evaluation uses only 30 videos, and the action recognition results still trail raw-pixel baselines. Open questions include whether LFQ scales to text-conditioned generation, whether the compression advantage holds at higher resolutions and longer sequences, and whether the tokenizer's benefits transfer to autoregressive LMs at scale.
