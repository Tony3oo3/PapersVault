## Problem

Existing controlled text generation (CTG) techniques rely on ad-hoc exponential weighting (P(a|text)^λ) that is difficult to interpret, cannot naturally combine multiple attributes with relative strengths, and often requires custom training data for each attribute. There is no principled, formula-based framework that allows precise, composable control over multiple model attributes while remaining efficient at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241375/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of autoregressive language models (each prompted or fine-tuned for a specific attribute), optional binary classifiers, and scalar weights λ that define a formula in logit space. It produces a composite token probability distribution P(x_k | x_{1:k-1}) sampled autoregressively, yielding controlled text. Classifiers are restricted to top-k evaluation (typically k=10 most likely tokens) because running them over the full vocabulary is computationally infeasible, and the union operator applies a max over log-probabilities rather than a linear combination, enabling selective biasing of only tokens where the target distribution exceeds the base model.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 1) shows that minimizing a linear combination of weighted KL-divergences D_KL^{[f_i]}(P||Q_i) yields a closed-form solution: P(x) ∝ σ(Σ f_i(x) log Q_i(x) / Σ f_i(x)), i.e., a weighted average in logit space. This gives rise to three operators: linear combination (weighted logit averaging), classifier guidance (equivalent to minimizing expected cross-entropy, expressible as D_KL(P||Q_C) − D_KL(P||U)), and a novel union operator that applies max(log Q_1, log Q_2) per token, enabling non-linear composition and selective negative biasing via Q_1 − λ·union(Q_1, Q_2). To counteract the inference overhead of evaluating multiple models, the paper extends speculative sampling by using partial sub-formulas as proposal models validated against the full formula, with per-term speculative factors s_i that control how many tokens are proposed before each expensive model call, reducing total model invocations by up to 64%.

## Results

On the /pol/ toxicity reduction task, the combined formula M−0.99·union(M_toxic, M)+0.01C achieves the lowest toxicity scores across all three models: 0.162 (Llama-2-13b), 0.150 (Pythia-12b), and 0.174 (MPT-7b), while maintaining perplexity at or below the unconditioned baseline. The union operator alone (M−0.96·union(M_toxic, M)) already outperforms PREADD at a much higher biasing strength (0.96 vs. 0.6) with lower perplexity (e.g., 11.08 vs. 12.73 on Llama-2-13b). GPT-4 preference analysis confirms the advantage, with the combined formula winning 43% of head-to-head comparisons against PREADD on Llama-2-13b. Speculative sampling reduces model calls from 4.00 to as low as 1.32 per token, yielding up to 2.24× inference speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three CTG baselines: PREADD (Pei et al., 2023), FUDGE (Yang and Klein, 2021), and SelfDebias (Schick et al., 2021). The proposed union operator significantly outperforms all three on toxicity reduction across all three model families (Llama-2-13b, Pythia-12b, MPT-7b). Critically, the union operator achieves lower toxicity than PREADD at a much higher biasing strength (0.96 vs. 0.6) while simultaneously maintaining lower perplexity (e.g., 11.08 vs. 12.73 on Llama-2-13b), demonstrating that the union operator selectively biases only relevant tokens without degrading fluency. The combined formula (union + classifier) achieves the best toxicity scores overall (0.162/0.150/0.174) with perplexity comparable to or below the unconditioned model. In GPT-4 head-to-head preference against PREADD, the combined formula wins 43% vs. 35% on Llama-2-13b and 42% vs. 34% on MPT-7b. On the sentiment control task (IMDB), the same pattern holds: the combined formula achieves the highest sentiment scores (e.g., 0.466 positive sentiment on Llama-2-13b) with lower perplexity than PREADD, and GPT-4 prefers it over PREADD by an average 12% margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a fragmented landscape of controlled text generation (CTG) techniques—FUDGE, PREADD, SelfDebias, classifier-free guidance, DExperts—under a single principled framework: minimizing a linear combination of weighted KL-divergences. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods introduce a strength parameter λ in an ad-hoc exponential form with unclear Bayesian semantics, whereas here λ corresponds to a transparent linear operator in logit space, making composition of multiple attributes interpretable and composable. The novel union operator (a max-in-logit-space combination) has no direct analogue in prior CTG work and enables selective biasing that linear combinations cannot achieve. The speculative sampling extension generalizes Chen et al. (2023) from a single small/large model pair to arbitrary multi-term formulas, deferring expensive model evaluations until validation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives practitioners a single, interpretable language for composing multiple LLMs and classifiers with precise, tunable attribute strengths—something that was previously either impossible (multi-attribute with relative weights) or required ad-hoc engineering. The union operator's ability to apply 60% stronger negative biasing than PREADD without perplexity degradation is a practically significant finding for safety-critical applications like toxicity reduction. Limitations include the top-k approximation required for classifier terms, the inference-time-only nature (no training), and dependence on the quality of prompted sub-models. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-autoregressive or diffusion-based generators, how it behaves with very large formulas (many terms), and whether the linear logit-space semantics hold for non-prompted (fine-tuned) sub-models at scale.
