## Problem

Existing molecular representation learning (MRL) models typically encode a single 2D graph or one 3D conformer, ignoring the fact that molecules continuously interconvert across conformations via bond rotations and vibrations. While a few recent studies explore learning from conformer ensembles, they are confined to small datasets, limited tasks, and restricted model architectures, leaving it unclear how much 2D models implicitly capture flexibility and whether explicit ensemble encoding improves 3D models across diverse chemical spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241163/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume 2D molecular graphs (with OGB-style atom/bond features), 3D conformer coordinates (single lowest-energy conformer or a set of up to 20 conformers per molecule), or SMILES strings (BPE-tokenized). The regression targets are Boltzmann-averaged properties computed over the full conformer ensemble at DFT level: ionization potential, electron affinity, electronegativity (Drugs-75K), four Sterimol steric descriptors (Kraken), enantiomeric excess from competing pro-R/pro-S transition-state ensembles (EE), and electronic binding energy (BDE). Notably, BDE conformers are force-field-level (Open Babel) rather than DFT-optimized, simulating a realistic setting where precise conformers are unavailable at inference.

## Method

MARCEL is a benchmark suite that evaluates 13 representative models (1D fingerprints/LSTM/Transformer, 2D GIN/GIN-VN/ChemProp/GraphGPS, 3D SchNet/DimeNet++/GemNet/PaiNN/ClofNet/LEFTNet) across four datasets spanning drug-like molecules, organophosphorus ligands, and organometallic catalysts. The key technical contribution is two conformer ensemble learning strategies applied to 3D models: (1) training-time data augmentation that randomly samples one conformer per epoch with uniform probability while keeping the same Boltzmann-averaged label, and (2) an explicit multi-instance set encoder that first produces per-conformer embeddings via a frozen or jointly-trained 3D GNN, then aggregates them using mean pooling, DeepSets (MLP + sum + MLP), or self-attention over the conformer set. For reaction datasets (EE, BDE), a two-tower architecture encodes each molecular component separately before concatenation. Across 54 experiments (9 tasks × 6 base 3D models), ensemble strategies improve over single-conformer 3D baselines in 48 cases, with DeepSets showing the most consistent gains, particularly on smaller datasets like Kraken.

## Results

Across 9 regression tasks on 4 datasets, conformer ensemble learning strategies (data augmentation via sampling and explicit set encoders) improve upon single-conformer 3D baselines in 48 out of 54 experiments. DeepSets as a set encoder yields the most consistent gains, improving in 42/54 experiments, with the largest relative improvements on the small Kraken dataset (e.g., BurB5 MAE drops from 0.1782 for GemNet to 0.1589 with ensemble). On the reaction-centric EE task, 3D models dramatically outperform 1D/2D baselines (best 3D ensemble MAE ≈ 11.6 vs. best 2D ≈ 61.6), while on the large Drugs-75K dataset, 2D models like GraphGPS (χ MAE 0.2212) remain competitive with 3D models. The best single-task results include GemNet+ensemble on Kraken BurB5 (0.1589) and DimeNet+++ensemble on BDE (1.4741).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 1D baselines, LSTM outperforms Random Forest and Transformer on Drugs-75K (IP: 0.4788 vs. 0.4987/0.6617) and BDE (2.8279 vs. 3.0335/10.0771), while Random Forest dominates on small datasets like Kraken (BurL: 0.1521) and EE (61.30). Among 2D GNNs, GIN achieves the best results on four tasks (IP, EA, χ, BurL on Kraken), and GraphGPS is competitive on BDE (2.4827) and Kraken B₅ (0.3450); however, all 2D models are substantially worse than 3D models on reaction tasks (EE: best 2D ≈ 61.6 vs. best 3D ≈ 14.6). Among 3D GNNs, GemNet and LEFTNet lead on Drugs-75K (IP: 0.4069/0.4174), while DimeNet++ excels on Kraken and BDE (BDE: 1.4503; Kraken BurL: 0.1526); ClofNet and PaiNN consistently underperform. The conformer ensemble strategies (DeepSets, Attention, Mean pooling) improve over their single-conformer 3D counterparts in 48/54 experiments, with the largest gains on Kraken (e.g., GemNet BurB5: 0.1782→0.1589) and EE (GemNet: 18.03→11.61), but provide minimal or no benefit on the large Drugs-75K dataset where computational overhead hinders training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of conformer ensemble learning initiated by Axelrod & Gómez-Bombarelli [23], Zahrt et al. [22], and Weinreich et al. [24], which demonstrated that aggregating information across multiple conformer structures can improve property prediction. However, those prior studies were confined to small-scale datasets, a limited set of tasks, and restricted model architectures. MARCEL departs from this by providing the first systematic benchmark spanning four chemically diverse datasets (including organocatalysts and transition-metal catalysts beyond drug-like molecules), nine regression tasks, and thirteen representative 1D/2D/3D models. The two ensemble strategies—training-time conformer sampling and explicit set encoders (DeepSets, mean pooling, self-attention)—draw directly on the multi-instance learning literature [50–53] and prior work on conformational invariance [19].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARCEL provides the field with a standardized, reproducible benchmark for evaluating conformer ensemble learning, extending chemical coverage to catalysts and reaction properties (enantiomeric excess, binding energy) that are poorly represented in existing GNN benchmarks. The finding that ensemble strategies improve 48 of 54 experiments yet fail on larger datasets like Drugs-75K highlights an open tension between model capacity and the computational burden of encoding full ensembles. Key open questions include developing more efficient set encoders that outperform the surprisingly consistent DeepSets, exploring physics-informed (Boltzmann-weighted) sampling strategies, and extending the benchmark to classification tasks and broader chemical space. The BDE result—that conformer sampling helps most when conformers are imprecise—suggests a practical pathway for robustness in settings where DFT-optimized structures are unavailable.
