## Problem

Maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) for autoregressive language modeling minimizes forward cross-entropy, which suffers from three structural deficiencies: recall-prioritization (only incentivizing high probability on the ground-truth token), negative diversity ignorance (treating all non-ground-truth tokens as equally incorrect), and train-test mismatch (optimizing an expectation over the data distribution P while evaluation samples from the model distribution Q_θ). These limitations cause degeneration phenomena and suboptimal distributional alignment even with large training corpora.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the method consumes the preceding token sequence x_{<t} as context and produces a next-token probability distribution Q_θ(·|x_{<t}) over the full vocabulary. The transport cost matrix C is derived from the language modeling head embeddings (cosine distances between token embedding vectors), which are initialized from a pre-trained MLE model and jointly updated during fine-tuning. The final loss is a dynamically weighted combination of MLE and the DEMD term, requiring no tunable hyperparameters.

## Method

EMO replaces the forward cross-entropy objective with a differentiable upper bound (DEMD) of the Earth Mover's Distance between the model distribution Q_θ(·|x_{<t}) and the data distribution P(·|x_{<t}) at each token position. The key insight is constructing a valid but suboptimal transport plan γ̃(v_i, v_j) = Q_θ(v_i)·P(v_j) that avoids the O(|V|³ log|V|) complexity of exact EMD solvers and preserves gradient flow, yielding the closed-form upper bound Q_θ^T C P = 1 − (Ê Q_θ)^T Ê P where Ê is the row-normalized LM head. The semantically-informed cost C uses cosine distances in the embedding space so that contextually similar tokens (e.g., synonyms) incur lower transport costs. The resulting gradient updates all vocabulary tokens weighted by their expected transport cost to the data distribution, simultaneously addressing recall-prioritization, negative diversity ignorance, and train-test mismatch without requiring external solvers or adversarial training.

## Results

On language modeling (Mauve score, unbiased sampling), EMO consistently outperforms MLE across all six domains and both model architectures (GPT-2, OPT-125M), achieving an average improvement of 6.2 points. For GPT-2, EMO reaches 87.5 on WikiText-2 and 87.4 on WritingPrompts, compared to MLE's 77.5 and 83.6. On downstream NLU tasks (8 datasets, in-context learning), EMO fine-tuning of LLaMa-7B/13B yields an average improvement of ~4 points over the pre-trained model, with the largest gains on Tweet Emotion (65.6 vs. 54.1 for 7B) and AG News (89.4 vs. 85.6 for 7B). EMO also demonstrates superior scaling: with only 4M fine-tuning tokens it matches the performance of MixCE trained on 100M tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMO is compared against two baselines that also modify the MLE objective: TaiLr (Ji et al., 2022), which optimizes a token-level factorization of total variation distance, and MixCE (Zhang et al., 2023), which interpolates forward and reverse cross-entropy. EMO decisively outperforms both across all six language modeling datasets and both model architectures. For GPT-2, EMO leads TaiLr by 8.0 points on WikiText-2 (87.5 vs. 79.6) and MixCE by 9.2 points (87.5 vs. 78.3); for OPT-125M, the margins are 4.5 points over TaiLr and 4.3 points over MixCE on WikiText-2. On downstream NLU tasks (LLaMa-7B), EMO achieves 65.6 on Tweet Emotion vs. 56.2 (TaiLr) and 60.0 (MixCE), and 89.4 on AG News vs. 86.3 (TaiLr) and 87.5 (MixCE). The paper attributes the baselines' limited gains to their degeneration into regularized variants of forward cross-entropy, meaning they still suffer from recall-prioritization and negative diversity ignorance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Earth Mover's Distance from optimal transport (Kantorovich, 1960) and its use in implicit generative modeling (WGAN, Arjovsky et al. 2017) to the discrete token-level setting of auto-regressive language modeling. It departs from prior EMD-based generative approaches by avoiding adversarial training and Sinkhorn iterations, instead deriving a simple differentiable upper bound (DEMD) that is jointly optimized with MLE in an end-to-end fashion. The semantically-informed transport cost, initialized from the pre-trained LM head's embedding matrix, distinguishes EMO from generic metric-space EMD formulations. Conceptually, EMO also extends the recall-precision diagnostic framework from generative model evaluation (Lucic et al. 2018; Sajjadi et al. 2018) into the training objective itself, rather than using it only post-hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EMO offers a hyperparameter-free, drop-in replacement for MLE that addresses three concrete pathologies (recall-prioritization, negative diversity ignorance, train-test mismatch) with a single additional loss term, making it attractive as a lightweight calibration step for large pre-trained LLMs. The finding that ~25K sentences of EMO fine-tuning yields ~4-point average gains across 8 NLU tasks suggests a practical path to improving LLM quality without the compute cost of full re-pretraining. Open questions include whether the semantically-informed cosine cost is optimal versus learned or graph-based costs, how EMO interacts with RLHF/DPO alignment pipelines at scale, and whether the approach extends naturally to encoder-decoder or diffusion-based language models.
