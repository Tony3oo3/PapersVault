## Problem

Most GNNs operate as low-pass filters, capturing only smooth low-resolution graph signals while missing high-frequency local disruptions and multi-scale community structures. Existing multiresolution graph methods rely on fixed, handcrafted spectral filters or downsampling strategies that cannot adapt to the distinct frequency characteristics of individual graph instances, limiting their effectiveness on diverse graph distributions (e.g., assortative vs. disassortative graphs).

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G = (X, A) with node features X ∈ R^{n×d} and adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n}. A meta-framelet learner (a 2-layer GCN) maps the graph instance to a compact 2(I−1)-dimensional parameter vector ω and a scalar resolution factor γ, which together define the adaptive frequency partition. The output is either per-node embeddings (for node classification) or a graph-level readout via MM-FGPool (for graph classification/regression), with the multiresolution transform matrix Φ_MM having dimensions n × R(I−1)n, decomposing the signal into R resolution levels.

## Method

The core contribution is the Multiresolution Meta-Framelet System (MMFS), which constructs learnable multiresolution bases from a small set of meta-band-pass filters (polynomial splines with learnable cutoff positions c_i and margins ε_i) parameterized by only 2(I−1) values. A key theoretical result (Proposition 2) shows that if the first-resolution filters form a unitary partition of [0,1], the iteratively constructed higher-resolution filters automatically yield a tight framelet system satisfying denseness, dilation, and translation properties, enabling exact multiresolution decomposition. The meta-framelet learner M_ξ (a 2-layer GCN) generates graph-instance-specific filter parameters, making the spectral partition adaptive per input graph. Training follows a MAML-style bi-level optimization: meta-parameters ξ are updated on a meta-training batch, then all parameters (θ, w, ξ) are jointly updated on a separate main batch. Efficient computation avoids full eigendecomposition by applying Chebyshev polynomial approximation (order T) to evaluate g_{r,i}^ω(γ^{−J+r}L) directly on the Laplacian.

## Results

MM-FGCN achieves state-of-the-art test accuracy on all eight node classification benchmarks. On assortative datasets, it reaches 84.4% on Cora, 73.9% on Citeseer, and 80.7% on Pubmed. On disassortative datasets, it achieves 88.9% on Cornell, 86.1% on Texas, 88.5% on Wisconsin, 73.97% on Chameleon, and 67.5% on Squirrel. For graph classification, MM-FGPool attains the best accuracy on all six benchmarks (e.g., 83.91% on Mutagenicity, 81.51% on D&D, 41.19 MSE on QM7). On large-scale OGBN datasets, MM-FGCN reaches 74.25% on OGBN-arxiv and 84.03% on OGBN-product.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-FGCN outperforms all 17 baselines across every dataset. The most dramatic gains appear on disassortative graphs: compared to GCN, MM-FGCN improves by 34.7 points on Cornell (88.9 vs 54.2), 25.0 on Texas (86.1 vs 61.1), and 28.9 on Wisconsin (88.5 vs 59.6), confirming that low-pass-only filters fail when neighboring nodes have dissimilar labels. Among multiresolution competitors, MM-FGCN beats PyGNN by 13.6 points on Cornell (88.9 vs 75.3) and 14.5 on Chameleon (73.97 vs 65.4), and surpasses UFGConv-R by 20.0 on Cornell (88.9 vs 68.9) and 10.9 on Chameleon (73.97 vs 63.1). On assortative datasets, gains over the strongest baselines (GAT, PyGNN, UFGConv-R) are more modest, ranging from 0.3 to 1.1 points (e.g., 84.4 vs 83.6 on Cora). On large-scale OGBN-arxiv, MM-FGCN edges out DRGAT (74.25 vs 74.16) and UniMP (74.25 vs 73.11), while on OGBN-product it leads AGDN by 0.69 points (84.03 vs 83.34). The ablation in Table 3 further shows that the meta-learning training paradigm (vs. direct joint training) adds 1.0–10.7 points, and the learnable meta-framelet generator outperforms fixed Haar/linear/quadratic framelets by 1.6–21.3 points on disassortative datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph framelet transform framework (Dong, 2017) and classical multiresolution analysis (Mallat, 1989) by making the multiresolution basis learnable and instance-adaptive via a meta-learning paradigm borrowed from MAML (Finn et al., 2017). Prior multiresolution GNNs (UFGConv, WFTG, PyGNN, MR-GNN) all rely on fixed, handcrafted spectral filters or downsampling strategies that remain static across all graph instances. The key conceptual departure is replacing these fixed transforms with a meta-framelet generator whose band-pass filter parameters are produced by a per-graph neural network, enabling the frequency partition to adapt to each graph's spectral structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation in spectral GNNs: fixed multiresolution transforms cannot simultaneously capture the low-frequency smoothness needed for assortative graphs and the high-frequency disruption needed for disassortative graphs. The theoretical guarantees (tightness, denseness, dilation, translation) provide a clean mathematical foundation, and the meta-learning training paradigm offers a principled way to avoid overfitting the transform to a single batch. Open questions include whether the meta-learning overhead (bi-level optimization, doubled data splits) is justified over simpler per-instance adaptation, how the method scales to graphs with millions of nodes beyond the OGBN experiments, and whether the Chebyshev approximation introduces systematic bias for very high-frequency components.
