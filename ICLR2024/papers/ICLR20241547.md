## Problem

Autoregressive language models lack explicit, interpretable sentence representations: their internal state is distributed across layers and attention heads, making it difficult to extract a canonical meaning vector without fine-tuning or prompt engineering. Existing zero-shot approaches (token pooling, conditional likelihood) are either semantically incomplete or unreliable for comparing different sentences, and prompt-based methods introduce language-dependent biases that do not scale with model size. The paper asks whether the model's own distribution over text continuations can serve as a faithful, prompt-free meaning representation that also supports compositional operations for asymmetric relations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text string (or an image for multimodal models like LLaVA) as a prompt and produces a meaning representation defined as a function M_s: A* → [0,1] mapping every possible continuation to an inverse-perplexity score. In practice, this infinite-dimensional object is approximated by sampling n=20 trajectories of up to m=20 tokens each via multinomial sampling at temperature 1.0. From two such representations, the method outputs a scalar semantic similarity score (expected absolute log-likelihood difference), a binary entailment direction, or a binary hyponym/hypernym direction. For word-level relations, contexts are retrieved from the WikiText corpus rather than sampled forward, since LLMs cannot sample backward.

## Method

The core representation identifies the meaning of a prompt s with the function M_s(t) = ∏ P_M(a_i | s, a_{<i})^{1/m} over all continuations t, i.e., the model's own likelihood distribution over future text. Semantic distance between two prompts u and v is computed as the expected absolute difference in log-likelihoods, E_{t ~ ½(M_u+M_v)} |log M_u(t) − log M_v(t)|, approximated by Monte Carlo over trajectories sampled from both prompts. The key structural advantage is that the meaning space [0,1]^{A*} carries a natural partial order (pointwise ≤) with meet (min) and join (max) operations, enabling the Entailment Test: u entails v if d(M_u ∧ M_v, M_v) < d(M_u ∧ M_v, M_u), and an analogous Hyponym Test for word-level containment. This requires no fine-tuning, no prompts, and applies identically to any autoregressive model or multimodal autoregressive model that assigns likelihoods to token sequences.

## Results

On the STS benchmark suite (7 datasets), the method applied to LLaMA-33B achieves 66.6 average Spearman correlation, outperforming the best zero-shot encoder (ST5-Enc-mean 11B, 58.0) by 14.8% relative margin and all autoregressive baselines (best: Cross Encoder at 47.5) by over 38% relative improvement. Applied to Falcon-7B, the method reaches 65.7 average, approaching the contrastive-trained CLIP paragon (67.0) without any fine-tuning or prompting. On multimodal Crisscrossed Captions, the method applied to LLaVA achieves 81.47 on image-image similarity and 67.63 on image-text similarity (with alignment prompt), outperforming CLIP-ViT-L/14 (66.33 and 64.25 respectively) by 13.3% on SITS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all autoregressive baselines on Falcon-7B: Cross Encoder (Muennighoff, 2022) achieves 47.5 avg vs. 65.7 for Ours (+38.3% relative), Joint Likelihood scores 33.5, and Last/Mean Token baselines score 22.4/24.5. Against zero-shot encoder models, Ours (Falcon-7B) surpasses ST5-Enc-mean 11B (58.0) by 13.3% and BERT-mean (54.8) by 20%, while Ours (LLaMA-33B) extends this to 14.8% over ST5. The method approaches but does not match contrastive-trained paragons: SimCSE-BERT (76.3) and CLIP (67.0) remain ahead, though the gap to CLIP narrows to 0.4 points with LLaMA-33B. On multimodal CxC, Ours (LLaVA) outperforms CLIP-ViT-L/14 on both image-image (81.47 vs. 66.33) and image-text (67.63 vs. 64.25 with alignment prompt) similarity. Prompt-based methods (PromptEOL, PromptBERT) are shown to be brittle and model-specific, with PromptEOL on LLaMA-13B (65.6 avg) underperforming the prompt-free Ours (65.3) only marginally, while Ours-Prompt-2 reaches 77.8 avg, suggesting prompts can further boost but are not required.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical Distributional Hypothesis (Harris, 1954) and the left-quotient construction from automata theory to define meaning in autoregressive LLMs as the full distribution over continuations, rather than as a fixed-dimensional vector. It departs from encoder-based sentence embeddings (BERT, ST5, Sentence-BERT) and contrastive fine-tuning (SimCSE) by requiring no additional training data or weight modification, and from prompt-based methods (PromptEOL, PromptBERT) by being entirely prompt-free. The closest prior work is Muennighoff's (2022) use of conditional likelihood for semantic search, but this paper shows that pairwise likelihood is insufficient and instead compares full trajectory distributions, enabling compositional (meet/join) operations that vector spaces cannot express. The theoretical framing connects to Bradley et al.'s (2022) functor from syntactic to semantic categories and Soatto et al.'s (2023) equivalence-class definition of meaning, but provides a more general and operationally rich representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a zero-shot, prompt-free, fine-tuning-free method that extracts semantically meaningful representations from any autoregressive model, including multimodal ones, without modifying weights. The ability to define Boolean-like operations (meet/join) on meanings to recover asymmetric relations—entailment direction and hyponym/hypernym—represents a genuine capability that vector-space embeddings lack, opening a path to compositional semantic reasoning directly from pre-trained models. The main limitation is computational cost: each pairwise comparison requires multiple sampling and forward-pass operations, making it impractical for large-scale semantic search without the fixed-trajectory approximation (which incurs a ~35% performance drop). Open questions include whether more sophisticated trajectory selection or amortized scoring can close this efficiency gap, and whether the method's alignment with human semantics continues to improve as models grow beyond 33B parameters.
