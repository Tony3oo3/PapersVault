## Problem

Existing initialization and learning rate scaling methods are largely agnostic to network architecture, treating all layers identically regardless of connectivity patterns, skip connections, or heterogeneous operations. This means NAS benchmarks train all architectures under a single fixed protocol, potentially masking true architectural quality and producing misleading rankings for AutoML algorithms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242007/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a network's directed acyclic graph (DAG) topology—specifying depth, width, in-degrees, and for CNNs the kernel size q—along with a base maximal learning rate obtained by grid search on a minimal L=1 network. It outputs per-layer initialization variances (scaled inversely by in-degree) and per-layer learning rates (scaled by the sum of cubed path depths and kernel size), which are then used to train the full network to convergence.

## Method

The paper derives two architecture-aware scaling principles for general DAG-structured networks. First, a modified fan-in initialization sets each edge's weight variance to C = 2/d_in, where d_in is the target layer's in-degree, provably preserving pre-activation variance across all layers regardless of topology. Second, by applying the μP maximal-update prescription (requiring E[(Δz_i^(ℓ))²] = Θ(1) at the first gradient step), the learning rate for hidden layers scales as η* ∝ (Σ_p L_p³)^(−1/2) for MLPs and η* ∝ (Σ_p L_p³)^(−1/2) · q^(−1) for CNNs, where the sum is over all P end-to-end directed paths and L_p is the ReLU depth of path p. The key analytical insight is that the in-degree-aware initialization collapses the complex DAG backward-pass analysis back to per-path sequential recursions, making the topology dependence tractable. In practice, one only needs to grid-search the learning rate on a tiny base network and then zero-shot transfer to any larger or topologically different architecture.

## Results

The proposed architecture-aware scaling achieves strong correlation with ground-truth maximal learning rates: r=0.838 for MLPs with diverse DAG topologies (Figure 2) and r=0.856 for CNNs with varying kernel sizes and topologies (Figure 3). On NAS-Bench-201, re-scaling learning rates and initializations per architecture yields average test accuracy improvements of +0.44 on CIFAR-10, +1.24 on CIFAR-100, and +2.09 on ImageNet16-120 over the benchmark's default training protocol. Critically, these improvements are non-uniform: top-performing architectures see the largest ranking shifts, demonstrating that current NAS benchmark rankings are fragile and can be broken by better training alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the NAS-Bench-201 default training protocol (Dong & Yang, 2020), the proposed architecture-aware scaling consistently wins: every sampled architecture achieves equal or better test accuracy, with average gains of +0.44 (CIFAR-10), +1.24 (CIFAR-100), and +2.09 (ImageNet16-120). More importantly, the comparison reveals that network rankings—especially among top-performing architectures—are substantially reshaped, implying that NAS algorithms evaluated on the original benchmark may be mis-ranked. Against μP (Yang et al., 2022), the paper reports superior performance in Appendix E.3, though specific numbers are deferred to the appendix; the key distinction is that μP provides width-dependent scaling only for first/last layers and assumes sequential topologies, whereas the proposed method captures non-trivial depth and DAG-topology dependence. The paper also shows that the performance gap between architectures is mitigated under the proposed scaling (right column of Figure 4), meaning networks become more uniformly well-trained and less distinguishable in accuracy, which has implications for how NAS search spaces should be evaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the μP (maximal update) framework of Yang et al. (2022) and the depth-scaling analysis of Jelassi et al. (2023) from sequential MLPs to arbitrary directed acyclic graph (DAG) topologies, including skip connections and heterogeneous layer types. The initialization scheme builds on the in-degree-aware scaling calculus of Defazio & Bottou (2022), generalizing standard fan-in initialization to account for multiple incoming edges at each vertex. The key conceptual departure from μP is that learning rates here depend non-trivially on both network depth and graph topology (via the sum of cubed path depths), rather than being width-dependent only in the first and last layers as in the original μP prescription.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most impactful finding is that architecture-aware hyperparameters can significantly alter network rankings in NAS-Bench-201, undermining the reliability of existing benchmarks used to compare AutoML algorithms. This raises a practical concern: NAS methods evaluated on these benchmarks may be misranked, and the 'gold standard' comparisons in the literature may be biased toward architectures that happen to work well under a fixed training protocol. Open limitations include the analysis being restricted to the first gradient step (early training), the absence of normalization layers, and the lack of a width-dependent scaling principle, all of which constrain the generality of the derived formulas.
