## Problem

Deep neural networks overfit to label noise, degrading generalization. Two prominent remedies—loss reweighting (reducing noisy examples' gradient contribution) and label correction (replacing noisy labels with estimated ground-truth)—have been pursued separately or combined as independent modules, lacking a unified probabilistic framework that naturally integrates both.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240283/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 Clothing1M/WebVision) paired with potentially noisy class labels. Internally, the network outputs a per-example mean vector in R^{K-1} and a low-rank covariance matrix in the ilr-transformed regression space; at test time, the mean is mapped back to the probability simplex via the inverse ilr transform, and the argmax component yields the predicted class ID. The covariance is discarded at inference, so the EMA network's mean prediction serves as the final classifier.

## Method

The paper adapts the isometric log-ratio (ilr) transform from compositional data analysis to convert one-hot classification labels (after label smoothing to avoid zeros) into unconstrained regression targets in R^{K-1}. In this regression space, a shifted multivariate Gaussian noise model t = μ_θ(x) + ε, ε ~ N(Δ_θ̄(x), Σ_θ(x)) is fitted via maximum likelihood, where the learned per-example covariance Σ_θ naturally down-weights noisy examples (loss reweighting) and the shift Δ_θ̄ = t − μ_θ̄, estimated from an exponentially moving averaged (EMA) network, indirectly replaces the observed label with the EMA's ground-truth estimate (label correction). The shift is introduced gradually during training via a smooth weighting (1−α^e) to avoid relying on the EMA network's poor early predictions. At test time, the inverse ilr transform maps the predicted mean back to the simplex, and the EMA network's prediction is used for improved generalization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 40% symmetric noise, SGN achieves 91.29% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline SOP (88.45%) by 2.8 pp and CE (56.56%) by 34.7 pp. On CIFAR-100 with 40% asymmetric noise, SGN reaches 71.01%, surpassing the next-best ELR (63.80%) by 7.2 pp. On the natural-noise CIFAR-100N dataset, SGN achieves 60.36% versus NAL's 56.33% (+4.0 pp). Notably, SGN also outperforms standard CE on noise-free CIFAR-10 (94.12% vs. 90.67%), attributed to the EMA network's better generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGN consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across the majority of settings. Against the strongest baselines, SGN leads ELR by 3.1 pp on CIFAR-10 40% symmetric noise (91.29 vs. 88.19) and by 7.2 pp on CIFAR-100 40% asymmetric noise (71.01 vs. 63.80). NAL is the closest competitor on CIFAR-10 (86.25% at 40% symmetric) but SGN still leads by 5.0 pp; on CIFAR-100N, SGN beats NAL by 4.0 pp (60.36 vs. 56.33). On Clothing1M, SGN achieves 73.9% top-1, marginally ahead of NAL (73.6%) and SOP (73.5%). On WebVision, NAL reports 74.1% top-1 under a different evaluation protocol (early stopping, single run), while SGN reports 72.72% under the paper's standard protocol (mean over 5 runs at end of training); the authors note this makes the WebVision comparison inconclusive. SGN also surpasses DivideMix and ELR+ on WebVision when augmented with mixup (Table 8), despite using only a single add-on versus their multiple add-ons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the isometric log-ratio (ilr) transform from compositional data analysis (Egozcue et al., 2003) to convert classification into a regression problem, then applies a shifted multivariate Gaussian noise model that unifies two previously separate robustness mechanisms: loss reweighting (via learned per-example covariance, building on Nix & Weigend 1994 and Kendall & Gal 2017) and label correction (via a learned shift Δ estimated from an EMA network). The authors explicitly position their method as a generalization of their prior Logistic-Normal likelihood work (Englesson et al., 2023), which corresponds to the special case of using the asymmetric alr transform without modelling the shift. Unlike prior approaches that combine loss reweighting and label correction as independent modules (e.g., SOP, NAL, ELR), this paper derives both from a single probabilistic regression objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified regression formulation provides a principled probabilistic framework that makes the interaction between loss reweighting and label correction transparent and analyzable, and opens the door to the broader robust regression literature (Huber losses, M-estimators, Gaussian processes) for future robustness improvements. The paper also highlights a promising direction for uncertainty estimation in classification, since the Gaussian likelihood in the ilr space admits conjugate Bayesian inference unlike the standard categorical likelihood. Key limitations include degraded performance under high unstructured (symmetric) noise where the network must memorize noise patterns, and sensitivity to the single hyperparameter α controlling the gradual introduction of the shift. Open questions include whether more sophisticated robust regression losses can further improve performance, and whether the regression view enables meaningful epistemic uncertainty estimates for classification.
