## Problem

Video diffusion models suffer from prohibitive memory and computational costs because they process high-dimensional video cubic arrays directly, while existing latent video diffusion models that reduce dimensionality fail to leverage pretrained image diffusion models, limiting generation quality. No prior method simultaneously achieves the efficiency of latent-space video generation and the visual quality benefits of fine-tuning a pretrained image diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240383/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (or class label) as conditioning and produces a 16-frame video clip at 512×1024 resolution. Internally, the video is decomposed into an image-like content frame (a softmax-weighted sum of all frames, encoded in Stable Diffusion's 4-channel latent space with 8× spatial downsampling) and a low-dimensional triplane motion latent (two 1D tensors z_x ∈ R^{D×L×H'} and z_y ∈ R^{D×L×W'} with D=8). The content frame is generated first by the fine-tuned image diffusion model, then the motion latent is generated by a lightweight DiT conditioned on both the text and the generated content frame, and finally the autoencoder decoder reconstructs the full video.

## Method

CMD is a two-stage framework: (1) a video autoencoder (TimeSFormer backbone) encodes each video into a content frame computed as a softmax-weighted sum of all frames and a triplane motion latent obtained by 2D-averaging the hidden features along spatial axes and projecting through 1×1 convolutions; (2) two diffusion models learn the joint distribution p(x̄, z|c) = p(z|x̄,c)·p(x̄|c). The content frame distribution is modeled by directly fine-tuning a pretrained Stable Diffusion model without adding any new parameters, exploiting the fact that content frames resemble natural images. The motion latent distribution is modeled by a lightweight DiT (L/2 or XL/2) that receives the content frame as input-level patch embeddings (providing dense spatial conditioning) and the text via AdaIN layers, with a larger patch size for the content frame than for the motion target to reduce sequence length. This decomposition avoids processing full video cubic tensors in the diffusion models, yielding ~16.7× fewer FLOPs and ~66% less GPU memory during sampling compared to prior approaches.

## Results

On WebVid-10M text-to-video generation, CMD achieves an FVD of 238.3 (cfg=9.0), which is 18.5% better than the prior state-of-the-art VideoFactory (292.4). On UCF-101 class-conditional generation, CMD attains FVD 107±9 (2,048 samples) and 73±2 (10,000 samples), outperforming all baselines including MAGVIT (76±2). In terms of efficiency, CMD samples a 512×1024, 16-frame video in 3.1 seconds with 5.56 GB GPU memory and 46.83 TFLOPs, compared to 35.1 seconds, 8.50 GB, and 939.0 TFLOPs for ModelScope — a 7.7× speedup in sampling time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMD outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary WebVid-10M FVD metric (238.3 vs. 292.4 for VideoFactory, 414.1 for ModelScope, 455.5 for LVDM) and on UCF-101 non-zero-shot FVD (107±9 vs. 173 for VideoFusion, 305 for CogVideo, 332±18 for TATS, 465±12 for DIGAN). On UCF-101 zero-shot, CMD (FVD 504) beats LVDM (642), ModelScope (640), VideoLDM (551), VideoGen (554), CogVideo (702), and VideoFactory (410), but trails PYoCo (355), which was trained on 22.5M samples versus CMD's 10.7M. On MSR-VTT CLIPSIM, CMD (0.2894) is competitive but slightly behind Make-A-Video (0.3049), VideoFactory (0.3005), ModelScope (0.2930), and VideoLDM (0.2929), though CMD's model is ~1.9× smaller than VideoLDM (1.6B vs. 3.1B parameters) and does not require joint training on 5B image-text pairs as ModelScope does. CMD also surpasses GODIVA (0.2402) and NUWA (0.2409) on MSR-VTT CLIPSIM by a wide margin. Overall, CMD achieves the best FVD across all three evaluation settings while using substantially less compute (46.83 vs. 939.0 TFLOPs for ModelScope at sampling time).

## Relation to Prior Work

CMD sits at the intersection of two prior lines: (1) content-motion decomposition in video GANs (MoCoGAN, DIBR, DIBR) and triplane latent video encoding (PVDM), and (2) latent diffusion models that leverage pretrained image generators (Stable Diffusion). The key departure from prior video diffusion models (ModelScope, VideoLDM, VideoFactory) is that instead of adding temporal layers to a pretrained image U-Net and processing full video cubic arrays, CMD designs a latent space where one component (the content frame, a learned weighted sum of video frames) is image-like and can be generated by directly fine-tuning a pretrained image diffusion model without adding parameters, while the other (a low-dimensional triplane motion latent) is handled by a lightweight DiT. This avoids the dimensionality explosion that plagues prior approaches while still exploiting rich visual knowledge from image pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.7× sampling speedup and ~16.7× computation reduction while simultaneously improving FVD by 18.5% over prior SOTA make this a practically significant efficiency-quality tradeoff for video generation. However, the method is currently limited to fixed 16-frame clips at a single resolution, and the authors note quality degradation for highly dynamic motion in the latent space. Open questions include whether the content-frame-as-weighted-sum design generalizes to longer videos, whether the approach scales to larger model sizes (CMD is only 1.6B vs. 3.1B VideoLDM), and whether better content/motion representations can close the remaining gap to real video quality.
