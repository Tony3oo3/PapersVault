## Problem

Training deraining models on mixed multiple datasets improves generalization over single-dataset training, but naively amalgamating datasets neglects inherent discrepancies in rain types, background scenes, and simulation techniques, leading to suboptimal optimization and poor real-world performance. No prior method explicitly learns representations that capture both rain and background detail characteristics to adaptively guide the deraining process across heterogeneous datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240353/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single rainy RGB image (H×W×3) as input and produces a derained RGB image of the same resolution. The input image is simultaneously fed to a lightweight feature extractor (base 32 channels, GAP, 128-dim projector) to produce an instance-level representation z that guides the main deraining network. The approach is architecture-agnostic, supporting both CNN backbones (trained on 64×64 to 128×128 patches) and Transformer backbones (96×96 or 128×128 patches), with the modulation adding minimal inference overhead (~30 ms for encoding).

## Method

CoIC combines two key components: (1) a joint rain-/detail-aware contrastive learning strategy using a MoCo-style encoder with two types of negative exemplars — rain-aware negatives constructed by replacing the rain layer with the most dissimilar one from a cross-dataset rain bank, and detail-aware negatives generated by Gaussian-blurring the clean image — which learns a unified embedding space where light-rain images are characterized by background detail and heavy-rain images by rain structure; (2) a Context-based Instance-level Modulation (CoI-M) mechanism that uses the extracted representation z to adaptively modulate model parameters layer-by-layer. For CNNs, CoI-M generates a LoRA-inspired adaptive weight matrix with a channel-wise temperature that controls the effective receptive field of each convolutional kernel; for Transformers, it injects z-derived context into the cross-attention mechanism to modulate keys and values. The overall training objective combines a standard data-fidelity loss with the contrastive embedding constraint, enabling a single shared model to handle diverse rain conditions across mixed datasets.

## Results

CoIC consistently improves all five base models (BRN, RCDNet, DGUNet, IDT, DRSformer) across all five synthetic datasets when trained on mixed data. The largest gains appear for BRN (+2.00 dB PSNR on Rain200L, +1.98 on Rain800) and RCDNet (+1.02 dB on Rain200H, +1.15 on Rain800), while the already-strong DGUNet and DRSformer see smaller but consistent improvements (+0.07–0.32 dB). On the real-world RealInt benchmark, BRN+CoIC achieves the best NIQE of 4.5963, and DGUNet+CoIC reaches 4.6008, both outperforming their unmodulated counterparts and the pre-trained Restormer (4.8498).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoIC-equipped models outperform their unmodulated counterparts on every dataset and metric, with the most substantial gains for weaker architectures: BRN improves by up to 2.00 dB PSNR (Rain200L) and RCDNet by up to 1.15 dB (Rain800). Among the strongest baselines, DGUNet+CoIC achieves the best PSNR on Rain200H (31.07), Rain800 (30.75), and DID-Data (35.11), while DRSformer+CoIC shows only marginal gains (+0.06–0.08 dB) because its Mixture-of-Experts modules already partially absorb cross-dataset discrepancies. On the real-world RealInt benchmark, BRN+CoIC (NIQE 4.5963) and DGUNet+CoIC (4.6008) both surpass the official pre-trained Restormer (4.8498) and their own unmodulated versions. The semi-supervised Syn2Real and unsupervised DCD-GAN baselines perform substantially worse (e.g., DCD-GAN scores only 16.04 PSNR on Rain200H), confirming that supervised mixed-dataset training with CoIC is far more effective. On the individual-dataset benchmark (Rain200H, Table 3), DRSformer+CoIC achieves 32.26 dB PSNR, edging out DRSformer (32.17) and Restormer (32.00), demonstrating that CoIC also benefits single-dataset training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) mixed-dataset training for deraining (Zamir et al., Mou et al., Wang et al.), which typically concatenates all datasets and trains a single model; (2) contrastive learning for image restoration (Ye et al., Chen et al., Wang et al., Li et al.), which learns degradation or rain-vs-clean representations; and (3) adaptive model modulation (He et al. 2019, Fan et al. 2019, Li et al. 2022a), which conditions restoration on input-specific signals. CoIC departs from prior mixed-dataset work by arguing that direct mixing neglects inter-dataset discrepancies, and from prior contrastive restoration work by learning *joint* rain-/detail-aware representations rather than isolating a single degradation factor. The CoI-M modulation mechanism is explicitly inspired by LoRA (Hu et al. 2021) from the LLM literature, repurposing low-rank adaptive weights to modulate convolutional kernels and self-attention layers in a channel-wise, temperature-controlled manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoIC provides a practical and architecture-agnostic recipe for training deraining models on mixed datasets, which is essential for real-world deployment where no single synthetic dataset captures all rain types. The joint rain-/detail-aware embedding space offers interpretability (quantifying whether a model relies on rain cues or background details) and reveals that the embedding shifts from detail-dominated to rain-dominated as density increases — a structural insight absent from prior work. Limitations include the Gaussian blur approximation for detail-aware negatives (acknowledged to fail under heavy occlusion), modest real-world NIQE gains (~0.1), and added inference latency (~20–50 ms). Open questions include extending CoIC to multi-degradation scenarios (rain + fog + blur + noise) and all-in-one restoration, as the authors note.
