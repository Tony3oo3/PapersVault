## Problem

Existing feature similarity measures (CKA, alignment, representational similarity matrices) are vague in describing the inherent structure of neural networks: similar features can yield different outputs, and these metrics provide little actionable insight into feature redundancy. The paper asks for a more intrinsic, formally grounded definition of when features are truly equivalent in their functional role, and a corresponding metric for the most compact representation of features at each layer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240644/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the weight matrices and bias vectors of a pre-trained network—no input data, no forward passes, and no fine-tuning are required. It produces a compressed network (with functionally equivalent features merged) and a per-layer feature complexity score defined as the width of the poset of non-equivalent features. Because it is entirely data-agnostic, it can be applied directly to any pre-trained checkpoint (e.g., torchvision ImageNet weights) without modifying the training procedure.

## Method

The paper formalizes a neural network structure as a category (objects = feature-map shapes, arrows = layer types) and a parameterization as a functor mapping that structure to concrete parameterized layers. Two features are defined as functionally equivalent if a natural isomorphism exists between the corresponding functors, meaning one feature map can be replaced by the other through an invertible linear transformation while preserving all downstream outputs; the layer-wise feature complexity is then the width of the poset induced by this equivalence relation. To operationalize the theory, the Iterative Feature Merging (IFM) algorithm computes a weight distance between every pair of features at layer l (summing the squared L2 distance of the corresponding row of W^l and column of W^{l+1}), then iteratively merges the closest pair by summing their weights at layer l and taking a weighted average at layer l+1, repeating until the minimum distance exceeds β times the maximum. The paper further proves that Linear Mode Connectivity between two networks implies the existence of such a natural isomorphism, thereby grounding the category-theoretic framework in the empirically observed LMC/LLFC phenomenon.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, IFM reduces VGG16 to 23.03% of its original parameters while retaining 91.78% test accuracy (vs. 93.51% original), and ResNet18 can shed up to 60% of parameters with minimal accuracy loss. On ImageNet, the compression is more modest: approximately 5% parameter reduction for VGG and ~10% for ResNet50, reflecting the higher feature diversity needed for the harder task. ResNet50_v2 (80.86% top-1) shows faster accuracy degradation under merging than ResNet50_v1 (76.13% top-1), indicating that better-performing checkpoints learn more diverse (less redundant) features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's only direct quantitative comparison is against INN (Integral Neural Networks, Solodskikh et al., CVPR 2023) on ResNet18/CIFAR-10 (Figure 6). IFM achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy at similar compression ratios without requiring any modification to the training procedure, whereas INN necessitates a new training algorithm. The key practical advantage of IFM is that it is fully data-agnostic and can be applied directly to vanilla pre-trained checkpoints, while INN requires retraining with a modified objective. The paper positions IFM as the first data-agnostic pruning method that requires neither training data nor fine-tuning post-pruning, distinguishing it from both conventional channel pruning (which needs fine-tuning) and data-free pruning-at-initialization methods (which must be applied before training).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of feature similarity measurement (CKA, alignment, RSM, topology-based metrics) by arguing that similarity is too vague to capture true redundancy, and instead introduces 'functional equivalence' formalized via natural isomorphisms in category theory. It builds conceptually on Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) and Layerwise Linear Feature Connectivity (LLFC) by proving that LMC implies functional equivalence (Proposition 2.2), thereby providing a category-theoretic interpretation of these empirical phenomena. On the compression side, IFM is positioned as a data-agnostic, fine-tuning-free alternative to channel pruning methods like INN, though it shares the structural goal of removing redundant channels. The use of category theory (functors, natural transformations) as the formal language for neural network structure is novel in this context, drawing on a small but growing body of categorical ML work (Fong et al., 2019; Harris, 2019; Shiebler et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, architecture-agnostic framework for quantifying layer-wise feature redundancy (feature complexity as poset width), which could guide future network design toward more compact representations. Practically, IFM demonstrates that substantial parameter reduction is possible without any data access or fine-tuning (e.g., VGG16 on CIFAR10 reduced to 23% parameters with ~2% accuracy loss), though the effect is limited on harder tasks like ImageNet (~5–10%). Key open questions include why feature complexity peaks in middle layers, whether the approach generalizes to non-CNN architectures (transformers, diffusion models), and whether training objectives can be designed to minimize feature complexity from the start. The category-theoretic lens also opens the door to formal reasoning about network equivalence that goes beyond empirical similarity metrics.
