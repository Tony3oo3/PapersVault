## Problem

Robot manipulation data is scarce and expensive to collect, making it difficult to apply the large-scale pre-training paradigm that has proven successful in NLP and CV. While prior robot pre-training methods rely on contrastive learning or masked image modeling, this paper asks whether large-scale video *generative* pre-training—predicting future frames from past frames and language—can directly transfer to multi-task language-conditioned visual robot manipulation, given that a robot trajectory inherently contains a video sequence.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a language instruction (encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder), a sequence of 10 observation images (encoded by a frozen MAE-pretrained ViT with a Perceiver resampler to reduce patch tokens), and a sequence of robot states (6D end-effector pose in SE(3) plus binary gripper status, encoded by linear layers). It outputs continuous arm actions (delta XYZ positions and delta Euler angles), binary gripper actions, and reconstructed future images in pixel space. The language token is repeated at every timestep in the token sequence to prevent it from being overwhelmed by other modalities, and learned relative timestep embeddings inject temporal information shared across all modalities at the same step.

## Method

GR-1 is a 12-layer, 12-head causal transformer (195M parameters, 46M trainable) that unifies video prediction and action prediction in a single GPT-style architecture. During pre-training on 800K Ego4D video clips (8M frames), the model learns language-conditioned video prediction by predicting the next frame given past frames and a text description, using [OBS] tokens as prediction targets. During finetuning on robot data, [ACT] tokens are added to the sequence and the model jointly predicts actions and future images with a combined loss of Smooth-L1 (arm), BCE (gripper), and MSE (video). A key architectural detail is that [OBS] and [ACT] tokens are masked in the causal attention mask—no token can attend to them—so they serve purely as prediction slots rather than information carriers. The CLIP text encoder and MAE image encoder remain frozen throughout both phases, and the same model architecture transitions seamlessly from video-only pre-training to multi-modal robot finetuning simply by adding robot state tokens and [ACT] tokens to the input sequence.

## Results

On the CALVIN benchmark (ABCD→D split), GR-1 achieves a 94.9% success rate on the first task and an average length of 4.21 tasks completed in a row of 5, outperforming the best baseline HULC (88.9% / 3.06). In the zero-shot unseen scene generalization setting (ABC→D), GR-1 improves the success rate from 53.3% (RT-1) to 85.4% and average length from 0.90 to 3.06. With only 10% of training data, GR-1 reaches 77.8% success rate and 2.00 average length versus 66.8% / 1.11 for the best baseline (HULC). On real robot experiments, GR-1 achieves 79% success on seen objects, 73% on unseen instances, and 75% on articulated object manipulation, substantially outperforming RT-1 (27%/13%/35%) and MT-R3M (15%/13%/30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GR-1 outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. Against HULC (the strongest baseline), GR-1 improves the average length from 3.06 to 4.21 on the standard ABCD→D split and from 0.67 to 3.06 on the zero-shot unseen scene ABC→D split — a 4.6× improvement in the latter. Against RT-1, GR-1 more than doubles the average length in the unseen scene setting (0.90 → 3.06) and improves the 10%-data success rate from 24.9% to 77.8%. Against MT-R3M, GR-1 consistently leads by a wide margin, e.g., 4.21 vs. 2.08 average length on ABCD→D and 3.06 vs. 0.93 on ABC→D. MCIL is the weakest baseline, achieving only 0.40 average length on ABCD→D and 0.31 on ABC→D, indicating that hierarchical latent-plan approaches struggle with long-horizon multi-tasking. On real robot experiments, GR-1 achieves 79% on seen objects vs. 27% (RT-1) and 15% (MT-R3M), and 75% on articulated object manipulation vs. 35% and 30% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

GR-1 extends the GPT-style causal transformer architecture (Radford et al., 2018) to multi-modal robot manipulation, building on the sequential decision-making-with-transformers line (Decision Transformer, GATO, RoboCat). It departs from RoboCat by performing large-scale video generative pre-training on out-of-domain human video (Ego4D) rather than training from scratch, and by being language-conditioned rather than goal-image-conditioned. Unlike prior pre-training approaches for robotics (R3M, RPT, VPT, VIPER) that either learn visual representations via contrastive/masked objectives or use in-domain video, GR-1 unifies video prediction and action prediction in a single end-to-end model pre-trained on 800K egocentric video clips, leveraging the observation that a robot trajectory inherently contains a video sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first strong evidence that large-scale out-of-domain video generative pre-training transfers effectively to multi-task robot manipulation, with particularly striking zero-shot scene generalization (85.4% vs. 53.3%) and data efficiency (77.8% vs. 66.8% at 10% data). The unified GPT-style architecture with a clear pre-train-then-finetune recipe is practical and likely to influence subsequent robot foundation model designs. Open questions include whether the benefits scale with more diverse robot data, whether manipulation-relevant video content outperforms general video, and how to incorporate unlabeled video alongside language-annotated data for further robustness gains.
