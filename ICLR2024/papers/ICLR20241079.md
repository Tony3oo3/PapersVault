## Problem

In Byzantine-robust distributed learning, even with robust aggregators, model accuracy drops significantly under attacks due to the large variance of stochastic gradients. When the total number of gradient computations is fixed, increasing batch size reduces variance but also reduces the number of model updates, creating a trade-off that has not been systematically analyzed. This paper asks: how should batch size be chosen as a function of the Byzantine worker fraction to minimize the theoretical convergence bound?

## Input / Output

Each of m workers independently draws mini-batches of B samples from the i.i.d. data distribution and computes local stochastic gradients, which are combined via local momentum before being sent to a central server. The server applies a robust aggregator (Krum, geometric median, coordinate-wise median, or centered clipping) to the m received momentum vectors and broadcasts the updated model parameters. The key constraint is that total gradient computation C = TBm(1−δ) is held fixed, so increasing B proportionally decreases the iteration count T. Experiments use ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB images, 10 classes) with 8 workers and up to 3 Byzantine workers under ALIE or FoE attacks.

## Method

The paper makes two main contributions. First, it derives a closed-form expression for the optimal batch size B* that minimizes the convergence upper bound of ByzSGDm under fixed total gradient computation, showing B* is strictly increasing in the Byzantine fraction δ — meaning larger batches are theoretically preferred under attack. Second, it proposes ByzSGDnm, which modifies the standard Byzantine-robust SGD update by normalizing the aggregated momentum vector (dividing by its Euclidean norm) before the parameter update, i.e., w_{t+1} = w_t − η_t · Agg(u_t^{(1)},...,u_t^{(m)}) / ‖Agg(...)‖. This normalization, borrowed from large-batch training literature, stabilizes updates when the aggregated direction is noisy. The paper proves ByzSGDnm achieves O(1/T^{1/4}) convergence for non-convex objectives under Byzantine attacks, matching the optimal rate of vanilla normalized SGD, with an extra factor [√(2cmδ(1−δ))+1]^{1/2}/(1−δ)^{1/4} that vanishes when δ=0.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20 under ALIE attack (3 Byzantine workers out of 8, δ=3/8), increasing batch size from 32×8 to 512×8 dramatically improves top-1 accuracy: e.g., with centered clipping (CC), accuracy rises from 72.83% to 87.46% for ByzSGDm, and further to 88.53% for ByzSGDnm. Under FoE attack, ByzSGDnm with batch 512×8 achieves 89.32% (CC) vs. 83.60% for the small-batch baseline. The paper also shows a 6.38× training speedup when increasing batch size from 32×8 to 512×8, confirming the bonus of training acceleration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external comparison is with Byz-VR-MARINA (Gorbunov et al., ICLR 2023), a variance-reduction-based Byzantine-robust method. The paper reports that ByzSGDnm significantly outperforms Byz-VR-MARINA in i.i.d. settings, with the key advantage being lower computation cost: Byz-VR-MARINA requires more than 2× the gradient computations per iteration due to its PAGE-based variance reduction (which intermittently uses full gradients), while ByzSGDnm achieves comparable or better accuracy using only large-batch stochastic gradients and momentum normalization. Internally, ByzSGDnm consistently outperforms ByzSGDm across all four robust aggregators (Krum, geometric median, coordinate-wise median, centered clipping) under both ALIE and FoE attacks, with the largest gains under ALIE (e.g., +14.17% for coordinate-wise median at batch 32×8). The only case where ByzSGDnm does not improve is Krum under FoE attack, where both methods fail (10.00%) because Krum is inherently not robust to FoE attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ByzSGDm (Karimireddy et al., 2021), which replaces the mean aggregator with a robust aggregator and uses local momentum to reduce gradient variance in Byzantine-robust distributed learning. The key departure is the introduction of whole-momentum normalization (adapted from Cutkosky & Mehta, 2020, and Zhao et al., 2020) into the Byzantine-robust setting, yielding ByzSGDnm. The paper also departs from the standard practice in BRDL literature of fixing batch size at 32 or 50 by systematically analyzing the batch-size trade-off under a fixed total gradient computation budget, showing that the optimal batch size increases with the Byzantine fraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is clear and actionable: practitioners running Byzantine-robust distributed training should use substantially larger batch sizes than the conventional 32–50, especially when the Byzantine fraction is high. The momentum normalization is a one-line change that further improves accuracy at negligible computational cost. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (CIFAR-10) with a single architecture (ResNet-20) and only 8 workers, and the theoretical analysis is restricted to i.i.d. settings. Open questions include whether the batch-size guidance generalizes to non-i.i.d. data, larger models, and more diverse attack models, and whether the normalization interacts favorably with other robustness techniques like NNM in non-i.i.d. regimes.
