## Problem

Existing RL-based red teaming methods for LLMs converge to deterministic policies that repeatedly generate a small set of effective test cases, resulting in low coverage of the full space of prompts that elicit undesirable responses. The training objective only maximizes reward (toxicity of elicited responses) without any incentive to explore novel test cases, so equally effective but different prompts are never discovered.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240800/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The red-team model (GPT2-137M) receives truncated text as input: the first four words of an IMDb review (text continuation) or a template listing three randomly sampled instructions from Alpaca/Databricks (instruction following). It generates a short continuation or new instruction that, when prepended to the target LLM's prompt, elicits a toxic response scored by a RoBERTa hate-speech classifier. Crucially, the novelty rewards require maintaining a growing memory of all previously generated test cases during training, making the reward signal non-stationary over the course of RL training.

## Method

The paper augments the standard RL red-teaming objective (reward minus KL penalty) with two curiosity-driven terms: an entropy bonus λ_E·log π(x|z) to prevent policy collapse, and two novelty rewards computed against all previously generated test cases — negative SelfBLEU (n-gram form overlap) and negative cosine similarity of Sentence-BERT embeddings (semantic overlap). Because novelty decays as a test case is repeated, the policy is pushed to discover unseen prompts rather than re-exploiting known effective ones. Training uses PPO with a GPT2-137M red-team model (LoRA for instruction-following, unfrozen first two layers for text continuation), and the novelty reward weights (λ_B=1.0, λ_C=1.0, λ_E=0.01) are kept fixed across all experiments. The key insight is that memory-dependent novelty rewards outperform memory-independent diversity strategies (temperature, entropy alone, or response-embedding diversity) because they explicitly penalize repetition of previously seen test cases.

## Results

In text continuation (GPT2-137M target), RL+Curiosity matches the RL baseline in quality (percentage of toxic responses across thresholds) while achieving significantly higher SelfBLEU and embedding diversity. In instruction following (GPT2-alpaca and Dolly-v2-7B targets), RL+Curiosity outperforms all baselines in both quality and diversity simultaneously. Against LLaMA2-7b-chat-hf (RLHF-tuned), CRT identified 196 prompts eliciting toxic responses where the official evaluation reported 0%. On Vicuna-7B and gpt-3.5-turbo-instruct, RL+Curiosity generates ~50,000 unique testcases at the 0.9 toxicity threshold, compared to ~500 for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the RL baseline (Perez et al., 2022), RL+Curiosity matches quality in text continuation but significantly outperforms in both SelfBLEU and embedding diversity; in instruction following, RL+Curiosity actually surpasses RL in quality as well, suggesting that improved exploration helps discover more effective test cases. Against RL+TDiv (Casper et al., 2023), RL+Curiosity wins on diversity metrics (both SelfBLEU and embedding) while matching or exceeding quality; the paper attributes RL+TDiv's inferior diversity to its objective of maximizing target LLM response diversity rather than test case novelty, causing it to repeatedly exploit already-discovered effective prompts. In the gpt-3.5-turbo-instruct experiment, the RL baseline shows high variance across seeds (ranging from 11 to 8,395 unique testcases at threshold 0.9), while RL+Curiosity consistently produces ~50K unique testcases. The paper also notes that a human pilot study (16 participants, 100 test cases) failed to elicit any response with toxicity probability exceeding 0.5 from LLaMA2, whereas CRT found 196 such prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two established lines of work: RL-based automated red teaming (Perez et al., 2022), which trains a red-team LLM via PPO to maximize the toxicity of target LLM responses, and curiosity-driven exploration in RL (Pathak et al., 2017; Burda et al., 2019), which augments task rewards with intrinsic novelty signals to encourage broader exploration. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the low coverage problem in RL red teaming—where the policy converges to a small set of effective prompts—is structurally identical to the exploration problem in RL, and that memory-dependent novelty rewards (SelfBLEU, cosine similarity against all previously generated test cases) directly address it. The paper departs from the concurrent RL+TDiv approach (Casper et al., 2023), which maximizes response diversity rather than test-case novelty, showing that the latter is the more effective objective for coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: curiosity-driven red teaming found 196 toxic prompts from LLaMA2-7b-chat-hf (officially reported as 0% toxic) using only a 137M-parameter GPT2 red-team model, and a human pilot study confirmed that 16 participants could not match this, underscoring both the value of automated exploration and the insufficiency of current RLHF safety training. The finding that curiosity improves not just diversity but also effectiveness (quality) challenges the assumed trade-off between exploration and exploitation in red teaming. Open questions include whether adaptive reward weighting (e.g., via EIPO) can remove the need for manual tuning, whether the approach scales to larger red-team models, and whether the memory-dependent novelty mechanism remains tractable as the test-case corpus grows to millions.
