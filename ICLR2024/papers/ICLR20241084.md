## Problem

Existing image deblurring methods, whether CNN- or Transformer-based, process every pixel uniformly, which distorts originally sharp backgrounds in locally blurred images and wastes computation on sharp regions—especially problematic for high-resolution inputs. No prior Transformer-based approach could selectively focus deblurring on localized blur regions while leaving sharp areas untouched.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241084/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single locally blurred RGB image (evaluated at full 2152×1436 resolution on ReLoBlur) and produces a deblurred RGB image of the same size. No blur mask or auxiliary input is required at inference; the network internally predicts blurriness confidence to decide which regions to process. Training is joint on GoPro (global blur) and ReLoBlur (local blur) at roughly 1:1 sampling ratio to support both deblurring modes.

## Method

LMD-ViT is a U-shaped sparse vision Transformer whose core AdaWPT blocks adaptively prune non-blurry windows before they enter window-based self-attention (W-MSA) and feed-forward (W-LeFF) layers. A confidence predictor (MLP with Softmax) estimates per-token blurriness, and a decision layer converts this to binary keep/discard maps—using Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization during training for gradient flow and a fixed threshold β during inference. Only windows whose average decision exceeds a pooling threshold s are forwarded through the Transformer layers, while pruned windows are restored to their original positions via a window compound layer to maintain spatial coherence. The network is trained end-to-end with a combined loss: a cross-entropy pruning loss supervised by manually annotated blur masks and a weighted reconstruction loss (L1 + SSIM + FFT) that emphasizes blurred regions. Pruning is applied at multiple resolutions (coarse at low-res encoder blocks, fine at high-res decoder blocks), reducing FLOPs by 66% and inference time by over 2× compared to full-attention Transformer baselines while improving PSNR by 0.28 dB.

## Results

On the ReLoBlur dataset (2152×1436 full resolution), LMD-ViT achieves 35.42 dB PSNR, 0.9289 SSIM, 30.25 dB weighted PSNR, and 0.8938 weighted SSIM, outperforming all CNN and Transformer baselines. It reduces FLOPs by 66% (1.485T vs. 4.375T for Uformer-B) and achieves 2.3× faster inference (0.56s vs. 1.31s) compared to the best Transformer baseline, while also surpassing the best CNN method (LBAG) by 0.51 dB PSNR and 0.99 dB weighted PSNR. On the GoPro global deblurring benchmark, LMD-ViT scores 32.16 dB PSNR and 0.9318 SSIM, comparable to Uformer-B (32.51/0.9376) and Restormer (32.15/0.9305).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMD-ViT outperforms all seven baselines on every quality metric (PSNR, SSIM, weighted PSNR, weighted SSIM) on the ReLoBlur local deblurring benchmark. Against the strongest CNN baseline LBAG, it gains +0.51 dB PSNR and +0.99 dB weighted PSNR while matching inference speed (0.56s vs. 0.51s). Against the strongest Transformer baseline Uformer-B, it improves PSNR by +0.28 dB and weighted PSNR by +0.33 dB while reducing FLOPs by 66% (1.485T vs. 4.375T) and cutting inference time by 57% (0.56s vs. 1.31s). Compared to Restormer, the gains are even larger: +0.57 dB PSNR, +0.97 dB weighted PSNR, and 6.6× faster inference (0.56s vs. 3.72s). The paper also shows that when baselines are retrained with local blur masks (Table 5), Uformer-B and Restormer improve but still trail LMD-ViT in both quality and speed, confirming the advantage of the adaptive window pruning architecture over simply adding mask conditioning to global Transformers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) U-shaped Transformer architectures for image restoration (Uformer, Restormer), which process all tokens globally; (2) dynamic token sparsification methods (DynamicViT, AdaViT, A-ViT) that learn to discard unimportant tokens; and (3) local motion deblurring (LBAG), which uses a gate to mitigate distortion on sharp regions but still processes the entire image. LMD-ViT departs from prior work by being the first to combine adaptive window-level pruning with a U-shaped Transformer specifically for local deblurring, replacing LBAG's gate-based mitigation with actual computational removal of sharp-region windows, and training the pruning mechanism end-to-end via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization and a dedicated pruning loss against annotated blur masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 66% FLOPs reduction with simultaneous quality gains over non-pruning baselines demonstrates that for local restoration tasks, selectively computing only on affected regions is both more efficient and more accurate than uniform processing—a principle that could transfer to other spatially-localized restoration problems (inpainting, shadow removal, local super-resolution). The carefully annotated ReLoBlur blur masks also provide a reusable resource for the community. Key limitations include the inability to run in real time (0.56s on A100 for 2152×1436 images) and the reliance on a fixed pruning threshold β at inference, which may not adapt to varying blur extents. Whether the window-pruning paradigm extends cleanly to tasks with more diffuse or multi-scale blur patterns remains an open question.
