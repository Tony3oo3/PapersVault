## Problem

Mesh-based GNN simulators rely on local message passing, making it difficult to propagate collision effects over long distances within the very short timeframes characteristic of flexible body dynamics. While Transformers offer global attention, their O(N²) complexity and the additional contact edges required for collision modeling make naive adoption prohibitively expensive. This paper addresses the under-explored question of whether learning-based simulators can effectively handle flexible body collision dynamics where instantaneous impacts must be propagated far from the collision point.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a mesh graph at time t (plus p previous frames) where each node carries physical properties (density, Young's modulus, node type) and each edge carries relative displacement vectors in both mesh space and world space. A novel contact edge feature connects nodes across different objects within a collision radius γ, encoding the relative displacement between colliding surfaces. The output is the next-step velocity per node (integrated via first-order Euler to obtain positions) and the next-step stress field, with training supervised by one-step MSE on both position and stress.

## Method

HCMT employs a contact-then-propagate strategy with two specialized Transformers: a Contact Mesh Transformer (CMT) that uses dual-branch self-attention to separately compute attention over mesh edges (within objects) and contact edges (between colliding objects), both incorporating edge features into the attention weights; and a Hierarchical Mesh Transformer (HMT) that propagates messages through a multi-level graph built via BFS node pooling and Delaunay remeshing, where each level halves the node count to enable shortcut long-range propagation. The hierarchical structure reduces computational complexity while allowing collision effects to reach distant nodes in fewer steps than flat message passing. The encoder/decoder follow the GNS/MGN framework, and the model is trained with one-step MSE loss on position and stress, using random-walk noise for rollout stability.

## Results

HCMT achieves the lowest rollout-all RMSE across all four datasets and both metrics (position and stress). On the novel Impact Plate dataset (flexible dynamics with collisions), HCMT reduces position RMSE to 20.71×10³ and stress RMSE to 14,742×10³, representing 49.2% and 58.9% improvements over the runner-up (MGN). On Deformable Plate, HCMT achieves a 28.9% position RMSE improvement (7.67 vs. 10.78×10³), while on Sphere Simple and Deforming Plate the gains are 8.6% and 4.3% respectively. HCMT also exhibits the lowest standard deviations across all metrics, indicating more stable predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares HCMT against two baselines: MGN (Pfaff et al., 2020), a message-passing mesh GNN with 15 iterations, and GT (Dwivedi & Bresson, 2020), a Graph Transformer with edge features and 15 blocks. HCMT outperforms both baselines on every metric across all four datasets, with no losses. The most dramatic margin is on Impact Plate (flexible dynamics with collisions), where HCMT's position RMSE of 20.71×10³ is roughly half that of MGN (40.73) and nearly one-third that of GT (59.18); the stress RMSE improvement is even larger (14,742 vs. 35,871 for MGN and 39,291 for GT). On Deformable Plate, HCMT achieves a 28.9% position improvement over MGN (7.67 vs. 10.78×10³), while on Sphere Simple the gap is smaller (30.41 vs. 33.26×10³, 8.6%). GT is consistently the weakest baseline, particularly on Sphere Simple where its position RMSE (243.85) is over 8× worse than MGN's, likely due to the high variance from self-contacts in that dataset. HCMT also shows the tightest standard deviations in all settings, suggesting more reliable long-horizon rollouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

HCMT extends the encoder-processor-decoder framework of GNS and MGN by replacing the local message-passing processor with a two-stage Transformer pipeline: a Contact Mesh Transformer (CMT) that handles instantaneous collision dynamics via dual-branch self-attention over mesh and contact edges, followed by a Hierarchical Mesh Transformer (HMT) that propagates collision effects over long ranges through BFS-pooled and Delaunay-remeshed multi-level graphs. The hierarchical pooling strategy draws directly from Cao et al. (2022) and the broader line of multi-scale mesh GNNs (Fortunato et al., 2022; Han et al., 2022; Janny et al., 2023), but departs from those works by introducing contact edges as a first-class edge type in the attention mechanism—a design absent from prior fluid or rigid-body mesh Transformers. The paper positions itself as the first to explicitly model flexible body collision dynamics (as opposed to rigid-body contacts or static flexible deformation) within a learned mesh simulator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: display-industry product design requires predicting stress distributions after short-duration impacts, where traditional explicit solvers (ANSYS) take ~14 seconds per step versus HCMT's ~81 ms. The Impact Plate dataset, grounded in real manufacturing workflows, provides a benchmark that prior learning-based simulators have not tackled. However, the evaluation is limited to two baselines (MGN, GT), all datasets are relatively small (≤2,208 nodes), and the Impact Plate experiments are 2D only; the authors acknowledge that 3D extension incurs 10–20× computational cost. Open questions include learnable pooling strategies, integration with generative mesh models for complex geometries, and whether the contact-then-propagate ordering generalizes to multi-body or self-contact scenarios beyond the single ball-plate setup.
