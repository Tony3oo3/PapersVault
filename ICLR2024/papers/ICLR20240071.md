## Problem

Existing DFS-based positional encodings for geographic coordinates assume a rectangular longitude-latitude domain even on global data, producing artifacts at the poles where all meridians converge. Additionally, prior work has focused almost exclusively on the positional embedding design while using a fixed FCNET architecture, leaving the interaction between embedding choice and network architecture largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240071/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The location encoder consumes raw longitude λ∈[-π,π] and latitude φ∈[-π/2,π/2] and produces a target variable y (classification logit, regression value, or probability vector) that can be integrated into downstream models. For the iNaturalist task, the location encoder's species probability P(y|φ,λ) is multiplied with a frozen InceptionV3 image classifier's P(y|I) to yield a joint prediction. The model learns an implicit neural representation: the spatial signal is encoded in network weights, enabling continuous prediction at arbitrary test points without explicit interpolation of neighboring grid cells.

## Method

The paper proposes Spherical Harmonic (SH) positional embeddings — orthogonal basis functions Y_l^m(λ,φ) defined natively on the sphere via associated Legendre polynomials up to degree L — paired with Sinusoidal Representation Networks (SIRENNET) using sine activation functions. A key theoretical contribution shows that a 1-layer SIRENNET with specific weight constraints is analytically equivalent to the GRID DFS encoding, meaning SIRENNET implicitly learns the multi-frequency scaling that DFS embeddings provide explicitly, which explains its strong performance even with trivial DIRECT inputs. The SH embedding is computed via pre-computed analytic polynomial expressions (faster than closed-form evaluation) and can be used with a simple LINEAR layer or combined with SIRENNET/FCNET. Across four benchmarks (checkerboard, land-ocean, ERA5 8-variable climate interpolation, iNaturalist 2018), SH+SIRENNET achieves the best or near-best results, with SH eliminating polar artifacts that plague DFS-based encodings and SIRENNET outperforming FCNET on classification tasks.

## Results

On the ERA5 multi-variable climate interpolation task (8 variables, 1% training data), the proposed Spherical Harmonic (SH) embedding with FCNET achieves 0.58±0.02 MSE, a 2.4× improvement over the next-best baseline (SPHEREC+ with FCNET at 1.38±0.03). On the synthetic Checkerboard classification, SH+LINEAR reaches 94.8±0.1% accuracy—demonstrating that the positional embedding alone is highly informative—while SH+SIRENNET achieves 95.8±0.0%. On iNaturalist 2018, SH+SIRENNET adds +12.3±0.2% top-1 accuracy over the image-only baseline (59.2%), the largest improvement among all tested combinations. SH consistently achieves the best or near-best results across all four datasets and all three neural network architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mac Aodha et al. (2019) WRAP+FCNET, the proposed SH+FCNET reduces ERA5 MSE from 4.13 to 0.58 (7.1× improvement) and raises Checkerboard accuracy from 68.3% to 94.9%. Against Mai et al. (2020) GRID and THEORY embeddings, SH dominates on ERA5 (0.58 vs. 1.51/1.61 MSE with FCNET) and on Checkerboard with LINEAR (94.8% vs. 10.5%/64.0%), showing that multi-scale sine-cosine grids are far less effective than spherical harmonics for global interpolation. Against Mai et al. (2023b) SPHEREC+/SPHEREM+, SH+FCNET achieves 0.58 vs. 1.38/1.54 MSE on ERA5 and 94.9% vs. 93.7%/87.9% on Checkerboard; the gap is most pronounced at high latitudes where DFS-based encodings produce polar artifacts. Against Sitzmann et al. (2020) SIRENNET used with simple DIRECT input, the paper shows analytically that a 1-layer SIRENNET subsumes the GRID DFS encoding (eq. 7), explaining why SIRENNET+DIRECT already reaches 94.7% on Checkerboard; combining SIRENNET with SH yields the best overall results (95.8% Checkerboard, +12.3% iNaturalist top-1) but the margin over SIRENNET+DIRECT is modest, suggesting SIRENNET's learned frequencies partially compensate for the lack of an explicit spherical basis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of DFS-based positional encodings (Mac Aodha et al. 2019; Mai et al. 2020b, 2023b) that project latitude/longitude into multi-scale sine-cosine features on a rectangular domain, replacing them with spherical harmonic basis functions that are natively defined on the sphere. It simultaneously repurposes SIRENNET (Sitzmann et al. 2020), previously used only for implicit neural representations of images, as a location-encoding backbone and provides an analytic proof that a 1-layer SIRENNET with constrained weights is equivalent to the GRID DFS encoding, thereby reframing the network as a learned positional embedding. The work also draws on the long tradition of spherical harmonics in geophysics (gravity field, magnetosphere modeling) and on recent non-Euclidean neural field literature (Koestler et al. 2022; Grattarola & Vandergheynst 2022), but focuses specifically on the sphere as a special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete, well-evidenced recommendation for practitioners: use SIRENNET for any geographic-coordinate task and spherical harmonic embeddings when data spans polar regions, where DFS-based methods produce visible artifacts. The analytic equivalence between SIRENNET and learned DFS blurs the established PE/NN decomposition and raises open questions about whether the two components can be further unified into more parameter-efficient encoders. Limitations include modest and task-dependent performance gains (on iNaturalist, SH+SIRENNET is only marginally better than SIREN(DIRECT) within standard deviations), a narrow computational-efficiency advantage over SPHEREC+ (only beyond L>20), and the fact that the iNaturalist location bias (few high-latitude observations) limits the practical benefit of spherical geometry in that setting.
