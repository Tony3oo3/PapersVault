## Problem

Existing robust RL methods assume adversarial perturbations are independent across timesteps (constrained only by a per-step L_p norm ball), which is unrealistic for physical systems where perturbations naturally persist over time. This non-temporally-coupled assumption leads to overly conservative policies that fail under temporally-coupled attacks, where the adversary's perturbation at time t+1 is constrained relative to its perturbation at time t. The paper introduces temporally-coupled perturbations as a new threat model and shows that state-of-the-art robust baselines (e.g., WocaR-RL) degrade significantly under such attacks.

## Input / Output

The agent receives continuous state observations from MuJoCo environments (Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Ant, Humanoid v2) and outputs continuous actions. The adversary is a separate RL agent whose action space is jointly constrained by a per-step perturbation budget ε and a temporally-coupled constraint ε̄ (typically set to ε/5), limiting how much the perturbation can change between consecutive timesteps. The final output is not a single policy but a meta-Nash equilibrium: a mixture strategy over a growing population of trained policies for both the agent and the adversary, computed via the PSRO meta-game.

## Method

GRAD formulates robust RL as a partially-observable two-player zero-sum game and solves it using Policy Space Response Oracles (PSRO). In each epoch, the agent trains a best-response policy against a fixed adversary sampled from the adversary's current meta-strategy, and vice versa; the adversary's policy is an LSTM-based RL agent that learns to generate temporally-coupled perturbations (constrained by both ε and ε̄) to minimize the agent's reward. New policies are added to each player's population, and the meta-Nash equilibrium over the empirical payoff matrix is recomputed, driving convergence to an approximate Nash equilibrium (guaranteed for finite-horizon MDPs with discrete actions). The key distinction from prior adversarial training is that GRAD does not target a specific worst-case attack or a fixed perturbation distribution; instead, the game-theoretic equilibrium naturally adapts to the adversary's learned strategy, yielding robustness across diverse attack domains (state, action, model uncertainty, and mixed perturbations) without retraining.

## Results

Under temporally-coupled state attacks, GRAD achieves 45% higher average episode reward than the strongest baseline (WocaR-PPO) across five MuJoCo tasks. Against temporally-coupled action attacks on Humanoid, GRAD outperforms baselines by 40%, and against temporally-coupled mixed attacks, GRAD leads by 30% in multiple environments (minimum 10%). Under non-temporally-coupled mixed attacks, GRAD still outperforms all baselines, with over 20% margin on Humanoid. GRAD also maintains comparable natural (no-attack) performance to baselines, showing robustness does not come at the cost of clean-environment performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAD consistently outperforms WocaR-PPO (Liang et al., 2022), the state-of-the-art worst-case-aware defense, across all attack domains: 45% higher under temporally-coupled state attacks, 40% higher under temporally-coupled action attacks on Humanoid, and 30% higher under temporally-coupled mixed attacks. Against PA-ATLA-PPO (Zhang et al., 2021; Sun et al., 2022), which uses alternating training with a learned adversary, GRAD achieves superior robustness in both temporally-coupled and non-temporally-coupled settings, with the gap widening under the temporally-coupled constraint. For action-robust baselines PR-MDP and NR-MDP (Tessler et al., 2019), GRAD demonstrates higher returns under both action perturbations and model uncertainty across all α values tested. Against RNAC-PPO (Zhou et al., 2023) on transition uncertainty with perturbed physical parameters, GRAD achieves competitive or superior performance (e.g., 2692 vs. 2359 on Hopper with leg_joint_stiffness perturbation, 1369 vs. 1143 on HalfCheetah with back_actuator_range perturbation). The key finding is that WocaR-PPO, while robust under standard Lp-norm attacks, becomes vulnerable specifically under temporally-coupled attacks, revealing a gap that GRAD's game-theoretic equilibrium approach closes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PSRO (Lanctot et al., 2017) as its algorithmic backbone, adapting the double-oracle framework from multi-agent game solving to the robust RL setting. It departs from the dominant line of robust RL work (WocaR-RL, ATLA, PR-MDP) that assumes per-timestep independent Lp-norm perturbation budgets, by introducing temporally-coupled constraints that link consecutive perturbations. Conceptually, the paper repositions robust RL from a worst-case optimization problem (as in WocaR-RL) or an alternating min-max training loop (as in ATLA) to a two-player zero-sum game whose approximate Nash equilibrium is found via iterative best-response computation, making the defense adaptive to the specific adversary rather than targeting a fixed worst case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a practical gap in prior robust RL threat models: defenses optimized against the standard non-coupled Lp ball can be *more* vulnerable to realistic temporally-correlated perturbations, as demonstrated by WocaR-RL's failure under temporally-coupled attacks. The game-theoretic framing offers a more general and adaptive solution that handles state, action, model-uncertainty, and mixed adversaries within a single framework. Key limitations include high computational cost (20–40 GPU-hours per environment), evaluation restricted to MuJoCo continuous control, and reliance on PSRO convergence which may be slow in practice. Open questions include scalability to pixel-based or real-world RL, whether alternative equilibrium-finding algorithms (e.g., Friction-FoReL, ESCHER) could reduce training cost, and whether the temporally-coupled constraint generalizes beyond the simple one-step coupling studied here.
