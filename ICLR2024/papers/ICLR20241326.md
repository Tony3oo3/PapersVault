## Problem

SDF-based neural reconstruction methods (NeuS, VolSDF) fail on reflective scenes because specular radiance entangles with geometry, while neural BRDF approaches (NeRO) produce over-smoothed shapes due to the low-frequency implicit bias of networks. Polarization priors offer a direct cue to surface normals but are noisy in diffuse-dominant regions and concentrated in specular regions, making naive supervision unreliable. No existing method simultaneously recovers high-frequency specular geometry and robustly handles the imbalanced, noisy nature of polarization signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241326/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N calibrated multi-view radiance images together with pixel-aligned polarization priors (Angle of Polarization and Degree of Polarization maps) captured in a single shot using 4-directional linear polarizers (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°), from which Stokes vectors are demosaiced. Camera poses come from a 360° calibration disk. The output is a 3D mesh extracted via Marching Cubes at 512³ resolution from the learned SDF, along with per-vertex surface normals. Training runs for 200K iterations on 4× RTX 3090 Ti GPUs (~6 hours).

## Method

GNeRP extends the NeuS SDF framework by representing the local distribution of surface normals as a 3D Gaussian at each sampled point: the mean encodes the overall normal orientation while the 3×3 covariance captures high-frequency geometric variation (edges, abrupt curvature). These 3D Gaussians are analytically splatted to 2D Gaussians in the image plane via a viewing-rotation and normal-projection transform, and all 2D Gaussians along a ray are composited by volume rendering. The resulting 2D Gaussian mean is supervised by the AoP-derived azimuth angle, and its covariance (decomposed via SVD into anisotropy ratio and eigenvectors) is supervised by local AoP variation, directly linking polarization cues to geometric detail without disentangling specular radiance. A DoP-based reweighting strategy modulates the loss as α(1−ρ)L_color + βρ(L_mean + L_cov), adaptively trusting radiance in diffuse regions (low DoP) and polarization in specular regions (high DoP) to counteract the noise and spatial imbalance of polarization priors.

## Results

On the PolRef real dataset, GNeRP achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 1.35 across four reflective scenes (Ironman, Duck, Cow, Snorlax), outperforming the closest competitor Ref-NeuS (2.34) by 42%. The method also leads on the synthetic PolRef dataset, achieving CD of 0.72 and MAE of 0.78 on Bunny, and CD of 0.59 and MAE of 1.03 on Dragon, versus Ref-NeuS (1.09/1.03 and 0.82/1.23) and NeRO (1.41/2.55 and 2.15/3.47). GNeRP wins on every individual scene in both real and synthetic evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GNeRP outperforms all eight baselines on every scene in the main comparison. Against the strongest competitor Ref-NeuS (mean CD 2.34), GNeRP (1.35) improves by roughly 42%, with the largest gap on Cow (1.39 vs. 3.66). NeRO, which uses neural BRDF for reflective objects, catastrophically fails on highly reflective scenes (Duck: 23.75, Snorlax: 24.30) due to the low-frequency bias of neural BRDFs preventing recovery of abrupt normal changes. PANDORA, the only other polarization-based method, is consistently worse (mean 5.90) because its full Stokes-vector supervision is noisy in diffuse-dominant regions. View-consistency methods (Geo-NeuS, NeuralWarp) perform poorly on reflective scenes because sparse SfM reconstruction is noisy and incomplete. Standard SDF baselines (NeuS, VolSDF, Unisurf) cannot disentangle specular radiance from geometry, yielding over-smoothed or distorted shapes. On the synthetic dataset, GNeRP also leads in both Chamfer Distance and Mean Angular Error of normals, confirming the advantage extends to normal accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NeuS's SDF-based volume rendering pipeline but departs from the dominant strategy of disentangling specular radiance via neural BRDFs (as in NeRO, Ref-NeuS), arguing that the low-frequency implicit bias of neural networks makes this inverse problem intractable for high-frequency geometry. Instead, it repurposes the Gaussian splatting paradigm (from EWA splatting and 3D Gaussian Splatting) to represent local normal distributions as 3D Gaussians that project exactly to 2D Gaussians in the image plane, enabling direct supervision by polarization priors without ever solving the BRDF decomposition. The DoP-based reweighting strategy addresses a known limitation of prior polarization-based methods (PANDORA, Sparse Ellipsometry) where diffuse-dominant regions introduce noise that distorts reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reflective object reconstruction remains a persistent gap in neural 3D reconstruction, and this paper demonstrates that bypassing the BRDF inverse problem entirely—by encoding geometry in a form that polarization can directly supervise—yields substantially better results than the current state of the art. The Gaussian normal representation is a reusable geometric encoding that captures anisotropic detail beyond what scalar SDF gradients or isotropic normal-distribution terms provide. However, the method is constrained by the need for polarimetric hardware (which has lower image quality than standard RGB cameras), fails in self-occluded/shaded regions, and has not been accelerated with modern techniques like Instant-NGP, limiting its practical adoption. The PolRef dataset, while a useful contribution, is small (8 scenes) and the quantitative evaluation rests on only 4 scenes with ground truth, leaving open whether the large margins generalize to more diverse reflective materials and complex scenes.
