## Problem

Generalist robots must recognize and manipulate novel objects and scenes absent from their training data, yet existing language-conditioned policies either lack semantic grounding for unseen objects or fail at low-level precision (imprecise grasps, poor localization) even when they understand the task. Prior approaches that incorporate pretrained vision-language models improve high-level visual generalization but do not translate that knowledge into precise low-level control, and video-prediction-based planners (e.g., UniPi) produce temporally inconsistent frames that confuse the low-level controller.

## Input / Output

At test time, the system takes a 256×256 RGB robot observation and a natural language command, and iteratively produces a 256×256 subgoal image (a hypothetical future frame k_test steps ahead) followed by chunks of 4 robot actions executed by the goal-conditioned policy. The subgoal generation model is action-agnostic—it never receives robot actions as input—while the low-level policy is language-agnostic, conditioning only on the current observation and the generated subgoal image. This decoupling means the diffusion model need not model robot dynamics, and the policy need not interpret language.

## Method

SuSIE decomposes language-guided manipulation into two independently trained phases. Phase I finetunes InstructPix2Pix (a text-guided image-editing diffusion model) on a mixture of robot rollouts and human manipulation videos (Something-Something), training it to output a single future subgoal frame k_min to k_max steps ahead given the current frame and language command, using classifier-free guidance on both the image and text conditions. Phase II trains a diffusion-based goal-conditioned policy (ResNet-50 encoder + MLP diffusion head predicting 4-action chunks with temporal ensembling) via goal-conditioned behavioral cloning on robot data, where goals are sampled within k_max + k_δ steps. At test time, the system alternates between sampling a new subgoal from the diffusion model and rolling out the goal-reaching policy for k_test steps, creating a hierarchical loop where Internet-scale semantic knowledge guides a simple visuo-motor controller that only needs to reach nearby image targets.

## Results

On the CALVIN benchmark (zero-shot: train A,B,C → test D), SuSIE achieves an average of 2.69 completed instructions out of 5, nearly doubling the previous best (AugLC at 1.43) and achieving 0.87/0.69/0.49/0.38/0.26 success rates for 1–5 chained instructions. On real-world manipulation across three scenes of increasing difficulty, SuSIE achieves average success rates of 0.87 (Scene A), 0.50 (Scene B), and 0.88 (Scene C), outperforming RT-2-X (0.43/0.00/0.75) — a 55B-parameter model trained on over 1.1M trajectories — by a wide margin, particularly in Scene B where SuSIE is the only method that can consistently grasp the bell pepper.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuSIE decisively outperforms all CALVIN baselines: it completes 2.69 instructions on average vs. 1.43 for AugLC [50], 1.38 for MdetrLC [33], 1.31 for LCBC, 0.92 for UniPi [16], 0.62 for HULC [44], 0.20 for MCIL [41], and 0.12 for UniPi(HiP) [2]. On real-world tasks, SuSIE (0.87/0.50/0.88 across Scenes A/B/C) beats RT-2-X [14] (0.43/0.00/0.75) in every scene, with the largest gap in Scene B where RT-2-X achieves 0% and SuSIE reaches 50%. SuSIE also substantially outperforms MOO [57] (0.47/0.05/0.10) despite MOO receiving ground-truth bounding boxes at test time, and UniPi [16] (0.00/0.05/0.05) which suffers from temporally inconsistent video generation. The paper further shows SuSIE beats an Oracle GCBC baseline (0.83/0.05/0.78) that receives privileged ground-truth final goal images, confirming that subgoal guidance itself improves low-level precision beyond what semantic understanding alone provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuSIE builds on InstructPix2Pix as a pretrained image-editing diffusion model and Diffusion Policy as the low-level goal-reaching controller, but the key architectural departure is the hierarchical decomposition: rather than using VLMs to directly produce actions (RT-2) or augment training data (GenAug, ROSIE), or generating full video sequences (UniPi, HiP), it uses the image-editing model solely to synthesize a single next subgoal frame. This decouples semantic reasoning (Phase I, finetuned on Internet-scale video) from visuo-motor execution (Phase II, trained on robot data), and avoids the physical-consistency burden that full video generation places on the generative model. The approach also departs from classical model-based RL by not conditioning the dynamics model on actions, enabling independent training of the large diffusion model and the policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that subgoal generation improves low-level precision beyond what semantic understanding alone provides—validated by beating an oracle goal-conditioned policy with privileged ground-truth goals—reframes the value of hierarchical planning in robotics as a precision mechanism, not just a semantic one. The data efficiency result (60k trajectories beating RT-2-X's 1.1M+ trajectories and 55B parameters) suggests that architectural choices about where to inject pretrained knowledge matter as much as scale. Key limitations include the separate training of the diffusion model and policy (the model is unaware of the policy's actual capabilities) and the bottleneck on low-level policy performance, pointing toward joint training or capability-aware subgoal generation as promising next steps.
