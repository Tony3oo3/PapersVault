## Problem

Greedy outlier sampling strategies for OOD detection select hard negatives based solely on predictive uncertainty, which biases the sampled set toward certain classes or domains and fails to capture the full auxiliary OOD distribution. This results in a locally compact but globally suboptimal decision boundary. The paper empirically demonstrates that diversity in outlier selection is a critical, previously underexplored factor for OOD detection performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-10) and an auxiliary unlabeled OOD training pool (e.g., TI-300K, INRC, or IN-990) that is substantially larger than the ID training set. At each mini-batch iteration, a candidate subset of outliers is drawn from the pool, clustered, and the most informative sample per cluster is selected for training. At test time, the model outputs a binary ID/OOD decision via a scoring function (absent category probability or energy score) compared against a threshold calibrated to 95% TPR on ID data.

## Method

DOS (Diverse Outlier Sampling) operates at each training iteration by first extracting normalized penultimate-layer features of a candidate outlier mini-batch, then partitioning them into k clusters via K-means (where k equals the batch size). From each cluster, the single most informative outlier—defined as the one with the highest absent category probability, i.e., closest to the ID/OOD decision boundary—is selected for training with absent category loss. Feature normalization before clustering is essential to prevent high-norm (confident) features from dominating the Euclidean distance metric and degrading cluster quality. A mini-batch scheme splits the full auxiliary pool into sequential groups to keep clustering tractable, and the strategy is orthogonal to the choice of regularization term, working equally well with energy loss. The resulting diverse and informative outlier subset shapes a globally compact decision boundary, reducing average FPR95 by up to 25.79% over greedy baselines on CIFAR-100 with TI-300K.

## Results

On the CIFAR-100 benchmark with TI-300K as auxiliary OOD data, DOS achieves an average FPR95 of 24.4% and average AUROC of 95.2%, reducing FPR95 by 25.79% over the greedy NTOM baseline (50.2%). On the larger INRC auxiliary dataset, DOS further reduces average FPR95 to 3.2% (AUROC 99.3%), outperforming the strongest competitor POEM (6.9%) by 3.7%. On the large-scale ImageNet-10/IN-990 benchmark, DOS achieves 20.71% average FPR95 and 96.76% AUROC, surpassing OE (23.78%) by 3.07%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOS outperforms all 14 compared baselines across every benchmark. The most dramatic win is against NTOM (greedy/ATOM sampling, Chen et al. 2021): on CIFAR-100 with TI-300K, DOS reduces average FPR95 from 50.2% to 24.4% (−25.8%), and on INRC from 8.0% to 3.2% (−4.8%). Against the strongest OE-based competitor POEM (Ming et al. 2022), DOS improves FPR95 by 3.7% on INRC (6.9%→3.2%) and by 31.3% on TI-300K (55.7%→24.4%). Compared to random sampling (Share, Bitterwolf et al. 2022), DOS gains 4.4% FPR95 on INRC and 15.5% on TI-300K. Against the original OE method (Hendrycks et al. 2019), DOS improves FPR95 by 6.8% on INRC and 22.7% on TI-300K. All post-hoc methods (MSP, ODIN, Maha, Energy score, ReAct, DICE) are substantially outperformed, with DOS achieving 24.4% vs. 49.7–83.0% average FPR95 on TI-300K. On the large-scale ImageNet-10 benchmark, DOS beats OE by 3.07% FPR95 and NTOM by 31.8%, confirming consistent superiority across dataset scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the outlier exposure (OE) paradigm for OOD detection, building directly on ATOM/NTOM (Chen et al., 2021), which selects hard-negative outliers via greedy uncertainty-based sampling, and OE (Hendrycks et al., 2019), which introduces the absent category loss. The key conceptual departure is the identification of a bias in uncertainty-only sampling: it concentrates outliers in specific feature-space regions, yielding a locally but not globally compact decision boundary. DOS addresses this by adding a diversity constraint through K-means clustering on normalized penultimate-layer features, selecting the most informative outlier from each cluster rather than the globally hardest negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25.79% FPR95 reduction on CIFAR-100/TI-300K over the greedy NTOM baseline is substantial, and the method's orthogonality to the choice of regularization loss (absent category, energy) and its robustness to auxiliary dataset scale make it a practical drop-in improvement for OE-based pipelines. The demonstration that DOS can boost pre-trained CLIP ViT performance suggests OE-based sampling strategies remain relevant in the large-model era. Open questions include whether the clustering-based diversity constraint generalizes to settings without a large auxiliary OOD pool, and whether the approach transfers to non-image modalities or open-world detection without any auxiliary data.
