## Problem

Diffusion models are typically trained to accept a single conditioning modality (e.g., text), and adapting them to other control signals like segmentation maps, face identities, or object locations requires expensive retraining. The fundamental obstacle to using arbitrary off-the-shelf guidance functions during diffusion sampling is the domain shift: guidance networks are trained on clean images but must operate on noisy latent states, causing them to produce meaningless gradients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242003/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen diffusion model (Stable Diffusion or unconditional ImageNet diffusion), a differentiable guidance function f (e.g., a segmentation network, face recognizer, object detector, or CLIP feature extractor), a corresponding loss ℓ, and a guidance prompt c (segmentation map, face image, bounding boxes with class labels, or style image). An optional text prompt can be supplied as standard conditioning to the diffusion model. The output is a single generated image that simultaneously satisfies the text description and the guidance constraint, with no retraining of either the diffusion model or the guidance function.

## Method

The key insight is that the DDIM-predicted clean image ẑ₀ (Eq. 3), while imperfect, is sufficiently realistic for off-the-shelf guidance functions to provide informative feedback, thereby closing the clean-to-noisy domain gap without retraining. Forward universal guidance computes the gradient of ℓ(c, f(ẑ₀)) with respect to the noisy state z_t through the ẑ₀ computation, replacing the standard noise prediction with a guided version (Eq. 7). Backward universal guidance supplements this by performing m steps of gradient descent in clean space to find Δz₀ that minimizes ℓ(c, f(ẑ₀ + Δ)), then linearly translating this change back to noisy space (Eq. 10), prioritizing constraint satisfaction over manifold preservation. A third component, Universal Stepwise Refinement (USR), re-noises the sampled z_{t-1} back to noise level t and repeats the full guidance loop k times before advancing, empirically improving image realism without sacrificing constraint adherence. The three components are combined in a single sampling step (Algorithm 1) and can be applied with multiple guidance functions simultaneously.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion, the universal guidance algorithm achieves strong constraint satisfaction across all tested guidance modalities: mIoU of 0.898 for segmentation guidance, face similarity of 0.642 (above the 0.5 same-person threshold) for face recognition guidance, and mAP@50 of 0.634 for object detection guidance, while maintaining CLIP text-prompt similarity within ~0.02 of the unguided baseline. On the unconditional ImageNet model, combining forward and backward guidance dramatically improves object location accuracy (mAP@50 from 0.39 to 0.90 for bounding box 1, and from 0.18 to 0.36 for bounding box 2) compared to forward guidance alone. On DrawBench, universal guidance with CLIP achieves a CLIP score of 0.2632, close to Stable Diffusion's native text-conditional generation at 0.2818.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline comparison is against Stable Diffusion's native text-conditional generation on DrawBench (20 randomly selected prompts, 10 images each). Universal Guidance with CLIP achieves a CLIP score of 0.2632 versus 0.2818 for the dedicated text-conditional Stable Diffusion model, a gap of ~0.019. The authors note this is achieved without any additional training of the underlying diffusion model, whereas Stable Diffusion was specifically trained for text conditioning. Qualitatively (Fig. 9), the guided images are described as comparable to the conditional model's outputs. The paper also implicitly compares against prior guidance methods: classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) requires retraining the classifier on noisy inputs, while the proposed method works with any off-the-shelf clean-image model. On ImageNet, the ablation in Table 2 shows that forward guidance alone produces mismatched object categories and locations (mAP@50 = 0.39), while adding backward guidance nearly triples accuracy (mAP@50 = 0.90), validating the necessity of the backward component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) by replacing the requirement for a classifier trained on noisy images with evaluation of any off-the-shelf guidance function on the DDIM-predicted clean image (ẑ₀), thereby closing the domain gap between noisy latents and clean-image-trained networks without retraining. It departs from the classifier-free guidance paradigm (Ho & Salimans, 2022), which requires retraining the diffusion model with a fixed conditioning ontology, by treating the diffusion model as a frozen foundation and injecting guidance at sampling time. The backward guidance component and Universal Stepwise Refinement are novel additions that address the over-prioritization of image realism and manifold drift observed with forward guidance alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single frozen diffusion model can be steered by arbitrary off-the-shelf networks (segmentation, face recognition, object detection, CLIP) without any fine-tuning, lowering the barrier to controlled generation for end users. Key limitations include per-task hyperparameter tuning (guidance strength s(t) and refinement steps k), increased sampling time from the USR loop, and the computational cost of backward guidance when the guidance function is a large network. Open questions include whether the ẑ₀ approximation degrades at very high noise levels, how the method scales to video or 3D generation, and whether automatic hyperparameter selection can replace the manual tuning currently required.
