## Problem

Standard conformal prediction controls only binary miscoverage—whether the true label falls inside the prediction set—but many practical tasks (multilabel classification, segmentation, open-domain QA) have natural error metrics such as false negative rate, graph distance, or token-level F1-score that are not indicator functions. This paper asks whether the distribution-free, finite-sample guarantees of split conformal prediction can be extended to control the expected value of any monotone loss function, not just miscoverage.

## Input / Output

Given a pre-trained model f, a calibration set of n feature-response pairs, a monotone bounded loss ℓ(C_λ(x), y) ∈ (−∞, B], and a target risk level α, the algorithm outputs a single calibrated threshold λ̂ that defines nested prediction sets C_λ̂(x) for all future inputs. The loss must be non-increasing in λ (larger threshold → more conservative set → lower loss) and right-continuous; the guarantee is on the joint expectation over both calibration and test randomness rather than a high-probability bound conditional on the calibration data.

## Method

The algorithm sets λ̂ = inf{λ : (n/(n+1))R̂_n(λ) + B/(n+1) ≤ α}, where R̂_n(λ) is the empirical risk on the calibration set and B is the worst-case loss bound. The key insight is that replacing the unknown test-point loss L_{n+1}(λ) with its worst-case value B in the empirical average yields a valid upper bound on the full (n+1)-point risk; exchangeability then ensures the test-point risk is controlled in expectation, since the test point is equally likely to be any of the n+1 points. The proof is a direct generalization of the conformal prediction argument and the guarantee is tight up to a 2B/(n+1) factor, with the algorithm reducing exactly to standard split conformal prediction when the loss is an indicator. Extensions cover distribution shift via importance weighting, quantile risk control, multiple simultaneous risks, adversarial perturbations, and U-statistic losses for ranking and metric learning.

## Results

Across four application domains, conformal risk control achieves risk almost exactly at the target level α. On tumor segmentation (pooled Kvasir/Hyper-Kvasir/CVC datasets, n=1000, α=0.1), the mean FNR over 1000 random splits is 0.0987 (std 0.0114). On MS COCO multilabel classification (n=4000, α=0.1), mean FNR is 0.0996 (std 0.0052). On hierarchical ImageNet (n=30000, α=0.05), mean graph distance is 0.0499 (std 0.0011). On Natural Questions open-domain QA (n=2500, α=0.3), mean F1 loss is 0.2996 (std 0.0150). In all cases the risk is tightly controlled with narrow variance, confirming the O(1/n) tightness guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares conformal risk control against two versions of the RCPS/LTT high-probability risk control framework (Bates et al., 2021; Angelopoulos et al., 2021a) on the polyp segmentation dataset (Figure 5, Appendix B). Baseline 1 uses RCPS with δ=0.5, while Baseline 2 integrates the LTT tail bound to recover an expectation guarantee. Conformal risk control is 50% closer to the target risk α than Baseline 1 and orders of magnitude closer than Baseline 2, consistent with its 1/n convergence rate versus the 1/√n rate of RCPS/LTT. Computationally, conformal risk control is 14× faster than Baseline 1 and 19% faster than Baseline 2, and requires only ~5 lines of code versus the complex discretization and multiple-testing correction needed by LTT. The paper emphasizes that RCPS/LTT provide high-probability (over calibration data) guarantees requiring an additional parameter δ, whereas conformal risk control gives a simpler expectation guarantee with a single parameter α.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends split conformal prediction (Papadopoulos et al., 2002; Vovk et al., 2005) from controlling binary miscoverage to controlling the expected value of any monotone loss function, unifying FNR, graph distance, and F1-score control under a single framework. It explicitly departs from the high-probability risk control line (RCPS/LTT: Bates et al., 2021; Angelopoulos et al., 2021a), which relies on concentration inequalities and converges at a 1/√n rate, by providing a simpler expectation-based guarantee with 1/n convergence. The distribution shift extension generalizes Tibshirani et al. (2019) beyond indicator losses, and the U-risk extension generalizes the two-point losses considered in Bates et al. (2021) to arbitrary k-point U-statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, 5-line-of-code tool for safety-critical deployment across CV and NLP, with guarantees that are tight up to O(1/n) and empirically almost exactly at the target α across four diverse applications. Two key limitations remain: the monotone loss requirement is difficult to lift (non-monotone losses can violate risk control by an arbitrary amount), and distribution shift extensions require knowledge of the shift form. Open questions include whether the broader class of quantile-based risks (e.g., CVaR) can be controlled unconditionally, and whether the asymptotic monotonization procedure for non-monotone losses provides finite-sample guarantees.
