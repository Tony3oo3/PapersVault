## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion and DALL-E 2 struggle to generate human images with coherent anatomy and natural poses because humans are articulated with nonrigid deformations that require structural information text prompts cannot convey. Prior controllable methods either rely on a single control signal (e.g., pose only) or treat different structural cues as independent guidance, failing to model the multi-level correlations between human appearance and structure from coarse skeleton to fine-grained geometry.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and a 2D body skeleton (rendered as a keypoint map on a black canvas, then encoded by the same VAE as the image latents) as input. The first stage simultaneously outputs a 512×512 RGB image, depth map, and surface-normal map; the second stage refines the RGB to 1024×1024 using the predicted structural maps as additional conditions. Users can optionally substitute their own depth or surface-normal maps into the refiner for more flexible control, and the model also supports unconditional generation (no pose) thanks to its random dropout training scheme.

## Method

HyperHuman is a two-stage pipeline built on Stable Diffusion. The first stage, a Latent Structural Diffusion Model, extends the SD-2.0 U-Net with structural expert branches: the conv_in, first DownBlock, last UpBlock, and conv_out are replicated for each of the three modalities (RGB, depth, surface-normal), while the middle shared backbone fuses features across branches. All three targets are denoised simultaneously with the same timestep t using v-prediction and a zero-terminal SNR schedule (α_T=0, σ_T=1) to prevent low-frequency leakage from the monotonous structural maps. The second stage, a Structure-Guided Refiner built on SDXL-1.0, composes the predicted depth, normal, pose, and text into a single conditioning signal via lightweight 4-layer convolutional embedders summed coordinate-wise, with 50% random dropout of conditions to mitigate error accumulation between stages. The entire system is trained on the newly curated HumanVerse dataset of 340M in-the-wild human images annotated with pose, depth, and surface-normal.

## Results

On the zero-shot MS-COCO 2014 validation human subset (8,236 images at 512×512), HyperHuman achieves the best image quality with FID 17.18 (25.2% lower than the second-best SD2.0 at 22.98), KID×1k 4.11 (48.5% lower than T2I-Adapter at 7.98), and FID_CLIP 7.82 (31.5% lower than SD2.0 at 11.41). It also leads on all four pose accuracy metrics: AP 30.38, AR 37.84, AP_clean 38.84, and AR_clean 48.70. On text-image alignment (CLIP score), HyperHuman scores 32.17, ranking second only to SDXL (32.90), which uses a 3× larger UNet with two text encoders. In user preference studies, HyperHuman is preferred over T2I-Adapter 98.06% of the time and over HumanSD 99.08% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperHuman outperforms all general T2I models (SD1.5/2.0/2.1, SDXL, DeepFloyd-IF) on image quality metrics by a wide margin, achieving FID 17.18 versus the best general model SD2.0 at 22.98, while matching SDXL's CLIP alignment score (32.17 vs. 32.90) despite using a much smaller backbone. Among controllable methods, HyperHuman beats T2I-Adapter (the strongest baseline) on FID (17.18 vs. 23.54), KID (4.11 vs. 7.98), FID_CLIP (7.82 vs. 11.95), and all four pose accuracy metrics (e.g., AP_clean 38.84 vs. 34.86). It also substantially outperforms ControlNet on every metric (FID 17.18 vs. 27.16; AP 30.38 vs. 20.46) and HumanSD, which suffers from the worst image quality among controllable methods (FID 52.49, KID 33.96) due to its artistic-style limitation. The only metric where HyperHuman does not rank first is CLIP text-image alignment, where SDXL's dual text encoders and 3× larger UNet give it a slight edge (32.90 vs. 32.17), though HyperHuman surpasses all other baselines with comparable text encoder capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion model paradigm (Stable Diffusion 2.0 for the first stage, SDXL 1.0 for the refiner) by introducing structural expert branches that simultaneously denoise RGB, depth, and surface-normal within a shared backbone. It departs from plug-and-play control methods like ControlNet and T2I-Adapter, which treat structural signals as external guidance and suffer from feature discrepancy between branches, and from HumanSD, which uses channel-wise concatenation but is confined to artistic styles. The key conceptual departure is modeling multi-level human structure (coarse skeleton to fine-grained geometry) as joint denoising targets rather than separate conditioning signals, enabling the network to learn complementary relationships between appearance and geometry natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 340M-image HumanVerse dataset with comprehensive multi-modal annotations (pose, depth, surface-normal, captions) is one of the earliest large-scale human-centric resources for generation foundation models, potentially benefiting a wide range of downstream tasks. The structural expert branch architecture is generalizable to arbitrary numbers of paired modalities with minimal overhead, making it a reusable design pattern. However, the pipeline still requires an explicit pose skeleton input, and fine-grained details (fingers, eyes) remain limited by the quality of off-the-shelf structure estimators. Open questions include whether text-to-pose generation via LLMs can eliminate the skeleton input requirement, and whether the joint denoising approach transfers to other structured domains beyond humans.
