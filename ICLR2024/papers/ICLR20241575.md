## Problem

Discriminative prompting for zero-shot text classification (predicting the label given the input) is brittle to prompt phrasing, suffers from surface form competition, and requires careful verbalizer design. In-context learning mitigates some of these issues but demands labor-intensive demonstration selection and remains sensitive to example order and format. The paper asks whether explicitly priming the language model with contextual information in natural language can match or exceed ICL performance in a fully zero-shot setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw text to classify and a set of natural language label descriptions that embed contextual variables (e.g., data source like 'Reddit', subject like 'race', author demographics like gender or age). For each candidate label, 1–10 paraphrased descriptions are evaluated, and the model computes the conditional probability of generating the input text given each description. The output is the label whose aggregated generative likelihood is highest. No training data, demonstrations, or fine-tuning are required; only the label descriptions (hand-written templates expanded via ChatGPT paraphrasing) are needed per task.

## Method

GEN-Z reframes text classification as a multivariate generative (noisy-channel) problem: instead of scoring p(z_i | x) as in discriminative prompting, it scores p(x | z_i) where z_i is a natural language description of label y_i enriched with contextual variables (domain, source, subject, author/audience attributes). The probabilistic derivation treats labels and context as jointly generating a description z, which then conditions text generation; the inference objective reduces to summing p(x | z_i) over all paraphrases z_i of each label's description. To reduce variance from phrasing choices, the method aggregates (sums) the generative probabilities across multiple paraphrases of each label description. The approach is grounded in a Dirac-delta simplification of the Bayesian in-context learning formulation, where the latent concept variable θ* is explicitly specified in natural language rather than inferred from demonstrations, eliminating the need for any training examples.

## Results

On GPT-J (6B), GEN-Z achieves 91.7 (±0.2) macro-F1 on SST-2, 89.9 (±0.1) on Yelp-2, 77.0 (±0.1) on AG News, and 62.6 (±0.3) on Hate Speech 2018, consistently outperforming all zero-shot baselines. Compared to the strongest few-shot baseline (ICL-DC, 8/16-shot), GEN-Z matches or exceeds performance on hate speech (62.6 vs 57.3 on HS18) and Ethos subsets (70.1 vs 43.8 on Religion; 56.3 vs 40.7 on National Origin) while trailing on some sentiment tasks (87.0 vs 93.1 on MR). GEN-Z exhibits the lowest variance across all methods (e.g., ±0.2 on SST-2 vs ±6.0 for ICL-Disc), demonstrating substantially improved robustness to prompt variation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the generative noisy-channel baseline of Min et al. (2022a) (GS-N), GEN-Z improves by 11.7 points on SST-2 (91.7 vs 80.0), 13.8 on Yelp-2 (89.9 vs 76.1), 12.1 on AG News (77.0 vs 64.9), and 12.4 on HS18 (62.6 vs 50.2), confirming that contextualized label descriptions and multi-paraphrase aggregation are the key gains. Against the PMI-calibrated discriminative baseline (Holtzman et al., 2021; DPM), GEN-Z leads by 19.4 points on SST-2 (91.7 vs 72.3) and 12.1 on AG News (77.0 vs 72.0). Against the strongest few-shot baseline ICL-DC (Fei et al., 2023b), GEN-Z trails on sentiment (87.0 vs 93.1 on MR; 91.7 vs 94.0 on SST-2) but substantially outperforms on hate speech and pragmatic tasks (62.6 vs 57.3 on HS18; 70.1 vs 43.8 on Ethos Religion; 56.3 vs 40.7 on Ethos NO). Against Z-ICL (Lyu et al., 2023), GEN-Z wins on nearly all tasks (e.g., 91.7 vs 82.6 on SST-2; 62.6 vs 51.1 on HS18) while requiring no access to an unlabeled corpus. Against standard ICL (Brown et al., 2020; ICL-Disc), GEN-Z matches or exceeds on most tasks (91.7 vs 91.0 on SST-2; 62.6 vs 51.5 on HS18; 70.1 vs 30.7 on Ethos Religion) with dramatically lower variance (±0.2 vs ±6.0 on SST-2).
