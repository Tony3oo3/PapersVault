## Problem

Zero-shot pretrained models inherit spurious correlations from their training data (e.g., associating waterbirds with water backgrounds), causing severe performance drops on rare data slices. Existing robustness methods require labeled data, fine-tuning, or manual identification of biases, undermining the out-of-the-box advantage of zero-shot models. The paper asks whether zero-shot models can be robustified without any labels, training, or manual specification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241350/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely on frozen pretrained model embeddings (e.g., CLIP ViT-B-32, ALIGN, AltCLIP, BERT, Ada) and requires only a short task description (e.g., 'classify landbirds vs. waterbirds') to query a language model for harmful and helpful concept pairs. The LM's textual answers are embedded via the model's own text encoder to produce insight vectors in the same latent space as the input and class embeddings. The output is a standard class label, but computed from modified input embeddings after vector rejection and amplification steps. No pixel-level or token-level input is modified; only the embedding-space geometry is altered.

## Method

ROBOSHOT extracts noisy concept insights from a language model (potentially the same model being improved) by prompting with the task description, then embeds these insights using the model's text encoder to form harmful and helpful direction vectors in the latent space. It applies orthogonal vector rejection to remove harmful components from input embeddings (projecting x onto the orthogonal complement of each harmful insight vector) and vector addition to amplify helpful components, all without any gradient-based training. Theoretically, the paper provides bounds showing that harmful coefficients scale down at a rate inversely proportional to the insight signal-to-noise ratio, while helpful coefficients are scaled up by a factor approaching 2× under perfect identification. An optional Label-Free Adaptation (LFA) variant learns a projection matrix via a simple dot-product loss using only 100 validation samples, initialized from the ROBOSHOT projection, to further maximize robustness.

## Results

ROBOSHOT achieves an average 15.98% improvement in worst-group accuracy (WG) across nine image and NLP classification tasks, with only a trivial decrease in overall average accuracy (AVG). On the Waterbirds dataset with CLIP (ViT-B-32), WG jumps from 27.9% (vanilla zero-shot) to 54.4% (ROBOSHOT), while AVG actually improves from 80.7% to 82.0%. For text classification, ROBOSHOT lifts BERT's WG on CivilComments from 33.3% to 42.3% and on HateXplain from 0.0% to 14.0%, outperforming both direct prompting baselines (BART-MNLI and ChatGPT) on all four NLP datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BART-MNLI direct prompting, ROBOSHOT (using BERT embeddings) wins decisively on all four NLP datasets: CivilComments WG 42.3% vs 15.7%, HateXplain WG 14.0% vs 5.3%, Amazon WG 64.4% vs 36.0%, and Gender Bias WG 84.9% vs 78.4%. Against ChatGPT direct prompting, ROBOSHOT with BERT embeddings also wins on all four datasets (e.g., CivilComments WG 42.3% vs 19.2%), and with Ada embeddings it wins on CivilComments (44.9% vs 19.2%) and HateXplain (21.1% vs 12.2%) but loses on Amazon (63.8% vs 73.5%) and Gender Bias (60.1% vs 86.6%), indicating that for some tasks a strong LLM's direct reasoning still outperforms embedding-space manipulation. Against the Holtzman et al. calibration method, ROBOSHOT generally achieves higher WG (e.g., CivilComments BERT: 42.3% vs 37.3%; Amazon BERT: 64.4% vs 57.7%), and the two methods are complementary—their combination further improves results in most cases (e.g., HateXplain Ada: WG 33.3% for the combination vs 21.1% for ROBOSHOT alone). On the multimodal side, ROBOSHOT outperforms the Group Prompt ZS baseline (which requires manual spurious-annotation access) on most dataset-model pairs, e.g., Waterbirds CLIP ViT-B-32 WG 54.4% vs 43.5%, while Group Prompt occasionally wins (e.g., Waterbirds CLIP ViT-L-14: 10.4% vs 45.2%—here ROBOSHOT actually wins by a large margin; the one case where Group Prompt wins is ALIGN on Waterbirds at 5.8% vs 41.0%—actually ROBOSHOT still wins). The key advantage is that ROBOSHOT requires no spurious-annotation access, no training, and no labeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROBOSHOT sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: zero-shot model robustness (Yang et al. 2023, Goyal et al. 2022, Zhang & Re 2022), which improves worst-group accuracy but requires labeled data and fine-tuning, and embedding debiasing (Bolukbasi et al. 2016, Dev & Phillips 2019, Lauscher et al. 2020), which removes unwanted subspaces but requires manual concept specification. The paper repurposes the vector-rejection mechanism from debiasing but automates concept discovery by querying language models with only the task description, thereby achieving the goals of both lines without their respective costs. It also relates to concurrent work on debiasing CLIP classifiers via biased prompts (Chuang et al. 2023) and to LM-as-prior approaches (Choi et al. 2022, Kiciman et al. 2023), but distinguishes itself by operating entirely in the embedding space rather than modifying prompts or classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that ROBOSHOT offers a drop-in, training-free robustness improvement for any frozen pretrained model (CLIP, ALIGN, AltCLIP, BiomedCLIP, BERT, Ada) with only a task description as input, making it broadly deployable in settings where labeling or fine-tuning is infeasible. The 15.98% average worst-group accuracy gain across nine datasets, sometimes matching fine-tuned baselines, is compelling for practitioners. Limitations include the assumption of separable harmful/helpful/benign subspaces (which fails for ALIGN on Waterbirds), the theoretical analysis being restricted to binary classification, and the method's dependence on the quality of LM-generated insights. Open questions include extending the theory to multi-class settings, understanding when intertwined concepts cause failures, and whether the approach generalizes to non-orthogonal concept geometries.
