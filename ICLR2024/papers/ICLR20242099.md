## Problem

GNN expressiveness has been assessed primarily through the Weisfeiler-Lehman hierarchy, which is inherently coarse, qualitative, and poorly aligned with practical requirements such as the ability to encode substructures. The paper asks a more fundamental question: what is the complete set of substructures that a given GNN architecture can encode, and how can this be measured quantitatively to enable direct comparisons between models?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242099/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a GNN architecture definition (MPNN, Subgraph GNN, Local 2-GNN, Local 2-FGNN, 2-FGNN, and their higher-order k-variants) and produces a complete graph family F^M — the exact set of substructures the model can count under homomorphism — described via nested ear decomposition (NED) variants. For empirical validation, the models take vertex-labeled graphs as input and produce graph-level, node-level, and edge-level representations, which are then tested on homomorphism/subgraph counting tasks and real-world benchmarks (ZINC, Alchemy) under a fixed 500K parameter budget.

## Method

The paper introduces homomorphism expressivity: a GNN model M can count a substructure F under homomorphism if its graph representation determines hom(F,G) for all G. The key technical contribution is characterizing the resulting graph family F^M using nested ear decomposition (NED) and its variants: Subgraph GNN corresponds to endpoint-shared NED, Local 2-GNN to strong NED, Local 2-FGNN to almost-strong NED, and 2-FGNN to general NED. The proof proceeds in three parts — establishing existence via tree decomposition and unfolding trees, translating to NED descriptions, and proving maximality via a novel connection between tree decomposition, the pebble game, and Fürer graphs. The framework extends naturally to node/edge-level expressivity (by specifying endpoints of the first ear) and to higher-order GNNs (via k-order ears and treewidth), yielding a complete expressiveness hierarchy and exact subgraph-counting characterizations for all studied architectures.

## Results

On the synthetic homomorphism counting task (Table 1), model performance aligns precisely with the theoretical expressivity hierarchy: MPNN achieves MAE of 0.300–0.505 (essentially random), while Local 2-FGNN achieves 0.003–0.008 across all 8 substructures. On real-world benchmarks (Table 2), Local 2-FGNN achieves the best MAE on all three tasks: 0.064±0.002 on ZINC-subset, 0.023±0.001 on ZINC-full, and 0.111±0.001 on Alchemy, outperforming MPNN (0.138/0.030/0.122) by 54%/23%/9% respectively. On the cycle counting task (Table 3), Local 2-GNN and Local 2-FGNN achieve MAE of 0.001–0.022 for 3/4/5/6-cycles at all levels, while Subgraph GNN degrades to 0.090–0.096 for 5/6-cycles at edge-level, confirming the theoretical prediction that Local 2-GNN can count all cycles/paths within 7 nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four GNN architectures (MPNN, Subgraph GNN, Local 2-GNN, Local 2-FGNN) under identical GIN-based design with matched parameter budgets (~500K). Local 2-FGNN consistently achieves the best MAE across all three real-world benchmarks, beating MPNN by 54% on ZINC-subset (0.064 vs 0.138) and 9% on Alchemy (0.111 vs 0.122). Local 2-GNN is a close second, within 0.001–0.005 of Local 2-FGNN on all tasks, confirming that the expressivity gap between strong NED and almost-strong NED is small in practice. Subgraph GNN (endpoint-shared NED) trails Local 2-GNN by 38% on ZINC-subset (0.110 vs 0.069), validating the theoretical strict inclusion F^Sub ⊊ F^L. On the synthetic homomorphism counting task, the performance ordering exactly matches the theoretical hierarchy: MPNN fails on all substructures (MAE > 0.23), Subgraph GNN fails on 5 of 8, Local 2-GNN fails on 2 of 8, and Local 2-FGNN succeeds on all 8, providing direct empirical confirmation of Theorem 3.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lovász–Dvořák–Dell theory (which characterizes WL distinguishability via homomorphism counts over bounded-treewidth patterns) to a broad range of practical GNN architectures, replacing the inapplicable treewidth characterization with novel variants of nested ear decomposition (NED) from Eppstein (1992). It departs from the standard WL-hierarchy yardstick—which the authors argue is too coarse and qualitative—by introducing homomorphism expressivity as a complete, quantitative measure that simultaneously captures subgraph counting power, polynomial expressivity, and qualitative distinguishability. The framework unifies results scattered across the Subgraph GNN, Local GNN, and Folklore-type GNN literatures (Zhang et al. 2023a, Frasca et al. 2022, Qian et al. 2022, Morris et al. 2020) under a single NED-based description, and connects to the pebble game and Fürer graph constructions used in prior lower-bound proofs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The homomorphism expressivity framework provides the first complete, quantitative expressivity measure for GNNs that enables direct set-inclusion comparisons and systematic counterexample construction, settling multiple open problems from the Subgraph GNN, Local GNN, and polynomial expressivity literatures. A practically important finding is that Local 2-GNN matches 2-FWL's subgraph counting power for all cycles/paths up to 7 nodes (even at edge-level) while being far more efficient, suggesting it as a strong default architecture for chemistry/biology tasks. Open questions remain for higher-order Local k-FGNN (no NED characterization found), distance-based and spectral GNNs, and the general existence conditions for homomorphism expressivity in arbitrary CR-based architectures.
