## Problem

Most deep learning video editing methods require large paired datasets of corrupted and clean sequences, which are impractical to collect for tasks like dehazing and relighting where replicating identical camera motion is difficult. Existing inference-time optimization alternatives (e.g., DoubleDIP) are task-specific, rely solely on appearance cues, and often underperform their data-trained counterparts. This paper asks whether a single, task-agnostic layered decomposition framework can match or exceed task-specific methods without any external training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241124/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB video sequence (processed at 856×480) and its forward optical flow, which is colorized into a flow-RGB representation (HSV hue encodes direction, value encodes magnitude) and fed to the alpha-net. Outputs are task-dependent: binary foreground/background masks for UVOS, a haze-free video for dehazing, a relit video for low-light enhancement, or per-frame edited layers for propagation. No ground-truth pairs or external training corpus are required at any stage; all network weights are optimized solely on the test video.

## Method

VDP decomposes a video into L RGB layers (predicted by shallow convolutional U-Nets called RGB-Net) and L opacity/transmission maps (predicted by a parallel U-Net called alpha-Net that takes flow-RGB as input). The key architectural insight is routing optical flow through the alpha-net rather than the RGB-net, so that motion cues guide layer separation while appearance cues guide layer content. For relighting, the paper introduces a novel logarithmic decomposition (log X_in = γ⁻¹(log(1/A_t) + log X_out)) that replaces the standard gamma-correction formulation, with γ⁻¹ as a trainable scalar. For UVOS, additional regularization losses (flow-similarity cosine loss on VGG embeddings, binarization mask loss, and per-layer reconstruction loss) enforce motion-disjoint, binary, appearance-faithful layers. Both networks are jointly optimized per-video using Adam with L1 + VGG perceptual reconstruction loss and an optical-flow warping loss for temporal consistency.

## Results

On the primary task of unsupervised video object segmentation, VDP achieves 81.1 IOU on DAVIS-16 and 73.2 IOU on FBMS, outperforming all other inference-time optimization baselines. For video relighting on SDSD, VDP attains 27.92 PSNR and 0.90 SSIM, surpassing the best baseline (ZeroDCE++ at 26.46 PSNR) by 1.46 dB. For video dehazing on REVIDE, VDP achieves 24.83 PSNR and 0.9164 SSIM, exceeding the strongest baseline (CG-IDN at 23.21 PSNR) by 1.62 dB. Notably, VDP requires no task-specific training data, optimizing directly on the test video sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VOS (DAVIS-16), VDP (81.1 IOU) outperforms all unsupervised baselines: DoubleDIP (24.6), ARP (76.2), CIS (71.5), MG (68.3), Deformable Sprites/DS (79.1), and DyStab* (80.0), with the largest margin over DoubleDIP (+56.5) and a consistent 1–2.5 point lead over the closest competitors DS and DyStab*. On FBMS, VDP ties DyStab* at 73.2 IOU while surpassing DS (71.8) and all others. For video relighting (SDSD), VDP (27.92 PSNR / 0.90 SSIM) beats ZeroDCE++ (26.46 / 0.85), SDSD (24.92 / 0.73), and StableLLVE (26.01 / 0.84) on both metrics, with the largest gap over SDSD (+3.0 dB PSNR). For video dehazing (REVIDE), VDP (24.83 PSNR / 0.9164 SSIM) surpasses EDVR (21.22 / 0.8707), DoubleDIP (17.32 / 0.7942), MSBDN (22.01 / 0.8759), and CG-IDN (23.21 / 0.8836), with the most significant margin over DoubleDIP (+7.5 dB PSNR). A key distinction is that VDP and DoubleDIP are the only methods requiring no external training data, yet VDP substantially outperforms DoubleDIP across all three tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep internal learning / inference-time optimization paradigm (Deep Image Prior, DoubleDIP) to video by introducing a unified layered decomposition framework inspired by professional video editing. The key departure from DoubleDIP is the incorporation of FlowRGB (optical flow rendered as RGB) as input to a dedicated alpha-net, providing motion cues that prior inference-time methods lacked. It also departs from task-specific layered editing methods (Kasten et al., 2021; Lu et al., 2021) by not relying on pretrained segmentation networks or user-provided masks, and from Deformable Sprites (Ye et al., 2022) by operating frame-by-frame rather than encoding a single canonical texture. The logarithmic decomposition formulation for relighting (Eqn. 5) is a new mathematical treatment that replaces the standard gamma-correction model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single, task-agnostic layered decomposition framework with FlowRGB can match or exceed trained methods across three distinct video editing tasks (VOS, dehazing, relighting) without any task-specific training data, which is practically valuable in settings where paired training data is infeasible to collect. The ablation showing that FlowRGB is critical for the alpha-net (PSNR drops from 24.83 to ~16.8 without it for dehazing) provides a clear mechanistic insight into why motion cues matter for opacity/transmission estimation. Limitations include dependence on RAFT flow quality, a fixed number of layers as a hyperparameter, and the inherent slowness of test-time optimization compared to trained models. Open questions include whether the framework extends to higher-level tasks like monocular depth estimation or semantic segmentation, and whether the FlowRGB insight generalizes to other decomposition-based video tasks.
