## Problem

While prior work has shown that parametric knowledge within LLMs is detectable and editable, its transferability across models of different scales remains an open question. Existing knowledge transfer paradigms—online and offline distillation—rely on the teacher model's outputs or fine-tuned logits, completely overlooking the rich implicit knowledge stored in the teacher's static parameters. This paper empirically investigates whether task-specific parameters can be directly extracted from a larger teacher and injected into a smaller student to enhance downstream performance.

## Input / Output

The method requires only 32 seed samples from the task's training set to compute sensitivity scores via a single forward-backward pass on the teacher model, with no further teacher involvement during student training. Extracted parameters are resized to match the student's architecture through sensitivity-guided layer selection and submatrix extraction, then SVD-factorized to initialize a LoRA module (rank 16) inserted into the embedding, FFN, and self-attention layers. The final output is a student model fine-tuned for 3 epochs on 1,000 task-specific instances, where only the LoRA parameters are updated while the base weights and the extracted teacher submatrix remain frozen.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages: sensitivity-based extraction and LoRA-driven injection. For extraction, parameter sensitivity is computed as the first-order Taylor approximation of loss change upon zeroing each parameter, aggregated over 32 seed samples; the top L_S layers by cumulative sensitivity are selected, and within each layer the submatrix matching the student's dimensions with the highest cumulative sensitivity is extracted. For injection, each extracted matrix is SVD-factorized and its top-r singular components initialize the LoRA B and A matrices. A key design choice is the modified initialization W_i* = W_i − W_{T_i,extract,r} + B_iA_i, which ensures training begins from the student's pre-trained weights rather than the teacher's, preserving downstream task performance while still encoding the teacher's task-specific knowledge into the LoRA module.

## Results

Across four benchmarks (GSM, MMLU, Super NI, AlpacaFarm), parametric knowledge transfer consistently improves student model performance over LoRA fine-tuning alone. For LLaMA-1, transferring 30B parameters to the 7B student yields an average score of 28.63 versus 27.00 for 7B-LoRA alone (+6.04% relative), while the 13B→7B transfer achieves 28.22. For LLaMA-2, the 13B→7B transfer raises the average from 32.28 to 34.47 (+6.78% relative). The 30B→13B transfer on LLaMA-1 improves the average from 33.89 to 35.54, confirming that larger teachers provide greater gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two distillation baselines (Table 5, Appendix A.4): vanilla knowledge distillation (KD, Hinton et al. 2015) and sequence-level knowledge distillation (SeqKD, Kim & Rush 2016), all using identical LoRA fine-tuning data and hyperparameters. For the 13B→7B transfer, the proposed method achieves an average of 28.60, outperforming KD (28.17) by 0.43 points and SeqKD (28.12) by 0.48 points. For the 30B→7B transfer, the proposed method reaches 29.08, beating KD (28.34) by 0.74 points and SeqKD (28.49) by 0.59 points. The gains are most pronounced on GSM (e.g., 18.63 vs. 17.81 for KD in the 30B→7B setting) and Super NI R-L (43.08 vs. 41.96), while AlpacaFarm shows the smallest margin. Crucially, the proposed method requires only 32 teacher inferences for sensitivity computation, whereas KD and SeqKD demand the teacher model to participate in student training, making the parametric approach substantially more efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a third paradigm alongside online distillation (which requires the teacher to participate in student training) and offline distillation (which generates a dataset but ignores the teacher's parametric knowledge). It builds on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) as the injection mechanism and on sensitivity-based pruning metrics (Molchanov et al., 2017) as the extraction criterion, but repurposes both for a cross-scale transfer setting that neither was designed for. The key departure from prior work on model merging and knowledge editing is that those operate within a single model or between identical architectures, whereas this paper explicitly addresses the layer-count and dimension mismatches between differently-scaled LLMs via sensitivity-guided layer selection and submatrix extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic empirical evidence that task-specific parametric knowledge is transferable across LLMs of different scales, requiring only 32 teacher inferences — a negligible overhead compared to distillation pipelines. The finding that preserving submatrix structural integrity is critical (single-weight transfers are essentially useless) and that FFN layers carry the most transferable knowledge offers practical guidance for future parameter-efficient transfer methods. However, improvements are modest (1–2 points on average), experiments are confined to the LLaMA/LLaMA-2 family, and the method has not been validated across architecturally diverse model families, leaving open whether sensitivity-based alignment generalizes beyond shared-architecture model pairs.
