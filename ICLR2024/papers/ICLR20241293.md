## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image translation methods suffer from three coupled limitations: they require per-image manual tuning of a sensitive guidance scale parameter (condition-rigid learning), they operate globally and cannot selectively translate only a specified region of interest while preserving the surrounding context (context-free incompetence), and their black-box nature offers no intermediate symbolic outputs for user inspection or correction (system opacity). The paper reframes image translation from a monolithic 'what to translate' question into a decomposed 'where-then-what' paradigm addressed through a neuro-symbolic pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB source image and a free-form text instruction (e.g., 'change the left dog to a sheep') as input, and produces a translated image as the final output. Uniquely, the pipeline also emits human-interpretable intermediate symbols at each stage—segmentation masks, inpainted backgrounds, and position-manipulated crops—enabling step-by-step error detection and correction. The text instruction need not be precise; GPT-4 handles ambiguous or multi-step commands, and the Prompter module (BLIP) can auto-generate detailed scene descriptions, removing the dependency on human annotations.

## Method

DVP is a training-free neuro-symbolic framework built on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with two core modules. First, a condition-flexible diffusion model replaces the standard classifier-free guidance linear combination with an instance normalization guidance (Eq. 5): the unconditional noise prediction is rescaled by the conditional prediction's standard deviation and shifted by the unconditional prediction's mean, then passed through a learned 1×1 conv layer, eliminating the need for a manually tuned guidance scale w. Second, an in-context visual programming module uses GPT-4 as a planner that decomposes the instruction into a sequence of five symbolic operations (GPlan, PG, Segment, Inpaint, PM), each invoking an off-the-shelf model (Mask2Former for RoI segmentation, Repaint for background inpainting, a position manipulator for spatial DSL commands like Enlarge/Left/Swap). The program executes line-by-line with parallel operation support, and null-text optimization is performed at inference time (1000 forward + 1000 backward DDIM steps) to recover the latent noise vector before the 50-step denoising translation.

## Results

On a 100-pair benchmark spanning ImageNet, ImageNet-R, COCO, and web-sourced images, DVP achieves the best CLIP-Score (0.839) and DINO-Score (0.697) among all compared methods. In the 50-participant user study, DVP scores highest on Fidelity (4.28/5) and Quality (3.95/5), while trailing DiffuseIT (3.57) and Text2Live (3.73) slightly on Diversity (3.56/5). The closest competitor, VISPROG, lags DVP by 0.026 CLIP-Score and 0.046 DINO-Score, with a Fidelity gap of 0.24 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVP outperforms all six baselines on both CLIP-Score and DINO-Score, with the largest margins over VQGAN-CLIP (+0.090 CLIP, +0.030 DINO) and SDEdit (+0.085 CLIP, +0.055 DINO). Against the closest competitor VISPROG (Gupta & Kembhavi, 2023), DVP gains 0.026 CLIP-Score and 0.046 DINO-Score, and leads by 0.24 points on user-study Fidelity (4.28 vs. 4.04), reflecting DVP's superior preservation of instance identity and background context. Prompt2Prompt (Hertz et al., 2023) trails DVP by 0.014 CLIP-Score and 0.040 DINO-Score, and the paper's qualitative analysis (Fig. 6) shows Prompt2Prompt fails at positional reasoning (e.g., translating both pigeons instead of only the right one). DiffuseIT (Kwon & Ye, 2023) is the only method that edges out DVP on Diversity (3.57 vs. 3.56), while Text2Live (Bar-Tal et al., 2022) leads on Diversity (3.73) but lags substantially on Fidelity (3.45 vs. 4.28). In the appendix, DVP also qualitatively outperforms Instruct-Pix2Pix (Brooks et al., 2023) on translation fidelity, with the added advantage of being training-free.

## Relation to Prior Work

DVP sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) classifier-free guidance in diffusion models (Ho & Salimans, 2021; Mokady et al., 2023), from which it departs by replacing the sensitive linear-combination guidance scale w with an instance-normalization-based distribution shift; (2) cross-attention-based editing methods like Prompt-to-Prompt (Hertz et al., 2023), which it supersedes by decomposing global attention manipulation into discrete, symbolically-grounded operations; and (3) visual programming for compositional reasoning (Gupta & Kembhavi, 2023), which it extends with a GPT-4-driven planner, RoI-aware segmentation/inpainting, and position manipulation to achieve the 'where-then-what' translation paradigm. The framework is built on Stable Diffusion v1.5 as the base generator, Mask2Former for segmentation, Repaint for inpainting, and BLIP for prompt generation, orchestrated by GPT-4 as an AI agent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The neuro-symbolic decomposition of image translation into explicit, human-interpretable steps (segment → inpaint → translate → reposition) offers a practical path toward controllable and explainable editing that pure end-to-end diffusion models cannot provide, which is valuable for safety-critical and industrial applications. The instance normalization trick removing the need for per-image guidance-scale tuning is a clean, training-free improvement that could transfer to other conditional diffusion settings. However, the framework remains limited by its segmentation module's inability to handle occluded objects and poor photometric conditions, and the GPT-4 dependency introduces cost and latency concerns (~2 min per edit). Open questions include whether instance normalization generalizes beyond text-guided inversion to broader text-to-image generation pipelines, and whether the modular design can absorb more sophisticated spatial reasoning without the planner becoming a bottleneck.
