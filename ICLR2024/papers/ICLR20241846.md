## Problem

Composing multiple trained LoRAs to combine their specialized capabilities is a critical but unsolved problem: linear arithmetic composition degrades the base model's generative quality or erases individual LoRA characteristics through weight normalization, while reference tuning-based composition (e.g., Mix-of-Show) requires full model retraining and relies on manually designed masks, limiting flexibility. The paper asks how to dynamically and efficiently compose multiple trained LoRAs while preserving all their individual characteristics.

## Input / Output

MOLE takes a set of N pre-trained LoRAs (each fine-tuned on a specific concept or task) and a text prompt or task-specific input as input. In the V&L domain, it produces multi-concept images at 512×512 resolution via a diffusion model (Stable Diffusion V2.1 with DreamBooth); in the NLP domain, it produces standard text outputs (translations, NLI labels, QA answers) from FLAN-T5. A distinctive feature is the two-mode inference: MOLE can either use all LoRAs with learned gating weights, or mask out unwanted LoRAs and proportionally redistribute the remaining weights without any retraining.

## Method

MOLE treats each layer of every trained LoRA as a distinct expert and introduces a learnable gating function at each layer that computes a softmax distribution (with learnable temperature τ) over the N LoRAs based on their concatenated and normalized outputs. The final block output is the pre-trained model's output plus the gating-weighted sum of all LoRA outputs, enabling layer-wise (hierarchical) weight control rather than a single uniform weight per LoRA. During training, only the gating parameters are optimized while all LoRA and base-model weights remain frozen, keeping computational cost minimal. A gating balancing loss (negative log of the product of per-LoRA average gate probabilities) is added to the domain-specific loss to prevent the gate from collapsing onto a single dominant LoRA. The key insight motivating this design is that different layers of a LoRA encode distinct features (e.g., color vs. shape in V&L, different NLI subtasks in NLP), so per-layer weight modulation can enhance desirable traits while suppressing unfavorable ones.

## Results

In the NLP domain (FLAN-T5), MOLE achieves average scores of 26.9 BLEU on Translation, 40.3 Rouge on Struct-to-Text, 50.2 EM on Closed-Book QA, 42.2 EM on Big-Bench Hard, and 80.5 EM on NLI, consistently outperforming LoRAHub and PEMs across all five task categories. In the V&L domain (Stable Diffusion V2.1 + DreamBooth), MOLE attains 0.759 average text-alignment and 0.757 average image-alignment across 15 three-concept compositions, surpassing SVDiff (0.728 / 0.728) and normalized linear arithmetic (NLA, 0.678 / 0.694). On BBH specifically, MOLE improves over LoRAHub by 3.8 points and over PEMs by 9.0 points, the largest margin observed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **SVDiff** (Han et al., 2023) in the V&L domain, MOLE wins on text-alignment by +0.031 average (0.759 vs 0.728) and on image-alignment by +0.037 for Concept 1 (0.783 vs 0.746), though SVDiff edges out MOLE on a few individual Concept-2/Concept-3 image-alignment cells. Against **LoRAHub** (Huang et al., 2023) in NLP, MOLE wins on every task category: +1.5 BLEU on Translation, +2.1 Rouge on Struct-to-Text, +1.9 EM on Closed-Book QA, +3.8 EM on BBH, and +1.3 EM on NLI. Against **PEMs** (Zhang et al., 2023), the margins are larger, especially on BBH where MOLE leads by 9.0 EM (42.2 vs 33.2) and on Translation by 2.7 BLEU. Against the full-parameter baselines **Custom** (Kumari et al., 2023) and **Textual Inversion** (Gal et al., 2022) in Table 2, MOLE outperforms Textual Inversion on both text-alignment (0.752 vs 0.717) and image-alignment (0.743 vs 0.728), and beats Custom on text-alignment (0.752 vs 0.734) while trailing slightly on image-alignment (0.743 vs 0.760) — all while training only gating parameters rather than the full model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA composition problem addressed by linear arithmetic methods (PEMs, LoRAHub, SVDiff) and reference tuning-based approaches (Mix-of-Show) by borrowing the Mixture-of-Experts gating mechanism and applying it at the layer level within LoRA. The key conceptual departure is treating each layer of a trained LoRA as a distinct expert rather than applying a single uniform composition weight across all layers, motivated by the observation that different LoRA layers encode different features. Unlike Mix-of-Show, which requires full model retraining and manually designed position masks, MOLE only trains lightweight gating functions while keeping all LoRA and base model parameters frozen, and unlike linear arithmetic composition, it avoids the trade-off between preserving generative capacity and retaining individual LoRA characteristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As organizations accumulate multiple task-specific LoRAs, the ability to compose them dynamically without retraining becomes practically critical; MOLE offers a lightweight, flexible solution that can also mask out unwanted LoRAs at inference time without retraining. The gating balancing loss addresses a non-trivial failure mode (gating collapse to a single expert) that would otherwise undermine the multi-expert design. However, the paper acknowledges that performance degrades with very large LoRA counts (128), leaving open the question of how to scale MoE-style composition to hundreds of experts. The layer-wise specialization finding (Observation 2) also suggests broader implications for understanding and manipulating LoRA internals beyond composition.
