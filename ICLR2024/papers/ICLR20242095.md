## Problem

As AI-synthesized data proliferates on the Internet and in training datasets (e.g., LAION-5B already contains images from StyleGAN, DALL-E, and Pix2Pix), generative models are increasingly trained on data produced by earlier generations of themselves, forming recursive 'autophagous' loops. The properties of these loops—particularly how quality and diversity evolve over generations and how sampling biases interact with this degradation—were poorly understood, with prior work limited to unbiased sampling and narrow model families.

## Input / Output

Each generation's training set combines synthetic samples drawn from the previous generation's model (with a controllable sampling bias λ that trades quality for diversity) with either no real data (fully synthetic loop), a fixed real dataset (synthetic augmentation loop), or freshly sampled real data (fresh data loop). The sampling bias is implemented model-specifically: as a truncation parameter for StyleGAN2, a classifier-free guidance factor for DDPMs, and a covariance scaling factor for Gaussian models. Evaluation produces FID (distributional distance), precision (quality), and recall (diversity) at each generation, with experiments run on FFHQ at 128×128 and MNIST at 28×28.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified framework of three autophagous loop variants and a universal sampling bias parameter λ ∈ [0,1], then provides both theoretical and empirical analysis. Theoretically, for the fully synthetic loop with Gaussian models, the authors prove via martingale convergence that the estimated covariance matrix Σ_t converges to zero almost surely, establishing inevitable variance collapse (diversity loss) even without sampling bias, while the mean undergoes a random walk (quality loss). Empirically, they train sequences of StyleGAN2, DDPM, WGAN, Normalizing Flow, and GMM models across up to 100 generations on FFHQ, MNIST, and the 2D Rosenbrock distribution, showing that fixed real data only delays degradation while fresh real data can prevent it entirely. A key finding in the fresh data loop is a phase transition: modest synthetic data increases effective sample size (n_e/n_r > 1), but exceeding a critical threshold causes MADness, and stronger sampling bias reduces the admissible synthetic data fraction.

## Results

In fully synthetic loops, FID increases monotonically while precision (quality) and recall (diversity) decrease over generations for both StyleGAN2 (FFHQ) and DDPM (MNIST) models. With sampling bias (λ=0.7 for StyleGAN2, λ=0.5 for DDPM), precision is preserved but recall degrades even faster, and FID still increases. In the fresh data loop, Wasserstein distance and FID converge to a limit point independent of the initial model, and the admissible synthetic-to-real data ratio (n_s/n_r for which effective sample size n_e ≥ n_r) shrinks as n_r grows. Appreciable MADness (visible artifact amplification and metric degradation) arises in as few as 5 generations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare a proposed method against external baselines; instead, it compares three autophagous loop variants (fully synthetic, synthetic augmentation, fresh data) and two sampling regimes (unbiased λ=1 vs. biased λ<1) across multiple model families (StyleGAN2, DDPM, WGAN, Normalizing Flows, Gaussian/GMM). The fully synthetic loop shows the fastest degradation, with FID rising and both precision and recall falling. The synthetic augmentation loop (fixed real data) slows but does not prevent the same degradation pattern. The fresh data loop is the only variant where quality and diversity do not degrade, provided sufficient fresh real data is available each generation. Sampling bias (λ<1) consistently trades diversity for quality: it stabilizes precision but accelerates recall loss and reduces the admissible amount of synthetic data in the fresh data loop. Results are consistent across GANs, diffusion models, normalizing flows, and Gaussian/GMM models on both FFHQ and MNIST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autophagous (self-consuming) training analysis initiated by Shumailov et al. (2023), who studied fully synthetic and synthetic augmentation loops without sampling bias and only fine-tuned language models. It also unifies and extends the fragmented findings of Martinez et al. (2023a,b), who provided qualitative evidence of degradation in small-scale settings. The key conceptual departures are: (1) introducing the sampling bias parameter λ as a first-class variable that trades off quality versus diversity, (2) proposing the fresh data loop as a new autophagous mechanism modeling Internet scraping, and (3) training models from scratch across a broader family of architectures (StyleGAN2, DDPM, WGAN, Normalizing Flows, GMMs) rather than fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical urgency is high: AI-synthesized images are already present in major training corpora like LAION-5B, and the paper demonstrates that appreciable quality/diversity degradation (MADness) emerges in as few as 5 generations. The most actionable finding is the fresh data loop phase transition—modest amounts of synthetic data can actually boost effective sample size, but only up to a threshold that shrinks as real data grows and as sampling bias increases. Open questions include whether the unbiased-sampling guarantee (n_e/n_r > 1 always) extends beyond Gaussian models, how MADness propagates to downstream tasks like classification, and whether autophagy-aware watermarking can identify synthetic data without amplifying its own artifacts through recursive training.
