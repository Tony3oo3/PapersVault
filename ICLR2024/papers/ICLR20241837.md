## Problem

For adversarial linear MDPs with bandit feedback and unknown transitions (no simulator access), the best prior regret bounds were Õ(K^{4/5}+poly(1/λ_min)) for a computationally inefficient algorithm and Õ(K^{6/7}) for an efficient one, leaving a significant gap to the √K lower bound. This paper asks whether the optimal √K rate is achievable at all in this setting, and what the best computationally efficient regret can be.

## Input / Output

The learner observes state-action features φ(s,a) ∈ R^d (with ‖φ‖₂ ≤ 1), receives only the scalar loss ℓ_k(s,a) for the single action taken (bandit feedback), and sees the next state s' sampled from the unknown transition. The state space may be infinite but is layered into H stages, and both transitions and losses are linear in the features. The output is a policy π mapping states to action distributions, with the guarantee expressed as a high-probability regret bound over K episodes against the best fixed policy in hindsight.

## Method

The paper contributes two algorithms. The first (computationally inefficient) reduces the linear MDP to a linear bandit over dH-dimensional policy feature vectors, using state-space discretization and a model-free occupancy-measure estimator (EstOM) that searches over valid occupancy measures consistent with historical data rather than first estimating transitions via linear regression; it then runs exponential weights with John's exploration and a bonus term, achieving Õ(√K) regret. The second (computationally efficient) uses FTRL with a log-determinant barrier regularizer to update policies over lifted covariance matrices, combined with an initial reward-free exploration phase to control bonus magnitude, optimistic least-square policy evaluation for bonus construction, dilated bonuses, and a tighter covariance concentration bound, yielding Õ(K^{3/4}) regret. The logdet barrier is key to the efficiency gain: it decouples the step-size constraint from the loss-estimator magnitude, allowing a larger η than exponential weights permits.

## Results

The paper establishes two regret bounds for adversarial linear MDPs with bandit feedback and unknown transitions: Algorithm 2 (computationally inefficient) achieves Õ(√K) regret, which is the first minimax-optimal K dependence in this setting, and Algorithm 3 (computationally efficient, polynomial-time) achieves Õ(K^{3/4}) regret. Both results significantly improve over the previous state-of-the-art: the best inefficient algorithm (Kong et al., 2023) had Õ(K^{4/5}+poly(1/λ_min)) regret requiring a problem-dependent constant λ_min > 0, and the best efficient algorithm (Sherman et al., 2023b) had Õ(K^{6/7}) regret. The √K bound matches the known lower bound, confirming no statistical barrier exists, while the K^{3/4} bound closes the gap for computationally tractable methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four prior baselines, both proposed algorithms strictly improve the regret exponent. Algorithm 3 (efficient, K^{3/4}) outperforms Luo et al. (2021) at K^{14/15}, Dai et al. (2023) at K^{8/9}, and Sherman et al. (2023b) at K^{6/7}, all of which are also efficient policy-optimization methods, making it a direct apples-to-apples improvement. Algorithm 2 (inefficient, √K) outperforms Kong et al. (2023) at K^{4/5}+poly(1/λ_min) in two ways: it achieves the optimal √K rate and removes the need for the problem-dependent assumption that some exploratory policy induces a covariance matrix ⪰ λ_min I (where λ_min can be arbitrarily small). The key technical improvements enabling these gains are: (1) for Algorithm 2, state-space discretization combined with model-free occupancy measure estimation that avoids the invalid-estimator problem of Kong et al.; and (2) for Algorithm 3, the use of logdet-barrier FTRL instead of exponential weights, which allows a tighter matrix concentration bound (τ = 1/γ vs. 1/γ² or 1/γ³ in prior work) and more flexible step-size selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: the occupancy-measure-based linear bandit reduction (Kong et al., 2023; Jin et al., 2020a) and the policy optimization framework with exploration bonuses (Luo et al., 2021; Sherman et al., 2023b). For the √K algorithm, it departs from Kong et al. (2023) by replacing their separate initial exploration phase with on-the-fly model-free occupancy measure estimation over a discretized state space, eliminating the need for a λ_min explorability assumption. For the K^{3/4} algorithm, it builds on the policy optimization framework but replaces exponential weights with logdet-FTRL (Zimmert and Lattimore, 2022; Liu et al., 2023a), which provides greater flexibility in learning rate selection and handles larger loss estimator bias more gracefully.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The √K result establishes that the minimax rate for adversarial linear MDPs with bandit feedback is indeed √K, closing a significant gap that had persisted since Luo et al. (2021) first showed no-regret was possible. The K^{3/4} efficient algorithm meaningfully improves the practical frontier over the previous best of K^{6/7}. The central open question the paper explicitly highlights is whether the optimal √K rate can be achieved with a computationally efficient algorithm, which would unify the two results. The logdet barrier technique and the model-free occupancy estimation approach are likely to be reusable in related settings (e.g., linear mixture MDPs, contextual bandits with structured action spaces).
