## Problem

Existing federated continual learning (FCL) methods assume that thorough memorization of all previous tasks is always beneficial, yet in heterogeneous federated settings—especially under a 'limitless task pool' where clients may encounter unrelated or antagonistic tasks—statistical heterogeneity injects biased or spurious features into the shared memory bank. The paper argues that indiscriminate retention of such biased knowledge actively degrades performance, and introduces the concept of 'accurate forgetting' to selectively discard harmful prior information while preserving useful knowledge.

## Input / Output

Each client receives a sequential stream of image classification tasks (e.g., 2-class EMNIST letter pairs, 20-class CIFAR-100 subsets, or 3-class MNIST/SVHN/FashionMNIST groups) with no overlapping classes across tasks within a client. The classifier operates on standard images (28×28 grayscale for EMNIST/MNIST/SVHN, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-100) and outputs class labels; the NF model operates in a 512-dimensional feature space extracted by the classifier's backbone. The final output is a global classification model trained via FedAvg that must maintain performance across all tasks seen by all N clients.

## Method

AF-FCL trains a conditional normalizing flow (NF) model in the classifier's 512-d feature space for generative replay, using affine coupling layers with random permutations. The key mechanism—accurate forgetting—works by mapping both local data features and NF-generated features into the NF's latent space, approximating the current task's feature distribution as a diagonal-covariance Gaussian, and computing the probability density of each generated latent vector under this distribution. This probability re-weights the cross-entropy loss for generated features, effectively down-weighting biased or irrelevant features from other clients while preserving correlated knowledge. A knowledge distillation loss in feature space (L2 distance between current and previous feature extractors) stabilizes the NF training by preventing feature-space drift. The full training objective combines raw-data cross-entropy, correlation-weighted generated-feature cross-entropy, and feature-space distillation, all aggregated across clients via FedAvg.

## Results

On the challenging EMNIST-LTP dataset (limitless task pool setting), AF-FCL achieves 47.5% average accuracy and 7.9% average forgetting, outperforming the best baseline FedCIL (42.0% / 12.4%) by 5.5 points in accuracy and 4.5 points in forgetting. On the more tractable EMNIST-shuffle setting, AF-FCL reaches 75.8% accuracy with 4.2% forgetting. On the harder CIFAR-100 and MNIST-SVHN-F datasets, AF-FCL attains 36.3% / 4.9% and 68.1% / 7.5% respectively, consistently leading all baselines on both accuracy and forgetting metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AF-FCL outperforms all seven baseline families across every dataset and both metrics (accuracy and forgetting). Against the strongest FCL baselines, AF-FCL beats FedCIL by 5.5% accuracy on EMNIST-LTP (47.5 vs. 42.0) and by 6.3% on MNIST-SVHN-F (68.1 vs. 57.2), while also reducing forgetting substantially (7.9 vs. 12.4 and 7.5 vs. 19.7). GLFC is the closest competitor on EMNIST-shuffle (74.9 vs. 75.8 accuracy) and CIFAR-100 (35.6 vs. 36.3), but AF-FCL still leads on forgetting in both cases. Notably, on the noisy EMNIST-noisy dataset, CL/FCL baselines (PODNet, ACGAN-Replay, FLwF2T, FedCIL) actually underperform the naive FedAvg as the number of noisy clients increases, whereas AF-FCL maintains stable performance (55.5% at M=1 to 54.0% at M=4) thanks to its selective forgetting mechanism. The generative-replay baselines (ACGAN-Replay, FedCIL) show moderate forgetting but are outperformed in accuracy by AF-FCL on all four main datasets, confirming that indiscriminate replay of heterogeneous features is suboptimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends federated continual learning (FCL) by introducing the limitless task pool (LTP) setting, where tasks across clients may be unrelated or antagonistic, departing from the task-reshuffling assumption in prior FCL work (Yoon et al., 2021; Qi et al., 2023). Methodologically, it replaces GAN-based generative replay (ACGAN-Replay, FedCIL) with a normalizing flow model in feature space, leveraging the NF's exact likelihood estimation to perform correlation-based selective forgetting. The concept of 'accurate forgetting' draws on the observation from noisy-label learning (Han et al., 2020) that forgetting can be beneficial, repurposing it to mitigate spurious correlations introduced by statistical heterogeneity in federated networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CL methods designed to prevent forgetting actually underperform naive FedAvg under the LTP setting challenges a core assumption in the FCL literature that thorough memorization is always beneficial. The NF-based correlation estimation provides a principled, differentiable mechanism for selective replay that is more efficient than GAN-based alternatives. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small-scale datasets (EMNIST, CIFAR100, MNIST-SVHN-F) and restriction to classification tasks; open questions include scalability to very large federated networks, applicability to non-classification tasks, and whether the diagonal-covariance Gaussian approximation in the latent space remains valid for highly complex feature distributions.
