## Problem

Existing protein language models (PLMs) such as the ESM family are trained solely on 1D residue sequences and lack explicit incorporation of 3D structural information, which is directly relevant to protein function. While structure-based PLMs exist (e.g., GearNet, MIF-ST), they either rely on limited structure datasets (~800K structures) or target narrow tasks like inverse folding, leaving no general-purpose PLM trained on large-scale predicted structures with a simple, drop-in-compatible representation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a structure-aware (SA) token sequence where each position is a pair (residue_token, Foldseek_3Di_token) drawn from a 441-token vocabulary (21×21, including mask symbols for missing structure or residue). Sequences are truncated to 1024 tokens, and regions with pLDDT < 70 are represented as residue-only tokens (s_i #) to handle low-confidence AF2 predictions. Outputs are per-position hidden states usable for zero-shot mutational effect scoring (via log-odds ratios over the SA vocabulary), supervised classification/regression heads for function prediction, or contact map prediction.

## Method

SaProt introduces a structure-aware vocabulary that fuses each residue with its corresponding Foldseek 3Di structure token into a single SA token, converting a protein into a 441-alphabet sequence that can be fed into a standard Transformer encoder. The architecture is identical to ESM-2 650M except for the expanded embedding layer, enabling direct comparison. Training uses BERT-style masked language modeling on ~40M AlphaFoldDB structures with an asymmetric masking strategy: when a position is masked, the structure token remains visible (#f_i) while only the residue is predicted, avoiding the model from learning to predict potentially inaccurate Foldseek tokens. This design was motivated by empirical findings that directly incorporating 3D coordinates (via GNNs or attention biases) causes overfitting to AF2 prediction artifacts, whereas the discrete token representation generalizes better to real PDB structures. The model was trained for ~3M steps on 64 A100 GPUs with AdamW, mixed precision, and a 15% mask rate.

## Results

SaProt achieves 0.909 AUC on ClinVar and 0.478 Spearman's ρ on ProteinGym (without MSA retrieval), outperforming all sequence-based and structure-based baselines. On 8 supervised fine-tuning tasks, SaProt leads on every metric: 0.724 Spearman's ρ on Thermostability, 86.41% on HumanPPI, 75.75% on Metal Ion Binding, 0.882 Fmax on EC, and 85.57%/93.55% on DeepLoc (Subcellular/Binary). On contact map prediction, SaProt reaches 57.11 P@L (short range) versus 44.87 for ESM-2, confirming stronger structural awareness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaProt outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated task. Against the ESM family (ESM-2 650M, ESM-1b, ESM-1v), SaProt gains +0.047 AUC on ClinVar (0.909 vs 0.862) and +0.003 Spearman's ρ on ProteinGym with MSA retrieval (0.489 vs 0.479), while leading on all 8 supervised tasks by margins of 1.6–12.4 points depending on the task. Against structure-based models, SaProt surpasses MIF-ST by +0.011 AUC on ClinVar and +10.87% on HumanPPI, and outperforms GearNet by +0.153 Spearman's ρ on Thermostability and +16.12% on DeepLoc Subcellular. SaProt also beats ESM-IF (designed for inverse folding) by a wide margin (0.909 vs 0.748 AUC on ClinVar), EVE (0.909 vs 0.878), MSA Transformer (0.909 vs 0.854), and Tranception L (0.909 vs 0.845) on ClinVar. The ensemble SaProt-GearNet further improves over ESM-GearNet on most tasks, confirming orthogonality with advanced fine-tuning techniques, though single-model SaProt remains the strongest overall performer.

## Relation to Prior Work

SaProt builds directly on the ESM-2 transformer backbone (Lin et al., 2022) and repurposes Foldseek's VQ-VAE structure tokenizer (van Kempen et al., 2023) to create a novel 'structure-aware vocabulary' that fuses residue and 3D conformation tokens into a single 441-token alphabet. This departs from prior structure-based PLMs like GearNet and MIF-ST, which model structures as graphs via GNNs, and from Evoformer/ESMFold-style approaches that inject distance/angle features as attention biases. Unlike ProstT5, which performs bilingual translation between residue and Foldseek sequences, SaProt treats the combined SA-token sequence as the native input to a standard MLM objective, making it a drop-in replacement for any residue-sequence PLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SaProt is the first general-purpose PLM trained on ~40 million predicted structures (vs. ~800K for GearNet), and its simple vocabulary-expansion strategy offers a practical, architecture-agnostic way to incorporate 3D information into existing protein language models. A key open question highlighted by the paper is the overfitting risk when training on AF2-predicted structures with BERT-style MLM, suggesting that predicted structures carry detectable artifacts that degrade generalization to real PDB structures. Limitations include dependence on Foldseek's encoding accuracy, the 650M parameter ceiling due to compute constraints, and unexplored applications such as protein complex structure prediction and structure-constrained protein generation.
