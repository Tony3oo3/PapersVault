## Problem

Existing dirty sample detectors are designed for either poisoned samples or noisy labels and exhibit poor generalization across categories and types of dirty samples. No universal framework exists that can simultaneously detect backdoor-poisoned samples, mislabeled samples, and hybrids of both without requiring task-specific training or clean data subsets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240750/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VDC takes a single RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet subsets) paired with its associated class label as input, and produces a visual-linguistic matching score (the proportion of generated questions whose MLLM answers align with expected answers) along with a binary dirty/clean decision via a fixed threshold (α=0.5). Notably, the method requires no clean data subset and no training on the target dataset; all detection is performed through zero-shot inference of an LLM and an MLLM.

## Method

VDC exploits the shared property of poisoned samples and noisy labels—visual-linguistic inconsistency between image content and the associated label—through a three-module pipeline. First, a Visual Question Generation module uses ChatGPT to produce both general questions (from predefined templates) and label-specific questions (leveraging LLM open-world knowledge about the class). Second, a Visual Question Answering module feeds each question and the image to InstructBLIP (an MLLM) to obtain semantic descriptions of the visual content. Third, a Visual Answer Evaluation module scores each response against its expected answer using string matching for deterministic label-specific questions and ChatGPT-as-judge for open-ended general questions, then aggregates via a vote-based ensemble. The entire pipeline is training-free and model-agnostic with respect to the MLLM choice, making it generalizable across datasets, trigger types, and noise patterns without any adaptation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (η=0.09), VDC achieves an average TPR of 99.91% and FPR of 2.75% across six backdoor attack types (BadNets, Blended, SIG, TrojanNN, SSBA, WaNet) without requiring any clean data. On ImageNet-100 (η=0.099), VDC maintains 99.94% average TPR with 1.55% FPR. For noisy label detection at η=0.4, VDC reaches 99.60% TPR on asymmetric CIFAR-10 noise and 92.34% TPR on asymmetric ImageNet-100 noise. In the hybrid setting (poisoning η₁=0.09 + noisy η₂=0.1), VDC still achieves 99.41% TPR with 2.79% FPR, far outperforming all single-purpose detectors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDC outperforms all seven poisoned-sample detection baselines on average TPR across all three datasets without requiring any clean data (0% vs. 4% for STRIP, SS, SCAn, Frequency, CT). On CIFAR-10, VDC's 99.91% average TPR surpasses the next-best CT (97.94%, which needs 4% clean data) and SCAn (95.46%), while SPECTRE shows extreme inconsistency with an 87.15% TPR swing between its best (TrojanNN: 97.44%) and worst (SIG: 8.16%) attacks. For noisy label detection, VDC dominates on the harder asymmetric setting, achieving 99.60% TPR on CIFAR-10 versus 59.67% for SimiFeat-V and 79.46% for SimiFeat-R, while also maintaining low FPR (2.62%). In the hybrid dirty-sample scenario, VDC's 99.41% TPR dramatically exceeds the best single-purpose detectors: SimiFeat-V drops to 75.08% and CL to 36.33%, confirming that no existing method generalizes across both poisoned samples and noisy labels simultaneously. On the fine-grained ImageNet-Dog dataset, VDC (98.68% TPR) substantially outperforms D-BR (9.28%) and SPECTRE (87.43%), which degrade severely under high class similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying two previously separate detection tasks—poisoned sample detection (backdoor defense) and noisy label detection—under a single framework built on visual-linguistic inconsistency. Rather than building on a specific prior detection method, it repurposes the cross-modal reasoning capabilities of MLLMs (InstructBLIP) and LLMs (GPT-3.5) as a zero-shot detector, departing from the statistical, spectral, and frequency-domain signatures that dominate prior work. The key conceptual departure is that no training on the dirty dataset or access to a clean subset is required, in contrast to nearly all baselines (STRIP, SS, SCAn, CT, BHN) that depend on external clean data or model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for data-centric AI pipelines: a single, training-free detector that handles backdoor attacks, label noise, and their mixture without knowing the attack type in advance simplifies real-world data curation. However, the approach is limited to label-corrupted dirty samples and cannot detect clean-label backdoor attacks, and its reliance on MLLM inference introduces computational cost and potential fragility to model errors. Open questions include scalability to very large datasets (millions of images), applicability to non-visual modalities, and whether the performance gap over CLIP-based baselines (Table 7) narrows as MLLMs become more standardized and cheaper.
