## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) has become the dominant paradigm for text-to-3D generation, but its practical success relies heavily on classifier-free guidance (CFG), which was always treated as an auxiliary trick rather than the core mechanism. The paper asks what actually drives the optimization: is it the generative prior (the conditional score ∇log p(x_t|y)) or the implicit classifier score (∇log p(y|x_t)) that emerges from CFG? The surprising answer is that the classifier score alone is both necessary and sufficient, fundamentally reframing the understanding of why SDS works.

## Input / Output

For text-to-3D generation, the method takes a single text prompt and produces a NeRF (optimized at 64×64 with DeepFloyd-IF stage-I) that is then converted to a triangular mesh and refined at 512×512 with Stable Diffusion 2.1. For texture synthesis, it takes a user-specified 3D mesh plus a text prompt and outputs a texture field parameterized by Instant-NGP, guided by Canny-edge and depth ControlNets built on Stable Diffusion 1.5. For 3D editing, it takes an existing NeRF scene, a target prompt, and an attribute prompt to modify, producing an edited scene. The method is a drop-in replacement for SDS in any existing pipeline, requiring no architectural changes.

## Method

The key insight is that the SDS gradient with CFG decomposes into a generative prior term δ_x^gen = [ε_φ(x_t;y,t) − ε] and a classifier score term δ_x^cls = [ε_φ(x_t;y,t) − ε_φ(x_t;t)], and with the large guidance weights (ω≈100) used in practice, the classifier score dominates the optimization direction. CSD discards the generative prior entirely and optimizes using only δ_x^cls, which corresponds to the gradient of an implicit noise-aware classifier p_φ(y|x_t) derived from the diffusion model via Bayes' rule. The paper further introduces an annealed negative-prompt strategy that uses dual classifier scores with independently weighted positive (ω_1) and negative (ω_2) prompts, where ω_2 is gradually decreased to balance visual quality against text fidelity. For 3D editing, the same dual-classifier formulation is repurposed by treating the attribute to change as the negative prompt. The paper also shows that Variational Score Distillation (VSD) can be reinterpreted as an adaptive form of negative classifier score optimization, where the negative direction is learned concurrently rather than fixed by a text prompt.

## Results

On text-to-3D generation evaluated with 81 diverse prompts, CSD achieves a CLIP Score of 78.6 and CLIP R-Precision of 81.8% (R@1), 94.8% (R@5), and 96.3% (R@10), outperforming DreamFusion (67.5 CLIP Score) and Magic3D (74.9 CLIP Score). In user studies with 30 participants, CSD is preferred in 59.4% of responses for text-to-3D generation and 57.7% for texture synthesis, substantially exceeding all baselines. For texture synthesis on 20 meshes from Objaverse, CSD produces more photo-realistic textures without seam artifacts, and training completes in ~1 hour on a single A800 GPU versus 8 hours for ProlificDreamer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSD outperforms DreamFusion by a large margin on CLIP Score (78.6 vs. 67.5) and R@1 (81.8% vs. 73.1%), though DreamFusion retains a slight edge on R@10 (97.2% vs. 96.3%). Against Magic3D, CSD leads on all metrics except R@10 (96.3% vs. 96.6%), with the largest gap on R@1 (81.8% vs. 74.1%). In the user study, CSD is preferred in 59.4% of text-to-3D responses versus 30.3% for DreamFusion and 10.3% for Magic3D, and 57.7% for texture synthesis versus 12.4% (Magic3D), 11.0% (Fantasia3D, TEXTure), and 7.9% (ProlificDreamer). Compared to ProlificDreamer (VSD), CSD achieves competitive visual quality while being 8× faster (1 hour vs. 8 hours on a single A800 GPU), since CSD does not require concurrently training a second diffusion model. Qualitatively, CSD produces more realistic, less over-saturated results than SDS-based methods and avoids the seam artifacts seen in TEXTure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) framework introduced by DreamFusion by decomposing the practical SDS gradient (which uses classifier-free guidance) into a generative prior term and a classifier score term, then demonstrating that only the classifier score term is necessary for effective text-to-3D generation. The classifier score is derived directly from the classifier-free guidance formulation of Ho & Salimans, which had been treated as an auxiliary trick rather than the core optimization driver. The paper further repositions Variational Score Distillation (ProlificDreamer) as an adaptive form of negative classifier score optimization, and reframes negative prompts as dual classifier score objectives, providing a unified lens through which to understand these previously disparate techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the classifier score alone suffices for text-to-3D generation fundamentally reframes the field's understanding of what drives SDS-based optimization, and CSD serves as a practical drop-in replacement that is both faster (1 hour vs. 8 hours for ProlificDreamer) and produces higher-quality results. However, the authors acknowledge they have not yet formulated a principled distribution-based objective underlying CSD, and notably CSD applied to 2D image optimization produces artifacts, suggesting the 3D multi-view structure plays a critical but unexplained role. Open questions include whether a proper probabilistic framework can be derived for classifier score distillation, and why the method succeeds in 3D but fails in 2D settings.
