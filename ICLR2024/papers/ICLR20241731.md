## Problem

Full fine-tuning of LLaMA for instruction following (as in Alpaca) requires updating all 7B parameters, making it time-consuming, computation-intensive, and difficult to transfer across downstream scenarios. The paper asks whether a lightweight, plug-and-play adaption mechanism can match full fine-tuning quality while introducing only a tiny fraction of learnable parameters, and whether the same mechanism can be extended to multi-modal instruction following.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241731/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes natural-language instructions (and optionally an RGB image for multi-modal mode) as input. Text is tokenized into M-length word tokens; images are encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT into multi-scale global features that are projected to a single image token. The output is a generated text response produced autoregressively by the frozen LLaMA backbone. In multi-modal mode, the image token is element-wise added to the learnable adaption prompts at each inserted layer, so the model conditions its generation on both visual and textual context without any architectural change to the LLM itself.

## Method

LLaMA-Adapter freezes the entire LLaMA 7B backbone and inserts K-length learnable adaption prompts as prefixes to the word tokens in the top L=30 transformer layers. The key mechanism is a zero-initialized attention: within each inserted self-attention layer, the attention score vector is split into a prompt component S^K and a word-token component S^{M+1}, each passed through an independent softmax. A per-head learnable gating scalar g_l, initialized to zero and passed through tanh, multiplies the prompt softmax scores, so at initialization the prompts contribute nothing and the pre-trained knowledge is fully preserved; as training proceeds, g_l grows and progressively injects instructional (or visual) semantics. For multi-modal extension, a frozen CLIP encoder's multi-scale features are concatenated, projected by a small bottleneck MLP into the word-embedding space, repeated K times, and element-wise added to the adaption prompts, allowing the same zero-gated attention to fuse visual information. In total only 1.2M parameters (prompts + gating scalars) are trained, converging in under one hour on 8 A100 GPUs, yet achieving instruction-following quality comparable to the fully fine-tuned 7B Alpaca and competitive multi-modal reasoning on MME, MMBench, and LVLM-eHub.

## Results

On ScienceQA, LLaMA-Adapter achieves 85.19% average accuracy with only 1.8M tuned parameters, surpassing GPT-4 CoT (83.99%) and ChatGPT CoT (78.31%), while the text-only variant (1.2M params) reaches 78.31%. On the Open LLM benchmark, LLaMA-Adapter attains 52.2 average score, outperforming full fine-tuned Alpaca (49.23) and Alpaca-LoRA (50.73). For zero-shot multi-modal evaluation, LLaMA-Adapter scores 973/249 on MME (Perception/Cognition), 39.5 on MMBench, and 0.67 on LVLM-eHub, all leading concurrent multi-modal LLMs. Training requires only 1 hour on 8 A100 GPUs with 1.2M learnable parameters, compared to 3 hours and 7B parameters for Alpaca.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Alpaca (7B full fine-tuning), LLaMA-Adapter wins on the Open LLM benchmark (52.2 vs. 49.23 avg) and the GPT-4 evaluation, while using 5800× fewer parameters (1.2M vs. 7B) and training 3× faster (1h vs. 3h). Against Alpaca-LoRA (4.2M params), LLaMA-Adapter achieves a higher average score (52.2 vs. 50.73) with fewer parameters and shorter training time (1h vs. 1.5h), and unlike LoRA can be extended to multi-modal input. On ScienceQA, LLaMA-Adapter (85.19%) surpasses GPT-4 CoT (83.99%) and ChatGPT CoT (78.31%) with only 1.8M tuned parameters, and also outperforms MM-COT (84.91%) which uses 223M parameters. In zero-shot multi-modal evaluation, LLaMA-Adapter leads LLaVA on MME Perception (973 vs. 503) and MMBench (39.5 vs. 36.2), and leads MiniGPT-4 on all three benchmarks (MME 973/249 vs. 867/292; MMBench 39.5 vs. 23.0; LVLM-eHub 0.67 vs. 0.55). On traditional vision tasks (VTAB-1k), the zero-initialized attention outperforms full fine-tuning (81.74/84.43/56.75 vs. 75.88/83.36/47.64) and VPT (78.48/82.43/54.98). On CLIP base-to-novel generalization, it achieves the best harmonic mean (56.75) over MaPLe (54.98), CoCoOp (33.43), and CoOp (33.43).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and instruction tuning of LLMs. It departs from LoRA (which modifies weight matrices via low-rank decomposition) and standard prompt tuning (which appends randomly initialized tokens) by introducing a zero-initialized gating factor that operates *inside* the self-attention mechanism, reweighting prompt attention scores before the softmax. The gating concept is related to Flamingo's cross-attention gating, but LLaMA-Adapter's gate is applied to attention scores within the LLM's existing layers rather than to residual connections outside them, and it introduces only 1.2M parameters versus Flamingo's 3B+ cross-attention modules. The multi-modal extension repurposes the same zero-initialized attention to inject CLIP image features, avoiding the full LLM fine-tuning required by concurrent works like LLaVA and MiniGPT-4.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: matching Alpaca's 7B full fine-tuning with 1.2M parameters and one-hour training on 8 A100s makes instruction tuning accessible on modest hardware and enables storing per-scenario adapters (1.8M) instead of full model copies (13G). The generalization to ViT, RoBERTa, and CLIP suggests the zero-initialized attention gating is a broadly applicable PEFT primitive, not just an LLaMA-specific trick. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, how it compares to newer PEFT methods (e.g., QLoRA, DoRA), and whether the multi-modal extension can match the performance of models trained with more sophisticated vision-language alignment strategies.
