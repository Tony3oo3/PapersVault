## Problem

Existing learned image compression methods, whether CNN-based or transformer-based, produce redundant latent representations because standard window self-attention treats all spatial directions uniformly and ignores the anisotropic frequency structure of natural images. No prior LIC model has performed explicit directional frequency decomposition within an end-to-end learned framework, leaving a gap between the well-established multiscale geometric analysis theory and practical neural compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241201/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at various resolutions (768×512 for Kodak, 1200×1200 for Tecnick, up to 2K for CLIC) and outputs a compressed bitstream via a range coder plus a reconstructed image. Internally, the analysis transform maps the input to a 320-channel latent at 1/16 spatial resolution, which is quantized and entropy-coded using a hyperprior (192-channel) and a 5-slice channel-wise autoregressive model. Training uses 256×256 (later 384×384) random crops from Flickr2W and ImageNet-1k.

## Method

The core contribution is a Frequency-Aware Transformer (FAT) block that performs multiscale directional decomposition via Frequency-Decomposed Window Attention (FDWA), which runs four parallel window-attention groups with shapes 4s×4s, s×s, s×4s, and 4s×s to capture low-frequency, high-frequency, vertical, and horizontal components respectively. A Frequency-Modulation Feed-Forward Network (FMFFN) then applies a block-based FFT, multiplies by a learnable frequency-domain filter, and inverse-transforms to adaptively amplify or suppress frequency components according to the operating bitrate. For entropy modeling, a Transformer-based Channel-wise Autoregressive (T-CA) model replaces CNN-based slice predictors with masked-slice channel attention and group convolutions, enabling precise inter- and intra-slice dependency modeling without proportional parameter growth. The full architecture stacks RBS, RBU, and FAT blocks for analysis/synthesis transforms, is trained with a two-stage Lagrangian R-D objective (hyperprior-only pretraining followed by joint fine-tuning with T-CA), and achieves 14.5% BD-rate savings over VTM-12.1 on Kodak.

## Results

FTIC achieves state-of-the-art rate-distortion performance, outperforming the standardized codec VTM-12.1 by 14.5%, 15.1%, and 13.0% in BD-rate on the Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC Professional Validation datasets, respectively. The model has 70.97M parameters with 141 GFLOPs (encoding) and 349 GFLOPs (decoding), and an inference latency of 125 ms (encoding) and 242 ms (decoding) on an RTX 4090. On the Kodak dataset, FTIC surpasses the strongest prior LIC method (Liu et al., 2023, BD-rate −11.9%) by a further 2.6 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FTIC outperforms all nine compared baselines on BD-rate across Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC. Against the strongest prior LIC method, Liu et al. (2023, CVPR), FTIC improves BD-rate from −11.9% to −14.5% on Kodak while using fewer GFLOPs (141 vs. 317 for encoding) and fewer parameters (70.97M vs. 76.57M). Compared to the transformer-based STF (Zou et al., 2022), FTIC gains 10.2 percentage points in BD-rate (−14.5% vs. −4.3%) with roughly half the model size (70.97M vs. 99.58M). Against the Swin-Transformer-based method of Zhu et al. (2022), FTIC improves BD-rate by 11.2 points (−14.5% vs. −3.3%) with comparable GFLOPs. The CNN-based baselines (Ballé et al. 2018, Cheng et al. 2020, Xie et al. 2021, He et al. 2022, Fu et al. 2023, Minnen & Singh 2020) are all substantially outperformed, with Cheng et al. (2020) actually performing worse than VTM-12.1 (+3.6% BD-rate) and suffering from >1000 ms inference latency due to its spatial autoregressive entropy model.

## Relation to Prior Work

FTIC extends the transformer-based LIC line initiated by Zhu et al. (2022) and Liu et al. (2023), which replaced CNN nonlinear transforms with Swin-Transformer blocks, by introducing a frequency-aware variant of window attention (FDWA) that uses anisotropic window shapes (square, tall, wide) to explicitly decompose low-, high-, vertical-, and horizontal-frequency components. The entropy model builds directly on the channel-wise autoregressive framework of Minnen & Singh (2020), replacing per-slice CNNs with a shared transformer that applies masked channel attention across slices. The overall architecture (RBS, RBU, hyperprior) follows the standard LIC recipe from Minnen et al. (2018) and Cheng et al. (2020), while the FMFFN module draws on global filter networks (Rao et al., 2021) for learnable frequency-domain modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling directional frequency structure within a transformer's attention mechanism yields a meaningful R-D gain over isotropic window attention, bridging classical multiscale geometric analysis (wavelets, contourlets) with end-to-end learned compression. The T-CA entropy model shows that a shared transformer with channel attention can outperform the heavier CNN-based CHARM with fewer slices, suggesting a more parameter-efficient path for autoregressive entropy modeling. A stated limitation is that FDWA currently supports only horizontal and vertical directions; extending to diagonal or arbitrary orientations (e.g., via scattering transforms) and generalizing to video compression are identified as open directions.
