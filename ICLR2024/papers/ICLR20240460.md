## Problem

Spherical linear interpolation in diffusion models produces high-quality results for diffusion-generated images but introduces noticeable artifacts when applied to natural images. The paper identifies the root cause as 'invalid noise': encoding natural images into the latent space yields variables that deviate from the expected Gaussian distribution, containing components above or below the denoising threshold that the model was never trained to handle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240460/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two natural RGB images (not generated by a diffusion model) and produces a single interpolated image blending their styles according to a coefficient λ. Images are encoded into the latent space via the probability flow ODE at a chosen noise level, manipulated, and decoded back. Experiments use 256×256 images from LSUN Cat-256 and LSUN Bedroom-256, with additional validation on Stable Diffusion. The user primarily controls λ for style blending, while boundary (2.0–2.4) and lubrication coefficient γ (0 to √0.1) are set within recommended ranges.

## Method

NoiseDiffusion corrects the invalid latent variables by (1) encoding both images via the probability flow ODE, (2) clipping extreme values to suppress noise components beyond the denoising threshold, (3) synthesizing a new latent variable as a weighted sum of the two clipped latent vectors, the two original images, and a shared Gaussian noise term, subject to the constraint √(α²+β²+γ²)=1 to preserve the hypersphere geometry, and (4) clipping again before denoising. The compensation coefficients μ and ν inject raw image information into the noisy space to counteract the signal-to-noise ratio loss caused by clipping and exogenous noise. The theoretical foundation rests on two results: high-dimensional Gaussians concentrate on a hypersphere (Theorem 1), and independent isotropic vectors are nearly orthogonal (Theorem 2), which justifies the norm-preserving combination. The method unifies spherical linear interpolation (γ=0, μ=α, ν=β) and SDEdit-style noise introduction (α=β=0) as special cases, while the boundary control and image injection steps address the respective failure modes of each.

## Results

This paper presents a primarily qualitative evaluation with no quantitative metrics (e.g., FID, IS) reported. The main results (Figures 13–14, 21–30) are visual comparisons on LSUN Cat-256 and LSUN Bedroom-256, as well as Stable Diffusion, showing that NoiseDiffusion reduces artifacts compared to spherical linear interpolation while better preserving original image information compared to the SDEdit-based noise-introduction method. Ablation studies (Figures 5–10) demonstrate that the lubricating coefficient γ, boundary control, and compensation coefficients μ/ν each contribute to artifact reduction and feature preservation, but no numerical scores are provided.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares NoiseDiffusion against two baselines: (1) Spherical Linear Interpolation (slerp, Song et al. 2021a) and (2) the SDEdit-based noise-introduction method (Meng et al. 2022). Against slerp, NoiseDiffusion clearly wins on natural images: slerp produces noticeable artifacts because the encoded latent variables of natural images do not obey the expected normal distribution (noise components fall above or below the denoising threshold), whereas NoiseDiffusion's clipping and Gaussian noise correction reposition latent variables onto the hypersphere, substantially reducing artifacts. Against the SDEdit-based noise-introduction method, NoiseDiffusion also wins on information preservation: adding more Gaussian noise yields realistic but information-losing results (resembling direct image overlay), while adding less noise introduces extraneous information; NoiseDiffusion's compensation coefficients μ and ν inject raw image information back into the noisy latent space, preserving original features with less exogenous noise. The paper also shows (Section 4.6) that both baselines are special cases of the NoiseDiffusion formulation, unifying them under a single framework. All comparisons are qualitative (visual), with no quantitative metric reported to measure the degree of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spherical linear interpolation (slerp) framework from Song et al. (2021a,b) and the SDEdit noise-injection paradigm (Meng et al., 2022) into a unified interpolation method for natural images. The key departure is diagnosing slerp's failure on non-diffusion-generated images as a noise-distribution mismatch (latent variables not lying on the expected hypersphere), then correcting it via clipping, subtle Gaussian noise injection, and original-image compensation. Theoretically, the design is grounded in two standard high-dimensional probability results (concentration of norm and near-orthogonality of independent isotropic vectors), which the authors use to justify the constraint √(α²+β²+γ²)=1 and the linear combination structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a practical gap: slerp produces artifacts on natural images, and SDEdit-based interpolation introduces unwanted information, so NoiseDiffusion offers a middle ground that preserves features while reducing artifacts. However, validation is limited to LSUN Cat-256, Bedroom-256, and Stable Diffusion with qualitative comparisons only (no FID, LPIPS, or user studies in the main text), and the extra encoding step doubles inference cost. Open questions include generalization to other modalities (video, audio), adversarial or out-of-distribution inputs, and whether the boundary-control and compensation coefficients can be learned rather than hand-tuned.
