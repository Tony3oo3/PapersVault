## Problem

Unidirectional auto-regressive human animation generation suffers from progressive texture drifting and appearance distortion because the motion-appearance ambiguity—infinitely many valid future appearances for a given motion—accumulates errors over time. This paper argues that bidirectional temporal modeling, where each frame is conditioned on both its past and future neighbors, suppresses this ambiguity and enforces temporal coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241732/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a pose sequence (DensePose UV maps or SMPL IUV surface coordinates) plus one of three appearance conditions: a single RGB image, a person-specific video, or no condition (random noise). It produces a 256×256 temporally coherent animation video; internally the BTU-Net generates 64×64 frames that are upscaled via a jointly trained SR3 super-resolution network. At inference, K=50 denoising steps are used for speed, with bidirectional recursive sampling alternating forward and backward passes across the frame sequence.

## Method

The core contribution is a Bidirectional Temporal Diffusion Model (BTDM) that jointly models forward and backward conditional distributions p(y_t | y_{t-1}, s_t) and p(y_{t-1} | y_t, s_{t-1}) within a single DDPM framework. Rather than conditioning on perfectly denoised frames (unavailable at inference), the model cross-conditions on noisy intermediate frames y^k at each diffusion step, which reduces overfitting and limits motion diversity between consecutive frames. The BTU-Net architecture uses a pair of weight-sharing U-Nets with bidirectional attention blocks at three resolutions (32×32, 16×16, 8×8): an appearance block transfers identity features from an encoder E_a via multi-head attention, and a spatiotemporal block performs cross-attention between t and t−1 features to enforce bidirectional temporal correlation. FiLM layers modulate intermediate features with pose, log-SNR, and learnable direction vectors (d_f, d_b). At inference, a bidirectional recursive sampling algorithm alternates forward and backward denoising passes, progressively refining local consistency into global temporal coherence.

## Results

On the person-specific animation task (Table 2), BTDM achieves an average LPIPS of 4.39×10⁻², tLPIPS of 3.22×10⁻³, and SSIM of 0.955, outperforming all five unidirectional baselines on SSIM and tLPIPS while being competitive on LPIPS (MDMT: 4.31 vs. 4.39). On the single image animation task (Table 1, graphics simulation data), BTDM outperforms TPSMM and MRAA across all five metrics (SSIM, LPIPS, tLPIPS, tOF, FID). The ablation confirms the bidirectional design is critical: switching to unidirectional training degrades SSIM from 0.958 to 0.937, LPIPS from 0.036 to 0.052, and tLPIPS from 0.003 to 0.005.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTDM is compared against seven unidirectional baselines across two tasks. In person-specific animation, BTDM achieves the best SSIM (0.955) and tLPIPS (3.22×10⁻³), surpassing the closest competitor MDMT (SSIM 0.949, tLPIPS 3.29×10⁻³) and clearly outperforming V2V, EDN, HFMT, and DIW on all three metrics. On LPIPS, MDMT edges out BTDM slightly (4.31 vs. 4.39), but the gap is marginal. In single image animation, BTDM outperforms both TPSMM and MRAA on every metric (SSIM, LPIPS, tLPIPS, tOF, FID), with the baselines exhibiting significant texture distortion and identity loss under large dynamic motions. The ablation further shows that removing the bidirectional conditioning (i.e., training the same BTU-Net unidirectionally) worsens tLPIPS by 67% (0.003→0.005) and LPIPS by 44% (0.036→0.052), confirming that the bidirectional cross-conditioning is the key differentiator over all unidirectional approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of auto-regressive human motion transfer methods (EDN, V2V, DIW, MDMT) by replacing their unidirectional forward prediction with a bidirectional temporal diffusion model. Conceptually, it draws on dynamic programming and message-passing algorithms (Felzenszwalb & Zabih; Arora et al.) to recursively cross-condition intermediate denoising features between consecutive frames in both temporal directions, and on DDPM-style denoising diffusion for the generative backbone. Architecturally, the BTU-Net introduces a bidirectional attention block (appearance + spatiotemporal multi-head attention) that is distinct from the 3D U-Net or cross-attention approaches used in recent video diffusion models, operating instead on a 2D U-Net with FiLM modulation and shared-weight forward/backward passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important failure mode in human animation—texture drifting under dynamic motion—by showing that bidirectional temporal conditioning in feature space suppresses the motion-appearance ambiguity that causes it. The newly created graphics-simulation dataset with perfect ground-truth motion transfer (different people, identical motion) fills a notable evaluation gap that real-world video datasets cannot address. However, the method remains limited by 2D pose conditioning (weakness under 3D body rotations) and pose estimation errors, and the authors identify integrating a full 3D body model (e.g., SMPL) as the natural next step. The bidirectional recursive sampling strategy, while effective, adds inference complexity compared to single-pass auto-regression.
