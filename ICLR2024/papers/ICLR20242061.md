## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models produce high-quality images, yet their internal representations of textual concepts remain largely unexplored. Existing concept-based interpretability methods (TCAV, ACE, ICE, CRAFT) are designed for discriminative classifiers and rely on prediction shifts or saliency, making them inapplicable to generative models. This paper asks: can we decompose how a diffusion model internally represents a concept into a small set of human-interpretable textual elements?

## Input / Output

The method takes a textual concept prompt (e.g., "a nurse") and a set of 100 representative images generated by Stable Diffusion for that concept. It operates over the CLIP text encoder's vocabulary (~50,000 tokens, pre-filtered to the top 5,000 by CLIP image similarity) and produces a pseudo-token: a weighted linear combination of the top n=50 vocabulary tokens, where each token is a human-readable word with a learned non-negative coefficient. For single-image decomposition, the output is the minimal subset of these tokens whose removal would semantically alter a specific generated image (determined by a CLIP similarity threshold of 95).

## Method

CONCEPTOR learns a 2-layer MLP that maps each CLIP word embedding w ∈ R^d to a scalar coefficient α = f(w) = σ(W₂(σ(W₁(w)))), then constructs a full-vocabulary pseudo-token w*_N = Σᵢ f(wᵢ)wᵢ and extracts the top-n tokens as the interpretable decomposition w*. The MLP is trained to minimize the diffusion model's noise-prediction MSE on noised concept images (reconstruction loss) plus a sparsity loss (1 − cosine similarity between w* and w*_N) that ensures the top-n tokens capture the full pseudo-token's semantics. The key design choice is parameterizing coefficients as a function of word embeddings rather than learning 50,000 independent scalars, which leverages CLIP's semantic geometry to reduce the optimization space and produce coherent, interpretable decompositions. For single-image decomposition, tokens are iteratively removed and the resulting image is compared to the original via CLIP; tokens whose removal causes no semantic change (CLIP score > 95) are discarded, yielding the minimal driving subset for that specific image.

## Results

Across 188 concepts, CONCEPTOR achieves the best faithfulness on all metrics: 86.2±8.3 CLIP pairwise similarity, 0.44±0.09 LPIPS (at least 10% lower than all baselines), 109.5±51.8 FID per concept, and 9.8 FID on the entire set (compared to a ground-truth train-test FID of 7.1). On token diversity (SentenceBERT dissimilarity), CONCEPTOR scores 69.8±3.4, slightly below PEZ (75.9) but with far more interpretable elements. A user study confirms that 70% of human raters prefer CONCEPTOR's decompositions over BLIP-2 captions (26.9%) and PEZ (3.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONCEPTOR outperforms all five baselines on every faithfulness metric. Against the closest competitor PCA (83.0 CLIP pairwise, 0.53 LPIPS, 130.8 FID per concept), CONCEPTOR improves CLIP pairwise by 3.2 points, reduces LPIPS by ~17%, and lowers FID per concept by ~21 points. The FID gap on the entire set is even more pronounced: CONCEPTOR achieves 9.8 versus 19.8 (PCA), 21.3 (k-means), 21.6 (NMF), 18.2 (PEZ), and 23.7 (BLIP-2 sentence). PEZ achieves the highest token diversity (75.9 vs. 69.8) but produces largely uninterpretable tokens (e.g., 'moschbizitalk', 'humpday'), violating the meaningfulness criterion. The matrix factorization baselines (NMF, PCA, k-means) cannot produce textual output at all, so their components are not human-interpretable. BLIP-2 sentence, while producing readable captions, is oversimplistic (e.g., 'man, hat, shirt' for 'rapper') and lags CONCEPTOR by 7.5 points in CLIP pairwise and 14.9 in FID per concept.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept-based interpretability paradigm (TCAV, ACE, ICE, CRAFT) from classifiers to text-to-image diffusion models, a setting where existing methods are inapplicable because they rely on prediction shifts or non-negative activations. It also draws on vocabulary-space decomposition techniques from language model interpretability (Geva et al., Ram et al.), repurposing the idea of projecting internal states onto a human-readable token space, but applied to the denoising objective of a diffusion UNet rather than a language model's feed-forward layers. The key methodological departure is learning a 2-layer MLP that maps CLIP word embeddings to coefficients, reducing the optimization from 50,000 independent weights to a semantic-space mapping, which the ablation confirms is critical for faithfulness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CONCEPTOR provides the first systematic, quantitative framework for dissecting how diffusion models internally represent concepts, revealing structures (exemplar interpolation, artistic-style reliance, homograph fusion, hidden biases) that are invisible from generated images alone. The bias-detection capability is practically significant, exposing associations like 'Jews'→'journalists' that are not visually apparent, enabling targeted debiasing via coefficient manipulation. Key limitations include restriction to single-token elements (no multi-word phrases), context-dependent token meanings that complicate interpretation, and evaluation on a single model (Stable Diffusion v2.1). Open questions include whether the decomposition is truly causal or merely correlational, whether findings generalize to other architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), and whether the 100-image training set is sufficient for rare or fine-grained concepts.
