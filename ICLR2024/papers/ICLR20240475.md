## Problem

While score-based generative models achieve remarkable empirical success in high-dimensional generation, their theoretical guarantees are limited because neural network approximation of the score function typically requires a number of parameters scaling like ε^{-d}, incurring the curse of dimensionality. This paper asks under what structural conditions on the target distribution the score function can be learned without this dimensional penalty, and provides the first dimension-independent sample complexity bounds for SGMs on a broad family of sub-Gaussian distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes N i.i.d. samples from a data distribution p_0 that is absolutely continuous with respect to the standard Gaussian, parameterized as p_0(x) ∝ exp(−‖x‖²/2 + f(x)) where f is the log-relative density. The output is a sample from the target distribution generated by simulating the reverse Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with a learned score function, accompanied by an explicit total variation bound TV(p_0, p̂) = O(ε) with a sample complexity N that is independent of the ambient dimension d. The key constraint is that f must satisfy a linear growth condition (−α‖x‖² ≤ f(x) ≤ β‖x‖² for large ‖x‖) and belong to the Barron space, ensuring the distribution's tails decay at least as fast as a Gaussian.

## Method

The central insight is that the score function of the forward diffusion process at time t is a tractable composition of the log-relative density f with Gaussian smoothing, and this composition preserves the low-complexity structure of f. Specifically, the j-th component of ∇_x log p_t(x) is expressed as (1/(1−e^{−2t}))(−x_j + e^{−t} F_t^j(x)/G_t(x)) where F_t^j and G_t are Gaussian-weighted integrals of f, and the paper proves that if f lies in the Barron space (i.e., can be approximated by shallow ReLU networks with path norm independent of d), then the score function inherits a similar Barron-space structure, yielding dimension-free L²(p_t) approximation rates. The generalization error is then controlled via the Rademacher complexity of the neural network class parameterized by path norm, and combined with existing discretization error bounds for the exponential integrator scheme to produce end-to-end total variation guarantees. The approach is validated on two concrete families: distributions whose log-relative density is itself a Barron function, and two-component Gaussian mixtures with constrained bandwidths, both yielding sample complexity of order ε^{-10} (up to prefactors) independent of d.

## Results

The paper proves that for sub-Gaussian distributions whose log-relative density f belongs to the Barron space, the total variation distance between the target distribution p₀ and the SGM output p̂ satisfies TV(p̂, p₀) = O(ε), with sample complexity N = Ω(ε^{-16-192δ} ||f||_B^4) (up to dimension-dependent prefactors). For two-component Gaussian mixtures with unit bandwidths, the bound simplifies to TV(p̂, p₀) = O(ε) with N = Ω(ε^{-24-768δ}). The key contribution is that the rate in ε is independent of the ambient dimension d, breaking the curse of dimensionality that plagues classical nonparametric density estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The paper positions its theoretical guarantees against prior SGM analysis: Oko et al. (2023) achieve minimax-optimal rates for Besov densities but with sample complexity suffering from the curse of dimensionality; Chen et al. (2023d) and De Bortoli (2022) obtain dimension-free rates but require the data to lie on a low-dimensional linear subspace or manifold. In contrast, this paper assumes no low-dimensional support structure—only that the log-relative density f is a Barron function—and still obtains a dimension-independent rate in ε. The trade-off is that the dimension appears in prefactors (e.g., (1+2α)^{12d} e^{d/2}), which the authors note as an open direction for improvement. The paper also extends beyond the log-Sobolev inequality (LSI) regime considered in earlier versions, allowing f to grow at infinity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: the theoretical analysis of score-based generative models (building on Song et al. 2021, Chen et al. 2023b, Benton et al. 2023 for discretization and distribution recovery bounds) and the approximation theory of Barron spaces for neural networks (Barron 1993, Klusowski & Barron 2018, Ma et al. 2022). It departs from prior SGM theory (Oko et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2023d) that either suffers from the curse of dimensionality or requires the data to lie on a low-dimensional manifold, by instead assuming the log-relative density with respect to a Gaussian belongs to the Barron space. This connects to the energy-based model literature (Domingo-Enrich et al. 2021a,b) and normalizing flow expressivity results (Lee et al. 2017) that also exploit Barron-space structure, but is the first to show the structure propagates through the diffusion forward process to yield dimension-free score approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous justification for why SGMs can succeed in high dimensions without low-dimensional data assumptions: low-complexity structure in the log-density (Barron space) induces a tractable composition structure in the score function that neural networks can exploit. This reframes the question from 'can neural networks approximate arbitrary high-dimensional scores?' to 'does the target distribution have a low-complexity log-density?', which is a more natural and verifiable condition. Key limitations include the sub-Gaussian tail requirement, the still-exponential prefactors in dimension, and the restriction to the Barron space (which excludes, e.g., log-densities of Gaussian mixtures with very different bandwidths). Open questions include whether the Barron assumption can be relaxed, whether the rates are minimax-optimal, and whether training dynamics (gradient flow of score matching) can be analyzed under the same assumptions.
