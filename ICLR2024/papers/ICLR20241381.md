## Problem

Existing universal approximation and memorization analyses for Transformers require excessively deep stacks of self-attention layers (2n layers by Kim et al., 2023; |V|^d+1 layers by Yun et al., 2019) to construct a contextual mapping, creating a stark discrepancy with the shallow Transformers deployed in practice. This overestimation stems from treating the softmax function as a mere approximation of hardmax, which obscures softmax's own structural properties. The paper asks: how many self-attention layers are both necessary and sufficient to capture the full context of an input sequence?

## Input / Output

The method operates on token sequences of length n with embedding dimension d, subject to a tokenwise (r_min, r_max, ε)-separatedness assumption and a no-duplicate-token constraint within each sequence. The self-attention layer produces a context id per token — a vector that uniquely identifies the (sequence, position) pair — which a downstream feed-forward network then maps to memorized labels or approximated function values. For the universal approximation result, the target functions are continuous and permutation equivariant on a compact domain in R^{d×n}, with approximation measured in L^p distance.

## Method

The central insight is that the softmax-based self-attention mechanism, when its weight matrices are rank-1 (W^(V)=W^(K)=W^(Q)=w^T for a carefully chosen unit vector w), reduces the attention computation to the Boltzmann operator boltz(a)=a^T σ_S[a], which provably preserves pairwise distances between input sequences (Lemma 1). The paper first proves a negative result: one-layer hardmax self-attention cannot be a contextual mapping because hardmax always attends to the extreme-valued tokens, making it impossible to distinguish sequences that differ only in intermediate positions. For softmax, the construction leverages a distance-preserving projection (via a lemma from Park et al., 2021) to ensure the Boltzmann operator outputs remain separated, yielding a one-layer single-head contextual mapping with arbitrary head size. This immediately implies that a one-layer Transformer (one self-attention layer plus two feed-forward layers) can memorize finite samples and is a universal approximator for continuous permutation equivariant functions. Empirically, rank-1 Transformers (head size 1, shared value/query vectors) trained on CoNLL-2003 reach 98.7% accuracy with a single layer, confirming the theoretical prediction.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with a single empirical validation on CoNLL-2003 (token classification). A 1-layer rank-1 Transformer (head size s=1, rank-1 W^(V), W^(K), W^(Q)) reaches 98.72% training accuracy after 800 epochs, nearly memorizing the dataset. At 400 epochs, the 1-layer model's accuracy is still increasing but at a slower rate than the 3-layer and 6-layer variants, suggesting that additional depth accelerates optimization rather than being necessary for expressiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baselines. The experiment is a self-contained sanity check: 1-layer, 3-layer, and 6-layer rank-1 Transformers (all with the same rank-1 self-attention architecture from Eq. 20) are trained on CoNLL-2003 to verify that the theoretically predicted memorization capacity is achievable in practice. The key finding is that the 1-layer variant already achieves near-perfect memorization (98.72% at 800 epochs), confirming the theoretical result that one self-attention layer suffices. The 3-layer and 6-layer variants converge faster, which the authors attribute to optimization dynamics rather than expressiveness, and leave as future work. No comparison with standard Transformers, BERT, or other sequence models is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the universal approximation framework established by Yun et al. (2019) and the memorization analysis of Kim et al. (2023), both of which required O(n) or more self-attention layers by treating softmax as an approximation of hardmax. The key departure is a conceptual reframe: rather than approximating softmax by hardmax, the authors exploit softmax's actual mathematical structure through the Boltzmann operator, showing that one layer of single-head, rank-1 self-attention suffices for contextual mapping. The construction also draws on Park et al. (2021) for a distance-preserving projection lemma and Zhang et al. (2016) for the feed-forward memorization component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result closes a significant gap between theoretical analyses (which required excessively deep Transformers) and practical deployments (which use few layers), suggesting that the softmax function's intrinsic properties—not its hardmax approximation—are what give self-attention its contextual power. The low-rank (rank-1) requirement also aligns with empirical observations that pre-trained Transformers operate at low intrinsic dimension. Open questions include whether the quantization feed-forward layer before self-attention is truly necessary for universal approximation, why deeper architectures learn faster in practice despite one layer being theoretically sufficient, and whether the analysis extends to non-permutation-equivariant functions without positional encodings.
