## Problem

Evaluating well-trained GNNs on real-world test graphs is infeasible under online deployment conditions because test nodes lack ground-truth labels, unknown distribution shifts exist between training and test graphs, and the original training graph is inaccessible due to privacy constraints. No existing method can estimate a GNN's generalization error on unlabeled, distribution-shifted test graphs without access to either the test labels or the training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241458/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled test graph (node feature matrix X_te ∈ R^{M×d} and adjacency matrix A_te ∈ R^{M×M}) together with a fixed, well-trained GNN model whose architecture and optimal weight parameters θ_tr* are known but whose training graph is unavailable. It outputs a single scalar LEBED score that is designed to be positively correlated (∝) with the ground-truth test error, enabling ranking of test graphs by expected model performance without any label annotations.

## Method

LEBED estimates generalization error by measuring the L2 distance between the well-trained GNN's weights and the weights of a re-trained GNN on the test graph. The re-training uses pseudo-labels generated by the well-trained GNN as supervision (node prediction discrepancy D_Pred), while a parameter-free optimality criterion based on structure reconstruction discrepancy (D_Stru) determines when re-training has converged: at each iteration, cosine-similarity-based graph structure reconstruction quality of the re-trained GNN's node representations is compared against that of the well-trained GNN's representations, and training stops when the binary cross-entropy gap falls below a threshold ε. This criterion requires no additional parameters or loss modifications, preserving a consistent learning objective between the original and re-trained models. The final LEBED score is simply ‖θ_tr* − θ_te†‖₂, capturing both node semantic and structural representation discrepancies in a single model-level metric.

## Results

LEBED achieves consistently the strongest Spearman rank correlation (ρ) and linear fit (R²) with ground-truth test errors across all six datasets and three distribution shift types. On node feature shifts, LEBED reaches an average ρ=0.76 (R²=0.595) on Cora and ρ=0.78 (R²=0.601) on Amazon-Photo across all GNNs. Under domain shifts, it achieves ρ=0.76 (R²=0.615) on DBLPv8, and under temporal shifts on OGB-arxiv it reaches ρ=0.56 (R²=0.287). In contrast, the best baseline (Entropy) only reaches ρ=0.59 on Cora and ρ=0.29 on DBLPv8, while ATC and Threshold-based methods frequently exhibit negative correlations, indicating they are unreliable for online GNN evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEBED outperforms all four baseline categories across every dataset and distribution shift type. ConfScore and Entropy (adapted from CNN evaluation) show moderate positive correlations (ρ≈0.3–0.6) but with substantially lower R² values (typically 0.1–0.35 vs. LEBED's 0.25–0.62), indicating weaker linear predictive power. ATC-MC and ATC-NE (from Garg et al., ICLR 2022) frequently produce negative Spearman correlations (e.g., ρ=-0.51 on Cora, ρ=-0.34 on ACMv9), meaning higher ATC scores paradoxically correspond to lower test errors, rendering them unreliable. Threshold-based methods (Deng & Zheng, CVPR 2021) similarly exhibit predominantly negative correlations across domain and temporal shifts (e.g., ρ=-0.33 on ACMv9, ρ=-0.26 on OGB-arxiv). The paper attributes these failures to the baselines' inability to account for GNN-specific neighbor aggregation mechanisms and the non-Euclidean structural characteristics of graph data, which LEBED captures through its re-training-based learning behavior discrepancy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on predicting model generalization error (Yu et al., 2022; Deng & Zheng, 2021; Garg et al., 2022) from CNN classifiers on Euclidean image data to GNNs on non-Euclidean graph structural data, with the additional constraint that the training graph is inaccessible in online deployment. The key methodological departure from Yu et al. (2022), which computed parameter-space projection norms for CNNs, is the incorporation of a parameter-free structure reconstruction criterion that leverages the inherent graph topology as a self-supervised stopping signal for GNN re-training. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from unsupervised graph domain adaptation and OOD generalization, which aim to improve model performance rather than evaluate a fixed, well-trained model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap in GNN deployment: how to assess model reliability on real-world unlabeled graphs when the training data is unavailable due to privacy constraints. The assumption that the label space remains unchanged across training and test graphs (covariate shift only) is a significant limitation that the authors acknowledge, and extending to label-space shifts or open-set scenarios remains open. The method's correlation strength degrades notably under domain and temporal shifts (average ρ ≈ 0.52) compared to feature shifts (ρ ≈ 0.76), suggesting the learning-behavior discrepancy signal becomes less discriminative when multiple distributional axes shift simultaneously. The re-training requirement also introduces computational overhead that may limit applicability to very large graphs in real-time serving.
