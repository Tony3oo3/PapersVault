## Problem

While teleportation (applying parameter space symmetries to jump to steeper points on the same loss level set) empirically accelerates optimization, the mechanism behind its success was not well understood—previous work showed momentary gradient increases but could not prove overall faster convergence. Additionally, the relationship between the geometric curvature of the minimum manifold (as distinct from Hessian-based sharpness) and generalization remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242151/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes current neural network parameters w, a symmetry group G (typically GL(R) between MLP layers) acting on the parameter space, and the loss gradient. For convergence, it performs gradient ascent on the Lie algebra to find the group element maximizing gradient norm; for generalization, it optimizes the group element to change sharpness φ (averaged loss over random perturbations) or curvature ψ (mean curvature of k random curves on the minimum manifold, requiring second and third derivatives of the loss). The output is the teleported parameter vector g·w at the same loss value, or in the meta-learning variant, a pair (local update f_t, group element g_t) produced by two LSTM meta-optimizers.

## Method

The paper provides three theoretical results: (1) SGD with teleportation converges to a basin of stationary points where every point on the symmetry orbit is also stationary (Theorem 3.1); (2) one teleportation followed by one gradient step equals a damped Newton step, yielding a quadratically contracting convergence term absent in vanilla gradient descent (Proposition 3.2); (3) sufficient conditions under which a single teleportation guarantees the entire subsequent gradient flow remains optimal (Propositions 3.4–3.6, via Lie bracket analysis). For generalization, the authors introduce a curvature metric ψ(w,k) measuring the mean curvature of the minimum manifold along k random Lie-algebra directions, show it is negatively correlated with validation loss on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST/CIFAR-10, and demonstrate that teleporting toward larger curvatures improves test performance. Finally, teleportation is integrated into AdaGrad, momentum, RMSProp, and Adam, and a meta-learning variant uses two LSTM optimizers to jointly learn the local update direction and the teleportation group element, eliminating the need for explicit optimization on the group manifold.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, teleporting to points with larger curvature of minima produces a more significant improvement in validation loss compared to teleporting to flatter (less sharp) points, which only yields a slight validation loss reduction. Sharpness (φ) shows strong positive Pearson correlation with validation loss across all three datasets (0.704 on MNIST, 0.790 on Fashion-MNIST, 0.899 on CIFAR-10), while curvature (ψ) shows negative correlation (−0.050, −0.232, −0.167 respectively). In the meta-learning experiment on two-layer networks, the proposed LSTM(update,tele) meta-optimizer achieves faster convergence than both the LSTM(update)-only baseline and the LSTM(lr,tele) baseline, demonstrating that jointly learning local updates and teleportation group elements outperforms learning either in isolation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the meta-learning experiment (Figure 6), the proposed LSTM(update,tele) method, which jointly learns the local update direction f_t and the teleportation group element g_t, achieves a faster convergence rate than the LSTM(update) baseline (which learns only the local update, corresponding to the Andrychowicz et al. 2016 approach) and the LSTM(lr,tele) baseline (which learns only the group element and learning rate). The improvement over LSTM(update) is consistent across 5 unseen test trajectories, confirming that adding the non-local teleportation degree of freedom provides a meaningful optimization advantage beyond local gradient-based updates. For standard optimizers on MNIST (Figure 5), a single teleportation at the first epoch improves convergence for AdaGrad, SGD with momentum, RMSProp, and Adam, with the runtime cost of teleportation being smaller than one epoch of training. In the generalization experiment on CIFAR-10 (Figure 4), teleporting to increase curvature yields a more pronounced validation loss improvement than teleporting to decrease sharpness, suggesting curvature is a more actionable target for generalization than sharpness in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symmetry teleportation framework introduced by Zhao et al. (2022) and Armenta et al. (2023), which used parameter-space symmetries to jump to steeper points on loss level sets for faster optimization. The key departure is providing the first theoretical convergence guarantees for teleportation-augmented SGD, showing it converges to a basin of stationary points and exhibiting a quadratic (Newton-like) convergence term. The paper also extends the teleportation objective beyond gradient-norm maximization to include sharpness and a novel curvature-of-minima metric, and integrates teleportation into meta-learning by augmenting the Andrychowicz et al. (2016) LSTM optimizer with a second network that learns the group element, moving beyond purely local parameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantees resolve a key open question about why teleportation works, showing it is not merely a heuristic but has a principled connection to Newton's method with quadratic convergence. The curvature-of-minima concept opens a new axis for understanding generalization beyond the well-studied sharpness/flatness paradigm, though the empirical evidence (weak negative correlations, single-dataset experiments with 3-layer MLPs) is preliminary. Open questions include the theoretical mechanism linking curvature to generalization under distribution shift, extension to convolutional and graph architectures, and whether the meta-learned teleportation approach scales beyond the toy 2-layer setting. The practical impact is currently limited by the need to know the symmetry group structure of the architecture.
