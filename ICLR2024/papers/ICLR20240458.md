## Problem

Existing post-training sparsification methods for LLMs (e.g., SparseGPT 2:4) introduce sparsity patterns that require additional data structures and yield limited end-to-end speedup on current hardware, while structured pruning approaches typically demand expensive recovery fine-tuning. The key gap is that no prior method reduces the embedding dimension of the network itself, meaning intermediate signal matrices remain full-size and the computational savings are constrained to individual weight matrices.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full pre-trained LLM weight matrices and a small calibration set (e.g., 1024 sequences of length 2048 from WikiText-2 or Alpaca) to compute per-block PCA transformations. The output is a dense model with a reduced embedding dimension D: all weight matrices are physically smaller (rows and columns deleted), all intermediate signal matrices shrink accordingly, and small D×D rotation matrices are inserted into residual connections. No sparsity structure or auxiliary data is needed at inference time, so the compressed model runs as a standard dense transformer with fewer parameters.

## Method

SliceGPT exploits a computational invariance of transformer networks: because RMSNorm commutes with orthogonal transformations, one can multiply each block's input weights by Q^T and output weights by Q (for any orthogonal Q) without changing predictions. The method first converts all LayerNorm blocks to RMSNorm by absorbing their linear components into adjacent weight matrices, then computes a per-block orthogonal matrix Q_ℓ via PCA on the calibration signal at each layer. After applying these transformations, it slices off the rows of W_in and columns of W_out corresponding to the minor principal components, reducing the embedding dimension. Because different blocks use different rotations, small Q_{ℓ-1}^T Q_ℓ matrices are inserted into residual connections to maintain invariance. The entire procedure is single-shot (no gradient updates), completing in 1–3 hours on a single GPU, with optional LoRA-based recovery fine-tuning to further close the performance gap.

## Results

SliceGPT achieves WikiText-2 perplexity of 4.60 for LLAMA-2 70B and 9.68 for OPT 66B at 25% slicing, compared to 3.32 and 9.33 for the dense models. On zero-shot tasks, 25%-sliced LLAMA-2 70B with Alpaca calibration and RFT achieves 74.3% average accuracy (vs. 76.6% dense), and Phi-2 at 25% slicing retains 90.3% of dense accuracy. On 24GB RTX6000 GPUs, total inference compute for LLAMA-2 70B drops from 1764 to 1075 GPUms (64%), and on 40GB A100s from 500 to 330 GPUms (66%), while requiring fewer GPUs (7→5 and 4→3 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is SparseGPT with 2:4 structured sparsity, the only sparsity scheme that achieves end-to-end speedup on current hardware. SliceGPT at 25% slicing achieves lower WikiText-2 perplexity than SparseGPT 2:4 across all LLAMA-2 sizes (4.60 vs. 4.98 for 70B; 6.30 vs. 7.07 for 13B; 7.24 vs. 8.69 for 7B) and for OPT models above 2.7B (9.68 vs. 10.22 for 66B; 10.04 vs. 10.53 for 30B). In per-operation timing on A100 GPUs, SliceGPT 25% matches SparseGPT 2:4 at 1.32× speedup for LLAMA-2 70B and slightly exceeds it at 1.33× for OPT 66B, while SparseGPT 2:4 provides better speedup for smaller models (1.48× vs. 1.33× for LLAMA-2 7B). The critical structural advantage of SliceGPT is that it reduces the embedding dimension of the signal matrix X, enabling fewer GPUs and larger batch sizes (e.g., 6.26× throughput for OPT 66B at 50% slicing on H100), which SparseGPT cannot achieve since it only sparsifies weight matrices without shrinking the activation dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

SliceGPT departs from the dominant line of LLM pruning work (SparseGPT, GPTQ, OBC) that zeros out individual or semi-structured weight entries, instead removing entire rows and columns to reduce the embedding dimension. Conceptually it is closest to channel/filter pruning in the convnet literature (ThiNet, He et al.), but the key departure is the theoretical foundation: the paper proves a computational invariance property showing that orthogonal transformations commute with RMSNorm, enabling a PCA-based rotation that aligns the signal with its principal components before slicing. Unlike low-rank factorization methods, SliceGPT replaces each weight matrix with a single smaller dense matrix rather than a product of two, and unlike prior structured-pruning approaches for LLMs (LLM-Pruner, LLM Surgeon), it operates as a single-shot method without requiring extensive recovery fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The computational invariance theorem is a clean, general result about transformer architectures that could inspire further compression techniques beyond slicing. Practically, the method reduces the GPU count needed to serve 70B-parameter models (from 4 to 3 on A100s, from 7 to 5 on consumer GPUs) with no code changes, which has direct cost and energy implications for deployment. Limitations include degraded performance on smaller models (<13B) and on Phi-2, where 25% slicing retains only ~90% accuracy even with RFT; the paper also notes that per-layer adaptive slicing helps OPT but hurts LLAMA-2, suggesting the method's effectiveness depends on the eigenvalue spectrum of intermediate signals. Open questions include combining slicing with quantization, finding better Q computation strategies, and whether the approach extends to encoder-decoder or non-RMSNorm architectures.
