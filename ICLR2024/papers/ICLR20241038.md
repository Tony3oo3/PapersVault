## Problem

Equivariant diffusion models for 3D de novo molecule generation have achieved strong results, but the design space remains poorly understood: it is unclear whether continuous or discrete state spaces, epsilon or x0 parameterization, and uniform or time-dependent loss weighting actually matter for molecular data. Additionally, these models struggle with large molecular structures and limited training data, and no systematic comparison isolating each design choice has been performed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241038/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a noisy molecular state consisting of 3D atomic coordinates (perturbed with Gaussian noise), one-hot encoded chemical elements, formal charges, and bond types (perturbed with categorical transition kernels), along with a scalar timestep embedding. For structure-based ligand design, a fixed protein pocket point cloud is concatenated as additional context. The output is a predicted clean molecular graph: 3D coordinates, element assignments, bond topology (single/double/triple/aromatic/none), and optionally hybridization states and aromaticity flags. Molecules are represented as fully connected graphs over all atom pairs, with self-loops excluded.

## Method

EQGAT-diff is an E(3)-equivariant graph attention network (12 layers, 12.3M parameters) that uses rotation-equivariant vector features as learnable vector bundles, updated via tensor products of scalar features with normalized relative positions. The key architectural insight is combining Gaussian diffusion for continuous coordinates with categorical diffusion for discrete modalities (elements, bonds, charges) under an x0 parameterization, where the network directly predicts the clean data sample rather than the noise. A critical training contribution is the time-dependent loss weighting w_s(t) = max(0.05, min(1.5, SNR(t))), which up-weights timesteps near the data distribution and dramatically accelerates convergence and improves molecule stability. For data efficiency, the model is pre-trained on ~95.7M PubChem3D molecules with implicit hydrogens (reducing graph size and training time), then fine-tuned for only a few epochs on target distributions like GEOM-Drugs or Crossdocked, consistently outperforming models trained from scratch even on 100% of the target data.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the best 1-Lipschitz method (Cayley transform) achieves 65.2% clean accuracy and 42.1% certified robust accuracy at epsilon=8/255. On Tiny-ImageNet the same approach reaches 28.7% clean accuracy. Training time is 1.8x slower than the fastest baseline but delivers the best certified robustness among all methods compared.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five 1-Lipschitz construction methods on a common protocol. The Cayley transform orthogonalization [3] achieves the best certified robust accuracy across all datasets but has moderate training overhead. Almost-orthogonal layers [7] are faster to train but sacrifice 2-3% certified accuracy. LOT [18] achieves competitive certified accuracy with the highest training cost (3.8x). Spectral normalization is the fastest but provides the weakest certified bounds. The unified algebraic approach [15] shows that several methods are mathematically equivalent, explaining their similar empirical performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the EQGAT equivariant graph attention architecture (Le et al., 2022) and the equivariant diffusion framework of EDM (Hoogeboom et al., 2022), while incorporating the categorical diffusion formalism of Austin et al. (2021) and the x0-parameterization with adaptive noise scheduling from MiDi (Vignac et al., 2023). Its key departure is a systematic ablation of design choices—parameterization (ε vs. x0), state-space type (continuous vs. categorical), and loss weighting—that prior molecular diffusion works had not isolated, effectively mapping the design space that EDM and MiDi each explored only partially. The paper further extends the lineage with two novel components absent from all prior work: a truncated-SNR time-dependent loss weighting and a pre-training/transfer-learning protocol on PubChem3D with implicit hydrogens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic ablation provides practitioners with concrete, evidence-based guidance on which architectural and training choices matter for 3D molecular diffusion, reducing the trial-and-error that has characterized the field. The finding that a simple truncated-SNR loss weighting substantially improves convergence and sample quality, and that pre-training with implicit hydrogens transfers better than explicit hydrogens, are practically actionable insights for anyone building molecular generative models. Open questions remain around whether DDIM-style accelerated sampling can be made to work for mixed continuous/discrete modalities, whether the approach scales to molecules beyond ~15 heavy atoms, and how post-processing pipelines (as in MolDiff) affect fair comparison across methods.
