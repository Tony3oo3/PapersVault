## Problem

Existing hyper-network-based model editors like MEND scale poorly to simultaneous editing of many facts: naively summing per-token parameter shifts causes cancellation effects across contradictory edits, and concatenating the hyper-network to the LM for meta-loss backpropagation demands memory proportional to the number of edits, quickly exceeding hardware limits. This paper addresses both the aggregation and memory bottlenecks to enable editing thousands of facts in a single operation.

## Input / Output

The method takes m prompt-answer edit tuples and the frozen LM, from which it caches keys u_{ℓ,j} and value gradients ∇_{v_{ℓ,j}}L for selected linear layers ℓ and answer-producing tokens j. The hyper-network g_θ maps each (u, ∇_v L) pair to a rank-1 parameter shift S_j, which are then aggregated into a single shift S* per layer via the normal equation. The output is the updated weight matrix W̃_ℓ = W_ℓ + S* for each edited layer, with the regularization coefficient λ_ℓ also learned during meta-training.

## Method

MALMEN retains MEND's low-rank hyper-network architecture but replaces the naive summation of parameter shifts with a regularized least-squares aggregation: given key matrix U and value-difference matrix D (where d_j = S_j u_j), the optimal shift is S* = DU^T(UU^T + λI)^{-1}, solved in closed form via the normal equation. To handle memory, the meta-gradient computation is decomposed into two stages: first, the meta-loss is backpropagated through the LM in arbitrary batches to obtain ∇_{W̃_ℓ}L_meta (constant size regardless of edit count); second, the hyper-network gradient is computed via a proxy loss L̃ = Σ tr(∇_{D_ℓ}L_meta^T D_ℓ) that routes gradients back through g_θ without caching intermediate activations across the full edit set. The regularization parameter λ_ℓ is jointly learned with the hyper-network weights, and the entire procedure is differentiable end-to-end via manually derived chain-rule terms (Theorems 1–2).

## Results

MALMEN achieves strong scalability in synchronous editing, editing up to 16,384 facts (2^14) on GPT-J (6B) and T5-XL (2.8B) while maintaining high Editing Success (ES ≈ 99.7%) and Generalization Success (GS ≈ 92.8%) on zsRE. On GPT-J, MALMEN edits more than two orders of magnitude more facts than MEND with comparable performance, and outperforms MEMIT, an editor specifically designed for GPT architectures. Computation time for editing 16,384 facts on GPT-J is 12.25 hours for MALMEN versus 33.75 hours for MEMIT on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU. On smaller models (BERT-base 110M, GPT-2 124M), MALMEN sustains strong ES and GS up to 4,096 edits where MEND degrades sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MALMEN is compared against MEND (Mitchell et al., 2022) and MEMIT (Meng et al., 2023). Against MEND, MALMEN achieves comparable or superior ES and GS while editing over an order of magnitude more facts on small models (BERT-base, GPT-2) and more than two orders of magnitude more on GPT-J, where MEND's performance degrades sharply beyond ~100 edits due to the cancellation effect in summing parameter shifts. The ablation in Table 1 quantifies this: replacing the normal equation with MEND's summing strategy drops ES from 99.7% to 52.2% and GS from 96.1% to 50.7% on GPT-2 at m=512. Against MEMIT, MALMEN outperforms on GPT-J in both editing quality and efficiency, completing 16,384 edits in 12.25 hours versus MEMIT's 33.75 hours on a single A100 GPU. On T5-XL, MALMEN's LS is slightly lower than the leading method by less than 0.02, but it maintains strong ES and GS at the largest edit counts tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

MALMEN builds directly on MEND (Mitchell et al., 2022), retaining its hyper-network architecture and gradient-conditioned parameter shift generation, but replaces the ad-hoc summing of shifts (which mimics gradient accumulation) with a principled least-squares aggregation solved via the normal equation. The key-value memory interpretation of FFN linear layers, borrowed from MEMIT (Meng et al., 2023), motivates the formulation: each shift should map its corresponding key to a target value difference, and the optimal aggregate shift is the one that best satisfies all such constraints simultaneously. The memory-economic training procedure, which decouples back-propagation between the hyper-network and the LM, is a novel contribution not present in prior meta-learning editors (De Cao et al., 2021; Mitchell et al., 2022; Hase et al., 2023b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By scaling simultaneous editing from tens to over 16,000 facts on a 6B model with a single GPU, MALMEN makes hyper-network-based model editing viable for real-world knowledge correction at industrial scale, where retraining after every few edits is prohibitive. The normal equation formulation is a clean, general fix for the cancellation effect that plagues shift-summing, and the memory decomposition trick is broadly applicable to any meta-learning setup with large batch inputs. Open questions remain: the meta-gradient computation still scales linearly in the number of edits, generalization to answer-side rephrasings (not just prompt-side) is incomplete, and whether the approach extends to sequential (non-simultaneous) editing at scale is unaddressed.
