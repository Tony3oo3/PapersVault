## Problem

Existing equivariant learning methods (Fourier Transform, Group Convolution) require explicit knowledge of how a group acts on data and assume the action is linear on input units (e.g., pixels). When the group action is nonlinear or implicit—as in fisheye-deformed image sequences or 2D renderings of rotating 3D objects—these methods cannot be applied, leaving a gap in learning equivariant structure from data where the symmetry is hidden behind an unknown nonlinear observation process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240264/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

NFT consumes short tuples or sequences of observations (e.g., consecutive image frames of length T=2–4, or 1D time-series signals of length 128) in which the group action is implicit and potentially nonlinear. The encoder produces a latent representation of shape ℝ^{a×m} that is block-diagonalized into a/2 frequency blocks (each a 2×2 rotation matrix for cyclic groups, or Wigner D matrices for SO(3)), and the decoder reconstructs the observation. The latent dimension directly caps the number of recoverable frequencies, making the spectral analysis data-dependent and compressive.

## Method

NFT learns a nonlinear encoder Φ and decoder Ψ such that the group action becomes linear in the latent space: Φ(g∘x) = M(g)Φ(x), with M(g) block-diagonalized into irreducible representations (frequencies). Training minimizes an autoencoder-type reconstruction loss E[‖g∘X − Ψ(M(g)Φ(X))‖²], where M(g) is either estimated from data (U-NFT via MSP), parameterized by known group structure (G-NFT), or fixed from known group elements (g-NFT). The encoder/decoder architectures (MLP, CNN, Vision Transformer) carry no inductive bias about the group action. Theoretically, the paper proves that a linear equivariant latent feature exists if and only if a G-invariant kernel exists on the data space (Thm 4.1), and that finite-dimensional NFT automatically selects the dominant irreducible representations as a data-dependent nonlinear Fourier filter (Thm 4.2).

## Results

On time-warped 1D signal compression, g-NFT achieves a reconstruction MSE of 3.59×10⁻⁵ (noiseless) and 2.53×10⁻³ (σ=0.1), outperforming G-NFT (1.98×10⁻² and 1.16×10⁻¹) and DFT (8.10) by orders of magnitude. For spectral analysis, NFT recovers the 5 major frequencies from 7 candidate frequencies with false-negative rate 0.035 and false-positive rate 0.022. On OOD classification (rotFashion-MNIST, rotKuzushiji-MNIST), g-NFT-norm consistently outperforms SimCLR, IAE, and both Steerable CNN variants, while supervised SteerableCNN wins only in-domain on rotMNIST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

g-NFT dramatically outperforms DFT on time-warped signals (3.59×10⁻⁵ vs. 8.10 noiseless MSE), confirming that standard Fourier analysis fails under nonlinear deformation while NFT captures the hidden linear structure. g-NFT also beats G-NFT by roughly 3 orders of magnitude, showing that knowing the specific group element g (not just the group G) yields substantially better compression. On OOD classification with rotated MNIST, g-NFT-norm consistently eclipses SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020), IAE (Winter et al., 2022), and both C_n and SO(2) Steerable CNNs (Cohen & Welling, 2017; Weiler et al., 2018) on rotFashion-MNIST and rotKuzushiji-MNIST. However, supervised SO(2) SteerableCNN outperforms all NFT variants in-domain on rotMNIST, which the authors attribute to task-specific frequency overfitting in the steerable architecture. For novel view synthesis on ModelNet10, BRDFs, and ABO, g-NFT with Wigner D matrices (l_max=8) and a Vision Transformer backbone produces visually accurate 3D rotation predictions from a single 2D rendering, resolving occluded regions that pixel-based methods cannot handle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the unsupervised equivariant learning approach of Miyato et al. (2022, MSP) into a unified framework called Neural Fourier Transform (NFT), providing the theoretical foundations that MSP lacked. It departs from group convolution and steerable CNNs (Cohen & Welling, Weiler et al.), which require the group to act linearly on the input space, by learning a nonlinear encoder that maps data to a latent space where the group action becomes linear. The theoretical core connects equivariant representation learning to group invariant kernels via RKHS, extending the classical Fourier analysis framework to arbitrary data spaces with implicit or nonlinear group actions, and relates to Koopman operator theory in its goal of linearizing nonlinear dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal existence and uniqueness guarantees for learning linear equivariant features from data with nonlinear group actions, resolving a foundational question in equivariant representation learning. The 'nonlinear Fourier filter' theorem (Thm 4.2) explains why NFT automatically selects dominant frequencies, giving practitioners a principled understanding of what the learned features capture. Key limitations remain: NFT requires tuple/sequence data to infer the action, does not preserve coefficient magnitudes in the latent space, and the authors acknowledge that no convergence guarantee exists for the autoencoder loss to find the optimal solution. Open questions include whether these guarantees extend to non-compact groups and whether the optimization landscape is benign in practice.
