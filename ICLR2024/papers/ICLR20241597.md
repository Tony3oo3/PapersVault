## Problem

Multi-agent reinforcement learning struggles to simultaneously handle decentralization, partial observability, and scalability to many agents, which are all essential for engineering collective behavior in swarms. Mean field control (MFC) provides a scalable framework for cooperative multi-agent systems, but existing MFC-based RL methods assume full observability and centralized coordination, leaving a gap for the broad class of decentralized, partially observable systems (e.g., Vicsek or Kuramoto swarms where agents see only local neighbor information). No discrete-time MFC algorithm with theoretical optimality guarantees had been proposed under partial information and decentralization outside of special linear-quadratic cases.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives only a local observation (e.g., the distribution of relative headings of neighbors within an interaction radius), while the system is initialized from a known state distribution. During centralized training, the algorithm additionally observes the empirical mean field and samples a single shared lower-level policy for all agents (correlating their actions); during decentralized execution, agents act independently from their local observations alone. The output is a pair of policies: an upper-level policy mapping the mean field to parameters of a lower-level policy, and the lower-level policy itself mapping each agent's observation to a stochastic action, with all agents sharing the same lower-level policy by permutation invariance.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-step reduction that transforms the intractable Dec-POMDP into a single-agent MDP: (1) taking the infinite-agent limit via propagation of chaos to obtain the Dec-POMFC, (2) exploiting the deterministic nature of the limiting mean field to allow policies to condition on it (Dec-MFC), and (3) reformulating as an MDP whose states are mean field distributions and whose actions are joint state-observation-action distributions parameterized by a shared lower-level policy. Training proceeds via Dec-POMFPPO, a PPO-based policy gradient method that operates on a finite N-agent system during training (observing the MF and correlating actions) but yields decentralized execution policies. A key algorithmic innovation is the use of RBF kernel representations for both the mean field (replacing histogram discretization) and the lower-level policy, which ensures Lipschitz continuity required for approximation guarantees and avoids the exponential-in-dimensionality cost of grid-based partitions. Theoretical results include a dynamic programming principle with existence of optimal stationary deterministic policies, ε-optimality of Dec-POMFC solutions in the finite Dec-POMDP, and a policy gradient approximation theorem showing that centralized training on the finite system recovers the true gradient of the underlying Dec-MFC MDP as N→∞.

## Results

On N=200-agent collective behavior tasks (Aggregation, Vicsek on torus/Möbius strip/projective plane/Klein bottle, Kuramoto on torus), Dec-POMFPPO achieves episode returns on par with state-of-the-art IPPO and MAPPO, with training stabilized by mean-field guidance and reduction to a single-agent MDP. The learned policies achieve a polar order parameter exceeding 0.9 across all topologies, reaching approximately 0.99 on the torus. In terms of computational efficiency, RBF-based MFC training steps take 5.6–8.3 seconds (d=2–5) versus 146–147 seconds for IPPO, and the RBF approach outperforms histogram discretization for dimensions d≥4 (e.g., 6.97s vs. 17.26s at d=4, 8.31s vs. 76.33s at d=5). Performance converges to its mean-field limit as N increases, supporting the theoretical approximation guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Dec-POMFPPO against two state-of-the-art MARL baselines: IPPO (independent PPO, Yu et al. 2022) and MAPPO (multi-agent PPO, Papoudakis et al. 2021). On all evaluated tasks with N=200 agents, Dec-POMFPPO achieves episode returns that are 'usually on par' with both IPPO and MAPPO, as shown in the training curves of Figures 3–4 and the appendix comparisons (Figures 23–24). The comparison is primarily qualitative via training curves rather than a single scalar metric, with Dec-POMFPPO matching or slightly trailing the best IPPO/MAPPO runs in some settings (e.g., Kuramoto) while showing more stable training due to mean-field guidance. A key practical advantage is computational: each Dec-POMFPPO training step is roughly 20× faster than IPPO (e.g., 5.64s vs. 146.58s at d=2), and the RBF kernel parametrization avoids the exponential blowup of histogram-based MFC in higher dimensions. The paper does not report a clear numerical win over IPPO/MAPPO in final performance; the contribution is primarily theoretical (optimality guarantees, tractable MDP reduction) and in enabling scalable, partially observable collective behavior with rigorous guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean field control (MFC) MDP reformulation of Carmona et al. (2019b) and Gu et al. (2021) from fully observable to decentralized, partially observable settings, building on MFC approximation theorems (Gu et al., 2021; Mondal et al., 2022; Cui et al., 2023) and the MFG theory of Huang et al. (2006). It departs from pairwise mean field MARL (Yang et al., 2018; Subramanian et al., 2021, 2022) by using a global mean field rather than neighbor-based approximations, and from standard Dec-POMDPs (Bernstein et al., 2002) by exploiting permutation invariance to reduce NEXP-hard problems to a tractable single-agent MDP. The kernel-based parametrization of mean fields and lower-level policies is a novel contribution that improves upon the histogram/discretization approach used in prior MFC learning (Carmona et al., 2019b; Gu et al., 2021), which fails Lipschitzness guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first rigorous optimality guarantees for decentralized, partially observable cooperative multi-agent control via mean field, reducing complex Dec-POMDPs to MDPs with the same complexity as fully observable MFC — a conceptual simplification that opens the door to applying standard single-agent RL theory to collective behavior problems. The kernel method's advantage over histograms in high dimensions (76s vs 8s wall-clock at d=5) makes the approach practically viable for higher-dimensional state spaces. Key limitations include restriction to non-stochastic mean fields (no common noise), permutation-invariant agents only, and the need for MF observation during training (CTDE paradigm). Open questions include extending to stochastic MFs, analyzing sample efficiency, incorporating history-dependent policies, and handling physical constraints like collisions or sparser interaction graphs.
