## Problem

Existing membership inference attack methods for detecting whether a text was in a model's training data rely on reference models trained on similar data distributions, which is impractical for pretraining data whose composition is undisclosed and whose scale makes retraining prohibitively expensive. Additionally, pretraining exposes each instance only once (unlike multi-epoch fine-tuning), reducing memorization and making detection inherently harder. The paper asks: can we detect pretraining data membership using only black-box token probabilities from the target LLM, with no reference model and no knowledge of the pretraining corpus?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242026/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a text sequence and black-box access to the target LLM to obtain per-token conditional probabilities p(x_i | x_1,...,x_{i-1}); no model weights, gradients, or auxiliary models are needed. It outputs a scalar membership score (the average log-likelihood of the k% lowest-probability tokens) and a binary member/non-member decision via a threshold. The sole hyperparameter is k (default 20%), controlling what fraction of tokens are treated as 'outliers' for the score computation.

## Method

MIN-K% PROB is a reference-free detection method built on the hypothesis that unseen text tends to contain a few outlier tokens with very low conditional probabilities, while seen text is less likely to exhibit such extreme low-probability tokens. For a given sequence, it computes the log-likelihood of every token given its left context, selects the k% tokens with the minimum probabilities (the Min-K% set), and averages their log-likelihoods as the detection score. A higher (less negative) average indicates the text is more likely a member of the pretraining data. The approach requires no shadow model, no additional training, and no knowledge of the pretraining distribution, departing from prior MIA methods that calibrate target-model probabilities against a reference model trained on shadow data. The key hyperparameter k=20% was selected via a small sweep on a held-out validation set and used uniformly across all experiments.

## Results

On WIKIMIA, MIN-K% PROB achieves an average AUC of 0.72 across five LLMs (Pythia-2.8B, NeoX-20B, LLaMA-30B, LLaMA-65B, OPT-66B), a 7.4% improvement over the best baseline (PPL at 0.67). In the paraphrase setting, MIN-K% PROB maintains a 0.70 average AUC versus 0.65 for the best baseline. For downstream contamination detection (Table 3), MIN-K% PROB achieves 0.86 average AUC vs. 0.84 for PPL, and for copyrighted book detection on GPT-3, it reaches 0.88 AUC vs. 0.84 for PPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIN-K% PROB outperforms all five baseline methods (Neighbor, PPL, Zlib, Lowercase, Smaller Ref) in 9 out of 10 model-setting columns on WIKIMIA, with the sole exception being LLaMA-65B original where Smaller Ref (0.74) ties MIN-K% PROB (0.74). The strongest baseline is PPL (perplexity-based LOSS Attack from Yeom et al., 2018), which achieves 0.67 average AUC; MIN-K% PROB improves upon it by 7.4% in absolute AUC. The neighborhood-based method (Neighbor, from Mattern et al. 2023 / DetectGPT) is the weakest reference-free baseline at 0.65 average AUC, particularly struggling in paraphrase settings (0.58–0.69). The Carlini et al. (2021) variants (Zlib, Lowercase, Smaller Ref) are inconsistent: Zlib performs well on original text but degrades sharply under paraphrase (e.g., 0.54 on Pythia-2.8B Para.), while Smaller Ref requires training a smaller reference model on the same data, making it impractical for black-box pretraining data. In the downstream contamination setting (Table 3), MIN-K% PROB again leads all baselines, achieving 0.86 average AUC vs. 0.84 for PPL and 0.66 for Neighbor, with the largest margin on TruthfulQA (0.74 vs. 0.71 for PPL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the membership inference attack (MIA) framework from Shokri et al. (2016) and its NLP fine-tuning applications (Song & Shmatikov, 2019; Shejwalkar et al., 2021) to the substantially harder setting of pretraining data detection, where each example is seen only once across trillions of tokens. It departs from prior MIA methods (Carlini et al., 2022; Watson et al., 2022) that require shadow reference models trained on the same data distribution, which is infeasible for pretraining due to data unavailability and computational cost. The proposed MIN-K% PROB method repurposes the simple intuition behind perplexity-based detection (Yeom et al., 2018) by focusing specifically on outlier tokens rather than aggregate sequence-level statistics, and is conceptually related to DetectGPT's probability curvature approach (Mitchell et al., 2023) but operates at the token level without requiring perturbation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reference-free nature of MIN-K% PROB makes pretraining data auditing practically feasible for the first time, with direct implications for copyright enforcement, privacy compliance, and benchmark integrity in the era of opaque LLM training pipelines. The WIKIMIA benchmark's dynamic, automated construction provides a reusable evaluation infrastructure for future models. Open questions include whether detection generalizes beyond Wikipedia-sourced data, how performance scales to frontier models (e.g., GPT-4 class), and whether paraphrase detection can be made more robust. The finding that detection difficulty depends on whether contaminants are distributional outliers versus in-distribution samples complicates the design of contamination audits for real-world pretraining corpora.
