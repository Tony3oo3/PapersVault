## Problem

Existing linear transformation-based methods for connecting frozen vision models to frozen LLMs (e.g., LiMBeR, FROMAGe) rely on direct representation comparison or contrastive objectives that inherently preserve the modality gap between independently pretrained unimodal models. This limits their effectiveness, especially for vision encoders without linguistic pretraining (e.g., BEiT vs. CLIP), and fails to fully bridge complex embedding spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240584/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VLAP takes an RGB image processed by a frozen vision encoder (BEiT or CLIP ViT-B/32) and a short text prompt (e.g., 'A photo of' for captioning, 'Question: {} Answer:' for VQA), producing free-form text output via a frozen LLM (OPT-1.3B or T5-Base). Only a single linear projection layer (~0.6M parameters) is trained; both the vision encoder and LLM remain entirely frozen. For retrieval tasks, the projected visual and text representations are directly compared by cosine similarity without invoking the LLM's generative pathway.

## Method

VLAP bridges vision and language spaces by formulating cross-modal alignment as an optimal transport-based assignment prediction problem over the LLM's frozen word embeddings. Rather than directly comparing visual and text representations, both modalities are assigned to the most relevant word embeddings via a Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, with the transport polytope constrained by the dataset's word frequency distribution (rather than uniform assignment). The key training signal is predicting one modality's word assignment from the other modality's representation, enforcing that paired image-text data share the same linguistic information. This assignment prediction loss is combined with a standard prefix language modeling (captioning) loss, and both are optimized simultaneously to train only the linear projection layer. Using the LLM's word embeddings as a fixed central space eliminates the need for learnable prototypes and preserves the LLM's semantic taxonomy in the visual representations, enabling visual semantic arithmetic.

## Results

On zero-shot image captioning, VLAP with CLIP RN50x16 + GPT-J achieves 64.5 CIDEr-D on NoCaps and 75.3 on MSCOCO, while VLAP with BEiT + T5Base (no linguistic supervision in vision pretraining) reaches 43.4 and 51.6 respectively. For zero-shot VQA, the best VLAP configuration (CLIP ViT-B/32 + T5Base) attains 41.1% accuracy, and in 4-shot setting reaches 52.6%. On cross-modal retrieval (Visual Dialog), VLAP with CLIP-T5Base achieves 26.9% R@1 for text-to-image retrieval and 21.1% R@1 for image-and-text-to-text retrieval, using only 0.6M trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LiMBeR (Merullo et al., 2023), the closest linear-transformation baseline, VLAP with the same CLIP RN50x16 + GPT-J backbone improves NoCaps CIDEr-D from 43.9 to 64.5 (+20.6) and MSCOCO CIDEr-D from 54.9 to 75.3 (+20.4). With BEiT (image-only pretraining, no linguistic supervision), VLAP + T5-Base (43.4 NoCaps, 51.6 MSCOCO) substantially outperforms LiMBeR + BEiT + GPT-J (28.5, 22.3), demonstrating that the assignment prediction objective compensates for the lack of text supervision in the vision encoder. Against the modular method MAGMA (Eichenberg et al., 2022), VLAP with CLIP RN50x16 + GPT-J gains +25.8 CIDEr-D on NoCaps and +27.8 on MSCOCO while training only a single linear layer versus MAGMA's adapter-based fine-tuning. On cross-modal retrieval, VLAP outperforms FROMAGe (Koh et al., 2023) by +4.0 NDCG and +2.9 MRR on IT2T, and by +6.1 R@1 on T2I, despite using 0.6M trainable parameters versus FROMAGe's 5.5M. VLAP slightly trails ESPER (Yu et al., 2022) on IT2T NDCG/MRR, but ESPER is trained on a superset (MSCOCO) that already contains in-context information from the Visual Dialog test set. On VQA, VLAP with BEiT + T5-Base (34.7% zero-shot) surpasses LiMBeR with CLIP RN50x16 + GPT-J (33.3%) and MAGMA (32.7%) despite using a smaller LLM and a vision encoder without text pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLAP extends the linear-transformation-based line of work (LiMBeR, FROMAGe) that bridges frozen vision encoders and LLMs with a single projection layer, but replaces their direct cross-modal comparison objectives (captioning loss, contrastive InfoNCE) with an optimal-transport-based assignment prediction. The assignment formulation is adapted from self-supervised clustering methods (DINO, SwAV), which use learnable prototypes as a central space; VLAP's key departure is substituting the LLM's frozen word embeddings as that fixed central space, eliminating the need for additional learnable embeddings. This positions VLAP between the lightweight linear-mapping approaches and the heavier modular methods (Flamingo, BLIP-2), offering a principled alternative to contrastive alignment that the authors argue better addresses the inherent modality gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the extreme parameter efficiency: a single 0.6M-parameter linear layer suffices to make frozen BEiT or CLIP encoders interface with frozen LLMs for captioning, VQA, and retrieval, with the added benefit that visual representations inherit the LLM's semantic taxonomy (enabling visual semantic arithmetic). However, the authors acknowledge a substantial performance gap remains versus modular methods (Flamingo, BLIP-2), attributing it to their much larger trainable parameter counts and training data. Open questions include whether the optimal-transport assignment objective transfers to modular architectures and whether scaling training data (CC3M → CC12M yielded only marginal gains) or word-distribution coverage is the true bottleneck.
