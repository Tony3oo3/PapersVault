## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for deformable image registration (DIR) rely exclusively on a pre-trained CNN at inference time without explicitly enforcing data fidelity between the warped and fixed images. While plug-and-play (PnP) priors have been widely applied to image denoising in inverse problems, their potential to regularize registration fields in DIR had never been explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241911/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D T1-weighted brain MRI volumes (moving and fixed) as input, padded to 192×128×224 for OASIS. The registration field is downsampled to half the image resolution before being passed through the DnCNN denoiser, then interpolated back to full resolution before warping the moving image. The output is a dense 3D displacement field that maps moving-image coordinates to fixed-image coordinates, producing the aligned (warped) image.

## Method

PIRATE performs iterative steepest-descent updates on the registration field with three terms: a global cross-correlation (GCC) data-fidelity penalty, a gradient smoothness regularizer, and a PnP residual term (φ − D(φ)) where D is a DnCNN denoiser pre-trained on AWGN-corrupted registration fields (ground truth from NODEO). A dynamic cosine-decaying step size is used to balance convergence. PIRATE+ extends PIRATE by applying Jacobian-Free Deep Equilibrium (JFB) to fine-tune the denoiser end-to-end: the fixed-point iteration is treated as an infinite-layer network, and gradients are computed via implicit differentiation through the fixed point using a three-term loss (local NCC, smoothness, and ReLU-penalized negative Jacobian determinant). The Anderson solver accelerates the forward fixed-point iterations during DEQ training.

## Results

On OASIS, PIRATE+ achieves the best average DSC of 0.7952 and the lowest ratio of negative Jacobian determinant (JD) of 0.0495%, outperforming all six baselines on both metrics. On CANDI, PIRATE+ similarly leads with DSC 0.7633 and negative JD 0.0660%. PIRATE (without DEQ fine-tuning) achieves competitive performance (DSC 0.7948 / 0.7624, negative JD 0.0567% / 0.0833%) at a lower inference cost of 32.01 s versus 49.47 s for PIRATE+, while both are substantially faster than NODEO (89.01 s) and Log-Demons (46.72 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIRATE+ outperforms all six baselines on both DSC and negative JD across OASIS and CANDI. Against the strongest iterative baseline NODEO (which was also used to generate ground-truth registration fields for the denoiser), PIRATE+ improves DSC by 0.0005 on OASIS (0.7952 vs 0.7947) and 0.0004 on CANDI (0.7633 vs 0.7629), while reducing negative JD from 0.0531% to 0.0495% (OASIS) and from 0.0726% to 0.0660% (CANDI), all at roughly half the inference time (49.47 s vs 89.01 s). Compared to the CNN-based baselines VoxelMorph and SYMNet, PIRATE+ gains 0.0297 and 0.0288 DSC on OASIS respectively, and reduces negative JD by approximately 47% and 38%. The deep-unfolding baseline GraDIRN is clearly outperformed, with PIRATE+ achieving 0.0641 higher DSC and 0.0923 lower negative JD on OASIS. PIRATE without DEQ already matches or exceeds NODEO on DSC (0.7948 vs 0.7947 on OASIS) while running in 32 s, demonstrating that the plug-and-play denoiser prior alone provides strong registration quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIRATE adapts the plug-and-play / regularization-by-denoising (RED) framework from image reconstruction to deformable image registration, using a pretrained AWGN denoiser on the registration field as an implicit regularizer within an iterative update — a direct extension of the SD-RED update (Romano et al., 2017) from pixel-space to displacement-field space. PIRATE+ further builds on deep equilibrium models (Bai et al., 2019) and Jacobian-free backpropagation (Fung et al., 2022) to fine-tune the denoiser end-to-end through the fixed point of the PIRATE iteration. This departs from prior CNN-based DIR methods (VoxelMorph, SYMNet) that rely solely on a pretrained network at inference without explicit data-fidelity, and from deep-unfolding approaches (GraDIRN) that are limited to a small number of unrolled iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that PnP priors can be meaningfully applied to registration fields, opening a new design axis for DIR that is orthogonal to and combinable with existing regularizers (smoothness, Jacobian constraints). However, the SOTA margins over NODEO are small (e.g., 0.7952 vs. 0.7947 DSC on OASIS), and the denoiser's ground truth was generated by NODEO itself, raising a mild circularity concern. Validation is limited to brain MRI and lung CT; the authors note that adaptability to whole-body registration, cross-modal registration, and other anatomies remains an open question. The DEQ fine-tuning step adds significant training time (50 epochs with Anderson solver) for a modest inference-time gain over PIRATE alone.
