## Problem

Time-domain audio-visual speech separation methods like CTCNet achieve state-of-the-art performance but require 7M parameters and 167G MACs, making them computationally prohibitive. Time-frequency domain methods are more efficient but have historically underperformed due to three gaps: failure to independently model time and frequency dimensions, neglect of multi-receptive-field visual cues, and disregard for the complex-valued nature of STFT features. This paper addresses all three gaps to close the performance-efficiency divide.

## Input / Output

The model takes a mono-aural mixed-speech audio signal at 16 kHz and grayscale lip-region video frames at 25 fps (96×96 pixels) as input, operating on 2-second clips (32,000 audio samples, 50 video frames). The audio is encoded via STFT with a 256-sample Hanning window and 128 hop length, producing complex time-frequency bins whose real and imaginary parts are concatenated as channels. The output is the estimated target speaker's audio waveform, reconstructed via inverse STFT from the separated complex features.

## Method

RTFS-Net is a TF-domain architecture built around R parameter-shared RTFS Blocks that compress features to a low-dimensional subspace, then apply a dual-path bidirectional SRU to independently model frequency and time dimensions before a TF-domain self-attention step captures inter-dimensional dependencies. A Cross-dimensional Attention Fusion (CAF) Block integrates multi-head visual features into the audio stream using both attention-weighted and gated element-wise multiplication, requiring only 7K parameters. A novel Spectral Source Separation (S³) Block performs target extraction via complex-number multiplication of the mask and encoded mixture, preserving amplitude and phase information that traditional real-valued masking discards. Temporal-Frequency Attention Reconstruction (TF-AR) units with depth-wise convolutions and sigmoid gating restore full resolution without checkerboard artifacts. The entire network uses only 0.7M trainable parameters and 56G MACs (R=12), reducing CTCNet's cost by 90% in parameters and 83% in MACs while surpassing it by 0.6 dB SI-SNRi on LRS2-2Mix.

## Results

RTFS-Net-12 achieves the best separation quality across all three datasets: 14.9 dB SI-SNRi / 15.1 dB SDRi on LRS2-2Mix, 17.5 / 17.6 on LRS3-2Mix, and 12.4 / 13.6 on VoxCeleb2-2Mix. Crucially, this is achieved with only 0.7M trainable parameters and 56.4G MACs, representing a 90% reduction in parameters and 83% reduction in MACs compared to the previous SOTA (CTCNet: 7.0M params, 167.2G MACs). The lighter RTFS-Net-4 variant (21.9G MACs, 57.8 ms inference on a 2080 GPU) already matches CTCNet's LRS2 performance (14.1 vs 14.3 dB SI-SNRi) at roughly one-tenth the computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTFS-Net-12 outperforms the previous SOTA, CTCNet, on all three datasets: +0.6 dB SI-SNRi on LRS2-2Mix (14.9 vs 14.3), +0.1 dB on LRS3-2Mix (17.5 vs 17.4), and +0.5 dB on VoxCeleb2-2Mix (12.4 vs 11.9), while using only 10% of the parameters (0.7M vs 7.0M) and 34% of the MACs (56.4G vs 167.2G). Against AV-ConvTasNet, RTFS-Net-12 gains +2.4 dB SI-SNRi on LRS2 and +3.2 dB on VoxCeleb2, with a 23× reduction in parameters. Compared to VisualVoice, the improvement is even larger (+3.4 dB on LRS2, +3.1 dB on VoxCeleb2) despite VisualVoice having over 100× more parameters. RTFS-Net-12 also surpasses AVLIT by +2.1 dB SI-SNRi on LRS2 and +3.0 dB on VoxCeleb2. The lighter RTFS-Net-4 variant already matches CTCNet on LRS2 (14.1 vs 14.3 dB) at 1/8 the MACs, and RTFS-Net-6 exceeds CTCNet on LRS2 (14.6 vs 14.3) while remaining at 1/5 the computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

RTFS-Net sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: time-domain AVSS methods (CTCNet, AV-ConvTasNet) that achieve high quality at heavy computational cost, and time-frequency domain AOSS methods (TF-GridNet, DualPathRNN) that exploit dual-path RNN architectures but have historically underperformed in the AVSS setting. The paper departs from CTCNet's T-domain paradigm by operating entirely in the STFT complex domain, and from TF-GridNet's heavy LSTM/transformer stacks by substituting lightweight SRUs and a compressed-subspace design. Its three novel components—RTFS Blocks with TF-AR reconstruction, the CAF fusion block, and the S³ complex-multiplication separation—each address a specific gap identified in prior TF-domain AVSS work (independent time/frequency modeling, multi-receptive-field visual fusion, and preservation of complex-valued acoustic structure).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 90% parameter reduction and 83% MAC reduction relative to the prior SOTA (CTCNet) while matching or exceeding its separation quality makes AVSS feasible on resource-constrained hardware and in near-real-time settings. The S³ block, which replaces element-wise masking with complex-number multiplication, is a simple, zero-cost modification that the authors argue should benefit all TF-domain separation methods, including AOSS. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to heavily reverberant environments, more than two speakers in training (the paper only tests 3-speaker generalization), and whether the modest SI-SNRi margins over CTCNet (0.6 dB on LRS2) hold under more challenging acoustic conditions. The reliance on a frozen pretrained video encoder (CTCNet-Lip) also limits the method's adaptability to unseen visual domains.
