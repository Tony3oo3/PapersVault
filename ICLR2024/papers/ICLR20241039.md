## Problem

While concept axes like color, category, and material can be named in language, the fine-grained visual nuances along each axis (e.g., a specific painting style or a particular shade) often exceed what discrete words can capture. Existing Textual Inversion methods optimize embeddings independently per image instance, ignoring the shared structure of concepts across instances and yielding poorly disentangled representations that fail at compositional remixing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241039/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, a single RGB test image is encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT/L-14 backbone, and K lightweight concept encoders (one per axis) each produce a continuous embedding in the T5 text-embedding space (dimension D matching the T2I model's conditioning vector). These embeddings are inserted into an axis-informed text template (e.g., 'a photo of <e1> with <e2> color and <e3> material') and fed to the frozen DeepFloyd IF diffusion model to generate a 64×64 image (upsampled to 256×256 for display). Embeddings from different images can be freely swapped or interpolated, and a ~600-step test-time finetuning on a single image adapts the encoders to unseen concepts.

## Method

The core architecture trains K separate concept encoders on top of frozen CLIP ViT/L-14 [CLS] tokens (each layer's token processed by a distinct linear layer, aggregated via average pooling + LeakyReLU, then a final linear projection), replacing the per-instance token optimization of vanilla Textual Inversion with amortized, feed-forward extraction. Training uses two losses: (1) a diffusion reconstruction loss that minimizes the denoising error of the frozen DeepFloyd IF model conditioned on the axis-informed template with predicted embeddings, and (2) a soft anchor loss that pulls each encoder's output toward the T5 embedding of a BLIP-2 VQA answer for that axis (e.g., 'what is the color?'), weighted by a small λ (0.0001–0.001) to prevent collapse to discrete text labels while enforcing cross-axis disentanglement. The entire pipeline is trained purely on synthetic images generated by DeepFloyd from combinatorial text prompts, requiring no human annotations. A lightweight test-time finetuning procedure that updates all encoder parameters using only the reconstruction loss (omitting the anchor loss) allows rapid adaptation to novel concepts within ~600 iterations while preserving disentanglement.

## Results

On the visual concept editing task, the proposed method achieves a human evaluation score of 0.968 for category editing and 0.840 for color editing, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: BLIP-2 baseline at 0.448 and 0.379 respectively). On CLIP-Score, the method scores 0.302 for overall color editing and 0.308 for overall category editing, competitive with or exceeding baselines. In a supplementary CIELAB ΔE* color accuracy metric, the method achieves mean errors of 4.76–35.50 across fruit categories, compared to 25.20–79.90 for the BLIP-2 baseline, confirming superior fine-grained color preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: Null-text Inversion (Mokady et al., 2022), InstructPix2Pix (Brooks et al., 2023), and a naive BLIP-2 text-description baseline. On human evaluation, the proposed method dominates all baselines by a wide margin, scoring 0.968 on category editing versus 0.448 (BLIP-2), 0.287 (Null-text Inversion), and 0.233 (InstructPix2Pix), and 0.840 on color editing versus 0.648 (InstructPix2Pix), 0.379 (BLIP-2), and 0.316 (Null-text Inversion). On CLIP-Score, the results are more nuanced: the BLIP-2 baseline slightly edges out the proposed method on category editing (0.313 vs. 0.308 for Cat.&Clr.), while the proposed method leads on color editing (0.302 vs. 0.287). InstructPix2Pix is more effective at changing color (0.268 Clr. CLIP) but fails to preserve category (0.226 Clr. when editing category), whereas the proposed method maintains strong category preservation across both editing tasks. The CIELAB ΔE* analysis further confirms that the proposed method captures fine-grained color nuances far more accurately than the BLIP-2 baseline (e.g., 4.76 vs. 25.20 on Mango), a gap that the CLIP metric alone fails to capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Textual Inversion paradigm (Gal et al., 2022) from per-instance token optimization to amortized, axis-specific concept encoders, and builds on the encoder-based personalization framework of Gal et al. (2023) by replacing the single shared linear layer with separate per-axis encoders on CLIP ViT features. The key departure is the introduction of a sparse text-anchor loss that grounds continuous concept embeddings to discrete VQA predictions from BLIP-2, repurposing the disentangled structure of language to enforce visual concept disentanglement — a mechanism absent from both personalization and unsupervised disentanglement (β-VAE, InfoGAN) lines of work. The reconstruction objective reuses the diffusion training loss of DeepFloyd IF as a frozen backbone, positioning the method as a distillation framework rather than a new generative architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, annotation-free route to disentangled visual concept extraction and remixing, with the test-time finetuning procedure (~600 iterations) providing a lightweight path to unseen concepts. However, the evaluation is confined to five small synthetic datasets (~669 images each) generated by the same T2I model used as the reconstruction backbone, raising open questions about robustness to domain shift, scale, and the number of concept axes. The authors acknowledge that concept axes must be specified a priori and that separate per-axis encoders do not exploit the hierarchical structure among axes (e.g., color as a sub-axis of style), leaving the generalization to open-ended, hierarchically organized concept spaces as a clear future direction.
