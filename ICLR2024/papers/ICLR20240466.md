## Problem

Existing activation functions in ANNs use static, pre-defined response thresholds (e.g., ReLU's fixed zero threshold, GELU's fixed Gaussian CDF), whereas biological neurons dynamically adjust their firing threshold based on input characteristics and environmental context. Additionally, ANN aggregated responses exhibit high-variance Gaussian distributions across categories, lacking the strong category specificity observed in biological neurons, which hampers the network's ability to discriminate between classes.

## Input / Output

DNRT is architecture-agnostic and operates on standard inputs: RGB images at 28×28 (MNIST), 32×32 (CIFAR), or 224×224 (ImageNet), and graph node features with adjacency matrices for GNN tasks. The RAA component operates at the feature-vector level (a d-dimensional vector per token/patch), while ARR operates on the globally aggregated feature vector—specifically the class token for ViTs or the globally pooled feature map for CNNs. Output remains standard class labels or node labels; ARR is a training-only regularizer that adds no inference overhead.

## Method

DNRT comprises two mechanisms. Response-Adaptive Activation (RAA) replaces the static activation by adding a learned linear offset f(x) = wᵀx + b (initialized to zero) to the input before applying the activation, e.g., x·Φ(x + wᵀx + b) for GELU, so the effective response threshold adapts per feature vector rather than being fixed. Aggregated Response Regularization (ARR) maintains a per-category moving average of the globally pooled feature vector (momentum m=0.1) and adds an L1 loss constraining each new aggregated response to stay close to its category's historical mean, thereby reducing response variance and sharpening category specificity. The total loss is L_task + λ·(1/J)Σ L_arr^j, applied across J layers. RAA introduces only d+1 parameters per layer (negligible: <0.2% extra parameters), and ARR introduces zero parameters, making the overall overhead minimal in both computation and memory.

## Results

DNRT consistently outperforms all 7 baseline activation mechanisms across MLP, ViT, and CNN architectures on 5 image classification datasets. On CIFAR-100 with ViT-Tiny, DNRT achieves 71.4% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best baseline (ReLU at 65.7%) by 5.7 points. On ImageNet-100 with PVT-Tiny, DNRT reaches 84.1% vs. the best baseline (ReLU at 81.9%), and on ImageNet-1K with PVT-Tiny it achieves 76.1% vs. ReLU's 73.7%. The method also generalizes to node classification (GCN AUC 74.9% vs. ReLU 72.5%) and long-tailed classification (ViT-Tiny top-1 56.5% at β=10 vs. ReLU 53.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNRT wins against all 7 baselines (Softplus, ELU, SELU, SiLU, ReLU, GELU, GDN) on every architecture and dataset tested, with no losses or ties. The largest margins appear on CIFAR-100 where DNRT gains 4.4–6.5 points over the best baseline (e.g., 71.4% vs. 65.7% ReLU on ViT-Tiny), and on ImageNet-100 where gains reach 4.5–5.2 points (e.g., 80.9% vs. 76.4% GELU on ViT-Tiny). Among baselines, ReLU and GELU are typically the strongest, while GDN consistently underperforms (often by 5–10 points), suggesting its multi-dimensional normalization is poorly suited to these settings. On the simpler MLP experiments (Table 1), DNRT still leads by 0.5–9.2 points, confirming the mechanism's benefit even in minimal architectures. On ImageNet-1K (Table 9), DNRT maintains a 1.3–2.4 point advantage over the best baseline, demonstrating scalability to large-scale training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on activation functions by departing from the static response-condition paradigm shared by ReLU, GELU, ELU, SELU, and SiLU. While prior learnable-parameter activations (ELU, SELU, SiLU) can modulate response strength, none adjust the response-triggering threshold based on input context. The RAA mechanism is mathematically derived from GELU's probabilistic formulation (x·Φ(x)), introducing a learned linear offset that makes the activation threshold input-dependent, while ARR draws on the neuroscience observation of category-specific neural responses to impose a moving-mean regularization on aggregated features. The paper also positions itself against Spiking Neural Network approaches (LIF, LIF-LT), arguing that its per-signal adaptive thresholding offers greater flexibility than layer-shared learnable thresholds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DNRT provides a drop-in replacement for any activation function with negligible parameter and compute overhead, making it broadly adoptable across MLPs, ViTs, CNNs, and GNNs without architectural redesign. The ARR component's demonstrated ability to mitigate overfitting under weak augmentation (Figure 5) suggests practical value for data-limited settings. Open questions include whether the biological inspiration can be pushed further (e.g., temporal dynamics, multi-timescale integration), whether the approach transfers to generative or self-supervised paradigms, and whether the linear offset in RAA is truly sufficient or whether richer input-dependent mappings would yield further gains. The paper's reliance on activation-function baselines rather than full SOTA training recipes for each architecture leaves some room for questioning the absolute magnitude of gains.
