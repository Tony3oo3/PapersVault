## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods are designed for supervised settings and do not produce condensed datasets that effectively support self-supervised pre-training for transfer learning. Moreover, naively substituting an SSL objective (which involves random data augmentations or input masking) into a bilevel optimization framework yields a biased gradient estimator, causing training instability that the paper formally proves and empirically confirms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242240/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled RGB image dataset (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, TinyImageNet at 64×64, or ImageNet resized to 64×64) and produces a compact synthetic image set X_s (m ≪ n, e.g., 1,000–2,000 samples) paired with learnable target feature representations Y_s ∈ R^{m×d_y}. The synthetic images are used to pre-train a model via MSE regression against Y_s, after which the linear head is discarded and the feature extractor is fine-tuned on a downstream labeled task. A pre-trained SSL target model (ResNet-18 with Barlow Twins) on the full source dataset provides fixed reference representations that anchor the outer optimization.

## Method

KRR-ST replaces both the inner and outer objectives of bilevel optimization with mean squared error, eliminating the randomness from data augmentations or masking that causes biased gradients (Theorem 1). The inner objective minimizes MSE between a model's representations of the synthetic images and learnable target representations Y_s; the outer objective minimizes MSE between the inner-trained model's representations of the original full dataset and those of a frozen SSL target model. To reduce computational cost, the model is decomposed into a feature extractor and a linear head, and the inner optimization is solved in closed form via kernel ridge regression on the frozen feature extractor's outputs. A pool of l=10 diverse feature extractors is maintained and sampled per meta-update to prevent overfitting, following the FRePo design. The distilled (X_s, Y_s) pair is then used to pre-train arbitrary architectures (ConvNet, VGG, AlexNet, MobileNet, ResNet) before fine-tuning on target tasks.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (2% compression, 1,000 synthetic samples), KRR-ST achieves 88.72% on CIFAR-10, 41.54% on Aircraft, 28.68% on Cars, 25.30% on CUB-2011, 26.39% on Dogs, and 67.88% on Flowers, outperforming all supervised baselines. On TinyImageNet (2%, 2,000 samples), the margins widen substantially: KRR-ST reaches 58.83% on Aircraft and 49.26% on Cars versus 48.21% and 30.35% for the best supervised baseline (MTT). On ImageNet (~0.08%, 1,000 samples), KRR-ST achieves 57.17% on Aircraft and 46.95% on Cars, far exceeding FRePo (39.03% and 20.00%) and the no-pretraining baseline (42.45% and 23.62%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KRR-ST consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every target dataset and source dataset tested. The closest competitor is KIP (kernel inducing points), which uses a similar KRR-based inner optimization but with supervised labels; KRR-ST still leads by 0.82% on CIFAR-10 and 3.87% on Aircraft (CIFAR-100 source). Among the gradient-matching and trajectory-matching methods, MTT is the strongest supervised baseline (e.g., 36.11% on Aircraft, 21.42% on Cars with CIFAR-100 source), yet KRR-ST surpasses it by 5.43% and 7.26% respectively. FRePo, which also uses KRR but with supervised cross-entropy, trails KRR-ST by 6.20% on Aircraft and 29.26% on Cars (ImageNet source). In the target data-free KD setting (Table 4), supervised distillation methods (DSA, DM, MTT, FRePo) actually underperform the simple Random baseline, while KRR-ST leads by 8–12 points across all architectures (e.g., 62.15% vs. 53.86% for Kmeans on VGG11). The Gaussian noise baseline (Raikwar & Mishra) is the weakest, confirming that a structured condensed dataset is essential for effective KD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset distillation/condensation literature (Wang et al. 2018, DSA, MTT, DM, KIP, FRePo) from the supervised setting to a novel unsupervised/self-supervised formulation where the goal is to preserve a representation space for pre-training rather than matching classification accuracy. Methodologically, it inherits the kernel ridge regression simplification of inner optimization from FRePo (Zhou et al. 2022) and KIP (Nguyen et al. 2021), but replaces the supervised cross-entropy objective with MSE against a pre-trained SSL target model (Barlow Twins). The key conceptual departure is the theoretical proof (Theorem 1) that naively plugging SSL objectives into bilevel optimization yields biased gradients due to stochastic data augmentations or masking, motivating the MSE substitution that eliminates this randomness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a practically important direction: distilling large unlabeled pre-training corpora into tiny synthetic sets to accelerate NAS, continual learning, and data-free knowledge distillation, where repeated pre-training on full datasets is prohibitively expensive. The clean theoretical characterization of gradient bias in bilevel SSL optimization provides a principled justification for the MSE replacement and may inform future work on other stochastic objectives in meta-learning or bilevel settings. Limitations include testing only Barlow Twins as the SSL backbone, restricting to CNN architectures, and the distilled images having worse FID scores than random subsets (76.28 vs 34.49 on CIFAR100), suggesting the synthetic samples trade visual realism for representational coverage. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to transformer-based SSL (DINOv2, MAE), larger-scale distillation, and non-visual modalities.
