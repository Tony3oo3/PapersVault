## Problem

In-context policy learning for offline RL, as demonstrated by Algorithm Distillation, requires collecting extensive RL training trajectories and training large Transformer models to capture the full learning process, incurring prohibitive computational costs. The paper asks whether in-context exploration-exploitation can be achieved through purely supervised sequence modeling on cheaply collected data, without explicit Bayesian inference or expensive RL training trajectories.

## Input / Output

The model takes a concatenated sequence of K episodes (observation, action, reward triplets) from the same task plus a two-component return-to-go signal: an in-episode cumulative reward and a binary cross-episode indicator (1 if the current episode's total return exceeds all previous episodes). Episodes need not originate from an RL learning algorithm. At inference, the model outputs a sampled action at each step conditioned on the growing trajectory history and a sampled in-episode return-to-go from an augmented distribution that biases toward higher values; for Bayesian optimization, the output is the next evaluation location among 1024 candidate points.

## Method

ICEE extends the Decision Transformer formulation to model multiple episodes in a single sequence, and proves that the predictive distribution of a sequence model trained via maximum likelihood inherently captures epistemic uncertainty over the latent sequence parameters, enabling exploration-exploitation without explicit Bayesian inference. The key training innovation is an importance-weighted objective that reweights each action by the ratio of a uniform policy to the data collection policy, removing the bias toward the collection policy's action distribution. A novel cross-episode return-to-go (a binary indicator of whether the current episode outperforms all prior ones) drives inter-episode improvement, while the in-episode return-to-go follows the standard cumulative-reward design. At inference, the cross-episode return-to-go is fixed to 1 to encourage improvement, and the in-episode return-to-go is sampled from a temperature-augmented distribution predicted by a separate auxiliary model, all without any gradient updates.

## Results

On the Dark Room (Hard) grid world, ICEE achieves an average return of 0.79 (±0.08) after 50 episodes, compared to 0.43 for Algorithm Distillation (AD) and 0.09 for Multi-game Decision Transformer (MGDT). On Dark Key-to-Door, ICEE reaches 1.84 (±0.08) after 20 episodes versus 0.94 for AD and 0.34 for MGDT. In the Bayesian optimization experiment on 16 2D benchmark functions, ICEE matches the search efficiency of GP-based BO with expected improvement (EI) while being an order of magnitude faster in wall-clock time on a single A100 GPU. The action entropy of ICEE decreases continuously across episodes, confirming emergent exploration-to-exploitation behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICEE clearly outperforms Algorithm Distillation (AD, Laskin et al., 2023) on both RL environments: on Dark Room Hard at 50 episodes, ICEE achieves 0.79 return versus AD's 0.43, and on Dark Key-to-Door at 20 episodes, ICEE reaches 1.84 versus AD's 0.94. AD essentially mimics the data collection policy and cannot improve beyond it, while ICEE's exploration-exploitation mechanism allows it to surpass the source policy (0.51 on Dark Room Hard, 1.10 on Dark Key-to-Door). Against Multi-game Decision Transformer (MGDT, Lee et al., 2022), which has no in-context learning capability, ICEE shows a dramatic advantage: 0.79 vs. 0.09 on Dark Room Hard and 1.84 vs. 0.34 on Dark Key-to-Door, confirming that in-context policy learning is essential for these tasks. The AD-sorted variant, which artificially orders episodes by quality to mimic RL training trajectories, approaches ICEE's final performance (0.75 vs. 0.79 on Dark Room Hard) but starts much worse (0.08 at 10 episodes vs. 0.48), showing that ICEE's EE mechanism is more sample-efficient. In the BO experiment, ICEE matches GP-based EI in search efficiency but is an order of magnitude faster in wall-clock time, and the biased variant (ICEE-biased, without the importance-sampling correction) is noticeably worse, validating the unbiased training objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICEE extends the Decision Transformer (Chen et al., 2021) from single-episode to multi-episode sequence modeling and introduces an importance-sampling-based unbiased training objective to remove the bias toward the data collection policy. It departs from Algorithm Distillation (Laskin et al., 2023) by eliminating the need for RL training trajectories, instead using cheap non-optimal data collection policies, and from Multi-game Decision Transformer (Lee et al., 2022) by adding a cross-episode return-to-go signal that encodes whether the current episode outperforms all previous ones. The theoretical analysis showing that supervised sequence models inherently capture epistemic uncertainty in their predictive distributions provides the conceptual bridge that enables exploration-exploitation at inference time without explicit Bayesian inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICEE demonstrates that in-context exploration-exploitation is achievable through pure sequence modeling, opening a path to gradient-free policy improvement that could significantly reduce the computational overhead of online RL adaptation. The practical speed advantage over GP-based Bayesian optimization (orders of magnitude faster on a single GPU) is notable for applications where inference latency matters. However, the RL experiments are confined to simple 9×9 grid-world POMDPs, and the unbiased objective requires knowledge of the data collection policy, limiting immediate applicability to more complex, continuous-action environments. Open questions include whether the binary cross-episode return-to-go generalizes to richer reward structures and whether the approach scales to high-dimensional state spaces.
