## Problem

Adversarial training (AT) is known to suffer from robust overfitting, where prolonged training degrades the robustness initially gained. Existing theoretical analyses either study only converged models or are limited to shallow (two-layer) networks, leaving unexplained why deep DNNs progressively lose their robustness during continuous AT. This paper provides the first NTK-based theoretical explanation for robust overfitting in general deep networks.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, SVHN) paired with class labels, using ℓ∞ adversarial perturbations of radius 4/255 or 8/255. Rather than training a finite-width network, Adv-NTK constructs an infinite-width kernel regression from a 10,000-sample training subset and optimizes a single trainable parameter vector of size |D_opt|·c (the diagonal entries of the regularization matrix Ξ) via SGD with gradient normalization on a 2,000-sample validation set. The output is a class prediction from the resulting infinite-width DNN.

## Method

The paper extends NTK theory to adversarial training by replacing the standard constrained-space adversarial search with a gradient-flow-based search controlled by a time-dependent learning rate, enabling continuous gradient-flow analysis. It proves that a wide MLP under AT is approximated by its linearized counterpart and derives closed-form AT dynamics in which a time-dependent regularization matrix Ξ(t) (built from a new Adversarial Regularization Kernel) captures all adversarial robustness. The key theoretical finding is an AT degeneration phenomenon: as training time grows, Ξ(t) fades to zero, causing the adversarially trained network to converge to the same solution as standard (non-adversarial) training, thereby explaining robust overfitting. Based on this, Adv-NTK directly optimizes the diagonal entries of Ξ via a minimax objective on a validation set using SGD with ℓ₂-GradNorm, yielding the first AT algorithm designed for infinite-width DNNs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (12K subset), Adv-NTK achieves robust test accuracy of 27.35–30.91% (MLP) and 26.61–31.04% (CNN) at ρ=4/255, and 13.13–26.79% at ρ=8/255, substantially outperforming the NTK baseline (2.81–24.43% and 1.31–10.04% respectively). At deeper architectures (8–10 layers), finite-width AT degrades to chance-level 10% accuracy, while Adv-NTK maintains 20–31% robust accuracy. Adv-NTK also surpasses AT in several settings, e.g., CNN-4 at ρ=4/255 (31.04% vs. 19.30%) and MLP-5 at ρ=4/255 (29.04% vs. 28.70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adv-NTK is compared against two baselines: standard adversarial training (AT, Madry et al. 2018) on finite-width DNNs, and NTK (Lee et al. 2019) which gives a closed-form infinite-width DNN without adversarial training. Against NTK, Adv-NTK wins decisively in nearly every configuration, e.g., 27.35% vs. 9.93% (MLP-3, ρ=4/255) and 26.79% vs. 1.31% (CNN-3, ρ=8/255), confirming that the regularization matrix Ξ(t) captures meaningful adversarial robustness. Against AT, Adv-NTK achieves comparable or superior robust accuracy in most settings: it matches AT at shallow depths (e.g., 27.35% vs. 30.64% for MLP-3 at ρ=4/255) but clearly outperforms AT at deeper architectures where AT collapses to 10% chance-level (e.g., 30.91% vs. 10.00% for MLP-10 at ρ=4/255; 26.61% vs. 10.00% for CNN-10 at ρ=4/255). The paper notes that Adv-NTK cannot beat the *best* (peak) model during the AT trajectory, attributing this gap to non-linear feature learning in finite-width networks that goes beyond the NTK approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK framework of Jacot et al. (2018) and the closed-form linearized dynamics of Lee et al. (2019) to adversarial training, a non-trivial step because standard AT's constrained perturbation search (PGD within an ℓ∞ ball) is incompatible with continuous gradient-flow analysis. The authors resolve this by replacing the constrained-space condition with a time-dependent learning rate term, introducing the Adversarial Regularization Kernel (ARK) as a new input-space kernel alongside the standard NTK. In the robust overfitting literature, prior theoretical work (Li & Li 2023) was limited to two-layer networks, while empirical studies (Rice et al. 2020) identified the phenomenon without mechanistic explanation; this paper is the first to provide a general-depth theoretical account of why robustness degrades during prolonged AT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AT degeneration phenomenon—that the regularization matrix encoding adversarial robustness provably vanishes as training time → ∞, causing the adversarially trained network to converge to the non-adversarially trained one—offers a clean mechanistic explanation for robust overfitting that prior work could not provide for general DNNs. Adv-NTK, the first AT algorithm for infinite-width networks, validates the theory empirically but cannot surpass the best finite-width AT checkpoint, suggesting that finite-width feature learning captures robustness beyond what the linearized regime admits. Open questions include extending the analysis beyond squared loss, to larger perturbation regimes where the positive-definiteness assumption in the degeneration proof breaks down, and understanding why finite-width networks retain robustness that the infinite-width theory predicts will be lost.
