## Problem

Existing theoretical algorithms for two-player zero-sum Markov games (TZMG) each suffer from at least two critical deficiencies: memory inefficiency (space growing with sample count T), heavy sample-complexity dependence on horizon H or state space S, high computational complexity, non-Markov or non-Nash output policies, and large burn-in costs. No prior model-free algorithm simultaneously achieves minimal space O(SABH), near-optimal sample complexity Õ(H⁴SAB/ε²), low computational complexity O(T·poly(AB)), low burn-in cost, and a single Markov/Nash output policy.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on tabular episodic TZMGs with S states, A and B actions for the max- and min-players, and horizon H, consuming only online state-action-reward transitions from self-play episodes (no generative model or transition kernel access). It outputs a single Markov policy pair (μ, ν) — the marginal policies of a Coarse Correlated Equilibrium — that constitutes an ε-approximate Nash equilibrium, stored in O(SABH) space (the minimum required to hold Q-values). Unlike prior self-play methods that return nested mixtures of Markov policies or history-dependent strategies, the output here is a clean, independent per-state action distribution for each player.

## Method

ME-Nash-QL is a model-free self-play algorithm that integrates three key techniques into a unified Q-learning framework: (1) reference-advantage decomposition, borrowed from single-agent RL and applied to TZMG for the first time, which subtracts a slowly-varying reference value from the Bellman backup to reduce update variance and shave one power of H from sample complexity; (2) an early-settlement rule that freezes reference values once the optimistic–pessimistic gap drops below 1, yielding a burn-in cost of Õ(SABH¹⁰) versus Õ(S³ABH⁴) for prior methods; and (3) Coarse Correlated Equilibrium (CCE) computation via linear programming at each state to produce a single joint distribution π_h, from which the marginal Markov policies (μ, ν) are extracted. The algorithm maintains four Q-estimate families (UCB, LCB, and their reference-advantage counterparts) with a linearly rescaled learning rate η_n = (H+1)/(H+n), combining them via min/max to preserve optimism/pessimism while tightening the confidence interval. The resulting regret bound Õ(√(H²SABT)) translates to sample complexity Õ(H⁴SAB/ε²) with optimal dependence on both H and S, and computational cost O(T·poly(AB)) independent of S.

## Results

ME-Nash-QL achieves sample complexity Õ(H⁴SAB/ε²) for finding an ε-approximate Nash equilibrium in two-player zero-sum Markov games, with space complexity O(SABH) (the minimum for storing Q-values in tabular cases) and computational complexity O(T·poly(AB)). The regret bound is Õ(√(H²SABT)), which becomes active once T exceeds the burn-in cost Õ(SABH¹⁰). The algorithm outputs a single Markov and Nash policy pair, unlike prior model-free methods that produce non-Markov or nested-mixture policies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ME-Nash-QL matches the best-known sample complexity Õ(H⁴SAB/ε²) shared by Nash-VI and Feng et al. (2023), but strictly dominates both on every other metric. Compared to Nash-VI (Liu et al., 2021), ME-Nash-QL reduces space complexity from O(S²ABH) to O(SABH), lowers computational complexity from O(T·poly(SAB)) to O(T·poly(AB)), and cuts the burn-in cost from Õ(S³ABH⁴) to Õ(SABH¹⁰) — a significant advantage when S > H³ (e.g., Go has S = 2³⁶¹, H ≤ 722). Against Feng et al. (2023), which also achieves H⁴SAB/ε² sample complexity, ME-Nash-QL outputs a Markov and Nash policy (Feng et al. outputs neither) and has a dramatically lower burn-in cost (SABH¹⁰ vs. S⁶A⁴B⁴H²⁸). Versus model-free baselines like V-learning (H⁶S(A+B)/ε²) and Nash Q-learning (H⁶SAB/ε²), ME-Nash-QL improves the horizon dependence from H⁶ to H⁴ and eliminates the T-dependence in space. Against model-based baselines such as OMNI-VI (H⁵S³A³B³/ε²) and Nash-UCRL (H⁴S⁴A²B²/ε²), ME-Nash-QL achieves superior dependence on both S and the action spaces while maintaining the lowest computational complexity O(T·poly(AB)). Overall, ME-Nash-QL is the only algorithm in Table 1 that simultaneously achieves the best space, the lowest computational complexity, the best burn-in cost, and a Markov/Nash output policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ME-Nash-QL is built directly on Nash Q-learning (Hu & Wellman, 2003) and augments it with two key techniques previously applied only in single-agent RL: reference-advantage decomposition (Zhang et al., 2020b) and an early-settlement mechanism (Li et al., 2022). For policy computation, it adopts Coarse Correlated Equilibrium (CCE) from Xie et al. (2022), which allows outputting a single Markov policy rather than the nested mixture of Markov policies produced by V-learning (Jin et al., 2022). The paper positions itself against the model-based Nash-VI (Liu et al., 2021), which matches its sample complexity H⁴SAB/ε² but requires O(S²ABH) space and O(T poly(SAB)) computation, and against model-free V-learning, which has worse H-dependence (H⁶) and outputs non-Markov policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model-free algorithm for two-player zero-sum Markov games to simultaneously achieve minimal O(SABH) space, O(T poly(AB)) computation, the best burn-in cost O(SABH¹⁰), and a Markov/Nash output policy—resolving a multi-front gap that no prior algorithm closed. The practical implication is that for long-horizon games (min{A,B} ≪ H², e.g., Go with H up to 722), ME-Nash-QL dominates all existing methods in memory and compute while matching the best sample complexity. Open questions the authors flag include whether O(A+B) sample complexity (breaking the curse of multi-agent) is achievable in the multi-player general-sum extension without sacrificing other guarantees, and how to design independent (uncorrelated) actions at that complexity.
