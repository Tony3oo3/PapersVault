## Problem

Maximum-entropy RL models the optimal policy as an energy-based model over Q-values, but computing the entropy of such EBMs remains an open problem. Existing work either implicitly estimates entropy via importance sampling (SQL), incurring high variance and poor scalability, or fits a simplified parametric distribution like a Gaussian (SAC), which cannot represent multimodal action distributions and converges to suboptimal solutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240890/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous environment state s_t and produces a set of m action particles {a^L} sampled from a multimodal policy distribution q^L(a|s) induced by L steps of SVGD dynamics. The entropy of this policy is computed in closed form from the particles' trajectories, requiring only first-order gradients of the Q-function and pairwise vector dot products—no Hessian or matrix inversion. At test time, an amortized feed-forward network f_ψ(s,z) replaces the unrolled SVGD dynamics, reducing inference to a single forward pass with a random vector z for stochasticity.

## Method

S²AC replaces the Gaussian actor in SAC with a parameterized SVGD sampler from an EBM over Q-values. The key theoretical contribution is a closed-form entropy estimate (Theorem 3.1) derived via the change-of-variable formula, which is valid because the SVGD update rule is provably invertible (Proposition 3.2) under a small step-size condition—unlike SGLD or HMC. The entropy formula (Theorem 3.3) depends only on first-order Q-gradients and pairwise particle distances weighted by an RBF kernel, making it computationally cheap. To reduce the number of SVGD steps needed for convergence, the initial particle distribution is parameterized as a learned isotropic Gaussian N(μ_θ(s), σ_θ(s)), which contours the high-density region of the target while SVGD refines toward individual modes. Particle updates are bounded within t standard deviations of the learned mean to handle non-smooth Q-landscapes, and an amortized SVGD network is trained post-hoc for efficient test-time action selection.

## Results

On the MuJoCo benchmark (Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Ant, Humanoid), S²AC(φ,θ) consistently outperforms SQL and SAC-NF across all five environments and surpasses SAC on four out of five, while also demonstrating higher sample efficiency (converging with fewer training steps). Only three SVGD steps are used during training, resulting in a small computational overhead. The amortized test-time variant S²AC(φ,θ,ψ) reduces action-selection run-time to 0.817–0.850 ms, comparable to SAC (0.708–0.731 ms) and SQL (0.815–0.839 ms), with only a slight performance tradeoff. In the multi-goal environment, S²AC recovers all three action modes and biases trajectories toward the higher-entropy goals (G₂/G₃), whereas SAC collapses to a single mode and SQL distributes trajectories uniformly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), S²AC wins on 4 of 5 MuJoCo environments and clearly outperforms it in the multi-goal setting: SAC's Gaussian actor quickly commits to a single mode and fails to maximize expected future entropy, while S²AC recovers all modes and biases toward higher-entropy goals. Against SQL (Haarnoja et al., 2017), S²AC wins on all 5 MuJoCo environments; in the multi-goal task, SQL recovers the three modes but distributes trajectories uniformly across goals (failing to maximize entropy), whereas S²AC preferentially approaches G₂/G₃. Against SAC-NF (Mazoure et al., 2020), S²AC wins on all 5 MuJoCo environments and is more stable in the multi-goal setting—SAC-NF collapses to local optima at smaller entropy weights α, while S²AC's SVGD dispersion term maintains multimodality consistently. Against DDPG (Silver et al., 2014) and PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), S²AC achieves superior returns on all MuJoCo environments, confirming the benefit of stochastic MaxEnt policies over deterministic or entropy-regularized baselines. The parameterized initial distribution is critical: S²AC(φ) without it shows poor performance compared to S²AC(φ,θ), highlighting the importance of learning the starting distribution for scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

S²AC sits at the intersection of Maximum Entropy RL and variational inference, building directly on the actor-critic soft policy iteration framework of SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) and the EBM-over-Q-values formulation of SQL (Haarnoja et al., 2017). The key departure is replacing SAC's Gaussian actor and SQL's implicit entropy (via importance sampling) with a parameterized SVGD sampler whose invertibility (under ε ≪ σ) enables a closed-form entropy estimate via the change-of-variable formula. The paper explicitly positions S²AC as a unification: it reduces to SAC when the number of SVGD steps is zero, and SQL becomes equivalent to S²AC if the entropy is computed explicitly with the derived formula. The amortized SVGD deployment at test time follows Wang & Liu (2016).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing open problem in MaxEnt RL—how to compute the entropy of expressive EBM policies without resorting to high-variance importance sampling (SQL) or restrictive Gaussian assumptions (SAC). The closed-form entropy formula requiring only first-order derivatives and vector products is computationally attractive and, as the authors note, could generalize beyond RL to other variational inference settings. However, the practical gains on MuJoCo are modest (4/5 environments over SAC), the multi-goal environment is custom-designed, and the parameterized initialization is critical for performance (S²AC(φ) without it degrades significantly), suggesting the method's success depends on careful tuning. Open questions include whether the SVGD-based variational distribution scales to very high-dimensional action spaces and whether the amortized test-time approximation preserves the entropy-maximizing behavior.
