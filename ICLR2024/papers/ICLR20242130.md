## Problem

Existing deep long-tailed recognition (DLTR) methods design fixed or instant-imbalance-based re-balancing strategies, but none address the intrinsic optimization conflicts (gradient conflicts) among categories that persist throughout training and cause dominated trajectories to degrade representation learning for minority classes. The paper empirically establishes that these conflicts are prevalent across mainstream DLTR methods and cannot be resolved by current dynamic re-weighting approaches due to their lack of conflict-averse design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242130/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-in training paradigm on top of existing DLTR models (e.g., LDAM-DRW, cRT+Mixup, MiSLAS, GCL) without any architecture modifications. It consumes standard RGB images from long-tailed training sets (imbalance ratios of 50–996) and produces class labels, with evaluation performed on balanced test sets. The key operational detail is that MOO is applied only during an early 'shared feature extraction' stage (first E epochs), after which training reverts to standard single-objective optimization, making it compatible with decoupling-based two-stage methods.

## Method

PLOT bridges multi-objective optimization (MOO) and DLTR by reinterpreting a K-way classification task as K binary classification objectives and transforming the shared-feature-extraction / task-specific-optimization split from a structural (backbone + branches) to a temporal (early epochs vs. later epochs) one. During the early SFE stage, CAGrad is used to compute dynamic class weights that mitigate gradient conflicts, while two theoretical-motivated augmentations ensure generalization and convergence: a variability collapse loss (standard deviation of per-class losses in a mini-batch) derived from a novel MOO-based DLTR generalization bound, and Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) to constrain the gradient Lipschitz constant H that is typically large for tail classes. The overall procedure computes a perturbative loss via SAM, adds the variability collapse loss, back-propagates to obtain class-specific gradients, solves the CAGrad subproblem for optimal weights, and updates parameters with the combined gradient direction.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (imbalance ratio 200), PLOT augments cRT+Mixup from 73.06% to 78.99% top-1 accuracy (+5.93), LDAM-DRW from 71.38% to 74.32%, and GCL from 79.25% to 80.08%. On large-scale benchmarks, cRT+Mixup+PLOT achieves 41.0% on Places-LT (outperforming GCL at 40.6%) and 54.3% on ImageNet-LT, while MiSLAS+PLOT reaches 72.1% on iNaturalist-2018, establishing state-of-the-art performance. The method consistently improves all tested baselines across CIFAR-10-LT and CIFAR-100-LT at imbalance ratios of 200, 100, and 50, with the largest gains observed for methods exhibiting the most severe gradient conflicts (cRT+Mixup, LDAM-DRW).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLOT consistently outperforms its vanilla counterparts across all tested baselines: cRT+Mixup gains the most (+5.93% on CIFAR10-LT imb=200), followed by LDAM-DRW (+2.94%) and M2m (+1.05%), while GCL shows only marginal improvement (+0.83% at imb=200) or slight degradation (−0.10% at imb=50) due to its already low conflict level. On Places-LT, cRT+Mixup+PLOT (41.0%) surpasses GCL (40.6%), MiSLAS (40.2%), ResLT (39.8%), RSG (39.3%), DisAlign (39.3%), LADE (38.8%), and Balanced Softmax (38.6%). On ImageNet-LT, cRT+Mixup+PLOT (54.3%) trails GCL (54.9%) but exceeds MiSLAS (52.7%), LDAM-DRW+SAM (53.1%), and DisAlign (52.9%); BCL+PLOT (57.2%) further extends the lead. On iNaturalist-2018, MiSLAS+PLOT (72.1%) edges out GCL (72.0%) and cRT+Mixup (69.5%). In the open-set long-tailed recognition evaluation, cRT+Mixup+PLOT achieves F-measures of 0.563 (ImageNet-LT) and 0.516 (Places-LT), outperforming LUNA (0.579/0.491) and CC-SAM (0.552/0.510) on ImageNet-LT and Places-LT respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLOT bridges multi-objective optimization (MOO) from the multi-task learning literature—specifically CAGrad (Liu et al., 2021) and SAM (Foret et al., 2020)—into the deep long-tailed recognition (DLTR) setting, where no prior work had applied Pareto-optimal gradient aggregation. The key conceptual departure from MOO-based MTL is the transformation of structural decoupling (shared backbone + task-specific branches) into temporal decoupling (MOO-guided shared feature extraction in early epochs, standard optimization thereafter), enabling MOO without any architectural modification. The paper positions itself as orthogonal to existing DLTR re-balancing strategies (sampling, loss re-weighting, logit adjustment, augmentation), framing optimization conflicts among categories as a distinct and previously underexplored axis of imbalance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, plug-and-play augmentation that consistently improves a wide range of DLTR methods across five benchmarks, making it immediately useful for practitioners working on long-tailed classification. The empirical identification of gradient conflicts and dominated conflicting as prevalent, measurable phenomena in DLTR—persisting even under dynamic re-weighting methods like difficultyNet—reframes how the community might think about the limits of existing re-balancing strategies. Limitations include additional computational overhead from multiple backpropagations and MOO solving, a fixed early-stopping hyperparameter, and the restriction of MOO to early training epochs. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned schedules for the temporal boundary could further improve results, and whether the framework extends naturally to true multi-task settings.
