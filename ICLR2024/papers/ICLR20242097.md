## Problem

Large pretrained text-to-video models (e.g., Imagen Video with 5.6B parameters) are proprietary black boxes whose weights are not publicly available, ruling out standard fine-tuning approaches like LoRA, DreamBooth, or ControlNet. This prevents adapting these models to domain-specific tasks such as robotics video generation, egocentric modeling, or personalized stylization. The paper asks whether the broad knowledge encoded in a black-box video diffusion model can be leveraged as a probabilistic prior to guide a small task-specific model without any access to the pretrained weights.

## Input / Output

During training, the small adapter model (330M–2.8B params) is trained on domain-specific video-text pairs (e.g., 7.2k Bridge robot clips, 97k Ego4D egocentric clips, or curated anime/sci-fi subsets) conditioned on either the first frame or Sobel edge maps. At inference, the method additionally consumes the denoising score outputs ε_pretrained(τ_t, t | text) from the frozen 5.6B black-box model at every diffusion timestep, which is the critical requirement that distinguishes this from purely prompt-based adaptation. The output is a generated video (24×40 or up to 192×320 via super-resolution) that combines the domain-specific content of the small model with the temporal consistency and visual fidelity of the pretrained prior.

## Method

The key insight is that a diffusion model's denoising function ε_θ(τ_t, t) is the score (negative gradient of the energy) of an underlying EBM, so the product of two diffusion distributions p_pretrained(τ|text)·p_θ(τ|text) corresponds to summing their energy functions, which in turn means summing their denoising predictions at each sampling step: ε(τ_t, t|text) = ε_θ(τ_t, t|text) + λ·ε_pretrained(τ_t, t|text). A scalar λ (or time-dependent schedule) controls the prior strength, and the small model is trained by standard maximum likelihood on the adaptation dataset while the pretrained model remains frozen. To generate sharp, text-conditioned samples, the authors adapt classifier-free guidance to the product distribution by constructing an implicit classifier from the composed text-conditioned density ratio p_product(τ|text)/p_θ(τ), yielding a modified denoising function that upweights the text-conditional component by an inverse temperature ω. This avoids the failure mode of directly interpolating classifier-free scores between the two models, which collapses to the teacher style at intermediate weights.

## Results

On Bridge Data, Video Adapter with a small model of only 1.25% parameters (0.07B) of the 5.6B pretrained model achieves FVD 177.4 and FID 37.6, substantially outperforming the pretrained model (FVD 350.1, FID 42.6). On Ego4D, the larger small model (2.8B) combined with the pretrained prior achieves FVD 52.5 and Inception Score 3.53, beating the pretrained model (FVD 91.7, IS 3.12) and even the pretrained model finetuned for equivalent TPU hours (FVD 75.5, IS 3.33). The key finding is that probabilistic composition with the black-box prior consistently improves the small model's metrics across both domains without requiring access to pretrained weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the 5.6B Imagen Video pretrained model (Ho et al., 2022), used both as-is and finetuned for an equivalent number of TPU hours. Video Adapter decisively outperforms the pretrained model on both datasets: on Bridge Data, the smallest adapter (0.07B, 1.25% of pretrained) achieves FVD 177.4 vs. 350.1 for the pretrained model, and on Ego4D, the larger adapter (2.8B) achieves FVD 52.5 vs. 91.7. Video Adapter also beats the pretrained model finetuned for equivalent compute (FVD 148.1 vs. 321.0 on Bridge; FVD 52.5 vs. 75.5 on Ego4D), demonstrating that probabilistic composition is more sample-efficient than full finetuning. The paper notes this finetuning comparison is included for curiosity rather than as a fair benchmark, since the core motivation is the unavailability of pretrained weights. An ablation (Table 2) further shows that directly interpolating classifier-free guidance scores between the two models (CFG Mix, FVD 167.4) underperforms Video Adapter (FVD 148.1) on Bridge, confirming that the product-distribution formulation is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EBM composition framework of Du et al. (2020) from image generation to video, using the score function of a pretrained video diffusion model as a probabilistic prior that can be combined with a small task-specific model via product distributions. It draws a direct analogy to in-context learning and prompting in LLMs (Brown et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2022), reframing domain adaptation of video models as a 'finetuning-free' problem solvable through score composition rather than weight modification. The approach departs from parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (LoRA, DreamBooth, ControlNet) that all require access to pretrained weights, and from prior video adaptation work that relies on text prompting or light training on a copy of the full model. The key theoretical bridge is the equivalence between diffusion sampling and Langevin dynamics on an EBM, which makes score addition a natural composition operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As large text-to-video models become proprietary services analogous to LLMs, black-box adaptation becomes an unavoidable practical need, and this paper provides a clean, theoretically grounded recipe for it. The main limitation is that it still requires training a small domain-specific model and, critically, requires the pretrained API to expose diffusion scores as outputs—a non-trivial infrastructure change the paper explicitly advocates for. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-domain composition without quality degradation, whether the required score access will actually be adopted by API providers, and whether the small-model training requirement can be further reduced toward truly zero-shot adaptation.
