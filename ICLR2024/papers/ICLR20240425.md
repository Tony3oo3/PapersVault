## Problem

Standard DP-SGD requires per-sample gradient clipping to bound sensitivity, which is computationally expensive (needing per-sample backward passes), memory-intensive at large batch sizes, and biases the average gradient direction. While Lipschitz-constrained networks have been studied for their input-space properties (robustness, generalization), their parameter-space properties—specifically analytically tractable bounds on gradient norms with respect to weights—remained largely unexplored, making them seemingly inapplicable to differentially private training.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard supervised inputs: RGB images (MNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10 32×32, Fashion-MNIST) or tabular feature vectors (Adbench datasets with 3–500 dimensions), which must be norm-bounded (clipped to a ball of radius X₀ or projected onto a sphere). It produces class labels under (ε,δ)-DP guarantees, with a running privacy accountant tracking the cumulative (ε,δ) budget across training steps. The output also includes per-layer sensitivity values Δ_d computed analytically from the architecture, which determine the noise scale for each Gaussian mechanism.

## Method

The core insight is a tight link between a network's input-Lipschitz constant and its parameter-wise gradient norm: by bounding each layer's Jacobian w.r.t. its parameters (which for bilinear layers scales as K·‖x‖), one can compute per-layer sensitivity bounds Δ_d without any per-sample backward pass. The 'Backpropagation for Bounds' algorithm (Algorithm 1) propagates input norm bounds forward and cotangent vector bounds backward, replacing vector-Jacobian products with cheap scalar-scalar products of element-wise spectral norms. These per-layer sensitivities feed into either a per-layer Gaussian mechanism (different noise per layer) or a global isotropic Gaussian mechanism, with privacy tracked via Rényi DP composition. The approach requires Lipschitz constraints enforced via a projection operator Π (spectral normalization or Björck orthogonalization), which acts as privacy-preserving post-processing. Gradient Norm Preserving (GNP) networks—1-Lipschitz networks with orthogonal layer Jacobians—yield the tightest bounds, avoiding the vanishing/exploding gradient phenomena that plague K≠1 configurations, and the resulting sensitivity scales as O(L√D(1+X₀)) for linear layers without biases.

## Results

On tabular Adbench datasets (ε=1), Clipless DP-SGD achieves competitive or superior AUROC to standard DP-SGD: it wins on yeast (75.1% vs 66.8%) and shuttle (99.4% vs 98.3%), ties on donors (100.0%), and is within 1 point on magic (89.7% vs 90.7%) and skin (99.8% vs 100.0%). In the speed benchmark on CIFAR-10 CNNs (130K–2M parameters), Clipless DP-SGD outperforms TF-Privacy, Opacus, and Optax in median batch processing time, with the critical advantage that its overhead is independent of batch size (O(Upd) vs O(BTpd)), enabling much larger batches. On CIFAR-10, the method produces certifiable robustness at multiple radii while maintaining competitive clean accuracy under (ε,δ)-DP training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016), Clipless DP-SGD wins on 2 of 5 comparable tabular datasets (yeast: +8.3 AUROC, shuttle: +1.1 AUROC), ties on donors, and trails by ≤1.0 AUROC on magic and skin. The key structural advantage is that Clipless DP-SGD eliminates per-sample gradient clipping entirely, replacing it with analytically computed per-layer sensitivity bounds from Lipschitz constraints, which removes the need for hyperparameter search on the clipping constant C. Against Opacus (Yousefpour et al., 2021) in the speed benchmark, Clipless DP-SGD achieves lower median batch processing time across all tested CNN sizes (130K–2M parameters on CIFAR-10), and critically, its time and memory overhead scales as O(Upd) (independent of batch size B) versus O(BTpd) for Opacus, making it the only method whose cost does not grow with batch size. This enables larger batches, which further reduce sensitivity (Δ ∝ 1/b) and improve the signal-to-noise ratio without the gradient-direction bias introduced by clipping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate literatures: Lipschitz-constrained neural networks (Anil et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019a), which have been studied almost exclusively for their input-space properties (robustness, generalization), and DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016), which relies on per-sample gradient clipping to bound sensitivity. The key conceptual departure is showing that the input-Lipschitz constant and the parameter-Lipschitz constant are tightly linked, so that enforcing 1-Lipschitz (or GNP) layer constraints yields analytically computable per-layer gradient sensitivity bounds, eliminating the need for clipping entirely. The closest prior work, Shavit & Gjura (2019), used a Lipschitz network as a bounded-sensitivity function approximator but did not analyze the sensitivity of the gradient query itself, which is the core object this paper bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a DP training pipeline whose overhead scales with model size but not batch size, enabling larger batches that reduce sensitivity (Δ ∝ 1/b) and variance simultaneously — a scaling advantage that clipping-based DP-SGD cannot match due to gradient bias from clipping. However, the approach is architecturally constrained: it requires GNP or spectrally-normalized layers, which are less expressive and harder to construct for convolutions than standard CNNs, and the bias shifts from the optimizer (clipping) to the function space (Lipschitz constraint). Open questions include developing better GNP convolutional layers, incorporating differentiable reparametrizations (trivializations) to avoid projection-based constraints, and whether the per-layer sensitivity accounting can be further tightened with layer-specific noise multipliers.
