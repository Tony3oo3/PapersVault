## Problem

Masked image modeling (MIM) methods increasingly use discrete tokens as reconstruction targets, yet the theoretical rationale for this choice remains unexplored. Empirically, different tokenizers (dVAE, VQGAN, perceptual, HOG) produce widely varying downstream performance, but no principled framework explains why some tokenizers help and others hurt. The paper asks: how does discrete tokenization affect the augmentation graph structure and downstream generalization, and how can one select or design a good tokenizer?

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images split into ViT-style patches (e.g., 16×16), applies a random binary mask at 75% ratio to create unmasked and masked views, and feeds the unmasked view to a ViT encoder (Small/Base/Large). The reconstruction target is a sequence of discrete tokens assigned to each masked patch via nearest-neighbor lookup in a K-means codebook (50–1000 centers), rather than raw pixels or continuous features. The pretrained encoder outputs visual representations evaluated via linear probing or fine-tuning on ImageNet-100 and ImageNet-1K.

## Method

The paper formalizes discrete tokenization as an equivalence relation on the masked-view space, showing via the mask graph framework that tokenization aggregates 2-hop connectivity: two unmasked views become positive pairs whenever their masked views share the same discrete token, not just the same pixel values. A toy two-class analysis proves that class-aligned tokenization increases intra-class edge weights and decreases inter-class edge weights in the augmentation graph, yielding a tighter downstream error bound, while cross-class tokenization has the opposite effect. Guided by this, the authors propose TCAS (Token-Class Alignment Similarity), a training-free metric that measures how well tokenizer-induced clusters align with true class labels via a normalized co-occurrence matrix. Motivated by TCAS, ClusterMIM replaces learned tokenizers (dVAE, VQGAN) with a simple K-means codebook built on patches in pixel space (ClusterMIM Pixel) or DINO feature space (ClusterMIM DINO), assigning each patch its nearest cluster center as the discrete reconstruction target.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 with ViT-S, ClusterMIM Pixel achieves 52.7% linear probing and 86.4% fine-tuning accuracy, outperforming MAE (45.9%/81.8%) by +6.8% and +4.6% respectively. On ImageNet-1K with ViT-B, ClusterMIM Pixel reaches 62.1% LP / 83.2% FT versus MAE's 55.4% / 82.9%, and ClusterMIM DINO achieves 67.4% LP / 83.8% FT, surpassing MaskFeat's continuous DINO version (63.2% / 83.7%). The gains hold across ViT-S, ViT-B, and ViT-L backbones, with ClusterMIM Pixel improving over MAE by 2.8–6.8% in linear probing depending on backbone size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ClusterMIM Pixel (K-means in pixel space, no extra model) consistently outperforms MAE across all backbone sizes: +6.8% LP on ViT-S, +1.9% on ViT-B, and +2.8% on ViT-L for ImageNet-100, and +6.7% LP on ImageNet-1K. Against BEiT (dVAE tokenizer), ClusterMIM Pixel wins by a wide margin (e.g., 52.7% vs. 43.2% LP on ViT-S), confirming that dVAE's discrete tokens are poorly aligned with data classes. ClusterMIM Pixel also surpasses PeCo (perceptual VQVAE tokenizer) in fine-tuning accuracy (86.4% vs. 83.6% on ViT-S) despite PeCo's slightly higher LP score (53.2% vs. 52.7%). When using DINO features as the clustering input, ClusterMIM DINO outperforms MaskFeat's continuous DINO target by +2.6% LP and +0.4% FT on ImageNet-100, and by +4.2% LP on ImageNet-1K, demonstrating that discretizing DINO features via K-means is more effective than using them directly as regression targets. The only comparison where ClusterMIM does not clearly win is PeCo vs. ClusterMIM Pixel on LP accuracy for ViT-S (53.2% vs. 52.7%), though ClusterMIM Pixel dominates in fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph-theoretic analysis of MAE from Zhang et al. (2022), which connected masked image modeling to contrastive learning via augmentation graphs, by formally incorporating discrete tokenization into that framework. While prior MIM methods (BEiT, PeCo, MaskFeat) adopted various discrete tokenizers (dVAE, VQGAN, perceptual codebooks) without theoretical justification for their choice, this work provides the first analysis showing how the equivalence classes induced by a tokenizer reshape the augmentation graph's intra-class and inter-class connectivity. The proposed ClusterMIM method is a minimal modification of MAE—replacing the identity reconstruction target with K-means cluster assignments—positioned as a theoretically motivated alternative to the learned tokenizers used in BEiT and PeCo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers practitioners a training-free metric (TCAS) for evaluating tokenizer quality and a simple, effective tokenizer design (K-means) that outperforms more complex learned alternatives, reducing the engineering burden of MIM pretraining. The theoretical insight that tokenizer-class alignment governs downstream performance provides a principled design criterion for future tokenizer development. Limitations include the reliance on a two-class toy model for the main theoretical results, the assumption that views share the same class label, and the fact that ClusterMIM DINO still requires a pretrained DINO model, partially undermining the self-supervised simplicity. Open questions include whether the framework extends to continuous tokenization targets, video MIM, or multi-modal settings, and whether the K-means approach scales gracefully to very large codebooks or non-ImageNet domains.
