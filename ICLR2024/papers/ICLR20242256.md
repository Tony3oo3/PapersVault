## Problem

Existing zero-shot TTS systems are trained with single-sentence prompts, so they cannot effectively exploit the additional speaker data available at inference time, and their prosodic information remains tightly coupled with timbre, preventing controlled transfer of speaking styles across speakers. This paper addresses both limitations by introducing a multi-sentence prompting mechanism and a prosody-timbre disentanglement strategy that enable flexible, fine-grained prosody control while preserving target-speaker identity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242256/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes target text, multi-sentence speech prompts from the target speaker (up to 300 seconds, far exceeding the single-sentence prompts used by prior work), and optionally an auxiliary prosody prompt from a different speaker for style transfer. It outputs synthesized speech audio via a GAN-based mel-spectrogram decoder followed by a pre-trained HiFi-GAN vocoder. The prosody interpolation weight γ (set to 0.8 in experiments) controls the degree of style transfer, and the model supports prompts of varying lengths from 3 seconds to 5 minutes, with performance improving monotonically as prompt length increases.

## Method

Mega-TTS 2 decomposes speech into content, timbre, and prosody through a two-stage training process. In stage 1, a compressive acoustic autoencoder uses a vector-quantised prosody encoder (with a max-pooling + VQ information bottleneck at compression rate r=8 and a 1024-entry codebook), a multi-reference timbre encoder (MRTE) that concatenates multiple reference mel-spectrograms and applies timbre-to-content cross-attention, and a Transformer content encoder, all decoded by an LSGAN-trained mel decoder. In stage 2, a 12-layer decoder-only prosody latent language model (P-LLM, 151M parameters) autoregressively predicts prosody codes from concatenated multi-sentence prompts using teacher-forcing with speaker-level attention masks, while a separate 8-layer autoregressive Transformer models phoneme-level durations. For prosody transfer, the method interpolates the autoregressive probability distributions of two P-LLM decoders (one conditioned on the target speaker's prosody, one on an auxiliary speaker's prosody) at each decoding step with weight γ, enabling fine-grained and controllable style mixing in the discrete code space rather than substituting time-averaged prosody representations.

## Results

On LibriSpeech test-clean, Mega-TTS 2 with 10s prompts achieves WER 2.28%, SIM 0.905, and QMOS 4.05, substantially outperforming VALL-E-10s (WER 5.54%, SIM 0.893, QMOS 3.92) and the fine-tuning baseline (WER 4.74%, SIM 0.895, QMOS 3.97). With 300s prompts, Mega-TTS 2 reaches WER 2.23%, SIM 0.932, and QMOS 4.12, matching or exceeding the fine-tuning baseline (WER 3.11%, SIM 0.934, QMOS 4.08) while remaining a zero-shot method. In prosody transfer on ESD→LibriSpeech, Mega-TTS 2 achieves the lowest duration error (32.8 µs) and pitch moments closest to ground truth (σ=72.62 vs. GT 74.91), while preserving target-speaker similarity (SIM 0.920) better than CopyCat (0.843) and Daft-Exprt (0.901).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VALL-E (zero-shot), Mega-TTS 2 wins decisively on every metric at every prompt length: at 10s, WER drops from 5.54% to 2.28% and QMOS rises from 3.92 to 4.05; VALL-E's performance actually degrades at 20s (WER 8.77%, SIM 0.805) due to its single-sentence training paradigm, while Mega-TTS 2 continues to improve with longer prompts. Against the fine-tuning baseline (FastSpeech 2 + GAN + Meta-StyleSpeech speaker encoder), Mega-TTS 2 surpasses it in WER, QMOS, and SMOS at both 10s and 60s prompt lengths, and matches it in SIM at 300s (0.932 vs. 0.934) while achieving lower WER (2.23% vs. 3.11%)—all without any per-speaker fine-tuning. In prosody transfer, Mega-TTS 2 outperforms CopyCat and Daft-Exprt on target-speaker similarity (SIM 0.920 vs. 0.843 and 0.901), duration error (32.8 vs. 37.2 and 36.5 µs), and pitch distribution moments (closer to GT), but yields lower source-speaker similarity (0.513 vs. 0.740 and 0.633), reflecting a trade-off between preserving the target timbre and retaining the source prosody identity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mega-TTS 2 extends the zero-shot TTS paradigm established by VALL-E, NaturalSpeech 2, and VoiceBox, which rely on single-sentence in-context learning, by introducing a multi-sentence prompting mechanism built around a prosody-timbre disentangled autoencoder. The P-LLM repurposes the autoregressive LLM in-context learning idea from VALL-E but applies it to a compact prosody codebook rather than full speech tokens, while the MRTE generalizes the speaker encoder concept from adaptive TTS (Meta-StyleSpeech, Attentron) to aggregate timbre from multiple reference utterances. For prosody transfer, the paper departs from CopyCat and Daft-Exprt's direct representation substitution by interpolating autoregressive probability distributions from multiple P-LLM outputs, enabling fine-grained and controllable style mixing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a zero-shot system can match or exceed fine-tuning baselines across 10 seconds to 5 minutes of prompt data is practically significant, as it eliminates the computational cost and data preparation overhead of fine-tuning for voice cloning. The prosody-timbre disentanglement via VQ encoding opens a path toward fine-grained, controllable prosody manipulation in zero-shot settings, which prior methods could not achieve. Key limitations include English-only training, reliance on a strong mutual-information assumption (Eq. 1) for disentanglement validity, and the need for external alignment tools for duration. Open questions include whether the multi-sentence prompting strategy transfers to multilingual settings, how it behaves with highly noisy or domain-shifted prompts, and whether the information bottleneck design generalizes to other speech attributes beyond pitch and energy.
