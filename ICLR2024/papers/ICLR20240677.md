## Problem

Existing text-based video editing methods require per-video-per-model finetuning, demand users to describe both the original and target videos (counterintuitive), and fail to maintain consistency across long videos that exceed a single model's frame capacity. This paper addresses all three limitations by introducing a universal one-model-all-video approach that requires only a single editing instruction and handles extended video sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip and a single editing instruction (e.g., 'make it snowy') as input, producing an edited video as output. Videos are processed in batches of 16 frames at 256×256 or 384×384 resolution, with Long Video Sampling Correction using the final N frames of each preceding batch as reference to maintain inter-batch consistency. Unlike prior methods requiring both an original-video prompt and a target-video prompt, InsV2V needs only the edit prompt, simplifying the user interaction to a single natural-language instruction.

## Method

InsV2V is built by inflating a pre-trained 2D image-to-image Stable Diffusion model into a video-to-video model: convolutional and attention layers are reshaped to handle 5D video tensors, and temporal attention modules are inserted after each spatial attention layer for frame consistency. Training data is a synthetic paired video dataset generated by an LLM (GPT-3/MPT-30B) producing prompt triplets (input, edit, edited) and a Prompt-to-Prompt pipeline adapted to video diffusion, where self-attention replacement is extended to temporal attention layers to preserve structural coherence. The model is trained with a dual-condition loss (reference video + editing text) using classifier-free guidance with separate video and text guidance scales. For long videos, Long Video Sampling Correction (LVSC) concatenates the final N frames of the previous batch as noisy reference frames and applies a score correction term during each denoising step, supplemented by optical-flow-based motion compensation for global camera motion.

## Results

On the TGVE benchmark, InsV2V achieves the highest PickScore (20.76) and the best user-study average (0.699), leading all baselines on Text Alignment (0.690), Structure preservation (0.717), and Quality (0.689). The method trails most baselines on CLIP Frame consistency (0.911 vs. 0.924–0.940 for competitors), which the authors argue is an imperfect metric for edited videos with scene changes. In the LVSC ablation (Table 1), adding Long Video Sampling Correction reduces motion-aware MSE from 2.02% to 1.44%, and further adding Motion Compensation lowers it to 1.37% while slightly improving CLIP Frame similarity from 0.9072 to 0.9095.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InsV2V decisively outperforms all seven baselines on the user-study average (0.699 vs. 0.439–0.591), with the largest margins over Vid2Vid-Zero (+0.260) and Video-P2P (+0.224). On PickScore, InsV2V leads by 0.05–1.18 points over all competitors, with the closest rival being CAMP* (20.71). The method wins on Text Alignment against every baseline (0.690 vs. 0.287–0.689), indicating superior instruction following, and on Structure preservation (0.717 vs. 0.357–0.601), confirming that editing does not destroy the original video layout. The sole metric where InsV2V underperforms is CLIP Frame consistency (0.911), trailing TokenFlow (0.940) and Video-P2P (0.935); the authors attribute this to CLIP Frame measuring per-frame semantic similarity, which naturally varies in well-edited videos with scene changes. Among tuning-free methods (TokenFlow, Pix2Video, Render-A-Video), InsV2V leads on all user-study dimensions while matching or exceeding their PickScore, validating the one-model-all-video strategy over per-video optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

InsV2V directly extends the Instruct Pix2Pix paradigm (Brooks et al., 2023) from image editing to video, replacing per-video-per-model finetuning (Tune-A-Video) and dual-prompt formulations (Vid2Vid-Zero, Video-P2P, ControlVideo) with a single model conditioned only on an editing instruction. The synthetic paired dataset generation pipeline adapts Prompt-to-Prompt (Hertz et al., 2022) from image to video diffusion, and the model architecture is obtained by inflating a pre-trained Stable Diffusion image-to-image model with temporal attention layers, following the model-inflation recipe of Wu et al. (2022) and Guo et al. (2023). The Long Video Sampling Correction (LVSC) is a novel sampling-time technique with no direct prior counterpart, addressing the inter-batch consistency problem that plagues all existing methods when videos exceed the model's fixed frame window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating per-video finetuning and reducing user input to a single editing prompt, InsV2V makes text-based video editing substantially more practical for real-world deployment, trading a one-time synthetic dataset creation cost for zero per-video optimization. However, the method is constrained to 32 frames at 256–384 resolution, and its CLIP Frame consistency score (0.911) trails several baselines (up to 0.940), suggesting that temporal coherence over longer or higher-resolution sequences remains an open challenge. The reliance on a public text-to-video model for synthetic data generation and the modest 3× success-rate gain from video-specific captions (WebVid-MPT) versus image captions (LAION-IPTP) highlight that data quality and diversity will be critical bottlenecks as the approach scales to more complex editing scenarios.
