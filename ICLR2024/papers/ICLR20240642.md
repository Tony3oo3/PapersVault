## Problem

Density ratio modeling has emerged as a powerful paradigm in offline RL, offering sample-efficient learning under weaker representation conditions than Bellman completeness, yet it has been conspicuously absent from online RL. The core obstacle is that density ratios require an exploratory dataset with good coverage, but the fundamental challenge in online RL is collecting such a dataset without having one to start. This paper asks whether density ratio modeling can nonetheless yield provable sample-efficiency guarantees for online RL under the structural condition of coverability.

## Input / Output

The learner interacts with an unknown episodic MDP over N rounds, receiving trajectories of length H at each round. It is given a value function class F (realizing Q*) and a weight function class W (realizing density ratios between any pair of policies in the induced policy class); no explicit knowledge of the coverability distribution is required. For the hybrid variant HYGLOW, an additional offline dataset D_off of size H·T from a distribution ν with C*-single-policy concentrability is provided, where ν need only cover the optimal policy rather than all policies. The output is an ε-suboptimal policy, with sample complexity scaling as Õ(H²C_cov/ε² · log(|F||W|/δ)) under strong realizability.

## Method

GLOW constructs a confidence set via a minimax average Bellman error constraint weighted by clipped density ratios, combining this with optimism to drive exploration. The key technical insight is that while individual density ratios d_h^{π(t)}/d̄_h^{(t)} may be unbounded early in learning, coverability guarantees that the cumulative loss from clipping (truncating weights at scale γt) is small on average across all iterations, enabling non-vacuous concentration. A quadratic penalty on the weight functions facilitates variance-dependent Bernstein-style concentration, yielding 1/ε² sample complexity. For computational efficiency, the H2O meta-algorithm provides a provable black-box reduction from hybrid RL to offline RL by repeatedly invoking a CC-bounded offline algorithm on a growing mixture of offline and online data; instantiating H2O with a clipped, regularized variant of MABO yields HYGLOW, which removes the need for global optimism and reduces to a convex-concave program.

## Results

Under strong density ratio realizability (Assumption 2.2'), GLOW achieves a PAC sample complexity of Õ(H²C_cov/ε² · log(|F||W|/δ)) trajectories and a regret bound of Õ(H√(C_cov·T·log(|F||W|/δ))), matching the rate of Xie et al. (2023) under Bellman completeness but without requiring it. Under the weaker pure-policy density ratio realizability (Assumption 2.2), the sample complexity degrades to Õ(H⁴C_cov²/ε⁴ · log(|F||W|/δ)). For the hybrid setting, HYGLOW achieves Õ(H²(C_cov+C_★)/ε² · log(|F||W|/δ)) trajectories while being computationally efficient (concave-convex optimization rather than global optimism).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets. The key theoretical comparison is to Xie et al. (2023), who achieved the same Õ(H²C_cov/ε²) sample complexity under coverability but required the stronger Bellman completeness assumption on the value function class; GLOW replaces this with density ratio realizability, which is incomparable (neither implies the other). The paper also contrasts its two realizability assumptions: the mixture version (Assumption 2.2') yields the tighter 1/ε² rate, while the pure-policy version (Assumption 2.2) incurs a polynomial penalty (1/ε⁴) due to the need to augment the weight class. Computationally, GLOW requires solving a global optimism problem (intractable in general), whereas HYGLOW reduces to a concave-convex program via the H2O meta-algorithm, making it practical for the hybrid RL setting. The paper notes that GLOW generalizes the GOLF algorithm (Jin et al., 2021a; Xie et al., 2023) by allowing weight functions to take negative values, recovering comparable results when Bellman completeness holds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously parallel research threads: density ratio modeling in offline RL (MABO, Xie and Jiang 2020; Zhan et al. 2022) and coverability-based online RL (Xie et al. 2023, which required Bellman completeness). GLOW departs from Xie et al. (2023) by replacing the Bellman completeness assumption with density ratio realizability, adapting the MABO minimax Bellman error constraint to the online setting via clipped weight functions to handle partial coverage. The H2O meta-algorithm generalizes Song et al. (2023)'s hybrid RL framework into a provable black-box reduction from hybrid to offline RL, with HYGLOW instantiating it using a clipped MABO variant. The global optimism framework of Jiang et al. (2017) underpins the exploration mechanism, and GLOW is shown to generalize GOLF (Jin et al. 2021a) when weight functions are allowed to take negative values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish that density ratio realizability plus value function realizability (without Bellman completeness) suffices for sample-efficient online RL under coverability, opening a path toward unifying online and offline RL theory. Key open questions include whether the 1/ε⁴ sample complexity under pure-policy density ratio realizability can be tightened to 1/ε², and whether value realizability and coverability alone (without any density ratio assumption) are sufficient. The computational intractability of GLOW (requiring global optimization over the function class) is a significant practical limitation, motivating the hybrid setting and HYGLOW, but no empirically efficient online density-ratio algorithm is yet available.
