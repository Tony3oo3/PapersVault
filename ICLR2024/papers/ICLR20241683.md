## Problem

Generalizable 3D scene representation models (e.g., SRT, OSRT) trained across many scenes produce blurry novel-view renderings because their L2 training objective forces them to average over uncertainty in unobserved regions. While diffusion models have achieved high-fidelity image synthesis, applying them to complex multi-object 3D scenes while maintaining geometric consistency and enabling object-level controllability remains an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241683/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of context views (5 for MultiShapeNet, 3 for Street View) and target camera poses as input. The context views are encoded by a frozen OSRT encoder into 32 Object Slots, which together with per-view sinusoidal pose embeddings form the conditioning signal. The output is a set of L 3D-consistent novel view renderings at 64×64 or 128×128 resolution, which can also be decomposed into per-object contributions for editing, or extended into smooth multi-frame camera path videos via a staged denoising procedure.

## Method

DORSal combines a frozen OSRT encoder (producing Object Slots via Slot Attention over a set-latent scene representation) with a video diffusion decoder (Multiview U-Net) that generates L novel views in parallel. The U-Net uses 2D convolutions within each view and axial self-attention across views for inter-frame consistency, following the Video Diffusion Models architecture. Conditioning on the K Object Slots and per-view pose is injected via cross-attention and FiLM modulation, treating the slots analogously to text tokens in text-to-image diffusion. Training uses DDPM with a cosine noise schedule and classifier-free guidance (conditioning dropout 0.1, guidance weight 2). For scene editing, individual slots are masked from the conditioning to remove or transfer objects; for long camera paths, three types of frame shuffling (identity, half-context shift, random permutation) are interleaved across denoising stages to enforce both local smoothness and global 3D consistency.

## Results

On MultiShapeNet, DORSal achieves a FID of 11.01, a 5–10× improvement over SRT (67.29), OSRT (100.7), and Sup-OSRT (112.1), while on Street View it reaches FID 16.24 versus 87.91 (SRT) and 165.1 (OSRT). DORSal trades some PSNR (18.76 on MultiShapeNet vs. 25.93 for SRT) for substantially sharper, less blurry renders, as reflected in its best LPIPS among object-representation methods (0.266 vs. 0.237 for SRT). Against the diffusion-based 3DiM baseline, DORSal strictly outperforms on all metrics on both datasets, with a particularly large PSNR gap on Street View (16.36 vs. 12.68). For scene editing, DORSal achieves 45.8% FG-mIoU and 70.0% FG-ARI on MultiShapeNet, approaching the supervised upper bound of Sup-OSRT (50.0% / 75.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SRT [44] and OSRT [42], DORSal achieves dramatically lower FID (11.01 vs. 67.29 and 100.7 on MultiShapeNet; 16.24 vs. 87.91 and 165.1 on Street View) at the cost of lower PSNR, confirming that the diffusion decoder resolves the blur inherent in L2-trained deterministic decoders. Against Sup-OSRT [31], which provides the supervised object slots used as conditioning, DORSal again wins on FID (11.01 vs. 112.1) and LPIPS, while Sup-OSRT retains a PSNR advantage. Against 3DiM [55], the pose-conditional diffusion baseline, DORSal outperforms on every metric on both datasets (e.g., FID 9.00 vs. 10.94 on MultiShapeNet; PSNR 16.36 vs. 12.68 on Street View), with the largest gap on Street View where 3DiM's single-view stochastic conditioning struggles with large camera gaps. For scene editing, DORSal (45.8% FG-mIoU, 70.0% FG-ARI) surpasses the unsupervised OSRT baseline (43.1% / 79.6%) on mIoU but trails both OSRT and Sup-OSRT on ARI, indicating that the diffusion decoder partially preserves but does not fully match the object-level disentanglement of the supervised encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

DORSal sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) object-centric scene representation models like OSRT and SRT, which learn structured, editable scene latents from few views but produce blurry renders due to L2 training, and (2) video diffusion models (Ho et al., 2022c), which generate high-fidelity sequences but lack explicit 3D scene structure. The paper's key architectural departure is using frozen OSRT object slots as cross-attention and FiLM conditioning for a Multiview U-Net diffusion decoder, rather than conditioning on raw images as in 3DiM (Watson et al., 2023). This structured conditioning replaces the stochastic image-to-image conditioning of prior diffusion-based NVS with a compact, object-level scene description, enabling both higher fidelity and object-level controllability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that object-level editing capabilities transfer to the diffusion decoder without any editing-specific training or segmentation supervision, suggesting that structured scene representations can serve as a general-purpose control interface for generative models. However, the approach is not end-to-end (limited by upstream OSRT quality), supports only coarse edits (removal/transfer, not transformation), and exhibits flickering in long camera paths. The paper opens a path toward joint training on large-scale video and 3D scene data, and the slot-conditioning pattern could generalize to other structured generative tasks, but fine-grained editing (rotation, translation) and resolution scaling remain open.
