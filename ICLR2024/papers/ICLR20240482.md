## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models can be trained or fine-tuned on artists' or datasets' images without permission, but existing defenses are inadequate: Glaze-style unlearnable examples block all training (including authorized) and rely on surrogate models with limited transferability, while membership inference methods achieve only ~66% accuracy on Stable Diffusion and are even harder in black-box settings. No prior method can detect unauthorized data usage in a model-agnostic, black-box manner while minimally affecting authorized training quality.

## Input / Output

The detector takes black-box access to an inspected text-to-image diffusion model and a set of N=50 text prompts sampled from the protected dataset (optionally prepended with a trigger word like 'tq' for trigger-conditioned memorization). It produces a binary verdict (unauthorized usage detected or not) via a t-distribution-based hypothesis test, along with an estimated memorization strength α(M,S,η) representing the fraction of generated images classified as containing the signal. The signal classifier itself is a ResNet-18 fine-tuned on the protected images (original vs. warped) and requires only the protected dataset, not the inspected model's weights.

## Method

DIAGNOSIS plants element-level injected memorization into any model trained on the protected data by applying a stealthy image warping function (from Wanet) to a subset of protected images before release, making the coated images visually near-identical (SSIM > 0.95) but carrying a transformation that diffusion models memorize and reproduce in generated outputs. Two memorization types are supported: unconditional (warping always appears in outputs) and trigger-conditioned (warping appears only when a text trigger is present), with the latter effective even at 2% coating rates. Detection works by generating images from the inspected model using sampled prompts, passing them through a fine-tuned ResNet-18 signal classifier to estimate memorization strength, and applying a one-sided t-test (threshold τ=0.05, significance γ=0.05) to distinguish models with unauthorized usage (α ≈ 91–100%) from clean models (α ≈ 4–6%). The approach is model-agnostic (Stable Diffusion v1/v2, VQ Diffusion), training-method-agnostic (LoRA, DreamBooth, standard training, Textual Inversion), and robust to adaptive augmentations like JPEG compression, Gaussian blur, and noise injection.

## Results

DIAGNOSIS achieves 100.0% detection accuracy (0 FP, 0 FN) across all fine-tuning settings (Stable Diffusion v1/v2 + LoRA, DreamBooth) on Pokemon, CelebA, and CUB-200 datasets, with memorization strengths of ~91.2% for unauthorized models vs. ~5.1% for authorized models. In the standard training scenario (VQ Diffusion on CUB-200), unauthorized models show 96–100% memorization strength versus 4–6% for authorized models. The method maintains 100% accuracy even when the infringer collects data from multiple sources (25–50% collect fraction), and the FID degradation is modest (199.29 → 218.28 for unconditional memorization).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DIAGNOSIS against Yu et al. (2021), a deepfake attribution method adapted for unauthorized data usage detection. In the multi-source scenario (25% collect fraction, Stable Diffusion v1 + LoRA on Pokemon), Yu et al. achieves only 50.0% detection accuracy (0 TP, 10 FN, 10 TN), failing to detect any unauthorized model. In contrast, both DIAGNOSIS-unconditional and DIAGNOSIS-trigger-conditioned achieve 100.0% accuracy (10 TP, 0 FP, 0 FN, 10 TN), correctly identifying all unauthorized models with zero false positives. The paper also notes that existing membership inference methods (e.g., SecMI) achieve only 66.1% success rate on Stable Diffusion v1.5 under white-box settings, and that unlearnable-example-based defenses like Glaze make authorized training impossible, positioning DIAGNOSIS as a complementary detection approach rather than a prevention one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the imperceptible image warping function from Nguyen & Tran (2021, originally a backdoor attack trigger) as a stealthy signal function for dataset coating, and adapts the statistical hypothesis testing framework from Li et al. (2023b, black-box backdoor watermarking for classifiers) to the text-to-image diffusion model setting. It departs from Glaze (Shan et al., 2023), which prevents unauthorized training but also blocks legitimate use, and from membership inference approaches (Carlini et al., 2023a; Duan et al., 2023), which target sample-level memorization and are shown to be ineffective for diffusion models. The key conceptual shift is from whole-sample memorization to element-level memorization of a specific transformation function, enabling detection without requiring the infringer to reproduce exact training samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize and exploit element-level memorization in text-to-image diffusion models, providing a practical black-box detection mechanism that works across Stable Diffusion v1/v2, VQ Diffusion, and multiple fine-tuning methods (LoRA, DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) with minimal impact on generation quality. The robustness to very strong training-time augmentations (5% JPEG compression, kernel-51 Gaussian blur) and effectiveness at as low as 2% coating rate (trigger-conditioned) suggest practical deployability for artists and data providers. Open questions include scalability to very large protected datasets, robustness to more sophisticated adaptive attacks (e.g., the infringer selectively removing coated images), and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D generation models.
