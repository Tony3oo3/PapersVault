## Problem

Optimizing macro-averaged performance metrics (precision, recall, F1) under a strict budget of exactly k predicted labels per instance is fundamentally harder than standard macro-averaging because the at-k constraint couples all binary label problems together, preventing independent per-label optimization. No statistically consistent algorithm existed for this budgeted-at-k setting in the population utility framework, despite its importance for extreme multi-label classification with long-tail labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an input instance x and a vector of estimated label marginals η̂(x) ∈ [0,1]^m produced by a separately trained label probability estimator (neural network or sparse linear model). It outputs a randomized k-hot binary vector ŷ ∈ {0,1}^m with exactly k ones, represented as a mixture of deterministic top-k classifiers with learned sampling weights α^i. The budget constraint ‖ŷ‖₁ = k is enforced via Madow's sampling scheme, which converts the continuous marginal vector h(x) ∈ Δ_m^k into a valid discrete prediction.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that the optimal classifier for any macro-at-k metric (even non-linear ones like F1) takes the form top_k(a ⊙ η(x) + b), i.e., selecting the k labels with highest scores under an affine transformation of the label marginals, where the coefficients a, b are determined by the gradient of the metric evaluated at the optimal confusion tensor. Since this optimal confusion tensor is unknown a priori, the paper proposes a Frank-Wolfe algorithm that iteratively: (1) computes the gradient of the metric w.r.t. the current confusion tensor to obtain a linearized gain tensor, (2) solves the resulting linear subproblem via top-k scoring with the derived affine scores, and (3) performs a line search to interpolate between the old and new confusion tensors. The algorithm is proven statistically consistent under mild assumptions (absolute continuity of η, differentiability and monotonicity of the metric), with regret bounds that decompose into label probability estimation error, uniform convergence error, and Frank-Wolfe optimization error.

## Results

The proposed Frank-Wolfe algorithm (MACRO-F1_FW) consistently achieves the best macro F1 scores across datasets: 13.82 on MEDIAMILL@5, 29.43 on FLICKR@5, 13.68 on RCV1X@5, and 41.92 on AMAZONCAT@5, compared to 7.71, 23.49, 7.64, and 35.06 for the standard TOP-K baseline respectively. For macro precision, MACRO-P_FW leads on all four datasets (e.g., 56.47 vs. 46.43 for TOP-K on AMAZONCAT@5). On the largest dataset (AMAZONCAT, 13,330 labels), the heuristic TOP-K+wPOW slightly outperforms MACRO-F1_FW on macro F1@5 (46.30 vs. 41.92), attributed to sparse label frequencies preventing accurate estimation of η and the confusion tensor. The algorithm converges within 3–10 Frank-Wolfe iterations in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the TOP-K baseline (Wydmuch et al., 2018), MACRO-F1_FW wins on macro F1@5 across all four datasets by margins of 5.9–6.9 points (e.g., 41.92 vs. 35.06 on AMAZONCAT, 29.43 vs. 23.49 on FLICKR), confirming that the Frank-Wolfe optimization effectively exploits the budgeted-at-k constraint to balance precision and recall across labels. Compared to TOP-K+ℓFOCAL (Lin et al., 2017), MACRO-F1_FW achieves substantially higher macro F1 on FLICKR (29.43 vs. 24.16) and RCV1X (13.68 vs. 7.75), but is slightly outperformed on AMAZONCAT (41.92 vs. 43.05), where focal loss's emphasis on hard labels partially compensates for the sparse positive counts. Against TOP-K+ℓASYM (Ridnik et al., 2021), MACRO-F1_FW wins on all four datasets (e.g., 41.92 vs. 40.60 on AMAZONCAT, 13.68 vs. 8.41 on RCV1X). The paper also notes that MACRO-R_FW exactly recovers the closed-form MACRO-R_PRIOR solution in all cases, validating the theoretical result that macro recall has a known optimal top-k rule. The main trade-off is that the Frank-Wolfe methods sacrifice instance-wise precision (e.g., MACRO-P_FW drops instance P@5 from 63.63 to 41.04 on AMAZONCAT) in exchange for superior macro-averaged performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the population utility framework for consistent algorithms from multi-class classification with constraints (Narasimhan et al., 2015; 2022) and standard multi-label classification (Koyejo et al., 2015; Kotłowski & Dembczyński, 2017) to the budgeted-at-k setting, where exactly k labels must be predicted per instance. The key departure is that the at-k constraint couples otherwise independent per-label binary problems, making the optimization fundamentally harder than the non-budgeted case. The authors use the Frank-Wolfe method to perform implicit optimization over the set of feasible confusion tensors, leveraging the theoretical result that the optimal classifier for any (even non-linear) confusion tensor measure takes the simple form of selecting top-k labels by an affine function of the marginal label probabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first statistically consistent algorithm for macro-at-k metrics in the population utility framework, which is important for extreme multi-label classification and recommendation systems where a fixed prediction budget is natural and tail labels must be treated fairly. The theoretical results are notably general, applying to any confusion tensor measure (not just macro-averages), and the Frank-Wolfe algorithm scales to datasets with thousands of labels. A key limitation is that on the most extreme dataset (AMAZONCAT, 13K labels), where most labels have fewer than 10 positive training instances, the method underperforms simple prior-weighted heuristics due to poor estimation of label marginals and confusion matrices. Open questions include how to incorporate regularization or prior information to handle the sparse-label regime, and whether the approach extends to other constrained prediction settings beyond fixed budget.
