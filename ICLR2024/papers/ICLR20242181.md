## Problem

Implicit neural representations (INRs) suffer from spectral bias, fitting low-frequency modes at the expense of the high-frequency detail that encodes fine structure. Existing remedies—sinusoidal positional encoding, Fourier features, and hierarchical hash encodings—require hardcoded inductive biases, hyperparameter tuning, or prohibitive hardware, and none simultaneously preserve both the positional and the raw value information of each coordinate input. The paper asks whether a single, simple MLP can overcome spectral bias without any preprocessing or tuning.

## Input / Output

The network receives a single coordinate pair (2D for images, 1D for audio, 3D spatiotemporal for video) scaled to [-1, 1] (or [-100, 100] for audio) and outputs a continuous scalar signal value, enabling arbitrary-resolution queries. Training is full-batch gradient descent on a single object (e.g., one 256×256 image channel, one 7-second audio clip, or one 250-frame video), so the model is fully overfit and the weights alone constitute the representation. Because the mapping is continuous, the same trained network can be evaluated at denser coordinate grids to produce super-resolved images (up to 8×), interpolated audio, or intermediate video frames without additional training.

## Method

SPDER replaces SIREN's pure sinusoidal activation with a semiperiodic nonlinearity sin(ω₀x)·δ(x), where δ is a sublinear damping function (default √|x|, with log|x|, arctan(x), and √ReLU(x) also effective). The sinusoidal factor lets each neuron automatically learn a positional embedding, while the damping factor modulates amplitude so that the raw coordinate magnitude is preserved across layers rather than being collapsed into a fixed [-1, 1] range. The local Lipschitz constant of the activation is bounded by Θ(δ(a)+∇δ(a)); restricting δ to be sublinear keeps this constant small enough to avoid the unstable, irregular oscillations that linear or superlinear δ produce. The architecture is a plain 5-layer MLP (256 neurons/layer, ω₀=30, SIREN-style weight initialization) trained with MSE loss and full-batch gradient descent—no positional encoding, no Fourier features, no hierarchy, and no dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning. This single design choice yields 10,000–100,000× lower reconstruction loss and ~10× faster convergence than SIREN on 256×256 image representation, with near-perfect amplitude-spectrum cosine similarity (ρ_AG > 0.9999).

## Results

On DIV2K 256×256 single-channel image representation, SPDER achieves a loss of 6.7e-8 and PSNR of 85.7 dB after 500 training steps, compared to SIREN's 0.0010 loss and 36.6 dB PSNR — a 10,000–100,000× reduction in loss. SPDER's frequency similarity (ρ_AG) reaches 0.99995, indicating near-perfect amplitude spectrum alignment with the ground truth. The method also achieves state-of-the-art audio representation on ESC-50 (ρ_AG = 0.9863 at 1000 steps vs. SIREN's 0.9647) and super-resolution PSNRs ~5 points above SIREN across 2×, 4×, and 8× factors on DIV2K, FFHQ, and Flickr2K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPDER decisively outperforms SIREN (its direct architectural predecessor) by 10,000–100,000× in MSE loss and ~49 PSNR points at 500 steps (85.7 vs. 36.6 dB), while also achieving near-perfect frequency alignment (ρ_AG = 0.99995 vs. 0.9988). Against Instant-NGP, SPDER achieves substantially lower loss (6.7e-8 vs. 0.0001) and higher PSNR (85.7 vs. 46.6) at 500 steps, though Instant-NGP trains ~3× faster (~5 s vs. ~15 s for 500 steps) and is better suited for extremely high-information media like gigapixel images. The ReLU-based baselines (plain, with positional encoding, and with Fourier features) are all far behind SPDER on every metric; even the best ReLU variant (ReLU w/ PE) reaches only 29.7 dB PSNR and 0.993 ρ_AG at 500 steps, roughly 56 dB below SPDER. On audio (ESC-50), SPDER with δ=arctan achieves ρ_AG of 0.9863 at 1000 steps versus SIREN's 0.9647, and its loss distribution has a noticeably sparser left tail, indicating fewer pathological failures. For super-resolution, SPDER consistently exceeds SIREN by ~5 PSNR points across all datasets and scale factors (e.g., 29.4 vs. 23.2 dB on DIV2K at 2×), with ρ_AG above 0.997 in all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPDER is a direct architectural modification of SIREN (Sitzmann et al., 2020), replacing its pure sinusoidal activation sin(ω₀x) with a semiperiodic activation sin(ω₀x)·δ(x) where δ is a sublinear damping function (default √|x|). It departs from the positional-encoding and Fourier-feature approaches (NeRF, Tancik et al.) that hardcode frequency information during preprocessing, and from hierarchical methods like Instant-NGP that partition objects into smaller receptive fields, by claiming that a single flat MLP with this modified nonlinearity can automatically learn positional structure without any such inductive biases. The paper positions itself as the simplest possible fix to spectral bias: one multiplicative factor on the activation, no extra layers, no preprocessing, no hyperparameter tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the claimed 10,000–100,000× loss reduction over SIREN generalizes beyond single-channel 256×256 overfitting, SPDER offers an extremely cheap and hardware-agnostic alternative to both SIREN and Instant-NGP for coordinate-based representation, running on consumer laptops. Key open questions include whether the advantage persists for full RGB images, 3D SDFs, and novel view synthesis (where the authors acknowledge underperformance), whether the optimal δ can be selected without per-domain experimentation, and whether combining SPDER with hierarchical encodings yields further gains. The ~1.3× training slowdown and ~1.5× memory overhead relative to SIREN are minor but non-trivial at scale.
