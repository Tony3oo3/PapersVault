## Problem

Existing 3D indoor scene synthesis methods either model object relations implicitly through individual attributes (hindering controllability) or require users to specify elaborate relation graphs that are impractical for natural interaction. Natural language instructions for interior design rely on abstract spatial relationships and appearance descriptions, but no prior method effectively bridges this gap while maintaining both generation fidelity and instruction adherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241015/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes natural language instructions (typically 1–2 (subject, relation, object) triplets with appearance descriptors, e.g., 'Place a dining table to the right of a coffee table') and produces a complete 3D indoor scene where each object is retrieved from the 3D-FRONT database and assigned 7-DoF attributes (category, 3D position, axis-aligned size, SO(2) rotation, and quantized semantic feature). For zero-shot tasks (stylization, re-arrangement, completion), partial scene attributes are additionally provided as intermediate masked states in the discrete diffusion process. The intermediate representation is a directed semantic graph whose nodes carry category and 4 quantized feature indices, and whose edges encode one of 11 predefined spatial relations.

## Method

INSTRUCTSCENE decomposes scene generation into two stages: a discrete semantic graph prior and a continuous layout decoder. The graph prior uses a novel discrete diffusion model where object categories, quantized semantic features (from a frozen OpenShape point-cloud encoder, compressed via an fVQ-VAE with a 64-entry codebook), and spatial relations are independently diffused using transition matrices with an absorbing [MASK] state, enabling cross-attribute information sharing during denoising. A Graph Transformer with FiLM-based node-edge fusion, AdaLN timestep injection, and cross-attention to a frozen CLIP text encoder learns the conditional prior p(G|y). The layout decoder then applies standard DDPM with variance-preserving Gaussian kernels to generate continuous 7-DoF layout attributes conditioned on the discrete graph, reducing the optimization burden by separating discrete and continuous modeling. The independent mask strategy (rather than joint masking or uniform transitions) is critical, as ablations show it enables the prior to exploit uncorrupted portions of other variables for recovery, yielding substantially better controllability (iRecall) and fidelity than baselines.

## Results

On the 3D-FRONT dataset, INSTRUCTSCENE achieves the highest instruction recall (iRecall) across all three room types: 73.64% (bedroom), 56.81% (living room), and 61.23% (dining room), surpassing ATISS and DiffuScene by 15–25 percentage points. In terms of generation fidelity, INSTRUCTSCENE also attains the best FID (114.78/110.39/129.76), FID_CLIP (6.65/5.37/7.67), and KID (0.32/8.16/13.24) scores. The scene classification accuracy (SCA), where values closer to 50% indicate better quality, is 56.02%/65.42%/64.20% for INSTRUCTSCENE versus 59–73% for baselines. The advantage is most pronounced in complex scenes (living and dining rooms with ~20 objects) compared to simpler bedrooms (~8 objects).

## Baselines & Comparisons

INSTRUCTSCENE is compared against two state-of-the-art baselines: ATISS (an autoregressive Transformer that sequentially generates unordered object sets) and DiffuScene (a Gaussian diffusion model treating scene attributes as a 2D matrix). INSTRUCTSCENE decisively outperforms both on the controllability metric (iRecall), achieving 73.64% vs. 48.13% (ATISS) and 56.43% (DiffuScene) on bedrooms, and 56.81% vs. 29.50%/31.15% on living rooms — a gap of roughly 15–25 percentage points. On fidelity metrics (FID, FID_CLIP, KID), INSTRUCTSCENE also leads across all room types, with the largest KID improvement on living rooms (8.16 vs. 17.60 for ATISS and 16.49 for DiffuScene). ATISS edges out DiffuScene on FID due to its discrete-space modeling, while DiffuScene shows slightly better iRecall than ATISS owing to its global visibility during generation. The paper notes that both baselines struggle with high-dimensional semantic feature distributions, and even after augmenting them with quantized features, they remain substantially inferior to INSTRUCTSCENE, particularly on complex scenes with more objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

INSTRUCTSCENE departs from prior language-driven scene synthesis methods (ATISS, DiffuScene) that model object joint distributions directly and express spatial relations only implicitly through attention. Instead, it introduces an explicit semantic graph as an intermediate latent, learned via discrete mask-based diffusion (building on D3PM's absorbing-state formulation) for the graph prior and continuous DDPM for the layout decoder. The two-stage decomposition—discrete graph generation followed by continuous layout decoding—differs from both the autoregressive sequential generation of ATISS and the single-stage Gaussian diffusion of DiffuScene, and the fVQ-VAE quantization of OpenShape features into discrete codes is a novel application of vector quantization to 3D scene semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15–25% iRecall improvement over SOTA and the zero-shot versatility across stylization, re-arrangement, completion, and unconditional generation (without fine-tuning) make this a practically useful framework for controllable interior design. The curated scene-instruction pair dataset addresses a real gap in the field, as 3D-FRONT previously lacked any textual descriptions. Key limitations include the small dataset scale (hundreds of scenes), reliance on object retrieval rather than generation, and the CLIP text encoder's difficulty with complex or abstract instructions—open questions that the authors identify for future work, including LLM integration and scaling to outdoor scenes.
