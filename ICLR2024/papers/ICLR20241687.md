## Problem

ML-augmented caching and MTS algorithms typically assume unlimited access to predictions, but generating predictions is computationally expensive. While Im et al. (2022) initiated the study of parsimonious algorithms for caching with next-arrival-time predictions, extending such results to the more general action-prediction setting and to MTS remained open. This paper designs algorithms that use predictions sparingly—either bounded in total count or well-separated in time—while maintaining strong consistency and smoothness guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives an online request sequence (caching) or cost functions (MTS) together with action predictions that specify the state of an offline algorithm (Belady for caching) at query times. For caching, each prediction is succinct: it reports only the indices of pages present in the algorithm's cache but absent from the predicted cache, requiring O(log k) bits per query. Predictions are constrained either by a total budget of O(f(log k))·OPT queries or by a minimum separation of a time steps between consecutive queries. The output is a sequence of cache eviction decisions or MTS state transitions.

## Method

The caching algorithm F&R alternates between a Follower phase (1-consistent, copying Belady's behavior via the predictor) and a Robust phase (one marking phase with randomized eviction of unmarked pages). The key mechanism is distributing predictor queries across logarithmically-sized windows within each Robust phase: window i has length k/2^i, and a convex function f controls how many predictions are requested per window, yielding O(f(log k))·OPT total queries. Synchronization with the marking cache at window boundaries prevents cache divergence from accumulating over time. For general MTS, the algorithm uses work functions to locate a suitable state near each prediction and interpolates between well-separated predictions following Emek et al. (2009), achieving O(a)·(OFF + 2η) cost. The smoothness bound O(f^{-1}(η/OPT)) is sublinear in prediction error for fast-growing f (e.g., f(i)=2^i−1 gives logarithmic smoothness with only O(√k)·OPT predictions).

## Results

On the CitiBike dataset (k=100), F&R with a=1 separation achieves competitive ratios of 1.800 (POPU predictor) and 1.878 (PLECO predictor), compared to Marker's 1.861 in both cases. With the PLECO predictor, F&R substantially outperforms FtP (1.878 vs. 2.277) and matches AQ_b8 (1.875). A key finding is that F&R makes the smallest number of predictor queries—exactly OPT with perfect predictions—and maintains near-identical performance even with a=20 separation (1.803 vs. 1.800 for a=1 with POPU), demonstrating that parsimonious prediction use incurs negligible performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Marker (Fiat et al., 1991), F&R wins with the POPU predictor (1.800 vs. 1.861) but is slightly worse with PLECO (1.878 vs. 1.861), indicating that with imprecise predictions the algorithm's advantage over classical baselines diminishes. Against FtP (Antoniadis et al., 2023), F&R loses with POPU (1.800 vs. 1.739) since FtP blindly follows predictions and is also 1-consistent, but F&R dramatically outperforms FtP with PLECO (1.878 vs. 2.277), showing F&R's robustness to poor predictions. Against AQ_b8 (Im et al., 2022), F&R is marginally worse with POPU (1.800 vs. 1.782) and essentially tied with PLECO (1.878 vs. 1.875), while using fewer predictor queries. Against FtPM (the non-parsimonious version of AQ with b=k), F&R is slightly worse with POPU (1.800 vs. 1.776) but comparable with PLECO (1.878 vs. 1.877). The paper also compares against L&V (Lykouris and Vassilvitskii, 2021) and LMark/LnonMark (Rohatgi, 2020) on the BrightKite dataset, where F&R with high-quality predictions is reported as superior to these next-arrival-time-based algorithms except for FtP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent threads: Im et al. (2022) initiated the study of parsimonious learning-augmented caching with next-arrival-time predictions, while Antoniadis et al. (2023) introduced action predictions for general MTS with a 1-consistent, (1+4η/OPT)-smooth algorithm. The authors bridge these by designing the F&R (Follower-Robust) algorithm that uses action predictions parsimoniously, achieving 1-consistency with O(f(log k)) OPT predictions and extending the analysis to general MTS via work functions. The robustness mechanism borrows from Blum and Burch (2000), and the well-separated-queries regime leverages the advice-complexity framework of Emek et al. (2009).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that heavy-weight predictors (e.g., the imitation-learning model of Liu et al., 2020) become feasible in resource-constrained settings, since the algorithm needs only O(√k) OPT predictions to match the smoothness of unlimited-prediction algorithms. A notable open question is whether sublinear-in-η/OPT smoothness can be achieved for MTS problems beyond caching, even with unlimited predictions. The lower bound showing that 0.5 OPT predictions cannot beat O(log k)-competitiveness delineates a fundamental limit on how far parsimony can push performance.
