## Problem

Existing label-only membership inference attacks, particularly the boundary attack, require over 1,000 queries per target sample to estimate its distance from the victim model's decision boundary. This high query budget makes the attack costly on MLaaS platforms, easily detectable via statistical monitoring, and vulnerable to defenses like LDL that create invariant hyper-spheres around samples. No prior label-only MIA achieves comparable inference accuracy with a single query to the victim model.

## Input / Output

The adversary takes a target sample (32×32 RGB image or tabular row), its ground-truth label, and a set of pre-trained shadow models (16 in-out pairs for online, 16 out-models for offline) as input. The method produces a single crafted query sample x' (same dimensionality as the target) that is sent to the victim model exactly once; the victim's returned hard label is compared against the ground-truth label to yield the binary membership decision. Unlike boundary attacks that iteratively probe the victim, YOQO performs all optimization locally on shadow models before the single remote query.

## Method

YOQO introduces the concept of an 'improvement area'—a local region around the target sample where the decision boundary shifts when the sample is added to training—so that any point in this area, when queried, reveals membership via its predicted label. The adversary crafts a query sample x' satisfying two properties: specificity (all out-models predict a label different from the ground truth) and sensitivity (all in-models predict the correct label). The online attack optimizes x' via SGD using a combined cross-entropy loss over N in-models and N out-models (Eq. 4), with a hyperparameter α balancing the two terms and a nearest-class selection strategy for the specificity target. The offline attack eliminates the costly in-model training by replacing the sensitivity term with an MSE regularization toward the original sample (Eq. 5), trading some accuracy for efficiency. Both variants require exactly one query to the victim model, reducing the budget from 20,000+ (boundary attack) to 1.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with CNN7 (2,500 training samples), YOQO Online achieves 81.65% membership inference accuracy, matching the Boundary Attack (l₂) at 81.38% while requiring only 1 query instead of 20,000+. YOQO Offline reaches 79.19%, still substantially outperforming the Gap Attack (67.85%). Across diverse architectures (VGG16, ResNet18, DenseNet121, InceptionV3, SeResNet18), YOQO Online maintains 75–82% accuracy, consistently exceeding the Gap Attack by 8–15 percentage points and matching or exceeding the Boundary Attack in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YOQO Online (81.65%) and Offline (79.19%) both substantially outperform the Gap Attack (Yeom et al., 2018) at 67.85% on CIFAR-10/CNN7, with margins of ~14 and ~11 percentage points respectively; this gap persists across all tested architectures. Against the Boundary Attack (Choquette-Choo et al., 2021; Li & Zhang, 2021), YOQO Online matches or slightly exceeds the l₂ variant (81.65% vs 81.38%) on CNN7 while requiring only 1 query versus 20,000+; on ResNet18 the Boundary Attack (l₂) edges ahead (83.88% vs 78.50%), but YOQO is more consistent across architectures. In the appendix comparison with Canary (Wen et al., 2022), YOQO Online achieves 81.65% versus Canary Online's 73.27%, and YOQO Offline reaches 79.19% versus Canary Offline's 71.54%, confirming that the improvement-area formulation is more effective in the label-only setting. YOQO also demonstrates superior robustness: under the LDL defense, the Boundary and Gap attacks degrade significantly while YOQO's accuracy remains high and stable.

## Relation to Prior Work

YOQO carves new ground within the label-only membership inference attack (MIA) literature by introducing the 'improvement area' concept, which reframes the membership question as finding a single crafted query whose hard label reveals whether the target sample was in training. It departs from the boundary attack (Choquette-Choo et al., 2021; Li & Zhang, 2021), which iteratively estimates distance to the decision boundary over 1000+ queries, and from the gap attack (Yeom et al., 2018), which uses one query but relies on overfitting heuristics. The offline variant draws on counterfactual explanation ideas (Elliott et al., 2021), while the overall adversarial-optimization framework for query crafting is conceptually adjacent to Wen et al. (2022), though adapted to the label-only setting where posteriors are unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reducing the query budget from over 1,000 to exactly one makes label-only MIA far more practical on MLaaS platforms that charge per query and far harder to detect via statistical anomaly monitoring, closing a significant gap in the attack-defense arms race. The demonstrated robustness against LDL—a defense specifically designed to neutralize label-only attacks—suggests that distance-based defenses may be insufficient, prompting the field to reconsider what properties a label-only defense must protect. Limitations include degraded performance on low-dimensional tabular data and the computational cost of training shadow in/out model pairs for the online variant; open questions include whether the improvement area concept extends to other privacy threats (e.g., model extraction) and whether it holds under stronger regularized or federated training regimes.
