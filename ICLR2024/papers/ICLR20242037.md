## Problem

Existing text-to-video editing methods inflate 2D spatial attention into dense spatio-temporal attention, causing every patch to attend to all patches across all frames. This introduces irrelevant cross-frame information that degrades visual consistency, producing flickering and temporal artifacts in the edited video. No prior work had incorporated motion-aware structure into the attention mechanism to selectively connect only temporally corresponding patches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242037/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB video (rescaled to 512×512, 32 frames) and a free-form text editing prompt, and produces a visually consistent edited video. Notably, no caption of the source video is required: DDIM inversion is performed with an empty text embedding. Optical flow is pre-computed with RAFT and downsampled to the latent space resolution (64×64) before trajectory sampling. The pipeline uses 100 DDIM inversion steps and 50 sampling steps, with feature injection from the inversion pass to preserve per-frame structure.

## Method

FLATTEN introduces optical flow into the U-Net attention blocks of a pre-trained latent diffusion model without adding any trainable parameters. RAFT estimates per-frame-pair displacement fields, which are downsampled to latent resolution and used to sample patch trajectories across all frames; each patch is uniquely assigned to one trajectory, with occlusion conflicts resolved by randomly continuing one trajectory. During the forward pass, dense spatio-temporal attention is performed first, and its output H is used directly (without new linear projections) as the input to flow-guided attention: for each query patch, the corresponding keys and values are gathered only from patches on the same trajectory, enabling targeted cross-frame information exchange. The inflated U-Net (3×3→1×3×3 kernels, spatial→dense spatio-temporal attention) is frozen, and FLATTEN is activated in both DDIM inversion and sampling. Feature injection from PNP-Diffusion preserves source structure, and the entire framework is training-free and plug-and-play compatible with other diffusion-based T2V editing methods.

## Results

On TGVE-D, FLATTEN achieves the best CLIP-T (28.05), PickScore (20.95), and editing score S_edit (57.01) among all compared methods, with a flow warping error E_warp of 4.92. On TGVE-V, FLATTEN attains the highest CLIP-T (26.70) and PickScore (20.63), with E_warp of 3.16 (nearly matching TokenFlow's 3.15). The user study confirms these gains, with FLATTEN receiving 41.12% preference for visual consistency and 41.59% for motion preservation, substantially ahead of the next-best method (TokenFlow at 26.74% and 24.30% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLATTEN outperforms all five baselines on the primary editing score S_edit on TGVE-D (57.01 vs. TokenFlow 50.21, FateZero 46.74, ControlVideo 40.70, Text2Video-Zero 12.62, Tune-A-Video 9.35). Against the closest competitor TokenFlow, FLATTEN achieves a significantly higher CLIP-T (28.05 vs. 26.91) while maintaining a comparable E_warp (4.92 vs. 5.36 on TGVE-D; 3.16 vs. 3.15 on TGVE-V), indicating better text alignment without sacrificing temporal consistency. Compared to ControlVideo, FLATTEN reduces E_warp by 27.6% (4.92 vs. 6.81) and improves S_edit by 40% (57.01 vs. 40.70), demonstrating that flow-guided attention is more effective than ControlNet-based structural conditioning for consistency. Against Tune-A-Video, which requires per-video fine-tuning, FLATTEN is training-free and reduces E_warp by over 83% (4.92 vs. 29.23). The plug-and-play experiment further shows that adding FLATTEN to ControlVideo reduces its E_warp from 6.81 to 4.78 and boosts S_edit from 40.70 to 56.42, confirming the method's generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLATTEN builds directly on the U-Net inflation paradigm established by Tune-A-Video and Video Diffusion Models, which convert 2D convolutions to 3D and spatial attention to dense spatio-temporal attention for video editing. It departs from this lineage by identifying that dense spatio-temporal attention introduces irrelevant cross-patch information, and instead uses pre-computed optical flow (via RAFT) to restrict attention to patches on the same trajectory. The editing pipeline itself draws on DDIM inversion and PnP-Diffusion feature injection from the T2I editing literature, while the flow-guided attention mechanism is a novel architectural insertion that requires no new parameters. Compared to TokenFlow, which enforces fixed linear combinations between diffusion features based on correspondences, FLATTEN replaces these rigid weights with a learned-free attention mechanism guided by flow trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of FLATTEN is its most practically significant contribution: it can be dropped into any existing diffusion-based T2V editing pipeline (demonstrated with ControlVideo, where E_warp drops from 6.81 to 4.78) without retraining, making it an immediately adoptable consistency module. The paper acknowledges a clear limitation—flow-guided attention excels at style, texture, and color editing but struggles with dramatic structural changes (e.g., sharks to drones), since the flow trajectories still encode the source geometry. Open questions include whether better flow estimators or learned trajectory models could close this gap, and whether the approach generalizes to longer videos or higher resolutions where flow estimation becomes less reliable.
