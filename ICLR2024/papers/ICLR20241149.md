## Problem

Existing mix-and-separate methods for conditional audio separation never encounter single-source conditioning signals during training, creating a distribution shift that severely degrades performance—especially as training mixture complexity grows. The paper asks how to generate supervision for single-source audio extraction without access to single-source audio samples, by exploiting the fact that single-source entities are trivially separable in the language modality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241149/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an audio mixture (converted to a magnitude spectrogram via STFT with 1024 window, 256 hop) and a natural-language prompt describing the target source (e.g., 'the sound of violin'), and outputs a soft binary mask applied to the mixture's magnitude spectrogram with the original phase copied through. Text prompts are extracted from mixture captions using NER and prompt engineering, and the frozen CLAP language encoder produces a 77×768 token embedding used as the conditioning signal at multiple U-Net resolutions via cross-attention.

## Method

The core mechanism is a bi-modal contrastive loss that computes cosine similarity (via frozen CLAP audio and language encoders) between the model's predicted single-source audio and all text prompts in the batch, forming a softmax-based discriminative signal that pushes predictions toward their corresponding text embedding. This weak supervision is combined with an unsupervised reconstruction loss on mixture-of-mixtures (MoM) and a consistency reconstruction loss enforcing that predicted single sources sum back to the original mixture. The separation model is a conditional U-Net with multi-scale cross-attention modules (8 heads, 512 channels) that inject the CLAP text embedding at every decoder scale, operating exclusively on magnitude spectrograms with a masking inductive bias. The framework requires no single-source audio during training and supports both purely unsupervised and semi-supervised regimes, where the latter adds a standard reconstruction loss on a small fraction of labeled single-source samples.

## Results

On the MUSIC dataset (2-source separation test), the proposed framework with bi-modal CLAP achieves 7.9±0.35 SDR under unsupervised training with 2-source mixtures, reaching 97.5% of the supervised upper bound (8.1±0.31). The advantage grows with training complexity: 7.1±0.42 SDR on 3-source and 6.2±0.38 SDR on 4-source training, representing 71%, 102%, and 129% SDR boosts over the best mix-and-separate baseline (CLIPSep-Text*). In the semi-supervised setting (5% single-source + 95% multi-source), the method achieves 8.8±0.28 SDR, surpassing the fully supervised baseline (8.1±0.31) by +0.7 SDR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (w/Bi-modal CLAP) decisively outperforms all baselines across every training complexity. Against the strongest language-conditional baseline CLIPSep-Text* (5.4±0.41 SDR on 2-source), it achieves +2.5 SDR; against the best image-conditional method CoSep* (4.9±0.37), it gains +3.0 SDR; and against the best unconditional method MixPIT (3.6±0.46), it gains +4.3 SDR. The gap widens dramatically as training mixtures become more complex: on 4-source training, the proposed method (6.2) leads CLIPSep-Text* (3.8) by +2.4 SDR and MixPIT (1.7) by +4.5 SDR. The classification-based weak supervision baseline (Weak-Sup, 3.1±0.47) underperforms even the unconditional MixIT, confirming the paper's argument that fixed-class classifiers are inferior to open-ended bi-modal embedding supervision. In the semi-supervised setting, the method with only 5% supervised data (8.8 SDR) beats the fully supervised baseline (8.1 SDR) by +0.7 SDR, and with 100% single-source data plus 100% multi-source data from VGGSound, it reaches 9.9 SDR — a +1.8 SDR improvement over supervised-only training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mix-and-separate framework (Zhao et al., 2018) for conditional audio separation by introducing a CLAP-based bi-modal contrastive loss that generates weak supervision for single-source prompts during training, addressing the distribution shift that plagues all prior mix-and-separate variants (SOP, CoSep, CLIPSep). It departs from classification-based weak supervision approaches (Pishdadian et al., 2020; Tzinis et al., 2020) by leveraging the open-ended, zero-shot nature of pretrained audio-language embeddings rather than a fixed set of classes. Architecturally, it replaces the post-conditioning or simple concatenation used in prior conditional U-Nets with multi-scale cross-attention modules that inject language features at multiple resolutions throughout the network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework achieves 97.5% of supervised performance in a purely unsupervised setting and, more strikingly, a semi-supervised variant using only 5% single-source data outperforms fully supervised training by 0.7–0.8 SDR, suggesting that cross-modal weak supervision acts as a powerful regularizer. The modality-agnostic formulation (any target modality with a separable conditioning modality plus a joint embedding model) opens the door to applications beyond audio, such as unsupervised image segmentation. Limitations include dependence on CLAP's representation quality, the need for text descriptions of mixtures, and the coarse nature of the weak signal that necessitates the reconstruction losses. Open questions include whether the approach scales to highly complex real-world scenes with many overlapping sources and whether the regularization benefit persists at larger model scales.
