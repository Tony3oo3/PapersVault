## Problem

Existing generalizable NeRF methods rely on image-based rendering, where features are projected from source views onto individually sampled 3D points. This paradigm suffers from three coupled failures: occlusions destroy stereo-matching consistency, per-point processing severs local shape connectivity causing distortions at geometric discontinuities, and the representation degrades severely when source views are far from the target view. No prior work has explored a point-based rendering paradigm for generalizable NeRF that explicitly handles visibility and local geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics as input. A PatchmatchMVS network first produces a sparse point cloud scaffold that serves as the geometric backbone; the number of points (typically 50K–200K) controls the trade-off between rendering quality and speed. The output is a neural point field — a point cloud with attached low-level appearance features, high-level geometry features, and per-point colors — which can be rendered to novel views via volume rendering, and also directly manipulated (moved, recolored, deformed) in 3D software like Blender without retraining.

## Method

GPF replaces image-based rendering with a point-based paradigm built on three key mechanisms. First, visibility-oriented feature fetching computes explicit physical visibility scores per point per view using a point-density-based depth estimation (emitting rays, computing local spatial density, and applying volumetric transmittance), then selects the top-k most visible source views and aggregates their UNet-extracted features through visibility-conditioned MLPs. Second, a robust density-guided log sampling strategy places 16 query points per ray in a symmetric non-uniform logarithmic spread around the estimated surface intersection, with random perturbations on the base and center to avoid local minima, eliminating the need for a separate coarse-to-fine stage. Third, a feature-augmented learnable kernel aggregates features from K=8 nearest scaffold points by first encoding spatial relationships (distances, camera-frame positions, relative coordinates) into initial weights, then concatenating those with the points' neural features to produce tuned weights and transformed feature vectors via an MLP, enabling robust generalization across drastically varying geometries. A three-stage hierarchical finetuning scheme (feature optimization, point completion/pruning, and iterative point position refinement with L2-regularized offsets) further improves per-scene quality without requiring source images.

## Results

Under the generalization setting (no per-scene training), GPF achieves PSNR of 29.31, 27.67, and 26.65 on NeRF Synthetic, DTU, and BlendedMVS respectively, outperforming all image-based baselines by 1.2–6.4 dB in PSNR. Under the finetuning setting, GPF reaches PSNR of 33.28, 31.65, and 28.78 on the same three datasets, with SSIM up to 0.983 and LPIPS as low as 0.037. The method also achieves the best depth reconstruction accuracy (Acc.T of 0.936 on DTU and 0.787 on NeRF Synthetic) and the fastest rendering speed (1.04 s per 800×800 image) among all compared sampling strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPF outperforms all four image-based generalizable NeRF baselines across every dataset and setting. Against the strongest image-based baseline Neuray (which implicitly models occlusions), GPF gains +2.24 dB PSNR on NeRF Synthetic and +3.41 dB on BlendedMVS under generalization, and +3.02 dB and +2.07 dB respectively under finetuning. IBRNet and ENeRF are consistently 2–5 dB behind GPF in PSNR, while MVSNeRF lags the most (up to 6.4 dB on BlendedMVS generalization), reflecting its sensitivity to occlusion-induced stereo mismatches. Among point-based methods, GPF surpasses PointNeRF by +2.57 dB (NeRF Synthetic) and +3.22 dB (DTU) in finetuning PSNR, and outperforms Point2Pix by +7.66 dB and +6.84 dB on the same datasets, demonstrating that the visibility-guided feature fetching and learnable kernel are critical for generalization beyond per-scene point rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant image-based rendering paradigm in generalizable NeRF (IBRNet, MVSNeRF, ENeRF, Neuray), where image features are projected onto uniformly sampled 3D points, and instead proposes a point-based rendering paradigm (GPF) that stores neural features on an explicit point scaffold initialized by PatchmatchMVS. It builds on NeRF's volume rendering equation for final synthesis and borrows the point-cloud initialization from PatchmatchMVS, but restructures the entire pipeline around visibility-guided feature fetching, density-guided log sampling, and a feature-augmented learnable kernel for aggregation. The work also connects to the point-based rendering line (PointNeRF, Point2Pix) but distinguishes itself by targeting cross-scene generalization rather than per-scene optimization, and by explicitly modeling occlusion through geometric visibility scores rather than learned occlusion modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from image-based to point-based rendering for generalizable NeRF addresses three concrete failure modes of prior methods (occlusion-induced stereo inconsistency, geometric discontinuity artifacts, and degradation when source views are distant), and the point-based representation enables interactive manipulation as a natural by-product. However, the reliance on PatchmatchMVS for point scaffold initialization limits end-to-end trainability and scalability to scenes where MVS struggles; the authors acknowledge this and plan a learned initialization module. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to dynamic scenes, whether the visibility scoring generalizes to transparent or reflective materials, and whether the log sampling strategy remains effective at much larger scene scales where point density becomes critical.
