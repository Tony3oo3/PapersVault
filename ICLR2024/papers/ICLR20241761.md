## Problem

As LLMs transition from narrow tools to lifelike conversational assistants, their psychological profiles—personality, motivations, emotional capacities—remain largely uncharacterized. Prior work has applied individual scales (mostly the BFI) to single models, but no comprehensive framework exists that systematically evaluates multiple psychological domains across a range of models and compares results to human norms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241761/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework feeds Likert-scale statements from 13 clinical psychology scales (ranging from 8 to 164 items per scale) to LLMs via structured system+user prompts that constrain responses to a single number per item, with explicit level definitions to reduce refusal. Output consists of mean ± SD subscale scores across four domains (personality traits, interpersonal relationships, motivational tests, emotional abilities), computed over 10 shuffled runs per scale, and benchmarked against crowd-sourced human response distributions from existing literature.

## Method

PsychoBench is a prompt-based evaluation framework that curates 13 validated psychometric scales into four categories and administers them to LLMs through a standardized prompt template that forces numeric-only Likert responses. Each scale is administered with shuffled item order across 10 independent runs, and statistical significance against human norms is assessed via F-test followed by Student's or Welch's t-test (α=0.01). A key differentiator is the use of CipherChat (Caesar cipher, shift 3) to jailbreak GPT-4's safety alignment, revealing intrinsic psychological tendencies that differ from the aligned model. Scale validity on LLMs is verified by assigning gpt-3.5-turbo distinct roles (psychopath, liar, ordinary person, hero) and confirming that resulting DTDD/EPQ-R scores correlate with downstream TruthfulQA accuracy and SafetyQA toxicity rates.

## Results

Across the 13 psychometric scales, LLMs consistently score higher than human norms on positive traits (e.g., BFI Openness: 4.2–4.8 vs. human 3.9; Conscientiousness: 3.9–4.7 vs. 3.5) and lower on negative traits (BFI Neuroticism: 1.5–2.7 vs. human 3.3). GPT-4 achieves the highest Emotional Intelligence score (EIS: 151.4 ± 18.7) exceeding 89% of human participants, while the jailbroken GPT-4 (gpt-4-jb) shifts toward more human-like profiles (e.g., BFI Openness drops from 4.2 to 3.8, DTDD Psychopathy rises from 1.2 to 4.7). LLaMA-2-13B outperforms LLaMA-2-7B on Conscientiousness (4.4 vs. 3.9) and Neuroticism (1.9 vs. 2.7), while the 7B model scores higher on GSE (39.1 vs. 30.4) and LOT-R optimism (12.7 vs. 19.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing evaluation frameworks; instead, it benchmarks five LLMs (text-davinci-003, GPT-3.5, GPT-4, LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-2-13B) plus a jailbroken GPT-4 against human crowd norms. GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3.5 on Conscientiousness (4.7 vs. 4.3), Agreeableness (4.8 vs. 4.4), and all three DTDD subscales (lower is better: Narcissism 2.0 vs. 6.6, Machiavellianism 1.1 vs. 5.4, Psychopathy 1.2 vs. 4.0), while GPT-3.5 scores higher on EPQ-R Neuroticism (21.8 vs. 3.9). LLaMA-2-13B surpasses LLaMA-2-7B on BFI Conscientiousness (4.4 vs. 3.9) and Neuroticism (1.9 vs. 2.7), but the 7B model leads on GSE self-efficacy (39.1 vs. 30.4) and LOT-R optimism (12.7 vs. 19.9). The jailbreak (CipherChat with Caesar cipher shift-3) shifts GPT-4 toward human-like scores: BFI Openness drops from 4.2 to 3.8 (human: 3.9), DTDD Psychopathy rises from 1.2 to 4.7 (human: 2.5), and EIS drops from 151.4 to 121.8 (human: ~125–131), suggesting safety alignment inflates positive self-presentation. All LLMs score substantially above human norms on emotional intelligence (EIS: 128–151 vs. 125–131) and below on ICB implicit culture belief (1.9–3.6 vs. 3.7), reflecting their safety-aligned, culturally neutral training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper consolidates a fragmented line of work on psychometric evaluation of LLMs—where prior studies (Miotto et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2022; Li et al., 2022; Karra et al., 2022; Bodroza et al., 2023; Safdari et al., 2023) each tested one or two scales (typically BFI)—into a unified framework spanning 13 scales across four domains. It also extends the scope beyond personality traits to include interpersonal relationships, motivational tests, and emotional abilities, drawing on scales (BSRI, CABIN, ECR-R, GSE, LOT-R, LMS, EIS, WLEIS, Empathy) that had not previously been applied to LLMs in a single study. The jailbreak component repurposes the CipherChat method (Yuan et al., 2024) from a safety-evaluation context to probe whether safety alignment distorts a model's intrinsic psychological profile.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PsychoBench provides the first comprehensive, multi-domain psychometric benchmark for LLMs, giving researchers a standardized tool to compare models' psychological profiles and track how they shift across model updates, sizes, and alignment conditions. The finding that jailbroken GPT-4 exhibits a psychological profile closer to human norms than its aligned counterpart raises an open question about whether safety alignment systematically inflates certain traits (e.g., conscientiousness, agreeableness) and whether this 'super-human' persona is an artifact of RLHF rather than a model property. Key limitations include the reliance on Likert-scale self-report (which LLMs may answer in socially desirable ways), the generalizability of human reliability evidence to LLMs, and the fact that only five models were tested. Future work could extend the framework to more models, test temporal stability, and investigate whether psychological profiles predict downstream behavioral differences.
