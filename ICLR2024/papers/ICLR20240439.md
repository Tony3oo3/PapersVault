## Problem

Prior work (Haim et al., 2022) demonstrated that training data can be reconstructed from trained homogeneous networks, but provided no formal characterization of when the attack succeeds, which datapoints are vulnerable, or why. This paper addresses the gap by providing a stronger reconstruction attack with provable guarantees in the infinite-width NTK regime, identifying the conditions under which finite-width networks are susceptible, and revealing a formal connection between reconstruction and dataset distillation.

## Input / Output

The attack requires the final trained parameters θ_f, the initialization (or an earlier checkpoint) θ_0, and the network architecture, computing Δθ = θ_f − θ_0 as the target to explain. Unlike Haim et al., it does not require homogeneous networks, directional convergence, or special initialization schemes, and is compatible with early stopping and cross-entropy loss. When the number of reconstruction images M exceeds the training set size N, the output is a set of reconstructed images matched to training examples; when M < N, the output becomes a distilled dataset with labels y_R = K_RR α^R suitable for retraining.

## Method

The attack formulates reconstruction as matching the KKT stationarity condition of the minimum-norm solution under MSE loss in the NTK regime, yielding the loss L = ||Δθ − α^T ∇_θ f_{θ_0}(X_T)||² optimized over reconstruction images and dual parameters α. Theorem 1 proves exact recovery in infinite width by showing the loss converges to the MMD between kernel measure embeddings, which vanishes iff the reconstruction equals the training set (by NTK universality on the unit sphere). For finite networks, reconstruction quality is shown to correlate strongly with the kernel distance d(K_0, K_f) between initialization and final NTK, with wider models, smaller datasets, and linearized dynamics all reducing this distance and improving recovery. Theorem 2 further proves that the reconstruction loss decomposes into the KIP distillation loss under a K_TT^{-1}-weighted norm plus a non-negative variance term λ_var of R|T, yielding the RKIP algorithm that transfers to finite-width networks where standard KIP fails. Outlier datapoints with large |α| values (equivalently, slow-to-fit points) are shown to be preferentially reconstructed, and iteratively removing them reveals a 'privacy onion' of newly vulnerable points.

## Results

On the dataset distillation task (500→20 images), RKIP achieves 89.61% test accuracy on MNIST Odd/Even (standard dynamics) and 72.23% on CIFAR-10 Animal/Vehicle, closely matching the full-dataset accuracy of 92.85% and 75.06% respectively. In the infinite-width NTK regime, the reconstruction attack provably recovers the entire training set (Theorem 1), and empirically produces visually indistinguishable reconstructions for 4096-width two-layer networks. The key finding is that RKIP transfers to finite-width networks with minimal accuracy drop (89.99% linearized), while KIP suffers a catastrophic 36-point drop from 91.53% (infinite-width) to 55.62% (finite-width) on MNIST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Haim et al. (2022), this paper's attack is strictly stronger: it works with biased networks (Haim requires homogeneous/no-bias), uses standard initialization (Haim requires a carefully tuned small-variance first-layer init), is compatible with early stopping (Haim requires training to directional convergence), and achieves significantly better reconstruction quality on MNIST (better L2 distances up to image index 50 at comparable width 1024, per Figure 10). Against KIP (Nguyen et al., 2021), RKIP dramatically outperforms in finite-width transfer: on MNIST Odd/Even, RKIP achieves 89.99% (linearized) vs. KIP's 55.62%, a 34-point gap, while both achieve similar infinite-width accuracy (~91.5%). On CIFAR-10 Animal/Vehicle, RKIP reaches 72.76% vs. KIP's 32.37% in the linearized finite-width setting. The paper attributes this gap to the λ_var-of-R|T regularization term in RKIP, which keeps distilled images close to the training distribution as measured by the NTK, preventing the domain shift that causes KIP's finite-width failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset reconstruction attack of Haim et al. (2022) by replacing the logistic-loss/max-margin formulation with an MSE-loss/KKT formulation, yielding the first provable reconstruction guarantee in the infinite-width NTK regime. It draws on NTK theory (Jacot et al., 2018; Lee et al., 2019) to characterize when the attack succeeds for finite networks, and on the KIP dataset distillation algorithm (Nguyen et al., 2021a,b) to establish a formal equivalence between the reconstruction loss and a kernel-based distillation objective. The work also positions itself relative to gradient inversion attacks (Zhu et al., 2019), showing that gradient inversion is a special case of the proposed attack restricted to a single gradient step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal proof that the reconstruction loss equals the KIP distillation loss under a different norm plus a variance term (Theorem 2) reveals a previously unrecognized duality between privacy attacks and dataset compression, suggesting that defenses against one may inform the other. The finding that reconstruction quality is strongly governed by kernel distance from the NTK limit provides a concrete diagnostic for model vulnerability. Key limitations include the restriction to two-layer fully-connected networks on small datasets, the requirement for initialization access, and unclear transfer to deeper convolutional architectures or high-resolution images; the connection to differential privacy remains open.
