## Problem

Bayesian optimization struggles in the few-shot regime: surrogate models are hard to fit accurately with sparse observations, candidate samplers lack effective prior knowledge about hyperparameter correlations, and warmstarting typically requires expensive data collection from related problems. This paper asks whether LLMs—whose in-context learning performs implicit Bayesian inference over encoded domain priors—can enhance these specific BO components without any finetuning.

## Input / Output

All BO inputs are serialized into natural language: hyperparameter configurations become text descriptions (e.g., 'max depth is 15, min samples split is 0.5, accuracy is 0.9'), and the optimization history is concatenated as few-shot examples. The LLM is queried with a structured prompt containing a model card, data card, task instructions, and these serialized observations. Outputs are parsed from the LLM's text response: predicted scores with empirical uncertainty (from K=10 MC samples), candidate configurations as JSON dictionaries, or binary classification probabilities for the generative surrogate. The candidate sampler additionally accepts a continuous target value s′ computed from observed min/max scores and an exploration hyperparameter α.

## Method

LLAMBO integrates GPT-3.5 into three BO components via in-context learning, requiring no finetuning. For warmstarting, zero-shot prompting with varying context levels (none, partial, full) generates initial hyperparameters that encode learned inter-parameter correlations. For surrogate modeling, a discriminative approach serializes the optimization trajectory as few-shot regression examples and uses MC sampling with random shuffling of in-context examples to produce calibrated uncertainty estimates; a generative alternative reformulates TPE's density ratio l(h)/g(h) as the binary classification probability p(s≤τ|h) via Bayes' rule. For candidate sampling, the method extends TPE's binary good/bad threshold to continuous conditional generation: the LLM proposes M=20 candidates targeting a specific objective value s′=s_min−α(s_max−s_min), enabling extrapolation beyond observed distributions when α>0. The shuffling mechanism addresses LLM sensitivity to in-context example ordering, and ablations confirm that the problem description (model/data cards) provides critical prior knowledge that substantially improves few-shot performance.

## Results

On the Bayesmark public benchmark (25 HPT tasks, 5 seeds, 25 trials), LLAMBO achieves the best average rank across ML models, with particularly strong results on DecisionTree (rank 2.02) and RandomForest (rank 3.64). On private and synthetic datasets (30 tasks), LLAMBO again leads overall, achieving rank 2.08 on RandomForest and 3.35 on DecisionTree. The paper reports that LLAMBO's advantage is most pronounced in the early stages of search (n < 10 observations), where sample efficiency is most critical. In surrogate modeling experiments, LLAMBO consistently outperforms GP and SMAC on NRMSE and R² across all sample counts (n ∈ [5, 30]), with the largest gains at n = 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLAMBO achieves the best overall average rank on public Bayesmark, winning on DecisionTree (2.02 vs. next-best SKOpt at 3.66), RandomForest (3.64 vs. SKOpt at 4.90), and AdaBoost (3.62 vs. SKOpt at 5.12), while being competitive but not dominant on MLP (5.06 vs. SKOpt at 4.40) and SVM (4.82 vs. SKOpt at 4.84). On private and synthetic datasets, LLAMBO leads on RF (2.08) and DT (3.35) but is outperformed by SKOpt (GP) on SVM (2.32 vs. 4.88), suggesting model-dependent variability. GP-DKL is a strong competitor on DT (4.30) and MLP (4.63) but trails LLAMBO on RF and ADA. DNGO (Bayesian NN) is consistently the worst performer across all model types (ranks 6.6–9.1). In surrogate modeling, LLAMBO outperforms GP on prediction accuracy (NRMSE, R²) at all sample sizes but GP retains an edge in uncertainty calibration (LPD, empirical coverage). In candidate sampling, LLAMBO achieves the lowest average and best regret compared to TPE (Ind), TPE (Multi), and random sampling, with the largest gap at n = 5 observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLAMBO extends the Bayesian optimization pipeline by repurposing LLM in-context learning as a lightweight substitute for learned surrogates and samplers, building directly on TPE's density-ratio formulation (Bergstra et al., 2011) and the GP/SMAC surrogate modeling tradition. It departs from prior LLM-for-optimization work (prompt optimization, evolutionary operators, and the single-component LLM surrogate for molecular BO in [30]) by systematically integrating LLMs into three distinct BO components—warmstarting, surrogate modeling, and candidate sampling—without any finetuning. The approach also positions itself against transfer-learning-for-BO methods (multitask GPs, pretrained Transformers) by arguing that generalist LLMs already encode sufficient domain priors to serve as a zero-shot knowledge source, eliminating the need for dedicated pretraining on related optimization problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLMs can meaningfully enhance BO in the few-shot regime where traditional surrogates struggle, and that this works even on private/synthetic datasets the LLM has never seen, mitigating memorization concerns. The modular design is practically valuable: individual components (e.g., LLM warmstarting or LLM candidate sampling) can be dropped into existing BO frameworks like Optuna or SMAC without replacing the entire pipeline. Key open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional search spaces (NAS, robotic control), the significant computational overhead of LLM API calls versus local BO, sensitivity to the choice of LLM, and whether the few-shot advantage persists as observation counts grow. The reliance on GPT-3.5 (an older model) also leaves open how results shift with more capable or open-weight LLMs.
