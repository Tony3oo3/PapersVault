## Problem

Variational inference maximizes the ELBO via reverse KL divergence, which can yield a high ELBO while both the variational and learned posteriors remain far from the true posterior—especially for multi-modal or heavy-tailed posteriors. While importance sampling (IS) could directly estimate the marginal log-likelihood, there is no principled guidance on selecting the optimal proposal distribution, and prior attempts to minimize the forward χ² divergence suffer from numerical instability.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the joint distribution p(x,z;θ) and observed data x, without needing the explicit factorization p(x|z;θ)p(z;θ) that ELBO-based methods exploit for closed-form KL terms. It produces a down-biased but asymptotically tighter estimate of ln p(x;θ) via K Monte Carlo samples, along with learned parameters θ and a proposal distribution q(z|x;φ) optimized for IS. The approach is demonstrated on binary mixture models, 28×28 grayscale MNIST images (z∈ℝ²), and partially observable spike-train sequences (T×N count matrices).

## Method

VIS alternates two updates: (1) maximize the IS estimate ln p̂(x;θ,φ) = logsumexp[ln p(x,z⁽ᵏ⁾;θ) − ln q(z⁽ᵏ⁾|x;φ)] − ln K with respect to θ, and (2) minimize the forward χ² divergence χ²(p(z|x;θ)||q(z|x;φ)) with respect to φ. The key theoretical result is that the forward χ² divergence simultaneously governs both the bias (≈ −χ²/(2K)) and the variance (∝ χ²/K) of the IS estimator, making its minimization the statistically optimal proposal selection criterion. For numerical stability, the χ² minimization is performed in log space via ln V(x;θ,φ) ≈ logsumexp[2ln p(x,z⁽ᵏ⁾;θ) − 2ln q(z⁽ᵏ⁾|x;φ)] − ln K, with score-function and pathwise gradient estimators derived for the proposal update. As K→∞, the IS estimator converges to the true marginal log-likelihood, whereas ELBO remains offset by the full reverse KL gap.

## Results

Across all three model families (toy mixture model, VAE on MNIST, and POGLM on synthetic and RGC data), VIS consistently achieves the highest marginal log-likelihood (LL), complete log-likelihood (CLL), and hidden log-likelihood (HLL) compared to all baselines. On the synthetic POGLM, VIS also attains the smallest average weight estimation error, with the bias error tied for best with VBIS. On the real RGC dataset, VIS always obtains the highest test marginal log-likelihood across H∈{1,2,3} hidden neurons, and its learned weight matrix reveals a clear OFF/ON cell differentiation in the hidden neuron's connections that no other method captures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIS outperforms all four baselines on every metric (LL, CLL, HLL) across all experiments. Against standard VI, VIS wins decisively because VI's reverse-KL mode-seeking behavior causes the variational posterior to collapse multi-modal posteriors (e.g., merging two large bumps into one on the toy mixture model), leading to higher ELBO but lower true marginal log-likelihood. Against CHIVI, which jointly minimizes both forward χ² and reverse KL, VIS still wins because CHIVI optimizes ELBO (not the marginal log-likelihood) for θ updates, resulting in inferior parameter recovery—particularly for hidden-to-visible and visible-to-hidden weights in POGLM. Against VBIS, which reuses the VI-learned q as the IS proposal, VIS wins because the reverse-KL-optimal q is not the IS-optimal proposal; the forward-χ²-optimal q provides wider mass-covering support that better captures all posterior modes. Against IWAE (VAE experiments only), VIS achieves the highest test log-likelihood on MNIST, with IWAE being the second-best method, while VI, CHIVI, and VBIS cluster together at lower performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of variational inference (ELBO/reverse KL) and importance sampling for latent variable models, building directly on CHIVI (Dieng et al., 2017), VBIS (Su & Chen, 2021), and IWAE (Burda et al., 2015). Its key departure is showing that the optimal IS proposal is characterized by minimizing the forward χ² divergence rather than the reverse KL divergence, and providing a numerically stable log-space gradient estimator for this objective—addressing the instability issues flagged by Pradier et al. (2019) and Finke & Thiery (2019). Unlike prior work that typically assumes an explicit p(x,z)=p(x|z)p(z) factorization, VIS is formulated for the general case where no such decomposition exists, as demonstrated on the POGLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIS offers a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ELBO maximization that directly targets the marginal log-likelihood, with the forward χ² divergence providing both a bias bound and a variance (effectiveness) bound for the IS estimator. The practical limitation is the need for many Monte Carlo samples (K=500–5000 in experiments), making it computationally expensive for high-dimensional latent spaces; the authors acknowledge that the practical significance of the statistically optimal proposal in very large-scale settings requires further investigation. Open questions include whether adaptive or amortized strategies can reduce the MC sample requirement, and whether the approach extends naturally to amortized inference with neural proposal families in high-dimensional problems.
