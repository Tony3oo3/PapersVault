## Problem

Reid et al. (2022) showed that pre-training Decision Transformer with the Wikipedia corpus improves offline RL performance, suggesting language data provides unique benefits. However, it remained unclear whether this gain is specific to the rich semantic and long-range dependencies in natural language, or whether much simpler pre-training schemes could achieve comparable results. This paper asks whether synthetic data with no relation to the target environment can replicate or exceed the benefits of language pre-training.

## Input / Output

For DT, the method consumes synthetic sequences of discrete tokens drawn from a 100-state Markov Chain during pre-training (20K updates), then fine-tunes on standard D4RL offline trajectories (state, action, reward) with return-to-go conditioning for 100K updates, producing an action policy. For CQL, synthetic MDP data consists of state-action pairs where discrete states and actions are mapped to fixed random vectors matching the MuJoCo task dimensions; the Q-network MLPs are pre-trained via forward dynamics prediction (MSE on next-state prediction) for 100K updates before fine-tuning with CQL for 1M updates. Notably, the synthetic data is entirely environment-agnostic: transition matrices and policy distributions are random and share no structure with the target task.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pre-training-then-fine-tuning pipeline using synthetic data generated from trivial stochastic processes. For DT, a one-step Markov Chain with 100 states and a randomly initialized transition matrix (softmax of uniform random values) generates token sequences, which are fed into the transformer with autoregressive next-token prediction for only 20K updates—far fewer than the 80K used for Wikipedia pre-training. For CQL, a synthetic MDP with random policy and transition distributions generates state-action sequences, and the Q-network MLPs are pre-trained to predict the next state via MSE loss. A key theoretical insight explains why even IID data (no state dependencies) works nearly as well as structured MDP data: the forward-dynamics objective reduces to computing the centroid of next-state observations for each (s,a) pair, and CQL's fine-tuning performance is largely insensitive to the spatial distribution of these centroids. Extensive ablations confirm robustness across MC order, state-space size, temperature, and number of pre-training updates, with the one-step MC at 100 states and τ=1 consistently performing best.

## Results

On D4RL Gym locomotion datasets (12 environments), DT pre-trained with synthetic Markov Chain data (DT+Synthetic) achieves an average normalized score of 73.6±4.9, outperforming both the DT baseline (67.7±5.4) and DT pre-trained with Wikipedia (DT+Wiki, 69.9±5.6). For CQL, synthetic MDP pre-training (CQL+MDP) achieves 84.9±3.7 average score versus 78.0±4.7 for the CQL baseline, with gains up to 84% on halfcheetah-medium-expert. Notably, DT+Synthetic requires only 20K pre-training updates (vs. 80K for DT+Wiki) and uses just 3% of the pre-training computation, while CQL+MDP pre-training takes only ~4 minutes on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DT baseline (Chen et al., 2021), DT+Synthetic wins on 10 out of 12 datasets, improving the average score from 67.7 to 73.6 (+8.7%). Against DT+Wiki (Reid et al., 2022), DT+Synthetic achieves a higher average (73.6 vs. 69.9, +5.3%) while using only 20K pre-training updates versus 80K for Wikipedia, and 3% of the pre-training computation time. For CQL (Kumar et al., 2020), CQL+MDP with 100-state synthetic data improves the average from 78.0 to 84.9 (+8.8%), with the largest gains on halfcheetah-medium-expert (35.9→63.1, +75.8%) and hopper-medium-expert (59.3→90.2, +52.1%). The paper also shows that even IID synthetic data (no Markov structure) matches or nearly matches the MDP synthetic data performance, suggesting the benefit comes from the pre-training process itself rather than from learning specific transition dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Reid et al. (2022), who showed that pre-training Decision Transformer on Wikipedia improves downstream offline RL performance, and Takagi (2022), who explored why language pre-training helps DT. It departs from this line by demonstrating that the language corpus is not special: synthetic data from a 100-state Markov chain with random transitions, or even IID samples, can match or exceed Wikipedia pre-training. The paper further extends the pre-training question beyond transformer-based DT to the MLP-based CQL algorithm, using forward dynamics prediction as the pre-training objective, connecting to the auxiliary-loss and model-based RL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: practitioners can replace hours of expensive Wikipedia pre-training with minutes of synthetic data generation (3% compute for DT, ~5 minutes for CQL) while achieving equal or better performance. However, the mechanism remains unexplained—the authors suspect pre-training smooths the optimization landscape during fine-tuning but provide only a partial theoretical account (centroid-finding equivalence for the forward-dynamics loss). The scope is limited to D4RL locomotion tasks and two specific algorithms (DT and CQL), leaving open whether the finding generalizes to other environments, algorithm families, or online RL settings. Understanding why entirely unrelated synthetic data helps is an important open question that could inform broader pre-training strategies in RL.
