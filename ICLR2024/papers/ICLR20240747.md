## Problem

Existing transformer-based multivariate time series forecasting methods produce point estimates of the conditional mean but neglect the full uncertainty distribution of future values, which is critical for risk-aware decision-making. Prior diffusion-based time series models inject conditional information only into the reverse denoising process, underutilizing the strong mean-estimation capability of well-designed transformers. This paper asks how to leverage transformer representations as priors across both forward and reverse diffusion processes to recover the complete conditional distribution of future time series.

## Input / Output

The method takes a historical multivariate time series x_{0:N} ∈ R^{d×N} (input length 96, or 36 for ILI) and produces a full probability distribution over the future window y_{0:M} ∈ R^{d×M} (prediction length 192, or 36 for ILI), approximated by 100 samples drawn from the learned diffusion process. From these samples, the model yields quantile-based prediction intervals (50% and 90%), PICP at the 2.5th/97.5th percentiles, and per-dimension CRPS scores. The framework is plug-and-play: swapping the internal transformer (NSformer, Autoformer, Informer) changes the conditional prior without modifying the diffusion component.

## Method

TMDM unifies a transformer-based condition generative model with a conditional diffusion model under a single Bayesian ELBO objective. A well-designed transformer T(·) encodes the historical series into a latent variable z, which generates a conditional representation ŷ_{0:M} that serves as the prior mean for the diffusion endpoint p(y^T | ŷ) = N(ŷ, I). Crucially, the forward process is modified to interpolate between the true data y^0 and ŷ (rather than pure noise), and the reverse posterior incorporates ŷ as an additional term in the mean, so the denoiser focuses on uncertainty rather than re-estimating the mean. Training uses a hybrid optimization that jointly optimizes the diffusion denoising loss and the conditional generation loss end-to-end via stochastic gradient descent. The denoising network is a lightweight MLP that concatenates the noisy sample with ŷ and the timestep embedding, predicting the forward diffusion noise.

## Results

TMDM achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six datasets on both probabilistic (QICE, CRPS, PICP) and point-estimation (MSE, MAE) metrics. On QICE, TMDM reduces the previous best by 17% on Exchange (4.38 vs. 5.32), 23% on ETTm2 (3.75 vs. 4.88), 27% on Electricity (3.81 vs. 5.26), 32% on Traffic (2.36 vs. 3.50), and 24% on Weather (3.87 vs. 5.14). On CRPS, TMDM achieves 0.32 (Exchange), 0.37 (ETTm2), 0.33 (Electricity), 0.26 (Traffic), and 0.36 (Weather), outperforming all baselines. For point estimation, TMDM also leads on MSE (e.g., 0.26 on Exchange vs. 0.48 for TimeDiff) and MAE (e.g., 0.25 on Weather vs. 0.37 for TimeDiff).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TMDM consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitors, TMDM reduces QICE by 17% over TimeGrad (4.38 vs. 5.32 on Exchange) and by 19% over SSSD (4.38 vs. 6.20 on Exchange), while also achieving lower CRPS (0.32 vs. 0.66 and 0.56 respectively). Point-to-point forecasting models D³VAE and TimeDiff, though competitive on CRPS (0.38–0.41 on Exchange), perform poorly on distributional metrics (QICE 13.34–13.51 vs. TMDM's 4.38), confirming that CRPS alone is insufficient for evaluating probabilistic forecasting quality. Among transformer-based baselines, Transformer-MAF is the closest on QICE (6.85 on Exchange) but still trails TMDM by 36%. On PICP (target 95%), TMDM achieves 74.54% on Exchange and 87.83% on ILI, substantially outperforming SSSD (71.12% and 79.57%) and CSDI (69.21% and 76.18%), while D³VAE and TimeDiff collapse to 21.38% and 20.80% on Exchange, indicating severely miscalibrated uncertainty. On MSE/MAE, TMDM also leads all baselines (e.g., 0.26/0.37 on Exchange vs. 0.48/0.43 for TimeDiff), demonstrating that the probabilistic framework does not sacrifice point-estimation accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

TMDM extends the conditional diffusion framework for time series (TimeGrad, CSDI, SSSD, TimeDiff) by injecting transformer-derived conditional information into both the forward and reverse diffusion processes, rather than only the reverse process as in prior work. It draws the conditional prior formulation (p(y^T | ŷ) = N(ŷ, I)) from CARD (Han et al., 2022) and repurposes well-designed point-estimation transformers (NSformer, Autoformer, Informer) as the condition generator, treating their output as a learned prior for the conditional mean. The hybrid ELBO objective that jointly optimizes the denoising network and the condition generative model is a departure from prior approaches that train these components separately or use fixed conditional embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means that any future improvement in transformer-based point forecasting can be directly transferred to probabilistic forecasting without redesigning the diffusion component, lowering the barrier for practitioners. The introduction of PICP and QICE as evaluation metrics addresses a real gap: CRPS alone can mask poor distributional calibration, as evidenced by D3VAE and TimeDiff achieving competitive CRPS while their PICP scores collapse to ~20% versus TMDM's ~75%. Open questions include whether the 1000-step diffusion schedule is necessary (faster samplers like DDIM or consistency models could reduce inference cost), whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional series beyond 862 variables, and whether the hybrid optimization's 16% QICE gain over separate training holds under different transformer backbones or dataset regimes.
