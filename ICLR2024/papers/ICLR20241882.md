## Problem

Despite LLMs' growing potential in biomolecular research spanning structural biology, computational chemistry, and drug development, no dedicated large-scale instruction dataset exists for this domain. Existing biomolecular datasets (e.g., Galactica, PCdes, MolT5) are primarily designed for pre-training smaller models and lack the instructional format needed for LLM fine-tuning, while general-domain instruction datasets contain no biomolecular content. The gap is compounded by the high cost of biomolecular annotation, the interdisciplinary knowledge required, and the absence of a standardized biomolecular representation.

## Input / Output

Molecular inputs use SELFIES strings rather than SMILES to guarantee syntactic and chemical validity of generated outputs, while protein inputs are amino acid sequences sourced from UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot and TrEMBL after MMseqs-based deduplication at 90% sequence identity. A distinctive input type is the protein design requirement, constructed by aggregating 20 UniProt annotation features (function, domain, GO terms, catalytic activity, etc.) into natural-language specifications via templated conversion. Outputs range from discrete values (molecular property numbers, GO terms, EC-number reactions) to free-form text (functional descriptions, Q&A answers) and structured sequences (SELFIES strings, amino acid sequences, interaction triplets).

## Method

Mol-Instructions is constructed through a four-sector pipeline: (1) human-AI collaboration where human-written task descriptions are expanded into diverse instruction phrasings via GPT-3.5-turbo with manual review; (2) information derivation from licensed databases (PubChem, USPTO, UniProtKB, MoleculeNet/QM9, BC4CHEMD, ChemProt, BC5CDR, MedMCQA, MMLU, PubMedQA) using both direct label mapping and AI-assisted generation (e.g., GPT-3.5-turbo formulating Q&A pairs from PubMed abstracts); (3) template-based conversion of structured protein annotations into textual design specifications, with random subset selection of conditions to manage input length; and (4) quality control via SELFIES validation for molecules and MMseqs clustering for proteins. The resulting 2,043,587 instructions span 17 subtasks across molecule-oriented (148.4K), protein-oriented (505K), and biomolecular text (53K) categories. For evaluation, LLaMA-7B is instruction-tuned with Alpaca-LoRA on each category and benchmarked against Alpaca, Baize, ChatGLM, Vicuna, Galactica, Text+Chem T5, MolT5, and PMC-LLaMA-13B.

## Results

On molecular property prediction (QM9), Mol-Instructions-tuned LLaMA achieves an MAE of 0.555, outperforming Galactica (0.568) and dramatically surpassing general LLMs (Alpaca: 322.1, LLaMA base: 5.553). Across all four molecular generation tasks (description-guided design, reagent prediction, forward reaction prediction, retrosynthesis), the tuned model achieves 100% molecular validity using SELFIES, compared to 72–77% for specialized T5-based models and <1% for general LLMs. On description-guided molecule design, the model reaches BLEU 0.345 and MACCS FTS 0.412, trailing MolT5 (BLEU 0.546) but matching or exceeding it on validity. For reagent prediction, the model achieves the best Levenshtein distance (23.167) and RDKit FTS (0.237) among all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mol-Instructions-tuned LLaMA decisively outperforms all five general-purpose LLM baselines (Alpaca, Baize, ChatGLM, LLaMA base, Vicuna) across every molecular generation metric, with validity jumping from <20% to 100% and BLEU improving by 1–2 orders of magnitude. Against Galactica, the domain-specific scientific LLM, the tuned model achieves 100% validity (vs. 94.6–99.5%) and superior Levenshtein distance on reagent prediction (23.17 vs. 30.76) and retrosynthesis (31.23 vs. 34.94), though Galactica retains a BLEU edge on forward reaction prediction (0.468 vs. 0.654 — actually Ours wins here too). Compared to the specialized smaller models Text+Chem T5 and MolT5, the tuned LLaMA achieves 100% validity versus 72–77%, but trails on BLEU and fingerprint similarity (e.g., description-guided design: BLEU 0.345 vs. 0.546 for MolT5; forward reaction: BLEU 0.654 vs. 0.782 for Text+Chem T5). The authors attribute this gap to LLMs being designed for broad task coverage at the expense of specialized performance, while noting that the 100% validity guarantee from SELFIES is a practical advantage for downstream chemical workflows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-tuning paradigm established by Alpaca, Dolly, and InstructGPT from general NLP to the biomolecular domain, where no comparable large-scale instruction dataset previously existed. Prior biomolecular resources (Galactica, PCdes, ChEBI-20, PubChemSTM, MoMu) were designed for pre-training smaller models and lacked the instruction format needed for LLM fine-tuning. The construction methodology repurposes self-instruct (Wang et al., 2023b) for task description generation, adopts SELFIES (Krenn et al., 2022) over SMILES to guarantee molecular validity, and introduces template-based conversion of UniProtKB annotations into textual protein design specifications — a combination not seen in prior biomolecular data efforts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mol-Instructions (2M+ entries, 17 subtasks) fills a critical gap by providing the first open, large-scale instruction dataset purpose-built for biomolecular LLM training, enabling the community to move beyond pre-training-only approaches. Key limitations remain: LLMs still trail specialized smaller models (MolT5, Text+Chem T5) on molecular generation similarity metrics, protein design validation relies on indirect BLAST alignment rather than experimental confirmation, and LoRA-based training constrains the model's ability to fully internalize biomolecular 'language.' Open questions include whether expanding vocabulary or integrating dedicated biomolecular encoders (ESM, BioT5+) can close the remaining gap, and whether the dataset's current property coverage (primarily QM9 quantum properties) generalizes to broader chemical spaces.
