## Problem

Conventional SDEs assume Brownian motion with independent increments, which cannot capture the long-range temporal dependencies present in many real-world dynamic systems. Fractional Brownian motion (fBM) extends BM to non-Markovian dynamics governed by a Hurst index, but existing inference methods for fBM-driven SDEs are either computationally demanding (sparse Gaussian process approximations) or limited to specific discretizations. No variational inference framework existed for neural-SDEs driven by path-wise approximations of fBM, and fractional or variational neural-SDEs had never been applied to video modeling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frame sequences (64×64 single-channel for Stochastic Moving MNIST, 128×128 for the double pendulum dataset) that are encoded into low-dimensional latent vectors (D=6 or D=8). The SDE operates in this latent space, with the control function receiving time-appropriate context information via linear interpolation of temporal convolution outputs at intermediate solver time-steps. The Hurst index H is a learned scalar parameter (converging to H≈0.90–0.93 in experiments), and the model outputs posterior path measures, reconstructed/predicted video frames, and the ELBO. The Stratonovich-Milstein solver is used with commutative (diagonal) diffusion to enable higher-order integration.

## Method

The core insight is to approximate fBM via its Markov representation as a finite linear combination of K Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes driven by a shared Wiener noise, then augment the state space Z(t)=(X(t),Y₁(t),…,Y_K(t)) to recover a standard Markovian SDE amenable to Girsanov's change of measure. This yields a tractable ELBO where the KL-divergence reduces to the expected squared norm of a control function u_φ, parameterized by a neural network. The combination coefficients ω_k are optimized in closed form by minimizing the L₂ approximation error over [0,T], solving a linear system Aω=b whose entries involve regularized incomplete gamma functions. The drift, diffusion, and control terms are all neural networks, and the Hurst index is jointly optimized during training. For video prediction, a novel architecture encodes frames to latent vectors, infers a static content vector via temporal median, applies temporal convolutions for context, and decodes SDE states back to frames, with the prior modeling the dataset distribution and the posterior reconstructing specific sequences with minimal control effort.

## Results

On Stochastic Moving MNIST, the MA-fBM model achieves a PSNR of 15.30 and ELBO of −608.00, outperforming the BM baseline (PSNR 14.90, ELBO −913.60) while remaining competitive with discrete-time methods SVG (PSNR 14.50) and SLRVP (PSNR 16.93). On the real-world double pendulum dataset, MA-fBM achieves PSNR 27.09 versus 26.11 for BM. The optimized Hurst index converges to H=0.90 (SM-MNIST) and H=0.93 (double pendulum), both indicating strong long-term memory in the underlying dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the BM baseline, MA-fBM clearly wins on Stochastic Moving MNIST, improving ELBO from −913.60 to −608.00 and PSNR from 14.90 to 15.30, confirming that fractional noise better captures the correlated motion of bouncing digits. On the double pendulum, MA-fBM achieves a higher PSNR (27.09 vs. 26.11) but a lower ELBO (−636.61 vs. −545.13), suggesting the chaotic dynamics are better captured in reconstruction quality even if the likelihood bound is slightly worse. Compared to the discrete-time SVG method (Denton & Fergus, 2018), MA-fBM improves PSNR by 0.80 dB (15.30 vs. 14.50). However, SLRVP (Franceschi et al., 2020) still leads on PSNR (16.93 vs. 15.30), though it does not report an ELBO. For time-dependent Hurst index estimation, the paper reports that its MA-fBM approach produces a more accurate H(t) estimate than the sparse-GP method of Tong et al. (2022) on the same toy problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges the stochastic analysis literature on Markov representations of fractional Brownian motion (Carmona & Coutin, 1998; Harms & Stefanovits, 2019) with the machine learning community's variational inference framework for SDEs (Li et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2019). It departs from Tong et al. (2022), who approximated fBM via sparse Gaussian processes limited to Type II, Euler integration, and H > 1/3, by instead using a path-wise strong approximation via a finite sum of Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes that is agnostic to the SDE solver and supports both fBM types. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the Markov-approximated fBMSDE becomes an ordinary (augmented) SDE, making Girsanov's change of measure directly applicable for ELBO derivation—a step that was previously intractable for non-Markovian noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first tractable variational inference framework for neural-SDEs driven by fractional noise, opening the door to modeling long-range temporal dependencies in continuous-time generative models. The closed-form optimization of approximation coefficients and the ability to jointly learn the Hurst index from data make the approach practical. However, empirical validation is limited to small-scale video datasets (64×64 SM-MNIST, 128×128 double pendulum) with modest PSNR gains over BM baselines, and the Markov approximation still lacks a tight convergence bound. Future extensions to fractional Lévy processes and larger-scale video benchmarks would strengthen the practical impact.
