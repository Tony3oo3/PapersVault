## Problem

The TACTiS model for multivariate probabilistic time series prediction learns attentional copulas via a permutation-based objective whose number of distributional parameters grows factorially (O(d!)) with the number of variables, leading to poor training dynamics and suboptimal predictions on high-dimensional series. This paper asks whether valid nonparametric copulas can be learned with a far simpler objective that scales only linearly (O(d)) in the number of variables.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240914/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series where each variable may have a different number of observations, irregular timestamps, and optional deterministic covariates (e.g., holiday indicators). A binary mask specifies which values are observed versus to be inferred, enabling arbitrary task definitions (forecasting, interpolation, backcasting, or combinations) without architectural changes. The output is a full joint probability density over all masked values, parameterized as a product of marginal Deep Sigmoidal Flows and an attentional copula, from which samples can be drawn via inverse-CDF transformation.

## Method

TACTiS-2 replaces TACTiS's permutation-invariant copula objective with a two-stage curriculum enabled by a dual-encoder architecture: one transformer encoder produces representations for marginal CDFs (parametrized by Deep Sigmoidal Flows via a hyper-network), and a second encoder produces representations for the attentional copula. In stage 1, only the marginal encoder is trained (equivalent to fitting independent marginals); in stage 2, the marginals are frozen and the copula encoder is trained via causal attention over the probability-integral-transformed values, factorizing the copula density as a product of conditional histogram distributions. The paper proves (Prop. 2) that this two-stage procedure yields mathematically valid copulas, in contrast to the single-stage joint optimization which admits infinitely many invalid solutions (Prop. 1). The result is a model that converges to better solutions using roughly 3× fewer FLOPs than TACTiS while achieving state-of-the-art CRPS-Sum and NLL on five real-world forecasting benchmarks.

## Results

On the Monash forecasting benchmark (5 datasets), TACTiS-2 achieves the lowest CRPS-Sum on 4 of 5 datasets (electricity: 0.020, fred-md: 0.035, kdd-cup: 0.234, solar-10min: 0.240) and the best average rank (1.9 vs. 2.4 for TACTiS, 3.8 for GPVar). In terms of NLL, TACTiS-2 outperforms TACTiS on all five datasets (e.g., electricity: 10.674 vs. 11.028; solar-10min: −4.333 vs. −2.572) while requiring roughly 3× fewer training FLOPs (e.g., 0.623×10¹⁶ vs. 1.931×10¹⁶ on electricity). For interpolation, TACTiS-2 also substantially outperforms TACTiS (e.g., kdd-cup NLL: 0.138 vs. 1.050; solar-10min: −3.262 vs. −0.948).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TACTiS-2 outperforms all seven baselines on average CRPS-Sum rank (1.9 vs. 2.4 for TACTiS, 3.8 for GPVar, 5.0 for TimeGrad, 5.2 for SPD, 5.4 for TempFlow, 6.0 for ETS, 6.2 for Auto-ARIMA). Against its direct predecessor TACTiS, TACTiS-2 wins on 4 of 5 datasets for CRPS-Sum (electricity 0.020 vs. 0.021, fred-md 0.035 vs. 0.042, kdd-cup 0.234 vs. 0.237, solar-10min 0.240 vs. 0.311) but is slightly outperformed on traffic (0.078 vs. 0.071). On NLL, TACTiS-2 dominates TACTiS across all five datasets with non-overlapping confidence intervals on three of them, indicating superior multivariate dependency modeling. In terms of training efficiency, TACTiS-2 requires roughly 3× fewer FLOPs than TACTiS (e.g., 0.623×10¹⁶ vs. 1.931×10¹⁶ on electricity) while achieving better final accuracy. Against GPVar (the prior state-of-the-art general-purpose method), TACTiS-2 improves CRPS-Sum by 43% on electricity (0.020 vs. 0.035), 48% on fred-md (0.035 vs. 0.067), 20% on kdd-cup (0.234 vs. 0.290), and 6% on solar-10min (0.240 vs. 0.254). For interpolation, TACTiS-2 dramatically outperforms TACTiS (e.g., kdd-cup NLL 0.138 vs. 1.050; solar-10min −3.262 vs. −0.948), and on uneven/unaligned data it also substantially outperforms TACTiS (e.g., solar-10min unaligned NLL: −3.218 vs. −0.038).

## Relation to Prior Work

TACTiS-2 directly extends TACTiS (Drouin et al., 2022), replacing its permutation-invariant copula objective—which requires O(d!) distributional parameters—with a two-stage curriculum that scales only O(d). The two-stage estimation strategy has classical roots in parametric copula statistics (Joe & Xu, 1996; Andersen, 2005), but the paper's contribution is showing it applies cleanly to nonparametric, neural-network-based attentional copulas, eliminating the need for the permutation machinery entirely. The marginal distributions continue to use Deep Sigmoidal Flows (Huang et al., 2018), and the overall architecture retains TACTiS's transformer-based dual-encoder design, now split into separate encoders for marginals and copula.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factorial-to-linear parameter reduction is practically critical for high-dimensional time series (e.g., d=8880 on the electricity benchmark), where the original TACTiS approach becomes intractable. The paper demonstrates that this simplification yields not just feasibility but strictly better accuracy and ~3× fewer training FLOPs, making it a drop-in upgrade for practitioners. Key limitations remain: the model handles only continuous data, copula validity is guaranteed only in the infinite-data/capacity limit, and the two-stage curriculum introduces a sequential training dependency. The authors identify promising directions including distribution-shift robustness via the marginal-copula decoupling, large-scale domain-specific training with shared copulas, and multitask pretraining toward time-series foundation models.
