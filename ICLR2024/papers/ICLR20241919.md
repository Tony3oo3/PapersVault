## Problem

While Ye et al. (2021) showed that OOD generalization error can be bounded by intra-class variation and inter-class separation, no practical learning algorithm existed that directly optimizes these two geometric properties. This paper asks: how can one design a loss function that provably reduces intra-class variation while promoting inter-class separation, thereby tightening the OOD generalization bound?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241919/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images at varying resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for PACS, Office-Home, VLCS, and Terra Incognita) and outputs a class label via nearest-prototype classification. Internally, a CNN backbone (ResNet-18/34/50) followed by an MLP projection head produces a feature vector that is L2-normalized onto the unit hypersphere S^{d-1}; at test time, the predicted label is argmax over dot products between the normalized embedding and the C learned class prototypes.

## Method

HYPO learns domain-invariant representations on a unit hypersphere via a two-term loss: (1) a variation loss that pulls each sample's normalized embedding toward its class prototype using a softmax over prototype dot products (equivalent to maximum likelihood under a von Mises-Fisher distribution), and (2) a separation loss that pushes different class prototypes apart via a log-sum-exp of inter-prototype dot products. Class prototypes are maintained as exponential moving averages of normalized embeddings and are not backpropagated through. The key theoretical result (Theorem 6.1) shows that when samples are sufficiently aligned with their prototypes (controlled by the variation loss), the intra-class variation is upper-bounded by a term involving the optimization error, Rademacher complexity, and sample size—directly reducing the OOD generalization error bound from Ye et al. (2021). For multi-source domain generalization, hard negative pairs from the same domain are incorporated into the variation loss denominator to better separate confusable classes.

## Results

On the PACS, Office-Home, and VLCS domain generalization benchmarks (ResNet-50, standard SGD), HYPO achieves 88.0%, 71.7%, and 78.2% OOD accuracy respectively, yielding a 79.3% average that outperforms the next-best method (GVRT, 78.1%) by 1.2 points. On CIFAR-10 vs. CIFAR-10-C, HYPO improves OOD accuracy over the ERM baseline on all 19 corruption types, with the largest gain on Gaussian noise (78.09% → 85.21%, +7.12%). When coupled with SWAD optimization, HYPO further reaches 89.0% on PACS, surpassing PCL+SWAD (88.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HYPO outperforms all 18 loss-based baselines on PACS (88.0% vs. VNE 86.9%, SagNet 86.3%, ERM 85.5%) and Office-Home (71.7% vs. GVRT 70.1%, CORAL 68.7%, ERM 67.6%), with margins of 1.1 and 1.6 points over the respective best competitors. On VLCS, HYPO (78.2%) trails GVRT (79.0%) by 0.8 points but still exceeds ERM (77.5%) and CORAL (78.8%). The average across all three benchmarks (79.3%) is the highest, beating GVRT (78.1%) and SagNet (77.4%). On CIFAR-10-C, HYPO improves over ERM on every one of the 19 corruption types, with the most dramatic gain on Gaussian noise (+7.12%) and Glass blur (+6.67%). Against the closest proxy-based contrastive method PCL, HYPO achieves 88.0% with plain SGD versus PCL's 86.3%, and 89.0% with SWAD versus PCL+SWAD's 88.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the theoretical framework of Ye et al. (2021), which established that OOD generalization error is bounded by intra-class variation and inter-class separation but left open how to practically optimize these quantities. HYPO fills this gap by proposing a hyperspherical prototypical loss whose minimization provably reduces the variation term in the OOD bound. It also differentiates itself from PCL (Yao et al., 2022), a proxy-based contrastive method for domain generalization, by providing a rigorous vMF-distribution interpretation of the loss and by achieving competitive performance without relying on specialized optimization like SWAD. The use of class prototypes updated via EMA in a normalized embedding space draws from the broader hyperspherical learning literature (Mettes et al., 2019; Khosla et al., 2020; Ming et al., 2023), but is the first to establish a provable connection to domain-invariant representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is bridging a gap between theory and practice in OOD generalization: Ye et al. (2021) identified the right quantities to control but offered no loss, and HYPO provides the first loss with a provable guarantee of reducing the variation term that appears in the OOD error bound. This gives practitioners a theoretically grounded alternative to heuristic domain-generalization losses. Limitations include the assumption of uniform domain/class distribution in the theory, the dependence on Rademacher complexity (hard to bound for deep networks), and the need to maintain and update C class prototypes. Open questions include whether the framework extends to label-shift or covariate+label-shift settings, whether the vMF interpretation yields additional algorithmic benefits beyond the current softmax formulation, and whether the theoretical bounds are tight enough to be practically informative.
