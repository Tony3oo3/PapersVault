## Problem

Training large Transformer models from scratch discards the knowledge already acquired by smaller pre-trained counterparts, incurring enormous computational cost. Existing lossless model expansion methods (Net2Net, bert2BERT, Staged Training) suffer from weight symmetry that prevents the expanded model from gaining capacity, cannot handle indivisible width increments (e.g., 512→768), or are restricted to specific architectures like Pre-LN Transformers with width doubling only.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240138/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LEMON takes the full pre-trained weight tensor of a smaller source model (e.g., ViT with 6 blocks and 512 hidden dim, or BERT with 6 blocks and 384/512 hidden dim) and produces an initialized weight tensor for a larger target model (e.g., 12 blocks, 768 hidden dim) that is guaranteed to produce identical outputs to the source before any further training. The method handles arbitrary width and depth increments simultaneously, including indivisible cases where the target hidden dimension is not a multiple of the source's, and is compatible with Pre-LN, Post-LN, and Res-Post-Norm Transformer variants as well as CNNs like ResNet.

## Method

LEMON decomposes expansion into three lossless, layer-level operators applied recursively: (1) general width expansion with symmetry breaking, where replicated neurons receive unequal fan-out weights (α≠β with α+β=1) instead of the equal split used by Net2Net, preserving losslessness while eliminating the weight symmetry that would otherwise cap the expanded model's capacity; (2) average width expansion for LayerNorm, which pads FC-layer outputs with their channel-wise average and rescales the LayerNorm gain by η=√(D_S/D_T) with zero-padded bias, enabling lossless handling of indivisible width increments; and (3) depth expansion by inserting new blocks adjacent to their source with either zeroed module outputs or canceling fan-out weights (α=−β). For training the expanded model, LEMON retains the default maximum learning rate (critical for generalization) but applies an accelerated cosine decay schedule, reducing total training epochs from 300 to 130 for ViT and from 220k to 132k–165k iterations for BERT, yielding 56.7% and 33.2% computational savings respectively.

## Results

LEMON achieves lossless model expansion, recovering the target model's performance in significantly fewer training steps: 130 epochs for ViT (vs. 300 from scratch, a 56.7% computational saving) and 132k–165k iterations for BERT (vs. 220k from scratch, yielding 33.2% and 25.5% savings respectively). On GLUE downstream tasks, BERT(12,768) expanded from BERT(6,384) via LEMON achieves the best performance across all eight tasks (e.g., STS-B 0.866, SST-2 90.94, QQP 90.40), outperforming both the from-scratch model (STS-B 0.744, SST-2 88.88) and the model expanded from BERT(6,512).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEMON outperforms all four baselines across both ViT and BERT experiments. Both bert2BERT-FPI (Net2Net generalization) and bert2BERT-AKI exhibit performance inferior to training from scratch, confirming that their weight-symmetry issues persist during training. Soft KI (KL-divergence matching) fails to accelerate training of the target model, while hard KI (matching hard predictions) provides some speedup—likely acting as implicit curriculum learning—but still falls short of LEMON. Notably, even when the optimized learning rate scheduler (faster cosine decay) is applied to the baselines, LEMON continues to outperform them, indicating the advantage comes from the lossless expansion algorithm itself rather than solely from the training recipe.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEMON extends the lossless model expansion lineage from Net2Net (neuron duplication for CNNs/MLPs) and bert2BERT (Transformer adaptation) by addressing two key limitations: the weight symmetry problem (solved by setting unequal fan-out coefficients α≠β rather than α=β=1/2) and the indivisible width increment problem (solved via average expansion for LayerNorm). It also generalizes beyond Staged Training's constraint of width doubling and Pre-LN-only compatibility, supporting Post-LN, Res-Post-Norm, and RMSNorm architectures. The training recipe contribution—using the same maximum learning rate as from-scratch training but with accelerated cosine decay—departs from prior work that simply reused the original scheduler unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEMON offers a practical, data-free recipe to initialize scaled Transformers from smaller pre-trained counterparts, reducing training compute by up to 56.7% (ViT) and 33.2% (BERT) compared to training from scratch—meaningful savings given that large-model training costs millions of dollars. The counterintuitive finding that a larger (default) maximum learning rate outperforms a smaller one for expanded models, despite higher initial loss, challenges the natural assumption that already-converged models need gentler optimization. Limitations include the need to tune total training epochs per expansion configuration and evaluation constrained to moderate model sizes (ViT-12, BERT-12); extending to much larger models and developing principled criteria for selecting free initialization parameters remain open.
