## Problem

Conventional reward models (RMs) for RLHF rely entirely on static internal representations, making them unable to perform arithmetic, execute code, or verify factual claims against real-time sources. Unlike human labelers who naturally reach for calculators or search engines when evaluating answers, vanilla RMs are opaque black boxes that cannot access external environments, leading to unreliable scoring on tasks requiring computation, code validation, or up-to-date knowledge.

## Input / Output

The RM agent receives a question prompt and a candidate answer, then autoregressively generates a multi-step tool invocation trace (Thought → Action → Observation → Rationale) before producing a final scalar reward via a feed-forward layer. The trace includes actual API calls to seven external tools (Calculator, Code Interpreter, Translator, Google Search, Weather, Calendar, WikiSearch) with their real execution results as observations. The output is thus both a numeric preference score and a transparent, interpretable reasoning trace that records which tools were called, with what arguments, and how their results informed the final judgment.

## Method

Themis extends the standard pairwise RM with a ReAct-style tool invocation loop: at each step the model generates a natural-language Thought (deciding whether and which tool to call), an Action (the API call with arguments), receives an Observation (tool output), and after T steps produces a Rationale that synthesizes all evidence before a feed-forward layer outputs the scalar reward. The training objective (Eq. 2) combines the standard pairwise ranking loss with an autoregressive language modeling loss over the tool trace, with hyperparameters β and ω controlling whether Observation and Rationale tokens are supervised. When α=0 the method reduces exactly to a vanilla RM, making tool use an optional, learned capability rather than a fixed pipeline. The authors construct the TARA dataset (15K instances, 7 tools) via a multi-agent GPT-4 simulation where a negative-generation agent, a tool agent, and a rationale agent interact with human-verified tool executions to produce training trajectories.

## Results

On the TARA benchmark across 8 tool tasks, Themis (Vicuna-7B) achieves 94.23% average accuracy in the single-tool setting and 93.31% in the mixed-tool setting, outperforming RM(Vicuna-7B) by +19.2% and +17.7% respectively. On TruthfulQA (zero-shot), Themis scores 36.8, surpassing Gopher 280B (29.5) by 7.3 points and GPT-3/OPT-175B (21.0) by 15.8 points. In RLHF, PPO trained against Themis achieves 7.88 perplexity (vs. 8.10 for vanilla RM) and attains a 32% average human win rate across four tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RM(Bert-Large), Themis achieves a +29.2% improvement in the single-tool setting (94.23% vs. 65.01%) and +24.2% in the mixed-tool setting (93.31% vs. 69.10%), with the largest gains on Translator (+64.1%) and Calendar (+36.8%). Against RM(Vicuna-7B), Themis improves by +19.2% (single-tool) and +17.7% (mixed-tool), reaching 100% on Calendar and Weather tasks where the baseline scores 80.91% and 86.08% respectively. On TruthfulQA, Themis (7B) outperforms Gopher 280B by 7.3 points (36.8 vs. 29.5) and GPT-3/OPT-175B by 15.8 points in zero-shot evaluation. On the standard HH-RLHF dataset, Themis (77.59) slightly trails RAFT(LLaMA-7B) (79.52), though RAFT is trained on 112K positive samples plus 100K pairwise samples versus Themis's multi-task setting. In RLHF, PPO(Themis) achieves lower perplexity (7.88) than PPO(RM) (8.10) and a 32% average human win rate over the vanilla RM baseline across four tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Themis directly extends the ReAct paradigm (Yao et al., 2023) — the thought-action-observation loop — from task completion to reward modeling, repurposing the sequential tool-invocation structure to produce interpretable preference scores. It generalizes the standard pairwise RM formulation (Stiennon et al., 2020; Ouyang et al., 2022) by adding an autoregressive language-modeling loss over tool traces alongside the conventional Bradley-Terry ranking loss, with the property that setting the autoregressive weight to zero recovers the vanilla RM exactly. The paper positions itself at the intersection of tool-augmented LLMs (Toolformer, Chameleon) and RLHF reward modeling, noting that prior tool-learning work focused on policy generation rather than reward scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a 7B tool-augmented RM can match or exceed 280B parameter models on factuality benchmarks (TruthfulQA), suggesting that external tool access is a viable alternative to pure scale for grounding reward signals. The TARA dataset (15K instances across 7 tools) and the transparent reasoning traces address two practical gaps: lack of tool-specific RM training data and the opacity of black-box RMs. Key limitations include the narrow tool scope (7 APIs), single-turn data, primary reliance on 7B-scale models, and preliminary RLHF/RLTAF exploration; open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of tools, multi-turn dialogue, and 100B+ parameter models, and how to handle tool failures and biased observations at scale.
