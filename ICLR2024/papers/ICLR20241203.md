## Problem

Existing CVaR-based risk-sensitive RL algorithms are restricted to tabular MDPs with finite state and action spaces, making them inapplicable to large-scale or continuous environments. Moreover, no prior work combines Iterated CVaR (which controls risk at every decision step) with function approximation or with human-in-the-loop feedback, leaving a gap for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving, finance, and LLM alignment where both tight per-step risk control and human preference integration are needed.

## Input / Output

The agent observes states from a potentially infinite state space and selects actions, with transition kernels unknown. In the standard ICVaR-RL setting, per-step rewards are deterministic and known in [0,1]; in the RLHF extension, rewards are unknown and the agent only receives binary comparison feedback (which of two trajectories is preferred) from a human oracle modeled by a Bradley-Terry-Luce link function. The output is a policy mapping states to actions that maximizes the Iterated CVaR objective—i.e., the expected reward when transitioning to the worst α-portion of successor states at every step—along with optimistic value and Q-value function estimates used for exploration.

## Method

The paper proposes three algorithms built on optimistic value iteration. ICVaR-L (linear function approximation) introduces two key techniques: an ε-approximation of the CVaR operator by replacing the continuous supremum over [0,H] with a discrete ε-net, and a novel ridge regression that uses CVaR-adapted features ψ_{(x−V̂)⁺} instead of the standard ψ_{V̂} from risk-neutral linear mixture MDPs, enabling efficient parameter estimation despite the nonlinearity introduced by the CVaR operator. ICVaR-G (general function approximation) constructs confidence sets over a class of transition kernels and employs a new elliptical potential lemma yielding a tighter O(log K) bound on the squared diameter sum, improving over the O(√K) bound in prior work. ICVaR-HF extends the framework to risk-sensitive RLHF by applying MLE over an infinite reward function set (using bracketing-number-based discretization rather than cardinality) and developing a novel regret decomposition that bridges the gap between the risk-sensitive value function and the cumulative reward used by the comparison oracle.

## Results

The paper establishes theoretical regret bounds as its primary quantitative contribution. ICVaR-L achieves a regret upper bound of Õ(√(α^-(H+1)(d²H⁴ + dH⁶)K)) for linear function approximation, with a matching lower bound of Ω(√(α^-(H-1)d²K)) confirming near-minimax optimality in d and K. ICVaR-G achieves Õ(√(α^-(H+1)D_P H⁴ K)) for general function approximation, and ICVaR-HF achieves Õ(√(KH³)α^-(H+1)(√(HD_P) + √(m⁻¹D_R))) for risk-sensitive RLHF. Empirically, on a custom risk MDP with S=20, d=2 and S=40, d=4, ICVaR-L exhibits sublinear cumulative regret across 10 runs, while the risk-neutral LSVI baseline shows nearly linear regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ICVaR-L against two baselines on a custom risk-sensitive MDP (H=6, A=2, α∈{0.15, 0.30}). Against ICVaR-VI (Du et al., 2023), the tabular ICVaR-RL algorithm, ICVaR-L demonstrates faster convergence to the optimal risk-sensitive policy, highlighting the efficiency gain from linear function approximation over tabular methods. Against LSVI (Zhou et al., 2021b), the risk-neutral linear mixture MDP algorithm, ICVaR-L shows significantly lower cumulative regret, while LSVI exhibits nearly linear regret, indicating it fundamentally fails to learn the optimal risk-sensitive policy. Across both state space sizes (S=20, d=2 and S=40, d=4) and both risk levels, ICVaR-L consistently achieves sublinear regret, validating the theoretical guarantee in Theorem 1. The comparison confirms that risk-neutral algorithms are inadequate for ICVaR objectives and that function approximation provides a meaningful efficiency improvement over tabular ICVaR methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tabular ICVaR-RL framework of Du et al. (2023) to both linear mixture and general function approximation settings, addressing the nonlinearity introduced by the Iterated CVaR operator that breaks the Bellman equation structure exploited by risk-neutral function approximation algorithms (Zhou et al., 2021a; Ayoub et al., 2020). It further departs from existing RLHF theory (Wang et al., 2023b; Zhan et al., 2023a) by formalizing the first risk-sensitive RLHF problem, replacing the finite reward set assumption with an infinite reward function class and developing a novel regret decomposition that bridges the gap between risk-sensitive value functions and cumulative-reward-based human comparisons. The CVaR operator approximation via discrete ε-nets and the CVaR-adapted ridge regression features are new technical tools not present in prior risk-neutral or tabular risk-sensitive work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first provably sample-efficient algorithms for ICVaR-RL with function approximation, removing the restrictive finite state-space assumption that limited prior tabular results to small problems. The matching lower bound (up to polylog factors in d and K) establishes that the exponential α^{-H} dependence is inherent to iterated risk-sensitive learning, guiding future work on whether this can be circumvented with different risk measures. The risk-sensitive RLHF formulation opens a new direction at the intersection of LLM alignment and safety, though the current regret bound's dependence on the eluder dimension and bracketing number of the reward class remains loose. Key open questions include closing the gap between upper and lower bounds on α and H, and extending the framework to more general risk measures and multi-agent or sequential human feedback settings.
