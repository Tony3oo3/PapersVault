## Problem

Single-cell RNA-seq data contains abundant zeros that are a mixture of biological (no expression) and technical (dropout) origins, and these zeros distort the joint distribution of gene expressions, biasing gene regulatory network inference. Existing approaches—imputation and parametric zero-inflated models—either lack theoretical guarantees due to the unidentifiability of the true distribution or impose restrictive parametric assumptions. This paper provides the first fully non-parametric theoretical treatment of the dropout problem in GRNI, showing that the underlying distribution is irrecoverable but the conditional independence structure is.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-cell RNA-seq count matrix (p genes × n cells) where zeros are unannotated (i.e., one cannot distinguish biological from technical zeros) and produces a CPDAG over the p gene expression variables representing the gene regulatory network. Optionally, by extending the CI tests to include the binary zero-indicator variables R, the method also recovers causal edges from gene expressions to dropout indicators (Z→R), revealing which genes' expressions influence other genes' dropout rates. The procedure operates on observational data from a single scRNA-seq run without interventions, requiring only that the remaining sample size after test-wise deletion is asymptotically sufficient for each CI test.

## Method

The paper introduces a Causal Dropout Model (CDM) where each observed expression X_i = (1−D_i)·Z_i, with D_i a latent binary dropout indicator causally influenced by Z_i (self-masking). The central theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that for any CI query Z_i ⊥ Z_j | Z_S, the equivalent test on observed data X_i ⊥ X_j | Z_S, R_S=0—i.e., performing the CI test only on samples where all conditioned variables are non-zero—recovers the correct CI relation asymptotically, even though the conditional distribution of the remaining samples is distorted. This 'test-wise deletion' procedure is seamlessly integrated into constraint-based methods (PC) and score-based methods (GES) by replacing each CI test with its zero-deleted counterpart, yielding asymptotic consistency without requiring imputation or parametric assumptions on the dropout mechanism. The framework is non-parametric, and existing parametric dropout models (zero-inflated, hurdle, truncated Gaussian copula) are shown to be specific instances of the CDM. Additionally, a generalized procedure (Definition 2) that deletes zeros for all variables involved in each test enables joint discovery of the GRN and dropout mechanisms up to their identifiability bound, even when the self-masking assumption is relaxed.

## Results

On the BEELINE benchmark (9 algorithms × 10 datasets = 90 pairs), the proposed testwise deletion outperforms full-sample (no correction) on 71% of pairs and outperforms MAGIC imputation on 68% of pairs, measured by F1-score of estimated skeleton edges. On linear SEM simulations with 30 variables, testwise deletion achieves SHD values close to the oracle (true Z) across all three dropout mechanisms (fixed-rate, truncation, probabilistic), while full-sample PC produces significantly denser (worse) graphs. On real-world Perturb-seq data (21 genes, 9,843 cells), PC with testwise deletion recovers predominantly known regulatory interactions (blue edges in Fig. 6), whereas PC on full samples introduces numerous spurious edges contradicted by prior knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three dropout-handling strategies against the proposed testwise deletion. MAGIC imputation (Van Dijk et al., 2018) is counterproductive in 50% of BEELINE pairs, performing worse than simply using full samples without correction, and is outperformed by testwise deletion on 68% of pairs. The mixedCCA parametric model (Yoon et al., 2020) performs reasonably well under Gaussian + truncation dropout (its native setting) but is still outperformed by testwise deletion on linear SEM simulations, confirming the advantage of the nonparametric approach. Binarization (Qiu, 2020) is the weakest baseline, performing worse than full samples on 64% of BEELINE pairs. Across all three dropout mechanisms and both PC and GES base algorithms, testwise deletion consistently approaches oracle performance, while all three baselines show significant degradation relative to the oracle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-wise deletion framework from the missing data causal discovery literature (Strobl et al., 2018; Tu et al., 2019) to the specific and more challenging setting of scRNA-seq dropouts, where missing entries are not explicitly marked but conflated with biological zeros. It departs from the dominant imputation-based paradigm (MAGIC, SAVER) and parametric zero-inflated/hurdle models by proposing a non-parametric causal graphical model (Causal Dropout Model) that unifies these parametric approaches as special instances. The method is then plugged into standard constraint-based (PC) and score-based (GES) causal discovery algorithms, requiring no modification to the underlying structure learning logic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first non-parametric theoretical guarantee for GRNI under dropouts, showing that the true distribution p(Z) is fundamentally unidentifiable from observations p(X) (ruling out imputation in principle), yet conditional independence relations are preserved under a simple test-wise deletion procedure. This reframes dropout handling from a data-preprocessing problem to a causal-structure problem with asymptotic correctness. A key open question is the practical impact of reduced sample size after deletion, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation; developing methods to mitigate this power loss (e.g., adaptive conditioning sets or variance reduction) remains for future work. The framework also enables empirical validation of the self-masking dropout assumption (A3) from data, which is a useful diagnostic for practitioners.
