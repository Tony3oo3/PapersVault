## Problem

Existing deep partial-label learning (PLL) methods rely on learning-centric training strategies for label disambiguation, but when candidate label sets become excessively large, these strategies fail to identify the true label, causing severe performance degradation. No prior work has addressed this from a data-centric perspective by reducing the candidate label set size before training, leaving a gap in how to handle high-ambiguity PLL data without modifying the training procedure itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PLL dataset of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, variable for PASCAL VOC) each paired with a candidate label set, along with a pre-trained feature extractor (e.g., BLIP-2 producing 768-dim embeddings) to compute instance representations. It outputs a pruned PLL dataset where each instance's candidate label set has been reduced by removing a proportion τ of the most likely false labels, which can then be directly fed into any existing deep PLL method without architectural changes. The process is entirely training-free, requiring only k-NN search via Faiss in the representation space.

## Method

The core mechanism is a per-instance 'down-voting' scheme based on the inconsistency between the representation space and the candidate label space: for each candidate label of an instance, a down-vote is tallied for every k-NN neighbor whose own candidate label set does not contain that label, so labels rarely shared with neighbors are flagged as likely false. The top-γ_i highest down-voted labels (where γ_i = ⌈τ(|Y_i|−1)⌉) are removed per instance, yielding a pruned candidate set. Theoretically, under a (k, δ_k, ρ_k)-label distinguishability assumption, the paper proves an upper bound on the per-instance pruning error rate (Theorem 1) and bounds the extra error from increasing the pruning extent (Theorem 2), showing that high-quality representations (small δ_k) and low label ambiguity (small ρ_k) make aggressive pruning safe. Empirically, the method uses BLIP-2 as the default feature extractor and Faiss for efficient k-NN search, with k and τ tuned per dataset and generation setting (e.g., large k=150 and τ=0.6 for uniform CIFAR-10, but small k=5 and τ=0.2 for instance-dependent settings with knotty label ambiguity).

## Results

The proposed CLSP method improves test accuracy in 145 out of 149 evaluated cases (≈97.3%) across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, and PASCAL VOC under uniform, label-dependent, instance-dependent, and long-tailed PLL settings. Gains are most dramatic for weaker baselines under high noise: on CIFAR-10 (q=0.6), CAVL jumps from 54.52% to 80.99% (+26.5%) and CC from 71.16% to 84.62% (+13.5%). On the real-world PASCAL VOC dataset, CC improves from 34.21% to 50.81% (+16.6%) and CAVL from 32.25% to 49.45% (+17.2%). For already-strong methods like PiCO and CR on CIFAR-10 (q=0.4), improvements are modest (0.2–0.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLSP is a data-centric preprocessing step applied before training, so it is compared against 11 deep PLL methods (CC, PRODEN, LWS, CAVL, PiCO, CR/CRDPLL, ABLE, IDGP, POP, SoLar, RECORDS) by re-running each method on both the original and pruned candidate label sets under identical training configurations. The method wins in 145/149 cases (≈97.3%) on test accuracy. Gains are largest for weaker or less robust baselines: CC improves by up to +16.6% on VOC and +13.5% on CIFAR-10 (q=0.6); CAVL improves by up to +26.5% on CIFAR-10 (q=0.6) and +17.2% on VOC; LWS gains up to +10.3% on VOC. Stronger methods like PiCO, CR, and POP see smaller but consistent gains of 0.2–1.5% on CIFAR-10. The four exceptions (≈2.7%) where performance slightly degrades involve ABLE and SoLar, which use very long training (500–1000 epochs) and overfit the small number of noisy instances introduced by pruning. On transductive accuracy, improvements are even more pronounced (147/149 ≈ 98.7%), confirming that CLSP directly reduces label ambiguity and boosts disambiguation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant learning-centric paradigm in deep partial-label learning, where prior work (CC, PRODEN, LWS, CAVL, PiCO, CR, ABLE, IDGP, POP, SoLar, RECORDS) designs training strategies for label disambiguation, by introducing a data-centric pre-processing step that operates before any model training. It repurposes k-NN in representation space—previously employed within training loops for label refinement in conventional PLL—as a standalone, training-free pruning mechanism, and leverages pre-trained vision-language models (BLIP-2, CLIP, ALBEF) as feature extractors, a departure from the CNN-based features typically used in the PLL literature. The paper also distinguishes itself from dataset pruning/distillation, which reduces the number of training examples, by instead reducing the magnitude of candidate label sets per example.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a simple, training-free, plug-and-play pre-processing step that consistently improves 11 state-of-the-art PLL methods across four datasets and multiple candidate generation settings (97.3% of cases show improvement). Limitations include dependence on feature extractor quality (higher error on complex real-world data like PASCAL VOC at 5.2%), the instance-dependent setting remaining particularly challenging due to knotty label ambiguity, and the method's reliance on a fixed global pruning ratio τ rather than adaptive per-instance decisions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other weakly supervised settings (multi-label, semi-supervised), whether adaptive per-instance pruning ratios could further reduce the α-error, and whether the theoretical bounds can be tightened to provide more actionable guidance for hyperparameter selection.
