## Problem

Single-image-to-3D reconstruction has been limited by category-specific priors, slow per-shape optimization, or dependence on 2D diffusion models that constrain quality and require delicate tuning. No prior work has demonstrated that a sufficiently large, fully data-driven transformer trained on massive multi-view data can reconstruct arbitrary in-the-wild objects from one image in a single forward pass without any generative 2D model or per-shape refinement.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 512×512 RGB image with the object centered on a white background (background removed via an off-the-shelf segmentation tool) and assumes fixed normalized camera intrinsics/extrinsics at inference. It outputs a triplane NeRF (three 64×64×80 feature planes) that can be queried to render novel views at arbitrary angles or extracted into a watertight mesh via Marching Cubes at 384³ resolution, all within ~5 seconds on one A100 GPU.

## Method

LRM uses a DINO ViT-B/16 encoder to produce 1025 patch-level image tokens, which are projected onto 3072 learnable triplane spatial-positional embeddings by a 16-layer transformer decoder that alternates cross-attention (triplane queries attend to image features) and self-attention (modeling intra-triplane spatial relations), with camera conditioning injected via adaptive layer normalization (adaLN) at every sub-layer. The decoder output is deconvolution-upsampled to the final 64×64 triplane, from which a 10-layer MLP decodes per-point RGB and density for differentiable volume rendering. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on ~1M objects (730K Objaverse renders + 220K MVImgNet video frames) using only MSE and LPIPS losses on rendered novel views, with no 3D-aware regularization or per-shape optimization. The key design choices—triplane as a compact structured 3D token set (vs. 4K–16K unstructured point-cloud tokens), treating 3D as an independent modality learned purely through cross-attention without explicit 2D-to-3D spatial alignment, and camera pose normalization—enable stable scaling to 500M parameters and strong generalization to unseen categories and in-the-wild images.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects benchmark (100 objects, 20 reference views), LRM achieves a FID of 31.44, CLIP-Similarity of 0.902, PSNR of 19.60, LPIPS of 0.163, and Chamfer Distance of 0.053, consistently outperforming all three baselines across every metric. The model reconstructs a 3D mesh from a single image in under 5 seconds on a single A100 GPU, without any per-shape optimization. Ablation studies confirm that combining synthetic (Objaverse) and real (MVImgNet) data, camera pose normalization, and LPIPS loss are all critical for achieving the reported quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRM outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against Point-E (image-to-point-cloud diffusion), LRM reduces FID from 123.70 to 31.44 and improves PSNR from 15.60 to 19.60, while also halving the Chamfer Distance (0.053 vs. 0.099). Against Shap-E (latent diffusion over point clouds), LRM achieves a 3.1× lower FID (31.44 vs. 97.05) and a 0.097 higher CLIP-Similarity (0.902 vs. 0.805). Against One-2-3-45 (2D diffusion for multi-view generation), LRM shows the largest margin: FID drops from 139.24 to 31.44, LPIPS improves from 0.448 to 0.163, and Chamfer Distance decreases from 0.123 to 0.053. The authors attribute LRM's advantage to the compact, structured triplane representation (3,072 tokens vs. 4K–16K point tokens) and the end-to-end trainable framework that avoids the instability of two-stage diffusion-based pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

LRM builds on the triplane NeRF representation from EG3D (Chan et al., 2022) and the volume rendering framework of NeRF (Mildenhall et al., 2021), but departs fundamentally from the prevailing trend of leveraging 2D diffusion models (Zero-1-to-3, Make-It-3D, One-2-3-45) as multi-view priors. Instead, it draws an explicit analogy to large language models: a highly scalable transformer architecture (500M parameters) trained end-to-end on ~1M objects with a simple image reconstruction loss, treating 3D as an independent modality grounded onto 2D image features via cross-attention. The use of DINO's structural/texture-rich features (rather than CLIP's semantic features) as the image encoder is a deliberate choice to preserve geometric detail needed for 3D reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LRM demonstrates that a purely data-driven, feed-forward transformer can match or exceed diffusion-prior-based methods for single-image-to-3D, suggesting that scale and representation choice (structured triplane vs. unstructured point clouds) may matter more than external generative priors. Key limitations remain: blurry occluded regions (deterministic model averaging multiple plausible modes), fixed camera assumptions at inference, no background handling, and Lambertian-only appearance. The paper opens a clear path toward scaling 3D reconstruction models and extending them to multimodal (text-to-3D) generation, but the fixed-camera and single-object constraints limit immediate real-world deployment.
