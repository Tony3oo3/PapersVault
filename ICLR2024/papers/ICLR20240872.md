## Problem

Deploying LLMs in streaming applications (e.g., multi-round dialogue) faces two barriers: KV cache memory grows unboundedly during decoding, and models collapse when sequence length exceeds the pre-training window. Window attention offers constant memory but fails catastrophically once the initial tokens' KV are evicted, and the only quality-preserving alternative (sliding window with recomputation) is impractically slow due to quadratic attention recomputation per token.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous token stream and produces next-token predictions indefinitely, using only a fixed-size KV cache composed of 4 initial tokens (attention sinks) plus a rolling window of the most recent tokens (e.g., 2048 for Llama-2). A key operational detail is that positional encodings (RoPE or ALiBi) are reassigned based on positions within the cache rather than absolute positions in the original text, so the model never sees positions beyond its pre-training window. This decouples the pre-training context length from the actual generation length, enabling stable modeling of 4M+ tokens.

## Method

StreamingLLM exploits the 'attention sink' phenomenon: autoregressive LLMs allocate disproportionately high SoftMax attention to initial tokens regardless of semantic relevance, because SoftMax requires scores to sum to one and initial tokens are visible to all subsequent tokens. The framework simply retains the KV of 4 initial tokens as attention sinks alongside a rolling window of recent tokens, reassigning positional encodings within the cache to keep relative distances bounded. This requires no fine-tuning and is compatible with any relative-position-encoding scheme (RoPE, ALiBi). Additionally, the paper shows that pre-training with a single learnable sink token at the start of every sample concentrates the attention sink into one dedicated token, eliminating the need for multiple initial tokens and further stabilizing streaming perplexity without harming standard NLP benchmark performance.

## Results

StreamingLLM reduces language modeling perplexity from 5158.07 (window attention, 0+1024 cache) to 5.40 (4+1020 cache) on Llama-2-13B over 65K tokens in PG19, and maintains stable perplexity over 4 million tokens across Llama-2, MPT, Falcon, and Pythia models. On streaming ARC question-answering, StreamingLLM matches the one-shot sample-by-sample baseline (e.g., 71.34% vs. 71.25% on ARC-Easy with Llama-2-7B-Chat) while dense attention fails with OOM errors. In efficiency benchmarks, StreamingLLM achieves up to 22.2× per-token decoding speedup over the sliding window with re-computation baseline while retaining comparable memory usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against window attention (the Longformer-style sliding window baseline), StreamingLLM is dramatically superior: perplexity drops from 5158.07 to 5.40 on Llama-2-13B, and streaming ARC accuracy goes from near-zero to matching the one-shot upper bound. Against the sliding window with re-computation baseline (the only other method with acceptable quality on long texts), StreamingLLM matches perplexity almost exactly but achieves up to 22.2× per-token speedup, since re-computation incurs O(TL²) quadratic attention cost while StreamingLLM uses O(T) linear decoding. Against dense attention, StreamingLLM handles sequences of arbitrary length (4M+ tokens) where dense attention either OOMs or degrades beyond the pre-training window. Against context-extended models (LongChat-7b-v1.5-32k and Llama-2-7B-32K-Instruct), StreamingLLM is complementary rather than competitive: it can be applied on top of these 32K-context models to extend effective streaming to 120K+ tokens on StreamEval, where the base models fail at their 32K limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the window attention approach (Longformer, Beltagy et al.) by identifying its fundamental failure mode—catastrophic perplexity collapse when initial tokens are evicted—and reframes the problem through the newly identified 'attention sink' phenomenon. It positions itself within the length extrapolation literature (RoPE, ALiBi) but targets a regime those methods cannot reach: truly infinite-length streaming without fine-tuning. The SoftMax-Off-by-One observation from the quantization-outliers literature and the 'register' tokens in Vision Transformers (Darcet et al.) provide conceptual parallels, but StreamingLLM is the first to connect these dots into a practical, zero-fine-tuning framework for autoregressive LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StreamingLLM decouples the pre-training window size from the actual generation length, enabling stable language modeling over 4M+ tokens with a 22.2× speedup over the only viable baseline—making persistent LLM deployment (multi-round dialogue, continuous assistants) practically feasible for the first time. The attention sink insight is a fundamental finding about Transformer attention mechanics that likely affects all SoftMax-based architectures, and the sink-token pre-training recommendation has implications for all future LLM training. Key limitations remain: the method cannot access information beyond the rolling cache (no long-term memory), and models demonstrably fail to fully utilize even the context within the cache, pointing to a broader open problem in long-context utilization.
