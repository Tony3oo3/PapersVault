## Problem

Full-matrix Adagrad captures all gradient cross-moments for optimal adaptive preconditioning but requires O(d²) memory and O(d³) computation, making it infeasible for modern neural networks. Existing practical approximations like Shampoo (Kronecker product) and diagonal Adagrad discard significant cross-moment information, and no prior method provably sits closer to the full-matrix ideal while retaining Shampoo's computational cost. CASPR addresses this gap by constructing a Kronecker-sum-based combination of per-axis preconditioners that is provably nearer to full-matrix Adagrad in Loewner order than Shampoo.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the method consumes a matrix-shaped gradient G_t ∈ R^{m×n} (the gradient of a fully-connected layer weight) and updates two running second-moment statistics: L_t = L_{t-1} + G_t G_t^T (m×m) and R_t = R_{t-1} + G_t^T G_t (n×n), optionally with exponential moving averages. The output is an updated weight matrix W_t computed via a two-step preconditioning of G_t using the inverse fourth roots L_t^{-1/4} and R_t^{-1/4}, requiring only O(m² + n²) memory and O(m³ + n³) flops per step. The inverse fourth roots are computed via a coupled Newton iteration and amortized by reusing them across 20 iterations.

## Method

CASPR derives per-axis preconditioners by solving a Frobenius-norm subproblem that finds the nearest block-diagonal PSD matrix with identical blocks to the full-matrix gradient statistic, yielding L* = (1/m)Σ G_s^T G_s and R* = (1/n)Σ G_s G_s^T. Rather than combining these via Kronecker product (as Shampoo does), CASPR uses the Kronecker-sum combination X_t = ((L_t^{-1/4} ⊗ I_n + I_m ⊗ R_t^{-1/4})/2)², which equals the average of the arithmetic mean and geometric mean of the row and column preconditioners. A key Loewner-order result shows √r·(rεI + Σ g_t g_t^T)^{-1/2} ⪰ X_t^{caspr} ⪰ X_t^{shampoo}, placing CASPR strictly between Shampoo and full-matrix Adagrad and yielding a tighter O(√T) regret bound in online convex optimization. The practical update reduces to two sequential matrix multiplications—U = L^{-1/4}G + GR^{-1/4} followed by U = L^{-1/4}U + UR^{-1/4}—which is a minimal modification of Shampoo's single-pass U = L^{-1/4}G, U = UR^{-1/4}, preserving identical computational and memory complexity.

## Results

On OGBG-molpcba (GNN), CASPR achieves a test average precision of 0.29, compared to 0.286 for Shampoo and 0.271 for AdamW (a 1.4% relative improvement over Shampoo and 7% over AdamW). On the Universal Dependencies POS-tagging task (Transformer, 16.6M params), CASPR reaches 69.76% validation accuracy versus 69.45% for Shampoo and 68.84% for AdamW. On C4 language modeling (14M and 234M parameter GLU Transformers), CASPR consistently outperforms Shampoo in log perplexity by up to 1% relative improvement, while both methods take nearly identical wall-clock training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASPR is compared against two baselines: Shampoo (Kronecker-product based adaptive optimizer) and AdamW (diagonal adaptive optimizer). Against Shampoo, CASPR wins on all three benchmarks: +1.4% relative test AP on OGBG-molpcba (0.290 vs. 0.286), +1% relative validation error reduction on Universal Dependencies (69.76% vs. 69.45% accuracy), and up to 1% relative log-perplexity improvement on C4 language modeling. Critically, CASPR and Shampoo require nearly identical training time since both compute the same axes preconditioners L^{-1/4} and R^{-1/4}, differing only in how they combine them (Kronecker-sum vs. Kronecker-product). Against AdamW, CASPR shows larger margins: +7% relative test AP on OGBG-molpcba and +3% relative error reduction on Universal Dependencies, though AdamW is given 3.5× more iterations to match wall-clock time. The paper omits AdamW from the C4 experiments due to their computational cost, citing the clear superiority of Shampoo/CASPR over Adam in the other two benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

CASPR is a direct extension of Shampoo (Gupta et al., 2018; Anil et al., 2020), which combines row and column axes preconditioners via a Kronecker product. The key departure is replacing the Kronecker-product combination with a Kronecker-sum-based one, which the paper shows via Loewner order to be strictly closer to the full-matrix Adagrad preconditioner (Duchi et al., 2011) than Shampoo is. The derivation starts from the full-matrix Adagrad subproblem of finding the nearest sparse approximation to the second-moment statistic, and the Kronecker-sum structure emerges naturally as a principled intermediate between the block-diagonal (axes) approximations and the full matrix. Theoretically, the paper builds on the online convex optimization regret analysis framework of Hazan et al. (2016) and Gupta et al. (2017), leveraging Loewner-order inequalities to tighten the bound relative to Shampoo's existing guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CASPR offers a drop-in replacement for Shampoo with only a small code modification (two extra matrix multiplications per update step) while providing a provably tighter regret bound and empirically better approximation of the full-matrix second-moment statistic. The practical impact is real but modest: 1–7% relative improvements over Shampoo or AdamW across GNN, Transformer, and LLM benchmarks, with no additional training time. Open questions include whether the Kronecker-sum combination can be extended to approximate the Fisher information matrix (connecting to K-FAC and natural gradient methods), and whether the theoretical advantage translates to larger gains at scale (experiments top out at 234M parameters). The paper does not explore non-convex convergence guarantees directly, leaving that to existing online-to-batch reductions.
