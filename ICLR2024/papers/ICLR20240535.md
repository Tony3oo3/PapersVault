## Problem

IBP-based certified training methods consistently outperform those using more precise bounding techniques, yet the mechanisms behind this success remain theoretically unexplained. The paper addresses this gap by asking: what does IBP training actually do to the network's weight structure, and how does it affect the precision of the resulting bounds?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240535/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on standard image classification inputs (MNIST 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10 32×32 RGB, TinyImageNet) with an ℓ∞ perturbation radius ε, producing class predictions, certified accuracy via MN-BAB verification, and a novel scalar propagation tightness τ ∈ [0,1] per network. For ReLU networks, tightness is computed locally at infinitesimal ε using the activation pattern at a concrete input, making it tractable despite the intractability of global optimal box bounds for nonlinear networks.

## Method

The paper introduces propagation tightness τ as the ratio of optimal box radius to IBP layer-wise box radius, then derives necessary and sufficient conditions on weight matrices for τ=1 (propagation invariance), showing these impose extreme sign-structure constraints that act as strong regularization. For deep linear networks, it proves tightness decreases polynomially (Θ(1/√d)) with width and exponentially with depth at initialization, and that IBP training gradients are aligned with increasing τ. Transferring these results to ReLU networks via local linearization, the empirical study trains CNN3/CNN7 architectures across widths (0.5×–16×) and depths (3–13 layers) on CIFAR-10, confirming that wider networks yield state-of-the-art certified accuracy while deeper ones do not, and that tightness is governed primarily by the propagated region size ξ=λε rather than the full perturbation ε—explaining why unsound methods like SABR that shrink ξ achieve better accuracy-robustness trade-offs than full IBP.

## Results

The paper's key empirical finding is that increasing network width (not depth) improves certified accuracy for all IBP-based training methods. On CIFAR-10 at ε=2/255, SABR with 2× width achieves 63.28% certified accuracy (vs. 62.84% at 1×), and IBP-R with 2× width reaches 62.03% (vs. 60.87% at 1×). On MNIST at ε=0.3, SABR with 4× width achieves 93.85% certified accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA MTL-IBP. The paper also introduces propagation tightness τ, showing IBP training drives τ to ~0.98 (near propagation invariance) while PGD training barely affects it (τ≈0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new training method but demonstrates that width scaling of existing IBP-based methods yields state-of-the-art certified accuracy. SABR (Müller et al., 2022b) consistently outperforms standard IBP (Shi et al., 2021) on CIFAR-10: at ε=2/255 with 1× width, SABR achieves 62.84% certified vs. IBP's 55.85%, a 7-point gap. IBP-R (Palma et al., 2022) also beats IBP (60.87% vs. 55.85% at ε=2/255) by using IBP only as a regularizer. The width-scaled SABR (2×) on CIFAR-10 at ε=2/255 (63.28%) and SABR (4×) on MNIST at ε=0.3 (93.85%) surpass the previous SOTA methods CROWN-IBP (Zhang et al., 2020) and MTL-IBP (Palma et al., 2023) shown as references in Figure 6. TAPS/STAPS (Mao et al., 2023) shows similar trends to SABR when varying regularization level (Figure 12), confirming the tightness-accuracy trade-off across methods. Non-IBP methods like PGD and COLT achieve higher standard accuracy but far lower certified accuracy (e.g., PGD has τ≈0.001 vs. SABR's τ≈0.182 at ε=2/255), confirming that IBP-based regularization is essential for certifiability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the IBP propagation framework (Gowal et al., 2018; Mirman et al., 2018) and the certified training methods that leverage it (Shi et al., 2021; SABR/Müller et al., 2022; IBP-R/Palma et al., 2022; TAPS/Mao et al., 2023), but departs from them by providing the first theoretical account of *why* IBP training is effective. While Jovanović et al. (2022) attributed IBP's success to the optimization landscape being easier for imprecise bounds, this paper introduces the propagation tightness metric and shows that IBP training actively increases tightness toward propagation invariance, imposing strong structural regularization on weights. The paper also distinguishes itself from Shi et al. (2021), who defined tightness as absolute box size Δ, by instead measuring the *ratio* of optimal to IBP bounds, yielding fundamentally different insights about architecture effects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The propagation tightness metric provides a principled, computable tool for analyzing and designing certified training methods, and the theoretical results (polynomial width decay, exponential depth decay, IBP training increasing tightness) give practitioners concrete architectural guidance: wider but shallower networks are preferable for certified training. The key open question the paper surfaces is whether one can achieve sufficient certifiability without the full regularization cost of near-propagation-invariance, since tightness is dominated by propagation region size rather than the robustness specification ε. Limitations include that theoretical results are proven for deep linear networks and transferred to ReLU networks only empirically, and that local tightness for ReLU networks is an approximation (though with <0.5% error for typical ε). Future work could leverage the tightness metric to design training objectives that target a *sufficient* rather than maximal tightness level.
