## Problem

Document-level contrastive learning in neural topic models captures low-level mutual information (e.g., word counts, frequency ratios) that distorts topic representations, and the ELBO's reconstruction objective conflicts with the contrastive objective's generalization pressure in ways that fixed linear weighting cannot resolve. The paper addresses both issues by moving contrastive learning to the set level and formulating the ELBO–contrastive trade-off as a multi-objective optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a mini-batch of B document word-count vectors (x ∈ R^V) and, before BoW encoding, uses ChatGPT to generate semantically related (positive) and unrelated (negative) augmentations for each document. Documents are grouped into sets of size K, and S index shuffles multiply the number of set pairs per iteration. The model outputs latent topic distributions z ∈ R^T per document and topic-word distributions, which are evaluated via NPMI, topic diversity, and downstream document classification (Random Forest on T=50 latent vectors).

## Method

The paper introduces setwise contrastive learning for NTMs: instead of contrasting individual document pairs, it groups K documents into a set, encodes each document's topic vector via the NTM encoder, and aggregates them with min-pooling for positive sets and max-pooling for negative sets before applying InfoNCE. Index shuffling (S permutations of the batch) multiplies the number of positive/negative set pairs from ⌊B/K⌋ to ⌊BS/K⌋ per iteration, increasing training signal. For balancing the two objectives, the paper derives an analytical Pareto-stationary solution α = [−(∇L_InfoNCE − ∇L_ELBO)^T ∇L_InfoNCE / ‖∇L_InfoNCE − ∇L_ELBO‖²]_+ that dynamically reweights the encoder gradient each step, replacing fixed linear weights or heuristic multi-task schedulers. The decoder is updated solely by the ELBO reconstruction gradient, while the encoder receives the α-weighted combination of both gradients.

## Results

On 20Newsgroups (T=50), the proposed setwise contrastive NTM achieves NPMI 0.340 and Topic Diversity 0.913, outperforming the strongest baseline NTM+CL (0.332 / 0.853). On Wikitext-103 (T=50), it reaches NPMI 0.496 and TD 0.959, surpassing NTM+CL (0.481 / 0.841) and DVAE (0.404 / 0.815). For downstream document classification, the model attains F1 scores of 57.0 (20NG), 86.4 (IMDb), and 78.6 (AG News), exceeding NTM+CL by 2.6, 2.2, and 2.4 points respectively. All improvements over the second-best method are statistically significant (p < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 11 baselines on both NPMI and TD across all four datasets and both topic counts (T=50, T=200). The closest competitor is NTM+CL (Nguyen & Luu, 2021), the individual-based contrastive NTM; the gap is most pronounced on Topic Diversity (e.g., 0.913 vs. 0.853 on 20NG T=50, 0.959 vs. 0.841 on Wiki T=50), indicating that setwise contrastive learning produces substantially less redundant topics. On NPMI, the improvement over NTM+CL is more modest but consistent (e.g., 0.340 vs. 0.332 on 20NG T=50; 0.496 vs. 0.481 on Wiki T=50). Among non-contrastive baselines, DVAE and SCHOLAR+BAT are the strongest on coherence, yet both are surpassed by a clear margin (e.g., DVAE 0.404 vs. 0.496 NPMI on Wiki T=50). On downstream document classification (F1), the model leads NTM+CL by 2.2–2.6 points and SCHOLAR by 4.6–24.2 points across 20NG, IMDb, and AG News. HyperMiner and TSCTM, which also employ contrastive or geometric strategies, trail the proposed method by 0.04–0.09 NPMI and 0.10–0.47 TD, confirming that the combination of setwise contrastive learning and Pareto-based multi-objective optimization yields the most effective topic representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive neural topic model line initiated by Nguyen & Luu (2021), which jointly optimizes the ELBO and an instance-level InfoNCE loss. The key departures are twofold: (1) replacing per-document contrastive pairs with setwise contrastive learning that aggregates topic vectors over groups of documents via pooling, motivated by the hypothesis that useful topic signals are shared across multiple documents rather than captured by single-instance discrimination; and (2) replacing the fixed linear combination of ELBO and contrastive losses with a gradient-based multi-objective optimization framework (Sener & Koltun, 2018) that dynamically computes the Pareto-optimal weighting α at each training step. The base architecture remains the standard VAE-based NTM (Miao et al., 2016), and the paper also introduces ChatGPT-based document augmentation as a practical replacement for word-level perturbation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete failure modes of contrastive NTMs—low-level feature leakage (e.g., word-count ratios) and the uncontrolled trade-off between reconstruction and generalization—that practitioners encounter when tuning contrastive topic models. The setwise formulation offers a principled way to encourage shared semantic structure without requiring cluster labels or external supervision, and the Pareto optimization removes the need for manual hyperparameter search over the ELBO/contrastive weight. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-VAE topic models (e.g., GAN-based or hyperbolic NTMs), whether the ChatGPT augmentation dependency limits reproducibility, and whether the multi-objective solver's per-step quadratic solve becomes a bottleneck at larger batch sizes or more objectives.
