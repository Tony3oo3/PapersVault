## Problem

Injective flows (bottleneck normalizing flows) jointly learn a data manifold and a distribution on it, but are limited by two constraints: they require restrictive architectures (coupling blocks, invertible functions) to compute the change-of-variables term, and naively optimizing maximum likelihood in the presence of a bottleneck leads to divergent curvature in the decoder manifold. This paper lifts both architectural and training-stability constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241676/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is a bottleneck autoencoder with a free-form convolutional encoder-decoder pair (e.g., d=64 latent for CelebA with D=12288 data, d=16 for MNIST with D=784). At inference, samples are generated by drawing z from a standard normal or a GMM fit to the training latent codes and passing through the decoder. The architecture imposes no invertibility or coupling-block constraints, allowing off-the-shelf convolutional layers with fully-connected latent-space layers.

## Method

The free-form injective flow (FIF) replaces the iterative conjugate-gradient estimator of rectangular flows with a single-step surrogate that uses the encoder Jacobian f'(x) as an approximation of the Moore-Penrose inverse of the decoder Jacobian, reducing the log-determinant gradient to two vector-Jacobian/Jacobian-vector products per Hutchinson sample. A key theoretical insight is that evaluating the encoder Jacobian at the data point x (off-manifold) rather than at the projected point x̂ (on-manifold) compensates for volume distortion caused by manifold curvature, preventing the divergent-curvature pathology identified in the paper. The per-sample loss combines the modified NLL term with a weighted reconstruction loss (β‖x̂−x‖²), and the trace is computed in latent space (d×d rather than D×D) with orthogonalized Gaussian noise to reduce estimator variance. Training requires roughly 1.5–2× the wall-clock time of a reconstruction-only autoencoder, independent of latent dimension.

## Results

On the Pythae benchmark (CelebA), FIF achieves SOTA FID of 62.3 (ResNet + standard normal sampling), 47.3 (ConvNet + GMM), and 55.0 (ResNet + GMM), outperforming all 15 compared generative autoencoders in those settings. On CelebA injective flow comparison, FIF reduces FID from 55.6 (DNF) and 56.2 (Trumpet) to 47.3 with a standard normal sampler, and from 52.7/47.7 to 37.4 with a GMM sampler. On tabular data, FIF outperforms rectangular flows on 3 of 4 datasets (e.g., MINIBOONE: 0.598 vs 1.001 FID-like metric) while training 1.5–6.1× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIF outperforms rectangular flows (Caterini et al., 2021) on 3 of 4 tabular datasets in FID-like metric (POWER: 0.041 vs 0.083; HEPMASS: 0.541 vs 0.779; MINIBOONE: 0.598 vs 1.001) while training 1.5–6.1× faster, though it loses on GAS (0.281 vs 0.110). On CelebA, FIF significantly outperforms both DNF (Horvat & Pfister, 2021) and Trumpet (Kothari et al., 2021) in FID under equal wall-clock budget: 47.3 vs 55.6/56.2 (N sampler) and 37.4 vs 52.7/47.7 (GMM sampler). In the broader Pythae benchmark, FIF achieves the best FID among all 15 compared generative autoencoders (VAE, IWAE, β-VAE, FactorVAE, VQVAE, WAE, RAE, etc.) on three of four architecture/sampler combinations, with the only non-winning setting (ConvNet+N, FID 56.9) still competitive with the best baseline (FactorVAE at 53.8). FIF also maintains high Inception Scores (1.7–2.1), indicating good sample diversity comparable to or exceeding baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the injective flow framework (Brehmer & Cranmer, 2020; Kothari et al., 2021) by removing the restrictive coupling-block architectures that were originally designed for tractable change-of-variables in full-dimensional normalizing flows. It builds directly on the rectangular flow estimator (Caterini et al., 2021) for the log-determinant gradient but replaces the iterative conjugate gradient with a single-step surrogate that exploits the encoder as an approximate Moore-Penrose inverse of the decoder. The key conceptual departure is identifying that naive maximum likelihood training with a bottleneck is ill-defined due to diverging curvature in nonlinear models, and proposing a simple fix (evaluating the encoder Jacobian off-manifold) that was not present in prior injective flow work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes free-form injective flows practical by reducing training cost to roughly 1.5–2× a reconstruction-only autoencoder, while removing architectural constraints that limited expressivity. The identification of the diverging-curvature pathology is a genuine theoretical contribution: it shows that the standard justification for joint manifold-and-likelihood training breaks down in nonlinear models in a way that reconstruction loss alone cannot fix. Open questions include whether the off-manifold Jacobian fix is the right principled correction (the authors acknowledge it is heuristic), why latent distributions are not perfectly Gaussian (GMM fitting helps), and whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or more complex data beyond the tested image and tabular benchmarks.
