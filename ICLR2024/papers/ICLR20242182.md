## Problem

Existing graph explainability methods rely on class labels and decision boundaries, making them inapplicable to unsupervised node representation models like GraphSAGE and DGI. While a few prior works attempt to explain embedding vectors, none provide counterfactual explanations—identifying minimal edge perturbations that would change a node's learned representation. This paper fills that gap by defining counterfactual reasoning in unsupervised settings through changes in top-k nearest neighbors in the embedding space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242182/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained unsupervised embedding model (e.g., GraphSAGE or DGI), the input graph with edge weights, a target node v, and a hyperparameter k specifying the number of nearest neighbors. It produces a sparse counterfactual explanation subgraph (a set of edges whose removal or weakening changes the top-k neighbors of v) along with an Importance score in [0,1] quantifying the fraction of top-k neighbors displaced. The perturbed graph is constructed by zeroing edge weights corresponding to the explanation subgraph, and kNN is computed efficiently via LSH hashing over Euclidean distances in the embedding space.

## Method

UNR-Explainer formulates the counterfactual explanation as an optimization: find the smallest subgraph whose removal maximizes the Importance (fraction of top-k neighbors changed). It searches over subgraphs using a tailored Monte Carlo Tree Search where each tree node corresponds to a graph node, actions add edges to the candidate subgraph, and the reward is the Importance score. The key innovation is a restart-based selection policy inspired by Random Walk with Restart: with probability p_restart, the search returns to the root and explores a non-optimal branch, counteracting the depth-first bias of vanilla MCTS (formally shown in Theorem 1). The UCB formula uses the maximum observed reward rather than the mean, and expansion samples child nodes to match the graph's average degree to control the search space. The algorithm terminates when Importance reaches 1.0 or after T iterations, then selects the minimum-size subgraph among those achieving maximum Importance.

## Results

On real-world datasets, UNR-Explainer achieves the highest Validity (0.911 on Cora, 0.778 on CiteSeer, 0.847 on PubMed), Importance (0.960, 0.910, 0.903), and Probability of Necessity (0.051, 0.054, 0.034) across all three citation networks, while maintaining competitive subgraph sizes (3.8, 2.8, 3.7 edges). On synthetic datasets with ground truth, UNR-Explainer reaches Importance of 1.000 on BA-Shapes and Tree-Cycles and 0.994 on Tree-Grids, with the highest Recall (0.288, 0.324, 0.519) among all baselines. For clustering impact, UNR-Explainer reduces top-k neighbor homogeneity the most (ΔHmg of 0.551, 0.591, 0.454 on Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed), indicating the strongest counterfactual effect on downstream clustering assignments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNR-Explainer substantially outperforms both prior unsupervised explanation methods. Against Taxonomy induction (Liu et al., 2018), which provides global cluster-based explanations, UNR-Explainer achieves 0.911 vs. 0.243 Validity and 0.960 vs. 0.548 Importance on Cora, while using a smaller subgraph (3.8 vs. 9.7 edges); the gap is even more dramatic on PubMed where Taxonomy induction scores only 0.035 Validity and 0.061 Importance. Against TAGE (Xie et al., 2022), which identifies subgraphs via mutual information with embeddings, UNR-Explainer achieves 0.960 vs. 0.482 Importance and 0.911 vs. 0.232 Validity on Cora, and 0.903 vs. 0.268 Importance on PubMed, while TAGE produces much larger subgraphs (11.8 edges on PubMed vs. 3.7 for UNR-Explainer). Among the simpler baselines, 1hop-3N achieves the highest Precision on synthetic datasets (0.943 on BA-Shapes) due to the small ground-truth motif size, but its Importance (0.659 on Cora) and Validity (0.428) are far below UNR-Explainer. The RW-G w/ Restart baseline, which shares the restart concept, improves over plain RW-G but still trails UNR-Explainer by a wide margin (e.g., 0.614 vs. 0.960 Importance on Cora), confirming that the full MCTS formulation with the new selection policy is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the counterfactual explanation paradigm from supervised graph neural networks (CF-GNNExplainer, RCExplainer, CF²) to the unsupervised node representation setting, where no class labels or decision boundaries exist. It builds on MCTS as a subgraph search framework (Sutton & Barto, 2018; Świechowski et al., 2022) and draws inspiration from Random Walk with Restart (Pan et al., 2004) for its novel restart-based selection policy. The key conceptual departure from prior unsupervised explanation work (TAGE, Taxonomy Induction) is the formalization of counterfactual property via k-nearest-neighbor set changes in embedding space rather than mutual information or hierarchical clustering, making the explanation directly tied to downstream tasks like link prediction and clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap in the XAI literature by providing the first principled counterfactual explanation framework for unsupervised graph embedding models, with a formal definition (k-NN set difference) that connects explanations to practical downstream tasks. The theoretical analysis of vanilla MCTS expressiveness limitations (Theorem 1) and the restart policy that addresses them offer a reusable insight for other MCTS-based subgraph search problems. Limitations include evaluation only on GraphSAGE and DGI with relatively small graphs (up to ~20K nodes), a single-layer GraphSAGE assumption in the upper-bound analysis, and the reliance on a tuned restart probability; open questions include scalability to industrial-scale graphs, applicability to multi-hop or heterogeneous GNNs, and whether the k-NN-based counterfactual definition generalizes beyond mean-aggregation architectures.
