## Problem

Existing data pruning methods for training acceleration suffer from two critical issues: static pruning (e.g., EL2N, Influence) requires expensive pre-training trials with O(MNT) overhead and introduces gradient expectation bias through hard sample removal, while dynamic pruning (e.g., UCB, ε-greedy) still uses deterministic hard pruning that biases the gradient and incurs O(log N) sorting overhead per epoch. No prior method achieves truly lossless training speedup with negligible per-sample overhead on large-scale datasets.

## Input / Output

InfoBatch is a plug-and-play wrapper around any existing training pipeline: it consumes the same inputs as the base task (RGB images with class labels, pixel-level segmentation masks, or text instruction pairs) and requires no modification to model architecture, data format, or resolution. The only additional signal used is the per-sample loss computed during forward propagation, which is already available at zero extra cost. The output is a trained model matching full-data quality while consuming 20–40% fewer overall node-hours, with the method's per-sample overhead being O(1) (a single mean comparison) versus O(log N) for sorting-based baselines.

## Method

InfoBatch achieves unbiased dynamic data pruning through three coupled operations. First, soft pruning: at each epoch, samples whose loss falls below the running mean are randomly pruned with probability r (default 0.5) rather than deterministically discarded, introducing stochasticity that eliminates the systematic bias of hard pruning. Second, expectation rescaling: the gradients of surviving low-loss samples are scaled up by 1/(1−r), which the paper proves makes the rescaled training objective a constant multiple of the original full-dataset objective (Eq. 6), thereby preserving the gradient expectation direction. Third, annealing: in the final (1−δ) fraction of epochs (δ=0.875), training reverts to the full dataset to eliminate residual bias from the reduced update count. The score for each sample is simply its most recent loss, updated only for samples that survive pruning, giving O(1) per-sample complexity with no sorting required.

## Results

On CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-18, InfoBatch achieves lossless accuracy (95.1% / 78.1% at 50% pruning) while saving 40% overall cost, outperforming all 14 static and dynamic baselines. On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50, InfoBatch reaches 76.5% top-1 accuracy (vs. 76.4% full data) at 40% pruning ratio with only 0.0028h overhead. For MAE pretraining (ViT-Base), InfoBatch saves 24.8% cost with 82.9% accuracy (vs. 82.8% original); for Latent Diffusion on FFHQ, it saves 27% cost while improving FID from 7.83 to 7.70.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 30% pruning, InfoBatch is the only method achieving lossless performance on both CIFAR-10 (95.6%) and CIFAR-100 (78.2%); the best static method (EL2N-20) drops 1.0% on CIFAR-100, and the best dynamic method (UCB/Random*) drops 0.9%. At 50% pruning, the gap widens: InfoBatch retains 78.1% on CIFAR-100 while UCB and Random* fall to 75.3% (−2.9%), and static methods like EL2N-20 drop to 72.1% (−6.1%). On ImageNet-1K (Table 2), InfoBatch achieves 76.5% accuracy matching full-data training (76.4%) while UCB reaches only 75.8% and GC (submodular) reaches 73.6%; critically, InfoBatch's overhead is 0.0028h versus >24h for GC and >17.5h for EL2N-20, making it the only method practical at scale. For LLaMA-7B instruction fine-tuning (Table 9), combining InfoBatch with DQ (dataset quantization) achieves the same average benchmark score (26.3) as DQ alone but in 14.4 min vs. 18.0 min, demonstrating compatibility with static compression. Across optimizers (SGD, AdamW, LARS, LAMB), InfoBatch consistently saves ~41% cost with zero accuracy loss, confirming architecture- and optimizer-agnostic robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoBatch extends the dynamic data pruning line initiated by Raju et al. (2021), which prunes low-loss samples during training to reduce iterations. The key departure is replacing deterministic (hard) pruning with randomized (soft) pruning and adding an expectation-rescaling step that multiplies the gradients of retained low-loss samples by 1/(1−r), making the gradient expectation approximately unbiased relative to full-dataset training. This connects conceptually to importance sampling in stochastic optimization (Zhao & Zhang, 2014; Csiba & Richtárik, 2016), where resampling weights correct for non-uniform sampling distributions. Unlike static pruning methods (EL2N, Forgetting, GraNd) that require O(MNT) pre-training scoring overhead, InfoBatch operates with O(1) per-sample cost using only the loss already computed during forward propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a trivially simple O(1)-per-sample operation yields 20–40% overall cost savings with negligible overhead (seconds vs. hours for sorting-based methods), making lossless acceleration feasible on very large datasets like ImageNet-22K or JFT-300M where prior dynamic pruning's O(log N) sorting becomes prohibitive. The demonstrated compatibility with LARS/LAMB (1.67× additional speedup), MixUp/CutMix, and coreset selection (10× speedup on LLaMA instruction tuning) suggests InfoBatch can be stacked with other efficiency techniques. Key limitations include reliance on multi-epoch training (inapplicable to single-epoch regimes like GPT-3 or ViT-22B) and a noted caveat that removing samples may introduce subtle biases in model predictions on ethically sensitive datasets, though no concrete evidence of such bias was found.
