## Problem

Foundation models like SAM and DINO fail at annotation-free instance segmentation because they cannot discern boundaries between individual objects: DINO cannot separate similar, closely-positioned instances, while SAM produces many edge-oriented masks but cannot distinguish which edges are true object boundaries versus internal edges (e.g., between an elephant's head and ear). This paper identifies this boundary-discrimination failure as the core bottleneck and shows that CLIP's intermediate-layer features, when clustered, uniquely reveal instance-level object boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240503/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed at 2048×2048 for superior clustering resolution) and a set of open-vocabulary text category names as input, requiring no instance annotations or in-domain labels. It produces per-instance segmentation masks, bounding boxes, and class assignments with confidence scores. The pipeline is training-free in its base form, though optional self-training (using Cascade Mask R-CNN) and label-efficient fine-tuning (1%–30% of COCO) can further boost performance.

## Method

Zip follows a classification-first-then-discovery pipeline that synergistically combines CLIP and SAM. First, CLIP's patch-level activation map provides rough semantic regions for each target category. Second, K-Means clustering is applied to CLIP's second-to-last layer features with a semantic-aware initialization that places cluster centers near the edges between active and inactive regions (via a Gaussian kernel on distance to the activation bounding box), causing object boundaries to emerge as distinct clusters. Third, instance segmentation is reformulated as a fragment-selection problem: connected components of the clustering are scored by a boundary metric (bounding-box-area-to-fragment-area ratio) and activation alignment, and non-boundary fragments are grouped into individual objects. Finally, SAM is prompted in two steps (bounding boxes, then center points) to refine each object's mask to pixel-level precision. The key insight is that CLIP's ResNet-based intermediate layer retains instance-level boundary information lost in both shallower and deeper layers, a property absent in DINOv2's ViT-based features.

## Results

On COCO val2017 annotation-free instance segmentation, Zip (SAM-B) without self-training achieves 11.3 AP and 19.0 AP50 in the class-agnostic setting, surpassing SAM-H (6.6 AP) by +8.7% AP and the previous SOTA CutLER (9.7 AP) by +1.6% AP. With self-training, Zip further improves to 15.1 AP (+12.5% over SAM-H) and 18.2 AP in the class-aware setting (+15.2% over CLIP+SAM-H). In open-vocabulary detection on COCO, Zip without any annotation achieves 27.1 AP50novel, comparable to ViLD (27.6) and Detic (27.8) which use base-category annotations, and reaches 40.3 AP50novel after self-training, approaching CORA+ (43.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Zip decisively outperforms SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023) on AP in both class-agnostic (+8.7% over SAM-H) and class-aware settings, confirming that SAM's edge-oriented segmentation alone is insufficient for instance-level tasks. Against DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023), Zip achieves 11.3 AP vs. 0.8 AP (class-agnostic), a 14× improvement, demonstrating that DINOv2's features lack instance-level boundary information. Zip surpasses the previous SOTA CutLER (Wang et al., 2023) by +1.6% AP without self-training and +5.4% AP with self-training in the class-agnostic setting, and by +9.7% AP in the class-aware setting. In the class-aware comparison, Zip (11.8 AP) substantially outperforms CLIP+SAM (Li et al., 2023) at 3.0–4.2 AP, validating the classification-first-then-discovery pipeline. For open-vocabulary detection (Table 2), Zip without any annotation (27.1 AP50novel) matches or slightly exceeds ViLD (27.6), RegionCLIP (26.8), and approaches Detic (27.8), all of which use base-category annotations; with self-training, Zip reaches 40.3, approaching CORA+ (43.1). On Pascal VOC (Table 8), Zip improves over CutLER by +5.6% AP (class-agnostic) and +11.1% AP (class-aware) without self-training, and outperforms LOST (Siméoni et al., 2021) and FreeSOLO (Wang et al., 2022) by large margins under self-training. Against weakly supervised semantic segmentation methods (Table 9), Zip (27.3 AP50) far exceeds GroupViT+SAM (5.5) and CLIP Surgery+SAM (4.7), confirming that CLIP's boundary clustering is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of unsupervised object discovery built on DINO's self-attention foreground masks (CutLER, TokenCut, LOST, FreeSOLO), which the authors diagnose as fundamentally unable to discriminate between individual instances. It also moves beyond the naive CLIP+SAM combination (CLIP Surgery, F-VLM) that prompts SAM directly from CLIP's activation map. The key conceptual departure is the discovery that CLIP's intermediate ResNet layer, when clustered with semantic-aware K-Means initialization, delineates instance-level object boundaries — a property absent from both DINO (which only captures outer salient edges) and SAM (which segments all edges indiscriminately). The proposed 'classification-first-then-discovery' pipeline inverts the conventional discovery-then-classification order to mitigate CLIP's susceptibility to in-context misclassification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of CLIP's intermediate-layer boundary property is a potentially reusable observation for any dense prediction task requiring instance discrimination without annotations, and the fragment-selection reformulation provides a clean, annotation-free mechanism for grouping superpixel-like fragments into instances. However, the boundary property appears specific to ResNet-based CLIP variants (not ViT), the pipeline involves multiple interacting components (semantic-aware initialization, boundary scoring, two-step SAM prompting), and a substantial gap to fully supervised methods persists even after self-training. Open questions include whether the boundary property can be elicited in transformer-based vision-language models, how to better exploit SAM's segmentation capacity without the 'object-level concept' deficiency the authors identify, and whether multi-round self-training can escape the sub-optimal trapping they observe.
