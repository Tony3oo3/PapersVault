## Problem

Existing dynamic sparse training methods either rely on gradient information from missing links (defeating the purpose of true sparsity) or regrow links randomly, and none have demonstrated that ultra-sparse (~1% connectivity) topologies can actually surpass fully connected networks in performance. This paper launches the 'ultra-sparse advantage challenge' to test whether brain-inspired, topology-guided sparse training can achieve a genuine learning advantage at extreme sparsity levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241805/cropped_page5_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard image inputs (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/EMNIST/Fashion-MNIST, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 RGB for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 RGB for ImageNet) and outputs class labels. Unusually, the method operates only on the final fully connected layers of CNNs (or the entire MLP), where the network topology itself is a dynamic variable: hidden layer dimensions are set at 1× or 2× the input dimension but can automatically shrink via node percolation (down to ~30% of original neurons on simpler tasks), and only 1% of inter-layer links are maintained throughout training.

## Method

CHT (Cannistraci-Hebb Training) is a 4-step methodology whose core is Epitopological Sparse Meta-deep Learning (ESML): instead of random or gradient-based link regrowth, ESML uses the CH3-L3 network automaton rule (a brain-inspired link predictor that scores missing links by summing over length-3 paths weighted by external local community links) to select which new connections to add, operating entirely gradient-free. The full pipeline comprises: (1) Correlated Sparse Topological Initialization using Pearson correlations between input features, (2) a variance-preserving Sparse Weight Initialization adapted for arbitrary sparsity, (3) iterative ESML evolution that prunes low-weight links, percolates inactive neurons, and regrows top-scored links via CH3-L3, and (4) an early-stop mechanism that halts topological evolution per layer once the overlap between removed and regrown links exceeds 90%, then refines weights on the stabilized topology. This process transforms random initial topologies into hyperbolic, power-law (γ≈2), community-organized 'meta-deep' structures that the authors show enable 1%-sparse networks to outperform fully connected baselines on VGG16, GoogLeNet, ResNet50, and ResNet152.

## Results

With only 1% of links retained during training, CHT surpasses fully connected (FC) networks on all six evaluated dataset-architecture combinations (VGG16-TinyImageNet, GoogLeNet-CIFAR100, GoogLeNet-TinyImageNet, ResNet50-CIFAR100, ResNet50-ImageNet, ResNet152-CIFAR100). For example, on GoogLeNet-CIFAR100 (1×), CHT achieves 77.52% accuracy vs. 76.64% for FC, and on ResNet50-CIFAR100 (1×), CHT reaches 79.45% vs. 78.13% for FC. CHT also consistently achieves the highest Area Across Epochs (AAE), indicating faster convergence than both sparse baselines and the dense counterpart. This ultra-sparse advantage (1% connectivity beating 100% connectivity) is the paper's central empirical claim.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHT outperforms all three baselines (SET, RigL, MEST) and the fully connected counterpart on every one of the six dataset-architecture combinations at 1% sparsity. Against SET (random regrowth), ESML/CHT shows consistent accuracy gains of 0.5–3.5% and substantially higher AAE across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, EMNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 (Figure 1). Against RigL (gradient-based regrowth), CHT leads by 0.1–3.4% accuracy; for instance, on GoogLeNet-CIFAR100 CHT reaches 77.52% vs. RigL's 74.12%, and on ResNet50-CIFAR100 CHT achieves 79.45% vs. RigL's 77.34%. Against MEST (weight+gradient removal, random regrowth), CHT leads by 0.1–2.3%; on GoogLeNet-TinyImageNet CHT scores 53.28% vs. MEST's 51.58%. Critically, while MEST occasionally matches or slightly exceeds FC (e.g., 51.47% vs. 51.34% on VGG16-TinyImageNet), CHT is the only method that consistently and significantly surpasses FC across all six tests, establishing the ultra-sparse advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends dynamic sparse training (DST) by replacing the standard random (SET) or gradient-based (RigL, MEST) link regrowth with a brain-inspired, gradient-free topology predictor (CH3-L3) derived from the Cannistraci-Hebb network automata theory. It builds directly on the CH3-L3 link prediction rule (Muscoloni et al., 2020) and the broader epitopological learning framework (Cannistraci, 2018), which posits that local community structure in sparse networks encodes sufficient information to predict future connectivity. The method also departs from prior DST by introducing network percolation (adaptive node removal) and a correlated sparse topological initialization (CSTI) that leverages input feature correlations, going beyond the purely weight-magnitude or gradient-magnitude criteria used in SET, RigL, and MEST.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that 1% connectivity can match or slightly surpass fully connected networks on VGG16, GoogLeNet, ResNet50, and ResNet152—would, if robust, represent a significant efficiency milestone for deep learning. The emergent percolation phenomenon (automatically reducing hidden layers to ~30% of original neurons while preserving accuracy) is a genuinely novel observation that connects sparse training to parsimony principles. However, the margins over fully connected baselines are small (typically 0.5–1.5% accuracy), evidence is limited to 3 random seeds and a restricted set of architecture-dataset pairs, and the unstructured sparsity pattern remains difficult to deploy efficiently on current GPU hardware. Open questions include whether the ultra-sparse advantage persists at larger scale, whether the percolation mechanism can be controlled a priori, and whether the CH3-L3 predictor generalizes beyond the bipartite sandwich-layer structure it was designed for.
