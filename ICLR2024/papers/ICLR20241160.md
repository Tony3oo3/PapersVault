## Problem

Supervised detectors of machine-generated text are brittle to distribution shifts from newer LLMs, new topics, and new domains, and prior zero-shot methods require access to the generating model's predictive distribution. This paper asks whether writing-style representations learned entirely from human-authored text can detect and attribute text from LLMs never seen during training, given only a handful of example documents from each model of concern.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241160/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a support set of N short documents (~128 tokens each, truncated at sentence boundaries) known to originate from a specific LLM, and a query set of similarly sized documents to classify. The style representation f maps each document collection to a fixed-dimensional vector, and the cosine similarity between support and query vectors yields a detection score that can be thresholded or calibrated via Platt scaling. In the multi-target variant, the minimum similarity across all LLM support samples determines whether the query is machine-generated, and the argmax identifies the source model.

## Method

The core mechanism is a RoBERTa-based style representation (UAR) trained with supervised contrastive learning on Reddit posts from 5 million authors, where same-author documents at different times form positive pairs and different-author documents form negative pairs, explicitly disentangling invariant stylistic features from topic and domain. For few-shot detection, the model computes f(support documents) and f(query documents) and scores their cosine similarity, requiring no access to the LLM's predictive distribution and no large corpus of its outputs. Two fine-tuning variants are explored: a multi-LLM variant that continues training on a mix of human and LLM-generated politics posts (treating each LLM as a distinct author), and a multi-domain variant augmented with Twitter and StackExchange data. The key finding is that features effective at distinguishing among human authors transfer to distinguishing human from machine authors and among different LLMs, outperforming Prototypical Networks, MAML, SBERT, CISR, and zero-shot baselines in pAUC at low false-alarm rates.

## Results

On the single-target detection task (Table 1), the proposed UAR style representation trained on 5M Reddit authors achieves a pAUC of 0.905 with N=5 documents per episode and 0.9806 with N=10, substantially outperforming all few-shot and zero-shot baselines. The multi-domain UAR variant (Reddit + Twitter + StackExchange) reaches 0.886/0.9676, while the multi-LLM variant (fine-tuned on AAC data) reaches 0.877/0.9400. In the multiple-target setting (Figure 2b), the multi-LLM UAR variant performs best, and in the paraphrase-robustness experiment (§4.6), including a paraphrased support sample mitigates the performance drop caused by DIPPER paraphrasing attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAR (Reddit 5M) outperforms the closest few-shot baseline, ProtoNet, by 3.4 pAUC points at N=5 (0.905 vs. 0.871) and 3.3 points at N=10 (0.9806 vs. 0.9475), despite ProtoNet having 40M more parameters. Against the other style representation, CISR (Wegmann et al., 2022), UAR leads by 6.6 points at N=5 (0.905 vs. 0.839). The gap widens dramatically against fast-adaptation (MAML: 0.662) and semantic-embedding (SBERT: 0.621) baselines, with UAR exceeding them by over 28 and 24 pAUC points respectively at N=5. Among zero-shot methods, LogRank (0.7640) is the strongest but still trails UAR by 14 points; the OpenAI AI Detector (both custom and off-the-shelf) and the Rank/Entropy metrics all fall below 0.77. In the watermarking comparison (Appendix E, Figure 5), the proposed few-shot approach matches or exceeds the statistical watermark test at ≥48 tokens and is considerably more robust to DIPPER paraphrase attacks, where the watermark detector degrades sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes authorship attribution representations—specifically UAR (Rivera-Soto et al., 2021), a RoBERTa-based model trained with the contrastive strategy of Andrews and Bishop (2019)—for the task of detecting machine-generated text. It departs from prior detection approaches (OpenAI's AI Detector, Zellers et al. 2019, Mitchell et al. 2023) that require supervised training on specific LLM outputs or direct access to model predictive distributions, instead leveraging the insight that features distinguishing human authors from one another also separate humans from machines. The few-shot metric-learning framing (cosine similarity between support and query style embeddings) is positioned as an alternative to both supervised classifiers and watermarking, requiring neither large LLM training corpora nor model internals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that this approach enables detection of novel LLMs with only a handful of examples, without access to the generating model, addressing a key limitation of prior supervised detectors that must be retrained as new models appear. The finding that human-only style representations generalize to machine detection is well-evidenced across Llama-2, ChatGPT, GPT-4, and multiple domains, and the paraphrase-robustness result (using paraphrased support samples) offers a concrete defense against evasion. Open questions include scalability to a much larger ecosystem of LLMs, extension to low-resource languages, and whether more sophisticated adversarial attacks (beyond DIPPER paraphrasing) can defeat style-based detection.
