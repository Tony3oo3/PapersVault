## Problem

Autoregressive surrogate models for 3D turbulent flow simulation face an inescapable dilemma: resolving small-scale vortices requires very small time steps, but producing realistic turbulent states demands hundreds of roll-out steps that accumulate catastrophic error, or a known turbulent initial state that itself requires a costly numerical solver. This paper reframes 3D turbulence simulation as a generative task, directly learning the distribution of all possible turbulent flow states without any initial condition or sequential roll-out.

## Input / Output

The model takes only a cell type tensor T (a K-valued label per grid cell encoding walls, inlets, outlets, and free space) as input—no initial velocity or pressure field is required. It outputs a 4-channel 3D volume (three velocity components plus pressure) on a 192×48×48 regular grid representing a 0.4 m × 0.1 m × 0.1 m channel at Re = 2×10⁵. Boundary cells are never generated by the model; their values are fixed by the Dirichlet conditions throughout sampling, so the model only synthesizes the interior flow field.

## Method

TurbDiff is a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) with N=500 steps and a log-linear signal-to-noise-ratio schedule, parameterized by a 3D U-Net (4 downsampling levels) with a transformer block using flash attention at the coarsest scale for global communication. The key architectural adaptation is an inpainting-style mask: during both training and sampling, cells with boundary conditions are sampled from the true posterior q(X_{n-1}|X_0, X_n) using their known Dirichlet values, while free cells are denoised by the learned p_θ, ensuring boundary values remain fixed throughout the reverse process. The model is trained by minimizing the KL-divergence between the learned and true posterior on turbulent flow states only (discarding the first 25 ms of each simulation), learning the marginal distribution p(X|B) directly. This leverages the ergodicity of turbulence—once fully developed, the flow distribution is independent of the initial state—allowing the model to generate realistic turbulent snapshots from pure noise conditioned solely on geometry.

## Results

On the 3D Turbulence Dataset (9 unseen test geometries), TurbDiff achieves W₂,TKE = 3.9 ± 0.4 and W₂,ℛ = 1.38 ± 0.04, closely matching the strongest baseline DilResNet-22 (W₂,TKE = 2.15 ± 0.06, W₂,ℛ = 1.240 ± 0.001) which requires a solver-generated initial state. In the full-surrogate setting without any solver input, TurbDiff (W₂,TKE = 3.9) dramatically outperforms DilResNet-init (W₂,TKE = 60 ± 47) and TF-Net-init (diverged). TurbDiff generates a sample in ~20.6 s on an A100, a 30× speedup over the ~10-minute numerical solver, while the DilResNet-22 pipeline requires 602 s of solver time plus 1.58 s of model inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TurbDiff is compared against two autoregressive architectures (TF-Net and DilResNet), each evaluated in two settings: -22 (starts from a true solver-generated turbulent sample and rolls out 22 steps) and -init (starts from the pre-turbulent solver initial state and rolls out 200 steps). Against the strongest baseline DilResNet-22, TurbDiff is slightly worse on W₂,TKE (3.9 vs. 2.15) and W₂,ℛ (1.38 vs. 1.240), but critically does not require the 602 s of numerical solver time to produce the initial state. In the full-surrogate -init setting where no solver is used, TurbDiff is dramatically superior: W₂,TKE of 3.9 vs. 60 ± 47 for DilResNet-init, and W₂,ℛ of 1.38 vs. 4.6 × 10⁸, while TF-Net-init diverged entirely. The RMSE for estimating the peak-TKE location behind the object is 5.9 cells (~1.2 cm) for TurbDiff versus 42 ± 23 cells for DilResNet-init, confirming that TurbDiff produces samples with physically meaningful turbulence structure usable for downstream engineering tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant autoregressive surrogate modeling paradigm for CFD (TF-Net, DilResNet, and related works) by reframing 3D turbulence simulation as a generative task that directly learns the marginal distribution of turbulent flow states, leveraging the ergodicity of turbulence to eliminate the need for initial conditions or long roll-outs. Methodologically, it builds on DDPMs (Ho et al., 2020) with a boundary-condition-guided sampling scheme inspired by the inpainting approach of RePaint (Lugmayr et al., 2022), applied here to Dirichlet boundary conditions on a 3D U-Net with a transformer bottleneck. The closest prior work is Drygala et al. (2022, 2023), who demonstrated that GANs can sample from the correct distribution for ergodic turbulent systems, but limited to 2D slices of 3D data; this paper extends the generative formulation to full 3D volumetric fields with arbitrary object geometries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and resolves a fundamental dilemma in autoregressive CFD surrogates for 3D turbulence: small time steps are needed to resolve small-scale vortices, but the hundreds of steps required to reach fully developed turbulence cause catastrophic error accumulation, making full solver replacement infeasible. By exploiting ergodicity to sample directly from the turbulent state distribution, the generative approach sidesteps this entirely, achieving comparable sample quality to the strongest autoregressive baseline (which itself requires a solver-generated initial state) while being 30× faster than the solver. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (cubic memory growth), whether equivariant architectures could improve sample efficiency, and whether the grid-based geometry representation is sufficient for smooth or complex industrial geometries.
