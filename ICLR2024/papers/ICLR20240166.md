## Problem

Existing Neural Contextual Bandit (NeuCB) algorithms either rely on NTK-based exploration (NeuralUCB, NeuralTS) whose regret degrades to Ω(T) in the worst case, assume i.i.d. contexts (EE-Net), or combine the SquareCB/FastCB reduction with only O(√T) online regression regret, yielding sub-optimal O(√K T^{3/4}) bandit regret. No prior method achieves sub-linear regret for NeuCBs under adversarial contexts without dependence on the NTK effective dimension d̃.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240166/cropped_page8_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

At each of T rounds the learner receives K context vectors x_{t,1},…,x_{t,K} ∈ R^d (with ‖x_{t,a}‖ ≤ 1) that may be chosen adversarially, produces a scalar prediction ŷ_{t,a} for every arm, selects one arm a_t via inverse-gap weighting, and observes only the scalar reward y_{t,a_t} ∈ [0,1] for the pulled arm. The algorithms operate on tabular classification data (six OpenML datasets) where contexts are constructed from feature vectors and rewards are binary (0/1) classification losses.

## Method

The key insight is that adding a small random Rademacher perturbation to the wide-network prediction, f̃(θ,x,ε) = f(θ;x) + c_p Σ_j ((θ−θ_0)^T e_j ε_j)/m^{1/4}, makes the per-step loss simultaneously almost convex, QG-satisfying, and uniquely minimizable—conditions that wide networks alone fail to meet due to non-unique minima. Averaging over S = Θ(log m) i.i.d. Rademacher draws yields a predictor whose average loss is ν-strongly convex (ν = O(1/√m)) while retaining the QG constant μ = O(1), enabling an O(log T) regret bound for projected OGD (Theorem 3.1). This O(log T) online regression regret is then plugged into the SquareCB and FastCB reductions to obtain NeuSquareCB with Õ(√KT) regret and NeuFastCB with the first data-dependent Õ(√L*K + K) regret for NeuCBs, both holding under adversarial contexts and independent of the NTK effective dimension.

## Results

The paper evaluates NeuSquareCB and NeuFastCB on 6 openml classification datasets (Covertype, Fashion, MagicTelescope, Mushroom, Plants, Shuttle) using adversarially chosen contexts, with results averaged over 20 runs. NeuFastCB persistently achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all six datasets, while NeuSquareCB also shows consistently lower regret than all baselines. The baseline algorithms (NeuralUCB, NeuralTS, EE-Net, Neural-ε-greedy) exhibit erratic performance with high variance, whereas both proposed methods show stable, low-variance regret curves. No specific numerical regret values are reported; results are presented as cumulative regret plots (Figure 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuFastCB outperforms all four baselines (NeuralUCB, NeuralTS, EE-Net, and Neural-ε-greedy) on cumulative regret across all six datasets, with the gap being most pronounced on Covertype and Shuttle. NeuSquareCB also consistently beats all baselines but is slightly behind NeuFastCB, consistent with the theoretical prediction that NeuFastCB's first-order bound Õ(√L*K + K) is tighter than NeuSquareCB's Õ(√KT) when L* < T. Beyond empirical gains, the paper proves that NeuralUCB and NeuralTS have Ω(T) worst-case regret under an oblivious adversary (Theorems A.1–A.4), while EE-Net's guarantees require i.i.d. contexts and storing all past networks—neither of which holds in the adversarial evaluation protocol used here. The baselines' high variance in the regret plots further underscores their instability compared to the proposed methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: the reduction from contextual bandits to online regression (SquareCB/FastCB by Foster & Rakhlin 2020, Foster & Krishnamurthy 2021) and the analysis of overparameterized neural networks via the NTK regime (NeuralUCB, NeuralTS, EE-Net). It departs from the NTK-based bandit approach by instead plugging neural online regression into the SquareCB/FastCB reduction, and departs from Chen et al. (2021)'s O(√T) regret (which exploits only almost convexity) by adding a Quadratic Growth condition and unique-minima requirement to achieve O(log T). The key technical departure is a small Rademacher perturbation to the network output that, surprisingly, restores unique minima and QG for the loss, enabling the sharper bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first data-dependent (first-order) regret bound for neural contextual bandits (NeuFastCB: Õ(√(L*K)+K)) and shows that existing NeuralUCB/NeuralTS guarantees degrade to Ω(T) under an oblivious adversary, which is a significant negative result for the NTK-bandit literature. The O(log T) online regression regret under QG is a meaningful theoretical improvement over the O(√T) bound. However, the perturbation mechanism is purely analytical (experiments run without it), the empirical evaluation uses small tabular datasets, and the QG + unique-minima assumptions are restrictive, leaving open whether the O(log T) rate is achievable in practice without the perturbation or for more general loss landscapes.
