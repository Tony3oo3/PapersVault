## Problem

Existing time series generative models rely heavily on GANs, which suffer from training instability and mode collapse, while VAEs—despite being more robust—are surprisingly underexplored for this task. The few VAE-based time series methods that do exist (e.g., TimeVAE, CR-VAE) use non-sequential standard normal priors that fail to capture latent temporal dynamics, and it remains unclear how to incorporate domain knowledge such as stability constraints into their generative process.

## Input / Output

KoVAE accepts multivariate time series of arbitrary length T with d channels per timestep, supporting both regular (equispaced) and irregular (non-equispaced) sampling; for irregular inputs, an NCDE embedding layer first interpolates to a regular grid before encoding. The model optionally accepts geospatial coordinates as conditional variables (via a small MLP) for location-specific weather generation. Beyond the synthetic time series output, KoVAE produces a learned k×k linear Koopman operator A whose eigenvalue spectrum directly encodes the qualitative dynamics (growth, decay, stability) of the generated process.

## Method

KoVAE replaces the standard non-sequential Gaussian prior in a sequential VAE with a novel linear Koopman prior: a GRU produces intermediate latent codes, from which a linear system (analogous to Dynamic Mode Decomposition) is solved to obtain a matrix A that governs the forward propagation z_t = A·z_{t-1}. The training objective combines a reconstruction term, a predictive loss that penalizes the mismatch between the sampled latents and their Koopman-propagated counterparts (enforcing linearity in the prior), and a KL regularization term, yielding a penalized ELBO. For irregularly-sampled data, a Neural Controlled Differential Equation (NCDE) module handles the non-uniform time gaps before the GRU encoder. Domain knowledge is incorporated by adding an eigenvalue penalty L_eig that constrains the modulus of selected eigenvalues of A (e.g., fixing |λ|=1 for stable oscillatory systems), enabling both physics-constrained generation and post-hoc stability analysis via spectral inspection.

## Results

On the regular discriminative task (lower is better), KoVAE achieves 0.005 (Sines), 0.009 (Stocks), 0.143 (Energy), and 0.076 (MuJoCo), yielding an average relative improvement of 58% over the second-best method (GT-GAN). On the regular predictive task, KoVAE reports MAE of 0.093, 0.037, 0.251, and 0.038, matching or approaching the 'Original' (real-data) baseline of 0.094, 0.036, 0.250, and 0.031. In the irregular setting (30–70% observations dropped), KoVAE maintains dominant performance with average relative improvements of 48%, 55%, and 43% on the discriminative task across the three missingness levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KoVAE outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset in the regular discriminative task, with the largest margins against GAN-based methods (TimeGAN, RCGAN, C-RNN-GAN) and forcing-based methods (T-Forcing, P-Forcing), which often score near 0.5 (random chance). The closest competitor is GT-GAN, which is the only other method designed for irregular sampling; KoVAE beats it by 54–75% relative improvement on the regular discriminative task. On the regular predictive task, KoVAE is essentially tied with TimeVAE on Sines (0.093 vs. 0.093) and Stocks (0.037 vs. 0.037), but clearly outperforms it on Energy (0.251 vs. 0.254) and MuJoCo (0.038 vs. 0.039), and substantially outperforms GT-GAN on Energy (0.251 vs. 0.312) and MuJoCo (0.038 vs. 0.055). In the irregular setting, KoVAE dominates overwhelmingly: most GAN and forcing baselines degrade to near-random scores (≈0.5) when 30–70% of observations are removed, while KoVAE maintains discriminative scores below 0.4 even at 70% missingness. The only exceptions where KoVAE does not achieve the best score are predictive Stocks at 50% missingness (GT-GAN: 0.018 vs. KoVAE: 0.019) and discriminative Energy at 70% missingness (GT-GAN: 0.325 vs. KoVAE: 0.392), both narrow margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

KoVAE departs from the dominant GAN-based paradigm for time series generation (TimeGAN, GT-GAN, C-RNN-GAN) by returning to the VAE framework, which the authors note is 'surprisingly' underexplored for this task. Its key architectural departure from existing sequential VAEs (TimeVAE, CR-VAE) is replacing the standard non-sequential Gaussian prior with a linear Koopman-operator prior, drawing on dynamical systems theory (Koopman, 1931; DMD, Schmid 2010) that had previously been applied to analysis and forecasting but not to generative modeling. For irregular data, it integrates NCDE (Kidger et al., 2020) as an embedding layer, similar in spirit to GT-GAN's use of Neural ODEs but within a variational rather than adversarial framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to impose physics constraints directly on the latent dynamics via eigenvalue penalties on the Koopman operator is a genuinely new capability for generative time series models, enabling domain-informed generation and interpretability through spectral analysis. The paper's weather application (ERA5) demonstrates practical relevance for sparse-sensor scenarios, though it acknowledges that fine-scale spatial details are lacking without explicit spatial constraints. Open questions include whether the linear Koopman assumption generalizes to highly nonlinear or chaotic systems, how the method scales to very long sequences, and whether spectral constraints can be automatically inferred rather than manually specified.
