## Problem

Existing QG/A frameworks for text-to-image evaluation suffer from nontrivial reliability issues: generated questions are often non-atomic (conflating multiple semantic details), duplicated, or hallucinated, and VQA modules produce inconsistent answers when dependent questions are asked after a parent question is answered negatively (e.g., 'no motorcycle' followed by 'the motorcycle is blue'). These problems distort the final alignment score and reduce the interpretability that motivates QG/A over single-score metrics like CLIPScore.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a free-form text prompt and a generated RGB image as input. It produces a directed acyclic graph (DAG) of atomic binary yes/no validation questions organized by semantic type (entity, attribute, relation, global), where directed edges encode entailment dependencies between parent and child questions. The final output is a per-question answer vector and an aggregated alignment score (proportion of correct answers), with dependent questions automatically skipped when their parent is answered 'no'. The released DSG-1k benchmark provides 1,060 prompts sourced from 10 diverse datasets, each paired with its pre-generated DSG question set.

## Method

DSG is inspired by Davidsonian formal semantics, which decomposes a sentence's meaning into a set of atomic propositions whose collective truth captures the sentence's full semantics. The prompt is decomposed into a DAG where nodes are atomic semantic tuples (1-tuples for entities/globals, 2-tuples for attributes, 3-tuples for relations) and edges represent entailment dependencies (a child proposition's truth is only evaluable if its parent is true). Generation uses a three-step LLM pipeline—Prompt→Tuples, Tuples→Questions, Tuples→Dependencies—each with task-specific in-context examples, which outperforms a single-step approach by allowing more than 20 examples per stage versus fewer than 10. During VQA validation, the dependency structure ensures that if a parent question receives a negative answer, all child questions in its subgraph are skipped (counted as 'no'), preventing invalid queries. The framework is modular: any LLM can handle the QG stage and any VQA model can handle the QA stage, with the paper's best configuration using PaLM 2 340B for question generation and PaLI 17B for answering.

## Results

DSG+PaLI achieves the best human correlation among all QG/VQA combinations on TIFA160, with Spearman's ρ=0.571 and Kendall's τ=0.458, substantially outperforming TIFA+PaLI (0.431/0.323) and VQ²A+PaLI (0.207/0.157). In question generation quality, DSG achieves 96.5% atomicity and 97.5% uniqueness (manual eval on 30 prompts), compared to TIFA's 78.5%/65.4% and VQ²A's 3.9%/89.5%. For per-question VQA-human matching on DSG-1k, PaLI reaches 73.8% overall accuracy, with strong performance on entity questions (83.0%) but notable weakness on text rendering (13.7%) and style (21.1%). In T2I model evaluation, DSG+PaLI correctly ranks Imagen* ≈ MUSE* > SD v2.1, matching human judgments, though PaLI is overly optimistic (~80% vs. ~60% human accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSG is compared against two prior QG/A baselines: TIFA (Hu et al., ICCV 2023) and VQ²A (Changpinyo et al., NAACL 2022). DSG decisively outperforms both on question quality metrics: atomicity of 96.5% vs. TIFA's 78.5% and VQ²A's 3.9%, and uniqueness of 97.5% vs. TIFA's 65.4% and VQ²A's 89.5% (manual eval, 30 prompts). On the end-to-end correlation metric (Spearman's ρ / Kendall's τ with human Likert ratings on TIFA160), DSG+PaLI achieves 0.571/0.458, beating TIFA+PaLI (0.431/0.323) by a wide margin and VQ²A+PaLI (0.207/0.157) by an even larger one. With mPLUG-large, DSG also leads (0.463/0.380 vs. 0.352/0.259 for TIFA and 0.212/0.164 for VQ²A). The one exception is Instruct-BLIP, where TIFA slightly edges out DSG (0.460/0.360 vs. 0.442/0.364), though the gap is small. In the by-category ablation (Table 11, PaLI), DSG leads on all four TIFA160 data splits (COCO, PaintSkill, PartiPrompts, DrawBench), confirming the advantage is not driven by a single prompt type.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the QG/A evaluation paradigm for text-to-image alignment established by TIFA (Hu et al., 2023) and VQ²A (Yarom et al., 2023), which use LLM-generated questions and VQA models to score prompt-image faithfulness. Its key departure is borrowing the formal-semantic decomposition of Davidson (1965) to structure questions as a directed acyclic graph of atomic propositions with explicit entailment dependencies, addressing reliability failures (non-atomicity, duplication, hallucination, invalid dependent queries) that the authors document in prior QG/A methods. The three-step LLM pipeline (prompt → tuples → questions → dependencies) is a practical instantiation of this idea, and the DSG-1k benchmark extends TIFA160's 160 prompts to 1,060 by sampling from nine additional public datasets covering diverse styles and skills.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DSG-1k benchmark and the dependency-aware question structure provide a more reliable and interpretable evaluation protocol that T2I researchers can adopt immediately, and the per-category VQA-human accuracy breakdown (Table 5) gives a concrete diagnostic map of where current VQA models succeed (entities, color, spatial relations) and fail (text rendering, counting, style, material). A notable open question is that even the strongest VQA model (PaLI) remains overly optimistic relative to human raters (~80% vs. ~60% overall accuracy), and subjectivity/domain-knowledge questions produce disagreement even among human annotators, suggesting that fully automated fine-grained T2I evaluation is not yet achievable. The paper also highlights that scaling VQA training data does not translate into improved performance on visually implicit or higher-order semantic categories, pointing to a fundamental limitation of current vision-language pretraining.
