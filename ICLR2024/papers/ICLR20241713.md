## Problem

Diffusion models require hundreds to thousands of expensive score function evaluations for high-quality sampling, and most prior efficient-sampling work (DDIM, DPM-Solver, DEIS) targets only data-space diffusion models. Augmented diffusion models, which couple data with auxiliary momentum variables and yield better sample quality, remain largely unexplored for fast inference. This paper addresses the gap by developing principled deterministic and stochastic integrators applicable to a broader class of diffusion models, including augmented spaces like PSLD.

## Input / Output

The samplers consume a Gaussian noise sample drawn from the stationary distribution of the forward process and a pre-trained score network parameterized as s_θ(z_t, t) = C_skip(t)z_t + C_out(t)ε_θ(C_in(t)z_t, C_noise(t)). They produce generated images in the data space (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 CelebA/AFHQ) after numerically integrating the reverse probability-flow ODE or SDE over a user-specified number of NFEs (typically 50–100). No retraining or architectural modification is required; the methods are drop-in replacements for the integration step at inference time.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary frameworks. First, Conjugate Integrators apply an invertible affine transformation A_t to project the probability-flow ODE into a space where the dynamics are more amenable to large-step numerical integration; the free parameter B_t generalizes DDIM (B_t=0) and exponential integrators, and a stability analysis (Theorem 2) shows that tuning B_t = λI conditions eigenvalues for better large-step accuracy. Second, Splitting Integrators, adapted from molecular dynamics, decompose the PSLD ODE/SDE into position and momentum subcomponents solved independently and composed in specific orders (e.g., Velocity Verlet, OBA); 'Reduced' variants reuse score evaluations between sub-steps and cancel leading error terms, cutting NFEs per step. The final Conjugate Splitting Integrators apply the conjugate transform within the position-space sub-update of the splitting scheme, achieving FID 2.11 (deterministic) and 2.36 (stochastic) at 100 NFE on CIFAR-10 with PSLD.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed Conjugate Splitting PSLD Sampler (CSPS-D) achieves an FID of 2.11 at 100 NFE and 3.21 at 50 NFE for deterministic sampling, outperforming the best deterministic baselines (DEIS and DPM-Solver-3, both at FID 2.57–2.59 at 100 NFE). For stochastic sampling, the Splitting PSLD Sampler (SPS-S) achieves FID 2.36 at 100 NFE and 2.76 at 50 NFE, beating the best stochastic baseline (SA-Solver, FID 2.63 at 100 NFE). With score network preconditioning, CSPS-D further improves to FID 2.65 at 50 NFE, approaching the performance of DEIS and DPM-Solver-3 in the low-NFE regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For deterministic sampling at 100 NFE on CIFAR-10, CSPS-D (FID 2.11) outperforms all baselines by a clear margin: DEIS (2.57), DPM-Solver-3 (2.59), EDM (3.06), PNDM (3.53), A-DDIM (3.55), and DDIM (4.16). At 50 NFE, CSPS-D without preconditioning (3.21) trails DEIS and DPM-Solver-3 (both 2.59), but with preconditioning (2.65) it becomes competitive with them. For stochastic sampling, SPS-S dominates all baselines at both 50 and 100 NFE: at 100 NFE it achieves FID 2.36 versus SA-Solver (2.63), EDM (2.71), SEEDS-2 (3.19), A-DDPM (4.45), SSCS (4.83), and Euler-Maruyama (7.83). The paper also notes that for higher-resolution datasets (CelebA-64, AFHQ-v2), the conjugate variant CSPS-S outperforms the non-conjugate SPS-S, indicating better scalability. Overall, the proposed samplers achieve the best-reported FID scores for diffusion models in augmented spaces at 100 NFE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies several previously distinct fast-sampling methods for diffusion models—DDIM, DPM-Solver, and DEIS—under a single conjugate integrator framework, showing they are all special cases (B_t = 0) of a more general projected-space ODE solver. It then introduces a second, complementary framework drawn from molecular dynamics: splitting integrators that alternate between position and momentum updates in augmented diffusion models like PSLD. The key departure from prior work is the deliberate extension of efficient sampling from data-space-only diffusions (VP, VE, EDM) to augmented (position-momentum) diffusions, where the coupling structure enables higher-order splitting schemes that were previously underexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, training-free framework for reducing NFE budgets in augmented diffusion models, with theoretical stability analysis (Theorem 2) explaining why the λ-DDIM hyperparameter improves large-step sampling. Open questions include extending the conjugate framework to stochastic samplers, a more principled (non-heuristic) choice of B_t, and understanding the role of the noise-injection parameter λ_s in stochastic splitting. The methods are evaluated primarily on PSLD, so generalizability to other augmented diffusion architectures remains to be established, and the FID improvements over the best baselines (2.11 vs. 2.57 at 100 NFE) are meaningful but not transformative.
