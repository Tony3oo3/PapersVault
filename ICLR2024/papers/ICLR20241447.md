## Problem

Existing convergence bounds for diffusion models without smoothness assumptions on the data distribution scale quadratically in the data dimension d, while linear-in-d bounds require Lipschitz score conditions that exclude many distributions of interest (e.g., those supported on submanifolds). This paper closes the gap by proving the first convergence bounds that are linear in d (up to logarithmic factors) assuming only finite second moments of the target distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a learned score approximation s_θ(x,t) whose time-discretized L² error is bounded by ε_score (Assumption 1), initializes the reverse process from the standard Gaussian π_d rather than the true q_T, and uses a discretization schedule with step sizes γ_k ≤ κ min{1, T−t_{k+1}}. It produces approximate samples from q_δ (the forward noised distribution at early-stopping time δ) and certifies a KL divergence bound of O(ε_score²) between q_δ and the model output p_{t_N}. The data distribution is assumed to have finite second moments and identity covariance, though the analysis extends to general covariance.

## Method

The key technical contribution is a refined treatment of the reverse SDE discretization error using stochastic localization. Rather than bounding the score difference E_{s,t} = E[‖∇log q_{T−t}(Y_t) − ∇log q_{T−s}(Y_s)‖²] via a Lipschitz assumption, the authors derive a differential inequality for E_{s,t} through Itô calculus (Lemmas 3–4), relate its coefficients to the posterior mean m_t and covariance Σ_t via Tweedie's formula (Lemma 5), and then exploit a differential identity from stochastic localization (Lemma 1: (σ_t³/2σ̇_t) d/dt E[Σ_t] = E[Σ_t²]) to bound the coefficients without smoothness assumptions. This yields a discretization error of Õ(κ²dN + κdT), which combined with a Girsanov-based path measure comparison (Lemma 8) and the exponential convergence of the OU forward process gives the final KL bound. The resulting iteration complexity is Õ(d log²(1/δ)/ε²), improving the previous state-of-the-art Õ(d² log²(1/δ)/ε²) by a factor of d.

## Results

The paper establishes the first convergence bound for diffusion models that is linear in the data dimension d (up to logarithmic factors) without any smoothness assumptions on the score. Specifically, Corollary 1 shows that an early-stopped diffusion model requires at most Õ(d·log²(1/δ)/ε²) steps to approximate an arbitrary distribution on ℝᵈ (with finite second moments) to within ε² in KL divergence. This improves the previous state-of-the-art bound of Õ(d²·log²(1/δ)/ε²) from Chen et al. (2023a, Theorem 2) by removing one factor of d. The key technical ingredient is Lemma 1, derived from stochastic localization, which provides a differential inequality controlling the discretization error of the reverse SDE without requiring Lipschitz bounds on the score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against Chen et al. (2023a), whose Theorem 2 provides the previous best bound without smoothness assumptions: Õ(d²·log²(1/δ)/ε²) steps. This work improves that to Õ(d·log²(1/δ)/ε²), removing one factor of d while maintaining the same minimal assumption (finite second moments only). Against Chen et al. (2023d), which achieves Õ(dL²/ε²) in TV distance but requires the score to be L-Lipschitz for all t ≥ 0, this work matches the linear-in-d scaling without any Lipschitz assumption, at the cost of working in KL divergence and requiring early stopping at time δ. The Lipschitz assumption in Chen et al. (2023d) is restrictive because it excludes distributions supported on submanifolds and can hide additional dimension dependence. The paper also notes that a concurrent work by Conforti et al. (2023) achieves similar linear-in-d bounds via a stochastic control approach under a finite Fisher information condition, which can also be removed with early stopping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Girsanov-based convergence analysis of Chen et al. (2023a;d), which established polynomial bounds for diffusion models but required either Lipschitz score assumptions (yielding dL² dependence) or accepted a d² dependence without smoothness. The key technical departure is the introduction of a differential inequality for the discretization error E_{s,t} derived via Itô calculus, whose coefficients are controlled using Lemma 1 — a result translated from the stochastic localization literature (Eldan, 2013; 2020) via the equivalence between diffusion models and stochastic localization processes (Montanari, 2023). This cross-pollination from the KLS conjecture literature is what enables the tighter d-linear bound without any smoothness assumptions on the data distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves an explicit open question raised by Chen et al. (2023a;d) about whether d-linear iteration complexity is achievable without smoothness, and Appendix H shows the linear d-dependence is optimal via a product-distribution argument. However, the paper identifies a fundamental trade-off: achieving better step-size dependence (e.g., O(η²) as in Mou et al., 2022) requires L∞ control on the drift, which reintroduces superlinear d-dependence and smoothness assumptions. Bridging this gap between tight d-scaling and tight η-scaling remains open, as does understanding whether the increasing noise schedule of diffusion models can fundamentally outperform Langevin Monte Carlo in weaker metrics like Wasserstein distance.
