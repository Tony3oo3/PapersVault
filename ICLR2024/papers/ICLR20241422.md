## Problem

Existing VFL benchmarks suffer from a narrow feature-distribution scope: real datasets (OARF, FedAds, NUS-WIDE, Vehicle) predominantly represent balanced, weakly-correlated parties, while synthetic benchmarks resort to arbitrary random or manual splits that only capture a subset of possible feature distributions. This misalignment between the 'uniform scope' of synthetic benchmarks and the 'real scope' of actual VFL deployments leads to inadequate and potentially misleading algorithm performance assessment. The paper identifies two previously uncontrolled factors—party feature importance and inter-party feature correlation—as the key dimensions along which VFL evaluation must be diversified.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a centralized global feature matrix (e.g., 581k×549 for covtype, 10k×20k for realsim) along with two scalar split parameters: α (a symmetric Dirichlet concentration controlling party importance imbalance, where smaller α yields more imbalanced splits) and β ∈ [0,1] (a linear interpolation between minimum and maximum achievable inter-party correlation). The number of parties K (typically 4) and per-party feature counts are also specified. The output is a set of K partitioned local datasets ready for VFL training, plus the computed Pcor and Icor values that characterize the resulting split. For real VFL datasets, the same (α, β) mapping is derived via Shapley-value importance and Icor optimization, enabling direct cross-dataset comparison of feature-split topology.

## Method

The paper decomposes the supervised VFL log-likelihood (Proposition 1) into per-party ratio terms, identifying feature importance (Shapley value or Shapley-CMI) and feature correlation as two orthogonal factors governing VFL performance. For correlation, the authors propose Pcor—the standard deviation of singular values of the cross-party Spearman correlation matrix—which generalizes the multi-way correlation coefficient (mcor) to handle unequal feature counts across parties (Proposition 2 proves Pcor(X,X)=mcor(X,X)). The overall inter-party correlation Icor is defined as the mean relative Pcor change across all party pairs, controlling for inner-party correlation. The importance-based split samples allocation probabilities from a Dirichlet(α₁,…,α_K) distribution and assigns each feature independently (Theorem 2 proves expected party importance ∝ αᵢ). The correlation-based split (Algorithm 1) uses a Biased Random-Key Genetic Algorithm to search over column permutation matrices, targeting an Icor value linearly interpolated between the optimization lower and upper bounds by β. Finally, real VFL datasets are mapped into the same (α, β) space, and the paper validates that synthetic datasets generated with matching (α, β) reproduce the relative algorithm performance rankings observed on real VFL data.

## Results

Across 11 datasets (9 synthetic, 2 real VFL), SplitNN and FedTree exhibit the most stable performance across varying imbalance (α) and correlation (β) settings. C-VFL shows the largest sensitivity, with accuracy drops of up to 10% on epsilon and 40% on letter when α varies; at α=0.1, C-VFL accuracy on letter and epsilon barely exceeds random guessing. GAL benefits from imbalanced splits (8% gain on letter and radar with varying α, 2–5% on others) while FedOnce is affected by α (5–10% on letter, gisette, epsilon) and β (1–3% on covtype, epsilon). The paper further confirms a positive correlation between relative algorithm rankings on VertiBench-synthetic datasets (matched to real α, β) and their rankings on real VFL datasets (NUS-WIDE, Vehicle).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five representative VFL algorithms (SplitNN, C-VFL, FedOnce, FedTree, GAL) across 11 datasets under systematically varied α and β. SplitNN consistently achieves the highest accuracy across most datasets due to its iterative transmission of full representations and gradients, outperforming all other methods in the majority of settings. C-VFL, which compresses representations for communication efficiency, exhibits the worst accuracy and the greatest sensitivity to imbalance (α), dropping to near-random performance at α=0.1 on letter and epsilon. FedTree (split-GBDT) performs competitively on high-dimensional, smaller datasets like gisette but degrades on larger datasets like epsilon and covtype, while GAL (ensemble-based) excels on binary classification and regression but loses significant accuracy as the number of classes increases (e.g., covtype with 7 classes, letter with 26 classes). The key benchmark insight is that no single algorithm dominates across all α–β combinations, underscoring the need for multi-scenario evaluation that VertiBench enables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VFL benchmarking line of work (OARF, FedML, LEAF, FedAds) by identifying two previously uncontrolled factors—feature importance and feature correlation—as the key axes along which synthetic VFL datasets should be systematically varied. It builds directly on Taylor's multi-way correlation coefficient (mcor), generalizing it via Pcor to handle the unequal feature counts inherent in VFL, and on Shapley-value-based feature importance (Shapley-CMI) to drive an importance-based Dirichlet split. The paper explicitly departs from HFL non-IID benchmarks (e.g., NIID-Bench) whose assumptions of independent, uniformly significant instances and a shared feature space are incompatible with VFL's partitioned-feature paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VertiBench provides the first systematic, parameterized framework for generating VFL datasets spanning the full imbalance-correlation space, filling a gap between the narrow 'real scope' of existing public datasets and the 'uniform scope' of random splits. The introduction of the Satellite image-to-image VFL dataset addresses a notable absence in the literature, and the demonstrated positive correlation between synthetic and real VFL performance under matched (α, β) validates the benchmark's ecological relevance. Open questions include whether joint optimization of importance and correlation yields additional insights, how findings extend to asynchronous or semi-federated VFL subtypes, and whether the scalability results hold under realistic communication constraints and privacy mechanisms (encryption, noise) that the current benchmark excludes.
