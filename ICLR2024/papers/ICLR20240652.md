## Problem

Prompt-tuning for continual learning achieves strong empirical anti-forgetting performance, but its underlying mechanism remains unexplained, and no rigorous theoretical guarantee exists. Meanwhile, gradient projection methods provide provable anti-forgetting guarantees but require task identifiers at inference, making them inapplicable to class-incremental settings. This paper bridges the gap by showing that prompt-tuning's instance-wise query mechanism eliminates the need for task identifiers, while gradient projection supplies the missing theoretical foundation for prompt-based continual learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-R and CUB-200) and produces a class label via a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone with a lightweight classifier head. During training, a prompt pool of query-key pairs selects instance-specific prompts that are prepended to the image embedding sequence; during inference, no task identifier or exemplar buffer is required. The only additional storage is the projection matrix (≤1 MB), which is updated per task via SVD on sampled features.

## Method

The key insight is deriving the anti-forgetting condition for prompt gradients through the ViT self-attention mechanism: to preserve old-task outputs, the prompt update Δp must satisfy x_t Δp^T = 0 and p_t Δp^T = 0, meaning the gradient must be orthogonal to both old input features and old prompts. This is solved by computing SVD on the element-wise sum space s_t = x_t + p_t and using the null-space singular vectors V_{t,0} as the projection matrix, yielding the projected gradient Δp' = Δp V_{t,0} V_{t,0}^T. The same projection is applied to key vectors in the prompt pool to preserve the query-key matching process. A threshold ε controls the stability-plasticity tradeoff by determining how many singular vectors define the projection subspace: low ε favors plasticity (more gradient passes through), high ε favors stability (more gradient is suppressed). The approach is applied to both prompt-tuning (L2P) and prefix-tuning (DualPrompt) paradigms, and extends to CLIP vision-language models.

## Results

On 10-Split-CIFAR100, DualPrompt-PGP achieves 86.92% average accuracy and 5.35% forgetting, setting a new SOTA among rehearsal-free methods. On 10-Split-ImageNet-R, DualPrompt-PGP reaches 69.34% ACC and 4.53% FOR, outperforming all compared baselines. L2P-PGP also improves over L2P by 0.57% ACC and 1.04% FOR on 10-Split-CIFAR100, and by 0.96% ACC and 0.97% FOR on 10-Split-ImageNet-R, all without requiring any exemplar buffer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualPrompt-PGP outperforms its baseline DualPrompt on all three datasets: +0.42% ACC / −0.42% FOR on 10-Split-CIFAR100, +0.76% ACC / −0.29% FOR on 20-Split-CIFAR100, and +1.21% ACC / −0.15% FOR on 10-Split-ImageNet-R. L2P-PGP similarly beats L2P by +0.57% ACC / −1.04% FOR on 10-Split-CIFAR100 and +0.96% ACC / −0.97% FOR on 10-Split-ImageNet-R. Against rehearsal-based methods (BiC, DER++, ICaRL, DER+MCG), both PGP variants achieve substantially higher accuracy and lower forgetting without storing any exemplars, e.g., DualPrompt-PGP reaches 86.92% ACC vs. BiC's 81.42% and DER++'s 83.94% on 10-Split-CIFAR100. Compared to regularization-based non-rehearsal methods (EWC, LWF), PGP methods dominate by large margins (e.g., 86.92% vs. 47.01% ACC for EWC). In the task incremental setting (Table 7), L2P-PGP further improves over L2P by +0.10% ACC / −0.05% FOR on 10-Split-CIFAR100 and +0.11% ACC / −0.11% FOR on 20-Split-CIFAR100, surpassing all gradient-projection baselines including Adam-NSCL and Connector.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established continual learning threads: prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt) that use instance-wise query mechanisms to select relevant prompts, and gradient projection methods (GPM, TRGP, Connector) that constrain updates to orthogonal subspaces of old inputs. The key conceptual bridge is that prompt-tuning's per-instance prompt selection naturally eliminates the task-identifier requirement that has limited gradient projection to task-incremental settings, while gradient projection supplies the theoretical anti-forgetting guarantee that prompt-tuning previously lacked. The authors derive the orthogonal condition for prompt gradients through the self-attention mechanism in ViT and implement it via SVD on an element-wise sum space of input and prompt features, making this the first explicit anti-forgetting mechanism for prompt-based continual learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rigorous theoretical foundation (orthogonality condition via Proposition 1) for why prompt-tuning resists forgetting, moving the field beyond purely empirical observations. The stability-plasticity trade-off analysis through the threshold ε offers practitioners a principled knob to tune, and the method is lightweight (≤1 MB memory, no exemplars needed). However, practical gains are modest (typically 0.1–1% on accuracy and forgetting over strong baselines like DualPrompt), the theoretical guarantee relies on ignoring higher-order terms, and the SVD-based projection matrix update introduces growing computation as the number of tasks increases. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to adapter-tuning, LoRA, and very large models, and whether the element-wise sum space is the optimal way to combine input and prompt subspaces.
