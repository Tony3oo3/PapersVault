## Problem

In off-policy RL, experience replay is confined to a single training run, and data collected during prior experiments is typically discarded despite potentially being useful for bootstrapping new runs. The paper asks whether a minimal workflow change—reloading prior experiment data into the replay buffer—can improve asymptotic performance and accelerate research iteration without introducing algorithmic complexity or domain-specific tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241240/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives environment observations that vary by domain: proprioceptive state vectors with task-specific features (ball/goal/opponent coordinates) for locomotion, 40×30 egocentric RGB renders for the vision variant, or multi-camera RGB images plus proprioception for manipulation. The key additional input is a prior experiment's replay buffer of (s, a, r, s') tuples, which is mixed with newly collected online data at a fixed 50/50 ratio throughout training. The output is a trained off-policy controller producing joint-angle commands at 40 Hz (locomotion) or Cartesian velocity commands (manipulation), with network weights re-initialized from scratch at the start of each new experiment.

## Method

RaE requires only a single infrastructure change: a second replay mechanism that allows sampling from both a prior-experiment buffer and the current online buffer at a fixed ratio (50% each) for the entire duration of training. No modifications to the RL algorithm, loss functions, or network architecture are needed—the approach is agnostic to the underlying off-policy method (validated with MPO, SAC-Q, CRR, and D4PG). At the start of each new experiment, policy and value-function weights are re-initialized, and the agent learns from the mixed data distribution, which provides a broader state coverage that prevents premature convergence and improves exploration. The method can be applied iteratively: data from an improved RaE run is fed back as the prior buffer for the next iteration, yielding incremental gains. Ablations show robustness to data quality (low-return data is surprisingly beneficial), data quantity (gains with as few as 10k episodes), and mixing ratio, with the 50/50 split performing well across diverse domains without tuning.

## Results

The primary metric across all domains is undiscounted episode return (accumulated reward). RaE consistently achieves the highest asymptotic performance across Locomotion Soccer (State and Vision), Manipulation RGB Stacking (Place and Stack Leave), and all three RL Unplugged tasks (Humanoid Run, Manipulator Insert Peg, Manipulator Insert Ball). In the ablation with data subsets (Table 1), RaE with 80% online / 20% offline mixing on 100k mixed-return episodes reaches 126% of from-scratch performance, and even 10k episodes yield gains up to 121%. On the full 400k-episode dataset, a 50–70% offline mix is optimal, while 90% online can slightly degrade performance relative to pure online learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CRR finetuning (Wang et al., 2020), RaE matches or exceeds performance on the densely rewarded Humanoid Run domain but clearly outperforms it on the sparsely rewarded Manipulator Insert Peg and Insert Ball tasks, where CRR finetuning struggles to leverage the offline data effectively. Against AWAC (Nair et al., 2020), RaE performs at-par or better across all domains while requiring no domain-specific hyperparameter tuning (AWAC requires choosing pretraining steps and a Lagrange multiplier λ). Against the Random Weight Resetting baseline (inspired by Nikishin et al., 2022), RaE consistently outperforms, confirming that the gains come from reusing prior experience rather than from the incidental benefit of resetting network weights. In the Manipulation RGB Stacking domain, RaE and CRR finetuning achieve similar asymptotic performance, though finetuning requires selecting the correct offline algorithm and hyperparameters. Overall, RaE wins or ties on every domain tested without any per-task tuning, using a fixed 50/50 offline-to-online data mix.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaE extends the experience replay mechanism (Lin, 1992) from within a single training run to across multiple experiments, requiring only a fixed-ratio mix of prior and online data in the replay buffer. It deliberately departs from the more complex offline-to-online RL literature (AWAC, CRR finetuning, demonstration-based methods like Vecerik et al. and Walke et al.) by showing that none of the additional algorithmic components—multi-stage training, BC losses, Q-filters, prioritized replay, or domain-specific hyperparameters—are necessary for effective data reuse. The paper positions itself as a minimalist, algorithm-agnostic workflow change rather than a new algorithm, building on the observation from Yarats et al. (2022) and Lambert et al. (2022) that existing off-policy algorithms can effectively leverage offline data given the right distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for RL research workflows: storing and reusing data across experiments (where storage is cheap relative to compute) can reduce iteration times and improve asymptotic performance across project lifetimes, with the paper demonstrating this across locomotion, manipulation, and vision-based control. Key limitations include the requirement that prior data come from compatible dynamics (though the paper shows surprising robustness to moderate dynamics changes), and a reproducibility concern that incorporating RaE into standard training may make cross-algorithm comparisons less fair without standardized data orderings. Open questions include how to handle more substantial environment changes, how to standardize RaE for benchmarking, and whether the iterative multi-run protocol can be automated to approach a practical lifelong learning pipeline.
