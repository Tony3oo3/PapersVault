## Problem

Existing multi-domain benchmarks for domain generalization and accuracy prediction either cover too few domains (PACS: 4, DomainNet: 6) or rely on synthetic corruptions (CIFAR-10-C: 50 intensity levels), limiting the ability to assess how algorithms behave across a broad, realistic spectrum of environmental discrepancies. This paper introduces CIFAR-10-Warehouse, a collection of 180 real-world and diffusion-generated domains that fill this gap by providing diverse, non-synthetic test environments with the same 10 CIFAR-10 categories.

## Input / Output

For accuracy prediction, a fixed trained classifier (e.g., ResNet44) processes unlabeled 32×32 test images from each of the 180 domains, and the method outputs a scalar accuracy estimate evaluated via leave-one-out MAE across the 180 sets. For domain generalization, a ResNet18 is trained on 1–4 source domains (each ~4,000 labeled images) and evaluated by top-1 accuracy on all 180 unseen target sets. Each domain contains 300–8,000 images spanning all 10 CIFAR-10 classes with moderate class imbalance, and images are available at both 32×32 and 224×224 resolutions.

## Method

The dataset is constructed by combining two collection strategies: (1) 143 real-world sets obtained by querying seven image search engines (Google, Bing, Baidu, 360, Sogou, Pexels, Flickr) with 12 color options crossed with 10 CIFAR-10 category names, plus a 'cartoon' variant, yielding 95 keyword-color (KW) and 48 keyword-cartoon-color (KWC) sets; and (2) 37 sets generated with Stable Diffusion 2-1 using color prompts (12), cartoon-color prompts (12), and 13 unnatural-composition prompts (e.g., 'photo of deer in a cage', 'oil painting of ship'). All images are manually cleaned, privacy-blurred, and resized. The paper then benchmarks 8 accuracy-prediction methods (prediction-score: Pred.s, Entropy, ATC-MC, DoC, Nuclear norm; feature-based: FD, BoP, MS-AoL) across 73 classifiers and 11 domain-generalization methods (ERM, SagNet, SelfReg, SD, Fishr, EQRM, RSC, CORAL, GroupDRO, VREx, VNE) under single- and multi-source settings, revealing that CIFAR-10-W is substantially harder than corruption-based testbeds for AccP and that DG methods lose effectiveness as the domain gap widens.

## Results

On the CIFAR-10-W accuracy prediction task (180 test sets), MS-AoL achieves the lowest MAE of 4.56% (ResNet44), compared to 6.65% average across all 8 methods, while on the synthetic CIFAR-10-Cs the average MAE drops to 3.62%. For domain generalization with 4 source domains, Spectral Decoupling (SD) achieves 89.01% average top-1 accuracy across all 180 test sets, outperforming the ERM baseline (87.85%) by ~1.2%. However, on the most challenging cartoon-searched (KWC) subsets, even the best DG method (SD, 76.47%) lags far behind its performance on diffusion-generated sets (98.85%), revealing a significant domain-gap effect. The paper demonstrates that CIFAR-10-W is substantially harder than existing benchmarks: average MAE for AccP methods roughly doubles, and DG improvements over ERM shrink from ~1.5% (near-OOD) to <1% (far-OOD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For accuracy prediction, MS-AoL (adapted from Baek et al., 2022) is the strongest method on CIFAR-10-W, achieving 4.56% MAE (ResNet44) versus 4.87% for BoP (Tu et al., 2023) and 6.94% for ATC-MC (Garg et al., 2022); on the easier CIFAR-10-Cs, Pred.s(0.9) leads at 3.06% MAE. The nuclear norm method (Deng et al., 2023) and Fréchet Distance (Deng & Zheng, 2021) show inconsistent rankings across classifiers, with FD performing worst on diffusion-generated sets (14.43% MAE) but best on some search-engine subsets. For domain generalization, Spectral Decoupling (Pezeshki et al., 2021) consistently outperforms ERM (Gulrajani & Lopez-Paz, 2020) by 0.4–1.2% across all source counts, while RSC (Chen et al., 2023) is the weakest DG method, trailing ERM by 3.6% in the single-source setting. Notably, all DG methods show diminished gains on far-OOD KWC (cartoon) subsets: SD improves over ERM by only ~5% on KWC (76.47% vs 74.78%) compared to ~1.2% on diffusion sets, indicating that existing DG techniques struggle most with the largest distribution shifts. The paper's key finding is that method rankings shift substantially between CIFAR-10-Cs and CIFAR-10-W (rank correlation τ=0.066 for ResNet44), meaning conclusions drawn on synthetic corruption benchmarks do not transfer to real-world diverse domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the two dominant paradigms for multi-domain generalization evaluation: small-domain real-world benchmarks (PACS with 4 domains, DomainNet with 6) and large-domain synthetic corruption benchmarks (CIFAR-10-C with 19–50 domains, ImageNet-C with 75). It departs from both by constructing 180 domains that are predominantly real-world (143 collected via image search engines with color/style prompts) supplemented by 37 Stable Diffusion-generated sets, bridging the gap between realism and domain count. Methodologically, it adapts the model-centric AoL framework (Baek et al., 2022) into MS-AoL for multi-set accuracy prediction, and benchmarks a broad suite of existing AccP and DG methods (ERM, SagNet, SD, VREx, GroupDRO, BoP, nuclear norm, etc.) under a unified protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 180-domain scale enables statistically meaningful comparisons that are impossible with 4–6 domain benchmarks, and the finding that method rankings on CIFAR-10-Cs and CIFAR-10-W have near-zero rank correlation (τ = 0.066 for ResNet44) challenges the assumption that corruption-based benchmarks are reliable proxies for real-world generalization evaluation. Key open questions include whether DG methods can be made effective on far-OOD domains (where current methods lose their advantage over ERM), how AccP methods can be made robust to the large variance observed across classifiers on diverse real-world sets, and whether these findings transfer to larger-scale benchmarks like ImageNet. Limitations include the small 10-class space, moderate per-domain size (300–8,000 images), and non-exhaustive domain coverage.
