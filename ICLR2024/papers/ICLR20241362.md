## Problem

Wide neural networks are well-approximated by linear models (the neural tangent kernel) under gradient descent, but this linearization fails to capture the 'catapult phase'—a non-linear training dynamic where the loss first increases exponentially before decreasing, observed when using super-critical learning rates. While Lewkowycz et al. (2020) empirically observed this phenomenon in finite-width networks and analyzed it for a two-layer linear network, a theoretical understanding of the catapult phase for general non-linear neural networks remained open. The paper asks: what is the simplest model class that can explain this non-linear optimization behavior, and does it also capture the associated generalization improvements?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a parametric model (e.g., a two-layer fully-connected ReLU network of width m, or a CNN) initialized at w₀, a training set of n examples with inputs xᵢ ∈ ℝᵈ and labels yᵢ ∈ ℝ, and a constant learning rate η. The NQM is constructed as the second-order Taylor expansion of the network function around w₀, fixing the Hessian H_f(w₀) throughout training. The method produces closed-form dynamics equations governing both the loss trajectory and the tangent kernel eigenvalues at each GD step, and empirically outputs best test loss and test accuracy across a sweep of learning rates on vision (CIFAR-2, MNIST, SVHN), speech (FSDD), and text (AG News) datasets.

## Method

The paper defines the Neural Quadratic Model (NQM) as the second-order Taylor expansion f_quad(w) = f(w₀) + (w−w₀)ᵀ∇f(w₀) + ½(w−w₀)ᵀH_f(w₀)(w−w₀) of a two-layer ReLU network around initialization, and derives exact discrete-time dynamics equations for both the model output g(t)−y and the tangent kernel λ(t) under full-batch GD with squared loss. By introducing scaled variables u(t), v(t), w(t), the authors prove three distinct learning-rate regimes: (1) sub-critical η < 2/λ₀ yields monotonic exponential convergence with a nearly constant kernel; (2) super-critical 2/λ₀ < η < 4/λ₀ yields catapult convergence where the loss grows to Ω(m(ηλ₀−2)²/log m) before decreasing to O(1), accompanied by a decrease in the top tangent kernel eigenvalue; and (3) η > 4/λ₀ causes divergence. The analysis extends to multiple training examples by projecting dynamics onto eigendirections of the tangent kernel, showing the catapult can occur selectively in the top eigendirection. Empirically, the NQM's test loss as a function of learning rate closely tracks that of the full neural network across architectures and datasets, while the linear model f_lin diverges in the super-critical regime, establishing the NQM as a tractable surrogate for understanding non-linear wide-network behavior.

## Results

The paper's main empirical finding is that with sub-critical learning rates (η < η_crit), the neural network f, its linear approximation f_lin, and the quadratic model f_quad all achieve nearly identical best test loss across all five datasets (CIFAR-2, MNIST, SVHN, Free Spoken Digit, AG News). With super-critical learning rates (η_crit < η < η_max), both f and f_quad consistently achieve smaller best test loss than at any sub-critical rate, while f_lin diverges. Theoretically, the catapult phase drives the loss up to Ω(m(ηλ₀−2)²/log m) before it decreases back to O(1), and the top eigenvalues of the tangent kernel decrease as a consequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three models: the actual neural network f, the NTK linearization f_lin (from Lee et al., 2019), and the Neural Quadratic Model f_quad. In the sub-critical regime, all three models are empirically indistinguishable in test loss, consistent with the theoretical transition-to-linearity result. In the super-critical regime, f_lin diverges (its loss grows without bound), while both f and f_quad exhibit the catapult phase and converge to a lower best test loss than any sub-critical rate achieves. The key result is that f_quad faithfully reproduces the generalization behavior of f across fully-connected and convolutional architectures with ReLU, Tanh, and Swish activations, on vision, speech, and text tasks. This demonstrates that the quadratic correction term (the Hessian) is sufficient to capture the non-linear generalization benefits of large learning rates that the linear NTK approximation misses entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NTK linearization framework of Lee et al. (2019), which shows wide networks behave as linear models under gradient descent, and the catapult phase discovery of Lewkowycz et al. (2020), who first observed non-linear training dynamics at super-critical learning rates but only analyzed a two-layer linear network. It extends the quadratic model framework of Roberts et al. (2022) by analyzing the large learning rate regime that was previously unexplored, proving that Neural Quadratic Models (second-order Taylor expansions) exhibit catapult dynamics for general non-linear ReLU networks. The key departure from prior work is showing that the transition from non-linear to linear dynamics is discontinuous in network width, contradicting the continuous transition-to-linearity picture established by Liu et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first tractable theoretical framework for understanding catapult dynamics in general non-linear neural networks, filling a gap left by Lewkowycz et al. who could only analyze a special linear case. The discontinuity finding—that the catapult domain [η_crit, η_max] does not shrink with width—challenges the expectation that infinite-width limits fully capture finite-width behavior and suggests that large learning rate training exploits genuinely non-linear structure that persists at all widths. Open questions include understanding the properties of the 'after kernel' (changed tangent kernel post-catapult) and whether quadratic models can capture representation learning effects, both of which the authors identify as future directions. The empirical validation across vision, speech, and text datasets with multiple architectures and activation functions strengthens confidence in the framework's generality.
