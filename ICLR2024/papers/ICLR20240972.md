## Problem

In knowledge distillation, the knowledge fusion ratio α that balances teacher imitation (KL divergence) against ground-truth supervision (cross-entropy) is typically set to a constant or adjusted by simple heuristics. The paper identifies that existing adaptive methods (ADA-KD, WLS-KD, RW-KD) ignore the student-teacher prediction discrepancy (ST) when determining α, despite empirical evidence showing that the optimal α depends critically on how far the student is from the teacher on each sample, in addition to teacher and student correctness.

## Input / Output

For each training sample, the method constructs a geometric feature vector Δ_i by concatenating six edge vectors (differences between S, T, G, and the class-averaged teacher prediction T̄ in C-dimensional probability space) and four vertex vectors (the raw softmax predictions S, T, T̄, and one-hot G). This Δ_i is fed into a small 2-layer MLP with a sigmoid output layer that produces a scalar fusion ratio α_i ∈ (0,1). The T̄ vertex is computed as the mean teacher prediction over all training samples belonging to the same class, providing a class-level reference that helps detect outliers.

## Method

TGeo-KD models the intra-sample trilateral geometry among student prediction S, teacher prediction T, and ground truth G by capturing all three edges (SG, TG, ST) and all three vertices, then extends to inter-sample geometry by adding a second triplet (S, T̄, G) where T̄ is the class-averaged teacher prediction. The concatenated geometric features are passed through a 2-layer MLP f_ω with sigmoid activation to produce a sample-wise α_i. Training is formulated as a bilevel optimization: the inner loop minimizes the combined KD+CE loss over student parameters θ given fixed ω, while the outer loop minimizes validation CE loss to optimize ω, making the fusion ratio network end-to-end trainable. The ST edge is the key novel contribution over prior work, and the inter-sample T̄ relation specifically protects against outliers where the teacher may be confidently wrong.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, TGeo-KD achieves 72.98% top-1 accuracy (ResNet-56→ResNet-20) and 77.27% (ResNet-32×4→ResNet-8×4), outperforming the strongest baseline WLS-KD by 0.83% and 1.22% respectively. On ImageNet, TGeo-KD reaches 72.89% top-1 (ResNet-34→ResNet-18) and 72.46% (ResNet-50→MobileNetV1), surpassing WLS-KD by 0.85% and 0.94%. On HIL (attack detection), TGeo-KD achieves 92.39% ACC and 71.65% AUC, exceeding the teacher's own ACC of 88.19%. On Criteo (CTR prediction), TGeo-KD attains 77.80% ACC and 77.00% AUC, outperforming the best baseline WLS-KD by 2.50% in ACC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGeo-KD consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and architecture pairing. The strongest competitor is WLS-KD (Zhou et al., 2021), which TGeo-KD surpasses by 0.76–1.26% on CIFAR-100, 0.85–0.94% on ImageNet, 2.34% ACC on HIL, and 2.50% ACC on Criteo. ADA-KD (Lukasik et al., 2021) is competitive on same-architecture CIFAR-100 pairs (72.22% vs. 72.98%) but degrades sharply on hetero-architecture settings (71.45% on ResNet-32×4→ShuffleNetV1 vs. TGeo-KD's 76.83%). RW-KD (Lu et al., 2021), despite also learning sample-wise ratios via meta-learning, underperforms TGeo-KD by 2.65% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-56→ResNet-20) and 2.75% on Criteo ACC, suggesting its meta-learning approach captures less geometric information. Among classical KD methods, CRD (Tian et al., 2020) is the strongest non-reweighting baseline (71.46% on ResNet-110→ResNet-20), still trailing TGeo-KD by 2.09%. Vanilla KD (Hinton et al., 2015) and ANL-KD (Clark et al., 2019) are consistently outperformed by 2–3% across all settings, confirming the benefit of adaptive sample-wise fusion over fixed or annealed ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

TGeo-KD extends the adaptive knowledge fusion ratio line initiated by WLS-KD (which uses teacher and student correctness) and RW-KD (which learns sample-wise ratios via meta-learning) by adding the student-teacher prediction discrepancy (ST) as a third geometric dimension, completing the trilateral (S, T, G) picture. The bilevel optimization formulation for learning the fusion ratio network is borrowed from hyperparameter optimization (Franceschi et al., 2018), repurposed here to optimize a small MLP that maps geometric features to per-sample α. The inter-sample relation—incorporating the class-averaged teacher prediction as an additional vertex—is a departure from all prior re-weighting methods, which operate purely on per-sample signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, low-overhead (2-layer MLP, negligible extra training time) solution to a problem that has resisted clean closed-form solutions, and its consistent gains across image classification, attack detection, and CTR prediction suggest broad applicability. The ablation showing that ST discrepancy alone contributes ~1.4% accuracy and inter-sample relations add another ~1.5% provides clear guidance on which geometric signals matter most. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that inter-sample relations are restricted to within-class averaging, leaving cross-class geometric structure unexplored; whether richer inter-sample relations (e.g., nearest-neighbor classes, cluster-level statistics) could further improve fusion ratio learning remains open.
