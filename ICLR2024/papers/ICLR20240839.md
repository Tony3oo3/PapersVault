## Problem

MMD gradient flows are a principled framework for generative modelling, but computing MMD gradients between empirical measures has quadratic complexity O(MN+N²) in the number of particles, making large-scale image generation infeasible. The paper identifies that Riesz kernels K(x,y)=-‖x-y‖^r possess a unique property—MMD coincides with sliced MMD—that unlocks efficient one-dimensional computation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single random sample drawn from the uniform distribution on [0,1]^d and produces a generated image by sequentially passing it through L trained UNets (e.g., 55 for MNIST 28×28, 86 for CIFAR10 32×32 RGB, 71 for CelebA 64×64 RGB). Each UNet outputs a displacement vector that is subtracted from the current sample, so the full generation path is a composition of L residual updates. Training uses 20,000–30,000 target samples and an equal number of initial particles, with P=500–1000 random projections per gradient step.

## Method

The core insight (Theorem 1) is that the d-dimensional MMD with a Riesz kernel equals the expectation of 1D MMDs over random projections, up to a known scaling constant c_{d,r}. This reduces gradient computation to the 1D case, where a sorting-based algorithm evaluates the derivative of the interaction and potential energy in O((M+N)log(M+N)) time instead of O(MN+N²). In practice, the expectation over projections is approximated by P random directions, with a proven error bound of O(√(d/P)). The particle flow is simulated with momentum (velocity update v←∇F_d + m·v) and explicit Euler steps, then a sequence of UNets Φ_1,…,Φ_L is trained sequentially—each network approximates T_l flow steps via a simple L2 loss—keeping GPU memory low since only one network is active at a time. For CIFAR10, a pyramidal schedule progressively increases resolution from d=48 to d=3072, and locally-connected projections replace full spherical projections at the highest resolution.

## Results

The Generative Sliced MMD Flow achieves the best FID scores on MNIST (3.1±0.06) and FashionMNIST (11.3±0.07), outperforming all compared methods. On CIFAR-10, it obtains an FID of 54.8±0.26, which is competitive with WGAN (55.2) but behind NCSN (25.3) and MMD GAN (48.1). On CelebA, the method achieves an FID of 32.1±0.17, slightly behind MMD GAN (29.2) but ahead of all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves the best FID on both MNIST (3.1 vs. 4.2 for MMD GAN and 4.5 for SIG) and FashionMNIST (11.3 vs. 13.7 for SIG), demonstrating clear superiority on these lower-dimensional datasets. On CIFAR-10, the method (54.8) is competitive with WGAN (55.2) and ℓ-SWF (59.7) but trails the score-based NCSN (25.3) and MMD GAN (48.1), which use fundamentally different training paradigms. On CelebA, the method (32.1) is second-best among all compared methods, narrowly behind MMD GAN (29.2) and ahead of SIG (37.3) and ℓ-SWF (38.3). Among flow-based and sliced-Wasserstein methods specifically, the proposed approach dominates: it outperforms SWF (225.1/207.6/91.2), SWF+AE+RealNVP (17.8/40.6/90.9), SIG (4.5/13.7/66.5/37.3), and ℓ-SWF (59.7/38.3) across all datasets where they overlap. The paper notes that the method's advantage is most pronounced on simpler datasets, while on higher-dimensional tasks (CIFAR-10, CelebA) the pyramidal schedule and locally-connected projections are necessary to remain competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of sliced probability metrics (Rabin et al., 2012; Kolouri et al., 2022) and MMD gradient flows (Arbel et al., 2019; Altekrüger et al., 2023). The key theoretical contribution is showing that Riesz kernels possess a special property: their sliced MMD coincides with the full MMD up to a dimension-dependent constant (Theorem 1), a fact that does not hold for Gaussian, Laplacian, or inverse multiquadric kernels. This allows the gradient computation to be reduced to the 1D setting, where a sorting algorithm yields O((M+N)log(M+N)) complexity instead of the quadratic O(N²+MN) required by naive evaluation. The generative modeling framework follows the sequential network training paradigm used in diffusion models and sliced Wasserstein flows, but applies it to MMD particle flows with the negative distance kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-linear scaling in the number of particles makes MMD-based generative modeling practical at the 10K–50K particle scale needed for image generation, whereas prior MMD flow methods were limited to a few hundred points. The O(√(d/P)) error bound for stochastic gradient estimation ensures the approach remains accurate in high dimensions (d=3072 for CIFAR10). However, the method has notable limitations: particle flows do not support batching, and the projection step scales linearly with d, making it less efficient than locally-connected projection variants. Open directions include extending to other kernels, non-Euclidean domains (sphere), and Bayesian inverse problems, as well as investigating whether the sequential UNet training can be replaced by more expressive architectures.
