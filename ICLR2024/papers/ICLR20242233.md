## Problem

The LipSDP framework for polynomial-time Lipschitz constant estimation requires activation functions to be slope-restricted on [0,1], excluding important gradient-norm-preserving activations like MaxMin, GroupSort, and Householder. Rewriting MaxMin as a residual ReLU network and applying standard LipSDP is too conservative to even recover the known 1-Lipschitz property of MaxMin (yielding ρ=2 instead of ρ=1). This paper derives new quadratic constraints that capture the structural properties of these activations, enabling tight SDP-based Lipschitz bounds beyond the slope-restricted setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fully-trained feed-forward (or residual) neural network's weight matrices W_i, bias vectors b_i, layer dimensions n_i, and group size n_g for the GroupSort/Householder activation, and produces a scalar Lipschitz constant upper bound (√ρ for ℓ₂→ℓ₂, ρ for ℓ∞→ℓ₁). For the ℓ∞ analysis, the network is assumed to have a scalar output. The SDP is solved via YALMIP with the MOSEK solver, and the block-diagonal decomposition (setting S=P=0) reduces the problem to l independent LMI constraints for tractability on larger networks.

## Method

The core contribution is a pair of novel quadratic constraints (Lemmas 1 and 2) that characterize the input-output behavior of GroupSort and Householder activations. For GroupSort, the constraint exploits two properties: (i) sorting within each subgroup is 1-Lipschitz, and (ii) the activation is sum-preserving within each group, leading to a matrix X parameterized by (T,S,P) built from Kronecker products of diagonal matrices with I_{n_g} and 1_{n_g}1_{n_g}^T. For Householder, the analogous construction uses the projection matrix I_{n_g} - vv^T instead. These constraints replace the sector-bounded matrix [0 T; T -2T] from LipSDP in the general SDP condition (eq. 5), yielding Theorems 1–4 for ℓ₂ and ℓ∞ bounds on non-residual, residual, and implicit (DEQ/Neural ODE) architectures. In practice, setting S=P=0 and decomposing the resulting LMI into l block-diagonal constraints (eq. D.1) enables efficient layer-by-layer SDP solving without loss of tightness.

## Results

On MNIST-trained MaxMin networks, LipSDP-NSR provides ℓ₂ Lipschitz bounds that are within ~3% of the exact FGL value for small networks (e.g., 22.59 vs. 22.02 for 2-layer/16-unit) and substantially tighter than the residual-ReLU reformulation LipSDP-RR (e.g., 224.53 vs. 388.0 for 5-layer/32-unit, a 42% reduction). For ℓ∞ bounds, LipSDP-NSR outperforms the norm-equivalence bound by 2–3× (e.g., 2969.5 vs. 6286.8 for 5-layer/32-unit) and the matrix-product bound by orders of magnitude (e.g., 2969.5 vs. 522712). Computation times range from 54 s (2-layer/16-unit) to 612 s (8-layer/256-unit) for the ℓ₂ SDP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LipSDP-NSR consistently outperforms LipSDP-RR (the residual-ReLU reformulation of MaxMin using original slope-restricted constraints) across all network depths: the gap widens from ~6% for 2-layer networks (22.59 vs. 23.92) to ~42% for 5-layer (224.53 vs. 388.0) and >57% for 8-layer (722.34 vs. 1683.7), demonstrating that the novel quadratic constraints capture the sum-preserving and 1-Lipschitz structure that the ReLU-based reformulation misses. Against the naive matrix-product bound (MP), LipSDP-NSR is tighter by 4–10× for ℓ₂ and by 2–3 orders of magnitude for ℓ∞ (e.g., 2969.5 vs. 522712 for 5-layer/32-unit). For small networks where the exact FGL value is computable, LipSDP-NSR is within 2.5% of the true Lipschitz constant (22.59 vs. 22.02 for 2-layer/16-unit), confirming near-tightness. The norm-equivalence bound (√n₀ × ℓ₂) is always looser than LipSDP-NSR's dedicated ℓ∞ SDP, with gaps of 2–3×, validating the benefit of the specialized ℓ∞ formulation. The Sample bound serves as a lower reference and is always below LipSDP-NSR, as expected.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LipSDP framework (Fazlyab et al., 2019), which computes tight ℓ₂ Lipschitz bounds via semidefinite programming but requires activations to be slope-restricted on [0,1]. The key departure is deriving novel quadratic constraints for GroupSort, MaxMin, and Householder activations—gradient-norm-preserving functions that are 1-Lipschitz but not slope-restricted—by exploiting their sum-preservation and reflection properties rather than sector-boundedness. The ℓ∞→ℓ₁ formulation builds on Wang et al. (2022), and the quadratic constraint methodology follows the control-theoretic framework of Megretski & Rantzer (1997). The paper also unifies extensions to residual networks, convolutional networks (via Pauli et al., 2023a), and implicit models like DEQs (via Revay et al., 2020) under a single SDP template.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes a fundamental restriction of LipSDP, enabling tight polynomial-time Lipschitz certification for the class of MaxMin/GroupSort/Householder networks that have become standard in certified-robustness research (e.g., Leino et al., 2021; Singla et al., 2022). The practical impact is significant: prior to this, the only SDP-based option for MaxMin networks was the residual ReLU reformulation, which the paper shows is too conservative to even certify 1-Lipschitzness of a single MaxMin layer. Open questions include scalability to very deep or wide networks (addressed partially via chordal sparsity and layer-wise decomposition), extension to other non-slope-restricted activations, and whether the exact-penalty reformulation from Wang et al. (2024) can be adapted to enable first-order solvers for these new constraints.
