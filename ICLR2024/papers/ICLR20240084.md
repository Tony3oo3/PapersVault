## Problem

Offline RL suffers from distributional shift when the agent encounters states outside the training data distribution. Existing conservatism methods constrain the policy or value function behaviorally, but this paper identifies an orthogonal gap: the function approximators themselves face an out-of-support (OOS) generalization problem that can be reframed as an out-of-combination (OOC) problem via bilinear transduction. The missing piece is an efficient mechanism to find in-distribution anchors and deltas for each state at test time, which brute-force search makes computationally infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous state vector (and an action for the Q-function) and first decomposes it into an anchor state drawn from the offline dataset and a delta (their difference) via a short anchor-seeking rollout through a learned forward dynamics model. The policy outputs a continuous action and the Q-function outputs a scalar Q-value, both computed through a bilinear inner product of separate embeddings of the delta and anchor, followed by a small MLP. The anchor-seeking horizon h (typically 1–3 steps) controls how far the anchor can be from the current state, effectively bounding the delta magnitude.

## Method

COCOA applies bilinear transduction to the policy and Q-function by decomposing each state s into an anchor s̃ and delta Δs = s − s̃, then computing φ₁(Δs)·φ₂(s̃) as the function output. To find in-distribution anchors efficiently, the paper introduces an anchor-seeking policy trained on trajectories generated by reversing divergent rollouts of a learned reverse dynamics model: the reverse model is trained to predict predecessor states, and a heuristic random-divergent reverse policy pushes rollouts away from the dataset, so reversing those trajectories yields paths from OOD states back into the data support. The anchor-seeking policy is trained with MSE loss to reproduce these reversed actions, and at test time it drives a forward dynamics rollout of h steps from the current state to land on an in-distribution anchor. This compositional conservatism is agnostic to and independent of the behavioral conservatism already present in the base algorithm (CQL, IQL, MOPO, or MOBILE), and is applied as a drop-in replacement for the standard state embedding in the actor and critic networks.

## Results

On the D4RL Gym-MuJoCo benchmark (12 tasks), COCOA improves the average normalized return of CQL from 61.6 to 64.0, IQL from 77.0 to 77.3, MOPO from 67.2 to 70.1, and MOBILE from 70.6 to 74.8. COCOA wins on 10/12 tasks for CQL, 3/9 for IQL, 7/12 for MOPO, and 9/12 for MOBILE. On the NeoRL benchmark, MOPO+COCOA achieves an average return of 44.40 versus 28.5 for MOPO alone, with particularly large gains on Hopper-L (6.2→23.02) and Hopper-M (1→61.65).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COCOA is applied as an add-on to four offline RL baselines: CQL, IQL, MOPO, and MOBILE. Against CQL, COCOA improves the average return from 61.6 to 64.0, winning on 10 of 12 tasks with the largest gains on hopper-medium-replay (86.3→96.5) and walker2d-medium (79.5→83.1). Against MOBILE, COCOA yields the most consistent improvement, raising the average from 70.6 to 74.8 and winning on 9 of 12 tasks, with notable gains on hopper-medium (92.9→103.7) and hopper-medium-replay (87.9→105.3). Against MOPO, COCOA improves the average from 67.2 to 70.1, winning on 7 of 12 tasks, with the strongest gain on halfcheetah-medium-expert (83.6→109.1). Against IQL, improvements are more marginal (77.0→77.3 average, winning only 3 of 9 non-random tasks), suggesting that IQL's implicit conservatism already partially addresses the distributional shift that COCOA targets. On the NeoRL benchmark, MOPO+COCOA substantially outperforms MOPO alone (44.40 vs. 28.5 average), with dramatic gains on low-quality Hopper data (6.2→23.02 and 1→61.65).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the bilinear transduction framework of Netanyahu et al. (2023) — originally developed for goal-conditioned navigation — to the broader offline RL setting, where every state must be decomposed rather than just a goal state. It departs from the dominant conservatism paradigm in offline RL (CQL, IQL, MOPO, MOBILE), which constrains the policy or penalizes out-of-distribution actions, by instead enforcing conservatism in the compositional input space of the function approximator. The anchor-seeking policy, trained on reverse-dynamics rollouts, is a novel mechanism that replaces the heuristic anchor selection of the original transductive approach, and the method is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to and stackable on top of existing behavioral conservatism techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COCOA offers a modular, architecture-level intervention that can be grafted onto any offline RL algorithm using function approximators, providing a complementary axis of conservatism independent of the algorithm's own regularization. However, the empirical gains are modest (a few normalized points on average across D4RL), and the evaluation is confined to continuous-control MuJoCo environments with relatively small state spaces. Open questions include whether the compositional decomposition generalizes to high-dimensional observations (images), discrete action spaces, or highly non-stationary dynamics, and whether the performance improvements can be explained theoretically beyond the Stone-Weierstrass universality argument provided in the appendix.
