## Problem

Circuit analysis in mechanistic interpretability has been criticized as inherently task-specific: each discovered circuit explains one narrow behavior, leaving open whether such analyses can contribute to higher-level understanding of model behavior. It remains unclear whether the larger structures recovered through circuit analysis (e.g., inhibition-mover subcircuits) are repurposed across different tasks or whether every task is handled idiomatically by the model.

## Input / Output

The method takes minimally different text prompt pairs (e.g., IOI sentences differing only in the subject token, or Colored Objects prompts differing only in which object is queried) and runs them through GPT2-Medium (16 layers, 16 heads per layer). Path patching requires caching all intermediate activations for both inputs, and the Colored Objects task is constrained to one-shot format with 17 single-token object classes and 8 colors, always tokenizing to the same length. Outputs include per-head path patching importance scores, a circuit overlap score (78% shared heads at the 2% threshold), and post-intervention accuracy on the Colored Objects task.

## Method

The paper applies path patching (Wang et al., 2022; Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2023) to both the IOI and Colored Objects tasks on GPT2-Medium, first reproducing the IOI circuit from Wang et al. (2022) on the larger model. Path patching is performed iteratively: first from individual attention heads to the logits, then to the query vectors of mover heads, then to the value vectors of inhibition/content-gatherer heads, revealing a three-stage algorithm (duplicate detection → signal propagation → token copying) in both tasks. The key finding is that 25/32 (78%) of the top-2% most important attention heads are shared between the two circuits, with the only structural difference being three content-gatherer heads replacing three inhibition heads plus a negative mover head. To validate task-generality, the authors intervene on the four inactive heads (three inhibition heads and one negative mover head) by forcing them to attend to the incorrect color options, which boosts Colored Objects accuracy from 49.6% to 93.7% and produces downstream changes in mover head attention and logit attribution exactly as predicted by the IOI circuit's inhibition-mover interactions.

## Results

The IOI and Colored Objects circuits share 25/32 (78%) of their top-2% most important attention heads, with the only major difference being three inhibition heads and a negative mover head (IOI) replaced by three content gatherer heads (Colored Objects). Intervening on the four inactive heads (three inhibition heads + negative mover head) to make them behave as in the IOI circuit boosts Colored Objects accuracy from 49.6% to 93.7%, with zero new mistakes introduced. The intervention causes mover heads to reduce attention to incorrect colors by 8.7% on average while increasing logit attribution ~3×, with a Spearman correlation of 0.69 (p<0.01) between the change in logit attribution and each head's original path-patching importance, confirming the IOI subcircuit's invariance across tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing interpretability methods; instead, it compares circuits discovered across four tasks to quantify component reuse. The IOI and Colored Objects circuits show the highest overlap (78%), confirming that the inhibition–mover subcircuit is a task-general algorithmic building block. In contrast, the Greater Than circuit (relying heavily on MLPs for numerical comparison) shares only 16.7–22.2% of components, and the World Capitals circuit (relying on early MLPs for factual recall) shares 25%, both consistent with prior work showing fundamentally different processing for these tasks. The intervention ablation shows that both the inhibition heads (81.5%) and the negative mover head (78.1%) contribute independently, with the combined intervention reaching 93.7%, indicating complementary roles. On GPT2-Large, 5/10 top heads overlap between tasks, but on GPT2-XL no top-10 heads overlap, suggesting that functional (rather than exact-head) reuse persists at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the circuit analysis framework established by Wang et al. (2022) for the IOI task and the path patching methodology of Goldowsky-Dill et al. (2023) to a new question: whether circuits discovered on one task are reused on another. While prior circuit analyses (Wang et al., 2022; Hanna et al., 2023; Lieberum et al., 2023) each reverse-engineered a single task in isolation, this work explicitly compares circuits across two tasks (IOI and Colored Objects) and quantifies component-level overlap. It also connects to the broader literature on induction heads (Olsson et al., 2022) and content gatherer heads (Lieberum et al., 2023), showing that these previously identified components serve analogous roles in both circuits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 78% overlap in in-circuit attention heads between IOI and Colored Objects, combined with the intervention that boosts accuracy from 49.6% to 93.7% by modifying just four heads, provides concrete evidence that mechanistic interpretability can yield task-general algorithmic insights rather than purely task-specific descriptions. This addresses a central criticism of the field and suggests LMs may be decomposable into a small set of reusable computational building blocks. Key limitations include the small model scale (GPT2-Medium), the fact that Colored Objects was selected because it was hypothesized to be algorithmically similar to IOI, and the lack of evidence for whether this reuse pattern holds across architectures or at production scale. Open questions include how to systematically predict which tasks will share circuits and whether the inhibition-mover subcircuit generalizes beyond selection-from-a-list scenarios.
