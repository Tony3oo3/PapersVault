## Problem

Existing discrete speech representations fall into two categories—semantic tokens (text-aligned but acoustically lossy) and acoustic tokens (high-fidelity but poorly text-aligned)—and neither is specifically designed for speech language modeling. No prior benchmark existed to systematically evaluate token suitability, and no single tokenizer could simultaneously provide strong text alignment and comprehensive speech information preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241251/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes 16 kHz speech waveforms (3-second crops during training) and produces an 8-layer RVQ token matrix at a fixed frame rate determined by the encoder's total stride. The downstream USLM takes a phoneme sequence as prompt for the AR model and a 3-second speech prompt (converted to tokens) for the NAR model, jointly generating the full 8-layer token matrix which is then decoded back to a waveform. The first-layer tokens are designed to be content-focused (analogous to semantic tokens), while layers 2–8 carry paralinguistic and timbre information (analogous to acoustic tokens).

## Method

SpeechTokenizer adopts an EnCodec-style convolutional encoder-decoder with a two-layer BiLSTM (replacing EnCodec's LSTM) to strengthen semantic modeling, followed by 8-layer Residual Vector Quantization. The key mechanism is semantic distillation: a frozen HuBERT teacher guides the first RVQ quantizer via a D-axis cosine-similarity loss (maximizing per-dimension alignment across all timesteps) or pseudo-label prediction against HuBERT units, forcing layer-1 tokens to encode content while the residual structure lets layers 2–8 capture the remaining paralinguistic information. Training combines a GAN objective (multi-scale STFT, multi-period, and multi-scale discriminators with hinge and feature-matching losses) plus L1/mel-spectrogram reconstruction and RVQ commitment losses, weighted together with the distillation term. The resulting USLM pairs a 12-layer autoregressive Transformer (modeling layer-1 tokens from phonemes) with a 12-layer non-autoregressive Transformer (generating layers 2–8 conditioned on layer-1 tokens, phonemes, and an acoustic prompt), enabling single-tokenizer zero-shot TTS without the multi-stage error accumulation of hierarchical systems.

## Results

On the zero-shot TTS task (VCTK, 108 speakers), USLM with SpeechTokenizer achieves WER 6.5, speaker similarity 0.84, MOS 3.63, and SMOS 3.45, outperforming VALL-E with EnCodec on all four metrics. On speech reconstruction (LibriSpeech test), SpeechTokenizer achieves WER 5.04 and MUSHRA 90.55, surpassing EnCodec (WER 5.11, MUSHRA 79.86) while matching its VISQOL score (4.30 vs. 4.37). On the novel SLMTokBench, SpeechTokenizer RVQ-1 achieves mutual information of 30.9 with text (vs. 16.5 for EnCodec RVQ-1), demonstrating strong text alignment while retaining content information (WER* 9.57 vs. 38.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EnCodec (Defossez et al., 2022), SpeechTokenizer wins on content preservation (WER 5.04 vs. 5.11) and subjective quality (MUSHRA 90.55 vs. 79.86), while matching on objective speech quality (VISQOL 4.30 vs. 4.37). On SLMTokBench, SpeechTokenizer RVQ-1 achieves 30.9 MI with text versus EnCodec RVQ-1's 16.5, a nearly 2× improvement in text alignment, while the full RVQ-1:8 tokens match EnCodec's reconstruction quality (WER* 5.04 vs. 5.11, SIM 0.97 vs. 0.98). Against HuBERT semantic tokens (Hsu et al., 2021), SpeechTokenizer RVQ-1 achieves comparable text alignment (MI 30.9 vs. 31.2) but dramatically better content preservation in resynthesis (WER* 9.57 vs. 16.26), and the full RVQ-1:8 tokens far exceed HuBERT in speaker similarity (0.97 vs. 0.77). In the end-to-end zero-shot TTS comparison, USLM with SpeechTokenizer outperforms VALL-E with EnCodec (Wang et al., 2023) on every metric: WER 6.5 vs. 7.9, speaker similarity 0.84 vs. 0.75, MOS 3.63 vs. 3.08, and SMOS 3.45 vs. 3.31, confirming that the unified tokenizer's hierarchical information disentanglement benefits both content accuracy and voice cloning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpeechTokenizer builds directly on the EnCodec/SoundStream RVQ-GAN architecture for neural audio codecs, but departs from their reconstruction-only objective by introducing semantic distillation from HuBERT to guide the first RVQ layer toward content information. Conceptually, it unifies the two-token paradigm of hierarchical speech LMs (AudioLM, SPEAR-TTS) — which require separate semantic and acoustic tokenizers plus multi-stage modeling — into a single tokenizer whose RVQ layers hierarchically disentangle content from paralinguistics. The AR+NAR modeling structure for the downstream USLM follows VALL-E's design, but operates on the unified token space rather than purely acoustic EnCodec codes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLMTokBench is the first systematic benchmark for evaluating whether a given speech tokenization is suitable for language modeling, filling a gap where prior work simply assumed token quality without measuring text alignment or information preservation. The clean disentanglement (content in RVQ-1, timbre in RVQ-2:8) validated via one-shot voice conversion and t-SNE visualizations provides a practical recipe for building simpler single-tokenizer speech LMs that avoid error accumulation from multi-stage pipelines. Open questions include whether the semantic distillation approach generalizes to low-resource or multilingual settings (the paper shows preliminary German/Chinese results), whether the D-axis distillation loss transfers to other distillation tasks, and how the approach scales to larger model sizes and longer-form speech.
