## Problem

Sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) improves generalization by finding flat minima in parameter space, but during fine-tuning, out-of-domain generalization also depends on preserving pretrained representation structure in function space. Trust region methods target this representation smoothness but do not optimize for parameter sharpness, and the two strategies have remained unexplored in combination. The paper asks whether jointly minimizing parameter sharpness and representation curvature can improve OOD generalization during fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

TRAM is an optimization algorithm applied during fine-tuning of pretrained models (ViT-base, GPT-2, XLM-RoBERTa), so it inherits the base model's input/output interface without architectural changes. The key operational cost is one additional forward pass per training step to estimate the trust region distance (for d_θ or d_x variants), or a diagonal Fisher Information approximation computed under Gaussian-noised inputs (for TRAM-Fisher). No new model parameters, adapters, or task-specific heads beyond the standard classification head are introduced.

## Method

TRAM replaces the fixed radius ρ in ASAM's adversarial perturbation with a trust region distance d, constraining the SAM ascent step to remain within a region of low representation curvature. The perturbation becomes ε_TRAM* = d·θ²∇L_S / ||θ∇L_S||₂, where d is either the KL divergence between consecutive or pretrained output distributions (d_θ) or the expected KL divergence under Gaussian input noise (d_x), making the SAM neighborhood adaptive to function-space geometry rather than a fixed Euclidean ball. Four variants are proposed: TRAM-θ_{t-1} (KL to previous step), TRAM-θ_0 (KL to pretrained model), TRAM-x (noise-based), and TRAM-Fisher (Fisher Information geometry of the trust region neighborhood). Because d ≤ ρ, TRAM inherits ASAM's PAC-Bayesian generalization bound as a subsolution, and the full algorithm requires only one additional forward pass over standard SAM training.

## Results

On cross-dataset image classification (ViT-base from ImageNet), TRAM-θ_{t-1} achieves 88.47% Top-1 on CIFAR-100, 93.49% on Stanford Cars, and 97.07% on Oxford Flowers, outperforming the strongest SAM-style competitor FSAM (88.39%, 93.42%, 95.26%) with p<0.01. On cross-domain language modeling (GPT-2 Base on M2D2), TRAM-θ_{t-1} achieves 22.5 zero-shot average perplexity on the Wikipedia split and 26.2 on S2ORC, improving over FSAM (23.2 and 26.8 respectively). On zero-shot cross-lingual transfer (XLM-RoBERTa on XNLI), TRAM-x achieves 75.2% average accuracy across 14 unseen languages, outperforming the best non-TRAM method R3F (74.5%) and ASAM (74.0%) with p<0.01 (Wilcoxon test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRAM consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all three tasks. Against the strongest SAM-style competitor FSAM, TRAM-θ_{t-1} gains +1.81 points on Oxford Flowers (97.07 vs 95.26), +0.7 perplexity on Wikipedia ZS AVG (22.5 vs 23.2), and +1.4 points on XNLI ZS Avg (75.2 vs 73.8). Against trust region baselines, TRAM-x surpasses R3F (the best non-TRAM on XNLI at 74.5%) by 0.7 points and outperforms TRPO (73.0%) by 2.2 points on cross-lingual transfer. On cross-domain language modeling, TRAM-θ_{t-1} improves over MESA by 1.8 perplexity on S2ORC ZS AVG (26.2 vs 27.8) and over TRPO/R3F by 0.8 on Wikipedia (22.5 vs 23.3). Naive combinations of ASAM with trust region regularizers (ASAM+TRPO, ASAM+R3F, ASAM+MESA) perform worse than Adam alone, confirming that TRAM's integrated approach avoids the cross-feature interference of simply adding regularizers. TRAM-Fisher, the most efficient variant matching FSAM's forward/backward pass count, remains competitive but trails the top TRAM variants by 0.5–1.0 points on XNLI.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRAM unifies two previously separate optimization paradigms: sharpness-aware minimization (SAM/ASAM/FSAM), which seeks flat minima in parameter space, and trust region regularization (TRPO/R3F/MESA), which constrains representation curvature in function space. The key conceptual departure is replacing SAM's fixed ρ hyperparameter with a data-dependent trust region distance, so the adversarial perturbation neighborhood is automatically sized to respect representation smoothness. The paper also demonstrates that naively adding a trust region regularizer to the ASAM loss fails (performing worse than Adam alone), whereas the integrated formulation—where the trust region *defines* the SAM neighborhood—succeeds, suggesting the two objectives must be structurally coupled rather than additively combined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRAM provides a practical, low-overhead fine-tuning algorithm (one extra forward pass over SAM) that consistently improves out-of-domain generalization across vision and language tasks, with the strongest gains in hard transfer between anticorrelated domains. A notable finding is that standard SAM/ASAM/FSAM methods are actually *worse* than Adam for out-of-domain ε-sharpness, suggesting current sharpness-aware optimizers may only flatten the in-distribution loss surface. Open questions include whether optimization alone can overcome very large distribution shifts (the paper acknowledges this limitation for S2ORC's ART/PHIL domains) and whether the sharpness–generalization link holds as tightly as assumed, a point the authors flag by citing recent work questioning this relationship.
