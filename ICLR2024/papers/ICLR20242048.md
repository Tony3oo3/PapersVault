## Problem

Despite the rapid proliferation of neural temporal point process (TPP) models since 2016, no centralized benchmark exists for standardized comparison, reproduction, or extension of these methods. Researchers evaluate models using ad-hoc experimental settings, different ML frameworks, and inconsistent data preprocessing, making fair comparison and reproducibility difficult and slowing progress in the field.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242048/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume time-stamped event sequences (t_i, k_i) where k_i is a discrete event type from K categories; within a batch, variable-length sequences are padded to uniform length with sequence masks and causal attention masks to prevent future information leakage. Outputs span three evaluation tasks: next-event time (RMSE) and type (0-1 error rate) via the minimum Bayes risk principle, goodness-of-fit (log-likelihood on held-out data), and long-horizon autoregressive sequence generation scored by optimal transport distance (OTD) against ground truth.

## Method

EasyTPP is a modular benchmarking library where TPP models are constructed by composing reusable modules (continuous-time LSTM, attention layers, neural ODE state evolution) under a unified BaseModel interface that requires implementing forward, loglike_loss, and compute_intensities_at_sample_times. The pipeline handles two non-trivial technical challenges: Monte Carlo approximation of the integral term in the NLL objective (matching sample count to event count during training, 10× for evaluation), and a batched thinning algorithm for exact sampling from intensity-based processes. Nine models spanning RNN-based (RMTPP, NHP), attention-based (SAHP, THP, AttNHP), ODE-based (ODETPP), fully neural (FullyNN), intensity-free (IFTPP), and classical (MHP) families are evaluated across six datasets under identical training protocols (Adam, early stopping on dev log-likelihood), with dual PyTorch and TensorFlow implementations verified to agree within ±1.5%.

## Results

On the goodness-of-fit task (log-likelihood), IFTPP achieves the best scores across all three evaluated datasets (Synthetic: 0.186, Retweet: -0.212, Taxi: 0.019), outperforming all intensity-based models due to its closed-form likelihood computation. On next-event prediction, no single model dominates: NHP achieves the best time RMSE on Retweet (21.90) and Taxi (0.369), while AttNHP achieves the best type error rate on Amazon (65.3%) and Taobao (53.7%), and THP achieves the best type error rate on StackOverflow (55.0%). On long-horizon prediction (OTD), THP is the single winner on Taxi (11.875 at 10-event horizon) and co-wins with AttNHP on Retweet (10.447 at 10-event horizon). The classical MHP baseline is consistently the worst performer across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 9 models: the classical Multivariate Hawkes Process (MHP), two RNN-based models (RMTPP, NHP), three attention-based models (SAHP, THP, AttNHP), one fully neural intensity model (FullyNN), one intensity-free model (IFTPP), and one ODE-based model (ODETPP). MHP is consistently the weakest baseline, with type error rates 10–20 percentage points worse than neural models across all datasets. NHP emerges as the strongest single baseline, winning time RMSE on Retweet (21.90) and Taxi (0.369) and type error rate on Retweet (40.0%), confirming prior findings that it is a comparably strong baseline to attention-based TPPs. Among attention-based models, AttNHP achieves the best type error rate on Amazon (65.3%) and Taobao (53.7%), while THP leads on StackOverflow (55.0%) and long-horizon prediction on Taxi (OTD 11.875). IFTPP dominates the goodness-of-fit task (log-likelihood) due to its closed-form computation, achieving 0.186 on Synthetic versus -0.443 for the best intensity-based model (NHP). ODETPP is competitive but never the best on any dataset, and FullyNN, while achieving the lowest time RMSE on several datasets, is excluded from type prediction and shows poor log-likelihood due to its PDF not integrating to 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

EasyTPP positions itself as the first centralized benchmark for neural temporal point processes, directly inspired by the Hugging Face paradigm for NLP and computer vision. It builds on the full lineage of neural TPPs—from RNN-based models (RMTPP, NHP) through attention-based variants (SAHP, THP, AttNHP) to ODE-based and intensity-free models (ODETPP, IFTPP)—and unifies them under a single evaluation framework. Prior TPP libraries (Tick, pyhawkes, Poppy) focused on classical statistical TPPs or had limited model coverage, whereas EasyTPP explicitly targets the modern neural TPP landscape with standardized data formats, evaluation protocols, and dual-framework (PyTorch/TensorFlow) support. The long-horizon prediction task extends the evaluation beyond the standard next-event prediction by incorporating the optimal transport distance metric from Xue et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a concerning performance saturation: attention-based models (2020–2022) only marginally outperform the 2017 NHP baseline, and no single architecture dominates across all datasets and tasks. The finding that IFTPP, which avoids intensity computation entirely, achieves the best log-likelihood by sidestepping Monte Carlo approximation errors challenges the assumption that richer intensity modeling is inherently superior. The authors identify three open directions—foundation models for event sequences, integration of external information sources (sensor data, knowledge bases, LLMs), and interventional learning—that suggest the field needs to move beyond architectural innovation. A key limitation is that all autoregressive models suffer from cascading errors in long-horizon prediction, pointing to the need for hybrid normalization or non-autoregressive approaches.
