## Problem

Protein pocket representation learning is severely constrained by the scarcity of experimentally determined pocket-ligand complex structures (fewer than 100k non-redundant pairs in the PDB), making large-scale interaction-aware pretraining infeasible. Existing self-supervised methods that leverage abundant pocket-only data (e.g., Uni-Mol's denoising objective) neglect the fundamental ligand-pocket interactions, producing representations that miss critical binding-specific features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240999/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D protein structures from the PDB, segmenting them into pseudo-ligand fragments of 1–8 consecutive residues (with N-terminal acetylation and C-terminal amidation corrections) and their corresponding pockets defined as residues with at least one heavy atom within 6 Å of the fragment, excluding the 5 nearest residues on each side. The pocket encoder operates on atom-level 3D coordinates and element types via a 3D invariant transformer, while the frozen molecule encoder processes 3D small-molecule structures. Outputs are normalized pocket representation vectors used downstream for druggability regression (RMSE), pocket-pair similarity (cosine → AUC), and binding affinity regression (RMSE/Pearson/Spearman).

## Method

ProFSA constructs 5.5 million pseudo-ligand–pocket complexes by extracting drug-like peptide fragments from non-redundant PDB entries and designating their long-range surroundings as pockets, with stratified sampling to match PDBBind's joint distribution of pocket/ligand sizes and rBSA. Training uses a symmetric InfoNCE-style contrastive objective (two losses: pocket-given-ligand and ligand-given-pocket) that aligns the trainable pocket encoder with a frozen pretrained small-molecule encoder (Uni-Mol by default), which acts as a fixed biochemical guide to transfer interaction knowledge from large molecular datasets. The key architectural choice is keeping the molecule encoder's weights constant during training, which the authors prove (Theorem B.1) ensures the learned contrastive representation generalizes from pseudo-ligands to true ligands when the encoding gap is bounded. The pocket encoder is a 3D invariant transformer where pairwise distance-based representations serve as attention biases, enabling SE(3)-invariant encoding of spatial interaction patterns.

## Results

On the ligand binding affinity (LBA) prediction task with a strict 30% sequence-identity split, ProFSA achieves RMSE 1.377, Pearson 0.628, and Spearman 0.620, outperforming all 16 baselines including pretraining-based methods (EGNN-PLM: RMSE 1.403) and structure-based methods (ProNet: RMSE 1.463). On pocket matching, ProFSA reaches 0.85 AUC on Kahraman and 0.94 AUC on TOUGH-M1 in the finetuning setting, surpassing the best traditional method (IsoMIF: 0.75 on Kahraman) and the best pretraining baseline (Uni-Mol: 0.71/0.79). In zero-shot druggability prediction, ProFSA reduces RMSE by up to 38% over Uni-Mol (e.g., Total SASA: 30.50 vs. 49.00), demonstrating strong out-of-distribution generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProFSA achieves the best LBA prediction across all three metrics at the 30% sequence-identity threshold, beating the closest pretraining baseline EGNN-PLM by 0.026 RMSE and 0.063 Pearson, and the best structure-based method ProNet by 0.086 RMSE. At 60% identity, ProFSA leads in RMSE (1.334 vs. ProNet's 1.343) and Spearman (0.762 vs. ProNet's 0.761) but trails ProNet by a marginal 0.001 in Pearson (0.764 vs. 0.765). On pocket matching, ProFSA's zero-shot AUC of 0.80 on Kahraman surpasses the best traditional method IsoMIF (0.75) and Uni-Mol (0.66), while its finetuned TOUGH-M1 score of 0.94 exceeds DeeplyTough (0.91) and Uni-Mol (0.79). In zero-shot druggability, ProFSA outperforms Uni-Mol on all four regression targets, with the largest gap on Total SASA (30.50 vs. 49.00 RMSE). On ligand efficacy prediction, ProFSA achieves AUROC 0.840 and AUPRC 0.806, beating Uni-Mol (0.782/0.695) and GeoSSL (0.776/0.694). The one task where ProFSA does not lead is protein-protein affinity prediction, where the zero-shot ProFSA score (Spearman 0.248) falls short of the dedicated PPI model GET (0.363).

## Relation to Prior Work

ProFSA builds directly on the Uni-Mol 3D invariant transformer architecture for both pocket and molecular encoders, and extends the CLIP-style contrastive learning paradigm (as in DrugCLIP) to the pocket-ligand domain. Its key conceptual departure from prior pocket pretraining methods (Uni-Mol's denoising, CoSP's co-supervised training) is the synthesis of pseudo-ligand-pocket pairs from protein-only structures, leveraging the observation that intra-protein non-covalent interactions (hydrogen bonds, π-stacking, salt bridges) are chemically analogous to protein-ligand interactions. The fixed pretrained molecular encoder as a contrastive anchor, combined with a theoretical generalization guarantee (Theorem B.1) from pseudo-ligands to real ligands, distinguishes this work from both the data-limited co-training approaches and the interaction-agnostic denoising methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.5M pseudo-complex dataset directly addresses the fundamental bottleneck in pocket-ligand interaction modeling (fewer than 100K real pairs in BioLip2), and the approach is explicitly scalable to the millions of AlphaFold/ESMFold predicted structures, potentially unlocking orders-of-magnitude more training data. The consistent SOTA across druggability, pocket matching, and binding affinity prediction—especially in zero-shot and strict 30% sequence-identity settings—demonstrates that interaction-aware pretraining from protein fragments captures biologically meaningful features that geometry-only denoising misses. Open questions include whether the pseudo-ligand approximation degrades for highly flexible or non-standard ligands, whether the approach transfers cleanly to predicted (rather than experimental) structures, and whether the fixed-encoder constraint limits expressiveness for novel chemical spaces.
