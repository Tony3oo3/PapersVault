## Problem

While prior work identified scale sensitivity of least-squares losses in low-dimensional linear cases, no one had established exact conditions under which square-based (MMSE) or log-likelihood-based losses (BIC, ELBO) are provably minimal for wrong DAGs in d-dimensional settings with non-linear dependencies. This gap is critical because wrong DAG identification can flip clinical treatment decisions, yet practitioners routinely optimize these losses without accounting for the units in which variables are measured.

## Input / Output

The methods consume an n×d data matrix where each column is a random variable that may be measured on a different scale, modeled as a linear rescaling c·X_i. The output is a DAG adjacency matrix W ∈ R^{d×d}; the paper's key insight is that the same data from the same distribution can yield different optimal DAGs depending solely on measurement units. The proposed Scale Robust Loss (SRL) modifies the objective by subtracting Var(X_i) for all parentless nodes, so the loss measures only structural dependency quality rather than unexplained variance, but it is restricted to discrete learners since continuous methods typically assign non-zero weights to all edges early in optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a family of propositions giving exact variance-ordering conditions under which MMSE, BIC, and ELBO are minimal for wrong DAGs: for chains, decreasing variance along the path makes the reversed chain achieve lower MMSE (Prop. 2); analogous conditions cause forks or colliders to be preferred over the ground-truth chain (Props. 4, 6). Under Gaussian additive noise, the log-likelihood reduces to MMSE (Prop. 7), and since BIC and ELBO are additive extensions of the log-likelihood (Props. 9–10), they inherit identical scale sensitivity even for non-linear dependencies. The paper proposes SRL, which subtracts free variance terms from the loss to decouple structural quality from measurement scale, and validates all results empirically on NT, GES, DAG-GNN, and GraN-DAG across synthetic (linear/non-linear, 3–10 nodes) and real-world Sachs data, showing that rescaling a single variable can flip predicted substructures in 100% of runs for continuous learners.

## Results

On 10-variable synthetic DAGs (chains, forks, colliders) with 10,000 samples, NT, DAG-GNN, and GraN-DAG predict the wrong graph structure 100% of the time when variables are measured on different scales, for both linear and non-linear dependencies. GES is more robust, predicting the expected wrong graph in only 0–100% of cases depending on the structure type, and the proposed Scale Robust Loss (SRL) further reduces GES's sensitivity (e.g., 50% vs. 100% for chain→fork). On the real-world Sachs protein-signaling dataset, all methods except GES exhibit severe scale-induced errors in 100% of simulations, with higher Structural Hamming Distance to the expected graph.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new structure learner that outperforms baselines; instead, it demonstrates that all four evaluated methods are vulnerable to variable scaling. NOTEARS (NT) and both non-linear learners—DAG-GNN and GraN-DAG—predict the wrong DAG 100% of the time under adversarial scaling, for both linear and non-linear ground-truth dependencies. GES (Chickering, 2003) is comparatively more robust due to its greedy search strategy, showing 0% wrong predictions for collider ground truth and only ~40% for fork/collider substructures in complex graphs. The proposed Scale Robust Loss (SRL), applied to GES, partially mitigates the issue (e.g., reducing chain→fork mispredictions from 100% to 50%) but does not fully eliminate scale sensitivity. On the Sachs dataset, GES remains the only method that avoids severe scale-induced errors in 100% of simulations, while NT, DAG-GNN, and GraN-DAG all fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variance-sensitivity analysis initiated by Loh & Bühlmann (2014) and Reisach et al. (2021), who showed that least-squares-based losses misidentify DAG structure in low-dimensional linear systems when exogenous noise variances are unknown. The key departure is generalizing these results to d-dimensional chain, fork, and collider structures and, crucially, to log-likelihood-based losses (BIC, ELBO) under Gaussian noise, showing that the scale problem is not confined to square-based objectives. The empirical evaluation benchmarks four widely used score-based learners—NT, GES, DAG-GNN, and GraN-DAG—positioning the theoretical findings within the continuous-optimization and graph-neural-network structure learning landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical stakes are high: the paper's medical example demonstrates that a scale-induced wrong DAG can flip a binary treatment decision, and the theoretical results show that all commonly used score-based losses (MMSE, BIC, ELBO) are susceptible to variable scaling, undermining a core assumption in score-based structure learning. The proposed Scale Robust Loss (SRL) offers a simple fix for discrete learners, but the paper explicitly leaves open how to make continuous learners (NT, DAG-GNN, GraN-DAG) scale-robust, and the analysis is restricted to immiscible substructures (chains, forks, colliders) with Gaussian noise. Future work on more complex DAG topologies, non-Gaussian noise, and scale-invariant score functions for continuous optimization remains an open and practically important direction.
