## Problem

Existing Bayesian few-shot meta-learning methods either model only a shallow readout head as random variables or inherit MAML's costly computational graph for inner-loop gradient steps, limiting both the depth of uncertainty modeling and scalability. No prior work provides a complete hierarchical Bayesian treatment of the full network in the few-shot meta-learning setting, despite the HBM paradigm being the natural statistical framework for learning across many related tasks with shared structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241572/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At meta-test time, the method consumes a small labeled support set (e.g., 1 or 5 images per class) and a query input, combined with the learned global variational parameters L_0 = {m_0, V_0, n_0} obtained during meta-training. The output is a full predictive distribution obtained by Monte Carlo averaging over M_S sampled network weights drawn from a test-time variational posterior, yielding calibrated confidence estimates alongside predictions. The framework is architecture-agnostic: the backbone can be a ResNet/WideResNet with a linear or NCC head, a ViT (DINO-small/base), or a set-based network (CNP, ANP, FEAT) that jointly processes the support set and query.

## Method

The paper introduces a hierarchical Bayesian model where a global Normal-Inverse-Wishart variable φ = (μ, Σ) governs episode-specific Gaussian parameters θ_i, enabling principled sharing of information across tasks. The key algorithmic contribution is exploiting NIW-Gaussian conjugacy to derive a closed-form local episodic solution L_i*(L_0) = (m_i*, V_i*) as an explicit function of the global parameters, eliminating the need to maintain a computational graph through inner-loop gradient steps (unlike MAML). Training proceeds by: (1) running SGLD on each episode to obtain a quadratic approximation (m̄_i, Ā_i) of the episodic loss, (2) computing the closed-form local optimum, and (3) performing SGD on the global parameters L_0 using the resulting ELBO. At test time, prediction is framed as Bayesian inference: the learned global prior q(φ) is combined with the test support data via a test-time variational posterior v(θ) ≈ p(θ|D*, φ*), and predictions are averaged over samples from v(θ). The framework provably unifies MAML, ProtoNet, and Reptile as special cases obtained by specific limiting choices of the variational parameters.

## Results

On miniImageNet with WRN28-10, NIW-Meta achieves 68.54% (1-shot) and 84.81% (5-shot) accuracy, outperforming the previous best MetaQDA (67.83%/84.28%). On tieredImageNet with WRN28-10, it reaches 74.59% (1-shot) and 89.76% (5-shot), again surpassing MetaQDA (74.33%/89.56%). On the Sine-Line regression benchmark, NIW-Meta achieves the lowest MSE of 0.7822 (vs. 0.8206 for MAML 1-step and 0.9160 for PMAML) and the best regression calibration (R-ECE 0.1728 vs. 0.2666 for PMAML). On pose estimation (ShapeNet-1D, ShapeNet-2D, Distractor), NIW-Meta with CNP/ANP backbones consistently achieves the best MSE across both intra-category and cross-category settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NIW-Meta consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across classification, regression, and calibration benchmarks. On miniImageNet (WRN28-10), it beats the closest competitor MetaQDA by 0.71% (1-shot) and 0.53% (5-shot), and leads S2M2 by 3.61%/1.63%. On tieredImageNet (WRN28-10), it surpasses MetaQDA by 0.26%/0.20% and S2M2 by 0.88%/1.17%. On the Sine-Line regression task, NIW-Meta achieves 0.7822 MSE, improving over MAML 1-step (0.8206) by 4.7% and over PMAML (0.9160) by 14.6%, while also delivering the best calibration (R-ECE 0.1728 vs. 0.2666 for PMAML). On pose estimation, NIW-Meta with ANP achieves the best ShapeNet-1D cross-category MSE (6.06 vs. 6.23 for ANP and 6.57 for C+R), and with CNP achieves the best Distractor intra-category pixel error (1.80 vs. 2.00 for CNP+FCL). For calibration (ECE on miniImageNet), NIW-Meta achieves 2.68/1.88 (1-shot/5-shot, Conv-4) and 10.79/7.11 (WRN-28-10), substantially lower than MetaQDA-FB (4.32/2.92 and 30.68/15.86) and SimpleShot (33.45/45.81 and 39.56/55.68). The only case where NIW-Meta underperforms is ShapeNet-2D with the C+R (conv-net + ridge head) backbone, where the set-based CNP/ANP architectures are clearly needed to model complex distractor interactions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first complete hierarchical Bayesian treatment of few-shot meta-learning, drawing on the hierarchical Bayesian modeling paradigm from statistics (Gelman et al., 2003) and the SGLD sampling technique (Welling & Teh, 2011) for quadratic loss approximation. It explicitly departs from prior Bayesian meta-learning methods (PMAML, BMAML, VAMPIRE) by introducing a local-global hierarchical structure with Normal-Inverse-Wishart conjugacy, which yields closed-form episodic solutions without maintaining MAML-style computational graphs. A notable conceptual contribution is showing that MAML, ProtoNet, and Reptile are all special cases of the proposed framework under different limiting assumptions on the variational densities, unifying methods that previously appeared unrelated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architecture-agnostic design (compatible with ViT, CNP, ANP, FEAT, and simple linear heads) combined with the closed-form local solution makes this a practical drop-in improvement for existing few-shot pipelines, with the added benefit of principled uncertainty quantification and improved calibration. The PAC-Bayes and regression-analysis generalisation bounds provide theoretical grounding that prior Bayesian FSL methods lacked. Open questions include whether the quadratic approximation via SGLD remains accurate for very deep or non-convex losses, whether the N→∞ assumption in the theorems holds in practice with finite episode counts, and whether the diagonal restriction on V0 limits expressiveness for high-dimensional parameter spaces.
