## Problem

Instruction-tuned LLMs are expected to generalize to new tasks via natural language instructions, but it is unclear how sensitive they are to the specific phrasing of those instructions. This paper shows that using novel but semantically appropriate instruction phrasings (unobserved during training) consistently degrades zero-shot performance by 5+ points on average, and that incorrect-but-observed instructions can even outperform correct-but-unobserved ones, revealing that models over-rely on memorized instruction templates rather than understanding instruction semantics.

## Input / Output

The evaluation framework pairs a natural language instruction (either observed in training or manually composed by NLP practitioners) with a task input (question with choices for QA, text for classification, article for summarization, source sentence for translation) and produces a text response. For the alignment method, the input is a batch containing one instance with the original reference instruction and multiple instances with GPT-4-generated paraphrases of that instruction; the soft prompt parameters are optimized so the model yields similar token-level output distributions across all paraphrased variants. Evaluation uses multiple-choice grading (argmax over choice logits) for classification/QA tasks and ROUGE-2, BLEU, and BERTScore for generation tasks, restricted to examples under 512 tokens.

## Method

The paper proposes a soft prompt alignment method that introduces learnable prefix embedding parameters of dimension R^{d×n} (equivalent to n novel tokens prepended to every input) and optimizes them under a combined objective: standard cross-entropy loss plus a weighted KL-divergence term that penalizes the model for producing different output probability distributions when given a reference instruction versus a GPT-4-generated paraphrase of that instruction for the same task. Only the soft prompt parameters are updated (not the full model weights), making the approach parameter-efficient (61K–122K trainable parameters vs. 3B–7B for full fine-tuning). The key insight, supported by tSNE visualizations and ℓ2 distance measurements, is that instruction-tuned models fail to induce similar internal representations for semantically equivalent instructions, and explicitly clustering these representations in latent space via the KL term closes the robustness gap. Ablations show that simply fine-tuning on paraphrased instructions (without the KL term) causes catastrophic forgetting, while the KL alignment term is the critical component driving improved robustness on unobserved instructions.

## Results

Using novel (unobserved) but semantically appropriate instructions consistently degrades zero-shot performance of instruction-tuned LLMs by 2–6 points on average across models, with the largest drops reaching 15.7 points (Alpaca-7B on BBL multi-class classification) and 11.4 points (T0++-11B on BBL binary classification). Strikingly, incorrect but observed instructions outperform correct but unobserved instructions on average (57.4 vs. 56.8 for Flan-T5-XXL), indicating models rely on memorized phrasings rather than semantic understanding. The proposed soft-prompt alignment method (PT+KL) recovers much of this gap: Alpaca-7B improves from 39.7→41.8 on MMLU (+2.1) and 42.9→46.6 on BBL (+3.7) for unobserved instructions, while Flan-T5-3B improves from 51.9→53.7 on BBL (+1.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between observed (seen in training) and unobserved (novel, human-written) instructions across five instruction-tuned model families (Flan-T5 at 3B/11B, Alpaca at 7B/13B, T0++ at 11B). Unobserved instructions lose to observed ones in 22 of 25 model×category cells, with the most severe degradation on classification tasks (Alpaca-7B drops 15.7 points on BBL-MC; T0++-11B drops 11.4 on BBL-BC). A key diagnostic comparison shows that 'incorrect but observed' instructions (from a different task with the same output format) outperform 'correct but unobserved' instructions by ~0.6 points on average for Flan-T5-XXL, revealing that models latch onto surface form rather than semantics. The proposed PT+KL soft-prompt alignment method is compared against four ablation baselines: full fine-tuning (FT) actually hurts performance (e.g., Flan-T5-3B drops from 47.8 to 39.8 average), FT+KL partially recovers but still underperforms, and prefix-tuning alone (PT) provides no benefit. Only the full PT+KL method yields consistent gains, improving unobserved-instruction accuracy by +0.4 (MMLU) and +1.8 (BBL) for Flan-T5-3B, and +2.1 (MMLU) and +3.7 (BBL) for Alpaca-7B, with statistically significant improvements (p<0.05) for both models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on prompt sensitivity in LLMs (Webson & Pavlick, 2022; Jang et al., 2023) by specifically targeting instruction-tuned models and their zero-shot robustness to semantically equivalent rephrasings. It builds directly on prefix-tuning (Li & Liang, 2021) as the mechanism for introducing soft prompt parameters, but departs from prior robustness analyses (notably the contemporaneous Gu et al., 2023) by evaluating three model families across 80+ tasks and, crucially, proposing a representational alignment objective (KL-divergence between output distributions of equivalent instructions) rather than merely characterizing the fragility. The paper positions itself as both a more comprehensive empirical study and the first to offer a concrete mitigation strategy grounded in the hypothesis that equivalent instructions should induce similar internal representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that instruction-tuned models are unduly sensitive to instruction phrasing has direct practical implications: the primary selling point of instruction-tuning is zero-shot adaptability via natural language, yet models perform worse with novel-but-correct instructions than with incorrect-but-familiar ones. The proposed soft-prompt-plus-KL alignment is a lightweight intervention (61K–122K trainable parameters) that consistently narrows the observed/unobserved gap, making it an attractive add-on for practitioners deploying mid-sized instruction-tuned models. Key open questions include whether the effect persists at 175B+ scale, whether it generalizes beyond the five task types studied, and whether the representational alignment mechanism transfers to decoder-only architectures where the encoder-hidden-state variant is unavailable.
