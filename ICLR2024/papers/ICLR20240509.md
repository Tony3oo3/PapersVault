## Problem

In cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning under partial observability, existing communication methods either require all-to-all information sharing (prohibitively expensive in bandwidth and computation) or rely on manually pre-defined communication topologies that exhibit high performance variance and may actively hinder cooperation. The paper addresses the gap of automatically learning an optimal sparse communication graph structure while simultaneously training agent policies, without requiring dynamic scheduling at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240509/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a sequence of N agents' local observations (o¹, …, oⁿ) and produces encoded representations that capture inter-agent dependencies through masked, edge-embedded attention. The decoder consumes these encoded observations along with previously generated actions (a⁰, …, a^{m-1}) in an auto-regressive fashion to produce the joint action sequence (a¹, …, aⁿ). The learned output also includes a binary adjacency matrix e ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} specifying the communication graph, constrained so that each agent connects to exactly k = S×N other agents, where S is a user-specified sparsity parameter (set to 0.4 in experiments).

## Method

CommFormer formulates communication architecture learning as a bi-level optimization: the outer loop updates a continuous adjacency matrix α via gradients on a validation loss, while the inner loop updates encoder (φ) and decoder (θ) parameters via a single training step on a PPO-based actor-critic objective. The discrete graph is relaxed using a k-hot Gumbel-Softmax trick, enabling differentiable sampling of exactly k outgoing edges per agent. The encoder employs a relation-enhanced Transformer block where attention scores are augmented with edge embeddings derived from α and masked to zero for non-adjacent agent pairs, ensuring agents only attend to communicated neighbors. The decoder generates actions sequentially (agent 1 through N) with a causal constraint (j < i), guaranteeing monotonic performance improvement, and is trained with clipped PPO using a joint advantage estimated from the encoder's value function. This design allows the communication topology and all network parameters to be co-optimized end-to-end via standard gradient descent, eliminating the need for discrete architecture search or dynamic scheduling at test time.

## Results

On SMAC, CommFormer (S=0.4) achieves 100% win rate on 12 out of 14 tasks, matching the fully-connected (FC) upper bound while using only 40% of communication edges. On the two hardest tasks where it falls short of FC, it still leads all baselines: 89.6% on 5m vs 6m (vs. FC 93.8%, HAPPO 73.8%) and 87.5% on 3s5z vs 3s6z (vs. FC 100%, HAPPO 82.8%). On Google Research Football, CommFormer reaches 100% success rate in 25.4 steps, outperforming MAGIC (98.2%, 34.3 steps) and GA-Comm (88.8%, 39.1 steps). On Predator-Prey and Predator-Capture-Prey, it achieves the best cumulative reward (−0.121 and −0.197) and fewest steps (4.99 and 7.61) across all communication baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CommFormer decisively outperforms all non-communication CTDE baselines (MAT, MAPPO, HAPPO, QMIX, UPDeT) across nearly all SMAC tasks, with the most dramatic gaps on Hard+ maps: 96.9% on 27m vs 30m vs. HAPPO's 0.0% and QMIX's 39.2%, and 100% on MMM2 vs. HAPPO's 0.3%. Against communication-based methods, CommFormer dominates on Hard+ SMAC tasks (e.g., 100% on 5z vs 1ul vs. MAGIC's 0.0% and TarMAC's 44.2%), with the sole exception being 1o10b vs 1r where QGNN edges it out at 98.0% vs. 96.9%. On GRF, PP, and PCP, CommFormer achieves the best success rate and fewest steps across all six communication baselines (MAGIC, HetNet, CommNet, I3CNet, TarMAC, GA-Comm), e.g., 25.4 steps on GRF vs. the next-best GA-Comm at 39.1. Critically, CommFormer matches the fully-connected (FC) upper bound on 12 of 14 SMAC tasks while retaining only 40% of edges, and on the two tasks where it falls short (5m vs 6m: 89.6% vs. 93.8%; 3s5z vs 3s6z: 87.5% vs. 100%), it still surpasses every other baseline by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

CommFormer sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the sequence-modeling view of cooperative MARL introduced by MAT (Wen et al., 2022), from which it inherits the auto-regressive decoder with monotonic improvement guarantees, and (2) graph-based attention mechanisms (Hu et al., 2023; Cai & Lam, 2020) that augment vanilla Transformer attention with explicit edge embeddings. Its key departure from prior communication-learning work is the formulation of the communication topology as a *static*, learnable graph optimized via bilevel optimization with a k-hot Gumbel-Max relaxation, rather than either fixing the architecture a priori (CommNet, GA-Comm) or dynamically re-routing messages at inference time (TarMAC, ToM2C, MAGIC). This positions CommFormer as a bridge between neural architecture search (NAS) and multi-agent communication design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the choice of communication topology is a first-order design decision whose impact can rival or exceed that of the policy network itself (Figure 1 shows >50-point variance across random architectures on the same task). By reducing the problem to a differentiable bilevel optimization, it removes the need for expert hand-design or complex runtime scheduling, making bandwidth-constrained multi-agent systems more accessible. However, the learned graph is static and must be re-optimized if the number or type of agents changes, and the paper only sketches (Appendix F) rather than validates a dynamic re-segmentation strategy for open environments. Open questions include whether the single-inner-step bilevel approximation remains valid at much larger agent counts, and whether the k-hot constraint is the right sparsity model for heterogeneous agent capabilities.
