## Problem

Existing path-norm and path-lifting definitions are restricted to simple layered fully-connected ReLU networks without biases, pooling, skip connections, or multi-dimensional output, making path-norm-based generalization bounds inapplicable to modern architectures like ResNets. Consequently, the promises of these theoretical guarantees have never been tested on standard real-world networks, leaving the gap between theory and practice entirely uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The toolkit takes a DAG ReLU network architecture (with ReLU, identity, and k-max-pooling neurons, biases, and skip connections) along with its parameter vector θ, and produces the L^q path-norm (computable in a single forward pass after replacing k-max-pooling neurons with identity neurons and applying |θ|^q coordinate-wise to weights with an all-ones input), a generalization bound of the form (4σ/n)·L·C·‖Φ(θ)‖₁, and a Lipschitz bound via the mixed path-norm ‖Φ(θ)‖_{1,r}. The constant C depends on depth D, the number of distinct pooling types P, the maximum kernel size K, and input/output dimensions, yielding an O(n^{-1/2}) decay that avoids the curse of dimensionality.

## Method

The paper formalizes path-lifting and path-activations for arbitrary DAG ReLU networks, with the key novelty being the definition of path-activations through k-max-pooling neurons (a path stays active only if its predecessor is the first in lexicographic order to be the k-th largest input). The main generalization bound (Theorem 3.1) is proved via a new peeling argument that removes neurons one by one from an arbitrary DAG (rather than layer by layer), combined with two new contraction lemmas: one extending Ledoux–Talagrand to multiple independent Rademacher copies indexed by z∈Z, and another handling vector-valued inputs with k-max-pooling functions. A global path-norm constraint ‖Φ(θ)‖₁≤r replaces layer-wise operator-norm constraints, with parameter rescaling (Algorithm 1) normalizing incoming weights at each neuron to exploit the rescaling invariance of the path-lifting. Numerical experiments on ResNets trained on ImageNet show the L¹ path-norm is ~10³⁰ for dense pretrained models (30 orders of magnitude above the informative threshold), but iterative magnitude pruning reduces it to ~10¹⁷ while maintaining competitive top-1 accuracy, demonstrating that sparsity is a promising route toward closing the theory-practice gap.

## Results

The paper establishes the first path-norm-based generalization bound for arbitrary DAG ReLU networks with biases, skip connections, and k-max-pooling (Theorem 3.1), which decays as O(n^{-1/2}) rather than the generic O(n^{-1/d_in}) for Lipschitz functions. Numerically evaluating on ResNet18 trained on ImageNet (n≈1.27M), the L1 path-norm is 1.3×10^30, making the bound roughly 30 orders of magnitude too large to be informative. Applying iterative magnitude pruning reduces the L1 path-norm to ~10^17 after 19 iterations (a 13-order-of-magnitude decrease) while maintaining competitive top-1 accuracy. The bound's multiplicative constant 4BC/√n ranges from 0.088 (ResNet18) to 0.23 (ResNet152).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed bound (Theorem 3.1) strictly improves upon all five prior path-norm or operator-norm-based generalization bounds in Table 1. Compared to Neyshabur et al. (2015), it removes the exponential 2^D depth factor and extends from scalar-output DAGs without biases to arbitrary output dimension with biases and k-max-pooling. Compared to Golowich et al. (2018), it replaces the product of operator norms R (which can be arbitrarily large even when the path-norm r=0) with the tighter path-norm r, and extends from layered fully-connected networks to arbitrary DAGs with skip connections. Compared to Barron & Klusowski (2019), it generalizes from LFCNs to arbitrary DAGs and handles pooling and biases, at the cost of a slightly larger logarithmic term (D·ln(PK) vs. D). The key practical advantage is that the bound is the first computable on modern architectures like ResNets, enabling the first numerical evaluation of path-norm generalization bounds on ImageNet, which reveals a 30-order-of-magnitude gap for dense networks that sparsity can partially close (13 orders).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the path-norm and path-lifting framework from Neyshabur et al. (2015), Barron & Klusowski (2019), and Bona-Pellissier et al. (2022) from simple layered fully-connected ReLU networks (without biases, pooling, or skip connections) to arbitrary DAG architectures covering modern networks like ResNets, VGGs, and U-nets. The generalization bound proof builds on the peeling argument of Golowich et al. (2018) and the contraction lemmas of Ledoux & Talagrand (1991), but requires substantial new technical machinery to handle the absence of a layer structure, k-max-pooling neurons, and global (rather than layer-wise) path-norm constraints. The introduction of mixed path-norms and the extension of the product-of-operator-norms comparison to DAGs are novel formal contributions that unify previously scattered results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to numerically evaluate path-norm generalization bounds on networks actually used in practice, revealing a 30-order-of-magnitude gap between the theoretical bound and observed generalization error for ResNet18 on ImageNet. The finding that iterative magnitude pruning reduces the L1 path-norm by 13 orders of magnitude while maintaining accuracy is a concrete and actionable lead, but the remaining 17-order gap suggests path-norm bounds may be fundamentally too coarse for dense modern networks. Open questions include whether expected path-activations (rather than worst-case), weight sharing, or L^q norms for q>1 (which are orders of magnitude smaller) can close the gap, and whether path-norm regularization during training could produce networks with both good performance and informative bounds.
