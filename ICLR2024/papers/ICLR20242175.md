## Problem

SNNs retain both rate information (the approximate linear relationship between average firing rates of adjacent layers) and temporal information (spike ordering and membrane potential dynamics), yet prior adversarial attacks have treated these separately—either via ANN-SNN conversion gradients or STBP gradients—without a unified framework. The paper asks how to jointly exploit both information types to maximize attack effectiveness and quantify how much temporal information SNNs actually retain as a function of neuron parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242175/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean input image (32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100, or multi-frame event-stream data for CIFAR10-DVS) and a target SNN model (VGG-11, ResNet-17, or VGG-DVS trained via STBP for 8–10 time-steps), and produces a perturbed adversarial image with L∞ norm bounded by ε (typically 8/255). For neuromorphic data, the perturbation is distributed across preprocessed data frames. The attack is instantiated through FGSM (single-step) or PGD (multi-step iterative) optimization, evaluated under both white-box (full model access) and black-box (surrogate model) settings.

## Method

HART modifies the STBP backpropagation chain by (1) pruning temporal connections—canceling ∂m^l(t+1)/∂m^l(t) and ∂m^l(t+1)/∂s^l(t) to decouple gradients across time-steps—and (2) merging the resulting per-time-step gradients by averaging ∂L/∂m^l(t) over all t before propagating to the previous layer. A tunable surrogate gradient (Eq. 15) with shape parameter γ controls the balance: larger γ yields a smoother, wider activation gradient emphasizing rate attributes, while smaller γ focuses on a narrow membrane-potential window emphasizing temporal attributes. The paper proves (Theorem 2) that under a proportional-relationship assumption, HART's expected gradient is mathematically equivalent to the redefined rate gradient (Eq. 10), and shows a pre-calculation property that reduces backpropagation cost from O(T) to O(1) by summing surrogate gradients during the forward pass.

## Results

HART achieves state-of-the-art attack success rate (ASR) across all evaluated settings. On CIFAR-10/VGG-11 with λ=0.5, HART reaches 100.00% ASR under PGD (white-box), surpassing the closest baseline RGA (99.92%). On robust SNNs (λ=0.9*, adversarially trained), HART achieves 83.54% PGD ASR on CIFAR-10, outperforming STBP (78.55%) and RGA (74.42%). On the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset, HART attains 93.03% PGD ASR (λ=1.0), exceeding STBP (92.63%) and RGA (89.08%). The advantage is particularly pronounced in black-box settings, where HART outperforms RGA by up to 8.54% (e.g., 85.82% vs 77.28% on CIFAR-10/VGG-11/λ=0.9/FGSM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HART consistently outperforms all four baselines across every dataset, architecture, λ value, and attack type. Against CBA (Sharmin et al., 2019), the gap is the largest: HART exceeds CBA by 25–51 percentage points (e.g., 96.28% vs 60.40% on CIFAR-10/VGG-11/λ=0.5/FGSM), confirming that the redefined rate gradient in Eq. 10 is substantially more effective than the ANN-SNN conversion approximation. Against STBP (Sharmin et al., 2020), HART wins in nearly all settings; the most notable margin is on robust models where HART achieves 83.54% vs 78.55% PGD ASR on CIFAR-10 and 87.68% vs 86.56% on CIFAR-100. Against BPTR (Ding et al., 2022), HART dominates by 5–35 points, with the largest gap on robust CIFAR-10 (83.54% vs 61.45% PGD). Against RGA (Bu et al., 2023), the closest competitor, HART still leads by 1–9 points; the advantage is most visible on robust models (83.54% vs 74.42% PGD on CIFAR-10) and in black-box settings (85.82% vs 77.28% FGSM on CIFAR-10/λ=0.9), where the temporal attribute of HART provides a meaningful edge over the purely rate-based RGA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two foundational SNN learning paradigms: STBP (Wu et al., 2018), whose backpropagation chain HART modifies by pruning temporal connections (canceling ∂m^l(t+1)/∂m^l(t) and ∂m^l(t+1)/∂s^l(t)) and merging gradients across time, and ANN-SNN conversion (Cao et al., 2015), whose rate relationship HART formalizes into an explicit activation gradient (Eq. 10). It departs from prior SNN adversarial attacks (CBA, STBP, BPTR, RGA) by being the first to simultaneously exploit both rate and temporal information in the attack gradient, rather than relying on one or the other. The key conceptual departure is the gradient pruning-and-merging operation that creates a hybrid surrogate gradient with an adjustable temporal attribute controlled by the parameter γ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that SNNs retain both rate and temporal information simultaneously, and that neither type alone is sufficient for optimal adversarial attacks—directly challenging the assumption that STBP (the 'most accurate' gradient for STBP-trained models) should be the strongest attack. The adjustable γ parameter provides a practical mechanism for tuning the rate/temporal balance, and the pre-calculation property reduces gradient computation from O(T) to O(1), making the attack more scalable. Open questions include how these insights can be leveraged for adversarial training of SNNs, whether the approach generalizes to other neuron models or deeper architectures, and how the brain's neural circuit characteristics might inspire more robust SNN designs.
