## Problem

Traditional class-independent data augmentation in long-tailed learning improves average accuracy but irreducibly sacrifices certain classes (especially tail classes), creating an intertwined imbalance between inherent data-wise imbalance and extrinsic augmentation-wise imbalance. No existing method simultaneously addresses both sources of imbalance while preserving inter-class fairness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240779/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DODA operates as a plug-and-play augmentation layer on top of any long-tailed learning algorithm, consuming standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist 2018) and producing class labels. Each sample is augmented with probability p_aug (default 0.5) using one of 10 predefined operations (flip, mirror, edge enhance, detail, smooth, auto contrast, equalize, invert, Gaussian blur, rotate), with the specific operation selected per-class according to a dynamically maintained probability distribution.

## Method

DODA maintains a per-class 'preference list' — a probability distribution over K optional augmentation methods — that is dynamically updated during training. After each epoch, the positive sample count (correctly predicted samples) for each class is compared to the previous epoch: DAs that increased the count are up-weighted, while those that decreased it are down-weighted, allowing each class to converge toward its most beneficial augmentation. The theoretical foundation (Theorems 1–4) proves that class-independent DA causes irreducible class-wise bias via level-set deviation, and that tail classes exhibit higher augmentation sensitivity (larger relative expansion of their feature-space distribution), making them disproportionately vulnerable to being 'sacrificed.' The method is orthogonal to existing long-tailed learning algorithms (CE, BS, LDAM-DRW, BCL, RIDE, etc.) and requires only ~9–13% additional training time.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (IR=100), DODA achieves the best overall accuracy across all baselines: BCL+DODA reaches 51.0% (vs. 44.2% for BCL alone, +6.8%), BS+DODA reaches 48.7% (+5.9%), and CE+DODA reaches 44.5% (+6.4%). On ImageNet-LT, CE+DODA achieves 48.1% overall accuracy (+6.5% over CE), and BCL+DODA reaches 56.9% (+1.3%). On iNaturalist 2018 (IR=500), RIDE+DODA achieves 73.7% overall accuracy (+2.8%), and BCL+DODA reaches 73.7% (+1.9%). DODA consistently outperforms the closest competing augmentation method CUDA by 0.5–2.5% overall accuracy across all datasets and baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DODA consistently outperforms the closest competing augmentation method CUDA (Ahn et al., ICLR 2023) across all baselines and datasets: on CIFAR-100-LT IR=100, DODA gains 0.5–2.5% more overall accuracy than CUDA (e.g., BCL+DODA 51.0% vs. BCL+CUDA 48.8%; CE+DODA 44.5% vs. CE+CUDA 42.0%), and the gap widens on tail classes (e.g., BS+DODA tail 31.2% vs. BS+CUDA tail 29.4%). Compared to CMO (Park et al., CVPR 2022), DODA achieves higher overall accuracy on CIFAR-100-LT IR=100 (e.g., CE+DODA 44.5% vs. CE+CMO 41.8%). Against the two-stage re-balancing baselines CE-DRW and LDAM-DRW (Cao et al., NeurIPS 2019), DODA provides the largest absolute gains (+6.7% and +5.1% respectively on IR=100), outperforming their CUDA counterparts by 1.2% and 1.2%. On the stronger baselines RIDE (Wang et al., ICLR 2021) and BCL (Zhu et al., CVPR 2022), DODA still yields consistent improvements (+2.7% and +6.8% on CIFAR-100-LT IR=100; +2.2% and +1.3% on ImageNet-LT). DODA also outperforms AutoAugment (Cubuk et al., CVPR 2019) on CIFAR-100-LT IR=100 with cRT (48.7% vs. 47.9% overall, with a sacrifice rate of 6% vs. 24%), and significantly surpasses the speech-domain auto-DA method of Zaiem et al. (Interspeech 2022) (44.5% vs. 40.2% with CE on IR=100). Finally, DODA improves over CC-SAM (Zhou et al., CVPR 2023) by 3.1% overall on CIFAR-100-LT IR=100 (52.8% vs. 50.7%) while reducing the sacrifice rate from 31% (with CUDA) to 6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of class-aware data augmentation for long-tailed learning initiated by FSR (Wang et al., 2023) and CUDA (Ahn et al., 2023), which adjusted augmentation strength per class but kept the augmentation function fixed. DODA departs by allowing each class to select *which* augmentation method to apply (not just how strongly), maintaining a dynamically updated preference list per class. The theoretical motivation draws on Balestriero et al. (2022)'s level-set analysis of class-dependent regularization effects, reframing it to show that class-independent DA introduces an 'extrinsic augmentation-wise imbalance' on top of the inherent data imbalance. The method is explicitly orthogonal to existing LTL baselines (CE-DRW, LDAM-DRW, BS, RIDE, BCL), positioning DODA as a plug-in augmentation layer rather than a competing training strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'sacrifice rate' framing provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners: a method that improves average accuracy while degrading tail-class performance is arguably counterproductive for long-tailed learning, and DODA offers a simple mechanism (±1 weight updates on a per-class preference list) to mitigate this. However, the empirical gains over CUDA are modest (typically 1–3% on 'All' accuracy), and the theoretical proofs—particularly Theorem 4's reliance on Chebyshev's inequality—are informal and do not tightly bound the claimed bias reduction. Open questions include whether the simple ±1 update rule is near-optimal, whether the approach transfers to transformer backbones or non-image modalities, and whether the preference list can be learned end-to-end rather than updated heuristically per epoch.
