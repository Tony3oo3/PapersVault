key_contribution: A systematic design-space exploration for Lipschitz-based certified robustness that introduces CHORD (Cholesky-orthogonalized Residual Dense) layers for efficient capacity augmentation, an improved EDM-based generated-data pipeline with confidence filtering, and a fair comparison of Lipschitz control mechanisms, achieving up to 8.5 percentage point improvements in deterministic verified robust accuracy over prior state-of-the-art on CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet.
limitations: The paper does not include an explicit limitations section, but the gap to the strongest randomized smoothing methods (e.g., DDS) remains substantial at smaller radii (69.5% vs. 79.3% at ε=0.25 on CIFAR-10), and the authors note they did not exhaustively try all combinations of Lipschitz control methods. The generated-data pipeline requires training a separate diffusion model and a large classifier for filtering, adding practical overhead, and the architecture exploration is limited to ResNet-based backbones with dense layers appended.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that adding 8 large dense CHORD layers to the end of LiResNet yields a larger VRA gain (+3.4 to +4.5 points) than any modification to the convolutional backbone, including Spatial-MLP blocks which provide almost no benefit (+0.2 to +0.3) despite excelling in standard training. This challenges the working assumption that capacity in Lipschitz-bounded networks should be added through the convolutional path, and the ablation evidence is strong (consistent across CIFAR-10/100, multiple radii, and controlled comparisons). The secondary surprise that generated data helps primarily by reducing the proportion of hard samples (making training 'easier') rather than by improving data quality is a useful conceptual reframing.

## Problem

Lipschitz-based deterministic certification methods lag significantly behind randomized smoothing in verified robust accuracy, largely because tight Lipschitz constraints cause networks to underfit even small datasets. Prior work has not thoroughly explored the architecture, Lipschitz-control, and data-augmentation design space in a synergistic way, leaving substantial performance gains unrealized.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs a class label together with a deterministic ℓ₂-norm certified robustness radius. Unlike randomized smoothing, the guarantee is exact (not probabilistic) and can be computed at inference time on the full validation set without the per-sample Monte Carlo overhead that limits RS to 1% subsets of ImageNet.

## Method

The proposed CHORD LiResNet extends the LiResNet L12W512 backbone by appending 8 Cholesky-orthogonalized Residual Dense (CHORD) layers of 2048 neurons to the neck, where each layer computes f(x) = Wx with W obtained by solving a triangular system from the Cholesky factorization of (I+V)(I+V)ᵀ — equivalent to Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization but roughly twice as fast as the Cayley transform. GloRo regularization controls the Lipschitz constant on convolutional layers while the dense layers are hard-constrained to be orthogonal, avoiding the slow convergence of GloRo on high-dimensional eigenvectors. Training uses an improved generated-data pipeline: EDM diffusion models (FID 1.79 on CIFAR-10) produce class-conditional samples that are filtered by a frozen ResNeXt101 classifier (bottom 20% by pseudo-label confidence removed), and batches mix real and generated samples at a 1:3 ratio to reduce the proportion of hard-to-classify outliers that consume model capacity without improving robustness.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed GloRo CHORD LiResNet achieves 87.0% clean accuracy and 78.1% verified robust accuracy (VRA) at ε=36/255, improving over the prior SOTA GloRo LiResNet (70.0%) by 8.1 percentage points. On CIFAR-100, it reaches 62.1% clean and 50.1% VRA at ε=36/255 (+8.6 pp over prior SOTA). On Tiny-ImageNet, it achieves 48.4% clean and 37.0% VRA at ε=36/255, and on ImageNet it reaches 49.0% clean and 38.3% VRA at ε=36/255 (+3.3 pp over prior SOTA). The paper reports up to 8.5 percentage point improvements over the state of the art in deterministic certification across datasets and perturbation sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed GloRo CHORD LiResNet outperforms all prior deterministic Lipschitz-based methods by large margins across all four datasets. On CIFAR-10 at ε=36/255, it achieves 78.1% VRA versus 70.0% for the closest prior deterministic method (GloRo LiResNet, Hu et al. 2023), 65.8% for SLL X-Large (Araujo et al. 2023), and 64.4% for CPL XL (Meunier et al. 2022). On CIFAR-100, the improvement over the prior SOTA is even larger: 50.1% vs. 41.5% (GloRo LiResNet) and 36.5% (SLL X-Large). On Tiny-ImageNet, it reaches 37.0% VRA versus 33.6% (GloRo LiResNet) and 24.7% (Sandwich, Wang & Manchester 2023). Against randomized smoothing (RS) methods in Table 5, the paper's deterministic method is competitive but does not uniformly dominate: it trails the strongest RS method (DDS, Carlini et al. 2022) at all radii (e.g., 69.5% vs. 79.3% at ε=0.25 on CIFAR-10) but outperforms several other RS approaches such as vanilla RS (Cohen et al. 2019), Denoised Smoothing (Salman et al. 2020), and SmoothMix (Jeong et al. 2021) at most certification radii, narrowing the gap between deterministic and probabilistic certification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LiResNet (Hu et al., 2023) as its architectural backbone and GloRo regularization (Leino et al., 2021) for Lipschitz control on convolutional layers, but reframes the problem as a systematic design-space exploration rather than a single-technique contribution. It departs from prior work by introducing CHORD (Cholesky-orthogonalized Residual Dense) layers as a new building block for the network's dense layers, and by replacing IDDPM with EDM for generated data augmentation with confidence-based filtering. The paper explicitly positions itself as a 'master class' that synthesizes techniques from multiple prior works (Cayley, SLL, Sandwich, Matrix Exponential, etc.) under a unified evaluation protocol, rather than proposing one method to supersede the rest.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.5 percentage point improvement in deterministic VRA over prior SOTA is substantial and narrows the gap to randomized smoothing methods, which remain the best-performing certified approach but offer only probabilistic guarantees and suffer from high inference cost. The finding that large dense layers (rather than deeper/wider convolutions) are the most effective way to add capacity under tight Lipschitz constraints challenges the default assumption in the field and provides a practical, low-cost recipe (CHORD layers) that practitioners can adopt. Open questions include whether this approach can fully close the gap to RS at larger radii, whether the dense-layer-centric capacity strategy generalizes to other architectures beyond ResNet, and whether the generated-data pipeline's benefits extend to settings where diffusion model quality is lower.
