## Problem

Existing theories of grokking attribute the delayed generalization phenomenon to weight decay and parameter weight norm decrease, yet grokking can occur with zero weight decay and an increasing weight norm, leaving a mechanistic gap. This paper asks what actually drives the transition from memorization to generalization, proposing that grokking arises when a network shifts from lazy (linearized/NTK) training dynamics to a rich, feature-learning regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240919/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes training data in three forms: isotropic Gaussian vectors for the polynomial regression toy model, MNIST images, and one-hot-encoded modular arithmetic pairs, paired with a two-layer MLP (committee machine with fixed readout weights of 1), a one-layer Transformer, or a student-teacher network. Two tunable parameters govern the dynamics: an output scale α that controls the rate of feature learning (laziness), and an NTK-task alignment parameter ε (or equivalently, centered kernel alignment CKA) that sets how much feature learning is necessary. The primary outputs are train/test MSE loss curves over epochs, parameter weight norm trajectories, CKA alignment metrics, and a three-term test-loss decomposition into variance error, misalignment error, and linear-mode power.

## Method

The paper introduces a minimal polynomial regression task (target y(x) = ½(β*·x)², activation φ(h) = h + εh²/2) on a two-layer committee machine trained with vanilla gradient descent and zero regularization, where grokking emerges purely from the interplay of α and ε. The key mechanism is that the network first fits a kernel regression solution using its initial NTK features (lazy regime), which reduces train loss but not test loss, and only later adapts its weight matrix M to align with β*β*ᵀ (rich regime), producing the characteristic delayed test-loss drop. A closed-form loss decomposition in terms of the sufficient statistics (w̄, M) isolates three error sources—variance mismatch, misalignment, and spurious linear power—and shows the initial test-loss rise is driven by the network depositing power into linear modes inherited from the NTK. The authors demonstrate that α continuously tunes the grokking delay (large α → more lazy → longer delay; small α → immediate feature learning → no grokking), while ε controls how much feature learning is required (small ε → worse initial alignment → more dramatic grokking but potentially better final performance). Evidence that this lazy-to-rich transition is the operative mechanism is provided by showing that tuning α alone makes grokking vanish or intensify on MNIST, one-layer Transformers with AdamW, and student-teacher networks, without altering weight norm or adding weight decay.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that grokking (delayed generalization) can be induced and eliminated by two parameters: the output scale α (controlling laziness/rate of feature learning) and the initial NTK-task alignment ε. On a polynomial regression task with a 2-layer MLP, vanilla GD, and zero weight decay, increasing α continuously lengthens the train-test loss gap (grokking delay), while decreasing α makes grokking vanish entirely. Critically, parameter weight norm *increases* during grokking, directly contradicting prior weight-decay-based theories. On MNIST, tuning α alone (without changing initialization weight norm) continuously eliminates grokking; on a one-layer Transformer trained with AdamW on modular arithmetic (Nanda et al. setup), α=0.15 makes grokking vanish while larger α makes it more dramatic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper provides explicit counterexamples to the two dominant prior theories of grokking. Against Varma et al. (2023), who attribute grokking to weight decay favoring a lower-norm 'generalizing circuit' over a 'memorizing circuit,' the paper shows grokking occurs with zero weight decay and with parameter weight norm *increasing* during training (Figures 1c, 2a), invalidating the weight-norm-decrease mechanism. Against Liu et al. (2022a), who associate grokking with large initial weight norm, the paper shows that weight norm at initialization is not the fundamental cause but rather a proxy for laziness: changing α (output scale) or label scale produces identical grokking control without altering weight norm, and on MNIST, grokking induced by Liu et al.'s weight-norm increase can be made to vanish by simply lowering α. Against Nanda et al. (2023), who mechanistically interpret the learned algorithm on modular arithmetic, the paper shows that the same laziness parameter α continuously controls grokking in their exact one-layer Transformer + AdamW + cross-entropy setup, suggesting the lazy-to-rich transition is a more general mechanism than task-specific circuit learning. The paper also shows that the eigenvector-label construction (Figure 5) provides a general method to induce grokking on arbitrary datasets by controlling NTK alignment, extending beyond the modular arithmetic tasks studied in Power et al. (2022) and Nanda et al. (2023).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NTK and lazy training framework (Jacot et al. 2018; Chizat et al. 2019; Lee et al. 2019) to reframe grokking as a transition from linearized (kernel) dynamics to a rich feature-learning regime, using the committee machine (Saad & Solla 1995) as a tractable toy model. It departs sharply from the dominant prior explanations by Liu et al. (2022a) and Varma et al. (2023), which attribute grokking to weight decay and parameter weight norm decrease; here, grokking is demonstrated with zero weight decay and increasing weight norm, and the role of weight norm is reinterpreted as an indirect effect through inducing laziness. The spectral bias analysis of kernel regression (Bordelon et al. 2020; Canatar et al. 2021) motivates the polynomial regression task, where the NTK requires P~D² samples for quadratic targets while feature learning needs only P~D, creating the intermediate regime where grokking can occur.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unified mechanistic account of grokking that subsumes prior empirical observations (weight norm effects, weight decay effects) under a single lazy-to-rich transition narrative, and identifies two concrete, independently tunable parameters (output scale α and NTK-task alignment ε) that control the phenomenon. Open questions remain: the paper does not give a sharp theoretical characterization of the critical sample size or training time for the lazy-to-rich transition, and the dynamics under adaptive optimizers (AdamW) remain poorly understood despite being ubiquitous in prior grokking studies. The 'goldilocks' dataset size requirement and the finding that worse initial alignment can yield better final generalization (because it forces feature learning) have broader implications for understanding when and why networks learn features versus memorize.
