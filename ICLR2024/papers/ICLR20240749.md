## Problem

Entropy-regularized Optimal Transport (EOT) solvers remain far less developed than Energy-Based Models (EBMs), with existing continuous EOT methods either relying on unstable dual-potential optimization for small regularization, requiring multiple auxiliary models, or being tied to Schrödinger bridge formulations that are resource-intensive and restricted to quadratic costs. The paper identifies that the weak dual formulation of EOT naturally yields a Gibbs-Boltzmann structure, opening a principled path to import the rich EBM toolkit (Langevin sampling, replay buffers, contrastive training) into EOT solving.

## Input / Output

The method takes source and target distributions accessible only by finite samples (e.g., 512×512 AFHQ cat/wild images and the standard Gaussian latent prior of a pre-trained StyleGAN2-ADA), a cost function c(x,y), and an entropy coefficient ε. It outputs a single trained potential network f_θ whose conditional distributions μ_x^f constitute the optimal EOT plan; for image translation, this is combined with the frozen StyleGAN generator to produce translated 512×512 images without requiring any data-to-latent encoder. In the 2D and Gaussian-to-Gaussian experiments, the output is the full conditional plan π(·|x) sampled via Langevin dynamics.

## Method

The core insight is that the inner minimization in the weak dual EOT objective (Theorem 1) admits a unique Gibbs-Boltzmann minimizer μ_x^f with energy E(y) = (c(x,y) − f(y))/ε, making the dual potential f an EBM-like object whose gradient (Theorem 3) is structurally identical to the standard EBM contrastive gradient. Training follows a two-stage procedure: sample x from the source distribution, then run K steps of Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm on the energy (c(x,y) − f_θ(y))/ε to obtain samples from μ_x^f, and perform gradient ascent on the weak dual loss. The paper proves generalization bounds (Theorem 4) decomposing the KL error between the learned and true EOT plan into estimation error (via Rademacher complexity) and approximation error. For high-resolution I2I translation, the method operates entirely in StyleGAN latent space with cost ½‖x − G(z)‖², training only a single MLP potential while keeping the generator frozen, and requires no source-distribution generative model or encoder network.

## Results

On Gaussian-to-Gaussian EOT (Table 1), the method achieves BW2²-UVP of 0.01–0.97 (ε=0.1), 0.03–0.31 (ε=1), and 0.11–0.29 (ε=10) across dimensions D=2,16,64,128, consistently outperforming all EOT baselines. On AFHQ 512×512 Cat→Dog unpaired I2I translation (Table 2), the method achieves FID 56.6, competitive with EGSDE (51.04) and StarGANv2 (54.88), and substantially better than ILVR (74.37), SDEdit (74.17), CycleGAN (85.9), and MUNIT (104.4). For Wild→Dog, FID is 65.8, beating ILVR (75.33) and SDEdit (68.51). On ColoredMNIST '2'→'3' transfer, FID is 109, which lags behind Gushchin et al. (6.3) and Shi et al. (91.3), attributed to the use of basic Langevin dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the core EOT benchmark (Gaussian-to-Gaussian, Table 1), the proposed method dominates all seven EOT baselines across all dimensions and ε values; the gap is most pronounced against Seguy et al. (2018), whose BW2²-UVP is 10–60× worse, and against Daniels et al. (2021), which degrades severely at small ε (e.g., 5.33 at D=128, ε=1 vs. 0.31 for ours). For the 512×512 I2I task (Table 2), the method achieves FID 56.6 (Cat→Dog) and 65.8 (Wild→Dog), outperforming ILVR, SDEdit, CycleGAN, and MUNIT by 10–48 FID points, while remaining within ~2–15 FID of the best baselines EGSDE and StarGANv2. On ColoredMNIST (Table 4), the method's FID of 109 is notably worse than Gushchin et al. (6.3), Shi et al. (91.3), and Daniels et al. (14.7 at ε=25); the authors attribute this to using the simplest long-run EBM with a persistent replay buffer rather than modern sharp-data EBMs. The method's key advantage over dual-potential EOT solvers (Daniels et al., Seguy et al.) is stability at small ε, where exponentiation of O(ε⁻¹) values causes numerical blowup in those approaches but is avoided here through Langevin-based sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges Energy-Based Models (EBMs) and Entropy-regularized Optimal Transport (EOT) by recognizing that the weak dual potential in the conditional entropic EOT formulation (Eq. 4) has the structure of an EBM energy function, enabling direct reuse of EBM training machinery (ULA sampling, contrastive-style gradient ascent). It departs from existing continuous EOT solvers that rely on either the KL-guided dual formulation (Genevay et al., 2016; Daniels et al., 2021) or Schrödinger bridge / SDE-based approaches (Bortoli et al., 2021; Gushchin et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2022), being the first to exploit the conditional entropic formulation. Compared to the closest prior work (Daniels et al., 2021), it requires only a single potential network rather than three models including a separate score-based model, and avoids the numerical instability of exponentiating O(ε⁻¹) quantities at small ε.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded pathway to import the rich EBM toolbox (informative initialization, advanced MCMC, contrastive training) into EOT solvers, with generalization bounds (Theorem 4) that characterize both approximation and estimation errors of the learned plan. The practical demonstration on 512×512 AFHQ translation using a pre-trained StyleGAN shows competitive FID scores without requiring data-to-latent encoders or source generative models. Key limitations include the reliance on MCMC for both training and inference (making it time-consuming), the use of only basic EBM techniques, and the notably weak ColoredMNIST FID (109 vs. 6.3 for Gushchin et al.), suggesting that more sophisticated EBMs are needed for sharp image generation. Open questions include deriving explicit numerical bounds from Theorem 4 for specific network architectures and scaling to truly large-scale setups with modern EBM methods.
