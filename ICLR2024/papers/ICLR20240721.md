## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of deep learning, including dilated CNNs for infinite-dimensional inputs, have been restricted to one-dimensional outputs, leaving a gap for practical tasks like text-to-image and speech-to-text that involve high-dimensional or infinite-dimensional outputs. This paper establishes the first minimax optimality guarantee for deep learning models in the setting where both input and output dimensions are infinite, and further demonstrates a separation from linear estimators (kernel ridge regression, k-NN) that highlights the value of feature learning.

## Input / Output

The input is an infinite-dimensional sequence X ∈ [0,1]^∞ drawn from a distribution P_X absolutely continuous w.r.t. the product Lebesgue measure, and the output is an infinite-dimensional sequence Y ∈ R^∞ with independent Gaussian noise per component. The true operator f°: M → R^∞ has components f°_i belonging to a γ-smooth space (mixed or anisotropic Besov-type) with L² norms decaying as B₂·i^{-1/r}, so the output energy concentrates in low-index coordinates. Estimation is performed from n i.i.d. pairs via empirical risk minimization over a class of dilated CNNs, and performance is measured by the expected ℓ² error over the joint distribution.

## Method

The method uses dilated CNNs (a stack of dilated convolutional layers followed by a ReLU FNN head) to approximate nonlinear operators whose components lie in γ-smooth spaces with non-uniform smoothness across coordinates. The key insight is that the approximation error depends only on the effective smoothness parameter a† and the output decay rate r, not on the (infinite) dimensionality, because the CNN adaptively selects the relevant frequency directions. For estimation, ERM over the CNN class achieves the minimax rate n^{-(2-r)(a†-v)/(2(a†-v)+1)} up to polylog factors, matching a lower bound derived via covering numbers that account for the L² decay of output components. In the sparsity setting where important features are scattered across coordinates, the paper shows that dilated CNNs outperform any linear estimator (including kernel ridge regression) by a factor controlled by the spread of the smoothness sequence, because linear methods cannot perform feature learning and must operate on the convex hull of the non-convex union of γ-smooth spaces.

## Results

The paper establishes that dilated CNNs achieve a minimax-optimal estimation rate of n^{-(2-r)(a†-v)/(2(a†-v)+1)} (up to polylog factors) for infinite-dimensional input-output problems, matching the lower bound n^{-(2-r)a†/(2a†+1)} in the regime p≥2 (v=0). The approximation error scales as 2^{-(1-r/2)(1-v/a†)T}, depending only on the smoothness parameter a† and output decay rate r, not on the (infinite) input or output dimension. For the separation result, dilated CNNs achieve rate n^{-(2-r)a*/(2a*+1)} on the extended class (F_{2,2}(Γ))^∞, while any linear estimator is lower-bounded by n^{-2a*/(2a*+1+c)}, yielding a strict gap when c > (2a*+1)r/(2-r).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between dilated CNNs and the class of all linear estimators (including kernel ridge regression and k-NN/Nadaraya-Watson estimators). On the extended function class (F_{2,2}(Γ))^∞ where the smoothness parameter a varies over a set Γ controlled by parameter c, dilated CNNs achieve an estimation rate of n^{-(2-r)a*/(2a*+1)} (up to polylog), while the minimax rate over all linear estimators is lower-bounded by n^{-2a*/(2a*+1+c)}. The CNN rate is strictly better (faster convergence) whenever c > (2a*+1)r/(2-r), with the gap widening as c increases. This separation is attributed to the feature-learning ability of CNNs: as c grows, the range of important (low-smoothness) coordinates spreads wider, making the convex hull of the function class much larger and causing linear estimators to suffer sub-optimality, while CNNs adaptively identify the relevant features. The paper notes that this separation is achieved without relying on spatial inhomogeneity adaptivity (a common source of DNN superiority in prior work), but rather through the non-adaptive feature extraction of the dilated convolutional architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Okumoto & Suzuki (2021), which established approximation and estimation rates for dilated CNNs with infinite-dimensional inputs but one-dimensional outputs, to the setting where both input and output are infinite-dimensional. The minimax lower bound proof adapts the covering/packing number framework of Raskutti et al. (2012) and Yang & Barron (1999) to the product structure of the γ-smooth space with output decay, while the separation from linear estimators leverages the convex-hull characterization of Hayakawa & Suzuki (2020). Unlike prior work on infinite-dimensional operator learning (Oliva et al., Talwai et al., Lanthaler et al.), which focused on linear operators or architecture-specific models like DeepONet, this paper establishes minimax optimality for a general class of nonlinear operators using dilated CNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result establishing minimax optimality of deep learning models when both input and output dimensions are infinite, providing a theoretical foundation for the success of CNNs in tasks like text-to-image and speech-to-speech where both modalities are high-dimensional. The separation from linear estimators demonstrates that nonlinear feature extraction is not merely a practical convenience but a fundamental theoretical necessity when smoothness varies non-uniformly across coordinates. Key limitations include the requirement p ≥ 2 (restricting the smoothness class) and the assumption of output decay (‖f_i‖₂ ≤ B₂ i^{-1/r}), which, while motivated by applications like audio synthesis, may not hold universally. Future work could extend the analysis to adaptive methods, other architectures, and weaker decay assumptions.
