## Problem

Existing theoretical definitions of memorization from supervised learning rely on class labels, making them inapplicable to self-supervised learning (SSL) where no labels exist. This gap prevents a structured assessment of whether and how SSL encoders memorize their training data, and whether such memorization plays a role in downstream generalization analogous to its established importance in supervised learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241567/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images and their augmented views (e.g., crops, masks, color jitter) processed through two SSL encoders trained on overlapping but slightly different datasets. It produces a continuous per-data-point memorization score normalized to [-1, 1], computed over the full encoder representation vectors (e.g., 49,344-dim for ViT-tiny, 151,296-dim for ViT-base) rather than just the CLS token, making the score independent of any particular downstream task. In practice, the expectation over augmentations is approximated with five random augmentations per data point.

## Method

SSLMem defines memorization via a leave-one-out comparison of alignment loss: two encoders f and g are trained on datasets that share 80% of data but differ in a 10% candidate subset (replaced by an independent subset for g to avoid confounding). The memorization score for a candidate x is the difference in expected ℓ₂ distance between representations of x's augmented views under g (trained without x) and f (trained with x), yielding a positive score when f aligns x's views more tightly than g. This formulation is method-agnostic because alignment of augmented views is a common optimization target across contrastive (SimCLR), non-contrastive (DINO, SimSiam), and masked autoencoding (MAE) SSL frameworks, unlike label-based or reconstruction-based objectives. The paper further demonstrates that higher memorization correlates with better downstream linear-probing accuracy across distributions, that removing the most memorized data points degrades performance on classification, semantic segmentation, and depth estimation more than removing random points, and that an explicit alignment-limiting regularization term reduces both memorization and downstream accuracy.

## Results

Across four SSL methods (MAE, DINO, SimCLR, VICReg) and four datasets (CIFAR-10, SVHN, STL-10, ImageNet), the paper reports average SSLMem memorization scores ranging from 0.271 (MAE on ImageNet) to 0.357 (SimCLR on SVHN), with a clear positive correlation to linear probing accuracy (e.g., SimCLR ResNet50 achieves the highest memorization of 0.339 and highest accuracy of 77.12% on CIFAR-10, while MAE ViT-tiny achieves the lowest at 0.307 and 67.40%). Removing the top 20k most memorized data points from ImageNet pre-training drops linear probing accuracy from 68.21% to 58.90%, compared to only 62.88% when removing random points. Differential privacy (DP-SGD) reduces memorization from 0.307 (ε=∞) to 0.107 (ε=8) but simultaneously drops accuracy from 69.40% to 33.66% on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Déjà Vu memorization (Meehan et al., 2023), SSLMem shows a key divergence on MAE: Déjà Vu assigns MAE the highest memorization (27.36% on CIFAR-10) due to MAE's heavy masking creating background-foreground correlations, while SSLMem assigns MAE the lowest score (0.307) among all methods. Both metrics agree that memorization is higher on CIFAR-10 than ImageNet. Compared to the supervised memorization metric of Feldman (2020), SSLMem identifies a roughly 50% overlap in the top-10 most memorized samples on CIFAR-10, with Kendall's Tau showing a statistically significant but moderate rank correlation (τ=0.099, p=0.584 for top-10; τ=0.192, p=5.48e-4 for top-200). Among SSL methods themselves, memorization rankings are more consistent (τ=0.207–0.258) than between SSL and supervised learning (τ=0.074–0.131), indicating that SSL methods share a common memorization pattern distinct from supervised learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Feldman's (2020) leave-one-out definition of memorization from supervised learning to the self-supervised setting, where the absence of class labels renders the original formulation inapplicable. The key conceptual move is identifying alignment of augmented views as the universal element shared across all SSL paradigms (contrastive, non-contrastive, masked autoencoding), enabling a method-agnostic memorization score computed directly on encoder representations. It departs from the Déjà Vu memorization framework (Meehan et al., 2023), which requires access to labeled data from the same distribution and depends on cropping-specific augmentations, by operating purely on unlabeled representations and being independent of augmentation type, downstream task, or auxiliary labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that SSL memorization is not merely a privacy liability but is actively required for downstream generalization across diverse distributions and task types (classification, semantic segmentation, depth estimation)—reframes memorization as a functional property of SSL encoders rather than a defect. This has direct practical implications: differential privacy mechanisms that reduce memorization (DP-SGD) simultaneously degrade downstream accuracy, creating a fundamental tension between privacy guarantees and encoder utility. Open questions include whether the alignment-based definition captures all relevant memorization dynamics at scale (e.g., billion-parameter models), whether the observed correlation between memorization and generalization is causal or confounded by training duration, and whether targeted memorization reduction (rather than global DP) could preserve utility while mitigating specific privacy risks.
