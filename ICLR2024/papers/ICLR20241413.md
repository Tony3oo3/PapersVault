## Problem

Enforcing hard physical constraints in neural networks via differentiable optimization requires solving a single global non-linear least squares problem over all mesh points, which becomes computationally and memory prohibitive for complex dynamical systems. While soft-constraint methods (e.g., PINNs) avoid this cost, they offer no guarantee of constraint satisfaction at inference time. The paper addresses the gap between constraint fidelity and scalability by asking whether hard constraints can be decomposed into smaller, parallelizable sub-problems without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model receives an initial condition field (e.g., a 1024-dim vector for 1D diffusion-sorption or a 64×64 spatial field for 2D Navier-Stokes), a spatiotemporal grid, and PDE parameters (boundary conditions, physical constants such as porosity or viscosity). The shared FNO backbone outputs N basis functions evaluated over the full grid; a MoE router then partitions these into K expert-local sub-grids, each of which is constrained by a non-linear least squares solve on m sampled points. The final output is the assembled scalar solution field, constructed as a piece-wise linear combination of the global basis functions with expert-specific weights ω_k. Training is entirely self-supervised via the PDE residual, with no interior solution data provided.

## Method

PI-HC-MoE decomposes a single global hard constraint into K independent expert-local constraints, each solved by a Levenberg-Marquardt non-linear least squares solver over a partitioned sub-domain Ω_k. A shared Fourier Neural Operator maps PDE parameters to N basis functions over the full spatiotemporal grid; the MoE router then assigns each spatial/temporal region to an expert, which computes local weights ω_k satisfying the PDE on its sampled points. During backpropagation, each expert performs a localized implicit differentiation step via the implicit function theorem (solving a second non-linear least squares problem for ∂z*/∂θ), and the per-expert Jacobians are reassembled into the full gradient using indicator functions over the domain partition. This design reduces each expert's constraint to 1/K the size of the global problem, enables parallelization across GPUs, and allows piece-wise linear combinations of basis functions rather than a single global linear combination. The approach is architecture-agnostic (any NN can serve as the basis-function generator) and solver-agnostic (any non-linear least squares method can be used), and it scales sub-linearly with the number of constrained points, unlike the quadratic scaling of a single global constraint.

## Results

On 1D diffusion-sorption, PI-HC-MoE achieves 3.60% ± 2.93% relative L2 error, outperforming PI-HC (7.55% ± 8.10%) and PI-SC (85.93% ± 5.07%). On 2D turbulent Navier-Stokes (Re=10⁴), PI-HC-MoE achieves 8.298% ± 2.345% relative L2 error versus 11.754% ± 2.951% for PI-HC and 18.081% ± 3.740% for PI-SC. In terms of scalability, PI-HC-MoE provides up to 12.8× training speedup and 12.9× inference speedup over PI-HC on Navier-Stokes at 10⁴ sampled points, while maintaining near-constant runtime as the number of constrained points increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PI-HC-MoE against two baselines: PI-SC (physics-informed soft constraint via penalty loss) and PI-HC (single global hard constraint via differentiable optimization, following Négiar et al. 2023). PI-HC-MoE decisively outperforms both on both problems. Against PI-SC, the improvement is dramatic: on diffusion-sorption, PI-SC fails to converge (85.93% error) while PI-HC-MoE achieves 3.60%; on Navier-Stokes, PI-SC's 18.08% error is more than double PI-HC-MoE's 8.30%. Against PI-HC (the closest competitor), PI-HC-MoE reduces error by roughly 52% on diffusion-sorption (3.60% vs. 7.55%) and 30% on Navier-Stokes (8.30% vs. 11.75%), while also exhibiting lower variance. Additionally, PI-HC-MoE is substantially faster: at 10⁴ sampled points on Navier-Stokes, it achieves a 12.8× training speedup and 12.9× inference speedup over PI-HC, with near-constant runtime scaling versus PI-HC's quadratic growth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable-optimization-as-a-layer paradigm (Amos & Kolter, 2017; Négiar et al., 2023) by decomposing a single global PDE constraint into K independent local constraints, each solved by a dedicated expert via Levenberg-Marquardt. The MoE routing concept is borrowed from NLP (Shazeer et al., 2017) but repurposed here for spatial/temporal domain decomposition rather than token-level routing. The key departure from Négiar et al. is the per-expert implicit differentiation (via the implicit function theorem) and the resulting parallelizability, which transforms an intractable global non-linear least squares problem into K smaller, independently solvable sub-problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-linear scaling of PI-HC-MoE (vs. quadratic scaling of a single global constraint) addresses a practical bottleneck that has limited the adoption of hard-constrained PDE solvers to small meshes or low sample counts. The framework is architecture-agnostic (works with any NN backbone) and solver-agnostic, making it broadly applicable to PDE-constrained learning. However, the paper is limited to two PDE families (1D diffusion-sorption, 2D Navier-Stokes), uses fixed uniform domain decompositions, and acknowledges sensitivity to hyperparameters (number of basis functions, sampled points, number of experts). Open questions include whether non-uniform or adaptive decompositions could further improve accuracy, how the approach scales to 3D problems, and whether the MoE formulation can be extended to coupled multi-physics systems with interacting constraints.
