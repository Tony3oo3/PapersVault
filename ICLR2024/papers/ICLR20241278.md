## Problem

Diffusion models trained on biased datasets inherit and amplify that bias in generated samples, but the prior approach of time-independent importance reweighting (Choi et al., 2020) relies on a density ratio estimated only at t=0, which suffers from severe estimation error in high dimensions with limited reference data. This error propagates through the entire score-matching objective, degrading the quality of the learned unbiased distribution. The paper asks whether estimating the density ratio at each diffusion time step—where the forward process brings the two distributions closer—can yield a more accurate and effective reweighting mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes two image sets: a large biased dataset D_bias (e.g., 95% of CIFAR-10 with long-tail class imbalance, or FFHQ with 80–90% female) and a small reference dataset D_ref (1.25%–50% of the full unbiased set). The only supervision is the binary label indicating which set each sample originates from; the latent bias factor (class, gender, hair color) is never annotated. The output is a trained score network that generates images following the unbiased data distribution, evaluated by FID against the full unbiased dataset and by latent subgroup proportions measured with a pre-trained classifier.

## Method

The core contribution is a time-dependent importance reweighted denoising score-matching (TIW-DSM) objective in which a time-dependent discriminator estimates the density ratio w_φ*^t(x_t) = p_data^t(x_t)/p_bias^t(x_t) at every diffusion step via a temporally weighted binary cross-entropy loss. This ratio plays two simultaneous roles: it reweights the per-sample loss and it enters as a score-correction term ∇log w_φ*^t(x_t) inside the L2 score-matching loss, pushing the learned score away from the biased distribution and toward the unbiased one. The forward diffusion perturbation is key: it reduces the density-chasm between the two distributions, cutting the integrated density-ratio estimation error by ~61% compared to the t=0-only ratio. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that TIW-DSM is equivalent to standard score-matching on p_data up to a θ-independent constant, guaranteeing convergence to the unbiased distribution, and shows that the score-correction component alone is sufficient for optimality while reweighting further improves sample quality by better utilizing D_bias.

## Results

TIW-DSM achieves the best FID across all tested settings on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, FFHQ, and CelebA. On CIFAR-10 (Long Tail, 5% reference), TIW-DSM reaches FID 11.51, outperforming IW-DSM (15.79), DSM(obs) (12.99), and DSM(ref) (16.47). On CIFAR-100 (LT, 5%), the gap widens to FID 14.46 vs. 20.44 for IW-DSM. On the CelebA benchmark with real-world gender/hair-color bias, TIW-DSM achieves FID 2.40 with latent subgroup proportions closest to the true unbiased distribution (e.g., 31.0% vs. 46.5% true for female-non-black-hair, compared to 42.8% for DSM(obs)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is IW-DSM (Choi et al., 2020), which applies time-independent importance reweighting using a single density ratio estimated at t=0. TIW-DSM consistently outperforms IW-DSM by a large margin: e.g., FID 11.51 vs. 15.79 on CIFAR-10 (LT/5%), 14.46 vs. 20.44 on CIFAR-100 (LT/5%), and 7.10 vs. 11.30 on FFHQ (80%/1.25%). The paper proves both objectives are equivalent under optimal density ratios, so the entire performance gap is attributed to the more accurate time-dependent density ratio estimation. Against the naive DSM(obs) baseline (training on all biased data without correction), TIW-DSM wins in nearly all settings, with the exception being marginal on CIFAR-100 (50% ref) where DSM(obs) achieves 6.17 vs. TIW-DSM's 5.89. Against DSM(ref) (training only on the small unbiased reference set), TIW-DSM dominates when the reference is small (5–25%), confirming that the method effectively leverages the larger biased dataset. On CelebA, TIW-DSM (FID 2.40) narrowly beats IW-DSM (2.43) but substantially outperforms DSM(obs) (3.55) in both FID and latent subgroup proportion accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the importance reweighting framework for fair generative modeling under weak supervision (Choi et al., 2020, originally for GANs) to the diffusion model setting, introducing a time-dependent density ratio estimated via a time-dependent discriminator. It builds on the SDE-based diffusion model framework (Song et al., 2020) and the denoising score-matching objective (Song & Ermon, 2019), with the key conceptual departure being the discovery that the time-dependent density ratio simultaneously serves as both a reweighting mechanism and a score correction term, rendering the otherwise intractable reweighted score-matching objective tractable. The density ratio estimation technique itself follows the noise-contrastive estimation / discriminative DRE lineage (Gutmann & Hyvärinen, 2010), but applied at each diffusion timestep rather than only at t=0.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As diffusion models become the dominant generative framework, dataset bias in training data directly propagates to generated outputs, making unbiased training increasingly critical. The method's weak supervision requirement (a small reference set from the unbiased distribution) is practical, and the theoretical guarantee of convergence to the true data distribution provides strong justification. However, the approach still requires knowing the provenance of each sample (D_ref vs. D_bias), and the density ratio estimation error, while reduced, is not eliminated. Open questions include scalability to large-scale text-to-image models (the appendix includes a Stable Diffusion fine-tuning experiment), handling multiple interacting bias factors, and whether the time-dependent discriminator can be replaced by cheaper alternatives.
