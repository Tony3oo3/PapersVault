## Problem

Existing learning-based video compression methods are optimized at fixed rate-distortion trade-off points and lack a mechanism to precisely encode at a user-specified target bitrate. Traditional rate control relies on empirical mathematical models (R-Q, R-ρ, R-λ) derived from statistical analysis of conventional codecs, which do not generalize to learned codecs, and prior learned rate control attempts still fail to jointly address content-adaptive bitrate allocation across frames.

## Input / Output

The rate allocation network consumes a set of consecutive frames spanning the current miniGoP (default 4 frames) plus one frame before and after, along with the previous frame's encoded bitrate, distortion, and λ, plus the miniGoP-level target bitrate; it outputs a per-frame allocation weight vector. The rate implementation network takes the current frame, its pixel-wise difference with the previous reconstructed frame, and the previous frame's coding statistics, and outputs a single scalar λ for that frame. Both networks operate on 256×256 patches during training and are lightweight (443K and 564K parameters respectively), adding only ~5 ms of encoder-side inference at 1080p.

## Method

The system comprises two networks trained in a step-by-step fashion. The rate allocation network uses a lightweight CNN to extract spatiotemporal features from the miniGoP frames, fuses them via channel-wise multiplication with an MLP-encoded vector of previous-frame statistics and target bitrate, then passes through ResBlocks and fully connected layers to produce per-frame weights; a two-level allocation scheme first distributes the sequence bitrate across miniGoPs using a sliding window (SW=40) for smooth transitions, then splits each miniGoP budget proportionally to the learned weights. The rate implementation network maps the per-frame target bitrate to λ via a CNN (on the current frame and its residual against the previous reconstruction) and an MLP (on previous-frame coding statistics), with a learned normalization module that modulates the bitrate feature vector to handle content-dependent R-λ curves. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) train a variable-rate codec using the modulated scheme of Lin et al. (2021), (2) freeze the codec and train the rate implementation network to minimize relative bitrate error, and (3) freeze both and train the rate allocation network to minimize the R-D loss over two consecutive miniGoPs. The framework is plug-and-play, requiring no modification to the underlying codec's decoder.

## Results

The proposed neural rate control system achieves an average relative bitrate error (ΔR) of 1.64%–2.24% across four baseline codecs (DVC, FVC, DCVC, AlphaVC) on six benchmark datasets (HEVC B/C/D/E, UVG, MCL_JCV). In terms of compression efficiency, the method delivers average BD-rate savings of 12.08% (DVC), 11.08% (FVC), 6.06% (DCVC), and 14.26% (AlphaVC) over the corresponding fixed-λ baselines. The largest gains appear on HEVC Class E (static scenes), where BD-rate savings reach up to 33.90% with AlphaVC, demonstrating the benefit of content-adaptive bitrate allocation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper integrates its rate control framework into four learned video codecs (DVC, FVC, DCVC, AlphaVC) and consistently outperforms each fixed-λ baseline on both rate accuracy and BD-rate. Against the closest prior rate-control method for learned video compression (Li et al., ICASSP 2022), which relies on a hyperbolic R-λ model, the proposed neural approach reduces bitrate error from 7.72%/8.43% to 1.18%/1.91% on HEVC Class C/D, while simultaneously improving BD-rate by 10.63%/12.17% versus Li's 3.92%/1.01% degradation. The largest BD-rate gains are observed with AlphaVC (−14.26% average) and DVC (−12.08% average), while DCVC shows the smallest improvement (−6.06%), likely because DCVC's conditional coding strategy already partially adapts to content. Across all four codecs, the rate error stays below 3% on every dataset, confirming the plug-and-play nature of the approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the learned video compression line (DVC, FVC, DCVC, AlphaVC) and the variable-rate modulation scheme of Lin et al. (2021), but departs fundamentally from prior rate-control attempts for learned codecs. Li et al. (2022b) transplanted the traditional hyperbolic R-λ model to learned compression, and Xu et al. (2023) used gradient-ascent back-propagation for pixel-level allocation; both rely on hand-crafted mathematical models or iterative optimization. Here, both the rate-allocation and rate-implementation stages are replaced by lightweight CNN/MLP networks trained end-to-end, making the entire rate-control pipeline fully differentiable and content-adaptive without empirical fitting functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Rate control is a prerequisite for practical deployment of any video codec, yet it was essentially absent from the learned video compression literature; this plug-and-play framework closes that gap with minimal encoder-side overhead (~5 ms for 1080p) and no decoder changes. The ~10% average BD-rate saving (up to 33% on static-scene Class E) demonstrates that content-adaptive bit allocation, long standard in traditional codecs, transfers effectively to neural codecs. Open questions include extension to B-frames and multi-reference-frame coding, which the authors acknowledge as future work, and whether the approach generalizes to transformer-based codecs (e.g., VCT) that have different rate-parameterization structures.
