## Problem

Imitation learning-based branching policies for MIP solvers are limited by the quality of their training samples: strong branching labels are unreliable due to dual degeneracy, and purely self-generated RL samples are expensive and unstable. Existing approaches either rely solely on expert demonstrations or use fixed hyperparameters to mix expert and self-generated data, lacking an adaptive mechanism to balance exploration and exploitation during sample collection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241234/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each B&B node, the method consumes bipartite graph features (constraint, edge, and variable features such as LP solution values, reduced costs, basis status, and fractionalities) along with score vectors from both the expert rule and the current GCNN policy. The online RL agent outputs a binary decision (sample from expert vs. learned policy), while the GCNN outputs a score for each fractional candidate variable, from which the highest-scoring variable is selected as the branching variable. The offline agent outputs an approximate cumulative reward used to filter the top-z fraction of samples.

## Method

HRL-Aug is an iterative data augmentation framework with three components: (1) an online ActorCritic RL agent that, at each B&B node, dynamically decides whether to generate the next sample from the expert (strong branching) or the current GCNN policy, using the expert score, learned-policy score, and bipartite graph features as state; (2) an offline BDRL-inspired agent that fits the cumulative dual-bound reward (with γ=1 to handle long plateaus in the bound) and filters samples retaining only those whose true reward exceeds z times the predicted reward; (3) a GCNN branching policy trained via imitation learning on the filtered samples. The online agent is retrained every Freq iterations using the evolving GCNN to avoid staleness, while the offline agent is trained only at the first iteration. This reduces training data by ~30% per iteration and cuts model training time by 28–38% compared to KIDA, while consistently improving branching quality across seven MIP benchmarks.

## Results

On the four binary integer programming problems (Combinatorial Auction, Set Covering, Maximum Independent Set, Capacitated Facility Location), HRL-Aug achieves the best 1-shifted geometric mean solving time and highest Win/Solved ratio across Easy, Medium, and Hard difficulty levels. For example, on Combinatorial Auction (Hard), HRL-Aug achieves 222.76s solving time with 100/200 wins, versus 237.10s and 16/200 for KIDA and 298.69s and 5/200 for SCIP. On the ML4CO 2021 competition datasets, HRL-Aug achieves a 34.16% Dual Gap on Item Placement (vs. 61.19% for GCNN and 63.24% for SCIP) and 45.64% on Anonymous (vs. 46.16% for KIDA), and even outperforms Gurobi (36.65% Dual Gap) on Item Placement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRL-Aug consistently outperforms SCIP across all four binary IP problems and all difficulty levels, with the gap widening on harder instances (e.g., 100/200 vs. 5/200 wins on Combinatorial Auction Hard). Against GCNN, HRL-Aug achieves lower solving time and more wins on most problems, though GCNN occasionally matches or slightly edges HRL-Aug on node counts for Easy instances. Compared to KIDA (the best ML4CO 2021 method), HRL-Aug shows the largest advantage on Hard instances (e.g., 100/200 vs. 16/200 wins on Combinatorial Auction Hard; 137/195 vs. 48/185 on Set Covering Hard), while Easy-instance performance is closer. On the ML4CO competition datasets, HRL-Aug achieves the best Dual Gap on Item Placement (34.16%) and Anonymous (45.64%), marginally beating KIDA. The paper also reports that HRL-Aug outperforms Gurobi on Item Placement (34.16% vs. 36.65% Dual Gap), and in Appendix A.5, HRL-Aug substantially outperforms both Scavuzzo et al. (2022) and Parsonson et al. (2023) on Set Covering (e.g., 5.40s vs. 8.88s and 8.86s solving time on Easy, with 186/200 vs. 8/200 and 2/200 wins).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative imitation learning framework for MIP branching (Gasse et al., 2019; Cao et al., 2022a) by replacing KIDA's fixed Bernoulli mixing of expert and model policies with an online RL agent that dynamically decides at each B&B node whether to collect samples from the expert (strong branching) or the learned GCNN policy. The offline filtering component draws on BAIL (Chen et al., 2020) and BDRL (Fujimoto et al., 2018) to approximate cumulative rewards and prune low-quality samples, while the exploration motivation is rooted in self-imitation learning (Oh et al., 2018). The key conceptual departure from KIDA is twofold: (1) the mixing decision is learned rather than pre-specified, and (2) samples are collected from both the expert and the model policy rather than exclusively from the expert.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid online-offline RL augmentation framework offers a practical recipe for improving IL-based branching policies without the high training cost of pure RL, reducing training data by ~30% while improving solving efficiency across multiple MIP problem classes. The demonstration that a SCIP-backend solver augmented with this learned policy can outperform Gurobi on item placement (dual gap 34.16% vs. 36.65%) suggests that learned branching can close the gap with commercial solvers, though this result is limited to a single problem. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other B&B components (node selection, presolve), whether the online RL agent's retraining frequency can be further reduced, and whether the approach scales to problems with millions of variables where the state-action space becomes prohibitive.
