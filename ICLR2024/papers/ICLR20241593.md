## Problem

SAM's most dramatic gains (tens of percentage points) come from robustness to random label noise, yet the mechanistic reason for this robustness is unexplained. The standard 'flat minimum' narrative breaks down because peak test accuracy occurs at early stopping, long before the loss converges, requiring an analysis of SAM's optimization trajectory rather than its final solution. This paper asks: which component of SAM's per-example perturbation actually drives label noise robustness in deep networks?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241593/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on standard RGB image classification (CIFAR-10 at 32×32) with synthetic label noise (30% of training labels randomly flipped to a wrong class), as well as toy Gaussian data for linear model proofs. The 'method' under study is the 1-SAM optimizer, which takes the current model parameters and a perturbation magnitude ρ, computes a per-example adversarial weight perturbation ε_i = ρ∇_wℓ_i/‖∇_wℓ_i‖, and evaluates the gradient at w+ε_i. The output is the updated model weights; the paper's practical contribution is a simplified regularization objective (Eq. 4.5) that replaces SAM's expensive per-example perturbation with explicit L2 penalties on the last layer weights and last hidden layer activations.

## Method

The paper decomposes each sample-wise gradient via the chain rule into a logit scale term σ(−t·f(w,x)) and a network Jacobian term ∇_w f(w,x), then isolates SAM's effect on each by defining L-SAM (perturb only the logit scale) and J-SAM (perturb only the Jacobian). In linear models, SAM's logit scale provably up-weights low-loss examples (Lemma 3.1), keeping clean examples' gradient contribution dominant longer. In deep networks (ResNet18, 2-layer DLN, 2-layer ReLU MLP), J-SAM recovers nearly all of 1-SAM's label noise robustness while L-SAM does not, revealing that the Jacobian perturbation—not the explicit reweighting—is the key mechanism. Proposition 4.1 proves that in a 2-layer deep linear network, J-SAM reduces to SGD with L2 norm penalties on intermediate activations and last layer weights, and the paper verifies this effect persists in ResNet18. Motivated by this, the authors propose a cheaper regularization (Eq. 4.5) that adds γ_z‖z‖_2 + γ_v‖v‖_2² to the loss, closing the SAM-vs-SGD gap from 17% to 9% on CIFAR-10 with 30% label noise.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 30% random label noise using ResNet18, 1-SAM (ρ=0.01) achieves 69.47% best test accuracy, outperforming plain SGD (52.48%) by 17 percentage points. The Jacobian-only variant (J-SAM) recovers nearly all of SAM's gains at 69.17%, while the logit-only variant (L-SAM) barely improves over SGD at 54.13%. The paper's proposed L2 regularization on the last layer weights and final hidden activations achieves 60.8%, closing the SAM–SGD gap from 17% to 9% without the per-sample forward passes required by 1-SAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison decomposes 1-SAM into its logit-scale (L-SAM) and Jacobian (J-SAM) components. J-SAM (69.17%) nearly matches full 1-SAM (69.47%), confirming that the Jacobian perturbation—not the explicit logit reweighting—is responsible for SAM's label-noise robustness in deep networks. In stark contrast, L-SAM (54.13%) provides only a marginal 1.6-point gain over SGD (52.48%), ruling out the logit-scale up-weighting mechanism as the primary driver. The proposed simplified regularization (L2 penalty on last-layer weights and final hidden activations) achieves 60.8%, recovering roughly half of SAM's 17-point advantage over SGD. Under clean (no-noise) conditions, the same regularization yields only ~1% improvement while SAM still gains 8% over SGD, indicating the Jacobian effect is particularly important under heavy label noise. The gap between SAM and SGD also grows with dataset size (Fig. 12) and model width (Fig. 13), suggesting the effect is not an artifact of small-scale experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM (Foret et al., 2020) and the subsequent mechanistic analyses by Andriushchenko & Flammarion (2022a,b) and Andriushchenko et al. (2023a), but deliberately departs from the dominant framing of SAM as a flat-minima finder at convergence. Instead, it reframes the question around early-stopping behavior under label noise, connecting to the clean-priority learning literature (Liu et al., 2020; 2023) that shows clean examples are fit before noisy ones. The key conceptual departure is from Andriushchenko & Flammarion (2022b), who attributed SAM's label noise robustness in neural networks to logit scaling; this paper argues the opposite, showing the Jacobian perturbation is the dominant mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SAM's label noise robustness in deep networks is driven by implicit Jacobian regularization (constraining activation and weight norms) rather than explicit logit reweighting reframes how the community should think about SAM's benefits and opens the door to cheaper alternatives that mimic this regularization without SAM's per-sample forward passes. However, the proposed regularized SGD only closes the gap from 17% to 9% relative to SAM, and the theoretical analysis is limited to 2-layer deep linear networks, leaving the full mechanism in deeper nonlinear architectures partially unexplained. A key open question is whether the remaining gap reflects additional higher-order Jacobian effects or other trajectory-level phenomena not captured by simple norm penalties.
