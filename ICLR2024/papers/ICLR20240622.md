## Problem

At ultra-low bitrates (below 0.01 bpp), existing image codecs produce severe, easily perceptible compression artefacts even when trained with adversarial or perceptual losses, because their distortion objectives make strong assumptions about the conditional image distribution that break down when very little information is transmitted. Prior diffusion-based compression work was limited to 64×64 images, and the only prior method exploring comparable ultra-low rates relied on binary contour maps that carry little color or texture information, yielding reconstructions unfaithful to the original.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes an RGB image (512×512 or 512×768) and produces a compressed bitstream with two components: vector-quantized spatial codes encoded via uniform coding, and a losslessly compressed (Lempel-Ziv) text caption generated by a frozen BLIP-2 or IDEFICS model. The decoder reconstructs the image by iterative diffusion sampling, so the output is stochastic—multiple plausible reconstructions can be drawn from the same bitstream. At the lowest tested rate of 0.003 bpp, a 512×768 Kodak image is compressed to fewer than 153 bytes.

## Method

PerCo combines a VQ-VAE-style encoder with a pretrained 1.4B-parameter text-conditioned latent diffusion model (GLIDE-like) as the decoder. The local spatial encoding pipeline maps the image through the frozen LDM encoder, a lightweight convolutional hyper-encoder, and vector quantization (with EMA codebook updates), while the global encoding is a BLIP-2/IDEFICS caption compressed losslessly. During decoding, the quantized spatial features are channel-wise concatenated to the U-Net input and the text caption is injected via cross-attention, with classifier-free guidance applied to the text conditioning only (λ_s=3). Training finetunes only ~15% of the diffusion model weights (linear layers) using a v-prediction denoising loss in the LDM latent space, optionally augmented with LPIPS at higher bitrates, replacing the MSE or LPIPS distortion terms that force inappropriate conditional-distribution assumptions at low rates.

## Results

PerCo achieves state-of-the-art FID and KID at ultra-low bitrates (0.003–0.04 bpp) on both Kodak and MS-COCO 30k, with FID and KID curves that are dramatically flatter across bitrates than all competing methods, indicating a decoupling of perceptual realism from bitrate. At 0.003 bpp, a 512×768 Kodak image is compressed to fewer than 153 bytes while maintaining realistic reconstructions. On MS-COCO 30k at 256×256, PerCo yields the best FID at both 0.107 and 0.175 bpp compared to HFD/DDPM, MS-ILLM, and the multi-realism approach. PerCo also obtains consistent improvements in CLIP score and mIoU (semantic alignment metrics) over all baselines, particularly at low rates, though it trails on pixel-level distortion metrics (LPIPS, MS-SSIM) at rates above 0.01 bpp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Text-Sketch (Lei et al., 2023), the only prior work evaluating at comparable ultra-low bitrates, PerCo achieves substantially better FID, KID, LPIPS, MS-SSIM, CLIP, and mIoU across all tested rates, with reconstructions that are more faithful in color and spatial alignment. Compared to the adversarial-loss codecs MS-ILLM and HiFiC, PerCo wins decisively on FID and KID at rates below 0.04 bpp (where these methods produce visibly blurry or blocky artefacts), but loses on LPIPS and MS-SSIM at rates above 0.01 bpp, consistent with the rate-distortion-perception tradeoff. Against the hand-crafted VTM codec, PerCo dominates on all perceptual and semantic metrics (FID, KID, CLIP, mIoU) at low rates while VTM retains an edge on MS-SSIM at higher rates. On MS-COCO 256×256, PerCo outperforms both HFD/DDPM (Hoogeboom et al., 2023) and the multi-realism approach of Agustsson et al. (2023) on FID at their lowest available bitrates (0.107 and 0.175 bpp). DIRAC (Ghouse et al., 2023) is also outperformed on FID and KID at low bitrates. Overall, PerCo's key advantage is the stability of FID/KID across the full bitrate range, whereas all baselines show significant degradation as bitrate decreases.

## Relation to Prior Work

PerCo sits at the intersection of two lineages: neural image compression (Ballé et al. 2017→2018, HiFiC, MS-ILLM) and diffusion-based generative modeling (GLIDE, Theis et al. 2022). Its key architectural departure is repurposing a large pretrained text-to-image diffusion model as a *decoder* within a VQ-VAE-like autoencoder, rather than using diffusion for compression directly (Theis et al. 2022) or relying on prompt inversion and binary contour maps (Lei et al. 2023). The distortion loss is reformulated as the diffusion denoising objective (Eq. 2–3) instead of MSE or LPIPS, and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021) is adapted to contrast local VQ features alone versus local+textual conditioning. This positions PerCo as the first method to combine learned VQ spatial features, feed-forward captioning, and a diffusion decoder in a single end-to-end compression pipeline at ultra-low bitrates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most impactful finding is that FID and KID remain remarkably stable across bitrates from 0.126 down to 0.003 bpp, empirically demonstrating the rate-distortion-perception decoupling predicted by Blau & Michaeli (2019) at a bitrate regime previously unexplored (a 512×768 image in <153 bytes). This suggests that generative decoders can fundamentally change the bitrate–realism tradeoff curve, making 'perfect realism' codecs more tractable. Key limitations temper the impact: decoding is 8–32× slower than adversarial codecs due to iterative denoising, PSNR/MS-SSIM/LPIPS remain worse than feed-forward methods (partly due to the frozen LDM autoencoder bottleneck), and evaluation is capped at 512×512/768×512 resolution. Open questions include whether patch-based or cascaded approaches can extend this to higher resolutions, and whether the autoencoder bottleneck can be overcome without sacrificing the generative prior's realism advantage.
