## Problem

Current zero-shot VLM classification methods generate class descriptions based solely on each class in isolation, without considering which other classes in the target dataset are likely to be confused with it. As a result, the generated attributes may provide no useful differentiating information—for example, describing a sparrow as 'brown' does not help distinguish it from a wren, which is also brown, whereas bill shape would. The paper asks whether class descriptions can be tailored to each dataset to resolve these specific ambiguities.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of class names (no labeled examples) and produces a single class label. It operates in two stages: a first-pass CLIP classification identifies the k most ambiguous classes per image, and a second-pass classification restricted to those classes uses LLM-generated differential descriptions. The LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo or Llama 2) is queried per pair of ambiguous classes to produce photo-caption-style descriptions highlighting visually distinguishing attributes, with no additional images or training data required.

## Method

FuDD (Follow-up Differential Descriptions) is a zero-shot two-stage classification pipeline. In stage one, standard CLIP cosine-similarity classification identifies the k most similar (ambiguous) classes for each image. In stage two, for every pair of ambiguous classes, an LLM is prompted via in-context learning to generate pairwise differential descriptions—photo captions that explicitly contrast a visually distinguishing attribute between the two classes (e.g., 'a photo of a black-footed albatross, with a yellow bill' vs. 'a photo of a laysan albatross, with a pink bill'). These pairwise descriptions are unioned per class to form a differential description set, which is then used in a follow-up CLIP classification restricted to only the ambiguous classes to resolve the initial ambiguity. The key insight is that effective class descriptions must be relative to the specific competing classes in the dataset, not generic attribute lists, and that focusing on the top-k most ambiguous classes captures most of the performance gain while keeping LLM query costs low.

## Results

FuDD achieves consistent improvements over all baselines across 12 image classification datasets. With the ViT-L/14@336px backbone, FuDD(k=|C|) outperforms naive LLM-generated descriptions by 2.41 percentage points on average, with the largest gain of 13.95 points on EuroSAT (60.64% vs. 46.69%). On ImageNet, FuDD(k=|C|) reaches 77.00% top-1 accuracy (L/14*), surpassing the template set (76.54%) and naive LLM (76.37%). Using only k=10 ambiguous classes captures most of the benefit while reducing computational cost, e.g., 76.70% on ImageNet and 60.64% on EuroSAT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP's standard prompts (Single Template and Template Set, Radford et al. 2021), FuDD wins on 11 of 12 datasets with both backbones, confirming that differential descriptions provide information beyond simple prompt ensembling. Compared to Naive LLM (Menon & Vondrick 2023), FuDD improves by 2.41 points on average (L/14*) and up to 13.95 points on EuroSAT, though it slightly underperforms on DTD (−1.06, B/32) and Food101 (−0.21, L/14*). Against LaBo (Yang et al. 2023), which requires labeled samples for description selection, zero-shot FuDD outperforms 16-shot LaBo on ImageNet (75.67% vs. 72.60%) and full-shot LaBo on Food101 (93.28% vs. 92.45%), while remaining competitive on CUB, DTD, Aircraft, and Flowers102. FuDD also surpasses WaffleCLIP (Roth et al. 2023) on 7 of 8 datasets, showing that high-level category names alone are insufficient for complex ambiguities. Versus CoOp (Zhou et al. 2022), FuDD without any labeled images beats 16-shot CoOp on Food101 (80.95% vs. 74.67%) and Oxford Pets (87.52% vs. 87.01%), and beats 4-shot CoOp on ImageNet (60.78% vs. 59.99%). Finally, FuDD matches or approaches SuS-X (Udandarao et al. 2022) on most datasets while avoiding the need for text-to-image generation and synthetic images entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based zero-shot classification paradigm (Radford et al., 2021) and the LLM-generated class description approach of Menon & Vondrick (2023) by making a key conceptual shift: instead of generating descriptions that characterize a class in isolation, FuDD generates *differential* descriptions that explicitly contrast a class against its most confusable neighbors in the target dataset. This departs from prior work (Naive LLM, LaBo, WaffleCLIP) that either describes classes generically or selects from pre-generated attributes using labeled data. The method also positions itself as a zero-shot alternative to prompt-tuning (CoOp) and text-to-image augmentation (SuS-X) approaches, showing competitive performance without any labeled samples or synthetic images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the *type* of semantic information in class descriptions matters as much as the quantity: non-differential descriptions can actually degrade performance, and only the top-5 most ambiguous classes account for most of the accuracy gains, enabling significant computational savings. The finding that a zero-shot method can match or exceed 16-shot prompt-tuning on several datasets is practically important for settings where labeled data is scarce. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-set or very large class hierarchies, whether differential descriptions transfer across VLM architectures, and whether the LLM fine-tuning results (especially the 23% EuroSAT gain with Llama 2) generalize to other rare-concept domains beyond the tested datasets.
