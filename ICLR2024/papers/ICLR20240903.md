## Problem

Feed-forward networks account for roughly two-thirds of parameters in a typical Transformer layer, yet they have been systematically excluded from norm-based attention-map analyses because their nonlinear activation (e.g., GELU) breaks the additive decomposition that makes those analyses work. This leaves a major gap in understanding how FF blocks reshape token-to-token contextualization and how their effects interact with surrounding residual and normalization layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240903/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained Transformer (BERT, RoBERTa, GPT-2, or OPT variants) and a batch of text sequences (Wikipedia excerpts or SST-2 movie reviews), tokenized at the subword level. For each sequence of length n, it produces a set of n×n refined attention maps at four progressively broader analysis scopes (ATB, ATBFF, ATBFFRES, ATBFFRESLN), from which it derives contextualization change scores (1 − Spearman ρ between consecutive scopes), FF-amp matrices (post-FF minus pre-FF attention maps), and manually annotated linguistic composition categories for the top-amplified token pairs.

## Method

The core technical contribution is extending the norm-based decomposition of a Transformer layer to include the feed-forward block. The key obstacle is that the element-wise nonlinear activation g (e.g., GELU) does not distribute over sums, so the layer output cannot be written as a sum of per-token transformed vectors in closed form. The authors resolve this by applying Integrated Gradients (IG) with a zero baseline (valid because g(0)=0) to decompose the activation: each token's pre-activation contribution F_i^{Pre g}(x_j) is attributed to the post-activation output via an IG integral along the straight-line path from 0 to the summed pre-activation. This yields an exact, completeness-satisfying decomposition of the entire layer into per-token vectors whose L2 norms form the refined attention map. By computing these maps at four scopes and taking differences (FF-amp matrices), the authors isolate each FFB component's contextualization effect and quantify how RES2 and LN2 subsequently cancel FF's changes—revealing that FF encodes its contextualization signal in a small set of outlier dimensions that LN's γ weights systematically suppress.

## Results

The paper reports by-layer average contextualization change scores (1−ρ) for each FFB component: FF incurs 0.21 (BERT-base) and 0.15 (GPT-2), while LN incurs 0.27 (BERT) and 0.38 (GPT-2). The FF's contextualization effect is largely canceled by subsequent components: RES2 bypass vectors are more than 2× larger in L2 norm than FF outputs, and LN's γ weight negatively correlates with FF output outlier dimensions (Pearson r from −0.45 to −0.78 in BERT, −0.22 to −0.59 in GPT-2). When ignoring the bottom 1% outlier dimensions (lowest γ), FF's contextualization change drops from 0.21→0.09 (BERT) and 0.15→0.02 (GPT-2), confirming that FF's effect is concentrated in very specific dimensions that LN actively suppresses. FF amplification is not explained by word co-occurrence (Spearman ρ with PMI: 0.06–0.14 across layers).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary methodological comparison is between Integrated Gradients (IG) and three Shapley Value (SV) variants (Exact, Sampling, Kernel) for decomposing the nonlinear GELU activation in FF. On BERT-tiny with inputs of length ≤15, all four methods yield nearly identical contextualization change scores for FF, RES2, and LN2 (Figure 11), confirming that the paper's findings are robust to the choice of attribution method. However, SV is computationally infeasible for practical use: while IG completes in under 1 second, all SV methods require over 15 minutes for input length n=15, making IG the only viable option for full-scale analysis. The paper also implicitly compares its norm-based + IG approach to DecompX (Modarressi et al., 2023), noting that DecompX uses a coarse linear approximation for the FF nonlinearity whereas this paper's IG-based decomposition is exact (satisfying the completeness axiom), though a direct empirical comparison is not performed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the norm-based analysis framework of Kobayashi et al. (2020, 2021), which decomposed the attention block (ATTN, RES1, LN1) into a sum of transformed input vectors whose norms yield refined attention maps, by incorporating the previously excluded feed-forward block (FF, RES2, LN2). The key technical departure is the use of Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017) to handle the nonlinear GELU activation that prevents a closed-form additive decomposition, enabling exact (completeness-satisfying) attribution through the FF. The closest competitor, DecompX (Modarressi et al., 2023), also addresses FF decomposition but relies on a coarse linear approximation, whereas this paper's IG-based approach is exact. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of norm-based attention-map analysis and mechanistic interpretability (e.g., Anthropic's Transformer Circuits Thread), providing a token-to-token lens on FF processing that complements prior FF-as-key-value-memory studies (Geva et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that FF's contextualization effects are substantially canceled by the surrounding residual and normalization layers—specifically through outlier dimensions in FF output that LN's γ parameter shrinks—reveals a concrete mechanism of redundancy within the Transformer layer, with implications for model compression and pruning strategies. The identification of specific linguistic compositions amplified by FF (subword-to-word, word-to-multi-word-expression) provides a new interpretability lens on FF function that is complementary to key-value-memory analyses. However, the analysis is limited to models up to ~125M parameters due to computational cost scaling with both model size and input length, leaving open whether the cancellation dynamics persist in billion-parameter models. Future directions include extending the analysis to architectures with local attention (Mistral) or mixture-of-experts (Mixtral), and studying inter-layer contextualization dynamics.
