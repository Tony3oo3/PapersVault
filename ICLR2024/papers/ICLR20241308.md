## Problem

Variational Information Pursuit (V-IP) produces interpretable predictions by sequentially asking binary queries about an image, but applying it requires a manually specified query set and densely annotated query answers to train answering classifiers, limiting it to small-scale tasks. This paper removes both bottlenecks by using GPT to auto-generate task-relevant semantic concept queries and by training a lightweight answering network with pseudo-labels derived from GPT and CLIP, eliminating the need for any manual annotation.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB image and a GPT-generated set of binary concept queries (e.g., 'Is the concept mammal present in the image?') as input. It outputs a class label prediction accompanied by a short, interpretable chain of binary yes/no query-answer pairs that serve as a faithful explanation. The Concept-QA answering network itself consumes CLIP ViT-B/16 image embeddings and concept text embeddings (both ℓ₂-normalized) and produces a scalar probability that is thresholded (τ=0.4 or 0.5) into a binary answer.

## Method

The core contribution is a lightweight Concept-QA network (a 70K-parameter MLP on top of frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 embeddings, ~150M total parameters) trained to answer binary 'Is concept C present in image x?' queries without any manually annotated data. Pseudo-labels are constructed by combining two signals: GPT's class-level judgment of whether a concept is salient for recognizing a given class (yielding a binary P_GPT), and CLIP's per-image min-max-normalized dot product between image and concept embeddings (yielding a continuous P_CLIP). The combined pseudo-label is P(C=1|X,Y) = P_GPT(C=1|Y)·P_CLIP(C=1|X), with the complementary probability assigned to C=0, and the network is trained via cross-entropy on these soft targets. At inference, the trained Concept-QA feeds binary answers into V-IP, which sequentially selects the most informative next query (via a learned querier network) until a posterior-based or stability-based stopping criterion is met, producing a short interpretable explanation. This design is an order of magnitude more parameter-efficient than VLM-based answering (e.g., BLIP-2 FlanT5 at 4.1B parameters) while achieving competitive or superior answer faithfulness, enabling feasible V-IP training on large datasets like ImageNet.

## Results

Concept-QA achieves 87% accuracy and 0.56 F1 on ImageNet, 83%/0.45 on Places365, 80%/0.54 on CUB-200, 80%/0.62 on CIFAR-10, and 80%/0.38 on CIFAR-100 for concept-presence faithfulness, substantially outperforming binarized CLIP dot products (50–58% accuracy across all datasets). In the end-to-end V-IP setting, Concept-QA+V-IP achieves 68.2% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet with an average explanation length of 49.6 queries, 95.6% on CIFAR-10 (5.5 queries), and 67.0% on CUB-200 (24.7 queries), approaching the accuracy of non-interpretable ViT-B/16 baselines (84.2%, 99.0%, 90.6% respectively). The Concept-QA model uses only 70K parameters (150M total with CLIP) and answers each query in ~0.04 s, versus ~1.52 s for BLIP2-FlanT5 (4.1B parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP-based baselines (CLIP-Bin_std and CLIP-Bin_norm), Concept-QA wins decisively on all five datasets, improving accuracy by 22–32 points (e.g., 87% vs. 55% on ImageNet, 80% vs. 51% on CIFAR-100), confirming that simple thresholding of CLIP dot products produces noisy, uninterpretable answers. Against the strongest VLM baseline BLIP2-FlanT5 XL (Li et al., 2023), Concept-QA is competitive on ImageNet (87% vs. 86%) and CIFAR-10 (80% vs. 83%), clearly superior on CUB-200 (80% vs. 70%), but trails on Places365 (83% vs. 87%) and CIFAR-100 (80% vs. 87%). For pseudo-label generation, GPT+CLIP slightly underperforms InstructBLIP (Dai et al., 2023) on Places365 F1 (0.42 vs. 0.66) but matches or exceeds it on all other datasets, while substantially outperforming LLaVa-1.5 7B (Liu et al., 2024) on every dataset (e.g., 87% vs. 63% accuracy on ImageNet). In the end-to-end V-IP comparison (Table 2), Concept-QA+V-IP reaches within 6–12 points of supervised ViT-B/16 (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020) and the fine-grained CUB-200 baseline (Demidov et al., 2023: 90.6%), and within 12 points of the Places365 baseline (Singh et al., 2022: 58.2%), while providing interpretable query-answer chains of 5.5–49.6 queries on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Variational Information Pursuit (V-IP), a variational implementation of the Information Pursuit framework for interpretable-by-design prediction, by removing its two major practical bottlenecks: the need for manually specified query sets and manually annotated query answers. For query generation, it leverages the concept-extraction pipeline from label-free Concept Bottleneck Model work (Oikarinen et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023), repurposing GPT-generated semantic concepts as binary interpretable queries. For answering, it departs from the natural choice of CLIP's continuous dot products (which the paper shows break the interpretability of V-IP's query-answer chains) and instead trains a 70K-parameter MLP (Concept-QA) on pseudo-labels that combine GPT's class-level concept salience priors with CLIP's image-level association scores, achieving competitive accuracy with billion-parameter VLMs like BLIP-2 at a fraction of the compute.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes V-IP practical for the first time on large-scale benchmarks like ImageNet, reducing one epoch of V-IP training from ~4,128 GPU hours (with BLIP-2) to ~0.5 GPU hours (with Concept-QA), an 80× speedup that removes a previously prohibitive barrier. The key open question is the accuracy gap introduced by pseudo-labels: Concept-QA+V-IP still lags black-box ViT-B/16 by 10–20 points on several datasets, and the authors acknowledge that a hybrid approach combining small amounts of manual annotation with pseudo-labels could close this gap. The method is also currently limited to image classification due to the assumption that the class object is the focal point of the image, leaving extension to segmentation, captioning, and visual reasoning as future work.
