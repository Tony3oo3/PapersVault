## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is the standard technique for balancing fidelity and diversity in conditional diffusion models, but it treats the condition space as unstructured, ignoring the continuity inherent in continuous conditions like CLIP text embeddings. The paper asks whether exploiting this continuity can yield a better fidelity-diversity trade-off without retraining the model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240175/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (encoded to a 77×768 CLIP embedding) and a pretrained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5 with its U-Net) as input, and produces a generated RGB image at the model's native 512×512 resolution. At each sampling step, the second-order ICFG requires three forward passes of the U-Net—at the unconditional embedding, the full condition embedding, and a scaled intermediate embedding mc—rather than the two passes of standard CFG. The condition space is explicitly structured as a cone with vertex at the empty-prompt embedding, and two variants (C_all scaling all dimensions, C_nouns scaling only noun positions) are evaluated.

## Method

The paper introduces Inner Classifier-Free Guidance (ICFG), which absorbs the guidance strength β directly into the condition vector (βc) under a cone-structure assumption on the condition space, so that the enhanced distribution q(x_t | βc) follows the original forward diffusion process exactly. CFG is then shown to be the first-order Taylor expansion of ICFG at β=1, and the paper derives a second-order expansion whose remainder is bounded under a derivative-growth assumption (Theorem 4.1). Practically, the second-order term is estimated via a finite-difference formula using three U-Net evaluations at conditions 0, c, and mc, with a free hyperparameter v decoupling the second-order strength from the first-order w to mitigate bias from the unchanged training policy. A companion training algorithm (Algorithm 1) normalizes conditions by a data-condition similarity metric to help the model learn the cone structure, validated via LoRA fine-tuning on 30 MS-COCO images.

## Results

On the MS-COCO validation set (10,000 images), the proposed second-order ICFG with default settings (w=2.0, v=0.25, m=1.1, C=C_all) achieves FID 15.28 and CLIP Score 26.11%, simultaneously improving over CFG at the same w=2.0 (FID 15.42, CLIP Score 25.80%). The best FID across all settings is 15.22 (w=2.0, C=C_nouns, v=0.25), while the best CLIP Score reaches 26.86% (w=5.0, C=C_all, v=1.0). The paper demonstrates that the second-order term introduces a new degree of freedom (v) that allows decoupling the FID–CLIP Score trade-off, with C_all yielding a superior balance and C_nouns yielding the lowest FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG, Ho & Salimans 2022), evaluated at guidance strengths w=1.0 through 5.0. Second-order ICFG consistently outperforms CFG at every w value on both FID and CLIP Score when using C_all with v=0.25: at the default w=2.0, FID drops from 15.42 to 15.28 (−0.14) while CLIP Score rises from 25.80% to 26.11% (+0.31 pp), a rare simultaneous improvement on both axes. With C_nouns, the FID advantage is slightly larger (15.22 vs. 15.42 at w=2.0) but the CLIP Score gain is smaller. At higher w values (4.0, 5.0), the FID gap narrows (e.g., 17.98 vs. 18.18 at w=4.0) while the CLIP Score gap widens (26.51% vs. 26.34%), confirming that the second-order term primarily helps maintain fidelity at aggressive guidance strengths. The paper also reports a U-ViT experiment (Table 4) where ICFG further reduces FID relative to CFG, and a speedup analysis showing that restricting the second-order term to timesteps 0.2–0.8 retains nearly full FID benefit while cutting extra inference time by 26.27%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets classifier-free guidance (CFG, Ho & Salimans 2022) as a first-order Taylor expansion of a more general 'inner classifier-free guidance' (ICFG) framework that assumes a cone structure on the condition space. The formulation is grounded in the score-based SDE framework of Song et al. (2021b) and connects to energy-based guidance (Zhao et al. 2022; Lu et al. 2023), but the key departure is treating the guidance strength as an intrinsic scaling of the condition vector rather than an external multiplier on the score difference. The method is applied specifically to Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al. 2022), leveraging the continuity of CLIP text embeddings to define the cone structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is modest: a second-order ICFG term improves FID/CLIP Score balance on Stable Diffusion v1.5 by small margins (~0.1–0.2 FID, ~0.3 CLIP) without retraining, at the cost of an extra forward pass per step. The cone-structure assumption on the condition space is somewhat ad hoc and its naturalness for arbitrary text encoders remains unvalidated. Open questions include whether higher-order expansions yield further gains, whether the cone structure is genuinely present in learned embedding spaces, and whether the approach transfers to other architectures (only U-ViT is briefly tested). The three-forward-pass requirement also limits practical adoption for latency-sensitive applications.
