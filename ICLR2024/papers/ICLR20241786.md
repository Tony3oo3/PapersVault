## Problem

Existing DRL-based solvers for Job-Shop Scheduling (JSSP) rely on construction heuristics that build schedules step-by-step, but their disjunctive-graph representations are ill-suited for encoding partial solutions, leading to severe information loss (e.g., missing disjunctive arcs among undispatched operations) and large optimality gaps. This paper asks whether reformulating the task as learning-to-search-over-complete-solutions (improvement) rather than learning-to-construct can circumvent the partial-representation problem and yield substantially better schedules.

## Input / Output

The method takes a JSSP instance (|J| jobs, |M| machines, processing times) and an initial schedule produced by the FDD/MWKR dispatching rule. At each improvement step, the state is a complete solution encoded as a directed disjunctive graph with per-node features (processing time, earliest start, latest start); the action space is dynamic, restricted to operation pairs within critical blocks on the current critical path (N5 neighbourhood). The policy outputs a single operation pair to swap, and after T steps the final output is the best makespan found along the trajectory.

## Method

The core contribution is a GNN-based policy that directly selects local moves in the N5 neighbourhood, eliminating the need to evaluate the entire neighbourhood. The graph embedding uses two parallel modules: a Topological Embedding Module (TPM) built on Graph Isomorphism Networks to capture structural differences between solutions, and a Context-aware Embedding Module (CAM) built on Graph Attention Networks that separately processes job-precedence arcs and machine-sequence arcs before averaging. Training uses a custom n-step REINFORCE algorithm that updates the policy every n steps along the trajectory, addressing sparse rewards and memory constraints for long episodes. A novel message-passing evaluator (proven equivalent to the Critical Path Method) computes earliest/latest start times for batches of graphs in parallel on GPU, achieving up to 11× speedup over sequential CPM at batch size 512. The full policy is proven to have linear time complexity in both |J| and |M|.

## Results

On the seven classic JSSP benchmarks (Taillard, ABZ, FT, LA, SWV, ORB, YN), the proposed DRL-guided improvement heuristic (Ours-500) achieves average gaps of 9.3%–17.5% to the best known solutions, substantially outperforming the best DRL construction baselines (ScheduleNet: 15.3%–23.7%, RL-GNN: 20.1%–32.0%, L2D: 24.7%–36.3%) while running 5–100× faster on large instances. With 5000 improvement steps, the method reaches a 3.0% gap on Taillard 100×20, exceeding CP-SAT (3.9% at 1h). On extremely large instances (up to 1000×40), Ours-500 outperforms CP-SAT by 16–24% in gap while solving in minutes rather than hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three DRL construction baselines, the proposed method wins decisively: Ours-500 reduces the gap by 5–15 percentage points over ScheduleNet (the strongest DRL baseline), 10–15 pp over RL-GNN, and 15–20 pp over L2D across most benchmark sizes, while also being faster on instances larger than 20×15. Against CP-SAT, Ours-500 is competitive on medium instances (e.g., 7.9% vs 3.9% on Taillard 100×20) but with 5000 steps it surpasses CP-SAT on Taillard 100×20 (3.0% vs 3.9%) and on all three extremely large instances (200×40, 500×60, 1000×40) by 16–24% gap. Against the online DRL method of Tassel et al. (2021), Ours-5000 achieves a 12.6% gap on Taillard 30×20 in 144s versus their 13.08% in 600s. Against the tabu search of Zhang et al. (2007) adapted to N5, the method trails by ~1.9% relative gap at 5000 fixed steps but outperforms it under a fixed 90-second wall-clock budget (e.g., 5.9% vs 6.1% on Taillard 15×15), owing to its ability to skip full neighbourhood evaluation. Against hand-crafted improvement rules (greedy, best-improvement, first-improvement), Ours-500 consistently achieves 2–4 pp lower gaps and is 5–10× faster, and unlike the greedy rule it avoids the 'cycling' failure mode that halts improvement after ~500 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant DRL-for-JSSP paradigm of learning construction heuristics (L2D, RL-GNN, ScheduleNet), which encode partial solutions as disjunctive graphs and struggle with incomplete topological information. Instead, it reframes the problem as learning an improvement heuristic over complete solutions, where disjunctive graphs naturally capture all structural information. The approach draws on the DRL-guided local search paradigm established for routing problems (Chen & Tian, 2019; Wu et al., 2021) but adapts it to JSSP with a novel dual-module GNN (GIN for topology + GAT for heterogeneous neighbour contexts) and a GPU-parallel message-passing schedule evaluator. It also builds on the N5 neighbourhood structure from classical tabu search (Nowicki & Smutnicki, 2005; Zhang et al., 2007), replacing hand-crafted move-selection rules with a learned policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual shift from construction to improvement heuristics addresses a fundamental representation bottleneck in DRL-based JSSP solvers, and the resulting performance gains (6–12% gap vs. 15–30% for prior DRL methods) are substantial. The demonstrated ability to outperform CP-SAT on instances up to 1000×40 jobs with only 500 improvement steps suggests learned local search can be competitive with or superior to highly optimized exact solvers at scale, which has practical implications for industrial scheduling. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex improvement frameworks (iterated local search, large-neighborhood search), other scheduling variants (flow shop, open shop), and whether the dual-module GNN design generalizes beyond the specific N5 neighbourhood structure.
