## Problem

Graph transformers rely on dense attention over all node pairs, incurring quadratic or cubic complexity that is infeasible for large-scale graphs. Mini-batch training is a natural remedy, but the limited number of nodes per batch cannot support effective dense attention to capture the full topological and feature information of the graph. The paper addresses how to enable scalable mini-batch graph transformer training while preserving local topology, global context, long-range interactions, and heterophilous structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph (adjacency matrix, node feature matrix) and optionally node labels for content-aware virtual connections. For each target node, a precomputed token list of length 1+L+k̄+k̂ is constructed offline by sampling neighbors via personalized PageRank on the original graph and on two rewired variants (with structure-aware and content-aware super nodes). The token list is then fed through a standard multi-head self-attention transformer to produce a node representation vector, which is read out (e.g., mean pooling) for downstream classification. PPR ranking scores serve as positional encodings, and the entire tokenization step runs in O(m + k log k) time, independent of graph size for the PPR sampling.

## Method

VCR-Graphormer decouples graph topology from model training by assigning each node a token list sampled via personalized PageRank, then applying standard multi-head self-attention only on that list. To enrich the token list beyond local PPR neighbors, the graph is rewired with two types of virtual connections: structure-aware super nodes (one per METIS cluster, connecting all cluster members) to capture global structure and long-range paths, and content-aware super nodes (one per label or feature cluster) to encode heterophilous and content-based global information. The final token list for node u comprises four components: u's own features, multi-hop PPR aggregations (local topology with decaying weights), top-k̄ structure-aware PPR neighbors, and top-k̂ content-aware PPR neighbors, each concatenated with its PPR score as a positional signal. The paper proves this PPR tokenization is equivalent to a GCN with a fixed polynomial filter and jumping knowledge, and that virtual connections provably transfer probability mass from faded to brightened nodes, extending the receptive field. The overall tokenization complexity is O(m + k log k) versus O(n³) for eigendecomposition-based approaches like NAGphormer.

## Results

On small-scale node classification benchmarks, VCR-Graphormer achieves 89.77% on PubMed, 71.67% on CoraFull, 91.75% on Computer, 95.53% on Photo, 95.37% on CS, and 97.34% on Physics, matching or exceeding the best scalable graph transformer NAGphormer on 5 of 6 datasets. On large-scale datasets, VCR-Graphormer reaches 93.69% on Reddit (beating NAGphormer's 93.58%) but trails slightly on Aminer (53.59% vs. 54.04%) and Amazon2M (76.09% vs. 77.43%). On the large-scale heterophilous arXiv-year benchmark, VCR-Graphormer achieves 54.15%, competitive with GloGNN++ (54.79%) and significantly outperforming GNN baselines like MixHop (51.81%) and GPR-GNN (45.07%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCR-Graphormer outperforms the closest scalable graph transformer NAGphormer on PubMed (+0.07), CoraFull (+0.16), Computer (+0.53), and Photo (+0.04), while NAGphormer edges ahead on CS (95.75 vs. 95.37) and they tie on Physics (97.34). Against dense-attention graph transformers (GT, Graphormer, SAN, GraphGPS), VCR-Graphormer achieves comparable or better accuracy while avoiding OOM failures that plague those methods on multiple datasets (e.g., Graphormer OOMs on PubMed, CoraFull, Computer, and Physics). On large-scale datasets, VCR-Graphormer wins on Reddit (93.69 vs. NAGphormer's 93.58) but trails NAGphormer on Aminer (53.59 vs. 54.04) and Amazon2M (76.09 vs. 77.43), though it still beats all scalable GNN baselines (GRAND+, GraphSAINT, PPRGo). On the heterophilous arXiv-year benchmark, VCR-Graphormer (54.15%) is competitive with GloGNN++ (54.79%) and GloGNN (54.68%) while substantially outperforming MixHop (51.81%), GPR-GNN (45.07%), and GCN (46.02%), and avoids OOM issues that affect WRGAT and GGCN. The paper also demonstrates that VCR-Graphormer's PPR-based tokenization is faster than NAGphormer's eigendecomposition on Amazon2M (~620s + ~409s vs. ~682s for eigendecomposition alone), with the key advantage being that PPR sampling scales to larger graphs without cubic complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tokenized graph transformer paradigm introduced by NAGphormer (Chen et al., 2023), which assigns each node a token list for mini-batch self-attention, by replacing eigendecomposition-based positional encoding with personalized PageRank (PPR) sampling. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of virtual connections—structure-aware super nodes via METIS clustering and content-aware super nodes via labels—that rewire the graph before PPR sampling, enabling the token list to capture global topology, long-range interactions, and heterophily without cubic-cost spectral methods. Theoretically, the paper proves that PPR-based tokenization with self-attention is equivalent to a GCN with a fixed polynomial filter and jumping knowledge (Xu et al., 2018), grounding the approach in the GCN literature (Kipf and Welling, 2017) while positioning it as a scalable alternative to dense-attention graph transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VCR-Graphormer provides a practical, theoretically grounded path to mini-batch training of graph transformers on million-node graphs (Amazon2M, 2.4M nodes) with O(m + k log k) tokenization complexity, avoiding the O(n³) eigendecomposition bottleneck of NAGphormer. The content-aware virtual connections offer a simple mechanism to address heterophily, a persistent weakness of structure-based GNNs, with competitive results on both small and large-scale heterophilous benchmarks. However, on large-scale homophilous datasets (Aminer, Amazon2M), VCR-Graphormer trails NAGphormer by 0.4–1.3 points, suggesting the virtual-connection heuristic has not yet fully matched the information content of full spectral decomposition. Open questions include whether the four-component token list design is optimal, how the method behaves under transductive vs. inductive settings, and whether the METIS partitioning granularity can be learned rather than fixed.
