## Problem

Applying contrastive learning (e.g., SimCLR) in federated settings is non-trivial because the global multi-view mutual information objective does not decompose into a sum of local client-wise losses, preventing straightforward use of FedAvg. Additionally, it remains unclear how different sources of non-i.i.d.-ness (label skew, covariate shift, joint shift) should influence the choice between local and global MI objectives for downstream task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and generates two augmented views per image via random cropping, horizontal flip, color distortion, and grayscale. A ResNet-18 encoder (with group normalization replacing batch normalization) maps each view to a 128-dimensional representation through an MLP projector. In the semi-supervised variant, 10% of each client's data carries class labels, which are used to restrict contrastive pairs to same-class datapoints and to train auxiliary label-prediction heads. Evaluation uses a linear probe trained on the frozen representations.

## Method

The paper derives a decomposition of the global multi-view MI into a local conditional MI term, a client-ID MI term, and an excess client-ID MI term (Eq. 2). The local term is bounded by the standard InfoNCE loss computed per client, while the client-ID terms are bounded by a user-verification (UV) loss: a shared linear classifier (no bias, unit-norm constraints, per-client weight updates) that predicts the client ID from each view's representation. Combining these yields Federated SimCLR, a loss that decomposes into local objectives and is optimizable with standard FedAvg/Adam. For the semi-supervised extension, the contrastive loss is restricted to same-class pairs and two additional label-prediction losses (one per view) are added. A key empirical finding is that the UV loss acts as a beneficial proxy for the downstream task under label skew (where I(y;s) is high) but becomes detrimental under covariate shift (where I(y;s)=0), in which case plain local SimCLR is preferable.

## Results

In the unsupervised setting on CIFAR-10, Federated SimCLR (with user-verification loss) achieves 85.0% linear-probe accuracy under label skew, outperforming Local SimCLR (79.4%) by ~6%, while under covariate shift Local SimCLR (74.3%) slightly edges out Federated SimCLR (73.8%). On CIFAR-100, the label-skew gap widens to 48.5% vs 42.2%, and under joint shift Federated SimCLR reaches 43.1% vs 38.1%. In the semi-supervised setting (10% labels), Federated SimCLR achieves 78.0% on CIFAR-10 under label skew, surpassing the fully supervised baseline (75.1%), and the UV loss generalizes to Spectral CL (87.8% vs 76.5% on CIFAR-10 label skew) and SimSiam (80.4% vs 75.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Spectral CL (HaoChen et al., NeurIPS 2021), adding the UV loss yields large gains under label skew (87.8% vs 76.5% on CIFAR-10, 41.0% vs 33.3% on CIFAR-100) but hurts under covariate shift (71.7% vs 73.5% on CIFAR-10), confirming the theoretical prediction that the UV term is beneficial only when I(y;s)>0. Against SimSiam (Chen & He, CVPR 2021), Federated SimCLR dominates by a wide margin (85.0% vs 40.0% on CIFAR-10 label skew), as SimSiam suffers from representation collapse under group normalization in the federated setting. Against FeatARC (Wang et al., ICLR 2022), Federated SimCLR outperforms the alignment-regularized variant across all three non-i.i.d. settings on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 85.0% vs 70.4% under label skew), with FeatARC's alignment loss causing instability under covariate shift. In the semi-supervised setting, Federated SimCLR (78.0%) outperforms both SemiFed (60.0%) and CBAFed (66.3%) on CIFAR-10 under label skew, and the UV loss rescues SemiFed to 76.7%, showing the auxiliary task is broadly applicable beyond SimCLR. Overall, the key finding is that the UV loss helps when non-i.i.d.-ness correlates with labels (label skew, joint shift) but hurts under pure covariate shift, where the local objective is preferable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SimCLR to the federated setting by deriving a tractable lower bound on the global multi-view mutual information that decomposes into per-client objectives, revealing that a user-verification (UV) loss is the principled mechanism for aligning client representations—rather than the ad-hoc global dictionaries (FedCA) or feature alignment regularizers (FeatARC) used in prior work. The MI decomposition (Eq. 2) connects federated contrastive learning to the user-verification literature (Hosseini et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2020), showing that the client-classification head naturally emerges from the global MI bound. The semi-supervised extension further builds on the label-dependent InfoNCE bound, treating same-class contrastive pairs as a form of hard-negative mining, and generalizes the UV-loss insight to non-contrastive methods like SimSiam and spectral contrastive learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that the choice between local and global MI objectives should be guided by the type of non-i.i.d.-ness: the UV loss is beneficial under label skew (where client ID proxies the label) but detrimental under covariate shift (where it forces irrelevant client-specific information into representations). This provides a clear decision rule for federated self-supervised practitioners and a privacy-preserving alternative to methods that communicate raw representations. Limitations include the UV loss's sensitivity to the number of local epochs (requiring frequent synchronization) and the persistent representation collapse of SimSiam under group normalization. Open questions include designing UV losses tolerant of more local optimization and tighter MI bounds for the federated setting.
