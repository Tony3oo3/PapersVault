## Problem

Sparse linear methods (SLIM) and their variants like EASE achieve outstanding collaborative filtering performance, but their quadratic space and nearly cubic time complexity make them memory-intensive and hard to scale. While the item-item weight matrix learned by SLIM can enrich embedding-based models, explicitly computing and storing this I×I matrix is prohibitively expensive. The paper asks whether the useful structural information in SLIM-like models can be extracted in a computationally cheap and memory-efficient way without ever materializing the full weight matrix.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse binary user-item feedback matrix X ∈ {0,1}^{U×I} (implicit feedback only) and an existing item or user embedding matrix Q of dimension L×I where L ≪ I, and produces improved embeddings V of the same dimension. The embedding matrix Q can be randomly initialized, iteratively refined by repeated ImplicitSLIM calls, or taken from a downstream model during training. The output embeddings are used either as initialization (replacing Q before downstream model training) or as a regularizer (alternating optimization between the CF loss and the SLIM regularizer), with the key constraint that all intermediate computations involve only matrices of size at most L×max(U,I).

## Method

ImplicitSLIM connects EASE (a simplified SLIM with closed-form solution) to Locally Linear Embeddings (LLE), showing that EASE's optimization is equivalent to LLE's first step without the sum-to-one constraint. Rather than explicitly computing the I×I weight matrix B̂ and then solving LLE's second step, the method substitutes EASE's closed-form solution into the embedding extraction problem and applies the Woodbury matrix identity to reduce all matrix inversions from I×I to L×L (where L is the embedding dimension, typically 64–4096 vs. I in the hundreds of thousands). A Neumann-series approximation further avoids storing I×I intermediate matrices, so all operations involve only L×max(U,I)-sized quantities. The auxiliary weight matrix A in the regularizer is set equal to Q, making the penalty an approximate pairwise distance, and the method is applied either iteratively for initialization or via alternating optimization as a regularizer within downstream models (MF, PLRec, VAEs, GCNs, BERT4Rec).

## Results

On MovieLens-20M, RecVAE+ImplicitSLIM achieves NDCG@100 of 0.447 (vs. 0.442 for RecVAE alone), and H+Vamp(Gated)+ImplicitSLIM reaches 0.450 (vs. 0.445), setting new state-of-the-art results. MF+ImplicitSLIM improves NDCG@100 from 0.399 to 0.423 on MovieLens-20M and from 0.314 to 0.366 on Million Songs, approaching EASE's 0.420 and 0.389 respectively while being far more memory-efficient. On Netflix Prize, H+Vamp(Gated)+ImplicitSLIM achieves NDCG@100 of 0.410 (vs. 0.407 baseline), and deep models with ImplicitSLIM converge in roughly half the training time to reach their best scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MF+ImplicitSLIM consistently outperforms vanilla MF (Hu et al., 2008) across all three main datasets, e.g., NDCG@100 improves from 0.399 to 0.423 on MovieLens-20M and from 0.314 to 0.366 on Million Songs; it also beats WMF (0.389→0.400 NDCG@100 on ML-20M). Against item-embedding-regularized MF variants, MF+ImplicitSLIM (0.423) surpasses both CoFactor (0.394) and GRALS (0.401) on MovieLens-20M NDCG@100. MF+ImplicitSLIM approaches EASE (Steck, 2019) performance (0.423 vs. 0.420 on ML-20M) while being far more memory-efficient, and clearly outperforms SLIM (Ning & Karypis, 2011) at 0.423 vs. 0.401. For nonlinear autoencoders, RecVAE+ImplicitSLIM improves over RecVAE (Shenbin et al., 2020) on all metrics (e.g., 0.447 vs. 0.442 NDCG@100 on ML-20M) and also beats MultVAE (Liang et al., 2018) and RaCT (Lobel et al., 2019); H+Vamp(Gated)+ImplicitSLIM (Kim & Suh, 2019) similarly improves its base model on all datasets. For GCN-based models, UltraGCN+ImplicitSLIM(items) (Mao et al., 2021) improves NDCG@20 from 0.0561 to 0.0573 on Yelp2018 and from 0.2642 to 0.2659 on MovieLens-1M, both beating LightGCN (He et al., 2020). For sequential models, BERT4Rec+ImplicitSLIM (Sun et al., 2019) improves NDCG@10 from 0.511 to 0.549 on MovieLens-1M at 30 epochs, achieving better final scores in fewer iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

ImplicitSLIM bridges two previously separate lines of work: sparse linear methods (SLIM/EASE) for item-item similarity and locally linear embeddings (LLE) for nonlinear manifold learning. The key conceptual move is recognizing that EASE's closed-form solution is equivalent to LLE's first step (without the sum-to-one constraint), then modifying LLE's second step to accept existing embeddings as a prior and compressing both steps into a single computation via the Woodbury identity. This positions the method distinctly from low-rank SLIM approximations (FISM, LRR) that aim to approximate SLIM itself; instead, ImplicitSLIM extracts structural information from SLIM-like models to improve *other* embedding-based CF models (MF, VAEs, GCNs, sequential models) without ever explicitly materializing the I×I similarity matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ImplicitSLIM offers a generic, low-cost plug-in that consistently improves a wide range of CF architectures and halves convergence time for deep models, making it practically attractive for practitioners who already have an embedding-based pipeline. The new SOTA results on MovieLens-20M and Netflix Prize (via RecVAE/H+Vamp(Gated)) demonstrate that SLIM-like structure, when injected implicitly, can outperform both the explicit SLIM/EASE baselines and the base deep models. Limitations include: the method is restricted to implicit feedback, user-embedding improvements were not observed (possibly due to the strong generalization setting), and it adds hyperparameters (λ, α, and optionally a popularity threshold) to already complex models. An open question is whether the approach extends to explicit-feedback settings or to models where item embeddings are not directly accessible (e.g., fully end-to-end sequential models without a separate item embedding table).
