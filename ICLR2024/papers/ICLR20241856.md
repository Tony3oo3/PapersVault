## Problem

SOTA POMDP algorithms compress observation-action histories into fixed-size RNN hidden states to approximate sufficient statistics, but these representations lack guarantees of preserving the information needed to optimize the return, potentially yielding suboptimal policies. The paper addresses this gap by providing a principled belief-update learning framework with formal guarantees that the learned latent belief representation is suitable for recovering the optimal value function.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241856/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the agent receives both the POMDP observation and the true environment state (Assumption 1), which is used to train the WAE-MDP latent model but is not available at test time. At inference, the agent consumes only the observation-action history, maintaining a sub-belief vector (parameters of a Masked Autoregressive Flow) that is updated via a feed-forward network at each step. The policy outputs an action conditioned on this sub-belief, while the MAF converts it into a full latent belief distribution over the WAE-MDP state space.

## Method

WBU learns three components in a round-robin fashion: (1) a WAE-MDP latent model trained on an augmented POMDP P↑ (with state space S×Ω and deterministic observation function) to capture both transition and observation dynamics; (2) a belief encoder φ built on Masked Autoregressive Flows that approximates the exact belief update rule τ̄ by minimizing a KL-divergence proxy of the Wasserstein distance, updated as a simple feed-forward network without back-propagation through time; and (3) an A2C policy conditioned on the sub-belief. The key architectural distinction from RNN-based methods is that the belief update is learned as a local transition function (analogous to model-based RL) rather than a recurrent compression, and the policy gradients do not flow back through the belief encoder. Theoretical guarantees follow from bisimulation metrics embedded in the WAE-MDP: Theorem 3.2 bounds the value gap between the original and latent POMDPs by the training losses, and Theorem 3.3 ensures the learned representation is almost-Lipschitz with respect to the optimal value function.

## Results

The paper evaluates WBU against R-A2C and DVRL on four POMDP environments (RepeatPrevious, StatelessCartPole, SpaceInvaders, and noisy variants) from POPGym and MinAtar, reporting undiscounted cumulative return as learning curves (Figure 3) rather than a single final metric. WBU is the sole method to demonstrate mid-to-long-term memorization in RepeatPrevious (identifying a card's suit 8 steps earlier). In SpaceInvaders, WBU earns the highest rewards among all three methods. In StatelessCartPole, WBU eventually matches R-A2C's final performance, while in the noisy variants WBU provides superior solutions with less variance than R-A2C.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against R-A2C (Hausknecht & Stone, 2015), WBU clearly wins on RepeatPrevious as the only method achieving mid-to-long-term memorization, and on SpaceInvaders where it earns the highest cumulative rewards. On StatelessCartPole, R-A2C initially excels by leveraging short-term memory to infer hidden velocities, but WBU eventually reaches the same final performance. Under noise (Gaussian on StatelessCartPole, binary radar-mask on SpaceInvaders), WBU swiftly provides superior solutions while R-A2C eventually catches up but with more variance. Against DVRL (Igl et al., 2018), WBU dominates across all environments: DVRL is overtaken on StatelessCartPole, fails to achieve memorization on RepeatPrevious, and earns lower rewards on SpaceInvaders. The paper attributes WBU's advantage to its provably sufficient belief representation (Theorem 3.3) and the decoupling of belief learning from policy optimization, which prevents the policy gradient from corrupting the history encoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WAE-MDP framework (Delgrange et al., 2023), which learns latent space models of fully observable MDPs via Wasserstein autoencoders with bisimulation metrics, to the partially observable setting. It departs from the dominant RNN-based approach (R-A2C, DVRL, FORBES) that compresses history into fixed-size vectors without guarantees, instead learning an explicit belief update rule using masked autoregressive flows and a latent POMDP model. The key methodological departure is the assumption of state access during training (analogous to centralized training in MARL), which enables direct optimization of belief update losses rather than relying on the RL objective to implicitly shape the representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WBU provides the first theoretical guarantees (value difference bounds and representation quality theorems) for belief representations in POMDPs, ensuring that the learned latent belief captures the optimal value function — a property no prior RNN-based method can offer. However, the state-access assumption during training significantly limits practical applicability to simulation-based or sensor-augmented settings, and experiments are confined to small-scale environments (POPGym, MinAtar). Open questions include scaling to high-dimensional observations (images), relaxing the state-access assumption, and whether the feed-forward belief update (without BPTT) generalizes to longer-horizon or more complex POMDPs.
