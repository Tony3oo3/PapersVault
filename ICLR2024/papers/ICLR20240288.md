## Problem

Scaling unsupervised world models for robotics has lagged behind GPT-style language model progress due to two bottlenecks: the observation space is complex and unstructured (unlike naturally tokenized text), and the generative model must decode tens of thousands of tokens per observation in parallel rather than one at a time. This paper addresses both by combining VQVAE tokenization with a discrete diffusion framework that enables parallel decoding of spatio-temporal data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240288/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of past Lidar point cloud observations (5 frames for KITTI/Argoverse2, 2 or 6 frames for NuScenes) and future ego vehicle SE(3) poses as actions, and produces future point cloud observations over a 1s or 3s horizon. Each point cloud is tokenized into a 128×128 Bird-Eye View grid of discrete indices from a 1024-entry codebook, covering a 160m×160m×9m region around the ego vehicle at 15.625cm voxel resolution. The world model operates entirely on these discrete tokens; the final point cloud is rendered via differentiable depth rendering through the tokenizer decoder.

## Method

Copilot4D is a two-stage pipeline: a VQVAE tokenizer encodes point clouds into BEV tokens (PointNet → BEV pooling → Swin Transformer encoder → codebook → Swin Transformer decoder with a Neural Feature Grid branch for differentiable depth rendering and a coarse binary voxel branch for spatial skipping), and a spatio-temporal U-Net Transformer world model predicts future tokens via discrete diffusion. The key algorithmic contribution is recasting MaskGIT as an absorbing-uniform discrete diffusion model: during training, up to η% uniform noise is injected into non-masked tokens and cross-entropy loss is applied to all positions; during sampling, already-decoded tokens can be iteratively re-noised and resampled, which reduces Chamfer distance by 29% over vanilla MaskGIT. The world model interleaves Swin spatial attention and GPT-2 temporal attention across three U-Net resolution levels, trained with a 50/40/10 mixture of future-prediction, joint past-future, and individual-frame objectives under causal temporal masking. Classifier-free diffusion guidance conditions on past agent history at inference, yielding an additional 60% Chamfer reduction.

## Results

Copilot4D achieves state-of-the-art point cloud forecasting across three autonomous driving datasets, reducing prior SOTA Chamfer distance by 65%–75% for 1s prediction and over 50% for 3s prediction. On NuScenes 1s, Copilot4D reaches a Chamfer distance of 0.36 (within ROI) versus 1.41 for 4D-Occ; on KITTI 1s it reaches 0.18 versus 0.51; and on Argoverse2 1s it reaches 0.26 versus 1.42. For 3s prediction, the method achieves Chamfer distances of 0.58 (NuScenes), 0.45 (KITTI), and 0.55 (Argoverse2), all substantially below the prior best of 1.40, 0.96, and 1.99 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Copilot4D outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and prediction horizon. Against the strongest prior method 4D-Occ (Khurana et al., 2023), Copilot4D reduces Chamfer distance by 74% on NuScenes 1s (0.36 vs 1.41), 59% on NuScenes 3s (0.58 vs 1.40), 65% on KITTI 1s (0.18 vs 0.51), 53% on KITTI 3s (0.45 vs 0.96), 82% on Argoverse2 1s (0.26 vs 1.42), and 72% on Argoverse2 3s (0.55 vs 1.99). The earlier baselines SPFNet (Weng et al., 2021) and S2Net (Weng et al., 2022) are outperformed by even larger margins on NuScenes (e.g., 0.36 vs 2.24 and 1.70 for 1s Chamfer), while ST3DCNN (Mersch et al., 2022) is surpassed by over 20× on KITTI 1s (0.18 vs 4.11). In a direct ablation, the proposed discrete diffusion algorithm also outperforms MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022) by 29% on NuScenes 3s Chamfer (0.58 vs 0.82), confirming that the absorbing-uniform diffusion formulation with iterative token revision is a meaningful improvement over the original masked-generation approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) world models for robotics (Ha & Schmidhuber, Dreamer, IRIS), which have scaled in Atari and Minecraft but not in high-dimensional sensor spaces like autonomous driving; (2) VQVAE-based tokenization (Van Den Oord et al., 2017; Xiong et al., 2023), which the authors adapt to point clouds using a NeRF-inspired differentiable depth rendering decoder; and (3) MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022), which the paper recasts as an absorbing-uniform discrete diffusion model (Austin et al., 2021) with three targeted modifications—uniform noise injection on non-masked tokens, cross-entropy loss on all tokens rather than only masked ones, and iterative re-denoising of already-decoded tokens during sampling. The key conceptual departure from prior point cloud forecasting work (SPFNet, S2Net, ST3DCNN, 4D-Occ) is that Copilot4D operates entirely on discrete token indices with a GPT-like autoregressive objective, rather than predicting continuous occupancy or depth fields directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 65–75% Chamfer distance reduction over prior SOTA on a 39M-parameter model demonstrates that the tokenize-then-diffuse paradigm can match or exceed purpose-built continuous predictors at a fraction of the architectural complexity, and the zero-shot cross-dataset transfer (4× smaller Chamfer) suggests the learned representation is more general. The paper explicitly positions this as a stepping stone toward GPT-like pretraining for robotics and model-based reinforcement learning, but leaves open whether the approach scales to longer horizons, larger scenes, or multi-sensor inputs, and acknowledges failure modes in hallucinating new agents entering the scene. The clean theoretical connection between MaskGIT and discrete diffusion (showing that MaskGIT's loss is nearly identical to the D3PM ELBO lower bound up to two small corrections) may influence how the broader community designs masked generative models.
