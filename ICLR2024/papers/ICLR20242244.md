## Problem

Bandit-based neural active learning methods (NeurAL-NTK, I-NeurAL) transform K-class classification into a K-arm contextual bandit problem, inflating input dimension from d to Kd and requiring K sequential forward passes per round. This causes both computational cost and test accuracy to degrade noticeably as K grows. The paper asks how to retain principled exploration and provable regret guarantees while eliminating the multiplicative dependence on K.

## Input / Output

The exploitation network f1 takes the original d-dimensional instance x_t directly (not K transformed Kd-dimensional arms) and outputs a K-vector of loss estimates in a single forward pass. The exploration network f2 takes an end-to-end embedding φ(x_t) ∈ R^{m+Km} that concatenates the first-layer ReLU activations of f1 with the vectorized gradient of f1's last-layer weights, encoding both instance representation and discriminative information. In the pool-based setting, a pool of B unlabeled instances is scored and ranked via a gap-inverse weighting scheme before one instance is sampled for labeling.

## Method

NEURONAL-S and NEURONAL-P replace the bandit transformation with two redesigned neural networks: an exploitation network f1 that maps x_t ∈ R^d to K loss estimates synchronously, and an exploration network f2 that maps the end-to-end embedding φ(x_t) to K uncertainty estimates. The combined score f = f1 + f2 drives both prediction (argmin over K classes) and the query decision: in stream-based mode, a label is queried when the gap between the best and second-best predicted loss falls below a confidence threshold; in pool-based mode, a novel gap-inverse weighting strategy ranks all B pool instances and samples proportionally to balance exploitation and exploration. Theoretically, the regret bound for NEURONAL-S grows as Õ(√(TK)·S) under Tsybakov noise α=0, removing the effective-dimension term L_H that appears in bandit-based bounds and yielding a slower K-dependence by a factor of at least O(√(T log(1+λ₀))) and up to Õ(√(md)).

## Results

In the stream-based setting (T=10,000 rounds, 30% label budget), NEURONAL-S achieves the best test accuracy across all six datasets, with the largest gains on high-K problems: 83.2% on Fashion (K=10) vs. 73.5% for I-NeurAL and 64.5% for NeurAL-NTK, and 74.4% on Covertype (K=7) vs. 65.2% and 61.9% respectively. Running time is dramatically reduced as K grows: on Fashion, NEURONAL-S takes 116.3s vs. 1712.7s (I-NeurAL) and 1819.4s (NeurAL-NTK), a ~15x speedup. In the pool-based setting (10 rounds × 100 queries), NEURONAL-P also leads on all datasets (e.g., 81.1% on Fashion vs. 80.4% CoreSet, 76.1% BADGE) while being the fastest method on most datasets (e.g., 25.5s on Fashion vs. 437.8s BADGE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two bandit-based baselines (NeurAL-NTK [58] and I-NeurAL [14]), NEURONAL-S wins on every dataset in both accuracy and running time, with the gap widening as K increases: on Fashion (K=10) it gains +9.7% over I-NeurAL and +18.7% over NeurAL-NTK in accuracy, while being ~15x faster than both. ALPS [22], a pre-trained hypothesis selection method, is consistently the weakest baseline (e.g., 35.9% on MT, 36.2% on Covertype) because its model parameters are frozen before the online active learning process. In the pool-based setting, NEURONAL-P outperforms all four baselines (CoreSet [52], BADGE [6], DynamicAL [57], ALBL [33]) on every dataset; the margin is largest on MT (+6.2% over CoreSet) and Covertype (+2.3% over ALBL). NEURONAL-P is also the fastest method on 4 of 6 datasets (e.g., 14.1s on Covertype vs. 545.7s for BADGE), because it requires only O(2B) forward passes per round whereas BADGE and DynamicAL additionally compute per-sample gradients at O(B) cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exploitation-exploration neural network framework from contextual bandits (EE-net [13]) and its application to active learning (I-NeurAL [14]), but fundamentally departs from the bandit-based formulation that prior works (NeurAL-NTK [58], I-NeurAL [14]) relied on. Instead of transforming K-class classification into a K-armed bandit problem—where each instance becomes K arms of dimension Kd—the paper redesigns both the exploitation and exploration networks to operate directly on d-dimensional inputs and output K-class scores synchronously in a single forward pass. It also introduces a novel end-to-end embedding for the exploration network and extends the framework from stream-based to pool-based active learning with a gap-inverse selection strategy, a setting not addressed by the bandit-based predecessors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the bandit transformation, previously considered essential for principled exploration with provable guarantees in neural active learning, is actually a source of unnecessary K-scaling in both computation and regret bounds. The theoretical results show regret growing as Õ(√(TK)S) versus Õ(√(TKmd)S) for prior work, and the empirical speedups (e.g., ~15× on Fashion-MNIST) make multi-class active learning substantially more tractable. Limitations include evaluation only on tabular datasets with simple fully-connected networks, and the pool-based theoretical analysis is less developed than the stream-based one. Open questions include whether the end-to-end embedding and gap-inverse strategy transfer to complex architectures (CNNs, transformers) and high-dimensional data like images or text.
