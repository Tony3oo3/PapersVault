## Problem

Existing backdoor defense methods like Neural Cleanse rely on reverse engineering to reproduce the attacker's trigger before repairing the model, but this fails when triggers are complex and invisible (e.g., Blended, WaNet), leaving the model un-repaired. The paper addresses the challenge of detecting and removing backdoor feature maps from a DNN without access to the actual trigger or any poisoned samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a potentially backdoored PreActResNet-18 model and a clean labeled dataset (X, Y) as input, with no access to the trigger or poisoned data. It produces a repaired DNN model and a ranked list of backdoor-related feature maps. The clean dataset is consumed iteratively: for each of the N feature maps, a single FGSM-style perturbation is generated per sample, and the model's prediction on the perturbed input is checked against the ground-truth label to score that feature map.

## Method

FMP adapts FGSM to operate at the feature-map level: for each feature map f_θ^i, it generates a perturbed input x' that maximizes ‖f_θ^i(x) − f_θ^i(x')‖², then feeds x' back into the full model and records whether the prediction still matches the true label. Feature maps whose own generated perturbations cause the most prediction errors (lowest accuracy) are identified as backdoor-related and their weights are zeroed out. The model is then fine-tuned with a small clean subset (default 10%) at a reduced learning rate to recover benign performance. Unlike neuron-level pruning methods (ANP, RNP), FMP operates at the feature-map granularity, which aligns with how backdoor triggers are encoded in the network and avoids the expensive iterative prune-finetune-evaluate cycles required by neuron-level approaches.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, FMP achieves an average ASR of 2.86% and RA of 87.40% across five backdoor attacks (BadNets, Blended, Low Frequency, SSBA, WaNet), with clean accuracy of 92.13%. On CIFAR-100, FMP reaches an average ASR of 0.25% and RA of 60.20%; on GTSRB, it achieves 0.32% ASR and 89.44% RA. FMP reduces the average ASR by 19.2% compared to baselines on CIFAR-10 and improves RA by 16.92% over the baseline average, while maintaining competitive clean accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMP achieves the lowest average ASR (2.86%) and highest average RA (87.40%) on CIFAR-10, outperforming all six main baselines (FT, FP, CLP, ANP, NC, NAD) on both metrics simultaneously. The closest competitor on ASR is NAD (22.06% avg ASR), which FMP beats by ~19 percentage points; on RA, NAD (70.48%) is the nearest rival, trailing FMP by ~17 points. Neural Cleanse (NC) is competitive on simple triggers (e.g., 1.32% ASR on BadNets) but collapses on complex/invisible triggers (99.90% ASR on Blended, 86.91% on WaNet), whereas FMP maintains ASR below 7% across all five attacks. On CIFAR-100, FMP achieves 0.25% avg ASR vs. NC's 1.39% and NAD's 32.27%, and on GTSRB, FMP reaches 0.32% ASR vs. NC's 2.69%. In the appendix comparisons, FMP also dominates neuron-level pruning methods (ANP: 68.27% avg ASR, AEVA: 50.96% on BadNets, RNP: 55.01% on BadNets) and detection-based methods (DeepInspect: 15.87% ASR, TABOR: 9.19%, ABS: 5.61% on BadNets), confirming that feature-map-level adversarial pruning is more effective than neuron-level or detection-based approaches under the same 10-epoch fine-tuning budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

FMP sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: adversarial perturbation methods (specifically FGSM, adapted here to operate in feature-map space) and feature-map pruning for backdoor defense (Fine-pruning, CLPA). Unlike Neural Cleanse and similar reverse-engineering defenses that attempt to reproduce the attacker's trigger before removing it, FMP bypasses trigger reproduction entirely by generating per-feature-map adversarial perturbations to identify which maps encode backdoor information. This departs from neuron-level pruning approaches (ANP, RNP) by operating at the feature-map granularity, which the authors argue better matches how backdoor triggers are actually encoded in convolutional layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FMP addresses a practical gap in backdoor defense: when triggers are complex or invisible (e.g., Blended, WaNet), reverse-engineering methods like Neural Cleanse fail catastrophically (ASR > 99%), leaving deployed models vulnerable. The feature-map-level pruning strategy is computationally lighter than iterative neuron-pruning cycles, making it more feasible for resource-constrained developers who download pre-trained models. However, the evaluation is limited to PreActResNet-18 on three small-scale image datasets, and the Blended attack still yields a notably higher residual ASR (~6.4%) compared to other attacks, suggesting the method's effectiveness depends on trigger type. Open questions include scalability to larger architectures (e.g., ViTs, ResNet-50+), applicability to non-visual modalities, and robustness against multi-trigger or adaptive attacks.
