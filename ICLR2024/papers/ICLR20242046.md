## Problem

Existing entailment cone constructions for modeling partial orders in hyperbolic space suffer from an ε-hole at the origin of the Poincaré ball where cones are undefined, requiring pretrained initialization that constrains representational capacity. Additionally, these methods are restricted to a single hyperbolic model and use angle-based energy functions that produce vanishing gradients for misclassified negative samples. The paper seeks a unified, physically intuitive framework that generalizes entailment cones across multiple hyperbolic models with better optimization properties.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of basic (transitively reduced) entailment edges from a hierarchical DAG, along with a configurable percentage of non-basic edges for training, and produces point embeddings in either the Poincaré ball or half-space. Entailment is evaluated by checking whether one point lies within the shadow cone of another, reducing region-region subset relations to point-region membership tests. Evaluation is performed by predicting unseen non-basic edges from the transitive closure, with negative samples generated by corrupting positive pairs.

## Method

The shadow cones framework models partial orders as subset relations between shadows cast by a light source and opaque objects in hyperbolic space, yielding two cone types: umbral (point light source, volumetric objects) and penumbral (volumetric light source, point objects), each instantiated in both the Poincaré ball and half-space for four total formulations. The key insight is that Ganea et al.'s entailment cones are proven equivalent to the penumbral-Poincaré-ball formulation, while the half-space formulations avoid the ε-hole entirely by placing the light source at infinity or on a horosphere. For optimization, the paper introduces a distance-based shadow loss that measures hyperbolic distance from a point to the cone boundary (rather than angle-based energy), with a two-case shortest-distance computation depending on relative altitude, and a contrastive-style objective that pulls positive pairs into cones and pushes negative pairs outside with configurable margins.

## Results

On the three large datasets (WordNet Noun, MCG, Hearst), Umbral-half-space shadow cones consistently outperform the Entailment Cone baseline across all non-basic-edge percentages and dimensions. The most dramatic gains appear on MCG (e.g., 91.3% vs. 63.8% F1 at d=10, 50% non-basic edges) and Hearst (86.9% vs. 58.2% F1 at d=10, 5% non-basic edges). On the smaller Mammal sub-graph, all four shadow cone formulations surpass both Euclidean box-embedding baselines (GBC-box, VBC-box) and the Entailment Cone, with Umbral-half-space reaching 80.3% F1 at d=2, 50% non-basic edges versus 66.5% for Entailment Cone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Entailment Cone baseline (Ganea et al., 2018), Umbral-half-space shadow cones win on every dataset and every non-basic-edge percentage, with the largest margins on MCG (+27.5 F1 at d=10, 50% non-basic) and Hearst (+28.7 F1 at d=10, 5% non-basic), where the deep hierarchies (depth 31 and 56) stress the baseline's ε-hole initialization issue. Penumbral-half-space also beats Entailment Cone across the board but trails Umbral-half-space, attributed to its height limit on the horosphere light source. On the Mammal sub-graph (Table 2), both Euclidean box-embedding baselines (GBC-box and VBC-box) are substantially outperformed by all four shadow cone formulations; for example, at d=2 with 0% non-basic edges, Umbral-half-space scores 57.7% F1 versus 23.4% (GBC-box) and 20.1% (VBC-box). The paper also proves that Ganea's entailment cones are a strict special case of Penumbral-Poincaré-ball shadow cones (Theorem 3.2), providing a unified theoretical explanation for the ε-hole limitation that the half-space formulations avoid.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Ganea et al. (2018)'s hyperbolic entailment cones by reframing them as a special case of a broader physics-inspired 'shadow cones' framework, where partial orders are encoded via subset relations between shadows cast by a light source and opaque objects in hyperbolic space. The key departures are: (1) extending constructions from the Poincaré ball to the Poincaré half-space, which eliminates the ε-hole initialization problem; (2) introducing two cone types (umbral and penumbral) across two models, yielding four distinct embedding schemes; and (3) replacing the angle-based max-margin energy with a distance-based contrastive loss that captures hierarchical depth. The framework also subsumes earlier Euclidean order embeddings (Vendrov et al., 2015) and box embeddings (Zhang et al., 2022; Boratko et al., 2021) as limiting cases, positioning shadow cones as a unifying geometric language for partial-order representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shadow cones framework offers practitioners a physically intuitive and more flexible toolkit for hierarchical graph embedding, with the half-space formulations providing a clean workaround for the ε-hole problem that has constrained entailment cone optimization. The consistent outperformance across four datasets of varying depth and width (Mammal, WordNet Noun, MCG, Hearst) validates the approach, and the connection to graph connectivity via learnable light source radius opens an interesting direction for adaptive embedding. Open questions include whether the framework scales to multi-relation settings with multiple light sources, whether the aperture/height constraints of individual formulations can be relaxed, and how well shadow-cone-based attention (Coneheads) transfers to large-scale transformer architectures beyond the small-scale evaluations reported.
