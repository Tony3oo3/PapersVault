## Problem

Existing methods for compactly modeling neural network training dynamics—such as Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) and Koopman-based approaches—rely on predefined operators or restrictive assumptions (e.g., exponential modes) that fail to capture the complex, non-linear evolution of network weights. While Li et al. (2023) demonstrated that training can occur in a low-dimensional subspace spanned by covariance eigenvectors, their method requires full gradient computation and a long warm-up phase. The paper identifies an unexploited structure: parameters exhibit strong intrinsic temporal correlations that can be clustered into a small number of synchronized 'modes,' enabling a more adaptive, data-driven decomposition without operator assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-epoch weight checkpoints from a standard training run; for the online variant, only the current-epoch weights and two 2×2 Gram matrices per mode are needed (no trajectory storage). The initial clustering step samples K weights (K ≪ N, e.g., K=1000 for ResNet18's 11.2M parameters) with a per-layer budget to ensure representativeness. The output is a compact model of M reference trajectories (M ≤ 10 in most experiments) plus per-weight affine coefficients (a_i, b_i), from which any weight trajectory can be reconstructed as w_i(t) ≈ a_i·w_m(t) + b_i. In the federated learning setting, the output additionally specifies which weights are 'embedded' (frozen to their CMD model) and thus excluded from client-server communication.

## Method

Correlation Mode Decomposition (CMD) clusters N weight trajectories into M modes via a correlation-based farthest-point hierarchical clustering on a K-weight sample, then assigns each weight to its most-correlated mode and fits per-weight affine coefficients (a_i, b_i) by least-squares regression against the mode's reference trajectory. The key insight is that within each mode, every weight trajectory is well-approximated by a single affine transform of one reference trajectory (w_i(t) ≈ a_i·w_m(t) + b_i), a specific case of general dynamics decomposition that avoids DMD's exponential-basis restriction. The online variant eliminates trajectory storage by iteratively updating the affine coefficients and 2×2 Gram matrices using only current-epoch weights (16N FLOPS per epoch, negligible vs. SGD). The embedded variant gradually freezes weights whose affine coefficients have stabilized (detected via a change-threshold criterion), reducing the number of actively trained parameters by ~50% while maintaining or improving accuracy. In federated learning, embedded coefficients are stored locally at each client, so only un-embedded weights and the M reference trajectories are communicated per synchronization round, cutting communication volume to ~48% of baseline.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Online CMD with 10 modes outperforms baseline SGD across all six architectures, achieving 92.43% on ResNet18, 93.21% on WideResNet-28-10, and 97.99% on ViT-b-16 (pre-trained on ImageNet-1K). In the Federated Learning setting (ResNet18, CIFAR-10), CMD reduces the average communicated parameter volume to 48.12% of baseline while improving test accuracy from 91.4% to 91.7%. As an implicit regularizer, CMD matches or slightly exceeds dedicated smoothing methods (EMA, SWA) across multiple optimizers on ResNet18/CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against P-BFGS (Li et al., 2023), the SOTA for compactly modeled dynamics, Online CMD wins on all six architectures with margins ranging from +0.58% (PreRes164, equal warm-up) to +3.09% (ResNet18, equal warm-up) and +2.94% (ViT-b-16). P-BFGS also suffers from memory constraints: it could not be run on ViT-b-16 with a single GPU, and the long-warm-up variant could not be computed on a 125 GB RAM machine for WideResNet. As a regularizer, CMD (92.26% with plain SGD) slightly outperforms both EMA (92.02%) and SWA (92.09%) on ResNet18/CIFAR-10, and this advantage persists across SGD+Momentum, SGD+Momentum+Scheduler, and Adam. In Federated Learning, CMD reduces communication volume to 48.12% of baseline, substantially below A-APF (60.75%) and APF (90.22%), while achieving 91.7% test accuracy—comparable to APF's 92.0% and above A-APF's 91.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of the low-dimensional subspace hypothesis (Li et al. 2018a, Gur-Ari et al. 2018, Gressmann et al. 2020) and classical time-profile correlation methods from fluid dynamics and image processing (Aubry et al. 1991, Burger et al. 2016). It most directly departs from Li et al. (2023), who use top eigenvectors of the weight-trajectory covariance matrix to span a 40-dimensional training subspace with P-BFGS, by replacing eigenvector-based projection with nonlinear correlation clustering into a small number of 'modes' (≤10). It also contrasts with DMD/Koopman-theory approaches (Schmid 2010, Dogra & Redman 2020, Naiman & Azencot 2021) that impose a predefined operator structure, arguing that such operators are too restrictive for neural network dynamics. The FL communication-reduction application extends the adaptive parameter freezing line of work (Chen et al. 2021) by replacing plateau-based freezing with dynamics-model-driven embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a training-time dynamics model with negligible computational overhead (16N FLOPS per epoch) and constant memory that consistently improves test accuracy by 1–2% over SGD across six architectures and multiple datasets, while also enabling a 48% reduction in communicated parameters in federated learning. The implicit regularization effect (smoother trajectories comparable to EMA/SWA) suggests CMD could serve as a lightweight, architecture-agnostic regularizer. However, the FL experiments are explicitly preliminary (single architecture, single dataset, 1500 rounds), the embedding schedule (when to freeze parameters) is heuristic, and the gains, while consistent, are modest. Open questions include whether the mode structure is interpretable, how it behaves under non-SGD optimizers at scale, and whether the FL communication savings hold in cross-device settings with heterogeneous clients.
