## Problem

U-Nets are widely used as neural PDE solvers, but their skip connections suffer from temporal misalignment: because the input field is earlier in time than the prediction target, features evolve across layers, causing the high-resolution skip-connected features to be spatially displaced relative to the upsampled features. This misalignment is especially detrimental for advection-dominated dynamics, where local patterns shift significantly between time steps, and limits the effectiveness of the multi-scale processing that makes U-Nets attractive for PDEs.

## Input / Output

The model takes h historical PDE solution fields (e.g., velocity, pressure, density) on a fixed uniform spatial grid—128×128 for INS and CNS, 96×192 for SWE—and predicts the solution at the next time step. Unlike neural operators, SineNet is trained for a specific discretization and does not generalize across resolutions natively, though super-resolution at integer multiples can be achieved via dilated convolutions at inference. Multi-step prediction is performed via autoregressive rollout, with the model conditioned on h past frames (h=4 for INS, h=10 for CNS, h=2 for SWE).

## Method

SineNet partitions the full temporal evolution into K sequential U-Net blocks called 'waves,' each advancing the latent state by a fraction δ_k of the total time step, thereby reducing the degree of spatial misalignment within each wave's skip connections. Each wave employs wave residuals for stable optimization and block residuals that enable a dual parallel/sequential multi-scale processing mechanism: the parallel branch carries the input downsampled directly to each scale, while the sequential branch processes features hierarchically, with the parallel branch disentangled via concatenation to avoid entanglement. A channel multiplier m_K is tuned so that total parameters remain roughly constant as K increases, and circular padding is used for periodic boundary conditions. Training is end-to-end supervised with a scaled L2 loss, and the authors show that performance improves monotonically with K at fixed parameter budget, plateauing around K=16.

## Results

SineNet-8 (35.5M params) achieves the lowest 1-step and rollout errors across all three PDE datasets. On Incompressible Navier-Stokes (INS), it reaches 1.66% 1-step and 19.25% rollout error; on Compressible Navier-Stokes (CNS), 0.93% and 2.10%; and on Shallow Water Equations (SWE), 1.02% and 1.78%. SineNet-16 (35.4M params) further improves to 1.46%/17.63% on INS, 0.87%/2.04% on CNS, and 0.92%/1.64% on SWE, confirming monotonic improvement with more waves at fixed parameter budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SineNet-8 outperforms both F-FNO and DIL-RESNET on every metric across all three datasets. Against F-FNO (30.1M params), SineNet-8 reduces 1-step error by 0.75 pp on INS (1.66% vs 2.41%), 0.53 pp on CNS (0.93% vs 1.46%), and 0.20 pp on SWE (1.02% vs 1.22%), with rollout improvements of 3.33 pp, 0.81 pp, and 0.68 pp respectively. Against DIL-RESNET (16.7M params), the gains are more pronounced on SWE, where SineNet-8 achieves 1.02% 1-step vs 2.23% and 1.78% rollout vs 4.12%, reflecting the large 48-hour timestep that causes severe misalignment in a single U-Net. Notably, SineNet-8 uses fewer parameters than the U-Net baselines (35.5M vs 135–144M) while achieving substantially lower errors, and the ablation against a deeper U-Net with matched layer count (DEEPER U-NET-8, 28.6M params) confirms the gains come from the multi-stage wave strategy rather than mere depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

SineNet builds directly on the U-Net architecture, repurposing its skip-connection-based multi-scale processing for temporal PDE dynamics rather than its original image segmentation role. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis that skip connections in a single U-Net become temporally misaligned when the network must evolve features forward in time, and the solution of decomposing the evolution into multiple sequential U-Net 'waves' to reduce per-stage misalignment. This positions SineNet between the sequential multi-scale paradigm of U-Nets and the parallel frequency-domain processing of FNOs, while also connecting to Neural ODE formulations through its iterative latent evolution. It differs from PDE-Refiner, which also applies U-Nets iteratively but for prediction refinement rather than temporal advancement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The misalignment diagnosis provides a concrete, visualizable explanation for why standard U-Nets underperform on advection-dominated temporal PDE tasks, and the multi-wave decomposition offers a practical, parameter-efficient remedy that practitioners can adopt. The monotonic improvement with increasing wave count at fixed parameters is a clean scaling result that validates the underlying hypothesis. Open questions include whether the approach extends to irregular grids (requiring graph-based generalizations), how to handle adaptive or input-dependent time steps in practice, and whether the benefits persist for PDEs with minimal temporal evolution where misalignment is less severe.
