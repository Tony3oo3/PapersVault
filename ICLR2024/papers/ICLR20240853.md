## Problem

All dominant dimensionality reduction methods (t-SNE, UMAP, PacMap) align pairwise probabilities between individual data point pairs, ignoring information from wider local neighborhoods, and none provide a theoretical framework explaining when normalization of weights is required. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a projective divergence that eliminates normalization entirely and by aligning marginal probabilities (node degrees) that aggregate information across multiple pairs simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240853/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

MAP IT takes high-dimensional data points (784-dim MNIST pixels, 16384-dim Coil-20 images, 1000-dim SIFT descriptors, or word-document matrices) and produces 2D embedding coordinates for visualization. Unlike t-SNE which requires computing a global normalization factor Z_q over all pairs at every iteration, MAP IT operates on unnormalized pairwise similarities (t-distribution or Gaussian kernel) and computes marginal probabilities over local neighborhoods of k nearest neighbors (typically k=5–15), with perplexity fixed at 15 and 1000 gradient descent iterations at learning rate 50.

## Method

MAP IT replaces the KL divergence in t-SNE with the Cauchy-Schwarz (CS) divergence, which is projective—meaning it is invariant to normalization of its arguments—thereby eliminating the expensive global normalization factor that t-SNE must recompute every iteration. Instead of aligning pairwise probabilities p_ij and q_ij, MAP IT aligns the distributions over marginal probabilities p_j = Σ_k p_jk and q_j = Σ_k q_jk (node degrees in the similarity graph), which naturally aggregate information from multiple pairs and capture wider local structure. The gradient yields an n-body update rule where each point experiences attractive forces weighted by input-space marginal probabilities and repulsive forces weighted by target-space marginal probabilities, with adaptive scaling by cross-entropy and Rényi entropy terms. A dual derivation via continuous densities and kernel smoothing (Parzen windowing) reveals the connection to RKHS methods and shows that the neighborhood structure in the input space (neighbors of neighbors) governs how marginal probabilities are computed during optimization. Proposition 11 proves that CS-based t-SNE (aligning pairwise probabilities) is a strict special case of MAP IT, confirming the broader neighborhood alignment as the key generalization.

## Results

The paper's primary evaluation is qualitative (visual class separation), with knn recall as the main quantitative metric. On MNIST, MAP IT achieves the lowest knn recall among all methods (0.35 at k=15, 0.48 at k=60), indicating stronger cluster-structure capture at the expense of some manifold preservation. On Frey Faces, all methods show similar knn recall (0.09–0.15 range), with MAP IT matching t-SNE. Class-wise knn recall (Figure 11) shows MAP IT and PacMap maintain higher class-purity than t-SNE and UMAP as k increases, consistent with the visual observation that MAP IT separates digit classes more distinctly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAP IT is compared against t-SNE (Barnes-Hut, default params), UMAP (Stanford implementation, Laplacian eigenmap init), and PacMap (author's implementation, PCA init) on six datasets. Visually, MAP IT most clearly outperforms all three on MNIST, where it separates four digit classes more distinctly and reduces overlap between the challenging digits 4, 9, and 7 (Figures 1e–f). On Coil-20, MAP IT separates all 20 object classes more cleanly than t-SNE, UMAP, or PacMap, though it exhibits somewhat more symmetry breaking (flattening ring structures). On Visual Concepts, MAP IT preserves group structure without splitting any class, whereas t-SNE splits the Australian terrier and PacMap splits the overlapping strawberry/lemon groups. On Newsgroups, MAP IT groups words by topic without the overly rigid exclusivity seen in PacMap. Quantitatively, MAP IT consistently shows the lowest overall knn recall on MNIST (e.g., 0.35 vs. 0.48 for t-SNE at k=15), which the authors interpret as evidence of stronger cluster-structure capture; on Frey Faces all methods are essentially tied.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAP IT departs from the t-SNE/UMAP/PacMap lineage by replacing the KL divergence over pairwise joint probabilities with a Cauchy-Schwarz divergence over marginal probabilities (node degrees), which captures wider local neighborhood information. The projective property of the CS divergence provides a clean theoretical justification for eliminating the normalization step that has been a persistent computational and conceptual obstacle in t-SNE and a key differentiator from UMAP. The kernel smoothing dual viewpoint connects the discrete marginal-probability formulation to continuous density estimation and RKHS methods, while the local neighborhood construction (neighbors of neighbors) is derived from the differentiation of the cost function rather than being a heuristic design choice as in PacMap's near/mid-near/non-neighbor scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The projective divergence insight—that normalization is provably unnecessary for optimization—resolves a long-standing theoretical question about the role of normalization in DR and provides a principled path toward scalable implementations. However, the empirical validation is limited to modest-sized datasets (≤2000 points), the method is not heavily optimized (no Barnes-Hut equivalent, random initialization), and the sampling-based scaling strategy is only preliminary. Open questions include whether the marginal-probability alignment advantage persists at scale, the role of the entropy-weighting terms in the gradient, and whether the method's tendency toward stronger cluster separation (at the cost of some manifold structure, as suggested by lower knn recall) is desirable across application domains.
