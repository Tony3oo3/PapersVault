## Problem

Existing sparse phase retrieval algorithms rely on first-order methods that are limited to linear convergence, and the only prior second-order approach (HTP) fails to establish quadratic convergence. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a Newton-type algorithm with hard thresholding that provably achieves non-asymptotic quadratic convergence while preserving the per-iteration computational efficiency of first-order methods.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes m = Ω(s² log n) magnitude-only measurements y_i = |⟨a_i, x⟩|² generated from i.i.d. Gaussian sensing vectors a_i ∈ ℝⁿ, along with the sparsity level s, and produces the s-sparse ground truth signal x^⊤ up to a global sign. A distinctive design choice is the dual-loss strategy: the smooth intensity-based loss f_I drives the Newton update, while the non-smooth amplitude-based loss f_A (via one-step IHT) identifies which s variables are free at each iteration. The sparsity level s is assumed known for theory but experiments also explore unknown-s settings.

## Method

The proposed algorithm is a two-stage procedure: a sparse spectral initialization (from Jagatap & Hegde, 2019) followed by iterative refinement via a Newton-type step with hard thresholding. At each iteration, the support of free variables (at most s indices) is identified by one-step IHT applied to the amplitude-based loss f_A, and the Newton direction is computed by solving an s×s linear system involving the Hessian of the intensity-based loss f_I restricted to those free variables; all other coordinates are set to zero. This dual-loss design is the key insight: f_I's smoothness enables Hessian computation for the Newton step, while f_A's superior curvature near the solution yields a more effective support estimate than f_I would. The algorithm achieves quadratic convergence dist(x^{k+1}, x^⊤) ≤ ρ·dist²(x^k, x^⊤) after at most O(log(‖x^⊤‖/x_min^⊤)) iterations, with per-iteration cost O((n+s²)s² log n) that matches first-order methods when s = O(√n). A non-asymptotic linear convergence guarantee also holds under additive Gaussian noise.

## Results

The proposed Newton-type algorithm achieves a non-asymptotic quadratic convergence rate after at most O(log(‖x^‖/x_min^‖)) iterations with Ω(s²log n) Gaussian measurements — the first such guarantee for sparse phase retrieval. In running time comparisons (n=5000, m=3000), the proposed method recovers s=80 sparse signals in 0.053 s (noise-free) and 0.080 s (noisy, σ=0.03), outperforming HTP (0.081 s / 0.113 s), ThWF (0.363 s / 0.282 s), SPARTA (1.006 s / 1.108 s), and CoPRAM (0.976 s / 1.343 s). For a 30,000-dimensional wavelet-sparse signal reconstruction, the proposed method achieves PSNR = 78 dB in 2.63 s, surpassing ThWF (PSNR 71, 11.6 s), SPARTA (PSNR 66, 38.4 s), CoPRAM (PSNR 61, 117.7 s), and HTP (PSNR 62, 6.7 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed algorithm outperforms all four baselines (ThWF, SPARTA, CoPRAM, HTP) on every reported metric: convergence iterations, running time, phase-transition success rate, and reconstruction PSNR. Against the closest competitor HTP (also a second-order method), the proposed algorithm is roughly 1.5× faster in running time (e.g., 0.053 s vs. 0.081 s for s=80 noise-free) and achieves a higher PSNR (78 vs. 62 dB) in the 30,000-dimensional signal reconstruction, attributed to more effective exploitation of second-order Hessian information in the Newton direction. Against first-order methods ThWF, SPARTA, and CoPRAM, the speedup is more dramatic: 6.8×–63× faster in running time, with the gap widening as sparsity increases (CoPRAM degrades to 4.1 s at s=100 noisy vs. 0.119 s for the proposed method). In phase-transition experiments (n=3000), the proposed method achieves a higher successful recovery rate across all measurement counts for both standard sparse and block-sparse signals. The proposed method also demonstrates superior robustness to unknown sparsity: when the true sparsity is 30 but the input sparsity is set to 150 or 200, the proposed method still achieves 93% and 85% success rates, whereas CoPRAM drops to 75% and 9%, HTP to 71% and 22%, and SPARTA to 0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Newton-type sparse optimization framework (Zhou et al., 2021; Meng & Zhao, 2020; Cai et al., 2023a) to the nonconvex sparse phase retrieval setting, building directly on HTP (Cai et al., 2022c), a prior second-order method that achieved finite-step convergence but not quadratic convergence. The key departure is a dual-loss strategy: the intensity-based loss provides the smooth Hessian for the Newton direction, while the amplitude-based loss drives the iterative hard thresholding step that identifies the active support. This contrasts with first-order baselines (ThWF, SPARTA, CoPRAM) that are limited to linear convergence, and with HTP, which uses only the amplitude-based loss and a less effective exploitation of second-order information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to establish a non-asymptotic quadratic convergence rate for sparse phase retrieval, with an iteration complexity of O(log(log(1/ε)) + log(‖x‖/x_min)), which is a meaningful theoretical advance over the linear convergence of all prior methods. However, the paper acknowledges that its refinement stage still requires Ω(s²log n) measurements, whereas competing methods need only Ω(s log n) in refinement; tightening this gap is an open problem. Additionally, the initialization step inherits the sub-optimal Ω(s²log n) sample complexity, and developing initializations closer to the information-theoretic limit of 2s measurements remains a key challenge for the broader field.
