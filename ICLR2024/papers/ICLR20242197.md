## Problem

Visual RL agents frequently become motorically inactive during early training, hindering effective exploration, and this inactivity correlates with a high fraction of dormant (nearly inactive) neurons in the policy network. Existing model-free algorithms struggle with sample efficiency, asymptotic performance, and robustness to random seeds on complex continuous control tasks such as Dog, Manipulator, and dexterous hand manipulation, often producing broken seeds where the agent never acquires meaningful behaviors.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a stack of three consecutive RGB frames as its observation (following the DrQ-v2 convention) and outputs a continuous action vector for the control task. The method is built directly on the DrQ-v2 codebase, inheriting its CNN encoder, replay buffer, and off-policy actor-critic (TD3-style) training loop, so no architectural changes are made to the observation or action spaces. The only additional computation is a periodic forward pass to compute the dormant ratio across all fully connected layers of the policy network.

## Method

DrM introduces three mechanisms that use the dormant ratio (fraction of neurons with activation below a threshold relative to the layer mean) as a self-adaptive signal for balancing exploration and exploitation. First, a dormant-ratio-guided perturbation periodically performs a soft reset of fully connected layer weights (interpolating toward random initialization), with the perturb factor α = clip(1 − kβ, α_min, α_max) so that higher dormant ratios trigger stronger resets. Second, an awaken exploration scheduler sets the exploration noise standard deviation via a sigmoid of (β − β̂)/T before the agent is 'awakened' (β drops below threshold β̂), then takes the maximum of this sigmoid and the original DrQ-v2 linear decay afterward. Third, a dormant-ratio-guided exploitation mechanism blends the TD3 target with a V-function estimate trained via expectile regression, where the mixing coefficient λ(β) = λ̄ / (1 + exp((β − β̂)/T′)) increases as the dormant ratio falls, shifting the agent from exploration toward exploiting historically successful trajectories.

## Results

DrM achieves 65%, 35%, and 75% asymptotic performance improvements over the best baseline on DeepMind Control Suite, MetaWorld, and Adroit respectively, while requiring 70%, 45%, and 60% fewer samples to match peak performance. Across 19 tasks and 76 random seeds total, DrM exhibits zero broken seeds, whereas baselines frequently fail to learn meaningful behaviors under certain initializations. DrM is the first model-free visual RL algorithm to reliably solve the Dog and Manipulator domains in DMC and three dexterous hand manipulation tasks in Adroit (Hammer, Door, Pen) directly from pixel observations without expert demonstrations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DrM outperforms all three primary baselines (DrQ-v2, ALIX, TACO) across all three benchmarks (DMC, MetaWorld, Adroit) in both sample efficiency and asymptotic performance. On DMC, DrM is the first model-free method to solve both Dog and Manipulator domains from pixels, while baselines exhibit broken seeds and sudden performance collapses in four dog tasks. On MetaWorld, DrM achieves success rates on sparse-reward tasks comparable to what baselines achieve with dense rewards, highlighting its advantage in low-signal settings. On Adroit, DrM reliably solves all three dexterous manipulation tasks (24 DoF) without demonstrations, a feat none of the baselines accomplish. In a direct comparison with ReDo (Sokar et al., 2023) on MetaWorld (Appendix F), DrM significantly outperforms the approach of resetting only dormant neurons, suggesting that perturbing non-dormant weights also contributes positively to exploration. DrM also shows notably smaller variance across random seeds compared to all baselines, indicating greater robustness to initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

DrM is built directly on DrQ-v2's data-augmented TD3 architecture and repurposes the dormant ratio metric from Sokar et al. (2023) — originally a diagnostic for network expressivity loss — as a practical, reward-independent control signal for exploration-exploitation tradeoff. The three mechanisms draw on existing components (soft-reset perturbation from D'Oro et al. 2023, expectile-regression exploitation from Ji et al. 2023, and standard exploration noise scheduling), but the unifying insight is that the dormant ratio can simultaneously gate all three: triggering weight perturbation when high, modulating exploration noise, and switching the critic target toward exploitation when low. This positions DrM as a departure from both reward-based exploration schedulers and static hyperparameter tuning, offering a self-adaptive alternative that requires minimal task-specific tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DrM is the first model-free visual RL algorithm to reliably solve Dog, Manipulator (DMC), and demonstration-free Adroit dexterous manipulation tasks from pixels, with zero broken seeds across 76 runs — a practical milestone for deploying visual RL in complex robotics. The conceptual finding that dormant ratio tracks behavioral variety independently of reward opens a path toward unsupervised or reward-free RL where no external signal exists to guide exploration. Open questions include a theoretical explanation for why dormant ratio correlates with action diversity, whether the approach extends to discrete-action settings, and whether combining DrM with intrinsic-reward methods (e.g., RND) yields further gains.
