## Problem

While single-index learning with standard two-layer architectures is well-understood under gradient flow, extending provable learning guarantees to symmetric (permutation-invariant) neural networks like DeepSets remains open. The three-layer DeepSets architecture, the need for complex-valued parameters to exploit symmetry, and the absence of a suitable orthogonality identity for the relevant feature space all prevent direct application of existing single-index analyses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241932/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N complex scalars x ∈ (S¹)ᴺ sampled from the squared Vandermonde density (equivalently, eigenvalues of a Haar-distributed N×N unitary matrix) and produces a complex scalar prediction via a DeepSets student network. The teacher function is F(x) = f(⟨h*, p(x)⟩) where p(x) denotes the infinite vector of normalized power sum polynomials and h* is a hidden unit-norm direction supported on the first √N coordinates. The student and teacher use distinct link functions, and the training objective is a correlational loss with an explicit regularization term rather than a standard squared-error loss.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a Hermite-like orthogonality identity (Proposition 2.3) for power sum polynomials under the Vandermonde measure, which reduces the expected loss to a function of only three summary statistics: the correlation magnitude r, the phase cos sθ, and the orthogonal norm v. The student is a DeepSets network with a single trainable vector w, frozen first-layer weights a_m ~ S¹, and a frozen student link function g(z) = (α_s/|α|·√s) z^s chosen to simplify the landscape. Training uses preconditioned gradient flow with (A†A)⁻¹, where A encodes the activation's power series coefficients, and the dynamics are derived via Wirtinger calculus for complex parameters. Under analytic activation and bounded-degree link function assumptions, the paper proves polynomial convergence to the planted direction h* with rates controlled by the information exponent s: O(log 1/ε) for s=1 and O(2^{s²} r₀^{-4s} + log 1/ε) for s>1.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a convergence rate for preconditioned gradient flow on the symmetric single-index DeepSets model: for information exponent s=1, ε-accuracy is reached in O(log(1/ε)) time, while for s>1 the rate is O(2^{s²}·N^{7s} + log(1/ε)) (Corollary 4.7). In experiments (N=25, M=100, 50k samples, 20k iterations, learning rate 0.0025), the simplified correlational loss L achieves very high rates of strong recovery (r→1) across ten independent runs, while the more general loss L̂ frequently gets stuck at local minima with r well below 1. The initial correlation r₀ scales as Ω(1/N^{7/4}) under the specified activation and initialization, and the hidden direction h* is recovered up to an s-th root of unity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not compare against external baseline methods; no competing architectures or learning algorithms are benchmarked. The only internal comparison is between two loss functions: the simplified correlational loss L (using a single monomial of the student link g) and the full correlational loss L̂ (using the complete teacher link f). Empirically, L consistently achieves strong recovery of the hidden direction, whereas L̂ often converges to a local minimum where r stalls below 1, suggesting the non-convex polynomial landscape in cos(θ) introduced by the full link function creates additional spurious minima. The paper positions its contribution as the first provable gradient-based learning guarantee for a fully non-linear (non-NTK) symmetric neural network, building on the single-index analysis of Arous et al. (2021) and the power-sum orthogonality of Zweig & Bruna (2022), but does not provide head-to-head numerical comparisons with those prior methods since the architectural setting (permutation-invariant DeepSets with Vandermonde inputs) is fundamentally different.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-index learning framework (Arous et al. 2021; Damian et al. 2023; Dudeja & Hsu 2018), where the information exponent of a link function governs gradient-based convergence rates, to the permutation-invariant DeepSets architecture (Zaheer et al. 2017). The key technical enabler is a Hermite-like orthogonality identity for power sum polynomials under the Vandermonde distribution (eigenvalues of Haar unitary matrices), analogous to the classical Hermite identity under Gaussian measure, building on the symmetric polynomial structure identified in Zweig & Bruna (2022). Unlike the Gaussian single-index setting, the analysis requires complex-valued parameters, a correlational loss with regularization, distinct student and teacher link functions, and a preconditioned gradient flow to reduce the dynamics to three dimension-free ODEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable learning guarantee for symmetric neural networks under gradient-based methods, establishing that the information exponent framework generalizes beyond fully-connected shallow networks to architectures enforcing predefined invariances. The identification of the Vandermonde distribution as a third 'nice' measure (alongside Gaussian and Boolean hypercube) enabling clean gradient dynamics analysis opens a new analytical direction. However, significant limitations remain: the result is for gradient flow (not SGD), requires complex inputs and analytic activations, and applies only to scalar set inputs rather than the high-dimensional vector sets typical in practice. Extending to multisymmetric powersums for vector inputs, discretizing the dynamics for SGD, and analyzing deeper or Transformer-based symmetric architectures are the natural next steps.
