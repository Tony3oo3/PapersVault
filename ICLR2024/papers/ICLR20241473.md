## Problem

Prior work on benign, tempered, and catastrophic overfitting in kernel ridge regression compares the interpolating model's risk to the Bayes error, conflating the direct cost of overfitting with the inherent difficulty of the learning problem. This paper asks a sharper agnostic question: how much worse is the ridgeless interpolant than the optimally-tuned ridge model, as a function of sample size, for an arbitrary target that may lie outside the RKHS?

## Input / Output

The analysis consumes the kernel eigenvalue spectrum {λ_i} (in descending order) and sample size n under a Gaussian universality ansatz, and produces a target-independent upper bound on the cost of overfitting expressed purely in terms of effective ranks r_k and R_k. The output is a three-way classification—benign, tempered, or catastrophic—determined by the asymptotic behavior of the ratio k/r_k as k→∞, with no dependence on the target eigencoefficients or noise level in the classification criterion itself.

## Method

The key insight is that the cost of overfitting C(D,n) = R(f̂_0)/inf_δ R(f̂_δ) is upper-bounded by the overfitting coefficient E_0 = (1 − (1/n)Σ_i L_{i,0}²)⁻¹, which depends only on the kernel spectrum and n, not on the target. Using the closed-form risk estimate from the Gaussian universality literature, the authors bound E_0 via effective ranks, showing E_0 ≤ (1−k/n)⁻²(1−n/R_k)⁻¹ for any k < n. They then establish that benign overfitting (E_0→1) holds if and only if k_n/n→0 and n/R_{k_n}→0, where k_n is the first index with n ≤ k + r_k. For tempered and catastrophic regimes, they derive that E_0 remains bounded when k/r_k→O(1) and diverges when k/r_k→∞, yielding a complete necessary-and-sufficient taxonomy. The framework is applied to inner-product kernels in the polynomial regime to recover the multiple descent curve.

## Results

The paper establishes that the cost of overfitting (ratio of interpolating to optimally-tuned ridge regression test error) is bounded above by the overfitting coefficient E_0, which depends only on the kernel eigenvalue spectrum and sample size, not on the target function (Theorem 1). The key quantitative bound (Theorem 2) shows E_0 ≤ (1−k/n)^(−2)(1−n/R_k)^(−1), where R_k is the effective rank, implying benign overfitting when k=o(n) and R_k=ω(n). The complete taxonomy (Theorem 6) characterizes overfitting as benign (k/r_k→0), tempered (k/r_k→c∈(0,∞)), or catastrophic (k/r_k→∞) based solely on the asymptotic behavior of the ratio k/r_k, providing a necessary and sufficient condition for each regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's contributions are theoretical rather than empirical, so comparisons are against prior theoretical results. It recovers the matching upper and lower bounds of Bartlett et al. (2020) on benign overfitting in linear regression, but with a simpler proof via the closed-form risk estimate and without requiring the target to be well-specified in the RKHS. It refines the taxonomy of Mallinar et al. (2022) by providing a necessary and sufficient condition (the limit of k/r_k) for each overfitting regime, resolving an open problem from that work. Unlike prior work that compares interpolating risk to Bayes error, this paper isolates the pure cost of overfitting by comparing to the optimally-tuned ridge model, making the analysis fully agnostic to the target function. The paper also extends the optimistic-rate uniform convergence result of Zhou et al. (2021) beyond the Gaussian feature setting using the Gaussian universality ansatz, and applies the framework to inner-product kernels in the polynomial regime to recover the multiple descent curve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the closed-form risk estimates for kernel ridge regression derived under the Gaussian universality ansatz (Hastie et al., 2019; Canatar et al., 2021; Simon et al., 2021) and the effective rank framework from Bartlett et al. (2020), but departs from the well-specified-model perspective that dominates prior overfitting analyses. Where Bartlett et al. and Mallinar et al. (2022) compare the interpolating model's risk to the Bayes error, this paper instead defines the cost of overfitting as a ratio to the best ridge model, yielding an agnostic guarantee that holds for any target function regardless of whether it lies in the RKHS. The optimistic-rate uniform convergence result from Zhou et al. (2021) is extended beyond the Gaussian feature setting to support the polynomial-regime application.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves an open problem from Mallinar et al. (2022) by giving a necessary-and-sufficient condition (via the ratio k/r_k) for when overfitting is benign, tempered, or catastrophic, and shows that the overfitting coefficient E_0 — depending only on the kernel spectrum and sample size, not the target — is the tightest agnostic upper bound on the cost of overfitting. This cleanly separates the 'price of overfitting' from the inherent difficulty of the learning problem, a distinction the authors argue prior work confounded. Limitations include reliance on the (non-rigorous) Gaussian universality ansatz and restriction to KRR; the authors note that extending the framework to kernel SVM or other regularizers is an open direction, as is connecting these results to finite-width neural networks.
