## Problem

Existing defenses against adversarial patches are passive: they process a single monocular observation and rely on prior knowledge of the adversary, making them vulnerable to unseen or adaptive attacks and poorly suited to dynamic 3D environments where the patch's appearance changes with viewpoint. No prior work has leveraged embodied active perception—proactive camera movement combined with recurrent belief refinement—to counter adversarial patches in 3D physical settings.

## Input / Output

At each timestep, EAD receives a 2D RGB image (112×112 for face recognition, variable resolution for object detection) rendered from a 3D scene at a camera state defined by yaw and pitch angles, with an adversarial patch projected onto a 3D surface (e.g., glasses or billboard). The model outputs a task-specific prediction (face embedding for identity verification, or detection boxes/masks) and a continuous camera rotation action (Δh, Δv) that determines the next viewpoint. Inference is sequential over a short horizon (τ=4 steps for FR), so the effective input is a temporal sequence of observations rather than a single image.

## Method

EAD comprises two recurrent sub-modules: a perception model (a Decision Transformer over frozen IResNet-50 features) that fuses the current observation with a previous belief to produce a refined belief and task prediction, and a policy model that maps the belief to a camera rotation action. Training is enabled by two key approximations: (1) a deterministic, differentiable environment model using Delta distributions for state transitions and EG3D differentiable rendering for observations, which creates a connected computational graph allowing backpropagation through the full multi-step trajectory; and (2) a Uniform Superset Approximation for Adversarial Patches (USAP) that samples patches uniformly from [0,1]^{H×W×C} rather than optimizing specific attacks, making the defense attack-agnostic. Theoretically, the InfoNCE training objective is shown to lower-bound the conditional mutual information I(o_t; y | b_{t-1}), which is equivalent to the conditional entropy decrease, thereby driving the learned policy toward greedy informative exploration that monotonically reduces the uncertainty induced by adversarial patches.

## Results

On CelebA-3D face recognition, EAD achieves 90.45% standard accuracy (highest among all methods, exceeding the undefended model's 88.86%) while reducing the 3DAdv impersonation ASR from 89.0% to 7.2% (84.3% reduction) and dodging ASR from 98.0% to 13.7% (81.8% reduction). Against the most challenging adaptive attack, EAD maintains an ASR of 8.3% (impersonation) and 22.1% (dodging) versus 100% for the undefended model. On CARLA-GEAR object detection, EAD achieves 39.4–39.5 mAP under MIM/TIM/SIB attacks, outperforming the undefended Mask-RCNN (30.6–34.2 mAP) while preserving clean mAP at 46.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAD dominates all five baselines across nearly every attack setting. Against JPEG compression (Dziugaite et al.), EAD reduces 3DAdv impersonation ASR from 93.0% to 7.2% and dodging ASR from 88.0% to 13.7%, while also achieving higher clean accuracy (90.45% vs. 89.98%). Against LGS (Naseer et al.), EAD is superior on all attacks except impersonation MIM where LGS is marginally lower (5.1% vs. 4.1%), but EAD's advantage is dramatic on 3DAdv (7.2% vs. 30.6%) and adaptive attacks (8.3% vs. 38.4%). Against SAC (Liu et al.), EAD wins on every metric: 3DAdv impersonation drops from 64.6% to 7.2%, and even the adversarially-trained SAC† (22.1% on 3DAdv) is outperformed. Against PZ (Xu et al.), EAD is competitive on impersonation MIM/EoT (4.1%/3.1% vs. 4.1%/5.2%) but decisively superior on 3DAdv (7.2% vs. 45.8%) and adaptive attacks (8.3% vs. 89.8%). Against DOA (Wu et al.), EAD is overwhelmingly better—DOA† achieves 89.9% ASR on 3DAdv impersonation while EAD achieves 7.2%, and DOA† also suffers the lowest clean accuracy (79.55%) among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of passive adversarial patch defenses (adversarial training, purification, segmentation-based removal) by introducing an embodied active defense framework grounded in active vision and POMDPs. It builds on the Decision Transformer for temporal sequence modeling and EG3D for differentiable 3D rendering, but the key conceptual departure is treating adversarial defense as a sequential decision-making problem where the agent strategically moves its camera to gather informative observations rather than processing a single static image. The theoretical grounding via InfoNCE and greedy informative exploration connects the training objective to mutual information maximization, providing a principled justification absent in prior empirical defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The attack-agnostic design (USAP with uniform random patches) enabling 95% ASR reduction across diverse unseen attacks is practically significant for safety-critical deployments where the adversary's strategy is unknown. However, the approach is currently confined to simulated 3D environments (EG3D, CARLA) with camera-rotation-only actions, raising open questions about scalability to full 6-DoF navigation, real-world deployment with hardware constraints, and whether the differentiable rendering assumption holds in unstructured scenes. The finding that EAD improves clean accuracy over the undefended model (90.45% vs 88.86%) challenges the common robustness-accuracy tradeoff narrative, but this benefit may be specific to the face recognition setup and the particular 3D reconstruction quality of CelebA-3D.
