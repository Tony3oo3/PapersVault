## Problem

Malicious server (MS) attacks in federated learning can steal client data from large batches and under secure aggregation, but all prior attacks (boosted analytical and example disaggregation) are detectable by principled client-side checks in weight or gradient space. No prior work systematically studied or reduced this detectability, leaving open whether practical, undetectable MS data-stealing attacks are possible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240973/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The server receives the aggregated gradient g of a client batch (B examples, up to 512) computed through a shared ResNet18 model with maliciously trained weights θ_f, and applies a secret disaggregator d (a linear projection into a lower-dimensional hidden space) followed by a secret reconstructor r (a linear layer for CIFAR, a linearized U-Net for ImageNet) to recover a single target image. The target is selected by a secret property P (e.g., minimal in-batch brightness) known only to the server, and the output is a pixel-level RGB reconstruction of that image.

## Method

SEER jointly trains the shared model f, a secret disaggregator d, and a secret reconstructor r end-to-end with SGD on auxiliary data, minimizing a weighted sum of a reconstruction loss L_rec and a nullification loss L_nul. The disaggregator d is a secret linear map that projects the aggregated gradient into a lower-dimensional space where gradients of non-target examples are driven into the null space, effectively isolating the target example's gradient contribution without any visible disaggregation in the original gradient space. The key insight is that this hidden-space disaggregation evades gradient-space detection (measured by the proposed D-SNR metric), while joint SGD training avoids the handcrafted weight modifications that make prior attacks detectable in weight space. For the single-client large-batch setting, SEER exploits batch normalization to define a local in-batch property P (e.g., the darkest image), achieving P(|I_rec|=1) > 0.9 for B up to 512, a significant improvement over the 1/e success rate of prior global-threshold approaches.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with B=128 and the Red property, SEER achieves 93.5% reconstruction success (Rec) with PSNR-All of 27.8±4.1 and PSNR-Top of 31.1±1.2. SEER scales to batch sizes up to 512 with minimal degradation (e.g., 87.8% Rec and 23.2±3.5 PSNR-All on CIFAR-10 at B=512) and works under secure aggregation with C=4–8 clients (41–53% Rec, PSNR-Top >25). In the head-to-head comparison (Table 4, CIFAR-10, B=128), SEER achieves 90% Rec with PSNR-All of 23.8±3.3, and critically, 90% of its successful reconstructions remain undetected by D-SNR, whereas all prior methods have 0–8% undetected success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEER outperforms Fishing (Wen et al., 2022) on both reconstruction success and quality: at the best Fishing setting (β=400), SEER achieves 90% Rec vs. 77% and PSNR-All of 23.8 vs. 21.7. More critically, SEER's 90% undetected success rate (Und-Rec) dwarfs Fishing's maximum of 8%, and SEER's PSNR-Und equals its PSNR-All (23.8), meaning no quality trade-off is needed to avoid detection—whereas Fishing's PSNR-Und is always well below its PSNR-All. Against Zhang23 (Zhang et al., 2023), SEER is overwhelmingly superior: Zhang23 recovers only 5% of images (PSNR-All 15.8) due to stride and pooling in realistic convnets, while SEER recovers 90%. LOKI (Zhao et al., 2023) achieves 100% Rec with PSNR-All of 143.4, but this relies on trivially detectable architectural modifications (e.g., inserting a large dense layer) and sending different models to clients, placing it outside the realistic threat model; its Und-Rec is 0%. The paper's central finding is that SEER is the only method that simultaneously achieves high reconstruction quality and near-complete evasion of gradient-space detection (D-SNR), a property no prior MS attack possesses.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEER departs fundamentally from both classes of prior malicious server (MS) attacks—boosted analytical and example disaggregation—by not lifting any honest attack and instead jointly training a secret server-side decoder (disaggregator + reconstructor) with the shared model via SGD on auxiliary data. It extends the property-based selection mechanism of Fowl et al. (2022b) by introducing local (in-batch) properties enabled by batch normalization, improving the success probability from 1/e to >0.9 for large batches. The paper also introduces D-SNR, a novel gradient-space metric that demonstrates all prior example disaggregation attacks are detectable, positioning SEER as the first MS attack designed to evade both weight-space and gradient-space detection by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely-held assumption that secure aggregation and large batch sizes provide robust privacy protection in federated learning, showing that a single malicious round can recover client images from batches of 512 even under secure aggregation. The finding that all prior MS attacks are client-side detectable via principled checks (D-SNR for disaggregation, weight inspection for boosted analytical) questions their practicality as zero-day exploits and motivates detection as a first-class design criterion for future attacks. Open questions include whether SEER itself can be detected, whether DP-SGD with stronger noise parameters can close the gap, and how the approach generalizes to other modalities and architectures. The paper advocates for principled, attack-categorization-based client-side defenses as the most promising path forward.
