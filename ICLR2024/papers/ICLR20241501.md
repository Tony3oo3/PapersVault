## Problem

Existing human trajectory prediction models rely predominantly on a single input cue (x-y ground-plane coordinates) and fail to exploit the rich non-verbal social signals—body pose, orientation, bounding boxes—that humans subconsciously communicate before changing direction or interacting with others. The paper asks how to build a single generic architecture that can flexibly consume any available combination of these diverse visual cues, in varying quantities and quality, without retraining.

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of past observations (9 time-steps at 2.5 fps on JTA/JRDB) where each time-step may include any subset of: 2D/3D x-y trajectory coordinates, 2D/3D body pose keypoints (e.g., 17 joints), and 2D/3D bounding boxes. It outputs a single deterministic future trajectory of 12 time-steps for the primary agent. A key design constraint is that the number of tokens passed to the Social Transformer is fixed regardless of how many or which cues are present, since only the motion cross-modal tensor (shape T_pred × D) is forwarded, decoupling the social-interaction stage from input modality count.

## Method

Social-Transmotion uses a dual-Transformer architecture: a Cross-Modality Transformer (CMT) with shared parameters processes each agent's multi-modal embeddings (trajectory, pose, bounding boxes) alongside a set of latent queries (T_pred − T_obs × D) to produce a fixed-size motion cross-modal tensor; a Social Transformer (ST) then attends across all agents' motion tensors to capture inter-agent interactions. The critical training innovation is a two-level masking strategy—modality-masking (randomly dropping an entire cue type across all time-steps) and meta-masking (randomly dropping a fraction of features within a cue at random time-steps)—which forces the network to learn robust representations that degrade gracefully when cues are missing or noisy. Cue-specific MLP embeddings plus three learned positional encodings (temporal order, person identity, keypoint type) feed into the CMT, and the final prediction is obtained by projecting the primary agent's latent-query portion of the ST output through an MLP.

## Results

On the JTA dataset, Social-Transmotion with trajectory + 3D pose (T+3D P) achieves ADE 0.89 / FDE 1.81, outperforming the strongest trajectory-only baseline EqMotion (ADE 1.13 / FDE 2.39) by 21% in ADE. Using only trajectory input, the model still leads all baselines with ADE 0.99 / FDE 1.98. On JRDB, incorporating 2D bounding boxes yields ADE 0.37 / FDE 0.73, surpassing Autobots (0.39 / 0.80). On the Pedestrians and Cyclists in Road Traffic dataset, Social-Transmotion with T+3D P achieves ASWAEE 0.40, beating the best pose-based baseline (Kress et al., 0.51) by 22%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Social-Transmotion outperforms all nine compared baselines on the JTA dataset, with the largest margin over EqMotion (best prior method): ADE drops from 1.13 to 0.99 (trajectory-only) and to 0.89 (with 3D pose), a 21–27% improvement. On JRDB, the model beats Autobots (0.39→0.40 ADE with T only; 0.37 with T+2D BB) and Trajectron++ (0.40→0.40/0.37), while Social-LSTM and Social-GAN-det are surpassed by a wider margin (0.47→0.40 and 0.50→0.40 ADE respectively). On the Pedestrians and Cyclists in Road Traffic dataset, Social-Transmotion with T+3D P (ASWAEE 0.40) substantially outperforms Kress et al.'s pose-based model (0.51) and their trajectory-only variant (0.57). On ETH-UCY, the model is competitive but not the best: it achieves average ADE/FDE of 0.51/1.03, slightly behind EqMotion (0.49/1.03) but ahead of Autobots (0.52/1.05) and MemoNet (0.55/1.15), and it leads on the most challenging ETH subset (0.93 vs. 0.96 ADE). Overall, the dual-Transformer architecture with masking consistently wins or ties across all four datasets, with the largest gains coming from incorporating 3D pose keypoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer-based trajectory prediction line (Giuliari et al. 2021, Yuan et al. 2021, Girgis et al. 2022) by introducing a dual-Transformer architecture where a Cross-Modality Transformer (CMT) fuses diverse per-agent visual cues and a Social Transformer (ST) captures inter-agent interactions. It departs from prior pose-based predictors (Kress et al. 2022) that used only the primary agent's pose in isolation, by explicitly modeling social interactions through pose keypoints of all agents. The 'promptable' framing repurposes the NLP prompt concept to trajectory prediction, treating trajectories, 2D/3D poses, and bounding boxes as interchangeable input modalities, while a masking training strategy (modality-masking and meta-masking) ensures the single model generalizes across cue availability—something prior multi-modal trajectory predictors did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The masking-based training strategy yields a single model that gracefully degrades when visual cues are missing or noisy, which is practically critical for real-world deployment where pose estimators are imperfect or occluded. The finding that the generic model's ADE degrades by only 23.6% under 50% trajectory + 10% pose availability, versus 232.6% for a specific model, highlights a robustness advantage that prior architectures did not provide. Open questions remain around incorporating scene context (barriers, road geometry), stochastic prediction, and additional cues like gaze direction or action labels, which the authors acknowledge as limitations. The observation that only the last observed pose frame nearly matches full temporal pose sequences also opens the door to lighter-weight real-time systems.
