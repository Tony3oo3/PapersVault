## Problem

Autoregressive models trained with maximum likelihood suffer from compounding error during generation: the MLE objective weights sequences by data frequency and provides no constraint on model behavior for out-of-distribution (OOD) sequences, so once the model samples an OOD token, its own outputs become inputs and errors cascade. This paper addresses the gap by reframing sequence generation as an imitation learning problem, enabling training against divergences that explicitly penalize OOD generated sequences, and by introducing a backspace action that lets the model correct erroneous tokens during generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial text sequence (prompt of up to 256 tokens) or a math question prompt as input and produces a generated continuation (up to 512 tokens) that may interleave standard token insertions with <bkspc> actions that delete the most recently generated token. The model is a Llama2-7b transformer fine-tuned with QLoRA adapters; a single additional row is added to the unembedding layer for the backspace logit. During training, data sequences are augmented with stochastic noise tokens followed by <bkspc> labels to teach the model backtracking dynamics, and model-generated trajectories from a replay buffer supply the on-policy samples needed for the occupancy divergence loss.

## Method

SequenceMatch formulates autoregressive generation as a Markov decision process over (partial) sequences with an action set comprising all vocabulary tokens plus a backspace action, then derives a fully supervised, non-adversarial loss by extending the IQ-Learn occupancy-divergence minimization framework (Garg et al., 2021) to infinite-dimensional state spaces. The key theoretical step is a change of variables from the critic r to Q-values via the soft Bellman operator, which collapses the min-max saddle-point problem into a single-variable optimization over policy logits, yielding a plug-in estimator that is unbiased and consistent. The paper identifies the χ²-mixture divergence (between data occupancy and the mixture of data and model occupancies) as the most effective objective, implemented via a specific φ function plus an on-policy regularization term. A novel masking scheme (Algorithm A) converts action sequences containing backspaces into valid single-pass transformer inputs with appropriate attention masks and position IDs, enabling training with no additional forward-pass overhead relative to MLE. The method requires no architectural changes beyond the extra unembedding row and can be applied as a finetuning step on pretrained models.

## Results

On the OpenWebText text generation task (Llama-2-7b, 1024 context), SequenceMatch achieves the highest MAUVE score of 0.91±0.02, outperforming MLE (0.85), MLE+contrastive sampling (0.86), and MLE+unlikelihood training (0.89). It also attains the best n-gram entropy (4.60±0.01) while maintaining diversity (0.56) comparable to MLE and unlikelihood training. On the arithmetic task (5,000 training examples), SequenceMatch with ground-truth noise reaches ~85–90% accuracy, substantially exceeding both MLE and behavioral cloning baselines, and demonstrates the ability to detect and correct out-of-distribution partial solutions via the backspace action.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MLE+contrastive sampling (Jiang et al., 2022), SequenceMatch wins decisively on MAUVE (0.91 vs. 0.86) and n-gram entropy (4.60 vs. 4.43), while contrastive sampling collapses diversity to 0.35 (vs. 0.56 for SequenceMatch), indicating it over-constrains generation. Against MLE+unlikelihood training (Welleck et al., 2019), SequenceMatch achieves a higher MAUVE (0.91 vs. 0.89) and n-gram entropy (4.60 vs. 4.57), though unlikelihood training edges out slightly on diversity (0.57 vs. 0.56) and perplexity (7.10 vs. 7.13). The paper notes that unlikelihood relies on a heuristic penalizing repetitions, which is inappropriate for tasks like arithmetic where repeated digits are valid. On the arithmetic task, SequenceMatch with ground-truth noise substantially outperforms both MLE and behavioral cloning, with the gap widening as noise level increases, while random noise helps SequenceMatch (by teaching backspace dynamics) but not behavioral cloning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the IQ-Learn framework (Garg et al., 2021) from finite-state MDPs to the infinite-dimensional state space of autoregressive sequence generation, and builds on the saddle-point occupancy divergence formulation from GAIL (Ho & Ermon, 2016). It adopts the chi-squared mixture divergence regularization from LS-IQ (Al-Hafez et al., 2023) as its primary training objective. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a backspace action into the MDP, which has no direct analogue in prior IL work and requires a novel masking scheme for efficient transformer training. The paper positions itself as a specialization of IQ-Learn to autoregressive models, showing that the MLE objective emerges as the α→0 limit of the general occupancy divergence framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compounding error problem in autoregressive generation is a well-recognized limitation, and this paper provides a principled, non-adversarial mechanism (backspace + occupancy divergence) to address it without architectural changes. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single model (Llama2-7b), small datasets (5,000 arithmetic examples), and modest gains (MAUVE 0.85→0.91), with a ~5× training overhead that may limit practical adoption. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and longer sequences, how the choice of divergence (χ² vs. JS vs. KL) affects generation quality at scale, and whether the noise-augmentation bias in the backspace training degrades performance on tasks where errors are less structured. The backspace mechanism could also be explored as an inference-time technique independent of the training objective.
