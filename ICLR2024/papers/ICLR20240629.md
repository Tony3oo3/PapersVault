## Problem

Post-training quantization (PTQ) of diffusion models is fast and data-free but fails to maintain denoising capability at 4-bit or lower, while quantization-aware training (QAT) recovers quality at the cost of requiring the original training dataset and incurring ~17× more GPU time and ~2.6× more memory. No existing method achieves QAT-level generation quality with PTQ-level data and time efficiency for low-bit diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240629/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During fine-tuning, the method is entirely data-free: inputs x_t are generated by iteratively denoising random Gaussian noise with the full-precision model for T−t steps, and the loss is the MSE between the FP and quantized models' denoising outputs at each step. At inference, the quantized model accepts the same inputs as the original (random noise, optionally conditioned on class labels or text prompts) and produces synthesized images at the target resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 256×256 for ImageNet, 256×256 for LSUN), with both weights and activations stored at the target bit-width (down to W2A8).

## Method

EfficientDM introduces QALoRA, a quantization-aware variant of LoRA where the low-rank update matrices BA are merged with the full-precision weights W₀ and jointly quantized to the target bit-width, so only the quantized updated weights need to be stored at inference with no extra storage or compute overhead. Fine-tuning is data-free: the same noise x_t is fed to both the FP and quantized denoising models, and the MSE between their estimated noise outputs is minimized, effectively distilling denoising capability without any training images. To counter the fact that quantization makes the BA→Ŵ mapping step-like (with step size equal to the per-channel quantization scale), scale-aware LoRA optimization multiplies LoRA gradients by the average weight quantization scale per layer, ensuring effective updates across layers with heterogeneous scales. Additionally, TALSQ extends learned step-size quantization to the temporal domain by assigning a separate learnable activation scale to each denoising step, addressing the pronounced variation in activation distributions across timesteps; learned scales are interpolated at inference when the number of sampling steps differs from fine-tuning.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with LDM-4 and DDIM (20 steps), EfficientDM achieves FID 6.17 and sFID 7.75 at W4A4, where all PTQ baselines (Q-Diffusion, PTQD) completely fail to generate images (N/A). At W4A8, it reaches FID 9.93 and sFID 7.34, outperforming PTQD (FID 8.74, sFID 7.98) and Q-Diffusion (FID 9.29, sFID 9.29) on sFID. On CIFAR-10 32×32, EfficientDM achieves FID 3.80 at W4A8, beating the QAT-based TDQ (FID 4.13) while using no training data and only 0.97 GPU hours vs. TDQ's 16.99 hours. The method also pushes quantization to W2A8 for the first time, achieving FID 7.60 and sFID 8.12 versus FID 39.37 for PTQD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EfficientDM decisively outperforms all PTQ-based methods (PTQ4DM, Q-Diffusion, PTQD, ADP-DM) at W4A4 on ImageNet 256×256, where every PTQ baseline fails entirely (N/A) while EfficientDM achieves FID 6.17 and sFID 7.75. Against the QAT-based TDQ (So et al.), EfficientDM achieves a lower FID (3.80 vs. 4.13) on CIFAR-10 W4A8 while requiring no training data and running 17.5× faster (0.97 vs. 16.99 GPU hours). Compared to the QAT-based LSQ (Esser et al.), EfficientDM matches or exceeds FID on CIFAR-10 W8A8 (3.75 vs. 3.87) and W4A8 (3.80 vs. 4.53) with 14× less GPU time and zero training data. On LSUN-Bedrooms W6A6, EfficientDM (FID 3.62) is within 0.07 of LSQ (FID 3.55) while being data-free and ~16× faster. Against PTQD (He et al.), the closest PTQ competitor, EfficientDM wins on sFID at W4A8 (7.34 vs. 7.98) and W8A8 (8.04 vs. 9.01) on ImageNet, and achieves a 31× FID improvement at W4A4 (6.17 vs. 259.73). The only scenario where a baseline matches is W8A8 on CIFAR-10, where Q-Diffusion ties at FID 3.75, though EfficientDM still uses less GPU time (0.97 vs. 0.95 hours is negligible).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA/QLoRA) and quantization-aware training for diffusion models. It departs from QLoRA by merging LoRA weights with model weights before joint quantization (eliminating full-precision storage at inference), and from prior QAT-for-diffusion work (TDQ) by operating in a data-free distillation regime rather than requiring the original training set. The scale-aware LoRA optimization and TALSQ are novel extensions that address specific failure modes (step-function quantization across heterogeneous layer scales, and temporal activation distribution shifts) not previously tackled in the diffusion quantization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EfficientDM offers a practical path to deploying diffusion models on resource-constrained hardware by achieving QAT-level generation quality at PTQ-level cost (no training data, ~16× faster than QAT), and pushes weight quantization to 2-bit for the first time on diffusion models. The remaining limitation is that the gradient-based fine-tuning still demands more GPU memory than pure PTQ, which becomes problematic for very large models; the authors note that memory-efficient optimizers could address this. Broader open questions include whether the framework extends to video/3D diffusion models and whether the distillation-based approach generalizes to conditional generation tasks beyond class-conditional ImageNet.
