## Problem

Data heterogeneity in federated learning causes local model divergence and limits global performance, yet existing methods treat it as an inherent property and only mitigate its effects through model-level corrections (regularization, gradient correction, aggregation tuning). This paper asks whether the heterogeneity level itself can be reduced by modifying the data, rather than merely compensating for it in model space.

## Input / Output

Each client consumes its local image dataset (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 FLAIR), the current global model, and its own previous-round local model to produce a set of learnable input tensors (H×W×C) paired with pre-defined target labels. The generated tensors are not real images but optimized inputs that the global model classifies correctly while the local model misclassifies; they are then used with the global model's soft outputs as distillation targets. The final output is the aggregated global model, with the generated data serving as an intermediate training signal that is discarded after local training.

## Method

FedCOG adds two client-side modules to the standard FedAvg loop. First, complementary data generation: each client optimizes a batch of learnable input tensors (initialized as noise) by minimizing a joint objective of task-driven cross-entropy loss under the frozen global model plus a Jensen-Shannon-divergence-based disagreement loss between the frozen global model and the frozen previous local model, yielding samples the global model 'knows' but the local model does not. Second, knowledge-distillation-based local training: the local model is re-initialized from the global model and trained for τ SGD steps on a combined loss — standard task loss on the original dataset plus a KL-divergence distillation loss on the generated dataset using the global model's soft predictions (reused from the generation step, avoiding recomputation). This dual supervision prevents overfitting to the heterogeneous local data while preserving consensual knowledge. The approach is plug-and-play (orthogonal to model-correction methods like SCAFFOLD or MOON), compatible with Secure Aggregation since no extra communication objects are introduced, and comes with a convergence proof showing O(1/√(τT)) rate under standard FL assumptions.

## Results

FedCOG achieves the highest average accuracy of 57.92% across Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 under both NIID-1 and NIID-2 heterogeneity settings, outperforming the best baseline FedAvgM (55.07%) by 2.85 percentage points. The gains are most pronounced under the more heterogeneous NIID-2 setting, where FedCOG reaches 73.68% on Fashion-MNIST (vs. 67.73% for FedAvgM) and 54.00% on CIFAR-10 (vs. 51.32% for FedAvgM). On the real-world multi-label FLAIR dataset, FedCOG achieves 51.35% O-F1 and 20.89% C-F1, surpassing FedAvg by 14% and 15% relatively, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCOG consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting in Table 1. The largest margins appear under NIID-2 (partial-label heterogeneity): FedCOG beats SCAFFOLD by 15.21 points on Fashion-MNIST (73.68 vs. 58.47) and by 5.65 points on CIFAR-10 (54.00 vs. 48.35), while SCAFFOLD actually underperforms FedAvg on Fashion-MNIST NIID-2. On the more balanced NIID-1 setting, FedCOG leads FedAvgM by 0.53 points on CIFAR-10 (64.83 vs. 64.36) and by 0.63 points on CIFAR-100 (42.88 vs. 42.25 for SCAFFOLD). On the real-world FLAIR multi-label dataset (Table 2), FedCOG achieves 51.35% O-F1, surpassing the next-best FedAvgM (48.43%) by 2.92 points and FedAvg (44.53%) by 6.82 points; notably, VHL and FedReg are inapplicable to this task. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 3) confirm that adding even a single round of FedCOG on top of FedAvg, FedAvgM, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, or MOON yields consistent gains of 0.2–8.9 points depending on the base method and dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedCOG departs from the dominant paradigm in FL heterogeneity research, which treats data heterogeneity as an inherent property to be mitigated through model-level corrections (regularization in FedProx, gradient correction in SCAFFOLD, feature alignment in MOON). Instead, it repurposes the inverse-optimization technique from data-free knowledge distillation (DeepInversion, Yin et al. 2020) for the FL setting, adding a novel disagreement loss between global and local models to generate client-specific complementary data. Unlike server-side data generation methods (FedGen, DENSE) that require access to individual local models, FedCOG operates entirely at the client side, making it compatible with Secure Aggregation protocols that only expose the aggregated global model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play property—consistently improving five different FL baselines with as few as one additional round—makes FedCOG a practical drop-in module for existing FL deployments, and its compatibility with Secure Aggregation addresses a real deployment constraint that many prior methods ignore. The finding that FedCOG simultaneously improves both generalization and personalization is somewhat counterintuitive, suggesting that consensus-oriented data generation does not sacrifice local fitting. Open questions include whether more advanced generative models (diffusion, GANs) could further improve generation quality, whether the approach extends well to non-visual modalities like text (where only embeddings can be optimized), and how it behaves under extreme resource heterogeneity across clients.
