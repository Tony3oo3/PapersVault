## Problem

While prior work on noisy label learning has focused heavily on accurately estimating the transition matrix T, the paper argues that how T is utilized during training is equally critical and underexplored. Existing utilization methods—Forward loss and importance Reweighting—suffer from practical limitations: Forward can memorize noisy labels even with a perfect T, and Reweighting's per-sample weights depend on the imperfect classifier output, causing a mismatch between estimated and true risk. The paper identifies resampling as an unexplored T utilization strategy that could overcome these issues.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy-labeled RGB image dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Clothing1M at 224×224) together with a transition matrix T estimated by any compatible method (Forward, DualT, TV, VolMinNet, Cycle, PDN, BLTM). At each training step, the current classifier f_θ produces per-sample importance weights ỹ_i = f_θ(x_i)_{ỹ_i} / (T f_θ(x_i))_{ỹ_i}, from which a categorical distribution is constructed to resample M instances (default M = N) for the next gradient update. The output is a standard softmax class prediction; the resampled dataset implicitly acts as a noise-filtered training set.

## Method

The paper introduces the Dirichlet-based per-sample Weight Sampling (DWS) framework, which unifies reweighting (α→∞, weights concentrate at the mean) and resampling (α→0, weights concentrate at simplex vertices) as sampling from Dir(αμ) with mean vector μ derived from the classifier and T. Three analyses of α show that smaller α is preferable: it increases risk variance (improving robustness per Lin et al. 2022), reduces the Mahalanobis distance between the estimated and true weight vectors (proportional to √α), and injects instance-adaptive label perturbation analogous to SNL. RENT is the α→0 instantiation, implementing Sampling-Importance-Resampling (SIR) where each mini-batch is drawn from a Categorical distribution parameterized by the normalized importance weights. A proposition proves that with a perfect T, RENT's empirical risk is statistically consistent with the true risk, and empirically RENT outperforms both Forward and Reweighting across 48 synthetic and 30 real-world noise settings.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with symmetric 20% noise, RENT combined with DualT achieves 82.0% test accuracy, outperforming Forward loss (79.9%) and Reweighting (80.6%). Under the more challenging 50% symmetric noise, RENT with Cycle reaches 70.4% while Forward loss fails entirely and Reweighting drops to 57.0%. On CIFAR-100, RENT with Cycle achieves 40.7% (SN 20%) and 32.4% (SN 50%), consistently leading all baselines. On real-world CIFAR-10N (Worst noise, ~40% noise ratio), RENT with BLTM reaches 69.7% versus 65.8% for Forward loss and 43.5% for Reweighting, and on Clothing1M it achieves 70.0% versus 67.2% for Forward loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RENT outperforms Forward loss (FL) in 42 out of 48 settings across CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, with the largest gaps appearing under high noise ratios (e.g., +11.6% on CIFAR-10 SN 50% with Cycle, where FL fails entirely). Against Reweighting (RW), RENT wins in all but two settings, and where RW does edge ahead the margin is under 0.5%, while RENT's wins are often 3–13% (e.g., 70.4% vs. 57.0% on CIFAR-10 SN 50% with Cycle). Compared to VRNL (Lin et al., 2022), RENT consistently achieves higher accuracy without requiring an additional variance-regularization hyperparameter; for instance, on CIFAR-10 ASN 20% with Forward, RENT reaches 82.0% versus VRNL's 54.2%. Against SNL (Chen et al., 2020), RENT consistently outperforms across all σ values tested, indicating that the implicit noise injection from resampling is more effective than explicit label perturbation. For resampled dataset quality (Figure 7), RENT surpasses both FINE (Kim et al., 2021) and MCD (Lee et al., 2019) in F1 score, confirming it produces cleaner and more diverse resampled subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transition matrix framework for noisy label learning (Patrini et al., 2017; Liu & Tao, 2015) by reframing the utilization phase through a Dirichlet-based per-sample weight sampling (DWS) lens, which unifies reweighting (α→∞) and resampling (α→0) under a single distributional family. The resampling mechanism in RENT is directly derived from the Sampling-Importance-Resampling (SIR) algorithm (Rubin, 1988), and the motivation that resampling can outperform reweighting is borrowed from An et al. (2020) in the context of sampling bias correction. The paper departs from prior work by arguing that the *utilization* of T (not just its estimation) is the underexplored bottleneck, and by providing a theoretical rationale—via variance, Mahalanobis distance, and implicit label perturbation—for why the resampling extreme of DWS is preferable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RENT offers a plug-and-play resampling alternative to Forward loss and Reweighting that consistently improves accuracy across six T-estimation methods and four datasets, including real-world noise (CIFAR-10N, Clothing1M), without requiring additional hyperparameters beyond the resampling budget M. The DWS framework provides a clean theoretical bridge between reweighting and resampling that could inform future importance-sampling-based corrections in other noisy-data settings. Limitations include the reliance on an invertible T, the fact that true T does not always yield the best performance (suggesting estimation error may interact beneficially with the resampling process), and the fact that the method still depends on the on-training classifier's output for weight computation, which can be inaccurate early in training. Open questions include whether RENT extends naturally to instance-dependent T(X) settings and whether the Dirichlet concentration parameter α could be adapted dynamically during training.
