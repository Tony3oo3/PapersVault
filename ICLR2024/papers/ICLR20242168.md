## Problem

Existing robust RL defenses optimize for worst-case adversarial attacks via minimax, which sacrifices natural performance when attacks are absent or weak. The paper asks whether a single framework can simultaneously handle non-worst-case attacks (random, periodic, probabilistic) while preserving worst-case robustness, and formalizes the test-time adaptation problem as regret minimization, proving it is intractable over a general continuous policy class.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242168/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the victim receives perturbed state observations (the attacker maps true states to nearby states within an l_p ball of radius ε) and scalar reward feedback from each completed episode. Rather than fine-tuning a policy network, the method maintains a weight vector ω ∈ Δ(Π̃) over a small finite set of pre-trained policies (|Π̃| = 3–5 in experiments), updating it via EXP3-style multiplicative weight updates. The output is a sampled policy from Π̃ each episode, producing standard continuous actions in the Mujoco environment.

## Method

PROTECTED has two phases. During training, Algorithm 2 iteratively discovers non-dominated policies by solving a max-min-max objective: at each iteration k, it finds the policy π^{k+1} that maximizes the worst-case reward gap over the current set {π^1,...,π^k}, using alternating gradient ascent (PPO for the victim, PA-AD for the attacker) on a lower-bound relaxation. Theorem 4.10 guarantees this converges to Gap(Π̃, Π) ≤ δ in finite iterations, and under uniqueness conditions yields the minimal optimal set. At test time, Algorithm 1 treats each policy in Π̃ as a bandit arm and runs EXP3, updating weights based on observed episode rewards to achieve sublinear regret against any (possibly adversarial) sequence of attackers. The key distinction from prior work is that the attacker in the training objective is not the worst-case minimizer of victim reward but rather the maximizer of the undominated gap, encouraging diversity in the discovered policy set.

## Results

On four Mujoco environments (Hopper, Walker2d, Halfcheetah, Ant), PROTECTED achieves the best natural rewards across all environments (e.g., 6319 on Walker2d vs. 4156 for WocaR-PPO) and the strongest robustness against the most powerful attack PA-AD (e.g., 5803 on Walker2d vs. 2722 for WocaR-PPO). The method also leads on all non-worst-case attacks (Random, RS, SA-RL) in 15 out of 16 environment-attack combinations, with the sole exception being SA-RL on Halfcheetah where WocaR-PPO edges ahead (5365 vs. 5089). The advantage is most dramatic on Walker2d, where PROTECTED nearly doubles the PA-AD reward of the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROTECTED outperforms all three baselines (ATLA-PPO, PA-ATLA-PPO, WocaR-PPO) on natural reward in every environment, with the largest margin on Walker2d (6319 vs. 4156 for WocaR-PPO, a 52% improvement). Against the strongest attack PA-AD, PROTECTED leads in all four environments, most dramatically on Walker2d (5803 vs. 2722 for WocaR-PPO) and Ant (4312 vs. 3164). Against non-worst-case attacks (Random, RS, SA-RL), PROTECTED wins 15 of 16 environment-attack pairs; the single loss is SA-RL on Halfcheetah where WocaR-PPO scores 5365 vs. 5089. The paper further shows (Table 2) that baselines like PA-ATLA-PPO and WocaR-PPO can occasionally match PROTECTED's best-run PA-AD performance but have much lower median performance, suggesting baselines are less reliable in finding the global optimum for worst-case robustness. An ablation (Table 3) confirms that the gains come from the iterative non-dominated policy discovery (Algorithm 2) rather than merely applying online reward feedback to a fixed policy set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the state-adversarial attack model (SA-MDP, PA-MDP) and the line of worst-case robust RL training (ATLA-PPO, PA-ATLA-PPO, WocaR-PPO) by shifting the test-time objective from deploying a single minimax-optimal policy to online regret minimization over a finite set of non-dominated policies. The online adaptation mechanism is directly borrowed from adversarial bandit algorithms (EXP3), while the training-time iterative discovery of non-dominated policies draws conceptual parallels to multi-objective RL and iterated elimination of dominated strategies in game theory. The key departure from prior work is the explicit treatment of non-worst-case attacks as a first-class concern, rather than a byproduct of worst-case training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: real-world attackers are often constrained (limited sensor access, computational budgets), making worst-case-only defenses overly conservative and sacrificing natural performance. The hardness results (Proposition 4.3, Theorem 4.11) delineate fundamental limits, while the empirical finding that only 3–5 non-dominated policies suffice on Mujoco suggests the approach is tractable. Open questions include identifying natural conditions (e.g., Lipschitz dynamics) that circumvent the hardness results, reducing the ~2× training overhead, and extending beyond the state-perturbation attack model to other threat models.
