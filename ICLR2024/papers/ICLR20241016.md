## Problem

Time-domain GAN vocoders (e.g., HiFi-GAN, BigVGAN) rely on cascaded transposed convolutions for upsampling, which are computationally expensive and susceptible to aliasing artifacts, while neglecting the inductive bias of time-frequency representations that align with human auditory perception. Direct reconstruction of complex-valued spectrograms has been historically problematic due to phase wrapping and adversarial training instability, leaving a gap between the efficiency of Fourier-based methods and the quality of time-domain approaches.

## Input / Output

Vocos takes 100-bin mel-spectrograms (n_fft=1024, hop=256, 24 kHz) or quantized EnCodec audio tokens as input and produces a 24 kHz audio waveform. The network maintains a constant temporal resolution equal to the STFT hop size across all layers; the final upsampling from spectral coefficients to waveform is performed entirely by the inverse STFT rather than learned transposed convolutions. In the codec setting, adaptive layer normalization conditions the generator on the quantized latent, and a projection discriminator is used instead of the standard MPD/MRD.

## Method

Vocos is a GAN-based vocoder that directly predicts complex STFT coefficients, using a ConvNeXt backbone (depthwise convolutions with inverted bottlenecks, GELU activations, and Layer Normalization) that preserves temporal resolution throughout. The key architectural insight is an isotropic design: no transposed convolutions are used, and the waveform is reconstructed solely via the inverse STFT, eliminating aliasing-prone upsampling layers. Phase is parameterized by mapping a scalar p onto the unit circle (x=cos(p), y=sin(p)), which naturally enforces phase wrapping into (−π, π] without requiring explicit periodicity-inducing modules like Snake. Magnitude is parameterized as exp(m) to ensure positivity. Training uses a multi-period and multi-resolution discriminator with a hinge adversarial loss, L1 mel-spectrogram reconstruction loss, and feature-matching loss, achieving state-of-the-art quality at roughly 13× the speed of HiFi-GAN and 70× the speed of BigVGAN.

## Results

On LibriTTS (24 kHz, 100 mel bins), Vocos achieves the best VISQOL (4.66), PESQ (3.70), V/UV F1 (0.9582), and lowest periodicity error (0.101) among all compared vocoders, while BigVGAN marginally leads on UTMOS (3.749 vs. 3.734). In the neural audio codec setting (EnCodec tokens), Vocos outperforms EnCodec on UTMOS at every bandwidth (e.g., 3.882 vs. 3.765 at 12 kbps). Inference speed is dramatically improved: Vocos runs at 6696× real-time on GPU, approximately 13× faster than HiFi-GAN and 70× faster than BigVGAN, with an even larger gap on CPU (169× vs. 5.8× and 0.4× real-time).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HiFi-GAN, Vocos wins on every objective metric: VISQOL 4.66 vs. 4.57, PESQ 3.70 vs. 3.093, V/UV F1 0.9582 vs. 0.9457, and periodicity error 0.101 vs. 0.129, while also being ~13× faster on GPU. Against iSTFTNet, Vocos similarly dominates across all metrics (e.g., PESQ 3.70 vs. 2.942) and is ~1.6× faster. Against the strongest competitor BigVGAN, Vocos edges ahead on VISQOL (4.66 vs. 4.65), PESQ (3.70 vs. 3.693), V/UV F1 (0.9582 vs. 0.9557), and periodicity (0.101 vs. 0.108), but trails by a narrow margin on UTMOS (3.734 vs. 3.749); subjective MOS/SMOS tests show no statistically significant difference between the two. In the codec setting, Vocos consistently surpasses EnCodec on UTMOS at all tested bandwidths (e.g., 3.882 vs. 3.765 at 12 kbps; 3.210 vs. 1.527 at 1.5 kbps), with subjective MOS gaps of 0.9–1.6 points in Vocos's favor.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vocos extends the GAN-based vocoder lineage (HiFi-GAN, BigVGAN) by fundamentally relocating the upsampling operation from the time domain to the frequency domain: instead of a cascade of transposed convolutions, the generator maintains a constant temporal resolution and reconstructs the waveform via a single inverse STFT. This builds on iSTFTNet's partial step (replacing only the last two upsample blocks with ISTFT) but pushes the idea to its logical conclusion by eliminating all learnable upsampling layers. The backbone replaces dilated ResBlocks with ConvNeXt blocks, and the phase head introduces a unit-circle (cos/sin) activation that implicitly handles phase wrapping — a design choice absent from prior Fourier-domain vocoding attempts like Gritsenko et al. (2020), which failed to train stably.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~70× speedup over BigVGAN while matching state-of-the-art perceptual quality is a major practical win for deployment-constrained TTS and audio codec pipelines, and the open-sourcing of code and weights lowers the barrier to adoption. The ablation showing that Snake activation (designed to inject periodicity into time-domain vocoders) actually degrades Vocos provides clean conceptual validation that the Fourier basis already encodes the relevant periodic structure. An open question raised by the MDCT experiments is whether the redundancy inherent in the STFT representation is genuinely beneficial for GAN training stability, or whether better parameterizations of compact transforms could close the gap. The paper does not explore multi-speaker or zero-shot generalization, leaving those as natural next steps.
