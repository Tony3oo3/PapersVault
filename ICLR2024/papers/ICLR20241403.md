## Problem

On-policy policy gradient methods are robust to model misspecification but cannot reuse historical data, while offline RL methods leverage pre-collected data yet require strong assumptions (e.g., Bellman completeness) and are unstable in practice. Prior hybrid RL approaches that mix offline and online data still rely entirely on off-policy learning, inheriting the same fragility to offline-specific assumptions. This paper asks whether one can design a hybrid RL algorithm that achieves offline RL guarantees when offline assumptions hold, while retaining on-policy NPG guarantees regardless of their validity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an offline dataset of (s, a, s', r) tuples from a suboptimal behavior policy alongside online on-policy samples (s, a, y) where y is a Monte-Carlo reward-to-go estimate under the current policy. The reset distribution is over the joint state-action space S×A (standard in NPG/CPI literature), and observations can be high-dimensional vectors or CLIP ViT-B/32-encoded features from 32×32 CIFAR-100 images, with a train/test split to stress generalization. The output is a parameterized neural-network policy π_θ mapping states to action distributions, with the final policy drawn uniformly at random from all iterates.

## Method

The core algorithm (HAC/HNPG) integrates Fitted Policy Evaluation into the Natural Policy Gradient framework via a subroutine (HPE) that solves a combined square-loss regression: an off-policy TD loss on offline data (bootstrapping from the previous iterate) plus an on-policy least-squares regression loss on online Monte-Carlo samples that involves no bootstrapping. This on-policy term is the key design choice — it anchors the value estimate to the true Q^π under the current policy's occupancy measure, so even when Bellman completeness fails and the off-policy TD component diverges, the value function remains a valid estimator for the NPG update. For parameterized policies (HNPG), the linear critic is fitted under both online and offline data, and the policy update direction is the solution to a weighted least-squares problem whose Gram matrix is the Fisher information matrix computed from both data sources. The theoretical guarantee is doubly robust: under approximate Bellman completeness, cumulative suboptimality scales with min{C_npg, C_off²}, matching state-of-the-art offline RL rates; without it, the bound degrades gracefully to the standard on-policy NPG rate depending only on C_npg.

## Results

On the Continuous Comblock benchmark, HNPG reliably solves up to horizon 50 using ~50k offline samples and ~30m online samples, while TRPO fails even at horizon 5 and RLPD only reaches horizon 15. On the more challenging Image-Based Continuous Comblock (using CIFAR-100 images via CLIP ViT-B/32 features), HNPG solves up to horizon 30 with ~10m online samples, whereas RLPD completely fails at horizon 10 and TRPO fails at horizon 5. In the image-based setting, HNPG achieves a test reward of ~0.8 versus ~0.6 for RLPD, demonstrating better generalization to unseen test images. The underlying CIFAR-100 classification accuracy of the function class is 77.7% (train) and 72.1% (test), confirming that Bellman completeness does not hold in this regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares HNPG against two baselines: TRPO (pure on-policy) and RLPD (hybrid off-policy using SAC). TRPO fails on both benchmarks due to the extreme exploration difficulty of the combination lock environment (uniform exploration has only exp(-H) probability of success), making it unable to solve even horizon 5. RLPD, which reuses past online data via off-policy SAC, improves sample efficiency over TRPO but is limited to horizon 15 on Continuous Comblock and fails entirely at horizon 10 on the image-based variant. The paper attributes RLPD's failure to unstable online critic loss from TD bootstrapping (which can diverge without Bellman completeness) and overfitting due to SAC's high updates-to-data ratio (1:1 to 10:1). HNPG wins decisively on both benchmarks: it solves the maximum horizon on each setting and achieves a larger test-reward margin (~0.8 vs ~0.6) on the image-based task, confirming that the on-policy supervised-regression component provides robustness when off-policy assumptions are violated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates Fitted Policy Evaluation (Antos et al., 2007), an off-policy bootstrapping procedure, into the Natural Policy Gradient framework (Kakade, 2001), creating a hybrid actor-critic algorithm that simultaneously performs on-policy supervised regression on online data and off-policy TD learning on offline data. It departs fundamentally from prior hybrid RL methods (HyQ, RLPD, CAL-QL), which all rely exclusively on off-policy Bellman-backup learning over mixed offline+online data. The key conceptual departure is that the on-policy component uses Monte-Carlo reward-to-go targets (no bootstrap), yielding a doubly-robust guarantee: offline RL-style bounds when Bellman completeness holds, and standard on-policy NPG bounds otherwise. The practical implementation follows TRPO-style conjugate gradient updates with GAE, and the Bellman error transfer coefficient from Song et al. (2023) is used as the offline coverage measure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The doubly-robust theoretical guarantee is the paper's central contribution: practitioners get offline RL sample-efficiency benefits when the function class is well-specified, but are protected from the known instability of TD-based methods when Bellman completeness fails. The CIFAR-100 image-based combination lock experiment provides a concrete, realistic demonstration of this failure mode, showing RLPD/SAC diverging while HNPG remains stable. However, the experimental validation is limited to small-scale combination lock environments (horizons 5–50), and the theoretical bounds involve loose constants and polynomial dependencies on 1/(1−γ) that may be pessimistic in practice. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional continuous control or language model fine-tuning, and whether the λ weighting between on-policy and off-policy losses can be adapted automatically rather than tuned manually.
