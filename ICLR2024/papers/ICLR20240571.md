## Problem

Pre-trained vision foundation models (e.g., DINOv2) produce features that attend to dynamic objects (pedestrians, vehicles) rather than the static discriminative landmarks (buildings, vegetation) critical for visual place recognition, and full fine-tuning on VPR datasets causes catastrophic forgetting of their generalization ability. Additionally, foundation models do not natively produce the dense local features required for re-ranking in two-stage VPR pipelines, and existing two-stage methods rely on time-consuming RANSAC-based geometric verification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB query image and a geo-tagged image database. It produces a 1024-dim L2-normalized global feature (via GeM pooling of the ViT patch-token feature map, deliberately not the class token) for fast candidate retrieval, and a dense 61×61 grid of 128-dim L2-normalized local features for re-ranking. Re-ranking uses mutual nearest neighbor cross-matching between query and candidate local features, with the match count serving directly as the similarity score—eliminating the need for RANSAC-based spatial verification.

## Method

SelaVPR freezes the DINOv2 ViT-L/14 backbone and adds two types of lightweight adapters: (1) a global adaptation inserting a serial bottleneck adapter after the multi-head attention layer and a parallel bottleneck adapter (scaled by 0.2) alongside the MLP in every transformer block, and (2) a local adaptation consisting of two up-convolutional layers after the backbone that upsample the 16×16×1024 feature map to 61×61×128 dense local features. Training uses a combined loss: a standard triplet loss on the GeM global feature plus a novel mutual nearest neighbor local feature loss that maximizes average cosine similarity within the mutual-match set for positive pairs and minimizes it for negative pairs, making the discrete match-count objective differentiable. Only ~53M parameters are tuned (vs. 304M for full fine-tuning), and the resulting local features are matched via mutual nearest neighbor cross-matching without geometric verification, reducing retrieval runtime to ~3% of RANSAC-based two-stage methods.

## Results

SelaVPR achieves the best R@1/R@5/R@10 across all four main benchmark datasets: 94.0/96.8/97.5 on Tokyo24/7, 90.8/96.4/97.2 on MSLS-val, 73.5/87.5/90.6 on MSLS-challenge, and 92.8/96.8/97.7 on Pitts30k-test. The method ranks 1st on the MSLS challenge leaderboard (R@5 = 0.89). In terms of efficiency, SelaVPR's total single-query runtime is 0.112 s on Pitts30k-test, less than 4% of TransVPR (3.018 s) and about 1% of SP-SuperGlue (6.681 s) or Patch-NetVLAD-p (11.144 s), while using only 53M tunable parameters (vs. 304M for full fine-tuning of DINOv2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelaVPR outperforms all nine compared baselines on every dataset for R@1. The largest margins are on Tokyo24/7, where SelaVPR (94.0 R@1) beats the previous best R²Former (88.6) by 5.4 points, and on MSLS-val where it surpasses R²Former (89.7) by 1.1 points. Among one-stage methods, even SelaVPR(global) alone (without re-ranking) already exceeds MixVPR (88.0 R@1) and CosPlace (82.8 R@1) on MSLS-val, and all two-stage methods on Tokyo24/7 for R@5/R@10. Among two-stage methods, SelaVPR beats R²Former by 0.5 R@1 on MSLS-challenge and 1.7 R@1 on Pitts30k-test, while also being dramatically faster: its 0.112 s total runtime is under 4% of TransVPR (3.018 s) and roughly 1% of SP-SuperGlue (6.681 s) and Patch-NetVLAD-p (11.144 s), since it eliminates RANSAC-based geometric verification entirely. On the additional Pitts250k benchmark, SelaVPR (95.7 R@1) also surpasses the previous best MixVPR (94.3 R@1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the adapter-based parameter-efficient transfer learning paradigm (Houlsby et al., 2019) to visual place recognition for the first time, applying lightweight serial and parallel bottleneck adapters within frozen DINOv2 transformer blocks to bridge the gap between generic pre-training and VPR-specific feature needs. It departs from prior two-stage VPR methods (Patch-NetVLAD, TransVPR, R²Former) by eliminating RANSAC-based geometric verification in re-ranking, instead training local features via a novel mutual nearest neighbor loss so that match count alone suffices as a similarity score. The global adaptation (adapters in each ViT block) and local adaptation (up-convolutional head) are combined into a hybrid scheme that simultaneously produces retrieval-ready global features and dense local features, a design not previously explored in the VPR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3% retrieval runtime relative to RANSAC-based two-stage methods makes large-scale, real-time VPR deployment substantially more feasible, and the parameter-efficient approach (53M tunable vs. 304M for full fine-tuning) reduces training cost while avoiding catastrophic forgetting of foundation-model knowledge. A notable open question is whether the elimination of geometric verification generalizes to scenarios with extreme viewpoint changes or highly repetitive structures where false matches may be more prevalent. The mutual nearest neighbor loss, which makes discrete match-count optimization tractable, could potentially transfer to other matching-based retrieval tasks beyond VPR.
