## Problem

Learning precise relative placement tasks (e.g., hanging a mug on a rack) from a small number of demonstrations requires the prediction to be both SE(3)-equivariant to object poses and highly accurate. Prior methods achieve one but not the other: TAX-Pose is empirically strong but not provably equivariant, while provably equivariant methods like Neural Descriptor Fields suffer from substantially lower precision. This paper seeks an architecture that is simultaneously provably SE(3)-equivariant and capable of sub-millimeter placement precision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241061/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-segmented 3D point clouds of two task-relevant objects (e.g., a mug and a rack), each represented as an N×3 matrix of 3D coordinates, typically extracted from multi-view RGB-D or LiDAR observations. It outputs a single SE(3) rigid transform (the cross-pose) that transports object A into its task-specific goal configuration relative to object B. Training requires only a handful of demonstrations (as few as one) showing the objects in their successful goal configuration, with no additional per-point labels.

## Method

The core insight is to factor the problem into an SE(3)-invariant representation learning step and an SE(3)-equivariant geometric reasoning step. First, per-point features are extracted via DGCNN with Vector Neuron layers (guaranteeing invariance), then augmented with task-specific cross-object information through multi-head cross-attention. A learned symmetric kernel then predicts a matrix of pairwise Euclidean distances (RelDist) between every point on A and every point on B in the goal configuration — a representation that is invariant by construction. These distances are fed into a novel differentiable multilateration layer (MUL), which computes the least-squares goal position of each point on A given its predicted distances to points on B, using a closed-form solution from Zhou (2009). Finally, a differentiable SVD-based Procrustes layer (PRO) extracts the rigid cross-pose from the initial and estimated goal point sets. Because every component is either invariant or equivariant by construction, the full pipeline is provably SE(3)-equivariant and can be trained end-to-end with standard placement losses.

## Results

On five RLBench relative placement tasks, the method achieves rotational errors below 1.25° and translational errors below 3 mm (frequently 1 mm) across all tasks, substantially outperforming TAX-Pose by 2–9× in rotation on 4 of 5 tasks (e.g., 0.624° vs 5.471° on hang_hanger, 1.209° vs 7.098° on insert_peg). On the NDF mug-hanging task, the method achieves 0.99/0.88 overall success (upright/arbitrary) at the unbounded penetration threshold, compared to 0.93/0.44 for TAX-Pose, and 0.35/0.19 at the strict <1 cm penetration threshold versus 0.15/0.14 for TAX-Pose. The method also demonstrates that a single demonstration suffices to learn highly precise placement relationships.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TAX-Pose (the closest baseline), the proposed method wins on nearly every RLBench precision metric, achieving 2–9× lower rotational error on 4 of 5 tasks and matching or beating translational error on all five. On the NDF mug-hanging task, the method outperforms TAX-Pose on all penetration-thresholded success rates (e.g., 0.35 vs 0.15 overall at <1 cm upright) and on unbounded overall success (0.99 vs 0.93 upright), while TAX-Pose retains a slight edge on grasp success in the arbitrary setting (0.50 vs 0.43). Against Neural Descriptor Fields (NDF), the method achieves substantially higher overall success on mug hanging (0.99/0.88 vs 0.88/0.58 at ∞ threshold) and provides the only method that reports meaningful precision at the <1 cm and <3 cm penetration thresholds. Against Dense Object Nets (DON), the method dominates across all NDF metrics (e.g., 0.99 vs 0.45 overall success on mug hanging at ∞). The paper notes that on the NDF Bottle and Bowl tasks, results are inconclusive due to inability to reproduce TAX-Pose's symmetry-breaking technique.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a bridge between two lines of work: provably equivariant architectures (Vector Neurons, NDF) and high-precision cross-pose estimation (TAX-Pose). The key departure from TAX-Pose is replacing learned cross-correspondences with a residual by an SE(3)-invariant pairwise distance representation (RelDist) decoded via differentiable multilateration (Zhou, 2009), making equivariance a structural guarantee rather than an emergent property of data augmentation. The factorization into an invariant encoder (VN-DGCNN + cross-attention) followed by equivariant geometric reasoning layers (MUL + PRO) is the paper's central architectural contribution, drawing on DGCNN as the point-cloud backbone and differentiable SVD for the final Procrustes step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that provable SE(3)-equivariance and high empirical precision are not in tension, resolving a trade-off that prior work (NDF vs. TAX-Pose) could not. The 2–9× rotation-precision gains over TAX-Pose on RLBench tasks, combined with the single-demonstration result, suggest that geometric inductive biases can substitute for large demonstration sets in manipulation. Key limitations remain: the method cannot handle symmetric objects or multimodal placement without external symmetry-breaking, and it assumes pre-segmented object pairs. Open questions include whether generative variants could address multimodality and whether the segmentation assumption can be relaxed.
