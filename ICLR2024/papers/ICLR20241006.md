## Problem

Existing speech self-supervised learning models uniformly process speech at a fixed 20ms frame resolution, overlooking the varying informational content present at different temporal scales. While multi-resolution modeling has shown benefits in downstream tasks, no prior work has explicitly integrated multi-resolution information during the pre-training phase itself, with earlier approaches requiring separate SSL models per resolution and incurring prohibitive pre-training costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241006/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a raw single-channel speech signal and first converts it to 20ms frame-wise features via a convolutional extractor, then processes these at two resolutions simultaneously: 20ms (high) and 40ms (low). The final output is a sequence of high-resolution (20ms) hidden representations of dimension D, produced after information has flowed through both resolution levels, making it directly compatible with standard downstream fine-tuning pipelines (CTC, linear probing, etc.) without architectural modification.

## Method

MR-HuBERT employs a three-stage hierarchical Transformer: a High Resolution Encoder (f1) processes masked 20ms frames, a downsampling module (combining deconvolutional upsampling, repeat upsampling, convolutional downsampling, and skip connections in a residual framework) reduces the sequence to 40ms for a Low Resolution Encoder (f2), and an upsampling module restores the sequence to 20ms where it is summed with the f1 output and fed into a second High Resolution Encoder (f3). Training uses HuBERT-style iterative K-means clustering for target units, with high-resolution units extracted at 20ms and low-resolution units obtained by skip-downsampling every second high-resolution unit. The total loss is a weighted sum of high-resolution and low-resolution masked unit prediction cross-entropy terms, with the low-resolution loss computed on a partial parameter set that excludes the upsampling module and f3. This architecture reduces self-attention sequence length at the low-resolution stage, yielding 9–13% MACs reduction at inference while maintaining or improving performance over single-resolution HuBERT.

## Results

On LibriSpeech ASR with 1-hour labeled data, mono-large achieves 6.37% WER on test-clean, a ~56% relative reduction over HuBERT-large (14.40%), while mono-base achieves 19.26% WER on test-clean versus 20.64% for HuBERT-base. On the SUPERB benchmark, mono-large scores 864.6 (General) versus 813.4 for HuBERT-large, and mono-base scores 708.7 versus 670.4 for HuBERT-base. On ML-SUPERB, the multilingual multi-base model achieves a composite score of 986.8 (1-hour setting), surpassing mHuBERT-base (812.7) and mHuBERT-base* (964.5). Inference efficiency improves by 9% (base: 431G→394G MACs) and 13% (large: 1116G→971G MACs) due to reduced sequence length in self-attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HuBERT (Hsu et al., 2021), MR-HuBERT consistently outperforms across all model sizes and data regimes. In the base setting, mono-base reduces WER by 1–4 points over HuBERT-base on all four LibriSpeech test sets (e.g., 24.46 vs. 28.87 on test-other with 1-hour data). In the large setting, the gains are dramatic: mono-large achieves 6.37% WER on test-clean with 1-hour data versus 14.40% for HuBERT-large (a 56% relative reduction), and maintains a small but consistent edge at 100 hours (3.01% vs. 3.14% on test-clean). On SUPERB, mono-large improves the General score from 813.4 to 864.6 and the Enhancement score from 456.0 to 609.5 over HuBERT-large. Against mHuBERT-base (Lee et al., 2022) on ML-SUPERB, the proposed multi-base model achieves a composite score of 986.8 (1-hour) versus 812.7 for mHuBERT-base, with particularly strong gains in Multilingual ASR (CER 39.6 vs. 45.6) and LID accuracy (85.1% vs. 72.5%). The only setting where MR-HuBERT does not clearly dominate is 100-hour ASR in the large model, where the gap narrows to <0.2 WER points, suggesting diminishing returns at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends HuBERT's masked unit prediction framework by introducing a hierarchical Transformer architecture that processes speech at multiple resolutions (20ms and 40ms) within a single model, rather than the fixed 20ms resolution used by HuBERT, WavLM, and other SSL speech models. It builds directly on Shi et al. (2023d), which demonstrated that combining separate SSL models trained at different resolutions improves downstream performance, but required training independent models per resolution. The key architectural departure is integrating upsampling/downsampling modules (adapted from Shi et al.'s fusion module) between high- and low-resolution Transformer encoders, creating an hourglass-like structure analogous to Hourglass networks in vision and Hourglass Transformers in NLP, while retaining HuBERT's iterative K-means clustering and masked prediction objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MR-HuBERT demonstrates that multi-resolution information can be captured during pre-training in a single model, eliminating the need to train and maintain separate SSL models per resolution as in prior work. The 9–13% inference speedup from reduced sequence length in self-attention is practically relevant for deployment. However, the ablation revealing that three resolutions (20/40/80ms) actually underperform two resolutions (20/40ms) on ASR is a notable caveat, suggesting that constrained per-resolution model capacity may limit the benefit of additional resolutions. Open questions include whether scaling model capacity per resolution could recover the three-resolution advantage, and whether the hierarchical design generalizes to more than two resolutions or to non-speech audio domains.
