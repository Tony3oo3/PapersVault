## Problem

Standard transformer self-attention has quadratic time complexity in sequence length, making long-context modeling impractical since a 10× increase in length yields a 100× increase in attention computation. While many efficient transformer variants exist (sparse, low-rank, compressive), they either approximate attention, require non-causal settings, or introduce recurrence that complicates sampling. The paper asks whether exact dense softmax-based self-attention can be computed in linear time within a standard decoder-only transformer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240240/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes token sequences for text (byte-level for Enwik8; SentencePiece tokens of vocabulary size 32,000 for PG-19) and flattened 64×64 RGB image byte sequences (~12,288 bytes) for ImageNet64, all processed autoregressively with a causal mask. Outputs are next-token probability distributions over the vocabulary, sampled via nucleus sampling to produce generated text passages or full 64×64 images. The architecture uses a gated attention unit (GAU) with key width 128 and value width 2× model width, replacing standard multi-head attention layers.

## Method

Transformer-VQ vector-quantizes the attention keys using a learned codebook of size S (typically 512) with a straight-through estimator for gradient flow, so each key is replaced by its nearest codeword. Because quantized keys can only take S distinct values, the T×T attention matrix has at most S unique columns, enabling an exact factorization into a T×S score matrix times an S×T one-hot indicator matrix. This factorization underpins a compressive cache that stores running sums (or averages) of value vectors grouped by shortcode, reducing per-query-block computation to O(L(S+2L)(D_k+D_v)) and total sequence cost to O(T(S+2L)(D_k+D_v)). The paper proves this cache is exactly equivalent to attending over the full uncompressed key-value history, and provides block-level recurrences (Theorems 3.6–3.7) for both element-wise and softmax attention nonlinearities. Training uses a combined cross-entropy plus VQ commitment loss, with codebooks updated via EMA-smoothed k-means and backpropagation through truncated windows of W timesteps.

## Results

Transformer-VQ achieves 0.99 BPB on Enwik8 (matching Transformer-XL with 33% fewer parameters), 26.6 word-level perplexity on PG-19 (near the 26.5 SOTA of Block-Recurrent Transformers), and 3.16 BPB on ImageNet64 (a new absolute state-of-the-art, improving over the previous best of 3.30). In throughput benchmarks, the optimized implementation is 3.2× faster than quadratic-time attention at sequence length 8192, 12.6× faster at 32768, and scales to 131072 tokens without out-of-memory errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On PG-19, Transformer-VQ (26.6 WLP) nearly matches the state-of-the-art Block-Recurrent Transformer (26.5 WLP) while using only self-attention without recurrence, and substantially outperforms Perceiver AR (28.9), Routing Transformer (33.2), Compressive Transformer (33.6), and MegaByte (36.4). On ImageNet64, the 1.2B Transformer-VQ sets a new absolute SOTA at 3.16 BPB, beating Efficient-VDVAE (3.30), Flow Matching (3.31), DenseFlow (3.35), VDM (3.40), Perceiver AR (3.40), and MegaByte (3.40); the 190M model also beats all prior small-model baselines at 3.22 BPB. On Enwik8, Transformer-VQ ties with Transformer-XL, Sparse Transformer, Longformer, and Routing Transformer at 0.99 BPB but trails methods with explicit forgetting or recurrence mechanisms (0.94–0.98 BPB). In throughput comparisons, Transformer-VQ with SHGA is 3.2× faster than full attention at T=8192 and 12.6× faster at T=32768, while the quadratic baseline hits out-of-memory at T=32768 for MQA/MHA configurations. The paper notes that its linear-time advantage becomes more pronounced at longer sequences, with comparable throughput maintained up to T=131072.

## Relation to Prior Work

Transformer-VQ repurposes vector quantization (originally from VQ-VAE, van den Oord et al. 2017) as the mechanism for compressing the key-value cache in a transformer, combined with the GAU gated-attention design from Hua et al. (2022). Unlike hierarchical attention methods (Combiner, Hourglass, Transformer-LS) that use heuristic pooling or convolutions to downsample, and unlike kernelizable attention (Performers, Linear Transformers) that replace softmax with a factorizable kernel, Transformer-VQ retains exact softmax semantics over quantized keys, yielding a deterministic equivalence to dense attention rather than an approximation. The compressive cache—a running per-codebook mean of value vectors—is the key architectural novelty that converts the O(T²) attention into O(T·S) while preserving the same output as an uncompressed variable-length cache.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact (not approximate) equivalence between VQ-Attention and dense softmax attention over quantized keys provides a theoretically clean path to linear-time transformers without sacrificing the softmax inductive bias, which has been a persistent concern with kernelized or linear-attention alternatives. The 12× speedup at 32k tokens and ability to scale to 131k tokens with stable throughput make long-context autoregressive modeling practically accessible on modest TPU hardware. However, the Enwik8 result (0.99 BPB) trails the best baselines (0.94), suggesting the quantization bottleneck may matter more on shorter, highly repetitive sequences; open questions include formal scaling laws for codebook size, whether the approach transfers to multi-head attention at scale, and porting to lower-level frameworks (Triton, CUDA) for broader adoption.
