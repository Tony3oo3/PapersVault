## Problem

As vision-language foundation models grow in size and the number of downstream tasks expands, full fine-tuning becomes impractical due to prohibitive compute and storage costs. Existing parameter-efficient methods (adapters, prompts, LoRA) for cross-modal modeling typically target a single modality or a single task type, and they ignore cross-modal interaction and knowledge sharing between adapters, which are essential for unified cross-modal transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240975/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For video tasks, the model uniformly samples 8 frames (224×224) during training and 16 during inference, concatenating per-frame ViT token sequences as the visual input to cross-attention layers; for image tasks, a single 384×384 or 480×480 image is used. Text inputs are BERT token sequences (captions for retrieval, questions for VQA). Outputs are retrieval rankings scored by R@K and median rank, generated answer tokens for VQA (via a shared multimodal decoder with beam search, max length 20), or caption tokens for the generation task. The frozen BLIP-base backbone (ViT + BERT + multimodal encoder) is never updated; only the inserted UniAdapter modules (4.8M–19.0M parameters, ~1–5% of the backbone) are trained.

## Method

UniAdapter inserts lightweight adapter modules into every transformer layer of the visual, textual, and multimodal encoders of a frozen BLIP backbone, but unifies them through a shared down-projection matrix W_down while retaining modality-specific up-projection matrices W_up^{V}, W_up^{T}, W_up^{C}. In the multimodal encoder, a Query-Residual adapter is added after the self-attention layer (sharing weights with the textual adapter) to preserve language query integrity that would otherwise be corrupted by the hybrid query+fusion features entering the cross-attention adapter. For the cross-modal up-projection, the textual up-projection W_up^{T} is reused in parallel with W_up^{C}, so the cross-modal branch can focus on fusion while textual information is captured separately. For video inputs, a Parameter-free Frame-aware Attention (PFA) mechanism computes per-frame weights via the dot-product between each frame's [CLS] token and the text [CLS] token, then scales all non-[CLS] tokens in that frame by the weight—highlighting salient frames and suppressing noise without any extra learnable parameters. This design reduces tunable parameters to 1–5% of the backbone while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning on retrieval, VQA, and captioning benchmarks.

## Results

On MSR-VTT video-text retrieval, UniAdapter (r=128, 4.8M tunable params, ~1.4% of the BLIP backbone) achieves 49.7% R@1, outperforming the previous parameter-efficient method CLIP-Prompt (36.7% R@1) by 13.0 points while using fewer parameters and fewer input frames. Scaling to r=512 (19.0M, ~5.6%) yields 50.6% R@1 on MSR-VTT and 53.7% R@1 on DiDeMo (16 frames), surpassing full fine-tuning methods like OmniVL (47.8%) and LAVENDER (40.7%) on MSR-VTT. On VideoQA (MSRVTT-QA), UniAdapter reaches 44.7% accuracy with 19.0M tunable parameters, matching or exceeding most full fine-tuning baselines that use 110M–308M parameters. On image-text retrieval (MSCOCO 5K), UniAdapter (r=512) achieves 80.1% R@1 with only 19.0M tunable parameters, approaching BLIP full fine-tuning (81.9% with 223M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest parameter-efficient competitor CLIP-Prompt (Ju et al., 2022), UniAdapter (r=128) wins decisively on MSR-VTT text-to-video R@1 (49.7 vs. 36.7) while using fewer tunable parameters (4.8M vs. 6.4M) and half the inference frames (8 vs. 16). Against full fine-tuning video-text retrieval methods, UniAdapter (r=512, 19.0M) surpasses OmniVL (47.8%), LAVENDER (40.7%), and SINGULARITY (41.5%) on MSR-VTT R@1, and is competitive with SINGULARITY (53.9%) on DiDeMo R@1 (53.7% with 16 frames). On VideoQA (MSRVTT-QA), UniAdapter (19.0M) achieves 44.7% accuracy, narrowly trailing LAVENDER (45.0%, 308M) and VINDLU (44.6%, 201M) but exceeding MERLOT (43.1%, 233M) and All-in-one (44.3%, 110M) with 6–16× fewer parameters. On image-text retrieval (MSCOCO 5K), UniAdapter (r=512, 19.0M) reaches 80.1% R@1, within 1.8 points of BLIP full fine-tuning (81.9%, 223M) and 5.3 points below BEIT-3 (84.8%, 1.9B). In the ablation comparison against standard Adapter (Houlsby et al., 2019), UniAdapter improves DiDeMo R@1 from 49.6 to 52.1 while reducing tunable parameters from 28.4M to 19.0M (33% fewer), and outperforms LoRA (43.3% R@1, 56.6M) by 8.8 points with 3× fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniAdapter extends the standard Adapter architecture (Houlsby et al., 2019) and the unified parameter-efficient transfer learning framework of MAM/Parallel Adapter (He et al., 2022) to the cross-modal vision-language domain. While prior adapter-based cross-modal works (VL-Adapter, ST-Adapter, CLIP-Prompt) each target a single modality or a single task type, UniAdapter unifies adapters across visual, textual, and cross-modal encoders through a shared down-projection layer with modality-specific up-projections, and introduces a query-residual mechanism tailored to the cross-attention structure of hybrid-stream models like BLIP. The parameter-free frame-aware attention for video tasks is inspired by LGDN but repurposed as a zero-cost reweighting of frame tokens during cross-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As vision-language foundation models grow to billions of parameters, the ability to adapt them to diverse downstream tasks (retrieval, VQA, captioning) with only 1–2% tunable parameters has significant practical value for storage and compute-constrained settings. The paper demonstrates that a single unified adapter design can handle both image and video inputs and both retrieval and reasoning tasks, reducing the need for task-specific or modality-specific adapter configurations. Limitations include the focus on BLIP-base as the primary backbone (larger backbones are only briefly explored), the restriction to retrieval and VQA/caption tasks without cross-modal generation, and the reliance on a specific hybrid-stream architecture. Open questions include whether the knowledge-sharing design generalizes to decoder-only or fully single-stream architectures, and whether the middle-layer preference for cross-modal fusion (layers 5–8) holds at greater model scale.
