## Problem

It is known that offline RL (K=1 batch) cannot solve policy evaluation or best-policy identification sample-efficiently under linear function approximation, while fully adaptive RL (K=n) can. The open question is whether any adaptivity (K>1) suffices, or whether the sample-efficiency boundary lies at a dimension-dependent level of adaptivity. This paper resolves the question by showing that a constant number of batches is insufficient: K=Ω(log log d) batches are necessary for sample-efficient algorithms.

## Input / Output

The learner interacts with an unknown MDP from a known class over K batches, submitting state-action queries and receiving exact feedback: the reward value and the full transition function (equivalent to infinite samples, removing statistical uncertainty). For policy evaluation, the learner additionally receives the target policy's action at all successor states. The output is either an ε-accurate estimate of the target policy's action-value at a fixed starting state (PE) or a near-optimal policy (BPI). Queries can be specified either explicitly (policy-free) or indirectly via trajectories induced by chosen policies (policy-induced), and the total number of queries across all batches must be polynomial in the feature dimension d for sample-efficiency.

## Method

The proof constructs a class of infinite-horizon discounted MDPs with a d-dimensional linear feature map where the environment can adversarially 'erase' information along m-dimensional subspaces of the feature space via bootstrapping: the Bellman equation's coefficient matrix X = Φ − γΦ⁺ has a null-space of dimension d^(1/4^k) after k rounds of polynomial-size queries. The key technical tool is a subspace packing bound in Grassmannian space with chordal distance (from Soleymani & Mahdavifar, 2021), which guarantees that for any set of n = poly(d) query directions, there exists a subspace of dimension d^(1/4) whose γ-hyperspherical sector contains none of them. The MDP construction uses nested subspaces B_K ⊂ … ⊂ B_1 ⊂ ℝ^d and a target policy defined via orthogonal projections onto these subspaces, with rewards concentrated in γ-hyperspherical caps around a hidden direction w. After each batch, the learner observes which subspace was 'erased' and can focus subsequent queries there, but the dimension only shrinks by a factor of 4 per round, requiring K = Ω(log log d) rounds before the null-space vanishes. This contrasts with Zanette (2021), where only a 1-dimensional subspace is erased, making K=2 sufficient.

## Results

The paper establishes that any sample-efficient learner (total queries n = poly(d)) solving Policy Evaluation or Best Policy Identification under d-dimensional linear function approximation in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs requires K = Ω(log log d) batches. Specifically, Theorem 4.4 shows this lower bound for PE under policy-induced (equivalently policy-free) queries, and Theorem 4.5 extends it to both PE and BPI under policy-free queries with the stronger all-policy realizability assumption (Assumption 4.2). The key implication is that the boundary of sample-efficiency does not lie between offline RL (K=1) and any adaptive setting (K>1), but rather within a dimension-dependent regime: Ω(log log d) ≤ K ≤ n. In the fully-adaptive setting (K=n), a matching upper bound of d queries is shown for PE under an additional feature-space coverage assumption (Theorem B.2), with a matching lower bound of d queries (Theorem B.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The key comparison is to Zanette (2021), who proved exponential lower bounds for offline RL (K=1) under linear function approximation, showing sample-efficiency is impossible in the single-batch setting. However, the MDP constructions in Zanette (2021) are solvable with just K=2 batches and d+1 queries, suggesting the boundary might be between K=1 and K>1. This paper's contribution is to show that this intuition is wrong: the boundary is actually at K = Ω(log log d), meaning even moderate adaptivity (constant K) is insufficient for sample-efficiency. On the other end of the spectrum, Lattimore et al. (2020) showed sample-efficient algorithms exist for the fully-adaptive setting (K=n) under all-policy realizability, and this paper provides a tighter d-query upper bound for PE in that regime (Theorem B.2), independent of the effective horizon 1/(1−γ) and accuracy ε. The paper also contrasts with deployment-efficiency results for finite-horizon linear MDPs (Huang et al., 2022; Qiao & Wang, 2023), where a dimension-independent number of deployments suffices, highlighting that infinite-horizon discounted MDPs under more general linear representations are fundamentally harder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Zanette (2021)'s information-theoretic lower bounds for offline RL (K=1) to the multi-batch setting, where data is collected in K batches with feedback processed between them. The key technical departure is replacing Zanette's 1-dimensional information erasure (which is solvable with K=2) with m-dimensional subspace erasure, enabled by subspace packing results from Soleymani & Mahdavifar (2021) in Grassmannian space with chordal distance. The multi-batch learning framework itself is adapted from Perchet et al. (2015) for bandits and prior RL work on finite-horizon or linear MDPs, but the paper is the first to characterize the adaptivity-sample-efficiency boundary for infinite-horizon discounted MDPs under general linear function approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that K=Ω(log log d) batches are necessary for sample-efficiency fundamentally reframes the offline-to-online spectrum: having any adaptivity (K>1) does not guarantee polynomial query complexity, and the boundary is dimension-dependent rather than a simple offline/adaptive dichotomy. This has direct practical implications for batched data collection in clinical trials, marketing, and safety-critical deployment where the number of feedback rounds is costly. A major open question is whether log log d is tight — the authors note that improving to log d would require new subspace packing results not currently available in the literature, and no matching upper-bound algorithm is known for K=O(log log d).
