## Problem

DICE-based offline RL methods impose state-action-level behavior constraint, which is theoretically ideal for handling both OOD states and actions, yet they consistently underperform methods that enforce only action-level constraint. The paper identifies the root cause: in the true-gradient update of the V-DICE objective, the backward gradient (on the next state) can conflict with and cancel out the forward gradient (on the current state), leading to catastrophic unlearning of the action-level constraint that the forward gradient provides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241155/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of transitions (s, a, r, s') for offline RL or (s, a, s') for offline IL, where the dataset may be heterogeneous and suboptimal. It produces a parameterized value function V(s) and a policy π(a|s) extracted via weighted behavior cloning using the optimal distribution correction weights ω*(s,a). A key implementation detail is the bidirectional target network trick: the forward gradient uses a target network on V(s') while the backward gradient uses a target network on V(s), decoupling the two gradient computations.

## Method

The core contribution is the orthogonal-gradient update rule for the V-DICE value learning objective. The Bellman residual gradient decomposes into a forward gradient (on s, imposing action-level constraint analogous to semi-gradient/EQL) and a backward gradient (on s', carrying state-level information). Instead of adding the backward gradient directly (true-gradient, which causes interference) or discarding it (semi-gradient, which loses state-level constraint), the method projects the backward gradient onto the normal plane of the forward gradient, making the two terms orthogonal so they cannot cancel each other. A hyperparameter η scales the projected backward gradient to balance between semi-gradient and true-gradient behavior. Theoretically, this update preserves the action-level constraint (Theorem 1: interference-free), guarantees monotonic loss decrease (Theorem 2), alleviates feature co-adaptation between consecutive states (Theorem 3), and thereby improves robustness to OOD states (Theorem 4). The resulting O-DICE algorithm alternates V and π updates, requires no double Q-learning trick, and achieves SOTA performance on D4RL benchmarks and offline IL tasks.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (15 MuJoCo and AntMaze tasks), O-DICE achieves the highest normalized score in 13 out of 15 tasks, outperforming all action-constrained baselines (TD3+BC, CQL, IQL, EQL) and all prior DICE-based methods. Notable margins include hopper-medium (86.1 vs. EQL's 74.6), antmaze-medium-play (86.0 vs. EQL's 77.5), and hopper-medium-replay (99.9 vs. EQL's 98.1). On offline imitation learning with a single expert trajectory, O-DICE consistently outperforms BC, ValueDICE, and IQLearn, e.g., achieving 78.8 on Hopper-expert Traj 1 (vs. IQLearn 75.1, BC 56.0) and 75.6 on Walker2d-expert Traj 1 (vs. IQLearn 50.2, BC 64.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest action-constrained baseline EQL, O-DICE wins on 13/15 D4RL tasks with the largest gaps on hopper-medium (+11.5), antmaze-medium-play (+8.5), antmaze-large-play (+10.3), and antmaze-medium-diverse (+12.7), confirming that state-action-level constraint (via orthogonal-gradient) surpasses action-level-only constraint. Against OptiDICE (true-gradient DICE), O-DICE shows a dramatic advantage, e.g., 86.0 vs. 0.0 on antmaze-medium-play and 99.9 vs. 20.0 on hopper-medium-replay, demonstrating that the unprojected backward gradient is actively harmful. Against S-DICE (semi-gradient DICE, the paper's own ablation), O-DICE consistently improves, e.g., 86.1 vs. 61.5 on hopper-medium and 83.6 vs. 37.3 on walker2d-medium-replay, showing the projected backward gradient adds meaningful state-level regularization. Against f-DVL, O-DICE wins on most tasks (e.g., 86.1 vs. 63.0 on hopper-medium, 86.0 vs. 56.7 on antmaze-medium-play). On offline IL with a single expert trajectory, O-DICE outperforms IQLearn (semi-gradient Q-DICE) on 7/8 trajectories, ValueDICE (true-gradient Q-DICE) on all 8, and BC on 6/8, with the largest margin on Walker2d-expert Traj 1 (75.6 vs. 50.2 for IQLearn). O-DICE also exhibits lower evaluation variance (smaller worst-episode percent difference) and lower feature dot product ∇V(s)ᵀ∇V(s′), empirically validating the theoretical robustness claims.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DICE (Distribution Correction Estimation) framework, specifically V-DICE (Lee et al., 2021) and the semi-gradient reformulation insight from Sikchi et al. (2023). It departs from both true-gradient DICE (OptiDICE) and semi-gradient DICE (EQL, f-DVL) by identifying that the backward gradient in the Bellman residual interferes destructively with the forward gradient, and proposing an orthogonal-gradient update that projects the backward gradient onto the normal plane of the forward gradient. This positions the work as a new update rule for the DICE family, bridging the gap between the theoretically principled state-action-level constraint and the empirically successful action-level constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing puzzle in offline RL: why DICE methods, derived from the more ideal state-action-level behavior constraint, consistently underperform simpler action-level constraint methods. The orthogonal-gradient fix is simple to implement (a few lines of code) and achieves SOTA on D4RL benchmarks while improving robustness to OOD states, which is critical for offline IL. Open questions include whether the approach extends to model-based or hybrid offline-online settings, whether the η hyperparameter requires task-specific tuning in more complex environments, and whether the feature co-adaptation analysis generalizes beyond the bounded-norm and curvature assumptions in the theorems.
