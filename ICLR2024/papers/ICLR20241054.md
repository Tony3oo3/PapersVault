## Problem

In causal inference, cross-validation cannot be used for model selection because both potential outcomes are never observed for the same individual. While numerous surrogate metrics have been proposed to select among CATE estimators using only observational data, prior benchmarking studies relied on few synthetic datasets, small hyperparameter grids for nuisance models, and often excluded competing baselines, leaving the relative effectiveness of these metrics poorly understood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241054/cropped_page19_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes observational data (X, W, Y) split into training and validation sets, a population of 415 trained CATE estimators (7 meta-learner types with 103 hyperparameter variants each for direct learners), and uses AutoML (FLAML, 30-min budget) to tune the nuisance models of each surrogate metric on the validation set. It outputs a selected CATE estimator or a softmax-weighted ensemble of estimators, scored by Normalized-PEHE computed against ground-truth counterfactuals obtained via RealCause generative modeling for realistic datasets (LaLonde CPS/PSID, TWINS) and known counterfactuals for ACIC 2016 synthetic data.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-component evaluation framework: (1) AutoML-tuned nuisance models for all 34 surrogate metrics, eliminating the bias from small manual grids used in prior work; (2) a two-level model selection strategy that first selects the best estimator within each meta-learner class using a type-specific metric (e.g., DR T Score for DR-Learners, R Score for DML-Learners), then selects across the reduced candidate set using a general surrogate metric; and (3) causal ensembling that returns a softmax-weighted combination of CATE estimators rather than a single top pick, reducing sensitivity to outliers. The study also introduces novel metrics adapted from policy learning (DR Switch, DR CAB), targeted maximum likelihood estimation (TMLE Score), calibration (Cal DR Score), and uplift modeling (Qini DR Score). Across 78 datasets and 20 random seeds, doubly robust and TMLE-based variants emerge as globally dominating metrics, while plug-in T-Learner metrics remain competitive when nuisance models are properly tuned via AutoML.

## Results

Across 78 datasets (75 ACIC 2016 + 3 realistic) with 415 CATE estimators each, the DR T Score and TMLE T Score achieve the lowest Normalized PEHE (0.56 ± 0.02 and 0.64 ± 0.03 on ACIC 2016, respectively), making them globally dominating metrics. The plug-in T Score matches DR T Score on ACIC 2016 (0.56 ± 0.02) and is never dominated by any other metric across all datasets, highlighting the critical role of AutoML-tuned nuisance models. The two-level model selection strategy improves over single-level in ~28.7% of cases with no degradation, and causal ensembling provides further gains in ~5.8% of experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The DR T Score (Saito & Yasui, 2020) and the plug-in T Score (Alaa & Van Der Schaar, 2019) are the strongest performers, achieving 0.56 ± 0.02 Normalized PEHE on ACIC 2016 and 0.16 on LaLonde CPS, and neither is dominated by any other metric across all datasets. The R Score (Nie & Wager, 2021), previously identified as best by Schuler et al. (2018), performs poorly at 4.0 ± 0.11 on ACIC 2016, roughly 7× worse than DR T Score. The Value Score (Zhao et al., 2017) and Value DR Score (Athey & Wager, 2017) are the worst baselines, with the Value Score reaching 1.05e+7 on ACIC 2016 due to extreme variance. The Match Score (Rolling & Yang, 2013) is moderate at 3.60 on ACIC but competitive on LaLonde CPS (0.23). The S-Learner variants of all metrics consistently underperform their T-Learner counterparts (e.g., S Score 0.95 vs. T Score 0.56 on ACIC), and the Influence Score (Alaa & Van Der Schaar, 2019) is highly unstable (1455.75 on ACIC). With the two-level strategy, the Qini DR Score (adapted from Surry & Radcliffe, 2011) improves dramatically from 2.08e+7 to 0.24 on TWINS, becoming a dominating metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of surrogate-metric benchmarking for CATE model selection initiated by Schuler et al. (2018) and Curth & van der Schaar (2023), but departs from them in three key ways: (1) it uses AutoML (FLAML) to tune the nuisance models of each surrogate metric rather than small manual grids, (2) it introduces a two-level selection strategy and causal ensembling inspired by super-learning, and (3) it incorporates realistic datasets via RealCause generative modeling. The novel metrics (DR Switch, DR CAB, TMLE Score, Cal DR, Qini DR) repurpose ideas from policy learning (Su et al., 2019; Thomas & Brunskill, 2016), targeted maximum likelihood estimation, calibration (Dwivedi et al., 2020), and uplift modeling (Surry & Radcliffe, 2011) into the CATE model-selection context for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that plug-in metrics like T-Score are never dominated when nuisance models are properly tuned via AutoML challenges the prior narrative (Curth & van der Schaar, 2023) that pseudo-outcome metrics are inherently superior, suggesting that much of the apparent gap was an artifact of insufficient hyperparameter search. The two-level strategy and causal ensembling offer practical, drop-in improvements for any surrogate metric. Open questions include whether a truly contextual (dataset-adaptive) metric selection rule can outperform the globally dominant DR/TMLE variants, and whether these findings extend to deep-learning-based CATE estimators and high-dimensional or non-tabular settings.
