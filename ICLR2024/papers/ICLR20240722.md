## Problem

LoRA, the most popular parameter-efficient fine-tuning method, becomes unstable when applied in privacy-preserving federated learning due to three discordances: (1) jointly optimizing the two low-rank matrices locally while aggregating them separately at the server introduces mismatched cross-terms, (2) the semi-quadratic structure of LoRA amplifies DP-SGD noise quadratically, and (3) final performance is highly sensitive to the scaling factor α. No prior work had identified or addressed these specific incompatibilities between LoRA's optimization dynamics and the FL+DP setting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained models (RoBERTa-Large 355M, LLaMA-7B, ViT) with task-specific data partitioned across 3 federated clients under heterogeneous splits (e.g., [0.1, 0.9] label distributions). Only the zero-initialized B matrix (half of LoRA's trainable parameters) is updated locally and communicated to the server, halving the per-round communication payload. For classification tasks the output is a class label; for GSM-8K it is a generated reasoning chain ending in a numeric answer; for Food-101 it is a food category.

## Method

FFA-LoRA freezes the randomly Gaussian-initialized matrix A₀ after initialization and trains only the zero-initialized matrix B, so the weight update becomes W = W₀ + B·A₀ (linear in B rather than the semi-quadratic B·A of vanilla LoRA). This single change makes FedAvg aggregation exact (no cross-term error), prevents DP-SGD noise from being amplified through a product of two noisy matrices, and renders the scaling factor α irrelevant (Theorem 1 proves FFA-LoRA equals LoRA in the limit α→∞). A smoothness theorem further shows the loss is Lipschitz-smooth in B with factor LC², whereas LoRA's joint (A,B) parameterization admits no such guarantee. The result is a method with half the trainable parameters, half the communication cost, and consistent performance across privacy budgets ε∈{6,3,1} and data heterogeneity levels.

## Results

On GLUE language understanding tasks (RoBERTa-Large, 3-client FL, r=8), FFA-LoRA consistently outperforms LoRA across all privacy budgets. The most dramatic gap appears on MNLI (three-class, severe heterogeneity): at ε=6, FFA-LoRA achieves 78.81% accuracy versus LoRA's 39.46%, and at ε=1, 75.05% versus 33.80%. On SST-2 the gap is smaller (e.g., 93.73% vs 93.70% at ε=6), while QQP and QNLI show consistent 1–3% advantages for FFA-LoRA under privacy. On GSM-8K with LLaMA-7B, FFA-LoRA reaches 17.12% accuracy versus LoRA's 15.68%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is vanilla LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), evaluated under identical FL and DP-SGD settings. FFA-LoRA wins on every task and every privacy level tested. The advantage is most pronounced on MNLI, where the three-class structure combined with severe label heterogeneity causes LoRA to collapse to near-chance accuracy (33–39%) under DP, while FFA-LoRA retains 75–79% accuracy. On binary tasks (SST-2, QQP, QNLI), the gap narrows to 1–3% under privacy but FFA-LoRA still leads consistently. In the non-private setting, FFA-LoRA also edges out LoRA (e.g., 85.05% vs 82.03% on MNLI) while exhibiting far lower variance (±1.1 vs ±10.7). On the LLaMA-7B GSM-8K generation task, FFA-LoRA achieves 17.12% versus LoRA's 15.68%, and on Food-101 (ViT) the two methods perform comparably in centralized settings but FFA-LoRA converges faster with less fluctuation in federated settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) by identifying three specific discordances between LoRA's joint two-matrix optimization and the federated learning + differential privacy setting: aggregation mismatch under FedAvg, noise amplification from DP-SGD's Gaussian perturbation through LoRA's semi-quadratic structure, and sensitivity to the scaling factor α. It builds on the federated PEFT line of work (FS-LLM, SLORA, FedBERT) and the DP-SGD literature, but departs from prior work by arguing that the standard LoRA formulation is fundamentally incompatible with separate aggregation of the two low-rank matrices. The fix—freezing the randomly initialized matrix A and training only the zero-initialized B—echoes the intrinsic dimensionality observations of Li et al. (2018) and Aghajanyan et al. (2020), but is motivated here specifically by FL aggregation and DP noise properties rather than generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is immediate for anyone federated-fine-tuning LLMs under privacy constraints: FFA-LoRA halves communication and computation costs while consistently outperforming LoRA, with the gap being most dramatic under strong heterogeneity and tight privacy budgets (e.g., MNLI: 79% vs 39% at ε=6). The theoretical diagnosis—that LoRA's product structure turns linear DP noise into a non-Gaussian quadratic term, and that FedAvg introduces an irreducible aggregation error absent in full fine-tuning—provides a clear design principle for future PEFT methods in FL. Open questions include whether the QVP adapter variant (freezing two matrices, training only a small r×r center) can push parameter budgets even lower for on-device settings, whether orthogonal or SVD-based initialization of the frozen matrix further improves performance, and how these findings extend to larger models and more clients.
