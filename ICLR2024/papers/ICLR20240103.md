## Problem

It remains unclear whether large language models learn coherent, grounded representations of the real world or merely memorize superficial statistical correlations. This paper tackles the question as literally as possible by attempting to extract an actual map of the world from LLM internal activations, asking whether spatial and temporal coordinates are linearly encoded, unified across entity types, and actually used by the model.

## Input / Output

The method takes entity names (cities, landmarks, historical figures, song/movie/book titles, news headlines) as text prompts to Llama-2 and Pythia models, extracting the residual stream hidden state at the last entity token for every layer. Probes are trained to predict continuous targets: 2D latitude/longitude for spatial datasets or a numeric timestamp for temporal datasets, spanning scales from individual NYC streets to the full globe and from 1000 BC to 2020 AD. Beyond probe predictions, the method identifies individual neurons whose weights align with the learned probe direction and performs causal interventions on them.

## Method

The paper constructs six datasets of entities with ground-truth spatial or temporal coordinates, then trains linear ridge regression probes on per-layer hidden state activations to recover those coordinates. Linearity is validated by showing that nonlinear MLP probes (256 hidden units) yield negligible R² improvement over linear probes. Robustness is tested through prompting variations (explicit questions, disambiguation hints, random distractors, capitalization), block holdout generalization (withholding entire countries, states, or decades from probe training), and cross-entity-type generalization. Dimensionality reduction via PCA reveals that spatial/temporal information concentrates in the top dozen principal components, with Spearman correlation rising much faster than R², suggesting the model uses its own coordinate system that the probe must translate. Finally, the paper identifies individual 'space neurons' and 'time neurons' by finding neurons with high cosine similarity to the probe direction, then confirms their causal role through ablation (zeroing activations) and intervention (pinning to specific values) experiments that measurably alter the model's next-token predictions about locations and dates.

## Results

Linear ridge regression probes on Llama-2-70B hidden states recover spatial and temporal coordinates with high fidelity: R² = 0.911 for world places, 0.864 for US places, 0.835 for historical figures, 0.885 for entertainment, and 0.746 for news headlines (at 60% layer depth). Nonlinear MLP probes (256 ReLU neurons) yield at most ~0.015 R² improvement over linear probes across all datasets, confirming that space and time are linearly represented. Performance scales with model size (Llama-2-7B → 70B) and with pre-training data volume (Llama 2T tokens substantially outperforms Pythia 300B tokens). The NYC dataset is the weakest (R² = 0.359 for 70B) due to entity obscurity, but shows the largest relative gain from scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability study without external method baselines; the key internal comparisons are (1) linear vs. nonlinear probes and (2) model scale. Linear probes match or slightly exceed MLP probes on 5 of 6 datasets for Llama-2-70B (e.g., World: 0.911 vs. 0.926; Headlines: 0.746 vs. 0.739), with the MLP only winning marginally on World and USA, confirming the linear representation hypothesis for continuous spatial/temporal features. Scaling from 7B to 70B parameters consistently improves R² by 3–18 points across datasets (e.g., NYC: 0.219 → 0.359; Headlines: 0.564 → 0.746), and Llama-2 (2T tokens) substantially outperforms Pythia (300B tokens) at comparable parameter counts, implicating pre-training data volume as a key factor. Block-holdout generalization (Table 3) shows probes retain meaningful signal when an entire country/state/decade is excluded from training (e.g., World proximity error rises from 0.071 to 0.164 for 70B, still far below the 0.5 random baseline), and cross-entity generalization (Table 4) confirms unified representations across entity types except for the entertainment dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear probing methodology (Alain & Bengio, 2016; Belinkov, 2022) and the linear representation hypothesis (Elhage et al., 2022b) from their typical domain of binary or categorical features to continuous spatial and temporal coordinates. It builds directly on prior work showing world representations in small synthetic sequence models (Li et al., 2022 for Othello; Nanda et al., 2023 for linearity) and on earlier, much weaker attempts to recover geographic information from word embeddings and small LMs (Liétard et al., 2021, who studied only ~345M-parameter models and a few hundred cities). The paper also connects to the factual recall literature (Meng et al., 2022a; Geva et al., 2023), which established that early-to-mid MLP layers encode subject information on the last token, providing the mechanistic context for where spatial/temporal features should appear.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic, multi-scale evidence that 70B-parameter LLMs learn coherent, linear, and unified representations of both space and time across diverse entity types, strengthening the case that LLMs build world models rather than merely memorizing surface statistics. Key open questions remain: the true internal coordinate system of the model (conjectured to be a discretized hierarchical mesh) is not yet extracted, the learning dynamics of how these representations emerge during training are unexplored, and it is unclear whether these spatiotemporal features are causally integrated into a broader predictive world model. The finding that fine-grained spatial understanding (NYC-scale) remains weak even in the largest model suggests a clear scaling frontier for future work.
