## Problem

Prompt Tuning (PT) appends trainable soft prompt tokens to model inputs, but this extends the input sequence length, which—due to the Transformer's quadratic attention complexity—substantially increases both training and inference time and memory. While Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning (PETL) variants address PT's slow convergence, they leave the computational overhead from longer sequences largely unaddressed, a critical bottleneck for LLMs queried millions of times daily.

## Input / Output

For NLP tasks, the input is a text sequence (max length 256, 348 for MultiRC) whose word embeddings are updated in-place via a low-rank product before being concatenated with a shorter prepended soft prompt; for vision-language tasks, CLIP visual features are projected and concatenated with T5 text embeddings. Outputs are standard task predictions: classification scores (GLUE/SuperGLUE), answer spans (MRQA), or generated captions (MSCOCO). The total number of trainable parameters is held constant with vanilla PT (e.g., 76.8k for T5-BASE with l=100), but the effective input sequence shrinks from l+s to m+s tokens.

## Method

DEPT decomposes the vanilla PT soft prompt P ∈ R^{l×d} into a shorter prompt P_s ∈ R^{m×d} and a pair of low-rank matrices A ∈ R^{s×r}, B ∈ R^{r×d} (r ≪ min(s,d)), whose product BA is added element-wise to the frozen word embedding matrix W_i. The constraint l×d = m×d + (s+d)×r ensures the trainable parameter count matches vanilla PT exactly, while the shorter sequence (m+s < l+s) reduces quadratic attention cost. Training uses two distinct learning rates: a large rate α₁ for P_s and a small rate α₂ for A and B (initialized Gaussian and zero respectively, following LoRA), which the paper shows is essential—using a single rate drops GLUE average from 85.7 to 40.8–54.7. This design permits representation updates within the frozen embedding space, increasing input adaptability beyond what a purely prepended prompt can achieve.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (T5-BASE), DEPT achieves a mean score of 85.9 with only 76.8k trainable parameters, surpassing full fine-tuning (84.9, 220M params) and LoRA (85.3, 3.8M params). On SuperGLUE, DEPT reaches 76.5, outperforming full fine-tuning (73.9) and matching LoRA (76.5) while using ~50× fewer parameters. On MRQA 2019, DEPT improves the vanilla PT average by +3.6% (73.0 vs. 69.4) and outperforms MPT (72.2) and ATTEMPT (71.4). On MSCOCO caption generation, DEPT achieves a CIDEr score of 113.7, the best among all PEFT methods, with only 0.74% updated parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEPT (76.8k params) outperforms full fine-tuning (220M params) on both GLUE (85.9 vs. 84.9) and SuperGLUE (76.5 vs. 73.9), a notable result given the 2,864× parameter reduction. Against LoRA (3.8M params), DEPT wins on GLUE (85.9 vs. 85.3) and ties on SuperGLUE (76.5 vs. 76.5), while using ~50× fewer parameters. DEPT substantially outperforms vanilla PT on SuperGLUE (76.5 vs. 60.0) and all PT variants with transfer learning: SPoT (62.9), ATTEMPT (70.5), and MPT (74.1). On MRQA 2019, DEPT (73.0) beats PT (69.4), MPT (72.2), and ATTEMPT (71.4) but still trails full fine-tuning (74.7). On VQA, DEPT (59.8) underperforms full fine-tuning (67.1) and Adapters (67.1), suggesting that for some vision-language tasks, more trainable parameters remain beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEPT directly extends Prompt Tuning (Lester et al., 2021) by decomposing its long soft prompt into a shorter prompt and a pair of low-rank matrices that update frozen word embeddings element-wise, borrowing the low-rank factorization idea from LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) but applying it to the embedding layer rather than internal weight matrices. It positions itself within the broader PEFT landscape (Adapters, BitFit, LoRA, LST) while specifically targeting PT's unique efficiency bottleneck—input sequence length inflation—and remains orthogonal to and combinable with other PEFT methods. The dual-learning-rate training scheme (high rate for the short prompt, low rate for the low-rank pair) is a novel training detail not present in either PT or LoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEPT addresses a practical bottleneck that becomes increasingly critical as LLMs are deployed with millions of daily queries: PT's quadratic-complexity penalty from longer sequences. The finding that efficiency gains amplify with model size (6.5% speedup at T5-SMALL → 37% at T5-3B) makes this particularly relevant for the LLM era. Key limitations include the extra hyperparameter search burden (dual learning rates), dependence on maximum sequence length s for parameter budgeting, and a persistent gap to full fine-tuning on some tasks (e.g., VQA, MRQA). Open questions include combining DEPT with LoRA/Adapters to close remaining performance gaps and evaluating behavior when s is extremely large.
