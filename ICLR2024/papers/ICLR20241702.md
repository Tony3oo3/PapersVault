## Problem

Instruction tuning LLMs requires reliable automated evaluation to guide hyperparameter optimization, but existing approaches rely on costly API-based judges (GPT-3.5/GPT-4) with privacy and reproducibility risks, or expensive human annotation. Traditional metrics like accuracy and perplexity fail to capture the subjective quality dimensions—conciseness, clarity, comprehensiveness, formality, and instruction adherence—that matter for generative LLMs. No open-source, privacy-protected judge model specifically designed for pairwise response evaluation existed prior to this work.

## Input / Output

PandaLM receives a prompt containing an instruction, an optional input context, and two candidate responses (each from a different instruction-tuned LLM), all truncated to 1024 tokens. It generates a free-text output comprising three components: a discrete evaluation result ('1', '2', or 'Tie'), a natural-language explanation of the judgment, and a reference response synthesizing the best qualities of both candidates. The sequence-to-sequence formulation means no separate classification head is used; the evaluation label is simply the first token of the generated text.

## Method

PandaLM is fine-tuned from LLaMA (7B and 70B) in a sequence-to-sequence paradigm with standard cross-entropy loss, trained for 2 epochs using AdamW (lr=2e-5, cosine schedule) on 8 A100 GPUs with DeepSpeed ZeRO Stage 2. Training data consists of 300K filtered samples where paired responses are generated by five similarly-sized models (LLaMA-7B, Bloom-7B, Cerebras-GPT-6.7B, OPT-7B, Pythia-6.9B) all fine-tuned with Alpaca's hyperparameters, and labels are distilled from GPT-3.5 following the self-instruct methodology. A key noise-mitigation step removes samples whose evaluation flips when the response order is swapped, addressing GPT-3.5's inherent position bias. The critical design choice is including evaluation reasons and reference responses in the training target alongside the win/tie/lose label, which ablation (Table 4) shows improves F1 from 0.315 to 0.546 over label-only supervision. For hyperparameter optimization, PandaLM-7B performs 800 pairwise comparisons across an 80-configuration search space (epochs, learning rates, optimizers, schedulers) per model, using a block-then-tournament selection strategy.

## Results

On the 1K-sample human-annotated test dataset, PandaLM-70B achieves the highest accuracy (0.6687) and F1 (0.6923), surpassing GPT-4 (accuracy 0.6647, F1 0.6180) and GPT-3.5 (accuracy 0.6296, F1 0.5820). PandaLM-7B achieves an F1 of 0.5456 with 0.5926 accuracy, while PandaLM-70B-LoRA reaches an F1 of 0.6654 with the highest precision (0.7757). When used for hyperparameter optimization, models tuned with PandaLM-selected hyperparameters achieve an average of 47.0 wins vs. 26.2 losses (GPT-4 judged) and 79.8 wins vs. 25.2 losses (human judged) over Alpaca-tuned counterparts across five base models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PandaLM-70B outperforms GPT-4 in both accuracy (0.6687 vs. 0.6647) and F1 (0.6923 vs. 0.6180), though GPT-4 retains a slight edge in recall (0.6815 vs. 0.6687). Against GPT-3.5, PandaLM-70B leads in all four metrics, with a notable F1 gap of 0.1103 (0.6923 vs. 0.5820). In the zero/few-shot log-likelihood evaluation setting (Appendix G, Table 10), PandaLM-7B (F1 54.56) substantially outperforms un-tuned LLaMA-7B 0-shot (F1 8.77), LLaMA-30B 5-shot (F1 42.93), Vicuna-7B (F1 14.90), and Vicuna-13B (F1 36.29), demonstrating that targeted instruction-tuning for evaluation far exceeds the capability of larger general-purpose models used without fine-tuning. For the downstream hyperparameter optimization task, PandaLM-selected hyperparameters yield consistent wins over Alpaca's defaults across all five base models (LLaMA, Bloom, Cerebras-GPT, OPT, Pythia) as judged by GPT-4, GPT-3.5, and human annotators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Alpaca instruction-tuning framework and the self-instruct methodology for data generation, but departs from prior evaluation approaches (BERTScore, MAUVE, perplexity, accuracy-based metrics) by introducing a dedicated judge LLM that assesses subjective response quality dimensions—conciseness, clarity, comprehensiveness, formality, and instruction adherence—rather than merely measuring content similarity or objective correctness. It also diverges from API-based evaluation (GPT-3.5/GPT-4) by providing an open-source, privacy-protected alternative trained on GPT-3.5-distilled data with heuristic noise filtering. The hyperparameter optimization component extends standard HPO literature (Hyperband, BOHB) into the specific context of LLM instruction tuning, where prior work has largely adopted Alpaca's default settings without systematic search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PandaLM offers a reproducible, cost-effective, and privacy-preserving evaluation pipeline that can be integrated into iterative hyperparameter optimization loops—something API-based methods make prohibitively expensive. The consistent improvement across all five tested models when using PandaLM-selected hyperparameters (vs. Alpaca defaults) validates the practical utility of the approach. However, open questions remain: the hyperparameter search space is limited to common practice, the training data is distilled from GPT-3.5 (raising alignment concerns), only supervised fine-tuning is considered, and the evaluation is outcome-based and resource-intensive. Future work on semi-supervised, noisy, and imbalanced training settings, as well as behavior-prediction-based evaluation, could extend the framework's applicability.
