## Problem

Existing model-based and model-free RL algorithms are designed for single-task learning with per-task hyperparameters, and naively scaling them (e.g., increasing model or data size) often leads to performance degradation rather than improvement. A generalist embodied agent that learns diverse continuous control tasks across multiple domains, embodiments, and action spaces from large uncurated datasets—without hyperparameter tuning—remains an open challenge.

## Input / Output

TD-MPC2 consumes proprioceptive state vectors of varying dimensionality (3 to 223 dimensions across tasks) along with a 96-dimensional learnable task embedding (L2-norm constrained to ≤1) in the multitask setting. It outputs a continuous action vector (1 to 39 dimensions) selected via MPPI planning over a 3-step horizon, bootstrapped by a learned terminal value function. To handle heterogeneous action spaces, all inputs and outputs are zero-padded to the maximum dimension, and invalid action dimensions are masked during both training and inference to prevent spurious entropy inflation and TD-target corruption.

## Method

TD-MPC2 learns an implicit (decoder-free) world model with five MLP components—encoder, latent dynamics, reward predictor, terminal value function, and a maximum-entropy policy prior—jointly trained via joint-embedding prediction, discrete regression (soft cross-entropy) of rewards and values in a log-transformed space, and TD-learning with an ensemble of 5 Q-functions using RED-Q-style targets. A key architectural innovation is SimNorm, which projects the latent state into L fixed-dimensional simplices via softmax, biasing the representation toward sparsity and preventing exploding gradients that plague the original TD-MPC. At decision time, MPPI performs derivative-free local trajectory optimization in latent space, warm-started from the previous step and seeded with samples from the policy prior to accelerate convergence. For multitask learning, all components are conditioned on a shared learnable task embedding, and the same hyperparameters (batch size 256, UTD ratio 1, learning rate 3×10⁻⁴) are used across all 104 tasks without per-task tuning.

## Results

Across 104 continuous control tasks spanning DMControl, Meta-World, ManiSkill2, and MyoSuite, TD-MPC2 consistently outperforms SAC, DreamerV3, and TD-MPC using a single set of hyperparameters. On the hardest tasks, TD-MPC2 achieves >60% success rate on Pick YCB (74-object manipulation) where all baselines fail to learn within the 14M-step budget, and substantially higher episode returns on high-dimensional Dog (A∈R³⁸) and Humanoid (A∈R²¹) locomotion. In the massively multi-task setting (80 tasks, 545M transitions), agent capabilities scale with model size: normalized score increases from 16.0 (1M params) to 70.6 (317M params), with no sign of saturation. Few-shot finetuning of a 19M pretrained agent to 10 held-out tasks yields a 2× improvement over training from scratch in the low-data regime (20k environment steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD-MPC2 outperforms all three baselines (SAC, DreamerV3, TD-MPC) across all four task domains using identical hyperparameters, while baselines require per-task tuning. Against SAC, TD-MPC2 achieves higher data-efficiency and asymptotic performance; SAC and DreamerV3 are both prone to numerical instabilities on high-dimensional Dog locomotion tasks (A∈R³⁸), whereas TD-MPC2 remains stable. Against DreamerV3 (~20M params, UTD=512), TD-MPC2 (5M params, UTD=1) is significantly more data-efficient on Humanoid tasks and excels at fine-grained object manipulation (lift, pick, stack) where DreamerV3 generally struggles. Against the original TD-MPC, TD-MPC2 eliminates the gradient explosion failures observed on tasks like Walker Stand and Walker Walk, and scales to 317M parameters with consistent performance gains, whereas naively scaling TD-MPC leads to net performance degradation. The MyoSuite results (physiologically accurate musculoskeletal control, A∈R³⁹) are particularly notable as no TD-MPC2 experiments were run on this benchmark prior to the reported results, yet TD-MPC2 still outperforms all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

TD-MPC2 is a direct successor to TD-MPC (Hansen et al., 2022), a model-based RL algorithm that performs local trajectory optimization in the latent space of an implicit world model. The key departures are architectural (LayerNorm, Mish, SimNorm simplicial normalization, 5-Q ensemble with RED-Q-style targets), a shift from continuous to discrete (C51-style) regression for reward/value prediction, a maximum-entropy policy prior replacing deterministic-plus-noise, and a multi-task framework with learnable task embeddings and action masking. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the generalist-agent line (Gato, RT-1/RT-2) that relies on expert demonstrations and discrete action tokenization, arguing instead that RL from uncurated, mixed-quality data is the more practical path to generalist embodied agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that model-based RL can scale consistently (1M→317M parameters) without the performance degradation commonly observed in RL, and that a single set of hyperparameters suffices across 104 diverse tasks spanning 4 domains, embodiments, and action spaces — a practical milestone for democratizing RL. Open questions include extending to discrete action spaces, zero-shot generalization to unseen embodiments, combining implicit world models with vision-language models, and leveraging non-reward signals (success labels, preferences, goal embeddings) for large-scale pretraining. The authors also flag risks of reward misspecification, robot safety, and data concentration that will constrain real-world deployment.
