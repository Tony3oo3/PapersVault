## Problem

Speculative decoding accelerates LLM inference by drafting tokens with a small model and verifying them in parallel with a large target model, but the realized speedup depends critically on how well-aligned the draft and target token distributions are. Standard knowledge distillation optimizes for the student's standalone task performance rather than draft-target alignment, and no systematic study had identified which distillation recipe (training data source, divergence function) best serves the specific goal of maximizing speculative decoding acceptance rate.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained target model (e.g., T5-XL 3B or a 234M GPT-like decoder) and a smaller draft model (e.g., T5-Small 77M or 33M GPT-like), along with task-specific input prompts from a ground-truth training set. It produces a distilled draft model that, when paired with the target in speculative decoding, generates text following the target model's exact distribution (lossless) or a tunable approximation (lossy via lenience functions), yielding 10–45% speedup over standard SD. The distilled draft also transfers across tasks: a model distilled on GSM8K achieves an average 26% speedup on 23 unseen BigBenchHard reasoning tasks.

## Method

DistillSpec trains the draft model by minimizing a divergence D between the target's and draft's next-token distributions over model-generated sequences, with two key design choices: (1) using on-policy data generated by the draft model itself rather than the target, which is theoretically justified by a bound (Theorem 4.1) showing that small on-policy TVD loss guarantees high sequence-level acceptance rate; and (2) selecting the divergence function (FKL, RKL, JSD, TVD) as a task- and decoding-strategy-dependent hyperparameter, since no single divergence is universally optimal. The training uses white-box access to both models' logits, and the paper demonstrates that model-generated data consistently outperforms fixed ground-truth data, while the optimal divergence shifts between FKL (greedy, GSM8K), JSD (greedy, XSum), and RKL (non-greedy, GSM8K). The framework extends to lossy SD via lenience functions f(p,ε) that relax the acceptance criterion, enabling fine-grained quality-latency control, and to multi-model settings where first distilling a large model into a medium target and then applying DistillSpec to train a small draft yields 6–10× latency reduction with minimal performance loss.

## Results

DistillSpec consistently improves speculative decoding speed by 10–45% over standard SD across six datasets (XSum, CNN/DM, WMT EnDe, GSM8K, LM1B, BigBenchHard) under both greedy (T=0) and non-greedy (T=1) sampling. The largest gains appear on CNN/DM (up to +0.46× speedup at γ=7, greedy) and XSum (+0.27× at γ=7, non-greedy), while WMT EnDe shows the smallest improvement (+0.10×). In the model-garden setting, combining KD for the target model with DistillSpec for the draft model reduces relative latency from 17.3 to 2.7 on XSum (6.4×) and from 15.0 to 1.4 on GSM8K (10.7×), with Rouge2 dropping only from 23.1 to 23.0 and GSM8K accuracy actually improving from 33.1 to 34.8. A draft model distilled on GSM8K transfers to 23 unseen BigBenchHard tasks, yielding an average speedup of 26% over standard SD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023), DistillSpec wins on every dataset and decoding strategy, with speedup improvements ranging from +0.05× (WMT, γ=3, greedy) to +0.46× (CNN/DM, γ=7, greedy). Among KD baselines, Supervised KD (Hinton et al., 2015; fixed ground-truth data + FKL) consistently ranks lowest in block efficiency and plateaus early in training, confirming that static data is insufficient for SD alignment. SeqKD (Kim & Rush, 2016; teacher-generated data + FKL) underperforms despite using target-model generations, demonstrating that white-box logit supervision is critical for SD. ImitKD (Lin et al., 2020; fixed + student data) sits between Supervised KD and the model-generated-data methods. f-Distill (Wen et al., 2023; TVD + both student and teacher data) and on-policy GKD (Agarwal et al., 2023; FKL/JSD + student data only) are the strongest KD baselines, with GKD achieving the best wall-time improvement due to lower generation cost. Notably, the optimal divergence is task- and decoding-strategy-dependent: JSD wins on XSum (greedy), FKL wins on GSM8K (greedy), and RKL wins on GSM8K (non-greedy), so no single KD recipe dominates all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) and knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015), repurposing KD not to improve the student's standalone task performance but to maximize draft-target alignment for higher token acceptance rates. It builds directly on the on-policy distillation framework of Agarwal et al. (2023) for data generation, but reframes the objective from task accuracy to TVD minimization between draft and target distributions. Unlike prior SD variants (SpecInfer, Medusa, SpecTr) that modify the verification or generation architecture, DistillSpec requires no changes to serving infrastructure and is orthogonal to those approaches. The paper also extends the lossy SD framework of Leviathan et al. (2023) by treating the lenience function as a precise quality-latency control knob rather than a one-off speedup mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6–10× latency reduction in the model-garden setting (distill a large model into a mid-size target, then DistillSpec a small draft) offers a concrete, deployable recipe for production inference pipelines that combine multiple model sizes. The finding that the optimal divergence function is highly task- and decoding-strategy-dependent (FKL for GSM8K greedy, JSD for XSum greedy, RKL for GSM8K non-greedy) is a practical caveat that complicates a one-size-fits-all recommendation. Limitations include evaluation on T5-family models up to 11B and a small GPT-like pair (234M/33M); whether the recipe transfers to 70B+ decoder-only LLMs with different tokenizer vocabularies and longer contexts remains open. The decoupling of draft task quality from SD compatibility (Figure 5a) suggests that future work should treat draft model selection as a distinct optimization problem from student model quality.
