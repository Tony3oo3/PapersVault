## Problem

Large autoregressive text-to-image models and LLMs have each achieved remarkable generative quality in their respective modalities, yet no unified model can coherently produce interleaved text-and-image outputs. Existing multimodal large models predominantly generate a single modality, leaving the task of seamless mixed-modal generation unaddressed. This paper tackles how to fuse two specialized 7B autoregressive decoder-only models (one for text, one for text-to-image) into a single architecture that retains both capabilities while enabling novel interleaved generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241901/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a natural-language instruction prompt and generates a free-form document that alternates between text tokens and 256×256 images (each encoded as 1024 VQ-VAE tokens from an 8192-entry codebook). A special <break> token signals modality switches during autoregressive decoding, and generation terminates at an <eos> token. In practice, outputs typically contain one to three images interleaved with explanatory text, constrained by the 4096-token context window.

## Method

The JAM framework proposes three fusion strategies for combining a 7B LLM (Molybog) and a 7B text-to-image model (CM3leon): simple weight averaging (JAM-Uniform, 7B), width concatenation doubling hidden dimensions (JAM-Width, 26B), and bidirectional cross-attention layers inserted between corresponding layers of the two models (JAM-Cross, 19B). Training proceeds in two stages: a data-efficient continued pretraining phase using only 50B tokens (~1.3% of original pretraining data) on a hybrid text/image-text corpus, followed by a short instruction-tuning phase on a small curated interleaved dataset (WikiHow articles, LIMA) that teaches the model the conversational format. The CM3 causal-masked objective is used throughout, and multimodal retrieval augmentation with CLIP encoders is incorporated to boost image quality. JAM-Cross emerges as the strongest strategy, matching or surpassing both parent models on their respective modalities while enabling coherent interleaved generation.

## Results

On zero-shot text reasoning, JAM-Cross (19B) achieves PIQA 75.4, ARC-Challenge 41.6, ARC-Easy 67.2, StoryCloze 79.8, Winograd 81.0, and Winogrande 66.0, approaching its parent LLM (LLM-4k: PIQA 76.7, ARC-C 45.9) while also retaining image generation capability. On the image-text modality, JAM-Cross achieves the best MS-COCO validation perplexity of 147.6, surpassing even the parent CM3leon 7B model (149.0). The simpler JAM-Uniform (7B) and JAM-Width (26B) variants show substantially degraded text performance (PIQA 62.4 and 57.8 respectively), confirming that cross-attention fusion is the most effective merging strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the parent LLM (LLM-4k, 7B), JAM-Cross (19B) retains most text capability with small drops: PIQA 75.4 vs 76.7, ARC-C 41.6 vs 45.9, StoryCloze 79.8 vs 79.3, Winogrande 66.0 vs 66.2 — while simultaneously gaining image generation. Against GPT-3 (175B) and LLaMA (7B), JAM-Cross trails by 4–10 points on most text benchmarks, but these are text-only models without any image generation. On the image-text modality, JAM-Cross (MS-COCO PPL 147.6) outperforms the parent CM3leon 7B (149.0) and substantially beats CM3 2.7B (200.1) and RA-CM3 2.7B (193.1). In qualitative comparison with GILL (Koh et al., 2023a), the paper reports that JAM-Cross produces more complete, exhaustive text responses and more contextually relevant images, though no quantitative interleaved-generation metric is provided. The JAM-Width variant (26B) underperforms JAM-Cross on both text (PIQA 57.8 vs 75.4) and image-text (PPL 159.5 vs 147.6) despite having more parameters, highlighting that simple width concatenation is inferior to cross-attention fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-merging literature (Kaddour et al., 2022; Wortsman et al., 2022) by treating the merged weights not as a final artifact but as an initialization for continued joint training on mixed-modal data, and it departs from Flamingo's unidirectional cross-attention (visual→language) by introducing a bidirectional cross-attention mechanism between two full autoregressive transformers. Architecturally, it builds directly on CM3leon (Yu et al., 2023) as the image-text backbone and a same-architecture LLM (Molybog et al., 2023) as the text backbone, adopting the CM3 objective (Aghajanyan et al., 2022) and retrieval-augmented training (Yasunaga et al., 2022). Conceptually, it extends the LIMA hypothesis (Zhou et al., 2023) from text style to image style, arguing that a small curated dataset suffices to teach interleaved generation format to a model whose foundational knowledge was acquired during pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The JAM framework demonstrates that two large autoregressive models can be deeply fused with less than 1.3% of their original pretraining data, yielding a model that surpasses its specialized parent on image-text perplexity while retaining near-parity text reasoning — a data-efficiency result with broad implications for model composition. The bidirectional cross-attention fusion is a reusable architectural primitive that could generalize to other autoregressive model pairs, and the instruction-tuning recipe for interleaved generation opens a path toward conversational multimodal systems. Key limitations constrain near-term impact: the study is restricted to same-architecture 7B models, the 4k context length caps output to roughly two images, and generation quality degrades for abstract concepts, leaving open questions about scaling, asymmetric fusion, and multi-turn coherence.
