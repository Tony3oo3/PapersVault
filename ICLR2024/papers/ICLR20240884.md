## Problem

Deep Ensembles achieve functional diversity only implicitly through independent weight initializations, and existing particle-based variational inference (ParVI) approaches that add explicit repulsion terms operate in either weight space (inefficient due to over-parameterization) or function space (computationally intractable and prone to underfitting on training inputs). Neither approach has yielded significant improvements over vanilla Deep Ensembles in accuracy or robustness to input corruptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) or 768-dimensional pretrained feature vectors from a ViT-B/16 model trained on ImageNet-21K, and produces per-class logits from an ensemble of M=10 networks whose predictions are averaged for classification and whose disagreement yields epistemic uncertainty. A key implementation detail is that the repulsion kernel only compares the gradient of the true-label logit (not the full Jacobian), reducing the per-sample gradient computation from C passes to one.

## Method

FoRDE performs Wasserstein Gradient Descent (WGD) in the space of first-order input gradients rather than weights or functions. At each training step, for every ensemble member the normalized true-label input gradient ∇_x f(x;θ_i)_{y_n} is computed and projected onto the unit sphere; a data-dependent RBF kernel with PCA-derived diagonal lengthscales then measures pairwise similarity between these gradient vectors across all M members. The WGD update rule combines the standard cross-entropy driving force with a repulsion force derived from the gradient of this kernel, pushing ensemble members to learn complementary input features. The PCA lengthscales (inverse eigenvalues of the data covariance) apply stronger repulsion along high-variance input dimensions, biasing the ensemble toward robust features, while a median heuristic adaptively sets the global RBF bandwidth. This guarantees functional diversity (since input gradients uniquely determine a function up to translation) without the underfitting that plagues function-space repulsion on training inputs.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18, 10-member ensembles), FoRDE-PCA achieves the best corruption accuracy of 56.1% (cA) and best corruption NLL of 1.90, outperforming the second-best methods (Node-BNNs, Feature-RDE) by +1.3% in cA. FoRDE-Identity and FoRDE-Tuned achieve the best clean accuracy of 82.1% (vs. 81.8% for Deep Ensembles) and best clean NLL of 0.70. On CIFAR-10, FoRDE-PCA achieves 80.5% corruption accuracy, a +2.4% gain over the second-best (LIT at 78.2%), while FoRDE-Identity achieves the best clean NLL of 0.113. FoRDE-PCA also attains the best clean ECE (0.039) on CIFAR-100, indicating superior calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), FoRDE-Identity and FoRDE-Tuned match or slightly exceed DE clean accuracy (82.1% vs. 81.8% on CIFAR-100), while FoRDE-PCA trades 0.4% clean accuracy for a substantial +1.8% corruption accuracy gain (56.1% vs. 54.3%). Weight-RDE and Function-RDE (D'Angelo & Fortuin, 2021) show no meaningful improvement over plain DEs on clean data and are outperformed by all FoRDE variants under corruption; Function-RDE in particular degrades significantly (80.1% clean accuracy, 51.9% cA). Feature-RDE (Yashima et al., 2022) achieves competitive clean accuracy (82.1%) but has the worst ECE (0.072) and no corruption advantage over DE. LIT (Ross et al., 2018) matches DE on clean metrics and is slightly better under corruption (54.4% cA) but is still outperformed by FoRDE-PCA by +1.7% cA. Node-BNNs (Trinh et al., 2022) and SWAG (Maddox et al., 2019) are the weakest baselines, with Node-BNNs at 79.7% clean accuracy and SWAG at 79.4%, both substantially below all ensemble methods. Overall, FoRDE-PCA is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best corruption accuracy, best corruption NLL, and best clean ECE across CIFAR-100, CIFAR-10, and Tiny-ImageNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the particle-based variational inference (ParVI) framework for neural network ensembles by introducing a third repulsion space—input gradients—beyond the previously explored weight space and function space. It builds directly on Wasserstein gradient descent (Liu et al., 2019) and the repulsive deep ensemble formulation of D'Angelo & Fortuin (2021), while drawing a close conceptual parallel to LIT (Ross et al., 2018), which also orthogonalizes input gradients but without the ParVI kernelized framework. The PCA-based lengthscale design is explicitly connected to the EmpCov prior (Izmailov et al., 2021a), which embeds data-manifold variance into the prior; FoRDE instead embeds this knowledge into the approximate posterior via the kernel, allowing it to be combined with EmpCov for further gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FoRDE provides a principled, ParVI-grounded mechanism for ensuring functional diversity in ensembles, with the practical benefit that input-gradient repulsion avoids both the redundancy of weight-space repulsion and the underfitting of function-space repulsion. The consistent corruption-robustness gains (+1.3–2.4% accuracy over second-best) across CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet, plus transfer learning results, suggest the approach is broadly applicable. However, the 3× training overhead and the need to tune lengthscale parameters (PCA vs. identity vs. weighted average) limit immediate adoption; reducing computational cost and hyperparameter sensitivity are the most pressing open questions for practical deployment.
