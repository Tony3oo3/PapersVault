## Problem

Existing graph domain adaptation methods assume source and target domains share an identical label space, which fails for in-the-wild target graphs that may contain entirely novel classes. Open-set recognition has been studied for Euclidean data but remains unexplored for graph-structured data, and the combined challenge of domain shift plus unknown classes in graphs has not been addressed. This paper introduces the open-set graph domain adaptation problem, requiring both classification of known target graphs and identification of OOD target graphs from unseen classes under domain shift and label scarcity.

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled source graphs (with known class labels) and unlabeled target graphs that may contain both known and unknown classes, where the target domain exhibits a density-based domain shift relative to the source. All unknown classes are collapsed into a single unified OOD class, so the output is either a specific known class label or a single 'unknown' flag. Graphs range from small (Letter-high, ~5 nodes) to large (CIFAR10, ~118 nodes), and a memory bank of size up to 1024 stores historical embeddings to enable cross-domain neighbor retrieval without full-dataset storage.

## Method

DREAM employs two complementary GNN branches: a graph-level branch that uses message passing followed by an attention mechanism to produce T super-node views aggregated into a single graph embedding, and a subgraph-enhanced branch that decomposes each graph into R subgraphs via Metis clustering, then applies hierarchical GNNs to capture local functional components and their inter-subgraph interactions. The two branches are coupled through an EM-style posterior regularization that minimizes KL divergence between their predictions on target graphs, generating pseudo-labels from the intersection of both branches' confident predictions to reduce overconfidence. For the open-set setting, dissimilar source samples are linearly interpolated in latent space (manifold mixup) to create virtual OOD samples that train an extra classifier dimension. For domain alignment, a k-mutual-nearest-neighbor graph-of-graphs is maintained via a FIFO memory bank, and each sample's cross-domain neighbors are combined with cosine-similarity weights to produce virtual counterparts, enforcing prediction consistency between original and mixed samples. The overall loss combines source classification (with virtual OOD supervision), target pseudo-labeling, and cross-domain consistency, optimized via alternating E-step/M-step updates.

## Results

On the four TUDataset benchmarks, DREAM achieves the best open-set domain adaptation accuracy across nearly all domain transfer directions, with the largest gains on COIL-DEL (44.0% P1→P2, a 18.9% improvement over the best baseline) and Letter-high (58.7% P1→P2, +12.0%). On the larger Benchmark Datasets, DREAM reaches 84.1% on MNIST (P1→P2) and 48.3% on CIFAR-10 (P1→P2), outperforming the strongest open-set recognition baseline (OSR) by 12.6% and 4.3% respectively. The method consistently outperforms both closed-set graph classifiers and open-set recognition methods, with the only exception being MSRC_21 P2→P1 where CSSR edges ahead by 0.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DREAM outperforms all seven closed-set graph classification baselines (GMT, GraphMix, GCN, GraphSage, GIN, SAG Pooling, GraphCL) by a wide margin on every dataset, with gaps of 7–24 percentage points, confirming that closed-set methods cannot handle unknown classes under domain shift. Among the three open-set recognition baselines, DREAM beats CSSR on 7 of 8 TUDataset directions (losing only by 0.6% on MSRC_21 P2→P1), surpasses DIAS by 6–13 points across all settings, and exceeds OSR by 4–11 points. On the larger MNIST and CIFAR-10 benchmarks, DREAM achieves 84.1% and 48.3% (P1→P2), outperforming the best baseline OSR (74.7% on MNIST) and DIAS (46.3% on CIFAR-10) by 12.6% and 4.3% respectively. In the extended comparison (Table 5), DREAM also dominates graph domain adaptation methods DEAL (68.3% on MSRC_21) and CoCo (69.8%), as well as open-world graph learning methods RIGNN (72.6%), OpenWGL (70.7%), and OpenWRF (73.0%), with the largest margin over CoCo on COIL-DEL (44.0% vs. 33.7%).

## Relation to Prior Work

DREAM sits at the intersection of three research threads: graph domain adaptation (DEAL, CoCo), open-set recognition (CSSR, DIAS, OSR), and open-world graph learning (RIGNN, OpenWGL). It departs from graph domain adaptation by relaxing the closed-set assumption (Y^s = Y^t) to an open-set one (Y^s ⊂ Y^t), and from open-set recognition by explicitly modeling cross-domain distribution shift. The dual-branch architecture (graph-level attention pooling + Metis-based subgraph hierarchy) is a novel combination in the graph DA context, and the EM-style posterior regularization borrowed from structured prediction (Ganchev et al., 2010) is repurposed here to enforce inter-branch consistency on unlabeled target graphs rather than to infer latent variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of open-set graph domain adaptation, filling a gap between closed-set graph DA and open-set recognition that ignores domain shift. The practical relevance is currently limited by the small evaluation datasets (563–70,000 graphs) and the modest number of unknown classes (2–4), making it unclear whether the approach scales to realistic open-world graph scenarios with many novel categories. The authors themselves flag universal and test-time graph domain adaptation as natural next steps, and the method's reliance on a memory bank, k-NN graph-of-graphs, and multiple hyperparameters (α, β, |M|, k) raises questions about robustness and ease of deployment.
