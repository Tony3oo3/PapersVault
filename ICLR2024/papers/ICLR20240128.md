## Problem

Existing hallucination detection methods for LLMs operate at the logit level (perplexity, entropy) or the decoded language level (lexical similarity, self-consistency), where dense semantic information is inevitably lost during token decoding. This paper asks whether the internal hidden states of LLMs, which retain highly concentrated sentence-level semantics, can be directly exploited for more effective hallucination detection without requiring an external embedding model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240128/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text question, the method generates K=10 responses via top-p/top-k sampling (temperature 0.5, top-p 0.99, top-k 5) and extracts the last-token hidden embedding from the middle layer (layer index int(L/2)) of each response as its sentence embedding. The output is a scalar EigenScore (log-determinant of the K×K covariance matrix of these embeddings, regularized with α=0.001) that is thresholded to yield a binary hallucination/non-hallucination label; higher EigenScore indicates greater semantic divergence and thus higher hallucination likelihood.

## Method

The INSIDE framework combines two zero-shot components. First, EigenScore computes the log-determinant of the covariance matrix of K sentence embeddings extracted from the LLM's middle layer, which the paper proves (via Remark 1) to represent the differential entropy of the multivariate Gaussian distribution over the embedding space—capturing fine-grained semantic divergence more effectively than pairwise lexical similarity. Second, a test-time feature clipping (FC) operation truncates extreme activations in the penultimate layer using a sliding memory bank of N=3000 token embeddings, setting per-neuron thresholds at the top and bottom 0.2% percentiles (analogous to the three-sigma rule) to suppress overconfident generations that produce self-consistent hallucinations invisible to standard consistency metrics. The entire pipeline requires no training or fine-tuning and operates entirely on the LLM's internal representations, avoiding the need for a separate sentence-embedding model.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B, EigenScore achieves 80.4% AUC_s on CoQA, 81.5% on SQuAD, and 76.5% on NQ, outperforming the strongest baseline (Lexical Similarity) by 5.6%, 6.6%, and 2.7% respectively. On LLaMA-13B, EigenScore reaches 83.8% AUC_s on SQuAD and 78.2% on NQ. Notably, on TriviaQA (where answers are typically 1–2 words), Perplexity performs best (83.6% AUC_s), while EigenScore remains competitive at 82.7%. Feature clipping provides an additional 1.1% AUC_s improvement on CoQA (79.3→80.4) and 1.2% on NQ (75.9→76.5) for LLaMA-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EigenScore consistently outperforms all logit-level baselines (Perplexity, LN-Entropy, Energy) by large margins on CoQA, SQuAD, and NQ, with the gap reaching 16+ AUC_s points over Energy. Against the closest competitor, Lexical Similarity (Lin et al.), EigenScore gains 5.6% AUC_s on CoQA and 8.9% on SQuAD for LLaMA-7B, confirming that dense embedding-space covariance captures semantic divergence more effectively than surface-level ROUGE-L similarity. In Appendix B, EigenScore also surpasses Semantic Entropy (Kuhn et al.) by 6–8 AUC_s points and SentSAR (Duan et al.) by 2–5 points on CoQA. SelfCheckGPT (Manakul et al.), which relies on an external sentence-embedding model, is outperformed by EigenScore by 1.7–4.7 AUC_s on CoQA across models. On TruthfulQA, ITI (Li et al.) achieves 83.3% accuracy by training 1024 binary classifiers on the validation set, slightly exceeding EigenScore's 81.3% (with 50 in-distribution prompts), but EigenScore is fully zero-shot and does not require task-specific training. The sole exception is TriviaQA, where Perplexity edges out EigenScore (83.6 vs. 82.7 AUC_s) because answers are typically one or two words, making token-level confidence sufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for LLM hallucination detection—logit-level uncertainty (Perplexity, LN-Entropy) and language-level consistency (Lexical Similarity, SelfCheckGPT, Semantic Entropy)—by operating directly on the dense internal hidden states of the model. It builds on Azaria & Mitchell's observation that internal states encode truthfulness signals, and repurposes the OOD-detection technique of activation rectification (Sun et al., Djurisic et al.) as a test-time feature clipping mechanism to suppress overconfident generations. The EigenScore metric itself is a natural application of the LogDet-of-covariance-matrix as a differential entropy estimator, a connection well-established in multivariate statistics and prior ML work on diversity measurement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded (differential entropy) and empirically validated method that consistently outperforms both uncertainty and consistency baselines across multiple LLMs and QA benchmarks, while also addressing the underexplored failure mode of self-consistent hallucinations through feature clipping. Key limitations include the requirement for white-box model access (ruling out API-only models like GPT-4), the sampling-based inference overhead, and the fact that the method only detects rather than mitigates hallucinations. Open questions include whether the EigenScore signal can be leveraged for active hallucination mitigation, whether the approach generalizes beyond QA to open-ended generation tasks, and whether the white-box constraint can be relaxed.
