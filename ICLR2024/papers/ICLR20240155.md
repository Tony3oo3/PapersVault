## Problem

Tree-based ensembles like XGBoost and CatBoost remain state-of-the-art for tabular data but lack the flexibility of gradient-based optimization (arbitrary differentiable losses, iterative training, multimodal integration). Existing gradient-based tabular methods such as NODE use soft, oblique splits that are biased toward smooth solutions, whereas tabular target functions are typically irregular and piecewise-constant. The paper addresses this gap by combining the inductive bias of hard, axis-aligned splits with end-to-end gradient descent.

## Input / Output

GRANDE consumes tabular data with mixed categorical and numerical features; low-cardinality categoricals are one-hot encoded, high-cardinality ones use leave-one-out encoding, and all features are gaussianized via quantile transformation to suit gradient-based optimization. The model outputs a binary class prediction together with instance-wise estimator weights (post-softmax), which indicate which individual trees in the ensemble contributed most to each specific sample's prediction, enabling local interpretability.

## Method

GRANDE extends GradTree from a single tree to a weighted ensemble of E hard, axis-aligned decision trees, all trained jointly via end-to-end gradient descent using a dense matrix/tensor representation that enables parallel computation. Splits use a softsign activation function (scaled to (0,1)) followed by a straight-through operator to produce hard decisions while propagating informative gradients that remain responsive even for large feature-threshold distances. The key architectural innovation is instance-wise estimator weighting: instead of a single learned weight per tree, the model assigns one weight per leaf per estimator (W ∈ R^{E×2^d}), and for each sample, only the weight corresponding to the leaf it falls into is selected, then a softmax across estimators produces the final weighted average. Regularization is applied via per-estimator feature subsets, data subsets, and dropout over estimators, all differentiable and incorporated into the joint optimization.

## Results

On a 19-dataset binary classification benchmark (OpenML), GRANDE achieves the highest normalized mean macro F1-score of 0.776 and MRR of 0.702 with optimized hyperparameters, outperforming CatBoost (0.671 / 0.570), XGBoost (0.483 / 0.417), and NODE (0.327 / 0.395). With default hyperparameters, GRANDE still leads with a normalized mean of 0.637 and MRR of 0.640, compared to XGBoost (0.587 / 0.518) and CatBoost (0.579 / 0.522). GRANDE ranks first on 12 of 19 datasets, with particularly large margins on small datasets such as climate-simulation-crashes (0.853 vs. 0.778 for CatBoost) and cylinder-bands (0.819 vs. 0.801). Average training runtime is 47 seconds, roughly 3× faster than NODE (130 s) though slower than GPU-accelerated XGBoost (~0.2 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRANDE outperforms all four primary baselines on aggregate: it ranks first on 12/19 datasets, while CatBoost wins 4 (madelon, Bioresponse, phoneme, Amazon_employee_access), XGBoost wins 2 (nomao, adult), and NODE wins 1 (wilt). The margin over CatBoost, the strongest competitor, is most pronounced on small-to-medium datasets (e.g., +7.5 F1 on climate-simulation-crashes, +1.8 on cylinder-bands) but narrows or reverses on larger, more homogeneous datasets like madelon (−5.8) and phoneme (−3.0). Against XGBoost, GRANDE leads on 13/19 datasets with an average gap of ~3 F1 points, though XGBoost retains an edge on nomao and adult. NODE, the closest architectural analogue (gradient-based tree ensemble), is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing GRANDE by 10–30 F1 points on most datasets and suffering especially on high-dimensional data (e.g., 0.571 on madelon vs. 0.803 for GRANDE). In extended benchmarks including SAINT, Random Forest, and ExtraTrees, GRANDE maintains its top aggregate ranking (normalized mean 0.817, MRR 0.668 with 250-trial HPO), while SAINT is highly unstable (0.333 on madelon, 0.486 on wilt) and the bagging-based methods are consistently outperformed by gradient-boosting baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRANDE directly extends GradTree (Marton et al., 2023) from single gradient-learned decision trees to a full weighted ensemble, preserving the dense matrix representation and straight-through operator for hard, axis-aligned splits. It departs conceptually from NODE (Popov et al., 2019) and other differentiable tree methods by retaining hard, axis-aligned splits rather than soft, oblique ones, arguing this avoids the spectral bias toward smooth solutions that hinders DL on tabular data. The novel instance-wise leaf-level weighting scheme distinguishes GRANDE from both standard boosting (sequential, greedy combination) and post-hoc ensemble weighting, embedding the weighting into the end-to-end gradient optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRANDE offers a principled way to combine the inductive bias of axis-aligned trees (which explains their dominance on tabular data) with the flexibility of end-to-end gradient training (arbitrary differentiable losses, iterative learning, multimodal integration). The instance-wise weighting provides a new angle on local interpretability—identifying which simple rules drive individual predictions within a complex ensemble—without sacrificing accuracy. However, the method remains substantially slower than GBDT frameworks (47s vs 0.175s average), and it still loses to CatBoost/XGBoost on several datasets, so it is best viewed as a complementary tool rather than a replacement. Open questions include whether deeper stacked tree layers, categorical embeddings, or integration into larger DL pipelines can close the remaining gaps.
