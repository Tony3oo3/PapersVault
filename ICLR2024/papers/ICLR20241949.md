## Problem

Learned simulators have emerged as alternatives to analytic physics engines, but they all require privileged ground-truth information such as precise object geometry, particle tracks, or segmentation masks for training. Meanwhile, 2D video prediction models avoid this requirement but lack 3D structure, preventing novel-view rendering and compositional scene editing. This paper asks whether a fully learned simulator with 3D interpretability and editability can be trained from multi-view RGB-D observations alone, without any privileged physics supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241949/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes posed multi-view RGB-D video sequences (128×128 per camera, 1–16 cameras arranged in a half-dome) and produces predicted future frames from arbitrary camera viewpoints via a volumetric renderer. The intermediate 3D latent point cloud (~2^14 particles per timestep) is directly editable, enabling scene manipulation before re-simulation. Training requires only pixel-level supervision on rendered rays—no object masks, segmentation, or state annotations are needed at any point.

## Method

VPD jointly learns three components end-to-end: (1) a UNet encoder that unprojects each pixel into 3D using depth and produces per-pixel latent features, forming a particle set per timestep; (2) a hierarchical 2-layer GNN dynamics model that uses a sparse set of randomly-placed abstract nodes to mediate message passing between the large point clouds of consecutive timesteps, avoiding the need for cross-timestep correspondences; and (3) a NeRF-style volumetric renderer with concentric annular kernels that decodes particles into RGB-D images from any ray. The dynamics model predicts per-particle position and feature deltas via 10 steps of abstract-node message passing, and the entire pipeline is trained with a multi-step pixel reconstruction loss (256 rays per step over T=6 rollout steps), requiring no intermediate supervision in latent space.

## Results

VPD achieves the highest PSNR across all seven evaluation datasets, with scores ranging from 23.14 (Kubric MOVi-C) to 33.08 (MuJoCo Block). The largest margins over baselines appear on Deformable Collision (28.73 vs. 23.15 for SlotFormer, +5.6 PSNR) and MuJoCo Block (33.08 vs. 25.86 for the Sequential ablation, +7.2 PSNR). VPD also demonstrates strong data efficiency, achieving a PSNR of 31.22 on MuJoCo Block with only 16 training trajectories—just 1.85 points below the 256-trajectory setting—and performs comparably with a single RGB-D camera versus nine.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPD outperforms SlotFormer (a 2D object-centric video model) on all seven datasets, with the largest gap on Deformable Collision (+5.6 PSNR) where SlotFormer struggles with multi-object soft-body contact, and on Kubric MOVi-C (+0.8 PSNR) where detailed textures challenge 2D slot representations. Against the two NeRF-dy-inspired ablations, VPD's end-to-end training with a 3D particle GNN consistently beats both the Global (single latent vector + MLP dynamics) and Sequential (frozen encoder/decoder) variants across all datasets. The Global ablation degrades most on multi-object scenes (Kubric MOVi-A: 27.60 vs. VPD's 29.01), confirming that a compositional 3D representation is necessary for multi-body interactions. The Sequential ablation shows that joint end-to-end training is critical: it loses up to 7.2 PSNR on MuJoCo Block, indicating that pixel-level supervision through the renderer is essential for learning accurate dynamics. Notably, VPD's advantage is most pronounced on deformable and collision scenarios, where 2D models and global representations cannot capture local contact physics.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPD sits at the intersection of three research threads: GNN-based learned simulators (Pfaff et al. 2021, Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. 2020) that model particle/mesh dynamics but require ground-truth state; NeRF-style volumetric renderers (Mildenhall et al. 2020, PointNerf/Xu et al. 2022) that map 3D representations to images; and unsupervised video prediction models (SlotFormer, NeRF-dy) that learn dynamics from pixels but lack 3D structure or editability. The key departure is that VPD jointly learns all three components end-to-end from multi-view RGB-D video alone, eliminating the need for object masks, precise geometry, or pre-trained dynamics models that prior learned simulators and perception-to-simulation pipelines require. The hierarchical GNN with abstract nodes for sparse message passing is inspired by RandLA-Net and Multiscale MeshGraphNets, adapted here to handle the large particle counts needed for video-scale scenes without explicit cross-timestep correspondences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VPD demonstrates that a physically interpretable, editable 3D simulator can be learned purely from sensor observations, which is a meaningful step toward closing the sim-to-real gap in robotics without a separate state-estimation pipeline. The data-efficiency result (16 trajectories approaching 256-trajectory performance) and the single-camera robustness suggest practical viability in settings where collecting diverse trajectories is expensive. However, all experiments are on synthetic datasets with simple scenes (single blocks, a few objects on a plane), and the model still requires RGB-D input and degrades with blurring over long rollouts. Open questions include whether the approach scales to complex real-world scenes with many interacting objects, whether predicted depth can fully replace ground-truth depth, and whether the single-camera advantage persists in more challenging settings.
