## Problem

Standard Vision Transformers collapse all input channels into a single patch token per spatial location, preventing the model from reasoning across semantically distinct channels (e.g., fluorescence vs. brightfield in microscopy, Sentinel-1 radar vs. Sentinel-2 multispectral in satellite imaging). Additionally, real-world multi-channel imaging often presents sparse channel availability during training and testing, which ViT cannot handle gracefully, as it is sensitive to changes in which channels are present.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-channel images of varying channel counts (3-channel RGB, 8-channel microscopy with 5 fluorescence + 3 brightfield, 18-channel satellite from Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2) and produces class labels (1000-way ImageNet, 160-way gene perturbation on JUMP-CP, 17-way climate zone on So2Sat, binary cancer on Camelyon17). A key design property is that the number of input channels can vary at test time without architectural changes, since the channel dimension is mapped into the sequence length dimension rather than being fused at the patch embedding stage.

## Method

ChannelViT modifies ViT by generating one patch token per channel per spatial location instead of one fused token, expanding the sequence length by a factor equal to the number of channels. A single linear projection W is tied across all channels (rather than per-channel weights), and learnable channel embeddings are added to each patch token alongside shared positional embeddings, enabling the Transformer to attend jointly across both spatial positions and channels. To handle channel sparsity, Hierarchical Channel Sampling (HCS) is applied during training: it first samples the number of active channels m uniformly from {1,…,C}, then samples a specific m-channel combination uniformly, ensuring equal probability mass over all channel counts (unlike channel dropout, which biases toward intermediate counts). At test time, the model simply receives whatever channels are available as a shorter or longer token sequence, requiring no architectural modification.

## Results

On JUMP-CP (160-way perturbation detection, 8 channels), ChannelViT-S/16 with HCS achieves 68.09% test accuracy, outperforming ViT-S/16 (56.87%) by 11.22 points; the high-resolution ChannelViT-S/8 reaches 74.77% vs. ViT-S/8's 66.44%. On ImageNet, ChannelViT-S/16 with HCS attains 74.64% top-1 accuracy on RGB and 69.90% on red-only, compared to ViT-S/16's 73.01% and 68.86% respectively. On So2Sat (17-way climate zone classification), ChannelViT-S/8 achieves 99.10% on the random split and 63.01% on the city split, surpassing ViT-S/8 (97.82% and 62.48%). HCS alone boosts single-channel ImageNet accuracy from 29.39% to 68.86% for ViT, demonstrating its value as a standalone regularizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChannelViT consistently outperforms the standard ViT across all four datasets: by 11.22 points on JUMP-CP (8-channel, S/16), by 1.28 points on So2Sat random split, and by ~1.2 points on ImageNet RGB. Against MultiViT (Hussein et al., 2022), which trains a separate ViT per channel and aggregates CLS tokens, ChannelViT achieves 68.09% vs. 30.25% on JUMP-CP with HCS (8 channels), demonstrating that cross-channel attention within a single shared encoder is far more effective than independent per-channel encoders. Compared to FAN (Zhou et al., 2022), which adds channel-wise self-attention, ChannelViT-S/16 edges out FAN-S/16 (conv patch) without HCS (66.22 vs. 65.13 on 8 channels) but pulls dramatically ahead when channels are reduced at test time (41.03 vs. 1.24 on 7 channels), as FAN's reliance on hidden-channel correlations makes it brittle to channel perturbations. Against ResNet-50/152 (He et al., 2016), ChannelViT-S/8 achieves 74.77% on JUMP-CP (8 channels) versus 66.54% for ResNet-152, and while ResNets degrade to ~2–5% accuracy with fewer than 7 channels, ChannelViT maintains 21.20% even with a single channel.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChannelViT extends the Vision Transformer by repurposing the sequence dimension to encode channels as separate tokens, with a shared linear projection and learnable channel embeddings analogous to positional embeddings. This departs from MultiViT (Hussein et al., 2022), which trains independent ViTs per channel and aggregates CLS tokens post-hoc, and from FAN (Zhou et al., 2022), which adds channel-wise attention within the encoder but still collapses channels into a single patch token. The Hierarchical Channel Sampling (HCS) regularizer draws on channel dropout (Tompson et al., 2015) but replaces independent per-channel Bernoulli masking with a two-stage uniform sampling over channel count and combination, yielding a flatter prior over the number of active channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The channel-sparsity robustness problem is a genuine practical bottleneck in microscopy, satellite, and medical imaging where sensor availability varies across experiments and sites; ChannelViT's token-per-channel design and HCS provide a simple, architecture-agnostic remedy that consistently outperforms ViT, MultiViT, FAN, and ResNet baselines across four datasets. The interpretability of learned channel embeddings (e.g., radar real/imaginary parts correlating as expected) and per-channel attention visualizations add diagnostic value beyond accuracy. Key limitations include the 1.6× inference overhead from the longer sequence and the quadratic attention cost, which the authors flag as addressable via linear-attention variants (Linformer, LongNet). An open question is whether the tied-filter and shared-classifier design choices generalize to domains with truly heterogeneous channel semantics (e.g., combining MRI and CT) rather than the same-modality multi-channel settings tested here.
