## Problem

Answering domain counterfactual queries (e.g., 'what would this image look like if taken at a different hospital?') is challenging when causal variables are latent and observations are non-linear mixtures. Existing causal approaches require recovering the full latent SCM under strong assumptions (linearity, atomic interventions, or access to counterfactual pairs), while naive ML domain-translation methods lack theoretical grounding and may produce invalid counterfactuals. This paper asks whether domain counterfactuals can be estimated with theoretical guarantees without recovering the ground-truth causal structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. samples from each domain (no paired counterfactuals required) along with domain labels, and produces a deterministic domain counterfactual for any source-target domain pair. For low-dimensional simulated data, the model is a normalizing flow trained via maximum likelihood; for high-dimensional images, invertibility is relaxed to pseudo-invertibility via a β-VAE where each domain has its own encoder/decoder but shares the initial encoder and final decoder layers (the mixing function g). The counterfactual is computed as g ∘ f_{d'} ∘ f_d^{-1} ∘ g^{-1}(x), yielding a unique output rather than a distribution.

## Method

The paper defines the Invertible Latent Domain (ILD) model class—a shared invertible mixing function g combined with domain-specific invertible autoregressive SCMs f_d—and proves three key results: (1) counterfactual equivalence is characterized by two free invertible functions, showing latent representation recovery is unnecessary; (2) the counterfactual estimation error is bounded by a data-fit term plus an intervention-sparsity term, motivating a sparsity constraint; (3) any ILD with sparsity k can be transformed into a canonical form where only the last k variables are intervened, collapsing the search space from C(m,k) structures to one. The practical algorithm trains a per-domain normalizing flow (or VAE for images) with the constraint that the first m−k autoregressive outputs are shared across all domains, enforced by tying parameters for non-intervened variables. This sparse canonical design is shown to outperform dense baselines on both simulated and image-based counterfactual generation.

## Results

On image-based counterfactual generation, ILD-Can (the proposed sparse canonical model) achieves substantially higher Reversibility and Preservation scores than both baselines across all three evaluated datasets. On 3D Shapes, Reversibility jumps from ~32.6% (ILD-Dense/ILD-Independent) to 79.84%, and Preservation from ~32.5% to 64.99%. On Causal3DIdent, Reversibility improves from ~49–51% to 79.73% and Preservation from ~49–52% to 79.73%. In simulated experiments, ILD-Can consistently achieves lower counterfactual MSE than the dense baseline regardless of which latent nodes are intervened on, and surprisingly performs best when the model sparsity (|I|=1) is even smaller than the true sparsity (|I*|=2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three model variants: ILD-Independent (no shared parameters between domains), ILD-Dense (shared mixing function g but no sparsity constraint on the latent SCM), and ILD-Can (shared g with intervention sparsity enforced via the canonical form). ILD-Can decisively wins on Reversibility and Preservation across all three image datasets — e.g., on 3D Shapes it achieves 79.84% Reversibility vs. 32.56–32.60% for both baselines, and 64.99% Preservation vs. ~32.5%. On CRMNIST, ILD-Can reaches 85.74% Reversibility vs. 59.88–62.29% for baselines. The trade-off is a modest drop in Effectiveness on Causal3DIdent (84.15% vs. 91.05–92.17%), suggesting the sparsity constraint slightly reduces the model's ability to fully shift the domain. In simulated experiments, ILD-Can consistently outperforms ILD-Dense in counterfactual MSE regardless of the true intervention set location, validating that the canonical sparsity structure is a beneficial inductive bias even when the ground-truth sparsity pattern differs from the assumed one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of causal representation learning (Brehmer et al. 2022, Squires et al. 2023, Varici et al. 2023, Khemakhem et al. 2021) that aims to identify the latent SCM, which requires strong assumptions like atomic interventions, linearity, or access to counterfactual pairs. Instead, it repurposes the Sparse Mechanism Shift hypothesis (Schölkopf et al. 2021) and the Rosenblatt transformation to show that domain counterfactuals can be estimated without recovering the latent causal structure at all. Practically, it builds on normalizing flows and β-VAEs (Higgins et al. 2017) as the generative backbone, adding autoregressive and shared-parameter constraints to enforce intervention sparsity. The canonical ILD form (Theorem 3) is a new theoretical device that reduces the structural search space from C(m,k) to a single configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that domain counterfactual estimation becomes a tractable generative modeling problem (shared-parameter VAE/flow with sparsity) rather than an intractable causal structure recovery problem, making it accessible to practitioners without strong causal assumptions. The error bound (Theorem 2) provides principled guidance: both data fit and intervention sparsity matter, and the canonical form means one only needs to tune the sparsity level k rather than search over structures. Limitations include the invertibility assumption (relaxed to pseudo-invertibility for images), the SMS hypothesis, and experiments on relatively simple image datasets (rotated MNIST, 3D shapes). Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-invertible or high-dimensional real-world settings, whether the sparsity level can be learned automatically, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific DCF query to other counterfactual types.
