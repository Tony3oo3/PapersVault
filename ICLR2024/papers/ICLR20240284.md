## Problem

Existing tactile simulators either approximate soft sensor deformation with rigid-body dynamics (sacrificing physical fidelity) or model soft sensors with FEM but restrict object interaction to rigid bodies only. No prior system simultaneously supports diverse object materials (elastic, elastoplastic, cables), provides system-wide differentiability for gradient-based optimization, and offers a data-efficient optical simulation for vision-based tactile sensors. This limits both sim-to-real parameter identification and efficient learning of contact-rich manipulation skills.

## Input / Output

The simulator consumes STL/OBJ mesh models for both the tactile sensor (voxelized into FEM tetrahedra) and objects (voxelized into MPM or PBD particles), along with control velocity commands and material parameters (Lamé constants, yield stress, friction coefficients). For system identification, it additionally ingests real-world sequences of 136 tracked marker 2D positions and 6-axis force readings. Outputs include per-pixel tactile images via an MLP-based optical renderer, per-node contact force distributions, surface deformation maps, and—when used for optimization—refined physical parameters or optimized end-effector trajectories. The entire pipeline runs in Taichi on GPU at roughly 7–25 FPS forward and 4–19 FPS backward depending on task complexity.

## Method

DIFFTACTILE couples four modules: (1) an FEM sensor model using a Neo-Hookean constitutive law to capture hyper-elastic elastomer deformation, (2) MLS-MPM for rigid/elastic/elastoplastic objects (with von Mises plasticity) and PBD for cables, (3) a penalty-based contact model that pairs FEM surface triangles with MPM/PBD particles to compute normal and tangential forces, and (4) an MLP with sinusoidal positional encoding that maps (viewing direction, surface normal) to RGB, replacing Taxim's polynomial lookup table to handle high spatial variation in sensor reflectance. The entire forward simulation is differentiable via Taichi's auto-differentiation, enabling two distinct gradient-based applications: system identification (backpropagating pixel-wise marker MSE and force errors to optimize Lamé parameters and contact stiffness/damping/friction coefficients from real-world data) and trajectory optimization (backpropagating task-specific losses—position, deformation, slip, force, angle—through the full simulation to update end-effector velocity commands). This differentiable pipeline outperforms CMA-ES, PPO, and SAC baselines by a large margin on four contact-rich manipulation tasks, and the identified parameters transfer to real-world GelSight sensor experiments for adaptive grasping.

## Results

On four contact-rich manipulation tasks, DIFFTACTILE's gradient-based optimization with tactile feedback achieves dramatically better performance than all baselines: ObjectRepose 60.82° (vs. best baseline SAC at 5.00°), CableStraighten 0.89 avg. displacement (vs. best SAC at 1.36), CaseOpen 9.83° (vs. best PPO at -0.80°), and SurfaceFollow 51.67 distance (vs. best SAC at 14.00). For system identification (Real2Sim), the gradient-based method achieves pixel-wise marker MSE of 3.08/2.38/3.99 across three contact scenarios, outperforming CMA-ES (3.42/2.67/4.99) and RNN (3.29/2.42/4.53). For optical simulation, the MLP-based approach reduces L1 error from 16.1 (Taxim) to 7.94 and improves PSNR from 38.47 to 39.42.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CMA-ES (Hansen et al., 2003), the gradient-based method wins decisively on all four manipulation tasks with tactile reward: ObjectRepose 60.82° vs. 4.50°, CableStraighten 0.89 vs. 1.97, CaseOpen 9.83° vs. -0.98°, and SurfaceFollow 51.67 vs. 1.67. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), the gap is even more pronounced — both RL methods fail to learn meaningful policies within 100 iterations, with losses that do not decrease, while the gradient-based method converges rapidly. For optical simulation, the MLP with positional encoding outperforms Taxim (Si & Yuan, 2022) across all metrics (L1: 7.94 vs. 16.1; MSE: 56.10 vs. 85.74; PSNR: 39.42 vs. 38.47), demonstrating that the neural approach better captures high-frequency spatial variation in sensor reflectance. In system identification, the gradient-based method also outperforms CMA-ES and an LSTM-based RNN baseline on all three Real2Sim contact scenarios, confirming that differentiable physics provides more effective parameter estimation than sampling or learned surrogates.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIFFTACTILE unifies several established simulation techniques into a single differentiable pipeline: FEM with Neo-Hookean constitutive models for the soft sensor elastomer (extending Narang et al. 2021 and IPC-GraspSim, which used FEM but only for rigid objects), MLS-MPM for multi-material object simulation (Hu et al. 2018), PBD for cables (Müller et al. 2007), and a penalty-based contact model adapted from Xu et al. 2023. Its key departure from prior tactile simulators (Tacto, Tactile-Gym, Tacchi) is that it avoids rigid-body approximations for the sensor while simultaneously supporting deformable and articulated objects, and it is the first to make the entire sensor–object–contact pipeline system-wise differentiable. The optical simulation module extends Taxim's pixel-based approach by replacing its polynomial lookup table with a positional-encoding MLP, trading Taxim's BRDF assumptions for better generalization to high spatial variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the full tactile simulation differentiable, DIFFTACTILE enables gradient-based trajectory optimization that dramatically outperforms RL and CMA-ES baselines on contact-rich tasks (e.g., 60.8° vs ~5° for object reposing), and supports system identification to narrow the sim-to-real gap using real GelSight data. However, the RL comparison is limited to 100 iterations with simple reward functions, and the authors acknowledge this may understate RL's potential; the practical impact is strongest for researchers who need a differentiable tactile simulation platform rather than a new learning algorithm. Open questions include whether the approach scales to dexterous multi-finger hands, whether the identified parameters transfer across sensor geometries, and whether the gradient-based advantage persists with more sophisticated reward shaping and longer RL training budgets.
