## Problem

Scene graph generation (SGG) benchmark datasets suffer from a long-tailed predicate distribution and massive missing annotations (95.5% of triplets in Visual Genome are labeled as background), causing models to over-predict general predicates while failing on fine-grained ones. While self-training has been widely adopted in image recognition, directly applying its thresholding techniques to SGG fails due to the semantic ambiguity among related predicate classes and the extreme class imbalance, making it the first work to design a self-training framework specifically for SGG.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image processed by a frozen Faster R-CNN (ResNeXt-101-FPN) detector that produces entity proposals (top 80 in SGDet, per-class NMS at IoU 0.5), along with a scene graph partitioned into annotated triplets (non-bg predicates) and unannotated triplets (bg-labeled). It outputs a scene graph of (subject, predicate, object) triplets with entity classes, bounding boxes, and predicate classes, and additionally assigns pseudo-labels to unannotated triplets whose model confidence exceeds a dynamically updated class-specific threshold. Pseudo-labeling is restricted to triplets whose subject and object bounding boxes overlap, with a maximum of 3 pseudo-labeled instances per class per image.

## Method

ST-SGG is a model-agnostic iterative self-training loop that, at each batch, assigns pseudo-labels to unannotated triplets whose confidence exceeds a per-class threshold and retrains the SGG model on both annotated and pseudo-labeled triplets. The key novelty is CATM (Class-specific Adaptive Thresholding with Momentum), which updates each predicate class's threshold via exponential moving average of the model's confidence on unannotated triplets predicted as that class, with class-specific momentum coefficients (λ_c^inc, λ_c^dec) derived from the imbalance ratio of class frequencies so that head-class thresholds rise faster and tail-class thresholds fall faster, actively steering pseudo-labels toward minority predicates. For MPNN-based backbones, a Graph Structure Learner (GSL) enriches the scene graph by sampling relevant edges via Gumbel-softmax reparameterization and a focal-loss-trained binary classifier, reducing spurious bg confidence and providing a cleaner candidate set for pseudo-labeling. The framework is plug-and-play, demonstrated on Motif, VCTree, BGNN, and HetSGG, and composes with existing debiasing strategies such as resampling and I-Trans.

## Results

On Visual Genome SGCls, ST-SGG consistently improves mR@K and F@K over vanilla backbones: Motif+ST-SGG raises mR@50/100 from 9.0/9.6 to 12.1/12.8 and F@50/100 from 14.5/15.3 to 18.2/19.1. Combined with resampling and I-Trans, VCTree+I-Trans+ST-SGG achieves mR@50/100 of 21.0/22.4 and F@50/100 of 26.6/27.9, outperforming PE-Net (17.8/18.9, 24.5/25.8) and IE-Trans (16.5/18.1, 22.0/23.6). For MPNN-based models, HetSGG+Resam+ST-SGG+GSL reaches mR@50/100 of 18.2/19.1 and F@50/100 of 24.1/25.1, surpassing the vanilla HetSGG+Resam baseline (15.8/17.7, 22.2/24.3). The gains are most pronounced on tail (fine-grained) predicate classes, while head predicate performance is largely preserved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-SGG outperforms all model-agnostic debiasing baselines on mR@K and F@K in SGCls: Motif+Resam+ST-SGG (F@50/100 = 24.2/23.8) beats DT2-ACBS (19.6/21.5), PCPL (22.2/23.2), TDE (17.8/19.9), DLFE (20.7/21.5), and NICE (22.1/23.5). Against the closest pseudo-labeling competitor IE-Trans, ST-SGG+I-Trans achieves higher mR@50/100 (18.0/19.3 vs. 16.8/17.9) and F@50/100 (22.8/24.1 vs. 22.2/23.3) for Motif, and VCTree+I-Trans+ST-SGG (21.0/22.4, 26.6/27.9) surpasses VCTree+IE-Trans (16.5/18.1, 22.0/23.6) by a wide margin. Among specific-architecture baselines, VCTree+I-Trans+ST-SGG exceeds PE-Net (17.8/18.9, 24.5/25.8) on both mR@K and F@K, while KERN and GBNet are far behind on mR@K (9.4/10.0 and 12.7/13.4 respectively). For MPNN-based models, adding ST-SGG+GSL to BGNN and HetSGG improves mR@K by ~3.4 and ~2.4 points (at @100) over their respective resampling-only baselines, confirming that the framework is model-agnostic and particularly effective for graph-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the self-training paradigm from image recognition (FixMatch, DASH, CREST) to scene graph generation, but identifies that standard confidence-based thresholding techniques fail in SGG due to semantic ambiguity among related predicates, the dominance of the background class (95.5% in VG), and long-tailed predicate distribution. The proposed CATM mechanism extends adaptive thresholding with class-specific momentum rates derived from class frequency, departing from the uniform or frequency-weighted thresholds used in prior semi-supervised work. For MPNN-based SGG models, the paper additionally introduces a Graph Structure Learner (GSL) using Gumbel-softmax reparameterization to enrich the scene graph topology before message passing, building on the bipartite graph and heterogeneous message-passing architectures of BGNN and HetSGG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ST-SGG provides a model-agnostic, plug-and-play framework that exploits the large volume of unannotated triplets (95.5% of VG triplets) that prior SGG methods discard as background, offering a practical path to improving fine-grained predicate prediction without architectural changes. The consistent gains across Motif, VCTree, BGNN, and HetSGG backbones suggest broad applicability, and the zero-shot performance improvement indicates better generalization to unseen triplet patterns. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger predicate vocabularies beyond the top-50 used in VG, whether the bounding-box-overlap constraint for pseudo-labeling limits recall, and whether the iterative self-training loop can be made more computationally efficient for very large datasets.
