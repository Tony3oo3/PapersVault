## Problem

Despite empirical evidence that information flow between training and test nodes strongly influences GNN generalization in transductive node classification, no formal theoretical bound had directly quantified how the topological interplay between these two sets affects generalization error. Additionally, existing methods to enhance long-range node interaction (e.g., graph transformers with pairwise attention) incur O(|V|²) memory complexity and fail to connect nodes across mini-batches in large-scale settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph's adjacency matrix and node feature matrix along with labels on a subset of training nodes (transductive semi-supervised setting), plus a precomputed node2vec positional encoding derived from the full graph structure. For large-scale graphs, the input is partitioned into subgraphs via METIS, and only the current batch's subgraph is processed per epoch while the global workspace retains cross-batch information. The output is a K-class label prediction for each unlabeled test node, produced after an additional message-passing step that fuses the workspace-retrieved information with local neighborhood embeddings.

## Method

The paper contributes a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound for L-layer GCN in transductive node classification that explicitly includes an L-hop interplay term measuring the fraction of test nodes reachable from training nodes within l ≤ L message-passing hops, providing the first theoretical link between train-test structural connectivity and generalization error. To operationalize this insight, the authors propose InterpGNN-GW, a plug-and-play Graph Global Workspace module with n_m memory slots of length l_m. In the write phase, memory slots act as queries attending over node embeddings (as keys and values) to selectively store important node representations, with a gated update mechanism ensuring persistence across batches. In the read phase, node embeddings act as queries attending over the memory slots to retrieve and broadcast summarized information to all nodes, reducing the O(|V_c|²) pairwise attention cost to O(|V_c|·n_m·l_m). Node2vec positional encodings are concatenated with feature embeddings to provide topological context for the attention-based read/write operations, and the final node representation is obtained by one additional GCN layer over the fused embeddings.

## Results

On small-scale graphs (full-batch), InterpGCN-GW achieves 71.72% on CoraFull, 82.92% on Wiki-CS, 92.10% on AmazonComputer, 95.46% on AmazonPhoto, and 52.41% on Flickr, outperforming all 13 baselines. On large-scale graphs (mini-batch), InterpGCN-GW reaches 93.35% on Reddit, 68.76% on AMiner-CS, and 89.11% on Amazon2M, with the largest margins over baselines appearing on AMiner-CS (+1.65% over GNNAutoScale) and Amazon2M (+1.10% over NodeFormer). The GAS-based variant (InterpGAS-GW) further improves on Wiki-CS (83.31%) and Flickr (53.55%), confirming that historical embeddings complement the global workspace for mini-batch training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterpGCN-GW outperforms all full-batch GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, APPNP) by 2–5% across datasets, with the largest gap on CoraFull (71.72% vs. 68.94% for GCN). Among scalable GNNs, it beats ClusterGCN by 2.5–4.2%, GraphSAINT by 0.4–1.4%, and GNNAutoScale by 0.6–3.1%; the closest competition is Grand+ on CoraFull (71.89% vs. 71.72%, a 0.17% gap) and GNNAutoScale on Photo (95.68% vs. 95.46%). Against scalable graph transformers, InterpGCN-GW surpasses NAGphormer by 0.2–1.7% and NodeFormer by 2.6–3.6%, while vanilla GraphGPS and Graphormer are infeasible (OOM) on most small graphs. On large graphs, the advantage widens: InterpGCN-GW leads GNNAutoScale by 1.65% on AMiner-CS and 2.79% on Amazon2M, and beats NAGphormer by 1.84% on AMiner-CS, confirming that the global workspace is most beneficial when mini-batch training isolates nodes across batches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayesian generalization analysis line for GNNs (Liao et al. 2021; Ma et al. 2021) by introducing the first bound that directly incorporates the L-hop interplay—the proportion of test nodes reachable from training nodes within L message-passing steps—rather than relying on proxies like graph diffusion or maximum node degree. Methodologically, it departs from graph transformers (NodeFormer, NAGphormer, GraphGPS) that use O(|V|²) pairwise attention, instead drawing on Global Workspace Theory from cognitive science to propose a fixed-size memory with key-value attention that selectively stores and broadcasts important node embeddings. The base encoder is standard GCN, and the mini-batch training protocol follows ClusterGCN's METIS partitioning, positioning the global workspace as a drop-in enhancement rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result provides the first quantitative link between graph topology (specifically train-test connectivity) and generalization error, formally explaining the empirically observed 'structure imbalance' phenomenon and offering a principled target for architectural design. The global workspace module is practically useful as a plug-and-play component that addresses the out-of-batch isolation problem in mini-batch training with fixed O(|V_c|·n_m·l_m) memory, outperforming both scalable GNNs and graph transformers on large graphs. Limitations include coverage restricted to message-passing GNNs, the inductive-setting experiments being acknowledged as non-rigorous, and the theoretical bound being derived specifically for GCN (though argued to generalize). Open questions include whether the interplay concept extends to non-message-passing architectures and whether alternative mechanisms beyond a global workspace could achieve similar interplay enhancement.
