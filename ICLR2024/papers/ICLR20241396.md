## Problem

Traditional Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) fixes the prior weight parameter to the number of classes and includes a variance-minimized regularization term in its loss, both of which are not mandated by the underlying subjective logic theory. Fixing the prior weight to C produces counter-intuitive predictions (e.g., extreme evidence in a 100-class task still yields only ~0.5 probability), while the variance term pushes the Dirichlet PDF toward a Dirac delta function, demanding infinite evidence and exacerbating overconfidence on misclassified and out-of-distribution samples.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (28×28 MNIST, 32×32 CIFAR-10, 84×84 mini-ImageNet) or video clips (UCF-101) and passes them through a CNN or I3D backbone followed by a single dense layer with softplus activation to produce non-negative evidence vectors e_X ∈ R^C_+. From these, it outputs Dirichlet concentration parameters α_X(x) = e_X(x) + λ, a projected probability P_X(x) = α_X(x)/S_X used as the predictive score, and an uncertainty mass u_X = C/S_X. The key structural difference from standard EDL is that λ = W/C is a tunable hyperparameter (typically 0.1–0.8) rather than the fixed value 1, and the uncertainty mass formula retains the C/S_X form regardless of λ.

## Method

R-EDL relaxes two rigid settings in EDL while strictly adhering to subjective logic. First, it treats the prior weight W as a free hyperparameter, yielding the generalized concentration parameter α_X(x) = e_X(x) + λ (where λ = W/C), which governs the trade-off between leveraging the proportion of evidence across classes and its absolute magnitude in computing the projected probability P_X(x) = (e_X(x) + λ)/(Σe_X(x') + Cλ). Second, it replaces the traditional EDL loss—which integrates MSE over the Dirichlet PDF and decomposes into an expectation term plus a variance-minimized regularization L_var—with a simpler objective that directly minimizes the squared distance between the projected probability P_X and the one-hot label, effectively deprecating L_var and avoiding the pressure to concentrate the Dirichlet into a Dirac delta. A KL-divergence regularization between a modified Dirichlet and a uniform distribution is retained to suppress evidence for non-target classes. The approach is validated across classical, few-shot, noisy, and video-modality settings, showing consistent AUPR gains of 4–9% over EDL and 1–2% over I-EDL on OOD detection.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, R-EDL achieves 90.09% classification accuracy and 98.98% AUPR for confidence estimation (MP), outperforming EDL (83.55%, 97.83%) and I-EDL (89.20%, 98.72%). For OOD detection, R-EDL reaches 85.00% AUPR on CIFAR-10→SVHN and 87.72% on CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100 (both MP), surpassing EDL by 6.13% and 3.42% respectively, and I-EDL by 1.74% and 2.37%. On MNIST, R-EDL attains 99.33% accuracy and 99.29% AUPR for OOD detection against FMNIST, the best among all compared methods. In the few-shot setting (5-way 5-shot on mini-ImageNet), R-EDL achieves 83.65% AUPR for OOD detection, gaining 9.19% over EDL and 1.61% over I-EDL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-EDL consistently outperforms all 12 baselines on CIFAR-10 across classification accuracy, confidence estimation, and OOD detection. Against the closest competitor I-EDL, R-EDL gains +1.74% AUPR on CIFAR-10→SVHN OOD detection (85.00 vs 83.26) and +2.37% on CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100 (87.72 vs 85.35), while also improving classification accuracy from 89.20% to 90.09%. Compared to traditional EDL, the gains are substantially larger: +6.13% on CIFAR-10→SVHN and +3.42% on CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100 OOD detection. R-EDL also surpasses the non-Dirichlet baselines PostN (+4.79% on SVHN OOD) and DUQ (+4.77% on SVHN OOD), while MC Dropout and KL-PN/RKL-PN are far behind on both accuracy and OOD detection. In the few-shot setting, R-EDL leads I-EDL by 1.61% AUPR on 5-way 5-shot OOD detection (83.65 vs 82.04) and by 1.08% on 10-way 5-shot (83.37 vs 82.29), while maintaining competitive classification accuracy. The ablation confirms both relaxations contribute: removing only the variance regularization yields +4.12% and fixing only λ=1 yields +4.92% on CIFAR-10→SVHN OOD detection relative to full EDL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Evidential Deep Learning (EDL, Sensoy et al. 2018) by identifying and relaxing two settings that the EDL community has widely adopted but that are not mandated by the underlying subjective logic theory (Jøsang 2001, 2016). Specifically, it treats the prior weight W as a tunable hyperparameter rather than fixing it to the number of classes, and removes the variance-minimized regularization term from the EDL loss. The method is positioned against the current SOTA EDL variant I-EDL (Deng et al. 2023) and other Dirichlet-based uncertainty methods (KL-PN, RKL-PN, PostN), as well as single-forward-pass alternatives like DUQ and MC Dropout, but remains firmly within the evidential deep learning family rather than proposing a new uncertainty framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical argument that two 'standard' EDL choices are actually counterproductive: fixing W=C produces counter-intuitive probability ceilings in high-class-count settings, and the variance-minimized regularization pushes the Dirichlet PDF toward a Dirac delta, exacerbating overconfidence. This reframing is useful for the EDL community because it identifies where the method's theoretical foundations (subjective logic) and its practical implementation have diverged. Open questions include what determines the optimal W for a given task (the paper treats it as a black-box hyperparameter) and whether optimizing other statistical properties of the Dirichlet PDF beyond its mean could yield further gains, as the authors themselves note the current objective is 'somewhat coarse.'
