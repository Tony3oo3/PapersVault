## Problem

As the number of open-sourced neural architectures grows, researchers lack an efficient way to situate new designs relative to existing ones or discover structurally similar architectures. Existing GNN-based graph pre-training strategies fail on neural architecture computational graphs because of dramatically varied graph sizes (e.g., LeNet-5 vs. ViT-L) and the presence of repeated motifs (ResNet blocks, self-attention modules) that are critical for meaningful similarity measurement but are not captured by whole-graph encoders.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query neural architecture's computational graph (a directed acyclic graph where nodes are atomic operations and edges encode connectivity) and produces a ranked list of K most similar architectures from a database of 12,517 real-world models collected from Hugging Face and PyTorch Hub, scored by cosine similarity in a learned embedding space. Each architecture is also associated with a fixed-dimensional embedding vector for efficient nearest-neighbor retrieval. A supplementary dataset of 30,000 NAS architectures generated within the DARTS search space (split into 10 classes by graph editing distance) is used for pre-training and transfer experiments.

## Method

The core framework splits each computational graph into motifs via an iterative neighborhood-encoding strategy that expands each node's receptive field over s steps, then discovers repeated subsequences as motifs. These motifs are embedded by a multi-layer GCN and used to rebuild a smaller macro graph where motif embeddings become nodes connected by shared-edge overlap, reducing computational burden. Training proceeds in two stages: first, motif-level contrastive learning pairs each motif with its k-hop context graph as a positive sample against other context graphs as negatives; second, graph-level pre-training on the macro graph combines contrastive learning (same model family as positives) with cross-entropy classification on coarse-grained labels (CNN-block, attention-block, other). At inference, the full pipeline F(A) = F_m(Agg[F_s(Mss(A))]) maps a query architecture to its embedding for retrieval.

## Results

On the real-world neural architecture dataset (12,517 architectures), the proposed method achieves MRR@20 of 0.825, MAP@20 of 0.593, and NDCG@20 of 0.705, outperforming GCN (MRR 0.737) and GAT (MRR 0.756) baselines. On the NAS dataset (30,000 architectures), all methods achieve perfect MRR@20 of 1.000, but the proposed method leads in MAP@20 (0.952 vs. 0.927 for GCN and 0.941 for GAT) and NDCG@20 (0.969 vs. 0.953 and 0.961). The paper reports average improvements of +5.4% (MRR), +2.9% (MAP), and +13% (NDCG) over baselines on real-world architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two mainstream graph embedding baselines: GCN (Kipf & Welling, 2016) and GAT (Veličković, 2017), both applied directly to the full computational graph without motif decomposition. On the real-world dataset, the proposed method wins on all metrics against both baselines: MRR@20 improves from 0.737 (GCN) and 0.756 (GAT) to 0.825, while NDCG@20 improves from 0.686 (GCN) and 0.610 (GAT) to 0.705. GAT underperforms GCN on MAP and NDCG for real-world architectures, suggesting attention mechanisms alone do not capture the repetitive block structures well. On the NAS dataset, all three methods tie on MRR (1.000), but the proposed method achieves the best MAP@20 (0.952 vs. 0.941 for GAT and 0.927 for GCN) and NDCG@20 (0.969 vs. 0.961 and 0.953), indicating superior ranking quality beyond the top hit. The gains are most pronounced on real-world architectures where graph sizes vary dramatically, confirming that the motif-based macro graph construction addresses the scalability challenge that plain GNNs struggle with.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining Neural Architecture Retrieval (NAR) as a formal problem, explicitly stating it is the first work to set up a retrieval system for neural architectures. Methodologically, it repurposes graph pre-training strategies (GCN, contrastive learning) from the broader GNN literature but introduces a motif-sampling decomposition and a two-stage (motif-level + graph-level) contrastive objective specifically tailored to the variable-size, repetitive-block structure of neural architecture computational graphs. It also draws on NAS search spaces (DARTS) to generate synthetic training data, bridging the NAS and information-retrieval communities in a way neither had previously done.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12k real-world architecture dataset with pre-computed embeddings provides a practical resource for researchers to quickly locate similar designs or verify the novelty of their own architectures, addressing a real pain point as the architecture landscape grows. However, the empirical evidence is limited by relatively weak baselines (vanilla GCN/GAT without any pre-training strategy), and the method's generalization to truly novel, out-of-distribution architectures remains underexplored. Open questions include whether the motif-based decomposition scales to extremely large models (e.g., LLMs with hundreds of layers), how sensitive retrieval quality is to the motif sampling depth parameter, and whether the approach transfers across fundamentally different architecture paradigms beyond CNNs and Transformers.
