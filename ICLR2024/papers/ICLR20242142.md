## Problem

Existing physics-informed machine learning (PIML) and deep learning methods fail to accurately forecast dynamical systems under out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions where both initial states and ODE parameters fall outside their training supports. While prior work demonstrated deep learning's OOD fragility, this paper shows that even PIML methods designed with OOD robustness in mind (e.g., DyAd, CoDA, APHYNITY) perform poorly, because their neural network components learn spurious associations and transductive variants cannot transfer knowledge across tasks with different parameters.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes M noisy trajectories (each of length T+1) from the same ODE system but with different initial conditions and task-specific ODE parameters. At test, it receives only (r+1) short initial observations (where r ≈ T/3 or T/10) of a new trajectory with potentially OOD initial state and OOD parameters, and must forecast all future states up to time T. The learned output is a sparse binary causal structure matrix Φ ∈ {0,1}^{d×m} identifying which basis functions drive each state derivative, plus task-specific coefficient vectors W^(te) adapted per test trajectory via a few gradient steps on the short observation window.

## Method

MetaPhysiCa models the ODE as dX̂_t/dt = (W^(i) ⊙ Φ) F(X̂_t; ξ), where Φ is a shared binary causal structure selecting which basis functions (polynomial, trigonometric) affect each state derivative, W^(i) are task-specific linear coefficients, and ξ are global basis-function parameters. Training uses a bi-level (or jointly optimized) objective combining prediction loss, L1 regularization on Φ for structural sparsity, and a V-REx penalty that minimizes variance of per-task prediction errors—empirically shown to be essential for retaining basis functions active in only a small fraction of training tasks. A straight-through estimator handles the discrete Φ optimization. At test time, Φ and ξ are frozen while only W^(te) is adapted via a few gradient steps on the short initial observations, enabling robustness to OOD parameters. Theorem 1 proves that under identifiability assumptions (linear independence of active basis functions per task and nonzero ground-truth weights), the true causal structure is the unique minimizer of the objective for sufficiently small L1 penalty.

## Results

MetaPhysiCa achieves 2× to 28× lower OOD NRMSE than the best working baseline across three dynamical system forecasting tasks (damped pendulum, predator-prey, epidemic/SIR). On the epidemic model, MetaPhysiCa reaches 0.019 NRMSE under OOD initial conditions and 0.100 under OOD initial conditions + parameters, compared to 0.544 and 0.898 for the best baseline (APHYNITY). On the damped pendulum, MetaPhysiCa achieves 0.070 and 0.181 NRMSE in the two OOD scenarios versus 0.764 and 1.011 for the best baseline (CoDA). Transductive PIML methods (SINDy, EQL) fail entirely (NaN) on most tasks due to numerically stiff learned ODEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaPhysiCa outperforms all six baselines on every OOD scenario across all three datasets. The deep learning baselines (NeuralODE, DyAd, CoDA) achieve competitive in-distribution NRMSE (0.004–0.006 on epidemic) but degrade by 100–300× under OOD shifts, e.g., NeuralODE jumps from 0.005 to 1.139 on epidemic OOD X_t0. The inductive PIML method APHYNITY is the strongest baseline OOD (0.544 on epidemic OOD X_t0) but still suffers 28× higher error than MetaPhysiCa (0.019). The transductive PIML methods SINDy and EQL fail catastrophically, returning NaN on most tasks because they fit a stiff ODE from only a few test observations without leveraging training data. The 2× to 28× improvement range reflects that MetaPhysiCa's advantage is largest when both initial conditions and ODE parameters shift simultaneously, where baselines that cannot adapt task-specific parameters (SINDy, EQL) or that learn spurious correlations (NeuralODE, DyAd) fail most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaPhysiCa sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) physics-informed machine learning (SINDy, EQL, APHYNITY, NeuralODE), (2) meta-learning for dynamical systems (DyAd, CoDA), and (3) continuous causal structure discovery (Zheng et al., 2018). The key departure is the explicit combination of l1-regularized causal structure discovery with a V-REx (invariant risk minimization) penalty within a meta-learning objective, which the authors show is necessary to recover the true ODE structure when training tasks have heterogeneous parameters. The paper also builds on the algorithmic alignment insight from Xu et al. (2021) by incorporating explicit basis functions (polynomial, trigonometric) into the architecture to enable OOD extrapolation, rather than relying on purely learned representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even PIML methods explicitly designed for OOD robustness (DyAd, CoDA) fail under combined OOD shifts in initial conditions and parameters, challenging the assumption that physics-informed architectures are inherently more robust than pure deep learning. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that the true causal structure is recovered under identifiability conditions provides a principled foundation, and the V-REx ablation (24× worse OOD NRMSE without it) highlights a concrete mechanism for why standard causal discovery fails in multi-task settings. Key limitations include restriction to ODEs (not PDEs), dependence on a pre-specified basis function library, and difficulty with stiff ODEs when extending to more expressive SCMs—open questions that limit immediate practical impact but point toward a promising direction for OOD-robust scientific ML.
