## Problem

Vision Transformers rely on 1-D positional encoding, which provides weak 2-D spatial inductive bias, while CNNs use fixed-size 2-D kernels that limit the range of spatial dependencies a layer can capture. No existing layer simultaneously offers two-dimensional position awareness, dynamic spatial locality, and the ability to model unrestricted horizontal, vertical, or diagonal dependencies with a small, fixed parameter count.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241634/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The 2-D SSM layer consumes a 2-D sequence of patches (e.g., 14×14 for 224×224 ViT inputs, 7×7 for 32×32 CIFAR) or a 2-D feature map from a CNN stage, and outputs a same-shaped 2-D signal that is added back via a residual connection. In the ViT/DeiT/Swin setting it is applied to the patch grid before each transformer block; in ConvNeXt it replaces the 7×7 depthwise Conv2D at each stage. The final network outputs standard class labels (ImageNet, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet) or 40-way multi-label attribute scores (Celeb-A).

## Method

The layer is built on Roesser's 2-D state-space model, which maintains separate horizontal and vertical latent states updated by a 2×2 system matrix (A₁–A₄) and input matrix (B₁, B₂). By diagonalizing the system matrices and unrolling the recurrence, the layer is shown to be equivalent to a 2-D convolution whose kernel K is computed from only 8–9 scalar parameters per channel, enabling kernels of arbitrary spatial extent. A sigmoid constrains eigenvalues to [0,1] for stability, a divide-by-two normalization prevents kernel explosion, and a relaxation in the first row and column counteracts an inherent diagonal bias. The kernel is computed in O(L_tot·L_max·N) time via cached Vandermonde products and applied via FFT-based convolution. Theoretically, the paper proves the layer generalizes S4ND (which can only express rank-1 kernels) and can express full-rank kernels, and empirically it is plugged into ViT, DeiT, Swin, MEGA, and ConvNeXt with negligible added parameters, improving accuracy across ImageNet-100, Celeb-A, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, and the Long Range Arena benchmark.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, the 2D-SSM layer improves ViT from 73.81% to 77.21% (+3.4%), Swin from 76.87% to 80.12% (+3.25%), and MEGA from 72.27% to 76.02% (+3.75%), with only ~0.3–1% additional parameters. On Tiny-ImageNet, gains are even larger: Swin improves from 60.87% to 65.77% (+4.9%). On the Long Range Arena CIFAR-10 grayscale task, MEGA + 2D-SSM achieves 91.31%, surpassing Hyena (91.20%) and CCNN-2D (91.12%). On ImageNet-100, DeiT-T improves from 78.21% to 81.16% (+2.95%) and DeiT-S from 82.27% to 84.82% (+2.55%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The 2D-SSM layer consistently outperforms all compared baselines across backbones and datasets. Against S4ND (the only prior multi-dimensional SSM layer), 2D-SSM wins decisively: on ViT+CIFAR-100, S4ND actually degrades performance below the baseline (72.60% vs. 73.81%), while 2D-SSM-r achieves 77.21%; on Swin+Tiny-ImageNet, S4ND reaches 64.60% versus 2D-SSM-r's 65.77%. Against the standard ViT backbone, 2D-SSM adds +3.4% on CIFAR-100 and +2.94% on Tiny-ImageNet with only 0.3% extra parameters. Against MEGA, the improvement is +3.75% on CIFAR-100, and on the LRA CIFAR-10 grayscale benchmark, MEGA+2D-SSM (91.31%) surpasses both Hyena (91.20%) and CCNN-2D (91.12%). Against ConvNeXt, replacing 7×7 Conv2D with 2D-SSM yields +1.09% on ImageNet-100 (Tiny) and +1.30% on CIFAR-100 (Nano) without increasing parameter count. Against DeiT on Celeb-A, the complex variant achieves +1.41% (DeiT-T) and +0.60% (DeiT-B) over the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 1-D state space model lineage (S4, H3, DLR) into the 2-D spatial domain by adopting Roesser's multidimensional state space formulation (Kung et al., 1977), which uses separate horizontal and vertical states coupled through cross-axis matrices A₂ and A₃. The closest prior work is S4ND (Nguyen et al., 2022), which applies independent 1-D SSMs per axis and combines them via outer products; this paper proves 2D-SSM strictly generalizes S4ND (S4ND is recovered by zeroing A₂ and A₃) and shows the expressiveness gap via tensor rank (full-rank vs. rank-1 kernels). The diagonal parameterization, sigmoid-constrained eigenvalues, and kernel-convolution computation strategy follow directly from S4 and DLR, while the novel normalization (divide-by-two) and first-row/column relaxation are specific to the 2-D recurrent structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The layer offers a principled, parameter-efficient mechanism (9 scalars per channel) to inject 2-D spatial inductive bias into both transformer and CNN backbones, and the finding that ViTs equipped with 2D-SSM outperform their PE-equipped counterparts even when positional encoding is removed suggests a fundamentally different route to positional awareness—baking it into the computation operator rather than the input representation. Limitations include relatively slow training (O(L_tot·L_max·N) kernel computation) and instability of the complex-SSM variant, and the authors identify multi-dimensional parallel scanners and optimal normalization as key directions. Broader open questions include whether the layer transfers to spatial vision tasks (segmentation, inpainting, video) and whether the expressiveness advantage over S4ND translates to consistent gains at larger scale.
