## Problem

Slot Attention, the dominant method for object-centric representation learning, represents slots solely as cluster centers (weighted means), making it impossible to distinguish groups of vectors that share the same expectation but differ in spread. This limited expressiveness causes underperformance on complex datasets like ClevrTex and COCO, training instability, and poor results under stringent evaluation thresholds for set property prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242024/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (rescaled to 64×64, 96×96, or 128×128 depending on dataset and task) through a CNN encoder that produces a flattened feature map with absolute positional embeddings. The SMM outputs a fixed number of slot vectors (e.g., 10 for CLEVR, 12 for SLATE) whose dimensionality matches Slot Attention slots via a learned 2D×D projection, preserving permutation equivariance. Downstream, these slots drive autoregressive image reconstruction (via Image GPT conditioned on dVAE tokens), set property prediction (matched via Hungarian algorithm with Huber loss), object segmentation masks (via Spatial Broadcast Decoder), or concept-sampled image edits.

## Method

The Slot Mixture Module (SMM) replaces the k-means-like dot-product attention in Slot Attention with a learnable Gaussian Mixture Model, where the E-step computes soft assignments via log-Gaussian density (replacing dot-product attention) and the M-step updates both cluster means μ and diagonal covariances Σ_diag (replacing the weighted-mean-only update). Slots are represented as the concatenation of μ and Σ_diag, making them strictly more expressive than SA's center-only slots, since two groups with identical means but different variances become distinguishable. A GRU plus residual MLP refines the means each iteration (analogous to SA's GRU), while prior mixture weights π are updated as the mean of soft assignments across spatial positions. The module uses no k/v projection matrices (saving D×D parameters to match SA's parameter count) and detaches slots from the gradient graph at the final iteration for training stability, running 5 iterations by default.

## Results

On the CLEVR set property prediction task, SMM achieves state-of-the-art AP of 98.4% at the stringent 0.25 distance threshold and 92.1% at 0.125, surpassing the previous best iDSPN (95.8% and 76.9% respectively). For image reconstruction via the SLATE architecture, SMM consistently improves FID over Slot Attention across all five datasets (e.g., 34.8 vs. 35.4 on CLEVR-Mirror, 50.4 vs. 56.6 on ShapeStacks, 122 vs. 129 on COCO) and reduces cross-entropy by 10–40%. On the object discovery task, SMM achieves 74.7 FG-ARI on the complex ClevrTex dataset versus 62.4 for Slot Attention, and also converges faster on simpler datasets (e.g., 91.3 vs. 85.6 FG-ARI on CLEVR10 with reduced training).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020), SMM wins decisively on every task: set property prediction AP at 0.25 threshold jumps from 10.8% to 98.4%, object discovery FG-ARI on ClevrTex improves from 62.4 to 74.7, and image reconstruction FID drops across all five datasets. Compared to the highly specialized iDSPN model (Zhang et al., 2019), SMM surpasses it at all stringent thresholds (AP₀.₂₅: 98.4 vs. 95.8; AP₀.₁₂₅: 92.1 vs. 76.9; AP₀.₀₆₂₅: 47.3 vs. 32.3), establishing a new state of the art. Against SA-MESH (Zhang et al., 2023), SMM is marginally behind at the loose AP₀.₅ threshold (98.8 vs. 98.9) but clearly ahead at all tighter thresholds (AP₀.₂₅: 98.4 vs. 91.1; AP₀.₁₂₅: 92.1 vs. 47.6; AP₀.₀₆₂₅: 47.3 vs. 12.5). For image reconstruction, SMM outperforms the SLATE baseline (Singh et al., 2022) on FID and cross-entropy for every dataset tested, with the largest relative gains on ShapeStacks (FID 50.4 vs. 56.6) and CLEVR-Mirror (cross-entropy 0.20 vs. 0.82). A vanilla GMM clustering ablation (no trainable layers) also outperforms vanilla k-means on set prediction, confirming that the Gaussian density formulation itself—beyond just the cluster centers—provides the core advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly generalizes Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020) by replacing its k-means-like clustering mechanism with a Gaussian Mixture Model, yielding the Slot Mixture Module (SMM). The key conceptual departure is that slots are no longer solely cluster centers but also encode per-cluster covariance (diagonal) and prior mixture weights, making them strictly more expressive than Slot Attention's representations. The experimental framework builds on SLATE (Singh et al., 2022) for image reconstruction and the Spatial Broadcast Decoder (Watters et al., 2019b) for object discovery, while the set property prediction benchmark follows the CLEVR protocol established by Locatello et al. and Zhang et al. (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SMM's ability to achieve state-of-the-art set property prediction at stringent distance thresholds (AP0.125 = 92.1% vs. iDSPN's 76.9%) addresses a known weakness of Slot Attention, which previously only performed well under loose thresholds. The consistent gains across reconstruction, object discovery, and set prediction tasks on both simple and texture-rich synthetic datasets suggest the covariance information is genuinely useful for distinguishing similar objects. However, performance on real-world data (COCO) remains subpar, the fixed slot count limitation persists, and SMM shows reduced robustness to varying inference-time iteration counts, leaving the broader challenge of scaling object-centric learning to complex real scenes open.
