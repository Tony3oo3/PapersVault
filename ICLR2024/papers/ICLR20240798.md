## Problem

Existing meta-learning approaches for physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) sample PDE tasks uniformly and allocate equal residual-point budgets to each, ignoring well-known differences in task difficulty driven by PDE parameters and available sampling budgets. This neglect leads to degraded average accuracy and significant performance disparity across PDE configurations, particularly at low residual budgets. No adaptive task-sampling strategy had previously been developed for meta-learning PINNs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete set of PDE configurations (e.g., 5–14 parameter values for Burgers', convection, reaction-diffusion, Helmholtz, or Navier-Stokes equations) and a total residual-point budget b_T, and produces a meta-learned PINN (HyperPINN or MAD-PINN) along with per-task sampling probabilities p(λ_i) updated every 200 training iterations. The probabilities are computed from gradient inner products between each task's training loss and the aggregate validation loss, requiring no additional network architecture changes. The meta-model then outputs PDE solution approximations for any configuration within or beyond the training range.

## Method

DATS derives a closed-form analytical solution for the optimal task-sampling probability by applying a first-order Taylor expansion of the validation loss around a single-step gradient descent update (inspired by Reptile), reducing the bi-level optimization to maximizing the dot product between each task's training gradient and the sum of all validation gradients. The solution is obtained via KKT first-order conditions with KL-divergence regularization (uniform or consecutive prior) controlled by hyperparameter β, yielding p(λ_i) ∝ r_i · exp(w_i/β). Two strategies operationalize the probability: DATS-w reweights each task's contribution to the meta-training loss, while DATS-rp adaptively allocates the residual-point budget so that harder tasks receive more sampling points. The approach is agnostic to the underlying meta-PINN architecture and residual-point sampling strategy, and requires only a 3–8% computational overhead over uniform sampling.

## Results

Across five benchmark PDEs (Burgers', Convection, Reaction-Diffusion, 2D Helmholtz, 3D Navier-Stokes) and two meta-PINN architectures (HyperPINN, MAD-PINN), DATS consistently reduces L2 error and performance disparity compared to uniform and self-paced task sampling at the same residual-point budget. At the lowest tested budgets, DATS achieves L2 errors of 0.052 (Reaction-Diffusion/HyperPINN, budget=20) and 0.051 (Reaction-Diffusion/MAD-PINN, budget=10), versus 0.410 and 0.931 for uniform sampling respectively. DATS reaches the performance of baselines at budget=10,000 using only 40% of that budget on Burgers' (HyperPINN) and less than 1% on the Convection equation, while also reducing disparity metrics by 30–60% at matched budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the self-paced task sampling baseline (extended from SelectNet [11] to the task level), DATS wins on L2 error across all five PDEs and both meta-PINN models at every matched budget; the margin is largest at low budgets (e.g., Reaction-Diffusion/HyperPINN at budget 20: DATS 0.052 vs. S-P 0.642, a 12× improvement). On the disparity metric, DATS also consistently reduces worst-to-best performance spread (e.g., Burgers'/HyperPINN at budget 500: 0.209 vs. 0.308 for S-P). Against GPT-PINN [5] (evaluated only on the Burgers' equation with HyperPINN), DATS achieves substantially lower L2 error at both budget 500 (0.079 vs. 0.301) and budget 10,000 (0.039 vs. 0.097), while also producing lower disparity (0.209 vs. 0.405 at budget 500). The paper notes that GPT-PINN's difficulty-awareness helps on hard Burgers' parameters (λ=0.001) but at the expense of easy ones (λ=0.1), whereas DATS balances performance across the full range. Computational overhead is minimal: DATS is only 3% slower than uniform and 1% slower than self-paced in HyperPINN, and 8%/6% slower respectively in MAD-PINN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-learning PINN framework (HyperPINN, MAD-PINN) by introducing the first adaptive task-sampling strategy for this setting, departing from the uniform task sampling used in all prior meta-PINN work. Conceptually, it draws on adaptive task scheduling in general meta-learning (Yao et al. 2021, Liu et al. 2020) but replaces their reinforcement-learning-based optimization with a closed-form KKT solution, and extends self-paced learning (Gu et al. 2021) from the residual-point level to the task level. The two practical instantiations—adaptive loss weighting (DATS-w) and adaptive residual-point allocation (DATS-rp)—are novel decompositions of the sampling probability that have no direct precedent in either the meta-learning or PINN literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DATS demonstrates that difficulty-aware task sampling can reduce residual-point budgets by 60–99% while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy, which is practically significant given that residual-point computation dominates PINN training cost. The finding that simple random residual sampling outperformed the more sophisticated RAD strategy in the meta-learning setting challenges the assumption that adaptive residual sampling always helps and opens a gap for understanding what sampling strategies are optimal in meta-learning versus single-PINN training. Open questions include convergence guarantees for the iterative KKT scheme, extension to continuous PDE-parameter distributions, generalization to operator learning, and whether the approach transfers to higher-dimensional or more complex PDEs beyond the five benchmarks tested.
