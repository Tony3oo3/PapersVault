## Problem

Subgraph GNNs achieve strong expressive power by performing message passing over all n node-marked subgraphs, but this O(n²·d) complexity makes them intractable on large graphs. While random subgraph sampling reduces cost, the paper proves that for families of WL-indistinguishable graphs, only a small number of specifically chosen subgraphs suffice for identification, and random selection fails to find them with probability tending to zero. The paper asks whether a learned, data-driven selection policy can recover these critical subgraphs efficiently.

## Input / Output

The method takes a node-attributed graph with adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} and node features X ∈ R^{n×c}, and produces a graph-level or node-level prediction. Unusually, the input graph is first transformed into a bag of T+1 subgraphs (the original graph plus T node-marked subgraphs selected iteratively by a learned policy), where each subgraph is obtained by concatenating a one-hot root indicator to the node features without altering connectivity. The selection is sequential: at each step the policy conditions on all previously selected subgraphs, and the final bag size T is a hyperparameter (typically 2–20) far smaller than n.

## Method

POLICY-LEARN comprises two DS-GNN Subgraph GNNs trained end-to-end: a selection network f that iteratively builds the subgraph bag, and a prediction network g that solves the downstream task. At each of T steps, f takes the current bag, runs message passing with decoupled marking and feature channels, pools across subgraphs, and outputs a distribution over nodes; the next root is sampled via the straight-through Gumbel-Softmax estimator, making the discrete selection differentiable. The key architectural insight is that using a Subgraph GNN (rather than an MPNN as in OSAN) for the selection network, combined with sequential rather than one-shot generation, allows f to distinguish nodes in already-selected CSL subgraphs from remaining nodes and assign them zero probability, thereby implementing policies that provably identify isomorphism types where random or one-shot selection provably cannot. The paper proves POLICY-LEARN can identify (n,ℓ)-CSL graphs with only ℓ subgraphs, while OSAN and random policies require Θ(ℓ ln ℓ) in expectation.

## Results

On the OGB molecular benchmarks, POLICY-LEARN with T=2 subgraphs achieves 0.877 RMSE on MOLESOL, 77.47 ROC-AUC on MOLTOX21, 78.40 ROC-AUC on MOLBACE, and 79.13 ROC-AUC on MOLHIV, matching or surpassing the full-bag baseline (FULL) on three of four datasets. On ZINC-12K under the 500k parameter budget, POLICY-LEARN (T=5) achieves 0.109 MAE, outperforming the random baseline (0.113) and OSAN (0.177) while approaching the full-bag result (0.087). On REDDIT-BINARY, where full-bag Subgraph GNNs run out of memory, POLICY-LEARN (T=2) achieves 93.0% accuracy, the best among all efficient baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POLICY-LEARN consistently outperforms standard MPNNs (GCN, GIN) across all datasets, e.g., on ZINC-12K it achieves 0.109 MAE vs. GIN's 0.163 and GCN's 0.321. Against the prior learnable selection method OSAN (Qian et al., 2022), POLICY-LEARN wins decisively: on ZINC-12K, 0.109 vs. 0.177 MAE (T=2), and on OGB MOLESOL, 0.877 vs. 0.984 RMSE, with the paper proving OSAN is theoretically incapable of identifying (n,ℓ)-CSL graphs while POLICY-LEARN can. Compared to the random selection baseline, POLICY-LEARN's advantage is largest when T is small (e.g., ZINC T=2: 0.120 vs. 0.136 MAE; MOLBACE T=2: 78.40 vs. 75.36 ROC-AUC), confirming that learned selection matters most under tight subgraph budgets. Against full-bag Subgraph GNNs (DS-GNN, DSS-GNN, SUN, GNN-SSWL+), POLICY-LEARN is competitive on ZINC (0.109 vs. 0.087 for FULL) but trails the strongest full-bag methods like GNN-SSWL+ (0.070) and CIN (0.079); however, on OGB it matches or exceeds FULL on MOLTOX21 (77.47 vs. 76.25) and MOLHIV (79.13 vs. 76.54). On REDDIT-BINARY, where full-bag methods OOM, POLICY-LEARN (93.0%) beats all efficient baselines including RANDOM (92.4%) and GIN (92.4%). On the Alchemy-12K dataset, POLICY-LEARN (T=5: 6.72 MAE) outperforms GIN+RWSE&LAPPE (7.197) and approaches FULL (6.65).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DS-GNN Subgraph GNN architecture (Bevilacqua et al., 2022) and the node-marking subgraph generation policy (Papp & Wattenhofer, 2022), repurposing them as the backbone for a learned selection mechanism. It departs most significantly from OSAN (Qian et al., 2022), the first framework to learn subgraph selection policies, by replacing OSAN's one-shot MPNN-based selection with a sequential Subgraph GNN that iteratively constructs the bag, and by using Gumbel-Softmax instead of I-MLE for differentiable sampling. The theoretical analysis draws on the WL isomorphism test and the coupon collector problem to establish a clean separation between what sequential Subgraph GNN selection can achieve versus what one-shot MPNN selection or random sampling can.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses the central practical barrier to Subgraph GNN adoption—the O(n²·d) computational cost—by showing that a small, learned subset of subgraphs can match or even exceed full-bag performance, thereby opening Subgraph GNNs to large-graph settings (e.g., REDDIT-BINARY) where full-bag methods simply run out of memory. The theoretical results (coupon collector lower bound for random policies, provable identification of (n,ℓ)-CSL families) provide a principled justification for learned selection over random sampling. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond the 3-WL expressiveness ceiling (it cannot distinguish strongly regular graphs of identical parameters), whether the sequential selection generalizes to higher-order (tuple-based) subgraph policies, and whether the Gumbel-Softmax approximation introduces meaningful bias in practice at scale.
