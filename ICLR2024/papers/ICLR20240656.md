## Problem

Sequence-based PPI prediction methods are computationally efficient but miss critical structural information, while structure-based methods (e.g., HIGH-PPI) achieve higher accuracy at prohibitive computational cost, making them impractical for million-scale PPI prediction. Additionally, existing microenvironment definitions rely on experimentally assayed physicochemical properties with extremely small vocabularies (a dozen types), insufficient to capture the diversity and complexity of local residue environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240656/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes protein amino acid sequences and AlphaFold2-predicted 3D structures, which are jointly represented as residue-level heterogeneous graphs with three edge types (sequential, spatial radius < 10Å, and K-nearest neighbors). It produces multi-label PPI interaction type predictions (7 types: Activation, Binding, Catalysis, Expression, Inhibition, Ptmod, Reaction) over a PPI graph, along with a reusable microenvironment codebook and frozen protein embeddings that can be applied to new proteins without retraining. The codebook is learned once via self-supervised pre-training on unlabeled sequence-structure pairs (e.g., CATH 4.2 with 42K proteins) and then frozen as an off-the-shelf encoding tool.

## Method

MAPE-PPI defines each residue's microenvironment as its ego subgraph capturing both sequential and structural contexts, then learns a fine-grained discrete codebook (512–1024 entries) via a VQ-VAE variant where a heterogeneous GNN encoder aggregates messages from three edge types and vector quantization maps residue embeddings to the nearest codebook entry. The key architectural novelty is Masked Codebook Modeling (MCM), which randomly replaces codebook entries with a [MASK] vector and trains the decoder to reconstruct inputs from the remaining unmasked codes, thereby capturing dependencies between microenvironments without masking input features or hidden codes. Training is decoupled into two stages: (1) self-supervised pre-training of the encoder, decoder, and codebook jointly via reconstruction + codebook + commitment + MCM losses, and (2) freezing the encoder and codebook, then training a lightweight GIN-based PPI encoder and classifier on the PPI graph using the frozen microenvironment-aware protein embeddings as node features. This decoupling yields a total training complexity of O(N(|V_P|·|A| + |E_P|) + |E_T|) versus O(N(|V_P|+|E_P|)·|E_T|) for end-to-end structure-based methods like HIGH-PPI, enabling scaling to million-level PPI prediction.

## Results

MAPE-PPI achieves the best average rank (3.44 without pre-training; 1.11 with CATH4.2 pre-training) across all three datasets (SHS27k, SHS148k, STRING) and three partitions (Random, DFS, BFS) in micro-F1. On the most challenging BFS partition, MAPE-PPI scores 70.38 (SHS27k), 74.76 (SHS148k), and 78.26 (STRING) without pre-training, and 72.47, 75.62, and 78.59 with CATH4.2 pre-training. With only 42K unlabeled sequence-structure pairs from CATH4.2, MAPE-PPI outperforms pre-trained models using 5M (KeAP) or 805K (GearNet-Edge) proteins. The method also scales to the full STRING dataset (~1.15M PPI entries) where HIGH-PPI cannot be applied due to prohibitive computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAPE-PPI (without pre-training) outperforms the best sequence-based baseline SemiGNN-PPI by +2.73 micro-F1 on SHS27k DFS (71.98 vs 69.25) and +2.44 on BFS (70.38 vs 67.94), and beats the structure-based HIGH-PPI by +1.74 on SHS27k DFS (71.98 vs 70.24) and +1.98 on BFS (70.38 vs 68.40). HIGH-PPI cannot be evaluated on the full STRING dataset due to its ~200-hour training cost, whereas MAPE-PPI achieves 96.12/86.50/78.26 micro-F1 on STRING Random/DFS/BFS. Among pre-trained models, MAPE-PPI with only 42K CATH4.2 proteins surpasses KeAP (5M proteins) by +1.93 on SHS27k Random (90.42 vs 88.49) and GearNet-Edge (805K proteins) by +1.06 on the same setting, while ProBERT and ESM-1b (sequence-only pre-training) are outperformed even by vanilla MAPE-PPI without any pre-training. On the generalization analysis (Table 2), MAPE-PPI shows the largest gains over SemiGNN-PPI on unseen-protein subsets: +8.10% on BFS-NS (67.15 vs 59.05) and +5.99% on Random-NS (60.70 vs 54.71), indicating stronger transfer to novel proteins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) for discrete representation learning via codebooks and on the masked modeling paradigm popularized by GraphMAE (Hou et al., 2022), but departs from both in a key way: instead of masking input features or hidden codes, it masks the codebook entries themselves (Masked Codebook Modeling) to capture dependencies between microenvironments. In the PPI prediction landscape, it positions itself between sequence-based methods (GNN-PPI, SemiGNN-PPI) that are fast but ignore structure, and structure-based methods (HIGH-PPI) that model residue-level 3D geometry end-to-end but are computationally prohibitive at scale. The heterogeneous GNN encoder that jointly captures sequential and structural contexts as 'microenvironments' draws on prior work defining residue microenvironments via physicochemical properties, but replaces the small experimental vocabulary with a learned, fine-grained codebook.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of protein encoding (via a reusable microenvironment codebook) from PPI prediction is the paper's most practically significant contribution: it enables scaling to million-level PPI graphs (STRING) where end-to-end structure-based methods like HIGH-PPI become intractable (~200+ hours). The finding that structure-aware pre-training on only 42K CATH proteins outperforms sequence-only pre-training on 2.1B BFD sequences suggests that structural context is a more information-dense signal for PPI prediction than raw sequence scale. Open questions include extending the framework to predict interaction interfaces or multi-protein conformations, and whether the learned codebook generalizes across species or protein families beyond the tested Homo sapiens datasets.
