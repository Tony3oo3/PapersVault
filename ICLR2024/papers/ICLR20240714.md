## Problem

Existing Inverse Constrained Reinforcement Learning (ICRL) methods assume deterministic environments and ignore the two distinct sources of uncertainty that degrade constraint inference: aleatoric uncertainty from stochastic transition dynamics (which causes constraint-violating behaviors in imitation policies) and epistemic uncertainty from limited training data (which causes inaccurate cost predictions on out-of-distribution state-action pairs). No prior method simultaneously addresses both uncertainty types, leaving a gap in robust constraint learning for safe control.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite set of expert demonstration trajectories (state-action sequences) from a stochastic environment, along with a task-dependent reward signal. It produces two outputs: a step-wise constraint function c_ω(s,a) = 1 − p(φ|s,a;ω) that assigns a Bernoulli feasibility probability to each state-action pair, and a risk-sensitive imitation policy π that maximizes expected reward subject to a CVaR-bounded cumulative cost constraint. The constraint function is updated iteratively via a modified ICI objective that incorporates a mutual-information regularization term, while the policy is optimized via a distributional Lagrangian objective.

## Method

UAICRL alternates between two uncertainty-aware phases. For aleatoric uncertainty, it introduces Distributional Lagrange Policy Optimization (DLPO), which replaces the scalar cost value function with a distributional estimator Z^c_θ(s) parameterized by N quantiles and updated via the distributional Bellman equation; the risk-sensitive constraint then applies CVaR(α) to this predicted cost distribution within a PPO-Lagrange policy update, making the policy sensitive to the tail risk of constraint violations. For epistemic uncertainty, it introduces Flow-based Trajectory Generation (FTG), which learns a trajectory flow function via flow matching (conditioned on feasibility labels φ or φ⁻) to generate diverse expert and nominal trajectories that augment the training set, and modifies the ICI objective with a dropout-based mutual information term I(ω;Φ|τ̄,D) that penalizes the constraint model for high uncertainty on out-of-distribution trajectories. The two components are integrated in an iterative loop: the policy is updated under risk-sensitive constraints, nominal trajectories are collected, both expert and nominal flows are trained, generated trajectories augment the datasets, and the constraint model is re-estimated under the augmented ICI objective until the imitation policy matches expert behavior.

## Results

In stochastic Gridworld environments (p_s=0.01), UAICRL achieves feasible rewards of 0.650, 0.683, and 0.359 across three settings with constraint violation rates of 0.13, 0.09, and 0.34, consistently outperforming all baselines. In stochastic MuJoCo (N(0,0.1)), UAICRL achieves 1714.4±222.0 feasible rewards in Blocked Half-Cheetah (vs. 615.4±299.9 for VICRL) with a constraint violation rate of 0.07±0.06 (vs. 0.57±0.21 for VICRL). In the HighD highway driving environment, UAICRL attains the highest feasible rewards while maintaining a satisfactorily low constraint violation rate, demonstrating robustness in high-dimensional realistic settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAICRL consistently outperforms all three baselines (GACL, ICRL, VICRL) across discrete, continuous, and realistic environments. GACL (reward-shaping via discriminator) is the weakest baseline, achieving near-zero rewards in Gridworld Setting 3 (0.000) and struggling in most MuJoCo environments due to early-stage estimation errors; it is too conservative to accumulate meaningful rewards. ICRL (risk-neutral MEnt framework) performs poorly under stochastic dynamics, with constraint violation rates of 0.53–0.73 in Gridworld and 0.67 in Blocked Half-Cheetah, confirming that ignoring aleatoric uncertainty leads to constraint-violating policies. VICRL (dropout-based epistemic uncertainty) is the strongest baseline, achieving 0.778 reward in Gridworld Setting 1 (p_s=0.001) and 615.4±299.9 in Blocked Half-Cheetah, but UAICRL surpasses it by 2.5% in reward and reduces violation rate from 0.19 to 0.09 in Gridworld, and from 0.57 to 0.07 in Blocked Half-Cheetah. The ablation variants (UAICRL-NRS without risk-sensitive constraints, UAICRL-NDA without data augmentation) each outperform the baselines individually, confirming that both the distributional estimator (aleatoric) and flow-based augmentation (epistemic) contribute independently to the overall gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICRL framework (Malik et al., 2021; Scobee & Sastry, 2020) by addressing a gap that prior methods overlooked: the simultaneous presence of aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties in stochastic environments. For aleatoric uncertainty, it draws on distributional RL (Bellemare et al., 2017; Luo et al., 2022) and risk-sensitive control (Chow et al., 2015) to construct a CVaR-based constraint via distributional Bellman updates, integrated into PPO as DLPO. For epistemic uncertainty, it builds on dropout-based mutual information estimation (Smith & Gal, 2018; Liu et al., 2023) but departs from prior variational approaches by using flow-based generative trajectory generation (GFlowNets, Bengio et al., 2021; Li et al., 2023) to augment training data, reducing OoD cost prediction errors. No prior ICRL method handled both uncertainty types simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Safe control in autonomous driving and robotics requires inferring constraints from demonstrations while accounting for environmental stochasticity; this paper provides a principled, empirically validated framework for doing so. The consistent outperformance across discrete Gridworlds, continuous MuJoCo, and the realistic HighD highway driving benchmark demonstrates practical relevance. Key limitations include the exclusive use of CVaR (VaR, EVaR untested), the absence of rigorous theoretical guarantees for the ICRL convergence under uncertainty, and the reliance on a small constraint bound (ε=1e-8) that may not generalize to all safety-critical settings. Future work on robust optimization integration and extending theoretical IRL results to the constrained setting are natural next steps.
