## Problem

Retrieval-augmented language models (RALMs) can suffer performance degradation when retrieved context is irrelevant or noisy, a problem that is especially acute in multi-hop reasoning where misuse of irrelevant evidence causes cascading errors. Since LLMs are not trained with retrieved passages, their brittleness to noisy retrieval is expected but unaddressed in prior work that focuses on in-context prompting or large-scale fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The system takes a question (single-hop or multi-hop) and interleaves retrieval at each decomposition step, prepending retrieved passages to the question before generation. For the NLI filtering variant, the input additionally includes a generated question-answer pair that is checked for entailment against the retrieved context. In the multi-hop setting, the model generates intermediate questions and answers sequentially, with the retriever called after each intermediate question, and the final output is the complete decomposition chain ending in the answer.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary approaches. First, a black-box NLI-based filter uses a BART-Large model trained on MNLI to classify whether the question-answer hypothesis is entailed by the retrieved context premise; when entailment is not confirmed, the system falls back to the LLM's parametric memory without retrieval. Second, and more effectively, the paper fine-tunes Llama-2-13B with QLoRA on automatically generated data that mixes relevant (top-1) and irrelevant (low-ranked or random) retrieved contexts at each decomposition step. For multi-hop training data, GPT-3 generates decompositions without retrieval, which are verified via intermediate answer matching or self-consistency across multiple samples. The key insight is that training on as few as 1,000 examples with intentionally varied context quality teaches the model to both leverage useful retrieval and ignore noise, outperforming both untrained in-context baselines and models fine-tuned without retrieval.

## Results

On the three in-domain training datasets with Google Search top-1 retrieval, SA-RetRobust achieves 45.7 on NQ, 66.9 on 2WIKIMQA, and 70.1 on STRATEGYQA, outperforming the no-retrieval fine-tuned baseline (SA-NoRet) by 2.7, 2.4, and 2.4 points respectively. Under random (irrelevant) retrieval, SA-RetRobust maintains performance within one standard deviation of SA-NoRet (e.g., 33.8 vs 31.1 on NQ, 41.6 vs 40.4 on 2WIKIMQA, 69.9 vs 66.6 on STRATEGYQA), demonstrating robustness to irrelevant context. On the out-of-distribution BAMBOOGLE dataset with top-1 retrieval, SA-RetRobust reaches 64.7, surpassing SA-NoRet (57.4) and SA-Ret@1 (61.3). A 13B SA-RetRobust model also outperforms all prompted Llama-2-70B variants by at least 3.3 points on NQ with top-1 retrieval (45.7 vs 42.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA-RetRobust consistently outperforms both fine-tuned ablations: it beats SA-NoRet (trained without retrieval) by 2.7, 2.4, and 2.4 points on average under top-1, low-ranked, and random Google Search retrieval, and by 2.7 points with ColBERTv2. Against SA-Ret@1 (trained only with top-1 evidence), SA-RetRobust wins by 0.2, 0.4, and 3.2 points under the same three retrieval settings, with the largest gap appearing under random retrieval where SA-Ret@1 degrades significantly (e.g., 37.5 vs 41.6 on 2WIKIMQA). The NLI-based filtering baseline prevents retrieval from hurting performance (matching the no-retrieval score in all settings) but sacrifices gains when retrieval is helpful, e.g., dropping from 56.0 to 39.9 on 2WIKIMQA with top-1 retrieval. In-context prompted Llama-2-13B models (SA-RMix) suffer large drops under random retrieval (e.g., 22.0 on NQ vs 29.6 without retrieval), while SA-RetRobust remains stable. Notably, the 13B SA-RetRobust outperforms all prompted Llama-2-70B variants on NQ with top-1 retrieval (45.7 vs best 42.4), indicating that retrieval-robust training is more effective than simply scaling model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Self-Ask decomposition paradigm (Press et al., 2023) and the in-context RALM formalism (Ram et al., 2023), extending them by addressing the well-known but under-studied problem of retrieval-induced degradation. It repurposes NLI models—previously applied to hallucination detection and statement attribution—as a lightweight filter for irrelevant retrieved passages, and introduces an automatic data-generation pipeline (leveraging GPT-3 decompositions and self-consistency) to fine-tune LLMs on mixed relevant/irrelevant contexts. The work departs from Li et al. (2023), which trains on over 200K examples for controllable memory, by demonstrating that as few as 1,000 automatically generated examples suffice for robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-cost recipe (QLoRA fine-tuning on ~1,000 examples, a few hours on one GPU) that makes RALMs robust to noisy retrieval while improving overall accuracy, which is immediately adoptable for practitioners deploying retrieval-augmented systems. The finding that in-context exemplars containing retrieved passages actually degrade performance compared to exemplars without retrieval challenges a common design assumption and has implications for prompt engineering broadly. Limitations include the in-domain focus (trained and evaluated on the same task distribution), reliance on the Self-Ask format, and evaluation on a single model family (Llama-2); open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other decomposition strategies, larger model families, and truly out-of-distribution retrieval noise.
