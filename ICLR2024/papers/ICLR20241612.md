## Problem

Existing image outpainting methods can only expand images in fixed discrete multiples, requiring N sequential model passes to reach an N-times expansion (e.g., 11.7x requires three passes through QueryOTR). Additionally, prior SOTA methods depend on pre-trained encoders and discriminator-based training, increasing computational cost and limiting flexibility. This paper addresses both limitations by enabling arbitrary continuous multiples and single-pass generation without a pre-trained backbone.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input sub-image (center-cropped to 128×128, 86×86, or 56×56 pixels for 2.25x, 5x, and 11.7x settings, then resized to 192×192) and a desired outpainting multiple N ∈ ℝ (any real value > 1, not restricted to discrete steps). It produces a single outpainted image at the specified multiple in one forward pass through the diffusion model. Unlike prior methods that require iterative re-feeding of the output as new input for larger expansions, PQDiff generates the full expanded image directly from the original sub-image regardless of the target multiple.

## Method

PQDiff learns outpainting by training on pairs of randomly cropped views (anchor and target) from the same image, where the model reconstructs the target view from the anchor view plus a relative positional embedding (RPE) computed via sin-cos encoding of the continuous offset (m, n) between the two crops. A position-aware cross-attention mechanism uses the RPE as queries and the concatenated anchor + noisy target latent sequence as keys/values, enabling the network to learn where to place generated content relative to the input. The diffusion process operates in VQVAE latent space with a ViT-based encoder-decoder (8–10 transformer blocks each), trained with standard noise-prediction loss. At inference, the input sub-image serves as the anchor, the desired multiple N determines the RPE, and a single diffusion sampling pass (with configurable timesteps, e.g., 20–500) produces the outpainted image at any continuous multiple without retraining or sequential re-feeding.

## Results

On the most challenging 11.7× outpainting setting, PQDiff+Copy achieves FID scores of 39.465 (Scenery), 39.616 (Building Facades), and 36.326 (WikiArt), substantially outperforming the previous SOTA QueryOTR (60.977, 64.926, 69.951 respectively). At 2.25×, PQDiff+Copy reaches FID 20.100 on Scenery and 7.968 on WikiArt, both setting new state-of-the-art. In terms of sampling speed, PQDiff with 20 timesteps generates 64 images in 9.638 s regardless of the outpainting multiple (2.25×, 5×, or 11.7×), whereas QueryOTR requires 23.7 s, 47.5 s, and 71.2 s respectively—meaning PQDiff uses only 40.6%, 20.3%, and 10.2% of QueryOTR's time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PQDiff+Copy outperforms all six baselines on every FID and IS metric across all three datasets and all three outpainting multiples. Against the previous SOTA QueryOTR (Yao et al., 2022), PQDiff+Copy reduces FID by 21.5 points on Scenery (39.465 vs. 60.977), 25.3 points on Building Facades (39.616 vs. 64.926), and 33.6 points on WikiArt (36.326 vs. 69.951) at the 11.7× setting, while also improving IS by 0.46, 0.14, and 2.04 respectively. The GAN-based baselines (SRN, NSIPO, IOH, Uformer) degrade sharply as the outpainting multiple increases—e.g., SRN's FID on Scenery jumps from 47.8 at 2.25× to 115.2 at 11.7×—whereas PQDiff's FID increases more gradually (20.1 → 39.5), reflecting the advantage of one-step generation over iterative multi-step outpainting. The vanilla diffusion baseline (Ho et al., 2020) without the PQ conditioning scheme achieves FID 73.6 on Scenery at 11.7× and Center PSNR of only 10.3, confirming that the positional query mechanism is critical for effective conditioning. In terms of speed, PQDiff with 20 timesteps takes 9.6 s for all multiples, while QueryOTR's time scales linearly with the number of required forward passes (23.7 s → 71.2 s from 2.25× to 11.7×).

## Relation to Prior Work

PQDiff builds on the latent diffusion framework (DDPM + VQVAE compression from LDM + ViT backbone from ViTDiff) and introduces a position-aware cross-attention mechanism where relative positional embeddings serve as queries against the anchor image's patch tokens. This departs fundamentally from the prior SOTA QueryOTR, which frames outpainting as a masked image modeling problem with fixed discrete positional embeddings and requires multiple sequential forward passes for larger multiples. The paper also contrasts with GAN-based outpainting methods (NSIPO, IOH, Uformer) by eliminating the discriminator and pre-trained encoder requirements, positioning the positional query scheme as a general conditioning mechanism applicable to both diffusion and GAN generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to outpaint at arbitrary continuous multiples in a single forward pass (rather than N sequential passes) addresses a practical bottleneck for applications like virtual reality and creative content generation where expansion ratios are not fixed. The finding that continuous training generalizes far better to unseen multiples than discrete training (e.g., 4x, 9x, 16x) highlights a broader principle about the importance of distributional coverage in positional conditioning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger images (beyond 192×192), whether the VQVAE bottleneck limits fidelity at high resolutions, and whether the positional query mechanism transfers effectively to related tasks like inpainting or super-resolution as the authors speculate.
