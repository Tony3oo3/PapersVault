## Problem

Autoregressive audio generation models (e.g., MusicGen, AudioGen) achieve high quality but suffer from high inference latency due to sequential token-by-token decoding, making them unsuitable for interactive applications. Diffusion-based alternatives decode in parallel but require hundreds of steps and struggle with long-form sequences. No prior work demonstrated a single-stage, purely non-autoregressive transformer capable of generating 30-second audio with quality comparable to autoregressive baselines while being significantly faster.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt as input, encoded into a semantic representation via a pretrained T5 model, and produces a 30-second audio waveform at 32 kHz (for music) or 10-second environmental sound at 16 kHz. Internally, the audio is represented as four parallel streams of discrete tokens at 50 Hz frame rate (codebook size 2048 each), obtained from EnCodec's residual vector quantization. The model uses classifier-free guidance with a condition dropout of 0.3 during training, and nucleus sampling (top-p 0.9) with temperature annealed from 3.0 to 0 during iterative decoding.

## Method

MAGNET is a single non-autoregressive transformer (300M or 1.5B parameters) that performs masked generative modeling over multi-stream RVQ audio tokens. Three key modifications distinguish it from prior masked-generation work: (1) span-based masking (60 ms spans) rather than individual tokens, preventing the model from exploiting adjacent-token information during teacher forcing; (2) restricted self-attention for codebooks beyond the first, limiting their temporal context to ~200 ms based on empirical receptive-field analysis of EnCodec; and (3) a rescoring mechanism that combines MAGNET's own confidence with an external pretrained model (MusicGen or AudioGen) via a convex combination to rank and select which spans to unmask at each decoding step. Inference proceeds iteratively from a fully masked sequence, unmasking the most confident spans at each step under a cosine masking scheduler, with classifier-free guidance annealed from λ₀=10 to λ₁=1 as the masking rate decreases. A Hybrid-MAGNET variant jointly trains an autoregressive objective for the first t timesteps and the MAGNET objective for the remainder, enabling a single model to generate an initial audio prompt autoregressively and complete the sequence in parallel.

## Results

On the MusicCaps benchmark for text-to-music generation, MAGNET-small (300M params) achieves FAD 3.3, KL 1.12, and CLAP 0.31 with a latency of 4.0 seconds, compared to MusicGen-small's FAD 3.1, KL 1.29, and 17.6s latency — a 4.4× speedup with comparable quality. MAGNET-large (1.5B params) reaches FAD 4.0, KL 1.15, and human-study scores of 84.26 (OVL) and 84.21 (REL) at 12.6s latency, versus MusicGen-large's FAD 3.4 and 41.3s latency. In human studies, MAGNET-large scores within ~1.4 points of MusicGen-large on overall quality and within 0.1 on text relevance, while using only 180 decoding steps versus 1500 for MusicGen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MusicGen (the primary autoregressive baseline), MAGNET-small achieves a slightly higher FAD (3.3 vs 3.1) but a notably better KL divergence (1.12 vs 1.29) and identical CLAP score, while running 4.4× faster (4.0s vs 17.6s). MAGNET-large trades a small FAD increase (4.0 vs 3.4) for a 3.3× latency reduction (12.6s vs 41.3s) and still wins on KL (1.15 vs 1.23). Against AudioLDM2 (latent diffusion), MAGNET-small loses on FAD (3.3 vs 3.1) and CLAP (0.31 vs 0.31, tied) but wins on KL (1.12 vs 1.20) and human-study scores (81.67 vs 77.69 OVL; 83.21 vs 82.41 REL), with 4.5× lower latency. Against Mousai, MAGNET dominates on every metric (FAD 3.3 vs 7.5, KL 1.12 vs 1.59, OVL 81.67 vs 73.97) with 11× lower latency. Against MusicLM, MAGNET-large matches on FAD (4.0) and is within 1.3 points on OVL and 1.4 on REL, though MusicLM's latency is not reported. On text-to-audio (AudioCaps), MAGNET-large achieves FAD 2.36 and KL 1.64, comparable to AudioGen-large (FAD 1.77, KL 1.58) while being significantly faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGNET extends the masked generative sequence modeling paradigm from Mask-Predict (Ghazvininejad et al., 2019) and MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022) to the audio domain, adapting span-based iterative unmasking to multi-stream RVQ token sequences. It departs from SoundStorm (Borsos et al., 2023b), the closest prior audio work, by using a single non-autoregressive transformer rather than conditioning on semantic tokens from a separate autoregressive model, and by introducing audio-specific modifications: 60ms span masking, temporally restricted context for higher codebook levels (motivated by EnCodec receptive field analysis), and CFG annealing tied to the masking schedule. The external rescoring mechanism borrows from ASR n-best rescoring, and the Hybrid-MAGNET variant fuses AR and non-AR objectives in a single model via a custom attention mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAGNET demonstrates that a single non-autoregressive transformer can generate 30-second audio at 7× lower latency than autoregressive baselines while maintaining comparable quality, making real-time and interactive audio generation (e.g., DAW integration) practically feasible. The Hybrid-MAGNET approach provides a tunable quality/latency knob, and the finding that a 1-second AR prompt plus non-AR completion can outperform full AR on FAD suggests a promising path to near-lossless speedups. Key limitations include the inability to cache key-value states across decoding steps (re-encoding the full sequence each iteration) and a persistent small quality gap versus AR on some metrics, pointing to open questions around efficient non-AR inference and better rescoring strategies.
