## Problem

Diffusion models can generate photo-realistic images from text prompts but lack explicit control over the 3D structure (pose, distance, viewpoint) of objects in the generated images. This prevents automatic acquisition of ground-truth 3D annotations and limits the ability to craft training data with specific 3D configurations, which is a major bottleneck for training 3D-aware vision models at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241753/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D CAD models (from ShapeNet or Objaverse, ~30 per class), randomized camera extrinsic parameters (viewing direction sampled uniformly over azimuth, Gaussian over elevation/theta; distance from a predefined distribution), and a composite text prompt built from the class name, CAD model keywords, and LLaMA-generated scene descriptions. It produces 224×224 photo-realistic images (2,500 per class) paired with automatically derived ground-truth 3D annotations including pose, key points, and depth, obtained directly from the rendering camera parameters without any additional annotation step.

## Method

3D-DST injects 3D geometry control into Stable Diffusion via ControlNet by using Canny edge maps of CAD models rendered from diverse viewpoints and distances as visual prompts, avoiding the need to retrain the diffusion backbone. The rendered edge maps are compact enough for the diffusion model to process while preserving the 3D shape, pose, and scale information needed for explicit structural control. To maximize diversity, the method combines randomized camera sampling (uniform azimuth, Gaussian elevation/theta) with LLM-generated text prompts (via LLaMA) that describe varied backgrounds, colors, and weather conditions, preventing the generation of redundant or overly similar images. The 3D annotations (pose, key points, depth) are obtained for free from the known camera parameters used during rendering, eliminating the need for separate annotation. The generated dataset is then used to pre-train downstream models (DeiT, ConvNeXt, Swin, NeMo) before fine-tuning on real data, yielding consistent gains in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution classification and 3D pose estimation.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 (ID), 3D-DST pretraining improves DeiT-B top-1 accuracy by 3.83 points (84.28% → 88.11%) and DeiT-S by 4.40 points (84.56% → 88.96%), while the Text2Img baseline without 3D control yields mixed or negative results. On ImageNet-R (OOD), 3D-DST delivers a consistent average gain of 4.70 points across architectures, with DeiT-S improving by 7.04 points (49.61% → 56.65%). For 3D pose estimation on PASCAL3D+, pretraining on 3D-DST data boosts NeMo's Acc@π/18 by 5.39 points (57.12% → 62.51%) in the ID setting and by 3.50 points on Acc@π/6 in the OOD-CV setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Text2Img (He et al., 2023), which generates images via text-to-image diffusion without 3D control, 3D-DST wins decisively on both ID and OOD classification: on ImageNet-100, 3D-DST gains +3.83 points on DeiT-B while Text2Img actually degrades by −0.18; on ImageNet-R, 3D-DST averages +4.70 points versus Text2Img's +1.06. For 3D pose estimation, 3D-DST outperforms AugMix (Hendrycks et al., 2020) on PASCAL3D+ ID: with NeMo, 3D-DST improves Acc@π/18 by 5.39 points versus AugMix's 1.10 points, and with ResNet by 3.92 versus 1.29 points. On OOD-CV, 3D-DST also edges out AugMix for ResNet (Acc@π/6: +2.37 vs. +1.39), though NeMo with 3D-DST slightly underperforms NeMo with AugMix on OOD Acc@π/18 (−0.13). For 3D object detection on ARKitScenes, pretraining CubeRCNN (Brazil et al., 2023) on 3D-DST synthetic scenes improves both AP2D and AP3D over the baseline, demonstrating the method's applicability to multi-object scene understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ControlNet (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023), which injects 2D visual conditions (edges, segmentation, keypoints) into latent diffusion models, and on Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as the underlying generative engine. It departs from prior diffusion-based data augmentation work (e.g., He et al., ICLR 2023; Azizi et al., 2023) by replacing free-form text-only conditioning with explicit 3D geometry control via rendered edge maps, and from simulation-based 3D data generation (e.g., Kubric, Structured3D) by using diffusion models to produce photorealistic appearances rather than relying on fixed textures. The LLM-based prompt generation strategy (using LLaMA) is a novel component that has no direct precedent in the diffusion data-augmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to automatically produce 3D annotations (pose, key points, depth) alongside photorealistic images addresses a fundamental bottleneck in training 3D-aware vision models, and the approach scales naturally to large 3D repositories like Objaverse-XL (10M+ objects). A key open question is the 25% failure rate identified by human evaluation, particularly for uncommon viewpoints, which limits the method's reliability without the proposed K-fold consistency filter. Whether the 3D-structure control (as opposed to mere diversity) is the primary driver of the OOD gains remains partially unresolved, as the ablation comparing 3D-DST to rendering+BG3D shows the latter also yields substantial OOD improvements, suggesting that scene diversity and 3D pose control contribute somewhat independently.
