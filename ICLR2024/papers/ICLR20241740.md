## Problem

Existing LLM benchmarks either expand evaluation breadth across many tasks or increase difficulty, but lack careful design in ability modeling (hierarchical connections between skills), data fairness (training-data leakage and evolving knowledge), and evaluation criteria (cross-task comparability and automatic assessment of knowledge creation). KoLA addresses this by arguing that meticulous, thoughtful design across these three factors is essential for thorough, unbiased, and applicable LLM evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241740/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the 19 tasks consumes a different input format: relation-specific sentence templates with a masked tail entity (KM), documents with entity/concept/relation/event queries (KU), multi-hop questions over Wikipedia passages or knowledge graphs (KA), and a narrative context C paired with annotated event knowledge K (KC). Outputs are reported as standardized scores in [0, 100] computed via z-score normalization across all 28 evaluated models followed by min-max scaling, alongside task-specific metrics (EM, F1, Acc., BLEU, Rouge-L). The KC level additionally produces a self-contrast similarity score comparing the model's free completion T against its knowledge-grounded completion T_k and the human reference R.

## Method

KoLA organizes 19 tasks into a four-level cognitive taxonomy (Memorization, Understanding, Applying, Creating) inspired by Bloom's taxonomy, modeling the hierarchical dependencies between knowledge-related abilities. Data is drawn from two complementary sources: a known corpus (Wikipedia 2019 dump via Wikidata5M) assumed to be in every model's pre-training, and an evolving corpus of 500+ newly crawled articles refreshed every 90-day season to test unseen knowledge and detect hidden retrieval modules. For evaluation, a contrastive system is introduced: (1) standardized scores computed as z-scores per task then min-max scaled to [0, 100], enabling intuitive cross-task and cross-model comparison; and (2) a self-contrast metric for knowledge creation that contrasts a model's freely generated completion T with its knowledge-grounded completion T_k (given the same context C plus annotated events K) and the human reference R, using Rouge-L F1 to isolate knowledge faithfulness from writing style. The overall KC score averages three contrasts: ∂(T,R), ∂(T,T_k), and ∂(T_k,R), where the self-contrast term ∂(T,T_k) specifically measures whether the model's imagined events align with ground-truth knowledge, reducing hallucination sensitivity.

## Results

Across 28 LLMs evaluated on 19 tasks in four cognitive levels (KM, KU, KA, KC), GPT-4 achieves the highest overall standardized z-score of 2.33, followed by GPT-3.5-turbo (1.34) and InstructGPT davinci v2 175B* (1.12). Open-source models collectively average a z-score of −0.29, below the overall mean, with most declining in rank from Season 1 to Season 2. For non-aligned models, model size correlates strongly with Knowledge Memorization (Spearman ρ = 0.79), while for instruction-tuned models the size–ability correlation for Knowledge Applying increases from 0.02 to 0.53, suggesting alignment unlocks higher-level reasoning potential but may incur a memorization tax (KM correlation drops to 0.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4 dominates across all four levels, ranking 1st in KM, KU, and KA, and 2nd in KC (behind InstructGPT davinci v2), yielding the highest overall z-score (2.33) with a clear margin over GPT-3.5-turbo (1.34). Among open-source models, Tulu (7B) is the strongest overall (4th, z = 0.64), benefiting from strong KA performance (3rd) despite weak KC (9th); FLAN-UL2 (20B) ranks 6th overall with the 2nd-best KA score but a very weak KC rank (20th). Notably, larger non-aligned models like LLaMa (65B) and GLM (130B) rank only 11th and 17th overall, while smaller instruction-tuned models like Alpaca (7B) and Llama2-chat (7B) achieve competitive KC scores (7th and 10th) but lag in KM and KA, illustrating the alignment trade-off. Commercial models (GPT-4, GPT-3.5-turbo, InstructGPT davinci v2, Cohere-command) occupy the top 5 overall, whereas all 20 open-source models fall below the mean z-score, with the gap widening from Season 1 to Season 2. The self-contrast metric for KC shows a Spearman correlation of 0.61 with human-judged faithfulness, and removing it from the composite score drops correlation with overall quality by 32%, validating its utility over standard BLEU/Rouge alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

KoLA builds on the knowledge-probing tradition initiated by LAMA (Petroni et al., 2019) and COPEN (Peng et al., 2022), but departs from prior LLM benchmarks (MMLU, AGIEval, C-Eval) that emphasize breadth by covering many disciplines and tasks. Instead, KoLA organizes 19 tasks into a four-level cognitive hierarchy inspired by Bloom's taxonomy (Krathwohl, 2002), emphasizing the structural dependencies between knowledge abilities. The evolving data mechanism draws on time-sensitive evaluation ideas (Kasai et al., 2022; Dhingra et al., 2022), while the self-contrast metric for knowledge creation extends knowledge-grounded generation evaluation (Chen et al., 2020; Ghazvininejad et al., 2018) into a hallucination-sensitive automatic protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KoLA addresses a practical gap in LLM evaluation: the inability to diagnose *which* knowledge ability a model lacks and to fairly compare models across tasks with incomparable metrics. The evolving data mechanism (new season every 3 months) is a practical innovation that mitigates test-data contamination and tracks model progress over time. Key limitations include narrow coverage (19 English tasks, focused on entities/concepts/events) and the self-contrast metric's reliance on human-provided references, which may underestimate models that generate novel but reasonable knowledge. Open questions include whether the four-level hierarchy generalizes beyond English and whether the self-contrast approach can be extended to non-narrative knowledge creation.
