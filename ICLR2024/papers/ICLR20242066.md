## Problem

Multi-subject neural decoding is hindered by individual differences in functional topography, and existing hyperalignment-based methods suffer from three key limitations: mapping functions (linear, MLP, or CNN) cannot capture global long-range functional connectivities, model parameters scale linearly with the number of subjects, and the topological relationships between neural responses of different subjects to semantically similar stimuli remain inadequately characterized.

## Input / Output

The method takes fMRI BOLD signals from multiple subjects as primary input, patchified either via ROI-based segmentation (NSD, PCA-reduced to 268 dimensions) or 3D-CNN preprocessing (HCP, reducing 113×136×113 volumes to 392 patches of 512 dimensions). Visual stimuli (images paired with WordNet labels or MSCOCO captions) are fed into a frozen CLIP model to extract low-level (first-layer image) and high-level (last-layer averaged image+text) features that guide training. The output is a vector of multi-label semantic class probabilities over 53 (HCP) or 80 (NSD) categories, trained with cross-entropy loss.

## Method

CLIP-MUSED replaces per-subject mapping functions with a shared Transformer encoder that processes BOLD patches, augmented with two learnable subject-specific tokens (low-level and high-level) per subject, so only these tokens are subject-specific while all other parameters are shared across subjects. CLIP guidance is implemented via representational similarity analysis (RSA): the squared F-norm difference between the RSM of neural token representations and the RSM of CLIP features is minimized separately for low-level and high-level tokens, aligning the topological structure of the shared neural space with CLIP's hierarchical representation space. An orthogonal constraint (minimizing the F-norm of z_llv · z_hlv^T) ensures the two token representations encode complementary information. The final classifier concatenates both token representations and passes them through an MLP, with the total loss combining cross-entropy, the orthogonal penalty, and both RSA terms weighted by tunable hyperparameters.

## Results

On the NSD dataset (where subjects view completely non-overlapping stimuli), CLIP-MUSED achieves mAP 0.258, AUC 0.877, and Hamming distance 0.030, outperforming all single-subject and multi-subject baselines. On the HCP dataset (shared stimuli), it reaches mAP 0.373, AUC 0.581, and Hamming distance 0.283. The method surpasses the best single-subject baseline (SS-ViT: mAP 0.238, AUC 0.815 on NSD) by a substantial margin in AUC (+6.2 points) while matching its mAP, and outperforms the best multi-subject baseline (MS-EMB: mAP 0.220, AUC 0.825) by 3.8 mAP points and 5.2 AUC points on NSD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MS-EMB (Chehab et al., 2022), which learns a single subject-identity token per subject with a ViT backbone, CLIP-MUSED wins on all three metrics on both datasets: on NSD it improves mAP from 0.220 to 0.258 (+3.8), AUC from 0.825 to 0.877 (+5.2), and reduces Hamming from 0.035 to 0.030; on HCP it improves mAP from 0.368 to 0.373 and AUC from 0.572 to 0.581. The key advantage is that CLIP-MUSED uses two subject-specific tokens (low-level and high-level) guided by CLIP's hierarchical representation space via RSA, rather than a single unstructured embedding token, enabling it to capture distinct processing patterns for low-level and high-level features. Against SRM (Chen et al., 2015), a probabilistic hyperalignment method evaluated only on HCP (since it cannot handle non-overlapping stimuli), CLIP-MUSED achieves higher mAP (0.373 vs. 0.348) and AUC (0.581 vs. 0.534), though SRM reports a lower Hamming distance (0.204 vs. 0.283), likely reflecting its generative modeling approach. All improvements over baselines are statistically significant (t-test, p < 0.05, Holm-Bonferroni corrected).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hyperalignment tradition (Haxby et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2015) for multi-subject fMRI decoding by replacing per-subject linear or CNN mapping functions with a shared Transformer encoder augmented by subject-specific low-level and high-level tokens. It departs from prior category-based alignment (Li et al., 2020) and subject-embedding approaches (MS-EMB, Chehab et al., 2022) by using CLIP's hierarchical representation space—via representational similarity analysis—to explicitly guide how neural representations should be organized across subjects, rather than relying solely on classification loss to implicitly learn inter-subject structure. The use of learnable tokens to encode individual differences draws on the broader Transformer literature (Touvron et al., 2021; Naseer et al., 2021), while the orthogonal constraint separating low-level and high-level token representations is inspired by multi-label co-regularization (Niu et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck in multi-subject neural decoding: per-subject mapping functions scale linearly in parameters and cannot handle the common scenario where subjects view non-overlapping stimuli. By sharing all parameters except two small tokens per subject, CLIP-MUSED is in principle scalable to hundreds of subjects and could serve as a building block for a neural-decoding foundation model. Open questions include whether the approach extends to the harder task of visual stimulus reconstruction, how to efficiently learn tokens for new unseen subjects without retraining, and whether the CLIP-guided RSA loss generalizes to non-visual modalities or other cortical regions. The finding that CLIP guidance provides little benefit for single-subject models (Table D5) suggests the guidance is specifically valuable for resolving inter-subject variability rather than improving within-subject representation quality.
