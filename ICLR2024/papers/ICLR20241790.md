## Problem

Idempotence (stability to re-compression) and perceptual image compression were treated as unrelated topics in the literature, with researchers in each area barely citing the other. This paper reveals a tight theoretical connection: conditional generative codec is inherently idempotent, and conversely, an unconditional generative model with an idempotence constraint is provably equivalent to a conditional generative codec. This equivalence enables a new perceptual compression paradigm that requires no new model training, only a pre-trained MSE codec and a pre-trained unconditional generative model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241790/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB image (center-cropped from the short edge) and produces a compressed bitstream using a pre-trained MSE codec (Hyper or ELIC). The receiver inverts a pre-trained DDPM with an idempotence constraint to produce a perceptual reconstruction at the same bitrate. Because the bitstream is identical to the MSE codec's, the receiver can optionally decode either an MSE-optimized image or a perceptual image, enabling a perception-distortion trade-off without re-encoding.

## Method

The core contribution is Theorem 2, which proves that sampling from an unconditional distribution p_X subject to the idempotence constraint f(X̂)=Y is equivalent to sampling from the posterior p_{X|Y}, making the approach theoretically identical to conditional generative codec. Practically, the receiver solves min ‖f₀(X̂)−Y‖² s.t. X̂~q_X by inverting a pre-trained DDPM using Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS), with the idempotence constraint implemented either in the y-domain (constraining quantized symbols via straight-through estimator) or x-domain (constraining the MSE reconstruction difference). No new models are trained; the method reuses a pre-trained MSE codec (Hyper or ELIC) and a pre-trained DDPM. The x-domain constraint with DDPM+DPS achieves the best FID while keeping MSE within the theoretical 2× optimal bound, outperforming HiFiC, ILLM, Po-ELIC, and CDC across FFHQ, ImageNet, COCO, and CLIC 2020.

## Results

The proposed method with ELIC as base codec achieves the lowest BD-FID across all four evaluation datasets: −54.89 (FFHQ), −55.18 (ImageNet), −58.45 (COCO), and −46.52 (CLIC 2020). With Hyper as base codec, it achieves second-lowest BD-FID on all datasets (−54.14, −52.12, −56.70, −44.52). The MSE of the perceptual reconstruction stays within twice the base codec's MSE, and PSNR is within 3.01 dB of the base codec, confirming the theoretical rate-distortion-perception bound. On KID (a divergence-based metric), the proposed method also achieves the best BD-logKID on all datasets, while on LPIPS (an image-to-image distortion metric) it is outperformed by baselines trained with LPIPS loss, consistent with the perception-distortion trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (ELIC base) outperforms all conditional generative model-based perceptual codecs on BD-FID across all four datasets. Against HiFiC, it improves BD-FID by 6.5 (FFHQ), 10.7 (ImageNet), 13.6 (COCO), and 10.4 (CLIC); against ILLM, by 4.3, 7.0, 7.8, and 3.6 respectively. Even against re-trained versions of HiFiC* and ILLM* (trained on the same FFHQ/ImageNet data), the proposed method still leads by 3.0 and 2.6 BD-FID on FFHQ, and by 8.0 and 7.2 on ImageNet. Against Po-ELIC (which shares the same ELIC base codec), the proposed method gains 4.1–7.6 BD-FID across datasets. Against CDC (which also uses DDPM as its generative model), the advantage is largest at 11.1–13.4 BD-FID. On BD-PSNR, the proposed method trades some PSNR (−0.99 to −2.65 dB relative to Hyper) for substantially better perceptual quality, while remaining within the theoretical 3.01 dB bound of the base codec.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research threads: idempotence in image codecs (studied in the context of JPEG, JPEG2000, and invertible NIC) and perceptual image compression (dominated by conditional generative models like HiFiC, ILLM, and CDC). The key conceptual departure is showing that inverting a pre-trained unconditional generative model (DDPM) with an idempotence constraint is theoretically equivalent to training a conditional generative codec, drawing on the generative model inversion literature (DPS, PULSE, ILO) originally developed for super-resolution. Rather than training K conditional decoders for K bitrates as prior perceptual codecs do, the method repurposes a single unconditional generative model and a standard MSE codec (ELIC or Hyper) as off-the-shelf components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical equivalence between idempotence and perceptual quality provides a unifying lens that could reshape how both communities think about their problems, and the practical consequence—no new model training required—lowers the barrier to deploying perceptual codecs. However, the ~60-second per-image inversion time (versus ~0.1s for conditional decoders) is a substantial practical limitation that restricts near-term adoption, and the resolution is fixed to the generative model's training size. Open questions include accelerating the inversion (e.g., via learned inverters), extending to variable resolution, and whether the framework generalizes to video compression or other restoration tasks where the theoretical guarantees already extend.
