## Problem

Pretrained diffusion-based motion generation models are constrained by the paucity of annotated motion data, which is expensive to acquire and typically consists of short, single-person sequences. This data bottleneck leaves critical tasks—long-sequence generation, multi-person interaction, and fine-grained joint-level control—unaddressed or poorly handled by dedicated models. The paper asks whether a fixed motion diffusion prior can be composed in different ways to tackle these tasks in zero-shot or few-shot settings without retraining the base model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240349/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For long-sequence generation, the method takes a sequence of text prompts (one per interval) and per-interval lengths, producing a single coherent 3D motion of arbitrary duration (demonstrated up to 10 minutes) from a prior trained only on ≤10-second clips. For two-person generation, it accepts either a text prompt or a 1-second motion prefix and outputs two simultaneously generated, coordinated 3D motions in SMPL or HumanML3D joint representation. For fine-grained control, it takes a text prompt plus user-specified control signals (root trajectory on the xz-plane, or relative joint positions for any subset of end-effectors) and produces a full-body motion that semantically respects all specified constraints simultaneously.

## Method

The paper introduces three composition strategies over a frozen Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) prior. (1) DoubleTake performs sequential composition at inference time: a first take generates all intervals in a single batch with shared 'handshake' frames (prefix/suffix segments forced equal via per-frame averaging at each denoising step), and a second take refines transitions by partially re-noising the handshake region and denoising it under a soft mask that linearly interpolates between full generation and full preservation. (2) ComMDM performs parallel composition by inserting a single-layer transformer communication block after a chosen layer of two frozen MDM instances; the block receives intermediate activations from both actors and outputs a shared correction term (exploiting symmetry), trained on as few as 10–55 two-person samples with only the communication parameters updated. (3) DiffusionBlending performs model composition by first fine-tuning separate MDM copies for individual control tasks (masking noise on the target control features during the forward diffusion pass so the model learns to rely on them), then blending N fine-tuned models at sampling time via a weighted sum of their clean-motion predictions—generalizing classifier-free guidance from a conditioned/unconditioned pair to an arbitrary set of aligned diffusion models with weights summing to one.

## Results

On the BABEL dataset for long-sequence generation, DoubleTake achieves a motion FID of 1.04 vs. TEACH's 1.12, and substantially outperforms on transition quality (FID 1.88 vs. 3.86 at 70 frames; 3.45 vs. 7.93 at 30 frames). For two-person prefix completion on CMU-Mocap, ComMDM reduces root L2 error from 0.62 m (MRT) to 0.43 m at 3 seconds, and joint error from 0.65 m to 0.49 m. For fine-grained joint control on HumanML3D, DiffusionBlending achieves FID 0.22 (left wrist + trajectory) and 0.18 (left wrist + right foot), compared to MDM inpainting's 1.18 and 0.81 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TEACH (Athanasiou et al., 2022) on BABEL, DoubleTake wins on FID for both motion (1.04 vs. 1.12) and transitions (1.88 vs. 3.86 at 70 frames; 3.45 vs. 7.93 at 30 frames), though TEACH edges ahead on R-precision (0.46 vs. 0.43) and MultiModal-Dist (7.14 vs. 7.39). A user study confirms DoubleTake is preferred in 82–85% of comparisons. Against MRT (Wang et al., 2021) on CMU-Mocap two-person prefix completion, ComMDM reduces 3-second root error from 0.62 m to 0.43 m and joint error from 0.65 m to 0.49 m, and is preferred over MRT in 76.7–79.2% of user study comparisons. Against the original MDM inpainting (Tevet et al., 2023) for joint/trajectory control on HumanML3D, DiffusionBlending dramatically improves FID (0.22 vs. 1.18 for left wrist + trajectory; 0.18 vs. 0.81 for left wrist + right foot) while also improving R-precision (0.67 vs. 0.65) and diversity (9.33 vs. 8.81), showing that fine-tuning plus model composition is essential for semantically coherent control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions a pretrained Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) as a frozen generative prior and introduces three composition mechanisms—sequential (DoubleTake), parallel (ComMDM), and model (DiffusionBlending)—to extend it to tasks it was never trained for. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of training dedicated models per task (TEACH for long sequences, MRT for two-person) by showing that a single prior, combined with lightweight composition layers or inference-time tricks, can cover multiple tasks in zero- or few-shot settings. The DiffusionBlending technique directly generalizes classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) from interpolating conditioned/unconditioned models to composing arbitrary pairs of fine-tuned diffusion models, while DoubleTake's soft-masking and handshake mechanism extends the inpainting principle from MDM to multi-interval temporal composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for a field bottlenecked by expensive, small motion-capture datasets: DoubleTake generates 10-minute coherent motions zero-shot from a 10-second-trained model, and ComMDM coordinates two-person interaction from as few as a dozen examples. However, the approach inherits the base model's quality ceiling, ComMDM generalizes only to interaction types seen during training, and physical contact between two people remains unaddressed. The broader conceptual contribution—that a diffusion prior acts as a proxy for the motion manifold and composition only needs to handle inter-part coordination—suggests a general recipe applicable beyond motion to other generation domains where data is scarce.
