## Problem

Existing LLM pruning methods either require expensive retraining or computationally intensive second-order weight updates, making them impractical for billion-parameter models. Meanwhile, the standard magnitude pruning baseline that works well on smaller networks fails dramatically on LLMs even at low sparsity levels, suggesting that LLMs are substantially harder to prune directly. The paper asks whether a simple, retraining-free pruning criterion can effectively identify sparse sub-networks within pretrained LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained LLM's weight matrices and a small calibration set (128 sequences of fixed context length from C4) to estimate per-channel input activation L2 norms via a single forward pass. It outputs pruned weight matrices with a user-specified sparsity ratio applied uniformly across all linear layers (skipping the embedding and final classification head, which together account for ~1% of parameters). The pruned weights are used as-is with no modification to surviving weights, and the method supports both unstructured sparsity and structured N:M patterns (4:8, 2:4) compatible with NVIDIA sparse tensor cores.

## Method

Wanda scores each weight W_ij by the product of its magnitude and the L2 norm of the corresponding input activation channel, S_ij = |W_ij| · ||X_j||_2, estimated from calibration data. Weights are then compared and pruned on a per-output basis (within each row of the weight matrix) rather than per-layer or globally, which the authors find is critical for LLMs but not for image classifiers. This metric is motivated by the emergent large-magnitude outlier features in LLMs: a small weight connected to a high-activation channel can be far more important than a large weight connected to a low-activation channel. Mathematically, Wanda's metric is an exact reduction of SparseGPT's second-order pruning metric when the Hessian damping λ is set to zero and only diagonal Hessian elements are retained, eliminating all matrix inverse computations. The entire procedure executes in a single forward pass with no weight update, making it ~300× faster than SparseGPT while achieving comparable perplexity at 50% unstructured sparsity.

## Results

On 7 zero-shot tasks, Wanda at 50% unstructured sparsity achieves 54.21% mean accuracy on LLaMA-7B (vs. 46.94% for magnitude pruning and 54.94% for SparseGPT), and 66.67% on LLaMA-65B (vs. 62.74% magnitude, 66.30% SparseGPT), nearly matching the dense model (66.97%). On WikiText perplexity, Wanda achieves 7.26 on LLaMA-7B at 50% sparsity (vs. 17.29 for magnitude pruning and 7.22 for SparseGPT), and 3.98 on LLaMA-2-70B (matching SparseGPT exactly). Wanda's pruning metric computation is ~300× faster than SparseGPT (0.54s vs. 203.1s for LLaMA-7B), and structured 2:4 sparsity yields ~1.6× inference speedup on linear layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wanda decisively outperforms magnitude pruning across all model sizes and sparsity levels: on LLaMA-7B at 50% unstructured sparsity, Wanda achieves 54.21% zero-shot accuracy vs. 46.94% for magnitude (a 7.3-point gap), and 7.26 vs. 17.29 WikiText perplexity. Against SparseGPT (the prior best LLM pruning method), Wanda is highly competitive without any weight update: it matches or slightly exceeds SparseGPT on larger models (e.g., 66.67% vs. 66.30% on LLaMA-65B zero-shot; 4.57 vs. 4.57 perplexity on LLaMA-65B) while being ~300× faster to compute. On smaller models (LLaMA-7B), SparseGPT has a slight edge (54.94% vs. 54.21% zero-shot; 7.22 vs. 7.26 perplexity), but the gap narrows with model scale. Wanda also overwhelmingly outperforms activation-based pruning methods (Molchanov et al., Babaeizadeh et al., Dubey et al.), which produce perplexities of 16–100,000+ on LLaMA-7B at 50% sparsity versus Wanda's 7.26. Similarly, BERT-era pruning methods adapted to LLMs (SNIP, BERT-LTH, Movement, Platon, PINS) all fail catastrophically (perplexities 17–349 without fine-tuning), while Wanda achieves 7.26 without any fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Wanda is positioned as a simplified, weight-update-free variant of SparseGPT, with the paper showing an exact mathematical reduction from SparseGPT's second-order pruning metric to Wanda's by setting the Hessian dampening to zero and retaining only diagonal elements. It also augments the classical magnitude pruning criterion (Han et al., 2015) by incorporating input activation norms, motivated by the emergent large-magnitude outlier features observed in LLMs (Dettmers et al., 2022). The authors further draw a conceptual lineage to Optimal Brain Damage (LeCun et al., 1989), framing Wanda as a per-neuron renaissance of OBD applied with local output reconstruction rather than a global training objective. The per-output comparison group is a novel grouping strategy that departs from the standard layer-wise or global comparisons used in prior pruning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Wanda provides a practically important tool: a 300x faster pruning procedure than SparseGPT that requires no retraining, no weight updates, and only a single forward pass, making it accessible for real-time or repeated pruning scenarios such as sparse training. The finding that per-output (rather than per-layer) comparison is critical for LLMs but not for image classifiers suggests a structural property unique to large language models that warrants further investigation. Limitations include significant performance degradation at 80% sparsity and SparseGPT still outperforming Wanda on structured 2:4 sparsity for smaller models. Open questions include whether Wanda's speed advantage makes it suitable for iterative sparse training loops and whether the per-output pruning insight generalizes to other emergent properties of LLMs.
