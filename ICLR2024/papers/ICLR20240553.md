## Problem

Discriminative classifiers dominate object recognition but are prone to shortcut learning (e.g., texture bias), while generative models are hypothesized to support more robust inference yet were previously impossible to evaluate as classifiers. This paper asks whether zero-shot classification decisions extracted from pre-trained text-to-image models can match or exceed discriminative models in human-like object recognition behavior on challenging out-of-distribution tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240553/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The classifier takes a single RGB test image (resized to 64×64 for Imagen, 256×256 for Parti, 512×512 for Stable Diffusion) and a set of candidate class labels, each converted to a text prompt via the template 'A bad photo of a <class>.' The output is a single class label: for diffusion models, the class whose reconstruction minimizes weighted L2 distance to the test image; for Parti, the class with the highest exact conditional likelihood. No training or fine-tuning is performed—the generative models are used entirely zero-shot, with a single prompt per class rather than an ensemble.

## Method

The paper turns three pre-trained text-to-image models (Imagen, Stable Diffusion, Parti) into zero-shot classifiers by estimating the conditional likelihood p(x|y_k) for each candidate class. For diffusion models, this is approximated via the diffusion variational lower bound: the test image is noised at a random timestep, then reconstructed conditioned on each class prompt, and the class with the minimum weighted L2 reconstruction error (w_t = exp(-7t)) is selected. For Parti, an autoregressive model, classification is a single forward pass computing the exact conditional likelihood. The key empirical finding is that the denoising process inherently emphasizes low-frequency spatial information (shape over texture), producing a record-breaking 99% shape bias for Imagen that matches human observers. A controlled experiment adding diffusion-style noise augmentation to a standard ResNet-50 increases its shape bias from 21% to 78%, confirming that diffusion training is the causal factor behind the shape bias rather than generative architecture per se.

## Results

On the model-vs-human benchmark (17 OOD datasets), Imagen achieves a record-breaking 99% shape bias (vs. 96% human average), 0.71 OOD accuracy, and 0.31 error consistency with humans — the highest of any model tested. Stable Diffusion reaches 93% shape bias and 0.69 OOD accuracy, while Parti achieves 92% shape bias and 0.58 OOD accuracy. All three generative classifiers are zero-shot (neither trained nor designed for classification), yet Imagen surpasses the previous state-of-the-art ViT-22B (87% shape bias, 0.26 error consistency) on both shape bias and human-aligned error patterns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (the primary zero-shot baseline), all three generative classifiers substantially outperform on shape bias (99/93/92% vs. 80% for CLIP-1prompt, 57% for CLIP-80prompts) and OOD accuracy (0.71/0.69/0.58 vs. 0.55 for CLIP-1prompt); CLIP-80prompts matches Imagen on OOD accuracy (0.71) but has far lower shape bias. Against ViT-22B (the strongest discriminative baseline), Imagen wins on shape bias (99% vs. 87%) and error consistency (0.31 vs. 0.26), but loses on OOD accuracy (0.71 vs. 0.80), where ViT-22B achieves super-human performance. Against standard discriminative models (ViT-L, RN-50), generative classifiers dominate on shape bias (92–99% vs. 21–42%) and error consistency (Imagen 0.31 vs. 0.21), while ViT-L slightly edges out Parti on OOD accuracy (0.73 vs. 0.58). The ResNet-50 trained with diffusion noise augmentation (78% shape bias) provides causal evidence that the denoising training objective — not generative architecture per se — drives the shape bias increase, though it still falls well short of Imagen's 99%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the method of Clark & Jaini (2023), who first showed that text-to-image diffusion models can be repurposed as zero-shot classifiers via the diffusion variational lower bound, by applying it to a comprehensive model-vs-human evaluation framework (Geirhos et al., 2021) spanning 17 out-of-distribution datasets and 90 human observers. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the long-standing cognitive science debate on discriminative versus generative models of vision (Helmholtz; DiCarlo et al., 2021) and the recent machine learning literature on shortcut learning and shape bias (Geirhos et al., 2019; Baker et al., 2018). Unlike prior work that studied shape bias in discriminatively trained networks or required explicit training interventions, this paper demonstrates that the property emerges naturally from generative pre-training, connecting it to the spatial-frequency emphasis inherent in diffusion-style denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes zero-shot generative classifiers as leading behavioral models of human object recognition, with Imagen achieving 99% shape bias (exceeding the human average of 96%) and near-human OOD accuracy without any classification training. The finding that adding diffusion-style noise augmentation to a standard ResNet-50 boosts shape bias from 21% to 78% provides a simple, practically useful technique and a causal link between denoising training and low-frequency emphasis. Key limitations include the computational infeasibility of the approach as a practical classifier and the imperfect comparability of models with different architectures, training data, and language encoders. Open questions include how biological vision integrates generative and discriminative processes, the specific role of language cross-attention in these emergent properties, and whether denoising diffusion training is truly the critical component or whether other aspects of generative pre-training contribute.
