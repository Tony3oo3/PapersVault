## Problem

Existing model fusion methods based on Optimal Transport (OTFusion) and vanilla weight averaging are designed for simple architectures (MLPs, CNNs, ResNets) and fail to handle the complex information streams inherent to Transformers—specifically residual connections, multi-head self-attention, and layer normalization. This paper asks whether the capabilities of independently-trained Transformer models can be combined into a single model that preserves and even exceeds the performance of the individual parents, without requiring retraining from scratch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241157/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two or more independently-trained Transformer models (ViT for vision, BERT for NLP) of the same depth but potentially different widths, along with a small calibration set of inputs to compute activations for alignment. It produces a single fused Transformer model that can be used immediately (one-shot) or after a brief finetuning pass. The fusion step itself runs in seconds on a CPU, and the output model has the same architecture and parameter count as the anchor model, reducing inference cost by a factor of n relative to ensembling.

## Method

The paper introduces a Transportation Map Flow Graph that formalizes how transport maps propagate through each Transformer component, enabling a divide-and-conquer fusion strategy. For residual connections, the outgoing TM is computed as a weighted combination of the current-layer and residual-branch TMs (with averaging found optimal). For multi-head attention, a cross-head alignment is proposed: Q, K, V weights are concatenated across the output dimension before OTFusion is applied, and only the V-branch TM is propagated to the output projection W^O. A key empirical finding is that soft alignment via the Sinkhorn algorithm (with a tuned regularization parameter) consistently outperforms hard alignment (EMD/permutation) for Transformers, reversing the observation from simpler architectures. The method also handles layer normalization (no TM needed, parameters aligned via incoming TM), embeddings (patch-embedding TM prioritized over class-token TM), and supports heterogeneous fusion of models with different widths.

## Results

After finetuning, the fused ViT model (weights-based soft alignment) outperforms the best individual parent by +0.86% on CIFAR-100 (65.80% vs 64.94%) with two models and +1.04% with six models (65.98% vs 64.94%). On ImageNet-1K, the fused model reaches 75.80% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best parent (75.33%) while Vanilla Fusion collapses to 67.83%. In the one-shot setting on CIFAR-10, activations-based soft alignment achieves 60.87% accuracy versus 7.59% for Vanilla Fusion, a gain of +53.28 points. Heterogeneous fusion (different model widths) yields a consistent +0.81 to +0.93% absolute gain over the smaller anchor model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OTFusion (soft, weights-based) against three baselines: Vanilla Fusion (one-to-one weight averaging), individual converged parent models, and model ensembling. Vanilla Fusion catastrophically fails in the one-shot setting, dropping to 7.59% on CIFAR-10 (vs ~92% for parents) and only recovering to 64.91% after finetuning on CIFAR-100—below the best parent. OTFusion with soft alignment consistently outperforms individual parents after finetuning: +0.86% (2 models) and +1.04% (6 models) on CIFAR-100, and +0.47% on ImageNet-1K, while maintaining ×1 computational cost. Ensembling achieves higher absolute accuracy (e.g., 68.04% vs 65.80% on CIFAR-100) but at ×2 to ×6 computational overhead. A key finding is that soft (Sinkhorn) alignment outperforms hard (EMD) alignment for Transformers, reversing the trend observed for CNNs and MLPs in the original OTFusion paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends OTFusion (Singh & Jaggi, 2020), which aligned and fused networks layer-by-layer via Optimal Transport, to the Transformer architecture where the standard layerwise TM propagation breaks down due to residual streams, multi-head attention, and normalization layers. It introduces a Transportation Map Flow Graph formalism to handle the complex TM dependencies across these components, and proposes cross-head alignment for multi-head self-attention. The work departs from prior OTFusion findings by showing that soft (Sinkhorn) alignment outperforms hard (EMD) alignment for Transformers, inverting the conclusion drawn for CNNs and ResNets. It also enables heterogeneous fusion of different-width models, a capability absent in vanilla fusion and most prior OT-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Transformers become the dominant architecture across vision and NLP, a principled fusion method that respects their internal structure (attention heads, residual streams, normalization) is practically important for model compression and knowledge transfer without retraining from scratch. The key limitation is that the method cannot handle models of different depths, only different widths, and one-shot performance remains substantially below individual parents (e.g., ~61% vs. ~92% on CIFAR10), requiring finetuning to realize gains. Open questions include extending to heterogeneous depth, understanding why soft alignment is specifically beneficial for the non-convex Transformer landscape, and scaling the approach to much larger foundation models where the cost of training individual parents is prohibitive.
