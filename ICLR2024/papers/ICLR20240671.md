## Problem

Open-world entity segmentation models like SAM depend on massive human-annotated datasets (e.g., 1 billion masks in SA-1B, each taking 14–34 seconds to annotate), making them expensive and difficult to scale. Existing self-supervised representation learners produce holistic image features that cannot distinguish individual entities or capture region-level structure, so they cannot be directly applied to open-world segmentation. The paper asks whether a high-quality open-world segmentation model can be trained purely from unlabeled raw images without any human annotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only raw unlabeled RGB images (resized to 1024×1024 during pseudo-label generation) with no ground-truth masks or class labels. It produces segmentation masks for both whole entities and their constituent parts/subparts, organized into a hierarchical forest structure where roots are complete entities and descendants are progressively finer parts. The hierarchy is encoded as a binary N×N ancestor-relation matrix predicted alongside the masks, enabling variable-depth part decomposition without a fixed number of levels.

## Method

SOHES operates in three self-evolving phases. In self-exploration, DINO ViT-B/8 patch features are merged bottom-up via agglomerative clustering at multiple cosine-similarity thresholds to generate a pool of candidate regions at diverse granularities; small regions are re-clustered at a finer local scale, boundaries are refined with CascadePSP, and a pixel-coverage test assigns each mask a position in a hierarchical forest. In self-instruction, a Mask2Former model (DINO ViT backbone + ViT-Adapter) is trained on these pseudo-labels, augmented with a novel ancestor prediction head that learns the N×N binary hierarchy matrix via two asymmetric linear projections on query features with a BCE loss. In self-correction, a teacher-student mutual-learning loop refines the model: the teacher (EMA of the student) generates improved pseudo-labels filtered by a dynamic confidence threshold that scales with mask area to prevent small-entity under-representation, and the student is trained jointly on both the initial and teacher-generated pseudo-labels.

## Results

SOHES achieves state-of-the-art self-supervised open-world entity segmentation, reaching 33.3 AR on SA-1B, 33.5 AR on EntitySeg, and 30.5 AR on COCO using only 2% unlabeled images (no human annotations). It outperforms the prior best self-supervised method HASSOD by +7.3 AR on SA-1B and EntitySeg, and +6.6 AR on LVIS. Notably, SOHES surpasses the fully supervised SAM on PartImageNet (36.0 vs. 28.3 AR) and nearly matches SAM on PACO (17.1 vs. 18.1 AR), while closing the self-supervised-to-supervised gap on SA-1B by 37% relative.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOHES consistently outperforms all three self-supervised baselines across every evaluation dataset. Against the prior state-of-the-art HASSOD, SOHES gains +7.3 AR on SA-1B (33.3 vs. 26.0) and EntitySeg (33.5 vs. 26.2), +6.6 AR on LVIS (29.1 vs. 22.5), and +5.7 AR on PACO (17.1 vs. 11.4). Against CutLER, the improvements are even larger on part-segmentation datasets: SOHES nearly doubles CutLER's PACO score (17.1 vs. 8.9) and leads on PartImageNet (36.0 vs. 28.7). Against the supervised SAM, SOHES closes the gap substantially on whole-entity datasets (e.g., 33.3 vs. 60.8 AR on SA-1B, a 37% relative reduction) but actually surpasses SAM on PartImageNet (36.0 vs. 28.3 AR) due to its ability to predict more fine-grained parts and subparts. FreeSOLO is far behind all other methods, confirming that the multi-phase pseudo-labeling paradigm is essential for competitive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOHES extends the self-supervised object discovery paradigm established by Siméoni et al. (2023), CutLER (Wang et al., 2023), and HASSOD (Cao et al., 2023), which all follow a pseudo-label discovery → model training pipeline. The key departures are: (1) a global-to-local agglomerative clustering strategy for pseudo-label generation that captures multi-scale entities, (2) a novel ancestor prediction head attached to Mask2Former that learns hierarchical entity-part relations (unlike prior flat segmentation), and (3) a dynamic-threshold teacher-student correction phase adapted from semi-supervised learning (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017). The backbone is DINO ViT-B/8 throughout, and the segmentation head is Mask2Former with ViT-Adapter for multi-scale features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOHES substantially narrows the gap between self-supervised and supervised open-world segmentation (e.g., 33.3 vs 60.8 AR on SA-1B) while using only 2% of SAM's training images and zero human annotations, making high-quality entity segmentation far more accessible. The hierarchical structure (whole entities → parts → subparts) is a meaningful extension that enables multi-granularity scene understanding, and the fact that SOHES outperforms SAM on PartImageNet part segmentation suggests self-supervised hierarchical learning can capture fine-grained structure that supervised models miss. Limitations include failure on discontinuous/occluded entities, imprecise text segmentation, and missed blurry backgrounds. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video, whether the hierarchical structure can be made more robust to occlusion, and whether the 2%-of-SAM data budget can be further reduced.
