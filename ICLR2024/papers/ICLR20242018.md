## Problem

Existing retrosynthetic planners evaluate reaction quality using single-step prediction probabilities, which are biased toward high-frequency reactions rather than genuinely high-yield or practically useful ones. Annotating every reaction along a route with real quality data (e.g., experimental yield or expert rating) is prohibitively expensive given route lengths of 2–8 steps. The paper addresses the trade-off between query cost and route quality by learning when to actively request quality annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target molecule (as a molecular graph or SMILES), a set of candidate reactions from a template-based single-step model (top-50 per step, max 100 total calls), and optionally discrete reaction quality annotations (continuous values in [0,1] from a surrogate yield-prediction model, or coarse 0–10 ratings from a chemist). It outputs a complete retrosynthetic route to building blocks (eMolecules), a binary query decision per reaction indicating whether its quality was annotated, and a normalized route quality score computed as the cumulative product of annotated reaction qualities. The critic consumes molecule embeddings from a pretrained estimator concatenated with a bucketed reaction-quality embedding (18 bins, 512-dim).

## Method

ARP formulates active retrosynthetic planning as an MDP solved with TD3 actor-critic RL. The actor π_φ maps each candidate reaction to a binary query decision (annotate or skip), while the critic Q_θ estimates a molecule's value conditioned on its preceding reaction quality, enabling preference for high-quality branches. The critic is designed in two architectures: Architecture A wraps a frozen offline estimator (e.g., Retro*'s value network) with a readout head, and Architecture B jointly trains an online estimator (e.g., GRASP's) with the critic. At inference, the actor and critic are embedded in MCTS over an AND-OR search tree using UCT for selection, AND-OR stump expansion, and discounted moving-average updates. The reward function combines normalized route quality (cumulative product of reaction qualities) with a per-query cost penalty c, and a hyperparameter λ stabilizes training when route quality is near zero. A binning strategy discretizes continuous reaction qualities into 18 equal-frequency buckets with learned embeddings, and a special 'missing' embedding is used when no query is made.

## Results

On the USPTO-50k benchmark, GRASP with ARP achieves the best normalized route quality of 0.711 while reducing the query rate to 83.4% (vs. 100% for all baselines), outperforming the best baseline Retro* (0.705) by 6.2% in route quality. On the expert dataset, GRASP with ARP reaches 0.836 normalized route quality at a 90.8% query rate, surpassing Retro* (0.787) by 4.9%. Success rates remain competitive: GRASP with ARP achieves 86.5% (benchmark) and 80.6% (expert), matching or exceeding the original GRASP. The double-blind chemist evaluation further confirms the advantage, with ARP+GRASP routes rated 9.1/10 versus 6.9/10 for GRASP alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All baselines (HgSearch, Retro*, Retro*+, EG-MCTS, GRASP) operate at a 100% query rate, meaning they rely on reaction quality annotations for every reaction encountered. ARP with GRASP wins on normalized route quality across both datasets: 0.711 vs. 0.602 (GRASP) and 0.705 (Retro*) on the benchmark, and 0.836 vs. 0.666 (GRASP) and 0.787 (Retro*) on the expert set, while simultaneously reducing the query rate by 12.8% and 9.2% respectively. ARP with Retro*+ achieves 0.767 (benchmark) and 0.821 (expert) route quality, also surpassing all baselines. On success rate, GRASP with ARP (86.5%/80.6%) marginally exceeds the original GRASP (86.0%/79.5%), while Retro*+ with ARP (73.0%/74.3%) slightly trails the original Retro*+ (72.5%/68.5%) on the benchmark but leads on the expert set. The ablation with random query (Tables 2–3) confirms that the learned actor selects more informative reactions than a fixed random rate, yielding 0.711 vs. 0.695 (benchmark) and 0.836 vs. 0.772 (expert) at comparable query rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the retrosynthetic planning line of work (Retro*, GRASP, Retro*+) by introducing an active query mechanism that decides which reactions to annotate with true quality information, rather than relying on single-step model probabilities as a proxy for reaction quality. It draws on active reinforcement learning (AMRL, TAMER) for the actor-critic query decision, and integrates with both offline planners (Retro*, Retro*+) via a pretrained estimator in the critic and online planners (GRASP) via joint training. The key conceptual departure is treating reaction quality as an expensive observation that must be selectively queried, framing retrosynthetic planning as a cost-aware MDP with a binary query action alongside the standard reaction selection action.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing planners optimize for reaction feasibility/frequency rather than actual chemical quality (yield, work-up difficulty), producing routes that may be short but impractical. The active query mechanism offers a deployable path toward integrating chemist-in-the-loop annotations (e.g., in platforms like SYNTHIA) without requiring exhaustive lab verification. Open questions include the reliance on a surrogate model as a stand-in for true chemist annotations, the observed trade-off between success rate and route quality (where optimizing one can suppress the other), and the small benchmark scale (178 molecules). The authors note multi-objective extensions (green chemistry, cost) as future work.
