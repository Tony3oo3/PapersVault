## Problem

Graph transformers offer greater theoretical expressivity than message-passing GNNs but suffer from quadratic complexity, while existing linear-complexity GTs underperform GNN baselines on popular benchmarks. The paper addresses how to achieve high polynomial expressivity in a graph transformer while maintaining linear complexity with respect to graph size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240736/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a graph G=(V,E) with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and produces node representations Y ∈ R^{n×d} for node classification. A key design choice is that the model learns a degree-2^L polynomial on node features (where L is the number of layers) whose coefficients are determined by attention scores, eliminating the need for expensive positional or structural encodings. The architecture supports both full-batch training on smaller graphs and mini-batch training on graphs with millions of nodes.

## Method

Polynormer is built on a base model where each layer computes X^(l) = (W^(l)X^(l-1)) ⊙ (X^(l-1) + B^(l)), a Hadamard product that provably yields 2^L-polynomial expressivity (Theorem 3.3). To enforce permutation equivariance, W is replaced by either a sparse GAT attention matrix A (local attention) or a linearized global attention using a sigmoid kernel trick: σ(Q)σ(K^T)V / (σ(Q)Σ_i σ(K_i^T)), which avoids the dense n×n attention matrix. The architecture adopts a local-to-global scheme where L1 local attention layers are applied sequentially before L2 global attention layers, rather than the parallel local-and-global scheme used by prior GTs. A shared bias vector β (scaled by sigmoid) controls lower-degree monomial coefficients, and an optional ReLU activation (Polynormer-r) introduces additional nonlinearity. This design achieves linear O(nd²) complexity per layer while capturing both local and global high-order feature interactions.

## Results

On 5 homophilic datasets, Polynormer (without ReLU) achieves 93.18% on Computer, 96.11% on Photo, 95.51% on CS, 97.22% on Physics, and 79.53% on WikiCS, outperforming all 16 baselines on 3 of 5 datasets. On 5 heterophilic datasets, Polynormer-r achieves up to 97.46% ROC AUC on minesweeper (vs. 93.91% for GAT-sep, +3.55%) and 92.55% accuracy on roman-empire (vs. 91.23% for DIR-GNN). On large-scale graphs, Polynormer-r reaches 83.82% on ogbn-products (2.4M nodes) and 86.10% on pokec (1.6M nodes), surpassing the best baseline (GOAT at 82.00% on ogbn-products) by up to 4.06%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Polynormer outperforms all 16 baselines on Computer (93.18% vs. 92.03% OrderedGNN, +1.15%), Photo (96.11% vs. 95.49% NAGphormer, +0.62%), and WikiCS (79.53% vs. 79.01% OrderedGNN, +0.52%), but is slightly behind NAGphormer on CS (95.51% vs. 95.75%) and Physics (97.22% vs. 97.34%). On heterophilic graphs, Polynormer dominates all 5 datasets, with the largest margin on minesweeper (96.96% ROC AUC vs. 93.91% GAT-sep, +3.05%) and roman-empire (92.13% vs. 91.23% DIR-GNN, +0.90%), while all GT baselines (GraphGPS, NAGphormer, Exphormer, NodeFormer, DIFFormer, GOAT) underperform SOTA GNNs on every heterophilic dataset. On large-scale graphs, Polynormer-r achieves 83.82% on ogbn-products (vs. 82.00% GOAT, +1.82%) and 86.10% on pokec (vs. 82.04% LINKX, +4.06%), while GraphGPS and Exphormer run out of memory on both ogbn-products and pokec. The polynomial-expressive design allows Polynormer to surpass baselines even without nonlinear activation functions, with ReLU (Polynormer-r) providing additional gains of 0.3–1.0% across datasets. Notably, tGNN (the closest prior polynomial-based GNN) performs poorly (83.40% on Computer), confirming that the attention-controlled polynomial coefficients in Polynormer are critical for practical expressivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Polynormer sits at the intersection of two research threads: polynomial neural networks (P-nets, tGNN) that explicitly learn high-degree feature interactions, and linear-complexity graph transformers (GraphGPS, NodeFormer, Exphormer, GOAT) that approximate or sparsify self-attention for scalability. The key departure is that prior linear GTs linearize the attention matrix itself (via kernels or sampling) while retaining the standard softmax-attention formulation, whereas Polynormer reinterprets attention scores as coefficients of a polynomial learned via Hadamard products, yielding 2^L-degree expressivity. The local-to-global (sequential) attention scheme also contrasts with the local-and-global (parallel) scheme used by most prior GTs, which the paper shows to be inferior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Polynormer provides a principled answer to the expressivity-scalability tension in graph transformers: by making attention scores control polynomial coefficients rather than merely weighting value vectors, the model captures high-order global feature interactions at linear cost. The finding that the model outperforms SOTA GNNs and GTs even without nonlinear activations suggests that polynomial structure, not activation-induced nonlinearity, is the primary driver of GT expressivity on graphs. Open questions include whether the 2^L expressivity bound is tight in practice, whether the local-to-global scheme generalizes to other tasks (link prediction, graph classification), and whether the sigmoid kernel can be replaced by richer kernels without sacrificing training stability on million-node graphs.
