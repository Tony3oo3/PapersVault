## Problem

Using proprietary LLMs like GPT-4 as evaluators for long-form text is unreliable for practitioners due to closed-source opacity, uncontrolled versioning, and prohibitive costs (e.g., >$2000 for a single evaluation run). Moreover, existing open-source evaluators are confined to coarse-grained, single-dimensional metrics (e.g., helpfulness/harmlessness) and cannot handle user-defined, fine-grained score rubrics. The paper asks whether an open-source LLM can match GPT-4's evaluation capability when accompanied by appropriate reference materials.

## Input / Output

The evaluator takes four input components: a user instruction, a response to evaluate, a reference answer (guaranteed score-5 response), and a user-defined score rubric specifying criteria plus descriptions for each score level 1–5. The model produces a detailed chain-of-thought feedback explaining the scoring rationale, followed by a '[RESULT]' delimiter and an integer score from 1 to 5. The score rubric is fully customizable (e.g., 'child-readability', 'formal enough to send to my boss'), and the reference answer allows the evaluator to leverage mutual information between the reference and the response rather than solving the instruction independently.

## Method

The authors construct the FEEDBACK COLLECTION dataset (1K fine-grained score rubrics, 20K instructions, 100K response-feedback pairs) through a four-step GPT-4 augmentation pipeline: curating 50 seed rubrics, expanding to 1K rubrics via iterative brainstorming and paraphrasing, generating rubric-relevant instructions, and sequentially generating score-calibrated responses with feedback. They then fine-tune Llama-2-Chat-13B using chain-of-thought fine-tuning, where the model learns to generate feedback first and then the score, with a '[RESULT]' token separating the two to prevent degeneration. A key design insight is that including both the score rubric and reference answer as reference materials is essential for inducing fine-grained evaluation capability, as ablations show the largest performance drop when the reference answer is removed. The dataset enforces uniform score distribution (20K instances per score level) and uniform response length across scores to prevent decision and length bias during training.

## Results

PROMETHEUS 13B achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.897 with human evaluators on 45 customized score rubrics, surpassing GPT-4 (0.882) and greatly outperforming GPT-3.5-Turbo (0.392). On the FEEDBACK BENCH with unseen score rubrics, PROMETHEUS 13B reaches a Pearson correlation of 0.860 with GPT-4-0613, outperforming Llama2-Chat 70B (0.558), GPT-3.5-Turbo (0.563), and GPT-4-0613 itself (0.743). In pairwise human feedback quality comparisons, PROMETHEUS is preferred over GPT-4 58.67% of the time. On the HHH Alignment reward-model benchmark, PROMETHEUS 13B achieves 79.19% total accuracy, outperforming the Almost Reward Model (76.02%) and StanfordNLP Reward Model (58.82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Llama2-Chat (Touvron et al., 2023), PROMETHEUS 13B shows a +0.420 Pearson improvement over its 13B base model on seen rubrics and +0.397 on unseen rubrics, and even surpasses the much larger Llama2-Chat 70B (0.860 vs. 0.558 Pearson on unseen rubrics), demonstrating that fine-tuning on fine-grained feedback is far more effective than scaling model size. Against the Almost Reward Model (Kim et al., 2023), PROMETHEUS 13B achieves 79.19% total accuracy on HHH Alignment versus 76.02% for Almost, and 57.72% on MT Bench Human Judgment versus 49.90%, showing that an absolute-grading evaluator can generalize to ranking-based reward modeling. PROMETHEUS also outperforms GPT-3.5-Turbo across all benchmarks (e.g., 0.860 vs. 0.563 Pearson on unseen FEEDBACK BENCH rubrics) and surpasses GPT-4-0613 on the FEEDBACK BENCH (0.860 vs. 0.743), though it trails GPT-4 on external benchmarks like Vicuna Bench (0.466 vs. 0.925) and MT Bench (0.473 vs. 0.952), which the authors attribute to distributional differences between the training data and those test sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-judge paradigm (AlpacaFarm, PandaLM, Flask) from coarse-grained, single-dimension evaluation (e.g., helpfulness/harmlessness) to fine-grained evaluation conditioned on user-customized score rubrics, and adopts the Chain-of-Thought Fine-tuning paradigm (Kim et al., 2023a) where generated feedback serves as the reasoning trace before the final score. The key conceptual departure is the systematic inclusion of reference materials—particularly a reference answer scoring 5—as inputs to the evaluator, which the authors argue is essential for inducing fine-grained evaluation capability rather than requiring the model to internally solve the instruction. The FEEDBACK COLLECTION dataset, with 1K diverse rubrics and 100K GPT-4-generated feedback instances, is positioned as the first dataset designed specifically to train an evaluator sensitive to thousands of unique preference criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PROMETHEUS offers a practical, open-source, and inexpensive alternative to GPT-4 for large-scale evaluation with custom criteria, directly addressing reproducibility, cost, and transparency concerns that limit proprietary LLM evaluators. The finding that a 13B model matches GPT-4's human-correlation (0.897 vs. 0.882) and is even preferred in 58.67% of pairwise feedback comparisons is practically significant, though the paper honestly notes that on external benchmarks (Vicuna, MT Bench) GPT-4 still leads. Open questions include whether the overly-critical bias observed in PROMETHEUS is a feature or limitation, whether domain-specific base models (e.g., Code-Llama) can close remaining gaps, and whether the absolute-to-ranking grading transfer generalizes beyond the two preference datasets tested.
