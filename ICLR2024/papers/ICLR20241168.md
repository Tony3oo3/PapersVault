## Problem

Independent leverage score sampling provides strong theoretical guarantees for agnostic active regression but produces spatially clustered samples that leave gaps in the domain, degrading practical approximation quality. Deterministic grid methods (e.g., Chebyshev nodes) distribute samples evenly but lack provable guarantees and do not generalize to arbitrary distributions. The paper asks whether one can match or improve the theoretical sample complexity of leverage score sampling while producing spatially well-distributed samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix A ∈ R^{n×d} (constructed from n points in a low-dimensional parameter space via feature transformation, e.g., polynomial features of degree p), query access to a target vector b ∈ R^n, and a budget k of samples to collect. It outputs exactly k sampled row indices and an approximate regression solution x̃* = argmin_x ‖Ãx − b̃‖₂², where sampled rows are rescaled by 1/√p̃_i to ensure unbiased estimation. The sampling tree is built from the original low-dimensional points X (before feature transformation), so the spatial structure of the parameter domain drives the sampling geometry even when A is high-dimensional.

## Method

The core method replaces independent Bernoulli sampling with pivotal sampling run on a binary tree whose structure encodes spatial proximity. The tree is built by recursively splitting data points via PCA (maximum-variance direction) or coordinate-wise partitioning, ensuring spatially close points are siblings and thus negatively correlated in the sampling process. Pivotal sampling then performs a bottom-up tournament: at each sibling pair, one index is promoted to the parent with updated inclusion probability while the other is resolved as sampled or not, guaranteeing exactly k samples with marginal probabilities proportional to leverage scores. Theoretically, the paper proves that any k-homogeneous sampling distribution with leverage-score marginals satisfying one-sided ℓ∞ independence (which pivotal sampling satisfies via its strongly Rayleigh property) achieves O(d log d + d/ε) sample complexity, and for polynomial regression specifically, exploits the connection between leverage scores and Chebyshev polynomial orthogonality to eliminate the log d factor, yielding O(d/ε) samples.

## Results

The pivotal sampling method reduces the number of samples needed to reach a given target accuracy by up to 50% compared to independent Bernoulli leverage score sampling, as measured by median normalized error ‖Ax̃*−b‖²/‖b‖² over 1000 trials. Theoretically, for polynomial regression (Theorem 1.2), the method achieves O(d/ε) sample complexity, improving on the O(d log d + d/ε) bound of independent leverage score sampling by a log d factor. The general guarantee (Theorem 1.1) shows that any sampling method satisfying one-sided ℓ∞ independence with leverage-score marginals matches the O(d log d + d/ε) complexity of independent sampling, so the pivotal method never performs worse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is independent Bernoulli leverage score sampling (as in Hampton & Doostan 2015 and Cohen & Migliorati 2017), where each row is sampled independently with probability proportional to its leverage score. The pivotal method consistently outperforms this baseline across all three test problems (damped harmonic oscillator, heat equation, and chemical surface reaction) at multiple polynomial degrees (p=12, 15, 20, 25), requiring up to 50% fewer samples to reach the same normalized error level. The advantage is most visible in the damped harmonic oscillator (Figure 1: 250 samples, degree 20) and heat equation (Figure 5: 240 samples, degree 20), where Bernoulli sampling produces visible artifacts from spatial gaps that pivotal sampling avoids. The paper also discusses the algorithm of Chen & Price (2019), which provably achieves O(d/ε) samples but is complex with large constants; in the authors' initial experiments it did not empirically beat independent leverage score sampling, whereas the pivotal method matches the same O(d/ε) bound for polynomial regression while clearly outperforming independent sampling in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the leverage score sampling framework for agnostic active regression (Sarlós 2006; Rauhut & Ward 2012; Hampton & Doostan 2015; Cohen & Migliorati 2017), which established O(d log d + d/ε) sample complexity via independent Bernoulli sampling. The key departure is replacing independent sampling with pivotal sampling on a spatially-structured binary tree, a technique borrowed from survey sampling (Deville & Tille 1998) and previously studied for its negative dependence properties (Bränden & Jonasson 2012). The theoretical analysis extends the matrix Chernoff bounds of Kaufman et al. (2022) for one-sided ℓ∞-independent distributions to the active regression setting, and the polynomial regression improvement draws on the connection between leverage scores and Chebyshev/Legendre polynomial orthogonality, following Kane et al. (2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, easily-implemented method that reduces sample requirements by up to 50% for PDE surrogate modeling, a practically important setting where each label requires an expensive PDE solve. The general theorem (Theorem 1.1) is a useful tool for analyzing any future non-independent sampling strategy that satisfies one-sided ℓ∞ independence, potentially unifying analysis of volume sampling, determinantal point processes, and other negatively correlated schemes. However, the improved O(d/ε) bound (Theorem 1.2) is specific to 1D polynomial regression, and extending it to higher-dimensional or non-polynomial function classes remains open. The method's advantage over the more complex but theoretically optimal O(d/ε) algorithm of Chen & Price (2019) is that it is simple to implement and empirically superior, though the gap between the two is not formally characterized.
