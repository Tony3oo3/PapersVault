## Problem

Existing training-free guided diffusion methods (DPS, FreeDoM, LGD, UGD) perform gradient-based guidance in the full ambient pixel space, causing intermediate samples to drift off the low-dimensional data manifold where the pretrained score function is valid. This off-manifold deviation forces practitioners to use large numbers of diffusion steps, careful step-size scheduling, or repainting heuristics to recover sample quality, and there is limited theoretical understanding of when and why these methods succeed or fail.

## Input / Output

The method requires a pretrained unconditional diffusion model (pixel-space DDPM or latent diffusion model), a differentiable guidance loss L(x; y) with access to its gradients, and task-specific conditioning: a noisy/degraded measurement y for linear inverse problems (super-resolution, deblurring at 256×256), a reference face image for FaceID guidance, or a text prompt paired with a reference style image for compositional text-to-image generation. It outputs a single generated image per sample, with the key constraint that only black-box access to the loss and its gradient is needed—no fine-tuning of the diffusion model or the guidance network is required. For latent diffusion models, the guidance loss is evaluated on decoded images D(z_{0|t}) while the gradient update operates in the latent space.

## Method

MPGD reformulates the per-step guidance optimization to occur on the tangent space of the noisy data manifold M_t rather than the ambient space, leveraging the manifold hypothesis that data (and its noisy versions) concentrate on low-dimensional structures. The central technical contribution is a shortcut update rule (Theorem 1): instead of computing ∇_{x_t} L(x_{0|t}; y) through the diffusion model as in DPS, one directly updates the Tweedie clean-data estimate x_{0|t} ← x_{0|t} − c_t ∇_{x_{0|t}} L(x_{0|t}; y) and re-noises, which is provably manifold-preserving when the gradient lies on the clean-data tangent space and avoids backpropagation through the score network. To ensure the gradient stays on-manifold, two projection strategies are provided: MPGD-AE routes the loss through a pretrained autoencoder (VQGAN) so that ∇_{x_{0|t}} L(D(E(x_{0|t})); y) is automatically tangent to M, and MPGD-Z performs the gradient step in the autoencoder's latent space before decoding back. For latent diffusion models (MPGD-LDM), the shortcut is inherently manifold-preserving because the diffusion process already operates in the autoencoder's latent space, making the decoded guidance gradient tangent to the data manifold by construction. The framework also supports multi-step optimization (nonlinear conjugate gradient, time-traveling) within the tangent-space constraint for harder tasks.

## Results

On the CelebA-HQ FaceID guidance task (50 DDIM steps), MPGD achieves the best FaceID score of 0.5163 while running in 5.82s per image—1.8× faster than FreeDoM (10.65s) and 2.5× faster than LGD-MC (14.64s). On the Stable Diffusion style-guided text-to-image task (100 DDIM steps), MPGD-LDM achieves a Style score of 441.0 and CLIP score of 26.61, finding a balance between style adherence and prompt fidelity, while running in 19.83s with only 15.53 GB VRAM (vs. 37.43s / 31.65 GB for LGD-MC). Across the FFHQ super-resolution experiments, all three MPGD variants (w/o Proj., AE, Z) outperform DPS, LGD-MC, and MCG on KID, LPIPS, and inference time across 20, 50, and 100 DDIM steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FreeDoM, MPGD wins on FaceID guidance quality (0.5163 vs. 0.5690) and inference speed (5.82s vs. 10.65s), and on the Stable Diffusion task it uses less VRAM (15.53 GB vs. 17.30 GB) and runs 1.3× faster (19.83s vs. 26.50s), though FreeDoM achieves a slightly higher CLIP score (30.14 vs. 26.61) at the cost of weaker style adherence. Against LGD-MC, MPGD dominates on every metric: better FaceID (0.5163 vs. 0.6783), better CLIP (26.61 vs. 21.16), 1.9× faster (19.83s vs. 37.43s), and half the VRAM (15.53 GB vs. 31.65 GB). Against unconditional DDIM, MPGD trades a small KID increase (0.0473 vs. 0.0442) for a large FaceID improvement (0.5163 vs. 1.3914), confirming that guidance is applied while preserving sample fidelity. In the linear inverse-problem setting (FFHQ super-resolution), all MPGD variants outperform DPS, LGD-MC, and MCG on KID, LPIPS, and wall-clock time at every tested DDIM step count (20/50/100), with manifold projection (MPGD-AE, MPGD-Z) providing the largest fidelity gains. Against DDNM on PSNR, MPGD-Z underperforms (24.97 vs. 29.47 at 100 steps), but the paper notes DDNM requires direct access to the operator's SVD and is not applicable to the general black-box loss setting MPGD targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the training-free guided diffusion line (DPS, FreeDoM, LGD, UGD), which decomposes the conditional score into an unconditional score plus a loss-gradient term and performs gradient updates in the ambient space. It departs from these methods by identifying that ambient-space optimization pushes samples off the data manifold (formalized via Proposition 1's concentration result), and instead constrains the guidance to the tangent space of the manifold using off-the-shelf autoencoders. The key theoretical shortcut (Theorem 1) shows that updating only the Tweedie clean-data estimate x_{0|t} with a tangent-space gradient is sufficient to keep noisy samples on the correct manifold, eliminating the need for backpropagation through the diffusion model. For latent diffusion models, the paper observes that the autoencoder latent space already coincides with the data manifold, making the shortcut inherently manifold-preserving without any additional projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MPGD provides a principled, theoretically grounded framework for training-free conditional generation that simultaneously improves speed (up to 3.8×) and sample quality, making guided diffusion practical in low-compute settings (e.g., fitting within a 16 GB GPU where baselines cannot). The manifold-preserving guarantee offers a clear diagnostic for why prior methods fail (off-manifold drift) and a concrete fix, which should inform future guided diffusion designs. Open questions include the behavior of multi-step optimization under the manifold constraint, the sensitivity to imperfect autoencoders at scale, and whether the tangent-space projection can be replaced by cheaper approximations. The paper also acknowledges that guidance loss quality remains a critical bottleneck and that small DDIM step counts can still produce artifacts.
