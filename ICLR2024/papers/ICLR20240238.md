## Problem

Training large transformer models requires storing all forward-pass activations for backpropagation, consuming more memory than parameters and optimizer states combined. Existing Activation-Compressed Training (ACT) methods like GACT are limited to a minimum of 1-bit precision per layer, which both caps achievable compression and causes significant accuracy degradation in LLMs when activations are deeply compressed. The paper asks whether activations can be compressed below 1-bit effective precision without harming training convergence.

## Input / Output

The framework takes forward-pass activations (flattened into adjacent groups), model parameters, and a user-specified target average precision across all layers. It produces per-layer bit precision allocations (including sub-1b assignments via Average Quantization) and compressed activations stored during the forward pass. During backpropagation, each group's stored average is simply repeated to reconstruct the full activation tensor—no additional mask, spatial index, or lookup table is required for recovery, making the decompression step trivially parallelizable.

## Method

ALAM combines two key components. First, Average Quantization (AQ) groups adjacent activation elements and stores only the group average quantized to a chosen bit width, achieving effective sub-1b precision (e.g., AQ 0.5-bit stores 2 bits for a group of 4). The paper proves (Theorem 1) that representing each group by its average minimizes the gradient-difference boundary function d‖ΔX‖², and empirically shows AQ 0.5-bit outperforms conventional 1-bit uniform quantization. Second, GradNormVar replaces GACT's expensive sensitivity calculation (which stores full parameter gradients from multiple seeds, requiring ~700 GB for GPT-3) with the variance of L2 norms of per-layer parameter gradients, reducing sensitivity memory from gigabytes to kilobytes while maintaining 0.9981 correlation with the original metric. The full pipeline: (1) compute per-layer sensitivity via GradNormVar, (2) greedily allocate bits to minimize gradient variance (Eq. 4), assigning AQ 0.5-bit to low-sensitivity layers instead of 1-bit, and (3) train with compressed activations, recalculating sensitivity at 10% of training epochs.

## Results

ALAM achieves a 10.6× activation memory compression rate on LLaMA2-7B at 1-bit precision (2.0 GB vs. 21.1 GB baseline) while matching baseline accuracy across all 7 evaluation benchmarks (e.g., MMLU 45.7 vs. 46, PIQA 79.3 vs. 79.5). On LLaMA2-13B and LLaMA-30B, ALAM at 1-bit achieves 10.3× and 9.9× compression respectively with accuracy within 0.3 points of the 16-bit baseline. In BERT fine-tuning, ALAM reaches up to 22.5× compression on BERT-Large with accuracy comparable to the uncompressed baseline, while GACT fails entirely below 2-bit precision. Sensitivity calculation memory drops from 3,817 MB (GACT, BERT-Large) to under 4.6 kB (ALAM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GACT (the prior state-of-the-art ACT method), ALAM wins decisively: GACT diverges at 3-bit precision on LLaMA2-7B and fails entirely at 1.5-bit and 1-bit on all BERT models, whereas ALAM trains successfully at 1-bit with accuracy within 0.5 points of the uncompressed baseline. On BERT-Large fine-tuning, ALAM at 1-bit achieves 22.5× activation compression with 92.8% SST-2 accuracy (vs. 93.0% baseline), while GACT cannot operate below 2-bit. ALAM also reduces sensitivity calculation memory by over 1000× (e.g., 4.6 kB vs. 3,817 MB on BERT-Large). Against LoRA and QLoRA, ALAM is complementary rather than competitive: LoRA/QLoRA reduce model-state memory (parameters, gradients, optimizer) while ALAM reduces activation memory. Combining them yields 56% total memory reduction on LLaMA2-7B (34 GB → 15 GB) and 75% on LLaMA2-13B (40 GB → 10 GB) with negligible accuracy loss (62.5 → 62.2 and 65.1 → 65.0 average accuracy, respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

ALAM extends the Activation-Compressed Training (ACT) lineage initiated by ACTNN (Chen et al., 2021) and advanced by GACT (Liu et al., 2022), which established sensitivity-based bit allocation and stochastic quantization for reducing activation memory. The theoretical foundation draws on the unbiasedness proof from ACTNN and the gradient-difference boundary functions from AC-GC (Evans & Aamodt, 2021), which ALAM generalizes into a unified d‖ΔX‖² form. The key departure is twofold: (1) Average Quantization replaces per-element stochastic rounding with group averaging to achieve effective sub-1-bit precision, and (2) GradNormVar substitutes the expensive multi-seed gradient-variance sensitivity calculation of GACT with a lightweight L2-norm-variance proxy, reducing sensitivity memory from gigabytes to kilobytes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling ~10× activation memory compression in LLMs (LLaMA2-7B/13B, LLaMA-30B) with negligible accuracy loss, ALAM makes it feasible to fine-tune large models on a single consumer GPU, reducing total training memory by 56–75% when combined with LoRA/QLoRA. The conceptual insight that a trivial group average outperforms 1-bit stochastic quantization challenges the assumption that finer per-element precision is always necessary for gradient fidelity. Open questions include whether the 11–23% training-time overhead from compression/decompression can be eliminated with optimized kernels, and whether the approach extends to full fine-tuning (beyond PEFT) where sensitivity calculation memory savings are less pronounced.
