## Problem

Prior theoretical guarantees for CVaR-based risk-sensitive RL were restricted to tabular MDPs with known, finite state spaces, making them inapplicable to realistic settings where the state space is enormous or infinite. This paper addresses the gap by extending CVaR RL to low-rank MDPs with nonlinear function approximation, where the transition kernel admits a low-rank factorization but the embedding functions are unknown, requiring simultaneous representation learning, exploration, and risk-aware planning.

## Input / Output

The algorithm interacts online with an episodic MDP of length H, receiving state-action-reward transitions, and is given a model class of d-dimensional embedding functions and a fixed risk tolerance τ ∈ (0,1]. The output is an augmented policy π̂ defined over the product space S × [0,H] (state paired with remaining budget) along with an optimal initial budget ĉ, such that the CVaR gap CVaR*τ − CVaRτ(R(π̂,ĉ)) ≤ ε. Notably, the sample complexity has no explicit dependence on the state space cardinality |S|, which may be infinite.

## Method

The ELA algorithm iteratively collects data via a UCB-bonus-driven exploration policy, learns the low-rank transition factorization (ψ̂, φ̂) through an MLE oracle, and constructs exploration bonuses from the empirical covariance of learned representations to balance exploration against the risk-sensitive value objective. The key structural insight is the augmented MDP formulation, which embeds the remaining budget c as an additional state variable, transforming the CVaR maximization into a minimization of a value function over the augmented state space. For computational tractability, the ELLA variant replaces exact value iteration with CVaR-LSVI: a discretized-reward LSVI procedure that performs ridge regression on the product feature φ̂ ⊗ φ_r, simulates greedy policies within the learned model, and selects the best policy-budget pair over a finite grid of budget values. This yields polynomial running time in d, H, A, and 1/ε (with an MLE oracle), avoiding any dependence on |S|.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) establishes that ELA achieves a sample complexity of Õ(H⁷A²d⁴ log(|F|/δ) / (τ²ε²)) trajectories to output an ε-optimal CVaR policy, with no explicit dependence on the state space size |S|. The computationally efficient variant ELLA (Theorem B.3) achieves the same sample complexity while requiring only Õ(H²⁹A³d¹² log(|F|/δ) / (τ¹⁶ε¹⁶)) additional computation beyond MLE oracle calls, establishing polynomial-time solvability. The regret bound is Õ(τ⁻¹H³Ad²√K · log(|F|/δ)), which is the first such bound for CVaR RL with function approximation in low-rank MDPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or head-to-head algorithm comparisons. The theoretical positioning is as follows: the dependencies on action space size A and representation dimension d match those of Rep-UCB (Uehara et al., 2022) for risk-neutral RL in low-rank MDPs, showing that the additional difficulty of budget control in CVaR RL does not inflate these dependencies. The 1/τ² sample complexity matches the rates of prior tabular CVaR RL algorithms (Wang et al., 2023; Bastani et al., 2022), and the paper notes an Ω(1/(τε²)) lower bound from Wang et al. (2023), leaving a gap in the τ-dependency. The H⁷ episode-length dependency is slightly worse than Rep-UCB's H⁵ for risk-neutral RL, though the authors note it can be reduced to H⁵ with reward normalization. The key novelty is that all prior CVaR RL guarantees were restricted to tabular MDPs with known representations, whereas ELA is the first to provide provable efficiency in the low-rank MDP setting with unknown nonlinear representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: risk-sensitive RL with CVaR objectives (Bastani et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023) and representation learning in low-rank MDPs (Jiang et al., 2017; Agarwal et al., 2020; Uehara et al., 2022). Prior CVaR RL results are restricted to tabular MDPs where the state space is small and representations are known, while prior low-rank MDP algorithms address only risk-neutral objectives. The paper departs from both by introducing the augmented MDP framework (budget as state variable) from Wang et al. (2023) into the low-rank setting, and by adapting the UCB bonus + MLE oracle paradigm from Rep-UCB to handle the non-linear CVaR Bellman structure. The CVaR-LSVI planning oracle is a novel computational contribution that discretizes the budget dimension and leverages the low-rank factorization of both transitions and rewards to avoid explicit state-space enumeration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably sample-efficient algorithm for CVaR RL in low-rank MDPs, closing a clear gap between tabular risk-sensitive RL and function-approximation-based RL. The result provides theoretical justification for deploying risk-averse policies in high-dimensional settings (autonomous driving, finance, healthcare) where tabular methods are infeasible. However, the sample complexity has a loose H^7 dependency (vs. H^5 for risk-neutral Rep-UCB) and d^4 scaling, and the paper acknowledges that tightening the τ-dependency (currently τ^{-2} vs. a conjectured τ^{-1} lower bound) remains open. Establishing matching lower bounds for CVaR RL in low-rank MDPs is identified as a key future direction, as existing lower bounds for risk-neutral low-rank MDPs do not directly transfer to the risk-sensitive landscape.
