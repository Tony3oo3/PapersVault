## Problem

Existing NF-based sRGB noise models require camera metadata (ISO, smartphone model) even during the sampling/inference phase, and methods like NeCA need a separate model per camera to handle diverse noise distributions. Additionally, prior NF approaches do not explicitly model the spatial noise correlation induced by demosaicking in the sRGB pipeline, limiting their ability to synthesize realistic noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240491/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model takes a clean image as conditioning and a real noisy image (160×160 patches from SIDD) as the target to fit. At inference via NAS, it additionally takes a noisy input image (without any metadata) to estimate the underlying camera configuration through latent-space likelihood scoring, then generates a new synthetic noisy image conditioned on the same clean image. The output is a full-resolution sRGB noisy image whose noise statistics match the input's distribution.

## Method

NAFlow is a single conditional normalizing flow that maps noisy images to a latent space where C=34 camera-configuration-specific Gaussian distributions (learnable μ_c, Σ_c) are placed, trained jointly via NLL loss. The key inference mechanism, Noise-Aware Sampling (NAS), uses the trained flow as a generative classifier: it encodes the noisy input into the latent, computes likelihoods under all C Gaussians, forms a weighted GMM mixture, samples from it, and inverts the flow to produce a metadata-free synthetic noisy image. To capture spatial noise correlation at multiple scales (ranging from 1 to 25+ pixels), the architecture employs L=3 scale-level blocks, each containing a squeeze operation, a transition block, and two conditional flow blocks (affine coupling + affine injector) that are conditioned on the downsampled clean image. This multi-scale design is the first NF-based approach to explicitly model sRGB noise correlation through hierarchical spatial resolution reduction.

## Results

On SIDD-Validation, NAFlow achieves the best average KLD of 0.0305 and AKLD of 0.1306 across five camera models, outperforming the previous best (NeCA-W) by 0.0037 in KLD and 0.013 in AKLD. On the unseen SIDD+ dataset, NAFlow achieves the lowest KLD of 0.0494 without requiring metadata at inference. For downstream denoising on SIDD Benchmark, a DnCNN trained on NAFlow-generated noise reaches 37.22 dB PSNR and 0.935 SSIM, surpassing NeCA-W by 0.39 dB and approaching the real-noise upper bound of 37.63 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAFlow consistently outperforms all four baselines on average noise quality metrics. Against C2N (ICCV 2021), NAFlow reduces average KLD from 0.2129 to 0.0305 (≈7× improvement) and AKLD from 0.2802 to 0.1306, while also using fewer parameters (1.1M vs 2.2M). Against Flow-sRGB (CVPR 2022), NAFlow achieves substantially lower KLD (0.0305 vs 0.2435) and AKLD (0.1306 vs 0.5180), and uses 1.1M parameters versus Flow-sRGB's 6K (though Flow-sRGB is a much simpler model). Against NeCA-S (CVPR 2023), NAFlow dominates on both KLD (0.0305 vs 0.2743) and AKLD (0.1306 vs 1.0470). Against the strongest baseline NeCA-W, NAFlow wins on average KLD (0.0305 vs 0.0342) and AKLD (0.1306 vs 0.1436), and achieves 0.39 dB higher PSNR in denoising (37.22 vs 36.82), while using a single 1.1M-parameter model instead of five separately trained NeCA-W models (each with ~8.1M parameters). The only per-camera KLD losses are on G4 (0.0254 vs 0.0242) and N6 (0.0309 vs 0.0206), but NAFlow's single-model design and metadata-free sampling provide practical advantages that NeCA-W lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional normalizing flow lineage for noise modeling (NoiseFlow → Flow-sRGB) by introducing a single unified NF that learns multiple camera-configuration-specific Gaussian distributions in latent space, rather than the per-camera separate models used by NeCA. The key methodological departure is the Noise-Aware Sampling (NAS) algorithm, which repurposes the trained NF as a generative classifier to estimate the most relevant noise distribution from an input noisy image without requiring camera metadata at inference. The multi-scale noise embedding with squeeze operations is the first NF-based attempt to explicitly model spatial noise correlation at varying scales, addressing a limitation identified in both Flow-sRGB and NeCA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of metadata dependency at inference and the use of a single 1.1M-parameter model (vs. NeCA-W's ~40M+ parameters across five separate models) make the approach significantly more practical for deployment in real-world denoising pipelines where camera settings are unknown. However, the SIDD dataset's limited camera diversity and simplified RAW-to-sRGB pipeline constrain generalizability, and the PSNR gap to the real-noise upper bound (37.22 vs. 37.63 dB) indicates room for improvement. Open questions include whether the GMM-in-latent-space approach scales to substantially larger numbers of camera configurations and whether the multi-scale embedding generalizes beyond the SIDD-specific noise statistics.
