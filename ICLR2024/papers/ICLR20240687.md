## Problem

In Dyna-style model-based RL, the learned dynamics model inevitably contains prediction errors that mislead policy learning during model rollouts. Existing mitigations are either myopic post-hoc sample filtering/weighting (which cannot prevent trajectories from entering uncertain regions) or pure exploration (which is sample-inefficient and discovers low-reward states irrelevant to policy improvement). No prior method simultaneously avoids model errors before rollouts and actively reduces them through targeted, reward-aware exploration within a single-policy framework.

## Input / Output

At each decision step, COPlanner consumes the current state (proprioceptive vector or RSSM latent representation z_t), the learned dynamics model ensemble (7 networks for MBPO, 8 for DreamerV3), and the learned policy π_φ to produce a single selected action via UP-MPC. The same UP-MPC call is reused in two modes: with α = +α_o for environment exploration and α = −α_c for model rollouts, so the only difference between the two phases is the sign of the uncertainty coefficient. The method is plug-and-play: it wraps around any existing Dyna-style MBRL pipeline without modifying the policy or model training objectives.

## Method

COPlanner's core component is Uncertainty-aware Policy-guided MPC (UP-MPC), which uses random shooting to sample K action candidates from the current policy, rolls each candidate forward H_p steps through the dynamics model ensemble, and computes the cumulative reward and model uncertainty (ensemble disagreement variance) along each trajectory. The selected action maximizes cumulative reward plus α times cumulative uncertainty, where α is positive during environment exploration (bonus, encouraging the agent to visit high-reward uncertain regions to reduce model error) and negative during model rollouts (penalty, steering rollouts away from uncertain regions to protect policy learning). This symmetric dual use of multi-step uncertainty within a single policy eliminates the need for separate exploration/exploitation policies and avoids introducing spurious intrinsic-reward behaviors. The framework is validated as a drop-in module for MBPO (proprioceptive) and DreamerV3 (visual), improving sample efficiency by ~2× and asymptotic performance by 16.9% (DMC), 32.8% (GYM), and 9.6% (visual DMC).

## Results

COPlanner improves sample efficiency by ~40% on proprioceptive DMC (e.g., Walker-walk reaches 700 in ~60k steps vs. 100k for MBPO) and nearly doubles it on GYM tasks. Asymptotic performance improves by 16.9% on proprioceptive DMC, 32.8% on MuJoCo-GYM, and 9.6% on visual DMC. On visual DMC at 1M steps, COPlanner-DreamerV3 achieves 937.8 on Hopper-stand, 444.2 on Hopper-hop, 803.9 on Quadruped-walk, and 787.9 on Quadruped-run, outperforming DreamerV3 by 5.6–13.2% across these tasks. The method also successfully solves Walker-run, which MBPO fails to address.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On proprioceptive control, COPlanner-MBPO substantially outperforms MBPO (16.9% on DMC, 32.8% on GYM), P2P-MPC, MEEE, M2AC, and D4PG across all 12 tasks in Figure 4. On MuJoCo (Table 4), COPlanner-MBPO wins on Hopper (3360 vs. 3274 PDML, 2845 MBPO), Walker2d (4595 vs. 4379 PDML), and Ant (5268 vs. 4993 PDML), but loses to PDML on Humanoid (4834 vs. 5397). On visual DMC (Table 5), COPlanner-DreamerV3 beats DreamerV3 on 7 of 8 tasks (losing only on Cartpole-swingup-sparse: 787.5 vs. 814.7), and dominates TDMPC, PlaNet, TACO, DrM, and DrQ-v2 on most tasks, with particularly large margins over PlaNet (e.g., 937.8 vs. 5.96 on Hopper-stand) and TDMPC (e.g., 444.2 vs. 189.2 on Hopper-hop). LEXA-reward-DreamerV3 is competitive on Hopper-stand (905.7) and Cartpole-swingup-sparse (683.5) but trails COPlanner on the remaining tasks. COPlanner-DreamerV2 (Appendix D.3) also shows consistent improvements over DreamerV2 across 7 visual tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

COPlanner sits at the intersection of two established lines of work in dyna-style MBRL: post-hoc model error mitigation (MBPO, M2AC, MEEE) and uncertainty-guided exploration (Plan2Explore, LEXA). It departs from both by replacing their separate mechanisms with a single Uncertainty-aware Policy-guided MPC (UP-MPC) that estimates multi-step model uncertainty via ensemble disagreement and symmetrically applies it as a penalty during model rollouts and a bonus during environment exploration. Unlike MEEE's one-step uncertainty or P2P-MPC's rollout-only focus, COPlanner's planning horizon captures long-term trajectory-level uncertainty, and unlike LEXA's dual-policy design, it operates with a single policy throughout, avoiding distribution mismatch between exploration and exploitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of COPlanner—demonstrated across both MBPO (proprioceptive) and DreamerV3 (visual) with consistent 40–100% sample efficiency gains—makes it a practical drop-in improvement for any dyna-style MBRL pipeline. The ablation revealing that conservative rollouts alone can be actively harmful in sparse-reward visual tasks (over-conservatism preventing policy learning) is a useful caution for practitioners. Key limitations include the additional MPC computation overhead (~7–37% wall-clock increase) and the reliance on ensemble disagreement for uncertainty, though the authors show the framework is agnostic to the uncertainty estimator. Open questions include whether parallelized planning can eliminate the compute cost, and whether the approach extends to discrete action spaces or offline settings.
