## Problem

Prior analyses of neuron alignment in two-layer ReLU networks under small initialization either rely on the vanishing initialization limit (ε→0) without finite-ε error bounds, or assume directional convergence without quantifying how long it takes to achieve. This paper provides the first non-asymptotic, quantitative characterization of the alignment phase, showing that the time to achieve good directional convergence is O(log n / √μ) and determining the explicit initialization scale ε required for this to hold.

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary classification dataset of n input vectors in R^D with labels in {−1,+1} satisfying an orthogonal separability condition (same-label pairs positively correlated, different-label pairs negatively correlated, with minimum correlation μ>0). The output is the trained first-layer weight matrix W(t) and second-layer vector v(t), which after the alignment phase exhibit an approximately low-rank structure (stable rank ≤ 2) and achieve zero training loss at an O(1/t) rate. For the MNIST experiments, raw grayscale digit images are centered (x_i ← x_i − x̄) to approximately satisfy the separability assumption.

## Method

The paper analyzes gradient flow dynamics for a two-layer ReLU network with balanced small initialization (W(0)=εW_0, v_j(0)=±‖w_j(0)‖), showing that neurons with positive second-layer weights (V_+) align with the positive data center x_+=Σx_i and those with negative weights (V_-) align with x_-=Σx_i. The key technical tool is an explicit error bound on the directional dynamics approximation d/dt(w_j/‖w_j‖) ≈ sign(v_j(0))·P_{w_j}·x_a(w_j), valid for t ≤ Θ((1/n)log(1/√(hε))). Under the separability assumption, the analysis tracks how each neuron's activation pattern evolves monotonically (V_+ neurons only gain positive-data activation and lose negative-data activation), bounding the total number of transitions by n and the time per transition by O(1/(n_a√μ)), yielding the O(log n/√μ) alignment time. After alignment, the network decomposes into two linear subnetworks (one per label), giving O(1/t) loss convergence and a stable rank bound of 2 on W(t).

## Results

The main contribution is a theoretical guarantee: under well-separated data (Assumption 1) and sufficiently small initialization ε = O(1/√h · exp(−n/√μ · log n)), all neurons achieve directional alignment within t₁ = O(log n / √μ) time, after which the loss converges to zero at an O(1/t) rate and the first-layer weight matrix has stable rank at most 2. On MNIST binary classification (digits 0 vs. 1, h=50, α=10⁻⁶), neurons in V₊ align with x₊ and neurons in V₋ align with x₋ after ~2000 iterations, the loss begins decreasing after ~3000 iterations, and the stable rank ‖W‖²_F/‖W‖²₂ drops below 2, consistent with the theoretical predictions. Additional MNIST pairs (3 vs. 8, 1 vs. 7) in the appendix show the same qualitative behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its two-phase training scheme (training both W and v via gradient descent) against the first-layer-only training schemes of Brutzkus et al. (2018) and Wang et al. (2019), where v is fixed at ±1 and only W is updated. Both schemes achieve small training loss on the MNIST binary classification task, so there is no accuracy-based win/loss distinction. However, the key finding is qualitative: the characteristic two-phase behavior (neuron directions change while norms stay small, then norms grow and loss drops) is clearly present when both layers are trained (Figure 12) but absent when only the first layer is trained (Figure 13). This demonstrates that the alignment-then-fit dynamics are a consequence of jointly training both layers under small initialization, not an artifact of the first-layer optimization alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper completes the 'align then fit' narrative for two-layer ReLU networks under small initialization that was initiated by Maennel et al. (2018) and Phuong & Lampert (2021), but which remained incomplete: Maennel's analysis only holds in the ε→0 limit, and Phuong & Lampert assumed directional convergence without bounding the time to achieve it. The key departure from Boursier et al. (2022), who provided quantitative alignment bounds, is the extension from orthogonal data (μ=0) to the more general orthogonally separable case (μ>0), which requires novel techniques to track activation pattern evolution across all neurons rather than restricting to those initially activating all same-label data. The paper also makes the implicit connection between data separability μ and required initialization scale ε explicit, yielding ε = O(1/√h · exp(−n/√μ · log n)).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(log n / √μ) alignment time bound is the first quantitative characterization of how long the 'alignment phase' lasts as a function of data geometry, directly informing how small the initialization must be for the theory to apply. This is practically relevant: as data becomes less separable (μ→0), the required initialization shrinks exponentially, explaining why small-init training can fail on harder datasets. Limitations include the balanced initialization assumption (though experiments use standard Gaussian), the well-separated data requirement (Assumption 1), and restriction to two-layer binary classification. Open questions include whether analogous quantitative bounds exist for deeper networks, non-well-separated data, or non-balanced initialization, and whether the low-rank bias (stable rank ≤ 2) persists in more realistic multi-class settings.
