## Problem

In distributed deep learning with data parallelism, local gradient methods (e.g., Local SGD) reduce communication by letting workers train locally for H steps before synchronizing, but choosing H is a critical and poorly understood hyperparameter. Prior work treated H as a trade-off between optimization speed and communication cost, yet empirical evidence shows that a properly chosen H can improve test accuracy through implicit regularization. The paper addresses the gap of providing a principled, theory-grounded rule for dynamically setting H that simultaneously improves generalization and reduces communication overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240177/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a decaying learning rate schedule (cosine, linear, or step decay), a gradient-based optimizer (SGD or AdamW), K distributed workers each with local batch size B_loc, and a base synchronization period H_base. It outputs trained model parameters and a per-round synchronization period H^(s) computed on-the-fly as max{H_base, (α/η_t)²}, where α is a small growth coefficient (typically 0.01–0.5) and η_t is the current learning rate. Unlike fixed-H local methods, the synchronization period grows quadratically as the learning rate shrinks, so early training uses frequent synchronization while late training uses very infrequent synchronization.

## Method

The Quadratic Synchronization Rule (QSR) dynamically sets the local training period H^(s) proportional to 1/η_t² at each communication round, where η_t is the current learning rate. The theoretical justification derives a new Slow SDE for Local SGD under the H~η^{-2} scaling (Definition 3.3), showing that the drift term on the minimizer manifold is K times larger than in standard SGD, leading to faster sharpness reduction and a stronger implicit bias toward flatter minima. A weak approximation theorem (Theorem 3.1, part 3) establishes that the discrete Local SGD trajectory tracks this SDE with error O(α²) when α is small. The paper proves a theoretical separation: QSR (H~η^{-2}) > H~η^{-1} > constant H in generalization performance, and validates this ordering empirically on ImageNet with ResNet-152 (Local SGD) and ViT-B (Local AdamW), where QSR improves top-1 accuracy by up to 1.7% while cutting communication volume to under 25% of data parallel training.

## Results

On ImageNet with batch size 4096, QSR improves top-1 validation accuracy of Local SGD on ResNet-152 from 79.54% (H=2) to 80.30% and of Local AdamW on ViT-B from 79.32% (H=4) to 80.98%, while reducing communication to 39.7% and 10.4% respectively. QSR surpasses the standard data parallel approach by 0.7% on ResNet-152 (80.30% vs. 79.53%) and by 1.1% on ViT-B (80.98% vs. 79.86%). On 64 GPUs, QSR cuts ViT-B training time from 8.6 to 5.5 hours while achieving 0.84% higher top-1 accuracy than parallel AdamW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Local SGD/AdamW with constant synchronization period (Stich, 2018), QSR consistently wins on top-1 validation accuracy: +0.76% on ResNet-152 (80.30% vs. 79.54%) and +1.66% on ViT-B (80.98% vs. 79.32%), while also reducing communication volume (39.7% vs. 50% and 10.4% vs. 25%). Against Post-local SGD (Lin, 2020), QSR achieves higher final test accuracy; the paper notes that Post-local SGD's generalization benefits appear shortly after the switch and then diminish as the learning rate decays, whereas QSR's benefits grow in the late phase. Against the H~η^{-1} scaling proposed by Gu et al. (2023), QSR (H~η^{-2}) wins on both models in Figure 2, confirming the theoretical prediction that the quadratic scaling produces a K-times larger drift term in the Slow SDE, leading to faster sharpness reduction. QSR also outperforms the communication-intensive data parallel baselines (Parallel SGD/AdamW) on test accuracy while using only 20–40% of the communication, and on 64 GPUs it reduces wall-clock training time by 36% for ViT-B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical framework of Gu et al. (2023), which showed that Local SGD generalizes better than SGD when H = Ω(1/η), by deriving the Slow SDE for the more aggressive H ∝ 1/η² scaling and showing it yields a K-times larger drift term toward flatter minima. It departs from the optimization-focused H-selection literature (Stich 2018, Haddadpour 2019, Wang & Joshi 2019) by reframing the problem around generalization rather than training loss, and from the empirical Post-local SGD schedule (Lin et al. 2020) by providing a continuous, theory-grounded rule rather than a single switching point. The work also extends prior results from SGD to adaptive optimizers (AdamW) and validates on large-scale ImageNet training rather than the small datasets (CIFAR-10/100) used in earlier adaptive-synchronization studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QSR provides a simple, plug-and-play rule (set H ∝ 1/η²) that simultaneously improves test accuracy and reduces wall-clock training time, which is practically valuable for large-scale distributed training where communication is a major bottleneck. The dual benefit—better generalization with less communication—is notable because prior approaches typically traded one for the other. Key limitations include: the benefit diminishes for smaller models or shorter training horizons (e.g., ResNet-50 for 90 epochs), and it is unclear whether the implicit-regularization mechanism transfers to unsupervised pretraining where regularization's role in closing the train-population gap is less clear. An open direction is adapting communication-efficient local methods for language model pretraining to improve downstream transferability.
