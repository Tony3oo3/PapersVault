## Problem

Randomized smoothing certifies robustness by injecting Gaussian noise and estimating the smoothed classifier via Monte-Carlo sampling, but the resulting MC variance, the base classifier's Lipschitz constant, and the prediction margin interact in ways that have not been jointly exploited. Specifically, the Lipschitz constant of the base classifier simultaneously governs both the smoothed classifier's Lipschitz bound and the MC sampling variance, while the choice of logit-to-probability mapping (hardmax, softmax, sparsemax) creates an unexploited variance-margin tradeoff that directly affects the certified radius.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained base subclassifier f (e.g., a ViT-denoiser or ResNet-150) and an input image x, and produces a predicted class label together with a risk-corrected certified robust radius R₂(p̄) that holds with probability 1−α. Crucially, the procedure operates in a zero-shot manner on existing pre-trained models without retraining: at inference time it selects the optimal simplex map and temperature per input from a small ensemble, using only Monte-Carlo samples of f(x+δ). The simplex mass r and temperature t are additional tunable hyperparameters that shape the robustness profile.

## Method

The LVM-RS procedure makes three coupled technical contributions. First, it applies the Gaussian-Poincaré inequality to show that constraining the Lipschitz constant of the base classifier s∘f directly reduces the MC variance of the smoothed classifier estimate, and uses the Empirical Bernstein inequality (rather than Hoeffding or Clopper-Pearson) to incorporate this empirical variance into the risk-α correction. Second, it introduces the r-simplex Δ_r^{c−1} (a simplex with total mass r) and a generalized sparsemax mapping that is 1-Lipschitz and interpolates between softmax and hardmax via a temperature parameter, providing a continuous knob to trade off margin size against variance. Third, it derives a new Lipschitz bound for the smoothed classifier L(f̃_k) ≤ L(s_k^r∘f)·erf(r/(2^{3/2}·L(s_k^r∘f)·σ)) that improves upon Salman et al. (2019) by a factor of 2, and identifies an optimal σ* at which the combined Lipschitz-plus-RS certificate is ~26% larger than either approach alone. At inference, the algorithm evaluates a small ensemble of (simplex map, temperature) pairs on a validation sample, selects the pair maximizing the risk-corrected radius, and then certifies with a larger sample.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, LVM-RS achieves best certified accuracy of 88.49% at ε=0 and 32.35% at ε=1.0 (best over σ∈{0.25, 0.5, 1.0}), outperforming the Carlini et al. (2023) ViT-denoiser baseline by up to 2.44 points. On ImageNet, LVM-RS reaches 80.66% at ε=0 and 14.31% at ε=3, improving over the same baseline by up to 1.90 points. In the improved Lipschitz bound experiment (Table 1), the new bound yields 52.56% certified accuracy at ε=0.14 on CIFAR-10, surpassing both standard RS (47.9%) and deterministic Lipschitz certification (40%), with the gap widening at larger perturbations (13.53% vs 0.0% at ε=0.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the state-of-the-art Carlini et al. (2023) ViT-denoiser baseline, LVM-RS wins on every perturbation level for both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, with the largest gains at high σ (e.g., +14.75 points at ε=0 on CIFAR-10 for σ=1.0: 63.72% vs 48.97%). Against standard randomized smoothing (Salman et al., 2019), the improved Lipschitz bound in Table 1 yields dramatically higher certified accuracy at larger perturbations: 21.9% vs 6.42% at ε=0.42 and 13.53% vs 0.0% at ε=0.5 on CIFAR-10. Against deterministic Lipschitz-margin certification (Tsuzuku et al., 2018), the combined RS+Lipschitz approach also dominates at all ε values (e.g., 52.56% vs 40% at ε=0.14), confirming the theoretical prediction that the synergy between RS and Lipschitz continuity yields a ~26% larger certificate than either method alone. Computationally, LVM-RS adds negligible overhead (7.11s vs 7.10s on CIFAR-10; 7.03s vs 6.46s on ImageNet per input).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously treated-in-isolation threads in certified adversarial robustness: randomized smoothing (Cohen et al., 2019; Salman et al., 2019), deterministic Lipschitz-margin certification (Tsuzuku et al., 2018), and sparse simplex mappings (Martins & Astudillo, 2016; Laha et al., 2018). The key conceptual departure is showing via the Gaussian-Poincaré inequality that the base classifier's Lipschitz constant directly controls the Monte-Carlo sampling variance, thereby coupling the Lipschitz-variance-margin tradeoff into a single optimization problem. The r-simplex (a simplex with total mass r ≠ 1) and the LVM-RS inference procedure are new tools introduced to navigate this tradeoff, while the improved Lipschitz bound on the smoothed classifier (Theorem 2) refines Salman et al.'s (2019) result by a factor of 2 under a mild additional assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The improved Lipschitz bound (Theorem 2) and the optimal σ* characterization (Proposition 3) are plug-and-play theoretical results that can tighten certificates in a range of subsequent RS works. The LVM-RS procedure's zero-shot applicability to pre-trained models (e.g., Carlini et al.'s ViT-denoiser) is practically valuable, as it avoids retraining. However, the empirical gains over the strong Carlini et al. (2023) baseline are modest (roughly 1–2% certified accuracy on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet at most σ values), and the method adds computational overhead from the temperature/simplex-map search. Open questions include whether the r-simplex mass can be learned end-to-end, how the approach interacts with margin-maximization training objectives, and whether the variance reduction from low Lipschitz constants persists for very deep or transformer-based backbones.
