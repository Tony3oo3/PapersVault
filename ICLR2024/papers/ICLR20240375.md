## Problem

Learning Nash equilibria in games beyond zero-sum and potential games remains largely unresolved, as standard optimistic mirror descent and extra gradient methods require the Minty variational inequality (MVI) property for convergence. This paper shows that even simple rank-1 bimatrix games (where A+B is rank 1, a natural generalization of zero-sum games) fail the MVI criterion, leaving no known online learning algorithm with NE convergence guarantees for this class.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a rank-1 bimatrix game (A, B) ∈ R^{n×m} × R^{n×m} satisfying A+B = ab^T for known vectors a ∈ R^n, b ∈ R^m (with entries in [-1,1]), and produces a pair of mixed strategies (x, y) ∈ Δ_n × Δ_m that constitutes an 18ε-approximate Nash equilibrium. The procedure is fully decentralized: both players run the same optimistic multiplicative weights update on a shared parameterized objective, requiring no coordination beyond the common parameter λ, and the total iteration complexity is O((log n + log m)/ε² · log(1/ε)).

## Method

The key insight is a two-level decomposition: the rank-1 game (A, B) is reduced to a parameterized zero-sum game (A − 1λb^T, −A + 1λb^T) via a rank-reduction lemma, and the correct λ is found by binary search guided by the sign of x^T a − λ. The inner loop solves a modified min-max problem f_λ(x,y) = x^T(A − 1λb^T)y − ½(x^T a − λ)² using Optimistic Multiplicative Weights Update (OMWU), where the quadratic regularization term penalizes deviation from the constraint x^T a = λ. The critical technical contribution is proving that any ε-approximate stationary point of f_λ with |x^T a − λ| ≤ √(5ε) is simultaneously an O(√ε)-approximate NE of the zero-sum game and, by a separate correspondence lemma, an O(ε)-approximate NE of the original rank-1 game. The binary search is valid because approximate NE of the zero-sum game maintain the sign of x^T a − λ (a convexity argument), ensuring the search interval always brackets a valid equilibrium.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.7) establishes that the proposed Algorithm 1 converges to an 18ε-approximate Nash equilibrium of a rank-1 game in O((log n + log m)/ε² · log(1/ε)) iterations, where n and m are the numbers of pure strategies for the row and column players respectively. The inner-loop OMWU (Algorithm 2) requires T = Ω((log n + log m)/ε) iterations per outer-loop step, with the outer binary search over λ requiring at most log(1/ε) steps. Experimentally, on a 5×5 rank-1 game, Algorithm 1 drives the Nash gap to zero while vanilla optimistic gradient ascent (OGA) enters a persistent limit cycle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed Algorithm 1 (optimistic multiplicative weights update with adaptive λ-parameterization) against vanilla optimistic gradient ascent (OGA), the Euclidean instance of optimistic mirror descent from Rakhlin & Sridharan (2013). On the tested 5×5 rank-1 game, OGA provably enters a limit cycle — the Nash gap oscillates indefinitely and the strategy trajectories trace a closed loop on the simplex (Figure 1) — while Algorithm 1 converges to a Nash equilibrium with the Nash gap monotonically decreasing to zero (Figure 2). This failure of OGA is not an artifact of the specific game: the paper proves (Example 3.2) that rank-1 games do not satisfy the Minty variational inequality (MVI) or weak MVI criterion, which are the structural conditions under which standard optimistic methods are known to converge. The proposed method overcomes this by reducing the rank-1 game to a parameterized zero-sum game and adding a regularization term (xᵀa − λ)² that enforces the coupling condition xᵀa = λ required for the zero-sum NE to coincide with the rank-1 NE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline algorithmic framework of Adsul et al. (2021) — which computes exact Nash equilibria in rank-1 games via binary search on a parameter λ combined with linear programming — into a decentralized online learning setting using Optimistic Multiplicative Weights Update (OMWU) as the inner-loop solver. It departs from the broader line of work on learning Nash equilibria (zero-sum games, potential games) by targeting rank-1 bimatrix games, the simplest non-trivial generalization beyond zero-sum, and demonstrates that these games fail the Minty variational inequality criterion that underpins convergence of standard optimistic mirror descent and extra-gradient methods. The key technical departure is the introduction of a regularization term (xᵀa − λ)² into the parameterized zero-sum objective, which ensures that stationary points of the modified min-max problem correspond to approximate equilibria of the original rank-1 game.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that rank-1 games — a natural and minimal extension of zero-sum games — admit efficient online learning algorithms with O(1/ε² log(1/ε)) convergence, filling a gap between the well-understood zero-sum/potential game settings and the PPAD-hard regime (rank ≥ 3). The negative result that even these simple games violate the Minty criterion is conceptually important, as it delineates the boundary of where standard first-order methods can be expected to converge. Open questions include whether a matching lower bound exists (suggesting the rate is optimal for this approach), and whether the parameterized zero-sum reduction can be extended to rank-2 games, whose computational complexity remains unresolved. The practical relevance to GAN training and multi-agent RL is indirect but the structural insights about when optimism suffices versus when additional mechanisms are needed are broadly applicable.
