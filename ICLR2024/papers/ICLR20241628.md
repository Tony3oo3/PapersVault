## Problem

Existing abstaining classifiers for fairness (e.g., LTD, FSCS) either lack hard fairness guarantees, support only a single fairness definition, or sacrifice group-level accuracy without provable bounds. No prior method simultaneously provides feasibility guarantees, compatibility with multiple fairness notions, hard disparity constraints, and a no-harm guarantee that each group's accuracy is no worse than the original classifier.

## Input / Output

FAN is a post-hoc wrapper around a pre-trained baseline classifier h: it consumes the feature vector X and the baseline's confidence score h(X) ∈ [0,1] (no retraining of h), and produces either a final binary label (possibly flipped from h's prediction) or an abstention decision deferring to a human. The abstention and flip decisions are parameterized by per-group thresholds δ_z (max abstention rate), ε (max disparity), and η_z (error-rate slack), all user-specified. At inference time, only the lightweight surrogate AB and FB models run, so the overhead is minimal compared to solving the IP.

## Method

FAN uses a two-stage procedure. Stage I formulates an Integer Program (IP-Main) over binary variables ω (abstain) and f (flip) for each training sample, minimizing 0-1 loss on non-abstained samples subject to hard constraints on disparity (DP, EOp, or EOd), per-group abstention rate, and a no-harm error-rate bound; quadratic terms are linearized via McCormick envelopes. Stage II trains two MLP surrogate models — an Abstention Block (AB) and a Flip Block (FB) — end-to-end on the IP solutions as labels, so they generalize the abstention/flip patterns to unseen test data without requiring ground-truth labels. A Prediction Adjustment step reassigns IP decisions to the lowest-confidence samples within each (y, ŷ_b, z) stratum to eliminate solution non-uniqueness and improve surrogate learnability. The paper further derives closed-form feasibility conditions (Theorem 4.1 for DP; always-feasible for EOp/EOd) that characterize the minimum abstention rate δ_z required as a function of ε, η_z, and group qualification rates τ_z.

## Results

FAN is evaluated on three real-world tabular datasets (Adult, Compas, Law) under three fairness notions (Demographic Parity, Equal Opportunity, Equalized Odds). The baseline optimal classifier achieves 92.08%, 72.33%, and 82.86% overall training accuracy on Adult, Compas, and Law respectively, with DP disparities of 0.59, 0.19, and 0.16. FAN achieves the largest disparity reduction across all datasets while the no-harm constraint ensures group-level accuracy is maintained or improved relative to the baseline. Surrogate models (AB and FB) learn the IP solutions with high training accuracy (e.g., 94.23% for AB and 94.26% for FB on Adult under DP at δ=0.1), confirming effective generalization from the integer programming stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAN is compared against two abstention-based fairness baselines: LTD (Madras et al., 2018) and FSCS (Lee et al., 2021). FAN outperforms both on disparity reduction across all three datasets and fairness notions while simultaneously maintaining or improving group-level accuracy (the no-harm property), whereas LTD and FSCS trade accuracy for fairness. LTD introduces abstention as a soft penalty in the objective, making precise abstention-rate control difficult; FAN instead enforces a hard per-group abstention-rate constraint via integer programming. FSCS is designed only for precision disparity and does not accommodate other fairness definitions (DP, EOp, EOd) or provide a no-harm guarantee. In the paper's visual comparisons (Figures 3, 9–11), FAN consistently achieves the greatest disparity reduction at comparable or lower abstention rates, and in many settings its group accuracy exceeds the baseline optimal classifier, a property neither LTD nor FSCS guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAN extends the fair selective classification line initiated by LTD (Madras et al., 2018b) and FSCS (Lee et al., 2021), which used soft penalty terms to balance fairness and abstention. The most direct methodological ancestor is Mozannar et al. (2023), who applied Mixed-Integer Programming to learning-to-defer; FAN repurposes the IP paradigm but adds hard fairness constraints, a no-harm accuracy guarantee, and a surrogate-model stage for generalization. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the fairness-accuracy tradeoff literature (Kleinberg et al., 2016; Menon & Williamson, 2018) by arguing that abstention relaxes both the data distribution and the hypothesis space, thereby circumventing the tradeoff rather than merely managing it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first theoretical characterization of the feasibility region—linking disparity tolerance, error-rate slack, and required abstention rate—gives practitioners a principled way to set design parameters before committing computational resources, which is practically important since high abstention rates are infeasible due to limited human review capacity. The no-harm guarantee (each group's accuracy is provably no worse than the baseline) addresses a real deployment concern that prior fair-selective methods ignored. Key limitations include restriction to binary classification on tabular data, dependence on true labels during training, and the gap between IP-optimal decisions and surrogate-model generalization; extending to multi-class settings and reducing IP complexity for large-scale problems remain open.
