## Problem

Existing perturbation-based time series explainers (e.g., Dynamask, Extrmask) generate perturbations that are either out-of-distribution for the explained model or still contain informative signal, leading to unfaithful saliency maps. This problem is exacerbated in heterogeneous samples where salient features vary across instances, causing cross-sample interference in shared mask parameters and significant generalization errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241696/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of multivariate time series (N×T×D) and a frozen black-box predictor f (typically a 1-layer GRU with 200 hidden units). It outputs a sample-specific, binary-skewed saliency mask m ∈ [0,1]^{N×T×D} identifying salient time steps and observation dimensions, along with learned counterfactual perturbations x^r that replace non-salient regions. During inference, random noise is removed from the gates to yield deterministic masks, and the perturbation function (a bidirectional GRU) and trend function (per-observation MLPs) are fixed.

## Method

ContraLSP learns a perturbation function φ via triplet-based contrastive learning: each counterfactual x_i^r is treated as an anchor, and a 2-kmeans partition of the batch into positive/negative clusters selects K⁺ nearest positives and K⁻ random negatives, with a Manhattan-distance triplet loss (margin b=1) pushing perturbations toward the negative cluster while an L1 term limits displacement. For mask generation, sample-specific sparse gates inject fixed-variance Gaussian noise into learnable parameters μ_i, and a per-observation trend function τ (MLP) modulates the gates through sigmoid-weighted units to enforce temporal smoothness while preserving binary skew. The l0 sparsity is approximated analytically via the Gaussian error function over the noisy gate outputs. The full objective jointly optimizes prediction fidelity between original and perturbed inputs, the erf-based sparsity penalty (weight α), and the contrastive loss (weight β), with all three parameter sets {μ, θ₁, θ₂} updated end-to-end.

## Results

On the white-box regression benchmarks (Rare-Time and Rare-Observation), ContraLSP achieves near-perfect recall of salient features (AUR 0.97 and 1.00) while reducing mask entropy by 65–97% compared to the strongest baseline Extrmask (S_m 4.65 vs 13.10 on Rare-Time; 0.32 vs 9.55 on Rare-Observation). On the real-world MIMIC-III mortality task (20% masking), ContraLSP outperforms Extrmask on 7/8 metrics, achieving 17.44 Comprehensiveness (vs 10.85) and 0.357 Cross-Entropy (vs 0.318) under zero substitution. Across the black-box Switch-Feature and State simulations, ContraLSP attains the best AUP (0.98 and 0.90) and an average 94.75% improvement in information content I_m over the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ContraLSP decisively outperforms all gradient-based and occlusion baselines (FO, AFO, IG, SVS, GradSHAP, DeepLIFT, LIME, RETAIN), which achieve AUR ≈ 0.13–0.16 and S_m ≈ 47–57 on the regression tasks, indicating they fail to identify the sparse salient regions. Among perturbation-based methods, Dynamask (AUR 0.67, S_m 37.24 on Rare-Time) and Extrmask (AUR 0.88, S_m 13.10) are the strongest competitors, but ContraLSP surpasses both by a wide margin on AUR, I_m, and S_m. On MIMIC-III, ContraLSP beats Extrmask on 7/8 metrics (winning on CE, Sufficiency, and Comprehensiveness under both substitution strategies, and on Accuracy under zero substitution), with the largest gap in Comprehensiveness (17.44 vs 10.85). The paper notes that when heterogeneous groups (DiffGroups) are present, mask-based baselines like Dynamask and Extrmask degrade significantly (e.g., Extrmask AUR drops from 0.88 to 0.83), while ContraLSP remains relatively stable (0.97 → 0.94), demonstrating robustness to inter-sample correlation. Ablation confirms both the triplet contrastive loss and the trend function are necessary: removing either degrades AUP by 0.06–0.13 and reduces I_m substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

ContraLSP extends the perturbation-based explanation framework established by Fong & Vedaldi (2017) and Fong et al. (2019), which learns binary masks to identify salient features by comparing original and perturbed predictions. In the time series domain, it builds directly on Dynamask (Crabbé & Van Der Schaar, 2021) and Extrmask (Enguehard, 2023), which introduced temporal smoothing and learned perturbations respectively, but addresses their distribution-shift and label-leakage weaknesses by replacing fixed or zero-anchored perturbations with counterfactuals generated via triplet contrastive learning. The locally sparse gate mechanism is adapted from Yang et al. (2022) and Yamada et al. (2020), with the sigmoid-weighted smooth constraint borrowed from Elfwing et al. (2018), repurposed here to enforce temporal trend consistency in the mask rather than activation smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a concrete and under-addressed failure mode in time series XAI: perturbation-based explainers can produce out-of-distribution or still-informative perturbations, undermining faithfulness—particularly in heterogeneous samples where salient features vary across instances. The counterfactual contrastive formulation offers a label-free mechanism to push perturbations toward negative-sample regions while preserving domain validity, which is practically important for clinical and financial time series where distributional assumptions are fragile. However, the authors acknowledge that label-free contrastive generation may not produce sufficiently strong counterfactuals, and the sparse gate hyperparameters (α, β, δ) require per-dataset tuning, limiting plug-and-play applicability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long sequences or high-dimensional observation spaces, and whether the contrastive objective can be strengthened with weak supervision without sacrificing the label-free property.
