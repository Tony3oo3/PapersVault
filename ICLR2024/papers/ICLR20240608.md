## Problem

Retrieval-augmented LLMs for open-domain QA typically require fine-tuning to effectively incorporate retrieved passages, which is infeasible for modern black-box API models. Naive prompting (simply appending passages) underexploits retrieved contexts, and how to improve retrieval-augmented LLMs via prompting alone has been under-explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240608/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question and N retrieved passages (typically N=10 from BM25, DPR, or Contriever over Wikipedia or dataset-specific corpora) as input. It produces a short predicted answer (≤3 words) along with candidate-conditioned summarizations that serve as explicit rationales. The pipeline internally generates K=2 answer candidates, one conditional summary per candidate, and a validity/ranking score per summary before selecting the final answer.

## Method

SURE operates entirely via zero-shot prompting in three stages: (1) an LLM generates K answer candidates from the question and retrieved passages; (2) for each candidate, the LLM writes a conditional summarization of the retrieved passages that specifically supports that candidate (reducing ~1035 words of passages to ~93 words of focused context); (3) the best candidate is selected by combining instance-wise validity (a binary check whether the summary properly supports its candidate rather than the other) and pair-wise ranking (comparing summaries two-at-a-time for relative informativeness, with order-bias mitigation). The key insight is that the quality of a conditional summary reflects the plausibility of its candidate: a correct answer yields a coherent, well-supported summary, while an incorrect one produces a degenerate or contradictory one, making the summary itself a discriminative signal for answer selection.

## Results

On four ODQA benchmarks (NQ, WebQ, 2Wiki, HotpotQA) with ChatGPT and BM25 (N=10), SURE achieves an average EM of 31.2 and F1 of 40.1, improving over the Base prompting baseline (26.6 EM / 36.1 F1) by 4.6% EM and 4.0% F1. SURE generalizes across LLMs and retrievers: with LLaMA2-chat + BM25 it yields a 7.9% EM gain (19.7→27.6), and with GPT-4 + BM25 a 2.9% EM gain (30.3→33.2). On the long-form ASQA dataset, SURE improves ROUGE-L from 38.00 to 39.83 and STR-EM from 39.81 to 42.63 over Base prompting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SURE outperforms all seven baselines on average across the four ODQA datasets. Compared to the closest competitor, Self-verification (Weng et al., 2022), SURE gains 7.2% EM (31.2 vs. 24.0) and 6.0% F1 (40.1 vs. 34.1); the gap is largest on NQ (33.5 vs. 25.2 EM) and 2Wiki (32.8 vs. 23.2 EM). SURE also surpasses the ensemble-based Rerank (Lazaridou et al., 2022) by 7.6% EM and RePlug (Shi et al., 2023) by 7.1% EM, indicating that per-passage aggregation strategies are less effective than SURE's candidate-conditioned summarization under zero-shot black-box API settings. Against Chain-of-thoughts (Kojima et al., 2022), SURE leads by 10.5% EM, showing that explicit intermediate reasoning prompts are inferior to SURE's structured verification. In the passage-reranking comparison (Table 4), SURE's similarity-based reranking (Sent-encoder(SURE)) achieves 21.4 average EM, beating LLM-rerank (Sun et al., 2023) at 19.6 EM and the generic-summation variant at 19.9 EM. Finally, in the MCQ-prompt ablation (Robinson et al., 2023), SURE's full pipeline (31.2 EM) exceeds the MCQ prompt (29.8 EM), confirming that the conditional summarization and verification steps add value beyond simply presenting candidates as multiple-choice options.

## Relation to Prior Work

SURE positions itself within the retrieval-augmented LLM literature, departing from fine-tuning-based approaches (RAG, REALM, RETRO) that are infeasible for black-box APIs, and from naive prompting strategies that simply append retrieved passages. It builds conceptually on per-passage aggregation methods (Lazaridou et al., RePlug) and self-verification (Weng et al.), but replaces their voting or masking mechanisms with a structured pipeline: candidate-conditioned summarization followed by instance-wise validity checking and pair-wise ranking. The key conceptual departure is treating summarization as a candidate-specific reasoning step rather than a generic compression, leveraging the observation that LLMs struggle with long naive contexts (Liu et al., 2023b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SURE offers a practical, zero-shot prompting recipe that works across black-box LLMs (ChatGPT, GPT-4) and open-source models (LLaMA-2) without any fine-tuning, making it immediately deployable in real-world search and chatbot systems. The generated conditional summaries serve a dual purpose as both a selection mechanism and an explainable rationale, which is valuable for user trust. Open questions include whether the fixed K=2 candidate limit can be relaxed, whether the approach extends to multi-hop or long-form settings beyond short-answer QA, and whether the cost of multiple LLM calls can be further reduced via smaller models for verification steps (partially explored in the paper).
