## Problem

Ising Graph Learning (IGL) achieves orders-of-magnitude speedup over GNNs for binary graph problems by harnessing the spontaneous energy decrease in CMOS-based Ising machines, but it is fundamentally limited to binary data because Ising spins have only two states. The paper proves that naively replacing binary spins with real-valued ones in the Ising Hamiltonian yields an energy landscape with no local minima (all stationary points are saddle points), making the model inexpressive for real-valued ML. A clear pathway to extend nature-powered graph learning to the real-valued domain remained elusive.

## Input / Output

The method consumes real-valued spatial-temporal graph data where nodes are physical sensors (traffic detectors, air quality monitors, taxi zones, etc.) and edges encode spatial correlations. For temporal prediction, spins are partitioned into previous, current, and future time steps; the previous and current step values are held fixed as known conditions while the future step spins are free to evolve. The output is a single real-valued prediction per node at the next time step, obtained when the dynamical system settles into its lowest-energy configuration.

## Method

NP-GL is a three-dimensional co-design of Hamiltonian, training algorithm, and hardware. The key architectural change replaces the linear self-reaction term in the Ising Hamiltonian with a quadratic term (h_i·σ_i²), which guarantees convexity and the existence of local minima for real-valued spins while preserving the all-to-all coupling structure. Training uses a conditional likelihood method that optimizes one spin at a time, enhanced by two techniques: Diagonal Line Reinforcement (DLR) to differentiate coupling from self-reaction parameters by orders of magnitude, and Spatial Pre-training coupled with Temporal Fine-tuning to initialize temporal couplings from learned spatial couplings. On the hardware side, the SOTA BRIM Ising machine is augmented by replacing each node's voltage regulator with a variable resistor, implementing the quadratic term in the circuit so that the CMOS dynamical system spontaneously drives real-valued spin voltages to their lowest-energy state at nanosecond latency and ~500 mW power.

## Results

Across 6 real-world datasets spanning traffic flow, air quality, taxi demand, and pandemic progression, NP-GL-L1 achieves the best MAE on all 6 datasets (e.g., 17.07 on PEMS04 vs. 17.65 for the best GNN baseline MegaCRN, and 22.04 on Texas COVID vs. 23.94 for DDGCRN). On RMSE, NP-GL-L1 is best on 5 of 6 datasets, with MegaCRN slightly ahead only on PEMS08 (21.03 vs. 21.69). The nature-based computer delivers an average inference speedup of 6.97×10³ over GPU/CPU baselines (ranging from 1.01×10³ on NYC Taxi to 1.70×10⁴ on Texas COVID) while consuming ~500 mW, yielding an overall ~10⁵× energy reduction compared to modern GPUs and CPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NP-GL-L1 (the best NP-GL variant using L1 loss) outperforms all five GNN baselines on MAE across every dataset, with margins ranging from ~0.02 (PM10 vs. MegaCRN) to ~1.9 (Texas COVID vs. DDGCRN). On RMSE, NP-GL-L1 wins on 5 of 6 datasets; the sole exception is PEMS08 where MegaCRN edges ahead (21.03 vs. 21.69). Among the GNN baselines, DDGCRN is the strongest competitor on NYC Taxi (MAE 3.059 vs. NP-GL-L1's 3.031) and Texas COVID (MAE 23.94 vs. 22.04), while MegaCRN is the closest rival on the traffic and air-quality datasets. ASTGCN is evaluated on only 4 of 6 datasets (no NYC Taxi or Texas COVID results) and is consistently outperformed. The speedup advantage is overwhelming: NP-GL's nature-based computer achieves 10³–10⁴× lower inference latency than both CPU and GPU execution of the GNN baselines, with an overall average speedup of 6.97×10³ and ~10⁵× lower energy consumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ising Graph Learning (IGL) paradigm, exemplified by Ising-Traffic (Pan et al., 2023) and Ising-CF (Liu et al., 2023), which were restricted to binary-valued problems, by providing a theoretical proof that the vanilla Ising Hamiltonian cannot support real values (all stationary points are saddle points) and then proposing a modified Hamiltonian with a quadratic self-reaction term. The hardware backbone is BRIM (Afoakwa et al., 2021), a CMOS-compatible, room-temperature Ising machine, which the authors augment with a single variable resistor per node to implement the new quadratic term. The training methodology draws on conditional likelihood optimization, augmented with two novel techniques (Diagonal Line Reinforcement and Spatial Pre-training with Temporal Fine-tuning) that differentiate the paper from prior IGL training approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NP-GL is positioned as the first end-to-end real-valued nature-powered ML solution that matches or exceeds GNN accuracy while delivering orders-of-magnitude speedup and energy savings, potentially opening a new hardware-software co-design paradigm for graph learning. However, the nature-based computer is evaluated via Cadence circuit simulation rather than fabricated silicon, so the reported 6.97×10³ speedup and 10⁵ energy reduction remain projections. Open questions include scalability to larger graphs (beyond the ~100-node scale tested), applicability to non-spatial-temporal graph tasks, and whether the CMOS circuit can be manufactured at scale with the required all-to-all connectivity. The narrow evaluation (4 applications, all spatial-temporal forecasting) leaves the generality of the approach untested.
