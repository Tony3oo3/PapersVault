## Problem

While Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) provides a principled width-scaling recipe that guarantees feature learning and hyperparameter transfer, no analogous theory exists for depth scaling. In practice, increasing the depth of residual networks beyond a moderate number of layers causes performance degradation and significant shifts in optimal hyperparameters, and the interaction between branch multipliers and learning-rate scaling with depth remains uncharacterized. The paper asks: what is the unique depth-scaling scheme that simultaneously preserves feature learning, maximizes feature diversity across adjacent layers, and ensures hyperparameter transferability from shallow to arbitrarily deep networks?

## Input / Output

The method is instantiated on vanilla residual networks with block depth 1 (a single biasless MLP layer per residual block) trained on CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB images) to produce 10-class labels. The input and output layers (U, V) are frozen during training; only the hidden weight matrices W^l are updated. A Mean Subtraction (MS) operation is applied to each block's activation before the residual addition, which plays a role analogous to the centering component of LayerNorm. Networks are trained with depths up to 2^10 = 1024 residual blocks, width n = 256, batch size 64, for 50 epochs using Adam.

## Method

Depth-μP prescribes two coupled scalings: (1) each residual branch g^l is multiplied by a/√L before being added to the skip connection, and (2) the effective parameter update for W^l is made proportional to 1/√L (concretely, Adam learning rate η/√L, while SGD uses constant η since the gradient already carries a 1/√L factor from the branch multiplier). Using the Tensor Programs framework, the paper rigorously proves that in the sequential limit n→∞ then L→∞, the training dynamics converge to a well-defined limit characterized by a system of functional integrals. A full classification of all (α, γ) depth parametrizations shows that α = γ = 1/2 is the unique choice that is simultaneously stable, nontrivial, faithful, induces feature learning, and achieves maximal feature diversity exponent κ = 1/2 (a Brownian-motion-like roughness in the layer-index path), whereas any α > 1/2 leads to redundant adjacent layers and α = 1 causes feature collapse into the input/output σ-algebra.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with vanilla ResNet (width 256, block depth 1, Adam, batch size 64, 50 epochs), Depth-μP (α=γ=1/2) maintains a stable optimal learning rate around 10⁻³ across depths from 2⁶ to 2¹⁰ (64 to 1024 layers), while consistently improving training loss as depth increases. In contrast, standard parametrization with learning-rate scaling (α=0, γ=1) exhibits a significant shift in optimal learning rate with depth and degrading training loss at large depths, and standard parametrization without any depth scaling (α=γ=0) shows even more severe hyperparameter drift. The block multiplier hyperparameter 'a' also converges to a stable optimal value under Depth-μP as depth grows, confirming full hyperparameter transferability across both width and depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three depth parametrization strategies rather than competing model architectures. Depth-μP (α=γ=1/2) is the proposed method; the two baselines are standard parametrization with learning-rate scaling (α=0, γ=1, where the branch multiplier is fixed at a=2⁻³) and standard parametrization without any depth scaling (α=γ=0, with a=2⁻⁴). Under Depth-μP, the optimal learning rate converges to ~10⁻³ by depth 2⁶ and remains stable through depth 2¹⁰, and training loss monotonically improves with depth. Both standard parametrization baselines fail: the α=0, γ=1 variant shows a clear shift in optimal LR and loss degradation at large depths, while the α=γ=0 variant shows even more pronounced LR drift. The paper does not report final test accuracy comparisons; the empirical evidence is framed around hyperparameter transferability and training-loss stability as the primary metrics, which is the core claim of the theoretical contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends μP (Maximal Update Parametrization, Yang et al. 2022), which provided optimal width scaling with hyperparameter transfer, to the depth dimension, yielding Depth-μP. It builds on the Tensor Programs framework (Yang & Littwin 2023) for rigorous infinite-width-then-depth analysis, and departs from prior depth-scaling work (Hanin & Rolnick 2018; Zhang et al. 2019; Hayou et al. 2021; Noci et al. 2022) that focused primarily on initialization stability without addressing training dynamics or learning rate scaling. The paper also contrasts with Jelassi et al. (2023), who showed depth^{-3/2} learning rate scaling for MLPs without skip connections, by providing a complete recipe for residual architectures that simultaneously guarantees stability, feature learning, and hyperparameter transfer across depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Depth-μP provides the first principled, complete recipe for depth scaling that guarantees feature learning in the infinite depth limit while enabling hyperparameter transfer from shallow to deep models, filling a critical gap left by width-only μP. The classification of all (α, γ) parametrizations and the identification of α=γ=1/2 as the unique optimal point, along with the feature diversity phase transition, gives practitioners a clear theoretical foundation for scaling depth. Key limitations include the restriction to MLP blocks with block depth 1 and the absence of empirical validation on more complex architectures like Transformers, which the authors explicitly leave as future work. The practical impact will depend on whether these principles transfer to attention-based architectures and multi-layer residual blocks.
