## Problem

Existing instance-reweighted adversarial training methods determine per-sample importance weights via heuristics or geometric intuitions, lacking rigorous theoretical justification. Additionally, adversarial training exhibits severe non-uniform robustness across classes (e.g., 49% average but only 14% for the weakest class on CIFAR-10), a disparity that worsens under class imbalance. The paper asks whether instance weights can be determined in a principled, theoretically grounded way that simultaneously boosts robustness on the most vulnerable examples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, SVHN, and GTSRB; 96×96 for STL-10) and outputs class labels. Adversarial perturbations are constrained to the ℓ∞ ball of radius ε=8/255. Beyond the class prediction, the framework jointly learns a per-instance weight vector over the training set, where the KL-divergence regularized DRO objective assigns higher weights to harder/more vulnerable samples. Evaluation reports standard accuracy, robust accuracy under 20-step PGD and AutoAttacks, and robust accuracy restricted to the 30% most vulnerable classes (RA-Tail-30).

## Method

DONE formulates a doubly robust bilevel optimization: the inner level finds worst-case ℓ∞-bounded adversarial perturbations, while the outer level maximizes a KL-divergence-regularized weighted adversarial loss over the probability simplex, yielding a closed-form softmax weight solution that produces a novel compositional bilevel optimization (CBO) problem. To handle the non-differentiability of the constrained inner minimizer, the authors apply a log-barrier penalty that keeps the solution strictly interior, making implicit differentiation applicable. The resulting CID (Compositional Implicit Differentiation) algorithm tracks a running average of outer-level composed function values (inspired by SCGD) and computes hypergradients via the implicit function theorem, with a proven convergence rate of O(1/√T + (1−αμ)^K). Three inner-loop solvers (GD, Adam, PGD) are instantiated, and the KL regularization parameter r is decayed during training to transition from near-uniform weights toward worst-case emphasis.

## Results

On balanced CIFAR-10, DONE-PGD achieves 58.54% robust accuracy against 20-step PGD (RA-PGD), outperforming the strongest baseline MAIL (55.12%) by +3.4%, and the standard AT method (49.29%) by +9.3%. The improvement on the 30% most vulnerable classes (RA-Tail-30) is particularly pronounced: DONE-ADAM reaches 40.36% vs. MAIL's 37.30% and AT's 28.35%. On imbalanced CIFAR-10 (0.2 ratio), DONE-GD achieves 48.29% RA-PGD and 17.19% RA-Tail-30, compared to AT's 42.37% and 6.25% respectively. On SVHN, DONE-PGD attains 66.20% RA-PGD and 48.32% RA-AA (AutoAttacks), surpassing AT by +8.4% and +12.1% on those metrics respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DONE outperforms all eight baselines on every robustness metric (RA-PGD, RA-Tail-30, RA-AA) across all four datasets. Against the standard AT method (Madry et al., 2017), DONE-PGD gains +9.3% RA-PGD and +11.8% RA-Tail-30 on balanced CIFAR-10, with even larger margins under class imbalance (+5.9% RA-PGD, +10.9% RA-Tail-30). Compared to the strongest instance-reweighted baselines MAIL (Liu et al., 2021) and GAIRAT (Zhang et al., 2020), DONE achieves +3.4% RA-PGD and +3.1% RA-Tail-30 on balanced CIFAR-10, and notably also surpasses them on RA-AA (AutoAttacks) where heuristic-weighted methods underperform standard AT. Against FAT (Zhang et al., 2020a) and WMMR (Zeng et al., 2021), DONE leads by +12% and +9% RA-PGD respectively on balanced CIFAR-10. In the fast AT setting (1-step attacks, 25 epochs), DONE achieves 55.17% RA-PGD and 37.13% RA-Tail-30, outperforming Fast-BAT (Zhang et al., 2022) by +6.0% RA-PGD and +11.1% RA-Tail-30, while Fast-AT (Wong et al., 2020) and Fast-AT-GA (Andriushchenko & Flammarion, 2020) trail by +9.8% and +7.6% RA-PGD respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) instance-reweighted adversarial training (GAIRAT, MAIL, WMMR), which assigns per-sample importance weights but relies on heuristics or geometric intuitions; (2) bilevel optimization for adversarial training (Fast-BAT, Zhang et al. 2022), which decouples the attack and defense objectives; and (3) distributionally robust optimization (DRO), which provides a principled way to optimize over worst-case distributions. The key departure is formulating the weight determination as a KL-regularized DRO problem nested inside a bilevel optimization, yielding a novel 'compositional bilevel optimization' (CBO) problem that had no prior theoretical treatment. The log-barrier penalty reformulation to restore differentiability of the inner minimizer, combined with a running-average estimator inspired by SCGD, distinguishes the algorithmic contribution from standard bilevel or compositional solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The doubly-robust formulation addresses a practically critical gap: traditional AT and even heuristic-reweighted AT produce severely non-uniform robustness across classes (e.g., 49% average vs. 14% worst-class on CIFAR10), which a targeted attacker can exploit. By optimizing weights through DRO, the method systematically boosts the weakest examples without sacrificing average performance, and the effect is most pronounced under class imbalance where prior methods collapse (RA-Tail-30 drops to 6% for AT but reaches 17% for DONE). Open questions include scalability to larger architectures/datasets (only ResNet-18 on 32×32 and 96×96 images is tested), the sensitivity to the KL-regularization decay schedule, and whether the CBO framework generalizes to other reweighting contexts (domain adaptation, data augmentation) the authors mention as future work.
