## Problem

In human-involved RL scenarios (healthcare, e-learning, robotics), offline trajectories collected from homogeneous behavioral policies provide limited and biased coverage of the state-action space, severely degrading off-policy evaluation (OPE) performance. Existing data augmentation methods are designed for RL policy optimization (targeting high-reward regions) or generic time-series generation, and do not respect the Markovian structure of MDP trajectories or the need for comprehensive coverage including low-reward regions that OPE requires. No prior method augments historical trajectories specifically to scaffold OPE.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline trajectories as (state, action, reward, next-state) tuples collected under a behavioral policy, along with the behavioral policy itself for action sampling during generation. It outputs augmented trajectories of the same horizon T, where only the identified Potential Sub-Trajectory (PST) segments are replaced with VAE-MDP-generated samples while non-PST segments are preserved verbatim. These augmented trajectories are then fed into standard OPE estimators (IS, FQE, DICE, DR, MB) to produce estimated policy returns. The PST length W is data-driven (typically ≤11 steps in Adroit) rather than spanning the full horizon, and the number of augmented samples V per PST set is a hyperparameter.

## Method

OAT is a three-stage pipeline. First, PST mining maps states to discrete clusters via MT-TICC (Toeplitz inverse covariance clustering), then identifies temporal discrete sub-sequences (TDSSs) with high cross-trajectory support (≥ξ), and selects the corresponding sub-trajectories as Potential Sub-Trajectories—regions where behavioral homogeneity implies under-coverage. Second, a VAE adapted to the MDP setting (VAE-MDP) is trained per PST set using an LSTM-based encoder q_ω(z_t|z_{t-1},a_{t-1},s_t) and decoder p_η(z_t|z_{t-1},a_{t-1}), p_η(s_t|z_t), p_η(r_{t-1}|z_t), maximizing an ELBO that jointly reconstructs states and rewards while regularizing the latent posterior toward a Gaussian prior. Third, a fusing step splices the generated augmented sub-trajectories back into original trajectories at the PST locations, selecting the augmented sample whose endpoint state-action is closest (Euclidean distance) to the original, and conditioning the initial generated state on the preceding original state to ensure smooth transitions. The key distinction from prior augmentation is the targeted, sub-trajectory-level generation that respects Markovian dynamics while preserving object-specific information in non-PST segments.

## Results

On Adroit (averaged across 5 OPE methods and 4 tasks), OAT reduces MAE by 40–70% relative to no augmentation (e.g., Pen: 3014→1042, Hammer: 5266→3188) and improves Spearman rank correlation from negative/near-zero to 0.28–0.68 across tasks. OAT also achieves the best Regret@1 (e.g., Hammer: 0.084 vs. 0.34 no-aug) and Regret@5 (e.g., Relocate: 0.158 vs. 0.298 no-aug). In real-world sepsis treatment and intelligent tutoring, OAT is the only method that consistently improves both MAE and rank correlation, while most baselines yield neutral-to-negative effects. The ablation VAE-MDP (without PST mining) is significantly outperformed by OAT on all metrics, confirming the importance of targeted sub-trajectory augmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OAT outperforms all nine augmentation baselines on the majority of metrics across all four Adroit tasks. Against TDA (the closest RL-oriented baseline), OAT achieves substantially better rank correlation (e.g., Hammer: 0.608 vs. −0.298; Door: 0.676 vs. 0.118) and lower Regret@1 (Hammer: 0.084 vs. 0.56), though TDA matches OAT on Pen MAE (1030 vs. 1042). The VAE baseline (vanilla, non-MDP-adapted) is consistently worse than OAT on rank correlation and regret metrics, while TimeGAN and SPAWNER show mixed results—TimeGAN occasionally matches OAT on Relocate rank correlation (0.408 vs. 0.360) but lags badly on MAE and other tasks. The VAE-MDP ablation (OAT without PST mining) is the second-best method overall but is clearly outperformed by OAT on rank correlation (e.g., Hammer: 0.020 vs. 0.608) and Regret@1 (Hammer: 0.454 vs. 0.084), confirming that targeted sub-trajectory augmentation is critical. In real-world e-learning and healthcare, OAT is the only method that improves both MAE and rank correlation, whereas most baselines (Permutation, Jittering, Scaling, VAE, TimeGAN, SPAWNER, DGW) produce neutral-to-negative percentage improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

OAT builds on VAE-based generative augmentation (Barak et al., 2022) and time-series augmentation methods (TimeGAN, SPAWNER, DGW), but departs from them by explicitly modeling the Markovian transition structure of MDP trajectories rather than treating them as generic multivariate time series. It also diverges from RL-oriented augmentation (TDA, permutation, jittering, scaling), which targets state-space enrichment for policy optimization, by instead aiming for comprehensive joint state-action coverage needed when evaluating heterogeneous target policies. The PST mining step leverages discrete representation mapping (TICC) from time-series pattern mining to identify sub-trajectories with homogeneous behavioral patterns, a technique repurposed here for targeted augmentation rather than classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap in deploying OPE for high-stakes human-involved domains (surgery, sepsis treatment, intelligent tutoring) where offline data is inherently limited and underrepresentative due to standardized human protocols. The key open question is whether augmented trajectories introduce distribution shift that existing OPE methods cannot handle, and whether theoretical error bounds can be established for the augmented data pipeline. The finding that standard augmentation methods (permutation, jittering, scaling) consistently degrade OPE performance in real-world settings—while targeted sub-trajectory augmentation helps—suggests that the Markovian and coverage-specific nature of OPE data demands purpose-built augmentation rather than generic time-series techniques.
