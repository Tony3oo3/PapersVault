## Problem

Identifying functional cell types in the visual system currently requires domain knowledge to handcraft fingerprinting stimuli and select response features, biasing discovery toward previously known types. In higher visual areas like V4, functional types remain largely unknown, and no unbiased, data-driven method exists that simultaneously discovers cell type clusters and generates interpretable stimuli that distinguish them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240898/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires a trained digital twin (a CNN ensemble with a shared core and neuron-specific Gaussian readout) that predicts each neuron's response to arbitrary visual stimuli, enabling receptive-field-centered stimulus optimization. Inputs are naturalistic video or image stimuli (e.g., 50-frame × 18×16 px × 2-channel UV/green for mouse retina; 100×100 px grayscale images for macaque V4) and the resulting predicted response traces. Outputs are one Most Discriminative Stimulus (MDS) per discovered cluster plus the neuron-to-cluster assignments; at deployment, the pre-computed MDS can be shown to new neurons to assign cell types in ~1 min 45 s without retraining the digital twin.

## Method

The algorithm alternates in an EM-like fashion between an M-step and an E-step. In the M-step, a stimulus x_c is optimized via gradient ascent (10 steps, learning rate 100) on a softmax-based objective (Eq. 1) that maximizes the mean predicted response of the target cluster while penalizing mean responses of all other clusters, with a temperature parameter τ=1.6 controlling the suppression strength; the stimulus is centered on each neuron's receptive field through the digital twin. In the E-step, each neuron is reassigned to the cluster whose MDS elicits the strongest predicted response. A splitting procedure iteratively subdivides clusters and retains sub-clusters only if the mean objective ⟨J_c⟩_c improves, determining the optimal number of clusters. The digital twin's core-readout architecture (space-time separable convolutions for retina; adversarially trained ResNet-50 for V4) is what makes receptive-field-centered optimization tractable, and the resulting MDS are interpretable visual patterns that unambiguously identify each functional type.

## Results

On the mouse RGC dataset (2,448 neurons, 17 Baden types), MDS clustering recovers 7 functional clusters that align with the known retinal hierarchy (OFF, ON-OFF, fast ON, slow ON, color-opponent), with the cluster response matrix showing strong diagonal dominance (100 on-diagonal vs. 11–84 off-diagonal). On marmoset RGCs (167 neurons), 4 clusters are found (fast/slow ON and OFF), and on macaque V4 (1,030 neurons), 12 well-separated clusters emerge. In a simulated in-silico experiment, MDS outperforms the conventional chirp-stimulus classifier (73.5% accuracy) after 9 repetitions (1 min 45 s), saving 20% (27 s) of experimental time. Clustering is robust across initializations: median ARI of 0.67 (mouse, 11 runs), 0.96 (marmoset, 9 runs), and 0.53 (macaque V4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Baden et al. (2016) 17-type taxonomy, MDS clustering finds 7 coarser clusters that match the known functional hierarchy (OFF, ON-OFF, fast ON, slow ON, color-opponent) but cannot separate direction/orientation-selective subtypes because the digital twin lacks direction-selectivity modeling; a reduced classifier using only chirp responses (the same information MDS has access to) drops from 79% to 49% accuracy, confirming the finer Baden types are not distinguishable without additional features. Against the conventional chirp-stimulus classifier (Qiu et al., 2023) as a deployment baseline, MDS achieves higher cluster-identification accuracy after 9 repetitions (1 min 45 s) while saving 20% (27 s) of total experimental time compared to the 2 min 12 s required by the chirp protocol. The chirp classifier achieves 73.5% accuracy on the merged 7-cluster task, while MDS surpasses this threshold at 9 repeats under simulated Poisson noise. On marmoset RGCs, MDS clustering agrees well with an independent STA-based clustering baseline (Fig. 10), recovering the same fast/slow ON and OFF groupings. On macaque V4, no ground-truth taxonomy exists, so the 12 MDS clusters are validated only by internal consistency (ARI 0.53 across seeds) and interpretability of the resulting stimuli.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on deep predictive models ('digital twins') for neural response prediction and stimulus optimization. Prior work optimized Maximally Exciting Inputs (MEIs) for individual neurons (Walker et al., 2019) or for suppressing other neurons (Bashivan et al., 2019), and more recently for driving predefined groups (Ustyuzhaninov et al., 2022). The key departure here is formulating functional cell type discovery as a joint EM-style procedure that simultaneously discovers clusters and optimizes their Most Discriminative Stimuli, removing the need for hand-crafted fingerprinting stimuli or domain knowledge about which neurons belong together. Unlike Ustyuzhaninov et al. (2022), which clustered non-interpretable model parameters, this approach clusters directly on interpretable stimulus-response relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MDS framework provides a general-purpose, interpretable tool for discovering functional cell types in brain areas where no taxonomy exists (e.g., V4), and the resulting stimuli can be deployed as fast fingerprinting probes without retraining the digital twin—enabling on-the-fly cell typing during experiments. Key limitations include the assumption of discrete cell types (recent evidence suggests continuity), dependence on digital twin quality (e.g., direction selectivity was not captured), and the need for in vivo validation of the optimized stimuli. Open questions include whether MDS can be validated against transcriptomic data (PatchSeq, spatial transcriptomics), whether multiple MDS per cluster improve granularity (promising initial results for ON-OFF cells), and how the method extends to continuous or hierarchical type structures.
