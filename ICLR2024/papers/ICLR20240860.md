## Problem

Lifting 2D diffusion model outputs to 3D is inherently ambiguous because 2D priors lack 3D spatial knowledge, leading to multi-view inconsistency (e.g., multiple faces or hands). The paper identifies that geometric inconsistency—misplaced 3D structures that project identically in 2D—is the dominant cause, and that aligning 2D geometric priors with well-defined 3D shapes during lifting resolves the vast majority of these artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240860/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as user input and produces a 3D object represented either as a DMTet mesh or a NeRF (Instant-NGP) volume. Internally, the aligned diffusion model consumes the text prompt plus camera extrinsic parameters (distance 0.9–1.1, FOV 45°, elevation −10° to 45°) and outputs a 64×64 canonical coordinate map (CCM) where each pixel encodes the (x, y, z) position in a unit cube, anisotropically scaled to [0,1] per channel. The CCM is treated directly as a latent image, bypassing the VAE encoder/decoder during fine-tuning.

## Method

The core contribution is Aligned Geometric Priors (AGP): fine-tuning Stable Diffusion v2.1 to generate viewpoint-conditioned canonical coordinate maps rendered from ~270k canonically oriented Objaverse objects, with camera extrinsics injected via an MLP into the diffusion model's middle layer. Only coarse geometry (no appearance) is used for alignment, preserving the 2D diffusion model's generalizability to unseen shapes and textures. During text-to-3D optimization, the AGP operates as a parallel branch that renders the current 3D representation into a CCM and computes an additional SDS loss, which is weighted and combined with the original RGB-based SDS loss (L = λ_ori · L_ori + λ_align · L_align). This integration is representation-agnostic and has been demonstrated with both DMTet (Fantasia3D) and NeRF (DreamFusion) pipelines, achieving 85%+ multi-view consistency rates versus ~30% for baselines.

## Results

On 80 text prompts from the DreamFusion gallery, the paper's DMTet-based pipeline achieves an 87.5% 3D consistency rate and the NeRF-based pipeline achieves 88.8%, compared to 7.5%–35.0% for all baselines. On appearance quality (CLIP R-Precision), the NeRF-based method scores 77.5% R@1 and 84.9% R@5 with CLIP ViT-B/32, and 88.7% R@1 and 92.3% R@5 with CLIP ViT-B/16, substantially outperforming the best baseline (Magic3D at 60.1% R@1 / 71.5% R@5 with B/32). The paper also reports a user study where its method is consistently preferred over competing methods for 3D consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method decisively outperforms all six baselines on the primary metric of 3D consistency rate, achieving 87.5–88.8% versus a range of 7.5% (SJC) to 35.0% (Magic3D-IF) for competing methods — a margin of over 50 percentage points. On appearance quality (CLIP R-Precision, Table 2), the NeRF-based variant also leads all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., 77.5% R@1 vs. Magic3D's 60.1% with CLIP B/32), confirming that the coarse-geometry-only alignment does not degrade visual fidelity. Against the concurrent work MVDream, the paper reports qualitative side-by-side comparisons showing that MVDream overfits to limited 3D training data, missing key prompt concepts (e.g., 'backpack', 'rocket', 'squirrel') and producing less photorealistic appearances due to fine-tuning on synthetic multi-view renderings. The DMTet-based pipeline specifically improves upon its base method Fantasia3D (32.5% → 87.5%), demonstrating that the AGP module alone is sufficient to resolve the multi-view inconsistency without requiring hand-crafted initialization shapes. The NeRF-based pipeline also surpasses DreamFusion-IF (30.0% → 88.8%), validating the approach across different 3D representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) framework introduced by DreamFusion by adding a second, geometry-specific supervision signal derived from a fine-tuned 2D diffusion model that produces canonical coordinate maps (CCMs) conditioned on camera extrinsics. It departs from concurrent work like MVDream, which fine-tunes a multi-view diffusion model on synthetic renderings, by using only coarse geometric information (CCMs) rather than full appearance renderings, and by remaining a modular add-on that can be plugged into heterogeneous pipelines (DMTet-based Fantasia3D, NeRF-based DreamFusion) rather than constituting a standalone generation method. The camera-conditioning mechanism follows MVDream, but the purpose is explicitly limited to producing rough geometry that will be refined by the original appearance priors, rather than generating photorealistic multi-view images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that geometric inconsistency, not appearance inconsistency, is the dominant cause of multi-view artifacts in text-to-3D—reframes how the community should think about the problem, and the 'coarse alignment' philosophy (using minimal 3D information to avoid overfitting) offers a broadly applicable design principle. The modular nature of AGP, which can be inserted into existing pipelines without modifying their appearance modeling, makes it practically attractive for practitioners already invested in specific 3D representations. Key open questions include whether appearance inconsistency can be addressed without the overfitting to synthetic renderings that the authors observed, and whether the approach generalizes beyond single-object generation to scenes or articulated objects.
