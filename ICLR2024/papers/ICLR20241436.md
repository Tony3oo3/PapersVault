## Problem

Global group fairness criteria like Demographic Parity and Equalized Odds, measured as population-wide averages, can mask persistent local disparities in subregions of the feature space where sensitive groups are treated unequally. This paper tackles the harder problem of enforcing fairness in local subgroups that are unknown at training time, without requiring expert-defined comparable categories or intersectional fairness constraints.

## Input / Output

The method operates on tabular data where each sample consists of a feature vector x ∈ R^d, a binary sensitive attribute s, and a binary label y. The sensitive attribute is used only during training (by the adversary and ratio networks) and is not required at test time. The output is a standard binary class prediction from the predictor network f, with fairness enforced implicitly through the adversarial training objective rather than as a post-hoc constraint.

## Method

ROAD combines fair adversarial learning with Distributionally Robust Optimization by introducing a third network r that learns instance-level importance weights to reweight the adversarial loss. The optimization is a min-max problem: the predictor f minimizes its task loss while an adversary g (reconstructing s from f(x)) and the ratio network r jointly maximize a weighted sensitive-reconstruction loss, with r focusing attention on feature regions where the adversary finds sensitive reconstruction easiest (i.e., locally unfair regions). Two key constraints shape r: a conditional validity constraint (per-group normalization ensuring q(s)=p(s)) that prevents the fairness effort from concentrating on the majority sensitive group, and a KL-divergence ball controlled by temperature τ that enforces spatial smoothness of the reweighting. The parametric r network (a small MLP with exponential output) introduces Lipschitz-based locality, ensuring nearby individuals receive similar fairness attention, while a non-parametric variant (BROAD) uses a closed-form Boltzmann distribution over adversarial losses.

## Results

The paper evaluates ROAD primarily through Pareto front plots (Accuracy vs. Worst-1-DI) rather than single-point metrics, subject to a global DI ≤ 0.05 constraint. Across three datasets (Compas, Law, German Credit), ROAD achieves Pareto dominance over all baselines, meaning it simultaneously attains higher global accuracy and lower worst-case local disparate impact. On the distribution drift experiment (Adult 1994 → Folktables 2014/2015), ROAD significantly outperforms all other methods on the Equalized Odds vs. Accuracy tradeoff under temporal shift. In the ablation on subgroup definition robustness (Law dataset), ROAD maintains near-consistently lower worst-1-DI than the globally fair Zhang et al. model across 12 different subgroup definitions (age bins of width 5–20, with/without gender splits), at comparable accuracy (~0.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROAD outperforms all five main baselines (Zhang et al. 2018, Adel et al. 2019, FairLR/Rezaei et al. 2020, RobustFairCORELS/Ferry et al. 2022, CUMA/Wang et al. 2023) on the primary local fairness metric (Worst-1-DI vs. Accuracy Pareto front) across Compas, Law, and German Credit. The globally fair adversarial methods (Zhang et al. and FAD/Adel et al.) achieve the weakest local fairness, as their global averaging mechanism leaves local disparities unaddressed, though they do slightly reduce local bias compared to an unconstrained model. Among DRO-based competitors, CUMA (Wang et al. 2023) is the closest challenger on in-distribution Adult data but is clearly outperformed by ROAD under distribution shift (2014/2015 Folktables). RobustFairCORELS (Ferry et al. 2022) and FairLR (Rezaei et al. 2020) are restricted to linear/rule-based models and trail ROAD on both local fairness and accuracy. In a separate comparison (Appendix A.5), ROAD also dominates the parametric DRO approach of Michel et al. (2022) on the German dataset, confirming that the adversarial fairness objective combined with DRO reweighting is more effective than applying DRO to the prediction loss alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROAD combines two established lines of work: the adversarial fairness framework of Zhang et al. (2018), where a min-max objective prevents an adversary from reconstructing the sensitive attribute from predictions, and the parametric DRO approach of Michel et al. (2022), which uses a neural network to learn importance weights under a KL-divergence ball. The key departure is repurposing DRO's reweighting mechanism not for accuracy robustness (as in prior DRO-fairness works like Rezaei et al. or Ferry et al.) but for enforcing worst-case local fairness across unknown subregions of the feature space. The paper also introduces a conditional validity constraint (q(s)=p(s)) that goes beyond the standard global normalization used in DRO, motivated by the observation that global normalization overconstrains the majority sensitive group.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored gap: global fairness metrics (DP, EO) can mask significant local disparities in underrepresented subgroups, and prior DRO-fairness methods either require predefined subgroups, restrict to linear models, or target distribution shift rather than intra-distribution local fairness. The conditional normalization analysis and the BROAD-vs-ROAD comparison (showing that local smoothness in the weighting network matters) provide useful design principles for future adversarial fairness methods. Open questions include extending to settings where the sensitive attribute is unavailable at training time, exploring other differentiable fairness penalties (e.g., mutual information), and whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or non-tabular data.
