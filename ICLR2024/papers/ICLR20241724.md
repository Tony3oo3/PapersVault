## Problem

Conformal prediction and PAC prediction sets provide finite-sample coverage guarantees under i.i.d. or exchangeable data, but these guarantees break under label shift, where the marginal label distribution changes between source and target domains. Standard importance-weighting corrections for label shift rely on point estimates of the confusion matrix and predicted-label distribution, and the resulting estimation error invalidates the PAC guarantee. The paper asks: how can one construct prediction sets that provably maintain PAC coverage under label shift while accounting for finite-sample uncertainty in the importance weights?

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a labeled source calibration set S_m (m samples with true labels), an unlabeled target set T_n^X (n samples with only model predictions available), and a pre-trained scoring function f: X×Y→R (e.g., softmax probabilities from ResNet50, CheXNet, Electra, or an MLP). For a new input x, it outputs a prediction set C(x) = {y : f(x,y) ≥ τ̂} — a subset of all K labels — along with the calibrated threshold τ̂. The threshold is computed by minimizing over an entire box W of possible importance weights (rather than a single point estimate), making the resulting set conservative but guaranteed valid. The method operates across modalities: 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10), chest X-ray images (ChestX-ray14), tabular health records (CDC Heart), and news article text (AGNews).

## Method

The core contribution is a novel interval Gaussian elimination algorithm that propagates elementwise Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals for the confusion matrix C_P and the target predicted-label distribution q* through each step of solving the linear system C_P w* = q* for the importance weights w*. At each elimination step, the algorithm computes lower and upper bounds on every matrix entry and right-hand-side component using worst-case algebraic combinations of the current interval bounds (e.g., the lower bound on c_ij^{t+1} uses the upper bounds on the numerator terms and the lower bound on the pivot). After the forward elimination and back-substitution phases, this yields a box W = ∏[w̲_k, w̄_k] containing the true importance weights with probability ≥ 1−δ. The final prediction set threshold is then obtained by applying the interval-based rejection sampling procedure of Park et al. (2021): for each candidate weight vector w ∈ W, rejection-sample the calibration set to simulate target-distribution data, compute the standard PAC threshold, and take the minimum (most conservative) threshold across all w ∈ W. This guarantees PAC coverage under the shifted target distribution Q while producing smaller prediction sets than the conservative PS-C baseline that uses only a scalar upper bound on the weights.

## Results

Across four datasets (CDC Heart, CIFAR-10, AGNews, ChestX-ray) and CIFAR-100, PS-W consistently satisfies the PAC coverage guarantee (prediction set error ≤ ε) under label shift, while producing smaller average prediction set sizes than PS-C, the only other baseline that satisfies the PAC guarantee. Under large label shifts (e.g., CIFAR-10 with one label shifted from 10% to 40%), PS-W outperforms PS-C by a large margin in set size. Under small shifts with limited calibration data, PS-W and PS-C produce similar (conservative) set sizes, though PS-W still maintains the guarantee. The PS, PS-R, and WCP baselines all violate the PAC guarantee under label shift, with prediction set error exceeding the target ε.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PS-W outperforms PS-C (the only other baseline with a valid PAC guarantee under label shift) on average prediction set size across all datasets, with the gap being largest under large label shifts (e.g., CIFAR-10 with a 40% tweaked label) and narrowing under small shifts with limited calibration data. PS (standard PAC prediction sets without shift handling) and PS-R (which uses point estimates of importance weights without confidence intervals) both violate the PAC guarantee under label shift, confirming that uncertainty in the estimated weights must be explicitly accounted for. WCP (weighted conformal prediction from Podkopaev & Ramdas, 2021) targets marginal coverage rather than PAC coverage and also fails to satisfy the PAC guarantee in these experiments. In the appendix, the class-conditional baseline PS-LW (adapted from Sadinle et al., 2019) approximately satisfies the error bound but produces larger prediction sets than PS-W because it enforces a stronger per-class guarantee than necessary. The APS scoring function (Romano et al., 2020) improves coverage under shift but does not satisfy the PAC guarantee, and its structure makes adaptation to the PAC setting non-trivial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC prediction set framework (Vovk 2012; Park et al. 2019) to the label shift setting, building on the importance-weighting formulation from Black Box Shift Estimation (Lipton et al. 2018) and the interval-based prediction set construction under covariate shift from Park et al. 2021. The key departure is that Park et al. 2021 relies on a source-target discriminator (unavailable under label shift) and a heuristic for weight intervals, whereas this paper derives principled confidence intervals by propagating Clopper-Pearson bounds through a modified Gaussian elimination algorithm. It also contrasts with Podkopaev & Ramdas (2021), who achieve marginal coverage under label shift but assume the true importance weights are known exactly, whereas this paper handles the estimation uncertainty that breaks the PAC guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provide a PAC (calibration-set conditional) guarantee for prediction sets under label shift, which is critical in safety-sensitive applications like medical diagnosis where a slight excess of the error rate can be unacceptable. The interval Gaussian elimination technique for propagating uncertainty through linear algebraic computations is noted by the authors as potentially of independent interest beyond this specific application. The main limitation is conservatism when the label shift is small or calibration data is limited, and an open question is how to tighten the intervals (e.g., via LU decomposition or other solvers) to reduce this conservatism. The approach also requires the confusion matrix to be invertible and the classifier to be sufficiently accurate on all labels.
