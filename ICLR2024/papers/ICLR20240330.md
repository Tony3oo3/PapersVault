## Problem

Existing neural operators for elliptic PDEs (e.g., GKN, FNO, GNN-PDE, MP-PDE) cannot handle inhomogeneous boundary values or complex boundary geometries, which are ubiquitous in real-world applications. Inhomogeneous boundary conditions introduce high-frequency solution fluctuations near the boundary, and any variation in boundary shape influences the interior solution globally due to the elliptic nature of the problem. All tested baselines fail to learn the solution operator under these conditions, with relative L2 errors near 1.0.

## Input / Output

The method takes a discretized domain with 32×32 (or 64×64) interior grid nodes and 128 boundary nodes, where each interior node carries coordinates, forcing term f, and distances to boundary, while each boundary node carries coordinates, boundary value g, and distance to the geometric center. The graph is constructed via Delaunay triangulation plus 8-nearest-neighbor edges. The output is the scalar PDE solution u at all interior nodes, obtained by summing the decoder outputs of two branches (one solving with f and zero boundary, the other with g and zero source).

## Method

BENO decomposes the Poisson equation following Green's function into two sub-problems solved by a dual-branch GNN: Branch 1 learns the interior source contribution (boundary set to zero) and Branch 2 learns the boundary value contribution (source set to zero). A Transformer encoder with multi-head self-attention processes all boundary nodes to produce a global boundary latent vector B, which is injected into the node-update step of every message passing layer in both GNN branches, enabling the global boundary geometry to modulate pairwise interior interactions. The graph backbone uses relative-position-augmented message passing (5 steps) with 3-layer MLPs and SiLU activations, and the two branch outputs are summed by a shared decoder. This design decouples boundary and interior learning while the Transformer captures long-range boundary dependencies that direct concatenation fails to model, yielding an average 60.96% improvement over the best baseline.

## Results

Trained on 900 4-Corners samples, BENO achieves relative L2 errors of 0.3523, 0.4308, 0.4910, 0.5416, and 0.5542 on the 4/3/2/1/No-Corner test sets, respectively, with corresponding MAE values of 0.97–1.75 (×10⁻³). All four baselines (GKN, FNO, GNN-PDE, MP-PDE) fail to learn the solution operator, with L2 errors hovering near 1.0 across all test sets. BENO outperforms the best baseline (MP-PDE) by 47–65% in relative L2 norm depending on the test geometry, and the paper reports an average improvement of 60.96% over state-of-the-art methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four baselines effectively fail to learn the solution operator for elliptic PDEs with inhomogeneous boundary values, with relative L2 errors near or above 1.0 (indicating predictions no better than a constant). GKN and FNO, the operator-based methods, perform worst with L2 errors of 1.07–1.11, likely due to their inability to handle irregular boundary geometries. GNN-PDE and MP-PDE, the graph-based methods, achieve L2 ≈ 1.00, meaning they essentially predict a trivial (near-zero) solution regardless of input. BENO outperforms the best baseline (MP-PDE) by 64.8% on 4-Corners (0.3523 vs. 1.0007 L2), 56.9% on 3-Corners, 50.5% on 2-Corners, 44.9% on 1-Corner, and 43.9% on No-Corner. The gap widens further on MAE, where BENO achieves 0.97–1.75 (×10⁻³) versus 3.0–3.7 (×10⁻³) for all baselines, confirming that no existing method can capture the high-frequency solution features induced by inhomogeneous boundary conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

BENO builds on the graph neural network lineage for PDE solving (GKN, GNN-PDE, MP-PDE) and neural operators (FNO), but departs fundamentally in how boundary conditions are handled. Prior graph-based solvers (GNN-PDE, T-FEN, FEONet) addressed complex domain shapes but restricted boundary values to zero, while operator-based methods (FNO, GKN) assumed regular grids and homogeneous conditions. The key conceptual departure is a Green's-function-inspired decomposition into a dual-branch GNN (interior source vs. boundary propagation) coupled with a Transformer that encodes global boundary geometry into a latent vector injected at each message-passing step, rather than simply concatenating boundary features into node inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically confirms a critical gap: all existing neural PDE solvers completely fail (L2 ≈ 1.0) on inhomogeneous boundary value problems, a setting that is ubiquitous in engineering applications. BENO's boundary-embedded architecture closes this gap with strong generalization across boundary shapes, values, and resolutions, and the novel 5-configuration dataset provides a useful benchmark. Limitations include restriction to 2D, primarily Dirichlet conditions (Neumann only in appendix), and relatively small datasets (1000 samples); open questions include extension to 3D, Robin/mixed conditions, and whether the Transformer-based global boundary encoding scales to very large or highly irregular boundaries.
