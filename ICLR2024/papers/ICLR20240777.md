## Problem

The Bandits with Knapsacks (BwK) framework assumes monotonic resource consumption, but many practical problems (inventory management, bilateral trade) involve resources that can be replenished over time. While Kumar and Kleinberg (2022) introduced the Bandits with Replenishable Knapsacks (BwRK) model, they only provided stochastic-setting guarantees. This paper addresses the missing adversarial and best-of-both-worlds guarantees for BwRK, providing the first positive results under adversarial inputs and the first instance-independent regret bound in the stochastic setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an action set X, an initial budget B over horizon T (with per-iteration budget ρ = B/T), and at each round observes a reward function f_t: X → [0,1] and a cost vector c_t: X → [-1,1]^m where negative components indicate replenishment. A key design choice is whether the per-round replenishment factor β (the minimum replenishment from the void action) is known a priori or must be learned online; the framework handles both cases with different regret minimizer instantiations. The output is a sequence of actions (x_t) satisfying the cumulative constraint Σ c_{t,i}(x_t) ≤ Tρ for all resources i, accompanied by either a constant competitive ratio α = ν/(1+β) (adversarial) or a Õ(√T) regret bound (stochastic).

## Method

The core contribution is a primal-dual template (Algorithm 1) that partitions rounds into T_G (all budgets ≥ 1, regret minimizers active) and T_∅ (some budget < 1, void action played), and further splits T_G at the last depletion time τ into pre-τ and post-τ phases. The primal regret minimizer (bandit feedback) plays actions maximizing the Lagrangian utility f_t(x) + ⟨λ_t, ρ − c_t(x)⟩, while the dual regret minimizer (full feedback) updates multipliers λ_t based on the realized cost vector. The critical insight is that the dual must be weakly adaptive: it should play large λ before τ (when the primal is spending heavily) and small λ after τ (when resources are being replenished), which standard no-regret algorithms cannot guarantee. For known β, the dual uses generalized fixed-share on the simplex {λ: ‖λ‖₁ ≤ 1/ν}; for unknown β, the dual uses OGD on R_+^m with a primal (EXP3-SIX) satisfying a weakly-adaptive regret condition, yielding a Õ(m⁴/ν² · √(KT)) bound in both settings.

## Results

In the adversarial setting, the framework achieves a constant competitive ratio α = ν/(1+β) (where ν = β + ρ), which reduces to the state-of-the-art 1/ρ rate of Castiglioni et al. (2022a) when β = 0 (monotonic consumption) and improves when replenishment is present. In the stochastic setting, the algorithm yields an instance-independent Õ(T^{1/2}) regret bound, complementing the instance-dependent O(Km²/Δ² log T) bound of Kumar and Kleinberg (2022). For the specific BwRK problem with K actions and m resources, the regret bound is Õ(m⁴/ν² · √(KT) · log(1/δ)) in both adversarial and stochastic settings, representing the first positive adversarial result for BwRK.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, so comparisons are between theoretical guarantees rather than empirical performance. Against Kumar and Kleinberg (2022), who only address the stochastic BwRK setting with an instance-dependent O(Km²/Δ² log T) regret bound, this paper provides (i) the first adversarial BwRK result with a constant competitive ratio, and (ii) the first instance-independent Õ(√T) stochastic regret bound. Against Castiglioni et al. (2022a), who achieve a 1/ρ competitive ratio for monotonic BwK, this paper recovers the same rate when β = 0 and strictly improves it to ν/(1+β) when replenishment is available. Against Balseiro et al. (2022), whose dual mirror descent framework requires monotonic resource consumption and thus cannot be applied here, this paper introduces a new primal-dual template that handles non-monotonic costs. Against Castiglioni et al. (2023), who handle one monotone budget plus one soft ROI constraint, this paper handles an arbitrary number of strictly-satisfied budget constraints with unknown replenishment parameters, at the cost of a slightly weaker m⁴/ν² dependence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bandits with Replenishable Knapsacks (BwRK) model introduced by Kumar and Kleinberg (2022), which itself generalizes the classical Bandits with Knapsacks (BwK) framework of Badanidiyuru et al. (2018) to allow non-monotonic resource consumption. The key departure from prior primal-dual approaches (Castiglioni et al. 2022a; Balseiro et al. 2022) is that those frameworks require a known Slater's parameter (per-round budget), which is unavailable when resources can be replenished by an unknown amount. The paper's solution is a best-of-both-worlds primal-dual template that uses a weakly-adaptive dual regret minimizer to handle the two distinct phases (pre- and post-last-budget-exhaustion) without requiring knowledge of the replenishment factor β.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide adversarial-input guarantees for BwRK and the first instance-independent regret bound for the stochastic BwRK setting, filling a significant gap left by Kumar and Kleinberg (2022), who only addressed the stochastic case with instance-dependent logarithmic bounds. The framework's modularity—requiring only a suitable primal regret minimizer—makes it applicable to a range of economic problems (inventory management, bilateral trade) where resources are naturally replenishable. Open questions include handling negative rewards in the adversarial setting (where the multiplicative competitive-ratio guarantee is not translation-invariant) and extending the simplified inventory model to more complex operations-research formulations with fixed ordering costs.
