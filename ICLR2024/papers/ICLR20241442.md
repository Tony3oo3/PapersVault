## Problem

Existing contextual dueling bandit algorithms provide only worst-case regret bounds that are agnostic to the variance of pairwise comparisons, failing to capture the intuitive fact that deterministic or low-variance comparisons should yield easier learning. This paper asks whether a dueling bandit algorithm can be designed whose regret bound explicitly depends on the per-round comparison variance σ_t, achieving Õ(d) regret in the deterministic limit rather than the standard Õ(d√T).

## Input / Output

Each round t, the algorithm receives a time-varying set of d-dimensional contextual vectors A_t ⊆ R^d (arms) and must output a pair (x_t, y_t) from A_t for comparison. The environment returns a binary outcome o_t ∈ {0,1} indicating which arm is preferred, generated via a GLM with a monotone link function μ satisfying μ(x)+μ(−x)=1. The algorithm maintains L = ⌈log₂(1/α)⌉ layers of regularized MLE estimators and covariance matrices, updating only the active layer per round, and requires no prior knowledge of the variance level.

## Method

VACDB employs a multi-layered elimination structure where arms are progressively pruned across L layers, each with a confidence radius scaled by 2^{−ℓ}. The key innovation is a variance-aware adaptive confidence radius β̂_{t,ℓ} that incorporates an empirical variance estimate (via a Freedman-type concentration inequality), replacing the fixed 2^{−ℓ} radius used in prior layered algorithms. A regularized MLE (adding a λθ term) eliminates the need for an initial random exploration phase, ensuring the covariance matrix remains non-singular even when the arm set is adversarially chosen. The exploitation phase uses a novel symmetric arm selection criterion (x+y)^T θ̂ + β‖x−y‖_{Σ̂^{−1}} that directly optimizes the average regret objective, unlike the asymmetric or maximum-uncertainty policies in prior work. Together these components yield a regret bound of Õ(d/κ_μ · √(Σσ_t²) + d(L_μ²/κ_μ² + 1/κ_μ)), which is tight in the worst case and reduces to Õ(d) when comparisons are deterministic.

## Results

On synthetic experiments (T=4000, d=5, |A_t|=2^d arms), VACDB achieves the lowest cumulative regret among all compared methods, with the gap widening over time. The variance-awareness is confirmed by scaling θ*: as the comparison variance decreases (scaling θ* by α∈{0.5,1,2,4}), VACDB's regret decreases correspondingly, consistent with the theoretical bound O~(d√(Σσ_t²)). On the real-world EventTime dataset (K=100 events), VACDB begins to outperform MaxInP in cumulative regret after approximately 2500 rounds. The worst-case regret bound O~(d√T) matches the known lower bound Ω(d√T) from Bengs et al. (2022).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VACDB outperforms all three baselines (MaxInP, MaxPairUCB, CoLSTIM) on the synthetic regret comparison (Figure 1a), with the advantage growing over the 4000-round horizon. MaxPairUCB and CoLSTIM show a slight empirical edge over MaxInP, which the authors attribute to MaxInP selecting pairs based solely on uncertainty rather than incorporating reward maximization. On the EventTime real-world dataset (Figure 2), VACDB surpasses MaxInP after ~2500 rounds, confirming the advantage in a non-synthetic setting. The variance-awareness experiment (Figure 1b) demonstrates that VACDB adapts to lower-variance instances (larger |θ*|) by achieving proportionally lower regret, a property the variance-agnostic baselines do not exploit. Theoretically, VACDB's worst-case bound O~(d√T) matches the lower bound and recovers the bounds of MaxInP and CoLSTIM, while improving upon Sta'D and SupCoLSTIM when the number of arms K is large (their bounds include an extra log K factor).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-layered elimination framework from Saha (2021) and Bengs et al. (2022) for contextual dueling bandits by incorporating the variance-aware confidence radius design from Zhao et al. (2023a), which was previously applied only to standard linear contextual bandits. The key technical departure is adapting the layered structure to the generalized linear model setting where mean and variance are coupled (Bernoulli feedback), requiring a regularized MLE to replace the initial exploration phase used in prior dueling bandit algorithms. The symmetric arm selection policy (maximizing (x+y)ᵀθ̂ + β‖x−y‖) is novel relative to the asymmetric or maximum-uncertainty criteria in MaxInP and CoLSTIM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first variance-aware regret bound for contextual dueling bandits, closing a gap where prior algorithms (Saha 2021, Bengs 2022) only provided worst-case Õ(d√T) guarantees regardless of comparison noise. The Õ(d) regret in the deterministic case is practically meaningful for settings with confident human preferences (e.g., RLHF with clear quality differences). Open questions include extending to subset-wise comparisons (K>2), broader nonlinear function classes beyond GLMs, and validation on larger-scale real-world preference data. The Brouwer invariance of domain argument fixing a gap in prior MLE existence proofs for GLM bandits is a useful technical contribution for the broader community.
