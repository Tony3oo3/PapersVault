## Problem

Existing deep information bottleneck (IB) methods for regression rely on KL divergence, which forces a Gaussian assumption on the decoder (reducing the prediction term to MSE) and requires variational or non-parametric upper-bound approximations for the compression term I(x;t). These approximations introduce inductive bias, make the solution sub-optimal, and are difficult to tune in practice. The paper asks whether IB for regression can be performed on arbitrarily distributed data without any distributional assumptions or variational approximations.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either tabular feature vectors (e.g., 8-dimensional housing features, 20-dimensional energy consumption readings, 42-dimensional PM2.5 sensor data) or RGB images (28×28 MNIST digits for rotation angle prediction, 224×224 face crops for age estimation). It produces a continuous scalar regression prediction ŷ and a stochastic latent representation t = f_enc(x) + w, where w is zero-centered Gaussian noise with learned covariance. For tabular data the encoder is a 3-layer fully-connected network or LSTM; for images, VGG-16 serves as the backbone. The decoder is deterministic, mapping t to ŷ.

## Method

The core contribution is a new IB Lagrangian (Eq. 17) that replaces both the KL-based prediction term and the Shannon MI compression term with Cauchy-Schwarz (CS) divergence quantities. The prediction term D_CS(p(y|x); q_θ(ŷ|x)) is estimated via kernel density estimation using Gram matrices over x, y, and ŷ, requiring no Gaussian assumption on the decoder and reducing to MSE only in the limit of zero kernel width. The compression term I_CS(x;t) (CS-QMI) is estimated in closed form from the Gram matrices of x and t with O(N²) complexity, yielding the true mutual information value rather than an upper bound. Theoretically, CS divergence is always bounded above by min{KL(p‖q), KL(q‖p)} for Gaussians, and CS-QMI is bounded above by Shannon MI, enabling tighter generalization bounds. The method also provides an adversarial robustness guarantee: when the kernel mean embedding norms are equal, CS-QMI monotonically relates to HSIC, and minimizing CS-QMI implies minimizing HSIC, bounding the power of ℓ∞ adversaries.

## Results

On six regression benchmarks, CS-IB achieves the lowest RMSE at both zero compression (r=0) and 50% compression (r=0.5) across all datasets. At r=0, CS-IB already outperforms all baselines (e.g., 0.251 vs. 0.258 RMSE on California Housing; 4.165 vs. 4.351 on Rotation MNIST), demonstrating that the CS divergence prediction term alone surpasses MSE. At r=0.5, the advantage widens (e.g., 0.245 vs. 0.267 on Housing; 3.930 vs. 4.102 on Rotation MNIST). Under adversarial attacks (FGSM and 5-step PGD), CS-IB also achieves the best robustness on all six datasets, e.g., 0.635 FGSM RMSE on Housing vs. 0.641–0.706 for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CS-IB outperforms all five baselines (VIB, NIB, Square-NIB, Exp-NIB, and HSIC-bottleneck) on every dataset at both r=0 and r=0.5. Notably, even without any compression (r=0), CS-IB already beats all baselines, indicating the CS divergence prediction term is inherently superior to MSE for extracting predictive information. The gap is most pronounced on Bike Sharing (0.404 vs. 0.428 at r=0) and Rotation MNIST (4.165 vs. 4.351 at r=0). Under adversarial attacks, CS-IB achieves the lowest RMSE on all six datasets for both FGSM and 5-step PGD; for example, on Appliance Energy under FGSM, CS-IB scores 0.290 vs. 0.385–0.502 for baselines, and under PGD it scores 0.402 vs. 0.463–0.543. VIB degrades most severely under compression (e.g., Housing RMSE jumps from 0.258 to 0.347 at r=0.5), while NIB and Square-NIB are the closest competitors but still consistently trail CS-IB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-parameterizes the information bottleneck Lagrangian by replacing the Kullback-Leibler divergence with the Cauchy-Schwarz (CS) divergence, building directly on the CS divergence framework of Principe et al. (2000) and the conditional CS divergence of Yu et al. (2023). Prior deep IB methods for regression (VIB, NIB, HSIC-bottleneck) all rely on KL-based mutual information estimated via variational bounds or non-parametric upper bounds, and use MSE loss derived from a Gaussian decoder assumption. The key departure is showing that CS divergence yields closed-form, non-parametric estimators for both the prediction and compression terms without any distributional assumptions, and that CS divergence is provably bounded above by both forward and reverse KL divergences for Gaussian distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, assumption-free alternative to KL-based IB for regression, with the theoretical guarantee that CS divergence is always no larger than KL divergence, enabling tighter generalization bounds. The adversarial robustness guarantee via CS-QMI (Proposition 4) connects the compression term to bounded adversarial perturbation effects, extending prior HSIC-based robustness results. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines are modest (typically 2-5% RMSE improvement), and the method introduces a kernel bandwidth hyperparameter that requires tuning. Open questions include whether the CS-IB framework extends naturally to classification, whether the theoretical tightness of the CS ≤ KL bound translates to meaningful generalization improvements at scale, and whether the closed-form estimators remain stable for very high-dimensional representations.
