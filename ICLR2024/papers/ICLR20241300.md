## Problem

In-context learning with LLMs relies on private data as few-shot demonstrations in prompts, creating a privacy risk where adversaries can extract or infer sensitive information through prompt injection or model regurgitation. Heuristic protections such as PII removal are insufficient against linkability attacks. The paper asks whether effective ICL can be achieved while providing formal differential privacy guarantees on the private demonstration data, without requiring any fine-tuning of the language model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241300/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a private labeled dataset D_priv, privacy parameters (ε, δ), a pre-trained LLM, and a task-specific instruction, and produces synthetic few-shot demonstrations generated token-by-token with DP guarantees. These demonstrations are generated once offline and then reused as ICL prompts for an unbounded number of downstream queries, incurring zero additional privacy cost per query due to DP's post-processing property. Labels can be from a fixed set (classification) or open-form (information extraction), and the generation is conditioned on a target label y to produce class-specific synthetic samples.

## Method

The core algorithm (Alg. 1) generates synthetic demonstrations token-by-token using a PATE-inspired private aggregation of LLM generation probabilities. At each token step, MN examples are randomly drawn from the private dataset and partitioned into M disjoint subsets of N samples; each subset is appended to the instruction, target label, and tokens generated so far, and fed to the LLM to obtain a next-token probability distribution. These M distributions are privately aggregated via either the Gaussian mechanism or Report-Noisy-Max with Exponential noise, with an optional vocabulary reduction (RVP) to top-K tokens from a public instruction to improve signal-to-noise ratio. Privacy amplification by subsampling (drawing MN from |D_priv| each iteration) combined with numerical composition across T_max token steps yields the overall (ε, δ)-DP guarantee. No fine-tuning is performed—the LLM's existing generation capability is leveraged directly, and the entire generation is a one-time offline cost before deployment.

## Results

On 4-shot ICL with GPT-3 Babbage, the DP few-shot generation algorithm (Alg. 1) achieves competitive accuracy with strong privacy guarantees: 71.3% on AGNews (ε=4), 81.2% on DBPedia (ε=1), 50.7% on TREC (ε=1), 55.9% on MIT-G (ε=8), and 74.6% on MIT-D (ε=4). These results substantially outperform the fully private 0-shot baseline (e.g., 47.9% → 71.3% on AGNews, 30.4% → 81.2% on DBPedia) and are competitive with or occasionally exceed the non-private ICL baseline (e.g., 71.3% vs. 69.3% on AGNews, 50.7% vs. 50.6% on TREC). Empirical membership inference attacks confirm that DP-generated demonstrations reduce MIA AUC to ~50.5% (random guess) across all ε values, versus 81.84% for non-private ICL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its DP few-shot generation against three baselines: (1) 0-shot ICL (fully private, no demonstrations), (2) instruction-only 4-shot generation (ε=0, model generates its own demonstrations without any private data), and (3) non-private ICL (ε=∞, best of random 4-shot from private data or Alg.1 with zero noise). The DP method substantially outperforms 0-shot across all datasets (e.g., +23.4 pts on AGNews, +50.8 pts on DBPedia at ε=1). Against the instruction-only 4-shot baseline, the DP method provides significant gains on DBPedia (+20.8 pts at ε=1), TREC (+5.0 pts at ε=1), and MIT-G (+6.2 pts at ε=1), confirming the value of private data. Against non-private ICL, the DP method is competitive or superior on AGNews (71.3% vs. 69.3% at ε=4), TREC (50.7% vs. 50.6% at ε=1), and MIT-G (55.9% vs. 54.4% at ε=8), but trails on DBPedia (81.2% vs. 82.3% at ε=1) and MIT-D (74.6% vs. 80.1% at ε=4). Notably, the instruction-only 4-shot baseline itself is competitive with non-private ICL on AGNews (68.0% vs. 69.3%), suggesting that for some tasks, privacy may come for free when the model can generate its own demonstrations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the PATE framework (Papernot et al., 2017) of private aggregation over disjoint subsets to a fundamentally different setting: instead of training a student model on privately labeled public data, it privately aggregates next-token generation probabilities from an LLM to produce synthetic few-shot demonstrations. It departs from concurrent DP-ICL work (Wu et al., 2024; Duan et al., 2023) by requiring no public data and by spending the privacy budget once during offline generation rather than per query, enabling unbounded inference. The privacy analysis follows the DP-SGD composition framework (Abadi et al., 2016; Gopi et al., 2021) with subsampling amplification, but applied to a token-by-token generation loop rather than gradient updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical, fine-tuning-free recipe for deploying ICL on sensitive data (healthcare, industrial) with formal DP guarantees, addressing a real deployment concern that heuristic PII removal cannot resolve. The finding that large models can generate self-sufficient demonstrations from instructions alone (ε=0 4-shot matching non-private ICL on AGNews and DBPedia with Davinci) suggests that for some tasks, privacy may come 'for free' without any private data. Key limitations include the per-token resampling overhead, a performance gap versus random sampling at 8–12 shots, and evaluation restricted to GPT-3-era models; open questions include whether the approach scales to modern LLMs, whether more advanced decoding (nucleus sampling) under the Gaussian mechanism closes the gap, and whether the self-generation phenomenon persists at frontier-model scale.
