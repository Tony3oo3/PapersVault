## Problem

Existing convergence analyses for FEDAVG and SCAFFOLD require the assumption of bounded data heterogeneity, and prior improvements to these algorithms rely on impractical modifications such as increasing batch sizes with iterations, computing full-batch local gradients, or solving local subproblems to high precision. This paper asks whether a simple, practical modification—adding momentum to local SGD steps—can remove the bounded heterogeneity requirement while simultaneously improving convergence rates.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local dataset drawn from its own non-IID distribution (e.g., CIFAR-10 partitioned via Dirichlet distributions with parameters 0.1–0.5), and the server maintains a global model and gradient estimate. The algorithm takes as hyperparameters a local learning rate η, global learning rate γ, and momentum coefficient β ∈ [0,1], and produces a trained model with a provable convergence rate of O(√(LΔσ²/NKR)) + O(LΔ/R) for FEDAVG-M or O((LΔσ/NKR)^{2/3}) + O(LΔ/R) for the variance-reduced variant, without any bounded data heterogeneity assumption. The method incurs no additional uplink communication overhead relative to the base algorithms.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the local stochastic gradient in FEDAVG and SCAFFOLD with a momentum-blended direction: g_i^{r,k} = β∇F(x_i^{r,k}; ξ_i^{r,k}) + (1−β)g^r (for FEDAVG-M) or g_i^{r,k} = β(∇F(x_i^{r,k}; ξ_i^{r,k}) − c_i^r + c^r) + (1−β)g^r (for SCAFFOLD-M), where g^r is a server-maintained global gradient estimate updated each round. This creates an 'anchoring' effect that counteracts client drift caused by data heterogeneity, effectively eliminating the need for bounded heterogeneity in the convergence proof. Variance-reduced variants (FEDAVG-M-VR, SCAFFOLD-M-VR) further incorporate SARAH/STORM-style recursive gradient corrections to reduce within-client noise, achieving O((LΔσ/NKR)^{2/3}) rates. Setting β=1 recovers the vanilla algorithms, and the momentum coefficient can be tuned to balance drift mitigation against gradient freshness; notably, FEDAVG-M admits a constant local learning rate, unlike prior FEDAVG analyses that require diminishing rates.

## Results

The primary contribution is theoretical: FEDAVG-M achieves a convergence rate of $(L\Delta\sigma^2/(NKR))^{1/2} + L\Delta/R$ under full participation without any bounded data heterogeneity assumption, matching the known lower bound. For partial participation, SCAFFOLD-M achieves $(L\Delta\sigma^2/(SKR))^{1/2} + L\Delta/R\cdot(1+N^{2/3}/S)$, yielding up to $N^{2/9}$× fewer communication rounds than vanilla SCAFFOLD when $S \asymp N^{2/3}$. The variance-reduced variants (FEDAVG-M-VR, SCAFFOLD-M-VR) further improve to $(L\Delta\sigma/(NKR))^{2/3} + L\Delta/R$ and $(L\Delta\sigma/(S\sqrt{K}R))^{2/3} + L\Delta/R\cdot(1+N^{1/2}/S)$ respectively. Experiments on CIFAR-10 with MLP and ResNet-18 confirm that momentum consistently accelerates convergence, with amplified benefits under severe data heterogeneity (Dirichlet parameter 0.1) and larger models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla FEDAVG (Konečný et al., 2016; McMahan et al., 2017), FEDAVG-M eliminates the bounded data heterogeneity assumption entirely while matching the best known rate $(L\Delta\sigma^2/(NKR))^{1/2} + L\Delta/R$; prior analyses (Karimireddy et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2021; Koloskova et al., 2020) all require bounded heterogeneity even with diminishing learning rates. Against SCAFFOLD (Karimireddy et al., 2020), SCAFFOLD-M is the first method to provably improve upon it without additional assumptions, achieving up to $N^{2/9}$× fewer communication rounds when $S \asymp N^{2/3}$ (e.g., ~4.6× for N=1000, S≈100). Against FEDCM (Xu et al., 2021), which is algorithmically identical, the paper's analysis is strictly stronger: it requires neither bounded gradients nor bounded heterogeneity, and the resulting rate is asymptotically optimal versus FEDCM's suboptimal rate. Against variance-reduced baselines (BVR-L-SGD, CE-LSGD, STEM), FEDAVG-M-VR matches or improves the rate while using only $O(1)$ minibatches per local step instead of $\mathcal{O}(K)$, and drops the bounded heterogeneity assumption required by STEM. Against partial-participation variance-reduced methods (MIMELITEMVR, MB-STORM, CE-LSGD1), SCAFFOLD-M-VR achieves the best rate under unbounded heterogeneity without requiring noiseless full-batch gradients or $\mathcal{O}(K)$ minibatches. Against LED (Alghunaim, 2023) and VRL-SGD (Liang et al., 2019), which also handle unbounded heterogeneity, FEDAVG-M achieves a strictly better rate by removing the $(L\Delta\sigma/\sqrt{K})^{2/3}$ term that degrades with local steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends FEDAVG (Konečný et al., 2016; McMahan et al., 2017) and SCAFFOLD (Karimireddy et al., 2020b) by adding a momentum term to their local SGD steps, drawing on the variance-reduced momentum directions from SARAH (Nguyen et al., 2017) and STORM (Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019). It departs from prior convergence analyses of FEDAVG (Karimireddy et al., 2020b; Yang et al., 2021; Koloskova et al., 2020) that all require bounded data heterogeneity, and from impractical variants like STEM, CE-LSGD, and MIME that demand large-batch or full-batch local gradients. The algorithm FEDAVG-M coincides with FEDCM (Xu et al., 2021b), but the paper provides a strictly stronger convergence guarantee without the bounded-gradient and bounded-heterogeneity assumptions that FEDCM's analysis requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a one-line algorithmic change (adding momentum to local steps) removes the bounded data heterogeneity assumption that has been a persistent limitation in FEDAVG convergence theory, while also enabling constant local learning rates that simplify hyperparameter tuning. This makes the resulting algorithms trivially implementable with no additional communication overhead, which is critical for real-world FL deployments where data heterogeneity is unbounded. Open questions include whether the momentum-based approach extends to other FL paradigms (decentralized, compressed, asynchronous) and whether the theoretical gains translate to larger-scale benchmarks beyond CIFAR-10. The experimental validation, while consistent with theory, is limited to a single dataset and moderate model sizes (MLP, ResNet-18).
