## Problem

Quantifying epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties for graph-based hyperspectral image classification (HSIC) remains unexplored, despite these uncertainties being critical for out-of-distribution (OOD) and misclassification detection. The paper further reveals theoretically that the standard uncertainty cross-entropy (UCE) loss used in evidential GCNs is insufficient to produce accurate epistemic uncertainty, as its optimal solution is equivalent to LDA and fails to map OOD nodes into the low-evidence detectable region near the decision boundary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240823/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a hyperspectral image where each pixel is a B-dimensional spectral vector (e.g., 103 bands for Pavia, 144 for Houston, 176 for KSC) and constructs a K=50 nearest-neighbor graph over labeled pixels using cosine-similarity-based edge weights (σ=0.1). It outputs per-pixel class labels along with two uncertainty scores: epistemic uncertainty (vacuity u = C/S, high for OOD) and aleatoric uncertainty (entropy of the predicted categorical distribution, high for misclassified in-distribution pixels). The OOD endmember signature m_o is jointly learned as an additional output parameter.

## Method

The paper proposes two physics-guided regularization terms added to the UCE loss for both EGCN and GPN backbones. First, an Unmixing Regularization (UR) term leverages the linear mixing model of HSI data: it approximates ID material abundances with predicted beliefs and the OOD material abundance with vacuity, then minimizes the reconstruction error ‖x^i − M·b^i(θ) − u^i(θ)·m_o‖², where m_o has a closed-form solution given θ. Second, an evidence-based Total Variation (TV) term is applied to the vacuity score over the 2D spatial grid to enforce spatial smoothness while preserving edges between ID and OOD regions. Training alternates between gradient descent on network parameters θ and the closed-form update of m_o. Theoretically, the paper proves that minimizing UCE alone yields an LDA-equivalent projection that assigns high evidence to far-away OOD points, while the UR term provably decreases total evidence for OOD instances and increases ground-truth class evidence for ID instances.

## Results

For OOD detection (the primary task), the proposed EGCN-UR-TV achieves 91.57% AUROC on UP, 90.69% on UH, and 92.21% on KSC, while GPN-UR-TV achieves 94.55%, 87.29%, and 88.78% respectively. GPN-UR-TV is the best overall method on UP (94.55% AUROC, 51.84% AUPR), while EGCN-UR-TV leads on UH (90.69% AUROC, 46.77% AUPR) and KSC (92.21% AUROC, 72.13% AUPR). For misclassification detection, the proposed methods are competitive with softmax-GCN, with EGCN-UR-TV achieving 90.37% AUROC on KSC (vs. 89.22% for softmax-GCN). The UR term alone yields up to 10.8% AUROC improvement over the base GPN on UP, and adding TV provides a further ~1% AUROC and ~3.1% AUPR gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed methods substantially outperform all baselines on OOD detection. Compared to softmax-GCN (Kipf & Welling, 2017), EGCN-UR-TV improves AUROC by 34.5 points on UP (91.57 vs. 57.04), 33.9 on UH (90.69 vs. 56.78), and 15.1 on KSC (92.21 vs. 77.12), confirming that softmax entropy is a poor proxy for epistemic uncertainty. The three anomaly detection baselines (RGAE, TLRSR, TRDFTVAD) all underperform the proposed methods by 10–60 AUROC points, as they detect spectral anomalies rather than distributional novelty. Against the direct uncertainty-quantification baselines, adding UR to EGCN improves AUROC by 3.2–4.4 points across datasets, and adding TV provides a further ~1 point; for GPN, UR alone yields a dramatic 10.8-point AUROC gain on UP (93.63 vs. 82.82) and 8.7 points on KSC (88.40 vs. 79.66). GPN-UR-TV is the best method on UP (94.55 AUROC), while EGCN-UR-TV leads on UH (90.69 vs. 87.29) and KSC (92.21 vs. 88.78), suggesting the GKDE regularization in EGCN is particularly beneficial for those datasets. On misclassification detection, the proposed methods are roughly on par with softmax-GCN (within 1–2 AUROC points on UP and UH) but slightly better on KSC (90.37 vs. 89.22 for EGCN-UR-TV).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two graph-based uncertainty quantification architectures—evidential GCNs (EGCN, Zhao et al. 2020) and graph posterior networks (GPN, Stadler et al. 2021)—from general semi-supervised node classification to the specific domain of hyperspectral image classification (HSIC). It departs from prior work by providing a theoretical characterization (Theorem 1) showing that the UCE loss, the standard training objective for evidential models, yields an optimal solution equivalent to LDA, which fundamentally cannot map OOD points into the low-evidence detectable region. To address this gap, the paper introduces a physics-guided unmixing-based regularization (UR) that exploits the linear mixing model inherent to hyperspectral data, and an evidence-based total variation (TV) term for spatial edge preservation—neither of which has appeared in prior graph uncertainty quantification literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to systematically address uncertainty quantification for graph-based HSIC, filling a gap between the well-studied HSIC literature and the uncertainty estimation literature. The theoretical result that UCE-optimized EGCNs reduce to LDA provides a clear diagnostic for why existing evidential models fail at OOD detection in this setting, and the UR term offers a principled, domain-informed fix. However, the approach's effectiveness hinges on the linear mixing model assumption and a single OOD material, limiting generalizability; the authors acknowledge that robustness to inaccurate mixing models and extension to multiple OOD categories remain open. The moderate empirical gains (1–10% AUROC) and niche application domain temper the broader impact, though the theoretical insights may inform uncertainty quantification in other structured-data settings.
