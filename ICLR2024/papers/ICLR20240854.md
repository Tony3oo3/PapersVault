## Problem

Existing data-driven physics simulators either operate on fixed spatio-temporal grids (autoregressive GNNs) or require explicit PDE knowledge (PINNs), and none simultaneously satisfy three requirements: being fully data-driven with unknown PDE, generalizing to unseen initial conditions, and enabling evaluation at arbitrary spatial and temporal locations. This paper tackles the combined challenge of continuous space-time prediction from sparse, scattered observations without any prior knowledge of the governing equations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240854/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse initial condition defined only at a small subset of scattered spatial positions (as low as 5% of the full simulation grid) along with a single query point (x, t) at any arbitrary location in space and time, including positions and instants never observed during training. The output is a scalar PDE solution value at the queried coordinate. Unlike grid-based baselines that require a fixed mesh and uniform time steps, the model handles irregular point-cloud inputs and produces predictions at non-uniform, off-grid query locations, with the number of query points potentially exceeding the number of observed points by up to 20×.

## Method

The core contribution is a double observation formulation with two interlinked dynamical systems. System 1 is a discrete-time GNN (with Delaunay-triangulated graph structure) that auto-regressively evolves latent 'anchor states' in a graph-structured latent space, deliberately avoiding the classic encode-process-decode loop to retain information across steps (Proposition 1 shows tighter error bounds than standard AR). System 2 is a continuous-time state observer that uses multi-head cross-attention with learned Fourier positional encoding to query anchor states at arbitrary (x,t) coordinates, followed by a GRU that aggregates the temporal sequence of conditioned embeddings into a final solution estimate. Training employs artificial generalization via random spatial subsampling and a coarser anchor-step Δ > Δ*, combined with a dual loss supervising both the continuous interpolation and the latent dynamics. Proposition 2 establishes the existence of the state estimator and provides an error bound that explicitly trades off sequence length against observability, justifying the use of multiple anchor states rather than local interpolation.

## Results

On the spatial extrapolation task (predicting at unseen locations), the proposed method achieves MSE × 10⁻³ of 0.2029 (Navier, 25% points), 0.5601 (Navier, 5% points), 2.800 (Shallow Water, 5% points), and 117.2 (Eagle, 25% points), outperforming all baselines by 4–100×. On the temporal interpolation task (Table 2), the method achieves 0.2138 MSE on Navier at half temporal resolution and 0.4802 on Shallow Water at quarter resolution, while baselines degrade by 10–100×. The method also generalizes to unseen grids (Table 4) and time extrapolation (Table 3), maintaining near-in-domain error on out-of-domain queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and subsampling level. Against DINo (Yin et al., 2022), the gap is most dramatic on Navier at 5% spatial subsampling (0.56 vs 5.49 MSE, ~10×) and on Shallow Water at 5% (2.80 vs 21.55, ~8×); DINo's Neural-ODE-based temporal modulation fails to capture data dependencies under heavy sparsity. Against Interpolated MeshGraphNet (Pfaff et al., 2020), the advantage is ~26× on Navier Low (0.56 vs 14.80) and ~3× on Shallow Water Low (2.80 vs 8.19), as MGN's cubic spatial interpolation cannot extrapolate reliably beyond observed points. Against MAgNet (Boussif et al., 2022), the margin is the largest: ~100× on Navier Low (0.56 vs 57.21) and ~11× on Shallow Water Low (2.80 vs 30.55), because MAgNet's interpolate-then-forecast scheme floods the auto-regressive solver with noisy interpolated IC values when query points far exceed observed points. In the interpolation ablation (Table 5), the Attentive Neural Process (Kim et al., 2018) baseline scores 1.920 MSE on Ext-𝒳/Ext-𝒯 versus 0.2371 for the proposed method (~8×), confirming that the learned spatio-temporal attention observer substantially exceeds handcrafted or attention-only interpolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard auto-regressive GNN paradigm (MeshGraphNet, MAgNet) that re-encodes the physical state at every timestep, instead maintaining a persistent latent 'anchor state' trajectory via a discrete dynamical system (System 1) and coupling it to a continuous-time state observer (System 2) built on cross-attention and a GRU. The theoretical backbone draws on finite-horizon observability from control theory, specifically extending the output-error state estimation framework of Janny et al. (2022b) to the PDE simulation setting. Conceptually, it positions itself between data-driven auto-regressive solvers (which satisfy unknown-PDE and new-IC generalization but are grid-locked) and physics-informed or implicit-representation methods (which are continuous but require known equations or fail to generalize), arguing that the 'double observation' formulation is the missing middle ground.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first (to the authors' knowledge) unified treatment of unknown-PDE simulation, generalization to unseen initial conditions, and continuous space-time evaluation from sparse observations, with formal error-bound guarantees that explain *why* latent-space auto-regression outperforms physical-space re-encoding. Limitations include reliance on regular time sampling, the observability assumption (A2), and evaluation restricted to 2D fluid dynamics; extending to 3D, irregular sampling, or incorporating physics priors are acknowledged as open directions. For practitioners building data-driven simulators, the key takeaway is that the choice of *where* to maintain state (latent vs. physical) has a provable and empirically large impact on long-horizon accuracy, and that a learned attention-based observer substantially outperforms handcrafted interpolation for spatial-temporal reconstruction.
