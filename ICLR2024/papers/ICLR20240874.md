## Problem

The influence function provides a principled way to quantify each training data point's impact on model predictions, but computing it requires inverting large Hessian matrices, making it infeasible for LLMs and diffusion models. Existing approximations like LiSSA and EK-FAC still rely on iterative solvers, eigenvalue decompositions, or architecture-specific factorizations that are either unstable at scale or not directly applicable to LoRA-tuned models. The paper addresses the gap of a closed-form, architecture-agnostic influence approximation that is practical for large-scale generative AI with parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a LoRA-fine-tuned model (RoBERTa-large, Llama-2-13B-chat, or stable-diffusion-v1.5), a labeled training set, and a validation set, and produces one scalar influence score per training point indicating its directional effect on the validation loss. Only the per-layer gradients of the LoRA parameters (not the full model) are needed, and a damping parameter λ_l is auto-computed from the data as 0.1×(nd_l)^{-1}Σ‖∇ℓ_i‖². The output is a vector of n real-valued scores that can be ranked to identify the most or least influential examples.

## Method

DataInf's key approximation swaps the order of matrix inversion and averaging: instead of inverting the summed gradient outer-product matrix (1/n Σ ∇ℓ_i ∇ℓ_i^T + λI)^{-1}, it approximates this as (1/n) Σ (∇ℓ_i ∇ℓ_i^T + λI)^{-1}. Each individual term is a rank-one update to a diagonal matrix, so the Sherman-Morrison formula yields a closed-form expression involving only scalar dot products of first-order gradients. This reduces computational complexity from O(n·d_l²) (LiSSA) to O(n·d_l) and memory from O(d_l²) to O(d_l) per layer, with no iterative loop. A theoretical bound (Theorem 1) shows the per-layer approximation error scales as O(d_l²), explaining why DataInf is especially accurate for LoRA fine-tuning where the trainable dimension d_l is small. The method is validated on RoBERTa-large (GLUE tasks), Llama-2-13B-chat (text generation), and stable-diffusion-v1.5 (text-to-image), outperforming LiSSA and Hessian-free baselines in correlation, AUC, and runtime.

## Results

On the influential data identification task (Table 2), DataInf achieves near-perfect class detection AUC (1.000±0.000) and recall (0.997–0.999) on all three Llama-2-13B-chat text generation tasks, substantially outperforming Hessian-free (AUC 0.770–0.772, recall 0.258). On stable-diffusion-v1.5, DataInf improves class detection AUC from 0.692 to 0.820 (style generation) and from 0.820 to 0.865 (subject generation) over Hessian-free. In terms of runtime, DataInf is 5.5× faster than LiSSA and 1149.6× faster than Exact on average across GLUE datasets (e.g., 13 s vs. 70 s vs. 11,279 s on GLUE-QQP for 4,500 influence values).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DataInf is compared against three baselines: LiSSA (Agarwal et al., 2017), Hessian-free (Charpiat et al., 2019; Pruthi et al., 2020), and the Exact influence function (Eq. 2). Against LiSSA, DataInf wins decisively on both accuracy and stability: LiSSA is highly unstable (often failing to converge or collapsing to Hessian-free on large models), while DataInf achieves higher correlation with the exact influence (e.g., 0.64 vs. 0.45 on GLUE-MRPC, r=1) and is 5.5× faster. Against Hessian-free, DataInf significantly outperforms on every metric across all five tasks in Table 2 (e.g., AUC 1.000 vs. 0.770 on math problems; AUC 0.820 vs. 0.692 on style generation) and on mislabeled data detection AUC across all four GLUE datasets. Against the Exact method, DataInf is 1149.6× faster while achieving comparable or occasionally superior mislabeled-data-detection AUC, suggesting the damping parameter can slightly degrade Exact's performance. The paper notes that LiSSA was entirely excluded from the influential data identification experiments (Table 2) due to repeated numerical failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

DataInf builds directly on the influence function framework of Koh & Liang (2017) and the LiSSA iterative inverse-Hessian-vector-product method of Agarwal et al. (2017), replacing the latter's iterative scheme with a closed-form expression derived from the Sherman-Morrison formula. The key conceptual departure is the approximation in equation 4—swapping the order of matrix inversion and averaging—which transforms an intractable matrix inversion into a sum of rank-one updates. This positions DataInf as an architecture-agnostic alternative to EK-FAC (Grosse et al., 2023), which relies on Kronecker-factored gradients specific to MLP-style layers and is not straightforwardly applicable to LoRA-tuned or transformer-based models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DataInf makes influence-function-based data attribution practically feasible for LLMs and diffusion models, where prior methods were computationally intractable, achieving 5.5× and 1149.6× speedups over LiSSA and Exact respectively. The theoretical guarantee that approximation error scales as O(d_l²) explains why the method is particularly well-suited to parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), where d_l is small. However, the paper acknowledges that non-influence-based data valuation methods often outperform influence functions on downstream tasks, and that quantitative evaluation metrics for influence scores remain underdeveloped—both are open directions. The method's reliance on negative log-likelihood losses and its degradation with larger LoRA ranks limit its generality.
