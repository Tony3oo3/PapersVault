## Problem

Standard image recognition models are trained at a fixed resolution and degrade sharply when deployed on low-resolution inputs or images with natural variations (fog, noise, compression artifacts). Prior work addresses either resolution degradation or input variation in isolation, and super-resolution-based solutions require separate models per scale factor, making them impractical for real-world deployment where both issues co-occur.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242243/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PAC-FNO accepts RGB images at arbitrary spatial resolutions (tested from 28x28 up to 299x299) that may simultaneously carry natural degradations such as fog, Gaussian noise, or motion blur, and outputs a single class label via a pre-trained backbone. The module is a lightweight plug-in (1-13% of backbone parameters) inserted before the backbone's first layer; for low-resolution inputs it internally applies zero-padding and interpolation to match the backbone's expected input size, so no external resizing is needed at inference.

## Method

The architecture replaces the standard FNO block's ideal low-pass filter with an 'all-component' (AC-FNO) block that retains all frequency components, since high-frequency detail is critical for fine-grained classification. Multiple AC-FNO blocks are arranged in a parallel configuration (n stages x m parallel branches) rather than the conventional serial stack, increasing first-layer capacity to encode diverse spatial patterns; outputs from all m branches are concatenated, projected by a linear layer, and batch-normalized before feeding the backbone. Training follows a two-stage procedure: stage 1 jointly trains PAC-FNO and the backbone at the target resolution to harmonize their hidden spaces, and stage 2 fine-tunes only PAC-FNO on a mixture of low-resolution and target-resolution images to build a resolution-invariant representation. The entire module adds only ~3.65M parameters (for ConvNeXt-Tiny) and runs in constant FLOPs (8.98 GFLOPs) regardless of input resolution.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, PAC-FNO achieves the best top-1 accuracy at low resolutions across all four backbone models. For ConvNeXt-Tiny, PAC-FNO reaches 63.2% at 32×32 (vs. 34.5% for Resize, an 83% relative gain) and 76.2% at 64×64, while maintaining 81.5% at the target 224×224 resolution. On fine-grained datasets (Table 2), PAC-FNO shows the largest margins: 77.3% at 32×32 on Oxford-IIIT Pets (vs. 36.4% Resize) and 74.9% on Food-101 (vs. 48.1% Resize). Under natural input variations (Table 3), PAC-FNO outperforms all baselines at low resolutions, e.g., 43.2% vs. 22.9% (Resize) for Spatter at 32×32.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAC-FNO consistently outperforms all FNO-based baselines (FNO, UNO, A-FNO) at low resolutions (≤64) across all four backbones, with the largest gap over A-FNO on ConvNeXt-Tiny (63.2% vs. 53.5% at 32×32). Super-resolution baselines DRLN and DRPN collapse at very low resolutions (e.g., DRLN scores 0.22% at 28×28 on ResNet-18) and are limited to 2×/4×/8× upscale factors, making them inapplicable at many tested resolutions. The latest SR model OSRT (Yu et al., 2023) achieves 71.2% at 56×56 on ConvNeXt-Tiny with fine-tuning, but PAC-FNO reaches 74.5% at the same resolution with only 3.65M additional parameters versus OSRT's 11.93M. At the target resolution (224×224), PAC-FNO is competitive but slightly below Resize/Fine-tune for some models (e.g., 81.5% vs. 82.5% for ConvNeXt-Tiny), indicating a small trade-off in clean-image accuracy. On fine-grained datasets, PAC-FNO is the only FNO variant that surpasses the Fine-tune baseline at low resolutions, with gains of 48.3% (28×28) to 6.5% (64×64) over Fine-tune on Oxford-IIIT Pets.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAC-FNO directly extends the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) framework of Li et al. (2020) by removing the ideal low-pass filter from the FNO block (yielding the AC-FNO block) and arranging multiple AC-FNO blocks in parallel rather than the conventional serial or U-shaped (UNO) configurations. It also departs from prior FNO-based visual recognition work (A-FNO, UNO) that embedded FNOs as internal components (e.g., token mixers within a ViT), instead using PAC-FNO as a standalone front-end module that processes raw images at arbitrary resolutions before feeding a pre-trained backbone. The paper explicitly positions itself against super-resolution pipelines (DRLN, DRPN, OSRT) that require separate models per upscaling factor, and against robustness methods that address only one degradation type (resolution or corruption) at a time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design—adding only 1–13% of the backbone's parameters while supporting all input resolutions and natural variations with a single model—offers a practical path for deploying recognition systems in real-world settings where input quality is unpredictable. The two-stage training algorithm minimizes disruption to the pre-trained backbone, making adoption straightforward. Open questions include whether PAC-FNO generalizes to tasks beyond classification (detection, segmentation), how it performs under compound degradations (e.g., low resolution combined with motion blur and fog simultaneously), and whether the parallel AC-FNO blocks can be further compressed without sacrificing the complementary frequency coverage that the spectral visualizations reveal.
