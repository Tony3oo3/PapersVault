## Problem

Existing auxiliary learning methods rely on optimization-based strategies (loss reweighting, gradient manipulation) that struggle to resolve negative transfer from conflicting task gradients. Architecture-based methods with soft parameter sharing avoid this conflict but typically increase inference cost because the primary and auxiliary branches depend on each other. The paper asks: can we design an architecture that exploits auxiliary labels during training yet reduces to a single-task network at inference with negligible extra cost?

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (321×321 for VGG-16/ResNet-50, 224×224 for ViT-Base) and produces task-specific dense or classification outputs (segmentation masks, surface normal maps, depth/disparity maps, or class labels). A key structural detail is that the training graph contains both a primary and an auxiliary branch with cross-task fusion connections, but the inference graph is simply the primary branch with all auxiliary-related computations removed—leaving only 2n BatchNorm parameters and n ReLU activations as residual overhead from the fusion operations.

## Method

The paper proposes an asymmetric soft-parameter-sharing architecture where only directed primary-to-auxiliary forward connections exist after convergence, enabling safe removal of the auxiliary branch at inference. Two variants are implemented: (1) Aux-G directly instantiates this asymmetric structure with layerwise primary-to-auxiliary connections, so the auxiliary branch's back-propagated gradients serve as additional regularization for the primary task; (2) Aux-NAS initializes a bi-directional search space (both primary-to-auxiliary and auxiliary-to-primary connections) and applies an L1 regularization exclusively on the auxiliary-to-primary architecture weights, gradually pruning them via a single-shot gradient-based NAS procedure so the network converges to the asymmetric form. The fusion operation concatenates incoming cross-task features, applies a 1×1 convolution to the removable (auxiliary) features, and adds the result to the primary residual path through BatchNorm and ReLU, ensuring that when auxiliary weights vanish the only remaining cost is negligible normalization. The method is orthogonal to optimization-based MTL techniques and scales linearly with the number of auxiliary tasks.

## Results

On NYU v2 with VGG-16, Aux-NAS achieves 36.0% mIoU for semantic segmentation (vs. 33.5% single-task and 35.0% Adashare) and 52.4% within 11.25° for surface normal prediction (vs. 46.4% single-task and 50.3% Adashare). On CityScapes with VGG-16, Aux-NAS reaches 71.1% mIoU for segmentation (vs. 68.3% single-task and 70.3% Adashare). With ViTBase on NYU v2, Aux-NAS reduces surface normal mean error to 12.5° (vs. 14.6° single-task and 13.2° Adashare). All methods maintain a single-task inference cost for the primary task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary architecture-based baseline is Adashare (Sun et al., 2020), which uses a select-or-skip policy for hard parameter sharing. The authors also implement Adashare-Aux, an auxiliary-learning variant that fixes the select-or-skip policy to all-1's and removes sparsity regularization, ensuring the same single-task inference cost as the proposed methods. Aux-NAS consistently outperforms both Adashare and Adashare-Aux across all datasets and backbones: on NYU v2 (VGG-16) it gains +1.0 mIoU over Adashare for segmentation and +2.1% within 11.25° for surface normal; on CityScapes (VGG-16) it gains +0.8 mIoU; on NYU v2 (ViTBase) it reduces surface normal mean error by 0.7°; and on NYU v2 (ResNet-50) it gains a substantial +3.5 mIoU over Adashare. The simpler Aux-G-Layer variant also beats Adashare on most metrics, confirming that the asymmetric architecture design itself (not just NAS) provides the main benefit. The paper further shows that combining Aux-NAS with optimization-based methods (e.g., GCS, PCGrad-Aux) yields additional gains, reaching 36.3% mIoU on NYU v2 segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the soft parameter sharing architecture lineage (Cross-Stitch Networks, NDDR-CNN, MTL-NAS) by introducing an asymmetric design where cross-task connections are directed only from primary to auxiliary, enabling the auxiliary branch to be removed at inference. It departs from the dominant optimization-based auxiliary learning paradigm (GCS, DWA, PCGrad) by treating the problem as an architecture search rather than a gradient/loss-weight manipulation problem, motivated by recent evidence that optimization-based methods struggle to resolve negative transfer. The NAS component builds on one-shot gradient-based search (DARTS) but introduces asymmetric L1 regularization on auxiliary-to-primary architecture weights to enforce the desired unidirectional topology, and eliminates the retrain phase by ensuring the searched model is directly evaluable after pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymmetric training/inference architecture offers a practical recipe for leveraging auxiliary labels (depth, normals, disparity) to boost a primary task like segmentation without any inference overhead, which is valuable in deployment-constrained settings. However, the performance gains over strong baselines like Adashare-Aux are modest (typically 0.5–1.5 mIoU), and the method requires a more complex NAS training procedure. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to very large numbers of auxiliary tasks, whether the linear scalability claim holds in practice beyond K=2, and whether the NAS search space could be further constrained to reduce training cost. The paper also does not explore whether the discovered primary-to-auxiliary connection patterns are interpretable or transferable across tasks.
