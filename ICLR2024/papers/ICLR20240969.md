## Problem

Existing preference-based RL methods (e.g., PEBBLE, SURF) treat the alignment evaluation objective as independent of the policy that generates the training trajectories, ignoring the fact that the data distribution at the upper level is a function of the lower-level optimal policy. This entanglement gap leads to a misaligned evaluation objective, causing policy drift and a measurable performance gap relative to an oracle reward. The paper identifies this as the first rigorous formulation of RLHF as a bilevel optimization problem where the upper-level stochasticity is coupled to the lower-level variable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an MDP (state space, action space, transition dynamics), pairwise trajectory preferences from a simulated human teacher (modeled via the Bradley-Terry model), and parameterized reward r_ν and policy π_θ. The upper-level evaluation collects trajectories under the current lower-level policy π_θ*(ν) to compute the alignment utility U_ν(τ), meaning the data distribution ρ(τ; θ*(ν)) shifts as the policy updates — a key structural difference from standard stochastic bilevel problems. The algorithm outputs a jointly optimized reward parameter ν_T and policy parameter θ^K(ν_T) that constitute a local KKT point of the bilevel problem.

## Method

PARL formulates policy alignment as a bilevel optimization: the lower level solves standard policy optimization for a given reward parameter ν, while the upper level maximizes an alignment utility G(ν, θ*(ν)) whose expectation is taken over trajectories generated by the lower-level optimal policy. The A-PARL algorithm computes the upper-level gradient via a hypergradient that requires ∇_ν θ*(ν), obtained by implicit differentiation of the lower-level first-order optimality condition, yielding a Hessian-inverse expression involving mixed second-order terms of the value function. Because the upper-level sampling distribution depends on the lower-level iterate, the convergence analysis introduces an f-divergence bound (Lemma 1) to quantify distributional drift between the approximate and optimal policies, a term absent from standard bilevel optimization analyses. Under PL-condition assumptions on the value function (rather than strong convexity), the algorithm achieves an O(1/T) sample complexity for reaching a first-order stationary point, matching the rate of non-convex bilevel optimization without lower-level strong convexity.

## Results

On large-scale continuous control tasks (Walker from DeepMind Control Suite, DoorOpen and ButtonPress from MetaWorld), A-PARL achieves near-oracle episodic return in significantly fewer environment steps compared to SOTA baselines, with up to 63% improvement in sample efficiency. The metric used is true episodic reward return (ground truth), averaged over 5 seeds. A-PARL also demonstrates correct aligned behavior (e.g., successfully opening a door) where PEBBLE fails, confirming that the bilevel formulation resolves reward hacking and misalignment issues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A-PARL outperforms both PEBBLE (Lee et al., 2021) and PEBBLE+SURF (Park et al., 2022) on all three evaluated environments (Walker, DoorOpen, ButtonPress) in terms of true episodic return, achieving near-oracle performance in substantially fewer environment steps. The headline quantitative gain is up to 63% reduction in samples required to solve the task compared to the best baseline. PEBBLE+SURF benefits from additional unsupervised data and augmentations, yet A-PARL still surpasses it with controlled augmentations, suggesting the bilevel formulation's corrected upper-level objective is the primary driver of improvement. Qualitatively, A-PARL learns the desired door-opening behavior while PEBBLE gets stuck, demonstrating that the distribution-shift correction in the upper-level objective prevents reward hacking. The paper notes that PEBBLE+SURF's performance varies rapidly with the quality and amount of unsupervised information, making the comparison somewhat asymmetric, but A-PARL's advantage persists under controlled settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the bilevel optimization algorithm of Ghadimi & Wang (2018) and the RLHF paradigm of Christiano et al. (2017), but departs from existing preference-based RL methods (PEBBLE, SURF) by identifying that they ignore the dependence of the upper-level alignment objective's data distribution on the lower-level optimal policy. The authors formalize RLHF as a bilevel optimization problem where the stochasticity at the upper level is coupled to the lower-level variable, creating a new class of stochastic bilevel problems distinct from standard formulations in the optimization literature. This positioning generalizes prior RLHF formulations while addressing the distribution shift issue that causes misalignment in iterative preference-based training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the distribution-dependence gap in existing RLHF formulations provides a principled explanation for why iterative preference-based methods (PEBBLE, SURF) exhibit performance degradation over training, and the bilevel framework offers a corrective. However, the practical impact is currently limited by the assumption of oracle access to first- and second-order quantities (Remark 2), the requirement for unique lower-level optima (restricting to tabular or softmax policies), and the non-convexity of the upper objective. Open questions include extending the analysis to fully stochastic settings with sampled estimates, handling non-unique lower-level solutions, and validating the framework at scale beyond the three environments tested.
