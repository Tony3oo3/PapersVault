## Problem

The convergence of primal-dual algorithms for constrained cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) relies on strong duality, which has never been formally validated in the multi-agent setting. This paper proves that strong duality actually fails in constrained cooperative MARL due to nonconvex quadratic constraints induced by the product policy structure, leaving existing convergence guarantees incomplete. It further asks whether constrained cooperative MARL is fundamentally more challenging than its special cases of cooperative MARL (no constraints) and constrained RL (single agent).

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate on a multi-agent MDP with M agents sharing a global state space, each with its own action space, connected via a decentralized communication graph, and subject to K safety constraints with thresholds ξ_k. The output is a product policy (independent per-agent policies) along with explicit sublinear convergence rate bounds on both the optimality gap and weighted constraint violation. A key structural detail is that the problem is reformulated in occupation measure space, where the product policy constraint introduces quadratic equality constraints that make the feasible set nonconvex—unlike the single-agent case where the problem reduces to a linear program.

## Method

The paper first reformulates constrained cooperative MARL as an optimization over the occupation measure, revealing that the product policy structure imposes nonconvex quadratic equality constraints (Theorem 1), which makes the problem fundamentally harder than both constrained RL (a linear program) and unconstrained cooperative MARL (solvable by greedy value iteration). It then constructs a concrete two-agent example with a strictly positive duality gap of 3/4 (Fact 1), and reanalyzes the primal-dual algorithm to show its constraint violation converges only up to an irreducible error of 2Δ (Theorem 2). To avoid the duality gap, the paper proposes a decentralized primal algorithm (Algorithm 2) that uses natural policy gradient updates on a selectively chosen target value function (reward or a violated safety score), achieving convergence rates that depend on an advantage gap ζ_k instead of Δ (Theorem 3). The advantage gap vanishes if and only if the Q function admits a factorized structure across agents. Finally, the paper constructs two examples showing that neither the primal-dual nor the primal algorithm uniformly dominates the other, requiring novel multi-statement induction proofs for the highly nonconvex potential functions involved.

## Results

The paper establishes that constrained cooperative MARL can have a strictly positive duality gap (Δ = 3/4 in a constructed two-agent example), invalidating the strong duality assumption underlying existing primal-dual algorithms. The reanalyzed primal-dual algorithm (Theorem 2) achieves an optimality gap of O(1/√T) but a constraint violation bounded by O(1/√T) + 2Δ, meaning it may converge to an infeasible policy. The proposed decentralized primal algorithm (Theorem 3) avoids the duality gap entirely, achieving both optimality gap and constraint violation at rate O(√(M/[T(1−γ)⁵])) + ζ_k/(1−γ)², with sample complexity O(ε⁻⁴) versus O(ε⁻⁵ ln ε⁻¹) for the primal-dual approach. In the single-agent case (M=1), the primal algorithm strictly improves upon CRPO (Xu et al., 2021) by a factor of √(|S||A|(1−γ)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between its own primal-dual algorithm (Algorithm 1) and its proposed decentralized primal algorithm (Algorithm 2), showing that neither uniformly dominates the other. In Example 1 (Theorem 4), the primal-dual algorithm generates an infeasible policy for all iterations (oscillating between (1,1) and (0,0)), while the primal algorithm converges to the optimal policy (1/4, 1/4) within O(α⁻¹ ln(α⁻¹)) iterations. Conversely, in Example 2 (Theorem 5), the primal-dual algorithm finds the optimal policy in a single iteration, while the primal algorithm remains infeasible for all t (V₁(π_t) = 4p_t(1−p_t) ≤ 1 < ξ₁ = 1.8). Against the CRPO baseline (Xu et al., 2021) in the single-agent case, the primal algorithm improves the convergence rate by a factor of √(|S||A|(1−γ)), achieved through a tighter bound on the policy change ‖π_{t+1} − π_t‖₁ that avoids the |S||A| factor present in CRPO's Lipschitz-based analysis. The paper also notes that existing convergence analyses for primal-dual methods in constrained cooperative MARL (Lu et al., 2021; Ying et al., 2023) only establish convergence to stationary points with vanishing gradient norm, without characterizing constraint violation or optimality of the output policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CRPO primal algorithm (Xu et al., 2021) from single-agent constrained RL to the multi-agent cooperative setting, incorporating decentralized natural policy gradient updates from the decentralized actor-critic framework (Chen et al., 2022). It departs fundamentally from the mainstream primal-dual approach (Diddigi et al., 2019; Lu et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2023) by proving that the strong duality condition underpinning those algorithms fails in the multi-agent product-policy setting, due to nonconvex quadratic constraints in the occupation measure space. The paper also contrasts with prior convergence analyses (Lu et al., 2021; Ying et al., 2023) that only established convergence to stationary points, by providing the first results that explicitly characterize the duality gap's impact on optimality and constraint violation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proof that strong duality fails in constrained cooperative MARL invalidates the theoretical foundation of the entire mainstream primal-dual approach and reveals that the problem is fundamentally more challenging than both its single-agent (constrained RL) and unconstrained (cooperative MARL) special cases. The finding that neither the primal-dual nor the primal algorithm uniformly dominates the other—each can be infeasible while the other converges—suggests that no single algorithmic paradigm will suffice, motivating future work on hybrid or problem-structure-aware methods. Open questions include whether the advantage gap ζ_k can be eliminated through richer policy parameterizations, and whether the nonconvex quadratic structure admits approximation schemes that restore tractable convergence guarantees.
