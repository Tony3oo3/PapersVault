## Problem

Theoretical guarantees for diffusion model sampling remain immature: prior analyses of deterministic (probability flow ODE) samplers exhibited exponential dependency on score smoothness, relied on continuous-time SDE/ODE toolboxes, or failed to account for score estimation errors in discrete time. This paper develops a unified non-asymptotic framework that directly analyzes discrete-time diffusion samplers under minimal data distribution assumptions (no smoothness required), quantifying how ℓ2 score estimation errors affect generation quality.

## Input / Output

The analysis assumes access to score function estimates {s_t} with bounded average ℓ2 error ε_score and, for the deterministic sampler, bounded average Jacobian error ε_Jacobi; the target distribution only needs to be absolutely continuous with support radius growing at most polynomially in T. The output is a TV distance bound between the generated sample distribution p_1 and the target q_1, with explicit polynomial dependence on dimension d, number of steps T, and the score/Jacobian estimation errors. Notably, the convergence is measured at step 1 (not step 0) because the learning rate schedule makes X_1 and X_0 nearly identical.

## Method

The core technical contribution is an elementary non-asymptotic analysis that directly tracks the discrete-time reverse process dynamics, eliminating the need for continuous-time SDE/ODE toolboxes and their associated discretization-error detours. For the deterministic sampler (discretized probability flow ODE: Y_{t-1} = (1/√α_t)(Y_t + (1-α_t)/2 · s_t(Y_t))), they establish TV distance O(d²/T + d⁶/T² + √d·ε_score + d·ε_Jacobi), yielding a 1/ε iteration complexity and removing prior exponential smoothness dependencies. For the stochastic DDPM-type sampler (which uses a full (1-α_t) score coefficient and injects noise σ_t·Z_t per step), they derive TV distance O(d²/√T + √d·ε_score·log²T), matching state-of-the-art ε-dependency. A carefully designed learning rate schedule minimizes the discretization error term (1-α_t)/(1-ᾱ_t) while keeping ᾱ_1 ≈ 1 and ᾱ_T ≈ 0. The framework also reveals that ℓ2 score error alone is insufficient for the deterministic sampler—Jacobian stability is provably necessary, as shown by a counterexample where arbitrarily small score error still yields TV distance 1.

## Results

For the deterministic (probability flow ODE) sampler, Theorem 1 establishes TV(q₁, p₁) ≤ C₁d²log⁴T/T + C₁d⁶log⁶T/T² + C₁√(d log³T)·ε_score + C₁d(log T)·ε_Jacobi, implying that Õ(d²/ε + d³/√ε) steps suffice for ε-accuracy with perfect scores. For the DDPM-type stochastic sampler, Theorem 2 yields TV(q₁, p₁) ≤ C₁d²log³T/√T + C₁√d·ε_score·log²T, requiring O(1/ε²) steps for ε-accuracy. Both results hold under only minimal assumptions on the target distribution (no smoothness or log-Sobolev conditions), and the deterministic sampler result is the first to account for both ℓ₂ score errors and Jacobian errors in discrete time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The deterministic sampler result (Theorem 1) improves upon Chen et al. (2023c), which provided the first non-asymptotic guarantees for DDIM-type samplers but exhibited exponential dependency on the Lipschitz constant of the score function and required exact score estimates; the present work achieves a concrete d²/ε scaling with no smoothness assumption and explicitly quantifies score estimation errors. Compared to Benton et al. (2023b), which also showed exponential Lipschitz dependency and did not account for discretization errors, this paper's elementary discrete-time analysis avoids both limitations. For the stochastic sampler, the 1/ε² iteration complexity matches the state-of-the-art ε-dependency of Chen et al. (2022a) and Benton et al. (2023a), though with a worse dimensional dependency (d² vs. d in prior work). The concurrent work Chen et al. (2023b) achieves better d-dependency (Õ(L²√d/ε)) for the probability flow ODE but requires additional stochastic corrector steps (overdamped/underdamped Langevin), making the sampler no longer purely deterministic; the present result shows the plain ODE already achieves 1/ε scaling without such correctors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-asymptotic convergence analysis of discrete-time diffusion samplers initiated by Chen et al. (2023c) for the probability flow ODE and Chen et al. (2022a) for DDPM-type samplers, but departs methodologically by analyzing the discrete-time processes directly with elementary tools rather than studying continuous-time SDE/ODE limits and then bounding discretization error. It builds on the foundational reverse-time SDE construction of Anderson (1982) and Haussmann & Pardoux (1986) and the SGM framework of Song et al. (2021b), while improving the deterministic sampler's convergence rate from prior work and, for the first time, incorporating both ℓ₂ score estimation errors and Jacobian estimation errors into the discrete-time ODE-based analysis. The key conceptual departure is showing that ℓ₂ score accuracy alone is provably insufficient for the deterministic sampler, necessitating an additional Jacobian stability assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that arbitrarily small ℓ₂ score error can still yield TV distance 1 for the deterministic sampler (via a discretization-induced quantization effect) is a useful caution for practitioners who assume score accuracy alone guarantees sample quality. The 1/T rate for the plain probability flow ODE without corrector steps is practically relevant since it validates the efficiency of DDIM-style sampling under minimal distributional assumptions. Open questions highlighted by the authors include tightening the sub-optimal dimension dependency, establishing Wasserstein-distance convergence (which might relax the Jacobian assumption), and developing end-to-end guarantees that jointly account for score learning and sampling errors.
