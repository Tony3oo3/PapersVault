## Problem

Gated-convolution language models (H3, Hyena, RWKV) are widely claimed to match attention quality, yet the paper shows a persistent perplexity gap of up to 2.1 points on the Pile. Through fine-grained analysis, the authors identify that 82% of this gap stems from a single capability—associative recall (recalling information previously mentioned in context)—and that prior synthetic AR benchmarks fail to capture this gap because they assume a single query at a fixed position with a small vocabulary, unlike real language which requires multiple recalls at varying positions over large vocabularies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240628/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume token sequences of up to 2048 tokens from the Pile (GPT-2 BPE tokenizer) and output next-token probability distributions over the vocabulary. For evaluation, tokens are stratified into 'AR Hits' (last token of a bigram previously seen in context but ≤1250× in training, ~6.4% of tokens) and 'Other tokens' to isolate recall performance. The synthetic MQAR benchmark uses vocabulary size 8192, sequences of 64–512 tokens, with D key-value pairs placed at the start and multiple queries at varying distances following a power-law distribution.

## Method

The paper introduces BASECONV, a minimal gated-convolution operator (linear projection ⊙ convolution) proven to simulate any arithmetic circuit and thus any gated-convolution architecture (H3, Hyena, RWKV, RetNet) with only poly-log parameter blowup. Theoretically, the authors show that solving MQAR with input-independent filters requires model dimension scaling as Õ(N log c) with sequence length N, while attention solves it with O(c²) parameters independent of N. Empirically, gated-convolution models require d ≥ N to achieve >0.9 accuracy on MQAR, whereas attention succeeds at constant d=64. To close the gap, the authors propose hybrid BASECONV-attention architectures where sparse attention is applied only at input-dependent positions (programmatic bigram-repeat detection or learned top-k selection), closing 97.4% of the AR perplexity gap while maintaining sub-quadratic scaling and outperforming pure attention by 0.85 PPL at 360M parameters.

## Results

On the Pile, attention outperforms all gated-convolution architectures by up to 2.1 perplexity points (e.g., 11.01 vs. 13.13 for Long Conv at 360M params). The associative recall (AR) slice accounts for 82% of the average quality gap: a 70M attention model achieves 2.16 PPL on AR Hits versus 5.00 for Hyena and 5.70 for RWKV at the same scale. The proposed BASECONV-Attention hybrid with full attention at 360M params achieves 8.59 overall PPL, outperforming the pure attention baseline (9.44) by 0.85 PPL while using 18% fewer FLOPs. Programmatic sparse attention (selecting only repeated-bigram positions) closes 85% of the AR gap, and learned selection with k=256 positions closes 72% while maintaining sub-quadratic scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five gated-convolution and recurrent baselines underperform attention on the Pile, with the gap concentrated almost entirely on the associative recall slice. H3 (Fu et al., 2023a) achieves 12.06 PPL at 70M vs. attention's 11.01, with AR Hits at 6.75 vs. 2.16; at 360M it reaches 10.38 vs. 9.44. Hyena (Poli et al., 2023a) is the strongest pure gated-convolution baseline at 10.07 PPL (360M) but still trails attention by 0.63 PPL overall and 1.85 PPL on AR Hits. RWKV (Peng et al., 2023) is competitive at 9.79 PPL (360M) but its AR Hits score (3.82) remains nearly 2 PPL worse than attention (1.98). Liquid S4 (Hasani et al., 2022), an input-dependent state-space model, lags attention by 4.4 PPL overall (16.80 vs. 12.37 at 5B tokens) with AR Hits at 22.75 vs. 2.32. RetNet (Sun et al., 2023), an input-dependent recurrent architecture, improves over gated convolutions on MQAR synthetics but still requires hidden state dimensionality that grows with the number of key-value pairs to recall, unlike attention which is independent of sequence length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the mechanistic interpretability literature connecting associative recall to in-context learning (Olsson et al., 2022; Elhage et al., 2021) and the recent wave of gated-convolution architectures (H3, Hyena, RWKV, RetNet) that were validated on single-query synthetic recall tasks. It departs from prior work by showing those synthetic formulations are insufficient: real language requires multi-query recall at variable positions with large vocabularies, which the authors formalize as MQAR. Theoretically, the paper generalizes the arithmetic circuit equivalency result of Dao et al. (2020) from linear functions to all polynomials via the BASECONV operator, enabling precise complexity analysis of the entire gated-convolution family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the field with a concrete diagnostic (MQAR) that explains why efficient architectures underperform attention on real data, and shows that a minimal fix—adding input-dependent sparse attention at just 3–10% of layers—closes 97.4% of the gap while preserving sub-quadratic scaling. This gives architecture designers a clear mechanistic target rather than relying on end-to-end perplexity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to fuzzy/semantic recall (synonymous bigrams, higher-order concepts), whether learned selection can match programmatic selection without the O(Ndk) overhead, and whether the gap persists or closes at the 7B+ parameter scale where compute budgets allow more layers.
