## Problem

Message-passing GNNs operate on a fixed input graph, making them vulnerable to noise and missing edges, while their local aggregation causes over-squashing, under-reaching, and expressive power capped at 1-WL. Existing rewiring strategies rely on heuristics (multi-hop neighbors, curvature-based flips) or expensive global attention (graph transformers), and no principled, data-driven method exists for learning task-relevant graph structures. The paper also identifies a theoretical gap: the impact of probabilistic rewiring on a model's expressive power had never been formally analyzed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an n-order attributed graph as an adjacency matrix A(G) ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} and a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}. The upstream model produces unnormalized edge priors θ ∈ R^{n×n} (or R^{n×n×M} for M independent prior sets), which parameterize a conditional probability mass function over adjacency matrices constrained to have exactly k edges. The output is a rewired adjacency matrix Ā = g(A(G), A⁽¹⁾, …, A⁽ᴺ⁾) — typically formed by subtracting sampled deletion matrices and adding sampled insertion matrices — which is then passed to a downstream MPNN to yield a graph-level or node-level prediction.

## Method

PR-MPNNs introduce an upstream GIN that computes per-edge prior weights, which parameterize a probability distribution over k-edge adjacency matrices via an exactly-k subset constraint, creating dependencies between edge random variables (unlike independent Bernoulli models used in prior graph structure learning). Exact sampling from this constrained distribution and differentiable marginal inference are performed using the SIMPLE estimator, which computes the exactly-k marginals μ(θ) in O(log k · log l) time and backpropagates via ∇_θ L ≈ ∂_θ μ(θ) ∇_A ℓ, achieving lower bias and variance than Gumbel-SoftSub-ST or I-MLE. The original graph is rewired by aggregating N sampled k-edge matrices with the original adjacency (e.g., A(G) − A⁽¹⁾ + A⁽²⁾ for simultaneous edge deletion and insertion), and the result feeds a downstream MPNN. Theoretically, the paper proves that PR-MPNNs preserve isomorphism invariance on discrete 1-WL colorings while separating non-isomorphic graphs with probability approaching 1, outperforming uniform random edge/node dropping, and identifies a negative result showing that on single-color-class graphs (e.g., regular graphs) no probabilistic rewiring can exceed random sampling.

## Results

On molecular property prediction, PR-MPNN with the SIMPLE gradient estimator achieves the best MAE on ZINC (0.139 without edge features, 0.085 with edge features) and ALCHEMY (6.447), and the best ROC-AUC on OGBG-MOLHIV (0.795), outperforming the base GIN model by up to 45% on ZINC and 42% on ALCHEMY. On the TUDATASET graph classification benchmarks, PR-MPNN achieves the highest accuracy on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., 98.4% on MUTAG, 80.7% on PROTEINS), with only the WL Kernel slightly ahead on NCI1 (86.0% vs. 85.6%). On synthetic expressive-power benchmarks (EXP, CSL), PR-MPNN reaches 100% accuracy, matching or exceeding more expressive architectures like OSAN and GIN+ID-GNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PR-MPNN (SIMPLE) consistently outperforms the base GIN model across all molecular datasets, reducing MAE by 46% on ZINC (0.139 vs. 0.258) and 42% on ALCHEMY (6.447 vs. 11.12). Against graph transformers, PR-MPNN beats SAT (Chen et al., 2022) on ZINC (0.139 vs. 0.166) and OGBG-MOLHIV (0.795 vs. 0.625), but GPS (Rampašek et al., 2022) retains an edge on ZINC +EDGE (0.070 vs. 0.085). On TUDATASET, PR-MPNN dominates all 14 compared methods (GK, PK, WL Kernel, DGCNN, IGN, PPGNs, Natural GN, GSN, CIN, CAN, CIN++, FOSR, DropGNN) on 4 of 5 datasets, with the sole exception being NCI1 where the WL Kernel leads by 0.4 points (86.0 vs. 85.6). On the 4-CYCLES expressive-power benchmark, PR-MPNN matches or outperforms DropGNN (Papp et al., 2021), achieving 100% accuracy with 10 priors. On QM9, PR-MPNN achieves the lowest MAE on HOMO, LUMO, gap, and Omega tasks, outperforming R-GNN, GNN-FiLM, SPN, and DRew (Gutteridge et al., 2023). On heterophilic WebKB node classification, PR-MPNN outperforms diffusion GNN, Geom-GCN, and SDRF, and even beats graph transformers on TEXAS, though transformers lead on CORNELL and WISCONSIN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SIMPLE gradient estimator for exactly-k subset sampling (Ahmed et al., 2023) and the standard message-passing GNN framework (Gilmer et al., 2017), combining them into a principled probabilistic rewiring pipeline. It departs from prior graph rewiring work—which has relied on heuristic strategies (multi-hop neighbors, curvature-based edge addition, spectral methods, expander graphs) or purely random approaches (DropGNN, DropEdge)—by learning task-relevant edge priors through an upstream MPNN and sampling from a proper probability mass function with exactly-k constraints. Unlike earlier probabilistic structure learning (Franceschi et al., 2019) that modeled edges as independent Bernoulli variables, PR-MPNNs introduce dependencies between edges via the k-subset constraint, and unlike graph transformers (GPS, SAT) that achieve global connectivity at quadratic cost, PR-MPNNs maintain local message passing with a controlled number of added/removed edges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of how probabilistic rewiring affects MPNN expressive power, showing that PR-MPNNs can separate non-isomorphic graphs beyond 1-WL while preserving isomorphism invariance, and identifying precise conditions under which they outperform uniform random sampling. A notable negative result (Proposition 4.4) establishes that for certain graph classes (e.g., regular graphs), no probabilistic rewiring strategy can exceed random sampling, delineating the method's theoretical limits. Open questions include whether combining probabilistic rewiring with downstream architectures designed to address over-smoothing (e.g., on heterophilic data) yields further gains, and how the approach scales to very large graphs where even the O(l) sampling cost becomes prohibitive.
