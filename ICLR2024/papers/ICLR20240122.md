## Problem

Existing benchmarks for time-series causal discovery (TSCD) suffer from a fundamental tradeoff: numerical datasets (VAR, Lorenz-96) are over-simplified and unrealistic, quasi-real datasets cover only a few well-understood domains, and real datasets lack ground-truth causal graphs. This means algorithm scores on synthetic benchmarks may not reflect true performance on real-world data, and no single benchmark simultaneously offers high data fidelity, verified causal ground truth, and generalizability across diverse fields.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes real multivariate time-series observations (N variables over T time steps) from a target scenario, plus an optional prior knowledge graph (e.g., a geometric distance graph for spatially distributed sensors). It produces a TSCD benchmark pair: a generated time-series of 2N variables (the original N variables plus N residual terms) of length 40 per sample (500 samples per dataset), and a 2N×2N ground-truth Actual Causal Graph (ACG) with a fixed block structure [H, J_N; I_N, 0], where H is the sparse Hypothetical Causal Graph, J_N is an all-ones matrix, and I_N is the identity matrix. The residual terms introduce instantaneous effects (x_j → x_i^R), so evaluation focuses only on the H block rather than the full ACG.

## Method

CausalTime fits real observations with a Causally Disentangled Neural Network (CDNN) — an LSTM-based architecture with a shared decoder and scheduled sampling to reduce autoregressive error accumulation — and separately fits per-variable noise distributions using Normalizing Flows. A Hypothetical Causal Graph (HCG) is then extracted either via DeepSHAP importance analysis on the fully-connected prediction model or from domain prior knowledge (e.g., thresholded geographic distances). The key technical contribution is splitting the fitted NAR model into three components: a causal term (predictions using only HCG parents), a residual term (the difference between full and HCG-restricted predictions, stored as an independent time-series), and a noise term. By treating the N residual terms as additional variables, the pipeline constructs a 2N×2N Actual Causal Graph that is known by construction, enabling high-fidelity time-series generation without circularly depending on a TSCD algorithm to define the ground truth.

## Results

On the CausalTime benchmark (AQI, Traffic, Medical), CUTS+ and LCCM emerge as the top-performing TSCD algorithms. CUTS+ achieves the highest AUROC on AQI (0.893) and Medical (0.820), while LCCM achieves the highest AUPRC on AQI (0.926) and Medical (0.755). Critically, most of the 13 evaluated algorithms fail to exceed AUROC 0.9 on any dataset, and several (GC, SCGL, TCDF) score below 0.5 on some datasets, indicating inverted classification. The paper highlights that scores on CausalTime are significantly lower than those reported on synthetic datasets (e.g., VAR, Lorenz-96), where some algorithms approach AUROC 1.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUTS+ dominates on AUROC for AQI (0.893) and Medical (0.820), outperforming the next-best LCCM (0.857, 0.801) and eSRU (0.823, 0.756) by 3.6 and 6.9 points respectively on AQI. LCCM, however, achieves the best AUPRC on AQI (0.926) and Medical (0.755), surpassing CUTS+ (0.798, 0.548) by a wide margin, suggesting LCCM produces more precise positive predictions. eSRU is the strongest runner-up on Medical AUPRC (0.735), while SVAR is the most consistent mid-tier performer across all six metric-dataset combinations. Constraint-based methods (PCMCI, N.NTS) and Granger-causality baselines (GC, SCGL, TCDF) perform poorly, with GC and SCGL scoring below 0.5 AUROC on AQI and Traffic, indicating inverted classification. The paper's central finding is that all 13 algorithms score substantially lower on CausalTime than on synthetic benchmarks (VAR, Lorenz-96), where some achieve near-perfect AUROC, underscoring the gap between synthetic and realistic evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the TSCD algorithmic literature (Granger causality, PCMCI, CCM, neural Granger methods like CUTS/CUTS+) and the benchmarking line of work (CauseMe, Lawrence et al. 2021, causalAssembly, CausalBench), but departs from all of them by addressing the core tradeoff: existing benchmarks sacrifice either realism (synthetic/quasi-real data), ground-truth availability (real data), or domain generalizability. The key technical departure is the model-splitting strategy—decomposing a fully-connected NAR fit into causal, residual, and noise terms—which converts a fitted prediction model into a generative model with a known 2N×2N causal graph, avoiding the circularity of using a TSCD algorithm to build the benchmark it is meant to test.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a significant gap between synthetic and realistic performance: algorithms scoring near-perfectly on VAR/Lorenz-96 drop to AUROC < 0.9 on CausalTime, suggesting the field's progress may be partly an artifact of over-simplified test data. Limitations include the NAR assumption (no multiplicative or confounded interactions), stationarity, no instantaneous effects in the main evaluation, and short generated sequences (40 steps), which constrains the types of dynamics that can be captured. Open questions include extending to full SCM models, multi-scale causal effects, longer generation horizons, and whether the DeepSHAP-extracted HCG in the medical setting truly approximates the underlying causal structure.
