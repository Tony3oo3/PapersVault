## Problem

While unlabeled data is known to improve semi-supervised and adversarially robust classification, prior theoretical results either assume the unlabeled data is in-domain or only provide asymptotic guarantees. This paper addresses the missing non-asymptotic trade-off between labeled (m) and unlabeled (n) sample sizes when the unlabeled data originates from a slightly out-of-domain distribution (Wasserstein shift α), for both robust and non-robust loss functions.

## Input / Output

The method takes m labeled feature-label pairs from an in-domain distribution P0 and n unlabeled feature vectors from a shifted distribution P1 (with W_c(P0,X, P1,X) = α), where n is typically much larger than m. In the real-world histopathology experiments, 224×224 or 96×96 RGB images are first encoded into 1024-dimensional embeddings via a pre-trained ResNet50, which then serve as the feature vectors for the RSS training objective. The output is a trained classifier (linear in theory, a 4-layer MLP in practice) that maps features to binary class labels, accompanied by explicit generalization bounds quantifying when the method outperforms ERM.

## Method

The proposed RSS (Robust Self-Supervised) training combines Distributionally Robust Optimization with self-training in a single minimax objective. The loss has two terms: a Wasserstein-DRO robust loss on the labeled data (adversary budget controlled by γ) and a self-supervised term on the unlabeled data where the current model assigns pseudo-labels and a second robust loss with budget γ' is applied. The key mechanism is that the unlabeled term constrains the feasible classifier set by forcing the decision boundary away from high-density regions, effectively reducing the Rademacher complexity of the hypothesis class. The inner maximization over adversarial perturbations is strictly concave for sufficiently large γ and γ', making the overall problem solvable in polynomial time (or via SGD for non-convex losses like neural networks). The theoretical analysis shows that when n ≥ Ω(m²/d) and α ≤ O(d/m), the generalization bound strictly improves over ERM, and when α = 0 the excess risk scales as (d³ log(1/δ) / (m²(2n+m)))^{1/8}.

## Results

On simulated Gaussian mixture data (d=200), RSS training with only 10 labeled samples and 10,000 unlabeled samples achieves 82% accuracy, compared to 59% for ERM with the same 10 labeled samples alone (a 23-point improvement). On the NCT-CRC-HE-100K histopathology dataset, RSS with 48 labeled and 2,000 unlabeled samples reaches 82% accuracy versus 65% for ERM (17-point gain), and with 1,040 labeled and 20,000 unlabeled samples reaches 91% versus 83% for ERM. Even under distribution shift (labeled from NCT-CRC-HE-100K, unlabeled from PatchCamelyon), RSS with 50 labeled and 3,000 unlabeled samples achieves 87% accuracy versus 82% for ERM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is standard Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) using only labeled data, which serves as the supervised-learning reference. RSS consistently outperforms ERM across all settings: on simulated data, the gap ranges from 4 points (40 labeled) to 23 points (10 labeled) with 10,000 in-domain unlabeled samples, and from 3 to 22 points with out-of-domain unlabeled samples (α=0.5‖μ₀‖₂). On the NCT-CRC-HE-100K histopathology dataset, RSS gains 17 points over ERM at 48 labeled samples (82% vs. 65%) and 8 points at 1,040 labeled samples (91% vs. 83%) with same-distribution unlabeled data. Under cross-distribution transfer (NCT-CRC-HE-100K labeled + PatchCamelyon unlabeled), RSS still improves over ERM by 3–5 points, confirming robustness to moderate distribution shift. The paper does not compare against other named semi-supervised or DRO baselines; the comparison is strictly RSS vs. ERM, with the theoretical contribution being the explicit non-asymptotic conditions (n ≥ Ω(m²/d), α ≤ O(d/m)) under which RSS provably beats ERM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper fuses two previously independent paradigms—Wasserstein-based Distributionally Robust Optimization (following Blanchet et al., 2019 and Sinha et al., 2017) and self-training via pseudo-labels (following Grandvalet & Bengio, 2004 and Najafi et al., 2019)—into a unified 'Robust Self-Supervised' (RSS) training objective. It extends the line of work by Carmon et al. (2019), Alayrac et al. (2019), and Zhai et al. (2019), which showed that in-domain unlabeled data closes the robustness sample-complexity gap for two-component GMMs, by explicitly permitting the unlabeled samples to come from a slightly shifted distribution (bounded Wasserstein distance α). The closest prior result is Deng et al. (2021), who also considered out-of-domain unlabeled data for adversarial robustness, but only for well-separated isotropic Gaussians; this paper removes the separation requirement and extends to non-isotropic covariance matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first non-asymptotic tradeoff between labeled (m) and unlabeled (n) sample sizes for GMM classification under distribution shift, showing that n ≥ Ω(m²/d) suffices to beat ERM when α ≤ O(d/m). This gives practitioners a concrete, if restrictive, criterion for when out-of-domain unlabeled data is worth collecting. Key open questions include relaxing the distribution-shift condition (currently α must shrink with m), deriving matching lower bounds or impossibility results, and extending the analysis beyond linear classifiers and GMMs to general hypothesis classes and arbitrary distributions. The histopathology experiments (NCT-CRC-HE-100K, PatchCamelyon) suggest practical relevance in medical imaging where labeled data is scarce and unlabeled scans from different scanners or institutions are abundant.
