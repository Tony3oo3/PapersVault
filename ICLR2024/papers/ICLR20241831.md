## Problem

Translating human motions to robots with significantly different morphologies (e.g., bipedal humans to quadrupedal robots) is inherently ambiguous due to kinematic and dynamic mismatches. Existing unsupervised retargeting methods require pre-collected robot motion datasets as prior skill knowledge, which is prohibitively expensive to obtain for high-dimensional legged robots. CrossLoco asks whether cross-morphology human motion driven control can be learned entirely without any prior robot motion data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241831/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes an 188-dimensional augmented human motion feature vector (23-dim kinematic pose plus root and joint velocities at future frames 1, 2, 10, and 30) alongside a 47-dimensional robot state (17-dim pose, root/joint velocities, and previous action), and outputs target joint angle commands for a 12-joint quadrupedal robot (Unitree Aliengo). Human motions are drawn from the LaFAN1 dataset (50 clips, ~8 s average), and the robot operates in Isaac Gym with 1024 parallel environments.

## Method

CrossLoco formulates the problem as skill-conditioned RL where the human motion sequence serves as the skill condition, and introduces a cycle-consistency-based correspondence reward that maximizes mutual information between human and robot poses. Two neural mappers are co-trained: an R2H-Mapper (robot pose → human pose) and an H2R-Mapper (human pose → robot pose), whose reconstruction errors jointly form the correspondence reward r_cpd = exp(−‖p^h − μ^r2h(p^r)‖² − ‖p^r − μ^h2r(μ^r2h(p^r))‖²). The cycle-consistency term prevents degenerate one-directional mappings where multiple robot poses collapse to the same human pose. The control policy is trained with PPO using a composite reward (correspondence + root tracking + torque/joint-limit penalties), while the mappers are updated via supervised regression on the same trajectory buffer, creating an iterative co-training loop that simultaneously discovers robot skills and their correspondence to human motions.

## Results

On the LaFAN1 dataset (50 human motion clips) transferred to a Unitree Aliengo quadruped, CrossLoco achieves an Averaged Correspondence Reward (ACR) of 0.785, Diversity (DIV) of 2.853, and Root Tracking Reward (RTR) of 0.743, outperforming the DeepMimic baseline (ACR 0.558, DIV 2.231, RTR 0.579) on all three quantitative metrics. In a 15-subject user study, CrossLoco receives a 43% preference rate (PR) versus 16% for DeepMimic, while DeepMimic edges out slightly on Correct Rate (CR: 67.5% vs. 65.5%). The ablation variants (Task-Only and R2H-Only) confirm that both the correspondence reward and the full cycle-consistency term are necessary for strong performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is DeepMimic with a manually engineered motion retargeting function (fixed tripod gait matching human feet to robot feet). CrossLoco substantially outperforms DeepMimic on correspondence (ACR 0.785 vs. 0.558), diversity (DIV 2.853 vs. 2.231), and root tracking (RTR 0.743 vs. 0.579), and is strongly preferred by users (PR 43% vs. 16%). DeepMimic achieves a marginally higher Correct Rate (67.5% vs. 65.5%), which the authors attribute to users matching motions based on the most visually obvious frames despite poor overall quality. The two internal ablations further validate the design: removing the correspondence reward entirely (Task-Only) collapses ACR to 0.556 and PR to 14%, while removing the robot-pose cycle-consistency term (R2H-Only) reduces ACR to 0.683 and PR to 27%, confirming that the full bidirectional cycle-consistency is critical for producing distinguishable, high-quality motion transfers.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossLoco sits at the intersection of unsupervised skill discovery (Eysenbach et al., 2018; Peng et al., 2022) and cycle-consistency-based unpaired translation (Zhu et al., 2017), repurposing the mutual-information-maximization reward from skill discovery to establish human-robot pose correspondence. Prior motion retargeting methods either require paired human-robot datasets (Sermanet et al., 2018; Delhaisse et al., 2017) or pre-collected robot motion corpora for unsupervised learning (Li et al., 2023b; Smith et al., 2019), while earlier cycle-consistency approaches in robotics (Zhang et al., 2020; Shankar et al., 2022) align agents across dynamics without jointly learning a control policy. The key departure is formulating the correspondence as a cycle-consistency reward within a PPO training loop, eliminating the need for any prior robot motion data or manual retargeting function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the requirement for expensive robot motion datasets and manual retargeting engineering, CrossLoco lowers the barrier to cross-morphology teleoperation, which is critical for deploying legged robots in unstructured environments. The demonstrated applications (language-to-robot via MDM, interactive control via LMM) suggest the learned controller can serve as a general-purpose human-motion-to-robot interface. However, the work is limited to a single quadrupedal platform (Aliengo), struggles with sharp turns and large lateral steps, and has not been validated on real hardware. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to legged-manipulation robots with arms, scales to longer-horizon demonstrations, and transfers reliably from simulation to the physical world.
