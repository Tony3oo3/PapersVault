## Problem

Prior work on online adversarial DR-submodular optimization has been fragmented across monotone vs. non-monotone objectives, different constraint geometries, and feedback models, with most results limited to monotone functions and full-information gradient queries. This paper addresses the lack of a unified treatment by providing projection-free Frank-Wolfe algorithms that span all combinations of function class, constraint set type, and feedback model (full-information, semi-bandit, bandit; exact or stochastic), achieving first or improved sub-linear α-regret bounds in nearly every non-monotone case and state-of-the-art bounds among projection-free methods in most monotone cases.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a bounded convex constraint set K ⊆ [0,1]^d (downward-closed, origin-feasible, or general), a horizon T, and access to either a stochastic non-oblivious gradient oracle (bounded by B₁) or value oracle (bounded by B₀) for each round's DR-submodular function. It is parameterized by block size L, number of linear maximization oracles K, and smoothing radius δ, with the query budget per function set to T^β for β ∈ [0, 1/2]. The output is a sequence of actions y_t ∈ K whose expected α-regret is sub-linear in T, where α is 1−1/e (monotone, 0∈K), 1/e (non-monotone, d.c.), 1/2 (monotone, general), or (1−h)/4 (non-monotone, general) with h = min_{z∈K}‖z‖_∞.

## Method

The unified algorithm (Generalized Meta-Frank-Wolfe) combines four key ideas: (i) an offline bound (Lemma 8) that decomposes the α-approximation gap into a smoothness term plus a linear combination of oracle-adv(∇F(x^(k)), x^(k)) inner products; (ii) meta-actions, where K parallel instances of an online linear optimization oracle (e.g., Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader) mimic the K iterations of an offline Frank-Wolfe step, with each oracle's 'adversary' set to the corresponding oracle-adv gradient after the function is revealed; (iii) random permutations of rounds within blocks of length L to reduce the per-function query count to K/L = T^β at the cost of a regret factor; and (iv) a spherical smoothing trick for value oracles that yields a one-point gradient estimator over a shrunk constraint set. A critical technical novelty is that stochastic (gradient or value) feedback is handled without momentum or other variance-reduction techniques, preserving the same regret order as the deterministic case. The (semi-)bandit variant (Algorithm 2) allocates K exploration steps per block (querying at intermediate Frank-Wolfe points) and L−K exploitation steps (replaying the final action), yielding O(T^{3/4}) and O(T^{5/6}) regret for gradient and value oracles respectively.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is a unified set of α-regret bounds (expressed as log_T exponents) across 8 problem configurations. For non-monotone DR-submodular functions over downward-closed sets with full-information stochastic gradient feedback, the proposed algorithm achieves a 1/2 exponent (i.e., Õ(T^{1/2}) regret) with T^β queries for β∈[0,1/2], improving over prior bounds of 1−β/3 (Thang & Srivastav, 2021; Zhang et al., 2023) and 4/5 (Zhang et al., 2023). For monotone functions over 0∈K with full-information stochastic gradient, the bound is 1/2 (vs. 1−β/3 for Chen et al., 2018a and 3/4 for Zhang et al., 2022). In the (semi-)bandit settings, the paper provides the first sub-linear α-regret bounds: 3/4 for semi-bandit and 5/6 for bandit feedback. Empirically, GMFW(β=1/2) achieves the lowest cumulative regret among all tested methods while using significantly fewer gradient queries and less computation than Meta(β) baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Meta-Frank-Wolfe (Chen et al., 2018b), the proposed algorithm improves the full-information deterministic gradient bound from 1−β to 1/2 for monotone 0∈K, and from 1−β/3 to 1/2 for stochastic gradients, while also extending to semi-bandit and bandit settings not covered by the prior work. Compared to Mono-Frank-Wolfe (Zhang et al., 2019), the paper improves the single-query stochastic gradient bound from 4/5 to 1/2 and provides the first semi-bandit (3/4) and stochastic bandit (5/6) results. Against the projection-based method of Zhang et al. (2022) (bound 3/4), the proposed projection-free algorithm achieves 1/2 for monotone 0∈K full-information stochastic gradient, a strict improvement without requiring expensive projections. For non-monotone functions over downward-closed sets, the paper improves upon Thang & Srivastav (2021) and Zhang et al. (2023) from 1−β/3 to 1/2 for full-information gradient feedback, and provides the first semi-bandit (3/4) and stochastic bandit (5/6) bounds. For non-monotone functions over general convex sets, the bound improves from 1−β (Mualem & Feldman, 2023) to 2/3−β/3. The only case where the paper does not strictly improve is monotone 0∈K bandit deterministic feedback, where it matches Niazadeh et al. (2023) at 5/6. Empirically, GMFW(β=1/2) outperforms ODC (Thang & Srivastav, 2021), Mono, and Meta(β) baselines in cumulative regret while using fewer queries and less computation per round.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends three key algorithmic ideas from prior work: meta-actions (Chen et al., 2018b) for converting offline Frank-Wolfe into online algorithms, random permutations (Zhang et al., 2019) for reducing query complexity, and the smoothing trick with shrunk constraint sets (Pedramfar et al., 2023) for handling value oracles. The critical departure is a refined analysis showing that stochastic gradient/value feedback can be handled without variance reduction techniques (momentum), which prior works (Chen et al., 2018a; Zhang et al., 2019; 2022) relied upon to control oracle noise. The paper also introduces the first treatment of noisy semi-bandit and bandit feedback for adversarial DR-submodular optimization, a setting entirely absent from prior literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in projection-free algorithms being the only viable option for large-scale problems (matrix completion, routing, graph problems) where solving projections is computationally prohibitive, yet prior projection-free methods had weaker regret guarantees or covered fewer settings. This unified framework fills multiple gaps: first sub-linear regret for monotone functions over general convex sets with bandit feedback, and improved bounds for nearly all non-monotone cases. Open questions remain about whether the (1-h)/4 approximation ratio for non-monotone functions over general convex sets is truly optimal in the online setting, and whether the query complexity trade-offs can be further tightened. The empirical validation is limited to small-scale quadratic problems, leaving open whether the theoretical advantages translate to large-scale real-world applications.
