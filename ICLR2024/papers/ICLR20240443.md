## Problem

Transformer models exhibit biased predictions across sensitive groups, but existing fairness interventions either require access to sensitive attributes (raising privacy and legal concerns) or rely on auxiliary networks and strong optimization constraints that are parameter-inefficient or unstable. This paper asks whether the internal structure of the attention mechanism itself can be leveraged to reduce bias without any demographic information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240443/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB face images (resized to 64×64 and split into 16 patches for ViT) or text sentences (tokenized for BERT) and produces a single class label (e.g., blond hair, gender, hate speech, entailment). Crucially, no sensitive attribute labels are required at any stage of training or inference. For pre-trained models, the method appends a single fairness-aware encoder layer on top of a frozen backbone (DINO ViT-16 or BERT), so only that one layer's parameters are updated, reducing GPU memory from ~11.8 GB to ~3.2 GB on CelebA.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage debiasing strategy applied within the attention mechanism of the last encoder layer. First, each query and key vector is normalized (zero-mean, unit-variance per dimension) and then passed through an element-wise absolute value operation before computing attention weights; Theorem 1 proves this bounds the cross-group attention weight disparity by a constant depending only on base rates and embedding dimension, whereas the vanilla inner product is unbounded. Second, the value vectors corresponding to the top-t debiased attention weights are concatenated, projected through a small nonlinear head, and trained with a supervised contrastive loss that pulls same-class representations together while pushing different-class ones apart. Running mean and standard deviation (momentum p=0.1) are maintained during training to normalize test-time queries and keys. For pre-trained models, only the appended fairness-aware encoder is trained, making the approach GPU-memory efficient while preserving backbone utility.

## Results

On CelebA (a=male, y=blond hair), the proposed method achieves DP 16.34, EOp 38.01, EOd 20.43, and 94.06% accuracy with 172.18 Wh energy consumption, outperforming ERM (DP 16.92, EOp 42.82, EOd 22.90) on all three fairness metrics while maintaining comparable accuracy. On UTK (a=race, y=gender), the method achieves the best fairness across all metrics: DP 9.72, EOp 8.46, EOd 7.76, with 83.11% accuracy. In NLP (HateXplain with BERT Base), the method achieves EOp 4.74 and EOd 3.40, outperforming ERM (EOp 8.55, EOd 6.62) while retaining 77.89% accuracy. Energy consumption is substantially lower than JTT (172 Wh vs. 936 Wh on CelebA) and LfF (172 Wh vs. 611 Wh).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CelebA, the proposed method achieves the best Demographic Parity (16.34) among all methods, outperforming ERM (16.92), DRO (16.88), KD (17.44), JTT (18.04), and LfF (19.95), while maintaining 94.06% accuracy comparable to ERM (94.26%). Against JTT, the method trades a slightly worse EOp (38.01 vs. 36.17) for substantially higher accuracy (94.06% vs. 92.75%) and 5.4× lower energy consumption (172 Wh vs. 936 Wh). Against LfF, the method sacrifices some EOp (38.01 vs. 31.79) and EOd (20.43 vs. 19.26) but recovers 6.09% accuracy (94.06% vs. 87.97%) and uses 3.5× less energy. On UTK, the method achieves the best fairness on all three metrics (DP 9.72, EOp 8.46, EOd 7.76), narrowly beating KD (DP 9.89, EOp 8.69, EOd 7.97) while improving accuracy by 1.78%. In NLP (HateXplain, BERT Base), the method outperforms ERM on EOp (4.74 vs. 8.55) and EOd (3.40 vs. 6.62) with comparable accuracy (77.89% vs. 77.74%), and uses less energy than JTT (34 Wh vs. 59.75 Wh).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: fairness without demographics (DRO, ARL, JTT, KD, LfF) and fairness-aware transformers (TADeT, DSA). It departs from prior transformer debiasing methods (Sudhakar et al., Qiang et al.) by eliminating the need for sensitive attributes, and from prior fairness-without-demographics methods by avoiding auxiliary networks, two-stage training, or strong worst-case constraints. The core novelty is a theoretically grounded (Theorem 1) intervention directly on the attention mechanism's Q, K, V components—normalizing and taking absolute values of Q and K to bound attention-weight disparity, combined with supervised contrastive alignment (Khosla et al., 2020) on top-attention value vectors—rather than constraining the overall representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that the method requires no sensitive labels (addressing privacy and legal concerns), consumes substantially less energy than multi-stage baselines like JTT (e.g., ~172 Wh vs. ~936 Wh on CelebA), and can be applied to pre-trained models by training only a single appended encoder layer, drastically reducing GPU memory. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is restriction to binary sensitive attributes, and the theoretical bound in Theorem 1 degrades significantly under highly imbalanced base rates (as seen in MultiNLI where λ₁=7.1%). Open questions include extending the approach to multi-class sensitive attributes, relaxing the Gaussian/independence assumptions in the theory, and whether the last-encoder-only strategy generalizes to deeper or more heterogeneous pre-trained architectures.
