## Problem

Rehearsal-free class-incremental learning suffers from inter-task confusion: because classes from different tasks are never trained together, the classifier assigns high scores to out-of-task samples, degrading accuracy. Additionally, existing prompt-based methods require a pool of task-specific prompts and a query mechanism to compose the right prompt at inference, incurring extra computation and parameters without clear necessity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241210/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images as input and outputs a class label from the full set of classes seen across all tasks (e.g., 200 classes for ImageNet-R split into 10 tasks of 20 classes each). At inference, no task identifier is provided; the model must discriminate among all classes simultaneously. The classifier head is decomposed per task, with each task-specific head producing scores only for its own classes, and the final prediction is the argmax over the concatenated scores.

## Method

OVOR combines two components: (1) OnePrompt, which replaces the prompt pool and query mechanism with a single learnable prompt inserted via prefix-tuning into a frozen ViT-B encoder, halving inference FLOPs and reducing trainable parameters to ~0.3% of the model; and (2) Virtual Outlier Regularization (VOR), which generates synthetic outliers from the current task's feature vectors using NPOS (boundary-sample selection + Gaussian noise + KNN distance filtering), then applies a Huber-based hinge loss that pushes the classifier to assign high energy to in-distribution samples (threshold τ_Current) and low energy to outliers (threshold τ_Outlier). Per-task training proceeds in three steps: standard cross-entropy training of prompt and head, freezing the prompt to compute feature vectors and generate outliers, then updating only the classifier head with the combined CE + outlier regularization loss. This tightens decision boundaries so each task's head produces high confidence only for its own class distribution, reducing inter-task confusion without requiring stored data or separate outlier detectors.

## Results

On the 10-task ImageNet-R benchmark, OVOR achieves 75.61% average accuracy (A_T) and 5.77% average forgetting (F_T), outperforming CODA-P (74.87%) and L2 (76.06% with far more parameters). On CUB-200, OVOR reaches 78.12% A_T, a 5.4-point improvement over CODA-P (72.74%). OVOR uses only 0.26M learnable parameters and 17.60G FLOPs at inference, compared to CODA-P's 3.99M parameters and 35.17G FLOPs, yielding a ~2× inference speedup and 13.3× fewer trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVOR outperforms all prompt-based baselines (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-P) on ImageNet-R across all task counts, with the largest margin over CODA-P on CUB-200 (+5.4 pts) and ImageNet-R 20-task (+1.4 pts). Against the full-model fine-tuning baseline L2 (76.06% on INR-10), OVOR-Deep (76.11%) matches it while updating only ~0.3% of parameters versus L2's full attention layers. On CIFAR-100, the prototype-classifier-based A-Prompt (87.87%) and ADAM (85.75%) still lead OVOR (86.68%), and on CUB-200 and ImageNet-A, ADAM (85.67% and 53.98%) also surpasses OVOR. However, OVOR achieves the best F_T among prompt-based methods on ImageNet-R (5.77 vs. CODA-P's 6.73), and its efficiency advantage is substantial: 13.3× fewer learnable parameters and 2× lower FLOPs than CODA-P. The paper notes that prototype-based methods tend to dominate on smaller or more fine-grained datasets (CUB-200, ImageNet-A), while prompt-based methods with VOR are competitive or superior on ImageNet-R.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of prompt-based rehearsal-free CIL (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-P) and energy-based OOD detection (Liu et al., 2020; NPOS from Tao et al., 2023). It repurposes NPOS—originally designed for standalone OOD detection—as a regularization signal for classifier heads in CIL, and adopts the hinge-loss formulation from Liu et al. (2020) to enforce confidence separation between in-distribution and virtual outlier samples. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant prompt-pool paradigm by showing that a single shared prompt (OnePrompt) suffices, eliminating the need for query-based prompt composition mechanisms that prior methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-P) rely on to isolate task knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: OnePrompt halves inference FLOPs and reduces learnable parameters by up to 13× compared to CODA-P while matching or exceeding accuracy, making rehearsal-free CIL more accessible in resource-constrained settings. The VOR technique is model-agnostic and can be plugged into any prompt-based CIL method, offering a simple accuracy boost without architectural changes. Key limitations include applicability only to non-overlapping input spaces (ruling out domain-incremental learning), scalability concerns from storing feature vectors for outlier generation, and evaluation restricted to image classification. Open questions include whether VOR can be integrated with prototype-based classifiers and whether the approach extends to textual, tabular, or time-series modalities.
