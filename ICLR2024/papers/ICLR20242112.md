## Problem

Existing sentence embedding models and retrieval methods are designed and evaluated on single-sentence queries, yet complex or intricate semantics in natural language are routinely expressed collectively through multiple example sentences. There is no framework that represents such compound semantics as structured units and supports well-defined operations (selection, deselection, and their compositions) for querying information against them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242112/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple sets of sentences (each set encoding one semantic concept), a target corpus of sentences to search, and a pre-trained sentence embedding model. It outputs an ordered set over the corpus where each sentence is ranked by a composite score: the sum of its average cosine similarity to all 'include' sets minus the sum of its average cosine similarity to all 'exclude' sets. Unlike standard top-k retrieval, the output preserves a total order reflecting degree of semantic match, and the operations are commutative within a series (A ∩ B \ C = A \ C ∩ B) but not between the corpus and the query sets.

## Method

SetCSE introduces an inter-set contrastive learning objective that treats sentences from different semantic sets as negative pairs, fine-tuning a sentence embedding model to sharpen discrimination between distinct semantics (Eq. 1, with temperature τ). At query time, the semantic similarity between a sentence and a set is defined as the mean cosine similarity between the sentence's embedding and all embeddings in the set (Eq. 2). SetCSE intersection ranks corpus sentences by ascending similarity to a target set, SetCSE difference ranks by ascending similarity to an exclusion set, and a general series A ∩ B₁ ∩ … ∩ B_N \ C₁ \ … \ C_M ranks by the difference of summed similarities (Eq. 3). The framework is model-agnostic, working with any sentence embedding model (BERT, RoBERTa, SimCSE, DiffCSE, MCSE, Contriever, SGPT), and the inter-set fine-tuning step is the only additional training required beyond the base model.

## Results

On SetCSE intersection (the primary evaluation), the framework achieves average improvements of 39% in accuracy and 37% in F1 over the best existing model across five datasets (AG News-T, AG News-D, FPB, Banking77, FMTOD). The best SetCSE configuration (SimCSE-RoBERTa) reaches 79.56% accuracy on AG News-T, 89.97% on AG News-D, 85.48% on FPB, 98.33% on Banking77, and 99.44% on FMTOD. For SetCSE difference, average improvements are 14% in accuracy and 21% in F1. Serial operations (intersection + difference) yield an additional 33% average improvement over existing models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SetCSE outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and both intersection and difference operations. Against the strongest existing sentence-embedding baselines (SimCSE-RoBERTa, MCSE-RoBERTa, DiffCSE-RoBERTa), SetCSE gains 25–33 percentage points in accuracy on AG News-T (e.g., 79.56% vs. 49.68% for SimCSE-RoBERTa) and 19–30 points on FPB (85.48% vs. 66.11%). Traditional IR methods (BM25, TFIDF, DPR) are consistently the weakest, with BM25 scoring only 24.90% on AG News-T versus 79.56% for SetCSE SimCSE-RoBERTa. The decoder-only SGPT model is the weakest neural baseline and benefits least from SetCSE fine-tuning (36.64% on AG News-T), a gap the authors attribute to decoder architecture limitations. On FMTOD, where semantics are already highly distinctive, improvements are smallest (12%) but still consistent. For the difference operation, SetCSE MCSE-RoBERTa achieves 99.63% accuracy on FMTOD versus 97.10% for the best existing model, and 89.86% on AG News-T versus 77.64% for SimCSE-BERT.

## Relation to Prior Work

SetCSE extends the contrastive learning paradigm for sentence embeddings (SimCSE, DiffCSE, MCSE) by shifting the unit of contrast from individual sentence pairs to entire sets of sentences, introducing an inter-set contrastive loss that pushes embeddings of sentences from different semantic sets apart. It draws conceptual inspiration from set theory in formal semantics (WordNet synsets, predicate logic representations) but repurposes set-theoretic operations (intersection, difference) at the sentence level for structured information retrieval, rather than at the word level as in prior embedding-improvement work. The framework is positioned as a querying layer on top of existing dense retrieval models (BERT, RoBERTa, Contriever, SGPT), fine-tuning them with the new objective before applying set operations for complex queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine gap: expressing and retrieving compound semantics (e.g., 'using technology to solve social issues while neglecting negative impact') that cannot be captured by any single-sentence query. The ~30–40% average improvement in semantic discrimination across five diverse datasets is substantial, and the practical applications (ESG analysis, active learning, topic discovery) demonstrate real utility. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger embedding models, how it performs on standard IR benchmarks (MS MARCO, BEIR), and whether the asymmetric (non-commutative) nature of the operations limits compositional expressiveness. The paper also notes that decoder-only models (SGPT) benefit less from the fine-tuning, suggesting the inter-set objective may interact differently with architectural choices.
