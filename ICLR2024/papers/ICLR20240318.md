## Problem

Existing graph matching methods assume all graphs belong to the same category, but real-world data often mixes different graph types (e.g., people, bicycles, cars in traffic frames), requiring simultaneous matching and clustering. The two prior MGMC methods—DPMC and GANN—suffer from convergence instability, rigid optimization structures, and poor robustness to outliers, with no prior work providing a theoretically convergent algorithm for this joint problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240318/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N keypoint graphs (10 keypoints per image, constructed via Delaunay triangulation from 256×256 cropped RGB images) that may span multiple object categories and contain random outlier keypoints. It outputs pairwise partial permutation matrices encoding node correspondences between all graph pairs, along with a cluster assignment partitioning the graphs into Nc groups. The formulation uses Lawler's QAP with inequality constraints to naturally accommodate outliers without requiring exact one-to-one matching.

## Method

M3C is a learning-free solver that converts the joint MGMC objective into a Minorize-Maximization framework, guaranteeing monotonic convergence of the objective function. The key innovation is a relaxed cluster indicator that replaces hard cluster boundaries with a global or local rank constraint (selecting the top-ratio graph pairs by affinity score), enabling flexible exploration across cluster boundaries while preserving discrete optimization. The maximization step performs multi-graph matching on a partially-connected supergraph whose adjacency equals the relaxed indicator. UM3C extends M3C into an unsupervised pipeline by learning edge-wise affinity matrices (via VGG16 + Spline CNN features) with a cross-entropy loss that decouples affinity quality from solver effects, and uses the relaxed indicator to select high-confidence pseudo-label pairs for training, improving robustness over GANN's all-pairs loss accumulation.

## Results

On Willow Object Class (3 clusters × 8 graphs), UM3C achieves 95.5% matching accuracy (MA) and 98.3% clustering accuracy (CA) without outliers, and retains 81.5% MA and 98.1% CA with 4 outliers. The learning-free solver M3C achieves 88.4% MA and 91.1% CA without outliers, outperforming DPMC (87.2% MA) and MGM-Floyd (87.9% MA) while running 2.3–11.5× faster than peer MGM methods. On Pascal VOC (3×8), UM3C with pretrained features achieves 94.9% CA and 96.3% CP, surpassing the supervised COMMON baseline (93.2% CA, 94.7% CP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct peer GANN (unsupervised, NeurIPS 2020), UM3C achieves a dramatic 30% MA improvement under 4 outliers (81.5% vs. 46.1%) and a 5.9% gain without outliers (95.5% vs. 89.6%), while running 8.4× faster (3.6s vs. 30.2s). The learning-free M3C outperforms DPMC (AAAI 2020) on MA without outliers (88.4% vs. 87.2%) and by a wider margin under 4 outliers on CA (64.6% vs. 60.0%), and beats MGM-Floyd (TPAMI 2021) on MA (88.4% vs. 87.9%) while being 2.9× faster. Remarkably, UM3C surpasses the supervised baselines BBGM (ECCV 2020) and NGMv2 (TPAMI 2020) on both MA and CA across all outlier settings (e.g., 95.5% vs. 93.9% MA vs. BBGM; 98.3% vs. 70.4% CA vs. BBGM without outliers). On Pascal VOC, the +UM3C extension (applying UM3C's solver to pretrained feature extractors of COMMON and GCAN) improves CA by 1.8% and 1.5% respectively over the original supervised models. M3C-hard (the hard-clustering ablation) underperforms M3C by 4.6% MA, confirming the benefit of the relaxed cluster indicator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Minorize-Maximization (MM) optimization framework (Hunter & Lange, 2004) and supergraph-based multi-graph matching (Jiang et al., 2021) to address Mixture Graph Matching and Clustering (MGMC), a task previously tackled by DPMC (tree-structured, no convergence guarantee) and GANN (graduated assignment in continuous space, slow convergence, hard clustering). The key departure is converting MGMC into a discrete MM framework with a relaxed cluster indicator that replaces rigid hard-clustering constraints with global/local rank-based pair selection, yielding the first theoretically convergent MGMC solver. The unsupervised extension UM3C further departs from GANN by introducing edge-wise affinity learning via Lawler's QAP (rather than Koopmans-Beckmann's QAP) and a pseudo-label selection mechanism guided by the relaxed indicator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convergence guarantee and relaxed indicator address fundamental limitations of prior MGMC methods, making the joint matching-clustering problem more tractable and flexible. The finding that an unsupervised method (UM3C) outperforms supervised baselines (BBGM, NGMv2) on MGMC is practically significant, reducing the need for expensive ground-truth correspondence labels. However, experiments are limited to small-scale keypoint graphs (10 nodes) on two datasets, and scalability to larger or more complex graph structures remains untested. The authors suggest future applications to protein docking and combinatorial problem generation, but these are aspirational rather than demonstrated.
