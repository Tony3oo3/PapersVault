## Problem

Existing text-attributed graph (TAG) pipelines either rely on shallow text features (BoW, skip-gram) that miss semantic nuance, or require intricate iterative LM+GNN training with small LMs (BERT, DeBERTa) that lack the reasoning and general knowledge of modern LLMs. Because LLMs like GPT-3.5 are accessed via API (text-in, text-out) and do not expose logits or support fine-tuning, prior LM-based TAG methods are architecturally incompatible with them, leaving the potential of LLMs for TAG representation learning largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

For each graph node, the method takes the raw text attributes (title and abstract) and the graph adjacency matrix. The text is wrapped in a task-specific prompt to query an LLM once per node, yielding a ranked prediction list and a free-text explanation. These LLM outputs, together with the original text, are encoded by a fine-tuned smaller LM (DeBERTa) into three frozen feature vectors per node (h_orig, h_expl, h_pred), which are then consumed by any standard GNN. The final output is a per-node class label, produced by averaging predictions from three independently trained GNNs (one per feature source).

## Method

TAPE introduces a three-stage, LMaaS-compatible pipeline: (1) a custom prompt instructs an LLM (GPT-3.5 or Llama2) to produce a top-k ranked prediction list and a textual explanation for each node; (2) a smaller LM (DeBERTa) is fine-tuned as an 'LLM-to-LM interpreter' to map both the original text and the LLM's explanation into fixed-length embeddings, while the ranked predictions are one-hot encoded and linearly projected into a compact vector; (3) three GNNs (e.g., RevGAT) are trained independently on each feature source with the LM features kept frozen, and their predictions are averaged. The key insight is treating the LLM's natural-language explanation as an informative intermediate feature that distills the LLM's reasoning and general knowledge into a form a compact LM can exploit, eliminating the need for iterative LM-GNN co-training. A supporting theorem shows that under fidelity (explanations capture the LLM's hidden state) and non-redundancy (the LLM holds information absent from the smaller LM) conditions, the explanations strictly reduce the conditional entropy of the target, guaranteeing a performance gain. This decoupled design yields a 2.88× training speedup over the iterative GLEM baseline while achieving higher accuracy on ogbn-arxiv.

## Results

TAPE achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy across all five datasets and four GNN architectures. On ogbn-arxiv with RevGAT, it reaches 77.50% test accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA GLEM (76.57%) while training 2.88× faster (≈3.2 h vs. 9.18 h). On the newly introduced tape-arxiv23 dataset (papers post-dating GPT-3.5's knowledge cutoff), TAPE with RevGAT achieves 84.23% accuracy, well above the LLM's zero-shot score of 73.56%. Gains over shallow-embedding baselines are particularly large: +9.42% on ogbn-arxiv (RevGAT) and +18.24% on ogbn-products (RevGAT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GIANT (Chien et al., 2021), which fine-tunes DeBERTa via self-supervised neighborhood prediction to produce node features, TAPE consistently wins across all five datasets and all four GNNs. The gap is most pronounced on Cora (e.g., 92.80% vs. 83.53% with RevGAT, a +9.27 pp margin) and ogbn-products (82.34% vs. 71.89% with RevGAT, +10.45 pp), while on ogbn-arxiv the advantage is +1.60 pp (77.50% vs. 75.90% with RevGAT). Against GLEM (Zhao et al., 2022), the prior SOTA that iteratively co-trains an LM and GNN, TAPE achieves higher test accuracy on ogbn-arxiv (77.50% vs. 76.57% with RevGAT) while reducing total training time from 9.18 h to approximately 3.2 h (a 2.88× speedup), thanks to its decoupled, one-shot feature-extraction pipeline that avoids GLEM's multi-stage iterative loop. On Cora and PubMed, TAPE also outperforms GLEM (e.g., 92.80% vs. 88.56% on Cora with RevGAT; 96.04% vs. 94.71% on PubMed with RevGAT). Notably, TAPE also surpasses the raw LLM zero-shot accuracy (GPT-3.5) on every dataset, with the largest margin on tape-arxiv23 (84.23% vs. 73.56%), confirming that the explanation-as-feature mechanism adds value beyond the LLM's own predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for TAG representation learning: shallow-embedding pipelines (BoW/skip-gram → GNN) and iterative LM-GNN co-training (GIANT, GLEM), which require fine-tuning LMs and accessing their latent representations. TAPE instead introduces an LMaaS-compatible, decoupled pipeline where a frozen LLM (GPT-3.5/Llama2) generates zero-shot predictions and textual explanations, and a smaller fine-tuned LM (DeBERTa) acts as an 'interpreter' to convert those explanations into fixed-length node features for any downstream GNN. The conceptual lineage draws on the 'pre-train, prompt, predict' paradigm for LLMs and the cascaded LM→GNN architecture, but the key departure is treating LLM-generated natural-language explanations as a transferable feature medium between the LLM and the GNN, rather than relying on the LLM's internal embeddings or logits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAPE provides the first practical, API-compatible recipe for leveraging LLMs in TAG tasks without requiring fine-tuning or logit access, making it accessible to researchers without large-scale compute. The 2.88× training speedup over GLEM at higher accuracy, combined with the modular frozen-feature design, lowers the barrier to incorporating LLM knowledge into graph pipelines. Open questions include automating prompt design (currently hand-crafted per dataset), scaling to graphs with millions of nodes where per-node LLM queries become prohibitive, and extending the explanation-as-feature paradigm to dynamic or evolving TAGs. The newly released tape-arxiv23 dataset also addresses the widespread label-leakage concern in LLM benchmarks.
