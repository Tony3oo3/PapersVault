## Problem

High-resolution diffusion models face two coupled obstacles: the noise schedule is resolution-dependent (the same noise level yields a higher frequency-domain SNR at higher resolutions, causing training-inference mismatch), and existing cascaded pipelines waste computation by regenerating low-frequency content from pure noise at each super-resolution stage while suffering from distribution mismatch between ground-truth and generated low-resolution conditions.

## Input / Output

The first stage takes a class label (or nothing for unconditional) and generates a 64×64 image via standard EDM diffusion. The second (super-resolution) stage takes the 64×64 output, nearest-upsamples it to 256×256, and continues diffusion from that upsampled state rather than from pure noise, producing a 256×256 (or 512×512, 1024×1024) image. The pipeline is fully Markovian: each stage's output feeds directly into the next stage's initial state, with no cross-attention conditioning on the low-resolution image.

## Method

RDM unifies the cascaded pipeline by introducing two key mechanisms in the super-resolution stage: (1) block noise, a correlated Gaussian noise whose covariance decays with Manhattan distance over a kernel of size s (matching the upsampling factor), which reproduces the frequency spectrum of independent Gaussian noise at the lower resolution; and (2) patch-wise blurring diffusion, which applies heat dissipation independently on each 4×4 patch (matching the upsampling grid) so that the forward process gradually smooths the blocky upsampled image into a natural high-resolution one. The training objective follows EDM but corrupts the target with a weighted mixture of the blurred image, block noise, and independent Gaussian noise. A stochastic sampler adapted from DDIM/EDM handles the non-isotropic blurring in the frequency domain via DCT, with a tunable η controlling the injected randomness. This eliminates the need for low-resolution conditioning, conditioning augmentation, and the full diffusion trajectory from pure noise, reducing both training and sampling steps.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, RDM achieves FID 5.27 and sFID 4.39 without guidance, and FID 1.99 (class-balanced FID 1.87) with classifier-free guidance (CFG=3.50), using only 125 NFE (vs. 250 for DiT/MDT). On unconditional CelebA-HQ 256×256, RDM achieves FID 3.15, Precision 0.77, and Recall 0.55, surpassing StyleSwin (FID 3.25) with roughly 1/18th the training cost. RDM achieves the best sFID among all compared methods on ImageNet 256×256 and requires only ~1/3 the training iterations of DiT-XL/2 and MDT-XL/2 to reach competitive FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Without guidance, RDM (FID 5.27) substantially outperforms ADM (10.94), LDM-4 (10.56), DiT-XL/2 (9.62), and MDT-XL/2 (6.23) on ImageNet 256×256, while also achieving the best sFID (4.39 vs. 5.23 for MDT-XL/2 and 6.85 for DiT-XL/2). With classifier-free guidance, RDM reaches FID 1.99 (1.87 with class-balancing), which is competitive with MDT-XL/2's dynamic CFG (1.79) and clearly beats DiT-XL/2 (2.27), LDM-4-G (3.60), and ADM-U,G (3.94), while using only ~70% of MDT-XL/2's training iterations and half the NFE. On CelebA-HQ 256×256, RDM (FID 3.15) edges out StyleSwin (3.25) with dramatically fewer training images (50M vs. 820M) and also achieves the best Precision (0.77) and Recall (0.55). RDM also outperforms CDM (FID 4.88) without requiring low-resolution conditioning or conditioning augmentation. At higher resolutions (512×512 and 1024×1024 on CelebA-HQ), RDM achieves FID 3.47 and 3.85 respectively, beating WaveDiff (6.40) and StyleSwin (4.43) with far less training cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDM repositions the cascaded diffusion pipeline (Ho et al., 2022) by replacing the low-resolution conditioning mechanism with a 'relay' that continues the diffusion process from the upsampled previous-stage output. This is enabled by two key adaptations: patch-wise blurring diffusion (extending Hoogeboom & Salimans, 2022 from full-image to 4×4-patch heat dissipation) and a novel block noise formulation that serves as the frequency-domain equivalent of low-resolution Gaussian noise at high resolution. The training framework follows EDM (Karras et al., 2022), and the stochastic sampler is derived by adapting DDIM (Song et al., 2020a) to the blurring diffusion forward process. Conceptually, the paper departs from CDM's paradigm of 'condition on low-res, denoise from pure noise' toward 'continue denoising from the relayed state,' eliminating the distribution mismatch problem that necessitated conditioning augmentation tricks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RDM offers a practical efficiency gain for high-resolution generation: fewer training iterations (~70% of MDT-XL/2), fewer sampling NFE (125 vs. 250), and no cross-attention overhead for low-resolution conditioning, making it attractive for text-to-image pipelines where inference cost is critical. The frequency-domain analysis (DCT-based SNR comparison) provides a clearer theoretical explanation for why noise schedules must be resolution-dependent, though the authors acknowledge that deriving an optimal schedule from this perspective did not yet yield practical improvements. Open questions include whether the relay mechanism generalizes beyond 4× upsampling ratios, whether it composes well with latent-space diffusion (LDM), and whether the Markovian artifact-correction property can be exploited for iterative refinement in production systems.
