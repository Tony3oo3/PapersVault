## Problem

Existing protein inverse folding methods, including the competitive PiFold baseline, ignore predictive confidence, fail to cover the vast protein sequence space, and do not incorporate common protein knowledge learned by large pretrained models. As a result, recovery plateaus around 52% because the model becomes trapped in a local optimum on the training distribution. The paper asks whether multimodal pretrained knowledge (structural and sequential) can be adaptively injected to iteratively refine low-confidence residues and push recovery beyond this ceiling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein 3D structure as input (residue Cα coordinates x ∈ R^{n,3}) and produces an amino acid sequence over 20 residue types plus a [mask] token. During refinement, the model samples virtual MSA sequences from its own predicted probability distribution (temperature T=0.05), masks residues with confidence below 0.99, and feeds these sequences into frozen pretrained encoders (ESM2-650M for sequence embeddings, ESM-IF or GearNet for structure-conditioned embeddings). The final output is a refined sequence with per-residue predictive probabilities, evaluated by recovery rate and perplexity on CATH, TS50, TS500, PDB, and TS45 benchmarks.

## Method

KW-Design builds on PiFold as an initial design model and stacks L confidence-aware knowledge-tuning modules, each consisting of a virtual MSA sampler, frozen pretrained knowledge extractors, a confidence-gated multimodal fusion layer, and a 10-layer PiGNN refinement module. The key mechanism is a confidence-aware gated attention that adaptively weights structural (ESM-IF/GearNet) and sequential (ESM2-650M) embeddings based on per-residue predictive confidence, so that low-confidence residues receive stronger correction from pretrained knowledge. A confidence-aware updating rule (Eq. 11) selectively blends pre- and post-refinement embeddings using the difference in confidence vectors, focusing refinement on difficult residues. To handle the large frozen pretrained models efficiently, the paper introduces a memory-retrieval mechanism that caches intermediate embeddings from early-stopped modules, eliminating redundant forward passes and saving over 50% of training time. Training follows a module-wise strategy where each refinement module is optimized sequentially with all prior modules frozen, which also mitigates GNN oversmoothing.

## Results

On CATH4.2, KW-Design achieves 60.77% recovery and 3.46 perplexity (all-chain), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art PiFold (51.66% recovery, 4.55 perplexity) by 9.11% in recovery and 23.95% in perplexity. On TS50 and TS500, KW-Design reaches 62.79% and 69.19% recovery respectively, surpassing PiFold by 4.07% and 8.77%. KW-Design is the first method to exceed 60% recovery on all four major benchmarks (CATH4.2, CATH4.3, TS50, TS500) and also leads on the multi-chain PDB dataset with 66% full-length recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KW-Design outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark. Against the closest competitor PiFold, it gains 9.11% recovery on CATH4.2 (60.77% vs 51.66%), 4.07% on TS50 (62.79% vs 58.72%), and 8.77% on TS500 (69.19% vs 60.42%). Against ProteinMPNN, the improvement is 14.81% on CATH4.2 and 11.11% on TS500. On CATH4.3, KW-Design (60.38%) surpasses ESM-IF trained on AF2DB (51.6%) by 8.78%, despite not using AF2DB for training. On the multi-chain PDB dataset, KW-Design leads PiFold by 8 percentage points in full-length recovery (66% vs 58%) and by 10 points in confidence (0.71 vs 0.61). The paper also reports a 5.12% advantage over the concurrent LMDesign method on CATH4.2, attributing the gap to multimodal knowledge fusion and the memory-retrieval mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

KW-Design builds directly on PiFold (the authors' own prior work) as its initial design model, extending the PiGNN graph-based protein design paradigm with iterative knowledge-tuning modules that fuse multimodal pretrained embeddings (ESM2-650M for sequence, ESM-IF/GearNet for structure) via confidence-aware gated attention. The virtual MSA and recycling mechanisms are conceptually borrowed from AlphaFold's iterative refinement, repurposed here to generate diverse sequence prompts for pretrained language models. The key departure from prior protein design methods (SPIN, GVP, ProteinMPNN, ESM-IF) is the explicit use of predictive confidence as a routing signal to selectively refine low-quality residues, combined with a module-wise training strategy and memory-retrieval mechanism for computational efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KW-Design establishes a new SOTA across all major protein design benchmarks (CATH4.2/4.3, TS50, TS500, PDB) and is the first method to exceed 60% recovery on all of them, with a 9.11% improvement over PiFold on CATH. The finding that combining ESM and ESM-IF knowledge yields a non-linear (super-additive) improvement over either alone suggests that sequential and structural pretrained features capture complementary information, a principle that could generalize to other multimodal refinement tasks. However, the method has not been validated through wet-lab experiments, and the reliance on frozen pretrained models (ESM2-650M) introduces significant inference overhead that limits practical deployment. Open questions include whether the confidence-aware refinement strategy transfers to other inverse problems (e.g., ligand binding site design) and whether the memory-retrieval mechanism generalizes beyond the specific module-wise training setup.
