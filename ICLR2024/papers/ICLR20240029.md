## Problem

Current large-scale TTS systems quantize speech into discrete tokens and generate them autoregressively, which suffers from error propagation (word skipping, repeating, collapse), unstable prosody, and a fundamental dilemma: low-bitrate single-token quantization loses fine-grained acoustic detail, while multi-layer residual quantization multiplies sequence length and makes language modeling harder. Additionally, these systems lack strong zero-shot generalization to unseen speakers and cannot extend beyond speaking to other styles like singing.

## Input / Output

The system takes a phoneme sequence (derived from text via G2P conversion) and a short speech prompt (typically 3 seconds of reference audio from the target speaker) as input. The prompt is encoded by the trained codec encoder into latent vectors rather than being used as raw waveform. The output is a 16KHz speech waveform reconstructed by the codec decoder from the diffusion-generated latent vectors, operating at 12.5ms per frame (hop size 200). The same architecture can produce singing voices when trained on mixed speech and singing data, using ground-truth pitch and duration at inference time.

## Method

NaturalSpeech 2 replaces discrete token generation with a latent diffusion model operating on continuous vectors from a SoundStream-based neural audio codec (16 residual VQ layers, 1024 codebook size, 256-dim embeddings), where the sum of residual vectors serves as the continuous target. A WaveNet-based diffusion model (40 dilated CNN blocks) predicts the clean latent z₀ conditioned on a prior vector c produced by a 6-layer Transformer phoneme encoder, a 30-layer conv duration predictor, and a 30-layer conv pitch predictor. For zero-shot in-context learning, a 6-layer Transformer prompt encoder processes the speech prompt, and its hidden sequence is injected via Q-K-V cross-attention in the duration/pitch predictors and via a two-stage attention mechanism (32 learned query tokens → FiLM conditioning) in the diffusion model to avoid leaking excessive detail. A novel cross-entropy loss (L_ce-rvq) regularizes the diffusion model by computing softmax over L2 distances to each residual quantizer's codebook, leveraging the discrete structure for training stability. The full system (435M parameters) is trained on 44K hours of MLS audiobook data with a linear noise schedule and Euler ODE solver at 150 diffusion steps.

## Results

On LibriSpeech, NaturalSpeech 2 achieves a CMOS of 0.00 (comparable to ground truth at +0.04), SMOS of 4.06±0.11, and WER of 2.01 (vs. 1.94 for ground truth). On VCTK, it achieves CMOS 0.00 (vs. −0.21 for ground truth), SMOS 3.62±0.11, and WER 6.72 (vs. 9.49 for ground truth). In the intelligibility test on 50 hard sentences, NaturalSpeech 2 has 0% error rate, matching other NAR models and far outperforming AR models like VALL-E (50% error rate) and FoundationTTS (32%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NaturalSpeech 2 outperforms all baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest AR baseline VALL-E, it gains 0.29/0.31 CMOS on LibriSpeech/VCTK, 0.14/0.12 SMOS, and reduces WER from 5.72→2.01 (LibriSpeech) and 14.68→6.72 (VCTK). Against FoundationTTS, the CMOS gap is 0.32/0.39 and WER drops from 4.63→2.01 and 12.55→6.72. Against FastSpeech 2 (adapted with cross-attention and latent target), NaturalSpeech 2 gains 0.53/0.64 CMOS and 0.61/0.40 SMOS. Against YourTTS, the advantage is 0.65/0.58 CMOS and 0.75/0.23 SMOS. In the intelligibility test, NaturalSpeech 2 (0% error) matches FastSpeech and NaturalSpeech but far exceeds VALL-E (50%), FoundationTTS (32%), Transformer TTS (34%), and Tacotron (24%). In latency, NaturalSpeech 2 at 150 diffusion steps (RTF 0.366) is 12.35× faster than VALL-E (RTF 4.52) while achieving 0.29 CMOS higher quality, though 33.3× slower than FastSpeech 2 (RTF 0.011).

## Relation to Prior Work

NaturalSpeech 2 departs from the dominant paradigm of discrete-token autoregressive TTS (VALL-E, AudioLM, FoundationTTS) by replacing discrete tokens with continuous latent vectors from a SoundStream-style neural audio codec and replacing the autoregressive language model with a non-autoregressive latent diffusion model built on WaveNet. The diffusion formulation follows the score-based SDE/ODE framework of Song et al. (2020), but is applied to codec latents rather than mel-spectrograms or raw waveforms. The speech prompting mechanism for in-context learning in both the duration/pitch predictor and the diffusion model is a novel architectural contribution that extends the NLP prompting concept to speech synthesis, and the CE-RVQ loss provides a regularization bridge between the continuous generation objective and the discrete codec structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper demonstrates that continuous latent diffusion can substantially outperform discrete-token autoregressive models on zero-shot TTS in naturalness, prosody similarity, and robustness (zero word-skip/repeat errors on 50 hard sentences), suggesting a paradigm shift for large-scale TTS. The ability to perform zero-shot singing synthesis from a speech-only prompt (without any singing reference) is a genuinely new capability that opens the door to unified speaking/singing models. Key open questions include inference efficiency (150 diffusion steps at RTF 0.366), the limited 30-hour singing dataset constraining singing quality, and whether the audiobook-biased MLS training data generalizes to casual real-world speech.
