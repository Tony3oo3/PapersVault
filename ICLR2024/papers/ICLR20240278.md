## Problem

Classifying dynamical regimes (e.g., fixed point vs. limit cycle) across diverse systems requires a detector that is selective for topological structure yet invariant to geometric nuisances, yet no existing data-driven method simultaneously achieves this selectivity-invariance trade-off, generalizes to unseen systems without retraining, and remains robust to noise. Prior approaches either rely on known governing equations, use time-series features that degrade under noise, or lack an explicit mechanism for enforcing topological invariance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 2D vector field sampled on a 64×64 grid, immediately converted to a single-channel angular representation (arctan of the y/x component) that discards magnitude information carrying little signal about cyclic behavior. For single-cell applications, high-dimensional RNA velocities are projected to 2D UMAP and interpolated onto the same grid. The output is a pair of logits (point attractor vs. periodic attractor) whose confidence near the bifurcation boundary can be inverted to recover the boundary location in parameter space, and whose averaged predictions across re-runs yield a continuous cell-cycle score for biological data.

## Method

The core mechanism is topological data augmentation: a single prototypical supercritical Hopf oscillator is warped by random diffeomorphisms generated from the random initialization of rational-quadratic spline flows (Durkan et al., 2019), producing many geometrically distinct but topologically equivalent vector fields that all share the same label. These augmented samples are fed to a convolutional network adapted from the SAGAN discriminator, with self-attention layers injected into the last two convolutional blocks to focus on the few dynamical keypoints (fixed point or limit cycle) that carry class-relevant information. A final MLP maps the extracted features to binary cross-entropy logits, and because the classifier must correctly label radically different-looking but topologically equivalent samples, it is forced to learn the true topological invariants of the Hopf bifurcation rather than geometric shortcuts. The angular representation, diffeomorphic augmentation, and attention mechanism each contribute independently to generalization, as confirmed by ablation studies.

## Results

On a diverse synthetic benchmark of 8 dynamical systems with Gaussian noise (σ=0.1), the proposed convolutional attention method achieves 87% average classification accuracy for distinguishing fixed-point vs. limit-cycle regimes, outperforming all baselines. The method generalizes from a single prototypical simple oscillator to complex systems including the Selkov glycolysis oscillator (65%) and Belousov-Zhabotinsky chemical oscillations (82%). On real single-cell pancreas data, the model achieves 94% accuracy in predicting proliferative (cyclic) vs. differentiating (fixed-point) dynamics, substantially exceeding all baselines (best baseline: 54%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three primary baselines by a wide margin on average accuracy under noise (σ=0.1): 87% vs. 60% for Critical Points (Helman & Hesselink), 72% for Lyapunov exponent (Zhang et al.), and 60% for Phase2vec (Ricci et al.). Critical Points achieves near-perfect accuracy on noiseless data but collapses to chance-level (50–56%) with even mild noise, making it unreliable for real-world applications. Lyapunov is more robust than Critical Points but still degrades substantially (e.g., 89%→70% on Supercritical Hopf with σ=0.1) and fails to generalize to non-centered systems. Phase2vec, the most recent deep-learning baseline, generalizes poorly beyond the simple oscillator and Supercritical Hopf, scoring at or near chance (48–50%) on all other systems. The Autoencoder baseline (not in the primary compares_to list but included in the main table) is the closest competitor at 80% average, but the proposed method still wins on 7 of 8 individual systems, with the largest gap on Selkov (65% vs. 49%) and Liénard Polynomial (86% vs. 63%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the authors' own prior work Phase2vec (Ricci et al., 2022) for dynamical systems embedding, but departs from it by explicitly targeting the selectivity-invariance trade-off: prior data-driven methods (Lyapunov exponents, persistent homology, compressed sensing) either require known governing equations, are time-series-based, or are sensitive to noise, and none explicitly enforces invariance to geometric deformations while remaining selective for topological structure. The key methodological novelty is the use of diffeomorphic data augmentation via rational-quadratic splines (borrowed from normalizing flow literature, Durkan et al., 2019) to generate consistently-labeled warped vector fields, combined with a SAGAN-style convolutional attention backbone and angular (magnitude-free) representation. This positions the work at the intersection of physics-informed ML, topological data analysis, and diffeomorphic augmentation strategies previously applied in computer vision (Vallez et al., 2022) and conjugate mapping (Bramburger et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a model trained exclusively on one simple oscillator can classify dynamical regimes across radically different physical and biological systems (Selkov glycolysis, BZ chemical oscillations, van der Pol circuits) and recover bifurcation boundaries in an equation-free manner, with 94% accuracy on real single-cell pancreas data without using any gene-specific prior knowledge. This opens a path toward universal, data-driven bifurcation detection in high-throughput biological and engineering settings where governing equations are unknown. Key limitations include confinement to 2D dynamics and the supercritical Hopf bifurcation specifically; extending to higher dimensions, other bifurcation types, and unsupervised or combinatorial invariant-set annotations are identified as important open problems. The Selkov upper-boundary failure (damping-induced slow spirals mimicking limit cycles) highlights a fundamental ambiguity that no purely topological method can resolve without additional information.
