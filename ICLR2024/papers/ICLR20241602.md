## Problem

Controlling reconfigurable soft robots that dynamically change their morphology during their lifetime poses three unresolved challenges: there is no formalization of such control as a reinforcement learning problem, the continuous 2D muscle-field action space is too high-dimensional for standard RL exploration to produce meaningful morphology changes, and no benchmark exists for evaluating fine-grained reconfigurable soft robot control. Existing robot co-design methods produce static morphologies and cannot handle robots that must reshape multiple times within a single task.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a 64×64×3 rasterized 2D image (occupancy and x/y velocity channels) of the robot and its immediate environment; in the fine stage this is concatenated with the upsampled coarse action to form a 64×64×5 input. The output is a discrete 2D action grid (8×8×2 coarse or 16×16×2 fine) that is bicubic-interpolated to a 64×64×2 continuous muscle field and then distributed to individual MPM particles via a grid-to-particle scheme, where each 2D vector encodes the stress magnitude and direction applied at that spatial location.

## Method

The simulation backbone is the Material Point Method augmented with a von Mises yield criterion for elasto-plastic behavior and a modified Cauchy stress term (F_p Σ_p^c F_p^T) that injects a 2D actuation signal per particle, modeling a continuous muscle field. The policy is a fully-convolutional network (Nature CNN-style encoder-decoder) that maps the 2D state image to a 2D action grid, sharing parameters across spatial locations to reduce the effective search space. The key algorithmic contribution is the Coarse-to-Fine Policy (CFP) curriculum: a low-resolution (8×8×2) policy is first trained to produce meaningful large-scale morphology changes, then frozen while a higher-resolution (16×16×2) residual policy is trained on top, blending its output with the coarse action through a learned sigmoid gating mask that selectively overrides coarse predictions where finer control is needed. Training uses SAC with a 256-batch replay buffer, and the entire pipeline runs on GPU-accelerated Taichi MPM at ~70 FPS for simulation.

## Results

CFP consistently outperforms all baselines across all 8 DittoGym environments in both sample efficiency and final episode reward. Higher action resolution (16×16×2) almost always yields higher achievable reward compared to medium (8×8×2) and coarse (4×4×2) resolutions across all environments, confirming the benchmark requires fine-grained morphology changes. Only CFP succeeds in the long-horizon multi-stage tasks (OBSTACLE, CATCH, SLOT) that require multiple morphology changes during execution. The simulation runs at ~70 FPS on an RTX 4090 GPU, with training at ~20 FPS per agent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFP is compared against four baselines: GNN Policy (Modular Policy, Pathak et al. 2019), Transformer Policy, Coarse Policy (8×8×2 ablation), and Fine-from-Scratch Policy (16×16×2 ablation). CFP wins on all 8 environments in both sample efficiency and converged reward. The GNN and Transformer modular policies (tested at both 16 and 64 segments) fall short because they require explicit topological structure that reconfigurable soft robots lack; the Transformer baseline in CATCH reaches comparable peak reward to CFP but is highly oscillated and unstable. The Fine-from-Scratch ablation fails to produce meaningful morphology changes due to the exploration problem in high-dimensional action spaces (random actions tug individual particles without macroscopic effect). The Coarse Policy ablation explores more efficiently than fine-from-scratch but is ultimately limited by its 8×8×2 granularity and cannot achieve the fine detail needed for tasks like SHAPE MATCH. CFP is the only method that succeeds in all long-horizon multi-stage environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robot co-design literature (RoboGrammar, SoftZoo, EvoGym), which jointly optimizes a static morphology and a control policy, to the fundamentally different problem of *reconfigurable* robots whose morphology changes dynamically during a single episode. The simulation backbone is the Material Point Method (Jiang et al., 2016), modified with a von Mises yield criterion for elasto-plastic behavior and a novel Cauchy stress term that maps a continuous 2D muscle field to per-particle actuation, inspired by the magnetic slime robot of Sun et al. (2022). The coarse-to-fine policy (CFP) departs from both standard RL (which fails in the high-dimensional action space) and modular/GNN-based control (which assumes fixed topology), instead using a hierarchical fully-convolutional architecture with a gated residual refinement stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DittoGym is the first benchmark specifically targeting multi-stage morphological reconfiguration, filling a clear gap between static co-design benchmarks and the emerging hardware reality of reconfigurable soft robots. The demonstration that only a coarse-to-fine curriculum can solve long-horizon multi-stage morphology-change tasks provides a concrete algorithmic recipe for the high-dimensional action spaces inherent to continuum-robot control. Key limitations include the 2D-only simulation, modest success rates (e.g., 15.4% on SLOT), and the absence of any real-hardware validation; open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D, whether the muscle-field parameterization generalizes to other actuation modalities, and how to bridge the simulation-to-reality gap for physical reconfigurable robots.
