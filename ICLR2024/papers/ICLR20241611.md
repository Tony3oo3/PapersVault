## Problem

Existing retrieval-based exemplar selection for in-context learning relies solely on question-to-exemplar similarity, ignoring the intermediate reasoning steps (CoT) that carry the most discriminative logical information. Moreover, high-dimensional sentence embeddings exhibit anisotropy and encode redundant features, making it difficult to distinguish genuinely logically relevant exemplars from those sharing only spurious word co-occurrence patterns with the query.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241611/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question and a set of initial n-shot exemplars (obtained via BM25 or Complex-CoT) as input. The question and initial exemplars are first fed to an LLM to generate a CoT, which is then concatenated with the question to form the retrieval query for a trained BERT encoder. The output is a re-ranked set of n exemplars (from a larger candidate pool of M) that are concatenated with the question and passed to the LLM for final answer generation.

## Method

DQ-LoRe introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Dual Queries (DQ) leverages an LLM to generate a CoT for the input question, then uses the concatenated question+CoT as the retrieval query against a BERT encoder trained with contrastive learning (positives/negatives constructed via BM25 retrieval followed by LM-based entailment scoring). Second, Low-Rank Approximation Re-ranking (LoRe) applies PCA to project the M retrieved exemplar embeddings into a lower-dimensional space, filtering redundant anisotropic information and amplifying the geometric gap between logically relevant and spurious exemplars; re-ranking then uses inner product for math tasks or a Gaussian kernel for commonsense tasks to preserve entity-level detail. The ablation shows DQ is essential for LoRe to work—dimensionality reduction without CoT information actually degrades performance—confirming that the CoT carries the discriminative signal that PCA can then sharpen.

## Results

Under the i.i.d. setting with GPT-3.5-Turbo, DQ-LoRe achieves 80.7% on GSM8K, 59.8% on AQUA, 85.3% on SVAMP, 90.0% on SVAMP* (distribution shift), 75.4% on StrategyQA, and 82.7% on QASC, outperforming all baselines on five of six datasets. With GPT-4 on GSM8K, DQ-LoRe reaches 94.2% accuracy, surpassing the prior best retrieval-based method CEIL (92.5%) by 1.7 percentage points. Under distribution shift (trained on GSM8K, tested on SVAMP/MultiArith/SingleEq), DQ-LoRe achieves 84.0% on SVAMP and 98.5% on MultiArith with GPT-3.5-Turbo, consistently leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DQ-LoRe outperforms all five baselines (CoT, Complex-CoT, Auto-CoT, EPR, CEIL) on GSM8K, AQUA, SVAMP*, StrategyQA, and QASC with GPT-3.5-Turbo, with the largest margins on AQUA (+2.0% over EPR) and SVAMP* (+2.0% over EPR). The sole exception is SVAMP under i.i.d. conditions, where EPR (89.0%) surpasses DQ-LoRe (85.3%) due to EPR exploiting word co-occurrence patterns prevalent in that dataset; however, under the distribution-shift SVAMP* setting, DQ-LoRe reverses this to 90.0% vs. 88.0%. With GPT-4 on GSM8K, DQ-LoRe (94.2%) beats CEIL (92.5%) by 1.7% and EPR (91.3%) by 2.9%. Under distribution shift (Table 3), DQ-LoRe leads on all three test datasets (SVAMP, MultiArith, SingleEq) for both engines, with the most notable gap on SVAMP where it gains +5.5% over EPR with GPT-3.5-Turbo. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that the Dual Queries component is essential for LoRe to be effective: applying LoRe alone without CoT information actually degrades EPR and CEIL performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

DQ-LoRe extends the retrieval-based exemplar selection paradigm (EPR, CEIL) by introducing a two-stage 'dual query' mechanism: first eliciting a chain-of-thought from the LLM, then using both the question and the generated CoT to query a contrastively-trained BERT retriever. The key conceptual departure from prior retrieval methods is that they operate solely on question embeddings, whereas DQ-LoRe incorporates intermediate reasoning steps into the retrieval signal, motivated by the observation that CoT similarity better captures logical relevance than surface-level question similarity. The LoRe re-ranking component draws on the anisotropy literature in sentence embeddings (Devlin et al., 2018; Li et al., 2020; Su et al., 2021), applying PCA to project high-dimensional embeddings into a lower-rank subspace where 'good' and 'bad' exemplars become more separable. The method is explicitly orthogonal to existing retrieval approaches, as demonstrated by ablations showing it can be plugged into EPR or CEIL as a post-hoc re-ranking step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and persistent problem in ICL: retrieval-based exemplar selection is vulnerable to spurious correlations (e.g., word co-occurrence in math word problems), and the distribution shift experiments (training on GSM8K, testing on SVAMP/MultiArith/SingleEq) provide evidence that CoT-aware retrieval with PCA re-ranking is more robust than question-only retrieval. The finding that LoRe without the dual-query CoT signal actually degrades performance highlights that dimensionality reduction alone is insufficient—the CoT information is what makes the low-rank projection meaningful. Limitations include reliance on LLM API calls for the first query (adding ~0.7s per question), evaluation limited to math and commonsense reasoning, and modest absolute gains (1.7% on GPT-4). Open questions include whether a unified regularization (combining PCA and kernel methods) can handle both math and commonsense tasks, and whether the approach generalizes to longer reasoning chains or multi-modal settings.
