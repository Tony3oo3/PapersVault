## Problem

Published conditional image generation models are evaluated under highly inconsistent experimental conditions—different datasets, inference hyperparameters, and human evaluation protocols—making cross-model comparisons unreliable and progress tracking nearly impossible. No centralized, continuously maintained benchmark exists to standardize how these diverse models are served and assessed under a common protocol.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241823/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the seven tasks takes a specific combination of conditions (text prompt, source image, binary mask, 3–5 subject reference images, or low-level control signals such as depth maps and Canny edges) and produces a single RGB image. All models are run under fixed hyperparameters and a unified prompt format to eliminate per-instance tuning, and outputs are scored by three human raters on two three-valued axes (Semantic Consistency and Perceptual Quality, each in {0, 0.5, 1}), combined via geometric mean into an overall score.

## Method

ImagenHub standardizes evaluation along three axes: (1) a unified inference library that ports dispersed model codebases into a common format with fixed hyperparameters and prompt templates, preventing per-instance tuning; (2) seven curated evaluation datasets of 100–200 instances each, drawn from existing public benchmarks (DrawBench, MagicBrush, SuTI, DreamEditBench, etc.) plus newly collected instances; and (3) a human evaluation protocol using two three-valued metrics—Semantic Consistency, which takes the minimum per-condition consistency score so that failure on any single condition zeros the score, and Perceptual Quality, which assesses artifacts, object recognizability, and naturalness—combined as O = √(SC × PQ). The geometric mean was chosen over weighted sum after ablation showed that sum-based scoring could be gamed by models that simply return unedited inputs (SC=0, PQ=1). Raters follow explicit checklist guidelines, and inter-rater reliability is measured via both Fleiss' Kappa and Krippendorff's Alpha, achieving Krippendorff's α > 0.4 on 76% of the ~30 evaluated models.

## Results

Across ~30 models evaluated on 7 conditional image generation tasks, 74% of models achieve an overall human score (geometric mean of Semantic Consistency and Perceptual Quality) below 0.5. The best-performing tasks are Text-guided Image Generation (DALLE-3: 0.76, Midjourney: 0.73) and Subject-driven Image Generation (SuTI: 0.58, DreamBooth: 0.55), while Text-guided Image Editing and Multi-concept Image Composition show the weakest results, with most models scoring below 0.15. No automatic metric (LPIPS, CLIP, FID, DINO, DreamSim) achieves a Spearman's correlation above 0.2 with human ratings, except in Subject-driven Image Editing where CLIP-I reaches 0.38. The evaluation achieves Krippendorff's Alpha > 0.4 on 76% of models, confirming reliable inter-rater agreement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In Text-guided Image Generation, DALLE-3 (0.76) and Midjourney (0.73) clearly outperform open-source models, with Stable Diffusion XL and DeepFloydIF tied at 0.59; DALLE-3 shows particular strength on complex prompts (e.g., 'A panda making latte art') where other models misinterpret the scene. In Subject-driven Image Generation, SuTI (0.58) and DreamBooth (0.55) lead, while DreamBooth-Lora (0.09) and TextualInversion (0.21) struggle significantly with subject fidelity despite reasonable perceptual quality. For Text-guided Image Editing, MagicBrush (0.47) dominates, while older methods like SDEdit (0.04), Text2Live (0.02), and Pix2PixZero (0.01) fail catastrophically—Text2Live frequently returns the input unchanged, and Pix2PixZero's paper claims do not generalize beyond word-swapping examples. In Mask-guided Image Editing, SDXL-Inpainting (0.37) is the best but still far from satisfactory, with BlendedDiffusion (0.05) producing largely unrecognizable outputs. For Multi-concept Image Composition, CustomDiffusion (0.29) consistently outperforms DreamBooth (0.13) and TextualInversion (0.05), though it often fails on action and positional words. In Control-guided Image Generation, ControlNet and UniControl are essentially tied (both 0.23), with both suffering from scratches and low perceptual quality. Overall, 83% of published paper ranking claims are validated by this benchmark, with notable exceptions for Pix2PixZero and the ControlNet vs. UniControl comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

ImagenHub does not build on a single technical lineage but rather carves new ground as a unifying evaluation framework for the rapidly fragmenting landscape of conditional image generation. It draws on existing evaluation datasets (DrawBench, DiffusionDB, MagicBrush, SuTI, DreamEditBench) and automatic metrics (FID, LPIPS, CLIP score, DreamSim, DINO) but departs from prior work by imposing a single standardized protocol—fixed hyperparameters, unified prompt formats, and a common three-valued human rating rubric—across all seven task types simultaneously. Where prior papers each curated their own test sets and reported results under different conditions, ImagenHub treats cross-paper comparability as the central problem and addresses it through a shared inference library and trained human raters with explicit inter-rater reliability measurement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical gap between the field's self-reported progress and standardized performance: 74% of evaluated models score below 0.5 overall, and widely-used automatic metrics (LPIPS, FID, CLIP, DreamSim, DINO) show Spearman correlations below 0.2 with human ratings in most tasks, undermining their reliability as proxies for perceived quality. The main limitation is the dependence on human raters, which is expensive and hard to scale as new models appear; the authors explicitly flag the need for automatic evaluation methods that approximate human judgment. As a continuously updated leaderboard, ImagenHub is positioned to become the de facto reference for fair comparison in conditional image generation, and its finding that published claims are 83% consistent (but not fully) gives the community a concrete tool for auditing future work.
