## Problem

Masked Language Modeling pretraining introduces a [MASK] token that exists only during pretraining but is absent from downstream data. This paper identifies and formally proves that [MASK] token representations exclusively occupy a subspace of model dimensions throughout the encoder, causing real token representations to be necessarily rank-deficient and limiting the model's expressiveness when applied to inputs without [MASK].

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the encoder receives only real token embeddings plus position embeddings at unmasked positions (sequence length reduced by the masking rate, e.g., 15%), with position indices aligned to the original sequence and masked positions skipped. A shallow bidirectional decoder then takes the encoder's output representations concatenated with [MASK] token embeddings at masked positions to predict the original tokens via cross-entropy loss. During fine-tuning, only the encoder is used, producing full-dimensional contextualized token representations for downstream classification or extraction tasks.

## Method

MAE-LM adapts the Masked Autoencoder architecture to text by excluding [MASK] tokens entirely from the encoder input, so that real token representations can theoretically utilize the full model dimension d. A shallow (4-layer, 1/3 encoder depth) bidirectional decoder receives the encoder outputs plus [MASK] position embeddings and is trained with the standard MLM cross-entropy objective; this decoder is discarded after pretraining. The theoretical contribution proves via Lemma 2.1 that [MASK] representations must increase in rank through encoder layers (to approximate the high-rank prediction target), and via Theorem 2.2 that this forces real token representations into a rank-deficient subspace because self-attention would otherwise collapse them to rank-1. Empirically, MAE-LM closes the effective-rank gap between masked and unmasked inputs and consistently outperforms RoBERTa and other MLM variants on GLUE and SQuAD 2.0 across base, base++, and large++ pretraining settings.

## Results

Under the base++ setting (160GB corpus), MAE-LM achieves 87.8 GLUE average and 83.5 EM / 86.5 F1 on SQuAD 2.0, outperforming RoBERTa (86.4 GLUE, 80.5 EM) and UniLMv2 (87.1 GLUE, 83.3 EM). Under the base setting (16GB corpus), MAE-LM reaches 86.1 GLUE average and 81.1 EM / 84.1 F1 on SQuAD 2.0, surpassing RoBERTa (Ours) (85.2 GLUE, 78.3 EM) and BERT (83.1 GLUE, 73.7 EM). The gains are consistent across individual GLUE tasks, with the largest improvements on RTE (+3.6 over RoBERTa base, +6.5 over RoBERTa base++) and STS-B (+1.4 over RoBERTa base++).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAE-LM consistently outperforms all MLM-based baselines across both pretraining settings. Against RoBERTa (the primary MLM baseline), MAE-LM gains +0.9 GLUE average in the base setting (86.1 vs 85.2) and +1.4 in base++ (87.8 vs 86.4), with the largest per-task advantage on RTE (+3.6 base, +6.5 base++). On SQuAD 2.0, MAE-LM improves over RoBERTa by +2.8 EM in base (81.1 vs 78.3) and +3.0 EM in base++ (83.5 vs 80.5). Against UniLMv2 in base++, MAE-LM edges out on GLUE average (87.8 vs 87.1) and SQuAD EM (83.5 vs 83.3) while matching on F1 (86.5 vs 86.1). Compared to BERT in the base setting, MAE-LM achieves a substantial +3.0 GLUE average improvement (86.1 vs 83.1) and +7.4 EM on SQuAD (81.1 vs 73.7). The paper also shows that naively adding a decoder to standard MLM (enc. w. [MASK] + dec.) does not help (85.1 GLUE), confirming that the key design choice is excluding [MASK] from the encoder rather than the decoder itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MLM pretraining paradigm established by BERT and RoBERTa, and repurposes the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) architecture from vision (He et al., 2022) to address a specific limitation of MLM. It departs from prior attempts to handle the [MASK] discrepancy (ELECTRA, XLNet) which proposed alternative training objectives, instead providing a representation-space analysis that identifies the root cause as rank deficiency. The theoretical proof extends the attention rank-collapse analysis of Dong et al. (2021) by partitioning representations into real-token and [MASK]-token subspaces, showing that self-attention necessarily drives real-token representations toward rank-1 when [MASK] representations must increase in rank to satisfy the prediction objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal explanation for why the [MASK] token discrepancy in MLM limits downstream expressiveness, grounding a long-suspected issue in a provable rank-deficiency mechanism. The proposed fix (excluding [MASK] from the encoder) is architecturally simple and requires no new training objective, making it easy to adopt. Limitations include scope restricted to MLM-based encoders (not autoregressive LMs, though implications are discussed), and the practical gains, while consistent, are modest (~1 point on GLUE average). Open questions include whether the same representation deficiency arises in other MAE-based domains (images, video, graphs) and whether the theoretical analysis extends to multi-head attention with residual connections in a tighter form.
