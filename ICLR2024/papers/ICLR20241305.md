## Problem

Existing 3D generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: SDS-based approaches require hours of per-asset optimization and produce geometry artifacts like the Janus problem, while 3D diffusion models demand ground-truth 3D training data and often yield unclean latent spaces on diverse category-free datasets. The paper addresses the gap of achieving fast, realistic, and category-agnostic 3D generation in a single stage without per-asset optimization or direct 3D supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241305/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a single 256×256 RGB image (kept noise-free as one of four sparse views) or a text prompt (encoded via CLIP), and outputs a 64×64×3 triplane NeRF with 32 channels representing the 3D shape and appearance. During inference, four views uniformly surround the object at a fixed 50° FOV, and the final denoising step yields the triplane NeRF directly—no per-asset optimization is needed. Generation completes in ~30 seconds on a single A100 GPU using DDIM sampling.

## Method

DMV3D repurposes a large transformer-based 3D reconstruction model (built on LRM) as the denoiser within a multi-view image diffusion framework: given noisy multi-view images at diffusion timestep t, the transformer reconstructs a clean triplane NeRF and renders denoised images at both input and novel viewpoints. Two key design choices distinguish it from prior work: (1) Plucker-coordinate-based camera conditioning (concatenating pixel-aligned rays with image pixels before ViT tokenization) instead of adaLN-Zero, which avoids interference between camera and time conditioning; and (2) novel-view rendering supervision during training, which prevents the model from degenerating into pasting input images on view-aligned planes. The model is trained end-to-end with only L2 + LPIPS image reconstruction losses on ~730k Objaverse renderings and ~220k MVImgNet real captures, using a 44-layer transformer (12 encoder + 32 decoder attention layers) on 128 A100 GPUs for 100K steps. Text conditioning is injected via cross-attention layers with CLIP embeddings, and classifier-free guidance (scale 5) is applied at inference.

## Results

On single-image 3D reconstruction, DMV3D achieves state-of-the-art results on both ABO and GSO datasets, outperforming all five baselines across every metric. On ABO, the full model attains FID 27.88, CLIP 0.949, PSNR 24.15, LPIPS 0.127, and Chamfer Distance 0.046; on GSO it reaches FID 30.01, CLIP 0.928, PSNR 22.57, LPIPS 0.126, and CD 0.040. For text-to-3D generation, DMV3D achieves the best CLIP precision (R-Prec 39.72, AP 47.96 with ViT-B/32; R-Prec 55.14, AP 61.32 with ViT-L/14), surpassing both Point-E and Shap-E. The entire pipeline runs in ~30 seconds on a single A100 GPU via direct diffusion inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMV3D outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric for both ABO and GSO. The closest competitor on appearance metrics is Magic123 (per-asset SDS optimization), which achieves FID 34.93/34.06 and PSNR 18.47/18.68 on ABO/GSO, but DMV3D improves FID by ~7 points and PSNR by ~5.7 dB while also producing a 3D NeRF in ~30 s versus hours of optimization. On geometry (Chamfer Distance), DMV3D is dramatically better: 0.046 vs. 0.136 (Magic123) on ABO and 0.040 vs. 0.113 on GSO, indicating far more accurate 3D shape recovery. The two-stage 3D diffusion models Point-E and Shap-E produce the worst results (FID >79 on ABO), confirming the difficulty of denoising in 3D latent spaces. Zero-1-to-3 is competitive on appearance (FID 31.59 on ABO) but cannot output a 3D model and suffers from severe multi-view inconsistency, which cascades into the poor reconstruction quality of One-2-3-45 (FID 190.81 on ABO). Even the smaller ablation model (Ours-S) already beats Magic123 on PSNR and CD, demonstrating the robustness of the reconstruction-based denoiser design.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMV3D builds directly on the LRM (Large Reconstruction Model) architecture for single-image-to-3D, repurposing it as a probabilistic multi-view denoiser within a 2D diffusion framework. Conceptually, it extends RenderDiffusion's idea of using a 3D-aware denoiser from single-view to a four-view multi-view setting, while departing from SDS-based methods (DreamFusion, Magic123) that require hours of per-asset optimization and from 3D latent diffusion models (Point-E, Shap-E) that need ground-truth 3D training data. The key architectural departure is introducing Plucker-coordinate-based camera conditioning in place of the adaLN-Zero approach used in LRM, and adding novel-view rendering supervision to prevent degenerate solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying 3D reconstruction and generation in a single transformer trained only on 2D image data, DMV3D eliminates both the need for 3D training assets and per-asset optimization, enabling ~30-second category-agnostic 3D generation. The probabilistic multi-view formulation resolves the mode-averaging blur inherent in deterministic LRM, producing diverse and sharp reconstructions of unseen object parts. Open questions include scaling to higher resolutions (currently 256×256), handling more complex multi-object scenes, and whether the four-view design generalizes to arbitrary sparse-view counts. The paper also acknowledges that training on ~1M objects is far smaller than 2D diffusion datasets, limiting coverage of the full diversity of possible inputs.
