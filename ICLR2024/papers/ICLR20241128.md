## Problem

Imitation learning of Strong Branching for MILP solving requires collecting expert samples by solving many instances with the computationally expensive Full Strong Branching strategy (e.g., 84.79 hours for 100k samples on Capacitated Facility Location). This data collection bottleneck becomes increasingly impractical as MILP complexity scales, limiting the applicability of learned branching policies in data-scarce settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a MILP at a B&B node encoded as a bipartite graph with constraint nodes (5 features), variable nodes (13 features), and edge features (1 feature), plus an augmented counterpart generated by variable shifting. The augmented graph's features are derived analytically from the original: most features are invariant under the shift, while bias, sol_val, inc_val, and avg_inc_val are shifted by the shift vector s. The output is a masked-softmax probability distribution over candidate variables, from which the branching variable is selected.

## Method

CAMBranch generates Augmented MILPs (AMILPs) by applying a random integer/real shift vector s to all variables, and proves (Theorem 3.1) that MILPs and their AMILPs produce identical Strong Branching decisions at every B&B step, making AMILPs free labeled samples. The augmented bipartite graph features are derived in closed form from the original graph (Theorems 3.2–3.3 establish invariance of dual solutions and reduced costs). A GCNN with bipartite graph convolution processes both the original and augmented graphs, producing graph-level embeddings via max/mean pooling and MLP merging. Training combines three losses: cross-entropy imitation of Strong Branching, an infoNCE contrastive loss pulling MILP–AMILP embeddings together while pushing apart other batch members, and an auxiliary L2 consistency loss between the branching probability distributions of the MILP and AMILP. This allows CAMBranch to achieve competitive or superior solving performance using only 10% of the expert data required by the original GCNN baseline.

## Results

CAMBranch, trained with only 10% of the expert data used by GCNN, achieves the best solving time on 3 out of 4 problems at the Hard difficulty level. On Hard-level Capacitated Facility Location, CAMBranch solves in 470.83s versus GCNN's 680.78s (~210s faster), and on Hard-level Maximum Independent Set, CAMBranch achieves 496.86s versus GCNN's 1020.58s (~524s faster). In terms of B&B tree size, CAMBranch(10%) generates 10,828 nodes on Hard MIS versus 21,925 for GCNN and 17,034 for GCNN(10%). On Set Covering (Hard), GCNN retains a slight edge (1344.59s vs. 1427.02s), making it the one problem where CAMBranch does not lead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Full Strong Branching (FSB), CAMBranch is dramatically superior on all four problems, reducing solving time by orders of magnitude (e.g., 470.83s vs. 995.40s on Hard CFL; FSB times out on Hard Set Covering and MIS). Against Reliability Pseudocost Branching (RPB), CAMBranch wins on solving time and wins-count for 3 of 4 problems at Hard level, though RPB occasionally produces fewer B&B nodes (e.g., 178 vs. 428 on Hard CFL) at the cost of longer per-node computation. Against GCNN trained on 100% data, CAMBranch(10%) achieves the best Hard-level solving time on Combinatorial Auction (131.79s vs. 158.81s), Capacitated Facility Location (470.83s vs. 680.78s), and Maximum Independent Set (496.86s vs. 1020.58s), while GCNN retains a narrow edge on Set Covering (1344.59s vs. 1427.02s). Against GCNN trained on only 10% data (the fair data-matched comparison), CAMBranch(10%) consistently outperforms across all four problems and all difficulty levels, with the largest gaps on Hard instances (e.g., 496.86s vs. 695.96s on MIS; 470.83s vs. 672.88s on CFL). The paper also reports that CAMBranch(100%), when given the full dataset, surpasses even GCNN(100%) on Combinatorial Auction, achieving 109.96s on Hard vs. GCNN's 158.81s with 83/100 wins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Gasse et al. (2019), which introduced bipartite graph encodings of MILPs and GCNN-based branching policies trained via imitation learning of Full Strong Branching. The key departure is a data augmentation strategy—variable shifting to generate Augmented MILPs (AMILPs) that provably share identical branching decisions with their source MILPs—combined with an infoNCE contrastive loss between MILP and AMILP graph representations. Theoretically, the paper establishes (via Lemmas and Theorems 3.1–3.3) that LP relaxation solutions, dual solutions, and reduced costs are invariant under the shift, providing the formal justification for the augmentation. The contrastive learning component draws on the broader self-supervised representation learning literature (SimCLR, MoCo, etc.) but applies it to the specific structure of MILP bipartite graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for the MILP-with-ML community: CAMBranch reduces expert data collection time by ~90% (e.g., 84.79h → 8.76h for Capacitated Facility Location) while matching or exceeding GCNN trained on 100% of the data on most benchmarks. The discussion in Section E.2 raises an intriguing open question—whether the contrastive loss enables the model to learn branching strategies that genuinely surpass Strong Branching rather than merely imitating it—but this remains speculative without a controlled experiment isolating that effect. Limitations include evaluation on only four problem classes from a single prior paper, weaker results on Set Covering, and dependence on the specific GCNN architecture of Gasse et al. (2019). Future work could explore whether the augmentation generalizes to heterogeneous MILP collections or to other solver architectures.
