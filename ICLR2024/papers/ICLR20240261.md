## Problem

It is unclear why transformers learn to reason relationally over abstract symbols (generalizing to symbols never seen in training) while classical fully-connected networks provably cannot, and why transformers require surprisingly large amounts of data even for simple two-template reasoning tasks. This paper provides the first theoretical characterization of when transformers succeed or fail at relational reasoning with unseen symbols, and identifies the architectural bottleneck limiting their data efficiency.

## Input / Output

The input is a length-k string of abstract symbols drawn from an alphabet, generated by substituting wildcards in a template with variable names. In the regression setting the output is a real-valued label; in the next-token-prediction setting the output is a token that may itself be a symbol unseen during training. Critically, the test distribution uses a disjoint alphabet from training, so the model must infer the underlying template (relational structure) rather than memorize symbol-specific associations.

## Method

The paper formalizes relational reasoning as 'template tasks' and analyzes a one-layer transformer in the infinite-width, infinitesimal-step-size limit where SGD converges to kernel ridge regression with a transformer random-features kernel. The key insight is that the attention mechanism implicitly accesses the incidence matrix XX^T, which is permutation-invariant and encodes which positions share the same symbol, enabling the empirical kernel matrix to become block-structured by template. The authors prove that for any collection of disjoint templates, this kernel yields vanishing test error on unseen symbols given sufficient data diversity, while MLPs fail due to permutation equivariance. To improve data efficiency, they propose adding one trainable scalar per head: replacing W_K W_Q^T with W_K W_Q^T + aI (emphasizing the incidence matrix in attention) and W_V W_O^T with W_V W_O^T + bI (enabling an attention-modulated skip connection for copying unseen symbols), each adding only two parameters per head and empirically reducing data requirements by an order of magnitude.

## Results

On Wikitext language modeling, the proposed modification (adding 2 trainable identity-scaling parameters per attention head, 288 total to a 117M-parameter GPT-2) reduces perplexity from 64.00 to 60.46 on Wikitext2 and from 16.83 to 16.40 on Wikitext103. On synthetic relational-reasoning template tasks, the same modification improves data efficiency by approximately an order of magnitude, requiring far fewer training samples to achieve good out-of-distribution generalization on unseen symbols. Theoretically, the paper proves that wide transformers trained by gradient flow generalize to unseen symbols on any regression template task (Theorem 3.4), while classical MLPs provably fail to generalize regardless of data size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between standard transformer/MLP architectures and the proposed modified transformer on synthetic template tasks. Standard transformers eventually generalize to unseen symbols but require very large training sets; the modified transformer (with W_K W_Q^T + aI and/or W_V W_O^T + bI) achieves the same generalization with roughly 10× fewer samples. MLPs (fully-connected networks) provably fail to generalize on unseen symbols regardless of data size, due to permutation-equivariance constraints on their training dynamics. On the real-data Wikitext benchmark, the modified GPT-2 outperforms vanilla GPT-2 by 3.54 perplexity points on Wikitext2 and 0.43 on Wikitext103, despite adding only 288 parameters to a 117M-parameter model. The paper also shows that pretrained GPT-2 heads develop strong diagonal attention patterns resembling the proposed modification, suggesting the inductive bias is naturally learned during pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes relational reasoning with abstract symbols through a new 'template task' framework, building on a neuroscience line of work (Marcus 1998; Webb et al. 2020b; Kim et al. 2018) that studied same/different tasks and psychometric tests empirically. It departs from prior work by providing the first theoretical analysis of when transformer random features kernels learn nontrivial sequence functions, proving a separation between transformers (which generalize) and MLPs (which provably fail, supporting Marcus's classical criticism of connectionism). The proposed architectural modifications (adding a trainable identity scaling to W_K W_Q^T and W_V W_O^T) are conceptually related to prior proposals for relational inductive biases (Santoro et al. 2017; Webb et al. 2020a) but are minimal—only two parameters per head—and are motivated directly by the kernel analysis rather than heuristic design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretical explanation for why LLMs learn to reason with more data and identifies a concrete, minimal architectural fix (two trainable parameters per attention head) that improves data efficiency by an order of magnitude on relational tasks and improves GPT-2 perplexity on Wikitext. The inverse-scaling result for copying unseen symbols (larger transformers fail) is a notable negative finding with implications for scaling. Open questions include tight quantitative bounds on required data and width, extension to more complex reasoning (syllogisms, compositional reasoning), and whether the NTK-regime guarantees transfer to finite-width, feature-learning regimes. The observation that pretrained GPT-2 heads develop strong diagonals resembling the proposed modification suggests the fix may be partially captured by pretraining, raising questions about the relative roles of architecture vs. data in enabling reasoning.
