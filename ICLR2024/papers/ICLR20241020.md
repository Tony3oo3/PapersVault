## Problem

High-throughput drug screens are split across multiple experimental batches, introducing batch effects that systematically distort molecular representations and create spurious structure-phenotype associations. Existing batch correction methods operate as task-agnostic preprocessing steps, while conditional contrastive learning (CCL) restricts negative sampling to the same batch, leading to poor generalization when batch-drug coverage is sparse. The paper asks how to integrate bias removal directly into the contrastive learning objective while retaining a large, diverse pool of negative samples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes drug molecular structures (featurized via Mol2vec), screening readouts (L1000 gene expression vectors or CellProfiler image features from Cell Painting), and a categorical batch identifier. It outputs paired latent representations for both modalities that are debiased with respect to batch. Unlike CCL, which hard-constrains negatives to the same batch, InfoCORE uses the batch identifier only to estimate posterior batch distributions over the latent space, so the full batch of K negatives remains available for contrastive learning. The resulting representations support downstream retrieval, property prediction, and fairness evaluation without further modification.

## Method

InfoCORE derives a multi-sample variational lower bound on the conditional mutual information I(Z_d; Z_g | X_b) by decomposing the joint posterior p(z_g, x_b | z_d) into a batch-independent similarity term p(z_g | z_d) and a posterior batch distribution p(x_b | z_d, z_g). The resulting loss, L_InfoCORE = L_CLIP + L_CLF, reweights each negative sample in the symmetric InfoNCE objective by the estimated probability that it belongs to the anchor's batch, so negatives with similar batch distributions are emphasized more. The joint posterior batch distribution is approximated as a weighted arithmetic average (with tunable α) of the individual posteriors from two separate batch classifiers trained on each modality's latent representation. This design avoids the adversarial optimization of I(Z_d; X_b) required by single-sample bounds, and as training progresses and batch information is stripped from the latents, the reweighting automatically diminishes, gracefully recovering standard CLIP behavior.

## Results

On molecule-phenotype retrieval with gene expression (GE) data, InfoCORE achieves 14.03% top-1 accuracy in the batch-restricted library, outperforming CLIP (12.23%) by 15% and CCL (12.76%) by 10%, while also leading in the whole library (6.39% vs. 5.96% for CLIP and 1.93% for CCL). For molecular property prediction, InfoCORE pre-trained on GE achieves the best average AUC-ROC of 78.3% across seven MoleculeNet benchmarks, compared to 77.6% for both CLIP and CCL and 75.9% for the no-pretraining Mol2vec baseline. On representation fairness, InfoCORE reduces equalized odds (EO) by 28% over CLIP on UCI Adult (14.9 vs. 20.7) and by 18% on Compas (15.3 vs. 18.4), while maintaining comparable prediction accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoCORE is compared against three baselines: CLIP (unconditional multimodal contrastive learning), CCL (conditional contrastive learning that restricts negatives to the same batch), and Mol2vec (no-pretraining baseline). On GE retrieval, InfoCORE clearly dominates: it outperforms CLIP by 15% in top-1 batch-restricted accuracy (14.03 vs. 12.23) and by 235% over CCL in top-1 whole-library accuracy (6.39 vs. 1.93), confirming that CCL's limited negative pool severely hurts generalization. On CP retrieval, InfoCORE matches or slightly exceeds CLIP in the whole library (6.93 vs. 7.23 top-1) and leads in the batch library (13.26 vs. 13.20 top-1), while CCL collapses (1.31 top-1 whole). For molecular property prediction, InfoCORE achieves the best average AUC (78.3% with GE, 78.0% with CP), edging out CLIP (77.6%/77.4%) and CCL (77.6%/77.5%), with the largest gains in ClinTox (91.9 vs. 89.6/90.5) and HIV (78.5 vs. 77.7/77.5). On fairness, InfoCORE consistently achieves the lowest EO and EOPP across all three datasets (e.g., UCI Adult EO 14.9 vs. 20.7/19.0; Compas EOPP 9.3 vs. 11.7/10.1) while maintaining accuracy within 0.5% of baselines, demonstrating that the reweighting strategy provides superior debiasing without sacrificing utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoCORE extends the multimodal contrastive learning framework of CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and the variational lower bound on mutual information from Poole et al. (2019) to the conditional setting, deriving a tractable multi-sample bound on conditional mutual information. The key departure from CCL (Ma et al., 2021), which restricts negative samples to the same batch/attribute value, is that InfoCORE instead reweights all negatives by their estimated posterior batch distribution, preserving the full negative pool while still debiasing. This shifts the debiasing mechanism from a sampling constraint to a loss-weighting scheme, avoiding CCL's poor generalization when per-batch observations are sparse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Batch effects are a pervasive and often under-addressed confound in high-throughput drug screening, and integrating their removal directly into the representation learning objective (rather than as a separate preprocessing step) is a practical advance for the drug discovery community. The framework's generality—demonstrated on fairness benchmarks—suggests applicability to any setting where a sensitive or confounding attribute must be removed from learned representations. Open questions include the sensitivity to the α hyperparameter governing the tradeoff between debiasing strength and negative-sample diversity, the quality of the weighted-average approximation to the joint posterior, and whether the approach scales to continuous or very high-cardinality confounders.
