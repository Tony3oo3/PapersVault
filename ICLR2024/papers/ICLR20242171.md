## Problem

Direct image alignment methods that train feature descriptors with the Gauss-Newton loss (e.g., GN-Net, PixLoc) bake the noise assumptions of their training data into the learned feature maps, limiting generalization to unseen motion patterns and pose initializations. The paper asks whether the Gauss-Newton loss admits a closed-form solution that decouples the convergence basin from any fixed training distribution, enabling dynamic, test-time control over robustness versus accuracy without pose supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242171/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image with a 3D point cloud in its coordinate frame, a query image, and an initial (potentially highly inaccurate) SE(3) pose. Interest points and their descriptors are extracted from the query image using an off-the-shelf detector (e.g., SuperPoint), while 3D points are projected onto the reference image to obtain reference descriptors via the same embedding network. The output is the refined relative 6DoF camera pose T ∈ SE(3), obtained by iterative Gauss-Newton optimization where the dense feature map is constructed analytically from sparse descriptors rather than produced by a trained network.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form minimizer of the expected Gauss-Newton loss over a noise distribution p(ε), showing that the optimal dense feature map f̃_q(x̃) = J̃_q(x̃)(x̃ − x_m(x̃)) + y_m(x̃) is a weighted linear interpolation of interest-point descriptors, with weights and Jacobian determined by weighted means and covariances under p. This solution is agnostic to the underlying feature extractor, so any self-supervised descriptor (SuperPoint, SIFT, SiLK) can be plugged in. At test time, a scheduler dynamically adjusts p(ε) from a wide truncated uniform distribution (large convergence basin) to a narrow Gaussian (high accuracy), enabling continuous coarse-to-fine alignment. The derivation also reveals that end-to-end training with the Gauss-Newton loss merely learns interpolation between interest-point features, exposing an inherent limitation of backpropagating through GN optimization and establishing a connection between featuremetric alignment and feature matching.

## Results

On Aachen Day-Night and CMU Seasons, the method uses self-supervised SuperPoint descriptors (no pose supervision) and reports recall at three thresholds: (0.25 m, 2°), (0.5 m, 5°), and (5 m, 10°). On Aachen Day, it achieves 66.3/72.5/78.8%, surpassing the best supervised baseline PixLoc (64.3/69.3/77.4%). On CMU Seasons, it leads in Suburban (79.8/85.0/92.4% vs. PixLoc 79.6/81.1/85.2%) and Park (63.4/67.9/77.5% vs. PixLoc 61.0/62.5/69.4%), while on Aachen Night it trails PixLoc (43.9/50.0/56.1% vs. 51.0/55.1/67.3%) due to the lack of day-night-specific training. The method also demonstrates significantly greater robustness to pose initialization noise compared to PixLoc (Fig. 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three supervised featuremetric alignment methods: GN-Net, LM-Reloc, and PixLoc. Against GN-Net and LM-Reloc, the proposed method wins decisively across nearly all settings, with the largest margins on CMU Suburban (+15.1 pp at 5 m/10° over GN-Net) and CMU Park (+12.2 pp over GN-Net). Against the strongest baseline PixLoc, the method wins on Aachen Day (+2.0 pp at 0.25 m/2°), CMU Suburban (+7.2 pp at 5 m/10°), and CMU Park (+8.1 pp at 5 m/10°), but loses on Aachen Night (−7.1 pp at 0.25 m/2°) and is slightly behind on CMU Urban at the tightest threshold (86.0 vs. 88.3 at 0.25 m/2°). The Aachen Night gap is attributed to SuperPoint not being trained on day-night correspondences, whereas PixLoc was explicitly supervised on such pairs. Notably, the proposed method achieves these results using only self-supervised descriptors without any pose supervision, while all baselines are trained end-to-end with ground-truth poses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GN-Net's Gauss-Newton loss formulation for featuremetric image alignment, but departs fundamentally from the end-to-end training paradigm shared by GN-Net, PixLoc, and LM-Reloc. Instead of learning feature descriptors with pose supervision and backpropagating through optimization, it derives a closed-form analytical solution to the expected GN loss, enabling the use of any off-the-shelf self-supervised descriptor (e.g., SuperPoint) with dynamically adjustable smoothness at test time. The derivation also reveals a conceptual bridge between featuremetric alignment and classical feature matching, since the optimal feature map is fully determined by sparse interest-point descriptors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form solution provides practitioners with a principled, interpretable mechanism to control the convergence basin during alignment, offering robustness to poor initialization without requiring pose-supervised training. The finding that end-to-end GN-loss training merely learns interpolation between interest-point features challenges the assumption that backpropagating through Gauss-Newton optimization yields sophisticated representations, with implications extending to other methods that optimize through LM/GN steps. Limitations include the absence of outlier filtering (hurting performance on occluded scenes like Aachen Night) and the reliance on a relaxed constraint that is only exactly satisfied for uniform noise distributions. Open questions include whether the interpolation characterization holds for the full probabilistic GN loss and whether learned optimization steps (à la RAFT) can surpass this analytical formulation.
