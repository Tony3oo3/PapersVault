## Problem

While accelerated second-order methods achieve fast convergence rates under exact oracles, the theoretical landscape for stochastic and inexact derivatives remained incomplete: no matching lower bounds existed for the stochastic Hessian term, and the optimal trade-off between gradient and Hessian inexactness was unknown. Prior methods (Extra-Newton, Accelerated Inexact Tensor) achieved suboptimal rates in the Hessian inexactness term (T^{-3/2} vs. T^{-2}), leaving a gap between upper and lower complexity bounds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a convex C² objective with L₂-Lipschitz Hessian, an unbiased stochastic gradient oracle with variance σ₁², and either a stochastic Hessian oracle (variance σ₂²) or a δ₂-inexact Hessian along the step direction. The subproblem at each iteration can be solved approximately to a verifiable gradient tolerance τ, with a dynamic schedule τ_t = c/t^{5/2} that avoids over-solving early iterations. The output is an iterate x_T with expected objective gap O(σ₁R/√T + σ₂R²/T² + L₂R³/T³), and for the strongly convex case a restarted variant yields linear convergence with Hessian batch size independent of target accuracy ε.

## Method

The Accelerated Stochastic Cubic Newton (ASCN) method builds on estimating sequences (Nesterov acceleration) adapted to second-order models, where each iteration solves a cubic-regularized subproblem ω_x(y) = φ_x(y) + (δ̄/2)‖x−y‖² + (M/6)‖x−y‖³ using inexact gradient g(x) and Hessian H(x). The key technical insight is the addition of a time-varying quadratic regularizer δ̄_t = 2σ₂ + (σ₁+τ)/R · (t+3)^{3/2}, which simultaneously majorizes the Hessian error and damps the stochastic Newton step to achieve the optimal T^{-2} rate in the Hessian term. The paper proves a matching lower bound via a reduction argument: fixing the Hessian oracle to a constant matrix reduces the problem to first-order optimization (yielding Ω(σ₁R/√T)), while setting all noise to zero recovers the deterministic second-order lower bound Ω(L₂R³/T^{7/2}). A tensor generalization extends the framework to p-th order derivatives with rate O(σ₁R/√T + Σᵢ δᵢRⁱ/Tⁱ + L_pR^{p+1}/T^{p+1}), and a restarted variant for strongly convex objectives achieves linear convergence with constant Hessian batch size.

## Results

The proposed Accelerated Stochastic Cubic Newton (ASCN) method achieves the convergence rate O(σ₁R/√T + σ₂R²/T² + L₂R³/T³) for stochastic Hessian with variance σ₂², and O(σ₁R/√T + δ₂R²/T² + L₂R³/T³) for δ₂-inexact Hessian. The paper proves matching lower bounds Ω(σ₁R/√T), Ω(σ₂R²/T²), and Ω(L₂R³/T^{7/2}), establishing optimality in gradient and Hessian inexactness. In experiments on logistic regression over the a9a dataset (d=123), ASCN consistently outperforms Extra-Newton and SGD across deterministic and stochastic oracle settings, with the Hessian inexactness term decaying as 1/T² (vs. 1/T^{3/2} for Extra-Newton), enabling the use of smaller Hessian batch sizes (e.g., 150 vs. 10000 for gradients).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Extra-Newton (Antonakopoulos et al., 2022), ASCN improves the Hessian inexactness rate from O(σ₂R²/T^{3/2}) to O(σ₂R²/T²), a strict improvement that is confirmed empirically: in stochastic experiments with gradient batch size 10000 and Hessian batch size 150, ASCN converges faster than EN on both train and test loss. Against the Accelerated Inexact Cubic Newton (Ghadimi et al., 2017), ASCN extends the setting from exact gradients to unbiased stochastic gradients while maintaining the same Hessian rate, and achieves the optimal O(σ₁R/√T) gradient rate rather than no gradient convergence guarantee. Against the Accelerated Inexact Tensor Method (Agafonov et al., 2023), ASCN improves the gradient convergence from O(δ₁R) (for bounded inexactness) to O(σ₁R/√T) (for unbiased stochastic gradients), and reduces the total number of stochastic gradient computations from O(ε^{-7/3}) to O(ε^{-2}) while keeping the same O(ε^{-2/3}) Hessian computations. In all three experimental regimes (deterministic, stochastic same-batch, stochastic different-batch), ASCN outperforms both EN and SGD, though all methods converge to the same point in the stochastic regime as the gradient noise term σ₁R/√T dominates near the solution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of accelerated cubic regularization (Nesterov & Polyak 2006; Nesterov 2008), inexact second-order methods (Ghadimi et al. 2017; Agafonov et al. 2023), and stochastic second-order optimization (Antonakopoulos et al. 2022, Extra-Newton). It departs from Extra-Newton by achieving a faster Hessian-inexactness rate (T⁻² vs. T⁻³ᐟ²) through a different acceleration mechanism based on estimating sequences with quadratic regularization, and from Agafonov et al. (2023) by requiring only unbiased (rather than merely inexact) gradients to attain the optimal T⁻¹ᐟ² gradient rate. The tensor generalization in Section 5 extends the framework to p-th order derivatives, building on the implementable tensor method structure of Nesterov (2021b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a gap in the theory of stochastic second-order optimization by establishing the first matching lower bounds for inexact gradient and Hessian oracles, proving that the proposed rates are information-theoretically optimal. This provides a clear benchmark for future stochastic second-order and tensor methods. However, the practical impact is currently limited: experiments are conducted on a single small tabular dataset (a9a, d=123), the subproblem still requires a dedicated solver, and the method's advantage over Extra-Newton is most visible in the Hessian-noise-dominated regime which may be less common in large-scale deep learning. Open questions include whether the lower bound extends to randomized methods and whether the constant Hessian batch size in the strongly convex mini-batch setting translates to meaningful savings in distributed training.
