## Problem

Deploying pretrained Transformer LLMs on CPUs is severely bottlenecked by the quadratic time and space complexity of self-attention on long sequences. Existing efficient-attention methods either require retraining, impose constraints incompatible with pretrained weights (e.g., normalized keys, shared Q/K), or cannot handle causal masks, leaving no practical acceleration method that works out-of-the-box on frozen LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the standard Q, K, V matrices (each of dimension d) and an optional causal mask from any pretrained Transformer attention layer, and produces the attention output matrix computed only over the top-k identified keys rather than all m keys. In the LLM experiments, IceFormer is applied selectively to a subset of layers (16th through 31st of Vicuna-7b-v1.5-16k) rather than all layers, and the k value scales with input length via a user-set ratio α clamped between 30 and 50.

## Method

IceFormer converts the attention-weight maximization into a k-nearest-neighbor search by embedding keys and queries into a (d+1)-dimensional space: keys are scaled by 1/c and augmented with √(1−‖k‖²/c²), while queries are L2-normalized and augmented with 0, making argmax of attention weights exactly equivalent to argmin of Euclidean distance in the embedded space. A ranking-based k-NNS algorithm (Prioritized DCI) then efficiently retrieves the top-k most relevant keys without computing all pairwise attention scores, after which standard softmax attention is computed only over those k keys. The approach requires no retraining, no architectural modification, and supports causal masking through incremental O(log m) database updates as new tokens are generated, eliminating the quadratic dependence on sequence length.

## Results

On the LRA benchmark (5 tasks, up to 4K tokens), IceFormer achieves an average 7.63× speedup over the vanilla Transformer while retaining 98.6% of its accuracy, and is 3.04× faster than the best efficient Transformer with comparable accuracy per task. On the ZeroSCROLLS benchmark with Vicuna-7b-v1.5-16k (LLaMA 2-based, up to 10K tokens), IceFormer achieves a 2.73× average speedup while retaining 99.6% of the vanilla model's score. IceFormer also achieves the lowest peak memory usage across all LRA tasks, saving up to 0.862 GB compared to the vanilla Transformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IceFormer dominates all eight efficient Transformer baselines on the LRA benchmark, achieving near-vanilla accuracy (e.g., 0.4153 vs. 0.4255 on ListOps, 0.5978 vs. 0.6019 on Text) while being 3–8× faster than the vanilla Transformer and 2–10× faster than the next-best method per task. Among the retraining-free baselines, Nyströmformer and LARA come closest in accuracy but are 2–10× slower than IceFormer (e.g., 0.80s vs. 0.38s on ListOps for Nyströmformer); H-Transformer-1D is faster on some tasks but suffers large accuracy drops (e.g., 0.3265 vs. 0.4153 on ListOps, 0.2286 vs. 0.4046 on Image). The retraining-required baselines (Longformer, Performer, Linformer) show the worst accuracy, often near chance level on ListOps (0.1975 vs. 0.4255 for vanilla), and Linformer is actually slower than vanilla on ListOps (3.15s vs. 2.92s). On the ZeroSCROLLS LLM benchmark, IceFormer retains 99.0–100% of Vicuna-7b-v1.5-16k's score across all 10 tasks while achieving 1.9×–3.0× speedup, whereas H-Transformer-1D drops to 6.8–90.9% of the vanilla score (averaging only 32.5%) with a mere 1.5× speedup. Reformer is excluded from LLM comparisons because it requires shared Q/K projections not present in pretrained LLMs, and LARA/Nyströmformer cannot handle causal masks natively.

## Relation to Prior Work

IceFormer sits at the intersection of two research threads: the efficient Transformers literature (sparsity-based methods like Longformer/Reformer and low-rank methods like Performer/Nyströmformer) and the approximate nearest neighbor search literature. Its core technical contribution is combining the Neyshabur & Srebro (2015) d+1-dimensional embedding trick—which converts inner-product maximization into Euclidean nearest-neighbor search—with Prioritized DCI (Li & Malik, 2017) as the underlying k-NNS engine. This combination is the key departure from prior efficient Transformers: by embedding keys and queries into a higher-dimensional space, the method identifies the top-k most important keys without requiring normalized keys, shared Q/K projections, or retraining, while still supporting causal masks that block landmark-based methods like LARA and Nyströmformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial for CPU-only deployment of LLMs, where the quadratic attention cost is the dominant bottleneck and retraining is infeasible. The 2.7×–7.6× speedup at 98.6%–99.6% accuracy retention is a strong result, but the validation scope is limited: only one LLM (Vicuna-7b-v1.5-16k) is tested, IceFormer is applied to only half the layers (16–31), and the LLM baselines are restricted to H-Transformer-1D since most efficient Transformers cannot handle causal masks. Open questions include whether the layer-selection heuristic generalizes across architectures, how the method behaves at even longer context lengths (beyond 16K), and whether the Prioritized DCI database construction cost becomes a bottleneck for very long sequences in autoregressive generation.
