## Problem

Training stateful policies (e.g., RNNs) in reinforcement learning relies on Backpropagation Through Time, which is inherently sequential, suffers from vanishing or exploding gradients over long trajectories, and requires history truncation that introduces bias into the policy update. No existing method provides an unbiased, parallelizable gradient estimator for stateful policies that integrates cleanly into modern actor-critic algorithms like SAC, TD3, or PPO.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a current environment observation (or state) and a learned internal policy state z, and outputs a joint Gaussian distribution over the action a and the next internal state z'. Unlike standard RNN policies where the hidden state transition is deterministic, here z' is sampled from a stochastic kernel, making the policy a joint distribution π(a, z'|s, z). The Q-function is correspondingly defined over the four-tuple (s, z, a, z'), and in POMDP settings the policy conditions on observations while the critic may access privileged state information for bootstrapping.

## Method

S2PG decomposes a stateful policy into a stochastic internal state transition kernel (a Gaussian over z' parameterized by η_θ(s,z)) and a stateless action policy (a Gaussian over a parameterized by f_θ(s,z)), both conditioned on the current (s, z) pair. Because the internal state transition is stochastic, the policy gradient depends only on local information at each timestep—(a_t, z_{t+1}, s_t, z_t)—rather than the full history, eliminating the need for BPTT and enabling fully parallel gradient computation. The paper derives a complete theoretical foundation: a Stateful Policy Gradient Theorem, a Stateful Deterministic Policy Gradient Theorem, and a Stateful Performance Difference Lemma, which together allow direct instantiation of stateful variants of SAC, TD3, PPO, GAIL, and LS-IQ with minimal code changes. A variance analysis shows that while S2PG introduces additional variance from the stochastic kernel (scaling linearly with trajectory length T), BPTT variance can grow exponentially with T when the internal transition Jacobian norm exceeds 1, making S2PG more robust to architecture choice and trajectory length.

## Results

The paper evaluates S2PG across MuJoCo Gym POMDP tasks (Ant, Humanoid, HalfCheetah, Walker2d with hidden velocities and randomized masses), complex IL locomotion tasks (Atlas with hidden carry-weight, Humanoid with dynamics randomization), memory tasks, and a blind ODE/CPG task. S2PG achieves major computational speedups over BPTT with long truncation lengths (32 steps) while incurring only a slight drop in asymptotic normalized return for SAC and TD3. In the no-privileged-information setting (PPO with Monte-Carlo rollouts), S2PG consistently outperforms BPTT in both return and training time. In the IL setting, S2PG-based GAIL and LS-IQ outperform their BPTT counterparts even in sample efficiency, and the method successfully trains a completely blind walking policy using a trainable CPG ODE on HalfCheetah and Ant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BPTT-based recurrent baselines (RTD3/RSAC from Yang & Nguyen, and recurrent PPO from Ni et al.), S2PG achieves comparable or slightly lower asymptotic return on high-dimensional tasks (Ant, Humanoid) but with substantially shorter training time, especially at truncation length 32 where BPTT suffers from vanishing/exploding gradients; on low-dimensional tasks the gap in return is slightly larger. Against SLAC (Lee et al.), the specialized model-based POMDP method, S2PG demonstrates better normalized return and faster training, making it a simpler and more efficient alternative. In the imitation learning setting, S2PG-based GAIL and LS-IQ outperform their BPTT counterparts in both normalized return and sample efficiency on the Atlas carry-weight and Humanoid dynamics-randomization tasks. The stateless window baselines (5 or 32 observations) show computational advantages over BPTT but degrade in performance as window length increases, and cannot encode inductive biases in the latent space. Overall, S2PG wins on training speed across all settings, ties or slightly loses on asymptotic return in privileged-critic RL, and wins on both return and sample efficiency in IL and no-privileged-information RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recurrent policy gradient lineage initiated by Wierstra et al. (2010), which relied on BPTT through RNN trajectories, by introducing a stochastic internal state transition kernel that severs the sequential gradient path. The closest conceptual predecessor is Zhang et al. (2016), who proposed a similar stochastic state kernel but did not extend it to the actor-critic setting or provide a full policy gradient theorem. The paper systematically derives stateful versions of the Policy Gradient Theorem (Sutton et al., 1999), the Deterministic Policy Gradient Theorem (Silver et al., 2014), and the Performance Difference Lemma (Kakade & Langford, 2002), grounding the approach in the stochastic computation graph framework of Schulman et al. (2015a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S2PG offers a drop-in replacement for BPTT in any actor-critic or imitation learning algorithm, reducing training time and eliminating vanishing/exploding gradient issues without requiring specialized architectures like LSTMs or GRUs. The variance analysis cleanly shows that BPTT's additional variance can grow exponentially with trajectory length while S2PG's remains bounded, providing theoretical justification for the empirical speedup. A key open limitation is that the method cannot simultaneously learn a bootstrapped value function and an internal state in a true POMDP without privileged information in the critic, restricting its applicability in fully partially observable settings. Future work on massively parallel environments and resolving the critic-representation coupling in POMDPs would strengthen the approach.
