## Problem

Existing LLM reasoning approaches for table understanding—whether generic Chain-of-Thought or program-aided methods like Text-to-SQL and Binder—represent intermediate reasoning steps as free-form text or single-pass code, which is poorly suited to the structured, multi-step nature of tabular reasoning. The paper asks how tabular data itself can be leveraged as the medium for intermediate thoughts in a reasoning chain, rather than being reduced to text or queried once via a static program.

## Input / Output

The method takes a semi-structured table (encoded in PIPE format as a textual representation) and a natural-language question or statement as input. It produces a final answer that varies by task: a short text span for WikiTQ, a free-form response for FeTaQA, or a Boolean (True/False) for TabFact. All table values are serialized into text, and the LLM interacts with the table exclusively through this textual encoding, with operations executed programmatically between LLM calls.

## Method

CHAIN-OF-TABLE extends Chain-of-Thought to the tabular setting by using iteratively transformed tables as proxies for intermediate reasoning steps. At each iteration, a DynamicPlan step prompts the LLM (via in-context learning) to select the next atomic operation from a pool of five (add_column, select_row, select_column, group_by, sort_by) based on the current table state, the question, and the operation history. A GenerateArgs step then produces the operation's arguments, which are parsed via regular expressions and executed programmatically to produce a new intermediate table. This loop continues until the LLM emits an ending tag, after which the final transformed table is fed back to the LLM for answer extraction. Unlike single-pass program-aided baselines, the multi-step dynamic planning allows the table to be progressively reshaped—enriched with computed columns or condensed to relevant rows—tailoring it to the specific question at each step.

## Results

CHAIN-OF-TABLE achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks. On WikiTQ with PaLM 2, it reaches 67.31% denotation accuracy, improving over the strongest baseline Dater (61.48%) by 5.83 points and over Chain-of-Thought (60.43%) by 6.88 points. On TabFact with PaLM 2, it scores 86.61% accuracy (+1.98 over Dater's 84.63%). With GPT-3.5, CHAIN-OF-TABLE also leads on both WikiTQ (59.94% vs. 52.81% for Dater) and TabFact (80.20% vs. 78.01% for Dater). On FeTaQA, it achieves BLEU 32.61 and ROUGE-1 0.66, outperforming Dater by +3.14 BLEU and +0.03 ROUGE-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHAIN-OF-TABLE outperforms all four compared baselines on both WikiTQ and TabFact across both LLM backbones. Against the strongest program-aided baseline Dater, it gains +5.83 on WikiTQ (PaLM 2) and +3.20 (GPT-3.5), while on TabFact the margins are +1.98 and +1.03 respectively. Against Binder, the improvements are larger: +12.43 on WikiTQ (PaLM 2) and +3.20 (GPT-3.5), with TabFact gains of +9.63 and +1.03. Against Chain-of-Thought, CHAIN-OF-TABLE leads by +7.56 on TabFact and +6.88 on WikiTQ (PaLM 2), and by +14.83 on TabFact and +6.46 on WikiTQ (GPT-3.5). Text-to-SQL is the weakest baseline, trailing CHAIN-OF-TABLE by 18.24 points on WikiTQ (PaLM 2) and 15.49 on TabFact (GPT-3.5). Notably, CHAIN-OF-TABLE also uses fewer generated samples (≤25) than Binder (50) and Dater (100), making it more efficient while achieving higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022) from free-form textual reasoning to the tabular domain, proposing that intermediate tables (rather than text or code) serve as more natural proxies for intermediate thoughts when reasoning over structured data. It departs from program-aided reasoning methods (Text-to-SQL, Binder, Dater) that rely on single-pass code generation or fixed decomposition procedures, instead introducing a dynamic, iterative planning loop where the LLM selects from a pool of atomic table operations (add column, select row/column, group by, sort by) and the table evolves step by step. The key conceptual departure from Dater is that operations are dynamically planned per question rather than following a fixed pipeline, and the framework is extensible to new operations via in-context learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured intermediate representations (evolving tables) outperform both free-form text (CoT) and code-based intermediates (SQL/Python) for table understanding, suggesting a broader principle that the format of intermediate reasoning should match the structure of the input data. Practical limitations include the small operation pool (only 5 atomic operations), reliance on large proprietary LLMs (PaLM 2, GPT-3.5), and marginal gains on FeTaQA where ROUGE metrics are insensitive to in-context learning improvements. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex operations (joins, nested aggregations), whether it works with smaller or open-source models, and whether the dynamic planning can be made more reliable without the greedy single-path strategy.
