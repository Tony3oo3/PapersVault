## Problem

Fine-tuning vision-language foundation models like CLIP on downstream tasks in few-shot settings causes severe overfitting on newly introduced parameters, leading to a significant drop in zero-shot generalization. Existing prompt tuning and adapter methods improve few-shot accuracy but cannot simultaneously preserve or improve the model's zero-shot capabilities, creating a fundamental trade-off between task-specific performance and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240254/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed through a ViT-B/16 patch encoder) and a class name formatted as a template sentence ('a photo of a [category]') as input, producing a class label via cosine similarity between image and text embeddings. Unusually, the text input is also fed through a pre-trained LLM (GPT-2 or GPT-3) to generate a more descriptive sentence used for the consistency constraint, and the image undergoes augmentation to create a perturbed version. The model is trained with 16 samples per class in a few-shot setting and evaluated on both base (few-shot) and novel (zero-shot) class partitions.

## Method

CoPrompt enforces a cosine-distance consistency constraint between the embeddings produced by the trainable model (with learnable prompts and adapters) and the frozen pre-trained CLIP, applied independently on both the image and text branches. To further regularize this constraint, the method uses perturbed inputs: a pre-trained LLM generates a descriptive sentence from the template text for the text branch, and data augmentation (random resized crop, horizontal flip) creates a perturbed image for the image branch. Additionally, CoPrompt integrates two-layer adapters (linear layers with non-linearity) on both branches alongside multi-modal prompts, combining the prompting and adapter paradigms into a single framework. The final training objective is a weighted sum of the supervised cross-entropy loss and the consistency constraint loss (L = L_ce + λ·L_cc), where the consistency term acts as a form of knowledge distillation from the frozen pre-trained encoders to the learnable ones, preventing the trainable parameters from deviating too far from the original model behavior.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization task averaged over 11 datasets, CoPrompt achieves 84.00% base accuracy, 77.23% novel (zero-shot) accuracy, and 80.48% harmonic mean (HM), setting a new state-of-the-art. This represents a 0.51% improvement in HM over the previous best (PromptSRC, 79.97%) and a 1.93% improvement over MaPLe (78.55%). On cross-dataset evaluation (trained on ImageNet, tested on 10 target datasets), CoPrompt achieves 67.00% average accuracy, outperforming MaPLe (66.30%) and PromptSRC (65.81%). On domain generalization, CoPrompt achieves 60.42% average, comparable to PromptSRC (60.65%) and Bayesian Prompt (60.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPrompt outperforms all seven compared methods on the harmonic mean metric in the base-to-novel generalization setting, achieving 80.48% vs. PromptSRC (79.97%), MaPLe (78.55%), KgCoOp (77.00%), ProGrad (76.16%), CoCoOp (75.83%), CoOp (71.66%), and CLIP (71.70%). On novel-class (zero-shot) accuracy, CoPrompt leads with 77.23%, beating the closest competitor PromptSRC by 1.13% and MaPLe by 2.09%. The only metric where CoPrompt does not lead is base-class accuracy, where PromptSRC edges ahead by 0.26% (84.26% vs. 84.00%), though the gap is negligible. On cross-dataset evaluation, CoPrompt wins on 8 out of 10 target datasets, with the largest gains on EuroSAT (51.90% vs. 48.06% for MaPLe) and UCF101 (69.73% vs. 68.69%). On domain generalization, CoPrompt is within 0.23% of the best method (PromptSRC), indicating competitive but not dominant performance in this setting. The paper also shows that adding the consistency constraint to CoOp improves its HM from 71.66% to 72.75%, validating the constraint as a broadly applicable regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoPrompt sits at the intersection of two established paradigms for adapting vision-language models: prompt tuning (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC) and adapter-based tuning (CLIP-Adapter). Prior work treated these as mutually exclusive—combining them naively caused overfitting and degraded zero-shot performance. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the consistency-regularization idea from self-supervised learning and repurposing it as a knowledge-distillation-like constraint between a frozen pre-trained encoder and a learnable one, which is what makes the combined prompt+adapter parameterization viable. The LLM-generated text perturbations (via GPT) extend the KgCoOp line of work but apply them as a consistency anchor rather than a direct input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent tension in VLM fine-tuning: adding more learnable parameters improves few-shot accuracy but erodes zero-shot generalization. The consistency constraint provides a principled mechanism to break this trade-off, and the ablation showing that adapters *without* the constraint perform worse than no adapters at all is a useful cautionary result for practitioners. Limitations include evaluation only on ViT-B/16 (and EVA-CLIP-B/16), the added training cost (~2× FLOPs, 25% more time) from running the frozen model in parallel, and the modest absolute gains (~0.5% over PromptSRC). Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViT-L, ViT-H), whether the LLM perturbation component is necessary at scale, and whether the cosine-based consistency loss generalizes to non-CLIP architectures.
