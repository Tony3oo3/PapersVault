## Problem

Existing sparse-view camera pose estimation methods predict compact global parametrizations (rotation and translation per camera), which the authors argue is suboptimal for neural learning that benefits from over-parameterized distributed representations. This paper recasts pose estimation as per-patch ray prediction, enabling tight coupling with spatial image features and naturally handling the inherent ambiguities (symmetries, partial observations) that arise with fewer than 10 views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240931/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse RGB images (typically 2–8), each cropped to a square around the object bounding box, and extracts spatial features via a frozen DINOv2 (S/14) backbone. For each image, rays are defined over a uniform p×p grid (default 16×16 = 256 rays), each represented as a 6D Plücker coordinate (3D direction + 3D moment). The network outputs predicted ray bundles, from which camera extrinsics (R, t) and intrinsics (K) are recovered via least-squares optimization: the camera center is found by minimizing distance to all rays, and R/K are extracted from a DLT-solved homography.

## Method

The core representation over-parameterizes each camera as a bundle of rays in Plücker coordinates, where each ray is associated with a known pixel coordinate. A DiT-based transformer (16 blocks) jointly processes all N·p² patch tokens—each token concatenating DINOv2 spatial features, normalized pixel coordinates, and (for diffusion) noisy rays plus a positionally-encoded time step—to predict the denoised ray for every patch. Two variants are trained: a regression model minimizing L2 loss on rays, and a denoising diffusion model (DDPM, T=100) that learns the distribution over rays to capture multiple plausible modes under uncertainty. The diffusion variant uses a modified DDPM inference that removes stochastic noise and stops early (at T=30), using the predicted x₀ directly, which empirically improves accuracy by selecting among distinct modes without over-refining around a single sample.

## Results

On CO3D with 8 images, Ray Diffusion achieves 93.3% camera rotation accuracy (@15°) on seen categories and 88.1% on unseen categories, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. For camera center accuracy (@0.1 scene scale), Ray Diffusion reaches 84.1% (seen) and 71.4% (unseen), again leading all competing methods. Even the simpler Ray Regression variant (93.3% seen, 81.9% unseen rotation accuracy) surpasses the strongest baseline RelPose++ (85.5% seen, 74.9% unseen) by 7.8 and 7.0 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ray Diffusion outperforms all four baselines across every setting. Against the strongest learning-based baseline RelPose++ (85.5% seen, 74.9% unseen rotation accuracy at 8 images), Ray Diffusion gains +7.8 and +13.2 points respectively. Against PoseDiffusion (78.8% seen, 67.7% unseen), the improvement is +14.5 and +20.4 points, demonstrating that denoising rays is substantially more effective than denoising camera extrinsics directly. The R+T Regression baseline (56.5% seen, 48.9% unseen) shows that directly regressing global camera parameters is far less effective than the distributed ray representation, even without diffusion. COLMAP (30.6% seen, 38.5% unseen) struggles severely in wide-baseline sparse-view settings due to insufficient image overlap for correspondence matching. Notably, the gap between Ray Regression and Ray Diffusion (+4.1 seen, +6.2 unseen) highlights the benefit of modeling uncertainty via diffusion for handling symmetries and partial observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in learning-based sparse-view pose estimation (RelPose, RelPose++, PoseDiffusion, SparsePose) by changing the *target representation* rather than the model architecture: instead of regressing or denoising global (R, t, K) parameters, it predicts per-patch rays in Plücker coordinates. The ray-based camera model draws on classical calibration work (Grossberg & Nayar, 2001) but repurposes it as a neural prediction target, and the diffusion framework is adapted from DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) with the noise applied to rays rather than camera extrinsics. The transformer backbone (DiT) and frozen DINOv2 features are standard components; the novelty lies entirely in the distributed ray parametrization and its natural alignment with patch-level spatial features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a simple reparameterization (rays over global extrinsics) with a basic transformer regressor already outperforms substantially more complex baselines (energy-based optimization, geometry-guided diffusion), suggesting that representation choice may matter more than model sophistication for sparse-view pose estimation. The diffusion extension adds the ability to sample multiple plausible modes under symmetry-induced ambiguity, which regression cannot capture. Open questions include whether enforcing geometric consistency (e.g., epipolar constraints) on the ray bundle would further improve accuracy, whether the approach extends to single-view or dense multi-view settings, and whether joint inference of rays and 3D geometry is feasible.
