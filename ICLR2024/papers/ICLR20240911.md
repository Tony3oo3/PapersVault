## Problem

Existing 3D texture synthesis methods entangle texture appearance with input geometry, so the same texture code produces different styles on different shapes, preventing controllable texture transfer and editing. While 3D shape generation has advanced significantly, generating high-fidelity, shape-disentangled textures from single-view 2D image collections remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240911/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D shape point cloud (sampled from a category, e.g., chairs or cars) and a randomly sampled texture style vector z_tex ~ N(0,1) as input, and produces a 3D-consistent rendered RGB image (trained at 512×512, rendered up to 1024×1024) along with a textured UV radiance field. Training requires only single-view 2D images from the same category—no multi-view captures, no paired shape-texture data, and no known camera poses. The texture is parameterized on a shared canonical UV sphere (2K ico-sphere vertices), making it directly transferable to any shape in the category via learned dense correspondence.

## Method

TUVF generates textures in a learnable canonical UV sphere space rather than directly on the 3D shape, achieving disentanglement through a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 trains a Canonical Surface Auto-encoder (DGCNN encoder + implicit decoders f_θ, g_θ) that maps UV sphere coordinates to surface points and normals via Chamfer Distance and Poisson Surface Reconstruction, establishing dense cross-instance correspondence. Stage 2 trains a CIPS-UV texture feature generator that modulates features with the style code via weight modulation (à la StyleGAN) while avoiding spatial convolutions and explicit 2D unwrapping to preserve 3D semantics. Rendering uses a point-based radiance field: K-nearest surface points provide texture features fused by inverse distance weighting, with volume density derived from the auto-encoded indicator grid via VolSDF. A patch-based GAN discriminator (128×128 crops) provides adversarial supervision on rendered images, enabling training from single-view 2D image collections without 3D color supervision.

## Results

On CompCars, TUVF achieves the best scores across all four metrics: LPIPS_g of 15.87 (texture diversity), LPIPS_t of 1.95 (texture transfer consistency), FID of 41.79, and KID of 2.95. On DiffusionCats, TUVF similarly leads with FID 64.13, KID 3.56, LPIPS_g 11.99, and LPIPS_t 1.50. On Photoshape, TUVF achieves the best controllable synthesis scores (LPIPS_g 14.93, LPIPS_t 2.55) but trails Texturify slightly on FID (51.29 vs. 45.92) and KID (2.98 vs. 2.61). A user study with 125 respondents confirms TUVF is preferred over both Texturify and EpiGRAF on both general quality and transfer consistency across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TUVF outperforms all baselines on CompCars across every metric. Against the strongest generalizable baselines, Texturify (FID 59.55, KID 4.97) and EpiGRAF (FID 89.64, KID 6.73), TUVF improves FID by 30% and 53% respectively, while also achieving substantially higher texture diversity (LPIPS_g 15.87 vs. 9.75 and 4.26) and better transfer consistency (LPIPS_t 1.95 vs. 2.46 and 2.34). The instance-specific Text2Tex (FID 46.91) is the closest competitor on image quality but lacks generalizability and correspondence-based transfer. On Photoshape, TUVF wins on both controllable synthesis metrics (LPIPS_g 14.93 vs. 6.74 for Texturify; LPIPS_t 2.55 vs. 2.89) but is slightly behind Texturify on FID (51.29 vs. 45.92) and KID (2.98 vs. 2.61), which the authors attribute to topological variation in the full chair category. On DiffusionCats, TUVF dominates all metrics, with FID 64.13 vs. 72.43 (Texturify) and 196.01 (EpiGRAF), and LPIPS_t 1.50 vs. 2.61 and 1.98. Mesh-based baselines (TexFields, LTG, EG3D-Mesh) and diffusion-based methods (TEXTure, Point-UV Diffusion) are all substantially outperformed on FID and KID, with TUVF also being the only method combining generalizability, unsupervised training, and dense correspondence for texture transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

TUVF builds on the Canonical Point Auto-encoder (Cheng et al., 2021) for learning dense UV-to-surface correspondences, the CIPS generator (Anokhin et al., 2021) for pixel-independent texture synthesis, and point-based radiance field rendering (Xu et al., 2022; Yariv et al., 2021) for efficient surface shading. The key departure from prior texture synthesis methods (Texturify, EpiGRAF, EG3D-Mesh) is that those approaches condition texture generation directly on input geometry, entangling appearance style with shape; TUVF instead generates textures in a shape-independent canonical UV sphere space, making a single texture code produce consistent appearance across all shapes in a category. Unlike mesh-based UV methods (AUV-Net) that require explicit unwrapping and suffer from topology constraints, TUVF uses a continuous radiance field that naturally handles non-genus-zero shapes without cutting or stitching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TUVF enables a practically important capability: one texture code corresponds to one appearance style that can be transferred, edited, and reapplied across arbitrary shapes in a category without retraining, which is directly useful for game asset pipelines, virtual try-on, and design tools. The data generation pipeline (Stable Diffusion + ControlNet + SAM) also provides a practical recipe for creating training data from untextured 3D shapes without requiring aligned real-world photos. Key limitations remain: the one-to-one dense correspondence assumption breaks down for shapes with large structural variation (e.g., bean chairs vs. straight chairs), and the point-cloud encoder blurs fine geometric details. Open questions include whether incorporating diffusion priors or ray transformers can push photorealism further, and whether the correspondence framework can be relaxed to handle many-to-many mappings.
