## Problem

Existing transformer compression methods are either architecture-specific, confined to a single modality (NLP or vision), or require computationally expensive re-training to recover lost accuracy. There is no unified one-shot pruning framework that generalizes across multiple task domains and transformer architectures while producing competitive models without any re-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241337/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a pre-trained transformer model, a target FLOPs budget, and a small batch of training data (batch size ablated between 16–128) to compute importance scores via gradient-free forward passes. It outputs a structurally pruned model (with specific attention heads and FFN neurons removed) and, for vision tasks, a per-layer token reduction schedule specifying how many tokens to retain at each transformer block. The pruning is fully one-shot: no weight updates or fine-tuning are performed after the mask is applied.

## Method

OPTIN introduces a 'trajectory' importance metric that measures each prunable parameter's long-range impact by masking it and propagating a forward pass to compute cumulative manifold distillation loss (L_MD) over all subsequent layer embeddings plus a weighted KL-divergence (L_KD) on the output logits. This captures inter-layer dependencies that magnitude- or activation-based metrics miss. After scoring all attention heads and FFN neurons, an expedited polynomial-time mask-search policy (adapted from PTF) selects the optimal pruning configuration under the FLOPs constraint. For vision transformers, the framework extends the trajectory metric to rank individual tokens per layer, producing an optimal token reduction schedule that can be paired with any merging algorithm (e.g., ToMe's bipartite matching), enabling constant batch sizes and further FLOPs savings without re-training.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with BERT_BASE at a 60% FLOP constraint, OPTIN achieves ≤2% average accuracy degradation from baselines (e.g., 81.90% on MNLI vs. 84.53% baseline), outperforming the one-shot PTF method by 0.69% on MNLI. On ImageNet-1K with DeiT-Tiny, OPTIN_τ achieves 71.25% top-1 accuracy at 0.91G FLOPs (29.7% reduction), a 0.5% improvement over SAViT at lower FLOPs. On DeiT-Small, OPTIN_τ reaches 79.24% at 3.15G FLOPs (31.6% reduction), outperforming CP-ViT by 0.4% at a ~10% higher FLOPs reduction. For semantic segmentation on Cityscapes (Mask2Former/Swin-Ti), OPTIN_β achieves 74.57% mIoU with 24.2% FLOPs and 46.6% parameter reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the GLUE benchmark (60% FLOP constraint), OPTIN outperforms the one-shot PTF method across all six tasks, with the largest margin on MRPC (85.13% vs. 83.14%, +2.0%) and MNLI (81.90% vs. 81.21%, +0.69%). On ImageNet-1K DeiT-Tiny, OPTIN_τ (71.25% at 0.91G) surpasses the re-trained SAViT (70.72% at 0.98G) by 0.5% at lower FLOPs, and outperforms the non-re-trained CP-ViT (71.06% at 1.00G) by 0.19% at a 9% greater FLOPs reduction. On DeiT-Small, OPTIN_τ (79.24% at 3.15G) beats CP-ViT (78.84% at 3.64G) by 0.4% while achieving a ~10% higher FLOPs reduction. In the token-reduction-only setting (OPTIN_τ(∞) on DeiT-B), OPTIN achieves a 0.52% accuracy drop versus ToMe's 0.80% at comparable FLOPs (11.75G vs. 11.81G). For CNN pruning on CIFAR-10 (VGG-16-BN), OPTIN matches CFDP at 94.10% Top-1 but converges in 100–150 epochs versus 200–300 for HRank and CFDP.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPTIN builds on the mask-search policy from PTF (Kwon et al., 2022) for efficient parameter selection and borrows the fine-grained manifold distillation formulation from Hao et al. (2022) as an in-place importance metric rather than a training signal. It departs from the dominant paradigm in transformer pruning—where one-shot methods still require re-training to recover accuracy—by framing parameter importance through a 'trajectory' metric that accumulates layer-wise manifold errors and KL-divergence over all subsequent layers, capturing long-range inter-layer dependencies. For vision tasks, it integrates ToMe's bipartite token merging (Bolya et al., 2023) as a pluggable reduction mechanism guided by an OPTIN-derived per-layer token schedule, positioning itself as a unified, architecture-agnostic compression framework rather than a domain-specific pruning recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value of OPTIN lies in enabling compression of already-trained transformer models on standard hardware without the costly re-training loop, which is particularly relevant for edge deployment and for practitioners who cannot afford to re-train large pre-trained checkpoints. The demonstrated generalizability across NLP (GLUE), image classification (ImageNet, CIFAR-10), semantic segmentation (Cityscapes/Mask2Former), and even CNN architectures (VGG-16) suggests the trajectory metric is a broadly applicable saliency signal. However, the importance computation requires O(N×d) masked forward passes, which may become prohibitive for very large models (e.g., 70B+ parameter LLMs), and the paper does not explore scaling beyond BERT-base and DeiT-Small. Open questions include whether the trajectory formulation captures higher-order interactions between simultaneously pruned parameters, and whether the approach extends naturally to generative or autoregressive transformer architectures.
