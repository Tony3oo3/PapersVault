## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image interpolation methods produce indirect morphing paths with ghosting artifacts, particularly when endpoint images have significant semantic differences, because the two images may lie on distinct conditioned distributions of the latent diffusion model. Conventional morphing techniques require manual feature alignment or are restricted to narrow domains, leaving a gap for an automatic, open-world approach that produces smooth, direct, and realistic transitions between arbitrary image pairs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB endpoint images and a coarse common text prompt (e.g., 'An image of [token]' where [token] is a shared root class or concatenation) as input. It produces a sequence of interpolated images where consecutive pairs maintain an approximately constant LPIPS perceptual difference (perceptually-uniform sampling), rather than being uniformly spaced in the interpolation parameter α. The entire pipeline operates in the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.4 model, with images encoded via its VAE and decoded back to pixel space after denoising.

## Method

IMPUS interpolates in two coupled spaces: (1) the CLIP text embedding space, where optimized embeddings (via textual inversion initialized from a common prompt to preserve local linearity) are linearly interpolated to bridge conditioned distributions, and (2) the Gaussian latent state space, where endpoint images are mapped to x_T via the probability flow ODE and connected via spherical linear interpolation (slerp) to preserve Euclidean norm. A key contribution is model adaptation using LoRA with a heuristic bottleneck constraint: the LoRA rank is automatically set by a relative perceptual path diversity (rPPD) score that measures the ratio of average consecutive LPIPS to endpoint LPIPS along a baseline morph, balancing directness (suppressed by larger rank) against fidelity (degraded by mode collapse at large rank). A separate small-rank LoRA corrects unconditional bias introduced by parameter sharing during fine-tuning. Finally, perceptually-uniform sampling uses binary search over α to produce a sequence where each consecutive pair has a target LPIPS difference, ensuring visually smooth transitions.

## Results

On the shared 25-image-pair evaluation set with Wang & Golland (2023), IMPUS achieves a PPL_{1/16} of 92.58 and FID of 148.45, substantially outperforming the baseline (143.25 and 198.16 respectively). On benchmark datasets (CelebA-HQ, AFHQ, LSUN-Church), the full IMPUS pipeline with textual inversion and heuristic LoRA adaptation reduces total LPIPS by ~20–30% and endpoint reconstruction error by ~50% compared to inversion-only baselines, while also lowering FID (e.g., 43.59 vs 52.76 on CelebA-HQ). The heuristic rPPD-based rank selection consistently outperforms fixed ranks (4 or 32) on internet-collected image pairs in terms of the directness–fidelity trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Wang & Golland (2023), IMPUS wins on both quantitative metrics (PPL_{1/16}: 92.58 vs 143.25; FID: 148.45 vs 198.16) and qualitatively produces more consistent, artifact-free intermediate frames without requiring pose estimation or fine-grained text prompts. Compared to DiffMorph (Nri, 2022) and Automatic Image Morphing (Jankovics, 2020), IMPUS generates noticeably smoother and more photorealistic morphing sequences, whereas both baselines produce blurred or unrealistic intermediates. Against Neural Crossbreed (Park et al., 2020), a GAN-based method, IMPUS generalizes to arbitrary image pairs while Neural Crossbreed fails on unseen data due to its reliance on GAN training distribution. The paper also demonstrates that IMPUS's directness advantage (smaller total LPIPS and max LPIPS to nearest endpoint) enables downstream applications like video interpolation and controlled editing magnitude that the baselines cannot support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the probability flow ODE formulation of DDIMs to deterministically map images to Gaussian latent states, extends textual inversion with a common-prompt initialization strategy to enable linear interpolation in CLIP embedding space, and repurposes LoRA (originally designed for LLM fine-tuning) as a mechanism to control morphing directness via a heuristic rank constraint. It departs from Wang & Golland (2023), the closest prior diffusion-based interpolation work, by showing that model adaptation on the endpoint pair—rather than careful prompt engineering or auxiliary pose guidance—is the key to suppressing indirect transitions and ghosting artifacts. The perceptually-uniform sampling via binary search on LPIPS is a practical addition not present in prior morphing or interpolation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IMPUS provides the first fully automatic, open-world diffusion morphing pipeline that simultaneously satisfies smoothness, realism, and directness without human intervention, which opens practical applications in video interpolation, data augmentation, and model explainability. The insight that a low-rank bottleneck (LoRA rank) acts as a natural control knob for the directness–fidelity trade-off, guided by the rPPD score, is a reusable design principle for other conditional generation tasks. However, the method still produces abrupt transitions for semantically distant image pairs and inherits known diffusion-model failure modes (e.g., incorrect hands), and the evaluation relies on a single main baseline (Wang & Golland) with limited dataset diversity, leaving open whether the approach scales to highly heterogeneous image pairs or larger model families.
