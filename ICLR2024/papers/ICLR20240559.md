## Problem

Existing backdoor identification methods either require access to clean data for threshold calibration or demand manual detection thresholds, making them impractical for real-world deployment. The vanilla Scaled Prediction Consistency (SPC) signature, while promising, suffers from trigger vanishing at high scales and false positives from structurally stable clean objects, and still requires a manual threshold. This paper addresses the underexplored problem of automatically identifying backdoor samples within a poisoned dataset without any clean data (P1) or predefined detection threshold (P2).

## Input / Output

The method takes a poisoned training dataset (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet200 at 224×224) and a pre-trained ResNet-18 model, requiring no clean reference set or knowledge of the poisoning ratio. It produces a per-sample MSPC loss score whose sign automatically determines classification (positive = backdoor, negative = clean), eliminating any manual threshold. An intermediate byproduct is an optimized pixel mask that localizes the effective trigger region within backdoor samples.

## Method

The core contribution is the Mask-Aware SPC (MSPC) loss, which applies a learned mask and a small linear shift τ to the input before scaling, preserving trigger visibility at high scales while suppressing background or object features that cause false positives in vanilla SPC. The identification problem is formulated as a bi-level optimization: the lower level optimizes the mask m to minimize KL divergence between original and scaled-masked predictions for backdoor samples (with an L1 sparsity penalty), while the upper level assigns binary labels w to minimize MSPC loss for clean samples. The combinatorial mask is relaxed to [0,1] and solved via alternating optimization, where the upper level trivially sets w_i=1 if the MSPC loss is positive and w_i=0 otherwise. This yields an automatic threshold at zero MSPC loss, satisfying both practical constraints P1 and P2 simultaneously.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed MSPC method achieves an average AUROC of 0.8941 across 8 backdoor attacks, outperforming SPC (0.6563), ABL (0.7937), SD-FCT (0.7212), and STRIP (0.8457). On ImageNet200, the method reaches 0.9908 average AUROC, surpassing all baselines. Against the adaptive AdapBlend attack (poisoning ratio 0.3%), the method achieves 0.9046 AUROC while all baselines fail (best baseline: ABL at 0.4521). The method also achieves near-perfect TPR (~0.99) with low FPR (0.10–0.16) using its built-in zero threshold, satisfying the no-clean-data (P1) and no-detection-threshold (P2) practical constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SPC (Guo et al., 2023), the proposed MSPC method consistently outperforms across all three datasets, with the largest gap on CIFAR-10 (0.8941 vs. 0.6563 average AUROC) and particularly dramatic improvements on Blend (0.9526 vs. 0.5032) and AdapBlend (0.9046 vs. 0.3313). Compared to ABL (Li et al., 2021), the method wins on CIFAR-10 (0.8941 vs. 0.7937) and ImageNet200 (0.9908 vs. 0.9437), with ABL only competitive on individual CIFAR-10 attacks like BadNet. Against SD-FCT (Chen et al., 2022), the method dominates across all datasets (e.g., 0.9908 vs. 0.6795 on ImageNet200). STRIP (Gao et al., 2019) achieves slightly higher average AUROC on Tiny-ImageNet (0.9972 vs. 0.9961) and wins on several individual CIFAR-10 attacks, but it requires a clean validation set to set its detection threshold, violating the P1 constraint. Meta-SIFT (Zeng et al., 2022) is compared qualitatively in Figure 5: while it accurately identifies small fractions of clean samples, its performance degrades sharply at 80–100% selection, whereas the proposed method maintains stable performance across all fractions, highlighting that identifying the minority (poisoned) samples is fundamentally harder than identifying the majority (clean) ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Scaled Prediction Consistency (SPC) signature from Guo et al. (2023), which exploits the scale-invariance of backdoor triggers, and on the bi-level data-splitting optimization framework from Meta-SIFT (Zeng et al., 2022). It departs from SPC by introducing a mask-aware variant (MSPC) that addresses SPC's failure modes—trigger vanishing at high scales and spurious scale-invariance in clean samples—drawing on the minimal-mask concept from Cognitive Distillation (Huang et al., 2023). The key positioning is that no prior backdoor identification method simultaneously satisfies both practical constraints of requiring no clean data (P1) and no manual detection threshold (P2); this paper is the first to achieve both by formulating identification as a hierarchical splitting problem with an automated zero-loss threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the clean-data and manual-threshold assumptions makes backdoor identification deployable in realistic settings where users receive third-party datasets without ground-truth labels or knowledge of poisoning ratios. The mask-aware SPC loss provides a principled fix for a well-known SPC limitation (trigger pixel vanishing under scaling), and the bi-level formulation yields an automatic decision boundary. However, the method's poor performance on DFST (a deep feature-space attack) highlights that current scale-invariance-based signatures may be fundamentally insufficient for attacks that operate in latent space rather than pixel space, leaving an open question about whether mask-based or entirely different signatures are needed for such threats.
