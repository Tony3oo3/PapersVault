## Problem

Existing diffusion-based text-driven image editing methods apply a fixed number of DDIM inversion steps uniformly across all editing pairs in an image. The paper shows that the optimal inversion step varies significantly per editing pair depending on editing difficulty, and using a single step for multiple pairs causes concept mismatch (e.g., a tiger's texture bleeding into a hat) or poor editing (objects left unedited). This gap is especially acute in multi-object editing scenarios where different objects require different levels of inversion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image, an original text prompt describing the image, and a target text prompt specifying the desired edit. It uses Grounded-SAM to segment editing regions and CLIP (base model for search, large model for evaluation) to score candidates. The output is a single edited image aligned with the target prompt while preserving non-editing regions. The entire pipeline is training-free and runs on Stable Diffusion v1.4 with 50-step DDIM inversion; multi-GPU parallelization reduces inference from ~298s to ~155s per image.

## Method

OIR introduces a search metric S = 0.5·(S_e + S_ne) that jointly measures editability (CLIP alignment between the masked editing region and the target prompt) and fidelity (negative MSE of the non-editing region against the original image) across all candidate images produced by denoising each intermediate DDIM inversion latent. The inversion step maximizing S is selected as optimal for that editing pair. For multi-object editing, OIR disassembles the task: each editing pair is inverted to its own optimal step and denoised independently with a guided prompt (original prompt with only that pair's target substituted), preventing cross-object concept mismatch. The separately denoised latents are then spliced into the non-editing region's latent at a reassembly step (~20% of total steps), followed by a short re-inversion (~20% of steps) to re-introduce noise and a final denoising pass guided by the full target prompt, which smooths region boundaries and restores global coherence.

## Results

On the OIRBench multi-object editing dataset, OIR achieves a CLIP score of 30.28, surpassing the previous best (Plug-and-Play at 28.45) by 1.83 points and Null-text Inversion (28.02) by 2.26 points. On structural similarity (MS-SSIM), OIR scores 0.653, marginally behind DiffEdit (0.736) and PNP (0.658), though the authors note these methods sometimes leave objects unedited, artificially inflating similarity scores. In a user study with 58 participants, OIR was preferred by 66.7% of raters over all baselines combined. The method also achieves the best LPIPS among the feature-injection baselines (0.329 vs. 0.359 for PNP and 0.357 for NTI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OIR clearly outperforms all four baselines on CLIP score (text-image alignment), the primary editing-quality metric: 30.28 vs. PNP (28.45), NTI (28.02), SD Inpainting (26.87), and DiffEdit (20.98). On MS-SSIM, OIR (0.653) is slightly behind DiffEdit (0.736) and PNP (0.658), but the authors argue this is misleading because DiffEdit and PNP sometimes fail to edit certain objects, leaving them unchanged and thus boosting the similarity score. On LPIPS, OIR (0.329) beats PNP (0.359) and NTI (0.357) but trails DiffEdit (0.195), again reflecting DiffEdit's tendency to under-edit. In the user study, OIR was the top choice for 66.7% of 58 participants, substantially ahead of all baselines. The paper also shows that OIR avoids concept mismatch (e.g., a tiger's texture bleeding into a hat) that plagues PNP and NTI when multiple editing pairs share a single inversion step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DDIM Inversion (Song et al., 2020) as its core mechanism but departs from the dominant paradigm in text-driven diffusion editing, where methods like PNP, Null-text Inversion, and Prompt-to-Prompt rely on feature injection or trained null-text embeddings at a fixed inversion depth. The key conceptual departure is reframing the editing problem as a search over inversion steps rather than a feature-manipulation problem: the authors show that the optimal inversion depth varies per editing pair and that a simple CLIP-score-plus-MSE metric can automatically select it, eliminating the need for attention injection or auxiliary training. The disassembly-then-reassembly strategy with re-inversion for global coherence is a new procedural contribution layered on top of this search paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single fixed inversion step is fundamentally suboptimal for multi-object editing challenges a widely adopted assumption in the diffusion editing literature and suggests a simpler, training-free alternative to feature-injection methods. The OIRBench dataset (208 single-object, 100 multi-object pairs) provides a useful benchmark for a scenario (simultaneous multi-object editing) that prior work has under-evaluated. However, the method incurs significant inference overhead (~298s single-GPU vs. ~224s for PNP), is validated only on Stable Diffusion v1.4 with small datasets, and its extension to video editing or other diffusion backbones remains unverified. The search-metric paradigm is also shown to be composable with existing methods (e.g., NTI), suggesting broader applicability beyond the specific OIR pipeline.
