## Problem

Existing decoding methods for autoregressive language models address only one of two distributional mis-specifications (unreliable long tail or degenerated mode) and cannot simultaneously align generated texts with human texts across multiple aspects such as repetition, coherence, diversity, and information content. The paper asks whether there exists a principled decoding framework that guarantees holistic metric alignment with human references while provably improving the underlying distributional fit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241080/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short text prefix (e.g., a few sentences) and a pre-trained autoregressive LM (GPT-2 XL or OPT-6.7B) as input, and produces a single generated continuation of fixed total length T. The decoding is governed by nine evaluation metrics (sequence-level and token-level repetition, coherence via SimCSE embeddings, n-gram diversity, and entropy-rate information content) whose expected values under the decoding distribution must match those computed on human reference texts. At inference time, M=25 candidate continuations are drawn in parallel from a temperature-modulated proposal (τ=0.97–0.99) and one is resampled based on energy-based importance weights.

## Method

DAEMON formulates decoding as a constrained optimization: minimize the reverse KL divergence D_KL(q‖p_θ) from the LM distribution subject to equality constraints that the expected values of K evaluation metrics under q match those under the human text distribution p_d. The reverse KL enforces mode-seeking behavior, restricting the decoding distribution to the support of the LM. The optimization admits a closed-form solution p_{θ,μ}(x) ∝ p_θ(x) exp(−μᵀf(x)), an energy-based model where the sequence-level energy is a linear combination of metric scores with coefficients μ determined by the constraints. Coefficients are estimated once on a development set via Weighted Importance Sampling and iterative gradient descent on the RMS relative error. Tractable sampling from the globally normalized EBM is achieved through Sampling-Importance-Resampling: M continuations are drawn from the LM proposal, weighted by exp(−E_μ), and one is resampled. The paper further proves that the optimal solution's support contains the human text support and that its perplexity on human texts is strictly lower than the original LM's, providing a distribution-level quality guarantee absent in heuristic decoding methods.

## Results

On the Wikipedia domain with GPT-2 XL, DAEMON achieves a MAUVE score of 88.1 (vs. 83.3 for the best baseline CS), the lowest sequence-level 4-gram repetition (SR-4 = 0.42, closest to the human reference of 0.48), and near-human coherence (62.5 vs. 62.3) and information content (e^ENT = 22.8 vs. 23.2). On the News domain, DAEMON reaches MAUVE 97.4 and SR-4 = 0.18 (human: 0.29). With OPT-6.7B, DAEMON similarly leads on MAUVE (90.7 Wikipedia, 97.2 News) and SR-4 (0.38, 0.25) while maintaining human-level coherence and diversity. Perplexity improves consistently (e.g., 23.1→22.0 for GPT-2 XL on Wikipedia), confirming the theoretical guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAEMON outperforms all six baselines (Greedy, Top-k, Nucleus, Typical, CD, CS) on MAUVE across both domains and model scales, with the largest gap over CS (88.1 vs. 83.3 on Wikipedia/GPT-2 XL; 97.4 vs. 95.7 on News/GPT-2 XL). On sequence-level repetition (SR-4), DAEMON achieves the lowest values (0.42 Wikipedia, 0.18 News for GPT-2 XL), beating Typical (0.81/0.42) and Nucleus (1.19/0.80) which are the closest competitors. CD achieves the highest coherence (68.7 Wikipedia) but at the cost of the worst diversity (85.9) and high token-level repetition (TR-32 = 28.2), while DAEMON matches human-level coherence (62.5 vs. 62.3) without sacrificing diversity (92.2 vs. 92.5 human). Typical decoding over-emphasizes diversity (DIV = 94.5) but suffers the lowest coherence (54.9) and highest information content (e^ENT = 30.1 vs. 23.2 human), indicating topic drift. In human evaluation, DAEMON is significantly preferred over CD, CS, Nucleus, and Typical on all three criteria (fluency, coherence, informativeness) with p < 0.005 for most comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAEMON builds on the information projection framework from information geometry (Csiszár & Matus, 2000) and the Energy-Based Model literature for text generation (Deng et al., 2020; Parshakova et al., 2019), but departs from prior EBM approaches by using a strong autoregressive LM as a proposal distribution with Sampling-Importance-Resampling rather than MCMC or a separately learned AR policy. It also contrasts with sampling-based methods (Top-k, Nucleus, Typical) that heuristically truncate the LM distribution, and with search-based methods (Contrastive Decoding, Contrastive Search) that maximize contrastive objectives, by formulating decoding as a principled constrained optimization with reverse KL and multi-metric matching constraints. The use of reverse KL (mode-seeking) rather than forward KL is a deliberate departure from standard MLE training, justified by the need to avoid the unreliable long tail of LM distributions during decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAEMON provides the first decoding framework with a provable perplexity improvement guarantee over the base LM, moving beyond heuristic trade-offs between quality and diversity. The ability to simultaneously align multiple aspects (repetition, coherence, diversity, information content) without hard trade-offs addresses a long-standing limitation of prior methods. Key limitations include the computational cost of computing sequence-level metrics during inference, the finite-M approximation error in SIR, and the restriction to equality constraints. Open questions include extending to inequality/structural constraints, incorporating human-value metrics beyond text quality, and developing more efficient sampling from the EBM to reduce the M=25 candidate overhead.
