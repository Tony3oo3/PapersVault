## Problem

Diffusion approximation of Markov chains is a central tool for analyzing sampling, optimization, and boosting algorithms, but existing results require Gaussian, state-independent noise and typically impose convexity or dissipativity assumptions on the drift. This paper asks: for the broadest possible class of homogeneous Markov chains (Ito chains) with arbitrary non-Gaussian, state-dependent noise and no convexity/dissipativity, can one still obtain a W₂-distance bound between the chain and its corresponding Ito diffusion, and at what rate?

## Input / Output

The framework takes a discrete-time Markov chain specified by a Lipschitz drift b, an unbiased noise function ε with unit covariance (only a CLT-type condition on its S-batch sums is required), a uniformly elliptic covariance coefficient σ, optional drift bias δ_k and covariance shift Δ_k, a step size η ∈ (0,1], and an initial state x₀. It produces an explicit W₂-distance bound between the law of X_k and the law of the corresponding Ito diffusion Z_{kη}, yielding a convergence rate O(η^θ + η^{θ/2+γ/4}) where θ = min{α, ((γ+1)(1+χ₀)+(γ+β)(1−χ₀))/4} depends on the smoothness parameters α, β, the noise-type indicator χ₀, and the step-size exponent γ (γ=0 for sampling, γ=1 for SGD). The bound holds uniformly over any fixed time horizon T, with exponentially growing constants in T that are unavoidable without dissipativity.

## Method

The proof proceeds through a four-stage coupling chain. First, a window coupling constructs an auxiliary Gaussian-noise chain Y by batching S steps of the non-Gaussian noise ε into a CLT Gaussian approximation ζ, and injecting a coupling term Lη̄(Y−X_{Sk}) that emulates the contraction of strong monotonicity without requiring it, yielding a tempered bound Δ_t^S = O(η^{2θ}) after optimizing S. Second, naive interpolation embeds Y into a continuous diffusion with a 'ladder' drift that mimics the discrete chain, giving a W₂ gap of O(η̄^{1+γ}R²(kη)). Third, a covariance-corrected interpolation introduces a second auxiliary diffusion Z^Y with matching covariance σ to eliminate the mismatched covariance term Σ*_t, reducing the problem to a drift-only difference. Fourth, a new one-time Girsanov formula for mixed Ito/adapted (non-Markovian) coefficients bounds the KL-divergence between Z and Z^Y, which is then converted to W₂ via a Bolley–Villani entropy inequality with an exponential-integrability constant C_W = O(η^{γ/2}e^{O(t)}) that cancels the η^{−γ} factor from Girsanov. The key novelty is that no convexity, dissipativity, or Gaussianity assumption is needed, and the W₂ rate is strictly stronger than the W₁ rates in prior work.

## Results

The paper establishes a general W₂ discretization error bound of O(η^θ + η^{θ/2 + γ/4}) for the Ito chain, where θ depends on the noise regularity parameters α, β and the step-size exponent γ. For SGLD with Gaussian noise, the rate is O(η^{1/4}), matching the best known result; for SGLD with arbitrary (non-Gaussian) noise, the same O(η^{1/4}) rate is obtained for the first time. For SGD with Gaussian noise, the paper achieves O(η^{3/4}) in W₂ (implying O(η^{1/4}) in W₁), substantially improving upon the O(η^{1/8}) W₁ rate of Cheng et al. (2020). For gradient boosting (SGB) and Langevin boosting (SGLB), the paper provides the first discretization error estimates: O(η^{1/2}) and O(η^{1/4}) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two primary baselines. Against Muzellec et al. (2020) for SGLD with Gaussian noise, the paper matches the O(η^{1/4}) W₂ rate exactly, confirming that the more general framework (allowing non-Gaussian, state-dependent noise) does not degrade the rate in the Gaussian special case. Against Cheng et al. (2020) for SGD, the paper achieves a strictly stronger guarantee: O(η^{3/4}) in W₂ for Gaussian noise versus O(η^{1/8}) in W₁, and O(η^{1/4}) in W₁ for arbitrary noise versus O(η^{1/8}) in W₁. The W₂ guarantee is strictly stronger than W₁ (W₂ convergence implies W₁ convergence but not vice versa), making the improvement particularly meaningful for quadratic-growth potentials like MSE. For SGB and SGLB, no prior discretization error estimates existed, so the paper's O(η^{1/2}) and O(η^{1/4}) rates are pioneering results with no direct baseline to compare against.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion approximation framework for Markov chains (Skorokhod, 1963; Raginsky et al., 2017) by introducing a unified Ito chain that subsumes Langevin dynamics, SGD, stochastic gradient boosting, and their variants under a single Euler-Maruyama discretization. It departs from the dominant assumption in prior work (Raginsky et al., 2017; Dalalyan, 2017; Cheng et al., 2020) that noise is Gaussian and state-independent, instead allowing arbitrary non-Gaussian, state-dependent noise with only a CLT-type condition. The window coupling technique and the new non-Markovian Girsanov theorem are the key technical departures, enabling analysis without the convexity or dissipativity assumptions that underpin most existing results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework provides the first W₂ discretization error bounds for SGD and SGLB with non-Gaussian noise, and improves the SGD rate from O(η^{1/8}) in W₁ (Cheng et al., 2020) to O(η^{3/4}) in W₂. The removal of dissipativity/convexity assumptions broadens applicability to genuinely non-convex ML problems (neural networks, GANs, adversarial training) where prior theory was inapplicable. However, the exponential dependence on the time horizon T limits use for sampling from invariant measures, and the uniform ellipticity requirement on σ remains a restriction compared to degenerate-diffusion analyses (Li et al., 2019a). Open questions include whether the window coupling idea can be adapted to handle non-uniformly elliptic diffusion coefficients or to yield uniform-in-time bounds.
