## Problem

Learning representations that encode long-term temporal dependencies in time-series data typically requires large amounts of on-policy data, since the frequency of long-horizon events decreases with the time scale. Prior contrastive predictive coding methods (e.g., MC InfoNCE for goal-conditioned RL) estimate the discounted state occupancy measure via Monte Carlo rollouts, making them sample-inefficient and unable to reuse data from different policies. This paper asks whether a temporal difference version of the InfoNCE loss can be derived to enable off-policy, sample-efficient estimation of future state visitation probabilities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations (low-dimensional vectors) or 64×64 RGB images, a goal state, and an action sampled from the current goal-conditioned policy. It produces ℓ2-normalized contrastive representations of dimension 16 (via 4-layer MLPs with 512 hidden units), a goal-conditioned policy network, and an implicit estimate of the discounted state occupancy measure encoded in the inner product between state-action and future-state representations. A notable implementation detail is that the loss operates on N×N logit matrices (N batch size), yielding N² negative examples per update rather than the N negatives in standard contrastive losses.

## Method

The core contribution is a temporal difference estimator for the InfoNCE loss, derived by (1) decomposing the MC InfoNCE objective via the recursive Bellman relationship of the discounted state occupancy measure into a next-state term and a future-state term, and (2) replacing the intractable expectation over the target policy's occupancy measure with importance weights computed as the softmax of the current critic (with stop-gradient). The resulting TD InfoNCE loss is equivalent to MC InfoNCE at the optimal critic but can be trained off-policy on arbitrary transition data. The method is shown to be a non-parametric successor representation: the learned inner-product critic converges to the true occupancy measure under a new InfoNCE Bellman operator that is a γ-contraction. For goal-conditioned RL, the policy is trained by maximizing the cross-entropy between the critic's prediction for the commanded goal and the batch of sampled future states, alternating with critic updates in an actor-critic loop.

## Results

On the online Fetch Robotics benchmark, TD InfoNCE achieves a 2× median improvement in success rate over the strongest baseline (QRL) on the most challenging tasks (pick & place and slide, both state and image), and is the only method achieving non-negligible success on image-based pick & place and slide. On offline D4RL AntMaze, TD InfoNCE outperforms most baselines on 5 out of 6 tasks, with the largest margins on medium-play-v2 (86.8 vs. 76.5 for QRL) and medium-diverse-v2 (82.0 vs. 73.4 for QRL). In tabular gridworld experiments, TD InfoNCE is 1500× more sample efficient than Monte Carlo InfoNCE (6.5×10³ vs. 10⁷ transitions) and ~20× more sample efficient than the successor representation (5.2×10⁵ vs. 10⁷ transitions). On stochastic variants of the Fetch tasks, TD InfoNCE maintains high success rates while QRL's performance degrades significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QRL (Wang et al., 2023), the strongest goal-conditioned RL baseline, TD InfoNCE wins on 5 of 6 offline AntMaze tasks (e.g., 91.7 vs. 80.1 on umaze-diverse-v2, 86.8 vs. 76.5 on medium-play-v2) but loses on large-play-v2 (47.0 vs. 52.9); on online Fetch tasks, TD InfoNCE achieves a 2× median improvement on the hardest tasks and is the only method with non-negligible success on image-based pick & place and slide. Against Contrastive RL (Eysenbach et al., 2022, MC InfoNCE), TD InfoNCE wins on all offline tasks (e.g., +13% on medium-play-v2) and is 1500× more sample efficient in tabular settings. Against GCBC (Ding et al., 2019 / Emmons et al., 2021), TD InfoNCE achieves 1.4× higher scores on average across AntMaze tasks. Against DT (Chen et al., 2021), TD InfoNCE retains a 3.8× higher score on average, with DT collapsing to near-zero on medium and large tasks. Against IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021), IQL edges out TD InfoNCE only on umaze-v2 (87.5 vs. 84.9) while TD InfoNCE dominates on all other tasks. Against TD3+BC (Fujimoto et al., 2021), TD InfoNCE wins by large margins on all tasks (e.g., 86.8 vs. 10.6 on medium-play-v2). In tabular experiments, TD InfoNCE is 130× more sample efficient than C-Learning (Eysenbach et al., 2020) and ~20× more sample efficient than the successor representation (Dayan, 1993), while also being applicable to continuous state spaces where the latter is not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper derives a temporal difference (TD) version of the InfoNCE contrastive loss, bridging two previously separate lines of work: contrastive RL methods that estimate the discounted state occupancy measure via Monte Carlo sampling (Eysenbach et al., 2022) and the successor representation framework (Dayan, 1993) that uses Bellman-style recursion. The key departure from prior contrastive RL is replacing the binary NCE loss (as in C-Learning, Eysenbach et al., 2020) with a categorical softmax loss and introducing an importance-weighted TD backup, yielding a non-parametric successor representation that operates in continuous state spaces. The resulting TD InfoNCE estimator is shown to be equivalent to the MC InfoNCE estimator at the optimal critic, and its convergence is proven via a new InfoNCE Bellman operator that is a γ-contraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1500× sample efficiency gain over MC InfoNCE and 20× over successor representation in tabular settings, combined with the demonstrated ability to stitch trajectories and find shortcuts in skewed datasets, establishes TD contrastive learning as a principled path to data-efficient goal-conditioned RL in continuous spaces. The robustness to stochastic dynamics (where QRL degrades significantly) addresses a practical limitation of prior state-of-the-art methods. Open questions include whether the TD benefits transfer to non-control domains (NLP, audio, video) where MC InfoNCE has already been applied, whether the convergence proof can be tightened to avoid the sum-to-expectation modification, and how the method compares to other off-policy evaluation techniques for arbitrary reward functions.
