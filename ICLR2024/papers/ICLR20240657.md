## Problem

3D medical imaging lacks a large, per-voxel annotated dataset comparable to ImageNet, making supervised pre-training for 3D segmentation infeasible at scale. Existing 3D pre-trained models rely on self-supervised methods or small annotated datasets, leaving open the question of whether supervised pre-training on a sufficiently large 3D annotated corpus can transfer better to segmentation tasks than self-supervised alternatives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240657/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D abdominal CT volumes (sourced from 88 hospitals with varying contrast phases, reconstruction kernels, and scanner types) and produces per-voxel segmentation masks for 25 anatomical structures plus 7 tumor types during pre-training, and fine-tuned outputs for novel classes (muscles, cardiac structures, vertebrae) or fine-grained pancreatic tumor classification (PDAC, Cyst, PanNET). The pre-training corpus spans 2,789,975 annotated images and 251,323 organ/tumor masks, with over 272.7B voxels annotated in total.

## Method

The paper constructs AbdomenAtlas 1.1 (9,262 CT volumes with per-voxel annotations of 25 anatomical structures and pseudo-annotations of 7 tumor types) via an interactive AI-radiologist annotation loop, then trains a suite of supervised pre-trained models (SuPreM) using multi-organ segmentation as the pretext task across U-Net, SegResNet, and Swin UNETR backbones. The key insight is that supervised pre-training on segmentation is dramatically more data- and computation-efficient than self-supervised pre-training: a model trained on just 21 CT volumes (672 masks, 40 GPU hours) matches the transfer performance of a self-supervised model trained on 5,050 CT volumes (1,152 GPU hours), and fine-tuning SuPreM requires 50% fewer target-task annotations. The learned features generalize directly to external datasets (OOD performance matching or exceeding IID baselines) and to novel classes and cross-task tumor classification without architectural changes.

## Results

On TotalSegmentator v1, SuPreM U-Net achieves 92.1% DSC for organ segmentation, 95.4% for muscle, and 92.2% for cardiac structures, outperforming the best prior pre-trained U-Net (Zhang et al., 2021) by 2.8, 1.6, and 3.1 points respectively. On the proprietary dataset, SuPreM U-Net reaches 90.8% organ, 76.2% gastro, and 70.5% cardiac DSC, surpassing the best Swin UNETR baseline (Liu et al., 2023) by 1.7, 1.6, and 2.9 points. In terms of efficiency, a SuPreM model trained on only 21 CT volumes (672 masks, 40 GPU hours) matches the transfer performance of a self-supervised model trained on 5,050 CT volumes (1,152 GPU hours), representing 99.6% fewer data and 96.5% less computation. Fine-tuning SuPreM on 512 annotated volumes achieves parity with self-supervised models fine-tuned on 1,024 volumes, halving annotation cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuPreM U-Net outperforms all five U-Net-family baselines across every metric: it beats the strongest prior U-Net (Zhang et al., 2021 DoDNet) by 2.8 DSC on organ, 1.6 on muscle, and 3.1 on cardiac (TotalSegmentator), and by 5.1, 3.5, and 32.2 on organ, gastro, and cardiac (proprietary). Against the self-supervised Swin UNETR (Tang et al., 2022), SuPreM Swin UNETR gains 2.0 DSC on organ and 1.0 on cardiac (TotalSegmentator), and 2.5, 3.4, and 30.9 on organ, gastro, and cardiac (proprietary). The CLIP-driven model (Liu et al., 2023), the strongest Swin UNETR baseline, is surpassed by SuPreM Swin UNETR by 1.6 DSC on organ and 0.9 on cardiac (TotalSegmentator), and by 1.3, 1.3, and 2.2 on organ, gastro, and cardiac (proprietary). In direct-inference external validation (Table 3), SuPreM's OOD performance matches or exceeds IID models trained specifically on each target dataset: on DAP Atlas it achieves 93.3% average DSC vs. 93.6% for Jaus et al. (IID), and on the proprietary dataset 86.7% vs. 86.1% for Wang et al. (IID), with statistically significant gains on 7 of 10 individual organs. On 66 novel classes (Table 4), supervised SuPreM consistently outperforms the self-supervised Tang et al. (2022) model, with the largest margins on fine-grained pancreatic tumor subtypes (PanNET: +10.5 DSC, Cyst: +7.9 DSC) and vertebrae (average +2.7 DSC). The only comparisons where SuPreM does not significantly outperform a baseline are a few individual classes (e.g., humerus left, trachea, C1 vertebrae) where the gap is within noise (P > 0.05).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ImageNet-style supervised pre-training paradigm to 3D medical imaging by constructing the first large-scale, fully per-voxel-annotated CT dataset (AbdomenAtlas 1.1) and training segmentation backbones (U-Net, SegResNet, Swin UNETR) on it. It deliberately positions against the dominant self-supervised pre-training line of work in 3D medical imaging (Tang et al. 2022, Chen et al. 2019b, Xie et al. 2020), arguing that when annotations are available, supervised pre-training with segmentation as the pretext task is more data- and compute-efficient than mask image modeling or contrastive learning. The dataset itself composes 17 existing public CT collections (FLARE, KiTS, LiTS, AbdomenCT-1K, etc.) with unified multi-organ annotations, filling the gap the authors identify between the scale of ImageNet and the fragmented, partially-annotated 3D medical datasets that existed prior to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AbdomenAtlas 1.1 (9,262 volumes, 25 structures, 88 hospitals) is the largest publicly committed per-voxel-annotated 3D CT collection to date and is likely to become a standard pre-training and benchmarking resource for 3D medical segmentation. The practical efficiency finding—that 21 annotated volumes with supervised pre-training match 5,050 unlabeled volumes with self-supervised pre-training—could substantially lower the barrier for groups without massive compute budgets. Open questions include whether the supervised advantage persists as self-supervised methods scale to billions of images, whether the results generalize beyond CT to MRI or other modalities, and whether the 0.01%-of-US-CT-volumes scale is sufficient for truly robust foundation-model behavior across all anatomical regions and pathologies.
