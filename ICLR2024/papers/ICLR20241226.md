## Problem

Existing 3D masked autoencoder methods (PointMAE, MaskSurfel, etc.) reconstruct the Cartesian positions of masked points as the pretext task, which is fundamentally different from 2D MAE where pixel-level features (e.g., colors) are restored. The authors argue that point location recovery is inessential and diverts the encoder toward capturing irregular point distributions rather than learning discriminative surface geometry, limiting downstream performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241226/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (2048 randomly sampled points from ShapeNet shapes) as input, applies patch-based masking (FPS with K=128 patches of k=32 nearest neighbors, 60% mask ratio), and feeds only unmasked points to the encoder. The decoder outputs per-point surface normals (unit 3D vectors) and surface variation (scalar in [0, 1/3]) at each masked query point. The final product is pretrained encoder weights, encoder-agnostic, that transfer to classification, segmentation, and detection tasks.

## Method

The core contribution replaces point-position reconstruction with prediction of two complementary intrinsic features: surface normals (first-order differential property, estimated as the smallest eigenvector of a local covariance matrix) and surface variation (second-order, the ratio of the smallest to total eigenvalues, related to mean curvature). A novel attention-based decoder stacks 4 transformer blocks, each containing a self-attention layer (propagating context among masked query points) followed by a cross-attention layer (querying encoder block feature pairs with block centroids as positional embeddings). This design is encoder-agnostic—supporting ViT-based, PointNet++-like, and sparse CNN encoders—since it only requires block feature pairs as input. The loss combines L2 on predicted normals and L1 on predicted surface variation, and masked point positions are accessible only to the decoder to avoid early positional leakage.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN (the most challenging split PB-T50-RS), MaskFeat3D with PointViT backbone achieves 87.7% accuracy, surpassing the best MAE baseline MaskSurfel (85.7%) by 2.0 points. On ShapeNetPart segmentation, it reaches 86.3% instance mIoU and 84.9% class mIoU, outperforming all prior MAE methods. When paired with the stronger PointNeXt encoder, MaskFeat3D achieves state-of-the-art results across all tasks: 92.7% on ScanObjectNN OBJ-BG, 88.6% on PB-T50-RS, and 87.4% ins. mIoU on ShapeNetPart. In few-shot classification on ModelNet40, MaskFeat3D leads all settings, e.g., 97.1% (5-way 10-shot) and 95.7% (10-way 20-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskFeat3D outperforms all five MAE-based baselines (PointBERT, PointMAE, MaskDiscr, MaskSurfel, and from-scratch PointViT) on every reported metric in Table 1, with the largest margin over PointViT-from-scratch (+11.8 on PB-T50-RS) and the smallest over MaskSurfel (+2.0 on PB-T50-RS). Against supervised baselines in Table 2, MaskFeat3D (PointViT) beats PointNet, PointNet++, PointCNN, DGCNN, MinkowskiNet, PointTransformer, and PointMLP on both ScanObjectNN and ShapeNetPart; it is only marginally behind the stronger PointNeXt supervised model (91.9 vs. 91.7 on OBJ-BG), but MaskFeat3D with the PointNeXt encoder reverses this to 92.7. In few-shot classification (Table 3), MaskFeat3D leads all eight settings, exceeding the closest competitor PointMAE by 0.8–1.1 points (e.g., 97.1 vs. 96.3 on 5-way 10-shot). For 3D object detection (Table 6), MaskFeat3D with CAGroup3D backbone achieves 75.6 AP25 / 62.3 AP50 on ScanNet, surpassing Point-M2AE (66.3 / 48.3) and all contrastive baselines (STRL, RandomRooms, PointContrast, DepthContrast) by 3–17 AP25 points. Overall, the paper reports no comparison where MaskFeat3D loses to a baseline under the same backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the masked autoencoder paradigm from vision (He et al., 2022) to 3D point clouds, following the masking strategy of PointBERT (Yu et al., 2022) but making a key conceptual departure: instead of reconstructing masked point positions as prior 3D MAE methods (PointMAE, MaskSurfel, MaskDiscr) do, it predicts intrinsic surface features (normals and surface variations) at masked points. The feature-prediction idea is inspired by Wei et al. (2022) in vision, who showed that predicting alternative image features outperforms raw pixel reconstruction. The novel attention-based decoder, which uses masked points as queries and encoder block features as keys/values, is explicitly designed to be encoder-agnostic, unlike prior MAE decoders tightly coupled to ViT architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that point position reconstruction is unnecessary—and that high-order surface features are the critical signal for 3D MAE—challenges the default assumption in the field and provides a clearer design principle for future 3D pretraining. The encoder-agnostic decoder makes the pretraining recipe broadly applicable across sparse CNNs, PointNet++-style, and transformer backbones, which is practically valuable. Open questions include which additional 3D features (beyond normals and surface variation) could further improve pretraining, and whether the approach scales effectively to very large, noisy scene-level point clouds beyond the limited ScanNet experiments presented.
