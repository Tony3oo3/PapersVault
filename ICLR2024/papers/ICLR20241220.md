## Problem

Flow-matching generative models (e.g., Rectified Flow) produce high-quality images but require many function evaluations (NFEs) for sampling, and existing distillation methods like reflow demand prohibitive retraining budgets (300K+ iterations, or ~200 A100-GPU-days for 1-step generation on high-resolution data). The paper asks: given a pretrained velocity network and a small NFE budget K, how can we adapt the model to generate high-fidelity images with only modest additional computation?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241220/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained flow-matching velocity network (U-Net/DDPM++ architecture) and a target NFE budget K (typically 4, 6, or 8), and produces a finetuned velocity network that maps a single Gaussian noise sample to a generated image in exactly K Euler steps. Experiments cover 32×32 (CIFAR-10) and 256×256 (CelebA-HQ, LSUN-Church, LSUN-Bedroom, AFHQ-Cat) RGB images. The finetuning requires only ~12,000 iterations (3.5–14 GPU-hours on an RTX A5000), and the stepsize computation itself takes under 2 minutes per dataset.

## Method

BOSS operates in two phases. Phase 1 computes Bellman-optimal non-uniform stepsizes: the interval [0,1] is discretized into K^max=100 anchor timestamps, local truncation errors c_jk are estimated between all pairs via a single batch of Euler trajectories, and a dynamic programming shortest-path algorithm (equivalently, a network-flow integer program) selects exactly K steps that minimize accumulated sampling error. Phase 2 straightens the velocity network by minimizing a new alignment loss that forces v_θ at each optimal timestamp to match the chord direction (average velocity) between consecutive optimal timestamps along the K^max-step reference path, effectively making the K-step Euler trajectory approximate a straight line. The key insight is that optimal stepsizes are non-uniform—smaller at the beginning and end where trajectory curvature is high—and the straightening loss is tailored to this specific schedule rather than a uniform grid, enabling competitive FID with only ~12K finetuning iterations versus 300K+ for standard reflow.

## Results

On the main distillation comparison (6 NFEs), BOSS achieves FID of 18.67 on CelebA-HQ, 17.43 on LSUN-Church, 26.10 on AFHQ-Cat, and 18.45 on LSUN-Bedroom, substantially outperforming both Distill-6-Reflow and Uniform-Reflow from Liu et al. (2022b). On CIFAR-10, BOSS-6 reaches FID 4.80, slightly behind Distill-6-Reflow (4.35) and Uniform-Reflow (4.33). In the training-free setting, Bellman optimal stepsizes reduce FID by up to 42% over uniform Euler (e.g., CelebA-HQ 4 NFE: 92.03 vs. 158.95), and Bellman Heun with 20 NFEs approaches the FID of RK45 requiring 208 NFEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Distill-6-Reflow (Liu et al., 2022b), BOSS-6 wins on 4 of 5 datasets with large margins: FID 18.67 vs. 34.56 on CelebA-HQ, 17.43 vs. 34.52 on LSUN-Church, 26.10 vs. 46.24 on AFHQ-Cat, and 18.45 vs. 41.17 on LSUN-Bedroom, while slightly losing on CIFAR-10 (4.80 vs. 4.35). Against Uniform-Reflow, BOSS-6 wins on all five datasets, with the largest gap on CelebA-HQ (18.67 vs. 43.57, a 2.3× FID improvement). In the training-free comparison, Bellman Heun with 20 NFEs achieves FID within ~1% of SciPy's adaptive RK45 solver, which requires 208 NFEs, demonstrating that the Bellman stepsize schedule recovers most of the quality of a high-budget adaptive solver at roughly 10× fewer function evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOSS builds directly on the Rectified Flow framework (Liu et al., 2022b), which introduced reflow as a straightening procedure for few-step sampling, but departs from it in two key ways: it replaces uniform stepsizes with Bellman-optimal non-uniform stepsizes found via dynamic programming, and it reformulates the straightening loss to align velocity vectors with the optimal schedule rather than a uniform grid. Conceptually, it is closest to Watson et al. (2021), who also used dynamic programming for sampling schedules, but BOSS minimizes local truncation error rather than per-step KL divergence, and adds a subsequent fine-tuning phase. The work also positions itself against concurrent efforts like Li et al. (2023) (evolutionary search for stepsizes) and consistency models (Song et al., 2023), emphasizing its computational efficiency (~10K retraining iterations vs. 300K+ for standard reflow).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the dramatic reduction in retraining cost: BOSS achieves competitive FID with roughly 10,000 iterations (a few GPU-hours on an A5000) versus the 300,000+ iterations or ~200 A100-GPU-days required by standard reflow for comparable quality, making few-step distillation accessible in low-resource settings. The LoRA variant (2% trainable parameters) further lowers the barrier. However, the method still requires an additional training phase (unlike training-free ODE solvers), is validated only on unconditional image generation at up to 256×256 resolution, and the authors acknowledge open questions around guided models and cheaper algorithms for computing Bellman stepsizes at scale.
