## Problem

Standard deep metric learning losses produce highly non-uniform intra-class and inter-class representation structures across test classes, meaning a single fixed distance threshold cannot achieve consistent false accept/reject rates across diverse data distributions. The paper identifies a Pareto frontier in the high-accuracy regime where improving accuracy actually degrades threshold consistency, and shows that post-hoc calibration methods (Platt scaling, conformal prediction) are impractical for open-world retrieval where test classes are unseen at training time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (typically 224×224) through standard backbones (ResNet-50/101 or ViT-S/B/L) to produce 512-dimensional L2-normalized embedding vectors. During training, the TCM regularizer operates on the full n×n cosine similarity matrix within a mini-batch (4 images per class, batch sizes 384–4000), identifying hard pairs via absolute similarity thresholds rather than relative rankings. Evaluation uses 1:1 pairwise L2 distance comparisons between test embeddings, with the OPIS metric computed over a calibration range defined by a target FAR interval (e.g., 1e-2 ≤ FAR ≤ 1e-1).

## Method

The Threshold-Consistent Margin (TCM) loss is an additive regularizer that selectively penalizes hard positive pairs (cosine similarity below margin m⁺) and hard negative pairs (similarity above margin m⁻) using indicator functions, normalizing by the count of hard pairs rather than total pairs. This hard-mining-by-absolute-threshold strategy acts as a 'local inspector' that prevents over-separation and excessive compactness near the decision boundary, encouraging equidistant class centroids and uniform representation compactness across all classes. The paper also introduces the Operating-Point-Inconsistency-Score (OPIS), a variance-based metric that measures the spread of per-class utility curves (F-score combining specificity and sensitivity) within a calibration range, requiring no separate calibration dataset. TCM is combined with any base loss (Smooth-AP, Recall@k Surrogate, ArcFace, ProxyNCA) via simple addition, and the two cosine margins implicitly encode the desired calibration range through the cosine-to-L2-distance bijection for hyperspherical embeddings.

## Results

On iNaturalist-2018 (the largest benchmark), TCM regularized with Recall@k Surrogate on ViT-B/16 achieves R@1 of 84.8% (beating the previous SOTA of 83.9%) while reducing OPIS by 54.1% (from 0.37×10⁻³ to 0.17×10⁻³). Across all four benchmarks, TCM consistently improves recall@1 by 0.7–2.9% and reduces OPIS by up to 77.3% (Cars-196, ResNet50+RS@k). The 10%-OPIS metric (measuring best-vs-worst class disparity) shows even larger relative reductions, up to 73.5% on iNaturalist-2018.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCM is evaluated as a regularizer added to four base DML losses (Smooth-AP, Recall@k Surrogate, ArcFace, ProxyNCA) and consistently improves both accuracy and OPIS over each base loss alone (Table 2: +1.0–1.7% R@1, up to 60.7% OPIS reduction). Against ROADMAP (Ramzi et al., 2021) at batch size 4000, TCM wins decisively: RS@k+TCM achieves R@1 84.8% vs. 79.4% for RS@k+ROADMAP (−4.5% drop) while also achieving lower OPIS (0.17 vs. 0.24). Among alternative regularizers (Table 7), Contrastive loss achieves the lowest OPIS (0.10) but degrades R@1 by 0.3%, whereas TCM simultaneously improves R@1 by 1.7% and reduces OPIS by 48%. With self-supervised pretraining (Table 10), TCM improves OPIS by 6.2–10.9% across CLIP, DINOv2, and Unicom initializations, with the largest accuracy gain (+4.5% R@1) observed under CLIP pretraining. On the open-world split of CUB and Cars (Table 11), TCM reduces OPIS by 45.2% (CUB) and 18.2% (Cars) while maintaining or improving R@1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of deep metric learning losses (Smooth-AP, Recall@k Surrogate, ArcFace, ProxyNCA) and calibration-aware training, but deliberately departs from both. It reframes the closed-set calibration problem (ECE, temperature scaling) as a threshold-consistency problem specific to DML, where similarity scores do not reflect empirical correctness. The closest prior work is OneFace (Liu et al., 2022), which introduced threshold consistency for face recognition but required a separate calibration dataset and was limited to a small number of face domains; TCM generalizes this to open-world image retrieval with arbitrary numbers of classes. The hard-mining strategy draws on FaceNet's semi-hard negative mining but operates on absolute cosine similarity thresholds rather than relative differences, and the additive regularizer design (L_base + L_TCM) distinguishes it from standalone margin-based softmax losses like ArcFace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: commercial image retrieval systems must deploy a single distance threshold across diverse user groups and product categories, and the paper shows this is non-trivial. The Pareto frontier finding—that pushing accuracy higher in the high-accuracy regime actively degrades threshold consistency—challenges the common assumption that better accuracy is uniformly beneficial and suggests practitioners should track OPIS alongside recall@k. Limitations include OPIS's requirement for sufficient per-class samples (ruling out few-shot settings) and TCM's vulnerability to significant distribution shift or label noise. Open questions include whether the cosine margins can be learned end-to-end rather than grid-searched, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-modal retrieval where the distance geometry is less well-behaved.
