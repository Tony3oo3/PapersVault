## Problem

Emergent languages produced by Lewis's signaling game under the conventional objective fail to reproduce two key statistical properties of natural languages: Zipf's Law of Abbreviation (frequent words should be shorter) and Harris's Articulation Scheme (word boundaries should align with branching-entropy spikes). Previous work attributed these failures to missing inductive biases (sender laziness, receiver impatience), but the paper identifies a more fundamental cause: the conventional objective implicitly regularizes against an unnatural prior distribution over messages that favors longer sequences, and this implicitness has obscured the mechanism.

## Input / Output

The sender receives an attribute-value object x, an n_att-tuple of integers each drawn from {1,…,n_val}, with the total object space fixed at 4096 across five (n_att, n_val) configurations ranging from (2,64) to (12,2). The sender generates a variable-length message m over a 9-symbol alphabet (including an eos token) with maximum length 32, and the receiver both reconstructs the object from m and simultaneously acts as a language model scoring m. Unlike prior EC work that restricted to one-character or fixed-length messages, this paper explicitly uses the variable-length setting to study word-length and segmentation properties.

## Method

The paper reformulates Lewis's signaling game as beta-VAE: the object x is the observed variable, the message m is the latent variable, the GRU sender S_φ is the encoder, the GRU receiver R_θ is the decoder, and a learnable GRU-based language model P_θ^prior(M) serves as the prior. The training objective becomes the ELBO, E[log R_θ(x|m)] − β·KL(S_φ(M|x)‖P_θ^prior(M)), where β is annealed from a small value to 1 via REWO to prevent posterior collapse. The key theoretical contribution is showing that the conventional objective J_conv is equivalent to maximizing the log-joint under an implicit uniform prior P_unif^prior, whose induced length distribution P_unif^prior(K=k) ∝ exp(γk) grows with k—making longer messages more probable and directly explaining the anti-ZLA artifact. Replacing this with a learnable prior (a neural LM that approximates the average sender behavior) introduces a surprisal term that creates a competing pressure: the reconstruction term requires the receiver to be 'surprised' at attribute boundaries, while the surprisal term penalizes overall unpredictability, naturally producing branching-entropy spikes at segment boundaries. Optimization uses the stochastic computation graph approach, combining REINFORCE for the sender with backpropagation for the receiver, plus a learned state-dependent baseline for variance reduction.

## Results

The paper evaluates emergent language quality using three criteria (C1–C3) across five (n_att, n_val) configurations: (2,64), (3,16), (4,8), (6,4), (12,2). With the proposed ELBO-based objective J_ours, C1 (n_bou increases monotonically with n_att) and C2 (n_seg increases monotonically with n_val) are both satisfied, while neither baseline achieves either criterion. C3 (Δ_w,c = W-TopSim − C-TopSim > 0) is not met by any method, but J_ours substantially improves both C-TopSim and W-TopSim over both baselines, indicating more meaningful segment structure. Results are reported as figures (threshold = 0 in main text; threshold = 0.25 in appendix) with 16 random seeds per configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed ELBO-based objective with a learnable neural language model prior (J_ours) against two baselines: (BL1) the conventional signaling game objective J_conv plus an entropy regularizer (λ^entreg = 1), and (BL2) an ELBO-based objective using the fixed exponential-length prior P_α^prior (α = log|A|). J_ours is the only method that satisfies both C1 (boundary count increases with number of attributes) and C2 (distinct segment count increases with number of values), confirming that the learnable prior is critical for producing structurally meaningful segments. Both baselines fail C1 and C2, indicating that neither the implicit uniform prior (BL1) nor the simplified exponential-length prior (BL2) provides sufficient inductive pressure for natural segmentation. On C3, all three methods yield Δ_w,c < 0, so none achieves word-level compositionality strictly exceeding character-level compositionality; however, J_ours improves both C-TopSim and W-TopSim substantially over both baselines, suggesting the segments are more semantically grounded even though the absolute gap remains negative. The authors attribute the persistent C3 failure to 'spelling variation' in segments rather than a fundamental limitation of the ELBO formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets Lewis's signaling game—the standard setting in emergent communication—as a beta-VAE, reformulating the conventional cross-entropy objective (Chaabouni et al., 2019) as an ELBO with a KL-weighing parameter β. The key conceptual departure from prior work is making the prior message distribution explicit and learnable (a neural language model) rather than leaving it implicit (uniform or length-penalized), and connecting the KL term to surprisal theory in computational psycholinguistics. It builds on the stochastic computation graph framework (Schulman et al., 2015) and REINFORCE for discrete sequence optimization, and is related to VIB-based games (Tucker et al., 2022) and the MH naming game (Taniguchi et al., 2022), but uniquely addresses variable-length messages and the impact of prior choice on ZLA and HAS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unifying theoretical lens that reframes previously unexplained failures of emergent languages to follow Zipf's law of abbreviation and Harris's articulation scheme as consequences of an implicit, unnatural prior distribution rather than missing inductive biases. This opens concrete design levers (choice of prior, β annealing) for producing more natural emergent languages. Open questions include extending the ELBO formulation to populated games with multiple agents, discrimination/contrastive objectives, and cognitively motivated language models as priors that might yield richer structures like syntax. The empirical validation is limited to att-val objects with GRU agents, so generalization to richer settings remains to be shown.
