## Problem

Existing composed image retrieval methods rely on strict one-to-one metric learning (e.g., InfoNCE, triplet loss) during training, which is misaligned with the one-to-many coarse-grained retrieval process at test time. This pair-based paradigm pushes away multiple valid candidates that share similar attributes, directly compromising recall rate when queries are ambiguous or descriptions are imprecise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242147/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference RGB image and a short text feedback sentence (e.g., 'similar style t-shirt but white logo print') as input. A ResNet-50 image encoder and RoBERTa text encoder extract features, which are fused via a CosMo-style compositor (content modulator + style modulator) into a single composed query vector. At inference, this vector is matched against pre-computed gallery features via cosine similarity to produce a ranked list of retrieved images.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified uncertainty-based training framework with two modules. First, an uncertainty modeling module adds Gaussian noise (drawn from the batch-level feature distribution) to target features, simulating intra-class jittering that converts strict one-to-one matching into one-to-many matching. Second, an uncertainty regularization module adaptively re-weights the InfoNCE loss by the noise standard deviation σ: larger fluctuations reduce the penalty on the first term while the log σ² term acts as a regularizer, so ambiguous queries receive less punishment. The total loss combines the fine-grained InfoNCE loss and the uncertainty regularization with a dynamically annealed weight γ = exp(−γ₀ · epoch/total_epochs), shifting emphasis from coarse to fine-grained matching over training. This prevents the model from over-fitting to strict one-to-one alignment while preserving fine-grained discrimination, yielding +4.03% Recall@50 on FashionIQ over the baseline.

## Results

On FashionIQ, the proposed method achieves 61.39% Recall@50 and 33.17% Recall@10 (average across Dress, Shirt, Toptee subsets), improving over the same-architecture baseline by +4.03% and +4.54% respectively. On Shoes, it reaches 79.84% R@50, 53.63% R@10, and 18.41% R@1 (+3.38% R@50 over baseline). On Fashion200k, it achieves 70.2% R@50, 52.1% R@10, and 21.8% R@1 (+2.40% R@50 over baseline). Notably, a single ResNet-50 backbone with the proposed method (61.39% R@50 on FashionIQ) surpasses CLIP4Cir with 4× ResNet-50 (59.03% R@50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all single-ResNet-50 baselines on FashionIQ: it beats CoSMo (52.31%), DCNet (53.89%), ARTEMIS (50.29%), and FashionViL* (57.04%) on average R@50, reaching 61.39%. It also surpasses multi-model ensembles: CLVC-Net with 2× ResNet-50 (58.41%) and CLIP4Cir with 4× ResNet-50 (59.03%), despite using only a single backbone. On Shoes, the method achieves 79.84% R@50, edging out ARTEMIS (79.31%) and DCNet (79.33%) while substantially exceeding CoSMo (75.64%) and VAL (73.53%). On Fashion200k, it reaches 70.2% R@50, slightly trailing ARTEMIS (70.5%) but surpassing CoSMo (69.3%) and VAL (68.8%), while leading on R@10 (52.1% vs. 51.1% for ARTEMIS). The method is also complementary to existing architectures: adding it to CLIP4Cir* improves R@50 from 56.74% to 59.23% on FashionIQ, and adding it to BLIP on Shoes boosts R@50 from 70.21% to 75.29%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant one-to-one metric learning paradigm (InfoNCE, triplet loss) used in composed image retrieval by introducing a one-to-many matching framework grounded in aleatoric uncertainty. Conceptually, it repurposes the Gaussian-noise-based uncertainty modeling from face recognition and person re-identification (Chang et al., 2020; Dou et al., 2022) and adapts it to the composed retrieval setting, where the noise is applied to target features to simulate intra-class jittering. Architecturally, it follows CosMo's compositor and standard InfoNCE training, positioning its contribution purely at the loss/optimization level rather than the network structure. The uncertainty regularization (a weighted InfoNCE with a log-variance penalty) is a direct extension of the heteroscedastic loss formulation from Kendall & Gal (2017), but applied here to a retrieval contrastive objective rather than a regression or classification head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete training/test misalignment—strict one-to-one supervision during training versus the one-to-many reality of coarse-grained queries at inference—and shows that a simple noise-augmentation-plus-weighted-loss strategy can close part of this gap without architectural changes. Because the method is orthogonal to the backbone and compositor, it can be stacked on top of CLIP4Cir, BLIP, or other composed retrieval systems, making it a practical plug-in for practitioners. Limitations include evaluation only on fashion-domain datasets, a single-round feedback setting, and the noise parameters (w₁, w₂) being tuned empirically. Open questions include whether the uncertainty modeling generalizes to multi-round interactive retrieval, non-fashion domains, or settings where the ambiguity is better captured by structured (e.g., attribute-level) uncertainty rather than isotropic Gaussian noise.
