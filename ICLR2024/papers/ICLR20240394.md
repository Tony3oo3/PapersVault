## Problem

In LDM-based Score Distillation Sampling for high-resolution 3D generation, gradients must backpropagate through the frozen VAE encoder (∂z/∂x) to reach individual image pixels, but this pathway has never been optimized or regulated. The resulting uncontrolled pixel-wise gradients introduce noise that obscures texture-relevant gradient directions, causing blurred or entirely lost texture details—especially severe with SDXL at 1024×1024 resolution. Prior work applied gradient regularization only in the second phase (image-to-3D-model) while ignoring this critical first-phase issue.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a fixed 3D mesh (normalized to [-0.8, 0.8]³) as input, and produces a textured mesh by optimizing a texture field and/or SDF field over 1200 iterations. PGC operates as a drop-in modification to the SDS gradient computation, compatible with any LDM backend (SD 1.5, SD 2.1-base, SDXL) and various 3D pipelines (Fantasia3D, Stable-DreamFusion, Zero123-SDS). The clipping threshold c is set to 0.1 by default, and the method requires no additional training data or architectural changes.

## Method

Pixel-wise Gradient Clipping (PGC) regulates the uncontrolled ∂z/∂x term in LDM-SDS by clipping the magnitude of the stochastic pixel-wise gradient E[x_t − x_{t−1}] along each pixel's own direction when it exceeds a threshold c, while preserving the gradient direction. The preferred variant, PGC-N (clipping-by-norm), computes min(‖E[x_t − x_{t−1}]‖, c) · u_t where u_t is the unit vector, avoiding the direction distortion of clipping-by-value. Theoretical analysis via Jensen's inequality shows that setting c ≈ σ (the bounded variance of the 2D pixel residual) constrains the clipped gradient norm to remain within the range of the true pixel residual, thereby preserving texture hue and detail while filtering out isolated noisy gradients. The method integrates seamlessly into existing SDS loss functions with minimal computational overhead, and optionally incorporates depth/normal ControlNet to improve shape-texture alignment.

## Results

The paper is primarily qualitative, relying on visual comparisons of textured meshes rather than standard quantitative metrics (e.g., FID, PSNR). The sole quantitative evaluation is a user study with 15 participants making 180 total picks across 12 textured meshes: PGC+SDXL (Ours) received 84.44% preference, Fantasia3D+PGC received 10.56%, Fantasia3D received 5%, and Fantasia3D+SDXL (without PGC) received 0% as it failed to generate any meaningful texture. The ablation on clipping threshold (Section A.3) shows texture quality is robust across thresholds {0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0}, with c=0.1 used as default.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Fantasia3D (Chen et al., 2023b) as the primary baseline, PGC consistently enhances texture detail in both fixed-mesh and shape-optimization settings (Figures 3–4), and the user study confirms a large preference gap (84.44% vs. 5%). Critically, simply replacing Stable Diffusion 1.5 with SDXL in Fantasia3D without PGC leads to 'consistent failure' producing no meaningful texture, whereas PGC activates SDXL's capabilities and yields high-quality results. Against Zero123-SDS (Liu et al., 2023a), PGC provides only modest texture improvement, which the authors attribute to the lower 256-resolution constraint of that pipeline. PGC is also shown to benefit Stable-DreamFusion (improved details and success rates) and Fantasia3D's geometry stage (reduced small mesh fragments), demonstrating its generality as a plug-in across multiple SDS-based pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion, Poole et al. 2023) and Latent Diffusion Models (Rombach et al. 2022), identifying a previously overlooked issue: the unregulated VAE encoder gradient (∂z/∂x) in the SDS pipeline. It repurposes the well-established gradient clipping technique from neural network training (Mikolov, 2012) by adapting it to a pixel-wise granularity specifically for the latent-to-image gradient path, rather than the parameter-wise level where it is typically applied. The method is positioned as a pipeline-agnostic plug-in that integrates with existing SDS frameworks (Fantasia3D, Stable-DreamFusion, Zero123) without modifying their core architecture or loss formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PGC offers a minimal-cost, drop-in solution to a practical bottleneck in high-resolution 3D texture synthesis, particularly when leveraging powerful image models like SDXL that otherwise produce failed or noisy results. The theoretical bounding via Jensen's inequality provides a principled justification for the clipping threshold, though the noise assumption (zero-measure out-of-boundary region) may not hold universally across all diffusion models or resolutions. Limitations include a small user study (15 participants, 180 picks), absence of standard quantitative metrics (FID, CLIP score) in the main text, and evaluation primarily on mesh optimization rather than full text-to-3D from scratch. Open questions include whether PGC generalizes to video-based 3D generation, 4D content, or even higher resolutions beyond 1024×1024.
