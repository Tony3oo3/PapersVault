## Problem

Over 98% of images on Twitter lack alt-text, leaving blind and low-vision users with no meaningful description of visual content. Existing image captioning systems treat images in isolation, ignoring the surrounding tweet text that often provides critical context (e.g., identifying election flyers vs. books) and is essential for producing accurate, contextually appropriate alt-text rather than generic captions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240477/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and the associated English tweet text as input, with both the alt-text and tweet text hard-cropped at 150 tokens (averaging 40 and 46 tokens respectively in the dataset). The image is encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT into a 512-dimensional vector, while the tweet text is tokenized and embedded in GPT-2's 768-dimensional word embedding space. Output is a single autoregressively generated alt-text caption, trained against user-written alt-text from 371k Twitter image-tweet pairs.

## Method

The core contribution is a multimodal prefix that concatenates a projected image embedding with the embedded tweet text, allowing a unimodal GPT-2 decoder to condition on both visual and textual context simultaneously. Specifically, a frozen CLIP image encoder produces a 512-dim vector that a mapping network (MLP) projects into k=10 sequences of 768-dim vectors in word embedding space; this image prefix is concatenated with the token-embedded tweet text to form a combined prefix fed to GPT-2. Training uses teacher forcing with cross-entropy loss, backpropagating into the mapping network and GPT-2 while keeping CLIP frozen, on a novel 371k-image Twitter dataset. Decoding employs beam search (size 5) with trigram blocking to prevent repetition, and an optional ROUGE-L reranking step selects the best of 5 candidates by similarity to the tweet text, ensuring contextually relevant details appear in the final output.

## Results

On the Twitter Alt-Text Dataset (20,260 test images), the proposed multimodal model (Text+Image) achieves a BLEU@4 of 1.826, METEOR of 3.067, ROUGE-L of 8.652, and CIDEr of 7.661 using beam search with no repeats. This represents more than a 2× improvement over the strongest baseline (ClipCap trained from scratch, BLEU@4 = 0.696) and approximately 16× over frozen BLIP-2 (BLEU@4 = 0.111). The text-only ablation (BLEU@4 = 0.693) confirms that visual and textual information sources stack, with the full multimodal model substantially outperforming either modality alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ClipCap (the closest architectural baseline), the proposed Text+Image model more than doubles BLEU@4 (1.826 vs. 0.696 from scratch, 0.681 finetuned) and nearly quintuples CIDEr (7.661 vs. 1.588/1.557), while also leading on METEOR (3.067 vs. 2.643/2.685). Against BLIP-2, the gap is even more pronounced: the proposed method achieves roughly 16× higher BLEU@4 (1.826 vs. 0.111) and 5.5× higher CIDEr (7.661 vs. 1.381), despite BLIP-2's far larger pretraining corpus. The text-only ablation (0.693 BLEU@4) matches ClipCap's from-scratch performance, confirming that tweet text alone carries substantial context but cannot replace visual information; the full multimodal model's 2.6× gain over text-only demonstrates that the two modalities provide complementary signal. Human evaluation corroborates these findings, with annotators preferring the proposed model over frozen ClipCap on 61.4% of examples for fluency and 66.9% for descriptiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ClipCap architecture (Mokady et al., 2021), which projects CLIP image embeddings into word embedding space as a prefix to GPT-2 for captioning, by concatenating the embedded tweet text alongside the projected image prefix to create a multimodal conditioning signal. Conceptually, it builds on Kreiss et al. (2022b), who demonstrated that contextual text improves alt-text generation on Wikipedia, but shifts the setting to social media where the surrounding post text is short, informal, and often contains information not visually recoverable from the image. The paper positions alt-text generation as a distinct subtask of image captioning that demands more literal, detail-oriented descriptions and benefits from domain-specific context, differentiating it from both generic captioning (COCO, Conceptual Captions) and tag-based systems like Facebook's AAT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With 98% of Twitter images lacking alt-text, this work addresses a concrete accessibility gap and provides the first large-scale (371k) benchmark dataset for social media alt-text generation, enabling future research in this underexplored area. The key finding that tweet text and image features provide independently useful, stacking signal suggests that multimodal conditioning is not merely additive but essential for this task. However, the authors are explicit that the system is not production-ready: evaluation relies on sighted annotators rather than BLV users, the model lacks OCR capability, and it cannot handle images without accompanying text. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Twitter to other platforms, whether the noisy user-written gold references adequately proxy for alt-text best practices, and whether automated alt-text could inadvertently reduce platform investment in human-written descriptions.
