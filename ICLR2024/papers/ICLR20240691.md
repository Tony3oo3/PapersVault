## Problem

Despite the availability of large DFT-labeled datasets and diverse downstream benchmarks, pre-training for atomic property prediction has been far less adopted than in NLP or CV, with most state-of-the-art results still coming from models trained from scratch. Existing pre-training strategies (e.g., denoising) are designed for equilibrium structures, yet over 99% of DFT training data comprises non-equilibrium configurations, and no prior work has demonstrated effective supervised pre-training that generalizes across the full chemical domain spectrum from small molecules to periodic materials.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D atomic structures as graphs with node features (atomic numbers) and edge features (interatomic distances and direction vectors), spanning systems from 4 atoms (QM9) to 500 atoms (QMOF). During pre-training, outputs are per-atom forces (node-level vectors) and total energy (graph-level scalar); during fine-tuning, the same backbone produces task-specific outputs via new prediction heads, ranging from forces and energies to OOD properties like band gaps, phonon frequencies, and HOMO/LUMO levels. Energy is computed by summing per-atom MLP outputs, while forces are computed either via a direct equivariant block (summing edge-weighted direction vectors) or as the gradient of energy with respect to positions.

## Method

JMP frames pre-training as a multi-task supervised regression problem over ~120M non-equilibrium structures from four chemical domains (OC20, OC22, ANI-1x, Transition-1x), using a GemNet-OC directional message-passing backbone with per-dataset prediction heads. Three key balancing mechanisms address the inherent imbalances: temperature sampling (T=2) over datasets to prevent large datasets from dominating, a structure-wise loss reduction that averages force loss per system before averaging across the batch (preventing large systems from dominating the force gradient), and per-dataset loss coefficients set to the mean system size to balance energy and force contributions. The training is regularized with weight decay 0.1, edge dropout (p=0.1), and EMA weights. Fine-tuning discards pre-training heads, attaches new randomly initialized heads for the target task, and trains the full model with a multi-phase LR schedule (warmup + cosine decay + layer-wise LR decay + ReduceLROnPlateau), achieving 59% average improvement over scratch training and 12× faster convergence.

## Results

JMP-L (235M params) achieves state-of-the-art MAE on 34 out of 40 fine-tuning tasks across six datasets spanning small molecules, large molecules, and materials. On QM9, JMP-L sets SOTA on 11/12 targets (e.g., ε_HOMO: 8.8 meV, ε_LUMO: 8.6 meV, Δε: 19.1 meV), losing only on R² (0.163 vs TorchMD-Net's 0.033 a₀²). On rMD17, JMP-L achieves SOTA force MAE on 5/10 molecules (e.g., Aspirin: 5.1 meV/Å vs MACE's 6.6). Across all 40 tasks, JMP-L delivers a 59% average relative improvement over the same-size model trained from scratch (GN-OC-L), while fine-tuning requires only 275 GPU-hours vs. 3,300 GPU-hours for scratch training (12× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JMP-L decisively outperforms the same-architecture scratch baseline GN-OC-L across all 40 tasks (59% average relative improvement), with the gap widening on low-data benchmarks (e.g., rMD17 Aspirin: 5.1 vs 24.7 meV/Å; MD22 Ac-Ala3-NHMe: 1.92 vs 6.27 meV/Å). Against MACE, JMP-L wins on QM9 (ε_HOMO: 8.8 vs 22.0 meV), all 9 MD22 molecules (e.g., DHA: 1.37 vs 2.80 meV/Å), and all MatBench tasks (e.g., MP E Form: 10.1 vs 17.0 meV/atom), but loses on 5/10 rMD17 molecules where MACE is competitive. Equiformer is beaten on QM9 (ε_HOMO: 8.8 vs 15.0 meV) but is not evaluated on the larger-molecule or materials benchmarks. The prior pre-training method (Zaidi et al., ET-OREO / GNS+TAT+NN) is outperformed on every QM9 target (e.g., Δε: 19.1 vs 22.0/26.4 meV) and every rMD17 molecule (e.g., Aspirin: 5.1 vs 15.1 meV/Å), confirming that supervised multi-domain pre-training on non-equilibrium data surpasses self-supervised denoising on equilibrium-only data. On QMOF band-gap prediction, JMP-L (0.16 eV) beats both PT CGCNN (0.28) and PT MOFTransformer (0.27). The sole loss is QM9 R², where TorchMD-Net (0.033 a₀²) substantially outperforms JMP-L (0.163 a₀²), a pattern also observed in prior pre-training work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GemNet-OC equivariant message-passing backbone and introduces a supervised multi-task pre-training strategy (JMP) that departs fundamentally from prior atomic pre-training work. Previous approaches (Zaidi et al. 2022, Zhou et al. 2023b) relied on self-supervised denoising of equilibrium structures, which applies to less than 1% of available DFT data; JMP instead frames each chemical domain as a separate regression task within a single multi-task objective, enabling training on the >99% non-equilibrium data that dominates real datasets. The structure-wise loss reduction and temperature-based dataset sampling are practical contributions to multi-task learning that address imbalances specific to atomic systems (varying system sizes, dataset scales spanning 2M–100M samples).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JMP demonstrates that cross-domain supervised pre-training acts as a powerful regularizer that reverses the overfitting typically seen when scaling up atomic models in low-data regimes (JMP-L gains 21% over JMP-S while GN-OC-L loses 8% over GN-OC-S), and enables 12× faster fine-tuning than training from scratch. The comprehensive 40-task benchmark spanning small molecules, large molecules, and materials provides a rigorous testbed for out-of-domain generalization that prior work lacked. Key limitations include reliance on a single backbone (GemNet-OC), the suboptimal practice of discarding pre-training heads during fine-tuning, and model sizes still dwarfed by NLP/CV foundation models. Open questions include whether larger models with data/model parallelism, alternative backbones, or retained pre-training heads can further close the gap toward a truly universal ML potential.
