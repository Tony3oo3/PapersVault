## Problem

Domain finetuning of language models improves task performance but causes catastrophic forgetting of general abilities, yet the internal mechanisms driving this trade-off remain poorly understood. Existing analyses focus on downstream task metrics, leaving open the question of which specific components of text modeling—topic, style, or factual knowledge—are most affected and how their learning dynamics differ.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241765/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained causal LM (LLaMA 2 7B/13B or GPT-2 XL) and a domain corpus (PubMed, Pile of Law, or Amazon reviews), finetunes it with standard causal language modeling loss, then probes it with ChatGPT-generated controlled-variable text pairs (1000 documents per condition, ≥500 characters each, truncated to 1024 tokens). The output is a set of log-likelihood ratios decomposed into topic, style, and factual components, measured as a function of training data volume, token position, learning rate, LoRA rank, and general-to-domain data mixture ratio.

## Method

The paper decomposes document probability into topic, style, and factual factors under an approximate independence assumption, then estimates each factor's likelihood ratio by averaging cross-entropy loss differences over paired documents that differ in exactly one factor. ChatGPT (GPT-3.5-turbo) is used to systematically rewrite 1000 documents from PubMed and C4, changing only topic, style, or factual content while holding the others fixed, enabling controlled-variable probing with ~95% instruction adherence. Learning and forgetting are ablated by introducing or removing one factor at a time on derived datasets, and the distinct dynamics of bias learning versus knowledge learning are characterized by varying token position (first 10 vs. later tokens), trainable parameter count (full vs. LoRA rank 1–8), learning rate, and Wikipedia mixing ratio. The key finding is that topic/style biases are learned rapidly, require minimal capacity, concentrate on early tokens, and scale with learning rate, while factual knowledge is learned slowly, uniformly across positions, and demands substantial model capacity.

## Results

Domain finetuning on PubMed causes the biomedical topic and academic style likelihood ratios to increase monotonically (approximately log-linearly) with training data, while the factual/counterfactual ratio changes at a significantly slower rate, indicating that topic/style bias dominates the total probability shift. With LoRA at rank r=1 (only 0.02% of parameters tunable), topic and style biases are learned comparably to full finetuning, whereas factual knowledge learning is significantly hindered. General abilities degrade with higher learning rates: at lr=2e-5 with 1M documents, MMLU 5-shot drops from 46.6% (baseline) to 42.1%, while medical knowledge (MMLU clinical subsets) improves only modestly (e.g., Professional Medicine from 51.8% to 54.4% at 1M documents).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical analysis paper rather than a method comparison, so the 'baselines' are different finetuning configurations. Full finetuning vs. LoRA (r=8, 2, 1) reveals that topic/style biases require minimal capacity (learned comparably at r=1, 0.02% parameters) while factual learning degrades substantially at low rank. Higher learning rates (2e-5 vs. 3e-6) amplify topic/style bias and accelerate forgetting of general abilities (MMLU 5-shot drops 4.5 points at 2e-5 vs. 0.1 points at 3e-6), while factual knowledge learning remains largely unaffected by learning rate. Mixing Wikipedia text into the PubMed training data reduces learned bias but only modestly, even at substantial general-to-domain ratios. The qualitative findings generalize across domains: similar topic/style bias dynamics are observed on Pile of Law (legal) and Amazon reviews (automotive), confirming the phenomenon is not specific to biomedical text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the probing methodology (Srivastava et al., 2022; Lin et al., 2022) and the emerging trend of LLM-based data generation (Honovich et al., 2023; Ho et al., 2023) to create a controlled-variable dissection of domain finetuning dynamics. It departs from prior analyses of catastrophic forgetting and domain adaptation (Jang et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2020) that rely primarily on downstream task performance, instead decomposing probability changes into topic, style, and factual components. The conceptual framing draws on shortcut learning and spectral bias literature (Geirhos et al., 2020; Rahaman et al., 2019) to explain why topic/style biases are learned like simple features while factual knowledge requires more capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that topic/style bias adaptation—not factual knowledge learning—dominates the probability change during domain finetuning reframes a common practice and offers a concrete explanation for why general abilities are lost. The proposed interventions (masking early-token loss, subtracting low-rank bias adapters) are suggestive but untested, leaving open the question of whether bias and knowledge can be cleanly separated in practice. Limitations include the approximate nature of the factor decomposition, reliance on LLM-generated rewrites with ~95% adherence, and training capped at 1M documents, so generalization to very large-scale finetuning remains uncertain.
