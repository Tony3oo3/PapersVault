## Problem

Existing invariance learning algorithms (DANN, IRM, etc.) discard all domain-specific information from both source and target domains, retaining only shared semantics. Under domain asymmetry—where the target domain contains semantically relevant information absent in the source (e.g., color and texture in photos vs. sketches)—this commutative invariance is provably suboptimal, as the globally optimal encoder's risk is strictly lower-bounded by the target-specific optimal encoder's risk. The paper asks whether a simpler, more efficient form of conditional invariance can be achieved without the complex disentanglement or additional constraints used by prior conditional invariance methods.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled images from multiple source domains and the target domain during training, with 70–75% of sample supports shared across domains and the remainder being domain-unique (complementary) instances. At test time, only single-modality target domain images are available for inference (no multi-modal access), producing class labels for classification benchmarks (PACS, Office-Home, DomainNet) or pixel-level segmentation masks for the multi-modal material segmentation task. The oracle comparison uses a multi-modal model with access to all imaging modalities (RGB, AOLP, DOLP, NIR) at test time, whereas NCI operates on a single modality.

## Method

NCI modifies the standard DANN adversarial training by making the invariance operator non-commutative and directed toward the target domain: the encoder loss (Eq. 3) penalizes only source-domain samples (forcing them to be classified as target by the discriminator) while leaving target-domain samples unaffected, so source representations are mapped into the target's semantic space but target representations are preserved intact. Theoretically, this drives the empirical H-divergence between source and target to 0 (versus a lower bound of 2 under commutative invariance), yielding a strictly tighter upper bound on target risk (Result 1). The paper further proves (Theorem 2) that under complementary semantics, source samples effectively act as augmentations in the target domain, satisfying the sample-complexity requirement of the optimal target encoder Φ*τ via Haussler's bound. The approach requires no architectural changes or additional disentanglement constraints—only a one-line modification to the encoder's adversarial loss in DANN.

## Results

On PACS, NCI achieves 83.40/81.55/91.05/81.37% accuracy (Photo/Art/Cartoon/Sketch), outperforming the best baseline SDAT+ELS (81.25/80.21/89.32/80.27%) by 1.2–2.1% across all four target domains. On Office-Home, NCI reaches 50.02/47.90/56.00/63.50% (Photo/Clipart/Product/Real), surpassing SDAT+ELS (48.50/45.43/55.39/60.97%) by 1.5–2.5%. On DomainNet, NCI leads all baselines by 1.0–2.7% per domain (e.g., 48.00% on Painting vs. 46.45% for SDAT+ELS). When combined with flat-minima methods, NCI adds further gains (e.g., Model Soups + NCI reaches 85.09% on PACS-Photo vs. 81.26% standalone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCI outperforms all 11 baselines across every target domain on PACS, Office-Home, and DomainNet. The largest margins are seen against commutative invariance methods: DANN lags NCI by 4.5% on PACS-Photo (78.86 vs. 83.40) and 5.3% on Office-Home-Real (58.17 vs. 63.50), while IB-IRM (which enforces stronger commutative invariance via information bottleneck) trails by 6.4% on PACS-Photo (77.01 vs. 83.40). Among the strongest conditional-invariance baselines, SDAT+ELS is the closest competitor, with NCI leading by 1.2–2.5% on PACS/Office-Home and 1.0–2.7% on DomainNet. Notably, DANN outperforms ERM on Cartoon/Sketch (where domain-specific info is less useful) but underperforms on Photo/Art (where it is useful), confirming the paper's asymmetry hypothesis. When stacked on top of flat-minima methods (Fishr, SDAT, Model Soups), NCI adds 2.5–3.9% accuracy, demonstrating orthogonality to loss-landscape smoothing. On the multi-modal material segmentation task, NCI (single-modality at test time) approaches the oracle (multi-modality at test time) within 0.3–1.0% across all seven backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Domain Adversarial Neural Networks (DANN, Ganin & Lempitsky 2015), modifying its encoder-discriminator adversarial objective from a commutative to a non-commutative form. It positions itself against the broader invariance learning literature (IRM, IB-IRM, CDANN, CICyc, CIRCE) by arguing that all commutative invariance methods discard target-domain-specific information that is semantically relevant under domain asymmetry. The key conceptual departure is framing invariance learning through the algebraic lens of commutativity: prior methods induce a commutative semigroup on domains (Theorem 4), while NCI breaks this symmetry to retain target-specific features. The sample complexity result (Theorem 2) connects to the data augmentation literature in sim-to-real transfer but provides a provable guarantee that source samples act as target-domain augmentations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the simplicity of the solution: a single modification to DANN's loss (Equation 3) achieves what conditional invariance methods (CDANN, CICyc, CIRCE) require complex disentanglement or kernel mechanisms to approximate, while providing stricter theoretical guarantees on target risk. The sample complexity theorem is particularly valuable for settings where target-domain data is scarce but source-domain data is abundant and semantically complementary. Open questions remain about the uniqueness of the NCI solution, its relationship to class-conditional invariance, and whether the orthogonality to flat-minima methods (observed empirically in Table 2) can be formally established. The asymmetry assumption (Assumption 1) limits applicability to scenarios where the target domain genuinely contains more label-relevant information than the source.
