## Problem

Existing spoken language models rely on discrete token quantization (e.g., HuBERT, SoundStream) that discards acoustic detail, or on cascaded ASR→LLM→TTS pipelines that introduce latency and lose speaker identity and prosody. This paper asks whether a pre-trained LLM can be directly adapted to consume and produce continuous spectrograms end-to-end, preserving both the LLM's world knowledge and the speaker's acoustic characteristics without any intermediate discrete representation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3-second speech prompt as a 128-channel Mel spectrogram (16 kHz, 12.5 ms frame step) and autoregressively generates three outputs in sequence: the prompt's text transcription, a free-form text continuation, and a frame-by-frame spectrogram continuation (at 12.5 ms/frame) that is converted to a waveform by a frozen WaveFit vocoder. The text is always decoded before the spectrogram, so the speech output is conditioned on the model's own textual reasoning rather than on the ground-truth transcript.

## Method

Spectron couples a 600M-parameter pre-trained Conformer speech encoder (12M hours) with a 350M/1B-parameter PaLM-2-style prefix decoder LM through a single projection layer, with no cross-attention. Lightweight pre-net and post-net MLPs project spectrogram frames into and out of the LM's embedding space, with the pre-net acting as a bottleneck to prevent repetitive decoding. The key training insight is a joint objective: cross-entropy loss supervises both speech recognition and text continuation, while a novel spectrogram regression loss (L1+L2 applied to the raw spectrogram, its 1st-order feature-deltas, and its time-deltas up to order K=3) supervises speech continuation. Because the LM must first emit the full transcript and continuation before generating spectrogram frames, the text acts as a cross-modal chain-of-thought scratchpad that conditions the acoustic output. The entire system is trained end-to-end on 60k hours of Libri-Light with SpecAugment, and the derivative-based loss encodes longer-range temporal and spectral shape information beyond pointwise reconstruction.

## Results

On spoken question answering (LLaMA-Questions), Spectron (1B LM) achieves 22.9% accuracy in a zero-shot setting, outperforming SpeechGPT (21.9%, 7B LM) and dramatically surpassing TWIST (0.5–1.0%) and AudioLM (6.7–7.0%). On semantic quality (log-perplexity), Spectron (350M) achieves 126.08, improving over SpeechGPT (136.42), AudioLM 12-RVQ (140.28), and GSLM (296.99). For acoustic quality, Spectron scores 3.68 N-MOS and 0.42 speaker similarity, the best speaker similarity among all compared methods, though slightly below AudioLM 12-RVQ on N-MOS (3.87).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spectron outperforms GSLM on every metric: log-perplexity (126.08 vs. 296.99), N-MOS (3.68 vs. 3.13), speaker similarity (0.42 vs. 0.11), and LLaMA-Questions QA (22.9% vs. 4.0%). Against AudioLM, Spectron wins on log-perplexity (126.08 vs. 138.96/140.28), speaker similarity (0.42 vs. 0.37/0.35), and QA accuracy (22.9% vs. 7.0%/6.7%), but trails the 12-RVQ variant by 0.19 on N-MOS (3.68 vs. 3.87). Compared to TWIST, Spectron dominates across all metrics, with the 7B TWIST model achieving only 0.5% QA accuracy versus Spectron's 22.9%. Against SpeechGPT (7B), Spectron (1B) achieves comparable QA accuracy (22.9% vs. 21.9% on LLaMA-Questions; 6.1% vs. 6.5% on Web Questions) while using a smaller LM, and clearly wins on log-perplexity (126.08 vs. 136.42), N-MOS (3.68 vs. 3.38), and speaker similarity (0.42 vs. 0.05), the latter because SpeechGPT does not aim to preserve speaker identity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Spectron departs from the dominant discrete-token paradigm in spoken language modeling (GSLM, AudioLM, TWIST, SpeechGPT) by operating directly on continuous spectrograms, leveraging a pre-trained Conformer speech encoder (USM) and a PaLM-2-style decoder LM connected only through a single projection layer. The key architectural novelty is the joint training objective that simultaneously supervises ASR, text continuation, and spectrogram regression, with the generated text acting as a cross-modal chain-of-thought scratchpad before speech synthesis. The derivative-based spectrogram loss (temporal and feature deltas) is a new regularization not seen in prior TTS or speech continuation work, and the spoken QA benchmarks (Web Questions + LLaMA-Questions) are introduced as new evaluation targets for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely-held assumption (Nguyen et al., 2022) that discrete tokenization is necessary for generative speech quality, showing that end-to-end spectrogram regression with a pre-trained LM can match or exceed token-based systems on semantic coherence and speaker preservation. However, significant limitations remain: the 12.5 ms per-frame autoregressive spectrogram generation is computationally expensive and non-parallelizable with text decoding, making streaming and long-form generation impractical. The spoken QA results, while promising, are evaluated on only 300 questions with a 3-second prompt constraint, leaving open whether the cross-modal CoT advantage scales to longer contexts or more diverse question types. The work opens a path toward unified speech-text models without quantization bottlenecks, but the latency and parallelization challenges must be resolved before practical deployment.
