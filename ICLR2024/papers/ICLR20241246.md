## Problem

Proxy-reward-based Imitation Learning from Observation (ILfO) methods fail on tasks with progress dependency, where an agent must master earlier behaviors (e.g., grasping a ball) before later ones (e.g., depositing it in a basket). The paper identifies that rewarding later trajectory steps causes the agent to prefer locally optimal but globally incorrect policies (e.g., sweeping the ball away with an empty gripper), because the high later-step rewards make the value of correctly performing early behaviors appear lower than the value of skipping them.

## Input / Output

The agent receives 10 observation-only expert demonstration sequences (RGB frame sequences with no actions or rewards) and observes the environment as stacks of 3 consecutive RGB images. A frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 extracts features, and cosine distance over these features serves as the cost function for both reward computation and progress tracking. The output is a continuous robot action policy trained entirely via RL on proxy rewards, with no access to environment rewards or success/failure signals during training.

## Method

The core contribution is Automatic Discount Scheduling (ADS), which dynamically adjusts the RL discount factor γ during training to implement a soft curriculum over the demonstration timeline. A progress recognizer Φ tracks how many initial steps k the agent can follow by computing the Longest Increasing Subsequence (LIS) of nearest-neighbor frame indices between the agent's trajectory and expert demonstrations, incrementing k when the agent's alignment matches that between two expert trajectories. The discount factor is then set to γ = α^(1/k), ensuring that rewards received after step k are weighted by at most α (default 0.2), effectively suppressing misleading later-step proxy rewards until earlier behaviors are mastered. Proxy rewards themselves are computed via Optimal Transport (Wasserstein distance) between the agent's and expert's trajectories, and the underlying RL algorithm is DrQ-v2. This mechanism is task-agnostic and can be plugged into any proxy-reward-based ILfO method.

## Results

On 9 challenging Meta-World manipulation tasks (evaluated over 2M environment frames with 8 random seeds), OT+ADS achieves superior final task success rates on 7 out of 9 tasks and significantly better sample efficiency on 8 out of 9 tasks compared to all baselines. The most substantial performance gains appear on basketball and lever-pull, which exhibit pronounced progress dependency. The sole exception is the door-lock task, where all methods (including OT+ADS) exhibit low final success rates. The method also generalizes: when ADS is applied to GAIfO, it improves performance on 5 of the 9 tasks (Appendix C.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OT+ADS against three baselines: OT (Papagiannis & Li, 2022), GAIfO (Torabi et al., 2018b), and BCO (Torabi et al., 2018a), all evaluated on the same 9 Meta-World tasks with DrQ-v2 as the underlying RL algorithm. OT+ADS outperforms OT on 7/9 tasks in final success rate and 8/9 in sample efficiency, with the largest margins on basketball and lever-pull where progress dependency is strongest. GAIfO is also outperformed on 7/9 tasks in final success rate; notably, applying ADS to GAIfO (GAIfO+ADS) further improves it on 5/9 tasks, confirming ADS as a general-purpose enhancement. BCO, the inverse-model-based method, is consistently outperformed across tasks. The only task where OT+ADS does not clearly win is door-lock, where all methods struggle with low success rates. All proxy-reward-based methods (OT, GAIfO, OT+ADS) share the same DrQ-v2 backbone and ResNet-50 cost function to ensure fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the proxy-reward-based ILfO framework, where agent trajectories are scored against expert demonstrations via Optimal Transport (Papagiannis & Li, 2022; Haldar et al., 2023a) and optimized with RL (DrQ-v2). Its conceptual departure is identifying 'progress dependency' as a specific failure mode of these methods—later-step proxy rewards actively sabotage learning of earlier behaviors—and proposing Automatic Discount Scheduling (ADS) as a soft curriculum mechanism. The idea is reminiscent of Curriculum Learning (Bengio et al., 2009) and prior handcrafted discount scheduling (François-Lavet et al., 2015), but the key distinction is that the schedule is driven by an automatic progress recognizer (LIS-based) rather than a fixed or manually tuned timeline, making it adaptive across tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic for why proxy-reward-based ILfO fails on manipulation tasks with sequential sub-goals, and offers a simple, plug-and-play fix (ADS) that can be layered onto existing methods like OT and GAIfO. Limitations include evaluation restricted to 9 Meta-World tasks with a frozen ResNet-50 cost function, and the progress recognizer's reliance on a relatively coarse LIS metric that may not capture fine-grained progress in more complex or longer-horizon tasks. Open questions include whether ADS generalizes to real-world robotic settings, whether it composes with model-based ILfO approaches, and whether the LIS-based progress signal remains reliable as task complexity and trajectory length grow.
