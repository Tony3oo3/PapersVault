## Problem

Existing privacy auditing techniques for differentially private federated learning require retraining the model thousands of times, depend on knowledge of the model architecture and a proxy dataset, and often assume the adversary can inspect all intermediate model iterates. In production FL settings where training takes days or weeks on decentralized data, these requirements are infeasible, leaving no practical way to empirically estimate the true privacy loss of a single training run.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the standard DP-FedAvg training protocol (client updates, noise multiplier, clip norm, learning rate, arbitrary client participation pattern) and augments it with k canary clients that return random unit-sphere vectors scaled to the clip norm at each round they participate. After a single training run, it produces a privacy loss estimate ε at a target δ by comparing the empirical distribution of canary-to-final-model cosines (fitted as a Gaussian) against the analytically known null distribution N(0, 1/d). No proxy data, architecture knowledge, or additional training runs are required.

## Method

The core insight is that in high-dimensional space, independently sampled unit vectors are nearly orthogonal with high probability, so random canary updates are automatically orthogonal to all true client updates without any crafting. The method inserts k canary clients into the FL protocol; after training, it computes the cosine similarity between each canary vector and the final model, fits these cosines to a Gaussian N(μ̂, σ̂²), and compares against the null distribution N(0, 1/d) (the known distribution of the cosine between an isotropic vector and any independent vector) to compute ε via the exact two-Gaussian privacy loss formula. Theorem 3.3 proves that as model dimensionality d → ∞ with k = o(d) and k = ω(1), the estimate converges in probability to the true analytical ε of the Gaussian mechanism. For the stronger threat model where all intermediate iterates are observed, Algorithm 3 uses the max-over-rounds cosine as the test statistic and requires a set of unobserved canaries (never inserted into training) to empirically estimate the null distribution, since the max-over-rounds distribution has no closed form. The approach makes no assumptions on client participation patterns and is fully model- and dataset-agnostic.

## Results

On the StackOverflow word prediction task (4.1M-param LSTM, 341k clients, 2048 rounds), the proposed one-shot method estimates ε_final ≈ 1.97 at noise multiplier 0.0496 (analytical ε = 300) and ε_final ≈ 0.569 at noise 0.2317 (analytical ε = 30), indicating that releasing only the final model provides substantially stronger privacy than the analytical bound suggests. In contrast, when all intermediate model deltas are observed, ε_est-all reaches 382 and 2.693 at the same noise levels, confirming that intermediate iterates leak far more information. On EMNIST (1.2M-param CNN, 3383 users, 5 epochs), the same pattern holds: ε_est-final drops to 1.20 at noise 0.16 (analytical ε = 33.8) while ε_est-all is 17.9. Adding 1k canary clients among 341k real clients causes at most a 0.1% absolute accuracy decrease.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CANIFE (Maddock et al., 2022) on Fashion-MNIST with a two-layer feedforward network (analytical ε = 34.5), the proposed method estimates ε = 6.8 ± 1.1, substantially higher than CANIFE's 0.88 ± 0.12, while a strong-attack lower bound gives 1.82 ± 0.46. This means CANIFE's estimate falls below the lower bound (an underestimate), whereas the proposed method's estimate lies within the plausible range [1.82, 34.5]. Against the modified Jagielski et al. (2020) FPR/FNR bounding method (reported as ε_lo), the proposed ε_est consistently exceeds the lower bound by a wide margin across all noise levels and threat models in Tables 2 and 5; for example, at noise 0.0496 on StackOverflow, ε_est-final = 1.97 versus ε_lo-final = 1.11, and ε_est-all = 382 versus ε_lo-all = 6.238 (the latter capped at 6.24 by the finite-sample thresholding classifier). The proposed method thus detects significantly more privacy leakage than either baseline, particularly in the all-iterates setting where the gap is orders of magnitude.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the privacy auditing literature (Jagielski et al. 2020, Nasr et al. 2021, Zanella-Béguelin et al. 2023) by replacing the standard requirement of thousands of retraining runs with a single-run, random-canary approach. It departs most directly from CANIFE (Maddock et al. 2022), the only prior FL-specific auditing method, by eliminating the need for adversarially crafted canaries (relying instead on the high-dimensional orthogonality of random vectors), removing the assumption of Poisson subsampling, and supporting arbitrary client participation patterns. The theoretical foundation draws on the FPR/FNR characterization of (ε,δ)-DP from Kairouz et al. (2015) and the privacy-loss random variable framework of Steinke (2022) to compute exact ε between two fitted Gaussians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a critical practical bottleneck: FL training runs take days to weeks, making the thousands-of-runs requirement of prior auditing methods infeasible in production. The finding that the final-model-only threat model yields dramatically lower ε (e.g., 4.60 vs. 45800 with all iterates at zero noise) has direct implications for how FL systems should be designed and what privacy guarantees they should claim. Key open questions remain: whether the empirical ε constitutes a formal worst-case bound, whether the canary-cosine attack is truly optimal, and how the estimate relates to tight analytical composition bounds for arbitrary participation patterns. The Gaussian approximation for the alternate distribution, while empirically validated, lacks a full proof of asymptotic joint Gaussianity.
