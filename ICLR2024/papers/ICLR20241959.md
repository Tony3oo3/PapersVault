## Problem

High-capacity deep models can fit all training data perfectly, making training-set fairness constraints trivially satisfied and rendering most existing fairness interventions ineffective. Bias instead emerges during test-time generalization, where disadvantaged subgroups suffer larger generalization gaps. The paper asks: how can we regularize the fine-tuning process to explicitly optimize validation fairness rather than the trivially-achieved training fairness?

## Input / Output

The method takes an ImageNet-pretrained ViT-Base model and a medical imaging dataset (dermatology, fundus, CT, MRI, or chest X-ray) with binarized sensitive attributes (age, sex, skin type, race) split into train/validation/test sets, and produces a fine-tuned model along with an optimized 36-bit PEFT mask specifying which ViT components (MHSA, MLP, LayerNorm per block) to update. The fairness objective is the most-disadvantaged subgroup AUC on the validation set, and the final model outputs a binary diagnosis label per image.

## Method

FairTune formulates fair fine-tuning as a bi-level optimization problem: the inner loop performs standard gradient-descent fine-tuning of a pre-trained ViT-Base using a binary PEFT mask ω that selects which of the 36 module-level components (12 layers × {MHSA, MLP, LN}) to update, while the outer loop uses TPE with successive halving to search over the 36-bit mask space (plus learning rate) to maximize validation min-subgroup AUC. The key insight is that the optimal freeze/update partition is dataset- and attribute-specific and differs for fairness versus overall performance, so it must be learned rather than prescribed by a fixed heuristic like Attention Tuning or LayerNorm Tuning. The search space subsumes full fine-tuning, linear readout, and standard PEFT methods as special cases, and the framework is agnostic to the choice of fairness metric, which is simply plugged in as the outer-loop objective.

## Results

Across seven medical imaging benchmarks (Fitzpatrick17K, HAM10000, Papila, OL3I, OASIS-1, Harvard-GF3300, CheXpert), FairTune achieves the best average Overall AUROC (85.7%) and best average most-disadvantaged-subgroup AUROC (83.1%), outperforming the strongest PEFT baseline Attention Tuning (81.5% / 77.9%) by 4.2 and 5.2 points respectively. FairTune also attains the lowest average AUROC gap (3.6%) between advantaged and disadvantaged subgroups, compared to 4.6% for full fine-tuning (ERM) and 5.7% for Attention Tuning. In terms of average rank across all dataset-attribute combinations, FairTune achieves rank 1.0 on both Overall and Min AUC, and rank 2.7 on Gap AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairTune outperforms all six baselines on average Overall AUC and Min AUC across the seven benchmarks. Against the strongest PEFT baselines, Attention Tuning (81.5% overall, 77.9% min) and LayerNorm Tuning (81.2%, 76.6%), FairTune improves overall AUC by 4.2 and 4.5 points and min-subgroup AUC by 5.2 and 6.5 points respectively. The dedicated fairness methods FairPrune (72.9% overall, 69.1% min) and FSCL (74.2%, 70.3%) are substantially worse than FairTune on both overall and fairness metrics, confirming the paper's argument that training-set fairness constraints are ineffective for high-capacity deep models. Full fine-tuning (ERM, 79.9% overall, 76.7% min) is the strongest non-PEFT baseline but still trails FairTune by 5.8 and 6.4 points on overall and min AUC. On the Gap metric, FairTune (3.6%) beats all baselines except Full FT (4.6%) on average, though Full FT achieves a near-zero gap (0.2%) on the Papila Gender subset at the cost of unacceptably low overall performance, illustrating the levelling-down phenomenon the paper warns against.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PEFT-for-medical-imaging framework established by Dutt et al. (2023) and the ViT component-level PEFT methods (Attention Tuning, LayerNorm Tuning) from Touvron et al. (2022) and Basu et al. (2023), but departs fundamentally from the dominant fairness literature that imposes constraints on training data. It operationalizes the conjecture from Zietlow et al. (2022) and the empirical findings of Zong et al. (2023) that deep models trivially satisfy training-time fairness constraints, reframing fairness as a generalization problem solvable through capacity control. The key methodological departure is formulating PEFT mask selection as a bi-level optimization problem where the outer loop searches for the mask that maximizes validation fairness, rather than prescribing a fixed heuristic partition of parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FairTune provides a practical, fairness-metric-agnostic framework that consistently outperforms both standard fine-tuning and purpose-built fairness interventions (FairPrune, FSCL) across seven medical imaging benchmarks, suggesting that the field's focus on training-time fairness constraints has been misdirected for deep models. The computational cost (48 GPUh vs. ~1h for unoptimized training) is a significant practical limitation, though the authors note it is comparable to the HPO cost already required for any well-tuned model. Open questions include whether gradient-based meta-learning can scale to higher-dimensional mask spaces, whether the approach extends to multi-label or regression settings, and whether the learned masks transfer across related tasks or must be re-optimized per dataset.
