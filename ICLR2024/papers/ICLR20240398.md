## Problem

LLM-powered dialogue agents struggle with proactive dialogue tasks (negotiation, emotional support, tutoring) because they are trained to passively follow user instructions rather than strategically steer conversations toward goals. Existing prompting-based approaches are bounded by the frozen LLM's inherent planning capability, while iterative AI-feedback refinement methods are not transferable to new cases, requiring costly re-simulation for every instance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240398/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The PPDPP plug-in (a RoBERTa-large classifier) takes the full dialogue history as a token sequence and outputs a discrete action label from a task-specific strategy set of 5–11 predefined dialogue acts. This label is mapped to a natural-language instruction (e.g., 'Please propose a new price or a new price range') that is prepended to the frozen LLM's prompt to condition the next response. Case background information (item description, target prices, emotional problem type, translation exercise) is supplied to the role-playing LLMs but not directly to the PPDPP, which conditions solely on the observed utterance sequence.

## Method

PPDPP decouples policy planning from response generation by inserting a small tunable language model (RoBERTa) as a plug-in that predicts the next-turn dialogue strategy, while a frozen LLM handles context understanding and text generation. Training proceeds in two phases: supervised fine-tuning on human-annotated dialogues to initialize the policy, followed by reinforcement learning via self-play simulation where two LLMs role-play as user and assistant with competing goals, and a third LLM serves as a reward model that converts goal-oriented verbal feedback into scalar rewards through multi-sample averaging. The RL phase uses vanilla policy gradient to optimize the plug-in toward maximizing cumulative goal-achievement rewards over multi-turn episodes. At inference, only the tuned plug-in and the frozen LLM are needed—no reward model or iterative simulation—making the system transferable to new cases and swappable across applications by simply replacing the trained plug-in.

## Results

PPDPP consistently outperforms all baselines across three proactive dialogue tasks. On CraisglistBargain (negotiation), PPDPP achieves the best Sale-to-List Ratio of 0.3376 (vs. 0.2680 for the best baseline Proactive+MI-Prompt) and a success rate of 0.6117. On ESConv (emotional support), PPDPP reaches a success rate of 0.8462 with only 4.56 average turns, surpassing ICL-AIF (0.8079) and Standard prompting (0.7692). On CIMA (tutoring), PPDPP achieves 0.8407 success rate in just 3.03 turns, substantially outperforming Standard prompting (0.6903 at 3.89 turns). PPDPP also requires only O(L) tokens per conversation, compared to O((M+1)L) for AnE and O((N+1)L) for ICL-AIF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPDPP wins against all five baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest turn-level strategy baselines (Proactive, ProCoT, AnE), PPDPP improves SL% by 6.9–10.9 points on negotiation and SR by 4.3–15.0 points on tutoring, while also reducing average turns. Against ICL-AIF (the only dialogue-level iterative-refinement baseline), PPDPP achieves higher SR on both negotiation (0.6117 vs. 0.3617) and tutoring (0.8407 vs. 0.6106), and requires far fewer tokens (O(L) vs. O((N+1)L)). Notably, on CIMA tutoring, even the Standard prompting baseline (SR=0.6903) outperforms Proactive, ProCoT, and ICL-AIF, indicating that ChatGPT's inherent multilingual capability already handles this homogeneous task well; PPDPP still pushes SR to 0.8407. The ablation rows confirm that RL is the dominant contributor: removing RL drops SL% from 0.3376 to 0.2280 on negotiation and SR from 0.8462 to 0.7308 on ESConv, while SFT alone provides only marginal gains over RL-only training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLAIF paradigm (Bai et al., 2022) from single-turn response quality to multi-turn dialogue policy planning, introducing goal-oriented AI feedback that converts LLM verbal assessments into scalar rewards for policy gradient optimization. It departs from prompt-based policy planning methods (Proactive, ProCoT, AnE, ICL-AIF) by replacing frozen-LLM self-reasoning with a tunable RoBERTa plug-in trained via SFT and RL, addressing the transferability limitation of per-case iterative refinement. The self-play simulation framework builds on Fu et al. (2023) but repurposes it as a training environment rather than an inference-time refinement loop, and the LLM-as-reward-model with multi-sample averaging draws on self-consistency techniques (Wang et al., 2023e).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play architecture offers a practical path to improving LLM dialogue agents without expensive full-model fine-tuning, and the learned policy planner can be swapped across applications (negotiation, emotional support, tutoring) without retraining the backbone LLM. The proposed interactive evaluation protocol (LLM user simulator + reward model measuring success rate and average turns) addresses a real gap in dialogue evaluation beyond turn-level metrics. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-ended strategy spaces beyond pre-defined taxonomies, how it scales to longer conversations or multi-agent settings, and whether the reliance on a relatively small plug-in (RoBERTa) limits expressiveness for more complex policy reasoning.
