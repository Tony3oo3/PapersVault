## Problem

Counterfactual distribution estimation has largely been limited to CDF approximation or mean-based targets, and existing PDF estimation approaches (kernel smoothing, f-divergence projections, L^p norms) require exact evaluation of the density, including its normalizing constant. This rules out important parametric families such as energy-based models, restricted Boltzmann machines, and Gibbs distributions, whose densities are only known up to a normalizing constant. No prior method could model counterfactuals from such unnormalized density classes while accounting for the sampling bias inherent in observational causal data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observational triples (X_i, A_i, Y_i) along with a parametric density class {q_θ} specified only through its score function ∇_y log q_θ(y) (the normalizing constant is never needed), a reproducing kernel k on the outcome space, and two nuisance estimators: a propensity score π̂ and a conditional mean embedding β̂ of the form Σ w_i(x) k(·, Y_i). The output is the counterfactual density q_{θ_n} where θ_n minimizes a doubly robust V-statistic; a key practical detail is that the nuisance estimators are fitted once on the data and then reused across all θ values via a linear mapping, rather than being refitted per parameter, which allows expensive estimators (e.g., deep networks) to be used.

## Method

The DR-MKSD estimator constructs a doubly robust embedding φ(z) = a/π(x){ξ_θ(·,y) − β_θ(x)} + β_θ(x) whose expectation under the observational distribution P equals E_{Y^1~Q^1}[ξ_θ(·,Y^1)], the RKHS element whose norm gives the KSD between q_θ and the counterfactual Q^1. The objective g_n(θ) is the squared norm of the empirical average of φ̂_θ(Z_i), computed via cross-fitting (two folds for nuisance estimation, each evaluated on the complementary fold) to avoid overfitting. The score-based kernel h_θ(y, ỹ) = ⟨ξ_θ(·,y), ξ_θ(·,ỹ)⟩_{H^d} has a closed form requiring only the score s_θ and kernel derivatives, so the full density is never evaluated. Double robustness is inherited from the efficient influence function structure of φ: consistency holds if either π̂ or β̂ is consistent, and the convergence rate is the product of the two nuisance rates. The paper establishes consistency of KSD(Q_{θ_n}‖Q^1) and asymptotic normality of √n(θ_n − θ*) under regularity conditions, with a sandwich variance estimator for inference.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that DR-MKSD achieves the desired double robustness: the estimator is consistent (MSE → 0 as n grows) whenever either the propensity score estimator π̂ or the conditional mean embedding estimator β̂ is consistent, even if the other is misspecified. For asymptotic normality, the estimated 95% confidence intervals achieve empirical coverage of 0.94 (n=200) and 0.96 (n=300), closely matching the nominal 0.95 level. In the intractable energy-based model experiment (n=500, 100 replications), the 2D parameter estimate θ_n exhibits approximately Gaussian behavior, confirmed via scatter plots, histograms, and QQ plots. On MNIST, DR-MKSD successfully identifies low-density (outlier) observations in the 20-dimensional latent space of a pretrained network for each digit class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly states that no natural external benchmark exists, as DR-MKSD is the first estimator to handle unnormalized densities in the counterfactual setting. Rather than comparing against prior methods, the experiments contrast three internal variants of the same estimator: inverse probability weighting (IPW), plug-in (PI), and the full doubly robust (DR) version. The DR version is consistent in all tested configurations (boosting + CME, logistic regression + 1-NN, logistic regression + CME), while IPW and PI each fail when their respective nuisance estimator is misspecified. The authors note that prior counterfactual density estimation methods (e.g., Kennedy et al. 2021, Kim et al. 2018) require exact density evaluation including normalizing constants, making direct comparison infeasible for the energy-based models considered here. The MNIST experiment serves as a qualitative illustration of outlier detection rather than a quantitative benchmark against competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Minimum Kernel Stein Discrepancy (MKSD) estimators (Barp et al., 2019; Oates et al., 2022) from the non-causal setting to counterfactual density estimation, where outcomes are only partially observed and treatment assignment must be accounted for. Prior counterfactual density estimation methods (Kennedy et al., 2021; Kim et al., 2018; Melnychuk et al., 2023) rely on f-divergences or L^p norms that require exact density evaluations including normalizing constants, ruling out energy-based models and RBMs. The doubly robust cross-fitting framework (Chernozhukov et al., 2018) is repurposed here not for mean estimation but for constructing a debiased V-statistic whose minimizer yields a consistent KSD-based counterfactual density estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first estimator capable of modeling counterfactual distributions from parametric families with unknown normalizing constants, opening the door to energy-based models, RBMs, and other intractable likelihoods in causal inference. The double robustness property means the estimator remains consistent if either the propensity score or the conditional embedding is correctly specified, providing practical robustness. Open questions include the non-convex optimization landscape (the potential E_θ could be a neural network), whether θ_n consistency follows from KSD consistency when the minimizer is non-unique, and extensions to time-varying treatments or off-policy evaluation settings.
