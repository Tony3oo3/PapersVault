## Problem

Existing Graph Lottery Ticket (GLT) algorithms suffer from three critical limitations: (1) they maintain identical topology across all layers, causing severe performance degradation in deep GNNs (e.g., >10% drop from 6 to 8 layers); (2) they prune weights and edges irreversibly, lacking the elasticity to restore mistakenly pruned elements; and (3) they require manually predefined pruning ratios via trial-and-error, introducing additional computational overhead and reducing flexibility across datasets and architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F}) along with an L-layer GNN's weight matrices as input. It produces a layer-adaptive pair of sparse structures: progressively sparser adjacency matrices (sparsity increasing from shallow to deep layers) and pruned weight matrices, jointly forming a GLT. Unlike prior methods that require a fixed target sparsity, AdaGLT automatically discovers the optimal sparsity level during training and selects the best-performing ticket within a user-specified sparsity interval on the validation set.

## Method

AdaGLT introduces trainable threshold vectors for both weights and adjacency matrices at each layer, replacing fixed pruning rates with an automated, end-to-end learnable sparsification schedule. For weights, row-wise magnitude comparison against per-row thresholds produces binary masks, made differentiable via a Straight-Through Estimator (STE) with a tempered sigmoid. For edges, an attention-based edge explainer computes continuous importance scores from node features, which are then thresholded layer-by-layer with a cumulative product term (∏_{k=0}^{l-1} m_A^{(k)}) that enforces progressively increasing sparsity across depth. A sparse regularization term (η·Σ exp(-t_i)) on the threshold vectors drives them upward during training, automatically increasing sparsity without manual scheduling. The key differentiator is dynamic restoration: because thresholds and element values are jointly optimized, previously pruned elements can be revived when gradient updates push |Q_ij| above t_i, enabling the model to correct early pruning mistakes. A GNTK-based theorem proves that layer-adaptive sparsification (with smallest singular value σ_0^{(l)} = 1 - α^l) guarantees a positive minimum eigenvalue of the kernel as depth → ∞, theoretically preventing over-smoothing.

## Results

AdaGLT identifies graph lottery tickets with 23%–84% graph sparsity and 87%–99% weight sparsity while maintaining or exceeding baseline accuracy across Cora, Citeseer, PubMed, and OGB datasets. In deep GNN scenarios (16-layer GAT), AdaGLT sustains 61–75% accuracy at 60% graph sparsity, whereas TGLT collapses to 37–47%. On the large-scale Ogbn-Products dataset (2.4M nodes), AdaGLT achieves 80.35% accuracy at 10% graph sparsity with 4-layer Cluster-GCN, surpassing UGS (78.44%) and the baseline (79.23%). In the deepest settings (28-layer DeeperGCN on Ogbn-Arxiv), AdaGLT surpasses TGLT by up to 21% in achievable graph sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaGLT is compared against two prior GLT methods: UGS (Chen et al., ICML 2021) and TGLT (Hui et al., ICLR 2023). Against TGLT, AdaGLT achieves a decisive advantage in deep GNN settings: at 16 layers with 60% graph sparsity on GAT, TGLT drops to 37.26% on Cora and 47.25% on Citeseer, while AdaGLT maintains 71.88% and 60.97% respectively — a gap of 34–35 percentage points. On GCN backbones (Table 7), the gap is even more extreme at 16 layers/60% sparsity, where TGLT collapses to 14.30% on Citeseer versus AdaGLT's 57.87%. Against UGS on the large-scale Ogbn-Products dataset (2.4M nodes), AdaGLT achieves 80.35% accuracy at 10% graph sparsity with 4-layer Cluster-GCN, outperforming UGS (78.44%) and even exceeding the unpruned baseline (79.23%). In the 28-layer DeeperGCN setting on Ogbn-Arxiv, AdaGLT surpasses TGLT by 21% in achievable graph sparsity while maintaining comparable accuracy. Across all 7 datasets and all backbone architectures tested, AdaGLT consistently identifies winning tickets at higher sparsity levels than either baseline, with the margin widening as network depth increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaGLT extends the Graph Lottery Ticket (GLT) paradigm established by UGS (Chen et al., 2021) and TGLT (Hui et al., 2023), which jointly prune GNN weights and graph edges but rely on fixed, manually-tuned sparsity rates and irreversible pruning. The key departure is threefold: (1) replacing fixed pruning ratios with trainable per-layer threshold vectors (borrowed from dynamic sparse training, Liu et al., 2020), (2) introducing an edge explainer (adapted from Luo et al., 2020) to compute continuous edge importance scores instead of learning a full adjacency-sized mask, and (3) enforcing progressively increasing sparsity across layers via a cumulative product in the adjacency mask (Eq. 7). The theoretical grounding draws on GNTK propagation analysis (Huang et al., 2021) to prove that layer-adaptive sparsification preserves positive kernel eigenvalues in the infinite-depth limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaGLT addresses a practical bottleneck: existing GLT methods degrade sharply beyond 4–6 GNN layers, limiting their utility as the field moves toward deeper architectures (DeeperGCN, 1000-layer GNNs). The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that layer-adaptive sparsification prevents GNTK eigenvalue collapse is a useful formal justification, though it relies on the strong assumption of shared singular vectors across layers and a specific parametric form for the smallest singular value. Open questions include whether the edge explainer's quadratic parameter overhead (W_Q, W_K) scales gracefully to billion-node graphs, whether the dynamic restoration mechanism introduces training instability at very high sparsity, and whether the approach transfers to other graph tasks (graph classification, heterophilic graphs) beyond node classification and link prediction.
