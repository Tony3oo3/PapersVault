## Problem

Conditional diffusion models trained on noisy labels converge to the noisy-label conditional score rather than the clean-label conditional score, causing condition mismatch and degraded sample quality. While noisy-label learning has been extensively studied for supervised classifiers and GANs, no theoretical analysis or remedy existed for diffusion models, whose time-dependent score vectors make direct adaptation of GAN-based solutions infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241336/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a clean class label and random noise and generates a conditional RGB image (28×28 for MNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Clothing-1M). At training, the unusual requirement is that the score network must be evaluated for multiple classes per sample (not just the given noisy label), and a separate time-dependent noisy-label classifier is trained on the same noisy dataset to supply instance-wise, timestep-dependent transition weights. A skip threshold (τ=0.01) prunes negligible weight terms to keep forward passes tractable.

## Method

The paper proves (Theorem 1) that under class-conditional label noise, the noisy-label conditional score equals a convex combination of clean-label conditional scores with weights w(x_t, ỹ, y, t) = p_t(Y=y | Ỹ=ỹ, x_t), which are both instance-dependent and time-dependent. Building on this, the TDSM objective (Eq. 6) minimizes the L2 distance between the weighted sum of score-network outputs across all classes and the perturbed data score, and Theorem 3 guarantees convergence to the clean-label conditional score under an invertible transition matrix. The weights are estimated via a time-dependent noisy-label classifier h̃_φ(x_t, t) trained with cross-entropy over timesteps, combined with the reverse transition matrix S (Eq. 8). A key negative result (Proposition 4) shows that replacing the instance/time-dependent weights with the static transition-matrix entries S_{ỹ,y} (as in GAN-based methods) cannot recover the clean score, motivating the full TDSM formulation. Practical training uses selective backpropagation only on the noisy-label class output and skips forward evaluations for weight terms below a threshold to reduce memory and compute.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under 40% symmetric noise, TDSM reduces CW-FID from 30.45 (DSM) to 15.92 and improves CAS from 47.21 to 62.28, while CW-Density jumps from 73.02 to 97.80. On CIFAR-100 under 40% symmetric noise, TDSM improves CW-FID from 100.04 to 93.24 and CAS from 15.41 to 21.17. On the real-world Clothing-1M dataset (61.54% label accuracy), TDSM achieves FID 4.94 vs. 6.67 for DSM and CAS 47.79 vs. 46.52. Notably, even on clean benchmark datasets, TDSM with a 5% symmetric noise assumption outperforms standard DSM (e.g., CIFAR-10 FID 1.91 vs. 1.92; CIFAR-100 Density 90.04 vs. 87.98), suggesting latent label noise in standard benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard Denoising Score Matching (DSM) trained directly on noisy labels. TDSM consistently outperforms DSM on all conditional metrics (CW-FID, CAS, CW-Density, CW-Coverage) across every dataset and noise setting, with the largest gains at 40% noise (e.g., CIFAR-10 CW-FID drops from 30.45 to 15.92 under symmetric noise). On unconditional metrics (FID, IS, Density, Coverage), TDSM wins in most cases but occasionally trails DSM slightly on FID for CIFAR-100 under high symmetric noise (4.26 vs. 2.96 at 20%), suggesting a minor quality trade-off for improved condition alignment. The paper also compares against label-noise robust GANs (Kaneko et al., 2019; Thekumparampil et al., 2018) in Appendix F.5, reporting that diffusion models generate substantially better images than GANs even under noisy labels, and TDSM further narrows the gap to clean-label performance. An ablation (Table 5) shows that the S-weighted DSM variant (using only the transition matrix without instance/time-dependent weights) is worse than full TDSM on CAS and CW-Density, confirming the importance of the time- and instance-dependent weight function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the label-noise robust generative modeling line initiated by GAN-based methods (Kaneko et al., 2019; Thekumparampil et al., 2018) to diffusion models, which had no prior theoretical treatment. The key departure is that, unlike GANs where a fixed class-conditional transition matrix p(Y|Ỹ) suffices, Theorem 1 shows that the noisy-label conditional *score* in a diffusion model is a convex combination of clean-label scores with instance-wise and time-dependent weights, making the problem structurally different. The method builds on the denoising score matching objective (Vincent, 2011; Song et al., 2020) and the EDM framework (Karras et al., 2022), and leverages transition matrix estimation techniques from the supervised noisy-label literature (e.g., VolMinNet) for practical implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical and empirical treatment of noisy labels in conditional diffusion models, providing a principled objective (TDSM) that provably recovers clean-label scores under an invertible transition matrix. The finding that TDSM improves generation quality even on standard 'clean' benchmarks (MNIST, CIFAR-10/100) suggests these datasets harbor hidden label noise, a risk that becomes more acute as diffusion models are trained on ever-larger, less-curated datasets. Limitations include the need for a (possibly estimated) transition matrix, the invertibility assumption, and the computational overhead of multi-class score evaluations (mitigated by thresholding). Open questions include extension to text-conditioned or video diffusion models, instance-dependent (non-class-conditional) noise, and integration with classifier-free guidance at scale.
