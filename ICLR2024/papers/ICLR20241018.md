## Problem

Convex relaxations are essential for certifying neural network robustness, yet a large accuracy gap between certified and standard networks persists. Prior work showed that the simple IBP relaxation cannot precisely express convex CPWL functions, but it remained unknown whether this limitation is fundamental to all single-neuron convex relaxations or specific to IBP. This paper asks whether the expressivity of ReLU networks under convex relaxations is fundamentally limited, and if so, at what level of relaxation precision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241018/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a ReLU network encoding a CPWL function (univariate or multivariate) and an input box region, and produces linear output bounds via a specified convex relaxation (IBP, DeepPoly-0/1, Triangle, or Multi-Neuron). The key output is a precise/imprecise characterization: whether the relaxation's output bounds exactly match the true function's range on every sub-box. The paper's results are expressed as a table mapping each relaxation to the function classes (CPWL, M-CPWL, C-CPWL, MC-CPWL) it can express precisely, with the critical finding that all single-neuron relaxations fail for multivariate MC-CPWL functions.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic theoretical analysis across all common convex relaxations, using constructive encodings for positive results and impossibility proofs for negative results. For univariate functions, it shows IBP can express M-CPWL via a two-ReLU step-function construction, DeepPoly-0/1 can express C-CPWL via single-layer encodings with constrained ReLU orientations, and the Triangle relaxation (via a convex-hull-sum lemma) allows arbitrary orientations, yielding an exponentially larger solution space. For multivariate functions, a four-step impossibility proof shows no finite ReLU network can Δ-express max(x,y): (1) locality identifies a neighborhood where all ReLUs switch at x=y, (2) network form coverage rewrites the network in a recursive functional form, (3) network simplification reduces it to a single layer b + Wx + α·ReLU(x−y), and (4) a direct calculation shows this form's Δ-bounds exceed the true max on [0,1]². Multi-neuron relaxations escape this limitation by computing the convex hull jointly over multiple neurons, allowing single-layer networks to express all univariate CPWL functions.

## Results

The paper establishes a complete expressivity hierarchy across five convex relaxations (IBP, DeepPoly-0, DeepPoly-1, Δ, Multi-Neuron) for univariate and multivariate CPWL functions. For univariate functions, IBP can precisely express only monotone CPWL (M-CPWL), while DeepPoly-0/1 and Δ extend this to convex CPWL (C-CPWL), and Multi-Neuron covers all CPWL. The Δ relaxation permits an exponentially larger network solution space for C-CPWL functions compared to DeepPoly (Theorems 11 vs. 15). For multivariate functions, no single-neuron relaxation—including the most precise Δ—can precisely express even the simple function max(x,y) = y + ReLU(x−y), a fundamental limitation (Theorem 20, Corollary 21).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five convex relaxations as 'baselines' for expressivity. IBP (Mirman et al., 2018) is the most limited single-neuron relaxation: it can precisely express M-CPWL but provably cannot express C-CPWL univariate functions (building on Mirman et al., 2022). DeepPoly-0 and DeepPoly-1 (Singh et al., 2019b) are shown to be expressively equivalent (Corollary 13) and both extend IBP's reach to C-CPWL, but with a restricted solution space (Theorem 11: exactly one valid network per function). The Δ relaxation (Wong & Kolter, 2018) matches DeepPoly's expressivity class for univariate C-CPWL but allows exponentially more valid encodings since ±ᵢ can be chosen freely (Theorem 15 vs. Theorem 11). Multi-Neuron relaxations (Müller et al., 2022) are strictly more expressive, covering all univariate CPWL with a single layer (Theorem 16). However, all single-neuron relaxations—including Δ—fundamentally fail for multivariate MC-CPWL: even max(x,y) cannot be Δ-expressed by any finite ReLU network (Theorem 20), a result that generalizes to IBP and DeepPoly as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mirman et al.'s (2022) expressivity analysis—which showed IBP cannot precisely encode convex CPWL functions—to all commonly used single-neuron convex relaxations (DeepPoly-0, DeepPoly-1, Triangle/Δ) and multi-neuron relaxations. It builds on the mathematical formulations of Singh et al. (DeepPoly), Wong & Kolter (Triangle), and Müller et al. (Multi-Neuron) to characterize, for each relaxation, exactly which univariate and multivariate CPWL function classes admit precise ReLU encodings. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the systematic comparison across relaxations, revealing a hierarchy: more precise relaxations strictly enlarge the expressible function class for univariate inputs, yet all single-neuron relaxations share a fundamental multivariate limitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results directly inform certified training: the finding that more precise relaxations (Δ, DeepPoly) yield exponentially larger network solution spaces for convex functions suggests that overcoming the known optimization difficulties of these relaxations could unlock higher-accuracy certified networks. However, the negative result—that even the most precise single-neuron relaxation (Δ) cannot express the trivially analyzable function max(x,y) precisely—establishes a hard ceiling that no amount of optimization can overcome within the single-neuron paradigm, pointing toward multi-neuron or non-convex methods as necessary for multivariate certified training. Open questions include whether multi-neuron relaxations can express multivariate MC-CPWL functions, and whether the exponential solution space advantage of Δ translates into measurable training gains once optimization is resolved.
