## Problem

Unsupervised few-shot learning (U-FSL) suffers from two structural shortcomings that prior work has largely overlooked. At pretraining, contrastive approaches enforce consistency only at the instance level, treating potential same-class positives within a batch as negatives due to the unrealistic assumption that each image corresponds to a unique class. At inference, the small number of labeled support samples introduces a severe sample bias—the support set is unrepresentative of the larger unlabeled query set—causing a distribution shift that degrades performance, especially in low-shot regimes.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, BECLR consumes unlabeled RGB images (resized to 224×224) from base classes, applying two random augmentations per image to form a 2B augmented batch. At inference, given an (N-way, K-shot) task, the frozen student encoder extracts features from NK labeled support images and NQ unlabeled query images (with Q > K, typically Q=15 per class); OpTA then requires |Q| > |S| for valid distribution mapping. The final output is a class label per query image, produced by a logistic regression classifier fitted on transported support prototypes—no fine-tuning of the encoder occurs.

## Method

BECLR combines two novel modules within a student-teacher EMA contrastive framework (ResNet backbone, patch-wise masking, 3-layer projection and 2-layer prediction MLPs). The Dynamic Clustered Memory (DyCE) module replaces standard memory queues with P dynamically updated cluster partitions, each represented by a prototype; it uses Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport to enforce equipartitioned assignment of batch embeddings to clusters, then selects top-k nearest neighbors from the assigned cluster to augment the batch with meaningful positive pairs, while iteratively updating partitions and prototypes via EMA. The Optimal Transport-based distribution Alignment (OpTA) module, applied at inference with no learnable parameters, computes an OT plan between support-set class prototypes and query embeddings to transport the prototypes into the query distribution region, iteratively (up to δ=5 passes) reducing sample bias before fitting the logistic classifier. The two modules are intertwined: OpTA's effectiveness is proportional to the quality of features produced by DyCE-enhanced pretraining, and the modified infoNCE loss (less biased variant from UniSiam) is applied over the DyCE-enhanced batch of size 2B(k+1).

## Results

On miniImageNet (5-way, 1-shot), BECLR with ResNet-18 achieves 75.74% accuracy, surpassing the prior unsupervised state-of-the-art (Meta-DM + UniSiam + dist, 65.64%) by over 10 percentage points, and with ResNet-50 reaches 80.57% vs. 66.68% for the best baseline. On tieredImageNet (5-way, 1-shot), BECLR (RN50) achieves 81.69% vs. 69.61% for UniSiam+dist. In 5-shot settings, gains narrow but remain consistent: 87.82% on miniImageNet and 87.86% on tieredImageNet (RN50), outperforming the best baselines by ~2.5%. On CIFAR-FS and FC100, BECLR leads by up to 15% (1-shot) and 5.5% (1-shot) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BECLR decisively outperforms all 29 compared baselines across every in-domain benchmark (miniImageNet, tieredImageNet, CIFAR-FS, FC100) in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings, with the largest margins in 1-shot (up to 14% on miniImageNet, 12% on tieredImageNet, 15% on CIFAR-FS). The closest competitors are UniSiam+dist and Meta-DM+UniSiam+dist (the latter using extra synthetic training data), which BECLR surpasses by 10–14 points in 1-shot and 2–2.5 points in 5-shot. BECLR also outperforms supervised baselines (MetaOptNet, Transductive CNAPS) despite using no labels during pretraining. In the cross-domain CDFSL setting, BECLR sets new state-of-the-art on ChestX (28.46% 5-shot), EuroSAT (88.55% 5-shot), and CropDiseases (93.65% 5-shot), while remaining competitive but not best on ISIC (44.48% vs. ConFeSS 48.85%). A key finding is that OpTA is pretraining-agnostic: plugging it onto UniSiam lifts miniImageNet 1-shot from 64.10% to 72.54%, yet BECLR still leads by 3.2 points, confirming that DyCE's pretraining gains are independent of and complementary to OpTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

BECLR extends the BYOL/SimSiam student-teacher EMA architecture and the MoCo memory queue concept into a class-aware contrastive framework for unsupervised few-shot learning. The key departure is from instance-level contrastive learning (where each batch image is assumed to be a unique class) to membership-level contrastive learning via the DyCE module, which uses optimal transport (Sinkhorn-Knopp) to maintain dynamically updated cluster prototypes that approximate latent class structure without labels. At inference, the OpTA module repurposes optimal transport for distribution alignment between support and query sets, addressing a sample bias issue the authors argue is overlooked by prior U-FSL methods (PDA-Net, UniSiam, PsCo). The loss function is a less-biased infoNCE variant adopted from UniSiam (Lu et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BECLR sets a new state-of-the-art across all major U-FSL benchmarks (miniImageNet, tieredImageNet, CIFAR-FS, FC100, CDFSL, CUB) with margins up to 14% in 1-shot settings, and the OpTA module is explicitly demonstrated to be a plug-and-play add-on that improves any existing U-FSL pretraining method (Table 4), making it broadly applicable. The finding that OpTA's benefit is proportional to pretraining quality (and can even degrade performance with weak encoders) highlights an important coupling between representation quality and inference-time distribution alignment that prior work has not formalized. Open questions include whether DyCE's hyperparameters (P, M) can be made dataset-agnostic, whether the approach scales to transformer backbones, and whether the optimal-transport-based alignment generalizes to non-image modalities or extremely low-shot (1-shot) cross-domain settings where query distributions may be poorly estimated.
