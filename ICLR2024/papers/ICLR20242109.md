## Problem

Simulating Markov processes (e.g., molecular dynamics) at long timescales is computationally prohibitive because accurate integration requires femtosecond steps while relevant dynamics unfold over milliseconds. Existing dimensionality-reduction approaches either rely on linear correlation maximization (TICA, VAMPNet) that struggles with nonlinear dynamics in low dimensions, or reconstruction-based objectives whose effectiveness depends heavily on decoder flexibility. The paper asks: can we learn a principled, information-theoretic representation that provably preserves all dynamics at a chosen timescale τ while discarding irrelevant fast information?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242109/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of high-dimensional system states [x_t] (e.g., 3D atomic coordinates from molecular dynamics) and produces a low-dimensional latent representation z_t (2D for the synthetic Prinz experiment, 16–32D for molecular systems) together with an unfolded target trajectory [y_{s+kτ}] of discrete properties (metastable state labels, inter-atomic distance bins). The encoder is time-independent and maps each frame independently; the latent transition model then 'jumps' τ time-steps at a time, replacing J fine-grained integration steps per jump. Evaluation compares the marginal and transition statistics of the unfolded trajectory against ground truth via Jensen-Shannon divergence.

## Method

The core contribution is the Time-lagged Information Bottleneck (T-IB) objective, which combines two terms: (i) a T-InfoMax term that maximizes mutual information I(z_t; z_{t−τ}) between representations at temporal distance τ using an InfoNCE contrastive loss with a learned critic F_ξ, and (ii) a bottleneck term β·log[p_θ(z_t|x_t)/q_φ(z_t|z_{t−τ})] that penalizes superfluous information (time-independent features and dynamics faster than τ) by measuring the mismatch between the encoder and the variational transition. Training follows a two-step procedure: first optimize the encoder (and jointly the transition for T-IB) to minimize the T-IB loss, then fix the encoder and fit the variational transition q_φ (a conditional Flow++) and predictive q_ψ (an MLP) by maximum likelihood. The paper proves that zero autoinformation gap at lag τ guarantees sufficiency for all future targets, preservation of the Markov property at resolution τ, and preservation of information at all slower timescales τ′≥τ, providing a theoretical upper bound on latent simulation error in terms of the autoinformation gap.

## Results

On the 2D Prinz potential, T-IB (β=0.01) achieves the lowest marginal JS divergence (MJS = 2.4±1.6 milli-nats) and transition JS divergence at 512 steps (TJS@512 = 2.4±0.3 milli-nats), outperforming even simulation in the original space (MJS = 4.1±4.7, TJS@512 = 4.0±1.2). T-IB preserves nearly all slow-mode information (I(z_t; y_t^s) = 1.30±0.01 nats) while completely discarding fast-mode information (I(z_t; y_t^f) = 0.00±0.00 nats). On molecular simulations (Alanine Dipeptide, Chignolin, Villin), T-IB consistently yields lower marginal and transition JS divergences for unfolded trajectories compared to TICA, VAMPNet, and unregularized T-InfoMax.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TICA (Molgedey & Schuster, 1994) achieves a moderate autoinformation gap (0.10) but retains some fast-mode information (I(z_t;y_t^f) = 0.06) and produces substantially worse unfolded trajectories (MJS = 14.5, TJS@512 = 17.9) compared to T-IB (MJS = 2.4, TJS@512 = 2.4). VAMPNet (Mardt et al., 2018) discards fast information (I(z_t;y_t^f) = 0.00) but loses significant slow-mode information (I(z_t;y_t^s) = 0.99 vs. 1.30 for T-IB) and yields the worst simulation fidelity among all non-trivial baselines (MJS = 20.5, TJS@512 = 10.4). T-IB outperforms both baselines on all simulation fidelity metrics by a wide margin, and even surpasses simulation in the original high-dimensional space, which the authors attribute to the simplification induced by the bottleneck regularization. On molecular simulations, the same pattern holds: T-IB consistently produces lower marginal and transition JS divergences than both TICA and VAMPNet across Alanine Dipeptide, Chignolin, and Villin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000) to the specific setting of Markov process simulation, building directly on Wang et al. (2019b)'s past-future information bottleneck for molecular time series and the contrastive InfoNCE framework of van den Oord et al. (2018). It departs from the dominant correlation-maximization lineage (TICA, VAMPNet, time-lagged autoencoders) by replacing linear autocorrelation with nonlinear mutual information maximization, and by introducing an explicit bottleneck term that removes superfluous fast-scale information. The key theoretical departure is the formalization of 'autoinformation' and the proof that preserving it at lag τ simultaneously guarantees sufficiency for all future targets, Markov property preservation, and information preservation at all slower timescales—results that unify and generalize the linear Koopman-operator perspective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, information-theoretic recipe for learning representations that enable large time-step simulation of Markov processes, with a demonstrated ~1000× speedup over direct molecular dynamics (6 GPU-hours vs. 2–3 months for Villin). The finding that a compressed T-IB representation outperforms simulation in the full original space is practically important: it means the bottleneck is not merely a regularizer but actively improves statistical fidelity by simplifying the transition model. Open questions include applicability to high-autoinformation (chaotic) systems where MI estimator variance explodes, generalization from short training trajectories to long-range prediction, and whether more flexible transition models (beyond Flow++) can further close the gap to ground-truth statistics.
