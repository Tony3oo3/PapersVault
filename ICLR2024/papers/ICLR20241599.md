## Problem

Offline RL for temporally extended tasks with delayed sparse rewards is challenging because existing methods either plan in the raw action space (inefficient and tied to the environment's temporal structure) or use discrete latent action spaces (e.g., TAP) that require a separate, expensive planning phase. No prior framework unifies continuous latent action representation learning with planning in a single end-to-end pipeline for offline decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241599/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline trajectory dataset of the form (s_t, a_t, r_t, G_t) per timestep along with an initial state s₁ as the conditioning signal. Trajectories are segmented into chunks of L timesteps, each encoded into a single continuous latent action, so the planning horizon H (40 for locomotion/Adroit, 100 for AntMaze) is expressed in latent steps rather than raw timesteps. At inference, the model outputs a full trajectory (states, actions, rewards, reward-to-go) via the decoder, executed with receding horizon control over a context window of C=20 timesteps.

## Method

LatentDiffuser is a VAE whose prior is a score-based diffusion model (SDM) operating in a learned continuous latent action space. The encoder is a GPT-2-style Transformer (without causal masking) that processes trajectory tokens and applies 1D max-pooling with kernel/stride L to produce T/L latent actions; the decoder is modular, using an inverse-dynamics MLP for actions and a separate MLP for rewards/returns. The score-based prior is a temporal U-Net (6 recurrent residual blocks) trained with classifier-free guidance over K=100 diffusion steps. The key theoretical contribution proves that the optimal latent policy is equivalent to energy-guided diffusion sampling: p₀(z₀|s₁) ∝ q₀(z₀|s₁) exp(−βE(h(z₀,s₁))), where E is defined by a Q-value function. To make this sampling exact at the sequence level, a time-dependent energy model f_η is trained via a contrastive loss over M support latent actions (16 or 32 depending on task), modifying QGPO to operate on full latent sequences rather than individual actions. Training proceeds in three sequential phases: VAE, score-based prior, then energy model, eliminating the need for a separate planning stage.

## Results

On Gym locomotion control (9 tasks), LatentDiffuser achieves an average normalized return of 87.5, competitive with D-QL (88.0) and QGPO (86.6), while clearly outperforming hierarchical baselines TAP (82.5) and HDMI (82.6). On the high-dimensional Adroit tasks (24 DoF), LatentDiffuser's advantage becomes pronounced, achieving an average of 54.6 (with expert data) and 21.3 (without expert data), surpassing TAP (51.9 / 19.6), D-QL@1 (46.1 / 17.1), and QGPO (49.5 / 18.8). On long-horizon AntMaze, LatentDiffuser attains a single-task average of 78.9, outperforming HDMI (77.1), TAP (73.7), and all non-hierarchical baselines (DD 56.4, D-QL@1 39.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LatentDiffuser clearly outperforms all baselines on Adroit (high-dimensional, 24 DoF), winning on 11 of 12 task-dataset combinations; the largest margins are on Hammer (4.6 vs. 1.4 for TAP/QGPO on Human) and Expert Pen (131.2 vs. 127.4 for TAP). On Gym locomotion, LatentDiffuser is competitive but does not dominate: D-QL edges it out on average (88.0 vs. 87.5), while LD wins on Medium Hopper (98.5 vs. 90.5 for D-QL) and Med-Expert Walker2d (111.3 vs. 110.7 for QGPO). Against the two-stage hierarchical methods TAP and HDMI, LatentDiffuser consistently wins on Adroit and AntMaze, confirming the benefit of end-to-end unified training over separate representation learning and planning phases. On AntMaze (long-horizon, sparse reward), LatentDiffuser (78.9 single-task avg) slightly edges HDMI (77.1) and TAP (73.7), while all sequence-modeling methods (DD, D-QL@1) and RL-based hierarchical methods (CompILE, GoFAR, HiGoC) trail by 10–40 points. The autoregressive sequence model TT performs worst across all three benchmarks (averages of 78.9, 20.1, and N/A), confirming that tokenization-based approaches struggle as action dimensionality increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

LatentDiffuser extends TAP's discrete latent action planning to a continuous space by replacing TAP's autoregressive prior with a score-based diffusion model, and eliminates TAP's separate, expensive planning phase by showing that optimal latent action sampling is equivalent to energy-guided diffusion sampling (building on QGPO's framework). The architecture follows the latent diffusion model paradigm of Vahdat et al. (2021), combining a VAE encoder/decoder with a diffusion prior, while the planning mechanism adapts the 'diffusion-sampling-as-planning' framework from Decision Diffuser (Ajay et al., 2023) to the latent space. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the end-to-end unification of representation learning and planning: unlike TAP (two-stage: learn discrete latent actions, then plan) or HDMI (hierarchical diffusion in raw space), LatentDiffuser jointly learns a continuous latent action space and performs planning through conditional diffusion sampling within that space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical bridge between latent action planning and energy-guided diffusion sampling, offering a principled justification for why planning in a learned latent space is both more efficient and more flexible than raw action space planning. Performance gains are most pronounced in high-dimensional (Adroit, 24 DoF) and long-horizon sparse-reward (AntMaze) settings, where raw action space methods degrade most severely. Key limitations include the iterative diffusion sampling cost at inference, the requirement that latent step size L and planning horizon H remain fixed, and untested applicability to stochastic dynamics. An open question is whether adaptive L and H, or accelerated sampling/distillation techniques, could close the remaining gap to the best baselines on lower-dimensional tasks where LatentDiffuser's advantages are less clear.
