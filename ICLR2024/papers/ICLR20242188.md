## Problem

Existing 3D portrait editing methods either support only preset attribute manipulation, require per-prompt optimization, or are restricted to synthetic faces. No end-to-end framework exists that accepts a single real-world portrait image and a free-form human instruction (including multi-semantic edits) and produces 3D-consistent results in a single forward pass without per-instruction optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242188/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB portrait image and a natural-language instruction string (which may concatenate multiple editing requests) as input. It outputs a NeRF representation that can be rendered into multi-view images (4 views spanning yaw [−30°, 30°] and pitch [−20°, 20°]) and 3D geometry, produced via 15-step DDIM sampling in the W+ latent space (14×512 dimensions) of a fixed EG3D generator.

## Method

InstructPix2NeRF trains a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) in the W+ space of EG3D, conditioned on both the original image's latent code (via concatenation and adaptive layer-norm identity modulation) and the instruction's CLIP text embedding (via cross-attention). Two key training strategies distinguish it: (1) Token Position Randomization (TPR), which randomly offsets the starting position of instruction tokens within the 77-token sequence so the model attends uniformly to all parts of a multi-instruction prompt, enabling simultaneous multi-semantic edits; and (2) an identity consistency module that injects ArcFace identity features through adaLN and adds a regularization loss (L_ID) between the 2D edited image and a one-step NeRF render at the front pose, applied only at low diffusion timesteps. The model is trained on 640K triplet examples (original face, edited face, single instruction) generated by combining e4e and InstructPix2Pix edits with ChatGPT-generated instructions, and uses classifier-free guidance with separate image and text guidance scales at inference.

## Results

On multi-view face editing with multiple instructions (Table 3), InstructPix2NeRF achieves the best identity consistency (ID 0.55), instruction alignment (CLIP 0.20), and 3D consistency (Md 0.105, Sd 0.038) among all methods. For single-instruction editing (Table 2), the method attains ID scores of 0.56–0.60 and CLIP scores of 0.13–0.20 across bangs, eyeglasses, and smile attributes, consistently outperforming baselines. The Token Position Randomization strategy improves the minimum attribute-altering score from 0.08 to 0.52 on multi-instruction editing, confirming balanced handling of all editing requirements in a single pass.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructPix2NeRF outperforms all three baselines across every metric in both single- and multi-instruction settings. Compared to InstructPix2Pix (the strongest baseline), the proposed method improves ID by 0.09 (0.55 vs. 0.46), CLIP by 0.01 (0.20 vs. 0.19), and reduces Md by 0.004 (0.105 vs. 0.109) on multi-instruction editing, while also achieving a substantially higher AAmin (0.52 vs. 0.31), indicating more balanced fulfillment of all editing requirements. Talk-To-Edit combined with PREIM3D performs poorly on instruction alignment (CLIP 0.05) and attribute altering (AAavg 0.24), largely because it relies on a fixed word bank and cannot handle natural-language instructions or multiple edits. The img2img baseline suffers from the lowest identity score (0.37) and highest attribute dependency (ADavg 0.88), reflecting its inability to disentangle editing requirements without the triplet data mechanism. A user study with 30 volunteers (1,440 votes) further confirms that InstructPix2NeRF is preferred over InstructPix2Pix (68.3%–83.3% preference rate) and Talk-To-Edit (74.2%–95.8%) on both instruction correspondence and identity consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of NeRF-based GAN portrait editing (EG3D, IDE-3D, PREIM3D) and instruction-following diffusion models (InstructPix2Pix, DiT), bridging two previously separate lines of work. It departs from prior text-guided 3D editing (Rodin, ClipFace) by operating on real-world faces end-to-end rather than synthetic avatars or per-prompt optimization, and from 2D instructed editing (InstructPix2Pix, Talk-To-Edit) by lifting the diffusion process into the W+ latent space of a NeRF generator. The triplet data mechanism and classifier-free guidance are directly inherited from InstructPix2Pix, while the token position randomization and identity consistency module are novel contributions specific to the 3D setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end framework for instructed 3D-aware portrait editing from a single real-world image, removing the need for per-prompt optimization and enabling interactive multi-semantic editing in a single pass. The authors acknowledge that semantically identical instructions (e.g., 'turn hair pink' vs. 'change hair to pink') produce inconsistent color results, and fine details like eye shape and eyelashes remain challenging. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the FFHQ-trained EG3D generator to more diverse 3D representations, and whether the identity consistency module can be made more robust for extreme edits (e.g., full style transfer to cartoon or bronze). The work positions itself as a strong baseline for future instructed 3D face editing research.
