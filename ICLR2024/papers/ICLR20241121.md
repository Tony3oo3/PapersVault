## Problem

Existing continual learning methods for language models each suffer from at least one practical limitation: replay-based approaches require access to past data (raising privacy and resource concerns), regularization-based methods degrade over long task sequences, and architecture-based methods require a task-ID at inference time. Moreover, prior work has largely been confined to a single task type (e.g., text classification), leaving the broader spectrum of language tasks unexplored. This paper seeks a unified solution that eliminates all three constraints simultaneously while generalizing across diverse task types and domains.

## Input / Output

At inference, a raw text instance is first mapped to a fixed-dimensional query vector by a frozen Sentence-BERT encoder; this query drives retrieval over a bank of learned key vectors, and the retrieved low-rank weight increments are combined to re-parameterize the frozen backbone. The output format is task-dependent: discrete class labels for classification benchmarks (AG News, Yelp, Amazon, DBPedia, Yahoo), a single-letter multiple-choice answer for MMLU, a sentiment word for Finance, and free-form generated text (up to 512 tokens, scored with BERTScore) for the Medical QA task. No task-ID or external metadata is required at inference.

## Method

SLM couples two components: Dynamic Task-related Knowledge Retrieval (DTKR) and Joint Adaptive Re-parameterization (JARe). DTKR maintains a bank of key–value pairs [k_i, Δθ_i] where each key is a learnable vector estimating a task-distribution centroid and each value is a LoRA-style low-rank weight increment (ΔW = BA, r ≪ min(d,k)) applied to the out-projection of attention layers. Keys are initialized as a (semi)-orthogonal matrix and refined during a preparation stage via gradient ascent on cosine similarity with Sentence-BERT queries; group-based retrieval (partitioning both query and keys into g sub-vectors) and random key masking (Bernoulli dropout of similarity scores) prevent the optimizer from collapsing onto a subset of keys. At inference, the frozen Sentence-BERT query retrieves the top-K most similar keys via cosine similarity, and JARe re-parameterizes the frozen pretrained model by computing a similarity-weighted average of the corresponding K weight increments (θ' = θ + Σ D(p,p_i)·Δθ_i / Σ D(p,p_i)), effectively performing a per-instance 'model soup' over task-specific LoRA adapters. The pretrained backbone remains frozen throughout; only the small LoRA matrices and the key vectors are trained, keeping additional parameters to ~0.3–0.7% of the base model size.

## Results

On the BERT continual learning benchmark (5 text classification tasks, 4 orders), SLM achieves 79.1% average accuracy without requiring experience replay or task-ID, surpassing the previous SOTA IDBR (76.3%, which requires replay) and ProgPrompt (77.9%, which requires task-ID). On the T5 few-shot benchmark, SLM reaches 73.1% average accuracy, outperforming LFPT5 (52.7%) by over 20 points. On the LLaMA-2 benchmark spanning medical QA, MMLU, and finance sentiment, SLM achieves 82.3% average versus 62.3% for Replay and 38.5% for Finetune, with forgetting reduced by up to 80% (≤0.5% degradation vs. 2.9% for IDBR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLM outperforms all baselines across every benchmark without requiring experience replay or task-ID at inference. Against IDBR (Huang et al., 2021), which uses data replay plus regularization, SLM gains +2.8 points on BERT (79.1 vs. 76.3) and reduces forgetting from 2.9% to 0.5%. Against ProgPrompt (Razdaibiedina et al., 2023), which requires task-ID to select the correct prompt set, SLM achieves +1.2 points on BERT (79.1 vs. 77.9) while operating in a strictly harder setting (no task-ID). On the T5 few-shot benchmark, SLM (73.1%) surpasses LFPT5 (Qin & Joty, 2021) by 20.4 points, with LFPT5 still requiring slight experience replay. On the LLaMA-2 benchmark, SLM (82.3%) dramatically outperforms the Replay baseline (62.3%, following He et al., 2021 and Huang et al., 2021 with 1% sampled instances) and Finetune (38.5%), demonstrating that direct fine-tuning causes near-complete catastrophic forgetting of earlier tasks. The MBPA++ baseline (de Masson D'Autume et al., 2019) trails SLM by 4.8 points on BERT while storing all seen examples in episodic memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) for the low-rank weight increment formulation and on the orthogonal key initialization strategy from Wang et al. (2022), but departs from the broader continual learning literature by replacing fixed prompts or adapters with a retrieval-based dynamic combination of weight increments. Where prior architecture-based methods (Progressive Prompts, LFPT5) require task-IDs at inference, and replay/regularization methods (MBPA++, IDBR) require stored data or optimization constraints, SLM eliminates all three simultaneously by using Sentence-BERT queries to retrieve and interpolate LoRA-style weight increments via KNN in a vector space. The conceptual leap is treating continual learning as a retrieval-and-reparameterization problem rather than a parameter-accumulation or regularization problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the three most common practical barriers (experience replay, optimization constraints, inference task-ID) in a single framework, SLM makes continual learning substantially more deployable in real-world settings like open-domain chatbots where task provenance is unknown. The extension to LLaMA-2 across heterogeneous task types (medical QA, MMLU, finance sentiment) is timely and demonstrates that the approach generalizes beyond the classification-only focus of prior work. Key limitations include the additional retrieval infrastructure (Sentence-BERT encoder, key storage) and the open question of how retrieval quality degrades as the number of stored tasks grows very large. The zero-shot evaluation showing SLM slightly outperforming base LLaMA-2 on some tasks hints at a knowledge-transfer benefit that warrants further investigation.
