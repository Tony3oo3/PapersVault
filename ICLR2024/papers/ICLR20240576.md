## Problem

Existing re-ID domain adaptation benchmarks (e.g., Market-1501, VeRi-776) have training sets with strong clusterability—each identity appears across multiple cameras—which is a dataset bias rarely present in real-world deployments and artificially inflates pseudo-label method performance. Additionally, there is no unified, fair benchmark specifically designed for evaluating synthetic-to-real (Syn2Real) domain adaptation in object re-ID, where models trained on editable synthetic data must generalize to real-world images under diverse conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240576/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The source domain consists of synthetic images generated from fully editable 3D models in Unity (PersonX: 1,266 person models, 14,760 images; VehicleX: 1,362 vehicle models, 45,538 images), where users can arbitrarily control cameras, scenes, illumination, and orientation. The target domain comprises newly collected real-world images: AlicePerson (5 cameras at mixed resolutions 720×576 to 1920×1080, 42,760 bounding boxes) and AliceVehicle (16 cameras at 1920×1080 on an urban road). Critically, the target training sets contain no identity labels and lack guaranteed clusterability, while test set annotations are hidden and only accessible through an online evaluation server. Outputs are standard re-ID metrics: CMC at ranks 1, 5, 10 and mAP.

## Method

The paper introduces a benchmark framework rather than a single algorithm, but its core technical contribution is the design of target datasets with deliberately unassured clusterability (no guarantee that each ID spans multiple cameras) to better reflect real-world deployment conditions. Baseline evaluation spans three DA levels: content-level (attribute descent optimizing orientation, light direction/intensity, camera height/distance via FID minimization), pixel-level (CycleGAN and SPGAN for style transfer), and pseudo-label-based (PUL, ECN, UDA, MMT with iterative label refinement). A key empirical finding is that state-of-the-art pseudo-label methods (e.g., MMT achieving 79.46% mAP on Market-1501) drop dramatically on AlicePerson (8.89% mAP), while pixel-level methods like SPGAN become competitive, revealing that clusterability assumptions in existing benchmarks mask the true difficulty of Syn2Real DA. The benchmark also provides an online evaluation server—the first in the re-ID community—for fair, reproducible comparison.

## Results

On the new AlicePerson target (PersonX→AlicePerson), pixel-level SPGAN achieves the best mAP of 10.24%, while the state-of-the-art pseudo-label method MMT only reaches 8.89% mAP — a reversal from existing benchmarks. On Market-1501 (PersonX→Market-1501), MMT dominates with 79.46% mAP, far exceeding SPGAN (19.30%) and ECN (43.26%). For vehicle re-ID (VehicleX→AliceVehicle), MMT still leads with 27.45% mAP over SPGAN (13.83%), but the improvement margin (↑mAP = +18.37%) is roughly half that on VeRi-776 (+25.69%), confirming AliceVehicle is more challenging for pseudo-label methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most striking finding is the reversal between pseudo-label and pixel-level methods on AlicePerson: MMT (8.89% mAP) underperforms SPGAN (10.24%) and even ECN (14.36%), whereas on Market-1501 MMT achieves 79.46% mAP, nearly doubling ECN (43.26%) and quadrupling SPGAN (19.30%). This gap is attributed to the unassured clusterability of AlicePerson's training set, which undermines the clustering-based pseudo-label mining that MMT and UDA rely on. Among direct-transfer baselines, MSINet (7.27% mAP) slightly outperforms IDE (6.22%) and PCB (6.29%) on AlicePerson, but all three are far below any DA method. Attribute descent (content DA) provides modest gains (+0.19% mAP over IDE on AlicePerson) but becomes substantially more effective when combined with pixel DA (e.g., +2.04% mAP over CycleGAN alone). On the vehicle task (Table 4), MMT retains its lead on both AliceVehicle (27.45% mAP) and VeRi-776 (36.82% mAP), but the relative improvement (↑mAP) drops from +25.69% on VeRi-776 to +18.37% on AliceVehicle, again reflecting the harder clustering conditions in the new dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing a dedicated benchmark for synthetic-to-real domain adaptation in object re-ID, repurposing the fully editable PersonX and VehicleX engines as source domains and contributing two new real-world target datasets (AlicePerson, AliceVehicle) with deliberately unassured clusterability in their training splits. It positions itself against the existing Syn2Real DA literature (Bak et al. 2018, Sun & Zheng 2019, Yao et al. 2020) by adding a critical missing element: an online evaluation server with hidden test labels, which is the first such platform in the re-ID community. The empirical evaluation draws on the full spectrum of prior DA strategies—pixel-level (CycleGAN, SPGAN), content-level (attribute descent), and pseudo-label-based (PUL, ECN, UDA, MMT)—but reframes them under a more realistic target-domain structure where identities are not guaranteed to span multiple cameras.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a unified, fair, and challenging benchmark that exposes a blind spot in prior re-ID DA research: pseudo-label methods that achieve strong results on Market-1501 and VeRi-776 degrade substantially when the target training set lacks guaranteed multi-camera clusterability, suggesting that prior SOTA numbers may overestimate real-world deployability. Open questions include how to design clustering strategies robust to non-clusterable targets, whether synthetic data can eventually replace real data entirely for both training and testing, and how the synthetic-to-real domain gap differs qualitatively from real-to-real gaps. The online server and editable synthetic engines also enable future 'synthetic-to-synthetic' controlled experiments that could dissect individual visual factors' contributions to the domain gap.
