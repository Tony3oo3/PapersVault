## Problem

Pre-trained sequential VAEs (seqVAEs) cannot be directly re-used on new neural recordings due to statistical heterogeneities across sessions, animals, and recording modalities. Existing alignment methods either require access to the original training data, demand paired samples between datasets, or fail to explicitly model the spatio-temporal structure of neural dynamics. This paper asks whether an alignment can be learned in a fully source-free and unsupervised manner that leverages the shared latent dynamics across datasets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a new neural time series (spike counts or behavioral signals) of arbitrary dimensionality d_w and a pre-trained seqVAE whose encoder and latent dynamics are frozen. It produces a lightweight alignment function g_θ (an MLP or linear map) that projects new observations into the source observation space, after which the frozen encoder infers latent trajectories and a newly trained linear likelihood reconstructs or predicts observations. No access to the source dataset or paired samples is required at alignment time.

## Method

The core idea is that maximizing the ELBO on new data with a frozen encoder and dynamics implicitly learns an alignment: the inferred latents are pulled toward the learned dynamical manifold, which constrains the alignment to produce observations the pre-trained model can decode. A dataset-specific linear likelihood p_{φ_w}(w_t|x_t) is trained jointly with the alignment function g_θ, while the encoder q_ψ and dynamics p_θ remain fixed. A key technical contribution is replacing the standard one-step-ahead prior with a K-step-ahead prior (sum of log p_θ(x_{t-K+j}|x_{t-K})), which prevents the optimizer from collapsing to latents that satisfy local but not global dynamics. In the linear-Gaussian case, the paper proves (Proposition 1) that the resulting alignment is a linear transformation of the optimal MSE alignment, with the gap controlled by the observation noise Q and the signal gain AA^⊤.

## Results

On the motor cortex reaching task (real neural data), the proposed method achieves 0.66±0.023 reconstruction r² and 0.39±0.071 five-step-ahead forecasting r² for monkey hand trajectories, substantially outperforming all baselines on forecasting. On the Lorenz attractor, the method matches re-training from scratch (0.99±0.0008 reconstruction, 0.99±0.0012 forecasting r²) while requiring no access to the source data. On the Van der Pol oscillator, the method achieves forecasting r² close to re-training and demonstrates strong few-shot performance, maintaining high r² even in the one-shot regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method clearly outperforms all four baselines on forecasting across both synthetic and real data. On the motor cortex task, it achieves 0.39 forecasting r² versus 0.15 for NoMAD (the closest competitor), 0.07 for Procrustes, and negative values for ERDiff (−0.23) and Cycle-GAN (−0.81). On the Lorenz attractor, the gap is even more dramatic: the proposed method reaches 0.99 forecasting r² (matching re-training) while NoMAD drops to −0.03, ERDiff to −0.23, and Cycle-GAN to −0.42. On reconstruction, the method ties with NoMAD on the Lorenz attractor (both 0.99) and is within 0.02 of NoMAD on motor cortex (0.66 vs. 0.68), while decisively beating ERDiff, Cycle-GAN, and Procrustes. The key differentiator is that the proposed method is the only baseline-free approach that matches re-training-level forecasting, whereas NoMAD requires fitting a Gaussian to source latents and Procrustes requires paired samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the seqVAE framework (Krishnan et al., 2015) for inferring latent dynamics from neural time series and repurposes the K-step ahead dynamics regularizer from Hafner et al. (2019) as an alignment objective. It departs from prior alignment methods (NoMAD, ERDiff, Cycle-GAN, Procrustes) by being both source-free and unsupervised: rather than fitting a separate density model to the source latents (ERDiff), minimizing a KLD to a fitted Gaussian (NoMAD), or requiring paired samples (Procrustes), it directly uses the pre-trained latent dynamics as a regularizer on the alignment function. The key conceptual move is recognizing that optimizing the ELBO with a fixed encoder and dynamics implicitly learns an alignment, eliminating the need for the original training data entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This approach significantly lowers the barrier to re-using pre-trained generative models in neuroscience, where each new session or animal traditionally requires training from scratch. The method's simplicity (a small MLP alignment function trained with a single ELBO objective) makes it practical for experimentalists. However, the core assumption—that latent dynamics are identical across datasets—limits applicability to same-task or similar-task recordings and does not account for behavioral variability. Open questions include whether dynamics can be learned jointly across multiple datasets to improve generalizability, and whether fine-tuning after alignment can handle tasks with different structure but shared computation.
