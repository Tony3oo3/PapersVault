## Problem

DETR-based 3D object detectors (e.g., 3DETR) fail to learn the locality inductive bias from the limited scale of 3D training data, causing decoder queries to attend to points far away from target objects rather than focusing on local regions. This violates the fundamental locality principle of object detection and results in suboptimal performance compared to fully convolutional 3D detectors. The paper addresses this by explicitly injecting geometric position information that guides cross-attention toward points inside predicted bounding boxes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240139/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud with per-point RGB and XYZ coordinates (N×6, typically ~200K points sampled down to 40K–100K) from an indoor scene and produces K=1024 oriented 3D bounding boxes (center, size, rotation angle) with semantic class labels. Box dimensions are parameterized using an object-normalized scheme (dividing width/height by the coarsely predicted box size) rather than the scene-scale normalization used in 2D DETR, since 3D object sizes are physically consistent unlike their 2D projections. Training uses one-to-many Hungarian matching with 3D NMS at inference, and test-time augmentation via flipping is applied by default.

## Method

The core contribution is 3D Vertex Relative Position Encoding (3DV-RPE), which computes a per-point position bias based on its relative offsets to all 8 vertices of the 3D box predicted by each query at the current decoder layer. Offsets are first transformed into a canonical object coordinate system (rotated by the predicted yaw angle) to make encoding invariant to object orientation, then passed through a signed-log non-linear transform and looked up in a pre-defined 10×10×10 table via 5-D grid_sample for efficiency. The 8 per-vertex biases are summed and added directly to the cross-attention logits, providing the model with explicit inside/outside and extremity information that enforces locality. The full pipeline uses a sparse ResNet34+FPN encoder, a light-weight FFN to predict initial boxes fed to the first decoder layer, 8 decoder layers with iterative box refinement, and one-to-many matching for faster convergence.

## Results

On ScanNetV2, V-DETR with TTA achieves AP25=77.8% and AP50=66.0%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art CAGroup3D (75.1%/61.3%) by +2.7%/+4.7% and SWIN3D+CAGroup3D (76.4%/63.2%) by +1.4%/+2.8%. On SUN RGB-D, V-DETR (TTA) reaches AP25=68.0% and AP50=51.1%, outperforming all prior methods. Compared to the original 3DETR baseline, V-DETR improves AP25/AP50 from 65.0%/47.0% to 77.8%/66.0% while reducing training epochs by 50% (360 vs. 1080).

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-DETR outperforms all 15 compared baselines on both benchmarks. On ScanNetV2, the largest gains are over the DETR-family baselines: +12.8% AP25 and +19.0% AP50 over 3DETR, and +8.7%/+13.2% over Group-Free, demonstrating that 3DV-RPE addresses the core locality failure of prior DETR-based 3D detectors. Against the strongest expansion-based competitor CAGroup3D, V-DETR wins by +2.7% AP25 and +4.7% AP50, with the larger AP50 gap indicating superior localization precision. Even against SWIN3D+CAGroup3D (which uses extra pre-training data), V-DETR still leads by +1.4% AP25 and +2.8% AP50. On SUN RGB-D, gains are more modest but consistent: V-DETR (TTA) beats Point-GCC by +0.3% AP25/+0.1% AP50 and CAGroup3D by +1.2%/+0.9%. Ablations confirm that 3DV-RPE is transferable: adding it to 3DETR improves AP50 from 47.0 to 60.8, and to Group-Free from 52.8 to 62.1, validating its generality across DETR-based 3D detection architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR framework from 2D to 3D point cloud object detection, building directly on 3DETR (Misra et al., 2021) and Group-Free (Liu et al., 2021b) for the overall pipeline and query construction, and on H-DETR (Jia et al., 2022) for one-to-many matching. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 3D Vertex Relative Position Encoding (3DV-RPE), which computes position biases from offsets to all 8 vertices of predicted 3D boxes in a canonical (rotation-aligned) object space, rather than using absolute position encodings or center-only relative encodings (CRPE) as in prior work. The paper also introduces object-normalized box parameterization, departing from the scene-scale normalization inherited from 2D DETR, motivated by the observation that 3D object sizes are physically consistent unlike their 2D projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively simple DETR-based architecture, augmented with a well-motivated position encoding, can outperform substantially more complex methods (voting-based, expansion-based, class-aware grouping) on indoor 3D detection, suggesting that the bottleneck in prior 3D DETR approaches was the lack of locality inductive bias rather than architectural complexity. The 3DV-RPE component is shown to be transferable to other DETR-based 3D detectors (3DETR, Group-Free), indicating broad applicability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to outdoor 3D detection (where rotation is more complex and scenes are larger), and whether the canonical-space formulation generalizes beyond the near-zero z-rotation assumption of current indoor benchmarks. The authors explicitly note plans to unify indoor and outdoor 3D detection under this framework.
