## Problem

Generative molecular design faces two underexploited challenges: explainability (understanding why certain substructures drive high rewards) and sample efficiency (reducing expensive oracle calls, e.g., molecular docking, needed to find high-quality candidates). Existing approaches treat these as separate concerns, and the oracle budget required by state-of-the-art methods remains prohibitive for high-fidelity oracles like molecular dynamics. The paper demonstrates that these two challenges can be addressed synergistically through a single mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained SMILES-based autoregressive RNN (Augmented Memory or REINVENT) and a composite reward function (e.g., AutoDock Vina docking score + QED + MW < 500 Da) under a fixed oracle budget (5,000 calls). It outputs generated SMILES molecules, a Pool of extracted molecular substructures (Bemis-Murcko scaffolds or arbitrary valid structures with a configurable minimum character size), and a self-conditioned generation loop where only molecules containing at least one pooled substructure are passed to the oracle. Substructure extraction operates on partial token sequences of length ≥ 3 characters, since each SMILES character loosely maps to one atom and three is the minimum for meaningful functional groups.

## Method

Beam Enumeration sequentially enumerates the top-k most probable tokens for N beam steps from the autoregressive model's conditional probability distributions, yielding an exhaustive set of k^N token sub-sequences. Valid molecular substructures are extracted by parsing all (sub)-sub-sequences of length ≥ 3 as partial SMILES and counting frequency; the most frequent substructures populate a Pool that self-conditions generation by filtering each sampled batch to retain only molecules containing at least one pooled substructure before oracle evaluation. Beam Enumeration is triggered when the reward improves for Patience consecutive epochs, ensuring extracted substructures reflect a converged optimization trajectory rather than stochastic noise. The core insight is that during successful RL, the model's weights shift to increase the probability of high-reward molecules, so the most probable partial trajectories serve as a reliable proxy for identifying structurally meaningful substructures 'on track' to yield high reward. Combined with Augmented Memory's experience replay and augmented likelihood, this yields up to a 29-fold improvement in sample efficiency for challenging docking objectives.

## Results

In three drug discovery case studies (DRD2, MK2, AChE) with a 5,000 AutoDock Vina oracle budget, Augmented Memory + Beam Enumeration (Structure, Min Size 15) achieves a 29× improvement in Generative Yield >0.8 for the most challenging target (MK2: 987±211 vs. 34±13 baseline) and reduces Oracle Burden >0.8 (100 molecules) from 'Failed' to 2,189±181 calls. For DRD2 and AChE, Yield >0.8 improves by ~5× and ~4× respectively (1,780 vs. 363; 2,059 vs. 556). Across all targets, the combined algorithm identifies 100 unique high-reward molecules in under 2,000 oracle calls, compared to 2,558–4,595 for the baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four configurations: Augmented Memory (AM) baseline, AM + Beam Enumeration, REINVENT baseline, and REINVENT + Beam Enumeration. Beam Enumeration (Structure, Min Size 15) dramatically outperforms the AM baseline on the challenging MK2 target, improving Yield >0.8 by 29× (987 vs. 34) and reducing Oracle Burden >0.8 (100) from 'Failed' to ~2,189 calls. For DRD2 and AChE, the improvement is more moderate (~5× and ~4× in Yield >0.8) but still substantial. AM + Beam Enumeration consistently outperforms REINVENT + Beam Enumeration across all targets and metrics, confirming that the experience-replay mechanism in AM synergizes with self-conditioned generation. REINVENT + Beam Enumeration also improves over REINVENT baseline (e.g., MK2 Yield >0.8: 179 vs. 2), but the gains are smaller than for AM. The only metric where baselines match or slightly beat Beam Enumeration is Oracle Burden >0.8 (1) for DRD2/AChE, where the pre-trained ChEMBL model already generates high-reward molecules at epoch 1, making the task trivially easy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Augmented Memory (Guo & Schwaller 2023), a SMILES-based LSTM RNN that casts molecular optimization as on-policy RL with experience replay, which itself extends REINVENT (Olivecrona et al. 2017; Blaschke et al. 2020). Conceptually, it departs from prior beam search applications to molecular design (Moret et al. 2021), which use beam search to find a small set of highest-probability full sequences, by instead exhaustively enumerating top-k token sub-sequences to extract molecular substructures for self-conditioned generation. It also distinguishes itself from post-hoc explainability methods (Grad-CAM, SHAP, MMACE, pBRICS) by deriving explainability directly from the generative model's own token probability distributions, modulated by reward feedback during RL training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key conceptual contribution is demonstrating that explainability and sample efficiency in molecular design can be made synergistic rather than competing objectives: extracted substructures both explain why certain molecules receive high reward and self-condition generation to focus oracle calls on promising chemical regions. The practical impact is significant for expensive high-fidelity oracles (e.g., MD simulations for binding energy), where the 29-fold sample efficiency gain on MK2 and the ability to identify excellent candidates in under 2,000 oracle calls could make direct optimization of such oracles feasible. Limitations include a diversity trade-off, dependence on language-based (SMILES) models, and the acknowledged difficulty of sparse reward environments. The Oracle Burden metric and the recommended default hyperparameters (validated across 2,224 experiments) lower the barrier to out-of-the-box application.
