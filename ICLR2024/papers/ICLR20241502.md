## Problem

Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT) preserves pairwise neuron angles during adaptation, yielding strong generalization, but its full orthogonal matrix requires O(d²) trainable parameters. The block-diagonal sparsity pattern used to reduce parameters introduces a fixed, index-based grouping that cannot approximate classic linear transforms and leaves the choice of sparsity pattern unprincipled. The paper asks whether a dense orthogonal matrix can be constructed with far fewer parameters while retaining expressiveness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained linear layer W⁰ ∈ R^{d×n} and a downstream task dataset, and produces a finetuned layer R(m,b)·W⁰ where R is a learned d×d orthogonal butterfly matrix. Because R is multiplicative and orthogonal, the learned butterfly components can be directly multiplied into the pretrained weights after training, introducing zero additional inference latency. For diffusion models the forward pass additionally consumes text prompts and optional spatial control signals (segmentation maps, landmarks); for vision tasks it consumes RGB images; for language tasks it consumes token sequences.

## Method

BOFT parameterizes the orthogonal update matrix R as a product of m sparse orthogonal butterfly components, each a block-diagonal matrix of 2b×2b Cayley-parameterized rotation blocks arranged in the recursive Cooley-Tukey butterfly topology. This yields a dense d×d orthogonal matrix with only ½(b−1)dm effective parameters (O(d log d) when b=2, m=log d), versus O(d²) for a full orthogonal matrix. The paper frames the sparsity design as an information-transmission problem on a d×(m+1) grid graph, where the butterfly topology achieves dense connectivity with O(d log d) edges. BOFT subsumes OFT as the m=1 special case (block-diagonal), and a multiplicative dropout that randomly replaces butterfly sub-blocks with identity provides regularization. A notable emergent property is free weight interpolation: setting trained butterfly components to identity one-by-one produces a smooth, untrained interpolation between the finetuned model and the original pretrained model.

## Results

On the VTAB-1K benchmark (19 vision tasks), BOFT(m=4, b=4) achieves 77.9% average top-1 accuracy with 1.99M trainable parameters, outperforming OFT (77.3%, 2.10M), LoRA (76.6%, 1.77M), and even full finetuning (74.6%, 304.4M). On GLUE, BOFT(2,8) reaches 89.89 average with 0.75M parameters, surpassing OFT(16) (89.77, 0.79M) and LoRA (88.50, 1.33M). On MMLU (5-shot), BOFT(2,8) achieves 47.9% average with only 0.12% of parameters, beating LoRA(r=32) (47.3%, 0.25%) and OFT(16) (47.5%, 0.13%). For high-quality segmentation with SAM, BOFT-SAM(2,8) matches HQ-SAM's 81.8% average mBIoU using only 3% of its trainable parameters (0.06M vs 1.33M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOFT consistently outperforms OFT (its direct predecessor) across all evaluated domains while using fewer or comparable parameters: on GLUE, BOFT(2,8) beats OFT(16) by +0.12 avg with 5% fewer parameters; on MMLU, BOFT(2,8) exceeds OFT(16) by +0.4 (5-shot) with fewer parameters; on VTAB-1K, BOFT(4,4) surpasses OFT(16) by +0.6 avg. Against LoRA, BOFT wins on every benchmark: GLUE (+1.39 avg), MMLU 5-shot (+0.6), GSM8K (+0.4), VTAB-1K (+1.3 avg), and ControlNet (5.667 vs 8.038 landmark error at comparable parameter budgets). Notably, BOFT also beats full finetuning on VTAB-1K by +3.3% average accuracy while using 153× fewer parameters, and matches HQ-SAM's segmentation quality (82.2 vs 81.8 avg mBIoU) with only 4.5% of its trainable parameters. The one area where BOFT does not clearly dominate is the S2I (segmentation-to-image) control task, where OFT with more parameters (20.89M) achieves slightly better mIoU (29.44 vs 28.83) than BOFT at the same budget, though BOFT still outperforms LoRA (24.72).

## Relation to Prior Work

BOFT directly extends Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT, Qiu et al., NeurIPS 2023), which introduced multiplicative orthogonal matrix updates to preserve pairwise neuron angles during finetuning of text-to-image diffusion models. The key departure is replacing OFT's block-diagonal sparsity pattern with a butterfly factorization inspired by the Cooley-Tukey FFT algorithm, enabling dense orthogonal matrices with only O(d log d) parameters instead of O(d²). Conceptually, BOFT positions itself as the sparse-structured counterpart to LoRA's low-rank structure, and its expressivity theorem connects to the kaleidoscope hierarchy of Dao et al. (ICLR 2020). The information transmission framework provides a unified graphical lens for reasoning about which sparsity patterns yield dense products, a perspective not previously applied to finetuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that orthogonal finetuning generalizes beyond controllable text-to-image generation to LLMs, vision transformers, and segmentation, validating it as a generic PEFT paradigm. The free weight interpolation property—smoothly transitioning between the finetuned and pretrained model by zeroing butterfly components without retraining—is a unique structural bonus with no analogue in LoRA or other PEFT methods. Open questions include whether butterfly is the optimal network topology for information transmission (the authors explicitly flag this), whether the training-time overhead from multiple sparse matrix multiplications can be reduced, and whether the inductive bias from butterfly structure (shared with FFT/DCT/Hadamard) explains the consistent cross-domain gains or is merely a parameter-counting artifact.
