## Problem

Existing large multimodal models (e.g., Flamingo, BLIP-2) train exclusively on next-text-token prediction with frozen vision encoders, providing no supervision signal to the visual pathway and severely restricting model capacity. Additionally, these models largely overlook video data as a scalable source of naturally interleaved multimodal sequences, missing a rich signal of dense cross-modal correlations that regular image-text documents lack.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240487/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Emu accepts arbitrary interleaved sequences of images, text, and video frames (videos sampled as 8 frames at 224×224 resolution). Each image is encoded by EVA-CLIP into dense features, then transformed by a Causal Transformer into N fixed-length causal visual embeddings bracketed by [IMG] and [/IMG] tokens; text is tokenized as usual. At inference, the model autoregressively generates either discrete text tokens (via a language modeling head) or N continuous visual embeddings (via a regression head), which are decoded into a 512×512 image by a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion U-Net conditioned on those embeddings.

## Method

Emu unifies vision and language under a single autoregressive objective: at each step, the model either classifies the next text token (cross-entropy loss) or regresses the next visual embedding (ℓ₂ loss), with all elements in the sequence contributing to the loss. The key architectural innovation is the Causal Transformer, a 12-block decoder-style module that converts 2D spatial image features from EVA-CLIP into a 1D causal latent sequence of N embeddings via cross-attention (image features as keys/values, learned queries), thereby imposing a left-to-right generative structure on images that naturally lack one. The full 14B-parameter model (EVA-CLIP-1B + Causal Transformer + LLaMA-13B + Stable Diffusion decoder) is trained end-to-end on ~82M samples (150B tokens) spanning image-text pairs, interleaved documents (MMC4), video-text pairs (WebVid-10M), and the newly introduced YT-Storyboard-1B dataset of 1.8 billion storyboard thumbnails interleaved with subtitles. After pretraining, a separate visual decoder fine-tuning stage (15k steps on LAION-COCO and LAION-Aesthetics) aligns the regressed embeddings to the Stable Diffusion latent space, and optional LoRA-based instruction tuning on multimodal conversational data yields the Emu-I assistant.

## Results

On zero-shot image captioning, Emu achieves a CIDEr score of 112.4 on COCO and 108.8 (Emu-I) on NoCaps, substantially outperforming Kosmos-1 (84.7) and Flamingo-9B (79.4). On visual question answering, Emu-I* reaches 62.0% on VQAv2 and 49.2% on OKVQA, surpassing Flamingo-9B* (51.8% and 44.7%) and Kosmos-1 (51.0% and N/A). For text-to-image generation, Emu achieves a zero-shot FID of 11.66 on MS-COCO, outperforming GILL (12.20) but trailing dedicated diffusion models like SDv1.5 (9.93). On the in-the-wild MM-Vet benchmark, Emu-I-14B scores 36.3 total, exceeding LLaVA-65B (35.5) and DreamLLM-7B (35.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Emu consistently outperforms Kosmos-1 and Flamingo-9B across most zero-shot benchmarks: on VizWiz, Emu achieves 34.4% vs. Kosmos-1's 29.2% and Flamingo-9B's 28.8%; on Flickr30K captioning, Emu scores 72.0 CIDEr vs. Kosmos-1's 67.1 and Flamingo-9B's 61.5. The instruction-tuned Emu-I* (14B parameters) surpasses the much larger Flamingo-80B on VQAv2 (62.0% vs. 56.3%), VizWiz (38.3% vs. 31.6%), and MSVDQA (37.0% vs. 35.6%). However, Emu trails PALI-X-55B (which uses per-task finetuning) by a wide margin on COCO (112.4 vs. 149.2) and VQAv2 (52.0 vs. 86.0), reflecting the gap between zero-shot and finetuned evaluation. On text-to-image generation, Emu (FID 11.66) beats the concurrent multimodal model GILL (12.20) but falls short of dedicated unimodal diffusion models such as SDv1.5 (9.93) and Imagen (7.27). On the MM-Vet in-the-wild benchmark, Emu-I-14B (36.3) edges out LLaVA-65B (35.5) and DreamLLM-7B (35.9), while trailing only in OCR and Math sub-scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

Emu departs from the dominant LMM paradigm (Flamingo, BLIP-2, Kosmos-1) in two fundamental ways: it replaces the text-only next-token prediction objective with a unified autoregressive objective that supervises both discrete text tokens (cross-entropy) and continuous visual embeddings (L2 regression), and it introduces a Causal Transformer module to convert 2D spatial image features into 1D causal sequences, addressing the mismatch between image structure and sequential modeling. Architecturally, it composes existing pretrained components (EVA-CLIP, LLaMA-13B, Stable Diffusion) into a single end-to-end trainable 14B system, while data-wise it extends beyond image-text pairs and interleaved documents to include video (YT-Storyboard-1B) as a scalable source of naturally interleaved multimodal sequences. This positions Emu as a direct response to the limitation that prior LMMs treat vision as a frozen input modality with no generative supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified autoregressive formulation enables a single model to serve as a generalist interface for both understanding (captioning, VQA) and generation (text-to-image, in-context image generation, image blending), with the emergent in-context image generation capability (style transfer from context examples) being a qualitatively new behavior not present in prior LMMs. The introduction of YT-Storyboard-1B (1.8B storyboard images with subtitles) as a practical, low-cost video data source is a concrete contribution to the multimodal data landscape. However, the model's text-to-image FID (11.66) remains inferior to dedicated diffusion models like SDv1.5 (9.93), it is English-only, and the autoregressive inference speed is a practical bottleneck—open questions include whether the Causal Transformer approach scales to higher resolutions and whether the unified objective can be extended to audio or other modalities.
