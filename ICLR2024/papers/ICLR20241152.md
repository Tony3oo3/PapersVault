## Problem

Current ML approaches for solving PDEs enforce physical constraints via the PINN loss, which requires expensive numerical quadrature of higher-order derivatives over many sampled points, leading to quadratic backpropagation cost and ill-posed optimization. Grid-based neural operators additionally suffer from aliasing error when tested at resolutions different from training, and their computational cost scales with grid size. This paper asks whether PDE constraints can be enforced exactly and efficiently by working entirely in the spectral domain.

## Input / Output

The model takes a PDE parameter field (source term, initial condition, or coefficient field) and outputs the corresponding solution field (potential, vorticity, or concentration), both represented as truncated spectral coefficient vectors. Periodic dimensions use Fourier bases while non-periodic dimensions use Chebyshev polynomials, with the number of modes fixed per layer (e.g., 31 spatial modes, 32 temporal modes). At inference, the model operates on fixed collocation points, so computational cost and prediction quality remain constant regardless of the query resolution, unlike grid-based methods that require re-interpolation.

## Method

NSM parameterizes the solution operator as a neural operator whose kernel integrals reduce to tensor contractions on spectral coefficients (exploiting orthogonality), with a diagonal kernel in the FNO style for linear complexity. The key training innovation is a spectral loss: the PDE residual is computed exactly by converting the differential operator to its spectral counterpart (where differentiation becomes algebraic multiplication on coefficients, e.g., d/dx → 2πm for Fourier modes), and Parseval's identity equates the L² residual norm to the ℓ² norm of the residual's spectral coefficients. Non-linear activations are applied at fixed collocation points in physical space (via interpolation) rather than directly on spectral coefficients, which the ablations show is critical for robustness. This eliminates the need for numerical quadrature, higher-order autograd through the network, and large point samples, reducing training cost by 100× while achieving 10× lower error than grid-based PINN-trained operators.

## Results

On the 2D periodic Poisson equation, NSM achieves 0.057% L2 relative error, outperforming FNO×256²+PINN (1.75%), T1+Spectral (0.302%), and SNO+Spectral (0.59%). On the 1D Reaction-Diffusion equation, NSM maintains 0.075–0.086% L2 error across all diffusion coefficients (ν = 0.005 to 0.1), while FNO×256²+PINN degrades from 0.33% to 4.07% and SNO+Spectral degrades from 4.56% to 152.8%. NSM requires only 15.0 GFLOP for training and 0.32 GFLOP for inference, compared to 138.5/15.8 GFLOP for FNO×256²+PINN and 200.3/25.0 GFLOP for CNO+PINN, representing a 100× training speedup and 500× inference speedup over grid-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NSM consistently outperforms all three baselines across every PDE tested. Against FNO+PINN (Li et al., 2020), NSM achieves 1–2 orders of magnitude lower L2 error (e.g., 0.057% vs. 1.75% on Poisson; 0.075% vs. 0.33% on Reaction-Diffusion at ν=0.005) while being 100× faster to train and 500× faster at inference; FNO+PINN also suffers from aliasing error when tested at resolutions different from its training grid, whereas NSM maintains constant error and cost. Against SNO+Spectral (Fanaskov & Oseledets, 2022), NSM is substantially more accurate (0.057% vs. 0.59% on Poisson; 0.075% vs. 4.56% on Reaction-Diffusion at ν=0.005), and the gap widens dramatically as problem difficulty increases (SNO reaches 152.8% error at ν=0.1 while NSM stays at 0.077%). Against TransformOnce (Poli et al., 2022), NSM again wins on accuracy (0.057% vs. 0.302% on Poisson; 0.075% vs. 0.68% on Reaction-Diffusion at ν=0.005), and T1+PINN degrades severely at higher ν values (reaching 94–101% error at ν=0.1). When compared to iterative numerical solvers at equivalent accuracy, NSM achieves a 10× speedup at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the neural operator framework (Kovachki et al., 2021) and the diagonal-kernel approximation of FNO (Li et al., 2020), but departs fundamentally from grid-based training by moving both the architecture and the loss into the spectral domain. It is conceptually adjacent to SNO (Fanaskov & Oseledets, 2022) and TransformOnce (Poli et al., 2022), which also operate in frequency/spectral space, but differs in applying non-linear activations at collocation points rather than on coefficients, and in using a self-supervised spectral loss derived from Parseval's identity rather than supervised data-fitting. The key departure from PINN-based training (Raissi et al., 2019) is replacing numerical quadrature of the residual with an exact algebraic computation on spectral coefficients, eliminating the need for large point samples and higher-order autodifferentiation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× training speedup, 500× inference speedup, and resolution independence (constant cost regardless of grid) make spectral-based neural operators a practical alternative to both grid-based ML solvers and traditional numerical solvers for smooth PDE solutions. The theoretical guarantee that the spectral loss yields exact residuals (via Parseval's identity) and the error analysis showing grid-based PINN loss can have non-zero expected error even when minimized are clean contributions that clarify a long-standing optimization pathology. Limitations include the requirement for solution regularity (spectral convergence), restriction to low-dimensional problems, and dependence on periodic or amenable boundary conditions; extensions to high-dimensional or singular problems remain open.
