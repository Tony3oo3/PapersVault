## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models struggle to generate realistic spatial layouts and temporal dynamics from complex prompts, often producing restricted or physically incorrect motion. While LLMs have been shown to generate static spatial layouts for image generation, it remains unexplored whether they can produce coherent spatiotemporal bounding box sequences (dynamic scene layouts) that capture object motion, physical properties, and camera geometry from text alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241957/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 16-frame video at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. The intermediate representation is a Dynamic Scene Layout (DSL): a sequence of 6 frames (at 2 fps on a 512×512 canvas) where each frame contains a list of bounding boxes with unique object IDs, object names, and [x, y, w, h] coordinates, plus a background keyword. The DSL is generated by the LLM and then used to condition the diffusion model via cross-attention guidance.

## Method

LVD is a two-stage, fully training-free pipeline. In Stage 1, an LLM (GPT-4 or GPT-3.5) is prompted with task instructions and only three in-context examples (demonstrating gravity, elasticity, and perspective projection) to generate a DSL — a sequence of per-frame bounding boxes with persistent object IDs — from the text prompt. In Stage 2, the DSL conditions an off-the-shelf text-to-video diffusion model (ModelScope) via classifier guidance on cross-attention maps: each DSL box is converted to a binary mask, and an energy function E = E_topk + E_CoM is minimized by gradient descent on the partially denoised video before each diffusion step. E_topk encourages high attention values inside the box and low values outside, while E_CoM enforces matching center-of-mass position and velocity between the attention map and the box mask to handle slow or overlapping motion. The key insight is that LLMs possess inherent physical knowledge (e.g., rocks don't bounce, paper airplanes glide) that generalizes beyond the three provided examples, enabling them to produce physically plausible spatiotemporal layouts without any task-specific training.

## Results

On the proposed 5-task spatiotemporal alignment benchmark (500 prompts, 1000 videos), LVD with GPT-4 achieves 49.4% average detection-based text-video alignment, nearly doubling ModelScope's 23.0% and substantially outperforming the retrieval-based baseline (9.7%). For DSL generation quality, GPT-4 achieves 98% average accuracy across all five tasks versus 14% for retrieval-based layouts. On video quality (FVD), LVD improves over the ModelScope baseline on both UCF-101 (828 vs. 984) and MSR-VTT (565 vs. 590). In human evaluation, LVD is preferred for prompt alignment 85.8% of the time and for visual quality 66.0% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LVD decisively outperforms its base model ModelScope (Wang et al., 2023) on the primary text-video alignment metric, achieving 49.4% vs. 23.0% average accuracy, with the largest gains on Visibility (+47 pts) and Sequential actions (+38 pts). The retrieval-based baseline (using WebVid-2M + GLIP + SORT) performs worse than even the unguided ModelScope, confirming that nearest-neighbor video retrieval cannot recover matching spatiotemporal layouts. On FVD video quality, LVD improves over the replicated ModelScope on both UCF-101 (828 vs. 984) and MSR-VTT (565 vs. 590), and surpasses CogVideo and MagicVideo on MSR-VTT, though it trails Make-A-Video (367) and VideoLDM (551) on UCF-101 FVD, likely due to differences in training data and model architecture. The paper notes that the gap between DSL generation accuracy (98% for GPT-4) and video alignment accuracy (49.4%) indicates the DSL-to-video stage remains the bottleneck. Human evaluators confirm the quantitative findings, preferring LVD over ModelScope in 96.8% of alignment comparisons and 85.5% of quality comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-grounded diffusion paradigm from static images (Lian et al., 2023) to dynamic video by introducing Dynamic Scene Layouts (DSLs) — sequences of bounding boxes linked across frames — as an intermediate representation between text prompts and video diffusion. The attention-guidance mechanism for enforcing layout constraints is adapted from training-free box-constrained diffusion (BoxDiff, Xie et al., 2023), with an additional Center-of-Mass energy term to handle temporal dynamics. Unlike concurrent work like VideoDirectorGPT, which trains diffusion adapters for multi-scene generation, LVD is entirely training-free and focuses on inference-time guidance, making it applicable to any video diffusion model that supports classifier guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a plug-and-play, training-free pipeline that can improve text-video alignment on existing models without retraining, plus a 500-prompt benchmark with rule-based verification that the field can adopt for evaluating spatiotemporal prompt adherence. However, the method inherits all quality limitations of the base model (e.g., poor cartoon generation, static backgrounds), and the cross-attention guidance can produce artifacts (e.g., objects hiding behind leaves to satisfy the energy function). The large gap between DSL accuracy (98% with GPT-4) and video alignment (49.4%) highlights that the DSL-to-video stage remains the bottleneck, and extending this to more complex dynamics (human poses, multi-object interactions) or higher resolutions is left open.
