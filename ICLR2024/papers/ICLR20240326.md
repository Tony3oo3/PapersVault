## Problem

Extending the context window of pre-trained LLMs via fine-tuning is prohibitively expensive: self-attention cost scales quadratically with sequence length, and prior methods like Position Interpolation and Focused Transformer require 32–128 GPUs for full fine-tuning. Plain LoRA, while parameter-efficient, fails to close the performance gap with full fine-tuning for long-context adaptation, and increasing LoRA rank does not help. The paper asks whether context extension can be done efficiently on a single 8×A100 machine without sacrificing quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Llama2 model (7B, 13B, or 70B) and long-context training sequences (up to 100k tokens from RedPajama) as input. It produces a set of trainable LoRA low-rank matrices for the attention projection layers plus updated embedding and normalization parameters, while all other weights remain frozen. At inference, the model uses its original standard full self-attention architecture unchanged, so it is directly compatible with existing inference infrastructure like Flash-Attention2 without any architectural modification.

## Method

LongLoRA combines two key components. First, Shifted Sparse Attention (S²-Attn) replaces full self-attention during training by splitting the sequence into groups and computing attention only within each group; in half the attention heads, tokens are shifted by half the group size so that neighboring groups exchange information, mimicking the shifted-window design of Swin Transformer. This requires only two lines of PyTorch code and reduces attention FLOPs by up to 4× at 65k context, while the model reverts to standard full attention at inference. Second, the paper identifies that making the embedding and normalization layers trainable (denoted LoRA⁺) is essential to close the perplexity gap between LoRA and full fine-tuning for context extension—normalization alone accounts for <0.004% of parameters in Llama2-7B yet is critical. Together, these two changes enable extending Llama2-7B to 100k and Llama2-70B to 32k context on a single 8×A100 machine with comparable perplexity to full fine-tuning.

## Results

On the LongBench benchmark, LongLoRA-7B achieves an average score of 36.8, outperforming LongChat-v1.5-7B (34.3), Vicuna-v1.5-7B (31.9), and Llama2-7B-chat (31.0), though trailing GPT-3.5-Turbo (44.0). On the LEval open-ended benchmark, LongLoRA-7B achieves a 39.06 GPT-4-judged win-rate, surpassing LongChat-7B (33.68) and Vicuna-v1.5-7B (25.52). In terms of efficiency, LongLoRA extends Llama2 7B to 100k context and 70B to 32k context on a single 8×A100 machine, using 56.6% of the training hours of plain LoRA at 65536 context length and up to 1.8× lower peak GPU memory than full fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LongChat-13B on the topic retrieval task (Table 5), LongLoRA-13B matches or slightly exceeds it at every context length, achieving 0.94 at 16k versus LongChat's 0.90, while requiring only next-token-prediction fine-tuning on RedPajama rather than full fine-tuning on self-collected conversation data. On LongBench (Table 9), LongLoRA-7B outperforms LongChat-v1.5-7B by 2.5 points on average (36.8 vs 34.3), with the largest gap in Multi-Doc QA (28.1 vs 20.6) and Summarization (27.8 vs 26.7), and beats Vicuna-v1.5-7B by 4.9 points on average. On LEval (Table 10), LongLoRA-7B leads all Llama2-based baselines with a 39.06 win-rate, exceeding LongChat-7B by 5.4 points and Vicuna-v1.5-7B by 13.5 points. In the attention-pattern ablation (Table 6), S²-Attn with full-attention testing achieves 8.12 perplexity at 32k context, substantially outperforming dilated attention (11.78), block sparse (8.30), and stride sparse (24.03), confirming that the shifted-sparse design is uniquely suited for fine-tuning pre-trained LLMs while retaining standard attention at inference. The only baseline that consistently outperforms LongLoRA is GPT-3.5-Turbo on LongBench (44.0 vs 36.8), which is a much larger proprietary model.

## Relation to Prior Work

LongLoRA builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning and borrows the shifted-window concept from Swin Transformer (Liu et al., 2021) to design S²-Attn, a sparse attention pattern used only during training. It departs from prior long-context extension methods (Position Interpolation, Focused Transformer, Landmark Attention) that require full fine-tuning on 32–128 GPUs, by showing that sparse local attention during training can approximate full attention while the model reverts to standard self-attention at inference. A key conceptual departure from standard LoRA is the finding that making embedding and normalization layers trainable—layers comprising less than 2% of total parameters—is essential for low-rank adaptation to succeed at context extension, a requirement absent from the original LoRA formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LongLoRA makes long-context extension practically accessible to individual research groups by reducing the hardware requirement from 32–128 GPUs to a single 8×A100 machine, while retaining the original model architecture at inference so that all existing infrastructure (Flash-Attention2, DeepSpeed, vLLM) remains compatible. The S²-Attn mechanism is remarkably simple (two lines of code) and the LoRA+ insight (trainable embedding/normalization) generalizes beyond this specific task to potentially improve other LoRA-based adaptations. Limitations include reliance on Position Interpolation (which causes perplexity degradation at shorter context lengths), validation primarily on Llama2, and the fact that S²-Attn introduces a small information-leakage risk at group boundaries. Open questions include compatibility with alternative position encodings (NTK-aware, YaRN), other model families, and whether the trainable-normalization finding extends to other PEFT methods.
