## Problem

Existing RLHF algorithms that learn from preference-based feedback are computationally intractable even for tabular MDPs, relying on intractable oracles (e.g., argmax over policy pairs with matrix-norm objectives) or exponentially large policy spaces. Moreover, prior theoretical work largely ignores query complexity, despite human feedback being expensive to collect. This paper seeks algorithms that are simultaneously statistically efficient (sublinear regret), computationally efficient (polynomial time), and query-efficient (near-optimal regret-query tradeoff).

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm generates two trajectories (one from the current greedy policy, one from the previous episode's greedy policy) and decides whether to query a human for a binary preference between them. The preference is modeled through a link function Φ (e.g., Bradley-Terry-Luce) applied to the cumulative reward difference, so the agent never observes per-step rewards—only trajectory-level comparisons. The output is a sequence of policies and binary query indicators; the query condition is a variance-style threshold on the expected absolute reward difference between two independently sampled reward models, making it computable via Monte Carlo sampling from the posterior.

## Method

The paper introduces two algorithms unified by the use of randomization to preserve the Markovian structure of the reward model, enabling standard dynamic programming for computation. PR-LSVI (model-free, linear MDPs) adds Gaussian noise to both the MLE reward estimate and the LSVI value estimates, with the reward covariance maintained over trajectory-wise feature differences to capture trajectory-level uncertainty while keeping the state-action-wise format needed for DP; a truncation trick controls abnormally high value estimates in high-uncertainty directions. PbTS (model-based, general function approximation) performs Thompson sampling over transition and reward posteriors, with analysis relying on ℓ₁-norm eluder dimension and bracketing numbers. Both algorithms use a novel randomized active learning procedure: a query is made only when the expected absolute reward difference between two independently drawn reward models exceeds a threshold ε, yielding a near-optimal Regret = Õ(T^{1−β}) vs. Queries = Õ(T^{2β}) tradeoff. The comparator policy is set to the previous episode's greedy policy rather than a fixed one, ensuring conditional independence from the current round's Gaussian noise and enabling optimism with constant probability.

## Results

For linear MDPs (Theorem 4.2), PR-LSVI achieves Regret_T = Õ(εT·d^{1/2} + √T·d³·H^{5/2}·γ + d^{17/2}·H^{11/2}·γ³) and Queries_T = Õ(d⁴·γ⁴/ε²), where γ = √(κ + B²). Setting ε = T^{-β} yields the clean tradeoff Regret_T = Õ(T^{1-β}) and Queries_T = Õ(T^{2β}), which matches the lower bound of Sekhari et al. (2023a) in T-dependence. For general function approximation (Theorem 5.4), PbTS achieves BayesRegret_T = Õ(T^{1-β}) and BayesQueries_T = Õ(min{T^{β+1/2}·dim₁(R̃, ε/2), T^{2β}·dim₂(R̃, ε/2)}), again achieving a near-optimal T-tradeoff. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical experiments or benchmark evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. Theoretically, the paper positions itself against prior RLHF algorithms (Saha et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2022; Zhan et al. 2023a,b) by being the first to simultaneously achieve sublinear worst-case regret and polynomial computational complexity, even for tabular MDPs where prior methods are intractable. The T-dependence of the regret-query tradeoff (T^{1-β} vs. T^{2β}) is shown to be optimal via the lower bound of Sekhari et al. (2023a) for contextual dueling bandits. Compared to RLSVI (Zanette et al. 2020), the paper highlights five key algorithmic differences including trajectory-wise noise design, MLE-based reward learning, a new regret decomposition, randomized active learning, and the use of a previous-episode comparator policy. The eluder dimension analysis uses the tighter ℓ₁-norm version (vs. ℓ₂-norm in prior TS works), yielding improved dependence on model complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the randomized value iteration line (RLSVI, Osband et al. 2016; Zanette et al. 2020) and Thompson sampling (Osband et al. 2013) to the preference-based RLHF setting, where feedback is trajectory-level comparisons rather than per-step rewards. It departs sharply from prior RLHF algorithms (Saha et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2022; Zhan et al. 2023) that achieved sublinear regret but were computationally intractable even for tabular MDPs due to intractable policy-pair optimization or version-space maintenance. The key conceptual departure is replacing UCB-style optimism with injected Gaussian noise, which preserves the Markovian structure of the reward model and enables standard dynamic programming for polynomial-time computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to simultaneously achieve sublinear regret, polynomial computational complexity, and near-optimal query complexity for RLHF, closing a gap that made prior theoretical results impractical. The near-optimal regret-query tradeoff (matching lower bounds from Sekhari et al. 2023a) provides a principled framework for deciding when to query expensive human feedback. Open questions include tightening the dependence on dimension d and horizon H in the bounds, and for the Thompson sampling algorithm, achieving simultaneously favorable T-dependence and eluder-dimension dependence in the query complexity. The practical insight that comparing a new policy against the previous episode's policy (rather than two samples from the same policy) better balances exploration is broadly applicable to RLHF implementations.
