## Problem

Diffusion generative models produce high-quality samples from relatively small training sets, seemingly escaping the curse of dimensionality, but it remains unclear whether they learn the true continuous data density or merely memorize training examples. The approximation class defined by the inductive biases of these networks is not well understood, making it difficult to determine when generalization occurs and what structural properties the learned denoising function possesses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241818/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The denoisers are universal and blind: they accept a single noisy image (corrupted with i.i.d. Gaussian noise at an arbitrary level) without explicit noise-level conditioning, and output a denoised estimate. For generation, a random Gaussian noise sample is iteratively refined through the reverse diffusion algorithm (Algorithm 1) to produce an image sample. All experiments use 80×80 RGB images (CelebA, LSUN Bedroom) or 40×40 (CelebA-HQ), with the denoiser's Jacobian eigendecomposition providing the key analytical output: adaptive basis vectors and shrinkage factors that characterize the learned representation.

## Method

The paper analyzes trained bias-free UNet and BF-CNN denoisers (trained with MSE on Gaussian-corrupted images) by performing a local eigendecomposition of their Jacobian, revealing that the denoiser performs shrinkage along a geometry-adaptive harmonic basis (GAHB) whose vectors are oscillating functions adapted to image contours and homogeneous regions. Generalization is demonstrated by training two networks on non-overlapping subsets of CelebA and showing they converge to the same score function (and thus generate nearly identical samples) at N=10⁵, while memorizing at smaller N. The GAHB hypothesis is validated on C^α geometric images, where the denoiser achieves the theoretically optimal PSNR slope α/(α+1) matching bandlet-based denoisers, and on low-dimensional manifolds (disks, rescaled faces, sine waves), where the network retains suboptimal GAHB components in unconstrained dimensions, confirming the inductive bias toward harmonic structure even when it is not optimal.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that DNN denoisers (UNet, 7.6M params) trained on 10^5 non-overlapping subsets of CelebA (80×80) converge to essentially the same score function, with generated samples from the two networks showing near-identical cosine similarity distributions (vanishing model variance). For geometric C^α images, the UNet denoiser achieves PSNR slopes closely matching the theoretical optimal α/(α+1) across regularity levels α=2,3,4, confirming near-optimal denoising. On a 5D disk manifold, the denoiser achieves a suboptimal PSNR slope below 1.0 at small noise levels, revealing GAHB inductive bias. The transition from memorization to generalization occurs around N=10^3–10^4, with train and test PSNR matching at N=10^5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against other generative or denoising methods in the traditional benchmark sense; instead, it evaluates the DNN denoiser against theoretical optima. On C^α images, the DNN denoiser matches the near-optimal bandlet-based denoising rate (PSNR slope α/(α+1)), confirming that its GAHB inductive bias is well-aligned with this image class. On low-dimensional manifolds (5D translating/dilating disks, 1D intensity-rescaled single face, 2D sine-wave cone), the DNN denoiser is suboptimal: it correctly projects onto the tangent space but retains additional GAHB components in unconstrained dimensions, yielding a PSNR slope below the theoretical optimum of 1.0. On shuffled faces (pixels randomly permuted to destroy spatial locality), the denoiser performs substantially worse than on unshuffled faces, with slower shrinkage-factor decay and lower PSNR slope, confirming that the GAHB inductive bias is mismatched when the data lacks geometric structure. The generalization experiments (N=1 to 10^5) show a clear phase transition: at N≤100 the network memorizes training images, while at N=10^5 two independently trained networks produce nearly identical samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based diffusion framework (Song & Ermon 2019; Ho et al. 2020) and the bias-free CNN denoiser architecture (Mohan et al. 2020), using the reverse diffusion sampling algorithm of Kadkhodaie & Simoncelli (2020). Its analytical core draws on classical sparse approximation theory (Donoho 1995; Mallat 2008) and the Miyasawa/Robbins relationship between denoising and score estimation, applying the Jacobian eigendecomposition as a lens for characterizing inductive biases. The paper directly responds to recent memorization reports (Somepalli et al. 2023; Carlini et al. 2023) by showing that the memorization-to-generalization transition is a function of training set size, and connects the learned representations to bandlet bases (Peyré & Mallat 2008; Dossal et al. 2011) known to be near-optimal for geometric C^alpha images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first concrete mechanistic characterization of what inductive biases DNN denoisers actually learn: geometry-adaptive harmonic bases (GAHBs) that perform shrinkage along oscillating patterns adapted to image contours and homogeneous regions. This bridges deep learning and classical signal processing, offering a testable hypothesis about the approximation class of these models. Key open questions remain: there is no formal mathematical definition of the GAHB function class, the role of ReLU nonlinearities in producing geometric adaptivity is only conjectured, and the analysis is limited to 80×80 images with relatively small networks. The finding that ~10^5 images suffice for model variance to vanish is practically useful for practitioners, but the suboptimality on low-dimensional manifolds and shuffled faces highlights that the inductive bias, while powerful, is not universally optimal.
