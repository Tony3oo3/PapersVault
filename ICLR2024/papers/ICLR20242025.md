## Problem

Existing RGB-D segmentation methods encode depth maps with RGB-pretrained backbones, causing a fundamental representation distribution mismatch: the backbone was never exposed to depth data during pretraining, yet must extract 3D geometric structure at finetuning time. This mismatch, combined with the need for dual backbones and heavy external fusion modules, leads to degraded depth feature extraction, representation destruction during finetuning, and unnecessary computational overhead. The paper asks whether a dedicated RGB-D pretraining framework can eliminate this gap by learning transferable RGB-D representations from the start.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a corresponding single-channel depth map of the same spatial size (H×W) as input. For pretraining, depth maps are generated by Adabins on ImageNet-1K; for finetuning, ground-truth depth is used at 480×640 (NYU Depthv2) or 480×480 (SUN-RGBD). The output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation map or binary saliency map at the original resolution, produced by a lightweight Hamburger decoder that consumes only the RGB-branch features from the last three encoder stages—depth features are deliberately excluded from the decoder since the RGB features already encode 3D geometry through pretraining.

## Method

DFormer introduces a hierarchical encoder composed of four stages of RGB-D blocks, each containing three sub-modules: a Global Awareness Attention (GAA) that concatenates depth features into the Query of a downsampled attention mechanism (reducing quadratic cost to a fixed k×k grid), a Local Enhancement Attention (LEA) that applies a large-kernel depth-wise convolution to depth features and uses the result as attention weights to reweight RGB features via Hadamard product, and a Base module that preserves RGB appearance information. The key architectural insight is that depth requires only half the channel dimension of RGB (C^d/C^rgb = 1/2), significantly reducing model size. The encoder is pretrained on ImageNet-1K image-depth pairs with a standard classification objective for 300 epochs, so that RGB-depth interactions are learned before any downstream task. Four variants (T/S/B/L) range from 6.0M to 39.0M parameters, with DFormer-L achieving 57.2% mIoU on NYU Depthv2 at less than half the FLOPs of the previous best method.

## Results

On NYU Depthv2, DFormer-L achieves a new state-of-the-art 57.2% mIoU with only 39.0M parameters and 65.7G FLOPs, less than half the computational cost of the previous best methods CMNext (131.9G) and CMX-B5 (167.8G). The lightweight DFormer-T reaches 51.8% mIoU with just 6.0M parameters and 11.8G FLOPs. On SUN-RGBD, DFormer-L achieves 52.5% mIoU, outperforming CMX-B5 (52.4%) and CMNext (51.9%). On RGB-D salient object detection, DFormer-L achieves the best S-measure (0.980 on DES, 0.971 on NLPR) and F-measure (0.948 on DES, 0.942 on NLPR) across all five benchmarks with the least computational cost (38.8M params, 26.2G FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFormer-L (57.2% mIoU) surpasses the previous best CMNext (56.9%) and CMX-B5 (56.9%) by 0.3% mIoU while using less than half the FLOPs (65.7G vs. 131.9G and 167.8G respectively) and roughly one-third to one-fifth the parameters (39.0M vs. 119.6M and 181.1M). DFormer-B (55.6%) outperforms CMX-B2 (54.4%) by 1.2% mIoU with half the parameters (29.5M vs. 66.6M) and fewer FLOPs (41.9G vs. 67.6G). On SUN-RGBD, DFormer-L (52.5%) edges out CMX-B5 (52.4%) and CMNext (51.9%) with substantially lower computational cost. The lightweight DFormer-T (51.8%, 6.0M params) matches or exceeds heavier methods like ESANet (50.3%, 31.2M) and Omnivore-Swin-S (52.7%, 51.3M) at a fraction of the cost. For RGB-D salient object detection, DFormer-T (5.9M params, 4.5G FLOPs) achieves comparable S-measure to SPNet (150.3M, 68.1G) with less than 10% of the computational cost, while DFormer-L achieves the best F-measure and E-measure across all five SOD benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DFormer departs from the dominant RGB-D segmentation paradigm that employs two separate RGB-pretrained backbones to independently encode RGB and depth features, then fuses them via external interaction modules (e.g., CMX, TokenFusion, CMNext). Instead, it introduces a unified RGB-D pretraining framework where a single backbone is trained from scratch on ImageNet-1K image-depth pairs, with modality interactions built into the encoder's own building blocks (GAA for global attention, LEA for local depth-aware refinement). Conceptually, it draws on the pretrain-then-finetune paradigm of ConvNeXt/SegNeXt and the depth-aware convolution ideas of ShapeConv/SGNet, but repurposes them within a joint-encoder architecture reminiscent of multi-modal pretraining approaches like MultiMAE and Omnivore, applied specifically to the RGB-D setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DFormer achieves SOTA on RGB-D segmentation and salient object detection with less than half the computational cost of prior best methods, and the finding that depth features require only ~1/2 the channels of RGB (and that the decoder needs only RGB features, not depth) offers a principled simplification for future RGB-D architectures. The empirical demonstration that RGB-pretrained backbones are fundamentally mismatched for encoding depth geometry (15% mIoU gap) provides strong evidence for why the field's reliance on dual RGB-pretrained branches is suboptimal. Open questions include whether the pretraining approach generalizes well to other modalities (the paper shows limited gains on RGB-T and RGB-L), whether it extends to 3D tasks like tracking or visual grounding, and whether the efficiency advantage persists at larger scales or on outdoor benchmarks.
