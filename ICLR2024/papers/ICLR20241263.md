## Problem

Prior mechanistic interpretability work on LLMs (e.g., Meng et al., Geva et al.) characterized how models correctly recall facts via attention transferring information from subject tokens, but the internal mechanisms underlying factual errors remained largely unexplored. This paper asks whether the LLM's internal attention patterns to the factual constraint can explain and predict when the model will hallucinate, framing factual queries as constraint satisfaction problems to systematically study this gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the attention weights from constraint tokens to the final generation token (A_{C,T}^{ℓ,h}) across all L layers and H heads, forming a feature vector of dimension L×H; for multi-token constraints, the maximum attention value across constraint tokens is taken. The output is a per-constraint satisfaction probability (sigmoid of a linear function of the attention features), which in multi-constraint settings is combined via product to yield an overall factual correctness estimate. A key practical property is that the probe can be evaluated using attention weights up to any intermediate layer, enabling early stopping of inference before the full forward pass completes.

## Method

The core empirical finding is that attention to constraint tokens positively correlates with factual correctness: when the LLM attends more strongly to the constraint tokens, it is more likely to produce a factually correct response, and weaker attention signals impending hallucination. SAT PROBE exploits this by training a logistic regression with L1 regularization (C=0.05) on the flattened attention weights A_{C,T}^{[L],[H]} to predict whether each constraint is satisfied. The approach is deliberately minimal—it uses only attention weights directed at constraint tokens, not hidden states, MLP contributions, or non-constraint tokens—making it a targeted probe of the model's internal interaction with the factual constraint rather than a general-purpose classifier. For multi-constraint queries, per-constraint predictions are combined via product to estimate overall factual correctness, and the method supports fine-grained partial constraint satisfaction reporting that confidence-based baselines cannot provide. The probe is evaluated across Llama-2 7B/13B/70B on 10 curated datasets (40,000+ prompts) and performs comparably to the model's own confidence while enabling early stopping that saves ~50% wall-clock time on predicted failures.

## Results

Across 10 datasets (40,000+ prompts) and three Llama-2 scales (7B, 13B, 70B), SAT PROBE (a linear probe on attention weights to constraint tokens) achieves AUROC scores comparable to the model's own CONFIDENCE: e.g., 0.81 vs 0.82 on 7B Basketball Players, 0.85 vs 0.86 on 13B Songs. The COMBINED predictor (attention + confidence) consistently achieves the best AUROC, reaching 0.88 on 13B Movies and 0.86 on 70B Books. Notably, for 70B Basketball Players, SAT PROBE substantially outperforms CONFIDENCE (AUROC 0.78 vs 0.58), and early stopping using intermediate-layer attention saves ~50% wall-clock time on failures for 7B and 13B models with no performance degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is CONFIDENCE (the LLM's own output probability, as used by Varshney et al. 2023 for hallucination detection). SAT PROBE matches or slightly trails CONFIDENCE on most single-constraint datasets (e.g., AUROC 0.81 vs 0.82 on 7B Basketball Players; 0.85 vs 0.86 on 13B Songs), but substantially outperforms it on 70B Basketball Players (0.78 vs 0.58) and 70B Football Teams (0.77 vs 0.71). The COMBINED predictor (concatenating attention features with confidence) achieves the best AUROC in most settings, e.g., 0.88 on 13B Movies vs 0.87 for CONFIDENCE and 0.79 for SAT PROBE alone. A key qualitative advantage of SAT PROBE over CONFIDENCE is fine-grained constraint-level prediction: in multi-constraint queries, SAT PROBE can identify which specific constraint is violated, whereas CONFIDENCE outputs a single scalar for the entire generation. The POPULARITY baseline (WikiData site-link count) is competitive on some datasets (e.g., 0.99 AUROC on 7B Movies) but is unavailable for CounterFact and multi-constraint settings, and the CONSTANT baseline (majority class) confirms that attention signals carry nontrivial information (AUROC well above 0.50 in nearly all cases).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability line of work on factual recall (Meng et al., 2022; Geva et al., 2023), which established that MLP layers store facts and attention layers transfer them from subject tokens, by investigating what happens when the model produces factually incorrect responses. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it reframes factual queries as constraint satisfaction problems (CSPs), enabling fine-grained per-constraint analysis rather than binary correct/incorrect labeling, and (2) it isolates attention specifically to constraint tokens rather than total attention across all tokens (as in the concurrent Li et al., 2023). The method is a simple linear probe (logistic regression) over attention weights, positioned against the confidence-based hallucination detector of Varshney et al. (2023) and black-box self-consistency approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic account of why LLMs fail on factual queries—attention to constraint tokens drops—and demonstrates that this signal is exploitable via a lightweight probe for early error detection, fine-grained constraint-level diagnostics, and compute savings through early stopping. Key limitations include restriction to conjunctive queries, assumption that constraint tokens are known a priori, evaluation only on Llama-2, and the opaque content of the attention signal. Open questions include whether attention manipulation can actively fix errors, how to extract constraints automatically, and whether the findings generalize to non-conjunctive or open-ended factual queries.
