## Problem

Zero-shot visual question answering with large vision-language models (LVLMs) is highly sensitive to question phrasing, and underspecified questions—lacking visual grounding, requiring implicit reasoning, or containing ambiguous references—frequently lead to incorrect answers. Existing approaches either require expensive additional pretraining, access to gold answers, or human clarification, none of which are practical for static VQA benchmarks. The paper asks whether questions can be automatically rephrased at inference time to be more visually grounded and less ambiguous, without any training or human feedback.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural-language VQA question as input and produces a single selected rephrased question along with its generated answer. Internally, it generates n=5 question candidates (including the original) by fusing visually-grounded details into the question text, then selects one via an unsupervised confidence score. All prompts used within REPARE contain no annotated examples from any VQA dataset, maintaining a strict zero-shot setting. The rephrased questions are human-readable text edits that preserve the original question's intent while adding modifiers, spatial references, or reasoning cues extracted from the image.

## Method

REPARE is a two-stage, gradient-free, instance-level framework. In Stage I(a), it extracts salient entities from the question via keyword extraction, samples rationales from the LVLM to identify reasoning-relevant objects, and generates an image caption; it then queries the LVLM for fine-grained visual details about each identified entity. In Stage I(b), it uses only the frozen LLM component (not the full LVLM, since image embeddings distract the language model during fusion) to perform sentence fusion, incorporating the extracted details into the question to produce n−1 modified candidates, with an NLI model filtering out contradictory rewrites. In Stage II, it scores each candidate by the LVLM's answer confidence P_LVLM(â_i | I, q_i) and selects the highest-scoring question-answer pair. The key design insight is exploiting the asymmetric strength of LVLMs—whose LLM components have far more capacity than their vision encoders—by offloading visual detail extraction to the full model but linguistic rephrasing to the LLM alone, thereby making questions easier for the LLM to answer while still benefiting from the image.

## Results

REPARE improves zero-shot VQA accuracy by up to 3.85% (BLIP-2 on VQAv2), 6.41% (MiniGPT-4 on A-OKVQA direct), and 7.94% (MiniGPT-4 on VizWiz) over the original question baseline. In the A-OKVQA multiple-choice setting with MiniGPT-4 Vicuna 7B, the improvement reaches 21.54% (41.66% → 63.20%). Oracle selection (using gold answers) establishes upper bounds of up to 9.84%, 14.41%, and 20.09% on VQAv2, A-OKVQA, and VizWiz respectively, with MiniGPT-4 reaching 33.94% on A-OKVQA MC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the original (unmodified) question as the zero-shot baseline, which REPARE consistently outperforms across all three datasets and all three LVLM architectures. The secondary comparison is against a Pegasus-based paraphrasing baseline (Zhang et al., 2020). In the oracle setting, REPARE outperforms paraphrasing on all three benchmarks (e.g., 72.42% vs. 70.99% on VQAv2, 49.24% vs. 46.66% on A-OKVQA direct, 81.14% vs. 79.91% on A-OKVQA MC with BLIP-2). Critically, in the unsupervised selection setting (no gold answers), paraphrasing actually degrades performance in 2 out of 3 settings (e.g., A-OKVQA direct drops from 41.86% to 40.23%), while REPARE still improves over the baseline (41.86% → 44.87%). This demonstrates that REPARE's semantic grounding (incorporating visual details and rationales) makes questions both easier to answer and easier to select via confidence scoring, whereas pure paraphrasing introduces surface-level variation that the confidence-based selector cannot reliably exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

REPARE sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) textual question reframing and clarification (Dong et al., 2017; Stengel-Eskin et al., 2023), which REPARE extends to the multimodal setting by grounding rephrasings in image content; (2) gradient-free prompt search (Prasad et al., 2023; Deng et al., 2022), from which REPARE departs by performing instance-level edits rather than template-level optimization and by requiring no labeled training set; and (3) sentence fusion (Geva et al., 2019), which REPARE repurposes to merge extracted visual details into questions using the frozen LLM component of an LVLM. The confidence-based candidate selection draws on Kadavath et al. (2022) for self-assessment, but applies it to a novel VL question-rewriting pipeline rather than to answer verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REPARE offers a practical, training-free recipe for improving zero-shot VQA across diverse LVLM architectures, which is valuable given the rapid proliferation of such models and the difficulty of fine-tuning them on every task. The decomposition showing that REPARE questions simultaneously improve LLM-only performance (leveraging the asymmetric strength of the LLM) while preserving a large image-dependent gap is a clean conceptual contribution that clarifies *how* rephrasing helps. However, the approach incurs linear inference cost in the number of candidates, 7% of rephrased questions inadvertently contain the gold answer, and the method is evaluated only on VQA—whether it generalizes to other underspecified VL tasks (e.g., grounding, dialogue) remains open. The finding that visual tokens actively harm the linguistic fusion step (up to 3.1-point drop) is a useful practical caveat for anyone building multi-stage LVLM pipelines.
