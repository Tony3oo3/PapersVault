## Problem

Channel-independent (CI) transformer forecasting models are robust to noise but ignore cross-channel correlations, while channel-dependent (CD) models capture those correlations yet overfit to noise in high-noise time series. The paper addresses how to build a transformer that effectively leverages cross-channel dependence without the overfitting penalty that plagues standard CD designs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240442/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series window of L steps across C channels, tokenized via patching (patch length P, stride S) into N tokens per channel plus a learnable T0 token that encodes longer-history context. Output is T future steps produced by a single MLP decoder over the flattened token sequence; for anomaly detection the same architecture is used in reconstruction mode where the input window is predicted as its own output and the reconstruction error serves as the anomaly score.

## Method

CARD alternates two attention passes per encoder layer: (1) attention over tokens within each channel, where queries and keys are smoothed by a fixed-parameter Exponential Moving Average to broaden attention and reduce overfitting, plus a second attention over hidden dimensions within each token to capture local patch-level structure; and (2) attention over channels at each token position, where keys and values are first compressed via a dynamic projection (MLP + softmax) to a low rank r ≪ C, reducing the O(C²) cost to O(Cr) and preventing spurious cross-channel correlations. A token blend module replaces standard multi-head concatenation by merging h₃ adjacent tokens within the same head, implicitly producing lower-resolution tokens that encode multi-scale temporal knowledge without explicit seasonal/trend decomposition. Training uses a signal-decay loss that weights the L1 error at forecast step l by l^{-1/2}, derived from maximum-likelihood estimation under a first-order Markov process with Laplace noise, which down-weights far-future predictions whose variance grows linearly with horizon.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (lookback 96, averaged over horizons {96, 192, 336, 720}), CARD achieves the best average MSE on 6/7 datasets and best average MAE on all 7 datasets. In single-horizon evaluations, CARD attains the best MSE in 82% of cases and best MAE in 100% of cases across all 28 dataset-horizon combinations. On high-dimensional covariate settings, CARD reduces MSE/MAE by over 9% on Electricity (321 channels) and over 7.5% on Traffic (862 channels) relative to the second-best method. On anomaly detection (F1 score), CARD achieves 0.890 average, outperforming the previous best (TimesNet, 0.864) by 3%, with a 14.2% gain on the SMAP benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARD outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary long-term forecasting benchmark. Against the closest channel-independent transformer PatchTST, CARD reduces average MSE by 1.2–22.6% and MAE by 1.5–22.5% across the seven datasets, with the largest gap on Traffic (MSE 0.450 vs 0.488). Against the nearest channel-dependent transformer Crossformer, CARD achieves over 20% MSE/MAE reduction on 6 of 7 datasets (e.g., ETTh2: 0.367 vs 0.966 MSE), demonstrating that its channel-aligned attention with dynamic projection is far more effective than Crossformer's hierarchical cross-dimension design. Against the strong MLP baseline DLinear, CARD still reduces MSE/MAE by 5%–27.5% across all datasets, confirming that the transformer architecture adds value beyond linear trend extrapolation. On high-dimensional settings (Electricity 321ch, Traffic 862ch), CARD's advantage over all baselines widens substantially (>9% over second-best), highlighting the benefit of its dynamic projection for handling many covariates without overfitting. On anomaly detection, CARD leads TimesNet (0.890 vs 0.864 avg F1) and PatchTST (0.849), with a dramatic 14.2% F1 gain on SMAP. On M4 short-term forecasting, CARD beats N-BEATS by 1.8% SMAPE, PatchTST by 1.5%, and TimesNet by 2.2%, confirming effectiveness even in univariate settings where channel attention is less relevant.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARD positions itself at the intersection of the channel-independent (CI) and channel-dependent (CD) debate in multivariate time series forecasting, building on PatchTST's patch-based tokenization and Crossformer's cross-dimension attention while departing from both: it uses an encoder-only structure with a lightweight token blend module instead of Crossformer's explicit hierarchical token merging, and it introduces dynamic projection (from Long-Short Transformer) to make channel attention tractable for high-dimensional covariates. The exponential moving average smoothing on queries/keys draws from ETSformer and MEGA, while the signal decay loss is a novel contribution grounded in a first-order Markov variance argument that has no direct precedent in the forecasting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical tension that has persisted since the CI-vs-CD empirical findings: how to exploit cross-channel dependencies without overfitting noise. The signal decay loss is particularly impactful because it transfers to unrelated architectures (FEDformer, Autoformer, TimesNet) with 3–12% MSE reductions, suggesting it captures a general property of finite-horizon forecasting rather than a CARD-specific artifact. Open questions include whether the fixed EMA parameter and hand-tuned blend size can be made adaptive, whether the dynamic projection rank r scales gracefully beyond ~862 covariates, and whether the Markov-based variance argument holds for non-stationary or regime-switching series where the Lipschitz-1 transition assumption breaks down.
