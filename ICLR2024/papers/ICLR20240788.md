## Problem

Previous feature distillation methods (FD, dBOT) claimed to improve attention diversity in ViT students, but this paper reveals that the diversity gain actually stems from asymmetric architectural designs (extra decoders, additional parameters) that simultaneously degrade the encoder's discrimination ability. The core gap is how to transfer both the token-level diversity of MIM and the semantic discrimination of CL/supervised pre-training to a student model without these architectural compromises.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240788/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images and their masked patch subsets (M⊙x) as input to a randomly initialized ViT student, guided simultaneously by two frozen teacher models: a CL/supervised teacher (DeiT or CLIP) and a MIM teacher (MAE). The output is a pre-trained ViT encoder whose representations are evaluated via fine-tuning on classification (ImageNet-1K, CIFAR-100, Cars, iNaturalist19), object detection and instance segmentation (COCO with Mask-RCNN), and semantic segmentation (ADE20K with UperNet). Notably, the student uses a symmetric architecture with no decoder, and the MIM teacher's contribution is its intermediate-layer token relations rather than its final features.

## Method

Hybrid Distill jointly distills from two teachers with carefully chosen targets and locations: it distills the MIM teacher's token relations R_m^i = Q_m^i K_m^i^T at layers L−1 and L−2 (where CL models suffer attention collapse) to inject diversity, while distilling the CL/supervised teacher's final-layer output features at layer L to preserve discrimination. The key insight is that MIM's diversity originates from low-level reconstruction objectives that shape token-level relational structure, whereas CL's discrimination resides in high-level semantic features—so each teacher's strength is extracted at the layer and representation level where it is strongest. A progressive redundant token masking strategy, inspired by Li et al. (2023), progressively drops tokens most similar to their layer mean at layers {0, L/3, 2L/3} of the MIM teacher, reducing distillation cost and acting as a regularizer against local optima. The overall objective minimizes a weighted sum of smooth-L1 distances between the student's masked-patch features and the CL teacher's final features, plus the student's intermediate token relations and the MIM teacher's corresponding relations.

## Results

With ViT-B backbone and MAE+CLIP teachers, Hybrid Distill achieves 85.1% top-1 on ImageNet-1K, 50.6 AP^box and 44.4 AP^mask on COCO, and 51.5 mIoU on ADE20K. Scaling to ViT-L with ImageNet-21K distillation further boosts results to 88.0% top-1, 54.6 AP^box, 47.6 AP^mask, and 56.3 mIoU. The gains are most pronounced on dense-level tasks (detection and segmentation), where attention diversity is most beneficial, while ImageNet-1K classification shows only modest improvement since diversity is less critical for image-level recognition under matched data distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hybrid Distill (MAE+CLIP, ViT-B) outperforms all single-teacher and self-supervised baselines across every metric. Against the strongest single-teacher distillation baseline Distill-CLIP, it gains +1.1 AP^box, +0.9 AP^mask, and +1.2 mIoU on ADE20K, while also improving ImageNet-1K by +0.3. Compared to the original CLIP and MAE pre-trained models, the gains are larger: +3.0 AP^box over CLIP and +2.2 AP^box over MAE on COCO, confirming that combining both teachers yields complementary benefits. Against supervised DeiT, the improvement is even more substantial (+3.7 AP^box, +2.9 AP^mask, +4.5 mIoU). On the ViT-L scale, Hybrid Distill surpasses both MAE and CLIP by +1.7 AP^box and +1.7 mIoU (vs. MAE) and +1.7 AP^box and +1.7 mIoU (vs. CLIP). The paper also shows that even a weak MAE teacher (pre-trained on only 1.28M ImageNet images) can boost the 400M-image CLIP teacher, and that 300-epoch Hybrid Distill with Cascade Mask-RCNN (53.4 AP^box) approaches 1600-epoch dBOT-CLIP (53.6 AP^box), demonstrating efficiency gains from the better representation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the feature distillation line of work (FD by Wei et al. 2022b, dBOT by Liu et al. 2022) that distills knowledge from a single pre-trained teacher into a student ViT, and on the progressive token masking strategy from Li et al. 2023. However, it departs fundamentally by arguing that prior single-teacher distillation cannot simultaneously transfer both diversity (from MIM) and discrimination (from CL/supervised), and that the diversity gains previously attributed to feature distillation actually stem from asymmetric architectural designs (extra decoder layers, additional learnable parameters) that simultaneously harm discrimination. The proposed Hybrid Distill framework instead uses two teachers jointly—distilling token relations from a MIM teacher at intermediate layers and final-layer features from a CL/supervised teacher—within a symmetric student architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual correction that diversity in prior distillation methods arises from asymmetric designs rather than the distillation objective itself is valuable for anyone designing or interpreting feature distillation pipelines, as it redirects attention toward architectural choices. Practically, the framework shows that combining two off-the-shelf teachers (e.g., MAE + CLIP) yields consistent 1–2 AP gains on dense prediction tasks (COCO, ADE20K) over single-teacher distillation, and that a small-scale MAE (1.28M images) can augment a large-scale CLIP (400M) teacher. Limitations include the need for two teacher models (though overhead is modest at ~1.2× training time) and the observation that gains on ImageNet-1K classification are negligible, suggesting the benefit is primarily for dense-level tasks. An open question is whether stronger MIM pre-training could close the gap with CLIP's discrimination, potentially making the hybrid approach even more effective.
