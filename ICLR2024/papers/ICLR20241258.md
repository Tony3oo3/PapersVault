## Problem

Self-supervised pretraining has scaled to billions of images, but it remains unclear whether such massive datasets are truly necessary or whether the data is being used efficiently. Previous attempts to learn image encoders from video adapted image-based SSL methods to treat frames as augmentations, yet found significant performance gaps relative to ImageNet-pretrained models. This paper asks whether a single long, uncurated, hours-long egocentric video can rival ImageNet for pretraining strong image encoders.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip of T=8 frames (sampled 1 second apart) from 4K (3840×2160) egocentric walking tour videos, with each frame split into 16×16 patches and processed by a ViT-S/16 encoder. It produces a pretrained visual encoder whose frozen or fine-tuned features transfer to downstream tasks, along with per-frame cross-attention tracking maps (k×n matrices) that delineate k spatially distinct objects. The tracking maps are reshaped and upsampled to full spatial resolution to form soft object masks applied to the student network's input.

## Method

DORA extends DINO's teacher-student EMA distillation framework by adding implicit multi-object tracking across video frames. In the first frame, k objects are discovered by selecting k attention heads and forming object prototypes P as weighted combinations of patch query embeddings using the [CLS]-attention vectors. A Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport step then establishes one-to-one correspondences between prototypes and patch embeddings, refining them into spatially distinct prototypes P'. These refined prototypes are tracked over subsequent frames via cross-attention with patch key embeddings, yielding per-object attention maps that serve as soft masks for the student network. The training loss combines a multi-object distillation term (teacher on a global view vs. student on the object-masked view) with a standard multi-crop local loss, averaged over all T frames. No external tracker, optical flow, or additional training data is required.

## Results

DORA pretrained on a single 1.5-hour Walking Tours video (~200k frames) achieves 45.4% top-1 linear probing accuracy on ImageNet-1k, outperforming DINO on the same video by 11.6% and iBOT by 8.6%. On ADE20k semantic segmentation, DORA (WT_Venice) reaches 35.4% mIoU, surpassing DINO pretrained on the full ImageNet-1k (33.9% mIoU) by 1.5%. On MS-COCO object detection, DORA pretrained on all 10 WT videos (1.5M frames) achieves 40.7% mAP, exceeding DINO on ImageNet (39.9% mAP). On unsupervised object discovery (Pascal-VOC 2012), DORA attains 56.2% CorLoc, a 5.0% gain over DINO on the same single video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DORA consistently outperforms all six baselines (SimCLR, SwAV, iBOT, AttMask, VicReg, DINO) when pretrained on the same single WT_Venice video, with the largest gains over DINO (+11.6% LP, +5.0% CorLoc) and SimCLR (+19.1% LP). Against DINO pretrained on the full ImageNet-1k (1.3M images), DORA on a single 200k-frame video still achieves higher mIoU on ADE20k (35.4 vs 33.9) and competitive mAP on MS-COCO (39.5 vs 39.9). When scaled to all 10 WT videos (1.5M frames), DORA surpasses DINO on ImageNet across all dense prediction tasks: +3.0% mIoU on ADE20k, +0.8% mAP on COCO detection, and +1.2% mIoU on COCO instance segmentation. On video tasks, DORA (WT_Venice) nearly matches DINO (IN-1k) on DAVIS-2017 VOS (56.4 vs 57.4 Jm) and achieves 4–6% gains over DINO (WT_Venice) on GOT-10k tracking. The only baseline that exceeds DORA on any metric is VITO (39.4 mIoU on ADE20k), but VITO requires the much larger VideoNet dataset (~3055 hours of curated 10-second clips) rather than a single continuous video.

## Relation to Prior Work

DORA extends DINO's teacher-student distillation framework from static images to continuous video by replacing standard multi-crop augmentation with implicit multi-object tracking via cross-attention. It departs from prior video-SSL approaches (VITO, R2V2, TimeTuning) that treat temporal frames as mere augmentations of an image or require an already image-pretrained backbone; instead, DORA uses the temporal dimension as the primary learning signal, discovering and tracking objects end-to-end without any off-the-shelf tracker. The use of Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport is repurposed from its prior role in clustering (SwAV, DINO) to re-balance spatial object-patch correspondences, and the 'track before recognize' philosophy draws on developmental psychology literature on infant visual learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that a single 1.5-hour egocentric video can rival ImageNet for dense prediction and tracking tasks—challenges the prevailing assumption that SSL quality scales primarily with dataset size and diversity. However, the '1 video = ImageNet' framing is most accurate for segmentation, detection, and tracking; on linear-probing classification the gap remains large (~45% vs ~71%), so the equivalence is task-dependent. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond continuous, cut-free egocentric video (cuts significantly degrade performance), whether it scales to larger ViT architectures and longer/more diverse video collections, and whether the tracking signal can be extracted from non-egocentric or object-centric footage. The open-source Walking Tours dataset and the simplicity of the method (no external tracker, no optical flow) make it a practical and reproducible contribution.
