## Problem

Existing one-shot 3D talking face methods fail to simultaneously achieve accurate 3D avatar reconstruction and stable motion-conditioned animation: GAN-inversion-based approaches suffer from temporal jitter and distortion, while predictor-based methods lack robustness due to insufficient multi-view training data. Additionally, prior works model only the head or treat head and torso as a rigid whole, overlooking the need for natural torso movement and switchable backgrounds to produce a realistic full-portrait video.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB source image of an unseen identity plus either a driving video (providing expression and pose) or a driving audio clip (converted to motion via the A2M model) as input. It outputs a 512×512 talking portrait video where the head is rendered via NeRF volume rendering, the torso is warped with 2D dense flows conditioned on predefined 3DMM keypoints, and the background is independently synthesized with KNN inpainting, all fused via an alpha-blending mechanism. The system is fully one-shot: no per-identity training is required at inference time.

## Method

Real3D-Portrait uses a four-stage sequential training pipeline. First, a large (87M) hybrid ViT+VGG Image-to-Plane model is pre-trained on multi-view images synthesized by EG3D to distill 3D prior knowledge, then fine-tuned on CelebV-HQ video data. Second, a lightweight (5.7M) SegFormer-based Motion Adapter takes concatenated source/driving PNCC feature maps and predicts a residual motion diff-plane that is element-wise added to the canonical tri-plane, avoiding the geometry degradation of deformation-field approaches; a Laplacian loss over adjacent frames enforces temporal smoothness. Third, a Head-Torso-Background Super-Resolution model individually renders the head (NeRF), torso (warping-based with 68 predefined 3DMM keypoints), and background (KNN-inpainted + conv features), then fuses them via occlusion-aware alpha blending to produce the final 512×512 frame. Fourth, a generic flow-enhanced VAE (A2M model) with HuBERT audio features predicts 3DMM expression codes, enabling audio-driven generation with explicit eye-blink and mouth-amplitude control.

## Results

On video-driven same-identity reenactment (CelebV-HQ), Real3D-Portrait achieves the best scores across all metrics: L1=0.067, PSNR=18.95, FID=37.50, CSIM=0.821, AED=0.111, APD=0.018, and AKD=2.829, outperforming the closest one-shot 3D baseline HiDe-NeRF (FID=50.04, CSIM=0.753). On audio-driven generation, Real3D-Portrait achieves CSIM=0.763, FID=43.02, AED=0.146, and Sync=6.565, surpassing one-shot 2D methods (MakeItTalk, PC-AVS) on all metrics and beating the person-specific RAD-NeRF on lip synchronization (Sync 6.565 vs 3.779) and expression accuracy (AED 0.146 vs 0.197), while RAD-NeRF retains a slight edge on identity preservation (CSIM 0.784 vs 0.763) and image quality (FID 39.45 vs 43.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three video-driven one-shot baselines, Real3D-Portrait wins decisively on every metric: it improves FID from 50.04 (HiDe-NeRF) and 65.37 (OTAvatar) to 37.50, and raises CSIM from 0.753/0.568 to 0.821, indicating substantially better identity preservation and image quality. OTAvatar, which relies on iterative 3D GAN inversion, shows the weakest performance overall (PSNR 13.17, FID 65.37), confirming the instability of the 2D-then-3D-lifting pipeline. In the audio-driven setting, Real3D-Portrait dominates the one-shot 2D methods MakeItTalk and PC-AVS on all four metrics (e.g., FID 43.02 vs 52.65/82.02; Sync 6.565 vs 3.286/6.483). Against the person-specific RAD-NeRF, Real3D-Portrait achieves better lip synchronization (Sync 6.565 vs 3.779) and expression accuracy (AED 0.146 vs 0.197) without any per-identity training, though RAD-NeRF retains a small advantage in CSIM (0.784 vs 0.763) and FID (39.45 vs 43.02). In the supplementary 2D video-driven comparison (Table 7), Real3D-Portrait outperforms TPS, DPE, and MCNet on all four metrics (CSIM, FID, AED, APD), with the largest gap over MCNet in APD (0.017 vs 0.030).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the predictor-based one-shot 3D talking face paradigm (Trevithick et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023a) by replacing the deformation-field animation of HiDe-NeRF with a lightweight PNCC-conditioned motion adapter that predicts residual diff-planes, and by pre-training the image-to-plane backbone on multi-view synthetic data from EG3D to overcome the lack of large-pose multi-view frames in video datasets. The torso branch repurposes the warping-based architecture of Face-vid2vid (Wang et al., 2021) with predefined 3DMM keypoints instead of learned unsupervised ones, and the audio-to-motion model adapts the flow-enhanced VAE design from GeneFace (Ye et al., 2023b). Unlike OTAvatar's 2D-then-3D-lifting pipeline, Real3D-Portrait performs end-to-end 3D reconstruction and animation in a single forward pass, while also being the first one-shot 3D system to individually model head, torso, and background segments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real3D-Portrait is the first one-shot 3D talking face system to simultaneously support both audio- and video-driven scenarios while generating full-portrait videos with natural torso movement and switchable backgrounds, making it directly applicable to video conferencing and virtual avatar use cases. The ablation showing that multi-view pre-training is critical (CSIM drops from 0.758 to 0.487 without it) provides a clear recipe for improving generalizable 3D face reconstruction. Key limitations remain: the method fails at large head poses (side views) due to training data constraints, the KNN-based background inpainting produces visible artifacts under large motion, and the four-stage sequential training pipeline (totaling ~178 GPU-hours on 8×A100) is computationally expensive. Future work on few-shot in-context learning and neural-network-based background inpainting (e.g., LAMA) are natural next steps.
