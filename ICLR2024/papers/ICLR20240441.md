## Problem

GNN-based enhancements to CDCL SAT solvers have either failed to improve solving effectiveness or, as in NeuroCore, require frequent online model inferences that demand substantial GPU resources—making parallel deployment impractical. NeuroBack addresses this by showing that a single offline inference on variable phases appearing in the majority of satisfying assignments is sufficient to improve CDCL solving, enabling CPU-only execution with no GPU dependency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240441/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a CNF formula converted into a compact undirected bipartite graph with variable nodes, clause nodes, and a meta node per connected component (reducing diameter to 4). The output is a binary phase prediction (true/false) for each variable, where only variables whose predicted probability exceeds a threshold α=0.9 (or falls below 1−α) are designated as 'important variables' and their phases are used to initialize Kissat's phase selection. The entire inference runs once on CPU (0.3–16.5 s per problem, averaging 1.7 s) before the SAT solving loop begins.

## Method

NeuroBack converts the CNF formula into a bipartite graph with meta nodes, then applies a GNN model consisting of four GINConv-based GNN blocks, three Graph Self-Attention (GSA) transformer blocks, and three Local Self-Attention (LSA) transformer blocks, followed by an FFN classifier. The key architectural innovation replaces global self-attention with GSA (attention only over directly connected node pairs, linear in edges) and LSA (attention over node patches, linear in nodes), reducing memory complexity from quadratic to linear. The model is trained via supervised learning on backbone variable phases using a two-stage protocol: pre-training on DataBack-PT (118,460 augmented formulas from CNFgen, SATLIB, model counting competitions, and SATCOMP random tracks) followed by fine-tuning on DataBack-FT (1,826 formulas from SATCOMP main tracks). The central insight is transfer learning: a model trained solely on backbone phases generalizes to predicting phases of non-backbone variables appearing in the majority of satisfying assignments, and these offline predictions serve as phase initialization in Kissat, improving solving by up to 5.2% and 7.4% on SATCOMP-2022 and SATCOMP-2023 respectively.

## Results

On SATCOMP-2022 (308 inferred problems), NeuroBack-Kissat solves 203 problems, a 5.2% improvement over Default-Kissat (193) and 3.0% over Random-Kissat (197). On SATCOMP-2023 (353 inferred problems), NeuroBack-Kissat solves 204 problems, a 3.0% improvement over Default-Kissat (198) and 7.4% over Random-Kissat (190). The fine-tuned GNN model achieves 92.8% F1 and 88.7% accuracy in classifying backbone variable phases on the validation set, up from 82.9% F1 and 75.1% accuracy for the pre-trained model. CPU inference time averages 1.7 seconds per problem (range 0.3–16.5 s), confirming the approach is practical without GPU resources.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroBack-Kissat consistently outperforms both baselines on both competition sets. Against Default-Kissat (which initializes all variable phases to true), NeuroBack-Kissat solves 43 additional problems in SATCOMP-2022 (reducing solving time by 117 s per problem) and 40 additional problems in SATCOMP-2023 (reducing solving time by 36 s per problem). Against Random-Kissat (which assigns random initial phases), NeuroBack-Kissat solves 22 more problems in SATCOMP-2022 (−98 s per problem) and 29 more problems in SATCOMP-2023 (−246 s per problem). Notably, Random-Kissat actually outperforms Default-Kissat on SATCOMP-2022 (197 vs. 193) but underperforms on SATCOMP-2023 (190 vs. 198), whereas NeuroBack-Kissat is the top performer in both settings. The improvement is more pronounced on sat problems (53.2% average speedup) than on unsat problems (14.6% average speedup), consistent with the fact that backbone phase predictions directly contribute to finding satisfying assignments.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuroBack builds on the GraphTrans architecture (Wu et al., 2021) but replaces its global self-attention with Graph Self-Attention (GSA) and Local Self-Attention (LSA) blocks to achieve linear memory complexity, making it feasible for SAT formulas with millions of variables. It departs from NeuroCore (Selsam & Bjørner, 2019) in two fundamental ways: targeting phase selection heuristics rather than branching heuristics, and using a single offline inference on static backbone information rather than repeated online inferences on dynamic unsat-core signals. The backbone-variable concept draws on recent work by Biere et al. (2021) and Al-Yahya et al. (2022), but NeuroBack is the first to predict backbone phases via a GNN in a fully offline, pre-solving manner rather than through heuristic search during solving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that NeuroBack removes the GPU dependency that has been the primary barrier to deploying GNN-enhanced SAT solvers at scale, enabling CPU-only inference that averages just 1.7 seconds per problem. The 5.2% and 7.4% improvements on SATCOMP-2022 and SATCOMP-2023 demonstrate that a single offline prediction can meaningfully guide a state-of-the-art solver, challenging the assumption that dynamic online inference is necessary. Open questions include whether predictions can be leveraged dynamically during rephasing for further gains, how the approach scales to formulas exceeding the current 135 MB memory threshold, and whether the pre-training data requirement (120K samples) can be reduced. The publicly released DataBack dataset and NeuroBack-Kissat implementation lower the barrier for future work in ML-augmented SAT solving.
