## Problem

Time series contrastive learning assumes that augmented views from the same instance share meaningful semantics, but this assumption is frequently violated in practice. The paper identifies two distinct failure modes unique to time series: noisy positive pairs (where the signal is dominated by noise, causing the model to learn noise patterns via 'noisy alignment') and faulty positive pairs (where augmentation destroys critical temporal patterns, causing the model to align irrelevant features via 'faulty alignment'). This is the first systematic study of the bad positive pair problem in the time series contrastive learning setting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on multivariate time series of varying channel counts and lengths (e.g., 12-channel ECG of length 1024 for PTB-XL, 9-channel accelerometer of length 128 for HAR, single-channel EEG of length 3000 for Sleep-EDF). During training, the encoder G(θ) maps each augmented view to a representation vector r ∈ R^H, and DBPM modifies the per-pair InfoNCE loss by multiplying it with a suppression weight w_(i,e) ∈ (0,1) for identified bad pairs. At evaluation, the frozen encoder produces representations that are fed to a single linear classifier for multi-label or multi-class classification, with AUROC, Accuracy, or AUPRC as the final metric.

## Method

DBPM is a parameter-free plug-in consisting of a memory module and a transformation module. The memory module maintains a running mean m_(i,e) of each positive pair's contrastive loss across all prior epochs, then at each epoch computes global statistics (μ_e, σ_e) over all N pairs. Pairs with m_(i,e) below μ_e − β_np·σ_e are flagged as noisy (consistently low loss indicating collapse to a trivial solution), and pairs above μ_e + β_fp·σ_e are flagged as faulty (consistently high loss indicating difficulty aligning destroyed patterns). The transformation module assigns each flagged pair a suppression weight via a Gaussian PDF evaluated at the pair's current loss relative to the global distribution, smoothly down-weighting its contribution to the gradient. This dynamic, history-based identification distinguishes DBPM from RINCE, which penalizes all high-loss pairs at each iteration and thus risks over-penalizing legitimately difficult but informative pairs.

## Results

On PTB-XL (the largest ECG dataset), DBPM consistently improves AUROC across all six contrastive frameworks and three classification tasks. The most notable gains are BTSF Diagnostic (69.48→73.32 AUROC, +3.84) and TF-C Rhythm (80.46→82.10 AUROC, +1.64). On the other three datasets, TSTCC+DBPM achieves the best Sleep-EDF accuracy (84.17%) and AUPRC (79.68%), while SimCLR+DBPM reaches 90.42% accuracy on HAR. DBPM adds only 0.26–1.52 s/epoch of extra runtime and ≤2.60 MB extra memory, confirming its lightweight plug-in nature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DBPM is integrated into six baselines (SimCLR, MoCo, SimSiam, TSTCC, TF-C, BTSF) and compared against RINCE as a prior faulty-view mitigation method. DBPM outperforms RINCE in the vast majority of settings: on PTB-XL, RINCE actually degrades MoCo Diagnostic (76.41→72.85) and TF-C Rhythm (80.46→78.38), while DBPM improves both (77.32 and 82.10 respectively). The largest absolute gains from DBPM occur on BTSF Diagnostic (+3.84 AUROC) and TF-C Rhythm (+1.64 AUROC), where the base methods are most susceptible to bad positive pairs. On the already-strong TSTCC, improvements are modest (≤0.12 AUROC) but still consistent, suggesting TSTCC's weak-strong augmentation strategy partially mitigates the bad pair problem. DBPM also reduces variance across runs in most cases (e.g., SimCLR Rhythm std drops from 5.22 to 3.85; MoCo Rhythm from 2.57 to 0.47), indicating more stable training. RINCE is not applicable to SimSiam (no negative pairs), so DBPM is the only tested enhancement there, yielding consistent small gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the faulty-view problem identified in image contrastive learning (RINCE, Weighted xID) to the time series domain, where it manifests in two distinct forms: noisy positive pairs (unique to time series due to sensor noise) and faulty positive pairs (from augmentation destroying temporal patterns). Conceptually, it draws on the label-error literature (Swayamdipta et al., Shen & Sanghavi) that showed mislabeled data exhibits characteristic training dynamics, repurposing this insight to track per-pair contrastive losses over epochs via a memory module. Unlike RINCE, which applies a per-iteration penalty on all high-loss pairs, DBPM uses historical training behavior for dynamic, more reliable identification and applies a Gaussian-PDF-based smooth down-weighting rather than a hard penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of the bad positive pair problem in time series contrastive learning, providing both theoretical analysis (noisy alignment, faulty alignment) and a practical lightweight plug-in that consistently improves six SOTA methods across four real-world datasets. The counterintuitive finding that noisy pairs produce small (not large) contrastive losses challenges the assumption that low loss always indicates good learning in contrastive settings. Limitations include the focus on augmentation-based methods only (not non-augmentation approaches like TS2Vec), heuristic threshold selection (β in [1,3]), and the authors' own caution that the method should not drive critical clinical decisions. Open questions include automatic threshold adaptation and extension to forecasting and anomaly detection tasks.
