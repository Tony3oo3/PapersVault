## Problem

Quality Diversity Reinforcement Learning (QD-RL) aims to combine principled exploration of diverse behaviors with RL's performance optimization, but prior methods were restricted to off-policy algorithms (TD3) and evolution strategies, leaving a significant performance gap compared to standard RL on continuous control tasks. On-policy RL, particularly PPO, had never been adapted to the Differentiable Quality Diversity (DQD) framework, and naively combining existing QD methods with RL fails to scale to high-dimensional, highly stochastic environments like Humanoid.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment state vectors (observations of dimension 17–227 depending on the MuJoCo/Brax locomotion task) and produces a 2D archive (e.g., 10×10 or 50×50 cells) where each cell stores a policy and its objective score, indexed by two behavioral measures: the proportion of time each foot contacts the ground. Although the original measure functions are trajectory-level (non-Markovian), PPGA converts them into per-timestep Markovian Measure Proxies (MMPs) so that standard actor-critic RL can estimate their gradients. Policies are evaluated over 10 parallel environments to average out stochasticity before archive insertion.

## Method

PPGA builds on CMA-MAEGA by replacing its gradient estimation and search-distribution mechanisms with PPO-based components. A Vectorized PPO (VPPO) implementation replicates the search policy actor k+1 times (one per objective/measure), jointly optimizes all reward functions in a single batched training loop, and extracts the objective-measure Jacobian as the parameter difference after N₁ PPO steps. The non-Markovian foot-contact measures are made RL-optimizable via Markovian Measure Proxies that reduce the trajectory-level measure to a per-state indicator. CMA-ES is replaced with xNES to maintain the gradient-coefficient distribution, avoiding the step-size divergence caused by CMA-ES's cumulative step-size adaptation under noisy, non-stationary QD objectives. Finally, instead of a single weighted-gradient step, PPGA 'walks' the search policy over N₂ PPO iterations using a multi-objective reward constructed from the dot product of the xNES mean coefficient vector with the objective and MMPs, enabling multiple conservative steps toward the direction of greatest archive improvement.

## Results

PPGA achieves state-of-the-art results across all four locomotion tasks, with a headline 4× improvement in best reward on Humanoid (8,324 vs. 446–498 for baselines) and the only algorithm to solve that domain. On QD-Score, PPGA leads on Humanoid (1.02×10⁵ vs. 1.27×10⁴ for sep-CMA-MAE), Walker2d (1.06×10⁵ vs. 1.84×10⁵ for sep-CMA-MAE), Half-Cheetah (7.26×10⁵ vs. 6.03×10⁵), and Ant (1.53×10⁷ vs. 1.79×10⁷ for PGA-ME). PPGA also achieves the highest coverage on Humanoid (0.52) and Half-Cheetah (0.58), while trailing sep-CMA-MAE on Walker2d coverage (0.39 vs. 0.64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPGA decisively outperforms all four baselines on Best Reward across every task, with the most dramatic margin on Humanoid where it achieves 8,324 versus 446 (PGA-ME), 498 (sep-CMA-MAE), 412 (QDPG), and 352 (CMA-MAEGA TD3, ES) — a 4× improvement over the strongest baseline. On QD-Score, PPGA leads on Humanoid (1.02×10⁵ vs. 1.27×10⁴), Half-Cheetah (7.26×10⁵ vs. 6.03×10⁵), and Ant (1.53×10⁷ vs. 1.79×10⁷ for PGA-ME), but trails sep-CMA-MAE on Walker2d (1.06×10⁵ vs. 1.84×10⁵). On Coverage, PPGA wins on Humanoid (0.52 vs. 0.42) and Half-Cheetah (0.58 vs. 0.61 for sep-CMA-MAE, though close), but loses to sep-CMA-MAE on Walker2d (0.39 vs. 0.64) and to PGA-ME on Ant (0.34 vs. 0.37). The CCDF analysis further confirms PPGA's advantage: its archive policies are more concentrated in the high-reward tail on all tasks except Ant, indicating superior quality-diversity trade-offs. The ablation TD3GA (replacing PPO with TD3 in PPGA) fails to find high-performing policies on Humanoid, confirming the critical synergy between on-policy PPO and the DQD framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPGA builds directly on the Differentiable Quality Diversity (DQD) framework, specifically CMA-MAEGA (Fontaine & Nikolaidis, 2023), which maintains a single search policy that branches offspring via gradient information to fill a behavior-space archive. The key departure is replacing the analytical gradient computation with on-policy RL (PPO) to approximate objective and measure gradients, introducing Markovian Measure Proxies to make trajectory-level measures RL-optimizable, and substituting CMA-ES with xNES for training stability. This contrasts with prior QD-RL methods (PGA-ME, QDPG, CMA-MAEGA(TD3,ES)) that relied on off-policy RL (TD3) or evolution strategies, which the authors show struggle to scale to high-dimensional stochastic locomotion tasks like Humanoid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PPGA is the first QD-RL algorithm to solve the challenging Humanoid locomotion task, achieving 4× best-reward improvement over baselines while matching standard PPO's peak performance without sacrificing behavioral diversity. The demonstrated synergy between on-policy RL and DQD—where TD3-based variants fail on Humanoid—suggests that the non-stationary, noisy QD objective is better handled by PPO's clipped trust-region updates than by off-policy value estimation. Limitations include sample inefficiency inherent to on-policy methods, sensitivity of PPO's adaptive action standard deviation to QD exploration, and untested scaling to distributed or real-robot settings, leaving open whether the approach generalizes beyond the four MuJoCo/Brax locomotion benchmarks.
