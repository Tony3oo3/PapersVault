## Problem

Existing Bayesian optimization methods rely on GP surrogate models that predict scalar function values, making them inapplicable to doubly-stochastic Gaussian Cox processes where observations are point events modulated by a latent GP intensity function. Prior GCP inference work either restricts to specific link functions or estimates only the posterior mean, whereas BO acquisition functions require both posterior mean and covariance to quantify uncertainty. This paper addresses the gap by providing a general MAP inference for both the posterior mean and covariance of the latent intensity under arbitrary smooth link functions, enabling a full BO framework over point-process data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of observed point events {t_i} in a compact spatio-temporal domain S, a symmetric positive definite kernel k(t,t'), a non-negative smooth link function κ(·), and a uniform discretization grid of size m over S. It outputs the posterior mean ĝ and covariance A^{-1} of the latent GP intensity at each BO step, which are then used to evaluate acquisition functions (UCB, idle-time probability, cumulative-arrival probability, or change-point probability) over candidate observation regions. The BO loop selects the next region τ to observe based on the acquisition function, expanding the observation set iteratively until a step budget is exhausted.

## Method

The core technique defines h(t) = κ^{1/2}(g(t)) so that h²(t) = κ(g(t)), then merges the L₂ integral term ∫_S h²(t)dt into the RKHS norm via Mercer's theorem, yielding a new kernel k̃(t,t') = Σ η_i(η_i+γ)^{-1} φ_i(t)φ_i(t') in a transformed RKHS. In this new space, the representer theorem applies directly, giving the posterior mean as ĝ = κ^{-1}(ĥ²) where ĥ(·) = Σ α_i k̃(t_i,·), and the posterior covariance is obtained from the Hessian of the log-likelihood evaluated at the MAP estimate. A Nyström approximation (using eigendecomposition of the grid Gram matrix K_xx) provides efficient computation of k̃ when no closed-form Mercer expansion exists, and the BO framework incrementally updates approximations across steps. Four acquisition functions are designed on top of the estimated posterior: UCB for peak intensity, a Poisson-arrival probability for idle-time detection, a cumulative-arrival threshold for finding high-density intervals, and a Bayesian online change-point probability for detecting sudden intensity shifts.

## Results

On three synthetic intensity functions (λ₁, λ₂, λ₃), the proposed method achieves the best l2-norm in 2 of 3 settings (28.58 on λ₂, 2.94 on λ₃) and the best IQL.50 in all 3 settings (11.44, 12.56, 30.02). The method outperforms all four baselines in 7 out of 9 metric-function combinations. On real-world data, the BO framework identifies the general spatio-temporal intensity pattern of 343 DC crime incidents within 10 steps (total runtime 91.56 s for 25 steps), and recovers structural patterns in 2D neuronal and Porto taxi data more faithfully than baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is compared against four Gaussian Cox process inference baselines: RKHS (Flaxman et al., 2017), MFVB (Donner & Opper, 2018), STVB (Aglietti et al., 2019), and PIF (Kim, 2021). The method wins 7 of 9 metric-function combinations, with the largest margins over RKHS (e.g., l2 of 28.58 vs. 44.78 on λ₂, a 1.43 improvement over STVB). The only losses are marginal: STVB edges out the proposed method on l2 for λ₁ (2.96 vs. 2.98), and MFVB achieves a lower IQL.85 on λ₃ (17.89 vs. 24.32), likely due to MFVB's specialized variational treatment of the quadratic link. PIF is competitive on IQL.85 for λ₂ (8.65 vs. 8.59) but lacks l2-norm reporting. On real-world 2D spatial data (neuronal, Porto taxi), the proposed method qualitatively recovers sharper structural patterns and smoother intensity surfaces than all baselines, and the BO framework identifies key spatial hotspots in DC crime data within 10 of 25 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RKHS-based Poisson intensity estimation framework of Flaxman et al. (2017) by introducing a change-of-kernel technique that merges the L2 integral term into the RKHS norm via Mercer's theorem, enabling the representer theorem to apply to the full Gaussian Cox process MAP objective. It departs from prior GCP inference methods (Adams et al. 2009, Donner & Opper 2018, Aglietti et al. 2019, Kim 2021) that either restricted to specific link functions or estimated only the posterior mean, by deriving both posterior mean and covariance under general smooth link functions. The change-point acquisition function directly extends the Bayesian online changepoint detection framework of Adams & MacKay (2007) into the GCP setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens the door to sample-efficient optimization over spatio-temporal point process data (crime, disasters, neuronal firing, taxi demand) where the observation process is doubly stochastic, a setting previously inaccessible to standard GP-based BO. The Laplace approximation relies on a large-event assumption (BIC simplification), and the Nyström discretization introduces approximation error that may accumulate in high-dimensional or very sparse regimes. Open questions include whether the framework scales to time-variant latent intensities, high-dimensional input spaces, and whether the kernel transformation can be made exact without discretization.
