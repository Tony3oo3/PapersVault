## Problem

Existing adversarial defenses, particularly white-box adversarial training, sacrifice 11–18% clean accuracy to achieve robustness against an unrealistic threat model where the attacker has full model access. The paper argues that the most practical and prevalent real-world threat is transfer attacks from publicly available surrogate models (TAPM), where a low-cost black-box adversary generates adversarial examples on public models and hopes they transfer to the target, yet no defense has been designed specifically for this setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs a class label via a standard CNN classifier (WideResNet-34-10 or ResNet-50). During training, each input image is paired with pre-generated ℓ∞-norm-bounded adversarial perturbations produced by PGD on 3–4 selected public source models; at each training iteration, one (source model, attack algorithm) pair is randomly sampled. The defended model is evaluated under worst-case TAPM attack across 24 public source models and 11 attack algorithms (264 total attack configurations).

## Method

PUBDEF formulates defense as a two-player game where the attacker chooses a discrete strategy (source model S, attack algorithm A) from a finite set, and the defender chooses continuous model weights. The key training procedure is: (1) select a diverse subset of 3–4 public source models, one from each of four training-methodology groups (normal, ℓ∞-adversarial, ℓ₂-adversarial, corruption-robust), using a simple heuristic that swaps in models if their transfer attacks are significantly stronger; (2) pre-generate multiple adversarial examples per training image using PGD on those source models; (3) train a single model with a loss that combines clean classification loss and adversarial loss on the pre-generated transfer attacks, randomly sampling one (S,A) pair per iteration. Unlike standard adversarial training, the adversarial examples are independent of the model being trained, making the training loop ~2× faster. The defense generalizes to unseen source models and attack algorithms, losing only 1.7–7.6% adversarial accuracy on the 260 unseen attacks out of 264, suggesting transfer adversarial examples occupy a low-dimensional subspace.

## Results

Under the TAPM threat model (24 public source models × 11 attack algorithms), PUBDEF achieves 88.6% adversarial accuracy on CIFAR-10, 50.8% on CIFAR-100, and 63.0% on ImageNet, while maintaining clean accuracy of 96.1%, 76.2%, and 78.6% respectively. On ImageNet, this represents a 26.8-point improvement in adversarial accuracy over the best white-box adversarially trained model (36.2%) with only a 2-point drop in clean accuracy relative to an undefended model (80.4%). The defense generalizes to unseen source models and attack algorithms, losing only 1.7–7.6% in adversarial accuracy when evaluated on the 260 attacks not used during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUBDEF outperforms all five baselines on every dataset where they are compared. Against the best white-box adversarial training (Addepalli et al., 2022b for CIFAR-10; Addepalli et al., 2022a for CIFAR-100; Salman et al., 2020 for ImageNet), PUBDEF gains 19.8, 18.0, and 26.8 points in adversarial accuracy respectively, while also recovering 10.8, 7.4, and 15.6 points in clean accuracy. Against the transfer-attack-specific defenses DVERGE (Yang et al., 2020) and TRS (Yang et al., 2021), both combined with adversarial training, PUBDEF leads by 29.0 and 21.9 points in adversarial accuracy on CIFAR-10, and by 11.7 and 11.7 points on CIFAR-100. Notably, DVERGE is only evaluated on CIFAR-10 (it is difficult to train on other datasets), and neither DVERGE nor TRS is evaluated on ImageNet due to the computational cost of ensembling plus adversarial training. PUBDEF's clean accuracy (96.1% on CIFAR-10) is nearly identical to an undefended model (96.3%), whereas white-box adversarial training sacrifices 11 percentage points of clean accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant white-box adversarial training paradigm (Madry et al., 2018) by reframing the threat model around transfer attacks from publicly available surrogate models (TAPM), arguing that the white-box assumption is unrealistic for practical deployment. Conceptually, it builds on the adversarial training loop but replaces on-the-fly attack generation with pre-generated transfer attacks from a diverse set of public models, and introduces a game-theoretic formulation (simple vs. complex game) to motivate the training objective. It also contrasts with ensemble-based transfer-attack defenses (DVERGE, TRS) by showing that a single model trained against a carefully selected subset of public source models outperforms multi-model ensembles, and it draws on the transfer attack literature (Papernot et al., 2016; Dong et al., 2018; Zhao et al., 2022) for the attack algorithms used in evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PUBDEF's practical significance lies in its near-zero clean accuracy cost (only 2% drop on ImageNet vs. 11–18% for white-box AT) while achieving 26 points higher adversarial accuracy, making it arguably the first adversarial defense that is deployable without unacceptable performance degradation. The surprising generalization from training on 4 of 264 transfer attacks to robustness against all 264 suggests a low-dimensional adversarial subspace structure, which if confirmed could reshape how we think about the geometry of transferable adversarial examples. Key limitations include vulnerability to white-box attacks (requiring model secrecy), restriction to ℓ∞-bounded perturbations, and the assumption that attackers cannot train their own surrogates. Open questions include whether the adversarial subspace structure persists with more diverse or larger model pools, whether more sophisticated ensemble-based attacks can break the defense, and how to extend the approach to broader threat models (e.g., ℓ2, unbounded, or semantic attacks).
