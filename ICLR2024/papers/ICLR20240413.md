## Problem

DPO and most existing preference optimization methods are restricted to reverse KL divergence regularization, which is mode-seeking and empirically reduces generation diversity and narrows the range of expressed viewpoints. The paper asks whether the DPO framework can be generalized to a broader class of f-divergences (forward KL, Jensen-Shannon, α-divergences) so that practitioners can explicitly trade off alignment accuracy against output diversity, all within a single supervised-learning stage without RL or a separate reward model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a preference dataset of (prompt, chosen, rejected) triples, a frozen reference model (typically the SFT checkpoint), and a user-selected f-divergence function f. It outputs a fine-tuned language model trained purely by SGD on the preference pairs—no model rollouts, no reward model, and no estimation of the partition function Z(x) are needed at any point. The divergence function f is the sole hyperparameter governing the alignment-versus-diversity trade-off, with β controlling the regularization strength.

## Method

The key technical insight is that for f-divergences satisfying 0 ∉ dom(f′) (which includes reverse KL, forward KL, JS divergence, and α-divergences with α ∈ (0,1)), the KKT complementary slackness condition forces all inequality Lagrange multipliers α(y) to zero, thereby eliminating the normalizing constant Z(x) from the reward-to-policy reparameterization. This yields the closed-form mapping r(y|x) = βf′(π*(y|x)/π_ref(y|x)) + const, where the additive constant cancels inside the Bradley-Terry sigmoid, producing the loss L = −log σ(βf′(π_θ(y_w|x)/π_ref(y_w|x)) − βf′(π_θ(y_l|x)/π_ref(y_l|x))). Training is a straightforward supervised negative-log-likelihood optimization over preference pairs, generalizing the original DPO objective (which corresponds to f′(u) = log u + 1). The paper further proves that the ECE difference between any two policies is bounded by a function of their f-divergence (Theorem 2), and empirically demonstrates that f-DPO achieves a better divergence-versus-reward frontier than both PPO (reward) and PPO (loss) variants while producing more diverse generations under forward KL and JS divergences.

## Results

On the Anthropic HH dataset (Pythia 2.8B), reverse KL achieves the highest alignment accuracy (67.19%) but the lowest generation diversity (Self-Bleu 0.880, Distinct-2 0.151), while forward KL achieves the lowest alignment accuracy (54.30%) but the highest diversity (Self-Bleu 0.834, Distinct-2 0.210). JSD provides a near-optimal balance, retaining 66.80% alignment accuracy while improving Distinct-2 to 0.159. On the IMDB-sentiment dataset (GPT-2 large), f-DPO outperforms both PPO (reward) and PPO (loss) along the divergence-vs-reward frontier, demonstrating superior divergence efficiency across all tested divergences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares f-DPO against two PPO variants: PPO (reward), which embeds the divergence penalty in the reward function, and PPO (loss), which treats it as a separate regularization term optimized by SGD. On the IMDB-sentiment divergence-vs-reward frontier (Figure 2), f-DPO dominates both PPO variants across all divergences (RKL, JSD, FKL, α-divergences), achieving higher reward at every level of divergence. PPO (loss) consistently outperforms PPO (reward) for JSD, forward KL, and α-divergences, because the larger value ranges of these divergences destabilize PPO's value function learning when the penalty is in the reward. On MT-Bench with GPT-4 evaluation (Figure 4), f-DPO achieves performance comparable to or significantly better than PPO, with JSD-based f-DPO being particularly favored by GPT-4 over RKL-based f-DPO despite the latter's higher alignment accuracy on Anthropic HH.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly generalizes DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), which established a closed-form mapping between the reward function and optimal policy under reverse KL divergence, by extending the regularization to the broader f-divergence family (forward KL, Jensen-Shannon, α-divergences). The key technical departure is a careful KKT complementary-slackness analysis that eliminates the normalizing constant for divergences where 0 ∉ dom(f′), yielding a clean supervised-learning loss without requiring reward model training or RL rollouts. It contrasts with Go et al. (2023), who also minimized f-divergences for alignment but required specifying a target distribution and estimating its normalizing constant, adding hyperparameters and algorithmic complexity that f-DPO avoids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives practitioners a principled dial to trade off alignment accuracy against generation diversity, addressing a well-documented limitation of reverse-KL-based RLHF (mode collapse, reduced political diversity). The calibration bound (Theorem 2) provides a theoretical justification for why divergence-efficient methods like f-DPO should preserve the base model's calibration better than PPO. Limitations include the restriction to divergences with 0 ∉ dom(f′) (excluding total variation and chi-squared), experiments on relatively small models (GPT-2 large, Pythia 2.8B), and the consistent accuracy-diversity trade-off that means no single divergence dominates. Open questions include whether the divergence choice interacts with other alignment techniques (e.g., constitutional AI) and whether the results hold at frontier-model scale.
