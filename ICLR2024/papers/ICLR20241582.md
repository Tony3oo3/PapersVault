## Problem

Existing assembly graph-based metagenomic binners (GraphMB, RepBin, CCVAE) simplify unitig-level assembly graphs into contig-level graphs, reducing the resolution of connectivity information and potentially introducing erroneous edges. No prior GNN model directly encodes unitig-level assembly graphs while simultaneously enforcing heterophilous constraints derived from single-copy marker genes, which specify that certain contigs must not be co-binned.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241582/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the raw unitig-level assembly graph (nodes = unitigs, edges = overlaps) produced directly by metaSPAdes or metaFlye, along with heterophilous constraint sets identifying contigs sharing the same single-copy marker gene (detected via FragGeneScan + HMMER) and per-node composition features. It outputs per-contig bin assignments, d-dimensional unitig embeddings, and contig embeddings obtained via a mean readout over constituent unitigs. Unlike prior tools that discard contigs shorter than 1,000 bp, UNITIGBIN bins all contigs including short ones, and the p-Batch module handles graphs reaching millions of nodes by splitting along contig boundaries.

## Method

UNITIGBIN has two stages. The Learning stage is a variational autoencoder whose encoder uses PageRank-based diffusive convolution (DIFFCONV) to propagate node features across the unitig-level graph, and whose decoder reconstructs the adjacency matrix via inner-product scoring; heterophilous constraints are enforced through a triplet Gaussian ranking loss that penalizes co-binned unitig pairs being closer than their positive neighbors, and a p-Batch strategy splits the graph into contig-aligned sub-batches for parallel training with a joint-node KL penalty. The Binning stage then performs (i) a greedy Matching algorithm that initializes bins from the largest constraint set and assigns remaining marked contigs by embedding similarity above a threshold, (ii) a GCN-based label propagation with a constraint-satisfaction penalty term that maximizes the probability of assigning different bins to constrained contig pairs, and (iii) a local Refining step that splits bins containing duplicate marker genes and merges sub-bins that share no markers.

## Results

On the six metaSPAdes-assembled datasets, UNITIGBIN achieves the highest number of CheckM high-quality (HQ) bins across all datasets: 20 on Sim20G, 43 on Sim50G, 76 on Sim100G, 7 on Sharon, 12 on DeepHPM, and 21 on COPD. On Sim100G, UNITIGBIN yields 76 HQ bins, approximately 9.2% more than the best baseline (MetaCoAG, 69). On the six metaFlye-assembled WWTP datasets, UNITIGBIN produces 1,775 bins meeting the strict completeness>90 & contamination<5 criterion, a 45.8% improvement over the best baseline (962 bins). On Sim20G with ground-truth evaluation (AMBER), UNITIGBIN achieves a sequence-level F1 of 95.2% and ARI of 97.4%, substantially exceeding the next-best methods (MaxBin2: F1 63.2%, ARI 77.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNITIGBIN outperforms all ten baselines on the majority of datasets. Against traditional binners, MetaBAT2 is dramatically outperformed (e.g., 76 vs. 3 HQ bins on Sim100G), while MaxBin2 is matched on Sim20G (20 HQ bins each) but surpassed on all other datasets (e.g., 43 vs. 35 on Sim50G, 21 vs. 9 on COPD). Among deep learning binners, VAMB and SemiBin are outperformed on Sim50G (43 vs. 31/38) and Sim100G (76 vs. 51/68), and on real datasets like DeepHPM (12 vs. 2/–) and COPD (21 vs. 6/–). Among assembly-graph-based methods, UNITIGBIN surpasses MetaCoAG (the strongest graph-based baseline) on Sim50G (43 vs. 34), Sim100G (76 vs. 69), DeepHPM (12 vs. 8), and COPD (21 vs. 17), while tying on Sharon (7 each). GraphMB and CCVAE are significantly outperformed across all shared datasets (e.g., 76 vs. 18/28 HQ bins on Sim100G). RepBin, the closest prior constraint-based method, is outperformed on Sim50G (43 vs. 15) and Sim100G (76 vs. 19). In the appendix comparisons, UNITIGBIN also beats Metadecoder (76 vs. 35 HQ on Sim100G) and SemiBin2 (76 vs. 72 HQ on Sim100G), while tying SemiBin2 and MetaCoAG on Sharon (7 HQ bins each).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constraint-based graph learning framework introduced by RepBin (Xue et al., 2022) by moving from simplified contig-level assembly graphs to the original unitig-level graphs produced by assemblers like metaSPAdes and metaFlye. The encoder architecture builds directly on the PageRank-based diffusive convolution of Klicpera et al. (2019), while the triplet Gaussian constraint loss adapts the KL-divergence ranking strategy from Bojchevski & Günnemann (2018) to enforce heterophilous relationships from single-copy marker genes. The key departure from prior assembly-graph binners (GraphMB, CCVAE, MetaCoAG) is the elimination of the lossy unitig-to-contig simplification step, combined with a p-Batch strategy to handle the resulting scale increase.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By preserving the full connectivity structure of unitig-level graphs, UNITIGBIN addresses a fundamental information bottleneck in prior graph-based binners, enabling it to bin short contigs (<1,000 bp) that composition/coverage-based tools discard, achieving 100% contig coverage on simulated datasets. The 45.8% improvement in qualifying bins over the best baseline on metaFlye-assembled real data suggests that graph resolution matters substantially in practice. Open questions include scalability to very large metagenomes (the p-Batch loss for joint-unitig pairs introduces approximation), inability to handle overlapping bins where contigs span multiple species, and whether the constraint satisfaction mechanism generalizes to multi-label or strain-level binning.
