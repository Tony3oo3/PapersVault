## Problem

Vanilla lightweight Vision Transformers (e.g., ViT-Tiny) still underperform their CNN and hybrid counterparts despite advances in self-supervised pre-training. Prior work has focused on training recipes (distillation, configuration tuning) rather than the architecture itself, and the widely-used structural re-parameterization technique—effective for CNNs via convolution and batch normalization—cannot be directly transferred to ViTs, which rely on linear layers and layer normalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240977/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images at task-specific resolutions (224×224 for classification, 512×512 for segmentation, 1024×1024 for detection) and produces class labels, pixel-wise segmentation maps, bounding boxes, or instance masks. A critical design constraint is that the multi-branch re-parameterized architecture is active only during training; at inference, all branches are algebraically merged into single linear layers, so the deployed model has identical parameter count and FLOPs to the vanilla ViT baseline.

## Method

The paper proposes TDRL (Two-Dimensional Reparameterized Linear module), which replaces linear layers in ViT's MHSA (Q, K, V projections) and FFN with a pyramid multi-branch structure. Each rep-branch stacks n linear layers with batch normalization in between (where n equals the branch index), creating depth-varying hierarchical representations that avoid the homogeneity problem of simple linear replicas. A skip branch (single linear layer) provides a short gradient path. A key transformer-specific challenge is that the additive multi-branch mechanism amplifies the variance of Q·K^T attention logits from C_k to C_k·N_Q·N_K, causing near-one-hot attention maps and training collapse; the authors solve this with a distribution rectification step (batch normalization on Q, K outputs). At inference, intra-branch fusion (merging linear+BN via Eq. 1, then cascading linears via Eq. 3) and inter-branch fusion (summing all branches via Eq. 2) collapse the entire module into a single linear layer. The default configuration P-W2S (pyramid, 2 rep-branches + skip) is applied uniformly across all transformer blocks, and the module is trained via MAE pre-training with generic distillation from a ViT-Base teacher, followed by supervised fine-tuning.

## Results

On ImageNet validation with ViT-Tiny (6M params), TDRL achieves 78.3% top-1 accuracy (200 FT epochs) and 78.7% (300 FT epochs) without specific distillation, and 78.6%/79.1% with distillation, setting new SOTA for lightweight ViTs. On dense prediction, TDRL reaches 42.5 mIoU on ADE20K and 46.5 AP^bbox / 41.5 AP^mask on MS COCO, outperforming MAE-Ti by over 3.2 mIoU and 3.9 AP^bbox. Inference speed remains identical to the baseline (0.35 s/iteration) since the re-parameterized branches are merged into single linear layers at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDRL outperforms all 20 baselines on ImageNet classification. Against the strongest ViT-Tiny competitors with pre-training, it beats G2SD-Ti (77.0%) by 1.3% at 200 epochs and D-MAE-Lite (78.4%) by 0.3% at 300 epochs. It also surpasses the best hybrid network MobileViT-S (78.3%) by 0.4% at 300 epochs and the best CNN ConvNeXt-V1-F (77.5%) by 1.2%. On dense prediction, TDRL exceeds MAE-Ti by 3.2 mIoU (ADE20K) and 3.9 AP^bbox (COCO), and edges out G2SD-Ti by 1.1 mIoU and 2.5 AP^bbox without requiring fine-tuning distillation. On robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-A/R/S/V2), TDRL leads all compared methods (e.g., 14.7% on IN-A vs. 13.9% for D-MAE-Lite). The generality experiments confirm gains across ViT-Small (+0.5%), Swin-Ti (+2.0%), MobileOne-S0 (+3.8%), VanillaNet-5 (+0.4%), and DDPM FID (10.4→9.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the structural re-parameterization paradigm established for CNNs (RepVGG, Diverse Branch Block) to vanilla Vision Transformers, which are built from linear and layer normalization layers rather than convolutions and batch normalization. The key conceptual departure is that layer normalization cannot be statically merged at inference, forcing the authors to design a new linear-ensemble architecture (stacked linear layers with interleaved batch normalization) and a pyramid multi-branch topology to replace the convolution-based multi-branch designs. Unlike concurrent lightweight ViT work (MAE-Lite, TinyMIM, G2SD) that focuses on training recipes and knowledge distillation, this paper targets the model architecture itself, increasing training capacity while preserving inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of a transformer-specific distribution rectification problem—where multi-branch additive re-parameterization amplifies attention logit variance by a factor of N_Q·N_K, causing training instability—is a genuinely useful insight for anyone combining over-parameterization with self-attention. The practical impact is that a 6M-parameter ViT-Tiny with TDRL achieves SOTA on ImageNet classification, ADE20K segmentation, and COCO detection, closing the gap with carefully designed CNN/hybrid networks. Limitations include ~45% increased training memory and time, and the method still benefits from (though does not strictly require) MIM pre-training and distillation for best results. Open questions include whether the pyramid depth schedule is optimal, whether stronger regularization is needed for larger backbones, and whether the approach transfers to non-vision transformer architectures.
