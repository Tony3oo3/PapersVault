## Problem

Computer storage inherently imposes an ordering on data, but for unordered structures like graphs, sets, and multisets this ordering is pure redundancy. Prior work achieved optimal compression only for multisets (Severo et al., 2023a) or simple unlabeled graphs (SZIP), leaving no general, model-agnostic method for losslessly compressing arbitrary unordered objects at the information-theoretic optimum.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of unordered objects (e.g., graphs with vertex and edge attributes) along with a chosen statistical model for their ordered counterparts (Erdős-Rényi G(n,p) or Pólya urn). It requires access to a canonical ordering function (computed via nauty/Traces) and the automorphism group of each object. The output is a single rANS bitstream whose length equals the ordered-model rate minus the discount log(n!/|Aut(f)|) per object, with a small O(log n!) initial-bits overhead amortized over the sequence.

## Method

Shuffle coding constructs a codec for unordered objects from any existing codec for ordered objects by exploiting the orbit-stabilizer theorem: the optimal rate for an unordered object equals the ordered rate minus log(n!/|Aut(f)|), where the discount equals the log of the number of left cosets of the automorphism group in S_n. The encoder canonizes the input object, then uses a uniform codec over those cosets (UniformLCoset) to *decode* a random permutation from the message, applying it to the canonical form to produce an ordered object that is then encoded with the standard ordered codec. The decoder reverses this by decoding the ordered object, recovering the canonical form, and re-encoding the coset element back into the message. The uniform coset codec itself is built from a Fisher-Yates-based uniform codec over S_n and a stabilizer-chain-based uniform codec over the automorphism group, making the method composable with any rANS-based ordered codec and any exchangeable statistical model.

## Results

Shuffle coding achieves state-of-the-art lossless compression rates on unordered graphs, measured in bits per edge. Against PnC (a neural graph compression method), shuffle coding with the Erdős-Rényi model wins on 5 of 8 TUDatasets (e.g., 1.82 vs. 1.99 bits/edge on ZINC_full, 4.88 vs. 6.49 on PTC_MR with attributes), while PnC retains an advantage on the two IMDB social-network datasets (0.54 vs. 1.50 on IMDB-BINARY). Against SZIP, shuffle coding with the Pólya urn model outperforms on all six large-scale datasets, with the largest margin on Collaboration(Erdos) at 4.37 vs. 5.08 bits/edge. The 'discount' from removing order information ranges from 26% to 47% across TUDatasets graph types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PnC (Bouritsas et al., 2021), shuffle coding with the simple Erdős-Rényi model achieves lower bits-per-edge on five of eight TUDatasets, with the largest margin on PTC_MR with attributes (4.88 vs. 6.49±0.54) and MUTAG (1.88 vs. 2.45±0.02). PnC wins on the two IMDB social-network datasets (0.54 vs. 1.50 on IMDB-BINARY; 0.38 vs. 1.14 on IMDB-MULTI), where its learned partitioning captures structure that the simple ER/PU models miss. Against SZIP (Choi & Szpankowski, 2012), shuffle coding with the Pólya urn model outperforms on all six large-scale datasets, with margins ranging from 0.37 bits/edge (YeastS: 5.70 vs. 7.05) to 3.89 bits/edge (Collaboration-geom: 4.41 vs. 5.28). Notably, shuffle coding's advantage over SZIP grows with graph size and symmetry (larger discount), and the method's model-agnostic design allowed the authors to swap in the PU model and immediately improve over ER without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Shuffle coding generalizes the multiset-specific compression framework of Severo et al. (2023a) to arbitrary permutable classes (graphs, hypergraphs, etc.) by replacing the simple multiset automorphism structure with the full coset structure of automorphism groups, formalized via combinatorial species. The core mechanism—bits-back with ANS (BB-ANS) from Townsend et al. (2019)—is reused to 'spend back' the bits corresponding to redundant ordering, but the paper's key conceptual step is showing that a uniform codec over left cosets of Aut(f) in S_n achieves exactly the log(n!/|Aut(f)|) discount term. This positions the work as a unifying treatment where prior methods (SZIP for simple graphs, Steinruecken's specialized codecs for multisets/permutations/combinations) each handled one object type with bespoke machinery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general, model-agnostic method achieving the information-theoretic optimal rate for sequences of arbitrary unordered objects, with practical 26–47% compression gains on real graph datasets. Two limitations temper immediate impact: canonical ordering has no known polynomial-time algorithm for general graphs (making worst-case runtime impractical), and the one-shot case incurs an O(log n!) initial-bit overhead that is only amortized over sequences. The most promising open direction is combining shuffle coding with deep generative graph models (e.g., Pólya urn variants or neural sequence models), which the authors note would replace the simple ER/PU models and likely yield further rate improvements without changing the codec structure.
