## Problem

Binary Neural Networks (BNNs) achieve low-power inference via pop-count operations but produce dense bi-polar activations with no sparsity, while Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) offer high activation sparsity (up to 80%) but require multiple time steps that increase latency and energy, negating their theoretical efficiency gains. The paper asks whether one can obtain SNN-like sparsity and BNN-like accuracy simultaneously in a single time step, bridging the two paradigms for efficient computer vision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242253/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 224×224 ImageNet) or event streams from dynamic vision sensors (DVS-CIFAR10, IBM-Gesture) and outputs class labels or bounding boxes (mAP on VOC2007). The first convolutional layer receives full-precision input, while all subsequent layers operate on binary (0/1) activations with ~75–84% sparsity. For object detection, BANN backbones (ResNet50, VGG16) are plugged into Faster R-CNN and RetinaNet frameworks pretrained on ImageNet.

## Method

The core contribution is a training framework for sparse uni-polar Binary Activation Neural Networks (BANNs) built on two coupled mechanisms: (1) a Hoyer regularizer H(u_l) = (||u_l||₁/||u_l||₂)² applied to clipped activations, which shifts the pre-activation distribution away from the threshold to reduce noise and improve convergence; and (2) a Hoyer threshold layer that sets each layer's binary threshold to the Hoyer extremum E(z_l^clip) = ||z_l^clip||₂²/||z_l^clip||₁ of the clipped activation tensor, which is provably ≤ 1 and thus dynamically down-scales the SGD-trained threshold v_l^th. The clipping value v_l^th is itself trained via gradient descent, and the non-differentiable step function is handled with a surrogate gradient of fixed width 2 centered at the threshold. This allows training from scratch with Kaiming initialization (no pre-trained DNN required), and the framework extends naturally to multi-time-step SNNs by incorporating LIF membrane potentials with soft reset while retaining the Hoyer-based threshold computation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed BANN (VGG16) achieves 93.44% top-1 accuracy with 78.13% activation sparsity using only 1 time step, compared to 94.10% for the full-precision DNN. On ImageNet, VGG16 BANN reaches 68.00% top-1 accuracy (vs. 70.08% DNN) with 75.52% sparsity. For object detection on VOC2007, Faster R-CNN with a BANN ResNet50 backbone achieves 73.7% mAP, surpassing ReActNet (73.1%) and Bi-Real (65.7%). The method yields 22.7× and 31.9× compute energy reduction (and 70.28× and 81.13× total energy reduction) on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet respectively compared to full-precision DNNs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against one-time-step SNNs (Chowdhury et al. 2021), BANN wins on both CIFAR-10 (93.44% vs. 93.05%) and ImageNet (68.00% vs. 67.71%) while requiring 19× less training time. Against multi-time-step SNNs, BANN matches or exceeds accuracy with dramatically lower latency: e.g., 93.44% at 1 TS vs. Rathi et al.'s 92.70% at 5 TS, and 68.00% at 1 TS vs. Rathi et al.'s 69.00% at 5 TS on ImageNet (a 1% gap offset by 5× lower latency). Against AddNNs (Chen et al. 2020), BANN achieves comparable accuracy (93.44% vs. 93.72% on CIFAR-10; 68.00% vs. 67.00% on ImageNet) but consumes ~5.1× less compute energy due to 75%+ activation sparsity that AddNNs cannot exploit. Against uni-polar BNNs, BANN with 1-bit weights outperforms Sakr et al. (92.80% vs. 89.6% on CIFAR-10) and Wang et al. (64.62% vs. 59.7% on ImageNet) by 3.2% and 4.9% respectively. Against bi-polar BNNs (Diffenderfer & Kailkhura 2021), BANN with 2-bit weights uses 2.6× less energy (5.66 mJ vs. 14.72 mJ on CIFAR-10). The Hoyer regularizer also improves advanced bi-polar BNNs (PokeBNN, MeliusNet, ReCU) by 0.2–1.4% accuracy when applied as a plug-in.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—uni-polar binary neural networks (which use a trainable scalar threshold to produce sparse 0/1 activations) and spiking neural networks (which achieve sparsity through temporal dynamics)—by proposing a Hoyer-extremum-based threshold that is dynamically computed from the activation distribution itself. The Hoyer regularizer, originally introduced for weight sparsity (Yang et al., 2020), is repurposed here to shape activation distributions away from the threshold, while the surrogate gradient mechanism from SNN training (Neftci et al., 2019) is adapted to handle the non-differentiable binary step. The LIF neuron model (Sengupta et al., 2019) is retained for the multi-time-step SNN extension, but the core BANN formulation eliminates the temporal dimension entirely, positioning BANNs as a single-time-step equivalent that inherits SNN sparsity without SNN latency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge and neuromorphic deployment: achieving ~75%+ activation sparsity in a single time step with BNN-competitive accuracy (93.44% CIFAR-10, 68% ImageNet) yields 22–32× compute energy reduction and 70–81× total energy reduction versus full-precision DNNs, without the multi-time-step memory overhead that plagues SNNs. The framework's portability—improving existing bi-polar BNNs by 0.2–1.3% and extending to multi-time-step SNNs—suggests it could serve as a general training technique across the BNN/SNN spectrum. Key limitations include the dependence on hardware that supports zero-gating for sparsity exploitation, the ~6% accuracy gap versus full-precision on ImageNet, and the fact that 1-bit-weight BANNs still trail SOTA bi-polar BNNs (which use more FLOPs-intensive architectures like ReactNet). An open question is whether the Hoyer-extremum threshold generalizes to non-convolutional architectures or transformer-based models.
