## Problem

Standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods retrieve a fixed number of passages indiscriminately, regardless of whether retrieval is actually needed, which diminishes LM versatility and can introduce irrelevant context that degrades generation quality. Moreover, generated outputs are not guaranteed to be consistent with retrieved passages because models are not explicitly trained to ground their claims in provided evidence. The paper addresses the gap of having no framework that both adaptively decides when to retrieve and self-evaluates generation quality at the segment level.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (instruction or query) and a large-scale passage collection (Wikipedia, web search results) as input, and produces segmented text output interleaved with special reflection tokens drawn from an expanded vocabulary. At inference, the model processes up to K retrieved passages in parallel per segment, generating K candidate continuations that are ranked via segment-level beam search. The final output includes per-segment citations with self-assessed support levels (fully/partially/no support), enabling fine-grained fact verification.

## Method

SELF-RAG trains a single LM (Llama2-7B/13B) to generate both task output and four types of reflection tokens (Retrieve, IsREL, IsSUP, IsUSE) as next-token predictions from an expanded vocabulary. Training is two-stage: first, a critic model C is distilled from GPT-4 to predict reflection tokens on 4k–20k samples per token type; then C is used offline to augment 150k instruction-output pairs with reflection tokens and retrieved passages, and the generator M is trained on this augmented corpus with a standard LM objective (retrieved passages are masked from the loss). At inference, adaptive retrieval is triggered when the normalized probability of Retrieve=Yes exceeds a threshold δ, and segment-level beam search scores each candidate as a weighted linear sum of critique token probabilities (IsREL, IsSUP, IsUSE), with weights adjustable at test time to trade off citation precision against fluency or to enforce hard constraints (e.g., filtering out segments with IsSUP=No support).

## Results

SELF-RAG (7B and 13B) achieves the best performance among all non-proprietary models across all six tasks. On PopQA, SELF-RAG 7B reaches 54.9% accuracy (vs. 29.3% for ChatGPT and 51.8% for Ret-Llama2-chat 13B); on PubHealth fact verification, it scores 72.4% (vs. 70.1% ChatGPT); and on ASQA long-form generation, it achieves 66.9% citation precision and 67.8% citation recall (vs. 65.1%/76.6% for Ret-ChatGPT). On biography generation, SELF-RAG 7B attains 81.2 FactScore, outperforming both ChatGPT (71.8) and the concurrent CoVE 65B (71.2). The 13B variant further improves to 74.5% on PubHealth and 73.1% on ARC Challenge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SELF-RAG 7B outperforms ChatGPT on PopQA (54.9 vs. 29.3), PubHealth (72.4 vs. 70.1), Bio FactScore (81.2 vs. 71.8), and ASQA MAUVE (74.3 vs. 68.8), but trails on TQA (66.4 vs. 74.3) and ARC (67.3 vs. 75.3). Against Ret-ChatGPT, SELF-RAG 7B wins on PopQA (54.9 vs. 50.8), PubHealth (72.4 vs. 54.7), and ASQA citation precision (66.9 vs. 65.1), while Ret-ChatGPT retains an edge on ASQA MAUVE (79.7 vs. 74.3) and recall (76.6 vs. 67.8). SELF-RAG 7B substantially outperforms Ret-Llama2-chat 13B on PubHealth (72.4 vs. 52.1), ARC (67.3 vs. 37.9), and ASQA citation precision (66.9 vs. 19.8). The concurrent CoVE 65B, which uses iterative prompt engineering on Llama2-65B, is outperformed by SELF-RAG 7B on Bio FactScore (81.2 vs. 71.2). Critically, Llama2-FT 7B—trained on the same 150k instruction-output pairs but without reflection tokens or retrieved passages—lags SELF-RAG 7B by 6.2 points on PopQA, 8.1 on PubHealth, and 61.9 on ASQA citation precision, confirming that gains stem from the self-reflection framework rather than training data alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

SELF-RAG extends the RAG paradigm (Lewis et al. 2020; Guu et al. 2020) by replacing indiscriminate fixed-document retrieval with on-demand retrieval decided by the model itself, and by adding a self-critique mechanism via special reflection tokens. Conceptually, it draws from RLHF's use of critic/reward models (Ziegler et al. 2019; Ouyang et al. 2022) but replaces online PPO with offline distillation of GPT-4 judgments into a small critic, then uses that critic to augment the training corpus—dramatically reducing training cost. It also relates to control-token approaches (Keskar et al. 2019; Lu et al. 2022) but repurposes special tokens not for conditioning generation style, but for the model to evaluate its own output quality segment-by-segment, enabling both soft re-ranking and hard constraints at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SELF-RAG addresses a practical failure mode of standard RAG—retrieval can hurt performance when unnecessary or when passages are irrelevant—by letting the model decide when to retrieve and self-assess whether its output is supported. The reflection-token mechanism provides a clean, training-free way to customize model behavior at test time (e.g., trading citation precision for fluency), which is practically valuable for deployment. Limitations include the requirement for token-level probability access (ruling out black-box APIs like ChatGPT), the modest 150k training data scale, and the fact that outputs can still be unsupported despite the critique mechanism. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models, whether better retrievers or joint retrieval-generation training would further close the gap, and whether the GPT-4-distilled critic introduces systematic biases.
