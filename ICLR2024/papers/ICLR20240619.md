## Problem

Adversarial training improves robustness but degrades natural accuracy, creating a persistent generalization-robustness trade-off, and prolonged adversarial training leads to robust overfitting where test accuracy declines. The underlying layer-wise learning dynamics during the transition from standard to adversarial training remain unexplored, and existing methods either address the trade-off or robust overfitting but not both simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240619/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN) and outputs class labels. A key requirement is a pre-trained model on natural images, which is then fine-tuned in the adversarial setting; adversarial samples are generated on-the-fly via PGD (10 steps, ε=8/255) during training. An auxiliary revision model is maintained alongside the training model, and the final inference uses only the single trained network.

## Method

CURE introduces a dynamic, gradient-based selective update mechanism with three stages. First, a Robust Gradient Prominence (RGP) score combines natural and adversarial gradient magnitudes (weighted by α) per weight to identify which parameters most influence joint performance; weights below a percentile threshold p are masked (conserved) while the rest are updated. Second, the adversarial training objective uses a TRADES-style loss (cross-entropy on natural images plus KL-divergence between natural and adversarial predictions) to encourage representation alignment. Third, a revision model is stochastically updated via momentum averaging at a fixed rate r, and a consistency regularization term (KL-divergence between revision and training model predictions on both natural and adversarial inputs) consolidates accumulated knowledge back into the training model, reducing overfitting and stabilizing the trade-off.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WideResNet-34-10, CURE achieves 87.05% natural accuracy, 58.28% PGD-20 robustness, and 55.25% C&W robustness, yielding the highest Natural-Robustness Ratio (NRR) of 67.60 among all compared methods. On ResNet-18, CURE reaches 86.76% natural accuracy and 54.92% PGD-20 robustness (NRR 65.04). On SVHN, CURE attains 92.77% natural accuracy and 61.56% PGD-20 robustness, outperforming the next-best method (HAT) by over 4% on PGD. In the robust overfitting evaluation (Table 3), CURE is the only method achieving Δ=0.00 (no accuracy decline from best to last checkpoint) on both PreActResNet-18 and WideResNet-34-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CURE achieves the highest NRR across all evaluated settings, outperforming the closest competitor LAS-AT by 0.41 (WRN-34-10) and 1.32 (ResNet-18) on CIFAR-10. On WideResNet-34-10, CURE surpasses TRADES by 1.46% on PGD-20 (58.28 vs 56.82) and by 0.96% on C&W (55.25 vs 54.29), while also maintaining the highest natural accuracy (87.05 vs 84.73). On SVHN (ResNet-18), CURE leads HAT by 4.21% on PGD-20 (61.56 vs 57.35) and by 2.57% on C&W (57.34 vs 54.77). In the robust overfitting comparison (Table 3), CURE is the only method with zero accuracy decline (Δ=0.00) from best to last checkpoint on both architectures, whereas AWP shows −0.62, KD shows −0.15, and IDBH shows −1.82 on AA accuracy. CURE slightly trails LAS-AT and ST-AT on AutoAttack for ResNet-18 (49.69 vs 49.94 and 50.50 respectively), but compensates with substantially higher natural accuracy and C&W robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

CURE builds directly on the TRADES adversarial regularization formulation (CE + KL-divergence between natural and adversarial predictions) as its training loss, but departs fundamentally from the standard practice of updating all network weights during adversarial training. The empirical layer-wise analysis draws on re-initialization studies from standard training (Li et al., 2020; Alabdulmohsin et al., 2021) and representation similarity metrics (CKA, Kornblith et al., 2019), applying them for the first time to the ST→AT transition to motivate selective weight updates. Conceptually, CURE bridges two previously disjoint research threads—trade-off improvement (TRADES, MART, LAS-AT) and robust overfitting mitigation (AWP, KD, TE, IDBH)—by showing that sparse gradient-based weight conservation addresses both simultaneously, rather than treating them as separate problems requiring separate mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous mitigation of the generalization-robustness trade-off and robust overfitting (achieving Δ=0) is practically significant, as prior methods that reduce overfitting (AWP, KD) typically sacrifice natural accuracy, and trade-off methods (TRADES) still suffer from overfitting. The layer-wise empirical findings—that middle layers are optimal for selective adversarial updating and that early-layer updates cause the most overwriting—offer new mechanistic understanding of adversarial learning dynamics. However, all experiments are confined to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, SVHN) and CNN architectures, leaving open whether the RGP criterion and revision mechanism scale to ImageNet or non-CNN backbones (e.g., Vision Transformers). The requirement for a pre-trained natural model (partially addressed by CURE^Eff) and the additional forward/backward pass for gradient prominence computation also limit immediate practical adoption.
