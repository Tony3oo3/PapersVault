## Problem

Complex multi-step reasoning remains a persistent bottleneck for LLMs, with existing prompting techniques like Chain-of-Thought providing only marginal gains on challenging tasks. The paper addresses the observation that humans often 'step back' to derive high-level principles before tackling detailed problems, and investigates whether LLMs can similarly benefit from an explicit abstraction step that grounds subsequent reasoning on first principles or high-level concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240813/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question from reasoning-intensive benchmarks (STEM, Knowledge QA, Multi-Hop Reasoning) along with 1–5 few-shot exemplars demonstrating the abstraction step. For Knowledge QA and Multi-Hop tasks, the generated step-back question additionally drives a second retrieval pass, and both the original-question and step-back-question passages are concatenated as context for the final reasoning prompt. The output is a free-form text answer, scored by a PaLM-2L judge model that checks semantic equivalence between the model's response and the ground-truth label rather than exact string match.

## Method

STEP-BACK PROMPTING decomposes problem-solving into two prompting stages: (1) Abstraction, where the LLM is prompted via few-shot demonstrations to generate a higher-level 'step-back question' (e.g., 'What are the physics principles behind this question?' or 'What was Estella Leopold's education history?') and retrieve relevant facts about that concept; and (2) Abstraction-grounded Reasoning, where the LLM answers the original question using the retrieved high-level principles or facts as grounding context. The step-back question is task-specific and designed to be substantially easier than the original, with a many-to-one mapping from specific questions to a shared abstract question. For STEM tasks the model recites first principles from its own parametric knowledge; for Knowledge QA and Multi-Hop tasks the step-back question drives retrieval augmentation to fetch relevant passages. The approach is model-agnostic (validated on PaLM-2L, GPT-4, and Llama2-70B), requires only a single exemplar to elicit the abstraction skill, and error analysis shows that >90% of residual errors occur in the reasoning step rather than the abstraction step.

## Results

On MMLU high-school Physics and Chemistry, Step-Back Prompting improves PaLM-2L by +7% (66.4%→73.2%) and +11% (70.9%→81.8%) respectively, and is competitive with GPT-4 (84.5% Physics, 85.6% Chemistry). On TimeQA, Step-Back + RAG achieves 68.7% accuracy, a +27% absolute gain over the 41.5% PaLM-2L baseline and substantially outperforming GPT-4's 45.6%. On MuSiQue, Step-Back + RAG reaches 42.8% (vs. 35.5% baseline), and on StrategyQA it achieves 86.4% (vs. 82.8% baseline), both significantly exceeding GPT-4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Step-Back Prompting consistently outperforms zero-shot CoT (Kojima et al., 2022) across all task categories: on MMLU Physics it gains +8.2% (73.2% vs. 65.0%), on TimeQA +25.2% (66% vs. 40.8%), and on MuSiQue +3.9% (42.6% vs. 38.7%). Against Take-a-Deep-Breath prompting (Yang et al., 2023), Step-Back shows similar margins: +7.5% on MMLU Physics (73.2% vs. 65.7%) and +25.1% on TimeQA (66% vs. 40.9%). On GSM8K, Step-Back (84.3%) slightly edges out Decomposed Prompting (Khot et al., 2022) at 82.2%, though both are close to zero-shot CoT (84.4%). The largest absolute gains appear on Knowledge QA tasks where Step-Back + RAG (68.7% on TimeQA) surpasses GPT-4 (45.6%) by 23.1 points, suggesting that abstraction-guided retrieval is particularly effective for fact-intensive queries. Error analysis shows Step-Back corrects up to ~40% of baseline errors while introducing at most ~12% new errors, with the dominant remaining failure mode being intrinsic reasoning errors rather than abstraction failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

Step-Back Prompting sits within the prompting/in-context-learning family alongside Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022b) and zero-shot CoT (Kojima et al., 2022), but departs from them by inserting an explicit abstraction step before reasoning. The authors explicitly contrast their approach with decomposition methods (Khot et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2022), noting that decomposition produces a one-to-many breakdown into sub-questions, whereas Step-Back produces a many-to-one mapping to a single higher-level question. It also relates to recitation-augmented language models (Sun et al., 2022) but differs by explicitly formulating and answering a step-back question rather than simply reciting relevant knowledge. The conceptual motivation draws on human cognitive science—abstraction as a mechanism for distilling principles from details—rather than on a specific prior NLP technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a remarkably simple prompting intervention (asking one higher-level question) yields consistent, model-agnostic gains across STEM, Knowledge QA, and Multi-Hop Reasoning, with the largest improvement (27% on TimeQA) on tasks where baselines struggle most. The error analysis is particularly informative: over 90% of Step-Back's remaining errors are reasoning errors rather than abstraction errors, and a single exemplar suffices to teach the abstraction skill, identifying reasoning as the true bottleneck for LLMs on complex tasks. This reframes where future effort should be directed—improving intermediate reasoning steps rather than retrieval or abstraction. An open question is whether Step-Back composes well with other reasoning-enhancement techniques (e.g., process supervision, self-consistency) and whether the abstraction step remains beneficial as base models improve their intrinsic reasoning.
