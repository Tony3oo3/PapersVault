## Problem

Naive error compensation (EC) with contractive compressors in distributed training often leads to unstable convergence or exponential divergence due to compression bias, especially under data heterogeneity. Existing convergence analyses require impractical assumptions such as bounded gradients, bounded data dissimilarity, or large batch sizes, and no prior method simultaneously supports arbitrary contractive compressors, arbitrary data heterogeneity, and linear parallel speedup across all standard optimization regimes (strongly convex, convex, nonconvex).

## Input / Output

Each of n clients computes a local stochastic gradient g_t^i at the current iterate, applies a contractive compressor C_δ (e.g., Top-K) to a fused signal combining the scaled error term, the gradient, and a learned local gradient estimator, and sends the single compressed message Δ_t^i to a central server. The server aggregates the compressed updates to produce the next global iterate x_{t+1}. The method requires no additional unbiased compressor, no large batches, and no repeated communication rounds per iteration, making it a lightweight drop-in extension to existing EC implementations.

## Method

EControl introduces a new hyperparameter η (set to cδ for a constant c) that scales the error term inside the compression operator, yielding the update Δ_t^i = C_δ(η e_t^i + g_t^i − h_t^i). Crucially, the algorithm simultaneously maintains a per-client gradient estimator h_t^i (updated as h_t^i ← h_t^i + Δ_t^i) and an error term e_t^i (updated as e_t^i ← e_t^i + g_t^i − h_t^i − Δ_t^i), fusing both into a single compressed message per iteration. The η parameter precisely controls the strength of the feedback signal, balancing the error compensation carried by the gradient estimator; setting η ∼ δ is essential for stability, as η = 1 can cause divergence depending on initialization. When η → 0 the method recovers EF21, but the η-scaled error injection is what enables linear speedup in the number of clients. The paper proves convergence in strongly convex, general convex, and nonconvex settings with complexities matching known lower bounds, without any assumptions on data heterogeneity or batch size.

## Results

EControl achieves asymptotically optimal convergence rates in all three standard regimes (strongly convex, general convex, nonconvex) with contractive compression and no additional assumptions on data heterogeneity. In the nonconvex setting, the rate matches the known lower bound of He et al. (2023) without requiring large batches or repeated communication rounds. Empirically, on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 and VGG-13, EControl reaches a smaller training loss (better stationary point) than both EF21 and EF21-SGDM, and achieves slightly better test accuracy than EF21 and considerably higher accuracy than EF21-SGDM. On the synthetic heterogeneous least-squares problem, EControl is unaffected by the gradient dissimilarity parameter ζ while classic EC deteriorates, and EControl outperforms SGD by roughly a factor of d/K = 10 in bits sent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EF21 (Richtárik et al., 2021), EControl achieves faster convergence in training loss and slightly better test accuracy on both MNIST logistic regression and CIFAR-10 deep learning tasks; EF21 exhibits noticeably more unstable convergence with frequent oscillations in training loss. Against EF21-SGDM (Fatkhullin et al., 2023), EControl achieves considerably higher test accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18 and VGG-13) and a smaller training loss, while EF21-SGDM lags behind on both metrics. Against classic EC (Stich, 2020), EControl is robust to increasing data heterogeneity (gradient dissimilarity ζ) whereas EC's convergence deteriorates as ζ grows; EControl also slightly outperforms EC in the synthetic least-squares experiment. Against Compressed-SGD without error compensation (Khirirat et al., 2018), EControl provably converges while Compressed-SGD diverges entirely under the same heterogeneous setting. In the theoretical comparison (Table 1), EControl is the first method to simultaneously achieve linear speedup in n, no large-batch requirement, and no additional heterogeneity assumptions across all three convexity regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

EControl extends the error compensation (EC) lineage from Seide et al. (2014) and Stich (2020) by introducing a single η parameter that scales the feedback signal, replacing the need for an additional unbiased compressor (as in DIANA-based methods) while preserving linear speedup in the number of clients (which EF21 lacked). Conceptually, it unifies the error term and gradient estimator updates into a single compressed message, with η providing the fine-grained control needed to balance error compensation against the drift carried by the gradient estimator. The paper positions itself as a lightweight drop-in extension to existing EC implementations rather than a fundamentally new algorithmic framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first distributed algorithm using only a contractive compressor that provably converges in strongly convex, general convex, and nonconvex regimes without any assumptions on data heterogeneity, matching known lower bounds in the nonconvex case. For practitioners deploying compressed communication in federated or data-parallel training, EControl offers a practical, low-overhead upgrade to existing EC pipelines that handles heterogeneous data without the large-batch or repeated-round requirements of prior methods. Open questions include the theoretical analysis of EControl combined with momentum (acknowledged as future work) and whether the η~δ scaling generalizes to more complex compressor families or asynchronous settings.
