## Problem

Image recognition and segmentation are treated as separate tasks with distinct models, architectures, and supervision: recognition models (ViT) learn global features from image-level labels without spatial grounding, while segmentation models (SAM) learn pixel-level boundaries without hierarchical part-whole organization. The paper argues that segmentation should not be an end task mastered through supervised learning but an internal process that evolves with and supports recognition, ensuring part-to-whole consistency that neither approach alone provides.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and decomposes it into ~196 superpixels via SEEDS (rather than fixed 16×16 patches), producing segment tokens at four progressively coarser granularities (196→64→32→16). Outputs include a hierarchical segmentation derived directly from pixel-to-segment index transitions at each level (no post-processing), a global class token for recognition, and multi-level segment tokens for downstream segmentation. During test-time adaptation, the model can iteratively refine its segmentation by maximizing recognition confidence, producing improved part-whole boundaries without additional labels.

## Method

CAST (Concurrent segmentation and recognition using Adaptive Segment Tokens) modifies ViT in two ways: (1) replacing fixed square patches with arbitrarily-shaped superpixels that adhere to visual contours, and (2) inserting graph pooling modules between encoder blocks that progressively merge fine segments into coarser ones via soft assignment probabilities computed from feature similarity. The graph pooling initializes coarse centroids using Farthest Point Sampling, computes assignment probabilities via cosine similarity, and updates segment features through an MLP head. The entire architecture is trained solely on image-level recognition objectives (self-supervised MoCo or supervised DeiT), with no segment-level supervision, so hierarchical segmentation emerges as a byproduct of optimizing recognition. At test time, TENT-based test-time adaptation backpropagates recognition confidence through the reverse hierarchy to refine segmentation, creating a mutual enhancement loop between part parsing and whole recognition.

## Results

On PartImageNet object segmentation, CAST-B (self-supervised on 1M unlabeled ImageNet images) achieves 29.66% region mIoU, outperforming SAM-H (21.97%, trained on 11M images and 1B masks) by 8% absolute and ViT-B (25.34%) by 4%, while using only 13 GFLOPS vs. SAM-H's 3166 GFLOPS. On ImageNet-100 linear probing, CAST-S achieves 79.9% top-1 accuracy with 3.4 GFLOPS, surpassing ViT-S (78.1%, 4.7 GFLOPS) and Swin-T (78.3%, 4.5 GFLOPS). For semantic segmentation on PASCAL VOC (self-supervised on COCO), CAST-S reaches 38.4% mIoU before fine-tuning and 67.6% after, compared to ViT-S's 30.9% and 65.8% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023), CAST-B wins on region mIoU for both objects (+8% over SAM-H, +12% over SAM-B) and parts (+1.1% over SAM-H), while SAM retains a lead on boundary F-score for objects (32.66 vs. 22.32 for SAM-H) due to its dedicated mask decoder and multi-scale boundary supervision. Against ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020), CAST-B outperforms on PartImageNet object mIoU by 4% (29.66 vs. 25.34) and on ImageNet-100 classification by 1.8% (79.9 vs. 78.1), with 30% fewer GFLOPS. Against HSG (Ke et al., 2022) on DensePose hierarchical segmentation, CAST-S surpasses at every granularity level, most notably at the whole-body (1-label) level where CAST achieves 33.2% F-score vs. HSG's 26.2%. Against Swin Transformer (Liu et al., 2021), CAST-S achieves higher IN-100 accuracy (79.9 vs. 78.3) and IN-1K accuracy (68.1 vs. 63.0) with lower computational cost (3.4 vs. 4.5 GFLOPS). The one area where CAST underperforms is boundary delineation quality, where SAM's pixel-level supervision gives it a clear advantage, and on Cityscapes where off-the-shelf superpixels struggle with thin structures like light poles.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAST builds directly on ViT by replacing its fixed-shape patch tokens with superpixel-based adaptive segment tokens and inserting graph pooling modules between encoder blocks to form a fine-to-coarse hierarchy. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm that treats segmentation and recognition as separate tasks requiring separate models and supervision: prior hierarchical segmentation methods (HSG, agglomerative clustering) optimize segmentation as a standalone objective, while SAM produces multi-scale boundaries without enforcing part-to-whole consistency. CAST's key departure is making hierarchical segmentation an internal, unsupervised byproduct of image-level recognition training, drawing on the older idea of concurrent segmentation and recognition (Yu et al., 2002; Maire, 2010) but realizing it entirely within a data-driven transformer architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that hierarchical part-to-whole segmentation can be obtained without any pixel-level or segment-level annotation, simply by training for image recognition—potentially reducing the annotation burden for downstream segmentation tasks. The striking efficiency result (4% of SAM-H's GFLOPS while outperforming it by 8% mIoU on PartImageNet) suggests that hierarchical consistency may matter more than raw boundary quality for recognition-relevant parsing. Key limitations include the dependence on off-the-shelf superpixel algorithms (SEEDS), which fail on thin structures in street scenes (Cityscapes), and the fact that the superpixel step is not differentiable or learnable end-to-end. Open questions include whether learnable superpixels or learned token grouping could eliminate this bottleneck, and whether the approach extends to video, 3D, or open-vocabulary settings.
