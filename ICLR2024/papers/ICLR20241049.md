## Problem

First-order optimizers are computationally efficient but converge slowly on ill-conditioned problems, while full second-order methods are intractable at scale due to Hessian computation. Existing hybrid optimizers approximate the Hessian and then invert it, amplifying approximation errors and gradient noise, and no single optimizer dominates across all problem geometries. FOSI addresses this by providing a meta-algorithm that improves any base first-order optimizer with second-order information while avoiding matrix inversion and requiring no additional hyperparameter tuning.

## Input / Output

FOSI takes as input a base first-order optimizer (e.g., Adam, Heavy-Ball, GD), a twice-differentiable loss function, and an initial parameter vector θ₀. It requires only Hessian-vector products (computed via Pearlmutter's algorithm at roughly 2× gradient cost) rather than the full Hessian, and a small number of extreme eigenvalue/eigenvector estimates (k largest, ℓ smallest, with k+ℓ ≪ n). The output at each iteration is a combined descent direction d₁ + d₂, where d₁ is a scaled Newton step on the extreme-eigenvalue subspace and d₂ is the base optimizer's step projected onto the orthogonal complement, yielding an updated parameter vector θ_{t+1}.

## Method

FOSI uses the Lanczos algorithm (via its Extreme Spectrum Estimation procedure) to obtain the k largest and ℓ smallest eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the Hessian at the current point, then implicitly splits the quadratic approximation of f into two functions on orthogonal subspaces: f₁ on the subspace spanned by the extreme eigenvectors and f₂ on the complement. On f₁, FOSI applies a scaled Newton step using the inverse preconditioner H₁⁻¹ = V̂ diag(1/|λ̂|) V̂ᵀ computed directly from the Lanczos output, avoiding the error amplification of inverting an approximate Hessian. On f₂, the base optimizer is called on the projected gradient g₂, and its descent direction is further projected to remove any component along V̂, ensuring the two steps remain orthogonal. FOSI additionally performs automatic learning-rate scaling for base optimizers with closed-form optimal rates (GD, Heavy-Ball, Nesterov), adjusting η to match the improved condition number of f₂, and provides convergence guarantees in the stochastic setting with N = O(ε^{-1/(1-β)}) iterations.

## Results

FOSI consistently reduces wall time to reach target validation accuracy or loss across all five DNN tasks. FOSI-HB achieves the target in 40.4% of HB's wall time on AudioSet (1850s vs. 3822s), 79.1% on Transfer Learning (53s vs. 93s), and 92.8% on Logistic Regression (8s vs. 16s). FOSI-Adam achieves 28.9% of Adam's wall time on AudioSet (3911s vs. 5042s) and 79.0% on Transfer Learning (33s vs. 68s). On average, FOSI reaches the same training loss as its base optimizer in 78% of the wall time and the same validation accuracy/loss in 75% of the wall time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOSI-HB outperforms Heavy-Ball on all five tasks, with the largest speedup on AudioSet (2.1× faster) and Transfer Learning (1.8× faster). FOSI-Adam outperforms Adam on four of five tasks (AC, LM, AE, TL) but is slightly slower on Logistic Regression (18s vs. 12s), where Adam's adaptive per-parameter scaling is already well-suited to the convex problem. Against second-order baselines, FOSI-HB dominates both K-FAC and L-BFGS across all tasks: K-FAC overfits on AudioSet and Logistic Regression, diverges on the autoencoder task, and cannot be applied to the RNN language model; L-BFGS converges much more slowly and to higher validation loss on every task, and diverges on the autoencoder. The paper also notes that no single base optimizer is best for all tasks (e.g., HB generalizes better than Adam on AudioSet), and FOSI's value lies in accelerating whichever base optimizer is already best for a given problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOSI builds on the Lanczos algorithm for extreme eigenvalue estimation and on stochastic quasi-Newton convergence theory (Wang et al., 2017), but departs fundamentally from prior hybrid optimizers (K-FAC, Shampoo, L-BFGS, K-BFGS) in its conceptual framing. Rather than approximating the Hessian or a block-diagonal preconditioner and then inverting it, FOSI estimates the inverse preconditioner directly from extreme eigenvectors, avoiding error amplification from matrix inversion. Its key architectural departure is the meta-optimizer design: instead of proposing a single new optimizer, it wraps any base first-order optimizer and splits the parameter space into two orthogonal subspaces, applying scaled Newton's method on the extreme-curvature subspace and delegating the remainder to the base optimizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOSI offers a practical, drop-in acceleration for any first-order optimizer with 48–77% wall-time reduction across diverse DNN tasks, and it consistently outperforms established second-order methods (K-FAC, L-BFGS) that require more tuning and are more prone to divergence. The per-subspace condition number analysis (showing improvement even when the global effective condition number worsens) reframes how practitioners should think about preconditioning quality. Open questions include automatic tuning of k and ℓ, the effect of stale spectrum estimates, and whether the approach scales to much larger models (experiments top out at ~4M parameters); the paper also notes that FOSI accelerates overfitting when the base optimizer is prone to it.
