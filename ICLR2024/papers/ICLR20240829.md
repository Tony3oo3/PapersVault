## Problem

Fine-tuning zero-shot CLIP models improves reference-data accuracy but degrades distribution-shift robustness, and existing robust fine-tuning methods (WiSE, TPGM, CAR-FT) do not effectively leverage the language modeling component of vision-language models. The paper further shows that the feature distortion theory fails to explain this trade-off in CLIP fine-tuning, motivating a new theoretical lens based on joint energy-based models.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and produces K-class probability distributions via a classification head initialized from the language model's encoding of class label texts (e.g., 'a photo of a class name'). During fine-tuning, it additionally consumes M randomly generated text tokens (sequence length 8, drawn from the pre-trained language model vocabulary) as guidance signals. Beyond class labels, the method outputs calibrated uncertainty estimates measured by expected calibration error and negative log-likelihood.

## Method

Lipsum-FT augments standard cross-entropy fine-tuning with a regularization term that minimizes the MSE between the fine-tuned and zero-shot models' inner products with M randomly generated text tokens (Eq. 7–8), serving as a stochastic approximation of the full energy gap (Eq. 6). The key insight is that fine-tuning disrupts the pre-trained vision-language connection, and random text guidance covers all potential discriminative models derived from the pre-trained model, not just context-aware ones as in CAR-FT. The MSE loss is preferred over KLD because it preserves the energy scale information encoding the pre-trained vision-language relationship. The approach is composable with post-hoc methods (WiSE, TPGM) and model soup, and requires no architectural changes—only an additional forward pass through the frozen language model with random tokens at each training step.

## Results

On DomainNet (B/16), Lipsum-FT achieves 89.0% reference accuracy and 66.3/68.0/46.0/56.2% on the four distribution shifts (P/C/I/S), outperforming standard fine-tuning (FT) by 3.8–5.6 points on shifts while matching or exceeding FT's reference accuracy. On ImageNet (B/16), Lipsum-FT reaches 83.3% reference accuracy and 73.6/75.9/49.9/51.4% on V2/R/A/S, with the largest gains over FT on ImageNet-R (+7.4 pts) and ImageNet-A (+10.7 pts). Lipsum-FT also achieves the best uncertainty metrics (ECE and NLL) across all benchmarks, e.g., 0.012 ECE on ImageNet-R vs. 0.066 for FT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lipsum-FT outperforms all six fine-tuning baselines (FT, EMA, L2SP, KD, CAR-FT) on every distribution shift benchmark for both DomainNet and ImageNet with B/16. The most dramatic gains are on ImageNet-R (75.9% vs. 68.5% for FT, +7.4 pts) and ImageNet-A (49.9% vs. 39.2% for FT, +10.7 pts), where standard fine-tuning degrades most severely. Among the robust fine-tuning baselines, KD is the strongest competitor (e.g., 72.9% on ImageNet-R), but Lipsum-FT still leads by 3.0 pts there and by 2.5–3.0 pts on other ImageNet shifts. On DomainNet, Lipsum-FT edges out CAR-FT (the closest baseline) by 1.9–3.0 points across shifts while also achieving the highest reference accuracy (89.0%). The zero-shot model retains an advantage on DomainNet-P (66.7 vs. 66.3) and DomainNet-S (56.9 vs. 56.2), but Lipsum-FT surpasses it on all ImageNet shifts, confirming that robust fine-tuning can exceed zero-shot robustness. Post-hoc methods (WiSE, TPGM) can be combined with Lipsum-FT for further gains (e.g., Lipsum-FT + WiSE(λ=0.5) reaches 58.3% on DomainNet-S), demonstrating orthogonality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust fine-tuning literature for vision-language models (WiSE, TPGM, CAR-FT, EMA, L2SP, KD) by introducing a fundamentally different regularization signal: random text tokens drawn from the language model's vocabulary. Conceptually, it departs from the feature distortion theory of Kumar et al. (2022), which the authors show does not explain the reference-vs-shift trade-off for CLIP-ViT, and instead reframes the problem through joint energy-based models (Grathwohl et al., 2019), arguing that fine-tuning disrupts the pre-trained vision-language alignment. Lipsum-FT is the first method to explicitly exploit the language modeling component of CLIP during fine-tuning, whereas prior robust fine-tuning approaches operate purely in weight space or output space without referencing the text encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The energy-gap analysis provides a unifying diagnostic that explains why diverse robust fine-tuning methods (weight-space, output-space, post-hoc) all work: they indirectly reduce the energy gap between fine-tuned and zero-shot models. Practically, Lipsum-FT is simple, composable with post-hoc methods (WiSE, TPGM) and model soups, and improves both accuracy and calibration/uncertainty metrics across three CLIP architectures and two benchmark suites. The main open question the authors flag is whether a purpose-designed text guidance strategy (rather than random tokens) could further improve robustness, and the method's reliance on 80 random tokens per iteration adds modest computational overhead. The finding that feature distortion theory fails for CLIP-ViT may redirect theoretical attention toward multimodal alignment preservation as the correct lens for understanding fine-tuning robustness.
