## Problem

Contrastive learning for time-series relies on augmentations to construct positive pairs, but physiological signals lack the well-established invariances (e.g., rotation, flipping) that make augmentation-based pairing effective in vision. Common time-series augmentations like shuffling and scaling can destroy temporal structure or alter clinical meaning, and the literature shows no consensus on which augmentations are beneficial. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a learned similarity measure that identifies positive pairs from naturally occurring subsequences rather than generating them via augmentation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes long unlabeled time-series (ranging from 5-minute accelerometry recordings to 9.25-hour ECG signals) segmented into shorter subsequences (2.56s–10s depending on modality). The REBAR cross-attention model takes a partially masked query subsequence and a candidate key subsequence, producing a reconstruction error that serves as a class-discriminative distance score. The final output is a frozen encoder embedding evaluated via linear-probe classification (Accuracy, AUROC, AUPRC) and k-means clusterability (ARI, NMI), with the REBAR model itself producing a per-pair similarity score used to label positive and negative examples during contrastive training.

## Method

REBAR is a two-stage self-supervised contrastive learning framework. In stage 1, a cross-attention model with dilated convolutions (replacing vanilla linear projections) is trained to reconstruct masked query time-points by retrieving matching motifs from a key subsequence; critically, the query is excluded from direct reconstruction and influences output only through attention probability weights p(x_k|x_q), forcing the model to learn motif-level similarity rather than trivial interpolation. Extended contiguous masking is used during training to encourage specific motif retrieval, while transient (scattered) masking is used at application time for broader motif coverage. In stage 2, the frozen REBAR model scores 20 randomly sampled candidate subsequences against an anchor, and the lowest-error candidate is labeled positive while others form within-time-series negatives; a combined NT-Xent loss (Eq. 8) with a tunable α balances within-time-series dynamics (class changes over time) against between-time-series differences (inter-patient variation), training a shared encoder to produce class-discriminative embeddings.

## Results

REBAR achieves state-of-the-art linear probe classification across all three datasets. On HAR, it reaches 95.35% accuracy, 99.65% AUROC, and 98.91% AUPRC, matching the fully supervised model's accuracy while exceeding it on AUROC and AUPRC. On PPG (stress detection), REBAR achieves 41.38% accuracy and 69.77% AUROC, outperforming the fully supervised baseline (62.41% AUROC) by 7.4 points. On ECG (atrial fibrillation detection), REBAR reaches 81.54% accuracy, surpassing the fully supervised model (78.14%) by 3.4 points, though with slightly lower AUROC (91.46% vs. 93.29%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REBAR outperforms all four contrastive learning baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against TS2Vec (Yue et al., 2022), the strongest augmentation-based method, REBAR gains +2.1% accuracy on HAR (95.35% vs. 93.24%), +1.1% on PPG (41.38% vs. 40.23%), and +5.4% on ECG (81.54% vs. 76.12%), with the largest AUROC margin on PPG (+5.5 points). Against TNC (Tonekaboni et al., 2021), the sampling-based baseline that selects positives by temporal proximity, REBAR achieves substantially higher accuracy on PPG (41.38% vs. 29.89%) and ECG (81.54% vs. 73.40%), demonstrating that similarity-based positive selection via REBAR is more effective than proximity-based selection. Against CPC (Oord et al., 2018), which contrasts future time-point predictions, REBAR leads by +8.7% accuracy on HAR and +6.9% on PPG. Against SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020) adapted with standard time-series augmentations (shifting, scaling, jittering), REBAR wins on all metrics, with the largest gap on ECG accuracy (+11.6%), suggesting that common augmentations are insufficient for capturing class-discriminative structure in time series. Notably, REBAR also matches or exceeds the fully supervised encoder on HAR and PPG, and surpasses it on ECG accuracy, validating that the retrieval-based positive pair identification captures meaningful semantic invariances without any labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both augmentation-based (SimCLR, TS2Vec, TF-C) and temporal-proximity-based sampling (TNC, CLOCS) approaches to positive pair construction in time-series contrastive learning. Instead of generating positives via data transformations or selecting them by temporal adjacency, REBAR introduces a learned cross-attention reconstruction measure that identifies positives by motif similarity between real subsequences. The cross-attention architecture draws on WaveNet-style dilated convolutions for local motif comparison and on cross-attention mechanisms previously applied in vision-language retrieval, repurposing them for time-series motif matching. The contrastive training itself uses the standard NT-Xent loss, so the novelty is concentrated in the pair-identification mechanism rather than the embedding objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental gap in time-series self-supervised learning: the lack of well-defined invariances that make augmentation-based positive pair construction reliable. By framing the problem as motif-similarity retrieval, REBAR provides a principled, modality-agnostic alternative that works across accelerometry, PPG, and ECG without domain-specific augmentation design. Limitations include the requirement for long time-series with class-labeled subsequences (ruling out short-sequence benchmarks like UCR/UEA), the need to pre-train the REBAR cross-attention before contrastive learning, and the fact that the method's benefit is most pronounced when within-time-series class variation is the primary signal. Open questions include whether the learned measure transfers across modalities without retraining, how it scales to multi-modal sensor fusion, and whether the reconstruction-based distance can be generalized beyond contrastive learning to other self-supervised objectives.
