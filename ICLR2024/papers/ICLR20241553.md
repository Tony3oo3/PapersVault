## Problem

Disentangled representation learning is fundamentally difficult because the underlying factors of variation in real-world data do not naturally exist and cannot be exhaustively enumerated. Prior approaches rely on unsupervised constraints (shown to be unreliable), task-specific synthetic factors, or structured annotations that limit generalizability. This paper asks whether natural language, which humans already use to describe diverse data characteristics, can serve as a task-agnostic supervisory signal for disentanglement.

## Input / Output

The model encodes text inputs (queries up to 256 tokens, documents up to 256 tokens for text-to-text; captions up to 77 tokens for cross-modal) and 224×224 RGB images into sparse |V|-dimensional vectors (|V|=29,522 from the BERT vocabulary), where each dimension corresponds to a specific token and its value encodes semantic relevance. The gating function activates only top-k dimensions (k=768 for text, k=512 for cross-modal) plus bag-of-words tokens, producing a sparse representation. A nonparametric inference mode (VDR^α) replaces the query encoder with a normalized bag-of-words vector, eliminating all neural network forwarding at inference time.

## Method

VDR is a sparse lexical bi-encoder where a base encoder (BERT for text, ViT-B/32 for images) feeds a Disentanglement (DST) head that projects hidden states into vocabulary space via a linear layer, elu1p activation (ensuring positive values), and max pooling. The training loss is a symmetric cross-entropy with two terms: a parametric term between the gated query representation and the target's weighting vector, and a nonparametric term between the query's bag-of-words and the target's weighting vector, which prevents irrelevant dimensions from accumulating excessive values. For cross-modal training from scratch, three key mechanisms address initialization bias: (1) elu1p with top-k sparsification replaces ReLU to ensure deterministic activation counts, (2) the nonparametric BoW entry provides stable supervision independent of the randomly initialized image encoder, and (3) a contrastive mask distributes previously unsupervised dimensions to in-batch negative pairs, ensuring dimension-wise supervision across the entire vocabulary space.

## Results

On the BEIR text-to-text benchmark (12 datasets, NDCG@10), VDR_t2t achieves an average of 44.5, outperforming DPR (35.8) by 8.7 points and SPLADE (42.4) by 2.1 points while using identical model size and training configuration. The nonparametric variant VDR^α_t2t (44.6) matches or exceeds the parametric version across most datasets without any neural network forwarding at inference. In cross-modal retrieval, VDR_cm achieves 42.7 R-mean on MS COCO image-to-text (vs. CLIP's 32.6, a 5.3-point gain) and 63.4 R-mean on Flickr30k image-to-text (vs. CLIP's 50.7, a 6.0-point gain), while also surpassing CLIP-BERT by 5.3 and 6.0 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baselines with identical model size and training cost, VDR_t2t (44.5 avg NDCG@10) substantially outperforms DPR (35.8) by 8.7 points and SPLADE (42.4) by 2.1 points on the BEIR benchmark. Among advanced baselines, VDR_t2t matches UnifieR (44.5) and slightly trails Contriever (44.3) and SimLM (44.4), but is outperformed by LexMAE (48.7) and E5 (46.8), which leverage retrieval-oriented pre-training, knowledge distillation, and Wikipedia data access. In cross-modal retrieval, VDR_cm (42.7 R-mean on MS COCO, 63.4 on Flickr30k) decisively beats CLIP (32.6, 50.7) and CLIP-BERT (37.4, 57.4) by 5–6 points in R-mean, and also surpasses SLIP (42.1, 61.7) and FILIP (35.7, 60.2), though DeCLIP (39.5, 65.3) remains competitive on Flickr30k. The nonparametric VDR^α variant achieves 44.6 average NDCG@10 in text-to-text without any neural network forwarding at inference, demonstrating that the learned target representations are sufficiently disentangled to be matched by simple bag-of-words queries. In cross-modal settings, VDR^α underperforms the parametric version (e.g., 38.6 vs. 49.7 R@10 on MS COCO text-to-image), reflecting that images contain information beyond their caption tokens and thus require the learned expansion mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse lexical retrieval paradigm established by SPLADE (Formal et al., 2021) and SparTerm (Bai et al., 2020), which encode data into weighted vocabulary-space representations via a weighting function and a gating function. The key conceptual departure is reframing this vocabulary-space alignment as a disentanglement mechanism, where each vocabulary token serves as a task-agnostic 'factor of variation' — a significant shift from prior disentanglement work that relied on synthetic factors (shape, color) or required labeled supervision. Architecturally, the paper introduces the Disentanglement (DST) head with elu1p activation, a contrastive mask to supervise inactive dimensions, and a nonparametric bag-of-words loss entry to prevent 'disentanglement laziness,' all specifically designed to enable cross-modal extension where the image encoder must be trained from scratch without MLM initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: the nonparametric inference mode (VDR^α) eliminates all neural network forwarding at query time, yielding over 10× efficiency gains while maintaining competitive retrieval accuracy, making sparse lexical retrieval viable for low-resource and latency-sensitive deployments. The interpretability results (92% human satisfaction vs. 85% for BLIP) suggest that vocabulary-space representations can serve as a lightweight alternative to captioning models for explainability. However, the 'disentanglement' is fundamentally vocabulary alignment rather than true factor independence, and the cross-modal nonparametric mode underperforms, indicating that images contain information beyond what their caption tokens capture. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to modalities without natural language counterparts and whether the vocabulary space is truly sufficient to capture all relevant data variations.
