## Problem

Existing embodied AI simulators treat agents as solitary occupants, assuming environment changes arise solely from the agent's own actions. This stands in stark contrast to assistive robotics, where a robot must share space with a human collaborator who dynamically alters the environment. The paper addresses three intertwined gaps: efficient and realistic simulation of deformable human bodies with diverse appearances and motions, infrastructure for real human-in-the-loop evaluation, and standardized collaborative tasks that require the robot to coordinate with a partner rather than act alone.

## Input / Output

The robot policy consumes a 224×171 arm depth image (hFOV 55°), a 480×640 arm RGB image (hFOV 47°), a 1-dim binary humanoid detector, a 2-dim humanoid relative GPS in polar coordinates, and proprioceptive state (arm joint angles, base egomotion). For social rearrangement, 3D object positions and goals in the robot's start frame are additionally provided. Outputs are continuous velocity commands (linear and angular, scaled to ±2.5 m/s) for the social navigation task, or discrete high-level skill selections (navigate, pick, place, or primitive move/turn actions) for social rearrangement, with the latter further decomposing into arm joint-angle deltas (7-dim, scaled by 5×10⁻²) and a binary gripper snap/desnap signal.

## Method

Habitat 3.0 represents humanoids as an articulated skeleton (for fast collision testing) coupled with a SMPL-X skinned mesh (for visual fidelity via linear blend skinning), caching 12 body shapes offline so that only LBS is computed at runtime, achieving ~1190 FPS with a humanoid and robot across 16 environments. Humanoid motion is generated hierarchically: a path planner produces waypoints, AMASS walking clips animate locomotion, and pre-computed VPoser poses (interpolated at evaluation time) drive reaching/picking motions. The robot uses a two-layer policy trained with DD-PPO: a learned high-level LSTM+ResNet-18 policy selects from a library of low-level skills (oracle or learned navigation, pick, and place), while population-based training (Plan-Pop with 1–4 scripted collaborators, or Learn-Pop with 8 randomly initialized learned collaborators) encourages generalization to unseen partners. A client-server human-in-the-loop tool allows real humans to control the humanoid via mouse/keyboard or VR, enabling interactive evaluation of the learned robot policy against diverse human behaviors.

## Results

In Social Navigation, the end-to-end RL policy achieves 97% finding success and 0.51 collision rate, closely matching the heuristic expert (100% success, 0.52 collision rate) despite lacking a map. In Social Rearrangement, Plan-Pop₃ and Plan-Pop₄ achieve the best zero-shot coordination (ZSC) success rates of 71.79% and 71.32% respectively, substantially outperforming Learn-Single (50.94%) and Learn-Pop (48.52%) when evaluated against unseen collaborators. In the human-in-the-loop study with 30 participants, both robot policies improve human task efficiency over solo performance: Learn-Single achieves 133.8% relative efficiency (936.6 steps vs. 1253.2 solo) and Plan-Pop₃ achieves 123.5% (1015.1 steps), with Plan-Pop₃ offloading more work to the robot (RC=0.44 vs. 0.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six population-based training strategies for the robot's high-level policy in Social Rearrangement. Learn-Single and Plan-Pop₁ achieve the highest train-population success rates (98.5% and 91.2%) but suffer severe generalization drops to ZSC evaluation (50.9% and 50.4%), as they are trained with only a single collaborator type. Plan-Pop₃ and Plan-Pop₄, trained with diverse planner-based populations of size 3–4, show the smallest train-to-ZSC gap and achieve the best ZSC success rates (71.79% and 71.32%), indicating superior adaptability to unseen partners. Learn-Pop, which learns a population of 8 randomly initialized humanoid policies, achieves high train-pop SR (92.2%) but poor ZSC performance (48.5%) due to low emergent behavioral diversity. In the HITL evaluation, Learn-Single outperforms Plan-Pop₃ on relative efficiency (133.8% vs. 123.5%) but Plan-Pop₃ achieves higher task offloading to the robot (RC 0.44 vs. 0.36), and the relative ordering between the two policies is preserved between automated and human evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

Habitat 3.0 extends the Habitat simulation platform (Savva et al., 2019; Szot et al., 2021) by adding realistic deformable humanoid simulation via SMPL-X with a caching strategy for speed, alongside the existing robot simulation. It generalizes the two-robot social rearrangement task from Szot et al. (2023) to heterogeneous human-robot collaboration and introduces a formal social navigation task (find-and-follow) that departs from prior social navigation work focused on avoidance (Li et al., 2021a; Biswas et al., 2021) or tracking without exploration (Luo et al., 2018). The population-based training framework draws on Jaderberg et al. (2019), and the RL policies use DD-PPO (Wijmans et al., 2019) with LSTM+ResNet-18 architectures consistent with prior Habitat navigation work. Unlike iGibson 2.0 (rigid-body humanoids on rails) or SEAN (3 scenes, navigation only), this work combines diverse parametric humanoids, object manipulation, and human-in-the-loop evaluation in a single high-speed simulator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Habitat 3.0 provides the first high-speed simulation platform that unifies realistic humanoid avatars, robot agents, and human-in-the-loop interaction for collaborative home tasks, addressing a critical gap where prior embodied AI treated the environment as static or single-agent. The finding that automated evaluation with learned/scripted humanoids predicts the relative ordering of robot policies when tested with real humans (30-participant study) validates simulation as a scalable proxy for human-robot interaction research, though the ZSC agents still under-capture human reactivity. Key open questions include closing the gap between learned and oracle low-level skills (77.8% → 41.1% SR drop), improving zero-shot coordination to better model human behavioral diversity, and extending the framework to more complex multi-object, multi-room collaborative scenarios.
