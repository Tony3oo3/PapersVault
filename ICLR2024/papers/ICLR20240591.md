## Problem

Decentralized multi-agent RL algorithms struggle to achieve reciprocity-based cooperation in general-sum games without being exploited. Existing opponent-shaping methods (LOLA, POLA) require differentiating through the opponent's optimization steps, which is sequentially expensive and memory-intensive, while meta-learning approaches (M-FOS) scale with the full policy parameterization. LOQA addresses this by eliminating both computational bottlenecks while still producing cooperative, non-exploitable policies.

## Input / Output

The agent receives the current environment state observation and, in the Coin Game, both agents' actions from the previous turn, processed through a GRU-based policy network. The output is a discrete action (cooperate/defect in IPD, or a grid-world move in the Coin Game) along with updated policy and Q-function parameters. Unusually, the actor loss also consumes an estimate of the opponent's action-value function, which the agent maintains via its own critic network, making the method fully decentralized without requiring access to the opponent's internals.

## Method

LOQA's key insight is that the opponent's Q-function is an expectation over trajectories whose probability measure is controlled by the agent's policy, making it differentiable with respect to agent parameters via a REINFORCE estimator. Assuming the opponent samples actions proportionally to its Q-function (analogous to SAC), LOQA approximates the opponent's policy and adds a second log-term to the standard actor-critic gradient: the log of this approximated opponent policy, which pushes the opponent's returns in a direction beneficial to the agent. This shaping term is computed using DiCE/Loaded-DiCE for variance reduction and requires no differentiation through opponent optimization steps or meta-learning over policy space. Training employs self-play with a replay buffer of previously encountered agents to increase opponent diversity, and the overall computational cost is equivalent to a standard REINFORCE estimator.

## Results

On the 3×3 Coin Game, LOQA achieves an average reward of 0.3 against itself (close to the 0.35 of an always-cooperate agent) and −0.05 against an always-defect opponent, while requiring only 2 hours of training on an A100 GPU compared to 8 hours for POLA. In scalability experiments on larger grids, all LOQA runs meet the strong performance threshold (normalized return ≥ 0.2 vs. each other and ≥ −0.2 vs. always-defect) up to 6×6 grid size, whereas every POLA and M-FOS run fails the strong threshold above 3×3. LOQA also reaches each threshold at least one order of magnitude faster in wall-clock time than both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOQA is compared against POLA (Zhao et al., 2022) and M-FOS (Lu et al., 2022), the only prior methods known to generate reciprocity-based cooperative policies in the Coin Game. On the 3×3 Coin Game, LOQA matches POLA closely on exploitability (−0.05 vs. −0.03 against always-defect) while achieving comparable self-cooperation (0.3 vs. the 0.35 always-cooperate baseline), and does so in roughly half the training time (2 h vs. 8 h on A100). On scalability, LOQA decisively outperforms both baselines: it passes the strong threshold on grids up to 6×6, while POLA and M-FOS fail it on any grid larger than 3×3. LOQA also reaches all thresholds (weak, medium, strong) at least one order of magnitude faster in wall-clock time, attributed to its lower per-step computational cost (no differentiation through opponent optimization steps and no second-order gradients). M-FOS shows similar failure patterns to POLA on larger grids, confirming that the meta-learning approach does not scale with environment complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOQA sits at the intersection of two lines of work on opponent shaping in multi-agent RL: the differentiable-optimization-step family (LOLA, POLA, COLA, SOS) and the meta-learning family (M-FOS, Meta-Value Learning). It departs from both by replacing the need to differentiate through the opponent's parameter updates or to learn a meta-policy over the joint policy space with a simpler assumption—that the opponent's policy is proportional to its action-value function (analogous to SAC). This allows LOQA to compute a shaping gradient via a single REINFORCE/DiCE estimator over the opponent's Q-function, avoiding sequential computation graphs and second-order gradients entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the dramatic reduction in compute: LOQA trains in ~2 hours on an A100 versus ~8 hours for POLA on the Coin Game, and scales to 6×6 grids where POLA and M-FOS fail to reach strong performance thresholds. The key limitation is the Q-proportional policy assumption, which restricts LOQA to shaping opponents that behave like value-based learners and to discrete action spaces; extending it to continuous actions or arbitrary opponent classes remains open. For practitioners building decentralized MARL systems with limited compute budgets, LOQA offers a drop-in alternative to POLA with a much lighter per-step cost.
