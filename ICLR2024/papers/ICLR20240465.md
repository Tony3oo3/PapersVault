## Problem

Weighted-averaging of model parameters is widely used for merging fine-tuned models, but the reasons it works and when it fails remain poorly understood. Existing schemes (arithmetic mean, task arithmetic, Fisher-weighted averaging) make implicit assumptions that are not explicitly justified, and there is no principled way to understand their inaccuracies or to design better merging methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240465/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the pretrained model parameters θ_LLM, T fine-tuned model parameters θ_t, and diagonal Hessian/Fisher estimates computed via a single squared-gradient pass over each model's training data (truncated to at most 100,000 examples). A small regularization constant δ=1e-10 is added to the Fisher matrices for numerical stability. The output is a single merged parameter vector θ̂_{1:T} that can be directly used for inference without any additional training or hyperparameter tuning of scaling factors.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form expression connecting the error Δ between a target model and a parameter-averaged merge to weighted gradient mismatches between individual models and the target. By applying a first-order Taylor approximation to reduce this mismatch, the authors obtain a new merging scheme that adds Hessian-based preconditioners H̄⁻¹H_{0+t} to task arithmetic, where H̄ accumulates all Hessians and H_{0+t} combines the pretrained model's Hessian with each task's Hessian. This scheme is shown to be the MAP estimate under a Laplace approximation of the Bayesian posterior over all datasets, and it recovers existing methods (AM, TA, Fisher Averaging, TIES) as special cases by setting H_0 or H_t to zero or binary masks. For data removal, the same principle yields an update that exactly recovers the classical influence function for linear models, providing a unified second-order framework for both task addition and task subtraction.

## Results

On sentiment classification with RoBERTa (adding 4 tasks to an IMDB-trained model), the proposed method achieves 94.5% average accuracy and 96.6% true average accuracy, approaching the 95.0%/96.8% all-data target. Without any hyperparameter tuning (α_t=1), it improves over Task Arithmetic by 2.7 points in average accuracy (94.5% vs. 91.8%). For data removal, the method reduces hallucination critic scores by up to 3.0 points (8.8 vs. 11.8 on FlanT5-780M) and toxicity scores by 0.2–0.5 points over Task Arithmetic on GPT-2 and GPT-J.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) across all settings: with tuned α_t it gains 0.1 points in average accuracy (94.5 vs. 94.4) and 0.3 in true average (96.6 vs. 96.3), while without tuning (α_t=1) the gap widens dramatically to 2.7 points (94.5 vs. 91.8). Against Fisher Averaging (Matena & Raffel, 2022), the method improves by 0.2 points in average accuracy (94.5 vs. 94.3) and 0.1 in true average. It ties RegMean (Jin et al., 2023) on average accuracy (94.5) but leads by 0.2 in true average (96.6 vs. 96.4), and beats TIES-Merging (Yadav et al., 2023) by 0.3 points in average accuracy (94.5 vs. 94.2). The method also surpasses simple Averaging (Wortsman et al., 2022) by 0.4 points in average accuracy. A key practical advantage is robustness: the method requires no tuning of scaling factors, whereas Task Arithmetic's tuned α_t still cannot match the untuned proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) and Fisher-weighted averaging (Matena & Raffel, 2022), deriving its new merging scheme by adding Hessian-based preconditioners to task arithmetic. The key conceptual departure is the gradient-mismatch framework, which unifies understanding of existing methods (AM, TA, FA, TIES, Sparse Finetuning) as special cases of a single equation (Eq. 12) with different choices of α_t, H_0, and H_t, thereby revealing their implicit assumptions. The paper also connects the merging problem to Bayesian posterior merging (Laplace approximation) and shows that its data-removal update recovers the classical influence function for linear models, linking model merging to the broader literature on data attribution and continual learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-mismatch framework provides practitioners with a principled diagnostic for why a particular merging scheme fails and how to fix it, rather than relying on ad-hoc hyperparameter tuning. The method's robustness to scaling factors (no need to tune α_t) is practically valuable as the number of tasks grows, and the Bayesian connection opens clear paths for improvement (iterative MAP optimization, variational inference). Limitations include reliance on diagonal Fisher approximations, a single non-iterative step, and the fact that the target model is unavailable in practice; open questions include whether iterative refinement and better curvature estimates yield further gains, and how the method behaves when fine-tuning procedures deviate from the assumed quadratic regularizer.
