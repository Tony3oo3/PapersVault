## Problem

Phylogenetic inference from sequence data is NP-hard due to the superexponential tree space ((2n-5)!! topologies for n species), and existing methods face a fundamental trade-off: MCMC struggles with multimodal posteriors at scale, variational inference methods either restrict the tree space (VBPI-GNN) or underperform on marginal likelihood (VaiPhy, GeoPhy), and parsimony tools like PAUP* rely on heuristics without theoretical guarantees. No prior method can simultaneously explore the full tree topology space and achieve high-fidelity modeling of the posterior distribution, including suboptimal trees.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-aligned DNA sequences (n sequences of m sites over {A,C,G,T}, with wild characters handled as a special symbol in parsimony or as a uniform vector in Bayesian mode) and, for parsimony, a temperature T that controls the smoothness of the target Boltzmann distribution. It produces weighted phylogenetic trees (unrooted topology plus branch lengths) sampled from the posterior in Bayesian mode, or tree topologies sampled from a parsimony-score-based energy distribution in parsimony mode. Branch lengths are modeled as discrete multinomial distributions over fixed bins (or as Gaussian mixtures in the continuous variant), and the model also yields importance-weighted marginal log-likelihood estimates.

## Method

PhyloGFN formulates tree construction as a bottom-up acyclic MDP where each state is a set of rooted trees and each action merges a pair of trees by introducing a new parent node and sampling branch lengths for the two new edges. The key representation insight is using Felsenstein features (Bayesian) or Fitch features (parsimony) computed via dynamic programming as tree-level state representations, which provably contain sufficient information for the optimal policy without introducing additional learnable parameters. A Transformer encoder processes the set of tree features in an order-equivariant manner, and MLP heads generate pairwise logits for tree-pair selection and branch-length sampling. Training uses the trajectory balance objective with a combination of ε-greedy on-policy rollouts, a replay buffer of high-reward trees, and cascading temperature annealing to balance exploration and exploitation. For parsimony, a temperature-conditioned variant conditions the policy, reward, and partition function on T, enabling a single trained model to sample from a continuum of Boltzmann distributions over tree topologies.

## Results

On 8 real benchmark datasets (27–64 sequences, 378–2520 sites), PhyloGFN achieves marginal log-likelihood (MLL) estimates competitive with the MCMC gold standard MrBayes SS (e.g., −7108.95 vs. −7108.42 on DS1; −33735.6 vs. −33735.44 on DS3) while operating over the full tree topology space. PhyloGFN outperforms φ-CSMC on all 8 datasets (e.g., −7108.95 vs. −7290.36 on DS1) and GeoPhy on 6 of 8. In terms of posterior fit quality (Pearson correlation between sampling log-density and true posterior log-density), PhyloGFN maintains correlations above 0.90 for high-, medium-, and low-probability trees across most datasets, whereas VBPI-GNN drops to 0.46–0.77 on medium/low-probability trees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MrBayes SS (MCMC gold standard), PhyloGFN achieves MLL within ~0.5 nats on most datasets (e.g., −7108.95 vs. −7108.42 on DS1), though it lags slightly on DS7 (−37359.96 vs. −37332.76). Against VBPI-GNN, PhyloGFN achieves comparable MLL but operates over the full tree space without requiring pre-generated topologies; critically, PhyloGFN maintains Pearson correlations >0.90 on medium- and low-probability trees while VBPI-GNN drops to 0.46–0.77, demonstrating superior posterior coverage. Against φ-CSMC (VaiPhy), PhyloGFN clearly wins on all 8 datasets with margins of 150–4200 nats (e.g., −7108.95 vs. −7290.36 on DS1). Against GeoPhy, PhyloGFN outperforms on 6 of 8 datasets, with GeoPhy winning only on DS7. For parsimony analysis, PhyloGFN identifies the identical set of most-parsimonious trees as PAUP* across all datasets (ranging from 1 optimal tree on DS1 to 21 on DS8), but unlike PAUP*'s heuristic branch-swapping, PhyloGFN provides a rigorous probabilistic framework with a smooth echelon of sampling probabilities distinguishing optimal from suboptimal trees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first adaptation of generative flow networks (GFlowNets) to phylogenetic inference, bridging the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021; Malkin et al., 2022) with classical phylogenetic algorithms. It departs from prior variational inference methods (VBPI-GNN, VaiPhy, GeoPhy) by constructing trees bottom-up over the full topology space rather than top-down over a constrained subset, and from MCMC methods (MrBayes) by learning an amortized sampler rather than running a chain. A key conceptual contribution is using Felsenstein and Fitch algorithm features as the GFlowNet state representation, with theoretical proofs (Propositions 1–2) showing these features are sufficient for the optimal policy without introducing additional learnable parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Phylogenetic inference is NP-hard with a superexponential tree space, and the paper demonstrates that GFlowNets can match MCMC-level marginal log-likelihood while operating on the full topology space and, critically, modeling suboptimal trees far more faithfully than state-of-the-art VI methods (Pearson correlation remains high across probability regimes where VBPI-GNN degrades sharply). This matters for downstream tasks like branch support estimation and confidence intervals on divergence times, where suboptimal trees carry non-negligible posterior mass. Open questions include training efficiency (3–8 days per dataset), whether the approach scales beyond ~64 sequences, and whether conditional GFlowNets can amortize inference across unseen sequence datasets as the authors propose.
