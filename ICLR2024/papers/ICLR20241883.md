## Problem

Offline RL must stitch suboptimal trajectory fragments from a static dataset while avoiding extrapolation errors from querying the Q-function on out-of-support actions. Prior diffusion-based approaches either diffused over raw state-action space (constraining architectures to simple U-Nets) or used VAEs with Gaussian priors that fail to capture the multi-modal latent distributions that emerge under temporal abstraction, leading to out-of-support sampling and degraded Q-learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes H-length state-action trajectory snippets from a static offline dataset (H=10–30 depending on task), with the diffusion prior conditioned on the current state s₀ (and optionally a goal state s_g for the LDGC variant). For image-based tasks like CARLA, 48×48 RGB frames are first compressed to a 32-dim vector via a separate β-VAE encoder. The output is an H-length action sequence produced by an autoregressive decoder conditioned on the selected latent skill, executed sequentially before re-planning.

## Method

LDCQ operates in two stages: (1) a β-VAE (β=0.05) with a bidirectional GRU encoder and autoregressive MLP decoder learns a 16-dim latent representation of H-step trajectories, where the low β keeps the latent space information-dense and multi-modal; (2) a ResNet-based conditional latent diffusion model (8 residual blocks, T=200–500 steps) is trained to model p(z|s₀) over the VAE latents using classifier-free guidance and Min-SNR-γ weighting. Q-learning then operates in latent space: a Q-function Q(s,z) is trained with clipped double Q-learning and prioritized experience replay, where the target argmax is constrained to latents sampled from the diffusion prior (ensuring in-support candidates). At test time, n=500 latents are sampled via DDPM, scored by Q, and the argmax latent is decoded into an H-step action sequence. The key insight is that increasing temporal abstraction H induces clear multi-modal structure in the latent space that Gaussian VAE priors cannot sample from faithfully, while diffusion models capture all modes, enabling reliable batch-constrained Q-learning and faster credit assignment in long-horizon sparse-reward tasks.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark suite, LDCQ and its goal-conditioned variant LDGC achieve state-of-the-art performance across all sparse-reward tasks. In maze2d-large-v1, LDGC scores 206.8 vs. Diffuser's 123.0 and IQL's 58.6; in antmaze-large-diverse-v2, LDGC reaches 73.6 vs. IQL's 47.5 and Diffuser's 22.0. On the Adroit suite, LDCQ achieves 74.1 on pen-human (vs. IQL 71.5) and 121.2 on pen-expert (vs. BCQ 114.9). On locomotion tasks, LDCQ is competitive but not consistently best (e.g., 90.2 on halfcheetah-medium-expert vs. CQL's 91.6), while on the image-based CARLA driving task it scores 24.7 vs. CQL's 20.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDCQ/LDGC decisively outperforms Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) on all maze2d and antmaze tasks where both are evaluated — e.g., 206.8 vs. 123.0 on maze2d-large and 73.6 vs. 22.0 on antmaze-large — by operating in a temporally abstract latent space rather than raw state-action sequences. Against Decision Diffuser (Ajay et al.), LDCQ wins on kitchen-partial (67.8 vs. 57.0) and antmaze-large (57.7 vs. 7.5), though DD edges ahead on kitchen-mixed (65.0 vs. 62.3). Compared to IQL, LDCQ/LDGC achieves substantially higher scores on sparse-reward navigation (e.g., 73.6 vs. 47.5 on antmaze-large) but trails on locomotion (e.g., 90.2 vs. 86.7 on halfcheetah-medium-expert is a win, but 69.4 vs. 78.3 on walker2d-medium is a loss). Against CQL, LDCQ dominates on antmaze and kitchen tasks but is slightly behind on hopper-medium-replay (86.2 vs. 95.0) and walker2d-medium-replay (68.5 vs. 77.2). Among latent-skill baselines (Table 5), LDCQ outperforms OPAL on antmaze-medium (68.9 vs. 57.5) and antmaze-large (57.7 vs. 52.0), beats PLAS on nearly all locomotion and adroit tasks, and surpasses Flow2Control on antmaze-large (57.7 vs. 52.8) though Flow2Control leads on kitchen-partial (74.9 vs. 67.8) and door-human (15.1 vs. 11.8). BCQ is consistently outperformed across all task families, confirming that the combination of latent diffusion and temporal abstraction provides a stronger batch-constraining mechanism than the original VAE-based BCQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Latent Diffusion Model architecture from image generation (Rombach et al., 2022) to offline RL by learning a β-VAE latent trajectory representation and diffusing in that space rather than over raw state-action sequences. It departs from prior diffusion-based offline RL methods (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser) that diffused directly over trajectories and were architecturally constrained to U-Nets, by separating the diffusion prior from the policy decoder, enabling a powerful autoregressive decoder for low-level actions. The Q-learning component is a direct adaptation of Batch-Constrained Q-Learning (BCQ, Fujimoto et al., 2019) to the latent space, while the temporal abstraction and latent skill learning framework builds on OPAL (Ajay et al., 2020) and Pertsch et al. (2021), replacing their constrained Gaussian priors with a diffusion model that can capture the multi-modal latent distributions that emerge at longer horizons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that BCQ, previously considered a weaker offline RL approach, becomes highly competitive when paired with an expressive generative model and temporal abstraction, reframing the bottleneck as the quality of the behavior model rather than the Q-learning algorithm itself. The empirical finding that increasing the sequence horizon reveals distinct behavioral modes in the latent space provides a concrete justification for temporal abstraction in offline RL and explains why prior VAE-based skill methods underperformed. Limitations include average performance on locomotion tasks (where high-frequency periodicity doesn't benefit from abstraction), the need for task-specific horizon tuning, and the two-stage training pipeline that adds complexity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to online RL, whether the VAE and diffusion stages can be jointly trained, and how the method scales to higher-dimensional or partially observable settings.
