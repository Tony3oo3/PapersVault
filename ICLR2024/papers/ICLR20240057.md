## Problem

Existing Rayleigh-Ritz projection methods for updating truncated SVDs densify the augmented matrix during orthogonalization and must project onto all singular vectors, making them impractical for sparse, frequently evolving matrices. No prior approach exploits the sparsity structure of the update in the orthogonalization step while avoiding full-matrix operations, leaving a significant efficiency gap for real-time representation learning on large-scale sparse data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240057/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an existing rank-k truncated SVD (U_k, Σ_k, V_k) of a sparse matrix A together with a sparse update matrix — either new columns E_c ∈ R^{m×s}, new rows E_r ∈ R^{s×n}, or a low-rank weight modification D_m E_m^T. It outputs an updated approximate rank-k SVD stored in an extended decomposition form U = U'·U'' and V = V'·V'', where U', V' are sparse m×k / n×k matrices and U'', V'' are dense k×k matrices. Querying a single row of U_k or V_k costs O(k²) rather than O(k), a deliberate trade-off that enables the update to run in O(nnz(E)(k+s)² + (k+s)³) time with no dependence on m or n.

## Method

The core mechanism is the SV-LCOV representation, which expresses each column of the augmented matrix (I − U_k U_k^T)B as a tuple (b, c)_{U_k} where b is a sparse m-vector and c = U_k^T b is a k-dimensional vector. Lemma 3 proves the column space of the augmented matrix is isometric to the inner product space of these tuples, so Modified Gram-Schmidt, GKL bidiagonalization, or Randomized Power Iteration can be executed entirely through sparse vector additions and k-dimensional dot products, eliminating the densification that plagues prior methods. A second key contribution is an extended decomposition U = U'·U'' (and analogously for V) that decouples the singular-vector update into a low-dimensional k×k matrix multiplication on U'' plus a sparse matrix addition on U', avoiding the O(m) or O(n) cost of projecting the full singular vector matrix. Theorem 1 establishes that the overall update runs in O(nnz(E)(k+s)² + (k+s)³) time, matching the output of Zha & Simon (1999) exactly while removing all terms linear in m or n. Two variants (ours-GKL, ours-RPI) approximate the augmented subspace in SV-LCOV space to handle coarse-grained updates with large s, reducing the s² dependence.

## Results

On the collaborative filtering task (MovieLens25M, k=16, batch update), the proposed method achieves MSE 0.8616 in 23s, matching Zha & Simon's MSE (0.8616) while reducing runtime from 192s to 23s (~8.3× speedup). For streaming updates (k=16), the method completes in 35s versus 626s (Zha & Simon), 2529s (Vecharynski & Saad), and 352s (Yamazaki et al.), a 10–72× speedup, while maintaining identical MSE. On link prediction (Slashdot, Flickr, Epinions), the proposed method achieves AP of 93.41%, 95.16%, and 95.62% respectively, matching or within 0.15% of all baselines, with Frobenius norms identical to Zha & Simon (e.g., 792.11 on Slashdot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three prior truncated-SVD update methods: Zha & Simon (1999), Vecharynski & Saad (2014), and Yamazaki et al. (2015). On accuracy, the proposed method is essentially tied with all baselines: its Frobenius norm and AP on link prediction are identical to Zha & Simon (e.g., 792.11 norm, 93.41% AP on Slashdot), and its MSE on collaborative filtering matches to 4 decimal places. The decisive advantage is runtime: the proposed method is 8.3× faster than Zha & Simon (23s vs 192s, k=16 batch), 14× faster than Vecharynski & Saad (23s vs 323s), and 12× faster than Yamazaki et al. (23s vs 278s). For streaming updates the gap widens dramatically—35s vs 2529s (72×) over Vecharynski & Saad. The GKL and RPI variants match the accuracy of their respective original counterparts (Vecharynski & Saad and Yamazaki et al.) while being faster, confirming that the SV-LCOV reformulation preserves theoretical equivalence while eliminating O(m) and O(n) terms from the complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Rayleigh-Ritz projection framework for updating truncated SVDs established by Zha & Simon (1999), and the subsequent approximations of the augmented matrix via GKL bidiagonalization (Vecharynski & Saad, 2014) and Randomized Power Iteration (Yamazaki et al., 2015). The key departure is the introduction of the SV-LCOV representation, which recasts the orthogonalization of the augmented matrix as an operation in an isometric inner product space of (sparse vector, low-dimensional vector) tuples, thereby preserving sparsity throughout. Additionally, the extended decomposition (storing singular vectors as a product U'·U'' and V'·V'') draws a conceptual parallel to Brand (2006) but is adapted to the truncated, sparse-update setting to eliminate O(m) and O(n) terms from the update step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-of-magnitude speedup (10×+) while maintaining comparable accuracy makes real-time SVD-based representation learning feasible for large-scale evolving graphs and streaming recommender systems, where prior methods were impractical for fine-grained updates. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that the method produces the same output as Zha & Simon (1999) at lower complexity is a clean and useful result. Open questions include the numerical stability of the extended decomposition under high condition numbers (acknowledged by the authors), whether the approach extends to other matrix decompositions (e.g., PCA, NMF), and how it behaves under adversarial or non-sparse update patterns.
