## Problem

Existing decomposing Bayesian Optimization algorithms (e.g., ADD-GPUCB, DEC-HBO) require the additive decomposition of the objective function to have a low Maximum Factor Size (MFS), which forces coarse approximations of complex functions and limits applicability. Additionally, decentralized BO algorithms suffer from over-exploration because their additive approximations of the GP-UCB exploration term are loose upper bounds. This paper asks whether the low-MFS constraint can be completely removed while preserving both no-regret guarantees and exact global maximization of the acquisition function.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a black-box objective function f: D ⊂ R^d → R along with its additive decomposition into n factor functions (or infers one via MCMC when unknown), represented as a factor graph with n factor nodes and d variable nodes. At each step t, it produces a single query point x^t ∈ D by decentralizedly maximizing the acquisition function via ADMM on the factor graph, and observes a noisy scalar output y = f(x^t) + ε. The key constraint is that the decomposition may have arbitrary MFS (no upper bound on factor dimensionality), and the algorithm operates fully decentralizedly with only local message-passing between adjacent factor and variable nodes.

## Method

DuMBO proposes a tighter additive approximation of the GP-UCB exploration term σ_t(x) that accounts for the factor graph topology: each factor i's contribution is weighted by 1/|N_i|² where N_i is the set of factors sharing at least one variable with i, yielding a bound provably tighter than the simple sum used by ADD-GPUCB (Theorem 3.5). The acquisition function φ_t(x) = μ_t(x) + β_t^{1/2} Σ_i √(Σ_{k∈N_i} (σ_t^{(k)})²/|N_k|²) is then maximized via ADMM on the factor graph, where factor nodes perform local gradient ascent on their augmented Lagrangian subproblem and variable nodes compute consensus averages and update dual variables. The paper proves that under mild smoothness assumptions on the kernel (L-Lipschitz, bounded Hessian), each factor's acquisition function is restricted-prox-regular and the augmented Lagrangian satisfies the Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz condition, guaranteeing that ADMM converges to the global maximum of φ_t. This yields an immediate regret bound strictly lower than DEC-HBO's, establishing asymptotic optimality (no-regret) without any MFS restriction.

## Results

On the 24-dimensional Powell function (MFS=4), DuMBO achieves a minimal regret of 496, outperforming DEC-HBO (7,937) and ADD-GPUCB (11,760) by over an order of magnitude, and beating the best non-decomposing method TuRBO (667). On the 100-dimensional Rastrigin function (MFS=5), DuMBO reaches 986 minimal regret while ADD-GPUCB and DEC-HBO fail to complete within 24 hours. On the real-world WLAN problem (12d, MFS=6), DuMBO achieves the best average negative reward of -120.67 (ADD-DuMBO: -121.11), surpassing all baselines including SAASBO (-116.40) and TuRBO (-118.39). On the 60-dimensional Rover trajectory planning problem, DuMBO attains the lowest regret (6.38) among all tested algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuMBO decisively outperforms both decomposing baselines (ADD-GPUCB and DEC-HBO) on all problems where the true MFS exceeds their assumed bound (MFS≤1 for ADD-GPUCB, MFS≤3 for DEC-HBO): on Powell (MFS=4), DuMBO's regret of 496 is 16× lower than DEC-HBO's 7,937 and 24× lower than ADD-GPUCB's 11,760. On the 100d Rastrigin function (MFS=5), both ADD-GPUCB and DEC-HBO fail to complete within 24 hours, while DuMBO achieves 986 regret. Among non-decomposing baselines, DuMBO beats SAASBO, LineBO, and MS-UCB on all synthetic functions and on WLAN and Rover; TuRBO is competitive on Cosmo (5.82 vs. 5.86) and Rover (7.01 vs. 6.38) but is outperformed by DuMBO on all other problems. The only case where a baseline matches or slightly beats DuMBO is the 2d Six-Hump Camel (MFS=2), where DEC-HBO (0.005) edges out DuMBO (0.006) because the problem satisfies DEC-HBO's sparse factor graph assumption. When given access to the true additive decomposition (ADD-DuMBO), performance improves further, e.g., 678 vs. 986 on Rastrigin and -121.11 vs. -120.67 on WLAN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of decomposing Bayesian optimization algorithms (ADD-GPUCB, QFF, DEC-HBO) that assume an additive structure for the objective function to handle high-dimensional spaces. The key departure is from DEC-HBO (Hoang et al., 2018), which tolerates larger Maximum Factor Sizes but still requires them to be low and uses a max-sum algorithm whose convergence depends on sparsity. DuMBO replaces this with ADMM on an augmented Lagrangian, leveraging restricted prox-regularity and Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz theory to guarantee global maximization of the acquisition function for *any* MFS. It also proposes a tighter decentralized approximation of GP-UCB's exploration term (Theorem 3.5) that exploits the factor graph structure, reducing over-exploration compared to the sum-of-std-devs bound used by ADD-GPUCB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first BO algorithm to completely remove the low-MFS assumption while retaining provable no-regret guarantees and global acquisition-function maximization, broadening the applicability domain of decomposing BO to arbitrarily complex additive structures. The practical impact is demonstrated on real-world problems (WLAN Shannon capacity, rover trajectory planning) where the natural additive decomposition has large MFS, and where prior methods either fail or produce coarse approximations. Limitations include the requirement that f be (or be approximable as) additive, the reliance on ADMM convergence for non-convex acquisition functions (backed by KL theory but not a finite-time guarantee), and the absence of batch-mode support. Future work targets batch optimization and deployment in distributed systems (networks, UAV swarms, robot teams).
