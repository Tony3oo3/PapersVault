## Problem

Large vision-language foundation models like CLIP are trained on fixed, static datasets, yet must operate in a world where data distributions shift continuously. No large-scale benchmark existed to study how to continually train CLIP models on naturally evolving web-scale data, and it was unclear whether legacy models (e.g., OpenAI's CLIP trained on data up to 2020) degrade on newer data or whether retraining from scratch is truly necessary. The paper demonstrates that OpenAI's CLIP loses ~8% zero-shot retrieval accuracy on 2021–2022 data compared to more recently trained OpenCLIP models, while standard static benchmarks (ImageNet shifts) mask this temporal degradation.

## Input / Output

The training input is a stream of timestamped image-text pairs arriving in yearly batches (7 steps for T1C-DataComp spanning 2016–2022, 4 steps for T1C-YFCC and T1C-RedCaps), with the model required to produce a deployable checkpoint after each batch under a fixed per-step compute budget (measured in MACs). Evaluation is zero-shot: static tasks (28 datasets including ImageNet and its 6 distribution shifts, Flickr30k retrieval, VTAB) report average accuracy across all T model checkpoints, while dynamic tasks (T1C-DataComp-Retrieval and T1C-DataComp-Net) produce a T×T performance matrix capturing in-domain, backward transfer, and forward transfer across time steps. The ViT-B/16 image encoder and text encoder are the standard CLIP architecture; no resolution or prompt-template constraints beyond the original CLIP recipe are imposed.

## Method

The paper introduces three Time-Continual (T1C) benchmarks—T1C-DataComp (12.7B pairs from CommonPool with crawl-time metadata), T1C-YFCC (15M pairs from Flickr), and T1C-RedCaps (12M pairs from Reddit)—and studies how to continually train CLIP under a fixed per-step compute budget C. The key finding is that a simple rehearsal-based method, Cumulative-All (warm-start from the last checkpoint and replay all previously seen data alongside new data for C iterations), matches the Oracle (retrain from scratch on all data with tC compute) within ~1% on dynamic tasks while using 2.5–4× less total compute. Ablations show that reducing the replay buffer to 2D (Cumulative-Exp or Cumulative-Equal) retains most of the benefit, that skipping learning-rate warmup on subsequent runs improves performance by 1.5–4.3%, and that keeping the same maximum LR across all runs outperforms decaying it. The paper also compares against Sequential (no replay), Patching (weight interpolation), LwF (KL-divergence distillation), and Restart (random init each step), finding that replay of old data is the dominant factor for maintaining static-task performance over time.

## Results

On T1C-DataComp (XL, Bestpool), Cumulative-All achieves 71.6% ImageNet accuracy and 64.8% average over 28 static datasets, within 1.7% of the Oracle (73.3% / 65.8%) while using 4× less compute. On dynamic retrieval, Cumulative-All reaches 70.7% backward transfer and 57.1% forward transfer, matching the Oracle's forward transfer (57.1%) and trailing by only 0.3% on backward transfer. With a modest compute increase (Cumulative-All*), the static-task gap narrows to 0.5% (72.8% vs 73.3% ImageNet) while retaining 2.7× compute savings. On smaller benchmarks (T1C-YFCC, T1C-RedCaps), Cumulative-All matches or slightly exceeds the Oracle on all dynamic metrics (e.g., 66.4% vs 66.1% backward transfer on YFCC) at 2.5× lower cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Patching (Ilharco et al., 2022), Cumulative-All wins decisively on all metrics: on T1C-DataComp (L), it gains +3.1% ImageNet (48.9 vs 45.8), +6.9% backward transfer (62.1 vs 55.2), and +0.3% forward transfer (41.2 vs 40.9). Patching does improve over Sequential on backward transfer (+2.6%) by interpolating weights, but this benefit does not transfer to static tasks where Patching (45.8%) still trails Cumulative-All by 3.1%. Against LwF (Li & Hoiem, 2017), Cumulative-All dominates on every metric while also being cheaper: on T1C-DataComp (M), Cumulative-All achieves 24.0% ImageNet vs LwF's 19.2% (+4.8%) and 33.8% backward transfer vs 25.6% (+8.2%), yet LwF costs 3.8×10¹⁸ MACs versus Cumulative-All's 3.0×10¹⁸. Even Cumulative-All* (3.9×10¹⁸ MACs), which uses only marginally more compute than LwF, outperforms it by 10.8% on ImageNet and 11.1% on backward transfer. The paper's central finding is that simple full-data replay with warm-start initialization surpasses both weight-interpolation and knowledge-distillation approaches at equal or lower compute cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual learning literature from small-scale, artificially segmented benchmarks (Split-CIFAR, Perm-MNIST) to the setting of web-scale vision-language model pretraining on naturally time-evolving data. It builds directly on the CLIP training recipe (Radford et al., 2021) and the DataComp/LAION-5B dataset infrastructure, augmenting them with temporal metadata from Common Crawl, YFCC, and Reddit. Unlike prior CLIP continual learning work (Ding et al., 2022; Ilharco et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2023b) that targets fine-tuning on disjoint downstream tasks, this paper addresses continual *pretraining* where the data distribution shifts organically over years, with no explicit task boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of T1C-DataComp (12.7B timestamped pairs spanning 9 years) fills a critical gap: no prior benchmark supported studying how foundation models degrade as the world changes. The finding that OpenAI's CLIP loses ~8% retrieval accuracy on 2021-2022 data while appearing competitive on ImageNet shifts the evaluation paradigm for vision-language models. The practical result that simple rehearsal (Cumulative-All) matches Oracle with 2.5–4× less compute provides an immediately actionable strategy for organizations that cannot afford to retrain from scratch. Open questions include whether these findings hold at finer (monthly) granularity, whether better learning rate schedules (e.g., Const-Cosine) can close the remaining gap, and how to extend the framework beyond CLIP to other foundation model architectures.
