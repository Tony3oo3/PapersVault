## Problem

Existing pseudo-label selection strategies in semi-supervised learning rely on confidence-based thresholding or hand-crafted policies that are task-specific (designed for classification but inapplicable to regression), modality-specific (requiring ad-hoc augmentations), and prone to confirmation bias. No prior method simultaneously achieves high-quality label selection, fast convergence, and versatility across both classification and regression tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241008/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The rewarder R takes two inputs: (1) the last-layer feature z^u ∈ R^D of unlabeled data x^u extracted by a pre-trained backbone f (e.g., ViT, BERT, or HuBERT), and (2) the pseudo label y^u ∈ R^C produced by the teacher model. For regression, scalar labels are converted to a soft one-hot vector by dividing the range into C bins. The output is a scalar reward score r ∈ [0,1] via Sigmoid, which is compared against a dynamic threshold τ (set to the batch-average reward score) to produce a binary selection mask for the student's unsupervised loss. The entire rewarder adds only 0.14M parameters and 0.198 MFLOPs (1.28% and 0.056% of the student model).

## Method

SemiReward introduces a reward score defined as scaled cosine similarity S(y^u, y^l) = (y^u·y^l)/(2‖y^u‖‖y^l‖) + 0.5, which is smooth, monotonic, and well-calibrated for both classification and regression. A lightweight rewarder R(x^u, y^u) = Sigmoid(MLP(CA(Emb(f(x^u)), Emb(y^u)))) predicts this score by modeling the semantic correlation between data features and pseudo labels through a cross-attention module (the critical component) followed by a 2-layer MLP. To prevent confirmation bias, training is decoupled from the student via a generator G that produces 'fake labels' used exclusively to train R. A two-stage pipeline is employed: Stage 1 pre-trains R and G alternatively on labeled data D_L for the first 10% of iterations (using MSE loss with stop-gradient between R and G), then Stage 2 continues training on a randomly subsampled set D_R ⊂ D_L ∪ D̂_U (λ=0.1) to adapt to the evolving pseudo-label distribution while maintaining high sampling rates (90–95%) throughout training.

## Results

On 13 SSL benchmarks across CV, NLP, and Audio modalities, SemiReward achieves average performance gains of +0.30 to +4.19% in top-1 error rate reduction when plugged into Pseudo Label, FlexMatch, or SoftMatch/FreeMatch, with training speedups of ×1.01 to ×3.53. On ImageNet (100 labels/class), FlexMatch+SR reaches 40.36% top-1 error, surpassing FreeMatch (40.57%) and SoftMatch (40.52%). For regression with 1% labels, PseudoLabel+SR achieves RMSE 61.71 / MAE 22.45 on RCF-MNIST, beating even the fully supervised baseline (62.02 / 22.81), while CRMatch degrades severely to RMSE 101.66.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemiReward consistently improves Pseudo Label across all 17 classification settings (e.g., ESC-50/250: 38.42→33.33%, STL-10/40: 20.53→17.37%), demonstrating its ability to unlock potential in basic self-training. Against FlexMatch, gains are more modest but still positive in most cases (e.g., CIFAR-100/200: 25.72→23.74%, ESC-50/250: 36.83→32.58%), with one exception on STL-10/100 where FlexMatch+SR (7.59%) slightly underperforms the baseline (7.13%). On SoftMatch/FreeMatch, SemiReward delivers the largest absolute gains in audio (ESC-50/250: 32.71→29.71%) and NLP (Yahoo! Answer/2000: 30.34→29.11%), and on ImageNet, FlexMatch+SR (40.36%) surpasses both FreeMatch (40.57%) and SoftMatch (40.52%). For regression, PseudoLabel+SR dramatically outperforms CRMatch (RMSE 61.71 vs. 101.66 on RCF-MNIST) and marginally beats Π-Model and Mean Teacher, while also exceeding the supervised baseline on RCF-MNIST. On ImageNet at 1% labels, CoMatch+SR (67.4%) edges out SimMatch (67.2%), the prior SOTA, and FixMatch+SR improves over FixMatch by 1.6% (55.1% vs. 53.5% accuracy).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-labeling paradigm in semi-supervised learning by replacing hand-crafted confidence-based thresholding (FixMatch, FlexMatch, FreeMatch, SoftMatch) with a learned reward model that scores pseudo-label quality via cosine similarity to ground-truth labels. Conceptually, it borrows the reward-modeling idea from RLHF (InstructGPT, SURF) and repurposes it for SSL pseudo-label selection, while the two-stage decoupled training with a generator network draws from debiased self-training (Chen et al., 2022a) to mitigate confirmation bias. The key departure from prior work is task and modality generality: unlike confidence thresholds that are inherently classification-specific and require modality-aware augmentations, the reward score operates on vectorized labels and works uniformly for both classification and regression across CV, NLP, and audio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SemiReward provides a lightweight, pluggable module (1.28% extra parameters) that consistently improves convergence speed (up to 3.5×) and accuracy across 13 datasets and three modalities, making it practically attractive for SSL practitioners who want a drop-in improvement without redesigning their pipeline. The extension to regression tasks is notable since prior consistency-regularization methods (CRMatch) fail catastrophically there, and SemiReward even surpasses the supervised baseline on RCF-MNIST. Open questions include whether the rewarder can be pre-trained as a general-purpose component transferable across tasks, whether it extends to token-level labels (object detection, segmentation), and whether the multi-forward-pass requirement for reliable pseudo-label selection can be reduced. The authors also suggest potential applications to RLHF and LLM instruction alignment, though this remains speculative.
