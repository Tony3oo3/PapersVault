## Problem

Current generative models (GANs, VAEs, diffusion models) are largely inscrutable: they provide no mechanism to identify which human-understandable features drive their outputs, debug whether a concept has been learned, or steer generation along specific concept dimensions. Existing interpretability approaches—probing classifiers, disentanglement, and latent-space search—cannot guarantee that model representations actually encode the desired interpretable features, and standard conditional generation baselines degrade sharply as the number of concepts grows.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a noise vector (GANs/VAEs) or a noisy image (diffusion) as its generative input, plus optional concept probability overrides for test-time steering. Training data must be annotated with pre-defined binary (or continuous) concept labels. The output is a generated image (up to 1024×1024) accompanied by a vector of per-concept probability scores that indicate which known concepts are active and how strongly. The CB bottleneck has dimension m(k+1), where k is the number of pre-defined concepts and m is the per-concept embedding size, and the framework scales to 155 concepts on LAION Aesthetics.

## Method

The paper inserts a model-agnostic concept bottleneck (CB) layer into the decoder path of GANs, VAEs, and diffusion models, partitioning each architecture into a pre-CB encoder, the CB layer, and a post-CB generator. The CB layer contains k concept sub-networks (each producing positive and negative context embeddings via a context network φ and a binary probability via Ψ) plus one additional unknown-concept sub-network; the final bottleneck embedding is a concatenation of weighted concept mixtures and the unknown embedding. Training jointly minimizes three losses: the standard task loss (GAN/VAE/diffusion objective), a binary cross-entropy concept loss on each concept probability, and a cosine-similarity orthogonality loss that forces the unknown-concept embedding to be orthogonal to all known concept embeddings. Steering is performed at test time by replacing a concept's predicted probability with a user-specified value, which re-weights the positive/negative context embedding before it is passed to the post-CB generator. Ablations show the concept loss is the most critical component (~15% steerability drop without it), while removing the unknown-concept embedding degrades both steerability (9.1%) and image quality (FID jumps from ~9 to 44).

## Results

The primary metric is steerability accuracy (fraction of generated images where a target concept is present after intervention). In the small balanced concepts regime, CB-GAN achieves 21.3% on CUB (10 concepts) and 25.6% on Celeb-A (8 concepts), outperforming the best baselines (ACGAN at 18.5% and 9.2% respectively). In the large unbalanced regime (40 Celeb-A concepts), CB-GAN maintains 23.1% while ACGAN collapses to 1.2% and CGAN to 2.9%, representing up to ~19× improvement. For diffusion models, CB-Diffusion reaches 14.8% on CUB versus 2.7% (CF-Diffusion) and 2.1% (CG-Diffusion), a ~5–7× gain. FID scores confirm that adding the CB layer does not degrade generation quality (e.g., CB-StyleGAN2: 9.1 vs StyleGAN2: 9.0 on Celeb-A).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CB-GAN dominates all GAN baselines across every dataset and regime. On Celeb-A with 40 concepts, CB-GAN (23.1%) outperforms ACGAN (1.2%) by ~19× and CGAN (2.9%) by ~8×; the gap widens dramatically as concept count increases, with ACGAN degrading from 9.2% to 1.2% while CB-GAN drops only 2.5 points. In the diffusion setting, CB-Diffusion (14.8% on CUB) surpasses both classifier-free guidance (2.7%) and classifier guidance (2.1%) by roughly 5–7×, and maintains a 2.4–2.6× advantage in the 40-concept regime. CB-VAE (10.7% on CUB, 16.3% on Celeb-A-8) outperforms CVAE (1.2%, 5.9%) by 8–9× in the small-concept regime. InfoGAN, included in the appendix per-concept tables, is also consistently outperformed by CB-GAN (e.g., 53.7% vs 6.6% on the 'Male' concept). The key differentiator is robustness to concept count: traditional conditioning methods degrade sharply as concepts increase, while CBGMs remain nearly stable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Concept Bottleneck Model (Koh et al., 2020) and its high-dimensional extension, the Concept Embedding Model (Espinosa Zarlenga et al., 2022), from supervised classification to generative modeling. The key conceptual departure is the addition of an 'unknown concept' embedding with an orthogonality constraint (Ranasinghe et al., 2021), motivated by the observation that pre-defined concept sets are inherently incomplete in the generative setting, unlike in classification where they are assumed near-complete. The CB layer is demonstrated as a model-agnostic plug-in across GANs, VAEs, and diffusion models, going beyond GlanceNets (Marconato et al., 2022) which applied a similar idea only to VAEs and enforced full disentanglement among concepts rather than only between known and unknown groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CB layer provides a single, unified mechanism for steering, interpreting, and debugging generative models—capabilities that prior approaches (probing, GANSpace, conditional generation) address in isolation and without guaranteed alignment to human-understandable features. The finding that conditioning baselines degrade dramatically as the number of concepts grows (e.g., ACGAN drops from 9.2% to 1.2%) while CBGMs remain stable suggests a practical path toward scalable controllability. Key limitations include the requirement for concept annotations on training data (though ~20% suffices), testing restricted to image tasks, and residual vulnerability to concept leakage. Open questions include extending the framework to text and other modalities, addressing leakage more fundamentally, and integrating CB layers into large-scale foundation models like Stable Diffusion.
