## Problem

Existing preference-based RL (PbRL) query selection schemes are designed to maximize the overall quality of the reward model (e.g., via ensemble disagreement or mutual information), yet they often only marginally outperform random query selection. The paper identifies the root cause as query-policy misalignment: the selected 'informative' queries frequently fall outside the current policy's visitation distribution, meaning they are irrelevant to the agent's current learning and waste scarce human feedback.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of trajectory segments (sequences of state-action tuples, typically length 50) drawn from a small policy-aligned buffer of the most recent N=10 trajectories, along with binary human preference labels indicating which segment is preferred. It produces a learned reward function parameterized by a neural network (trained via Bradley-Terry cross-entropy loss with temporal data augmentation generating τ=20 shorter snippet pairs per queried segment), a trained SAC policy, and Q-function estimates updated via hybrid experience replay. The key constraint is that query selection is restricted to the policy-aligned buffer rather than the full replay buffer, and the reward model is a single network (no ensemble), reducing computational cost compared to disagreement-based baselines.

## Method

QPA (Query-Policy Alignment) enforces bidirectional alignment between queries and the policy through two complementary mechanisms. First, policy-aligned query selection restricts segment pair selection to a small first-in-first-out buffer (D^pa) containing only the most recent N trajectories, ensuring queried segments lie within the current policy's visitation distribution d^π rather than being 'informative' for the global reward model. Second, hybrid experience replay samples half of the Bellman-updating transitions from the full replay buffer D and half from D^pa, ensuring the Q-function is accurately approximated in the region where preferences were recently labeled. A temporal data augmentation step further expands the preference dataset by subsampling multiple shorter snippet pairs from each queried segment. The entire method requires only ~20 lines of code modification on top of the B-Pref/PEBBLE framework, and an error bound (Appendix A) shows that the value approximation error decomposes into a reward error term ε/(1−γ) and a Q-estimation error α, both measured under d^π, justifying the focus on on-policy accuracy.

## Results

On DMControl locomotion tasks, QPA achieves episode returns of 802.65 (Walker_walk), 467.63 (Walker_run), 652.56 (Cheetah_run), 669.75 (Quadruped_walk), 495.71 (Quadruped_run), and 467.09 (Humanoid_stand), consistently outperforming PEBBLE and SURF by substantial margins. On MetaWorld manipulation tasks, QPA reaches success rates of 80.2% (Door-unlock), 65.0% (Drawer-open), and 84.4% (Door-open), again surpassing both baselines. Notably, in some complex tasks (e.g., Quadruped_walk, Quadruped_run), QPA can even surpass SAC with ground truth reward during early training stages when given sufficient feedback throughout training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QPA consistently outperforms PEBBLE (Lee et al., ICML 2021) across all 9 tasks, with particularly large gaps on Cheetah_run (652.56 vs. 521.44) and Quadruped_walk (669.75 vs. 491.04). Against SURF (Park et al., ICLR 2022), which employs pseudo-labeling and semi-supervised learning, QPA achieves superior results on every task despite using a simpler single-reward-model approach with only 20 lines of code modification. Compared to SAC with ground truth reward (the reference upper bound), QPA closes the gap substantially on most tasks (e.g., 802.65 vs. 949.21 on Walker_walk) and in some complex tasks (Quadruped_walk, Quadruped_run) can even surpass SAC during early training when given continuous feedback. Against MRN (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2022), QPA also consistently outperforms in most locomotion tasks (Appendix D.2). The paper also demonstrates that QPA's gains hold under varying feedback amounts (60–3000 queries) and feedback frequencies, confirming robustness beyond the standard evaluation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PEBBLE/B-Pref off-policy PbRL framework (Lee et al., 2021a,b) and positions its contribution as a minimal modification to existing query selection schemes. It departs conceptually from the dominant paradigm in PbRL query design—selecting segments that maximize information gain for the global reward model (ensemble disagreement, mutual information, entropy)—by arguing that the relevant objective should instead be local reward accuracy within the current policy's visitation distribution. This connects to the broader 'local decision-aware model learning' literature in model-based RL, where learning dynamics only near the current policy outperforms global model fitting, but applies that principle to the reward-learning side of PbRL for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of query-policy misalignment provides a unifying explanation for a long-standing empirical puzzle: why carefully designed active query schemes in PbRL often barely outperform random selection. The proposed fix (policy-aligned buffer + hybrid replay) is remarkably lightweight—20 lines of code—making it immediately adoptable in any off-policy PbRL pipeline, including those with LLM-based reward models where ensemble disagreement is prohibitively expensive. Open questions include whether the insight transfers to on-policy PbRL (where the issue is structurally absent but sample efficiency suffers), whether it generalizes to multi-agent or non-stationary settings, and the intriguing observation that learned rewards can encode long-horizon structure that per-step ground-truth rewards lack, potentially outperforming them in early training.
