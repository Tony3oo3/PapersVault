## Problem

Offline RL algorithms require reward-annotated datasets, but hand-crafting reward functions is often difficult and labor-intensive. Prior offline imitation learning methods that learn rewards from expert demonstrations either rely on distribution-matching schemes (DICE family) that suffer from training instability and over-conservatism, or solve complex optimal transport problems (e.g., OTR). The paper asks whether meaningful reward signals can be obtained without training any neural network or solving non-trivial optimization problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single expert demonstration trajectory (containing either state-action pairs or pure observations) and an unlabeled offline dataset of transitions collected by an unknown behavior policy. For each unlabeled transition (s, a, s'), it outputs a scalar reward computed from the Euclidean distance to the nearest expert neighbor, producing a fully labeled dataset ready for any offline RL algorithm. Notably, the method operates per-transition rather than per-trajectory, so unlabeled trajectories can be fragmented or of varying lengths, and the same procedure applies whether the expert data includes actions or only observations.

## Method

SEABO builds a KD-tree over the expert demonstration transitions, then for each unlabeled query sample (s, a, s'), searches the tree to find its nearest neighbor in the expert data and computes the Euclidean distance d between them. The reward is obtained via a squashing function r = α·exp(−β·d/|A|), where |A| is the action-space dimension to normalize across tasks, and α, β are simple hyperparameters controlling reward scale and distance sensitivity. The key insight is that proximity to the expert trajectory serves as a direct proxy for transition quality, eliminating the need for discriminators, reward models, or optimal transport. The resulting labeled dataset can be plugged into any off-the-shelf offline RL algorithm (e.g., IQL, TD3_BC) without modification, and the entire annotation step runs in O(d_f·log|D_e|) time using only CPUs.

## Results

On 9 D4RL MuJoCo locomotion tasks (medium, medium-replay, medium-expert), IQL+SEABO achieves a total normalized score of 713.3, surpassing IQL with ground-truth rewards (692.4) and IQL+OTR (675.8). SEABO beats ground-truth IQL on 6 out of 9 datasets and TD3_BC on 5 out of 9. On AntMaze and Adroit, IQL+SEABO achieves total scores of 400.8 and 153.6 respectively, outperforming IQL (378.0, 116.4) and IQL+OTR (384.0, 122.2) by 6.0% and 32.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IQL+SEABO (total 713.3) outperforms IQL with ground-truth rewards (692.4) on 6/9 tasks, most notably on hopper-medium (80.9 vs 66.2) and hopper-medium-expert (97.5 vs 91.5). Against the strongest reward-learning baseline IQL+OTR (675.8), SEABO wins on 7/9 tasks with a 5.4% total-score margin. IQL+ORIL (588.5) and IQL+UDS (495.1) are substantially outperformed, with SEABO exceeding them by 21.1% and 44.1% respectively. Among offline IL methods (Table 4), SEABO beats DemoDICE (585.3), SMODICE (450.6), PWIL-action (626.1), and SQIL (372.2) in total score, with the largest gap over PWIL on hopper-medium (80.9 vs 60.4). In the state-only regime (Table 5), SEABO (707.6) outperforms LobsDICE (531.8) and OTR (685.6) on 8/9 tasks, while PWIL-state collapses to a total of 2.7. On Adroit, SEABO achieves a 32% improvement over IQL with raw rewards (153.6 vs 116.4), the largest relative gain across all domains tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEABO carves new ground by repurposing the classical KD-tree nearest-neighbor search (Bentley, 1975) as a reward annotation mechanism for offline imitation learning, departing from the dominant paradigms of distribution matching (DICE family), adversarial reward learning (ORIL), and optimal transport (OTR). While OTR (Luo et al., 2023) similarly decouples reward annotation from policy optimization, it requires solving complex optimal transport problems; SEABO replaces this with a simple distance-to-nearest-neighbor computation that needs no trained neural network. The paper also positions itself against ILR (Ciosek, 2022), a nearest-neighbor-based reward for online IL, by extending the idea to the offline setting, supporting state-only expert demonstrations, and making the search algorithm a first-class design choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEABO offers a practically attractive alternative to complex reward learning pipelines: it requires no trained discriminator or reward model, runs in ~1 minute on CPU for 1M transitions, and works with a single expert trajectory. However, it remains sensitive to the weighting coefficient β on some tasks, fails on high-precision manipulation benchmarks (e.g., IKEA furniture assembly), and the HNSW ablation reveals that the choice of search algorithm is non-trivial. Open questions include whether SEABO extends to visual offline RL (requiring learned image encoders) and cross-domain offline imitation, both flagged as future work by the authors.
