## Problem

Cross-subject emotion recognition from EEG is hindered by extreme individual differences that make direct feature-distribution alignment infeasible, and existing multi-modal approaches either ignore spatio-temporal interactivity between modalities or neglect their heterogeneity. No prior work has combined multi-modal physiological signals with domain adaptation by aligning the distribution of spatio-temporal *relationships* between features rather than the raw signal features themselves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242044/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-modal physiological signals (EEG 32ch, EOG 2ch, EMG 2ch, GSR 1ch on DEAP; EEG 14ch, ECG 2ch on DREAMER) cropped into 4-second non-overlapping segments, with a leave-one-subject-out split providing labeled source data, a small labeled target subset, and unlabeled target data for evaluation. Output is a binary label for each of two dimensions—valence (high/low) and arousal (high/low)—produced by a shared classifier over the RDA-weighted node embeddings.

## Method

VBH-GNN first maps each modality through modality-specific networks (Wav-to-Node) into node embeddings, then a Node-to-Edge convolution generates edge embeddings representing pairwise spatio-temporal relationships. The core Relationship Distribution Adaptation (RDA) operates in two stages: Bayesian Graph Inference (BGI) models the heterogeneous graph edge distribution of the target domain as a posterior of the source domain's prior, using the De Moivre-Laplace theorem to approximate an infinite-parameter Binomial with a tractable Gaussian and minimizing a closed-form KLD upper bound; Emotional Graph Transform (EGT) then conditions a second Gaussian on the BGI sample to produce emotion-specific graph edges that further separate the two affective categories. A Graph Attention module multiplies the EGT-derived adjacency with node embeddings to produce domain-invariant, emotion-discriminative representations fed to the classifier. The total loss combines BGI KLD, EGT KLD, and binary cross-entropy for both domains, with all weights set to 1.

## Results

VBH-GNN achieves the best cross-subject emotion recognition accuracy on both DEAP and DREAMER datasets. On DEAP, it reaches 73.5% accuracy (arousal) and 71.21% accuracy (valence), with F1 scores of 71.53% and 71.85% respectively. On DREAMER, it achieves 70.64% accuracy (arousal) and 73.38% accuracy (valence), with F1 scores of 69.66% and 69.08%. These results outperform the strongest baselines (MMDA-VAE at 70.39% DEAP arousal accuracy and SST-AGCN-DA at 68.25%) by 2–5 percentage points across all four evaluation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VBH-GNN outperforms all 15 baselines on every metric across both datasets. Among DA methods, it surpasses the strongest competitor MMDA-VAE by 3.11 points on DEAP arousal accuracy (73.5 vs. 70.39) and by 2.06 points on DREAMER valence accuracy (73.38 vs. 71.32). Among non-DA multi-modal methods, it exceeds HetEmotionNet (the best non-DA baseline at 69.16% DEAP arousal) by 4.34 points and SST-AGCN-DA (best DA method with spatio-temporal inference at 68.25% DEAP arousal) by 5.25 points. The gap is most pronounced on DEAP valence, where VBH-GNN (71.21%) leads the next-best MMDA-VAE (70.16%) by over 1 point and SST-AGCN-DA (66.94%) by 4.27 points. On DREAMER, the advantage over SST-AGCN-DA is largest for arousal (70.64% vs. 67.84%, +2.8 points), while for valence the margin over MMDA-VAE is 2.06 points (73.38% vs. 71.32%). Single-modality or non-relationship-aware methods (DGCNN, EEGNet, ACRNN) trail by 5–14 points, confirming that both multi-modal spatio-temporal relationship modeling and domain alignment are necessary for competitive cross-subject performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on HetEmotionNet (Jia et al., 2021) for its modality-specific feature extraction (Wav-to-Node) but departs fundamentally from existing domain adaptation methods (MEKT, JTSR, MSADA, MMDA-VAE, SST-AGCN-DA) that align signal feature distributions between domains. Instead, VBH-GNN aligns the distribution of spatio-temporal relationships between modalities, framed as a Variational Bayesian inference over heterogeneous graph edge distributions. The paper positions itself as the first to combine multi-modal physiological signals with DA for cross-subject ER, addressing the gap where prior multi-modal methods ignore inter-modality spatio-temporal relationships during fusion and prior DA methods ignore such relationships during domain alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual shift from aligning signal features to aligning relationship distributions offers a more principled approach to cross-subject ER given the extreme individual differences in EEG, and the interpretability analysis showing inferred brain connectivity patterns consistent with established neuroscience (frontal lobe activation under positive emotion, central sulcus under negative) lends biological plausibility to the learned representations. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets with a leave-one-subject-out paradigm, and the performance gains over the strongest baselines (SST-AGCN-DA, MMDA-VAE) are moderate (~3-5% accuracy). Open questions include whether the Bayesian graph inference framework scales to larger subject pools, whether it generalizes to continuous emotion estimation or multi-class settings, and whether the relationship-alignment principle transfers to other cross-subject physiological signal tasks beyond emotion.
