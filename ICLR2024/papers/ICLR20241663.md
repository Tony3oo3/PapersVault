## Problem

Existing part-body association methods either rely on multi-stage pipelines (detect parts and bodies separately, then associate) or extend anchor-based detectors with per-part center offsets from body to part, which scales poorly as the number of parts grows and becomes invalid when parts are occluded. There is no one-stage, anchor-free formulation that handles arbitrary numbers of parts-to-body associations with a compact, always-valid representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241663/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (1024×1024 for BodyHands/COCOHumanParts, 1536×1536 for CrowdHuman) and produces, per detected object, a bounding box, a class label (body or one of K part classes), and a 2D part-to-body center offset. Unlike BPJDet's body-to-part representation of length 6+K+2K, PBADet's part-to-body representation has length 5+K+2, since the single 2D offset is shared across all parts and does not require per-part offset slots in the body representation.

## Method

PBADet extends a one-stage anchor-free detector (YOLOv7 or YOLOv5l6 with anchor-free heads) by adding a third output branch that regresses a 2D vector (m_i, n_i) encoding the offset from each part's anchor point to its parent body's center, scaled by a factor λ. The key insight is that in anchor-free detection, every point inside a part's bounding box is also inside the body's bounding box, so the part-to-body center offset is always well-defined and provides a one-to-one ground-truth correspondence, unlike body-to-part offsets which can be one-to-many or invalid under occlusion. Training uses TOOD-style task-alignment learning (t = s^α · u^β) for anchor assignment across all three branches, with a dedicated L1 association loss on the top-K aligned anchors. The anchor-free design is essential because part-body center offsets can fall far outside any predefined anchor box, making anchor-based learning of the association intractable. At inference, NMS filters overlapping predictions, each part's predicted body center is computed from the offset, and the nearest unassigned enclosing body is assigned via Euclidean distance.

## Results

On BodyHands, PBADet (YOLOv7) achieves Hand AP of 89.1, Conditional Accuracy of 92.62%, and Joint AP of 85.98, surpassing the strongest baseline BPJDet (YOLOv5l6) by +3.2 AP, +5.8 Cond. Acc., and +1.6 Joint AP. On COCOHumanParts (6 body parts), PBADet (YOLOv5l6) reaches AP_M of 66.2 and AP_L of 67.2, exceeding BPJDet (YOLOv5l6) by +2.4 and +5.4 respectively, while maintaining comparable overall AP_5:.95 (43.0 vs. 43.6). On CrowdHuman, PBADet (YOLOv7) attains the best body MR⁻² of 36.5 and body AP of 91.6, with a competitive mMR⁻² of 50.8 for face-body association.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PBADet outperforms all 22 baselines on the BodyHands dataset, with the most notable margin over BPJDet (YOLOv5l6): +3.2 Hand AP, +5.7 Cond. Accuracy, and +1.6 Joint AP, while using a smaller input resolution (1024 vs. 1536) and fewer parameters (36.9M vs. 86.1M for the YOLOv7 variant). On COCOHumanParts, PBADet (YOLOv5l6) slightly trails BPJDet (YOLOv5l6) on overall AP_5:.95 (43.0 vs. 43.6) but decisively wins on association-sensitive metrics AP_M (+2.4) and AP_L (+5.4), confirming the advantage of the part-to-body formulation for multi-part scenarios. For the mMR⁻² association metric on COCOHumanParts, PBADet (YOLOv7) achieves the lowest values across all six parts (e.g., 29.2 for head vs. 33.4 for BPJDet-YOLOv5s6; 41.1 for left hand vs. 50.2), demonstrating superior part-body matching. On CrowdHuman, PBADet (YOLOv7) achieves the best body MR⁻² (36.5) and body AP (91.6) but is marginally behind BPJDet (YOLOv5l6) on mMR⁻² (50.8 vs. 50.1), which the authors attribute to the single-part nature of the face-body task where PBADet's multi-part scalability advantage is less pronounced. In the comparison with ED-Pose (multi-person pose estimation), PBADet substantially outperforms on all four limb parts in F1 score (e.g., 46.50 vs. 22.87 for left hand; 51.08 vs. 40.46 for left foot), validating that explicit part bounding boxes with association are more reliable than keypoint-based grouping for part-body correspondence.

## Relation to Prior Work

PBADet builds directly on the anchor-free detection paradigm established by FCOS and refined by TOOD's task-alignment learning, repurposing the per-pixel 4D bounding-box regression to also encode a 2D part-to-body center offset. It departs conceptually from BPJDet (Zhou et al., 2023b), which extends the body representation with K separate offsets to each part (body-to-part), by inverting the relationship: each part carries a single offset to its parent body (part-to-body), reducing the representation length from 6+K+2K to 5+K+2. This inversion also shifts the method from anchor-based YOLO variants to anchor-free heads, which the authors argue is necessary because part-body offsets can fall far outside predefined anchor regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The part-to-body formulation offers a clean scalability argument: adding more body parts does not increase the per-object representation, unlike BPJDet's per-part offsets. This makes the approach a practical drop-in for real-time systems (medical scan rooms, gesture-based HCI) where multiple parts must be associated with the correct person. Limitations include qualitative-only validation on animal datasets, and the method's advantage over BPJDet is less pronounced in single-part scenarios (e.g., face-body on CrowdHuman). Open questions include robustness under heavy occlusion where the body center may be poorly localized, and whether the one-to-one part-to-body assumption holds in degenerate cases (e.g., two overlapping bodies).
