## Problem

Existing object-centric learning methods rely on auto-encoding objectives with architectural or algorithmic biases to implicitly encourage object-level disentanglement, but this misalignment makes them highly sensitive to hyperparameters (number of slots, encoder/decoder architectures) and often produces suboptimal decompositions by position or partial attributes rather than whole objects. No prior method directly optimizes the compositionality of the learned representations in the objective function itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241196/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two unlabeled RGB images (128×128) simultaneously, which are first encoded by a pretrained KL-8 autoencoder into 16×16×4 latent features. It produces N slot representations (each 192-dimensional) expected to capture individual objects, slot attention masks for unsupervised segmentation, and composite images generated by mixing slots across the two input images. The diffusion decoder operates in the 16×16 latent space, while a one-shot surrogate Transformer decoder produces the composite image in the same latent space for differentiable gradient computation.

## Method

The core contribution is a composition path that explicitly regularizes slot representations for compositionality. Slots are extracted independently from two images via Slot Attention, then mixed (via random sampling or shared slot initialization to avoid invalid combinations) into a composite slot set. A one-shot bidirectional Transformer surrogate decoder renders the composite slots into an image, whose likelihood under a diffusion-based generative prior (reusing the auto-encoding diffusion decoder) is maximized via a DreamFusion-style gradient approximation that avoids expensive Jacobian computation. The encoder is jointly optimized by both the auto-encoding path (diffusion denoising loss + surrogate reconstruction loss) and the composition path (prior likelihood + attention-mask regularization), with the decoder frozen during composition-path updates to prevent encoder-decoder collusion. This directly injects compositionality into the objective rather than relying solely on architectural inductive biases.

## Results

On unsupervised object segmentation, the method achieves FG-ARI of 93.06 on CLEVRTex, 90.65 on MultiShapeNet, 89.8 on PTR, and 63.08 on Super-CLEVR, improving FG-ARI by 8–29% over baselines in most datasets. On mIoU, the gains are especially large where backgrounds are monolithic: 40.89 on MultiShapeNet (vs. 14.1 for SLATE+), 59.21 on PTR (vs. 15.55 for SLATE+), and 47.17 on Super-CLEVR (vs. 29.12 for SLATE+). The method also demonstrates robustness to architectural choices, maintaining strong performance across varying numbers of slots (11–17), encoder architectures (CNN vs. UNet), and decoder capacities (100%–200%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two strong baselines: SLATE+ (Singh et al., 2022a, autoregressive Transformer decoder with VQGAN features) and LSD (Jiang et al., 2023, diffusion-based slot decoder). The proposed method outperforms both baselines on mIoU and mBO across all four datasets, with the largest margins on MultiShapeNet (40.89 vs. 14.10 mIoU over SLATE+) and PTR (59.21 vs. 15.55 mIoU over SLATE+), where baselines struggle to separate objects from monolithic backgrounds. On FG-ARI, the method leads on CLEVRTex (93.06 vs. 76.44 LSD), PTR (89.8 vs. 70.44 SLATE+), and Super-CLEVR (63.08 vs. 54.79 LSD), but is marginally behind SLATE+ on MultiShapeNet (90.65 vs. 91.25). The consistent improvement over LSD (which is the method's own auto-encoding path without the composition objective) isolates the contribution of the compositional regularization. Qualitatively, baselines frequently split objects into multiple slots or merge objects with background, while the proposed method produces tight, object-level masks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Slot Attention + auto-encoding paradigm (Locatello et al., 2020; Jiang et al., 2023) by adding a composition path that directly optimizes for compositional validity of slot representations, rather than relying solely on architectural biases (competitive attention, decoder design) to induce object-level disentanglement. The gradient approximation for the prior loss is borrowed from DreamFusion (Poole et al., 2022), but repurposed: instead of distilling a 2D image prior into a 3D model, the diffusion decoder is reused as a generative prior to score composite slot renderings. The method is positioned as a drop-in regularization on top of existing object-centric frameworks (LSD, SLATE), with the key conceptual departure being that compositionality is enforced in the objective function itself rather than through inductive bias in the architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the demonstrated robustness: the method is largely insensitive to the number of slots, encoder architecture, and decoder capacity—three factors that have made object-centric learning notoriously fragile and hard to tune. This suggests that the field's heavy reliance on architectural bias may be partially compensating for a missing signal in the objective. Limitations include the compute cost of the diffusion decoder and the need for a surrogate one-shot decoder for backpropagation, as well as evaluation primarily on synthetic benchmarks (CLEVRTex, MultiShapeNet, PTR, Super-CLEVR) with only a single real-world dataset (BDD100k). Open questions include whether the composition path scales to very large scenes with many objects, whether the surrogate decoder can be eliminated, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D object-centric learning.
