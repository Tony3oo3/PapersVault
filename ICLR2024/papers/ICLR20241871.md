## Problem

Directly processing neural fields for 3D tasks like classification and segmentation has been limited to either shared-network frameworks (which sacrifice reconstruction quality) or large single-MLP representations (which suffer from high-dimensional weight spaces, permutation symmetries, and sensitivity to random initialization). Tri-plane hybrid neural fields, a compact and effective alternative representation, had not been investigated for direct neural processing, leaving a gap between neural-field-based methods and established architectures operating on explicit representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241871/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method ingests only the discrete tri-plane component (three orthogonal 2D feature maps of size 16×32×32, totaling 64K parameters per shape), discarding the small MLP entirely. For classification, the output is a single class label; for part segmentation, 3D query point coordinates are additionally fed to a decoder that produces a part label per point. The tri-plane resolution and channel count are not critical hyperparameters, with performance remaining stable across 8–32 channels and 24×24 to 40×40 spatial sizes.

## Method

The key insight is that tri-plane features encode rich geometric and semantic information, and the primary variance across randomly initialized fits of the same shape reduces to a permutation of channel order. The architecture is a Transformer encoder without positional encoding, where each channel of each plane is flattened into a token of dimension HW, yielding a sequence of 3C tokens; the absence of positional encoding combined with a final max-pool operator makes the representation invariant to channel order. For segmentation, a Transformer decoder with cross-attention lets each query point (encoded via positional encoding) attend globally to the tri-plane tokens. This design allows standard, well-engineered architectures to process tri-planes seamlessly, achieving classification accuracy nearly on par with explicit-representation methods (e.g., 87.0% vs 88.8% on ModelNet40) while preserving the high reconstruction fidelity of individual neural fields.

## Results

On the neural field classification benchmark, the proposed tri-plane Transformer achieves 87.0% accuracy on ModelNet40 (UDF), 94.1% on ShapeNet10 (UDF), 69.1% on ScanNet10 (UDF), 86.8% on Manifold40 (SDF), 91.8% on ShapeNet10 (OF), and 92.6% on ShapeNetRender (RF), setting new state-of-the-art across all field types. Compared to the best explicit-representation baselines (PointNet, MeshWalker, Conv3DNet, ResNet50 ensemble), the gap is narrowed to 1.8–3.2 percentage points. For 3D part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, the method outperforms inr2vec by 20% instance mIoU and 16.7% class mIoU while maintaining the same reconstruction quality as the underlying tri-plane fields.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-MLP processing methods, the tri-plane approach dominates by large margins: DWSNet (the strongest MLP-based method) reaches only 56.3% on ModelNet40 and 47.9% on Manifold40 versus 87.0% and 86.8% for the proposed method, while inr2vec, NFN, and NFT all fall below 42% on most datasets. Against shared-network frameworks, Functa is competitive on ModelNet40 (87.3% vs 87.0%) but lags significantly on ShapeNet10 UDF (83.4% vs 94.1%), ScanNet10 (56.4% vs 69.1%), and OF (36.3% vs 91.8%), and its reconstruction quality is far inferior (CD 2.85 mm vs 0.18 mm for tri-planes). Compared to explicit-representation architectures, the gap is minimal: PointNet achieves 88.8% on ModelNet40 vs 87.0%, MeshWalker 90.0% vs 86.8% on Manifold40, Conv3DNet 92.1% vs 91.8% on ShapeNet10 OF, and a ResNet50 ensemble 94.0% vs 92.6% on ShapeNetRender RF. For part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, the method surpasses inr2vec (the only prior method handling random initialization) by 20% instance mIoU and 16.7% class mIoU, while remaining within a few points of PointNet++ and DGCNN operating on explicit point clouds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the line of neural field processing work (inr2vec, DWSNet, NFN, NFT) that operates on single large MLPs (e.g., SIREN networks) by instead processing the discrete tri-plane component of hybrid neural fields, which were previously used only for reconstruction (EG3D, PET-NeuS, Tri-MipRF). The key conceptual shift is recognizing that the tri-plane feature maps alone encode rich geometric and semantic information, making the small MLP dispensable for downstream tasks and enabling the use of standard architectures (Transformers, CNNs) rather than specialized equivariant layers designed for MLP weight spaces. The channel-permutation invariance insight—showing that different random initializations of the same shape produce tri-planes differing only by channel order—provides a clean mechanism for robustness that is absent in the single-MLP setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first comprehensive benchmark for neural field classification spanning UDF, SDF, OF, and RF, and demonstrates that hybrid tri-plane representations can be processed with off-the-shelf architectures to nearly close the gap with explicit-representation methods (e.g., 87.0% vs 88.8% on ModelNet40). The practical implication is significant: if neural fields become a standard storage format for 3D assets, one can classify or segment them directly without reconstruction, rendering, or specialized equivariant architectures. Limitations include the restriction to 3D object-level tri-planes (not large scenes or time-varying fields), and the universal classifier, while promising, still shows degradation when trained on a single field type and tested on others. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other hybrid structures (sparse feature sets, 4D tensors) and to scene-level tasks like 3D object detection.
