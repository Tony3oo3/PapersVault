## Problem

When pre-trained image diffusion models are applied frame-by-frame to video tasks, the independent noise samples per frame destroy temporal correlations, producing either high-frequency flickering (random noise) or texture-sticking artifacts (fixed noise). Existing work lacks a principled way to transport noise between frames that simultaneously preserves the motion-induced correlations and the statistical properties of the noise (Gaussian distribution, pixel independence, correct variance).

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete D×D Gaussian noise sample (obtained from noise inversion, random sampling, or a prior frame) and a deformation field (optical flow or motion vectors) mapping between two frames, and produces a temporally-correlated noise sample for the target frame that retains unit variance and pixel independence. The deformation field need not be diffeomorphic; disocclusions and undefined regions are handled by a two-stage fallback that fills gaps from the previous frame or random resampling. The noise is internally upsampled to a higher-resolution grid (factor 2^k, typically k=3) to support accurate sub-pixel warping.

## Method

The core idea is to reinterpret each discrete noise pixel as the integral of an underlying infinite-resolution white Gaussian noise field (∫-noise), enabling a conditional sampling procedure that lifts a coarse noise sample to a finer grid while preserving the original pixel value (Equation 3). A novel noise transport equation (Equation 4) then warps this continuous noise via a deformation field, incorporating a Jacobian-determinant scaling factor that corrects the variance distortion introduced by local stretching or compression. In practice, each pixel's unit-area contour is subdivided into segments, warped backward through the deformation, triangulated, and rasterized onto the high-resolution grid; the covered sub-pixels are summed and normalized by the square root of their count (Equation 5), yielding a distribution-preserving warped value. For long video sequences, the accumulated deformation from the anchor frame is used rather than incremental frame-to-frame warping, ensuring long-term temporal coherence. A 1-D toy example shows the warped value follows a Brownian bridge between neighboring pixels, with the stochastic component exactly compensating the variance loss from linear interpolation.

## Results

On the appearance transfer task (SDEdit on LSUN Bedroom), ∫-noise achieves the best warp error of 2.50×10⁻³, outperforming Random (10.00), Fixed (4.35), PYoCo progressive (4.90), and Control-A-Video (5.09). On 4× video super-resolution (I²SB on DAVIS), ∫-noise achieves the lowest warp error of 6.49×10⁻³ versus 8.91 (Random) and 7.97 (Fixed), with competitive LPIPS of 0.196. On pose-to-person video generation (PIDM), ∫-noise reduces warp error from 34.22 (Random) to 2.92, though Fixed noise (2.26) and Bilinear (2.13) are slightly lower on this specific metric. Visual quality (FID, Precision, LPIPS) remains on par with standard Gaussian noise priors, confirming the distribution-preserving property.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PYoCo (Ge et al., 2023), ∫-noise achieves substantially lower warp error across all four tasks: 2.50 vs. 7.26 (mixed) / 4.90 (progressive) on appearance transfer, 6.49 vs. 8.48 / 8.10 on video SR, 5.98 vs. 7.77 / 7.48 on JPEG restoration, and 2.92 vs. 20.92 / 11.97 on pose-to-person. Against Control-A-Video (Chen et al., 2023), ∫-noise similarly dominates on temporal coherency: 2.50 vs. 5.09, 6.49 vs. 7.98, 5.98 vs. 7.73, and 2.92 vs. 6.45. On visual quality metrics (FID, Precision, LPIPS), ∫-noise is competitive with both PYoCo and Control-A-Video, confirming that the distribution-preserving warping does not degrade sample quality. The main caveat is that traditional interpolation methods (Bilinear, Bicubic) achieve slightly lower warp error on JPEG restoration and pose-to-person, but at the cost of severely degraded visual quality (FID >140, LPIPS >0.37), making them impractical for diffusion-based pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reinterpreting the discrete Gaussian noise used in diffusion models as the integral of a continuous white noise field (∫-noise), drawing on the mathematical framework of white noise from stochastic PDEs. It departs from prior noise-prior approaches—random noise, fixed noise, PYoCo's correlated sampling, and Control-A-Video's residual sampling—by deriving a principled noise transport equation (Eq. 4) that preserves the noise distribution under deformation, rather than relying on ad-hoc interpolation or heuristic correlation. The method also contrasts with feature-warping and cross-attention approaches by operating directly on the noise signal, which can represent high-frequency patterns that compressed features cannot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ∫-noise framework provides a theoretically grounded, distribution-preserving mechanism for temporal noise correlation that generalizes across multiple video diffusion tasks (appearance transfer, restoration, pose-to-person, fluid SR). However, the method is computationally expensive (~1 s/frame at 256×256), and the authors show it has limited impact in latent diffusion models—the dominant paradigm—because latent-space noise controls low-frequency structure rather than high-frequency detail. Open questions include extending the prior to latent spaces, improving efficiency, and understanding when the assumption that correlated noise yields better temporal coherency breaks down for constrained pipelines.
