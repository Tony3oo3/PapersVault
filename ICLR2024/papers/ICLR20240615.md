## Problem

Existing visual prosthesis processing frameworks either generate stimulation patterns without any quality verification (region-based detection) or supervise training with image reconstruction, which misaligns with how the visual cortex actually encodes perception as neural spikes rather than pixel values. This paper argues that stimulation quality should be assessed by whether the resulting V1 neuron firing rates match those of sighted individuals, a criterion no prior framework has addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240615/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes sparse spike trains from a dynamic vision sensor (DVS) at 120×120 resolution, where only moving scene components are encoded, yielding high-temporal-resolution, low-data-volume inputs. The primary output is a stimulation amplitude pattern mapped to an implanted electrode array; the secondary output is a predicted firing rate for 166 V1 neurons, which serves as the quality-verification signal during training. The DVS-V1 dataset provides paired spike-train inputs and recorded macaque V1 responses (averaged over 4 sessions per stimulus) as supervision.

## Method

StimuSEE is an end-to-end framework with three stages: (1) a spiking recurrent neural network (SRNN) with two spike layers and one recurrent block using LIF and MP_LIF neurons to predict stimulation amplitudes from DVS spike trains in an energy-efficient, spike-based manner; (2) a biologically grounded phosphene model that applies a reverse wedge-dipole coordinate transform, computes effective stimulation intensity with leak-current subtraction, and models phosphene brightness, size, and streak elongation, with a differentiable ReLU surrogate replacing the non-differentiable threshold; (3) a PVS-net (from Lindsey et al., 2019) pre-trained on CIFAR-10 that mimics LGN-to-V1 processing and uses an online trainable feature bank of 50 embeddings with a read-out layer to predict V1 firing rates. Training minimizes MAE between predicted and recorded V1 firing rates plus an L2 Laplacian smoothness penalty on weights, with the phosphene model held fixed at human-experiment-derived parameters and the PVS-net updated via stop-gradient.

## Results

StimuSEE achieves a Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) of 0.78 between predicted V1 neuron firing rates and recorded responses of normal neurons on the DVS-V1 test set, outperforming all compared baselines. In the spatial domain, over 80% of test scenes yield a PCC above 0.75, while in the temporal domain approximately 69% of single-neuron comparisons exceed this threshold. The SRNN-based retina network consumes approximately 851 μJ per prediction, roughly 5× less energy than a comparable CNN (3.982 mJ), with an overall spike firing rate of only 7.29%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StimuSEE outperforms all three baselines on the PCC metric for fitting V1 neuron responses. Against Cadena et al. (2019), a CNN-based model targeting V1 cells with PCC 0.72, StimuSEE improves by 0.06 while additionally incorporating a biological phosphene model and PVS-net for quality verification. Against Wang et al. (2022b), a spiking neural network (SNN) also targeting V1 cells with PCC 0.65, StimuSEE achieves a 0.13 gain, demonstrating that the recurrent architecture and end-to-end biological validation pipeline contribute meaningfully. Yan et al. (2020) targets ganglion cells rather than V1 cells (PCC 0.70), making it a less direct comparison, but StimuSEE still exceeds it by 0.08. Notably, none of the baselines include a validation component (phosphene simulation or cortical network), whereas StimuSEE's BSM + PVS-net pipeline provides a biologically grounded quality check that the authors argue is essential for practical prosthesis deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

StimuSEE extends the hybrid neural autoencoder line of work (Granley et al., 2022; Burcu et al., 2022) that pairs a stimulation-prediction network with a phosphene simulation model, but departs fundamentally in its supervision signal: instead of using image reconstruction quality as the training objective, it uses recorded V1 neuron firing rates as the target. The PVS-net component is adapted from Lindsey et al.'s (2019) unified theory of early visual representations, and the phosphene model inherits its brightness, size, and streak formulations from Granley & Beyeler (2021) and van der Grinten et al. (2022). The SRNN backbone for energy-efficient stimulation prediction builds directly on the authors' prior SpikesEE framework (Wang et al., 2022a), which demonstrated DVS+SRNN processing for retinal prostheses but lacked any biological verification loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from image-reconstruction supervision to V1-spike supervision addresses a genuine gap: prior frameworks optimize for pixel-level similarity to the original scene, which does not guarantee that the resulting stimulation will evoke appropriate cortical responses. However, the framework remains entirely in silico—validated only on the DVS-V1 macaque dataset with no in-vivo or human validation—so the practical impact on actual vision restoration is still speculative. Open questions include whether the fixed phosphene parameters (derived from a small set of human experiments) generalize across patients, whether the 0.78 PCC translates to perceptually meaningful vision, and whether the SRNN's energy advantage holds when deployed on actual implant hardware with its own power constraints.
