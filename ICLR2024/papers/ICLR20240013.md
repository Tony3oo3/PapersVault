## Problem

Existing graph distillation algorithms (e.g., DOSCOND, KIDD) replicate gradient trajectories of a specific GNN, which paradoxically requires training on the full dataset and ties the distilled data to a particular architecture and hyperparameter configuration. This undermines the core purpose of distillation—avoiding full-dataset training—and forces re-distillation whenever the modeling pipeline changes, inflating storage and maintenance overhead.

## Input / Output

MIRAGE takes a labeled graph training set, the maximum number of GNN layers L, and a per-class frequency threshold θ ∈ [0,1]. Rather than producing a smaller set of graphs, it outputs a distilled dataset of frequent co-occurring computation tree sets (each tree set is a multiset of rooted L-hop trees), along with GNN parameters trained on those tree sets. The distilled dataset is architecture-independent: a single distillation run suffices for any message-passing GNN (GCN, GAT, GIN, etc.) with ≤ L layers, and the entire pipeline runs on CPU only.

## Method

MIRAGE exploits the observation that an L-layer message-passing GNN decomposes each graph into a multiset of computation trees (L-hop rooted neighborhoods), and that the frequency distribution of these trees follows a power-law. It mines frequently co-occurring tree sets per class using FP-Growth (treating each graph's tree multiset as a transaction), retaining only sets whose co-occurrence frequency exceeds θ. During training, the GNN samples batches of tree sets proportionally to their frequency and computes graph-level embeddings by applying the COMBINE function to root-node representations within each tree set. Because the distillation operates on the computation structure of message-passing rather than on model gradients, it is agnostic to architecture, hidden dimensions, dropout, and other hyperparameters—requiring only L and θ as inputs.

## Results

MIRAGE achieves the best AUC-ROC in 8 out of 17 dataset-architecture combinations and ranks top-2 in all remaining cases. On compression, MIRAGE produces distilled datasets ~4× smaller than DOSCOND and ~5× smaller than KIDD on average (e.g., 1,612 bytes vs. 23,176 bytes on ogbg-molbace). On distillation efficiency, MIRAGE runs ~150× faster than DOSCOND and ~500× faster than KIDD (e.g., 2.57 s vs. 191–256 s on ogbg-molbace), using only CPU operations. In the node classification extension on ogbg-molbace, MIRAGE achieves 88.92% AUC-ROC at 1,816 bytes, outperforming DOSCOND's 81.29% at 7,760 bytes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIRAGE outperforms DOSCOND (Jin et al., 2022) in the majority of dataset-architecture combinations, with particularly large margins on ogbg-molbace/GCN (77.03 vs. 67.34) and DD/GAT (76.08 vs. 63.45); DOSCOND edges out MIRAGE only on ogbg-molhiv/GCN (73.16 vs. 69.59) and ogbg-molhiv/GIN (72.72 vs. 71.58). Against KIDD (Xu et al., 2023), MIRAGE wins in most settings (e.g., 73.10 vs. 69.79 on ogbg-molhiv/GAT, 76.08 vs. 73.14 on DD/GAT), though KIDD narrowly leads on ogbg-molbace/GIN (77.09 vs. 76.18) and ogbg-molbbp/GIN (64.21 vs. 61.21). HERDING (Welling, 2009) is consistently outperformed by MIRAGE across all datasets and architectures, with gaps of 5–15 AUC-ROC points. In terms of compression, MIRAGE produces datasets ~4× smaller than DOSCOND and ~5× smaller than KIDD on average (e.g., 1,612 vs. 23,176 bytes on ogbg-molbace; 318 vs. 70,168 bytes on NCI1). On distillation time, MIRAGE is ~150× faster than DOSCOND and ~500× faster than KIDD (e.g., 2.57 s vs. 191–256 s on ogbg-molbace), and runs entirely on CPU while the baselines require GPUs.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIRAGE departs fundamentally from the gradient-matching paradigm that underpins existing graph distillation methods (DOSCOND, KIDD, GCOND), which replicate the training gradient trajectory of a specific GNN architecture. Instead, it repurposes the FP-Growth frequent itemset mining algorithm (Han et al., 2004) to mine co-occurring computation trees extracted from the message-passing decomposition of input graphs. The conceptual bridge is the observation that message-passing GNNs decompose graphs into multisets of computation trees whose frequency distribution follows a power law, enabling lossy compression of the computational data itself rather than the gradient signal. This positions MIRAGE as a model-agnostic alternative that requires only the number of GNN layers as architectural input, in contrast to prior methods that are locked to a specific architecture and hyperparameter configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnosticism eliminates the need to regenerate distilled datasets for every architecture or hyperparameter change, reducing storage overhead and maintenance burden in practical ML pipelines. The 150× distillation speedup and CPU-only execution make graph distillation accessible in resource-constrained settings where GPU access is unavailable. However, the method's reliance on a skewed computation-tree distribution may not hold for heterophilous or highly diverse datasets, and its applicability to graph transformers, equivariant GNNs, and temporal graphs remains unexplored. Generalization to unseen tasks is also an open limitation shared with all current graph distillation approaches.
