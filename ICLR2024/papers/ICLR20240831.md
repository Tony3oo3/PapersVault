## Problem

Active learning for multiple deep models requires iteratively retraining all target models at each query round, making it prohibitively expensive when dozens of architectures are involved. Existing one-shot AL methods select instances based on feature distances that ignore the fact that different deep backbones produce fundamentally different representations of the same data, so they cannot effectively serve multiple models simultaneously. This paper asks: can we design a single-shot querying strategy that is provably effective for learning linear prediction heads across k distinct deep representations, without any iterative model retraining?

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the feature matrices L^j and U^j (j=1,...,k) obtained by passing a small labeled set and a large unlabeled set through k fixed network backbones (50 OFA architectures in experiments), along with a query budget τ. It outputs a set of τ unlabeled instances selected for label querying and trained linear prediction layers θ̃^j for each model. In the fine-tuning scenario, only the prediction head is trained (backbone frozen); in the vanilla scenario, the full network is trained with cross-entropy loss using the selected instances. The key structural detail is that the sampling matrix S is shared across all k models, so a single set of queried labels serves every target model.

## Method

The core insight is that a deep model decomposes into a fixed nonlinear backbone and a linear prediction layer, so training k deep models reduces to solving k ℓ_p-regression problems min_θ ||f(A^j θ) − y||_p^p on different feature matrices A^j. The method constructs a shared reweighted sampling matrix S by sampling each unlabeled instance with probability proportional to its maximum Lewis weight across all k representations, i.e., p_i ∝ max_j w_i(U^j), and reweighting selected rows by (m·p_q)^(−1/p). For each model j, the linear head is then trained by solving the constrained ℓ_p regression min_{θ∈E} ||Sf(A^j θ) − Sy||_p^p, where E enforces a norm bound on the prediction. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.5 / Corollary 3.6) shows that m = Õ(T·d^{max{p/2−1,0}}/ε⁴) samples suffice for a constant-factor approximation on all k models simultaneously, where T = Σᵢ max_j w_i(A^j) is empirically much smaller than kd. This improves upon Gajjar et al.'s single-model, p=2 result and removes the log n factor from prior Lewis weight subspace embedding bounds.

## Results

On 8 classification benchmarks with 50 deep models, the proposed one-shot Lewis weight sampling method achieves mean accuracy comparable to the state-of-the-art iterative DIAM method while reducing GPU training time from 45–120 hours to 5.3–12.7 hours (a 5–10× speedup). On 3 regression benchmarks (Biwi, FLD, CelebA) in the fine-tuning scenario, the method achieves the lowest mean MSE across all query budgets (3000 and 6000 instances), outperforming Coreset, Random, and QBC baselines. The empirical observation that the sum of maximum leverage scores grows very slowly as the number of models increases (well below the theoretical upper bound of min{k·d, n}) confirms the practical efficiency of the approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DIAM (the state-of-the-art iterative AL for multiple models), the proposed one-shot method achieves comparable mean accuracy on most classification datasets while being 5–10× faster in GPU hours; DIAM is notably less stable, performing well on MNIST-related datasets but degrading on CIFAR-100 and EMNIST. Against QBC, the proposed method clearly outperforms, as QBC causes severe class imbalance that hurts its accuracy. Against Entropy, both methods perform similarly to Random, suggesting that mean entropy scores have extremely small standard deviation across the 50 models, making them uninformative for selection. Against Coreset, the proposed method wins because Coreset's reliance on a single supernet representation fails to account for the distinct feature spaces of 50 different backbones, making it worse than Random in this multi-model setting. In the fine-tuning (regression) scenario, the proposed method outperforms all three baselines (Coreset, Random, QBC) in mean MSE at both 3000 and 6000 query budgets, demonstrating robustness to budget size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) active learning for multiple target models, initiated by Tang & Huang (2022) with their iterative DIAM algorithm; (2) one-shot active learning methods that avoid repeated model training (Yang & Loog, 2019; Viering et al., 2019; Jin et al., 2022), which have not addressed the challenge of distinct deep representations; and (3) Lewis weight sampling for active ℓ_p-regression (Chen & Price, 2019; Parulekar et al., 2021; Musco et al., 2022) and its extension to single-neuron models with Lipschitz nonlinearities (Gajjar et al., 2023a,b). The key departure is using the maximum Lewis weight across multiple representations as a shared sampling criterion, unifying the active regression literature with the multi-model AL setting and extending Gajjar et al.'s single-model, p=2 result to k models and general p.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-shot formulation offers a 5–12× reduction in GPU-hours over iterative baselines (Table 1), making multi-model AL practical for resource-constrained deployment scenarios. The empirical observation that the sum of maximum leverage scores grows very slowly with the number of models (Figure 1, Figure 4) is a potentially useful lens for interpreting deep representation learning, as prior work has not identified simple correlations across architectures. Limitations include the theoretical assumption T = poly(d), the removal of the constraint set E in the fine-tuning experiments for practicality, and the fact that in the vanilla deep learning scenario the theoretical ℓ_p-regression solver is replaced by standard deep training, weakening the connection between theory and practice. Open questions include whether the slow growth of max leverage scores persists for more architecturally diverse model families or non-visual domains.
