## Problem

Randomized smoothing (RS) certification of deep neural networks is computationally expensive, requiring tens of thousands of noisy inferences per input. When a DNN is modified post-training (e.g., quantized or pruned for deployment), existing practice mandates re-running RS certification from scratch for the modified model, which can take hours even for 500 test images. This paper asks how certification guarantees from the original smoothed model can be reused to certify an approximated model with far fewer samples.

## Input / Output

IRS takes the original classifier f, an approximated classifier f^p (e.g., int8-quantized or pruned), an input image x, and a cache C_f containing the top predicted class, its lower confidence bound p_A, and the random seeds used to generate Gaussian corruptions during the original RS run. It outputs a certified l2-radius for the smoothed classifier g^p and a class prediction. The certified radius is computed as σΦ⁻¹(p_A − ζ_x), where ζ_x is an upper confidence bound on the probability that f and f^p disagree on a random Gaussian corruption of x; the same Gaussian samples (recovered from stored seeds) are reused for both the original p_A estimation and the ζ_x estimation, making the approach 2× faster than naive Monte Carlo estimation of ζ_x.

## Method

IRS exploits the observation that common approximations (int8 quantization, light pruning) cause f and f^p to disagree on very few Gaussian corruptions, yielding a small disparity ζ_x (typically <0.01). The key mechanism is a three-step procedure: (1) retrieve p_A and the Gaussian seeds from the cached RS certification of f; (2) estimate ζ_x via a Clopper-Pearson upper confidence bound on the fraction of corruptions where f and f^p produce different predictions, reusing the same seeds; (3) apply Theorem 2 to combine p_A, p_B, and ζ_x into a certified radius for g^p, simplified by Theorem 3 to σΦ⁻¹(p_A − ζ_x) when p_A − ζ_x ≥ 1/2. A threshold hyperparameter γ (chosen via grid search, e.g., 0.99 for CIFAR-10) switches between the ζ_x-based radius and standard RS when p_A is already close to 1, where estimating ζ_x offers no sample-efficiency benefit. The soundness guarantee (Theorem 4) ensures the combined result holds with confidence at least 1−(α+α_ζ), and the approach achieves up to 4.1× certification speedup over non-incremental RS on ResNet-110/CIFAR-10 with int8 quantization.

## Results

IRS achieves up to 4.1x certification speedup over standard non-incremental RS on ResNet-110/CIFAR-10 with int8 quantization (σ=1), reducing certification time from 2.91 hours to 0.71 hours while achieving a higher ACR (>0.565 vs. baseline's maximum). On ResNet-50/ImageNet (int8, σ=1), IRS saves 5.36 hours (1.24x) at ACR=0.875 and 13.35 hours (1.33x) at ACR=0.90. Across all quantization types (fp16, bf16, int8) and σ values, average speedups range from 1.14x to 3.02x, with larger σ consistently yielding greater speedups (e.g., 2.85x at σ=1.0 vs. 1.37x at σ=0.25 for ResNet-20/CIFAR-10 fp16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard non-incremental Randomized Smoothing (CERTIFY, Cohen et al. 2019), which certifies the approximated smoothed classifier g^p from scratch using n_p fresh Gaussian samples. IRS consistently outperforms this baseline on certification time for any given ACR across all tested configurations (3 architectures × 3 quantization types × 3 σ values). The speedup is most pronounced for larger σ (up to 4.1x at σ=1.0 on CIFAR-10) because larger σ produces smaller p_A values, making the ζ_x-based estimation more sample-efficient than direct probability bound estimation. For pruning, speedups are smaller and can drop below 1x (0.87x) for aggressive 20% pruning on ImageNet, reflecting the higher ζ_x values that reduce the benefit of incremental certification. Overall, IRS dominates the baseline in every quantization setting tested, with the gap widening as σ increases and the approximation becomes milder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the randomized smoothing certification framework of Cohen et al. (2019) by introducing an incremental mechanism that reuses certification guarantees from an original network to certify a modified (quantized or pruned) network with far fewer samples. Conceptually, it draws on the incremental program verification literature (Johnson et al., 2013; O'Hearn, 2018; Stein et al., 2021), where partial results from prior analyses are reused to accelerate re-analysis of modified code. Unlike prior incremental DNN certification work (Fischer et al., 2022b; Ugare et al., 2022, 2023), which relies on deterministic formal methods that cannot scale to ImageNet, this paper brings the incremental paradigm to the probabilistic RS setting, enabling much greater scalability. The key technical departure is the introduction of the disparity bound ζ_x, which quantifies the probability that two similar networks disagree on a noisy input, and the proof that estimating this small probability requires fewer samples than re-estimating the full class probability bounds from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: certifying a single network on 500 test images can take hours, and iterative DNN deployment workflows (quantization tuning, pruning search) require certifying many similar variants. IRS reduces this cost by up to 4.1× on CIFAR-10 and 1.3× on ImageNet, making robustness-aware approximation tuning feasible within reasonable time budgets. Key limitations include the requirement for a certification cache from the original network, diminishing speedups under aggressive pruning (20% pruning can yield <1× speedup on ImageNet), and the dependence on larger σ values for the best gains. Open questions include whether certification caches can be standardized and shipped with pre-certified models, whether the approach extends to other smoothing distributions (e.g., ℓ∞ or discrete noise), and how it interacts with ensemble-based or higher-order RS variants.
