## Problem

Transformers represent syntax and semantics in an implicit, entangled form, while existing recursive models (DIORA, R2D2) that explicitly model hierarchical structure restrict information flow to either inside or outside a span, preventing cross-span communication. This paper asks how to combine the explicit hierarchical composition of recursive models with the powerful inter-span interaction of Transformers, without requiring gold parse trees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241276/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a raw text sentence of n tokens and produces 2n−1 node representations corresponding to all spans in an induced binary tree, where each node encodes both intra-span composition and inter-span context. These multi-grained representations are fed into subsequent Transformer layers for downstream tasks (span classification, sentence classification, NLI). A byproduct is the induced binary syntactic tree itself, recovered by greedily selecting the highest-scoring split at each node from the top down.

## Method

ReCAT stacks multiple Contextual Inside-Outside (CIO) layers between the embedding layer and Transformer layers. Each CIO layer performs a pruned inside pass (composing sub-span representations via a Transformer-based Compose function, weighted by compatibility scores over valid splits) followed by a contextual outside pass (merging information from a span's siblings and parents to contextualize its representation). Stacking CIO layers creates an iterative up-and-down mechanism that refines span representations and underlying structures layer by layer. A key innovation is that the outside pass contextualizes rather than auto-encodes, which introduces information leakage but enables joint pre-training with Transformers via masked language modeling plus a hard-EM parser feedback loss. The pruned inside-outside algorithm reduces space complexity from O(n³) to O(n) and parallel time complexity to approximately O(log n) by encoding cells in batches according to a merge order derived from a top-down parser.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, ReCAT_noshare[3,1,3] achieves 75.75/75.79 on MNLI (matched/mismatched), 57.40 on RTE, and 82.01 on QNLI, outperforming Fast-R2D2+Transformer (68.25/67.30, 55.96, 76.93) by approximately 7.5% on NLI tasks. On span-level OntoNotes tasks, ReCAT_noshare[3,1,3] achieves 94.18 (NEL), 93.01 (SRL), 98.58 (CTL), and 93.19 (COREF) F1, surpassing Transformer×6 by ~4 F1 on average and Fast-R2D2 by up to 12.8 F1 on SRL. On unsupervised grammar induction (PTB), ReCAT_noshare[3,1,3] reaches 65.00 F1, outperforming Fast-R2D2 (57.22) by 7.78 points and matching TN-PCFG (64.1) which is purpose-built for this task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCAT decisively outperforms all RvNN-based baselines on NLI tasks: ReCAT_noshare[3,1,3] scores 75.75/75.79 on MNLI versus 68.25/67.30 for Fast-R2D2+Transformer and 68.87/68.35 for DIORA*+Transformer, a gap of roughly 7–8 points. On span-level OntoNotes tasks, ReCAT beats Transformer×6 by ~4 F1 on average (e.g., 93.01 vs 88.99 on SRL) and crushes Fast-R2D2 on SRL (93.01 vs 80.21) due to the contextual outside pass providing inter-span information that Fast-R2D2 lacks. On grammar induction (PTB), ReCAT_noshare[3,1,3] achieves 65.00 F1, surpassing Fast-R2D2 (57.22) by 7.78 points and matching the purpose-built TN-PCFG (64.1). However, ReCAT underperforms RvNN baselines on single-sentence tasks (SST-2: 86.80 vs 90.71 for Fast-R2D2) and CoLA (26.69 vs 40.11), which the authors attribute to the contextualization mechanism sacrificing local compositional fidelity. ReCAT_noshare[3,1,6] (109M params) approaches Transformer×9 (88M params) on most GLUE tasks but still trails on QNLI (82.04 vs 83.20) and QQP (86.82 vs 88.18), suggesting that additional Transformer layers partially compensate for the structural inductive bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReCAT extends the deep inside-outside algorithm lineage (DIORA, R2D2, Fast-R2D2) by introducing Contextual Inside-Outside (CIO) layers that break the information access constraint: unlike DIORA and R2D2, information can flow across span boundaries via an iterative top-down/bottom-up mechanism, enabling joint pre-training with Transformers without information leakage. The paper also departs from Transformer Grammars (Sartran et al., 2022) by recovering hierarchical structures in a fully unsupervised manner rather than relying on gold parse trees. A key technical contribution is reducing the space complexity of the pruned inside algorithm from cubic to linear and parallel time complexity to approximately logarithmic, making the CIO layers scalable to deep stacks and large corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReCAT demonstrates that explicit hierarchical syntactic structure modeling and Transformer scaling can coexist, yielding interpretable multi-grained representations that significantly improve span-level tasks and grammar induction. The finding that induced trees from fully unsupervised training show strong consistency with human-annotated constituency trees is encouraging for interpretability. However, the model underperforms on single-sentence tasks compared to RvNN baselines, revealing a trade-off between contextualization (needed for Transformer joint training) and local composition quality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger pre-training corpora and model sizes, and whether the computational overhead during training (mitigated but not eliminated by fast encoding) limits practical adoption.
