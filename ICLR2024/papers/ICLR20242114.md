## Problem

Despite the success of text-to-image diffusion models, they have not been leveraged as a unified foundation for standard visual recognition tasks, which instead rely on bespoke task-specific architectures and loss functions. Prior unified vision models (e.g., Pix2Seq, Painter) use uninterpretable numerical prompts or visual tokens that limit generalization to new datasets, categories, and user phrasings. This paper asks whether a natural language interface can replace these opaque prompts to enable a single generative model to execute diverse vision tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242114/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 RGB input image and a free-form natural language instruction (e.g., 'Segment the cat', 'Detect the car') and produces a 256×256 RGB output image whose pixel values encode the task result. The encoding is task-specific: a binary mask for segmentation, a red bounding-box overlay on the original image for detection, a grayscale depth map (linearly mapped from 0–10 m to 0–255) for depth estimation, and a solid color block for classification. At inference, post-processing (channel averaging, contour extraction, color-distance scoring) converts the generated image back into the conventional task output.

## Method

InstructCV repurposes a pretrained Stable Diffusion latent diffusion model by instruction-tuning it with the InstructPix2Pix architecture, where the encoded input image is concatenated with the noisy latent and the instruction text conditions the U-Net via cross-attention. The training dataset pools four standard CV datasets (MS-COCO, ADE20K, NYUv2, Oxford-IIIT-Pets) into 180k image pairs, with task outputs visually encoded into RGB space and task identifiers replaced by natural language instructions generated by a T5-based LLM that paraphrases per-task prompt templates. A classifier-free guidance scheme with separate scales γ_I and γ_T independently controls how strongly the output adheres to the input image structure versus the semantic content of the instruction. Training requires only 20 epochs (~2,000 steps) on 8 A100 GPUs with a learning rate of 1e-4, leveraging the EMA weights of the Stable Diffusion v1.5 checkpoint, and achieves competitive multi-task performance with notably stronger out-of-distribution and open-vocabulary generalization than sequence-based or visual-prompting generalist baselines.

## Results

InstructCV-FP achieves the best depth estimation performance across all settings, reaching 0.275 RMSE on NYUv2 and 0.268 on SUNRGB-D, outperforming task-specific models (BinsFormer: 0.330/0.433) and generalist baselines (Unified-IO: 0.387/0.287). For semantic segmentation, InstructCV-FP scores 52.3 mIoU on ADE-20K and 53.5 on VOC, surpassing Painter (49.9/52.5) and Unified-IO (25.7/27.7) but trailing Mask2Former (56.1). On open-vocabulary segmentation (FSS-1000), InstructCV-RP achieves 69.8 mIoU, outperforming Generalist Painter (62.3) and Inpainting (58.5). Classification remains the weakest task, with InstructCV scoring 80.4% on Oxford-Pets versus 98.4% for ViT, and 75.1% on ImageNet-sub versus 89.9% for Unified-IO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructCV-FP dominates on depth estimation, achieving 0.275 RMSE on NYUv2 (vs. 0.330 for BinsFormer and 0.339 for DepthFormer) and 0.268 on SUNRGB-D (vs. 0.427 for DepthFormer and 0.433 for BinsFormer), a ~35% improvement over the best task-specific model on the external dataset. For semantic segmentation, InstructCV-FP (52.3 mIoU on ADE-20K) outperforms Painter (49.9) and Unified-IO (25.7) but falls short of the task-specific Mask2Former (56.1). On object detection, InstructCV-FP (49.1 mAP on COCO) trails DETR (60.6) and Mask R-CNN (60.5) but significantly outperforms the generalist Pix2SeqV2 (57.4 on COCO, 38.5 on VOC), with a +23.2 mAP gap on the external VOC dataset. Classification is where InstructCV is weakest: it scores 80.4% on Oxford-Pets versus 98.4% for ViT and 97.5% for ResNet, and 75.1% on ImageNet-sub versus 89.9% for Unified-IO. On open-vocabulary segmentation (FSS-1000), InstructCV-RP achieves 69.8 mIoU, beating Generalist Painter (62.3) by +7.5 and Inpainting (58.5) by +11.3, demonstrating the advantage of language-based instructions over visual prompting for zero-shot category generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

InstructCV directly extends the InstructPix2Pix framework (Brooks et al., CVPR 2023), which instruction-tuned a latent diffusion model for image editing, by repurposing the same architecture for vision recognition tasks (segmentation, detection, depth, classification) instead of editing. It departs from prior vision generalist models (Unified-IO, Pix2Seq, Painter, Florence) that rely on sequence-to-sequence tokenization or uninterpretable visual prompts, replacing these with natural language instructions processed by a pretrained text-to-image diffusion backbone (Stable Diffusion). The LLM-based prompt paraphrasing strategy for generating diverse training instructions is a novel data-construction step not present in prior generalist vision work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InstructCV demonstrates that a generative diffusion model, fine-tuned for only ~2,000 steps on a modest multi-task dataset, can serve as a competitive multi-task vision model with a natural language interface, outperforming or matching task-specific and generalist baselines while generalizing more robustly to unseen datasets, categories, and user-written instructions. Key limitations include 5-second inference (not real-time), notably weaker classification performance, and instruction diversity constrained by template rephrasing. Open questions include whether this paradigm can handle more nuanced compositional instructions (e.g., constraining object counts) and whether integration with RLHF or larger instruction datasets could close the gap on classification and enable broader task coverage.
