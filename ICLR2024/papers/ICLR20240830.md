## Problem

Existing few-shot visual reasoning benchmarks either rely on synthetic diagrams, use closed vocabularies of object categories, or restrict concepts to fixed structures like human-object interactions. No benchmark simultaneously evaluates few-shot induction of free-form, open-vocabulary visual concepts (ranging from object attributes to commonsense factual knowledge) on real-world images with hard negatives that partially overlap the target concept, making it impossible to solve by recognizing only a subset of the concept's terms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240830/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each problem instance provides 6 positive images, 6 negative images, and 1 query image (all real-world photographs). The model must output a binary label (positive or negative) for the query and optionally an induced visual concept caption. Uniquely, the positive images contain distractor content beyond the target concept, and the negative images are constructed to partially overlap with the concept (e.g., 'animals' present but 'running' absent), forcing full concept induction rather than partial recognition. Query images are adversarially selected via CLIP embedding distance to maximize difficulty.

## Method

The paper introduces Bongard-OpenWorld, a 1.01K-problem benchmark where visual concepts are extracted from CC-3M captions via grid sampling (sliding windows of size 2–5 over POS-tagged tuples, top-k selection within 300-caption grids) and augmented with crowd-sourced commonsense concepts across 9 predefined categories. To evaluate the benchmark, the authors benchmark four model families: (1) canonical few-shot learners (SNAIL, ProtoNet, MetaOptNet, Meta-Baseline) with ConvNeXt-base backbones pretrained on scratch/IN-1K/IN-22K/LAION-2B, optionally with an auxiliary BLIP-2 captioning loss; (2) single-round VLM+LLM pipelines where BLIP-2 or InstructBLIP captions are fed to ChatGPT/GPT-4 for in-context reasoning; (3) multi-round ChatCaptioner interactive Q&A between LLM and VLM; and (4) a neuro-symbolic approach where GPT-4 extracts concepts from VLM captions and iteratively applies set-theoretic logical operations (intersection of positives filtered to ≥4 occurrences, union of negatives filtered to ≥2) until convergence. The best system (SNAIL + OpenCLIP + caption auxiliary) reaches 64% accuracy versus 91% for humans, revealing that holistic multi-image perception-reasoning over open-vocabulary concepts remains unsolved.

## Results

On Bongard-OpenWorld, the best few-shot learner (SNAIL with OpenCLIP backbone and auxiliary captioning) achieves 64.0% average accuracy, while the best LLM-based approach (GPT-4 with InstructBLIP captions) reaches 63.8%. All other methods fall between 49.3% (OpenFlamingo, Otter) and 61.6% (GPT-4 with BLIP-2), compared to 91.0% for human participants. Performance drops consistently on longer concepts (e.g., SNAIL: 66.1% short vs. 61.5% long) and commonsense concepts (e.g., GPT-4: 59.3% CS vs. 65.6% non-CS), confirming that free-form compositional and abstract visual concepts remain a significant challenge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNAIL (Mishra et al., 2018), a memory-based transformer meta-learner, emerges as the strongest few-shot approach at 64.0% average accuracy with OpenCLIP pretraining and auxiliary captioning, outperforming ProtoNet (58.5%), MetaOptNet (58.0%), and Meta-Baseline (56.3%) by 5.5–7.7 percentage points. Among LLM-based methods, GPT-4 with InstructBLIP captions (63.8%) narrowly edges out SNAIL, while ChatGPT with BLIP-2 (58.8%) trails by roughly 5 points; fine-tuning ChatGPT on the training set recovers most of this gap (63.3%). End-to-end VLMs (OpenFlamingo, Otter) and GPT-4V with raw images all perform near chance level (49.3–54.0%), indicating that multi-image reasoning remains a critical bottleneck. The neuro-symbolic approach (55.5%) underperforms even single-round VLM+LLM pipelines, suggesting that logical operations on noisy VLM-extracted concepts do not compensate for perception failures. Critically, all methods fall 27+ points below human performance (91.0%), with the gap widening on commonsense and long-concept subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bongard Problems lineage (Bongard-LOGO with synthetic shapes, Bongard-HOI with closed-vocabulary human-object interactions) by introducing open-vocabulary, free-form visual concepts drawn from CC-3M and crowd-sourced commonsense categories, while retaining the core few-shot concept induction formulation. It departs from prior Bongard benchmarks in two key ways: the concept space is no longer a fixed set of 2-tuples or geometric properties but arbitrary natural-language sentences, and the real-world images are augmented with distractors and hard negatives that force full concept induction rather than partial recognition. The multi-round VLM+LLM interactive scheme (ChatCaptioner) and the neuro-symbolic logical-reasoning pipeline are borrowed from recent LLM/VLM integration work but applied here for the first time to a Bongard-style task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 27-point gap between the best model (SNAIL at 64%) and humans (91%) on a task that requires only 12 images per problem highlights a fundamental limitation in current visual intelligence: the inability to perform holistic, reasoning-aware perception across multiple images simultaneously. The paper identifies that neither powerful zero-shot VLMs (GPT-4V, OpenFlamingo, Otter) nor neuro-symbolic pipelines can compensate for the VLM's failure to capture task-relevant details in captions, suggesting that future progress requires integrated perception-reasoning architectures rather than modular caption-then-reason pipelines. As an open-vocabulary benchmark with 1.01K unique concepts, it resists the 'just train on more data' solution and will likely become a standard stress test for next-generation VLMs.
