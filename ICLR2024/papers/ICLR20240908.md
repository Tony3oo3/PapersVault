## Problem

Task vector-based multi-task learning methods (Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging) merge independently fine-tuned models into a single MTL model using a fixed merging coefficient λ, but this coefficient is highly sensitive: an ill-suited value can cause the merged model's accuracy to drop below even simple weight averaging. The core challenge is to determine optimal per-task or per-layer merging coefficients without access to the original training data, and to do so efficiently rather than via infeasible grid search when many tasks and layers are involved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240908/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained backbone (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, or ViT-L/14), K independently fine-tuned model weight sets (from which task vectors T_k = θ_k − θ_pre are computed), and a small set of unlabeled test samples (as few as 0.1% of the full test set suffices). It outputs a single merged multi-task model whose weights are θ_MTL = θ_pre + Σ_k λ_k T_k (or the layer-wise variant), with only 8 (task-wise) or 1,248 (layer-wise) scalar coefficients as trainable parameters against ~907M total model parameters. No labels, gradients, or original training data are required at any point.

## Method

AdaMerging replaces the fixed merging coefficient in Task Arithmetic (or Ties-Merging) with learnable per-task coefficients λ_k or per-layer coefficients λ_k^l, then optimizes them via entropy minimization on unlabeled multi-task test samples as a surrogate for the true prediction loss. The key empirical finding is a strong positive Spearman correlation (0.87 on average across eight datasets) between per-sample Shannon entropy and cross-entropy loss, justifying entropy as a valid proxy objective. Coefficients are initialized to 0.3 and updated for 500 iterations using Adam (lr=0.001, batch size 16) with standard backpropagation through the frozen merged model. The AdaMerging++ variant applies the same adaptive coefficient learning on top of Ties-Merging's Trim/Elect-Sign/Disjoint-Merge (Φ) operation, yielding θ_MTL = θ_pre + Σ_k λ_k Φ(T_k). This yields up to 11% average accuracy improvement over Task Arithmetic on ViT-B/32, better generalization to unseen tasks, and greater robustness to test-time distribution shifts.

## Results

On ViT-B/32 across eight image classification tasks, Layer-wise AdaMerging achieves 80.1% average accuracy, improving 11.0% over Task Arithmetic (69.1%) and 7.7% over Ties-Merging (72.4%). On the larger ViT-L/14 architecture, AdaMerging reaches 90.8% average accuracy, a 6.3% gain over Task Arithmetic (84.5%). The method also demonstrates superior generalization to unseen tasks (up to 9.1% improvement) and robustness to seven corruption types (average 8.45% improvement over Task Arithmetic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaMerging outperforms all four baselines across both ViT-B/32 and ViT-L/14. Against Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023), Layer-wise AdaMerging improves average accuracy by 11.0% on ViT-B/32 (80.1% vs. 69.1%) and 6.3% on ViT-L/14 (90.8% vs. 84.5%). Against Ties-Merging (Yadav et al., 2023), the gains are 7.7% on ViT-B/32 (80.1% vs. 72.4%) and 4.8% on ViT-L/14 (90.8% vs. 86.0%). Fisher Merging (Matena & Raffel, 2022) and RegMean (Jin et al., 2023) trail further behind at 68.3% and 71.8% respectively on ViT-B/32, both substantially below AdaMerging's 80.1%. The Task-wise variants offer more modest gains (1.3–2.0% over their respective base methods), confirming that the layer-wise granularity is critical for the full performance benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task-vector-based model merging paradigm introduced by Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023) and Ties-Merging (Yadav et al., NeurIPS 2023), which merge fine-tuned model weights into a pretrained backbone using a single fixed coefficient λ. The key departure is making this coefficient learnable—either per-task or per-layer—and optimizing it via entropy minimization on unlabeled test samples, an idea borrowed from test-time adaptation (TENT, Wang et al., ICLR 2021). Conceptually, the paper bridges two previously separate threads: dataless model merging for multi-task learning and unsupervised test-time adaptation, showing that the entropy-loss correlation that underpins TENT also holds in the multi-task merging setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe is attractive: only 8 (task-wise) or 1,248 (layer-wise) scalar parameters are added, training takes ~500 iterations on a single GPU, and even 0.1% of unlabeled test data suffices for most of the gain. This makes adaptive model merging feasible in settings where original training data is unavailable (e.g., federated or privacy-constrained scenarios). Open questions include whether the entropy-loss surrogate generalizes beyond classification (e.g., detection, segmentation), whether the approach extends to non-ViT architectures or LLMs, and whether the learned coefficients are stable across different random initializations or data subsets. The paper also notes it has not yet explored merging across different base architectures.
