## Problem

Existing differentiable QP layers (e.g., OptNet, CvxpyLayer) enforce primal feasibility during training by introducing auxiliary variables that structurally guarantee a non-empty feasible set, which limits modeling expressiveness and prevents learning certain constraint structures (e.g., a fixed equality constraint vector that may not lie in the range of a learned matrix). This paper addresses the gap of differentiating through QPs that are potentially infeasible during training while still driving the layer toward feasibility at test time.

## Input / Output

The layer takes QP parameters H(θ), g(θ), C(θ), u(θ) as input, where θ is the trainable parameter vector. In the forward pass, it outputs the QP solution x*(θ) (or the closest feasible solution in ℓ₂-sense when primal infeasible) along with the optimal shift s*(θ) that quantifies the infeasibility distance. In the backward pass, it produces the Extended Conservative Jacobian ∂x*/∂θ (and ∂z*/∂θ, ∂s*/∂θ) computed by solving a linear system, which is then used in the chain rule to propagate gradients to θ. The optimal shift s* can be incorporated into the loss to penalize infeasibility and drive the learned layer toward feasibility at test time.

## Method

The paper introduces a hierarchic problem QP-H(θ) that augments the original QP with a slack variable s, so that when the QP is infeasible, the solution corresponds to the closest feasible QP in ℓ₂-sense. A nonlinear map G is constructed whose zeros encode the KKT conditions of QP-H(θ), and the paper proves G is path differentiable via Lipschitz continuity and semialgebraic structure arguments. The Extended Conservative Jacobian (ECJ) is then defined as the least-squares solution of the linearized implicit function theorem system applied to G, which reduces to the standard Jacobian under strict complementarity and nonsingularity of the active-constraint KKT matrix. For computation, the forward pass solves a QP via a primal-dual augmented Lagrangian solver (ProxQP) that naturally handles infeasibility, while the backward pass solves a single linear system whose structure exploits the binary diagonal matrices encoding active/inactive constraints. Under primal feasibility, the formulation simplifies to a smaller system matching the OptNet backward pass, and iterative refinement can be used for numerical robustness when the KKT matrix is singular.

## Results

On the Sudoku benchmark (9000 training / 1000 test puzzles), QPLayer achieves 33 prediction errors over the last 5 epochs, compared to 162 for OptNet and 1531 for CvxpyLayer. QPLayer also reaches a training/test MSE loss roughly an order of magnitude lower than both baselines by the 10th iteration. In terms of speed, QPLayer's forward and backward pass takes approximately 0.45 ± 0.07 seconds per batch, while OptNet requires over 8.99 ± 0.91 seconds per batch (~20× slower). Additionally, QPLayer can learn linear programs (LPs) and handle primal infeasibility during training, capabilities that OptNet (restricted to strictly convex QPs) and CvxpyLayer do not support.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QPLayer substantially outperforms both baselines on the Sudoku task. Against OptNet (Amos & Kolter, 2017), QPLayer reduces prediction errors by roughly 5× (33 vs. 162) and is approximately 20× faster per batch (0.45 s vs. 8.99 s). A key structural advantage is that QPLayer can learn LPs (H=0), whereas OptNet is restricted to strictly convex QPs, limiting its representational power. Against CvxpyLayer (Agrawal et al., 2019), QPLayer reduces prediction errors by nearly 47× (33 vs. 1531). Furthermore, QPLayer uniquely handles primal infeasibility during training via its extended conservative Jacobian formulation, enabling it to learn constraint matrices (e.g., the Sudoku rule matrix A in Ax=1) that are not guaranteed feasible a priori, while driving the layer toward feasibility at test time by penalizing the optimal shift s* in the loss. OptNet, by contrast, must enforce structural feasibility through auxiliary variables, which the paper shows leads to learned constraint matrices that violate the underlying Sudoku rules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable QP layer paradigm established by OptNet (Amos & Kolter, 2017) and CvxpyLayer (Agrawal et al., 2019) by removing the requirement that the QP be primal feasible during training. Theoretically, it builds on the conservative Jacobian and path-differentiability framework of Bolte & Pauwels (2020) and Bolte et al. (2021), introducing an Extended Conservative Jacobian (ECJ) that unifies the treatment of feasible and infeasible QPs. Practically, it leverages the augmented Lagrangian solver ProxQP (Bambade et al., 2022) and the infeasibility-handling results of Chiche & Gilbert (2016) to compute the closest feasible solution in the forward pass, then differentiates through the resulting KKT map G via a least-squares formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to differentiate through infeasible QPs removes a significant architectural constraint that previously forced practitioners to add auxiliary variables (e.g., learning u = Cz + exp(h)) solely to guarantee feasibility, limiting the expressiveness of optimization layers. The Sudoku experiments demonstrate that this relaxation enables learning LPs (not just strictly convex QPs) and driving the layer toward feasibility at test time, yielding substantially fewer prediction errors. However, the theoretical foundations have acknowledged gaps (e.g., the scope of the implicit function theorem for degenerate matrices, and whether ECJs fully reduce to conservative Jacobians in the general case), and the empirical evidence is concentrated on a single task. Future work on extending to second-order cones and broader constraint classes would strengthen the practical impact.
