## Problem

Learning effective communication in fully decentralized multi-agent RL remains difficult because agents must develop a shared, mutually-intelligible protocol without sharing parameters or gradients. Existing decentralized approaches either rely on autoencoding individual observations (which ignores inter-agent message relationships) or use reward-based signals that are high-variance and poorly grounded. The paper asks whether contrastive learning over the relationship between sent and received messages can provide a stronger, self-supervised grounding for decentralized communication.

## Input / Output

Each agent consumes a partial grid observation (e.g., a 3×3 field of view in Find-Goal) concatenated with continuous messages (dimension D=4, sigmoid-activated) received from all other agents at the previous timestep. The agent outputs a discrete action and a new continuous message vector. For the contrastive loss, a replay buffer stores messages from multiple trajectories, and a sliding window of 5 timesteps defines positive pairs while messages from other trajectories serve as negatives.

## Method

CACL reframes multi-agent communication as a multi-view contrastive learning problem: messages from different agents at the same timestep are treated as different incomplete 'views' of the same environment state, analogous to image augmentations in vision. Using SupCon, messages within a 5-step sliding window of the same trajectory are positive pairs, and messages from other trajectories in the batch are negatives, with a low temperature (η=0.1) and normalized message vectors. The total loss is L_RL + κ·L_CACL (κ=0.5), where L_RL (IA2C with n-step returns) trains the policy and value heads while L_CACL trains only the message head, decoupling communication learning from reward optimization. This design requires no additional decoder network (unlike AEComm) and no gradient flow through other agents (unlike DIAL), keeping the setup fully independent and decentralized.

## Results

CACL outperforms all baselines across all three communication-essential environments (Traffic-Junction, Predator-Prey, Find-Goal) in both final performance and learning speed, with the largest gains in Find-Goal where partial observability is most severe. On the Goal Location representation probing test, CACL achieves 91.28%±1.71 accuracy, substantially exceeding AEComm (76.14%±3.36), PL (78.31%±2.39), and DIAL (68.15%±1.76). CACL also learns the most symmetric communication protocol (0.95–0.98 across environments) compared to AEComm (0.80–0.89), and in Predator-Prey captures both preys in 66.67% of evaluation episodes versus 51.67% for AEComm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CACL consistently outperforms all four baselines across all three environments. Against the strongest baseline AEComm (Lin et al., 2021), CACL achieves a ~15-point advantage on the Goal Location test (91.28% vs. 76.14%) and higher protocol symmetry in every environment (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.89 in Predator-Prey), indicating that contrastive alignment across agents' messages captures more global state information than autoencoding individual observations. Against DIAL (Foerster et al., 2016), which backpropagates gradients through other agents' message heads, CACL wins on all environments despite being fully independent, with a 23-point gap on Goal Location (91.28% vs. 68.15%). Against PL (Eccles et al., 2019), CACL outperforms by 13 points on Goal Location and achieves markedly higher protocol symmetry (0.92 vs. 0.49 in Find-Goal). Against IAC (no communication), CACL's advantage is largest in Find-Goal where the small 3×3 field of view makes communication essential, confirming that the contrastive grounding mechanism is what drives the performance gains rather than the communication channel alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoencoding-based communication grounding of Lin et al. (2021, AEComm) by reframing agents' messages as multi-view encodings of the same environment state, then applying SupCon-style contrastive learning to align the message space across agents. It departs from prior decentralized communication work (DIAL, PL) that relies on reward-driven or heuristic losses by introducing a purely self-supervised contrastive objective that requires no extra parameters beyond the message head. The multi-view learning perspective from representation learning (Bachman et al., 2019) is repurposed: instead of image augmentations as positive pairs, different agents' messages at nearby timesteps serve as positives, while messages from other trajectories serve as negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes contrastive learning as a principled and effective alternative to autoencoding for grounding decentralized multi-agent communication, with the added benefit of inducing highly symmetric protocols that improve mutual intelligibility. Key open questions remain: combining SSL and reward-based gradients (DIAL augmentation consistently hurts performance), extending to adversarial or partially cooperative settings, and achieving zero-shot communication with unseen partners (where all methods still perform poorly). The protocol symmetry and representation probing analyses provide useful diagnostic tools for evaluating emergent communication quality beyond raw task performance.
