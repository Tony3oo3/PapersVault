## Problem

Modeling spatiotemporal fluid densities and velocities from sparse, noisy measurements while satisfying the continuity equation (mass conservation) remains challenging: PINNs enforce the PDE only weakly via collocation points that scale poorly with dimension, divergence-free neural networks require expensive second-order derivatives, and semi-Lagrangian data assimilation relies on iterative ODE solvers that become stiff during training. No existing method guarantees exact physical consistency by construction while remaining computationally efficient in ill-posed settings where initial and boundary conditions are unknown.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse density and velocity measurements (e.g., from 37 weather radar stations at 5-minute intervals and 200 m altitude bins) or, in the optimal transport setting, only endpoint densities with a known cost functional. It outputs continuous density ρ(t,x) and velocity v(t,x) fields, plus Lagrangian flow maps X_t(x) that directly yield particle trajectories without additional numerical integration. A key structural property is that evaluating both ρ and v requires only the forward pass of the time-conditioned bijection Φ_t, never its inverse, which is critical for layers like invertible densenets whose inversion is done via fixed-point iteration.

## Method

LFlows exploit the classical result that continuity-equation solutions correspond to pushforwards of an initial density by a Lagrangian flow map, and reparameterize this by modeling the density at every time as a change-of-variables transform of a fixed unnormalized Gaussian base density through a single time-conditioned diffeomorphism Φ_t. The velocity is derived analytically as v(t,x) = −[JΦ_t(x)]⁻¹ ∂Φ_t/∂t(x), requiring only a Jacobian inversion rather than inverting the full bijection, which enables the use of expressive Lipschitz-constrained invertible densenets with free-form Jacobians. The architecture stacks conditional i-DenseNet blocks (depth 3–5, sinusoidal activations, Lip < 1) with activation normalizations and conditional SVD layers (orthogonal components via Householder reflections), all conditioned on a time embedding from a residual MLP with swish activations. Training minimizes a data-fidelity loss on observed densities (and optionally velocities or OT cost) plus a total-mass penalty, with no PDE residual or ODE solver involved.

## Results

On the simulated 2D/3D fluid flow benchmark, LFlows achieve the highest average R² for density prediction across both dimensions, while PINNs degrade severely in 3D due to insufficient collocation points. All CE-enforcing methods (LFlows, SLDA, DFNNs) achieve a physical consistency sMAPE on the order of 1e-4 or lower, whereas PINNs show measurably higher inconsistency. In the dynamical optimal transport experiment, LFlows produce W₂² estimates closest to the discrete OT solver reference, while DFNNs significantly overestimate and the minimax ICNN underestimates the distance. On the real-world bird migration task, LFlows achieve the lowest test density MSE among all methods, with DFNNs and SLDA nearly competitive but at substantially higher computational cost (peak VRAM and per-epoch runtime).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DFNNs (Richter-Powell et al., 2022), LFlows win on predictive accuracy in the simulated fluid flow (higher R² in 2D and 3D) and bird migration (lower MSE), while DFNNs and SLDA are roughly comparable to LFlows in the OT setting where LFlows' W₂² estimates are closest to the discrete reference. Against PINNs (Raissi et al., 2019), LFlows dominate on both accuracy and physical consistency: PINNs are competitive in 2D fluid flow but degrade in 3D, and in bird migration PINNs produce inconsistent density-velocity pairs (higher sMAPE) with the highest peak memory usage due to 100k collocation points. Against SLDA (implemented via the neural adjoint of Chen et al., 2018), LFlows match or slightly exceed density accuracy while avoiding the increasing per-epoch runtime caused by ODE stiffness and the need for repeated network evaluations. Against the minimax ICNN OT baseline (Makkuva et al., 2020), LFlows produce W₂² estimates much closer to the discrete reference, whereas the minimax formulation systematically underestimates the transport cost. Overall, LFlows are the only method that simultaneously achieves top density accuracy, near-zero physical inconsistency, and the lowest computational overhead (memory and training time) across all three experimental settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

LFlows establish a direct theoretical link between conditional Normalizing Flows and Lagrangian flow maps for the continuity equation, showing that the time-evolved density is exactly the NF change-of-variables formula applied to a fixed base density. This departs from PINNs (which enforce the PDE via collocation penalties), DFNNs (which use divergence-free parameterization requiring expensive higher-order derivatives), and SLDA (which relies on numerical ODE solvers with adjoint backpropagation). The closest concurrent work, TIPF (Li et al., 2023), also uses Lagrangian flow maps but targets well-posed problems with known initial conditions, whereas LFlows handle ill-posed data assimilation with unknown initial densities and sparse, noisy observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enforcing the continuity equation by construction rather than via penalty, LFlows eliminate a class of physically inconsistent predictions and remove the need for expensive numerical solvers or second-order derivatives, making them practical for 3D problems and real-world applications like bird migration modeling. Key limitations inherited from Normalizing Flows include topological constraints on disconnected density modes and dependence on the expressive power of bijective layers. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-conservative systems (e.g., with sources/sinks), how it scales to very high-dimensional domains, and whether the total-mass penalty can be replaced by more principled priors for truly ill-posed settings.
