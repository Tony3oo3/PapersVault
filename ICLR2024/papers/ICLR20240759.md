## Problem

Diffusion models for inverse problems are highly sensitive to the choice of variance schedule, which controls the noise-injection dynamics and typically requires application-specific hyperparameter search that is time-consuming and suboptimal. While Kingma et al. (2023) showed that schedules can be learned during training for unconditioned generation, extending this to the conditioned case (where the schedule must depend on the input data while remaining monotonic in time) introduces non-trivial technical challenges that had not been addressed.

## Input / Output

The model takes a conditioning signal x (a diffraction-limited microscopy image, a pair of defocused intensity images at ±d, or a low-resolution image) and produces multiple samples from the conditional distribution p(y|x), from which a reconstruction and pixel-wise uncertainty estimate (variance across samples) are derived. A key design choice is that the learned schedule is pixel-wise: each spatial location in the output receives its own schedule function, enabling the model to allocate different noise-injection rates to structurally complex versus simple regions. The architecture accepts inputs at arbitrary resolutions without retraining, thanks to the separation of the time-dependent and data-dependent components of the schedule.

## Method

CVDM extends Variational Diffusion Models to the conditioned case by factoring the noise rate as β(t,x) = τ_θ(t)·λ_φ(x), where τ_θ is a positive time-dependent function and λ_φ is a positive data-dependent function, yielding γ(t,x) = exp(−λ_φ(x)·ρ_χ(t)) with ρ_χ a monotonic-convolutional network that guarantees monotonicity in t. The ODE constraint ∂γ/∂t = −β·γ is enforced via a Deep Galerkin Method term in the loss, and boundary conditions γ(0,x)=1, γ(1,x)=0 are added as soft penalties. A critical novel regularization term L_γ = E_t[‖γ″(t,x)‖²] prevents the schedule from collapsing to a degenerate step function, which the authors prove is necessary because the discrete-time diffusion loss converges to its continuous-time limit at a rate dependent on the schedule's second derivative. The full loss combines the ELBO (prior KL + noise-prediction diffusion loss), the ODE constraint, and the curvature regularization, optimized end-to-end with a standard noise-prediction U-Net that receives the learned schedule as direct input.

## Results

On the BioSR super-resolution microscopy benchmark, CVDM achieves the best resolution across all four biological structures (96 nm for CCP, 152 nm for ER, 97 nm for MT, 98 nm for F-actin), outperforming CDDPM by 4.42% and DFCAN by 26.27% on average. On the quantitative phase imaging (QPI) synthetic HCOCO dataset, CVDM achieves MS-SSIM of 0.943 and MAE of 0.073, surpassing US-TIE (0.907 / 0.110) and CDDPM (0.881 / 0.134). On real clinical brightfield microscopy images, CVDM consistently outperforms both baselines across all three samples (e.g., MS-SSIM 0.892 vs. 0.740 for US-TIE on sample 1). On ImageNet 1K super-resolution, CVDM matches Saharia et al. (2021) without any schedule fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DFCAN (Qiao et al., 2021), CVDM wins decisively on the resolution metric across all BioSR structures (e.g., 96 vs. 107 nm for CCP, 98 vs. 151 nm for F-actin) while matching or slightly trailing on MS-SSIM for simpler structures. Against CDDPM (Saharia et al., 2021), CVDM improves resolution by 4–26% depending on structure, wins on MS-SSIM for the more complex ER and F-actin structures, and dramatically outperforms on QPI (MAE 0.073 vs. 0.134, a 45.5% reduction). Against US-TIE (Zhang et al., 2020), CVDM achieves a 50.6% MAE reduction (0.073 vs. 0.110) and 3.9% MS-SSIM gain (0.943 vs. 0.907) on synthetic QPI, and extends this lead to real clinical brightfield images where US-TIE degrades significantly. Against Kawar et al. (2022) on ImageNet super-resolution, CVDM is slightly better without requiring any schedule hyperparameter tuning, demonstrating that the learned schedule generalizes across tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Variational Diffusion Models (VDMs) from Kingma et al. (2023), which learned the variance schedule during training for unconditioned generation, to the conditioned setting required for inverse problems. It also adapts the conditioned denoising diffusion framework of Saharia et al. (2021) by replacing the fixed schedule with a learned, pixel-wise schedule parameterized via a separation-of-variables decomposition β(t,x) = τ_θ(t)λ_φ(x). A key technical departure from Kingma et al. is the proof that the discrete-to-continuous convergence rate of the diffusion loss depends on the second derivative of the schedule, motivating a novel L_γ regularization term absent from the original VDM formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that the standard practice of hyperparameter-searching the variance schedule for each application can be replaced by learning it end-to-end, reducing engineering overhead and potentially yielding better solutions. The theoretical result that continuous-time schedule invariance breaks under discretization is a useful correction to the VDM literature and explains why schedule choice still matters in practice. Open questions include whether the separation-of-variables assumption (β = τ(t)λ(x)) is sufficient for all inverse problems, whether pixel-wise schedules scale to very high-resolution images, and whether the method's advantage over well-tuned fixed schedules persists as model capacity increases.
