## Problem

Bayesian uncertainty quantification methods for deep learning (MCMC, sampling-based variational inference, Bayesian dropout) carry substantially higher computational cost than standard training, limiting their practical adoption. Single-pass uncertainty methods often require significant architectural or training modifications. This paper asks whether a principled Bayesian last layer can be trained with a sampling-free, mini-batch-compatible objective at negligible additional cost over standard neural network training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242121/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any standard neural network input (RGB images at 32×32 for CIFAR, text embeddings from a frozen OPT-175B model, tabular UCI features, or 2D state vectors for bandits) and produces full predictive distributions: Gaussian mean and variance for regression, class probabilities with epistemic uncertainty for classification, and feature-density-based OOD scores. The last layer operates on learned features φ of dimension N_φ from an arbitrary backbone, with computational complexity at most O(N_y N_φ²) (reducible to O(N_y N_φ) with diagonal covariances), matching standard network evaluation cost.

## Method

The paper derives deterministic, sampling-free variational lower bounds on the marginal likelihood for three Bayesian last layer models (Gaussian regression, discriminative logistic classification, and generative Gaussian discriminant classification), enabling standard mini-batch gradient descent training without any Monte Carlo sampling of last-layer parameters. The key insight is that by marginalizing the last layer analytically (exploiting conjugacy) and only sampling feature weights when using variational features, the training objective is Rao-Blackwellized, yielding lower-variance gradients than full sampling-based SVI. Three training strategies are provided: joint optimization of features and last layer, post-training on frozen features, and collapsed variational inference combining last-layer SVI with variational feature learning. The generative classification model yields quadratic decision boundaries (a strict generalization of linear classifiers), and the entire VBLL module adds only a small number of covariance parameters and closed-form regularization terms to any existing architecture.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, D-VBLL achieves 96.4% accuracy (vs. 95.7% for SNGP and 95.8% for DNN) with competitive OOD detection (SVHN AUC 0.969 vs. 0.960 for SNGP) and NLL of 0.160. On CIFAR-100, D-VBLL reaches 80.7% accuracy (vs. 80.3% SNGP, 80.4% DNN) with SVHN AUC 0.849. In the wheel bandit task, VBLL achieves cumulative regret of 4.82 at δ=0.95, roughly halving the regret of the best baseline (NeuralLinear-MR at 9.56), and 24.44 at δ=0.99 versus 49.63 for NeuralLinear-MR. On UCI regression, VBLL is competitive with baselines like BBB and Dropout, though full variational feature learning (VBLL BBB) shows mixed results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-VBLL outperforms SNGP on CIFAR-10 accuracy (96.4% vs. 95.7%) and far-OOD detection (SVHN AUC 0.969 vs. 0.960), but trails SNGP on ECE (0.022 vs. 0.017) and NLL (0.160 vs. 0.149); on CIFAR-100, D-VBLL again beats SNGP on accuracy (80.7% vs. 80.3%) and SVHN OOD (0.849 vs. 0.846) while losing on ECE and NLL. D-VBLL clearly dominates BBB on all classification metrics (e.g., CIFAR-10 NLL 0.160 vs. 0.333, CIFAR-100 accuracy 80.7% vs. 79.6%), and the combined D-VBLL BBB model degrades substantially in OOD detection (SVHN AUC drops to 0.832). In the wheel bandit, VBLL achieves roughly half the cumulative regret of NeuralLinear-MR across all δ values (e.g., 4.82 vs. 9.56 at δ=0.95; 24.44 vs. 49.63 at δ=0.99), indicating superior exploration. On UCI regression, VBLL is competitive with BBB and Dropout but does not uniformly dominate; the paper attributes this to the variational approach's robustness to model misspecification versus exact marginal likelihood training (GBLL), which can over-concentrate. The G-VBLL model underperforms D-VBLL in OOD detection, particularly as a post-training method (CIFAR-10 SVHN AUC drops to 0.729), suggesting the generative formulation is more sensitive to feature quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian last layer (BLL) framework from Harrison et al. (2018, 2020) by replacing exact marginal likelihood training with deterministic, sampling-free variational lower bounds derived via stochastic variational inference (Hoffman et al., 2013). Prior BLL methods (deep kernel learning, few-shot meta-learning) required full-dataset passes or logit sampling, making them impractical at scale; here, the authors show that careful parameterization yields mini-batch-compatible losses at quadratic complexity in last-layer width. The collapsed variational inference strategy (combining last-layer SVI with Blundell et al.'s variational features) is a Rao-Blackwellized extension that reduces gradient variance without full-network sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: VBLLs add only 5–13% training overhead on CIFAR-10 while improving calibration, OOD detection, and predictive accuracy across regression, image classification, and contextual bandits, making Bayesian uncertainty nearly free to bolt onto any standard architecture. A notable open question is how to simultaneously achieve well-calibrated label predictions and effective OOD scores from the same last-layer posterior, since the MAP noise covariance that improves OOD detection yields over-confident labels. The authors also flag few-shot adaptation via recursive Bayesian least squares and the interaction between optimizer choice (SGD vs. AdamW) and uncertainty representation as important future directions.
