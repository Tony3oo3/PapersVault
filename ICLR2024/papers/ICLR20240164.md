## Problem

Post hoc privacy auditing of differentially private models can only establish lower bounds on privacy loss, requires the auditor to receive intermediate model updates and training data (compromising confidentiality), and demands prohibitive computational resources (millions of retraining runs or large sample sizes). No existing method allows a third-party auditor to verify the exact (ε,δ)-DP guarantee of a DP-SGD training run while preserving the confidentiality of the training data, model parameters, and randomness.

## Input / Output

The prover commits to a training dataset (X, Y) and the entire DP-SGD training procedure is encoded as a single public arithmetic circuit with fixed hyperparameters (clipping norm C, learning rate η, noise multiplier σ, loss function L) and a privately generated randomness seed s. The circuit's output is the final model parameters W^T, and the ZK proof π certifies that the circuit was evaluated correctly with the agreed seed. The auditor's output is a binary Accept/Reject decision, from which the certified (ε,δ)-DP guarantee follows via a public privacy accounting technique (moments accountant).

## Method

Confidential-DPproof is a three-phase interactive ZK protocol co-designed with DP-SGD: (1) data commitment via IT-MACs binds the prover to the dataset without revealing it; (2) an unbiased randomness seed s = k ⊕ r is generated interactively (prover commits to k, auditor picks r and signs H([k], r)), preventing either party from biasing the subsampling or noise randomness; (3) the full DP-SGD training—per-example gradient computation, L2 clipping, Box-Muller noise sampling, and parameter updates over T iterations—is encoded as a circuit and proven correct in ZK using EMP-toolkit. The key design insight is that all randomness (minibatch subsampling and Gaussian noise) is derived from the single unbiased seed s and verified inside the circuit, so the prover cannot cherry-pick favorable random values while the auditor never learns the seed. The protocol is instantiated on feature-extracted representations (SimCLR/ResNeXt for CIFAR-10, ScatterNet for MNIST) with a logistic regression head, achieving 91% accuracy at (ε=0.55, δ=10⁻⁵)-DP in ~100 hours.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Confidential-DPproof achieves 80% test accuracy with a certified (ε=0.18, δ=10⁻⁵)-DP guarantee in 4 hours, and 91% test accuracy with ε=0.55 in approximately 100 hours (225 DP-SGD iterations). On MNIST, it reaches 88% accuracy with ε=0.28 in 25 hours and 98% accuracy with ε=0.74 in 100 hours (55 iterations). The per-example gradient computation + clipping takes 0.324 s at 1024 attributes and 3.068 s at 8192 attributes, while per-minibatch noise sampling takes 1.087 s and 9.385 s respectively, demonstrating linear scaling with the number of attributes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper introduces a novel cryptographic protocol and does not run head-to-head comparisons against competing methods in a shared benchmark. Instead, it positions itself qualitatively against existing post-hoc privacy auditing approaches (Jagielski et al. 2020; Nasr et al. 2021, 2023; Lu et al. 2022; Zanella-Beguelin et al. 2023), which can only establish lower bounds on privacy loss, require access to intermediate model updates or training data, and incur prohibitive computational costs (e.g., training models millions of times). Confidential-DPproof addresses all three limitations simultaneously by providing an exact upper-bound certificate via ZKP without revealing any training data or model parameters. The paper also briefly demonstrates extensibility to DP-FTRL (Table 5), showing that the framework generalizes beyond DP-SGD with modest additional overhead (noise sampling increases from 0.519 s to 3.460 s per minibatch due to the tree structure). No direct accuracy or privacy-budget comparison against other DP training methods (e.g., Tramer & Boneh 2021, De et al. 2022) is performed, as the contribution is the verification protocol rather than a new training algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of differential privacy and zero-knowledge proofs, departing fundamentally from the post-hoc empirical auditing paradigm (Jagielski et al., 2020; Nasr et al., 2021, 2023) that can only establish lower bounds on privacy loss. It builds on DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) as the training algorithm to be verified, and on recent interactive ZK protocols (Weng et al., 2021a; Yang et al., 2021) and IT-MAC-based commitments (Franzese et al., 2021) as cryptographic primitives. The key conceptual departure is the co-design of the DP training algorithm with a tailored ZK protocol—handling randomness via an interactive seed commitment (Algorithm 2) and noise generation via Box-Muller in ZK—rather than applying a generic ZK compiler to an off-the-shelf DP implementation. Prior ZK-for-ML work (Weng et al., 2021b; Shamsabadi et al., 2022) verified inference or fairness of decision trees, but no prior work provides a ZK certificate for the correctness of an entire DP-SGD training run.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Confidential-DPproof shifts privacy verification from an adversarial, computationally expensive empirical process to a proactive cryptographic certification, closing the gap between the theoretical (ε,δ)-DP upper bound and what an auditor can actually confirm. This is particularly impactful for regulated industries (healthcare, finance) where institutions must demonstrate compliance without revealing proprietary models or training data. Key limitations include the reliance on feature extractors plus logistic regression rather than end-to-end deep network training, the ~100-hour proof generation time, and the inability to certify that no adversarial data manipulation occurred before commitment. Open questions include scaling the ZK protocol to complex architectures, integrating with federated learning settings, and extending to hidden-state privacy accounting techniques.
