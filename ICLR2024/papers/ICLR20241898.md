## Problem

The dominant paradigm for LLM-based task planning—iteratively prompting an LLM to generate one action at a time—suffers from two compounding inefficiencies: prompt tokens (instructions, environment description, in-context examples) are re-consumed at every timestep, inflating cost, and error correction is either too local (missing earlier mistakes) or too global (regenerating the entire plan). This limits scalability for large-scale deployment and frequent real-world use.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-level task description (e.g., 'Take nap'), a partial observation of the environment rendered as English sentences describing object states and inter-object predicates (only objects in the current room and not inside enclosed containers are visible), and a fixed action-space specification of 28 typed actions with 1–3 object arguments. At each grounded-deciding step, the LLM does not freely generate an action but instead selects one from the child nodes of the current node in the pre-built action tree, constraining the output to a small candidate set. The final output is a sequence of mid-level skills (e.g., [Walk] <bedroom>(1), [Find] <bed>(1), [Lie] <bed>(1)) executed in a closed loop with the VirtualHome simulator.

## Method

TREE-PLANNER decomposes LLM-based planning into three phases: (1) a single plan-sampling call where the LLM generates N diverse candidate plans (temperature 0.8, top-p 0.95) using its commonsense knowledge, incurring the full prompt cost (global info + in-context examples) only once; (2) an action-tree construction step that aggregates sampled plans by merging shared prefixes into a single tree, converting plan-level filtering into action-level search; (3) multiple grounded-deciding calls where the LLM, given only the current observation and execution history, selects among the child nodes of the current tree node. Error correction operates on the tree: a failed action marks its subtree invalid, the system backtracks to the nearest valid fork node, executes inverse actions to restore state, and re-decides—intermediate between LOCAL REPLAN (retry at current step) and GLOBAL REPLAN (regenerate from scratch). A formal token-efficiency analysis derives a critical sampling bound (N < 197.72 for their setup) guaranteeing that TREE-PLANNER is cheaper than ITERATIVE-PLANNER, and experiments show 92.2% token reduction and 40.5% fewer corrections versus the best baselines.

## Results

On VirtualHome, TREE-PLANNER (N=50) achieves the best success rate of 41.58% and goal conditions recall of 59.55% under the with-correction setting, while TREE-PLANNER (N=25) achieves the highest executability of 89.13%. In terms of efficiency, TREE-PLANNER (N=25) reduces token cost by 92.24% compared to GLOBAL REPLAN ($3.30 vs. $42.55) and by 74.36% compared to LOCAL REPLAN ($3.30 vs. $12.88). Error corrections are reduced by 40.52% (1.85 vs. 3.43) relative to GLOBAL REPLAN and 37.99% (1.85 vs. 3.29) relative to LOCAL REPLAN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Zero-Shot Planner (Huang et al., ICML 2022), TREE-PLANNER (N=50) improves success rate from 1.07% to 41.58% (with correction) and executability from 16.49% to 88.26%, a dramatic gain of over 40 points on SR. Against ProgPrompt (Singh et al., 2022), TREE-PLANNER (N=50) raises SR from 12.54% to 41.58% and GCR from 19.99% to 59.55% under the with-correction setting. Compared to the Iterative-Planner baseline (the standard autoregressive paradigm), TREE-PLANNER (N=25) cuts token cost by 53.29% ($2.39 vs. $5.12) while simultaneously improving SR from 27.04% to 28.33% and GCR from 33.25% to 39.96% without correction. Under the with-correction setting, TREE-PLANNER (N=25) outperforms both Local Replan and Global Replan on executability (89.13% vs. 79.66% and 82.09%) and GCR (56.65% vs. 51.90% and 52.46%), while TREE-PLANNER (N=50) achieves the highest SR (41.58% vs. 37.46% and 37.93%). The efficiency advantage is most pronounced against Global Replan, where token cost drops by 92.24% and error corrections decrease by 40.52%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant ITERATIVE-PLANNER paradigm (generate one action per LLM call, execute, observe, repeat) by reframing closed-loop task planning into three decoupled phases: plan sampling, action tree construction, and grounded deciding. Conceptually, it repurposes the tree-of-thought idea (Yao et al., 2023) from reasoning tasks to embodied action planning, and borrows the generate-then-select paradigm (Glass et al., 2022; Suzgun et al., 2022) but applies it at the action level rather than the full-plan level. The action tree serves as a structural prior that enables selective backtracking, positioning error correction as an intermediate between LOCAL REPLAN and GLOBAL REPLAN. The POMDP formulation follows Li et al. (2022a), and the efficiency motivation aligns with batch prompting (Cheng et al., 2023) and other efficient-inference work, but the contribution is purely architectural—no additional training is required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 92.2% token reduction over the best correction baseline is practically significant for anyone deploying LLM-based planners at scale, and the formal derivation of the critical N threshold provides a useful design guideline. However, the evaluation is confined to a single simulated environment (VirtualHome), a single LLM (GPT-3.5), and a small dataset of 35 tasks across 4 scenes, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more complex or real-world settings. The error analysis reveals that plan sampling remains the primary bottleneck (54.5% of failures), and the finding that increasing N does not consistently improve performance suggests a fundamental tension between coverage and precision that the current framework does not resolve. Future work could explore adaptive N selection, integration with learned world models, or extension to multi-agent and long-horizon settings.
