## Problem

Existing work on information acquisition via scoring rules is limited to single-agent settings, where a principal hires one agent to gather costly information. Extending to multiple agents introduces externalities: agents' payments depend on others' signals, creating complex incentive compatibility constraints and equilibrium selection problems when mechanisms are not IC. The paper addresses both the offline problem of computing an optimal IC mechanism and the online problem of repeatedly interacting with unknown agents without prior knowledge of the game parameters.

## Input / Output

In the offline setting, the algorithm takes as input the full game parameters (joint signal distributions P(s,θ|b), cost functions c_i, utility u(a,θ), budget M) and outputs an optimal correlated IC mechanism (μ, γ, π) where μ is a distribution over action profiles, γ is a scoring rule mapping (b,s,θ)→[0,M], and π is the principal's action policy. In the online setting, the principal additionally requires a set of strictly IC scoring rules Γ (one per agent-action pair) and a confidence level δ; the output is a sequence of IC mechanisms over T rounds achieving Õ(T^{2/3}) regret. The principal observes agents' actions, reported signals, and the state of nature each round, but never observes the true signals received by agents.

## Method

For the offline problem, the paper formulates mechanism design as a quadratic program and provides a polynomial-time LP relaxation by introducing product variables x_i[b,s,θ]=μ[b]·γ_i[b,s,θ] and y[b,s,a]=μ[b]·π[b,s,a], reducing the exponential number of IC constraints to polynomial ones via auxiliary upper-bound variables z_i. Theorem 3.1 guarantees that dividing x_i by μ recovers an optimal mechanism. For the online problem, the algorithm proceeds in three phases: (1) ESTIMATEPROB commits to strictly IC scoring rules from Γ to collect samples estimating joint distributions and posteriors with Hoeffding confidence bounds; (2) ESTIMATECOSTS uses a binary search over convex combinations of scoring rules to estimate pairwise cost differences, with a split phase handling cases where agents deviate to unexpected actions; (3) COMMIT solves the LP with estimated parameters and takes a convex combination between the approximately optimal scoring rule and two strictly IC uncorrelated scoring rules to guarantee IC under estimation uncertainty. A key design choice is using uncorrelated mechanisms during exploration (to avoid unpredictable multi-agent equilibria) and correlated IC mechanisms during commitment (to achieve near-optimal utility).

## Results

The paper provides two main theoretical guarantees. First, an optimal incentive-compatible mechanism for the multi-agent information acquisition problem can be computed in polynomial time via a linear programming relaxation (Corollary 3.2). Second, the online algorithm (Algorithm 1) achieves Õ(T^{2/3}) cumulative regret with respect to an optimal mechanism, matching the state-of-the-art bound for single-agent settings (Chen et al., 2023). The regret bound holds with probability at least 1−δ and depends on instance parameters M, ρ, ℓ, |B|, |S|, m, n, k, l through the factor (M³/(ρℓ))|B||S|mnk³l²√(ln(1/δ)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or baseline comparisons. The only quantitative comparison is to the single-agent online information acquisition result of Chen et al. (2023), which achieves Õ(T^{2/3}) regret for one agent. The present paper extends this bound to the multi-agent setting (n agents, correlated signals, correlated mechanisms), showing that the same Õ(T^{2/3}) regret rate is achievable despite the added complexity of coordinating multiple agents' efforts and handling externalities introduced by correlated scoring rules. The paper also establishes that uncorrelated mechanisms (where each agent's payment depends only on her own signal) can be strictly suboptimal compared to correlated mechanisms (Theorem 4.1), but become optimal when signals are independent across agents (Theorem 4.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent online information acquisition framework of Chen et al. (2023), which achieved Õ(T^{2/3}) regret for a principal hiring one agent, to the multi-agent setting where a principal simultaneously hires n agents to gather correlated information. The key technical departure is the need to handle externalities among agents: optimal mechanisms must correlate agents' efforts, introducing complex incentive compatibility constraints and equilibrium selection problems absent in the single-agent case. The paper also draws on the broader principal-agent and contract design literature (Dutting et al., 2019; Zhu et al., 2023) and online Bayesian persuasion (Castiglioni et al., 2021; Bernasconi et al., 2022), but is the first computational work to address scoring rules with multiple agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first polynomial-time algorithm for optimal multi-agent mechanism design and the first no-regret online algorithm for the multi-agent setting, matching the single-agent state-of-the-art regret bound despite the added coordination complexity. The characterization of when correlated versus uncorrelated mechanisms are optimal (Theorems 4.1–4.2) offers practical guidance: uncorrelated mechanisms suffice when signals are independent, avoiding the equilibrium-selection pathology of non-IC correlated mechanisms. Open questions include whether the constant-n assumption can be relaxed, whether the Õ(T^{2/3}) bound is tight for the multi-agent case, and how the framework extends to settings where agents have heterogeneous information structures or the principal's utility is not fully known.
