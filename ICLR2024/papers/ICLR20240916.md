## Problem

Existing fairness toolkits in machine learning support only a limited range of fairness definitions (typically those with linear statistics) and have seen little integration with automatic differentiation libraries like PyTorch, despite these libraries being central to modern ML pipelines. This paper addresses the gap by formalizing a unified framework of differentiable fairness regularization terms that can handle the broader class of linear-fractional statistics and be plugged directly into standard training loops as a loss term.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a feature vector X ∈ R^{d_x}, a sensitive feature vector S ∈ R^{d_s} (which may be one-hot encoded for categorical traits, continuous for variables like age, or multi-dimensional to encode multiple non-exclusive sensitive traits simultaneously), and a binary label Y ∈ {0,1}. The classifier h: R^{d_x} → (0,1) outputs a probability score, and the FAIRRET R_γ(h) outputs a non-negative scalar quantifying unfairness. For projection FAIRRETS, an additional convex optimization (solved via cvxpy in ~10 iterations) is performed at each training step to compute the projection f* onto the c-fixed fair set, with c set to the current model's overall statistic γ̄(h).

## Method

The FAIRRET framework defines fairness through linear-fractional statistics γ(k;f) = E[S_k(α₀ + f·β₀)] / E[S_k(α₁ + f·β₁)] and enforces parity γ(k;f) = γ̄(f) across sensitive groups. Two archetypes of FAIRRETS are proposed: (1) Violation FAIRRETS (Norm, SmoothMax) that directly penalize the normalized violation vector v_k(h) = |γ(k;h)/γ̄(h) − 1|, and (2) Projection FAIRRETS (D_KL, D_JS, D_SED) that minimize the expected divergence between h and its projection f* onto the c-fixed fair set F_γ(γ̄(h)). The key technical insight (Proposition 1) is that fixing the statistic to a constant c converts the linear-fractional fairness constraint into a linear constraint, making the projection a convex optimization solvable by standard tools. The c value is set to γ̄(h) of the current model and evolves during training, avoiding the need for a meta-algorithm. The framework generalizes to multiple sensitive traits and continuous sensitive values, and all FAIRRETS are strict (zero iff the model is fair) and differentiable with respect to h.

## Results

The paper evaluates FAIRRETs (SmoothMax violation and D_KL-projection) against three FFB baselines on four datasets (Bank, CreditCard, LawSchool, ACSIncome) across four fairness notions (DP, EO, PP, TE). For linear-statistic notions (DP, EO), the D_KL-projection FAIRRET achieves the best fairness-violation-to-AUROC trade-off, clearly outperforming the SmoothMax violation FAIRRET and all baselines. For linear-fractional-statistic notions (PP, TE), all methods struggle substantially; SmoothMax is the most consistent (never worsening fairness violation) while D_KL-projection often degrades both fairness and AUROC. The FFB baselines (PRemover, HSIC, AdvDebias) consistently underperform FAIRRETs because they only optimize independence for a single categorical sensitive attribute, whereas FAIRRETs handle multiple sensitive traits simultaneously and target specific fairness statistics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines from the Fair Fairness Benchmark (FFB): PRemover (Kamishima et al., 2012), HSIC (Pérez-Suay et al., 2017), and AdvDebias (Adel et al., 2019). All three baselines are outperformed by the FAIRRET methods on both fairness violation and AUROC across all four datasets. The baselines are limited to optimizing independence between model output and a single categorical sensitive attribute, while FAIRRETs can be configured for any linear-fractional fairness statistic (DP, EO, PP, TE) and simultaneously handle multiple sensitive traits (including continuous ones). For the linear-statistic notions DP and EO, the D_KL-projection FAIRRET achieves the lowest fairness violation at comparable or better AUROC than all baselines. For the harder linear-fractional notions PP and TE, even the best FAIRRET (SmoothMax) only marginally improves over the baselines, and the D_KL-projection actually worsens both metrics in most cases — indicating that non-linear fairness constraints remain an open challenge for all in-processing regularization approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of fairness regularization—violation-based penalties (Zemel et al., 2013; Padala & Gujar, 2021) and projection-based costs (Buyl & De Bie, 2021; Alghamdi et al., 2020)—into a single modular framework (FAIRRETs) that plugs directly into PyTorch's automatic differentiation. It builds on Celis et al.'s (2019) linear-fractional statistics formalization but departs from their meta-algorithm by introducing a c-fixed variant that yields linear constraints without requiring an outer search. Unlike existing toolkits (Fairlearn, AIF360) that require scikit-learn estimators or full training-loop control, FAIRRETs act as a simple loss term added to the cross-entropy objective, and unlike FairTorch or FFB, they support a formally defined, extensible class of fairness notions beyond a fixed menu.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework makes it practical to enforce fairness notions with linear-fractional statistics (Predictive Parity, Treatment Equality) during training—previously largely ignored in the regularization literature—while also supporting multiple sensitive traits and continuous sensitive variables. However, all methods (FAIRRETs and baselines alike) struggle substantially with these non-linear fairness notions, revealing a significant open problem: how to reliably optimize fairness constraints that are inherently non-convex in the classifier. A key limitation is that fairness is enforced per axis of discrimination, so intersectional groups (e.g., 'Black woman') are not explicitly protected, and the c-fixing sacrifices a degree of generality in the fair classifier set.
