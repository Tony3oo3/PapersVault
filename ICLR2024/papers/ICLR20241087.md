## Problem

Masked time series modeling (MTM) methods like PatchTST and SimMTM train Transformers to predict masked patches from unmasked ones, implicitly learning inter-patch dependencies. This paper argues that capturing such dependencies is suboptimal for time series representation learning, and that a patch-independent strategy—both in the pretraining task and the encoder architecture—yields better representations with far fewer parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241087/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multivariate time series (e.g., 7-channel ETT, 862-channel Traffic), applies per-instance normalization (zero mean, unit std) before splitting into fixed-size patches (P ∈ {12, 18, 24}). Each patch is embedded independently by a shared 2-layer MLP into a D-dimensional vector (D ∈ {32, 64, 128}), and the second-layer representation z₂ is used for downstream tasks. For forecasting, a linear projection head maps z₂ to future values at horizons 96–720; for classification, patch embeddings are aggregated (max, avg, or concat) and passed to a classifier.

## Method

PITS replaces the conventional patch-dependent (PD) masked modeling with a patch-independent (PI) strategy on two axes. First, the pretraining task autoencodes each unmasked patch independently (reconstructing x_p from its own embedding z₂ via a linear head) rather than predicting masked patches from unmasked context. Second, the encoder is a simple 2-layer MLP with ReLU that processes each patch in isolation, eliminating self-attention entirely. To capture adjacent temporal information without inter-patch attention, PITS introduces complementary contrastive learning: two views are generated by complementary 50% random masking, and a hierarchical CL loss (inspired by TS2Vec) is computed at the first MLP layer by repeatedly max-pooling patch embeddings along the temporal axis. The total loss is the sum of the reconstruction loss and the hierarchical contrastive loss, and dropout (rate 0.2) before the projection head prevents the trivial identity-mapping solution when D > P.

## Results

On multivariate time series forecasting across 7 datasets (ETTh1/h2, ETTm1/m2, Weather, Traffic, Electricity) and 4 horizons (96–720), PITS achieves an average MSE of 0.301 under fine-tuning, outperforming PatchTST (0.314) and SimMTM (0.306) in the self-supervised setting, while using only 5,772 parameters versus PatchTST's 406,028. On the SleepEEG classification task, PITS reaches 95.67% accuracy and 95.64 F1, surpassing SimMTM (94.75% ACC) and TF-C (93.96% ACC). In cross-domain transfer learning (e.g., SleepEEG→EMG), PITS achieves 100% accuracy, far exceeding the next-best SimMTM (97.56%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PITS outperforms PatchTST on average across all three evaluation settings (FT: 0.301 vs 0.314, LP: 0.306 vs 0.320, Sup.: 0.304 vs 0.307 MSE), though PatchTST wins on ETTh2 supervised (0.331 vs 0.337) and Traffic supervised (0.396 vs 0.401). Against SimMTM (a concurrent work), PITS achieves lower average MSE in the self-supervised setting (0.301 vs 0.306) and substantially lower in supervised (0.304 vs 0.343). On classification, PITS (95.67% ACC on SleepEEG) beats all contrastive learning baselines including TF-C (93.96%), TS2Vec (92.17%), and CoST (88.07%), as well as the MTM-based SimMTM (94.75%). In cross-domain transfer (SleepEEG→EMG), PITS achieves perfect 100% accuracy versus SimMTM's 97.56% and TF-C's 78.90%. PITS also outperforms supervised-only baselines DLinear (0.343 avg MSE) and TSMixer (0.332) in the self-supervised setting, while using roughly 70× fewer parameters than PatchTST (5,772 vs 406,028) and training ~5× faster (15 min vs 77 min pretraining).

## Relation to Prior Work

PITS directly departs from the dominant masked time series modeling (MTM) paradigm established by PatchTST, SimMTM, and TimeMAE, which all rely on patch-dependent tasks (predicting masked patches from unmasked ones) and patch-dependent architectures (Transformers with self-attention). The paper's core conceptual move is to replace both: the pretraining task becomes patch-wise autoencoding (reconstructing unmasked patches without looking at others), and the encoder becomes a simple two-layer MLP that processes each patch independently. The hierarchical contrastive learning component adapts the max-pooling-based multi-scale CL from TS2Vec, while the overall design is informed by the linear-model literature (DLinear, TSMixer) showing that inter-patch attention may be unnecessary for forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in time series SSL—that capturing inter-patch dependencies via attention is essential for good representations—and shows that a 70x smaller model (5.7K vs. 406K parameters) can match or slightly exceed Transformer-based SOTA on standard benchmarks. This has practical implications for deployment in resource-constrained settings and for robustness under distribution shift, where the PI approach shows consistent advantages. Open questions include whether the findings generalize to very long sequences, non-stationary regimes beyond the tested ETT/Weather/Traffic/Electricity benchmarks, or tasks where genuine long-range cross-patch dependencies are critical (e.g., multi-scale anomaly detection).
