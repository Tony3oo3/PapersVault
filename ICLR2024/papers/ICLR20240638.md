## Problem

Existing retraining-free structured pruning algorithms for encoder-based PLMs suffer severe accuracy degradation at high compression rates (e.g., 80%) because their one-shot pruning process fails to handle accumulated pruning errors across multiple layers. The paper asks how to accurately compress pretrained encoder-based language models without the expensive retraining that retraining-based methods require.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240638/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fine-tuned encoder-based PLM (BERT or DistilBERT), a small sample dataset of only 100K tokens (~0.64% of MNLI), and a target FLOPs budget. It produces a compressed model with specific attention heads and neurons removed, along with re-tuned output projection weights for the remaining units. The entire pruning process is retraining-free: the only optimization step is a per-sublayer linear least-squares weight-tuning that completes in under one second, requiring no hyperparameter tuning or full-dataset training.

## Method

K-prune performs iterative, sublayer-wise pruning from bottom to top, with each iteration consisting of three steps. First, knowledge measurement estimates the predictive knowledge (via Fisher Information approximation of temperature-scaled KL divergence) and representational knowledge (via Frobenius norm of intermediate layer outputs) for every attention head and neuron in the target and all above sublayers. Second, knowledge-preserving mask search (KPMS) computes per-unit importance scores as a weighted sum of both knowledge types normalized by FLOPs, applies a balance coefficient μ to account for head-vs-neuron sensitivity differences, and selects a global threshold across all unpruned sublayers to determine which units to prune in the target sublayer. Third, knowledge-preserving weight-tuning (KPWT) prunes the selected units and solves a linear least-squares problem to re-tune the output projection weights of remaining units, minimizing representational knowledge loss. The key insight is that distributing a small amount of pruning per sublayer and reconstructing knowledge after each step prevents the unrecoverable multi-layer knowledge loss that causes one-shot methods to fail at high compression rates.

## Results

Under an 80% compression rate on SQuAD 1.1, K-prune achieves 74.16 F1 score, up to 58.02%p higher than the best existing retraining-free pruning algorithms. On MRPC at the same compression rate, K-prune reaches 84.80 accuracy. In terms of inference speed, K-prune delivers up to 2.93× acceleration over the uncompressed model (2.65× geometric mean across tasks), compared to 1.87× for Kwon et al. and 1.15× for KCM. Against retraining-based methods, K-prune achieves comparable or higher accuracy at 75% compression with up to 422× lower pruning time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the retraining-free baseline Kwon et al. (2022b), K-prune achieves substantially higher accuracy at high compression rates (up to 58.02%p F1 improvement on SQuAD at 80% pruning) and faster inference (2.65× vs. 1.87× geometric mean speedup). Against KCM (Nova et al., 2023), K-prune wins decisively on both accuracy and speed; KCM suffers drastic accuracy degradation at higher compression rates because it cannot prune attention heads, and its speedup is limited to 1.15× on average. Against the retraining-based DynaBERT (Hou et al., 2020), K-prune matches or exceeds accuracy on MNLI and QNLI at 75% compression while requiring up to 422× less pruning time (seconds vs. hours of retraining). Similarly, against EBERT (Liu et al., 2021), K-prune achieves comparable or better accuracy with dramatically lower computational cost, making it the best accuracy-cost trade-off among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

K-prune extends the retraining-free structured pruning line initiated by Kwon et al. (2022b), which introduced Fisher-information-based importance scoring and a one-shot mask search for transformers, by replacing the single-pass pruning with an iterative sublayer-wise process. The knowledge measurement step repurposes two established knowledge-distillation objectives — the temperature-scaled KL divergence from Hinton et al. (2015) for predictive knowledge and the intermediate-representation MSE from FitNets (Romero et al., 2015) for representational knowledge — as per-unit saliency metrics rather than training losses. The key conceptual departure from both retraining-free (Kwon et al., KCM) and retraining-based (DynaBERT, EBERT, CoFi) predecessors is framing pruning error as recoverable knowledge loss and distributing it across iterations, with a linear-least-squares weight-tuning step that reconstructs each sublayer's output in under a second.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 422× reduction in pruning cost while matching or exceeding retraining-based accuracy on GLUE makes high-quality structured pruning of PLMs accessible without GPU clusters or full training datasets, which is practically significant for deploying compressed models in resource-constrained settings. The ablation revealing that KPWT (weight-tuning) is by far the most critical component — removing it collapses SQuAD F1 from 74.16 to 16.50 at 80% compression — suggests that the iterative mask search alone is insufficient and that local output reconstruction is the true mechanism preventing error accumulation. Limitations include evaluation only on encoder-based models (BERT, DistilBERT) and modest pruning rates (≤20%) on decoder-based OPT models in the appendix, leaving open whether the approach scales to the 7B+ decoder LLMs where structured pruning is most needed.
