## Problem

Existing multivariate time series forecasting methods assume complete input data, while real-world sensors frequently produce partially observed series due to device malfunction or costly acquisition. The common workaround of first imputing missing values and then forecasting introduces error accumulation and decouples the two tasks. No prior architecture explicitly integrates missing-pattern awareness into both the temporal and spatial modeling components for joint forecasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241172/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a partial multivariate time series X ∈ R^{N×H×D} (N instances, H=24 history steps, D features) together with a binary mask M ∈ R^{N×H} marking observed entries, and produces F=24 future-step predictions for all N instances simultaneously via a final linear layer. Training uses a masked MAE loss that only computes error on observed future entries, so the supervision signal adapts to the missing pattern. The mask is not a static input but is progressively updated inside the network as information propagates through layers.

## Method

BiTGraph stacks L=3 Biased TCGBlocks, each containing a Multi-Scale Instance PartialTCN (temporal) followed by a Biased GCN (spatial). The PartialTCN applies instance-shared partial convolutions with kernel sizes 3, 5, and 7, computing outputs only over observed time steps and rescaling by K/sum(mask) to preserve magnitude; the mask is then updated so that any time step with at least one observed neighbor in its receptive window becomes 'filled.' The Biased GCN learns an adaptive adjacency matrix A = ReLU(tanh(E₁E₂ᵀ)) + β·B, where B is a softmax of the outer product of the current mask rows plus learnable per-node scalar biases to enforce directed information flow from well-observed to poorly-observed nodes; top-k sparsification retains only the strongest edges. After graph convolution, the mask is further updated by taking the element-wise max over a node and its neighbors, so missing patterns are progressively resolved along both temporal and spatial dimensions across the L stacked blocks.

## Results

BiTGraph achieves the best forecasting performance across all five benchmark datasets (Metr-LA, Electricity, PEMS, ETTh1, BeijingAir) at every missing rate from 0.1 to 0.8, evaluated by MAE, RMSE, and MAPE. On the Electricity dataset (highest variance), BiTGraph delivers up to 9.93% RMSE improvement over the strongest baseline (STWA_t). At r=0.1 on Metr-LA, BiTGraph achieves MAE 5.96 / RMSE 10.71 / MAPE 11.13, outperforming the next-best MTGNN_t (MAE 6.10 / RMSE 10.69 / MAPE 12.02). The performance gap widens as the missing rate increases, with BiTGraph maintaining consistent superiority even at r=0.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiTGraph outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset and missing rate tested. The strongest competitors are the imputation-assisted variants STWA_t and MTGNN_t, which use TimesNet to fill missing entries before forecasting; BiTGraph still leads them by 2–6% in MAE/RMSE, with the gap widening at higher missing rates (e.g., on Electricity at r=0.8, BiTGraph's RMSE advantage over STWA_t reaches ~9.93%). Methods designed specifically for missing values (SPIN, GRIN, GCN-M) show competitive results but are limited by their reliance on pre-defined graph structures and cannot be applied to all five datasets (SPIN and GRIN lack results on Electricity and ETTh1). Zero-filling variants (Mode₀) of Transformer-based and GNN-based methods degrade substantially compared to their imputation counterparts (Mode_t), confirming that naive imputation introduces significant error accumulation. Among the missing-value-specific methods, BRITS and CRUs consistently underperform the graph-based approaches, while GCN-M and GRIN are competitive on traffic datasets but do not generalize to Electricity or ETTh1. BiTGraph's joint temporal-spatial design with explicit missing-pattern awareness gives it a structural advantage over both the two-step imputation-then-forecast pipeline and the single-step methods that lack adaptive graph bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiTGraph sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) temporal convolution networks for sequence modeling (Bai et al., 2018), (2) adaptive graph structure learning for spatial correlation in multivariate time series (AGCRN, MTGNN), and (3) missing-value-aware modeling via partial convolutions (Liu et al., 2018) and decayed RNNs (GRU-D, BRITS). The key departure from prior work is the explicit injection of missing-pattern information as a 'bias' into both the temporal (Multi-Scale Instance PartialTCN) and spatial (Biased GCN) modules, rather than treating imputation as a separate preprocessing step or relying on decayed hidden states. The progressive mask-updating mechanism (Eqs. 5, 10) that fills the mask as information diffuses through the network is a novel architectural detail not present in prior partial-convolution or graph-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: most SOTA forecasting methods assume complete inputs, while real-world sensor data is routinely partially observed. By unifying temporal and spatial modeling with missing-value awareness in a single end-to-end architecture, BiTGraph avoids the error accumulation inherent in two-step imputation-then-forecasting pipelines. Limitations include reliance on a TCN backbone (the authors themselves note a Transformer variant would be needed for long-term forecasting), evaluation on only five datasets, and the relatively simple symmetric-then-asymmetric bias correction in the GCN. Open questions include whether the progressive mask-filling strategy generalizes to structured or sequential missingness patterns, and how the method scales to very long horizons or high-dimensional sensor networks.
