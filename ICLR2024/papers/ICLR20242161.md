## Problem

State-of-the-art off-policy RL algorithms like REDQ and DroQ achieve high sample efficiency by performing 20 critic gradient updates per environment step (UTD=20), but this incurs a large computational cost in wallclock time and energy. The paper asks whether comparable sample efficiency can be achieved at UTD=1 by modifying the network architecture rather than increasing the number of updates, thereby reducing compute by an order of magnitude.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242161/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes low-dimensional state vectors from MuJoCo and DeepMind Control Suite environments (e.g., Hopper, Walker2d, Humanoid) and outputs continuous action vectors. Transitions (s, a, r, s') are stored in a replay buffer of size 10^6; during each critic update, a batch of 256 transitions is sampled and the policy generates next actions a'~π(s'), which are concatenated with the original (s, a) pairs along the batch dimension for a single joint forward pass through the critic.

## Method

CrossQ modifies SAC in three ways: (1) it removes target networks entirely, relying on Batch Renormalization (BRN) in the critic for training stability; (2) it applies BRN to both actor and critic networks, with the critical trick of concatenating current (s,a) and next (s',a') pairs into one forward pass so that BN normalization moments are computed over a 50/50 mixture of both distributions, avoiding the out-of-distribution mismatch that would occur with a separate target network; (3) it widens critic layers to 2048 units (vs. SAC's 256). The key insight is that BatchNorm's running statistics, when estimated from a single mixed batch, naturally bridge the distributional gap between replay-buffer actions and current-policy actions, making target networks redundant. This yields a UTD=1 algorithm that matches or surpasses REDQ/DroQ (UTD=20) in sample efficiency while requiring only 5% of their gradient steps.

## Results

CrossQ (UTD=1) matches or surpasses REDQ and DroQ (UTD=20) in sample efficiency across six MuJoCo environments, while requiring 20× fewer gradient steps and achieving ~4× faster wallclock training (e.g., 1.3h vs 4.1h on Hopper, 2.2h vs 8.7h on HumanoidStandup). On the challenging Humanoid and HumanoidStandup tasks, CrossQ substantially outperforms all baselines. The method uses only 5% of the gradient steps of REDQ/DroQ while maintaining competitive or superior episodic returns, as measured by interquartile mean (IQM) and 70% quantile intervals over 10 seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against REDQ (UTD=20, 10-critic ensemble), CrossQ (UTD=1, 2 critics) matches performance on Hopper, Walker2d, and HalfCheetah, substantially outperforms it on Humanoid and HumanoidStandup, and eventually matches it on Ant (where REDQ leads early). Against DroQ (UTD=20, dropout ensemble), CrossQ matches or outperforms on all six environments with the largest gaps on the Humanoid tasks. Against vanilla SAC (UTD=1, target networks, no BatchNorm), CrossQ substantially outperforms across all environments, confirming that the architectural changes (removing target networks + adding Batch Renormalization + wider critics) drive the gains. The CrossQ(TD3) variant, which swaps SAC for TD3 as the base algorithm, also substantially surpasses standard TD3, particularly on Humanoid and HumanoidStandup, demonstrating the method's generality across off-policy actor-critic frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossQ builds directly on SAC as its base algorithm but deliberately strips away algorithmic complexity that accumulated over years of off-policy RL development. It departs from the REDQ/DroQ paradigm of high UTD ratios (20) with explicit or implicit ensembles for bias reduction, instead achieving competitive sample efficiency at UTD=1 through purely architectural changes: removing target networks, introducing Batch Renormalization in the critic, and widening critic layers. The key conceptual departure is identifying that BatchNorm—which prior work (Hiraoka et al., 2021) reported as destabilizing in off-policy RL—becomes effective when the target network is removed and a joint forward pass over both current and next state-action pairs resolves the distribution mismatch in normalization statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CrossQ offers a 4× wallclock speedup over REDQ/DroQ while matching or surpassing their sample efficiency, with a simpler implementation (a few lines on top of SAC), making it particularly attractive for real-time robot learning and compute-constrained settings. The finding that Q estimation bias does not straightforwardly predict learning performance challenges the design rationale behind ensemble-based bias reduction in REDQ/DroQ, though the paper does not fully resolve this relationship. Open questions include a theoretical explanation for why the BatchNorm + no-target-network combination stabilizes training, whether the approach extends to visual observations and real robot systems, and whether the wider critic networks (which add compute) can be made more efficient.
