## Problem

Directly applying diffusion models to 3D molecule geometry faces two fundamental obstacles: multi-modality (continuous coordinates, discrete atom types, and discretized charges must be modeled jointly) and noise sensitivity (small coordinate perturbations rapidly disrupt inter-atomic bond distances, rendering intermediate diffusion steps uninformative). Existing remedies—latent-space diffusion, decomposed modality modeling, or per-modality noise schedulers—rely on ad-hoc designs without principled guarantees on the modeling space.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes only the number of atoms as input; for conditional generation, it additionally receives target molecular property values (polarizability, HOMO/LUMO orbital energies, their gap, dipole moment, heat capacity) and the atom count. The output is a full 3D molecular geometry: continuous atom coordinates in zero-center-of-mass space, discretized atomic charges (mapped to K bins via a Gaussian-bucket scheme), and optionally discrete atom types. A distinctive feature is that sampling can be performed with any number of steps (50 to 4600+) without retraining, trading off quality for a 20× speedup at 50 steps.

## Method

GeoBFN adapts Bayesian Flow Networks to molecular geometry by performing all probabilistic reasoning in the differentiable parameter space of factorized distributions (mean/precision for continuous variables, probability simplex for discrete variables) rather than in sample space. An EGNN serves as the equivariant inter-dependency module Φ, taking the full parameter vector across all modalities and producing updated parameters, which guarantees SE(3)-invariant density and roto-translational equivariant generation (formally proven in Theorem 3.1). For continuous coordinates, the Bayesian update is a simple weighted-average of means with additive precision; for discretized charges, a Gaussian output distribution is bucketed into K bins and a 'nearest-center' sampling rule fixes a mode-redundancy artifact at bin boundaries. The unified continuous-time training loss (Eq. 19) is a weighted sum of squared errors across modalities with accuracy-scheduler weights, and because the parameter space has substantially lower variance than the sample space, the generative trajectory is smooth and any-step sampling is possible without additional training overhead.

## Results

On QM9, GeoBFN with 1,000 sampling steps achieves 90.87% molecule stability and 99.08% atom stability, surpassing the previous best (GEOLDM: 89.4% Mol Sta, 98.9% Atom Sta). On GEOM-DRUG, GeoBFN reaches 85.60% atom stability, outperforming GEOLDM (84.4%) and EDM-Bridge (82.4%). Notably, GeoBFN achieves competitive performance with only 50 sampling steps (85.11% Mol Sta on QM9), yielding a 20× speedup over the 1,000-step setting. For conditional generation on QM9, GeoBFN achieves the lowest MAE across all six molecular properties (e.g., 328 meV for ε_HOMO vs. 340 for GEOLDM and 356 for EDM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoBFN outperforms all five baselines on QM9 molecule stability, achieving 90.87% (1k steps) versus GEOLDM's 89.4%, EDM-Bridge's 84.6%, and EDM's 82.0%. On QM9 atom stability and validity, GeoBFN_1k (99.08%, 95.31%) also leads all baselines, with the closest competitor being GEOLDM (98.9%, 93.8%). On GEOM-DRUG atom stability, GeoBFN_1k (85.60%) surpasses GEOLDM (84.4%) and EDM-Bridge (82.4%), though it trails GEOLDM on DRUG validity (92.08% vs. 99.3%). For conditional generation (Table 2), GeoBFN achieves the lowest MAE on all six QM9 properties, e.g., 577 meV for Δε vs. 587 (GEOLDM) and 655 (EDM), and 0.998 D for dipole moment vs. 1.108 (GEOLDM) and 1.111 (EDM). The older autoregressive baselines (G-Schnet, ENF) are substantially outperformed across all metrics, with ENF achieving only 4.9% molecule stability on QM9.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoBFN adapts the recently introduced Bayesian Flow Networks (Graves et al., 2023) to 3D molecular geometry, replacing the dominant diffusion-model paradigm (EDM, GeoLDM, EDM-Bridge) with a parameter-space Bayesian update mechanism. The key conceptual departure is that BFNs operate on distribution parameters rather than samples, yielding a lower-variance process that naturally mitigates the noise-sensitivity problem of molecular coordinates that has motivated prior work on latent-space diffusion (GeoLDM) and decomposed modality modeling (MolDiff). The equivariant inter-dependency module uses EGNNs (Satorras et al., 2021b) to enforce SE(3) equivariance, and the zero-center-of-mass constraint follows the standard formulation from Kohler et al. (2020) and Xu et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× sampling speedup at competitive quality is practically significant for iterative molecular design workflows where thousands of candidates must be generated. The any-step sampling property and the theoretical SE(3) invariance guarantee provide both flexibility and correctness assurances that diffusion-based methods lack. However, the framework is still in its infancy (BFN itself is from mid-2023), evaluation is limited to QM9 and GEOM-DRUG, and scalability to larger biomolecular systems (proteins, complexes) or to property-conditional generation beyond QM9-scale properties remains unexplored. The finding that discretised charge alone outperforms including explicit atom types (Table 3) hints at underexplored redundancy in molecular representations that could simplify future architectures.
