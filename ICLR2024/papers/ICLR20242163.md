## Problem

Non-linear sequential models such as RNNs and NeuralODEs require serial evaluation over the sequence length, creating a training bottleneck that has long hindered their use on long sequences. Prior parallelization efforts either demanded special architectural structures (e.g., linear recurrence amenable to prefix scan) or relied on zeroth-order fixed-point iterations that may fail to converge. The paper challenges the long-held belief that non-linear sequential models are fundamentally unparallelizable over their sequence dimension.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of input vectors x(r) (e.g., 17,984 time samples for EigenWorms, or reshaped CIFAR-10 image patches), an initial state or boundary condition, and learnable model parameters θ, and produces the full sequence of hidden states y(r) or ODE trajectory. A key practical requirement is computing the Jacobian of the non-linear function f with respect to each of its P shifted state arguments at every sequence position, which introduces O(n²LP) memory overhead. The method is architecture-agnostic: it works with any non-linear RNN cell (GRU, LSTM) or NeuralODE without modifying the model's internal structure.

## Method

DEER restates a non-linear differential or difference equation as a fixed-point iteration by adding terms G_p(r)·y(r−s_p) to both sides, where G_p is chosen as the negative Jacobian of f with respect to its p-th state argument (G_p = −∂_p f). This makes the left-hand side a linear operator L_G that can be inverted in parallel: for ODEs, the inverse is computed via matrix exponentials combined with a parallel prefix scan (Blelloch, 1990); for RNNs, the same prefix scan applies to the discrete recurrence. The iteration achieves quadratic convergence equivalent to Newton's method in Banach space, typically requiring only a handful of iterations when warm-started from the previous training step's solution. The backward pass requires only a single evaluation of L_G^{-1} (via the dual operator / vector-Jacobian product) rather than the multiple iterations needed in the forward pass, yielding even greater speedups for training. The only hyperparameter is a convergence tolerance (10⁻⁴ for float32, 10⁻⁷ for float64), and the method is implemented in JAX using jax.lax.associative_scan for the prefix scan.

## Results

The DEER method achieves up to 500× speedup on forward evaluation and over 1000× speedup on forward+gradient computation for a 1M-length GRU sequence (n=1, batch=16) on a V100 GPU. For training, DEER is 11× faster than RK45 for NeuralODE (HNN on a two-body gravitational system) and up to 22× faster than sequential GRU training on the EigenWorms dataset (17,984 time samples). On EigenWorms, the GRU trained with DEER achieves 88.0±4.4% classification accuracy, competitive with modern long-sequence methods. On sequential CIFAR-10, the multi-head GRU (32 heads × 8 channels) achieves 90.25% test accuracy, the best result among non-linear recurrent architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EigenWorms, the DEER-trained GRU (88.0%) falls below LEM (92.3%) and UnICORNN (90.3%) but substantially outperforms ODE-RNN (47.9%), NCDE (66.7%), and NRDE (83.8%), demonstrating that a classical GRU becomes competitive on very long sequences once the training bottleneck is removed. On sequential CIFAR-10, the multi-head GRU (90.25%) is the best non-linear recurrent method, surpassing r-LSTM (72.2%) and UR-GRU (74.4%), but trails state-space models S4 (91.80%) and Liquid-S4 (92.02%) as well as MultiresNet (93.15%). The paper's primary comparison is computational: DEER training is 11× faster than RK45 for NeuralODE and 22× faster than sequential GRU for EigenWorms, with validation losses/accuracies nearly identical between the two methods. The authors also reproduce LEM's results (92.3%) using DEER, completing one epoch in 18 seconds versus ~116 seconds with the sequential method, confirming that the speedup generalizes beyond GRU to other sequential architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEER unifies and extends two previously separate lines of work: parallel ODE solvers (multiple shooting, Chartier & Philippe 1993; Massaroli et al. 2021) and fixed-point/root-finding approaches in deep learning (DEQ, Bai et al. 2019; Wang & Ragni 2021). The key departure is that prior parallelization of sequential models either required architectural restrictions (linear recurrent layers amenable to prefix scan, element-wise multiplications) or retained sequential sub-computations (each segment solver in multiple shooting). By choosing the Newton-optimal G_p matrices (Eq. 5), DEER makes the linear sub-problem at each iteration exactly solvable via parallel prefix scan (Blelloch 1990) with quadratic convergence, removing the architectural constraint while preserving the model's non-linearity. Unlike zeroth-order Picard iterations (Shih et al. 2023) or unverified fixed-point truncations (Wang & Ragni 2021), DEER inherits Newton's quadratic convergence guarantee locally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method challenges the long-held assumption that non-linear sequential models are fundamentally unparallelizable over sequence length, opening a new computational axis for RNN and NeuralODE research. However, the O(n³) time complexity from explicit Jacobian computation and matrix multiplications in the prefix scan severely limits speedup as hidden dimensionality grows (only ~25% at n=64), and the local convergence guarantee means far-from-solution initializations may diverge. Practical adoption is further constrained by JAX-only implementation (no PyTorch support for associative scan). Open questions include globally-converged Newton variants, efficient PyTorch backends, and whether the multi-head decomposition (splitting large n into small-n heads) can fully recover the speedup for standard hidden sizes without sacrificing expressivity.
