## Problem

Neural ODEs, like discrete networks, are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, and existing adversarial training methods are computationally expensive while treating the adversary as an input perturbation. This paper identifies that the min-max adversarial training objective can be recast as a continuous-time min-max optimal control problem where the adversary acts as an antagonizing control (disturbance) rather than an input offset, enabling a principled second-order optimizer with convergence guarantees that was previously unexplored for Neural ODEs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard 3×32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 or SVHN along with the Neural ODE vector field parameters. Unusually, the network maintains two identical sets of weights (u for minimization, v for maximization), effectively doubling parameters; the c-GTSONO variant restricts the antagonizing set to convolution layers only to reduce this overhead. The output is updated weight configurations for both control sets, producing a classifier whose robustness is evaluated under FGSM, PGD, and CW attacks at ε=0.03.

## Method

GTSONO recasts Neural ODE training as a min-max trajectory optimization problem with symmetric layer-wise dynamics, where control u minimizes and control v maximizes the accumulated loss Q. The key mechanism is a set of backward ODEs (Theorem 3.2) that propagate first- and second-order derivatives of Q in a single ODE solver pass, avoiding recursive backpropagation. To handle the large Hessian matrices efficiently, the paper exploits Kronecker product structure to decompose Q_uu, Q_uv, Q_vv into outer products of low-rank vectors (Theorem 3.4), reducing the update to matrix operations on small factors A_j and B_j per layer. The resulting open-loop min-max DDP update law generalizes Gradient Descent Ascent with Hessian preconditioning, and the quadratic running cost with |R_v|>|R_u| ensures the disturbance is penalized more heavily, yielding provable convergence to locally stable saddle points.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, C-GTSONO achieves 50.6% robust accuracy under PGD-20 (ε=0.03) and 36.3% under CW∞, outperforming all first-order baselines (Adam, SGD) and the second-order SNOpt by 3–4% and 18–29% respectively. On SVHN, C-GTSONO reaches 80.3% under PGD-20 (ε=0.03) and 50.5% under CW∞, surpassing the best baseline (SGD) by 7.9% and 27.1% respectively. Natural accuracy is slightly lower for GTSONO/C-GTSONO on CIFAR-10 (74.7% vs. 78.7–79.1%) but competitive on SVHN (97.3–99.6%). The performance gap widens with increasing perturbation magnitude, e.g., under PGD-20 (ε=0.05) on CIFAR-10, C-GTSONO achieves 35.0% vs. 29.1–32.1% for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GTSONO and its convolution-only variant C-GTSONO against three baselines: Adam, SGD with momentum (both first-order), and SNOpt (a second-order Neural ODE optimizer from Liu et al., 2021). On CIFAR-10, C-GTSONO wins on every adversarial metric: +4.8% over Adam and +3.8% over SNOpt under PGD-20 (ε=0.03), and a dramatic +20.9% over SNOpt under CW∞ (36.3% vs. 7.5%). On SVHN, C-GTSONO achieves 80.3% under PGD-20 (ε=0.03), beating SGD (72.4%) by 7.9% and Adam (71.8%) by 8.5%, and 50.5% under CW∞ versus 20.3–23.4% for all baselines. The trade-off is a ~4% drop in natural accuracy on CIFAR-10 (74.7% vs. 78.7–79.1%), though on SVHN GTSONO actually achieves the highest natural accuracy (99.6%). When adapted to adversarial training frameworks (FreeAT, TRADES), C-GTSONO further improves robust accuracy by ~2% (FreeAT) and >4% (TRADES) over SGD, while converging in roughly half the epochs due to second-order convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper recasts Neural ODE training as a min-max optimal control problem, drawing directly on the continuous-time min-max DDP framework of Sun et al. (2018) and the second-order Neural ODE optimizer (SNOpt) of Liu et al. (2021). The key conceptual departure from standard adversarial training is modeling the adversary not as an input perturbation but as an antagonizing control variable in the ODE dynamics, which yields a game-theoretic formulation with provable saddle-point convergence. The efficient computation leverages Kronecker factorization (building on K-FAC-style approximations) to decompose second-order matrices into vectors obtainable via a single backward ODE pass, avoiding recursive backpropagation. The work also connects to Poznyak et al. (2023), who independently suggested viewing min-max robust control through differential neural networks, and to the broader adversarial training literature (FreeAT, TRADES) where GTSONO is shown to serve as a drop-in optimizer replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a game-theoretic second-order optimizer can produce adversarially robust models even under natural (clean) training, without explicit adversarial examples, and that plugging GTSONO into existing defenses like FreeAT and TRADES yields 2–4% robust accuracy gains with roughly 8× fewer epochs. The convergence guarantees to stable local saddle points address a known weakness of gradient-based min-max methods (e.g., GDA can converge to non-critical or unstable points). However, experiments are limited to CIFAR-10 and SVHN with small networks on a single GPU, and the authors note robust overfitting and higher per-iteration cost; scaling to larger architectures (ResNets, Transformers) and exploring distributed DDP formulations remain open.
