## Problem

Multi-domain federated learning, where each client's data comes from a distinct domain with different feature distributions, has received far less attention than the more commonly studied skewed label distribution problem. Existing solutions like FedBN keep batch normalization layers locally per client, but this requires stateful clients and full participation every round, making them unsuitable for cross-device FL. The paper asks a more fundamental question: is normalization even necessary for learning a general global model across heterogeneous domains?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242257/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client provides RGB images from a single domain (e.g., 28×28 digit images from MNIST/SVHN/USPS/SynthDigits/MNIST-M, 244×244 object images from DomainNet's six domains, or real-world object images from Office-Caltech-10's four domains), and the server produces a single global model that classifies images into class labels without any per-domain personalization. The key constraint is that within each client all samples share the same domain, while across clients the marginal feature distributions P_k(x) differ substantially, and the global model must generalize to all domains simultaneously.

## Method

FedWon removes all normalization layers (BN, GN, LN) from the network and reparameterizes each convolution layer with Scaled Weight Standardization (WSConv), where each output channel's weight vector is standardized by its own mean and variance, then scaled by a learnable per-channel gain parameter. This eliminates batch dependency entirely, resolves the training/inference discrepancy inherent to BN, and requires zero extra computation at inference. The server aggregates only the reparameterized convolution weights following the standard FedAvg protocol, with no normalization statistics to track or synchronize. For large batch sizes (B=32), adaptive gradient clipping (AGC) is applied to stabilize training of the normalization-free network, while for small batch sizes (B=1,2,4) AGC is bypassed. The approach is architecture-agnostic, applied to 6-layer CNNs, AlexNet, ResNet-18, and MobileNetV2, and works for both cross-silo and cross-device FL settings.

## Results

On the three multi-domain FL benchmarks, FedWon achieves the best average testing accuracy across all datasets: 88.5% on Digits-Five, 75.6% on Office-Caltech-10, and 51.1% on DomainNet. The most striking gains appear on individual domains, e.g. 95.3% on DSLR (Office-Caltech-10) vs. 85.4% for FedBN, and 51.9% on Sketch (DomainNet) vs. 38.2% for FedBN — improvements exceeding 10 percentage points. FedWon also maintains strong performance at batch size 1 (66.7/55.1/96.9/89.8 on the four Office-Caltech-10 domains) and under cross-device client selection (93.3% average on Digits-Five with only 10/100 clients active per round).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedWon outperforms FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) on every dataset and nearly every domain, with the largest gaps on DomainNet (51.1% vs. 40.0% average) and Office-Caltech-10 (75.6% vs. 66.3%). Against FedBN (Li et al., 2021), the closest multi-domain competitor, FedWon wins on average across all three datasets (88.5 vs. 88.4, 75.6 vs. 71.4, 51.1 vs. 46.8) and achieves >10-point gains on individual domains such as DSLR (95.3 vs. 85.4) and Sketch (51.9 vs. 38.2), though it trails FedBN by <1% on MNIST-M and Amazon. FedProx (Li et al., 2020) and the BN-personalization methods SiloBN (Andreux et al., 2020) and FixBN (Zhong et al., 2023) all perform at or near FedAvg level, confirming they are not designed for multi-domain FL. PartialFed (Sun et al., 2021) achieves 70.6–71.3% average on Office-Caltech-10, still 4+ points below FedWon's 75.6%. Notably, FedWon is the only method that simultaneously supports both cross-silo and cross-device FL without requiring stateful clients, a practical advantage over FedBN and SiloBN which are limited to cross-silo settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the normalization-free network paradigm introduced by Brock et al. (2021a, 2021b), which demonstrated that scaled weight standardization and adaptive gradient clipping can replace batch normalization in centralized training. It departs from the dominant line of FL work that addresses non-i.i.d data by modifying how BN is handled (FedBN keeps BN local, SiloBN freezes statistics, FixBN trains BN only initially) by instead eliminating normalization layers entirely. The paper also extends the multi-domain FL problem formulation from FedBN and FedNorm, which are limited to cross-silo settings, to the more general cross-device scenario where clients are stateless and only a fraction participates per round.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedWon offers a simple, drop-in replacement for BN in FL that simultaneously resolves domain heterogeneity, supports both cross-silo and cross-device settings, and operates at batch size 1—making it particularly relevant for resource-constrained edge devices. The finding that removing normalization outperforms all BN-modification strategies challenges the implicit assumption that some normalization is necessary for stable deep network training in heterogeneous settings. Open questions include whether the approach extends to transformer backbones, semi-supervised or unsupervised FL, and whether the gains persist on larger-scale benchmarks like ImageNet. The paper's evaluation is limited to relatively small datasets (≤244×244) and CNN architectures, leaving scalability to modern vision transformers untested.
