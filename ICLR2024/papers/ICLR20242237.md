## Problem

Existing cooperative MARL methods (MAPPO, HAPPO, HATRPO) optimize a standard joint reward objective that encourages deterministic convergence to whichever Nash Equilibrium the agents encounter first, often a suboptimal one. No prior actor-critic framework provides theoretical guarantees for learning stochastic policies in cooperative MARL that can escape such suboptimal equilibria while maintaining monotonic improvement and convergence properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242237/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training (CTDE paradigm), each agent receives the global state observation and teammates' actions; during execution, only its own local observation is used. The method outputs a stochastic joint policy where each agent's action distribution is a Boltzmann (softmax) over the soft Q-values, parameterized by neural networks, alongside a centralized soft Q-function. For discrete action spaces, Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization is applied to enable gradient-based policy optimization.

## Method

The paper embeds cooperative MARL into a probabilistic graphical model and derives the MaxEnt MARL objective (reward plus per-agent entropy) via structured variational inference. The key technical contribution is the Joint Soft Policy Decomposition (Proposition 1), which shows that the joint policy update minimizing KL divergence to the Boltzmann target can be factored into a sequence of per-agent KL minimizations over a random permutation, enabling heterogeneous-agent soft policy iteration (HASPI). HASAC implements this with a centralized soft Q-network trained on Bellman residuals and per-agent policy networks trained by minimizing the expected KL objective, using a replay buffer for off-policy learning. The framework is further generalized into the MEHAML template, which subsumes HAML (at α=0) and guarantees monotonic improvement of the MaxEnt objective and convergence to a quantal response equilibrium for any induced algorithm using valid drift functionals and neighborhood operators.

## Results

HASAC achieves the best performance in 31 out of 35 tasks across six benchmarks (Bi-DexHands, MAMuJoCo, SMAC, GRF, MPE, LAG). On SMAC, HASAC attains over 90% median win rate on 7 of 8 maps, including 100% on 3s5z and 97.5% on MMM2 (Super Hard), while FOP collapses to 0% on all Super Hard maps. On the matrix game, HASAC escapes the suboptimal Nash equilibrium (A,A) and converges to the Pareto-optimal (C,C), whereas MAPPO and HAPPO deterministically lock onto (A,A). On Bi-DexHands, HASAC outperforms all five baselines by a large margin on Catch Abreast, Two Catch Underarm, and Lift Underarm, where on-policy methods fail within 10M steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HAPPO/HATRPO [13], HASAC wins on 6 of 8 SMAC maps (e.g., 97.5% vs 83.8% on 8m_vs_9m; 95.0% vs 76.2% on 6h_vs_8z) but loses to HATRPO on 10m_vs_11m (95.0% vs 98.8%). Against MAPPO [42], HASAC dominates on most tasks but concedes the corridor map (90.0% vs 97.5%). Against QMIX [24], HASAC is consistently superior, with the gap widening on Super Hard maps (95.0% vs 7.0% on 6h_vs_8z). Against FOP [44], HASAC achieves a dramatic margin: FOP scores 0.0% on all three Super Hard maps (3s5z_vs_3s6z, corridor, 6h_vs_8z) due to its reliance on the IGO assumption, while HASAC maintains 82.5–95.0%. Against HATD3 [45] on Bi-DexHands and MAMuJoCo, HASAC shows higher sample efficiency and lower variance, as HATD3's Gaussian-noise exploration is less effective than HASAC's intrinsic stochastic policy for covering the reward landscape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Heterogeneous-Agent Mirror Learning (HAML) framework of Kuba et al. to the maximum entropy setting, deriving a MaxEnt MARL objective from a probabilistic graphical model (following Levine's control-as-inference perspective) and proving convergence to quantal response equilibrium rather than Nash equilibrium. It builds on the soft actor-critic lineage (SAC, SQL) from single-agent RL and addresses the IGO-assumption limitation of FOP by providing guarantees without restrictive factorizability conditions. The key conceptual departure is the Joint Soft Policy Decomposition (Proposition 1), which shows the joint MaxEnt policy update decomposes into sequential per-agent KL minimizations, enabling a heterogeneous-agent algorithmic template (MEHAML) that generalizes HAML as the α→0 special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretically-grounded actor-critic method for stochastic policy learning in cooperative MARL, directly addressing the well-documented suboptimal NE convergence problem that plagues MAPPO, HAPPO, and related methods. The MEHAML template offers a principled design space for future algorithms via choice of drift functionals and neighborhood operators, though the paper leaves open which specific instantiations yield the best practical performance. A key limitation is that the theoretical guarantees require finite action spaces and the η-soft policy assumption, while the practical algorithm uses function approximation without finite-sample guarantees. The approach is also computationally heavier than standard MAPG methods due to sequential per-agent updates and centralized Q-function evaluation.
