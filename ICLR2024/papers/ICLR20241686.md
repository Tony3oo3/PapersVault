## Problem

Federated learning algorithms are predominantly designed for minimization, yet minimax optimization and variational inequality problems (VIPs) are increasingly central to ML applications such as GANs, adversarial training, and multi-agent RL. Existing local training methods for distributed VIPs fail to improve communication complexity over vanilla distributed GDA, and prior analyses rely on restrictive bounded-heterogeneity assumptions that are unrealistic in federated settings. This paper closes both gaps by providing the first communication-accelerated local GDA methods for distributed VIPs that work without bounded heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a distributed operator F = (1/n)Σf_i where each client i privately stores its local operator f_i, with the problem reformulated via a consensus structure that expands the variable dimension from d′ to nd. The expensive proximal step in the consensus form corresponds exactly to a communication round (averaging across clients), so the algorithm's design centers on how often to invoke this step. The output is the equilibrium point z* satisfying F(z*) = 0, with linear convergence to a neighborhood whose size is controlled by the step-size γ and the estimator variance parameters D₁, D₂.

## Method

The core algorithm, ProxSkip-VIP, combines two mechanisms: (1) randomized prox-skipping, where the expensive proximal operator (equivalently, the communication round in FL) is invoked with probability p rather than every iteration, and (2) a control variate h_t that tracks the drift introduced by skipping the proximal step, converging to F(x*) to stabilize iterations toward the solution. The unified analysis under a general estimator assumption (Assumption 2.2) recovers ProxSkip-SGDA, ProxSkip-GDA, and a variance-reduced ProxSkip-L-SVRGDA as special cases, yielding O(κ ln(1/ε)) communication complexity for the deterministic case versus O(κ² ln(1/ε)) for vanilla GDA. In the federated interpretation, skipping the proximal operator translates to local gradient updates on each client, while invoking it triggers a server-client averaging round, making the probability p directly control the communication frequency.

## Results

The paper provides the first communication-accelerated local GDA methods for distributed variational inequalities. For the deterministic strongly monotone and smooth setting, ProxSkip-GDA-FL achieves O(κ ln 1/ε) communication rounds, improving upon the O(κ² ln 1/ε) of vanilla distributed GDA and previous Local GDA analyses. The variance-reduced ProxSkip-L-SVRGDA-FL further achieves O(κ ln 1/ε) communication complexity in the stochastic finite-sum setting. In numerical experiments on strongly-monotone quadratic games and robust least squares (California Housing), the proposed methods consistently converge faster in communication rounds than all baselines, with the variance-reduced variant converging linearly to the exact solution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to centralized GDA/SGDA (Fallah et al., 2020), ProxSkip-SGDA-FL achieves a square-root improvement in communication complexity, going from O(max{κ², σ*²/μ²ε} ln 1/ε) to O(√max{κ², σ*²/μ²ε} ln 1/ε). Against Local SGDA (Deng & Mahdavi, 2021), the proposed method provides communication acceleration (the first provable improvement over vanilla distributed GDA) and removes the need for bounded heterogeneity assumptions, which are unrealistic in federated settings. Versus Local SEG (Beznosikov et al., 2020) and FedAvg-S (Hou et al., 2021), ProxSkip methods achieve strictly better communication complexity in both deterministic and stochastic regimes (see Table 2 in the appendix). In the numerical experiments on both the strongly-monotone quadratic game and the robust least squares problem (California Housing dataset), ProxSkip-SGDA-FL and ProxSkip-L-SVRGDA-FL consistently outperform Local SGDA, Local SEG, and FedGDA-GT (Sun & Wei, 2022) in terms of communication rounds to reach a target relative error, with the variance-reduced variant converging linearly to the exact solution while Local EG converges only to a neighborhood due to its bounded heterogeneity requirement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ProxSkip algorithm (Mishchenko et al., 2022) from composite minimization to the broader setting of distributed variational inequalities, using a consensus reformulation that turns inter-client communication into an expensive proximal operator call. It also generalizes the L-SVRGDA variance-reduced method of Beznosikov et al. (2022b) by wrapping it in the ProxSkip framework. The key conceptual departure from prior local GDA/SGDA work (Deng & Mahdavi, 2021; Beznosikov et al., 2020) is the introduction of a control variate h_t that stabilizes iterations when the proximal step is skipped, enabling provable communication acceleration that was previously unattainable for local GDA in the VIP setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result showing that local GDA can provably reduce communication complexity below that of vanilla distributed GDA for variational inequalities, matching the known lower bound of Ω(κ ln(1/ε)) in the variance-reduced case. The analysis avoids the bounded-heterogeneity assumption that plagues most federated minimax methods, making it applicable to realistic FL scenarios with arbitrarily dissimilar client data. Open questions include whether the quasi-strong monotonicity and star-cocoercivity assumptions hold for practical non-convex GAN training or multi-agent RL, and whether the communication acceleration persists at scale with deep network architectures rather than the small quadratic problems tested here.
