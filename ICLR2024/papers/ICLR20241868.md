## Problem

Generalizable NeRF (GNeRF) methods typically replace NeRF's MLP backbone with transformers or cost volumes to aggregate reference features, making them difficult to integrate with NeRF derivatives (mip-NeRF, NeRF++) and computationally expensive. The paper asks whether generalizability can be instilled directly into the original NeRF framework by adaptively modifying its weights per scene, without architectural replacement or scene-specific retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241868/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N nearby reference images (N=8 or 10 depending on setting) with known camera poses plus a target view pose, and renders a novel view by sampling K=64 points along each ray. Reference features are extracted at three U-Net scales (global to dense), and the network directly predicts hitting probabilities w_i for volume rendering rather than only volume density, enabling implicit occlusion modeling. The plug-and-play design extends to mip-NeRF (conical-frustum sampling with K=65) and NeRF++ (foreground-background decomposition on unbounded scenes).

## Method

InsertNeRF inserts multiple plug-and-play HyperNet modules before each MLP layer in both F_geo and F_app, where each module comprises a sampling-aware graph-convolution filter (modeling inter-sample relationships to denoise epipolar features), a dynamic MLP that generates scene-specific weights and biases, and a DFiLM dynamic activation function. Scene features are aggregated via a multi-layer dynamic-static strategy: dynamic weights M_l^DY are learned from multi-scale features with auxiliary RGB supervision, combined with static geometric-proximity weights M^ST, and fused with max-pooled features across views. F_geo uses multi-scale features progressively (global→dense) across its MLP depth, while F_app employs parallel progressive branches with residual connections and no DFiLM to preserve the smooth BRDF prior. Training is end-to-end with three losses: photometric MSE, a backbone autoencoding loss for feature extraction, and a dynamic-weights auxiliary loss that supervises M_l^DY via weighted reference RGB reconstruction.

## Results

Under Setting I (N=8 references), InsertNeRF achieves PSNR 30.35 / SSIM 0.938 / LPIPS 0.065 on NeRF Synthetic, PSNR 26.44 / SSIM 0.844 / LPIPS 0.169 on LLFF, and PSNR 29.75 / SSIM 0.925 / LPIPS 0.077 on DTU, outperforming all SOTA baselines. The paper reports improvements of approximately 1.16 dB in PSNR and 23.6% reduction in LPIPS over the best prior method (NeuRay). Under Setting II (N=10, no depth priors), InsertNeRF achieves PSNR 27.57 / LPIPS 0.056 on NeRF Synthetic and PSNR 25.68 / LPIPS 0.126 on LLFF, matching or exceeding GNT (Wang et al., 2022) while using a more compact architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InsertNeRF outperforms all 15 baselines across the three primary datasets. Against the strongest competitor NeuRay (CVPR'22), InsertNeRF gains +1.43 dB PSNR on NeRF Synthetic (30.35 vs 28.92), +0.59 dB on LLFF (26.44 vs 25.85), and +1.45 dB on DTU (29.75 vs 28.30), while also achieving substantially lower LPIPS (0.065 vs 0.096 on NeRF Synthetic). Against IBRNet (CVPR'21), the gap is even larger: +3.62 dB PSNR on NeRF Synthetic and +3.99 dB on DTU. Under Setting II, InsertNeRF matches GNT (ICLR'23) on SSIM (0.936 vs 0.937) and LPIPS (0.056 vs 0.056) for NeRF Synthetic while achieving higher PSNR (27.57 vs 27.29), and leads on all three metrics for LLFF (25.68 vs 25.59 PSNR). For sparse-input settings (Table 3), InsertNeRF without retraining (PSNR 19.41, SSIM 0.618) is competitive with FreeNeRF (19.63, 0.612) and outperforms RegNeRF (19.08, 0.587) and GeCoNeRF (18.77, 0.596). On ShapeNet (Table 10), the HyperNet modules improve pixelNeRF by ~0.8 dB PSNR in the 1-view setting, though gains are smaller than on multi-view datasets due to simpler scene geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant GNeRF paradigm (IBRNet, GNT, NeuRay, GeoNeRF) that typically replaces NeRF's MLP backbone with transformers or cost volumes, making integration with NeRF derivatives like mip-NeRF and NeRF++ difficult. Instead, it repurposes the hypernetwork concept (Ha et al., 2016) as a plug-and-play mechanism that generates scene-specific weights while preserving the original NeRF architecture. The multi-layer dynamic-static aggregation strategy departs from IBRNet's single-layer static visibility weighting by introducing learned dynamic weights over multi-scale U-Net features, and the sampling-aware filter introduces graph convolutions over ray-sampled points to model inter-sample occlusion relationships—neither of which appears in prior GNeRF work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play HyperNet module design offers a practical pathway to retrofit generalizability onto any NeRF-like system (demonstrated on mip-NeRF and NeRF++) without architectural overhaul, which is valuable as the NeRF ecosystem continues to fragment into specialized variants. However, the method is constrained by requiring the same number of sample points (K=64) during training and evaluation, limiting its flexibility compared to transformer-based methods that can train on fewer samples and evaluate on more. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unbounded scenes (mip-NeRF 360), whether the graph-based sampling filter scales to larger K, and whether fine-tuning on target scenes could close the remaining gap to per-scene NeRF quality.
