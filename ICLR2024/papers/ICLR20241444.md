## Problem

While noisy label learning studies how to train robust models from noisy training data, the effect of label noise in the (often inaccessible) pre-training data on downstream task generalization remains unexplored. This paper asks: how does noise in pre-training shape the learned feature space, and can its malignant effects on out-of-domain transferability be mitigated without full fine-tuning of the pre-trained model?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241444/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen features from a pre-trained model (ResNet, ViT, CLIP, BERT, RoBERTa, GPT-2, or API-only models like text-ada-002) and a small set of downstream labeled examples. It produces a transformed feature representation via a 2-layer MLP and a class prediction. The pre-trained backbone is never updated; only the lightweight MLP head is trained, making the approach applicable to black-box or API-only models where full fine-tuning is infeasible due to parameter size or access restrictions.

## Method

NMTune adds a 2-layer MLP on top of frozen pre-trained features and trains it with a cross-entropy loss plus three singular-value-spectrum regularizations: (1) an MSE consistency loss between normalized original and transformed features to preserve pre-trained knowledge, (2) a covariance regularization that penalizes off-diagonal entries of the feature covariance matrix to flatten the singular value distribution (improving SVE and discriminability), and (3) a dominant singular value regularization that directly maximizes the ratio σ₁/Σσⱼ to restore transferability (improving LSVR). The key empirical insight driving this design is that pre-training noise decreases the largest singular value (reducing OOD transferability) while increasing the feature space spanning dimension (initially aiding ID generalization but eventually memorizing noise structure). The method is validated across supervised, contrastive, and self-supervised pre-trained vision and language models, showing consistent gains over linear probing and plain MLP tuning on both in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks.

## Results

On vision models pre-trained on noisy datasets, NMTune consistently improves both in-domain (ID) and out-of-domain (OOD) performance over linear probing (LP) and MLP tuning. For example, on Swin-L (ImageNet-21K), NMTune achieves 84.16% ID accuracy and 52.35% OOD accuracy, compared to 81.91% and 50.88% for LP. On Laion-2B CLIP models, ConvNext-L reaches 70.30% OOD accuracy (vs. 66.86% LP) and ViT-L reaches 70.47% (vs. 66.89% LP). On language models, NMTune improves OOD performance by 2.5–2.4 points on RoBERTa-L (47.01 vs. 44.55) and GPT-2 (39.07 vs. 36.68) on GLUE-X.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares NMTune against two baselines: Linear Probing (LP, which only trains a linear classifier on frozen features) and MLP tuning (which adds a 2-layer MLP without the proposed regularization terms). NMTune wins on every model and every metric across both vision and language tasks. The gains over LP are most pronounced on OOD tasks: Swin-L improves by 1.47 points in OOD accuracy (52.35 vs. 50.88), ConvNext-L by 3.44 points (70.30 vs. 66.86), and ViT-L by 3.58 points (70.47 vs. 66.89). Over MLP tuning, NMTune also consistently wins, with the largest OOD gap on ConvNext-L (70.30 vs. 68.43) and Swin-L (52.35 vs. 51.21). Notably, MLP tuning sometimes underperforms LP (e.g., ConvNext-L ID accuracy drops from 88.86 to 88.53), confirming that the regularization terms—not just extra parameters—are responsible for the improvement. On language models, NMTune outperforms both baselines on all four models, with the largest OOD gain on RoBERTa-L (47.01 vs. 44.55 LP, 45.22 MLP) and GPT-2 (39.07 vs. 36.68 LP, 37.24 MLP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new research direction it terms Noisy Model Learning (NML), explicitly distinguishing it from the well-studied noisy label learning paradigm where noise resides in the downstream training set and the model is trained from scratch or fine-tuned with noise-aware objectives. Conceptually, it builds on the PT-FT transfer learning paradigm and prior observations that pre-training data quality matters more than quantity (Nguyen et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2022), but shifts the focus to the black-box setting where the pre-trained model is frozen and only a lightweight adapter can be trained. The proposed NMTune method repurposes covariance regularization ideas from Barlow Twins and VICReg, combined with a dominant singular value objective inspired by transferability analysis (Chen et al., 2019), to reshape the feature space without touching the pre-trained weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In the era of foundation models where downstream users typically only have API access or frozen weights, understanding how pre-training label noise propagates into feature geometry and degrades OOD generalization is a practically critical and previously unaddressed question. The paper's SVD-based analysis (SVE and LSVR) provides a mechanistic explanation linking noise to feature space structure, offering a diagnostic tool beyond just a fix. Key limitations include the analysis being restricted to ResNet-50 with synthetic noise, and the practical gains of NMTune being modest (1–3 points), leaving open whether the approach scales to truly massive models or handles more realistic, instance-dependent noise patterns. The broader open question is whether similar feature-space regularization can address other pre-training pathologies (distribution shift, data poisoning) in the black-box regime.
