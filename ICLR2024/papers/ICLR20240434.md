## Problem

GFlowNets are designed for deterministic environments, and the prior stochastic extension (Pan et al., 2023) treats environment transitions as fixed-policy actions, producing constraints that can be unsatisfiable and making the global optimum depend on the training policy. This paper asks whether flow network constraints can be reformulated to remain satisfiable and unique under stochastic transitions, and whether the resulting framework can be extended to adversarial two-player zero-sum games.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240434/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For game-playing, the input is a board state (3×3 tic-tac-toe or 6×7 Connect-4) with a binary legal-move mask and the current player identity; the output is a probability distribution over legal moves. For the protein design task, the input is a partial 8-symbol DNA sequence and the output is the next symbol to append. In both settings, the environment transition distribution P_env is either known or jointly learned from observed transitions, and the policy must account for stochasticity in how actions map to successor states.

## Method

EFlowNets replace the deterministic detailed balance constraint with an expected detailed balance (EDB) constraint: at agent states, flow is conserved through the learned policy, while at environment states, flow equals the expectation of child flows under P_env, guaranteeing satisfiability and uniqueness (Proposition 1). For two-player zero-sum games, AFlowNets define each player as an EFlowNet whose 'environment' is the opponent's policy, with a branch-adjusted reward that divides the outcome reward by the product of branching factors along the trajectory. The key algorithmic contribution is a trajectory balance loss (Proposition 4) that exploits the zero-sum structure R₁°(x)R₂°(x)=1 to yield a single scalar constraint per complete trajectory, enabling efficient credit assignment across the full game without Monte Carlo tree search.

## Results

On the TFBind protein design task (stochastic environment), EFlowNets outperform all baselines in both top-100 reward and diversity (sphere exclusion), with the largest gains when the reward is peaky (high β) and environment stochasticity is high. In Connect-4, AFlowNet₁₅ achieves an Elo of 1835.3 ± 154.9, roughly 800 points above AlphaZero, and learns to select optimal moves in >80% of board states after 3 hours on a single RTX 8000 GPU. In tic-tac-toe, AFlowNet₁₀ reaches an Elo of 338.4 ± 14.1, matching AlphaZero's performance, with both agents drawing nearly all head-to-head games.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stoch-GFN (Pan et al., 2023), EFlowNets achieve higher top-100 reward and greater diversity on TFBind, particularly at high β and α, where the Stoch-GFN detailed-balance constraints become unsatisfiable. Against discrete SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), EFlowNets also lead in both metrics under peaky-reward settings, though SAC narrows the gap at lower β. In Connect-4, AFlowNets (λ = 10, 15) decisively outperform AlphaZero (Silver et al., 2018), winning 50/50 points in head-to-head matches and achieving ~800 Elo points higher; AlphaZero scores 0 against AFlowNet₁₀ and AFlowNet₁₅. In tic-tac-toe, AFlowNets match AlphaZero (both draw nearly all mutual games), but AlphaZero converges ~10× faster in training steps due to MCTS. Against minimax DQN (Fan et al., 2020) and SoftDQN on tic-tac-toe, AFlowNets achieve a higher percentage of optimal moves and a lower loss rate against a uniform opponent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021; 2023) from deterministic to stochastic environments by proposing expected flow networks (EFlowNets), which marginalize over environment transition uncertainty rather than treating it as a fixed-policy action as in Pan et al. (2023). The authors show the prior stochastic GFlowNet formulation induces unsatisfiable constraints, motivating the expected detailed balance (EDB) constraints that provably satisfy key desiderata (satisfiability, uniqueness). For adversarial settings, AFlowNets compose multiple EFlowNets and derive a novel trajectory balance loss (Proposition 4) for two-player zero-sum games that does not directly generalize from the deterministic GFlowNet TB of Malkin et al. (2022), exploiting the zero-sum reward structure R₁(x)R₂(x)=1. The paper also connects GFlowNet policies to Luce quantal response agents from behavioral economics, providing a game-theoretic interpretation of the learned policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EFlowNet/AFlowNet framework provides a principled, provably convergent alternative to MCTS-based methods (AlphaZero, MuZero) for learning in stochastic and adversarial settings, with the practical advantage of requiring only a single forward pass per state rather than expensive tree search. However, experiments are limited to small games (tic-tac-toe, Connect-4) and a single protein design task; scaling to larger games like chess or Go remains open, and the trajectory-level TB objective suffers from high variance and memory cost on long horizons. The branch-adjusted reward, while empirically essential for good play, introduces a path-dependent reward structure that may not generalize cleanly to games without a natural tree structure. Open questions include generalization to incomplete-information games, continuous action spaces, and cooperative multi-agent settings.
