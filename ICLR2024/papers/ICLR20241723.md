## Problem

Long-term video-language learning is computationally prohibitive when modeling entire videos, so existing methods divide videos into short clips and captions. However, this introduces multi-granularity noisy correspondence (MNC): coarse-grained misalignment where clips and captions are asynchronous or irrelevant, and fine-grained misalignment where only subsets of frames and words within a pair are actually correlated. Prior work addresses at most the coarse-grained issue, leaving frame-word misalignment unexplored and degrading temporal modeling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of 8 video clips (each 3–16 seconds, sampled at 1 fps from 30 fps source via S3D) paired with 8 corresponding text captions (8–32 tokens each), forming a 128-frame video sequence per instance. It produces dual-encoder representations (6-layer video Transformer, 12-layer text Transformer) that support clip-caption retrieval, video-paragraph retrieval via OT sequence distance, action segmentation, and video QA. The OT transport assignment matrix (n×m) is computed in a late-interaction fashion on encoder outputs, avoiding the O(t²) cost of encoding the full long video through a single Transformer.

## Method

Norton formulates a unified optimal transport framework with two contrastive losses: a video-paragraph loss (L_video) that measures sequence distance between 8 clips and 8 captions via Sinkhorn iterations, and a clip-caption loss (L_clip) that exploits faulty negatives within a batch. For fine-grained alignment, a log-sum-exp soft-maximum operator (Eq. 5) identifies crucial words and key frames per clip-caption pair, reducing the influence of irrelevant tokens. An alignable prompt bucket (APB) appends a row and column with a fixed similarity margin p (set to the bottom 30% of aligned pair similarities) to the OT matrix, forcing irrelevant clips or captions to align with the bucket and be discarded. For asynchronous misalignment, the OT assignment naturally realigns each clip to multiple related captions and vice versa. In the clip-caption loss, the within-batch OT assignment Q̂* replaces the identity target with a soft-alignment matrix T = (1−β)I + βQ̂*, recalibrating attractive and repulsive forces to correct semantically similar faulty negatives.

## Results

On the primary video-paragraph retrieval task (YouCookII, Background Removed), Norton achieves 88.7% R@1 with DTW measurement, outperforming TempCLR (83.5%) by +5.2% and VideoCLIP (56.0%) by +32.7%. With Cap.Avg. measurement, Norton reaches 75.5% R@1 vs. TempCLR's 74.5%. Across downstream tasks, Norton also leads on zero-shot VideoQA (77.1% vs. 74.4% for TempCLR), action segmentation on COIN (69.8% vs. 68.7%), and noisy correspondence alignment on HTM-Align (46.9% vs. 44.4% for VideoCLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Norton consistently outperforms the two strongest baselines—VideoCLIP and TempCLR—across all evaluated tasks. On video-paragraph retrieval with DTW, Norton gains +5.2% R@1 over TempCLR and +32.7% over VideoCLIP, demonstrating the benefit of OT-based alignment over DTW. On clip-caption retrieval (YouCookII), Norton achieves 24.2% R@1 and 51.9% R@5, surpassing TempCLR (23.3%, 51.0%) but slightly trailing on R@10 (64.1% vs. 64.5%). On MSR-VTT zero-shot retrieval, Norton improves over VideoCLIP by +0.3% R@1, +1.9% R@5, and +1.6% R@10. For zero-shot VideoQA, Norton reaches 77.1% accuracy, a +2.7% gain over TempCLR (74.4%) and +3.2% over VideoCLIP (73.9%). On action segmentation (COIN), Norton achieves 69.8% frame accuracy, edging out both VideoCLIP and TempCLR (68.7%). On the HTM-Align noisy correspondence benchmark, Norton (46.9% Recall) outperforms VideoCLIP (44.4%) and TempCLR (44.1%), confirming robustness to misaligned clip-caption pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

Norton extends the temporal alignment paradigm established by TempCLR (which uses Dynamic Time Warping to measure clip-caption sequence distance) and VideoCLIP (clip-caption contrastive pre-training) by replacing DTW with a noise-robust optimal transport formulation. The key conceptual departure is identifying and unifying two previously unaddressed failure modes—fine-grained frame-word misalignment and coarse-grained irrelevant misalignment—within a single OT framework, augmented by a log-sum-exp soft-maximum operator and an alignable prompt bucket. The Sinkhorn-Knopp iteration from Cuturi (2013) provides the computational backbone, while the faulty-negative exploitation via OT draws on the broader contrastive learning literature on debiased negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in video-language pre-training: only ~30% of clip-caption pairs in HowTo100M are visually aligned, and prior temporal learning methods (DTW-based, sorting-based) silently degrade under this noise. The OT-based late-interaction approach offers a computationally efficient alternative to encoding entire long videos through Transformers, making long-video temporal learning more accessible. Open questions remain around extending the framework to multi-modal noisy correspondence (video + text + audio), whether filtered-out noisy samples can be repurposed as training signal via large multimodal models, and whether the approach generalizes beyond instructional video domains.
