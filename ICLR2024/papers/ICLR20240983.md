## Problem

Semantic segmentation models suffer from significant pixel-level variance—features of the same category deviate across images, resolutions, and domains—yet prior work has largely ignored this variance or addressed it only through global prototypes and simple priors that cannot capture the full complexity of the pixel feature distribution. This paper identifies the causes of pixel-level variance (intra-distribution, inter-distribution, inter-domain, and pseudo-label error) and argues that per-pixel alignment is needed to achieve consistent categorical representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240983/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

PiXL consumes RGB street-scene images (GTA5, SYNTHIA, or Cityscapes) at 1024×1024, which are simultaneously cropped into a 512×512 high-resolution patch and resized into a 512×512 low-resolution patch to form a multi-resolution local pixel distribution. The model outputs a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask over 19 Cityscapes classes, with inference performed via a 1024×1024 sliding window (512×512 overlap) that fuses high- and low-resolution predictions.

## Method

PiXL treats each image as a local distribution of pixels and partitions them into joint pixels (low entropy, assumed to follow the global class distribution) and drift pixels (high entropy, deviating) via an entropy-based threshold (1−η quantile). Joint pixels from two sampled images are aggregated into multi-resolution local prototypes per class, with an adaptive selection rule choosing the high- or low-resolution prototype based on pixel count to balance detail and context. Drift pixels are then aligned to their same-class prototype while being repelled from other-class prototypes using an asymmetric InfoNCE loss where gradients are stopped on the prototypes, preventing drift pixels from corrupting the alignment targets. The full training objective combines this Drift Pixel Alignment loss with cross-entropy (weighted by pseudo-label confidence in semi-supervised/UDA settings) and a Thing-Class ImageNet feature-distance regularizer, all on top of an HRDA (MiT-B5 + DAFormer decoder) or DeepLabV3+ backbone.

## Results

In the UDA setting, PiXL achieves 75.0% mIoU on GTA5→Cityscapes and 67.1% mIoU on SYNTHIA→Cityscapes, surpassing the SOTA CLUDA by 0.6% and 0.3% respectively, and the HRDA baseline by 1.2% and 1.3%. In the semi-supervised setting on Cityscapes with only 1/30 (3.3%) labeled images, PiXL (HRDA) reaches 71.73% mIoU, outperforming the previous best (SegSDE at 62.09%) by a large margin of 9.64%. In the fully supervised setting, PiXL achieves 82.74% mIoU at 512×512 resolution, competitive with methods trained at higher resolutions (e.g., SegFormer at 84.00% with 1024×1024).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiXL outperforms the HRDA baseline by 1.2% (GTA5→Cityscapes) and 1.3% (SYNTHIA→Cityscapes) mIoU, and surpasses the SOTA CLUDA by 0.6% and 0.3% respectively, with notable gains on rare classes like pole (+1.8% over CLUDA) and motorbike (+5.0% over CLUDA) in GTA5→Cityscapes. In the semi-supervised setting, PiXL (HRDA) dramatically outperforms all baselines at the 1/30 labeled ratio (71.73% vs. SegSDE's 62.09%, a +9.64% margin), but is slightly behind AEL (77.90%) and CPS (79.21%) at 1/8 and 1/4 ratios respectively, though it remains within 1.5% of the best. In the fully supervised setting, PiXL at 512×512 (82.74%) trails SegFormer (84.00%) and Mask2Former (84.50%) which use higher input resolutions, but surpasses HRNetV2 (81.10%) and HRViT-b1 (81.63%). The ablation confirms that the DPA module contributes +0.9% and the APG module adds a further +0.3% over the HRDA baseline, validating the asymmetric contrast and adaptive prototype selection as the key drivers of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

PiXL builds directly on the HRDA multi-resolution architecture (Hoyer et al., 2022b) and departs from prior prototype-based domain adaptation methods (ProDA, BAPA, SePiCo) by introducing a divide-and-conquer pixel partitioning strategy: pixels are split into 'joint' (low-entropy, distribution-conforming) and 'drift' (high-entropy, deviating) subsets, with only drift pixels undergoing asymmetric contrastive alignment toward adaptively selected multi-resolution prototypes. This contrasts with CLUDA and SePiCo, which apply symmetric pixel-to-pixel contrastive losses without explicitly modeling the intra-distribution variance that causes some pixels to deviate from the global category distribution. The pixel learning scheme also unifies UDA, semi-supervised, and fully-supervised segmentation under a single intra-/inter-distribution alignment framework, going beyond the task-specific formulations of prior contrastive segmentation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pixel learning scheme offers a principled, unified lens for addressing pixel-level variance across multiple segmentation paradigms, and the plug-and-play PiXL framework (validated on both MiT-B5 and ResNet101 backbones) is immediately applicable to existing pipelines. However, evaluation is confined to street-scene datasets (Cityscapes, GTA5, SYNTHIA), leaving open whether the entropy-based partition and asymmetric contrast generalize to medical imaging or other domains with different variance structures. The η hyperparameter (controlling the joint/drift split ratio) is tuned heuristically, and the paper acknowledges that boundary pixels and semantically similar categories (e.g., sidewalk vs. road) remain failure points, suggesting the divide-and-conquer strategy may need finer-grained partitioning for truly ambiguous regions.
