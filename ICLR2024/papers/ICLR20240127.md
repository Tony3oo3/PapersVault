## Problem

Diffusion model backbones (U-Net, DiT) are computationally expensive in both training and sampling, lack test-time reconfigurability (e.g., skipping layers to reduce inference cost), and struggle to generate images at resolutions higher than the training data. This paper addresses the under-explored challenge of designing an efficient, flexible network backbone for iterative denoising that supports selective layer skipping and variable-resolution generation without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240127/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and produces RGB images. Each LEGO brick operates on non-overlapping patches of varying sizes (e.g., 4×4, 16×16, 64×64 for a 64×64 image), with each patch further divided into local receptive fields (e.g., four 8×8 sub-patches within a 16×16 brick). For higher-resolution generation (256×256, 512×512), images are first encoded into a latent space via a Stable Diffusion autoencoder (8× downsampling). A key output property is the ability to generate images at resolutions far exceeding training resolution (e.g., 2048×600 panoramas from a model trained on 256×256).

## Method

The LEGO brick is a network unit that combines Local-feature Enrichment (projecting non-overlapping local receptive fields into token embeddings via an MLP) and Global-content Orchestration (processing those tokens with DiT-style Transformer blocks whose attention span is as short as 4). Multiple bricks of different patch sizes are stacked vertically, each maintaining a full-resolution image at its input and output, enabling progressive spatial refinement across the stack. Three stacking strategies are explored: Progressive Growth (small-to-large patches), Progressive Refinement (large-to-small), and LEGO-U (hierarchical, U-Net-inspired). During training, bricks operate on sampled patches rather than full images, drastically reducing FLOPs; during sampling, bricks can be selectively skipped at appropriate noise levels (e.g., skipping the global image-brick at low noise in PG, or the local patch-brick at high noise in PR) to cut inference time by ~60% without significant quality loss.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with classifier-free guidance, LEGO-XL-PG achieves FID 2.05 and IS 289.12, while LEGO-XL-U achieves the highest IS of 338.08 and best sFID of 4.21 among all methods. On ImageNet 512×512 with CFG, LEGO-XL-PG reaches FID 3.74 and IS 285.66 with only 512M iterated images (vs. 768M for DiT-XL/2). On CelebA 64×64, LEGO-S-PR achieves FID 2.09 with 0.2G FLOPs, outperforming DiT-S/2 (FID 2.52, 0.4G FLOPs) at half the computational cost. LEGO also achieves a 60% reduction in sampling time compared to DiT at the same NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiT-XL/2 (Peebles & Xie, 2022), LEGO-XL-PG achieves a lower FID (2.05 vs. 2.27 with CFG) and substantially fewer FLOPs (68.8G vs. 161.4G on ImageNet 64×64), while also reducing sampling time by 60% at the same NFE; however, DiT-XL/2 uses 1792M iterated images versus LEGO's 512M, so the FID gap narrows when accounting for training budget. Against MDT-XL/2 (Gao et al., 2023), MDT achieves the best FID with CFG (1.79 vs. LEGO-PG's 2.05), but LEGO-XL-U surpasses it on IS (338.08 vs. 283.01) and sFID (4.21 vs. 4.57), and requires far fewer iterated images (350M vs. 1664M). Against U-ViT-H/2 (Bao et al., 2022), LEGO-XL-PG wins on FID with CFG (2.05 vs. 2.29) and uses fewer FLOPs. Against ADM (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) and LDM-4 (Rombach et al., 2022), LEGO achieves substantially better FID with CFG (2.05 vs. 4.59 and 3.60 respectively). On ImageNet 512×512, DiT-XL/2 retains a FID advantage (3.04 vs. 3.74 for LEGO-PG), but LEGO-PG achieves a higher IS (285.66 vs. 240.82) and better Recall (0.64 vs. 0.54) with fewer iterated images. On CIFAR-10, integrating a pre-trained VP model as the first LEGO brick improves FID from 2.5 to 1.88 (unfrozen) or 1.92 (frozen), demonstrating that LEGO can enhance existing diffusion models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2022), repurposing its Transformer blocks as the internal mechanism of each 'LEGO brick,' while grounding the training objective in the standard DDPM/EDM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020; Karras et al., 2022). It departs from both U-Net and full-image ViT backbones by decomposing the denoising network into modular patch-level units that operate on small local receptive fields with very short attention spans (as few as 4 tokens), rather than processing entire images through deep stacks of convolutional or attention layers. The concept of patch-level diffusion training connects to prior work on patch-based generation (Luhman & Luhman, 2022; Wang et al., 2023), but LEGO introduces the key distinction of a vertically stackable, test-time skippable ensemble where each brick maintains a consistent full-resolution image interface, enabling reconfigurable inference without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60% sampling-time reduction over DiT at matched NFE, combined with the ability to generate images at resolutions far exceeding training data (e.g., 2048×600 from 256×256), addresses two of the most practically limiting aspects of diffusion models: inference cost and resolution rigidity. The modular brick design also enables straightforward integration of pretrained diffusion models as individual bricks, lowering the barrier to building high-quality generators. Open questions remain around principled (rather than heuristic) brick-skipping strategies, optimal combinatorial sizing of brick stacks, and extension to text-conditioned generation. The authors note that training still demands substantial GPU resources (32 A100s for ~14 days on ImageNet 512×512), limiting accessibility for smaller groups.
