## Problem

Offline RL from observational data is vulnerable to hidden confounding, where unobserved variables influence both the behavioral policy and environment dynamics, inducing structural bias that cannot be reduced with more data. Prior confounding-aware methods assume identifiability of the confounders (e.g., access to proxy variables or environment interventions), but in realistic settings such as clinical decision-making, the confounders are nonidentifiable. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a practical method to quantify and mitigate confounding bias without requiring the confounders to be identifiable.

## Input / Output

The method takes an offline trajectory dataset of (state, action, reward) sequences generated by an unknown context-aware behavioral policy, where the confounding context z is absent from the observations. It produces a context-independent policy (mapping states to actions without access to z), a per-state-action delphic uncertainty estimate computed as the variance of counterfactual Q-values across an ensemble of compatible world models, and a penalised Q-value function used for policy extraction. The world models are trained via variational inference with the reparametrisation trick, and counterfactual Q-estimates are obtained through importance sampling that reweights by the ratio of the target policy to the learned behavioral policy.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-way variance decomposition (Theorem 4.2) that separates total Q-value uncertainty into aleatoric (environment stochasticity), epistemic (parameter uncertainty from finite data), and a newly defined delphic uncertainty arising from variation across compatible world models—each a different factorisation of the same marginalised observational distribution. Compatible worlds are learned via variational inference, jointly optimising a latent confounder posterior ν_w(z|τ), a behavioral policy π_b,w(a|s,z), and a Q-function Q_w(s,a,z) by maximising an ELBO over the trajectory data. An ensemble of W world models is trained with varied confounder dimensionalities (|Z| ∈ {1,2,4,8,16}), prior variances, and architectures to approximate the set of compatible worlds. Delphic uncertainty u_d^π(s,a) = Var_w(Q_w^π(s,a)) is then used as a pessimism penalty subtracted from the Bellman target in Conservative Q-Learning: Q'_target = Q_target − λ·u_d^π(s,a), steering the learned policy away from state-action pairs where confounding bias is most ambiguous.

## Results

On the HiRID ICU dataset (33k+ patient stays), Delphic ORL achieves the highest OPE score of 62.2 ± 1.0 when all 14 confounders are hidden (Γ≈200), outperforming CQL (59.3 ± 0.9), BC (59.6 ± 0.8), and BCQ (54.6 ± 1.3). The advantage grows with confounding strength: for the single strongest confounder {age} (Γ=48.2), Delphic ORL reaches 66.5 ± 0.9 versus CQL's 64.4 ± 0.8 and BCQ's 58.8 ± 0.8. In the unconfounded baseline (Γ=1), CQL slightly edges out Delphic ORL (68.2 vs. 67.6), confirming the penalty is most beneficial when confounding is present. On the sepsis simulation, Delphic ORL similarly outperforms all baselines as Γ increases from 1 to 100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Delphic ORL consistently outperforms all three baselines (BCQ, BC, CQL) in the majority of confounded settings on the HiRID dataset, with the largest margins under strong confounding: a 2.9-point gap over CQL and 7.6-point gap over BCQ when all 14 confounders are hidden (Γ≈200). BCQ is the weakest baseline throughout, suffering the most from confounding bias (e.g., 54.6 vs. 62.2 under full confounding). Behaviour Cloning is competitive in low-confounding regimes but fails to exploit the reward signal, capping around 60–65 regardless of confounding strength. CQL is the strongest baseline in the unconfounded case (Γ=1, score 68.2) and in a few low-Γ settings (e.g., {surgical status}, Γ=3.2), where the delphic penalty provides little benefit and slightly hurts performance (66.8 vs. 67.4). Overall, Delphic ORL wins or ties in 13 of 16 confounder configurations, with the two losses ({resp. diag.} and {surgical status}) occurring at low Γ where confounding is minimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the aleatoric/epistemic uncertainty decomposition of Kendall and Gal (2017) by introducing a third term—delphic uncertainty—arising from variation over compatible world models that are all consistent with the same observational distribution. It builds on Conservative Q-Learning (Kumar et al., 2020) as the base offline RL algorithm, modifying its Bellman target with a delphic uncertainty penalty analogous to how CQL penalises epistemic uncertainty. The key departure from prior confounding-aware RL work (Lu et al., 2018a; Zhang and Bareinboim, 2020; Wang et al., 2021) is that those methods assume identifiability of the confounder through environment access or proxy variables, whereas this paper explicitly targets the nonidentifiable setting using variational inference over an ensemble of world models, positioning itself as complementary to sensitivity analysis frameworks (Kallus and Zhou, 2020) that require domain-expert-set bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hidden confounding is a pervasive and often unaddressed source of bias in offline RL for high-stakes domains like healthcare and autonomous driving, where the behavioural policy relies on unrecorded information. The paper's main open question is how to best approximate the set of compatible worlds W, which directly affects the calibration of delphic uncertainty; the current ensemble-based approach is a practical but potentially suboptimal approximation. Limitations include the focus on medically-motivated evaluation, the computational cost of training multiple world models (10–15 ensembles of 5 bootstraps each), and the moderate (rather than dramatic) performance gains over strong baselines like CQL. Future work on dynamic confounders, scalability to high-dimensional settings, and tighter theoretical characterisation of W would strengthen the approach.
