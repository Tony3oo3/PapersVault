## Problem

Federated Learning claims natural privacy protection since participants never share raw data, yet no prior work examines whether an attacker masquerading as a benign participant can invert the shared global model to recover other participants' data. This participant-role model inversion threat is harder to detect than gradient-hijacking or server-masquerading attacks, and the paper shows that state-of-the-art defenses (MID, BiDO) fail to mitigate it, leaving a critical gap in FL privacy evaluation.

## Input / Output

The attacker, acting as one of k selected participants per round, receives the FL global model ω_t and uses it to query class probabilities on generated images. The attacker also pre-trains a GAN on a public auxiliary dataset structurally similar to the target (e.g., MNIST digits 5–9 to attack digits 0–4, or 30,000 CelebA images to attack 1000 held-out identities). Output is a set of reconstructed images per round, scored by an independent evaluation classifier for attack accuracy and top-5 accuracy, plus FID to measure reconstruction quality and diversity.

## Method

FedInverse embeds three optimization-based MI attackers—GMI (WGAN with prior + identity loss), KED-MI (two-step GAN with soft-label distillation from the global model), and VMI (StyleGAN with variational KL regularization)—as a single participant in standard FL training, evaluating attack success at every communication round. The key technical contribution is an HSIC-based diversity regularizer: each attack epoch splits the generated batch into two halves, computes the Hilbert-Schmidt independence criterion between them, and adds it to the attack loss to penalize redundant, low-diversity reconstructions. This prevents the generator from collapsing toward the prior auxiliary distribution (e.g., generating digit 9 when targeting digit 4) and consistently improves attack accuracy by 3–10% and reduces FID across all three attack variants on CelebA, MNIST, and CIFAR-10.

## Results

On MNIST, KED-MI achieves 80.00% attack accuracy and 100.00% top-5 attack accuracy by FL round 3, with KED-MI-HSIC marginally improving to 80.20% attack accuracy. On CelebA (face recognition), KED-MI reaches 57.80% attack accuracy and 85.47% top-5 accuracy, while KED-MI-HSIC improves these to 60.13% and 85.80% respectively. GMI achieves lower but still significant leakage (56.00% attack accuracy on MNIST, 11.33% on CelebA), and HSIC consistently improves attack accuracy by 3–7% and reduces FID across all settings. Against SOTA defenses MID and BiDO, KED-MI still achieves 71.57% and 61.01% attack accuracy respectively, confirming that current defenses are ineffective in FL settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates FedInverse attacks against two SOTA model-inversion defenses: MID (Wang et al., AAAI 2021) and BiDO (Peng et al., KDD 2022). Both defenses fail to prevent data leakage in FL settings. On MNIST with MID, KED-MI achieves 71.57% attack accuracy and KED-MI-HSIC reaches 74.03% by round 4, while with BiDO the corresponding values are 61.01% and 66.85%. For GMI attacks, MID yields 50.00% (GMI) and 58.00% (GMI-HSIC) attack accuracy, and BiDO yields 40.00% and 42.00% respectively. In all cases, top-5 attack accuracy reaches 100% within 1–3 rounds regardless of defense, indicating that the defenses are data-oriented and not transferable to FL participant-role attacks. The paper concludes that no current defense can effectively mitigate this class of privacy leakage when the attacker masquerades as a benign FL participant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes three established model inversion attacks (GMI, KED-MI, VMI) originally designed for centralized deep learning into the federated learning setting, where the attacker masquerades as a benign participant rather than an external adversary. Prior FL security research focused on gradient inversion or model poisoning, but the participant-role MI threat was unexplored. The paper further introduces HSIC as a diversity regularizer for the attack generator, drawing on kernel-based independence measures from statistical learning theory to improve reconstruction quality. Conceptually, it bridges the MI attack literature and FL privacy evaluation by showing that the global model's averaging mechanism does not sufficiently obscure individual participant data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SOTA MI defenses (MID, BiDO) fail in FL settings is practically important: FL systems currently marketed as naturally privacy-preserving may be vulnerable to insider participant attacks with no effective countermeasure. FedInverse provides a reusable evaluation framework for FL practitioners to quantify their system's leakage risk before deployment. Key limitations include the white-box assumption (attacker knows model architecture), restriction to image modalities, and the fact that the attack requires access to structurally similar public data for GAN pre-training. Open questions include whether any defense can simultaneously preserve FL utility and resist participant-role MI attacks, and whether the findings generalize to non-image data or black-box FL deployments.
