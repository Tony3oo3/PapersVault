## Problem

Existing compressed federated learning methods face a fundamental trade-off: they either cannot accommodate arbitrary data heterogeneity and partial client participation, or they require stringent assumptions on compression errors such as bounded gradient dissimilarity or bounded compression error. No non-convex FL algorithm simultaneously handles all four challenges—arbitrary data heterogeneity, local updates, partial participation, and communication compression—relying solely on standard compressibility conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241103/cropped_page14_idx8.webp)

## Input / Output

Each participating client receives the global model x^t and global control variable c^t, performs K local stochastic gradient steps on its private non-iid data, and transmits back a single compressed increment variable δ_i^t (same dimensionality as the model) rather than the two variables (model update plus control variable update) required by original SCAFFOLD. The server aggregates these compressed messages to update the global model and control variable. The key structural property is that the compressed quantity is an increment that asymptotically vanishes as training converges, so compression errors disappear even under severe data heterogeneity—unlike compressing raw gradients, which introduces a persistent error floor proportional to the local gradient norm at the global optimum.

## Method

The paper first reformulates SCAFFOLD to show that the model update and control variable update share a common increment variable Δ_i^t, so only Δ_i^t needs to be communicated, halving uplink costs per client. SCALLION applies an ω-unbiased compressor to a scaled version of Δ_i^t, achieving state-of-the-art communication and computation complexities for non-convex FL under unbiased compression while supporting partial participation and arbitrary data heterogeneity. SCAFCOM extends this to biased (q²-contractive) compressors by introducing a local momentum variable v_i^t that accumulates gradient information across rounds, reducing the variance of the compressed message and mitigating the adverse effects of compression bias. Both methods require only L-smoothness and bounded stochastic gradient variance—no bounded gradient dissimilarity, bounded compression error, or averaged contraction assumptions—making them the first stochastic FL methods robust to arbitrary data heterogeneity, partial participation, local updates, and communication compression under standard compressibilities alone.

## Results

Theoretically, SCALLION achieves asymptotic communication complexity of (1+ω)/ε and computation complexity of (1+ω)/(Nε²) under unbiased compression, while SCAFCOM achieves 1/((1−q)ε) communication and 1/(Nε²) computation under biased compression — both supporting arbitrary data heterogeneity, partial participation, and standard compressibilities without additional assumptions. Empirically, SCAFCOM with TOP-0.01 (transmitting only 1% of entries) achieves test accuracy very close to full-precision SCAFFOLD on both MNIST and FMNIST, saving 20–100× uplink communication costs. SCALLION with 4-bit random dithering (~100× compression) produces training curves that essentially overlap SCAFFOLD, while 2-bit dithering saves even more communication at a slight accuracy cost in later rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FED-EF (Li & Li, 2023), SCAFCOM outperforms on both MNIST and FMNIST in training loss and test accuracy under identical TOP-r biased compressors, thanks to control variables and local momentum; theoretically, SCAFCOM improves the asymptotic communication complexity by a factor of 1/(1−q) and removes the (1−q)² slowdown in computation that FED-EF suffers under partial participation. Against FEDCOMGATE (Haddadpour et al., 2021), SCALLION achieves the same asymptotic communication complexity but without requiring a large uniform bound on compression errors (which is invalid for practical compressors like random sparsification), and additionally supports partial client participation. Against QSPARSE-SGD and LOCAL-SGD-C, SCAFCOM improves the communication complexity from 1/((1−q)²ε) to 1/((1−q)ε) and supports arbitrary data heterogeneity and partial participation, whereas both baselines require iid clients and full participation. Against full-precision SCAFFOLD, both SCALLION (4-bit dithering) and SCAFCOM (TOP-0.01) match test accuracy while reducing uplink communication by 20–100×. Against FED-SGD/FEDAVG, the compressed methods approach full-precision performance as compression assuages, confirming that the control-variable framework mitigates the interplay between client drift and compression distortion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SCAFFOLD (Karimireddy et al., 2020b), a controlled-averaging method that uses local and global control variables to counteract client drift under data heterogeneity. The authors derive an equivalent reformulation that halves uplink communication by transmitting only the increment variable Δ_i^t rather than both the model update and control variable update, then layer compression on top. This departs from prior compressed FL methods (FEDPAQ, FEDCOM, FEDCOMGATE, FED-EF) that either require iid data, bounded gradient dissimilarity, or non-standard compression assumptions, by showing that compressing the vanishing increment variable naturally eliminates compression-error accumulation even under arbitrary heterogeneity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCALLION and SCAFCOM are, to the authors' knowledge, the first stochastic FL methods that simultaneously tolerate arbitrary data heterogeneity, partial participation, local updates, and communication compression while relying only on standard compressibility definitions (ω-unbiased or q²-contractive). This removes a major theoretical barrier that forced practitioners to choose between robustness to heterogeneity and communication efficiency. However, empirical validation is limited to MNIST and FMNIST with small CNNs, leaving open whether the theoretical advantages (e.g., the 1/(1-q) vs 1/(1-q)² communication complexity gap) translate to meaningful gains on larger-scale, more heterogeneous FL deployments. The momentum-based SCAFCOM also suggests that error feedback may not be the optimal mechanism for biased compression in FL, a direction worth further exploration.
