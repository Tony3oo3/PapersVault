## Problem

Entropy-regularized RL methods such as Soft Q-Learning and Soft Actor-Critic are not invariant to changes in the action space: states with more actions receive more regularization, breaking the balance between reward and exploration. This is particularly problematic for state-dependent action spaces where different states are regularized inconsistently, and for practical scenarios where changing units or discretization granularity should not alter the optimal policy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a regularized MDP where the action space A(s) can vary per state (state-dependent actions). It takes the standard temperature parameter τ and the regularizer Ω, and produces a per-state adjusted temperature τ' = τ / L(Ω, A(s)) that normalizes the regularization effect. In the dynamic variant, the temperature is learned online via a modified dual-variable update where the target entropy is set as a fraction α of the regularizer's range rather than the negative action dimensionality used in SAC.

## Method

The paper defines the range L(Ω, A(s)) of a regularizer over an action space and proves that for a broad class of 'standard regularizers' (including entropy, KL divergence, and Tsallis entropy), this range grows monotonically with the number of actions, causing over-regularization at high-cardinality states. The fix is to divide the regularizer by its range, yielding a 'decoupled regularizer' with constant range across all action spaces. Two practical schemes are provided: a static temperature rescaling (τ' = τ/log|A(s)| for entropy) and a dynamic temperature mechanism that modifies SAC's automatic temperature update to target a fraction α of the regularizer's range instead of the negative dimensionality heuristic. The approach is general: it applies to any regularizer in the Geist et al. (2019) framework and requires only a one-line change to the Bellman backup or temperature update rule.

## Results

On the DeepMind Control Suite with static temperature (τ=0.25), decoupled SQL matches or improves test reward across all action scales (β ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 4}) compared to standard SQL. In the dynamic temperature setting, the proposed approach with α≈0.77 achieves performance comparable to SAC on standard [-1,1] actions, while SAC's heuristic becomes infeasible (target entropy below achievable minimum) for smaller action ranges like [-0.1, 0.1], causing learning failure. On the drug design task, decoupled SQL achieves a higher median reward than both standard SQL and GFlowNets throughout training, and discovers a greater number of distinct high-quality molecular modes. On the toy hypergrid MDP, standard SQL's episode length diverges for n>6 dimensions, whereas decoupled SQL remains stable and near-optimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Soft Actor-Critic (Haarnoja et al., 2018), the paper's dynamic temperature scheme wins decisively on non-standard action scales: SAC's heuristic of setting target entropy to −n (negative action dimensionality) becomes infeasible when the action range is small (e.g., [-0.1, 0.1]), driving the temperature to infinity and causing complete learning failure (Figure 4b), while the decoupled approach with α≈0.77 maintains stable performance. On the standard [-1,1] action range, both methods achieve similar test rewards, confirming the decoupled scheme is at least as good as SAC where SAC works. Against GFlowNets (Bengio et al., 2021) on the drug design MDP, decoupled SQL (using path consistency learning) achieves a higher median reward than GFN (using trajectory balance) throughout training and discovers more distinct molecular modes (Figure 5). Standard SQL, by contrast, over-regularizes hub states with many actions, biasing the policy toward larger molecules and producing fewer diverse modes. The paper also notes that GFN requires knowledge of inverse dynamics (which states can reach a given state) and specific structural assumptions (single initial state, no self-loops, deterministic transitions), whereas decoupled SQL has none of these requirements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the regularized MDP formalism of Geist et al. (2019) and the automatic temperature mechanism of Soft Actor-Critic (Haarnoja et al., 2018), identifying a structural flaw in both: the regularization strength implicitly scales with the action space size, so states with more actions receive more regularization. The key departure from Haarnoja et al. is replacing their dimension-based target entropy heuristic with a range-based constraint that reflects the actual geometry of the action space, making the approach generalizable to arbitrary regularizers (Tsallis entropy, KL, mellowmax) rather than being tied to entropy and negative dimensionality. The drug design experiments also position the work against GFlowNets (Bengio et al., 2021), showing that path-consistency-learning SQL with decoupled regularization can match or exceed GFN performance without requiring inverse dynamics knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: practitioners can change action units, rescale action ranges, or work with state-dependent action spaces without re-tuning temperature, which is a common and tedious hyperparameter search in practice. The drug design result is notable because Bengio et al. (2021) explicitly excluded SQL from their comparison, citing instability and a bias toward larger molecules; showing that decoupling resolves both issues validates the approach in a high-stakes application. Open questions remain around optimization-side scale invariance (the authors acknowledge they do not use scale-invariant optimizers or losses), the treatment of non-standard regularizers (e.g., πᵀAπ with non-identity A), and whether the range-normalization principle extends cleanly to continuous action spaces with differential entropy.
