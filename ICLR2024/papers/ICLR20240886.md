## Problem

Studying real-world human-LLM interactions requires access to diverse, multi-model conversation data, but no such dataset existed in the research community due to prohibitive hosting costs, vendor data secrecy, and difficulty incentivizing users to interact with multiple open LLMs. Existing alternatives (Anthropic HH, OpenAssistant, Chatbot Arena) are either single-model, small-scale, or lack the breadth of real user queries needed for safety, moderation, and benchmarking research.

## Input / Output

The dataset consists of raw, unprocessed conversation text in OpenAI API JSON format, each sample containing a conversation ID, model name, detected language tag (via Polyglot), and OpenAI moderation API output per message. PII is removed but unsafe conversations are intentionally retained for safety research. Downstream outputs include a fine-tuned 7B content moderator producing category labels with natural-language explanations, a 50-conversation jailbreak benchmark yielding per-model success rates, instruction-tuned Llama-2-7B variants evaluated on MMLU and MT-Bench, and Arena-Hard-200, a set of 200 prompts scored by GPT-4 as judge.

## Method

The dataset was collected over 5 months (April–August 2023) by hosting a free, registration-free LLM service on dozens of A100 GPUs serving 25 models, with Chatbot Arena's gamified battle interface and periodic leaderboard releases to sustain user engagement. For content moderation, Vicuna-7B is fine-tuned in a generative (not classifier) setup to produce explanations and category labels, trained on 5K GPT-4-annotated flagged messages plus 1K normal messages and 3K ShareGPT conversations. For the safety benchmark, jailbreak conversations are identified via OpenAI moderation API flags on model responses, then the top 5 attempts per model across 10 models are compiled into a 50-prompt set. For instruction tuning, two subsets are extracted: 'HighQuality' (45K conversations from proprietary models) and 'Upvote' (39K user-voted open-model conversations), used to fine-tune Llama-2-7B. For Arena-Hard-200, GPT-3.5-Turbo scores each Chatbot Arena prompt from 1–10 on problem-solving/creativity/truthfulness potential, and the 200 prompts scoring 9+ (agreed by GPT-3.5-Turbo, Claude-2, and GPT-4) are selected.

## Results

On the instruction-following use case, HighQuality-7B (fine-tuned on 45K conversations from proprietary models in LMSYS-Chat-1M) achieves 47.7 MMLU (5-shot) and 6.03 MT-Bench score, approaching Vicuna-7B-v1.5 (49.8 / 6.17) while using only 33M fine-tuning tokens vs. 370M. On content moderation, the fine-tuned Vicuna-moderator-7B reaches 0.70 one-shot Micro-F1, matching GPT-4 (0.69) and substantially outperforming the OpenAI text-moderation-latest API (0.36). On the safety benchmark, open models without safety training (Alpaca-13B: 74%, Vicuna-13B-v1.5: 66%) are far more vulnerable to jailbreaks than proprietary models (GPT-4: 34%, Claude-2: 18%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4, the paper's Vicuna-moderator-7B matches or slightly exceeds it on content moderation (0.70 vs. 0.69 one-shot Micro-F1), while GPT-4 remains a strong baseline on the safety benchmark with a 34% jailbreak success rate. Against the OpenAI text-moderation-latest API, Vicuna-moderator-7B achieves a 0.70 one-shot Micro-F1 versus the API's 0.36, a 34-point improvement, demonstrating that a small fine-tuned model can substantially outperform a dedicated moderation service. Against Vicuna-7B-v1.5 (trained on ShareGPT), HighQuality-7B trained on LMSYS-Chat-1M achieves 47.7 MMLU and 6.03 MT-Bench versus Vicuna's 49.8 and 6.17, a gap of only 2.1 and 0.14 points respectively, while using roughly 11× fewer fine-tuning tokens (33M vs. 370M). Against Llama-2-7B-chat, HighQuality-7B slightly underperforms on MT-Bench (6.03 vs. 6.27) but the base Llama-2-7B-chat scores 0.00/0.01 on the moderation task, highlighting that safety alignment does not transfer to moderation utility. Against Alpaca-13B, the safety benchmark shows Alpaca is the most jailbreak-vulnerable model (74% success rate) compared to GPT-4 (34%) and Claude-2 (18%), confirming that open models without safety training are significantly less robust.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM conversation datasets (Anthropic HH, OpenAssistant, Chatbot Arena's 33K conversations) to an order-of-magnitude larger scale (1M conversations, 25 models, 210K users) collected from a free public platform. Unlike synthetic datasets (Alpaca, UltraChat, Baize) where both prompts and responses are LLM-generated, LMSYS-Chat-1M preserves human-authored prompts, and unlike pre-LLM dialogue corpora (UbuntuDialogue, DailyDialog), it captures in-the-wild interactions with state-of-the-art models. The four use cases repurpose established techniques—fine-tuning for instruction following (as in Vicuna/ShareGPT), LLM-as-judge scoring (as in MT-Bench), and k-means clustering for topic analysis—on this new data rather than introducing novel methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a critical gap: prior to this work, no publicly available dataset offered 1M real-user, multi-model LLM conversations, making it a foundational resource for safety research, instruction tuning, and benchmark construction. The Arena-Hard-200 benchmark is likely to become a standard evaluation tool given its demonstrated ability to separate open from proprietary models more sharply than MT-Bench. Key limitations include a biased user population (LLM hobbyists rather than general users), absence of human preference annotations, and unfiltered low-quality/duplicate data, all of which constrain generalizability. Open questions include whether quarterly updates are sustainable, how to calibrate topic distributions after de-duplication, and whether the jailbreak patterns observed generalize beyond the specific models tested.
