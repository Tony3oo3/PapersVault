## Problem

Aligning text-to-image diffusion models with human preferences or other differentiable rewards has relied on sample-inefficient RL methods (e.g., DDPO) or supervised reweighting approaches that have not scaled to broadly improve generation quality across diverse prompts. The paper asks whether one can directly backpropagate reward gradients through the diffusion sampling process to fine-tune model parameters, and whether this can be done efficiently enough to train on hundreds of thousands of prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240188/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The fine-tuning procedure takes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 1.4 model, a differentiable reward function (e.g., HPSv2, PickScore, LAION Aesthetics, OWL-ViT detection scores, JPEG compressibility), and a prompt dataset, and produces LoRA weight matrices (rank 8 or 32) that are added to the frozen UNet's feedforward and cross-attention layers. At inference, the adapted model takes a text prompt and random 4×64×64 latent noise, runs 50 DDIM steps with classifier-free guidance (weight 7.5), and decodes the final latent to a 512×512 RGB image. A distinctive property is that LoRA weights can be linearly combined across rewards or scaled by a scalar to interpolate between the pre-trained and fine-tuned model without retraining.

## Method

DRaFT computes the reward gradient ∇_θ r(sample(θ, c, x_T), c) by backpropagating through the entire T-step DDIM sampling chain, using LoRA to limit trainable parameters and gradient checkpointing to store only input latents per step. The key practical finding is that full backpropagation causes exploding gradients, so DRaFT-K truncates the backward pass to only the last K steps (inserting a stop-gradient at step K), and K=1 yields the best reward-vs-compute tradeoff. DRaFT-LV further reduces gradient variance at K=1 by re-noising the generated image n=2 times with fresh Gaussian noise and summing the reward gradients over these additional one-step denoising predictions, achieving ~2× faster convergence than DRaFT-1 at only ~10% extra compute. The paper unifies DRaFT, DRaFT-K, DRaFT-LV, and ReFL under a single algorithm parameterized by where stop-gradient operations are placed in the sampling loop, showing ReFL (which evaluates reward on a one-step predicted clean image at a random timestep) is a special case equivalent to DRaFT-1 when the random timestep is fixed to the last step.

## Results

On the HPSv2 benchmark (averaged across Animation, Concept Art, Painting, and Photo categories), DRaFT-LV achieves the best score of 0.3536, outperforming DRaFT-1 (0.3467), ReFL (0.3427), and the base Stable Diffusion 1.4 (0.2695). DRaFT-5 (0.3500) and DRaFT-10 (0.3431) show that smaller K values are beneficial, while full backpropagation (DRaFT-50) underperforms at 0.3033 due to gradient explosion. In terms of sample efficiency, DRaFT is >200× faster than DDPO for aesthetic reward maximization, and DRaFT-LV trains approximately 2× faster than ReFL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPO (Black et al., 2023), DRaFT achieves the same aesthetic reward improvement >200× faster in terms of reward queries, since gradient-based optimization is far more sample-efficient than REINFORCE-style policy gradients. Against ReFL (Xu et al., 2023), DRaFT-1 slightly outperforms on HPSv2 (0.3467 vs. 0.3427 averaged) while being simpler to implement (deterministic K=1 vs. random timestep), and DRaFT-LV further improves to 0.3536 while training ~2× faster than ReFL. Against DOODL (Wallace et al., 2023), DRaFT matches or exceeds reward quality at standard inference speed, whereas DOODL requires ~60× slower per-prompt latent optimization at test time. Against the base Stable Diffusion 1.4 (0.2695), all DRaFT variants substantially improve HPSv2 scores, with DRaFT-LV gaining +0.084. Against GLIDE 1.4 (0.2355) and DALL-E 2 (0.2695), DRaFT-LV (0.3536) achieves the highest HPSv2 score among all compared systems, surpassing even SDXL Base 0.9 (0.2773) and Dreamlike Photoreal 2.0 (0.2786) from the same benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRaFT unifies and generalizes several prior gradient-based reward fine-tuning approaches for diffusion models. It builds most directly on ReFL (Xu et al., 2023), which backpropagates reward gradients through a single one-step denoising prediction at a random timestep; DRaFT shows ReFL is a special case of its general algorithm (Algorithm 1) where stop_gradient is inserted at a random step rather than at a fixed truncation point. The paper departs from RL-based methods (DDPO, DPOK) that treat sampling as a sequential decision problem and discard analytic gradient information, and from guidance methods that apply reward signals to intermediate noisy latents (risking out-of-distribution inputs). It also contrasts with DOODL, which backpropagates through sampling but optimizes the initial noise rather than model parameters, making it infeasible at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DRaFT provides a simple, highly efficient (>200× faster than RL, ~2× faster than ReFL) recipe for reward fine-tuning that is broadly applicable to any differentiable reward, demonstrated across aesthetics, human preferences, compressibility, object detection, and adversarial generation. The LoRA-based parameterization enables practical compositionality—linearly combining independently trained LoRA weights to mix reward effects without additional training—which opens a path toward multi-objective control of generative models. Key open questions remain: reward hacking and diversity collapse persist even with regularization (KL, early stopping, dropout), and the method has only been validated on Stable Diffusion 1.4, leaving unclear whether the K=1 truncation finding and efficiency gains transfer to larger or architecturally different diffusion models.
