## Problem

Most target shift adaptation methods assume a finite categorical target space, making them inapplicable to continuous regression targets where the importance weight function cannot be reduced to a finite set of parameters. The two existing approaches for continuous target shift (KMM and L2IWE) lack theoretical guarantees—neither consistency nor convergence rate bounds have been established—and both require solving quadratically constrained quadratic programs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241826/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source pairs (x, y) with continuous y ∈ [0,1] and unlabeled target features x ∈ [0,1]^p, with no target labels available in the testing domain. It produces importance weight estimates ω̂(y_i) at the n source data points, which are then plugged into a weighted ERM objective to train any regression model f ∈ F for the target domain. The entire weight estimation is optimization-free, reducing to a single n×n linear system solve rather than an iterative QP.

## Method

ReTaSA formulates continuous importance weight estimation as solving the Fredholm integral equation Tρ₀ = η (first kind), where T is a conditional expectation operator mapping L²(y)→L²(x) and η(x) = p_t(x)/p_s(x) − 1. Because this equation is ill-posed, the method applies Tikhonov regularization, yielding the first-order condition (αI + T*T)ρ = T*η. Kernel methods with generalized kernel functions (Nadaraya-Watson estimators) approximate the conditional expectation operators, collapsing the functional equation into a finite linear system (αI + C_{x|y}C_{y|x})ρ̂ = C_{x|y}η̂ that is solved in closed form. The paper establishes the first theoretical consistency and convergence rate bound (Theorem 1) for a continuous target shift estimator, showing that ρ̂_α → ρ₀ in probability under standard smoothness and bandwidth conditions.

## Results

On the SOCR dataset, ReTaSA achieves a Weight MSE of 0.025±0.009 and a ΔPrediction MSE improvement of 6.705±0.977%, substantially outperforming KMM (Weight MSE 0.477±0.269, ΔPred MSE 3.076±5.175%) and L2IWE (Weight MSE 0.178±0.025, ΔPred MSE −5.933±1.763%). On the Szeged Weather dataset, ReTaSA achieves a Weight MSE of 0.756±0.878 and a ΔPrediction MSE improvement of 19.567±15.679%, compared to KMM (2.343±1.488, 0.516±65.107%) and L2IWE (1.047±0.650, −8.400±27.622%). In synthetic experiments, ReTaSA reduces weight MSE by over 75% relative to Non-Adaptation, versus ~25% for KMM and ~60% for L2IWE, and improves prediction accuracy from ~15% to ~22% as sample size grows from 100 to 1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReTaSA consistently outperforms both KMM (Zhang et al., 2013) and L2IWE (Nguyen et al., 2016) on both real-world datasets. On SOCR, ReTaSA reduces Weight MSE by ~95% relative to KMM (0.025 vs. 0.477) and ~86% relative to L2IWE (0.025 vs. 0.178), while also achieving a positive ΔPrediction MSE of 6.7% where L2IWE actually degrades performance (−5.9%). On Szeged Weather, ReTaSA achieves a 19.6% prediction MSE improvement versus only 0.5% for KMM and −8.4% for L2IWE, and reduces Weight MSE by ~68% over KMM and ~28% over L2IWE. In the synthetic nonlinear regression study, ReTaSA also dominates both baselines across varying sample sizes, target shift magnitudes, and source-to-target size ratios, maintaining Weight MSE below 0.8 for all shift levels where KMM and L2IWE exceed 1.5. Notably, ReTaSA's standard deviations are smaller than both baselines, indicating greater estimation stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the target shift adaptation literature, which has been dominated by classification (label shift) methods that reduce importance weight estimation to a finite-dimensional linear system. The two prior works addressing continuous target shift—KMM (Zhang et al., 2013) and L2IWE (Nguyen et al., 2016)—rely on distribution matching via quadratically constrained quadratic programs and lack theoretical guarantees. ReTaSA departs from this by reformulating the problem as an ill-posed Fredholm integral equation of the first kind and applying Tikhonov regularization with kernel-based Nadaraya-Watson estimators, a technique borrowed from the nonparametric instrumental regression literature in econometrics (Carrasco et al., 2007; Darolles et al., 2011). The high-dimensional extension also repurposes the black-box shift estimation idea from Lipton et al. (2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a consistency guarantee and convergence rate for importance weight estimation under continuous target shift, filling a notable theoretical gap. The optimization-free solution (a single linear system solve) is practically attractive compared to the QP-based alternatives, and the method generalizes naturally to high dimensions via the black-box trick. Open questions remain: the paper assumes the target's support is a subset of the source's (excluding out-of-distribution cases), does not address uncertainty quantification for continuous targets, and the density ratio estimation step uses a simple plug-in approach whose comparison with more sophisticated methods (e.g., RuLSIF) is deferred to supplementary material.
