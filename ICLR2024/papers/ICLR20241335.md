## Problem

Nearly 40% of images in web-scale datasets like LAION contain text that overlaps with their captions, incentivizing contrastive models to learn OCR rather than visual features. Naively removing all text-containing images is suboptimal because a substantial fraction (≈19%) contain both useful visual cues and overlapping text. The paper addresses how to selectively filter out only the text-dominated samples while preserving those where visual features also contribute to caption alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241335/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a web-scale image-caption pool (e.g., 2M–128M pairs from LAION-400M or DataComp) and produces a filtered subset, typically retaining the top 50% of pairs ranked by masked-image-to-caption CLIP cosine similarity. Text regions are localized via the FAST detector and replaced with the average color of surrounding pixels; the original unmasked images from the retained subset are then used for training. A fixed compute budget (total samples seen = pool size) is maintained, so smaller filtered subsets receive proportionally more epochs.

## Method

T-MARS (Text-Masking and Re-Scoring) operates in three steps: (1) detect text bounding boxes with the FAST detector (localization only, not recognition, making it ~10× cheaper than text-matching baselines); (2) mask detected text regions by inpainting with the mean color of neighboring pixels; (3) compute the cosine similarity between the masked image embedding and the original caption embedding using a pre-trained CLIP model, then discard the lowest-scoring 50% of pairs. The key insight is that masking removes the OCR shortcut, so pairs whose alignment depended solely on text collapse in score and are filtered, while pairs with genuine visual features retain high masked similarity. The paper additionally contributes two baselines—C-SSFT (adapted second-split forgetting time) and C-RHO (adapted robust hold-out loss)—from the supervised hard-example-mining literature, and shows that intersecting T-MARS with these baselines yields additive accuracy gains because each method removes largely disjoint bad subsets.

## Results

On the DataComp medium-scale benchmark (128M pool), T-MARS achieves 33.0% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy, 36.3% VTAB, and 36.1% average across four evaluation suites, outperforming the previous best (Text-Match at 29.4% ImageNet) by 3.6 points. On the LAION 64M pool with ResNet-50, T-MARS reaches 32.47% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy (25.78% with ViT-B-32), a 6.4-point gain over unfiltered LAION and 3.7 points over Text-Match filtering. The intersection T-MARS ∩ C-SSFT further improves to 33.8% ImageNet on DataComp medium. Scaling experiments from 2M to 64M show near-linear accuracy gains as pool size grows exponentially, indicating the benefit of filtering does not saturate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Text-Match (Radenovic et al., 2023), T-MARS wins decisively: on the 64M LAION pool, T-MARS achieves 32.47% ImageNet vs. 29.11% for Text-Match (+3.4 pts), and on DataComp medium, 33.0% vs. 29.4% (+3.6 pts). Critically, T-MARS retains 46 of 96 images containing both visual and text features, whereas Text-Match retains only 21, because Text-Match discards all images with any caption-overlapping text. Against CLIP score filtering (the DataComp baseline and LAION's original curation), T-MARS outperforms by 6.5 points on DataComp medium ImageNet (33.0% vs. 27.3%) and by 6.8 points on the 64M LAION pool (32.47% vs. 25.66%); notably, CLIP score filtering alone performs *worse* than no filtering on several scales. On the DataComp leaderboard, T-MARS and T-MARS ∩ C-SSFT occupy two of the top three positions at medium scale, with other top entries requiring complex mixtures of multiple rules to achieve comparable or worse results. The paper also shows that T-MARS ∩ C-RHO at 32M compute (27.20% ImageNet) surpasses unfiltered LAION at 64M compute (26.34%), demonstrating that filtering bad data yields ~3× more utility than adding more unfiltered samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the emerging literature on data curation for web-scale vision-language pretraining, building directly on the hard-example-mining ideas of SSFT (Maini et al., 2022) and RHO (Mindermann et al., 2022) by adapting them to the contrastive, single-pass web-scale setting as C-SSFT and C-RHO. Its core method, T-MARS, departs from the prior text-matching approach of Radenovic et al. (2023), which removes all images containing caption-overlapping text, by instead masking text and re-scoring to retain images where visual features still correlate with the caption. The work is also situated within the DataComp benchmark (Gadre et al., 2023) and the broader CLIP/OpenCLIP training paradigm, where CLIP-score thresholding has been the standard curation baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that pruning bad examples yields roughly 3× the utility of adding new good examples, combined with the near-linear scaling of accuracy gains as data and compute grow exponentially, shifts the practical emphasis from simply collecting more data to carefully filtering existing pools. T-MARS is notably simple and scalable (text detection is ~10× cheaper than text recognition), making it accessible for practitioners working at web scale. Open questions include whether dynamic, per-epoch data re-scoring could further improve over the static subset approach, whether better inpainting or generative re-captioning could recover the visual information lost in masked regions, and whether the linear scaling trend persists at truly massive (billion-scale) pools.
