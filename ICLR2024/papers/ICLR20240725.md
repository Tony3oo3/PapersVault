## Problem

Image-goal navigation (ImageNav) requires the perception module to solve an extremely wide-baseline visual correspondence problem between the current observation and the goal exemplar image, yet prior end-to-end methods attempt to learn this implicitly from weak reward signals alone, which has proven insufficient. The correspondence bottleneck has been largely overlooked in the navigation literature, and existing work either resorted to panoramic multi-view inputs to ease the pose estimation sub-problem or relied on explicit feature matching modules that break end-to-end differentiability.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives a single 112×112 RGB observation image and a 112×112 RGB goal image, and selects one action from {MOVE FORWARD 0.25 m, TURN LEFT 10°, TURN RIGHT 10°, STOP} (plus LOOK UP / LOOK DOWN for Instance-ImageNav). The binocular encoder b(x_t, x*) produces a 3136-dim latent embedding encoding goal direction and relative pose, while the monocular encoder m(x_t) produces a 512-dim embedding for scene-level perception; both are concatenated with the previous action embedding and fed through a single-layer GRU whose hidden state drives a softmax action head and a value head. Navigation succeeds when STOP is called within 1 m geodesic distance of the goal.

## Method

DEBiT (Dual Encoder Binocular Transformer) pairs a large-capacity binocular ViT (Siamese encoder + cross-attention decoder, following CroCo's architecture) with a small half-width ResNet-18 monocular encoder, trained via a three-stage pipeline: (1) CroCo cross-view completion pre-training on 1.8 M rendered image pairs, where the binocular model reconstructs masked patches of one view from the other, teaching low-level geometric correspondence; (2) fine-tuning on a new 68.8 M-pair RPEV dataset that supervises relative translation, rotation, and a visibility score (fraction of goal-image patches visible in the observation), with pose supervision gated by a visibility threshold τ; (3) PPO reinforcement learning of the GRU policy and monocular encoder from scratch, while the binocular encoder is either frozen (only the projection head g is trained) or adapted via AdaptFormer adapter layers trained jointly with the policy. The key insight is that patch-level correspondence solutions emerge naturally in the decoder's cross-attention maps during pre-training without any explicit correspondence supervision, and that early-fusion cross-attention (as opposed to late-fusion Siamese comparison) is architecturally necessary to exploit the geometric learning signals from the pretext tasks.

## Results

On ImageNav (Gibson-val, mono-view), DEBiT-L with RL-trained adapters achieves 94.0% SR and 71.7% SPL after 200M steps, surpassing the previous best OVRL-v2 (82.0% SR, 58.7% SPL) by +12 points in both metrics. On Instance-ImageNav (HM3D), the same model reaches 61.1% SR (max) and 33.5% SPL (max), outperforming the prior SOTA feature-matching method (Krantz et al., 2023) at 56.1% SR and 23.3% SPL. The frozen (no-adapter) DEBiT-B variant already achieves 83.0% SR and 55.6% SPL on ImageNav, competitive with the strongest prior methods while using only 200M training steps versus 500M for most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEBiT with adapters (94.0% SR, 71.7% SPL) decisively outperforms all prior end-to-end methods on ImageNav. Against the strongest prior baseline OVRL-v2 (Yadav et al., 2023; 82.0% SR, 58.7% SPL), it gains +12 SR and +13 SPL while using half the training steps (200M vs. 500M). The Visual Cortex model VC1-ViT-L (Majumdar et al., 2023) reports 81.6% SR but no SPL; DEBiT-L without adapters already matches its SR (82.0%) and reports 59.6% SPL. Earlier methods—ZSON (36.9% SR), ZSEL (29.2% SR), OVRL (54.2% SR), and the Siamese hwRN18 baselines (10.1% SR)—are all substantially outperformed. On Instance-ImageNav, DEBiT-L + adapters (61.1% SR, 33.5% SPL) surpasses the feature-matching SOTA of Krantz et al. (2023; 56.1% SR, 23.3% SPL) by +5 SR and +10 SPL, demonstrating that explicit correspondence computation is not required. The modular ANS + DEBiT-L integration (32.0% SR) underperforms the end-to-end agent, confirming that latent embeddings carry richer information than raw pose/visibility values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CroCo (Weinzaepfel et al., 2022), a cross-view completion pretext task for 3D vision, and repurposes it as the first stage of a two-phase pre-training pipeline for image-goal navigation. It departs from the dominant Siamese (late-fusion) architecture paradigm in ImageNav (Zhu et al., 2017; Mezghani et al., 2022; Majumdar et al., 2023) by introducing a binocular ViT with cross-attention that performs early patch-level fusion, and adds a novel RPEV (relative pose + visibility estimation) pretext task tailored to the navigation setting. The adapter-based fine-tuning strategy draws on AdaptFormer (Chen et al., 2022b), and the modular integration proof-of-concept reuses ANS (Chaplot et al., 2020b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that geometric correspondence solutions emerge in a navigation agent's perception module without any explicit correspondence supervision, provided the right pretext tasks and architecture are used — a finding that could reshape how perception is designed for embodied agents. The Instance-ImageNav result (61.1% SR) surpasses the prior SOTA that relied on explicit feature matching, suggesting that end-to-end differentiable pipelines can handle out-of-distribution camera intrinsics without modular matching components. Key limitations include the 112×112 input resolution, pre-training conducted only under equal-intrinsics conditions, and evaluation entirely in simulation; extending to real-world deployment and higher resolutions remains open.
