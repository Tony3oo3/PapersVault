## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, PixelNeRF) produce blurry, averaged predictions in unobserved regions because they make single deterministic predictions rather than sampling from a posterior. Conversely, generative 3D methods are limited to bounded, canonically-aligned object-centric scenes and cannot handle large-scale, in-the-wild scenes with arbitrary camera poses. No prior work provides a principled way to integrate image-based rendering into a diffusion model without the model collapsing to trivial pseudo-3D solutions like flat textured planes.

## Input / Output

The model takes a variable number (6–8 during training, 1–6 at test time) of RGB images with relative camera poses (from COLMAP/SfM) as input, requiring no masks, depths, or canonical alignment. It outputs an explicit IB-planes 3D scene representation that can be rendered at up to 1024×1024 resolution from arbitrary viewpoints, along with depth maps. Training operates at 96×96 or 256×256 resolution, and the model supports both unconditional generation (zero conditioning images) and conditional reconstruction (one or more noise-free conditioning views with t=0).

## Method

GIBR introduces IB-planes, a neural scene representation that places per-view 2D feature maps in camera view frusta (as in PixelNeRF) but outputs them jointly through a multi-view U-Net with cross-view linear attention, making the representation strictly more expressive by allowing features to be arranged based on other views. Polar (equirectangular) features are added to define geometry outside the union of view frusta, and features are fused across views via element-wise max-pooling before an MLP decodes density and color. The denoising diffusion model operates in image space: at each step, the U-Net encoder produces IB-planes features from noisy multi-view images, which are rendered via volumetric ray-marching to yield the denoised prediction, guaranteeing 3D consistency throughout the diffusion process. A key training trick—representation dropout—removes the features of the target view when rendering to that view, preventing trivial flat-plane solutions while still allowing latent noise from all views to flow into the 3D scene through cross-view attention. Training uses an L1 photometric diffusion loss with only ~5% of rays rendered per step for efficiency, and supports varying view counts via setwise max-pooling of pose embeddings.

## Results

GIBR achieves the best single-view 3D reconstruction across all datasets, e.g. PSNR_D of 18.09 on CO3D apple (vs. 16.71 for RenderDiff++ and 16.25 for PixelNeRF++) and 29.74 on ShapeNet cars (vs. 25.50 for RenderDiff++). For multi-view (6-image) reconstruction, GIBR reaches PSNR_D of 33.46 on ShapeNet and 22.09 on MVImgNet, substantially outperforming all baselines. On unconditional generation, GIBR achieves FID_D of 50.5 on CO3D apple and 99.8 on MVImgNet, roughly halving the FID of RenderDiff++ (149.2 and 234.1 respectively), though Viewset Diffusion* slightly edges it on ShapeNet (56.0 vs. 62.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIBR outperforms all four baselines on single-view and multi-view 3D reconstruction across every dataset. Against RenderDiff++ (the closest generative baseline), GIBR gains 1.4–3.9 dB PSNR on single-view reconstruction and 1.6–6.6 dB on multi-view, with the largest margin on ShapeNet cars (29.74 vs. 25.50 PSNR_D). Against the non-generative PixelNeRF++, GIBR consistently leads by 1–4 dB PSNR and substantially lower LPIPS, confirming the benefit of sampling from a posterior rather than predicting a blurred mean. Viewset Diffusion* is competitive on the simpler, canonical ShapeNet dataset (28.00 vs. 29.74 PSNR_D single-view) but degrades sharply on in-the-wild CO3D and MVImgNet scenes (13.18 vs. 16.07 on CO3D hydrant), where its fixed voxel grid cannot adapt capacity. On unconditional generation (FID_D), GIBR nearly halves the FID of RenderDiff++ on all datasets (e.g. 50.5 vs. 149.2 on CO3D apple) and beats Viewset Diffusion* on CO3D and MVImgNet, though VSD* retains a slight edge on ShapeNet (56.0 vs. 62.8) due to its canonical alignment advantage. SparseFusion (score-distillation) is only reported on CO3D hydrant and trails GIBR by 4 dB PSNR (12.06 vs. 16.07).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-based rendering line (PixelNeRF, IBRNet) by replacing per-image independent feature extraction with a joint multi-view U-Net using cross-view attention, yielding the IB-planes representation that is strictly more expressive. It builds on the denoising diffusion framework of Ho et al. (2020) and departs from prior diffusion-based 3D generation methods (RenderDiffusion, Viewset Diffusion) that rely on bounded triplane or voxel-grid representations requiring canonical scene alignment. The key conceptual departure is the principled representation dropout mechanism, which prevents the model from collapsing to trivial per-camera plane solutions without requiring held-out views, canonical poses, or additional regularizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GIBR is the first diffusion model to unify 3D scene generation and reconstruction for unbounded, in-the-wild scenes using only RGB images and relative camera poses—no masks, depths, or canonical alignment. This removes a major practical barrier to applying generative 3D models to real-world data collected with a phone camera and COLMAP. Key limitations include the assumption of static scenes and slower training than 2D diffusion due to volumetric ray-marching; open questions include extending to dynamic scenes, scaling to even larger environments, and whether the representation dropout generalizes to other 3D-aware generative architectures.
