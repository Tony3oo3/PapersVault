## Problem

Designing RNA sequences that fold into a specified tertiary structure remains largely underexplored, despite the success of protein inverse folding. Direct transplantation of protein design methods fails because RNA exhibits greater structural complexity, conformational flexibility, and suffers from extreme data scarcity (less than 1% of the high-resolution structures available for proteins). No systematic, data-driven pipeline existed for tertiary structure-based RNA design with a fair benchmark.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RNA tertiary structure as 3D coordinates of six backbone atoms (P, O5', C5', C4', C3', O3') per nucleotide, modeled as an attributed graph with K=30 nearest neighbors, and additionally derives the secondary structure in dot-bracket notation from the same coordinates. It outputs a nucleotide sequence over {A, U, C, G} of the same length as the input structure. Sequences are capped at 500 nucleotides to manage computational cost, and the secondary structure constraint is enforced at inference by correcting paired positions to satisfy canonical base-pairing rules.

## Method

RDesign represents RNA tertiary structure as an attributed graph with intra-nucleotide features (dihedral angles via sin/cos, RBF-encoded distances, and local-coordinate directions) and inter-nucleotide features (quaternion orientation encoding, RBF distances, and directions), processed by a 3-layer MPNN encoder. The key training innovation is a hierarchical contrastive representation learning framework: cluster-level learning groups structures by TM-score similarity to attract topologically similar embeddings, while confidence-aware sample-level learning uses Gaussian-perturbed structures weighted by exp(-RMSD) to capture local geometric invariance. Both levels project graph-level representations onto a hyperspherical space to explicitly impose data relationships within a bounded geometry, improving data efficiency. A secondary structure imposing strategy sharpens predictions at paired positions via a lower temperature (τ'=0.5) in the cross-entropy loss and corrects mismatched base pairs at inference. The total loss combines supervised cross-entropy with the two contrastive terms weighted by λ=0.5.

## Results

On the curated RNA tertiary structure benchmark (223 test structures), RDesign achieves 41.53% recovery and 40.89% Macro-F1 overall, substantially outperforming all baselines. The best baseline (SeqRNN h=256) reaches only 28.24% recovery and 13.64% Macro-F1, while the best structure-based baseline (StructGNN) achieves 28.23% recovery and 24.87% Macro-F1. On external datasets, RDesign generalizes strongly, reaching 56.12% recovery on Rfam and 50.12% on RNA-Puzzles, compared to 33.34% and 37.10% for the best secondary-structure-based baseline (eM2dRNAs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDesign outperforms all six categories of baselines across every metric and dataset. Against protein-design-derived baselines (StructGNN, GraphTrans, PiFold from Ingraham et al. 2019 and Gao et al. 2022), RDesign gains ~13 percentage points in recovery on the benchmark (41.53% vs. 28.23% for StructGNN) and ~28 points on Rfam (56.12% vs. 27.64%), demonstrating that direct transfer of protein structure modeling to RNA is fundamentally insufficient. Against sequence-only baselines (SeqRNN, SeqLSTM), RDesign achieves roughly 13–16 points higher recovery, confirming that structural information is essential. Against secondary-structure-based RNA design methods (MCTS-RNA, LEARNA, eM2dRNAs, aRNAque), RDesign leads by 17–23 points in recovery on Rfam (56.12% vs. 33.34% for eM2dRNAs) and 13–19 points on RNA-Puzzles (50.12% vs. 37.10%), showing that tertiary structure modeling with hierarchical contrastive learning captures information beyond what secondary structure constraints alone can provide. Notably, the gap between recovery and Macro-F1 for most baselines is large (e.g., SeqRNN h=128: 26.23% recovery but only 17.74% Macro-F1), indicating they over-predict high-frequency nucleotides, whereas RDesign maintains a small gap (41.53% vs. 40.89%), reflecting balanced per-nucleotide prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of protein design and RNA biology. While protein inverse folding has been extensively studied with GNN-based approaches (GraphTrans, PiFold), the authors demonstrate that directly transplanting these methods to RNA fails due to RNA's greater structural complexity and the extreme data scarcity (RNA structures constitute <1% of protein structures). The paper repurposes the GNN message-passing paradigm but introduces a fundamentally different structural encoding (all six backbone atoms with dihedral angles, RBFs, and quaternion orientations rather than Cα-centric modeling) and a hierarchical contrastive learning objective in hyperspherical space to compensate for limited training data. The secondary structure imposing strategy draws on the well-established dot-bracket notation and canonical base-pairing rules as explicit prior knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic benchmark and a reliable baseline for tertiary structure-based RNA design, a task that has been largely underexplored due to the scarcity of high-resolution RNA 3D structures. The finding that protein design methods perform no better than sequence-only baselines on RNA is an important negative result that redirects the field away from naive transfer. Key open questions include wet-lab validation of designed sequences, scalability to longer RNA molecules (>500 nt), and whether the hyperspherical contrastive learning framework generalizes to other data-scarce molecular design problems. The current ~41% recovery rate, while substantially above baselines, indicates significant room for improvement before the method is practically useful for therapeutic RNA design.
