## Problem

Domain shift in time series forecasting—where the target environment deviates from training data—has been far less studied than in classification, and existing deep forecasting architectures like N-BEATS lack any mechanism to learn domain-invariant representations. The doubly residual stacking principle that gives N-BEATS its forecasting power makes straightforward one-shot feature alignment infeasible, as each stack's inputs are entangled with residual operations from all prior stacks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a lookback window of α=50 consecutive time series values and produces a forecast of β=10 future values. Training uses K=3 source domains (e.g., commodity, income, interest rate from FRED or pressure, rain, temperature from NCEI) with 75,000 sliding-window instances per domain, while evaluation targets unseen domains under three shift protocols (ODG, CDG, IDG) of increasing severity. The data spans multiple frequencies (daily through yearly), and the model is evaluated via sMAPE and MASE.

## Method

The key architectural contribution is stack-wise feature alignment: for each of M=3 stacks, a composite feature map g^m is defined by composing the feature extractor ψ^m with L−1 residual operations within the current stack and L residual operations from each of the m−1 preceding stacks, followed by a normalization function σ (softmax or tanh) that removes domain-specific scale information. The alignment loss minimizes the maximum pairwise Sinkhorn divergence (a debiased entropic-regularized Wasserstein-2 approximation computed via GeomLoss) between the pushforward measures (σ∘g^m)_#P_X^k across source domains, for each stack m. The total training objective combines the sMAPE forecasting loss with λ times the sum of per-stack alignment losses, optimized via an alternating scheme: forecasting parameters (Θ↓, Θ↑) are updated with fixed feature extractors Φ, then Φ is updated under the combined loss. A representation learning bound (Theorem 3.6) shows the stack-wise Sinkhorn alignment loss is upper-bounded by a constant times the pairwise entropic Wasserstein distance on the input space, justifying the approach theoretically.

## Results

On FRED (financial data), Feature-aligned N-BEATS-G achieves the best ODG sMAPE of 0.150 (vs. 0.172 for vanilla N-BEATS-G, 0.570 for Autoformer, and 1.214 for Informer), with the improvement being most pronounced under severe domain shift. On NCEI (weather data), N-HiTS+FA reaches 0.713 sMAPE in ODG, outperforming all transformer and linear baselines (1.112–1.630). Across all three shift protocols (ODG, CDG, IDG), the +FA variants consistently reduce sMAPE by 2–15% over their unaligned counterparts, with the largest gains in ODG where domain shift is most severe. Runtime remains practical at 0.68–0.97 sec/iteration for the proposed models versus 0.04–0.58 sec for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed stack-wise feature alignment (+FA) consistently improves upon all three N-BEATS-family backbones: N-BEATS-G+FA reduces ODG FRED sMAPE from 0.172 to 0.150 (−12.8%), N-HiTS+FA from 0.148 to 0.134 (−9.5%), and N-BEATS-I+FA from 0.232 to 0.214 (−7.8%). Against transformer-based baselines, the gap is dramatic: in ODG FRED, N-BEATS-G+FA (0.150) outperforms Autoformer (0.570) by 3.8× and Informer (1.214) by 8.1× in sMAPE. Linear baselines (NLinear, DLinear) are competitive only in IDG FRED (0.197, 0.843) but degrade severely under ODG/CDG, with DLinear reaching sMAPE > 2,200 on MASE. The improvement from +FA is largest under ODG (severe cross-superdomain shift) and smallest under IDG (same-superdomain shift), confirming that the alignment loss is most beneficial when domain shift is pronounced. Runtime overhead is modest: +FA adds ~0.4–0.6 sec/iteration over the base model, remaining far below the 314 sec/iteration cost of the classic Wasserstein-2 ablation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the doubly residual stacking architecture of N-BEATS (Oreshkin et al., 2019) into a domain-generalized representation learning framework by introducing stack-wise alignment of marginal feature measures via the Sinkhorn divergence. It departs from prior domain generalization work in time series (Hu et al., 2020; Jin et al., 2022), which relies on target domain data (domain adaptation), by formulating a purely source-domain training objective grounded in the H-divergence error analysis of Albuquerque et al. (2019). The key conceptual departure from standard feature alignment is the stack-wise (rather than one-shot or block-wise) alignment, motivated by the hierarchical residual structure of N-BEATS, and the substitution of the intractable H-divergence with the computationally efficient Sinkhorn divergence (Feydy et al., 2019; Chizat et al., 2020), justified by a Lipschitz-based representation learning bound (Theorem 3.6).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a gap in time series forecasting where domain shift has been far less studied than in classification, and provides a theoretically grounded, practically feasible method that requires no target domain information. The stack-wise alignment design is a nontrivial architectural insight that avoids gradient vanishing/exploding in deep residual stacks while preserving N-BEATS's interpretability. Open questions include the convergence analysis of the alternating optimization, the theoretical gap between Sinkhorn divergence and H-divergence, and whether the approach extends beyond MLP-based architectures to transformers. The improvements, while consistent, are modest in absolute magnitude, and the evaluation is limited to two real-world data sources (FRED, NCEI) with relatively short horizons.
