## Problem

While dynamic NeRF methods successfully reconstruct scenes from monocular videos, their semantic representations remain underexplored. Simply adding a semantic head to point-based dynamic NeRF methods fails because individual points lack motion information, forcing the model to overfit to training views and limiting generalization to novel scenes or few-label settings. Additionally, extracting 3D flow features from 2D video frames introduces a 2D-to-3D ambiguity in the viewing direction that must be resolved for semantic supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 12 monocular video frames (one per camera in a 12-camera rig) with known poses, precomputed optical flow maps from RAFT, and depth maps, along with pixel-wise semantic labels during training. It outputs a continuous 3D semantic field that renders per-pixel semantic logits along camera rays via volume integration, enabling novel semantic view synthesis, instance-level scene editing (by zeroing densities of target instances), semantic completion from 50% labeled frames, and dynamic scene tracking from 25% labeled frames.

## Method

Instead of learning semantics from individual 3D points, Semantic Flow learns from continuous point trajectories (flows) that encode motion information. A flow network (MLP with residual links) predicts 3D trajectories, and a flow feature aggregation module projects each trajectory point onto 2D frames to sample local features from a frozen SlowOnly encoder, concatenating them with positional embeddings of flow displacements. A multi-head flow attention module then extracts motion-aware semantic features, which a semantic MLP converts to per-flow logits. To resolve the 2D-to-3D ambiguity, volume densities of the dynamic field serve as opacity priors in a volume rendering integral that aggregates flow logits along each camera ray, enabling supervision with 2D semantic labels via cross-entropy. A separate static background field (ResNet18 encoder with random non-occluded view sampling) is pretrained first, then jointly optimized with the dynamic field using a blending weight, with an additional temporal semantic consistency loss enforcing label stability across warped rays.

## Results

On the Semantic Dynamic Scene Dataset, Semantic Flow achieves the best mIoU across all seven scenes when learning from multiple scenes (e.g., 0.844 on Balloon1, 0.839 on Balloon2, 0.703 on Jumping) and on novel scene adaptation (e.g., 0.884 on Umbrella, 0.765 on Truck). In the few-label settings, Semantic Flow reaches 0.961 Total Acc / 0.818 mIoU on semantic completion (50% labels) and 0.942 Total Acc / 0.767 mIoU on dynamic scene tracking (25% labels), outperforming MonoNeRF (0.956/0.786 and 0.935/0.716) and DynNeRF (0.934/0.738 and 0.896/0.660). The mIoU gains are particularly large on novel scene adaptation, where Semantic Flow improves over MonoNeRF by up to 35 mIoU points (e.g., 0.765 vs. 0.425 on Truck).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Semantic Flow consistently outperforms both DynNeRF+semantic head and MonoNeRF+semantic head on mIoU across all seven scenes, with the largest margins on novel scene adaptation (e.g., +34 mIoU on Truck: 0.765 vs. 0.425 for MonoNeRF; +38 mIoU on Playground: 0.742 vs. 0.393 for MonoNeRF). DynNeRF suffers the most from cross-scene confusion due to its position-embedding-based semantics, yielding the lowest mIoU on Balloon2 (0.229) and Playground (0.387). MonoNeRF, while competitive on accuracy, lags significantly on mIoU because its point-based features lack motion information, leading to blurry boundaries on dynamic objects. In the few-label settings (Table 2), Semantic Flow also beats the 2D video segmentation baseline DeAOT by a wide margin (0.818 vs. 0.412 mIoU on completion; 0.767 vs. 0.359 on tracking). In the extended appendix (Table 9), Semantic Flow also surpasses NeRF+time, NSFF, RoDyNeRF, and D-NeRF (all with added semantic heads) on mIoU, confirming that flow-based semantic learning generalizes better than point-based approaches across the full range of dynamic NeRF architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic NeRF lineage (NeRF → D-NeRF → DynNeRF → MonoNeRF) by introducing a semantic representation that learns from continuous 3D flows rather than individual points. While prior dynamic NeRF methods reconstruct geometry and appearance from spatio-temporal point trajectories, and static semantic NeRF methods (Semantic NeRF, NeSF) attach semantic heads to per-point features, Semantic Flow departs by treating the flow of each point as the fundamental semantic unit, using a flow attention module to extract motion-conditioned semantics. The use of volume densities as opacity priors for integrating flow-level semantic logits along camera rays is a key conceptual bridge that resolves the 2D-to-3D ambiguity when supervising 3D semantics from 2D labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to learn semantic fields of dynamic scenes from monocular video in a flow-based manner, enabling downstream tasks (instance-level editing, semantic completion, dynamic tracking, cross-scene adaptation) that were infeasible with point-based semantic heads. The strong generalization with only 25% labels for tracking suggests flow features encode transferable motion semantics. However, the evaluation is limited to 7 synthetic scenes from a single dataset, reliance on pretrained optical flow (RAFT) introduces a quality bottleneck, and the approach has not been validated on real-world dynamic scenes or at scale, leaving open whether the flow-attention paradigm generalizes beyond controlled environments.
