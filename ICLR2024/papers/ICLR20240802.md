## Problem

Local supervision methods aim to replace backpropagation with layer-wise updates, but they face a trade-off: Local Learning (LL) achieves competitive accuracy using module-wise auxiliary networks at the cost of substantial extra parameters and memory, while Forward Learning (FL) eliminates both BP and auxiliary networks yet suffers from poor performance because its contrastive objectives are vulnerable to task-irrelevant information in local features. The paper asks whether a contrastive objective can be designed that effectively discards task-irrelevant information without relying on auxiliary networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240802/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard grayscale (28×28 MNIST/Fashion-MNIST) or RGB (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, SVHN; 96×96 STL-10) images and produces class labels through a final cross-entropy classifier. Uniquely, because every intermediate layer is trained with the dictionary contrastive loss against a shared label embedding dictionary, any layer can independently generate a prediction via argmax dot-product with the pooled embeddings, and the dot products themselves yield spatially-resolved saliency maps that highlight label-relevant image regions.

## Method

DCL replaces the standard in-batch contrastive loss (which compares local features to each other) with a dictionary contrastive loss L_dict that maximizes the dot-product similarity between a layer's pooled local feature vector and its corresponding label embedding vector while minimizing similarity to all other label embeddings in a dictionary D^Z of Z vectors. Each label is mapped to a C_D-dimensional embedding vector (initialized as standard normal), and a 1D average pooling operator aligns the embedding dimension to each layer's channel count. A stop-gradient operator on each layer's input prevents error signals from propagating backward, making the training fully forward-pass compatible. Two variants are studied: DCL-S uses static (frozen) embeddings for the strict FL setting, while DCL uses adaptive embeddings updated by L_dict gradients, approaching BP/LL accuracy with only Z×C_D extra parameters. The paper proves that minimizing L_dict maximizes a lower bound on the mutual information I(h,y), and empirically shows that L_dict reduces task-irrelevant information I(h,r) in local features to levels comparable to those achieved by auxiliary networks.

## Results

On the VGG8B architecture, DCL (adaptive label embeddings) achieves test errors of 0.33% (MNIST), 8.64% (CIFAR-10), 31.75% (CIFAR-100), 2.19% (SVHN), and 22.87% (STL-10), outperforming BP on SVHN (2.19 vs. 2.29) and STL-10 (22.87 vs. 33.08). In the FL scenario with static embeddings (DCL-S), the method achieves 25.86% error on CIFAR-10 and 54.21% on CIFAR-100 with Conv networks, substantially outperforming all other FL baselines. DCL introduces only 5.12K additional parameters (Z×C_D) compared to 9.53M for LL-predsim, while also reducing peak GPU memory from 1291 MiB to 747 MiB on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BP, DCL wins on SVHN (2.19 vs. 2.29) and STL-10 (22.87 vs. 33.08) but trails on MNIST (0.33 vs. 0.26), CIFAR-10 (8.64 vs. 5.99), and CIFAR-100 (31.75 vs. 26.20). Among FL baselines, DCL-S (Conv) outperforms CaFo on CIFAR-10 (25.86 vs. 32.57) and CIFAR-100 (54.21 vs. 59.24) but loses on MNIST (3.21 vs. 1.20); DCL (FC) beats FF (35.12 vs. 41.00), PFF (35.12 vs. 50.14), DRTP (35.12 vs. 51.27), and SymBa (35.12 vs. 40.91) on CIFAR-10, with similar margins on CIFAR-100. Against LL methods from Nøkland & Eidnes (2019), DCL consistently outperforms LL-bpf across all six datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100: 31.75 vs. 37.60) and is competitive with single-loss variants LL-cont, LL-pred, and LL-sim, though it trails the two-loss LL-predsim (e.g., CIFAR-10: 8.64 vs. 5.58). Compared to LL-contrec from Wang et al. (2020), DCL achieves lower error on MNIST (0.33 vs. 0.65), F-MNIST (5.52 vs. 5.71), CIFAR-100 (31.75 vs. 31.35 is close), SVHN (2.19 vs. 2.34), and STL-10 (22.87 vs. 29.74), while LL-contrec edges ahead on CIFAR-10 (9.02 vs. 8.64 is actually DCL winning). Critically, DCL achieves these results with only 5.12K extra parameters versus 9.53M for LL-predsim and 918K–2.07M for other LL variants, while also using less GPU memory than both BP and all LL methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of local learning (LL) and forward learning (FL), two alternatives to backpropagation. It builds directly on the contrastive learning framework for local supervision established by Wang et al. (2020) and the InfoNCE objective of Oord et al. (2018), but departs from both by replacing in-batch feature-feature comparisons with comparisons against a fixed dictionary of label embedding vectors. The theoretical analysis explicitly follows the mutual information lower-bound proof from Wang et al. (2020), extending it to the dictionary contrastive setting. The method is positioned as a solution to a specific failure mode identified in FL: without auxiliary networks (as in Nøkland & Eidnes, 2019), standard contrastive objectives like L_feat are susceptible to task-irrelevant information, a problem the paper attributes to the absence of the filtering role that auxiliary networks play in LL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic parameter efficiency gain (5.12K additional parameters vs. 9.53M for LL-predsim) while achieving competitive accuracy makes local supervision without auxiliary networks practically viable for the first time, removing a major barrier to deploying BP-free training. The saliency map interpretability and semantic clustering of adaptive label embeddings offer a qualitative window into what the network learns at each layer, which is absent in standard BP. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet-level datasets, whether it extends to non-classification tasks (segmentation, detection), and whether the residual performance gap with BP on harder datasets (CIFAR-100, STL-10) can be closed. The relationship to neural collapse, noted in Appendix I, suggests a deeper theoretical connection that remains underexplored.
