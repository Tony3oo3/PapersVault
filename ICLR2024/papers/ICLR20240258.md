## Problem

Spectral clustering is the most effective method for recovering cluster structure in well-clustered graphs, yet almost nothing is known about differentially private variants of these spectral methods. This paper bridges that gap by providing an (ε,δ)-DP algorithm for well-clustered graphs with k nearly-balanced clusters whose misclassification ratio nearly matches the best-known non-private algorithms, and by showing that pure ε-DP algorithms necessarily incur substantial error.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an undirected graph G=(V,E) with n vertices and m edges, along with privacy parameters (ε, δ) and a regularizer parameter λ. It outputs a k-partition {Ĉ_i} of the vertex set. The utility guarantee (misclassification ratio O(k⁴/c² · φ_out/φ_in²)) requires the graph to be c-balanced (k, φ_in, φ_out)-clusterable with m ≥ n·φ_in⁴/φ_out² · log(2/δ)/ε² edges, but the (ε,δ)-DP guarantee holds for any input graph regardless of clusterability.

## Method

The algorithm solves a semi-definite program that minimizes ⟨L_G, X⟩ + (n/λm)‖D_G^{1/2}XD_G^{1/2}‖_F² subject to constraints enforcing sufficient total pairwise vector distance and non-negativity, then adds Gaussian noise W ~ N(0, 24(λ+3)m·log(2/δ)/ε²) to the scaled solution nD_G^{1/2}X_1D_G^{1/2}. The first k eigenvectors of the noisy matrix form a spectral embedding, on which a constant-approximation k-means produces the final partition. The key technical insight is proving that the SDP objective is (2n/λm, D_G^{1/2}, D_G^{1/2})-strongly convex, which via a generalized stability lemma bounds the ℓ₂-sensitivity of the scaled solution by √(24(λ+3))m, enabling the Gaussian mechanism to certify (ε,δ)-DP. The regularizer parameter λ trades off between the non-private clustering error (φ_out/φ_in² + 1/λ) and the privacy noise (λ·n/m·log(2/δ)/ε²), with the optimal choice λ = √(mε²/(n·log(2/δ))) balancing both terms.

## Results

On synthetic SBM(n,k,p,q) graphs with ε=1 and δ=1/n², Algorithm 1 consistently outperforms the randomized-response baseline (RR+SDP) across all six parameterizations. The most striking improvement occurs at n=150, k=3, p=0.25, q=0.05, where AMI jumps from 0.06 (RR+SDP) to 0.57 (Algorithm 1), and NMI from 0.06 to 0.58. Theoretically, the algorithm achieves a misclassification ratio of O(k⁴/c² · φ_out/φ_in²), nearly matching the best non-private spectral clustering bound of O(k³ · φ_out/φ_in²) under the condition φ_out/φ_in² = O(k⁻⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its (ε,δ)-DP Algorithm 1 against a randomized-response (RR) baseline from Mohamed et al. (2022), where the adjacency matrix is noised by flipping each edge with probability p_RR = 1/(1+e^ε) ≈ 0.27 before running the same SDP + spectral clustering pipeline. Algorithm 1 wins on every one of the six SBM parameterizations tested, with AMI improvements ranging from +0.07 (n=100, k=2, p=0.25) to +0.51 (n=150, k=3, p=0.25). The advantage is most pronounced for the k=3, p=0.25, q=0.05 setting, where RR+SDP yields near-random clustering (AMI ≈ 0.06) while Algorithm 1 recovers the ground-truth structure with AMI = 0.57. The paper attributes this gap to the fact that randomized response obfuscates most of the clustering signal at ε=1, whereas Algorithm 1's SDP regularizer and noise added to the SDP solution (rather than the raw adjacency matrix) preserve more structural information while still satisfying (ε,δ)-DP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP spectral clustering framework of Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2023), which handled only two nearly-balanced clusters in stochastic block models, to the general k-cluster setting for well-clustered graphs. The utility analysis builds directly on Peng et al. (COLT 2015), who established that spectral clustering achieves near-optimal misclassification on well-clustered graphs, while the privacy mechanism generalizes the SDP-stability lemma from Chen et al. via a novel notion of generalized strong convexity. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior DP graph-clustering work (which typically perturbs the adjacency matrix via randomized response or Laplacian noise) by instead adding Gaussian noise to the SDP solution, leveraging the convexity structure to bound sensitivity without requiring the graph to satisfy any clusterability condition for the privacy guarantee to hold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is significant because it shows that differential privacy need not come at a substantial accuracy cost for structured graph clustering: the misclassification ratio nearly matches the best non-private spectral algorithms under the same parameter regime. The complementary lower bound (Theorem 2) clarifies that pure ε-DP is fundamentally insufficient, motivating the (ε,δ)-DP formulation and delineating the privacy-utility frontier. Limitations include the restrictive condition φ_out/φ_in² = O(k⁻⁴), the requirement that the graph be well-clustered, and the fact that the SDP must be solved exactly (polynomial time but potentially expensive at scale). Open questions include whether the approach extends to graphs without strong cluster structure, whether the SDP can be approximated efficiently for large n, and whether the k-cluster generalization tightens as k grows.
