## Problem

Prior work on knowledge conflicts in LLMs relied on heuristic word-level edits (negation injection, entity substitution) to construct counter-memory, producing incoherent evidence that made it trivial for LLMs to detect and ignore. It remained unclear whether LLMs would still cling to their parametric memory when presented with coherent, convincing counter-evidence that mirrors real-world tool outputs. This paper presents the first comprehensive, controlled investigation into LLM behavior under knowledge conflicts using generation-based, high-quality counter-memory.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a question from POPQA (entity-centric) or StrategyQA (multi-step True/False reasoning) along with one or more evidence passages (parametric memory, counter-memory, or both), and the LLM must select from a constrained multiple-choice set (memory answer, counter-answer, "Uncertain"). The free-form QA is deliberately converted to multiple-choice to eliminate ambiguity in answer extraction and limit the generation space. The primary quantitative metric is the memorization ratio M_R = f_m / (f_m + f_c), where higher values indicate stronger reliance on parametric memory over counter-memory.

## Method

The core contribution is a four-step pipeline for constructing controlled knowledge-conflict experiments: (1) elicit parametric memory via closed-book QA where the LLM provides both an answer and supporting background; (2) construct a counter-answer (entity substitution for POPQA, polarity flip for StrategyQA) and instruct ChatGPT to generate a coherent counter-memory passage from scratch, avoiding the incoherence of prior word-level editing; (3) filter with DeBERTa-V2 NLI entailment checking to ensure each evidence passage genuinely supports its corresponding answer; (4) verify answer consistency by re-presenting the parametric memory as explicit evidence and confirming the LLM gives the same answer, thereby isolating firm internal beliefs. All experiments are conducted zero-shot across eight LLMs (ChatGPT, GPT-4, PaLM2, Qwen-7B, Llama2-7B/70B, Vicuna-7B/33B) under single-source and multi-source evidence settings, systematically varying evidence popularity, presentation order, quantity proportions, and the presence of irrelevant passages. The key finding is a dual behavior: LLMs are highly receptive to coherent counter-memory when it is the sole evidence, yet exhibit strong confirmation bias when both supportive and contradictory evidence are present simultaneously.

## Results

In the single-source setting, LLMs are highly receptive to coherent generation-based counter-memory, with memorization ratios dropping to 1.7–15.8% when no parametric memory is present (0/2 proportion in Table 6), directly contradicting prior findings that LLMs are stubborn. In the multi-source setting, LLMs exhibit strong confirmation bias: GPT-4 reaches 99.8% memorization ratio when parametric memory is the only evidence (2/2), and 80% for the most popular POPQA questions. Order sensitivity is pronounced, with PaLM2 and Llama2-7B showing >30% memorization ratio swings depending on evidence order (Table 5). LLMs also follow the herd, with memorization ratios rising monotonically from ~3% (0/2) to ~99% (2/2) as the proportion of parametric-memory-aligned evidence increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the entity substitution counter-memory method from Longpre et al. (2021) and the paper's generation-based counter-memory. With entity substitution, closed-source LLMs (ChatGPT, GPT-4, PaLM2) stubbornly cling to their parametric memory, reproducing the prior conclusion that LLMs are 'stubborn sloths.' However, with the generation-based coherent counter-memory, the same LLMs become highly receptive, with memorization ratios dropping to single digits (e.g., ChatGPT 3.7%, GPT-4 8.9% at 0/2 proportion), directly contradicting Longpre et al.'s findings. The paper attributes this reversal to the incoherence of entity-substitution counter-memory (e.g., 'Washington D.C. London, USA's capital, has the Washington Monument'), which makes it trivially detectable. In the multi-source setting, even GPT-4 and Vicuna-33B—typically the most capable models—show the strongest confirmation bias, with GPT-4 maintaining 50.3% memorization ratio even when only 1/3 of evidence supports its parametric memory, outperforming (i.e., being more stubborn than) all other models at that proportion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior knowledge-conflict studies (Longpre et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2022) that used word-level heuristics (negation injection, entity substitution) to construct counter-memory, which produced incoherent evidence and led to the conclusion that LLMs are 'stubborn.' Instead, this work introduces a systematic framework that uses LLMs to generate coherent, convincing counter-memory from scratch, with quality checks via NLI entailment and answer consistency filtering. It also extends the experimental scope from single-source to multi-source evidence, examining dimensions like popularity, order, quantity, and irrelevant evidence that prior work did not address. The paper positions itself as the first comprehensive and controlled investigation of LLM behavior under knowledge conflicts, building on the parametric memory elicitation literature while fundamentally changing how counter-memory is constructed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The findings have direct safety implications: LLMs' high receptiveness to coherent external evidence means they can be easily deceived by disinformation from malicious third-party tools, while their confirmation bias in multi-source settings undermines the reliability of generative search engines and retrieval-augmented systems. The paper also raises ethical concerns by demonstrating that LLMs can generate convincing misinformation sufficient to mislead even themselves. Open questions include how to build validation systems to prevent tool-based deception, how to reduce confirmation bias through alignment techniques, and how to present fragmented evidence in formats that LLMs can better integrate. The systematic framework and evaluation metrics (memorization ratio) provide a reusable testbed for future work on tool-augmented LLMs.
