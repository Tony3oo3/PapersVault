## Problem

Tabular generative models for oversampling minority classes degrade when the number of minority samples is too small to learn a reliable distribution, and while pre-trained language models carry rich prior knowledge that could mitigate this, no systematic framework existed for leveraging them specifically for tabular oversampling. Additionally, even with class-conditioned prompting, language models can generate noisy or majority-class samples, introducing harmful noise into the oversampled training set.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an imbalanced tabular dataset in CSV format (rows as samples, columns as features with mixed continuous and categorical types) and a fine-tuned tabular language model (Distill-GPT2-based GReaT). Tabular rows are encoded as text prompts following the format '[column_name] is [value],'. The output is a set of synthetic minority-class samples that, when appended to the original training set, yield a class-balanced dataset ready for standard ML classifier training. The framework also extends to black-box LLMs (e.g., GPT-3.5-turbo) via in-context learning without fine-tuning.

## Method

LITO converts majority samples into minority samples through a two-stage pipeline. First, importance-aware borderline sampling uses the TLM's last-layer self-attention scores (aggregated over label tokens) to rank columns by importance, then progressively 'punctures' (masks) the top-k most important columns and imputes them under minority-class conditioning via autoregressive decoding; the puncture count increases iteratively (progressive imputation) until the sample converts or confidence stops improving. Second, self-authentication rejects defective samples by having the same TLM re-predict the label of its own generated output and discarding any sample whose predicted label does not match the target minority class. This sample-and-authenticate loop, combined with the adaptive puncture schedule, ensures only reliably converted minority samples enter the balanced training set.

## Results

On extreme class-imbalance settings (α=100), LITO-B achieves F1 scores of 65.52 (Default), 74.58 (Shoppers), 85.63 (Sick), and 64.23 (Diabetes, α=20), consistently outperforming all baselines. The closest TLM-based baseline, GReaT, lags by 14.1 F1 points on Default (51.38 vs. 65.52) and 17.9 points on Shoppers (56.64 vs. 74.58), highlighting the critical role of self-authentication. On mild imbalance (α=10), LITO-B still leads, e.g., 66.86 F1 on Default vs. 65.22 for GReaT and 62.81 for SMOTE. The in-context LITO variant with GPT-3.5-turbo achieves 67.37 F1 on Diabetes (α=20), surpassing all fine-tuned baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LITO-B outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and imbalance setting. Against SMOTE (the strongest classical method), LITO-B gains 10.3 F1 on Default (65.52 vs. 55.25) and 9.9 F1 on Shoppers (74.58 vs. 64.72) under extreme imbalance. B-SMOTE degrades severely on Sick (48.68 F1) and Diabetes (59.77 F1), while LITO-B reaches 85.63 and 64.23 respectively. Among deep generative baselines, CTGAN and TVAE struggle under extreme imbalance (e.g., CTGAN scores only 55.13 F1 on Default), and SOS, despite being designed for imbalance, still trails LITO-B by 9.4 F1 on Default. The most telling comparison is against GReaT (the same Distill-GPT2 backbone without self-authentication or progressive imputation): LITO-B improves F1 by 14.1 points on Default, 17.9 on Shoppers, and 15.2 on Sick, confirming that the self-authentication and progressive imputation components are the primary drivers of the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GReaT (Borisov et al., 2023), which established that autoregressive language models fine-tuned on text-encoded tabular data can perform conditional generation, and adapts the borderline sampling concept from classical SMOTE variants (Han et al., 2005) to the language-model setting by replacing geometric interpolation with column 'puncturing' and class-conditioned imputation. The key departure from prior tabular oversampling work (SMOTE, CTGAN, SOS) is the use of a pre-trained language model's rich prior knowledge and self-verification capability rather than training a task-specific generative model from scratch. The self-authentication mechanism draws an analogy to self-alignment in LLMs (Sun et al., 2023), repurposing the model's own label-prediction ability as a rejection-sampling filter for its generated samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a model-agnostic, data-centric solution to class imbalance that is particularly relevant when minority samples are too scarce to train a dedicated generative model, a common practical scenario in healthcare and finance. The extension to black-box LLMs via in-context learning (demonstrated with GPT-3.5-turbo) lowers the barrier to adoption, though it introduces API cost and token-limit constraints. Open questions include scalability to very large or high-dimensional tabular datasets, the sensitivity of the attention-based importance scoring to the specific TLM architecture, and whether the progressive imputation strategy generalizes to more complex multi-class imbalance structures beyond the two multi-class datasets tested.
