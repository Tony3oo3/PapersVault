## Problem

3D point-cloud pre-training has yet to deliver universal performance gains over from-scratch training, largely because public 3D datasets are orders of magnitude smaller than 2D image datasets and self-supervised pre-task design is difficult without strong supervision. Existing approaches rely on contrastive learning or self-reconstruction, which require large batch sizes or carefully defined positive/negative pairs, and none effectively leverage the massive pre-trained 2D image models that have become standard in computer vision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of calibrated RGB-D scans from the same scene, which are back-projected via camera intrinsics into a single colored point cloud of M points (3 coordinate + 3 RGB channels). The SR-UNet encoder produces a dense per-point feature volume of shape M×C (C=768 for MVNet-L), which is then projected back onto the original pixel grid to yield H×W×C 2D feature maps. The final deliverable is the pre-trained 3D encoder weights, which are transferred (encoder-only, not decoder) to downstream tasks such as segmentation, detection, and classification.

## Method

MVNet uses a 3D Sparse Residual U-Net as the feature encoder, projecting the resulting per-point feature volume onto two camera views to obtain 2D feature maps. A frozen DINOv2 ViT-B encoder processes the corresponding RGB images, and a trainable ViT-B student (f_k) processes the projected 2D feature maps; a hierarchical L2 loss (L_2d) aligns their block outputs starting from the third layer, transferring 2D knowledge into the 3D encoder. To prevent the 3D features from discarding geometric cues, a multi-view consistency module (L_m) uses a cross-attention transformer decoder to predict dense pixel-wise correspondences between the two projected views, with a cycle-consistency term; since such correspondences implicitly encode 3D depth, this forces the feature volume to retain 3D structure. The total loss is L_2d + 0.5·L_m, and the entire pipeline (3D encoder + 2D student + correspondence decoder) is jointly optimized with AdamW for 200 epochs on 8×V100 GPUs.

## Results

On scene-level semantic segmentation, MVNet achieves 75.6 mIoU on ScanNet and 78.3 mIoU on S3DIS 6-Fold, outperforming the previous best (PointContrast at 74.1 mIoU on ScanNet) by +1.5 points. For 3D object detection, MVNet with VoteNet reaches 64.0 mAP@0.25 / 41.5 mAP@0.5 on ScanNet, and with the stronger CAGroup3D decoder achieves 76.0 mAP@0.25 / 62.4 mAP@0.5. At the shape level, MVNet-L pre-trained on Objaverse attains 95.2% on ScanObjectNN OBJ-BG and 91.0% on the most challenging PB-T50-RS split, surpassing all prior pre-training methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVNet outperforms all scene-level pre-training baselines on semantic segmentation: it beats PointContrast (74.1→75.6 mIoU on ScanNet), DepthContrast (71.2→75.6), and SceneContext (73.8→75.6) by 1.5–4.4 mIoU. On 3D detection, MVNet with VoteNet surpasses MaskPoint (63.4→64.0 mAP@0.25), RandomRooms (61.3→64.0), 4DContrast (–→41.5 mAP@0.5 vs 40.0), and PointContrast (59.2→64.0) on ScanNet. At the shape level, MVNet-B pre-trained on Objaverse edges out ULIP (91.3→91.5 OBJ-BG), MaskSurfel (91.2→91.5), and PointMAE (90.0→91.5) on ScanObjectNN, with the gap widening to +5.2 points (95.2 vs 90.0) when scaling to MVNet-L. The multi-view consistency loss design borrows the correspondence + cycle-consistency formulation from CoTr (Jiang et al., 2021), but applies it to projected 3D feature maps rather than raw images, making it a methodological dependency rather than a direct performance baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVNet departs from the dominant contrastive-learning paradigm for 3D pre-training (PointContrast, RandomRooms, DepthContrast, 4DContrast) by instead supervising a 3D sparse-convolutional encoder (SR-UNet) through hierarchical feature alignment with frozen 2D pre-trained ViTs (DINOv2, CLIP, SAM). It also inverts the common 'lift 2D features to 3D' strategy used by prior cross-modal work (Dong et al. 2022, Zhang et al. 2022b, Xu et al. 2021): here the 3D network is the primary feature extractor, and 2D projections serve only as a training signal. The multi-view consistency loss, which predicts dense pixel-wise correspondences between two projected views via a cross-attention transformer decoder, adapts the correspondence + cycle-consistency formulation from CoTr (Jiang et al., 2021) to the 3D pre-training setting, ensuring the learned feature volume retains geometric structure rather than collapsing to 2D-agnostic representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bridging the 2D–3D domain gap through projection-based knowledge transfer, MVNet offers a practical recipe for 3D pre-training that sidesteps the data-scarcity bottleneck without requiring contrastive pair construction or large batch sizes. The consistent SOTA gains across four heterogeneous downstream tasks (scene segmentation, 3D detection, shape classification, part segmentation) and the generality across 2D backbones (DINOv2, CLIP, SAM) make the approach broadly applicable. However, all scene-level experiments are confined to indoor RGB-D data (ScanNet, S3DIS), and the authors explicitly flag outdoor settings, larger encoders, and additional datasets (e.g., NYUv2) as open directions. Whether the multi-view consistency mechanism transfers to unstructured or outdoor point clouds where calibrated multi-view pairs are harder to obtain remains an important open question.
