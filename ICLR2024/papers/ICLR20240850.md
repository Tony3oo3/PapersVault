## Problem

Existing methods for integrating multiple graph pre-training tasks (e.g., AutoSSL, ParetoGNN, AGSSL) focus exclusively on importance weighing while ignoring task compatibility — the conflicts that arise when incompatible tasks are combined. As the task pool expands, these conflicts become more severe, depriving weighing-only schemes of the ability to sustain performance gains. The paper also notes that prior work has been evaluated almost entirely on node-level tasks, leaving graph-level tasks unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes K frozen pre-trained teacher models (each trained with a distinct graph pre-training task such as AttrMask, GraphCL, or EdgePred) and their per-instance output distributions as input. For each instance (a molecular graph or a node), it produces binary selecting indicators κ(k,i)∈{0,1} and continuous importance weights λ(k,i)∈(0,1] that jointly determine which teachers contribute and how much. The final output is a student GNN encoder plus projection head that predicts class labels (node classification) or molecular property scores (graph classification), trained via multi-teacher knowledge distillation with KL divergence.

## Method

WAS builds a decoupled siamese network with two same-architecture modules — a Weighing module W and a Selecting module S — that operate on the teacher distributions for each instance. W computes importance weights via a softmax over element-wise products of per-teacher latent factors and the student representation, optimized by binary cross-entropy against a Bayesian teacher approximation. S is decoupled from W through stop-gradient and momentum updating (θ_selecting = m·θ_selecting + (1−m)·θ_weighing), plus an additional MLP projection head that further separates the two outputs, preventing the selecting results from collapsing into the importance weights. Selection is made differentiable via Gumbel-Softmax sampling over the normalized selecting probabilities, enabling a 'dynamic quit' mechanism where unhelpful teachers are gradually excluded. Only the selected teachers are then re-weighed by a second softmax to produce final weights summing to 1, and the weighted teacher distributions are distilled into the student via KL divergence alongside the downstream task loss.

## Results

On 8 molecular property prediction datasets (graph-level), WAS achieves the best average rank of 1.8, outperforming the next-best method GraphMVP (rank 3.8) and winning on 6 out of 8 datasets (e.g., BACE 80.7% ROC-AUC vs. GraphMVP 80.3%, HIV 78.4% vs. 76.2%). On 8 node classification datasets, WAS achieves the best average rank of 1.8, beating AGSSL (rank 3.1) and AutoSSL (rank 4.9), with wins on 7 of 8 datasets (e.g., Cora 84.4% vs. AGSSL 84.2%, Physics 94.9% vs. 94.8%). Notably, WAS uses only simple classical pre-training tasks (e.g., AttrMask, GraphCL, EdgePred) yet matches or exceeds methods designed with more complex single-task objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WAS outperforms all 7 individual teacher tasks (AttrMask, ContextPred, EdgePred, GPT-GNN, GraphCL, GraphLoG, InfoGraph) on every one of the 8 molecular datasets, with the largest margin on BACE (80.7% vs. best teacher GPT-GNN 78.6%). Against 8 SOTA graph pre-training methods, WAS wins on 6/8 datasets, most notably beating GraphMVP by 2.2 points on HIV (78.4% vs. 76.2%) and 2.6 points on BBBP (70.5% vs. 67.9%), while losing on ClinTox (73.1% vs. G-Motif 81.4%) and Toxcast (63.7% vs. GraphMVP 63.5% — actually WAS wins here too; the loss is on ClinTox to G-Motif). For node-level tasks, WAS beats the closest multi-task competitor AGSSL on 7/8 datasets (e.g., Cora 84.4% vs. 84.2%, Physics 94.9% vs. 94.8%) and substantially outperforms AutoSSL (e.g., Cora 84.4% vs. 83.2%) and ParetoGNN (e.g., Cora 84.4% vs. 83.5%). WAS also surpasses all 5 single-task methods (GRLC, CG3, GCA, MVGRL, GRACE) on node classification, though GCA achieves OOM on ogbn-arxiv. A key differentiator is scalability: as the task pool grows from 2 to 7 tasks, WAS's performance improves consistently while AGSSL's performance plateaus, confirming that the selecting mechanism addresses the compatibility issue that weighing-only methods cannot resolve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-teacher knowledge distillation framework introduced by AGSSL (Wu et al., 2022a), which first demonstrated that instance-level task weighing outperforms global-level approaches (AutoSSL, ParetoGNN, AUX-TS). The key departure is the identification of task *selecting* as a distinct process from task *weighing*: prior methods treat all tasks as always active and only adjust their relative importance, whereas WAS introduces a decoupled siamese architecture where a separate selecting module (updated via momentum and a projection head) determines which tasks participate for each instance, using Gumbel-Softmax sampling for differentiable discrete selection. The paper positions itself as the first to formally decouple these two collaborative processes and show that they must be treated independently to resolve task compatibility conflicts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that as the pool of available pre-training tasks grows (hundreds have been proposed), weighing-only methods hit a performance ceiling due to inter-task conflicts, while WAS demonstrates consistent gains with more tasks. This provides a scalable recipe for integrating an ever-expanding task library without manual curation. Limitations include the computational overhead of pre-training and storing multiple teacher models, the need to tune the momentum rate and temperature per dataset, and validation limited to relatively small molecular and citation graphs. Open questions include whether the decoupling mechanism generalizes to non-graph multi-task settings, whether the Gumbel-Softmax selection remains effective with very large task pools (K >> 10), and whether the approach can be adapted to online or streaming task addition.
