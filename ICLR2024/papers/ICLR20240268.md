## Problem

LoRA reduces trainable parameters during fine-tuning but still incurs substantial storage overhead when deploying many per-user or per-task adapted models (e.g., 288 MB for GPT-3 at rank 16). Prior work showed that the intrinsic dimensionality of pretrained models is far lower than the parameter budgets LoRA employs, suggesting significant room for further reduction without sacrificing performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240268/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard transformer inputs: text sequences (up to 512 tokens for RoBERTa-base, 128 for RoBERTa-large) for NLU/NLG tasks, and RGB images for ViT-based classification. The critical output detail is that only the trained scaling vectors (b and d) plus a single RNG seed must be stored per adapted model, rather than full low-rank matrices; these vectors can be merged into the frozen base weights at deployment, introducing zero additional inference latency.

## Method

VeRA reparameterizes LoRA's low-rank update by freezing a single pair of randomly initialized matrices A and B, shared across all adapted layers, and replacing the trainable matrices with two small per-layer scaling vectors d ∈ R^{1×r} and b ∈ R^{1×m} applied as diagonal matrices: ΔW = Λ_b B Λ_d A. Because the frozen matrices are regenerated from a fixed RNG seed, they need not be stored, reducing the per-model footprint to just the vectors. The parameter count scales as L_tuned × (d_model + r) versus LoRA's 2 × L_tuned × d_model × r, yielding roughly a 10× reduction at typical ranks. Matrices use Kaiming uniform initialization to maintain consistent variance across ranks, b is zero-initialized (preserving the original weights at step zero), and d is initialized to a small non-zero constant (0.1) that acts as a tunable hyperparameter controlling early optimization flexibility.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, VeRA achieves an average score of 87.8 on RoBERTa-large with only 0.061M trainable parameters, matching LoRA (87.8) which uses 0.8M parameters — a 13× reduction. On the E2E generation benchmark, VeRA outperforms LoRA in BLEU score (70.1 vs 68.9 on GPT-2 Medium; 70.3 vs 70.1 on GPT-2 Large) with 3–4× fewer parameters. For instruction-tuning Llama-2 13B, VeRA scores 5.93 on MT-Bench versus LoRA's 5.77, using only 2.4M vs 250.3M trainable parameters (a 100× reduction). On image classification with ViT-Large, VeRA outperforms LoRA on CIFAR-100 (87.5 vs 87.0), Flowers-102 (99.2 vs 99.1), and RESISC45 (78.6 vs 78.3) with over 10× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (the primary baseline), VeRA ties on GLUE-large (87.8 avg, 0.061M vs 0.8M params) and is within 1.4 points on GLUE-base (85.2 vs 86.6, 0.043M vs 0.3M params), while actually outperforming LoRA on E2E generation (70.1 vs 68.9 BLEU on GPT-2 Medium; 70.3 vs 70.1 on GPT-2 Large) and on Llama-2 13B instruction tuning (5.93 vs 5.77 MT-Bench score). Against all adapter variants (Adapter^H, Adapter^L, Adapter^P, Adapter^D), VeRA achieves higher average GLUE scores with far fewer parameters (e.g., 87.8 vs 84.9–87.6 on RoBERTa-large). Against LoRA-FA, VeRA matches the average (87.8 vs 87.7) with 60× fewer parameters. On E2E, VeRA outperforms DyLoRA (70.1 vs 69.2 BLEU), AdaLoRA (70.1 vs 68.2), and Delta-LoRA (70.1 vs 68.9) on GPT-2 Medium. The one area where VeRA trails is GLUE-base RTE (78.7 vs LoRA's 86.6), though it leads on CoLA (65.6 vs 63.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

VeRA is a direct reparameterization of LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), replacing the trainable low-rank matrices A and B with a single frozen random pair shared across all layers plus per-layer trainable scaling vectors. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of two research threads: the low-rank adaptation line (LoRA, AdaLoRA) and the random/frozen projection line (Aghajanyan et al., 2021; Frankle & Carbin, 2019; Lu et al., 2022), which demonstrated that pretrained models operate in a low intrinsic dimensionality and that random projections can capture most of the needed expressivity. The key departure from LoRA is that the random matrices are never trained and are shared globally, reducing the per-layer trainable footprint from O(d_model × r) to O(d_model + r).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× (GLUE) to 100× (instruction tuning) reduction in trainable parameters with no additional inference latency makes it practical to store and swap thousands of per-user or per-task adapted models on a single GPU, directly addressing a storage bottleneck in personalized AI deployment. The method is architecture-agnostic within Transformers (validated on RoBERTa, GPT-2, LLaMA/LLaMA-2, and ViT) and trivially integrable into existing LoRA pipelines. Open questions include whether the shared-matrix assumption holds for non-Transformer backbones, whether dynamic rank or per-layer budget allocation could close the small remaining gaps (e.g., on RTE for RoBERTa-base), and how the method interacts with quantization and multi-task composition.
