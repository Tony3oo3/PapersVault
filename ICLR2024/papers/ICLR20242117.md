## Problem

Existing SO(3)-equivariant point cloud methods for Scan-to-CAD conflate local and global equivariance, rely on fixed predefined patches that cannot adapt during learning, and struggle with zero point-to-point correspondence between scans from different sensing devices. The paper addresses the gap of seamlessly integrating adaptive local and global contextual information in a rotation-equivariant, generalizable manner for registration, retrieval, and completion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242117/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes instance-based point clouds (source and target pairs for registration, a query cloud for retrieval, or a partial cloud for completion), first subsampled to f_no points via Furthest Point Sampling. At each sampled point, a sphere of C points is generated using Vogel's sunflower sampling, yielding |ν| = f_no × C spherical samples. The output is an SO(3)-equivariant latent vector per patch that feeds into task-specific decoders: a 9-DoF transformation (rotation, translation, scale) for registration, a cross-covariance-based retrieval ranking, or a completed point cloud.

## Method

DeepSPF introduces Spherical Patch Fields (SPF), which maps a point cloud to many spherical patches each producing a rotation-equivariant representation grounded in the full (non-simplified) Spherical Gaussian definition from Narcowich & Ward (1996), incorporating an asymmetric edge function from DGCNN to capture inter- and intra-patch graph relationships, a differentiable spherical volume term V(r) for adaptive patch sizing, and a Legendre Polynomial upsampling for low-frequency shape information. The Patch Gaussian Layer (PG-Layer) formulates convolution between SG-based representations while preserving the original SG mathematical form (avoiding the deformation that limited prior work to a single convolution iteration), enabling deeper stacked networks. The backbone follows a PointNet-style architecture with three Set Abstraction layers, each containing multiple PG-Layer modules initialized with decreasing radii (r = [1.0, 0.5, 0.25]) to progressively shift from global to local information. Rotation equivariance is proven analytically for both the Spherical Harmonics component and the edge-based distance terms under the assumption R_ν ≈ R_p.

## Results

On ModelNet40 with zero-intersection noise, DeepSPF (full: E+U+V) achieves a rotation error (RRMSE) of 5.17°, reducing it by ~92% compared to DeepGMR (67.28°) and ~93% compared to DeepUME (70.82°), while also achieving the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.0004) and translation RMSE (0.0022). On the Scan2CAD benchmark, DeepSPF reaches an average score of 60.57, outperforming SceneCAD (57.11) by 3.46 points. For point cloud completion on ShapeNet, DeepSPF+PCN reduces Chamfer Distance from 8.99 (PCN) to 6.34 and improves F-score from 29.9% to 39.4% on seen classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepSPF decisively outperforms all registration baselines on zero-intersection noise: it reduces RRMSE from 67.28° (DeepGMR) and 70.82° (DeepUME) to 5.17°, and from 93.22° (DCP) and 100.97° (RGM) to 5.17°, while also achieving the best Chamfer Distance (0.0004 vs. 0.011 for DeepUME and 0.026 for DeepGMR). Against the closest prior method SGPCR (8.57° RRMSE), DeepSPF improves rotation error by 40% and Chamfer Distance by 60%. On the Scan2CAD benchmark, DeepSPF wins on average (60.57 vs. 57.11 for SceneCAD) and on 4 of 6 classes, but loses on Chair (78.39 vs. 81.23) and Sofa (73.56 vs. 82.86), where SceneCAD's object-to-layout relationships provide an advantage. For retrieval on ShapeNet, DeepSPF improves Top-1 accuracy to 70.84% (Chair) and 69.44% (Table) over SGPCR's 60.23% and 63.39%, and reduces Top-1 Chamfer Distance by ~43% (0.008 vs. 0.014 for Chair). In point cloud completion, DeepSPF+PCN beats both PCN (d_C 6.34 vs. 8.99, F-score 39.4% vs. 29.9%) and VN+PCN (d_C 6.34 vs. 12.4, F-score 39.4% vs. 25.0%) on seen classes, showing that the patch-wise equivariant encoder captures local detail better than either the original PointNet or the Vector Neurons variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Spherical Gaussian Point Cloud Representation (SGPCR) from Salihu & Steinbach (2023) by replacing its single global sphere with multiple adaptive spherical patches, each producing a local SO(3)-equivariant encoding. The graph-based inter- and intra-patch relations are borrowed from DGCNN's asymmetric edge function, while the full SG formulation (with Legendre polynomial expansion) follows Narcowich & Ward (1996) rather than the simplified form used in prior SG-based point cloud work. A key departure from SGPCR is the Patch Gaussian Layer, which retains the original SG mathematical form during convolution (avoiding the Tsai & Shih simplification that deforms the representation), enabling deeper iterative encoding and introducing a differentiable radius for adaptive patch sizing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeepSPF provides a drop-in replacement for PointNet-style encoders that simultaneously captures local and global structure with SO(3)-equivariance, addressing the zero-correspondence registration problem that arises when scans come from different sensors. The adaptive patch radius is a practical mechanism that lets the network allocate representational capacity where information density demands it, potentially benefiting any instance-based point cloud task. However, the method is constrained by FPS-based initialization, limiting scalability to large scenes, and the evaluation is primarily on instance-level point clouds (ModelNet40, ShapeNet) with limited evidence on full-scene or multi-instance settings. Open questions include whether the patch-wise formulation generalizes to scene-level S2C without VoteNet's error propagation, and whether the FPS bottleneck can be replaced with learned or hierarchical sampling.
