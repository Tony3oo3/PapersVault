## Problem

Existing confidence elicitation methods for LLMs depend on white-box access to token likelihoods or require model fine-tuning, rendering them inapplicable to closed-source commercial APIs like GPT-4. This paper investigates whether meaningful confidence estimates can be obtained through purely black-box approaches—using only textual inputs and outputs—and systematically benchmarks how well LLMs can express uncertainty when prompted to do so.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240941/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a text question as input and produces an answer paired with a verbalized confidence score expressed as a percentage (0–100%). In practice, the same question is queried M times (typically M=5) with varying prompts or sampling perturbations, and the individual responses are aggregated into a single confidence estimate. The confidence is elicited as a natural-language number rather than derived from internal model states, and the answer format is constrained to a fixed structure (e.g., 'Answer and Confidence (0-100): [answer], [number]%').

## Method

The paper proposes a unified black-box confidence elicitation framework with three composable components: (1) prompting strategies (Vanilla, CoT, Self-Probing, Multi-Step, Top-K) that shape how the model verbalizes confidence, (2) sampling strategies (Self-Random, Prompting, Misleading) that generate multiple responses to capture output variance, and (3) aggregation strategies (Consistency, Avg-Conf, Pair-Rank) that combine individual responses into a final confidence score. The Pair-Rank aggregator is particularly novel, formulating a conditional maximum likelihood estimation problem over pairwise ranking events in Top-K responses to recover a categorical distribution over candidate answers, with a closed-form conditional probability P(S_u ≻ S_v | P, E_uv) = P(S_u)/(P(S_u)+P(S_v)). The key empirical finding is that LLMs are systematically overconfident (confidence concentrated at 80–100% in multiples of 5, mimicking human expression patterns), and that combining verbalized confidence with multi-response consistency via ensemble methods substantially improves failure prediction (AUROC) over single-query verbalized confidence, though all methods remain suboptimal on tasks requiring professional knowledge.

## Results

Across five LLMs (GPT-3, Vicuna 13B, LLaMA 2 70B, GPT-3.5, GPT-4) and eight datasets, vanilla verbalized confidence exhibits severe overconfidence: GPT-4 achieves the best average ECE of 18.0% and average AUROC of 62.7%, while smaller models (GPT-3, Vicuna) show average ECE >43% and AUROC near random (51–56%). Combining Top-K prompting with Self-Random sampling (M=5) and Avg-Conf aggregation improves average AUROC to 74.5% and ECE to 14.8% on GPT-3.5, with GSM8K AUROC jumping from 54.8% (CoT, M=1) to 92.7% (Self-Random+Consistency, M=5). White-box token-probability methods outperform black-box methods but the gap is narrow, e.g., 0.522 vs. 0.605 AUROC on Prf-Law.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper incorporates Top-K prompting from Tian et al. (2023) as a baseline component. On GPT-3.5, Top-K alone (M=1) achieves average AUROC of 65.9% and ECE of 24.6%, while combining Top-K with Self-Random sampling (M=5) and Avg-Conf aggregation improves average AUROC to 74.5% and ECE to 14.8% (Table 13). On GSM8K specifically, the ensemble approach boosts AUROC from 59.9% (Top-K, M=1) to 92.5% (Self-Random+Avg-Conf, M=5). Against white-box token-probability methods (seq-prob, len-norm-prob, token-prob) on GPT-3, the best white-box method (token-prob) achieves AUROC of 61.24 on Prf-Law versus 49.54 for black-box verbalized confidence, but the gap narrows to ~5–10 points on most datasets, with all methods remaining near the 0.5–0.6 AUROC range. The paper concludes that no single black-box strategy consistently dominates, and all methods struggle on professional-knowledge tasks (Prf-Law AUROC ≤ 68% even with the best configuration).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the verbalized confidence concept introduced by Lin et al. (2022) from a fine-tuning setting to a zero-shot, black-box setting, and broadens the prompting-strategy exploration initiated by the concurrent work of Tian et al. (2023) into a unified three-component framework (prompting, sampling, aggregation). It deliberately departs from the dominant white-box paradigm (token-likelihood, model fine-tuning) motivated by the inaccessibility of internal representations in closed-source LLMs like GPT-4. The framework subsumes prior methods as special cases (e.g., Top-K from Tian et al. becomes one prompting strategy with M=1 and Avg-Conf aggregation), positioning the paper as a systematic benchmark rather than a single new technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark of black-box confidence elicitation across multiple LLMs, task types, and method families, providing practitioners with concrete recommendations (Top-K + Self-Random + Avg-Conf/Pair-Rank) and a clear picture of where current methods fail (professional knowledge tasks, failure prediction). The finding that the white-box-to-black-box gap is narrow (0.522→0.605 AUROC) is practically important: it suggests that API-only users are not as disadvantaged as previously assumed, but also that the fundamental problem of LLM uncertainty estimation remains largely unsolved. Open questions include extending to open-ended generation tasks, leveraging partial white-box access (e.g., GPT-3 logits), and understanding why all methods plateau on tasks requiring specialized knowledge.
