## Problem

Model-homogeneous federated learning methods (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova, Scaffold, MOON) degrade substantially when clients face heterogeneous resource constraints that force different model architectures. The paper identifies the root cause: deep layers exhibit markedly lower CKA similarity across clients compared to shallow layers, and this dissimilarity correlates positively with accuracy loss. The goal is to bridge homogeneity and heterogeneity by enhancing deep-layer similarity using information already available within the server model, without requiring new training paradigms or extra communication.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240082/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The server holds a full-size prototype (e.g., ResNet26 or ViT-S/12) that is partitioned via layer, stage, or hetero splitting into sub-models of varying depths and widths matched to each client's resource budget R_i. Clients train locally on their private data and upload only weight deltas; the server performs all cross-layer gradient mixing and convex optimization internally, so no additional client-server communication rounds are introduced. The output consists of updated per-client weights at their respective sizes, with measurably higher CKA similarity in deep layers and improved test accuracy across all clients.

## Method

InCo Aggregation comprises three server-side components: (1) model splitting (layer, stage, or hetero) to distribute appropriately sized sub-models to clients; (2) internal cross-layer gradients, where for each deep layer k within a stage the server augments its gradient G_k^t with the gradient G_0^t from the first layer of the same stage, transferring shallow-layer knowledge to deep layers; and (3) gradient divergence alleviation via L2 normalization of both gradients followed by a convex optimization that projects the combined gradient to preserve alignment with the shallow-layer direction. The convex problem admits a closed-form solution (Theorem 3.1) that automatically determines the mixing coefficient θ = β/α without manual tuning, and the entire procedure is plug-and-play: it can be inserted into any model-homogeneous FL method to extend it to heterogeneous settings, with non-convex convergence and a convergence rate of O(1/T) established under standard FL assumptions.

## Results

InCo Aggregation improves all five model-homogeneous FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova, Scaffold, MOON) when extended to heterogeneous settings, with gains of 0.4–4.8% test accuracy across Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10, and CINIC-10. The largest improvements appear on the harder datasets: InCoAvg achieves 67.8% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.5, +3.0 over HeteroAvg) and 53.0% on CINIC-10 (α=0.5, +4.4 over HeteroAvg), while InCoScaffold reaches 73.8% on CIFAR-10 (α=1.0, +4.1). When applied to existing heterogeneous methods (HeteroFL, InclusiveFL, FedRolex, ScaleFL), InCo adds 0.6–3.9% accuracy with zero additional communication overhead and only 0.4M extra FLOPs on the server side.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InCo Aggregation consistently outperforms all five extended homogeneous baselines: InCoAvg beats HeteroAvg by 2.4–4.4% (e.g., 67.8% vs. 64.8% on CIFAR-10 α=0.5), InCoProx beats HeteroProx by 0.9–3.7% (74.5% vs. 72.5% on CIFAR-10), InCoScaffold beats HeteroScaffold by 1.5–4.8% (85.4% vs. 80.6% on SVHN α=0.5), InCoNova beats HeteroNova by 0.4–2.7%, and InCoMOON beats HeteroMOON by 1.2–3.4%. Among the existing heterogeneous FL methods in Table 3, InCo also improves HeteroFL (68.2% vs. 65.2% on CIFAR-10 α=0.5), InclusiveFL (69.1% vs. 65.7%), FedRolex (67.9% vs. 64.7%), and ScaleFL (73.8% vs. 71.1%), confirming that the cross-layer gradient mechanism is complementary to existing heterogeneity-handling strategies. The gains are most pronounced on CIFAR-10 and CINIC-10 (the most challenging datasets) and on ResNet architectures, while ViT improvements are more modest (0.5–2.7%), which the authors attribute to the different bias patterns in transformer layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established FL research threads: model-homogeneous aggregation methods (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova, Scaffold, MOON) and model-heterogeneous FL (HeteroFL, InclusiveFL, FedRolex, ScaleFL). Rather than designing a new aggregation rule for heterogeneous models, it introduces a server-side gradient manipulation—mixing shallow-layer gradients into deep-layer updates via a convex optimization formulation—that can be grafted onto any existing homogeneous FL method to extend it to the heterogeneous setting. The diagnostic framework draws on CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019) to quantify layer similarity, and the gradient divergence mitigation builds on normalization techniques from FedNova and the non-IID FL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that InCo Aggregation is a zero-communication-overhead, server-side plug-in that consistently improves five major FL baselines and four state-of-the-art heterogeneous methods, making it an attractive drop-in for practitioners deploying FL across devices with varying compute budgets. However, the paper acknowledges that the smallest models still underperform relative to larger ones, and ViT improvements are notably more modest than ResNet gains, suggesting the cross-layer gradient mechanism may interact differently with attention-based architectures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to architectures without natural stage/block structure, how it behaves under extreme heterogeneity (e.g., 10× size ratios), and whether the convex optimization step can be further simplified for real-time server deployment.
