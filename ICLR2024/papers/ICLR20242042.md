## Problem

For the DeepSets architecture, the minimal bottleneck dimension L needed to represent any continuous set function is well-understood for scalar features (D=1, where L=N suffices), but remains open for high-dimensional features (D>1). Prior analyses either restricted to D=1, relied on multivariate symmetric polynomials yielding L exponential in min{N,D}, or imposed complex analytic activation assumptions that exclude practical networks. The paper asks whether polynomial L suffices when φ and ρ are allowed to be any continuous real or complex functions with non-analytic activations.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered set of N vectors in R^D (or C^D), represented as an N×D matrix X, and produces a scalar for permutation-invariant functions or an N-dimensional vector for permutation-equivariant functions. The architecture enforces permutation invariance through sum-pooling of element-wise embeddings, and the central design question is how large the embedding dimension L must be so that the pooled vector is injective—i.e., no two distinct sets (up to row permutation) map to the same L-dimensional summary. The paper provides both upper and lower bounds on this L as a function of N and D.

## Method

The paper constructs two embedding layers—linear + power mapping (LP) and linear + exponential activation (LE)—and proves that L = poly(N,D) suffices for exact representation of any continuous set function. The key technical ingredient is an "anchor": a linear combination of features (from K₁ = O(N²D) weights in general position) that distinguishes all distinct set elements, combined with a coupling scheme that aligns each feature channel with the anchor to recover global permutation equivalence. For LP, a linear coupling with K₂ = N(N−1)+1 coefficients ensures pairwise alignment; for LE, bivariate monomial coupling achieves the same with a tighter bound. Injectivity of the sum-pooling, combined with the Curgus–Mascioni homeomorphism between polynomial roots and coefficients, guarantees a continuous inverse, yielding exact representation via ρ = f ∘ Φ⁻¹ with L ≤ N⁵D² (LP) or L ≤ N⁴D² (LE), and a non-trivial lower bound of N(D+1) for LP.

## Results

The paper proves that for the DeepSets architecture with high-dimensional features (D ≥ 2), the bottleneck dimension L can be polynomial in set size N and feature dimension D. Specifically, for the LP (linear + power) embedding, N(D+1) ≤ L < N⁵D², and for the LE (linear + exponential) embedding, ND ≤ L ≤ N⁴D². These are the first results achieving exact representation (not merely approximation) for D > 1 with polynomial L, and they extend to permutation-equivariant functions and the complex field. Empirical validation in Appendix A confirms that the critical width L* scales polynomially (log L* grows linearly with log N and log D) rather than exponentially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Zaheer et al. (2017) and Wagstaff et al. (2019), which achieve tight L = N bounds but only for one-dimensional features (D = 1), this paper extends exact representation to D > 1 at the cost of a polynomial (rather than linear) dependence on D. Against Segol & Lipman (2020), which handles D > 1 but requires L = (N+D choose N) − 1 (exponential in min{N,D}) and only provides approximation, this paper reduces L to polynomial order while achieving exact representation. Against Zweig & Bruna (2022), which shows L = exp(min{√N, D}) is necessary under the restrictive assumption of complex analytic activations, this paper demonstrates that relaxing to general continuous (possibly non-analytic, real-valued) activations reduces the required L from exponential to polynomial, and additionally covers the equivariant case which Zweig & Bruna do not address. The paper also provides a non-trivial lower bound of N(D+1) for the LP architecture, correcting the misconception that L = ND (the input degree of freedom) suffices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSets framework (Zaheer et al., 2017) from the fully resolved D=1 case to high-dimensional features (D>1), where prior analyses either relied on multivariate symmetric polynomials yielding exponential L (Segol & Lipman, 2020) or imposed complex analytic activation assumptions that also led to exponential bounds (Zweig & Bruna, 2022). The key technical departure is the introduction of an 'anchor' construction combined with linear coupling (LP) or monomial coupling (LE) schemes to prove injectivity of the sum-pooling, while continuity of the inverse is established via the homeomorphism results of Curgus & Mascioni (2006). The extension to permutation-equivariant functions leverages a specific lemma from Wang et al. (2023), and the lower bound proof generalizes the unique recoverability framework of Tsakiris & Peng (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves an explicit open question from Zweig & Bruna (2022) by showing that the exponential dependence of L on N and D is an artifact of the complex analytic assumption, not an inherent limitation of the DeepSets architecture. This has direct practical implications for GNN neighbor aggregation and hypergraph neural operators where only element-wise (not pairwise) encoding is feasible. However, the bounds are loose (N(D+1) to N^5 D^2), the complexity of the decoder ρ is uncharacterized, and concurrent work by Dym & Gortler (2024) and Amir et al. (2024) achieves a much tighter upper bound of 2ND+1, albeit without guaranteeing continuity of ρ on open domains. Tightening the gap between lower and upper bounds and analyzing ρ's complexity remain open.
