## Problem

Existing data-driven typhoon trajectory prediction methods rely on ERA5 reanalysis data, which is only available 3–5 days after the event and thus unsuitable for real-time forecasting. Real-time NWP outputs (e.g., the Unified Model) contain systematic biases relative to reanalysis, and prior real-time data-driven approaches are limited to less than 24-hour lead times, far short of the 72-hour advance warning needed for effective evacuation and damage control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240616/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 12 timesteps of 9-channel atmospheric fields (geopotential height, zonal wind, meridional wind at 250/500/700 hPa) at 240×320 spatial resolution from the real-time UM forecast, together with the typhoon's historical center coordinates as a 1D (lon, lat) sequence. It outputs 12 future center-coordinate pairs at 6-hour intervals spanning up to 72 hours ahead. All variables are z-score normalized, and the UM input is bias-corrected toward the ERA5 distribution before being fed to the pre-trained encoder.

## Method

LT3P uses a two-phase training strategy. In phase 1, a physics-conditioned encoder (3D CNN encoder → 3D transformer → 3D CNN decoder) is pre-trained on ERA5 for a weather-forecasting reconstruction task, learning representations of atmospheric variables relevant to typhoon dynamics. In phase 2, all encoder weights are frozen and a lightweight bias corrector is fine-tuned to map UM forecast fields into the ERA5 representation space, while a diffusion-based trajectory predictor (MID) is trained jointly with the bias corrector. The predictor fuses the 3D atmospheric features from the frozen transformer with 1D coordinate features via cross-attention, enabling the model to leverage physics-conditioned context regardless of feature shape or sequence length. This design decouples the expensive pre-training (done once on 70 years of ERA5) from the real-time inference path, which only requires the UM forecast and a small bias-correction network.

## Results

On the ensemble FDE metric at the critical +72-hour lead time, LT3P (Bias-corrected UM) achieves 143.03 km, outperforming the best NWP ensemble model (ECMWF-EPS at 210.71 km) by 32% and the best data-driven typhoon method (MGTCF at 655.48 km) by 78%. The non-real-time variant LT3P (ERA5 Only) achieves an even lower FDE of 70.94 km at 72 hours. At shorter lead times, LT3P (Bias-corrected UM) records 6.30 km at 6h and 39.81 km at 24h, compared to 57.75 km and 78.87 km for ECMWF-EPS respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LT3P (Bias-corrected UM) decisively outperforms all five NWP ensemble baselines (JTWC, JMA-GEPS, ECMWF-EPS, NCEP-GEFS, UKMO-EPS) at every reported lead time, with the 72-hour FDE of 143.03 km beating the best NWP model (ECMWF-EPS, 210.71 km) by 67.68 km. Against data-driven typhoon trajectory predictors, LT3P's advantage is even more pronounced: at 72 hours it achieves 143.03 km versus 655.48 km for MGTCF (the strongest data-driven typhoon method) and 1300.59 km for MMSTN. Among human trajectory prediction methods repurposed for typhoons, LT3P (Bias-corrected UM) beats PECNet (518.46 km), SocialGAN (716.90 km), STGAT (682.16 km), and MID (881.34 km) by margins of 375–738 km at 72 hours. The non-real-time LT3P (ERA5 Only) variant achieves the absolute best FDE (70.94 km at 72h) but requires reanalysis data unavailable until 3–5 days post-event, whereas the bias-corrected UM variant retains real-time capability while still surpassing all operational NWP ensembles. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that the bias-correction module is the critical component bridging the UM-to-ERA5 domain gap, reducing 72-hour FDE from 198.11 km (pre-trained only) to 143.03 km.

## Relation to Prior Work

LT3P builds on the CLIMAX foundation model architecture (Nguyen et al., 2023) for its physics-conditioned encoder, which uses a 3D convolutional encoder–transformer–decoder to encode geopotential height and wind fields, and on the MID diffusion-based trajectory predictor (Gu et al., 2022) as its trajectory generation backbone. The key departure from prior typhoon trajectory prediction work (MMSTN, MGTCF) is the introduction of a bias-correction module that bridges the domain gap between real-time Unified Model (UM) NWP output and ERA5 reanalysis, enabling 72-hour predictions without waiting for post-hoc data assimilation. The cross-attention fusion between 3D physics features and 1D coordinate sequences is a novel architectural choice that allows the model to leverage both data-driven and physics-based signals regardless of their differing shapes and temporal lengths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first data-driven method to achieve real-time 72-hour typhoon trajectory prediction without reanalysis data, outperforming all major operational NWP centers (ECMWF, NCEP, JMA, UKMO, JTWC) by 30–40% in FDE at 72 hours—a practically significant margin for emergency planning. The released PHYSICS TRACK dataset (combining ERA5, UM, and Best Track data) provides a valuable resource for the climate-AI community. Key limitations include restriction to the Western North Pacific basin, continued dependence on NWP model outputs, and degraded performance for stationary typhoons due to data scarcity. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other tropical cyclone regions (zero-shot tests on Indian Ocean cyclones show partial transfer) and how to select a single trajectory from the probabilistic output rather than relying on ensemble averaging.
