## Problem

The most widely used calibration measure (Binned ECE) and its associated reliability diagram suffer from discontinuity, sensitivity to bin-width choice, and lack of theoretical guarantees. While consistent calibration measures exist in the literature, none come with a natural reliability diagram that visually encodes the measure. This paper bridges the gap by introducing a calibration measure that is both theoretically consistent and directly visualizable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. pairs of predicted probabilities f ∈ [0,1] and binary outcomes y ∈ {0,1} (e.g., model confidences and correctness labels). It produces a single hyperparameter-free calibration score (SmoothECE) and a smoothed reliability diagram showing both the kernel-regression estimate of the calibration function and the kernel density estimate of prediction frequencies. The bandwidth σ* is determined automatically via a fixed-point equation, requiring no user-specified hyperparameters.

## Method

The core construction applies Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression with a reflected Gaussian kernel to smooth the residual (y − f) rather than the outcome y alone, yielding a smoothed residual function r̂(t) and a smoothed density δ̂(t). The calibration measure smECE_σ is defined as ∫|r̂(t)|δ̂(t)dt, which is monotone decreasing in σ. The bandwidth σ* is chosen as the unique fixed point satisfying smECE_σ*(D) = σ*, found via binary search. The paper proves this fixed-point measure is a consistent calibration measure (polynomially bounded by the Wasserstein distance to perfect calibration) and that the reliability diagram—plotting kernel regression on y at the same σ*—visually encodes the SmoothECE within a constant factor. Efficient computation is achieved via FFT-based convolution with the reflected Gaussian kernel.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it proves that SmoothECE is a consistent calibration measure satisfying (1/2)·dCE(D) ≤ smECE_*(D) ≤ 2√(dCE(D)) (Theorem 6), and that it can be estimated with m ≳ σ₀⁻¹ε⁻² samples (Theorem 9). Empirically, the method is demonstrated on four datasets (ImageNet with ResNet34 at 50K samples, solar flare forecasts over 731 days, Finnish precipitation forecasts over 346 days, and a synthetic dataset of 1000 samples), where smooth reliability diagrams are visually compared against optimally-binned reliability diagrams. No single quantitative calibration-error table is reported; the comparison is qualitative, showing that the smooth diagrams better capture the structure of miscalibration (e.g., non-monotonic calibration in the synthetic example) while encoding the SmoothECE value directly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the classical binned reliability diagram (Naeini et al., 2015), where the number of bins is chosen optimally via cross-validation for regression test MSE loss. The paper does not report a head-to-head numerical comparison of calibration error values between SmoothECE and Binned ECE on the same datasets; instead, the comparison is visual, with Figure 2 showing side-by-side binned and smooth diagrams on four datasets. The smooth diagrams are shown to better handle boundary effects (via the reflected Gaussian kernel), avoid the discontinuity artifacts of binning, and naturally encode the SmoothECE as the integrated absolute deviation from the diagonal weighted by the kernel density. The authors note a limitation: when predictions are known to take only a small finite set of values (e.g., the Finnish rain forecasts use only multiples of 10%), correctly-aligned bins remain a reasonable alternative, suggesting the smooth method's advantage is most pronounced for continuous or high-cardinality prediction distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: the theoretical framework of consistent calibration measures (Blasiok et al., 2023), which provides principled bounds on distance to perfect calibration, and the practical tradition of reliability diagrams (Naeini et al., 2015; Bröcker, 2008), which visually encode miscalibration structure. While kernel smoothing for reliability diagrams was previously suggested (Bröcker, 2008), no prior work proved that such smoothing induces a consistent calibration measure or identified a principled, hyperparameter-free bandwidth. The method is built directly on Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression, with the key departure being the smoothing of residuals (y−f) rather than outcomes, and the use of a reflected Gaussian kernel to handle boundary effects on [0,1].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical gap: Binned ECE remains the dominant calibration measure in ML despite well-documented flaws (discontinuity, bin-width sensitivity, inefficient estimation), partly because it has a natural visual representation. SmoothECE resolves this by providing a consistent calibration measure that is simultaneously readable from a smooth reliability diagram, with the bandwidth determined by a data-driven fixed-point rather than manual tuning. Limitations include restriction to binary outcomes (multiclass requires reduction), and the authors note that for predictors with discrete outputs (e.g., multiples of 10%), simple binning may still be preferable. Open questions include whether the method's theoretical guarantees translate to meaningful differences in model selection at scale, and how it compares to other consistent measures (e.g., MMCE) in practice.
