## Problem

Self-supervised learning methods suffer from dimensional collapse, where learned representations converge to a limited set of points in embedding space, degrading generalization and robustness. Existing mitigations (contrastive losses, regularization, centering/sharpening) operate in a deterministic framework and do not explicitly model the uncertainty inherent in representation learning. This paper asks whether a probabilistic formulation—specifically, minimizing a proper scoring rule between two networks' output distributions—can naturally prevent collapse while improving representation quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (256×256 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR) that are independently augmented via multi-crop (random size 0.8–1.0, aspect ratio 3/4–4/3), horizontal flip, color jittering, and grayscale. The online network's predictor outputs a multivariate normal distribution (mean μ and standard deviation σ) over the embedding space rather than a single point, from which r samples are drawn via the reparameterization trick. Downstream, the frozen encoder features (not the probabilistic head) are used for classification, while the probabilistic latent representation directly supports OOD scoring and calibration metrics (ECE, NLL).

## Method

ProSMin employs a DINO-style online/target network pair (ViT-S/16 or ResNet backbone + 3-layer MLP projector + 2-layer MLP predictor) where the predictor outputs distributional parameters (μ, σ) instead of a point estimate. The training objective is a modified energy scoring rule S*(P_θ, z_ξ) = λ·S¹ + (1−λ)·S², where S¹ measures the distance between online samples and the target output, and S² measures pairwise distances among online samples (an entropy-encouraging term). Samples are generated via the reparameterization trick z_j = μ + σ⊙ε_j, enabling backpropagation through the stochastic loss. The target network is updated by EMA (α scheduled from 0.9 to 1) without backpropagation. The key insight is that the S² term increases the entropy of the online network's predictive distribution, preventing collapse without requiring batch normalization, centering, or sharpening—components that DINO and BYOL rely on for the same purpose.

## Results

On ImageNet linear evaluation (ViT-S/16, 800 epochs), ProSMin achieves 78.4% top-1 accuracy, 76.2% κ-NN accuracy, 0.900 NLL, and 0.006 ECE, outperforming all baselines on every metric. For OOD detection on ImageNet-O (800 epochs), ProSMin reaches 77.1% AUROC and 65.8 mCE on ImageNet-C, surpassing DINO (76.0/66.5) and i-BOT (76.6/66.2). In semi-supervised learning with 1% labels, ProSMin attains 62.1% top-1 accuracy versus 60.3% (DINO) and 61.9% (i-BOT), and in transfer learning to CIFAR-100 it reaches 91.2% versus 90.5% (DINO) and 90.7% (i-BOT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProSMin consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated task. Against DINO (Caron et al., 2021), ProSMin gains +1.6 pp top-1 accuracy (78.4 vs 76.8), +1.7 pp κ-NN, and reduces ECE from 0.015 to 0.006 on ImageNet linear evaluation; on OOD detection it improves AUROC from 76.0 to 77.1 and mCE from 66.5 to 65.8. Against i-BOT (Zhou et al., 2021), ProSMin leads by +0.5 pp top-1 (78.4 vs 77.9), +1.0 pp κ-NN, and halves ECE (0.006 vs 0.013), while also winning on semi-supervised 1% (62.1 vs 61.9) and low-shot 1% (66.1 vs 65.9). Against MoCo-V3 (Chen et al., 2021), the margin is largest: +5.2 pp top-1 (78.4 vs 73.2) and +11.5 pp κ-NN (76.2 vs 64.7), reflecting MoCo-V3's weaker representation quality. In transfer learning, ProSMin beats DINO and i-BOT on all four target datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, iNat-18, Oxford Flowers), with the largest gap on iNat-18 (74.4 vs 72.0 and 73.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

ProSMin builds directly on the DINO/BYOL self-distillation architecture (online/target networks with EMA updates and multi-crop augmentation) but replaces the deterministic cross-entropy distillation loss with a probabilistic scoring rule objective derived from the energy score of Gneiting & Raftery (2007). The key conceptual departure is treating the online network's output as a multivariate normal distribution (via the reparameterization trick) rather than a point estimate, thereby formulating knowledge distillation in a probabilistic manner. This positions the work at the intersection of probabilistic forecasting (scoring rules) and self-supervised representation learning, a combination not previously explored in the SSL literature, while prior anti-collapse methods (VICReg, Barlow Twins, iBOT) operate entirely in the deterministic regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic framing offers a principled mechanism for mitigating dimensional collapse without relying on batch normalization or prediction layers—components that DINO and BYOL specifically require—suggesting that distributional diversity in the online network's output is itself a sufficient anti-collapse signal. However, the practical gains are modest (≈0.5% Top-1 over i-BOT, ≈1% AUROC over DINO on OOD), and the computational cost is notably higher (98h vs. 72.6h for DINO at 300 epochs). An open question is whether the 'improper' modified scoring rule (with λ-weighting) can be theoretically justified beyond empirical observation, and whether the probabilistic framework extends naturally to non-vision modalities or to generative downstream tasks where the learned distribution is directly exploitable.
