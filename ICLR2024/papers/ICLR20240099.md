## Problem

Most practical OOD detection methods for deep neural networks require access to training data or modifications to the training procedure (open-box methods), making them inapplicable to third-party pre-trained models whose training details are unavailable. The paper addresses this by proposing a white-box OOD detection method that operates solely on model weights, and further investigates why between-layer representation transformations differ in smoothness between in-distribution and out-of-distribution data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sequence (or image) as input, runs a forward pass through a pre-trained Transformer (RoBERTa, ELECTRA, or ViT), and extracts the [CLS] token's intermediate representations at each layer. It then computes an OOD uncertainty score per instance by estimating the squared Frobenius norm of the Jacobian between consecutive layer representations using M=50 random Gaussian vector pairs. Two score variants are produced: BLOOD_M (mean across all L−1 inter-layer transitions) and BLOOD_L (score from the final transition only), with higher scores indicating greater likelihood of being OOD.

## Method

BLOOD quantifies the smoothness of each between-layer transformation as the squared Frobenius norm of the Jacobian ∂f_{l+1}/∂h_l evaluated at the intermediate representation h_l. To avoid the prohibitive cost of computing full Jacobians in high-dimensional spaces, the authors derive an unbiased estimator via random vector-Jacobian-vector products (a generalization of Hutchinson's trace estimator), requiring only M forward-mode AD passes per layer. The core insight is that training smooths the ID region of representation space while leaving OOD regions unchanged or even sharpened (for complex tasks), creating a measurable smoothness gap. The method is applied to pre-trained language models fine-tuned on text classification, and the last-layer variant (BLOOD_L) consistently outperforms the average variant because upper layers undergo the most task-specific changes during fine-tuning.

## Results

BLOOD_L (using the last-layer Jacobian Frobenius norm) achieves the best white-box/black-box AUROC across most of the 8 text classification datasets with both RoBERTa and ELECTRA. For example, on BigPatent with ELECTRA, BLOOD_L reaches 96.72% AUROC versus 78.56% for MSP and 84.63% for Energy; on MovieGenre with ELECTRA it reaches 90.63% versus 73.83% for MSP. Against open-box methods, BLOOD_L is outperformed by Mahalanobis distance (MD) in nearly all settings (e.g., 96.72% vs. 98.28% on ELECTRA/BP) but remains competitive with Ensemble (96.72% vs. 84.20%) and Temperature Scaling (96.72% vs. 84.69%). The advantage of BLOOD over other white-box methods is most pronounced on complex datasets (BP, AR, MG, NG) and with ELECTRA, where the final layer undergoes the largest representation change during fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLOOD_L outperforms all six white-box/black-box baselines (MSP, ENT, EGY, MC, GRAD, ASH) on the majority of dataset–model combinations, with the largest margins on complex datasets: e.g., on ELECTRA/BigPatent it beats MSP by 18.2 points (96.72 vs. 78.56) and EGY by 12.1 points (96.72 vs. 84.63). Against the open-box Mahalanobis distance (MD), BLOOD_L is consistently outperformed (e.g., 96.72 vs. 98.28 on ELECTRA/BP; 93.20 vs. 98.35 on RoBERTa/AR), as MD requires access to training data for class-conditional covariance estimation. BLOOD_L surpasses the open-box Ensemble (ENSM) on most settings (e.g., 96.72 vs. 84.20 on ELECTRA/BP; 93.20 vs. 92.44 on RoBERTa/AR) and Temperature Scaling (TEMP) on complex datasets (e.g., 96.72 vs. 84.69 on ELECTRA/BP), while being slightly behind on simpler ones like TREC. The only white-box method that occasionally matches or beats BLOOD_L is MC (Monte-Carlo dropout) on RoBERTa/AGN (77.55 vs. 61.95) and ELECTRA/SUBJ (77.71 vs. 77.41), where the task is relatively simple and the last-layer transformation smoothness signal is weaker. On background-shift Near-OOD detection, BLOOD_L notably outperforms all other methods including open-box ones (62.70% vs. 54.26% MSP, 59.40% MD with RoBERTa), whereas on semantic shift it is outperformed by MSP and MD.

## Relation to Prior Work

BLOOD departs from the dominant paradigm in OOD detection that relies on model outputs (softmax probabilities, energy scores) or representation-space distances to training data, instead probing the internal dynamics of how intermediate representations transform across layers. The stochastic estimation of the Jacobian's Frobenius norm via random vector projections is a direct application of Hutchinson's trick (1990) and the more general norm-estimation framework of Bujanovic & Kressner (2021), repurposed here as an OOD signal rather than a trace estimator. Conceptually, the method bridges the gap between white-box methods that require architectural access (gradient norms, activation shaping) and open-box methods that require training data (Mahalanobis distance, ensembles), by extracting information from the model's internal computation without any training-set access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BLOOD offers a practically deployable OOD detection method for pre-trained Transformer models where training data is unavailable, a common real-world constraint. The finding that BLOOD's advantage grows with dataset complexity—and that for complex tasks the OOD region of representation space is actively sharpened rather than merely left unchanged—challenges the simple narrative that training only smooths the ID region. However, the method is consistently outperformed by Mahalanobis distance (an open-box method) in most settings, and its performance on semantic shift is suboptimal, leaving open the question of whether between-layer smoothness is a universally reliable OOD signal or primarily sensitive to background-level distributional changes.
