limitations: The pseudo videos generated by random concatenation lack genuine temporal coherence and semantic continuity, and the authors explicitly note the approach has only been proven effective in the vision-language pretraining field; whether the concatenated-sample paradigm generalizes to other modalities or truly sequential tasks requiring causal reasoning remains unexplored.

surprise_factor: The most conceptually interesting finding is that random cross-sample concatenation outperforms both vision-similarity and text-similarity-based grouping (Table 9), and that simply duplicating a single sample (COSA-copy) actually degrades performance below the SST baseline—showing the benefit comes from cross-sample diversity and explicit event-sentence correspondence, not from longer sequences. This is a mild conceptual surprise (relevant samples should intuitively help) backed by consistent ablations across multiple benchmarks and scales, though the overall performance gains, while substantial, follow the expected pattern of better pretraining data → better downstream results.

## Problem

Most large-scale vision-language foundation models are pretrained exclusively on image-text corpora because open video-text datasets (e.g., WebVid-2.5M) are two orders of magnitude smaller than their image-text counterparts (e.g., LAION-2B). This forces models to ignore temporal semantics and event-level correlations that are critical for video-language reasoning. The paper asks whether large-scale image-text data alone can be repurposed to train a unified model that captures both static visual-linguistic and temporal event-level representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241948/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, COSA consumes batches of image-text pairs and, at each step, online-concatenates 4 samples (1 anchor + 3 randomly selected from the batch) into a pseudo video-paragraph: 4 individually encoded 224×224 frames with learnable temporal embeddings concatenated along the sequence dimension, paired with 4 captions concatenated into a single paragraph using BERT's position embeddings. At inference, the model accepts either a single image or a set of video frames (8–32 frames depending on the task) plus a text query or question, and produces ranked video/image candidates (via contrastive similarity + ITM reranking), autoregressively generated captions, or open-ended text answers.

## Method

COSA's core mechanism is online sample concatenation: for each image-text sample in a batch, n_c=3 other samples are randomly selected and their images and texts are concatenated in the same order, converting the image-text corpus into a pseudo video-paragraph corpus on the fly. The architecture pairs a ViT/Swin/EVAClip vision encoder with a BERT text encoder augmented with cross-attention layers that activate only during cross-modality forward passes. Four concatenated training objectives (CITC, CITM, CMLM, CGM) are modified to operate on the grouped samples, alongside two vanilla single-sample objectives (ITC, ITM) to preserve single-sample processing ability. A key empirical finding is that random sampling outperforms vision- or text-similarity-based grouping, because random sampling reduces visual redundancy and enforces a strict one-sentence-per-frame correspondence that sharpens event-level temporal alignment, whereas similarity-based grouping introduces semantic redundancy that confuses the model's temporal learning.

## Results

On text-to-video retrieval, COSA (1.2B, 415M examples) achieves R@1 of 57.9 on MSRVTT, 70.5 on DiDeMo, 39.4 on LSMDC, and 67.3 on ActivityNet, setting new state-of-the-art on DiDeMo and ActivityNet. The smaller COSA-B (5M) already outperforms all same-scale baselines by large margins (e.g., 42.2 vs. 38.6 R@1 on MSRVTT vs. Clover). On video QA, COSA (1.2B) reaches 49.2/60.0/79.5/49.9 accuracy on MSR/MSVD/TGIF/ANet, and on video captioning it achieves CIDEr of 74.7/178.5/96.5/131.3/70.7 across five benchmarks, surpassing GIT2 (5.1B) with only 24% of its parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSA-B (5M) outperforms all same-scale video-language pretraining baselines by substantial margins: it beats VIOLETv2 (37.2→42.2 R@1 on MSRVTT), Clover (38.6→42.2), and BridgeFormer (37.6→42.2), with the largest gap on paragraph-to-video retrieval (DiDeMo: 57.8 vs. 47.9 for VIOLETv2). At 17M scale, COSA-B surpasses HiTeA (46.8→46.9 MSRVTT, 56.5→64.1 DiDeMo) and VINDLU-B (45.3→46.9 MSRVTT, 59.2→64.1 DiDeMo), confirming that concatenated sample training is more effective than specialized temporal objectives or retrieval-optimized recipes. At the large-scale setting (~417M examples), COSA-L matches UMT-L on MSRVTT (54.4 vs. 58.8, a 4.4-point gap) but leads on DiDeMo (68.3 vs. 70.4, a 2.1-point gap) and LSMDC (38.6 vs. 43.0). The 1.2B COSA model achieves the best R@1 on DiDeMo (70.5, edging out UMT-L's 70.4) and ActivityNet (67.3 vs. 66.8), while trailing UMT-L on MSRVTT (57.9 vs. 58.8) and LSMDC (39.4 vs. 43.0). On image-text tasks (Table 5), COSA (1.2B) surpasses BLIP-2 (90.2 vs. 89.7 Flickr30K R@1) and BEiT-3 (68.5 vs. 67.2 MSCOCO R@1), and achieves the best SPICE score (27.0) on MSCOCO captioning, demonstrating that the concatenated-sample pretraining strategy benefits both video and image tasks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

COSA sits at the intersection of unified vision-language foundation models (Frozen, CLIP, BLIP, GIT, Flamingo) and video-language temporal learning methods (VIOLET, HiTeA, LF-VILA, OmniVL), but departs from both in a key way: it eliminates the need for video-text corpora entirely by transforming image-text pairs into pseudo video-paragraph inputs through online cross-sample concatenation. While Singularity (Lei et al., 2022) argued temporal modeling is unnecessary due to static appearance bias in benchmarks, COSA counters that event-level temporal correspondence *is* learnable and beneficial if the training data provides explicit scene-to-sentence alignment—something random concatenation of unrelated image-text pairs surprisingly provides. The method repurposes the standard contrastive/matching/generative training objectives (ITC, ITM, MLM, GM) into concatenated variants (CITC, CITM, CMLM, CGM) without architectural changes, positioning itself as a drop-in training recipe rather than a new model family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: COSA demonstrates that the two-orders-of-magnitude gap between image-text (LAION-2B) and video-text (WebVid-2.5M) corpora need not limit video-language model quality, since temporal understanding can be bootstrapped from image-text data alone. The finding that random sampling outperforms similarity-based grouping is a useful negative result for anyone designing multi-sample training strategies. However, the pseudo videos lack genuine temporal coherence (random scene jumps), and the authors acknowledge the approach has only been validated in vision-language pretraining; whether the concatenated-sample paradigm transfers to other modalities or to truly sequential tasks (e.g., long-form video understanding requiring causal reasoning) remains open. The simplicity of the method (no architectural change, 0.9× training overhead) makes it an easy addition to existing foundation model pipelines.
