## Problem

Classical RL agents in sparse-reward environments rely on extensive trial and error because they cannot absorb and integrate external knowledge to guide exploration. Existing approaches face three coupled challenges: acquiring and representing knowledge without costly human labeling, bridging the gap between discrete natural-language knowledge and continuous internal policies, and maintaining synergistic updates so that both the knowledge source and the policy improve over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241653/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Planner receives a fully observed grid state (H×W×3 symbolic features in BabyAI; a 21×79 glyph matrix plus a 21-dimensional agent-statistics vector in MiniHack) together with a set of natural-language instructions (652 templated entries in BabyAI; 256-character gameplay messages in MiniHack), and outputs a single selected instruction. The Actor receives a partially observed 7×7 ego-centric grid (BabyAI) or the same observation as the Planner (MiniHack) plus that single instruction, and outputs a distribution over 7 (BabyAI) or 75 (MiniHack) discrete actions. Knowledge is encoded via a frozen BERT-base model, so no fine-tuning of the language model is required.

## Method

PAE comprises three components in a circular feedback loop: a Planner, an Actor, and an Evaluator. The Planner encodes state with a shape-preserving 2D CNN and knowledge with frozen BERT, then applies scaled dot-product cross-attention (knowledge as query, state as key/value) to align each instruction with the current environment before selecting one via softmax. The Actor mirrors this cross-attention pipeline on its partial observation and the single selected instruction, and additionally maximizes mutual information I(s; k) between visited states and the guiding knowledge using a trainable discriminative network q_φ(k|s) that reverse-infers the Planner's choice from observed states, yielding an intrinsic MI reward. The Evaluator closes the loop by giving the Actor a +1 intrinsic reward per subtask completed under guidance plus the MI term, and giving the Planner a reward based on whether the Actor finishes the guidance within an adaptive step threshold t*, which increases linearly every ten successful completions—effectively implementing an automatic curriculum that progressively raises knowledge difficulty as the Actor's capability grows.

## Results

Across 11 environments (6 BabyAI, 5 MiniHack), PAE achieves near-perfect extrinsic rewards (0.87–1.00) with convergence in 6–150M steps, while non-knowledge-guided baselines (IMPALA, RND, ICM, AMIGo) largely fail to converge within the step budget. PAE improves average performance by 148% over the best non-knowledge-guided baseline and converges at least 40% faster. Against language-guided baselines (L-AMIGo, L-NovelD), PAE achieves at least 13% higher average performance and converges at least 31% faster, with the largest gaps on MiniHack's LavaCross tasks where PAE reaches 1.00 reward in 22–35M steps versus L-AMIGo's 0.45–0.58 without convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAE decisively outperforms all six baselines across all 11 environments. Against vanilla baselines (IMPALA, RND), PAE achieves 0.87–1.00 extrinsic reward where both baselines score 0.00 in nearly every environment, indicating complete failure to solve the tasks. ICM shows marginal progress on a few BabyAI tasks (e.g., 0.31 on OBSTRMAZE1DL) but still fails to converge on most. AMIGo reaches 0.10–0.45 reward but never converges within the step limit in any environment. The closest competitors are the language-guided baselines: L-AMIGo achieves 0.86–0.93 on BabyAI Key Corridor tasks (nearly matching PAE) but degrades sharply on Obstructed Maze (0.23–0.47) and MiniHack (0.31–0.58), while L-NovelD peaks at 0.99 on one MiniHack task but remains unstable (high variance) and fails to converge in most settings. PAE's key advantage is consistent convergence: it is the only method that converges in all 11 environments, with the largest absolute gaps on MiniHack's LavaCross tasks (1.00 vs. 0.45–0.58 for L-AMIGo).

## Relation to Prior Work

PAE extends the language-guided exploration line (L-AMIGo, L-NovelD from Mu et al., 2022) by introducing an explicit three-component architecture where a Planner selects difficulty-matched knowledge via cross-attention alignment, an Actor integrates that knowledge into its policy through a second cross-attention layer plus a mutual information objective (derived from Eysenbach et al., 2018), and an Evaluator provides intrinsic rewards that create a circular curriculum feedback loop. The Planner's reward mechanism directly inherits from AMIGo's adversarial teacher-student formulation (Campero et al., 2020), while the entire training loop runs on the IMPALA/TorchBeast infrastructure. The key departure from L-AMIGo is that PAE does not treat language as a fixed external signal but actively learns to select, align, and progressively escalate knowledge based on the Agent's current capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAE demonstrates that structured knowledge selection (not just language presence) is critical for sparse-reward exploration: removing the Planner entirely causes complete failure across all six BabyAI tasks, and removing the curriculum mechanism causes failure on the harder Key Corridor variants. The automatic curriculum property (Planner progressively increasing knowledge difficulty) offers interpretability that purely numerical intrinsic rewards lack. However, the approach is limited to environments that natively provide language knowledge (BabyAI, MiniHack), and the authors acknowledge that extending to LLM-generated knowledge or multimodal inputs remains open. The 13% improvement over the closest baseline (L-AMIGo) is meaningful but modest, and the evaluation is confined to grid-world and roguelike environments, leaving open whether the framework transfers to continuous-control or high-dimensional perceptual settings.
