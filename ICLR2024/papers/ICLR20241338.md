## Problem

Existing patch pruning methods for Vision Transformers evaluate patch importance only within a single layer, causing patches that are unimportant in shallow blocks but critical in deeper blocks to be irreversibly pruned. Additionally, most approaches rely on manually tuned per-layer pruning rates or expensive iterative search, limiting generalizability across tasks and models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241338/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained ViT (DeiT or LV-ViT) processing 224×224 RGB images split into 16×16 patches (N=196 tokens plus a [CLS] token). During inference, it dynamically discards patches at each layer based on fused importance scores, producing a class label with progressively fewer tokens flowing through deeper blocks. The inter-layer importance scores and per-layer retention rates are pre-computed offline on the training set (using 500 sampled images for ACS and T=10 LRP rounds), so the only online overhead is the attention-map computation and a CDF lookup for the adaptive variant.

## Method

STAR fuses two complementary importance signals: (1) intra-layer scores derived online from the [CLS] attention map at each block (averaged over heads), and (2) inter-layer scores computed offline via a modified Layer-wise Relevance Propagation that aggregates patch-level relevance to the [CLS] across all layers and top-γ classes. Because both scores are positive, they are treated as probability mass functions and fused by minimizing a weighted KL divergence, yielding the closed-form geometric mean S^fused ∝ S^intra^α × S^inter^(1−α). For automatic per-layer retention rate selection, STAR computes the Average Cosine Similarity (ACS) between patches at each layer offline and establishes a bilinear mapping p_l = a·ACS_l + b anchored at p_1=1 and p_L=ρ, giving a single hyperparameter ρ that monotonically controls overall FLOPs reduction. An adaptive variant (a-STAR) repurposes p_l as a weighted percentile threshold on the sorted fused scores, making the number of retained patches input-dependent while provably achieving at least the same compression as the static variant.

## Results

On ImageNet, STAR achieves 42.0% FLOPs reduction on DeiT-Base with only 0.3% Top-1 accuracy drop (81.5% vs. 81.8% baseline) and 69.5% throughput speedup. For DeiT-Small at 50% FLOPs reduction, STAR reaches 4,080.6 images/s throughput (104.5% speedup) with just 0.6% Top-1 drop, surpassing the unpruned DeiT-Tiny by +6.8% Top-1 and +3.1% Top-5 accuracy while being 60% faster. On LV-ViT-S, STAR achieves 50.8% FLOPs reduction with 99.4% speedup and only 0.6% Top-1 degradation. The adaptive variant a-STAR further increases compression (e.g., 48.9% vs. 47.2% on DeiT-Base) with negligible accuracy cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR consistently outperforms all 12 baselines on both accuracy and throughput across DeiT and LV-ViT models. At comparable FLOPs reduction (~42%) on DeiT-Base, STAR (81.5% Top-1) beats SCOP (79.7%), VTP (80.7%), and CP-ViT (81.1%) while achieving 69.5% speedup vs. SCOP's 43.1%. On DeiT-Small at ~50% FLOPs reduction, STAR (79.2% Top-1, 4,080.6 img/s) surpasses HVT (78.0%, 2,835.7 img/s) by +1.2% accuracy and +44% throughput, and outperforms ToMe (79.4% Top-1 at 41.3% reduction) with greater compression and higher speedup. On DeiT-Tiny, STAR (72.0% Top-1, 5,042.9 img/s) exceeds VTC-LFC (71.0%, 4,352.8 img/s) by +1.0% accuracy and +16% throughput at 46.2% vs. 41.7% FLOPs reduction. For LV-ViT-S at 50.8% FLOPs reduction, STAR (82.7% Top-1, 3,353.2 img/s) dominates VTC-LFC (81.8%, no throughput reported) and EViT (82.5% at only 40% reduction). In the dedicated DiffRate comparison (Table 8), STAR achieves +14.3% throughput speedup over DiffRate at the same 81.6% Top-1 accuracy, while a-STAR reaches +24.4% speedup with only −0.1% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR builds on the LRP-based interpretability framework of Chefer et al. (CVPR 2021) by adapting it from pixel-level to patch-level relevance and extending it to capture multi-class correlations, yielding offline inter-layer importance scores. It departs from prior patch-pruning methods (EViT, VTP, VTC-LFC, etc.) that rely solely on intra-layer attention scores or manual per-layer retention schedules, by fusing intra-layer (online attention) and inter-layer (offline LRP) scores through a KL-divergence minimization with a closed-form geometric-mean solution. The automatic retention-rate selection via Average Cosine Similarity (ACS) also distinguishes STAR from methods that require empirical tuning or iterative sensitivity analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAR offers a practical, lightweight compression pipeline for ViT deployment: a single hyperparameter ρ controls overall FLOPs reduction, and the method requires no retraining (only optional fine-tuning), making it attractive for resource-constrained settings. The demonstrated throughput advantage (pruned DeiT-Small exceeding unpruned DeiT-Tiny by 66% while being more accurate) is a compelling practical result for practitioners choosing between model size and compression. Open questions include whether the offline LRP computation generalizes to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), whether the ACS-based rate selection holds for architectures with non-uniform patch redundancy, and whether the method extends to other transformer backbones beyond DeiT and LV-ViT.
