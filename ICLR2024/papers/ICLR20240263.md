## Problem

Position-only equivariant networks (e.g., SchNet) are forced to use isotropic, rotation-invariant kernels, preventing them from learning direction-sensitive representations, while full SE(3) group convolutions capture directional features at prohibitive computational cost. The paper addresses this by formalizing weight-sharing in equivariant message passing as sharing message functions over equivalence classes of point pairs, and by identifying the homogeneous space R³×S² (positions and orientations) as an optimal middle ground that supports directional information at a fraction of the cost of full group convolutions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse 3D point clouds (atomic coordinates with one-hot atom types, or particle positions/velocities/charges) and lifts them onto a dense position-orientation space R³×S² via ScalarToSphere (constant spherical signals) or VecToSphere (first-order spherical harmonic embedding) modules. Each position carries a spherical signal sampled on a shared grid of N=20 orientations, and outputs are read back via SphereToScalar (integration over the sphere) for invariant quantities like total energy, or SphereToVec (weighted integration) for equivariant vector outputs like forces and displacement vectors. Forces are not predicted directly but computed as gradients of the predicted energy with respect to atomic positions.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which derives bijective invariant attributes for equivalence classes of point pairs on SE(n) homogeneous spaces (Rⁿ, Rⁿ×Sⁿ⁻¹, and SE(n) itself), proving these attributes are all that is needed for equivariant weight-sharing and universal approximation. The PΘNITA architecture exploits the structure of the R³×S² attribute (parallel displacement, perpendicular displacement norm, and orientation angle) to factor the group convolution kernel into three separable components: a spatial convolution over neighborhoods (depth-wise, no channel mixing), a spherical convolution over orientations (batched over positions and channels with a precomputed kernel), and a linear channel-mixing layer. This separability, combined with a ConvNeXt-style residual block structure and MLP-based kernels parameterized via polynomial embeddings of the invariant attributes, yields a fully convolutional network that is ~3.5× faster than NequIP while matching or exceeding state-of-the-art accuracy on interatomic potential prediction, molecular generation via equivariant diffusion, and N-body trajectory forecasting.

## Results

On rMD17 (10 small molecules), PΘNITA achieves the best or near-best energy MAE (e.g., 1.7 kcal/mol on Aspirin vs. 2.2 for MACE) and force MAE (e.g., 5.8 kcal/mol/Å on Aspirin vs. 6.6 for MACE) while training in 6.1 sec/epoch — approximately 3.5× faster than NequIP (20.7 sec/epoch). On QM9 molecule generation, PΘNITA achieves the best NLL (−137.4), atom stability (98.9%), and molecule stability (87.8%), outperforming EDM (−110.7, 98.7%, 82.0%). On N-body trajectory prediction, PΘNITA matches SEGNN G+P (MSE 0.0043) and is within 0.0004 of CGENN (0.0039) while training 2.4× faster (0.66 vs. 1.59 sec/epoch).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On rMD17, PΘNITA outperforms all tensor-field and message-passing baselines (UNiTE, GemNet, NequIP, BOTNet, Allegro, MACE, ViSNet) on the majority of molecules for both energy and force MAE, with the largest gaps on Aspirin (E: 1.7 vs. 2.2 for MACE; F: 5.8 vs. 6.6 for MACE) and Azobenzene (F: 2.3 vs. 3.0 for MACE). The position-only ablation PNITA (using only distance as attribute) is substantially worse (e.g., 4.7 vs. 1.7 energy MAE on Aspirin), confirming that orientation-sensitive representations are critical. On QM9 molecule generation, PΘNITA beats EDM (NLL −137.4 vs. −110.7; molecule stability 87.8% vs. 82.0%) and all prior equivariant diffusion baselines (G-Schnet, GDM, GDM-aug, E-NF). On N-body trajectory prediction, PΘNITA matches SEGNN G+P (MSE 0.0043) and is within 0.0004 of CGENN (0.0039), while training 2.4× faster than SEGNN (0.66 vs. 1.59 sec/epoch) and substantially outperforming SE(3)-Transformers (0.0244), TFN (0.0155), NMP (0.0107), Radial Field (0.0104), and EGNN (0.0070).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 'convolution is all you need' theorem (Cohen et al. 2019; Bekkers 2019) and the separable group convolution framework of Knigge et al. (2022), but departs from the dominant tensor-field/steerable paradigm (NequIP, MACE, TFN) by adopting a regular group convolution viewpoint on the homogeneous space R³×S². It also extends the position-orientation space idea from DimeNet/GemNet, which use sparse edge-direction grids, by employing a dense shared spherical grid and a ConvNeXt-style fully convolutional architecture. The key conceptual departure is formalizing weight-sharing as conditioning message functions on bijective attributes of equivalence classes of point pairs (Theorem 1), providing a unified theoretical lens that subsumes prior ad-hoc invariant attribute choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple, fully convolutional architecture requiring no Clebsch-Gordan tensor products or deep representation-theory knowledge can match or exceed state-of-the-art equivariant methods while being ~3.5× faster, lowering the barrier to building competitive equivariant models. The clean theoretical framework (homogeneous spaces → equivalence classes → bijective attributes) gives practitioners a principled recipe for constructing equivariant weight-sharing in any SE(n) setting. Open questions include whether the fixed dense spherical grid (N=20) is always optimal versus adaptive or learned grids, how the approach scales to very large systems (tens of thousands of atoms), and whether the small discretization-induced equivariance errors become problematic in safety-critical applications.
