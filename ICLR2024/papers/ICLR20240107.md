## Problem

Existing crystal graph representations (CGCNN, Matformer, PotNet, ALIGNN, etc.) cannot achieve geometric completeness: they fail to capture periodic lattice patterns, cannot distinguish crystals with different unit cell structures sharing the same local distances, and cannot differentiate chiral crystals. This means structurally distinct crystals with different properties can be mapped to identical graph representations, producing identical predictions. The paper addresses this by constructing SE(3)-invariant and SO(3)-equivariant crystal graphs that provably distinguish any two non-isometric crystals while satisfying all crystal passive symmetries.

## Input / Output

The method takes a crystal structure as a triple (A, P, L): a d_a × n atom feature matrix, a 3 × n position matrix, and a 3 × 3 lattice matrix, and produces a scalar property value (regression, e.g., formation energy in meV/atom) or a discrete class label (classification, e.g., band gap). Graph construction uses a k-nearest-neighbor cutoff radius (k=12 or 16 in experiments), and the method must remain invariant under arbitrary unit cell SE(3) transformations and periodic boundary shifts, meaning the same infinite crystal described by different unit cells yields identical graph representations. The lattice representation is derived from the three nearest periodic duplicates of each atom rather than directly from the input lattice vectors, ensuring periodic invariance.

## Method

ComFormer introduces a lattice-based node representation where each atom i is associated with three periodic duplicates (i₁, i₂, i₃) selected by nearest-neighbor distance with a right-hand system constraint, yielding three lattice vectors {e_ii₁, e_ii₂, e_ii₃} that encode periodic patterns in a periodic-invariant manner. Two variants are proposed: iComFormer uses SE(3)-invariant edge features (Euclidean distance plus three angles between the bond vector and the lattice vectors) processed by node-wise and edge-wise transformer layers, while eComFormer uses SO(3)-equivariant vector edge features embedded via spherical harmonics (orders 0, 1, 2) and processes them through stacked tensor product layers to capture two-hop bond angle information. Both variants achieve O(nk) computational complexity, matching Matformer while being geometrically complete. Geometric completeness is proven by mathematical induction showing that the graph uniquely determines the infinite crystal structure, and verified empirically by reconstructing crystal structures from graphs with RMSD < 10⁻⁶ on Materials Project and T2 datasets.

## Results

On JARVIS, iComFormer achieves the best MAE on 4 out of 5 tasks (Form. Energy: 27.2 meV/atom, Bandgap OPT: 0.122 eV, E_total: 28.8 meV/atom, Bandgap MBJ: 0.26 eV), while eComFormer achieves the best on E_hull (44 meV). On The Materials Project, iComFormer leads on Band Gap (0.193 eV), Bulk Moduli (0.0380 log(GPa)), and Shear Moduli (0.0637 log(GPa)), while eComFormer leads on Form. Energy (18.16 meV/atom). On MatBench e_form (132K crystals), both variants achieve the best MAE of 16.5 meV/atom, outperforming coGN (17.0) and ALIGNN (21.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ComFormer variants outperform all 10 baselines on the majority of tasks across three benchmarks. Against the closest competitors, iComFormer improves over PotNet by 8% on JARVIS Form. Energy (27.2 vs. 29.4 meV/atom) and by 15% on E_hull (47 vs. 55 meV), while eComFormer improves over PotNet by 20% on E_hull (44 vs. 55 meV). On The Materials Project, iComFormer beats PotNet on Band Gap (0.193 vs. 0.204 eV), Bulk Moduli (0.0380 vs. 0.040 log(GPa)), and Shear Moduli (0.0637 vs. 0.0650 log(GPa)), while eComFormer edges out PotNet on Form. Energy (18.16 vs. 18.8 meV/atom). On MatBench e_form, both variants (16.5 MAE) surpass coGN (17.0), M3GNet (19.5), and ALIGNN (21.5), though MODNet (33.2 MAE) remains stronger on the small-scale jdft2d task (636 crystals) where iComFormer (34.8) is competitive. Efficiency-wise, iComFormer (4.1M params, 69s/epoch) is comparable to Matformer (2.9M, 64s) and significantly faster than ALIGNN (4.0M, 327s) which has O(nk²) complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the periodic graph transformer lineage initiated by Matformer (Yan et al., 2022) and the multi-edge crystal graph construction from CGCNN (Xie & Grossman, 2018), but departs fundamentally by introducing lattice-based node representations built from periodic duplicates to achieve provable geometric completeness. While prior complete representations for crystals (AMD, PDD) exist in matrix form, they cannot distinguish chiral structures and are impractical for property prediction; prior complete molecule graphs (GemNet, SphereNet, ComENet) fail to handle periodicity. The ComFormer architecture combines these complete graphs with transformer-style message passing (iComFormer using invariant angles, eComFormer using equivariant vectors with tensor product layers), maintaining O(nk) complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably geometrically complete crystal graph representation that is also computationally efficient (O(nk)), resolving a gap explicitly identified as unsolved in the literature. The completeness guarantee means the model can in principle distinguish any two different crystals, eliminating a class of silent failures where structurally distinct materials receive identical predictions. Limitations include restriction to crystalline (not amorphous) materials, inability to predict higher-order properties, and the fact that completeness requires a sufficiently large neighbor radius k. Open questions include whether the completeness guarantee translates to better generalization on unseen crystal families and whether the approach extends to dynamic or disordered systems.
