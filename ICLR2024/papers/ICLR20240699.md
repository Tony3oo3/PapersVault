## Problem

Diffusion models can edit images at a coarse scale but struggle to precisely control the layout, position, pose, and shape of objects. Existing motion-based editing methods are limited to sparse, single-point displacements, require text prompts, and impose strong architectural constraints (e.g., cross-attention from specific text tokens or per-image finetuning), making them unable to handle dense, complex, nonrigid deformations in a zero-shot manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 source image (real or synthetic) and a dense target flow field of shape w×h×2 specifying per-pixel displacement, along with an optional text prompt. The flow field can be hand-crafted via a GUI (translations, rotations, stretches, homographies, interpolated deformations) or extracted from a video. Output is a single motion-edited image at the same resolution, generated in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.4 with the guidance function precomposed with the VAE decoder to operate on latent codes.

## Method

Motion guidance steers the diffusion denoising process by backpropagating through an off-the-shelf optical flow network (RAFT) to compute gradients of a composite loss: a flow-matching term (L1 distance between estimated flow and target flow) plus a color-consistency term (L1 distance between source pixels and backward-warped generated pixels, masked for occlusions). The guidance is applied to a one-step clean approximation x̂₀ of the noisy sample to avoid distribution mismatch with the flow network, and the augmented noise estimate follows the standard classifier guidance form. Stability is achieved through recursive denoising (K=10 sub-steps per timestep), gradient norm clipping, an automatic edit mask that preserves non-moving regions, and a guidance schedule that disables guidance for the final 100 of 500 DDIM steps. The entire pipeline is zero-shot, requires no training or finetuning, and is architecture-agnostic with respect to the underlying diffusion model.

## Results

The paper evaluates on two datasets — a curated set of 204 examples with handcrafted target flows and an automatically generated KITTI set of 226 examples — using two metrics: Flow Loss (measured with both RAFT and GMFlow to avoid overfitting) and CLIP Similarity (cosine distance between source and generated image embeddings). The method achieves the best trade-off between flow adherence and visual fidelity: it reaches lower flow loss than InstructPix2Pix while maintaining substantially higher CLIP similarity than the forward-warp baselines (SDEdit and RePaint), which sacrifice image quality to satisfy the explicit warp. No single numerical table is provided; results are presented as scatter plots (Figure 6) showing the Pareto frontier across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InstructPix2Pix, the proposed method clearly wins: InstructPix2Pix is 'never able to move objects significantly' because text is a poor medium for specifying dense motion, and feature-copying methods struggle with structural edits. Against Forward Warp + SDEdit, the proposed method wins on CLIP similarity (visual fidelity) while SDEdit can match or beat it on flow loss at low noise levels, but SDEdit produces severe artifacts in failure cases. Against Forward Warp + RePaint, the proposed method wins on overall quality: RePaint achieves low flow loss via the explicit warp but introduces aliasing artifacts and inpainting errors (e.g., hallucinating an extra teapot), resulting in lower CLIP similarity. Against DragGAN, the proposed method wins on generality: DragGAN is restricted to narrow StyleGAN domains and produces many artifacts on out-of-domain images, while motion guidance works zero-shot on real and synthetic images without domain-specific GANs. Overall, the method dominates on the joint flow-loss/CLIP-similarity trade-off across both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier guidance framework (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) by plugging an off-the-shelf optical flow network (RAFT) as the differentiable guidance function, building on the reconstruction-guidance technique of Bansal et al. (2023) and Ho et al. (2022) to handle the distribution mismatch between noisy diffusion samples and clean-image-trained flow models. It departs from concurrent motion-editing methods (DragGAN, DragDiffusion, SelfGuidance) that rely on sparse point handles, internal cross-attention features, or per-image finetuning, by instead using a dense, pixel-level flow field as the control signal in a fully zero-shot, architecture-agnostic manner. The recursive denoising strategy is borrowed from RePaint (Lugmayr et al., 2022) to stabilize optimization through the complex flow network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key insight—that a low-level, differentiable CV model (optical flow) can serve as an effective guidance signal for diffusion sampling—opens a general recipe for repurposing other off-the-shelf vision models (segmentation, depth, pose) for controllable generation without retraining. Practical limitations remain significant: sampling takes 15–70 minutes per image, guidance is occasionally unstable, and out-of-distribution flows (e.g., flips) fail entirely. Open questions include whether the approach can be accelerated, whether it extends to temporally coherent video generation (the paper notes disocclusion fill-in is incoherent across frames), and whether more robust flow estimators would close the gap on identity drift and out-of-domain failures.
