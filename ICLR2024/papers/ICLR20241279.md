## Problem

Score distillation sampling (SDS)-based text-to-3D methods (DreamFusion, SJC, ProlificDreamer) produce geometrically inconsistent scenes—the 'Janus problem'—because the pretrained 2D diffusion model has no awareness of camera pose or 3D structure, causing frontal features to be replicated across all viewpoints. Ad-hoc workarounds like appending 'side view' to prompts are too ambiguous to cover the continuous range of camera poses, leaving the generation process vulnerable to severe distortions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt and produces a NeRF representing a 3D scene. Internally, the pipeline first generates a 2D image from the prompt (via Karlo/unCLIP), constructs a coarse point cloud from that image (via Point-E or MCC), and then projects the point cloud into sparse depth maps at each rendering viewpoint during SDS optimization. The sparse depth maps are the key intermediate condition: they are explicitly 3D-consistent but contain only a few thousand points with inherent noise and artifacts, which the diffusion model must implicitly complete into dense geometry.

## Method

3DFuse injects 3D awareness into a frozen Stable Diffusion model through a ControlNet-style sparse depth injector that receives projected point-cloud depth maps and adds its features residually to the U-Net's intermediate decoder blocks. The injector is trained on two complementary datasets: sparse depth maps from Co3D point clouds (with random subsampling and noisy-point augmentation to handle sparsity and artifacts) and dense MiDaS-predicted depths on text-image pairs for category generalization. A semantic code sampling step (textual inversion on an initially generated image) locks in the scene's identity, and LoRA layers fine-tuned on that image further enforce cross-viewpoint semantic consistency. The diffusion model backbone remains frozen throughout; only the depth injector and LoRA parameters are trained, preserving the pretrained model's full generative capacity while conditioning it on explicit 3D geometry.

## Results

On the proposed COLMAP-based 3D consistency metric (lower is better), SJC+3DFuse achieves a variance of 0.0499, nearly halving the 0.0870 variance of the SJC baseline across 42 generated scenes. In a user study with 102 participants, SJC+3DFuse is preferred for 3D coherence (60.1%), prompt adherence (59.2%), and overall quality (60.9%), compared to Stable-DreamFusion (23.4%, 23.4%, 22.7%) and SJC (16.5%, 17.4%, 16.4%). Qualitative comparisons on ProlificDreamer, DreamFusion, and SJC backbones all show substantially improved geometric consistency and fidelity when 3DFuse is applied.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SJC (Wang et al., 2022a), 3DFuse nearly halves the COLMAP 3D-consistency variance (0.0499 vs. 0.0870) and dominates the user study by a wide margin (60.1% vs. 16.5% for 3D coherence). Against Stable-DreamFusion (Tang, 2022), 3DFuse is preferred roughly 2.5× more often across all three user-study criteria. Against ProlificDreamer (Wang et al., 2023), the comparison is qualitative only (Figure 4), but 3DFuse visibly eliminates the multi-face 'Janus' artifacts and produces coherent geometry from all viewpoints. Against Zero-123 (Liu et al., 2023), 3DFuse generates more expressive and higher-fidelity 3D scenes (Figures 10, 14), which the authors attribute to preserving the full generative capacity of the frozen diffusion model rather than fine-tuning it for pose conditioning. Against Magic123 (Qian et al., 2023), 3DFuse again produces more detailed results in the image-to-3D comparison (Figure 14), benefiting from its loosely conditioned depth prior that allows the diffusion model greater freedom to synthesize realistic details.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDS-based text-to-3D generation lineage (DreamFusion, SJC, ProlificDreamer) by addressing their fundamental 3D inconsistency problem (the 'Janus problem') through a ControlNet-style sparse depth injection module. It departs from the concurrent Zero-123 approach, which conditions on relative camera pose in the 2D image domain, by instead projecting an explicit coarse 3D point cloud (from Point-E or MCC) into view-aligned depth maps, providing geometrically consistent structural priors. The semantic code sampling (textual inversion) and LoRA layers build on established personalization techniques to lock in semantic identity across viewpoints, while the overall framework remains a plug-in module compatible with existing SDS baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, modular solution to a well-known failure mode in text-to-3D generation that has persisted across multiple SDS variants, and its plug-in design means it can immediately improve existing pipelines without retraining the diffusion backbone. The proposed COLMAP-based 3D consistency metric fills a gap in quantitative evaluation for zero-shot text-to-3D, though its resolution limits discrimination among high-quality outputs. Open questions include whether the coarse 3D prior quality (from Point-E/MCC) becomes a bottleneck for complex multi-object scenes, whether the approach generalizes beyond single-object generation, and how it interacts with emerging 3D-native diffusion models that may obviate the need for 2D-to-3D distillation altogether.
