## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs compress knowledge into next-token conditionals, making tractable inference limited to left-to-right sampling from a prefix. Many important tasks—sequence infilling, chain-of-thought reasoning, constrained generation—require sampling from intractable posterior distributions over latent token sequences. Existing alternatives (MCMC, reward-maximizing RL like PPO) are either prohibitively slow, struggle with multi-modal distributions over language, or collapse to a small number of modes, especially under reward misspecification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt X (and optionally a suffix Y for tasks like infilling) as conditioning input to the GFlowNet policy, which is initialized as a copy of the pretrained LLM. It produces sampled latent token sequences Z (reasoning chains, story infills, sentence continuations) autoregressively until a stop token. For prediction tasks, the final answer Y is obtained by Bayesian model averaging: multiple Z's are drawn from q_GFN(Z|X), each is fed to p_LM(Y|XZ), and the majority vote over sampled Y's gives the prediction. The reward R(Z)=p_LM(XZY) is evaluated by the same base LLM, making the setup self-referential.

## Method

The core contribution is fine-tuning an LLM with a GFlowNet learning objective (modified SubTB for terminable states) so that the policy samples sequences with probability proportional to p_LM(XZY), i.e., the intractable posterior. The GFlowNet policy is initialized as a copy of the pretrained LLM conditioned on X (or X,Y), and trained via gradient descent on the SubTB loss using trajectories from three sources: the current policy, a tempered version of the policy, and a replay buffer seeded with dataset rationales. Amortization is achieved by making X (and optionally Y) an explicit input to the policy, enabling generalization to unseen prompts. For classification and reasoning tasks, the trained GFlowNet serves as the E-step of an EM algorithm: it samples diverse latent rationales Z, which are then used in an M-step (supervised fine-tuning of p_LM on the sampled XZY triples). This distribution-matching objective avoids the mode collapse of reward-maximizing RL and is robust to reward misspecification, as demonstrated by large gains over PPO and supervised fine-tuning on arithmetic and classification tasks.

## Results

On integer arithmetic with a GPT-J 6B model, GFlowNet fine-tuning achieves 95.2% accuracy on 3-operand expressions and 40.7% on out-of-distribution 5-operand expressions, compared to 72.1% and 12.8% for supervised fine-tuning and 30.6% and 5.6% for PPO. On subjectivity classification (SUBJ) with only 10 labeled examples, GFlowNet fine-tuning reaches 71.4% test accuracy versus 64.3% for supervised fine-tuning, and 75.2% when combined with a subsequent supervised fine-tuning step (one EM iteration). On story infilling (ROCStories), GFlowNet fine-tuning achieves a BERTScore of 0.184 and GPT-4 coherence rating of 3.4, roughly doubling the BERTScore of the prompting baseline (0.081) and supervised fine-tuning (0.094).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFlowNet fine-tuning decisively outperforms PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) on integer arithmetic, achieving 95.2% vs. 30.6% on 3-operand expressions and 40.7% vs. 5.6% on out-of-distribution 5-operand expressions; the paper attributes PPO's failure to mode collapse and overoptimization to a misspecified reward. Against supervised fine-tuning, GFlowNet wins on both in-distribution (95.2% vs. 72.1%) and out-of-distribution (40.7% vs. 12.8%) arithmetic, and on SUBJ with 10 examples (71.4% vs. 64.3%), though supervised fine-tuning catches up at 50 examples (89.7% vs. 87.7%). On sentence continuation, GFlowNet fine-tuning produces higher-likelihood samples than diverse beam search (Vijayakumar et al., 2018) and nucleus sampling (Holtzman et al., 2019) while maintaining greater semantic diversity, even when those baselines are given 5× the compute. Compared to zero-shot chain-of-thought prompting (Kojima et al., 2022; 10.2%) and few-shot CoT (Wei et al., 2022; up to 35.5% at k=20), GFlowNet fine-tuning achieves 95.2% on 3-operand arithmetic, demonstrating that learning to sample the full posterior over reasoning chains is far more effective than in-context demonstration. On story infilling, GFlowNet fine-tuning (BERTScore 0.184) roughly doubles the prompting baseline (0.081) and supervised fine-tuning (0.094), with GPT-4 coherence ratings of 3.4 vs. 2.4 and 2.7 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021) from scientific discovery and biological sequence design to LLM fine-tuning, using the SubTB objective (Madan et al., 2023) with the terminable-state modification from Deleu et al. (2022). Conceptually, it departs from both MCMC-based approaches to intractable LLM inference (Miao et al., 2019; Lew et al., 2023), which are slow and hard to tune for language, and from reward-maximizing RL (PPO; Schulman et al., 2017), which collapses to a few modes. The key reframing is treating chain-of-thought reasoning as Bayesian posterior inference over a latent variable Z, positioning GFlowNet fine-tuning as amortized inference rather than policy optimization or supervised fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, distribution-matching alternative to the two dominant LLM fine-tuning paradigms (MLE and reward-maximizing RL), with concrete benefits in data efficiency (10 examples for classification, 50 for arithmetic) and out-of-distribution generalization. The diversity-preserving property of GFlowNet sampling is shown to be practically useful for Bayesian model averaging (self-consistency), connecting the method to the broader CoT aggregation literature. Key limitations include experiments restricted to 6B-parameter models, open exploration challenges for complex latents, and the fact that the method improves inference over existing knowledge without addressing hallucination or miscalibration. Future directions include scaling to larger models, building a universal reasoner shared across tasks, and extending to structured (tree/list) chains of thought.
