## Problem

Existing causal inference benchmarks in NLP primarily test whether models can recall empirical causal knowledge (e.g., commonsense cause-effect pairs) from training data, rather than whether they can perform pure formal causal reasoning. This paper asks whether LLMs can actually deduce causal relationships from correlational statistics using the formal rules of causal discovery, a skill that is independent of domain-specific knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241131/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input consists of a long correlational premise (averaging ~424 tokens) describing all statistical independence/correlation relations among N abstract variables (A, B, C, …) in a closed system with no hidden confounders, paired with a short hypothesis (~11 tokens) asserting one of six causal relations (Is-Parent, Is-Child, Is-Ancestor, Is-Descendant, Has-Confounder, Has-Collider) between two variables. The output is a single binary validity label: 1 if the hypothesized causal relation is necessarily entailed by the correlations (i.e., holds for every DAG in the Markov equivalence class), and 0 otherwise.

## Method

The dataset is constructed by a fully programmatic pipeline grounded in the PC algorithm: (1) enumerate all unique DAGs with 2–6 nodes via topological ordering and graph isomorphism checks; (2) compute all d-separation sets for each DAG to derive the induced statistical correlations and cluster DAGs into Markov equivalence classes (MECs); (3) for each MEC, verbalize the shared correlation pattern as a premise and generate hypotheses for all six causal relations between every variable pair; (4) assign validity=1 only when the hypothesis is a shared property of every DAG in the MEC (a bijective mapping), yielding 207,972 samples. The paper then evaluates 17 off-the-shelf LLMs zero-shot, finetunes 17 additional models on the training split, and probes robustness via two adversarial perturbations—paraphrasing the hypothesis templates and reversing variable names (A→Z, B→Y, …)—to separate genuine reasoning from surface-form overfitting.

## Results

On the CORR2CAUSE benchmark (207,972 samples), all 17 evaluated LLMs perform near-random on pure causal inference. The best off-the-shelf model, BART MNLI, achieves only 33.38% F1, while GPT-4 reaches 29.08% F1 and GPT-3.5 reaches 21.69% F1 — barely above the random-uniform baseline of 20.38% F1. After finetuning, RoBERTa-Large MNLI jumps to 94.74% F1 on the in-distribution test set, but drops to 67.87% F1 after variable refactorization, revealing that the learned skill is not robust. The paper's central finding is that no existing LLM possesses reliable pure causal inference ability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's key finding is that no LLM family dominates: BART MNLI (F1=33.38) outperforms GPT-4 (29.08), GPT-3 Davinci (27.82), Alpaca-7B (27.37), and LLaMA-7B (26.81) by 4–10 F1 points, showing that a smaller BERT-based NLI model beats the largest autoregressive LLMs on this task. Among GPT-based models, GPT-4 leads (29.08) but is followed closely by GPT-3 Davinci (27.82) and GPT-3 Babbage (27.45), while the instruction-tuned variants (text-davinci-001/002/003) perform worse (15.72–21.87), suggesting instruction tuning does not help causal reasoning. GPT-3.5 (21.69) and RoBERTa MNLI (22.79) sit in the middle, while BERT MNLI (2.82) and GPT-3 Ada (0.00) are effectively at chance or below. All models remain within ~13 F1 points of the random-uniform baseline (20.38), confirming that none possess genuine causal inference skill. After finetuning, RoBERTa-Large MNLI achieves 94.74% F1 in-distribution but drops to 67.87% after variable refactorization, while GPT-3 Davinci finetuned reaches 85.52% F1 — yet all finetuned models lose 25–62 F1 points under perturbation, demonstrating the acquired skill is superficial rather than robust.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs sharply from the dominant line of causal NLP research, which frames causality as empirical commonsense knowledge (e.g., ATOMIC, SocialIQA, WikiHow) and evaluates LLMs as knowledge bases. Instead, it draws on the formal causal discovery framework—specifically the PC algorithm (Spirtes et al., 2000) and d-separation (Pearl, 1988)—to construct a task where the model must apply abstract logical rules to deduce causal structure from purely statistical correlations, with no domain knowledge required. The authors explicitly position against the 'causal parrots' critique (Zečević et al., 2023), arguing that prior work conflates memorized causal facts with genuine causal reasoning ability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all 17 tested LLMs—including GPT-4—perform at or near random on pure causal inference exposes a fundamental gap between LLMs' pattern-matching strengths and their ability to execute formal deductive procedures. The robustness analysis (paraphrasing and variable refactorization) further reveals that even finetuned models achieving 94%+ F1 are exploiting spurious surface correlations rather than learning transferable reasoning rules, dropping to ~55–67% F1 under minimal perturbation. Open questions include whether larger or differently-architected models can close this gap, whether the closed-system assumption (no hidden confounders) is too restrictive, and how to connect this symbolic benchmark to natural-language causal reasoning in real-world narratives.
