## Problem

Existing observation-space adversarial attacks on reinforcement learning agents are effective at degrading victim performance but are readily detectable by both automated out-of-distribution detectors and human inspection, making them impractical for real-world adversaries who must avoid triggering security escalations. This paper identifies the missing information-theoretic detectability constraint and formalizes attacks that are simultaneously effective and statistically undetectable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240259/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The adversary policy ν takes as input the true environment state s_t, the victim's full action-observation history (o_{<t}, a_{<t}), and the known state-transition function p, and outputs a perturbed observation o_t to feed the victim. The adversary's state space is effectively infinite because it must track the victim's entire history, turning the victim's decision process into a POMDP. The output observations are constrained so that the KL divergence between the attacked and unattacked observation trajectory distributions remains below a threshold ε, while individual per-step perturbations are additionally bounded by an L2 budget B.

## Method

The paper introduces the ε-illusory attack objective, which minimizes the victim's expected return subject to KL(ρ_v(·)‖ρ_v(·,ν)) ≤ ε, where ρ_v denotes the trajectory distribution over the victim's observations. To solve this constrained optimization, a dual-ascent algorithm alternates between (i) updating the adversary policy ν to minimize victim reward minus a λ-weighted KL penalty, and (ii) updating the dual variable λ to enforce the constraint. The KL objective is estimated via a scalable Monte-Carlo estimator derived from Jensen's inequality, avoiding the intractable nested expectation over full trajectory densities. The adversary is trained end-to-end with SAC (Soft Actor-Critic) using a single-sample constraint approximation pulled into the reward, while the victim is pre-trained with PPO and frozen. For perfect illusory attacks (ε=0), a closed-form construction exploits environment symmetries to generate observations that are statistically indistinguishable from unattacked dynamics while still fooling the victim.

## Results

Across CartPole, Pendulum, Hopper, and HalfCheetah (B=0.2), ε-illusory attacks achieve detection rates of 0–34% compared to ~100% for SA-MDP and MNP attacks, while maintaining comparable raw adversary scores (reduction in victim reward). After adjusting for detection (setting adversary return to zero on detected episodes), ε-illusory attacks yield the highest detection-adjusted adversary scores across all four environments, whereas SA-MDP and MNP scores drop to near zero. For robustification, randomized smoothing and ATLA provide only 15% and 8% average performance improvement at B=0.2, confirming their ineffectiveness against large-budget attacks. In a human study (n=10), participants classified ε-illusory attacked trajectories as suspicious at a rate statistically indistinguishable from unattacked trajectories (32.4% vs. ~33%), while detecting SA-MDP and MNP attacks with >80% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ε-illusory attacks against two state-of-the-art observation-space adversarial attacks: SA-MDP (Zhang et al., 2021a) and MNP (Kumar et al., 2021). On raw adversary score (reduction in victim reward), SA-MDP and MNP attacks achieve higher unadjusted scores in most environments (e.g., SA-MDP reduces Pendulum reward to −1387 vs. −980 for ε-illusory at B=0.2). However, the critical distinction is detectability: the state-of-the-art OOD detector (Haider et al., 2023) with a CUSUM rule flags SA-MDP and MNP attacks at ~100% detection rate, while ε-illusory attacks are detected at only 0–34% (and perfect illusory attacks at ~3%, matching the 3% false-positive baseline). After adjusting for detection (zeroing adversary return on detected episodes), ε-illusory attacks achieve the highest detection-adjusted scores across all four environments, while SA-MDP and MNP scores collapse to near zero. In the human study, participants detected SA-MDP and MNP attacks with >80% accuracy but could not distinguish ε-illusory attacks from unattacked trajectories (z-test, α=0.05, hypothesis not rejected). Thus, ε-illusory attacks win decisively on the detection-adjusted metric that reflects real-world security outcomes, while losing on raw attack strength alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack literature on RL agents (SA-MDP, MNP) by introducing an information-theoretic detectability constraint grounded in steganography (Cachin, 1998) and sequential hypothesis testing (Neyman-Pearson, Wald). The closest prior work, Russo & Proutiere (2022), studies information-theoretic detectability but only for action-space attacks on low-dimensional stochastic control systems; here the authors generalize to high-dimensional observation-space attacks and evaluate against learned OOD detectors and human observers. The dual-ascent algorithm for solving the constrained optimization draws on constrained RL (RCPO) and convex optimization, while the KL-estimation strategy uses a Jensen-inequality-based Monte Carlo bound to avoid the intractable nested density estimation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that state-of-the-art observation-space attacks are trivially detectable by both automated OOD detectors (~100% detection) and human inspection, rendering them ineffective in realistic threat models where detection triggers contingency actions. This reframes the adversarial attack design problem: effectiveness without stealth is insufficient. Key limitations include evaluation only in four simple simulated control environments, reliance on the adversary knowing the environment transition function, and a small human study (n=10). Open questions include characterizing Nash equilibria of the resulting infinite-state game, extending to real-world cyber-physical systems, and developing better anomaly detectors for sequential decision-making agents.
