## Problem

LLMs frequently generate self-contradictory hallucinations—two logically inconsistent sentences about the same subject within a single response—yet existing detection methods either require access to model internals, demand tens of sampled outputs, or depend on external knowledge retrieval that fails for a substantial fraction of contradictions (e.g., 35.2% for ChatGPT are unverifiable online). This paper asks whether self-contradiction can be leveraged as a self-contained signal of non-factuality that is both detectable and mitigable through logical reasoning alone, without any external knowledge source.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a gLM-generated text (a sequence of sentences about an entity) and, for each sentence, constructs a context tuple of (entity name, preceding sentences, and a relation triple (s, r, o) extracted by CompactIE with the object o deliberately omitted). The analyzer LM receives this context plus the original and a newly generated sentence, and outputs a free-form explanation followed by a binary Yes/No contradiction verdict. Mitigation produces a locally revised sentence that strips the conflicting information while retaining all non-conflicting facts and maintaining coherence with the prefix.

## Method

The framework is a three-step prompting pipeline operating entirely on black-box LMs. (1) Trigger: for each sentence x_i, a cloze-style prompt feeds the entity, prefix, and the subject+relation of the extracted triple (omitting the object) to gLM, which generates an alternative sentence x_i' constrained to the same scope but free to contradict. (2) Detect: a zero-shot chain-of-thought prompt asks aLM to first write a free-form explanation of whether x_i and x_i' are contradictory within context c, then conclude with a single Yes/No token, enabling both interpretability and easy parsing. (3) Mitigate: an iterative procedure (Algorithm 3, typically 3 rounds) calls aLM.revise on each detected contradiction, instructing it to remove only the conflicting information while preserving fluency and informativeness; sentences without detected contradictions are left untouched. The key insight underpinning the entire approach is that two contradictory sentences cannot both be true, so removing the conflict strictly reduces non-factuality—making logical reasoning sufficient for both detection and correction without any retrieval or model-internal access.

## Results

On the MainTestSet (360 descriptions across 4 LMs and 30 entities), self-contradictions are triggered in 15.7%–22.9% of all sentences depending on the generator LM, with 35.2% of ChatGPT's contradictions unverifiable via online text. Using ChatGPT as the analyzer LM, detection achieves 83.7% F1 (84.2% precision, 83.2% recall) on ChatGPT-generated text, and mitigation removes 89.5% of self-contradictions while retaining 100.8% of informative facts with only a 0.44 perplexity increase. On PopQA question answering, the method detects 12.7%–38.0% self-contradictions with 74.2%–83.8% precision in both vanilla and retrieval-augmented settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary external comparison is against SelfCheckGPT (Manakul et al., 2023b), which uses three black-box hallucination detection methods (unigram probabilities, BertScore, and MQAG) requiring 20 alternative samples. The authors report that their prompting-based detection substantially outperforms all three SelfCheckGPT variants in both their sentence-pair contradiction setting and SelfCheckGPT's own 20-sample non-factuality setting (Figure 6). Beyond this, the paper's internal ablations show that their cloze-style trigger prompt (17.7% self-contradictions triggered, 84.2% detection precision) significantly outperforms alternatives: Continue (5.1%, 76.8%), Rephrase (0.2%, 33.3%), and Q&A (10.3%, 81.9%). For detection prompting, their two-turn chain-of-thought approach (83.7% F1) beats direct Yes/No asking (73.1% F1), step-by-step reasoning (67.4% F1), and multi-path reasoning (82.0% F1) while being cheaper than multi-path. The key practical advantage over SelfCheckGPT is that the method requires only 2 sampled sentences per position rather than tens of samples, and additionally provides mitigation, which SelfCheckGPT does not address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out self-contradiction as a distinct, well-defined sub-problem of LLM hallucination detection, departing from prior sampling-based methods (SelfCheckGPT, Kuhn et al.) that require tens of samples and from retrieval-based approaches (Shuster et al., Peng et al., Min et al.) that depend on external knowledge. The key conceptual move is exploiting the logical guarantee that two contradictory sentences cannot both be factual, reducing the detection problem to a binary NLI-like judgment on just two sentences from the same LM, and extending the pipeline to include iterative mitigation (revision) which prior hallucination work does not address. The method is purely prompting-based (chain-of-thought for detection, zero-shot for revision), making it applicable to black-box proprietary LMs without access to internals, in contrast to Lee et al. (2022) which requires training/inference access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~18% of ChatGPT sentences contain self-contradictions and that 35% of these cannot be verified via online text challenges the assumption that retrieval-augmented generation can resolve most hallucinations, positioning self-contradiction detection as a necessary complement rather than a redundant check. The practical release of ChatProtect as a push-button tool lowers the barrier for non-experts to detect and fix hallucinations in real-time. Key limitations include handling only same-position contradictions (not cross-sentence), operating purely as post-processing, and not addressing the harder case of consistent hallucinations where the LM is confidently wrong in all samples. Open questions include whether fine-tuning smaller open-source LMs can match proprietary aLMs, and whether training LMs to avoid generating contradictory content is feasible.
