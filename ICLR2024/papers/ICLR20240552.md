## Problem

Image recognition and generation have been developed independently, and prior attempts to unify them (iGPT, ViT-VQGAN, MAGE) operate entirely in either raw-pixel space or VQ-token space, resulting in poor performance on one task or the other. Specifically, VQ-token-based methods are substantially inferior on dense recognition tasks (detection, segmentation) because pixels preserve spatially sensitive information better than discrete tokens, while pixel-based methods struggle to generate high-fidelity images. ADDP asks whether a single representation learning framework can exploit both spaces simultaneously.

## Input / Output

For generation, ADDP starts from a sequence of <MASK> token embeddings and iteratively produces a 256×256 RGB image over T denoising steps, using a pre-trained VQGAN tokenizer (16×16 spatial grid of 8192-entry codebook). For recognition, the learned ViT encoder takes full 256×256 RGB images as input and feeds task-specific heads (classification, Mask R-CNN for detection, UperNet for segmentation). During training, the encoder's input is a noisy synthetic image decoded from a mixture of reliable and unreliable VQ tokens, not a clean natural image, which creates a distribution gap that explains the model's weaker linear-probing performance relative to its fine-tuning results.

## Method

ADDP alternates between two operations at each denoising step: (1) token-to-pixel decoding, where an off-the-shelf VQ decoder reconstructs a noisy image from a combination of reliable tokens z_t and unreliable tokens z̄_t, and (2) pixel-to-token generation, where a learnable ViT encoder + 8-block Transformer decoder predicts the next step's VQ tokens conditioned on the decoded image. The corresponding forward diffusion process gradually masks VQ tokens, with unreliable tokens produced by a separate token predictor (MAGE-Large) conditioned on the current reliable tokens rather than the previous step's tokens. Training minimizes the ELBO of the alternating process, which reduces to a cross-entropy loss on masked positions using the token predictor's output as the target. A WeightedSum mapping (weighted average of all codebook embeddings by predicted probability) is applied to unreliable tokens before VQ decoding to improve synthetic image quality. The key architectural distinction from MAGE is that the encoder consumes raw pixels rather than VQ tokens, which is the critical factor enabling competitive dense recognition performance while retaining strong generation quality.

## Results

On unconditional ImageNet 256×256 generation, ADDP achieves FID 7.6 and IS 105.1, surpassing the previous best unified method MAGE (FID 9.1) by 1.5 FID and outperforming generation-only methods like MaskGIT (FID 20.7) and IC-GAN (FID 15.6). For recognition, ADDP (ViT-L) reaches 85.9% ImageNet-1k fine-tuning accuracy (matching MAE), 54.6 AP^box on COCO detection, and 54.3 mIoU on ADE20k segmentation — competitive with recognition-only methods like MAE (55.6 AP^box, 53.6 mIoU) and BEiT (53.3 AP^box, 53.3 mIoU). Notably, ADDP is the first method to achieve strong performance on both generation and dense recognition tasks simultaneously, with a 23.2 AP^box improvement over MAGE on COCO detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADDP decisively outperforms MAGE (the previous SoTA for unified recognition+generation) across all metrics: FID improves from 9.1 to 7.6, ImageNet fine-tuning from 83.9% to 85.9%, and COCO AP^box jumps from 31.4 to 54.6 — a 23-point gap that the authors attribute to using raw pixels as inputs rather than VQ tokens. Against recognition-only methods, ADDP ties MAE on ImageNet fine-tuning (85.9% vs 85.9%) and slightly trails on COCO AP^box (54.6 vs 55.6) but matches or exceeds them on ADE20k segmentation (54.3 vs 53.6 for MAE, 54.3 vs 53.3 for BEiT). On generation, ADDP's FID of 7.6 substantially beats all generation-only baselines: IC-GAN (15.6), MaskGIT (20.7), ADM (26.2), and BigGAN (38.6). The main weakness is linear probing accuracy (23.8%), which is far below recognition-only methods (75–81%); the authors attribute this to the gap between natural images and the noisy synthetic images used as training inputs, and note that linear probing and fine-tuning are largely uncorrelated in prior work. For the smaller ViT-B backbone (Table 7), ADDP similarly beats MAGE by 2.1 FID (8.9 vs 11.0) and 15.4 AP^box on COCO (51.7 vs 36.3), while matching or exceeding recognition-only methods like MAE (51.6 AP^box) and SiameseIM (52.1 AP^box).

## Relation to Prior Work

ADDP extends the line of unified representation-and-generation methods (iGPT, ViT-VQGAN, MAGE) by introducing an alternating denoising process that bridges raw-pixel and VQ-token spaces, rather than operating entirely in one or the other. It builds directly on MAGE's MaskGIT-style iterative decoding and VQ tokenizer, and adopts the reparameterization trick from VQ-Diffusion to simplify the ELBO into a cross-entropy objective. The key conceptual departure is the observation that pixels are essential for recognition (especially dense tasks) while VQ tokens are beneficial for generation, motivating the alternating pixel↔token denoising loop as a single unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADDP is the first method to demonstrate that a single pre-trained representation can serve both high-quality image generation and dense recognition tasks (detection, segmentation), closing a gap that prior unified methods (limited to classification) left open. The large margin over MAGE on dense tasks (e.g., 54.6 vs 31.4 AP on COCO) validates the pixel-input hypothesis. Limitations include reliance on a frozen pre-trained VQ tokenizer (constraining generation diversity), a notable linear-probing gap attributed to the distribution shift between natural and noisy synthetic training inputs, and the current restriction to 256×256 resolution. Open directions include integrating continuous diffusion processes and scaling to higher resolutions.
