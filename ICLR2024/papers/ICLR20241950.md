## Problem

ZeRO-3, the dominant memory-efficient data-parallel training strategy, requires 3M cross-node communication volume per iteration (two weight all-gathers and one gradient reduce-scatter), which becomes the throughput bottleneck on low-bandwidth clusters or when per-GPU batch sizes are small. No prior work had systematically reduced this communication volume while preserving training convergence at extreme quantization levels (down to 2-bits).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241950/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes FP16 model weights partitioned across all GPUs (ZeRO-3 primary partition), training data micro-batches, and optimizer states, and produces a trained model whose weights are naturally quantized to the chosen precision (8/6/4/2-bits) as a byproduct of the communication protocol. Unlike post-training quantization, the local weight shards remain in FP16 and are only quantized transiently during all-gather, so the final model is inference-ready without any separate quantization step. The method also reduces per-device memory for frozen/untrainable weights, enabling larger batch sizes or fewer GPUs for the same model.

## Method

ZeRO++ composes three communication-reduction techniques that together cut cross-node traffic from 3M to 0.75M per iteration. (1) qwZ applies block-based quantization (e.g., FP16→INT8/INT6) during the forward all-gather, with custom fused CUDA kernels that overlap quantization with communication to hide overhead. (2) hpZ maintains a secondary FP16 weight partition within each compute node, replacing the expensive cross-node backward all-gather with a fast intra-node all-gather (reducing that volume to 0 cross-node). (3) qgZ replaces the ring-based reduce-scatter with a hierarchical 2-hop all-to-all: intra-node all-to-all on quantized gradients, dequantization and reduction locally, then inter-node all-to-all on the reduced data, combined with a tensor-slice reordering scheme to correct gradient placement. The three components compose multiplicatively, yielding up to 2.4× throughput on 384 GPUs and 3.3× speedup for RLHF training, while maintaining on-par convergence with standard ZeRO-3.

## Results

ZeRO++ reduces cross-node communication volume by 4× (from 3M to 0.75M per iteration) and achieves up to 2.16× throughput speedup over ZeRO-3 on a 384-GPU low-bandwidth cluster (1 IB connection), reaching 45% sustained peak throughput. On high-bandwidth clusters (8 IB), the speedup is more modest at up to 1.3×. For RLHF training, ZeRO++ delivers up to 3.3× throughput improvement over ZeRO-3. Convergence is preserved: pretraining GPT-13B with 8-bit/6-bit quantized all-gather matches ZeRO-3 validation loss, and fine-tuning OPT-30B/LLaMA-30B with 4-bit (even 2-bit) quantization shows only 0.27–0.41% perplexity deviation from the FP16 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ZeRO-3 (the primary baseline), ZeRO++ wins across every tested configuration: on low-bandwidth clusters (1 IB, 100 Gbps) it achieves 79–116% throughput speedup (up to 2.16×) for models from 18B to 138B, and on high-bandwidth clusters (8 IB, 800 Gbps) it still delivers 13–30% speedup. For RLHF training specifically, ZeRO++ reaches 3.3× throughput over ZeRO-3 on OPT-30B and 2.97× on LLaMA-2-70B. In terms of convergence, ZeRO++ matches ZeRO-3 validation loss for GPT-13B pretraining at 8-bit and 6-bit quantization, and fine-tuning perplexity deviates by only 0.27–0.41% at 4-bit (and remains usable at 2-bit). Against MiCS (Zhang et al., 2022c), the hpZ component achieves comparable or slightly higher per-GPU throughput where both methods fit in memory (e.g., 52.42 vs. 52.72 TFLOPs for 18B/2K tokens), but critically, MiCS runs out of memory at 18B model size on a 4-node cluster while hpZ succeeds, because hpZ replicates only weights rather than all model states in the secondary partition.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeRO++ is a direct communication-optimization extension of ZeRO-3 (Rajbhandari et al., 2020), which already established the all-gather/reduce-scatter communication pattern for memory-efficient data parallelism. For quantized weight communication (qwZ), it adopts block-based quantization from Dettmers et al. (2022) and Yao et al. (2022), but applies it to the all-gather path rather than optimizer states, and implements custom CUDA kernels since no high-performance block-quantization kernels existed. The hierarchical weight partition (hpZ) is conceptually related to MiCS and HSDP, which also exploit intra-node bandwidth, but ZeRO++ replicates only weights in the secondary partition rather than all model states, avoiding the massive memory overhead that causes MiCS to OOM at 18B scale. The gradient communication (qgZ) departs from both ring-based reduce-scatter and prior gradient compression methods (1-bit SGD, etc.) by using a novel 2-hop all-to-all with tensor slice reordering, sidestepping the sequential quantization error accumulation and optimizer-state assumptions that make prior approaches infeasible under ZeRO-3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4x cross-node communication reduction (3M → 0.75M) makes large-model training viable on low-bandwidth clusters, effectively democratizing access to efficient training by reducing the minimum bandwidth requirement by 4x. The automatic weight quantization byproduct—where the trained model is naturally in low-precision (down to 2-bit for finetuning) without post-training quantization—is a practical simplification that eliminates a separate quantization step. Limitations include the 8.9x memory overhead of hpZ relative to ZeRO-3, convergence verification only up to 13B for pretraining and 30B for finetuning, and the 2-bit result showing a still-measurable perplexity gap (1.544 vs 1.490 on LLaMA-30B). Open questions include whether error-feedback techniques can push pretraining to 2-bit, and whether the approach generalizes to non-Transformer architectures or heterogeneous cluster topologies.
