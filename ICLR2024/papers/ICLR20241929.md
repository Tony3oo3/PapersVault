## Problem

While GNNs are known to be vulnerable to adversarial attacks, most existing defenses target structural perturbations with heuristic strategies lacking theoretical guarantees, and the theoretical robustness of GNNs to node feature-based attacks remains largely unexplored. This paper addresses this gap by formally defining 'expected adversarial robustness' for graph functions, deriving upper bounds on the robustness of GCNs and GINs to both feature and structural attacks, and proposing a principled defense grounded in these bounds.

## Input / Output

The model takes a graph adjacency matrix (normalized as D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}) and a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×K} as input, with features normalized to a continuous space to enable flexible feature-based attacks. Beyond standard node class labels (primary task) and graph class labels (secondary task), the framework also produces an empirical estimate of the expected vulnerability Adv_ε^{α,β}[f] via stratified sampling of perturbations from a ball of radius ε, providing an attack-independent, model-agnostic robustness metric. The robustness estimation requires specifying a budget ε, a threshold σ for output deviation, and a number of Monte Carlo samples L_max per input graph.

## Method

GCORN modifies the standard GCN layer by projecting each weight matrix W to its closest orthonormal matrix Ŵ via the Björck iterative algorithm (a Taylor-expansion-based fixed-point iteration) during every forward pass, making the orthonormalization differentiable and compatible with backpropagation. The key insight is that the derived upper bound on expected robustness (Theorem 4.1) is proportional to the product of weight matrix norms ∏‖W^{(i)}‖, so enforcing orthonormality (‖Ŵ‖=1) directly minimizes this bound without requiring additional regularization hyperparameters. A spectral-norm-based scaling factor is applied before the iterative projection to guarantee convergence (‖W^T W − I‖ ≤ 1). The paper further introduces a probabilistic evaluation method that estimates the expected vulnerability by stratified sampling: first drawing a radius r from a Beta-like distribution p_ε(r) = K(r/ε)^{K−1}/ε, then uniformly sampling a perturbation Z from the sphere S_r, yielding an unbiased Monte Carlo estimator of Adv_ε^{α,β}[f] that is both attack-independent and model-agnostic.

## Results

On node classification under feature-based attacks, GCORN achieves the highest attacked accuracy across all five datasets and four attack types. For example, under PGD on Cora, GCORN reaches 71.1% accuracy versus 54.1% for vanilla GCN, 68.6% for ParsevalR, and 68.2% for AIRGNN. Under the targeted Nettack attack on CiteSeer, GCORN achieves 77.5% versus 71.7% for GCN-k and 70.8% for RGCN. The paper reports that GCORN outperforms GCN-k by an average of ~12% in accuracy across feature-based attack settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCORN outperforms all eight baselines in the majority of feature-based attack settings (Table 1). Against GCN-k (Seddik et al., 2022), GCORN gains an average of ~12% accuracy, with the largest margin on CiteSeer under Nettack (77.5% vs. 71.7%). Against AIRGNN (Liu et al., 2021), GCORN wins on 18 of 20 feature-attack settings, with the clearest advantage under PGD on Cora (71.1% vs. 68.2%). Against RGCN (Zhu et al., 2019), GCORN is consistently superior, e.g., 72.3% vs. 69.5% on PubMed under PGD. Against ParsevalR (Cisse et al., 2017), GCORN edges ahead in most settings (e.g., 71.1% vs. 68.6% on Cora/PGD), suggesting the iterative Björck orthonormalization is more effective than Parseval regularization for GNNs. For structural attacks (Table 2), GCORN outperforms GCN-Jaccard, GNN-SVD, and GNN-Guard in 8 of 12 experiments, though GNN-SVD and GNN-Guard occasionally match or exceed GCORN on PubMed (e.g., GNN-Guard 84.8% vs. GCORN 71.8% under Mettack). GCORN also achieves the best certified robustness under sparse randomized smoothing on Cora, improving certified accuracy over vanilla GCN across all radii.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Parseval/orthonormality regularization line from DNNs (Cissé et al., 2017; Ablin & Peyré, 2022) to the GNN setting, repurposing the Björck iterative orthonormalization algorithm (Björck & Bowie, 1971) to project GCN weight matrices during training. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant worst-case adversarial robustness framework in the GNN literature (Zügner & Günnemann, 2019; Bojchevski et al., 2020) by introducing a probabilistic 'expected adversarial robustness' formulation that treats robustness as a distributional property over the input neighborhood rather than a single worst-case perturbation. The theoretical upper bounds build on sensitivity/Lipschitz analysis for message-passing architectures (Anil et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2021) but are novel in incorporating graph-structural quantities (normalized walks) into the bound, and in addressing the under-explored feature-attack setting as opposed to the structural-attack focus of most prior GNN defense work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable gap by providing the first theoretical upper bounds on GCN/GIN robustness to node feature attacks, and by offering a model-agnostic probabilistic evaluation metric that complements (rather than replaces) worst-case accuracy. The GCORN method is practically attractive because it is differentiable, attack-agnostic, and adds minimal overhead (O(e³) per layer, independent of graph size). However, the bounds are loose upper bounds whose tightness is not characterized, the analysis is primarily for GCNs with 1-Lipschitz activations, and the expressiveness cost of enforcing orthonormality on all layers is not deeply explored. Open questions include whether the expected-robustness framework yields tighter or more actionable guarantees for deeper/heterophilic GNNs, and whether the probabilistic evaluation can be made computationally feasible for very large graphs.
