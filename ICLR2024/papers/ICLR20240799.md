## Problem

Sampling-based backpropagation acceleration methods can skip 'unimportant' data to reduce computation, but they introduce uncontrolled variance that degrades convergence and requires task-specific tuning of sample ratios. Existing approaches (loss-based, gradient-norm-based) suffer from a 'no-free-lunch' tradeoff where aggressive sampling sacrifices robustness, and the optimal sample ratio varies across tasks and training phases, making it hard to set in advance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard training batch of shape N×T×K (batch size × tokens per datum × feature dimensionality) and model parameters θ, along with variance tolerance thresholds τ_act and τ_w that bound the additional sampling variance as a fraction of the original stochastic gradient variance. It produces an unbiased approximated stochastic gradient (ASG) computed via partial backpropagation, updated model parameters, and layerwise sample ratios {ρ_l} and {ν_l} that are themselves learned as part of the training process. The adaptation overhead requires M Monte-Carlo repetitions of exact and sampled backpropagation every F SGD iterations, with M=2 and F set to roughly 1/50 of total training steps.

## Method

VCAS constructs the ASG through two complementary samplers applied at different granularities: SampleA filters data in the batch dimension for activation gradient computation using importance sampling proportional to per-datum gradient norm, while SampleW filters (data, token) pairs in the token dimension for weight gradient computation using leverage score sampling. The sampling is progressive across layers—more samples are dropped as backpropagation proceeds from the topmost to the bottommost layer, exploiting the increasing sparsity of gradients in deeper layers. To preserve accuracy, VCAS jointly learns the sample ratios with model parameters via a zeroth-order adaptation algorithm: a single hyperparameter s controls all activation sample ratios {ρ_l} through a gradient-norm sparsity schedule, while per-layer weight ratios {ν_l} are updated multiplicatively, both targeting a preset variance budget (τ_act·Var[SG] and τ_w·Var[SG] per layer). This variance-controlled framework ensures the ASG mirrors the exact training trajectory, achieving up to 73.87% BP FLOPs reduction and 49.58% total training FLOPs reduction while preserving validation accuracy across BERT, ViT, and CNN tasks.

## Results

VCAS preserves the original training loss trajectory and validation accuracy across all tasks while reducing backpropagation FLOPs by up to 73.87% and total training FLOPs by up to 49.58%. On BERT-base finetuning, VCAS achieves 41.56–48.28% FLOPs reduction with eval accuracy within 0.1% of exact training (e.g., 84.23% vs 84.33% on MNLI, 93.04% vs 92.59% on SST-2). On ViT-large finetuning on ImageNet-1k, VCAS reaches 49.58% FLOPs reduction with 82.27% accuracy (vs 82.04% exact). Wall-clock time reduction is 19–21% on BERT-large/MNLI and ViT-large/ImageNet-1k with a single NVIDIA 3090Ti.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCAS is compared against two online BP-sampling baselines: SB (Selective Backprop, Johnson & Guestrin 2018, a loss-based method) and UB (Upper Bound, Katharopoulos & Fleuret 2018, a gradient-norm-based method), both fixed at 44.44% FLOPs reduction. VCAS consistently outperforms both on final train loss and eval accuracy across all 14 task-dataset combinations. SB degrades most severely: on BERT-base/MNLI its train loss jumps from 0.2372 (exact) to 0.3833, and on ViT-base/CIFAR-100 it reaches 2.248 vs 0.8760 exact. UB is closer but still lags VCAS, e.g., 0.2957 vs 0.2428 train loss on BERT-base/MNLI and 90.51% vs 91.57% accuracy on BERT-large/QQP. VCAS matches or exceeds exact accuracy on several tasks (e.g., 93.04% vs 92.59% on BERT-base/SST-2, 82.27% vs 82.04% on ViT-large/ImageNet-1k) while achieving 41–50% FLOPs reduction, whereas SB and UB at the same FLOPs budget show 0.5–1.5% accuracy drops. The wall-clock experiments confirm VCAS translates FLOPs savings into comparable real-time speedups (19–21%) as the simpler baselines (18–22%), validating that the fine-grained sampling overhead does not erode practical gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

VCAS builds directly on the gradient-norm-based importance sampling framework of Katharopoulos & Fleuret (2018) for activation gradient computation, and borrows the leverage score sampling distribution from randomized numerical linear algebra (Drineas & Mahoney, 2018) for weight gradient computation. It departs from prior online batch selection methods (SB, UB) in two key ways: (1) it constructs the approximated gradient in a fine-grained, progressive manner—dropping more samples at lower layers and further subsampling tokens within retained samples—rather than making a single coarse-grained keep/drop decision; and (2) it introduces an explicit variance-control loop that adaptively learns layerwise sample ratios during training, whereas prior methods rely on fixed, manually tuned ratios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VCAS offers a theoretically grounded, drop-in acceleration for backpropagation that preserves the exact training trajectory with up to ~50% FLOPs reduction, which is practically significant for large-scale pretraining and fine-tuning pipelines. However, the wall-clock speedup (~19–21%) is substantially smaller than the FLOPs reduction due to overhead from the variance-estimation loop and the fact that only the backward pass is accelerated. The weight sampler is limited to linear layers (not convolutions), and the method explicitly aims to mirror rather than improve upon the exact training path, leaving open whether variance-controlled sampling could be combined with other efficiency techniques or used to explore alternative, potentially better, convergence trajectories.
