## Problem

LoRA's design requires all examples in a minibatch to share the same adapter weights, making it incompatible with real-world serving scenarios where each incoming request may need a distinct task- or user-specific adapter. Processing heterogeneous requests either forces adapter sharing (degrading quality) or sequential execution (destroying throughput), creating a fundamental bottleneck for personalized, multi-adapter deployment of foundation models.

## Input / Output

Each input example carries its own pair of low-rank adapter matrices (B_i ∈ R^{d×r}, A_i ∈ R^{r×k}) alongside the standard text prompt or audio clip. The forward pass consumes these per-example adapters via element-wise multiplication with the frozen pre-trained weights, producing generated code (pass@1 evaluated on MultiPL-E) or transcribed text (WER evaluated on Common Voice). The rank r is typically 1 for most languages, with rank 4 used for a few low-resource cases, keeping the per-adapter parameter footprint at roughly 0.03% of the base model.

## Method

FLORA replaces LoRA's additive weight update (W_0 + BA) with an element-wise multiplicative formulation: W_i = W_0 ∘ (B_i A_i), where the low-rank factorization is applied via broadcasting. The key algebraic insight is that the forward pass decomposes as y_i = φ(A_i ∘ (W_0^T (B_i ∘ x_i))), where the per-example adapters enter through element-wise multiplications that are inherently batch-friendly, avoiding the expensive batch matrix multiplication (BMM) required by a naive multi-adapter LoRA implementation. Vectorizing across the minibatch yields Y = φ(A ∘ ((B ∘ X)W_0)), a single standard matrix multiplication plus element-wise operations whose cost is invariant to the number of distinct adapters in the batch. The paper derives a closed-form inequality (2c₁/(dc₂) + 1/r ≥ 1) showing FLORA is cheaper than BMM-LoRA, with the advantage most pronounced at rank 1 and for models with smaller hidden dimensions. FLORA retains the same expressive power as LoRA by construction, and IA3 is identified as a rank-0.5 special case that only rescales columns rather than the full weight matrix.

## Results

On the MultiPL-E code generation benchmark (StarCoder 15B), FLORA and LoRA achieve roughly 20% average relative improvement in pass@1 over the base model across six low-resource languages, with FLORA matching LoRA within ~1% on most languages (e.g., Dlang: FLORA 17.26 vs LoRA 17.15; Perl: FLORA 21.44 vs LoRA 21.46). On multilingual speech recognition (Whisper 1.5B, Common Voice), FLORA reduces WER from 46.03 to 30.21 on Arabic and from 115.20 to 42.12 on Mongolian, matching LoRA (30.18 and 41.57 respectively). In serving experiments on StarCoder 15B, FLORA delivers >3× throughput over batched-LoRA at rank 1 and a 2–5× latency reduction at ranks 1–4, with the advantage persisting up to rank 8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (the primary baseline), FLORA matches accuracy within ~1% pass@1 on most code-generation languages (e.g., Dlang 17.26 vs 17.15; Swift 20.38 vs 20.35) and within ~1 WER point on speech recognition (e.g., Arabic 30.21 vs 30.18; Czech 10.76 vs 10.77), while delivering 2–5× lower latency and >3× higher throughput at rank 1 in vLLM serving. Against IA3, FLORA consistently wins on every language in both tasks: on StarCoder 15B code generation, FLORA's average relative improvement (~20%) far exceeds IA3's (~7%), and on speech recognition FLORA achieves lower WER than IA3 in 5 of 6 languages (e.g., Marathi 33.17 vs 35.03; Mongolian 42.12 vs 46.47). Against the un-fine-tuned Whisper base model, all three PEFT methods reduce WER by 30–60% across low-resource languages, confirming that the adaptation mechanism itself is effective. The one area where LoRA edges out FLORA is on the smaller StarCoderBase 3B model, where LoRA achieves slightly higher pass@1 on Dlang (6.97 vs 5.72) and Perl (13.31 vs 13.01), though the gap narrows on larger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLORA directly extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) by replacing the additive weight update (W₀ + BA) with an element-wise multiplicative formulation (W₀ ∘ BA), which is the key architectural change that makes per-example adapters batch-compatible. The paper also positions IA3 (Liu et al., 2022a) as a rank-0.5 special case of FLORA, unifying the two under a single framework. On the serving side, the work builds on the continuous-batching paradigm from vLLM/Orca, identifying LoRA's shared-adapter constraint as a specific bottleneck within that infrastructure and providing a theoretical cost model (involving the BMM-to-GEMM ratio c₁/c₂) that predicts when FLORA is cheaper.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone deploying LoRA-adapted models to diverse user populations: a 2–3× throughput gain and halved latency at low ranks (r ≤ 4) on a 15B model is a substantial serving-cost reduction. The limitation that FLORA still requires per-adapter fine-tuning (rather than being derivable from existing LoRA weights) is acknowledged as the main open question, and the advantage's dependence on rank, hidden dimension, and architecture (the c₁/c₂ ratio) means practitioners must profile their specific stack. The connection to quantization—where reducing effective model size extends FLORA's rank advantage—suggests the technique becomes more relevant as 4-bit/8-bit inference becomes standard.
