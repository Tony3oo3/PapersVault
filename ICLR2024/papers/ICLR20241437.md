## Problem

Neural Network Potentials (NNPs) trained on static conformational datasets suffer from distribution shift during iterative energy minimization, because the conformations encountered along optimization trajectories are underrepresented in training data, leading to inaccurate force predictions and suboptimal or divergent optimizations. While enriching training data with full DFT optimization trajectories resolves this, it requires ~5×10⁵ expensive quantum-chemistry evaluations. The paper asks whether a data-efficient strategy can match oracle-level optimization quality with far fewer additional oracle interactions.

## Input / Output

The NNP (PaiNN) takes a molecular conformation specified by atomic numbers z ∈ ℕⁿ and 3D Cartesian coordinates X ∈ ℝⁿˣ³, and outputs a scalar potential energy and per-atom interatomic forces F = −∂f/∂xᵢ. During the GOLF training loop, the surrogate oracle (RDKit MMFF) evaluates MMFF energies at each step of the NNP-generated trajectory to flag the conformation where the NNP's predicted forces are unreliable; only that flagged conformation is sent to the expensive DFT oracle (Psi4, ωB97X-D/def2-SVP) for ground-truth energy and force labels. The final output is a low-energy conformation after T=100 L-BFGS optimization steps using NNP gradients as forces.

## Method

GOLF is an active-learning-inspired framework that couples an external optimizer (L-BFGS) with a selective data-collection scheme to train NNPs for conformational energy minimization. At each iteration, the current NNP generates an optimization trajectory; a cheap surrogate oracle (MMFF, ~120 µs per evaluation) monitors the trajectory and identifies the first conformation sₜ where the MMFF energy increases relative to sₜ₋₁, indicating the NNP predicted incorrect forces at sₜ. Only that single conformation is evaluated with the expensive DFT oracle (Psi4, ~590 CPU-s) and added to the training set, after which the NNP is updated for U steps (U=50 for the 10k variant). This 'query only where the model is wrong' strategy, combined with a replay buffer that samples 10% of each mini-batch from the original dataset to prevent recency bias, allows f^GOLF-10k to match the DFT oracle's optimization quality (mean residual energy < 1 kcal/mol) using only 10,000 additional DFT interactions—a 50× reduction over the naive trajectory-enrichment baseline that requires 500,000.

## Results

On the nablaDFT test set (~19,477 conformations, ~10,273 molecules), GOLF-10k achieves 98.8% average energy minimization (pct_T), 0.5 kcal/mol residual energy, and 77.3% success rate (residual < 1 kcal/mol), matching the DFT oracle's optimization quality while using only 10,000 additional oracle interactions — 50× fewer than the 500,000 needed by the straightforward trajectory-enrichment baseline. GOLF-1k (just 1,000 additional interactions) already reaches 97.3% pct_T and 62.9% success rate, outperforming the 100k-trajectory baseline. On the SPICE dataset, GOLF-10k achieves 94.2% pct_T and 2.1 kcal/mol residual energy, comparable to the 220k-trajectory baseline. On large molecules (36–57 atoms), GOLF-10k reaches 97.7% pct_T and 1.0 kcal/mol residual energy, matching the 500k-trajectory model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOLF-10k decisively outperforms all generative baselines on every optimization metric: Torsional Diffusion (23.8% pct_T, 33.8 kcal/mol E_res, 0% success) and ConfOpt (39.1% pct_T, 27.9 kcal/mol E_res, 0.2% success) suffer from high divergence rates (61–71%) and fail to produce chemically accurate conformations, while Uni-Mol+ (54.6% pct_T, 18.6 kcal/mol E_res) is closer but still far below GOLF. Against the non-neural RDKit/MMFF baseline (85.5% pct_T, 5.5 kcal/mol E_res, 4.1% success), GOLF-10k improves pct_T by 13.3 points and success rate by 73 points. The key data-efficiency comparison is against the straightforward trajectory-enrichment approach: GOLF-10k (10k additional DFT calls) matches or exceeds f^traj-500k (500k calls) on pct_success (77.3% vs 73.4%) and COV (71.2% vs 67.0%), achieving a 50× reduction in expensive oracle interactions. Even GOLF-1k (1k calls) surpasses f^traj-100k (100k calls) on pct_T (97.3% vs 96.2%) and success rate (62.9% vs 52.7%), demonstrating a 100× data efficiency gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established paradigm of using Neural Network Potentials (NNPs) like PaiNN and SchNet to predict molecular energies and derive forces via gradients, replacing expensive DFT oracles during conformational optimization. It departs from prior work in two key ways: first, it identifies and quantifies the distribution shift that causes NNPs trained on static conformations to fail during iterative optimization, and second, it introduces GOLF, an active learning framework that uses a cheap surrogate oracle (MMFF) to selectively query the expensive DFT oracle only where the NNP's force predictions are unreliable. This contrasts with naive trajectory enrichment (requiring ~500k oracle calls), per-molecule ensemble-based active learning (Kulichenko et al., 2023), and generative approaches (ConfOpt, Torsional Diffusion, Uni-Mol+) that reframe optimization as conditional generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50x reduction in oracle interactions makes NNP-based conformational optimization practically viable for drug discovery pipelines where DFT computations are the bottleneck. The distribution shift finding provides a clear diagnostic for why well-performing energy predictors fail as force fields during optimization, guiding future NNP training strategies. However, the approach is limited to gas-phase drug-like molecules up to ~57 atoms, and the SPICE results show less dramatic gains, suggesting the method may be sensitive to molecular diversity and size. Open questions include whether GOLF extends to molecular dynamics simulations, solvated or protein-bound environments, and whether the surrogate oracle choice (MMFF) generalizes to other force fields or molecular classes.
