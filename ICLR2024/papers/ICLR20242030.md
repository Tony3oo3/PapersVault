## Problem

Natural language debate between LLMs suffers from information loss because the token sampling step compresses the model's full belief distribution over the entire vocabulary into a single token, discarding valuable uncertainty information. This loss is especially detrimental for smaller open-source models, which prior work shows fail to benefit from debate or self-refinement, unlike GPT-4. The paper asks whether LLMs can communicate more effectively by bypassing token sampling and exchanging information directly in the embedding space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (question, instructions, and prior-round responses) tokenized into embeddings, along with n LLM debater models that must share the same tokenizer vocabulary. At each autoregressive step, instead of emitting a single token, the model outputs a weighted-average embedding vector over the full vocabulary. The final debate answer is recovered by converting the embedding sequence back to natural language via nearest-neighbor search over the vocabulary, then selecting the response from the lowest-temperature debater. When debaters use different embedding-vocabulary mappings (e.g., LLaMA-65B vs. LLaMA2-70B), a per-vocabulary mapping table is used to compute the weighted average in the receiver's embedding space.

## Method

CIPHER replaces the token sampling step in autoregressive generation with a weighted average of all token embeddings, using the softmax probability distribution p^(t) as weights (Eq. 2), so the generated vector encodes the model's full belief across the vocabulary rather than a single sampled token. This embedding vector is fed directly back into the LLM as the next input, bypassing the tokenizer decode step entirely. The multi-agent debate protocol follows Du et al. (2023): agents produce initial responses, then iteratively refine by receiving other agents' CIPHER responses concatenated into the prompt embedding. No model weights are modified; the approach is purely a change in the generation and communication protocol. The paper draws an analogy to Expected SARSA in reinforcement learning, where computing expectations over all actions (here, all vocabulary tokens) outperforms sampling a single action. Temperature selection is critical: CIPHER benefits most from pairing a low-temperature agent (for interpretable, confident output) with a high-temperature agent (whose more uniform distribution conveys complementary uncertainty information), as confirmed by 2D contour analysis and ablation studies showing that CIPHER's gains concentrate at positions of high generation uncertainty.

## Results

CIPHER achieves consistent improvements of 1.0–5.0% over Natural Language Debate (NLD) across five reasoning tasks using LLaMA2-70B (e.g., 66.0% vs. 64.8% on GSM8K, 52.4% vs. 49.2% on MMLU Formal Logic, 85.0% vs. 81.1% on Arithmetic). The same trend holds for the smaller LLaMA-65B, with gains of 1.0–4.8% (e.g., 50.8% vs. 46.0% on Formal Logic, 33.0% vs. 30.4% on Arithmetic). Across additional open-source models (Falcon-40B-Instruct, MPT-30B, WizardMath-70B), CIPHER provides an additional 0.5–3.5% boost over NLD on GSM8K without any modification to model weights. Notably, CIPHER's deterministic embedding generation yields zero variance (±0.0) compared to the 0.5–3.0% variance observed in token-sampling baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIPHER is compared against two baselines: Natural Language Debate (NLD, Du et al. 2023) and Self-Consistency / Major@5 (Wang et al. 2023b). CIPHER consistently outperforms NLD by 1.0–5.0% on LLaMA2-70B and 1.0–4.8% on LLaMA-65B across all five datasets, with the largest gap on MMLU Formal Logic (52.4% vs. 49.2% for LLaMA2-70B; 50.8% vs. 46.0% for LLaMA-65B) and Arithmetic (85.0% vs. 81.1% for LLaMA2-70B). CIPHER also surpasses Self-Consistency (Major@5) on every dataset for both model sizes, confirming that embedding-based debate is more effective than simply sampling multiple independent answers and voting. The advantage is particularly pronounced for the weaker LLaMA-65B, where NLD only marginally beats Single Answer on GSM8K (51.7% vs. 50.8%) while CIPHER achieves a clearer gain (52.9%), suggesting that embedding communication helps smaller models benefit from debate in ways that natural language does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multiagent debate framework of Du et al. (2023) by replacing the natural language communication channel with a continuous embedding-space protocol (CIPHER). Rather than sampling a single token from the model's output distribution at each generation step, CIPHER computes the expectation over all token embeddings, drawing a direct analogy to Expected SARSA in reinforcement learning. The approach also builds on self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023b) as a baseline and incorporates chain-of-thought prompting, but its core departure is the argument that the token sampling step—an operation designed for human readability—introduces unnecessary information loss for machine-to-machine communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even smaller open-source LLMs (e.g., LLaMA-65B, MPT-30B) can benefit from multiagent debate, contradicting prior findings that debate only helps the most powerful models. This suggests the bottleneck for weaker models was not insufficient reasoning capacity but the information loss inherent in discrete token communication. A key limitation is that CIPHER requires all debaters to share the same tokenizer, restricting cross-family communication; extending it to models with different tokenizers would require non-trivial embedding-space alignment. The paper also raises the open question of whether even more efficient belief-transfer mechanisms exist beyond weighted embedding averages, given that LLMs are only trained to process natural language token embeddings.
