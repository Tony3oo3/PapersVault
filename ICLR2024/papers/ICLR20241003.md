## Problem

Standard active learning assumes a cooperative labeler who willingly provides labels on informative examples. This paper identifies and formalizes a critical gap: when the labeler will be replaced by the trained model (e.g., a worker automating their own job), they have a strategic incentive to abstain from labeling informative examples to slow down learning and maximize compensation. The paper initiates the theoretical study of this Human-AI Substitution game, proving that strategic abstention can exponentially inflate query complexity compared to the non-abstention setting.

## Input / Output

The learner operates over a finite unlabeled data pool X and a hypothesis class H ⊆ (X → {+1,−1}), adaptively querying unqueried examples. The labeler responds with either a binary label h*(x) or an abstention symbol ⊥, and the learner incurs cost 1 only for non-abstained responses. The game terminates when the Effective Version Space (E-VS) reduces to a single hypothesis, at which point the learner pays the labeler for all binary labels provided. The key unusual aspect is that abstention itself carries information: it can render hypotheses unidentifiable and eliminate them from the E-VS without any binary label being revealed.

## Method

The paper introduces the Effective Version Space (E-VS), a refinement of the standard version space that tracks which hypotheses remain identifiable given the set of queried examples, accounting for the fact that abstention can make hypotheses indistinguishable. Building on this, the authors formulate a minimax learning game with a recursive value function Cost(V, S_X) and prove it lower-bounds the worst-case query complexity of any deterministic algorithm. The proposed E-VS Bisection algorithm (Algorithm 2) greedily queries the point that maximally bisects the E-VS, achieving near-optimal minimax query complexity O(Cost(H,∅)·ln|H|) via a new Global Identification Cost (GIC) measure. A C-ERM oracle-based subroutine enables efficient E-VS membership checks, circumventing the exponential polytope structure that makes direct E-VS sampling intractable. The algorithm is robust: it guarantees bounded query cost even against non-identifiable or arbitrary labeling oracles, while correctly identifying h* when the labeler is identifiable.

## Results

The paper establishes that strategic abstention can exponentially inflate query complexity: Proposition 1.1 shows a setting where query complexity is O(log|X|) without abstention but Ω(|X|) with abstention. The proposed E-VS Bisection Algorithm (Algorithm 2) achieves near-optimal minimax query complexity of O(Cost(H,∅)·ln|H| + 1) (Theorem 3.4), matching the lower bound up to a log factor. In the multi-task setting, Theorem 5.3 characterizes when the multi-task cost is bounded by the sum of single-task costs (under Cartesian product VS and c_all labeling cost), while Propositions 5.1–5.2 show the labeler can amplify complexity beyond this sum under other conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the classical VS Bisection algorithm (Tong & Koller, 2001). Theorem 3.8 constructs a concrete learning setup where the E-VS bisection algorithm queries only O(log|X|) labeled examples, while VS bisection requires Ω(|X|) labels — an exponential gap. The key advantage is that E-VS bisection is robust to strategic (non-identifiable) labeling: it uses E-VS cardinality to detect when the labeler's strategy cannot guarantee identification and halts early, whereas VS bisection has no such mechanism and continues querying inefficiently. The second comparison is with EPI-CAL (Huang et al., 2016), which handles labeler abstention but under a fundamentally different assumption: the labeler abstains due to genuine uncertainty and later has an 'epiphany' to label a region. In the strategic setting studied here, the labeler knows all labels but deliberately withholds them to maximize payment, making EPI-CAL's 'mellow' approach inapplicable. The paper's E-VS framework subsumes both the non-strategic abstention setting and the strategic setting, providing a unified theoretical treatment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard active learning framework (Hanneke et al., 2014; Tong & Koller, 2001) by introducing a game-theoretic twist: the labeler is strategic and aims to maximize the learner's query cost through selective abstention. While prior work on abstaining labelers (Yan et al., 2016; Huang et al., 2016) assumes non-strategic or fixed-rate abstention, this paper models the labeler as an adaptive adversary who can condition abstention on the full interaction history. The authors introduce the effective version space (E-VS) as a new state representation that captures identifiability under abstention, and develop a minimax game formulation to analyze query complexity, positioning the work at the intersection of active learning theory and mechanism design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical evidence that a labeler's strategic behavior can exponentially inflate active learning's query complexity, challenging the implicit assumption of labeler cooperativeness in the field. The E-VS bisection algorithm achieves near-optimal minimax guarantees and is robust to non-identifiable labelers, offering a principled defense against strategic labeling. Open questions include the computational tractability of E-VS access (shown to be intractable in general via exponential polytope structure), whether the results extend to stochastic/online settings, and how to design incentive-compatible contracts that align labeler and learner objectives. The broader societal framing—AI job displacement and fair compensation during automation—gives the theoretical results practical urgency.
