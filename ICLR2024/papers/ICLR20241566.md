## Problem

Adversarial training (AT) achieves optimal robustness against known attacks but overfits to them, failing to generalize to unseen attacks, while adversarial purification (AP) generalizes well but cannot match AT's robustness. Both approaches also degrade standard accuracy on clean inputs. The paper asks whether a single defense can simultaneously achieve optimal robustness, generalization to unseen attacks, and preserved standard accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241566/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) or 256×256 (ImageNette), either clean or adversarially perturbed. The input passes through a random transform (Gaussian noise σ=0.25 plus binary masking at rate r=0.25 with mask size P/8), then a pre-trained purifier (GAN-based DeepFill or AE-based masked autoencoder), and finally a frozen classifier (ResNet or WideResNet). The final output is a class label; the intermediate purified image is also produced and can be inspected. During fine-tuning, adversarial examples are generated via FGSM and fed through the full pipeline with the classifier held fixed.

## Method

AToP decomposes adversarial purification into two components: (1) perturbation destruction via random transforms (RT), where three variants progressively corrupt the input with Gaussian noise and binary masks (RT1: mask only; RT2: noise + mask; RT3: N=4 non-overlapping masks with pixel aggregation from multiple purifier passes), and (2) fine-tuning (FT) the purifier with an adversarial loss L_cls computed from the frozen classifier's output on adversarial examples, combined with the original generative loss (L_df for GAN or L_mae for AE) weighted by λ=0.1. The key distinction from standard AT is that the adversarial gradient updates only the purifier parameters θ_g while the classifier θ_f remains frozen, so the purifier learns to reconstruct clean examples that the classifier correctly labels without the classifier overfitting to specific perturbation patterns. This design lets RT handle generalization to unseen attacks (by destructing perturbation structure regardless of attack type) while FT boosts robustness against known attacks (by ensuring purified outputs don't fool the classifier).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 against AutoAttack l∞ (ε=8/255), AToP achieves 90.62% standard accuracy and 72.85% robust accuracy with WideResNet-28-10, and 91.99% / 76.37% with WideResNet-70-16, outperforming all baselines without extra data. On CIFAR-100 (l∞, ε=8/255), the method reaches 74.61% standard and 43.55% robust accuracy (WRN-28-10), and 76.95% / 44.44% (WRN-70-16). Against unseen attacks (StAdv, l2) on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-50, AToP achieves 56.4% robust accuracy on StAdv and 73.4% on l2, surpassing the previous best (DiffPure) by 1.4% and 2.5% respectively. The method also improves the underlying purifier models substantially: +13.28% robust accuracy on the GAN-based purifier and +14.45% on the AE-based purifier under l∞ AutoAttack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AToP outperforms all 16 baselines on robust accuracy across every dataset and threat model tested. On CIFAR-10 l∞ (Table 2), it beats the closest competitor Nie et al. (DiffPure, 70.64%) by 2.21% on WRN-28-10 and by 5.08% on WRN-70-16 (76.37% vs. 71.29%), while also exceeding methods that use extra unlabeled data (Gowal et al. 2020: 62.70%). On CIFAR-100 l∞ (Table 4), AToP surpasses Cui et al. (39.18%) by 4.37% and Wang et al. (42.67%) by 1.77% on robust accuracy. Against the two purifier baselines it builds upon (Table 5), AToP yields dramatic gains: +13.28% over Ughini et al. (59.57→72.85) and +14.45% over Wu et al. (44.73→59.18) on l∞, confirming that adversarial fine-tuning of the purifier is the key driver. On unseen-attack generalization (Table 6), AToP (71.2% l∞, 73.4% l2, 56.4% StAdv) beats DiffPure (70.0/70.9/55.0) and Adv.CRAIG (40.0/33.9/49.6) on all three threat models, while also maintaining the highest standard accuracy (89.1%) among all defenses. The only trade-off is a slight standard-accuracy drop versus a few AT baselines (e.g., Augustin et al. 92.23% vs. 90.62% on l2), but the robust-accuracy gain (+2.54%) more than compensates.

## Relation to Prior Work

AToP sits at the intersection of two established defense paradigms: adversarial training (AT), which achieves high robustness against known attacks but overfits to them, and adversarial purification (AP), which generalizes to unseen attacks but underperforms on known ones. The method directly builds on Ughini et al. (2022) and Wu et al. (2023), which use pre-trained GAN-based and AE-based inpainting models as fixed purifiers, and on Yang et al. (2019) for the random mask transform. The key departure is applying the adversarial classification loss to fine-tune the purifier model (while freezing the classifier), rather than training the classifier as in standard AT or relying solely on consistency/generative losses as in prior AP work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AToP addresses the trilemma of robustness, generalization to unseen attacks, and standard accuracy simultaneously, achieving SOTA robust accuracy on RobustBench without extra data while preserving clean-image accuracy. The practical limitation is computational: fine-tuning a diffusion-model purifier takes 144 minutes versus 62 seconds for a GAN-based one, raising open questions about whether the approach scales to more powerful generative purifiers. A broader open question is whether the adversarial-loss fine-tuning strategy can be extended to other pre-processing defenses or to multi-modal settings beyond image classification.
