## Problem

Transformers trained on short sequences (e.g., 2048 tokens) suffer significant performance degradation when evaluated on longer inputs, a critical limitation for practical long-context applications. Existing positional encodings either fail to extrapolate (APE, RoPE), impose fixed inductive biases that prevent learning diverse attention patterns (Alibi, Kerple), or rely on accelerator-unfriendly vector operations (T5's RPE). The paper asks whether a learned, function-based relative position encoding can simultaneously achieve strong length generalization and task-adaptive bias learning.

## Input / Output

FIRE takes the query position i and key position j from a causal attention setting (where 0 ≤ i−j < i) and produces a scalar attention bias added to the pre-softmax attention logits, forming a bias matrix B ∈ R^{n×n}. The progressive interpolation normalizes the relative distance by the query position, guaranteeing the MLP input is always in [0,1] regardless of sequence length. The MLP is extremely small (two hidden layers of 32 neurons with ReLU), making it accelerator-friendly and parameter-efficient compared to T5's RPE which uses per-bucket learnable scalars.

## Method

FIRE replaces hardcoded position bias functions with a learnable MLP f_θ that maps a normalized relative position to an attention bias. The key mechanism is progressive interpolation: the query-key distance (i−j) is divided by the query position i, bounding the input to [0,1] for all sequence lengths so that longer sequences simply form finer interpolation grids over the same domain. Two refinements improve quality: a log transformation ψ(x)=log(cx+1) amplifies differences among local positions, and a learnable threshold L switches to exact distances for short contexts (i ≤ L) while applying interpolation for longer ones. The paper proves constructively that FIRE can exactly represent T5's RPE, Alibi, Kerple, and Sandwich, unifying a class of existing methods while remaining expressive enough to learn anti-local attention patterns in some heads.

## Results

On C4 language modeling (base model, trained at 2048), FIRE achieves 21.24 perplexity at sequence length 8192, outperforming the best baseline Kerple (23.52) by 2.28 points. On the SCROLLS long-text benchmark, FIRE attains the best average score of 25.64 (base) and 27.05 (large), surpassing all competing methods by over 1 point. On zero-shot NarrativeQA with context lengths up to 32768, FIRE achieves 8.705 average F1, consistently outperforming all baselines across every context length. On GLUE/SuperGLUE (short-sequence tasks), FIRE achieves 71.14 average accuracy (base), matching RoPE, Kerple, and T5's RPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIRE consistently outperforms all seven baselines on long-context tasks. On C4 language modeling at 8192 tokens, FIRE (21.24 perplexity) beats Kerple (23.52), T5's RPE (23.18), RoPE (23.52), Alibi (23.54), NoPE (23.41), and YaRN, with the gap widening as sequence length exceeds the 2048 training length. On SCROLLS, FIRE leads by 1.53 points over the next-best method (RoPE-PI, 23.23) for base models and by 1.01 points over Kerple (25.72) for large models. On zero-shot NarrativeQA, FIRE (8.705 avg F1) surpasses Kerple (8.338), T5's RPE (4.788), RoPE-PI_32768 (5.589), and NoPE (4.238), with the advantage growing at longer contexts where RoPE and RoPE-PI_8192 collapse. On short-sequence GLUE/SuperGLUE, FIRE (71.14) is on par with RoPE (71.22), Kerple (71.18), and T5's RPE (71.05), confirming no quality sacrifice. Alibi and NoPE lag behind on both long and short tasks, while YaRN (concurrent work) underperforms FIRE on long sequences and sacrifices short-sequence quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIRE builds directly on the additive relative positional encoding (RPE) line of work, including T5's bucketed RPE, Alibi's linear decay, Kerple's kernelized log/power biases, and Sandwich's cosine formulation. The key conceptual departure is replacing hand-crafted inductive biases (linear, logarithmic, power-law decay) with a small learnable MLP, combined with a progressive interpolation scheme that normalizes relative distance by the query position to keep inputs bounded in [0,1]. The paper also positions itself against RoPE-based interpolation methods (RoPE-PI, YaRN) that require knowing the target sequence length a priori, and against the NoPE finding that decoder-only models can learn positional information without explicit encodings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIRE offers a practical, parameter-efficient (32-unit MLP) drop-in replacement for positional encoding that achieves strong zero-shot length generalization without requiring knowledge of the target sequence length or additional finetuning — a significant practical advantage over RoPE-PI and YaRN for autoregressive generation. The theoretical unification showing FIRE can represent T5's RPE, Alibi, Kerple, and Sandwich under one framework provides a clean lens for understanding these methods. Limitations include evaluation only at 125M/350M parameter scale, restriction to decoder-only models, and no analysis of how other architectural or training components interact with length generalization. Whether the progressive interpolation principle transfers to encoder models, multimodal settings, or very large-scale pretraining remains open.
