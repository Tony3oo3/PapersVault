## Problem

Neural network training is stochastic, producing different models across repeated runs, and the resulting test-set accuracy variance has been treated as an obstacle to reproducibility and fair comparison. However, it was unclear whether this variance reflects genuine differences in model quality on the underlying test distribution or is merely finite-sample noise from evaluating on a limited test set. This paper disentangles these two sources of variance and shows that for standard trainings, the true distribution-wise variance is an order of magnitude smaller than the observed test-set variance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes predictions from many independently trained networks (60,000 runs for CIFAR-10, 1,000 for ImageNet and BERT) evaluated on fixed test sets, yielding per-example error indicators across all runs. From these, it computes per-example error rates ε_i, the empirical test-set accuracy distribution, and an unbiased estimate of distribution-wise variance via Equation 3, which subtracts the independent-errors-predicted variance from the observed test-set variance and rescales by n/(n−1). The key output is a corrected variance estimate that isolates genuine model-quality variation from finite-sample noise.

## Method

The paper introduces the independent errors assumption (Definition 1), stating that errors on different test examples are uncorrelated across training runs, and shows it recovers the shape and variance of the empirical accuracy distribution when training to convergence. The central theoretical contribution is Theorem 2, which provides an unbiased estimator for distribution-wise variance using only test-set statistics (Equation 3), and Theorems 3–4, which prove that class-wise calibration (Jiang et al., 2021) implies a lower bound on test-set variance of err(A)/(nk), yielding the simple formula err(A)/(2n) for binary classification. Empirically, the paper validates these results across 60,000 CIFAR-10 ResNet runs, 1,000 ImageNet ResNet-18 runs, and 1,000 BERT finetuning runs, showing that standard trainings have distribution-wise stddev of ~0.03% despite ~0.15% test-set stddev, while distribution-shifted test sets and BERT_LARGE finetuning exhibit genuine excess variance.

## Results

For a standard 64-epoch CIFAR-10 ResNet training, the test-set accuracy standard deviation across 60,000 runs is 0.149%, but the estimated distribution-wise standard deviation is only 0.033% — a 22× reduction. For ImageNet (ResNet-18, 100 epochs), the test-set std is 0.118% while the distribution-wise std is 0.034% (12× smaller). The class-calibration-based variance formula (err(A)/(2n)) predicts test-set variance with R²=0.996 across 511 binary classification tasks derived from CIFAR-10, and is 70.5× more accurate than the standard binomial approximation in average squared distance to empirical variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the binomial variance approximation (Dietterich, 1998; Raschka, 2018), which estimates test-set variance as err(1−err)/n. The class-calibration formula err(A)/(2n) derived in Theorem 3 is 70.5× more accurate than the binomial formula in terms of average squared distance to the empirical variance across 511 binary classification tasks, achieving R²=0.996 versus a substantially lower fit for the binomial assumption. The second comparison is against the independent errors assumption (Definition 1), which the paper shows recovers the shape and standard deviation of the empirical accuracy distribution when training to convergence (64 epochs) but fails for shorter trainings (4 epochs), where the observed std of 0.56% far exceeds the 0.22% predicted by independent errors. The paper also contrasts its findings with the BERT finetuning literature (Dodge et al., 2020; Mosbach et al., 2020), showing that BERT_BASE's apparent high test-set variance (0.80% std) is mostly finite-sample noise (distribution-wise std only 0.21%), while BERT_LARGE genuinely has high distribution-wise variance (2.08% std), validating the use of multiple runs only for the latter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the class-calibration property discovered by Jiang et al. (2021), who showed that ensembles of identically trained networks are calibrated and that disagreement rate equals error rate. Here, the author extends that property into a variance framework: proving that class-calibration mathematically implies a lower bound on test-set variance (Theorems 3–4), and introducing the independent errors assumption to decompose observed test-set variance into genuine distribution-wise variance plus finite-sample noise. The work also positions itself against a large body of prior studies on sources of training stochasticity (Fort et al., Bouthillier et al., Summers & Dinneen) and BERT finetuning instability (Dodge et al., Mosbach et al.), but departs from them by distinguishing *where* the variance lives (test-set vs. test-distribution) rather than *which random seed component* causes it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is significant: for standard CIFAR-10 and ImageNet trainings, the distribution-wise standard deviation is only ~0.03%, meaning re-running training to find a 'lucky' seed is essentially wasted compute, since test-set improvements do not generalize to unseen data. The unbiased estimator (Equation 3) and the calibration-based variance formula (Equation 6) give practitioners tools to quantify genuine model-quality variance from a single test-set without thousands of re-runs. Open questions remain: why neural network trainings satisfy class-calibration in the first place, why distribution shift dramatically increases distribution-wise variance (6× or more on ImageNet-R, ObjectNet, ImageNet-Sketch), and what causes the pathological instability of BERT_LARGE finetuning that BERT_BASE does not exhibit.
