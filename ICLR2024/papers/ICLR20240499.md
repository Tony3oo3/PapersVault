## Problem

Existing imitation learning methods require many demonstrations, extensive environment interaction, or running full RL routines for each new behavior to imitate, making them slow and sample-hungry. No prior approach can produce an imitation policy from a few demonstrations in seconds while simultaneously supporting multiple IL principles (behavioral cloning, reward-based, distribution matching, goal-based). This paper asks whether a pre-trained behavior foundation model can solve arbitrary imitation tasks at inference time without any RL or fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240499/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method consumes a small number of expert trajectories (as few as one, optionally with actions) and the pre-trained FB model's F, B networks and Cov B matrix, but does not reuse the unsupervised training dataset. The output is a low-dimensional parameter vector z that indexes into the pre-trained policy family (π_z), yielding an immediately deployable imitation policy. For reward-based variants (ER_FB, RER_FB), z is computed with a single forward pass through B in under 5 seconds; for optimization-based variants (BC_FB, FM_FB, BBELL_FB), z is found via a short gradient descent taking 1–4 minutes, still three orders of magnitude faster than offline RL baselines.

## Method

The paper builds on the Forward-Backward (FB) framework, which pre-trains on unsupervised transitions to learn a rank-d approximation of successor measures via F and B maps, plus a family of policies (π_z) that are near-optimal for rewards in the linear span of B. The key insight is that FB's ability to recover a near-optimal policy for any reward r via z = E[r(s)B(s)] eliminates the need for RL at test time. The paper derives multiple FB-IL algorithms by instantiating different IL principles: behavioral cloning minimizes a trajectory likelihood over z; reward-based methods (ER_FB, RER_FB) compute z from expert state distributions; feature matching (FM_FB) minimizes the B*-norm distance between successor features; Bellman residual (BBELL_FB) minimizes Bellman gaps of the expert's transition kernel; and goal-based (GOAL_FB) sets z_t = B(s_{t+k}) at each step to track non-stationary behaviors. Theoretical results (Theorems 2, 4, 5, 7, 8) provide tractable loss expressions and prove that Bellman gap and BC losses bound distribution matching losses, unifying the IL principles under a common successor-measure framework.

## Results

On 21 tasks across 4 DeepMind Control Suite domains (Maze, Walker, Cheetah, Quadruped) with a single expert demonstration, FB-IL methods achieve imitation scores (ratio of cumulative return to expert return) on par with or exceeding all offline IL baselines, while computing the imitation policy in seconds rather than hours. Specifically, ERFB requires <5s, BCFB requires 1m, and BBELLFB requires 4m, compared to 3h14m (BC), 7h3m (TD3-IL), and 12h59m (Demodice) for offline baselines — a speedup of roughly three orders of magnitude. In the non-stationary waypoint imitation experiment (yoga poses), GOAL_FB matches GOAL-TD3 and outperforms GOAL-GPT, confirming competitiveness even in settings where only goal-based methods are applicable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against offline IL baselines, FB-IL methods consistently match or exceed performance: ERFB and RERFB (reward-based) perform on par or better than SQIL, ORIL, and OTR, while FB-IL variants for distribution/apprenticeship matching (FM_FB, BBELLFB) achieve roughly 4× the performance of Demodice and IQ-Learn, which struggle due to conservative offline RL biases. BCFB substantially outperforms vanilla BC with a single demonstration, confirming that the FB policy family provides a useful inductive bias. Among BFM baselines, FB-IL outperforms DIAYN across all domains and IL principles; GOAL_FB matches GOAL-TD3 (the strongest non-FB BFM) and outperforms GOAL-GPT and MASKDP in the waypoint imitation setting. The key trade-off is that goal-based methods (GOAL_FB, GOAL-TD3) are limited to single-trajectory copying and cannot produce a reusable stationary policy, whereas ERFB, BCFB, and BBELLFB produce general-purpose policies from the same pre-trained model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Forward-Backward (FB) framework for successor measures (Touati & Ollivier, 2021; Touati et al., 2023), originally designed for zero-shot RL, as a universal imitation learning engine. It departs from the standard offline IL paradigm—where each new task requires training a full RL agent (SQIL, ORIL, DICE, IQ-Learn)—by showing that a single pre-trained FB model can implement behavioral cloning, reward-based IL, distribution matching, feature matching, and goal-based IL through simple forward passes or short gradient descents over a low-dimensional parameter z. The work also positions itself against other behavior foundation model approaches (DIAYN, masked trajectory models, goal-conditioned policies) by demonstrating broader IL principle coverage with competitive or superior performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3-orders-of-magnitude reduction in inference time (hours to seconds) while matching or exceeding SOTA offline IL performance makes few-shot imitation practically deployable in settings where retraining is infeasible. The theoretical results connecting Bellman gap losses to distribution matching and BC losses to KL divergence provide a unified lens on seemingly disparate IL principles. Key limitations include the requirement for environment-specific pre-training (not cross-environment transfer), evaluation restricted to state-vector inputs in DeepMind Control Suite, and the acknowledged gap between BFM approximation quality and true optimal performance. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional sensory inputs (vision, language) and whether test-time fine-tuning of the BFM can close the remaining performance gap.
