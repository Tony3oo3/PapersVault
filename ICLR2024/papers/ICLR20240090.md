## Problem

Existing zero-shot industrial anomaly detection methods rely on external information—text prompts (WinCLIP, APRIL-GAN) or normal reference images (few-shot methods)—while ignoring the abundant normal and abnormal cues already implicit in the unlabeled test images themselves. The paper identifies that normal image patches can find many similar counterparts across other unlabeled images, whereas abnormal patches have few, a discriminative property that prior methods have not exploited for anomaly scoring.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240090/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a batch of unlabeled industrial test images (RGB, scaled to 518×518) as its sole input—no training set, no text prompts, no normal reference images are required. It produces two outputs: an image-level anomaly classification score (a single scalar per image) and a pixel-level anomaly segmentation map (upsampled from the patch grid to original resolution). The test set size directly affects performance; the method can be split into subsets (s=1,2,3) to manage inference time and GPU memory with less than 1.1% accuracy loss.

## Method

MuSc operates entirely on unlabeled test images using a frozen ViT-L/14-336 backbone in three stages. First, Local Neighborhood Aggregation with Multiple Degrees (LNAMD) applies adaptive average pooling at r∈{1,3,5} over patch tokens at each of 4 ViT stages, producing multi-scale features that capture anomalies of varying sizes. Second, the Mutual Scoring Mechanism (MSM) computes, for each patch of image I_i, the minimum L2 distance to all patches of every other unlabeled image I_j, then applies an Interval Average over the minimum 30% of these distances to suppress false positives from appearance variation, and finally averages across all stage–degree combinations to yield a per-patch anomaly score. Third, Re-scoring with Constrained Image-level Neighborhood (RsCIN) builds an edge-weighted graph over class tokens using dot-product similarity, applies a multi-window k-NN mask (k∈{2,3} on MVTec AD, k∈{8,9} on VisA) to constrain influence, and refines the image-level classification score via a self-smoothing-operator-style update that averages each image's score with its weighted neighborhood scores.

## Results

On MVTec AD, MuSc achieves 97.8% image-AUROC, 99.1% image-AP, 97.3% pixel-AUROC, 62.7% pixel-AP, and 93.8% PRO in the zero-shot setting, surpassing the best prior zero-shot method (ACR) by +6.0% AUROC-cls and +21.1% PRO. On VisA, MuSc reaches 92.8% image-AUROC and 98.8% pixel-AUROC, improving over the best zero-shot baseline (APRIL-GAN) by +14.7% and +4.6% respectively. On the BTAD dataset, MuSc attains 94.8% image-AUROC and 97.3% pixel-AUROC in zero-shot, outperforming APRIL-GAN (0-shot) by +22.5% image-AUROC and even exceeding 4-shot methods like RegAD (91.0%) and PatchCore (91.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot baselines, MuSc dominates across all metrics: on MVTec AD it beats ACR (the strongest zero-shot competitor) by +6.0% AUROC-cls, +4.8% AUROC-segm, +21.1% PRO, and +21.9% AP-segm; on VisA it surpasses APRIL-GAN by +14.7% AUROC-cls and +19.4% AP-segm. Against few-shot methods, MuSc (using no reference images) outperforms RegAD (4-shot) by +8.7% AUROC-cls and +5.8% PRO on MVTec AD, and exceeds PatchCore (4-shot) by +7.5% AUROC-cls and +7.8% PRO on VisA. MuSc also surpasses GraphCore (8-shot) by +4.9% AUROC-cls on MVTec AD. Compared to full-shot methods (Table 2), MuSc's 97.8% AUROC-cls is competitive with NSA (97.2%) and IGD (93.4%), and its 97.3% pixel-AUROC is close to PatchCore full-shot (98.2%) while using zero training images. On BTAD, MuSc (0-shot) outperforms APRIL-GAN (0-shot) by +22.5% AUROC-cls and exceeds 4-shot RegAD and PatchCore, though it remains slightly below full-shot PyramidFlow (95.8%) and PaDiM (93.7%) in classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

MuSc departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm in industrial anomaly detection, where test images are scored against external references—either a bank of normal training images (PatchCore, PaDiM) or text prompts via CLIP (WinCLIP, APRIL-GAN). Instead, it exploits the mutual similarity structure among unlabeled test images themselves, drawing on the nearest-neighbor scoring idea from PatchCore's memory bank and the self-smoothing operator from manifold learning (Jiang et al., 2011) for image-level re-scoring. The LNAMD module adapts the adaptive average pooling from PatchCore to multi-scale neighborhood aggregation, while the overall architecture repurposes a frozen ViT-L/14-336 (CLIP) backbone without any fine-tuning or prompt engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that unlabeled test images contain sufficient normal and abnormal cues to score each other (97.26% normal pixels in MVTec AD test set) challenges the assumption that anomaly detection requires external references, opening a new design space for zero-shot industrial inspection. Practical limitations remain: inference time and GPU memory scale with the number of test images (mitigated by subset partitioning), and the method requires a batch of images rather than operating on a single image. Open questions include robustness when the test set is very small, highly imbalanced, or contains systematic appearance variations across categories, and whether the mutual scoring principle generalizes beyond industrial product images to other domains like medical imaging or natural scene anomaly detection.
