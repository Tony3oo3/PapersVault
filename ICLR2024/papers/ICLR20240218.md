## Problem

Procedural planning—decomposing a high-level goal into a temporally ordered sequence of steps—has been addressed primarily by large LLMs (e.g., GPT-3 175B), which suffer from high API costs, reproducibility issues, and inaccessibility to model parameters. The paper asks whether orders-of-magnitude smaller models (770M–11B) can be endowed with sufficient procedural and commonsense knowledge to match or surpass these large teachers, and introduces the previously unexplored task of counterfactual replanning under natural-language constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240218/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a free-form natural-language goal (e.g., 'see a movie'), optionally augmented with a condition (e.g., 'the store is closed') for constrained planning, or an initial plan plus condition for counterfactual replanning. Output is a sequence of natural-language steps; in the embodied VirtualHome setting, these steps are mapped to executable actions via embedding-space nearest-neighbor matching against 42 supported environment actions. Conditions are open-vocabulary and span categories like location, equipment, safety, and user physical condition, making the task more realistic than fixed-constraint benchmarks.

## Method

PLASMA combines two components. First, symbolic procedural knowledge distillation: GPT-3 (text-curie-001 / text-davinci-003) is few-shot prompted with randomized instructions to generate 140K goal-plan pairs, conditions, and counterfactual plans; trained RoBERTa-Large critic models (using 13K human annotations) filter low-quality samples, yielding the COPLAN dataset (57,794 planning + 43,690 constrained/counterfactual tuples). T5 student models (770M, 3B, 11B) are then fine-tuned on COPLAN using either task-specific or joint multi-task autoregressive objectives. Second, at inference, a verifier-guided step-wise beam search (PLASMA+) replaces greedy decoding: at each step, N candidate next-steps are sampled via mixed decoding (beam search + nucleus sampling), scored by a weighted combination of the student's log-likelihood and a RoBERTa step-verifier's validity score (which checks sequential ordering and semantic completeness), and the top-K candidates are retained in a beam until K complete plans are produced.

## Results

On goal-based planning (5-point Likert human evaluation), the best 11B PLASMA-Mul+ achieves 4.58 overall quality, 4.77 order, and 4.53 coverage, matching human performance (4.57 overall) and surpassing its GPT-3 Curie teacher (3.75 overall) by 22.59% relatively. On constrained planning, 11B PLASMA-Mul+ achieves a 93.33% validity rate, and on counterfactual replanning, 11B PLASMA+ achieves 86.33%. In the VirtualHome embodied environment, PLASMA-Mul+ (11B) reaches 94.18% executability and 43.68% correctness, substantially outperforming the 175B GPT-3-based Planner (77.17% executability, 18.33% correctness).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its GPT-3 Curie teacher (few-shot, 5 examples), all PLASMA variants with verifier-guided decoding (PLASMA+) substantially outperform: the 11B PLASMA-Mul+ scores 4.58 vs. 3.75 overall quality (22.59% relative improvement), and even the 770M student reaches 4.28 vs. 3.75. Against the 175B Davinci model, PLASMA-Mul+ (11B) at 4.58 overall quality is competitive but slightly below Davinci's zero-shot (4.84) and few-shot (4.90) scores; however, on constrained planning (93.33% vs. ~93%) and counterfactual replanning (86.33% vs. 90%), statistical T-tests show no significant difference. Against CoCoGen (175B, 16-shot code LLM), the 11B PLASMA-Mul+ marginally wins on overall quality (4.58 vs. 4.55) while being ~16× smaller. On the out-of-domain Collins et al. (2022) constrained planning benchmark, PLASMA achieves 71% good plans, outperforming both the GPT-3 plans reported by Collins et al. (36%) and a fresh GPT-3 zero-shot baseline (64%). In VirtualHome, PLASMA-Mul+ (11B) outperforms the 175B GPT-3 Planner by 17% absolute on executability (94.18% vs. 77.17%) and 25% absolute on correctness (43.68% vs. 18.33%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symbolic knowledge distillation framework of West et al. (2022) and Bhagavatula et al. (2023) from commonsense QA to procedural planning, combining LLM-generated training data with a novel verifier-guided step-wise beam search at inference. It departs from the dominant LLM-centric planning paradigm (Madaan et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2022; Ahn et al., 2022) by demonstrating that orders-of-magnitude smaller T5 models, augmented with structured decoding, can match or exceed their GPT-3 teachers. The decoding strategy is conceptually related to function-guided generation (Krause et al., 2021; Yang & Klein, 2021) but operates at the step level rather than the token level, using a separate RoBERTa verifier rather than per-token logits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical pathway to making procedural planning accessible without expensive LLM API calls, which is significant for embodied AI and resource-constrained deployments. The COPLAN dataset (11× larger than prior procedural planning data) and the introduction of counterfactual replanning as a task formulation open new research directions. Key limitations include cultural bias toward North American/English-speaking norms in the generated data, reliance on GPT-3 for data generation (limiting quality ceiling), and the fact that the 11B model still trails Davinci-003 (4.58 vs. 4.90 overall quality), meaning the scale gap is narrowed but not fully closed. Extending to multimodal settings and more socio-culturally diverse goals remains open.
