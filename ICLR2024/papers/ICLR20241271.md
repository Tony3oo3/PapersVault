## Problem

Existing alignment scores for conditional diffusion models are tailored to specific condition-output pairs (primarily text-to-image) and rely on separately trained scoring networks with large curated datasets, making them impractical for the vast diversity of condition types (edge maps, scribbles, audio, domain-specific styles) that conditional diffusion supports. No universal, training-free metric exists that can assess condition alignment across arbitrary modalities using only the generative model itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241271/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a generated sample (image or audio waveform), its corresponding condition (text prompt, reference image, canny edge, scribble, or editing instruction), and the frozen conditional diffusion model that produced the sample. It outputs a single scalar CAS value; higher values indicate stronger alignment between the sample and its condition. Notably, the same diffusion model used for generation is reused unmodified for scoring (self-rejection), and no external scoring network, CLIP model, or human annotation is required at inference time.

## Method

CAS is defined as log p_θ(x|c) − log p_θ(x), which equals log p_θ(c|x) up to a constant, thereby debiasing the conditional likelihood by the unconditional likelihood to counteract the diffusion model's tendency to underestimate p(x) for out-of-distribution conditions. Because Classifier-Free Guidance breaks the stochastic modeling needed for forward-process probability computation, both probabilities are instead obtained during a DDIM inversion pass from the generated sample back to noise, where the ODE form of DDIM (Eq. 6) allows exact log-probability integration via the change-of-variable formula. To correct the approximation error inherent in standard DDIM inversion (which substitutes x_{t−1} for x_t in the noise prediction), the authors introduce a recursive inversion scheme that iteratively refines the latent estimate, reducing inversion error to under 10% of the original. Finally, the Skilling-Hutchinson trace estimator for the divergence term is approximated by a two-forward-pass finite difference (Eq. 10) with σ=10⁻³, eliminating backpropagation and cutting total computation time to 16% of the original cost.

## Results

On the Van Gogh style-fidelity dataset, CAS achieves 47.0% accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (Pick Score at 33.8%) by 13.2 percentage points. On the Pick Score dataset (T2I alignment), CAS reaches 62.2% accuracy, surpassing CLIP Score (58.0%) and matching Image Reward (62.1%), though trailing HPS (69.7%) and Pick Score (72.1%). In cross-modality human preference evaluations, CAS achieves 58–64% preference accuracy across InstructPix2Pix, ControlNet (Canny/Scribble), and AudioLDM. For fake image detection, a CAS ensemble MLP reaches 92% accuracy with only 500 training samples, surpassing the state-of-the-art CLIP-ViT(LC) at 87%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAS decisively outperforms all four T2I baselines on the Van Gogh style-fidelity task (47.0% vs. 33.8% for Pick Score, 28.4% for CLIP Score, 24.7% for Image Reward, and 22.9% for HPS), demonstrating its unique ability to detect domain-specific style alignment without any pre-trained scoring model. On the Pick Score dataset, CAS (62.2%) beats CLIP Score (58.0%) and is essentially tied with Image Reward (62.1%), but falls short of HPS (69.7%) and Pick Score (72.1%), which benefit from large-scale human-preference training data. For fake image detection, CAS combined with a 3-layer MLP achieves 92% accuracy and 96% average precision, surpassing the state-of-the-art CLIP-ViT(LC) detector (87% accuracy, 91% AP) while using only 500 training samples. The paper also shows CAS outperforms directional CLIP similarity in InstructPix2Pix, with higher CAS ranks correlating with greater directional CLIP similarity. Overall, CAS's key advantage is universality: it requires zero additional training data or pre-trained scoring models, making it applicable to any conditional diffusion model out of the box.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the exact probability computation framework of Song et al. (2021b) — originally designed for unconditional log-likelihood estimation — into a conditional alignment metric by extending it to the CFG-augmented diffusion process and debiasing with the unconditional probability. It builds on DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2021a) as the mechanism to recover latents for probability computation, but identifies and corrects the approximation error in standard DDIM inversion via a recursive scheme. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of learning-based alignment scores (CLIP Score, HPS, Image Reward, Pick Score), which each require large curated datasets and are tied to specific condition-output pairs, by instead leveraging the generating diffusion model itself as a self-contained scorer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universal, training-free nature of CAS is practically significant: any conditional diffusion model (text-to-image, image editing, edge-to-image, text-to-audio) can immediately score its own outputs without collecting preference data or training a separate network, which is especially valuable for fine-tuned or domain-specific models where such data is unavailable. The self-rejection property — high CAS for in-distribution samples, low for out-of-distribution — opens a path toward lightweight fake-image detection and source attribution without dedicated detectors. Limitations include the non-trivial per-sample computation cost (even after the 16× speedup), the somewhat ad hoc choice of λ=1, and evaluation on relatively small datasets with only five human raters for multimodal settings, leaving open whether the approach scales to video generation or very large model families.
