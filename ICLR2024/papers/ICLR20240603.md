## Problem

Consistency training (CT) previously lagged significantly behind consistency distillation (CD) in sample quality, and both approaches relied on LPIPS, a learned metric that introduces evaluation bias (since LPIPS and the Inception network in FID share ImageNet pre-training) and extra compute. Additionally, the original theoretical justification for CT contained an overlooked flaw: when the teacher network uses a nonzero EMA decay rate, the consistency objective becomes independent of the data distribution in the continuous limit, rendering it untrainable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240603/cropped_page17_idx16.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single sample of Gaussian noise (z ~ N(0, σ_max²I) with σ_max=80) and optionally a class label (for ImageNet 64×64) as input, and produces a natural RGB image in one or two forward passes. CIFAR-10 images are 32×32 and unconditional; ImageNet images are 64×64 and class-conditional. Two-step generation inserts an intermediate noise level (e.g., σ=0.821 for CIFAR-10) and re-noises the partially denoised sample before a final denoising step, yielding substantial FID improvement over one-step sampling.

## Method

The paper identifies and fixes a theoretical flaw in CT: when the teacher network's EMA decay rate μ > 0, the consistency matching objective in the N→∞ limit becomes independent of the data distribution (Proposition 1), so the correct fix is setting μ=0 (i.e., teacher = student). To replace LPIPS, the authors adopt the Pseudo-Huber loss d(x,y) = √(‖x−y‖²+c²) − c, which is twice-differentiable, robust to outliers, and reduces training variance. They further introduce a lognormal noise schedule (mean=−1.1, std=2.0) that down-weights high noise levels, an exponential curriculum that doubles the number of discretization steps N every K′ iterations (from s₀=10 to s₁=1280), a weighting function λ(σᵢ)=1/(σᵢ₊₁−σᵢ) that emphasizes low-noise consistency, reduced Fourier embedding scale (0.02), and dropout (0.3 on CIFAR-10). Together these changes enable CT to achieve FID 2.51 (CIFAR-10) and 3.25 (ImageNet 64×64) in one step, surpassing CD and prior CT by 3.5×–4×.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, iCT-deep achieves FID 2.51 in one sampling step and 2.24 in two steps, representing a 3.5× improvement over prior consistency training (CT with LPIPS: FID 8.70). On ImageNet 64×64, iCT-deep reaches FID 3.25 (one-step) and 2.77 (two-step), a 4× improvement over prior CT (FID 13.0). The two-step FID of 2.24 on CIFAR-10 nearly matches Score SDE (FID 2.20) which requires 2000 sampling steps, while the one-step FID of 2.51 already surpasses StyleGAN2-ADA (FID 2.92) and DDPM (FID 3.17 at 1000 NFE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

iCT-deep decisively outperforms the original consistency training (CT with LPIPS) from Song et al. 2023: FID drops from 8.70 to 2.51 (one-step) and from 5.83 to 2.24 (two-step) on CIFAR-10, a 3.5× and 2.6× improvement respectively. It also surpasses consistency distillation (CD with LPIPS, FID 3.55 one-step / 2.93 two-step) without requiring a pre-trained diffusion model. Against fast diffusion samplers, iCT-deep one-step (FID 2.51) beats DDIM (13.36), DPM-solver-fast (4.70), UniPC (3.87), and all distillation-based methods (TRACT 3.78, DFNO 3.78, PD* 8.34). Among GANs, iCT one-step (2.83) exceeds StyleGAN2-ADA (2.92) and iCT-deep one-step (2.51) far surpasses BigGAN-deep (14.7). However, iCT-deep two-step (2.24) still trails the best multi-step diffusion models: EDM-G++ (1.77 at 35 NFE), EDM (2.04 at 35 NFE), and RIN (1.23 at 1000 NFE), though it matches Score SDE deep (2.20 at 2000 NFE) with only 2 NFE. On ImageNet 64×64, iCT-deep one-step (FID 3.25) beats BigGAN-deep (4.06) and all distillation methods, while two-step (2.77) approaches ADM (2.07 at 250 NFE) and iDDPM (2.92 at 250 NFE). Notably, iCT methods achieve higher recall (0.63) than CT (LPIPS) (0.47) and BigGAN-deep (0.48), indicating better mode coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Song et al. (2023, ICML) 'Consistency Models,' which introduced both consistency distillation (CD) and consistency training (CT) as two pathways to train single-step generative models. The key conceptual departure is identifying a theoretical flaw in the original CT analysis: the Exponential Moving Average on the teacher network, which the original paper justified via an asymptotic argument, is actually incompatible with the gradient-matching argument and should be set to zero for CT. The paper also draws on robust statistics (Charbonnier et al., 1997) for the Pseudo-Huber loss as a principled replacement for LPIPS, and on Karras et al. (2022) for the lognormal noise schedule, while reusing the Score SDE/ADM architectures from the diffusion model literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the gap between CT and CD (and surpassing CD) without requiring a pre-trained diffusion model or learned perceptual metrics, this work establishes consistency models as a genuinely independent family of generative models rather than a distillation byproduct. The theoretical correction (Proposition 1) is important for the broader score-based/consistency modeling literature, as it invalidates a previously accepted asymptotic equivalence argument. Open questions include whether these techniques scale to higher resolutions (256×256, 512×512) and other modalities, whether the remaining gap to top diffusion models (e.g., EDM-G++ at FID 1.77 on CIFAR-10) can be closed with further architectural or training innovations, and whether the Pseudo-Huber constant heuristic generalizes beyond natural images.
