## Problem

Evaluating the robustness of image watermarks relies on a limited set of known, non-adaptive attacks, making it impossible to determine whether a watermark is truly secure or merely resistant to the specific attacks tested. No comprehensive, principled method exists to validate robustness against adaptive attackers who know the watermarking algorithm but not the secret key. This paper addresses the gap by formulating adaptive watermark evasion as an optimization problem with a well-defined objective function.

## Input / Output

The attacker takes a watermarked image generated by the provider's Stable Diffusion v2.0 model (512×512 RGB) along with a less capable open-source surrogate generator (Stable Diffusion v1.1) to locally replicate a differentiable surrogate watermarking key. The output is a modified image—either an ε-bounded adversarial perturbation or a single autoencoder compression pass—that evades watermark verification (reducing TPR@1%FPR below 10%) while preserving perceptual quality (LPIPS < 0.08). The optimized attack parameters (θ_A) are computed once and reused across all subsequent images, requiring no interaction with the provider's API during the attack phase.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage framework: (1) GKEYGEN (Algorithm 1) trains a ResNet-50 decoder from scratch to extract watermark messages from images, producing a differentiable surrogate key θ_D that approximates the provider's secret key; (2) two learnable attacks are then optimized against this surrogate key. Adversarial Noising (Algorithm 2) iteratively computes an L∞-bounded perturbation θ_A via projected gradient descent, maximizing the L1 distance between the initially extracted message and the message extracted from the perturbed image. Adversarial Compression (Algorithm 3) fine-tunes a pre-trained autoencoder's encoder-decoder pair to minimize LPIPS quality loss while simultaneously maximizing the message distance, requiring only a single compression pass at inference. The key insight is that a single surrogate key suffices for effective attacks across all five surveyed methods (TRW, WDM, DWT, DWT-SVD, RivaGAN), and the entire optimization completes in under 1 GPU hour on an A100, reducing detection accuracy to 6.3% or less with negligible visible quality degradation.

## Results

The paper's adaptive attacks break all five surveyed watermarking methods, reducing detection accuracy (TPR@1%FPR) to 6.3% or below. Adversarial Noising (ε=2/255) achieves the best result against TRW with 5.2% accuracy and the lowest perceptual distortion (LPIPS 3.2e-2). Adversarial Compression (r=1) is the most universally effective attack, breaking WDM (2.0%), DWT (0.8%), DWT-SVD (1.9%), and RivaGAN (6.3%) with LPIPS values between 5.6e-2 and 7.7e-2. All attacks require less than 1 GPU hour to optimize, and FID/CLIP scores remain statistically unchanged after the attack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates its adaptive attacks against five watermarking methods: TRW (Wen et al., 2023), WDM (Zhao et al., 2023), DWT/DWT-SVD (Cox et al., 2007), and RivaGAN (Zhang et al., 2019). Adversarial Compression (r=1) is the dominant attack, breaking four of five methods (WDM, DWT, DWT-SVD, RivaGAN) with detection accuracies between 0.8% and 6.3%, all well below the 10% threshold. TRW is the most vulnerable to Adversarial Noising (ε=2/255), which achieves 5.2% accuracy with the lowest LPIPS (3.2e-2), indicating the least perceptual degradation. DWT is the most vulnerable overall, with adversarial compression reducing accuracy to just 0.8%. RivaGAN is the most robust, requiring the highest LPIPS (7.3e-2) and retaining the highest residual accuracy (6.3%), though it is still broken. Non-adaptive attacks (blurring, JPEG, cropping, Gaussian noise, jittering, quantization, rotation) are all ineffective at removing any watermark, confirming that only adaptive, learnable attacks can break these methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive attack framework for watermarking robustness testing established by Lukas et al. (2022), who demonstrated that handcrafted adaptive attacks could break image classifier watermarks but did not scale. The key conceptual departure is formulating adaptive attacks as a differentiable optimization problem, using a GKEYGEN procedure to create surrogate keys that enable gradient-based parameter search. The threat model and evaluation protocol are built on the no-box watermarking framework (KEYGEN, EMBED, VERIFY) defined by Lukas & Kerschbaum (2023), and the paper positions its learnable attacks as a systematic replacement for the non-adaptive attacks (blurring, JPEG, cropping) that prior watermarking papers use to claim robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single surrogate key suffices to break all five surveyed watermarking methods reveals a potential design flaw in key generation: if KEYGEN were sufficiently randomized, one key should not generalize. This shifts the burden of robustness claims from 'survives known attacks' to 'survives optimized, learnable attacks,' a higher bar that current methods fail. Open questions include how much model divergence between surrogate and target generators the attacks tolerate, whether more powerful editing tools (inpainting, instruction-based editing) further weaken watermarks, and whether any watermarking scheme can be certified robust rather than merely empirically tested. The paper's limitation to Stable Diffusion and its refusal to release code publicly temper immediate reproducibility.
