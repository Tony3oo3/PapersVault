## Problem

Bayesian optimization under contextual uncertainty (BOCU) encompasses many uncertainty objectives—distributionally robust optimization, worst-case sensitivity, robust satisficing, mean-risk tradeoffs, and conditional value-at-risk—that have been studied in isolation with separate algorithms and analyses. This paper identifies that these disparate objectives are all recoverable from the DRO objective and its first derivative with respect to the distribution-shift margin, and asks whether a single algorithm and regret analysis can cover the entire family.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the learner selects a decision x from a compact convex set X ⊂ R^m before observing a context c drawn from a finite set C of size n; the true context distribution p*_t may differ from the known reference distribution p_t by at most ε_t in a chosen convex distance d (TV or MMD in experiments). The algorithm takes as input a GP kernel k, the reference distributions {p_t}, and the uncertainty parameters (α, β, {ε_t}), and outputs the sequence of decisions {x_t} that maximizes the general objective g(x; α,β,ε) = αv(x,ε) + βδ(x,ε), where v is the worst-case expected value and δ is its right derivative. The theoretical output is a T-period Bayesian regret bound that is sublinear under standard kernel and distribution assumptions.

## Method

The key insight is that the right derivative δ(x,ε) of the DRO objective v(x,ε) with respect to the margin ε encodes risk notions (range, variance, CVaR) and, combined with v itself via scaling parameters α and β, yields a general uncertainty objective g(x;α,β,ε) that recovers SO, RO, DRO, WCS, mean-risk tradeoff, and robust satisficing as special cases (Table 1). The proposed algorithm TS-BOCU uses Thompson sampling: at each iteration it draws a function f̃_t from the GP posterior and solves argmax_x g̃(x; f̃_t, p_t, α, β, ε_t), where g̃ is computed by solving the convex DRO program for v and a finite-difference approximation for δ. The theoretical analysis adapts Russo & Van Roy's posterior-sampling regret framework to the DRBO setting, proving a sublinear Bayesian regret bound under the TV distance and SE/Matern(ν>2) kernels; the derivative term introduces a p_min^{-1} factor and requires the closed-form TV solution to handle the bilevel structure. Thompson sampling is essential for tractability: the UCB counterpart would require solving a bilevel optimization for the derivative upper confidence bound, whereas TS-BOCU only needs posterior samples and the same convex program used for v.

## Results

TS-BOCU consistently demonstrates sublinear cumulative regret across all four experimental settings (GP samples, Hartmann 3-D, plant growth simulator, and COVID-19 epidemic model) and all three uncertainty objectives (DRO, WCS, and GEN), with both TV and MMD distribution distances. The algorithm achieves the lowest cumulative regret relative to all baselines in most settings, particularly excelling in settings where the uncertainty objective includes the derivative term (β=1). Notably, TS-BOCU maintains strong performance under MMD even though the theoretical regret bound (Theorem 4.1) is only proven for the TV distance, suggesting the theory may extend to a broader class of distribution distances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TS-BOCU against five baselines: Random, UCB-SO, UCB-RO (both from Kirschner et al., 2020), and two naive UCB extensions (UCB-BOCU-1 and UCB-BOCU-2) that incorporate tractable but theoretically unsupported derivative upper-bound terms. TS-BOCU outperforms all baselines in most settings, with the clearest advantage appearing when β=1 (WCS and GEN objectives) where UCB-BOCU-1 fails to perform well due to its inability to handle the derivative term. UCB-BOCU-2 performs better than UCB-BOCU-1 in these derivative-inclusive settings but relies on a theoretically unsupported heuristic, whereas TS-BOCU provides both theoretical guarantees and superior empirical performance. UCB-SO and Random struggle across most settings, while UCB-RO shows inconsistent performance. The UCB-BOCU variants perform competitively on the DRO setting (β=0) as expected since they were designed for that case, but TS-BOCU still matches or exceeds them there.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distributionally robust Bayesian optimization (DRBO) framework of Kirschner et al. (2020) by showing that the first derivatives of the DRO objective v(x,ε) with respect to the margin ε naturally recover worst-case sensitivity (Gotoh et al., 2020), mean-risk tradeoffs (Iwazaki et al., 2021), and robust satisficing (Long et al., 2023) as special cases. The algorithmic contribution builds on Thompson sampling (Russo & Van Roy, 2014) and the predecessor work of Tay et al. (2022) on efficient DRBO with WCS, adapting their proof techniques to handle the additional derivative term. The key conceptual departure from prior BOCU work is treating these previously disparate uncertainty objectives as points on a single continuous spectrum parameterized by (α, β, ε), rather than as separate problem formulations requiring separate algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework provides practitioners with a single algorithm (TS-BOCU) and a single regret bound that covers all major uncertainty objectives in the BOCU literature, simplifying the choice of risk-aversion mechanism. A notable practical finding is that Thompson sampling is tractable for the general objective while UCB-based acquisitions require solving a bilevel optimization, making TS the natural choice for this problem family. Open questions include whether the regret bound can be extended beyond the total variation distance assumption (empirical evidence suggests it holds for MMD), and whether the BOCU framework can be further generalized to subsume other BO settings such as causal BO or BO with uncertain inputs that currently fall outside its scope.
