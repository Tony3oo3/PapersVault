## Problem

Existing neural PDE solvers that use adaptive meshes face two key limitations: they require expensive optimal mesh data generated by traditional solvers for supervised training, and h-adaptation (adding/removing nodes) changes the solution space's topology and degree of freedom, complicating learning. This paper addresses both by proposing a data-free moving mesh (r-adaptation) approach that preserves mesh topology while concentrating resolution where the solution varies most.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240182/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The DMM takes a discrete PDE state u (on a regular grid like 48×48 or an irregular triangular mesh of 2521 nodes) along with time and spatial coordinates (t, x), and outputs a coordinate transformation mapping that relocates existing nodes to form an adaptive mesh with fixed topology. MM-PDE then takes the discrete state on the original uniform mesh and produces the next-timestep solution on that same original mesh, internally routing messages through both the original and the DMM-generated moving mesh. The method supports 2-D and 3-D settings, with the 3-D Gray-Scott equation tested on a 24×24×24 grid.

## Method

The core contribution is the Data-free Mesh Mover (DMM), a DeepONet-style neural operator trained entirely with a physics loss derived from the Monge-Ampère equation, eliminating the need for supervised optimal mesh data. The monitor function M is chosen via a theoretical interpolation error bound analysis (Proposition 1, Theorem 1), which shows that M proportional to the Sobolev semi-norm of u minimizes the error bound; a small regularization parameter α is selected to make the bound independent of the density approximation error. DMM is trained with a monitor-function-weighted sampling strategy and a two-stage optimizer (Adam followed by BFGS on the final decoder layer). On top of DMM, MM-PDE uses a two-branch GNN architecture: one branch (G1) passes messages on the original mesh to preserve data integrity, while the other (G2) passes messages on the DMM-generated moving mesh to concentrate resolution in high-activity regions. A learnable interpolation framework with two weight-generating networks (Itp1, Itp2) and a residual cut network handles the bidirectional mapping between original and moving meshes, and the interpolation module is pretrained before joint training.

## Results

On the 2-D Burgers' equation, MM-PDE achieves the lowest MSE of 1.04e-05, outperforming GNN (3.10e-05), FNO (1.18e-05), and LAMP (1.61e-05), with a comparable inference time of 0.52s. On the flow-around-a-cylinder problem (a real Navier-Stokes system on an irregular triangular mesh), MM-PDE achieves MSE 0.0846, substantially beating GNN (0.2892), Geo-FNO (0.2844), and LAMP (0.4040). On the 3-D Gray-Scott equation, MM-PDE (1.852e-04) also outperforms GNN (2.106e-04), confirming scalability to 3-D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-PDE consistently outperforms all five baselines across both primary benchmarks. Against the GNN baseline (Brandstetter et al., 2022), MM-PDE reduces MSE by ~3× on Burgers' (1.04e-05 vs 3.10e-05) and ~3.4× on the cylinder flow (0.0846 vs 0.2892), and this gain is not attributable to model size since a 'bigger GNN' with comparable parameters only reaches 1.19e-05 and 0.1548 respectively. Against FNO (Li et al., 2020), MM-PDE achieves a modest but consistent improvement on Burgers' (1.04e-05 vs 1.18e-05), while FNO is inapplicable on the irregular triangular mesh of the cylinder problem. Against LAMP (Wu et al., 2023), which uses reinforcement learning for h-adaptive mesh refinement, MM-PDE wins by a large margin on both tasks (1.04e-05 vs 1.61e-05; 0.0846 vs 0.4040) and is ~2.8× faster in inference. Against Geo-FNO (Li et al., 2022) on the cylinder flow, MM-PDE achieves 0.0846 vs 0.2844, a 3.4× improvement. Finally, when combined with Mesh Graph Nets (Pfaff et al., 2020) as the GNN backbone, MM-PDE(MGN) still improves over standalone MGN (2.13e-05 vs 2.46e-05), demonstrating the mesh-mover component's generality across GNN architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the moving mesh / r-adaptation literature rooted in optimal transport and the Monge-Ampère equation (Huang & Russell, 2010), (2) neural operator architectures for PDE simulation, particularly DeepONet's encoder-decoder design (Lu et al., 2021) and MP-PDE's graph message-passing solver (Brandstetter et al., 2022), and (3) recent attempts to learn adaptive meshes with deep learning, including h-adaptation via reinforcement learning (LAMP, Wu et al., 2023) and supervised mesh-movement networks (M2N, Song et al., 2022). The key departure is that DMM trains a neural operator to solve the MA equation in a data-free, physics-informed manner—requiring no precomputed optimal mesh labels—and that MM-PDE embeds the resulting moving mesh into a dual-branch GNN with learnable interpolation, rather than altering mesh topology as h-adaptation methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive optimal-mesh supervision and fixing mesh topology, this work removes two major practical barriers to adaptive-mesh neural PDE solvers, making the approach more broadly applicable to dynamic, multi-scale systems. The theoretical guarantee that the learned monitor function minimizes the interpolation error bound provides a principled foundation absent in prior data-driven mesh methods. Open questions remain: the monitor function depends only on the current state and may not anticipate future dynamics (the authors suggest a surrogate model as a remedy), DMM training is memory-intensive (73 GB for the cylinder problem), and the two-stage training pipeline (DMM first, then MM-PDE) adds practical complexity. Extending the approach to truly complex 3D geometries and longer rollouts will be important next steps.
