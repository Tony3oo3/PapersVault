## Problem

Safety mechanisms for text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., concept removal via fine-tuning, negative prompts, or safety checkers) have been evaluated primarily on fixed prompt sets, leaving their robustness against adversarially crafted prompts largely unexplored. Existing red-teaming approaches either rely on manual prompt selection (not scalable) or require white-box access to the target model, making them impractical for evaluating deployed online services. This paper asks how reliable these safety mechanisms truly are when confronted with systematically generated problematic prompts.

## Input / Output

Ring-A-Bell takes a target sensitive concept (e.g., nudity, violence) and a set of N semantically similar prompt pairs that differ only in that concept, along with a base prompt to be transformed. It outputs discrete adversarial text prompts of tunable length K (16–77 tokens) that, when submitted to a T2I model, elicit generation of the target inappropriate content. The entire pipeline runs offline using only a CLIP text encoder (ViT-L/14), requiring no access to the target diffusion model or its parameters, and the generated prompts can be deployed against any online service or local model.

## Method

Ring-A-Bell is a model-agnostic, prompt-based red-teaming framework with three stages. First, it extracts an empirical concept representation ĉ by averaging pairwise differences of CLIP text-encoder embeddings over N prompt pairs that are semantically similar but contrast in the target concept, yielding a context-independent encoding of the sensitive notion. Second, it constructs a continuous target embedding by adding η·ĉ to the CLIP embedding of a base prompt, effectively infusing the target concept at a tunable strength. Third, it solves a discrete optimization problem—finding a text prompt of length K whose CLIP embedding is closest to the continuous target—using a genetic algorithm over the token vocabulary. The key insight is that safety mechanisms (whether detection-based filters or removal-based fine-tuning) may fail to eliminate implicit text-concept associations, so prompts that encode the concept indirectly without using explicit sensitive words can bypass these defenses. The framework is entirely offline and black-box with respect to the target model, requiring only a general text encoder.

## Results

The primary metric is Attack Success Rate (ASR), measuring the proportion of prompts that successfully generate inappropriate images. For nudity, Ring-A-Bell achieves 93.68% ASR on base Stable Diffusion (vs. 52.63% for original prompts and 51.58% for QF-Attack), and Ring-A-Bell-Union reaches 97.89%. For violence, Ring-A-Bell achieves 96.4% on SD (vs. 60.4% original, 62% QF-Attack), with Ring-A-Bell-Union reaching 99.6%. Even with a safety checker deployed, Ring-A-Bell-Union maintains 49.47% ASR for nudity and 99.2% for violence on SD, demonstrating that safety checkers are far less effective against violence detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is QF-Attack (Zhuang et al., 2023), a query-free adversarial prompt method that minimizes cosine distance in CLIP embedding space. Ring-A-Bell decisively outperforms QF-Attack across nearly all concept removal methods and both concepts. For nudity without a safety checker, Ring-A-Bell achieves 93.68% ASR on SD versus 51.58% for QF-Attack (a 42-point gap), and the advantage is even more dramatic on ESD (35.79% vs. 6.32%) and SLD-Medium (91.58% vs. 28.42%). For violence, Ring-A-Bell reaches 96.4% on SD versus 62% for QF-Attack, and 80% on SD-NP versus 24.8%. The Ring-A-Bell-Union variant further widens the gap, reaching 99.6% (violence) and 97.89% (nudity) on SD. The paper also notes that P4D (Chin et al., 2023) is not included as a baseline because it requires white-box access to the target model and cannot be applied to online T2I services.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of red-teaming T2I diffusion models and prompt engineering, departing from two prior lines of work. Rando et al. (2022) demonstrated that Stable Diffusion's safety filter can be evaded through manual prompt manipulation, while the concurrent P4D (Chin et al., 2023) formalized prompt-based red-teaming but requires white-box access to the target model. Ring-A-Bell carves new ground by showing that a purely text-encoder-based (CLIP) pipeline, combined with genetic-algorithm discrete optimization, can generate effective adversarial prompts entirely offline and model-agnostically, eliminating the need for any knowledge of the target diffusion model's architecture or parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that Ring-A-Bell increases attack success rates by over 30% across nearly all state-of-the-art concept removal methods (and reaches near-100% ASR on SLD-Medium and CA under the union setting) provides concrete evidence that deployed safety mechanisms are far less robust than their evaluations suggest. The model-agnostic, offline nature of the tool makes it practically deployable by safety teams before model release, shifting the red-teaming paradigm from reactive to proactive. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond nudity and violence to other sensitive concepts (e.g., copyrighted styles, political content), whether the reliance on specific detectors (NudeNet, Q16) limits the validity of the ASR metric, and whether the safety mechanisms can be redesigned to resist the implicit text-concept associations that Ring-A-Bell exploits.
