## Problem

Standard data augmentations (rotations, flips, color jitter) cannot alter high-level semantic attributes of images, such as object species, design, or texture, limiting the diversity they introduce. Prior work using generative models for augmentation either requires millions of training images to fine-tune the full model or relies on descriptive text prompts that fail for fine-grain or novel concepts outside the diffusion model's vocabulary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240583/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of labelled real images (512×512 RGB) per class, a pretrained Stable Diffusion v1-4 checkpoint, and a few examples per class to learn class-specific pseudo-prompt embeddings via Textual Inversion. It produces M synthetic augmentations per real image (M=10 by default, 50 for the Leafy Spurge dataset) at 512×512 resolution, which are mixed with real images at a configurable ratio (α=0.5) during downstream classifier training. The final output is a fine-tuned ResNet50 (or DeiT) classifier trained on the real-and-synthetic mixture.

## Method

DA-Fusion combines two mechanisms: (1) Textual Inversion to learn a single pseudo-prompt token per class from a handful of labelled images, inserted into the prompt as 'a photo of a <w_i>', which adapts the diffusion model to novel or fine-grain concepts without fine-tuning the full network; and (2) SDEdit-based image editing, where each real image is noised and spliced into the reverse diffusion process at a randomly sampled timestep t₀ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}, then denoised under the guidance of the class-specific pseudo-prompt to produce a semantically modified variant. The randomization of t₀ controls the extent of modification (lower t₀ preserves more of the original image), analogous to randomizing rotation angles in standard augmentation. During training, each batch position independently samples a real or synthetic image with probability (1−α) and α respectively, balancing the two sources. This design makes the method universal across concept novelty levels, controllable via t₀ and α, and requires no descriptive prompt engineering.

## Results

DA-Fusion improves few-shot classification accuracy by +5 to +15 percentage points across all seven benchmark domains (Caltech101, Flowers102, FGVC Aircraft, Stanford Cars, COCO, PASCAL VOC, Leafy Spurge) compared to the RandAugment baseline. When stratified by concept novelty, normalized gains are 12.8% for common concepts, 24.2% for fine-grain concepts, and 20.8% for concepts unseen by Stable Diffusion. Under data-centric leakage prevention, gains reach up to +10 percentage points on PASCAL VOC and COCO; under the stronger model-centric defense (concept erasure), gains are up to +5 percentage points. On the full Leafy Spurge dataset, DA-Fusion Specific yields a 1.2% accuracy gain over baseline augmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA-Fusion consistently outperforms RandAugment (Cubuk et al., 2020) across all seven datasets, with the largest margins in fine-grain domains like Flowers102 and FGVC Aircraft where geometric/color transforms provide minimal semantic diversity. Against Real Guidance (He et al., 2022), which uses SDEdit with a descriptive text prompt, DA-Fusion wins in every domain; Real Guidance performs comparably to the RandAugment baseline on fine-grain and novel concepts because descriptive prompts fail for hard-to-describe classes (e.g., 'giant white arum lily' or 'leafy spurge'), whereas DA-Fusion's learned pseudo-prompts (Textual Inversion tokens) bypass this limitation. In the model-centric leakage prevention setting (where class knowledge is erased from Stable Diffusion weights), Real Guidance drops to baseline level, confirming its gains partly stem from the class name in the prompt, while DA-Fusion retains its advantage. Against the stronger CutMix baseline (Yun et al., 2019) in Appendix E, DA-Fusion still improves on the PASCAL task, demonstrating gains over more aggressive augmentation strategies. Randomizing the SDEdit insertion position (k=4 intensities) adds a further 51% relative improvement over the fixed-intensity variant, highlighting the importance of diversity in augmentation strength.

## Relation to Prior Work

DA-Fusion combines two existing mechanisms—SDEdit (Meng et al., 2022) for splicing real images into the reverse diffusion process, and Textual Inversion (Gal et al., 2022) for learning class-specific pseudo-prompts—into a unified data augmentation pipeline. It departs from He et al. (2022), who use SDEdit with hand-written descriptive prompts, by replacing those prompts with learned embeddings that generalize to concepts outside the diffusion model's vocabulary. Compared to Azizi et al. (2023), which fine-tunes the entire diffusion model on millions of images, DA-Fusion adapts only a handful of text-encoder tokens per class, preserving the model's generative capacity for other concepts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic analysis of synthetic-data leakage from internet-scale diffusion models in a few-shot classification setting, offering both model-centric (concept erasure via ESD) and data-centric (prompt removal) defenses and showing that gains are partly attributable to the model's prior knowledge. The consistent finding that learned pseudo-prompts outperform descriptive prompts for fine-grain and novel concepts (24.2% vs. near-baseline for Real Guidance) gives practitioners a clear, low-cost recipe for semantic augmentation without prompt engineering. Open questions include fine-grained control over which attributes to modify, temporal consistency for video/RL settings, and whether the approach scales to detection or segmentation tasks.
