## Problem

Existing diffusion-based time series methods are either restricted to task-specific settings (imputation, forecasting) or limited to univariate/short sequences, and they typically rely on autoregressive RNN backbones that suffer from error accumulation and slow inference. More fundamentally, the gradual noising process in diffusion destroys the independent temporal components (trend, seasonality, local idiosyncrasy) without any inductive bias to preserve them, making generated series lack interpretability and struggle with periodic patterns.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes a standard Gaussian noise vector of shape τ×d×T (where τ is sequence length, d is feature dimension, T is total diffusion steps) and produces a full multivariate time series sample along with its disentangled trend, seasonality, and residual components. For conditional tasks (imputation or forecasting), the input is a partial time series with observed values at known positions; the model fills in missing or future values while preserving the observed portions, operating on sequences of 48 time steps with geometrically distributed missing blocks for imputation.

## Method

Diffusion-TS uses a non-autoregressive encoder-decoder transformer where the decoder is restructured as a deep decomposition architecture: each decoder block contains a transformer block, a feed-forward network, and interpretable layers that synthesize trend via a low-degree polynomial regressor (degree p=3) and seasonality via Fourier synthetic layers that select the top-K dominant frequency components from the DFT of intermediate activations. The key training innovation is predicting the clean signal x₀ directly (rather than noise ε) at each diffusion step, combined with a dual loss that applies both time-domain MSE and frequency-domain FFT MSE to enforce accurate reconstruction of both temporal and spectral structure. For conditional generation, a reconstruction-based sampling strategy injects gradient guidance from a target metric (reconstruction error on observed values) into the reverse diffusion process, enabling plug-and-play adaptation to imputation and forecasting without any architectural or parameter changes.

## Results

On the primary unconditional generation benchmark (24-step multivariate time series across 6 datasets), Diffusion-TS achieves the best score on all four metrics (Context-FID, Correlational, Discriminative, and Predictive) for every dataset. For example, on the high-dimensional fMRI dataset (50 features), Diffusion-TS obtains a Context-FID of 0.105 vs. 0.340 for DiffTime and 1.292 for TimeGAN, and a Discriminative Score of 0.167 vs. 0.245 for DiffTime. The paper reports an average ~50% improvement in Discriminative Score over the next-best baseline across all six datasets. On conditional tasks (imputation at 70–90% missing, forecasting on Solar), Diffusion-TS also outperforms CSDI, SSSD, and TLAE, with MSE of 0.73×10⁻³ at 90% missing on MuJoCo vs. 4.84×10⁻³ for CSDI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diffusion-TS outperforms all five unconditional generation baselines (TimeGAN, TimeVAE, Diffwave, DiffTime, Cot-GAN) on every metric across all six datasets, with the largest margins on high-dimensional data (fMRI, Energy, MuJoCo). The closest competitor is DiffTime, which ties Diffusion-TS on Sines Context-FID (0.006) and Predictive Score (0.093) but is substantially worse on all other datasets and metrics. For conditional generation, Diffusion-TS (with reconstruction-guided sampling) beats CSDI and SSSD on MuJoCo imputation at 80% and 90% missing ratios (MSE 0.43 vs. 0.61 and 1.00 at 80%; 0.73 vs. 4.84 and 1.90 at 90%), and on Solar forecasting (MSE 3.75×10² vs. 9.0×10² for CSDI and 5.03×10² for SSSD). TLAE (Nguyen & Quanz, 2021) is also outperformed on Solar forecasting (6.8×10² vs. 3.75×10²). The replace-based variant (Diffusion-TS-R) matches CSDI at low missingness but degrades at 75–90% missing, confirming the benefit of the reconstruction-guided strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DDPMs (Ho et al., 2020) to multivariate time series generation by replacing the typical RNN backbone used in prior diffusion-based time series work (Lim et al., 2023; Das et al., 2023) with an encoder-decoder transformer whose decoder explicitly performs seasonal-trend decomposition, drawing on the N-BEATS architecture (Oreshkin et al., 2020) and classical STL decomposition (Cleveland et al., 1990). It departs from the standard ε-prediction parameterization by training the network to directly reconstruct x₀, augmented with a Fourier-domain loss term inspired by Hypertime (Fons et al., 2022). For conditional tasks, it introduces a reconstruction-based sampling strategy that contrasts with the classifier-guided or replace-based approaches in CSDI (Tashiro et al., 2021) and SSSD (Alcaraz & Strodthoff, 2022), enabling plug-and-play adaptation without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that embedding interpretable decomposition into the denoising network of a diffusion model yields explainable generative outputs without sacrificing quality, addressing a gap where prior diffusion-based time series methods lacked both interpretability and explicit periodicity inductive bias. The reconstruction-based conditional sampling is a practical contribution: it allows a single pre-trained unconditional model to handle imputation and forecasting via gradient guidance at inference time. Key limitations include the high inference cost inherent to iterative DDPM sampling (acknowledged by the authors) and the fact that the interpretability gains are most pronounced on datasets with strong frequency structure (e.g., fMRI), while on smoother data the decomposition adds minimal benefit. Open questions include whether faster samplers (DDIM, consistency models) can preserve the decomposition structure, and whether the approach extends to truly irregularly-sampled or event-driven time series.
