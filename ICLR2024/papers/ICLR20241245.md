## Problem

GNNs achieve strong node classification performance but require abundant high-quality labels that are expensive and error-prone to obtain, while LLMs can annotate without labels but cannot capture graph structure and incur prohibitive inference costs on large-scale graphs. This paper asks whether LLMs can serve as zero-shot annotators for a small, strategically selected subset of nodes to train a GNN that then predicts labels for the remaining nodes, eliminating the need for any ground truth labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241245/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text-attributed graph (nodes with raw text descriptions encoded via SentenceBERT into embeddings, plus an adjacency matrix) and a set of class names as input, requiring no ground truth labels. It produces class labels for all nodes in the graph. The LLM annotation step operates only on a small budget of selected nodes (20× number of classes), keeping total annotation cost under $1 even on the 2.4M-node OGBN-PRODUCTS dataset, while the GNN handles the remaining nodes efficiently.

## Method

LLM-GNN is a four-stage pipeline: (1) difficulty-aware active node selection combines a C-Density heuristic (inverse distance to the nearest k-means cluster center, proxying annotation ease) with traditional graph active learning scores (e.g., FeatProp, AGE, RIM) via scale-invariant ranking aggregation; (2) confidence-aware annotation uses a zero-shot hybrid prompt (TopK + multi-query consistency) to elicit both a label and a calibrated confidence score from GPT-3.5-turbo; (3) optional post-filtering iteratively removes low-confidence nodes while preserving label diversity via a change-of-entropy (COE) score, balancing confidence, COE, and C-Density in a ranking-aggregated filter score; (4) a GCN is trained on the filtered pseudo-labeled nodes using weighted cross-entropy loss where LLM confidence scores serve as per-sample weights, then predicts labels for all remaining nodes.

## Results

On the large-scale OGBN-PRODUCTS dataset (2.4M nodes), LLM-GNN achieves 74.91% node classification accuracy at a cost of under $1 (specifically $0.74), matching the performance of LLMs-as-Predictors (75.33%) which costs $1,572 — a 2,124× cost reduction. On OGBN-ARXIV, LLM-GNN reaches 66.32% accuracy at $0.63. The best-performing configuration (PS-FeatProp-W) achieves 76.23% on CORA, 78.84% on PUBMED, and 69.06% on CITESEER, consistently outperforming traditional active learning baselines by 2–5 percentage points on small-scale datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot graph methods (SES, TAG-Z) and the text classifier BART-large-MNLI, LLM-GNN achieves a decisive margin: 74.91% vs. 47.08% (TAG-Z) and 28.8% (BART) on OGBN-PRODUCTS, and 66.32% vs. 37.08% (TAG-Z) on OGBN-ARXIV. Compared to LLMs-as-Predictors (Chen et al., 2023), LLM-GNN trades a small accuracy gap (74.91% vs. 75.33% on OGBN-PRODUCTS; 66.32% vs. 73.33% on OGBN-ARXIV) for a dramatic cost reduction of ~2,124× on PRODUCTS and ~125× on ARXIV. Among traditional graph active learning baselines, the PS-FeatProp-W variant of LLM-GNN outperforms FeatProp (Wu et al., 2019) on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., 76.23% vs. 72.82% on CORA, 78.84% vs. 73.90% on PUBMED) while maintaining comparable scalability. DA-AGE-W (Cai et al., 2017) improves over vanilla AGE on CORA (74.96% vs. 69.15%) and WIKICS (59.19% vs. 55.51%) but underperforms on PUBMED (65.85% vs. 74.55%), indicating that difficulty-aware selection helps most when annotation quality is the bottleneck. DA-RIM-W (Zhang et al., 2021) beats RIM on CORA (74.73% vs. 69.86%) and WIKICS (68.22% vs. 66.72%) but loses on CITESEER (60.80% vs. 63.44%), and both RIM variants cannot scale to OGBN-ARXIV or OGBN-PRODUCTS due to prohibitive runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of graph active learning (AGE, RIM, FeatProp, GraphPart), LLMs for graph tasks (Chen et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2023a), and LLMs as data annotators (Bansal & Sharma 2023, Gilardi et al. 2023). It departs from traditional graph active learning, which assumes ground truth labels are available but expensive to obtain, by introducing a fully label-free setting where LLMs generate all training annotations. The key methodological departure is the C-Density heuristic (nodes near cluster centers receive higher-quality LLM annotations) and confidence-based post-filtering, which integrate annotation quality into the node selection criterion alongside traditional diversity and representativeness measures. Unlike LLMs-as-Predictors (Chen et al. 2023), which queries LLMs for every node, LLM-GNN uses LLMs only on a small selected subset and delegates the remaining predictions to a trained GCN, trading a small accuracy gap for a ~2000× cost reduction on OGBN-PRODUCTS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-$1 cost to achieve 74.9% accuracy on OGBN-PRODUCTS (2.4M nodes) makes label-free node classification practically viable for large-scale industrial graphs where human annotation is infeasible. The noise transition matrix analysis revealing asymmetric, class-dependent LLM annotation errors (e.g., 'distributed computing architecture' flipping to 'web technology' but not vice versa) provides important guidance for downstream model design, as these errors behave fundamentally differently from the uniform synthetic noise assumed in prior noisy-label literature. Open questions include whether structure-aware prompting can close the remaining gap between LLM annotations and ground truth, whether the framework extends to non-text-attributed graphs, and whether future LLMs with better structural reasoning will reduce the need for the GNN component. A notable limitation is that the method still requires knowing the set of class names a priori.
