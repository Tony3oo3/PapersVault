## Problem

Existing MTPP methods model interevent dependencies as a monolithic intensity function, making it difficult to understand how individual events influence the overall stochastic process over time. Additionally, intensity-based approaches require expensive sequential computation and additional Monte Carlo sampling for integrals (survival rates, expected event times) that lack closed-form solutions, leading to exponential computational cost during inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241110/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of (time, mark) event pairs, where marks are discrete event types (e.g., 984 categories in Reddit, 3 in Retweet). Beyond the standard next-event prediction, Dec-ODE outputs per-event influence trajectories μ(t;eᵢ) and mark-influence functions f̂(k|t,eᵢ) that can be selectively combined for any conditioning history, enabling parallel computation of likelihood, survival rate, and expected arrival time in a single ODE solve rather than separate sampling passes.

## Method

Dec-ODE assigns each event eᵢ an independent hidden state h(t;eᵢ) ∈ ℝᵈ initialized by a mark-dependent embedding Wₑ(kᵢ), then propagates it forward in time via a shared Neural ODE γ(h(t;eᵢ), t, kᵢ; θ). The hidden states are decoded into per-event influence functions μ(t;eᵢ) and f̂(k|t,eᵢ), which are aggregated via linear summation (softplus for ground intensity, softmax for mark probability) to reconstruct the full MTPP. A key computational insight is that the compensator Λ_g(t), the CDF F(t), and E[t] are solved simultaneously with the hidden-state ODE as an augmented multi-dimensional differential equation, eliminating the need for separate Monte Carlo integration. Because each event's trajectory is independent, all hidden states are propagated in parallel as a single multi-dimensional ODE, reducing per-iteration training time by 2–5× compared to sequential IVP solving.

## Results

Dec-ODE achieves the best or second-best RMSE on 4 out of 5 datasets (e.g., 0.467 on MOOC, 0.934 on Reddit, 0.810 on MIMIC-II), and the best mark-prediction accuracy on 3 out of 5 datasets (42.08% on MOOC, 60.17% on Retweet, 85.06% on MIMIC-II). On NLL, ANHP is marginally better on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., 1.203 vs. 1.367 on Reddit; −3.134 vs. −2.897 on Retweet), while IFL leads on MOOC (−2.895 vs. −2.289). The parallel training scheme reduces per-iteration time by 2–5× compared to sequential IVP solving (e.g., 15.5 s vs. 78.7 s on Reddit).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dec-ODE outperforms all four baselines on RMSE in 4 of 5 datasets, with the largest margin on Reddit (0.934 vs. THP's 6.151) and MIMIC-II (0.810 vs. THP's >10); on Stack Overflow it essentially ties RMTPP (1.018 vs. 1.017). For mark-prediction accuracy, Dec-ODE leads on MOOC (42.08% vs. next-best IFL at 32.30%), Retweet (60.17% vs. THP at 60.68%), and MIMIC-II (85.06% vs. THP at 85.98%), but trails ANHP on Reddit (62.32% vs. 63.45%) and Stack Overflow (55.58% vs. 56.80%). On NLL, ANHP is the strongest competitor, winning on Reddit (1.203 vs. 1.367), Retweet (−3.134 vs. −2.897), and Stack Overflow (1.873 vs. 2.063), while IFL dominates MOOC (−2.895 vs. −2.289) and MIMIC-II (0.939 vs. 1.354). THP is consistently the weakest baseline, particularly on RMSE where it fails to converge on Reddit and MIMIC-II. The authors note that THP's thinning algorithm could not correctly sample from its intensity function on Reddit, inflating its RMSE to 6.151.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dec-ODE generalizes the Hawkes Process structure—where the intensity is a sum of individual event excitations—by replacing the fixed kernel with Neural ODE-driven continuous dynamics for each event's influence. It departs from prior neural MTPP methods (RMTPP, THP, ANHP) that encode the entire event history into a single RNN/Transformer state, instead maintaining separate hidden state trajectories per event that are decoded and aggregated only at the output stage. The IVP solving technique with varying time intervals is adopted from Chen et al. (2021), and the parallel computation scheme exploits the linear aggregation to avoid sequential propagation through the full event sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled formulation provides per-event interpretability (e.g., visualizing how a large-follower post decays more slowly than a small-follower post) that black-box neural MTPP methods cannot offer, and the parallel ODE solving yields 2–5× training speedups. However, the linear variant can only model excitatory effects, and the non-linear extension that permits inhibition catastrophically fails on Reddit (NLL degrades from 0.52 to 18.26), revealing an open question about how to robustly capture both excitatory and inhibitory dynamics without collapsing the intensity to zero. The framework's potential for counterfactual and out-of-distribution analysis remains largely unexplored beyond the imputation experiment.
