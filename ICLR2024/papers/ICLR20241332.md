## Problem

Existing Graph Lottery Ticket (GLT) methods identify sparse subgraphs and subnetworks through iterative multi-round pruning, which is computationally expensive, and they largely ignore the structural information of the graph. The paper identifies that low-degree edges are critical for GNN generalization and spectral stability (supported by Laplacian energy analysis and theoretical bounds from Tang & Liu, 2023), yet prior edge sparsification strategies do not explicitly account for this degree-based structural importance.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph (adjacency matrix A and node feature matrix X), a GNN architecture f_Θ, and target sparsity ratios p_g and p_θ for edges and parameters respectively. It outputs a sparse subgraph A' (with the smallest-scoring edges removed) and a sparse subnetwork Θ' (with exactly ⌈(1−p_θ)d⌉ non-zero parameters). Notably, the edge pruning step uses only degree statistics from the adjacency matrix without consuming node features, and both sparsification steps are completed within a single training run rather than multiple iterative rounds.

## Method

TEDDY performs one-shot edge sparsification by constructing edge-wise scores from multi-level degree information: it defines a monotonically decreasing node score g(v)=1/√deg(v), computes neighbor-averaged scores via sparse matrix multiplication (an edge-centric message-passing step), and forms the final edge score matrix T_edge as the outer product of the resulting node vectors. Edges with the smallest scores (corresponding to high-degree or redundant multi-hop pathways) are removed in a single shot. For parameter sparsification, TEDDY applies projected gradient descent onto the ℓ₀ ball during a single training run, enforcing exactly h non-zero weights at each step without iterative pruning rounds. A knowledge distillation loss (KL divergence between sparse and dense GNN logits) is added to the cross-entropy objective to preserve the information captured by the full graph. The entire pipeline—pretraining on the dense graph, one-shot edge removal, and single-training parameter sparsification with distillation—requires no iterative pruning schedule.

## Results

On small/medium-scale benchmarks (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed) with GCN/GAT/GIN, TEDDY improves over vanilla GNN accuracy in more than half of all settings across sparsity levels, with a standout 5.4% gain (73.20% vs. 67.8%) for GIN on Citeseer at 43.12% graph sparsity. At maximum graph sparsity, TEDDY outperforms the best baseline by 12.8%–20.4%. On large-scale Reddit with SAGE, TEDDY achieves 82.72% accuracy at 40% graph sparsity, surpassing vanilla SAGE by 7.4% and UGS by 12.4%, while reducing MACs by 5.5×. Under extreme sparsity (up to 87% graph + weight sparsity with GIN), TEDDY still exceeds vanilla accuracy on all datasets except one configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEDDY is compared against three baselines: UGS (Chen et al., 2021), WD-GLT (Hui et al., 2023), and DropEdge/random pruning (Rong et al., 2019). TEDDY consistently outperforms both UGS and WD-GLT across all datasets and architectures, with the gap widening at higher sparsity (12.8%–20.4% at maximum graph sparsity). On large-scale Arxiv, TEDDY identifies GLT up to 33.66% (GCN) and 45.96% (SAGE) graph sparsity where UGS fails entirely; on Reddit, TEDDY surpasses UGS by 12.4% at 40% sparsity. WD-GLT is infeasible on large-scale datasets (OOM due to Sinkhorn iterations) and is 20–70× slower on small-scale ones. Against DropEdge (random edge dropping), TEDDY also wins across all GNNs on all three citation datasets, confirming that degree-aware structural pruning is essential. In terms of efficiency, TEDDY's one-shot PGD on the ℓ₀ ball is roughly 2× faster than UGS's iterative pruning, with the gap growing on larger graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEDDY extends the Graph Lottery Ticket (GLT) framework introduced by Chen et al. (2021), which established that sparse subgraphs and subnetworks can be jointly identified from dense GNNs. It departs from prior GLT methods (UGS, WD-GLT) in two key ways: (1) replacing learned differentiable masks with a one-shot, degree-based edge scoring mechanism that explicitly encodes multi-hop structural information via an edge-centric message-passing formulation, and (2) substituting iterative parameter pruning with a single-pass projected gradient descent on the ℓ₀ ball. The method is further motivated by the theoretical generalization bounds of Tang & Liu (2023), which show that GNN generalization is governed primarily by graph structure (specifically the degree ratio) rather than node features or parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TEDDY demonstrates that structural information alone—without any node feature awareness—can outperform feature-aware iterative pruning methods, challenging the implicit assumption in prior GLT work that learned masks must incorporate feature information. The one-shot nature of both edge and parameter sparsification yields substantial wall-clock savings (2–3× faster than UGS, orders of magnitude faster than WD-GLT), making GLT identification practical for large-scale graphs. Open questions include whether the degree-based scoring generalizes to heterogeneous or directed graphs, whether incorporating node features (acknowledged as future work) would further improve results, and whether the theoretical properties of the edge-centric MP scoring can be formally characterized.
