## Problem

Pose-guided person image synthesis requires generating a photorealistic image of a person under a new pose from a single source image, but the pose mismatch between source and target makes this an inherently unaligned image-to-image task. Existing GAN, VAE, and flow-based methods produce distorted textures and artifacts under large deformations or occlusions, while recent single-stage diffusion approaches still lack dense source-target correspondence, leading to less realistic results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240414/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB person image and a target pose skeleton (extracted via OpenPose) as input, and produces a synthesized RGB person image at the target pose. Experiments use 256×176 and 512×352 resolutions on DeepFashion and 128×64 on Market-1501. Internally, the pipeline consumes CLIP image embeddings, DINOv2 fine-grained features, and pose coordinate encodings, operating in the latent space of Stable Diffusion V2.1 for the second and third stages.

## Method

PCDMs decomposes synthesis into three progressive conditional diffusion stages. Stage 1 (Prior) is a 20-block transformer that predicts the unnoised CLIP embedding of the target image from source image embedding and source/target pose coordinates, following the unCLIP formulation. Stage 2 (Inpainting) concatenates source/target images, poses, and a mask along the width dimension, injects the Stage-1 global features into the timestep embedding, and uses a ControlNet-style pose encoder plus frozen DINOv2 source features to generate a coarse-grained target image with dense source-target alignment. Stage 3 (Refining) conditions on the coarse image (channel-concatenated with the noisy latent) and infuses DINOv2 source texture features via cross-attention to restore fine details. All stages are trained with standard denoising MSE loss and classifier-free guidance (10% condition dropout), using AdamW at 1e-4 on 8 V100 GPUs.

## Results

On DeepFashion (512×352), PCDMs achieves SSIM 0.7601, LPIPS 0.1475, and FID 7.5519, outperforming all GAN- and flow-based baselines on SSIM and LPIPS. On Market-1501 (128×64), PCDMs attains the best scores across all three metrics (SSIM 0.3169, LPIPS 0.2238, FID 13.897), surpassing the strongest diffusion baseline PIDM (SSIM 0.3054, LPIPS 0.2415, FID 14.451). The only metric where PCDMs trails is FID on DeepFashion, where PIDM achieves 5.8365 vs. PCDMs' 7.5519, though PCDMs leads on both SSIM and LPIPS in that setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCDMs outperforms all GAN-based baselines (Def-GAN, PATN, ADGAN) and flow-based baselines (GFLA, DPTN) by a wide margin on both SSIM and LPIPS across all dataset configurations. Against the strongest flow-based method CASD (SSIM 0.7248, LPIPS 0.1936 on DeepFashion 256×176), PCDMs improves SSIM by ~2% and LPIPS by ~0.057. Compared to the closest diffusion-based competitor PIDM, PCDMs wins on SSIM and LPIPS in all three settings (e.g., SSIM 0.7601 vs. 0.7419, LPIPS 0.1475 vs. 0.1768 on DeepFashion 512×352) but trails on FID (7.552 vs. 5.837), suggesting PIDM produces slightly more diverse samples while PCDMs produces more structurally faithful and perceptually similar outputs. On Market-1501, PCDMs achieves the best score on all three metrics, beating PIDM by 0.0115 SSIM, 0.0177 LPIPS, and 0.554 FID. The ablation (PCDMs w/o Refining vs. full PCDMs) confirms the refining stage contributes consistent gains of ~0.007 SSIM and ~0.01 LPIPS, while the prior stage is critical for pose alignment in extreme-pose scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-model line of work on pose-guided person image synthesis (PIDM, Bhunia et al. 2023) by decomposing the single-model generation into a three-stage progressive pipeline: a CLIP-embedding prior model (following unCLIP's direct-embedding prediction), an inpainting stage with ControlNet-style pose encoding and DINOv2 fine-grained features, and a texture-refining stage. Conceptually, it departs from prior single-step or single-diffusion approaches by explicitly separating global feature prediction, dense correspondence establishment, and local texture restoration into distinct conditional diffusion models, each operating on a progressively more refined representation. The base architecture for stages 2 and 3 leverages pretrained Stable Diffusion V2.1, positioning the work within the latent diffusion paradigm rather than pixel-space diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive decomposition directly addresses the core difficulty of pose-guided synthesis—the lack of dense correspondence between source and target under large pose changes—and the ablation convincingly shows that the prior stage is critical for structural coherence. The finding that the refining model can be plugged into other SOTA methods (Def-GAN, GFLA, PIDM, etc.) to improve their outputs suggests a broadly useful component for the person-image-synthesis community. However, the three-model pipeline increases inference cost and latency, and the paper acknowledges this as a key limitation; whether the stages can be unified or distilled into a single efficient model remains an open question. The FID on DeepFashion 256×176 is actually worse than PIDM's, indicating the gains are primarily in perceptual fidelity (LPIPS, SSIM) and user preference rather than distributional realism.
