## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods are designed for uni-modal tasks and rely on updating normalization layers to bridge domain gaps, but they overlook a critical multi-modal challenge: when distribution shifts corrupt one modality more than another, information discrepancies arise between modalities (reliability bias), causing the model to over-weight the degraded modality during cross-modal fusion. Simply minimizing entropy on all or confident predictions fails in this setting because noisy predictions from the corrupted modality can dominate the adaptation process, leading to overfitting or underfitting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 10-second video clips (10 sampled frames fed into a visual Transformer encoder) and corresponding 10-second audio waveforms (converted to spectrograms for an audio Transformer encoder), producing a single class label for action recognition or event classification. During test-time adaptation, only the Q, K, V projection parameters of the final cross-modal attention layer are updated (1.8M parameters) using unlabeled test pairs in mini-batches of 64, with a fixed confidence threshold γ = e^{-1} governing the loss. The backbone is CAV-MAE, a contrastive audio-visual masked autoencoder pre-trained on web-scale data and fine-tuned on Kinetics50 or VGGSound.

## Method

READ addresses reliability bias through two coupled mechanisms. First, a self-adaptive attention-based fusion (SAF) module updates the query, key, and value projection matrices within the cross-modal attention layer at test time, enabling the model to dynamically shift attention weight toward the more reliable modality rather than treating both modalities equally as in late fusion or vanilla attention. Second, a confidence-aware loss L_ra = (1/B)Σ p_i·log(eγ/p_i) produces non-monotonic gradients: for high-confidence predictions (p > γ), the gradient magnitude increases with confidence, amplifying their contribution to optimization; for low-confidence predictions (p < γ), the gradient reverses direction and shrinks, preventing noisy predictions from corrupting the adaptation. The final objective combines this loss with a negative-entropy balancing term, and the entire adaptation is performed online per mini-batch using Adam at learning rate 1e-4, updating only the attention fusion layer while keeping all other parameters frozen.

## Results

On the Kinetics50-C benchmark with corrupted video modality (severity 5), READ with self-adaptive attention fusion (SAF) achieves 62.5% average accuracy, outperforming the best baseline EATA (60.1%) by 2.4 points and the source model (59.9%) by 2.6 points. With late fusion, READ (Dyn. LN) reaches 54.8% average, surpassing MM-TTA (54.5%) and Tent (49.4%). On VGGSound-C with corrupted video, READ(SAF) achieves 56.9% average versus 56.2% for EATA and 56.0% for the source model. Under audio corruption on VGGSound-C, READ(SAF) reaches 32.4% average, substantially outperforming Tent (4.5%) and MM-TTA (7.6%) which collapse under the reliability bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

READ consistently outperforms all four TTA baselines (Tent, MM-TTA, EATA, SAR) across both fusion paradigms and both benchmarks. On Kinetics50-C with video corruption (severity 5), READ(SAF) achieves 62.5% average accuracy, beating EATA (60.1%) by 2.4 points and Tent (59.4%) by 3.1 points; the gap widens under severe corruptions like Fog (75.5% vs. 75.2% for EATA) and Gaussian noise (49.4% vs. 46.8% for EATA). Under audio corruption on VGGSound-C, the advantage is dramatic: READ(SAF) reaches 32.4% average while Tent collapses to 4.5% and MM-TTA to 7.6%, indicating that normalization-layer-only adaptation fails catastrophically when the representative modality is biased. Compared to QMF (Zhang et al., 2023), which modifies the training process to estimate modality confidence, READ achieves superior robustness at test time without any training-time changes. READ also demonstrates efficiency advantages, completing adaptation in 134.1 seconds versus 207.6–286.1 seconds for LN-updating baselines despite having more learnable parameters (1.8M vs. 0.2M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant TTA paradigm of updating normalization layers (Tent, EATA, SAR) by proposing to modulate cross-modal attention fusion layers instead, arguing that LN updating cannot fully reconcile intra-modal distribution shifts that create inter-modal reliability bias. It also distinguishes itself from imbalanced multi-modal learning work (Peng et al. 2022, Zhang et al. 2023/QMF) that handles modality imbalance during source-domain training, by addressing the same imbalance at test time without labeled data. The confidence-aware loss extends entropy minimization with a non-monotonic gradient structure, and the CAV-MAE backbone provides the transformer-based multi-modal architecture upon which the self-adaptive attention mechanism operates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'reliability bias' as a distinct failure mode in multi-modal TTA provides a useful diagnostic framework for understanding why standard TTA methods degrade when one modality is corrupted. The two introduced benchmarks (Kinetics50-C, VGGSound-C) with 15 video and 6 audio corruption types across 5 severity levels offer a structured evaluation resource for the community. However, the method is validated only on audio-video pairs with a single backbone (CAV-MAE) and two tasks, leaving open whether the self-adaptive attention paradigm generalizes to more modalities, other architectures, or tasks beyond classification. The dramatic failure of MM-TTA on VGGSound audio corruption (dropping to ~5% accuracy) highlights how fragile existing multi-modal TTA approaches are, motivating further work on robustness guarantees.
