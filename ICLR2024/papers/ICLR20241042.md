## Problem

When training data is large, noisy, or of variable quality, selecting a useful subset is critical for efficiency and performance, yet existing methods are either task-specific, require labels, demand large target sets, or rely on arbitrary data-size hyperparameters without a principled stopping criterion. This paper asks whether a natural information-theoretic objective—minimizing KL divergence between a small target distribution and the selected training set—can be made computationally tractable at scale while remaining task- and domain-agnostic.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large candidate pool G and a small (potentially unlabeled) target set X, both represented as continuous vectors (e.g., 768-d MPNet embeddings for text or normalized 784-d image vectors for FashionMNIST), plus an optional initial set D. It produces a selected subset V ⊆ G along with the per-iteration KL divergence trajectory, which doubles as the natural stopping signal. Crucially, no labels are required on any input, and the target set X can be as small as ~12K examples, making the method applicable when only a modest number of representative examples are available.

## Method

GIO makes the intractable KL-divergence minimization objective scalable through three coupled optimizations. First, a *derivative trick* relaxes the discrete constraint v_i ∈ G to a continuous search: it gradient-descends on the KL divergence functional to find an optimal point v_opt, then snaps to the nearest centroid in G, reducing per-iteration cost from O(|G|·C) to O(S·C) where S is the number of gradient steps. Second, a *quantization-explosion* step clusters both G and X with K-means (e.g., K=1500), runs the iterative selection on centroids, and then maps selected centroids back to their member points in the original data. Third, the paper modifies the Wang et al. (2009) kNN-based KL divergence estimator by averaging over all k = 1…m nearest-neighbor distances, which provably eliminates the '0-gradient problem' where the gradient vanishes for candidate points far from X. The algorithm iteratively adds the centroid closest to v_opt to the selected set D_c and removes it from G_c, terminating when the KL divergence increases—a natural, data-driven stopping criterion that requires no pre-specified data size.

## Results

On WMT14 EN-FR, GIO-selected data (40% of full corpus) achieves 42.2–42.3 BLEU, surpassing the full-data model (41.8 BLEU) by +0.4 to +0.6 BLEU. On EN-DE, GIO reaches 99% of full-model performance (27.9 vs. 28.2 BLEU) with only 60% of the data. In the spelling correction task, GIO selects 73% high-quality synthetic data versus 50% in the full pool, outperforming all baselines. On FashionMNIST, GIO-selected 25% of training data yields 92.2% accuracy versus 91.5% for a random 25% subset and 94.5% for the full set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIO outperforms all three baselines (BM25, self-pruning, and submodular optimization) in 10 out of 12 WMT14 evaluations (EN-FR and EN-DE at 0%, 25%, 50% initialization). The only losses are at 0% initialization on EN-DE, where BM25 scores 24.9 vs. GIO's 24.3 and submodular scores 24.5 vs. 24.3. At 25% and 50% initialization, GIO leads by an average of +0.7 BLEU on WMT14 test and +0.6 BLEU on dev test over all baselines. In the spelling correction task, GIO selects 73% high-quality data compared to 59% (submodular), 55% (BM25), and 54% (pruning), with the lowest KL divergence (224 vs. 241–284). On FashionMNIST, GIO's 25% subset achieves 92.2% accuracy, beating the random 25% baseline (91.5%) by +0.7% while losing only 2.3% to the full model (94.5%), compared to a 3.0% drop for random selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIO builds directly on the kNN-based KL divergence estimator of Wang et al. (2009), modifying it by averaging over all k values to resolve a 0-gradient problem that would otherwise prevent gradient-based optimization. Conceptually, it departs from task-specific heuristics (BM25 retrieval, perplexity-based filtering, submodular optimization) and from active learning by framing data selection as a continuous-space information-theoretic optimization problem solvable via gradient descent on the KL divergence, combined with K-means quantization-explosion for scalability. Unlike similarity search or submodular methods, GIO provides a natural stopping criterion (KL divergence increase) rather than requiring an arbitrary data-size hyperparameter, and unlike data pruning it explicitly targets a user-specified distribution X rather than self-referential criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GIO offers a plug-and-play, label-free data selection method that works across modalities (text, images) and tasks without re-derivation, which is practically valuable given the growing volume of variable-quality synthetic and scraped training data. The finding that minimizing KL divergence between selected and target data correlates strongly with downstream model performance (Spearman 0.83) provides empirical validation of the information-theoretic objective. Open questions include whether gradient descent on the non-convex KL landscape can be replaced by global optimizers (e.g., particle swarm), whether alternative statistical distances (e.g., max density difference) improve performance in gapped distributions, and how to choose X when the true deployment distribution is uncertain. The method's reliance on a good embedding model and the local density assumption for the derivative trick remain practical limitations.
