## Problem

Existing text-to-3D methods that distill from 2D diffusion priors suffer from the 'Janus issue': individual novel views appear plausible but the assembled 3D object exhibits semantic and stylistic inconsistencies across viewpoints. Additionally, these methods struggle to simultaneously achieve the texture fidelity of modern 2D diffusion models and global 3D coherence, as they distill from a fixed 2D distribution that only guarantees per-view plausibility rather than holistic 3D consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240459/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input, first generating a high-quality 2D reference image via a state-of-the-art T2I model (Stable Diffusion or DeepFloyd). The reference image is accompanied by an alpha mask, a predicted depth map, and a normal map (computed via an off-the-shelf single-view estimator) that provide additional geometric supervision. The output is a textured 3D mesh represented as a DMTet (deformable tetrahedral grid) at 128 grid resolution with 512-pixel rendering resolution, initialized from a NeuS implicit surface representation optimized at 64-to-384 resolution via Instant NGP.

## Method

DreamCraft3D decomposes 3D generation into two hierarchical stages. In geometry sculpting, it combines a hybrid SDS loss that simultaneously distills from a 2D diffusion prior (DeepFloyd IF at 64×64 for coarse structure) and a 3D-aware view-conditioned diffusion prior (Zero-1-to-3) to enforce multi-view geometric consistency, supplemented by photometric, mask, depth-correlation, and normal losses with timestep annealing and progressive camera sampling. In texture boosting, it introduces Bootstrapped Score Distillation (BSD): a DreamBooth model is finetuned on Gaussian-noise-augmented multi-view renderings of the scene being optimized, and the 3D mesh and this personalized diffusion prior are alternately optimized in a bootstrapped loop—improved renderings yield a more 3D-consistent prior, which in turn provides increasingly coherent texture gradients. This evolving-prior mechanism replaces the fixed 2D distribution used by SDS/VSD, enabling rich texture detail while preserving view consistency across 360° renderings.

## Results

On a 300-image test benchmark, DreamCraft3D achieves a CLIP score of 0.896, contextual distance of 1.579, PSNR of 31.801, and LPIPS of 0.005, substantially outperforming both image-to-3D baselines on all four metrics. The PSNR improvement is particularly dramatic (31.8 vs. 18.9 for Make-it-3D and 22.8 for Magic123), and LPIPS drops from ~0.053–0.054 to 0.005, indicating far superior perceptual fidelity at the reference view. Total training time is approximately 2.3 hours (1.5 h geometry sculpting + 0.8 h texture boosting), comparable to the baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two image-to-3D baselines with quantitative results, DreamCraft3D wins decisively on every metric: PSNR improves by 12.9 dB over Make-it-3D (31.8 vs. 18.9) and 9.0 dB over Magic123 (31.8 vs. 22.8), while LPIPS drops from ~0.053–0.054 to 0.005, a roughly 10× reduction in perceptual error. CLIP score and contextual distance also favor DreamCraft3D (0.896 / 1.579 vs. 0.872 / 1.609 and 0.843 / 1.628). In the qualitative comparison (Figure 3), all three text-to-3D baselines—DreamFusion, Magic3D, and ProlificDreamer—suffer from multi-view consistency failures (Janus problem, semantic drift); ProlificDreamer produces realistic textures but fails to form a coherent 3D object. Make-it-3D yields quality frontal views but struggles with geometry, while Magic123 (which also uses Zero-1-to-3) improves geometry regularization yet still generates overly smoothed textures. DreamCraft3D's Bootstrapped Score Distillation is credited with producing richer texture detail at novel views while eliminating multi-face Janus artifacts, and a user study (32 participants, 480 responses) shows 92% preference for DreamCraft3D over alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamCraft3D extends the score distillation lineage initiated by DreamFusion (SDS) and ProlificDreamer (VSD) by introducing a hierarchical decomposition—geometry sculpting followed by texture boosting—that mirrors the manual artistic workflow. Its key departure is the Bootstrapped Score Distillation (BSD) loss, which distills from an *evolving* DreamBooth fine-tuned on the scene's own multi-view renderings rather than a fixed 2D distribution, and alternates optimization between the 3D mesh and the diffusion prior. The geometry stage additionally incorporates Zero-1-to-3 as a view-conditioned 3D prior alongside the standard 2D diffusion prior, a hybrid strategy not present in prior SDS/VSD formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bootstrapping loop—where the 3D scene improves the diffusion prior, which in turn improves the 3D scene—offers a principled mechanism for resolving the long-standing Janus problem and multi-view texture inconsistency that plagues optimization-based 3D generation. Limitations acknowledged by the authors include depth ambiguity causing frontal-view details to leak into texture, and the absence of explicit material/lighting decomposition. Open questions include whether the two-round BSD protocol generalizes to more complex scenes (multi-object, indoor), whether the approach scales to real-time or interactive generation, and whether the hierarchical decomposition can be further extended (e.g., to animation or relighting).
