## Problem

Existing causal sensitivity analysis methods are each restricted to a specific combination of sensitivity model (MSM, f-sensitivity, Rosenbaum's), treatment type (binary or continuous), and causal query (CATE, distributional effects, interventional density, multiple outcomes), leaving many practical settings without any available solution. No unified framework exists that can compute valid bounds across all these dimensions simultaneously, particularly for settings like continuous treatments under f-sensitivity or Rosenbaum's models, or simultaneous effects on multiple outcomes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular observational dataset of (confounders X, treatment A, outcome Y) tuples, a choice of sensitivity model (any GTSM, specified by its distributional functional D_{x,a}), and a sensitivity parameter Γ ≥ 1. It outputs upper and lower bounds on an arbitrary causal query Q(x,a,P) — which can be a CATE, a quantile, a set probability over multiple outcomes, or an interventional density — and additionally yields the analytical form of the potential outcome density that achieves the bound, enabling sensitivity analysis on the full interventional distribution rather than just a scalar functional.

## Method

NEURALCSA defines a Generalized Treatment Sensitivity Model (GTSM) that subsumes the MSM, f-sensitivity, and Rosenbaum's models by constraining the distributional shift between P(U|x) and P(U|x,a) induced by removing the U–A edge upon intervention. The framework is a two-stage procedure using two separately trained conditional normalizing flows: Stage 1 fits a CNF f*_{x,a} that maps a fixed standard Gaussian on U to the observational outcome distribution P_obs(Y|x,a), while Stage 2 fits a second CNF that learns the shifted latent distribution P(U|x) to maximize (or minimize) the causal query subject to the GTSM constraint D_{x,a}(P(U|x), P(U|x,a)) ≤ Γ. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that fixing P*(U|x,a) and choosing P*(Y|x,u,a) = δ(Y − f*_{x,a}(u)) as a Dirac delta is sufficient to attain the optimal bound, reducing the nested optimization to a single constrained problem. Stage 2 is solved via Monte Carlo estimation of the query and sensitivity constraint, with the constraint enforced through an augmented Lagrangian method, and the two CNFs are concatenated so that gradients flow through both during optimization.

## Results

On synthetic data, NEURALCSA bounds almost exactly coincide with proven-optimal closed-form (CF) solutions for the MSM on both binary (Dataset 1) and continuous (Dataset 2) treatments across sensitivity parameters Γ ∈ {2, 4, 10}. On the semi-synthetic MIMIC-III dataset (n = 14,719), all sensitivity models achieve at least 50% coverage of the true CATE, with the KL-sensitivity model achieving the tightest intervals (0.31 ± 0.01 length at 54% coverage) while the MSM is most conservative (0.77 ± 0.03 length at 91% coverage). The real-world case study demonstrates that NEURALCSA can handle simultaneous effects on multiple outcomes (heart rate and blood pressure), a setting with no prior solution, showing that increasing Γ from 2 to 4 shifts probability mass toward the 'danger area' of high heart rate and blood pressure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against closed-form optimal bounds for the MSM from Dorn & Guo (2022) and Frauen et al. (2023b), which are proven to be sharp. On both synthetic datasets (binary and continuous treatment), NEURALCSA bounds almost exactly coincide with these CF solutions across all tested sensitivity parameters (Γ = 2, 4, 10), confirming that the neural two-stage procedure recovers the optimal bounds when they are analytically available. The key advantage of NEURALCSA is that it extends to settings where no closed-form solution exists: f-sensitivity models (KL, TV, HE, χ²) with continuous treatments, Rosenbaum's model with continuous treatments, and simultaneous effects on multiple outcomes. In these previously intractable settings, NEURALCSA provides the first valid bounds, as confirmed by coverage validation on synthetic and semi-synthetic data. The paper does not report a head-to-head numerical comparison against CF bounds on the semi-synthetic data (since CF bounds are only available for MSM), but the synthetic validation establishes that the neural approach is as tight as the analytical one when both are applicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conditional normalizing flow (CNF) architecture of Winkler et al. (2019) and the normalizing flow framework of Rezende & Mohamed (2015) to learn invertible mappings between latent and outcome spaces. Conceptually, it extends the sharp-bounds framework of Frauen et al. (2023b), which was limited to MSM-type sensitivity models, by introducing the Generalized Treatment Sensitivity Model (GTSM) that subsumes MSM, f-sensitivity, and Rosenbaum's sensitivity models under a single distributional-shift formulation. The key departure from prior causal sensitivity analysis is the use of two separately trained CNFs to explicitly learn the latent confounder distribution shift induced by treatment intervention, rather than relying on closed-form or problem-specific optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEURALCSA is the first framework to provide a unified solution for causal sensitivity analysis across sensitivity models, treatment types (binary and continuous), and causal queries including the previously unaddressed multiple-outcomes setting. This is practically important because practitioners can now choose the sensitivity model that best matches their domain knowledge without being locked into a single analytical framework, and can perform sensitivity analysis on joint outcome distributions (e.g., simultaneous heart rate and blood pressure thresholds) that no prior method handles. Open questions include the computational cost of training two CNFs with augmented Lagrangian constraints, the tightness of bounds in high-dimensional latent spaces, and whether non-neural alternatives could match the flexibility of the CNF-based approach.
