## Problem

Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning assumes all agents act cooperatively, but real-world systems face Byzantine failures where any agent may malfunction or be compromised by a non-oblivious adversary. Existing robust MARL methods (e.g., M3DDPG, ROMAX) treat all agents as potential adversaries, yielding overly conservative ex ante equilibria that sacrifice cooperation with genuine allies. No prior method could simultaneously identify and cooperate with allies while defending against adversaries by learning posterior beliefs about each agent's type.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives its own partial observation and full action-observation history H^i as input, and produces an action from its local action space. Additionally, a learned belief network outputs a binary posterior probability p(θ|H^i) for each other agent, indicating the likelihood that agent is adversarial (θ=1) versus cooperative (θ=0). The critic network consumes the global state, the perturbed joint action (after adversarial substitution), and the belief vector, while the actor consumes only local history and beliefs, ensuring the deployed policy requires no access to global state or true types.

## Method

The paper formalizes Byzantine robustness as a Bayesian Adversarial Robust Dec-POMDP (BARDec-POMDP), where nature assigns each agent a binary type (cooperative or adversarial) at episode start, and adversarial agents' actions are replaced by a worst-case policy. The solution concept is an ex interim robust Markov perfect Bayesian equilibrium, where each agent acts on its posterior belief over others' types rather than a fixed prior, and the paper proves this equilibrium exists (via Kakutani's fixed point theorem) and weakly dominates ex ante equilibria as t→∞. To realize this equilibrium, the authors derive a robust Harsanyi-Bellman equation that updates two Q-functions (pre- and post-perturbation) under the posterior belief, and propose a two-timescale actor-critic algorithm where the adversary policy updates on a faster timescale and the defender on a slower one, guaranteeing almost sure convergence via stochastic approximation. The policy gradient naturally incorporates the type indicator θ^i as a multiplicative gate: (1−θ^i) scales the defender's gradient and θ^i scales the adversary's, so compromised agents' gradients are automatically zeroed. A separate belief network, trained with binary cross-entropy against the true type label during training, approximates the posterior at deployment time.

## Results

Under worst-case non-oblivious adversarial attacks, EIR-MAPPO achieves robust performance close to the maximum achievable reward in each environment (50 for Toy, 1.0 for LBF, 20 for SMAC), matching the ideal 'True Type' oracle that has access to the true adversary type. Averaged across all four attack types (non-oblivious, random, observation-based, and transferred), EIR-MAPPO surpasses baselines by 5.81% on Toy, 5.88% on LBF, and 25.45% on SMAC. Cooperative (unattacked) performance remains on par with MAPPO, confirming that robustness is achieved without sacrificing collaboration quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EIR-MAPPO outperforms all four baselines by large margins on robustness across all three environments while matching MAPPO's cooperative performance. MADDPG and M3DDPG are easily swayed by adversaries, with agents diverted from battle and failing to coordinate focused fire. MAPPO and RMAAC fail to develop useful micromanagement skills (kiting, focused fire) under attack, though RMAAC is specifically robust against observation perturbations but fails against unseen action perturbations. The ex ante variant (EAR-MAPPO), which is an ablation of EIR-MAPPO without belief tracking, occasionally demonstrates kiting but spreads fire over multiple enemies instead of concentrating on one, and can be tricked into retreating by advancing adversaries. EIR-MAPPO uniquely learns both kiting and focused fire simultaneously under worst-case adversarial perturbation, achieving robustness equivalent to the True Type oracle in nearly all LBF and SMAC settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Harsanyi-Bellman framework from ad hoc coordination (Albrecht & Ramamoorthy, 2015) to adversarial robust MARL, introducing a robust Harsanyi-Bellman equation that incorporates worst-case adversaries and posterior beliefs over agent types. It departs from prior robust MARL methods (M3DDPG, ROMAX, RMAAC) that implicitly pursue an ex ante equilibrium by treating all agents as potentially adversarial, instead formulating the problem as a Bayesian game where agents maintain and update beliefs about which allies are compromised. The convergence analysis draws on robust MDP theory (Iyengar, 2005) and stochastic approximation (Borkar), while the practical algorithm is built on the MAPPO architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ex interim RMPBE provides a theoretically grounded solution concept that provably weakly dominates the ex ante equilibria used by all prior robust MARL methods, resolving the tension between cooperation and robustness that conservative ex ante approaches struggle with. The belief-based mechanism allows agents to adaptively cooperate with identified allies while defending against adversaries, which is more realistic than assuming all agents are threats. Limitations include the single-adversary assumption, fixed type within an episode, and convergence guarantees only for finite spaces; finite-sample convergence and scaling to open-ended type spaces remain open. The paper identifies applications to robot swarms, traffic control, and power grids as future directions.
