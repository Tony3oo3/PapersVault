## Problem

Despite extensive optimization results for deep ResNets in the mean-field (infinite depth and width) regime, no generalization guarantees existed beyond the lazy training (NTK) regime, where parameters are assumed to remain near initialization. The NTK framework cannot capture the true non-linearity of ResNets because it requires a fixed, time-invariant kernel, whereas mean-field training allows unrestricted parameter movement. This paper asks whether a generalization analysis can be built for trained deep ResNets in the mean-field setting, where the relevant kernel becomes time-varying and distribution-dependent.

## Input / Output

The method operates on binary classification data (x, y) pairs drawn from an unknown distribution μ, with inputs normalized to unit norm and labels bounded by 1. The network is parameterized by two probability measures in the infinite depth/width limit: ν over the ResNet encoder (a path of measures over [0,1]) and τ over the MLP output layer, with scaling hyperparameters α and β chosen independently of width. The outputs are a generalization bound of O(1/√n) for the 0-1 loss, a linear convergence rate for the empirical squared loss, and explicit upper bounds on the KL divergence between the trained and initial parameter distributions, all expressed in terms of architecture constants and the scaling parameters.

## Method

The core technical shift is replacing the fixed NTK with a time-variant, distribution-dependent Gram matrix G(τ_t, ν_t) = α²G₁(τ_t, ν_t) + G₂(τ_t, ν_t) that evolves during training. The paper proves a global lower bound on the minimum eigenvalue of G₂ (Lemma 4.3–4.4) by showing that under proper scaling of β relative to n, the parameter distributions remain within a bounded Wasserstein ball of initialization, preventing the eigenvalue from collapsing. This eigenvalue bound yields linear convergence of the empirical loss and, via the gradient flow of KL divergence (Lemma 4.6), a uniform bound on KL(τ_t‖τ₀) and KL(ν_t‖ν₀) for all t ≥ 0 (Theorem 4.7). The KL bound then restricts the function class to F_KL(r) with r = O(1/√n), whose Rademacher complexity is shown to be O(β√(r/n)) (Lemma 4.8), ultimately yielding the O(1/√n) generalization rate via standard uniform convergence arguments (Theorem 4.9). The adjoint ODE structure of the ResNet encoder (Eq. 15–17) is essential for computing the functional derivatives that define the Gram matrix entries.

## Results

The paper establishes a generalization bound of O(1/√n) for the 0-1 classification error of trained deep ResNets in the mean-field regime (Theorem 4.9), matching the classical rate from the NTK/lazy training regime. The empirical loss converges exponentially at rate e^{−β²Λ/(2n)·t} (Theorem 4.7), where Λ = Λ(d) is a dimension-dependent constant from the minimum eigenvalue lower bound of the Gram matrix. The KL divergence between the trained parameter distributions and initialization is uniformly bounded by C_KL(d,α)/(Λ²β̄²) for all t ≥ 0, provided β/n > 4√(C_KL(d,α))/(Λ·r_max). The minimum eigenvalue of the output-layer Gram matrix G₂ is shown to remain bounded below by λ₀/2 throughout training (Lemma 4.4), enabling the global convergence guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or baseline comparisons on datasets. The key theoretical comparison is to the NTK (lazy training) regime: the O(1/√n) generalization rate matches the standard result from Du et al. (2019b), but is derived without requiring the kernel to remain fixed during training. Unlike prior mean-field convergence results for deep ResNets (Lu et al., 2020; Ding et al., 2021, 2022), which established global convergence but lacked generalization guarantees, this paper provides the first generalization analysis for deep ResNets beyond the NTK regime. The analysis also differs from Barboni et al. (2022), which showed local linear convergence via RKHS parameterization but with a ball radius dependent on N-universality that is difficult to estimate; the present work requires only minimal assumptions under proper scaling of α and β.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-field ODE formulation of deep ResNets established by Lu et al. (2020) and Ding et al. (2021, 2022), which provided global convergence guarantees but lacked generalization analysis. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the shift from the time-invariant NTK (used in lazy training analyses by Du et al. 2019b, Hayou et al. 2021) to a time-variant, distribution-dependent Gram matrix whose minimum eigenvalue must be controlled throughout training. The gradient flow in Wasserstein metric from Chizat & Bach (2018) and the functional derivative computations from Ding et al. (2022) provide the technical foundation, but the combination of KL divergence tracking with Rademacher complexity to yield a generalization bound is new to the deep ResNet mean-field setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap in the deep learning theory literature: while optimization (global convergence) results for mean-field ResNets existed, no generalization guarantees were available beyond the lazy training regime. The recovery of the classical O(1/√n) rate under fundamentally different assumptions (time-varying kernel, unrestricted parameter movement) validates the mean-field framework as a viable alternative to NTK analysis. Limitations include the requirement for specific scaling of α and β, the restriction to binary classification with squared loss, and the fact that under stronger data assumptions (e.g., k-sparse parity), the trained distribution could move far from initialization in a way the current framework cannot capture. The open question of what adaptive features are actually learned under the mean-field regime remains unresolved.
