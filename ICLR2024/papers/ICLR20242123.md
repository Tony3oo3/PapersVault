## Problem

Learning transfer operators for dynamical systems from data requires a well-chosen observable space, but existing deep learning approaches (e.g., VAMPNets, Deep-CCA) suffer from metric distortion: the norms in the learned representation space do not match those of the true data space, causing spectral pollution and unreliable eigenvalue estimates. This paper formalizes the search for an invariant representation as a constrained optimization problem and proposes a score functional that explicitly overcomes metric distortion while remaining differentiable and numerically stable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes trajectory data pairs (X, X') sampled from a time-homogeneous stochastic process, where X and X' are the state at time 0 and time t respectively. Inputs range from scalar time-series (logistic map, Langevin SDE) to high-dimensional structured data: MNIST image sequences, 2D fluid flow grid snapshots (velocity, pressure, scalar field), and molecular dynamics snapshots of the Chignolin peptide (500K+ points). The primary output is a learned feature map ψ_w: X → R^r defining an invariant subspace; this is then fed into standard operator regression (OLS) to produce an r×r transfer operator matrix, from which multi-step forecasts, spectral decompositions, and generator eigenvalues are extracted.

## Method

DPNets optimize two neural-network feature maps ψ_w and ψ_w' by maximizing a score P^γ that combines the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of the projected cross-covariance (C_X^w)^{†/2} C_{XX'}^w (C_{X'}^w)^{†/2} with a metric distortion penalty R(C) = tr(C² − C − ln C) that drives the within-space covariances toward the identity, aligning the representation norm with the data-space norm. To avoid the numerical instability of pseudo-inverses during training, the authors derive a relaxed score S^γ = ‖C_{XX'}^w‖²_HS / (‖C_X^w‖·‖C_{X'}^w‖) − γ(R(C_X^w)+R(C_{X'}^w)) that is a tight lower bound (Theorem 1), differentiable, and has unit-conditioning. For continuous-time dynamics (e.g., Langevin SDEs), the objective becomes a partial trace tr((C_X^w)^† C_{X∂}^w) involving the Itô formula, and Theorem 2 guarantees recovery of the leading invariant subspace of the generator under a spectral gap condition. The learned representation is then plugged into standard OLS operator regression to obtain the transfer operator matrix, enabling forecasting via extended DMD and spectral analysis.

## Results

On the non-normal logistic map, DPNets achieves the best optimality gap (0.64 ± 0.01) while DPNets-relaxed achieves the best spectral error (0.06 ± 0.05), both outperforming VAMPNets (gap 0.97, spectral error 0.21) and kernel baselines. On the Chignolin protein (500K+ MD snapshots), DPNets identifies 12.84 metastable states with a transition time of 17.59 ns and ΔH = −1.97 kcal/mol, substantially closer to the reference (40 ns, −6.1 kcal/mol) than Nyström-PCR (5.27 ns, −1.76) or Nyström-RRR (0.89 ns, −1.44). On ordered MNIST, DPNets maintains >95% classification accuracy over time while all baselines degrade, and on the 2D fluid-flow forecasting task, DPNets forecasting RMSE remains stable over time unlike every other method including a physics-informed neural network baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VAMPNets (Mardt et al., 2018), DPNets wins on optimality gap (0.64 vs 0.97) and DPNets-relaxed wins on spectral error (0.06 vs 0.21) on the logistic map; on Chignolin, VAMPNets failed to converge entirely while DPNets-relaxed (GNN-based) succeeded. Against kernel baselines (Cheby-T/ExtendedDMD and NoiseKernel), DPNets achieves a 2× smaller optimality gap (0.64 vs 1.24/2.17) and DPNets-relaxed a 3–4× smaller spectral error (0.06 vs 0.19–0.20). On Chignolin, DPNets outperforms the Nyström-sketching kernel methods (Meanti et al., 2023) by identifying more metastable states (12.84 vs 7.02 for Nys-PCR, 2.22 for Nys-RRR) and producing transition times and enthalpies closer to the DFT reference. On ordered MNIST and the 2D fluid-flow task, DPNets outperforms Dynamic AE (Lusch et al., 2018), Consistent AE (Azencot et al., 2020), DMD (Schmid, 2010), KernelDMD, and ExtendedDMD (Williams et al., 2015): DPNets sustains >95% accuracy where all others degrade, and its multi-step forecasting RMSE stays flat while every baseline (including a PINN) shows growing error.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPNets unifies and extends the VAMPNets (Mardt et al., 2018) and Deep-CCA (Andrew et al., 2013) frameworks by showing both are special cases (γ=0, nonsingular covariances) of a more general projection-based score. The key departure is the addition of a metric distortion loss R(C) that penalizes deviation of the feature covariance from identity, directly addressing the spectral pollution problem identified in Kostic et al. (2023) where norm mismatch between the RKHS and the true L²(π) space corrupts eigenvalue estimates. The paper also extends the discrete-time SVD-based formulation to continuous-time generator learning for time-reversal invariant SDEs (e.g., Langevin dynamics), a setting not covered by VAMPNets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding representation learning in orthogonal projections and providing a numerically well-conditioned relaxed score (no matrix inversion), DPNets offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc autoencoder objectives for Koopman/transfer operator learning, with theoretical guarantees tying the optimum to the leading singular or eigen-subspace. The fluid dynamics result—where forecasting error remains flat over time while all baselines degrade—suggests the metric distortion correction has practical value beyond spectral accuracy. Open questions include the statistical learning theory of the empirical score (only pointwise concentration is shown, not uniform over w), the instability of the continuous-time score due to covariance inversion, and whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or non-stationary settings without the compactness assumption.
