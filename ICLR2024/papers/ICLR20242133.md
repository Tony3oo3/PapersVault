## Problem

DPO and SLiC both estimate the optimal policy from preference pairs that are not sampled from that policy: DPO uses human-labeled pairs from unknown mixed policies, while SLiC can only sample from the SFT policy. Since the maximum likelihood estimator of the target optimal policy requires preference pairs drawn from the policy itself, this distributional mismatch limits estimation accuracy. The paper asks how to construct preference data from the estimated optimal policy without requiring human labels from that policy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242133/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a supervised fine-tuned (SFT) policy (T5-large or T5-XXL), a pairwise T5-XXL reward-ranking model trained on human preference data, and a set of prompts. It first generates 64 response candidates per prompt via temperature sampling from the SFT policy, then applies statistical rejection sampling to sub-sample 8 candidates guided by the reward model, and finally constructs labeled preference pairs from those 8 samples. The output is a preference-optimized policy model trained with either a sigmoid-norm (logistic regression) or hinge-norm (SVM) loss on the generated pairs.

## Method

RSO introduces a statistical rejection sampling algorithm that uses the SFT policy as a proposal distribution and a pairwise reward-ranking model to accept or reject candidates, producing samples from the estimated optimal policy π_{r_ψ} = π_sft(y|x)·exp(r_ψ(x,y)/β) / Z(x). The acceptance probability for each candidate is exp((r_ψ(x,y) − r_max)/β), where r_max is the maximum reward among remaining candidates, so β controls the trade-off between reward exploitation and regularization toward the SFT policy (β→0 recovers top-k-over-N, β→∞ recovers pure SFT sampling). The paper also unifies DPO and SLiC from a preference-modeling standpoint: DPO is logistic regression on normalized likelihood ratios, and SLiC is an SVM with hinge loss; RSO proposes a hinge-norm variant that normalizes by the SFT policy, making it the true SVM counterpart of DPO. The key insight is that comparing two responses via an explicit reward model is easier to learn than generating high-quality responses directly, and that sampling from the estimated optimal policy (rather than the SFT policy or raw human data) yields a more accurate MLE of the target policy.

## Results

On Reddit TL;DR (T5-large), RSO with sigmoid-norm loss and rso-sample-rank achieves 92.37% proxy reward, 82.22% gold reward, and 71.86% AutoSxS win rate, outperforming DPO (88.63/78.14/69.02) and SLiC (93.36/84.40/69.26). On AnthropicHH, RSO sigmoid-norm reaches 86.94% proxy, 59.15% gold, and 40.98% AutoSxS, surpassing DPO (85.09/58.65/39.56) and SLiC (82.21/55.22/32.56). Scaling to T5-XXL, RSO improves AutoSxS over DPO by 1.1% on Reddit TL;DR and 33.1% on AnthropicHH. Human evaluation confirms RSO is preferred over 2× more often than DPO on both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSO consistently outperforms DPO on both tasks: on Reddit TL;DR, RSO sigmoid-norm gains +3.74% proxy, +4.08% gold, and +2.84% AutoSxS over DPO with sft-sample-rank; on AnthropicHH, the gains are +1.85%, +0.50%, and +1.42% respectively. Against SLiC (hinge loss with rso-sample-rank), RSO sigmoid-norm trades a small proxy reward deficit (92.37% vs 93.36% on Reddit TL;DR) for a clear AutoSxS advantage (71.86% vs 69.26%), suggesting SLiC's hinge loss exhibits mild reward hacking. The gap over RAFT and ReST is substantial across all metrics (e.g., +17.5% AutoSxS over RAFT on Reddit TL;DR, +25.4% over ReST on AnthropicHH), confirming that statistical rejection sampling with balanced exploitation-regularization far exceeds simple best-of-N or threshold-based selection. The rso-sample-rank preference pair construction is the dominant factor: it improves over sft-sample-rank by 3.7–4.1% AutoSxS on Reddit TL;DR and 1.4–6.0% on AnthropicHH regardless of loss function. On cross-task transfer to CNN/DailyMail, RSO hinge-norm achieves 42.18% AutoSxS versus 38.63% for DPO sft-sample-rank and 33.21% for SLiC sft-sample-rank.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies DPO and SLiC-HF under a common preference-modeling framework, showing that DPO is logistic regression and SLiC is an SVM variant on human preference data, then improves both by introducing a hinge-norm loss. It repurposes classical statistical rejection sampling (Neal, 2003) to generate preference pairs from the estimated optimal policy rather than the SFT policy or raw human data, and shows that the widely-used top-k-over-N procedure in RLHF (AnthropicHH, Llama2) is a degenerate special case (β→0) of this more general algorithm. The approach also departs from DPO's 'language model is secretly a reward model' philosophy by demonstrating that an explicit pairwise reward-ranking model yields better preference pair labels for policy fitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RSO provides a practical, scalable offline recipe that avoids the instability and memory overhead of full RLHF while achieving consistent gains over DPO and SLiC across summarization and dialogue tasks, validated by gold reward models, LLM-based evaluation, and human raters. The unification of DPO and SLiC loss functions gives practitioners a clearer theoretical lens for choosing between them. Key limitations include evaluation only on T5-scale models (770M–11B) and two primary tasks, leaving open whether the gains persist at the scale of modern 70B+ LLMs, whether online or iterative variants are needed, and how the method extends to multi-objective alignment (harmlessness, safety) beyond single-dimension helpfulness.
