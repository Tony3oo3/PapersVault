## Problem

Transformer architectures are heavily overparameterized, yet prior work on layer reuse (e.g., fixed cyclic patterns, Subformer) relies on hand-designed, static sharing schemes that reduce at most 50% of parameters. The paper asks whether a general, data-driven pattern of layer replication can be discovered dynamically during a single training pass, achieving far greater parameter reduction (75–87%) without sacrificing perplexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242228/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard transformer (GPT-2 with 48 decoder blocks, BERT with 12 blocks, or ViT with 32 blocks) and a text or image training corpus, and produces a trained model in which the vast majority of layers share weights with a single earlier layer, along with the learned replication pattern. The Q-network (a small MLP with one 128-unit hidden layer) is used only during training and discarded at inference. At the end of training, only 5–10 of 48 GPT-2 layers (or 3–5 of 12 BERT layers) remain independently trainable, yielding 12–17% of the original parameter count.

## Method

A deep Q-learning agent drives a dynamic layer-tying process: every k training steps, the agent selects an action vector a where each element a_i ∈ {0,…,i} specifies whether layer i trains independently (a_i = i) or copies weights from layer a_i. The state vector s is derived from a via a transductive recursion that resolves chains of ties to their lowest-index source. Training begins with only layer 0 trainable; the Q-network (an MLP mapping s to per-layer Q-values) is updated via the Bellman equation using negative perplexity on a random batch as reward, with an ε-greedy policy whose exploration decays from 1.0 to a floor of 0.1. The key mechanism enabling stability despite wholesale weight replacement is that layer 0's early training, constrained by randomly initialized downstream layers, establishes a global alignment of attention-head ordering and embedding structure that propagates through all replicated copies, keeping inter-layer weight correlations above 0.93.

## Results

On the GPT-2 architecture (48 layers), the method reduces perplexity from 88.35 to 72.35 on the 1-billion words dataset and from 53.57 to 49.37 on WikiText-2, while reducing trainable parameters from 1.6B to a mean of 151M–218M (≈90% reduction) and independent layers from 48 to 6–10. On BERT (12 layers), perplexity drops from >1000 to 215.50 on 1-billion words, with parameters reduced from 376M to 46M–67M. Peak GPU memory during GPT-2 training drops from 12,567 MB to 4,514 MB (65% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against conventional (untied) transformer training, the method wins on perplexity for GPT-2 on Wiki-2 (49.37 vs 53.57), Lambada (93.84 vs 94.96), and 1-billion (72.35 vs 88.35), with a near-tie on Wiki-103 (22.35 vs 22.32). For BERT, it wins on all four datasets, most dramatically on 1-billion words (>1000 → 215.50). Against the fixed parameter-sharing patterns of Takase & Kiyono (2021) — Cycle (51.93), Cycle Rev (52.83), and Sequence (54.01) on Wiki-2 — the dynamic RL-driven method (49.37) outperforms all three by 2.6–4.6 perplexity points, and similarly on Shakespeare (161.1 vs 173.9–180.0). On CIFAR-10 with ViT, the method achieves 99.5% accuracy vs 99.9% for the full ViT, a 0.4% drop, while using only 80M mean trainable parameters (13% of the 630M baseline) and 7 independent layers out of 32.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RL-based Neural Architecture Search (Zoph & Le, 2017; Baker et al., 2017; Cai et al., 2018), which typically proposes a fixed architecture and trains it from scratch; (2) parameter sharing in transformers (Takase & Kiyono, 2021; Bhojanapalli et al., 2021; Reid et al., 2021), which uses fixed, hand-designed repetition patterns; and (3) deep Q-learning (Mnih et al., 2013), from which the ε-greedy policy, Bellman updates, and Q-network architecture are directly inherited. The key departure is that the architecture is not fixed before or during training: a small Q-network dynamically reassigns layer-to-layer weight ties every k steps, making this what the authors call 'the most dynamic form of NAS presented.' Unlike prior RL-for-training work (Xu et al., 2019; Subramanian et al., 2023) that tunes learning rates, here RL controls the topology of the network itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 75–87% of transformer layers can be tied to earlier layers while matching or slightly beating full-model perplexity challenges the implicit assumption that each layer in a deep transformer must learn a distinct transformation. Practically, the 65–68% reduction in peak GPU memory and the order-of-magnitude drop in trainable parameters make training substantially cheaper, which is relevant as models continue to scale. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively small models (48-layer GPT-2, 12-layer BERT) and standard benchmarks; whether the same degree of redundancy persists in 100B+ parameter models remains an open question. The paper also leaves open whether the method can be adapted to fine-tuning pre-trained models (as opposed to training from scratch), and the persistent dominance of layer 0 as a replication source suggests the learned pattern may be less 'architectural search' and more 'progressive unfreezing,' a distinction the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
