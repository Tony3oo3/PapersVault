## Problem

Finite-dimensional diffusion models assume fixed-resolution grids, making them scale poorly to high-resolution data, while existing infinite-dimensional generative models based on conditional neural fields treat coordinates independently and rely on latent compression, resulting in substantially lower sample quality. The gap is a diffusion framework that operates in an infinite-dimensional function space without point-wise independence or latent bottlenecks, enabling both resolution invariance and high-fidelity generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241190/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes randomly subsampled coordinate subsets (up to 8× fewer than full resolution) from 256×256 RGB images, with a diffusion autoencoder providing a 1024-dim latent condition to reduce training variance. At sampling time, the model accepts Gaussian noise of any spatial size and produces images at arbitrary resolutions from 64×64 up to 1024×1024 (exceeding the 256×256 training resolution) using DDIM with 100 steps. For super-resolution, a low-resolution image is encoded and decoded at higher resolution; for inpainting, an image plus binary mask is used with reconstruction guidance.

## Method

∞-Diff defines a Gaussian diffusion process in an L² Hilbert space where both the noise and data are mollified (convolved with a Gaussian kernel) to ensure they lie in the space, yielding closed-form forward marginals and posteriors. The denoising function is parameterised by a multi-scale neural operator architecture: sparse depthwise convolutions (kernel size 7, via TorchSparse) operate directly on raw irregularly sampled coordinates to capture local detail, then points are interpolated to a fixed 128×128 grid where a standard U-Net with attention aggregates global structure. This hybrid design avoids the point-wise independence of neural fields and the need for latent compression, instead using non-local integral operators to aggregate spatial context. Training uses a simplified noise-prediction loss (‖f_θ(x_t,t) − Tξ‖²) with a cosine schedule over 1000 steps, and the diffusion autoencoder framework reduces stochasticity enough to train on a single A100 GPU with batch size 32.

## Results

On FFHQ-256, ∞-Diff achieves a FID_CLIP of 3.87, competitive with finite-dimensional methods like StyleSwin (3.25) and UT (3.05), while substantially outperforming all infinite-dimensional baselines (GASP: 24.37, GEM: 35.62). On CelebA-HQ-64, ∞-Diff scores 4.57 FID_CLIP, nearly halving the best prior infinite-dimensional result (GASP: 9.29) and dramatically improving over GEM (14.65) and D2F (40.4). The model retains high quality even at 8× subsampling (FID_CLIP 6.48 on FFHQ-128) with a 1.6× speedup, and can sample at resolutions up to 1024×1024 (FID_CLIP 15.84) despite training only on 256×256 coordinate subsets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

∞-Diff decisively outperforms all infinite-dimensional baselines: on FFHQ-256 it achieves 3.87 FID_CLIP versus GASP's 24.37 and GEM's 35.62 (a 6–9× improvement), and on CelebA-HQ-64 it scores 4.57 versus GASP's 9.29 and DPF's 13.21. Against finite-dimensional methods, ∞-Diff is competitive but slightly behind the strongest baselines: on FFHQ-256 it trails StyleGAN2 (2.35) and UT (3.05) but beats CIPS (5.29); on Church-256 it is close to StyleSwin (8.28) and CIPS (10.80) but behind UT (5.52) and StyleGAN2 (6.21). The key distinction is that ∞-Diff achieves these results while training on only 1/4 of pixels (4× subsampling), decoupling memory and runtime from resolution, and enabling sampling at arbitrary resolutions (64×64 to 1024×1024) with a single model—capabilities none of the finite-dimensional baselines offer. Among infinite-dimensional methods, the gap is largest on higher-resolution datasets, where GEM and GASP degrade severely (e.g., GEM reaches 87.57 on Church-256) while ∞-Diff remains stable at 10.36.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) to infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, replacing the standard U-Net denoiser with non-local integral operators from the neural operator literature (Kovachki et al., 2021). It deliberately departs from all prior infinite-dimensional generative models (D2F, GASP, GEM, DPF), which rely on point-wise neural fields conditioned on compressed latent vectors, arguing that such compression is fundamentally incompatible with diffusion architecture design. The practical training recipe borrows from diffusion autoencoders (Preechakul et al., 2022) to reduce variance and from the grid-based U-Net of Nichol and Dhariwal (2021) for the multi-scale architecture's regular-grid component, combining these with sparse depthwise convolutions (via TorchSparse) on raw irregular coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a substantial quality gap between infinite-dimensional (resolution-independent) and finite-dimensional generative models, achieving FID_CLIP scores within ~1.5 points of StyleGAN2 on FFHQ-256 while retaining the ability to sample at arbitrary resolutions from a single model. The finding that 8× coordinate subsampling causes only modest quality degradation (FID 6.48 vs 3.15) has direct practical implications for memory and compute budgets in high-resolution generation. Open questions include whether neural operators can operate effectively at even higher sparsity ratios, whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D data, and whether the remaining gap to finite-dimensional SOTA can be closed with more powerful operator architectures or better integral approximations.
