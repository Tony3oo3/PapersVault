## Problem

Current vision-language models (VLMs) exhibit 'bag-of-words' behavior, memorizing frequent word co-occurrences rather than composing visual entities and their relationships. Architecturally, they feed a single holistic image embedding into the LLM and generate text without linking individual words to specific visual regions or relations, causing them to fail on compositional reasoning tasks involving object-attribute-relation structures. The critical gap is the absence of iterative, bidirectional communication between the language generation process and a visual detection system during decoding.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image (encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14 into N×N×D patch embeddings) and a text prompt as input. During auto-regressive generation, the LLM dynamically emits special communication tokens that trigger the detection network (a YOLOX-style module) to propose bounding boxes conditioned on the LLM's last hidden state; the resulting ROI features (mean-pooled to D-dimensional vectors) are injected back into the token sequence. The final output is a generated text sequence interleaved with bounding box proposals, enabling both language generation and spatial grounding in a single pass.

## Method

CoVLM introduces a set of six communication tokens (<obj>, </obj>, <visual>, <box>, <previsual>, <prebox>) that decompose language generation into entity and relation components, each triggering a round of vision-language communication. Top-down (language-to-vision): when the LLM emits <visual> or <previsual>, its last hidden state is concatenated with image patch embeddings and fed to a YOLOX-like detector that proposes ROIs relevant to the generated context so far. Bottom-up (vision-to-language): the mean-pooled features of top-scoring ROIs are injected as <box> or <prebox> tokens, conditioning subsequent token generation on localized visual evidence. This loop repeats iteratively until EOS, enabling the LLM to compose entities and relations step-by-step with grounded visual feedback. The system is pre-trained on 97M grounded image-text pairs (constructed via a GroundingDINO + spaCy pipeline over BLIP-2 data) using Pythia-1.4B/2.8B as the LLM backbone, with a combined language modeling and detection loss.

## Results

On compositional reasoning benchmarks, CoVLM 1.4B achieves 32.46% ARO top-1 accuracy, 44.29% Cola top-1 accuracy, and 39.00% HICO-DET full mAP in a zero-shot setting. This represents improvements of ~3% over BLIP-2 on ARO, ~14% over BLIP-2 on Cola, and ~18% over KOSMOS-2 on HICO-DET full mAP. On referring expression comprehension, CoVLM 2.8B reaches 62.33% on RefCOCOg test and 52.51% on RefCOCO+ testA, outperforming KOSMOS-2. On VQAv2, CoVLM 2.8B achieves competitive accuracy with a human-evaluated score of 57.11% versus BLIP-2's 56.62%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoVLM 1.4B outperforms all generative VLM baselines (BLIP-2, BLIP, KOSMOS-2, OpenFlamingo) and alignment models (CLIP, FLAVA) on all three compositional reasoning benchmarks, with the largest margin over KOSMOS-2 (~18% on HICO-DET full mAP, ~14% on Cola). On HICO-DET, CoVLM's zero-shot full mAP of 39.00 surpasses all supervised task-specific methods except RLIPv2-ParSeDA (45.09), though CoVLM leads on the Rare subset (50.82 vs. 43.23). On Cola, CoVLM (44.29) beats the best task-specific supervised method CLIP+MM-Pred (41.42) by ~3%. On referring expression comprehension, CoVLM 2.8B outperforms KOSMOS-2 on RefCOCOg (62.33 vs. 61.65) and RefCOCO+ (52.51 vs. 50.73) but trails on RefCOCO (53.67 vs. 57.42), where KOSMOS-2 benefited from instruction fine-tuning. On VQAv2, CoVLM 2.8B is slightly below Flamingo 3B and BLIP-2 in automatic evaluation but matches BLIP-2 in human evaluation (57.11% vs. 56.62%), suggesting the gap is due to answer format diversity rather than comprehension.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoVLM extends the grounding-token paradigm introduced by KOSMOS-2, which appends location tokens after visual entities, by making the vision-language interaction bidirectional and iterative: the LLM can query a detection network mid-generation and receive region features back before continuing. It departs from holistic VLMs (BLIP-2, Flamingo, LLaVA) that inject a single image embedding at the start, and from one-way grounding systems (VisionLLM, LISA, GPT4RoI) that either generate masks from language or feed regions in once without feedback. The detection component borrows from YOLOX-style single-stage detectors, and the pre-training grounding pipeline follows KOSMOS-2's use of GroundingDINO and spaCy dependency expansion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit, step-by-step communication between a language model and a detection network—rather than a single holistic image embedding—substantially improves compositional reasoning (relations, attributes, spatial interactions) in VLMs, with zero-shot results that exceed supervised task-specific methods on HICO-DET Rare. However, the authors acknowledge that object-attribute and spatial-event compositionality remain unaddressed, and the communication token design is tightly coupled to a specific autoregressive decoding loop, raising questions about scalability to longer sequences or more complex multi-entity scenes. The approach offers a concrete architectural recipe for integrating perception into generation, but whether it generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks and to larger LLM backbones remains open.
