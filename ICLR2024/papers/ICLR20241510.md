## Problem

Prior tester-learners for halfspaces in the testable learning model (RV23, GKK23) achieve error opt+ε but require time exponential in 1/ε, matching the hardness of ordinary agnostic learning. The open question is whether the more efficient guarantees obtained by nonconvex SGD under structured distributions—opt+ε for Massart noise and O(opt)+ε for agnostic noise—can be achieved in the testable learning framework, where the algorithm must verify its own distributional assumptions rather than assume them.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes two independent labeled training sets S₁ and S₂ drawn from an unknown joint distribution D_XY over ℝᵈ × {±1}, along with a specified target marginal D* (isotropic strongly log-concave). It either rejects the input or outputs a unit weight vector w ∈ S^{d-1} defining an origin-centered halfspace. The two-sample design is essential: S₁ drives the PSGD optimization while S₂ is used exclusively for the label-aware distributional tests, ensuring the tests are independent of the optimization trajectory. In the agnostic setting, the algorithm is invoked multiple times over a geometric grid of σ values and returns the candidate with minimum empirical error.

## Method

The algorithm runs projected stochastic gradient descent (PSGD) on a smooth ramp surrogate loss ℓ_σ to obtain candidate stationary points, then applies three label-aware testers to each candidate: T₁ verifies approximate isotropy via moment checks, T₂ confirms the band probability P[|⟨w,x⟩|≤σ] = Θ(σ), and T₃ checks that low-degree moments of the distribution conditioned on the band match those of D* conditioned on the band. The key insight is that the critical regions in the gradient analysis are rectangles axis-aligned with w, so by conditioning on the band {x: |⟨w,x⟩|≤σ} and exploiting the full strength of the moment-matching framework of GKK23, the algorithm converts weak additive approximations of conditional probabilities into the multiplicative closeness needed to prove stationary points are near-optimal. This localization trick—borrowed from the iterative localization principle of ABL17—allows the use of only constant-degree moment tests (polynomial time) rather than the high-degree tests that would be needed to directly verify the geometric proportionality of critical region masses.

## Results

The paper provides the first fully polynomial-time tester-learners for halfspaces in the testable learning model. For Massart noise (rate η < 1/2) with any isotropic strongly log-concave target marginal, the algorithm achieves error opt + ε in poly(d, 1/ε, 1/(1−2η)) time and sample complexity (Theorem 4.1). For adversarial/agnostic noise with a Gaussian target marginal, it achieves error O(opt) + ε in poly(d, 1/ε) time (Theorem 5.3). For general strongly log-concave targets in the agnostic setting, it achieves Õ(opt) + ε in quasipolynomial time poly(2^polylog d, (1/ε)^polylog d) (Theorem 5.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against prior testable learning results of Rubinfeld–Vasilyan (RV23) and Gollakota–Klivans–Kothari (GKK23), which achieved opt + ε error for halfspaces but required d^Õ(1/ε²) time — exponential in 1/ε. This work reduces that to fully polynomial time for Massart noise (opt + ε) and to poly(d, 1/ε) for agnostic noise with Gaussian marginals (O(opt) + ε), at the cost of relaxing the optimality guarantee in the agnostic case. Compared to the non-testable agnostic learning algorithms of Diakonikolas et al. (DKTZ20b), which achieve O(opt) + ε under an assumed structured distribution, this paper provides the same guarantee in the harder testable learning framework where the algorithm must verify its distributional assumptions and may reject. The concurrent work of Diakonikolas et al. (DKK+23) also provides an efficient tester-learner for homogeneous halfspaces with O(opt) + ε error, but only for Gaussian targets and without the opt + ε Massart guarantee or the extension to arbitrary strongly log-concave marginals that this paper provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: the testable learning framework of Rubinfeld and Vasilyan (RV23) and Gollakota et al. (GKK23), which introduced tester-learners for halfspaces but with exponential dependence on 1/ε, and the nonconvex SGD approach of Diakonikolas et al. (DKTZ20a; DKTZ20b) for polynomial-time learning of noisy halfspaces under structured distributions. The key departure from prior testable learning work is the use of label-dependent, band-conditioned moment tests rather than the label-oblivious global moment checks of RV23/GKK23. By conditioning on a band around a candidate halfspace and exploiting the localization principle of Awasthi et al. (ABL17), the authors convert the inherently additive guarantees of moment matching into the multiplicative guarantees needed to certify that nonconvex stationary points are near-optimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result showing that the polynomial-time guarantees of the best non-testable halfspace learning algorithms (opt+ε for Massart, O(opt)+ε for agnostic) can be achieved in the strictly harder testable learning model, where the algorithm must verify its own distributional assumptions. The band-conditioned moment-matching technique is a reusable tool that may apply to other problems where one needs to certify local distributional properties. Open questions include whether the quasipolynomial-to-polynomial gap for general strongly log-concave targets in the agnostic setting can be closed, whether the approach extends to non-origin-centered halfspaces, and whether similar label-dependent testing strategies can be applied to other concept classes beyond halfspaces.
