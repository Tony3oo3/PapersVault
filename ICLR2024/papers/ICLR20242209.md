## Problem

Higher-order topological models on simplicial complexes offer greater expressivity than graph-based methods, but their computational cost grows combinatorially with simplex order and neighborhood size. Existing simplicial neural networks such as MPSN are bounded in expressivity by the simplicial Weisfeiler-Leman test, and their message-passing design makes it difficult to trade off computational cost against model capacity. The paper asks whether a random-walk-based architecture can capture higher-order structure with tunable computational cost while achieving expressivity provably incomparable to message-passing simplicial networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a simplicial complex with per-order simplex features (f_k: X_k → R^{d_k}) and a user-specified number of random walks m (which can range from a small sample to one walk per simplex). Each walk of length ℓ on k-simplices is converted into a feature matrix of shape ℓ × (d_k + d_{k-1} + d_{k+1} + 3s − 2), where s is a local window size. The model outputs updated hidden states for every simplex at every order, which are then fed into task-specific heads (e.g., an MLP for vertex classification or a regression head for citation imputation).

## Method

SCRaWl samples random walks on k-simplices using either uniform connection sampling or uniform neighbor sampling, where each step traverses via a shared face or coface. Each walk is encoded into a six-part feature matrix capturing the current simplex features, the face/coface used for traversal, and three binary structural channels (local identity, lower adjacency, upper adjacency) within a sliding window of size s. These matrices are processed by a 1D convolutional network over the walk-step axis, and the convolved outputs are pooled by the central simplex in each window before being passed through a trainable MLP to update hidden states. The same collection of walks is reused across all layers and all simplex orders within a layer, and information propagates between orders through the face and coface features embedded in the walk matrices. The key theoretical result is that SCRaWl's expressivity is incomparable to MPSN: there exist simplicial complexes distinguishable by one but not the other, extending the analogous incomparability between CRaWl and message-passing GNNs to the simplicial setting.

## Results

On the Semantic Scholar co-authorship network (citation imputation), SCRaWl achieves 95–99% accuracy across all missing rates (10%–50%), matching MPSN and outperforming all other simplicial methods, with the gap widening substantially above 10% missing data. On the social contact vertex classification task, SCRaWl reaches 0.927±0.026 accuracy on the primary school dataset and a perfect 1.000±0.000 on the high school dataset, compared to 0.727±0.043 and 0.943±0.033 for MPSN. The graph-based CRaWl variant (operating only on the 1-skeleton) drops to 0.415±0.030 and 0.823±0.042, confirming the importance of higher-order interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCRaWl clearly outperforms all simplicial baselines on the social contact datasets: it beats MPSN by 20 points on primary school (0.927 vs. 0.727) and by 6 points on high school (1.000 vs. 0.943), and dominates SCNN (0.166/0.228), k-simplex2vec (0.398/0.308), and SAT by wide margins. On the Semantic Scholar imputation task, SCRaWl is on par with MPSN at low missing rates (both ~99% at 10%) but pulls ahead as missingness increases, reaching 0.96–0.98 at 50% missing while MPSN drops to 0.94–0.97. The only method that marginally beats SCRaWl is CAt-Walk on the primary school dataset (0.932 vs. 0.927), though SCRaWl wins decisively on high school (1.000 vs. 0.907). The graph-based CRaWl variant (0.415/0.823) confirms that the simplicial structure is essential: restricting to pairwise edges loses 51 and 18 points respectively. Among hypergraph methods, SCRaWl outperforms HyperGCN and AllSetTransformer on both datasets, with the gap most pronounced on primary school (0.927 vs. 0.852 and 0.898).

## Relation to Prior Work

SCRaWl is a direct simplicial extension of CRaWl (Tönshoff et al., 2023), which replaced message-passing on graphs with random-walk trajectories processed by 1D convolutions. The key conceptual departure from existing simplicial neural networks (MPSN, SCNN, SAT, SAN) is that instead of computing expensive convolutional filters over the simplicial adjacency structure, SCRaWl samples random walks on simplices and applies fast 1D convolutions to the resulting walk feature matrices, encoding local structural information (identity, lower/upper adjacency) within the walk itself. This design yields a provable expressivity incomparability with message-passing simplicial networks, mirroring the analogous result for CRaWl vs. GNNs on graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable incomparability to MPSN is a clean theoretical contribution that expands the landscape of simplicial learning architectures beyond the message-passing paradigm. However, the empirical validation is limited to two small datasets (Semantic Scholar co-authorship with ~20k simplices, and social contact networks with ~250–330 vertices), and the authors themselves note that one-walk-per-simplex sampling will not scale to larger complexes. Open questions include approximation guarantees for sub-sampling walks, performance on edge-flow and other higher-order tasks, and whether the computational trade-off (walk count/length vs. expressivity) can be made more principled.
