## Problem

DetectGPT, the leading zero-shot detector for machine-generated text, requires approximately 100 model calls per passage because each perturbation forces a full re-evaluation of the Markov chain, making it computationally prohibitive for large-scale deployment. This paper asks whether a fundamentally more efficient detection signal exists that avoids the perturbation-and-rescore loop while maintaining or improving accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240985/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text passage x, a sampling model q_φ (e.g., GPT-J or the source model itself), and a scoring model p_θ (e.g., Neo-2.7 or the source model). It produces a single scalar conditional probability curvature score d̂_x, which is compared against a threshold ε to yield a binary decision. Critically, the scoring model is invoked only once (a single forward pass over x) to obtain all token-level log probabilities, and the sampling model is invoked once to generate 10,000 alternative tokens per position in a batched categorical draw—reducing inference from ~100 sequential model calls to effectively 2 parallel calls.

## Method

Fast-DetectGPT replaces DetectGPT's perturbation-based probability curvature with a conditional probability curvature computed at the token level. The key hypothesis is that LLMs, pre-trained on large corpora, systematically select higher-probability tokens given a context, so the conditional probability function p_θ(x̃|x) exhibits a positive curvature (peak) at machine-generated tokens, while human-written tokens sit near the mean of the conditional distribution. Concretely, for each token position j, the method samples N=10,000 alternative tokens from q_φ(x̃_j | x_{<j}), computes their log-probabilities under p_θ in a single forward pass, and estimates the curvature as (log p_θ(x|x) − μ̃)/σ̃, where μ̃ and σ̃ are the sample mean and standard deviation of the alternative log-probabilities. This conditional independence across positions eliminates the need to re-score the full sequence for each perturbation, yielding a 340× speedup over DetectGPT while achieving ~75% relative AUROC improvement in both white-box and black-box settings.

## Results

Fast-DetectGPT achieves 0.9887 average AUROC in the white-box setting (5 open-source models) and 0.9338 in the black-box setting (ChatGPT/GPT-4), surpassing DetectGPT (0.9554 and 0.7225 respectively) by a relative ~75% improvement. In the black-box setting, Fast-DetectGPT (GPT-J/Neo-2.7) even outperforms DetectGPT's white-box performance by 27.6% on average. The method achieves a 340× inference speedup over DetectGPT (233 seconds vs. 79,113 seconds on a Tesla A100 GPU for XSum generations from five models).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fast-DetectGPT outperforms DetectGPT by a relative 74.7% (white-box) and 74.5% (black-box) in average AUROC, with the gap widening for larger source models (e.g., 79.3% relative on GPT-J). Against the enhanced variant NPR, Fast-DetectGPT leads by 68.2% relative in the white-box setting. Among simpler statistical baselines, Likelihood (0.8683) and LogRank (0.8993) trail Fast-DetectGPT (0.9887) by 0.12 and 0.09 AUROC respectively, while Entropy is near-random (0.5048). In the black-box setting against ChatGPT/GPT-4 (Table 3), Fast-DetectGPT (0.9615/0.9061 avg) surpasses the strongest supervised detector GPTZero (0.9348/0.8853) and DetectGPT (0.8223/0.6228) by substantial margins. Notably, Fast-DetectGPT's black-box performance (0.9677) exceeds DetectGPT's white-box performance (0.9554) by 27.6% relative, demonstrating that the conditional probability curvature feature is robust even without access to the source model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DetectGPT (Mitchell et al., 2023), which detects machine-generated text by measuring probability curvature via ~100 T5-generated perturbations scored by a GPT model. The key conceptual departure is replacing full-passage perturbation with token-level conditional probability curvature: instead of asking 'how does log-probability change under perturbation?', the method asks 'how does the original token's conditional probability compare to alternative tokens in the same context?', exploiting conditional independence to reduce scoring to a single forward pass. The paper also reveals that the curvature numerator decomposes into the sum of two long-standing baselines (Likelihood and Entropy), providing a unifying interpretation for why these simple features work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 340× speedup (22 hours → 4 minutes) makes zero-shot detection practically deployable at scale, while the ~75% relative AUROC improvement over DetectGPT challenges the assumption that more model calls yield better detection. The black-box setting outperforming DetectGPT's white-box by 28% suggests surrogate models can be surprisingly effective, though the optimal surrogate remains unclear and depends on training corpus overlap. Open questions include theoretical grounding for the curvature hypothesis, generalization to non-English and low-resource languages, and whether the approach's reliance on pre-trained model biases (noted for non-native English speakers) can be mitigated.
