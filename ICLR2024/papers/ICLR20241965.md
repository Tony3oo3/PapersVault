## Problem

Classical CCA and PLS algorithms require full covariance matrices and scale quadratically with dimension, making them intractable for large-scale data. Existing stochastic and deep CCA methods are either slow, produce biased gradients requiring large mini-batches, or demand complex hyperparameter tuning. The paper seeks a single unconstrained objective that unifies the GEP formulations of CCA, PLS, Deep CCA, and CCA-family SSL methods into one simple, scalable training procedure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes I views of data as mini-batches of M samples (e.g., 82 regional brain volumes paired with 582,565 genetic variants for 33,333 UK Biobank subjects, or two augmented views of a 32×32 RGB image). It produces K-dimensional correlated representations Z^(i) = f^(i)(X^(i); θ^(i)) per view, structured as a top-K subspace of the underlying GEP. A key implementation detail is that the loss requires two independent mini-batches per gradient step to form unbiased covariance estimates, and for the linear case the output is a set of canonical directions U ∈ R^{D×K} with O(MKD) gradient cost.

## Method

The core contribution is an unconstrained Eckhart-Young-inspired loss for Generalized Eigenvalue Problems: L_GEP-EY(U) = trace(−2UᵀAU + (UᵀBU)(UᵀBU)), whose global minima characterize the top-K GEP subspace and which provably has no spurious local minima. For the CCA family this specializes to L_EY(θ) = −2·tr(C(θ)) + ‖V_α(θ)‖²_F, where C(θ) is the total between-view covariance and V_α(θ) is the ridge-regularized total within-view variance of the learned representations. Unbiased batch estimates are constructed by computing sample covariances on two independent mini-batches, yielding a differentiable loss amenable to standard SGD. This single objective, optimized via backpropagation, simultaneously gives stochastic CCA (CCA-EY), stochastic PLS (PLS-EY), Deep CCA (DCCA-EY), and a hyperparameter-free SSL method (SSL-EY) that matches Barlow Twins and VICReg on CIFAR-10/100.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SSL-EY achieves 91.43% Top-1 and 99.75% Top-5 accuracy, competitive with Barlow twins (92.1% / 99.73%) and VICReg (91.68% / 99.66%) despite using out-of-the-box hyperparameters. On CIFAR-100, SSL-EY reaches 67.52% Top-1, slightly below Barlow twins (71.38%) and VICReg (68.56%). For stochastic CCA on MediaMill, CCA-EY consistently outperforms both γ-EigenGame and SGHA in Proportion of Correlation Captured (PCC) across all mini-batch sizes (5–100). For Deep CCA on SplitMNIST, DCCA-EY achieves higher Total Correlation Captured (TCC) than DCCA-STOL and DCCA-NOI, and for Deep Multiview CCA on mfeat, DCCA-EY outperforms DMCCA and DGCCA in Total Multiview Correlation Captured (TMCC). The paper also performs the first PLS analysis of UK Biobank data (33,333 subjects, 582,565 genetic variants, 82 brain regions) on a standard laptop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For stochastic CCA on MediaMill, CCA-EY wins against both γ-EigenGame and SGHA on PCC across all mini-batch sizes; γ-EigenGame significantly underperforms especially at small batches, while SGHA learns faster initially but is overtaken as more samples are seen. For Deep CCA on SplitMNIST, DCCA-EY outperforms DCCA-STOL (which breaks down when batch size < K=50 due to low-rank empirical covariances) and DCCA-NOI (which requires careful tuning of an additional time-constant hyperparameter and converges more slowly). For Deep Multiview CCA on mfeat, DCCA-EY consistently beats both DMCCA (which also breaks down at batch < K) and DGCCA (which does not break down but significantly underperforms at small batch sizes). For SSL on CIFAR-10/100, SSL-EY is slightly behind Barlow twins (by ~0.7% Top-1 on CIFAR-10, ~3.9% on CIFAR-100) and VICReg (by ~0.25% on CIFAR-10, ~1% on CIFAR-100), but this is notable because SSL-EY uses unoptimized hyperparameters borrowed from Barlow twins while the baselines use heavily tuned settings. The paper also reports that SSL-EY is more stable when reducing representation dimensionality compared to the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the CCA family (CCA, PLS, multiview CCA, Deep CCA) under a single Generalized Eigenvalue Problem (GEP) framework inherited from Nielsen (2002) and Somandepalli et al. (2019), then introduces a novel unconstrained Eckhart-Young-inspired loss that characterizes the top-K GEP subspace. The key departure from prior stochastic CCA methods (SGHA, EigenGame) and Deep CCA methods (DCCA-STOL, DCCA-NOI) is that it replaces their complex optimization machinery—Lagrange multipliers, Nash-equilibrium formulations, adaptive whitening—with plain SGD on a simple trace-based objective. The paper also extends this framework to self-supervised learning, providing a theoretical bridge showing that VICReg and Barlow twins are implicitly solving CCA-like problems, going beyond the partial analysis of Balestriero & LeCun (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the method enables the first PLS analysis of UK Biobank-scale data (33K subjects, 582K genetic variants) on a laptop, and provides a drop-in replacement for existing Deep CCA pipelines with faster convergence and smaller batch sizes. The no-spurious-local-minima guarantee (Proposition 3.2) gives theoretical justification for SGD convergence that prior stochastic CCA methods lacked. Open questions include whether the approach extends to sparse/regularized settings for interpretability, whether correlation can replace classification accuracy as a representation quality metric in unsupervised settings, and whether the SSL-EY method's advantage of having no objective hyperparameters translates to ImageNet-scale benchmarks.
