## Problem

Existing personalized federated learning methods require client-side finetuning or multi-round communication to produce models for new clients, incurring high latency and computational cost on resource-constrained devices. Stateful approaches that store per-client descriptors (e.g., pFedHN) do not scale to large client populations and cannot serve previously unseen clients without iterative optimization. The paper addresses these gaps by asking whether a server-side learning-to-learn mechanism can generate ready-to-use personalized models for any client—including future ones—via a single forward pass, with theoretical guarantees of generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a client data batch (images with labels during training, images only for unlabeled clients) and produces the full parameter vector of a LeNet-style personalized model (two conv layers, three FC layers, output dimension equal to the number of classes). The client data is consumed by the embedding network in a single forward pass over a mini-batch of size 32, yielding a low-dimensional descriptor vector of dimension l = n/4 (where n is the number of training clients). The descriptor is the only client-side quantity communicated to the server; the hypernetwork then outputs the complete model parameters, which are sent back to the client for immediate use without further optimization.

## Method

PeFLL jointly trains two networks in a learning-to-learn framework: an embedding network φ that maps individual data points to a latent descriptor space (averaged over a batch to form the client descriptor v), and a hypernetwork h that maps this descriptor to the full parameter vector of a personalized LeNet model. Training follows a split-learning protocol: the client evaluates the embedding network and performs local SGD on the generated model, while the server evaluates the hypernetwork and backpropagates through it using the chain rule, distributing computation so that the large hypernetwork never leaves the server. The optimization is stateless—no per-client parameters are stored—allowing clients to enter or leave at any time, and the objective includes L2 regularization terms on both network parameters and the generated model, derived from a novel PAC-Bayesian generalization bound that is tighter than prior bounds in the large-n, small-m federated regime. For inference on new or unseen clients, the procedure reduces to a single forward pass through the embedding network followed by a single forward pass through the hypernetwork, requiring no client-side training or iterative communication.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 90 training clients, PeFLL achieves 89.0% test accuracy (training clients) and 89.3% (unseen clients), outperforming the best baseline (pFedHN at 87.8%) by over 1 point. On CIFAR-100 with 90 clients, PeFLL reaches 56.0% vs. pFedHN's 53.6%, and on FEMNIST it achieves 90.1% vs. pFedMe's 85.9%. A key finding is that PeFLL's accuracy on unseen clients is nearly identical to training clients (e.g., 88.9% vs. 88.9% on CIFAR-10 with 450 clients), whereas baselines like pFedHN degrade substantially (77.4% → 60.9%) as the number of clients grows and per-client data shrinks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PeFLL outperforms all six baselines in nearly every setting (the sole exception being CIFAR-100 with 90 unseen clients, where kNN-Per edges ahead at 41.8% vs. 35.2%). Against the closest competitor pFedHN (also a server-side hypernetwork), PeFLL leads by 1.2 points on CIFAR-10 (n=90) but the gap widens dramatically as clients increase: on CIFAR-10 (n=900) PeFLL scores 87.8% vs. pFedHN's 66.0%, a 21.8-point margin, because pFedHN's per-client stored descriptors become unreliable with fewer samples per client. Against finetuning-based methods (Per-FedAvg, pFedMe, FedRep), PeFLL's advantage grows with the number of clients (i.e., less data per client): on CIFAR-100 (n=900) PeFLL reaches 40.9% while pFedMe, FedRep, and Per-FedAvg score 34.6%, 30.8%, and 20.5% respectively. On FEMNIST, PeFLL achieves 90.1% (training) and 90.7% (unseen), beating the best baseline pFedMe (85.9%/86.1%) by over 4 points. The most striking result is on unseen clients: PeFLL's accuracy drops by less than 1 point from training to unseen clients across all datasets, while pFedHN drops by 10–25 points and Per-FedAvg by 5–10 points, confirming that PeFLL's feed-forward descriptor generation generalizes without requiring per-client optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

PeFLL extends the server-side hypernetwork approach of pFedHN (Shamsian et al., 2021) by replacing its non-parametric, per-client stored descriptor vectors with a parametric embedding network that computes descriptors on-the-fly via a single forward pass. This shift from a stateful to a stateless formulation is the paper's core architectural departure, enabling zero-shot model generation for previously unseen clients without iterative optimization. The training procedure follows the Split Learning paradigm (Gupta and Raskar, 2018) to distribute forward and backward passes between server and clients, while the learning-to-learn framing draws on PAC-Bayesian meta-learning theory (Baxter, 2000; Pentina and Lampert, 2015) to derive a generalization bound tighter than prior federated-specific instantiations (Rezazadeh, 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PeFLL addresses a practically critical gap in personalized federated learning: generating high-quality models for new clients without any client-side training or multi-round communication, which is essential for large-scale deployments like mobile keyboards where devices join and leave dynamically. The PAC-Bayesian generalization bound is tighter in the many-clients/few-samples regime typical of FL, and the convergence analysis handles the non-standard optimization structure of split hypernetwork training. Limitations include the absence of formal privacy guarantees (differential privacy is mentioned only as future work), evaluation restricted to small LeNet-style models and standard benchmarks, and a still-visible accuracy gap between training and unseen clients on CIFAR100 (e.g., 56.0 vs. 35.2 at n=90). Open questions include scaling to larger architectures (ResNet experiments are preliminary), incorporating formal DP, and whether the embedding network's descriptor quality degrades under more extreme distributional shift.
