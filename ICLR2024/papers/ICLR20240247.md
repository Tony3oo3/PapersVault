## Problem

LLMs are vulnerable to noise in tabular data: only a small subset of table cells is relevant to answering a given question, yet the full table is typically fed to the model, degrading performance especially on large tables. Existing mitigations either hard-prune or decompose the table (risking loss of relevant information that cannot be recovered downstream) or retrieve content, but none softly reweight table content by relevance while preserving all information for the QA model.

## Input / Output

CABINET takes a natural language question and a table, which is linearized into a flat token sequence using [HEAD] and [ROW] k markers with pipe-separated cells (following the Liu et al. 2022 scheme). The output is either a short 1–2 word answer (evaluated by exact-match accuracy on WikiTQ and WikiSQL) or a long-form ~18-word natural language answer (evaluated by Sacre-BLEU on FeTaQA). Internally, the method produces per-token relevance scores in [0,1] that multiplicatively scale table token embeddings before they enter the QA LLM encoder, effectively suppressing irrelevant content without removing any tokens.

## Method

CABINET comprises two complementary modules. First, an Unsupervised Relevance Scorer (URS) — a transformer encoder initialized from the QA LLM encoder — treats token relevance as a latent variable estimated via variational inference (Gaussian reparameterization + sigmoid), trained end-to-end with the QA LLM through cross-entropy answer loss. The URS latent space is structured by a trainable t-SNE-style clustering loss that separates tokens into relevant and non-relevant clusters, augmented by a centroid-separation loss and a sparsification loss that pushes relevance logits toward 0 or 1. Second, a weakly-supervised cell predictor uses a Parsing Statement Generator (Flan T5-xl fine-tuned on ~300 manually annotated question-table pairs) to produce natural-language criteria for relevant rows/columns, and a Cell Highlighter (Flan T5-xl fine-tuned on ToTTo) to identify matching cells; the resulting binary cell scores are linearly combined with URS scores (λ_uns=0.7, λ_cell=0.3) to form the final per-token relevance weight. The QA LLM backbone is OmniTab (BART-Large), and the entire pipeline is trained for 30 epochs with AdamW at lr 1e-5 on 8 A100 GPUs.

## Results

CABINET (560M params) achieves new state-of-the-art on all three main benchmarks: 69.1% exact-match accuracy on WikiTQ, 40.5 Sacre-BLEU on FeTaQA, and 89.5% accuracy on WikiSQL. These results are achieved with orders of magnitude fewer parameters than GPT-3/Codex-based in-context learning methods (175B), outperforming the best few-shot method DATER by 3.2% on WikiTQ and 9.6 S-BLEU on FeTaQA. On the reading comprehension task (SQuAD-v2), the URS component alone improves BART-large from 42.9 to 48.0 accuracy, demonstrating task-level generality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CABINET outperforms all 20 baselines across the three main datasets. Against the strongest table-specific LLM (OmniTab, 406M), CABINET gains +6.4% on WikiTQ (69.1 vs 62.7), +5.6 S-BLEU on FeTaQA (40.5 vs 34.9), and +1.6% on WikiSQL (89.5 vs 87.9). Against the best few/zero-shot GPT-3-based method DATER (175B), CABINET leads by +3.2% on WikiTQ and +9.6 S-BLEU on FeTaQA, despite having ~313× fewer parameters. On WikiSQL, CABINET surpasses the previous SoTA ReasTAP by 0.7% (89.5 vs 88.8), which the authors note represents ~6% of the remaining improvement headroom. Against fine-tuned text-based LLMs (FlanT5-xl, 2.9B), CABINET wins by +4.7% on WikiTQ, +4.3 S-BLEU on FeTaQA, and +1.7% on WikiSQL. Notably, simple prompting of ChatGPT (43.3%) and StructGPT (48.4%) perform poorly on WikiTQ, underscoring the difficulty of table QA for general-purpose LLMs without task-specific relevance mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

CABINET builds on the table-specific LLM backbone (OmniTab/BART-Large) and departs from the hard-decomposition paradigm of DATER and similar GPT-3/Codex-based methods that extract sub-tables via in-context learning. Instead of removing irrelevant content, CABINET introduces a soft, differentiable relevance-weighting mechanism that suppresses noise while preserving all table content, drawing on trainable t-SNE-style clustering (van der Maaten & Hinton, 2008) to structure the latent space of table tokens into relevant and non-relevant groups. The weakly supervised cell-highlighting component repurposes the ToTTo table-to-text generation task as a proxy for identifying question-relevant cells, bridging table understanding and controlled generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly important problem as tables grow larger: LLMs degrade significantly with irrelevant tabular content, and existing retrieval or pruning approaches risk losing critical information. CABINET's generality (validated on both OmniTab and TAPEX backbones) and its extension to reading comprehension (SQuAD-v2) suggest the relevance-weighting principle could transfer beyond table QA. Open questions include whether the exact-match cell highlighting is robust to paraphrasing or OCR noise, whether the ~300-sample annotation requirement can be eliminated, and whether the approach extends to decoder-only LLMs or multimodal table inputs.
