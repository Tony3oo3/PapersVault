## Problem

Existing hyperbolic neural networks for computer vision rely on Euclidean CNN backbones and only project features into hyperbolic space in the task heads, limiting their ability to exploit the hierarchical structure inherent in visual data throughout the feature hierarchy. Additionally, critical Lorentz-model components—2D convolutional layers, batch normalization, and multinomial logistic regression—were missing, preventing the construction of fully hyperbolic vision encoders.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet and CelebA) and produces class labels via a novel Lorentz MLR classifier for classification, or generated images and 2D latent embeddings for VAE-based generation. Unusually, every intermediate feature map is a channel-last tensor where each spatial position holds an n-dimensional hyperbolic vector in the Lorentz model (with the time component stored as an extra channel), so all internal representations live on the hyperboloid rather than in Euclidean space.

## Method

HCNN generalizes the full ResNet-18 architecture to the Lorentz model by providing one-to-one hyperbolic replacements for each CNN component. The Lorentz convolutional layer applies a Lorentz fully-connected (LFC) transformation to the hyperbolic concatenation (HCat) of receptive-field vectors, with padding via origin vectors. Lorentz batch normalization re-centers features using the closed-form Lorentzian centroid and re-scales them by parallel transporting to the origin's tangent space, multiplying by a learnable scale, and transporting back—avoiding the iterative Fréchet mean. The Lorentz MLR classifier computes logits from the signed hyperbolic distance to class hyperplanes, using an Euclidean reparameterization (distance a and orientation z) to eliminate non-convex constraints. Residual connections add space components and recompute the time component, and ReLU is applied to the space component with the time component recalculated, both avoiding costly tangent-space round-trips. The framework is instantiated as both a fully hyperbolic HCNN and a hybrid HECNN that replaces only the highest-hyperbolicity encoder blocks, and is evaluated on classification (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet) and generation (VAE with FID) tasks.

## Results

On image classification with ResNet-18, the hybrid encoder HECNN (Lorentz) achieves the best accuracy across all three datasets: 95.16% on CIFAR-10, 78.76% on CIFAR-100, and 65.96% on Tiny-ImageNet, outperforming the Euclidean baseline by up to 1.04%. The fully hyperbolic HCNN (Lorentz) is competitive at 95.14%, 78.07%, and 65.71% respectively. For image generation, the HCNN-VAE achieves the lowest FID scores (e.g., 89.20 generation FID on CIFAR-10 vs. 92.40 for Euclidean), and under adversarial attacks (PGD, ε=3.2/255 on CIFAR-100), HCNN retains 31.77% accuracy versus 26.30% for the Euclidean baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Euclidean ResNet-18 baseline (He et al., 2015b), HECNN improves accuracy by +1.04% on CIFAR-100 (78.76% vs. 77.72%) and +0.77% on Tiny-ImageNet (65.96% vs. 65.19%), while matching it on CIFAR-10 (95.16% vs. 95.14%). The Hybrid Poincaré baseline (Guo et al., 2022) is outperformed by HECNN on all three datasets, with the largest gap on CIFAR-100 (+1.57%). The fully hyperbolic Poincaré ResNet (van Spengler et al., 2023) is consistently the weakest hyperbolic model, trailing HECNN by up to 3.95% on Tiny-ImageNet (65.96% vs. 62.01%), confirming the Lorentz model's numerical stability advantage. For image generation, the HCNN-VAE outperforms both the Hybrid Poincaré VAE (Mathieu et al., 2019) and Hybrid Lorentz VAE (Nagano et al., 2019) on reconstruction and generation FID across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and CelebA, with the largest improvement on CIFAR-10 generation FID (89.20 vs. 90.13 and 90.91). Notably, the hybrid encoder (HECNN) outperforms the fully hyperbolic model (HCNN) on classification, suggesting that selectively applying hyperbolic geometry to the most hierarchical encoder blocks is more effective than a fully hyperbolic pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of hyperbolic neural network research from NLP and graph domains into computer vision by providing the first fully hyperbolic CNN architecture. It builds directly on Shimizu et al.'s (2020) convolutional layer formalization, Chen et al.'s (2021) Lorentz fully-connected layer, and Qu & Zou's (2022) hyperbolic concatenation, while introducing novel Lorentz-model formulations for 2D convolution, batch normalization (using the Lorentzian centroid from Law et al., 2019), and multinomial logistic regression (generalizing Lebanon & Lafferty's 2004 hyperplane-margin approach). A key departure from prior vision HNN work is the shift from the Poincaré ball to the Lorentz model, motivated by superior numerical stability (Mishne et al., 2022), and the move from hybrid architectures (Euclidean encoder + hyperbolic head) to fully hyperbolic encoders where every layer operates in hyperbolic space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical toolkit of Lorentz-model components that enables fully hyperbolic vision encoders for the first time, and demonstrates that the Lorentz model consistently outperforms the Poincaré ball in vision settings. However, the finding that the hybrid encoder (HECNN) outperforms the fully hyperbolic model (HCNN) suggests that not all network layers benefit from hyperbolic geometry, complicating the design space. Significant open questions remain: the computational overhead is very large (~14× slower than Euclidean ResNet per epoch), scalability to larger architectures and datasets is untested, and the modest accuracy gains (1–2%) must be weighed against this cost. The paper also raises the question of whether current layer generalizations, designed as one-to-one replacements for Euclidean components, truly capture the unique properties of hyperbolic geometry.
