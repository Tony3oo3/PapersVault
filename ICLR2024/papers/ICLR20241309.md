## Problem

Diagonalizing the HiPPO matrix that underlies S4 state-space models is exponentially ill-conditioned, making exact diagonalization numerically infeasible. Existing diagonal SSMs (S4D, S5) sidestep this by discarding the low-rank component of the DPLR structure, but this changes the underlying problem and introduces high-frequency spikes in the transfer function, rendering the models fragile to Fourier-mode input perturbations. The paper asks whether one can retain the full HiPPO structure while still obtaining a stable diagonal initialization.

## Input / Output

The models consume sequential inputs of length L (ranging from 1024 to 16000 in LRA; flattened 32×32 CIFAR-10 pixel sequences for the robustness study) and produce a class label or per-step sequence output. The continuous-time LTI system is discretized at a trainable step size Δt using either bilinear or zero-order-hold methods, so the raw input is a discrete sequence that is mapped through the SSM's state recurrence. For the MIMO variant (S5-PTD), the input and output are multi-channel (m = p > 1), enabling cross-channel communication via parallel scans.

## Method

The paper introduces a perturb-then-diagonalize (PTD) methodology: a small perturbation matrix E is added to the non-normal HiPPO matrix A_H, the perturbed matrix A_H + E is diagonalized using a standard eigensolver, and the resulting eigenvalues initialize the diagonal state matrix A. The perturbation E is computed by solving a soft-constrained optimization that trades off the eigenvector condition number κ(Ṽ_H) against the perturbation size ‖E‖, with a hyperparameter γ controlling the balance. The key theoretical insight is that only the backward error ‖E‖ matters for the transfer function (not the forward error in eigenvalues), and Theorem 3 shows the uniform transfer-function deviation is O(ε log n), where ε = ‖E‖. This yields S4-PTD and S5-PTD models that are uniformly close to the original S4/S4D transfer functions, eliminating the high-frequency spikes that make S4D/S5 vulnerable to Fourier-mode noise, while preserving the computational and architectural advantages of a purely diagonal A.

## Results

On the Long-Range Arena (LRA) benchmark, S5-PTD achieves 87.61% average accuracy across six tasks, the highest among all compared models, slightly edging out S5 (87.46%) and Liquid-S4 (87.32%). S4-PTD achieves 86.58% average accuracy, outperforming S4D (84.89%) by 1.69 points and even surpassing the DPLR-structured S4 (86.09%) by 0.49 points. In robustness experiments on sequential CIFAR-10, S4-PTD maintains high test accuracy under 10% sinusoidal noise perturbations at critical Fourier frequencies, while S4D's accuracy drops significantly, confirming the theoretical prediction of non-uniform convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S4-PTD outperforms S4D (Gu et al., 2022a) by 1.69 points in average LRA accuracy (86.58% vs. 84.89%), with the largest gap on Path-X (96.39% vs. 91.95%) and Text (88.32% vs. 86.18%). S4-PTD also surpasses the original DPLR-structured S4 (Gu et al., 2022b) by 0.49 points on average, despite using a simpler diagonal structure. S5-PTD (Smith et al., 2023 architecture) matches S5's performance (87.61% vs. 87.46% avg), with S5-PTD winning on ListOps, Text, Retrieval, and Pathfinder while S5 retains a slight edge on Path-X. Against the Transformer baseline (Tay et al., 2021), both PTD models achieve substantially higher accuracy (86.58–87.61% vs. 53.66%). The key differentiator is robustness: on sequential CIFAR-10 with Fourier-mode noise at the spike frequencies of |G_Diag|, S4D's test accuracy degrades sharply while S4-PTD remains stable, a property not shared by any of the compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the S4/S4D/S5 line of work on state-space models for sequence modeling by addressing a fundamental gap: the diagonal initialization used in S4D and S5 simply discards the low-rank component of the HiPPO matrix, which changes the underlying diagonalization problem and abandons the theoretical guarantees of HiPPO. The authors instead introduce a 'perturb-then-diagonalize' (PTD) methodology grounded in the pseudospectral theory of non-normal operators (Banks et al., 2021), which regularizes the exponentially ill-conditioned eigenvector problem into a well-posed one while preserving the HiPPO transfer function up to a controlled perturbation. The paper also provides the first fine-grained theoretical comparison of S4 (DPLR) and S4D (diagonal) transfer functions, showing input-wise linear convergence but non-uniform (system-wise) divergence as the state dimension grows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PTD methodology is general and applicable to any non-normal matrix diagonalization in machine learning, not just HiPPO, making it a reusable tool for future SSM initialization schemes. The robustness finding—that S4D/S5 models are vulnerable to specific Fourier-mode perturbations while S4-PTD/S5-PTD are not—has practical implications for deploying SSMs in adversarial or noisy environments. Open questions include whether PTD initialization also improves trained (not just initialized) models, whether the approach extends to other SSM variants (e.g., Mamba, Liquid-S4), and whether the modest LRA accuracy gains (S4-PTD: 86.58 vs. S4D: 84.89) scale to larger models or more complex tasks.
