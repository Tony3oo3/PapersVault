## Problem

While recurrent neural networks can solve tasks with long temporal dependencies, it remains unclear whether long timescales emerge primarily from single-neuron properties (e.g., membrane time constant τ) or from network-mediated interactions (τ_net). Prior work proposed trainable per-neuron timescales as the key mechanism, but the relative contribution of each mechanism and how it depends on the learning objective had not been systematically compared.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240781/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network receives a binary sequence presented one bit per time step, with sequence lengths uniformly sampled from [N+2, 4N]. For N-parity, the output is the XOR of the last N bits; for N-delayed match-to-sample, the output indicates whether the current bit matches the bit N steps earlier. Each RNN has 500 leaky-ReLU neurons (α=0.1) with a per-neuron trainable timescale τ≥1, and a linear readout layer per task head. The network is driven by uncorrelated Bernoulli inputs for timescale estimation over T=10⁵ time steps across 10 trials.

## Method

The paper trains leaky RNNs with per-neuron trainable τ via BPTM (SGD + Nesterov momentum, cross-entropy loss) under two curricula: single-head (train one N at a time, discarding previous heads) and multi-head (add a new readout head for N+1 while retaining and co-training all previous heads). The key finding is that single-head networks increase τ with N and rely on long single-neuron timescales (stabilized by strong inhibition), whereas multi-head networks keep τ→1 and develop long timescales exclusively through recurrent connectivity (τ_net). Network-mediated timescales are estimated by fitting the autocorrelation of each neuron's activity to single- or double-exponential decays, with model selection via AIC. Multi-head networks outperform single-head ones in maximum N solved (≥100 vs ~35 for N-parity), training speed, robustness to ablation and perturbation, and retrainability to unseen N, and the advantage extends to GRU and LSTM architectures.

## Results

On the N-parity task, multi-head curriculum networks reliably reach N≥100 within 1000 epochs, compared to N≈35 for single-head networks. On the N-DMS task, multi-head networks also reach N≥100 versus N≈90 for single-head. Critically, in multi-head networks the single-neuron timescale τ converges to 1 (memoryless neurons) while long timescales emerge purely from recurrent connectivity (τ_net), whereas single-head networks increase τ with N and require strong inhibitory connectivity for stability. Multi-head networks are also more robust to neuron ablation, weight perturbation, and retraining on unseen N values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the multi-head curriculum against the single-head curriculum as the primary baseline, and additionally tests both curricula on GRU (Chung et al., 2014) and LSTM (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997) architectures. Without any curriculum, both GRU and LSTM are extremely slow and unstable for large N, failing to solve N-parity beyond small N values. Introducing the multi-head curriculum dramatically speeds up training for both architectures, enabling them to reach large N comparable to the leaky-RNN. The multi-head curriculum consistently outperforms the single-head curriculum in training speed for GRU and LSTM as well (Fig. S15, S16). The key finding is that the curriculum effect is architecture-agnostic: the multi-head strategy generalizes beyond the leaky-RNN to gated architectures, suggesting it is a general principle for training long-memory RNNs rather than an artifact of the specific leaky-ReLU model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: (1) work showing that trainable single-neuron timescales (membrane time constants) improve RNN performance on long-memory tasks (Perez-Nieves et al., 2021; Tallec & Ollivier, 2018; Quax et al., 2020; Smith et al., 2023b), and (2) work on network-mediated timescales arising from recurrent connectivity structure (Ostojic, 2014; Chaudhuri et al., 2014; Litwin-Kumar & Doiron, 2012; Shi et al., 2023). The key departure is that prior studies treated these mechanisms in isolation or assumed single-neuron timescale adaptability was universally beneficial, whereas this paper explicitly contrasts them under different training curricula and shows the optimal mechanism depends on the learning objective. The multi-head curriculum framing also extends curriculum learning ideas (Bengio et al., 2009; Krueger & Dayan, 2009) by showing that the curriculum itself determines which timescale mechanism the network adopts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that trainable single-neuron timescales provide no advantage (and fixed τ=1 is as good as trained τ) in multi-head settings directly challenges a widely held assumption in the RNN literature, and the practical implication—that a simple multi-head curriculum significantly improves GRU and LSTM training for large-N tasks—offers a broadly applicable training strategy. The neuroscience connection (biological networks primarily modulate synaptic strengths rather than biophysical time constants) strengthens the biological plausibility of the network-mediated mechanism. However, the scope is limited to two simple binary-sequence tasks with controllably increasing memory requirements, 500-neuron networks, and a specific leaky-ReLU architecture; whether the multi-head curriculum generalizes to more complex, realistic tasks (language, time series) or to architectures without an explicit τ parameter remains an open question.
