## Problem

While double descent is well-characterized in supervised learning, the effect of overparameterization on Temporal Difference (TD) learning in RL remains largely unexplored. The discount factor γ introduces non-symmetric matrices that invalidate standard random matrix tools, and the non-i.i.d. nature of RL samples complicates analysis. This paper asks: how does the ratio of network parameters N to distinct visited states m govern the Mean-Squared Bellman Error (MSBE) of regularized LSTD, and does a double descent phenomenon emerge in RL analogous to supervised learning?

## Input / Output

The method consumes n transition tuples (state, reward, next-state) sampled from a Markov Reward Process, a fixed random weight matrix W ∈ R^{N×d} with i.i.d. Gaussian entries, a scaled l2-regularization parameter λ = λ_{m,n}/(mn), and a discount factor γ ∈ [0,1). States are represented as d-dimensional vectors (d=50 in experiments). The output is the LSTD parameter vector θ̂ ∈ R^N, but the primary theoretical deliverables are deterministic limit expressions for the empirical MSBE and true MSBE, each decomposed into a leading term plus a second-order correction factor (Δ or Δ̂) that depends on the correction factor δ — the unique positive solution to a fixed-point trace equation involving the deterministic Gram matrix Φ and the transition structure matrices Û_n, V̂_n.

## Method

The paper analyzes regularized LSTD with random features in a novel double asymptotic regime where N (parameters), m (distinct visited states), and d (state dimension) all grow to infinity while maintaining constant ratios. In the lazy training regime, the overparameterized single-hidden-layer network is replaced by a fixed random feature map σ(Ws), reducing the problem to linear TD learning. The key technical contribution is deriving a deterministic equivalent for the resolvent Q_m(λ) of a non-symmetric matrix (arising from the discount factor γ), which requires new analysis of eigenspectra of products of positive-definite symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices. From this resolvent, the paper obtains closed-form deterministic limits for both the empirical MSBE (Theorem 5.2) and the true MSBE (Theorem 5.3), each featuring a correction factor δ (from the N/m ratio) and a second-order term Δ (from finite-N fluctuations). These correction terms are shown to be responsible for the double descent peak at the interpolation threshold N=m, and they vanish as λ increases or as all states are visited (m→|S|).

## Results

The paper's main quantitative finding is that the correction factor δ (governing the double descent) drops sharply from an order of 10⁷ in the under-parameterized regime (N < m) to an order of 10¹ in the over-parameterized regime (N > m) at the interpolation threshold N = m, for a small regularization parameter λ = 10⁻⁹. The deterministic limits derived in Theorems 5.2 and 5.3 match the numerically evaluated empirical and true MSBE 'almost perfectly' across Taxi-v3 (m=310, n=5000), Gridworld (m=386, n=5000), and a synthetic ergodic MRP (m=499, n=3000), averaged over 30 random feature instances. The double descent peak appears only in the true MSBE (not the empirical MSBE) and vanishes as λ increases or as the fraction of visited states m/|S| approaches 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no competing methods to benchmark against; the 'comparison' is between the derived deterministic asymptotic predictions (Theorems 5.1–5.3) and direct numerical evaluation of the original MSBE definitions on finite-size instances. The theory matches the numerical results closely across all three environments (Taxi-v3, Gridworld, synthetic ergodic MRP) and across a range of N/m ratios, regularization strengths, and discount factors. The paper also contrasts the empirical MSBE (which shows no double descent, remaining near its minimum at N=m) with the true MSBE (which exhibits the characteristic peak at the interpolation threshold), identifying the second-order correction term Δ as the primary driver of the discrepancy. The discount factor γ has little effect on the double descent shape, and the phenomenon disappears entirely when all states are visited (m = |S|), consistent with Remark 7.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the double asymptotic regime analysis from supervised learning (Louart et al., 2018; Liao et al., 2020) to reinforcement learning, specifically for regularized LSTD with random features in the lazy training regime. The key technical departure is that the discount factor γ renders the central matrix non-symmetric, invalidating standard random matrix tools (e.g., Stieltjes transforms) used in the supervised setting, requiring a more involved analysis based on eigenspectra of products of positive-definite symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices. It also generalizes Thomas (2022), who studied off-policy linear TD on rank-1 transition matrices, to the on-policy setting with a more general ratio (N/m rather than N/|S|) and no rank assumptions on the transition matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical characterization of double descent in RL, identifying the correction terms (δ and Δ) responsible for the peak in true MSBE around the interpolation threshold N=m. The finding that double descent appears in the true MSBE but not the empirical MSBE, and that it vanishes when all states are visited or l2-regularization is increased, provides concrete guidance for practitioners tuning model size and regularization in TD-based RL. Limitations are significant: the analysis covers only one hidden layer, on-policy policy evaluation, and small environments (≤500 states), leaving open whether the results extend to deep networks, off-policy settings, or control tasks like Q-learning.
