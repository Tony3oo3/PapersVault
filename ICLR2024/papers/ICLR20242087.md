## Problem

Existing layout-to-image diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off: fully fine-tuning a pretrained text-to-image model (e.g., FreestyleNet) achieves layout faithfulness but destroys text editability, while freezing the base model and adding a lightweight adapter (e.g., ControlNet, T2I-Adapter) preserves text control but fails to reliably comply with the layout condition. The root cause is that the standard MSE noise-prediction loss only implicitly leverages layout information, providing no direct per-pixel supervision to enforce alignment.

## Input / Output

The model takes a per-pixel semantic label map (19 classes for Cityscapes, 150 for ADE20K) and a free-form text prompt as joint conditions, and produces a single photorealistic RGB image at 512×512 resolution (512×1024 for Cityscapes evaluation). The layout is injected into the UNet denoiser via a ControlNet-style conditioning branch, while the text prompt is processed through the standard cross-attention mechanism inherited from Stable Diffusion v1.5. Inference uses DDIM sampling with 25 steps in the latent space of the SD autoencoder.

## Method

ALDM augments the standard diffusion training loop with two components. First, a trainable segmentation-based discriminator (UperNet) is trained to perform multi-class semantic segmentation on real images and to classify generated denoised images into an additional 'fake' class, yielding an explicit per-pixel adversarial loss that directly supervises layout alignment—complementary to the original MSE noise loss. Second, a multistep unrolling strategy recursively applies K=9 denoising steps from a randomly sampled timestep, feeding all intermediate denoised estimates into the discriminator so that the adversarial objective enforces consistent layout adherence over a time window rather than at a single step. The discriminator is jointly trained (not frozen) to prevent the generator from collapsing to a mean mode, and the adversarial loss is weighted at λ=0.1 relative to the noise loss. The entire approach is applied on top of ControlNet + Stable Diffusion v1.5, requiring no architectural changes to the base diffusion model.

## Results

On Cityscapes, ALDM (ControlNet + adversarial supervision + multistep unrolling) achieves 63.9 mIoU, 51.2 FID, and 0.856 TIFA, outperforming ControlNet (55.2 mIoU, 57.1 FID, 0.822 TIFA) and T2I-Adapter (37.1 mIoU, 58.3 FID, 0.902 TIFA). On ADE20K, ALDM reaches 36.0 mIoU and 0.888 TIFA, surpassing all SD-based baselines. For domain generalization (Cityscapes→ACDC), ALDM-augmented HRNet improves mIoU from 41.48 to 53.03 (+11.55 points), and SegFormer from 47.04 to 53.78 (+6.74 points), approaching the oracle upper bound of 65.90/63.56.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet (the default backbone), ALDM wins on all three key metrics on Cityscapes: mIoU improves from 55.2 to 63.9, FID drops from 57.1 to 51.2, and TIFA rises from 0.822 to 0.856; on ADE20K, mIoU jumps from 30.4 to 36.0 and TIFA from 0.838 to 0.888. Against T2I-Adapter, ALDM dramatically improves layout faithfulness (mIoU 63.9 vs 37.1 on Cityscapes, 36.0 vs 24.0 on ADE20K) at a small cost in text editability (TIFA 0.856 vs 0.902 on Cityscapes). Against FreestyleNet, ALDM trades a modest mIoU gap (63.9 vs 68.8 on Cityscapes) for a massive gain in text controllability (TIFA 0.856 vs 0.300) and sample diversity (Recall 0.68 vs 0.44), making it far more useful for data augmentation. In the domain generalization task (Table 4), ALDM consistently outperforms ControlNet (53.03 vs 51.31 HRNet-ACDC), FreestyleNet (53.03 vs 50.27), and ISSA (53.03 vs 50.05) on the full ACDC benchmark, with the largest per-class gains on small objects like traffic light (+2.03) and person (+1.38) over ControlNet, confirming that layout faithfulness is critical for downstream segmentation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the segmentation-based discriminator concept from GAN-based layout-to-image synthesis (OASIS, Sushko et al. 2022) into the training pipeline of latent diffusion models, specifically on top of ControlNet-style layout conditioning for Stable Diffusion. It departs from prior diffusion-based L2I work (FreestyleNet, T2I-Adapter, ControlNet) by arguing that the standard MSE noise-prediction loss only implicitly leverages layout information, and introduces explicit per-pixel adversarial supervision to close this gap. The multistep unrolling strategy, which recursively unrolls K denoising steps during training to mimic inference-time sampling, draws an analogy to Model Predictive Control from control theory and has no direct precedent in diffusion model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is demonstrated through a ~12 mIoU gain in domain generalization (Cityscapes→ACDC) for semantic segmentation, which is directly relevant to safety-critical autonomous driving deployment. The method is modular and consistently improves multiple SD adaptation strategies (OFT, T2I-Adapter, ControlNet), suggesting broad applicability. Open questions include the lack of theoretical justification for combining adversarial and diffusion objectives, the attribute leakage problem inherited from Stable Diffusion during text editing, and whether the multistep unrolling can be productively combined with RLHF or time-dependent loss weighting as the authors speculate.
