## Problem

Conventional network embedding methods use low-dimensional vectors (e.g., 128-dim) that are inherently low-rank approximations of the typically high-rank information matrix encoding node interactions, limiting expressiveness. Directly increasing embedding dimension causes over-fitting and linearly scaling computational cost, making high-dimensional NE impractical. This paper opens a new paradigm where embedding dimension grows exponentially with the number of parameters (e.g., 2^32-dim) while maintaining O(p) time and space complexity.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a non-attributed undirected network G=(V,E) or node sequences as input, and produces node embeddings as product states x_i = ⊗_{c=1}^{C} u_{ic} in an (p/C)^C-dimensional real Hilbert space. Crucially, the full high-dimensional embedding is never materialized: all operations (inner products, Euclidean distances, gradient computation) are performed through the O(p) decomposition of sub-embedding inner products. For node2ket+, an index table I(i,c) enables parameter sharing across nodes, reducing storage to C(Nq)^{1/C} sub-embedding parameters plus NC index entries.

## Method

Each node embedding is constructed as a tensor (Kronecker) product of C sub-embeddings u_{ic} ∈ R^{p/C}, yielding a product state in exponentially high-dimensional Hilbert space. The key computational trick is that the inner product ⟨x_i, x_j⟩ = ∏_c ⟨u_{ic}, u_{jc}⟩ decomposes into C independent O(p/C) operations, so training never touches the full embedding. Two constraints are enforced during optimization: unit-norm on each sub-embedding (preventing collapse and ensuring valid quantum states) and positive sub-embedding inner products (preventing sign ambiguity in the product). The optimizer uses Riemannian Adagrad on the unit hypersphere with three orders of geodesic update, asynchronous batch updates, and active monitoring/correction of negative inner products. Theoretically, the paper shows via the Hadamard product structure X^T H = A_{(1)}(G) ⊙ ... ⊙ A_{(C)}(G) that node2ket achieves high-rank (potentially full-rank) approximation of the information matrix, with a sufficient condition involving Kruskal rank. node2ket+ further introduces parameter sharing guided by multi-resolution Louvain community detection for compressive embedding.

## Results

On medium-scale networks (p=128, C=8), node2ket achieves network reconstruction precision of 97.90% on GR-QC, 88.97% on DBLP, and 81.56% on PPI, dramatically outperforming the best conventional baseline (node2vec at 59.92%, 18.74%, and 43.46% respectively). For link prediction, node2ket reaches 96.55% on GR-QC and 95.37% on DBLP, surpassing the strongest baseline (node2vec at 91.72% and 82.09%). The compressive variant node2ket+ matches or exceeds node2ket on network reconstruction (e.g., 98.07% on GR-QC) while using only 72–88% of the parameters, demonstrating that explicit community-structure encoding via parameter sharing does not sacrifice quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Node2ket decisively outperforms all eight baselines on network reconstruction, with the largest gaps over node2vec (the strongest conventional SGNS-based method): 97.90 vs. 59.92 on GR-QC (+38 pts) and 88.97 vs. 18.74 on DBLP (+70 pts). On link prediction, node2ket leads node2vec by 4.83 pts on BC (89.28 vs. 77.58) and 13.28 pts on DBLP (95.37 vs. 82.09), while ProNE and HHNE are the closest competitors on LP (ProNE at 85.31 on DBLP, HHNE at 85.92) but still trail by ~10 pts. LINE is consistently the weakest baseline across both tasks, and GraphSAGE performs poorly on all tasks (e.g., 0.93 on GR-QC NR) because it relies on node features unavailable in the non-attributed setting. The word2ket-inspired variant w2k+n2k (CP-decomposition embeddings optimized with node2ket's constraints) is the second-best method overall, confirming that the product-state construction (rank-1) is superior to the entangled-state (rank-R) formulation. Node2ket+ with Louvain-based parameter sharing matches or slightly exceeds node2ket on NR (e.g., 98.07 vs. 97.90 on GR-QC) while reducing parameter count by 12–28%, validating the compressive embedding approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends word2ket (Panahi et al., 2020), which introduced quantum Hilbert space embeddings for word quantization using CP decomposition (entangled states), by adapting the tensor-product construction to network embedding with product states instead. This substitution is critical: product states enable O(p) inner product computation via the factorization ⟨x_i, x_j⟩ = ∏_c ⟨u_ic, u_jc⟩, whereas word2ket's entangled-state form required explicit high-dimensional vectors and a neural decoder, limiting it to dimensions ≤ 8000. The theoretical analysis grounds the method in implicit matrix factorization (Levy & Goldberg, 2014; Qiu et al., 2018), showing that the Hadamard product structure of X^T H yields exponentially higher rank than conventional low-rank factorization, and the optimization (Riemannian Adagrad with normalization and positive-inner-product constraints) departs from the standard asynchronous SGD used in prior NE methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges two entrenched assumptions in network embedding: that high-dimensional embeddings inevitably overfit, and that they scale linearly in computational cost with dimension. By demonstrating that product-state construction in Hilbert space achieves both O(p) training complexity and superior downstream performance across five datasets and three tasks, it opens a viable path to high-rank information preservation that was previously considered impractical. Limitations include restriction to non-attributed networks, optimization only in the spherical (quantum Hilbert) geometry, and reliance on standard sampling strategies without explicit structural exploitation. The LIBN2K C++ toolkit and the compressive node2ket+ variant (using Louvain partition for parameter sharing) provide practical entry points, but the quantum-mechanical framing remains largely decorative—the core mathematics is Kronecker/Khatri-Rao/Hadamard products.
