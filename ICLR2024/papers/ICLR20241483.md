## Problem

Adversarial training (AT) suffers from robust overfitting, where robust test accuracy degrades as training progresses, creating a large gap between best-epoch and last-epoch performance. Conventional remedies such as regularization, data augmentation, and early stopping fail to close this gap effectively, and early stopping is further complicated by the double-descent phenomenon in AT. The paper asks whether actively forgetting information in the network—inspired by biological active forgetting—can mitigate this overfitting while improving robust generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241483/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces class labels, operating within the standard ℓ∞ or ℓ2 adversarial threat model (e.g., ε=8/255 for ℓ∞). A distinguishing detail is that inference is performed by a separate stable model (an exponential moving average of intermediate network states) rather than the last-trained network, effectively using a self-ensemble of consolidated knowledge for prediction.

## Method

FOMO cycles through three phases during adversarial training: (1) a forgetting phase that randomly reinitializes a fixed fraction (s=3.5–5%) of weights in the last two residual blocks to a uniform distribution, (2) a relearning phase of e_r=5 epochs where the network is trained with the standard adversarial loss plus a consistency regularization term (KL divergence between the current network and a stable model on both clean and adversarial inputs), and (3) a consolidation phase that updates the stable model via exponential moving average (α_c=0.999). Forgetting is restricted to later layers because they have higher memorization capacity and are more prone to overfitting, while earlier layers retain generalized features. The stable model accumulates knowledge across multiple relearning cycles and serves as the inference model, yielding a flatter loss landscape and a near-zero gap between best and last robust test accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PreAct-ResNet-18 (ε∞=8/255), FOMO achieves 56.68% best and 56.46% last PGD-20 robust accuracy, with a best-to-last gap of only −0.22% compared to −7.88% for vanilla PGD-AT. The last-epoch robust accuracy of FOMO (56.46%) exceeds the best-epoch robust accuracy of every baseline, including KD+SWA (54.59%) and AWP (55.39%). FOMO also attains the highest trade-off score (67.04) between natural and robust accuracy, outperforming PGD-AT (58.12) and AWP (65.50). Under AutoAttack, FOMO reaches 51.37% best and 51.28% last robust accuracy on CIFAR-10, again the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOMO outperforms all five main baselines (PGD-AT, TRADES, KD+SWA, PGD-AT+TE, AWP) on both best and last PGD-20 robust accuracy across both architectures. The most striking result is that FOMO's last-epoch robust accuracy (56.46% on PreAct-ResNet-18) surpasses the best-epoch robust accuracy of every baseline, effectively eliminating the need for early stopping. Against the strongest baseline AWP, FOMO gains +1.29% best and +1.73% last PGD-20 accuracy on PreAct-ResNet-18, and +0.84% best / +0.47% last on WideResNet-34-10. Under the more challenging AutoAttack evaluation (Table 3), FOMO achieves 51.37%/51.28% (best/last) on CIFAR-10 and 27.57%/27.49% on CIFAR-100, beating MLCATWP (50.70%/50.32% and 25.86%/25.18%) and IDBH[Strong] (50.74%/49.99%). On CIFAR-100 and SVHN (Table 2), FOMO similarly leads all baselines in both best and last robust accuracy, with a trade-off score of 40.73 on CIFAR-100 and 72.44 on SVHN, the highest among all methods tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOMO builds directly on the adversarial training framework of Madry et al. (2017) but departs from prior robust-overfitting mitigations (KD+SWA, AWP, temporal ensembling, MLCAT) that treat the network as a monolithic unit and apply external regularization (weight perturbation, knowledge distillation, loss smoothing). Instead, FOMO introduces a biologically-motivated 'active forgetting' mechanism—periodically reinitializing a random subset of weights in later layers—combined with a stable-model consolidation step and consistency regularization, framing the training loop as an interleaved cycle of forgetting, relearning, and consolidation. This positions the work at the intersection of adversarial training and cognitive neuroscience, where the key conceptual departure is that destructive weight perturbation (rather than smoothing or ensembling) is the primary driver of robust generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Robust overfitting remains a practical bottleneck for deploying adversarially trained models, and FOMO's near-zero best-to-last accuracy gap (Δ ≈ −0.22 vs. −7.88 for PGD-AT) with simultaneous absolute robustness gains offers a simple, computationally cheap (137 s vs. 133 s per epoch) remedy that avoids the validation overhead of early stopping. However, the underlying mechanism—why random reinitialization of a small weight subset in later layers specifically regularizes adversarial training—lacks a theoretical account, and the authors explicitly flag this as future work. Generalization beyond small-scale CNNs (CIFAR, SVHN, Tiny-ImageNet) to larger architectures and datasets (e.g., ImageNet, transformers) remains untested, limiting confidence in the approach's scalability.
