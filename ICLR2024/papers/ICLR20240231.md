## Problem

Batch Normalization is widely used in CNNs and empirically contributes to generalization, but its role has been attributed almost exclusively to improving training dynamics rather than expressiveness. Frankle et al. (2021) observed that training only BN parameters on randomly initialized networks can achieve nontrivial classification accuracy, yet no rigorous theoretical guarantee existed for whether normalization layers alone are sufficient for universal function approximation in convolutional architectures.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a target convolutional layer (weight tensor W^(t) ∈ R^{c2×c0×kt} and bias vector) and produces affine normalization parameters (γ, β) for a random source network that exactly or approximately matches the target. Empirically, the method operates on 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, producing 10- or 100-class labels. The input domain is assumed compact and contained in [-1,1]^{c0×d0}, and the source network uses VGG18-like architectures with 64-channel intermediate layers and 3×3 convolutional filters.

## Method

The core result proves that two consecutive random convolutional layers with trainable normalization parameters (γ, β) can exactly represent any target convolutional layer, provided the hidden width satisfies c1 ≥ c2·c0·dim(k2,k1) − c2 + 1. The mechanism works by composing two random convolutions to form a larger tensor, then using the scale parameters γ^(1) and γ^(2) as linear combination coefficients to select the correct features; the problem reduces to solving a homogeneous linear system Mγ^(1) = 0 whose nullspace is guaranteed nontrivial with high probability. For nonlinear activations (ReLU, LeakyReLU, tanh, sigmoid), the paper shows approximate reconstruction by operating in a region where the activation is nearly linear, with error bounded by ε. The construction extends to deeper source networks (Theorem 2.5) where L layers of lower width can replace two very wide layers, trading additional trainable parameters for reduced per-layer width, and to residual blocks by modifying the linear system to Mγ^(1) = c.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, reconstructing a fully trained VGG18-like target (width 64, 592,657 params) using only trained BN parameters over random weights matches the target accuracy within 0.1–0.5% across all tested activation functions (ReLU: 94.12% target vs. 94.12±0.07% with orthogonal init; Tanh: 93.66% vs. 93.5±0.1%; Sigmoid: 92.9% vs. 92.8±0.04%). On CIFAR-100, the same protocol yields ReLU 70.46% target vs. 70.34±0.02% reconstructed and Tanh 69.65% vs. 69.5±0.03%. At width 100, reconstruction achieves 94.93±0.03% (CIFAR-10) and 73.37±0.05% (CIFAR-100), matching targets of 94.94% and 73.34% respectively. In contrast, training BN parameters from scratch on a comparable architecture (width 100, +10⁴ extra channels) reaches only 90.35±0.1% on CIFAR-10, far below the 94%+ target.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between a fully trained target network and a source network with frozen random weights whose only trained parameters are the BN scale (γ) and shift (β) vectors. Across all four activation functions and four random initialization schemes (He Normal, He Uniform, Orthogonal, Sparse He Normal), the reconstructed network matches the target within ≤0.5% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 and ≤0.3% on CIFAR-100, confirming the theoretical sufficiency of BN parameters for exact representation. All initialization schemes perform comparably, with orthogonal and sparse He Normal showing the tightest confidence intervals. The paper also contrasts its explicit construction approach with the 'train BN from scratch' protocol of Frankle et al. (2021): even with substantial overparameterization (+10⁴ channels) and extended warmup, training BN alone from random initialization plateaus at 90.35% on CIFAR-10, roughly 4 points below the 94%+ target, suggesting that standard optimization schedules are insufficient to discover the required affine combinations. Theoretically, the paper improves on Giannou et al. (2023) by reducing the minimum width requirement for fully-connected layers from c₂c₀ to c₂c₀−c₂+1 and extending the result to convolutional and residual architectures with general activation functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the expressiveness analysis of Giannou et al. (2023), who proved that tuning only normalization layers suffices for universal approximation in fully-connected ReLU networks, to the broader setting of convolutional and residual architectures with a wider class of activation functions (ReLU, LeakyReLU, tanh, sigmoid). It also improves the width bound for the fully-connected case by exploiting the scale parameters of the second normalization layer. The work is closely related to lottery ticket existence proofs (Burkholz 2022, da Cunha et al. 2022) in that both construct target representations from random source networks, but replaces the pruning mechanism with affine linear transformations. It provides a rigorous proof of the conjecture raised by Frankle et al. (2021) based on their empirical observations that training BN alone yields nontrivial performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result reframes normalization layers from being purely a training-dynamics tool to also being a representational mechanism that linearly combines random features, which has implications for memory and compute savings if a high proportion of network parameters can remain random. However, the practical impact is tempered by two findings: the width requirements scale quadratically in input/output dimensions, and standard training schedules (SGD with cosine annealing) fail to achieve the theoretical reconstruction even with overparameterization, suggesting that specialized optimization algorithms for normalization-only training remain an open problem. Future work on inductive biases in random weight distributions and on learning schedules that can exploit the available degrees of freedom could make these theoretical guarantees practically accessible.
