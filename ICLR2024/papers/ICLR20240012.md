## Problem

Network pruning for LLMs typically requires expensive fine-tuning or re-training to recover performance, which is impractical given the massive parameter counts and training data volumes of modern LLMs. Existing training-free pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) produce sparse models but suffer significant performance degradation, especially at high sparsity levels (>50%), leaving a gap between the initial pruning step and the ideal sparse model. The paper asks whether sparse LLMs can be further refined—effectively fine-tuned—without any backpropagation or weight updates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-pruned sparse LLM (produced by any existing criterion such as Wanda, SparseGPT, or Magnitude) along with calibration data (128 segments of 2048 tokens from C4) that provides input activations A ∈ R^{C_in × N×L} for each layer. It operates layer-by-layer on weight matrices W ∈ R^{C_out × C_in} and their binary masks, and outputs an updated binary mask at the same sparsity rate that reduces the layer-wise reconstruction error between sparse and dense outputs. Crucially, no weight values are modified—only the 0/1 mask entries change, meaning the method is purely a mask-adaptation procedure with no parameter updates.

## Method

DSOT adapts Dynamic Sparse Training's pruning-and-growing mechanism to a training-free setting by converting the optimization objective from loss minimization to reconstruction-error minimization between dense and sparse layer outputs. For each weight row, it iteratively grows one pruned weight (selected via a criterion that considers both the expectation and variance of reconstruction error reduction across input tokens, with the sign of E[Δ_r] determining the search direction) and prunes one active weight (selected via a transformed Wanda metric |W|·||A||_2 with an additional constraint that the pruned weight must positively contribute to error reduction, preserving outlier channels). The reconstruction error is updated via simple addition/subtraction after each grow-prune pair, and the loop terminates when the error drops below a threshold ε or a maximum cycle count T is reached. The entire procedure requires only initial matrix multiplications for error computation and subsequent arithmetic updates—no backpropagation, gradients, or Hessian inverses—making it linear-time and GPU-efficient.

## Results

At 60% sparsity on WikiText-2, DSOT consistently reduces perplexity across all model families (LLaMA-V1/V2, Vicuna, OPT, 7B–70B). For LLaMA-7B, Wanda+DSOT achieves 10.22 ppl vs. Wanda's 10.69 (−0.47), and SparseGPT+DSOT reaches 10.41 vs. ~11.17 (−0.76). The gains grow substantially at higher sparsity: at 70% sparsity on LLaMA-7B, Wanda+DSOT cuts perplexity from 88.84 to 62.05 (−26.79). In terms of efficiency, Wanda+DSOT completes in 4.3 s on a single A100 for LLaMA-7B, versus 209 s for SparseGPT and 4 h for LoRA fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSOT is applied as a post-pruning refinement on top of three existing pruning methods and one PEFT baseline. Against **Magnitude pruning**, DSOT yields dramatic gains, e.g., reducing OPT-13B perplexity from 1.1e6 to 14.01 and LLaMA-V2-70B from 13.35 to 6.77 at 60% sparsity. Against **SparseGPT** (Frantar & Alistarh, ICML 2023), DSOT consistently wins by 0.76–1.6 ppl across LLaMA sizes at 60% sparsity, while being ~50× faster (4.3 s vs. 209 s for 7B). Against **Wanda** (Sun et al., 2023), DSOT improves perplexity by 0.47 ppl at 60% sparsity (LLaMA-7B: 10.69→10.22) and by 26.79 ppl at 70% sparsity (88.84→62.05), with the gap widening at higher sparsity levels. Against **LoRA** (Hu et al., 2021) fine-tuning on 50%-sparse LLaMA-7B, DSOT achieves a slightly higher perplexity (7.12 vs. 6.87) but in 4.3 s versus 4 h, and without introducing additional parameters. DSOT also extends to N:M structured sparsity (2:4, 4:8), where it still outperforms both SparseGPT and Wanda by 0.2–0.64 ppl.

## Relation to Prior Work

DSOT repurposes the pruning-and-growing mechanism from Dynamic Sparse Training (Mocanu et al., 2018) by decoupling it from the backpropagation loop entirely, converting the optimization target from loss minimization to layer-wise reconstruction error reduction. It inherits the layer-wise reconstruction problem formulation from SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023) and incorporates a transformed Wanda metric (Sun et al., 2023) into its pruning criterion to preserve outlier features. The key conceptual departure is that DSOT treats mask adaptation as a standalone, training-free iterative procedure that can be layered on top of any existing pruning method (Magnitude, SparseGPT, Wanda), rather than being an alternative pruning algorithm itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for on-device LLM deployment: DSOT reduces fine-tuning cost from hours (LoRA) to seconds while achieving comparable perplexity, making sparse LLM refinement accessible without GPU clusters. However, the absolute performance gains are moderate (e.g., ~0.5 ppl at 60% sparsity), and at extreme sparsity (80–90%) the models remain largely unusable, suggesting that mask-level adaptation alone cannot compensate for the information lost by removing too many weights. Open questions include whether the approach extends to structured sparsity patterns beyond N:M, whether it composes well with quantization, and whether the reconstruction-error objective is truly optimal versus other surrogate signals for mask quality.
