## Problem

Structural models for knowledge graph link prediction suffer from a severe scalability problem because they operate on the entire KG, making them infeasible on large-scale graphs like OGBL-WIKIKG2 (2.5M entities, 17M edges). The paper asks whether all graph information is necessary for a specific query, motivated by the observation that only a small fraction of entities (e.g., 10%) is relevant to answering a given link prediction query.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241755/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full knowledge graph G=(V,R,E) and a query (u,q,?) as input, and produces a query-dependent subgraph G_s (with |V_s| ≪ |V|) plus prediction scores ŷ_o for each entity o in the sampled subgraph. The sampling ratios r_V^q and r_E^q are relation-dependent hyperparameters, meaning different query relations can yield different subgraph scales. Unlike full-graph methods that score all |V| entities, the output is restricted to the ~10% of entities retained in the subgraph, which the PPR sampler guarantees covers the true answer with high probability (Coverage Ratio > 0.87 across datasets).

## Method

The framework decouples link prediction into a one-shot PPR-based sampler and a GNN-based predictor. The sampler iteratively computes Personalized PageRank scores from the query entity u (damping α=0.85, K=100 steps) and extracts top-K entities and edges via TopK operations, preserving local structure without any learnable parameters. The predictor then performs L-layer message passing (using DRUM, NBFNet, or RED-GNN message functions) on the subgraph with query-dependent initialization h_o^(0)=1(o=u), trained with binary cross-entropy. A bi-level optimization problem is solved via sequential model-based Bayesian Optimization with a random forest surrogate to jointly search over data-space hyperparameters (r_V^q, r_E^q) and model-space hyperparameters (L, aggregation, activation, dimension, etc.). A data-split trick (partitioning training edges into observation and query sets) accelerates the search by avoiding full-graph training. The paper further proves a theoretical bound (Theorem 1) showing that extrapolating from small training subgraphs to much larger test subgraphs causes prediction collapse, justifying the need for balanced subgraph scales.

## Results

The one-shot-subgraph method achieves state-of-the-art MRR on all five benchmarks: 0.567 (WN18RR), 0.547 (NELL-995), 0.606 (YAGO3-10), 0.8430 (OGBL-BIOKG), and 0.6755 (OGBL-WIKIKG2). On the largest dataset OGBL-WIKIKG2, it delivers a 16.6% improvement in Test MRR over the best semantic baseline (TripleRE, 0.5794) while using only 6.8M parameters versus 500M+. The method achieves an average 94.4% reduction in prediction time and 6.9% improvement in effectiveness, requiring only ~10% of entities (e.g., 4,090 out of 40,943 on WN18RR) to answer each query.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 baselines across all five datasets. Against the strongest structural baselines, it improves MRR by 1.6 points over NBFNet (0.567 vs 0.551) on WN18RR, by 0.4 points over RED-GNN (0.547 vs 0.543) on NELL-995, and by a substantial 4.7 points over RED-GNN (0.606 vs 0.559) on YAGO3-10. On the OGB leaderboards, it surpasses the best semantic model TripleRE by 16.6% in Test MRR on OGBL-WIKIKG2 (0.6755 vs 0.5794) and by 0.8 points on OGBL-BIOKG (0.8430 vs 0.8348), while using 976K–6.8M parameters versus 187M–1.25B for semantic models. Among sampling-based structural methods, it significantly outperforms DPMPN (0.567 vs 0.482 MRR on WN18RR) by decoupling sampling from prediction rather than coupling them layer-by-layer. The efficiency gains are dramatic: training time drops from 493.8 min to 28.2 min for NBFNet and from 1382.9 min to 76.3 min for RED-GNN on YAGO3-10 (~94% reduction), while simultaneously improving accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper decouples KG link prediction into a one-shot subgraph sampling step (using non-parametric Personalized PageRank from Jeh & Widom, 2003) followed by prediction on the extracted subgraph, departing from both semantic models that encode the entire graph into embeddings and structural models (NBFNet, RED-GNN, DRUM) that propagate over the full graph. Unlike layer-wise sampling methods (DPMPN, AdaProp, AStarNet) that couple sampling with each GNN layer, or subgraph-wise methods (GraIL, CoMPILE) that extract one enclosing subgraph per candidate answer, the proposed framework extracts a single query-dependent subgraph in a decoupled, predictor-agnostic manner. The bi-level optimization for searching data-adaptive configurations (sampling ratios, model depth, message functions) via Bayesian optimization with random forests further distinguishes it from prior work that fixes hyperparameters manually.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that only ~10% of entities identified by a simple non-parametric heuristic suffice for link prediction, yielding 94.4% efficiency improvement and 6.9% effectiveness gain across five large-scale benchmarks, making deep GNNs (8+ layers) feasible on graphs where they previously ran out of memory. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) reveals a fundamental limitation: extrapolating from small training subgraphs to much larger test subgraphs degrades prediction reliability, suggesting a necessary balance in subgraph scale. Open questions include robustness to adversarial perturbations (deleting 50% of edges drops MRR by ~40%), extension to inductive settings with new entities/relations, and potential synergy with LLMs for few-shot KG reasoning.
