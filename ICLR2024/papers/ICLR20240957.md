## Problem

Data pruning and dataset distillation methods aim to reduce training cost by selecting or generating small subsets of examples, but their expensive subset-selection overhead (e.g., pretraining auxiliary models for hundreds of minutes) and modest accuracy gains raise the question of whether they are justified from a time-to-accuracy perspective. This paper shows that a simple, overlooked extension of the random baseline—repeatedly sampling a fresh random subset each epoch—outperforms 32 state-of-the-art pruning and distillation methods in both accuracy and end-to-end runtime across four datasets including ImageNet.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard labeled dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224, or OpenWebText for GPT-2 pretraining) along with a selection ratio r ∈ (0,1], batch size, and total number of training rounds X. The output is a conventionally trained neural network (ResNet-18, ViT, or GPT-2) with no architectural modification; the only change is how mini-batches are assembled each epoch. A critical practical detail is that the learning rate schedule must be compressed to decay inversely proportional to r, since fewer SGD steps are taken per round.

## Method

RS2 (Repeated Sampling of Random Subsets) resamples a subset of size r·|S| from the full dataset at every training epoch rather than selecting a static subset once. Two variants are studied: with replacement (uniform sampling from the full dataset each epoch, optionally stratified by class) and without replacement (a single random permutation is consumed in sequential chunks of r·N, reshuffling after a full pass). The key insight is that repeated random sampling maximizes data coverage and diversity across training while incurring negligible overhead (sub-second on CIFAR-10), whereas importance-based methods require expensive pretraining or per-epoch forward passes. Theoretically, the paper proves that RS2's convergence rate scales linearly with r relative to full-dataset SGD, and derives a generalization error bound showing that data-independent random sampling yields tighter bounds than data-dependent selection strategies. Empirically, RS2 without replacement reaches 91.7% on CIFAR-10 and 66% on ImageNet at r=10%, surpassing the best competing method by 5–11 accuracy points while training 1.6–9× faster end-to-end.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 and r=10%, RS2 without replacement achieves 91.7% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best static-subset baseline (AUM/CCS at 86.1%) by 5.6 points and reaching within 3.8 points of full-dataset training (95.5%). On ImageNet at r=10%, RS2 reaches 66% accuracy versus 55% for the next-best method and 69.5% for full-dataset training, while being 9× faster than full-dataset training and 1.6× faster than the next-best pruning method. RS2's subset selection overhead is negligible (<1 second on CIFAR-10) compared to 250+ minutes of pretraining required by most competing methods on ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS2 outperforms all 24 data pruning baselines on end-model accuracy across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-30, and ImageNet at practical selection ratios (r ∈ [10%, 50%]). On CIFAR-10 at r=10%, RS2 (91.7%) beats the strongest static-subset methods—AUM/CCS (86.1%), AL(Margin) (84.5%), and Forgetting (79.0%)—by 5.6, 7.2, and 12.7 points respectively. Even when baselines are modified to perform per-round resampling (e.g., Margin-RS at 90.4%, GraNd-RS at 90.1%), RS2 still leads, and when baselines recompute importance from current model weights each round (RC variants), most degrade severely (e.g., GraNd-RC drops to 24.1% at r=10%). On ImageNet at r=10%, RS2 (66%) surpasses the next-best method by 11 points. Against dataset distillation methods (Table 2), RS2 outperforms Trajectory Matching (the best DD method) in 8 of 9 dataset/ratio combinations while requiring 10–20× less generation time. RS2 is also the most robust to noisy labels, achieving 74.4% accuracy at 30% label noise versus 63.4% for the next-best method (SP-Easy-RS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions RS2 as a natural but overlooked extension of the static random-subset baseline that has served as the default comparison in data pruning literature. It builds on the generalization error framework of Nikolakakis et al. (2023), which shows that data-independent mini-batch schedules generalize optimally, and on convergence results from Ghadimi & Lan (2016) for nonconvex optimization. Conceptually, the paper departs from the entire importance-score-based data pruning and dataset distillation literature by arguing that the substantial computational overhead of sophisticated selection strategies is not justified when total time-to-accuracy is the objective, and it connects to prior observations that random exploration helps in adversarial training (Kaufmann et al., 2022) and that static random sampling is competitive for LLM pretraining (Marion et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes RS2 as a mandatory baseline that future data pruning and distillation work must outperform in both accuracy and end-to-end runtime, fundamentally shifting the evaluation standard for the field. Open questions include how to close the remaining gap between RS2 and full-dataset training (e.g., via importance sampling or improved subset training procedures), whether RS2's advantage persists at extreme compression ratios where it currently underperforms, and how to combine random resampling with quality-based selection for LLM pretraining. The practical impact is immediate: RS2 is trivially implementable (a few lines of code) and offers 4–9× speedups with minimal accuracy loss, making it highly relevant for hyperparameter search, architecture search, and cost-constrained training.
