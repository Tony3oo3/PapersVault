## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods, particularly trajectory-matching approaches, become ineffective as the synthetic dataset size (IPC) grows, often performing worse than random selection. The paper identifies that this failure stems from a mismatch between the difficulty of learned patterns and the synthetic set size: early-training trajectories produce easy patterns suited to small sets, while large sets require hard patterns from late-training trajectories. No prior method has achieved truly lossless distillation at any scale.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large real dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32, CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Tiny ImageNet at 64×64) along with pre-computed expert training trajectories (parameter sequences from training a surrogate network on the real data for a fixed number of epochs). The synthetic dataset is initialized by sampling real images that a pretrained model classifies correctly, with soft labels set to the model's softmax outputs. The output is a small synthetic dataset of images paired with jointly-optimized soft labels (not one-hot), where both the pixel values and the logit vectors are updated during distillation.

## Method

DATM controls the difficulty of generated patterns by restricting trajectory matching to a tunable window [T⁻, T⁺] of expert training steps: early steps yield easy patterns for low IPC, late steps yield hard patterns for high IPC. A sequential generation (SG) strategy gradually expands the upper bound T from a small value to T⁺ over the course of distillation, embedding easy patterns first to stabilize optimization before introducing harder ones. Soft labels are initialized from a pretrained model's logits (filtered to correctly-classified samples to avoid mislabeling) and are jointly optimized with synthetic images via backpropagation through the bi-level trajectory-matching loss. The inner loop trains a surrogate network on the synthetic set for N steps from a sampled expert parameter θ_t*, and the outer loop updates synthetic images and soft labels to minimize the normalized parameter distance between the surrogate's endpoint and the target expert parameter θ_{t+M}*. This difficulty alignment, combined with learnable soft labels and sequential generation, enables lossless distillation at 1/5 of CIFAR-10/100 and 1/10 of Tiny ImageNet size.

## Results

DATM achieves lossless dataset distillation for the first time: on CIFAR-10 at IPC=500 (83.5% vs. 84.8% full dataset) and IPC=1000 (85.5%), on CIFAR-100 at IPC=100 (57.5% vs. 56.2% full), and on Tiny ImageNet at IPC=50 (39.7% vs. 37.6% full). At IPC=50, DATM reaches 76.1% on CIFAR-10, 55.0% on CIFAR-100, and 39.7% on Tiny ImageNet, outperforming the best prior TM-based method FTD (73.2%, 48.5%, 28.2%) by 2.9, 6.5, and 11.5 points respectively. Critically, at high IPC (≥500 on CIFAR-10), all previous distillation methods degrade below random selection, while DATM continues to improve toward full-dataset accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DATM outperforms all 12 baselines in 11 of 12 evaluated settings, losing only on CIFAR-10 IPC=1 where kernel-based methods RCIG (53.9%) and KIP (49.9%) exceed it (46.9%). The most dramatic advantage appears at high IPC: on CIFAR-10 IPC=500, DATM reaches 83.5% while the best prior method (DM) achieves only 75.1% and random selection gets 73.2%; at IPC=1000, DATM hits 85.5% (exceeding the full dataset's 84.8%) while all prior methods fall at or below random (78.4%). Among trajectory-matching baselines specifically, DATM beats FTD by 2.9 points on CIFAR-10 IPC=50 (76.1 vs. 73.2), 6.5 points on CIFAR-100 IPC=50 (55.0 vs. 48.5), and 11.5 points on Tiny ImageNet IPC=50 (39.7 vs. 28.2). A key finding is that previous TM-based methods (MTT, TESLA, FTD) actually perform worse than random selection at high IPC (e.g., MTT at 71.6% vs. random 73.2% on CIFAR-10 IPC=500), whereas DATM's difficulty-alignment strategy prevents this degradation. On cross-architecture evaluation (Table 4), DATM's distilled datasets generalize to unseen networks (ResNet18, VGG11, AlexNet, LeNet, MLP) with consistent improvements over random selection, outperforming coreset selection methods (Glister, Forgetting) that are tuned per-architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on trajectory-matching (TM) based dataset distillation methods, particularly MTT (Cazenavette et al., 2022), FTD (Du et al., 2023), and TESLA (Cui et al., 2023), which optimize synthetic data by matching surrogate model training trajectories to expert trajectories. The key departure is identifying that the *stage* of the expert trajectory being matched (early vs. late) controls the difficulty of patterns embedded in the synthetic data, and that previous methods implicitly default to easy patterns, explaining their failure at high IPC. The paper further extends TESLA's use of soft labels by making them optimizable during distillation and introduces a sequential generation strategy to stabilize this optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of lossless dataset distillation (CIFAR-10/100 to 1/5, Tiny ImageNet to 1/10 with no performance drop on the distillation backbone), which was previously considered unattainable. The difficulty-alignment principle provides a general diagnostic for why scaling synthetic datasets backfires and offers a principled fix applicable to any TM-based method. Key limitations remain: losslessness holds only for the distillation backbone (cross-architecture gaps persist), TM-based methods are computationally expensive at high IPC, and the approach has not been validated on ImageNet-scale data. Open questions include whether the difficulty-alignment insight transfers to non-TM methods (gradient matching, distribution matching) and how to reduce the distillation cost for very large IPC.
