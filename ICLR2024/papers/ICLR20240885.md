## Problem

Existing ensemble-based continual learning methods train all experts on every task, making them all susceptible to catastrophic forgetting and incurring high computational cost. In exemplar-free class-incremental learning, particularly under equal-split task distributions where no strong initial feature extractor is available, methods struggle to balance stability (retaining old knowledge) with plasticity (learning new features), and prior approaches either freeze the backbone (sacrificing plasticity) or apply regularization to all experts (limiting diversification).

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-Subset and DomainNet) as input and produces a single class label as output. Internally, each expert maps an input to a 64-dimensional latent vector, and class predictions are made by computing log-likelihoods under per-class multivariate Gaussian distributions in each expert's latent space, then averaging softmax-normalized likelihoods across all K=5 experts. The method supports both task-agnostic inference (predicting among all classes seen so far) and task-aware inference (restricting to the current task's classes).

## Method

SEED maintains K experts (default K=5) that share initial convolutional layers f (frozen after the first task) but have distinct final layers g_k, each producing a unique latent space where every class is represented by a multivariate Gaussian distribution (mean and full covariance). When a new task arrives, SEED selects exactly one expert to fine-tune by computing the symmetrized KL divergence between all pairs of new-class distributions in each expert's latent space and choosing the expert where these distributions overlap the least (KL-max criterion), thereby minimizing representational drift. The selected expert is fine-tuned with a cross-entropy loss combined with L2 knowledge-distillation regularization against its own frozen copy, after which its class distributions are updated. At inference, each expert independently computes log-likelihoods for all classes, applies a temperature-scaled softmax (τ=3), and the final prediction is the class with the highest average softmax value across all experts, yielding a task-agnostic ensemble of Bayes classifiers.

## Results

On the equal-split CIFAR-100 benchmark (ResNet32), SEED achieves 61.7% average incremental accuracy at T=10, 56.2% at T=20, and 42.6% at T=50, outperforming the second-best method (FeTrIL) by 14.7, 17.5, and 15.6 percentage points respectively. On DomainNet with significant domain shift, SEED reaches 45.0% (T=12), 44.9% (T=24), and 39.2% (T=36), surpassing FeTrIL by 11–12 points. On ImageNet-Subset (T=10), SEED attains 67.8% versus FeTrIL's 58.7%. In the task-incremental setting with limited parameters, SEED achieves 91.2% (50-split) using only 3.2M parameters, outperforming CoSCL (87.9%, 4.6M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEED decisively outperforms all 14 baselines in the equal-split scenario (Table 1), with the largest gaps over FeTrIL (the strongest competitor) reaching 17.5 pp on CIFAR-100 T=20 and 11.7 pp on DomainNet T=36. Older regularization-based methods (EWC, LwF*) and prototype-based methods (PASS, IL2A) are far behind, confirming that SEED's selective expert training and Gaussian-based inference are critical. In the large-first-task setting (Table 2), SEED still leads on CIFAR-100 (by 4.6 pp at T=6) but narrows to a 1.4 pp margin at T=21, and actually loses to FeTrIL on ImageNet-Subset at T=11 (70.9 vs 71.2) and T=21 (63.0 vs 67.1), where a frozen backbone suffices. In the task-incremental limited-parameter setting (Table 3), SEED beats CoSCL by 7.4 pp (20-split) and 3.3 pp (50-split) while using 30% fewer parameters (3.2M vs 4.6M), and also surpasses HAT, MARK, and BNS. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that removing multivariate Gaussians, covariance, or temperature each degrades performance by 2.5–8.2 pp, validating each design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEED extends the mixture-of-experts paradigm for continual learning by departing from CoSCL's approach of training all experts simultaneously with a cooperation loss, instead selectively fine-tuning only one expert per task based on multivariate Gaussian distribution overlap (measured via symmetrized KL divergence). It builds on the Gaussian-based classification framework of SLDA (Hayes & Kanan, 2020) and incorporates knowledge distillation regularization from LwF (Li & Hoiem, 2016), but applies them within a fixed-size ensemble where shared initial layers are frozen after the first task. Unlike Expert Gate, which grows unboundedly, and Doan et al., which requires exemplars, SEED maintains a fixed parameter budget while being fully exemplar-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEED demonstrates that selective single-expert training naturally induces diversity without explicit cooperation losses, achieving 14–17 percentage point gains over the best prior exemplar-free CIL methods in equal-split settings. The approach also maintains high plasticity (unlike frozen-backbone methods such as FeTrIL) while controlling forgetting, addressing the stability-plasticity trade-off that limits many existing methods. Limitations include the need to fix the number of experts upfront, potential failure when tasks are semantically unrelated with limited parameters, and singular covariance issues in low-data regimes. Open questions include whether the Gaussian-based selection generalizes to non-i.i.d. task streams or to settings where the number of tasks far exceeds K.
