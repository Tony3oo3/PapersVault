## Problem

LLMs hallucinate on knowledge-intensive questions, and existing retrieval-augmented methods suffer from three gaps: they use a single fixed knowledge source for all questions (failing on domain-specific queries), they rely on LLMs that are poor at generating structured queries like SPARQL, and they edit rationales in parallel, allowing errors to propagate across reasoning steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240380/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a knowledge-intensive question, the system first samples multiple CoT rationales and answers; only questions whose answers fall below a self-consistency threshold (less than half of sampled answers agree) proceed to external retrieval. The framework then queries heterogeneous sources—Wikidata (SPARQL), Wikipedia (natural sentences), tables (SQL), medical flashcards, and domain-specific websites—across up to four knowledge domains (factual, medical, physics, biology). Output is a chain of progressively corrected rationales followed by a consolidated final answer.

## Method

CoK operates in three stages: (1) reasoning preparation generates preliminary CoT rationales via few-shot prompting with self-consistency and identifies relevant knowledge domains via in-context learning; (2) dynamic knowledge adapting performs sequential, progressive correction—each rationale is retrieved against and edited using external knowledge before the next rationale is generated, preventing error propagation; (3) answer consolidation derives the final answer from the corrected chain. The key architectural component is the Adaptive Query Generator (AQG), a plug-in that produces queries in the language matching each source: SPARQL for Wikidata (via LoRA-fine-tuned LLaMA-2-7B trained on LC-quad/KQA-pro with contrastive negative examples), SQL for tables (via ChatGPT with schema in prompt), and natural sentences for unstructured sources (via either ChatGPT or domain-specific LoRA-tuned LLaMA-2-7B). This design lets a single framework access both structured knowledge graphs and unstructured text without requiring the LLM itself to generate structured queries.

## Results

CoK consistently outperforms all baselines across six knowledge-intensive benchmarks spanning factual, medical, physics, and biology domains. On 3-shot settings, CoK achieves 63.4% accuracy on FEVER, 34.1% exact match on HotpotQA, 25.0 BLEU on FeTaQA, 70.5% on MedMCQA, 45.5% on MMLU Physics, and 83.0% on MMLU Biology. The average improvement over the CoT baseline is 4.3% on factual tasks and 4.9% on domain-specific tasks (medical, physics, biology). On 6-shot settings, CoK further improves to 73.3% on MedMCQA and 47.0% on MMLU Physics, maintaining its lead over all competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoK outperforms all five baselines on every dataset. Against Standard prompting (Ouyang et al., 2022), CoK gains 11.6 points on FEVER (63.4% vs. 51.8%) and 8.9 points on MedMCQA (70.5% vs. 61.6%) in the 3-shot setting. Against CoT (Wei et al., 2022), CoK improves by 5.6 points on FEVER and 4.2 points on HotpotQA, with the average gain reaching 4.9% on domain-specific tasks. Against CoT-SC (Wang et al., 2023), CoK leads by 3.5 points on FEVER and 3.3 points on HotpotQA. Against the closest retrieval-based competitor, Verify-and-Edit (Zhao et al., 2023), CoK gains 2.8 points on FEVER, 2.3 on HotpotQA, and 2.7 on MedMCQA. Against ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), CoK shows a larger improvement over the CoT-SC baseline on HotpotQA (+2.0% vs. +0.8%) while being comparable on FEVER (+3.5% vs. +4.2%), at roughly 40% of ReAct's API cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoK extends the retrieval-augmented chain-of-thought lineage initiated by Verify-and-Edit (Zhao et al., 2023c) and ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), both of which incorporate external knowledge to correct LLM rationales. The key departure is twofold: (1) CoK replaces parallel, independent rationale editing (VE) or error-accumulating context (ReAct) with a progressive, sequential correction mechanism where each corrected rationale conditions the next; and (2) it introduces the Adaptive Query Generator (AQG), a modular component that unifies structured queries (SPARQL, SQL) and unstructured queries (natural sentences) across heterogeneous knowledge sources, whereas prior methods predominantly relied on unstructured retrieval. The reasoning preparation stage builds directly on CoT prompting (Wei et al., 2022) and self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023) as a filtering gate before invoking external knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoK offers a practical, modular recipe for grounding LLMs in authoritative, domain-specific knowledge without requiring full model retraining, which is valuable as hallucination remains a central barrier to deploying LLMs in high-stakes domains (medical, scientific). The progressive correction mechanism and the AQG are independently reusable components that can be plugged into other RAG pipelines. However, the framework's effectiveness is bounded by the quality and coverage of its external sources, and the paper acknowledges that conflicting or unreliable sources could propagate misinformation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the four tested domains, how it scales to open-ended generation tasks where self-consistency filtering is inapplicable (as noted for FeTaQA), and whether the gains hold when the backbone LLM is replaced with a weaker or open-weight model.
