## Problem

Diffusion models struggle to scale to high-resolution image generation because direct pixel-space denoising over the full image is computationally prohibitive. Existing workarounds—super-resolution cascades or latent-space optimization—still require large models and high memory. The paper asks whether a compact, patch-level diffusion model can directly synthesize high-resolution images without the boundary artifacts that plague naive patch-based generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241833/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model always operates on 64×64 RGB patches regardless of target resolution, making it resolution-agnostic. Each patch is conditioned on a global semantic code (512-dim, extracted via CLIP ViT-B/16 for high-res datasets or a jointly trained encoder for 256×256 datasets) and sinusoidal positional embeddings indicating the patch's (i,j) location in the full image grid. At inference, a latent diffusion model first samples the global code, then the patch-based U-Net iteratively denoises 50 DDIM steps to produce the full image (e.g., 1024×1024 from a 16×16 patch grid, or 2048×1024 by adding internal patches).

## Method

Patch-DM's key mechanism is feature collage: after the U-Net encoder processes each 64×64 noisy patch into multi-scale feature maps, those maps are split into quadrants and recombined with corresponding quadrants from the four spatially neighboring patches to form a shifted feature representation, which the decoder then uses to predict noise for the shifted patch. This multi-level feature-space interaction (as opposed to pixel-space shifting) lets each patch attend to adjacent context at every resolution level, eliminating border artifacts without extra parameters. The denoising U-Net (based on Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) is modulated by concatenated time, position, and global semantic embeddings via classifier-free guidance (dropout 0.1 for global, 0.5 for position). Because the architecture is fixed to 64×64 patches, the same 70M-parameter model handles 256×256, 1024×512, and 1024×1024 targets, and can be extended to 2048×1024 by simply adding more patches at inference with interpolated position embeddings.

## Results

On the 1K-resolution benchmarks, Patch-DM achieves FID 20.37 / sFID 34.41 on Nature-21K (1024×512), FID 23.78 / sFID 37.22 on LHQ (1024×1024), and FID 19.70 / sFID 36.51 on FFHQ (1024×1024), using only a 64×64 patch size and a 148M-parameter model. On the 256×256 benchmarks, Patch-DM achieves FID 10.02 on FFHQ, 6.04 on LSUN-Bedroom, and 5.49 on LSUN-Church, remaining competitive with full-image diffusion models (e.g., LDM: 8.76 on FFHQ) while using substantially fewer parameters (70M vs. 274M for LDM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Patch-DM decisively outperforms all three prior patch-based methods (COCO-GAN, InfinityGAN, Anyres-GAN) on every 1K-resolution dataset, with FID improvements of 24–155 points depending on dataset and baseline. On the 256×256 benchmarks, Patch-DM (FID 10.02 on FFHQ, 6.04 on LSUN-Bedroom, 5.49 on LSUN-Church) remains competitive with full-image diffusion models like LDM (8.76, 3.40, 4.23) and UDM (5.54 on FFHQ), while using roughly 3–4× fewer parameters (70M vs. 274M for LDM, 552M for ADM). Against non-patch GAN baselines (StyleGAN, PGGAN), Patch-DM is slightly behind on FID for LSUN-Bedroom (6.04 vs. 2.35) and LSUN-Church (5.49 vs. 4.21) but ahead on sFID for LSUN-Church (14.80 vs. 10.48 for PGGAN). The paper also qualitatively compares against StyleGAN3-T and StyleGAN-XL on FFHQ1024, acknowledging that patch-based generation can struggle with global structural consistency (e.g., asymmetric facial features) compared to full-image GANs. Overall, Patch-DM establishes state-of-the-art among patch-based generators while approaching the quality of much larger full-image diffusion models.

## Relation to Prior Work

Patch-DM builds directly on the denoising diffusion framework of Ho et al. (2020) and the U-Net architecture of Dhariwal & Nichol (2021), adapting them to operate on small 64×64 patches rather than full images. Its key departure from prior patch-based synthesis methods (COCO-GAN, InfinityGAN, Anyres-GAN) is performing collage in the feature space of the U-Net encoder/decoder rather than in pixel space, enabling multi-level feature interaction between neighboring patches. The unconditional synthesis pipeline borrows the latent diffusion model from Preechakul et al. (2022) and uses DDIM (Song et al., 2021) for sampling, while CLIP ViT-B/16 provides global semantic conditioning for high-resolution training. The shifted-window mechanism is conceptually distinct from Swin Transformers' shifted windows, which serve a different purpose in hierarchical attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Patch-DM demonstrates that a compact 70M-parameter patch-level model can match or exceed full-resolution diffusion models (552M+ parameters) on 1024×1024 synthesis, offering a practical path to high-resolution generation with significantly reduced memory and compute. The resolution-agnostic design enables free applications like outpainting, inpainting, and super-resolution-by-adding-patches without retraining. However, the paper acknowledges a clear limitation: on highly structured datasets like FFHQ1024, global consistency (e.g., facial symmetry) degrades because 256 independent 64×64 patches lack strong global coordination. Open questions include whether larger patch sizes, learned global consistency mechanisms, or attention across patches could close this gap without sacrificing the efficiency advantage.
