## Problem

Diffusion-based video generation has predominantly relied on U-Net backbones, with limited exploration of transformer architectures that are inherently better suited for modeling long-range temporal dependencies and scaling to large model sizes. Moreover, existing methods typically address individual video generation tasks (unconditional generation, prediction, interpolation, completion) with specialized modules, lacking a unified framework that handles all tasks within a single model without architectural modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240773/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Video clips are projected into a latent space via a frozen pre-trained VAE tokenizer (8× spatial downsampling), then divided into N×N non-overlapping patches with spatial and temporal positional embeddings. The unified input is formulated as I = F ∧ (1−M) + C ∧ M, where a spatial-temporal mask M determines per-token whether the input is noise or real video content, enabling the same model to accept pure noise (unconditional generation), a prefix of real frames plus noise (prediction), scattered real frames (interpolation), or a single image at an arbitrary temporal position (image-to-video). Outputs are generated video frames decoded from latent space at resolutions of 64×64, 128×128, or 256×256 with 16 frames per clip.

## Method

VDT employs a pure transformer architecture where each block contains alternating multi-head temporal attention, multi-head spatial attention, and a feed-forward network, with diffusion timestep conditioning injected via adaptive layer normalization (adaLN). For conditional video generation, the paper identifies token concatenation—simply appending conditional frame tokens to noisy frame tokens—as the most effective and fastest-converging strategy over adaptive layer normalization or cross-attention. The core unifying mechanism is a spatial-temporal mask M that, during training, is randomly modulated to sample from multiple task distributions (unconditional generation with M=0, bidirectional prediction, arbitrary frame interpolation, image-to-video, and spatial-temporal block completion inspired by BEiT), allowing a single trained model to handle all tasks at inference by simply setting M appropriately. Training follows a two-stage protocol: spatial-only pretraining (removing temporal attention, sampling single frames) followed by joint spatial-temporal training, which yields better FVD with less total training time than direct joint training.

## Results

On UCF101 unconditional generation (16×64×64), VDT achieves an FVD of 225.7, outperforming all GAN-based methods (best: DIGAN at 577.0) and all U-Net-based diffusion methods at the same resolution (best: VDM at 295.0). On Cityscapes video prediction (128×128, 2→28 frames), VDT achieves FVD 142.3 and SSIM 0.880, matching MCVD-concat on FVD (141.4) while substantially surpassing it on SSIM (0.690). On the Physion physical prediction benchmark, VDT reaches 65.3% VQA accuracy, exceeding all scene-centric baselines (best: pDEIT-lstm at 63.1%) and approaching the object-centric SlotFormer (69.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDT decisively outperforms all GAN-based baselines on UCF101, with FVD 225.7 versus DIGAN's 577.0 and TGANv2's 1209.0. Among U-Net-based diffusion methods at the same 64×64 resolution, VDT improves over VDM (295.0) by 24% and PyoCo (310.0) by 27%, and dramatically surpasses MCVD (1143.0), which was designed for prediction rather than unconditional generation. On Cityscapes video prediction, VDT matches MCVD-concat on FVD (142.3 vs. 141.4) but achieves substantially higher SSIM (0.880 vs. 0.690), indicating better structural fidelity; it also outperforms all RNN-based (vRNN1L, Hier-vRNN) and VAE-based (GHVAE) predictors by large margins. On Physion physical prediction, VDT (65.3%) beats all scene-centric methods—PRIN (57.9%), pVGG-lstm (58.7%), and pDEIT-lstm (63.1%)—but remains below the object-centric SlotFormer (69.3%) and the human upper bound (80.0%). The paper notes that several top U-Net baselines (Make-A-Video, VideoFusion, VDM) were trained on train+test splits, making direct comparison slightly favorable to those methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

VDT extends the transformer-based diffusion paradigm pioneered by DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2022) from image generation to video by inserting temporal attention modules into spatial transformer blocks, while operating in the latent space of LDM's pre-trained VAE (Rombach et al., 2022). It departs from the dominant U-Net-based video diffusion line (VDM, MCVD, PVDM) by adopting a pure transformer architecture, and draws on BEiT's block masking strategy for its spatial-temporal completion task. The key conceptual departure is the unified spatial-temporal mask modeling mechanism, which reframes diverse video tasks (generation, prediction, interpolation, completion) as a single masked-inpainting problem over a shared token sequence, eliminating the need for task-specific modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VDT establishes that a pure transformer backbone can match or exceed U-Net-based video diffusion models on standard benchmarks while offering a single unified architecture for multiple video generation tasks, reducing the engineering burden of maintaining separate pipelines. The finding that simple token concatenation outperforms cross-attention and adaptive layer normalization for conditioning, and that the model zero-shot generalizes to longer conditional sequences than seen in training, provides practical guidance for future video diffusion designs. However, the model is constrained by limited training scale (no large-scale pretraining, no text conditioning), and the FVD gains over U-Net baselines are moderate, leaving open whether the transformer advantage persists at the scale of modern text-to-video systems.
