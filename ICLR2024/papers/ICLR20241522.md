## Problem

Existing training-free ANN-to-SNN conversion methods are limited to convolutional networks and cannot handle Transformers, which introduce two fundamental incompatibilities: non-causal variable-variable multiplications in self-attention that conflict with SNN's additive temporal accumulation, and complex test-time nonlinearities (GELU, LayerNorm, Softmax) that exceed the piece-wise linear representational capacity of spiking neurons. No prior work has directly converted a pretrained mainstream Transformer into a purely event-driven SNN without additional training.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a pretrained ViT-B/32 image encoder from CLIP (processing 32×32 or 224×224 RGB images with natural-language text prompts for zero-shot classification) and produces a purely spiking SNN that requires no floating-point operations at inference. The converted SNN operates over T discrete time steps (32–256), where each neuron emits binary spikes and all computations reduce to Boolean ANDs and weighted additions. The Universal Group Operators are pre-trained on synthetic floating-point data independent of any real training set, making the converted model universally applicable to unseen inputs.

## Method

STA approximates Transformer operations along two axes. Spatially, the Universal Group Operator (UGO) replaces each nonlinear function (GELU, exp, inverse, 1/√) with a small fully-connected group of N=8–32 IF neurons, justified by the Universal Approximation Theorem and pre-trained on synthetic data covering the empirically bounded input range; high-dimensional operations like LayerNorm and Softmax are decomposed into weighted additions, UGO calls, and spike multiplications. Temporally, the Temporal-Corrective Self-Attention Layer (TCSA) resolves non-causal variable multiplications via an estimation-correction mechanism: at each step t, the full product is estimated from the t-point partial spike sequences (yielding an unbiased, time-invariant estimate), then corrected by the increment from the newly arrived spikes, distributing each spike's contribution uniformly across all T steps to maintain stable firing rates. The complete conversion is training-free, combining UGOs, TCSA, threshold balancing (MMSE), signed neurons with memory, and burst spikes to convert the pretrained ViT-B/32 while preserving its zero-shot and transfer-learning capabilities.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 standard classification, the converted ViT-B/32 (STA) achieves 84.15% top-1 accuracy at T=32 and 85.98% at T=256, starting from an ANN accuracy of 87.35% (a 3.2-point conversion loss). This substantially outperforms the best ResNet-50 baseline (Calib., 75.61% at T=32) by 8.5 points at the same time step. For zero-shot classification on CIFAR-10.1, the converted ViT retains 83.05% at T=32 versus 84.15% ANN accuracy, demonstrating minimal degradation while inheriting CLIP's generalization to distribution shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STA on ViT-B/32 dominates all baselines on CIFAR-100 at every time step: at T=32 it reaches 84.15%, exceeding the best ResNet-50 baseline (Calib., 75.61%) by 8.5 points and the best ResNet-20 baseline (SNM, 74.58%) by 9.6 points. Notably, ViT+STA at T=32 (84.15%) already surpasses ResNet-50+SNM at T=256 (80.75%), meaning the Transformer conversion achieves higher accuracy with 8× fewer simulation steps. Among the ResNet-50 baselines, Calib. (Li et al., 2021) is the strongest at low T (75.61% at T=32), while SNM (Wang et al., 2022a) catches up at T=256 (80.75%); both remain well below ViT+STA. For zero-shot classification, the conversion loss for ViT+STA is consistently under 2 points (e.g., CIFAR-10.1: 84.15→83.05), whereas ResNet-50 baselines suffer 8–29 point drops depending on the method and time step, highlighting that the Transformer architecture is inherently more amenable to training-free SNN conversion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established ANN-to-SNN conversion paradigm (Diehl et al., 2015; Rueckauer et al., 2017; Li et al., 2021) from CNNs to Transformers, a setting where prior methods fail due to non-causal self-attention multiplications and test-time normalization (LayerNorm, Softmax). It composes existing conversion techniques—MMSE threshold calibration, Signed Neurons with Memory, and burst spikes—into a unified pipeline, but introduces two genuinely new spiking modules: the Universal Group Operator (spatial approximation of nonlinearities via small pre-trained neuron populations) and the Temporal-Corrective Self-Attention layer (temporal approximation of variable-variable multiplications via estimation-correction). The theoretical grounding draws on the Universal Approximation Theorem (Hornik et al., 1989) and rate-coding convergence analysis, positioning the work at the intersection of approximation theory and neuromorphic computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free conversion of a pretrained mainstream Transformer (ViT-B/32 from CLIP) into a purely event-driven SNN, opening a path to deploy large pretrained models on neuromorphic hardware without retraining or fine-tuning. The counterintuitive finding that the 87.8M-parameter ViT suffers less conversion loss than the 25.6M-parameter ResNet-50 suggests Transformer architectures may be inherently more amenable to SNN conversion, which could reshape which models are targeted for neuromorphic deployment. Key limitations include modest energy savings compared to spiking CNNs, the added computational overhead of UGOs, and evaluation on a single Transformer architecture; open questions include scaling to larger ViT variants, LLMs, and whether the estimation-correction mechanism generalizes to other non-causal operations beyond self-attention.
