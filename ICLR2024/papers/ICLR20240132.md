## Problem

Existing face recognition algorithms primarily optimize margins and loss functions, while the feature representation space itself has been largely overlooked. This paper asks whether a model's features contain useful information ('innovation') orthogonal to a weaker model's features, and whether such innovation can be learned and incorporated to improve a single model's recognition accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240132/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB face images as input and produces a 512-dimensional face embedding vector via a CNN backbone (ResNet-50, ResNet-100, or IR-SE-50). Unusually, the training pipeline requires a pre-trained teacher network of identical architecture; the student is initialized with the teacher's weights and trained with an additional intra-class incoherence loss applied at one or more intermediate block outputs and/or the final embedding layer, rather than at the logit level as in standard knowledge distillation.

## Method

The paper first demonstrates via Gram-Schmidt orthogonal decomposition that a superior model's feature (e.g., ArcFace) decomposes into a 'pro-feature' parallel to an inferior model (e.g., CosFace) and an 'innovation' orthogonal to it, and that the innovation alone retains substantial face distinguishability. Motivated by this, the authors propose a knowledge-distillation-style training paradigm where a student network (same architecture as the teacher) is trained with L_S = L_FR + γ·L_dissim, where L_dissim is the cosine similarity between teacher and student embeddings of the same face image, thereby forcing the student to learn a feature space that is incoherent (dissimilar) to the teacher's while preserving recognition capability. The incoherence constraint can be applied at individual intermediate blocks, the final output layer, or a combination thereof, with the optimal configuration found to be the final layer alone. This 'feature augmentation' effect is most pronounced on smaller training sets, analogous to how data augmentation helps under limited data.

## Results

On CASIA with ResNet50, IIC consistently improves all four baseline FR methods: ArcFace+IIC reaches 94.24% average accuracy (vs. 93.85% for ArcFace), and AdaFace+IIC reaches 94.58% (vs. 94.42% for AdaFace). On the larger MS1MV2 dataset with ResNet100, gains are smaller but still positive for most methods (e.g., ArcFace+IIC: 96.76% vs. 96.72%). On IJB-C at FPR=1e-6, IIC yields the largest relative gains, with ArcFace+IIC(R50) improving TPR from 85.64% to 89.50% and MagFace+IIC(R50) from 81.70% to 85.88%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IIC improves upon all four baselines on the CASIA/ResNet50 setting. ArcFace+IIC gains +0.39% average accuracy over ArcFace (94.24% vs. 93.85%), with notable improvements on CPLFW (+1.25%) and VGGFace2 (+0.70%). AdaFace+IIC, the strongest overall configuration, achieves 94.58% average, surpassing AdaFace by +0.16%, with the largest per-benchmark gain on CFP-FP (+0.53%). CosFace+IIC improves by +0.13% on average, and MagFace+IIC by +0.08%. On the larger MS1MV2/ResNet100 setting, gains shrink (ArcFace+IIC: +0.04%, AdaFace+IIC: +0.02%), and MagFace+IIC actually drops slightly (−0.05%), suggesting IIC acts as a form of 'feature augmentation' most beneficial when training data is limited. On IJB-C at FPR=1e-6, IIC delivers the most dramatic gains: ArcFace+IIC(R50) jumps +3.86 TPR points and MagFace+IIC(R50) jumps +4.18 points, indicating the method is particularly effective under stringent false-positive constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant FR training paradigm of increasing intra-class correlation and inter-class separation (SphereFace, CosFace, ArcFace, MagFace, AdaFace) by introducing intra-class incoherence as a complementary objective. It repurposes the knowledge distillation framework (Hinton et al., 2015) in an inverted form: rather than the student mimicking the teacher's feature space, the student is explicitly pushed to learn a dissimilar representation, which the authors term 'innovation.' The orthogonal decomposition analysis connecting two FR models' feature spaces is a new empirical lens, but the training mechanism itself is a straightforward cosine-dissimilarity loss layered on top of any existing FR loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counterintuitive finding that adding intra-class incoherence (rather than the standard intra-class compactness) can improve recognition accuracy challenges a core assumption in margin-based FR training. The method is lightweight and model-agnostic, consistently improving ArcFace, CosFace, MagFace, and AdaFace by 0.2–0.5% on average, suggesting a simple plug-in for practitioners. However, the gains are modest, the 'feature augmentation' hypothesis is only partially validated (stronger on smaller datasets), and the authors acknowledge the approach is currently limited to face recognition with unclear generalizability to other recognition or representation-learning tasks.
