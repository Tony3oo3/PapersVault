## Problem

Strictly equivariant GNNs for molecular property prediction rely on Clebsch-Gordan tensor products with finite low-degree representations, which impose excessive constraints on the function form and limit the network's expressive power for capturing complex atomic interactions. While relaxing equivariance with neural networks can recover expressiveness, naively doing so breaks rotational symmetry and degrades performance. The paper addresses how to achieve the expressive power of high-degree representations while maintaining approximate equivariance.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D atomic coordinates, atomic type embeddings (H, C, N, O, F for QM9), and molecular graph topology (defined by a cutoff radius for 3D structures or SMILES-induced 2D graphs). Node embeddings are steerable vectors of size (L+1)² × C channels where L (typically 4–6) is the maximum spherical harmonic degree. Outputs include scalar molecular energies, per-atom force vectors (equivariant), and various invariant chemical properties (HOMO/LUMO gaps, dipole moments, polarizabilities, etc.), with an optional lightweight invariant branch contributing to final predictions.

## Method

HDGNN introduces a hybrid message block (Multi-TP) that combines a strictly equivariant Mean-Extension Tensor Product (METP) pathway with learnable non-equivariant MLP pathways. The key mechanism rotates node embeddings into a local coordinate frame via Wigner-D matrices, then applies shared-weight MLPs to both the rotated representation x′ and the residual (D(R)−I)x, so the network learns the SO(3) transformation pattern while the residual term compensates for the randomness in the chosen rotation. An attention module (computed from an invariant branch) dynamically weights the four message pathways, balancing equivariance and expressiveness. The update block performs channel interactions by expanding messages to the spatial domain via spherical harmonics (analogous to IFFT), concatenating values at both r and −r to capture both convolution and correlation paths, then transforming back via FFT on S².

## Results

On the IS2RE task of OC20, HDGNN achieves the best average energy MAE of 548 meV, outperforming the previous SOTA SCN (573 meV) and Equiformer (586 meV). The advantage is most pronounced on out-of-distribution splits: HDGNN scores 550 meV on OOD Both and 618 meV on OOD Ads, versus 604/643 and 630/688 for SCN/Equiformer respectively. On QM9, HDGNN achieves the best MAE on 2 of 12 properties and second-best on 3, while trailing SOTA methods (Equiformer, TorchMD-NET) on energy-related targets (G, H, U, U₀).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDGNN outperforms all 15 baselines on the IS2RE OC20 average energy MAE (548 vs. 573 meV for SCN, the closest competitor), with the largest margins on OOD splits (550 vs. 604 meV on OOD Both). Against strictly equivariant models (Equiformer, SEGNN, TFN), HDGNN gains 25–62 meV on average MAE, suggesting that relaxing equivariance via learned modules improves generalization to unseen adsorbates and catalysts. Among directional GNNs (DimeNet++, GemNet-OC, SphereNet), HDGNN leads by 11–83 meV on average. On QM9, HDGNN is competitive but not uniformly best: it ties Equiformer on α (0.046 bohr³) and beats it on Δ (32 vs. 30 meV is actually Equiformer winning), while trailing Equiformer and TorchMD-NET on energy targets G, H, U, U₀ by 3–18 meV. The paper attributes the QM9 energy gap to the absence of attention mechanisms and neighbor-neighbor interactions used by those baselines, and notes that HDGNN's hybrid equivariance design is particularly beneficial on the smaller QM9 dataset where purely learned-equivariance approaches (SCN) struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

HDGNN extends the equivariant message-passing lineage initiated by TFN (Thomas et al., 2018) and carried forward by SEGNN and Equiformer, which all rely on the Clebsch-Gordan tensor product as their core equivariant operation. It departs from this line by replacing parts of the CG product with learned MLP pathways (Multi-TP), drawing on the local-coordinate-frame rotation trick from Kofinas et al. (2021) and Zitnick et al. (2022) to make the learned components approximately equivariant. Unlike SCN, which relaxes equivariance almost entirely and relies on data to recover it, HDGNN retains a strictly equivariant METP pathway alongside the learned terms, creating an explicit hybrid that the authors argue is more robust on smaller datasets like QM9.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental tension in equivariant molecular ML: finite-degree CG products limit expressiveness, but naively relaxing equivariance destroys generalization. The OOD generalization advantage on OC20 (564 vs. 592/613 meV for SCN/Equiformer) suggests that carefully constrained learned equivariance captures physically meaningful features that purely equivariant or purely learned models miss. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher degrees L' without prohibitive cost, whether the learned equivariance transfers to truly novel chemical spaces beyond the training distribution, and whether the hybrid design generalizes to other symmetry groups beyond SO(3).
