## Problem

While fine-tuning LLMs on generalized tasks such as arithmetic and code generation consistently improves downstream performance, the mechanistic explanation for this gain remains unclear: it is unknown whether fine-tuning creates entirely new circuits or enhances the existing circuitry of the base model. This paper investigates the question through entity tracking, a fundamental language comprehension capability that shows substantial accuracy gains after arithmetic fine-tuning, asking whether the same circuit, the same functionality, and the same sub-mechanism persist across base and fine-tuned models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained and fine-tuned Llama-7B model weights together with synthetic entity tracking tasks (sentences placing 7 single-token objects into 7 letter-labeled boxes, followed by a query like 'Box F contains the ___') and produces a sparse circuit defined as a set of (attention_head, token_position) pairs organized into four groups at the last-token, query-label, and previous-query-label positions. Each group is annotated with a functionality label (Value Fetcher, Position Transmitter, Position Detector, Structure Reader) derived from DCM binary masks, and the final output is a performance attribution identifying which specific sub-mechanism (positional information quality vs. value retrieval) accounts for the fine-tuning gain. The circuit is evaluated via faithfulness (circuit accuracy / full-model accuracy) and completeness (matching knockout behavior) criteria.

## Method

The paper combines three interpretability techniques in sequence. First, Path Patching discovers the entity tracking circuit in Llama-7B, yielding 72 attention heads in four groups whose information flow follows a V-composition chain from Structure Reader → Position Detector → Position Transmitter → Value Fetcher. Second, Desiderata-based Component Masking (DCM) trains sparse binary masks over each group to reveal that the circuit tracks the positional index of the correct object rather than its value or label, with Group A heads fetching the object value at that position. Third, the authors introduce Cross-Model Activation Patching (CMAP), which patches the output of specific head subsets from fine-tuned models (Goat-7B, FLoat-7B) into the base Llama-7B on the same input, showing that patching only the Value Fetcher heads recovers the full fine-tuned performance and that the Position Transmitter heads carry augmented positional information. The key finding is that fine-tuning preserves the circuit topology and per-group functionality while adding roughly 100 extra heads and improving the quality of positional encoding, rather than implementing a fundamentally different mechanism.

## Results

The entity-tracking circuit identified in Llama-7B (72 attention heads in 4 groups) achieves a faithfulness score of 1.00 on the base model and 0.88–0.97 on fine-tuned models (Vicuna-7B, Goat-7B, FLoat-7B), confirming the same circuit persists after fine-tuning. Fine-tuning on arithmetic tasks (Goat-7B, FLoat-7B) improves entity-tracking accuracy from 0.66 (Llama-7B) to 0.82, while the circuit alone recovers 0.72–0.73 of that performance. Random circuits with the same head count achieve near-zero accuracy (0.00–0.01 vs. chance 0.14), confirming the identified circuit is non-trivial. CMAP shows that patching only the Value Fetcher heads from fine-tuned models into Llama-7B recovers the full fine-tuned performance gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the key contrasts are internal. The identified 72-head circuit is compared against random circuits of the same size, which achieve near-zero accuracy (0.00–0.01), confirming the circuit captures genuine task-relevant computation. The Llama-7B circuit is evaluated unmodified on three fine-tuned models: Vicuna-7B (instruction-tuned) retains nearly identical performance (faithfulness 0.97), while arithmetic-tuned Goat-7B and FLoat-7B show slightly lower faithfulness (0.89, 0.88), indicating additional components are needed to reach their full 0.82 accuracy. CMAP cross-model patching reveals that the Value Fetcher heads alone, when patched from fine-tuned models into Llama-7B, recover the entire performance gap (0.66 → 0.82), while Position Transmitter heads provide a secondary but significant boost. The paper also compares LoRA-based fine-tuning (Goat-7B) versus full fine-tuning (FLoat-7B), finding both produce circuits of similar size (175 heads) and identical functionality, suggesting the mechanism enhancement is robust to the fine-tuning method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability and fine-tuning analysis, building directly on Path Patching (Wang et al., 2022; Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2023) for circuit discovery and Desiderata-based Component Masking (Davies et al., 2023) for functional attribution. It departs from prior circuit-discovery work, which typically identifies circuits within a single model, by explicitly asking whether the same circuit persists across a base model and its fine-tuned variants, and introduces Cross-Model Activation Patching (CMAP) to compare mechanisms across models. The entity-tracking task follows Kim & Schuster (2023), who demonstrated that arithmetic-tuned models outperform base models on this task, and the finding that fine-tuning enhances rather than replaces mechanisms resonates with Jain et al. (2023), though this paper studies established LLMs rather than synthetic tracr models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fine-tuning preserves the base model's circuit and its functional decomposition (Position Detector → Position Transmitter → Value Fetcher) while enhancing positional information encoding provides a concrete mechanistic account of why generic fine-tuning transfers to specific tasks. The CMAP method is a general-purpose tool for comparing sub-mechanisms across models and could be applied to other fine-tuning scenarios. However, the study is limited to a single task (entity tracking with 7 boxes, no operations), a single base architecture (Llama-7B), and three fine-tuned variants; whether mechanism invariance holds for more complex tasks, different architectures, or more aggressive fine-tuning remains open. The paper also leaves the precise nature of the 'positional information' only partially characterized, and does not study training dynamics to identify when circuit enhancement occurs.
