## Problem

Sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) effectively prevents pre-training-free molecular graph transformers from converging to sharp local minima, but it doubles training cost by requiring two forward/backward passes per step. Existing efficient SAM variants (AE-SAM, RST, LookSAM) designed for computer vision fail on molecular graphs because they ignore the specific gradient direction and magnitude relationships in this domain, yielding performance no better than or worse than vanilla Adam.

## Input / Output

GraphSAM is an optimizer applied on top of existing graph transformer backbones (GROVER, CoMPT) rather than a new architecture. It consumes the same molecular graph inputs (atom node features, bond edge features) and produces the same outputs (binary/multi-class labels for BBBP, Tox21, Sider, ClinTox; continuous values for ESOL, Lipophilicity), but modifies only the gradient computation schedule during training. The key operational detail is that at most training steps, GraphSAM performs a single forward/backward pass (matching Adam's cost), with an additional pass only at the first step of each epoch to re-anchor the perturbation gradient.

## Method

GraphSAM replaces SAM's per-step perturbation gradient computation with a moving-average approximation: ε_{t+1} = β·ε_t + (1−β)·ω_t/‖ω_t‖₂, where ω_t is the updating gradient already computed for the parameter update, so the approximation costs nothing extra. This is justified by two empirical observations: the perturbation gradient changes slowly during training (‖ε_{t+1}−ε_t‖₂ is small), and its direction is positively correlated with the previous step's updating gradient (67.45% consistent cosine pairs). To prevent drift, the perturbation gradient is periodically re-anchored via an exact forward/backward pass at the start of each epoch, and a gradient ball size scheduler (ρ_new = ρ_initial · γ^{epoch/λ}) decays ρ over training to keep the approximation error bounded. Theoretically, the paper proves that GraphSAM's adversarial loss upper-bounds SAM's (Conjecture 1) and that the generalization gap is proportional to the gradient approximation error ‖ε̂_G − ε̂_S‖ (Conjecture 2), which the ρ scheduler keeps small.

## Results

On the six MoleculeNet benchmark datasets, GraphSAM achieves average improvements of 1.52% (GROVER) and 2.16% (CoMPT) over the vanilla transformer baselines, matching or exceeding SAM's 1.55% and 1.97% improvements respectively. For example, on CoMPT, GraphSAM reaches 0.961 ROC-AUC on BBBP, 0.937 on ClinTox, and 0.511 RMSE on ESOL, while SAM achieves 0.962, 0.927, and 0.517. In terms of efficiency, GraphSAM achieves 135.3% (GROVER) and 155.4% (CoMPT) throughput relative to SAM, meaning it processes 35–55% more graphs per second while maintaining comparable generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphSAM matches or slightly exceeds SAM on most datasets: on CoMPT it wins on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., ClinTox 0.937 vs 0.927, ESOL 0.511 vs 0.517 RMSE) while losing marginally on BBBP (0.961 vs 0.962), and on GROVER it wins on 4 of 6 (e.g., Tox21 0.846 vs 0.840, Sider 0.665 vs 0.660) but trails on ClinTox (0.866 vs 0.872) and ESOL (0.625 vs 0.619 RMSE). All efficient SAM variants (SAM-One, SAM-k, LookSAM, AE-SAM, RST) underperform GraphSAM on both accuracy and consistency: for instance, on GROVER/BBBP, SAM-One drops to 0.899, SAM-k to 0.915, LookSAM to 0.919, AE-SAM to 0.923, and RST to 0.912, versus GraphSAM's 0.928. Against GNN baselines, GraphSAM on CoMPT outperforms CMPNN (the strongest GNN) on BBBP (0.961 vs 0.925), ClinTox (0.937 vs 0.918), ESOL (0.511 vs 0.582), and Lipophilicity (0.608 vs 0.633), confirming that the sharp-minima problem, not model capacity, limits pre-training-free transformers. In throughput, GraphSAM processes 135–155% as many graphs per second as SAM, while SAM-One and SAM-k are faster (142–178%) but sacrifice 2–4% AUC. The paper also shows that on pre-trained GROVER, GraphSAM still provides modest gains (e.g., BBBP 0.939 vs 0.930 baseline), though the improvement is smaller than in the pre-training-free setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM optimization framework (Foret et al., 2020) to the molecular graph transformer domain, where no prior SAM-type method had been applied. It departs from existing efficient SAM variants (LookSAM, AE-SAM, RST, SAM-k) that were designed based on gradient patterns observed in computer vision and NLP, arguing that these 'generic' techniques overlook the specific gradient direction and magnitude relationships in molecular graph transformers. The key conceptual departure is the use of a moving-average approximation of the perturbation gradient informed by the observation that ||ω_t|| >> ||ε_t|| and that ε_{t+1} is directionally consistent with ω_t in this domain, combined with a periodic ρ scheduler to control approximation error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GraphSAM provides a practical path for pre-training-free graph transformers to achieve SAM-level generalization without the 2× computational overhead, which is significant given that large-scale pre-training (e.g., GROVER's original protocol) demands substantial resources and expert task design. However, the method's domain specificity is a limitation: the gradient approximation strategy and ρ scheduler are tuned to molecular graph transformers, and the paper's own CV experiments (Table 11) show GraphSAM underperforms LookSAM on image benchmarks, confirming the approach does not transfer. Open questions include whether the moving-average gradient approximation generalizes to other graph domains (e.g., social networks, knowledge graphs) and whether the per-epoch re-anchoring can be further relaxed without significant performance loss.
