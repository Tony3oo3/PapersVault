## Problem

Classifier-guided diffusion language models suffer from a failure mode the authors term update-forgetting: uniform, context-agnostic token updates at each denoising step overwrite previously guided semantic edits, erasing control signals and degrading both fluency and controllability. Prior token-ordering approaches in masked diffusion rely on hard, confidence-based unmasking that is agnostic to semantic importance and limited to discrete formulations, leaving no principled mechanism to preserve classifier-driven modifications across timesteps.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on a pretrained simplex diffusion language model (TESS/SSD-LM, 330M parameters) and takes a text prompt plus an external classifier (e.g., sentiment, toxicity, or paraphrase classifier) that provides per-token gradient signals. The classifier gradients drive an adaptive per-token timestep schedule, and the output is a full controlled text sequence (up to 512 tokens for long-form) generated through iterative denoising with as few as 50 diffusion steps. Because the allocation is purely inference-time, it requires no retraining of the base diffusion model and applies uniformly across simplex, continuous, and discrete diffusion formulations.

## Method

TTA-DIFFUSION replaces the uniform timestep applied to all tokens with a per-token local timestep t_i = f(i, t), creating a soft semantic ordering where tokens with high classifier gradient magnitude (i.e., already strongly shifted toward the target attribute) receive smaller timesteps and thus less perturbation, while uncertain tokens receive larger timesteps for continued refinement. The adaptive schedule is t_i^adaptive = α_smooth · t + (1 − α_smooth) · (1 − ĝ_i) · t, where ĝ_i is the normalized gradient norm and α_smooth controls the interpolation between uniform and fully gradient-driven allocation. A theoretical analysis (Appendix A.5) shows this allocation is the affine KKT solution to a per-time convex program that simultaneously minimizes the cross-entropy upper bound on perplexity and the one-step classifier margin-drop bound. The method is combined with progressive step reduction—fine-tuning the model from 5000 down to 50 diffusion steps via cross-entropy loss—to achieve fast inference without sacrificing control fidelity.

## Results

On sentiment control, TTA-DIFFUSION (T=200) achieves 94.7% accuracy with PPL 20.5, outperforming the best autoregressive baseline LM-Steer (85.4%, PPL 78.8) by over 9 points in accuracy. On detoxification, TTA-DIFFUSION (T=200) attains the lowest average toxicity (12.2) and maximum toxicity (26.0) among all methods, compared to TESS (14.6 / 32.3) and DExperts (15.1 / 32.0), while also achieving lower perplexity (40.6 vs. 58.8 for TESS). In lexically-constrained generation, TTA-DIFFUSION reduces perplexity from 248.6 (Diffusion-LM) to 111.4 while improving syntax tree accuracy from 86.0% to 93.1%. Notably, these gains are achieved with as few as 50 diffusion steps, compared to 1000–2500 steps used by prior diffusion baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTA-DIFFUSION consistently outperforms all autoregressive baselines (PPLM, GeDi, DExperts, Air-decoding, LM-Steer) on both detoxification and sentiment control. On sentiment accuracy, TTA(50) at T=200 reaches 94.7%, surpassing the strongest autoregressive method LM-Steer (85.4%) by 9.3 points and the best diffusion baseline SSD-LM (76.2%) by 18.5 points. On detoxification, TTA-DIFFUSION achieves the lowest maximum toxicity (26.0) compared to TESS (32.3), DExperts (32.0), and GeDi (36.1), while also matching or beating their perplexity (40.6 vs. 58.8 for TESS, 48.0 for DExperts). Against the closest diffusion competitor TESS (T=1000), TTA-DIFFUSION uses only 200 steps (5× fewer) yet improves both toxicity and fluency. In lexically-constrained generation, TTA-DIFFUSION nearly halves perplexity relative to Diffusion-LM (111.4 vs. 248.6) and improves syntax tree accuracy (93.1% vs. 86.0%), though it slightly trails on syntax span accuracy (92.3% vs. 93.8%). The adaptive allocation also transfers to discrete diffusion (D-CBG on QM9), improving validity from 989% to 998% and mean property from 0.474 to 0.494.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the simplex diffusion language model framework (TESS, SSD-LM) by introducing per-token timestep allocation as a soft alternative to the hard unmasking-based token ordering explored in masked diffusion (Kim et al., Nie et al.). The classifier-guided update mechanism is inherited from Diffusion-LM, while the per-token timestep concept draws from AR-Diffusion, though here it is decoupled from positional training-time speed and instead driven by semantic importance signals (classifier gradients). The progressive step-reduction strategy adapts progressive distillation but replaces the distillation objective with direct cross-entropy fine-tuning, sidestepping instability observed in small-timestep regimes. The key conceptual departure is reframing token ordering as a continuous, reversible scheduling problem rather than a discrete, irreversible masking decision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying update-forgetting as a formal, measurable failure mode and showing that a simple inference-time scheduling rule mitigates it, the paper provides a practical lever for making diffusion-based controllable generation deployable at 50–100 steps rather than the 500–1000+ steps typical of prior methods. The approach is model-agnostic (validated on simplex, continuous, and discrete diffusion) and requires no retraining, which broadens its applicability. Limitations include reliance on an external classifier for the adaptive schedule (limiting use to settings where such a signal is available), evaluation primarily at 330M parameter scale, and theoretical guarantees that depend on smoothness and budget assumptions (A1–A3) not always satisfied in practice. Open questions include extension to multi-attribute control, alternative importance signals beyond classifier gradients, and whether the benefits persist at larger model scales.
