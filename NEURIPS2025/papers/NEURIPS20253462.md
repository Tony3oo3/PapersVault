## Problem

Graph Foundation Models are bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale, semantically rich graph corpora, and existing synthesis methods (edge-level operations, feature interpolation, graph mixup) cannot generate coherent textual node attributes while preserving structural validity. Directly applying LLMs to graph synthesis is further impeded by context window limits, hallucination, and the inability to maintain topological consistency across generated elements, particularly in data-limited settings where few examples are available to learn graph patterns.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data-limited text-attributed graph (nodes with free-text attributes, an adjacency matrix, and class labels) as input and produces an enhanced graph containing newly synthesized nodes with coherent textual descriptions, new intra-synthetic edges, and cross-edges linking synthetic nodes back to the original graph. The retrieved knowledge subgraph is hard-constrained to N=30 nodes to fit within the LLM context window, and the number of newly generated nodes per iteration is capped at M%=15% of that subgraph. Downstream, textual attributes are encoded via Sentence-BERT before being fed into standard GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, JKNet) for node classification evaluation.

## Method

GraphMaster is a four-agent LLM framework formalized as a hierarchical Retrieval-Augmented Generation loop: a Manager Agent formulates adaptive queries and selects between semantic and topological enhancement modes by optimizing a multi-objective utility over semantic coherence, structural integrity, and class balance; a Perception Agent retrieves a representative subgraph via semantic-enriched modularity maximization, mode-adaptive seed community selection, and stochastic Personalized PageRank diffusion; an Enhancement Agent generates new node text through conditional autoregressive decoding and models edge connections via a sigmoid function combining semantic similarity, neighborhood overlap, and degree normalization; and an Evaluation Agent scores each generated node against both the initial and current environmental reports, applying an adaptively updated acceptance threshold and a temporal quality-gradient convergence test. The entire cycle iterates until convergence, with the base LLM (QwQ-32B) assuming each agent role through targeted prompting, and the architecture ensures that structural and semantic quality are jointly enforced rather than handled in a single pass.

## Results

On the GCN downstream model, GraphMaster achieves the best Accuracy and F1 across all six data-limited TAG benchmarks: 93.7% Acc / 92.5% F1 on SubCora, 88.3% / 87.7% on SubCiteseer, 87.9% / 86.8% on SubWikiCS, 92.6% / 63.4% on SubHistory, 87.9% / 66.3% on SubArxiv2023, and 68.8% / 47.8% on SubChildren. The largest gains over the strongest LLM-based baseline (Synthesis-LLM) appear on SubArxiv2023 (+6.9% Acc) and SubChildren (+7.9% Acc), while the smallest margin is on SubWikiCS (+3.0% Acc). All experiments are repeated 50 times with QwQ-32B as the base LLM, N=30 background nodes, and M=15% new-node ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphMaster outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset for both Accuracy and F1. Among classic synthesis methods, IntraMix and AGMixup perform worst (e.g., 80.9% and 84.7% Acc on SubCora vs. GraphMaster's 93.7%), while GraphAdasyn and FG-SMOTE are the strongest classic baselines but still trail by 3.5–4.5% Acc. The LLM augmentation baselines (GraphEdit, LLM4RGNN) improve over classic methods but remain 2.5–4% below GraphMaster. Among LLM synthesis baselines, GAG is the strongest on Cora (91.0% Acc) yet still 2.7% behind GraphMaster; LLM4NG underperforms dramatically on Children (44.6% vs. 68.8% Acc) due to its simplistic label-only text generation and noisy edge prediction. The two author-created baselines (Mixed-LLM and Synthesis-LLM) are the closest competitors, with Synthesis-LLM reaching 89.8% on SubCora and 81.0% on SubArxiv2023, but GraphMaster extends the lead to 3.9% and 6.9% respectively, confirming that the multi-agent RAG loop with iterative evaluation provides a meaningful advantage over single-pass LLM synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph data synthesis literature (GraphSMOTE, G-Mixup, IntraMix, etc.) by introducing a multi-agent RAG architecture specifically for text-attributed graphs in data-limited settings. It departs from prior LLM-for-graphs work (GAG for social network simulation, LLM4NG for few-shot node generation) by framing synthesis as an iterative retrieval-generate-evaluate loop with specialized agents, rather than a single-pass generation task. The conceptual backbone draws on the graph RAG survey [54], repurposing the retrieve-augment-generate paradigm from question-answering into a closed-loop data synthesis pipeline where the 'retrieval' step is a semantic-aware subgraph sampler and the 'generation' step is conditional node/edge synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck for Graph Foundation Models—the lack of large-scale, semantically rich graph corpora—and provides both a method (GraphMaster) and evaluation infrastructure (Sub* data-limited benchmarks, Grassmannian-manifold interpretability metric) that the community can reuse. However, the practical impact is currently bounded: experiments are limited to six small academic citation/social networks, the framework depends on a 32B-parameter LLM for every synthesis iteration, and the multi-agent loop adds significant latency. Open questions include whether the approach scales to industrial-scale graphs (millions of nodes), whether the RAG framing generalizes to heterogeneous or temporal graphs, and whether the Grassmannian coherence metric transfers to non-textual node attributes.
