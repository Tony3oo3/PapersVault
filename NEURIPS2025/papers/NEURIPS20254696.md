## Problem

In performative stochastic optimization, decisions influence the distribution of uncertain parameters, but practitioners only have access to imperfect reference distribution maps, leading to severely suboptimal solutions under misspecification. Existing distributionally robust performative prediction methods rely on unrealistic smoothness/sensitivity assumptions or use KL-divergence ambiguity sets that cannot handle non-overlapping supports, and no prior work established convergence guarantees for robust performative algorithms. This paper fills the gap by introducing the first Wasserstein-based distributionally robust framework for performative optimization with rigorous convergence and suboptimality theory.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite-support reference distribution map (scenarios ẑ_s(θ) with probabilities p̂_s(θ)), a closed decision set Θ ⊆ ℝ^d, a Wasserstein ambiguity radius ρ, and a loss function ℓ(z, θ) satisfying model-specific regularity conditions. The output is a robust performatively stable decision θ_RPS — a fixed point of the robust decoupled performative risk J_θ(θ) = sup_{Q ∈ B(P̂(θ))} E_Q[ℓ(z̃, θ)] — computed as the limit of an iterative sequence where each step solves a tractable conic or convex program. The algorithm terminates when successive iterates fall within a user-specified tolerance Δ, with the number of iterations bounded by (1−εκ)^{-1} log(‖θ_0 − θ_RPS‖/Δ).

## Method

The core algorithm, Repeated Robust Risk Minimization (RRRM), decouples the decision-dependent ambiguity set from the loss by fixing the ambiguity set at the previous iteration's decision θ_t, reducing the nonconvex bilinear DRPO problem to a sequence of tractable subproblems. Three reformulations are provided: Model 1 shows that under a 1-Wasserstein ball with Schatten-∞ cost and Lipschitz-composed quadratic loss, the robust risk equals a Tikhonov-regularized empirical risk (generalizing prior results that required martingale constraints); Model 2 handles max-of-quadratics losses via log-sum-exp smoothing and exponential cone constraints, yielding a convex conic program solvable in seconds; Model 3 exploits a 2-Wasserstein ball with convex-concave losses to derive a finite convex program through convex conjugate and support function representations. Theoretical analysis establishes linear convergence to a unique robust performatively stable point under ε-sensitivity of the distribution map, with the robust framework accelerating convergence relative to non-robust counterparts and providing explicit suboptimality bounds that vanish as the sensitivity parameter ε → 0.

## Results

The paper evaluates its distributionally robust performative optimization (DRPO) framework on three applications: strategic classification (credit scoring), revenue management (newsvendor pricing), and demand response portfolio optimization. In strategic classification (200 training samples, ~3,600 test points, ρ=0.1, 40 iterations), both type-1 and type-2 Wasserstein robust models significantly outperform the non-robust baseline and the AMKL (KL-divergence) algorithm across 50 independent trials, with the type-2 Wasserstein variant performing best. In revenue management, the robust price consistently achieves a smaller optimality gap relative to the true optimal price compared to the non-robust surrogate-based price. In demand response portfolio optimization, the robust scheme outperforms the non-robust scheme, with the gap widening substantially under high demand loads where the non-robust scheme frequently incurs losses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: (1) the AMKL algorithm from Xue & Sun (2024), which uses a KL-divergence ambiguity set with alternating minimization, and (2) the non-robust performative prediction baseline from Perdomo et al. (2020). In strategic classification, both Wasserstein-based robust models (type-1 and type-2) outperform AMKL, which the authors attribute to AMKL's susceptibility to local optima and the inability of KL-divergence to guard against distributional shifts with non-overlapping supports. All robust variants significantly outperform the non-robust baseline, with the type-2 Wasserstein model achieving the best out-of-sample performance, likely due to its wider effective range of robustness radius values. In revenue management, the robust price (Model 2, piecewise quadratic loss) consistently outperforms the non-robust price derived from the misspecified surrogate model and approaches the true optimal price (available in closed form when the true distribution is known). In demand response portfolio optimization, the robust scheme (also Model 2) dominates the non-robust scheme, with the advantage being most pronounced under high demand where the non-robust approach frequently produces negative profits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate literatures: performative prediction (Perdomo et al., 2020), where decisions shift the data distribution, and Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization (Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn, 2018), which guards against distributional misspecification. It departs from prior distributionally robust performative prediction work (Xue & Sun, 2024; Peet-Pare et al., 2022) by adopting a Wasserstein ambiguity set instead of KL/phi-divergence, which the authors argue provides better coverage guarantees for non-overlapping supports. The Tikhonov regularization equivalence in Model 1 generalizes Li et al. (2022) from strongly convex quadratic losses with martingale constraints to a broader class of Lipschitz-composed losses without such constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first Wasserstein DRO framework for performative optimization with convergence and suboptimality guarantees, extending the performative paradigm beyond prediction to general decision-making (revenue management, portfolio optimization). The convergence result showing that distributional robustness accelerates convergence relative to non-robust performative prediction is a useful theoretical insight. Open questions include whether the ambiguity radius can be adaptively shrunk over time as more data accumulates, and whether contextual side information can tighten the ambiguity set. A practical limitation is the reliance on reference distributions at finitely many decisions, which may be difficult to obtain in high-dimensional settings.
