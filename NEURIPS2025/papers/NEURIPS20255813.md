## Problem

For the maximum of n i.i.d. samples (ℓ=1), it is known that M₁(n)=o(n) and this bound is tight: any sublinear growth rate is achievable by some finite-expectation distribution. The natural conjecture is that the same characterization holds for the ℓ-th maximum with ℓ>1. This paper shows a sharp contrast: for any fixed ℓ≥2, the achievable growth rate is fundamentally capped, with a precise threshold separating functions g(n) for which M_ℓ(n)=Ω(g(n)) is possible from those for which it is impossible for all finite-expectation distributions.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes an arbitrary probability distribution F on non-negative reals with finite expectation, a sample size n, and a fixed order statistic index ℓ≥1, and produces a tight Θ characterization of M_ℓ^F(n)=E[X_{n-ℓ+1:n}] in terms of the step sizes δ_k of an associated 'escalator' distribution. The key output is a necessary-and-sufficient convergence condition: M_ℓ^F(n)=Ω(g(n)) for some finite-expectation F if and only if ∑_n g(n)/n² < ∞, which pins the maximal achievable rate at approximately n/(log n · log log n) for ℓ≥2. The empirical component estimates M_ℓ(n) via 1,000,000 Monte Carlo trials using inverse-transform sampling with a Pareto(1) auxiliary variable.

## Method

The core technique is a reduction from arbitrary distributions to 'escalator distributions' F⁺ whose CDF takes only values 1−1/k, parameterized by non-negative step sizes δ_k. For such distributions, M_ℓ^{F⁺}(n) decomposes exactly into ∑_k P[Y_k≥ℓ]·δ_k where Y_k~Binomial(n,1/k), reducing the problem to bounding binomial tail probabilities. The paper proves asymptotically tight (constant-factor) bounds on P[Y_k≥ℓ] in two regimes: for k≤n the probability is bounded below by 1/(2e·ℓ!), and for k>n it scales as (n/k)^ℓ up to constants. Combining these yields Theorem 1: M_ℓ^F(n)=Θ(∑_{k<n}δ_k + ∑_{k≥n}(n/k)^ℓδ_k). The main Theorem 2 then follows by an averaging argument: summing the upper bound over all n and interchanging summation indices shows that M_ℓ^F(n)≥g(n) for all n forces ∑g(n)/n²<∞, while the converse is established by explicitly constructing an escalator distribution with δ_k proportional to k^ℓ·(1/(k^{ℓ-1}h(k)) − 1/((k+1)^{ℓ-1}h(k+1))) where h(n)=n/g(n).

## Results

The paper establishes a sharp characterization of the largest achievable growth rate for the expected ℓ-th maximum M_ℓ(n) of n i.i.d. samples. For ℓ ≥ 2, M_ℓ^F(n) = Ω(g(n)) for some finite-expectation distribution F if and only if Σ_n g(n)/n² < ∞, yielding a threshold at n/(log n · log log n): the rate n/(log n · (log log n)^{1.01}) is achievable, but n/(log n · log log n) is not. This stands in stark contrast to ℓ = 1, where any sublinear g(n) is achievable. Empirically, under an infinite-variance Pareto-type tail, the ratio M̂₁(n)/M̂₂(n) grows rapidly with n, but after censoring the top 1% of extreme trials, the ratio stabilizes to a near-constant value across all tested n (10 to 10,000).

**Summary of achievable growth rates for M_ℓ(n) under finite-expectation distributions.**

| Setting | Achievable rate | Not achievable | Key result |
|---|---|---|---|
| ℓ = 1 (maximum) | Any sublinear g(n) = o(n) | Linear O(n) | Downey [10]; Correa & Romero [8] |
| ℓ ≥ 2, finite E[X] | n / (log n · (log log n)^{1+ε}) | n / (log n · log log n) | Theorem 2 (this paper) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between ℓ = 1 and ℓ ≥ 2, revealing a sharp phase transition. For the maximum (ℓ = 1), prior work [10, 8] shows that for any sublinear g(n) there exists a finite-expectation F with M_1^F(n) = Ω(g(n)), meaning the liminf characterization matches the limsup one. For ℓ ≥ 2, the limsup result still holds (Proposition 5: any sublinear g(n) is exceeded infinitely often), but the liminf result fails: no distribution can maintain M_ℓ^F(n) = Ω(n/(log n · log log n)) for all n. The empirical section further contrasts infinite-variance and finite-variance tails: under T(y) = y/log(y)^{1.01} (infinite variance), M̂₁(n) is highly variable across 10⁶ trials while M̂₂(n) remains tightly concentrated, and the ratio M̂₁/M̂₂ grows with n; under T(y) = √(y/log(y)^{1.01}) (finite variance), all order statistics are well-behaved and the ratio stays stable. Censoring the top 1% of extreme maxima in the infinite-variance case restores the near-constant ratio, confirming that the divergence is driven by rare extreme events rather than systematic separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical line of work on distribution-free bounds for order statistics, building directly on Downey's (1990) result that M_1(n) = o(n) and Correa & Romero's (2021) strengthening showing the maximum can grow as fast as any sublinear function. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the 'escalator distribution' as a reduction tool, which transforms the analysis of arbitrary finite-expectation distributions into a structured discrete setting amenable to binomial tail bounds. The paper then proves that the ℓ=1 picture (any sublinear rate achievable) breaks down sharply for ℓ≥2, yielding a necessary-and-sufficient summability condition (∑g(n)/n² < ∞) that has no analogue in the maximum case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sharp contrast between ℓ=1 and ℓ≥2 has direct implications for second-price auction revenue bounds, best-arm identification in bandits, and tail-sensitive scoring in OOD detection, where the second-highest value is the operative quantity. The empirical observation that the M_1/M_2 ratio is stable in 99% of trials despite worst-case divergence suggests typical-case behavior is far more benign than the theory permits, which is practically reassuring. Open questions include the intermediate regime ℓ ∈ [ω(1), o(n)], the distribution of the ratio X_{n:n}/X_{n-1:n}, and extending the characterization to higher moments under finite-variance assumptions.
