## Problem

Automated Verilog code generation is hampered by the scarcity of HDL training data, the absence of publicly available chain-of-thought reasoning traces for hardware design, and the lack of reliable correctness-checking mechanisms for scraped code. Existing Verilog datasets provide neither paired natural-language prompts nor formal verification-based quality labels, making it impossible to systematically study how reasoning, prompt quality, and hallucination affect downstream code generation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250288/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each data point is a tuple {V, Q, R, V*, Lc} where V is ground-truth synthesizable Verilog (≤10K characters, sourced from MetRex), Q is a Gemini-generated natural-language question specifying module name and I/O interface, R is a DeepSeek-R1 reasoning trace, V* is the generated Verilog, and Lc is a binary formal-equivalence label. For fine-tuning, Q serves as the prompt and either V (ground truth) or V* (generated) serves as the target; the VeriThoughts benchmark evaluates models on 291 holdout questions using Yosys-based formal equivalence checking rather than testbench simulation.

## Method

The pipeline has four stages: (1) collect synthesizable Verilog from MetRex, (2) prompt Gemini-2.0-Flash-Thinking to generate a question Q whose answer is V, with constraints to include module/I/O names and avoid leaking implementation details, (3) feed Q to DeepSeek-R1-670B (temperature 0.6, top_p 0.95, 8192-token max) to obtain a reasoning trace R and candidate Verilog V*, and (4) run Yosys formal equivalence checking (combinational miter + SAT solver, with clocked flip-flops converted to combinational logic) to produce the self-consistency label Lc = E(V = V*). The key insight is that formal equivalence checking serves as a scalable proxy for question-answer consistency, eliminating the need for manual test-case generation. The resulting 20K-sample dataset is then used for supervised fine-tuning of Qwen 2.5-Instruct models (7B and 14B) via Llama-Factory, with separate subsets isolating the effects of reasoning traces, self-consistency, and hallucinated prompt-answer pairs on downstream VerilogEval and VeriThoughts benchmark performance.

## Results

The VeriThoughts-14B model (fine-tuned Qwen2.5-14B with ~10K self-consistent reasoning samples) achieves 43.7% Pass@1 on VerilogEval, the highest among all open-source models tested, and 78.5% Pass@1 on the new VeriThoughts formal-verification benchmark. On VerilogEval Pass@5 and Pass@10, it scores 52.2% and 55.1%, trailing DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B (62.1% and 69.0%). On the VeriThoughts benchmark, Qwen3-14B leads with 87.4% Pass@1 versus VeriThoughts-14B's 78.5%, while closed-source ChatGPT-o3 reaches 92.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VerilogEval Pass@1, VeriThoughts-14B (43.7%) outperforms all open-source baselines: Qwen3-14B (28.1%), DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B (38.7%), Qwen2.5-14B (30.7%), CodeLlama-13B (20.7%), and CL-Verilog-7B/13B (21.7%/26.0%). However, on VerilogEval Pass@5 and Pass@10, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B clearly leads (62.1%/69.0%) over VeriThoughts-14B (52.2%/55.1%), suggesting the distilled reasoning model retains stronger multi-sample diversity. On the VeriThoughts formal-verification benchmark, Qwen3-14B (87.4% Pass@1) surpasses VeriThoughts-14B (78.5%), though both trail closed-source ChatGPT-o3 (92.6%). Against the Verilog-specific baselines (CL-Verilog-7B/13B, CodeLlama-13B), VeriThoughts-14B achieves substantial gains of 50–73 percentage points on Pass@1 across both benchmarks, demonstrating the value of reasoning-trace fine-tuning over base-model or instruction-tuned approaches for HDL generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MetRex dataset of synthesizable Verilog designs and extends the Verilog code generation line of work initiated by VeriGen and RTL-Coder, which provided training corpora but lacked paired natural-language prompts, reasoning traces, and quality labels. The key methodological departure is the use of formal equivalence checking via Yosys (a SAT-based miter construction) as a self-consistency proxy for question-answer quality, replacing the weaker testbench-simulation evaluation used in VerilogEval and prior benchmarks. The pipeline also repurposes frontier reasoning models (Gemini-2.0-Flash for question generation, DeepSeek-R1 for CoT trace extraction) in a structured data-annotation loop, a pattern borrowed from the broader reasoning-model SFT literature but applied here for the first time to HDL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset and formal-verification benchmark address a real gap: prior Verilog evaluation relied on hand-written or LLM-generated testbenches that are incomplete and buggy, whereas formal equivalence checking provides a mathematically sound correctness signal without manual test design. The finding that training on 'hallucinated' (inconsistent) question-Verilog pairs can outperform consistent pairs is intriguing but confounded by the fact that the inconsistent subset contains systematically harder designs (more lines, more sequential logic, longer reasoning traces), so the causal mechanism remains open. Limitations include reliance on a single base-model family (Qwen 2.5/3), no error bars or multi-seed runs, and a training set of only ~10K samples, leaving open whether the conclusions generalize across architectures and scale.
