## Problem

Alternating Least Squares (ALS) is the dominant practical algorithm for tensor decomposition, yet it lacks global convergence guarantees in the non-convex setting. While overparameterization has been shown to enable global convergence for gradient descent variants (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2020, requiring k = O(r^{7.5} log n)), no analogous guarantee existed for ALS. This paper asks whether ALS admits a polynomial-time global convergence guarantee with moderate overparameterization that is a function of the rank r rather than the ambient dimension n.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a third-order n×n×n tensor T with a mildly conditioned rank-r decomposition (condition numbers ≤ r^{c₀}) and an error tolerance ε, and produces rank-k factor matrices X, Y, Z ∈ R^{n×k} with k = O(r²) that reconstruct T to within ε in Frobenius norm. The algorithm runs in O(1) iterations (concretely, a constant number of parallel ALS updates) with polynomial time per iteration. For the low-rank approximation variant, the input tensor may include additive noise E, and the output achieves error bounded by poly(r)·||E||_F + ε, with the multiplicative loss depending only on r and not on n.

## Method

The paper analyzes a parallel-update variant of ALS where all three factor matrices are updated simultaneously via least-squares subproblems solved with the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse. The key insight is that after the first ALS iteration, the updated matrices X, Y, Z lie in the column spans of the true factors A, B, C, and the Khatri-Rao product X⊙Y spans the Kronecker product space A⊗B (dimension r²), which is sufficient for exact recovery when k = Ω(r²). The main technical challenge is that the first iteration introduces complex dependencies among X, Y, Z, destroying the independence present at random initialization. The authors overcome this with new matrix anticoncentration arguments: they show the pseudoinverse (X⊙Z)† is close to (1/n²)(X⊙Z)(I+E) via Gershgorin's theorem on the Gram matrix, then prove a least singular value lower bound for the resulting structured random matrix using an ε-net argument combined with the Carbery-Wright inequality for degree-2 polynomials. For the robust low-rank approximation setting, a Davis-Kahan perturbation argument controls how noise in the tensor propagates through the ALS iterates.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 2.1) shows that ALS on a rank-r tensor T ∈ ℝ^{n×n×n} with mildly conditioned factors (κ ≤ r^{c₀}), when overparameterized to k = O(r²) and randomly initialized, converges to a global minimum (objective ≤ ε) in O(1) iterations with probability 1-o(1). Theorem 2.2 extends this to the low-rank approximation setting, where ALS achieves an objective value within a poly(r) multiplicative factor of OPT_r, independent of n. The key technical ingredient (Theorem 4.1) establishes a least singular value bound σ_{r²}(X̂ ⊙ Ŷ) ≥ 1/(n⁴ poly(k)) via new matrix anticoncentration arguments, which is the crux of showing that one ALS iteration preserves enough randomness to span the correct subspace.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is with the gradient descent analysis of Wang et al. [WWL+20], which requires overparameterization k = O(r^{7.5} log n) for global convergence. This work reduces the required overparameterization to k = O(r²), a significant improvement in the dependence on r (quadratic vs. r^{7.5}) while also removing the log n factor. Compared to the Orthogonalized ALS of Sharan and Valiant [SV17], which operates at k = r but requires orthogonality or incoherence of the factors, this work provides guarantees for standard ALS at k = O(r²) under only mild conditioning (condition number polynomial in r), without any incoherence or orthogonality assumptions. Against spectral methods [BCV14, SWZ19] that also achieve k = O(r²) guarantees, the ALS approach is far more scalable in practice as it avoids expensive eigenvalue computations. The paper also provides the first approximation guarantee for an iterative method in the general low-rank approximation setting (OPT_r > 0), which was not previously known for gradient descent or other iterative algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the overparameterization framework for non-convex optimization to the ALS algorithm for tensor decomposition, building directly on Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2020) who showed gradient descent converges with k=O(r^{7.5} log n) overparameterization via mean-field analysis. It departs from prior approaches by introducing new matrix anticoncentration techniques (leveraging the Carbery-Wright inequality and Gershgorin disc theorem) to handle the complex dependencies that arise in ALS iterates, rather than relying on lazy-training or standard mean-field analyses that require overparameterization polynomial in the ambient dimension n. The work also generalizes beyond exact decomposition to the low-rank approximation problem, for which no prior iterative-method guarantees existed, and avoids the orthogonality/incoherence assumptions required by Sharan & Valiant's orthogonalized ALS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALS is the dominant practical algorithm for tensor decomposition yet lacked any global convergence guarantee in the overparameterized regime; this paper closes that gap with a dramatically tighter overparameterization bound of O(r²) versus O(r^{7.5} log n) for gradient descent. The new matrix anticoncentration framework is a potentially reusable tool for analyzing other iterative methods in the overparameterized setting. Open questions include whether O(r²) is the tight overparameterization threshold, whether the mild conditioning assumption on factor matrices is truly necessary, and whether the techniques extend to standard (sequential) ALS or to gradient descent. The low-rank approximation guarantee (poly(r) competitive with OPT_r) is the first such result for any iterative method.
