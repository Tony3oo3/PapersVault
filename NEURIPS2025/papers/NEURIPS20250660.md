## Problem

Standard SGD requires tuning the learning rate to both the unknown noise level and the training horizon, and existing schedule-free algorithms either depend on noise-specific hyperparameters or sacrifice last-iterate convergence quality. This paper asks whether a single, noise-adaptive step-size rule can eliminate both the noise-dependent and horizon-dependent tuning while preserving the key trajectory properties (no initial plateau, no explosion, no saturation) that make schedule-free methods attractive when tuning budgets are small.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a β-smooth loss function, a stream of unbiased stochastic gradient estimates G_{t+1} ≈ ∇L(θ_t), and the smoothness constant β as its sole hyperparameter. At each step it produces an updated parameter θ_{t+1} and an adaptive step size η_t = Stab(G_{t+1}|F_t)/β, where the stability ratio is estimated online from a small batch (ζ samples, typically 10–100) of gradient draws at the current iterate using a Jackknife resampling procedure with O(1) space complexity. No noise variance σ² or horizon T appears in the algorithm's parameters.

## Method

The core mechanism is the stability ratio Stab(X) = ‖E[X]‖²/ E[‖X‖²] ∈ [0,1], which quantifies the fraction of gradient energy that is signal versus noise. Stab-SGD sets the step size to η_t = Stab(G_{t+1}|F_t)/β, so that in low-noise regimes the step approaches the deterministic optimum 1/β, while in high-noise regimes it shrinks proportionally to the signal-to-noise ratio, guaranteeing a strict expected loss decrease at every step. A Jackknife-based estimator R_n = (n/(n−1))(‖M_n‖² − Z_n)/Z_n computed from n iid gradient samples provides a consistent, low-bias estimate of the stability ratio with relative error O(R_★^{-1}·n^{-1/2}). Convergence is proven via a stochastic Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz integration framework that composes the sufficient-decrease inequality with affine variance bounds, yielding last-iterate rates of O(T^{-1/3}) (weakly convex), O(T^{-1}) (strongly convex), and O(T^{-1/2}) (non-convex) that match known optimal SGD rates without any noise- or horizon-dependent tuning.

## Results

Stab-SGD achieves optimal last-iterate convergence rates across all smooth settings without noise-dependent or horizon-dependent hyperparameters: O(T^{-1/3}) for convex, O(T^{-1}) for strongly convex, and O(T^{-1/2}) for non-convex problems under affine variance. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-56, Algorithm 1 (inline Stab-SGD) reaches performance comparable to tuned SGD with a step-decay schedule, requiring approximately 2× the iterations (raw count including stability-estimation overhead) but eliminating the need for any noise-dependent hyperparameter tuning. In convex experiments, Stab-SGD outperforms COCOB and D-Adapt by 8 orders of magnitude on strongly convex problems at 10⁴ iterations, while remaining competitive on high-noise weakly-convex problems where SGD requires ≥71% of total budget for hyperparameter tuning to match it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Stab-SGD against three schedule-free baselines: COCOB (Orabona & Tommasi, NeurIPS 2017), D-Adapt (Defazio & Mishchenko, ICML 2023), and Schedule-free SGD (Defazio et al., NeurIPS 2024). On the strongly-convex low-noise problem (QSC), COCOB and D-Adapt—which rely on iterate-averaging and are designed for the noisy regime—fail to exploit strong convexity and are 8 orders of magnitude behind Stab-SGD at 10⁴ iterations. On the high-noise weakly-convex problem (QWC), the leaderboard reverses: COCOB performs much better than Stab-SGD, while Schedule-free SGD becomes indistinguishable from equivalently-tuned SGD. Stab-SGD's key advantage is consistent performance across both regimes without any noise-dependent tuning, whereas each baseline excels in only one setting. On CIFAR-10, Stab-SGD (Algorithm 1) matches tuned SGD-with-schedule in final accuracy but at roughly 2× the raw iteration cost due to stability-estimation overhead, making it competitive whenever the scheduler requires more than 2 tuning runs (k > 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the schedule-free optimization literature by introducing the stability ratio as a local, computable measure of gradient noise that directly modulates the step size. It departs from the Lipschitz-model branch (COCOB, D-Adapt) by working in the smooth (Lipschitz-gradient) regime with last-iterate guarantees, and from the smooth-model branch (Bach and Moulines) by eliminating both the noise-dependent and horizon-dependent hyperparameters through a single adaptive mechanism. The analysis technique—a stochastic Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz integration—repurposes continuous-time desingularizer ideas into a discrete stochastic setting, and the Jackknife-based stability estimator borrows from classical bias-correction literature to avoid the strong bias of naive sample-mean estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stability ratio provides a conceptually clean and computationally cheap (O(1) space) mechanism for noise-adaptivity that could serve as a building block for future accelerated or non-convex schedule-free algorithms. However, the current method's practical impact is limited: on ResNet-56/CIFAR-10 it requires roughly twice as many iterations as tuned SGD and exhibits higher seed variance, and its asymptotic rate is suboptimal compared to averaging-based methods. Key open questions include constructing accelerated noise-adaptive variants (which the authors conjecture would be substantially more complex), characterizing noise-continuity conditions under which infrequent stability re-estimation is provably safe, and extending the analysis to non-convex settings with last-iterate loss guarantees rather than gradient-norm bounds.
