## Problem

Existing NeRF-based wireless methods (NeRF2, NeWRF) can synthesize WiFi signals at new locations but do not learn the true physical environment—their virtual transmitters overfit to sparse measurements without embedding correct multipath reflection physics. This paper asks whether a redesigned NeRF can solve the inverse problem: recovering the indoor floorplan (wall positions and orientations) from sparse WiFi RSSI measurements that contain a mixture of line-of-sight and multipath reflections.

## Input / Output

The method takes M fixed transmitter locations, N sparse receiver locations (1000–2000 points sampled along a walking trajectory), and the scalar RSSI measured at each Rx for each Tx (M×N total measurements) as input. The output is a 2D floorplan on a 512×512 grid, represented implicitly as an MLP that predicts per-voxel opacity δ∈[0,1] and discrete orientation ω (multiples of π/4). Beyond the floorplan, the learned representation enables forward tasks: predicting RSSI at novel ⟨Tx,Rx⟩ pairs and performing basic first-order ray tracing for interpretability.

## Method

EchoNeRF models received power as the sum of a LoS term (Friss equation with an occlusion product over voxels along the ray) and a first-order reflection term aggregated over a pruned 'plausible set' of voxels. The key mechanism is discretizing voxel orientation to K_ω=4 values, which geometrically restricts which voxels can plausibly reflect a ray between a given ⟨Tx,Rx⟩ pair, turning an under-determined many-component decomposition into a tractable optimization. Training proceeds in two stages to resolve a gradient imbalance where LoS gradients (shorter paths, fewer product terms) dominate reflection gradients: Stage 1 trains only the LoS model to convergence, then Stage 2 freezes the LoS estimate and trains the reflection model against the residual power (measured minus LoS). A simple 8-layer MLP (256 hidden units) predicts opacity via sigmoid and orientation via Gumbel-softmax, with total-variation regularization on neighboring orientations and spatial-gradient supervision of ω from the learned opacity field.

## Results

On the ZIND dataset with 2000 receiver locations, EchoNeRF achieves a Wall_IoU of 0.38±0.06 and F1 score of 0.55±0.06 for floorplan reconstruction, substantially outperforming all baselines. For RSSI prediction at new locations, EchoNeRF attains an RPE of 3.56 dB, competitive with the best baselines (MLP: 1.03, Heatmap Seg: 1.32) while uniquely producing a geometric floorplan. With only 1000 receiver locations, EchoNeRF still leads on Wall_IoU (0.32) and F1 (0.48), demonstrating robustness to sparser measurements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoNeRF decisively outperforms NeRF2 on floorplan quality: Wall_IoU of 0.38 vs. 0.14 and F1 of 0.55 vs. 0.24 (2000 Rx), confirming that NeRF2's virtual transmitters do not correspond to true wall surfaces despite achieving low CIR prediction error. Against Heatmap Segmentation, EchoNeRF more than triples the Wall_IoU (0.38 vs. 0.12) and F1 (0.55 vs. 0.21), as the heatmap approach is limited by reflection-induced RSSI pollution and cannot capture boundary walls. On the forward RSSI prediction task (RPE), EchoNeRF (3.56 dB) is slightly worse than MLP (1.03 dB) and Heatmap Seg (1.32 dB), but those methods do not produce a floorplan; notably, EchoNeRF's RPE advantage over NeRF2 (3.56 vs. 4.36) is most pronounced when the Tx location changes from training, validating the benefit of solving the inverse problem first. The LoS-only ablation (EchoNeRF_LoS) confirms the reflection model's contribution: adding first-order reflections improves Wall_IoU from 0.27 to 0.38 and F1 from 0.42 to 0.55, while reducing RPE from 9.12 to 3.56.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the NeRF volumetric scene function (Mildenhall et al., 2021) for wireless signal modeling, departing from prior wireless NeRF work (NeRF2, NeWRF) that focuses on forward CIR/RIR prediction. The key conceptual departure is targeting the inverse problem—recovering a 2D floorplan from sparse RSSI measurements—rather than synthesizing signals at new locations. The reflection model draws on the physics of specular reflection (discrete wall orientations, plausible reflection sets) in a way that is distinct from optical NeRF reflection work (Ref-NeRF, NeRF-Casting), which handles known surfaces, and from LIDAR-based NeRFs (PlatoNeRF, NeTF) that benefit from temporal separation of multipath components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a physics-informed NeRF can produce visually legible floorplans from only RSSI scalars, establishing feasibility for camera-free indoor mapping. A notable finding is that NeRF2 and NeWRF achieve low CIR prediction error without learning correct wall geometry, revealing a gap between prediction accuracy and physical understanding. Key limitations include reliance on simulation (Sionna), 2D-only formulation, first-order reflections only, and no through-wall modeling. Open questions include extending to 2nd-order reflections for small objects, 3D floorplans, real-world validation, and whether the learnt floorplans can bootstrap better RIR synthesis in an alternating optimization loop.
