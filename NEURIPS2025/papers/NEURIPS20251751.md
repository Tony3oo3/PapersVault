## Problem

Existing deletion-based randomized smoothing for sequence classification uses a fixed deletion rate for all inputs, ignoring natural variation in sequence length and complexity. This one-size-fits-all approach leads to suboptimal robustness-accuracy trade-offs, particularly for inputs that deviate from the average length. The paper addresses the largely unexplored problem of input-dependent (variable-rate) deletion smoothing for discrete sequences, which requires a new theoretical analysis to obtain sound edit distance certificates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a variable-length text sequence (word-level tokens) and a deletion probability function ψ(x) that maps the input to a per-token deletion rate. It produces a class label prediction along with a certified edit distance radius r, guaranteed with confidence 1−α that the prediction is invariant to all perturbations within edit distance r (including deletions, insertions, and substitutions). The deletion rate adapts to input length: shorter sequences receive lower deletion rates (clipped at p_lb to avoid vacuous certificates), while longer sequences receive higher rates, reflecting their greater tolerance to noise.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical framework for variable-rate deletion smoothing where the deletion probability ψ(x) can depend arbitrarily on the input. The key analytical result (Lemma 3) bounds the smoothed class probability at a neighboring input using the longest common subsequence (LCS) between the two inputs, reducing the bound computation to a bounded knapsack problem over deletion indicator sets. For the specialized case of length-dependent deletion, the certification algorithm (Algorithm 1) efficiently enumerates only the number of edits of each type (deletions, insertions, substitutions) rather than all neighboring sequences, enabling polynomial-time certification. AdaptDel parameterizes the deletion rate as ψ(x) = max(p_lb, p(1 − k/|x|)), smoothly increasing with input length, while AdaptDel+ further optimizes per-bin expected post-deletion lengths via golden section search on the training set, achieving up to 30 orders of magnitude improvement in median certified region cardinality over fixed-rate baselines.

## Results

Across four NLP datasets (Yelp, SpamAssassin, IMDB, LUN), AdaptDel and AdaptDel+ improve the mean certified radius by an average of over 50% and the median cardinality of the certified region by up to 30 orders of magnitude compared to CERT-ED and RanMASK, while maintaining clean accuracy within 0–2% of the baselines. AdaptDel+ achieves the highest certified accuracy for larger certified regions, with particularly pronounced gains on SpamAssassin and LUN where input length variation is more uniform. The improvements scale with input size: for the largest length quartile, both methods achieve substantial gains in certified accuracy, confirming that adaptive deletion rates are most beneficial for longer sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: CERT-ED (fixed-rate deletion, Huang et al. 2024) and RanMASK (randomized [MASK] with Hamming distance certificates, Zeng et al. 2023). AdaptDel and AdaptDel+ consistently outperform both baselines in certified accuracy at every log-cardinality threshold across all four datasets, with the gap widening for larger certified regions. Against CERT-ED, the advantage is most pronounced for longer sequences (fourth quartile), where the fixed deletion rate becomes suboptimal; for short sequences (first quartile), AdaptDel is configured to match CERT-ED's deletion rate, so gains are modest. Against RanMASK, AdaptDel methods dominate because they certify under the broader edit distance threat model (deletions, insertions, substitutions) rather than the more limited Hamming distance. AdaptDel+ further improves over AdaptDel by using per-bin empirical calibration of the expected post-deletion length via golden section search, yielding the strongest certified accuracy at high cardinalities, especially on SpamAssassin and LUN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the fixed-rate deletion smoothing mechanism of Huang et al. (RS-Del, CERT-ED) to variable-rate deletion, where the deletion probability adapts to input length. The theoretical analysis extends the randomized smoothing framework of Cohen et al. and the Clopper-Pearson certification approach of Lecuyer et al. to the discrete, variable-length setting, requiring a new bounded-knapsack formulation to handle the non-uniform deletion rates. It also draws conceptual inspiration from input-dependent Gaussian smoothing work (Sukenik et al.), but the discrete edit-distance setting demands fundamentally different analysis since the smoothing mechanism changes input dimensionality and cannot be expressed as additive noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for certified robustness in NLP, where input lengths vary widely and fixed deletion rates are demonstrably suboptimal. The 30-orders-of-magnitude improvement in median certified region cardinality, while partly driven by near-zero certificates under fixed rates for short sequences, confirms that adaptive rates unlock substantially larger certified regions. Key limitations remain: edit distance is a syntactic surrogate for semantic attacks, and the paper only explores length-dependent (not fully input-dependent) deletion rates, leaving open whether richer input-dependent mechanisms could yield further gains. The framework's generality (arbitrary ψ via exhaustive search in Algorithm 2) provides a foundation for future work on more sophisticated adaptation strategies.
