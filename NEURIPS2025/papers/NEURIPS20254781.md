## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks designed for SAM (image segmentation) fail to transfer to SAM2 (video segmentation), with DarkSAM reducing SAM's mIoU by 98.25% but only 22.26% for SAM2. The paper identifies two architectural causes: directional guidance from the persistent first-frame prompt and semantic entanglement across consecutive frames via the memory bank. No cross-prompt universal adversarial attack for SAM2 had previously been proposed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of video frames (resized to 3×1024×1024) and produces a single universal adversarial perturbation δ (l_p norm bounded by ε=10/255 for UAP, ε=8/255 for the sample-wise variant) that generalizes across videos, frames, and prompts. During optimization, each frame is divided into m=256 regions, each randomly assigned a point or box prompt, so the perturbation is not tied to any specific prompt. The perturbation is applied to the image encoder's input, and the resulting adversarial frames cause SAM2 to output near-zero masks (objects 'vanish into thin air').

## Method

UAP-SAM2 optimizes a single UAP via a dual semantic deviation objective combining three losses: (1) a semantic confusion attack using BCE loss to push foreground logits toward the background class while reinforcing background pixels, with a target-scanning strategy (256 random prompts per frame) to decouple optimization from any specific prompt; (2) a feature shift attack using contrastive learning to maximize the cosine distance between the adversarial frame's image-encoder features and a prototype built from ρ random augmentations of the benign frame, treating other-video frames as positive pairs; and (3) a memory misalignment attack that maximizes the feature discrepancy between consecutive adversarial frames, progressively disrupting SAM2's memory bank and inducing an 'avalanche effect' where inter-frame semantic inconsistency amplifies across the sequence. The key design choice is to target the image encoder's output features rather than prompt-dependent masks, which is what enables cross-prompt transferability.

## Results

On video segmentation with point prompts, UAP-SAM2 reduces SAM2's mIoU to 37.03% (YouTube), 42.47% (DAVIS), and 33.67% (MOSE), compared to a benign mIoU above 76%. The best baseline (UAPGD) only achieves 42.59%, 53.60%, and 50.80% mIoU on the same datasets, showing UAP-SAM2 degrades segmentation by an additional 5.6–17.1 percentage points. On image segmentation (point prompts), UAP-SAM2 reaches 27.54%, 48.45%, and 50.13% mIoU, again outperforming all baselines. The sample-wise variant UAP-SAM2* further reduces DAVIS mIoU by over 54.77% relative to benign performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAP-SAM2 outperforms all seven baselines on video segmentation across all three datasets under point prompts, with the largest margins over DarkSAM (67.51→37.03 on YouTube, a 30.48-point gap) and AttackSAM (64.35→37.03). Among the SAM-specific attacks, DarkSAM and AttackSAM are the weakest against SAM2, confirming the paper's observation that existing SAM attacks transfer poorly to SAM2 due to the memory bank and persistent prompt. UAPGD is the strongest baseline on video (42.59/53.60/50.80), yet UAP-SAM2 still leads by 5.6–17.1 points. On image segmentation, UAP-SAM2 also dominates, achieving 27.54% mIoU on YouTube* versus 50.05% (VOSPGD) and 54.42% (UAPGD) for the next-best methods. UAD, which is prompt-free by design, shows identical results under point and box prompts and is outperformed by UAP-SAM2 on all six datasets. The consistent advantage on video over image tasks validates the memory misalignment and feature shift components that exploit temporal semantic entanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack line on SAM (DarkSAM, Attack-SAM, UAD, S-RA) to its video-capable successor SAM2, which introduces a memory bank and persistent first-frame prompt that fundamentally change the attack surface. The key departure is the dual semantic deviation framework: prior SAM attacks operate purely in the spatial/image domain, whereas UAP-SAM2 must simultaneously distort intra-frame semantics and disrupt inter-frame temporal consistency to exploit the memory attention mechanism. The target-scanning strategy (dividing frames into m regions with random prompts) generalizes the cross-prompt transferability idea from DarkSAM's shadow-target approach to a more systematic prompt-agnostic optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that SAM2, a model increasingly deployed in safety-critical video applications (autonomous driving, medical imaging), is vulnerable to a single universal perturbation that works across videos, frames, and prompts. The avalanche effect—where perturbing consecutive frames progressively degrades segmentation through the memory bank—reveals a structural weakness in temporal memory architectures that may generalize beyond SAM2. However, the paper acknowledges that transferability to non-SAM segmentation frameworks remains unexplored, and the defense study shows only that two common defenses (pruning, input corruption) fail, leaving the broader defense landscape open.
