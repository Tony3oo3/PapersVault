## Problem

Existing LLM pruning methods operate within a fixed parameter space and only select which weights to remove, leaving the underlying importance distribution unchanged. This limits robustness, particularly under semi-structured sparsity constraints (e.g., 2:4) where pruning choices are structurally restricted and critical weights may be forced to be removed. No prior method reshapes the importance landscape itself before pruning to make the model inherently more amenable to sparsification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense pretrained LLM (LLaMA3, Qwen2.5, or Mistral architectures) and a small calibration set (128 sequences of 2048 tokens from C4) to compute the Hessian H = XX^⊤. It produces a pruned sparse model where learned orthogonal matrices of shape (hidden_size, hidden_size) per Transformer layer have been merged into the weight matrices, adding roughly 6.7% parameter overhead for LLaMA3-8B. The orthogonal matrices are trained independently before any pruning is applied, making the framework plug-and-play with any existing pruning pipeline (Magnitude, Wanda, SparseGPT) at any sparsity level.

## Method

DenoiseRotator inserts two pairs of learnable orthogonal matrices into each Transformer layer: a layer-level pair R1 (applied before RMSNorm and after residual addition) and an attention-level pair R2 (applied to Value and Output projections). These rotations redistribute parameter importance without altering model output, leveraging the computational invariance property of Transformers. The training objective minimizes the information entropy of normalized importance scores (treated as a discrete probability distribution) across all linear layers within each Transformer layer, which provably concentrates importance onto fewer parameters while preserving total importance due to the norm-preserving property of orthogonal matrices. Orthogonality is maintained during gradient-based optimization via QR decomposition reparameterization, where an unconstrained matrix A is decomposed and only the orthogonal factor Q is used in the forward pass. After training, the rotations are merged into the weight matrices and standard pruning is applied to the transformed weights, decoupling importance concentration from the pruning step itself.

## Results

Under 2:4 semi-structured sparsity with SparseGPT on LLaMA3-70B, DenoiseRotator reduces WikiText-2 perplexity from 10.97 to 6.25 (dense: 2.86), narrowing the perplexity gap to the dense model by 58% (from 8.11 to 3.39 points). Under 50% unstructured sparsity with SparseGPT on the same model, perplexity drops from 5.99 to 4.61. On zero-shot accuracy (average of PIQA, WinoGrande, HellaSwag, ARC-e, ARC-c), LLaMA3-70B with SparseGPT at 2:4 improves from 69.16% to 76.37% (dense: 80.05%), and Qwen2.5-72B improves from 75.43% to 77.16% (dense: 78.69%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DenoiseRotator is evaluated as a plug-in on top of three pruning baselines—Magnitude, Wanda, and SparseGPT—across seven model variants and two sparsity patterns. Against Magnitude, the gains are the most dramatic: on LLaMA3-8B at 50% unstructured sparsity, perplexity drops from 30.39 to 14.43, and on Qwen2.5-72B at 2:4 it drops from 287.70 to 8.81, effectively rescuing an otherwise unusable model. Against Wanda, improvements are consistent but more moderate: LLaMA3-70B at 2:4 improves from 9.39 to 6.60 perplexity, and Qwen2.5-72B from 6.69 to 6.16. Against SparseGPT, the headline result is LLaMA3-70B at 2:4, where perplexity falls from 10.97 to 6.25 (a 58% reduction in the gap to the dense model's 2.86). DenoiseRotator wins on every single model–sparsity–method combination in both perplexity and zero-shot accuracy, with the largest relative gains appearing under the more restrictive 2:4 semi-structured constraint and with the weakest baseline (Magnitude).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the computational invariance principle from SliceGPT [2]—which showed that orthogonal transformations preserve Transformer outputs—into the pruning domain, building on RotPruner [7] which first applied rotations for pruning but lacked theoretical justification. The key conceptual departure is reframing pruning from a pure selection problem (which weights to remove) to a two-stage process: first reshape the importance distribution via entropy minimization, then apply any existing pruning metric. The QR-decomposition reparameterization for maintaining orthogonality during optimization is borrowed from the quantization literature (SpinQuant [29], OSTQuant [22]), and the OBD/Taylor-approximation importance framework is inherited from Wanda [38] and SparseGPT [13].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—where learned orthogonal matrices are optimized independently and then merged before any pruning step—makes this approach immediately composable with the full ecosystem of post-training pruning methods, lowering adoption barriers. The 58% reduction in perplexity gap on LLaMA3-70B under 2:4 sparsity (8.1→3.4) demonstrates that importance redistribution can substantially close the gap to dense models, particularly under hardware-friendly semi-structured constraints where selection is most restricted. Open questions include whether the semi-structured sparsity pattern can be explicitly encoded into the entropy objective, whether the approach integrates into pretraining or continual learning, and whether the ~6.7% parameter overhead from the layer-level rotation matrices can be further reduced without sacrificing performance.
