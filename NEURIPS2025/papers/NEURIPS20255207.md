## Problem

In federated multi-armed bandit (FMAB) settings with heterogeneous rewards and fully distributed communication (no central server), existing algorithms either fail to achieve optimal regret bounds or ignore communication costs at the bit level. No prior work simultaneously addresses optimal individual and group regret, heterogeneous feedback, and efficient bit-level communication in a decentralized network. This paper also establishes the first regret lower bound for heterogeneous federated bandits, which had been missing from the literature.

## Input / Output

Each agent operates on a connected communication graph G=(N,E,A) with a doubly stochastic weight matrix W=I−βL, and pulls from K arms yielding ½-sub-Gaussian scalar rewards in [0,1] over horizon T. Communication is structured in two phases: Phase I broadcasts suboptimal-arm indices and epoch labels (O(log K) bits), while Phase II exchanges adaptively quantized differentials δ_{i,j}^r = μ̄_{i,j}^r − μ̄_{i,j}^{r−1} requiring only O(1) bits per message rather than the O(log T) bits of full estimates. The algorithm outputs per-agent arm selections, per-arm global mean estimates, a shared elimination set, and achieves total communication cost O(Σ√N·Δ_i^{-1}·√log T) bits.

## Method

EpoInc-SE extends successive elimination to a fully distributed setting via three key mechanisms. First, incremental epoch-based exploration partitions time into epochs of growing size a(r+1), so agents batch samples before communicating, reducing communication rounds to O(√log T). Second, adaptive difference communication transmits only the quantized change between consecutive global estimates (using ⌈1+log₂r⌉ bits for the quantized value), exploiting temporal smoothness to compress each message to O(1) bits. Third, an Epoch-based Consensus Estimation Subroutine (EBCES) combines neighbors' historical quantized estimates with the agent's local empirical reward via the Perron matrix W, with a time-varying weight σ_i(r)=2/(r+2) that balances new local data against accumulated consensus. A two-phase communication strategy separates high-frequency elimination broadcasts (Phase I) from low-frequency estimate exchanges (Phase II), and a confidence radius U_{i,j}^r incorporating quantization, consensus, and concentration errors drives arm elimination with a propagation delay of ⌈D/(a(r+1))⌉ epochs across the graph diameter.

## Results

EpoInc-SE achieves the optimal individual regret bound O(Σ_{i:Δ_i>0} N^{-1}Δ_i^{-1} log T) and group regret bound O(Σ_{i:Δ_i>0} Δ_i^{-1} log T) in the heterogeneous, fully distributed setting, matching the newly established lower bounds (Theorem 3). The total communication cost is O(Σ_{i:Δ_i>0} √N Δ_i^{-1} √log T) bits, which is the first O(√log T)-order bit-level bound for heterogeneous federated bandits. In experiments (N=8, K=10, T=10^6, ring graph), EpoInc-SE's group and individual regrets are only slightly worse than Fed2-UCB (which uses a central server), while its bit-level communication overhead is the lowest among all five compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gossip_UCB and Dis_UCB (the two prior heterogeneous, serverless baselines), EpoInc-SE strictly improves both regret bounds (eliminating the N or N_min factors) and reduces communication from O(T log T) to O(√log T) bits. Against UCB-TCOM, which achieves the same optimal regret in the homogeneous setting with Õ(log T) bits, EpoInc-SE extends optimality to the heterogeneous setting while further reducing communication to O(√log T) bits. Against Fed2-UCB, which uses a central server and achieves the same regret bounds in the homogeneous setting, EpoInc-SE matches regret performance in experiments (slightly worse due to the absence of a central server) while reducing communication from O(T log T) to O(√log T) bits and removing the central-server requirement. Overall, EpoInc-SE is the first algorithm to simultaneously achieve optimal individual and group regret in the heterogeneous, fully distributed setting with sub-logarithmic bit-level communication.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated multi-armed bandit (FMAB) literature by introducing bit-level communication optimization into the heterogeneous, fully distributed setting. It builds on the epoch-based exploration and information quantization techniques from Boursier & Perchet (2019) and Wang et al. (2019), which were originally developed for homogeneous or centralized bandit structures, and adapts them to fully distributed graphs with heterogeneous rewards. The consensus estimation subroutine (EBCES) leverages the Perron-matrix framework from Olfati-Saber et al. (2007) for distributed averaging. The key departure from prior FMAB work (Gossip_UCB, Dis_UCB, Fed2-UCB, UCB-TCOM) is the two-phase communication design that separates high-frequency arm-elimination broadcasts from low-frequency differential estimate exchanges, reducing per-message cost from O(log T) to O(1) bits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to simultaneously achieve optimal regret bounds and O(1)-bit messages in heterogeneous federated bandit learning, and the first to establish a regret lower bound with explicit N-dependence for this setting, closing the gap between upper and lower bounds. The differential encoding technique—transmitting only quantized changes between consecutive estimates rather than full values—offers a practical recipe for bandwidth-constrained sensor networks and edge devices. Open questions remain: the communication-cost lower bound for heterogeneous settings is still unresolved (Remark 2), and the paper acknowledges that more complex network dynamics (time-varying topologies) and privacy-preserving mechanisms are left for future work.
