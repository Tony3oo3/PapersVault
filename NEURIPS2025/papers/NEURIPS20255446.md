## Problem

Nearly half the layers in modern Pre-LN LLMs contribute negligibly to learning, as evidenced by their resilience to pruning and near-identity Jacobians. Prior work documented this ineffectiveness but neither identified the root cause nor proposed a fix. This paper pinpoints Pre-Layer Normalization's uncontrolled output variance growth as the culprit and introduces a minimal remedy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255446/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text token sequences for autoregressive language modeling, producing next-token predictions and a scalar language modeling loss. The only modification to the forward pass is a per-layer scalar multiplication (1/√ℓ) applied to the LayerNorm output before the attention and FFN sub-layers, with no change to input format, sequence length constraints, or output structure. Because the scaling is depth-dependent and applied at every training step (not just initialization), it dynamically controls variance throughout training rather than merely adjusting initial weight magnitudes.

## Method

LayerNorm Scaling (LNS) modifies Pre-LN by multiplying the LayerNorm output at layer ℓ by 1/√ℓ, a single hyperparameter-free scalar that requires no additional learnable parameters. The theoretical analysis shows that in standard Pre-LN, output variance grows sub-exponentially (bounded between Θ(L) and Θ(exp(L))) with depth, causing the Jacobian of deep Transformer blocks to collapse toward the identity matrix and rendering those layers ineffective. LNS provably slows this variance growth to at most Θ(L^(2−ε)), which in turn keeps the gradient norm of the full network growing as ω(1) rather than saturating at a constant, so deeper layers retain meaningful transformations. Empirically, this one-line change consistently reduces perplexity across model sizes from 130M to 7B (e.g., 2.69→2.50 loss on OLMo-7B), improves downstream fine-tuning accuracy, and outperforms alternatives like DeepNorm, Mix-LN, LayerScale, and SkipInit without introducing training instability.

## Results

LayerNorm Scaling (LNS) consistently achieves the lowest perplexity across all tested model sizes (130M–1B), reducing perplexity by 0.97 over Pre-LN on LLaMA-130M (25.76 vs. 26.73) and by 1.31 on LLaMA-1B (15.71 vs. 17.02). On the 7B OLMo model trained for 20B tokens, LNS reduces final loss from 2.69 to 2.50. In supervised fine-tuning on Commonsense170K, LNS improves average accuracy by 1.80% over Mix-LN on LLaMA-250M and by 1.86% over the best baseline on LLaMA-1B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LNS outperforms all baselines across every model size where they remain stable. Post-LN [2] diverges entirely beyond 130M (perplexity >1300), making it non-competitive at scale. DeepNorm [47] and Mix-LN [26] both fail to converge at 350M and 1B parameters, with perplexities exceeding 1300, while LNS maintains stable training and achieves the lowest perplexity at every size. Against the strongest stable baseline, Pre-LN [3], LNS improves perplexity by 0.97 (130M), 1.57 (250M), 1.38 (350M), and 1.31 (1B). In the ablation against other scaling methods (Table 4), LNS beats Scaled Initialization [38] (25.76 vs. 26.04 on 130M), Depth-Scaled Initialization [57] (25.76 vs. 26.95), LayerScale [43] (25.76 vs. 27.93), and SkipInit [9] (25.76 vs. 27.41). On the OLMo-1B 20B-token benchmark, LNS (loss 2.85, PPL 17.28) also beats OLMo's own Scaled Initialization [57] (loss 2.96, PPL 19.30).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Pre-LN Transformer architecture (Baevski & Auli, ICLR 2019) and the growing body of empirical evidence that deep layers in LLMs are ineffective (Gromov et al., Yin et al., Men et al., Lad et al.). It departs from prior work by identifying the root cause—Pre-LN's exponential output variance growth causing deep-layer Jacobians to collapse toward identity—rather than merely documenting the phenomenon. The proposed LayerNorm Scaling is positioned as a simpler, hyperparameter-free alternative to Mix-LN (which introduces Post-LN instability), DeepNorm (which rescales residual connections), and learnable-parameter methods like LayerScale and SkipInit, all of which the paper shows to be inferior or unstable at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the very normalization mechanism adopted for training stability (Pre-LN) simultaneously renders roughly half of deep layers near-identity is a fundamental diagnostic for the field, with direct implications for how future LLMs are architected and trained. LNS's trivial implementation (a single 1/√ℓ factor) makes it immediately adoptable, and the consistent gains across 130M–7B models and multiple architectures (LLaMA, OLMo, Qwen2.5) suggest broad applicability. Open questions remain around whether the benefit extends to MoE architectures, domain-specific or long-context tasks, and what specific types of representational information (syntactic, semantic, factual) are recovered in deeper layers under LNS.
