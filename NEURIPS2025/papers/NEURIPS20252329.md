## Problem

Distributed LLM training with sharded optimizers (e.g., ZeRO-1) suffers from communication overhead that dominates computation time as worker count grows, leaving GPUs idle during gradient synchronization. Existing overlap methods like DPU (ZeRO-Offload) hide this latency but introduce one-step gradient staleness that degrades convergence, while local-update methods (SlowMo, CO2) require full optimizer states, defeating the memory savings of sharding. No prior approach simultaneously achieves communication-computation overlap, sharded-optimizer memory efficiency, and training dynamics matching standard DDP without extra hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

Each worker consumes a mini-batch of training samples (split internally into two halves of size N_i/2) and maintains sharded optimizer states in single precision alongside bfloat16 parameters and gradient buffers. The method supports time-varying per-worker batch sizes, allowing faster GPUs to accumulate additional mini-batches while slower workers catch up, which is the mechanism by which heterogeneous hardware is handled. The output is a fully updated parameter vector θ^(t+1) per iteration, with the trained model exhibiting perplexity and loss curves indistinguishable from standard AdamW DDP.

## Method

ACCO splits each mini-batch into two halves and runs two parallel streams (computation and communication) in a two-stage pipeline. In Stage 1, the second half of the mini-batch computes gradients g_i^(t) w.r.t. current parameters θ^(t) while the communication stream simultaneously estimates next-step parameters θ̃^(t+1) using gradients accumulated from the previous round. In Stage 2, the first half computes gradients g̃_i^(t+1) w.r.t. the estimated parameters θ̃^(t+1) while the communication stream finalizes θ^(t+1) using the full mini-batch (averaging both g_i^(t) and g̃_i^(t)). This accumulation mechanism compensates for the inherent one-step delay of overlapping execution without applying gradients to stale parameters, unlike DPU or Weight Prediction. A Lyapunov-based analysis with potential V(θ,θ̃) = f(θ)−f(θ*)+ηL(f(θ̃)−f(θ*))+L‖θ−θ̃‖² proves that ACCO recovers the standard O(1/T) convergence rate for both GD and SGD, matching the guarantees of vanilla distributed optimization.

## Results

ACCO achieves a 25% wall-clock speedup over DDP+ZeRO-1 on 125M-parameter pre-training (10h55m vs 14h41m on 32 A100s, 50B tokens) and an 87% speedup on 2.7B fine-tuning across 2 nodes (29min vs 3h46m). Perplexity on LAMBADA and OpenWebText is within 0.1–1.4 of the DDP baseline, confirming that convergence quality is preserved. On heterogeneous hardware with one 4× slower worker, ACCO avoids the 75% idle time that DDP incurs, yielding significantly smaller wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDP+ZeRO-1 (the primary baseline), ACCO matches training dynamics almost exactly—perplexity on LAMBADA is 47.1 vs 47.5 (1×8) and 45.5 vs 44.1 (4×8), and OpenWebText perplexity is 24.2 vs 24.3 (1×8) and 22.5 vs 21.7 (4×8)—while delivering 25–87% wall-clock speedups that grow with model size and inter-node communication cost. Against DPU (ZeRO-Offload's delayed parameter update), ACCO clearly wins on convergence: DPU with 40 warmup steps still shows noticeably worse loss than DDP on TinyStories, and even 500 warmup steps only partially recovers, whereas ACCO's curve is indistinguishable from DDP from step zero. Against Weight Prediction (WP), which applies a second optimizer step to predict next-step parameters, ACCO again matches DDP perfectly while WP exhibits degraded early-training loss (Fig. 6). In the heterogeneous hardware experiment (one GPU 4× slower), DDP idles 3 of 4 workers for 75% of the time, while ACCO lets fast workers accumulate gradients during the wait, producing a substantially faster overall training curve.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACCO sits at the intersection of three research threads: local-update methods (SlowMo, CO2, DiLoCo) that reduce communication frequency but require full optimizer states, communication-computation overlap techniques (DPU from ZeRO-Offload, Weight Prediction from pipeline parallelism), and sharded-optimizer frameworks (ZeRO-1/2/3). The key departure is that ACCO is the first method to achieve communication-computation overlap while remaining compatible with sharded optimizers, which prior overlap methods could not do because local updates necessitate materializing full optimizer states. Its novel two-stage mini-batch splitting mechanism distinguishes it from DPU (which accepts one-step staleness) and Weight Prediction (which applies a second optimizer step), providing a provable convergence guarantee matching standard SGD rather than relying on approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Communication overhead is a dominant and growing bottleneck in LLM training that is expected to persist with future hardware, making efficient overlap strategies practically critical. ACCO's compatibility with ZeRO-1 sharding and its lack of new hyperparameters or warmup phases make it directly deployable in existing training frameworks, and the 87% multi-node fine-tuning speedup demonstrates substantial real-world impact. However, the theoretical guarantees are limited to SGD (not Adam/AdamW, which is what LLM training actually uses), the heterogeneous hardware experiment is simulated rather than on truly heterogeneous hardware, and the split-mini-batch requirement means the method needs at least two samples per worker per step. Open questions include extending the convergence analysis to adaptive optimizers and validating the approach at truly large scale (10B+ parameters, hundreds of nodes).
