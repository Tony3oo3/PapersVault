## Problem

Off-policy RL for LLMs is practically necessary due to the sample inefficiency and engineering complexity of strictly on-policy training, yet existing off-policy methods often yield suboptimal performance or training instability. The paper identifies that the choice of the REINFORCE baseline V in an off-policy setting fundamentally changes the algorithm's behavior—unlike the on-policy case where V only affects variance—and asks how to set V to balance the influence of positive versus negative reward signals when training on data generated by a different (outdated) policy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts from a task distribution, generates G=8 full token sequences (up to 2048 tokens) from a behavior policy that is an outdated snapshot of the current model, and assigns binary rewards (+1 correct, −1 incorrect). The key tunable input is the baseline offset δV added to the per-prompt empirical mean reward, which controls the asymmetry between how strongly the model pushes up successful trajectories versus pushes down failed ones. The output is a fine-tuned LLM policy that retains diverse response distributions across prompts rather than collapsing to deterministic outputs.

## Method

AsymRE performs gradient ascent on the off-policy objective J(π) = E_{y~μ}[log π(y)(r(y)−V)], where μ is the behavior policy and V is a tunable baseline, without any importance-sampling correction. The core theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) shows that for tabular softmax policies, the limit policy's support shrinks monotonically as V increases, and a phase transition occurs at V = V^μ (the behavior policy's expected reward) where the support collapses to a singleton. Theorem 4.3 proves that when V < V^μ, repeated application of AsymRE in a policy-improvement scheme monotonically increases expected reward and converges to the optimal policy, while V ≥ V^μ causes premature convergence to a suboptimal deterministic policy. For LLMs, the paper introduces a context-corrected loss that subtracts the per-prompt empirical mean reward plus a small offset δV, making the critical threshold δV = 0 uniform across prompts, and empirically validates that δV ≈ −0.1 provides stable training and outperforms GRPO in the off-policy regime.

## Results

In the controlled bandit setting (100 arms), AsymRE exhibits a sharp phase transition at V^μ ≈ 0.54: below this threshold the policy maintains broad support and monotonically improves, while above it the support collapses to a singleton and the limit reward is suboptimal (~0.89 vs. optimal 0.999). In LLM experiments on MATH (12.5k problems) and NuminaMath (142k problems) with Llama 8B, Llama 3B, and Qwen 3B, setting the context-corrected baseline δV = −0.1 yields stable training, whereas δV = 0 causes a systematic collapse in all 7 independent runs. AsymRE with δV = −0.1 achieves faster convergence and higher final test accuracy than GRPO in the off-policy (delayed-update, N=250 steps) setting across all three model sizes and both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), a REINFORCE-style algorithm that incorporates both positive and negative examples with group-relative advantages. In the off-policy setting (behavior policy updated every N=250 gradient steps), AsymRE with δV = −0.1 consistently outperforms GRPO on both training speed and final test accuracy for Llama 8B and Qwen 3B on the MATH dataset (Figure 5, right), and the same pattern holds on the larger NuminaMath dataset (Figure 13). The advantage is attributed to AsymRE's asymmetric treatment of rewards: by keeping the baseline slightly below the empirical mean, the algorithm emphasizes positive examples while down-weighting negative ones, which is particularly beneficial when training on off-policy data where the behavior policy's failures are less informative. GRPO, which treats positive and negative samples symmetrically, is more susceptible to the off-policy distribution shift and converges more slowly to a lower final accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the REINFORCE policy gradient algorithm (Williams, 1992) to the off-policy setting by studying how the value baseline V, traditionally a variance-reduction device with no effect on expected dynamics in the on-policy case, fundamentally alters the limit policy when sampling is done from a behavior policy μ. It departs from the dominant off-policy correction strategies for LLMs—importance sampling (PPO, IMPALA) and KL-regularized consistency conditions (DPO, Soft Policy Optimization)—by proposing no correction at all, instead relying on a conservative baseline choice. The work is concurrent with Roux et al. (2025) on tapered off-policy REINFORCE and Zhu et al. (2025) on negative reinforcement, but provides the first systematic theoretical characterization of the baseline's role in off-policy REINFORCE, including a phase transition at V = V^μ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that adding a small negative correction (δV ≈ −0.1) to the advantage term in off-policy RL training of LLMs prevents the policy collapse observed when the baseline reaches or exceeds the behavior policy's expected reward—is simple, actionable, and directly applicable to current RLHF/RLVR pipelines. The theoretical phase transition result explains *why* off-policy training with aggressive negative-sample weighting is dangerous in a way that on-policy training is not, giving practitioners a principled criterion for baseline selection. Open questions include whether the analysis extends to importance-sampling-corrected objectives, KL-regularized losses, and non-binary reward structures, and whether the tabular softmax analysis faithfully captures the high-dimensional dynamics of LLM fine-tuning. The absence of released code and the reliance on binary rewards limit immediate reproducibility and generalizability.
