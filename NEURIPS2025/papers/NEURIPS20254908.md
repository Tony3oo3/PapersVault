## Problem

RL policies trained in simulation suffer significant performance degradation when deployed on real robots due to the sim-to-real gap. Existing System Identification (Sys-Id) approaches that calibrate simulator physical parameters to match reality encounter three critical failures: high-dimensional parameter identification collapse, low identification accuracy, and unstable convergence dynamics during the alignment process. This paper addresses these limitations by reframing parameter search as a diffusion-evolutionary optimization guided by adversarial fitness signals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes (s, a, s') state-action-transition tuples sampled from both a target domain (real-world or ground-truth simulator) and a source domain (parameterized simulator), along with a UDR-trained policy π₀ that generates rollouts in 200 parallel Isaac Gym environments. The discriminator is a 2-layer MLP (256 units, ReLU) with weight clipping for WGAN training. The output is a calibrated parameter vector θ* (covering friction, restitution, mass, motor stiffness/damping/armature) that minimizes the Wasserstein distance between source and target transition distributions, typically converged within 50 denoising steps in under 6 minutes on a single RTX 4060 Ti.

## Method

DEAL embeds Diffusion Evolution (DE) as the generator inside a WGAN, where a discriminator scores the similarity of (s,a,s') transitions between evolving simulators and the target environment. Instead of training a noise-prediction network, DE estimates the optimal parameter θ̂₀ by softmax-weighting the entire population by discriminator-derived fitness and computing a Gaussian-neighborhood-weighted average (Eq. 1), then derives the noise ε̂ algebraically from the diffusion schedule (Eq. 2). Two key mechanisms differentiate DEAL from prior adversarial Sys-Id: (1) automatic noise adaptation, where a fitness-dependent factor δ = λ·exp(−E[D]) modulates the denoising noise schedule σ̂ₜ to balance exploration (high δ when fitness is low) and exploitation (low δ when fitness is high); and (2) parameter normalization within the initial search range to prevent crossover errors from scale imbalances (e.g., stiffness vs. damping differing by orders of magnitude). Unlike EASI, which selects fixed elite parameters and loses population diversity, DEAL applies soft probabilistic weighting over all N individuals, preserving diversity and enabling stable convergence in high-dimensional spaces (up to 100+ parameters for AllegroHand).

## Results

DEAL achieves the lowest average search error across all five sim-to-sim tasks, with particularly strong performance in high-dimensional settings. At the standard search scale (ξ=10), DEAL attains 0.85% error on Cartpole, 0.81% on Humanoid, and 0.83% on AllegroHand, compared to 4.03%/2.94%/4.25% for EASI and 5.20%/4.67%/3.75% for BO. In the sim-to-real Cartpole task, DEAL reduces pole-angle error from 3.655×10⁻² (UDR) to 1.372×10⁻², and enables successful Go2 platform climbing where both UDR and Unitree's built-in RL policies fail entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEAL consistently outperforms both baselines across all tasks and search scales. Against EASI (CMA-ES + GAN), DEAL reduces search error by roughly 4–5× at ξ=10 (e.g., 0.85% vs 4.03% on Cartpole) and by over 3× at ξ=20 (2.63% vs 8.40%). Against BO (Bayesian optimization), the gap is even wider, with DEAL achieving 0.81% vs 4.67% on Humanoid at ξ=10 and 2.50% vs 10.06% at ξ=20. The advantage grows with search scale, indicating DEAL's superior adaptability when initial parameter priors are severely limited. In sim-to-sim transfer, policies retrained in DEAL-calibrated simulators approach Oracle performance, while EASI and BO-calibrated simulators cause negative optimization in high-dimensional tasks (Humanoid, AllegroHand), producing policies that perform worse than UDR. In sim-to-real, DEAL's calibrated simulator enables Go2 to successfully climb a platform, a task where both UDR and the manufacturer's built-in RL controller fail completely.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEAL builds on Diffusion Evolution (DE), which reinterprets diffusion model denoising as an evolutionary search process, and embeds it as the generator within a WGAN framework for adversarial system identification. It departs from EASI, which also combines evolutionary search with GAN but relies on fixed elite selection via manual sorting, by instead using soft probabilistic weighting over the entire population through fitness-driven denoising. The approach also contrasts with Bayesian Sys-Id methods (BayesSim, BayRn) that formalize parameter inference as posterior estimation but suffer from poor scalability in high-dimensional spaces, and with Domain Randomization methods that require expert-defined randomization ranges. Two practical innovations—automatic noise adaptation to balance exploration/exploitation and parameter normalization to counteract scale imbalances—distinguish DEAL from prior diffusion-based optimizers applied to continuous parameter search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEAL addresses a persistent bottleneck in deploying RL controllers to physical robots: the sim-to-real gap caused by unmodeled physical parameters. By achieving sub-1% parameter identification error in high-dimensional settings (21-DoF humanoid, 16-DoF hand) with only a few minutes of compute and limited real-world demonstrations, it offers a practical path to reducing costly hardware trials. The demonstrated robustness to low-quality trajectories (early-training checkpoints) is practically important since real-world deployment rarely provides perfectly executed demonstrations. A key open question is whether the binary GAN discriminator can provide adequate fitness signals for multimodal or highly non-stationary dynamics, a limitation the authors acknowledge but do not resolve.
