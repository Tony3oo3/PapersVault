## Problem

While large-scale datasets and CNN-based encoding models have advanced understanding of the ventral visual stream (object recognition), the dorsal stream (spatial/motion processing) lacks comparable resources. The largest existing dorsal stream dataset contained only 45 neurons from area MT, orders of magnitude smaller than ventral stream benchmarks, severely constraining the development of data-driven models for MT/MST. This paper addresses that gap by releasing STSBENCH, a dataset of 2,244 single-neuron recordings from the superior temporal sulcus collected while macaques viewed ~4,500 natural video clips.

## Input / Output

For encoding, the model consumes a 150×150 spatial crop of a 360×640, 5-frame (200 ms, 24 fps) natural video and produces a scalar z-scored firing rate prediction per neuron, with the readout factorized into spatial and feature weight matrices. For reconstruction, the model takes a vector of firing rates from all recorded neurons (2,244 for STSBENCH, or all V4 neurons for TVSD) and generates a 256×256 RGB image (first video frame) or a 5-frame grayscale video. The encoding crop is chosen to cover the receptive fields of most neurons, and firing rates are computed over a 40–240 ms post-stimulus window.

## Method

The encoding pipeline pairs a video feature extractor (hand-tuned 3D Gabor filters, pretrained 2D/3D ResNets, or end-to-end 3D CNNs with 1–7 layers and 32 channels) with a neuron-specific factorized linear readout, trained via MSE plus Laplacian and L2 regularization. The reconstruction pipeline adapts Stable Diffusion by replacing the CLIP text embedding in the U-Net's cross-attention layers with a neural activity vector, while the VQ-VAE and diffusion model are trained from scratch on the target dataset. A key finding is that a 5-layer 3D CNN trained end-to-end (R²=0.338) outperforms all hand-tuned and pretrained baselines, and that dorsal stream reconstructions capture low-spatial-frequency luminance contours and motion statistics while ventral stream reconstructions recover object identity, color, and fine texture—consistent with the canonical what/where dichotomy.

## Results

On STSBENCH, the best encoding model (3D CNN-5, trained end-to-end) achieves R² = 0.338 ± 0.005 on the held-out test set, outperforming all hand-tuned and pretrained baselines. For reconstruction, the conditional latent diffusion model achieves PSNR 14.161 and LPIPS 0.668 for dorsal stream (MT/MST), substantially exceeding the mean (PSNR 11.335, LPIPS 0.873) and shuffled (PSNR 9.848, LPIPS 0.751) null models. Motion direction is decoded from neural activity with a cosine similarity of 0.923 (vs. 0.051 for the shuffled control), confirming that STSBENCH encodes motion information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks encoding models against two key baselines from prior dorsal stream work. The hand-tuned 3D Gabor filter bank model of Nishimoto & Gallant (2011) achieves a best R² of 0.266 (Simple cell, 224 px input), while the 3D ResNet pretrained on self-motion estimation from Mineault et al. (2021) reaches a best R² of 0.289 (Layer 2, 112 px input). The end-to-end 3D CNN-5 model (R² = 0.338) outperforms both baselines by 0.072 and 0.049 respectively, indicating that neither hand-tuned Gabor features nor self-motion-optimized features fully capture MT/MST responses. Notably, the 3D ResNet-Kinetics pretrained on action recognition performs best at its earliest layers (R² = 0.303 at Layer 2), with performance degrading sharply at deeper layers (R² = 0.147 at Layer 4), suggesting MT/MST neurons encode lower-level visual features rather than action-classification features. For reconstruction, the diffusion model clearly outperforms both the mean and shuffled null models on LPIPS for both dorsal and ventral streams, confirming that neural activity provides meaningful conditioning signal for image generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ventral stream benchmarking paradigm (TVSD, BrainScore, MacaqueITBench) to the dorsal stream, where prior datasets contained only ~45 neurons (Nishimoto & Gallant, 2011). For encoding, it builds on the 3D Gabor filter model of Nishimoto & Gallant and the self-motion estimation model of Mineault et al. (2021), while for reconstruction it repurposes the Stable Diffusion architecture (Rombach et al., 2021) by replacing text embeddings with neural activity vectors. The key conceptual departure is leveraging Neuropixels 1.0 NHP probes to achieve a ~50-fold increase in dorsal stream neuron count, enabling the first large-scale benchmarking of MT/MST encoding models and the first single-neuron-level reconstruction from the dorsal stream.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STSBENCH fills a critical gap: the dorsal stream has been underrepresented in large-scale neural datasets relative to the ventral stream, constraining model development. The finding that end-to-end 3D CNNs outperform both hand-tuned Gabor and self-motion baselines suggests current mechanistic theories of MT/MST are incomplete, and the learned filters reveal chromatic and 'on-off' properties absent from textbook models. Open questions include what the deeper 5-layer CNN features represent, whether interpretability methods can extract a mechanistic account, and whether the reconstruction approach generalizes to other dorsal stream areas. Limitations include only two monkeys, potential contamination from area 7a or white matter, and Kilosort split/merge errors.
