## Problem

Standard stochastic initializations (Glorot, He) are assumed to require randomness for effective training, yet they inherently produce a high proportion of skewed neurons—neurons whose activation is disproportionately positive or negative—undermining representational capacity and slowing convergence. No deterministic initialization had previously demonstrated the ability to surpass random schemes in both final accuracy and convergence speed across diverse architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252285/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only the layer dimensions (m output neurons × n input features) as input, requiring no data, batch, or forward pass—unlike LSUV which needs a data batch. The output is a deterministic weight matrix whose entries follow an arcsine distribution (concentrated near ±a rather than near zero) with the exact structural property that every row sums to zero. The amplitude a is analytically calibrated so the matrix variance matches the Glorot target 2/(m+n), making it a direct drop-in replacement for any existing initialization call.

## Method

Each weight is set as W[i,j] = a·sin(2πi·j/n + 2πi/m), where the frequency k_i = 2πi gives row i exactly i full oscillations over the interval [0,1], and the phase offset φ_i = 2πi/m prevents identical first values across rows. This construction guarantees exact row cancellation (Theorem 3): every neuron's incoming weights sum to zero, so the preactivation is perfectly centered from the first forward pass. The paper proves (Theorems 1–2) that any zero-mean random initialization asymptotically produces skewed neurons with probability one, while the sinusoidal structure eliminates this by enforcing S = 0 for every neuron. Distinct frequencies and phases across rows evoke Fourier-basis orthogonality, yielding an Overfitting-Underfitting Indicator of 0.98 versus ~0.56–0.59 for stochastic baselines. Empirically, the method yields an average 4.9% accuracy gain and 20.9% AUC improvement over default initialization across ResNet-50, MobileNetV3, EfficientNetV2, ViT-B16, and BERT-mini.

## Results

Across five model-dataset configurations (ResNet-50/CIFAR-100, MobileNetV3/Tiny-ImageNet, EfficientNetV2/Tiny-ImageNet, ViT-B16/ImageNet-1k, BERT-mini/WikiText), Sinusoidal initialization yields an average final validation accuracy improvement of 4.9% over the default (He) initialization, 1.7% over Orthogonal, and 3.4% over LSUV. In terms of convergence speed (measured by AUC of the validation accuracy curve), Sinusoidal achieves an average improvement of 20.9% over default, 5.7% over Orthogonal, and 18.0% over LSUV. The method also reduces the proportion of skewed neurons from ~83% (α=0.1) under stochastic schemes to just 0.2%, and achieves an Overfitting-Underfitting Indicator (OUI) of 0.98 versus 0.56–0.59 for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sinusoidal initialization outperforms the default He initialization across nearly all model-dataset-optimizer combinations, with the largest gains in convergence speed (20.9% AUC improvement on average). Against Orthogonal initialization, Sinusoidal wins on accuracy and AUC in most settings, with the sole exception being MobileNetV3 on Tiny-ImageNet where Orthogonal slightly edges out Sinusoidal (attributed to an interaction with the HardSwish activation; replacing it with ReLU restores Sinusoidal's advantage). Compared to LSUV, Sinusoidal achieves higher final accuracy (+3.4% average) and faster convergence (+18.0% AUC), while also being fully deterministic and data-independent, unlike LSUV which requires a data batch. Against the two prior deterministic methods (Zhao et al.'s Zero initialization and Blumenfeld et al.'s near-all-zero scheme), Sinusoidal clearly outperforms both on ResNet-50/CIFAR-100, achieving faster convergence and higher final accuracy. In the activation-balance analysis, Sinusoidal reduces skewed neurons to 0.2% versus 49–84% for all stochastic baselines, and achieves an OUI of 0.98 versus 0.56–0.59, indicating substantially greater functional independence among neurons at initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the variance-preservation framework of Glorot & Bengio (2010) and He et al. (2015b), adopting their variance targets (2/(m+n)) to calibrate the sinusoidal amplitude, but departs fundamentally by replacing stochastic sampling with a deterministic sinusoidal construction. It positions itself against earlier deterministic attempts (Blumenfeld et al. 2020, Zhao et al. 2022), which the authors note merely match random initialization performance without improving convergence or providing theoretical insight into why randomness is harmful. The key conceptual departure is the formalization of 'skewed neurons' and the proof (Theorem 2) that any zero-mean i.i.d. random initialization asymptotically produces them, reframing randomness from a neutral or beneficial property to an active source of inefficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By proving that randomness in initialization is not merely unnecessary but actively detrimental (creating ~82% skewed neurons under standard schemes), this work challenges a foundational assumption in deep learning and offers a drop-in replacement that is simpler, reproducible, and faster. The 20.9% convergence speedup has direct practical implications for training cost and carbon footprint at scale. Open questions include why MobileNetV3 with HardSwish deviates from the general trend, whether the row-cancellation guarantee extends cleanly to all architectures (the theory is strongest for the first layer), and how the broader space of structured deterministic initializations might be explored beyond the sinusoidal family.
