## Problem

State space model (SSM) layers in deep networks achieve strong long-sequence performance but are poorly compressible after standard training, because the optimizer distributes Hankel singular values without encouraging fast decay. Without a mechanism to shape the singular value spectrum during training, compressing the SSM order via model reduction causes severe accuracy loss, limiting practical deployment of these architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes flattened image sequences (32×32 grayscale CIFAR-10 as 1,024-length vectors, 28×28 MNIST as 784-length vectors, PATH/PATH-X pathfinder images as 1,024/16,384-length vectors) and one-hot encoded character sequences (IMDB reviews, max length 4,096 over 129 symbols). Each sequence passes through a stack of SSM layers (4–6 layers depending on task) with task-specific state dimensions (n=128–384) and input/output dimensions (m=p=128–512), and the final output is a class label obtained by mean-pooling the last SSM layer's output along the sequence dimension followed by softmax.

## Method

The paper parametrizes each SSM layer's system matrix A as a block-diagonal matrix of 2×2 scaled rotation matrices, which guarantees stability (via tanh-thresholded scaling factors) and enables an O(n²) algorithm for computing the controllability and observability Gramians by decomposing the Lyapunov equations into independent 2×2 blocks. A nuclear norm regularizer—the sum of all Hankel singular values across all layers—is added to the training loss, and the paper proves this sum is differentiable even though individual singular values are not. For efficient forward passes, an associative scan operation is defined over the rotation parameters (ρ, α) that avoids explicit block products, matching the O(log n_s · n) scaling of diagonal-matrix SSMs. After training, compressed models are obtained via balanced truncation with layer-adaptive ranks selected by a bisection algorithm that preserves a fixed fraction of Hankel energy in each layer, followed by eigenvalue diagonalization to restore the structure needed for fast inference.

## Results

On the Long Range Arena benchmarks, HSVR-regularized SSMs maintain near-full accuracy at 80% truncation: 81.4% on sCIFAR (grayscale), 98.9% on sMNIST, and 87.0% on IMDB, compared to 21.1%, 11.1%, and 51.3% respectively for the same architecture trained without regularization. The headline claim of up to 10× compressibility is most visible on sMNIST at 80% truncation, where HSVR retains 98.9% accuracy while the unregularized model drops to 11.1%. Even at 90% truncation, HSVR holds 86.4% on IMDB and 87.0% on sMNIST, whereas all baselines collapse to near-chance. On the harder PATH-X task (sequence length 16,384), HSVR achieves 54.0% at 80% truncation versus ~50% (chance) for all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares HSVR against three pruning strategies from [26] (LAST, global, uniform) and against its own unregularized training. At 80% truncation, HSVR dominates all baselines by large margins on sCIFAR (81.4% vs. 17.4% for LAST, 11.1% for global, 19.2% for uniform) and sMNIST (98.9% vs. 62.4% for LAST, 52.5% for global, 44.4% for uniform). On IMDB, HSVR leads by 6.7 points over the best baseline (global, 83.8%) at 80% truncation and by 22.6 points at 90% truncation (86.4% vs. 63.8%). On PATH and PATH-X, where all methods struggle, HSVR shows a modest but consistent edge: 50.5% vs. 50.2% (global) on PATH and 54.0% vs. 50.5% (LAST) on PATH-X at 80% truncation. The unregularized variant of the same architecture collapses dramatically under compression (e.g., 11.1% on sMNIST at 80%), confirming that the HSV regularizer—not the architecture itself—is responsible for the compressibility gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent threads in SSM research: Hankel singular value (HSV) regularization for deep state-space models (Forgione et al., IEEE CDC 2024) and layer-adaptive state pruning (Gwak et al., NeurIPS 2024). It departs from both by introducing a block-diagonal rotation-matrix parametrization of the system matrix A, which enables an O(n²) algorithm for computing the controllability and observability Gramians (versus O(n³) for the general Bartels-Stewart solver), making HSV regularization practical during training. The base architecture follows S5 (Smith et al., ICLR 2023), but replaces its diagonal A with 2×2 rotation blocks, and the paper proves the sum of HSVs is differentiable even though individual HSVs are not, enabling a nuclear-norm regularizer on the Hankel operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As SSM-based architectures (Mamba, H3, etc.) become the go-to for long-sequence modeling, post-training compression is critical for deployment, yet standard SSMs trained without regularization are poorly compressible. The demonstrated 10× compressibility gap (e.g., ~99% vs. ~10% accuracy at 80% truncation on sMNIST) shows that training-time regularization is a fundamentally different and more effective strategy than post-hoc pruning. Key limitations include: the method requires training from scratch with the regularizer (inapplicable to pre-trained checkpoints), it is restricted to linear time-invariant systems (not the time-varying/selective SSMs in Mamba), and compression is linear (balanced truncation), leaving nonlinear model reduction as open work.
