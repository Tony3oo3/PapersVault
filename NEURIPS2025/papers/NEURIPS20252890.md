## Problem

Existing free-trajectory 3DGS methods degrade severely in high-speed (low-frame-rate) scenarios because sparse observations under-constrain the scene and large inter-frame displacements break continuity assumptions relied upon by pose and geometry priors. Event cameras record pixel-wise brightness changes asynchronously at high temporal resolution, but no prior work has integrated them into free-trajectory reconstruction where camera poses are unknown and must be jointly optimized with the scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252890/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video of free-trajectory frames (no precomputed poses) and a synchronized event stream, dividing the inter-frame interval into N subintervals (e.g., 1/6 s for Tanks and Temples, 1/25 s for the DAVIS346-based RealEv-DAVIS at 346×260). It jointly outputs a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (positions, opacities, scales, rotations, spherical-harmonic colors) and the full camera trajectory as quaternion-parameterized poses. Event-based losses operate in the grayscale domain while photometric losses operate in RGB, and the method is evaluated at frame rates from 1 to 6 FPS to stress-test high-speed robustness.

## Method

EF-3DGS bridges differential event data and absolute-brightness 3DGS rendering through three coupled components. First, an Event Generation Model (EGM) loss reconstructs latent intensity images at intermediate timestamps by exponentiating accumulated event contrasts onto the most recent frame, extending 3DGS supervision to the continuous event stream. Second, a unified CMax/LEGM module derives optical flow from rendered depth and relative camera motion, warps prior event frames to the current timestamp to form an Image of Piece-Wise Warped Events (IPWE), and simultaneously (a) maximizes IPWE variance as a pose-calibration signal and (b) enforces a gradient-domain constraint equating C·IPWE to the log-intensity difference between adjacent rendered frames. Third, a Photometric Bundle Adjustment term enforces cross-view RGB consistency, and a two-stage Fixed-GS strategy first optimizes all Gaussian parameters under event losses (structure + brightness) then freezes geometry and refines only spherical-harmonic coefficients on RGB frames to recover accurate color.

## Results

On the Tanks and Temples benchmark, EF-3DGS achieves 26.66 PSNR at 6 FPS and 23.96 PSNR at 1 FPS, outperforming the best frame-based pose-free method CF-3DGS by 0.61 dB and 3.43 dB respectively. For pose estimation, EF-3DGS reduces Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) by up to 58% compared to CF-3DGS at 1 FPS (0.3753 vs. 0.8972). On the real-world RealEv-DAVIS dataset, EF-3DGS achieves 21.12 PSNR in FAST (high-speed) scenarios, surpassing the best baseline (Event-3DGS at 20.04) by 1.08 dB while simultaneously performing pose estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EF-3DGS consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every frame rate tested. Against the strongest frame-based pose-free baseline CF-3DGS, the advantage grows from 0.61 dB PSNR at 6 FPS to 3.43 dB at 1 FPS, confirming that event data becomes increasingly critical as inter-frame displacement grows. Compared to the best event-based method requiring precomputed poses (Event-3DGS), EF-3DGS gains 1.55 dB at 1 FPS while also jointly optimizing camera poses—a capability Event-3DGS lacks. Against the closest pose-free event baseline EvCF-3DGS (which uses Time Lens for frame interpolation before CF-3DGS), EF-3DGS leads by 2.23 dB at 1 FPS, demonstrating that direct event integration outperforms event-to-frame conversion. For pose estimation, EF-3DGS achieves 58% lower ATE than CF-3DGS at 1 FPS (0.3753 vs. 0.8972) and 63% lower than LocalRF (1.0006). On the real-world RealEv-DAVIS dataset, EF-3DGS surpasses Event-3DGS by 1.08 dB PSNR in FAST scenarios while simultaneously producing accurate trajectories (RPE_t of 1.80 vs. unavailable for Event-3DGS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: free-trajectory 3DGS reconstruction (CF-3DGS, LocalRF, Nope-NeRF) and event-based novel view synthesis (E-NeRF, Event-3DGS, EvDeblurNeRF). It builds directly on CF-3DGS's progressive pose-and-scene optimization pipeline and LocalRF's dynamic scene allocation, while importing the CMax and LEGM imaging models from the event-camera literature to derive new loss terms. The key departure is that all prior event-based reconstruction methods require precomputed poses, and all prior free-trajectory methods rely solely on frames; this is the first work to jointly optimize poses and scene using both modalities, bridging the gap through EGM-driven continuous supervision, CMax/LEGM gradient-domain constraints, and a PBA + Fixed-GS color-recovery strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: high-speed or low-frame-rate free-trajectory capture (autonomous driving, FPV exploration) where frame-based methods degrade severely due to sparse observations and unreliable motion priors. The introduction of RealEv-DAVIS, the first free-trajectory event+frame dataset, fills a notable resource gap. Limitations include the requirement for synchronized event and frame streams, sensitivity to several empirically tuned hyperparameters (λ_cm, λ_grad, λ_PBA, subinterval N, warping range r), and the fact that the motion-blur extension (EDI) is relegated to the appendix without full ablation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether self-adaptive parameter scheduling (mentioned in the conclusion) can reduce the tuning burden, and how the method scales to very long sequences beyond the 50-frame local window.
