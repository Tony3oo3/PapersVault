## Problem

Selective SSMs (the core of Mamba) are nonlinear and input-conditioned, making the control-theoretic stability tools used for LTI SSM generalization bounds inapplicable. Despite their empirical success, no covering-number-based generalization bound existed for selective SSMs, leaving open whether the spectral abscissa of the continuous-time state matrix governs length-dependent generalization and whether length-independent guarantees are achievable.

## Input / Output

The model consumes fixed-length sequences—binary symbols (Majority), tokenized movie reviews (IMDb), or nested bracketed expressions (ListOps)—embedded into d-dimensional vectors, and outputs a scalar classification score z = w^T y[T] computed from the final time step of the SSM output. The theoretical analysis treats the generalization gap (empirical training loss minus expected test loss) as the quantity of interest, studying how it scales with sequence length T and the spectral abscissa s_A. Sequences are padded or truncated to a fixed T, with experiments sweeping T from 100 to 2000 tokens.

## Method

The key insight is unrolling the selective SSM's recursive state update into a closed-form sum that reveals an embedded linear-attention structure, where W_B and W_C play the roles of key and query projections. This enables a two-tiered covering number construction: A_c is covered via matrix-norm techniques (as in RNN analysis), while W_B, W_C, q, and w are covered as data-dependent linear function classes (as in Transformer attention analysis). The spectral abscissa s_A enters through Gelfand's formula to bound the growth of ‖A^t‖₂, determining whether the resulting Rademacher complexity—and hence the generalization bound—is length-independent (s_A < 0) or grows exponentially in T (s_A > 0). The final bound is obtained via Dudley's integral, and a corollary for linear attention (A_c = 0, Δ[t] = 1) explicitly demonstrates the SSM-to-attention reduction.

## Results

The paper derives a covering-number-based generalization bound for selective SSMs (Theorem 3.3) showing that when the spectral abscissa s_A < 0, the bound is length-independent (scaling as d^{1/2} in hidden dimension), while for s_A > 0 it grows exponentially as T·ρ_A^T. A lower bound (Theorem 4.1) confirms that no sub-exponential Rademacher complexity bound is possible when s_A > 0. Empirically, initializing models with s_A = 0.1 shows that successful training consistently drives s_A toward zero, and with s_A = 0 initialization, the generalization gap (train-test loss difference) remains stable across sequence lengths from 100 to 2000 tokens on IMDb, 100 to 1000 on ListOps, and up to 500 on Majority.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are between the derived bound for selective SSMs and existing bounds for other architectures rather than empirical accuracy comparisons. The selective SSM bound with s_A < 0 matches the length-independence of softmax attention [27] and stable RNNs [32], but at the cost of a d^{1/2} dependence (vs. d for RNNs) and a B_u^4 scaling (vs. B_u for RNNs, B_u^3 for attention). Compared to LTI SSMs [5, 35], the selective SSM bound is more practical: it applies directly to the discrete-time model used in Mamba without requiring the input to satisfy Σ‖u[k]‖² < ∞ (as in [5]) or Hölder continuity (as in [35]). The linear attention corollary (Proposition 3.4) shows a T-linear dependence, which is strictly worse than the length-independent selective SSM bound under s_A < 0, confirming that the state matrix dynamics provide an implicit normalization absent in linear attention. The paper does not report head-to-head accuracy comparisons against other sequence models; its empirical experiments validate the theoretical predictions about stability and length-independence rather than benchmarking against competing architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of analysis: the control-theoretic generalization bounds for LTI SSMs (Rácz et al., Liu and Li) and the covering-number-based Rademacher complexity framework developed for Transformers (Bartlett et al., Trauger and Tewari, Edelman et al.). The key technical move is recognizing the linear-attention structure hidden in the unrolled selective SSM (building on the SSM-attention duality of Dao and Gu), which allows the input-dependent B and C projections to be treated as query-key-value function classes and covered using techniques originally designed for self-attention. This departs from prior LTI SSM work that relies on impulse-response and H₂-norm arguments unavailable for nonlinear, input-conditioned systems, and instead constructs a two-tiered covering argument combining RNN-style matrix covers for A_c with attention-style function-class covers for W_B, W_C, q, and w.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generalization bound for selective SSMs (the core of Mamba/Mamba-2), filling a notable gap given the architecture's empirical dominance. The clean separation—length-independent bound when s_A < 0, provably exponential growth when s_A > 0 (with a matching lower bound on Rademacher complexity)—gives practitioners a concrete stability criterion for initialization and training. However, the analysis is limited to the Mamba-style ZOH/Euler discretization, assumes i.i.d. in-distribution data, and the empirical validation uses a single SSM block on relatively small-scale tasks; extending to deeper stacks, out-of-distribution length generalization, and tighter bounds (the upper and lower bounds differ by O(T)) remain open.
