key_contribution: A hypergraph neural network-based multi-view clustering framework (HOPER) that captures high-order sample correlations through view-specific k-NN hypergraphs, employs a dual contrastive learning mechanism (inter-view alignment plus intra-hyperedge discrimination) to address false negatives and HGNN over-smoothing, and incorporates hyper-Laplacian manifold regularization to preserve local geometric structure, achieving state-of-the-art clustering on five of six benchmark datasets with the largest gains on BBCSport (95.04% ACC vs. 80.70% for the best baseline) and COIL-20 (82.85% ACC vs. 74.03%).
limitations: The paper explicitly acknowledges that hypergraph-based modeling introduces higher computational complexity compared to traditional pairwise graph approaches due to more complex incidence and Laplacian matrix operations. ## Problem

Existing deep multi-view clustering methods predominantly model pairwise sample relationships, overlooking higher-order structural dependencies that are critical for effective clustering. Additionally, contrastive learning-based approaches suffer from false negative misidentification across views and lack explicit mechanisms to preserve high-order local geometric structures in the latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251826/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes M raw feature matrices of shape N×D_v (one per view) and produces k-means cluster assignments over N samples. Internally, each view's features are first compressed by a view-specific autoencoder into latent embeddings, from which a k-NN hypergraph is constructed; the final unified representation is the horizontal concatenation of per-view HGNN node embeddings (N × Σd_v), which is then fed to k-means. The hypergraph structure is fixed after the autoencoder pretraining stage and remains static during the joint fine-tuning phase.

## Method

HOPER operates in two stages: (1) view-specific autoencoders are pretrained to denoise raw features into compact latent embeddings, and (2) a shared-weight two-layer hypergraph neural network (HGNN) processes each view's k-NN hypergraph to learn node representations that capture high-order correlations. A dual contrastive learning mechanism then regularizes these representations: an inter-view InfoNCE loss aligns same-instance embeddings across views, while an intra-hyperedge contrastive loss treats same-hyperedge neighbors as negatives to counteract HGNN over-smoothing and preserve sample-level discriminability. A hyper-Laplacian manifold regularization term (tr(P L_h P^T)) is added to enforce smoothness of embeddings over the hypergraph topology, preserving local geometric structure beyond pairwise constraints. The total loss L = L_inter + λ₁ L_intra + λ₂ L_reg is optimized jointly over the shared HGNN and projection head for 200 epochs after the 2000-epoch autoencoder pretraining.

## Results

HOPER achieves state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art clustering performance across six multi-view benchmark datasets. On BBCSport, HOPER reaches 95.04% ACC, 85.09% NMI, and 86.77% ARI, substantially outperforming the best baseline DealMVC (80.70% ACC). On COIL-20, HOPER improves over the second-best method SEM by approximately 8.8% in ACC (82.85% vs. 74.03%), 5.8% in NMI, and 12.8% in ARI. On Hdigit, HOPER achieves 99.61% ACC, trailing only DealMVC (99.80%) by a marginal 0.19%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOPER outperforms all six baselines on five of six datasets across all three metrics (ACC, NMI, ARI). The largest margins appear on BBCSport (HOPER 95.04% ACC vs. DealMVC 80.70%, a 14.3-point gap) and COIL-20 (HOPER 82.85% ACC vs. SEM 74.03%, an 8.8-point gap), where the hypergraph-based high-order modeling provides the most benefit. On WebKB, HOPER (98.29% ACC) surpasses the previous best SEM (94.86%) by 3.4 points in ACC and 16.1 points in NMI. On Handwritten, HOPER (96.85% ACC) edges out MAGA (94.15%) by 2.7 points. The sole exception is Hdigit, where DealMVC achieves a marginally higher ACC (99.80% vs. 99.61%), though the gap is negligible at under 0.2%. Overall, HOPER's dual contrastive learning and hyper-Laplacian regularization consistently yield the most discriminative representations, particularly on datasets with complex high-order structure (BBCSport, COIL-20, WebKB).

## Relation to Prior Work

HOPER extends the deep multi-view clustering (DMVC) literature by replacing the standard pairwise graph structure with hypergraphs, building directly on the HGNN+ convolutional formulation of Gao et al. (2022). While prior DMVC methods (e.g., MFLVC, SEM, MAGA) model sample relationships via shared GNN encoders and contrastive losses over pairwise edges, HOPER elevates the relational structure to hyperedges that connect k+1 samples simultaneously, capturing high-order dependencies. The hyper-Laplacian regularization, previously explored in shallow matrix/tensor factorization settings (Luo et al., Lu et al., Xie et al.), is here integrated into a deep HGNN pipeline for the first time in a multi-view clustering context. The dual contrastive mechanism—pairing inter-view alignment with intra-hyperedge discrimination—departs from standard InfoNCE-style multi-view contrastive losses by explicitly treating same-hyperedge neighbors as negatives to counteract HGNN over-smoothing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a recognized gap in DMVC: the inability of pairwise graph models to encode group-level structural priors, and the false-negative problem in cross-view contrastive learning where semantically similar samples are mislabeled as negatives. The substantial gains on structured datasets (BBCSport, WebKB, COIL-20) suggest that high-order relational modeling is particularly beneficial when cluster boundaries are defined by group-level co-occurrence rather than simple pairwise proximity. However, the method's reliance on k-NN-based hypergraph construction and its noted higher computational complexity raise open questions about scalability to very large or incomplete multi-view settings, and whether learned (rather than heuristic) hyperedge construction would yield further gains. The near-parity with DealMVC on Hdigit also suggests diminishing returns when views are already highly aligned.
