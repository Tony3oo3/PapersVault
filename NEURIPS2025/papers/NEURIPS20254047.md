## Problem

Standard RAG pipelines optimize each component (query rewriting, retrieval, filtering, generation) independently via supervised fine-tuning, creating misalignment between individual module objectives and the end goal of accurate answer generation. Existing RL-based approaches either target only two-component retriever-generator pipelines or optimize modules in isolation, failing to model the collaborative dynamics among multiple interacting components in complex RAG architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254047/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language question and a Wikipedia document corpus, producing a short predicted answer. The pipeline processes the question through four stages: a Query Rewriter decomposes it into sub-questions, a fixed Contriever retriever fetches K=10 candidate documents, a Selector outputs a constrained set of document IDs (from a vocabulary of {"0"..."K-1", "Document", ","}), and a Generator produces the final answer. The Selector's restricted action space and the fixed document count (K=10 regardless of sub-question count) are key design choices that reduce exploration complexity during RL training.

## Method

MMOA-RAG models the RAG pipeline as a Cooperative Multi-Agent RL problem where the Query Rewriter, Selector, and Generator each act as an RL agent sharing a single global reward (F1 score of the final answer against ground truth). The authors employ MAPPO with parameter sharing—all three agents use the same Llama-3-8B-Instruct backbone—alongside a fixed retriever treated as part of the environment. Training proceeds in two stages: a warm-start SFT phase to establish instruction-following baselines, followed by joint MAPPO optimization using GAE for advantage estimation and a KL penalty against the SFT policy (analogous to InstructGPT). Per-agent penalty terms (e.g., discouraging excessive sub-questions, duplicate document IDs, or overly long answers) are added to the shared reward to stabilize multi-agent training without requiring module-specific reward models.

## Results

Using Llama-3-8B-Instruct with Contriever as the retriever, MMOA-RAG achieves F1 scores of 48.29 (HotpotQA), 46.40 (2WikiMultihopQA), and 48.59 (AmbigQA), surpassing the best baseline by +1.80, +1.89, and +2.67 F1 points respectively. In terms of Exact Match, MMOA-RAG reaches 36.15, 41.52, and 34.75, and for Accuracy it scores 39.15, 42.73, and 38.85 across the three datasets. The gains are most pronounced on the single-hop AmbigQA dataset, where the multi-agent joint optimization yields the largest margin over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMOA-RAG outperforms all five compared baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest single-module RL baselines, Rewrite-Retrieve-Read [30] (PPO-trained query rewriter) and BGM [22] (PPO-trained bridge/selector), MMOA-RAG gains +1.12 Acc / +1.80 F1 on HotpotQA and +2.60 Acc / +2.67 F1 on AmbigQA, confirming that jointly optimizing QR, Selector, and Generator via MAPPO surpasses optimizing any single module in isolation. RetRobust [54], the best baseline on HotpotQA and 2WikiMultihopQA, is exceeded by +1.46 Acc and +1.89 F1 on 2WikiMultihopQA. RAG-DDR [27], which uses DPO to align data preferences between modules, is the closest competitor on AmbigQA (F1 45.83 vs. 48.59) but still trails by 2.76 F1 points. SELF-RAG [1] underperforms all other trained baselines on every dataset, suggesting that self-reflective retrieval without explicit multi-module optimization is insufficient for multi-hop QA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RAG optimization work that has applied RL (PPO, DPO) to individual modules—such as Rewrite-Retrieve-Read optimizing a query rewriter or BGM optimizing a bridge/selector—by reframing the entire multi-module RAG pipeline as a Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning problem. It builds directly on MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022) for the joint optimization mechanism and borrows the KL-penalty-against-SFT-baseline structure from InstructGPT (Ouyang et al., 2022) for the reward formulation. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods optimize one module at a time with module-specific or proxy rewards, whereas MMOA-RAG aligns all trainable modules (Query Rewriter, Selector, Generator) toward a single shared F1-based reward, explicitly modeling inter-module collaboration rather than treating each as an independent optimization target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real and acknowledged misalignment problem in RAG pipelines: individually SFT-optimized modules do not necessarily cooperate to produce the best final answer. The Co-MARL framing offers a generalizable template that can be adapted to different module configurations (demonstrated with 2-agent and 3-agent variants) and different retrievers, making it a practical recipe for practitioners building multi-stage RAG systems. However, the retriever is held fixed, the reward is a simple F1 score without cost/latency considerations, and the gains over strong baselines are modest (~1–3 points), leaving open whether the multi-agent overhead is justified versus simpler single-module RL. The authors themselves flag future directions toward DAG-structured workflows, dynamic agentic RAG, and cost-aware reward design as important next steps.
