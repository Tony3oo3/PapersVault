## Problem

Existing fire datasets are limited to small-scale (≤5k images), single-task benchmarks focused on detection or classification, with high duplication rates and no textual annotations. This gap prevents training or evaluating modern multi-modal models for fire understanding, including diffusion-based synthetic data generation and vision-language reasoning about fire severity and context.

## Input / Output

The dataset consumes high-resolution fire-related images, video clips (minimum 10 seconds), and 75-token text prompts describing burning objects, environment, and severity. It produces YOLO-format bounding boxes, human-verified captions structured around a four-level severity taxonomy (No/Low/Moderate/High Risk), synthetic fire images generated by fine-tuned diffusion models, and 12k binary preference pairs for RLHF alignment. Non-fire images are deliberately curated as hard negatives (sunsets, bright lights in darkness) to stress-test false-positive resistance.

## Method

DetectiumFire is constructed through a four-stage pipeline: multi-source data collection (web, IoT sensors, prior benchmarks), deduplication via PHash and CNN-based similarity (reducing duplication from 0.55 in D-Fire to 0.23), expert annotation with bounding boxes and GPT-4o-generated captions (human-verified, capped at 75 tokens), and a synthetic data generation pipeline combining LoRA-based SFT on Stable Diffusion v1.5/2/XL with a Diffusion-DPO RLHF stage trained on 12k human preference pairs. The paper validates the dataset across three task families: object detection (YOLOv11m, Faster R-CNN, YOLO-World-M with 5-fold cross-validation), synthetic image quality (GPT-4o pairwise Elo scoring), and fire reasoning (LoRA fine-tuning of LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct to infer burning object, environment, and severity from images). The key differentiator is the joint provision of real and synthetic data with rich textual annotations, enabling both discriminative and generative modeling within a single resource.

## Results

On the DetectiumFire test set, YOLOv11m trained on real-world DetectiumFire data achieves mAP 43.74%, mAP@50 75.96%, and mAR@100 69.80%, outperforming the same model trained on D-Fire (mAP 40.28%). Cross-dataset evaluation shows DetectiumFire-trained models generalize to D-Fire (mAP 40.32%) while D-Fire-trained models drop sharply on DetectiumFire (mAP 24.88%). For VLM fire reasoning, fine-tuning LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct on DetectiumFire improves burning-object accuracy from 62.73% to 87.37%, environment from 71.82% to 89.39%, and severity from 56.06% to 83.84%. Combining real and synthetic data yields a slight further gain (mAP 44.52% vs 43.74% real-only).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against D-Fire [17], DetectiumFire demonstrates superior dataset quality (CNN duplication ratio 0.23 vs 0.55) and stronger generalization: a YOLOv11m trained on DetectiumFire achieves mAP 40.32% on the D-Fire test set, while a D-Fire-trained model drops to mAP 24.88% on DetectiumFire, a 15.4-point gap indicating D-Fire's limited scenario coverage. Against FLAME_SD [36], the DetectiumFire-based synthetic pipeline is dramatically better: SFT-generated data yields mAP 33.50% (YOLOv11m) versus FLAME_SD's 2.10%, a >30-point margin, confirming that diffusion models fine-tuned on diverse real-world fire scenes produce far more useful training data than mask-guided wildfire-only synthesis. Against the base LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct [40], fine-tuning on DetectiumFire image-caption pairs improves all three fire-reasoning tasks by 17–28 percentage points (e.g., severity accuracy 56.06% → 83.84%), validating the dataset's utility for multi-modal fire understanding. The RLHF pipeline produces slightly lower detection performance than SFT (mAP 32.12% vs 33.50%), suggesting preference optimization may reduce synthetic data diversity, though both substantially outperform FLAME_SD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fire detection dataset lineage (D-Fire, FireNET, and smaller Kaggle collections) by adding multi-modal text annotations, video data, and a synthetic data generation pipeline, moving beyond the purely discriminative CV framing of prior work. For synthetic generation, it departs from the training-free, mask-guided approach of FLAME Diffuser by fine-tuning diffusion models via SFT and DPO-based RLHF on real-world fire data, and for reasoning it replaces the simulation-based HAZARD benchmark with real-world annotated scenes. The methodological toolkit (LoRA, DPO, YOLOv11, LLaMA-3.2-Vision) is standard; the contribution is in dataset curation, annotation design, and domain-specific evaluation rather than algorithmic novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DetectiumFire fills a concrete gap: no prior public fire dataset combined images, videos, bounding boxes, and domain-specific text captions at this scale, and the inclusion of controlled (low-risk) fire scenarios alongside uncontrolled ones is essential for reducing false positives in deployed systems. Key limitations include web-sourced data with academic-use-only licensing, incomplete geographic/cultural coverage, and the risk that some captions or images may still contain misleading content. Open questions include whether the synthetic data pipeline generalizes to video generation, whether richer scene-level annotations (human presence, fire progression) can support AI-agent-based response systems, and whether the 75-token caption constraint is sufficient for more advanced reasoning tasks.
