## Problem

Current organ-offer acceptance models are trained on observational data under a fixed allocation policy, inheriting confounding bias and failing to generalize to counterfactual, policy-relevant scenarios. Although clinicians routinely record categorical refusal reasons (e.g., 'old donor age'), these direction-only signals remain unused because existing explanation-guided methods require richer supervision such as precise counterfactual distances or monotonicity constraints. The paper formalizes this direction-only supervision regime and shows how to exploit it for causal, policy-invariant acceptance prediction.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a patient feature vector X ∈ R^{d_x} and an organ-offer feature vector O ∈ R^{d_o} (tabular clinical and logistical attributes) and outputs a scalar acceptance probability in [0,1]. During training, negative samples additionally carry a categorical refusal reason R that is mapped via a fixed lookup M to a signed direction vector δ ∈ {-1,0,1}^{d_x+d_o}; the model never sees the magnitude of the required edit, only the direction. Counterfactual edits are constrained to a feasible hyper-box F defined by per-feature minima and maxima observed in the training data.

## Method

CLEXNET couples a shared encoder Φ_{θ_φ}(X,O) with two heads: an acceptance head Y_{θ_Y} and an organ-cluster head c_{θ_p} (k-means clusters over the organ space). A gradient-reversal layer before the cluster head adversarially balances the representation so that, conditional on φ, the organ-cluster distribution resembles a randomized trial, attenuating selection bias from the observed policy π_obs. The key novelty is a guarded explanation-guided augmentation loss: for each refusal with reason δ, the model samples M step-size vectors α within the feasible region F, forms augmented inputs (X+α_X δ_X, O+α_O δ_O), and incurs a BCE penalty toward a target probability p_ex only if none of the augmented samples already exceeds p_ex (the guard G_i). The full objective L_CLEX = L_BCE − λ·L_CE + ρ·L_EXPL is optimized via a min–max scheme (SGD for φ, Y; max for the cluster head), jointly correcting observational confounding and enforcing directional refusal constraints without requiring distance or monotonicity information.

## Results

On the unbiased synthetic test set, CLEXNET achieves the best performance across all metrics: BCE 0.377, AUC 0.872, AUPRC 0.948, and Brier 0.117, outperforming the strongest baseline ORGANITE (AUC 0.840, Brier 0.140). On the semi-synthetic UNOS-PTR liver data (~1.1M offers), CLEXNET reaches AUC 0.704 and Brier 0.170, substantially ahead of ORGANITE (AUC 0.598) and PATIENTNET (AUC 0.593). Even CLEXNET without balancing (λ=0) already surpasses ORGANITE on the semi-synthetic set (AUC 0.655 vs. 0.598), indicating that the explanation-guided augmentation loss alone provides a meaningful boost. The gap between CLEXNET and ORGANITE widens from ~3 AUC points on synthetic data to ~11 AUC points on real semi-synthetic data, suggesting the explanation signal is particularly valuable when the observational distribution is complex.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLEXNET outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both the synthetic and semi-synthetic evaluations. Against ORGANITE [8] (the closest competitor, which also uses adversarial balancing), CLEXNET gains +3.2 AUC points on synthetic data (0.872 vs. 0.840) and +10.6 AUC points on UNOS-PTR (0.704 vs. 0.598), with Brier improving from 0.140→0.117 and 0.235→0.170 respectively. Against PATIENTNET [40] (a single-task neural model without balancing), the advantage is even larger: +17.3 AUC points on synthetic and +11.1 on semi-synthetic, confirming that both the balancing and explanation components are necessary. Traditional baselines—Logistic Regression [33] and Random Forest [33,10]—perform poorly on the unbiased test set (AUC 0.54–0.60 on UNOS-PTR), illustrating how severely confounding bias degrades generalization when no causal correction is applied. Notably, CLEXNET with λ=0 (explanation loss only, no balancing) already beats ORGANITE on the semi-synthetic set, isolating the contribution of the direction-only refusal-reason supervision as an independent source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLEXNET extends the adversarial balancing framework introduced by ORGANITE [8] and the domain-adversarial training literature [22, 13] by adding a second, orthogonal supervision channel: direction-only refusal reasons. While ORGANITE learns a policy-invariant representation via gradient-reversal on organ clusters, CLEXNET augments this with a guarded explanation-guided loss that converts categorical refusal codes into feasible counterfactual edits. The paper explicitly positions itself against existing explanation-guided methods (counterfactual distance constraints, gradient supervision, monotonicity rules) that require richer supervision than the sparse, directional signals available in organ allocation records, and contrasts its data-driven approach with hard-coded domain-knowledge injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical, drop-in improvement for organ allocation simulators that currently rely on simplistic logistic-regression acceptance models, and more broadly demonstrates that routinely recorded but unused categorical explanations can serve as a form of causal supervision in observational settings. Key open questions include: how to handle coarse or latent refusal reasons that map to multiple feature directions, how to replace the conservative hyper-box feasible region with clinically-aware constraints (blood type, size matching), and whether the approach generalizes beyond the semi-synthetic evaluation to fully real-world deployment under distribution shift and performative prediction. The formalization of 'direction-only counterfactual supervision' as a distinct learning regime may find applications beyond transplantation wherever human explanations are categorical and qualitative rather than quantitative.
