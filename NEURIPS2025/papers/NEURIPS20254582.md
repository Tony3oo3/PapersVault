## Problem

Existing ML-based separator configuration methods for MILP solvers treat separator activation as a static or few-round decision, ignoring how separator efficacy shifts across separation rounds due to decaying marginal gains and interaction effects among separators. This paper identifies that the optimal configuration changes dynamically as cuts accumulate and the LP relaxation tightens, yet no prior method adapts configurations round-by-round at multiple B&B nodes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254582/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decision step, the method consumes only the incremental triplet subgraph (newly added cut nodes, tightened variable bounds, updated separator statistics) rather than re-encoding the full MILP graph, keeping inference cost bounded as the problem grows. The output is a joint categorical action: a scalar maximum separation round m_t ∈ {1,…,T} deciding when to halt cut generation, plus a K-dimensional activation vector η_t ∈ {−1, 0, +1}^K specifying for each of the 22 SCIP separators whether it is invoked early, deactivated, or deferred to a late phase. Decisions are made autoregressively at every separation round for nodes at predefined B&B depths (every 10th level).

## Method

DynSep formulates dynamic separator configuration as an MDP solved with PPO, where the reward combines normalized runtime penalty, relative dual-bound improvement, and aggregate separator effectiveness (cut application rate, domain reductions, node prunings). A GCN encoder tokenizes each incremental subgraph into a block of K+1 embeddings (one graph-level token plus K separator-node tokens), which are fed sequentially into a decoder-only Transformer that autoregressively predicts the next round's configuration. A blocked positional encoding assigns the same position vector to all K+1 tokens within a block, capturing inter-block temporal order while rendering intra-block tokens interchangeable; this guarantees permutation equivariance so that each token maps one-to-one to its corresponding action component (graph token → max round, separator token → activation status). The actor and critic are instantiated as two separate decoder-only Transformers sharing the same architecture, trained end-to-end with PPO.

## Results

DynSep achieves a 64% average speedup in solving time on five easy and medium MILP benchmarks (e.g., 1.51s vs. 6.56s for L2Sep(R1) on Set Covering; 0.53s vs. 5.42s on Maximum Independent Set) and reduces the average primal-dual gap integral by 16% on four hard datasets within a 300-second time limit (e.g., 8511.58 vs. 10383.49 for L2Sep(R1) on MIPLIB mixed neos; 3720.26 vs. 10548.89 for L2Sep(R1) on Load Balancing). DynSep is the only method that solves all CORLAT instances to optimality with zero primal-dual gaps. Generalization tests on 4× and 9× larger MIS instances confirm that DynSep maintains its advantage (97.20% and 91.11% time improvement) while baselines degrade substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynSep consistently outperforms both learning-based baselines across all nine benchmarks. Against L2Sep (R1/R2), DynSep achieves 4–10× lower solving time on easy datasets (e.g., 0.53s vs. 5.36–5.42s on MIS) and 3–8× lower PD integral on hard datasets (e.g., 8511.58 vs. 10383.49–13989.09 on MIPLIB mixed neos; 3720.26 vs. 10548.89–10860.13 on Load Balancing). Against LLM4Sep, DynSep is substantially better on easy/medium datasets (e.g., 1.51s vs. 11.73s on Set Covering) and on hard datasets (e.g., 9212.24 vs. 22618.21 PD integral on MIPLIB mixed supportcase), though LLM4Sep is competitive on Load Balancing (4769.47 vs. 3720.26). Among human-designed rules, Search(50) is the strongest baseline on hard datasets but requires expensive offline evaluation of 50 configurations and is unstable across problem types, while DynSep adapts online without any pre-screening. DynSep is the only method that solves all CORLAT instances to optimality (zero primal-dual gap), a distinction no other baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ML-based separator configuration line initiated by L2Sep (Li et al., NeurIPS 2023), reusing its GCN encoder and triplet graph representation, but departs fundamentally by formulating configuration as a sequential RL decision problem rather than a one-shot or fixed-round prediction. Where L2Sep trains separate bandit models per round and LLM4Sep provides only cold-start configurations, DynSep makes activation and stopping decisions dynamically at every separation round and node via a decoder-only Transformer over incremental subgraphs. The blocked positional encoding and permutation-equivariant token-to-action mapping are novel architectural contributions not present in prior separator configuration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 64% average speedup on easy/medium benchmarks and 16% PD-integral reduction on hard industrial-scale problems (MIPLIB 2017, Load Balancing, Anonymous) demonstrate that dynamic, round-aware separator configuration is a practically significant lever for MILP solver performance. Generalization to 4× and 9× larger unseen instances suggests the learned policy captures structural rather than instance-specific patterns. Key open questions include: (i) whether the approach scales beyond 22 SCIP separators or transfers across solvers, (ii) how to maintain global B&B tree context with a lightweight model (acknowledged limitation), and (iii) whether the RL formulation can be extended to jointly optimize branching and cutting decisions.
