## Problem

In selective prediction, a forecaster can choose when and over what window to predict, and prior work (Drucker 2013; Qiao & Valiant 2019) showed vanishing error is achievable with full selectivity. However, many real-world scenarios restrict predictions to specific time windows (e.g., trading seasons, outdoor activity planning). This paper introduces Prediction with Limited Selectivity (PLS), where the forecaster may only predict at a given subset of timesteps, and characterizes the optimal worst-case error as a function of the instance structure.

## Input / Output

The forecaster is given a sequence length n and a stopping time set T ⊆ {0,…,n−1} specifying which timesteps permit prediction. They observe a sequence x ∈ [0,1]^n one element at a time; at any t ∈ T, they may choose a window length w and output a single point prediction μ̂ for the average of the next w unseen elements, incurring squared error (μ̂−μ)². The instance is equivalently represented by a block-length vector L = (l₁,…,l_m) where each l_i counts the timesteps between consecutive stopping times, and the forecaster must commit to exactly one prediction before the sequence is fully revealed.

## Method

The paper introduces approximate uniformity Û(L) = max_{i≤j} (l_i+…+l_j)/max{l_i,…,l_j} as an instance-dependent complexity measure capturing the effective horizon length. For the upper bound, the algorithm merges the m blocks into Ω(Û(L)) approximately uniform super-blocks, then applies a randomized recursive procedure (RandomSelect) that, at each level, either predicts the second half's mean from the first half's mean with probability 1/k or recurses into a half chosen proportionally to its length, yielding O(1/log Û(L)) worst-case error. For lower bounds, a variance argument gives Ω(1/Û²), while a novel ternary tree construction—where each internal node splits based on whether a dominant block exists, and edge noise magnitudes are set to σ(v)=√(1−ln(size(v))/ln m)—yields Ω(1/log m) for any m-block instance. An average-case analysis shows that for k-monotone random stopping time sets with expected size m₀ = Ω((k log²n)^{1+c}), the upper and lower bounds match up to a constant factor with high probability.

## Results

The paper establishes that for any PLS instance L, there exists a forecasting algorithm with worst-case error O(1/log Û(L)) (Theorem 1), where Û(L) is the approximate uniformity of the block structure. Complementary lower bounds show every algorithm incurs Ω(max{1/[Û(L)]², 1/log m}) error (Theorem 2). For randomly generated stopping time sets from k-monotone probability sequences, the upper and lower bounds match up to a constant factor with high probability (Corollary 5), provided the expected number of stopping times is at least polylog(n) for constant k. In the fully-selective special case (all blocks of length 1), the bounds recover the known Θ(1/log n) rate from Drucker [Dru13] and Qiao & Valiant [QV19].

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The results are positioned relative to prior theoretical work: the PLS model generalizes the fully-selective prediction setting of Drucker [Dru13] (O(1/log n) upper bound) and Qiao & Valiant [QV19] (matching lower bound and extension to general functions). The paper demonstrates that limited selectivity can fundamentally degrade performance: for geometrically growing block lengths L_m = (1, 2, 4, …, 2^{m-1}), the worst-case error remains Ω(1) even as n → ∞ (Corollary 3), in stark contrast to the vanishing O(1/log n) error in the fully-selective case. Similarly, a Cantor-set-inspired family of instances with m = 2^{k+1}−1 blocks and n = 3^k also forces Ω(1) error. The ternary tree construction for the lower bound extends the binary tree approach of [QV19], which only handled equal-length blocks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the selective prediction framework of Drucker (2013) and Qiao & Valiant (2019), where a forecaster may predict the average of a future window at any chosen time, by restricting predictions to a given subset of timesteps (the 'stopping time set'). The upper-bound algorithm generalizes the randomized multi-scale prediction of Drucker via a block-merging reduction, while the lower-bound proof extends the binary-tree construction of Qiao & Valiant to a ternary tree that handles non-uniform block lengths. The model also connects to the broader literature on online prediction with abstention, where the forecaster may skip predictions at a cost, though here the restriction is structural rather than penalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a clean complexity measure (approximate uniformity) that captures instance-dependent hardness, but the upper bound O(1/log Û) and lower bound Ω(1/Û²) leave a significant gap that the authors show cannot be closed by Û alone (Proposition 16, a Cantor-set-like construction). The average-case analysis is satisfying: for randomly generated instances from k-monotone probability sequences, the bounds match up to constants with high probability. The main open problem is identifying a sharper complexity measure that accounts for 'skipping' short blocks, and extending the limited-selectivity lens to other classic online learning problems like the experts problem.
