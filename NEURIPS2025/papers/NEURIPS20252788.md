## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection methods are specialist-oriented: training-dependent approaches require annotated data per scenario, while training-free methods still rely on handcrafted prompts, static preprocessing, and manual post-processing that break down on unseen scenes or novel anomaly types. No prior method can autonomously adapt to arbitrary environments and anomaly categories without any training data or human pipeline engineering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252788/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PANDA consumes a video sequence sampled at 1 FPS (processed in clips of 5 frames) together with a free-form user query specifying which anomaly types to detect. It outputs, per clip, a discrete status (Normal, Abnormal, or Insufficient), a continuous anomaly score in [0,1], and a natural-language reasoning trace. The system supports both offline mode (perception over 300 uniformly sampled frames) and online streaming mode (perception over only the first 10 frames), and the Insufficient status triggers an internal reflection loop before a final score is committed.

## Method

PANDA is a training-free agentic framework built on two MLLMs (Qwen2.5VL-7B for visual reasoning, Gemini 2.0 Flash for planning and reflection) orchestrated via LangGraph. Its core mechanism is a four-stage loop: (1) a scene-aware RAG planner perceives the environment and retrieves top-k anomaly rules from a pre-generated knowledge base to produce a tailored detection strategy; (2) a goal-driven heuristic reasoner feeds the strategy plus short-term chain-of-memory (last 5 reasoning steps, both textual and visual) into the VLM for per-clip judgment; (3) when the VLM returns Insufficient, a reflection module queries long-term memory for similar past cases, then invokes a suite of tools (YOLO-World object detection, CLIP image retrieval, Real-ESRGAN super-resolution, web search, etc.) to gather supplementary evidence and re-reasons; (4) a self-improving chain-of-memory accumulates all reasoning, reflection, and refined-reasoning outputs across the video, enabling progressively more stable decisions. The key distinction from prior training-free VAD is that the detection pipeline is not static — the strategy, rules, and tools are dynamically selected per scene and per ambiguity, eliminating the need for scenario-specific hand engineering.

## Results

PANDA achieves 84.89% AUC on UCF-Crime, 70.16% AP on XD-Violence, 75.78% AUC on UBnormal, and 73.12% AUC on CSAD in offline mode, all without any training or manual pipeline design. On the open-set UBnormal benchmark, PANDA (75.78%) surpasses the best training-free baseline AnomalyRuler (71.90%) by 3.88 points and LAVAD (64.23%) by 11.55 points. On the complex-scene CSAD benchmark, PANDA (73.12%) edges out the instruction-tuned Holmes-VAU (72.47%) and substantially outperforms LAVAD (57.26%). In online mode, PANDA still leads all training-free baselines across all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PANDA outperforms all training-free baselines on every dataset: on UCF-Crime it beats LAVAD (80.28%) by 4.61 AUC points, on UBnormal it surpasses AnomalyRuler (71.90%) by 3.88 points and LAVAD (64.23%) by 11.55 points, and on CSAD it exceeds LAVAD (57.26%) by 15.86 points. Against the strongest training-dependent baseline Holmes-VAU (88.96% UCF, 87.68% XD), PANDA trails by 4.07 and 17.52 points respectively, reflecting the cost of instruction tuning on in-domain data. However, on the open-set UBnormal benchmark where Holmes-VAU drops to 56.77%, PANDA leads by a substantial 19.01 points, demonstrating superior generalization to unseen anomaly types. On CSAD, PANDA (73.12%) narrowly beats Holmes-VAU (72.47%) in offline mode, though Holmes-VAU retains a slight edge in online mode (72.47% vs. 71.25%), which the authors attribute to CSAD's heavy overlap with Holmes-VAU's training distribution. Overall, PANDA is the only method that is simultaneously training-free, manual-free, and competitive with or superior to supervised approaches on open-set and complex-scene settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

PANDA departs from both established VAD paradigms: training-dependent methods (semi-supervised, weakly-supervised, instruction-tuned) that require domain-specific data, and training-free LLM/VLM pipelines (LAVAD, AnomalyRuler) that still rely on handcrafted prompts and static preprocessing. Rather than building on a specific prior VAD architecture, the paper repurposes the broader LLM-agent paradigm—tool-augmented reflection, RAG-based planning, and iterative self-improvement—into a unified agentic framework for VAD. The chain-of-memory mechanism and scene-adaptive RAG are novel architectural contributions that have no direct counterpart in prior VAD work, though each component (retrieval, tool use, memory) draws on the general LLM-agent literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully automated, training-free agent can match or exceed fine-tuned VLMs on open-set and multi-scenario VAD, challenging the assumption that domain-specific fine-tuning is necessary for robust anomaly detection. The introduction of CSAD, a complex-scene benchmark with degraded visual conditions, fills a gap in existing evaluation. However, practical adoption is limited by slow inference (~0.5–0.86 FPS), the absence of spatial anomaly localization, and a fixed toolset that may not cover all real-world modalities (e.g., thermal imaging). Open questions include whether the agentic loop can be made real-time, whether the approach generalizes beyond the curated tool suite, and how the chain-of-memory scales over very long video streams.
