## Problem

First-order optimization methods with exponentially decaying memory (AdamW, Lion, GD with momentum, Nesterov) are ubiquitous in deep learning, yet there is little theoretical understanding of how this memory implicitly biases the optimization trajectory and affects generalization. The paper asks: can we identify an effective, memoryless loss function that a memoryful optimizer is actually minimizing, and use that to predict whether memory helps or hurts generalization?

## Input / Output

The framework accepts any first-order optimization algorithm whose dependence on past iterates decays exponentially (covering AdamW, Lion-K, Nesterov, NAdam, heavy-ball GD, and beyond) together with a loss function, and produces a memoryless iteration of the form θ̃^(n+1) = θ̃^(n) − h[F(θ̃^(n)) + M^(n)(θ̃^(n))], where the correction term M^(n) depends only on the current iterate. The approximation is valid with O(h²) uniform error over any finite time horizon T (i.e., ⌊T/h⌋ iterations), requiring only that the learning rate h be sufficiently small and that memory decay satisfy a summability condition (Assumption 3.1).

## Method

The core technique replaces every past iterate θ^(n−k) in the update rule with the current iterate θ^(n), then adds a correction term M^(n)(θ) = h·Σ_k (∂F_r^(n)/∂θ^(n−k))(θ)ᵀ Σ_s F^(s)(θ) derived via Taylor expansion, which captures how the update's sensitivity to past iterates interacts with past updates. Under a memory-decay assumption bounding first and second derivatives of F with respect to past iterates, the paper proves a one-step O(h³) error bound (Theorem 3.2) and a global O(h²) error bound on finite horizons (Corollary 3.3). The correction term is then interpreted as a perturbation of the loss: for AdamW it reveals an implicit anti-regularization of the L1-norm of the gradient (when β₂ > β₁), while for Lion the correction is O(ε) and negligible, providing a theory-based explanation for Lion's superior generalization. The framework further extends to deriving modified ODEs (recovering results of Kovachki–Stuart and Ghosh et al.) and to identifying additional implicit regularization from mini-batch noise.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a general memoryless approximation with a 1-step error bound of O(h³) (Theorem 3.2) and a global error bound of O(h²) on any finite time horizon T (Corollary 3.3), where h is the learning rate. Applied to GD with momentum, the framework shows memory implicitly adds the regularizer (hβ)/(2(1−β)²)‖∇L(θ)‖² to the loss, pushing iterates toward flatter minima. The key practical finding is that AdamW's memory implicitly *anti*-regularizes by penalizing the one-norm of the gradient ‖∇L(θ)‖₁ (when β₂ > β₁), whereas Lion's memory correction is O(ε) and thus negligible, providing a theory-based explanation for Lion's empirically better generalization over AdamW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical benchmark comparisons, so there are no standard baseline accuracy/loss tables. The central qualitative comparison is between AdamW and Lion: AdamW's memory correction term is proportional to (β₂/(1−β₂) − β₁/(1−β₁)) and acts as an *anti*-regularizer on ‖∇L(θ)‖₁, while Lion's correction is proportional to ε/(|∇ᵣL|²+ε)^{3/2} and vanishes as ε→0, meaning Lion does not suffer from memory-induced anti-regularization. For GD with momentum, the framework recovers and extends the modified-loss analysis of Ghosh et al. [26] and Kovachki & Stuart [37], showing the implicit sharpness regularizer (hβ)/(2(1−β)²)‖∇L(θ)‖². The paper also notes that Adam with β₁=β₂ and Lion with ρ₁=ρ₂ (i.e., Signum) produce identical correction terms, offering a novel theoretical explanation for the empirically observed similarity between these two optimizers [69].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the modified-equations / backward-error-analysis approach to optimization algorithms from specific cases (GD with momentum in Ghosh et al. [26], Kovachki & Stuart [37]) to a broad class of first-order methods with exponentially decaying memory, including adaptive optimizers like AdamW, NAdam, and Lion. It extends Cattaneo et al. [11]'s continuous-time analysis of Adam by working in discrete time, providing explicit approximation bounds between the memoryful and memoryless iterations, and by incorporating weight decay. The key methodological departure is a general Taylor-expansion-based construction of a correction term M^(n)(θ) that captures the effect of memory, valid under a single decay assumption (Assumption 3.1) rather than algorithm-specific derivations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a systematic way to identify how memory implicitly (anti-)regularizes any optimizer with exponentially decaying memory, with the concrete finding that AdamW's memory anti-regularizes (negatively penalizes the ℓ₁-norm of the gradient) while Lion's memory does not, offering a theory-based explanation for Lion's empirically observed better generalization. Limitations include the requirement of sufficiently small learning rates, primarily full-batch analysis, and only preliminary empirical validation. Open questions include extending the analysis to Shampoo and other second-order-preconditioned methods, characterizing the interplay between memory-induced anti-regularization and mini-batch noise at scale, and determining whether the anti-regularization effect actually degrades generalization in large-scale training regimes where scaling dominates.
