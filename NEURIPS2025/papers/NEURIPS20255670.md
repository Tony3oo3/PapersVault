## Problem

Existing prototype-based PML methods derive class prototypes from noisy candidate labels, producing biased estimates that degrade confidence estimation—especially when positive samples are scarce. Additionally, the semantic gap between unsupervised clustering-derived prototypes and weakly supervised label-based prototypes remains unexploited, leaving their complementary structure unaligned.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255670/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a d-dimensional feature matrix X ∈ R^{d×n} and an n×q binary candidate label matrix Y where each row contains both ground-truth labels and false-positive noise labels (e.g., 3–11 candidate labels per instance across 5–374 classes). It outputs a predicted ground-truth label set per instance via a kernel-based multi-label classifier, with the label confidence matrix R providing soft reliability scores that suppress noisy candidates during prediction.

## Method

CAPML operates in two stages. Stage 1 learns unsupervised prototypes M and fuzzy membership F via entropy-regularized fuzzy c-means, computes weakly supervised prototypes O from the noisy candidate labels, then aligns the two prototype spaces using a permutation matrix P (solved by the Hungarian algorithm) and an orthogonal rotation matrix H (solved by the Procrustes problem), yielding a label reliability indicator matrix C that encodes per-instance, per-label confidence. Stage 2 jointly optimizes a label confidence matrix R and a Gaussian-kernel classifier W under a reliability-weighted fidelity term (C ⊙ (Y−R)), an ℓ₁ sparsity penalty on Y−R to suppress noise, and an ℓ₂,₁ row-sparse regularizer on W for feature selection, solved by alternating multiplicative updates and soft-thresholding.

## Results

CAPML achieves state-of-the-art Average Precision on 87.5% of the 24 dataset configurations and Ranking Loss on 83.3%, with zero losses across all pairwise comparisons. Representative AP values include 0.820 on Mirflickr (vs. 0.815 FBD-PML, 0.814 fPML), 0.628 on Music_emotion (vs. 0.626 PAMB, 0.607 FBD-PML), and 0.306 on corel5k (avg.#CLs=7, vs. 0.282 LENFN, 0.274 FBD-PML). Statistical t-tests at the 0.05 level confirm 607 wins and 113 ties out of 720 total comparisons, with no significant losses against any baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAPML dominates all six baselines with zero significant losses across 720 pairwise t-tests (0.05 level). Against the closest competitor FBD-PML (also prototype-based), CAPML wins 99/21/0 across all five metrics, with the gap widening on high-noise datasets (e.g., +3.7 AP on YeastBP, +3.2 on corel5k). Against LENFN, the margin is tighter (93/27/0), with LENFN narrowly edging CAPML on Music_style (0.745 vs. 0.743) and medical #CLs=5 (0.878 vs. 0.876), but CAPML recovers on all other configurations. The advantage over PAMB (91/29/0) is most pronounced on sparse-label datasets like corel5k where PAMB's ECOC decomposition degrades (0.239 vs. 0.306 AP). Against PML-NI (105/15/0), PARTICLE (108/12/0), and fPML (109/11/0), CAPML shows the largest margins, with PARTICLE and fPML particularly weak on high-noise settings (e.g., fPML drops to 0.371 AP on birds #CLs=5 vs. CAPML's 0.589).

## Relation to Prior Work

CAPML extends the prototype-based PML line initiated by FBD-PML [19], which uses fuzzy clustering to learn class prototypes and confidence values under manifold assumptions. The key conceptual departure is identifying an explicit 'prototype misalignment' problem: unsupervised clustering prototypes (M) and weakly supervised label-aggregation prototypes (O) capture the same class structure but in arbitrary, noise-corrupted orderings. CAPML bridges this gap by jointly learning a permutation matrix (via Hungarian algorithm) and an orthogonal rotation matrix (via Procrustes) to establish correspondence between the two prototype spaces, then converting aligned fuzzy memberships into a label reliability indicator that guides classifier training with ℓ₂,₁-norm regularization. This contrasts with prior explicit-disambiguation approaches (low-rank/sparse decomposition, label propagation, feature-induced methods) that attempt to remove noise directly from the candidate label set without leveraging the structural complementarity between unsupervised and supervised representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prototype alignment mechanism offers a general principle—using an unsupervised structural prior to correct biased supervised estimates—that could extend beyond PML to other weakly supervised settings where noisy labels corrupt prototype estimation. However, the method's effectiveness hinges on the assumption that fuzzy clustering recovers structure aligned with true label semantics; the authors acknowledge degradation when intra-class variability fragments categories or when label counts substantially exceed natural cluster structures. Open questions include scalability to very large label spaces (q ≫ n), the behavior under non-sparse noise distributions that violate the Theorem 1 bound, and whether the multi-stage alternating optimization (clustering → alignment → classifier) can be unified into a single end-to-end objective. The computational cost of three sequential optimization stages also limits applicability to very large-scale problems.
