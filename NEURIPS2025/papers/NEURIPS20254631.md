## Problem

Current text-to-image diffusion models lack explicit spatial reasoning, producing poor alignment in complex multi-object scenes with precise layouts and depth relationships. Existing layout-guided methods decouple layout planning from generation, using a static pre-computed layout that cannot be refined as the image is synthesized, leading to suboptimal spatial control in dense or physically complex scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254631/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing a scene (e.g., 'a vase in front of a chair in the room') and produces a generated RGB image along with an intermediate 3D scene layout specifying per-entity local prompts, 3D sizes (length, width, height in meters), and 3D positions (x, y, z). Generation runs on FLUX.1-schnell with 20 denoising steps, and the MLLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) performs up to 5 feedback iterations (adaptive early stopping averages 2.9 steps), each consuming the current noisy latent prediction to re-evaluate and refine the layout before the next denoising step.

## Method

CoT-Diff tightly entangles a multimodal LLM with a diffusion model so that 3D layout reasoning and image generation proceed in tandem within a single sampling trajectory. At each denoising step, the MLLM inspects the predicted clean image, diagnoses spatial misalignment against the prompt, and updates entity sizes/positions in the 3D scene plan. The updated plan is converted into two condition types: a semantic layout (global and per-entity prompt embeddings via T5) and a depth map (rendered from 3D bounding boxes projected through the camera), each injected through dedicated LoRA modules trained with flow matching loss. A condition-aware attention mechanism constructs a binary mask over a unified token sequence (semantic tokens, depth tokens, image latents) that isolates cross-condition interference while restricting each local prompt to attend only to its projected 2D bounding-box region, enabling spatially localized and modality-disentangled control. Training data is auto-annotated by back-projecting monocular depth (Depth Pro) and segmentation (SAM) masks into 3D point clouds and fitting oriented bounding boxes via minimum-volume optimization.

## Results

On the newly introduced 3DSceneBench (800 prompts, 95.3% complex), CoT-Diff achieves the highest spatial alignment scores across all eight relation categories, with improvements of +10.2% to +22.4% over the best baseline per category. On the hardest categories (Multi-Relation and Complex), CoT-Diff scores 48.7 and 50.8 respectively, outperforming the strongest baselines (FLUX at 35.2 and EliGen at 40.6) by 38.3% and 25.1%. On DVMP, CoT-Diff leads in Single (80.9) and Multi (78.8) attribute binding, and on T2I-CompBench it achieves the best Spatial (55.8) and Complex (50.0) scores. A user study confirms CoT-Diff's superiority with the highest 3D Layout Accuracy (4.08/5) and Visual Quality (4.12/5) ratings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoT-Diff outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated category. Among standard T2I models, FLUX is the strongest baseline (e.g., 48.0 on Front, 35.2 on Multi-Relation) but is surpassed by 6.9–13.5 points; SD1.5, SDXL, and PixArt trail further behind. Among layout-guided methods, EliGen is the closest competitor (48.4 on Front Left, 52.5 on Front Right, 40.6 on Complex), yet CoT-Diff still leads by 18.0, 16.5, and 10.2 points respectively; RPG and LooseControl perform substantially worse, with LooseControl scoring as low as 12.7 on Multi-Relation. In the appendix comparison (Table 10), CoT-Diff also beats unified generation models (Janus-Pro, VILA-U) and other CoT-based T2I methods (Image-CoT, T2I-R1) by 6–20+ points on spatial categories, confirming that explicit 3D layout reasoning via MLLM feedback provides a clear advantage over semantic-level or token-level CoT approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the layout-guided T2I generation line (GLIGEN, EliGen, RPG) by replacing static 2D bounding-box conditions with iterative 3D scene planning driven by an MLLM, and by entangling that planning with the diffusion sampling trajectory rather than decoupling it as a pre-processing step. It builds directly on EliGen's entity-level LoRA training and FLUX's DiT backbone, while drawing on the CoT-reasoning paradigm from LLMs (Image-CoT, T2I-R1) to introduce stepwise feedback within a single denoising round. The condition-aware attention mechanism with disentangled semantic and depth LoRA branches departs from prior plug-and-play spatial control (MultiDiffusion, RPG's resize-and-concatenate) by enforcing spatially localized, modality-specific token interactions through a learned binary mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3DSceneBench benchmark fills a gap in evaluating spatial reasoning for T2I models, and the inline CoT-style 3D layout refinement paradigm offers a principled alternative to multi-round regeneration pipelines. However, the 101.4s inference time (vs. 28.1s for FLUX) and the full dependence on an external MLLM for all spatial reasoning limit practical deployment; the authors themselves flag integrating layout reasoning into the generative model as the key open question. Absolute spatial accuracy scores (50–69%) remain moderate, indicating that robust 3D compositional generation is still far from solved, and the method's sensitivity to MLLM quality (Gemini > GPT-4o > Qwen) raises questions about scalability and cost.
