## Problem

Current machine learning climate emulators achieve stable long-term rollouts but lack physical grounding: they rely on correlations rather than causal relationships, do not respect known climate teleconnections, and cannot support counterfactual attribution experiments. This paper addresses the gap by developing an interpretable causal climate emulator that learns physically meaningful latent dynamics while maintaining stability over multi-decadal autoregressive projections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252992/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes five consecutive monthly surface temperature fields (normalized and deseasonalized at 250 km resolution on an icosahedral grid) and produces a probabilistic next-month temperature field. During autoregressive rollout, the Bayesian filter additionally consumes a fixed spatial Fourier spectrum (computed as the time-mean of observed spectra) as a proxy observation to constrain each step. The learned outputs include a sparse causal DAG over latent variables, N latent trajectories representing the predictive distribution, and the capacity to intervene on specific grid cells or latents for counterfactual attribution.

## Method

PICABU extends CDSD by (i) replacing the sparsity penalty with a hard constraint via the augmented Lagrangian method to directly control graph density and encode domain knowledge, (ii) adding CRPS and ℓ¹ spatial/temporal spectral loss terms to preserve probabilistic accuracy and power-spectrum structure during training, and (iii) introducing a particle-based Bayesian filter for stable autoregressive rollouts. The filter samples R candidate next-step latents per trajectory, decodes each to observation space, computes its spatial Fourier spectrum, and assigns a Laplace likelihood against the fixed ground-truth spectrum, retaining only the highest-likelihood sample to prevent error accumulation and mean-reversion. The single-parent decoding assumption (each grid cell maps to exactly one latent) ensures causal identifiability and makes the learned latents interpretable as regional climate modes, enabling direct counterfactual interventions in either observation or latent space.

## Results

On NorESM2 pre-industrial data, PICABU achieves the closest match to ground truth across all statistics for both GMST (mean −0.052, std 0.277, range 2.02, LSD 0.444) and Niño3.4 (mean −0.091, std 1.05, range 5.99, LSD 0.191), compared to ground truth (GMST: 0, 0.177, 1.357, 0; Niño3.4: 0, 0.927, 5.73, 0). On the SAVAR causal graph recovery benchmark, PICABU achieves F1-scores of 1.0 on all 4-latent datasets and 0.81–0.99 on 25- and 100-latent datasets, outperforming or matching CDSD and substantially outperforming V-PCMCI (which drops to F1 0.22 on the hardest 25-latent case). PICABU also captures the ~3-year ENSO peak in the power spectrum of Niño3.4, a feature missed by all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CDSD (the direct causal-representation-learning predecessor), PICABU is the only method that produces stable 100-year rollouts on NorESM2; CDSD diverges entirely without the additional spectral and CRPS loss terms. On the SAVAR causal graph recovery task, PICABU matches CDSD on easy/medium datasets (F1 = 1.0) and slightly outperforms it on the hardest 4-latent case (F1 1.0 vs. 0.82) and 100-latent hard case (0.84 vs. 0.80). Against V-PCMCI, PICABU wins decisively on all climate statistics: V-PCMCI produces unrealistically low variability (GMST std 0.078 vs. ground truth 0.177; Niño3.4 std 0.346 vs. 0.927) and lower F1-scores on SAVAR (e.g., 0.22 vs. 0.81 on the hardest 25-latent case). Against the ViT + positional encoding baseline, PICABU achieves substantially lower GMST variability (std 0.277 vs. 0.458; range 2.02 vs. 4.91) and a lower Niño3.4 LSD (0.191 vs. 0.326), while the ViT exhibits overly high variability in both indices. Overall, PICABU is the only method that simultaneously maintains stable long-term rollouts, matches ground-truth climate statistics, and recovers the correct causal graph structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CDSD (Causal Discovery with Single-parent Decoding) by Brouillard et al. [2024], a differentiable causal representation learning framework for time series, into a physically-guided climate emulator called PICABU. The key departures from CDSD are: (i) replacing the sparsity penalty with a hard constraint via the augmented Lagrangian method to encode domain knowledge, (ii) adding CRPS and spatial/temporal Fourier spectral losses to enforce physical consistency, and (iii) introducing a particle-based Bayesian filter that uses the spatial power spectrum as a proxy observation to prevent error accumulation during autoregressive rollouts. The work also positions itself against recent climate emulation approaches (ACE2, LUCIE, Spherical DYffusion) that achieve stable long-term projections but lack causal interpretability, and against constraint-based causal discovery methods (PCMCI) that scale poorly to high-dimensional climate data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PICABU provides a rare combination of causal interpretability, stable long-term climate emulation, and the ability to run rapid counterfactual experiments for attribution studies—capabilities that traditional ESMs offer only at enormous computational cost. The single-parent decoding assumption, while restrictive, yields latents that correspond to interpretable regional climate modes, making the model accessible to climate scientists. Key open questions include relaxing the single-parent assumption to capture more complex prediction spaces, incorporating anthropogenic forcing and multi-variable dynamics, and addressing the persistent high-frequency noise in emulated spectra. The negative R² values in out-of-distribution generalization (Table 3) suggest that causal structure helps but is insufficient for robust projection to substantially warmer climates without explicit forcing inputs.
