## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for causal effects are restricted to binary or discrete treatments and typically assume known propensity scores, making them inapplicable to the common setting of continuous treatments (e.g., drug dosing) where the propensity must be estimated from observational data. No prior work provides finite-sample validity guarantees for prediction intervals of potential outcomes under continuous treatments with unknown propensities, leaving a critical gap for safety-critical applications like personalized medicine.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250057/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained outcome prediction model φ, a trained propensity estimator π̂, a held-out calibration dataset, a new patient's covariates, a continuous treatment intervention (hard a* or soft A+Δ_A), a significance level α, and a user-specified bound M on the propensity estimation error. It outputs a conformal prediction interval for the potential outcome under the specified intervention, which can be combined via a Bonferroni-style argument to yield a valid interval for the individual treatment effect. Unlike standard split conformal prediction that simply takes a quantile of calibration scores, the interval boundary S* is found through an iterative binary search coupled with an optimization solver at each step.

## Method

The core idea is to condition the conformal calibration on the propensity shift induced by the intervention, parameterized by a tilting function f that maps the observational propensity π(a|x) to the interventional propensity. For known propensities (soft interventions), the distribution-shift-calibrated quantile regression is reformulated via strong duality into a convex optimization over dual variables η_i bounded in [−α, 1−α], yielding a tractable problem whose solution directly gives the interval boundary. For unknown propensities (hard interventions), the Dirac delta of the point intervention is approximated by a Gaussian with vanishing variance σ, and the propensity estimation error is bounded by a ratio c_a ∈ [1/M, M]; the resulting non-convex problem is shown to be Type-I invex with LICQ, making the KKT conditions both necessary and sufficient for a global optimum. The algorithm performs a binary search over the imputed non-conformity score S*, calling the optimization solver at each iteration to check the dual variable condition, and terminates when the search interval is below a tolerance ε.

## Results

On synthetic Dataset 1 with hard intervention a*=7x, the proposed CP method achieves empirical coverage of 1.00 (α=0.05), 0.90 (α=0.1), and 0.83 (α=0.2), meeting or exceeding the target 1−α in all cases. MC dropout coverage is dramatically lower at 0.19, 0.13, and 0.11 respectively. On the semi-synthetic TCGA dataset (9,659 patients, 4,000 gene features), the CP method achieves coverage of 0.968 (α=0.05, Δ=0.5) and 0.940 (α=0.05, Δ=1.5), while MC dropout reaches only 0.828 and 0.788, and the deep ensemble is near-random at 0.064 and 0.072.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MC dropout [Gal & Ghahramani, ICML 2016], the proposed CP method wins decisively on faithfulness: on Dataset 1 (a*=7x, α=0.05), CP achieves 1.00 coverage versus MC dropout's 0.19, and on TCGA (Δ=0.5, α=0.05) CP reaches 0.968 versus MC's 0.828. MC dropout consistently undercovers by 10–80 percentage points across all settings. Against the deep ensemble [Lakshminarayanan et al., NeurIPS 2017], the gap is even more extreme: ensemble coverage on TCGA is near-random (0.064 at α=0.05), making it effectively uninformative. Against Lei & Candès [JRSS-B 2021] (the only prior method with finite-sample guarantees under estimated propensities, but restricted to binary treatments), the paper reports that CP intervals on the continuous treatment have average width 0.6255 versus 3.2876 for the binarized-treatment intervals, making the proposed method roughly 5× more informative while maintaining valid coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends split conformal prediction (Papadopoulos 2002; Vovk et al. 2005) and conformalized quantile regression (Romano et al. 2019) to the causal inference setting, building directly on the distribution-shift-calibrated quantile regression framework of Gibbs et al. (2025). It departs from prior causal conformal prediction work (Lei & Candès 2021; Alaa et al. 2023; Jonkers et al. 2024) in two critical ways: (1) it handles continuous rather than binary/discrete treatments, and (2) it accounts for propensity score estimation uncertainty, which prior methods either assume known or address only asymptotically. The key technical novelty is reformulating the Dirac-delta hard intervention as a limit of Gaussian tilting functions and proving coverage via KKT conditions on a Type-I invex optimization problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provide finite-sample conformal prediction intervals for potential outcomes of continuous treatments under unknown propensity scores, filling a gap that blocks reliable causal ML in safety-critical settings like personalized dosing. The practical impact is significant: clinicians can obtain valid uncertainty ranges for treatment effects without requiring known treatment policies. Key limitations include reliance on a user-specified propensity error bound M (which must be set conservatively), conservative interval widths in low-data regions, and computational cost of the non-convex optimization that may scale poorly with large calibration sets. Open questions include extending to multiple simultaneous continuous treatments, developing more efficient solvers, and relaxing the bounded propensity error assumption.
