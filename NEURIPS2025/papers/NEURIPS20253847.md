## Problem

Existing 3D vision-language grounding benchmarks rely heavily on explicit object names in queries, enabling models to exploit semantic shortcuts (e.g., category detection) rather than genuinely interpreting spatial relationships. When object names are replaced with neutral placeholders, model performance drops sharply, confirming that current benchmarks measure recognition rather than spatial reasoning. SURPRISE3D addresses this by constructing queries that deliberately omit object names, forcing models to reason about scene geometry, viewpoint-dependent references, and relational semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253847/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The task takes a 3D point cloud scene (from ScanNet++ v2, 900+ indoor environments) and a natural language query as input, and produces a 3D segmentation mask over the point cloud as output. Each query is paired with recorded camera extrinsics and intrinsics to establish the observer's viewpoint context, which is critical for egocentric and parametric perspective queries. The dataset contains 200k+ query-mask pairs spanning 2,800+ object classes, with 89k+ human-annotated spatial reasoning queries (covering relative position, narrative perspective, parametric perspective, and absolute distance) and 110k+ LLM-generated knowledge reasoning queries (common sense and human intention).

## Method

SURPRISE3D is constructed via a dual annotation pipeline: a human-in-the-loop process for spatial reasoning (annotators select a camera viewpoint, write a spatial description that identifies a target object without naming it, and draw a 3D segmentation mask) and an LLM-augmented pipeline for knowledge reasoning (DeepSeek V3 generates candidate questions from scene metadata, followed by GPT-4o-based cleaning to remove direct object mentions and logical errors). A key design principle is the deliberate avoidance of object names in spatial queries, enforced through Gricean conversational maxims and category-specific disambiguation rules; for relative position queries, annotators must describe targets via physical attributes or spatial relations rather than category labels. The parametric perspective subset is derived by converting narrative perspective descriptions into explicit camera parameter tuples (position, rotation, up-vector), creating a complementary challenge that tests numerical spatial understanding. The paper defines the 3D-SRS benchmark protocol with mIoU and precision/recall at fixed IoU thresholds (0.25, 0.50), and evaluates five SOTA methods (MLLMfor3D, 3D-Vista, Reason3D, Intent3D, ChatScene) in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings, showing that all models perform significantly worse without explicit naming cues, with parametric perspective queries being the most challenging across all architectures.

## Results

Under zero-shot evaluation, all five baselines perform very poorly on SURPRISE3D, with the best overall A25 score of only 13.63% (MLLMfor3D) and a cross-method average of 8.27%. After fine-tuning on the dataset, performance improves substantially (~3× for spatial reasoning), with Intent3D achieving the best overall A25 of 23.98% and MLLMfor3D the best mIoU of 16.46%. Parametric perspective remains the most challenging sub-task for all models, with the best fine-tuned A25 of only 12.57% (Intent3D), while narrative perspective also proves difficult (best A25: 25.77% for 3D-Vista). These results confirm that current 3D vision-language models lack genuine spatial reasoning capability when explicit object names are removed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLLMfor3D is the strongest zero-shot performer (overall A25: 13.63%) and retains the best fine-tuned mIoU (16.46%), benefiting from its label-free 2D-to-3D mask projection paradigm. Intent3D achieves the highest fine-tuned A25 (23.98%) and dominates on knowledge reasoning tasks (common-sense A25: 30.09%, human intention A25: 31.16%), but collapses on absolute distance reasoning (A25: 5.75%). 3D-Vista is the most consistent mid-range method, leading on narrative perspective (A25: 25.77%) and relative position (A25: 23.86%) after fine-tuning, yet it cannot produce segmentation masks (no mIoU reported). Reason3D shows moderate performance across all tasks and is the only method besides MLLMfor3D that reports mIoU, reaching 11.00% overall. ChatScene is the weakest baseline across nearly all spatial reasoning sub-tasks (e.g., narrative perspective A25: 13.98%, parametric perspective A25: 4.58%), likely due to its reliance on pre-computed instance segmentation that degrades on the denser ScanNet++ scenes. No single method excels across all reasoning types, and the ~3× gap between zero-shot and fine-tuned spatial reasoning scores underscores that existing models have not internalized spatial reasoning from prior training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in 3D vision-language grounding (ScanRefer, ReferIt3D, 3D-GRAND) where queries explicitly name target objects, enabling models to exploit category-detection shortcuts rather than genuine spatial inference. It extends the nascent line of reasoning-focused 3D benchmarks (Intent3D, ScanReason, Reason3D, SegPoint) by systematically removing object names, formalizing four distinct spatial reasoning subtypes (narrative perspective, parametric perspective, relative position, absolute distance), and requiring segmentation masks rather than bounding boxes. The annotation pipeline draws on Gricean pragmatic maxims for disambiguation and on LLM-augmented generation (DeepSeek V3, GPT-4o) for knowledge-based queries, but the core spatial queries are human-authored after the authors found LLMs incapable of producing them with sufficient fidelity. The scene substrate is ScanNet++ v2, and the evaluation protocol follows standard referring-segmentation metrics (mIoU, precision/recall at IoU thresholds).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SURPRISE3D provides the first large-scale, shortcut-free benchmark for 3D spatial reasoning segmentation, exposing that state-of-the-art 3D-VL models (MLLMfor3D, 3D-Vista, Reason3D, Intent3D, ChatScene) drop to single-digit or low-double-digit scores when semantic cues are removed — a quantitative confirmation of the shortcut-bias problem that had been discussed anecdotally. The consistently poor performance on parametric perspective queries (lowest across all models and settings) highlights a specific architectural gap in handling numerical spatial parameters, pointing toward the need for learned egocentric priors or dynamic pose embeddings. Limitations include restriction to static indoor ScanNet++ scenes, scalability constraints from human annotation, and the question of whether parametric-perspective queries are natural enough for real-world deployment. The benchmark is likely to become a standard evaluation suite for embodied AI and robotic planning research.
