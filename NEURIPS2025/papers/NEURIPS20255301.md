## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods require white-box logit access or training, exclude API-based deployment, degrade text quality through probability manipulation, and struggle to generalize across languages and domains. No prior approach simultaneously achieves black-box compatibility, multilingual/domain-agnostic operation, multi-bit personalized message embedding, and quality preservation without modifying model outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255301/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a watermark key k, a multi-bit message m ∈ {0,1}^b, and black-box API access to a target LLM, while applying a pre-trained SAE to a separate anchor model (not the target LLM) for feature extraction. Text is segmented into domain-appropriate units (sentences for natural language, function blocks for code), each carrying one symbol of the watermark signal. At detection, given a candidate text and a set of keys, the system outputs a decoded message or a reject (⊥) after alignment filtering and statistical testing.

## Method

SAEMARK performs inference-time feature-based rejection sampling: for each text unit, it generates N candidate completions from the LLM and selects the one whose Feature Concentration Score (FCS) is closest to a key-derived target, without modifying any model weights, logits, or generated tokens. The FCS is computed by applying a pre-trained SAE to an anchor model, identifying the most salient feature per token (after masking ubiquitous background features), and measuring the fraction of total L1 activation mass concentrated in those salient features. Normalized FCS values are mapped through an empirical CDF to [0,1], and the watermark key seeds a PRNG to produce deterministic target sequences. Detection applies a two-stage CheckAlignment filter (range similarity and overlap rate) before Student's t-test to distinguish watermarked from unwatermarked text. The framework is extractor-agnostic with worst-case theoretical bounds showing success probability scales as 1−(1−p_min)^N, and the SAE instantiation achieves >99% per-unit success with N=50 candidates while maintaining 1.00× baseline latency through parallel generation.

## Results

SAEMARK achieves 99.7% F1 on English (C4), 99.2% F1 on Chinese (LCSTS), 66.3% F1 on code (MBPP), and 84.6% F1 on instruction-following (PandaLM) at 1% FPR, while also attaining the highest text quality score (67.6% win-rate) among all watermarking methods. In the multi-bit setting, SAEMARK outperforms the best multi-bit baseline Waterfall by 6.5 F1 points on English (99.7% vs. 93.2%) and by 54.7 points on code (66.3% vs. 11.6%). On BIGGen-Bench, SAEMARK preserves text quality closest to unwatermarked output across three backbone LLMs (e.g., 4.05 vs. 4.13 unwatermarked on Qwen2.5-7B), whereas KGW and Waterfall degrade quality more substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-bit logit-manipulation baselines (KGW, EXP, DIP, Unbiased, SynthID, UPV, Unigram), SAEMARK matches or exceeds detection accuracy on English and Chinese while dramatically outperforming on code: 66.3% F1 vs. KGW's 48.0%, EXP's 28.4%, and DIP's 36.5%. SAEMARK also achieves the highest text quality (67.6% PandaLM win-rate) compared to KGW (41.5%), EXP (23.2%), and SWEET (47.2%), confirming that selection-based watermarking avoids the quality degradation inherent in logit manipulation. Against the code-specific SWEET method, SAEMARK wins by 3.9 F1 points (66.3% vs. 62.4%) despite being a general-purpose method, while SWEET completely fails on Chinese (0.0% F1). In the multi-bit setting, SAEMARK outperforms Waterfall—the current best training-free multi-bit watermark—by 6.5 F1 points on English (99.7% vs. 93.2%) and by a striking 54.7 points on code (66.3% vs. 11.6%), demonstrating that semantic feature-based selection substantially outperforms vocabulary-permutation approaches in low-entropy domains. On BIGGen-Bench quality evaluation, SAEMARK stays within 0.08–0.16 points of unwatermarked text across three LLMs, while KGW degrades by 0.12–0.28 points and Waterfall by 0.07–0.28 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAEMARK departs fundamentally from the dominant watermarking paradigm of modifying generation (logit reweighting in KGW/EXP, vocabulary permutation in Waterfall, or rewriting via auxiliary models). Instead, it repurposes Sparse Autoencoders—originally interpretability tools for decomposing LLM activations—into deterministic feature extractors that drive a rejection-sampling selection process over naturally generated candidates. The framework is explicitly extractor-agnostic in its theoretical analysis, positioning SAEs as one effective instantiation rather than a core dependency. This places the work at the intersection of model interpretability and content provenance, a connection not previously exploited in the watermarking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: SAEMARK is the first watermarking method that simultaneously supports multi-bit personalized attribution, multilingual operation (English, Chinese, code), black-box/API-only access, and quality preservation without any model modification. This combination addresses a real deployment gap where model providers expose only APIs and organizations need fine-grained user-level attribution. Key limitations include dependence on SAE feature quality (mitigated by open-source SAE availability), inapplicability to very short outputs, and no claim of cryptographic unforgeability against key-holding adversaries. Open questions include whether the approach scales to thousands of users without degrading per-unit success probability, and whether adversarial forgers who know the SAE architecture can craft texts that mimic watermarked feature statistics.
