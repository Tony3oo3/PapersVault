## Problem

Existing long-context evaluation benchmarks face a fundamental trade-off: real-world tasks are expensive, uncontrollable, and susceptible to data contamination, while synthetic tasks (e.g., Needle-in-a-Haystack, RULER) insert semantically incoherent needles into irrelevant contexts, creating retrieval shortcuts and implicit biases toward numerical information. This paper argues that an ideal synthetic benchmark must satisfy three properties simultaneously—seamless context coherence, controllable/extensible task design, and sound exact-match evaluation—which no prior benchmark achieves.

## Input / Output

Each data point consists of a long context (2K–128K tokens) composed of artificially generated biographies, where one or more 'needle' biographies are seamlessly embedded among 'haystack' biographies, followed by a question. Outputs vary by task category: exact-match answers for understanding and reasoning tasks (e.g., a person's hobby, an age difference, a ranked list), answer-plus-citation-index pairs for the trustworthiness citation task, and a fixed refusal string ('The answer is not explicitly stated') for the IDK task. All biographies are generated on-the-fly from sampled attribute pools, ensuring zero overlap with any model's parametric knowledge.

## Method

LongBioBench constructs each data point by sampling 7 attributes (name, birthdate, birthplace, hobby, university, major, working city) from predefined pools and filling them into human-written sentence templates to produce coherent, fluent biographies. A needle bio is inserted into a haystack of other bios at a configurable position, with controllable distractor density (probability of repeating the same attribute value across bios) and number of required needles. Ten subtasks span three capability categories—understanding (Standard, Multi-Standard, Paraphrase, Pronoun), reasoning (Calculation, Rank, Multi-hop, Two-diff), and trustworthiness (Citation, IDK)—each extending the base retrieval task with a specific additional requirement. Evaluation uses deterministic exact-match scoring (all-or-nothing for multi-retrieval), avoiding LLM-as-Judge. The paper further demonstrates extensibility via an in-context learning task where models must infer a hidden university-to-category mapping from demonstrations, and analyzes how long-context continual pretraining (Qwen2.5-7B, 4K→32K tokens) primarily adjusts RoPE embeddings with only marginal capability gains.

## Results

LongBioBench achieves a 0.853 Spearman correlation with the real-world HELMET benchmark (vs. 0.559 for RULER), validating it as a stronger proxy for authentic long-context tasks. Among 18 evaluated LCLMs, GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-14B-1M, and Qwen2.5-7B-1M exceed 85% accuracy on understanding tasks, but the highest reasoning accuracy is only 66.5% and no model surpasses 90% on trustworthiness tasks. Performance degrades sharply with context length, and at 512k tokens, understanding and trustworthiness scores drop to near zero (0.3–2.7%) even for the strongest models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongBioBench is the only benchmark in Table 1 that satisfies all eight design criteria (cheap, fluent, coherent, configurable, extendable, leakage prevention, and reliable metric), whereas every prior benchmark fails on at least one. Against real-world benchmarks (L-Eval, LongBench-v2, NoCha, ∞-Bench, Helmet), LongBioBench trades authenticity for controllability and reproducibility, but compensates with a 0.853 Spearman correlation to HELMET scores across 12 overlapping models at 128k context. Against synthetic benchmarks (RULER, BABILong, Michelangelo, NoLiMa, MRCR), LongBioBench uniquely combines coherent needle-haystack integration with configurability; RULER and Michelangelo lack fluent/coherent context, BABILong and NoLiMa lack coherence, and MRCR lacks extensibility. The paper further shows that incoherent NIAH-style contexts (as in RULER) create retrieval shortcuts that inflate scores by up to 28.3% on harder tasks, and that numerical needles (used in RULER) are disproportionately easy to retrieve, biasing evaluations. On the ICL extension task, even Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-1M drops from 51.5% at 2k to 17.0% at 128k context, far above the 10% random-guess baseline but still indicating substantial room for improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two existing benchmark families for long-context evaluation: natural-language benchmarks (HELMET, NoCha, LongBench-v2) that are authentic but uncontrollable, and synthetic NIAH-style benchmarks (RULER, BABILong, Michelangelo) that are controllable but semantically incoherent. Drawing on the biography format from Allen-Zhu and Li's bioS dataset, the authors repurpose it as a controlled evaluation environment where the 'needle' (target biography) is fluent and coherent with the 'haystack' (surrounding biographies), directly addressing the shortcut problem they identify in incoherent NIAH constructions. The framework is positioned as a middle path that achieves the 0.853 Spearman correlation with HELMET versus RULER's 0.559, while retaining full configurability for ablation studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical gap between retrieval and reasoning: models that successfully locate relevant information still fail at elementary numerical reasoning and constrained planning over that information, suggesting that long-context capability is not merely a retrieval problem. The finding that long-context continual pretraining saturates within ~4K steps and primarily adjusts RoPE embeddings challenges the current industry practice of extensive long-context pretraining, implying that post-training alignment (RLHF) may be more impactful for trustworthiness. Open questions include whether the biography format generalizes to other domain-specific long-context tasks, whether the coherence advantage persists at 512K+ context lengths (where performance approaches zero), and how the benchmark's findings transfer to models with architectural differences (linear attention, hybrid Mamba-Transformer).
