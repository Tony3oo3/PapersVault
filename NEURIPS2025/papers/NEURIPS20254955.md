## Problem

Counterfactual inference at the individual level is generally unidentifiable without strong assumptions. Existing approaches either require a known structural causal model (SCM) or rely on homogeneity of exogenous variables plus strict monotonicity, while the quantile regression method of Xie et al. imposes a single-model constraint on factual and counterfactual quantile functions and requires solving a bi-level optimization per individual. This paper seeks a simpler identifiability assumption and a principled, convex loss that avoids both SCM estimation and per-individual quantile fitting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular dataset of (x_k, z_k, y_k) triples where x is a discrete treatment, z is a (potentially high-dimensional) covariate vector, and y is the factual outcome, along with a specified counterfactual treatment value x'. It outputs a single scalar counterfactual outcome y_{x'} for the queried individual. The kernel-based estimator requires a propensity score model and a learned low-dimensional representation of z to avoid the curse of dimensionality in kernel smoothing; the method is currently restricted to discrete treatments (with a stated extension to continuous treatments in the appendix).

## Method

The paper introduces a rank preservation assumption (Kendall rank correlation ρ(Y_x, Y_{x'}|Z)=1) that is strictly weaker than the prior homogeneity + strict monotonicity assumptions, and proves it identifies y_{x'} as the τ*-th quantile of P(Y|X=x',Z=z) where τ* is the quantile of the observed y in P(Y|X=x,Z=z). For estimation, the authors construct a novel ideal loss R_{x'}(t|x,z,y) = E[|Y_{x'}−t||Z=z] + E[sign(Y_x−y)|Z=z]·t, which is convex in t and uniquely minimized at the true counterfactual outcome, thereby eliminating the need for SCM estimation, a shared quantile model, or per-individual quantile optimization. A practical kernel-based estimator replaces the intractable conditional expectations with propensity-score-weighted, kernel-smoothed sample averages over a learned low-dimensional covariate representation, and a consistency result is established under standard bandwidth conditions.

## Results

On the IHDP dataset, the proposed method achieves √ε_PEHE of 1.41 (in-sample) and 1.50 (out-sample), outperforming the strongest baseline Quantile-Reg (1.43/1.56) and all other methods. On the JOBS dataset, it achieves R_Pol of 0.08 (in-sample) and 0.11 (out-sample), substantially beating Quantile-Reg (0.14/0.15) and all other baselines. On the synthetic Sim-40 dataset (out-sample), the method achieves √ε_PEHE of 3.66, compared to 4.12 for Quantile-Reg and 4.43 for ESCFR, demonstrating consistent gains across dimensions up to m=80.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 12 baselines on both IHDP and JOBS across all reported metrics. The closest competitor is Quantile-Reg [19], which also leverages quantile-based identification but requires a single shared quantile model and per-individual quantile estimation; the proposed method improves √ε_PEHE from 1.43 to 1.41 (IHDP in-sample) and from 0.14 to 0.08 in R_Pol (JOBS in-sample), a relative improvement of ~43% on JOBS. Among representation-learning baselines, ESCFR and CFRNet are the strongest on IHDP (√ε_PEHE ≈ 1.46 in-sample), but the proposed method still leads by ~0.05. CEVAE and DESCN perform substantially worse (√ε_PEHE > 2.0 on IHDP), indicating that generative and cross-network approaches struggle with individual-level counterfactual estimation. On the synthetic Sim-40 out-sample setting, the proposed method achieves √ε_PEHE of 3.66 versus 4.12 for Quantile-Reg and 4.43 for ESCFR, with the gap widening as covariate dimension increases (e.g., 9.28 vs. 11.59 on Sim-80 with ρ=0.3). The advantage is most pronounced under correlated covariates and higher heterogeneity degrees, where the rank preservation assumption provides a more flexible identification guarantee than the homogeneity assumption underlying Quantile-Reg.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the counterfactual identification framework of Xie et al. [19], which established identifiability under homogeneity and strict monotonicity assumptions via quantile regression. The key departure is replacing those two assumptions with a single, more intuitive rank preservation condition (Kendall rank correlation = 1 between factual and counterfactual outcomes), which the authors prove is strictly weaker. On the estimation side, the paper departs from both Pearl's three-step procedure (which requires SCM estimation) and quantile regression (which requires bi-level optimization and a shared model for both treatment groups) by proposing a convex ideal loss with a kernel-based empirical estimator that avoids both pitfalls.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rank preservation assumption offers a more transparent and easily verifiable identifiability condition for practitioners who cannot assume a known SCM, and the convex ideal loss guarantees a unique minimizer, simplifying optimization compared to the bi-level quantile regression approach. However, the assumption is only slightly weaker than homogeneity plus monotonicity (it merely allows U_x to be a monotone function of U_x' rather than identical), so the practical relaxation is modest. Open questions include scalability of the kernel-based estimator to high-dimensional covariates (mitigated here by learning low-dimensional representations) and instability when propensity scores are extremely small in sparse data regimes, both acknowledged by the authors as limitations.
