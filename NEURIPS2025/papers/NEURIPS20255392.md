## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning of Large Vision-Language Models on embodied reasoning datasets for robot control is limited by heuristically constructed QA pairs that fail to capture precise quantitative details needed for low-level control, and by catastrophic forgetting that degrades general capabilities. This paper asks whether reinforcement learning, specifically the generate-reason-then-answer paradigm from DeepSeek-R1, can train LVLMs to develop embodied reasoning that is both more accurate for robot control and more generalizable than SFT.

## Input / Output

The model receives a 224×224 RGB scene image from RLBench, the current end-effector Cartesian position (x, y, z), and environment metadata encoding fixed reference points, axis directions, and object dimensions for scale. It outputs a structured response containing a reasoning trace enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in answer tags, selecting the correct next waypoint from four shuffled candidates (one ground-truth, three distractors). Two auxiliary tasks produce similar MCQA outputs for current-state identification and rule-based movement direction (e.g., 'slightly move backward').

## Method

ROBOT-R1 reformulates the continuous next-keypoint prediction problem as a multiple-choice QA task to constrain the action space for RL exploration, then trains Qwen2.5-VL-7B using GRPO with a composite reward of format compliance plus exact-match answer correctness. Three QA tasks are jointly trained: waypoint prediction (primary), current-state prediction, and movement prediction (auxiliary), all constructed from 50 expert demonstrations per task across five RLBench tasks (~7.5K QA pairs total). The key design choice is that the model must generate an explicit reasoning chain before answering, and GRPO reinforces reasoning pathways that lead to correct predictions by computing per-query advantage scores over G=5 sampled responses. This RL-trained 7B model outperforms GPT-4o on low-level spatial and movement reasoning, achieves 31% improvement on EmbodiedBench Manipulation, and shows ~40–60% gains on SpatialRGPT, while SFT baselines suffer catastrophic forgetting on out-of-distribution tasks.

## Results

On the newly introduced ROBOT-R1 Bench (low-level control), the 7B-parameter ROBOT-R1 model achieves Movement 0.76 and Spatial 1.51 (out of 3), outperforming GPT-4o (0.72 / 1.43) and all other evaluated commercial models. On EmbodiedBench Manipulation, ROBOT-R1 reaches 11.68% average success rate, a ~31% relative improvement over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (8.92%), while both SFT baselines score 0%. On SpatialRGPT, ROBOT-R1 improves quantitative accuracy from 9.88% to 15.89% (~60% relative gain) and qualitative accuracy from 29.07% to 40.79% (~40% relative gain) over the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest commercial baseline, GPT-4o, ROBOT-R1 wins on both low-level control metrics (Movement 0.76 vs 0.72; Spatial 1.51 vs 1.43) but loses on high-level control (Planning 1.44 vs 1.96; High-level Action 1.30 vs 2.02), which the authors attribute to the training objective focusing on next-keypoint prediction rather than long-horizon planning. Against the SFT baselines, ROBOT-R1 shows dramatic gains: Direct SFT collapses to near-zero (0.06 Movement, 0.06 Spatial) and 0% on EmbodiedBench, while CoT SFT reaches only 0.70 Movement and 0.29 Spatial, far below ROBOT-R1's 0.76 and 1.51. On EmbodiedBench Manipulation, ROBOT-R1 (11.68% avg) outperforms the base model (8.92%) and GPT-4o-mini (4.6%), though Claude-3.5-Haiku (13.6%) remains slightly ahead. On SpatialRGPT, ROBOT-R1 surpasses the base model by ~60% in quantitative accuracy (15.89% vs 9.88%) and ~40% in qualitative accuracy (40.79% vs 29.07%), while both SFT variants degrade below the base model. Among RL algorithm ablations, GRPO and RLOO perform comparably, while REINFORCE++ underperforms due to weaker reward normalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly adapts the DeepSeek-R1 RL-for-reasoning paradigm (GRPO, generate-reasoning-then-answer) from LLM domains (math, coding) to embodied robot control, replacing the dominant SFT-based training of embodied reasoning QA datasets (Zawalski et al., Chen et al., Shi et al.). The key methodological departure is reformulating continuous next-waypoint prediction as multiple-choice QA to make the action space tractable for RL exploration, and adding auxiliary state/movement prediction tasks to ground spatial understanding. It also introduces ROBOT-R1 Bench, a new open-ended evaluation benchmark specifically targeting low-level control reasoning (movement, spatial) and high-level reasoning (planning, action) in tabletop manipulation, filling a gap left by existing VQA or simulation-based robotics benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 7B model trained with RL on ~7.5K QA pairs can outperform GPT-4o on domain-specific embodied reasoning and that SFT-trained models catastrophically fail (0% success) on EmbodiedBench while the RL model achieves 11.68%, suggesting RL is a more robust training paradigm for embodied reasoning. The emergence of high-level action reasoning from exclusively low-level control training is a notable finding with implications for curriculum design. However, the work is limited to xyz end-effector position (no rotation, no gripper state), a single robot platform (Franka Panda), and primarily simulation-based evaluation, leaving open whether the approach scales to dexterous manipulation or multi-robot settings. The LLM-as-judge evaluation (GPT-4o scoring) introduces a potential circularity concern, though human correlation analysis partially mitigates this.
