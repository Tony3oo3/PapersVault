## Problem

Existing deep constrained clustering methods fall into two flawed paradigms: end-to-end DCC relies on anchors that cause mismatches between local pairwise similarities and global cluster decisions, and requires the exact number of clusters; deep constraint embedding methods operate in Euclidean space where unbounded distances necessitate manual margin tuning and produce non-convex, poorly separable clusters. No prior method simultaneously avoids anchors, eliminates hyperparameter sensitivity, and handles an unknown cluster count.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw dataset instances (32x32 RGB images or bag-of-words text vectors) together with a set of pairwise must-link/cannot-link constraints sampled from ground-truth labels, and produces unit-normalized spherical embeddings in R^D that are clustered by standard K-means. A distinguishing output is the inferred cluster number K, obtained by detecting a plateau in tail-averaged minimal inter-cluster angles across PCA subspaces of the learned embeddings, requiring no retraining or post-hoc validation. At deployment, unseen instances are assigned to the nearest latent centroid by angular distance.

## Method

SpherePair formulates an anchor-free pairwise loss in angular space: for each constrained pair, the cosine of the inter-vector angle is mapped to a similarity score and optimized via logistic loss, with a negative-zone factor omega that enforces a minimum angular separation of pi/omega for cannot-link pairs. The loss is combined with a reconstruction objective in a deep autoencoder whose decoder receives L2-normalized latent codes, preserving angular geometry during regularization. Theoretically, omega is fixed at 2 (the minimal admissible value for any K >= 2 in sufficiently high dimension), and the embedding dimension D >= K is the sole remaining hyperparameter. Under zero angular loss, the K cluster centroids converge to vertices of a regular simplex in a (K-1)-dimensional subspace, a geometric property that the paper exploits via PCA to infer K without retraining. This angular formulation bounds all distances to [0, pi], eliminating the unbounded-margin problem of Euclidean pairwise losses while naturally balancing positive and negative constraints.

## Results

Across 72 comparisons (8 datasets × 3 constraint levels × 3 metrics), SpherePair ranks first in over 60 cases and second in nearly all others. On the most challenging dataset CIFAR-100-20 at 10k constraints, SpherePair achieves 62.8% test ACC, outperforming the next-best CIDEC (50.1%) by 12.7 points. On Reuters at 1k constraints, SpherePair reaches 91.7% test ACC versus CIDEC's 88.0%, and on RCV1-10 at 1k it achieves 91.4% versus DCGMM's 87.1%. Even in the closest contests (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 10k: 89.9% vs. CIDEC's 89.8%), the gap is within 1–2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpherePair dominates all six baselines in the vast majority of 72 comparisons, with the largest margins over AutoEmbedder (which lags by 20–40% ACC on most datasets due to its Euclidean margin-based loss) and over CIDEC/DCGMM on CIFAR-100-20 (12–16% ACC gap at 10k constraints). On easier datasets like MNIST and CIFAR-10 at high constraint levels, SpherePair still edges out CIDEC and DCGMM by 0.5–1.5% ACC, while SDEC and VolMaxDCC trail by 3–5%. A key differentiator is robustness to imbalanced constraints: as imbalance increases (IMB0→IMB2), all baselines degrade significantly, whereas SpherePair maintains stable performance. Without pretraining, SpherePair† still exceeds CIDEC† by 10–20% ACC on nearly all datasets, whereas CIDEC benefits substantially from pretraining only at 1k constraints. On the highly imbalanced RCV1-10, SpherePair achieves 91.4% test ACC at 1k constraints versus DCGMM's 87.1%, confirming its advantage under skewed constraint distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within deep constrained clustering (DCC) by introducing an angular (cosine-similarity) pairwise loss that departs from both dominant paradigms: anchor-based end-to-end methods (VanillaDCC, CIDEC, DCGMM, VolMaxDCC) and anchor-free Euclidean embedding methods (AutoEmbedder, CPAC). Conceptually, it repurposes the geometric ideas from supervised angular classification losses (SphereFace, CosFace, ArcFace) and prototype-based hyperspherical methods, but applies them for the first time to instance-level pairwise constraint satisfaction without class labels. The theoretical backbone—showing that optimal embeddings form a regular simplex in a (K−1)-dimensional subspace and that PCA reveals the intrinsic dimension—provides a principled link between the loss geometry and cluster-number inference that neither prior DCC paradigm offers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpherePair addresses two persistent practical pain points in constrained clustering: the need to pre-specify the number of clusters and the fragility of anchor-based methods under imbalanced constraints. The theoretical guarantee that ω=2 universally suffices and that D≥K is the only dimensional requirement removes a significant hyperparameter-tuning burden, making the method more deployable in real-world settings where K is unknown and constraints are unevenly distributed. However, the approach is limited to single-view unstructured data and does not handle noisy or incomplete annotations, and the cluster-number inference fails on strongly imbalanced datasets (RCV1-10). Extending SpherePair to multi-view, semi-structured data and robustifying it against annotation noise are the most immediate open questions.
