## Problem

Existing evaluations of multi-agent collaboration in medicine are confined to narrow task types (primarily multiple-choice QA) and typically compare only against single LLMs, omitting rigorous baselines from established conventional machine learning methods. This leaves unclear whether multi-agent frameworks genuinely outperform well-prompted single LLMs or specialized conventional models across the diverse, realistic range of clinical tasks that practitioners actually encounter.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254851/cropped_page3_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes four distinct input types: (1) medical text or image+text questions for QA/VQA, (2) complex biomedical articles for lay summarization, (3) structured EHR records (demographics, lab values, longitudinal visit histories) formatted as text prompts for LLM-based prediction, and (4) analytical questions paired with dataset schemas for workflow automation. Outputs range from option letters or free-form answers, single-paragraph simplified summaries, binary clinical predictions (mortality/readmission), to executable Python code with visualizations and integrated reports. For VQA, images are base64-encoded into multimodal prompts; for EHR prediction, patient data is serialized into structured text with reference ranges and units.

## Method

MedAgentBoard is a unified benchmark that systematically compares three modeling paradigms—conventional methods (fine-tuned classifiers, sequence-to-sequence models, gradient-boosted trees, and EHR-specific deep learning architectures like AdaCare and GRASP), single LLMs (with zero-shot, few-shot, CoT, and self-consistency prompting), and multi-agent collaboration frameworks (MedAgents, ReConcile, MDAgents, ColaCare, AgentSimp, SmolAgents, OpenManus, Owl)—across four task categories spanning text, medical images, and structured EHR data. The key empirical finding is that multi-agent collaboration does not universally outperform: specialized conventional VLMs (M³AE, MUMC) dominate medical VQA, fine-tuned seq2seq models (BART-CNN, PEGASUS) lead in lay summarization on ROUGE/SARI, and conventional ML/DL models (XGBoost, AdaCare) substantially outperform all LLM-based approaches in EHR prediction. Multi-agent collaboration shows its clearest advantage in clinical workflow automation, where tool-use capabilities (Python code execution) and task decomposition improve completeness over single LLMs, though overall end-to-end correctness remains modest. The benchmark uses LLM-as-a-judge for free-form QA, expert panel evaluation with inter-rater agreement (Fleiss' Kappa) for workflow tasks, and standard metrics (accuracy, AUROC, AUPRC, ROUGE-L, SARI) for the remaining tasks.

## Results

Across four medical task categories, multi-agent collaboration does not universally outperform single LLMs or conventional methods. In textual medical QA, CoT-SC prompting achieves 89.90% accuracy on MedQA (MC), while specialized conventional VLMs (MUMC: 91.40% on PathVQA MC; M³AE: 90.65%) dominate medical VQA over all LLM-based approaches. For EHR prediction, AdaCare reaches 94.28% AUROC on MIMIC-IV mortality and XGBoost 98.05% on TJH mortality, substantially exceeding the best single LLM (GPT-4o: 85.99%) and multi-agent methods (ColaCare: 82.91%). In clinical workflow automation, multi-agent frameworks (SmolAgents: 90.25% correct on MIMIC-IV data tasks) improve task completeness over single LLMs, but end-to-end correctness for modeling and reporting remains below 50% across all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In textual medical QA, advanced single-LLM prompting (CoT-SC: 89.90% MedQA) outperforms all multi-agent frameworks (MedAgents: 85.25%, ColaCare: 84.65%, MDAgents: 78.80%), while conventional models (BioLinkBERT: 32.45%, GatorTron: 36.60%) are far behind. In medical VQA, specialized conventional VLMs (MUMC: 91.40% PathVQA, M³AE: 90.65%) decisively beat both single VLMs (zero-shot: 66.90%) and multi-agent VQA (MedAgents: 75.90%), with general-purpose models like LLaVA-Med (59.25%) and Med-Flamingo (66.15%) performing poorly. For lay summary generation, fine-tuned conventional models (BART-CNN: 42.24% ROUGE-L Cochrane; PEGASUS: 59.11% PLABA) consistently outperform the best single LLM (Opt+ICL: 35.58%) and the multi-agent AgentSimp pipeline (34.25%). In EHR prediction, conventional models (AdaCare: 94.28% AUROC MIMIC-IV mortality; XGBoost: 98.05% TJH) substantially exceed GPT-4o (85.99%), DeepSeek-R1 (83.95%), and all multi-agent methods (ColaCare: 82.91%, MedAgents: 81.53%, ReConcile: 77.81%). The one area where multi-agent collaboration shows a clear advantage is clinical workflow automation, where SmolAgents (90.25% correct on MIMIC-IV data tasks) and OpenManus (64.0% on TJH modeling) reduce 'No Result' rates compared to single LLMs (50% No Result on TJH modeling), though absolute correctness for complex sub-tasks remains modest across all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-agent collaboration evaluation line (MAST, Multi-agent Debate, MedAgentsBench) by introducing a critical third comparison axis—specialized conventional methods (XGBoost, AdaCare, BART, MUMC, etc.)—that prior medical LLM benchmarks largely omitted. It also departs from the narrow focus on medical QA seen in MedHELM and MedAgentsBench by spanning four task categories (QA/VQA, lay summarization, EHR prediction, workflow automation) and three data modalities (text, medical images, structured EHR). For the lay summarization task, it adapts the AgentSimp multi-agent pipeline [59] to a medical domain, and for workflow automation it benchmarks general-purpose agent frameworks (SmolAgents, OpenManus, Owl) in a healthcare context for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that multi-agent collaboration does not universally outperform well-prompted single LLMs or, more critically, specialized conventional methods—challenges the prevailing narrative in the field and provides practitioners with a task-specific, evidence-based framework for selecting AI approaches in medicine. Key open questions include whether hybrid architectures combining conventional feature extraction with LLM reasoning can close the gap in EHR prediction and VQA, whether modality-specific multi-agent strategies (beyond text-centric discussion) can overcome the information-fidelity bottleneck in visual tasks, and how these systems should be evaluated for robustness, interpretability, and ethical safety as they approach clinical deployment. The benchmark's scope (100 curated workflow tasks, 200-sample test sets) and reliance on current-generation LLMs mean rankings may shift with model progress.
