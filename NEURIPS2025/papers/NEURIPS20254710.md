## Problem

Existing DABP methods rely on high-reliability samples to suppress negative information from low-reliability samples, which causes premature overfitting and discards valuable spatial information contained in unreliable samples. The paper asks whether the spatial relationships between samples and controlled 'hallucination' perturbations can be exploited to improve class discrimination without the overfitting pitfalls of targeted suppression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254710/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target-domain RGB images and pre-computed hard predictions from a black-box cloud API (no source data or model weights are accessed). Internally, it generates 'hallucination' versions of each image by applying attention-guided masking in the model's region of interest, and maintains a persistent memory storage M that tracks each sample's hierarchical reliability label, normalized bottleneck features, and softmax predictions. The final output is a per-sample class label from a ResNet-50/101 backbone with a 256-dim bottleneck and linear classifier.

## Method

CVH-TDN comprises three coupled modules inspired by the thalamus-driven decoupling network. Hallucination Generation (HG) computes gradient-based class-location weights and attention maps to produce progressive, attention-guided masking blocks on sharpened images, so that early training uses unmasked inputs while later training masks increasingly specific regions. Hallucination Alignment (HA) applies bidirectional contrastive losses between original and hallucination features, weighted by their cosine similarity, to encourage clustering of spatially similar samples while a backward term prevents overfitting from common features. Hallucination Calibration (HC) performs hierarchical KL-divergence clustering of low-reliability samples toward their nearest high-reliability neighbors and a common-feature-exclusion regularization that separates ambiguous high-reliability samples, all driven by a dynamic momentum schedule for adaptive label smoothing that replaces the fixed momentum used in prior DABP methods. The overall loss combines HA, HC, and a conditional self-regularization task loss, and the paper provides a generalization-bound analysis showing how the hallucination modules tighten the error gap between predicted and oracle target-domain risk.

## Results

CVH-TDN achieves state-of-the-art performance on all four DABP benchmarks. On Office-Home (ResNet-50), it reaches 78.6% mean accuracy and 73.2% hard-task mean, outperforming the previous best SEAL by 4.1% and 6.9% respectively. On VisDA-17 (ResNet-101), it achieves 90.6% mean accuracy (+1.4% over SEAL), on Office-31 (ResNet-50) it reaches 90.7% mean accuracy (+1.5% over RFC), and on DomainNet it improves by 1.6% over the prior best. The gains are most pronounced on hard tasks (source-only accuracy < 65%), where CVH-TDN outperforms BETA by up to 8.8% on Office-Home.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CVH-TDN outperforms all 9 DABP baselines (LNL-KL, HD-SHOT, SD-SHOT, DivideMix, DINE, BiMem, BETA, RFC, SEAL) and 4 UDA baselines (CDAN, MCC, HMA, CST) across all four benchmarks. The largest margin is on Office-Home, where CVH-TDN's 78.6% mean exceeds SEAL (74.5%) by 4.1% and BETA (72.1%) by 6.5%, with particularly dramatic gains on the hardest task A→C (71.7% vs. 58.5% for SEAL, +13.2%). On VisDA-17, CVH-TDN (90.6%) edges out SEAL (89.2%) by 1.4% and BETA (85.1%) by 5.5%. On Office-31, it surpasses RFC (89.2%) by 1.5% and SEAL (88.8%) by 1.9%. On DomainNet, the improvement over the prior best is 1.6%. The method consistently wins on hard tasks (H.Mean), suggesting its hallucination-based spatial learning is most effective when the black-box predictor is least reliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DINE (Liang et al., CVPR 2022), adopting its conditional self-regularization loss and modifying its adaptive label smoothing by replacing the fixed momentum with a dynamic schedule. It departs from the broader DABP lineage (BETA, RFC, SEAL, BiMem) that relies on suppressing low-reliability sample information via high-reliability aggregation, instead proposing a spatial-similarity exploration paradigm framed through a neuroscience-inspired 'controlled hallucination' mechanism. The conceptual bridge to Thalamus-driven Decoupling Network (TDN) from pathology literature is novel in the visual domain, repurposing the hallucination-cognition relationship as an analogy for attention-guided feature masking and contrastive alignment, though the underlying operations (gradient-based attention, cosine-similarity contrastive loss, KL-divergence clustering) are standard components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DABP setting addresses a real privacy constraint (cloud API access without source data or model weights), and the reported SOTA gains—particularly the 4.1% mean improvement on Office-Home and 13.2% jump on the hardest task A→C—are practically meaningful for deployment scenarios where only black-box predictions are available. However, the neuroscience framing, while creative, risks obscuring that the technical contributions are a combination of attention-guided masking, bidirectional contrastive alignment, and hierarchical KL clustering; whether the 'hallucination' metaphor yields transferable insights beyond this specific pipeline remains an open question. The paper does not report error bars or multiple random seeds, and evaluation is limited to image classification with ResNet backbones, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to segmentation, detection, or non-CNN architectures.
