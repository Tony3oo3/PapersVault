## Problem

Most generalization methods for transporting RCT treatment effects to target populations focus exclusively on the risk difference, yet clinical guidelines mandate reporting multiple absolute and relative effect measures (RR, OR, NNT, etc.) that can yield qualitatively different interpretations. No unified framework existed for identifying and estimating a broad class of first-moment causal measures under covariate shift, particularly for non-collapsible measures like the odds ratio whose transportability was previously unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RCT dataset (covariates X, binary treatment A, outcome Y) and a target population dataset (covariates X only); under the weaker exchangeability-in-effect-measure assumption, it additionally requires target population control outcomes Y^(0). It produces a transported causal effect estimate for any first-moment measure in the class (RD, RR, OR, NNT, ERR, SR, log-OR, etc.) along with closed-form asymptotic variance expressions and confidence intervals. The framework handles both continuous and binary outcomes, and the estimators are constructed via cross-fitted semiparametric procedures to enforce independence between nuisance estimation and EIF evaluation.

## Method

The paper defines a unified class of first-moment population causal measures as Φ(E[Y^(1)], E[Y^(0)]) and derives identification formulas under two transportability assumptions: exchangeability in mean (conditional outcome equality across populations) and the weaker exchangeability in effect measure (conditional treatment effect equality, requiring target control outcomes). Under each assumption, it constructs two families of estimators: classical weighting (Horvitz-Thompson) and regression (G-formula) approaches with full asymptotic normality proofs via M-estimation, and semiparametric efficient estimators built from the efficient influence function using either one-step bias correction or estimating equation solutions. A central technical finding is that for linear measures like RD the one-step and estimating equation estimators coincide, but for nonlinear measures (RR, OR) they diverge into fundamentally different estimators, with the EE variant being provably doubly robust. The framework extends identification to non-collapsible measures (OR, NNT) under the weaker assumption, which the authors note has not been previously derived.

## Results

Under exchangeability in mean with a non-linear (non-logistic) outcome model, estimating equation (EE) estimators remain unbiased across all causal measures (RD, RR, OR), while G-formula-based estimators exhibit substantial bias due to model misspecification; among one-step estimators, only the RD variant is accurate. Under exchangeability in effect measure, Section 3 estimators (wHT, wG, tG) fail to converge for RR and OR due to their non-linearity, whereas all Section 4 estimators (transported Γ-formula, EE, OS) remain unbiased. Theoretically, the paper establishes the variance ordering V_{tG}^{OLS} ≤ V_{wG}^{OLS} ≤ V_{HT} under correct linear specification, showing the transported G-formula is most efficient. On the CRASH-3/Traumabase real-world data, all estimators (except one) indicate a positive treatment effect of tranexamic acid on mortality, but confidence intervals are wide and include the null, precluding definitive conclusions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external methods from prior work in a head-to-head numerical comparison; instead, it compares its own family of estimators (weighted Horvitz-Thompson, weighted/transported G-formula, one-step, estimating equation, and transported Γ-formula) under two identification assumptions. Under exchangeability in mean with correct linear specification, the transported G-formula achieves the lowest asymptotic variance, followed by the weighted G-formula, then the weighted Horvitz-Thompson (V_{tG} ≤ V_{wG} ≤ V_{HT}). Under model misspecification (non-linear outcome), the estimating equation estimators are the only ones that remain unbiased for all measures (RD, RR, OR), thanks to their double-robustness property, while G-formula and wHT estimators are biased. For the RD specifically, one-step and EE estimators coincide (up to a scaling factor), but for RR and OR they yield fundamentally different estimators—a key finding of the paper. Under the weaker exchangeability-in-effect-measure assumption, only the Section 4 estimators (Γ-formula, EE, OS) remain valid for non-collapsible measures like the OR, while Section 3 estimators fail for RR and OR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalization/transportability literature for RCTs (Cole & Stuart 2010; Dahabreh et al. 2020; Colnet et al. 2022–2024), which has been almost exclusively focused on the risk difference, to a unified framework covering all first-moment population causal measures (RD, RR, OR, NNT, and a dozen others). It builds on Colnet et al. (2023), who studied which measures are easier to generalize via collapsibility, but departs by providing concrete identification formulas, classical estimators (weighted HT, G-formula), and semiparametric efficient estimators (one-step, estimating equation) for every measure in the class under two distinct transportability assumptions. The semiparametric machinery draws on the EIF framework (Kennedy 2022) and cross-fitting (Chernozhukov et al. 2017), while the M-estimation-based asymptotic analysis for density-ratio-weighted estimators is, to the authors' knowledge, novel even for the RD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Clinical guidelines and regulatory bodies mandate reporting multiple effect measures, yet no prior framework provided a unified estimation strategy for transporting all of them from an RCT to a target population. The practical finding that one-step and estimating equation estimators diverge for nonlinear measures (RR, OR) gives researchers a concrete reason to prefer the doubly-robust EE estimators, and the identification of non-collapsible measures under the weaker exchangeability-in-effect-measure assumption (requiring only target control outcomes) opens a new identification pathway. Limitations include the need for access to target control outcomes under the weaker assumption, the sensitivity of all estimators to nuisance model misspecification, and inconclusive real-world results (wide CIs on CRASH-3/Traumabase). Open questions include extending to multi-RCT settings, continuous outcomes, and time-to-event data.
