## Problem

Existing structural and positional encodings for graph neural networks are computed exclusively at the graph level, failing to capture higher-order relational information inherent in hypergraph representations. While hypergraph neural network architectures have been proposed, they rely on message-passing schemes that may not effectively encode multi-way structure, and reparametrizing hypergraphs via clique expansion introduces redundant pairwise edges that obscure higher-order patterns. The paper asks whether hypergraph-level encodings can augment graph-level architectures to recover this lost information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255782/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a graph (with node features and pairwise edges) or a hypergraph (with node features and hyperedge incidence matrix) as input. Graphs are 'lifted' to hypergraphs by converting each clique into a single hyperedge, while hypergraphs are 'clique expanded' into graphs for use with graph-level architectures. Encodings are computed as a fixed preprocessing step and appended to node feature vectors; the augmented features are then fed into standard GNN or transformer architectures that produce node embeddings, graph embeddings, or classification/regression predictions.

## Method

The paper defines four hypergraph-level encodings computed from classical hypergraph characteristics: (1) H-k-LAPE using eigenvectors of the Hodge Laplacian, (2) H-k-RWPE using Equal-Nodes and Equal-Edges random walk return probabilities on hypergraphs, (3) HCP summarizing Forman or Ollivier Ricci curvature over all hyperedges incident to each node, and (4) H-LDP capturing the degree distribution over a node's hypergraph neighborhood. Each encoding is proven to be strictly more expressive than its graph-level counterpart and the 1-WL test, with explicit counterexamples from the BREC dataset. Empirically, the encodings are most effective when paired with graph-level architectures (GCN, GIN, GraphGPS) applied to clique expansions or lifted graphs, yielding over 10% accuracy gains on social network datasets, while providing only marginal benefit to native hypergraph-level architectures like UniGCN.

## Results

On social network datasets, hypergraph-level encodings yield the largest performance gains: GCN with EN H-19-RWPE improves Imdb accuracy from 48.10% (no encoding) to 73.84% (+25.7 pp) and Reddit from 67.87% to 83.30% (+15.4 pp). On Collab, GCN with HCP-FRC reaches 72.03% versus 61.94% without encodings (+10.1 pp). For the hybrid GraphGPS architecture, hypergraph encodings similarly boost performance, with EN H-19-RWPE achieving 84.25% on Reddit and 51.28% on Enzymes, outperforming all graph-level encoding baselines. On hypergraph-native datasets (Cora-CA, Citeseer, Pubmed, DBLP), GCN with hypergraph encodings consistently outperforms the hypergraph-level UniGCN architecture, which shows no benefit from any encoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against graph-level encodings (LCP-FRC, LCP-ORC, 19-RWPE, 20-LAPE from Fesser & Weber 2024, Dwivedi et al. 2021, Kreuzer et al. 2021, Cai & Wang 2018), the proposed hypergraph-level encodings are consistently at least as effective and often substantially better: on Imdb, EN H-19-RWPE (73.84%) beats the best graph-level encoding LCP-ORC (67.93%) by nearly 6 pp, and on Reddit, EN H-19-RWPE (83.30%) surpasses LCP-ORC (80.75%) by 2.5 pp. Against the hypergraph-level architecture UniGCN (Huang & Yang 2021), GCN with hypergraph encodings dominates: on Cora-CA, GCN(HCP-ORC) reaches 79.25% versus UniGCN's best of 75.03%, and on DBLP, GCN(Hodge H-20-LAPE) achieves 77.48% versus UniGCN's best of 71.26%. Against GIN (Xu et al. 2018), which is provably as expressive as 1-WL, hypergraph encodings still provide significant gains (e.g., on Reddit, GIN with LCP-ORC reaches 87.19% vs. 75.38% without), confirming benefits beyond expressivity. Against GraphGPS (Rampášek et al. 2022), hypergraph encodings outperform graph-level ones on nearly all datasets, with EE H-19-RWPE achieving the best Enzymes score (51.83%) and EN H-19-RWPE the best Reddit score (84.25%). The paper also shows that on the BREC expressivity benchmark, hypergraph-level encodings distinguish 91–100% of 1-WL-indistinguishable graph pairs in the Basic and Strongly Regular categories, versus 0% for their graph-level counterparts (LDP, LCP-FRC, 2-RWPE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of graph-level structural and positional encodings (LCP, RWPE, LAPE) to the hypergraph domain, building directly on Fesser & Weber's Local Curvature Profiles, Coupette et al.'s hypergraph random walks and Ollivier-Ricci curvature, and Leal et al.'s Forman-Ricci curvature on hypergraphs. It departs from the dominant paradigm in topological deep learning—which has focused on extending message-passing and transformer architectures to hypergraphs (UniGCN, UniGIN, HGNN, PhenomNN)—by instead proposing a data-augmentation route: computing hypergraph-level encodings as preprocessing features fed into standard graph-level architectures. The expressivity analysis builds on the 1-WL/GIN equivalence framework of Xu et al. and the BREC benchmark of Wang & Zhang to prove strict expressivity gains over both unencoded MPGNNs and graph-level encodings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication—that a simple GCN or GraphGPS augmented with hypergraph-level encodings can outperform dedicated hypergraph architectures on naturally hypergraph-structured data—offers a low-cost, architecture-agnostic recipe for higher-order relational learning. However, the field still lacks benchmarks of genuinely multi-way data (most datasets are graphs lifted to hypergraphs or vice versa), and the negative result that hypergraph encodings do not help hypergraph-level architectures remains unexplained. Open questions include extending these encodings to simplicial and polyhedral complexes, understanding why hypergraph architectures fail to exploit the additional structural information, and developing scientific-domain benchmarks where multi-way interactions arise naturally.
