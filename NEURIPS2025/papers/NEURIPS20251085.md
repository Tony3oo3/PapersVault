## Problem

Masked Generative Models (MGMs) achieve strong image synthesis quality but require many iterative decoding steps, each incurring O(N²) full-attention cost over all tokens due to bidirectional attention. Reducing the number of steps degrades quality because simultaneously decoded tokens are sampled from independent univariate marginals, failing to capture inter-token dependencies. The paper asks whether per-step computation can be reduced while preserving the benefits of fine-grained iterative generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251085/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditioning signal (class label for ImageNet256 class-conditional generation, or a text prompt for 512×512 text-to-image) and a fully masked sequence of N visual tokens (discrete VQ indices or continuous latent embeddings) as input, and produces a complete RGB image decoded from the generated token sequence. The token sequence is consumed iteratively: starting from all-masked, a subset of tokens is unmasked at each step, with the growing decoded context conditioning subsequent predictions. The method is applied at 256×256 (ImageNet) and 512×512 (MJHQ-30k) resolutions across decoder-only and encoder-decoder transformer architectures.

## Method

ReCAP (Reused Context-Aware Prediction) is a plug-and-play inference module that interleaves expensive Full-FE steps (full O(N²) attention over all tokens) with cheap Local-FE steps (O(n̂_t·N) attention, recomputing QKV only for the small target subset while reusing cached key-value pairs for unchanged context tokens). The key empirical insight, validated via cosine-similarity analysis on 50K ImageNet samples, is that context token representations change minimally when only a few tokens are updated, and this stability increases as decoding progresses. The decoding is organized into T groups, each containing one Full-FE (which refreshes the KV cache) followed by l_t Local-FE sub-steps that decode disjoint token subsets using the cached context. No architectural modifications or additional training are required; the method is applied identically to discrete MGMs (MaskGIT, MAGE) and continuous-valued MGMs (MAR), achieving up to 2.4× speedup on MAR-Huge with negligible FID degradation.

## Results

On ImageNet256 class-conditional generation with CFG, MAR-H+ReCAP (96+32)×2 achieves FID 1.57 (IS 300.6) in 1.00s per image, matching the original MAR-H at 256 steps (FID 1.56) while being 2.4× faster (2.40s → 1.00s). MAR-L+ReCAP (64+20)×2 reaches FID 1.80 in 0.33s, outperforming the 64-step MAR-L baseline (FID 1.83) and matching REPA† (FID 1.80, 0.35s). On the MJHQ-30k text-to-image benchmark at 512×512, Harmon-0.5B+ReCAP (32+32) achieves FID 6.467 in 0.347s versus the 64-step baseline's FID 6.461 in 0.657s, yielding a ~2× speedup with negligible quality loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAR-H+ReCAP (96+32)×2 achieves FID 1.57 in 1.00s, matching the original MAR-H at 256 steps (FID 1.56, 2.40s) with a 2.4× speedup and only 0.01 FID degradation. MAR-L+ReCAP (64+20)×2 (FID 1.80, 0.33s) outperforms the 64-step MAR-L baseline (FID 1.83, 0.31s) and ties REPA† (FID 1.80, 0.35s) while being slightly faster. Against the autoregressive VAR-d30 (FID 1.92, 0.04s), MAR+ReCAP wins on generation quality (1.57 vs. 1.92 FID) but VAR remains substantially faster due to native KV caching. In the w/o CFG setting, MaskGIT-r+ReCAP (22+10) achieves FID 3.98 in 0.137s versus MaskGIT-r at 32 full steps (FID 3.97, 0.189s), and outperforms StraIT (FID 3.97, no time reported) and MaskGIT+AutoNAT (FID 4.45, 0.07s) at comparable or lower cost. For unconditional generation, MAGE+ReCAP (80+48) reaches FID 7.14 in 1.018s, approaching MAGE at 128 steps (FID 7.12, 1.56s) with 35% less time. On the 512×512 T2I benchmark, Harmon+ReCAP (32+32) achieves FID 6.467 in 0.347s versus the 64-step baseline (FID 6.461, 0.657s), confirming generalization to higher-resolution multimodal settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the KV caching mechanism from autoregressive transformers (Shazeer, 2019) to the fundamentally different setting of bidirectional masked generative models, where all tokens attend to all others and no causal structure exists to make caching naturally safe. It builds directly on the MGM lineage—MaskGIT, MAGE, and MAR—by identifying that context token features remain stable when only a small subset of tokens is updated between steps, and formalizes this as a grouped decoding pipeline that interleaves full and partial (Local-FE) evaluations. Unlike prior efficiency work on MGMs (AutoNAT, AdaNAT) that search for better sampling schedules, ReCAP is orthogonal: it reduces per-step cost rather than reducing the number of steps, and requires no retraining or architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.4× speedup for MAR-Huge with only 0.01 FID degradation is practically significant for deploying SOTA masked generative models in latency-sensitive settings, and the plug-and-play nature means any existing MGM checkpoint can benefit immediately. The paper's limitation is that ReCAP's gains depend on the base model already improving with more decoding steps, and the stability assumption holds best in high-step regimes, making it less impactful for models that peak at very few steps. Open directions include adaptive insertion of cheap steps via learning, principled combination of full and partial evaluation outputs, and extending the low-cost-step concept beyond attention reuse to other components of the generation pipeline.
