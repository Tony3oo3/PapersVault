## Problem

Hyperbolic deep learning suffers from three intertwined issues: overfitting due to the manifold's high representational capacity, numerical instability when curvature is treated as a learnable parameter (causing NaN crashes because prior projections and momenta become misaligned with the new geometry), and prohibitive computational cost from lack of optimized CUDA kernels. Existing Riemannian optimizers update curvature as a plain Euclidean scalar without adjusting the dependent hyperbolic parameters, gradients, or momentum vectors, creating geometric inconsistencies that degrade training.

## Input / Output

The framework is domain-agnostic but is instantiated across five settings: RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet) for classification and VAE generation, EEG time series X ∈ R^{C×T} for per-subject classification, image triplets for hierarchical metric learning (Recall@k), and graph adjacency structures for node classification. In all cases, hyperbolic parameters (class prototypes, batchnorm means, hierarchical proxies) are constrained to the upper sheet of the Lorentz hyperboloid L_K^n, and the curvature K itself is a learnable scalar that changes the manifold geometry during training. Outputs are standard class labels, similarity rankings, generated images, or node embeddings, but the internal representations exploit the exponentially growing distance metric of negative-curvature space.

## Method

The paper introduces a curvature-aware optimization schema that staggers parameter updates per timestep: hyperbolic parameters are updated first on the old curvature, then Euclidean parameters, then the curvature scalar itself; afterward, a tangent-based manifold mapping (logarithmic map to the tangent space at the old origin, then exponential map onto the new manifold) re-projects all hyperbolic parameters, gradients, and momentum vectors while preserving distances and angles relative to the origin. For regularization, the authors derive Riemannian AdamW by reformulating the weight-decay step as a weighted Lorentzian centroid between the parameter and the manifold origin, avoiding expensive parallel transport. A flexible tanh-based distance rescaling function, applied on the tangent plane of the origin with a factorized closed-form for the spatial components, constrains all hyperbolic vectors within the maximum numerically stable radius (≈9.1 in fp32), replacing brittle parameter clipping. Finally, a convolution trick parameterizes Lorentz linear layers as a rotation followed by a boost, allowing the spatial computation to reuse standard CUDA convolution kernels and yielding ~48% memory and ~66% runtime reductions over prior fully hyperbolic ResNets.

## Results

On ResNet-50 image classification, HECNN+ achieves 80.86% accuracy on CIFAR-100 and 67.18% on Tiny-ImageNet, outperforming the Euclidean baseline (78.52%/66.23%) and the original HECNN (79.83%/66.30%) while reducing VRAM from 15.6 GB to 8.1 GB and runtime from 300 s to 100 s per epoch. In hierarchical metric learning, LHIER improves Recall@1 by 1.49–4.71% over HIER across CUB, Cars, and SOP. For EEG classification, HyperMAtt achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on SSVEP (68.12%) and AUC on ERN (77.98%), with a 42× speedup over MAtt. The fully hyperbolic VAE (HCNN+L) reduces reconstruction FID to 57.69 on CIFAR-100 and 52.73 on CelebA, the best among all compared models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In metric learning (Table 1), LHIER outperforms all four baselines—NSoftmax, ProxyNCA++, Hyp, and HIER—on every dataset and metric, with the largest gain of +4.71% R@1 over HIER on CUB. In EEG classification (Table 2), HyperMAtt surpasses all 12 baselines (ShallowConvNet, EEGNet, SCCNet, EEG-TCNet, TCNet-Fusion, FBCNet, MBEEGSE, Inception, and MAtt) on SSVEP and ERN, though it trails MAtt by 0.78% on the MI dataset. For image classification (Tables 3, 5), HECNN+ and HCNN+ consistently beat the Euclidean, HECNN, HCNN, Hybrid Poincaré, and Poincaré ResNet baselines on both CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet while using 25–50% less memory and 18–66% less runtime. In VAE generation (Table 4), HCNN+(L) achieves the lowest reconstruction and generation FID on both CIFAR-100 and CelebA compared to Euclidean, Hybrid(P), Hybrid(L), and HCNN(L). For graph node classification (Table 9), the full configuration (learnable curvature + RAdamW + tangent mapping) matches or exceeds HyboNet [6] on Disease (94.82 vs 96), Airport (93.6 vs 90.9), PubMed (78.3 vs 78), and Cora (82.1 vs 80.2), with the largest margin on Airport (+2.7%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Riemannian optimization framework of GeoOpt (Kochurov et al., 2020) by identifying and fixing a fundamental geometric inconsistency: when curvature is treated as a learnable Euclidean parameter, prior projections, gradient momentums, and hyperbolic parameters become misaligned with the updated manifold. The authors adopt the tangent-space mapping idea from Fu et al. (2021) and Guo et al. (2021) for curvature transitions, and derive a Riemannian AdamW by reformulating weight decay as a Lorentzian centroid operation (Law et al., 2019) rather than subtraction. On the architecture side, the work directly extends Bdeir et al. (2024) fully hyperbolic ResNets and Kim et al. (2023) HIER metric learning to the Lorentz model, while replacing expensive manual matrix multiplications with a Lorentz boost + rotation parameterization that reuses standard CUDA convolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The curvature-aware staggered update scheme is a general, drop-in fix for any Riemannian optimizer that learns manifold curvature, removing the need for burn-in epochs, separate curvature optimizers, and extremely small learning rates that prior work required. The practical impact is significant: a 42× per-epoch speedup in EEG classification and ~48% memory reduction in image classification make hyperbolic models far more accessible, while the flexible tanh rescaling enables fp16 training that was previously infeasible. Open questions remain around the computational cost of hyperbolic operations at scale, the theoretical analysis of approximation errors in exponential/logarithmic maps, and whether the tangent-based mapping is always optimal compared to task-specific alternatives.
