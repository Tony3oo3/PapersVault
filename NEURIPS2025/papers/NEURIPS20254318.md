## Problem

Existing Theory of Mind (ToM) methods either rely on LLM prompting, which produces systematic errors in complex scenarios, or use handcrafted, domain-specific agent models for Bayesian inverse planning, which are robust but cannot generalize across diverse contexts. No unified method exists that automatically constructs the appropriate agent model and performs model-based mental inference for any ToM question in any domain without manual specification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254318/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AutoToM takes a ToM question (asking about a specific mental variable of a target agent) along with a story context in text form; for multimodal benchmarks, video frames are first fused into text via prior information-fusion methods. The output is a full posterior probability distribution over the target variable's hypothesis space (e.g., belief or goal), from which a point answer and calibrated confidence estimate are derived. In the embodied assistance setting, the method produces sequential goal hypotheses with confidence levels at each timestep to drive an uncertainty-aware helping planner.

## Method

AutoToM consists of two coupled components forming a self-improvement loop. First, Automated Bayesian Inverse Planning uses an LLM as a computational backend to sample a small set of hypotheses for each latent mental variable, estimate local conditionals, and compute the posterior via explicit marginalization over non-target variables—requiring no assumptions about model structure or variable representations. Second, Automated Agent Model Discovery iteratively proposes and refines a Bayesian-network agent model guided by a utility function U(M,q) = −H(P(q|X)) − α|M|, which trades off inference confidence (negative posterior entropy) against model complexity (number of latent variables). Model refinement proceeds via two operations: variable adjustment (introducing goal, belief, observation, or interactive-state variables following standard MDP/POMDP/I-POMDP causal structures) and timestep adjustment (expanding the context window backward when the current window is insufficient). The loop terminates when utility exceeds a threshold or no further improvement is possible, yielding a parsimonious model that confidently answers the query. For online embodied inference, the framework is wrapped in a Sequential Monte Carlo scheme that maintains K weighted goal hypotheses updated as new observations arrive.

## Results

On the overall average across five ToM benchmarks (ToMi, BigToM, MMToM-QA, MuMA-ToM, Hi-ToM), AutoToM with a GPT-4o backend achieves 82.43% accuracy, outperforming the strongest large reasoning model (Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking, 74.18%) by 8.2 points and its own LLM backend (GPT-4o, 63.39%) by 19.0 points. The largest gains appear on MMToM-QA (83.00% vs. 64.67% for o3-mini-high) and MuMA-ToM (81.44% vs. 82.56% for Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking, a near-tie). In the embodied assistance experiment (O-WAH), AutoToM achieves a 27.7% speedup over the helper agent's baseline, compared to 6.8% for GPT-4o and 6.3% for random goal sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoToM (82.43% overall) decisively outperforms all LLM baselines: GPT-4o (63.39%), Llama 3.1 70B (56.89%), Gemini 2.0 Pro (65.76%), and Gemini 2.0 Flash (60.91%), with the largest gap on MMToM-QA (83.00% vs. 44.00% for GPT-4o). Against large reasoning models, AutoToM beats Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking (74.18%) by 8.2 points and o3-mini-high (73.94%) by 8.5 points overall, though it slightly trails o3-mini-high on Hi-ToM (72.50% vs. 75.00%) and DeepSeek-R1 on ToMi (88.30% vs. 89.40%). Compared to prior model-based methods, AutoToM substantially outperforms BIP-ALM (42.10%) and LIMP (48.94%), demonstrating that automated model discovery resolves the domain-specificity limitation of handcrafted BIP implementations. Among ToM prompting methods, AutoToM surpasses SimToM (65.41%) by 17 points overall, while SymbolicToM achieves 98.60% on ToMi (its designed domain) but cannot generalize to other benchmarks. In the cognitive studies experiment, AutoToM's confidence estimates correlate with human judgments at r=0.88–0.93, far exceeding GPT-4o (r=0.04–0.81) and o3-mini-high (r=0.03–0.97) on partially observable tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoToM extends the Bayesian Inverse Planning (BIP) framework for Theory of Mind (Baker et al. 2009, 2017) by automating the agent model construction step that prior BIP+LLM methods (BIP-ALM, LIMP) require to be manually specified per domain. It departs from LLM prompting approaches (SimToM, SymbolicToM, thought-tracing) by constructing explicit Bayesian network structures over mental variables rather than relying on free-form chain-of-thought, and from thought-tracing specifically by adjusting model structure (adding variables, expanding timesteps) rather than merely reweighting fixed hypotheses. The I-POMDP formulation (Gmytrasiewicz & Doshi 2005) and Sequential Monte Carlo (Del Moral et al. 2006) provide the theoretical scaffolding for recursive reasoning and online inference, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoToM is the first fully automated model-based ToM method that eliminates manual model specification, enabling a single framework to handle diverse mental variables, agent counts, recursion depths, and modalities without domain-specific engineering. The finding that a structured Bayesian method with a standard LLM backend outperforms large reasoning models (o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1) by ~8 points challenges the assumption that more elaborate chain-of-thought reasoning is necessary for structured inference tasks. Key limitations include the need for a separate multimodal fusion step before inference, occasional failure to identify relevant mental variables during model adjustment, and reliance on an LLM for all local conditional estimation. Open questions include whether native multimodal integration, implicit model proposal, and learned (rather than LLM-estimated) conditionals can further reduce cost while maintaining robustness.
