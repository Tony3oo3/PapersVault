## Problem

Existing rotation-matrix optimization methods for LLM quantization (e.g., SpinQuant, OSTQuant) treat orthogonal matrices as learnable parameters and fine-tune them end-to-end with task-specific losses, incurring hundreds of GiB of GPU memory and tens of GPU hours for 70B models while remaining prone to overfitting on small calibration sets. This conflicts with the fast-deployment goal of post-training quantization and makes rotation calibration infeasible on consumer hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254819/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM and a small calibration set (128 WikiText2 sequences of 2048 tokens) and produces two learned orthogonal rotation matrices (R1, R2 of dimension C_in × C_in) plus two fixed random Hadamard matrices (R3, R4). The learned rotations are absorbed into adjacent weight matrices at no additional inference cost, while R3 and R4 are applied online via fast Hadamard kernels. The final output is a fully quantized model (e.g., W4A4KV16) ready for deployment, with the latent parameter Z discarded after calibration.

## Method

DartQuant redefines rotation optimization as a distribution calibration problem: instead of minimizing a task-specific loss end-to-end, it searches for the orthogonal matrix that transforms activation distributions toward a uniform shape, reducing reliance on calibration data and mitigating overfitting. The key loss is the Whip function, Whip = Σ exp(−|x_i|), which is continuously differentiable with large gradients near zero, pushing small-magnitude activations outward and compressing outliers inward (leveraging norm invariance of rotations) to flatten the Laplace-like activation peak into a near-uniform distribution. For enforcing orthogonality, QR-Orth replaces expensive Cayley/Riemannian optimizers by parameterizing the rotation as R = QR(Z) via Householder QR decomposition of a free latent matrix Z, reducing per-step overhead from ~6n³ to ~4n³ flops and enabling standard SGD/Adam. This combination yields a 47× speedup and 10× memory reduction over SpinQuant/OSTQuant on 70B models, completing calibration in ~3 hours on a single RTX 3090.

## Results

On Llama-2 70B under W4A4KV16, DartQuant achieves an average PPL of 11.51 and 69.02% zero-shot accuracy, with only a 0.5% performance loss relative to full precision. On the harder-to-quantize Llama-3 70B (W4A4KV16), DartQuant limits the average performance loss to 3.31%, outperforming SpinQuant by 3.33% and OSTQuant by 1.45% on zero-shot tasks. In terms of calibration efficiency, DartQuant completes rotation optimization for the 70B model in 0.91 GPU-hours (A800) or ~3 hours on a single RTX 3090, achieving a 47× speedup and 10× memory reduction over SpinQuant and OSTQuant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QuaRot (random Hadamard rotation), DartQuant consistently achieves lower PPL and higher zero-shot accuracy across all models and bit-widths, confirming that learned rotations outperform fixed Hadamard transforms. Compared to SpinQuant (end-to-end Cayley fine-tuning), DartQuant matches or slightly improves PPL while substantially outperforming on zero-shot tasks—e.g., on Llama-3 70B W4A4KV16, DartQuant scores 69.39% vs. SpinQuant's 66.06%—indicating that end-to-end fine-tuning overfits to the calibration set. Against OSTQuant (orthogonal + scaling with KL-top loss), DartQuant again wins on zero-shot accuracy (69.39% vs. 67.94% on Llama-3 70B W4A4KV16) while remaining competitive on PPL. Against scaling-based methods (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant) and GPTQ, DartQuant is dramatically superior at 4-bit activation quantization, where those methods suffer catastrophic PPL degradation (e.g., SmoothQuant reaches PPL >3000 on Llama-2 7B W4A4KV16). In terms of calibration cost, DartQuant requires only 0.91 GPU-hours and 23.47 GiB for the 70B model versus 42.90 GPU-hours and 238.89 GiB for SpinQuant, a 47× speedup and 10× memory saving.

## Relation to Prior Work

DartQuant builds directly on the computational invariance framework introduced by QuaRot, which showed that orthogonal rotation matrices can be inserted into Transformer blocks without altering outputs, and on SpinQuant, which made these rotation matrices learnable parameters optimized end-to-end with the Cayley optimizer. The key departure is a reframing of the rotation optimization problem: rather than minimizing a task-specific loss (perplexity or KL divergence) through end-to-end fine-tuning, DartQuant treats rotation calibration as a distribution-shaping problem, using a novel Whip loss derived from the Laplace-to-Uniform CDF transformation. Additionally, QR-Orth replaces Riemannian/Cayley optimizers on the Stiefel manifold with a simple QR decomposition step, decoupling orthogonality enforcement from the optimization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 47× speedup and 10× memory reduction make rotation-based quantization of 70B models feasible on a single consumer GPU (RTX 3090, ~3 hours), dramatically lowering the barrier to deploying quantized LLMs in resource-constrained settings. The finding that end-to-end fine-tuning of rotation matrices overfits on small calibration sets—hurting zero-shot generalization while improving perplexity—challenges the default assumption that task-specific loss optimization is always preferable. Open questions include whether the Laplace-distribution assumption holds for all model families and architectures (e.g., Mamba, state-space models), and whether the distribution-calibration paradigm extends to other transformation types beyond orthogonal rotations (e.g., affine or diagonal scaling).
