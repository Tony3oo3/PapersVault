## Problem

Existing sequence-model-based offline safe RL methods (e.g., Constrained Decision Transformer) condition action generation symmetrically on return-to-go and cost-to-go tokens, treating both as equivalent flexible targets. However, RTG is a performance objective to pursue while CTG is a rigid safety boundary to enforce, and this conflation leads to sparse, boundary-aligned supervision that fails to generalize when the policy encounters out-of-distribution cost trajectories, resulting in unreliable constraint satisfaction.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a fixed-length context window of K timesteps containing state vectors, RTG tokens, realigned CTG tokens (all shifted so the initial CTG equals the deployment threshold κ), and prior action tokens, encoded with rotary positional embeddings. It outputs a Gaussian action distribution N(μ_t, σ_t²) at each step, sampled autoregressively during inference. The unusual design choice is that CTG is not a free variable at deployment: it is locked to κ, and the model must learn to associate this single boundary token with the diverse behaviors spanning the entire safe region rather than a narrow band near the constraint.

## Method

B2R introduces region-wide supervision through three interlocking components: (1) trajectory filtering retains only trajectories with cumulative cost C(τ) ≤ κ, (2) CTG realignment applies a uniform shift C'_t = C_t + (κ − C(τ)) to every safe trajectory so that C'_0 = κ while preserving the original temporal cost profile, and (3) rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) encode relative temporal structure to capture step-by-step cost dynamics. The key insight is decoupling the conditioning signal (a single fixed boundary token κ) from the supervision signal (diverse state-action sequences drawn from the full safe region), transforming sparse boundary-conditional learning into dense region-conditional behavior cloning. The transformer is trained with a standard negative log-likelihood loss on the realigned dataset, and at inference the model is queried with C'_0 = κ and updated CTG via C'_{t+1} = C'_t − c_t. Theoretical analysis provides both a high-probability safety guarantee (via Azuma-Hoeffding concentration on cumulative cost prediction error) and a reward lower bound showing B2R cannot underperform boundary supervision.

## Results

On the DSRL benchmark (38 tasks across SafetyGymnasium, BulletSafetyGym, and MetaDrive), B2R satisfies safety constraints (normalized cost < 1) in 35 out of 38 tasks and achieves the highest normalized reward in 20 tasks. B2R ranks first in average score across all three benchmark suites: SafetyGymnasium (reward 0.42, cost 0.73), BulletSafetyGym (reward 0.62, cost 0.50), and MetaDrive (reward 0.72, cost 0.62). The three tasks where B2R fails (CarCircle1, CarCircle2, AntCircle) are also failed by most baselines due to insufficient safe data in the offline dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

B2R outperforms CDT (the closest Transformer-based competitor) on all three suites: on SafetyGymnasium, B2R achieves lower cost (0.73 vs. 1.06, making CDT unsafe) while maintaining competitive reward (0.42 vs. 0.54); on MetaDrive, B2R nearly doubles the reward (0.72 vs. 0.42) at comparable cost. Against BC-Safe, B2R improves reward substantially across all suites (e.g., 0.72 vs. 0.18 on MetaDrive) while keeping cost in a similar safe range. B2R also surpasses TraC on SafetyGymnasium (reward 0.42 vs. 0.40, cost 0.73 vs. 0.92) and MetaDrive (reward 0.72 vs. 0.35), though TraC achieves lower cost on MetaDrive (0.05 vs. 0.62). Value-based baselines (BCQ-Lag, CPQ, COptiDICE) are consistently unsafe (cost > 1) on at least two of three suites, confirming that B2R's sequence-modeling approach with region-wide supervision provides a more reliable safety-performance balance. In the extended comparison (Table 2), B2R also outperforms recent methods FISOR, OASIS, LSPC, and FAWAC on average reward (0.63 vs. 0.41–0.57) while maintaining the lowest average cost (0.33).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Decision Transformer (DT) paradigm for offline RL and its safety-constrained variant, CDT, which conditions action generation on both return-to-go and cost-to-go tokens. The key departure is identifying what the authors call a 'symmetry fallacy': prior DT-style methods treat RTG (a flexible performance target) and CTG (a rigid safety boundary) as equivalent conditioning signals, leading to sparse, boundary-aligned supervision. B2R resolves this by introducing CTG realignment—shifting all safe trajectories' cost-to-go to a fixed deployment threshold—thereby converting narrow boundary supervision into dense region-wide supervision, combined with trajectory filtering and rotary positional embeddings for temporal modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why DT-style methods struggle with safety constraints and offers a simple, effective fix (uniform CTG offset) that practitioners can adopt with minimal architectural changes. The theoretical guarantees (probabilistic and expected-cost safety bounds) add rigor to what is otherwise a data-manipulation technique. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned realignment strategies can further improve performance in high-risk states, how the method scales to multi-constraint or stochastic cost settings, and whether the reliance on sufficient safe trajectory coverage limits applicability in extremely data-scarce environments.
