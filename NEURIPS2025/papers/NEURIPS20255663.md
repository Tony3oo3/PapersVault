## Problem

Existing semi-supervised medical image segmentation (SSMIS) frameworks use SAM only as an auxiliary pseudo-label generator rather than as the backbone, limiting its full segmentation potential and making them dependent on unreliable prompts. Additionally, conventional semi-supervised strategies such as Mean Teacher are incompatible with ViT-based SAM architectures due to weaker inductive biases, yielding only marginal gains from unlabeled data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes medical images (CT or MRI) at 224×224 or 512×512 resolution and produces pixel-level segmentation masks without requiring any explicit prompts (points, boxes, or masks). Segmentation is driven entirely by an updated default prompt embedding, making the pipeline fully automated. The student model (10.1M parameters with TinyViT-5M encoder) is the sole inference-time model, while the teacher (full SAM ViT-B, ~100M+ parameters) is used only during training.

## Method

The framework employs a two-stage pipeline: first, both a teacher (original SAM with LoRA fine-tuning on the frozen image encoder, updated default embedding, and updated mask decoder) and a student (efficient SAM with TinyViT-5M encoder) are fine-tuned on labeled data using Dice + cross-entropy loss. Second, the frozen teacher guides the student on unlabeled data via hierarchical knowledge distillation—computing KL divergence between teacher and student encoder embeddings (global semantics) and between their decoder logit outputs (local boundary details)—combined with a dynamic loss weighting (DLW) strategy that multiplicatively decays the distillation weight whenever the student's supervised loss drops below the teacher's, progressively shifting reliance from teacher guidance to self-learning. This design replaces the ConvNet backbone entirely with an efficient SAM variant and eliminates prompt dependency, addressing both the underutilization of SAM and the incompatibility of standard semi-supervised paradigms with ViT architectures.

## Results

On the LA dataset with 5% labeled data, the proposed method achieves 88.42% DSC and 79.47% Jaccard, outperforming the strongest baseline BCP (88.02% DSC) by 0.40%. With 10% labeled data, it reaches 90.47% DSC and 82.71% Jaccard, improving over BCP by 0.85% DSC. On Brats-2019 with 10% labeled data, the method obtains 85.14% DSC, surpassing the best baseline MLRPL (84.29%) by 0.85%. On the Synapse multi-organ CT dataset with 10% labeled data, the method achieves 75.05% mean DSC with only 10.1M parameters, compared to 108–131M parameters for competing prompt-free SAM variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the LA dataset, the proposed method outperforms all seven SOTA semi-supervised baselines (UA-MT, SASSNet, DTC, URPC, MC-Net, SS-Net, BCP) on DSC and Jaccard at both 5% and 10% labeled ratios, with the largest margin over SS-Net (+2.09% DSC at 5%) and a consistent lead over the strongest competitor BCP (+0.40% at 5%, +0.85% at 10%). On Brats-2019, it surpasses all seven baselines (DAN, DTC, CPS, URPC, CPCL, AC-MT, MLRPL), beating the best method MLRPL by 0.85% DSC at 10% and 0.99% DSC at 20% labeled data, while also reducing 95HD by 0.99 at 10%. Against SAM-assisted semi-supervised methods (SemiSAM, UP-SAM, SFR) on LA, the method achieves the highest DSC (90.47% at 10%), exceeding SFR by 0.48% and SemiSAM by 6.02%. On the Synapse multi-organ CT benchmark, the method with only 10.1M parameters outperforms fully supervised prompt-free SAM variants (SAM Adapter, AutoSAM, SAMed, H-SAM) with 108–131M parameters, achieving 75.05% mean DSC versus H-SAM's 75.31% (nearly tied) and SAMed's 67.20% (+7.85%), while also yielding the best 95HD (17.87 vs. 21.34 for H-SAM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) SAM adaptation for medical imaging (SAMed, H-SAM, Med-SA), which established prompt-free fine-tuning via LoRA and default embedding updates; (2) efficient SAM variants (MobileSAM, EfficientSAM, TinySAM), which demonstrated that encoder-level knowledge distillation can compress SAM by 10× without major accuracy loss; and (3) semi-supervised medical image segmentation (Mean Teacher, BCP, SS-Net), which has historically relied on ConvNet backbones with consistency regularization or pseudo-labeling. The key departure is that prior SAM-assisted semi-supervised methods (SemiSAM, SFR, UP-SAM) treat SAM as an auxiliary pseudo-label generator bolted onto a ConvNet backbone, whereas this work makes an efficient SAM variant the backbone itself and replaces the standard SSL objective with a hierarchical teacher-student distillation pipeline (encoder embeddings + decoder logits) coupled with a dynamic loss weighting schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the 10× parameter reduction (10.1M vs. ~108–131M for full-SAM variants) while matching or exceeding their segmentation accuracy, which directly addresses the deployment bottleneck in clinical settings where storage and inference latency are constrained. The finding that standard semi-supervised paradigms like Mean Teacher yield only marginal gains (<4% DSC) with a ViT-based SAM backbone, attributed to weaker inductive biases, is a useful caution for the community attempting to port ConvNet-era SSL recipes to transformer architectures. Open questions include whether the hierarchical distillation hierarchy (encoder + decoder) and the specific DLW decay schedule generalize to 3D volumetric data, whether the approach benefits from larger unlabeled pools beyond the 95% ratios tested, and whether the frozen-teacher assumption remains valid when the student surpasses the teacher on labeled data. The absence of released code and error bars limits immediate reproducibility.
