## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models produce repetitive patterns and structural distortions when generating images beyond their training resolution, and training on higher-resolution data is prohibitively expensive. Existing training-free upscaling methods either incur heavy computational redundancy from overlapping patch processing or are incompatible with recent Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architectures, leaving a gap for an efficient, model-agnostic solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253196/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces high-resolution RGB images up to 4096² pixels via an iterative 1024² → 2048² → 4096² pipeline. It operates entirely in latent space using pretrained U-Net (SDXL) or DiT (FLUX) models without any weight modification, requiring only a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU. The pipeline is training-free: a low-resolution image is first generated at native resolution, then progressively upscaled and refined through SDEdit-style denoising.

## Method

ScaleDiff introduces Neighborhood Patch Attention (NPA), which splits self-attention queries into non-overlapping patches of size h/2 × w/2 while extracting overlapping key-value neighborhoods of size h × w from each patch's spatial vicinity, reducing self-attention FLOPs from O(s⁴) to O(s²) while keeping non-self-attention layers (linear, conv, cross-attention) at their natural O(s²) cost—avoiding the ~4× redundancy of MultiDiffusion's full-patch processing. The upscaling pipeline combines Latent Frequency Mixing (LFM), which fuses low-frequency components from latent-space upsampling with high-frequency components from RGB-space upsampling to prevent the oversmoothing bias of pure RGB resizing, with Structure Guidance (SG) applied in latent space to align low-frequency components of intermediate denoising predictions with the reference latent, enforcing global structural coherence without the overhead of RGB-space guidance.

## Results

On SDXL at 4096², ScaleDiff achieves FID 61.87, KID 0.0025, IS 19.56, and patch-level FIDp 38.89 / ISp 20.41, outperforming all training-free and training-based baselines while running in 113 s (8.9× faster than DemoFusion). On FLUX at 4096², ScaleDiff reaches FID 64.06 and FIDp 44.29 in 407 s, a 3.1× speedup over direct FLUX inference (1251 s) while dramatically improving quality (FID 459.07 → 64.06). At 2048², ScaleDiff on SDXL attains FID 62.98 and CLIP 33.11 in 31 s, matching or exceeding the best baselines (DiffuseHigh FID 63.27, DemoFusion FID 63.36) at roughly 4× lower inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScaleDiff outperforms all training-free baselines on SDXL at 4096²: it beats DiffuseHigh (FID 63.91→61.87, FIDp 42.30→38.89) and DemoFusion (FID 65.06→61.87, FIDp 41.29→38.89) on every quality metric while being 2.9× and 8.9× faster respectively. Against the training-based UltraPixel, ScaleDiff improves FID (65.39→61.87), KID (0.0055→0.0025), and ISp (20.64→20.41) while cutting inference time from 386 s to 113 s. ScaleCrafter and HiDiffusion degrade severely at 4096² (FID 86.66 and 105.37), confirming their architecture-specific limitations, whereas ScaleDiff maintains strong quality on both U-Net (SDXL) and DiT (FLUX) backbones. On FLUX at 4096², ScaleDiff (FID 64.06, FIDp 44.29) surpasses both BSRGAN (FID 64.76, FIDp 49.30) and OSEDiff (FID 64.22, FIDp 48.37) on patch-level detail metrics, though BSRGAN remains faster (34 s vs. 407 s). FreeScale and AccDiffusion v2 are slower (517 s and 1599 s) with slightly worse FID (64.33 and 64.64) and notably lower ISp (18.91 and 18.42 vs. 20.41), indicating weaker fine-detail synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScaleDiff builds on the SDEdit iterative upsample-diffuse-denoise pipeline and the patch-based decomposition strategy introduced by MultiDiffusion, but departs from both in key ways. Where MultiDiffusion and similar methods (DemoFusion, AccDiffusion) route all layers through overlapping patches—causing ~4× redundancy in non-self-attention operations—NPA's insight is that only self-attention requires patching, while linear, convolutional, and cross-attention layers can process the full tensor in a single pass. The paper also extends the editing-based line of work (DiffuseHigh, FreeScale) by identifying that RGB-space upsampling biases denoising toward oversmoothing (mimicking training-time resizing), and addresses this with Latent Frequency Mixing, a frequency-domain blend of latent-space and RGB-space upsampling paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.9× speedup over DemoFusion at 4096² while matching or exceeding quality metrics makes high-resolution diffusion generation substantially more practical for real-world deployment, and the model-agnostic design (validated on both U-Net/SDXL and DiT/FLUX) ensures relevance as transformer-based architectures become dominant. Open questions include whether the iterative 1024→2048→4096 cascade can be collapsed into fewer stages, whether the architecture-specific noise timestep τ can be made adaptive, and whether NPA's non-overlapping query strategy generalizes to 3D or video diffusion. The paper does not discuss limitations in the main text, leaving unclear how performance degrades beyond 4096² or on non-square aspect ratios.
