## Problem

Existing event-based depth estimation methods either process raw asynchronous event streams (which are prone to overfitting due to limited training data) or rely on a simple argmax over disparity space image (DSI) ray counts, which fails to exploit complex geometric patterns across disparity levels and surrounding pixels. This results in fewer reliable depth estimates and lower accuracy than the DSI representation could potentially support. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a learning-based framework that extracts depth from DSIs while remaining compatible with SLAM pipelines that provide camera poses.

## Input / Output

The method takes asynchronous event streams from one or two event cameras along with known camera poses, back-projects events into 3D space to build DSIs of size D×W×H (e.g., 100 depth levels), and fuses stereo DSIs via harmonic mean. An adaptive Gaussian threshold filter selects pixels with sufficient ray density, and for each selected pixel a fixed-size Sub-DSI patch (100×1×7×7) is extracted and normalized. The network outputs either a single normalized depth value or a 3×3 grid of depth predictions per selected pixel, mapped to the physical range [z_min, z_max], producing a semi-dense depth map whose density is controlled by the filter threshold.

## Method

DERD-Net processes each Sub-DSI independently through a compact architecture: a 3D convolution (3×3×3 kernel, 4 channels, stride 2 along depth) captures local spatial patterns and halves the depth dimension, the result is flattened and fed sequentially into a GRU that encodes inter-layer depth dependencies while maintaining a constant parameter count (70k) regardless of the number of depth levels D. The final hidden state passes through a dense ReLU layer and an output dense layer producing either one or nine depth values. Training uses supervised MAE loss with ensemble learning (two independently initialized networks trained on disjoint data subsets, averaged at inference), and converges in just 3 epochs. The key design insight is that restricting input to small local subregions around selected pixels enables full GPU parallelization, constant memory/compute cost independent of camera resolution or event rate, and improved generalization by forcing the network to learn transferable ray-intersection patterns rather than scene-specific semantics.

## Results

On MVSEC indoor_flying (stereo, F_orig), DERD-Net achieves a mean absolute error of 11.69 cm and median error of 5.50 cm, reducing MAE by 42% compared to the prior SOTA MC-EMVS (20.07 cm). On DSEC Zurich_City_04_a, the multi-pixel variant achieves 1.59 m MAE and 0.47 m median error, a 55% MAE reduction over MC-EMVS+MF (3.51 m) while producing 1.72× more depth points. Using purely monocular data, DERD-Net (23.68 cm MAE on MVSEC) matches or exceeds stereo SOTA methods like ESVO (22.70 cm). The multi-pixel version increases depth completeness by a factor of 5.47 (F_orig) over the single-pixel version while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest baseline MC-EMVS [12] (argmax on DSIs), DERD-Net stereo reduces MAE by 42% on MVSEC F_orig (11.69 vs 20.07 cm) and by 55% on DSEC F_orig (1.59 vs 3.51 m with morphological filter), while also reducing bad-pixel rates from 1.35% to 0.89% (MVSEC) and from 11.81% to 3.81% (DSEC). The monocular DERD-Net outperforms the monocular EMVS [11] by 30% in MAE (23.68 vs 33.78 cm on MVSEC) and by 45% on DSEC (3.12 vs 5.64 m), achieving performance comparable to stereo methods without requiring a second camera. Against ESVO [19], DERD-Net stereo+F_orig wins on all accuracy metrics (11.69 vs 22.70 cm MAE on MVSEC; 1.61 vs 3.93 m on DSEC) while SGM [38] produces more points (14.46M vs 0.79M on MVSEC) but at substantially higher error (35.42 cm vs 11.69 cm). GTS [39] is dramatically outperformed (389 cm vs 11.69 cm MAE on MVSEC). Among the 12 end-to-end deep stereo methods in Table 4 (DDES, EIT-Net, DTC-SPADE, StereoSpike, SCS-Net, etc.), DERD-Net is the only method that generalizes robustly across all three MVSEC indoor_flying splits (11.69, 11.11, 12.28 cm), whereas most others either fail to report split-2 results or show significant degradation (e.g., DDES: 16.7→29.4 cm; EIS: 13.74→18.43 cm). The multi-pixel variant surpasses MC-EMVS's morphological filter in depth completeness (4.32M vs 3.00M points on MVSEC F_orig; 6.59M vs 3.83M on DSEC F_orig) while maintaining lower error, demonstrating a learned alternative to heuristic dilation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DERD-Net extends the DSI-based multi-view stereo pipeline established by EMVS [11] and MC-EMVS [12], which back-project events as rays into 3D space and select depth via argmax on ray counts. The key departure is replacing this argmax with a compact neural network (3D convolution + GRU over depth layers) that processes small local Sub-DSI neighborhoods, drawing architectural inspiration from RAFT-Stereo's recurrent design and FireNet's lightweight philosophy. The paper also positions itself as the first learning-based multi-view stereo method to explicitly consume camera poses alongside events, enabling long-interval depth accumulation as used in SLAM, whereas prior deep stereo methods (DDES, EIT-Net, etc.) operate instantaneously without pose information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's ultra-lightweight design (70k parameters, 0.37 ms per Sub-DSI, resolution-independent) makes it practically deployable as a depth module within real-time event-based SLAM systems, a setting where prior deep methods were infeasible. The demonstrated robustness to noisy poses from ES-PTAM and ESVO2, combined with 3×+ depth completeness gains, suggests a path toward self-sustaining event-based mapping loops. Open questions include generalization beyond the two tested datasets (MVSEC indoor, DSEC driving), whether the approach can operate without external pose estimates, and how performance scales to higher-resolution or longer-baseline event cameras. The requirement for ground-truth depth during training remains a practical limitation for deployment in novel environments.
