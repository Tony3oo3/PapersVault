## Problem

Standard diffusion models produce high-fidelity images through a fixed number of denoising steps, and meeting bandwidth constraints requires aggressive post-hoc lossy compression that erases fine textures and wastes computation. Naively truncating the sampling process yields poor visual quality because the model was never trained for early termination. The paper asks whether bandwidth-awareness can be integrated directly into the diffusion generation process to produce entropy-compliant images without post-generation compression.

## Input / Output

The method takes a scalar target entropy budget H_target (drawn uniformly from [0.2, 2.0] bpp during training, user-specified at inference) and Gaussian noise as input, optionally a text prompt for the Stable Diffusion variant. It outputs a decoded RGB image whose empirical entropy (measured by a differentiable neural entropy estimator) adheres to the target budget, eliminating the need for a separate compression step. Experiments span 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 256×256 (CelebA-HQ, LSUN), and up to 1024×1024 resolutions, with three bitrate regimes: low (0.2–0.5 bpp), medium (0.5–1.0 bpp), and high (1.0–2.0 bpp).

## Method

BADiff extends the DDPM reverse kernel by conditioning every denoising step on a target entropy budget via a lightweight FiLM-style modulation: the scalar H_target is mapped to a 128-dim embedding through a 3-layer MLP and added to each UNet residual block's activation, adding <0.1% parameters. A differentiable neural entropy estimator (a small CNN with discretized logistic per-pixel distributions and causal context) provides a hinge loss that penalizes reconstructions exceeding the entropy budget, while a calibration loss aligns the estimator's probabilities with a reference learned codec. A separate 3-layer MLP stopping policy, trained via self-distillation from offline oracle labels, decides at each step whether to terminate sampling, reducing inference cost by ~50% under low-bandwidth budgets. The full training objective jointly optimizes the standard denoising loss, entropy hinge, calibration, and stopping-policy losses with randomly sampled entropy targets, enabling the model to generalize across the full bandwidth spectrum.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with the DDPM-1k backbone at low bitrate (0.2–0.5 bpp), BADiff achieves FID 11.4, outperforming DDPM+BPG (15.2), DDPM+LIC (13.6), PNDM+LIC (18.1), DPM-Solver+LIC (17.8), and naive Early-Stop+LIC (22.9). Under the LDM-200 backbone at the same setting, BADiff reaches FID 12.6 versus 17.3 for LDM+BPG and 15.6 for LDM+LIC. BADiff also reduces inference latency to 65 ms (DDPM-1k) and 27 ms (LDM-200) at low bitrate, compared to 115 ms and 47 ms for the cascade+LIC baselines, yielding a 1.7× speed-up.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BADiff consistently outperforms all six baselines across every dataset, backbone, and bitrate regime. Against the strongest cascade baseline (DDPM+LIC), BADiff reduces FID by 2.2 points on CIFAR-10 at low bitrate (11.4 vs. 13.6) and by 3.8 points on CELEBA-HQ (21.7 vs. 25.3). The naive Early-Stop+LIC baseline performs worst overall (FID 22.9 on CIFAR-10 low), confirming that simply truncating sampling without entropy-aware training degrades quality severely. Accelerated solvers (PNDM, DPM-Solver) combined with LIC improve over plain DDPM+LIC but still trail BADiff by 4–7 FID points at low bitrate, indicating that faster sampling alone does not substitute for explicit bandwidth conditioning. On inference speed, BADiff (65 ms) is slightly slower than Early-Stop (58 ms) on DDPM-1k at low bitrate but achieves 7.5 FID points better quality, while remaining 1.7× faster than the full cascade pipeline (115 ms). Under the LDM-200 backbone, BADiff maintains its advantage, beating LDM+LIC by 3.0 FID points on CIFAR-10 low (12.6 vs. 15.6) and running 1.7× faster (27 ms vs. 47 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

BADiff extends the DDPM/LDM diffusion framework by injecting a scalar entropy budget as a continuous conditioning signal (via FiLM-style modulation) into every reverse step, drawing its differentiable entropy estimator directly from the learned image compression literature (Ballé et al.'s discretized logistic + hyperprior architecture). It departs from the standard 'generate-then-compress' cascade and from acceleration methods (PNDM, DPM-Solver, DDIM) that reduce steps without any quality-target awareness, and from naive early-stopping which simply truncates a fixed-step sampler. The adaptive stopping policy is conceptually related to StepSaver and AdaDiff but is specifically trained to satisfy an entropy constraint rather than a generic quality threshold, making it a bandwidth-aware variant of step-selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck—cloud-to-device image delivery under real-time bandwidth constraints—by showing that diffusion's progressive refinement can be steered to produce bitrate-compliant images without post-hoc compression artifacts. Key limitations include spatially uniform entropy budgets (no per-region bit-allocation), evaluation primarily at 256² resolution (with limited 512²/1024² validation), and reliance on a single scalar target that may not capture spatially heterogeneous content complexity. Open questions include extending to video diffusion where bandwidth constraints are far more stringent, integrating with faster ODE solvers at scale, and whether the approach generalizes to spatially varying or perceptually-weighted entropy targets.
