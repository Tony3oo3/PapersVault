## Problem

Existing self-gated activations such as GELU and Swish rely on symmetric gate functions (Gaussian CDF, Sigmoid), while ReLU suffers from the dying-neuron problem. No prior activation exploits the right-skewed asymmetry of the Gompertz function—the CDF of the standard Gumbel distribution—to achieve stronger variance reduction in latent representations while preserving smooth gradient flow.

## Input / Output

GoLU is a drop-in scalar activation that takes a single pre-activation value x ∈ ℝ and returns x · e^(−e^(−x)). It replaces any existing activation in CNNs, Transformers, or diffusion models without architectural changes, and its output is systematically closer to zero than GELU or Swish outputs across the entire input range due to the Gompertz gate's compression effect.

## Method

GoLU is defined as x · Gompertz(x) where Gompertz(x) = e^(−e^(−x)), the CDF of the standard Gumbel distribution. Unlike the symmetric Sigmoid or Gaussian CDF gates used by Swish and GELU, the Gompertz gate is right-skewed, yielding smaller gate values across the full input range and a reduced slope at the origin (≈0.37 vs. 0.5), which analytically translates to lower output variance via Var[f(x)] ≈ f'(μ)²σ². This variance reduction acts as implicit regularization, producing smoother loss landscapes and encouraging a broader weight distribution that captures more diverse feature transformations. The authors implement a custom CUDA kernel for GoLU, achieving training and inference speeds on par with baseline activations, and validate the approach across image classification, language modeling, semantic segmentation, object detection, instance segmentation, and diffusion tasks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, GoLU achieves the highest top-1 accuracy across nearly all architectures: 76.63% on ResNet-50 (vs. 76.07% GELU, 75.44% ReLU), 80.72% on ViT-B/16 (vs. 79.39% GELU, 80.06% ReLU), and 83.21% on TinyViT (vs. 83.05% GELU). In language modeling, GoLU attains the lowest perplexity on GPT2-S/OpenWebText (17.30 vs. 17.53 for GELU) and babyGPT/TinyStories (4.44 vs. 4.46 for GELU). On MS-COCO object detection, GoLU achieves 38.31 Box mAP with Faster R-CNN-FPN and 40.77 with RetinaNet-FPN, both the best among all tested activations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoLU outperforms all six baselines (ReLU, LeakyReLU, ELU, GELU, Swish, Mish) on 8 out of 9 ImageNet-1k architectures, with the sole exception being EfficientNet-B0 where GELU edges ahead by 0.04 points (76.90 vs. 76.86). GELU is consistently the strongest baseline, ranking second on virtually every task and architecture; the gap between GoLU and GELU ranges from ~0.1 points (TinyViT, EfficientNet-B0) to ~1.1 points (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16). ELU is uniformly the worst performer, trailing GoLU by 4–10 points depending on architecture. On CIFAR-10, GoLU wins across all 8 tested architectures (e.g., 93.25% on ResNet-110 vs. 93.21% ReLU, 95.03% on WRN-28-2 vs. 94.96% ReLU). In language modeling, GoLU achieves the lowest perplexity on both babyGPT (4.44 vs. 4.46 GELU) and GPT2-S (17.30 vs. 17.53 GELU), while on MS-COCO detection and segmentation tasks GoLU leads GELU by 0.15–0.4 mAP/mIoU points. At default learning rates for segmentation and diffusion tasks, GoLU is a close second to Swish or GELU, but at the optimal (tuned) learning rate it consistently achieves the best result across all activations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-gated activation function family (GELU, Swish, Mish) by replacing the symmetric gate (Gaussian CDF or logistic) with the Gompertz function, the CDF of the Gumbel distribution, which introduces a right-skewed asymmetry. The Gompertz function itself is a 19th-century mortality model repurposed here as a neural network gate, and the probabilistic interpretation as a Gumbel CDF provides a natural connection to the existing self-gated framework. The paper also introduces Flipped Mish (Appendix B) as a controlled experiment isolating the role of skew direction, confirming that right-leaning asymmetry—not the specific distribution shape—is the key differentiator from GELU and Swish.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GoLU offers a drop-in replacement for GELU/Swish with consistent (if modest, typically 0.1–0.5%) gains across nine architectures and six task families, backed by a clean variance-reduction mechanism (output variance ≈ slope²·σ²) and a CUDA-optimized kernel at parity speed. The spread-weight-distribution observation and the learning-rate sensitivity (GoLU sometimes requires a different optimal LR than baselines) are practical caveats that limit straightforward adoption. Open questions include whether the variance-reduction benefit persists in very deep or very wide networks, whether the asymmetry advantage generalizes beyond the tested architectures, and whether the LC-PFN negative result signals a fundamental limitation in certain in-context learning settings.
