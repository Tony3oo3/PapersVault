## Problem

Existing model extraction attacks against tree-based models either rely on strong assumptions (e.g., access to leaf identifiers), lack formal guarantees of functional equivalence, or provide no rigorous characterization of their worst-case query complexity. This paper introduces the first formal competitive-analysis framework for model extraction, quantifying how many counterfactual queries are needed to provably reconstruct a tree-based model's decision boundary, and identifies the security risk that counterfactual explanations—increasingly mandated by regulation—expose as an attack surface.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two oracle queries per iteration: a prediction query returning the label f(x) and a counterfactual explanation oracle returning a locally optimal x' (not necessarily globally optimal) within the current input subspace E. The input space X may mix continuous, binary, categorical (one-hot encoded), and ordinal features. The output is a complete axis-parallel decision tree that is functionally identical to the target model over the entire input space (100% fidelity by construction), with the guarantee that a valid partial classifier is available at every intermediate step (anytime property).

## Method

TRA (Tree Reconstruction Attack) is a divide-and-conquer algorithm that maintains a priority queue of input subspaces, starting with the full space X. At each step it queries the geometric center of the top subspace, obtains a locally optimal counterfactual, and splits the subspace along every feature where the counterfactual differs from the query point, peeling off the half containing the query and recursing on the remainder. When no counterfactual exists within a subspace, that region is assigned a leaf label. The key theoretical contribution is a tight competitive-ratio bound C_TRA^(n,m) = (2∏(s_j+1) − 1)/(n+1), proven to be optimal among all divide-and-conquer strategies regardless of query-point selection, with worst-case complexity O(∏(s_i+1)) and an upper bound of O((1+n/m)^m). Empirically, TRA requires orders of magnitude fewer queries than PATHFINDING and achieves perfect fidelity far faster than surrogate-based attacks (CF, DUALCF), while scaling sub-linearly (Θ(#Nodes^0.38)) with random-forest size due to structural redundancies.

## Results

TRA achieves 100% fidelity (certified functional equivalence) on all tested decision trees and random forests, requiring orders of magnitude fewer queries than baselines. On random forests (COMPAS), TRA needs only 74–148 counterfactual queries to fully reconstruct forests of 487–18,369 nodes, versus 3,000 queries for CF and DUALCF. Empirically, TRA's query counts are far below its theoretical worst-case bound (e.g., 153 queries vs. 1,073 worst-case on COMPAS; 37,000 vs. 2.45×10¹⁴ on Adult). The competitive ratio is bounded by C_TRA^(n,m) = (2∏(s_j+1) − 1)/(n+1), and no divide-and-conquer algorithm can improve upon this bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRA decisively outperforms all three baselines. Against PATHFINDING (Tramèr et al., 2016), TRA requires orders of magnitude fewer queries to achieve functional equivalence (e.g., ~150 queries vs. thousands on COMPAS), and unlike PATHFINDING, TRA does not require a leaf identifier in the API response, making it applicable to random forests where PATHFINDING cannot operate. Against CF (Aivodji et al., 2020) and DUALCF (Wang et al., 2022), TRA achieves strictly higher anytime fidelity at every query budget and converges to perfect fidelity far faster; on random forests, TRA needs only 74–148 queries versus 3,000 for both CF and DUALCF. Critically, TRA is the only method that certifies functional equivalence (100% fidelity over the entire input space), whereas CF and DUALCF train surrogate models that approach but do not guarantee perfect agreement. The paper also shows that for CF and DUALCF, using non-optimal DiCE counterfactuals paradoxically yields better surrogate fidelity than optimal OCEAN counterfactuals, highlighting that TRA's structured divide-and-conquer approach is what unlocks the full value of optimal counterfactuals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of model extraction attacks and online discovery theory. While prior extraction attacks (PATHFINDING, CF, DUALCF) either required strong assumptions like leaf identifiers or trained surrogate models without functional equivalence guarantees, TRA is the first to achieve provable functional equivalence for tree ensembles using only locally optimal counterfactuals. The conceptual departure is the formalization of model extraction as an online exploration problem, importing competitive analysis from the map-discovery literature (Deng et al., 1991; Ghosh and Klein, 2010) to derive the first theoretical bounds on extraction query complexity. The divide-and-conquer structure repurposes the counterfactual oracle not as a data-generation tool for surrogate training (as in CF/DUALCF) but as a geometric probe that directly reveals axis-parallel decision boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As regulatory frameworks (EU AI Act) increasingly mandate counterfactual explanations in MLaaS APIs, this work quantifies the inherent security cost of that transparency: locally optimal counterfactuals are sufficient to perfectly reconstruct tree ensembles with far fewer queries than prior methods. The competitive ratio framework (showing no D&C algorithm can beat TRA's ratio) provides a principled yardstick for future attack/defense research, while the sub-linear scaling with forest size (Θ(#Nodes^0.38)) suggests large ensembles are not meaningfully harder to extract. Open questions include whether privacy-preserving counterfactual mechanisms can break the functional equivalence guarantee, whether the competitive analysis extends to non-axis-parallel or neural models, and whether adaptive exploration strategies can close the large gap between worst-case bounds and empirical query counts.
