## Problem

Existing graph-based RAG methods (GraphRAG, LightRAG, PathRAG, HippoRAG2) model knowledge using ordinary graphs whose edges connect only two entities, restricting them to binary relations. This forces multi-entity facts (e.g., a medical diagnosis involving patient gender, lab values, and a condition) to be decomposed into multiple binary triples, causing representation sparsity and loss of relational context. The paper asks whether a hypergraph-structured representation, where a single hyperedge can connect n≥2 entities, can preserve n-ary relational completeness and improve retrieval-augmented generation.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text question and a corpus of domain knowledge documents (medical guidelines, agricultural texts, CS papers, legal provisions). During construction, each document is parsed by an LLM into hyperedges (natural-language descriptions with a 0–10 confidence score) and typed entities (name, type, explanation, 0–100 extraction score), which are stored in a bipartite graph database alongside separate vector indexes. At query time, the question is first decomposed into key entities by the LLM, then dual top-60 retrieval over entity and hyperedge embeddings (with score-weighted cosine similarity) is performed, followed by bidirectional neighborhood expansion and optional chunk retrieval; the final output is a CoT-generated text answer grounded in the fused n-ary facts and chunk passages.

## Method

HyperGraphRAG introduces a three-stage pipeline built around hypergraph-structured knowledge. First, an LLM-based n-ary relation extraction prompt segments each document into hyperedges expressed as natural-language descriptions (rather than structured relation labels) and extracts all contained entities with types and confidence scores; the resulting hypergraph is stored as a bipartite incidence graph in a standard graph database (proven lossless via a bijection Φ) with parallel vector databases for entities and hyperedges. Second, retrieval operates on two axes simultaneously: entity retrieval matches question-extracted entities against the entity vector index (score-weighted cosine similarity, threshold τ_V=50, top-60), and hyperedge retrieval matches the question embedding against the hyperedge vector index (score-weighted, τ_H=5, top-60); a bidirectional expansion step then pulls all hyperedges incident to retrieved entities and all entities incident to retrieved hyperedges to assemble a complete n-ary fact set. Third, generation fuses the expanded hypergraph knowledge with traditional chunk-based RAG passages into a single prompt and uses GPT-4o-mini with a unified Chain-of-Thought template. The key architectural distinction from prior graph-based RAG is that each hyperedge is a first-class, naturally-described n-ary fact rather than a collection of binary edges, enabling lossless representation of multi-entity relations and more compact, semantically complete retrieval under a fixed token budget.

## Results

HyperGraphRAG achieves the best performance across all five domains (Medicine, Agriculture, CS, Legal, Mix) on all three metrics (F1, Retrieval Similarity, Generation Evaluation). In the Overall setting, it reaches F1=48.71, R-S=68.21, and G-E=66.90 on the Mix domain, and F1=35.35, R-S=70.19, G-E=59.35 on Medicine. Compared to the strongest non-graph baseline (StandardRAG), HyperGraphRAG gains +7.45 F1, +7.62 R-S, and +3.69 G-E on average across domains. Notably, all four graph-based baselines (GraphRAG, LightRAG, PathRAG, HippoRAG2) underperform StandardRAG in most domains, while HyperGraphRAG consistently outperforms both.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperGraphRAG outperforms all six baselines across every domain and metric. Against StandardRAG (the strongest non-graph baseline), it gains +7.45 F1, +7.62 R-S, and +3.69 G-E on average; the gap is largest in Medicine (+7.45 F1) and Legal (+6.47 F1). A striking finding is that all four binary graph-based RAG methods—GraphRAG, LightRAG, PathRAG, and HippoRAG2—actually underperform StandardRAG in most domains (e.g., GraphRAG scores F1=17.60 vs. StandardRAG's 27.90 on Medicine), suggesting that decomposing n-ary facts into binary triples introduces knowledge fragmentation that degrades retrieval. HyperGraphRAG's advantage is most pronounced on N-ary Source questions, where it gains +5.3 F1 over StandardRAG, confirming that hyperedge representation specifically addresses the multi-entity reasoning gap. In the case study (Appendix H), HyperGraphRAG achieves a perfect F1=100 and G-E=92.86 on a complex medical question, while the best baseline (HippoRAG2) scores only F1=19.35 and G-E=53.25.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph-based RAG lineage initiated by GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024) and refined by LightRAG, PathRAG, and HippoRAG2 by replacing binary edges with hyperedges that connect n≥2 entities, enabling direct representation of n-ary relational facts. It draws on the n-ary relation extraction methodology from the authors' prior Text2nkg work (Luo et al., 2024) but repurposes it for RAG rather than link prediction, and adapts hypergraph embedding literature (Galkin et al., 2020; Rosso et al., 2020) to a retrieval-augmented generation setting. The key conceptual departure is that prior graph-based RAG methods are structurally limited to pairwise relations, forcing n-ary facts into lossy binary decompositions, whereas HyperGraphRAG preserves the full relational structure in a single hyperedge with a natural-language description.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both an information-theoretic proof (Proposition 1) and empirical evidence that binary graph representations are lossy for n-ary knowledge, offering a principled justification for hypergraph-structured RAG. Practically, it demonstrates consistent gains across medicine, agriculture, CS, and law, and the bipartite-graph storage trick makes the approach compatible with existing graph databases. Open questions include scalability to very large corpora (construction cost is moderate but non-trivial), reliance on a single LLM (GPT-4o-mini) for extraction quality, and whether the gains persist when the underlying LLM or embedding model changes. The authors outline multimodal extension, RL-guided retrieval, and federated deployment as natural next steps.
