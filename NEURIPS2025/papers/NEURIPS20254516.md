## Problem

Prior theoretical guarantees on gradient descent convergence—saddle-point avoidance, stability of minima—rely on the assumption that the GD map is non-singular (preserves measure-zero sets under preimages), yet this property has never been rigorously established for neural networks with piecewise analytic activations such as ReLU. Existing proofs either require fully analytic activations where standard calculus suffices, or impose restrictive conditions like Lipschitz smoothness of the loss and small step-sizes, neither of which holds for practical deep ReLU networks with cross-entropy loss.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a neural network with fully connected, convolutional, or softmax attention layers using piecewise analytic activations (ReLU, sigmoid, leaky ReLU, etc.), a fixed dataset, an analytic loss function, and a step-size η, and certifies that the resulting GD or SGD map is non-singular for almost all η—excluding only a measure-zero set where η coincides with 1/λ_i for a Hessian eigenvalue. As a downstream application, it yields explicit stability criteria μ(θ) and λ(θ) that determine whether a global minimum is stable under GD or SGD, respectively, enabling concrete predictions about which minima attract or repel nearby initializations.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a chain-rule analogue for neural networks (Proposition 6) that overcomes the failure of the standard chain rule for a.e. analytic compositions, as demonstrated by a counterexample in Remark 5. By exploiting the layered structure, the paper shows the network function θ ↦ f_θ(x) is a.e. analytic for any input x, which in turn implies the empirical loss is a.e. analytic (Corollary 9). On this a.e. analytic domain, the GD map reduces to G_η(θ) = θ − η∇L(θ), and the paper proves its Jacobian determinant det(I − ηH_L) vanishes on a measure-zero set for almost all η by parameterizing Hessian eigenvalues along analytic paths and invoking the identity theorem for analytic functions. Non-singularity then follows via a submersion argument (Lemma 10), and the result extends to SGD since each mini-batch step is a GD step on a different analytic loss, and compositions of non-singular maps remain non-singular.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that for neural networks with fully connected, convolutional, or softmax attention layers and piecewise analytic activations (ReLU, sigmoid, leaky ReLU, etc.), the (S)GD map G_η(θ) = θ − η∇L(θ) is non-singular for almost all step-sizes η, for any fixed data and analytic loss. The illustrative experiment on a 2-layer ReLU network with loss L(θ₁,θ₂) = 3.53(1−ReLU(θ₂ReLU(θ₁)))² demonstrates that as η increases, higher-order periodic orbits emerge and lower-order ones become unstable (bifurcation diagram, Figure 3). For GD vs. SGD stability, the paper shows that at η=0.15, p=0.5, stable SGD minima form a proper subset of stable GD minima, while at η=0.3, p=0.58, the stable minima sets for GD and SGD do not overlap at all (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing optimization methods; instead, it validates and extends assumptions made in prior theoretical works. Compared to Lee et al. [2019] and Panageas & Piliouras [2016], which require Lipschitz smoothness of the loss and small step-sizes (conditions that fail for deep ReLU networks with cross-entropy loss), this paper removes both restrictions by proving non-singularity directly for piecewise analytic activations at almost all step-sizes. Compared to Jentzen & Riekert [2022a,b,2023], which prove a.e. differentiability only for ReLU activations with polynomial data-generating functions, this paper generalizes to any piecewise analytic activation (sigmoid, tanh, GELU, etc.), any data distribution, and broader architectures (convolutional, softmax attention). The experimental section compares GD and SGD stability (not competing algorithms), showing that the two can select different stable global minima depending on step-size and batch composition, consistent with empirical observations in Wu et al. [2018].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-singularity analysis of the GD map from the analytic-activation setting (where standard chain-rule arguments suffice) to piecewise analytic activations like ReLU, sigmoid, and leaky ReLU, where the composition of a.e. analytic functions need not be a.e. analytic (Remark 5). It builds directly on Lee et al. [2019] and Panageas and Piliouras [2016], who proved saddle-point avoidance under the assumption of non-singularity or restrictive Lipschitz-smoothness conditions, and on Chemnitz and Engel [2024], who assumed non-singularity to characterize stability of minima. The closest prior work is Jentzen and Riekert [2022a,b, 2023], who showed the loss is C¹ a.e. for ReLU networks with polynomial data; this paper generalizes to arbitrary piecewise analytic activations, broader architectures (convolutional, softmax attention), and removes restrictions on the data-generating process. The key technical departure is a layered chain-rule analogue (Proposition 6) that exploits the sequential structure of neural networks to recover analyticity guarantees that fail for arbitrary compositions of a.e. analytic maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By rigorously establishing non-singularity of the (S)GD map for realistic architectures and activations, this paper validates the foundational assumption underlying multiple influential results on saddle-point avoidance and minima stability, extending their applicability from idealized settings to practical deep learning. The result holds for almost all step-sizes without requiring Lipschitz smoothness of the loss (which fails for deep ReLU networks with cross-entropy loss) or small-step-size restrictions, significantly broadening the theoretical guarantees available to practitioners. Open questions include extending the proof to recurrent networks (where the current techniques are insufficient), other architectures like graph or residual networks, and other optimization algorithms such as mirror descent or adaptive methods. The illustrative experiments showing that GD and SGD can have entirely disjoint sets of stable global minima, and that periodic trajectories emerge at larger step-sizes, suggest rich dynamical behavior that warrants further investigation.
