## Problem

Existing online bilevel optimization (OBO) methods rely on deterministic window-smoothed regret minimization, which requires large smoothing windows (w = o(T)) to achieve sublinear regret and may misrepresent true system performance when objectives change rapidly. No prior work provides stochastic bilevel regret guarantees without window smoothing, and no OBO algorithm operates with only function-value (zeroth-order) feedback, limiting applicability to black-box and large-scale settings.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel momentum-type search direction that augments the standard recursive update with a correction term (1−η)(∇f_t(x_t,y_t) − ∇f_t(x_{t−1},y_{t−1})), which accounts for temporal drift in the objective without requiring window averaging, enabling sublinear stochastic bilevel local regret at window size w=1. SOGD (Algorithm 1) simultaneously updates y, v, and x in a single loop using one projected OGD step per variable, with the auxiliary variable v decoupling the implicit hypergradient structure so that the linear system is solved with a single iteration rather than conjugate gradients. ZO-SOGD (Algorithm 2) replaces all gradient, Hessian, and Jacobian oracles with finite-difference estimators built from function-value queries at ±ρ perturbations, introducing Gaussian-smoothed surrogates and dimension-dependent batch sizes to control the resulting estimation variance. The regret bound for SOGD is O(T^{1/3}(σ²+Δ_T) + T^{2/3}Ψ_T), and for ZO-SOGD it is O((d₁+d₂)^{3/4}T^{1/3}(σ̂²+Δ̂_T) + (d₁+d₂)^{3/2}T^{2/3}Ψ̂_T), both sublinear under mild regularity on the time-varying sequence.

## Results

The paper evaluates two applications on MNIST: (1) online black-box adversarial attacks (BBAA) and (2) parametric loss tuning for imbalanced data. In BBAA, ZO-SOGD (Adam) achieves the highest test accuracy on perturbed samples among all methods while keeping the infinity norm of adversarial perturbations below 4, outperforming single-level baselines ZO-O-GD, ZO-O-Adam, ZO-O-SignSGD, and ZO-O-ConservSGD. In the loss tuning experiment, SOGD is the fastest method (single OGD step per round vs. 5 CG iterations for SOBOW) and achieves competitive test and balanced accuracy compared to OAGD and SOBOW across four 100-timestep phases transitioning from imbalanced to balanced class distributions. No specific numerical accuracy values are reported in the text; results are presented in Figures 2 and 3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online bilevel optimization (OBO) framework initiated by Tarzanagh et al. [67] (OAGD) and Lin et al. [50] (SOBOW), both of which require window-smoothed regret (w = o(T)) and multiple conjugate-gradient iterations for the implicit linear system. The key departure is a novel momentum-type search direction (Eq. 7) that achieves sublinear stochastic bilevel regret with w = 1, eliminating the window-smoothing assumption entirely. Additionally, the paper draws on the single-loop quadratic formulation of the linear system from Dagréou et al. [15] to reduce the solver to a single iteration, and extends the framework to zeroth-order (function-value-only) oracles for the first time in the OBO literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the window-smoothing requirement is practically significant because, as the paper illustrates (Figure 1), smoothed objectives can misrepresent true performance when functions change rapidly—a common scenario in adversarial and non-stationary settings. The first ZO OBO regret guarantee opens the door to bilevel optimization in truly black-box environments (e.g., API-based models, physical systems) where gradient access is unavailable. A stated limitation is that all guarantees are for the nonconvex outer objective; extending to convex settings or establishing minimax optimality of the T^{-2/3} rate remains open. The experimental validation, while illustrative, is confined to MNIST-scale problems, leaving questions about scalability to larger architectures and more complex non-stationary dynamics.
