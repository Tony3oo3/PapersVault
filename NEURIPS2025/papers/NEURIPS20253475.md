## Problem

LLM agents used as personalized assistants struggle to model how the relative strength of a user's multiple coexisting preferences shifts over time. Two specific gaps remain: (i) existing methods rely on explicit user feedback to estimate preference strengths, which is impractical in real interactions, and (ii) inserting numeric preference weights into text prompts is unreliable due to the inherent ambiguity of natural language, limiting controllability of personalized generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253475/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user's historical interaction set, a current user-agent dialogue, and a set of M user preferences (typically top-2) extracted from history. It first produces continuous preference strength weights in [0,1] (normalized alignment scores), then generates a personalized response token-by-token by sampling from a weighted mixture of M preference-conditioned next-token distributions. The generation is fully training-free and operates at inference time with a local LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) for distribution computation and an external LLM for alignment scoring.

## Method

AdaPA-Agent introduces Adaptive Preference Arithmetic with two components. For strength estimation, it performs dual-side augmentation—constructing three-level preference chains (raw → refined → example) via CoT on the preference side, and generating K paraphrased interaction variants on the interaction side—then scores each preference-interaction pair with an LLM-based alignment scorer and normalizes scores into relative weights. For generation, it linearly combines the next-token distributions of the LLM conditioned on each individual preference chain, weighted by the estimated strengths, which is equivalent to the closed-form solution of a weighted KL minimization over preference-conditioned distributions. This logit-mixing approach replaces unreliable prompt-based weight specification with explicit arithmetic control over the output distribution, enabling fine-grained, continuous, and interpretable preference modulation at every decoding step.

## Results

On the conversational recommendation task (Reddit-Movie), AdaPA-Agent achieves 41.45% RSR at 3 steps, 67.24% at 5 steps, and 75.41% at 7 steps, consistently outperforming all baselines while maintaining the lowest Average Interaction Rounds (2.64 at 3 steps). On the personalized web interaction task (PWABench), AdaPA-Agent reaches 0.851 overall function accuracy and 0.367 response accuracy in the single-turn setting, and 0.917 F. Acc / 0.386 R. Acc with only 3.59 average steps in the multi-turn setting. The paper reports over 18.9% and 14.2% relative improvements over ReAct on the two tasks respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaPA-Agent outperforms all five baselines on the primary RSR metric across every step budget in the conversational recommendation task. Against ReAct [16], the gap is largest at 3 steps (41.45% vs. 30.20% RSR, +11.25 pp) and narrows at 7 steps (75.41% vs. 63.40%, +12.01 pp), while AdaPA-Agent also achieves the lowest AIR (2.64 vs. 2.85 at 3 steps). Against Reflexion [17], AdaPA-Agent leads by 7.65 pp at 3 steps and 4.01 pp at 7 steps, with Reflexion showing a notable weakness on short-term preference tasks. Against RecMind [38], AdaPA-Agent wins by 5.05 pp at 3 steps and 1.61 pp at 7 steps; RecMind is competitive on short-term tasks but lags on long-term preferences. Against InteRec [39], which explicitly models long- and short-term memory, AdaPA-Agent still edges ahead by 1.85 pp at 3 steps and 0.41 pp at 7 steps, while matching or beating its AIR. Against SimToM [37], AdaPA-Agent leads by 5.44 pp at 3 steps and 2.81 pp at 7 steps. On the PWABench web interaction task, AdaPA-Agent achieves the highest overall function accuracy (0.851 single-turn, 0.917 multi-turn) and response accuracy (0.367, 0.386) while requiring fewer average steps than ReAct (4.198) and Reflexion (4.579) in the multi-turn setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the logit-mixing framework of Dekoninck et al. (ICLR 2024), which showed that complex textual styles emerge from weighted combinations of next-token distributions, to the domain of LLM-agent personalization. Where prior personalization methods (RAG, prompt engineering, RecMind, InteRec) encode preferences as static text or require explicit user feedback to adjust them, AdaPA-Agent instead treats preference strength as a continuous, dynamically-estimated weight that directly modulates token-level distributions. The alignment-based strength estimation component draws on LLM-as-judge scoring and dual-side data augmentation, positioning the work at the intersection of controllable generation and adaptive agent frameworks like ReAct and Reflexion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in personalized LLM agents: how to adapt to shifting user priorities without burdening users with explicit feedback, and how to exert fine-grained control over generation beyond what text prompts allow. The logit-level preference arithmetic provides a principled, continuous control mechanism that avoids the ambiguity of encoding numeric weights in natural language. However, the evaluation is constrained to M=2 preferences, a single local LLM (Llama-3.1-8B), and LLM-simulated users rather than real human subjects, leaving open questions about scalability to many concurrent preferences, computational cost of M parallel forward passes per token, and robustness in open-ended real-world deployments.
