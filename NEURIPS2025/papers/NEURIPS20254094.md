## Problem

Existing reaction prediction models suffer from two critical limitations: they are sensitive to input permutations (molecule ordering, SMILES token ordering), producing inconsistent predictions under trivial reordering, and they fail to explicitly model the substructural interactions between chemically meaningful fragments that drive reactivity. This leads to poor generalization, especially on out-of-sample reactions that better reflect real-world chemical diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254094/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D conformers (atom types and spatial coordinates) of all reactant molecules and product molecules, with conformers generated via ETKDG (up to 100 sampled, one selected per molecule). Shingles are extracted as circular subgraphs up to radius 3, expressed as SMILES strings for the symmetric difference operation, with a cap of 280 shingles per reaction and 100 per molecule. The model outputs a scalar prediction (yield, enantioselectivity, or conversion rate) for regression tasks or a categorical label (reaction type) for classification, produced by a two-layer MLP with GELU activation applied to a learned [SSUM] summary token.

## Method

ReaDISH encodes each molecule with the SE(3)-invariant Uni-Mol encoder to obtain atom-level embeddings, then extracts molecular shingles (circular subgraphs up to radius 3) and computes the symmetric difference between reactant and product shingle sets, yielding a permutation-invariant set of structurally changed fragments. Atom embeddings are mean-pooled within each shingle to produce shingle-level representations. Three pairwise metrics—geometric center distance (intra-molecular), binary same-molecule connectivity, and 1 minus Tanimoto similarity of Morgan fingerprints (structural distance)—are encoded via Gaussian Kernel with Pair Type (GKPT) to form a pairwise bias matrix. This bias is injected into L transformer-style attention blocks, where it is updated layer-by-layer via query-key interactions, enabling the model to capture both intra- and inter-molecular shingle interactions. The model is pre-trained on 3.7M reactions with multi-scale pseudo-reaction-type classification (K-means clustering of DRFP fingerprints at K=100, 1000, 4000) before fine-tuning on downstream tasks.

## Results

Under out-of-sample splits (which better reflect real-world deployment), ReaDISH achieves the best R² on BH (0.480), SM (0.549), and N,S-acetal (0.790) among all baselines. Under permutation perturbations (random molecule/SMILES orderings), ReaDISH shows an average 8.76% improvement in R² over non-permutation-invariant baselines (11.11% on BH, 6.40% on SM), with the lowest prediction variance across five runs. On random splits, ReaDISH matches or leads on six of seven datasets, achieving R² of 0.976 on BH, 0.886 on SM, 0.916 on N,S-acetal, and 0.763 on C-heteroatom.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReaDISH outperforms all five baselines on the BH and SM out-of-sample splits in R², with the largest margin over YieldBERT-DA (0.480 vs. 0.215 on BH) and UA-GNN (0.549 vs. 0.420 on SM). Against the strongest baseline ReaMVP, ReaDISH gains +0.001 R² on BH and +0.015 on SM, while also showing substantially lower variance (±0.032 vs. ±0.056 on BH). On the NiCOlit out-of-sample split, UA-GNN reports the highest R² (0.563), though ReaDISH achieves the lowest MAE (2.02) and RMSE (3.42) as reported in Table 9. On random splits, ReaDISH ties UAM for best R² on BH (0.976) and leads on N,S-acetal (0.916) and C-heteroatom (0.763), while UA-GNN edges ahead on SM (0.886 vs. 0.886, essentially tied) and NiCOlit (0.550 vs. 0.539). The permutation-invariance advantage is most pronounced against YieldBERT-DA, which relies on data augmentation rather than architectural invariance and shows the highest prediction variance under input perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReaDISH extends the differential reaction fingerprint (DRFP) framework of Probst et al. (2022), which computes a binary symmetric difference of molecular shingles between reactants and products, by lifting shingles into continuous high-dimensional embeddings and modeling their pairwise interactions with a transformer. It departs from sequence-based (YieldBERT, MolFormer) and graph-based (UA-GNN, UAM) reaction models by operating at the substructure (shingle) level rather than the atom or whole-molecule level, and incorporates the SE(3)-invariant Uni-Mol encoder for 3D geometry. The Gaussian Kernel with Pair Type (GKPT) mechanism from Shuaibi et al. (2021) is repurposed to inject geometric and structural priors into shingle-level self-attention, a design not previously applied to reaction prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Permutation invariance is a practical reliability concern: models sensitive to molecule or SMILES ordering produce inconsistent predictions, undermining trust in real-world deployment. ReaDISH's set-based symmetric-difference formulation addresses this by construction, yielding an 8.76% average R² gain under permutation perturbations and consistent out-of-sample superiority across six benchmarks. However, all methods still struggle on the structurally diverse ELN dataset (R² < 0.3), and the model does not yet incorporate reaction conditions (solvent, temperature, time), which are known to significantly affect outcomes. Open questions include whether the shingle-level interaction paradigm scales to retrosynthesis planning and whether adaptive shingle pruning can reduce the computational overhead of generating up to 280 shingles per reaction.
