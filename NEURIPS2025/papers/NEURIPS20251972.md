## Problem

Existing multimodal representation learning methods focus on alignment or fusion strategies but mistakenly treat aleatoric uncertainty noise within each modality as complementary information, failing to simultaneously exploit consistent and complementary signals while eliminating task-agnostic noise. No prior approach effectively captures all three information types—consistent, complementary, and uncertain—within a unified framework with theoretical grounding.

## Input / Output

FCD is a plug-and-play module inserted between unimodal encoders and the original fusion module in any intermediate-fusion architecture. It takes the raw unimodal feature vectors (e.g., 768-d text embeddings, 2048-d image embeddings, or audio/video features) as input and outputs a single processed feature per modality (synergistic + unique components summed) that preserves the original dimensionality, so the downstream fusion head and prediction layer remain unchanged. The final model produces sentiment intensity scores (regression) or discrete class labels (classification) depending on the task.

## Method

FCD decomposes each unimodal feature into three causality-defined components via two submodules. The Causality Components Decomposition (CCD) module first disentangles the feature into a modality-invariant (synergistic) part and a modality-specific part using two MLPs; the modality-specific part is then further split into a unique (task-related) and a redundant (noise) component by a Unique Redundant Decompose (URD) module that enforces a measure-preserving bijective mapping via SVD-based full-rank constraints, with backdoor-adjustment implemented as an InfoNCE loss maximizing mutual information between the unique feature and the label. A contrastive loss pushes redundant features from different modalities apart to preserve modality discrimination. The Synergistic Distribution Alignment (SDA) module projects synergistic features into a shared subspace, applies a parameter-sharing MLP, and minimizes Sinkhorn divergence (a regularized optimal-transport distance) between all modality pairs to enforce cross-modal alignment, with a residual shortcut to preserve original semantics. The total training loss combines the original task loss with weighted FCD losses (L_MI, L_Dis, L_SDA), requiring no architectural modification to the base model.

## Results

FCD consistently improves all 9 tested multimodal fusion methods across 5 datasets. On CMU-MOSI, the best absolute performance is achieved by MMML+FCD with MAE 59.86 (vs. 61.11 base) and Corr 87.55 (vs. 86.83), while Self-MM+FCD achieves the largest relative MAE reduction (72.51→68.29, −4.22). On CMU-MOSEI, MMML+FCD reaches MAE 50.01 and Corr 81.78. On classification datasets, URMF+FCD attains 92.94% Acc on Food101 and MVCN+FCD achieves the largest gain on MVSA-S (72.44→75.33% Acc, +2.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCD is evaluated as a plug-and-play add-on to 9 existing intermediate-fusion methods, and it improves every baseline on every dataset tested. On CMU-MOSI/CMU-MOSEI, the largest absolute MAE gains come from Self-MM (−4.22/−1.55) and MMIM (−3.95/−0.93), while the already-strong MMML still benefits (−1.25/−1.94 MAE, +0.72/+1.04 Corr). On classification datasets, MVCN shows the biggest Acc jump on MVSA-S (+2.89), URMF leads on Food101 (92.94% Acc), and CLMLF gains the most on HFM (+0.78 Acc). Notably, MCL-MCF's Corr improvement is comparatively small (+0.70/+1.14), which the authors attribute to its progressive-fusion design that gradually weakens complementary information. The consistent gains across heterogeneous architectures (attention-based, contrastive, multi-loss) support the claim that FCD is architecture-agnostic and that simultaneously addressing modality heterogeneity and aleatoric uncertainty is beneficial regardless of the underlying fusion strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the causal decomposition framework of Martínez-Sánchez et al. (2024), which formalizes the split of information into synergistic, unique, and redundant components, and adapts the cascaded decomposition architecture of Zou et al. (2022) and Zhou et al. (2025) for the multimodal setting. It departs from prior multimodal fusion work (Self-MM, MMIM, MCL-MCF, AtCAF, MMML, URMF) that typically addresses only one or two of {consistent, complementary, uncertain} information simultaneously, by explicitly separating aleatoric noise from complementary signal via backdoor adjustment. The alignment mechanism replaces the KL-divergence-based approaches (URMF, VIB-based fusion) with Sinkhorn divergence from optimal transport, motivated by the known instability of KL for gradient descent. The key conceptual departure is treating unimodal uncertainty noise as a confounder in a structural causal model rather than as part of the modality-specific signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (insertable between encoder and fusion module without architectural changes) makes FCD broadly applicable across existing multimodal pipelines, which is practically valuable for practitioners who cannot re-architect their models. The theoretical guarantee linking backdoor adjustment to CMI maximization (Theorem 4.1) provides a principled justification for the decomposition, though the measure-preserving bijective assumption on the extraction function is strong and its practical satisfaction via SVD-based rank enforcement is only approximate. Open questions include whether the approach extends to early or late fusion paradigms, whether the Sinkhorn alignment scales to more than three modalities without combinatorial cost, and whether the theoretical guarantees hold under finite-sample and non-Gaussian conditions. The absence of error bars and the reliance on single-run results limit confidence in the magnitude of reported gains.
