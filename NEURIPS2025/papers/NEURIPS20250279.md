## Problem

Existing Preference Optimization (PO)-based LLM unlearning methods suffer from two critical weaknesses: (1) rigid reward settings—either fully reference-based (NPO) or fully reference-free (SimPO)—limit unlearning effectiveness by either causing early-stage gradient instability or losing instance-specific distinctions; and (2) unlearned models lack robustness, with forgotten knowledge easily recovered through small fine-tuning retraining (as few as 10 samples) or adversarial prompts that bypass safety alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a forget set (prompt-response pairs to be unlearned), a retain set for utility regularization, a frozen reference model (the pre-unlearning LLM), and two auxiliary prompt collections (500 harmful prompts from AdvBench and 500 harmless prompts from Alpaca) used to compute layer-wise refusal feature directions. The output is the updated LLM weights that have suppressed knowledge on the forget set while preserving general utility. Unusually, the training loop operates not only on token-level logits but also on intermediate hidden representations: refusal features are extracted from the residual stream of the last token at each of the last 75% of transformer layers and dynamically re-computed every fixed number of training steps.

## Method

ERU combines two mechanisms. First, an Elastic Unlearning (EU) loss replaces the rigid NPO reward with a joint reference model π̂_ref(y|x) = U(y|x)·(π_θ(y|x)/π_ref(y|x))^α, where α interpolates between reference-free (α=0) and reference-based (α=1) regimes; the resulting elastic reward margin M blends a constant offset γ′ with a z-score-normalized policy-reference log-likelihood ratio, and is held constant via stop-gradient during backpropagation, yielding an adaptive smoothing weight W′_θ that remains effective in early training (unlike NPO's weight which starts near 1). Second, Refusal Feature Adversarial Training (RFAT) formulates robust unlearning as a max-min problem: the inner maximization simulates worst-case adversarial perturbation by projecting out the refusal feature direction (computed as the mean difference of last-token residual activations between harmful and harmless prompts) from hidden states at each layer, applied with probability p to model varying attack strength; the outer minimization then drives the unlearning loss on these perturbed representations, eliminating the need for expensive iterative PGD-style adversarial sample generation while still improving robustness against relearning and adversarial attacks.

## Results

On the RWKU benchmark (LLaMA-2-7B-Chat), ERU achieves the lowest forget scores across all three metrics (FB=29.2, QA=27.1, AA=25.5), outperforming the second-best method NPO (FB=33.6, QA=31.3, AA=32.8) by 16.3% on average. On WMDP-Bio, ERU reduces AccBio to 24.8 and AccCyber to 28.4, a 6% improvement over the next-best method. Under a 1,000-sample fine-tuning retraining attack, ERU retains 83% of its unlearning effectiveness, whereas GA, DPO, and NPO lose 72.9%, 80.2%, and 69.6% respectively after only 10 samples. Under Enhanced GCG adversarial attack, ERU's dangerous knowledge recovery rate is only 10.1%, compared to 75.6% for DPO and 18.4% for AdvNPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERU outperforms all five baselines on the primary unlearning effectiveness metrics. Against NPO (the closest competitor), ERU reduces RWKU forget scores by 16.3% on average and WMDP-Bio accuracy by 6%, while also achieving the best PrivLeak score (12.3 vs. 30.4) on MUSE, indicating less over/under-unlearning. GA achieves extreme MUSE VerbMem/KnowMem scores of 0.0 (complete forgetting) but at the cost of near-total utility collapse (MMLU drops to 48.3, TOFU probability to 0.00), making it practically unusable. DPO and SimNPO both suffer from significant utility degradation (MMLU 46.8 and 50.2 vs. ERU's 50.6) and are far more vulnerable to retraining attacks (80.2% and 34.3% knowledge recovery with 10 samples vs. ERU's 8.1%). AdvNPO, which also targets robustness via latent-space adversarial training, achieves comparable unlearning effectiveness to ERU on some metrics but requires 539 minutes of training versus ERU's 243 minutes, and still shows 18.4% knowledge recovery under Enhanced GCG compared to ERU's 10.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NPO/DPO lineage of preference-optimization-based LLM unlearning by replacing the binary choice between reference-based and reference-free rewards with a continuous 'elastic' interpolation controlled by a hyperparameter α. For robustness, it repurposes refusal feature ablation (RFA) from the LLM safety-alignment literature [26, 37] as a lightweight surrogate for adversarial training, avoiding the expensive PGD-style inner-loop optimization used by AdvNPO. The max-min formulation connects unlearning robustness to the standard adversarial-training framework, but the key departure is that the 'adversary' is a fixed, analytically defined direction in activation space rather than a learned perturbation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 83% unlearning-effectiveness retention after a 1,000-sample retraining attack (versus near-total collapse for NPO, DPO, and SimNPO) is practically significant for deployment scenarios where unlearned models may be further fine-tuned on benign data. The efficiency advantage over AdvNPO (243 min vs. 539 min) makes robust unlearning more accessible. Open questions include whether RFA-based robustness generalizes to attack families that do not operate through the refusal direction (e.g., embedding-space attacks), whether the elastic reward's α should be tuned per-domain, and whether the approach scales to multi-modal or larger models beyond 8B parameters.
