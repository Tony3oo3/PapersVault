## Problem

Existing multimodal judge models (e.g., LLaVA-Critic, Prometheus-Vision) require 100K+ modality-specific preference annotations and fail to generalize across evaluation formats or unseen modalities. The paper asks whether a small corpus of text-only reasoning data (~1K samples) is sufficient to train a cost-efficient, modality-agnostic judge that generalizes zero-shot to images, video, audio, and molecules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253625/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, FLEX-Judge accepts any multimodal input (RGB images, video clips, audio waveforms, 2D/3D molecular structures) alongside a text instruction and one or more AI-generated candidate responses. The model first emits a structured reasoning chain within <think> tags, then produces a judgment in <answer> tags using one of several formats: a scalar score (1–10 or 1–5), a pairwise verdict (A>B, B>A, tie), or a batch-level ranking string. Notably, the model was never fine-tuned on any multimodal evaluation data; all training supervision is text-only, yet it handles diverse label formats and instruction styles at test time.

## Method

FLEX-Judge fine-tunes a pretrained MLLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or Qwen2.5-Omni-7B) exclusively on ~1K text-only reasoning annotations generated by JudgeLRM-7B over the JudgeLM-100K prompt set. Data curation prioritizes high-quality, high-difficulty samples (filtering out rating mismatches with GPT-4o), longer reasoning chains, and lower-temperature on-policy decoding to prevent catastrophic forgetting of the backbone's multimodal encoders. Outputs are post-processed to cover format diversity (single-score vs. pairwise, 1–10 vs. 1–5 scales), enabling the model to follow varied evaluation instructions. The core insight is that structured textual reasoning explanations encode generalizable decision-making patterns that transfer across modalities without any modality-specific supervision. At inference, the model performs zero-shot multimodal judgment and further benefits from inference-time scaling via majority voting or budget forcing, unlike prior non-reasoning evaluators that degrade under such techniques.

## Results

FLEX-VL-7B, trained on only 1K text-only reasoning samples, achieves an average Pearson correlation of 0.332 on MLLM-as-a-Judge (Score), 0.538 accuracy on Pair w. Tie, and 0.655 on Pair w.o. Tie, matching or exceeding GPT-4V and Gemini-2.5-Pro on several subtasks. On VL-RewardBench, FLEX-VL-7B reaches 48.60 overall accuracy (50.79 macro), surpassing LLaVA-Critic-7B (43.7) trained on 113K vision-language pairs. On MJ-Bench, FLEX-VL-7B achieves 57.51% on Safety and 82.32% on Artifact, substantially outperforming commercial APIs like Claude 3 Opus (13.4% Safety, 11.9% Artifact). With majority voting, FLEX-VL-7B reaches 49.29 overall on GenAI-Bench, matching GPT-4o (49.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLEX-VL-7B (1K text-only training) outperforms LLaVA-Critic-7B (113K vision-language pairs) on VL-RewardBench Overall (48.60 vs. 43.7) and MJ-Bench Safety (57.51 vs. not competitive), and surpasses Prometheus-Vision-13B (150K pairs) on all MLLM-as-a-Judge formats (e.g., Score 0.332 vs. 0.213). Against commercial APIs, FLEX-VL-7B matches GPT-4V on Pair w. Tie (0.538 vs. 0.538) and Gemini-2.5-Pro on VL-RewardBench Overall (48.60 vs. 48.4), but trails GPT-4V on Pair w.o. Tie (0.655 vs. 0.717) and Score (0.332 vs. 0.424). On MJ-Bench, FLEX-VL-7B dramatically outperforms Claude 3 Opus on Safety (57.51 vs. 13.4) and Artifact (82.32 vs. 11.9), and beats all training-required reward models (PickScore, HPS-v2.1, ImageReward) by large margins. On GenAI-Bench with majority voting, FLEX-VL-7B (49.29) matches GPT-4o (49.20) and exceeds Gemini-1.5-Pro (48.94). For audio, FLEX-Omni-7B achieves NISQA LCC 0.545 / SRCC 0.590, outperforming all training-free baselines (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B: 0.210/0.243) and Gemini-2.0-Flash (0.408/0.415), though falling short of task-specific fine-tuned SOTA (0.894/0.887). The training-free backbone Qwen2.5-VL-7B is consistently the weakest baseline, confirming that the 1K reasoning fine-tune is the critical factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm [89] to multimodal settings by drawing an explicit analogy to cross-lingual transfer in multilingual LLMs: just as fine-tuning in one language improves others, fine-tuning on text-only reasoning data transfers to visual, audio, and molecular evaluation. It builds directly on JudgeLRM [12] for generating structured reasoning annotations, but departs from prior multimodal judges (LLaVA-Critic [76], Prometheus-Vision [32]) that require 100K+ modality-specific preference pairs by showing that 1K text-only reasoning samples suffice. The inference-time scaling analysis (majority voting, budget forcing) connects to the broader test-time compute literature [50, 69], but the finding that reasoning-trained judges benefit from such scaling while prior non-reasoning judges do not is a new observation in the evaluation context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: it dramatically lowers the barrier to deploying reliable evaluators in underexplored modalities (molecules, 3D, LiDAR) where modality-specific annotation is infeasible, as demonstrated by the FLEX-Mol-LLaMA case study achieving SOTA on PAMPA. A key limitation is that the approach still depends on a pretrained MLLM backbone with native multimodal perception; the text reasoning data teaches the *how* of judging but not the *what* of perceiving, so truly novel modalities absent from the backbone's pretraining remain untested. Open questions include whether the 1K-data recipe generalizes to larger model scales or more complex multi-criteria evaluation, and whether the cross-modal transfer degrades as the target modality diverges further from text.
