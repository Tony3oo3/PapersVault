## Problem

All prior multi-objective bandit algorithms with Pareto regret guarantees are UCB-based; Thompson Sampling, despite its strong empirical performance in single-objective settings, has no theoretical analysis for multi-objective bandits. Additionally, the standard Pareto regret definition ignores cumulative rewards, so a policy that repeatedly selects a single Pareto-optimal arm incurs zero regret even when alternative arm sequences would yield strictly higher total rewards across all objectives.

## Input / Output

At each round, the algorithm receives d-dimensional context vectors (bounded in norm) for a finite set of arms, adversarially chosen, along with L objective-specific reward vectors after arm selection. The key design parameter M (number of optimistic samples per objective) scales as O(log L) to maintain constant joint-optimism probability. The algorithm outputs a single arm per round and guarantees worst-case effective Pareto regret of Õ(d^{3/2}√T), with the effective Pareto front being a strict subset of the standard Pareto front that accounts for cumulative reward dominance.

## Method

MOL-TS maintains a regularized least-squares estimate θ̂_t^(ℓ) for each objective ℓ, then samples M independent parameters per objective from N(θ̂_t^(ℓ), c²V_t^{-1}). Each arm's reward is evaluated optimistically by taking the maximum over the M samples per objective (Equation 2), and the empirical effective Pareto front is computed as the set of arms whose estimated reward vectors are not dominated by any convex combination of other arms' estimates. An arm is then selected uniformly at random from this front. The critical technical contribution is the optimistic sampling strategy: with M = ⌈1 − log L / log(1−p)⌉ samples, the probability that the selected arm is jointly optimistic across all L objectives remains a constant p = 0.15, avoiding the exponential p^L degradation that would arise with a single sample per objective. This enables a worst-case regret analysis via the saturated/unsaturated arm decomposition, yielding the Õ(d^{3/2}√T) bound with only logarithmic dependence on L and M.

## Results

Over T=10,000 rounds with K=50 arms, d=5 feature dimensions, and L=4 objectives (10 random instances), MOL-TS achieves the lowest Pareto regret and effective Pareto regret among all compared algorithms while simultaneously maximizing cumulative rewards across all four objectives. The theoretical worst-case bound is Õ(d^{3/2}√T), matching the best known order for single-objective randomized linear bandits. The optimistic sampling variant (M=O(log L)) further reduces regret compared to standard single-sample TS (M=1), confirming the necessity of the multi-sample strategy for multi-objective settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOL-TS outperforms MOL-UCB (the MOGLM-UCB algorithm from Lu et al. [11]) on both Pareto regret and effective Pareto regret, while also achieving higher cumulative rewards in all four objectives. Against MOL-ε-Greedy (ε=0.05), MOL-TS similarly wins on both regret metrics, though MOL-ε-Greedy paradoxically yields higher total cumulative rewards than MOL-UCB due to the averaging effect of randomly sampling across the Pareto front without a consistent preference direction. In Appendix F, MOL-TS is additionally compared against PFIwR (Kim et al. [10]) under varied settings of K, d, and L, maintaining its advantage. An ablation comparing M=1 versus M=O(log L) confirms that the optimistic sampling strategy is essential: the multi-sample variant achieves meaningfully lower regret, validating the theoretical requirement that M must scale with log L to prevent exponential degradation in the number of objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Thompson Sampling framework for linear contextual bandits (Agrawal & Goyal 2013; Abeille & Lazaric 2017) to the multi-objective setting, where all prior work has relied exclusively on UCB-based algorithms (Drugan & Nowe 2013; Lu et al. 2019; Kim et al. 2023). The key methodological departure is an optimistic sampling strategy that draws M independent parameter samples per objective and takes the maximum, adapting the multi-sample evaluation idea from Oh & Iyengar (2019) to handle the exponentially small joint-optimism probability across L objectives. Conceptually, the paper also introduces the 'effective Pareto front'—a strict subset of the standard Pareto front defined via convex-combination dominance—which refines the performance metric beyond what prior Pareto-regret analyses capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first randomized (TS) algorithm with worst-case Pareto regret guarantees for multi-objective linear contextual bandits, closing a notable gap given TS's consistent empirical superiority over UCB in single-objective settings. The effective Pareto front concept exposes a real blind spot in standard Pareto regret: policies can achieve zero regret while accumulating strictly suboptimal cumulative rewards, a failure mode that worsens with T. Open questions include whether the O(log L) dependence on the number of objectives can be tightened, whether the approach extends to non-linear or adversarial settings, and how the algorithm scales when the arm set is very large (the effective Pareto front computation via convex combinations is combinatorially expensive).
