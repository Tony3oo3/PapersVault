## Problem

Existing one-shot pruning methods for LLMs ignore activation sparsity observed at runtime, and while activation sparsity alone can accelerate decoding, it still requires storing the full dense model in GPU memory. Naively combining unstructured weight pruning with activation sparsity degrades accuracy because the calibration process does not account for the information loss introduced by sparse activations. The paper addresses how to construct dual-sparse (sparse weight + sparse activation) LLM workloads that simultaneously reduce memory, storage, and compute while preserving accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dense LLM (e.g., LLaMA-2/3 at 7B–70B scale), 128 calibration samples of 2048 tokens from C4, and user-specified target sparsity levels for both weights (p^w) and activations (p^x). It produces a dual-sparse model where weight matrices are unstructured-pruned to p^w and, at inference time, only weight rows corresponding to non-zero activations are fetched from HBM to SRAM during GEMV decoding. The calibration runs in under 140 minutes on a single A100 80GB GPU for a 70B model, and the resulting model achieves up to 1.51× end-to-end speedup over the dense baseline while reducing model size to 50%.

## Method

DuoGPT extends the Optimal Brain Compression (OBC) framework by reinterpreting activation sparsity as dynamic structured weight sparsity and incorporating it directly into the layer-wise pruning calibration. The key mechanism is asymmetric calibration: the pruning objective minimizes ||Δw·X̂ − r||²_F where X̂ is the sparse activation from the pruned model and r = w(X̃ − X̂) is the output residual from the dense model, yielding a closed-form weight update that compensates for activation-sparsity-induced errors. An efficient GPU implementation reduces the per-layer complexity from O(nmk² + nk³) to O(mk²) by precomputing three correction terms (a, b, c) via Cholesky decomposition of the Hessian inverse and exploiting row independence, enabling a single shared Hessian across all weight rows. The resulting pruning score S = W²(1/H_pp⁻¹ + a − b + 2c) decomposes into four orthogonal error terms that jointly capture intrinsic weight importance, sparse-dense activation discrepancy, and their cross-correlation, ensuring the mask selection faithfully approximates the exact OBC solution.

## Results

Under 50% dual-sparsity (50% weight + 50% activation), DuoGPT achieves the lowest WikiText2 PPL and highest average zero-shot accuracy across all five LLaMA models tested. On LLaMA-2-7B, DuoGPT reaches 8.58 PPL and 60.52% average accuracy, outperforming SparseGPT (8.98 PPL, 60.02%) and Wanda (9.13 PPL, 58.96%). Against structured pruning baselines at iso-speedup (~1.39×), DuoGPT outperforms ShortGPT by 9.17% average accuracy (60.52% vs 51.35%) while also yielding the smallest model size (3.50B vs 4.72B). Calibration of a 70B model completes in ~2.3 GPU-hours on a single A100 80GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unstructured pruning baselines (SparseGPT, Wanda) at 50% dual-sparsity, DuoGPT consistently achieves the lowest PPL and highest average accuracy across all five LLaMA models, with gains of 0.5–1.5% average accuracy over SparseGPT and 2.5–3.5% over Wanda. Against structured pruning methods (ShortGPT, 2SSP, BlockPruner, SliceGPT) at comparable ~1.3–1.4× speedup on LLaMA-2-7B, DuoGPT outperforms ShortGPT by 9.17% average accuracy (60.52% vs 51.35%), 2SSP by 3.18%, BlockPruner by 6.71%, and SliceGPT by 3.84%, while also producing the smallest model (3.50B vs 4.72–5.29B). Against R-Sparse (activation sparsity + SVD compression) at 50% sparsity, DuoGPT achieves 2.91% higher average accuracy (49.05% vs 46.14%) and a 1.97× smaller model (3.50B vs 6.90B). On the GSM8K reasoning benchmark at iso-SRAM weight loads, DuoGPT (36.77%) edges out TEAL (36.47%) and SparseGPT (36.39%). Against ReLUfication at 22% global sparsity, DuoGPT achieves 28.46% higher average accuracy (64.64% vs 36.18%), reflecting the severe accuracy degradation of ReLU-based activation sparsity without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

DuoGPT extends the Optimal Brain Compression (OBC) framework as implemented in SparseGPT by incorporating activation sparsity directly into the pruning calibration objective. The key conceptual departure is reinterpreting runtime activation sparsity as dynamic structured weight sparsity in GEMV operations, thereby unifying it with static unstructured weight pruning into a single 'dual-sparse' calibration problem. The asymmetric calibration strategy from GPTQV2 (Li et al. 2025) provides the mechanism for compensating information loss: dense-model output residuals serve as correction terms in the OBC closed-form solution. This positions DuoGPT between the unstructured-pruning line (SparseGPT, Wanda) and the activation-sparsity line (TEAL, R-Sparse, CATS), which previously treated the two sparsity sources independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-sparse formulation offers a practical path to LLM deployment that simultaneously reduces model storage (via unstructured weight sparsity) and runtime compute (via activation sparsity), achieving 1.39× speedup with up to 9.17% accuracy gains over structured pruning baselines at iso-speedup. However, the paper acknowledges that a full GPU kernel for the spMspV operation is not yet implemented—current speedup measurements exploit only the activation-sparse (spVM) component, meaning real-world efficiency gains could be higher. Open questions include integrating dual-sparsity into attention layers (KV cache pruning), developing custom spMspV kernels or ASIC accelerators, and whether the uniform-sparsity assumption across layers can be relaxed for further gains.
