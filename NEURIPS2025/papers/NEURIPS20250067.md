## Problem

Vision-Language Models incur substantial computational and memory costs from multi-head attention, particularly in the KV cache and QKV projection matrices. Existing compression approaches either apply SVD separately to individual Q, K, V matrices (wasting shared structure) or rely solely on quantization without accounting for low-rank structural changes. No prior work has unified joint QKV SVD with quantization in a way that handles the outlier amplification introduced by the SVD decomposition itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250067/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a post-training compression applied to standard VLMs (LLaVA-v1.5, LLaVA-Next, SmolVLM) that accept RGB images and text prompts and generate text responses autoregressively. During inference, the compressed model stores only a low-rank intermediate representation C_qkv (of size r×L per token) instead of full K and V vectors, reducing KV cache from 2LE to rL per token. Quantization is applied to both model weights and activations across the visual encoder and all language model layers, with per-token symmetric quantization for activations and per-channel symmetric quantization with learnable clipping for weights.

## Method

QSVD concatenates the Q, K, V weight matrices into a single W_concat ∈ R^{E×3E} and applies joint SVD, yielding a shared down-projection W_qkv^d ∈ R^{E×r} and separate up-projections for each of Q, K, V, reducing weight parameters from 3E² to 4rE and KV cache from 2LE to rL. A cross-layer rank allocation strategy computes per-singular-value importance scores via first-order loss sensitivity (an empirical Fisher information approximation computed in O(E²) memory), then globally ranks all singular values across layers to retain only the top-k most important ones under a fixed rank budget. For quantization, orthogonal rotation matrices H₁ and H₂ are inserted to smooth channelwise outliers in both the input activations X and the compressed intermediate C_qkv, and a per-layer scalar β is learned to optimally distribute singular value magnitudes between the down and up projections, minimizing quantization error on a calibration set.

## Results

On LLaVA-v1.5 13B, QSVD-noQ (SVD-only) achieves 71.79% on ScienceQA-IMG and 56.15% on VizWiz at R₁=60%, R₂=22.5%, matching or exceeding the FP16 baseline (71.78% / 53.63%) while reducing KV cache to 22.5% of original. With W8A8 quantization, QSVD reaches 72.12% SciQA, 55.42% VizWiz, and 62.91% SEED-Bench at R₂=18.75%, surpassing FP16 on ScienceQA. Under the more aggressive W8A4 setting, QSVD maintains 70.12% SciQA and 62.95% SEED-Bench using only 9.38% of the original KV cache. On SmolVLM 2B, QSVD-noQ retains 83.78% SciQA accuracy at R₁=100%, R₂=37.5%, while ASVD and SVD-LLM drop to 53.84% and 65.89% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QSVD-noQ substantially outperforms ASVD across all models: on LLaVA-v1.5 13B, QSVD-noQ achieves 71.79% SciQA vs. ASVD's 64.70% (a 7.1-point gap) at comparable hardware cost, and on SmolVLM 2B the gap widens to 83.78% vs. 53.84%. Against SVD-LLM, QSVD-noQ edges ahead (71.79% vs. 71.44% SciQA on LLaVA-v1.5 13B) and pulls further ahead under aggressive compression (70.80% vs. 70.50% at R₁=40%). In the quantization setting (Table 2), QSVD W8A8 on LLaVA-v1.5 13B beats DuQuant by 2.46 points on SciQA (72.12% vs. 69.66%), QVLM by 1.47 points, and QASVD by 1.87 points, while using only 18.75% of the KV cache versus 50% for all baselines. Under W8A4, the advantage grows: QSVD scores 70.12% SciQA vs. DuQuant's 67.22%, QVLM's 66.46%, and QASVD's 65.34%, at just 9.38% KV cache. Notably, QASVD collapses to near-zero accuracy on SmolVLM under W8A8 (11.94% SciQA), while QSVD retains 76.20%.

## Relation to Prior Work

QSVD builds on the SVD-based compression lineage (ASVD, SVD-LLM, AdaSVD) and the rotational outlier-smoothing methods from QuaRot/DFROT, but departs from prior work in two key ways. First, rather than applying SVD independently to Q, K, and V weight matrices as ASVD and SVD-LLM do, it concatenates them into a single matrix before decomposition, sharing a common down-projection — a design inspired by DeepSeek's Multi-Head Latent Attention but applied as a post-hoc compression rather than a trained architecture. Second, it introduces a cross-layer rank allocation scheme based on a Fisher-information-style importance score for each singular value, and couples the low-rank factorization with a learnable β parameter that controls outlier amplification in the compressed intermediate representation, enabling stable low-bit quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: QSVD achieves up to 13.1× speedup on a 12GB consumer GPU by eliminating CPU offloading, and maintains near-FP16 accuracy at 9.38% of the original KV cache under W8A4. An intriguing secondary finding is that low-rank QKV compression appears to act as a regularizer against hallucination (HallusionBench scores improve over FP16), though the authors cautiously note this needs further investigation. The main limitation is that compression targets only self-attention QKV weights, leaving feed-forward and other linear layers uncompressed; extending joint optimization across all blocks is identified as future work. The complete failure of QASVD (ASVD + QuaRot) under W4A4 while QSVD remains functional highlights that naive composition of SVD and quantization is insufficient — the learnable β and joint decomposition are what make the combination work.
