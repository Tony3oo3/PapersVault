## Problem

Existing reasoning segmentation models (LISA, PixelLM) trained on natural images perform poorly on remote-sensing imagery due to extreme scale variability, spatial clutter, and fine-grained class differences, while geospatial foundation models (RS-GPT4V, EarthGPT) produce only text outputs and cannot generate segmentation masks. This gap is compounded by the absence of large-scale datasets pairing natural language reasoning queries with pixel-level segmentation annotations in satellite imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253756/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LISAT takes a 512×512 RGB satellite image and a natural language reasoning query (e.g., 'Segment the blue car in the bottom right with a long rectangular shape...') as input. The model produces both a text response and, when the LLM emits a special <SEG> token, a pixel-level segmentation mask generated by a SAM decoder. The GRES dataset provides three semantically equivalent query variants per image (original plus two GPT-rephrased versions), and queries are split 50/50 between those with and without explicit spatial references.

## Method

LISAT combines a Remote-CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder, a Vicuna-7B LLM, and a SAM segmentation decoder in a two-stage training pipeline. In stage one, the MLLM is pretrained on PreGRES (1.2M QA pairs across 119K images) using LoRA to acquire broad geospatial reasoning; in stage two, it is fine-tuned on GRES (27,615 query-mask pairs across 9,205 images) plus FP-Ref-COCO and ReasonSeg, with LoRA on the LLM and full fine-tuning of the SAM decoder. The key mechanism extends LISA's embedding-as-mask paradigm: a <SEG> token is added to the LLM vocabulary, and when emitted, its final-layer hidden state is projected via an MLP into SAM's prompt-query space, where it is combined with image features to decode a binary mask. Training uses a joint loss of autoregressive cross-entropy for text and a weighted combination of per-pixel BCE and DICE loss for the mask (λ_bce=2.0, λ_dice=0.5). The GRES dataset itself is semi-synthetic: xView bounding boxes are filtered for uniqueness and visual interest, GPT-4V generates disambiguating natural language queries, and GeoSAM produces pixel-level masks from the bounding boxes.

## Results

On the GRES reasoning segmentation benchmark, LISAT achieves cIoU 0.245±0.023 and gIoU 0.275±0.009 (All objects), outperforming the strongest open-domain baseline PixelLM-13B (cIoU 0.145, gIoU 0.162) by 69% and LISA-7B (gIoU 0.113) by 143.36% in gIoU. For captioning on UCM-Captions, LISAT_PRE achieves BLEU-4 72.34 and CIDEr 355.32, surpassing RS-GPT4V (65.74/333.23) by 10.04% in BLEU-4. On RSVQA-LR, LISAT_PRE attains 92.36% Presence and 92.20% Comparison accuracy, the highest among all compared models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary GRES reasoning segmentation task, LISAT decisively outperforms all four open-domain baselines (LISA-7B, LISA-13B, PixelLM-7B, PixelLM-13B) on both cIoU and gIoU across All, Small, and Large object categories, with the largest relative gains on Small objects (e.g., gIoU 0.240 vs. 0.062 for LISA-7B, a 287% improvement). For captioning, LISAT_PRE beats RS-GPT4V by 10.04% BLEU-4 on UCM-Captions (72.34 vs. 65.74) and by a wide margin over LLaVA-v1.5/v1.6 (5.54/5.44 BLEU-4), but is slightly outperformed by RS-GPT4V on Sydney-Captions (54.24 vs. 62.23 BLEU-4) and RSICD (36.10 vs. 36.83 BLEU-4), though it wins on CIDEr for RSICD (118.39 vs. 102.94). On NWPU-Captions, LISAT_PRE matches EarthGPT (65.8 vs. 65.5 BLEU-4, both 32.2 SPICE) and substantially outperforms MLCA-Net (47.8 BLEU-4). On RSVQA-LR, LISAT_PRE achieves the highest Presence (92.36%) and Comparison (92.20%) accuracy, while RS-GPT4V-LoRA-FT edges it out marginally on Count (70.34% vs. 70.24%). Overall, LISAT dominates on the novel geospatial segmentation task and is competitive or superior on standard geospatial VLM benchmarks, with the notable exception of Sydney-Captions where RS-GPT4V retains a lead.

## Relation to Prior Work

LISAT extends the embedding-as-mask paradigm from LISA to the remote-sensing domain, replacing LISA's generic CLIP+Llama backbone with a geospatial-specific Remote-CLIP+Vicuna MLLM pretrained on the newly introduced PreGRES corpus. It departs from prior geospatial foundation models (RS-GPT4V, EarthGPT, GeoChat) that produce only textual outputs by adding a SAM-based segmentation decoder, and from open-domain reasoning segmentation models (LISA, PixelLM, GSVA) by addressing the unique challenges of top-down satellite imagery—extreme scale variation, fine-grained class distinctions, and spatial clutter. The two-stage training pipeline (PreGRES pretraining → GRES fine-tuning) is a novel data-driven contribution that no prior geospatial VLM has attempted for pixel-level outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open dataset and benchmark for geospatial reasoning segmentation, filling a clear gap: prior remote-sensing VLMs cannot produce masks, and prior segmentation VLMs cannot handle satellite imagery. However, absolute performance remains low (0.275 gIoU), the ground-truth masks are semi-synthetic (GeoSAM-generated from xView bounding boxes), and the scaling curve plateaus at ~7K images, suggesting that data diversity beyond xView's 60+ classes is the key bottleneck. Open questions include whether temporal reasoning, multi-sensor inputs (SAR, LiDAR), and human-authored queries (vs. GPT-generated) would substantially improve generalization, and whether the GeoSAM mask noise in GRES is a ceiling on achievable accuracy.
