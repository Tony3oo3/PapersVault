## Problem

Prior analyses of gradient-based learning of multi-index models either restricted to narrow width (P=O(1)), assumed identical second-layer coefficients, or relied on layer-wise training that deviates from standard practice. This paper addresses the extensive-width regime (P→∞) with a diverging condition number in the second layer, asking whether a natural single-phase SGD algorithm can achieve polynomial complexity and whether the superposition of sharp per-task learning transitions yields smooth power-law scaling in the cumulative loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251280/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. isotropic Gaussian samples x~N(0,I_d) and a teacher model with P orthonormal signal directions and non-negative coefficients a_p (normalized so Σa_p²=1, typically following a power law a_p∝p^{-β}). The student is a width-m two-layer network with 2-homogeneous parameterization (second-layer weights coupled to first-layer norms), trained via online SGD under MSE loss. The primary outputs are sharp per-direction recovery times T_p, the population MSE loss trajectory L(t), and closed-form scaling law exponents relating loss to student width m, number of SGD steps t, and ambient dimension d.

## Method

The student network uses a 2-homogeneous parameterization f(x)=Σ_k‖v_k‖²σ(v̄_k·x) that couples norm growth to directional convergence, trained simultaneously on both layers via online SGD under MSE loss (no layer-wise decomposition). A greedy maximum selection procedure orders student neurons by initial weighted overlap a_q·ν̄_{k,q}^{2I-2}, establishing which neuron learns which teacher direction and in what sequence. The key technical insight is an automatic deflation mechanism: once a direction is recovered, its contribution to the gradient vanishes for remaining neurons, approximately decoupling the P single-index subproblems despite their interaction through the squared loss. The analysis proceeds via a continuous induction on gradient flow (showing sharp transitions at time T_p≈(η·a_{π(p)}·ν̄_{p,π(p)}^{2I-2}(0))^{-1}) followed by a martingale-plus-drift discretization to online SGD, with the learning rate scaled to the weakest *learned* signal (not the weakest overall) to handle the large condition number. In the power-law setting a_p∝p^{-β}, the superposition of P staircase-like loss drops at geometrically spaced times produces the smooth scaling law L(t)∝(t·η·d^{1-I})^{(1-2β)/β}∨m^{1-2β}.

## Results

The main theorem establishes that online SGD recovers the top P* teacher directions with sample and runtime complexity n ≍ T ≍ a_{P*}^{-2} · d^{k*−1} poly(P), which is polynomial in all problem parameters including the condition number κ = a_max/a_min. In the power-law setting a_p ∝ p^{−β} (β > 1/2), the population MSE follows the scaling law L(t) ∼ (tηd^{1−k*/2})^{(1−2β)/β} ∨ m^{1−2β}, where the first term is the optimization bottleneck and the second is the approximation bottleneck. Under the compute-optimal allocation m ∼ T^{1/(1+β)}, the loss scales as L ∼ T^{(1−2β)/(1+β)}; for β = 0.8 this gives L ∼ (mt)^{1/3}, which is confirmed empirically in Figure 2. The sharp transition time for the p-th neuron is T_p ∼ p^β d^{I−1}/η, confirming that individual tasks exhibit abrupt phase transitions whose superposition yields the smooth power-law.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without empirical baselines, so comparisons are against prior theoretical results. The key improvement over OSSW24 is that their layer-wise training procedure requires m ≳ P^{Ω(1/a_min)} student neurons (exponentially many in the condition number), whereas this work achieves m = Θ̃(P*) with a natural single-phase SGD algorithm, yielding polynomial (rather than exponential) dependence on κ. The scaling law exponents match those predicted by the additive-model toy theories of MLGT24 and NFLL24, and the unstable discretization exponent (1−2β)/(2β) is consistent with linear-model scaling law analyses in BAP24, LWK+24, and PPXP24, though the underlying mechanism here involves nonlinear feature learning rather than linear regression. The empirical validation in Figure 2 (d=2048, P=1024, σ=h_4, β=0.8) confirms the predicted compute-optimal slope L ∼ (mt)^{1/3}, with the empirical curve slightly steeper due to finite-width truncation of the infinite power-law sum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on SGD learning of single-index and multi-index models (Ben Arous et al. 2021; Damian et al. 2022; Ren & Lee 2024) from the narrow-width regime (P = O_d(1)) to the extensive-width regime (P → ∞), where the effective dynamics become infinite-dimensional. It departs from prior multi-index analyses (Oko et al. 2024; Simsek et al. 2024) that either assumed identical second-layer coefficients or incurred exponential dependence on the condition number, by showing that single-phase MSE training with a 2-homogeneous parameterization (inherited from tensor decomposition literature, Li et al. 2020; Ge et al. 2021) naturally deflates learned tasks and achieves polynomial complexity. The greedy maximum selection scheme for ordering student neurons is adopted from Ben Arous et al. (2024), and the martingale-plus-drift proof technique follows the standard toolkit from Ben Arous et al. (2021) and Ren & Lee (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rigorous theoretical mechanism connecting the well-observed phenomenon of sharp per-task phase transitions to the smooth power-law scaling laws empirically seen in large-scale training, resolving a tension between these two perspectives. The key open questions include extending to anisotropic data (where a two-parameter scaling law may emerge), designing decaying learning rate schedules that achieve the optimal 'unstable' scaling at any time, and covering the IE(σ) = 2 case where the decoupling argument breaks down. The results are confined to Gaussian inputs, even activations, and orthogonal teacher directions, so generalization to realistic data distributions and non-orthogonal features remains a significant gap.
