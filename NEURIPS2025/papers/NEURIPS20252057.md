## Problem

Function-on-function regression (FoFR) requires modeling mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces, but existing approaches either project functions onto fixed basis expansions or kernels (limiting data-driven discovery) or treat functions as fixed-grid vectors (ignoring inherent continuity and irregular sampling). No prior method simultaneously handles arbitrarily sampled inputs, captures intra-functional continuity and inter-functional nonlinear couplings, and adapts to feature heterogeneity across functions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252057/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a d-tuple of input functions observed at irregular, non-uniform time points (each sample may have a different number of observations, and input/output grids need not align) and produces an m-tuple of continuous output functions evaluable at arbitrary query locations. All integrals are interpreted in the Young sense, requiring only finite 1-variation of the driving paths, so the output is provably invariant to the choice of discretisation grid. The decoder naturally accommodates misaligned or irregular target indices without resampling.

## Method

FAME builds a continuous attention encoder on a bidirectional Neural Controlled Differential Equation (NCDE): a forward and a backward CDE integration produce smoothly varying Query, Key, and Value trajectories so every query instant attends to both past and future context. A per-function Mixture-of-Experts router (a lightweight 1-D convolution + global average pooling summary fed through a softmax gate) assigns a convex combination of K specialist vector fields to each input function, enabling adaptive handling of heterogeneous scales, frequencies, and noise regimes. Multi-head cross attention then fuses the per-function representations at every time point to model inter-functional nonlinear couplings. A final NCDE decoder maps the stacked latent path to continuous output functions. The entire pipeline is end-to-end differentiable, sampling-invariant by construction (Young integrals depend only on the underlying functions), and comes with Lipschitz stability, universal approximation, and Rademacher-complexity generalisation guarantees.

## Results

On synthetic function-on-function regression (Case 1, Ns=200), FAME achieves a test MSE of 0.0783, outperforming the strongest baseline FNN (0.2083) by 62% and the Kernel Method (0.1728) by 55%. On real-world benchmarks, FAME attains MSE of 0.0748 on Hawaii Ocean Salinity, 0.0034 on Human3.6M Eating, and 0.0262 on ETT-small Oil Temperature, consistently ranking first across all six real-world tasks. Under stress tests (feature heterogeneity, noise, high dimensionality), FAME maintains its lead, e.g., achieving 0.0798 MSE in the heterogeneity case (Case 4) versus 0.1928 for the best baseline (Kernel Method).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAME outperforms all five baseline families across every synthetic and real-world setting. Against basis-expansion pipelines (B-spline/Fourier paired with Linear, Ridge, Lasso, or Elastic Net), FAME achieves roughly 4–5× lower MSE on synthetic data (e.g., 0.0783 vs. 0.3650 for the best Elastic Net variant in Case 1, Ns=200). FPCA and the operator-valued Kernel Method are the strongest classical baselines; FAME still leads by 45% (0.0783 vs. 0.1728 for Kernel) on synthetic data, though the gap narrows on real-world tasks where the function space is smoother (e.g., 0.0748 vs. 0.0754 on Hawaii Salinity). Gaussian Process regression is competitive in low-noise settings but degrades sharply under irregular sampling (Case 3: 0.2926 vs. FAME's 0.0796 at Ns=200). The functional neural network (FNN) is the closest deep-learning competitor, yet FAME maintains a 2–3× MSE advantage on synthetic benchmarks and a consistent 5–15% edge on real-world tasks, with the largest gains appearing under feature heterogeneity (Case 4: 0.0798 vs. 0.2102) and high input dimensionality (Case 7, d=10: 0.3330 vs. 0.5164).

## Relation to Prior Work

FAME builds directly on the Neural CDE framework (Kidger et al., 2020) for continuous-time dynamics on irregularly sampled paths, and on the Transformer attention mechanism (Vaswani et al., 2017) for Q/K/V-based feature interaction. The key departure is twofold: (1) standard NCDEs are strictly causal (forward-only), whereas FAME introduces a bidirectional NCDE that integrates both forward and backward to capture global functional context; and (2) standard attention operates on discrete token sequences, whereas FAME reformulates attention as a Young integral over continuous function space, eliminating the need for fixed sampling grids. The MoE routing layer is borrowed from the NLP literature but repurposed here to handle feature heterogeneity across input functions with different scales, smoothness, or noise regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAME is the first FoFR model that operates natively in continuous function space without predefined bases or discretisation grids, and it provides a complete theoretical package (Lipschitz stability, universal approximation, Rademacher complexity bounds) that is rare in the FDA literature. The practical implication is that the model handles irregularly sampled inputs, misaligned output grids, and heterogeneous function characteristics in a single end-to-end pipeline. A noted limitation is that when the underlying operator is simple or low-rank, lighter classical methods may offer a better accuracy-complexity trade-off. Future work extends the continuous-attention principle to functional classification and function-on-scalar prediction, where the sampling-invariance property is expected to transfer.
