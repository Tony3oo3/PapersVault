## Problem

The standard Monte Carlo estimator for KL divergence between language models is unbiased but suffers from high variance and can produce negative estimates of a non-negative quantity. Existing alternatives, such as Schulman's control variate estimator, lack a theoretical guarantee of lower variance and can exhibit unbounded variance in practice. The paper asks whether a provably unbiased estimator with variance no greater than the MC estimator can be constructed without additional computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253745/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two language models (a policy p_θ and a reference q), M strings sampled from p_θ, and the full next-token distributions over the vocabulary at every position in each sampled string. The latter are already produced by the forward pass, so no extra model evaluations are needed. The output is a scalar KL divergence estimate (guaranteed non-negative) and, when used in training, a vector gradient estimate of KL(p_θ‖q) with respect to θ, which can be computed via automatic differentiation in the off-policy setting.

## Method

The core contribution is a Rao-Blackwellized Monte Carlo estimator that replaces the single sampled log-ratio at each token position with the exact KL divergence between the two models' next-token distributions conditioned on the sampled prefix: μ_RB = (1/M) Σ_m Σ_n KL(p̄(·|Y_{<n}^{(m)}) ‖ q̄(·|Y_{<n}^{(m)})). The paper proves (Theorem 2) that this estimator is unbiased and has variance at most that of the standard MC estimator, using a three-step argument that Rao-Blackwellizes each step-wise summand, shows variance reduction for truncated estimators via Jensen's inequality, and extends to the infinite-length limit. Each term is itself a KL divergence, so the estimator is guaranteed non-negative. The authors further derive an analogous Rao-Blackwellized estimator for the gradient of KL (Theorem 4–5), which in the off-policy setting can be differentiated automatically, and show empirically that it reduces gradient-norm variance by ~25% and makes RLHF training more stable, with 78% of Pareto-optimal models coming from RB-trained runs.

## Results

On the IMDB sentiment-controlled generation task (GPT-2), the RB estimator achieves the lowest standard deviation across all sample sizes: 0.11 (M=1), 0.05 (M=5), and 0.03 (M=10), compared to 0.16, 0.07, and 0.05 for the standard MC estimator. In the RLHF fine-tuning loop, the RB gradient estimator reduces gradient-norm variance by 24.6% (45.44 vs. 59.90 for MC). Across 36 fine-tuned models (18 per estimator, varying KL coefficient β), 78% of the overall Pareto frontier points (reward vs. KL) come from RB-trained models, rising to 95% when restricting to KL < 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed RB estimator against two families of baselines: the Horvitz-Thompson (HT) estimator and Schulman's control-variate (CV) estimator. The HT estimator shows no meaningful variance reduction over plain MC (identical standard deviations in Table 1), and on the larger Zephyr-7B/Mistral-7B setup (Table 2) it actually exhibits much higher variance (12.64 vs. 3.19 at M=1) and numerical bias. Schulman's CV estimator with α=1 is both biased (estimates 6.28 vs. the true 6.76) and has dramatically higher variance (2.54 vs. 0.16 at M=1 on IMDB), with unbounded variance on certain adversarial prompts. Even with the optimal α estimated from a pilot sample, the CV estimator fails to reduce variance below MC. The RB estimator consistently achieves the lowest standard deviation across all settings and sample sizes, is unbiased, and is guaranteed non-negative, making it the clear winner on every metric examined.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the classical Rao-Blackwellization technique from statistics to the problem of estimating KL divergence between language models, a setting where it had not previously been exploited. It builds on the local KL decomposition from Malagutti et al. [24] to derive both the estimator and its gradient counterpart, and provides a comprehensive formal analysis of existing alternatives including Schulman's widely-used control variate estimator [33] and the Horvitz-Thompson estimator [16]. The key departure is showing that the standard MC estimator can be improved with zero additional computational overhead by analytically computing the expectation over each next-symbol distribution rather than relying on the single sampled token at each position.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KL estimation is a bottleneck in RLHF, interpretability, and knowledge distillation, and the RB estimator offers a drop-in replacement that is provably unbiased, non-negative, and has lower variance than MC with no extra forward passes. The finding that Schulman's α=1 control variate can exhibit unbounded variance for certain prompts is practically important given its ubiquity in open-source RLHF libraries. However, all experiments use GPT-2 on a sentiment task, and the authors explicitly note that validation on larger models and more complex alignment objectives remains open, limiting confidence in the practical impact at scale.
