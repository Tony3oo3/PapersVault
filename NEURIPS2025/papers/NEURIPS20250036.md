## Problem

While dense measurement matrices for sparse signal recovery have well-characterized information-theoretic and algorithmic phase transitions, the sparse measurement setting lacked matching sufficient conditions for recovery. This paper closes that gap by establishing the exact sample-size threshold at which the MLE reliably recovers the signal support, and further addresses the practical question of how much an originally dense measurement matrix can be sparsified while still permitting recovery.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse Gaussian measurement matrix X ∈ R^{n×p} (each entry is a Bernoulli(d/p) mask times a standard Gaussian), a noisy observation vector Y = Xβ* + Z with Z ~ N(0, σ²I), and a known sparsity level s, and produces the support of the binary signal β* ∈ {0,1}^p (equivalently the signal itself). In the sparsification setting, observations are generated with a dense Gaussian X but recovery uses a sparsified version X̃ = B∘X together with naively rescaled observations Ỹ = (d/p)Y, introducing a bias that the analysis must account for.

## Method

The core technique is a large-deviation bound on the probability that a wrong support S has lower MSE than the true support S*, followed by a union bound over all competing supports. For the sparse measurement case (Theorem 1), the per-row MGF of the loss difference reduces to E[e^{-θ(Σ B_ij N_ij)²}], which is evaluated by conditioning on the Bernoulli mask (yielding a chi-squared MGF) and then applying a CLT to the number of active positions plus a uniform-integrability lemma to pass from almost-sure to expectation convergence. For the sparsification case (Theorem 3), the rescaled observations Ỹ do not correspond to true projections of the sparsified data, so the MSE difference decomposes into a product of two correlated Gaussians; the MGF is then bounded via a closed-form lemma for E[e^{N₁N₂}] of correlated normals, and the final threshold is conditional on a mild uniform-integrability conjecture. The resulting threshold n_INF^SP = Θ(s log(p/s) / log(ds/p)) matches the necessary condition of Wang et al., revealing an information-theoretic phase transition and making the 'price of sparsity' Γ = log(s)/log(ds/p) explicit.

## Results

The paper establishes that in the high-SNR regime (ds/p → +∞), the information-theoretic threshold for sparse recovery using sparse measurements is n_INF^SP = Θ(s log(p/s) / log(ds/p)), matching the necessary condition of Wang et al. and revealing a sharp phase transition. The 'price of sparsity' — the inflation factor over the dense-case threshold — is Γ = Θ(log s / log(ds/p)), which can range from just above 1 to +∞ depending on the sparsity regime. For the sparsification setting (s = αp, d = ψp), the sufficient threshold is n_INF^Sp-ified = Θ(p/ψ²) in the strong-sparsification regime, implying a sparsification budget of ψ_budget = Θ(√(p/n)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Wang et al. [19], the paper's Theorem 1 provides the matching sufficient condition to their necessary condition, closing the gap and establishing a sharp information-theoretic phase transition at n_INF^SP = 2s log(p/s) / log(ds/p) — a result neither prior work achieved alone. Compared to the dense-case threshold of Reeves et al. [16] (n_INF = 2s log(p/s) / log s), the sparse threshold is strictly larger by the price-of-sparsity factor Γ = log s / log(ds/p) > 1, quantifying the exact sampling cost of measurement sparsity. Against Omidiran and Wainwright [15], the paper's information-theoretic result holds under a strictly weaker sparsity assumption (ds/p → +∞, i.e. d = ω(1) when s = Θ(p)) compared to their algorithmic threshold which requires d = ω(p^{2/3}) in the linear-sparsity regime, though the paper's MLE-based recovery is computationally intractable (exponential time) while LASSO achieves polynomial-time recovery under their stronger assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper completes the information-theoretic picture for sparse recovery with sparse measurement matrices by establishing a sufficient condition (Theorem 1) that matches the necessary condition previously shown by Wang et al. [19], thereby revealing a sharp phase transition analogous to the one known in the dense setting (Reeves et al. [16], Gamarnik and Zadik [8]). The proof technique—Chernoff bounds on the MLE loss difference combined with a union bound over candidate supports—follows the standard large-deviation toolkit used in the dense-case literature, but the sparse structure of the measurement matrix introduces additional technical challenges in bounding the moment generating function. The sparsification result (Theorem 3) extends the 'missing covariates' framework of Loh and Wainwright [12] from algorithmic ℓ₂-error bounds to information-theoretic support-recovery thresholds, and the algorithmic comparison draws on Omidiran and Wainwright [15], whose Lasso guarantee requires a slower sparsity decay than the paper's information-theoretic threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first complete (necessary + sufficient) characterization of the information-theoretic phase transition for sparse recovery with sparse measurements, making the 'price of sparsity' factor log(s)/log(ds/p) explicit and quantifying the sampling-complexity-versus-measurement-sparsity trade-off for practitioners designing measurement systems. The sparsification result—that arbitrarily aggressive sparsification (ψ → 0) still permits recovery with O(p/ψ²) samples—is a useful design guideline, though it is conditional on an unproven uniform-integrability conjecture. Key open questions include: whether the all-or-nothing property extends to the sparse setting, what the algorithmic (polynomial-time) threshold is in the more sparse regime beyond Omidiran–Wainwright's assumptions, and whether recovery becomes information-theoretically impossible in the sub-linear sparsification regime (d = o(p)), as the authors conjecture.
