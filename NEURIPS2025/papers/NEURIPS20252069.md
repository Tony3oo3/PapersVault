## Problem

Existing federated mutual learning methods perform only direct output matching between private and proxy models, overlooking deeper mismatches in their learned representations and decision behaviors (termed semantic-decision dual drift). This shallow alignment leaves critical representation- and decision-level discrepancies unresolved, causing knowledge transfer to plateau and constraining client performance under dual model and data heterogeneity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252069/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client processes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 150×150 for Flowers102, 256×256 for Skin-Lesions-14) through a heterogeneous private model (e.g., 4-layer CNN, GoogleNet, MobileNet-v2, ResNet variants, ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32) and a homogeneous proxy model, producing class labels. Beyond standard forward passes, the method additionally generates HAPM-mixed samples at patch granularity (√g × √g blocks with per-patch Beta-sampled mixing ratios) to probe model disagreement, and the server aggregates only class-level feature prototypes (K=512-dim) and logit vectors rather than full model parameters.

## Method

FedKWAZ introduces a two-stage mutual learning framework that identifies and exploits Knowledge Weak-Aware Zones (KWAZ) where private and proxy models exhibit maximal cognitive divergence. In Stage I (global), the server aggregates class-level semantic prototypes and decision distributions from all clients to construct unified anchors for coarse-grained bidirectional alignment. In Stage II (local), the Hierarchical Adaptive Patch Mixing (HAPM) module generates multiple mixed samples by partitioning images into √g×√g patches with random spatial jitter and per-patch Beta-distributed interpolation, while the Knowledge Discrepancy Perceptron (KDP) searches over (α, g) pairs to select the configuration inducing the largest model divergence. The selected zones are decomposed into SWAZ (symmetric, measured by MSE in feature space) and DWAZ (asymmetric, measured by temperature-scaled KL divergence in decision space), each driving path-specific bidirectional distillation losses that are combined with a base mutual alignment loss for a second gradient descent step. This design eliminates the need for frequent proxy model synchronization, reducing per-round communication to class-level prototypes and logits (3.50M vs. 37M for FML/FedMRL) while achieving targeted refinement of the most cognitively misaligned regions.

## Results

FedKWAZ achieves the best accuracy across all five datasets under both data heterogeneity settings. Under the Dirichlet (Practical) split, it reaches 90.39% on CIFAR-10, 51.69% on CIFAR-100, 57.52% on Flowers102, and 30.85% on Tiny-ImageNet, outperforming the strongest baselines (FedTGP, FedKTL) by 2.3–4.5 percentage points. Under the Pathological split, gains are even larger: 66.39% on CIFAR-100 (+4.66% over FedTGP) and 38.61% on Tiny-ImageNet (+3.87% over FedKTL). In the most heterogeneous model setting (HtFE₈-HtC₄), FedKWAZ achieves 47.91% on CIFAR-100, surpassing FedTGP (44.65%) by 3.26%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedKWAZ outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and both heterogeneity settings. The closest competitors are FedTGP and FedKTL: on CIFAR-100 (Dirichlet), FedKWAZ achieves 51.69% vs. FedTGP's 47.15% and FedKTL's 47.09%, a gap of ~4.5 pp. Under the Pathological split, the advantage widens to 4.66 pp over FedTGP on CIFAR-100 (66.39% vs. 61.73%) and 3.87 pp over FedKTL on Tiny-ImageNet (38.61% vs. 34.74%). Mutual-learning baselines (FML, FedKD, FedMRL) trail significantly, with FedMRL reaching only 42.22% on CIFAR-100 (Dirichlet) and 58.55% (Pathological), roughly 9–8 pp below FedKWAZ. Prototype- and generator-based methods (FedProto, FedGen, FedKTL) show the largest degradation under model heterogeneity (HtFE₈-HtC₄), dropping to 30.80%, 40.66%, and 44.42% respectively on CIFAR-100, while FedKWAZ maintains 47.91%. In the large-client scalability test (200 clients, ρ=0.5), FedKWAZ retains a 3.89 pp margin over the best baseline (FedKTL at 34.29%), confirming robustness under data fragmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated mutual learning paradigm (FML, FedKD) where clients train private and proxy models in parallel with bidirectional distillation. The key departure is identifying that prior methods perform only shallow output-level alignment, ignoring deeper representation and decision-space mismatches (termed 'semantic-decision dual drift'). FedKWAZ introduces Knowledge Weak-Aware Zones (KWAZ), decomposed into semantic and decision sub-zones, discovered via a novel Hierarchical Adaptive Patch Mixing (HAPM) mechanism and a Knowledge Discrepancy Perceptron (KDP) that selects the most divergent mixed samples. The two-stage design—global class-level prototype/logit aggregation followed by local KWAZ-guided refinement—also distinguishes it from prototype-based (FedProto, FedTGP) and generator-based (FedGen, FedKTL) approaches by avoiding full model or generator transmission.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in heterogeneous FL: proxy-model-based mutual learning methods (FML, FedKD, FedMRL) incur 33–37M parameter communication per round, while FedKWAZ reduces this to ~3.5M by transmitting only class-level semantic prototypes and decision distributions. The 4–5% accuracy gains over strong baselines (FedTGP, FedKTL) across five datasets and multiple heterogeneity settings suggest that targeting specific cognitive misalignment zones is more effective than uniform output matching. Open questions include whether the KWAZ concept generalizes beyond image classification to other modalities, whether the KDP search overhead scales with model complexity, and whether the fixed feature dimension K=512 assumption limits applicability to very deep or very shallow architectures.
