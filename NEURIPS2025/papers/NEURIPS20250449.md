## Problem

Existing machine learning methods for Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian prediction treat the task as deterministic pointwise regression, failing to capture the structured distributional correlations within Hamiltonian matrices. Moreover, Hamiltonians exhibit a high-order block-level SE(3) equivariance (governed by Clebsch-Gordan coupling of angular momentum quantum numbers) that is more restrictive than the global equivariance handled by prior equivariant architectures, making accurate and physically consistent prediction challenging.

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular configuration (atomic coordinates and D-dimensional atom features), the overlap matrix S, and during the flow ODE the current noisy Hamiltonian H_t at time t, producing the target Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian matrix H of size B×B where B is the number of basis functions. The Hamiltonian is structured into K×K blocks indexed by (n_k, ℓ_k) quantum number pairs, and each block must transform covariantly under SO(3) via the corresponding Wigner D-matrices. Derived outputs include orbital energies (HOMO, LUMO, HOMO-LUMO gap) computed by solving the generalized eigenvalue problem on the predicted Hamiltonian.

## Method

QHFLOW formulates Hamiltonian prediction as conditional flow matching: a continuous normalizing flow learns an SE(3)-equivariant vector field that transports a simple prior to the target Hamiltonian distribution over time t∈(0,1]. Two SE(3)-invariant priors are designed — a Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE) prior with element-wise Gaussian noise, and a tensor expansion (TE) prior that samples irrep vectors and applies Clebsch-Gordan tensor expansion to produce matrices with the correct block-level covariance structure. The vector field is parameterized by an equivariant GNN extending QHNet with Equiformer-style attention layers, time conditioning via sinusoidal encoding, and Wigner-Eckart projections of H_t and S into irrep features. A post-hoc fine-tuning stage applies a weighted alignment loss (WALoss) to explicitly match predicted orbital energies to DFT references, improving HOMO/LUMO/gap accuracy without degrading Hamiltonian MAE.

## Results

On MD17, QHFLOW reduces Hamiltonian MAE by up to 73% compared to the best prior method (e.g., 4.93 vs. 15.67 μE_h on Water, 3.68 vs. 19.36 μE_h on Uracil). On QH9, QHFLOW achieves 22.95 μE_h Hamiltonian MAE on the stable-id split (vs. 45.48 for SPHNet and 77.72 for QHNet), and with energy-alignment fine-tuning (WA-FT) further reduces LUMO/HOMO/gap errors to 187/92/206 μE_h. The model also accelerates DFT SCF convergence, achieving a 46% total runtime ratio (54% speedup) on QH9 test molecules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QHFLOW outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. SPHNet [26] is the closest competitor, but QHFLOW still halves its Hamiltonian MAE on QH9 (e.g., 22.95 vs. 45.48 μE_h on stable-id; 45.91 vs. 108.19 on dynamic-mol) and on MD17 (e.g., 3.68 vs. 19.36 μE_h on Uracil). QHNet [23] and WANet [25] are substantially worse on QH9, with QHNet's LUMO error reaching 18,257 μE_h versus QHFLOW's 438 μE_h on stable-id. On MD17, the older baselines SchNOrb [19] and PhiSNet [20] are far behind, with SchNOrb's Hamiltonian MAE exceeding 165 μE_h on Water compared to QHFLOW's 4.93. The energy-alignment fine-tuning (WA-FT) further narrows orbital-energy gaps: on QH9 dynamic-geo, WA-FT reduces ε_LUMO from 425 to 137 μE_h and ε_Δ from 547 to 155 μE_h, while slightly increasing Hamiltonian MAE (25.94→27.12), confirming a favorable trade-off for downstream energy properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends QHNet's SE(3)-equivariant GNN architecture by replacing its pointwise regression objective with a conditional flow matching framework, following the equivariant flow matching paradigm of Song et al. (NeurIPS 2023). The key conceptual departure from all prior Hamiltonian prediction methods (SchNOrb, PhiSNet, QHNet, WANet, SPHNet) is reframing the task as distributional matching rather than deterministic regression, with the novel design of SE(3)-invariant priors (GOE and tensor expansion) that respect the blockwise Wigner D-matrix structure of Hamiltonian matrices. The attention-based message passing is adapted from Equiformer, and the energy alignment fine-tuning stage directly repurposes the weighted alignment loss from WANet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 73% Hamiltonian error reduction on MD17 and the demonstrated 54% DFT runtime speedup via SCF initialization make this a practically significant advance for quantum chemistry simulation, where SCF convergence is a major bottleneck. The finding that a generative (flow matching) formulation substantially outperforms direct regression for structured matrix prediction challenges the default assumption that deterministic regression is the appropriate paradigm for equivariant prediction tasks. Open questions include scalability to periodic solids and larger systems, whether the ODE-solving overhead can be eliminated via one-step generation, and whether the approach generalizes to hybrid or double-hybrid functionals beyond PBE and B3LYP.
