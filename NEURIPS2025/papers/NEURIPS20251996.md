## Problem

Existing preference alignment methods for text-to-image diffusion models, particularly DPO-based approaches, rely on paired comparative data and suffer from non-linear sigmoid-based probability estimation that complicates training. Moreover, paired data is labor-intensive to collect and may fail to provide meaningful learning signals when both compared samples are of similar quality. This paper addresses these limitations by formulating preference learning as a ranking problem that requires only positive expert demonstrations, eliminating the need for negative samples or preference pairs entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts and a set of expert demonstration images (top-K pairs selected from Pick-a-Pic v2 by HPSv2 or PickScore, default K=500) as training input, and produces fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 1.5 weights that generate 512×512 RGB images at inference. During training, the policy model generates online negative samples via DPMSolver++ with 20 steps (no classifier-free guidance), while inference uses DDPM with 50 steps and guidance scale 7.5, selecting the median-PickScore image from 5 generations per prompt.

## Method

Diffusion-DRO casts preference alignment as a max-margin inverse reinforcement learning problem, parameterizing the trajectory reward model as a log-ratio of probability densities (Eq. 9). A key theoretical result shows the optimal policy is identical to the reward probability model, collapsing the alternating reward-policy optimization into a single reward-modeling objective. The resulting ranking loss (Eq. 13) has two terms: a supervised-fine-tuning-like term that pulls the reward model toward expert demonstrations, and a push-away term that distinguishes the reward model from policy-generated samples, all expressed in noise-prediction space without any sigmoid or Jensen's inequality. A Thresholded Ranking Loss (TRL) clips the margin once the inductive criterion is satisfied, preventing overfitting to already-correctly-ranked samples. The policy model is periodically synchronized with the reward model (every M steps), enabling stable single-stage training that integrates offline expert data with online policy-generated negatives.

## Results

Diffusion-DRO achieves win rates exceeding 87% against the base SD v1-5 model on PickScore and ImageReward across both Pick-a-Pic v2 Test and HPDv2 Benchmark. Against the strongest DPO-based baselines (Diffusion-DPO and Diffusion-KTO), Diffusion-DRO maintains 74–84% win rates on PickScore, Aesthetic, and ImageReward, though it trails on CLIP Score (41–42% win rate) due to slight deviation from the original text encoder distribution. In the user study, Diffusion-DRO achieves a 75% preference rate over SD v1-5, 56.67% over Diffusion-DPO w/ SFT, and 66.67% over Diffusion-KTO w/ SFT, confirming the automated metric trends.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base SD v1-5 model, Diffusion-DRO wins decisively on PickScore (87.80%), Aesthetic (85.20%), and ImageReward (88.60%) on Pick-a-Pic v2 Test, but loses on CLIP Score (48.40%), which the authors attribute to slight deviation from the original text encoder distribution. Against SPIN-Diffusion, Diffusion-DRO wins on all four metrics (56.20–70.60% win rates), with the largest margin on ImageReward (70.60%), benefiting from its single-stage max-margin IRL formulation versus SPIN's multi-stage self-play pipeline. Against Diffusion-SPO, Diffusion-DRO wins on all metrics (62.80–78.00%), with the strongest advantage on CLIP Score (71.40%) and ImageReward (78.00%). Against Diffusion-DPO, Diffusion-DRO wins on PickScore (78.40%), Aesthetic (83.20%), and ImageReward (84.20%) but loses on CLIP Score (41.40%), a pattern shared with Diffusion-KTO (42.20% CLIP win rate) where the SFT-based baselines preserve the text encoder distribution better. Notably, Diffusion-DRO is fine-tuned directly from SD v1-5, yet it outperforms the stronger 'w/ SFT' variants of Diffusion-DPO and Diffusion-KTO that start from an already SFT-enhanced base model, demonstrating that the ranking objective effectively captures preference signals from expert demonstrations alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Diffusion-DRO sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: (1) DPO-based preference alignment for diffusion models (Diffusion-DPO, Diffusion-KTO, D3PO), which removes the explicit reward model but still requires paired preference data and relies on a non-linear sigmoid for probability estimation, and (2) max-margin inverse reinforcement learning (Abbeel & Ng; Ng & Russell), which learns a policy from expert demonstrations without negative examples. The paper's key departure is showing that the KL-regularized optimal policy is identical to the reward probability model, collapsing the alternating reward-policy optimization into a single denoising objective. This eliminates both the paired-data requirement of DPO methods and the multi-stage self-play pipeline of SPIN-Diffusion, while avoiding the sigmoid-based estimation that plagues prior DPO formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing preference alignment to a single-stage denoising task that only requires positive expert demonstrations, Diffusion-DRO offers a more practical and theoretically grounded alternative to the paired-data paradigm that dominates current diffusion alignment work. The thresholded ranking loss provides a clean mechanism to balance online policy negatives against offline expert data, addressing the distributional limitations of purely offline training. However, the approach is validated only on Stable Diffusion 1.5, and the 'expert demonstrations' are selected via automated proxy metrics (HPSv2, PickScore) rather than genuine human annotations, leaving open whether the method generalizes to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models) and to truly diverse human preference signals. The paper also acknowledges that binary expert/non-expert treatment discards listwise ranking information, suggesting a natural extension path.
