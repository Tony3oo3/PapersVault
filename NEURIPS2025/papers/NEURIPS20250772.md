## Problem

Classification-based object detectors face fundamental optimization challenges when scaling to vast vocabularies (C > 10⁴ categories): positive gradient dilution, where sparse positive signals are overwhelmed by the aggregate of thousands of negative gradients, and hard negative gradient dilution, where informative hard negatives are buried among numerous easy negatives. Existing mitigations like Focal Loss only partially address these issues, leaving a persistent performance gap on benchmarks like V3Det (13,204 categories) compared to smaller-vocabulary settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces bounding boxes with category labels from a vocabulary of up to 13,204 categories. Category queries are initialized from OpenCLIP text embeddings, and the image-guided selection module dynamically reduces the effective category space from C to C′ (typically 100) per image via cross-attention before detection. A two-stage training protocol is used: category queries and the selection module are pre-trained for 10 epochs to establish high target-category recall, then the full detection pipeline is fine-tuned.

## Method

CQ-DINO reformulates category classification as a contrastive alignment task between object queries and learnable category queries, replacing the fixed FFN classification head. The core mechanism is image-guided query selection: cross-attention between all C category queries and image features identifies the top-C′ most relevant categories per image, achieving a ~100× gradient rebalancing factor while implicitly performing hard negative mining. For structured datasets like V3Det, hierarchical tree construction propagates child-node information upward with adaptive weighting (α_v based on log child count) to encode category relationships; for flat datasets like COCO, an 8-head self-attention module learns implicit correlations. The selected category queries feed into GroundingDINO's feature enhancer and cross-modality decoder, where object queries are generated and final predictions are made via contrastive scoring against the selected category subset.

## Results

On the V3Det benchmark (13,204 categories), CQ-DINO achieves 53.0% AP with Swin-L, surpassing the previous best method Prova by 2.1% AP and DINO by 4.5% AP. With the Swin-B-22k backbone, CQ-DINO reaches 52.3% AP, outperforming Prova (50.3%) by 2.0% AP. On the standard COCO benchmark (80 categories), CQ-DINO achieves 58.5% AP with Swin-L, matching or slightly exceeding recent DETR variants like Rank-DETR (58.2%) and DINO (58.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CQ-DINO consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across both benchmarks. On V3Det, the most competitive comparison is against Prova [4] (the prior SOTA for vast vocabulary detection), where CQ-DINO leads by 1.8–2.1% AP across all three backbone configurations. Against the general-purpose DINO [47] baseline, CQ-DINO gains 4.3–4.5% AP, demonstrating the benefit of the category query formulation over the standard classification head. Traditional two-stage and one-stage detectors (Faster R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN, FCOS, ATSS) trail by 8–39% AP, highlighting the fundamental scalability limitations of fixed classification heads at 13k+ categories. On COCO, CQ-DINO (58.5% AP) narrowly beats Rank-DETR (58.2%), DINO (58.0%), and Relation-DETR (57.8%), confirming that the method does not sacrifice performance in limited-vocabulary settings. Generation-based open-world methods (GenerateU at 0.4% AP zero-shot, ChatRex at 1.3% AP) are far outperformed, underscoring their inability to control category granularity for detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

CQ-DINO extends the category query paradigm from Query2Label (classification) and RankSeg (segmentation) into the object detection setting, building its decoder on Grounding DINO's feature enhancer and cross-modality architecture. It departs from the dominant classification-head design (FFN + Focal Loss) used by DINO, Deformable DETR, and Prova by reformulating category prediction as a contrastive alignment between object queries and learnable category queries initialized from CLIP text embeddings. The image-guided query selection mechanism, which dynamically retrieves top-K relevant categories per image via cross-attention, is the key architectural departure that distinguishes CQ-DINO from prior category-query methods that applied selection only at inference time (e.g., RankSeg).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic theoretical analysis of why sigmoid-based classification heads fundamentally fail at 10k+ category scales (positive and hard-negative gradient dilution), giving practitioners a principled explanation for the performance ceiling observed in V3Det challenge methods. The 2.1% AP gain over Prova and the demonstrated scalability to 130k categories make this a practical path toward real-world detection systems with very large taxonomies. Open questions remain around the two-stage training paradigm, the 83.3% category-recall ceiling of the selection module, and whether the approach transfers to truly open-vocabulary or incremental learning settings where the category set is not fixed.
