## Problem

Recurrent neural networks struggle with continual learning: sequentially training on new tasks causes catastrophic forgetting, and compositional reuse of computational building blocks has only been shown to emerge implicitly during simultaneous multi-task training rather than being explicitly leveraged for sequential learning. The paper asks whether explicitly separating the inference of *what* computation to perform (a time-varying computational context) from *how* to perform it (the actual input-to-output transformation) can enable robust continual learning, forward/backward transfer, and rapid compositional generalization to unseen tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253038/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system receives 5-dimensional time-varying input sequences s_t and a task identity label c, and must produce 3-dimensional target response sequences y_t. During training, the what system additionally consumes the target responses y_t (as part of q_t = [s_t, y_t]) for smoothing-based inference, while at test time it operates on inputs alone via filtering. Tasks are structured as sequences of discrete epochs (fixation, stimulus, memory, response variants) with a trial-specific latent variable x (e.g., stimulus direction) that couples observations across epochs, and the six tasks in the benchmark share a common pool of 10 epochs with task-dependent transition structures.

## Method

The architecture comprises two interacting systems. The *what* system is a probabilistic generative model (a mixture of HMMs with task-dependent Markov transitions over epochs and a shared trial variable x) learned via an online incremental EM algorithm that builds its epoch vocabulary incrementally as new tasks arrive, inferring a time-varying context belief p_t(z) per trial. The *how* system is an RNN whose recurrent weights are a convex combination of low-rank components W^rec = Σ_z p_t(z) U_z V_z^T, with context-specific input and output weights, so that each inferred context selectively gates a distinct set of dynamics. Training is fully sequential (one task at a time, no revisiting), with the task model updated via online EM and the RNN trained by Adam on weighted MSE. The key mechanism enabling continual learning is that new tasks composed of previously seen epochs require no new RNN parameters—only the task model's transition structure is updated—while genuinely novel epochs trigger creation of new low-rank components without disturbing existing ones.

## Results

On the compositional generalization task (learning MemoryAnti from scratch after pre-training on M'Pro, M'Anti, and MemoryPro), the proposed two-system approach reaches ~83% accuracy within only 40 trials while keeping the RNN frozen and updating only the task model. In contrast, baselines trained on the same pre-training tasks with full model updates for 512 trials achieve only 56% (Adam), 53% (OWP), and 64% (Hypernetwork) accuracy. For continual learning across six compositional tasks, the task model + context-modulated RNN maintains high test performance on all previously learned tasks throughout sequential training, whereas all baselines exhibit significant forgetting or capacity degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines across continual learning and compositional generalization. (1) EWC [Kirkpatrick 2017] fails to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in RNNs, with performance degrading sharply after each task switch (Fig. 4b). (2) OWP [Duncker 2020] outperforms Adam and EWC on continual learning but suffers from progressive capacity limitation, making later tasks harder to optimize (Fig. 4c); on compositional generalization it achieves only 53% after 512 trials. (3) The neuromodulated RNN [Costacurta 2024], which is architecturally closest to the proposed method but uses end-to-end learned modulation rather than interpretable contextual inference, quickly forgets previously learned tasks during sequential training (Fig. 4d). (4) Hypernetworks [Von Oswald 2019] show limited improvement over EWC and still forget some earlier tasks by the end of training (Fig. 4e); on compositional generalization they reach 64% after 512 trials. The proposed method wins on all comparisons: it maintains performance on all tasks during continual learning, exhibits both forward and backward transfer (which OWP and other baselines lack), and achieves ~83% accuracy on a novel task in just 40 trials with a frozen RNN—far exceeding all baselines that require 512 trials of full-model training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neuromodulated RNN architecture of Costacurta et al. [16], where a second RNN generates a time-varying signal that gates low-rank recurrent weight components, by replacing the learned end-to-end signal with the interpretable output of an unsupervised probabilistic task model (a mixture-of-HMMs with a shared trial variable). It also builds on the contextual inference framework from Heald et al. [1] and Gershman et al. [20], which uses a latent discrete variable to explain distributional shifts, but generalizes it to a family of compositionally related tasks sharing a vocabulary of epochs. The key departure from prior continual learning work in RNNs (EWC [31], OWP [13], hypernetworks [23]) is that compositional reuse of computational building blocks is made explicit through the what/how separation, rather than emerging implicitly from joint training on multiple tasks [13–16].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit probabilistic context inference can solve a problem (catastrophic forgetting in sequential RNN training) that weight-regularization and subspace-projection methods fail to address, and it uniquely exhibits backward transfer — previously learned tasks continue to improve when new tasks are trained — a property rare in continual learning [40]. The compositional generalization result (83% accuracy in ~40 trials by recombining frozen RNN components, vs. 53–64% after 512 trials for baselines) suggests a principled route to rapid skill acquisition. Limitations include the requirement that contexts be distinguishable from observations alone (fixation and memory epochs are merged), the discrete trial variable assumption, and evaluation on a single small task family of synthetic neuroscience tasks, leaving open whether the approach scales to richer, continuous-valued task structures or benefits from RNN-to-task-model feedback.
