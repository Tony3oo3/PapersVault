## Problem

Current GUI navigation agents struggle with long-horizon reasoning because they encode interaction history either as bare action sequences (losing visual state and progress context) or as full-screen screenshots (computationally expensive and forcing truncated context windows). This leads to poor cross-domain generalization and incoherent multi-step behavior, as the agent cannot effectively estimate task progress or maintain goal awareness over extended interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255505/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the agent consumes a single current GUI screenshot (w×h×3), the user's natural-language instruction, and a compact textual history summary (a few sentences) rather than raw prior screenshots or action lists. It produces three serialized outputs in a fixed XML-tagged order: a structured CoT split into Progress Estimation and Decision Reasoning, a parseable action in dictionary form {action, value, position:[x,y]}, and an updated one-to-two-sentence history summary that replaces the previous one for the next step.

## Method

GUI-Rise is built on a Qwen-VL encoder-decoder MLLM and operates a three-subtask cycle (structured reasoning → action prediction → history summarization) at every interaction step. Training is two-stage: first, SFT on pseudo-labeled trajectories where GPT-4o-mini retrospectively generates CoT and summary labels conditioned on ground-truth actions; second, RL via GRPO with three complementary rewards — a binary format reward enforcing the four-tag output structure, a composite action reward (format + type match + bounding-box position), and a novel history-summary reward that, when the current action is correct, performs k additional rollouts using the generated summary as input and scores whether the resulting next-step action is also correct. This history reward directly links summary quality to downstream action performance, creating a self-improving loop that encourages the model to retain task-relevant context without fixed window limits or external semantic processors.

## Results

On Mind2Web (out-of-domain), GUI-Rise with Qwen2-VL-2B achieves 39.7% Step SR on the cross-domain split, surpassing ShowUI-2B (35.2%) and the base Qwen2-VL-2B (30.7%). In the zero-shot setting (trained on GUIAct, tested on Mind2Web), GUI-Rise reaches 29.7% cross-domain Step SR, a 38.7% relative improvement over ShowUI-2B (21.4%). On AITW in-domain, GUI-Rise attains 71.1% overall action accuracy (vs. 70.0% for ShowUI-2B), and in the zero-shot setting achieves 54.1% overall (vs. 35.9% for ShowUI-2B, a 50.7% relative gain). On online benchmarks, GUI-Rise-2B scores 30.6 on MiniWob (zero-shot), 72.8 on MiniWob (fine-tuned), 10.4 on Android World, and 8.7 on OSWorld, all setting new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUI-Rise consistently outperforms ShowUI-2B [19], the closest prior SOTA, across all benchmarks: on Mind2Web cross-domain Step SR it gains +4.5 pts (standard) and +8.3 pts (zero-shot); on AITW zero-shot overall it gains +18.2 pts (54.1 vs. 35.9); and on MiniWob zero-shot it gains +3.5 pts (30.6 vs. 27.1). Against the base Qwen2-VL-2B [38] and Qwen2.5-VL-3B [3] models, GUI-Rise improves cross-domain Step SR by +9.0 and +3.5 pts respectively in the standard setting, confirming that the structured reasoning and history summarization framework adds value beyond the backbone. On AITW in-domain, GUI-Rise (71.1%) edges out ShowUI-2B (70.0%) and SeeClick [8] (59.3%), while the zero-shot gap widens dramatically (54.1% vs. 35.9% for ShowUI, 34.7% for base Qwen2-VL-2B). On online benchmarks, GUI-Rise-2B surpasses ShowUI-2B on MiniWob (30.6 vs. 27.1 ZS; 72.8 vs. 71.5 FT), Infigui-2B [21] on Android World (10.4 vs. 9.0), and UI-Tars-2B [25] on OSWorld (8.7 vs. 6.5). The paper does not directly compare against MindAct [9], GPT-4 [28], OmniParser [24], CogAgent [13], ChatGPT-CoT [51], or PaLM2-CoT [30] under identical training conditions; these are reported as reference baselines from their original papers, and GUI-Rise's small-model results (2B–3B) exceed several of them (e.g., GPT-4's 26.4% cross-domain Step SR) despite using far fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GUI navigation agent line of work by formalizing a three-subtask reasoning pipeline (structured CoT with progress estimation, action prediction, and history summarization) on top of MLLM backbones like Qwen-VL. It builds on GRPO from DeepSeekMath for RL training and borrows the retrospective pseudo-labeling strategy from UI-TARS for cold-start initialization, but departs from prior history representations (action-only sequences, raw screenshots, or compressed visual tokens) by introducing a learned textual summary whose quality is directly rewarded through a history-aware RL objective. Unlike UI-R1, which applies GRPO to single-step GUI tasks, this work targets multi-step navigation where coherent long-horizon reasoning and context tracking are essential.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The history summarization mechanism offers a token-efficient alternative to screenshot-based memory that scales better to long trajectories, and the RL reward linking summary quality to future action correctness creates a self-improving loop for context management. The strong zero-shot cross-domain generalization (38.7% improvement over ShowUI on Mind2Web) suggests the structured reasoning framework transfers better than pure SFT to unseen environments. However, the model is trained entirely offline, limiting real-time adaptation; future work on online learning and reflection from live interactions remains an open direction. The modest in-domain gains (+1–2 points over ShowUI) indicate the framework's primary value lies in generalization rather than peak in-distribution accuracy.
