## Problem

Remote sensing (RS) vision-language data must be synthetically generated because RS images lack the naturally aligned web-crawled image-text pairs available for natural images, yet no systematic framework exists for automated quality assessment of such synthetic data. Rule-based construction produces informationally sparse pairs, and VLM-generated data risks propagating hallucinations, but there is no learned scoring model to rank and filter these samples before training downstream RS VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255699/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The scoring model takes a remote sensing image (orthorectified, from diverse global urban areas) paired with an associated text — either a single caption or a multi-turn vision instruction conversation — and outputs a single scalar quality score. The text can range from a short factual description to a multi-turn Q&A with open-ended or close-ended questions. The score is used downstream for three distinct purposes: ranking training samples for data curation, computing per-response rewards during GRPO reinforcement learning, and selecting the best candidate among N generated answers at test time.

## Method

ScoreRS is initialized from Qwen2VL-7B with the language head replaced by a linear value head, and trained via a three-stage progressive recipe: (1) the value head is pre-trained on the UltraFeedback text preference dataset for stable initialization, (2) the ViT and value head are unfrozen and trained on a 72K image-caption preference dataset, and (3) the full LLM is unfrozen for full-parameter training on a 59K vision instruction preference dataset augmented with RLHF-V. Preference pairs are constructed by generating multiple candidate texts per image using diverse policy models (LHRS-Bot-Nova, Qwen2VL-7B, InternVL-2.5-8B) and selecting the best via GPT-4o or Qwen2VL-72B judges scored across five quality dimensions (relevance, specificity, completeness, clarity, semantic richness). Pairwise preferences are modeled with the Bradley-Terry loss, and the resulting scalar scores enable top-30% data selection that outperforms full-dataset and CLIP-score-based filtering, a novel reference-based reward formulation for open-ended GRPO training, and best-of-N test-time selection that pushes LHRS-Bench accuracy above 70%.

## Results

Fine-tuning Qwen2VL-7B on only the top 30% of image-caption data and top 60% of instruction data ranked by ScoreRS yields Qwen2VL-7B-RS, which achieves 84.59% average RS classification accuracy, 86.45% average RSVQA accuracy, 58.34% on VG-DIOR visual grounding, and 67.08% on LHRS-Bench general knowledge. For CLIP fine-tuning, ScoreRS-filtered 30% data improves average top-1 classification to 70.97% (vs. 63.52% full data and 69.98% CLIP-score) and average retrieval R@1 to 27.11% (vs. 25.19% full data). After GRPO reinforcement learning with ScoreRS as reward model, the final Qwen2VL-7B-RS-R1 model reaches 64.52% on VG-DIOR and 69.13% on LHRS-Bench, and using ScoreRS as a best-of-N selector pushes LHRS-Bench accuracy above 70% for the first time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2VL-7B-RS outperforms all general-purpose VLMs (LLaVA-1.6-7B, InternVL-2.5-8B, Qwen2VL-7B) by large margins on RS classification (84.59% vs. 54–70%), grounding (58.34% vs. 12–42%), and LHRS-Bench (67.08% vs. 64.78–65.86%). Among RS-specific VLMs, it surpasses LHRS-Bot-Nova on LHRS-Bench (67.08% vs. 52.46%) and VG-DIOR grounding (58.34% vs. 31.51%), while VHM leads on classification (85.65% vs. 84.59%) but trails significantly on LHRS-Bench (33.04% vs. 67.08%). GeoChat and SkysenseGPT are outperformed on nearly all metrics, with the largest gaps on grounding (58.34% vs. 19.77% and 12.87%) and LHRS-Bench (67.08% vs. 36.23% and 36.37%). For CLIP fine-tuning, ScoreRS-filtered data beats both the full RemoteCLIP dataset (70.97% vs. 63.52% avg top-1) and CLIP-score filtering (70.97% vs. 69.98%), and similarly outperforms LongCLIP-based filtering in the large VLM setting across all evaluation thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-wise data selection paradigm—established in the LLM domain by QaRater and LESS, and adopted in recent VLMs like Llama-3.2-Vision, DeepSeekVL2, and Qwen2VL—to the remote sensing domain, where no open-source scoring model previously existed. It builds on Qwen2VL-7B as the scoring model backbone (replacing the language head with a value head trained via Bradley-Terry preference learning) and on GRPO for the RL application, while departing from the dominant RS data curation approaches of rule-based template construction (RemoteCLIP, Skyscript) and unfiltered synthetic generation (VHM, GeoChat, LHRS-Bot). The multi-stage progressive training recipe (text preferences → image-caption → vision instruction) is a novel adaptation of standard VLM training pipelines to the scoring-model setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies data quality, not merely data volume, as the primary bottleneck for RS VLMs—a finding with direct practical implications for the RS community's ongoing data-scaling efforts. The dual utility of ScoreRS as both a data filter and an RL reward model / BoN selector makes it a broadly applicable tool, and the first open-source RS-specific preference dataset lowers the barrier for future work. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4o and Qwen2VL-72B for preference judgment (introducing potential judge bias and cost), evaluation confined to a single 7B model family for large-VLM experiments, and the scoring model's 7B size constraining deployment. Open questions include whether the five quality dimensions generalize beyond RS, how the approach scales to video or multi-sensor data, and whether the preference construction pipeline can be further automated without flagship VLMs.
