## Problem

Unified adverse weather restoration models struggle with the inherently spatially heterogeneous nature of different weather degradations—haze causes smooth global attenuation while rain streaks and snowflakes introduce sharp local occlusions. Most existing architectures lack dedicated mechanisms to explicitly estimate and leverage these fine-grained, spatially variant degradation patterns, limiting their ability to adapt restoration strategies to the specific degradation present in each image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251581/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (rain, haze, snow, or raindrop artifacts) and outputs a restored clean image at the same resolution. Training uses a composite all-weather dataset (Snow100K, RainDrop, Outdoor-Rain) with paired degraded/GT images in Stage 1, while Stage 2 operates on degraded images alone with a frozen DDEM providing degradation supervision. Evaluation spans resolutions from 256×256 to 1024×1024, with the SSM's linear complexity enabling near-linear scaling with image size.

## Method

MODEM integrates two key components: (1) a Morton-Order 2D-Selective-Scan Module (MOS2D) that converts 2D features into locality-preserving 1D sequences via Morton coding (bitwise interleaving of spatial coordinates) before processing with a selective S6 block, and (2) a Dual Degradation Estimation Module (DDEM) that extracts a global degradation descriptor Z₀ (via global average pooling + MLP) and spatially adaptive kernels Z₁ (via outer product of convolved features). These priors dynamically condition the restoration backbone through Degradation-Adaptive Feature Modulation (DAFM, applying Z₀ as channel-wise affine weights) and Degradation-Selective Attention Modulation (DSAM, using Z₁ to gate the SSM's B, C, and Δ parameters). The paper formally interprets the SSM recurrence as a degradation estimator: CAh_{k−1} captures long-range propagated degradation context while CBx_k captures immediate local artifact impact. Training follows a two-stage protocol where Stage 1 trains DDEM with both degraded and GT inputs, and Stage 2 distills degradation knowledge via KL divergence between a frozen and trainable DDEM, enabling inference-time degradation estimation from the degraded image alone.

## Results

MODEM achieves state-of-the-art average PSNR of 34.18 dB and SSIM of 0.9452 across four unified weather restoration benchmarks (Snow100K-S, Snow100K-L, Outdoor-Rain, RainDrop), surpassing the previous best method Histoformer (33.68 dB / 0.9437) by 0.5 dB on average. The largest gain is on Outdoor-Rain (joint deraining & dehazing), where MODEM reaches 33.10 dB PSNR, a 1.02 dB improvement over Histoformer. On task-specific benchmarks, MODEM also outperforms dedicated methods: 38.08 dB on Snow100K-S desnowing (vs. ConvIR 37.98 dB), 33.01 dB on RainDrop removal (vs. Restormer 32.18 dB), and 33.10 dB on Outdoor-Rain deraining (vs. Restormer 30.03 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MODEM outperforms the previous state-of-the-art unified method Histoformer [65] on average PSNR (34.18 vs. 33.68 dB) and SSIM (0.9452 vs. 0.9437), with the most substantial margin on Outdoor-Rain (33.10 vs. 32.08 dB, +1.02 dB) and Snow100K-S (38.08 vs. 37.41 dB, +0.67 dB). The only metric where MODEM trails Histoformer is RainDrop PSNR (33.01 vs. 33.06 dB, a negligible 0.05 dB gap), while MODEM still leads on RainDrop SSIM in the ablation-free full model. Against the diffusion-based WeatherDiff [55], MODEM gains 2.61 dB average PSNR (34.18 vs. 31.57) and also achieves superior perceptual quality (lower LPIPS, higher Q-Align and MUSIQ scores). On task-specific benchmarks, MODEM beats dedicated desnowing methods (ConvIR: 38.08 vs. 37.98 dB on Snow100K-S), raindrop removal methods (Restormer: 33.01 vs. 32.18 dB), and deraining methods (Restormer: 33.10 vs. 30.03 dB on Outdoor-Rain). On real-world datasets evaluated via no-reference Q-Align, MODEM achieves the best scores across Snow100K-Real (3.7324), RainDrop (4.0664), NTURain (3.2891), RESIDE (3.3164), and WeatherStream (1.9863), consistently surpassing both Histoformer and WeatherDiff.

## Relation to Prior Work

MODEM builds directly on Mamba's selective state-space model (S6 block) for image restoration, extending the line of SSM-based restoration work (MambaIR, VMambaIR, FreqMamba) by replacing raster or zigzag scanning with Morton-order spatial traversal to better preserve 2D locality in the 1D sequence. Conceptually, it departs from prior unified weather restoration methods (TransWeather, WGWSNet, WeatherDiff, Histoformer) by introducing an explicit dual degradation estimation module (DDEM) that extracts global and local degradation priors to dynamically condition the SSM, rather than relying on implicit feature learning or histogram statistics. The two-stage training strategy, where a frozen teacher DDEM supervises a student DDEM via KL divergence, draws on knowledge distillation principles applied to degradation representation consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit degradation-estimation-as-guidance framing offers a principled way to make unified restoration models spatially adaptive, addressing a recognized gap in prior unified architectures that lack dedicated mechanisms for modeling spatially heterogeneous weather artifacts. The near-linear computational scaling (vs. quadratic for Transformers) demonstrated at 1024×1024 resolution makes SSM-based restoration more practical for high-resolution deployment. However, the paper acknowledges limitations on extreme unseen degradations (e.g., very large high-contrast snowflakes), and the absence of error bars or multi-seed statistics leaves the robustness of the reported gains somewhat uncertain. Open questions include whether the Morton-order advantage generalizes to non-square or very anisotropic inputs, and whether the two-stage degradation estimation can be replaced by end-to-end learning without the KL consistency loss.
