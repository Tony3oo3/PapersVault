## Problem

ML-based surrogate solvers for parametric constrained optimization are fast but frequently produce infeasible solutions, rendering them unusable in safety-critical applications. Existing constraint-enforcement strategies—penalty methods, projection-based methods, and iterative correction—suffer from a tradeoff among solution quality, computational cost, and feasibility guarantees. FSNet addresses this gap by providing a general framework that provably enforces both equality and inequality constraints while preserving end-to-end trainability.

## Input / Output

The method takes problem parameters x ∈ R^d (e.g., demand vectors, network admittance data) that vary across instances of a fixed-structure optimization problem, and produces a feasible decision vector y ∈ R^n satisfying all equality and inequality constraints. The pipeline is two-stage: a standard NN first predicts a candidate y_θ(x), then a feasibility-seeking step (L-BFGS on a constraint-violation minimization) refines it into a feasible ŷ_θ(x). The framework is evaluated on problems with up to 500 decision variables and 200 equality/inequality constraints each, spanning convex, nonconvex, smooth, and nonsmooth settings, as well as the AC optimal power flow problem on IEEE 30-bus and 118-bus systems.

## Method

FSNet integrates a differentiable feasibility-seeking step directly into the NN's forward pass: given the NN's prediction y_θ(x), it solves the unconstrained problem min_s ‖h(s;x)‖² + ‖g⁺(s;x)‖² via L-BFGS with backtracking line search, using y_θ(x) as a warm-start point. Training is end-to-end using unrolled differentiation through the feasibility-seeking iterations (with optional truncation to K′ tracked steps for computational efficiency), minimizing a loss that combines the original objective f evaluated at the feasible output with a penalty (ρ/2)‖y_θ(x) − ŷ_θ(x)‖² that encourages the NN to predict near-feasible points. Under L-smoothness and the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition, the feasibility-seeking step converges exponentially to a feasible point, and SGD training converges to a stationary point of the network parameters even with truncated backpropagation. Increasing the penalty weight ρ drives the minimizer of the composite loss to coincide with the true constrained optimum, analogous to the exact penalty method in classical optimization.

## Results

On smooth convex problems with 100 decision variables, FSNet achieves near-zero constraint violations (equality ~1.3e-4, inequality ~1.6e-6) and optimality gaps below 0.2% (0.015% QP, 0.035% QCQP, 0.159% SOCP), while delivering 31×–1125× batch speedups over traditional solvers. On nonconvex problems, FSNet not only matches solver feasibility but finds *better* local solutions, with negative optimality gaps of −0.67% (QCQP) and −2300% (SOCP) relative to IPOPT. On the ACOPF problem, FSNet achieves 3× and 11× sequential speedups on IEEE 30-bus and 118-bus systems respectively, with less than 1% optimality gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSNet decisively outperforms Penalty and Adaptive Penalty [10] on constraint satisfaction: while FSNet maintains inequality violations below 2e-6, both penalty methods exhibit violations of 0.01–0.03, rendering their (sometimes negative) optimality gaps meaningless since they exploit infeasibility to lower the objective. Against DC3 [13], FSNet wins on inequality constraint enforcement (DC3 shows 0.12–0.13 mean inequality violations vs. FSNet's ~1e-6) and is faster in batch mode, though DC3 handles equality constraints comparably well; DC3 also diverged on nonconvex problems. The Projection method (qpth) [20] matches FSNet on feasibility but is ~113× slower in batch, as orthogonal projection is computationally expensive in high dimensions. Against traditional solvers, FSNet achieves 31× (OSQP [47] on QP), 104× (SCS [48] on QCQP), and 1125× (SCS on SOCP) batch speedups at 100 variables, with speedups growing to 188×–2754× at 500 variables. On nonconvex problems, FSNet surpasses IPOPT [49] in both speed (85–3000× batch) and solution quality, finding lower-cost local optima (negative optimality gaps), a result not achievable by the other NN baselines which remain infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSNet sits at the intersection of amortized optimization (learning parameter-to-solution mappings) and differentiable optimization layers, but its core novelty is in the feasibility-enforcement mechanism. It departs from penalty methods (which lack feasibility guarantees), projection-based methods (which are computationally expensive in general), and prior iterative correction methods like DC3 (which handle equality and inequality constraints separately and lack guarantees). By formulating feasibility enforcement as an unconstrained violation-minimization problem solved via unrolled L-BFGS with truncated backpropagation, FSNet unifies both constraint types in a single differentiable step while retaining end-to-end trainability—a combination not present in prior iterative or projection approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSNet provides the first NN-based framework with provable feasibility guarantees (exponential convergence to a feasible point under PL conditions) and convergence guarantees for the training procedure, which is critical for safety-critical applications where infeasible solutions are unacceptable. The practical speedups (orders of magnitude over IPOPT/OSQP) make real-time constrained optimization feasible in domains like power systems and robotics. Open questions include scalability to very large-scale problems, extending the analysis beyond the PL condition (the authors note FSNet works empirically when assumptions are violated), and the strong assumption in Theorem 3 that the NN prediction is a global minimizer of the auxiliary loss F.
