## Problem

Training PDE foundation models on data from multiple physical systems requires handling tensors of varying spatial dimensionality (1D Burgers, 2D Navier-Stokes, 3D compressible flow), but existing architectures are locked to a fixed dimension: 2D patchify operations cannot process 1D or 3D inputs without entirely separate parameters, and padding or flattening strategies are inefficient and cannot generalize beyond a pre-set maximum rank. This prevents a single model from being pretrained across dimensions and fine-tuned on unseen dimensionalities.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a PDE solution tensor x ∈ R^{d1×d2×...×dK×C} of arbitrary rank K (1D, 2D, or 3D spatial axes plus a channel dimension) spanning s consecutive timesteps, and outputs the next-timestep solution at the same spatial resolution. Crucially, the same set of parameters processes all ranks without padding, flattening, or dimension-specific encoders; for example, a 1D input of shape 16×16×C and a 3D input of shape 16×16×16×C flow through identical weight matrices. The only dimension-dependent component is the number of axis-permutation branches, which scales linearly with K.

## Method

The Axial Neural Network (XNN) achieves dimension-agnosticism by treating tensor axes as interchangeable elements and enforcing axis-permutation equivariance through parameter sharing, directly generalizing the Deep Sets and GNN message-passing principles to the axis dimension. Two variants are proposed: the set-based SXNN applies an identical transformation (e.g., 1D conv or self-attention) along each axis permutation and aggregates via a permutation-invariant operation, while the graph-based GXNN first lifts the input into an axis-permutation equivariant space via a GNN-style message-passing layer over an axis graph, then applies subsequent GNN layers on the lifted features before a final pooling aggregation. The paper converts two state-of-the-art PDE foundation models (MPP and CViT) into their XNN counterparts (X-MPP and X-CViT) by replacing 2D convolutions in patch embedding/de-embedding with axial convolutions and flattening-based attention with axial self-attention, then evaluates across three scenarios: single-PDE training from scratch, multi-PDE pretraining on 1D+2D+3D data, and few-shot fine-tuning on unseen dimensions, demonstrating competitive 2D accuracy and superior generalization to 1D and 3D.

## Results

On 2D PDEs (evaluated via NRMSE, lower is better), X-MPP trained from scratch achieves 0.0118 on Diffusion-Reaction, 0.0012 on Shallow Water Equation, 0.0118 on CFD M0.1, and 0.0163 on CFD M1.0, outperforming MPP (0.0157, 0.0015, 0.0132, 0.0181) across all tasks. The headline result is that X-MPP pretrained on 1D+2D+3D PDEs and fine-tuned on a single 2D PDE achieves NRMSE of 0.0058 (DR), 0.0011 (SWE), 0.0209 (CFD M0.1), and 0.0294 (CFD M1.0), substantially beating MPP pretrained on 2D only (0.0516, 0.0022, 0.0319, 0.0422). In the unseen-dimension generalization experiment, X-MPP fine-tuned on 3D CFD converges faster and to lower NRMSE than MPP with 3D-inflated layers, while MPP pretrained on 1D+2D (with zero-padding) degrades severely (NRMSE 0.2183 on DR vs. 0.0430 for X-MPP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MPP (the primary baseline), X-MPP consistently wins: trained from scratch, X-MPP improves NRMSE by 25% on DR (0.0118 vs. 0.0157) and 11% on CFD M0.1 (0.0118 vs. 0.0132). The most striking comparison is in the fine-tuning setting: X-MPP pretrained on 1D+2D+3D data achieves NRMSE 0.0058 on DR and 0.0209 on CFD M0.1, versus 0.0516 and 0.0319 for MPP pretrained on 2D only — a roughly 9× improvement on DR. Notably, naively padding 1D data to 2D for MPP causes severe degradation (NRMSE 0.2183 on DR), confirming that dimension-agnostic parameter sharing is essential rather than simply mixing dimensionalities. Against CViT, X-CViT is competitive but slightly worse on NS (0.1148 vs. 0.1078) and SWE (0.1948 vs. 0.1876), while marginally better on DR (0.0382 vs. 0.0389), suggesting the axial modification preserves but does not uniformly improve upon the original architecture in the from-scratch 2D setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the parameter-sharing principle from Deep Sets and GNNs to the axis dimension of tensors, treating axes as interchangeable set elements (SXNN) or graph vertices (GXNN). It departs from prior dimension-agnostic approaches (Levin & Diaz's SVD-based equivariant weights, Lee et al.'s neural-process dimension aggregator) by avoiding both the flattening of high-dimensional data and the pre-computation of dimension-equivariant weights, instead relying on permutation-equivariant message passing over axes. The paper concretizes this by converting two existing 2D-only PDE foundation models (MPP and CViT) into their axial variants (X-MPP, X-CViT), replacing their dimension-dependent 2D convolutional patch embeddings with lifting layers and their spatial attention with axial attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a principled, computationally efficient path toward truly dimension-agnostic scientific foundation models, removing the need for zero-padding, separate encoders, or dimension-specific patchifiers that plague current PDE solvers. The empirical demonstration that multidimensional pretraining (1D+2D+3D) followed by fine-tuning outperforms 2D-only pretraining on unseen dimensions highlights a practical recipe for building more general physics models. Open questions include extending XNNs to cross-attention between tensors of different dimensions, improving GXNN's computational efficiency for high-rank tensors, and validating the approach at larger model and dataset scales where the current 7–12M parameter models may not fully exploit the architecture's potential.
