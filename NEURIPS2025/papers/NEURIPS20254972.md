## Problem

AI weather prediction models trained on reanalysis data inherit data assimilation biases and suffer up to 6-hour temporal lags, limiting their responsiveness. Direct observation prediction (AI-DOP) could bypass reanalysis entirely, but prior methods (EarthNet, Transformer-DOP) face a fundamental input-output distribution shift: satellite observations are irregular and sparse due to orbital motion, while forecasts must be dense and regular, causing severe degradation during multi-step rollout.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254972/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes irregular, sparse observations from four polar-orbiting satellite sensors (AMSU-A 15ch microwave, ATMS 9ch brightness temperature, HIRS 20ch infrared, MHS 5ch microwave) at 0.16° resolution over 12-hour windows, processed as 144×144 sub-images with missing patches masked out. It produces complete global satellite observation forecasts at 1-hour temporal resolution up to 72 hours lead time, and via a separate mapping network, derives total column water vapor (TCWV) and surface precipitation (SP) products without requiring satellite precipitation products as input.

## Method

DAWP is a two-stage framework: (1) an AIDA module using a Multi-modal Masked Autoencoder (MMAE) that tokenizes each satellite's observations via pretrained mask ViT-VAE encoders, concatenates all spatiotemporal tokens within a 12h window, and reconstructs masked tokens to impute the irregular observation space into a uniform dense grid; (2) an AIWP module consisting of 12 spatiotemporal decoupling transformer blocks (hidden dim 768) with Cross-Regional Boundary Conditioning (CBC), which uses a global state cache to supply neighboring sub-images as boundary context, ensuring spatial continuity across the 8×16 sub-image tiling of the globe. The mask ViT-VAE (patch size 16, 10 encoder + 12 decoder transformer blocks) handles multi-channel satellite data with missing values through mask attention. The key insight is that AIDA initialization converts the irregular-to-regular prediction problem into a standard dense spatiotemporal forecasting task, eliminating the distribution shift that destabilizes rollout in prior AI-DOP methods.

## Results

On the composite satellite observation dataset (12×1152×2304, 0.16° resolution), DAWP achieves the lowest MAE across all four satellite modalities (AMSU-A, ATMS, HIRS, MHS) and all three lead-time windows (0–12h, 12–24h, 24–36h). At the 24–36h lead time, DAWP's MAE on AMSU-A ch0 is 3.66 (×10⁻⁵) versus 5.17 for EarthNet and 4.91 for Transformer-DOP, and on ATMS ch0 it is 7.84 versus 12.52 and 11.35 respectively. For precipitation forecasting (12h), DAWP achieves CSI-0.5 of 0.197 on surface precipitation (44.8% higher than Transformer-DOP's 0.136) and CSI-30 of 0.807 on TCWV with the lowest FAR-30 of 0.120 among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAWP outperforms all eight baselines across every modality and lead-time window. Against the two closest AI-DOP competitors, EarthNet and Transformer-DOP, DAWP achieves a consistent 12-hour lead-time advantage: its 24–36h MAE on AMSU-A ch0 (3.66) is lower than EarthNet's (4.12) and Transformer-DOP's (3.84) 12–24h MAE. On ATMS ch0 at 24–36h, DAWP (7.84) reduces error by 37% over EarthNet (12.52) and 31% over Transformer-DOP (11.35). Among classical spatiotemporal methods, ConvLSTM and EarthFormer fail catastrophically (MAE >100 on AMSU-A), while PredRNN, SimVP, TAU, and RainFormer all trail DAWP by 15–40% in MAE at 24–36h. For precipitation skill, DAWP's CSI-0.5 on surface precipitation (0.197) exceeds Transformer-DOP (0.136) by 44.8% and EarthNet (0.127) by 55%, while its FAR-30 on TCWV (0.120) is 30% lower than EarthNet's (0.172), indicating both higher detection accuracy and fewer false alarms.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAWP extends the direct observation prediction (DOP) line initiated by Transformer-DOP and Graph-DOP, which attempt to bypass reanalysis data but suffer from input-output distribution shift during rollout due to irregular satellite inputs. The key departure is the AIDA module, which uses a multi-modal masked autoencoder (building on MultiMAE and EarthNet's assimilation concept) to impute the observation space into a regular grid before spatiotemporal forecasting, decoupling the imputation problem from the dynamics-learning problem. The forecasting backbone follows the spatiotemporal decoupling transformer paradigm (ViViT, EarthFormer, PredFormer) but adds cross-regional boundary conditioning via a global state cache to enable efficient sub-image-based global prediction, analogous to context-window strategies in image generation (SD3, Flux).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By operating entirely in observation space, DAWP eliminates the 6-hour temporal lag and data assimilation biases inherent in reanalysis-driven AIWP, enabling near-real-time global weather prediction directly from satellite feeds. The finding that standard spatiotemporal training diverges without AIDA initialization (loss → 1.0) suggests that irregular observation spaces are fundamentally incompatible with conventional dynamics learning, making imputation a prerequisite rather than an optional preprocessing step. Key limitations include reliance on homogeneous satellite sources (no ground-based or aircraft observations), a 36-hour evaluation horizon, and baselines that were reimplemented by the authors due to closed-source code, which may understate the gap. Open questions include whether the framework scales to longer lead times, integrates heterogeneous observation types, and can directly predict physical variables like wind or pressure.
