## Problem

Vision-language models face a fundamental tension: increasing visual token density improves fine-grained understanding but incurs quadratic attention cost in the LLM decoder, making high-resolution and video inference prohibitively expensive. Existing token-pruning methods either select tokens at the encoder or LLM stage using static importance scores, overlooking the hierarchical, two-stage nature of human visual attention where a rapid global glance precedes focused gazing on salient regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252340/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image (576 tokens at 336×336 for LLaVA-1.5, up to 2880 tokens at 4× resolution for LLaVA-NeXT) or a video (8 frames × 256 tokens = 2048 tokens) paired with a free-form text instruction, and produces a natural-language response. A key design constraint is that Glance Fusion operates before the LLM without changing the token count, while Gaze Compression progressively reduces tokens from N down to 2 across decoder layers, so the LLM processes a shrinking visual sequence as it generates output.

## Method

Glance2Gaze is a two-stage cognitively inspired framework. The Glance Fusion module samples four intermediate ViT layers (indices 7, 13, 19, 23), projects them into the LLM embedding space, and computes per-layer cross-modal attention scores between visual tokens and instruction tokens; a softmax over the layer dimension yields task-aware weights that fuse multi-level features into a single N-token representation without adding tokens. The Gaze Compression module, embedded within the LLM decoder starting at layer r, uses a shared learnable query pool Q_s (with 2D RoPE for spatial awareness) to iteratively compress visual tokens via cross-attention at each subsequent layer, following a monotonically decreasing schedule p_r ≥ p_{r+1} ≥ … ≥ p_R = 2. The shared query design constrains the optimization space and outperforms layer-specific queries, while retaining full tokens in shallow layers ensures the LLM builds a complete global understanding before progressive compression focuses attention on semantically critical regions.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, Glance2Gaze achieves 99.9% average performance at 33% FLOPs, 98.7% at 22% FLOPs, and 95.6% at 11% FLOPs, consistently outperforming all baselines at every compression level. On the high-resolution LLaVA-NeXT-7B backbone, it reaches 99.2% at 22% FLOPs and 94.8% at 5% FLOPs. For video understanding on Video-LLaVA at 6% FLOPs, Glance2Gaze retains 96.2% of full-model performance while FastV drops to 52.1%. Inference efficiency analysis shows a 6.39× speedup in prefill time and 3.15× reduction in overall latency compared to the uncompressed model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Glance2Gaze outperforms all four token-compression baselines (FastV, SparseVLM, PDrop, VisionZip) at every FLOPs level on LLaVA-1.5-7B. The closest competitor is VisionZip, which Glance2Gaze edges out by 0.8% at 33% FLOPs (99.9% vs 99.1%) and by 0.7% at 11% FLOPs (95.6% vs 94.9%); the gap widens to 6.6% over SparseVLM at 11% FLOPs. FastV suffers the largest degradation, dropping to 73.8% at 11% FLOPs versus Glance2Gaze's 95.6%. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B, Glance2Gaze leads VisionZip by 1.2–1.3% on average across three FLOPs settings, with particularly strong gains on OCR-heavy benchmarks like TextVQA (62.1 vs 60.8 at 22% FLOPs). For video understanding at 6% FLOPs, Glance2Gaze achieves 96.2% average versus VisionZip's 93.2% and SparseVLM's 86.5%. In the fusion-strategy comparison (Table 6), Glance Fusion surpasses Dense Connector by 0.1–1.1 points and MMFuser by 0.5–1.9 points on LLaVA-1.5-7B, while using fewer ViT layers (4 vs 24 and 5, respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the visual token compression literature for VLMs, departing from prior methods that apply pruning at a single stage (encoder-side like VisionZip or LLM-side like FastV and SparseVLM) by introducing a two-stage hierarchical process inspired by human glance-to-gaze eye movements. The Glance Fusion module repurposes the multi-layer ViT feature fusion idea from Dense Connector and MMFuser but adds text-conditioned attention for task-aware weighting, while the Gaze Compression module introduces a shared learnable query pool for progressive cross-attention-based token reduction within the LLM decoder. Unlike GG-Transformer, which uses a glance-and-gaze concept to improve unimodal ViT representational capacity, Glance2Gaze targets cross-modal efficiency in the full VLM pipeline. The framework is evaluated on the LLaVA/LLaVA-NeXT/Video-LLaVA architecture family, building directly on their training recipes and visual encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gains (6.39× prefilling speedup, 3.15× latency reduction) with near-lossless performance retention (99.9% at 33% FLOPs, 96.2% at 6% FLOPs for video) make this a useful tool for deploying VLMs in resource-constrained or real-time settings. However, the authors acknowledge that the internal mechanisms remain largely heuristic and lack explicit modeling of the cognitive processes they invoke, leaving open the question of whether tighter integration with cognitive theories could yield further gains. The absence of error bars and the reliance on a single architecture family (LLaVA variants) limit the generalizability claims, and it remains unclear whether the query-based compression strategy transfers cleanly to other LLM backbones or encoder architectures.
