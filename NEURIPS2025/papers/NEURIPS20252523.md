## Problem

Most existing contextual stochastic optimization (CSLO) and decision-focused learning literature assumes uncertainty only in the objective function, ignoring uncertain parameters in constraints. When constraint parameters are uncertain, the feasible set changes with context and solutions may become infeasible with respect to true parameters. This paper extends the SPO framework to handle robust constraints by introducing feasibility-sensitive loss functions and a training procedure that accounts for context-dependent feasible sets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252523/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes tabular contextual features x ∈ R^p and a pre-constructed uncertainty set U(x) (e.g., an ℓ₂ or ℓ₁ ball centered at a predicted constraint parameter) as input, and produces a predicted cost vector ĉ = f(x) ∈ R^d. The predicted cost vector is then plugged into a robust optimization problem P(ĉ, Û) whose feasible set S(x) depends on the context, yielding a decision vector w*. The training signal is the SPO-RC+ loss, which compares the cost of the induced robust solution against the hindsight optimal cost under the true constraint parameter a, with a penalty Δ_S(C) applied when a falls outside Û.

## Method

The paper introduces the SPO-RC loss, a feasibility-sensitive extension of the SPO loss that measures decision error under a robust predict-then-optimize pipeline and assigns a bounded penalty when the true constraint parameter lies outside the uncertainty set. Its convex surrogate, SPO-RC+, replaces the non-differentiable cost metric with the cost+ metric (analogous to SPO+), and Fisher consistency between the two is proven under standard assumptions (singleton optimal solution at the conditional mean, centrally symmetric cost distribution, non-empty feasible interior). To handle the fact that SPO-RC+ is only a valid upper bound when a ∈ Û, the training procedure truncates the dataset to feasibility-guaranteed samples and corrects the resulting covariate shift via Kernel Mean Matching (KMM) importance reweighting, reducing the problem to a standard covariate-shift correction under the conditional independence assumption between cost and constraint parameters given x. The uncertainty set is constructed independently via split conformal prediction, and the overall pipeline is validated on fractional knapsack (SOCP/LP reformulations) and alloy production (robust covering LP) instances.

## Results

On the fractional knapsack problem (ℓ₂-norm conformal prediction, 3000 test samples), SPO-RC+ with a linear model achieves NormSPORCTest values comparable to random forests and consistently outperforms MSE-trained linear models as cost complexity (deg_c) increases from 2 to 8. All robust-constraint methods (MSE, RF, SPO-RC+) achieve only 0.02% infeasible solutions on the test set, versus 45.00% for the non-robust PTO baseline. Training time for SPO-RC+ is roughly 4–5× that of MSE (e.g., 157.96 s vs. 32.90 s for knapsack n=5, 5000 samples), but randomized solution caching reduces this gap substantially with only marginal quality loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four approaches: PTO (predict-then-optimize without robust constraints), MSE-trained linear regression, random forest (RF), and the proposed SPO-RC+ linear model, each trained on the original (D_O), truncated (D_T), and importance-reweighted (D_IR) datasets. SPO-RC+ consistently outperforms MSE as the polynomial degree of the cost function increases (deg_c = 6, 8), while the two are comparable for simpler cost functions (deg_c = 2, 4). SPO-RC+ on D_IR outperforms SPO-RC+ on D_T in nearly every scenario, with the gap most pronounced at small training sizes (n = 1000). The linear SPO-RC+ model achieves performance on par with the non-parametric random forest, demonstrating that the decision-focused loss compensates for the parametric restriction. All robust-constraint methods reduce infeasibility from 45% (PTO) to 0.02%, confirming that the conformal-prediction-based uncertainty sets effectively guarantee feasibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SPO/SPO+ decision-focused learning framework (Elmachtoub and Grigas, 2022) from the standard setting of uncertain objective parameters to the more general case where inequality constraints also involve uncertain parameters predicted from contextual data. The robust optimization formulation with contextual uncertainty sets (constructed via conformal prediction) provides the decision-making backbone, while the SPO-RC and SPO-RC+ losses are direct analogues of SPO and SPO+ adapted to context-dependent feasible sets. The generalization bounds extend those of El Balghiti et al. (2023) to accommodate the context-dependent feasibility structure, and the data truncation with Kernel Mean Matching importance reweighting borrows from the covariate shift literature to correct the distributional bias introduced by restricting training to feasibility-guaranteed regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: most decision-focused learning methods assume uncertainty only in the objective, yet real-world optimization problems (supply chain, energy, production) frequently have uncertain constraint parameters where infeasibility carries severe costs. The Fisher consistency guarantee and generalization bounds provide theoretical grounding for the SPO-RC+ surrogate, and the truncation-plus-reweighting pipeline offers a concrete recipe for handling the coverage failures inherent in finite-sample uncertainty sets. Key limitations include restriction to linear objectives, the two-step procedure that learns uncertainty sets independently of cost predictors, and evaluation limited to synthetic instances; extending to nonlinear objectives and joint end-to-end learning of both uncertainty sets and cost functions are natural next steps.
