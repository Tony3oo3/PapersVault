## Problem

Shallow neural networks lack provable high-order approximation rates: ReLU MLPs are bounded by the curse of dimensionality O(N^{-2r/d}), and spline-based KANs are capped at O(N^{-k}) by their fixed spline degree. No prior work established that a single-layer architecture with principled activation design could achieve arbitrarily fast convergence, leaving open whether depth is truly necessary for expressive approximation.

## Input / Output

The network accepts scalar or vector inputs of arbitrary dimension (tested from 1D to 100D), including PDE coordinates (x, t) and RL state vectors, and produces a single scalar output (function value, PDE solution, or policy/value estimate). Each Cauchy neuron operates on a linear projection w_i^T x + b_i of the full input, so the architecture scales to high dimensions without architectural changes. The single hidden layer contains N basis functions (typically 20–5000), and the output is a weighted sum of their activations.

## Method

XNet is a single-hidden-layer network where each neuron applies the Cauchy activation φ(x) = (λ₁x + λ₂)/(x² + d²), a rational function derived from the Cauchy integral formula with trainable parameters λ₁, λ₂, and d. The key mechanism is that the parameter d_i per neuron controls localization: small d_i captures sharp discontinuities while large d_i smooths over noisy regions, providing neuron-wise adaptivity without grid partitions. The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) proves that for any real-analytic f on a compact set K ⊂ ℝ^d, a single-layer XNet achieves ‖f − f_N‖_{C⁰(K)} = O(N^{-r}) for arbitrary r > 0, via a three-step argument combining the Cauchy approximation theorem, multivariate polynomial lifting, and classical polynomial approximation of analytic functions. Training uses standard Adam optimization with no special initialization or grid scheduling, and the architecture is simply [input_dim, N, 1] with no depth.

## Results

On the Heat equation (PINN framework), XNet [2,200,1] achieves MSE 3.69×10⁻⁹, approximately 41× lower than KAN [2,10,1] (1.51×10⁻⁷) and ~6,600× lower than MLP [2,20,20,1] (2.45×10⁻⁵), while training 2.4× faster than KAN (108s vs 255s). On the Heaviside step function, XNet [1,64,1] reaches MSE 8.99×10⁻⁸, roughly 666× lower than KAN [1,1] with 200 grids (5.98×10⁻⁴). In PPO-based RL, XNet achieves reward 3298.52 on HalfCheetah-v4 (64% above KAN's 2010.52) and 100.38 on Swimmer-v4 (132% above KAN's 43.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

XNet outperforms KAN (Liu et al., ICLR 2025) across every experimental domain. On the Heaviside step function, XNet achieves MSE 8.99×10⁻⁸ versus KAN's 5.98×10⁻⁴ (~666× lower), and on the 100-dimensional test function, XNet's MSE of 6.85×10⁻⁴ is ~9.6× lower than KAN's 6.59×10⁻³ while training 3.5× faster. For PDE solving, XNet [2,200,1] on the Poisson equation reaches MSE 1.09×10⁻⁹, approximately 53× better than KAN [2,10,1] (5.74×10⁻⁸) and ~16,500× better than the standard PINN MLP. In reinforcement learning, XNet improves final reward by 64% over KAN on HalfCheetah-v4 (3298.52 vs 2010.52) and by 132% on Swimmer-v4 (100.38 vs 43.25), while also converging faster. XNet also consistently beats MLP/ReLU baselines by orders of magnitude on discontinuous and high-dimensional approximation tasks, and in noisy settings its advantage over KAN grows from 28× (noise-free) to 14.5× (high noise) in MSE, suggesting KAN overfits while XNet's adaptive Cauchy localization remains robust.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Cauchy activation function and XNet architecture introduced by Li, Xia, and Zhang [16] (Neural Networks 2025), which established universal approximation properties for deep XNet variants. Here, the authors focus exclusively on the shallow single-layer setting and provide the first constructive convergence-rate analysis (Theorem 3.2), proving O(N^{-r}) for any r > 0 on real-analytic targets. Conceptually, the work positions itself against the depth-centric paradigm by contrasting with KANs [19] (B-spline activations, fixed-degree O(N^{-k}) rates) and ReLU MLPs (curse-of-dimensionality O(N^{-2r/d}) rates), arguing that principled activation design rooted in the Cauchy integral formula can substitute for multi-layer composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a theoretically grounded argument that expressive approximation need not come from depth, which is timely as the field grapples with scaling bottlenecks. However, the central theoretical guarantee applies only to real-analytic functions, while the most striking empirical results (600×+ error reduction on the Heaviside step, 20,000× on PDEs) involve discontinuous or non-analytic targets where the theory does not yet reach. Open questions include extending the convergence analysis to broader function classes (e.g., piecewise-smooth or Sobolev spaces), understanding whether the dense kernel matrix (condition number O(N)) becomes a practical bottleneck at scale, and validating the RL and PDE gains across more diverse tasks and seeds.
