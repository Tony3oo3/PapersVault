## Problem

Existing Video-LMMs are trained predominantly on videos shorter than 10 minutes, yet are evaluated on hour-scale benchmarks, creating a critical train-test length mismatch that prevents models from explicitly learning long-term temporal dependencies. This gap is compounded by the fact that naively feeding hour-long videos at 1 FPS into a transformer causes token budgets to explode due to quadratic attention complexity, making direct long-video training impractical under standard GPU constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250591/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model ingests video frames sampled at 1 FPS (videos ranging from 3 minutes to 1 hour in training, up to 2+ hours at inference) and a natural-language question. Each frame is encoded by SigLIP and average-pooled to a fixed 8×8 spatial grid before projection into the language embedding space. Only ~6% of the full video tokens (decayed tokens after 1/4 spatial × 1/4 temporal compression) are fed to the LLM decoder, while the complete 1-FPS feature sequence is retained in a memory repository for retrieval. The output is a free-form text answer, evaluated primarily via multiple-choice accuracy on four benchmarks.

## Method

Hour-LLaVA introduces a Memory Augmentation (MemAug) mechanism that decouples the token count processed by the LLM from the temporal resolution of the stored video context. A forgetting mechanism compresses full 1-FPS video tokens to ~6% via uniform spatial (1/4) and temporal (1/4) downsampling, producing decayed tokens that serve as the LLM input. The MemAug module, implemented as 4 transformer blocks, then enriches these decayed tokens through cross-attention (decayed tokens and question tokens as queries attend to the full video repository as keys/values) and self-attention (question-relevant semantics flow from question tokens to video tokens), recovering discarded spatiotemporally informative and question-relevant content. A 1D structured RoPE is applied to both decayed and full tokens to preserve spatial-temporal positional correspondence during cross-attention. Training proceeds in three stages—image-language pretraining, video-language adaptation, and video instruction tuning—using the newly constructed VideoMarathon dataset (9,700 hours, 3.3M QA pairs across 22 tasks) mixed with short-video data at a 3:1 long-to-short ratio.

## Results

Hour-LLaVA achieves the best performance among open-source Video-LMMs on all four main benchmarks. Hour-LLaVA-3B (1 FPS) scores 63.6 on TempCompass, 57.8 on LongVideoBench, 60.6/66.7 (w/o/w/ subtitles) on Video-MME, and 44.7 on LVBench. Hour-LLaVA-7B (1 FPS) further improves to 68.1, 60.4, 63.6/70.2, and 45.6 respectively, outperforming the second-best open-source models by 2.6–3.4 points on LongVideoBench and LVBench. Notably, Hour-LLaVA-3B surpasses more than half of the 7–8B Video-LMMs, and LVBench's average video length (67 min) exceeds the maximum training video length (60 min), demonstrating strong generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hour-LLaVA-3B outperforms its direct peer LLaVA-Video-3B by 2.6 points on LongVideoBench (57.8 vs. 55.2), 1.9 points on Video-MME overall (60.6 vs. 58.7), and 3.0 points on LVBench (44.7 vs. 41.7), while matching on TempCompass (63.6 vs. 63.4). At the 7B scale, Hour-LLaVA-7B leads LLaVA-Video-7B by 2.2 points on TempCompass (68.1 vs. 64.3), 2.2 on LongVideoBench (60.4 vs. 58.2), and 3.4 on LVBench (45.6 vs. 42.2). Against the strongest 7–8B open-source baselines, Hour-LLaVA-7B surpasses Apollo-7B by 3.2 points on TempCompass and 1.9 on LongVideoBench, and outperforms Kangaroo-8B by 5.6 points on LVBench (45.6 vs. 39.4). On Video-MME long-length videos (2466s average), Hour-LLaVA-7B achieves 55.0/65.1, exceeding LLaVA-Video-7B (53.0/55.0) by up to 10.1 points with subtitles, highlighting its advantage in long-video comprehension. The 3B model also surpasses all 7–8B baselines on LVBench except GPT-4o (48.9), confirming that the MemAug mechanism and hour-scale training data yield substantial gains even at smaller model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA-OV initialization pipeline into the hour-scale video regime, addressing a gap where existing Video-LMMs are trained on sub-10-minute videos but evaluated on hour-long content. Architecturally, it positions itself between compression-based methods (keyframe selection, token merging) that sacrifice fidelity and extension-based methods (LongVA, LongVILA) that scale compute linearly with video length, introducing the MemAug module as a learnable middle ground inspired by human memory consolidation. The VideoMarathon dataset itself builds on the synthetic QA generation paradigm of LLaVA-Video-178K and ShareGPT4Video but scales it to 3–60 minute videos using a hierarchical captioning pipeline (Qwen2VL-7B for clips, DeepSeek-V3 for event/global summaries).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VideoMarathon (9,700 hours, 3.3M QA pairs) is the first large-scale hour-long video instruction-following dataset, filling a critical training-data gap that has limited long-video Video-LMM research. The MemAug mechanism offers a practical recipe for hour-scale video understanding at 1 FPS without the quadratic token explosion of full-context LLMs, making it broadly applicable to any Video-LMM. Key open questions remain: the reliance on synthetic data introduces noise that the current pipeline does not explicitly handle, audio is entirely absent despite being crucial for lectures and documentaries, and evaluation is still dominated by multiple-choice QA rather than open-ended generation. The finding that sparse-sampling models (LLaVA-Video) actually degrade with more long-video training data suggests the field needs fundamentally different training paradigms, not just more data.
