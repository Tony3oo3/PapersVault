## Problem

In multi-animal Neuropixels recordings, no single session captures all brain areas of interest simultaneously, yet existing multi-area models (DLAG, LFADS, MtM) can only reconstruct dynamics for areas observed within a given session. This paper asks whether shared anatomical and functional structure across animals can be exploited to infer the latent dynamics of brain areas that were never recorded in a particular session.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10 ms binned spike counts from a subset of 3–7 brain areas per session (out of 8 target areas), along with area and hemisphere labels, and produces area-specific latent factors for all 8 areas—including those unrecorded—plus predicted firing rates via a per-area GLM readout. Unrecorded areas are represented by learned mask tokens at inference, making them architecturally indistinguishable from randomly masked areas during training. The latent factors are deliberately area-specific (not shared across areas) to preserve interpretability of inter-area interactions, with dimensionality per area set by the maximum participation ratio of smoothed activity across sessions plus a margin of 10.

## Method

NeuroPaint is a masked transformer autoencoder with four components: a Perceiver-IO-style cross-attention stitcher that maps neuron-level spike activity (concatenated with area, hemisphere, and unit embeddings) into area-specific embedding factors via learnable latent-token queries; a tokenizer that converts embedding factors into temporal tokens and substitutes learned mask tokens for masked or unrecorded areas; an encoder-only transformer with RoPE positional encoding that produces area-specific latent factors; and a simple GLM readout (linear + exponential) for firing-rate reconstruction. Training uses an inter-area masking scheme (0–60% of recorded areas masked per batch) so the model learns to reconstruct missing areas from the remaining ones. Two auxiliary losses complement the Poisson NLL reconstruction: a consistency loss that penalizes cosine-distance deviations between per-batch embedding-factor correlation matrices and an EMA-smoothed session-averaged target, encouraging cross-session generalization to unrecorded areas; and an L1 temporal regularization that smooths latent trajectories. The key architectural distinction from prior multi-animal models (MtM, NDT) is that unrecorded areas receive their own mask tokens and area-specific latent factors, enabling direct inference of their dynamics rather than only modeling observed areas.

## Results

On the MAP dataset (40 sessions, 8 brain areas), NeuroPaint achieves a mean DFE of 0.10 ± 0.003 for predicting single-neuron firing rates in held-out areas, outperforming LFADS (0.08 ± 0.006). On the IBL dataset (20 sessions), NeuroPaint similarly outperforms LFADS by a large margin (Fig. 3C). On synthetic data with known ground-truth firing rates, NeuroPaint trained with reconstruction + consistency loss comes closest to the upper bound, surpassing both GLM and LFADS baselines. The GLM baseline fails catastrophically on real data, yielding mean DFE values on the order of −10³² (IBL) and −10²³ (MAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroPaint outperforms LFADS on both real datasets: on MAP, NeuroPaint achieves DFE 0.10 ± 0.003 versus 0.08 ± 0.006 for LFADS, and on IBL the gap is even larger (Fig. 3C). Critically, scaling LFADS to larger architectures (256 units, up to 243 latent factors) does not close the gap and in fact degrades performance (DFE drops to −9.12 × 10²⁰), confirming that NeuroPaint's advantage stems from its masked multi-area architecture rather than model capacity. The GLM baseline is non-competitive on real data, producing severely negative DFE values (−3.8 × 10³² on IBL, −5.1 × 10²³ on MAP) due to its inability to capture nonlinear cross-area interactions. On synthetic data, NeuroPaint with the consistency loss term outperforms both GLM and LFADS and approaches the ground-truth upper bound, while the full three-loss variant (reconstruction + consistency + regularization) performs best on lower-firing-rate synthetic data and real data. NeuroPaint is the only method that produces area-specific latent factors for unrecorded areas, enabling downstream analyses (decoding, representational similarity) that the baselines cannot support.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuroPaint builds directly on the multi-task-masking (MtM) transformer architecture for neural dynamics [61] and the Perceiver-IO cross-attention mechanism [23], but introduces two key departures: treating unrecorded brain areas identically to masked areas during inference, and maintaining area-specific latent factors for interpretability. It positions itself against three prior lines of work—multi-area generative models (DLAG, mDLAG) that assume fixed communication structures, stitching/quilting methods (LFADS, graph quilting) that infer connectivity or shared latents but cannot produce area-specific dynamics for unrecorded regions, and large-scale multi-animal transformers (NDT, NEDS) that model only observed areas. The paper explicitly claims to be the first method designed to infer the dynamics of entirely unrecorded brain areas by leveraging shared structure across animals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuroPaint enables a new class of multi-area analyses—such as full representational similarity matrices across all brain areas at single-trial resolution—that were previously infeasible because no single Neuropixels session records all areas simultaneously. Key limitations include quadratic attention scaling with the number of areas and time steps, a somewhat arbitrary latent dimensionality selection procedure (participation ratio + margin of 10), and evaluation on only 8 areas and 20–40 sessions per dataset. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the full 279+ areas in IBL or 293 in MAP, whether more automated hyperparameter selection can replace the current dimensionality heuristic, and whether the consistency loss generalizes to datasets with less cross-animal overlap.
