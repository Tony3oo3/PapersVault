## Problem

Existing knowledge graph-based retrieval-augmented generation methods fail to fully exploit two types of prior knowledge embedded in KGs: structural information (relation paths connecting topic entities to answers) and explicit/implicit constraints (type, multi-entity, temporal, ordinal). This underutilization leads to unfaithful relation path generation and unreliable response generation, particularly in complex multi-hop KGQA settings where hallucinations are costly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254127/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language question, a knowledge graph (Freebase or WikiMovies), and one or more topic entities identified in the question as input. It produces a grounded answer response along with an instantiated reasoning path (a chain of entity-relation-entity triples). Notably, the framework operates on a one-to-many question-to-relation-path mapping rather than the one-to-one mapping used by prior work like RoG, and the output is conditioned on constraint verification: if the selected path violates extracted constraints, a backtracking loop re-executes path selection and instantiation before a final answer is emitted.

## Method

DP (Deliberation on Priors) comprises four modules operating in two stages. In the offline Distillation stage, weak supervision signals are collected by extracting all shortest relation paths (via Dijkstra) from topic entities to answer entities within a k-hop subgraph; a path generator (LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct with LoRA) is then trained via supervised fine-tuning on these question-to-path mappings, followed by Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO) on synthetically perturbed negative paths (truncation, entity-path swapping, relation deletion) to handle the 1:3 positive-to-negative class imbalance. In the online stage, Planning generates diverse candidate relation paths per topic entity, Instantiation grounds the semantically selected path into concrete KG triples, and Introspection verifies the instantiated path against five predefined constraint types (type, multi-entity, explicit time, implicit time, ordinal) extracted from the question; constraint violations trigger explicit feedback and iterative backtracking through path re-selection until a satisfying path is found or candidates are exhausted.

## Results

DP with GPT-4.1 achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three KGQA benchmarks: H@1 of 86.7 on WebQSP, 75.8 on CWQ, and 95.5 on MetaQA. The headline improvement is a 13% absolute H@1 gain on CWQ over the previous best (GNN-RAG, 62.8), and a 21.3% H@1 gain over the strongest ICL baseline DoG (65.4) on WebQSP. DP also narrows the gap between Hit and F1 scores to ~10% on WebQSP (vs. 46.2% for ToG), indicating more precise answer generation. Results are stable across three independent runs with standard deviations under 1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP (GPT-4.1) outperforms the strongest SL baseline GNN-RAG by 3.9% H@1 on WebQSP (86.7 vs. 82.8) and by 13.0% H@1 on CWQ (75.8 vs. 62.8), though GNN-RAG retains an edge on MetaQA 3-hop (98.6 vs. 95.5). Against the best ICL method DoG (GPT-4), DP achieves substantially larger gains: +21.3% H@1 on WebQSP (86.7 vs. 65.4) and +34.8% H@1 on CWQ (75.8 vs. 41.0), while also improving F1 by 24.5 points on CWQ (69.4 vs. 46.4). On MetaQA, DP surpasses DoG by 5.4% H@1 (95.5 vs. 90.1). Compared to the HL baseline Interactive-KBQA (GPT-4), DP improves F1 by 8.9 points on WebQSP (80.1 vs. 71.2) and 20.3 points on CWQ (69.4 vs. 49.1). Notably, DP with even the smaller LLaMA3.1-8B (H@1=82.8 on WebQSP) matches or exceeds GNN-RAG's performance, demonstrating that the framework's gains are not solely attributable to using a stronger LLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based KGQA reasoning paradigm established by RoG (Luo et al., ICLR 2024) and Think-on-Graph (Sun et al., ICLR 2024), which prompt or fine-tune LLMs to generate relation paths over knowledge graphs. It departs from RoG's one-to-one question-to-path mapping by collecting one-to-many weak supervision signals via Dijkstra shortest paths, and replaces simple fine-tuning with a progressive distillation strategy combining SFT and Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO) to handle class-imbalanced preference data. The constraint-based introspection module draws on the constraint taxonomy from Bao et al. (COLING 2016), which originally defined these constraints for dataset construction but never leveraged them as reasoning-time verification signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's most practically significant finding is that deliberation over KG priors (structural paths + constraint verification) enables both higher accuracy and substantially fewer LLM interactions (2.5–2.9 calls vs. 10–22 for baselines), making trustworthy KGQA more cost-effective for deployment. The constraint introspection mechanism—using predefined constraint types as a backtracking trigger—offers a generalizable pattern for improving LLM reliability in structured reasoning tasks beyond KGQA. A key limitation is the reliance on human-defined constraints for domain adaptation, and the paper acknowledges that automatic constraint extraction remains an open problem, with ablation showing a 2.8% H-score drop when replacing manual definitions with LLM-generated summaries.
