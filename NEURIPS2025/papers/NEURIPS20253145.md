## Problem

Empirical studies have shown that aligned LLMs exhibit clear separation between safe and harmful prompts in latent representation space, yet the underlying mechanism causing this separation effect remained unexplained. The paper asks whether this separation is an incidental artifact or a fundamental consequence of alignment, and whether it can be formally characterized, predicted, and leveraged for improved safety.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253145/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base LLM as reference policy along with prompt-response pairs labeled as aligned (D+) or unaligned (D−), constructed either as compliance-refusal (CR) data or standard preference (Pref) data. The output is an aligned policy whose hidden representations separate safe and harmful prompts, with the degree of separation (measured by Bhattacharyya distance) serving as a statistically significant proxy for adversarial robustness. Unlike standard alignment that only produces a policy, this framework explicitly characterizes the distributional geometry of the learned representations and connects it to measurable safety properties.

## Method

The paper establishes a unified theoretical framework showing that popular alignment losses are divergence estimators between aligned and unaligned response distributions: KTO optimizes total variation distance, BCO optimizes Jensen-Shannon divergence, and DPO is lower-bounded by TV. Building on this, they derive KLDO, a new alignment method based on the Donsker-Varadhan representation of KL divergence (which exhibits the highest sensitivity to large distributional shifts), and generalize to FDO, a family of f-divergence optimizers parameterized by a convex function f and link function g. They prove that all these methods satisfy 'alignment consistency'—the optimal policy redistributes probability mass toward aligned responses in proportion to the likelihood ratio R(x,y)—and that this consistency guarantees perfect recovery of safety labels, with CR data yielding strictly stronger separation than Pref data. Empirically, KLDO achieves the best overall robustness scores across five model families (1B–7B) while maintaining competitive utility on AlpacaEval, MMLU, and GSM8K.

## Results

Across five base models (LLaMA3.2-1B, LLaMA2-7B, Gemma2-2B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-1.5B), KLDO consistently achieves the highest Overall Robustness Score (85.87–95.02) and best average rank (1.4–1.6) among all alignment methods. KLDO and BCO induce the strongest latent-space separation (Bhattacharyya Distance), with KLDO reaching D_B = 10.13 on Gemma2-2B and 9.19 on Qwen2.5-1.5B, compared to DPO's 1.20 and 4.10 respectively. On safety benchmarks, KLDO achieves the lowest AdvBench Clean ASR (0.19–8.08%) and best ToxiGen accuracy (44.80–56.97%), while the correlation between D_B and overall robustness is r = 0.70 (p < 0.001), confirming separation as a reliable proxy for safety.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KLDO outperforms DPO on every metric across all five models: separation (D_B) is 1.5–8.4× higher, AdvBench Clean ASR drops from 4.62–87.69% to 0.19–8.08%, and Overall Robustness Score improves from 0.00–52.59 to 85.87–95.02. Against KTO, KLDO similarly dominates, with KTO's TV-based objective yielding the weakest separation and highest ASR in most settings (e.g., KTO achieves 72.61% GCG ASR on LLaMA3.2-1B vs. KLDO's 31.88%). The closest competitor is BCO (JS-divergence based): BCO occasionally achieves higher D_B (e.g., 11.77 vs. 9.19 on Qwen2.5-1.5B) and lower SALAD ASR on Mistral-7B (66.68% vs. 77.40%), but KLDO consistently surpasses BCO on ToxiGen accuracy (by 1.5–10.3 points) and Overall Robustness Score (by 16–23 points), reflecting KL divergence's superior sensitivity to large distributional shifts. The overall ranking across methods is DPO < KTO < BCO < KLDO for robustness, while for separation it is DPO < KTO < KLDO ≈ BCO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the lineage of preference-optimization alignment methods (RLHF, DPO, KTO, BCO) under a single theoretical lens: each is a variational estimator of a specific divergence (TV, JS, or a non-parametric one) between aligned and unaligned response distributions. It builds directly on DPO's variational structure and borrows the moving-average gradient trick from MINE (Belghazi et al., 2018) to construct KLDO, a new KL-divergence-based optimizer. The key departure from prior work is providing a *causal* explanation for the empirically observed latent-space separation effect (Lin et al., 2024a; Zheng et al., 2024): separation is not an artifact but a necessary consequence of any alignment method that estimates a divergence, formalized through the 'alignment consistency' property and Theorem 4.5.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The divergence-estimation framework gives practitioners a principled design space for new alignment objectives (the FDO family) and explains why certain data formulations (compliance-refusal vs. preference) yield stronger safety alignment—a practical recommendation with immediate applicability. The Bhattacharyya-distance-as-robustness-proxy finding (Table 3) offers a cheap diagnostic for model safety without running full attack suites. Limitations include validation only up to 7B parameters, a heuristic (not formal) argument for DPO's alignment consistency, and the small effective sample size (5 models × 4 methods) behind the correlation analysis. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-turn alignment, reasoning tasks, or settings where the aligned/unaligned split is not binary.
