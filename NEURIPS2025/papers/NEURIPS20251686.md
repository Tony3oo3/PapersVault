## Problem

Existing local search clustering algorithms (LS++, MLS, LSDS++) require O(ndk) time because each local search step must scan the entire dataset to maintain pairwise distances for D²-Sampling and to evaluate candidate swap pairs. This makes them impractical for datasets exceeding 100 million points, where even the fastest single-swap methods take 24–48 hours. The paper asks whether the joint dependence on n and k can be reduced to Õ(nd + poly(k)) while preserving constant approximation guarantees, without relying on coreset constructions that also fail to scale in practice.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a point set P ⊂ R^d of n points, a target number of clusters k, and a set C of k random initial centers, along with a probability parameter η (set to 1/(2n⁴) in experiments) and a deviation bound σ. The dimension is first reduced to O(log n) via a Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform in Õ(nd) preprocessing. The output is a refined set of k centers C ⊂ R^d. In the practical BanditFastLS variant, the Metropolis-Hastings chain length is fixed at 20 steps and the maximum bandit sample size is capped at 50,000, making the method parameter-light for deployment on large-scale tabular data.

## Method

The BanditLS algorithm combines two key accelerations. First, an adaptive sampling method embeds the dataset into a binary tree (derived from an HST) with height O(log²(dΔ)), dynamically maintaining weight distributions and probability lists at each node so that D²-Sampling is approximated by a root-to-leaf traversal with rejection sampling in Õ(k) expected time per sample, with tree updates in O(log²(dΔ)) per center swap. Second, the best swap pair is identified via a multi-armed bandit formulation where swap pairs are arms; a grouping strategy introduces virtual zero-cost arms to relax the strict Gaussian assumption to a sub-Gaussian prior with bounded deviations, enabling elimination of suboptimal swaps in Õ(k²) samples. The more practical BanditFastLS variant replaces the tree-based sampling with a Metropolis-Hastings Markov chain of length Õ(n/k²) to approximate D²-Sampling without any tree structure, yielding Õ(nd + k⁴) expected runtime under mild assumptions on optimal cluster sizes and exponential tails. The k-median extension uses weighted sampling from the dynamic tree to estimate costs within (1±ε) using Õ(√n/ε) samples, achieving Õ(nd + √n·k³) without distributional assumptions.

## Results

On the SIFT dataset (100M × 128), BanditFastLS achieves a clustering cost of 1.0541E+13 in 99.81s at k=10, compared to 24,286s for MLS and 19,994s for LSDS++, yielding a >200× speedup. At k=100 on SIFT, BanditFastLS completes in 163.82s while MLS, LSDS++, and LS++ all exceed 48 hours, achieving up to 1000× speedup. Across all large-scale datasets (HIGGS, SIFT, SUSY, USC_1990), BanditFastLS is approximately 210× faster than competing local search methods with only a 2% increase in clustering cost. Compared to state-of-the-art coreset construction (Draganov et al.), BanditFastLS achieves ~20× speedup on average with better final clustering quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BanditFastLS dominates all local search baselines on runtime while matching or slightly improving clustering quality. Against LS++ (Lattanzi & Sohler, ICML 2019), the fastest prior single-swap method, BanditFastLS is ~200× faster on SIFT (k=10: 99.81s vs. >24h) and ~370× faster on HIGGS (k=100: 9.87s vs. >48h), with clustering costs within 0.1–0.5%. Against MLS (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2023), the multi-swap method with the best prior approximation guarantee, BanditFastLS is ~243× faster on SIFT (k=10) and achieves a lower cost (8.13E+12 vs. >48h timeout at k=100). Against LSDS++ (Fan et al., ICML 2023), the dual-sampling method, BanditFastLS is ~200× faster on SIFT (k=10) and achieves comparable costs on HIGGS. Against k-means++ (Arthur & Vassilvitskii, SODA 2007), BanditFastLS achieves 4–5% lower clustering cost on SIFT while being comparable in runtime at k=10 but dramatically faster at larger k. Against the coreset method of Draganov et al. (POMOD 2024), BanditFastLS provides ~20× speedup on coreset construction time and ~80× end-to-end speedup with consistently better final clustering quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LS++ local search framework (Lattanzi & Sohler, ICML 2019) by replacing its O(ndk) distance-maintenance overhead with an adaptive tree-embedding structure (building on Cohen-Addad et al.'s HST rejection sampling, NeurIPS 2020) that approximates D²-Sampling in sublinear time. For swap pair identification, it adapts the bandit formulation from BanditPAM/BanditPAM++ (Tiwari et al.) but relaxes the strict Gaussian cost-change assumption to a sub-Gaussian prior with a grouping strategy, and introduces a Metropolis-Hastings-based practical variant that avoids the tree structure entirely. The work explicitly positions itself as bypassing coreset construction (Draganov et al., POMOD 2024), which the authors show remains impractical at 100M-point scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1000x speedup on 100M-point SIFT with comparable clustering quality makes constant-approximation local search viable for datasets where even O(ndk) methods are infeasible, and the k-median extension requiring no distributional assumptions is a clean theoretical contribution. Key limitations include the sub-Gaussian prior needed for k-means (hard to validate in practice since optimal clusters are unknown) and potential degradation when k approaches n. An open direction the authors highlight is designing fast local search for k-means without any data distribution assumptions, which would close the gap with the k-median result.
