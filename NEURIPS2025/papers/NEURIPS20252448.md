## Problem

Current visual encoders in VLMs produce a fixed number of tokens per image regardless of content complexity, and even dynamic-resolution encoders scale tokens only with image dimensions. Existing token reduction methods either impose a fixed compression ratio, require full model retraining, or depend on extra textual conditions, leaving no training-free, content-adaptive solution for mainstream open-weight VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252448/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images or video frames along with short text queries (typically ~24 tokens on benchmarks) and produces a text response. The key distinction is that the visual encoder outputs a variable-length token sequence whose length adapts to image complexity (e.g., 89±27 tokens for simple images vs. 394±57 for complex ones, out of a full 576), rather than a fixed count. The approach requires a ViT-based visual encoder (CLIP, SigLIP) and a RoPE-based LLM backbone, and is compatible with both fixed-resolution (LLaVA 1.5) and any-resolution (LLaVA-OneVision) VLM architectures.

## Method

DYMU comprises two training-free components. First, Dynamic Token Merging (DToMe) extends ToMe's bipartite soft matching by ranking similarity edges across an entire batch of images (rather than per-instance) to determine per-layer merging thresholds offline; at inference, tokens exceeding the threshold are merged with size-weighted averaging, so simpler images naturally lose more tokens. Second, Virtual Token Unmerging (VTU) reconstructs the full-length RoPE self-attention matrix from the reduced unique-token set by exploiting the linearity of RoPE's complex-number rotation, decomposing attention into sparse matrix products of cost O(N·N_un·D) instead of O(N²·D), then re-merging outputs to preserve redundancy for subsequent layers. The entire pipeline requires no fine-tuning: DToMe thresholds are computed once on a diverse image-only dataset, and VTU is a drop-in replacement for standard attention in any RoPE-based LLM.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B (576 visual tokens, Avg 63.1), DYMU-high (394±57 tokens) achieves an average score of 63.2, matching or slightly exceeding the full model while using only 68% of the tokens. DYMU-mid (195±47 tokens) retains 99.2% of baseline performance (Avg 62.6) with 66% fewer tokens, and DYMU-low (89±27 tokens) retains 97.5% (Avg 61.5) with 84.5% fewer tokens. On LLaVA-OneVision (AnyRes), DYMU-ov-mid (~25% tokens) preserves 99.2% of MMBench and 99.3% of MME performance. The method is entirely training-free and produces variable-length outputs that adapt to image complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DYMU consistently outperforms ToMe [3] at comparable token budgets: at ~90 tokens, DYMU-low (Avg 61.5) beats ToMe-94 (Avg 59.2) by 2.3 points; at ~200 tokens, DYMU-mid (62.6) beats ToMe-209 (61.4) by 1.2 points; at ~394 tokens, DYMU-high (63.2) beats ToMe-393 (62.2) by 1.0 point. Against fixed-length training-free methods, DYMU-mid (195 tokens) surpasses SparseVLM (128 tokens, no Avg reported but 57.2 GQA vs. 61.7), VisionZip (128 tokens, 57.6 GQA vs. 61.7), and FastV (128 tokens, 49.6 GQA vs. 61.7) on GQA and MMBench while using more tokens but achieving substantially higher scores. DYMU-high (394 tokens) matches or slightly exceeds the full LLaVA-1.5 baseline (63.2 vs. 63.1 Avg) while using only 68% of the visual tokens. Against training-required methods like MQT-LLaVA (256 tokens, 61.6 GQA) and LLaVA-Mini (1 token, 60.9 GQA), DYMU-mid achieves higher GQA (61.7) without any fine-tuning. The largest relative drop for DYMU occurs on TextVQA (53.1 vs. 58.3 for full model at DYMU-low), which the authors attribute to spatial sensitivity of visual text recognition to token merging. On LLaVA-OneVision, DYMU-ov-mid (~25% tokens) outperforms ToMe at the same compression ratio (76.0 vs. 71.2 MMBench; 70.3 vs. 63.1 MME), confirming the benefit of dynamic-length merging over fixed-length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ToMe's bipartite soft matching for ViT token merging by replacing its fixed per-instance compression ratio with a batch-level threshold-finding procedure that naturally yields content-adaptive, variable-length outputs. It departs from the broader VLM efficiency literature (FastV, SparseVLM, MQT-LLaVA, LLaVA-Prumerge, TokenPacker) on three axes: it is entirely training-free, it modifies both the visual encoder and the LLM decoder (via the novel Virtual Token Unmerging algorithm), and it requires no auxiliary textual conditions. The VTU component introduces a theoretical framework for efficiently reconstructing RoPE self-attention from a reduced token set using sparse matrix factorization, which has no direct precedent in prior VLM compression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DYMU offers a practical, plug-and-play path to reducing VLM inference costs by 32–85% without retraining, which is significant given that most open-weight VLMs do not release full training recipes. The training-free, content-adaptive nature also enables flexible integration with task-specific tools (background removal, OCR, object detection) for further cost control. Key limitations remain: performance degrades on text-dense tasks (TextVQA, OCR, DocVQA) where fine-grained spatial detail is critical, and the gap between theoretical FLOP savings and actual wall-clock speedup is non-trivial due to kernel-level implementation challenges. Extending the approach to reduce temporal redundancy in video and developing complexity-aware merging for information-dense scenes are identified as open directions.
