## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction methods (H2O, TOVA, SnapKV) rely on attention scores computed over the full prompt to rank token importance, but in resource-constrained edge deployments the prompt must be processed in small blocks to respect strict memory limits. Under block-wise processing, attention scores reflect only the partial context seen so far, causing premature eviction of tokens that would be important for later blocks. The paper identifies this design mismatch and asks whether token importance can be determined from key geometry alone, without access to attention weights or future tokens.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt segmented into non-overlapping blocks of size B (e.g., 128 tokens) and the current KV cache of at most N entries (e.g., 2K–8K tokens). After each block is processed, KEYDIFF scores every cached key by its cosine similarity to an anchor vector (the mean of all normalized cached keys) and evicts the most similar (redundant) entries to restore the cache to budget N. Because no attention matrix is materialized, the method is compatible with FlashAttention and runs in O(N+B) time and memory per block, producing a bounded-size KV cache that is passed to the next block or to autoregressive token generation.

## Method

KEYDIFF is a training-free, attention-free eviction policy that exploits the empirical observation that geometrically distinctive keys (those with low pairwise cosine similarity to other cached keys) consistently receive higher attention scores across layers and heads. Concretely, it computes an anchor vector μ(Ķ) as the mean of all L2-normalized cached keys, then scores each key by CosSim(μ(Ķ), kᵢ) and retains the N keys with the lowest scores (most dissimilar to the anchor), effectively maximizing key diversity in the cache. The paper provides a theoretical justification: Lemma 3.1 bounds the attention weight of a key in terms of its cosine similarity to the query, and Theorem 3.2 shows that when the mean key has negative cosine similarity with the query (empirically true across all heads), keys aligned with the query necessarily have low cosine similarity to the mean key—exactly the criterion KEYDIFF optimizes. An efficient O(n) variant replaces the full pairwise cosine similarity matrix with a single dot product against the anchor, and an optional sliding-window extension reserves a fraction of the budget for the most recent tokens to aid reasoning and coding tasks.

## Results

On LongBench (English subset, block size B=128), KEYDIFF achieves a 0.17-point average accuracy drop (49.03 vs. 49.20 non-evicting baseline) with an 8K cache budget (~23% KV cache reduction) on Llama 3.1-8B-Instruct, and a 0.69-point drop (48.51 vs. 49.20) with a 6K budget (~33% reduction). On the Math-500 reasoning benchmark, KEYDIFF with a sliding window achieves near-baseline performance (0.894 Flex Match vs. 0.898 full cache) on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B with 32K max generation length. KEYDIFF also reduces end-to-end time-to-first-token latency by up to 30% compared to TOVA and SnapKV by avoiding attention weight materialization and enabling FlashAttention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KEYDIFF outperforms all five compared eviction methods across every cache budget on LongBench. Against H2O [43], the gap is largest: at 8K, KEYDIFF scores 49.03 vs. H2O's 38.20 (a 10.8-point advantage), reflecting H2O's reliance on accumulated attention weights that degrade under block-wise processing. Against TOVA [24], KEYDIFF leads by 2.0 points at 8K (49.03 vs. 47.09) and 3.3 points at 6K (48.51 vs. 45.24). Against StreamingLLM/sink attention [35], KEYDIFF gains 4.4 points at 8K (49.03 vs. 44.68), as sink attention's fixed first-token retention cannot adapt to content. Against SnapKV [20], the closest competitor, KEYDIFF leads by 1.0 point at 8K (49.03 vs. 48.06) and 1.4 points at 6K (48.51 vs. 47.12). The L2-norm eviction method of Devoto et al. [9] is compared in supplementary Table 11 but does not appear in the main results; KEYDIFF's advantage over attention-based methods is attributed to its query-independent scoring, which avoids the mismatch between block-local attention and global token importance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of attention-score-based KV cache eviction (H2O, TOVA, SnapKV) by proposing an attention-free method grounded in key geometry. It builds on the attention-sink phenomenon (StreamingLLM) and the anisotropy/collinearity properties of transformer representations (Godey et al.), but reframes them as a practical eviction signal: keys that are geometrically distinctive (low cosine similarity to the cache mean) are the ones that receive high attention. The block-wise inference setting, while used in systems work (Sarathi, PagedAttention), had not been paired with a principled eviction policy that avoids materializing full attention matrices, which is what KEYDIFF addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KEYDIFF enables long-context LLM inference on edge devices with strict, fixed memory budgets by combining linear-complexity eviction with FlashAttention compatibility, achieving near-baseline accuracy (≤0.04% drop at 8K) with 23–33% KV cache reduction and up to 30% lower latency than attention-based eviction. The theoretical grounding (Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.2) provides a principled explanation for why key diversity proxies importance, which could inform future eviction designs. Limitations include evaluation primarily on GQA-based Llama and Qwen models; the authors note future work is needed for MLA and other attention variants, and the Qwen results show more sensitivity to eviction, suggesting architectural dependence.
