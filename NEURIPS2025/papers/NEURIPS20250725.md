## Problem

Existing motion transfer methods for diffusion transformers either require costly backbone fine-tuning (e.g., GWTF needs ~40 GPU days), disrupt the pretrained latent distribution by warping initial noise, or incur high computational overhead from full attention-based feature computation (DiTFlow). There is no efficient, training-free approach that can faithfully transfer motion from a reference video while preserving both prompt fidelity and the integrity of the pretrained model's latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250725/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video (from which dense optical flow is extracted via RAFT and downsampled to the latent grid) and a text prompt describing the desired content. It produces a generated video using the Wan2.1-1.3B backbone that matches the prompt's subject and scene while following the reference video's spatio-temporal motion. The optical flow is consumed as per-row and per-column cumulative displacements that modulate positional indices, and the output is a standard decoded video at the backbone's native resolution.

## Method

RoPECraft modifies only the rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) of a frozen diffusion transformer, requiring no backbone training. First, optical flow displacements from the reference video are used to warp the complex-exponential RoPE tensors along height and width dimensions, creating unique motion-augmented embeddings for each spatial row and column (Algorithm 2), while the temporal component is left unmodified. Second, during the first t=10 denoising steps, the motion-augmented RoPE tensors are further optimized via a flow-matching objective that aligns the model's predicted velocity with the target velocity derived from the reference latent. Third, a Fourier phase regularization term—projecting phase angles onto the unit circle via cos/sin to avoid discontinuities—is added to suppress duplicate generations and high-frequency artifacts. The entire pipeline operates at inference time with only 5 optimization steps per denoising step, achieving competitive motion fidelity at ~109 seconds per video.

## Results

On the DAVIS benchmark, RoPECraft achieves the best Motion Fidelity (MF = 0.5816) and the lowest content-debiased Fréchet Video Distance (CD-FVD = 1284.58), surpassing the strongest baseline GWTF by +0.0103 MF (~+1.8%) and −200.6 CD-FVD (~−13.5%). It ranks second on CLIP similarity (0.2350 vs. GWTF's 0.2378) and on both Fréchet Trajectory Distance variants (FTD-FG = 0.2644, FTD-FG+BG = 0.2584), where GWTF leads (0.2457 and 0.2308 respectively). On the more challenging RealCamVid dataset with camera motion and multiple subjects, RoPECraft dominates all metrics, achieving MF = 0.7019, CLIP = 0.2141, and FTD = 0.1697.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoPECraft outperforms all five baselines on Motion Fidelity and CD-FVD, the two metrics measuring motion alignment and video fidelity respectively. Against GWTF (the strongest competitor), RoPECraft gains +0.0103 MF and −200.6 CD-FVD, but trails on CLIP similarity (0.2350 vs. 0.2378) and both FTD variants (0.2644 vs. 0.2457 FG; 0.2584 vs. 0.2308 FG+BG), indicating GWTF captures slightly tighter trajectory geometry at the cost of overall video quality. Against SMM, MOFT, DiTFlow (both latent and RoPE variants), and ConMo, RoPECraft wins on every reported metric, with the largest margins over DiTFlow-RoPE (CD-FVD 1284.58 vs. 1852.90) and MOFT (MF 0.5816 vs. 0.4606). On the harder RealCamVid subset with camera motion and multiple subjects, RoPECraft extends its lead, achieving FTD = 0.1697 versus the next-best GWTF at 0.2072, and MF = 0.7019 versus 0.5796. A user study further confirms that RoPECraft is the most preferred method overall (30% first-choice) and that the proposed FTD metric correlates more strongly with human motion judgments than the prior MF metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoPECraft extends DiTFlow's insight that positional embeddings can be optimized at test time for motion transfer, but departs in two key ways: it initializes RoPE with optical-flow-warped displacements (rather than starting from default embeddings) and restricts optimization to RoPE tensors only, avoiding the latent-space deviation that plagues noise-warping methods like GWTF. The flow-matching velocity alignment and Fourier phase regularization are novel additions that address orientation errors and subject duplication, respectively. The proposed Fréchet Trajectory Distance (FTD) metric also carves new ground by replacing the direction-only Motion Fidelity score with a geometric curve-distance measure that is sensitive to magnitude, path shape, and spatial offset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving competitive motion transfer purely through RoPE manipulation without any backbone fine-tuning (unlike GWTF's ~40 GPU-day training requirement), RoPECraft lowers the practical barrier to controllable video generation and suggests that positional encodings carry more motion-relevant structure than previously exploited. The FTD metric exposes a blind spot in existing evaluation: Motion Fidelity can report perfect scores for trajectories that are geometrically offset or scaled, undermining confidence in prior comparisons. Open questions include handling extreme occlusions, zoom camera motions (where CoTracker3 degrades), and whether the approach generalizes to higher-resolution or longer-duration generation without the current backbone's decoding artifacts.
