## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for LVLMs optimize either local (attention-based) or global (diversity-based) objectives in isolation, leading to suboptimal pruning: attention-based methods preserve current-layer outputs but accumulate error in deeper layers, while diversity-based methods sacrifice local output consistency. No prior work jointly considers the impact of pruning on both the current layer and subsequent layers, and pruning layer selection relies on manual tuning rather than model-intrinsic properties.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on standard LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5/1.6, Qwen2.5-VL) that encode RGB images into 576–2880 visual tokens plus a text prompt. It progressively prunes image tokens across multiple transformer layers, retaining as few as 22% of original tokens, and is fully compatible with FlashAttention. A small calibration set of 64 samples from LLaVA-655k is used once to determine pruning layers, after which no further calibration is needed per benchmark.

## Method

BTP formulates a local-global pruning objective that combines an attention-based term (preserving current-layer output) with a diversity-based term (preserving downstream representations), weighted by λ that increases from early to late pruning stages. Pruning layers are selected via a task-independent strategy: computing cosine similarity of image token hidden states across layers and identifying layers where representations shift significantly. For the attention objective, a rebalancing operation counteracts positional-encoding bias by over-selecting top-k' tokens and then filling from earlier positions first. For the diversity objective (Max-Min Diversity Problem), a spatial-position initialization avoids the O(n²) exact solver by seeding the selection with tokens that are far apart in the 2D image grid, reducing practical latency. The entire pipeline is plug-and-play, requiring no retraining and adding only O(n) attention computation per pruning layer.

## Results

BTP achieves a 78% compression rate (retaining only 22% of original image tokens) while preserving 96.7% of the original models' average performance across six benchmarks (GQA, MME, MMBench, POPE, SQA, MM-VeT). On LLaVA-1.5-7B with 128 tokens, BTP scores 98% average vs. 96% for DivPrune and FastV, 94% for PyramidDrop, and 89% for VTW. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 25% token retention, BTP reaches 97% average, substantially outperforming PyramidDrop (82%) and VTW (65%). End-to-end latency is reduced by 7–17% compared to the unpruned model, while DivPrune's latency actually increases by 4–17% due to its O(n²) diversity computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTP consistently outperforms all four baselines across model families and compression ratios. Against DivPrune (the closest competitor, also using 128 tokens on LLaVA-1.5-7B), BTP gains +2% average (98% vs. 96%), with notable improvements on MME (+81.6) and GQA (+0.2), while matching or exceeding DivPrune on POPE and SQA. Against FastV (172 tokens), BTP achieves higher average performance (98% vs. 96%) with fewer tokens (128 vs. 172), and substantially outperforms it on MME (1487 vs. 1465) and GQA (59.0 vs. 57.6). PyramidDrop (192 tokens) trails BTP by 4% on LLaVA-1.5-7B (94% vs. 98%) and by 15% on Qwen2.5-VL (82% vs. 97%), where its fixed pruning schedule fails to adapt to the model's image-token processing dynamics. VTW performs worst overall, dropping to 65% on Qwen2.5-VL and 89% on LLaVA-1.5-7B, as its single-layer complete token elimination strategy discards all visual information after one layer. On LLaVA-1.6-7B (dynamic resolution), BTP matches FastV's 94% average but with better MME (1490.8 vs. 1469.1) and MMVET (30.3 vs. 32.3 is a loss), while DivPrune drops to 92%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of visual token pruning for LVLMs: attention-based methods (FastV, PyramidDrop, VTW) that select tokens by text-image attention scores, and diversity-based methods (DivPrune) that maximize semantic spread among retained tokens. The key conceptual departure is the identification of a local-vs-global optimization tension: attention-based pruning preserves current-layer outputs but accumulates error in deeper layers, while diversity-based pruning preserves downstream representations but degrades local consistency. BTP resolves this by formulating a joint local-global objective with stage-dependent weighting (diversity-dominant early, attention-dominant late), and inherits DivPrune's Max-Min Diversity Problem formulation while replacing its O(n²) solver with a spatial-position initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: 78% token compression retaining 96.7% average performance across LLaVA and Qwen2.5-VL, with a plug-and-play design compatible with FlashAttention. A notable practical finding is that DivPrune's end-to-end latency actually exceeds the uncompressed model (0.224s vs 0.145s) despite lower theoretical FLOPs, highlighting that FLOPs alone mislead practitioners about real-world speedup. Open questions include whether the calibration-based layer selection generalizes to video inputs, very high-resolution settings beyond 2880 tokens, and architectures with fundamentally different image-token processing pipelines. The authors also note future work on lightweight on-device deployment and multi-agent applications.
