## Problem

Offline meta-RL methods for zero-shot task generalization typically require expensive supervision signals—high-quality expert demonstrations or warmup explorations—to infer task beliefs at test time, making them infeasible for truly unseen tasks. The paper asks whether natural language, a far broader and cheaper source of supervision, can replace these restricted signals to train generalist decision agents that follow arbitrary textual instructions without any task-specific data at evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252645/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes paired offline datasets (state, action, reward, next-state tuples collected by SAC policies) alongside a single natural language caption per task (e.g., 'Please walk toward the target direction of θ'). At test time, the agent receives only a free-form language instruction and the current environment state, then outputs an H-step action plan (T2DA-D, H=10–50 depending on environment) or a single autoregressive action per timestep (T2DA-T). The text encoder is a frozen pre-trained model (CLIP ViT-B/32, BERT-base, or T5-small) fine-tuned via LoRA, so the textual input is a short sentence rather than a structured goal vector.

## Method

T2DA is a three-stage pipeline. First, a generalized world model—a 6-layer bi-directional transformer encoder that tokenizes states, actions, and rewards into 256-dim vectors and pools them into a dynamics-aware embedding z, paired with MLP decoders for reward and next-state prediction—is pre-trained to capture task-specific environment dynamics. Second, inspired by CLIP, the text encoder is fine-tuned (via LoRA, <1% parameters) with a symmetric contrastive loss that maximizes cosine similarity between matched (trajectory, text) pairs and minimizes it across N×N in-batch negatives, distilling the world-model structure into the text modality. Third, the aligned text embedding ψ(l) conditions one of two scalable policy architectures: a DiT-based return-conditioned diffusion model (T2DA-D) that denoises H-step state-action trajectories with classifier-free guidance, or a causal GPT-style transformer (T2DA-T) that prepends a single text-embedding token to the return-state-action sequence and predicts actions autoregressively. The modular design—separately training the world model, alignment, and policy—yields 20–56% gains over joint training and enables zero-shot text-to-decision generation at test time.

## Results

On zero-shot test return (higher is better), T2DA-T and T2DA-D consistently achieve the top two positions across all four main benchmarks using Mixed datasets. On Ant-Dir, T2DA-T reaches 970.3±8.7, surpassing the strongest language-conditioned baseline BAKU (786.9±17.3) by ~183 points and the best offline meta-RL baseline CSRO (317.1±27.6) by ~653 points. On Meta-World, T2DA-D achieves 1376.4±39.6, outperforming BAKU (1187.6±18.2) by ~189 points. On Cheetah-Vel, T2DA-D attains −60.4±1.8, beating CSRO (−92.6±5.9) by ~32 points, while on Point-Robot T2DA-T reaches −7.2±0.1 versus UNICORN's −11.4±1.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2DA outperforms all seven main baselines across every environment. Against context-based offline meta-RL methods (CSRO, UNICORN), T2DA achieves the largest margins on Ant-Dir (970.3 vs. 317.1/407.3) and Meta-World (1376.4 vs. 1005.2/754.6), where these methods struggle to infer task beliefs without warmup exploration. Against the in-context RL baseline AD, T2DA wins on all four benchmarks, with the gap most pronounced on Ant-Dir (970.3 vs. 268.9). Against language-conditioned policy baselines BC-Z and BAKU, T2DA still leads consistently—BAKU is the closest competitor on Ant-Dir (786.9) and Meta-World (1187.6), but T2DA surpasses it by 183 and 189 points respectively. In the extended comparison (Table 13), T2DA also beats additional OMRL baselines Meta-DT (922.1), MetaDiffuser (858.5), and Prompt-DT (895.2) on Ant-Dir, and on the Humanoid benchmark (Table 12) T2DA-T leads all eight baselines with 610.4 return versus the next-best BC-Z at 531.7.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts CLIP's contrastive alignment framework from the text-image domain to the text-decision domain, introducing a three-stage pipeline: (1) a generalized world model that encodes multi-task trajectories into dynamics-aware embeddings, (2) CLIP-style contrastive pre-training that aligns text encoder outputs (initialized from CLIP, BERT, or T5) with those decision embeddings via LoRA fine-tuning, and (3) text-conditioned policy learning using either a Decision Diffuser (DiT-based) or Decision Transformer architecture. It departs from prior offline meta-RL methods (CSRO, UNICORN, PEARL) that require expert demonstrations or warmup explorations to infer task beliefs, and from language-conditioned policy methods (BC-Z, BAKU) that use pre-trained language embeddings without any alignment to environment dynamics. The key conceptual departure is treating the semantic gap between unbounded text representations and bounded decision-level representations as a learnable alignment problem rather than assuming pre-trained embeddings are sufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that natural language can serve as a scalable, task-agnostic supervision signal for offline meta-RL, eliminating the need for expensive expert demonstrations or environment-specific warmup at test time. The t-SNE visualizations showing aligned text embeddings forming cyclic (Ant-Dir) or rectilinear (Cheetah-Vel) structures that match the physical periodicity of task parameters provide compelling evidence that the alignment genuinely captures environment dynamics rather than mere linguistic similarity. However, the evaluation is limited to lightweight simulation benchmarks (200 trajectories per task, 45–50 tasks per domain) and does not yet address the scaling to large-scale, multi-domain datasets or real-robot deployment that would be needed for practical impact. Open questions include whether the contrastive alignment generalizes to continuous, unstructured language instructions in complex embodied settings, and whether the approach can be integrated into VLA models for real-world robotics.
