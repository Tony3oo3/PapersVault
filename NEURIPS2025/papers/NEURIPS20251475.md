## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods struggle to simultaneously achieve effective knowledge removal and preserved model utility, and critically lack controllability: they produce hallucinations, gibberish, or monotonous 'I don't know' responses when prompted with unlearned data, rather than emulating the base model's natural, contextually-aware expression of knowledge gaps. Additionally, gradient-ascent and preference-optimization-based methods incur high computational and memory costs that scale poorly with model size, limiting real-world deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three text sets: a forget set of QA pairs to unlearn, a retain set of QA pairs to preserve, and a reference set of prompts that activate the model's existing 'I don't know' behavior (either harmful prompts triggering safety guardrails or unverifiable prompts about fictitious entities). The reference set need not be semantically contrastive with the forget set, which is a key departure from prior steering-vector approaches. The output is a surgically modified single MLP down-projection matrix (at one selected layer) that, when re-inserted into the frozen base model, causes the model to generate coherent, contextually-appropriate refusal responses to forget-set queries while maintaining full performance on retain-set queries.

## Method

LUNAR computes an unlearning vector (UV) as the difference in mean residual stream activations between reference prompts (triggering 'inability to answer' behavior) and forget-set prompts at a selected intermediate layer. It then redirects forget-set activations by adding the UV and optimizes only the single MLP down-projection matrix at that layer to minimize the L2 distance between original and redirected activations (the LUNAR loss). The layer is selected by maximizing cosine similarity between generated refusal responses and desired coherent refusals while minimizing similarity with unrelated refusal reasons. Because the down-projection is a linear layer without activation, the optimization admits a closed-form solution (ridge-regularized least squares), and the convex, smooth objective guarantees SGD convergence. This single-matrix design reduces trainable parameters from O(L·m·r·2d) to O(r·2d), yielding ~20× computational savings, and the concentrated parameter modification provides inherent robustness against quantization and reverse-direction attacks.

## Results

LUNAR achieves the lowest Deviation Score (DS) and highest Control Score across all model-dataset combinations. On PISTOL, LUNAR reduces DS to 7.8 (Llama2-7B), 6.3 (Gemma-7B), and 5.9 (Qwen2-7B), representing 2.9×–11.7× improvement over the best baseline (DPO or UKL). On TOFU, LUNAR achieves DS of 14.9, 13.1, and 14.3 respectively, with Control Scores of 0.608–0.659, consistently outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. LUNAR also achieves near-perfect forget ROUGE1 (0.000) while retaining >0.85 retain ROUGE1 when unlearning pre-trained factual data from base models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LUNAR dominates all seven baselines on both DS and Control Score across every model and dataset. The closest competitor is DPO, which achieves DS of 22.8 on PISTOL/Llama2-7B and 15.2 on TOFU/Llama2-7B, but LUNAR still improves by 2.9× and 1.0× respectively; on Gemma-7B PISTOL, DPO's Control Score (0.692) is close to LUNAR's (0.701) but its DS (23.4) is 3.7× worse. Gradient-based methods (GA, GD, UKL) and NPO consistently produce the worst DS (30–86) and lowest Control Scores (0.34–0.46), indicating severe degradation of both forgetting and retention. RMU (Representation Misdirection) performs poorly across all settings (DS 38–86), confirming that random activation misdirection is inferior to targeted redirection. Notably, even 'retrain from scratch' (exact unlearning) underperforms LUNAR by 2.1×–5.5× on DS, suggesting that LUNAR's activation-space intervention is more effective than simply re-optimizing on the retain set.

## Relation to Prior Work

LUNAR extends the representation-engineering / steering-vector line of work (Panickssery et al. 2023; Marks & Tegmark 2023) by demonstrating that explicit contrastive feature pairs are not a prerequisite for effective activation redirection, broadening the applicability of linear steering to instance-level unlearning. It builds on ROME (Meng et al. 2022) in identifying MLP down-projections as the locus of factual knowledge, but departs sharply by restricting all parameter updates to a single down-projection matrix rather than editing multiple layers. The target activation region is informed by the refusal-direction literature (Arditi et al. 2024), repurposing the model's existing safety or ignorance-acknowledgment behavior as the behavioral target for unlearned queries, rather than relying on open-ended or monotonous refusal strings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× computational and memory reduction from updating a single matrix makes unlearning far more tractable at scale, and the closed-form solution provides a principled convergence guarantee absent in gradient-ascent or preference-optimization baselines. Introducing controllability as a first-class evaluation criterion addresses a real gap: prior methods often 'succeed' on ROUGE metrics while producing hallucinations or incoherent gibberish, which is unacceptable for deployment. Open questions include whether single-layer intervention suffices for more entangled or multi-faceted knowledge, how the method scales to 70B+ models, and whether the broad-vs-precise activation-region asymmetry that underpins robustness to reverse-direction attacks holds universally.
