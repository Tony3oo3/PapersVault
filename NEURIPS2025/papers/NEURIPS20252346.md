## Problem

Training-aware visual token compression in MLLMs introduces substantial feature-space perturbations that shift the model's optimal point in parameter space, yet existing methods largely overlook this increased learning difficulty. Directly training with compressed tokens often traps optimization in poor local minima, especially under high compression ratios, resulting in significant performance degradation that architectural modifications alone cannot fully recover.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at 336×336 resolution by CLIP ViT-L/14 into 576 patch tokens) and a text prompt, producing autoregressive text tokens. During both training and inference, a plug-and-play compression operator (DART, FastV, or random pruning) removes a configurable fraction of visual tokens at a specific Transformer layer, reducing the sequence from 576 to as few as 36 tokens. The same trained model supports flexible token counts at inference without retraining, adapting to varying computational budgets.

## Method

EPIC is a progressive consistency distillation framework in which a single MLLM simultaneously serves as teacher and student via weight sharing. Token Consistency Distillation (TCD) progressively increases the student's compression ratio from ~5% to 90% over training while the teacher operates at a slightly lower ratio (gap also grows progressively), with a KL-divergence loss between their temperature-scaled output logits providing guidance. Layer Consistency Distillation (LCD) shifts the compression location from the deepest Transformer layer to progressively shallower layers, exploiting the observation that visual tokens receive negligible attention in deep layers. A 1D theoretical prototype proves that the progressive path has strictly shorter total variation than direct training, formalizing why the easy-to-hard curriculum yields smoother optimization. The framework requires no architectural changes—only a single SFT stage (~12 hours on 8 A100s)—and is compatible with any plug-and-play token compression strategy.

## Results

With 128 visual tokens (77.8% reduction from vanilla LLaVA's 576), EPIC achieves 61.3% average across 10 benchmarks, essentially matching vanilla LLaVA-v1.5 (61.4%). At 192+ tokens, both TCD and LCD surpass the vanilla model (61.4%→61.7% and 62.0% respectively). At 64 tokens (88.9% reduction), performance degrades by only 2.0% (59.4%), while inference FLOPs drop by 83.9% and KV cache by 88.9%. Training completes in ~12 hours on 8 A100 GPUs, compared to 26–48 hours for architecture-modifying baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPIC at 128 tokens (61.3% avg) outperforms MQT-LLaVA at the same token count (57.6%) by 3.7 points and TokenPacker at 144 tokens (59.9%) by 1.4 points, while using fewer tokens. At 64 tokens, EPIC (59.4%) beats Average-Pooling (55.9%) by 3.5 points and MQT-LLaVA (56.8%) by 2.6 points. LLaVA-Mini suffers severe degradation at 144 tokens (24.8% MMBench vs. EPIC's 63.9%), indicating its architectural approach is far less robust. At 192+ tokens, EPIC's LCD variant surpasses vanilla LLaVA-v1.5 (62.0–62.2% vs. 61.4%), whereas all other token-compression baselines remain below the vanilla model. The paper also demonstrates that EPIC generalizes across compression strategies: a model trained with DART compression transfers effectively to FastV and Random pruning at inference, with consistent gains over untrained baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLaVA's architecture and training pipeline, positioning its contribution as a training strategy rather than an architectural modification. It departs from prior training-aware token compression methods (MQT-LLaVA, TokenPacker, VoCo-LLaMA, LLaVA-Mini) that introduce new modules or replace components, instead reframing token compression as a progressive learning problem where a single shared-weight model acts as both teacher and student at different compression ratios. The theoretical grounding via a 1D scalar prototype (Theorem 1) provides a clean but simplified justification for why progressive distillation yields shorter optimization paths than direct training under heavy compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: EPIC achieves comparable performance to vanilla LLaVA with only 128 visual tokens (77.8% reduction) while requiring just ~12 hours of training on 8 A100s versus 30–48 hours for architecture-modifying baselines, and without any architectural changes. The 'High ROI Area' analysis (64 tokens as the sweet spot) challenges the field's assumption that extreme compression (1–2 tokens) is always beneficial, showing diminishing latency returns and sharp performance drops beyond that point. Key limitations include evaluation only on LLaVA-v1.5/Vicuna-7B, application only to Stage 2 instruction tuning (not pretraining), and the theoretical analysis being restricted to a 1D prototype that may not capture the full complexity of high-dimensional feature space perturbations.
