## Problem

Machine learning interatomic potentials have accelerated force evaluation in molecular dynamics, but simulations remain constrained to ~1 fs integration steps dictated by the fast timescale of atomic motion, making experimentally relevant timescales inaccessible. Prior direct trajectory-prediction methods were limited to single systems under fixed thermodynamic conditions and largely ignored fundamental physical constraints such as energy conservation, symplecticity, and chaoticity. FlashMD addresses these gaps by providing a universal, long-stride MD predictor with explicit treatment of the mathematical and physical pitfalls of bypassing Hamiltonian integration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252961/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single atomic configuration at time step τ — positions, momenta, and atomic numbers — and deterministically predicts the mass-scaled displacements (q_i′−q_i)√m_i and mass-scaled momenta p_i′/√m_i at time τ+Δτ, where Δτ spans 1–2 orders of magnitude beyond conventional MD steps (up to 64 fs demonstrated). The mass-scaling equalizes the contribution of light and heavy atoms to the loss, and center-of-mass momentum and position constraints are enforced to prevent unphysical drift. Optional inference-time filters (energy rescaling, thermostat/barostat split operators, random rotation) can be applied around each prediction step.

## Method

FlashMD is built on the Point-Edge Transformer GNN, modified so that node features are initialized with atomic momenta (via an MLP) alongside chemical species, and two separate MLP heads predict the new positions and momenta. The key technical contributions are: (i) an inference-time energy-conservation filter that rescales momenta after each step to eliminate drift in NVE trajectories; (ii) a training-time model-selection criterion that penalizes energy misalignment of predicted structures; (iii) a split-operator integration scheme (OBABO) that embeds a single FlashMD step between half-stride thermostat/barostat propagators, enabling sampling of arbitrary thermodynamic ensembles; and (iv) a near-zero-overhead uncertainty quantification scheme combining a mean-variance estimator for aleatoric (chaoticity-driven) uncertainty with a last-layer Laplace approximation for epistemic uncertainty. The universal model is trained on 1 million NVE trajectory frames generated with the PET-MAD MLIP across 10,000 diverse structures and temperatures up to 1500 K.

## Results

For liquid water NVT simulations, water-specific FlashMD with energy conservation enforcement achieves temperature deviations within ~1 K (e.g., −0.3(0.8) K for the 1 fs Langevin model), while the universal model without energy conservation shows deviations up to 33.8 K. With energy conservation, the universal model's all-atom temperature deviation drops to 0.2–0.7 K across all stride lengths (1–16 fs). The universal FlashMD model successfully captures the superionic transition of γ-Li₃PS₄ at 675 K (within the established range from reference PET-MAD simulations) and reproduces the Ramachandran distribution of solvated alanine dipeptide with strides up to 32× larger than the 0.5 fs stability limit of conventional MD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against MDNet (Zheng et al., 2021) and TrajCast (Thiemann et al., 2025) in Appendices H and I, respectively, though the specific numerical comparisons are not reported in the main text. The key qualitative distinction is that both prior methods are restricted to reproducing MD trajectories for a single specific system under prescribed thermodynamic conditions, whereas FlashMD demonstrates universal applicability across diverse chemical systems (water, alanine dipeptide, Al(110) surface, LiPS) and multiple thermodynamic ensembles (NVE, NVT, NpT) with a single model. The paper also contrasts FlashMD with non-conservative direct-force MLIPs (e.g., Orb-v3), noting that FlashMD shares their lack of exact symplecticity but achieves a far larger effective time stride (up to 64 fs vs. ~1 fs), and that the energy-conservation enforcement at inference time is a critical differentiator that stabilizes long trajectories where prior direct-propagation methods would drift.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashMD builds directly on the Point-Edge Transformer (PET) architecture [18], adapting it from force/energy prediction to direct trajectory prediction by adding momentum inputs and multi-head position/momentum outputs. It departs from prior direct MD prediction methods (MDNet [62], TrajCast [63]) which were restricted to single chemical systems and specific thermodynamic conditions, by providing a universal model trained on the MAD dataset and a Liouvillian-splitting framework for arbitrary ensembles. The paper also draws conceptual motivation from the 'direct force' MLIP literature [20–22], which demonstrated that explicit potential energy surfaces are not strictly necessary for stable dynamics, and adopts the random-rotation correction strategy of Langer et al. [20] to handle the PET architecture's lack of strict rotational equivariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlashMD extends the accessible time scales of atomistic simulation by 1–2 orders of magnitude with a single model pass, and the universal model's ability to handle diverse chemical systems and thermodynamic ensembles makes it a practical drop-in accelerator for exploratory MD. Key limitations remain: the approach is non-symplectic (requiring energy rescaling at inference), chaoticity imposes a hard ceiling on usable stride length that is system-dependent, and the universal model shows non-negligible deviations in some observables (e.g., NpT density). Open questions include whether symplectic-preserving architectures [81] can retain the speed advantage, whether multi-stride single models are feasible, and how to scale training data and model capacity to push stride lengths further without the polynomial error growth observed here.
