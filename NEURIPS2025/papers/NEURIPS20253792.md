## Problem

Existing few-shot learning methods that augment support features with textual semantics rely solely on class names, ignoring the visual patterns present in the few support images. This causes semantic hallucinations where generated descriptions contradict the actual visual evidence, and naive prompting fails to leverage LLMs' full reasoning capacity. Moreover, standard pairwise contrastive objectives neglect the global geometric structure among multiple cross-modal representations, limiting the quality of multimodal integration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253792/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes class names and K-shot support images (resized to 224×224) to generate textual descriptions via Qwen2.5-VL-32B and synthetic images via Janus-Pro, all in a one-time offline stage. At inference, a query image is classified against prototypes that are convex combinations of text-enhanced support features and synthetic-image-augmented features, yielding a class label with softmax probabilities over N classes. The LLM and text-to-image models are not invoked during training or inference, so the downstream FSL model incurs no additional computational overhead beyond the lightweight Visformer-T backbone.

## Method

VT-FSL introduces two key modules. Cross-modal Iterative Prompting (CIP) conditions a VLM on both the class name and K support images through four structured reasoning stages (Strategy, Perception, Refinement, Conclusion) in a single inference pass, producing precise, visually grounded descriptions that drive zero-shot synthesis of K semantically consistent images per class. Cross-modal Geometric Alignment (CGA) then jointly aligns the fused textual (CLIP), enhanced support, and synthetic visual embeddings by minimizing the kernelized volume of the 3-dimensional parallelotope they span in an RKHS defined by an RBF kernel, capturing global and nonlinear cross-modal dependencies beyond pairwise similarity. Textual features are injected into support features via a two-layer MLP for channel modulation followed by spatial concatenation with multi-head self-attention. Training uses a two-stage episodic protocol (pre-training then meta-tuning) with a combined cross-entropy and volume-based contrastive loss, and the final prototype is a convex combination of the text-enhanced and visual-enhanced class centers.

## Results

On miniImageNet, VT-FSL achieves 83.66% (1-shot) and 88.38% (5-shot) accuracy, surpassing the previous best SemFew (78.94%/86.49%) by 4.72%/1.89%. On tieredImageNet, it reaches 88.02% (1-shot) and 91.71% (5-shot), outperforming CPEA (76.93%/90.12%) and FeatWalk (75.25%/89.92%). Across all ten benchmarks (standard, fine-grained, and cross-domain), VT-FSL improves average accuracy by 4.2% over prior state-of-the-art methods, while using a lightweight Visformer-T backbone (10M params) that outperforms methods with 22–36.5M parameter backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VT-FSL outperforms all 43 compared baselines across every benchmark. On standard FSL, it surpasses the strongest semantic-based competitor SemFew (CVPR'24) by 3.7%–5.7% in 1-shot and 1.0%–2.7% in 5-shot, and beats ECER (AAAI'25) by 2.5% on miniImageNet 1-shot, while using the same Visformer-T backbone with only 0.7M additional fusion parameters versus SemFew's 4.3M. On fine-grained datasets (CUB, Dogs, Cars), VT-FSL exceeds the second-best SUITED (AAAI'25) by 3.0%–10.3% in the challenging 1-shot setting. In cross-domain transfer (miniImageNet→CUB/Places/Plantae), it leads the second-best SVasP (AAAI'25) by 4.35%–15.31% in 1-shot. Notably, VT-FSL also achieves the lowest training time (1.1 min) and inference latency (76 ms) among all LLM-based methods, compared to ECER's 3.0 min and 119 ms, confirming that its one-time offline prompt generation adds negligible runtime overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic-enhanced few-shot learning line (AM3, SP, SIFT, SemFew, ECER) by conditioning LLMs on both class names and support images rather than names alone, and by adding a text-to-image generation step for visual prompt augmentation. The core classification paradigm remains prototypical networks (Snell et al., 2017), but the alignment mechanism departs from standard pairwise contrastive learning (InfoNCE) by introducing a kernelized volume-based objective in RKHS, building on the Gram-matrix volume formulation from Cicchetti et al. (2024) and extending it with an RBF kernel for nonlinear interactions. Structured Chain-of-Thought prompting (CIP) is borrowed from LLM reasoning literature and adapted as a single-pass, tag-delimited inference procedure for class description generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design—where LLM and text-to-image models are invoked once offline to produce fixed cross-modal prompts—makes the approach practical: downstream FSL models incur virtually no extra training or inference overhead, and VT-FSL actually achieves the lowest training/inference times among LLM-based competitors. The kernelized volume-based alignment offers a geometrically principled alternative to pairwise contrastive objectives that captures higher-order (non-anchor) interactions among modalities, a perspective that could transfer to other multimodal alignment problems. Open questions include sensitivity to the specific LLM/T2I choices (Qwen2.5-VL-32B, Janus-Pro), whether the approach extends to video or 3D few-shot settings, and whether the 3-vector parallelotope formulation generalizes cleanly to more than three modalities.
