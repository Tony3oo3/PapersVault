## Problem

Existing synthetic data generation and evaluation methods for medical data treat all variables identically, ignoring the causal structure between covariates, treatments, and outcomes that is essential for downstream treatment effect estimation. Standard joint-distribution metrics lose sensitivity to the treatment assignment and outcome generation mechanisms as covariate dimensionality grows, and generic generative models lack inductive biases to preserve these critical conditional distributions. This paper establishes desiderata for treatment-aware synthetic data and proposes both aligned evaluation metrics and a generation framework that explicitly target the three causal components.

## Input / Output

The method takes real observational or experimental data D_r = {(X, W, Y)} with d binary/continuous covariates, a binary treatment W, and a binary/continuous outcome Y, along with a user-chosen base generative model for the covariate distribution. It produces synthetic data D_s with the same structure, generated in three sequential steps rather than jointly. Evaluation outputs four metrics—P_α,X, R_β,X (covariate precision/recall), JSD_π (propensity score preservation), and U_PEHE (CATE estimation parity)—that answer specific analyst questions about representativeness, treatment assignment accuracy, and treatment effect estimation error, rather than a single holistic score.

## Method

STEAM is a model-agnostic, three-step generation framework that mimics the data-generating process of treatment data: (1) covariates X are drawn from a generic generative model (e.g., CTGAN, TabDDPM) trained to match P_X; (2) treatments W are assigned via a logistic regression propensity classifier trained on D_r (or set directly if experimental data with known P_{W|X} is available); (3) outcomes Y are generated using S-learner potential outcome estimators trained on D_r, selecting the relevant arm based on the assigned treatment. The key insight is that decomposing the joint distribution P(X,W,Y) = P_X · P_{W|X} · P_{Y|W,X} and modeling each factor separately directly targets the distributions critical for causal inference, avoiding the dimensionality-dominated failure mode of joint-distribution minimization (formally proven in Theorem 1). For evaluation, the paper proposes JSD_π (Jensen-Shannon distance between propensity score distributions estimated by classifiers on real vs. synthetic data) and U_PEHE (average PEHE between CATE learners trained on real vs. synthetic data, averaged over a family of learners to handle the model selection dilemma), complementing standard covariate precision/recall metrics.

## Results

Across three medical datasets (ACTG, IHDP, ACIC), STEAM consistently outperforms all five generic generative model baselines on the proposed causal-inference-aligned metrics. The most dramatic gains appear in U_PEHE (outcome generation preservation): e.g., STEAM_NFlow reduces U_PEHE from 3.835 to 2.177 on IHDP (−1.658) and STEAM_CTGAN from 4.518 to 2.268 on ACIC (−2.250). JSD_π (treatment assignment preservation) also improves substantially, e.g., from 0.838 to 0.971 on ACIC with STEAM_NFlow (+0.133). Covariate metrics (P_α,X, R_β,X) show only marginal changes, confirming that STEAM's primary benefit lies in preserving the treatment and outcome mechanisms rather than the covariate distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against generic generative models (CTGAN/TVAE, ARF, NFlow, TabDDPM), STEAM wins on JSD_π and U_PEHE in virtually every dataset–model combination, with the largest U_PEHE reductions seen for TabDDPM (e.g., −4.784 on ACIC, from 9.281 to 4.497) and NFlow (e.g., −1.658 on IHDP, from 3.835 to 2.177). Covariate metrics (P_α,X, R_β,X) show only marginal differences, as expected since STEAM delegates covariate generation to the same base model. Against causal generative models (DCM and ANM via DoWhy-GCM), STEAM outperforms on JSD_π and U_PEHE across all three datasets, with U_PEHE gaps of +0.104 to +1.827 in favour of STEAM; the sole exception is P_α,X on ACIC, where DCM (0.942) and ANM (0.929) slightly exceed STEAM_TVAE (0.900). In simulated experiments, the performance gap between STEAM and generic generation widens monotonically with covariate dimensionality, propensity complexity, and outcome heterogeneity, confirming that STEAM's structural decomposition becomes increasingly advantageous as the DGP grows more complex.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself between two existing paradigms for synthetic data generation: generic tabular generators (CTGAN, TVAE, TabDDPM, NFlow, ARF) that model the full joint distribution without causal structure, and causal generative models (DCM, ANM) that require knowledge of the complete causal graph. STEAM departs from both by assuming only the standard treatment DGP (X → W → Y) without requiring the full causal graph, and by decomposing generation into three targeted sub-problems. For evaluation, it builds on the P_α/R_β sample-level metrics from Alaa et al. [2] for covariate assessment, but introduces new metrics (JSD_π, U_PEHE) specifically for treatment assignment and outcome generation preservation, which no prior synthetic data evaluation framework addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in medical research: regulatory restrictions on patient data make synthetic data essential, yet current generators produce data that are poor for the causal inference tasks (CATE, ATE estimation) that medical analysts actually need. The theoretical result (Theorem 1) showing that joint-distribution metrics like KL divergence lose sensitivity to treatment and outcome mechanisms as covariate dimensionality grows provides a principled justification for the new evaluation protocol. Open questions include extending STEAM to multiple/continuous treatments, incorporating instrumental variables, and developing sophisticated differential privacy mechanisms that allocate privacy budgets across the three component models. The framework's model-agnostic design means it can benefit from future improvements in base generative models without architectural changes.
