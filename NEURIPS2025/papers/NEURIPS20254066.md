## Problem

Incomplete multi-view multi-label classification (iMvMLC) suffers from dual data incompleteness where both views and labels are partially missing, yet existing methods inadequately address three coupled challenges: robust feature reconstruction without amplifying noise, semantic distinctness of extracted representations across views, and the coupling between label correlation semantics and feature connotation. Prior approaches either mask missing samples rather than recovering them, rely on linear geometric constraints that miss complex feature interactions, or treat labels as independent binary classifications ignoring interdependent label semantics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N×d_v feature matrices per view with binary view-missing indicators W∈{0,1}^{N×V} and a binary label matrix Y∈{0,1}^{N×C} with label-missing indicators G∈{0,1}^{N×C}, where each sample is guaranteed at least one complete view. Outputs include per-view multi-label predictions fused via reconstruction-confidence weighting (higher-confidence views contribute more), label-specific shared and private feature tensors of shape N×C×d per view, and reconstructed missing view features that serve as proxies for downstream classification. The confidence score for each missing instance is derived from the maximum cross-view attention score, naturally down-weighting poorly reconstructed views.

## Method

TDLSR comprises three tightly coupled modules: (1) a proximity-aware graph attention recovery mechanism that builds view-specific k-NN relation graphs via attention scores, transfers similarity across available views to identify relevant neighbors for missing instances, and performs weighted message propagation for imputation; (2) a universal view extraction framework that disentangles shared and view-private representations through mutual information optimization enforcing three principles—information shift (reconstruction fidelity), information interaction (maximizing shared cross-view MI while minimizing private cross-view MI), and information orthogonality (zero MI between shared and private from the same view)—implemented via variational lower/upper bounds with KL divergences; (3) a multi-label semantic module that learns label prototypes via a GIN layer on a Jaccard-correlation graph, then uses sigmoid-activated prototypes as feature importance scores to extract label-specific shared and private features, enabling bidirectional selection between discriminative views and sensitive label subsets. The paper further proves three theorems: discriminability conditions for label-specific representations, optimality of the disentangled solution in maximizing I(R;Y), and a generalization error bound that tightens as the information-theoretic objective is optimized.

## Results

At the standard 50% PER / 50% LMR setting, TDLSR achieves the best average rank (1st) across all six benchmark datasets. Headline AP values are 0.450 (Corel 5k), 0.328 (ESPGame), 0.358 (IAPRTC12), 0.631 (Mirflickr), 0.692 (OBJECT), and 0.590 (Pascal07), with corresponding AUC values of 0.919, 0.863, 0.899, 0.875, 0.918, and 0.880. On the real-world NBA dataset, TDLSR reaches AP 0.668 at 90% missing ratio, whereas all baselines fall below 0.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDLSR achieves rank 1 on all six datasets, outperforming every baseline on every metric. The closest competitors are MTD and SIP, which consistently occupy ranks 2–3; on Corel 5k, TDLSR improves AP from 0.414 (MTD/SIP) to 0.450 (+3.6 pts) and AUC from 0.910 to 0.919. On OBJECT, the gap widens to 4.3 AP points (0.692 vs. 0.649 for MTD). Against the attention-based imputation method AIMNet, TDLSR gains roughly 4–5 AP points across datasets (e.g., 0.450 vs. 0.404 on Corel 5k), attributed to the proximity-aware graph strategy that suppresses reconstruction noise. Traditional multi-label methods (DM2L, iMVWL) and deep frameworks that ignore label correlation (DICNet, DIMC) trail by 10+ AP points, confirming the importance of the label-prototype GIN module. At extreme missingness (90% PER on Corel 5k and ESPGame), all nine baselines collapse while TDLSR retains usable performance, and on the NBA dataset at 90% missing ratio TDLSR reaches AP 0.668 versus <0.60 for all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

TDLSR extends the incomplete multi-view multi-label classification (iMvMLC) line initiated by iMVWL, NAIM3L, and deep methods like DICNet, LMVCAT, and DIMC, which primarily address missing data through contrastive learning or transformer-based aggregation. It departs from MTD's linear geometric constraints for view separation by formulating a mutual information optimization model with three explicit principles (information shift, interaction, orthogonality), and from AIMNet's global attention imputation by introducing proximity-aware k-NN graph construction that suppresses reconstruction noise. The label-specific representation learning builds on label prototype modeling (SIP, [27]) and label-specific feature selection ([14]), but is the first to jointly extract label-specific shared and private features across views, with label correlation propagated via GIN layers over a Jaccard-distance graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, end-to-end framework for a practically important but underexplored setting where both views and labels are partially missing, with guarantees on discriminability and generalization that are rare in the iMvMLC literature. The information-theoretic disentanglement of shared and view-specific representations offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc geometric constraints, potentially transferable to other multi-view tasks. Open questions include scalability to very large view/label counts, whether the Jaccard-based label correlation graph captures higher-order dependencies, and whether the mutual information bounds remain tight in high-dimensional regimes. The NBA application demonstrates real-world viability but remains a single-domain validation.
