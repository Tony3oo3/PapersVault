## Problem

Contextual activation sparsity accelerates single-query LLM inference but fails at large batch sizes because the union of active neurons across sequences rapidly approaches dense computation, while attention layers—which become the dominant cost under batching—remain unexploited. No prior work has demonstrated that contextual sparsity can scale effectively to the large-batch, high-throughput regime that real-world serving systems require.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a batch of token sequences (B×1×d), the per-sequence KV cache (B×H×N×d_h), frozen model weights, and per-layer router logits that determine which neurons or attention heads to activate. QKV projections are kept dense to preserve KV-cache consistency across decoding steps; sparsity is applied only within the attention kernel and MLP computation. Different sequences in the same batch may activate entirely different sets of heads, producing a per-batch head index tensor that drives selective computation.

## Method

Polar Sparsity exploits the observation that MLP sparsity degrades with batch size (union activations grow) while attention head sparsity remains stable and batch-invariant. Lightweight routers—a two-layer FFN for MLP neurons and a single-layer FC for attention heads—are trained with binary cross-entropy to predict activations, then used to select top-k neurons or heads per layer via a dynamic, layer-wise threshold calibrated to 99% recall. Two custom GPU kernels realize the sparsity: a Selective GEMM kernel that fuses indexing and matrix multiplication for batched sparse MLP (up to 5.5× speedup), and a Selective FlashAttention kernel that modifies the FlashAttention algorithm to compute attention only for activated heads per batch, reducing both compute and KV-cache memory I/O (up to 2.8× speedup). Layer 0 is kept dense based on per-layer importance scores, and for GQA models the method extends to group-level sparsity.

## Results

Polar Sparsity achieves up to 2.2× end-to-end decoding throughput improvement over the dense baseline at large batch sizes (OPT-66b, batch 512, seq len 1920), with the Selective GEMM kernel delivering up to 5.5× speedup and the Selective FlashAttention kernel delivering up to 2.8× speedup at 30% head sparsity. At critical attention density thresholds (30% for OPT-66b, 50% for LLaMA-2-7b, 62.5% for LLaMA-3.1-70b), average zero-shot accuracy remains within 1% of the dense baseline across nine downstream tasks. For LLaMA-3.1-70b with GQA, Polar Sparsity still achieves a 1.51× throughput speedup despite the higher 62.5% density requirement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LLaMA-2-7b (Table 3), Polar Sparsity at 50% attention density matches or exceeds all seven compared sparsity methods on most benchmarks: it leads on COPA (0.89 vs. 0.86 GRIFFIN, 0.83 ReLUfication), PIQA (0.779, tied with ReLUfication and TEAL), Winogrande (0.687 vs. 0.686 ReLUfication, 0.675 CATS), ARC-e (0.762 vs. 0.746 R-Sparse, 0.744 CATS), and ARC-c (0.439 vs. 0.428 GRIFFIN, 0.412 CATS). The one consistent weakness is MMLU (5-shot), where Polar Sparsity scores 0.381 versus 0.455 (ProSparse) and 0.421 (CATS), which the authors attribute to MMLU's higher sensitivity to head sparsity. In throughput comparisons (Figure 6), Polar Sparsity delivers up to 2× higher decoding throughput than DejaVu-style activation sparsity at large batch sizes, because DejaVu's union neuron sparsity collapses under batching while Polar Sparsity's head sparsity remains batch-invariant. Against the dense baseline, Polar Sparsity achieves up to 2.2× throughput on OPT-66b and 1.85× on LLaMA-2-7b, with accuracy within 1% of dense on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the contextual activation sparsity line of work (Deja Vu, ShadowLLM, PowerInfer) that showed per-token neuron-level sparsity in MLP layers, and on attention head sparsity studies (MoA, MoH, TEAL) that identified head-level redundancy. It departs from both by identifying a regime shift: prior activation sparsity methods target MLP layers and are effective only at small batch sizes, while prior head sparsity work either required retraining or failed to deliver wall-clock gains due to lack of sparse attention kernels. Polar Sparsity unifies both sparsity forms under a single framework, introducing custom Selective GEMM and Selective FlashAttention kernels that make the sparsity actually executable at scale, and reframes the problem around which layer's sparsity matters as a function of batch size and sequence length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that contextual sparsity scales to the large-batch, high-throughput regime that dominates real-world LLM serving, delivering up to 2.2× end-to-end speedup with <1% accuracy loss across OPT, LLaMA-2/3, Mistral, and Qwen. The practical impact is significant: it shows that the sparsity techniques previously limited to single-query latency reduction can be repurposed for throughput-oriented deployment with minimal architectural changes. Open questions include whether dynamic per-token or per-task head selection can recover the remaining accuracy gap (especially for GQA models where group sparsity is inherently weaker), whether combining head sparsity with token sparsity yields multiplicative gains, and how the approach extends to million-token contexts, beam search, and speculative decoding where sparsity patterns may differ.
