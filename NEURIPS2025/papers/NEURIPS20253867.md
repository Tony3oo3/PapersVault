## Problem

Most contrastive attributed graph clustering (CAGC) methods rely solely on node-level embedding augmentation, ignoring edge-level embedding augmentation and the collaborative interaction between the two granularities. Additionally, they treat all contrastive sample pairs equally in the InfoNCE objective, failing to differentially handle positive-hard, positive-easy, negative-hard, and negative-easy pairs, which limits discriminative representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph G = (V, E, X) with N nodes, D-dimensional node features, and a binary adjacency matrix, and produces K cluster assignments for all nodes via K-means on the fused node-level embedding Z ∈ R^{N×d}. The embedding dimension d is set to 1500 (CORA, CITESEER, BAT, EAT) or 1000 (AMAP, UAT), and the number of clusters K matches the ground-truth class count of each dataset. Training runs for 400 epochs with a dynamic confidence factor τ that gradually decreases to expand the high-confidence sample set over time.

## Method

RAGC comprises two mutually reinforcing modules. The Hybrid-Collaborative Augmentation (HCA) module simultaneously generates node-level augmented views (via Gaussian noise + random masking → multi-order low-pass Laplacian filtering → fusion → two parameter-unshared MLPs) and edge-level augmented views (via two parameter-unshared MLPs on the adjacency matrix), then constructs a comprehensive cross-view contrastive similarity S^{l,m} = αZ^l(Z^m)^T + (1−α)E^l(E^m)^T with a learnable balance α; this similarity in turn refines the edge structure through a dynamically updated semantic correlation matrix A_aug, creating a collaborative feedback loop. The Contrastive Sample Adaptive-Differential Awareness (CSADA) module performs K-means on the fused embedding to obtain pseudo-labels, selects a dynamic high-confidence sample set H using a confidence score based on distance to cluster centers, and applies a weight modulation function W(v_i^l, v_j^m) that assigns weights ≥ 1 to high-confidence pairs: positive pairs receive e^{(1−Norm(S))^{β}} with β∈(0,1) (up-weighting hard positives), while negative pairs receive e^{(1−Norm(S))^{γ}} with γ∈[1,5] (down-weighting hard negatives), thereby differentially emphasizing all four sample-pair categories within the InfoNCE loss.

## Results

RAGC achieves the best or near-best clustering performance across all six benchmark datasets. On BAT, RAGC reaches 79.77% ACC, 55.74% NMI, 54.64% ARI, and 79.66% F1, outperforming the sub-optimal HSAN by 2.65%, 2.53%, 2.44%, and 2.53% respectively. On CORA, RAGC attains 78.74% ACC and 60.62% NMI, exceeding HSAN by 1.67% and 1.41%. The method also demonstrates superior noise robustness, with only 11.36% average performance degradation under σ_N=0.3 Gaussian noise, compared to 19.03% for DCRN and 21.36% for CCGC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAGC is compared against 13 baselines spanning generative (DAEGC, SDCN, DFCN, ARGA), classic contrastive (AGE, NCLA, CCGC, GraphLearner), and hard-sample-aware contrastive (GDCL, ProGCL, HSAN) methods. RAGC achieves the best result on 22 out of 24 dataset-metric combinations, losing only on CITE ARI (46.51 vs. HSAN's 47.05) and CITE F1 (62.49 vs. HSAN's 63.01). Against the strongest baseline HSAN, RAGC gains an average of +1.66% ACC, +1.25% NMI, +1.53% ARI, and +1.51% F1 across all six datasets, with the largest margins on BAT (+2.65% ACC) and CORA (+2.32% ARI). RAGC substantially outperforms generative methods, e.g., exceeding DFCN by an average of 17.28% ACC and 21.08% F1. Among classic CAGC methods, RAGC surpasses CCGC by 4.86% ACC on CORA and GraphLearner by 2.53% ACC on BAT. The CSADA module also demonstrates strong plug-and-play scalability, improving SCGC and HSAN when their contrastive loss is replaced with CSADA while keeping their original augmentation unchanged.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive attributed graph clustering (CAGC) lineage, building directly on SCGC's node-level augmentation pipeline (low-passing Laplacian filter followed by parameter-unshared MLPs) and the hard-sample-aware contrastive strategies of HSAN, ProGCL, and GDCL. Its two key departures are: (1) introducing edge-level embedding augmentation alongside node-level augmentation to form a hybrid-collaborative similarity metric, and (2) replacing the partial hard-sample focus of prior work with a comprehensive weight modulation function that simultaneously differentiates all four quadrants of contrastive pairs (positive-hard, positive-easy, negative-hard, negative-easy) guided by dynamic pseudo-label confidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CSADA module's demonstrated plug-and-play scalability—improving SCGC and HSAN when their contrastive objective is swapped—makes it a potentially reusable component for the broader CAGC community, not just a monolithic method. The paper's limitations are acknowledged: evaluation is confined to six relatively small static graphs (131–7,650 nodes), and the authors note that highly sparse and dynamic graphs remain unaddressed. An open question is whether the dual-level augmentation and adaptive weighting strategy generalizes to large-scale or temporal graph settings where the iterative K-means-based pseudo-label selection and dynamic confidence scheduling may become computationally prohibitive or unstable.
