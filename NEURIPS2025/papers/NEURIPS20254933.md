## Problem

Attributing Transformer behavior to internal computations requires methods that are simultaneously faithful, fine-grained, and unified across granularities. Existing approaches are either computationally expensive (exhaustive ablation or activation patching), theoretically limited (gradient-based methods), or too coarse to reach individual neurons and attention heads (approximation-based methods), and none provide a single framework that handles token-level, component-level, and subspace-level attribution without auxiliary training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254933/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a pretrained Transformer and an input sequence, DePass produces a tensor of decomposed hidden states (N × M × D) at every layer, where M is the number of additive components defined by the chosen granularity (e.g., N for token-wise, H+1 for head-wise, N_MLP+1 for neuron-wise, or 2 for subspace-wise). These decomposed states sum exactly to the original hidden state at every position and layer, and can be projected onto the LM head for output attribution, onto specific component indices for importance scores, or onto arbitrary linear directions for subspace attribution. No auxiliary training or model modification is required.

## Method

DePass decomposes hidden states into additive components at a chosen layer, then propagates them through all subsequent layers with attention scores and MLP activations frozen. In the attention sublayer, each component is independently multiplied by the fixed attention matrix and value-output projection, preserving the residual connection per component. In the MLP sublayer, a softmax-based allocation (Eq. 14) distributes each neuron's fixed activation across components proportionally to their pre-activation relevance, ensuring the sum of component outputs equals the original MLP output. This design eliminates second-order cross-component interactions (guaranteeing faithfulness) while maintaining exact additive reconstruction (guaranteeing completeness). The same propagation machinery applies uniformly whether components correspond to input tokens, attention heads, MLP neurons, or subspace projections, making DePass a single unified framework for multi-granularity attribution.

## Results

On the subspace attribution task (Table 1), DePass-based masking consistently outperforms TACS across all six model-dataset combinations. For example, on Llama-2-7b-chat-hf with CounterFact, accuracy rises from 10.16% (misinformation only) to 43.13% with DePass masking, versus 25.68% with TACS; on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct the gap widens to 59.16% vs. 16.03%. On token-level output attribution (Figure 1), DePass achieves substantially higher comprehensiveness than all gradient-based and attention-based baselines on both Known_1000 and IOI with Llama-2-13b-chat-hf, while sufficiency scores are comparable across methods. For component-wise attribution (Figure 3), DePass produces a sharper accuracy drop under Top-k masking and better retention under Bottom-k masking than AtP, Norm, and Coef baselines on Llama-2-7b-chat-hf.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For token-level output attribution, DePass substantially outperforms gradient-based methods (Input × Gradient, Integrated Gradients, Gradient SHAP) and attention-based methods (Mean Attention, Last-layer Attention, Attention Rollout) on comprehensiveness across both Known_1000 and IOI, while sufficiency scores are comparable with attention-based methods performing slightly better. On subspace-level attribution, DePass consistently beats TACS (the only direct baseline) on 10 out of 12 model-dataset settings in Table 1, with the largest gains on CounterFact generation (e.g., +38.3 pp over TACS on Llama-3.1-8B). For component-wise attribution, DePass outperforms AtP (Activation × Gradient), Norm (ℓ₂ norm), and Coef (absolute MLP activations) on both Top-k masking (sharper accuracy drop) and Bottom-k masking (better retention) on IOI and CounterFact. The one setting where DePass does not clearly win is Qwen2-7B-Instruct on TruthfulQA, where TACS (68.42%) slightly edges out DePass (64.87%), suggesting the advantage is most pronounced on smaller models and generation-style tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DePass generalizes the token-decomposition idea from DecompX (Modarressi et al., ACL 2023) to arbitrary Transformer modules in generative models, adding finer-grained decomposition of MLP activations via a softmax-based apportioning rule. It departs from gradient-based attribution (Integrated Gradients, Gradient SHAP) and ablation-based methods (activation patching, noise ablation) by performing a single forward pass with frozen attention scores and MLP activations, guaranteeing lossless additive reconstruction without auxiliary training. The paper also positions itself as complementary to Sparse Dictionary Learning (SAE, Transcoder, CrossCoder), which identifies interpretable features but cannot trace which input tokens activate them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DePass offers a unified, training-free attribution framework that operates consistently across token, component (attention head / MLP neuron), and subspace granularities, filling a gap where prior methods were task-specific or required expensive repeated ablations. The demonstrated two-orders-of-magnitude speedup over ablation-based neuron attribution and the clean language/semantic subspace separation in multilingual models suggest practical utility for mechanistic interpretability at scale. Open questions include how the frozen-attention approximation behaves under strong non-linear interactions in very deep models, whether the softmax apportioning rule for MLP neurons is theoretically justified beyond empirical validation, and how DePass integrates with circuit-discovery pipelines for end-to-end interpretability.
