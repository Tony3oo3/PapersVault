## Problem

Existing video understanding methods based on large vision-language models rely on single-pass reasoning without dynamic feedback, limiting self-correction in complex multi-step scenarios. Prior attempts to add reward signals either depend on expensive human annotations, use static reward templates that cannot capture real-time reasoning states, or employ inefficient multi-agent debate frameworks that lack reward generation during inference. No prior framework simultaneously provides real-time reward feedback for iterative answer refinement and automatic high-quality data selection for downstream training (SFT, DPO, GRPO).

## Input / Output

The framework takes a video (processed into frames), a natural-language query, and a configurable toolset (OCR, ASR, Grounding DINO, CLIP, Scene Graph, Action Detector, Captioning Model, etc.) as input. It produces a refined final answer, a structured evaluation report containing a scalar reward score (0–10) and per-dimension feedback (visual alignment, temporal accuracy, option disambiguation, reasoning specificity, linguistic precision), per-perspective confidence scores, and the selected key-frame set. The evaluation report doubles as a data-importance signal: samples with scores below 5 are retained as challenging training instances for GRPO fine-tuning.

## Method

ReAgent-V is a three-stage agentic pipeline. Stage 1 selects task-relevant frames via an Entropy-Calibrated Relevance Score (ECRS) that multiplies CLIP cosine similarity by per-frame pixel-level information entropy, applied iteratively with an exponentially increasing threshold to suppress redundant frames. Stage 2 has a target agent dynamically invoke a subset of tools from a tool factory on the selected frames and query to produce an initial answer. Stage 3 introduces a critic agent that, if the initial answer is unsatisfactory, generates targeted sub-questions, invokes additional tools, and produces a structured evaluation report serving as a real-time process reward; the target agent then revises its answer from three perspectives—conservative (final answer only), neutral (entity/scene grounding), and aggressive (full reasoning chain)—each yielding a revised answer and confidence score, with the final answer aggregated from all three if all confidences exceed 0.6 or taken from the highest-confidence perspective otherwise. The same evaluation reports generated during inference are reused to filter high-quality samples for SFT, DPO, and GRPO training, closing the loop between inference-time reasoning and model improvement.

## Results

On video understanding (Table 1), ReAgent-V achieves up to 6.9% average improvement over the base model (LLaVA-Video-72B: 73.5 vs 67.1 on VideoMME overall; Qwen2.5-VL-72B: 75.1 vs 74.1). On video LLM reasoning (Table 2), GRPO+ReAgent-V outperforms vanilla GRPO by 2.1% relative average while using only 45% of the training data (52k vs 116k samples), and surpasses full-dataset SFT by 4.3%. On VLA alignment (Figure 3), ReAgent-V as a reward model yields a 9.8% overall improvement over the second-best baseline (GRAPE) in the SIMPLER environment, with 9.0% gain on the original in-domain task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models, LLaVA-Video-72B+ReAgent-V (73.5 on VideoMME) surpasses GPT-4o (71.9) while using only 38 frames versus GPT-4o's 384, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B+ReAgent-V (75.1) marginally edges out Gemini-1.5-Pro (75.0). Among open-source agent frameworks, ReAgent-V outperforms VideoAgent (56.0) and VideoMemAgent (57.4) on VideoMME overall while using fewer frames (34–38 vs 72–87) and substantially lower inference time (e.g., 28.4s vs 143.7s per sample on VideoMME for 7B models). In the video LLM reasoning setting, GRPO+ReAgent-V (54.2 on VideoMME, 63.0 on MMVU) beats vanilla GRPO (53.8, 60.6) and full-dataset SFT (52.8, 61.3) across all six reasoning benchmarks. For VLA alignment, ReAgent-V as a reward model achieves 9.8% overall success-rate improvement over GRAPE (template-based reward) and outperforms OpenVLA-SFT and OpenVLA-DPO baselines across in-domain, subject, physical, and semantic generalization tasks in SIMPLER.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReAgent-V sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) single-pass LVLM video understanding (LLaVA-Video, Qwen2.5-VL), which it extends with iterative tool-augmented reasoning; (2) offline reward-model-based RL for video reasoning (Video-R1, GRPO, DPO), which it departs from by generating real-time, inference-aware reward signals via a critic agent rather than relying on pretrained or template-based rewards; and (3) multi-agent video frameworks (VideoAgent, VideoMemAgent), which it differentiates from through its entropy-calibrated frame selection and the novel three-perspective (conservative/neutral/aggressive) reflection mechanism. For the VLA alignment application, it directly builds on GRAPE's TPO paradigm [57], replacing the static template reward with ReAgent-V's dynamic evaluation to produce preference pairs for OpenVLA-7B.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's main practical value is its modularity: the same pipeline can serve as an inference-time enhancer for any LVLM, a data-selection filter for RL fine-tuning, and a reward model for VLA alignment, reducing the need for expensive human annotation. The finding that conservative reflection (only changing the final answer) outperforms aggressive reflection (revising reasoning steps) is a useful counterintuitive insight for designing self-correction loops. Open questions include whether the multi-agent reflection overhead scales to very long videos or real-time robotic deployment, whether the entropy-calibrated frame selection generalizes beyond CLIP-based embeddings, and whether the VLA alignment gains (measured on only 80 trajectories in SIMPLER) transfer to more complex or longer-horizon manipulation tasks.
