## Problem

Existing cross-dye virtual staining methods (e.g., HE-to-IHC) cannot control the staining tone in the target domain, producing unpredictable appearance variations. When tone conditioning is naively introduced via a target-domain image, the model confuses tone and pathology signals, causing 'pathology leakage' where the pathological status of the conditioning image incorrectly transfers to the output. Additionally, diffusion-based staining of billion-pixel whole slide images suffers from mosaic artifacts at patch boundaries due to independent patch processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250108/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two inputs: a source-domain histopathology image (e.g., HE-stained) that provides pathological structure, and a target-domain tone-conditioning image (e.g., IHC-stained) from the same WSI but a non-overlapping patch that provides only tone style. The model operates at 512×512 pixel resolution per patch but extends to billion-pixel WSIs (up to 40,000×32,000) via the frequency-aware adaptive patch sampling strategy, producing a cross-dye virtually stained image that matches the conditioning tone while preserving the source pathology.

## Method

D-VST builds on DiT-XL/2 (PixArt-α) with two conditioning branches: a lightweight 4-layer convolutional pathology encoder whose embeddings are channel-concatenated with the noise latent, and a pretrained CLIP ViT tone encoder whose embeddings are injected via multi-head tone attention modules inserted into the Transformer blocks. A two-stage curriculum learning scheme decouples the conditions: Stage 1 trains only pathology extraction (ignoring hue differences), then Stage 2 introduces tone conditioning with Gaussian blur on the conditioning image and 20% random dropout of the tone embedding to suppress pathology leakage. For WSI inference, a frequency-aware adaptive patch sampling strategy computes FFT-based local frequency statistics over an n×n grid and preferentially samples additional patches from low-frequency regions (where mosaic artifacts are most pronounced), achieving quality comparable to MultiDiffusion at only 1/8 the computational cost (β=8 vs. β=64).

## Results

D-VST achieves the best performance on all three perception-oriented metrics (DISTS, FID, KID) across all three virtual staining tasks. On HE2IHC, D-VST attains DISTS 0.154, FID 33.16, KID 0.0055, PSNR 18.11, SSIM 0.407; on HE2mIHC, DISTS 0.106, FID 20.36, KID 0.0016, PSNR 28.01, SSIM 0.861; and on FFPE2HE, DISTS 0.090, FID 14.26, KID 0.0005, PSNR 17.98, SSIM 0.538. On the downstream HER2 classification task, D-VST achieves 94.17% accuracy and 0.9430 F1, closely matching real IHC images (95.00% ACC) and substantially outperforming all baselines. For zero-shot large-image staining, D-VST's frequency-aware adaptive sampling (β=8) matches or exceeds MultiDiffusion (β=64) quality at only 1/8 the computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-VST outperforms all GAN-based baselines (Pix2pix, Pix2pixHD, CycleGAN) by a wide margin on every metric across all three tasks; for example, on HE2mIHC, D-VST achieves FID 20.36 versus 28.92–83.16 for Pix2pixHD and CycleGAN. Against the task-specific SOTA methods, D-VST beats Peng et al. [61] on HE2IHC (FID 33.16 vs. 33.92, DISTS 0.154 vs. 0.174) and Hu et al. [34] on FFPE2HE (FID 14.26 vs. 31.56, DISTS 0.090 vs. 0.126), though it trails [34] on PSNR (17.98 vs. 19.86) and SSIM (0.538 vs. 0.644) due to imperfect pixel-level registration in that dataset. Among diffusion-based baselines, D-VST substantially surpasses StainFuser (FID 33.16 vs. 104.5 on HE2IHC) and SynDiff (FID 33.16 vs. 225.3), which suffer from poor structural fidelity. For the zero-shot large-image sampling comparison, D-VST's frequency-aware adaptive strategy (β=8) achieves FID 33.16 on HE2IHC, matching MultiDiffusion (β=64, FID 34.66) at 1/8 the compute cost, while SpotDiffusion (β=1) degrades severely (FID 93.35). On the downstream HER2 classification task, D-VST reaches 94.17% accuracy versus 89.17% for Peng et al. [61], 87.50% for Pix2pix, and 72.50% for StainFuser, approaching the 95.00% of real IHC images.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-VST builds on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture from PixArt-α as its denoising backbone and repurposes the pretrained CLIP ViT as a tone encoder, extending the diffusion-based virtual staining line (StainFuser, StainDiff, SynDiff) that previously addressed only same-dye normalization or uncontrolled cross-dye translation. It departs from MultiDiffusion's uniform sliding-window strategy for high-resolution generation by introducing a frequency-aware adaptive patch sampling scheme tailored to the heterogeneous information density of whole slide images. The two-stage curriculum learning scheme for decoupling pathology and tone conditions is a novel training strategy not seen in prior virtual staining or controllable diffusion work, addressing the 'pathology leakage' problem that arises when tone-conditioning images carry confounding pathological structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

D-VST is the first method to achieve tone-controllable cross-dye virtual staining without pathology leakage, validated by a downstream HER2 classification task where virtually stained images reach 94.17% accuracy versus 95.00% for real IHC images—nearly indistinguishable for clinical decision-making. The frequency-aware sampling strategy reduces WSI inference cost to 1/8 of MultiDiffusion while matching or exceeding quality, making billion-pixel virtual staining practically feasible. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to unpaired data (currently requiring paired cross-stain sections), whether faster samplers (flow rectification, consistency models) can overcome the multi-step denoising bottleneck, and whether pathology-specific VAEs would further improve fidelity.
