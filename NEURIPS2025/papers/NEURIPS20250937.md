## Problem

Generative-model-based visuomotor policies (diffusion or flow matching) require multi-step iterative sampling to produce actions, creating a latency bottleneck for real-time robotic control. Existing one-step acceleration methods (consistency distillation, distribution matching) were adapted from image generation, where samples are temporally independent, but robotic manipulation demands continuous, temporally coherent action trajectories. The paper identifies that the temporal structure inherent in action chunks has been underexploited as a regularizer for enabling reliable one-step generation.

## Input / Output

The policy accepts either 2D RGB images (encoded via ResNet-18) or 3D point clouds (encoded via a lightweight MLP), combined with proprioceptive state, and optionally a language instruction in VLA settings. It outputs a single action chunk of H timesteps (e.g., H=16 in simulation, H=48 in real-world) from which the first portion is executed. Crucially, the action chunk is treated as a 1D temporal signal along its time axis, and the DCT is applied along this axis to extract frequency components for the consistency loss.

## Method

FreqPolicy builds on rectified flow matching with a 1D CNN U-Net backbone that predicts velocity vectors mapping Gaussian noise to expert action chunks. The key innovation is a frequency consistency constraint: instead of enforcing velocity consistency in the spatial domain (as in Consistency-FM), the model projects velocity vectors at two arbitrary timesteps into the frequency domain via type-II DCT and minimizes the ℓ₂ distance between their spectral coefficients, promoting straighter flows suitable for one-step inference. An adaptive frequency component loss, inspired by Focal Loss, dynamically reweights each DCT band by the exponential of its inter-timestep discrepancy, so the model emphasizes frequency components that carry informative dynamic variation (e.g., high-frequency gripper motion during grasping) while downweighting smooth, low-information bands. The total training objective jointly optimizes the standard flow matching loss and the frequency consistency loss, requiring no teacher model. At inference, a single forward pass (NFE=1) suffices, achieving 93.5 Hz on real hardware.

## Results

On 53 3D manipulation tasks (Adroit + MetaWorld), FreqPolicy achieves 78.5% average success rate with a single NFE, outperforming the best one-step baseline FlowPolicy (77.2%) and the distillation-based SDM (74.8%). On the LIBERO VLA benchmark (40 tasks), OpenVLA-FreqPolicy reaches 94.8% average success at 6.05 Hz, surpassing OpenVLA-DP (68.1% at 0.32 Hz) and OpenVLA-FlowMatching with 10 NFE (93.7% at 1.26 Hz). In real-world experiments, FreqPolicy achieves 70% success on the Fruit Sorting task at 93.5 Hz inference, compared to 55% at 19.8 Hz for multi-step Diffusion Policy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqPolicy outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary 3D benchmark (Table 2): it leads FlowPolicy [78] (the closest one-step flow-based competitor using spatial-domain consistency) by 1.3% average (78.5% vs. 77.2%), with the gap widening on Hard (+3.0%) and Very Hard (+3.6%) splits. Against distillation-based methods, it surpasses SDM [28] by 3.7% and ManiCM [42] by 9.5%, while eliminating the need for a pretrained teacher. On Robomimic (2D, Table 1), FreqPolicy beats Consistency-FM [68] by 6% on Transport and 5% on Tool Hang, and outperforms IMLE Policy [57] on Transport (90% vs. 90% tie) and Tool Hang (85% vs. 81%). In the VLA setting (Table 3/7), OpenVLA-FreqPolicy (94.8%) edges out OpenVLA-OFT [32] (94.2%) in average success while matching its inference speed (6.05 vs. 6.15 Hz), and dramatically outperforms OpenVLA-DP [32] (68.1%) especially on long-horizon tasks (87.6% vs. 11.8%). In real-world experiments, FreqPolicy matches or exceeds multi-step Diffusion Policy [11] and Flow Matching Policy [59] success rates while running 4–5× faster (93.5 Hz vs. ~20 Hz).

## Relation to Prior Work

FreqPolicy builds directly on the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., Liu et al.) and the velocity-consistency structure of Consistency Flow Matching (Yang et al. 2024), which enforces straight flows by aligning velocities at different timesteps. The key conceptual departure is moving the consistency constraint from the spatial domain (as in FlowPolicy [78]) to the frequency domain via DCT, motivated by the observation that action chunks in robotic manipulation are temporal signals with structured, non-stationary frequency content. This borrows a perspective from time-series forecasting and speech processing that had not previously been applied to visuomotor policy acceleration, distinguishing it from prior one-step methods (Consistency Policy, ManiCM, SDM, OneDP) that adapted image-generation distillation techniques without exploiting temporal structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-step action generation at 93.5 Hz is practically critical for real-time robotic control and edge deployment, and FreqPolicy demonstrates that frequency-domain consistency can match or exceed multi-step policies without requiring a pretrained teacher model. The adaptive frequency component loss addresses a real structural property of manipulation (alternating low- and high-dynamic phases), though the paper acknowledges the method requires twice the forward passes of a standard flow matching step and has only been validated on flow matching, not diffusion-based policies. Open questions include whether the frequency consistency idea transfers to diffusion policies, whether the computational overhead can be reduced, and whether the modest gains over spatial-domain consistency (FlowPolicy) persist at larger scale or on more diverse robot embodiments.
