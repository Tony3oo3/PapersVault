## Problem

Vision-language models consume exponentially growing visual tokens (e.g., 2,678 tokens for a single smartphone photo in Qwen2.5-VL), causing prohibitive memory and latency for real-world deployment. Existing efficient VLM methods apply fixed pruning ratios or attention-score thresholds uniformly across all samples, yet the paper observes that most general VQA tasks tolerate 75% token reduction with minimal loss while OCR-dependent tasks suffer severe degradation. The missing capability is a per-sample, content-aware decision of whether high-resolution visual input is actually needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253182/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a 1/4-resolution downsampled image alongside a text question in a single prompt. If the downsampled image contains insufficient information, the model emits a structured tool-call token (a JSON-formatted resize_image function call within special XML tags) to request the full-resolution image, after which it continues reasoning in a second turn. The final output is a text answer wrapped in <answer></answer> tags, preceded by a reasoning chain in <think> tags, with the entire interaction limited to at most two turns (one low-res pass and optionally one high-res pass).

## Method

VisionThink extends GRPO to a multi-turn setting where the policy can emit a tool-call token to request a higher-resolution image, masking non-generated tokens from the loss. To enable RL on open-ended general VQA (where rule-based answer matching fails), the paper introduces an LLM-as-Judge strategy: an external LLM compares the model's text answer against the ground truth and returns a binary 0/1 correctness score, avoiding visual biases. The reward function combines accuracy (0 or 1), format compliance (0.5 for correct think/answer/tool-call tags), and a threshold-based penalty that discourages the model from collapsing into always requesting or never requesting high resolution. Training data is curated by running 8 rollouts at temperature 1 on candidate samples and classifying them as high-resolution-required or low-resolution-sufficient based on accuracy gaps, yielding 10K samples of each type for RL training on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

## Results

VisionThink autonomously retains ~51.3% of visual tokens on average (vs. fixed 50% or 70% for prior methods) while achieving 101.4% of vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-7B performance across nine benchmarks. On OCR-critical tasks, it matches or exceeds the full-resolution baseline (ChartQA 79.8 vs. 79.8; OCRBench 80.8 vs. 81.5) while outperforming FastV (72.6) and SparseVLM (73.2) at 50% retention by 9.0% and 8.3% respectively. On general VQA, VisionThink surpasses the base model on MME (2400 vs. 2316), MMVet (68.5 vs. 61.6), and MathVerse (48.0 vs. 46.3), and on DocVQA it is more than 2× faster than the full-resolution reasoning model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, VisionThink improves MME (2400 vs. 2316), MMVet (68.5 vs. 61.6), and MathVerse (48.0 vs. 46.3) while using only ~51% of the visual tokens, and slightly trails on OCRBench (80.8 vs. 81.5) and RealWorldQA (67.1 vs. 68.6). Versus efficient-VLM baselines at 50% retention, VisionThink outperforms FastV by 9.0% on ChartQA (79.8 vs. 72.6) and 8.3% on OCRBench (80.8 vs. 75.8), and beats SparseVLM by 11.7% on ChartQA and 15.2% on DocVQA (94.4 vs. 66.8). Even at 70% retention, FastV (77.2) and SparseVLM (75.8) remain well below VisionThink on ChartQA, confirming that fixed-ratio pruning cannot match per-sample adaptive decisions. Among closed-source models, VisionThink surpasses GPT-4o (69.1) and Claude-3.5 Sonnet (68.3) on MME (2400) and is competitive on MathVista (67.5 vs. 63.8/67.7). Among open-source 7–8B models, VisionThink leads InternVL2.5-8B on MathVerse (48.0 vs. 39.5) and MMVet (68.5 vs. 62.8), and outperforms all reasoning models (LLaVA-CoT, Insight-V, Mulberry, Vision-R1) on MME and MathVista. On DocVQA, VisionThink is >2× faster than the full-resolution QwenRL model while maintaining 94.4 vs. 95.1 accuracy, and on MME/POPE it is ~1/3 faster than the full-resolution baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) to a multi-turn formulation to handle the iterative image-resize interaction, and adapts the penalty mechanism from Search-R1 to control the resolution-request ratio. It departs fundamentally from prior Efficient VLM methods (FastV, SparseVLM, VisionZip) that process full-resolution images and prune tokens via fixed attention-score thresholds; instead, VisionThink inverts the pipeline by starting with downsampled tokens and letting the model autonomously request higher resolution. It also extends the line of RL-based VLM reasoning (Vision-R1, LLaVA-O1, Mulberry), which has been confined to visual math, to general VQA via the LLM-as-Judge reward strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LLM-as-Judge strategy is a practical unlock for applying RL to open-ended general VQA, a setting where rule-based rewards fail, and the 130K-sample scaling experiment provides evidence of broad generalization. The dynamic-resolution paradigm is complementary to (not competitive with) token-level pruning methods, suggesting a layered efficiency stack for deployment. Key limitations include the restriction to 2× upscaling and at most two interaction turns, the use of simple image resizing rather than learned token reduction, and evaluation on a single 7B base model; extending to flexible multi-level resolution, cropping tools, and longer multi-turn chains are natural next steps.
