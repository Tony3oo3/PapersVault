## Problem

Despite the empirical success of generative model inversion attacks (MIAs) that use GANs to reconstruct private training data, the mechanism by which private information is actually extracted during inversion remained unexplained. This paper identifies that inversion-time loss gradients are highly noisy and that the GAN generator implicitly denoises them via tangent-space projection, then hypothesizes that gradient-manifold alignment is a previously unrecognized factor governing a model's vulnerability to inversion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252375/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes a white-box target classifier (e.g., ResNet-18, DenseNet-121, VGG16), a target class label, and a pre-trained GAN generator (StyleGAN or DCGAN) trained on a public auxiliary dataset with disjoint labels. Optimization is performed in the GAN's low-dimensional latent space (k ≪ d), and the output is a reconstructed face image at 64×64 or 224×224 resolution. For the alignment-aware training variant, a pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE decoder provides Jacobian-based tangent space estimates at each training point, requiring ~30 GB of storage for 2,700 images at 64×64.

## Method

The paper's core insight is a geometric decomposition: backpropagating the inversion loss through the GAN generator applies the Jacobian J_G, which projects ambient loss gradients onto the generator manifold's tangent space, filtering off-manifold noise while preserving semantically meaningful directions. To validate that stronger alignment increases vulnerability, the authors propose an alignment-aware training objective (Eq. 7) that augments cross-entropy with a term encouraging input gradients to align with the VAE-estimated data manifold tangent space, using an upper-bound relaxation to reduce per-sample projection cost from C to 1. For practical attack enhancement without retraining, AlignMI introduces two training-free gradient-smoothing strategies: Perturbation-Averaged Alignment (PAA), which averages loss gradients over Gaussian-perturbed copies of the synthetic input, and Transformation-Averaged Alignment (TAA), which averages over semantically invariant augmentations (cropping, flipping, rotation). Both are plug-in modifications to any generative MIA pipeline, with TAA outperforming PAA because it preserves input realism and avoids the confidence drop caused by noise.

## Results

In the high-resolution setting (224×224), integrating TAA into PPA yields the largest gains: on FaceScrub, ResNeSt-50 improves from 71.58% to 84.38% Acc@1 (+12.80), and DenseNet-121 from 75.66% to 86.54% (+10.88). On CelebA, ResNeSt-50 improves from 70.75% to 79.19% Acc@1 (+8.44). KNN distance decreases consistently (e.g., ResNeSt-50 on FaceScrub: 0.827 → 0.753), confirming closer reconstructions to true training samples. PAA provides smaller but still consistent improvements (e.g., +4.96 Acc@1 for ResNeSt-50 on CelebA), while TAA outperforms PAA in nearly all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's core comparison is PPA (Struppek et al., 2022) as the base attack method, into which PAA and TAA are plugged as training-free enhancements; TAA consistently outperforms PPA by 3–13 points in Acc@1 across all three target models and both datasets, with the largest gap on FaceScrub (e.g., +12.80 for ResNeSt-50). In the low-resolution setting, the same plug-in strategy is applied to KEDMI (Chen et al., 2021) and PLG-MI (Yuan et al., 2023), yielding consistent improvements over their unmodified versions (reported in Appendix E.2). Against MIA defenses—BiDO (Peng et al., 2022), NegLS (Struppek et al., 2024), and TL-DMI (Ho et al., 2024)—the paper evaluates whether AlignMI can still improve inversion success under these protections, showing that the alignment-enhanced gradients remain effective even when defenses are in place. The hypothesis-validation experiments (Table 1) further show that alignment-aware training increases MIA vulnerability (Acc@1 rises from 77.92% for the vanilla model to 80.76% for Model B) despite a drop in test accuracy (96.53% → 93.75%), confirming that gradient-manifold alignment is an independent vulnerability axis beyond predictive power.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the generative model inversion framework of Zhang et al. (CVPR 2020), which constrains inversion optimization to a GAN's latent space, and on PPA (Struppek et al., ICML 2022) as the primary high-resolution attack baseline. Its key departure is a geometric reinterpretation: rather than treating the generator as merely a prior that constrains the search space, the paper shows via the manifold hypothesis that backpropagation through the generator Jacobian implicitly projects loss gradients onto the tangent space, acting as a denoising filter. The training-free AlignMI method (PAA/TAA) adapts the gradient-averaging idea from SmoothGrad (Smilkov et al., 2017) but motivates it through this manifold-alignment lens rather than through saliency-map smoothing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies gradient-manifold alignment as a previously unexplored dimension of MIA vulnerability, complementing the well-known role of predictive accuracy, and provides a practical training-free plug-in (AlignMI) that consistently improves SOTA generative MIAs by 3–12% in Acc@1. However, the hypothesis validation is restricted to low-resolution (64×64) settings due to the prohibitive cost of computing VAE decoder Jacobians at 224×224, and the observed alignment–accuracy trade-off remains unexplained. Open questions include whether reducing gradient-manifold alignment can serve as a principled defense, and whether the non-monotonic vulnerability curve (peaking at intermediate alignment) reflects a fundamental architectural limitation or an artifact of the specific training protocol.
