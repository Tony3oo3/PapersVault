## Problem

Spectral initialization methods like PiSSA improve LoRA convergence by leveraging SVD components of pretrained weights, but they introduce costly matrix decomposition and storage overhead that undermines LoRA's efficiency advantage. Moreover, the reason spectral initialization works remains poorly understood—the common 'knowledge preservation' justification lacks theoretical grounding. This paper identifies weight update magnitude as the fundamental driver of LoRA performance and shows that spectral methods succeed primarily through magnitude amplification rather than feature preservation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained weight matrix W ∈ R^{n×m} and a target rank r, requiring only the scalar magnitude ν[W] = ‖W‖²_F/(mn) and the matrix dimensions—no SVD or singular vectors are needed. It outputs initialized LoRA parameters A^(0) = β·Φ_m^T ∈ R^{r×m} and B^(0) = β·Φ_n ∈ R^{n×r} constructed from deterministic Discrete Sine Transform (DST) orthogonal bases, along with a modified pretrained weight W' = W − β²Φ_nΦ_m^T that absorbs the initialization term. The DST basis is computed analytically via a closed-form sine formula, ensuring reproducibility across devices with zero additional storage beyond the standard LoRA adapter parameters.

## Method

The paper establishes a magnitude principle proving that low-rank structure intrinsically bounds LoRA update magnitudes (Proposition 2), showing the linear growth rate k₁ = r(mσ_A⁴ + nσ_B⁴) is significantly smaller than full fine-tuning, and unifying learning rate, scaling factor, and initialization as equivalent magnitude-regulation mechanisms (Proposition 1). It then demystifies PiSSA by deriving the spectral gain factor Q[r] = ρ[r]·r/R[W] that quantifies how top-r singular value concentration amplifies update magnitude, demonstrating this—not knowledge preservation—drives spectral initialization's effectiveness. LoRAM replaces SVD-derived bases with deterministic DST orthogonal matrices scaled by β = (Q[r]·ν[W]/ν[Φ_nΦ_m^T])^{1/4}, where Q[r] ≈ log_{min(n,m)}(r) approximates the concave spectral gain via a logarithmic fit. This 'Basis & Basis' scheme achieves the same magnitude dynamics as PiSSA while maintaining O(mr+nr) time and O(r(m+n)) space complexity identical to vanilla LoRA, eliminating all SVD computation and residual storage overhead.

## Results

On NLG tasks with LLaMA-2-7B (rank 16), LoRAM achieves 40.32% on GSM8K, 5.30% on MATH, 18.92% on HumanEval, 28.83% on MBPP, and 75.19% on Commonsense, outperforming PiSSA (37.68/5.16/18.37/28.62/73.72) and naive LoRA (31.51/4.16/15.98/28.65/66.56) across all five benchmarks. At rank 128, LoRAM reaches 51.12% GSM8K and 22.03% HumanEval, matching or exceeding PiSSA (51.48/21.62). On GLUE (DeBERTa-v3-base, rank 8), LoRAM attains the best scores on 5 of 8 tasks (e.g., MRPC 89.95, QQP 91.70, RTE 74.72, STS-B 89.93), and on LLaVA multimodal benchmarks it leads on 6 of 8 tasks (e.g., MMMU 0.350, ChartQA 0.238, TextVQA 0.551, ScienceQA 0.700).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRAM consistently outperforms naive LoRA across all tasks and both rank settings (e.g., +8.81 pts on GSM8K at r=16, +10.85 at r=128), confirming that the magnitude-driven initialization provides substantial gains over the default 'Noise & Zeros' scheme. Against the strongest spectral baseline PiSSA, LoRAM wins on 4 of 5 NLG tasks at rank 16 (GSM8K +2.64, MATH +0.14, HumanEval +0.55, Commonsense +1.47) and on 3 of 5 at rank 128 (MATH +0.21, HumanEval +0.41, MBPP +0.46), while PiSSA narrowly edges LoRAM on GSM8K at r=128 (51.48 vs 51.12). LoRAM also surpasses RsLoRA (which scales α=√r) on GSM8K and MATH at both ranks, and outperforms LoRA+ (which uses asymmetric learning rates) on all five NLG tasks at rank 16. MiLoRA (minor singular components) and OLoRA (QR-based orthogonal init) are clearly dominated by LoRAM on every benchmark. On GLUE, LoRAM achieves the best score on 5/8 tasks (MRPC, QQP, RTE, STS-B, QNLI) while PiSSA leads on MNLI and SST-2 by <0.1 pts; on LLaVA multimodal tasks, LoRAM wins on 6/8 benchmarks (MMMU, AI2D, ChartQA, TextVQA, ScienceQA, MME-Cog within 3 pts of PiSSA). Notably, LoRAM achieves these gains without any SVD computation, matching PiSSA's performance while reducing initialization time from 51 s to <1 s (r=16) and eliminating the 12.5 GB storage overhead of PiSSA's pre-processing workflow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) and the wave of spectral initialization methods that followed, particularly PiSSA (Meng et al., NeurIPS 2024) and MiLoRA (Wang et al., NAACL 2025), which use SVD of pretrained weights to initialize low-rank adapters. The key conceptual departure is reframing spectral initialization's success: rather than attributing gains to preserving principal components or 'knowledge,' the paper proves the benefit is primarily magnitude amplification of weight updates, unifying it with hyperparameter tuning strategies like RsLoRA (Kalajdzievski, 2023) and LoRA+ (Hayou et al., ICML 2024) under a single magnitude principle. LoRAM then replaces SVD-derived bases with deterministic Discrete Sine Transform (DST) matrices scaled by a logarithmic factor, eliminating decomposition overhead while matching spectral performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The magnitude principle provides a unified theoretical lens that connects previously independent hyperparameter choices (learning rate, scaling factor, initialization) into a coherent framework, giving practitioners a principled basis for tuning LoRA rather than relying on ad hoc heuristics. Practically, LoRAM eliminates the SVD computation and storage overhead of PiSSA while matching or slightly exceeding its performance, making spectral-level initialization accessible in resource-constrained and production settings. Open questions remain: whether layer-specific magnitude optimization could yield further gains, whether the principle extends to non-linear or higher-rank adaptations, and whether the logarithmic approximation of Q[r] is truly optimal or merely a convenient surrogate. The paper explicitly notes it does not address full optimization dynamics or convergence guarantees.
