## Problem

Existing long-tailed semi-supervised learning methods assume the unlabeled data follow a predefined distribution (long-tailed, uniform, or inverse long-tailed), but in practice the unlabeled distribution is unknown and arbitrary. Under this realistic setting, confidence-threshold-based pseudo-labeling becomes unreliable, as high-confidence predictions still contain substantial errors that propagate through training and cause confirmation bias and performance degradation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled image dataset with a known long-tailed class distribution and an unlabeled image dataset with an unknown arbitrary distribution, both sharing the same feature and label spaces. For each unlabeled sample, the method generates weak and strong augmentation views to derive pseudo-labels and confidence scores. The output is a trained encoder-classifier pair that maps unseen RGB images to class labels, evaluated on standard benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Food-101, SVHN) at their native resolutions with imbalance ratios ranging from 10 to 200.

## Method

CPG operates through a controllable self-reinforcing optimization cycle: a dynamic controllable filtering mechanism selects reliable pseudo-labels by requiring both weak and strong augmentation views to exceed a confidence threshold and agree on the predicted class, with a voting strategy ensuring label consistency across training steps. These reliable pseudo-labels are merged into the labeled dataset to form an updated dataset with a known class distribution, on which a Bayes-optimal classifier is constructed via logit adjustment (LA loss). The improved classifier then identifies more reliable pseudo-labels in the next step, creating a self-reinforcing loop that progressively approximates the true unlabeled distribution without ever explicitly estimating it. Two auxiliary components enhance performance: a class-aware adaptive augmentation module that synthesizes minority-class representations with a radius inversely proportional to class compactness, and an auxiliary branch applying consistency regularization across all labeled and unlabeled samples to maximize data utilization during early training. The paper further provides a theoretical generalization error bound (Theorem 1) showing the cycle reduces empirical risk as the number of training samples grows and the pseudo-labeling error rate decreases.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (N_max=400, M_max=4600), CPG achieves an average accuracy of 76.41% across all imbalance ratios (γ∈{100,150,200}) and unlabeled distributions, outperforming the best SSL baseline SoftMatch (65.27%) by 11.14 pp and the best ReaLTSSL baseline SimPro (61.68%) by 14.73 pp. The headline gain of 15.97 pp is achieved at γ=100 with arbitrary unlabeled distribution (82.10% vs. SoftMatch's 66.13%). On CIFAR-100-LT, CPG reaches 47.25% average accuracy (vs. 43.82% for SoftMatch), and on SVHN-LT it attains 94.06% (vs. 88.19% for SoftMatch). CPG also outperforms the supervised LA upper bound on CIFAR-10-LT under arbitrary and inverse distributions (82.10% and 82.37% vs. 87.00% and 86.84% respectively, approaching but not exceeding the SL ceiling).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPG outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset, imbalance ratio, and unlabeled distribution setting tested. Among SSL methods, SoftMatch is the strongest competitor (65.27% avg. on CIFAR-10-LT), yet CPG still leads by 11.14 pp; FreeMatch (64.90%) and FixMatch (59.34%) trail further behind. Among ReaLTSSL methods, SimPro (61.68%) is the closest baseline, with CPG exceeding it by 14.73 pp on average; ACR (59.18%) and CDMAD (60.99%) are even further behind, with ACR degrading severely under arbitrary distributions (e.g., 48.22% at γ=150). Against the supervised LA upper bound (85.22% avg.), CPG narrows the gap to under 9 pp on CIFAR-10-LT and approaches it within 2 pp on SVHN-LT (94.06% vs. 93.25%). Statistical significance testing (pairwise t-test, α=0.05) confirms CPG wins in 130 out of 162 comparisons with zero losses, validating that the improvements are not due to chance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPG builds on the ReaLTSSL line initiated by ACR and CPE, which handle distribution mismatch between labeled and unlabeled data but assume the unlabeled distribution belongs to a small set of predefined types (long-tailed, uniform, inverse long-tailed). SimPro relaxes this via EM-based distribution estimation from high-confidence pseudo-labels, but degrades under truly arbitrary unlabeled distributions. CPG departs conceptually by abandoning unlabeled distribution estimation entirely: instead of modeling what the unlabeled data looks like, it progressively folds reliable pseudo-labels into the labeled set so that training always operates on a known distribution, making the Bayes-optimal (logit-adjusted) classifier distribution-agnostic with respect to the unlabeled pool. The dynamic controllable filtering (requiring both weak and strong views to agree and exceed a threshold) and the class-aware adaptive augmentation are novel components layered on this core self-reinforcing cycle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a practical gap: in real deployment (e.g., traffic monitoring across heterogeneous urban zones), the unlabeled data distribution is genuinely unknown and non-stationary, yet prior ReaLTSSL methods either require it to match a predefined anchor or estimate it imperfectly. The 15.97 pp gain over SimPro on CIFAR-10-LT under arbitrary distribution, backed by a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bound and statistical significance tests across four datasets, makes the approach credible for practitioners who cannot assume distributional alignment. Open questions include whether the confidence-threshold-based filtering remains effective under extreme label noise or very small labeled sets, and whether the self-reinforcing cycle's convergence properties hold when the initial model is poorly calibrated. The smaller gains on CIFAR-100-LT (3.09 pp average) suggest the method's advantage may narrow as per-class sample counts drop and inter-class similarity rises.
