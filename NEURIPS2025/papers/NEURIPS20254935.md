## Problem

Cross-Domain Few-Shot Object Detection (CD-FSOD) requires detecting novel objects with only 1–5 labeled samples from previously unseen domains, but existing data augmentation strategies (copy-paste, text-to-image generation) fail to simultaneously preserve exact object semantics and produce backgrounds coherent with the target domain. This dual requirement of object fidelity and domain alignment has not been addressed by prior few-shot augmentation methods, which either shift the object category or generate backgrounds that lack precise domain characteristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254935/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes annotated support images (with ground-truth bounding boxes) from the target domain and a retrieval database of diverse natural-scene images (COCO, 80 classes) as input. It produces n augmented images per support sample at 1024×1024 resolution where the foreground object is pixel-identical to the original (preserving valid bounding-box annotations) while the background is replaced with a domain-aligned synthetic scene. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play: the augmented images are simply added to the support set before fine-tuning an off-the-shelf detector (GroundingDINO with Swin-B backbone).

## Method

Domain-RAG follows the principle 'fix the foreground, adapt the background' through three stages. First, the foreground is removed via LaMa inpainting to obtain a clean background, which is then used to retrieve semantically similar images (CLIP cosine similarity) and re-rank them by style similarity (per-channel mean/std of shallow ResNet features, 128-D style vector). Second, FLUX-Redux encodes both the inpainted background and the top retrieved image into prompt embeddings, which are linearly fused (λ₁=1.0, λ₂=0.8) and fed to the FLUX generator to synthesize diverse domain-aligned backgrounds. Third, FLUX-Fill performs mask-guided composition: a binary mask marks the bounding-box region as foreground (0) and the rest as background (1), and the model repaints only the masked region while preserving foreground pixels via VAE-encoded latent blending. The retrieval-augmented design injects structured visual priors from real images rather than relying solely on text prompts, enabling domain-consistent generation without any additional training.

## Results

On the CD-FSOD benchmark (six target domains), Domain-RAG achieves 33.6 / 42.7 / 45.4 mAP under 1/5/10-shot settings, improving over the GroundingDINO baseline by +7.3 / +2.3 / +1.5 mAP respectively. On NWPU VHR-10 (RS-FSOD), it reaches 70.67 mAP average, surpassing the prior SOTA SAE-FSDet (68.36) by +2.31 mAP. On CAMO-FS (camouflaged FSOD), Domain-RAG attains 68.0 mAP average versus 66.6 for GroundingDINO, with the largest relative gain on ArTaxOr 1-shot (+117.5% over baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Domain-RAG outperforms all 18 compared baselines on average mAP across every shot setting. The closest competitor, ETS [42] (which also builds on GroundingDINO but uses a different augmentation strategy), is beaten by +5.8, +1.6, and +1.0 mAP under 1/5/10-shot respectively. The prior SOTA CD-ViTO [10] trails by a wide margin (19.7 / 16.6 / 15.8 mAP), while older few-shot detectors (Meta-RCNN, TFA, FSCE, DeFRCN, Detic, DE-ViT) remain far behind at 2–20 mAP. On RS-FSOD (NWPU VHR-10), Domain-RAG improves the strong SAE-FSDet [34] by +2.31 mAP on average and beats all other RS-FSOD baselines (P-CNN, FSCE, VFA, ICPE, etc.) by 10+ mAP. On CAMO-FS, Domain-RAG surpasses the original CAMO-FS methods (FS-CDIS-ITL/IMS, ~7 mAP) by over 60 mAP, though this gap is largely attributable to the GroundingDINO backbone; the incremental gain over GroundingDINO alone is +1.4 mAP. The largest single-domain gain is on ArTaxOr 1-shot (26.3 → 57.2 mAP, +117.5% relative), while on NEU-DET (industrial defects) the method still improves all shot settings, confirming robustness under severe domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of cross-domain few-shot object detection (CD-FSOD, introduced by CD-ViTO and formalized in the NTIRE 2025 challenge) and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG, originally an NLP paradigm). Prior augmentation strategies for few-shot detection either generate new foregrounds via text prompts (risking category drift) or paste objects onto random backgrounds (lacking domain coherence). Domain-RAG departs from both by fixing the foreground and using retrieved real images as structured visual priors—encoded via FLUX-Redux—rather than relying solely on textual descriptions to guide background synthesis, making it the first RAG-inspired, training-free compositional generation framework applied to CD-FSOD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of Domain-RAG makes it immediately deployable with any pretrained detector (e.g., GroundingDINO) without retraining the augmentation pipeline, which is critical in the 1-shot regime where compute and data are most scarce. The consistent gains across eight diverse domains (industrial, underwater, aerial, cartoon, camouflage) suggest the retrieval-guided background strategy is a general principle rather than a dataset-specific hack. However, the observed foreground leakage in some generated images and the slight performance drop at 20-shot (where base training is already sufficient) highlight that the method's benefits are most pronounced under severe data scarcity and may introduce annotation noise in edge cases. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object scenes, video detection, or domains where the COCO retrieval database lacks sufficient stylistic coverage.
