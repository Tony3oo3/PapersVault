## Problem

In-context learning operates in two modes—task recognition (recalling priorly learned tasks) and task learning (adapting to the given demonstrations)—but prior work has only analyzed their relative power at test time. This paper asks how these two abilities emerge, coexist, and eventually diverge during the pretraining phase, revealing that the model first learns both together but then forgets the prior while overfitting to the in-context signal.

## Input / Output

The transformer receives an input context matrix Z ∈ R^{(d+1)×(n+1)} containing n in-context covariate-response pairs and one query covariate, and outputs a scalar prediction for the query. Under the demonstration-query task irrelevance assumption, the demonstration task w and the query task w_q are drawn independently from the same noncentral Gaussian N(μ, σ²I_d), so the model must rely on the shared prior μ (task recognition) rather than the irrelevant demonstration w (task learning). The key analytical outputs are the time-dependent prior strength α(t), in-context strength κ(t), and the generalization gap ΔL = −2bκ, all derived in closed form from the gradient-flow ODE.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-parameter linear-attention transformer T_θ with θ = [α, κ]ᵀ, where α weights the task center μ (prior/task recognition) and κ weights the in-context task w (task learning), reducing to the Von Oswald et al. gradient-descent construction when μ = 0. By assuming isotropic covariates and a noncentral isotropic task distribution, the training loss becomes a quadratic form in (α, κ) with a positive-definite Hessian C₂, so gradient flow reduces to a linear ODE with a closed-form solution involving two eigenmodes. The solution reveals that α(t) = −2c·e^{−β_b t}·sinh(γ_b t) first decreases (prior strengthens) then returns to zero (prior forgetting), while κ(t) monotonically decreases to κ* = −n/(n+d+1) (in-context overfitting). The generalization gap ΔL = −2bκ grows monotonically with κ, and the task dispersion b controls the geometry of the loss landscape: for small b the two eigen-directions are roughly [1,1] and [1,−1], while for large b they align with the κ-axis and α-axis respectively, explaining why the prior dip becomes shallower as b increases.

## Results

The paper derives closed-form solutions for the gradient flow dynamics of a two-parameter linear-attention transformer on in-context linear regression. The training loss is quadratic with a unique minimum at θ* = [0, −n/(n+d+1)]ᵀ, giving L_train* = (d+1)/(n+d+1) · L₀. The prior strength α(t) follows a sinh-based trajectory that first decreases (prior strengthening) then returns to 0 (prior forgetting), while the in-context strength κ(t) monotonically decreases to κ* = −n/(n+d+1), causing the generalization gap ΔL = −2bκ to increase monotonically (in-context overfitting). Empirically, with n=10, d=5 and b ∈ {0.2, 0.8, 3.2}, the analytical predictions match both the full-parameter transformer and a practical 12-layer, 8-head softmax-attention transformer trained with AdamW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional method-vs-method baselines. Instead, the analytical two-parameter model is validated against two empirical configurations: (1) the full-parameter linear-attention transformer from Eq. (1), and (2) a practical multi-head (h=8), multi-layer (ℓ=12) transformer with softmax attention and residual connections. In both cases (Figure 3), the empirical training loss, generalization loss, and generalization gap closely track the theoretical gradient-flow predictions (dotted lines), confirming that the two-parameter reduction captures the essential dynamics. The paper also compares dynamics across three task dispersion values b = σ²d ∈ {0.2, 0.8, 3.2}: for small b, the loss landscape is nearly isotropic with eigenvectors v₊ ≈ [1,1]ᵀ and v₋ ≈ [1,−1]ᵀ, producing a pronounced dip in α(t); for large b, the sharper direction aligns with the κ-axis (v₊ ∥∼ [0,1]ᵀ), suppressing the prior-forgetting dip and making the in-context overfitting effect more dominant. The generalization risk at optimum L_test* = ((d+1)L₀ + nL_r)/(n+d+1) lies between the zero-task risk L₀ = 1+b and the random-task risk L_r = 2b, and approaches L_r as n grows large, indicating that with many irrelevant demonstrations the model essentially performs a random task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context linear regression analysis initiated by Garg et al. (2022) and the linear-attention gradient-descent construction of Von Oswald et al. (2023) and Ahn et al. (2024) by introducing two new analytical settings—demonstration-query task irrelevance and a noncentral task distribution—that allow the task recognition and task learning modes of ICL to be disentangled within the model parameters. While prior work (Pan et al. 2023, Min et al. 2022, Wei et al. 2023) characterized the relative dominance of these two modes at test time, this paper is the first to analytically trace their emergence and disappearance *during* pretraining via gradient-flow dynamics on a quadratic loss landscape. It also connects to Wang et al. (2024a), who empirically observed competition between the two modes, by providing a closed-form theoretical account of that competition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'prior forgetting'—where the model initially acquires task recognition but then loses it as training progresses—challenges the implicit assumption that pretraining monotonically improves all capabilities and suggests that the balance between task recognition and task learning is a dynamic, time-dependent phenomenon rather than a fixed property of model scale. The 'in-context overfitting' finding (monotonic growth of the generalization gap) provides a mechanistic explanation for why larger models are more susceptible to irrelevant demonstrations. Key limitations include the restriction to a two-parameter linear-attention model and a unimodal task distribution; whether these dynamics persist in multi-layer softmax transformers with heterogeneous task distributions or real NLP tasks remains open.
