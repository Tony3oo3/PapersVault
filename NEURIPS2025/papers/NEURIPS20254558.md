## Problem

Causal generative models (CGMs) such as causal normalizing flows provide exact do-operators and identifiability guarantees, but they assume causal sufficiency—no hidden confounders—severely limiting their real-world applicability. Existing approaches to hidden confounding (e.g., Deconfounder, Neural Causal Models) are either tailored to a single treatment-outcome pair requiring per-query training, restricted to discrete variables, or limited to simple causal graphs. No practical, scalable CGM existed that could handle continuous variables, multiple hidden confounders, large causal graphs, and both interventional and counterfactual queries from a single end-to-end training.

## Input / Output

DeCaFlow consumes i.i.d. observational samples of continuous variables together with the faithful causal graph G (used to mask both encoder and decoder layers), and for counterfactual queries additionally the observed factual vector x^f. It is trained once per dataset via ELBO maximization, after which it produces exact samples from any interventional distribution p(x|do(t:=α)) or counterfactual distribution p(y^cf|do(t^cf), x^f) through an adapted do-operator that exploits the bijectivity of the flow given z. The hidden confounders z are never observed; they are inferred by the encoder and integrated out during interventional sampling or sampled from the approximate posterior during counterfactual sampling.

## Method

DeCaFlow is a VAE-structured model comprising (i) a conditional masked autoregressive normalizing flow (an adapted CNF) as the generative/decoder network, which maps exogenous variables u to endogenous variables x while being conditioned on hidden confounders z, and (ii) a conditional normalizing flow as the inference/encoder network approximating the posterior q_φ(z|x) using only the children of z and their parents. Training maximizes the ELBO with KL-balancing terms to prevent posterior collapse, and the causal graph G is exploited to mask both networks so the learned SCM is structurally faithful by design. The key architectural insight is that the bijectivity of the flow given z trivializes the abduction step for counterfactuals and yields an exact, efficient do-operator. Theoretically, the paper extends proximal identifiability results (Miao et al.; Wang & Blei) to counterfactual queries and to settings with common observed ancestors, then integrates proximal identifiability as an additional base case within do-calculus, proving that DeCaFlow correctly estimates all queries reducible to observational or proximal-identifiable sub-queries.

## Results

On the Sachs protein-signaling and Ecoli70 gene-network semi-synthetic benchmarks, DeCaFlow outperforms all baselines (CNF, ANM, DCM, Deconfounder) on both ATE and counterfactual (CF) error, closely matching the oracle CNF that observes the hidden confounders. On the LSAC law-school fairness task, the DeCaFlow-based predictor achieves RMSE 1.604 with inter-group MMD of 0.0054, substantially reducing bias (MMD) compared to the Unfair predictor (MMD 0.163) while incurring only a modest accuracy drop (RMSE 1.413 → 1.604), and far outperforming the Fair K (RMSE 2.817) and Fair Add (RMSE 2.826) baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCaFlow outperforms all four causal generative model baselines (CNF, ANM, DCM, Deconfounder) on both ATE and CF error across the Sachs and Ecoli70 datasets, staying on par with the oracle that directly observes hidden confounders. The gap over CNF is particularly large, confirming that the variational deconfounding architecture is essential since a CNF is equivalent to DeCaFlow with zero latent dimension. The Deconfounder [75] appears competitive only when evaluated on the narrow subset of queries satisfying its stricter assumptions; on the full set of proxy-identifiable queries, DeCaFlow achieves significantly lower error. On the LSAC fairness task, DeCaFlow achieves near-zero inter-group MMD (0.0054) at a modest RMSE cost (1.604), whereas the Fair K and Fair Add baselines [40] collapse to near-mean predictions (RMSE ≈ 2.82), making DeCaFlow the only method that is both fair and accurate.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCaFlow extends Causal Normalizing Flows (CNFs) [Javaloy et al., NeurIPS 2023] by adapting their masked autoregressive architecture to condition on hidden confounders, wrapping it in a variational autoencoder structure trained via the ELBO. On the identifiability side, it builds on proximal causal inference results from Miao et al. [Biometrika 2018] and Wang and Blei [ICML 2021], extending them to counterfactual queries and to settings with observed common ancestors between treatment and outcome. Unlike prior CGMs (CNFs, ANMs, diffusion-based causal models) that assume causal sufficiency, and unlike prior proximal methods that are tailored to a single treatment-outcome pair and require per-query training, DeCaFlow provides a single end-to-end model that answers all do-calculus- and proxy-identifiable interventional and counterfactual queries on a given causal graph after one training run.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified generative framework that correctly estimates both interventional and counterfactual queries under hidden confounding in a single model, which is critical for real-world settings where unmeasured confounders are ubiquitous and randomized trials are infeasible. The theoretical result establishing a one-to-one correspondence between proxy-identifiable interventional and counterfactual queries is a clean and useful extension of the proximal inference literature. Key limitations include the untestable requirement for sufficiently informative proxies, the assumption of continuous variables, and the need for perfect knowledge of the causal graph; open questions include robustness to noisy or misspecified graphs, extension to discrete variables and time-varying treatments, and integration with causal discovery methods.
