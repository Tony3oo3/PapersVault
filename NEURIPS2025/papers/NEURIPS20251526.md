## Problem

Existing LLM serving systems (vLLM, SGLang) rely on LRU eviction for prefix caches, which ignores the semantic content and continuation likelihood of conversations, leaving a large gap to an oracle policy with perfect future knowledge. No learned eviction method had been designed for the unique characteristics of LLM prefix caching—variable-length, semantically interconnected conversational segments—unlike the fixed-size, independent objects handled by learned caches in CDNs.

## Input / Output

The predictor consumes the current user prompt plus up to 4 preceding user prompts (N=4), with a 512-token budget split evenly across turns and each turn's allocation drawn half from the prompt's beginning and half from its end to respect the embedding model's length limit. The MLP receives a 384-dimensional text embedding concatenated with a scalar turn count and outputs a single continuation probability in [0,1]. This probability, combined with a time-based exponential decay, drives a min-heap eviction structure where the lowest-probability KV block is evicted when the prefix cache is full.

## Method

LPC pairs a frozen multilingual-e5-small sentence encoder (118M params, 384-dim output) with a lightweight 3-layer MLP (128 hidden neurons per layer) trained via binary cross-entropy to predict whether a conversation will continue. Only the MLP head is trained (frozen embeddings), completing in under 10 minutes, enabling daily retraining to adapt to shifting user behavior. The eviction algorithm replaces LRU's last-access timestamp with a dynamically decayed continuation probability stored in a min-heap, where the decay factor is exp(−elapsed_time × scale) with scale tuned to the inverse of the average turn interval (~100 s). For KV blocks shared across multiple conversations (e.g., common system prompts), the effective probability is the maximum across all associated conversations to prevent premature eviction. The entire predictor runs as a standalone component in parallel to LLM inference, requiring only 1 GB of GPU memory and processing up to 1,000 predictions per second per GPU.

## Results

LPC achieves 18–47% reductions in required prefix cache size to match LRU's hit ratio across three real-world conversational datasets (LMSys, ShareGPT, Chatbot-Arena). At a fixed 40 GB cache, LPC delivers 5–18% higher hit ratios on LMSys, 11–15% on ShareGPT, and 7–16% on Chatbot-Arena compared to LRU. In an emulated disaggregated prefilling server, the hit ratio gain translates to 11.1% higher prefilling throughput (10.01 → 11.11 req/s) and up to 73% TTFT reduction for cache-hit requests. The predictor adds only 1 GB GPU memory (~1.25% overhead) and can process up to 1,000 predictions/s per GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is vLLM's LRU prefix cache eviction (Kwon et al., SOSP 2023), which is the default in all major LLM serving frameworks. LPC consistently outperforms LRU on every dataset and every configuration tested: across the full cache-size sweep (60K–160K tokens), LPC achieves 13–98% relative hit-ratio gains depending on dataset and capacity; at the full 40 GB budget with varying user think times, gains are 5–18%. On Chatbot-Arena, LPC reaches a 30% hit ratio with only 85K tokens versus 150K for LRU (a 43% memory saving). In the emulated prefilling microbenchmark, LPC's higher hit ratio (42% vs 35%) yields 11.1% higher throughput. The paper also reports that LPC reduces the gap to an Oracle (perfect-future-knowledge) policy, confirming the learned signal captures meaningful continuation structure that LRU cannot. No other heuristic eviction baselines (e.g., FIFO, LFU, Greedy-Dual) are directly compared, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learned caching paradigm from CDNs (LRB, MAT, HALP) to LLM prefix cache eviction, a domain where all major serving frameworks (vLLM, SGLang) rely on LRU and where prior LLM-specific work (CachedAttention, Marconi, PromptCache) addresses scheduling or attention reuse but not learned eviction. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that LLM prefix caches manage semantically interconnected, variable-length conversational segments rather than independent fixed-size objects, necessitating content-aware prediction (text embeddings + MLP) rather than access-pattern-only features. The design also departs from prior learned caches by enforcing near-zero overhead constraints (1 GB GPU, <10 min retraining) appropriate for co-deployment with large inference models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For production LLM serving, the 18–47% reduction in required cache size for equivalent hit ratios translates directly into lower hardware costs or higher user capacity on the same GPUs, and the 11% prefilling throughput gain in disaggregated inference is a concrete, deployable win. However, the evaluation relies on synthetic timestamps (public datasets lack real arrival times), compares primarily against LRU rather than a broader set of heuristics, and tests only a single model family (Qwen3-32B), leaving open whether the gains generalize across architectures and real-world traffic patterns. The remaining gap to the Oracle policy (22–66% of the LRU-to-Oracle gap still unaddressed) suggests that textual content alone may be insufficient, pointing toward incorporating user access patterns or multi-modal signals as a natural next step.
