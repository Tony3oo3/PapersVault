## Problem

Temporal vision-language models frequently rely on static features (backgrounds, objects, attributes) as shortcuts rather than analyzing dynamic visual changes, leading to systematic prediction errors. Existing automated approaches for discovering such failure modes are designed for single-image settings and cannot handle multi-image sequences, while quantitative evaluation in temporal settings is hampered by the lack of ground-truth bias annotations for pretrained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250335/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

TROVE takes a frozen pretrained temporal VLM and a labeled validation dataset of multi-image sequences (each with a ground-truth class label) and produces a ranked list of image clusters—each cluster representing a discovered static feature bias—paired with the specific class labels on which that feature degrades performance. The method operates at the image level within sequences rather than treating each sequence as a single unit, and requires no image-level feature annotations; it works even when the static feature appears in only a subset of frames within a sequence.

## Method

TROVE first extracts image-level embeddings by feeding each individual image (replicated n_i times to form a static sequence) through the VLM's vision encoder, then clusters these embeddings via spherical K-means with cosine distance (optimal K selected by Silhouette score). Each cluster is scored by two components: an Error Contribution Score (ECS) measuring the accuracy gap on a class label between sequences containing vs. not containing the cluster's feature, and a Static Bias Score (SBS) measuring the mean softmax confidence the model assigns to its (incorrect) prediction when classifying a static, unchanging sequence built from misclassified images in that cluster. The final TROVE score is the sum of ECS and SBS, ranking features by both their error-inducing effect and the degree to which the model has learned to rely on them. This two-pronged design distinguishes true learned static biases from mere dataset correlations, and the image-level granularity enables detection of features visible in only a portion of a sequence.

## Results

On the synthetic evaluation framework of 101 trained temporal VLMs, TROVE achieves Precision@10 of 100.0 (Background), 97.8 (Object), and 100.0 (Attribute), with R-Precision of 95.0, 92.8, and 89.3 respectively, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. The headline result is a 28.6% improvement over the closest baseline (Confidence). On real-world test-time mitigation (Kinetics400), TROVE-guided prompt learning improves Accuracy@5 on bias-affected class labels by up to 111% (e.g., ViCLIP-B: 45.3→95.8 on Label ŷ) and by 4.5 points overall (73.4→77.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TROVE outperforms all four baselines across every feature category and every metric. The closest competitor is Confidence (Li et al. [16]), which ranks images by maximum prediction confidence; TROVE achieves a 28.6% improvement over Confidence overall, with the largest gaps on Background (P@10: 100.0 vs. 61.0) and Attribute (P@10: 100.0 vs. 58.5) features, while matching Confidence on Object features (97.8 vs. 97.8 P@10) but substantially exceeding it on R-Precision (92.8 vs. 78.0). The three non-temporal methods—Domino, George, and Distilling Failures—perform poorly when naively extended to the temporal setting (P@10 typically 45–69), because they operate at the sequence level and cannot isolate the specific images within a sequence that contain the static feature. Distilling Failures is the strongest of the non-temporal baselines (P@10 up to 69.2 on Attribute) but still trails TROVE by 30+ points. TROVE's consistent near-perfect performance across all three feature types (background, object, attribute) and all sequence lengths (2–10 images) contrasts sharply with the baselines, which show large performance swings depending on feature type and sequence length.

## Relation to Prior Work

TROVE extends the systematic error discovery literature (Domino, George, Distilling Failures) from non-temporal single-image settings to temporal multi-image sequence inputs, where naive extensions fail because error-inducing features may appear in only a subset of frames. It also builds on fine-grained region-level error detection in the image domain (RAVL, Yang et al.) and draws on CoOp for its test-time mitigation strategy. A key conceptual departure from prior static-bias work (Li et al., Wang et al., Ding et al.) is that TROVE discovers biases in already-pretrained models rather than modifying the training procedure to reduce them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 101-model synthetic evaluation framework provides the first rigorous quantitative benchmark for temporal static-bias discovery, addressing a gap the authors identify as a major obstacle to evaluating such methods. The practical test-time mitigation via CoOp (up to 111% worst-group improvement without retraining) demonstrates immediate deployability for safety-critical applications like medical imaging. Limitations include restriction to image sequences (not audio or signals), dependence on a labeled validation set, and the fact that the strongest quantitative evidence comes from synthetic data; extending TROVE to generative VLMs and non-image temporal modalities remains open.
