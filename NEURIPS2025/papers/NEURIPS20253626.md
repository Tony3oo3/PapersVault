## Problem

Sample-efficient dynamic learning from low-resolution measurements is hindered by existing symmetry-based methods that assume a single global symmetry group and decouple symmetry discovery from dynamic modeling, causing error accumulation and limited expressiveness. Real dynamical systems exhibit symmetry breaking along two axes: multiple symmetry groups acting simultaneously (mixture of groups) and equivariance relations spanning different temporal scales (short- vs. long-term), neither of which is captured by prior approaches in a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes irregularly sampled, low-resolution time-series states (with 30%, 60%, or 90% random data drops) through an ODE-RNN encoder that produces a Gaussian posterior over the initial latent state. The decoder generates a dense latent trajectory at arbitrary time resolution via structured Lie group updates, which is then mapped back to the observation space by an MLP to yield interpolated reconstructions, extrapolated future states, or a learned dynamics model for model-based control (e.g., power system frequency regulation).

## Method

Latent MoS replaces the unstructured ODE update in Latent ODE with a Mixture-of-Experts layer where each expert is a parameterized Lie group action (planar rotation, translation, scaling, or their second-order compositions) expressed in homogeneous coordinates. A time- and state-dependent gating network (Softmax over K experts) linearly mixes the expert transformations, enabling the model to capture multi-modal, context-dependent symmetries without explicit symmetry discovery. The key theoretical guarantee (Lemma 1, Proposition 1) shows that equivariance is preserved when the dynamic Lie group H is a subgroup of the symmetry group G and its centralizer C_G(h) is nontrivial, which holds for the rotation, translation, scaling, and their composed transformations. A hierarchical multi-scale architecture (Algorithm 1) partitions the latent vector across S levels with different subinterval counts L^(s), so coarser levels model longer-term equivariance while finer levels capture local dynamics. Training maximizes the ELBO with the ODE-RNN encoder and the structured decoder, and the discrete Δt update (Eq. 10) admits a continuous-time limit via the matrix logarithm of each expert's group action.

## Results

On extrapolation tasks (Table 1), Latent MoS achieves the lowest MSE (×10⁻²) across most datasets and drop rates. The most dramatic gains appear on Glycolytic oscillators (MSE 0.32 vs. 13.57 for Contiformer at 90% drop) and ECG signals (MSE 1.08 vs. 3.49 for Contiformer at 90% drop). Across all 8 datasets and 3 drop rates, Latent MoS delivers relative improvements of 15%–97% over the strongest baseline. The only notable exception is Lotka-Volterra at 90% drop, where Latent ODE (0.43) slightly outperforms Latent MoS (0.61).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Latent MoS outperforms all six baselines on the majority of dataset–drop-rate combinations. Against the closest competitor, Latent ODE (Rubanova et al., 2019), Latent MoS wins on 22 of 24 settings; the sole exception is Lotka-Volterra at 90% drop where Latent ODE achieves 0.43 vs. 0.61. Contiformer (Chen et al., 2024) is competitive on ECG and Solar at lower drop rates but degrades sharply on Glycolytic (13.57 vs. 0.32 at 90% drop) and Power events (8.09 vs. 5.32). NCDE (Kidger et al., 2020) and RNN-Δt (Che et al., 2018) are consistently outperformed across all datasets, with NCDE particularly weak on high-dimensional systems (e.g., 25.33 on Solar at 90% drop). Informer (Zhou et al., 2021) and Autoformer (Wu et al., 2021) perform well only at 30% drop (e.g., Autoformer reaches 0.16 on Glycolytic) but collapse at 90% drop (12.92 and 20.44 on Glycolytic and Solar respectively), confirming their reliance on pre-interpolation rather than continuous dynamics modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Latent ODE (Rubanova et al., 2019) for irregular time-series modeling and on GAN-based latent symmetry discovery methods (Yang et al., 2023, 2024), but departs from both in key ways. Rather than treating symmetry discovery and dynamic learning as separate, decoupled stages (as prior symmetry-discovery methods do), Latent MoS embeds Lie group structure directly into the latent dynamics, eliminating the need for a separate discovery algorithm. It also generalizes from a single global symmetry group to a mixture of groups (rotation, translation, scaling, and their compositions) with time-varying gating, and extends to multi-scale equivariance via a hierarchical stacking of MoS blocks at different temporal resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically important gap: learning accurate dynamics from the low-resolution, irregularly sampled measurements common in power grids, biomedical monitoring, and environmental sensing, where data scarcity is the norm. The Lie-group grounding provides interpretable latent representations that could support downstream geometric analyses (stability, safety, contraction), and the control experiment demonstrates tangible benefit in power-system frequency regulation. Key limitations include the restricted candidate set of transformations (planar rotations, translations, scalings, and second-order compositions), the assumption that at least local symmetries exist, and the lack of an automated mechanism for selecting which experts to include—currently requiring validation-based manual pruning.
