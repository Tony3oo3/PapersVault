## Problem

Zero-shot HOI detection must generalize to unseen verb-object compositions, but existing prompt-learning approaches fail to handle two key visual complexities: intra-class diversity (the same verb appears in drastically different poses and contexts) and inter-class entanglement (semantically distinct verbs like 'eating' and 'licking' produce visually similar patterns). Most prior methods assign a single static prompt per verb and ignore region-specific semantics, limiting both generalization and discriminability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254006/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces human bounding boxes, object bounding boxes, and a multi-label verb prediction (over 117 verb classes) for each detected human-object pair. A frozen DETR (ResNet-50) performs detection in stage one, and a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder with lightweight cross-attention adapters extracts patch features in stage two; region features are then cropped via RoIAlign from the human, object, and union bounding boxes to form three d-dimensional vectors used for verb classification.

## Method

VDRP introduces two complementary prompt modules. First, visual diversity-aware prompt learning (VDP) computes group-wise visual variance from union-region CLS features of semantically similar verbs, injects it into a shared learnable context embedding via an MLP modulation, and applies Gaussian perturbation scaled by the group-wise standard deviation to the resulting CLIP text-encoder output, so each prompt reflects the expected spread of verb appearances. Second, region-aware prompt augmentation (RAP) retrieves region-specific concepts generated by LLMs (LLaMA-7B, GPT-4) for each verb and region type, selects the most relevant concepts via Sparsemax over cosine similarities between the region feature and a concept pool, and adds the weighted concept vector to the diversity-aware prompt. The final classification averages logits computed independently against three region-specific prompt sets (human, object, union), and the model is trained with focal loss for multi-label verb prediction using only 4.50M trainable parameters.

## Results

On HICO-DET, VDRP achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot HOI detection across all four evaluation settings. Under NF-UC, it reaches 36.45 mAP (Unseen) and 33.85 HM, surpassing the previous best (CLIP4HOI) by +5.01 and +4.31 respectively. Under RF-UC, UO, and UV, it achieves 31.29/32.77, 36.13/34.41, and 26.69/29.80 (Unseen/HM) respectively, all leading prior methods. The method uses only 4.50M trainable parameters, substantially fewer than EZ-HOI (6.85M) and CLIP4HOI (56.7M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDRP outperforms all 14 baselines across all four zero-shot settings on HICO-DET. Against the strongest prompt-learning baselines, it beats EZ-HOI [25] by +2.09 HM (NF-UC), +1.59 HM (RF-UC), +2.27 HM (UO), and +0.71 HM (UV), while using fewer trainable parameters (4.50M vs. 6.85M). It surpasses CLIP4HOI [26] by +4.31 HM on NF-UC and +2.43 HM on UO, with a much smaller parameter budget (4.50M vs. 56.7M). Compared to CMMP [24], gains are +3.03 HM (NF-UC), +1.67 HM (RF-UC), +1.97 HM (UO), and +0.57 HM (UV). Against older baselines (GEN-VLKT [23], HOICLIP [27], ADA-CM [10], EoID [52], FCL [1], ATL [2]), improvements are substantially larger (5–17 HM points). In the fully-supervised setting (Table 10), VDRP leads all two-stage methods (39.07 mAP vs. EZ-HOI's 38.61) but trails the one-stage UniHOI [11] (40.06), which benefits from end-to-end optimization and full supervision of all components.

## Relation to Prior Work

VDRP extends the prompt-learning paradigm for zero-shot HOI detection (CLIP4HOI, CMMP, EZ-HOI) by addressing two limitations those methods share: a single static prompt per verb and region-agnostic text embeddings. It draws on distribution-based prompt learning (Zhu et al., Lu et al.) to inject group-wise visual variance and Gaussian perturbation into context tokens, and on retrieval-augmented detection (Kim et al.) to fuse LLM-generated region concepts via Sparsemax. The key departure is the explicit modeling of intra-class visual diversity through variance statistics and the use of region-specific concept retrieval to resolve inter-class visual entanglement—neither of which prior zero-shot HOI prompt methods attempt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 4.5M-parameter prompt-based model can match or exceed 56M-parameter CLIP-adaptation methods across four zero-shot HOI settings, suggesting that distributional and region-aware prompt design is a more parameter-efficient path than heavy visual-branch adaptation. However, the reliance on LLM-generated region concepts introduces a noise floor that the authors acknowledge, and the Gaussian variance assumption may not capture multimodal appearance distributions for rare verbs. Open questions include whether the group-wise variance prior generalizes beyond HICO-DET's 117 verbs, whether vision-aligned concept refinement (beyond LLM text) could reduce retrieval noise, and whether the framework's benefits persist under extreme long-tail or open-vocabulary settings.
