## Problem

Invariant Point Attention (IPA), the core geometry-aware module in most protein and RNA structure models, scales quadratically O(L²) in sequence length, forcing practitioners to truncate training data to ~512 residues despite 42% of PDB structures exceeding that length. This quadratic bottleneck restricts dataset size, necessitates expensive multi-GPU training spanning days to months, and prevents modeling of biologically relevant longer biomolecules.

## Input / Output

FlashIPA consumes per-residue sequence vectors s_i, SE(3) local frames T_i = (R_i, t_i) defined on backbone atoms, and a factorized pair representation z_ij = z_i^{1T} z_j^2 where the two pseudo-factors each have shape (L, r, d_z) with a small contraction rank r (typically 1–2). Instead of a full L×L distogram, it uses k-nearest-neighbor distances (k=20) with positional encodings on neighbor indices to preserve SE(3) invariance while avoiding quadratic activations. The output is an updated sequence representation s̃_i per residue, structured identically to standard IPA so the module is a drop-in replacement. Lifted query/key/value dimensions are constrained by FlashAttention's 256-dim head limit: max(c + 5N_query + r·d_z, c + 3N_value + r·d_z) ≤ 256.

## Method

The key reformulation expands the sum-of-squared-norms geometric term in the IPA softmax argument and collects it into a single inner product, lifting the scalar and geometric query/key/value components into augmented vectors ĥq, ĥk, ĥv of dimension c + 5N_query + r·d_z (and analogous for values). The pair-representation bias is handled by factorizing z_ij = z_i^{1T} z_j^2, which lets the pair attention output be computed as z_i^{1T}(Σ_j a_ij z_j^2) without ever materializing the L×L tensor. With all terms expressed as a standard q^T k inner product plus a factorized value, the entire IPA update reduces to one call to FlashAttention's tiled online-softmax kernel, replacing O(L²) HBM traffic with O(L) memory while retaining exact SE(3) invariance. Empirically, rank-2 factorization with k=20 nearest-neighbor distograms matches or slightly exceeds original IPA validation performance (sc-RMSD for FoldFlow, sc-TM for RNA-FrameFlow) while enabling linear-memory training on structures up to 8,800 protein residues and 4,417 RNA nucleotides.

## Results

FlashIPA reduces GPU memory scaling from quadratic (y = 2.4×10⁻³·L² MB) to linear (y = 7.5×10⁻²·L MB) and achieves a 30× speedup for RNA generation at sequence length 2048. On FoldFlow protein backbone generation, FlashIPA achieves lower sc-RMSD than original IPA at 200k steps, with further improvement when training is extended to the full PDB monomer dataset (up to 8.8k residues). On RNA-FrameFlow, FlashIPA matches the original model's validity (scTM 0.38–0.41 vs 0.42) while enabling single-GPU training at a quarter of the compute cost (20 vs 80 GPU-hours). At protein length 512, FlashIPA supports an effective batch size of 39 versus 1 for original IPA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original AlphaFold2 IPA, FlashIPA matches or exceeds downstream performance while reducing memory from O(L²) to O(L); in FoldFlow, FlashIPA achieves consistently lower sc-RMSD across all tested lengths (100–500 residues) at 200k steps, and the gap widens when training is extended to the full dataset without length restrictions. Against the original FoldFlow model, FlashIPA converges slightly faster (larger effective batch size) and shows notably faster reduction in steric clash loss, attributed to the k-nearest-neighbor distogram emphasis. Against the original RNA-FrameFlow, FlashIPA achieves comparable validity (scTM 0.38–0.41 vs 0.42) and novelty scores, with the single-GPU variant matching 4-GPU performance at only 20 GPU-hours versus 80. The paper notes that ESMFold (which itself uses IPA) runs out of memory beyond ~500 residues, preventing sc-RMSD evaluation for the longest FlashIPA-generated structures, so the comparison at extreme lengths remains qualitative.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashIPA reformulates the Invariant Point Attention (IPA) operation introduced in AlphaFold2 into a standard attention-compatible form by algebraically expanding the sum of squared geometric norms into a single inner product and factorizing the quadratic pair representation into low-rank components. This reformulation makes the entire IPA update amenable to FlashAttention's tiled online-softmax computation, eliminating the need to materialize the O(L²) attention matrix. The approach also draws on the low-rank factorization philosophy seen in state-space models like Mamba, where small contraction dimensions with nonlinear factors replace large linear projections, and introduces a k-nearest-neighbor distogram as a practical substitute for full pairwise distance encodings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlashIPA removes the primary practical barrier to training geometric deep learning models on long biomolecules: GPU memory exhaustion from quadratic attention. By enabling linear memory scaling and 30× speedups at long sequence lengths, it opens the door to multi-chain complex modeling, atomic-detail representations, and training on the full PDB without length truncation. Key open questions include whether the pair-representation factorization (rank 2) generalizes to denser or more heterogeneous pair features, whether the FlashAttention head-dimension cap of 256 will constrain future model capacity, and whether replacing softmax with linear-attention variants (e.g., Mamba-style) can reduce compute from O(L²) to O(L).
