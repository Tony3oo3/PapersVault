## Problem

Unified multimodal generative models like Transfusion are trained from scratch, requiring trillions of tokens and still underperforming pretrained text-only LLMs on language tasks. Naive finetuning of a pretrained LLM on multimodal data causes catastrophic forgetting of its language capabilities. The paper asks how to equip a pretrained text-only LLM with image understanding and generation while fully preserving its original language performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250310/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts interleaved sequences of discrete text tokens and continuous noisy image representations (256×256 RGB images VAE-encoded to 32×32×8 latents, then U-Net downsampled to 256 patch tokens), with a maximum context of 4096 tokens. At text positions it outputs vocabulary logits for autoregressive generation; at image positions it outputs predicted noise ε for the DDPM reverse process. A hybrid attention mask applies causal masking to text tokens and bidirectional masking to image tokens within the same sequence.

## Method

LMFusion introduces deep modality separation into the Transfusion architecture: text and image data are processed by independent QKV projections, output projections, and FFNs (all initialized from Llama-3 8B), while a single shared self-attention layer enables cross-modal interaction via concatenation of queries, keys, and values across modalities. The text-specific modules are frozen (η_text = 0) to preserve Llama-3's language capabilities, while the image-specific modules and a 0.27B-parameter U-Net downsampler/upsampler (trained from scratch) are optimized with the combined LM + DDPM loss. Ablations show that deep separation (modality-specific attention + FFN) outperforms both shallow separation (FFN-only) and no separation, and that freezing text modules is essential—naive finetuning drops HellaSwag by 15% initially and never fully recovers. The framework extends to vision-language models (LLaVAFusion) by freezing the VLM's transformer and adding a parallel image-specific module.

## Results

On language-only benchmarks, LMFusion exactly preserves Llama-3's performance (HellaSwag 60.0, SIQA 48.1, WinoGrande 72.8) by freezing all text modules. For image understanding, LMFusion achieves a CIDEr score of 38.4 on MS-COCO, a 20% improvement over Transfusion (32.0). For image generation, LMFusion achieves FID of 8.61 (with CFG 1.55) and CLIP score of 24.4, outperforming Transfusion's FID of 8.70 while matching its CLIP score. The 0.5× FLOPs variant (using only image data, no text data) already achieves CIDEr 38.3 and FID 8.75, demonstrating that text data is unnecessary when reusing pretrained language weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transfusion (trained from scratch), LMFusion wins decisively on all metrics: CIDEr 38.4 vs. 32.0 (+20%), FID 8.61 vs. 8.70 with CFG (3.6% improvement), and HellaSwag 60.0 vs. 51.0 (+11.6%), while matching CLIP scores. Against Llama-3 (the base model), LMFusion exactly matches all language benchmarks (60.0/48.1/72.8), confirming zero degradation from adding image capabilities. In the LLaVAFusion extension (Table 2), the method outperforms EMU-3 (FID 8.2 vs. 12.8, MMMU 41.7 vs. 31.6), Show-O (FID 8.2 vs. 9.2, MME-P 1603.7 vs. 1435.7), Janus (FID 8.2 vs. 8.5, MMMU 41.7 vs. 30.5), and Chameleon (FID 8.2 vs. 26.7, ChartQA 69.5 vs. 0.0) across image understanding and generation. Against MetaMorph, LLaVAFusion wins on ChartQA (69.5 vs. 37.1), RealWorldQA (60.0 vs. 58.3), and FID (8.2 vs. 11.8), while MetaMorph edges out by 0.1 on MMMU (41.8 vs. 41.7), an essentially tied result.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMFusion builds directly on Transfusion's unified architecture (autoregressive text + diffusion image in a single transformer) and the modality-separation idea from Mixture-of-Experts work (MoMA, EVE, Mixture-of-Transformers), but pushes separation deeper by making both self-attention and FFNs modality-specific rather than only FFNs. Its key departure from prior unified multimodal models (Transfusion, Chameleon, Unified-IO, EMU-3) is that instead of training from scratch on trillions of text tokens, it initializes from a pretrained Llama-3 8B and freezes all text modules, training only the image-specific parameters. The extension to vision-language models (LLaVAFusion on LLaVA-Next) further generalizes the recipe beyond text-only LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by reusing pretrained LLM weights and excluding text data from training, LMFusion roughly halves the compute needed for unified multimodal generation while simultaneously improving image understanding (20%) and generation (3.6%) over Transfusion trained from scratch. The ablation showing that naive finetuning causes a persistent 7–15% drop in language benchmarks, and that deep separation (attention + FFN) is necessary rather than shallow (FFN-only), provides clear architectural guidance for future multimodal adaptation. Open questions include whether pretrained image-generation components (e.g., from Stable Diffusion) can also be reused to further reduce compute, whether the approach scales beyond 8B, and how it extends to additional modalities (video, audio).
