## Problem

Standard Neural Processes rely on a single global latent variable, producing poorly calibrated uncertainty estimates and failing to capture local input dependencies. While attention-based variants like AttnNP introduce target-specific representations, their learned embeddings can distort input geometry, undermining the distance-based similarity relations that are critical for reliable uncertainty quantification and out-of-distribution detection.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a variable-size context set of input-output pairs (sampled from [3,50] for regression, [16,128] for classification) and a target input, producing a factorized predictive distribution: a Gaussian (mean and variance) for regression or a categorical distribution over classes for image classification. For image tasks, 32×32 RGB images are first mapped to fixed-size embeddings via a frozen VGG-16 backbone before entering the NP encoder-decoder. The context set size is randomly sampled per training batch, and at test time a fixed number of context points (30 for regression, 100 for classification) is drawn from the training set.

## Method

DNP augments the standard NP global latent path with a distance-aware local latent path. A shared MLP encoder maps each input to a latent embedding, and bi-Lipschitz regularization constrains both the minimum and maximum singular values of every weight matrix (via a penalty on σ_min and σ_max, approximated efficiently with LOBPCG), ensuring the latent space approximately preserves input distances. Cross-attention between target and context embeddings uses a Laplace kernel over these distance-preserving representations to compute attention weights, which then aggregate per-context Gaussian parameters into a target-specific local prior. The decoder conditions on both the global and local latent variables to produce the predictive distribution. Training maximizes the ELBO plus a weighted bi-Lipschitz regularization term, and at inference the learned priors (not posteriors) generate the latent variables for prediction.

## Results

On 1D synthetic regression (Table 2), DNP achieves the best log-likelihood across all three GP kernels: 0.696 (RBF), 0.620 (Matérn-5/2), and 0.635 (Periodic), while also attaining the lowest ECE in most cases (0.093, 0.083, 0.074). On multi-output real-world regression (Table 4), DNP achieves the lowest MSE on SARCOS (0.886) and SCM20D (0.887) and the best ECE across all three datasets (0.083, 0.084, 0.088). For CIFAR-10 classification, DNP reaches 91.97% accuracy with the lowest ECE (0.011) and the highest OOD AUPR (97.05 on SVHN), outperforming all NP baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNP outperforms all eight baselines on the primary 1D regression benchmark. Against the closest competitor DSVNP [63], DNP improves LL by 0.108 (RBF), 0.108 (Matérn-5/2), and 0.223 (Periodic), while reducing ECE by 0.008, 0.018, and 0.018 respectively. AttnNP [25] is the strongest baseline on Matérn-5/2 ECE (0.078) but DNP still achieves a lower ECE (0.083) with a substantially higher LL (0.620 vs 0.499). ConvNP [8] achieves the second-best RBF LL (0.609) but has the worst ECE among stochastic baselines (0.190), whereas DNP improves both LL and ECE simultaneously. On multi-output regression, DNP beats DSVNP [63] on SARCOS MSE (0.886 vs 0.938) and SCM20D MSE (0.887 vs 0.912), and achieves the best ECE on all three datasets. For CIFAR-10 OOD detection, DNP achieves 97.05 AUPR on SVHN, surpassing AttnNP (93.69) and DSVNP (93.42) by over 3 points, while also matching the deterministic VGG-16 accuracy (91.97 vs 92.08) with far superior calibration (ECE 0.011 vs 0.032). The exact GP [66] baseline underperforms all NP variants on the predator-prey task (LL −9.632 on real data vs DNP's −2.967), confirming that NP-based context conditioning is more effective than kernel-based interpolation with limited context points.

## Relation to Prior Work

DNP extends the Neural Process family (CNP, NP, AttnNP, DSVNP) by introducing a distance-aware local latent path that complements the standard global latent variable. Its key departure from AttnNP and DSVNP is the use of bi-Lipschitz regularization on the local encoder's weight matrices—borrowed from the GAN and deep kernel learning literature—to construct a latent space that approximately preserves input geometry, replacing the self-attention-over-context mechanism with cross-attention over distance-preserving embeddings. The bi-Lipschitz constraint itself extends spectral normalization by explicitly bounding both the smallest and largest singular values per layer, and its application to Neural Processes is, to the authors' knowledge, novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DNP provides a principled mechanism for improving uncertainty calibration and OOD detection in NPs by grounding attention weights in a geometrically faithful latent space, yielding consistent gains in ECE and AUPR across regression and classification benchmarks. The main limitations are the restriction to Euclidean distance (limiting applicability to non-Euclidean or graph-structured inputs) and the added computational cost of cross-attention, which the authors suggest could be mitigated with sparse attention. Open questions include whether the bi-Lipschitz constraint generalizes to task-specific similarity metrics and whether the approach scales to very large context sets without the O(NM) attention cost.
