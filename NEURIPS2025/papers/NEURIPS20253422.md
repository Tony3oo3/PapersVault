## Problem

How to optimally allocate a fixed rollout budget across competing candidate trajectories during parallel test-time search remains underexplored, with existing strategies (Beam Search, DVTS, REBASE) relying on human-designed heuristics that lack principled optimality guarantees. The paper identifies a core structural limitation: solution-level allocation conflates reasoning direction quality with candidate count, systematically biasing resources toward overrepresented directions and wasting test-time compute.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253422/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem as input, along with PRM scores (normalized to [0,1]) for each candidate partial solution and semantic embeddings (via BGE-M3, 568M parameters) of the candidate trajectories. At each search step it produces an integer rollout allocation vector specifying how many of the N total rollouts to assign to each of the k active candidates, and ultimately a final answer selected via weighted majority voting where each completed trajectory is weighted by its final PRM score.

## Method

The paper formulates test-time search as a convex resource allocation problem under a Bayesian model where each candidate's success probability follows a Beta prior parameterized by its PRM score and a confidence parameter κ. Applying KKT conditions yields a closed-form optimal allocation that interpolates between temperature sampling (κ→0) and beam search (κ→∞), with REBASE emerging as the optimal solution-level strategy in the low-confidence regime. The key insight is that solution-level allocation is provably suboptimal when candidates share reasoning directions, as it implicitly weights directions by their candidate count (Proposition 2). DORA corrects this by computing a soft semantic clustering via row-wise softmax over cosine similarity of BGE-M3 embeddings, using each solution's diagonal affinity as a uniqueness score to reweight PRM-based quality weights, thereby decoupling direction quality from candidate count and recovering the provably optimal direction-level allocation (Theorem 1).

## Results

On MATH500 with LLaMA-3.2-1B-Instruct, DORA achieves 68.7% accuracy using only 64 rollouts, surpassing REBASE's 67.4% at 256 rollouts while reducing total FLOPs by 3.5× and inference latency by 4×. On AIME2024, DORA matches REBASE's 14.7% accuracy at 64 rollouts versus REBASE's 256 rollouts. Across the broader benchmarks (HMMT24, HMMT25, AMC23, AMC24) at N=64, DORA achieves the highest accuracy for every policy model, e.g., 6.7% on HMMT24 and 31.1% on AMC24 with LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct, compared to REBASE's 4.7% and 27.5% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DORA consistently outperforms all four baselines across every policy model and benchmark. Against Temperature Sampling (Brown et al., 2024), DORA gains 10–20+ points of accuracy, confirming that reward-guided allocation is essential. Against Beam Search (Snell et al., 2024) and DVTS (Beeching et al., 2024), DORA achieves 2–5 points higher accuracy on MATH500 and AIME benchmarks, with the advantage growing at larger rollout budgets. The most competitive comparison is against REBASE (Wu et al., 2025a), the strongest solution-level baseline: DORA surpasses REBASE by 1.3 points on MATH500 (68.7 vs. 67.4) using only 64 rollouts versus REBASE's 256, and by 3.6 points on AMC24 with LLaMA-3.2-3B (31.1 vs. 27.5). The gap between DORA and REBASE widens with increasing rollout budget, which the authors attribute to growing solution redundancy that solution-level allocation fails to correct.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the parallel test-time search literature (Snell et al., 2024; Beeching et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2025a), which introduced Beam Search, DVTS, and REBASE as heuristic allocation strategies. It departs from this line by formulating the allocation problem as a convex optimization under a Bayesian Beta-prior model, showing via KKT conditions that existing methods are limiting cases of a single optimal rule parameterized by reward confidence κ. The key conceptual departure is identifying that all prior methods operate at the solution level, which provably biases allocation toward overrepresented reasoning directions—a structural flaw absent from the theoretical analysis of any prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, provably optimal allocation strategy for test-time search, replacing ad-hoc heuristics with a unified Bayesian framework that practitioners can reason about. The practical impact is notable: DORA matches or exceeds REBASE's accuracy at 1/4 the rollout budget (3.5× fewer FLOPs, 4× lower latency), which matters for deployment cost. However, the analysis assumes a low-confidence regime (κ→0) and a static PRM signal, leaving open how allocation should adapt as confidence accumulates over multi-step reasoning. Extension beyond mathematical reasoning and beyond PRM-based scoring (e.g., to code generation or open-ended tasks) remains unexplored.
