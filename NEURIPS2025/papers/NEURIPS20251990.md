## Problem

Existing simplicial neural networks rely on discrete polynomial filters of Hodge Laplacians, which fix the receptive field per layer and offer limited control over over-smoothing and robustness to structural perturbations. No continuous filtering method had been proposed for learning on simplicial complexes, leaving a gap between the well-studied continuous GNN literature and the discrete-only SNN landscape.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251990/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

COSIMO consumes a simplicial complex with multi-dimensional signals defined on k-simplices (nodes, edges, triangles) and their incidence matrices B_k. The exponential Hodge filters are computed via truncated eigenvalue decomposition of the lower and upper Hodge Laplacians, selecting K dominant eigenvalue-eigenvector pairs (chosen by cross-validation or spectral entropy). Outputs are task-dependent: node/graph class labels, regression values for partial mesh shapes, or predicted trajectory positions, all produced through standard supervised training with learnable linear projections and a nonlinearity at each layer.

## Method

COSIMO replaces discrete polynomial Hodge filters with continuous exponential filters derived from a system of heat-diffusion PDEs on simplicial complexes, featuring independent lower dynamics (∂x/∂t_d = -L_{k,d}x), independent upper dynamics (∂x/∂t_u = -L_{k,u}x), and a joint coupling term. The l-th layer applies e^{-t_d L_{k,d}} and e^{-t_u L_{k,u}} to the simplicial signal and its lower/upper projections (B_k^T X_{k-1} and B_{k+1} X_{k+1}), followed by learnable linear projections and a nonlinearity. Crucially, the receptive fields t_d and t_u are differentiable learnable parameters, providing continuous control over information propagation range—unlike discrete SNNs where polynomial orders T_d, T_u must be manually tuned. The paper proves stability under additive incidence-matrix perturbations (error bounded by O(ε_k) + O(ε_{k+1})) and shows that adjusting t directly controls the over-smoothing convergence rate, a flexibility absent in the discrete setting.

## Results

On node classification, COSIMO achieves 90.0% accuracy on high-school and 69.0% on senate-bills, outperforming the best simplicial baselines (SCCNN at 88.0% and 64.0%, respectively). On graph classification (proteins), COSIMO reaches 79.0% accuracy, surpassing SaNN (77.0%) and GSAN (77.0%). On the Shrec-16 mesh regression benchmark, COSIMO achieves the lowest MSE of 0.010 (small) and 0.027 (full), roughly halving the error of the next-best method SCCNN (0.020 and 0.063). On trajectory prediction, COSIMO attains 55.0% accuracy on ocean-drifts, marginally ahead of SCCNN (54.5%), though SCNN leads on the synthetic dataset (67.7% vs. 65.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSIMO outperforms all 14 baselines on the Shrec-16 mesh regression task, achieving 0.010 MSE (small) and 0.027 MSE (full) versus SCCNN's 0.020 and 0.063, respectively—a 2× and 2.3× improvement. On node classification, COSIMO beats the strongest simplicial baselines SCCNN (88.0%) and GSAN (88.0%) on high-school by 2 percentage points, and surpasses GCN (67.0%) and SCCNN (64.0%) on senate-bills by 2 and 5 points. On graph classification (proteins), COSIMO (79.0%) edges out SaNN (77.0%) and GSAN (77.0%) while substantially outperforming all GNN baselines (57–61%). The one notable exception is the synthetic trajectory prediction dataset, where SCNN (67.7%) slightly outperforms COSIMO (65.9%), which the authors attribute to zero-valued node and triangle features making inter-simplicial couplings ineffective. Overall, COSIMO consistently matches or exceeds the best simplicial baselines (SCCNN, SaNN, GSAN) across all tasks, with the largest margins on mesh regression and senate-bills node classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

COSIMO extends the continuous GNN paradigm (PDE-based heat diffusion on graphs, as in Han et al. 2024) to simplicial complexes, where prior work has been exclusively discrete. It builds directly on the SCCNN framework (Yang et al., TMLR 2025), which decoupled lower and upper Hodge Laplacians for discrete polynomial filtering, by replacing fixed-order polynomial powers with matrix exponentials governed by learnable continuous time parameters. The original SNN architecture (Ebli et al., 2020) and subsequent Hodge-aware variants (SCNN, SCoNe, Bunch) all rely on discrete simplicial filters; COSIMO is positioned as the first method to model continuous dynamics over simplicial complexes, filling a gap explicitly identified in the related work section.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical stability guarantees for SNNs under simplicial perturbations (linear in perturbation size for small errors) and a detailed over-smoothing analysis showing that the continuous receptive field t acts as a tunable knob for controlling the rate of feature homogenization—something discrete SNNs cannot achieve without manually changing polynomial order. Practical limitations include the reliance on eigenvalue decomposition (O(KN²) per layer), which constrains scalability, and the fact that empirical gains over SCCNN are modest on most benchmarks (though more pronounced on Shrec-16 mesh regression). Open questions include whether the EVD requirement can be replaced by cheaper approximations (the authors mention implicit Euler and Cholesky), and whether the over-smoothing control generalizes to higher-dimensional complexes beyond 2-simplices.
