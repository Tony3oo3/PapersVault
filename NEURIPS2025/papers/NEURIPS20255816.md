## Problem

In the low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) regime, standard gradient descent on neural networks overfits to noise before learning the signal, leading to harmful overfitting and poor generalization. While prior work identified this transition and proposed fixes like sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) or graph convolution smoothing, no simple, zero-overhead modification of GD was shown to provably improve generalization in this regime. This paper asks whether injecting random label noise into the gradient updates can serve as an implicit regularizer that suppresses noise memorization while preserving signal learning.

## Input / Output

In the theoretical model, each input is a 2d-dimensional vector split into two d-dimensional patches: one contains the label-dependent signal y·μ and the other contains Gaussian noise ξ orthogonal to μ, with SNR = ‖μ‖₂/(σ_p√d). The two-layer convolutional network with squared ReLU activation and shared filters maps this to a scalar score, and the binary label is recovered via sign. Empirically, the method is validated on CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB images) with VGG-16 where SNR is modulated by adding noise to high-frequency Fourier components, and on synthetic data matching the theoretical signal-noise model.

## Method

The proposed algorithm, label noise GD, randomly flips each training label with probability p at every iteration before computing the logistic-loss gradient, requiring no architectural change or extra forward/backward passes. The key theoretical insight is that this label flipping introduces a supermartingale structure on the noise memorization coefficients: in the second training phase, noise memorization is confined to oscillate within a constant range (bounded via Azuma's inequality), while signal learning coefficients continue to grow exponentially until they dominate the network output. The analysis uses a two-stage decomposition with squared ReLU (2-homogeneous) activation, which yields a distinct low-SNR boundary (n⁻¹SNR⁻² = Õ(1)) different from standard ReLU analyses. The result shows label noise GD achieves vanishing test error (O(exp(−Cd/n²))) in the low SNR regime where standard GD provably incurs a non-vanishing Ω(1) test error despite reaching arbitrarily small training loss.

## Results

The main results are theoretical: under the low SNR regime (n⁻¹SNR⁻² = Ω̃(1)), standard GD drives the logistic training loss to ≤ ε but the 0-1 test error remains bounded below by 0.24 (Theorem 3.2), while label noise GD keeps the training loss at Θ(1) yet achieves a vanishing test error of at most 2exp(−Cd/n²) (Theorem 3.3). Empirically on CIFAR-10 with VGG-16, label noise GD consistently outperforms standard GD, and the accuracy gap widens as the SNR decreases (Figure 1). In synthetic experiments (n=200, d=2000, p=0.1), the heatmap (Figure 3) confirms that standard GD fails to generalize when SNR = O(n^{-1/2}), whereas label noise GD achieves near-perfect test accuracy across a broader range of SNR values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between standard GD and label noise GD on the same two-layer convolutional network with squared ReLU activation. Standard GD achieves arbitrarily small training loss but suffers harmful overfitting with a non-vanishing test error lower bound of 0.24, while label noise GD trades a constant training loss for a vanishing test error. Compared to Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) [10], label noise GD requires a weaker signal-strength condition: SAM needs ‖μ‖₂ ≥ Ω̃(1) (a sufficiently strong signal), whereas label noise GD generalizes without this requirement, making it robust even when the signal is considerably weaker than the noise. Additionally, label noise GD introduces no computational overhead beyond standard GD (no extra forward/backward passes), unlike SAM which requires two forward and backward passes per step. The paper also notes that graph convolution smoothing [25] mitigates noise overfitting but requires the graph to be sufficiently dense with high homophily, a structural assumption not needed here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the signal-noise feature learning framework of Cao et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and Kou et al. (ICML 2023) by introducing label noise into the gradient descent updates, building on the label noise SGD literature (HaoChen et al., COLT 2021; Damian et al., NeurIPS 2021) that previously focused on linear regression or shallow regression settings. The key departure is a rigorous two-stage analysis of how random label flipping suppresses noise memorization via a supermartingale argument while preserving exponential signal growth, in a binary classification setting with squared ReLU activation. The paper also positions itself against sharpness-aware minimization (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2023), showing that label noise GD achieves comparable or stronger generalization guarantees without requiring a strong signal norm condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a theoretically grounded, zero-overhead modification to standard GD that provably prevents harmful overfitting in low SNR regimes where standard GD fails, offering a simple alternative to more complex methods like SAM. However, the analysis is restricted to a two-layer convolutional network with squared ReLU activation and a specific SNR scaling (n·SNR² ≪ 1), leaving open whether the mechanism extends to deeper architectures, standard ReLU, SGD, or adaptive optimizers. The empirical validation is limited to synthetic data and a small CIFAR-10 subset, so the practical transferability of the label-flipping trick to real-world training pipelines remains an open question.
