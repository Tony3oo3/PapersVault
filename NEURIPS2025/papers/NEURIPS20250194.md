## Problem

Continual instruction tuning of LLMs suffers from catastrophic forgetting, and existing pseudo-rehearsal methods either require extra generation modules that inflate trainable parameters or produce self-synthesized samples that poorly align with real task distributions, exhibit unstable outputs, and ignore inter-task relationships. The paper addresses how to generate rehearsal samples that faithfully reflect prior-task knowledge while dynamically balancing knowledge transfer against forgetting based on task similarity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250194/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of real samples (1% of prior-task data) and a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA2-7B or ChatGLM-6B) to synthesize pseudo rehearsal samples equivalent to 10% of the training set, then fine-tunes via LoRA on the new task data combined with these pseudo samples. Pseudo inputs are generated by the frozen base model LLM⁰ filling semantic masks, and pseudo labels are produced by blending outputs from both the base model and the current fine-tuned model, so no additional generation architecture is needed. The final output is a continually updated LLM that retains prior-task performance while absorbing new instruction-following capabilities.

## Method

SERS decouples pseudo-input synthesis from label creation in three stages. First, Semantic-Guided Pseudo-Input Generation uses a real sample as a structural template and a second masked sample as semantic context, passing both through the frozen base model LLM⁰ to fill masks and produce diverse pseudo inputs without any extra generation module; varying mask ratios and template combinations control diversity. Second, Label Self-Evolution operates in two sub-stages: self-questioning identifies over-specialized samples by flagging the top-k% pseudo inputs where LLM⁰ and LLM⁰ outputs diverge most, and self-evolution relabels those samples via a weighted blend (coefficient α) of base-model and fine-tuned-model outputs to prevent rehearsal from overfitting to narrow domain knowledge. Third, a dynamic regularization term L_reg = ½Σ‖θᵢ‖² is added to the cross-entropy loss with a strength λ modulated by the Wasserstein distance W between old and new task distributions: when W is small (similar tasks), λ relaxes to permit knowledge transfer, while large W or low rehearsal ratio r_replay tightens the constraint to protect prior knowledge, formalized as λ = [λ_min + (λ_max − λ_min)(1 − e^(−W/W_th))] · (1 − r_replay).

## Results

On the SuperNI benchmark, SERS achieves an average Rouge-L (AR) of 52.92% on LLaMA2-7B (5 tasks) and 63.78% (10 tasks), with the lowest backward transfer (BWT) of −0.48 and −1.70 respectively, closely matching the Multi-Task Learning (MTL) upper bound of 53.07% and 64.72%. On ChatGLM-6B, SERS reaches 49.98% (5 tasks) and 63.23% (10 tasks), surpassing MTL (48.92% and 62.04%) in both settings. SERS outperforms the strongest pseudo-rehearsal baseline SSR by 2.16% AR on LLaMA2-7B (5 tasks) and by 1.17% on ChatGLM-6B (5 tasks), while maintaining stable performance across all three task orders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SERS consistently outperforms SSR (Huang et al., 2024), the strongest pseudo-rehearsal baseline, across all four model/task settings: by 1.00% AR on LLaMA2-7B 5 tasks (52.92 vs 51.92), 0.87% on ChatGLM-6B 5 tasks (49.98 vs 49.11), 1.35% on LLaMA2-7B 10 tasks (63.78 vs 62.43), and 0.98% on ChatGLM-6B 10 tasks (63.23 vs 62.25), while also reducing BWT by roughly 0.9–1.1 points in each case. Against SAPT (Zhao et al., 2024), the architecture-based method, SERS gains 2.34% AR on LLaMA2-7B 5 tasks and 2.04% on ChatGLM-6B 10 tasks, with substantially lower forgetting (BWT −0.48 vs −3.70 on LLaMA2-7B 5 tasks). Notably, SERS surpasses the MTL upper bound on ChatGLM-6B in both 5-task (49.98 vs 48.92) and 10-task (63.23 vs 62.04) settings, attributed to ChatGLM's global attention and 2D positional encoding causing task confusion under joint training. On LLaMA2-7B, SERS remains within 0.15–0.94% of MTL, confirming near-optimal knowledge retention without requiring access to all task data simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

SERS extends the self-synthesized rehearsal line (Huang et al., 2024) by decoupling pseudo-input generation from label creation: instead of full in-context learning that produces both inputs and outputs, it uses semantic masking and template guidance with the base model to generate only inputs, reducing cognitive load and improving structural fidelity. The label self-evolution module adapts the two-stage self-questioning/self-evolution paradigm from Zhong et al. (2022) (originally for pretraining efficiency) to the continual-learning setting, where it detects and smooths over-specialized pseudo labels. The dynamic regularization via Wasserstein distance between task distributions departs from static L2 or orthogonal-subspace constraints by making the stability-plasticity trade-off task-order-aware, a direction not previously explored in LLM continual instruction tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SERS offers a practical, lightweight recipe for continual instruction tuning that avoids both the storage burden of real-sample replay and the parameter overhead of extra generation modules, making it attractive for deployment scenarios where historical data is inaccessible. However, the evaluation is confined to a single benchmark (SuperNI) and two 6–7B models, with no error bars or multiple seeds, limiting confidence in the reported 2-point gains. Open questions the authors flag include whether pseudo samples can inject knowledge beyond the original training distribution and how task interactions at the parameter level drive per-task gains or losses—insights that would be needed before the approach can be trusted at scale or across diverse task taxonomies.
