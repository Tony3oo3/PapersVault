## Problem

LLMs exhibit a persistent multilingual reasoning gap, with performance heavily concentrated in high-resource languages like English and Chinese while degrading sharply in low- and mid-resource languages. Drawing on cognitive neuroscience evidence that human reasoning operates largely independently of language processing, the paper hypothesizes that LLMs similarly encode reasoning and language as separable components in their activation spaces, and that explicitly disentangling them can transfer reasoning ability across languages without additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251290/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multilingual reasoning prompt (math, commonsense, or knowledge QA) in any of 11 typologically diverse languages and operates on the LLM's hidden states during inference. A pre-computed language-specific subspace M_s (derived via SVD from mean final-token embeddings across all target languages) is projected out of mid-layer hidden states using a tunable ablation coefficient λ. The output is a reasoning response generated from the modified, language-agnostic representations; top-layer language features are deliberately preserved to maintain input-output language fidelity, and the entire pipeline runs training-free within the vLLM inference framework.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free causal intervention that disentangles language-specific signals from reasoning representations in LLM activation spaces. First, a language-specific subspace M_s is identified by decomposing the multilingual mean-embedding matrix M into a low-rank language-agnostic component M_a and a language-specific component M_s via SVD, following the common-specific low-rank decomposition formulation of Piratla et al. and Xie et al. Second, at inference time, for each hidden state h at the target layer, the projection ĥ = h − λ M_sᵀ M_s h removes the language-specific component, yielding a more language-invariant representation that better reflects the underlying reasoning process. Layer-wise analysis reveals that mid-layer intervention provides the best trade-off: it substantially improves reasoning accuracy while preserving output fidelity, whereas upper-layer ablation degrades linguistic coherence because top layers encode critical language-specific generation signals. The approach is validated across 10 open-weight LLMs (Qwen-2.5/3, DeepSeek-R1-Distill, GLM-Z1, QwQ-32B) and 3 benchmarks (MGSM, XWinograd, M-MMLU), showing consistent gains that match or exceed supervised fine-tuning and PPO-based reinforcement learning without any model modification.

## Results

Across 10 open-weight LLMs and 11 typologically diverse languages, language-reasoning disentanglement (L-R Disentangle) consistently improves multilingual reasoning. On MGSM, average accuracy gains range from +1.3 (Qwen-3-8B-Thinking: 84.15→85.42) to +2.4 (Qwen-2.5-7B: 69.82→70.76), with the largest absolute improvements in low-resource languages (e.g., Swahili and Telugu often double in accuracy). On XWinograd, Qwen-2.5-7B jumps from 68.13 to 74.00 average, and on M-MMLU from 61.25 to 63.88. In the direct comparison against post-training baselines (Table 3), the training-free intervention achieves 59.02 average MGSM accuracy, surpassing SFT (49.09) and approaching RL/PPO (60.33) without any additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the training-free L-R Disentangle intervention against two multilingual post-training baselines—supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (PPO)—on MGSM using Qwen-2.5-Instruct-3B. L-R Disentangle (59.02 avg) substantially outperforms SFT (49.09 avg), which actually degrades performance in most languages (e.g., Japanese drops from 56.0 to 30.0, Bengali from 35.2 to 10.0), likely due to translation noise and the small model's inability to mimic the 32B teacher's reasoning traces. Against RL/PPO (60.33 avg), L-R Disentangle is competitive, trailing by only 1.31 points on average while requiring zero additional training compute. Notably, RL achieves its best gains in low-resource languages (Bengali: 35.2→53.2), whereas L-R Disentangle provides more balanced improvements across the full resource spectrum. The authors conclude that the training-free intervention is a lightweight, scalable alternative to costly post-training pipelines and suggest that combining disentanglement with SFT or RL could yield further gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the common-specific low-rank subspace decomposition from domain generalization (Piratla et al., 2020) and multilingual representation analysis (Xie et al., 2022; Pires et al., 2019) into a causal inference-time intervention, following the representation engineering paradigm (Zou et al., 2023) of manipulating hidden states to alter model behavior. It departs from the dominant line of multilingual reasoning research—which focuses on training-time strategies such as multilingual data construction, cross-lingual distillation, and reinforcement learning—by proposing a training-free projection that removes language-specific components from mid-layer activations. The cognitive neuroscience motivation (Monti et al., 2009; Amalric & Dehaene, 2019; Fedorenko et al., 2024) that human reasoning operates largely independently of language provides the conceptual framing that prior LLM interpretability work has not explicitly leveraged for multilingual reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a lightweight, training-free projection at inference time achieves multilingual reasoning gains comparable to PPO-based reinforcement learning while completely avoiding the data-quality pitfalls of translated SFT—a finding that could reduce the cost barrier for deploying strong reasoning in low-resource languages. The mechanistic insight that language-specific activation negatively correlates with reasoning accuracy (and that upper layers must retain language signals for output fidelity) provides a diagnostic tool for understanding cross-lingual failure modes. Open questions include why English serves as the convergence anchor even in Chinese-bilingual models, whether the approach extends to non-reasoning tasks like planning or explanation, and how projection-based disentanglement might be integrated into training objectives for more systematic gains.
