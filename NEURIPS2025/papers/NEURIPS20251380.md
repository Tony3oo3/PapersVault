## Problem

Mainstream test-time compute scaling for language models relies on verbalized chain-of-thought, requiring specialized long-form training data, very long context windows, and constraining all reasoning to what can be expressed as discrete tokens. This paper asks whether a model can instead scale its test-time computation by iterating a recurrent block in continuous latent space, reasoning without verbalization and without any bespoke reasoning data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a standard text token sequence, which is embedded once by a prelude block and then injected at every recurrent iteration alongside the evolving latent state. The number of recurrent iterations r is a free test-time parameter (sampled from a log-normal Poisson distribution during training), so the same checkpoint can be run at different compute budgets. The latent state is initialized from a random Gaussian, making the output stochastic even at fixed r, and the coda block decodes the final state into a next-token probability distribution over the vocabulary.

## Method

The architecture is a decoder-only transformer split into three groups — a prelude (2 layers), a core recurrent block (4 layers), and a coda (2 layers) — where the core block is iterated r times, unfolding to an effective depth of 2+4r+2 layers. Each iteration of the core block concatenates the previous latent state with the embedded input through an adapter matrix, processes them through transformer layers with a 'sandwich' RMSNorm ordering, and outputs an updated state. Training samples r from a log-normal Poisson distribution per sequence and backpropagates through only the last k=8 iterations (truncated backpropagation), keeping memory independent of r. The 3.5B-parameter model (hidden size 5280) is trained from scratch on 800B tokens with a constant learning rate, and at test time the model naturally supports zero-shot per-token adaptive compute via KL-divergence early exits, KV-cache sharing across recurrent steps, and self-speculative decoding by drafting with fewer iterations and verifying with more.

## Results

The 3.5B-parameter recurrent-depth model (0.8T tokens) shows strong test-time scaling: at r=32 iterations it achieves 69.91% ARC-E, 38.23% ARC-C, 65.21% HellaSwag, 31.38% MMLU, and 93.50% SciQ, compared to only 49.07% ARC-E and 23.39% MMLU at r=4. On math, the model reaches 28.05% flexible GSM8K and 12.58% Minerva MATH at r=32, surpassing OLMo-7B-0424 (27.07% GSM8K, 5.56% MATH). On code, it achieves 23.17% HumanEval and 24.80% MBPP, beating all general-purpose open-source models of comparable size but trailing dedicated code models like StarCoder2-3B (31.09% HumanEval). A non-recurrent baseline with the same architecture and 180B tokens scores only 1.82/2.20 on GSM8K CoT, while the recurrent model at the same checkpoint already reaches 9.02/10.24, a 5× advantage on harder tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Pythia-2.8B (2.8B params, 0.3T tokens), the recurrent model at r=32 wins on every standard benchmark, e.g. 69.91% vs 58.00% on ARC-E and 31.38% vs 25.05% on MMLU, despite using only 0.8T tokens. Against OLMo-7B (7B, 2.5T), the model is roughly competitive: it wins on MMLU (31.38 vs 28.39) and SciQ (93.50 vs 88.50) but trails on HellaSwag (65.21 vs 75.52) and WinoGrande (59.43 vs 67.09). Against the stronger OLMo-7B-0424 (7B, 2.05T) and OLMo-2-1124 (7B, 4T), the model lags substantially on most benchmarks (e.g. 31.38 vs 47.46 and 60.56 on MMLU), reflecting the gap in training data volume. On math, the model beats OLMo-7B-0424 on GSM8K (28.05 vs 27.07 flexible) and Minerva MATH (12.58 vs 5.56) but is far behind OLMo-2-1124 (66.72 GSM8K, 19.08 MATH). On code, the model outperforms all general-purpose open-source models on HumanEval (23.17% vs 20.12% for OLMo-7B-0724 and 10.36% for OLMo-2-1124) but is well below the dedicated StarCoder2-3B (31.09% HumanEval, 43.00% MBPP). The key differentiator is test-time scaling: increasing r from 4 to 32 yields a 20.8-point gain on ARC-E and 7.99 points on MMLU, a flexibility absent in fixed-depth baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper scales the recurrent-depth (looped/universal transformer) architecture from small-scale 'deep thinking' experiments (Schwarzschild et al., 2021; Bansal et al., 2022) to 3.5B parameters and 800B tokens, adopting their randomized unrolling and input-injection training recipe along with path-independent random state initialization from Anil et al. (2022). It departs from the dominant test-time scaling paradigm of verbalized chain-of-thought (OpenAI o1, DeepSeek-R1) by arguing that expensive internal computation need not be projected to discrete tokens, instead proposing latent recurrence as a 'third axis' of scaling alongside parameter count and CoT length. The architecture also connects to deep equilibrium models and latent diffusion in its fixed-point iteration structure, but differs in using truncated backpropagation through unrolled steps rather than implicit bilevel optimization or surrogate objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that recurrent-depth language models can be trained stably at multi-billion-parameter scale and show meaningful test-time compute scaling on realistic benchmarks, validating a long-standing architectural hypothesis. The zero-shot emergence of per-token adaptive compute, KV-cache sharing, and self-speculative decoding without any specialized training is practically significant, as these features typically require dedicated training or architectural modifications in standard transformers. Key open questions include whether this approach can match or exceed verbalized CoT at matched FLOP budgets, how it scales beyond 800B tokens, and whether the emergent geometric computation patterns (orbits, sliders) can be leveraged for targeted improvements. The paper is explicitly a proof-of-concept, and the model still lags behind larger, more carefully curated open-source models like OLMo-2 on most benchmarks.
