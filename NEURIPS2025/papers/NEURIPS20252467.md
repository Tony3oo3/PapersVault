## Problem

Existing publicly available airfoil CFD datasets are largely restricted to incompressible, fully turbulent flows with smooth field characteristics, overlooking the strongly nonlinear physics of laminar-turbulent transition and shock-wave interactions that dominate transonic aerodynamics. This limits the ability to train and benchmark ML surrogate models on realistic, multi-regime aerodynamic behavior. UniFoil addresses this gap by providing the largest publicly available dataset of 2D airfoil RANS simulations spanning both transitional and turbulent regimes across subsonic and transonic conditions.

## Input / Output

The ML model takes 17 POD modal coefficients (encoding airfoil shape) plus three flow-condition scalars (Mach, Re, AoA) as input and predicts the full 2D pressure-coefficient field on a reshaped O-grid of shape 84×292. The O-grid data is reordered into a uniform rectangular array without interpolation to preserve native grid resolution, and min/max Cp values from each simulation are appended to the latent representation to handle variable field magnitudes. The dataset itself also provides velocity vector fields, lift/drag coefficients, and for transition cases, skin-friction coefficients, turbulence intermittency, N-factor amplification data, and transition location markers.

## Method

Data generation uses the ADflow compressible RANS solver with Spalart-Allmaras turbulence modeling for fully turbulent cases and an e^N-based linear stability transition model coupled to SA for transitional cases, on structured O-type meshes generated by pyHyp's hyperbolic marching scheme. Airfoil geometries are sampled from a POD/SVD modal basis of classic airfoil shapes using Lattice HyperCube Sampling, yielding 30,000 fully turbulent and 4,800 natural laminar flow airfoils, each simulated at ~13 freestream conditions. The preliminary ML model is a two-stage encoder-decoder: a Conv2D autoencoder (two down-sampling blocks with MaxPooling, two up-sampling blocks with Conv2DTranspose) learns a 6132-dim latent representation of Cp fields, and a 5-layer DNN (6134 hidden units each) maps the 17 modal coefficients to that latent space, enabling direct geometry-to-field prediction. Training uses 80/20 splits, 100 epochs, batch size 32, ReLU activations, and [-1,1] normalization with per-simulation extrema appended to the latent vector.

## Results

UniFoil contains over 500,000 RANS simulations across 34,800 airfoils (30,000 fully turbulent + 4,800 NLF), spanning Mach [0.1, 0.85], Re [1, 10]×10⁶, and AoA [−2°, 6°]. The preliminary ML benchmark uses an encoder-decoder + DNN architecture trained on pressure coefficient (Cp) fields; across 400,000 FT-airfoil test simulations, roughly 35,000 cases exhibit the highest relative L₂ norm error of approximately 10⁻¹, concentrated near shock-wave regions. The model reliably captures overall Cp field structure and shock locations but shows elevated errors in the background field due to non-uniform O-grid resolution and small-magnitude Cp values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniFoil is the only dataset among the seven compared that simultaneously includes both laminar-turbulent transition modeling (e^N method) and compressible flow (Mach up to 0.85), a combination absent from all six baselines. In terms of scale, UniFoil (34,800 airfoils, 500,000 simulations) exceeds the next largest dataset, Ramos et al. 2023a (1,830 airfoils, 250,000 simulations), by roughly 19× in airfoil count and 2× in simulation count. Cornelius et al. 2025 (PALMO) is the only other compressible dataset (Mach [0.25, 0.9]) but lacks transition modeling and covers only 52,480 simulations across 18,000 airfoils. Schillaci et al. 2021 and Bonnet et al. 2022 (AIRFRANS) are limited to incompressible, fully turbulent flows with no transition physics, and provide only 425 and 1,000 simulations respectively. Ramos et al. 2023a and 2023b include transition modeling but are restricted to incompressible flow at a fixed Mach of 0.1, limiting their applicability to transonic regimes where shock waves dominate. Agarwal et al. 2024 provides only integral coefficients (no field data) for 2,900 airfoils, making it unsuitable for field-level ML tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of open-access airfoil CFD datasets (Schillaci et al. 2021, AirfRANS/Bonnet et al. 2022, Ramos et al. 2023a/b, Cornelius et al. 2025) by being the first to simultaneously incorporate compressible flow (Mach up to 0.85), shock-wave resolution, and laminar-turbulent transition modeling at a scale of 500,000+ simulations. It builds on the ADflow solver, pyHyp hyperbolic meshing, POD-based geometry sampling (Li et al. 2019), and the e^N transition framework (Shi et al. 2020), all previously validated tools, but combines them in a way no prior dataset has. The ML surrogate component (encoder-decoder + DNN) follows standard architectures already applied to smaller incompressible airfoil datasets, with the novelty lying in the data rather than the model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniFoil fills a critical gap: no prior public dataset captured both shock-wave discontinuities and laminar-turbulent transition, two strongly nonlinear phenomena that are central to transonic and natural laminar flow design. The preliminary results confirm that standard CNN-based surrogates struggle with shock fronts (localized high L2 error) and weakly varying background fields, providing a concrete benchmark for physics-aware architectures. Key open questions include whether ML models can generalize across the sharp regime boundaries (laminar→transitional→turbulent, subsonic→transonic) and whether the steady-state, 2D, unvalidated nature of the data limits transfer to real 3D unsteady applications. The dataset's scale and permissive licensing make it likely to become a standard benchmark for ML-for-CFD research.
