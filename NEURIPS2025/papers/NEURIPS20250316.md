## Problem

Both full-data visual instruction tuning and existing state-of-the-art data selection methods for large multimodal models inherit underlying dataset biases such as position bias and spurious correlations, causing significant performance degradation under minor input perturbations (e.g., shuffling answer choices or replacing option letters). No prior data selection approach explicitly targets robustness against these biases while avoiding the computational cost of gradient-based influence estimation or the need for downstream task data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250316/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the full LLaVA-665K multi-turn conversation corpus and a warmed-up proxy model (LLaVA-1.5-7B, trained on 1000 random samples for 4 epochs) as input. Each conversation is encoded into a 2×d conversation vector by concatenating the last-token hidden state with an attention-score-weighted sum of all preceding visual and textual token embeddings, requiring only a single forward pass. The output is a robust training mixture (e.g., 30% of the original data) that, when used for LoRA-based visual instruction tuning, yields a model with substantially improved robustness against symbol attacks, permutation attacks, and visual spurious correlations while matching full-data clean accuracy.

## Method

ARDS constructs a robust training mixture in three stages. First, it builds a conversation vector database using an attention-score-weighted aggregation mechanism that captures cross-turn and text-vision dependencies more richly than last-token embeddings. Second, it performs spherical K-means clustering (K=70) in the embedding space to partition conversations into semantically coherent subgroups, then applies dual perturbations—diffusion noise on images and task-aware textual perturbations (symbol replacement and adversarial permutation)—to identify the top-B samples per cluster with the largest loss difference, forming worst-case evaluation subgroups near the decision boundary. Third, it scores each training sample by its average cosine similarity to each worst-case subgroup, aggregated via a softmax-weighted scheme where weights are proportional to exp(average subgroup loss), so that harder subgroups contribute more to the final score. The highest-scoring samples are retained as the robust training mixture. Unlike gradient-based methods (LESS, D3M), ARDS requires only one forward pass per sample and no downstream few-shot examples, reducing selection time from ~128 hours to ~30 minutes on 8 GPUs.

## Results

Using only 30% of the LLaVA-665K training data, ARDS achieves the highest robust accuracy (average across clean, permutation, symbol, and combined attacks) on all eight evaluation benchmarks, surpassing the full-data (100%) model on most tasks. Headline numbers include 61.21% avg. on ScienceQA (vs. 56.87% Full), 46.80% on SEED-Bench (vs. 44.69% Full), 72.95% on A-OKVQA (vs. 71.54% Full), and 42.29% on BoolQ (vs. 16.50% Full). The paper reports up to 20.62% robust accuracy improvement over the strongest data-selection baseline on multimodal tasks. Cross-model transfer experiments show the 7B-curated mixture also boosts the 13B target model's robustness by 1.93–3.70% under the strongest combined attack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARDS outperforms all three baselines on every one of the eight benchmarks. Against LESS (gradient-based targeted selection), ARDS gains +5.28 on ScienceQA (61.21 vs. 55.93), +6.14 on SEED-Bench (46.80 vs. 40.66), and +7.02 on BoolQ (42.29 vs. 35.27), while requiring no downstream few-shot examples and no per-sample gradient computation (30 min vs. 128 h for the gradient store). Against COINCIDE (feature-based diversity selection), ARDS leads by +8.94 on ScienceQA, +7.22 on SEED-Bench, and +9.66 on BoolQ, indicating that targeting worst-case subgroups is more effective than maximizing diversity alone. Against RHO-LOSS (excess-loss scoring), the margins are largest: +15.77 on ScienceQA and +19.64 on BoolQ, suggesting that fitting a proxy model on a small held-out set is intrinsically unreliable for LMMs. Notably, ARDS with only 30% data also exceeds the full-data model's robust accuracy on ScienceQA (+4.34), SEED-Bench (+2.11), MMBench-EN (+3.64), MMBench-CN (+4.17), A-OKVQA (+1.41), MMMU (+1.96), ARC-e (+7.09), and BoolQ (+25.79), demonstrating that the selection strategy removes bias-inducing samples rather than merely preserving coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARDS extends the targeted data selection paradigm introduced by LESS (gradient-based influence functions for instruction tuning) and the feature-based selection of COINCIDE, but departs from both by replacing gradient computation with attention-weighted conversation vectors and by shifting the selection objective from quality/diversity to robustness against worst-case subgroups. The robustness-aware selection objective draws on D3M's worst-group loss minimization for NLP, but ARDS is the first to apply this principle to large multimodal models and to do so without gradient access or downstream few-shot examples. The method also builds on the LLaVA visual instruction tuning framework and incorporates perturbation strategies (diffusion noise, permutation/symbol attacks) inspired by adversarial robustness literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that robustness against dataset biases (position bias, spurious correlation) can be substantially improved through data curation alone—without modifying the training pipeline, adding inference-time calibration, or requiring downstream task data. The cross-model transferability (7B proxy curating data for 13B target) and the 30-minute selection time versus LESS's 128 hours make this approach accessible for practitioners. Open questions include whether the robustness gains generalize to bias types beyond position and spurious correlation, whether the approach holds under truly dynamic/streaming data arrival, and whether the specific perturbation choices (diffusion noise, brute-force permutations) are optimal or merely sufficient.
