## Problem

Prior theoretical work on learning branch-and-cut (B&C) policies has established sample complexity guarantees only for linear scoring functions, while modern practice increasingly deploys neural networks (e.g., MLPs with ReLU activations) for node, cut, and branching decisions. This paper addresses the gap by providing the first generalization guarantees for B&C policies whose scoring functions have a general piecewise polynomial structure, unifying the linear-model theory with the neural-network models used in contemporary empirical studies.

## Input / Output

Each scoring function f_k(s, a, w^k) takes a state-action pair (s, a), maps it through a fixed feature extractor φ_k to a feature vector in R^{ℓ_k}, and combines it with learnable parameters w^k ∈ W_k to produce a real-valued score; the selected action is the argmax over the candidate set A_k^s (bounded by ρ_k). The overall cost V(I, w) is the accumulated penalty over M sequential decision rounds (e.g., B&C tree size when penalties are set to 0, 1, 2 for node/cut/branch actions). The theoretical output is a pseudo-dimension bound on the cost function class V = {V(·, w) : w ∈ W}, which via standard uniform convergence yields a distribution-independent sample complexity guarantee of O(H√((Pdim + log(1/δ))/N)).

## Method

The core technical device is a (Γ, γ, β)-structure (Definition 3.1): a function class whose members, for any finite collection of N functions, are piecewise polynomials of degree ≤ β over at most N^γ Γ regions of the parameter space. The key theorem (Theorem 3.3) proves that if each of the d scoring function classes F_k^* has a (Γ_k, γ_k, β_k)-structure, then the overall cost function V(I, ·) is piecewise constant in w, with a (Γ', γ', 0)-structure whose parameters depend on M, ρ_k, Γ_k, γ_k, β_k, and W. A sign-pattern counting argument (Lemma 3.2, building on polynomial sign-pattern bounds from Anthony & Bartlett) then converts this structure into a pseudo-dimension bound. Lemma 3.7 establishes that MLPs with piecewise polynomial activations satisfy the required structure, with Γ depending exponentially on depth and polynomially on width, γ = LW, and β = Lα^L; for ReLU (α=1, p=2) this simplifies substantially. The resulting bounds are O(WM Σ log ρ_k) for linear policies (improving on Balcan et al. 2021 by removing a W log W term) and O((Σ L_k W_k)(M Σ log ρ_k + log Σ U_k)) for ReLU MLPs. A complementary data-dependent bound via empirical Rademacher complexity (Proposition 3.9) replaces the worst-case action-count ρ_k ρ̄^M with the empirically observed sum of distinct state-action pairs Q_{M,k}(I_i), yielding tighter guarantees when the actual search tree is much smaller than the worst case.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it establishes that piecewise polynomial scoring functions (including linear and ReLU MLP policies) yield a piecewise constant cost function, enabling pseudo-dimension bounds. For linear scoring, Pdim(V^L) = O(WM Σ log ρ_k), improving on Balcan et al. [2021c] by removing a W log W term. For ReLU MLPs, Pdim(V^ReLU) = O((Σ L_k W_k)(M Σ log ρ_k + log(Σ U_k))). Empirically, the generalization gap f(N) on packing instances (m=10,n=20 and m=15,n=30) fits the predicted O(1/√N) rate with R²=0.951 and R²=0.959 respectively over 30 trials for N∈[1,100].

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper without a traditional method-vs-method comparison table. The key theoretical improvement is over Balcan et al. [2021c, Theorem 5.2], whose linear scoring bound included an additional W log W term; the present paper's Proposition 3.4 removes this term, yielding Pdim(V^L) = O(WM Σ log ρ_k) versus the prior O(WM Σ log ρ_k + W log W). The framework also extends coverage to ReLU MLP scoring functions (Proposition 3.8), a setting not addressed by the prior linear-only theory. The empirical validation in Section 5 does not compare against competing learned policies; instead it confirms that the observed generalization gap decays at the O(1/√N) rate predicted by the theoretical upper bounds, with high R² values (0.951 and 0.959) on two packing instance sets from Tang et al. [2020], suggesting the theoretical constants are of practical magnitude.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC learning framework for B&C algorithm configuration initiated by Balcan et al. (2024b, 2021c, 2021a, 2022b), which established sample complexity bounds for linear scoring policies via piecewise constant structure. The key conceptual advance is the (Γ,γ,β)-piecewise polynomial structure (Definition 3.1), which subsumes the prior piecewise constant analysis of linear policies and, via Lemma 3.7 (building on Anthony & Bartlett 1999 and Bartlett et al. 2019), also captures ReLU MLP scoring functions used in recent empirical work (Huang et al. 2022, Paulus et al. 2022, Yilmaz & Yorke-Smith 2021). The paper also improves the linear-case pseudo-dimension bound over Balcan et al. [2021c, Theorem 5.2] by removing a W log W term, and generalizes the analysis to a broader sequential decision framework beyond B&C.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first distribution-independent generalization guarantees for neural network-based B&C policies, bridging a gap between the predominantly linear-policy theory and the increasingly prevalent use of ReLU MLPs in practice. The empirical validation (R² ≈ 0.95 for the predicted O(1/√N) convergence) suggests the theoretical constants are of practical magnitude for packing instances, lending credibility to the bounds. Open questions include characterizing the expressive power and approximation limits of parameterized policies, understanding how expert oracle quality affects learned policy performance, extending to infinite action spaces (e.g., CGF-based cut generation), and incorporating stochasticity in state transitions.
