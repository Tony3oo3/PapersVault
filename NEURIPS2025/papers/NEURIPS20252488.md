## Problem

Existing latent graph diffusion models embed node, edge, and graph-level features into a shared Euclidean latent space, ignoring the curvature heterogeneity inherent in graph data and causing geometric feature entanglement. While Riemannian approaches could capture these distinct manifold signatures, they suffer from numerical instability in exponential/logarithmic mappings and manifold deviation during diffusion sampling, making them impractical for multi-level graph learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252488/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a graph G=(X, E) with node features X ∈ R^{N×d_n} and edge features E ∈ R^{N×N×d_e}, optionally conditioned on a graph property label Y_G. All tasks—unconditional molecular generation, conditional generation given a property, graph regression, and node classification—are unified as conditional generation in a shared latent space, with unconditional generation handled via k-means pseudo-labels on the graph-level embedding. Outputs include full molecular graphs (adjacency + node/edge features), scalar property values, or discrete node class labels.

## Method

GeoMancer replaces numerically unstable exponential maps with a Riemannian gyrokernel: a generalized Fourier mapping φ_gF(x) defined over a gyrovector ball G_κ^n with learnable curvature κ, which preserves isometric geometric invariants while keeping all representations in Euclidean space for standard diffusion. The encoder is an edge-enhanced graph transformer that produces multi-level embeddings, which are then endowed with product-manifold geometry via the gyrokernel and decoupled onto task-specific sub-manifolds (Euclidean, hyperbolic, spherical) using a decoupling module grounded in a Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunction product rule (Proposition 3.1). For generation, the model employs CFG++ (manifold-constrained classifier-free guidance) to project denoised samples back onto the clean data manifold, and introduces a self-guidance mechanism that clusters graph-level latent representations to produce pseudo-labels, reformulating unconditional generation as conditional generation so that all tasks share a single training objective.

## Results

On unconditional molecular generation (QM9), GeoMancer achieves 100% validity, 0.09 FCD, and 0.0002 NSPDK, setting new state-of-the-art on validity and distributional similarity metrics while maintaining 90.43% novelty. On conditional generation (QM9), it achieves the lowest MAE across all six molecular properties (e.g., 0.832 for dipole moment μ, 304 for ε_HOMO), outperforming LGD on every property. On node classification, GeoMancer reaches 97.05% on Photo, 98.78% on Physics, and 93.10% on PubMed, surpassing all GNN and Graph Transformer baselines. On ZINC12k graph regression, it achieves the lowest MAE among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On unconditional QM9 generation, GeoMancer achieves perfect 100% validity (vs. 99.69% for GruM and 98.46% for LGD) and the best FCD (0.09 vs. 0.11 for GruM), but trails GDSS (98.46%) and LGD (97.53%) on uniqueness and GraphDF (98.54%) on novelty. On conditional QM9 generation, GeoMancer beats LGD on all six properties (e.g., MAE 0.832 vs. 0.879 for μ; 304 vs. 313 for ε_HOMO) and also outperforms GeoLDM and EDM. On node classification, GeoMancer surpasses the strongest baselines NAGphormer (95.49% Photo, 97.34% Physics) and LGD (96.94% Photo, 98.55% Physics) on all five datasets, with the largest margin on Cora (83.50% vs. 82.84% for GraphGPS). On ZINC12k graph regression, GeoMancer outperforms all 10 baselines including GPS, CIN, and LGD, demonstrating that the Riemannian decoupling mechanism captures multi-level geometric structure more effectively than flat Euclidean latent spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoMancer extends the latent graph diffusion framework of LGD [15], which first unified generation and prediction under a single conditional-generation paradigm, by replacing the Euclidean latent space with a product Riemannian manifold. The Riemannian gyrokernel (generalized Fourier mapping in a gyrovector ball) is directly inherited from MotifRGC [18] and HyLA [29], which showed that isometric-invariant kernel mappings can substitute for numerically unstable exponential/logarithmic maps. The manifold-constrained sampling strategy builds on CFG++ [20], and the product-manifold decoupling draws on mixed-curvature representation learning [10, 12]. The key departure is the self-guidance mechanism, which converts unconditional graph generation into a conditional problem via k-means pseudo-labels on the geometric latent space, thereby unifying all task types under one conditional-generation objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical recipe for incorporating non-Euclidean geometric priors into graph diffusion models without the numerical instability that has plagued exponential-map-based Riemannian ML, which could make Riemannian graph learning more accessible. However, the ablation reveals that the headline Riemannian component contributes minimally to the most impressive result (novelty), with the large gap over LGD driven primarily by self-guidance and CFG++; the Riemannian geometry mainly improves distributional similarity (FCD, NSPDK) and convergence speed. Open questions include whether the geometric priors provide genuine inductive bias beyond added capacity, how the approach scales to larger graphs and more diverse domains beyond molecular and citation networks, and whether the product-manifold decoupling generalizes to tasks with more than three feature levels.
