## Problem

Inferring stochastic dynamics from cross-sectional single-cell snapshots is complicated by the fact that cells divide and die in addition to changing molecular state. Existing methods either ignore growth entirely, requiring prior knowledge of growth rates, or handle it through unbalanced optimal transport relaxations that lack a natural continuous-time dynamical interpretation. Neglecting growth leads to spurious drift toward fast-dividing branches, producing incorrect state transitions and gene regulatory network inferences.

## Input / Output

The method takes K+1 discrete cross-sectional snapshots (each a set of N_i sampled cell states from scRNA-seq or similar destructive assays) at successive times, along with a known diffusivity model (fixed for additive noise, or coupled to the drift for Chemical Langevin Equation multiplicative noise). It produces a time-dependent drift field v_t(x), a growth/fitness rate g_t(x), and sample SDE trajectories. Because the total cell count is not conserved, the inferred density is a measure rather than a probability density, and the method operates directly on discrete weighted point masses without ever computing continuous densities.

## Method

UPFI leverages the Lagrangian (probability flow) formulation of the Fokker-Planck equation with a source term, rewriting the drift-diffusion operator as a transport term whose phase-space velocity depends on the score ∇log ρ_t(x). This converts the d-dimensional PDE into a (d+1)-dimensional ODE system tracking both particle position x_t and mass m_t, where mass evolves as dm/dt = g_t(x_t)m_t. Training is a simple two-step procedure: (i) the score is estimated offline via denoising score matching (independent of dynamical parameters), and (ii) neural networks parameterizing the drift and growth are trained by integrating the ODE between successive snapshots and minimizing the unbalanced Sinkhorn divergence between inferred and observed discrete measures, regularized by a Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao energy term. Theoretical analysis in the OU-with-quadratic-fitness case proves that drift and growth are fundamentally non-identifiable without regularization, and that the proposed loss admits a unique minimizer in the continuous-time limit.

## Results

On the 10-dimensional bistable system, UPFI achieves a path energy distance of 0.05±0.04, substantially outperforming PFI (1.03±0.18), TIGON++ (0.61±0.06), and DeepRUOT (1.32±0.05). UPFI also attains the highest Pearson fate correlation (0.99±0.00) and the lowest force L2 error (4.31±0.08) at d=10. On the real monocyte-neutrophil lineage-tracing dataset, UPFI achieves the best fate prediction accuracy with Kendall's τ = 0.22±0.03 and Pearson r = 0.26±0.04, compared to PFI (0.07/0.09) and fitness-ODE (−0.02/−0.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPFI consistently outperforms PFI [14] across all dimensions and metrics: path energy distance is roughly 10–20× lower (e.g., 0.05 vs. 1.03 at d=10), and Pearson fate correlation is 0.99 vs. 0.65, confirming that ignoring growth causes PFI to misattribute proliferation to spurious drift. Against TIGON++ [24], UPFI achieves lower path energy distance (0.05 vs. 0.61 at d=10) and higher fate correlation (0.99 vs. 0.84), while TIGON++ produces only deterministic (binary) fate probabilities. DeepRUOT [27] is competitive on path energy distance at d=25 (0.14 vs. 0.22) but performs poorly at other dimensions and has the worst force L2 error (6.17 vs. 4.31 at d=10), likely due to its multi-stage training instability. UOTFM [26] achieves the best path energy distance at d=5 (0.36) but still trails UPFI (0.04) and has lower fate correlation (0.95 vs. 0.99). On the real monocyte-neutrophil dataset, UPFI and TIGON++ perform comparably on cosine distance to RNA velocity, but UPFI clearly wins on fate prediction (Kendall's τ = 0.22 vs. 0.19) and correctly identifies higher division rates in progenitor states, whereas fitness-ODE predicts the opposite.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Probability Flow Inference (PFI) [13, 14], which fits the Fokker-Planck equation in the Lagrangian frame to infer drift and noise from cross-sectional snapshots, by incorporating cellular birth and death into the model. The key methodological departure is the use of unbalanced Sinkhorn divergence as the training loss, which operates directly on discrete weighted measures and naturally accommodates non-conserved mass, avoiding the need for density estimation. The approach also draws on denoising score matching for the score estimation step and Neural ODEs for the dynamics parameterisation, while the theoretical analysis builds on the Schrödinger bridge literature for branching Brownian motion [16]. Compared to competing methods (TIGON, DeepRUOT, OTFM/UOTFM), UPFI's distinguishing feature is a simple two-step training scheme that jointly learns drift and growth without requiring prior growth estimates or multi-stage optimisation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability analysis (Corollary 2.2) provides a clean theoretical demonstration that drift and growth are fundamentally confounded even in the simplest OU setting, which has practical implications: without additional inductive biases (e.g., autonomous drift), no method can uniquely recover the gene regulatory network from snapshots alone. The empirical results on the Weinreb et al. lineage-tracing dataset show that ignoring growth leads to qualitatively wrong fate predictions (PFI biases toward monocyte lineage), validating the biological relevance of the modelling choice. Open questions include scalability to very high-dimensional settings (the method handles up to ~50 dimensions), the treatment of cell-cell interactions and temporally varying environments, and whether the autonomous drift assumption is sufficient for more complex developmental processes.
