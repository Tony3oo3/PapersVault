## Problem

Existing LLM routers perform a single-round, one-to-one mapping that assigns each query to one model in isolation, which is insufficient for complex tasks (e.g., multi-hop QA) that require coordinated, multi-round interactions among multiple LLMs with complementary strengths. The discrete, non-differentiable nature of model selection at each round further precludes straightforward gradient-based training, making it unclear how to learn a policy that interleaves internal reasoning with targeted model invocations.

## Input / Output

The router receives a natural-language question along with textual descriptors for each candidate LLM in the routing pool (parameter size, pricing tier, task specialization summaries) and produces a structured sequence of think blocks, search tags (specifying which LLM to query and a sub-question), info blocks (the routed LLM's response), and a final answer block. Routing is capped at 4 steps per query, and the routed LLM responses are excluded from the policy loss to stabilize training. The system is trained on 14K samples (7K NQ + 7K HotpotQA) and evaluated on 500 test instances per dataset.

## Method

Router-R1 formulates multi-LLM routing as a sequential decision process and trains the router (a 3B-parameter LLM) via PPO to interleave internal 'think' deliberation with 'route' actions that invoke specific models from a pool of six LLMs (ranging from 7B to 70B parameters). The reward is a lightweight rule-based composite: a format reward (−1/0) enforcing well-structured tag sequences, an exact-match outcome reward, and a cost reward inversely proportional to the selected model's parameter count times its output token count, with a hierarchical design that nullifies outcome and cost rewards if the format is violated. A cost coefficient α controls the performance–cost trade-off, and the router conditions only on simple model descriptors (pricing, latency, capability summaries) rather than learned embeddings, enabling zero-shot generalization to newly added LLMs by simply appending their descriptors to the prompt. Training uses veRL with a batch size of 64 over 225 steps, and the policy converges within ~100 steps as evidenced by rising rewards and decreasing action entropy.

## Results

On seven QA benchmarks (3 general, 4 multi-hop), Router-R1-Qwen achieves an average Exact Match of 0.416 and Router-R1-Llama achieves 0.409, outperforming all baselines. The strongest single-model baseline (Largest LLM, always routing to LLaMA-3.1-70B) reaches only 0.338 (Qwen) and 0.350 (Llama) average EM. On the hardest multi-hop dataset (Musique), Router-R1-Qwen scores 0.138 EM versus 0.104 for the best baseline, and on Bamboogle it reaches 0.512 versus 0.480. Router-R1 also generalizes to five out-of-domain datasets without retraining, having been trained only on NQ and HotpotQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Router-R1 wins decisively across all baseline categories. Against basic methods (Direct, CoT, SFT, RAG), it improves average EM by 0.15–0.28 points, with the largest gap on multi-hop tasks (e.g., Musique: 0.138 vs. 0.078 for RAG). Against the closest multi-round baseline Search-R1 (which also supports iterative search-engine calls), Router-R1-Qwen gains +0.125 avg EM (0.416 vs. 0.291) and Router-R1-Llama gains +0.098 (0.409 vs. 0.311). Among learned single-round routers, the strongest is Largest LLM (always query the 70B model) at 0.338/0.350 avg EM; Router-R1 surpasses it by +0.078/+0.059 respectively. Against the most sophisticated learned routers (RouterDC at 0.314/0.324, GraphRouter at 0.297/0.300), Router-R1 leads by +0.102 to +0.119 avg EM. Even the enhanced sub-query decomposition baselines (Prompt LLM*, KNN Router*) underperform Router-R1 by 0.128–0.248 avg EM, confirming that multi-round interleaved reasoning and routing outperforms one-shot decomposition strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

Router-R1 extends the query-based LLM routing literature (GraphRouter, RouterDC, RouteLLM, FrugalGPT) by reframing routing from a single-shot, one-to-one dispatch into a sequential decision process where an LLM-based router interleaves internal reasoning ('think') with external model invocations ('route') over multiple rounds. The RL training paradigm draws directly on Search-R1's approach of using PPO to teach an LLM to interact with an external tool (search engine), but replaces the search engine with a pool of candidate LLMs and adds a cost-aware reward component absent in prior routing work. The general policy-optimization objective (Eq. 1) is deliberately agnostic to the specific RL algorithm, encompassing PPO, GRPO, and KL-constrained variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-round formulation opens a path toward orchestrating heterogeneous LLMs for genuinely complex tasks (multi-hop reasoning) that single-shot routers cannot address, and the cost reward provides a tunable, RL-learned performance-cost trade-off rather than a hand-engineered cascade. The descriptor-based generalization to unseen LLMs without retraining is practically valuable in rapidly evolving model ecosystems. However, evaluation is confined to QA tasks, the rule-based reward may not capture nuanced objectives like factual consistency, and the multi-round nature introduces inference latency that limits time-sensitive deployment. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to dialogue, code generation, or other domains with different routing dynamics, and whether learned (rather than rule-based) rewards could further improve the framework.
