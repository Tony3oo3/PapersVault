## Problem

The Asymmetric Traveling Salesman Problem (ATSP) remains one of the most challenging combinatorial optimization problems, with existing solvers falling into two camps: handcrafted heuristics like LKH-3 that degrade on large or highly asymmetric instances, and learning-based methods that fail to generalize beyond their training distribution. Assignment-problem relaxations provide tight lower bounds but lack a principled continuous mechanism to suppress subtours while remaining differentiable. This paper bridges that gap by introducing an exact continuous relaxation that provably recovers the true TSP optimum at its global minimum.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single n×n non-negative cost matrix D (no coordinates, no graph structure beyond the matrix) and produces a valid Hamiltonian cycle (permutation of all n nodes) minimizing total directed travel cost. It operates on general non-metric ATSP instances where d_ij ≠ d_ji and the triangle inequality need not hold. The intermediate output is a doubly stochastic assignment matrix X = exp(Y) optimized in the log-domain, from which the discrete tour is extracted either directly (when the assignment is a single cycle) or via a greedy decoding heuristic followed by 2-opt refinement.

## Method

The core formulation relaxes ATSP to minimize ⟨D, X⟩ over the doubly stochastic polytope Dⁿ subject to the trace-based acyclicity constraint Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ⁻¹ tr(Xⁱ) = 0, which eliminates all subtours of length < n. A key theoretical result (Proposition 1) proves this relaxation is exact: by the Birkhoff-von Neumann decomposition, any fractional doubly stochastic matrix satisfying the trace constraint must be a single Hamiltonian-cycle permutation matrix. For optimization, the authors reparameterize X = exp(Y) with Y ≤ 0 and apply a projected exponentiated gradient descent where projection onto Dⁿ is achieved via Sinkhorn normalization, with an adaptive step size that halves when the underlying linear assignment changes and doubles otherwise. The expensive O(n³) matrix-power gradient is approximated in O(n²) by substituting the current hard assignment (a permutation matrix whose powers are cyclic shifts). When the optimization does not converge to a single cycle, a greedy decoding procedure follows highest-probability edges with fallback to nearest unvisited nodes, and a 2-opt pass further refines the tour.

## Results

On large-scale pure random ATSP instances, the proposed method achieves negative GAP (i.e., better than LKH-3(10,10000)) for the first time: −10.77% average GAP on ATSP500 and −21.40% on ATSP1000, while running in 0.87 s and 5.22 s respectively—far faster than LKH-3(10,10000) at 29.99 s and 46.64 s. On medium-scale ATSP200, the method already beats LKH-3(10,10000) in median GAP (−1.01%) with a runtime of 0.13 s. Learning-based baselines (GOAL, MatPOENet) degrade catastrophically on pure random ATSP, with GAPs exceeding 300% and 6400% on 500-node instances, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LKH-3, the proposed method wins decisively on large instances: on ATSP1000 (pure random) it achieves −21.40% average GAP versus 0.00% for LKH-3(10,10000), while running ~9× faster (5.22 s vs. 46.64 s). On small instances (ATSP20/50), LKH-3 retains a slight quality edge (0.00% vs. 1.9% avg GAP) but is 300–1000× slower. Against GOAL, the method dominates across all scales; GOAL's GAP explodes to 305.84% on ATSP1000 (pure random) and 1.26% on ATSP1000 (triangle inequality), whereas the proposed method achieves −21.40% and −0.35% respectively. Against MatPOENet, the gap is even more extreme: MatPOENet's GAP reaches 15017% on ATSP1000 (pure random) due to distribution shift from its triangle-inequality training data, while the proposed method remains at −21.40%. On triangle-inequality ATSP, the method still outperforms LKH-3 on 500+ node instances (−0.06% to −0.35% GAP) and matches or beats GOAL, though the margin over LKH-3 narrows compared to the pure random setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the trace-based differentiable DAG constraint from Bayesian network structure learning (Wei et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2022, 2025) into the classical assignment-problem relaxation of the TSP, creating a continuous formulation whose global minimum provably recovers the optimal tour. It departs from the dominant learning-based TSP literature (GOAL, MatPOENet, POMO) by using no neural network or reinforcement learning at all, instead relying on projected exponentiated gradient descent over the doubly stochastic polytope. It also extends the classical AP-relaxation line (Karp-Steele patching, branch-and-cut) by adding a differentiable acyclicity penalty that directly suppresses subtours during optimization rather than eliminating them in a separate post-processing phase.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exactness proof (Proposition 1) provides a clean theoretical guarantee that the continuous relaxation is tight, which is rare for non-convex TSP relaxations and opens the door to principled analysis of local minima. The empirical finding that a simple gradient-based method with no learned components outperforms LKH-3 by over 20% on 1000-node random ATSP instances challenges the assumption that increasingly sophisticated heuristics or neural solvers are necessary for large-scale TSP. Key open questions include whether the advantage persists on structured real-world instances (TSPLIB results show only 1–2% gaps), how the method scales beyond 1000 nodes, and whether the trace-based acyclicity constraint generalizes to other combinatorial problems requiring acyclic substructures. The catastrophic generalization failure of learning-based solvers on non-triangle-inequality ATSP (GAPs exceeding 6000%) also highlights a significant robustness gap in the neural CO literature.
