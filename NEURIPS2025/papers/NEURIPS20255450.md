## Problem

Existing RAFT-style iterative fine-tuning methods for chain-of-thought reasoning allocate a fixed inference budget (best-of-n sampling) uniformly across all prompts, ignoring prompt-specific difficulty and convergence behavior. This leads to inefficient stochastic gradient estimation: easy prompts waste budget on redundant accepted samples while hard prompts receive too few samples to produce stable gradients. The paper identifies this variance in gradient estimation as the main bottleneck and asks how to dynamically allocate sampling budget to minimize gradient variance under a fixed computational budget.

## Input / Output

The method takes prompt-answer pairs (x, z) from a mathematical dataset (Numina-Math) and a symbolic verifier (Math-Verify) that returns a binary correctness signal for the final answer. The model generates a chain-of-thought rationale y followed by a final answer z, with generation capped at 3072 tokens and prompts at 1024 tokens. During training, the E-step samples N' candidate rationales per prompt to estimate accept rates and gradient norms, then allocates a prompt-specific budget n_i for the actual rejection sampling phase, producing accepted (x, z, y) triples for the M-step fine-tuning.

## Method

GVM-RAFT formalizes CoT reasoning as a latent-variable EM problem where the rationale y is hidden, and derives a closed-form optimal sample allocation per prompt: n_i ∝ G_i / √(p_i + α/p_i^(β−1)), where p_i is the prompt's rejection-sampling accept rate (proxy for difficulty) and G_i is the expected gradient norm (proxy for learning signal). A regularization term with hyperparameters α and β prevents excessive sampling on prompts with near-zero accept rates. The strategy is updated every k M-steps (k=10 in practice), and the resulting GVM-RAFT++ incorporates PPO-style importance sampling and clipping to handle the distribution shift from multiple gradient steps per iteration. The same budget allocation mechanism is also plugged into GRPO by dividing the per-prompt budget across rollout copies, yielding GVM-GRPO. Theoretical analysis provides convergence guarantees under smoothness and convexity conditions, showing the dynamic allocation reduces the variance term Ω(k,T) and accelerates loss decrease compared to uniform sampling.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, GVM-GRPO achieves a 5-benchmark average of 40.42 (vs. 38.11 for vanilla GRPO and 36.42 for RAFT++), while GVM-RAFT++ reaches 39.64. On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, GVM-GRPO attains 49.86 average accuracy, marginally exceeding both GRPO (49.07) and RAFT++ (49.06). The paper reports a 2–4× speedup in convergence (measured in update steps) and up to ~5% absolute accuracy gain upon convergence for the larger sampling budget (N′=32). Evaluation uses Average@8 (mean accuracy over 8 stochastic rollouts) on Math500, Minerva Math, Olympiad Bench, AIME24, and AMC23.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GVM-GRPO consistently outperforms vanilla GRPO across all four model sizes: +2.31 on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (40.42 vs. 38.11), +0.79 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (49.86 vs. 49.07), +0.31 on Llama-3.2-1B (12.68 vs. 12.37), and +0.17 on Llama-3.2-3B (25.98 vs. 25.81). GVM-RAFT++ shows a larger gain over RAFT++ on the 1.5B model (+3.22, 39.64 vs. 36.42) but ties or slightly trails on the 7B model (49.13 vs. 49.06) and Llama-3.2-1B (11.38 vs. 11.38), which the authors attribute to pass@n degradation in the 7B model's training paradigm rather than a failure of GVM itself. The convergence speedup is the most consistent benefit: GVM-RAFT++ achieves roughly 2× fewer update steps to reach target accuracy with N′=8 and up to 4× with N′=32. On individual benchmarks, GVM-RAFT++ excels on AMC23 (51.88 vs. 44.06 for RAFT++ on 1.5B) while GVM-GRPO leads on AIME24 (12.92 vs. 10.00), suggesting the two variants complement each other in different difficulty regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-derives the RAFT/RAFT++ pipeline through an EM lens (building on Singh et al. 2023 and Sordoni et al. 2023) and identifies the key bottleneck as stochastic gradient variance from uniform best-of-n sampling, rather than the imitation-of-best-of-n motivation used in prior work. It departs from RAFT++ (Xiong et al., 2025a) and GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by replacing their fixed per-prompt inference budget with a closed-form dynamic allocation that minimizes gradient variance, derived from accept rates and per-prompt gradient norms. The strategy is then shown to be algorithm-agnostic, transferring from the EM-based RAFT++ to the REINFORCE-based GRPO without modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–4× convergence speedup under a fixed compute budget is practically significant for LLM post-training pipelines where inference cost dominates. The dynamic allocation rule is simple to implement (two empirical estimates per prompt) and generalizes across algorithm families, suggesting it could be a drop-in component for other RL fine-tuning methods (PPO, REINFORCE). However, the accuracy gains are modest (1–2 points), the 7B model exhibits distribution collapse that GVM does not resolve, and validation is limited to Qwen and Llama on mathematical reasoning, leaving open whether the speedup and accuracy benefits transfer to other model families, tasks, or reward structures.
