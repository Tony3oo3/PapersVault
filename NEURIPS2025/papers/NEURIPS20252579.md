## Problem

The literature on model collapse has reached contradictory conclusions: some analyses show severe degradation from synthetic data, while others suggest accumulation of real data can prevent collapse entirely. It remains unclear under which assumptions iterative MLE is safe and whether MLE consistency on real data alone suffices to prevent collapse. This paper provides the first rigorous characterization of both the positive (when collapse is avoidable) and negative (when it is not) regimes in the accumulating-data setting.

## Input / Output

The setting takes a parametric family of PDFs {p_θ}, n i.i.d. real samples from the ground-truth distribution p_{θ*}, and at each iteration T draws n new synthetic samples from the current model p_{θ^(T)}, accumulating them with all prior data. The key constraint for the positive result is n ≥ c(log T + 1)² log²(7dT/δ), i.e., polylogarithmic in the number of iterations. The output is a parameter estimate θ^(T) at each iteration with a non-asymptotic bound ‖θ^(T) − θ*‖ ≤ C√(log(4d/δ)/n) that is independent of T, along with corresponding KL and TV distance guarantees.

## Method

The positive result (Theorem 4.1) proves consistency via a Taylor-expansion argument: since θ^(t+1) is a stationary point of the accumulated log-likelihood, the parameter update θ^(t+1) − θ^(t) can be written as −H_t^{-1} ∇ℓ_t(θ^(t)), where H_t aggregates Hessians over all t+1 datasets. A telescopic sum then decomposes the total drift into a martingale term (scaled by 1/(t+1) per iteration, so its variance is bounded by π²/6) and a correlation term bounded by log(T)/n. The 1/(t+1) scaling from data accumulation is the key mechanism that prevents error from growing with T. The negative results (Theorems 5.1–5.2) construct highly non-smooth mixture distributions where the MLE is TV-consistent on real data, yet a single synthetic sample landing in a rare high-density region causes the MLE to jump to a far-away parameter, demonstrating that smoothness (beyond mere MLE consistency) is necessary to prevent collapse.

## Results

The main positive result (Theorem 4.1) establishes that under standard regularity, smoothness, and Fisher-information conditions, iterative MLE with accumulating data satisfies ‖θ⁽ᵀ⁾ − θ⋆‖ ≤ C√(log(4d/δ)/n) with probability ≥ 1−δ, provided n ≥ c(log T + 1)² log²(7dT/δ). Crucially, this bound is independent of T, meaning the fraction of real data can vanish (1/T → 0) without causing collapse. On the negative side, Theorem 5.1 constructs a TV-consistent family where the first iteration achieves TV ≤ log(n)/n with high probability, yet the second iteration suffers TV ≥ C (a constant) with constant probability. Theorem 5.2 further shows that for any strictly increasing φ(n)→∞, there exists a TV-consistent family where collapse (TV ≥ 3/8) occurs within O((1/δ)log(4/δ)·max(φ(n),1)) iterations, with φ(n) = log log(n+1) yielding collapse in as few as O(log log n) iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines, but it positions its results against several prior theoretical analyses. Compared to Dey and Donoho (2024), who fix T and take n→∞ (yielding only a universal multiplicative constant degradation), this paper provides non-asymptotic bounds that explicitly quantify the dependence between n and T, showing consistency even as T→∞. Compared to Gerstgrasser et al. (2025) and Schaeffer et al. (2025), who suggest data accumulation inherently prevents collapse, this paper proves that such claims require structural assumptions (smoothness, bounded Fisher information) beyond mere MLE consistency, and constructs the first rigorous counterexamples where rapid collapse occurs in the accumulating-data setting. Compared to Seddik et al. (2024), whose TV bound scales as O(√(T/n)) in the accumulation setting, this paper's bound is independent of T entirely. Compared to Bertrand et al. (2024) and Ferbach et al. (2024), who analyze non-accumulating or finite-n settings, this paper handles the natural accumulating-data regime where synthetic data grows unboundedly across iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on model collapse in iterative generative modeling (Shumailov et al. 2024, Alemohammad et al. 2024, Dey and Donoho 2024, Gerstgrasser et al. 2025) by providing the first non-asymptotic guarantees for general parametric distribution families in the accumulating-data setting. While Dey and Donoho analyzed exponential families in the limit n→∞ with fixed T, and Gerstgrasser et al. studied specific architectures, this work applies to arbitrary distribution families under standard MLE regularity assumptions (sub-Gaussian gradients, bounded Hessian/third derivatives, positive-definite Fisher information). The negative results (Theorems 5.1–5.2) depart from prior work by constructing the first rigorous examples where MLE is consistent on real data yet collapse occurs with accumulating synthetic data, showing that data accumulation alone is insufficient without structural smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves a key ambiguity in the model-collapse literature by showing that collapse is neither inevitable nor universally avoidable in the accumulating-data setting—it hinges on smoothness and regularity conditions that are standard in classical MLE theory but had not been explicitly connected to the iterative generative modeling context. The practical implication is that well-behaved models (e.g., neural networks with softmax outputs and weight decay) may be robust to synthetic data contamination even as real data vanishes, while non-smooth or poorly-regularized models remain vulnerable. Open questions include whether the smoothness assumptions can be weakened, whether the results extend to approximate MLE (e.g., SGD-trained models), and whether the gap between the positive and negative results can be closed to characterize necessary-and-sufficient conditions for avoiding collapse.
