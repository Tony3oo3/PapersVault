## Problem

Listwise reranking with LLMs is typically performed over fixed-size subsets with a predetermined number of reranker calls, regardless of query difficulty or document ambiguity. This rigid allocation leads to over-computation on straightforward queries and under-computation on complex ones, and fails to leverage intermediate signals from prior reranking steps to focus effort where refinement is most needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250890/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query, a set of 100–1000 retrieved documents (from BM25, SPLADE, or Contriever), and a listwise LLM reranker (e.g., RankZephyr-7B or RankGPT) that operates on subsets of m=20 documents per call due to context-length constraints. It produces a fully ranked document list with probabilistic relevance estimates (Gaussian mean and variance per document), from which a top-k list (typically k=10) is extracted for downstream use. Unlike fixed-budget methods, the number of reranker calls per query is determined adaptively at runtime.

## Method

AcuRank maintains each document's relevance as a Gaussian belief distribution using the TrueSkill Bayesian rating system, initialized from first-stage retrieval scores (μ_i = score, σ_i = score/3). At each iteration, it computes the probability s_i = P(x_i > t(k)) that document i falls in the top-k via a threshold approximation (t(k) found by binary search such that the expected count of documents exceeding it equals k), then selects only uncertain candidates (ε < s_i < 1−ε) for reranking. These candidates are partitioned into ordered groups of size m by descending μ_i, passed to the listwise reranker, and the resulting rankings update TrueSkill parameters via closed-form Bayesian posterior updates. The loop repeats until the uncertain set shrinks below a threshold τ, the top-k stabilizes, or a compute budget is exhausted, yielding a final ranking by updated μ_i values. This uncertainty-driven selection avoids redundant updates to confidently ranked documents while concentrating computation on ambiguous cases.

## Results

On the TREC-DL and BEIR benchmarks (BM25 top-100, RankZephyr-7B), AcuRank achieves an average NDCG@10 of 55.5 with 19.7 reranker calls per query, outperforming SW-2 (54.5, 17.6 calls) and SW-3 (54.6, 26.4 calls) while using fewer calls than SW-3. The budget-matched variant AcuRank-9 (8.8 calls) achieves 54.6 NDCG@10, slightly exceeding SW-1 (54.3) at the same compute budget. Scaling to BM25 top-1000, AcuRank reaches 58.0 NDCG@10 with 68.4 calls, surpassing SW-1 (56.2, 94.6 calls) and TourRank-1 (56.0, 117.1 calls) with substantially fewer invocations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Sliding Windows, AcuRank (55.5 NDCG@10, 19.7 calls) outperforms both SW-2 (54.5, 17.6 calls) and SW-3 (54.6, 26.4 calls) in accuracy while using fewer calls than SW-3; the budget-matched AcuRank-9 (8.8 calls) edges out SW-1 (54.3) by 0.3 NDCG@10. Against TourRank, AcuRank dominates on the accuracy-efficiency frontier: it beats TourRank-1 (53.4, 12.7 calls) by 2.1 NDCG@10 with only ~7 more calls, and surpasses TourRank-2 (54.9, 25.5 calls) by 0.6 NDCG@10 with 5.8 fewer calls. TourRank-5 and TourRank-10 reach comparable accuracy (55.1–55.2) but require 63.7 and 127.4 calls respectively—3–6× more than AcuRank. On the harder BM25 top-1000 setting, AcuRank (58.0, 68.4 calls) outperforms SW-1 (56.2, 94.6 calls) by 1.8 NDCG@10 with 28% fewer calls, and beats TourRank-1 (56.0, 117.1 calls) by 2.0 NDCG@10 with 41% fewer calls.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes TrueSkill, a Bayesian rating system originally designed for multiplayer game matchmaking, as the probabilistic backbone for document relevance estimation in listwise reranking. It departs from the dominant fixed-computation paradigm (sliding windows, tournament-style reranking) by introducing uncertainty-guided adaptive allocation, where only documents near the top-k boundary with high ranking uncertainty are selected for further reranking. Conceptually, it extends the Bayesian uncertainty modeling of Cohen et al. (2021) from post-hoc calibration of static retrieval scores to an online, iterative setting where uncertainty both drives computation allocation and is refined through successive reranker observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anytime-prediction property (AcuRank-9 through AcuRank-HH) gives practitioners a practical knob to trade latency against accuracy in production RAG and search pipelines, a flexibility that fixed-schedule methods lack. The framework is reranker-agnostic and retriever-agnostic, demonstrated across RankZephyr, RankGPT, RankVicuna, and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct with BM25, SPLADE, and Contriever first-stage signals. Open questions include how the threshold-based rank approximation behaves at very large candidate pools (beyond top-1000), whether the approach composes with emerging reasoning-based rerankers that produce richer signals than simple orderings, and whether the TrueSkill update is optimal when the reranker's output is noisy or inconsistent across calls.
