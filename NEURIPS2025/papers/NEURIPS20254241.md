## Problem

Existing hand-object reconstruction methods either depend on dense 3D surface annotations (SDF-based) or are trained on short video clips with fixed environments and limited motion trajectories (NeRF-based), making them unable to generalize to unseen scenes or diverse interaction patterns. This paper tackles the challenge of achieving cross-scene and cross-motion generalizable, photo-realistic hand-object modeling from unconstrained monocular RGB images without per-sequence optimization or retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254241/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes monocular RGB images (hand-object region cropped and resized to 224×224 for the visual encoder), MANO hand pose parameters, and object 6-DoF poses (initialized by an off-the-shelf regressor) as input, requiring no dense 3D surface annotations. It produces a deformable 3D Gaussian representation for both hand and object, photo-realistic novel-view renderings, explicit 3D meshes extracted via Marching Tetrahedra on a Gaussian Opacity Field, and refined hand-relative object pose offsets. At inference, the trained model adapts to new sequences in a purely feed-forward manner without any fine-tuning.

## Method

HOGS represents hand and object as pose-deformable 3D Gaussian primitives (LBS-driven for the hand, 6-DoF rigid transform for the object) and introduces two generalizable modules co-optimized in a differentiable rendering pipeline. The Vision-driven Perception Module (V-PM) uses a ViT backbone to extract pixel-aligned visual features, then an MLP decouples each Gaussian's parameters into geometry-invariant canonical templates (SH coefficients) and vision-dependent deltas (color, position, scale, rotation), enabling adaptation to transient lighting and appearance changes across scenes. The Geometry-driven Pose Refinement Module (G-PM) employs a ULIP-pretrained PointNet++ to encode Gaussian primitive geometry into 3D features, followed by a Transformer encoder that predicts hand-relative object translation and rotation offsets, improving pose accuracy and contact plausibility. Training uses a unified loss combining decomposed RGB/mask/perceptual (LPIPS) photometric terms, pose supervision, contact and penetration constraints, skinning regularization, and isometric deformation penalties, all optimized from 2D photometric supervision alone.

## Results

On DexYCB, HOGS achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.24 cm² for object reconstruction (CDo), substantially outperforming the best SDF-based method gSDF (1.55) and the 2D-supervised GOF† (0.68), while reaching Fo@10 = 0.918. For photo-realistic rendering, HOGS attains PSNR 31.12 / SSIM 0.9728 / LPIPS 26.83, surpassing 3DGS-Avatar† (29.71 / 0.9690 / 30.52) and GOF† (29.58 / 0.9686 / 31.69). On HO3D, HOGS achieves CDpose = 2.7 mm, a 4× improvement over HOLD (11.3 mm), while matching its pose-independent reconstruction quality (Fo@10: 96.2 vs 96.5). For hand-held object pose estimation, HOGS reports OCE 18.1, MCE 26.1, and ADD-S 12.0, all best among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDF-based 3D-supervised baselines (Hasson et al., Grasping Field, AlignSDF, gSDF), HOGS dramatically outperforms on object reconstruction (CDo 0.24 vs. 1.55–2.06; Fo@10 0.918 vs. 0.642–0.709) but trails on hand metrics (CDh 0.481 vs. 0.302 for gSDF), reflecting the trade-off of forgoing dense 3D surface annotations. Among 2D-supervised methods, HOGS surpasses MOHO (Fo@10 0.918 vs. 0.810) and the re-implemented GOF† (CDo 0.24 vs. 0.68; Fo@10 0.918 vs. 0.834). On HO3D, HOGS matches HOLD's pose-independent quality (Fo@10 96.2 vs. 96.5) while achieving a 4× lower pose-dependent Chamfer distance (CDpose 2.7 vs. 11.3 mm), demonstrating superior generalization to unseen motion. For photo-realistic rendering, HOGS leads both 3DGS-Avatar† and GOF† by 1.4–1.5 dB in PSNR and 3.7–4.9 in LPIPS. In pose estimation, HOGS edges out HOISDF (OCE 18.1 vs. 18.4; ADD-S 12.0 vs. 13.3) and substantially outperforms AlignSDF, gSDF, and other attention-based pose estimators on all three metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOGS sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) SDF-based and NeRF-based hand-object reconstruction (AlignSDF, gSDF, HOLD, DiffHOI), which either require dense 3D surface annotations or are locked to short, scene-specific video clips, and (2) deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting for animatable humans (3DGS-Avatar, HUGS, GauHuman), which has not been applied to closely interacting hand-object pairs. The paper departs from both by adopting 3DGS as the core representation and introducing two generalization-oriented modules—V-PM (Vision-driven Perception) that disentangles geometry-invariant canonical templates from vision-dependent appearance via a ViT backbone, and G-PM (Geometry-driven Pose Refinement) that leverages ULIP-pretrained PointNet++ features over Gaussian primitives for contact-aware pose correction. Mesh extraction is adapted from Gaussian Opacity Fields (GOF) with inward-ray sampling to handle the dynamic, single-view setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that HOGS reduces novel-scene hand-object setup from hours of per-sequence optimization to a feed-forward inference pass, which is critical for consumer AR/VR pipelines. The dramatic object Chamfer distance improvement (0.24 cm² vs. 1.55 for gSDF) and pose accuracy gain (CD_pose 2.7 vs. 11.3 for HOLD) suggest that 3DGS with learned perceptual adaptation can outperform purpose-built SDF pipelines even without 3D supervision. The acknowledged limitation—that generalization is bounded by the object categories seen during training and does not extend to entirely novel categories—leaves open the question of whether scaling to large-scale hand-object corpora (e.g., Ego4D, ARCTIC) could close that gap. A secondary open question is whether the V-PM's reliance on a ViT image backbone generalizes to extreme lighting or material changes beyond the color-jittering stress test.
