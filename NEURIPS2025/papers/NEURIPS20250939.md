## Problem

Long CoT reasoning models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1 distillates) suffer from overthinking, generating redundant reasoning steps even for simple problems, while Short CoT models lack the reflective capability needed for difficult ones. Standard SFT on (question, Long CoT response) pairs overrides the model's original Short CoT patterns, causing indiscriminate Long CoT usage. The paper asks whether a model can be trained to adaptively select between concise Short CoT and reflective Long CoT reasoning based on question difficulty.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a standard text question (e.g., a math word problem) and produces a reasoning chain ending with a boxed final answer. The unusual aspect is at training time: the input question is entirely removed (replaced with an empty context), so the model is trained solely on the Long CoT response tokens via causal language modeling. At inference, the model defaults to concise Short CoT output but dynamically inserts reflective Long CoT segments (marked by keywords like 'wait') when it detects errors or uncertainty mid-reasoning.

## Method

QFFT (Question-Free Fine-Tuning) removes the input question from all training samples and optimizes the model exclusively on Long CoT responses using a standard causal LM objective: L_QFFT = -(1/|R|) Σ log P_θ(R_t | R_<t, ∅). The key insight is that SFT's Q→R mapping overwrites the model's native Short CoT patterns, whereas training without questions prevents this override while still imparting Long CoT reflective reasoning capabilities (verification, backtracking, sub-goal setting). The paper formalizes this as the model learning P(B_r | U_L) — the conditional probability of reflective behaviors given uncertainty in Long CoT context — which transfers to Short CoT context at inference. The approach is equivalent to 'null-question SFT' or a specialized form of continued pre-training on reasoning responses, requiring no architectural changes beyond removing the question from the training template.

## Results

On the primary mathematical benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, AIME25), QFFT achieves accuracy within 0.4–1.0% of SFT while reducing average token generation by 50.5% (7B, S1.1 data: 5.3K vs 8.2K tokens) and 44.4% (32B: 5.3K vs 7.5K tokens). The Reasoning Adaptability Cohen's Kappa (RAK) score jumps from ~1.8 (SFT) to ~34.7 (QFFT) on average, confirming adaptive pattern selection. Among Long-to-Short baselines, QFFT achieves the highest Accuracy-Efficiency Score (AES) of 3.4 (7B) and 2.3 (32B), outperforming SFT-Shortest (1.2/−12.9), DPO-Shortest (0.7), SimPO-Shortest (−3.6/−1.6), and O1-Pruner (−1.7/−42.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QFFT outperforms all Long-to-Short baselines on the AES metric, which penalizes accuracy drops more heavily than token savings. Against SFT-Shortest [11], QFFT achieves 80.6% MATH accuracy (vs. 78.3%) with fewer GSM8K tokens (0.7K vs. 1.2K), yielding AES 3.4 vs. 1.2. DPO-Shortest [11] and SimPO-Shortest [11] both sacrifice MATH accuracy (79.5% and 75.8% respectively) and produce negative or near-zero AES scores, while QFFT maintains 80.6%. O1-Pruner [14] achieves the largest token reduction on AIME25 (12.2K vs. QFFT's 15.6K) but drops AIME25 accuracy to 14.2% (vs. 17.2%), resulting in AES −1.7 (7B) and −42.5 (32B). The Difficulty-Adaptive Distillation (DAD) baseline, which explicitly mixes short and long CoT data by difficulty, achieves AES 2.4 but uses more tokens on MATH (4.8K vs. 4.1K) and AIME25 (17.7K vs. 15.6K) than QFFT, suggesting that explicit difficulty-based data mixing is less efficient than QFFT's implicit adaptive mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of Long CoT distillation (from DeepSeek-R1 into smaller models like Qwen2.5) and the emerging 'overthinking' problem, departing from both Long-to-Short compression methods (SFT-Shortest, DPO-Shortest, O1-Pruner, CoT-Valve) and Difficulty-Adaptive Distillation. Its key conceptual departure draws on the catastrophic-forgetting/override literature [18]: rather than compressing or mixing reasoning patterns, QFFT simply removes the question from the training objective, reframing distillation as a form of continued pre-training on reasoning responses. The transfer-learning argument (Assumption 2) that reflective behaviors learned in Long CoT context transfer to Short CoT context is the paper's main theoretical bridge, though it is supported only by a single empirical check in the appendix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% token reduction at comparable accuracy is practically significant for deployment cost, and the robustness to noisy, out-of-domain, and low-resource settings (especially the Level IV irrelevant-answer result) suggests QFFT is a more stable distillation recipe than standard SFT. However, the evaluation is confined to mathematical reasoning with a small set of base models (Qwen2.5 7B/32B, Phi-4-mini), and the 'adaptive reasoning' metric (RAK) is defined relative to a reference model's Short CoT capability, making it somewhat circular. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning (coding, science), whether the transfer-learning assumption holds for more complex reflective behaviors, and whether the method's simplicity masks a deeper limitation in how Long CoT capabilities are actually acquired without question grounding.
