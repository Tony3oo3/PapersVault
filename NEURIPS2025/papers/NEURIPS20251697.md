## Problem

Existing LLM-based multi-agent systems are tightly coupled to specific domains, requiring complete architectural redesign and full retraining of all components when deployed in new application areas. This rigidity sharply limits cross-domain transferability and scalability, as frameworks like MetaGPT or MALT hard-code role sets and pipelines that cannot be adapted without rebuilding the entire agent ensemble.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251697/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a natural language task description along with associated task files (PDFs, Excel spreadsheets, images, audio, video) and produces a final text answer. Internally, the Planner generates a structured subtask decomposition plan (an ordered list of subtasks tagged to specific worker capabilities), which the Coordinator then dispatches through a shared task channel. Workers post only their final results back to the channel, keeping detailed execution context isolated within each subtask's scope to maintain clean per-worker context windows.

## Method

WORKFORCE is a hierarchical multi-agent architecture with three decoupled components: a domain-agnostic Planner that decomposes tasks based on a worker capability registry, a Coordinator that assigns subtasks and manages dependencies, and specialized Worker Nodes (Web, Document Processing, Reasoning/Coding) equipped with domain-specific toolkits. This separation enables plug-and-play domain transfer at inference time by simply swapping worker nodes while preserving the planning core. For training, OWL applies a two-stage paradigm to the Planner only: supervised fine-tuning on 1,599 filtered expert trajectories (synthesized via GPT-4o-mini across HotpotQA, WikiTableQuestions, Math, and Infinity-MM) to initialize decomposition skills, followed by DPO on 1,009 preference pairs to enhance generalization beyond imitation. A replanning mechanism allows test-time scaling by prompting the Planner to regenerate subtasks when a Worker reports failure, up to a configurable threshold. This 'stable core, variable periphery' design means only the Planner requires intensive optimization, reducing training overhead while enabling the OWL-trained Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct to achieve 52.73% on GAIA (+16.37% over base) without any GAIA-specific data.

## Results

On the GAIA validation set, WORKFORCE with Claude-3.7-Sonnet achieves 69.70% average accuracy (84.91% Level 1, 68.60% Level 2, 42.31% Level 3), establishing open-source state-of-the-art and surpassing OpenAI's Deep Research (67.36%) by 2.34%. With GPT-4o as the backbone, WORKFORCE reaches 60.61%, outperforming the Single Agent baseline (37.58%) by 23.03% and the Role Playing multi-agent baseline (54.55%) by 6.06% under identical toolkits. The OWL-trained Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct planner achieves 52.73% (+16.37% over its 36.36% base), surpassing GPT-4o-mini (47.27%) and Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (49.09%), while matching GPT-4o on the hardest Level 3 tasks (26.92%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WORKFORCE with Claude-3.7-Sonnet (69.70%) surpasses OpenAI's Deep Research (67.36%) by 2.34%, making it the first open-source system to exceed this proprietary benchmark. Against controlled baselines using identical GPT-4o toolkits, WORKFORCE (60.61%) outperforms the Single Agent (37.58%) by 23.03% and the CAMEL Role Playing multi-agent system (54.55%) by 6.06%, confirming the benefit of the hierarchical planner-coordinator-worker design. On the OWL training side, the post-trained Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct planner (52.73%) beats both GPT-4o-mini (47.27%) and the larger Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (49.09%), and matches GPT-4o (26.92%) on the most challenging Level 3 tasks. WORKFORCE still trails the top proprietary systems Langfun Agent v2.1 (71.52%) and Trase Agent v0.3 (70.30%) on average, though it leads on Level 1 (84.91% vs. 83.02%). Among open-source frameworks, WORKFORCE leads all prior systems by a wide margin over Open Deep Research (55.15%) and TapeAgents (55.76%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CAMEL's multi-agent infrastructure but departs sharply from the dominant paradigm of domain-specific multi-agent pipelines (MetaGPT, ChatDev, MALT) that hardcode role sets and require full retraining when migrating to new domains. Its core architectural contribution is the explicit decoupling of a domain-agnostic Planner from domain-specific Worker Nodes, enabling plug-and-play domain transfer at inference time. On the training side, OWL diverges from prior multi-agent training methods (e.g., MALT's per-agent fine-tuning, Subramaniam et al.'s debate-driven specialization) by optimizing only the planner via SFT + DPO, treating workers as fixed, swappable peripherals. The DPO-based preference learning follows Rafailov et al. (2023) but applies it to planner trajectory quality rather than general response quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that cross-domain transferability in multi-agent systems can be achieved through architectural modularity plus targeted planner training, rather than the costly full-system retraining that prior work necessitates. The ablation showing planner-only training (45.45%) substantially outperforms worker-only training (31.51%) provides a clear design principle for future agentic systems. However, the approach remains dependent on the availability of high-quality domain-specific toolkits, and the RL phase is bottlenecked by real-world tool latency. Open questions include how well the trained planner generalizes to domains with fundamentally different tool ecosystems, and whether the 2.34% gap over Deep Research holds as both systems evolve.
