## Problem

LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations suffer from systematic structural biases (verbosity, position, bandwagon, sentiment) that undermine reliability, yet existing mitigation strategies are limited: in-context prompting fails to correct deep-rooted biases especially in weaker models, and fine-tuning is inapplicable to closed-source evaluators widely used in practice. The paper asks whether a lightweight external module can detect and correct these biases without modifying the evaluator itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250221/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The RBD receives the biased evaluation prompt (question plus answer options), the evaluator's initial judgment, the evaluator's model name (to calibrate reasoning about its susceptibility), and the bias type under consideration. It outputs a structured reasoning trace in the format `<think>{analysis}{Yes/No}`, where the analysis comprises three parts: bias-type identification, a comparative content analysis of the options, and an assessment of how the evaluator's capabilities influence its susceptibility. This reasoning trace is then fed back to the evaluator as additional context for iterative self-correction until RBD confirms no bias or a maximum iteration count is reached.

## Method

RBD is a plug-in reasoning model trained via knowledge distillation: a teacher LRM (DeepSeek-R1) generates structured bias-analysis reasoning traces over 1.67k curated examples spanning four bias types, which are filtered to retain only correctly-labeled instances and used to fine-tune base LRMs (1.5B–14B) with standard next-token prediction. A key design choice is joint training across all four bias types rather than per-bias models, enhancing generalization. The paper demonstrates that reasoning-based fine-tuning is essential—label-only fine-tuning overfits to superficial format cues and collapses under distribution shifts, while reasoning-based RBD maintains robustness. At inference, RBD operates in an iterative loop with the evaluator: it flags biased judgments with explanatory reasoning, the evaluator revises, and the cycle repeats, requiring no access to the evaluator's weights or architecture.

## Results

RBD-8B improves LLM evaluator accuracy by an average of 18.5% and consistency by 10.9% across four bias types (verbosity, position, bandwagon, sentiment) and eight evaluators. The largest gains appear on verbosity bias, where LLaMA-3.1-8B accuracy jumps from 0.202 to 0.920 (+71.8%) and Claude-3.5-haiku from 0.226 to 0.870 (+64.4%). On the RBD fine-tuning task itself, RBD-14B achieves 0.828 accuracy and 0.822 F1, surpassing zero-shot DeepSeek-R1 (0.762 accuracy). RBD-8B + LLaMA-3.1-70B reaches 0.940 accuracy on verbosity, 0.710 on bandwagon, and 0.900 on sentiment, outperforming all prompting-based and fine-tuned judge baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Using LLaMA-3.1-70B as the evaluator, RBD-8B (0.940 verbosity, 0.614 position, 0.710 bandwagon, 0.900 sentiment) substantially outperforms both prompting-based baselines (vanilla prompt: 0.562/0.534/0.566/0.861; bias-aware prompt: 0.576/0.558/0.664/0.856) and fine-tuned judges. Against Prometheus 2 (8×7B), RBD wins by a wide margin on every bias type—most dramatically on bandwagon bias (0.710 vs. 0.014) and verbosity (0.940 vs. 0.226)—where Prometheus appears to have collapsed. Against Skywork-LLaMA-3.1-70B, RBD leads on verbosity (0.940 vs. 0.592), bandwagon (0.710 vs. 0.664), and sentiment (0.900 vs. 0.614), while Skywork edges ahead slightly on position (0.606 vs. 0.614 is actually RBD winning). On the RBD fine-tuning task, RBD-8B (0.818 accuracy) surpasses zero-shot DeepSeek-R1 (0.762) and all same-size base LRMs under zero-shot, 4-shot-label, and 4-shot-reasoning prompting, confirming that distilled reasoning-based fine-tuning is essential rather than merely prompting a reasoning model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant strategies for bias mitigation in LLM-as-a-Judge—prompting-based in-context learning and fine-tuning of evaluator models—by introducing an external reasoning-based bias detector (RBD) that operates as a plug-in companion module without modifying the evaluator. Conceptually, it repurposes the language reasoning model (LRM) paradigm (exemplified by DeepSeek-R1) not as a judge itself, but as a meta-evaluator that generates structured bias analyses to guide the original evaluator's self-correction. The iterative feedback loop between RBD and the LLM evaluator draws on self-reflection and multi-turn deliberation ideas from multi-agent evaluation work, but applies them in a novel asymmetric configuration where the reasoning model never produces the final judgment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in architecture is practically significant because it works with closed-source evaluators (GPT-4o, Claude) that cannot be fine-tuned, making bias mitigation accessible in real deployment pipelines where model weights are inaccessible. The finding that larger, more capable models exhibit higher stubbornness (resistance to RBD correction) challenges the assumption that scale improves evaluability and suggests that behavioral biases may persist or intensify through distillation. Open questions include whether RBD generalizes to more nuanced or domain-specific biases beyond the four structural types studied, whether the synthetic bias construction faithfully captures real-world evaluation failures, and how the iterative loop behaves when the evaluator and RBD disagree persistently.
