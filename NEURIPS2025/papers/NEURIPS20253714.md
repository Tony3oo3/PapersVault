## Problem

Existing DPO-based multimodal preference optimization methods for hallucination mitigation suffer from two key flaws: (1) their vision-oriented contrastive objective non-rigorously cancels partition functions that cannot be eliminated when the two image inputs differ, yielding a misaligned loss; and (2) they provide only indirect preference supervision by varying images while fixing the response, rather than using direct response pairs as DPO's Bradley-Terry formulation intends. This paper identifies both issues through a formal derivation and proposes a theory-consistent fix.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., a VQA question) as input and generates a free-form text response. For training, each sample is a symmetric quartet: two CLIP-similar but distinct images (m, m′), a shared prompt x, and two preferred responses (y_w, y_w′) that are semantically aligned with only minor claim-level differences, constructed via a caption-anchored claim extraction and rewriting pipeline using Qwen2.5-VL-32B for captioning and DeepSeek-V3 for claim extraction/rewriting. The contrastive images are selected as nearest neighbors in CLIP embedding space rather than generated by geometric transforms or noise injection.

## Method

SymMPO replaces the non-rigorous vision-oriented contrastive loss with a symmetric pairwise preference objective that simultaneously maximizes P(y_w ≻ y_w′ | m, x) and P(y_w′ ≻ y_w | m′, x) under the Bradley-Terry model. Because each sigmoid term conditions on a single fixed multimodal input, the intractable partition functions cancel exactly as in standard DPO, yielding a theoretically sound loss. Beyond ordinal ranking, SymMPO adds a preference margin consistency regularizer (L_Margin) that penalizes squared differences between the log-likelihood gaps Δ(m,x,y_w,y_w′) and Δ(m′,x,y_w′,y_w), enforcing that near-identical visual contexts produce near-identical preference magnitudes. The full objective combines the standard response-oriented DPO loss, the symmetric pair loss, the margin regularizer, and an anchored preference term (L_AncPO) that prevents the model from lowering preferred-response likelihoods during optimization, weighted by hyperparameters λ=0.5, γ=1e-4, η=1.0.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, SymMPO achieves the best overall accuracy on HallusionBench (aAcc=44.28), the highest AMBER F1 (87.7), the best MMStar score (34.8), and the lowest MMHal-Bench hallucination rate (42.7%) among all compared methods. On LLaVA-1.5-13B, SymMPO similarly leads with HallusionBench aAcc=44.55, AMBER F1=89.1, MMStar=35.2, and MMHal-Bench hallucination rate of 39.6%. In the controlled comparison (same data, same hyperparameters), SymMPO outperforms both standard DPO and mDPO on four out of five benchmarks, with the sole exception being Object-HalBench where DPO achieves lower hallucination rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the controlled comparison (identical 21.4k training data, same hyperparameters, DeepSeek-V3 feedback), SymMPO outperforms standard DPO on HallusionBench aAcc (44.28 vs. 40.21), MMHal-Bench score (2.89 vs. 2.44), AMBER F1 (87.7 vs. 82.6), and MMStar (34.8 vs. 33.4), but DPO achieves lower Object-HalBench hallucination rates (Resp 12.9 vs. 19.5; Ment 8.8 vs. 9.7). Against mDPO under the same setup, SymMPO wins on HallusionBench aAcc (44.28 vs. 42.78), MMHal-Bench hallucination rate (42.7% vs. 50.0%), AMBER Acc (82.6 vs. 80.6), F1 (87.7 vs. 86.3), and MMStar (34.8 vs. 34.2), while mDPO marginally edges out on Object-HalBench (Resp 19.9 vs. 19.5). The authors attribute the Object-HalBench underperformance of both SymMPO and mDPO relative to DPO to their caption-anchored data construction pipeline, which introduces noise in fine-grained visual details. Among externally trained baselines with different data and feedback sources, SymMPO achieves the best AMBER F1 (87.7) and MMStar (34.8) on 7B, surpassing TPO (F1 87.2, MMStar 34.2) and OPA-DPO (F1 85.6, MMStar 33.1), while TPO and OPA-DPO report much lower Object-HalBench rates (Resp 5.0 and 6.1) due to their different evaluation protocols and data scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-based multimodal preference optimization line initiated by mDPO (Wang et al., 2024) and OPA-DPO (Yang et al., 2025), which introduced vision-oriented contrastive objectives using image perturbations with a fixed response. SymMPO departs from this paradigm by arguing that existing vision-oriented objectives are mathematically non-rigorous (partition functions Z(m_w,x) and Z(m_l,x) cannot be canceled when images differ) and use indirect preference supervision (same response for different images rather than response pairs). The proposed symmetric pairwise formulation restores theoretical consistency with standard DPO by pairing each contrastive image with its own preferred response, and adds a preference margin consistency regularization absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the partition-function cancellation flaw provides a principled justification for why existing vision-oriented DPO variants may be suboptimal, and the gradient analysis (Appendix B) clarifies that the ignored term acts as a context-dependent offset that prevents adaptive weighting across image pairs. However, the practical gains over mDPO are modest (e.g., +1.5 aAcc on HallusionBench-7B), and the method underperforms plain DPO on Object-HalBench due to caption-anchored data construction limitations. Open questions include whether the symmetric formulation generalizes beyond LLaVA-1.5, whether the partition-function correction yields larger benefits at scale, and whether the additional cost of generating preferred responses for contrastive images is justified by the marginal improvements.
