## Problem

Traditional rigid-body docking methods and existing deep learning approaches fail to capture the adaptive conformational changes that targets undergo upon binding different drugs, particularly substantial backbone and side-chain rearrangements. While Transformers and GNNs can encode structural relationships, they lack explicit mechanisms for enforcing physical plausibility (bond geometry, van der Waals exclusion, electrostatics) and for generating drug-specific adaptive conformations within a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250063/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein 3D structure (decomposed into a residue-level backbone graph and a heavy-atom side-chain graph) and a drug molecule (as SMILES converted to 3D via RDKit, then represented as an atomic graph) as input. It produces all-atom 3D coordinates of the full target-drug complex (backbone residues, side-chain atoms, and drug atoms) as the primary output, and a scalar binding affinity score as a secondary output. An initial drug pose is generated via AutoDock pre-docking to provide geometric initialization before the diffusion-based refinement.

## Method

DynaPhArM is an SE(3)-Equivariant Transformer that encodes target and drug graphs through a cooperative scalar-vector representation with bidirectional gated updates coupling geometric (vector) and semantic (scalar) channels, followed by vector-quantization of backbone embeddings into discrete tokens via the FoldToken4 codebook. A physics-constrained interaction module extends cross-attention by adding Lennard-Jones potential scores (computed from AutoDock docking) as additive priors to the attention logits across six directional backbone/side-chain/drug pairs, producing a drug-specific fused embedding. A conditional DDPM then denoises a latent variable conditioned on this fused embedding to generate a conformation-aware refined representation, which an SE(3)-equivariant decoder maps to 3D coordinates. Training uses a multi-task loss combining structure reconstruction, diffusion noise prediction, and seven physics/geometry constraints (bond lengths, angles, dihedrals, vdW, electrostatics, hydrogen bonds, π-π stacking) with dynamically adaptive inverse-proportional weighting to balance the competing objectives.

## Results

On the primary complex structure reconstruction benchmark (21,762 PDB/DrugBank complexes), DynaPhArM achieves the lowest overall RMSD of 2.01±1.85 Å, side-chain RMSD of 0.29±1.04 Å, and ligand RMSD of 2.17±1.45 Å, with success rates (RMSD<2 Å) of 65.30%, 70.50%, and 61.60% respectively. In cross-docking (CDK2, EGFR, CASF2016-Drug, DUDE27-HoloEns, ANN), DynaPhArM attains the highest overall success rate on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., 56.01% on CDK2, 70.06% on CASF2016-Drug, 60.85% on ANN). For binding affinity prediction, DynaPhArM achieves the best PCC of 0.820 and lowest RMSE of 1.20, outperforming the second-best method (GraphWater-Net, PCC 0.814, RMSE 1.28).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaPhArM outperforms all 11 structural baselines on every RMSD metric in Table 1. The closest competitor, FlexPose (GNN-based, 2.44 Å overall RMSD), is surpassed by 0.43 Å in overall RMSD and by 31.36 percentage points in ligand success rate (61.60% vs. 30.24%), indicating DynaPhArM's diffusion module is critical for ligand pose accuracy. Among diffusion-based methods, PackDock (2.52 Å) and SurfDock (2.55 Å) trail DynaPhArM by 0.51 and 0.54 Å respectively in overall RMSD. In cross-docking (Table 2), DynaPhArM leads on 4 of 5 datasets; the only near-tie is CASF2016-Drug where it edges out FlexPose by 0.82 percentage points (70.06% vs. 69.24%). For binding affinity prediction (Table 3), DynaPhArM (PCC 0.820, RMSE 1.20) beats the runner-up GraphWater-Net (PCC 0.814, RMSE 1.28) and the transformer-based Interformer (PCC 0.802, RMSE 1.27), with margins of 0.006 PCC and 0.08 RMSE. Conventional docking tools (Glide, RosettaLigand, AutoDock) are substantially outperformed, with overall RMSDs 4–11 Å higher than DynaPhArM.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaPhArM extends the SE(3)-Transformer architecture (Fuchs et al., 2020) by introducing a cooperative scalar-vector coupling mechanism that overcomes the channel decoupling in the original formulation, and integrates a conditional DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) for generating conformation-aware latent embeddings. It departs from prior flexible docking methods (FlexPose, PackDock, DynamicBind, SurfDock) by embedding Lennard-Jones interaction potentials directly into the cross-attention computation as physics-constrained priors, and by discretizing backbone representations via the FoldToken4 codebook (Gao et al., 2024) for more compact structural encoding. The multi-task loss with seven geometric/physical regularization terms and adaptive inverse-proportional weighting represents a new loss design that goes beyond the structure-reconstruction or denoising objectives used in prior diffusion-based dockers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent SOTA across structure reconstruction, cross-docking (including out-of-distribution scenarios like apo-holo and ANN), and binding affinity prediction positions DynaPhArM as a versatile tool for structure-based drug design, particularly for targets undergoing substantial conformational changes upon ligand binding. The drug-specific adaptation demonstrated in the case study (3APW with Disopyramide vs. Zolpidem) suggests the model can capture how a single target reorganizes for different ligands, which is relevant for understanding selectivity and off-target effects. Key limitations include the dependency on AutoDock-generated LJ scores as input priors (introducing a circularity concern), the computational cost of the diffusion sampling loop, and the fact that the model was trained and evaluated on PDB-derived complexes, leaving generalization to truly novel targets or multi-target systems untested.
