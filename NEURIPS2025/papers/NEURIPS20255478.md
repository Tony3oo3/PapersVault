## Problem

Approximating an arbitrary finite metric space with a tree metric of minimal distortion is a fundamental problem in hierarchical representation, yet existing methods either lack theoretical distortion guarantees (Neighbor Joining, TREEREP) or provide coarse worst-case bounds without minimizing actual distortion (Gromov's algorithm). No prior approach combines differentiable optimization with provable distortion improvements over the Gromov baseline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255478/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite metric space (X, d_X) specified by its n×n distance matrix (e.g., shortest-path distances on a graph or cosine distances on a feature matrix) and a designated root node w. It produces an optimized surrogate distance matrix D* and, via Gromov's tree embedding applied to D*, a finite metric tree (T, d_T) with an explicit embedding Φ: X → T. The distance matrix need not satisfy the triangle inequality in the input (e.g., cosine distance), as the Floyd-Warshall projection step repairs violations during optimization.

## Method

DELTAZERO formulates tree metric approximation as a differentiable optimization over the space of distance matrices, minimizing a combined loss of ℓ₂ fidelity to the input metric and a smooth log-sum-exp surrogate of Gromov hyperbolicity. The key technical insight is a two-level smoothing: the inner LSE_{-λ} replaces the min in the four-point condition, the outer LSE_λ replaces the max over all quadruples, yielding a fully differentiable hyperbolicity penalty. To make computation tractable, the O(n⁴) quadruple evaluation is replaced by K independent batches of m points with an additional LSE aggregation, reducing cost to O(K·m⁴). Optimization proceeds via projected gradient descent: ADAM steps on the upper-triangular entries of the distance matrix, followed by a Floyd-Warshall projection onto the metric cone (the set of valid distance matrices). A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) shows that when the regularization parameter μ ≥ 1/(2 log₂(n−2)), the final tree embedding achieves strictly smaller worst-case distortion than applying Gromov's embedding directly to the original metric.

## Results

DELTAZERO achieves the lowest ℓ∞ distortion across all seven benchmark datasets (five unweighted graphs and two non-graph metrics). The largest relative improvements over the second-best method are 43.8% on C-ELEGAN (1.87 vs. Gromov's 3.33) and 44.1% on ZEISEL (0.24 vs. Gromov's 0.43). On the largest graph (CORA, n=2485), DELTAZERO reaches 7.59 ℓ∞ error, outperforming LayeringTree (7.76) by 2.3%. The method also improves on the ℓ₁ average distortion metric, though NJ remains best on that criterion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DELTAZERO outperforms all five baselines on every dataset in ℓ∞ distortion. The second-best method varies by dataset: Gromov's embedding is closest on C-ELEGAN, CS PHD, ZEISEL, and IBD, while LayeringTree is closest on CORA, AIRPORT, and WIKI (the three largest unweighted graphs where it is applicable). NJ is competitive on IBD (0.90 vs. DELTAZERO's 0.70) but lags significantly on the larger graphs (e.g., 16.81 on CS PHD vs. 10.31). TREERep and HCC are consistently the weakest baselines, with HCC performing worst on CS PHD (23.35) and CORA (12.28). Notably, DELTAZERO's advantage is most pronounced on datasets with low intrinsic hyperbolicity (small δ_X), such as C-ELEGAN (δ=1.5) and ZEISEL (δ=0.19), where the optimization can push the surrogate metric much closer to a true tree metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Gromov's 1987 theorem establishing that δ-hyperbolic metric spaces admit tree embeddings with O(δ log n) additive distortion, and on the Gromov tree embedding procedure (via Single Linkage Hierarchical Clustering) as a final step. It departs from prior tree metric fitting methods—Neighbor Joining, TREEREP, HCCROOTEDTREEFIT, and LayeringTree—which are either heuristic without distortion guarantees or provide coarse worst-case bounds—by formulating the problem as a differentiable optimization over distance matrices. The key technical departure is the introduction of a log-sum-exp smooth surrogate for Gromov hyperbolicity combined with batched sampling, enabling gradient-based optimization with a projected gradient descent scheme where the projection onto the metric cone is solved via Floyd-Warshall.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DELTAZERO provides the first differentiable framework for tree metric approximation that simultaneously achieves improved worst-case distortion guarantees (Theorem 3.1) and consistently outperforms all baselines in ℓ∞ distortion across seven diverse datasets. The batched hyperbolicity estimator, while theoretically justified only under a restrictive uniform random metric assumption, performs well empirically and opens a path toward scalable curvature-aware metric learning. Key limitations include the O(T·n³) runtime dominated by Floyd-Warshall projections, the non-convexity of the objective, and the gap between ℓ₂ optimization and ℓ∞ distortion control. The speculative but intriguing connection between Gromov hyperbolicity and oversquashing in GNNs suggests a broader research direction for curvature-aware graph learning.
