## Problem

When offline RL data is collected under different transition dynamics than the online environment (shifted dynamics), existing methods that penalize or filter offline transitions rely on KL divergence or mutual information to estimate the dynamics gap, which can be ill-defined when the two dynamics have mismatched support. This paper seeks a principled, well-defined measure of the dynamics gap that remains stable under such distributional mismatch and can guide both data selection and exploration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253095/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an offline dataset of (s, a, s', r) tuples from a source environment and a limited stream of online transitions from the target environment, where both share the same state and action spaces but differ in transition dynamics. It produces a learned policy for the target environment and, as an intermediate artifact, a per-state-action dynamics gap estimate Δ̂(s,a) computed as a Monte Carlo approximation of the 2-Wasserstein distance between the offline and online conditional next-state distributions. The gap estimate is used both to filter which offline transitions enter the replay buffer (keeping the lowest-ξ% gap fraction) and to shape exploration bonuses in the Q-target.

## Method

COMPFLOW introduces a composite flow architecture: a pretrained offline flow transports a Gaussian latent to the offline transition distribution, and an online flow (trained via optimal-transport flow matching) then transports from that offline output distribution to the online transition distribution, rather than starting from a Gaussian prior. The transport cost for the online flow incorporates the conditioning variables (s, a) with a regularization weight η, enabling the OT coupling to align samples with similar state-action pairs even in continuous spaces. The resulting expected squared displacement of the composite flow yields a Monte Carlo estimator of W₂(p_off(·|s,a), p_on(·|s,a)), providing a well-defined dynamics gap that avoids the support-mismatch pathology of KL-based metrics. Theoretically, the composite structure provably tightens the generalization bound whenever the offline distribution is closer to the online one than a Gaussian is (Theorem 3.1), and an optimistic exploration bonus β·Δ̂(s,a) provably reduces the performance gap to the optimal policy (Theorem 3.5). Practically, the method is instantiated on SAC with dual critics, a behavior-cloning regularizer on the actor, and rejection sampling to retain only the lowest-gap fraction of offline transitions per minibatch.

## Results

On the ODRL benchmark (27 tasks across HalfCheetah, Hopper, and Walker2D with morphology, kinematic, and friction shifts, using D4RL medium/medium-replay/medium-expert datasets), COMPFLOW achieves an average return of 2193 after 40K environment interactions, outperforming the strongest baseline BC-SAC (1920) by 14.2%. COMPFLOW attains the best return on 19/27 tasks and ties for best on 5 additional tasks. In the wildlife conservation task, COMPFLOW outperforms the best baseline BC-PAR by 8.8% and improves over SAC from scratch by 20.8% under the same interaction budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMPFLOW outperforms all four baselines on average: BC-SAC (1920), BC-VGDF (1868), BC-PAR (1803), H2O (1783), and SAC without offline data (878). Against the strongest baseline BC-SAC, COMPFLOW wins on 23/27 tasks and matches on the remaining 3, with the largest margins on HalfCheetah Morphology (e.g., 3119 vs. 2495 on MR, +25%) and Hopper Friction (e.g., 280 vs. 228 on MR, +23%). The paper attributes the weaker performance of H2O and BC-PAR to their reliance on KL divergence or mutual information for dynamics-gap estimation, which becomes ill-defined under mismatched support. BC-VGDF, which filters data based on value-target consistency, is similarly limited by bootstrapping bias and target nonstationarity. In the wildlife conservation experiment, COMPFLOW outperforms BC-PAR by 8.8% and SAC by 20.8%, confirming the advantage of Wasserstein-based gap estimation in a realistic transfer setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMPFLOW builds on the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) and its optimal transport variant (Pooladian et al., 2023), repurposing the OT-FM connection to estimate a Wasserstein-based dynamics gap between offline and online transition distributions. It departs from prior off-dynamics RL methods (H2O, BC-PAR, BC-VGDF) that rely on KL divergence or mutual information—metrics that become ill-defined under support mismatch—by instead leveraging the composite flow architecture, where the online flow is initialized from the offline flow's output distribution rather than a Gaussian prior. This is distinct from the closest OT-based competitor (Lyu et al., ICLR 2025), which estimates gap on concatenated (s,a,s') tuples in a fully offline setting rather than on conditional transition distributions, and thus cannot support the exploration bonus used here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: offline RL data collected under one set of dynamics (e.g., a different robot morphology or a different national park) is often unusable under standard offline-to-online methods when the target environment's dynamics have shifted. The Wasserstein-based gap estimator provides a more robust and well-defined criterion than KL/MI alternatives, and the composite flow structure offers a principled way to improve generalization under limited online data. Key limitations include the requirement of shared state/action spaces, evaluation entirely in simulation, and the absence of uncertainty quantification in the gap estimate. Open questions include whether the approach extends to latent-space dynamics comparison, real-world deployment, and how to calibrate the exploration bonus β adaptively.
