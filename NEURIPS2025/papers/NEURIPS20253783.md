## Problem

Current video generation benchmarks (VBench, VideoPhy, T2V-CompBench) evaluate generic quality metrics like temporal flickering and aesthetic score but lack the shot-level cinematic structure and fine-grained control dimensions that professional filmmaking demands. There is no standardized framework that decomposes a shot into its constituent creative controls—setup, events, lighting, and camera—nor a benchmark that tests whether T2V models can honor each control independently, leaving the question 'Are current models ready for professional use?' unanswered.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253783/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes structured text prompts (2,089 total) that embed sampled values from 76 taxonomy control nodes, paired with generated video clips (preprocessed at 2 fps, native resolution) and per-node evaluation questions targeting a single cinematic parameter. Human annotators (84 film professionals) produce 1–5 alignment ratings per video-question pair (248,536 total annotations across 13 models and 20K videos), while the fine-tuned VLM evaluator takes one video, its prompt, and a single evaluation question as input and outputs a scalar score used to derive binary pairwise preferences.

## Method

The core contribution is a four-pillar hierarchical taxonomy (Setup, Events, Lighting, Camera) with 76 leaf-level control nodes, developed iteratively with industry professionals to capture the full spectrum of shot-level cinematic decisions. Prompts are generated in two stages: SCINE-Scripts encode narrative content via LLM sampling from the Events taxonomy, then SCINE-Visuals enrich them by injecting sampled values from Camera, Lighting, and Setup nodes, enabling structured prompt upsampling that mirrors the script-to-shot workflow. An automatic pipeline categorizes each prompt element back to its taxonomy node and generates a targeted evaluation question per node, supporting disentangled per-control annotation. For scalable evaluation, the authors fine-tune Qwen-2.5-VL-7B with a Bradley-Terry pairwise objective and a linear projection head over the final token, trained on 44,062 aggregated human preference samples, achieving 72.36% pairwise accuracy—roughly 20 points above the zero-shot 7B baseline and outperforming larger 32B/72B zero-shot variants.

## Results

Across 13 T2V models evaluated on 2,089 SCINE prompts with 248,536 expert annotations, WAN-14B and Minimax emerge as top performers on both SCINE-Scripts (narrative events) and SCINE-Visuals (cinematic controls). Models struggle most with Events and Camera pillars, while Setup yields the highest absolute scores for top models and Lighting shows the most consistent performance across the board. For the automatic VLM evaluator, the fine-tuned Qwen-2.5-VL-7B achieves 72.36% pairwise preference accuracy aligned with human annotators, representing an absolute improvement of ~20% over the zero-shot 7B baseline, with no significant gains observed when scaling to 32B or 72B zero-shot models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 13 T2V models evaluated, WAN-14B and Minimax form the top tier, with Minimax leading in nearly all Events sub-categories (especially Dialogues and Change in Environment) and WAN-14B outperforming in Advanced Controls and showing the lowest standard deviation across genres. Luma Ray 2, Hunyuan, and WAN-1B form a second tier, while LTX-Video consistently underperforms across all pillars. All models show performance degradation on advanced prompts (more control nodes specified), with the largest drops in Lighting Source, Color Temperature, and Creative Intent. For the VLM evaluator, the fine-tuned Qwen-2.5-VL-7B (72.36% accuracy) outperforms all zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct baselines (7B, 32B, 72B) by approximately 20% absolute, demonstrating that model scale alone is insufficient without domain-specific fine-tuning on expert annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing T2V evaluation benchmarks (VBench, VideoPhy, T2V-CompBench) that use fixed prompt sets lacking shot-level cinematic structure, and from structured video generation approaches (MovieNet, VDS, VAST, VideoGen-of-Thought) that focus on multi-shot or storyboard-level planning rather than fine-grained single-shot control. It builds on Qwen2.5-VL as the base architecture for a fine-tuned VLM evaluator trained with a Bradley-Terry objective, but the core contribution is a taxonomy-driven evaluation framework that formalizes 76 cinematic control nodes into four hierarchical pillars (Setup, Events, Lighting, Camera). The prompt generation pipeline leverages LLMs for screenplay creation and taxonomy-guided upsampling, extending prior work on LLM-based screenplay generation into a structured, reproducible benchmark construction process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCINE provides the first structured, extensible taxonomy for evaluating professional video generation, filling a gap where existing benchmarks omit critical cinematic controls like camera trajectory, lighting position, and event temporal structure. The finding that even top-performing models (WAN-14B, Minimax) show substantial and consistent gaps in Events and Camera controls, with performance degrading further under joint multi-control specification, highlights that current T2V models are not yet production-ready for professional workflows. Open questions include whether fine-tuning or customization can close these gaps, whether the taxonomy adequately captures cross-cultural cinematic conventions, and whether the 72.36%-accuracy VLM evaluator can be further improved to enable truly scalable professional evaluation without human annotation.
