## Problem

Existing deep low-light image enhancement (LLIE) methods, particularly Transformer-based architectures, incur high computational and energy costs that limit deployment on resource-constrained devices. While spiking neural networks (SNNs) offer event-driven, energy-efficient computation, they have been almost exclusively applied to classification or detection tasks and remain largely unexplored for continuous-tone regression problems like image enhancement. This paper pioneers the synergistic integration of SNNs into a Retinex-based Transformer architecture for LLIE, addressing both the energy bottleneck and the representational challenge of fine-grained pixel-level restoration in the spike domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light RGB image I ∈ [0,1]^{H×W×3} and a derived channel-average illumination prior L_p, encoding both via a log(1+ζz) transform and T-normalizing the injected current across T discrete time steps (default T=8). The output is a single enhanced RGB image produced by a one-step readout at the final time step: Y = clip(I_lu + Head(H_out[T]), 0, 1), where I_lu is the light-up image from the illumination estimator and Head is a 1×1 convolution to 3 channels. The entire pipeline operates in the spike domain with LIF neurons, and the readout is identical in training and inference.

## Method

Spike-RetinexFormer is a one-stage Retinex-based pipeline composed of two spiking modules: a shallow Spiking Illumination Estimator (E) that predicts a light-up map and illumination features, and a U-Net-style Spiking Corruption Restorer (G) that refines the light-up image via residual prediction. The key architectural innovation is the Spiking Illumination-Guided Attention (SIGA) block, which replaces dense QK^T matrix multiplication and softmax with spike-coincidence hard gating: when a query and key neuron fire simultaneously, a binary gate opens to route value spikes, with per-head FiLM parameters predicted from scale-aligned illumination features modulating projections and gating thresholds. All layers use Leaky Integrate-and-Fire neurons with soft reset, trained end-to-end via surrogate gradients with a composite loss combining L1 image fidelity, a firing-rate regularizer, and temporal total variation to suppress flicker. The design achieves competitive PSNR (25.50 dB on LOL-v1) with ~1.5M parameters and 16.2G FLOPs while reducing theoretical energy by ~77% relative to the ANN RetinexFormer baseline.

## Results

On the standard LOL-v1 benchmark, Spike-RetinexFormer achieves 25.50 dB PSNR and 0.842 SSIM with 1.50M parameters and 16.2G FLOPs, surpassing RetinexFormer (25.16 dB, 0.845 SSIM) in PSNR while matching its SSIM. On the more challenging LOL-v2 synthetic split, it reaches 26.47 dB PSNR / 0.938 SSIM, a 0.8 dB gain over RetinexFormer. Across the extremely dark SID, SMID, and SDSD datasets, the method attains 24.68, 29.43, 30.45 (indoor), and 30.37 (outdoor) dB PSNR respectively, consistently outperforming all ANN baselines by 0.2–0.9 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spike-RetinexFormer outperforms all nine ANN baselines on PSNR across every evaluated dataset. The closest competitor is RetinexFormer [43], which the method surpasses by +0.34 dB on LOL-v1 (25.50 vs 25.16), +0.58 dB on LOL-v2-Real (23.38 vs 22.80), and +0.80 dB on LOL-v2-Synthetic (26.47 vs 25.67), while SSIM is within 0.003 on all splits. Against the next-strongest baseline SNR-Net [56], gains are larger: +0.89 dB on LOL-v1 and +2.33 dB on LOL-v2-Synthetic. Heavier Transformer-based methods like Restormer [26] (26.10M params, 144.3G FLOPs) and MIRNet [54] (31.60M, 785G) trail by 3–8 dB in PSNR despite 17–21× more parameters. On the extremely dark SDSD dataset, the method leads RetinexFormer by +0.68 dB (indoor) and +0.53 dB (outdoor), and outperforms Uformer [55] by over 6 dB on SDSD-outdoor. The only metric where the method is marginally behind RetinexFormer is SSIM on LOL-v1 (0.842 vs 0.845), a negligible 0.003 gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct spiking re-instantiation of RetinexFormer (Cai et al., ICCV 2023), replacing its continuous-valued Transformer blocks with LIF spiking neurons and a novel Spiking Illumination-Guided Attention (SIGA) mechanism that substitutes dense softmax attention with spike-coincidence hard gating. It also draws on the broader spiking Transformer literature (SpikFormer, Spike-Driven Transformer, Masked Spiking Transformer) for the general idea of event-driven attention, but departs from those works by targeting a continuous-tone regression task (pixel-level image enhancement) rather than classification or detection. The Retinex decomposition framework (illumination × reflectance) is inherited from the classical vision literature and prior deep Retinex methods, while the FiLM-based illumination conditioning and U-Net backbone follow standard low-level vision design patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a spiking architecture can match strong ANN baselines on LLIE (within ~0.3 dB on LOL-v1) while theoretically reducing energy by 5–10×, opening a pathway toward energy-efficient low-level vision on neuromorphic hardware. However, all energy savings remain theoretical—no power measurements on actual spiking hardware are provided—and the SIGA mechanism's spike-co-firing computation can still scale as O(N²) in high-illumination regions where sparsity collapses. Key open questions include hardware-validated efficiency, cross-domain generalization (RAW sensors, video), and whether the modest PSNR gains over RetinexFormer persist at higher resolutions or on more diverse datasets. The work is a proof-of-concept that the SNN + Transformer + Retinex combination is viable for continuous image restoration, but practical deployment on resource-constrained devices remains unproven.
