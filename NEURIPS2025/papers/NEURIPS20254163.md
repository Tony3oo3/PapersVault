## Problem

Existing theoretical analysis of coevolutionary algorithms (CoEAs) focuses on runtime to *discover* a Nash equilibrium, but provides no guarantees that a discovered equilibrium will be *retained* once found. Without such retention guarantees, a CoEA could lose a Nash equilibrium to mutation after discovery, undermining confidence in its output and making termination decisions unreliable. This paper fills this gap by developing a general framework for proving when a CoEA is 'stable'—i.e., overwhelmingly likely to maintain a Nash equilibrium following discovery.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a description of the selection operator (from which two bounding functions α and β are derived via routine computation) and a target stability proportion γ ∈ (0,1], and produces a critical mutation threshold q₀(γ) that separates the regime where γ-stability is guaranteed from where instability is inevitable. The threshold is expressed as a supremum over a one-dimensional optimization problem involving α and β, making it computable for any concrete algorithm. When combined with a runtime bound, the stability guarantee additionally yields an ε(λ)-Hannan consistency (regret) bound where ε decays exponentially in population size λ.

## Method

The core contribution is a pair of complementary lemmas: Lemma 3.3 provides sufficient conditions for γ-stability by requiring that the product of mutation survival probability q and the selection-induced probabilities α(a,γ/a)·β(a,γ/a) exceeds γ uniformly over a ∈ [γ,1]; Lemma 3.4 provides necessary conditions for instability under unbiased mutation on bitstrings, with a stronger conclusion (A2) showing the Nash proportion drops to near-zero in O(n²) generations. The proof technique uses McDiarmid's inequality to show the number of Nash pairs concentrates around its expectation (preventing sudden drops when the expectation exceeds γλ²), and additive drift analysis to show rapid decay when the expectation falls below. Applied to PDCoEA and TSCoEA, the framework yields explicit closed-form thresholds (e.g., q₀(γ) = (3√γ − 2γ)⁻² for PDCoEA on strict Nash singleton problems) that are shown to be tight via matching instability constructions, and empirical validation on Bilinear, PlantedBilinear, and MBJR_2024 games confirms the predicted stability boundaries.

## Results

The paper derives necessary and sufficient stability thresholds q₀(γ) for CoEAs to retain a strict Nash equilibrium after discovery. For PDCoEA on STRICTNASH ∩ SINGLETONSOLUTION, the critical threshold is q₀(γ) = (3√γ − 2γ)⁻² (Theorem 3.7), while for TSCoEA on STRICTNASH ∩ ZEROSUM it is q₀(γ) = γ/((1−(1−γ)ᵏ)(1−(1−γ)ˡ)) (Theorem 3.8). A universal lower bound shows no CoEA can be γ-stable if mutation strength q < γ (Theorem 3.5). Empirically, across 25 runs per (γ,q) combination with λ=10⁴ and 4×10⁵ evaluations on Bilinear, PlantedBilinear, and MBJR_2024, the observed stability boundary consistently falls between the theoretical stability and instability curves, confirming the derived bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing algorithms in the standard empirical sense; instead, it compares theoretical stability/instability bounds against empirical observations. The key comparison is between the guaranteed-stable region (from Lemma 3.3 / Theorems 3.7–3.8) and the guaranteed-unstable region (from Lemma 3.4 / Theorems 3.5–3.6), with the empirical boundary observed in Figure 2 falling between them for all three problem instances (Bilinear, PlantedBilinear, MBJR_2024). The paper further shows that the empirical boundary shifts depending on the specific game instance, suggesting the gap between the theoretical bounds is due to problem-instance variation rather than loose proofs. For PDCoEA, the bound tightens progressively from STRICTNASH (q₀ = (5√γ − 3γ/2)⁻²) to STRICTNASH ∩ SINGLETONSOLUTION (q₀ = (3√γ − 2γ)⁻²), and the latter is proven best-possible via a matching instability result (Theorem 3.7 C2.2). The regret analysis (Theorem 5.1) shows that combining any polynomial runtime bound with γ-stability yields τ(λ)·e^(−cλ)-Hannan consistency, extending zero-regret guarantees to superpolynomial strategy spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the existing runtime analysis framework for coevolutionary algorithms (CoEAs) — which has focused on expected time to *discover* a Nash equilibrium (Lehre [40], Hevia Fajardo [30,31,32], Benford [5,6]) — by addressing the previously unanalyzed question of whether a discovered equilibrium is *retained*. The stability technique (Lemmas 3.3–3.4) is a direct coevolutionary analogue of error-threshold results from standard EA theory [38,50], repurposed to bound the mutation rate above which populations inevitably lose the solution concept. The algorithmic model (Algorithm 1) is a coevolutionary extension of the PVSA framework [38,52], and the regret guarantee (Theorem 5.1) bridges CoEA stability with the zero-regret/Hannan-consistency literature from online learning [3,23,64].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners configuring CoEAs: the derived bounds (e.g., q₀(γ) for PDCoEA and TSCoEA) give concrete, application-specific thresholds on mutation rate that guarantee the algorithm won't forget a found equilibrium, directly informing parameter selection and termination criteria. A key open question highlighted by the authors is that the proven stable regions do not reach γ=0, meaning no general guarantee exists that even a single Nash-equilibrium individual will be retained — designing algorithms that provably achieve 0-stability on broad problem classes remains unresolved. The empirical finding that the stability/instability boundary shifts across problem instances (Bilinear, PlantedBilinear, MBJR_2024) suggests the theoretical gaps are instance-dependent rather than artifacts of loose proofs, but this is not yet rigorously confirmed.
