## Problem

The Nadaraya-Watson (NW) interpolator is a classical kernel method known to be consistent only when its bandwidth parameter β equals the ambient dimension d, but the generalization behavior for β ≠ d was previously uncharacterized. This paper asks: what is the nature of the inconsistency when β deviates from d — is the overfitting merely tempered (error proportional to noise) or catastrophic (constant error independent of noise)?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250515/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy binary classification dataset S = {(x_i, y_i)}_{i=1}^m ⊂ R^d × {±1} where labels are flipped independently with probability p ∈ (0, 0.49), along with a test point x ∈ R^d, and outputs a binary class label via a sign of an inverse-distance-weighted vote. The predictor interpolates the training set exactly (assigning each training point its own label), and the single tunable hyperparameter β > 0 controls the decay rate of the distance-based weights, determining how local or global the prediction is.

## Method

The paper proves that varying the single hyperparameter β relative to the data dimension d (or intrinsic dimension d_int) produces three distinct overfitting regimes: benign overfitting at β = d, tempered overfitting for β > d (clean error bounded by Õ(p)), and catastrophic overfitting for β < d (constant error independent of p). The key technical insight is characterizing the 'locality' of the predictor: for β > d, the inverse-distance weights decay fast enough that the prediction at any test point depends only on a finite number of nearest neighbors (analogous to k-NN), so only nearby label flips cause errors; for β < d, the weights decay too slowly, making the predictor overly global so that distant points with the opposite label dominate the vote and induce constant misclassification. The proofs leverage order statistics of distances approximated via exponential random variables, concentration inequalities, and an explicit adversarial construction (inner ball vs. outer annulus) for the lower bound. A practical implication is that over-estimating the intrinsic dimension when setting β is far less harmful than under-estimating it, a claim supported by experiments on synthetic data, spherical manifolds, and MNIST.

## Results

The paper proves three regimes for the NW interpolator's clean classification error as a function of the bandwidth parameter β: (1) for β = d, benign overfitting (error → 0 as m → ∞, extending Devroye et al. 1998); (2) for β > d, tempered overfitting with error bounded between Ω(p^{c(β/d)}) and Õ(p), where c(β/d) = (8·2^{β/d}/(β/d−1))^{4/(β/d−1)}; (3) for β < d, catastrophic overfitting where error is Ω(1) independent of noise level p. On MNIST 0/1 data (m=12,665, p=0.1), the optimal β is approximately 8, matching the intrinsic dimension estimate of MNIST, while the extrinsic pixel dimension is 784. On spherical data (S² ⊂ R³), the optimal β is 2 (intrinsic dimension) rather than 3 (ambient dimension).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper that does not compare against competing methods in the traditional sense. Instead, it extends the prior consistency result of Devroye et al. [19] (which only covered β = d) to characterize the full overfitting profile for all β > 0. The key comparison is internal: varying a single hyperparameter β sweeps the NW interpolator non-monotonically from catastrophic (β < d), through benign (β = d), to tempered (β > d) overfitting. The paper also contrasts its proof technique (locality-based analysis) with the spectral analysis approach used in most prior kernel interpolator results [36–41], noting that spectral methods typically require additional assumptions like Gaussian universality. The practical comparison is between over-estimating vs. under-estimating the intrinsic dimension: over-estimating β (leading to tempered overfitting) is substantially less harmful than under-estimating it (which can lead to catastrophic overfitting with constant error regardless of noise level).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical consistency result of Devroye et al. (1998) for the Nadaraya-Watson/Shepard interpolator at β=d into a full characterization of generalization behavior across all bandwidth values, using the benign/tempered/catastrophic taxonomy introduced by Mallinar et al. (2022). Unlike prior kernel-interpolation analyses that typically rely on spectral decompositions and require strong distributional assumptions (e.g., Gaussian universality), the proofs here are based on characterizing the locality of the predictor via order statistics of distances. The paper also connects to the long history of inverse distance weighting (Shepard, 1968) and Hilbert kernel estimation, providing the first known example of a single kernel provably exhibiting all three overfitting regimes by varying only the bandwidth parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single classical interpolating rule sweeps through catastrophic, benign, and tempered overfitting as one hyperparameter varies is a clean and instructive counterpoint to the common narrative that overfitting is uniformly harmful or uniformly benign. The practical asymmetry—over-estimating the intrinsic dimension of the data is far less damaging than under-estimating it—gives practitioners a concrete heuristic for tuning β, supported by experiments on MNIST where the optimal β≈8 matches intrinsic-dimension estimates rather than the 784-pixel ambient dimension. The main open question is formalizing the conjecture that d can be replaced by the intrinsic dimension d_int in all results, which the authors note is not covered by their current proofs. More broadly, the paper motivates revisiting other classical learning rules through the modern overfitting taxonomy rather than the older consistency-vs.-inconsistency dichotomy.
