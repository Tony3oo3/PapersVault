## Problem

Off-policy DRL agents suffer from a fundamental divergence between the behavior policy that generated stored experiences and the target policy being optimized, leading to instability and requiring large replay buffers and many training steps. Existing causal bounds in the Neyman-Rubin framework bound the counterfactual loss, but no prior work bounds the factual (on-policy) loss using observed off-policy data. This paper closes that gap by establishing a causal upper bound on the factual loss, enabling a principled bridge between on-policy and off-policy optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard environment observations (84×84 grayscale stacked frames for Atari, continuous state vectors for MuJoCo) and an experience replay buffer whose transitions are augmented with a single 64-bit scalar: the Q-value (or V-value) computed by the behavior policy at the moment the experience was collected. This stored value is normally discarded after action selection. The output is the standard policy action; the only architectural change is the extra scalar in each buffer entry, adding 0.003% space overhead for a 4K buffer.

## Method

SUFT establishes a causal upper bound theorem showing that the expected factual loss is bounded by the expected counterfactual loss plus an estimated treatment effect term plus a constant δ independent of the network. Translated to DRL, the on-policy loss is bounded by the standard off-policy temporal-difference loss plus a SUFT OPE term (the loss between the stored behavior-policy Q/V value and the current target-policy Q/V value on the same state-action pair) plus δ. Because δ is constant with respect to the network, it is dropped during gradient descent, and the optimized objective becomes the standard off-policy loss plus λ_TF · SUFT OPE term. The key practical mechanism is recycling value-network outputs that are already computed during action selection but normally thrown away: for Q-networks, the single Q(s,a;θ_behavior) is appended to each transition tuple; for V-networks, V(s;θ_behavior) is stored. The bound is generalized from a single control treatment to N control treatments (matching the N distinct behavior policies present in a replay buffer), and the method applies uniformly to DQN, Double DQN, SAC, and PPO agents.

## Results

On the Atari 2600 benchmark (57 games, 4K replay buffer, 400K steps), SUFT DQN achieves a Human Normalized Mean of 63.45%, outperforming Vanilla DQN (41.26%), Double DQN (42.31%), and DQN with a 25× larger 100K buffer (29.29%). Across agents, SUFT yields mean reward ratio improvements of 383.19% (DQN), 226.91% (Double DQN), 197.34% (PPO) on Atari, and 224.53% (SAC), 111.21% (PPO) on MuJoCo. The method reduces the required replay buffer size by up to 96% while outperforming a 25× larger buffer by 437.05%, at only 0.8% additional computational overhead and 0.003% space overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUFT DQN (63.45% Human Normalized Mean) decisively outperforms both Vanilla DQN [Mnih et al., 2015] at 41.26% and Double DQN [Van Hasselt et al., 2016] at 42.31%, representing gains of 53.8% and 49.9% respectively, all under the same 4K buffer constraint. Against the resource-increased baseline of DQN with a 100K buffer (29.29%), SUFT with only a 4K buffer achieves a 437.05% mean reward ratio improvement, demonstrating that the causal OPE term is far more effective than simply storing more experiences. In the controlled same-agent comparison (identical architecture, only the SUFT term added), Double DQN SUFT beats its baseline in 35 of 40 valid Atari games with a 226.91% mean reward ratio, and PPO SUFT beats its baseline in 39 of 42 valid games with a 197.34% mean reward ratio. On MuJoCo, SAC SUFT surpasses its baseline in 4 of 5 environments (224.53% mean reward ratio), and PPO SUFT in 4 of 5 (111.21%). An inertia regularizer ablation confirms the gains stem from the causal bound mechanism rather than mere regularization, as the regularizer degrades Double DQN to 83.21% mean reward ratio versus SUFT's 226.91%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neyman-Rubin potential outcomes framework (Shalit et al., 2017) by reversing the traditional loss-bounding direction: instead of bounding the counterfactual loss (as in prior causal ML work), it establishes a bound on the factual loss, which maps naturally to the on-policy loss in DRL. It also generalizes from single-intervention treatments to policy-level treatments (sequences of actions) and from one control to N controls, matching the multi-policy nature of experience replay buffers. On the DRL side, it builds directly on standard DQN and PPO by adding a single OPE-inspired loss term computed from Q/V values that are already calculated during action selection but normally discarded.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is notable: a single stored scalar per experience (0.003% space overhead, 0.8% compute overhead) yields up to 383% reward ratio improvement and allows 96% buffer reduction, making DRL substantially more accessible under resource constraints. The inertia-regularizer ablation (Section 5.5) is important for credibility, showing the gains stem from the causal bound structure rather than mere weight regularization. Open questions include whether the L2-loss assumption (which holds empirically ~82% of the time but lacks a theoretical guarantee) limits the bound's tightness, whether the constrained-buffer experimental setup generalizes to larger-scale training, and whether the approach transfers to physical robotics where the paper identifies as a promising but untested domain.
