## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D segmentation methods either rely on a single 3D language field (suffering from weak feature distinctiveness and boundary artifacts) or on pre-computed class-agnostic segmentations followed by post-selection (suffering from error accumulation). The fundamental gap is the lack of a mechanism to integrate complementary segmentation and language cues throughout both training and inference, which is necessary for high-quality open-vocabulary 3D segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250379/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view posed RGB images (reconstructed into a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation) and a natural language text query (or optionally a novel query image) as input. It produces a 3D Gaussian point-level segmentation — a subset of Gaussians corresponding to the queried object — which can be rendered into multi-view 2D segmentation views for evaluation. The 2D supervision signals come from SAM-generated instance masks and CLIP/SigLIP language feature maps extracted from the training views.

## Method

COS3D introduces a collaborative field comprising a 16-dim instance field and a 512-dim CLIP-aligned language field, both attached per-Gaussian in a 3D-GS representation. Training uses a two-stage strategy: first, the instance field is learned via InfoNCE contrastive loss with SAM mask supervision to encode discriminative, boundary-aware segmentation features; second, an instance-to-language (Ins2Lang) mapping (implemented as shallow MLPs or a Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression) transfers the instance feature space to the language feature space, constructing the language field efficiently without per-Gaussian language optimization. At inference, a text query is encoded by CLIP to produce a 3D relevance map from the language field, which serves as a prompt for an adaptive language-to-instance (Lang2Ins) refinement: starting from high-relevance Gaussians, the method expands into local neighborhoods defined by instance-feature cosine similarity and adaptively filters regions using opacity-weighted relevance scores, yielding boundary-aware segmentation that leverages both fields collaboratively.

## Results

On the LeRF benchmark, COS3D with kernel regression achieves a mean mIoU of 50.76 and mAcc of 72.08, setting a new state-of-the-art over all language-based and segmentation-based baselines. On ScanNetv2 (19-class protocol), the same configuration reaches 32.47 mIoU and 49.05 mAcc, again leading all compared methods. The shallow MLP variant is slightly lower (50.76 → 49.75 mIoU on LeRF) but still outperforms every baseline. Training is notably efficient: the full pipeline converges in ~50 minutes on a single RTX-4090, and even a reduced 8-minute run (3K instance-field steps) already reaches 50.16 mIoU, surpassing all baselines trained for 60–240 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COS3D (kernel regression) outperforms all five baselines on both benchmarks. Against the strongest language-based method, Dr. Splat [12], it gains +7.18 mIoU (50.76 vs. 43.58) and +8.21 mAcc on LeRF, and against the strongest segmentation-based method, InstanceGaussian [17], it gains +5.46 mIoU (50.76 vs. 45.30). The gap over the earlier language-based methods LangSplat [10] (9.66 mIoU) and LEGaussians [11] (16.21 mIoU) is particularly large, exceeding 34 mIoU points, underscoring the benefit of the collaborative instance–language design over direct language distillation. On ScanNetv2 (19 classes), COS3D beats OpenGaussian [16] by +7.74 mIoU (32.47 vs. 24.73) and +7.51 mAcc (49.05 vs. 41.54), while the shallow MLP variant is competitive on mIoU (26.72 vs. 24.73) but slightly trails on mAcc (39.89 vs. 41.54). Notably, COS3D achieves its best results with only ~50 minutes of training, compared to 60–240 minutes for the baselines, making it both more accurate and more efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for open-vocabulary 3D segmentation on Gaussian splatting: language-based methods (LeRF, LangSplat, Dr.Splat) that directly distill CLIP features into a 3D language field, and segmentation-based methods (OpenGaussian, InstanceGaussian) that perform class-agnostic segmentation followed by language-based post-selection. COS3D introduces a 'collaborative field' that unifies both by learning a boundary-aware instance field (via SAM-supervised contrastive learning) and a text-aligned language field, connected through an instance-to-language mapping (either a shallow MLP or a Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression) during training and an adaptive language-to-instance prompt refinement during inference. The 3D-GS backbone and differentiable rendering pipeline follow standard practice, while the two-stage training strategy and the prompt-refinement inference are the core architectural novelties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COS3D sets new SOTA on both LeRF and ScanNetv2 with substantially better training efficiency (50 min vs. 240 min for earlier language-based methods), making open-vocabulary 3D segmentation more accessible. The finding that a learning-free kernel regression outperforms a learned MLP for the instance-to-language mapping is practically useful, suggesting that when the source features are already discriminative, complex learned mappings may be unnecessary. Key open questions remain: the method lacks reasoning for relational or multi-object queries, operates only in an offline setting, and its reliance on SAM for instance supervision means it inherits SAM's segmentation errors. Extensions to online settings and integration with LLM-based reasoning for complex queries are natural next steps.
