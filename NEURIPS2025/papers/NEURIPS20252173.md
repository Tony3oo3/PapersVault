## Problem

Event-based vision research is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale, multi-view-consistent event datasets. Existing synthesis approaches either rely on dense RGB videos (lacking viewpoint diversity and geometric consistency), expensive hardware rigs (difficult to scale), or graphics engines (introducing domain gaps from non-photorealistic rendering). Additionally, most simulators use fixed contrast thresholds, creating a significant distribution shift between synthetic and real event streams that limits model generalization.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes sparse multi-view RGB images (approximately 30 views from MVImgNet, 100 from DL3DV, at ~1080p resolution) along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses. It produces temporally dense event streams at 2400 FPS with microsecond-precision timestamps stored as (x, y, t_i, p_i) tuples, paired with novel-view RGB frames, motion-blurred sequences, and multiple virtual camera trajectories per scene. The resulting GS2E benchmark spans 1050 scenes covering both object-level and scene-level scales, with each scene supporting multiple interpolated trajectories for multi-view evaluation.

## Method

GS2E reconstructs photorealistic 3D scenes via 3D Gaussian Splatting (30k iterations), then generates dense virtual camera trajectories through velocity-controlled cubic B-spline interpolation with adaptive speed profiles (e.g., v(t) = 0.25·sin(t) + 1.1) and a novel-view augmentation stage that samples keyframe groups to create diverse mini-trajectories. Rendered RGB sequences are refined by a diffusion-based module (Difix3D+) to suppress 3DGS artifacts before event simulation. Events are synthesized using the DVS-Voltmeter stochastic simulator, which models per-pixel voltage evolution as Brownian motion with drift, with a physically-informed contrast threshold (c=1) chosen to balance event density against floater artifacts from 3DGS rendering. This combination of geometry-consistent 3DGS rendering, adaptive trajectory control, and sensor-realistic stochastic event generation yields multi-view event streams that are both temporally coherent and geometrically aligned with the underlying scene structure.

## Results

On the DSEC dataset, GS2E achieves an Event Quality Score (EQS) of 0.761, outperforming Vid2e (0.725) and v2e (0.738), with further improvement to 0.782 when combined with Difix3D+ RGB refinement. For event-based 3D reconstruction on GS2E, the best event-fusion result is Event-3DGS at 24.31 PSNR / 0.884 SSIM / 0.118 LPIPS under mild motion, degrading to 19.36 PSNR under strong motion. The 3DGS reconstruction stage itself achieves 29.8 PSNR, 0.92 SSIM, and 0.14 LPIPS on MVImgNet, establishing a solid photometric foundation for downstream event synthesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For event stream quality (EQS on DSEC), GS2E (0.761) outperforms both video-driven baselines Vid2e (0.725) and v2e (0.738), with the Difix3D+ variant reaching 0.782. For event-based 3D reconstruction on GS2E, Event-3DGS with event-fusion supervision dominates E-NeRF across all motion speeds (e.g., 24.31 vs. 21.89 PSNR at mild speed), while event-only Event-3DGS performs poorly (11.19 PSNR), indicating the dataset's RGB-event alignment is critical. In the deblurring task, EFNet (31.26 PSNR) surpasses D2Net (29.61 PSNR), and for video reconstruction, TimeLen++ (MUSIQ 48.68) edges out E2VID (46.52). The paper does not directly compare GS2E against graphics-engine-based simulators (e.g., Blender+ESIM pipelines) in a head-to-head quantitative table, limiting the scope of the baseline comparison to video-driven methods only.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against three existing paradigms for event dataset construction: real-hardware capture (expensive, hard to scale), video-driven synthesis (Vid2E, V2E — limited viewpoint diversity and no geometric consistency), and CG-engine simulation (Blender/Unreal — domain gap from non-photorealistic rendering). It departs from all three by using 3D Gaussian Splatting as an intermediate photorealistic scene representation reconstructed from sparse multi-view RGB, then feeding rendered novel views into the DVS-Voltmeter stochastic event simulator with a velocity-controlled B-spline trajectory generator. The key conceptual departure from prior simulators is the explicit treatment of contrast threshold as a data-dependent parameter (c=1 chosen to suppress 3DGS floater artifacts) rather than a fixed heuristic, and the use of Difix3D+ diffusion refinement to reduce rendering artifacts before event generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in event-based vision: the scarcity of large-scale, multi-view-geometrically-consistent event datasets with aligned RGB, which has constrained hybrid event+RGB 3D reconstruction research. The 1150+ scene benchmark with controllable trajectories and physically-informed noise modeling provides a practical testbed for evaluating generalization across motion speeds and lighting. However, the pipeline is limited to static scenes and inherits 3DGS photometric constraints (no exposure/tone-mapping modeling), so extreme illumination and dynamic object motion remain open. The modest EQS gains over video-driven baselines (~0.02–0.05) suggest the primary value is in geometric consistency and scalability rather than per-frame event realism.
