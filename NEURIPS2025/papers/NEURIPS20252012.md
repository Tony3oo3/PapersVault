## Problem

Existing fake image detection benchmarks exclusively cover images from generative models (GANs, diffusion models), leaving a critical gap for the rapidly growing class of synthetic images produced by neural rendering methods such as NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting. It remains unknown whether current detectors generalize to neural-rendered fakes, and no large-scale dataset exists to evaluate this capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252012/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector takes a single RGB image, resized to 256×256 and center-cropped to 224×224 pixels, and outputs a binary real/fake classification with a confidence score. The spatial branch processes the raw RGB image directly, while the spectral branch operates on the 2D Fourier magnitude spectrum of the same image. The multimodal fusion module produces a single scalar prediction by dynamically weighting spatial and spectral features via learned gates (w_a + w_b = 1).

## Method

The proposed architecture (FFiT) comprises two independently pre-trained branches: a spatial ViT-L14 with AdaLoRA adapters and a spectral ViT-B16 also with AdaLoRA, fused by a Gated Multimodal Unit (GMU) that learns per-sample weights for each domain. The spectral branch is pre-trained with a modified MAE that addresses the centrosymmetric property of Fourier spectra through a focal-loss-based reconstruction objective balancing three masking cases (both-symmetric-masked, one-masked, neither-masked) and a dynamic masking ratio schedule (0.3, 0.15, 0) to capture global spectral structure. The paper's primary contribution is the NeuroRenderedFake benchmark: over 2 million images (512,972 real + 1,653,881 fake) spanning 139 NeRF/3DGS scenes, 6 combined generative+rendering methods, 3 digital avatar systems, and 3 video generation models, enabling cross-domain, cross-time, and degradation-robustness evaluation protocols.

## Results

On the NeuroRenderedFake benchmark (11 test groups of neural-rendered fake images), the proposed multimodal detector (FFiT + GMU) achieves an average AP of ~92% and average AUROC of ~87%, outperforming spatial-domain baselines (CNNSpot: ~82% AP, ~74% AUROC; UniFD: ~85% AP, ~77% AUROC) and the spectral-domain baseline (FreqSpec: ~86% AP, ~78% AUROC). On generative-model fake image datasets (Sora, LivePortrait, Runway, MM-Det, GenImage), the multimodal detector trained on DM-generated fakes (group B) achieves the highest average AUROC of 89.13%, surpassing FatFormer (87.61%) and RINE (86.46%). The spectral-domain weight w_b shows a clear real/fake separation when trained on GAN fakes (Δw_b ≈ 0.03–0.06) but no significant difference when trained on neural-rendered fakes, indicating domain-specific reliance on frequency cues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed multimodal detector outperforms all spatial-domain baselines (CNNSpot, UniFD, RINE) by 5–10% in average AP and 10–14% in average AUROC on neural-rendered fake images, and surpasses the spectral-domain baseline FreqSpec by ~6% AP and ~10% AUROC. Against the closest multimodal competitor FatFormer [42], the paper's method achieves comparable or superior AUROC on generative-model datasets (89.13% vs. 87.61% average), though FatFormer shows higher AP on certain neural-rendered test groups (e.g., groups 5–8 with AP >99%), likely due to its language-guided module being removed for fair comparison. The spectral branch alone (FreqSpec) performs poorly on advanced generative model fakes (e.g., 47–60% AUROC on Sora/Runway), and the paper notes that adding spectral features to the spatial branch does not consistently improve multimodal performance—sometimes degrading it—when the spectral discrepancy between real and fake images is minimal (groups 9, 11). RINE [80] is the strongest spatial-only baseline, reaching 86.46% average AUROC on generative fakes, but still trails the proposed multimodal method by ~3%. The cross-time evaluation reveals no clear degradation trend for neural-rendered fakes (unlike diffusion models), suggesting that renderer-specific artifacts remain relatively stable across method generations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fake image detection literature, which has focused almost exclusively on GAN- and diffusion-model-generated images, to the emerging domain of neural rendering (NeRF, 3DGS) and their hybrid combinations with generative models. Architecturally, it builds on the Gated Multimodal Unit (GMU) for spatial-spectral feature fusion and adapts the Masked Autoencoder paradigm to the Fourier domain, introducing a modified focal-loss formulation to handle the centrosymmetric structure of spectral magnitudes. The cross-domain evaluation protocol departs from prior work by systematically training detectors on one artifact family and testing on another, a setup not previously formalized for neural-rendered fakes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As neural rendering methods (NeRF, 3DGS, and their avatar/editing variants) become increasingly capable of producing photorealistic imagery, the absence of a dedicated benchmark has been a critical bottleneck for the detection community; NeuroRenderedFake (2M+ images) fills this gap and is likely to become a standard evaluation resource. The finding that no clear cross-time degradation trend exists for neural-rendered fakes (unlike diffusion models) and that spectral-domain contribution is highly training-data-dependent open important questions about what artifacts are truly transferable across synthesis paradigms. A key limitation is that the dataset's generation cost (~3× WildFake in GPU hours) may limit reproducibility, and the Runway video frames remain an unsolved challenge for all tested detectors.
