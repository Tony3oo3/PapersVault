## Problem

Existing 3D pointmap regression methods like DUSt3R and MonST3R align correspondences solely via camera poses, which breaks down in dynamic scenes where object motion disrupts rigid-transform-based alignment. MonST3R extends training to dynamic videos but still models all pointmaps under a single global rigid transformation, lacking explicit mechanisms to link corresponding points on moving objects across frames. This results in noisy correspondences, inaccurate depth in dynamic regions, and degraded reconstruction quality even in static areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250045/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (W×H×3) and predicts Static-Dynamic Aligned Pointmaps (SDAP) expressed in the first camera's coordinate system, where static pixels are aligned via known camera poses and dynamic pixels are aligned via optical flow. Two auxiliary heads produce a per-pixel dynamic mask (single-channel logit map via DPT) and dense optical flow (RAFT-based with cross-attention maps instead of 4D correlation volumes). Downstream, the SDAP enables multi-frame depth estimation, camera pose recovery via PnP-RANSAC on 2D-3D matches, and direct 2D-2D correspondence extraction in dynamic regions.

## Method

The core contribution is the SDAP representation, which unifies spatial and temporal alignment by warping static pixels with camera poses and dynamic pixels with optical flow into a single world-coordinate pointmap. Occlusion masks are derived from forward-backward flow consistency, and dynamic masks are computed by comparing observed optical flow against camera-induced flow projected from depth. Training uses two separate confidence-aware losses: a standard regression loss restricted to static pixels (weighted by 1−M_dyn) and a dynamic alignment loss that enforces pointmap consistency between flow-warped correspondences in both view directions. The architecture builds on DUSt3R's vision-transformer encoder-decoder, freezing the encoder and fine-tuning only the decoder and DPT head, with additional DPT and RAFT heads for dynamic mask and flow prediction. This design lets the network learn precise cross-attention correspondences in dynamic regions without the rigid-motion assumption that limits prior methods.

## Results

On multi-frame depth estimation, D²USt3R achieves the best overall performance: AbsRel 0.142 / δ₁ 83.9 on TUM-Dynamics, 0.060 / 95.8 on Bonn, and 0.324 / 57.5 on Sintel (all-scene metrics), outperforming MonST3R (0.145/81.2, 0.068/94.4, 0.345/56.2) and DUSt3R (0.176/76.5, 0.135/82.4, 0.370/58.5). On dynamic-region depth, D²USt3R leads on TUM-Dynamics (AbsRel 0.148, δ₁ 82.9) and Bonn (0.059, 95.7) but trails MonST3R on Sintel Dynamic (0.568 vs 0.525 AbsRel) and KITTI (0.104 vs 0.070 AbsRel). For pointmap alignment in dynamic objects (EPE↓), D²USt3R achieves 16.19 on Sintel-Clean and 8.91 on KITTI, roughly halving the error of DUSt3R (30.96, 14.19) and MonST3R (38.47, 14.91); with the additional flow head, it reaches 3.57 EPE on KITTI, surpassing SEA-RAFT (4.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DUSt3R, D²USt3R wins decisively on all dynamic-scene benchmarks: TUM-Dynamics dynamic-region AbsRel drops from 0.221 to 0.148 (−33%), Bonn dynamic from 0.127 to 0.059 (−54%), and Sintel dynamic from 0.672 to 0.568 (−15%). Against MonST3R (the closest competitor), D²USt3R leads on TUM-Dynamics (both all-scene and dynamic), Bonn (both), and Sintel all-scene, but loses on Sintel dynamic (0.568 vs 0.525 AbsRel) and KITTI all-scene (0.104 vs 0.070 AbsRel), which the authors attribute to the absence of driving scenes in training data. Against MASt3R, D²USt3R is superior on every dynamic-scene metric tested. On pointmap alignment EPE in dynamic objects, D²USt3R halves the error of all pointmap-based baselines (e.g., 16.19 vs 30.96 on Sintel-Clean), and with the auxiliary flow head it surpasses the dedicated optical-flow method SEA-RAFT on KITTI (3.57 vs 4.43 EPE). The retrained MonST3R* (same training data) confirms that the gains come from the SDAP formulation and dynamic alignment loss rather than data differences, as D²USt3R still leads MonST3R* on TUM-Dynamics and Bonn.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DUSt3R feed-forward pointmap regression framework (Wang et al., CVPR 2024) to dynamic scenes, departing from MonST3R (Zhang et al., 2024) which fine-tunes DUSt3R on dynamic video but still enforces a single global rigid transformation across all pixels. The key conceptual departure is the Static-Dynamic Aligned Pointmap (SDAP) representation, which explicitly decouples static alignment (via camera poses) from dynamic alignment (via optical flow), rather than treating the entire scene under one transformation. The method also draws on cross-attention correspondence insights from ZeroCo and incorporates RAFT-based optical flow as an auxiliary head, positioning itself as a unified correspondence-and-reconstruction framework for dynamic scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SDAP formulation provides a clean, minimal extension to the DUSt3R/MonST3R family that practitioners can adopt to handle dynamic scenes without architectural overhaul, and the consistent gains across TUM-Dynamics, Bonn, Sintel, and KITTI suggest broad applicability. However, the method remains limited to two-frame input and depends on an off-the-shelf optical flow estimator, leaving open the question of how to extend temporal alignment to longer video sequences without a separate global optimization step. The bullet-time reconstruction application hints at downstream uses (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting on dynamic scenes) that could become practically important if the video extension challenge is resolved.
