## Problem

REPA and its variants accelerate diffusion transformer training by aligning intermediate denoising features with pretrained vision model representations, but this external alignment is entirely absent during inference, meaning the discriminative semantic knowledge never actively guides the generation process. The paper argues this structural disconnect impedes both discriminative semantic learning and overall generative capability, leaving the full potential of foundation model representations unrealized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250264/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes random Gaussian noise of two shapes—32×32×4 for image latents (corresponding to 256×256 RGB images via Stable Diffusion VAE) and 1×D_vf for the class token (from DINOv2-B)—along with a class label for conditional generation. During inference, it jointly reconstructs both the image latents and the corresponding global class token from pure noise, so the output is not just a generated image but also a semantically meaningful class token that was co-denoised throughout the process. The class token is projected into the same embedding space as the patchified latents and prepended as a single extra token, adding less than 0.5% FLOPs.

## Method

REG entangles low-level image latents with a single high-level class token from a pretrained foundation model (DINOv2) by applying synchronized noise injection to both and spatially concatenating them as a unified input to SiT transformer blocks. The training objective simultaneously predicts velocity for both the noised latents and the noised class token (weighted by β), and additionally applies a REPA-style cosine-similarity alignment loss at a specific intermediate layer to reinforce discriminative feature quality. The key architectural difference from REPA is that the class token is part of the denoising input rather than an external alignment target, so during inference the model autonomously reconstructs both the image and its global semantics from noise, with the recovered semantic knowledge actively steering image generation at every denoising step. This design requires no auxiliary networks at inference, adds only one token to the sequence, and achieves 63× and 23× faster FID convergence than SiT and REPA respectively on ImageNet 256×256.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 without CFG, SiT-XL/2 + REG achieves a record FID of 1.8 at 4M iterations, compared to 8.3 for SiT-XL/2 at 7M iterations and 5.9 for SiT-XL/2 + REPA at 4M iterations, representing 63× and 23× faster convergence respectively. At just 400K iterations, SiT-L/2 + REG (FID 4.6) surpasses SiT-XL/2 + REPA trained for 4M iterations (FID 5.9), a 10× training cost reduction. With classifier-free guidance, REG at 480 epochs achieves FID 1.40, matching REPA's 800-epoch result (FID 1.42) while requiring fewer than 40% of the training iterations, all with only +0.38% FLOPs and +0.49% latency overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SiT (Ma et al., ECCV 2024), REG achieves 63× faster convergence: SiT-XL/2 requires 7M steps to reach FID 8.3, while SiT-XL/2 + REG reaches FID 8.2 in only 110K steps (98.36% GPU-hour reduction). Against REPA (Yu et al., 2024), REG achieves 23× faster convergence: REPA needs 4M steps for FID 5.9, while REG reaches FID 5.6 in 170K steps (95.72% GPU-hour reduction). Among SOTA latent diffusion methods with CFG, REG at 480 epochs (FID 1.40) outperforms MDTv2-XL/2 (FID 1.58, 1080 epochs), DiffiT (FID 1.73), DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27, 1400 epochs), MaskDiT (FID 2.28, 1600 epochs), U-ViT-H/2 (FID 2.29, 240 epochs), and all pixel-space methods (ADM-U FID 3.94, VDM++ FID 2.40, Simple Diffusion FID 2.77, CDM FID 4.88). REG also surpasses LDM-4 (FID 3.60) and SD-DiT (FID 3.23) by large margins. The only method REG does not clearly outperform at matched compute is REPA at 800 epochs (FID 1.42 vs. REG's 1.36 at 800 epochs), where REG still wins but the gap narrows to 0.06 FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SiT (Scalable Interpolant Transformers) as the denoising backbone and REPA (Representation Alignment for Generation) as the key prior method for incorporating pretrained visual representations into diffusion training. The critical departure is conceptual: REPA treats discriminative representations as an external alignment target applied only during training (absent at inference), whereas REG restructures the problem by making a single class token from a foundation model (e.g., DINOv2) a first-class input to the denoising process, so that the model jointly reconstructs both image latents and global semantics from noise. This shifts the paradigm from 'align intermediate features to a frozen target' to 'denoise image and semantics together,' enabling persistent semantic guidance throughout inference with negligible overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 63× and 23× training convergence speedups over SiT and REPA respectively, achieved with less than 0.5% additional FLOPs, represent a major practical efficiency gain that could substantially reduce the compute cost of training high-fidelity image generators. The finding that a single global class token provides more effective discriminative guidance than full dense feature alignment challenges the assumption that richer (spatially distributed) representations are necessary for semantic steering. Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-to-image generation, whether it generalizes beyond class-conditional ImageNet to open-domain or multi-modal settings, and whether the reliance on a frozen pretrained encoder (DINOv2) limits the ceiling of achievable quality. The paper acknowledges compute limitations preventing extended training at higher resolutions.
