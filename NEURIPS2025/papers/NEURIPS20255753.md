## Problem

Enabling reasoning in vision-language models for long videos is hindered by two major gaps: the absence of large-scale, high-quality long video reasoning datasets with chain-of-thought annotations, and the prohibitive computational cost of applying reinforcement learning to long video sequences due to massive visual embeddings, heavy rollouts, and long-context LLM prefilling. Existing multi-modal reasoning approaches focus on single images or short videos (e.g., 16 frames), leaving long video reasoning largely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255753/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts long videos of up to 8,192 frames with configurable FPS settings alongside a text question, and produces a structured chain-of-thought reasoning trace (formatted as <think>...<answer>...</answer>) followed by a final text answer. Questions span four reasoning categories—Temporal, Goal and Purpose, Spatial, and Plot and Narrative—and include both multiple-choice and open-ended formats. The model is trained on 512-frame inputs by default but scales to hour-long videos (3,600 frames) on a single 8×A100 node.

## Method

LongVILA-R1 introduces a full-stack framework with three components: (1) LongVideo-Reason, a 104K-sample dataset of Question-Reasoning-Answer pairs generated via an automated pipeline that segments videos into ~10-second clips, captions them with NVILA-8B, and uses DeepSeek-R1-671B to produce diverse CoT reasoning across four categories; (2) a two-stage training pipeline—Stage 1 performs Long CoT-SFT on 36K filtered samples using MM-SP to warm up reasoning abilities, and Stage 2 applies GRPO-based RL on 68K challenging samples plus 102K additional open-source video data to scale reasoning; (3) MR-SP (Multi-modal Reinforcement Sequence Parallelism), a training infrastructure that parallelizes video encoding across GPUs during rollout (with cached embedding reuse across 8–16 rollouts per step) and applies sequence parallelism to both policy and reference model prefilling, achieving up to 2.1× speedup and enabling 1024-frame RL training without OOM on a single A100 node.

## Results

LongVILA-R1-7B achieves 65.1% and 71.1% accuracy on VideoMME without and with subtitles, respectively, using 512 input frames. On the new LongVideo-Reason-eval benchmark, it reaches 72.0% overall accuracy, surpassing Video-R1-7B (68.1%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (69.3%). Across six video benchmarks (ActivityNet-QA, LongVideoBench, Perception Test, NExT-QA, VNBench, VideoMME), LongVILA-R1-7B consistently outperforms its base model LongVILA-7B, with the largest gains on Perception Test (+10.8 pts) and VNBench (+12.5 pts).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongVILA-R1-7B outperforms its base model LongVILA-7B on every benchmark, with the most substantial gains on Perception Test (68.9 vs. 58.1) and VNBench (75.5 vs. 63.0), indicating that RL-based reasoning particularly benefits tasks requiring fine-grained temporal and spatial understanding. On the proposed LongVideo-Reason-eval, LongVILA-R1-7B (72.0%) surpasses both Video-R1-7B (68.1%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (69.3%), though it trails Gemini-1.5-Pro on VideoMME (71.1% vs. 81.3% w/ subtitles) and GPT-4o (71.1% vs. 77.2% w/ subtitles), reflecting the advantage of proprietary models' larger scale. Among open-source 7B/8B models, LongVILA-R1-7B leads all baselines on every reported benchmark, including LLaVA-OV-7B, Kangaroo-8B, and ShareGPT4Video-8B. The ablation (Table 5) confirms that both CoT-SFT and RL contribute: skipping CoT-SFT and training RL directly drops accuracy from 61.9% to 52.4%, while using only SFT without RL yields 60.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LongVILA's multi-modal sequence parallelism (MM-SP) training system into the reinforcement learning setting, introducing MR-SP to handle the unique memory and compute bottlenecks of long-video rollouts and prefilling. Conceptually, it follows the two-stage CoT-SFT + GRPO recipe popularized by DeepSeek-R1 and adapted to vision by Video-R1 and LMM-R1, but scales it from short clips (16 frames in Video-R1) to videos with up to 8,192 frames. The dataset construction departs from prior video QA datasets by using a leading reasoning LLM (DeepSeek-R1-671B) to generate structured chain-of-thought annotations across four reasoning dimensions (temporal, goal, spatial, narrative), rather than relying on human annotation or simple caption-based QA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MR-SP framework is a practical infrastructure contribution that makes long-video RL training feasible on a single 8×A100 node (3,600 frames), lowering the barrier for the community to experiment with RL on long videos. The 104K-sample LongVideo-Reason dataset and the four-dimensional LongVideo-Reason-eval benchmark provide much-needed structured resources for evaluating reasoning beyond recognition. However, the paper acknowledges that scaling to significantly longer sequences, adding audio for omni-modal VLMs, or using large batch sizes still requires multi-node distributed training, and the performance gains over the strong LongVILA-7B baseline, while consistent, are moderate (e.g., +5 points on VideoMME w/o subtitles). Open questions include whether the four reasoning categories generalize to unseen domains and whether the LLM-generated CoT annotations introduce systematic biases that limit true reasoning gains.
