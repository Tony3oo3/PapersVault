## Problem

Reasoning models trained with standard outcome-reward RL (e.g., GRPO) consistently exhibit overthinking: they generate unnecessarily lengthy chain-of-thought sequences containing redundant and irrelevant reasoning steps. This occurs because binary 0/1 final-answer rewards provide no signal about whether intermediate reasoning is already sufficient, so the model never learns to terminate thinking early. The paper asks how to use RL to regulate intermediate reasoning processes and enable early exit without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (a math or science problem) and produces a concise chain-of-thought reasoning path followed by a final answer. During training, the input is consumed in a two-phase rollout: first a full reasoning path is generated, then the path is truncated at m randomly sampled temporal positions and a 'stop thinking and start answering' prompt is inserted at each truncation point to elicit intermediate answers. The resulting serial group (one full path plus m truncated paths with their answers) is the unit on which rewards and advantages are computed, replacing GRPO's parallel group of independently sampled CoTs.

## Method

S-GRPO replaces GRPO's parallel-group sampling with a serial-group construction: a single complete CoT is generated, then m random truncation points are selected along it, and the model is prompted to produce an answer at each truncation position, forming a serial group of m+1 responses ordered by exit position. A decaying reward strategy assigns exponentially diminishing positive rewards (1/2^(N_right−1)) to correct answers based on their cumulative correct count from front to back, while incorrect answers receive zero, thereby incentivizing both correctness and early termination. Advantages are computed as the reward minus the group mean (without standard deviation, for training stability) and broadcast to all tokens in each response. The policy is updated using the same clipped PPO objective as GRPO, with the serial-group relative advantages driving the gradient. This design preserves the integrity of the original reasoning path (the full CoT is always included in the group) while teaching the model to produce sufficient reasoning earlier and exit promptly.

## Results

Across four reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B/14B, Qwen3-8B/14B) and five benchmarks, S-GRPO achieves 0.72%–3.92% absolute accuracy improvement over vanilla models while reducing average token count by 40.4%–61.1%. For example, on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, S-GRPO reaches 76.10% overall accuracy (vs. 72.18% vanilla) with only 3,556 tokens (vs. 9,147), and on Qwen3-8B it reaches 84.26% (vs. 81.96%) with 4,900 tokens (vs. 8,293). The method also generalizes beyond math: on MMLU-Pro and LiveCodeBench v5, S-GRPO maintains accuracy while cutting tokens by 31%–56%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-GRPO outperforms all five baselines on overall accuracy while achieving the largest or near-largest token reduction. Compared to GRPO, S-GRPO matches or exceeds accuracy (e.g., 76.10 vs. 76.00 on DeepSeek-7B; 84.26 vs. 82.30 on Qwen3-8B) while cutting tokens by 61% vs. a mere 1.7% increase, showing that serial-group sampling does not hinder exploration. Against RL + Length Penalty, S-GRPO avoids the accuracy collapse seen on DeepSeek-14B (81.08 vs. 77.24) while achieving comparable token savings (3,254 vs. 3,699). DEER achieves similar token reduction but sacrifices accuracy on harder tasks (e.g., 53.3 vs. 56.0 on AIME 2024 for DeepSeek-7B; 83.82 vs. 85.80 overall on Qwen3-14B). ConCISE_SimPO degrades AIME accuracy substantially (48.3 vs. 56.0 on DeepSeek-7B), and ShorterBetter (evaluated only on AIME/AMC) is outperformed by S-GRPO on both accuracy and tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

S-GRPO builds directly on GRPO's group-relative policy optimization framework but replaces the parallel-group sampling paradigm (multiple complete CoTs per query) with a serial-group paradigm (one CoT with multiple early-exit truncations along its length). The decaying reward strategy departs from the binary 0/1 outcome rewards used in GRPO, DEER, ConCISE, and RL+Length Penalty by assigning exponentially diminishing rewards to correct answers based on their temporal position within the reasoning path. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between training-free early-exit methods (DEER) and training-based length-penalty RL (ShorterBetter, RL+Length Penalty) by internalizing early-exit behavior through RL rather than relying on inference-time heuristics or off-policy fitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S-GRPO offers a practical, drop-in post-training stage that simultaneously reduces inference cost (40–61% fewer tokens) and improves accuracy across four model families and five+ benchmarks, addressing the widely-recognized overthinking problem in reasoning models. Its two-stage rollout design preserves the model's existing reasoning capability, making it suitable as a final optimization step without risking catastrophic forgetting. A noted limitation is that in later training stages, uniformly sampled truncation points become too short, causing reward decline; optimizing the truncation distribution (e.g., curriculum-based or adaptive) is identified as a key direction for future work. Broader open questions include whether the serial-group idea extends to multi-turn or agentic reasoning settings where 'sufficiency' is harder to define.
