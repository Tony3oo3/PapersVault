## Problem

Practical dynamic 3D reconstruction requires simultaneously achieving online operation, global pose and map consistency, expressive appearance modeling via 3D Gaussian Splatting, and flexibility for both RGB and RGB-D inputs. Existing SLAM methods either ignore dynamic content or lack photometric detail, offline methods do not scale to long sequences, and feedforward transformer approaches sacrifice global consistency and produce only point clouds without detailed rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252943/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ProDyG consumes a monocular RGB video stream (or RGB-D) processed incrementally in batches triggered by Splat-SLAM's keyframe selection strategy. It outputs camera poses, a static 3D Gaussian map, a time-varying dynamic 3D Gaussian map parameterized relative to Motion Scaffolds nodes (not world coordinates), per-frame fine-grained motion masks from SAM2, aligned depth maps for non-keyframes, and photorealistic novel view renderings. The system is the first to perform both tracking and dynamic reconstruction online from pure RGB input.

## Method

ProDyG integrates a motion-agnostic tracking pipeline with progressive dynamic reconstruction. For tracking, it computes residual flow (optical flow minus camera-induced flow) after each Dense Bundle Adjustment iteration, thresholds it to produce coarse motion masks, then refines them into fine-grained per-frame masks via SAM2 semantic point prompting; these masks suppress dynamic pixels in the DBA reprojection loss for robust pose estimation. For reconstruction, it adopts Motion Scaffolds (MoSca) as a graph of SE(3) trajectory nodes with RBF radii, initialized by lifting CoTracker3 2D tracks into 3D using estimated poses and aligned monocular depths. Dynamic Gaussians are attached to MoSca nodes and warped across time via Dual Quaternion Blending, enabling progressive extension as new frame batches arrive. A novel motion mask loss penalizes the rendered opacity of dynamic Gaussians at static pixels, enforcing clean separation between static and dynamic components during photometric optimization.

## Results

On the iPhone Dataset for novel view synthesis, ProDyG in its fully online configuration (online tracking + online reconstruction, RGB-D) achieves 17.87 PSNR and 0.643 SSIM, outperforming the offline Shape of Motion (17.43 PSNR, 0.591 SSIM) while trailing the offline MoSca (18.44 PSNR, 0.666 SSIM) by a small margin. In RGB-only mode, ProDyG still reaches 15.40 PSNR / 0.582 SSIM, a setting no baseline supports. For camera tracking (ATE RMSE), ProDyG achieves 3.29 cm average on Bonn and 1.89 cm on TUM, substantially improving over its Splat-SLAM baseline (10.6 cm / 2.43 cm) and remaining competitive with the best methods WildGS-SLAM (2.94 / 1.63) and MegaSaM (3.6 / 1.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the offline MoSca [29], which shares the same Motion Scaffolds representation, ProDyG in RGB-D mode trails by only 0.57 PSNR (17.87 vs 18.44) and 0.023 SSIM while operating fully online with self-estimated poses—a significantly harder setting. ProDyG clearly outperforms the offline Shape of Motion [68] in both PSNR (+0.44) and SSIM (+0.052) under RGB-D, and surpasses the only other online method DynOMo [56] by a large margin (17.87 vs 11.98 PSNR). For tracking, ProDyG improves over its Splat-SLAM [54] backbone by roughly 3× on Bonn (3.29 vs 10.6 cm ATE RMSE) and modestly on TUM (1.89 vs 2.43 cm). The only method that beats ProDyG on both tracking datasets is WildGS-SLAM [89] (2.94 / 1.63 cm), which the authors attribute to its aggressive soft uncertainty masking (it reconstructs only the static background) and additional DINOv2 features. ProDyG is the first method to combine online tracking, online dynamic reconstruction, and RGB-only input simultaneously, a configuration no baseline supports.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProDyG integrates three established components—Splat-SLAM for flow-based camera tracking, MoSca (Motion Scaffolds) for dynamic Gaussian deformation, and 3D Gaussian Splatting for rendering—into a unified online pipeline. The key departure from prior work is making MoSca, originally an offline global-optimization method, operate progressively with incremental updates as new keyframes arrive, while adding SAM2-based semantic mask refinement and a novel motion-mask opacity loss to enforce clean static/dynamic separation. Unlike concurrent dynamic 3DGS SLAM methods (DynaGSLAM, 4D-GS SLAM, D4DGS-SLAM) that require RGB-D input, ProDyG is the first to achieve this with pure monocular RGB, and unlike feedforward methods (DUSt3R-based) it maintains global pose and map consistency through the SLAM factor graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously satisfy online operation, global consistency, 3DGS-level appearance fidelity, and RGB-only input for dynamic scene reconstruction, filling a gap that no prior SLAM, offline, or feedforward approach covers. The practical impact is significant for AR/VR and robotics where depth sensors are unavailable and real-time processing is required. Key limitations remain: per-scene test-time optimization precludes true real-time use, objects that leave and re-enter the frustum suffer from insufficient photometric constraints, and large novel viewpoint changes require generative priors the system lacks. Open questions include whether the progressive MoSca extension can be made fully real-time and whether the SAM2 dependency can be replaced with lighter-weight segmentation for embedded deployment.
