## Problem

Existing camera motion datasets (MovieNet, AVE, DREAM-1K, etc.) suffer from inaccurate motion specifications that conflate translation with rotation or zoom, contradictory labels, and a lack of expert quality control, making them unreliable for benchmarking camera motion understanding. No prior work provides a comprehensive taxonomy covering object-centric, ground-centric, and camera-centric reference frames, nor a rigorous evaluation of how well SfM/SLAM and VLMs actually perceive camera motion in real-world dynamic videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254477/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark operates on 3,381 video clips (average 5.7 s, 29.4 FPS) manually segmented into single continuous shots. For binary classification, models receive a video and must predict ~50 motion primitive labels (e.g., dolly-in, pan-left, zoom-out) in the camera-centric frame. For VQA, each question is paired with a positive and a negative video (opposite Yes/No answers) to prevent blind guessing, covering 9 skills and 81 sub-tasks including object-centric tracking, scene dynamics, and logical reasoning. For captioning and retrieval, the model produces or scores natural-language camera motion descriptions against reference annotations.

## Method

The paper introduces a 50+ primitive taxonomy of camera motion developed iteratively with professional cinematographers, spanning translation (dolly/pedestal/truck), rotation (pan/tilt/roll), intrinsic change (zoom), object-centric movements (arc, tail/lead/side/aerial tracking), and perceptual attributes (steadiness, speed). A 'label-then-caption' annotation framework handles complex or ambiguous motions by first classifying unambiguous primitives and then writing free-form descriptions for conflicting or sequential movements, with a multi-stage quality control pipeline (dual labeling, reviewer consensus, author audit) and a 5-round training program that improves annotator accuracy by 15–20%. For model evaluation, the authors benchmark 20 models including six SfM/SLAM methods (COLMAP, VGGSfM, DUSt3R, MASt3R, CUT3R, MegaSAM) and 14 VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA, InternVL, etc.) across classification, VQA, captioning, and retrieval tasks. They then fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL (7B/32B/72B) via full supervised fine-tuning on a separate ~1,400-video training set (5 epochs, DeepSpeed ZeRO-3, frozen vision tower), achieving ~2× AP improvement over the base model and matching MegaSAM's SOTA on binary classification while substantially outperforming all baselines on VQA and captioning.

## Results

On CameraBench's binary motion-primitive classification (camera-centric frame), the best SfM/SLAM method MegaSAM achieves 58.2% average AP, while the best pre-SFT generative VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) reaches only 37.7% AP. After supervised fine-tuning on ~1,400 videos, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (SFT) achieves 62.8% average AP, surpassing MegaSAM by ~4.6 points and improving over its own pre-SFT performance by ~25 points. On the VQA benchmark (9 skills, 81 sub-tasks), most open-source VLMs perform at or below chance (50% Acc / 25% Q-Acc), while the SFT model reaches 74.5% Acc and 49.9% Q-Acc on Motion & Steadiness, and 83.5% Acc on Motion Speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among SfM/SLAM methods, MegaSAM (58.2% AP) significantly outperforms COLMAP (27.3%) by over 2x, with CUT3R (50.9%), MASt3R (49.5%), DUSt3R (43.0%), and VGGSfM (41.3%) falling in between; however, all SfM/SLAM methods struggle with zoom (10–47% AP) and static detection (7–60% AP). Among discriminative VLMs, CLIPScore variants (UMT-B16/L16, LanguageBind, InternVideo2-S2) and ITMScore variants all score near or below random chance (13–21% AP), confirming that contrastive video-text models cannot capture fine-grained camera motion. Among generative VLMs, GPT-4o (36.4%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (37.7%) lead, while smaller models like LLaVA-Video-7B (25.6%), InternVideo2-Chat-8B (22.0%), and mPLUG-Owl3-7B (20.8%) perform substantially worse. The SFT model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, 62.8% AP) beats MegaSAM by 4.6 points and the best pre-SFT VLM by 25.1 points, with the largest gains on zoom-out (78.5 vs. 45.4) and static detection (76.6 vs. 21.6). On VQA, the SFT model achieves 74.5% Acc / 49.9% Q-Acc on Motion & Steadiness versus GPT-4o's 55.8% / 27.0%, and 83.5% Acc on Motion Speed versus GPT-4o's 61.2%, while all other open-source VLMs (InternVL3-78B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B pre-SFT, LLaVA-Video, etc.) remain at or below the 50% chance level on most skills.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two largely separate lines of work on camera motion: geometry-based SfM/SLAM methods (COLMAP, MegaSAM, DUSt3R, MASt3R, CUT3R) that estimate trajectories from visual cues, and vision-language models (Qwen2.5-VL, GPT-4o, LLaVA-Video, InternVL) that reason about video content semantically. It departs from prior camera motion datasets (MovieNet, AVE, DREAM-1K) by identifying and correcting fundamental specification errors—conflation of translation, rotation, and zoom; contradictory labels; and absence of expert oversight—and by introducing a 50+ primitive taxonomy across three reference frames (camera, object, ground) developed with professional cinematographers. The fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL model is built directly on the Qwen2.5-VL architecture, and a preliminary experiment extends CogVideoX-1.5 for motion-controlled generation, positioning the dataset as a training resource for both understanding and generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a clear complementary gap: SfM/SLAM methods (even SOTA MegaSAM) plateau at ~50% AP on basic motion primitives and fail on object-centric or low-parallax scenes, while generative VLMs handle semantic reasoning (tracking shots) but lack geometric precision. The finding that all open-source VLMs score at or below chance on the VQA tasks, yet a small ~1,400-video SFT set yields 1–2× improvements matching SOTA SfM, suggests camera motion is severely underrepresented in current pretraining corpora and that targeted data is highly effective. Open questions include integrating SfM and VLM signals for complementary strengths, extending post-training beyond SFT (e.g., RL or prompt optimization), and developing reliable evaluation for camera motion control in video generation.
