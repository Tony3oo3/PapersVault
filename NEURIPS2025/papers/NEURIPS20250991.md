## Problem

Learning denoisers that provably satisfy the pseudo-contractive (SPC) property is essential for convergence guarantees in Plug-and-Play and RED-PRO frameworks, yet existing spectral methods (power iteration) only constrain the spectral norm on finite training samples and cannot guarantee the truly global SPC condition. This paper identifies that the gradient step (GS) denoiser, when parameterized by an input convex neural network (ICNN), inherently satisfies the SPC property without any additional spectral penalty, resolving an open challenge posed by the RED-PRO model.

## Input / Output

The GS denoiser T_θ = I − ∇ψ_θ takes a noisy image x + n (Gaussian noise at σ = 5/255) and outputs a denoised estimate, with ψ_θ being a scalar-valued ICNN with non-negative weights and softplus activations. In the RED-PRO inverse problem setting, the method consumes a degraded observation y = Ax + n (e.g., blurred image with known kernel, or sparse-view CT sinograms from 200 parallel-beam projections) and iteratively produces a restored image via a hybrid steepest descent algorithm. Experiments cover MNIST (28×28 grayscale), CelebA (face images), BSD400 (natural images), and Mayo-CT (medical CT volumes).

## Method

The key theoretical result (Proposition 4.1) shows that the GS denoiser T_θ = I − ∇ψ_θ with ψ_θ an L_θ-smooth ICNN is provably a (L_θ−2)/L_θ-SPC operator, by connecting the cocoercivity of the ICNN gradient (from convexity + L-smoothness) to the SPC inequality via the equivalence between SPC, conically averaged, and relaxed FNE operators. Training requires only the standard denoising loss E‖T_θ(x+n) − x‖² with no spectral norm penalty or power iteration, since the non-negative weight constraint of the ICNN structurally embeds the SPC property. The learned SPC denoiser is then plugged into RED-PRO, where a relaxed operator T_w = wT_θ + (1−w)I (with w < 2/L_θ) is combined with a diminishing step-size gradient step on the data fidelity, and the paper proves both sequence convergence (Theorem 4.5) and an O(k^{−α}) objective convergence rate (Theorem 4.6). A second construction (Proposition 4.3) shows that any L_θ-Lipschitz CNN R_θ yields a pseudo-contractive denoiser T_θ = I − (R_θ + L_θ I), offering an alternative without ICNN expressivity limitations.

## Results

On the CelebA Gaussian deblurring task (20 samples, σ_blur=1, σ_noise=0.02), RED-PRO with the learned truly SPC denoiser achieves 32.4±2.8 dB PSNR and 0.92±0.03 SSIM, ranking second only to DPIR (33.2±3.0 dB) while outperforming PnP-PGD (31.4±1.9 dB) and DiffPIR (30.8±2.0 dB). On the 5-image Gaussian deblurring benchmark (Table 1), the proposed method achieves PSNR of 27.17–32.40 dB, competitive with the GS denoiser [35] (27.38–32.58 dB) and clearly surpassing DnCNN (25.30–26.74 dB) and DRUNet (27.00–30.44 dB). On sparse-view CT reconstruction (Mayo-CT, 128 images), RED-PRO achieves 30.06±0.49 dB PSNR, substantially outperforming FBP (20.23 dB) but trailing PnP-ADMM (34.22 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed RED-PRO with the ICNN-based truly SPC denoiser is compared against 8 baselines. Against spectral-method denoisers (SPC-DnCNN [61] and MMO [48]), the proposed method wins by 1.3–1.5 dB PSNR on the 5-image deblurring benchmark (e.g., 27.17 vs. 25.73 on Parrot), and Figure 3 confirms that both spectral methods violate the SPC property at low noise levels. Against standard DnCNN [67] and DRUNet [66] (non-SPC), the gap is even larger (1.9–5.7 dB), demonstrating the benefit of the SPC constraint. Against the closest competitor, the GS denoiser of Hurault et al. [35], the proposed method is within 0.2 dB on most images but slightly trails on Parrot (27.17 vs. 27.38) and House (32.40 vs. 32.58), while winning on Boat (29.54 vs. 29.41), Couple (29.17 vs. 29.08), and Man (30.25 vs. 29.91). On CelebA deblurring (Table 3), RED-PRO trails DPIR [66] by 0.8 dB PSNR but matches its SSIM (0.92), and clearly outperforms DiffPIR [69] by 1.6 dB and PnP-PGD [34] by 1.0 dB. On CT reconstruction (Table 4), PnP-ADMM [28] outperforms RED-PRO by 4.2 dB PSNR, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation of the theoretical-guarantee approach in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the gradient step (GS) denoiser introduced by Cohen et al. (2021) and Hurault et al. (2022), which parameterizes convex potential functions via input convex neural networks (ICNNs), and on the RED-PRO framework (Cohen et al., 2021) that requires demicontractive denoisers for convergence guarantees. It departs from the dominant spectral-method approach (Ryu et al. 2019, Pesquet et al. 2021, Wei et al. 2024) that enforces pseudo-contractivity through power-iteration-based spectral norm penalties, by showing that the ICNN structure inherently and exactly satisfies the d-SPC condition via the cocoercivity of the gradient of an L-smooth convex function. The paper also connects to the broader operator-theoretic literature on averaged, firmly nonexpansive, and conically averaged operators, providing a unified equivalence diagram that positions the GS denoiser within this hierarchy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a training-free, architecturally guaranteed path to truly SPC denoisers, eliminating the computational overhead and global-norm limitations of spectral methods and resolving an open challenge posed by the RED-PRO framework. However, the ICNN's non-negative weight constraint limits expressivity, and empirically the method remains competitive but not superior to non-guaranteed approaches like DPIR on deblurring benchmarks. A key open question is whether more expressive architectures (e.g., the Lipschitz-constrained CNN construction in Proposition 4.3) can match ICNN-level interpretability while closing the performance gap, and whether the SPC guarantee translates to stronger convergence rates in practice for high-dimensional inverse problems like CT reconstruction where RED-PRO currently underperforms PnP-ADMM.
