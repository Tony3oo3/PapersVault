## Problem

Despite significant progress in text-to-image diffusion models, residual misalignment between text and image representations persists. The paper argues that conventional T2I training—minimizing score or flow matching losses on positive pairs only—is suboptimal from a representation alignment standpoint, and that contrastive learning exploiting both positive and negative pairs within a batch can yield substantially better alignment. However, applying contrastive objectives to generative models remains underexplored due to the fundamental mismatch between discriminative representation learning and generative sample synthesis.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and produces an image at 512×512 (SD1.5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL, SD3). For text-guided image editing, it takes a source image plus a target text prompt and produces an edited image while preserving structure and background. The only trainable component is a set of learnable soft tokens (<1M parameters) that are prepended to text features at selected transformer layers; the entire pretrained backbone remains frozen, making the method a plug-and-play add-on to any existing T2I model with negligible memory and latency overhead.

## Method

SoftREPA introduces layer- and time-indexed learnable soft tokens (defined via an embedding function) that are prepended to text hidden representations across selected layers of a frozen diffusion model, modifying the denoising function to v_θ(x_t, t, y, s). Training optimizes these tokens via a contrastive score matching loss (L_SoftREPA) that repurposes the negative of the denoising or flow matching loss as a logit in an InfoNCE-style objective over positive and negative image-text pairs within a batch, with an exponential wrapper to constrain logit values and stabilize training. The paper proves that minimizing this contrastive objective is equivalent to maximizing the mutual information between text and image representations under the diffusion model, providing a theoretical grounding for the approach. For SD3's MM-DiT architecture, soft tokens of length 4 are applied to layers 1–5; for SD1.5/SDXL UNet architectures, they are applied to Down or Middle blocks. A single Monte Carlo sample approximates the expectation in the logit, and the same noise and timestep are shared across the batch for computational efficiency.

## Results

On COCO-val 5K, SoftREPA consistently improves human preference (ImageReward) across all three model architectures: SD1.5 (17.72→32.89), SDXL (75.06→85.29), and SD3 (94.27→108.5), while maintaining or slightly improving CLIP text-alignment scores (e.g., SD3: 26.30→26.91) and HPS (28.09→28.91). On GenEval, SD3+SoftREPA achieves a mean score of 0.70 with strong gains in Two-object (0.95), Colors (0.92), Position (0.34), and Color Attribution (0.68), though it underperforms on Counting (0.29 vs. 0.56 baseline). The method adds fewer than 1M trainable parameters with negligible latency increase (e.g., SD3: 4.637→4.695 sec/image) and no change in peak GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, SD3+SoftREPA outperforms both CaPO [20] and RankDPO [16] on Two-object (0.95 vs. 0.87/0.90), Colors (0.92 vs. 0.86/0.87), Position (0.34 vs. 0.31/0.31), and Color Attribution (0.68 vs. 0.59/0.66), but trails significantly on Counting (0.29 vs. 0.63/0.72), which the authors attribute to over-generation of object instances. For image editing, SoftREPA consistently improves all baselines: FlowEdit+Ours achieves ImageReward 102.24 vs. 87.70 (PIEBench) and 46.68 vs. 38.08 (DIV2K), while PnP+Ours and MasaCtrl+Ours show smaller but consistent gains in CLIP and HPS scores. In the complementarity experiments, plug-and-play integration of SoftREPA soft tokens into Diffusion-DPO [43] boosts ImageReward from 22.26 to 33.10 and CLIP from 26.51 to 27.41, and into DDPO [3] from −8.83 to 8.07 (ImageReward) and 26.21 to 27.13 (CLIP), confirming that representation alignment and preference optimization are orthogonal and complementary. Against LoRA fine-tuning with the same contrastive loss (Table 3), soft tokens achieve ImageReward 106.32 vs. 97.72 for LoRA+Contrastive on SD3, demonstrating the synergy between the soft token design and contrastive training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SoftREPA extends the representation alignment paradigm introduced by REPA (Yu et al., ICLR 2025), which aligned DiT internal features with an external visual encoder, by instead aligning text and image representations through a contrastive objective that uses the diffusion model's own score-matching loss as the logit. This departs from prior contrastive vision-language work (CLIP, ALIGN, ALBEF) that operates in a discriminative setting, and from preference-optimization methods (Diffusion-DPO, DDPO, RankDPO) that require tailored preference datasets or full fine-tuning. The soft-token mechanism is conceptually related to prompt tuning (VPT, CoOp) but differs in being sample-independent and optimized over a distribution of image-text pairs rather than per-sample or per-class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a sub-1M-parameter add-on with zero memory overhead can be plugged into any pretrained T2I model (UNet or DiT) to improve text-image alignment, and it composes additively with diffusion RL methods like DPO and DDPO. The counting-metric degradation on GenEval reveals a tension between text adherence and compositional accuracy that remains unresolved; the authors' partial fix (limiting soft tokens to early layers or adding a YOLOv8 counting loss) is ad hoc. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video generation, 3D synthesis, or other conditioning modalities, and whether the mutual-information framing can yield tighter theoretical guarantees beyond the Monte Carlo approximation used here.
