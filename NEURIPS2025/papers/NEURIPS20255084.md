## Problem

Prior brain-informed fine-tuning of language models has been restricted to monolingual English settings, overlooking the widespread prevalence of bilingualism and neuroscientific evidence that bilingual individuals share semantic representations across languages. This paper asks whether fine-tuning language models with bilingual brain data (English and Chinese) can elicit generalizable multilingual capabilities, improving both brain encoding and downstream NLP performance across languages the model was never explicitly trained on.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255084/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes word-by-word text transcripts of naturalistic stories (20-token sequences) and voxelwise fMRI BOLD responses (TR ≈ 2 s) from bilingual participants reading in English or Chinese. The text is fed through a pretrained Transformer; the last token of the last hidden layer is extracted per word, then temporally aligned to the fMRI sampling rate via differentiable Lanczos interpolation and a learned FIR filter with four hemodynamic delays (2, 4, 6, 8 s) before linear projection to voxel space. Outputs include the full fine-tuned model weights, voxelwise BOLD predictions scored by Pearson r via ridge-regression encoding models, and NLP task scores on GLUE, CLUE, XGLUE, and XTREME benchmarks across up to seven languages.

## Method

The core contribution is an end-to-end brain-informed fine-tuning pipeline that embeds temporal alignment (Lanczos downsampling and FIR hemodynamic response modeling) directly into the differentiable architecture, rather than treating them as fixed preprocessing steps as in prior brain-tuning work. A dropout layer (p=0.2) sits between the LM's last-token representation and the temporal module to mitigate overfitting on limited brain data. Full-model fine-tuning is performed with the NT-Xent loss between predicted and recorded BOLD responses, using AdamW (lr 1e-4, weight decay 1e-3) for 30 epochs with mixed precision and early stopping. The small-N design fine-tunes and evaluates independently per participant, enabling within-participant inference and cross-participant transfer tests. The key empirical finding is that bilingual brain data—unlike monolingual brain data—introduces shared semantic structure that improves downstream NLP performance not only in the fine-tuned language but also in the participant's other language and in five entirely unseen languages (German, French, Spanish, Japanese, Korean), with multilingual models (mBERT, XLM-R, XGLM, LLaMA-3.2) benefiting more than monolingual ones.

## Results

Brain-informed fine-tuning with bilingual fMRI data improves voxelwise brain encoding performance in 76–84% of well-predicted voxels (max Δr ≈ 0.15), with cross-participant generalization showing consistent gains of Δr ≈ 0.03–0.05 in high-level semantic regions. On downstream NLP tasks (same-language evaluation), BERT-ft-en outperforms vanilla BERT-en on 7/9 GLUE tasks (avg +0.80 pp, max +3.57 on WNLI), while mBERT-ft-en improves on 7/9 tasks (avg +0.89 pp). Cross-language transfer is stronger: mBERT-ft-zh (fine-tuned with Chinese brain data) evaluated on English GLUE improves on all 9 tasks (avg +1.61 pp), and zero-shot transfer to five unseen languages (German, French, Spanish, Japanese, Korean) yields average gains of +0.85 to +2.11 pp on XGLUE tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between each vanilla pretrained model and its brain-informed fine-tuned counterpart. On GLUE (English, same-language), BERT-ft-en beats vanilla BERT-en on 7/9 tasks (avg +0.80 pp), with the largest gain on WNLI (+3.57 pp); mBERT-ft-en similarly wins on 7/9 tasks (avg +0.89 pp). On CLUE (Chinese, same-language), BERT-ft-zh improves on 5/7 tasks (avg +0.65 pp) and mBERT-ft-zh on 6/7 (avg +0.53 pp). The cross-language transfer setting shows stronger effects: mBERT-ft-zh (fine-tuned with Chinese brain data) evaluated on English GLUE outperforms vanilla mBERT on all 9 tasks (avg +1.61 pp, max +2.96 on MRPC Acc.), and BERT-ft-zh beats vanilla BERT-zh on 8/9 tasks (avg +1.01 pp). For zero-shot transfer to five unseen languages, mBERT-ft-en improves over vanilla mBERT on 3/3 XGLUE tasks in German, French, and Spanish (avg gains +0.85, +2.06, +2.11 pp), though gains are smaller for Japanese and Korean. The paper also shows that fine-tuning with monolingual English brain data yields smaller cross-linguistic gains than bilingual brain data, confirming the effect is specific to bilingual representations rather than brain data in general.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the brain-tuning paradigm (Schwartz et al., 2019; Moussa et al., 2025; Vattikonda et al., 2025), which fine-tunes pretrained Transformers using fMRI BOLD responses as supervisory signals, from monolingual English to bilingual (English–Chinese) brain data. A key architectural departure is integrating Lanczos downsampling and FIR temporal-delay modeling directly into the differentiable pipeline rather than treating them as fixed preprocessing steps, enabling end-to-end optimization. Conceptually, the work bridges two parallel observations: that bilingual individuals share semantic representations across languages (Chen et al., 2024b) and that multilingual LMs operate in a shared, language-agnostic embedding space (Wendler et al., 2024; Schut et al., 2025), asking whether bilingual brain data can elicit the latter in the former.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that bilingual brain-informed fine-tuning yields zero-shot improvements on five unseen languages (German, French, Spanish, Japanese, Korean) suggests that bilingual semantic structure in the brain is language-agnostic and can be transferred into multilingual model representations. However, the evidence is constrained by a small-N design (6 bilingual participants), only two source languages, and modest effect sizes (Δr ≈ 0.15 for encoding; ~0.5–2 pp for NLP tasks), leaving open whether the effect scales with more participants, more languages, or larger models. A critical open question is which specific linguistic properties (syntax, morphology, discourse structure) are captured by the brain-informed update, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the English–Chinese pairing to typologically diverse language pairs.
