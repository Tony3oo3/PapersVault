## Problem

Neural network architectures in model-free reinforcement learning have remained notably simple—typically a few convolutional layers followed by fully connected layers—because architectural changes have historically yielded only marginal performance gains. This paper challenges that assumption by asking whether a fundamentally different encoder design, combining Hadamard multiplicative interactions with max-pooling-based downsampling, can produce large performance improvements in pixel-based RL without any algorithmic or hyperparameter modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255092/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes the standard Atari observation of 4 temporally stacked grayscale frames at 64×64 resolution (shape 4×64×64), which is transposed to channel-last and normalized to [0,1]. It produces a vector of Q-values, one per discrete action in the environment, via a single 512-unit dense layer followed by a final linear projection. The encoder is deliberately shallow (3 convolutional blocks) yet uses parallel branches within each block, so the spatial dimensions are reduced purely by max-pooling (4×4, 2×2, 3×3 windows) rather than by strided convolutions.

## Method

The Hadamax encoder replaces the conventional Nature CNN with three convolutional blocks, each consisting of two parallel convolutional layers (32, 64, 64 filters) that share the same input. Each branch is passed through LayerNorm and GELU before the two branches are combined via an element-wise Hadamard product, followed by a max-pooling operation for spatial downsampling. A final max-pool (3×3, stride 1) is applied before flattening to the MLP head, emphasizing the strongest features rather than averaging them. The key insight is that Hadamard products between parallel GELU-activated layers create richer high-order interactions and increase effective rank without adding parameters, while max-pooling-based downsampling (inspired by ResNet/Impala) selects the most salient features and adds mild translation invariance. The encoder is plugged directly into PQN—which already uses λ-returns, LayerNorm, and omits the replay buffer and target network—requiring zero algorithmic or hyperparameter changes to achieve an 80% median human-normalized score gain over vanilla PQN.

## Results

On the full Atari-57 benchmark (200M frames, 5 seeds), Hadamax-PQN achieves an 80% median human-normalized score improvement over the PQN baseline and surpasses Rainbow-DQN's official 200M-frame score at approximately 90M frames, while remaining more than an order of magnitude faster. On Atari-10, applying the Hadamax encoder to C51 yields a ~70% performance boost. On VizDoom Deathmatch, Hadamax-PPO reaches 29.38±10.31 at 40M frames versus 5.21±1.27 for the baseline PPO encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PQN (the primary baseline), Hadamax-PQN delivers an 80% aggregate gain on Atari-57, with over 17 individual games showing more than 100% improvement and only one game suffering a >50% decrease. Against Rainbow-DQN, Hadamax-PQN significantly surpasses the state-of-the-art model-free method, reaching Rainbow's 200M-frame score at roughly 90M frames while training more than 10× faster. Against PQN with the deeper Impala ResNet-15 encoder, Hadamax-PQN clearly outperforms, demonstrating that the architectural gains come from the Hadamard/max-pooling design rather than simply increasing convolutional depth. On Atari-10, Hadamax-C51 improves over the C51 baseline by approximately 70%, and on Atari-3, Hadamax boosts DQN and Rainbow by 20% and 30% respectively. On VizDoom Deathmatch, Hadamax-PPO achieves 29.38±10.31 at 40M frames versus 5.21±1.27 for the baseline PPO, confirming generalization to actor-critic methods and 3D environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PQN algorithm, which simplified DQN by removing the replay buffer and target network while adding LayerNorm and λ-returns for training stability. Architecturally, it combines three previously separate ideas: Hadamard product representations (from the authors' prior work [31] showing improved effective rank in RL), max-pooling-based downsampling (borrowed from the Impala ResNet-15 encoder and original ResNet), and GELU activations (from transformer literature). The key departure is from the field's dominant trend of scaling encoder depth (ResNet-15, BBF); instead, Hadamax remains shallow at 3 convolutional layers, arguing that richer multiplicative interactions and selective feature compression matter more than depth in pixel-based RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80% performance gain over PQN from a purely architectural change—without any algorithmic or hyperparameter modifications—challenges the long-standing assumption in RL that network architecture is a second-order concern relative to algorithmic innovation. The encoder generalizes to C51 (+70% on Atari-10) and PPO/VizDoom, suggesting it could serve as a new default encoder for model-free RL. Open questions include whether the encoder scales effectively in depth or width (deeper variants showed no improvement at 40M frames), how it interacts with model-based algorithms like DreamerV3, and whether it can address hard-exploration games where PQN's ε-greedy regime is limiting. A practical limitation is the ~9× increase in training memory (2247 MB vs. 254 MB for PQN).
