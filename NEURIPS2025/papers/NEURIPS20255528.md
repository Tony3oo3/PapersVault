## Problem

The information exponent (IE) framework predicts sample complexity for SGD in single-index models based solely on the lowest nonzero Hermite coefficient, but for multi-index models this misses critical structure: when IE=2, rotational invariance of second-order terms prevents directional recovery, yet when both second- and higher-order terms coexist (e.g., φ = h₂ + h₄), the vanilla IE analysis still reports IE=2 and yields suboptimal Õ(d^{L-1}) bounds. This paper shows that jointly exploiting the second-order terms for subspace recovery and the higher-order terms for direction recovery reduces the sample complexity to Õ(dP^{L-1}).

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. samples (x, f*(x)) where x ~ N(0, I_d) and f*(x) = Σ_{k=1}^P φ(v_k*·x) with orthonormal directions v_k* and a link function φ whose Hermite expansion contains a nonzero h₂ coefficient and a lowest higher-order term at degree L ≥ 3. The constraint log^C(d) ≤ P ≤ d ensures the relevant subspace is genuinely low-dimensional. The output is a width-Õ(P) two-layer network f(x) = Σ a_i φ(v_i·x) whose first-layer neurons converge to the ground-truth directions v_k*, followed by a ridge regression step that selects the correct neurons and fits the output weights.

## Method

The training procedure uses online spherical SGD on the first layer with a correlation loss, followed by ridge regression on the second layer. The key analytical insight is a two-stage decomposition: Stage 1.1 leverages the second-order (h₂) terms to boost the correlation with the P-dimensional relevant subspace from Θ(P/d) to Θ(1) in Õ(dP) steps, while preserving the initial gap between the largest and second-largest coordinates within the subspace; Stage 1.2 then exploits the L-th order terms to drive each neuron to its corresponding ground-truth direction, requiring only Õ(P^{L-1}) additional steps because the starting correlation is Ω(1/P) rather than Õ(1/d). The paper formalizes this via stochastic Gronwall-type lemmas (Lemmas 4.1–4.2) that track noisy SGD dynamics against their deterministic gradient-flow counterparts, with a recoupling argument handling the polynomial-growth regime of Stage 1.2. The overall sample complexity is Õ(dP^{L-1}), matching the cost of learning P independent single-index models with the subspace known a priori.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2.1) establishes that for orthogonal multi-index models $f_*(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{P} \phi(v_k^* \cdot x)$ where $\phi = \hat{\phi}_2 h_2 + \sum_{l \geq L} \hat{\phi}_l h_l$ with $L \geq 3$, online SGD on a width-$\tilde{O}(P)$ two-layer network recovers all ground-truth directions using $\tilde{O}(dP^{L-1})$ samples. This is a significant improvement over the vanilla information exponent bound of $\tilde{O}(Pd^{L-1})$, which would apply if only the L-th order term were considered. The key insight is that the second-order terms first identify the relevant $P$-dimensional subspace in $\tilde{O}(dP)$ samples, after which the higher-order terms only need to resolve directions within that subspace, reducing the effective dimensionality from $d$ to $P$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines, so comparisons are between theoretical sample complexity bounds. The vanilla information exponent (IE) framework [BAGJ21] and its multi-index extension [OSSW24] yield $\tilde{O}(Pd^{L-1})$ samples when IE $\geq 3$, and provide no guarantee for directional recovery when IE = 2 (only subspace recovery is possible). The generative exponent (GE) framework [DPVLB24] similarly only considers the lowest order and suffers the same limitation. This paper's $\tilde{O}(dP^{L-1})$ bound improves the dependence on the ambient dimension from $d^{L-1}$ to $d$ (linear), at the cost of a $P^{L-1}$ factor, which is polynomial in the relevant dimension rather than the ambient dimension. The bound matches the sample complexity of separately learning $P$ independent single-index models with the relevant subspace known a priori, up to logarithmic factors. The improvement is most dramatic when $P \ll d$ and $L > 3$: for example, with $L=4$ and $P = \sqrt{d}$, the bound improves from $\tilde{O}(d^{3.5})$ to $\tilde{O}(d^{2.5})$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information exponent framework of Ben Arous et al. (2021) from single-index to multi-index models, but makes a key conceptual departure: rather than relying solely on the lowest-degree Hermite coefficient (as both the information exponent and the later generative exponent do), it exploits the interplay between second-order and higher-order terms. The closest prior work is Oko et al. (2024), which handles multi-index models with IE ≥ 3 but cannot address the IE = 2 case due to rotational invariance of second-order tensors. The paper also draws on orthogonal tensor decomposition techniques from Ge et al. (2018) and Li et al. (2020) for the directional recovery stage, and on the stochastic induction methodology from Ben Arous et al. (2021) and Abbe et al. (2022) for converting gradient flow analysis to online SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result demonstrates that the information/generative exponent, which has become the standard lens for predicting SGD sample complexity, is fundamentally insufficient for multi-index models: it misses the structural advantage that second-order terms provide as a 'free' subspace identification step. The sample complexity improvement from Õ(d^{L-1}) to Õ(dP^{L-1}) is substantial when P ≪ d, matching the cost of learning P independent single-index problems with the subspace known a priori. Key limitations include the strict orthogonality assumption on teacher directions, the isotropic signal strength requirement, and the Gaussian input assumption; relaxing these (particularly to near-orthogonal or non-isotropic settings) is identified as important future work. The broader implication is that fine-grained analysis of the full Hermite spectrum, not just its lowest nonzero term, may be necessary to characterize SGD behavior in structured high-dimensional problems.
