## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors either require training on specific real-data distributions (limiting generalizability to unseen domains) or depend on latent diffusion model representations (tying performance to a particular LDM choice). This paper asks whether the RandomResizedCrop (RRC) invariance learned by self-supervised models during pre-training can be exploited as a training-free detection signal, since AI-generated images may fail to maintain embedding consistency under cropping-like perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of arbitrary resolution (tested from 128×128 up to 4928×3264) and produces a scalar detection score where higher values indicate a real image. Internally, the input is rescaled to a fixed multiple of the backbone's native resolution (e.g., 1344×1344 or 896×896) and partitioned into a grid of 224×224 patches (36 or 16 patches respectively), each of which is independently scored. The final output is the mean cosine-similarity score across all patches, requiring no threshold tuning beyond a single operating point on the ROC curve.

## Method

WaRPAD approximates the effect of RandomResizedCrop without performing random crops: it extracts high-frequency components via 2-level Haar wavelet decomposition, then perturbs the image along that high-frequency direction (x − α·HF(x), α=0.1) and measures the cosine similarity between the DINOv2 [CLS] embeddings of the original and perturbed versions (the HFwav base score). To simulate multiple RRC instances deterministically, the image is rescaled to d_rescale×d_rescale and split into (d_rescale/d_patch)² patches of 224×224; the final WaRPAD score is the mean of HFwav over all patches. The key insight is that DINOv2, trained to be invariant to RRC on real images, maintains high embedding similarity under high-frequency perturbations for real images but shows degraded similarity for AI-generated images, especially when examined at the patch level. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring only a pre-trained self-supervised backbone (DINOv2 ViT-L14 by default) and standard wavelet operations, and generalizes to other RRC-trained backbones such as CLIP, SwaV, and DINO.

## Results

WaRPAD achieves a mean AUROC of 0.834 on the Synthbuster benchmark (9 generative models), 0.946 on GenImage (8 models), and 0.934 on Deepfake-LSUN-Bedroom (10 models), consistently outperforming all training-free baselines. The largest gains are over RIGID and MINDER (both DINOv2-based), with improvements of 6.5–24.7% in AUROC depending on the benchmark. On Synthbuster, WaRPAD reaches 0.999 AUROC on GLIDE and 0.959 on SDv1.3, while on GenImage it achieves 0.998 on BigGAN and 0.986 on ADM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WaRPAD outperforms all six baselines on the mean AUROC across all three benchmarks. Among training-free methods, the closest competitor is AEROBLADE (0.703 mean on Synthbuster, 0.928 on GenImage), which leverages Stable Diffusion's autoencoder reconstruction loss; WaRPAD surpasses it by 13.1% on Synthbuster and 1.8% on GenImage, though AEROBLADE remains competitive on GenImage where several generative models share the same autoencoder. Manifold Bias [11] performs well on Deepfake-LSUN-Bedroom (0.920) but lags significantly on Synthbuster (0.565) and GenImage (0.736). The DINOv2-based baselines RIGID [15] and MINDER [16] are the most directly comparable (same backbone), and WaRPAD improves over them by 24.7% (Synthbuster), 12.6% (GenImage), and 7.3% (Deepfake-LSUN-Bedroom) in AUROC. Training-based methods AIDE [4] and FatFormer [37] fail to generalize beyond their training distributions, with AIDE scoring only 0.165 on Firefly and FatFormer dropping to 0.186 on DALL-E 3, while WaRPAD maintains strong performance across all generative model types.

## Relation to Prior Work

WaRPAD carves new ground by repurposing RandomResizedCrop (RRC) invariance—a standard training-time augmentation in self-supervised learning—as a detection signal for AI-generated images, rather than relying on diffusion-model autoencoder reconstruction (AEROBLADE) or latent-space curvature (Manifold Bias). It extends the DINOv2-based training-free detection line (RIGID, MINDER) by replacing their simple Gaussian noise/blur perturbations with a structured high-frequency perturbation derived from Haar wavelet decomposition, combined with a deterministic rescale-and-patchify procedure that simulates multiple RRC instances. The conceptual bridge between SSL augmentation invariance and generative artifact detection is the paper's core positioning, distinguishing it from both diffusion-based and prior self-supervised detection approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training-free detection addresses a critical practical gap: training-based detectors overfit to dataset-specific artifacts and fail on unseen generative models or domains, while WaRPAD requires no labeled data and generalizes across 23 generative models and multiple domains. The finding that the method transfers across self-supervised backbones (CLIP, SwaV, DINO) trained with RRC suggests a broadly applicable principle, though performance degrades for masked-image-modeling backbones that lack RRC invariance. Key open questions include whether the approach remains effective as future generators faithfully reproduce high-frequency statistics, whether the patchification overhead is acceptable at scale, and whether the method extends to multimodal (text+image) AI-generated content detection.
