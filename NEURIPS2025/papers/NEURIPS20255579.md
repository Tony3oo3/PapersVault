## Problem

The Straight-Through Estimator (STE) enables backpropagation through non-differentiable quantization operations but occasionally produces gradients pointing in wrong directions, causing parameter oscillations at rounding boundaries that slow convergence. Zeroth-order (ZO) methods like n-SPSA are theoretically sound but require 2n forward passes per iteration, making them prohibitively expensive for deep networks. The paper asks whether the STE can serve as a bias source to accelerate n-SPSA, yielding a method that outperforms the STE while remaining computationally practical.

## Input / Output

FOGZO takes continuous network parameters θ, a training batch, and a per-layer quantization scale α, and produces updated parameters after each iteration. Each FOGZO step requires one standard forward+backward pass (for the STE gradient) plus 2n additional forward passes for the finite-difference ZO estimate; in practice n=1 for all deep models, adding only 2 forward passes over STE. The smoothness ε and perturbation distribution p(u) are not free hyperparameters but are derived analytically from the specific STE surrogate (identity, tanh, hardtanh, ApproxSign) being used, ensuring FOGZO's randomized smoothing aligns with the STE's implicit smoothing.

## Method

FOGZO constructs a mixed perturbation direction v_i = √β · s_i · ĝ + √(1−β) · u_i, where ĝ is the normalized STE gradient, s_i is a random ±1 sign flip, and u_i is a random isotropic perturbation. A central finite difference along v_i then yields the gradient estimate G, analogous to n-SPSA but with the STE-biased direction mixed in. The heuristic derivation shows E[G] ≈ (βĝĝᵀ + (1−β)I)∇L̂_smooth, a rank-1 projection that amplifies the gradient along the STE direction when the STE is correct (ĝᵀ∇L̂_smooth is large) and suppresses it when the STE points orthogonally to the true gradient (ĝᵀ∇L̂_smooth ≈ 0), thereby automatically correcting the STE's occasional wrong-direction errors. The mixing ratio β is linearly decayed from 1 to β_min over training, starting with high trust in the STE and gradually increasing the unbiased ZO component as training enters later stages where STE flaws are more prevalent. The smoothness ε and perturbation p(u) are set by solving for the implicit randomized-smoothing representation of the chosen STE surrogate, so no grid search is needed.

## Results

With 2-bit weights and learnable quantization scales (LSQ), FOGZO consistently improves over the STE baseline: DeiT-Small on ImageNet-100 goes from 79.55% to 80.06% top-1 accuracy, ResNet-50 on ImageNet-100 from 82.81% to 83.67%, and LLaMA-9m on 3.28B C4 tokens from 82.54 to 79.94 perplexity. For 1-bit quantization, FOGZO yields larger gains (e.g., DeiT-Tiny on ImageNet-100: 30.16% → 39.61% with ApproxSign). On a 2-layer MLP on MNIST, FOGZO achieves the same loss as n-SPSA with a 796× reduction in computation (n=1 vs n=7960).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Identity-STE (Bengio et al., 2013), FOGZO wins on most configurations in Table 2 (e.g., +0.51% on DeiT-Small, +0.86% on ResNet-50, −4.6 perplexity on LLaMA-9m/3.28B C4), though ResNet-18 on ImageNet-100 shows a slight loss (80.23% vs 80.04%). Against n-SPSA (Spall, 1997), FOGZO achieves the same training loss on a 2-layer MLP with 796× fewer forward passes (n=1 vs n=7960), while n-SPSA with low n underperforms the STE due to high gradient variance. For 1-bit quantization, FOGZO substantially outperforms both tanh-STE (Qin et al., 2020: 41.98% → 46.8% on DeiT-Tiny) and ApproxSign-STE (Liu et al., 2018: 30.16% → 39.61% on DeiT-Tiny). Against LSQ (Esser et al., 2020) and QuEST (Panferov et al., 2025) as QAT frameworks, FOGZO acts as a drop-in gradient estimator that improves both: in Table 3, LSQ-FOGZO reduces perplexity from 33.28 to 32.41 (125M LLaMA) and QuEST-FOGZO from 22.90 to 22.72 (300M LLaMA) with 2-bit weight-activation quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOGZO sits at the intersection of three research threads: the Straight-Through Estimator (Bengio et al. 2013 and its many variants), zeroth-order optimization via n-SPSA (Spall 1992; Malladi et al. 2023 for MeZO; Zhou et al. 2025 for QuZO), and biased gradient estimation (Bi & Gunn 2019; Driggs et al. 2022). Prior ZO methods for deep learning (MeZO, QuZO) relied solely on forward passes and were specialized for fine-tuning, while STE-based QAT methods (LSQ, QuEST) are first-order and theoretically questionable at low precision. FOGZO departs from both by using the STE gradient as a structured bias source to guide n-SPSA, making it the first partially-zeroth-order method to consistently outperform a first-order STE in the pre-training setting. The paper also establishes a principled link between each STE's surrogate function and an implicit randomized smoothing pair (ε, p(u)), which prior work had not formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOGZO offers a practical, low-overhead upgrade to QAPT pipelines: only 2 extra forward passes per iteration yield consistent 1–8% accuracy gains (vision) and 1–22 perplexity reductions (LLMs up to 300M params) over the STE, while requiring 796× less computation than pure n-SPSA. The method is architecture- and STE-agnostic, integrating cleanly with SOTA quantizers like LSQ and QuEST, which broadens its applicability. Key limitations include the requirement for 32-bit weight storage (BF16 causes precision loss during in-place perturbation), the need to tune β_min per model, and validation only up to 300M parameters. Open questions include whether FOGZO scales to billion-parameter pre-training, whether the 32-bit constraint can be lifted with future mixed-precision techniques, and whether the implicit-smoothing framework can guide the design of new STE surrogates.
