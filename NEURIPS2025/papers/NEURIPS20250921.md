## Problem

Self-supervised motion estimation from video remains limited: photometric-loss methods require situation-specific heuristics (smoothness, cycle consistency) that fail in complex real-world scenes, while supervised methods depend on synthetic data with an inherent sim-to-real gap. The Counterfactual World Modeling (CWM) framework offered a promising heuristic-free alternative by probing a pretrained video predictor, but its hand-designed fixed perturbations are out-of-distribution and image-agnostic, yielding flow accuracy well below state-of-the-art. This paper asks how to make counterfactual probes adaptive and learnable without any labeled flow data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video frame pair (I₁, I₂) at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution along with a query pixel location p₁ in the first frame. The base predictor Ψ^RGB receives the full first frame and a sparsely masked second frame (90% of patches hidden), while the counterfactual probe places a learned Gaussian perturbation at p₁. At inference, multi-mask averaging (10 random masks) and 4 iterations of multiscale crop refinement are applied to produce a sub-pixel flow vector φ̂ = p̂₂ − p₁ and a binary occlusion indicator derived from the maximum magnitude of the difference image Δ.

## Method

Opt-CWM introduces two coupled innovations. First, the counterfactual perturbation is parameterized as a Gaussian whose parameters (center, width, color, magnitude) are predicted by a small MLP conditioned on the patch embedding token from the frozen Ψ^RGB encoder, making the probe image- and position-specific. Second, the probe generator is trained self-supervised via a sparse-prediction information bottleneck: a randomly initialized flow-conditioned next-frame predictor Ψ^flow receives only the first-frame RGB and the sparse putative flow vectors (no second-frame RGB patches), and is jointly optimized end-to-end with the probe generator under an MSE reconstruction loss on the predicted next frame. Because Ψ^flow can only minimize reconstruction if the flow vectors are geometrically correct, gradients flow back to sharpen the probe generator. The base Ψ^RGB is a ViT-based masked autoencoder trained with a highly asymmetric masking policy (0% masked in frame 1, 90% masked in frame 2) that forces temporal factoring of appearance and dynamics.

## Results

On the TAP-Vid First protocol (tracking points from first visibility across large frame gaps), Opt-CWM 1B achieves AJ=51.88, AD=7.70, and <δxavg>=68.63 on DAVIS, outperforming the best supervised baseline DPFlow (AJ=49.20, AD=16.86) and all self-supervised methods by wide margins. On Kinetics, Opt-CWM matches DPFlow on AJ (47.03 vs. 47.33) while substantially reducing average distance (11.25 vs. 17.65). On the synthetic Kubric benchmark, Opt-CWM also leads with AJ=79.98 and AD=3.36, surpassing DPFlow (78.45, 5.05). The improvement over the original hand-designed CWM probes is dramatic: DAVIS AJ jumps from 15.00 to 51.88.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Opt-CWM 1B outperforms all self-supervised baselines across every dataset and metric. On DAVIS, it beats GMRW (the prior SOTA self-supervised method) by 15.4 points in AJ (51.88 vs. 36.47) and reduces AD from 20.26 to 7.70; it surpasses SMURF by 21.2 AJ points and Doduo by 28.5 AJ points. Against supervised methods, Opt-CWM exceeds DPFlow (the strongest supervised baseline) on DAVIS in AJ (51.88 vs. 49.20) and AD (7.70 vs. 16.86), and on Kinetics in AD (11.25 vs. 17.65) while matching it in AJ (47.03 vs. 47.33). On Kubric, Opt-CWM leads all methods including DPFlow in both AJ (79.98 vs. 78.45) and AD (3.36 vs. 5.05). The comparison with the original CWM (fixed hand-designed perturbations) is the most striking: Opt-CWM improves DAVIS AJ from 15.00 to 51.88 and OF1 from 18.22 to 68.43, demonstrating that the learned counterfactual probes are the critical contribution. On the easier CFG protocol (5-frame gap), Opt-CWM 175M reaches AJ=69.53, competitive with supervised SEA-RAFT (69.89) and close to DPFlow (78.09), while the distilled DPFlow variant reaches AJ=74.77.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Counterfactual World Modeling (CWM) [3, 44], which extracts motion from a pretrained temporally-factored video prediction model via fixed hand-designed perturbations. The key departure is twofold: (1) the counterfactual probe is parameterized as an image-conditioned Gaussian perturbation predicted by a small MLP, making it context-specific and in-distribution, and (2) a novel self-supervised learning objective—jointly training a sparse flow-conditioned next-frame predictor—provides gradients to optimize the probe generator without any flow labels. This positions Opt-CWM as a principled fix for CWM's main weakness (out-of-domain, image-agnostic probes) while remaining in the self-supervised, heuristic-free lineage, distinct from photometric-loss methods (SMURF, Doduo) and supervised architectures (RAFT, DPFlow).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Opt-CWM demonstrates that a general-purpose video prediction model, probed with learned counterfactuals, can match or exceed purpose-built supervised flow architectures on challenging real-world benchmarks with variable frame gaps, without any labeled data. The counterfactual probing paradigm is explicitly task-generic, opening a path to self-supervised extraction of depth, segments, and 3D shape from the same base model. Key limitations include high compute costs for video ViT training and inference, though distillation into lightweight architectures (DPFlow) offers a practical deployment pathway. Open questions include multi-frame extension, scaling to diffusion or autoregressive video models, and whether the sparse-prediction bootstrapping principle generalizes beyond motion to other visual quantities.
