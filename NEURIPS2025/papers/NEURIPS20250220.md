## Problem

Existing zero-shot TTS systems excel at short, single-speaker utterances but struggle with the two defining characteristics of podcasts: multi-minute duration with multiple speakers requiring contextual coherence, and the spontaneous, oral speaking style (filler words, hesitations, breathing pauses) that sharply contrasts with formal written text. Prior dialogue generation work is limited to under 90 seconds and ~2000 hours of data, leaving long-form, spontaneous multi-speaker podcast generation largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The system takes text-only knowledge sources (PDF, Web URL, or TXT) plus 3–10 seconds of reference speech from each of two unseen speakers, and produces a two-speaker podcast audio of up to ten minutes. A key design choice is that the LLM script generation module first converts the raw text into a spontaneous script that deliberately emulates ASR transcript characteristics (filler words like 'um'/'uh', spaces and commas marking breathing pauses, non-verbal onomatopoeia), so that the downstream TTS model—trained on ASR-transcribed spontaneous speech—receives input in its native distribution rather than formal written text.

## Method

MoonCast decomposes podcast generation into an LLM-powered script generation stage and a long-context audio modeling stage. The audio model is a 2.5B-parameter, 16-layer Llama-style transformer with 40K-token context, trained on ~500K hours via three-stage curriculum learning (single-speaker single-turn → two-speaker audiobook → two-speaker conversational podcasts), using a novel full-text-to-full-audio interleaving sequence format with special speaker-change tokens to holistically model multi-turn interactions. A key empirical finding motivates the design: ASR transcripts partially encode spontaneity (filler words, pause punctuation, non-verbal sounds), so conditioning the TTS model on ASR-style text and feeding it LLM-generated scripts that mimic ASR style elicits spontaneous output. For efficient long-sequence decoding, a 0.8B DiT-based flow-matching detokenizer operates chunk-wise (3-second chunks) with a chunk-wise causal attention mask that lets each noisy chunk attend to all previous clean chunks, followed by a 250M BigVGAN vocoder for waveform reconstruction.

## Results

On English podcast generation, MoonCast achieves a spontaneity score of 4.54 and coherence of 4.50, substantially outperforming CosyVoice2 (3.86/3.88) and the Concat Baseline (3.73/3.71). On Chinese podcast generation, MoonCast scores 4.33 in spontaneity and 4.26 in coherence, again leading both baselines. Objectively, MoonCast achieves a WER of 1.81 (English) and 2.15 (Chinese), demonstrating strong intelligibility, though its SIM-O speaker similarity scores (0.53 English, 0.77 Chinese) are lower than the baselines, which the authors attribute to limitations of single-speaker embedding models in capturing prosodic features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoonCast is compared against two baselines: CosyVoice2 (a strong open-source single-speaker zero-shot TTS model) and a Concat Baseline (same architecture but trained only on single-speaker, single-turn data, with turns generated individually and concatenated). MoonCast decisively outperforms both on all subjective metrics for both Chinese and English: on English, it gains +0.68 in spontaneity and +0.62 in coherence over CosyVoice2, and +0.81 in spontaneity and +0.79 in coherence over the Concat Baseline. On Chinese, the gains are +0.39 in spontaneity and +0.28 in coherence over CosyVoice2. However, MoonCast underperforms both baselines on the objective SIM-O speaker similarity metric (0.53 vs. 0.73/0.75 for English; 0.77 vs. 0.85/0.86 for Chinese), which the authors attribute to the single-speaker embedding model's inability to capture temporal prosodic features and to diarization errors in the English conversational training data. In a supplementary comparison (Appendix C), MoonCast also outperforms dialogue generation baselines Sesame and Dia on WER (1.81 vs. 2.71 and 3.10) while matching their SIM-O scores, highlighting its advantage in long-form intelligibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoonCast extends the zero-shot multi-speaker dialogue generation line (CoVoMix, DGSLM, CHATS) from short (<90s) turn-by-turn synthesis to holistic, long-context (up to 10 minutes) podcast generation, using a 2.5B-parameter language model with 40K-token context. For the speech detokenizer, it builds on DiT-based flow matching (Peebles & Xie) and chunk-wise causal attention (Liu et al.) to handle long sequences efficiently, while the semantic codec follows the MaskGCT VQ-VAE design on W2v-BERT features. The key conceptual departure is the observation that ASR transcripts inherently encode spontaneous speech cues (fillers, pauses, non-verbal sounds), which the paper leverages as a training signal and inference-time conditioning strategy—something absent from prior spontaneous TTS work (SponTTS, BaseTTS) that focused on model-side style transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap between short-utterance TTS and real-world long-form multi-speaker content, and the finding that script-level spontaneity (ASR-like text) is a first-order driver of output spontaneity—dropping 0.95 in subjective score when removed—offers a simple, model-agnostic lever for any TTS pipeline. Limitations include support for only two speakers, a persistent quality gap versus ground-truth audio (attributed to ASR/diarization errors and non-speech sounds), and a SIM-O discrepancy suggesting current speaker-embedding metrics miss prosodic identity. Open questions include scaling to more than two speakers, closing the remaining quality gap, and whether the ASR-transcript-as-spontaneity-proxy insight generalizes beyond the specific podcast domain.
