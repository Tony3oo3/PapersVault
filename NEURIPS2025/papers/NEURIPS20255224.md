## Problem

Existing learning-based tractography methods either predict fiber orientations voxel-by-voxel in isolation, ignoring spatial dependencies, or model streamlines as sequences using RNNs that struggle with long-range dependencies and parallelization. No prior approach effectively unifies local microstructural context from the diffusion volume with the full trajectory history in a single architecture, leading to degraded predictions in anatomically complex regions such as fiber crossings and kissing bundles.

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of DWI values sampled along a streamline's coordinate path (not the full volume at once), with each voxel's representation enhanced by a 3D-CNN over its local 3×3×3 neighborhood. The DWI signal is standardized to 100 gradient directions or order-12 spherical harmonic coefficients, and streamlines are resampled to a fixed step size (1 mm for ISMRM, 3 mm for TractoInferno). At inference, the model outputs a conditional fODF as a 725-way discrete probability distribution (724 spherical directions + one end-of-fiber class) at each step, which is used to deterministically advance the streamline until a stopping criterion is met.

## Method

TractoTransformer frames tractography as an autoregressive sequence modeling task: a single 3×3×3 3D-CNN extracts local microstructural features from the voxel neighborhood around each streamline point, and these spatially enriched tokens are fed into an 8-layer decoder-only Transformer (10 attention heads, 512-dim FFN) with causal masking so that each fODF prediction is conditioned on the entire preceding trajectory. Training uses KL-divergence against Gaussian-smoothed soft labels on the unit sphere (σ=0.1, ~3.5° angular resolution), which respects the geometric structure of directional errors. At inference, the model propagates streamlines deterministically via argmax over the predicted fODF, with KV caching in the Transformer decoder to reuse attention states across steps and reduce inference time to ~43 minutes per subject on four V100 GPUs.

## Results

On the ISMRM 2015 Tractography Challenge, TractoTransformer with spherical harmonics input achieves the best overall performance: 84% valid connection (VC), 79% overlap (OL), 27% overreach (OR), and 75% F1 score (2015 Tractometer), improving to 78% F1 under the 2023 Tractometer edition. On the in-vivo TractoInferno dataset (4 test subjects), TractoTransformer attains the highest Dice (0.628) and Overlap (0.589) among all compared methods, with an Overreach of 0.263. The method outperforms the strongest baseline (Prob-Gaussian/SOTA) by 1.6 percentage points in Dice and 1.1 in Overlap on TractoInferno.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TractoTransformer outperforms all six baselines on the ISMRM benchmark in VC and OL. Against the closest competitor DeepTract (Benou 2019), it gains 11–13 points in VC (82–84% vs. 71%) and 10–13 points in OL (79–82% vs. 69%), with F1 improving from 70% to 75%. The RF classifier (Neher 2017) achieves 67% VC and 75% OL but lacks an F1 score; TractoTransformer surpasses it by 15–17 points in VC. LearnToTrack (Poulin 2017) and Entrack (Wegmayr 2021) trail substantially, with F1 scores of 64% and 58% respectively versus 75% for TractoTransformer. CTtrack (Hosseini 2022), the only other CNN+Transformer method, lags by 25–27 points in VC and 29–32 in OL, though it achieves the lowest overreach (16%) among all methods. On TractoInferno, TractoTransformer edges out the SOTA Prob-Gaussian baseline by 1.6 Dice points (0.628 vs. 0.612) and 1.1 Overlap points (0.589 vs. 0.578), while maintaining comparable overreach (0.263 vs. 0.288).

## Relation to Prior Work

TractoTransformer sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: RNN-based sequential tractography (LearnToTrack, DeepTract) that models streamlines as sequences of DWI measurements, and CNN+Transformer fODF estimation (CTtrack) that captures local spatial context. The key departure is a spatio-sequential formulation: a 3D-CNN extracts microstructural features from local voxel neighborhoods, and a decoder-only Transformer then performs autoregressive fODF prediction conditioned on the full trajectory prefix. This contrasts with CTtrack's non-sequential processing and with RNNs' limited long-range dependency handling, positioning the method as a principled upgrade that unifies spatial and sequential context in a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves the best reported F1 on the ISMRM benchmark (75–78%) and demonstrates generalization to in-vivo multi-site data (TractoInferno), with open-source code lowering the barrier for the community. However, the in-vivo margins over strong baselines are modest (Dice 0.628 vs. 0.612), and the computational cost (~43 min per subject on four V100s, no error bars reported) limits immediate clinical scalability. Open questions include whether efficiency gains from FlashAttention or model compression can close the gap to real-time tractography, and whether the autoregressive formulation extends naturally to probabilistic or multi-fiber tracking without the deterministic argmax step.
