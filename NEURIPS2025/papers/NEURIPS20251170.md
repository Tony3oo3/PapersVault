## Problem

Vanilla LoRA's low-rank constraint fundamentally limits its representation capacity, causing it to underperform full-rank fine-tuning on downstream tasks. Existing LoRA variants improve initialization or numerical stability but lack a principled mechanism to restore full-rank expressiveness without incurring the memory and parameter costs of full-rank training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251170/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RidgeLoRA operates as a drop-in PEFT module on pre-trained LLMs (Llama-2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, RoBERTa, DeBERTa) and vision-language models (Vicuna-7B-1.5). For language tasks it consumes text prompts and produces generated text or multiple-choice answers; for multi-modal tasks it takes image+text pairs and produces text answers. The trained ridge and low-rank matrices can be absorbed back into the original weight matrix (W' = (λΣ + (α/r)AB)W), restoring the original architecture at inference with no additional latency.

## Method

RidgeLoRA replaces LoRA's parallel additive update (W + (α/r)AB) with a series multiplicative update X_in(λΣ + (α/r)AB)W, where λΣ is a learnable diagonal ridge matrix of rank d_in that dominates the spectrum and restores full-rank expressiveness. A novel initialization strategy (Algorithm 1) splits a small r×r diagonal portion of the identity into the ridge and low-rank parts, then computes B via SVD of A to satisfy the transform-calibrating constraint λΣ + (α/r)AB = I without forcing B to zero. A Ridge Squashing Loss (nuclear-norm penalty with negative coefficient β_RS) is added to the training objective to encourage the non-diagonal portion of the weight update to grow in rank. The paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that the combined ridge-plus-low-rank approximation achieves a strictly tighter Frobenius-norm upper bound on the full-rank weight update than LoRA alone, and validates this with experiments across commonsense reasoning, math/code, multi-modal, and NLU benchmarks.

## Results

On commonsense reasoning (8 datasets), RidgeLoRA achieves 81.37% average accuracy with Llama-2-7B (only 0.15% below FFT's 81.52%), 86.76% with Llama-3.1-8B (surpassing FFT's 81.40%), and 87.43% with Mistral-v0.3-7B (surpassing FFT's 81.96%), while using only 0.26–0.29% trainable parameters. On math & code benchmarks, RidgeLoRA reaches 78.07% on GSM8K and 30.12% on MATH with Llama-3.1-8B, outperforming FFT by 2.42% on GSM8K. On multi-modal understanding, RidgeLoRA achieves 69.28% average across GQA, SQA, TextVQA, and POPE, matching or exceeding DoRA with fewer trainable parameters (4.26% vs 4.63%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RidgeLoRA consistently outperforms all four LoRA baselines across all three base models. Against vanilla LoRA, the improvement is substantial: +4.22% average on Llama-2-7B (81.37 vs 77.15), +2.23% on Llama-3.1-8B (86.76 vs 84.53), and +0.62% on Mistral (87.43 vs 86.81). Against DoRA, gains are similar in magnitude (+4.02%, +1.96%, +0.33% respectively). Against the strongest SVD-based baselines, RidgeLoRA edges out PiSSA by 0.86% on Llama-2-7B, 0.08% on Llama-3.1-8B, and 0.01% on Mistral, while beating KaSA by 4.77%, 2.27%, and 0.93% respectively. Notably, RidgeLoRA nearly matches full-rank training (FFT) on Llama-2-7B (81.37 vs 81.52, a 0.15% gap) and actually surpasses FFT on Llama-3.1-8B (+5.36%) and Mistral (+5.47%), suggesting that for stronger base models, the full-rank update space of FFT is not strictly necessary. On math & code tasks, RidgeLoRA outperforms PiSSA by 0.15% on GSM8K and 0.48% on MATH with Llama-2-7B, and beats FFT by 2.42% on GSM8K with Llama-3.1-8B.

## Relation to Prior Work

RidgeLoRA extends the LoRA family by replacing the standard parallel connection with a series connection and incorporating a diagonal ridge term inspired by the Matrix Ridge algorithm (Zhang, 2014). Unlike SVD-based variants (PiSSA, KaSA) that require expensive decomposition of the full pre-trained matrix for initialization, RidgeLoRA only needs SVD on the small low-rank matrix A, making it computationally lighter while achieving comparable or better full-rank approximation. The series architecture also relaxes the 'transform-calibrating restriction' that forces vanilla LoRA to initialize B as zeros, enabling better initialization strategies across all trainable parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RidgeLoRA demonstrates that a minimal architectural change (series connection + d_in+1 ridge parameters) can close the performance gap to full-rank training without the computational overhead of SVD-based initialization, offering a practical and lightweight PEFT alternative. The theoretical bound (Theorem 3.1) provides rigorous justification that the ridge-enhanced approximation strictly dominates vanilla LoRA's error. However, the paper does not explore scalability beyond 8B-parameter models, continual learning, or model editing scenarios where LoRA is commonly deployed, leaving open whether the series-connection advantage persists at larger scale or in more complex adaptation settings.
