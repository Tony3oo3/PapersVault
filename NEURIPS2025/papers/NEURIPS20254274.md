## Problem

Creating strong, narrow AI systems faces two fundamental challenges: (1) hierarchical task dependencies may require training on broad data distributions to efficiently learn specific narrow skills, and (2) model skills are often nonlocal and distributed across many components, making it difficult to prune away unwanted capabilities while preserving desired ones. The paper investigates both challenges through a novel synthetic task and empirical comparisons on MNIST and LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254274/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The CMSP task takes binary strings of length m+n where m control bits select which subtask(s) to solve and n task bits are the actual inputs; when multiple control bits are ON, the output is the parity of the union of corresponding task-bit subsets. For MNIST, inputs are standard 28×28 grayscale digit images and the narrow task is classifying only even digits. For LLMs, inputs are Python code documents (max length 512–1024 tokens) and the output is next-token prediction. Pruning operates on structured groups: entire hidden neurons (in-weights, bias, out-weights) in MLPs, or MLP-block neurons and residual-stream dimensions in transformers.

## Method

The paper introduces the compositional multitask sparse parity (CMSP) task, where composite labels depend on atomic subtask parities, and shows that training on atomic samples dramatically accelerates learning of composite tasks (27/40 seeds converge vs. 0/40 without atomic data). For narrowing models, the core technique is group-lasso regularization (L1 norm of per-group L2 norms) applied during continued training on the target narrow distribution, which simultaneously sparsifies weights at the neuron level and unlearned irrelevant skills. Pruning uses first-order attribution scores (|∂L/∂θ · (θ*_g − θ)|) to rank components by importance, followed by greedy removal and recovery training. Empirically, pruning-based approaches (with or without group-lasso) Pareto-dominate both distillation and training-from-scratch on the data-parameter frontier for both MNIST and Llama-3.2-1B on Python code, though random pruning recovers to similar performance as attribution-guided pruning, underscoring the nonlocality of learned features.

## Results

On the synthetic CMSP task, training on atomic + composite samples allows 27/40 networks to converge on the composite subtask within 2×10⁹ samples, while training on the composite subtask alone yields 0/40 convergence in the same budget—a dramatic curriculum effect. On MNIST (even-digit classification), pruning-based approaches Pareto-dominate distillation and training from scratch, achieving the 97% accuracy threshold with fewer datapoints at high neuron counts. On LLMs (Python code next-token prediction), pruning Llama-3.2-1B followed by recovery training is substantially more efficient than training Llama-3-architecture models from scratch or distilling from Llama-3.1-8B on the data-parameter frontier. For unlearning, at 63% and 80% sparsity all three pruning methods (random, attribution, group lasso) robustly unlearn non-Python skills, with fine-tuned scores ≥10% below the base model across AI2-ARC, WMDP-Bio, and WMDP-Cyber.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is knowledge distillation (Hinton et al. [26], KL-divergence with temperature T=20 on MNIST and T=2 on LLMs), which the paper's pruning-based methods consistently outperform. On MNIST even-digit classification, both group-lasso pruning and attribution pruning + recovery Pareto-dominate distillation, reaching the 97% accuracy threshold with fewer training datapoints at high neuron counts, while distillation cannot consistently cross 97% with fewer than 25 hidden neurons. On LLMs (Python code), pruning Llama-3.2-1B + recovery training achieves lower cross-entropy loss than both distillation from Llama-3.1-8B and training Llama-3-architecture models from scratch at equivalent parameter budgets. A notable finding is that random pruning performs comparably to attribution-based pruning after recovery training, consistent with the paper's argument that model features are nonlocal and distributed across components. For unlearning, attribution pruning at 30% sparsity fails to robustly unlearn (FT scores remain close to base), whereas group lasso and random pruning at the same sparsity do achieve unlearning; at 63%+ sparsity all methods succeed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse parity and multitask sparse parity tasks (Barak et al. 2022; Michaud et al. 2023) into a new compositional variant (CMSP) that introduces hierarchical dependencies between subtasks, and repurposes group-lasso regularization (Yuan & Lin 2006; Wen et al. 2016) and attribution-based pruning (Nanda 2023; Syed et al. 2023) for the specific goal of unlearning and narrowing. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate literatures: task-structure-driven learning dynamics (Liu et al. 2025; Chen et al. 2023) and the mechanistic interpretability finding that model features are distributed across components (Bricken et al. 2023; Elhage et al. 2022), showing that nonlocality directly impedes clean pruning for narrow AI. Distillation (Hinton et al. 2015) serves as the primary baseline against which pruning-based narrowing is compared.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies two concrete obstacles to creating strong narrow AI: (1) hierarchical task structure may necessitate training on broad data distributions before narrow skills emerge, and (2) the nonlocality of learned features means pruning cannot cleanly separate wanted from unwanted skills. The finding that random pruning performs comparably to attribution-based pruning in LLMs challenges the assumption that task-relevant features are identifiable and localizable. Key limitations include the reliance on a synthetic toy task for the curriculum effect, small-scale LLM experiments (1B model, single domain), and unoptimized hyperparameters, leaving open whether these dynamics generalize to real-world narrow-AI creation at scale.
