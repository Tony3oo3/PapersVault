## Problem

In standard autoregressive generation, the full next-token probability distribution is discarded after sampling a single discrete token, forcing the model to commit to one reasoning path and losing uncertainty information that could guide subsequent steps. This paper asks whether preserving that distributional context as part of the model's input can improve multi-step reasoning without any retraining or architectural modification.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the method consumes the sampled token y_t, its full vocabulary-level probability distribution p_t (|V| values), and the model's embedding matrix {e_i} ∈ R^{d×V}. It produces a single continuous mixed embedding vector h_t ∈ R^d that replaces the conventional one-hot token embedding as the input to the next forward pass. The final output is the standard generated text sequence; the only change is the internal representation fed back between steps, so the method is drop-in compatible with any existing decoding strategy (top-k, nucleus, temperature scaling).

## Method

MOI formulates the blending of the sampled token and its distribution as a Bayesian posterior inference problem in a Dirichlet-Multinomial model. The model's output distribution p_t serves as a Dirichlet prior with concentration α = H(p_t)·p_t, where H is the normalized entropy (so the prior spreads out when the model is uncertain and concentrates when confident). The sampled token y_t acts as a single pseudo-count observation with weight (β+1−H), where β is a hyperparameter controlling the balance between distributional and discrete evidence. The posterior mean yields mixing weights w_{t,i} = (H·p_{t,i} + (β+1−H)·y_{t,i})/(β+1), which smoothly interpolate between the full distribution (H→1) and the one-hot token (H→0). The resulting mixed embedding h_t = Σ w_{t,i}·e_i is passed as input to the next decoding step, enabling the model to reason over a distribution of plausible tokens simultaneously. The entire procedure is training-free, requires no architectural changes, and adds only ~3–4% throughput overhead in vLLM.

## Results

Across 16 model-task pairs (4 LLMs × 4 benchmarks), MOI achieves an average absolute gain of +1.8% over standard nucleus sampling, with the largest improvements on Count Down 4 (+3.7% mean) and GPQA-Diamond (+4.05% for Nemotron-Super-49B). The best single-model average gain is +2.36% for Nemotron-Super-49B (55.45% vs. 53.09%). Throughput overhead is negligible: only 2.40% on input speed and 3.66% on output speed (tokens/s) in vLLM, with a median 1.7% difference in generated text length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard nucleus sampling with temperature scaling (Holtzman et al., 2019), which represents the default inference recipe for each model. MOI outperforms this baseline on 15 of 16 model-task pairs, with the sole exception being AIME on DAPO-Qwen-32B (−0.23%, within noise). The second comparison is a Direct Mixture ablation that feeds the raw output distribution as a weighted embedding without Bayesian reconciliation; this degrades performance in most cases (e.g., −22.9% on LiveCodeBench for Nemotron-Super-49B, −11.9% on AIME for QwQ-32B), confirming that the posterior estimation step is essential. On MT-Bench (instruction-following), MOI also improves over standard decoding for all six models tested, with gains ranging from +0.74% (Nemotron) to +5.54% (DAPO-Qwen-32B), and reduces 4-gram repetition from 4.27% to 3.13%.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOI sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the observed linearity of LLM embedding spaces, which makes linear combinations of token embeddings semantically meaningful; (2) continuous-reasoning approaches like COCONUT, which feed hidden states back as inputs but require retraining and architectural changes; and (3) model-merging and soft-prompt work that exploits representational linearity at the weight or prompt level. MOI departs from all of these by operating at the individual-token input level during autoregressive decoding, using a Dirichlet-Multinomial Bayesian posterior to blend the sampled token with its output distribution—requiring no retraining, no architectural modification, and no external data projection. The key conceptual departure from COCONUT is that MOI works entirely in the input embedding space rather than the hidden state space, and from Vector-ICL is that it leverages the model's own generation-time distribution rather than projecting external continuous data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOI offers a clean, training-free, drop-in enhancement to autoregressive generation with negligible overhead (~3.7% throughput cost), making it immediately deployable in existing LLM serving stacks. The finding that using the output distribution alone (Direct Mixture) actually degrades performance—sometimes severely—while the Bayesian blend of token + distribution consistently helps, reveals a non-trivial interaction: the discrete anchor and the probabilistic context are complementary rather than redundant. Open questions include the task-dependent optimal β (low for open-ended reasoning, high for combinatorial enumeration), whether adaptive or test-time β tuning can close this gap, and whether the approach extends to open-ended or creative generation tasks where objectives are less formally defined.
