## Problem

Antimicrobial peptide (AMP) research is hindered by fragmented datasets with inconsistent annotation granularity, the absence of a standardized multilabel benchmark, and the predominance of binary classification that ignores AMPs' multi-target activity. No existing framework allows fair, reproducible comparison of AI methods across a unified, large-scale peptide collection with biologically coherent functional labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254303/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two modalities per peptide: (1) an amino acid sequence tokenized over a 27-symbol vocabulary and zero-padded to 200 residues, and (2) a 224×224 single-channel distance matrix computed from Cα-atom Euclidean distances of the 3D structure (sourced from UniProt/PDB or predicted via RoseTTAFold/AlphaFold3 when experimental structures are unavailable). The output is a 5-label probability vector corresponding to antibacterial, antifungal, antiviral, antiparasitic, and general antimicrobial activity, evaluated with mAP and F1 under severe class imbalance.

## Method

ESCAPE Baseline is a dual-branch transformer that encodes sequence and structure independently before fusing them via bidirectional cross-attention. The sequence branch maps tokens to 256-dim embeddings with a [CLS] token and 4 transformer layers (8 heads); the structure branch patches the 224×224 distance matrix into 16×16 tiles, projects them to 192-dim embeddings, and processes them through an identically configured transformer stack. Bidirectional cross-attention then lets each modality's queries attend to the other's keys and values, with residual connections and feedforward refinement, so that sequence residues can attend to spatial neighborhoods and vice versa. The updated [CLS] vectors from both branches are concatenated and passed through a linear head for the 5-class multilabel prediction. Trained end-to-end with AdamW (lr 1e-4, batch 64, 100 epochs on an A100 GPU), the architecture achieves its best performance when both modalities are present, with ablations showing the sequence branch contributes ~21% more mAP than structure alone, while cross-attention adds a further ~3% over sequence-only.

## Results

On the ESCAPE Benchmark (5-class multilabel AMP classification), the ESCAPE Baseline achieves 72.1% mAP and 69.8% overall F1-score, outperforming all seven state-of-the-art methods. The headline improvement is a 2.56% relative gain in mAP and 1.90% relative gain in F1-score over the second-best method (AMPlify, 70.3% mAP / 68.5% F1). The largest per-class gain is in the antiparasitic category, where ESCAPE Baseline reaches 37.6% AP versus 27.7% for AMPlify, a 35.7% relative improvement. The model is trained with 2-fold cross-validation, 3 random seeds (42, 1665, 8914), and evaluated via probability-averaged ensembles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESCAPE Baseline outperforms all seven baselines on both mAP and F1-score. The closest competitor is AMPlify (bidirectional LSTM with attention), which achieves 70.3% mAP and 68.5% F1, trailing ESCAPE Baseline by 1.8 and 1.3 percentage points respectively. Among feature-augmented methods, AVP-IFT (contrastive learning + transformer with biophysical descriptors) reaches 68.8% mAP, while amPEPpy (Random Forest on CTD features) and PEP-Net (residual CNN + Transformer with PLM embeddings) score 68.5% and 68.4% mAP. BERT-based sequence models (AMP-BERT at 66.9% mAP, TransImbAMP at 64.9%) underperform despite domain-adaptive pretraining, and the GNN-based AMPs-Net lags far behind at 54.6% mAP. The most striking per-class win is antiparasitic AP, where ESCAPE Baseline (37.6%) surpasses AMPlify (27.7%) by 35.7% relatively, highlighting the benefit of structural information for rare classes. Notably, the second-best overall method (AMPlify) is a sequence-only model, while the least computationally demanding method (amPEPpy, a Random Forest) ranks fourth, suggesting that architectural complexity alone does not guarantee superior AMP classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper consolidates 27 previously fragmented AMP databases (dbAMP, DRAMP, LAMP2, SATPdb, etc.) into a single standardized multilabel collection, departing from prior work that typically trained on a single dataset of a few hundred to a few thousand peptides. Methodologically, it extends the binary AMP-vs-non-AMP classification paradigm (AMPlify, AMP-BERT, TransImbAMP) to a five-class multilabel setting and introduces a dual-branch transformer with bidirectional cross-attention that fuses amino-acid sequence tokens with 3D distance-matrix patches — a modality combination not previously explored in AMP classification. The benchmark itself is novel: no prior work had adapted and fairly compared seven state-of-the-art AMP classifiers under a unified multilabel protocol with fixed splits and multiple seeds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With AMR projected to cause over 39 million deaths by 2050, a reproducible, large-scale benchmark is essential for the field to move beyond non-comparable, dataset-specific claims. The finding that BERT-based peptide language models (TransImbAMP, AMP-BERT) underperform simpler sequence models and that model size does not correlate with accuracy challenges the assumption that scaling pretrained language models will solve peptide classification. Open questions include whether the structural modality's contribution (only ~3% mAP gain over sequence-only) justifies the cost of structure prediction, whether the benchmark generalizes to peptides outside the 5–250 residue window, and whether the severe class imbalance (417 antiparasitic vs. 16,106 antibacterial) can be addressed without inflating the dataset with synthetic examples.
