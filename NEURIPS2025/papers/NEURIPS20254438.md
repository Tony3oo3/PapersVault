## Problem

Existing monocular 3D detection models struggle on in-the-wild images because large-scale 3D datasets with high-quality bounding box annotations are scarce outside indoor and autonomous driving domains. Prior auto-labeling approaches (e.g., OVM3D-Det) rely on metric depth and category-level size priors, which fail for objects with high intra-class size variation (e.g., baby vs. adult elephant). The paper asks how to produce high-quality 3D annotations on natural images with minimal human supervision.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single RGB image (super-resolved 4× by InvSR) and 2D instance segmentation masks from COCONut as input, along with text prompts for open-vocabulary detection. It outputs per-object 3D bounding boxes (center, yaw orientation, and dimensions) embedded in a metric-scale 3D scene point cloud. The resulting COCO3D benchmark contains 2,039 images with 5,373 annotated instances spanning all 80 MS-COCO categories, with 10 categories excluded from evaluation due to extreme aspect ratios or too few instances.

## Method

LabelAny3D adopts an analysis-by-synthesis paradigm: it reconstructs a holistic 3D scene from a monocular image and extracts 3D annotations from the synthesized geometry. The pipeline chains (1) diffusion-based super-resolution, (2) amodal completion via Gen3DSR followed by single-view 3D mesh reconstruction via TRELLIS, (3) scene geometry estimation by aligning MoGe's relative depth to Depth Pro's metric depth via RANSAC-based scale fitting, (4) pose estimation through dense 2D-2D correspondence matching (MASt3R) between the real image and rendered mesh views, solved with a PnP+RANSAC solver, and (5) metric scale recovery via median depth-ratio alignment between real and rendered depth maps. The key design choice is relying on relative depth (more reliable than metric depth from a single view) combined with object-centric shape reconstruction, rather than metric depth plus category size priors. Downstream, the pseudo-labels are used to fine-tune OVMono3D's lifting head with a disentangled 3D loss (per-attribute L1 + Chamfer loss) and uncertainty weighting.

## Results

On the new COCO3D benchmark, fine-tuning OVMono3D with Omni3D + LabelAny3D pseudo-labels achieves 10.92 AP₃D and 20.10 AR₃D, a 5.05 AP₃D gain over the Omni3D-only baseline (5.87). In terms of pseudo-annotation quality, LabelAny3D reaches 64.17 AP₃D against human-refined ground truth, compared to only 10.03 for the OVM3D-Det baseline. On Omni3D's novel-category split, the combined training yields 16.98 AP₃D versus 16.05 for the baseline, indicating improved open-vocabulary generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OVM3D-Det [27], the primary auto-labeling baseline, LabelAny3D wins decisively on both annotation quality and downstream detection. On pseudo-annotation quality (Table 2), LabelAny3D achieves 64.17 AP₃D versus 10.03 for OVM3D-Det — a 6.4× gap — with per-IoU-threshold scores of 82.11 (AP₁₅) and 57.34 (AP₅₀) versus 16.88 and 1.44 respectively. For downstream monocular 3D detection on COCO3D, combining Omni3D with LabelAny3D labels yields 10.92 AP₃D, outperforming the Omni3D + OVM3D-Det combination (6.82) by 4.1 AP₃D. Against the OVMono3D baseline trained only on Omni3D [75], LabelAny3D fine-tuning improves COCO3D AP₃D from 5.87 to 10.92 and relative-layout AP₃D from 20.86 to 32.02, while also improving Omni3D Novel AP₃D from 16.05 to 16.98. However, on Omni3D Base categories, the fine-tuned model shows a modest degradation (24.77 → 22.74 AP₃D), which the authors attribute to catastrophic forgetting and residual label noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on OVMono3D as the downstream detection model and Gen3DSR for amodal completion, but its core conceptual departure is from prior auto-labeling approaches like OVM3D-Det (which lifts 2D boxes using metric depth + LLM-derived size priors) and 3D Copy-Paste (which inserts synthetic meshes). LabelAny3D instead adopts an analysis-by-synthesis paradigm: it reconstructs each object's actual 3D geometry via TRELLIS, estimates scene structure via MoGe relative depth aligned to Depth Pro metric scale, and localizes objects through MASt3R 2D-3D correspondence matching with PnP solving. This shifts the annotation problem from 'predict depth and guess size' to 'reconstruct the object and align it to the scene,' which is more robust to intra-class size variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COCO3D is the first monocular 3D detection benchmark covering all 80 MS-COCO categories in in-the-wild settings, filling a critical gap left by Omni3D's indoor/driving focus. The pipeline's 64.17 AP3D agreement with human-refined labels (vs. 10.03 for OVM3D-Det) demonstrates that foundation-model-driven 3D annotation can approach human quality at scale, potentially unlocking large-scale 3D dataset construction. Open questions remain around robustness to heavy occlusion, textureless objects, and whether pseudo-label noise can be mitigated during training; the paper also notes that fine-tuning on in-the-wild data causes some catastrophic forgetting on base categories.
