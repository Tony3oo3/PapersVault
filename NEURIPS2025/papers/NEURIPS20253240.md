## Problem

Thermal infrared (TIR) images suffer from coupled degradations—low contrast, blurring, and fixed-pattern noise—that existing single-degradation methods cannot jointly address, while all-in-one restoration frameworks designed for visible light fail to transfer due to fundamentally different imaging models. Additionally, the field lacks a high-quality, multi-scenario TIR benchmark with diverse corruption types to properly evaluate enhancement under realistic composite conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253240/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded 640×512 thermal infrared image (8–14 μm wavelength) along with two discrete prompts: a degradation-specific prompt identifying which impairment (noise, blur, or contrast) to remove in the current step, and a type-specific prompt indicating whether the scenario is single or composite degradation. It produces an enhanced TIR image with the targeted degradation removed. In composite scenarios, the model is invoked sequentially three times (denoise → deblur → decontrast), with each output feeding the next iteration, while in single-degradation scenarios a single forward pass suffices.

## Method

The Progressive Prompt Fusion Network (PPFN) encodes degradation-specific and type-specific prompts through lightweight encoders, concatenates the resulting prompt features, and projects them via a linear layer plus non-linear activation into FiLM-style modulation parameters (γ, β) that are applied channel-wise to the backbone's intermediate features at each layer. This prompt fusion block is plug-and-play and can be inserted into any restoration backbone (Restormer, NAFNet, UFormer, XRestormer, FocalNet). The Selective Progressive Training (SPT) mechanism handles composite degradations by iteratively removing one degradation per step in reverse order, computing gradients at each step without updating parameters until all steps complete, and using stop-gradient on intermediate outputs to prevent residual-degradation interference; single-degradation training follows a standard supervised protocol. The paper also introduces the HM-TIR benchmark (1,503 images across 8 scene categories and 5 corruption types) to support reproducible evaluation.

## Results

On the real-world Iray dataset, PPFN achieves NIMA 3.8327, MUSIQ 30.9072, and NIQE 8.4693, outperforming all baselines across all three no-reference IQA metrics. On the multi-degradation Normal Set, integrating PPFN with Restormer yields an 8.76% PSNR improvement over the unmodified baseline. The plug-and-play PPFN module consistently improves PSNR and SSIM across five different backbone architectures (FocalNet, UFormer, NAFNet, XRestormer, Restormer), with Restormer+PPFN reaching an average PSNR of 25.32 and SSIM of 0.818 on the Normal Set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPFN outperforms all 18 baselines across every evaluated metric and dataset. On the Iray real-world dataset, PPFN leads NIMA by +0.10 over the best TIR-specific method (WFAF, 3.7321) and by +0.13 over the best all-in-one visible method (DA-CLIP, 3.7004); it leads MUSIQ by +1.87 over IE-CGAN (29.0350) and NIQE by −0.21 over LRSID (8.6838). TIR-specific single-degradation methods (WFAF, LRSID, TSIRIE, IE-CGAN) all underperform because they target only one degradation type and fail on composite scenarios. All-in-one visible restoration methods (DA-CLIP, DiffUIR) perform better than TIR-specific methods but still lag behind PPFN due to fundamental differences between visible and infrared imaging models. For single-degradation tasks, PPFN surpasses dedicated denoising (AP-BSN, CycleISP, IDR, SDAP), deblurring (DeBlurGANv2, MIMOUNet, FFTformer, Stripformer), and contrast-enhancement (MSBDN, FFA-Net, LLFormer, SCI) methods, which retain artifacts due to modeling differences between TIR and visible images. The PPFN module also consistently improves every backbone architecture it is plugged into, confirming its plug-and-play utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the prompt-based all-in-one restoration paradigm (PromptIR, ProRes, InstructIR) from the visible-image domain to thermal infrared imaging, where the degradation physics (fixed-pattern noise, stripe noise, optics noise, radiation-induced low contrast) are fundamentally different from RGB degradations. It builds on Restormer and other transformer/convolutional restoration backbones as plug-and-play hosts for its PPFN module, and draws on TIR-specific degradation modeling from prior denoising and deblurring literature. The key conceptual departure is introducing a physically-motivated fixed degradation ordering (contrast → blur → noise) and a Selective Progressive Training scheme that iteratively removes degradations in reverse order without cascading separate networks, which distinguishes it from both single-degradation TIR methods and RGB all-in-one approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIR imaging underpins critical applications (autonomous driving, surveillance, object detection) yet lacks a unified framework for handling the composite degradations that co-occur in real-world thermal scenes; this paper is the first to address that gap and provides the HM-TIR benchmark (1,503 images, 640×512, 8 scenarios) to fill a notable data scarcity. The main limitation acknowledged is that the synthetic degradation pipeline, while physically motivated, may not fully capture the complexity of real-world TIR distortions (motion artifacts, radiation attenuation, diffraction), and the fixed degradation ordering assumption may not generalize to all sensor configurations. Open questions include whether the prompt design can be made input-adaptive (self-prompting or language-based) and whether the progressive training strategy extends to more than three degradation types or to video TIR enhancement.
