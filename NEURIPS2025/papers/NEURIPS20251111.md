## Problem

As LLM context windows stretch to hundreds of thousands of tokens and parameter counts reach trillions, processing long inputs becomes computationally prohibitive. Existing compression methods either route all tokens through the heavy LLM itself or use text encoders that fail to address the inherent redundancy in long text, making it difficult for the LLM to allocate attention to key content. This paper asks whether a lightweight vision encoder can serve as a cost-efficient 'fast path' to skim distant, low-salience context—mirroring how skilled readers fixate on rare content words while glossing over function words.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long text sequence of T tokens, splits it into T_e distant tokens (rendered into M images of 224×224 RGB at 10px Google Noto Sans font) and T_d proximal raw text tokens. The vision encoder path produces a fixed 64 visual tokens per image (plus a CLS token), yielding a 2.3× compression ratio (e.g., 1024 text tokens → 448 visual tokens). The LLM consumes both the compressed visual tokens (via cross-attention) and the raw proximal tokens to perform standard next-token prediction.

## Method

VIST is a slow-fast compression framework where a frozen ViT-L/14 (OpenCLIP) processes rendered text images and a trainable Perceiver Resampler (from Flamingo) compresses the resulting features into a fixed set of 64 tokens per image, which are injected into the LLM via cross-attention layers placed between self-attention and FFN in each decoder block. The key training objective is Probability-informed Visual Enhancement (PVE), a contrastive loss that aligns pooled visual CLS features with text token embeddings from the LLM's own tokenizer, bridging the semantic gap between visual and text representations. To combat text redundancy, PVE incorporates frequency-based masking: each token's importance is scored as s_w = log(|S|/(1+count(w))) following Shannon information theory, and the top 50% most frequent (lowest-information) tokens are selectively masked from the supervision signal, steering the Resampler to prioritize semantically rich content. The entire system is trained end-to-end on 1B tokens from RedPajama with TinyLlama, using fp16 precision and DeepSpeed Zero-2, with the vision encoder kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On long-context language modeling (LCM), VIST achieves the lowest perplexity among all compression methods (e.g., 2.993 on ArXiv, 14.973 on Book at T_e=2048), while requiring 2.3× fewer tokens than the text-encoder baseline CEPE*, reducing FLOPs by 16% and memory by 50%. On 11 in-context learning (ICL) datasets, VIST achieves 46.3% average accuracy (n_e=18, n_d=2), outperforming CEPE* (44.1%) by 2.2 points and TinyLlama with 20 demos (57.3%) at a fraction of the compute cost. On open-domain QA, VIST reaches 25.20 EM on TriviaQA (k_e=5, k_d=10), surpassing CEPE* (16.41) by 8.79 points and TinyLlama (21.45) by 3.75 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary text-encoder baseline CEPE*, VIST consistently wins: on ICL it achieves 46.3% vs. 44.1% average accuracy (n_e=18, n_d=2) and 50.4% vs. 46.8% (n_e=48, n_d=2); on open-domain QA it reaches 25.20 EM on TriviaQA vs. CEPE*'s 16.41 (k_e=5, k_d=10), a gap of 8.79 points, while also using 16% fewer FLOPs and 50% less memory. Against vision-centric compression methods ToMe† and FastV† (which merge/prune visual features from natural-image-trained ViTs), VIST achieves substantially lower perplexity (2.993 vs. 3.536/3.491 on ArXiv at T_e=2048), confirming that text-specific PVE training is critical. Against long-context baselines Replug and Stream (which process all tokens through the LLM), VIST achieves lower perplexity (2.993 vs. 3.220/3.116 on ArXiv) at 3.8× throughput versus 0.2×/1.6×. Against the uncompressed TinyLlama baseline, VIST extends effective context from 2048 to 14,336+ tokens where TinyLlama's perplexity exceeds 10³, and on ICL with 20 total demos VIST (46.3%) approaches TinyLlama's 20-demo result (57.3%) while processing 18 demos through a 300M-parameter vision encoder instead of the full LLM. On Mistral 7B, VIST† outperforms CEPE† on both LCM (2.82 vs. 2.83 ArXiv PPL) and ICL (92.8 vs. 90.8 SST2, 95.3 vs. 94.2 DBP), validating generality across model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIST departs from the dominant token-compression paradigm (soft-prompt compression and LLM-based token selection à la LLMLingua) by offloading distant context to a frozen vision encoder rather than the LLM itself. Its closest predecessor is CEPE, which uses a lightweight text encoder (RoBERTa-large) with cross-attention; VIST replaces that text encoder with a CLIP ViT-L/14 + Perceiver Resampler pipeline and introduces the PVE contrastive objective with frequency-based masking as a training signal. The vision-centric text-processing lineage (Pixel, CLIPPO, tokenizer-free approaches) provides the conceptual foundation, but VIST is the first to combine a pretrained image-text vision encoder with an LLM cross-attention decoder specifically for long-context compression, and to ground the masking strategy in Shannon information theory rather than LLM-computed entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.3× token reduction with 50% memory savings and 16% FLOPs reduction is practically meaningful for deployment of long-context LLMs, and the empirical demonstration that rare tokens carry disproportionate semantic weight (validated via embedding-distance experiments) offers a simple, LLM-free importance signal that could inform other compression or pruning methods. However, the evaluation is limited to TinyLlama (1.1B) and Mistral-7B, English-only, and ICL accuracy still trails a full-LLM baseline with more demos, so the practical ceiling of the approach at scale remains open. Broader questions include whether the vision-encoder-as-text-tokenizer framing generalizes to non-Latin scripts, whether the PVE objective transfers to larger vision encoders, and whether the 50% masking ratio is optimal across domains.
