## Problem

Unified multimodal large language models (U-MLLMs) that jointly perform visual understanding and autoregressive image generation exhibit significant gender and race biases in their outputs, yet it remains unclear whether these biases originate in the vision tokenizer or the language model component. Existing debiasing methods are designed for diffusion-based generators and do not transfer to the discrete-token autoregressive paradigm used by U-MLLMs, leaving a gap in both diagnosis and mitigation for this architecture class.

## Input / Output

The method takes a neutral text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a construction worker') with no explicit demographic specification and produces a set of generated images that should be demographically balanced. Internally, the U-MLLM encodes images via a VQ-VAE into discrete token sequences and decodes them back, while the autoregressive language model treats text and image tokens under a single next-token distribution. For training, each neutral prompt is paired with exactly one image per demographic group (K groups), forming a balanced synthetic dataset of 300K images generated by FLUX with explicit demographic-augmented prompts.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage debiasing pipeline: (1) supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a demographically balanced synthetic dataset, and (2) Balanced Preference Optimization (BPO), which minimizes a novel pairwise balanced preference loss over all demographic group pairs. The BPO loss, L_bal = log[1 + (σ(log OR_θ) − 0.5)²], penalizes deviations of the odds ratio between any two demographic groups from unity, driving the model toward equal generation probability across groups without requiring a separate reference model. A component-wise audit using linear probing on vision encoder embeddings and Jensen-Shannon divergence on image-token distributions localizes the bias primarily to the language model's autoregressive token selection rather than the vision tokenizer. The approach is trained with LoRA (rank 32) and requires only 1–2 additional epochs beyond SFT, reducing VILA-U's gender bias by ~72% while preserving CLIP score and Inception score.

## Results

On the VILA-U model, BPO-Gender reduces gender bias from 0.89 to 0.25 (a ~72% reduction) while maintaining CLIP-S at 27.74 and improving Inception Score from 1.87 to 2.10. BPO-Mix (joint gender-race debiasing) achieves the best intersectional bias of 0.15, outperforming prompt engineering (0.18) and T→I fine-tuning (0.17), with CLIP-IQA of 0.80 and Inception Score of 2.06. Across all six evaluated U-MLLMs, gender bias ranges from 0.84 (TokenFlow) to 0.90 (Janus-Pro), substantially higher than Stable Diffusion's 0.66, confirming that unified architectures amplify demographic bias relative to generation-only systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five U-MLLM baselines, all exhibit substantially higher gender bias (0.84–0.90) than Stable Diffusion (0.66), with Janus-Pro being the worst at 0.90. When debiasing VILA-U, BPO-Gender achieves the lowest gender bias (0.25) among all methods, beating prompt engineering (0.56) by 55% and T→I fine-tuning (0.27) marginally, while also yielding a higher Inception Score (2.10 vs. 1.85). For race debiasing, T→I fine-tuning achieves the lowest race bias (0.23) but BPO-Race is competitive (0.26) with a notably higher Inception Score (2.31 vs. 1.98). In the joint G×R setting, BPO-Mix achieves the best intersectional bias (0.15), outperforming both prompt engineering (0.18) and T→I fine-tuning (0.17), while maintaining CLIP-IQA at 0.80. The paper also shows that fine-tuning the understanding pathway (I→T) yields only marginal generation-bias reduction (gender: 0.89→0.83), confirming that understanding debiasing does not transfer to generation and that BPO's direct optimization of the generation distribution is necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fairness-in-image-generation literature, which has primarily targeted diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion debiasing via re-balanced training data or fairness objectives), to the emerging class of autoregressive unified multimodal LLMs (U-MLLMs) that generate images as discrete tokens. Methodologically, it adapts the odds-ratio preference optimization framework from ORPO/DPO (originally for text LLM alignment) into a balanced preference loss for image-token generation, and it introduces a component-wise diagnostic protocol (linear probing on VQ-VAE embeddings, Jensen-Shannon divergence on token distributions) to localize bias sources within the U-MLLM architecture. The paper departs from prior debiasing work by showing that the language model, not the vision tokenizer, is the primary bias origin, and by demonstrating that understanding-side debiasing (I→T fine-tuning) does not transfer to generation-side fairness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As U-MLLMs become the dominant paradigm for unified visual understanding and generation, this work provides the first systematic audit of demographic bias in these systems and a practical, low-overhead debiasing recipe (SFT + 1–2 epoch BPO with LoRA) that practitioners can adopt. The 'partial alignment' finding—that models can appear unbiased in text VQA yet produce heavily skewed images—challenges the assumption that standard alignment training suffices for generative fairness and motivates generation-specific interventions. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond VILA-U to other U-MLLM architectures, whether it extends to additional demographic axes (age, disability, intersectional identities), and whether the discrete Western-centric category framework used for evaluation is adequate for real-world deployment.
