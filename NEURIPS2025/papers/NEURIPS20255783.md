## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces high-quality renderings but recovers inaccurate surface geometry because its discrete, unstructured Gaussian primitives lack mechanisms to enforce global surface consistency. Supervision from RGB rendering loss alone leads to illumination-induced artifacts (shadows, specular highlights), ambiguous object boundaries, and inconsistent multi-view alignment—two persistent challenges that existing Gaussian-based reconstruction methods (SuGaR, 2DGS, GOF, GS-Pull, PGSR) have not fully resolved.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed RGB images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics (obtained via COLMAP), which are downsampled to a lower resolution for training. A COLMAP sparse point cloud initializes the 3D Gaussians. For surface reconstruction, per-pixel depth and normal maps are rendered from all training views and fused via TSDF to extract a mesh; for novel view synthesis, one out of every eight images is held out as the test set. The final outputs are the optimized 3D Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, spherical-harmonic color), an extracted mesh, and rendered novel-view images.

## Method

VA-GS retains the full anisotropic 3D Gaussian representation (unlike 2DGS or SDF-based variants) and instead enforces geometric fidelity through five complementary loss terms applied during optimization. Single-view alignment combines an edge-aware reconstruction loss (adding L1 gradient supervision to the standard L1+SSIM term) with an edge-weighted normal consistency loss and a normal smoothing loss that penalizes abrupt normal changes while preserving true surface edges via a ReLU-thresholded Iverson bracket. Multi-view alignment introduces a visibility-aware photometric consistency loss that warps 7×7 reference patches to source views via homography, weights each pixel by an occlusion-aware projection error term, and measures consistency with normalized cross-correlation; a parallel feature alignment loss uses pretrained Vis-MVSNet embeddings and cosine similarity to remain robust under illumination variation. Training proceeds sequentially: 7,000 steps of color-only pretraining, then edge and normal losses, followed by 8,000 steps of photometric alignment and 5,000 steps of feature alignment (N=3 source views), with an additional 10,000 steps for rendering quality in the novel-view-synthesis setting.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, VA-GS achieves the lowest mean Chamfer distance of 0.49 mm, outperforming the next-best explicit method PGSR (0.53) and GausSurf (0.52). On Tanks and Temples, it attains the highest mean F1-score of 0.54, surpassing PGSR (0.52) and GausSurf (0.50). For novel view synthesis on Mip-NeRF 360, VA-GS reports average PSNR 27.50 dB, SSIM 0.837, and LPIPS 0.174, achieving the best LPIPS and tying for best SSIM among all Gaussian-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VA-GS outperforms all explicit Gaussian-based baselines on both surface reconstruction benchmarks: on DTU it improves mean Chamfer distance by 0.04 mm over PGSR (0.53) and 0.03 mm over GausSurf (0.52), and on TNT it gains 0.02 F1 over PGSR (0.52) and 0.04 over GausSurf (0.50). Against implicit methods, VA-GS matches or exceeds PSDF (0.55 on DTU) while being orders of magnitude faster (15.5 min vs. >12 h for NeuS/Neuralangelo). On Mip-NeRF 360 novel view synthesis, VA-GS achieves the best LPIPS (0.174) and ties RaDe-GS on SSIM (0.837), though RaDe-GS edges it slightly on PSNR (27.65 vs. 27.50). Notably, planar-constrained variants (2DGS, SuGaR, GS-Pull) degrade rendering quality relative to vanilla 3DGS on Mip-NeRF 360, confirming that VA-GS's full 3D Gaussian representation preserves rendering fidelity while improving geometry. The method's training time (15.5 min on DTU) is comparable to PGSR (15 min) and GausSurf, making it practical despite the multi-view alignment overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3DGS as its geometric representation and rendering pipeline, adapting the normal consistency loss from 2DGS and the edge-aware per-pixel weighting from PGSR. It departs from the dominant trend of modifying the Gaussian representation itself (planar constraints in 2DGS, SDF branches in GS-Pull/GSDF, opacity fields in GOF) by instead introducing a suite of view-alignment losses—edge-aware reconstruction, normal smoothing, homography-based photometric consistency, and deep feature matching—applied during optimization without altering the underlying 3D Gaussian parameterization. The multi-view photometric alignment draws explicitly from classical MVS patch-warping, while the feature alignment leverages Vis-MVSNet pretrained embeddings, bridging traditional stereo geometry with learned representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that careful multi-view alignment losses can close the geometry gap between Gaussian splatting and dedicated surface reconstruction methods without sacrificing rendering quality, addressing two persistent failure modes (illumination artifacts and boundary ambiguity) that prior Gaussian-based methods have struggled with. The practical implication is that practitioners can obtain high-fidelity meshes from a standard 3DGS pipeline with only loss-function modifications, avoiding the complexity of additional SDF networks or representation changes. The main limitation is training speed (~15.5 min vs. 3.4 min for vanilla 3DGS), and the approach has not been tested on dynamic scenes or very large-scale environments, leaving open whether the multi-view alignment scales efficiently to those settings.
