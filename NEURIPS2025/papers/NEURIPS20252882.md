## Problem

Prior theoretical results for contextual cascading bandits all suggested that regret grows with the cascade length K (polynomially or logarithmically), contradicting the intuition that longer cascades provide more feedback opportunities. This paper resolves the gap by proving that regret actually decreases once K is sufficiently large, and provides the first N-independent lower bound for contextual cascading bandits to confirm the correct K-scaling.

## Input / Output

At each round, the agent receives d-dimensional contextual feature vectors x_{t,i} ∈ ℝ^d (bounded by ‖x‖ ≤ 1) for all N candidate arms and must select an ordered cascade of K distinct arms. The environment returns binary click feedback only for the observed prefix of the cascade (stopping at the first click), creating a partial-observation structure. The algorithm outputs the selected cascade and, theoretically, a regret bound of Õ(min{Kp̄^{K-1}, 1}·d√T) that explicitly captures how cascade length modulates statistical efficiency.

## Method

UCB-CLB combines three key components: (1) online mirror descent (OMD) for parameter estimation of the logistic click model, achieving O(K²d³) per-round computation and O(d²) storage independent of T, in contrast to MLE-based baselines; (2) a UCB construction where the optimistic expected reward of a cascade is 1 − ∏σ₀(u_{t,i}), reducing cascade selection to a top-K procedure over optimistic scores u_{t,i} = x_{t,i}ᵀθ_t + β_t‖x_{t,i}‖_{H_t⁻¹}; and (3) Doubly Optimistic Exposure Swapping (DO-SWAP), which places the arm maximizing σ̇₁(xᵀθ_t)‖x‖_{H_t⁻¹} in position 1 and the arm maximizing ‖x‖_{H_t⁻¹} in position 2, ensuring the unobserved-arm leftover in the Gram matrix is controlled. The central theoretical insight is that the product of non-click probabilities across K arms introduces a multiplicative factor Kp̄^{K-1} into the regret, which is monotonically decreasing in K for p̄ ≤ e⁻¹, yielding the tightest known upper bound Õ(min{Kp̄^{K-1},1}·d√T) and a near-matching lower bound Ω(min{Kp̲^{K-1},1}·d√T).

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is a regret upper bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\min\{K\bar{p}^{K-1},1\}\,d\sqrt{T})$ for contextual cascading logistic bandits (Theorem 5.6), with a nearly matching lower bound of $\Omega(\min\{K\underline{p}^{K-1},1\}\,d\sqrt{T})$ (Theorem 5.7). The critical finding is that the multiplicative factor $K\bar{p}^{K-1}$ is non-increasing in $K$ (after a plateau), meaning regret *decreases* once the cascade length is sufficiently large — contradicting all prior contextual cascading bandit results that showed regret growing with $K$. Empirically on MovieLens 100K ($N=1682$, $d=25$), UCB-CLB achieves lower cumulative regret than CLogUCB and VA-CLogUCB for both $K=5$ and $K=10$, and its runtime scales linearly in $T$ versus exponential growth for MLE-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UCB-CLB is compared against four existing contextual cascading bandit algorithms: UCB-CCA [7], CLogUCB [21], VA-CLogUCB [21], and EVA-CLogUCB [21]. Theoretically, UCB-CLB is the first to achieve a regret bound that *decreases* with cascade length $K$ (via the $\min\{K\bar{p}^{K-1},1\}$ factor), whereas all four baselines have bounds that are either flat or increasing in $K$. Empirically on MovieLens 100K, UCB-CLB and UCB-CCA both outperform CLogUCB and VA-CLogUCB in cumulative regret for $K=5$ and $K=10$, which the authors attribute to the small $\kappa$ value weakening $1/\kappa$-dependent methods. On computational efficiency, UCB-CLB (using OMD) exhibits linear runtime scaling in $T$, while all MLE-based baselines (UCB-CCA, CLogUCB, VA-CLogUCB) show exponential runtime growth, making UCB-CLB substantially more scalable for long-horizon settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contextual cascading bandit framework introduced by Li et al. [19] by combining three prior threads: (i) the optimistic exposure swapping technique from Choi et al. [7], extended here to a 'doubly' optimistic variant (DO-SWAP) that controls both the first- and second-position arms; (ii) OMD-based online parameter estimation from Zhang & Sugiyama [27] and Lee & Oh [13,14], adapted to the cascading setting for constant per-round compute; and (iii) the non-contextual gap-dependent bounds of Kveton et al. [11], which already showed regret decreasing with K in the non-contextual case. The key departure from all prior contextual results [19,28,26,20,7,21]—which uniformly show regret growing (or at best constant) in K—is the introduction of the multiplicative factor min{K·p̄^(K−1), 1}, which makes regret decrease for sufficiently large cascade length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a long-standing open question in contextual cascading bandits: the true dependence of regret on cascade length K. The near-matching upper and lower bounds (differing only in log factors and the gap between p̄ and p̲) establish that the K·p̄^(K−1) scaling is intrinsic to the cascade structure, not an artifact of the logistic model. Practically, this means longer recommendation lists can reduce regret rather than increase it, which has direct implications for system design in search and recommendation. Open questions include closing the p̄ vs p̲ gap in the bounds, extending to settings where p̄ = 1 (arms with zero click probability), and understanding whether the OMD-based estimator's constant-compute advantage persists in more complex cascade structures (e.g., multi-level cascades).
