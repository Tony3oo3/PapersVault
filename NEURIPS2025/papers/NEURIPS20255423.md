## Problem

Graph Transformers (GTs) were introduced to overcome the local receptive field limitation of GNNs, yet recent empirical studies show that well-tuned classical GNNs can match or surpass state-of-the-art GTs on standard node classification benchmarks. This raises the fundamental question of whether the Transformer's global attention mechanism actually provides meaningful gains for GNNs, or whether it merely introduces topological noise and test error oscillations without improving generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and the graph adjacency matrix A, and produces two outputs: (1) enhanced node features X' generated by blending the original features with multi-channel guided noise via learned per-node weights a_i ∈ [0,1], and (2) final node class labels obtained by passing X' through a pluggable downstream GNN. The architecture is explicitly global-to-local: the Transformer-based filter bank operates first to generate guidance, and the GNN encodes second, which is the reverse of the common local-to-global or local-and-global cascade used by prior GTs.

## Method

G²Former reinterprets the Transformer's self-attention as a band-pass global-aware graph filter by constructing a learned symmetric adjacency matrix A_S from normalized sigmoid-projected query and key vectors, deriving its symmetric normalized Laplacian L̃_S, and applying the filter F(L̃_S; α, β, Θ) = Γ(α+β+2)(L̃_S)^α(2I−L̃_S)^β / (Γ(α+1)Γ(β+1)·2^{α+β+1}), where α and β tune the pass-band frequency. Multiple channels (full-spectrum or low-spectrum initialization) are instantiated by varying α and β across a bank of filters, and their outputs are summed to produce guided noise N. A graph-guided filtering step (adapted from image guided filtering) then blends N with the original features via learned weights: X'_i = a_i·N_i + (1−a_i)·X_i, where a_i is predicted by a separate head supervised by the training labels. The overall loss includes an additional cross-entropy term on the guided noise N itself, encouraging the attention to learn meaningful spectral structure rather than degenerating into a low-pass similarity kernel. Linear complexity O(|V|((α+β)d'² + dd') + |E|) is preserved by computing all filter applications in the linear-attention order (reordering matrix multiplications to avoid forming the full n×n attention matrix).

## Results

On 8 homophilous graphs, G2Former achieves the best or near-best accuracy across all datasets (e.g., 97.60% on Physics, 97.06% on Photo, 81.14% on WikiCS), improving the best-performing GNNs by 0.16%–0.36% and the best GTs by 0.28%–1.27%. On 6 heterophilous graphs, gains are substantially larger: G2Former outperforms the best GNNs by 0.51%–1.7% and the best GTs by 0.51%–6.88% (e.g., 99.45% ROC-AUC on Minesweeper vs. 97.79% for GCN; 93.03% on Roman-Empire vs. 91.23% for GCN). On 3 large-scale graphs (up to 2.4M nodes), G2Former improves the best GNNs by 0.2%–0.6% while maintaining linear-time training (e.g., 83.98% on ogbn-products, 86.81% on Pokec). The paper also reports the lowest generalization gap (train–test loss difference) across all evaluated datasets compared to SGFormer, Polynormer, and tuned GNNs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 8 Graph Transformer baselines (GraphGPS, NAGphormer, EXphormer, GOAT, NodeFormer, SGFormer, Polynormer, Spexphormer), G2Former achieves the highest accuracy on 6 of 8 homophilous datasets and all 6 heterophilous datasets, with the largest margin on Minesweeper (99.45% vs. 97.50% for Polynormer, +1.95%) and Roman-Empire (93.03% vs. 92.56% for Polynormer, +0.47%). Against the 3 well-tuned classical GNNs (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT), G2Former wins on 7 of 8 homophilous graphs (e.g., +0.38% on Physics, +0.43% on Photo) and all 6 heterophilous graphs (e.g., +1.66% on Minesweeper over GCN, +1.81% on Roman-Empire over GCN). Against the 6 heterophilous-specialized GNNs (H2GCN, FAGCN, CPGNN, GPRGNN, FSGNN, GloGNN), G2Former dominates by wide margins—up to +19.8% on Squirrel (45.53% vs. 25.73% best) and +9.3% on Roman-Empire (93.03% vs. 83.73% for FSGNN). Against the 4 spectral GNNs (BernNet, JacobiConv, OptBasisGNN, UniFilter), G2Former achieves the best accuracy on all 9 evaluated datasets (5 homophilous + 4 heterophilous), with gaps of 0.5–2.5% (e.g., 97.60% vs. 96.81% on Physics; 99.45% vs. 93.50% on Minesweeper). Notably, G2Former's advantage is most pronounced on heterophilous and large-scale graphs, where single-channel low-pass GNNs and GTs struggle to capture high-frequency structural signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

G2Former reinterprets the Transformer's self-attention mechanism through the lens of graph signal processing, building on the linear attention formulation (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) to achieve O(n) complexity and on guided image filtering (He et al., 2012) to define a graph-guided filtering kernel. It departs fundamentally from prior Graph Transformers (SGFormer, Polynormer, GraphGPS, etc.) by arguing that global attention does not augment GNN expressiveness but instead acts as a multi-channel band-pass regularization that suppresses test error oscillations. The spectral filtering perspective connects to the broader line of spectral GNNs (BernNet, JacobiConv, UniFilter), but uniquely makes the filter topology learnable via attention rather than fixed by the graph Laplacian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the core motivation of the Graph Transformer research line by showing that global attention modules largely fail to improve over well-tuned GNNs and can even introduce topological noise, reframing Transformers as data-augmentation/regularization components rather than expressiveness boosters. The multi-channel band-pass constraint provides a principled mechanism to suppress attention 'laziness' and stabilize generalization, with consistent (if modest) gains across 17 datasets. Open questions include whether this global-to-local framework extends to other graph tasks (link prediction, graph classification), whether the channel count T can be learned automatically, and whether the regularization benefit persists when the downstream GNN is already very strong. The paper explicitly acknowledges that G2Former 'falls short of surpassing the inherent performance ceiling imposed by GNNs,' limiting its practical impact to incremental generalization improvements.
