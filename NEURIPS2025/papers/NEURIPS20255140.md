## Problem

Existing membership inference attacks (MIAs) against vision-language models predominantly assume access to full-model logits, which are typically unavailable in deployed systems. This paper tackles the more realistic and restrictive label-only setting, where only the model's generated text output is observable, and asks whether pre-trained VLLMs remain vulnerable to membership inference under this constraint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255140/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image paired with its ground-truth caption as input and queries a VLLM (with a fixed max token length of 32) to produce a generated description. From this, it computes text-text semantic similarity (via a sentence embedding model) and/or image-text cosine similarity (via CLIP) between the generated description and the ground-truth caption or target image. The output is a continuous membership score (the negative of the regression-predicted perplexity) and a binary member/non-member decision based on a threshold τ.

## Method

LOMIA is a two-stage framework with three attack variants (TTFA, ITFA, DUFA) built on the insight that memorized training samples yield generated descriptions with higher semantic alignment and lower perplexity. In the regression stage, a surrogate VLLM is queried on known samples to fit a linear (or multivariate linear) relationship between similarity features and the perplexity of generated descriptions. In the inference stage, the target VLLM is queried on the test sample, the same similarity features are extracted, and the fitted regression model predicts the PPL, which is negated to form the membership score. TTFA uses only text-text similarity, ITFA uses only image-text (CLIP) similarity, and DUFA combines both in a multivariate regression, making it more robust when surrogate and target architectures differ or when non-member data are real images rather than synthetic ones.

## Results

On LOMIA/LAION with LLaVA-1.5-7B as target, DUFA achieves the best label-only AUC of 0.621, ITFA reaches 0.617, and TTFA reaches 0.601, all substantially outperforming the only prior label-only baseline (Query Attack, AUC 0.516). In TPR@1%FPR, TTFA achieves 3.0% on LLaVA versus 0.3% for Query Attack, approaching logits-based methods like Perplexity (3.0%) and Max0%Rényi(α=2) (10.0%). On the closed-source GPT-4o, ITFA attains the highest AUC of 0.669 on LOMIA/LAION, demonstrating that even proprietary VLLMs remain vulnerable to label-only membership inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the sole prior label-only baseline, Query Attack [18], all three LOMIA variants achieve large gains: TTFA improves AUC by 0.105 on LLaVA (0.601 vs. 0.516) and TPR@1%FPR from 0.3% to 3.0% on LOMIA/LAION. Compared to the PPL attack [4,54], DUFA (0.621) is within 0.004 AUC on LLaVA, making it nearly as effective as this logits-based method while requiring only top-1 outputs. Against the strongest logits-based baselines from Li et al. [27] (Max10%Rényi α=1, AUC 0.750; Max0%Rényi α=2, AUC 0.696) and Shi et al. [44] (Min10% Prob, AUC 0.738), LOMIA trails by 0.1–0.13 AUC, but these methods require full token-level logit access that is unavailable in deployed systems. On LOMIA/CC, the gap narrows: DUFA reaches AUC 0.630 versus 0.646 for Max100%Rényi(α=∞), and TTFA's TPR@1%FPR of 4.0% matches or exceeds several logits-based methods. On GPT-4o, ITFA (AUC 0.669) outperforms both TTFA (0.602) and DUFA (0.612) on LOMIA/LAION, suggesting cross-modal alignment is the most informative signal for closed-source models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the membership inference attack (MIA) literature from LLMs to VLLMs under a significantly more restrictive threat model. Prior MIA work on VLLMs (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024) assumes grey-box access to full token-level logits, while the only prior label-only approach (Hu et al., 2025) targets fine-tuned VLLMs and relies on shadow models trained on large similar datasets. LOMIA departs from both by operating on pre-trained VLLMs with only top-1 text outputs available, repurposing the perplexity-based signal from LLM MIA research [Carlini et al., Yeom et al.] through a linear regression that maps semantic similarity features to predicted perplexity, thereby eliminating the need for logits or shadow training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant: in deployed VLLM APIs (e.g., GPT-4o), adversaries typically receive only generated text, making label-only attacks the realistic threat model. The paper demonstrates that even closed-source models remain vulnerable (AUC 0.669 on GPT-4o), underscoring that current deployment practices do not mitigate this privacy risk. However, the absolute TPR@1%FPR values remain low (1–5%), and the paper acknowledges that effective defenses (differentially private pre-training, machine unlearning) for this specific label-only setting remain an open problem. Future work should investigate whether these attacks transfer to fine-tuned or instruction-tuned VLLMs and whether the regression-based PPL approximation holds under distributional shift.
