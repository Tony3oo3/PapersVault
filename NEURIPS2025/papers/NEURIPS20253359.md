## Problem

ML conferences lack an official, reputable mechanism for publishing refutations and critiques of previously accepted research. When peer review admits flawed, misleading, or incorrect work, the field has no structured way to correct the scientific record with equivalent prestige and visibility, forcing valid critiques into informal channels (blogs, social media) that lack peer review, archival permanence, and independent expert scrutiny.

## Input / Output

As a position paper, the input is the existing ML conference ecosystem and its peer review infrastructure; the output is a proposed R&C Track design with review principles, governance considerations, and an illustrative example submission. The example submission takes a published ICLR 2025 Oral (min-p sampling) as its subject and produces a multi-pronged critical re-analysis covering human evaluation statistics, NLP benchmark sweeps across 9 models and 31 temperatures, and LLM-as-a-Judge methodology audits.

## Method

The paper proposes a dedicated 'Refutations and Critiques' Track within major ML conferences with bespoke review criteria requiring submissions to be rigorous, substantive, constructive, and significant, and explicitly linking accepted critiques to the papers they address. The illustrative example demonstrates the track's operation by re-analyzing the min-p sampling paper's four lines of evidence: (1) re-examining human evaluation data with proper multiple-comparison corrections (Bonferroni, Intersection-Union Tests) revealing min-p is statistically indistinguishable from baselines in 11 of 12 comparisons, (2) conducting comprehensive hyperparameter sweeps on GSM8K and GPQA across 9 models showing min-p does not surpass baselines when controlling for hyperparameter count, (3) auditing LLM-as-a-Judge reporting for inconsistent score selection, and (4) verifying unsubstantiated community adoption claims. The authors argue a standalone track is necessary to reduce reviewer indifference to critical work, create tailored reviewing standards (e.g., inviting original authors to respond), and confer the same prestige as Main Track acceptances.

## Results

In the illustrative R&C submission critiquing the ICLR 2025 Oral on min-p sampling, re-analysis of the original human evaluation data via 12 one-sided paired t-tests (3 temperatures × 2 metrics × 2 baselines) shows that after Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons, min-p significantly outperforms baselines in only 1 of 12 comparisons at α=0.05 and 0 of 12 at α=0.01, contradicting the original claim of consistent superiority. A comprehensive NLP benchmark sweep across 9 models, 4 samplers, 31 temperatures, and 6 hyperparameters per sampler on GSM8K and GPQA similarly finds that min-p does not surpass basic, top-p, or top-k sampling when controlling for the number of hyperparameters. The authors' revised human evaluation (conducted in response to the critique) further confirms that min-p offers no apparent advantage over basic or top-p sampling in quality, diversity, or their trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The illustrative R&C submission compares min-p sampling against three established baselines: basic (temperature-only) sampling, top-p sampling, and top-k sampling. Across all four lines of evidence (human evaluations, NLP benchmarks, LLM-as-a-Judge, and community adoption), min-p fails to demonstrate consistent superiority over any baseline. In human evaluations, min-p is statistically indistinguishable from basic and top-p in the majority of settings after proper multiple-comparison correction; qualitative annotator preferences actually favor basic sampling over min-p. In the NLP benchmark sweep (GSM8K, GPQA) across 9 models and 31 temperatures, min-p does not surpass top-p or top-k when the number of tunable hyperparameters is held equal. The authors' own revised human evaluation, conducted after the critique was raised, further confirms that min-p's apparent advantages appear only under conditions yielding lower absolute quality and diversity scores than other achievable settings, offering no practical advantage for practitioners seeking maximal output quality or diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper extends the tradition of post-publication critique—well-established in journal comment/response systems (e.g., JMLR), the Journal of Comments and Replications in Economics, and the Data Ark initiative—into the ML conference ecosystem, where no equivalent formal mechanism exists. It builds on and explicitly complements existing ML reproducibility efforts such as the ICLR Reproducibility Challenge, ML Retrospectives, and TMLR's reproducibility certification, arguing that these focus too narrowly on reproducibility verification rather than broader scientific correction. The paper draws a structural analogy to the NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks Track (Vanschoren & Yeung, 2021) to justify carving out a dedicated track, and its illustrative example applies standard statistical re-analysis (paired t-tests with Bonferroni correction, Intersection-Union Tests) to the min-p sampling paper (Nguyen et al., 2024), a technique well-known in the statistics literature but underutilized in ML evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If adopted, an R&C Track would provide the first official, high-visibility mechanism for ML conferences to self-correct, potentially reducing the propagation of flawed results that misdirect subsequent research and waste community resources. The illustrative example—showing that a top-18 ICLR 2025 Oral's central claims fail under proper statistical analysis—concretely demonstrates the track's value and raises the uncomfortable question of how many other highly-rated papers contain similar analytical gaps. Key open questions include whether the track will attract sufficient submissions without becoming adversarial, how to ensure fairness when authors of critiqued papers participate in review, and whether major conferences will actually implement the proposal. The paper's reliance on community anecdotes and its not-yet-peer-reviewed example limit the strength of its evidence base, but the structural argument for a corrective mechanism is compelling given the documented cases of flawed work receiving prestigious recognition.
