## Problem

As LLM context lengths grow at inference time, standard attention becomes increasingly diffuse, with attention to the local context (e.g., the most recent 100 tokens) decaying rapidly while distant tokens receive disproportionate weight. Existing entropy-control methods like LogN/SSMax sharpen attention globally but still forfeit local context attention. The paper identifies two formal desiderata—scale-invariant total attention and scale-invariant attention sparsity—that effective long-context attention should satisfy, and shows that no existing mechanism meets both simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard transformer attention scores (query-key dot products) and the relative token position t (distance back from the query). It produces modified attention logits via a position-dependent affine transform L_t = a_t·L̄_t + m_t, where both the multiplicative scale a_t and additive bias m_t vary with t. Unlike LogN, which applies a single position-independent multiplier s·log N to all logits, this transformation has distinct per-position parameters, with a_t growing as √log(t) and m_t decreasing as −log(t). The final output remains standard next-token prediction, but the attention distribution is guaranteed to allocate roughly equal total mass to each logarithmic token range (1–10, 10–100, 100–1000, etc.).

## Method

Under the assumption that base attention logits are marginally Gaussian, the paper derives a closed-form position-dependent affine transformation of the logits that provably satisfies scale-invariant total attention (expected total unnormalized attention in each [t, tΔ) range is Θ(1)) and scale-invariant unnormalized attention sparsity. The transformation is L_t = a_t·L̄_t + m_t with a_t² = 2[log(t/τ+1) − log α + β/α] and m_t = −a_t² + β/α, where τ is a lengthscale hyperparameter. Boundary conditions (a_0²=1, m_0=0) fix α=β=e^{0.5}, reducing the free parameters to just τ (empirically τ=10). The method is paired with p-RoPE, which excludes low-frequency RoPE components that would otherwise interfere with the position-dependent scaling. Theorem 1 proves the Gaussian-logit construction satisfies both scale-invariant properties, and empirical analysis shows the resulting entropy in each range scales as ~√log(t), satisfying weak but not strong scale-invariant sparsity. Experiments on 162M and 304M GPT-2-style models trained on FineWeb demonstrate that scale-invariant p-RoPE achieves the lowest validation loss across all training lengths and enables zero-shot generalization up to 16× beyond the training context, while matching LogN+p-RoPE on needle-in-a-haystack retrieval at 64k.

## Results

On a 162M GPT-2-style model trained at 4k context length, scale-invariant p-RoPE achieves validation losses of 3.244 (4k), 3.235 (16k), and 3.247 (64k), demonstrating near-perfect zero-shot length generalization with only a 0.003 loss increase from 4k to 64k. In contrast, standard RoPE degrades from 3.261 to 5.260 (a 61% increase) over the same range. On the needle-in-a-haystack retrieval task, scale-invariant p-RoPE achieves 96.9% accuracy at 64k context, matching LogN+p-RoPE (93.9%) while all other baselines drop to near-zero accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scale-invariant p-RoPE substantially outperforms all positional-encoding-only baselines (RoPE, p-RoPE, RoPE+NTK, YaRN) at 64k context, achieving 3.247 vs. losses of 5.26–5.74 (a 60–77% reduction in loss). Against the closest competitors that also control attention entropy, it beats LogN+p-RoPE (3.317) and ALiBi (3.270) at 64k, with the gap over LogN+p-RoPE being most meaningful (0.07 lower loss). On the needle-in-a-haystack retrieval task, scale-invariant p-RoPE (96.9% at 64k) slightly edges out LogN+p-RoPE (93.9%) and both saturate the task, while RoPE, p-RoPE, RoPE+NTK, YaRN, and ALiBi all collapse to 0–2% accuracy at 64k. In the Infini-attention comparison (Table 3), scale-invariant p-RoPE (3.247) outperforms both Infini-RoPE (3.310) and Infini-p-RoPE (3.311) at 64k. The method also maintains its advantage at the 304M scale (Fig. 4) and in continual pretraining of Llama-2 7B (Table 4), where it achieves 2.252 loss at 64k versus 2.744–8.815 for all other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two main lines of work: (1) positional encoding methods for length extrapolation (RoPE, ALiBi, NTK-aware scaling, YaRN, p-RoPE), and (2) entropy-control approaches like LogN/SSMax that multiply all attention logits by s·log N to prevent attention from becoming diffuse at long contexts. The key departure is that LogN applies a position-independent multiplicative bias, whereas this paper derives a position-dependent transformation L_t → a_t·L_t + m_t where both the scale a_t and bias m_t vary with the token's distance from the query. This is motivated by formalizing two desiderata—scale-invariant total attention and scale-invariant attention sparsity—borrowed from the scale-invariance statistics of natural images, which prior entropy-control methods do not explicitly target. The method is specifically paired with p-RoPE (which excludes low-frequency RoPE components), as standard RoPE's low-frequency components were found to interfere with the position-dependent transform.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a simple position-dependent linear transform on attention logits enables near-flat validation loss when zero-shot generalizing from 4k to 64k context (16×), avoiding the expensive long-context pretraining that most current LLMs require. The theoretical grounding (Gaussian logit assumption, provable scale-invariance of total attention and unnormalised sparsity) is clean and provides a principled design space rather than a heuristic. Key limitations include evaluation only at 162M–304M parameters from scratch (plus 7B continual pretraining), the Gaussian assumption on logits, and restriction to dense attention. Open questions include whether the approach extends to sparse/efficient attention mechanisms, whether it holds at multi-billion-parameter scale trained from scratch, and whether the p-RoPE requirement (excluding low-frequency components) generalizes or is an artifact of the small-model regime.
