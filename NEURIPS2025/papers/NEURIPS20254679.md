## Problem

Creating 3D human avatars that support both photorealistic rendering and physically accurate animation of loose garments remains unsolved. Existing learning-based methods (piecewise linear transforms, pose-dependent correctives) overfit to training motions and fail on novel inputs, while the only prior physics-based avatar (PhysAvatar) relies on C-IPC simulation that breaks down when SMPL-X body meshes contain small self-penetrations, requiring manual collider adjustments and yielding only 37.6% simulation success rate.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (24 frames for physics learning and 200 for appearance on ActorsHQ; 11 and 100 on 4D-DRESS) along with a canonical mesh from the first frame. It produces a hybrid avatar — a triangular mesh carrying per-garment physical parameters (Young's modulus E, density ρ, rest-geometry scalar α) plus 3D Gaussian Splats attached to mesh faces — that can be re-animated with arbitrary SMPL-X body parameters and rendered from free viewpoints. The physical parameters are not learned end-to-end but recovered via a separate finite-difference inverse-physics stage before appearance optimization begins.

## Method

MPMAvatar represents the avatar as a mesh with physical parameters for dynamics and 3D Gaussian Splats (in local face coordinates) for appearance, then animates non-garment regions via Linear Blend Skinning while driving garments with a tailored Material Point Method simulator. Two key modifications make MPM effective for garments: (1) an anisotropic constitutive model (Jiang et al. 2017) that decomposes strain energy into normal, shear, and in-plane terms using Lagrangian mesh material directions, capturing the codimensional stiffness of cloth; and (2) a mesh-based collision handler that transfers collider face velocities and normals to nearby Eulerian grid nodes via B-spline weights and projects relative velocities onto the tangent plane, reducing collision-check complexity from O(N_grid³) to O(N_f). Physical parameters (E, ρ, α) are optimized by finite-difference inverse physics minimizing vertex L2 loss against tracked meshes, where α compensates for gravity-induced deformation in the canonical geometry. Appearance is learned by optimizing shared Gaussian Splats across all frames and views with a combined L1+SSIM+LPIPS photometric loss plus position/scale regularization, and quasi-shadowing via an ambient-occlusion neural network modulates per-Gaussian color for self-shadowing.

## Results

On ActorsHQ, MPMAvatar achieves the best scores across all geometry and appearance metrics: CD of 0.42×10³, F-Score of 95.7, LPIPS of 0.033, PSNR of 32.0 dB, and SSIM of 0.963. On 4D-DRESS, it similarly leads with CD 0.33×10³, F-Score 97.2, LPIPS 0.018, PSNR 34.1 dB, and SSIM 0.977. In terms of robustness, MPMAvatar achieves a 100% simulation success rate at 1.1 s/frame, compared to PhysAvatar's 37.6% success rate at 170.0 s/frame — a 155× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary physics-based baseline PhysAvatar [78], MPMAvatar wins on every metric on both datasets: on ActorsHQ it reduces CD from 0.55 to 0.42 (−24%), improves F-Score from 92.9 to 95.7, and gains 1.8 dB in PSNR (30.2→32.0); on 4D-DRESS the margins are smaller but consistent (CD 0.37→0.33, PSNR 33.2→34.1). Against non-physics baselines on ActorsHQ, MPMAvatar outperforms GS-Avatar [12] by 0.23 in CD and 1.4 dB in PSNR, TAVA [32] by 0.24 in CD and 2.4 dB in PSNR, and ARAH [65] by 0.70 in CD and 3.4 dB in PSNR. Against the concurrent learning-based method Gaussian Garments [55], MPMAvatar achieves a dramatically lower CD (0.42 vs. 2.39) and higher PSNR (32.0 vs. 29.5), while against MMLPHuman [74] it leads in CD (0.42 vs. 0.47), LPIPS (0.033 vs. 0.039), and PSNR (32.0 vs. 29.3). The most striking gap is in simulation robustness: PhysAvatar fails on 62.4% of frames without manual collider adjustment, whereas MPMAvatar never fails, and runs 155× faster per frame (1.1 s vs. 170.0 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PhysAvatar (Zheng et al., ECCV 2024), the current state-of-the-art physics-based avatar, by replacing its C-IPC cloth simulator with a tailored Material Point Method (MPM) pipeline. The key departures are: (1) adopting the anisotropic constitutive model of Jiang et al. (2017) to capture the codimensional, direction-dependent mechanics of garments, and (2) introducing a mesh-based collision handling algorithm that transfers collider velocities/normals via B-spline weights, replacing MPM's original level-set-based collision scheme. For rendering, it follows the 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm (Kerbl et al., 2023) with mesh-attached Gaussians (Qian et al., 2024) and quasi-shadowing (Bagautdinov et al., 2021), moving away from PhysAvatar's mesh-based rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in physics-based avatar creation: C-IPC's failure on noisy SMPL-X body meshes (37.6% success rate) and its 170 s/frame simulation cost. By achieving 100% robustness and a 155× speedup while improving all geometry and appearance metrics, MPMAvatar makes physics-based avatar animation substantially more tractable for production pipelines. The zero-shot generalization to unseen scene interactions (chairs, sand) is the first empirical demonstration that a physics-based avatar can handle novel external objects without retraining, a capability learning-based simulators lack. Open questions include scalability of the finite-difference parameter optimization to per-vertex material maps, the absence of relighting support, and whether differentiable MPM could replace the current gradient-approximation scheme.
