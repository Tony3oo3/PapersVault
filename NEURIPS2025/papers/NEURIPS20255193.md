## Problem

Using LLMs for zero-shot node classification on text-attributed graphs faces two intertwined challenges: individual node texts provide limited structural context to the LLM, and the resulting pseudo-labels are unreliable due to hallucination and information insufficiency. Prior approaches either isolate text from graph topology or produce noisy node-level labels that degrade downstream GNN training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255193/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text-attributed graph (node text descriptions plus edge topology) and an LLM backbone (default GPT-4o). It produces zero-shot node class labels by training a GNN (GCN for homophilic graphs, GloGNN for heterophilic graphs) supervised by bundle-level pseudo-labels. The LLM is queried 100 times with bundles of 5 concatenated texts each, and the GNN is trained for 500 epochs with bundle refinement at epochs 300 and 400, evicting one low-confidence node per refinement step.

## Method

DENSE (Dynamic text bundling supervision) replaces individual node queries with bundle-level queries and supervision. First, bundles of 5 nodes are sampled via topological proximity (k-hop neighborhood with adaptive hop size) for homophilic graphs or semantic proximity (top-5 nearest neighbors in embedding space) for heterophilic graphs. Second, the concatenated texts in each bundle are prompted to the LLM to predict the mode category, yielding a bundle label. Third, a GNN is trained with two losses: an entropy-based cross-entropy on the softmax of averaged bundle logits, and a ranking-based loss that penalizes bundles where the predicted label is not the top class. Fourth, during training, the least-confident node in each bundle is evicted to reduce noise. Theoretically, the paper proves bundle supervision imposes a smaller gradient penalty on outlier nodes than individual supervision (Theorem 3.1) and that gradient descent converges to a stationary point under bounded smoothness conditions (Theorem 3.3).

## Results

DENSE achieves the best zero-shot node classification accuracy across all 10 text-attributed graph datasets, ranging from 31.75% on Children to 92.51% on Texas. The method outperforms the strongest baseline (LLM-BP) by 1.5–14.0 percentage points, with the largest gains on Sportsfit (+13.96) and Texas (+10.85). On homophilic citation/knowledge graphs (Cora, CiteSeer, WikiCS), DENSE reaches 75.09%, 72.37%, and 71.03% respectively, surpassing text-encoder baselines (SBERT, RoBERTa, TE-3-Large, LLM2Vec) and generative LLM baselines (GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4o) by 3–12 points. On heterophilic web-page graphs (Cornell, Texas, Wisconsin, Washington), DENSE achieves 84.82%, 92.51%, 87.17%, and 81.66%, substantially outperforming graph foundation models (GOFA, ZeroG, UniGLM) that degrade significantly out of their training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DENSE wins on all 10 datasets against all 15 baselines. The closest competitor is LLM-BP [77], which also uses LLM-generated pseudo-labels to supervise a GNN but queries individual nodes rather than bundles; DENSE outperforms it by 2.5–14.0 points, with the largest gap on Sportsfit (75.88 vs. 61.92) and Texas (92.51 vs. 81.66). Text-encoder baselines (SBERT, RoBERTa, TE-3-Large, LLM2Vec) ignore graph topology and trail DENSE by 3–12 points on homophilic graphs and by 3–26 points on heterophilic web-page graphs. Generative LLM baselines (GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4o) used for direct node classification are outperformed by 5–39 points, confirming that bundle-level querying plus GNN supervision is far more effective than single-node LLM classification. Graph foundation models (GOFA, ZeroG, UniGLM, OFA) show inconsistent performance, degrading severely on out-of-distribution heterophilic graphs (e.g., GOFA drops to 31.02% on Washington vs. DENSE's 81.66%), while graph self-supervised methods (DGI, GraphMAE) and LLM-graph hybrids (GraphGPT, LLAGA) remain far below competitive accuracy without LLM assistance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of querying LLMs with individual node texts to obtain node-level pseudo-labels (as in LLM-BP, ZeroG, and related work) by introducing a bundle-level abstraction: grouping topologically or semantically proximate nodes and asking the LLM for the mode category of the bundle rather than per-node labels. It also contrasts with graph foundation model approaches (GOFA, UniGLM, OFA) that attempt to encode graph topology into LLM token sequences, which the authors argue incur information loss and require large training data. The bundle supervision design (entropy-based + ranking-based losses) and dynamic bundle refinement are new loss mechanisms not present in prior LLM-to-GNN supervision pipelines, and the theoretical analysis of outlier tolerance and convergence is novel for this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Zero-shot node classification on text-attributed graphs is practically important because full annotation of large-scale networks (citation, social, e-commerce) is prohibitively expensive, and the bundling strategy offers a simple, architecture-agnostic way to extract more reliable supervision from LLMs without retraining them. The paper's limitation is that it requires textual node attributes and depends on the quality of the LLM backbone, and the heuristic refinement schedule (evicting one node at epochs 300 and 400) lacks a principled stopping criterion. Open questions include whether the approach scales to graphs with millions of nodes, how it performs when text attributes are sparse or noisy, and whether the bundling idea transfers to other LLM-assisted graph tasks beyond classification.
