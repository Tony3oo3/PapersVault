## Problem

Standard RL paradigms cannot simultaneously handle temporally ordered goal sequences and non-stationary safety constraints in complex continuous environments. Existing LTL-based RL methods rely on sparse binary rewards and ad-hoc safety mechanisms (rollout termination, reward shaping) that fail to scale, while scalable GCRL and Safe RL methods lack support for most LTL task specifications. No prior method robustly supports both stage-wise goal progression and arbitrary safety constraints in high-dimensional settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes an STL task specification (restricted to the recurrence class, translatable to a Deterministic Büchi Automaton) and a continuous environment state as input. The STL formula is compiled via the SPOT library into a DBA, from which per-automaton-state safety propositions and subgoal lists are extracted. The output is a single goal-conditioned policy mapping augmented states (environment state × subgoal list × automaton state) to actions, with a separate learned safety function gating which actions are admissible. The safety limit is normalized to [-1,1], allowing a task-agnostic default of L=0.

## Method

ACQL constructs an augmented product CMDP whose state space is S × G⁺ × Q (environment state, subgoal list, automaton state), enabling Hindsight Experience Replay to densify the otherwise sparse task-completion reward by retroactively relabeling trajectories with achieved subgoals. For safety, instead of the standard discounted sum-of-costs CMDP constraint, ACQL uses a minimum-safety formulation inspired by Hamilton-Jacobi reachability: the constraint function c^A takes negative values on safety violations, and the policy is constrained to keep the expected minimum of c^A above a threshold, reducing safety to a classification problem that avoids long-horizon cost regression. The algorithm learns two Q-networks (Q^r for reward, Q^c for safety) via a three-timescale stochastic approximation scheme with a safety discount γ_c that asymptotically approaches 1, and the policy selects the maximum-reward action among those whose predicted minimum safety exceeds the limit. A convergence proposition guarantees asymptotic optimality under finite MDP assumptions with Robbins-Monro step sizes on three nested timescales.

## Results

Across 15 environment-task combinations (3 agents × 5 LTL task types), ACQL achieves substantially higher reward than both baselines in nearly all settings, e.g., 858.7±3.2 reward and 100% success rate on the PointMass safety task (◇g₁ ∧ □¬o₁) where both LOF and CRM-RS score 0.0 reward. On the most complex looping task (□◇(g₁∧○◇g₂)∧□¬o₁), ACQL is the only method achieving non-zero success (62.5–82.5% across environments), while both baselines fail completely. CRM-RS fails on almost all tasks (0.0 reward in 12 of 15 settings), and LOF, while achieving high success rates on simple goal-reaching tasks, drops to 0% success on all safety-constrained and looping tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACQL is compared against two baselines for online RL with single LTL specifications: CRM-RS (Reward Machines with reward shaping and rollout termination for safety) and LOF (Logical Options Framework with hierarchical per-subgoal policies and reward-penalty safety). ACQL dominates CRM-RS on virtually every metric: CRM-RS achieves 0.0 reward in 12 of 15 settings and near-zero success rates, while ACQL maintains high reward (typically 200–860) and success rates above 60% in all settings. Against LOF, ACQL wins decisively on all safety-constrained and looping tasks (Tasks 3–5) where LOF scores 0.0 reward and 0% success, and also achieves substantially higher reward on goal-reaching tasks (e.g., 829.6 vs. 11.0 on PointMass sequential, 813.5 vs. 32.0 on Quadcopter sequential). The one area where LOF matches or slightly exceeds ACQL is success rate on the simplest goal-reaching tasks without safety (e.g., 98.8% vs. 83.8% on PointMass sequential), but ACQL compensates with far higher reward by remaining near the final goal after task completion. Overall, ACQL is the only method that reliably satisfies both liveness and non-stationary safety constraints simultaneously across all tested environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) LTL/STL-guided RL via automata and reward machines (Toro Icarte et al., Camacho et al., Hasanbeig et al.), which suffers from sparse rewards and ad-hoc safety enforcement; (2) goal-conditioned RL with HER (Andrychowicz et al., Schaul et al.), which densifies reward signals but has not been integrated into the LTL product-MDP setting; and (3) safe RL / constrained MDPs (Achiam et al., Kalweit et al.) and Hamilton-Jacobi reachability (Fisac et al., Hsu et al.), which handle stationary safety but not the non-stationary constraints induced by LTL. ACQL's key departure is encoding subgoals directly into the product CMDP state to enable HER, and replacing the standard discounted sum-of-costs constraint with a minimum-safety formulation inspired by HJ reachability, thereby unifying GCRL and Safe RL within a single LTL-satisfying algorithm rather than decomposing the task hierarchically as in LOF (Araki et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACQL addresses a practical gap: no prior method simultaneously handles temporally ordered subgoals and non-stationary safety constraints in continuous, high-dimensional environments at scale. The minimum-safety CMDP formulation is a clean reformulation that eliminates manual tuning of the violation threshold and converts a regression problem into a classification one, which may be broadly applicable beyond LTL tasks. Limitations include restriction to the recurrence class (DBAs), conditioning only on current (not future) subgoals, and a narrow sim-to-real validation (single UR5e task with perfectly aligned geometry). Open questions include extending to more expressive automata (GfMDPs), handling partial observability, and whether the minimum-safety formulation generalizes to stochastic or partially known dynamics.
