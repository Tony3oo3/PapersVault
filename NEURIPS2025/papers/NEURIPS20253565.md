## Problem

Existing neural compilers (TVM, MetaSchedule) navigate the exponentially large space of program transformations via stochastic search (evolutionary search, MCMC), which is sample-inefficient and blind to the non-local, compositional dependencies between successive transformations. The paper asks whether LLM reasoning—used zero-shot with no retraining—can exploit the structural context of compilation decisions to dramatically improve sample efficiency in compiler optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253565/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a neural network layer as a TVM IRModule, a target hardware platform, and a finite catalog of legal transformations (TileSize, Parallel, ComputeLocation, Unroll). At each MCTS expansion, the LLM receives a structured prompt containing the current program's source code, its parent and grandparent programs with their ordered transformation traces and predicted cost-model scores, and the full transformation set. The output is a validated transformation sequence applied to the original program, producing an optimized code variant whose speedup is measured against the unoptimized baseline on real hardware.

## Method

The REASONING COMPILER casts compiler optimization as a finite-horizon MDP and navigates it with Monte Carlo tree search, where an LLM (GPT-4o mini by default) acts as a context-aware proposal engine during the expansion phase. At each expansion, the LLM is prompted with hierarchical context—current, parent, and grandparent programs with their transformation histories and cost-model scores—and instructed via chain-of-thought reasoning to analyze performance trends, identify synergistic or antagonistic transformation interactions, and propose a justified next transformation. MCTS balances exploration and exploitation through UCT selection, scores new nodes with a learned hardware cost model to avoid expensive real-hardware runs in the inner loop, and backpropagates rewards to refine future selections. The LLM operates zero-shot with no fine-tuning; invalid proposals are parsed and filtered, with a fallback to random sampling when all LLM outputs fail validation. This structured, history-aware search achieves a 5.0× average speedup with 5.8× fewer samples than TVM's Evolutionary Search, yielding a 10.8× sample-efficiency gain across five hardware platforms.

## Results

Across five layer-wise benchmarks (Llama-3-8B Attention, DeepSeek-R1 MoE, FLUX Attention, FLUX Convolution, Llama-4-Scout MLP) on five hardware platforms, the REASONING COMPILER achieves a geometric-mean 5.0× speedup using 5.8× fewer samples than TVM with Evolutionary Search, yielding a 10.8× sample-efficiency gain. For end-to-end Llama-3-8B, it achieves a 4.0× speedup with 3.9× fewer samples (5.6× efficiency improvement). The most dramatic case is the Llama-3-8B Attention Layer on AMD EPYC 7R13, where the REASONING COMPILER reaches 12.1× speedup in just 200 samples versus TVM's 2.1× in 1,400 samples (40.3× efficiency gain).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is TVM MetaSchedule with Evolutionary Search [Shao et al., NeurIPS 2022], which the REASONING COMPILER is implemented as an extension of. The REASONING COMPILER wins on every one of the 25 platform–operator pairs in Table 1, achieving both higher speedup and lower sample counts simultaneously. The geometric-mean speedup improves from 2.7× (TVM) to 5.0× (REASONING COMPILER) while sample count drops by a factor of 5.8, for a net 10.8× sample-efficiency gain. The largest individual wins occur on AMD EPYC 7R13 (up to 40.3× efficiency gain on Llama-3-8B Attention) and Intel Core i9 (26.1× on Llama-4-Scout MLP). For end-to-end Llama-3-8B (Table 2), the REASONING COMPILER again dominates across all five platforms, with efficiency gains ranging from 3.2× (AMD EPYC) to 11.8× (Intel Core i9). An internal ablation (Table 3) also shows that plain MCTS without LLM guidance is consistently outperformed by the full REASONING COMPILER, confirming that the LLM's contextual reasoning—not just the tree-search structure—is responsible for the sample-efficiency gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural compilation lineage of TVM/Ansor and MetaSchedule, which use learned cost models combined with stochastic search (evolutionary search, simulated annealing) to navigate the space of loop tiling, fusion, and layout transformations. It departs from this lineage by replacing the stochastic proposal mechanism with an LLM that performs chain-of-thought reasoning over transformation history, program structure, and performance feedback, embedded within a Monte Carlo tree search framework. The conceptual departure is significant: prior neural compilers treat each transformation as an independent or locally-correlated decision, whereas this paper explicitly models the non-local, compositional dependencies between transformations through the LLM's contextual reasoning, drawing on the broader LLM-for-code literature but applying it to a structured planning loop rather than standalone code generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.8× geometric-mean sample efficiency improvement over TVM's Evolutionary Search, achieved with zero LLM retraining, has direct practical implications for reducing the cost and time of model deployment across heterogeneous hardware. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full-model compilation beyond individual layers, how it performs on GPU/accelerator targets (all evaluations are CPU-only), and whether the gains persist as the transformation space grows with more complex operator fusion patterns. The dependence on external LLM APIs introduces cost and reproducibility concerns, though the ablation showing competitive performance with open-source 7B/8B models partially mitigates this. A key open question is whether the LLM's contextual reasoning provides genuine structural insight or merely acts as a smarter random proposal, which the paper's ablations (historical trace depth, branching factor) only partially address.
