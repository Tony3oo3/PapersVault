## Problem

Existing equivariant diffusion policy frameworks achieve strong data efficiency but rely on point cloud inputs from multiple fixed depth cameras, making them incompatible with the now-standard eye-in-hand monocular RGB setup (e.g., a wrist-mounted GoPro). This paper asks whether full SO(3)-equivariance can be achieved directly from a single RGB image without explicit 3D reconstruction, enabling symmetry-aware policy learning in the most common and practical robotic sensing configuration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250201/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a sequence of eye-in-hand RGB images (84×84 in simulation; 224×224 in the real world, center-cropped from a 720×720 GoPro Max Lens Mod capture) together with a 7-dimensional proprioceptive vector encoding the end-effector's 6D pose and gripper state. It outputs a 16-step action chunk where each action is a 10-dimensional absolute world-frame end-effector pose (3D position, 6D rotation vector, 1D gripper openness); only the first 8 steps are executed per control cycle. The observation window spans 2 history steps for the SO(3) variant and 1 step for the SO(2) variant.

## Method

ISP lifts 2D image features onto the unit sphere via a learnable orthographic projection, then applies an equivariance correction that left-multiplies the spherical-harmonic coefficients by the Wigner D-matrix of the gripper orientation, aligning the camera-frame signal to a world-aligned frame and restoring global SO(3)-equivariance (Proposition 1) while simultaneously yielding local SO(2) invariance to in-plane camera rotation (Proposition 2). The corrected spherical signal is processed by S²→SO(3) and SO(3)→SO(3) spherical convolution layers to produce Fourier-coefficient conditioning vectors, which are fused with equivariantly encoded proprioception. The diffusion denoiser enforces end-to-end equivariance by sampling a finite subgroup of SO(3) (icosahedral I₆₀ or cyclic C₈) and applying a shared 1D temporal U-Net pointwise across group elements, so that the same parameters act on every sampled rotation. Training uses standard DDPM behavior cloning with SO(2) data augmentation (joint rotation of gripper and scene in proprioception and actions), and the full pipeline is end-to-end differentiable and SO(3)-equivariant from raw RGB to predicted action chunk.

## Results

On the MimicGen simulation benchmark (12 tasks), ISP-SO(3) achieves a mean success rate of 65.2% with 100 demonstrations and 75.0% with 200 demonstrations, outperforming the best baseline (Diffusion Policy at 53.6%/64.1%) by +11.6% and +10.5% respectively. Notably, ISP trained with only 100 demos surpasses all baselines trained with 200 demos. On four real-world tasks, ISP-SO(3) achieves an average 42.5% improvement over Diffusion Policy, with the largest gains on Box-Pipe Disassembly (80% vs. 10%) and 3D-Pipe Disassembly (75% vs. 15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISP-SO(3) consistently outperforms all three baselines across 21 of 24 simulation task settings. Against Diffusion Policy (the primary non-equivariant baseline), ISP gains +11.6% mean success rate with 100 demos and +10.5% with 200 demos, with the largest per-task margins on Square D2 (+23% at 100 demos) and Pick Place D0 (+6% at 100 demos). Against EquiDiff (the closest equivariant baseline, adapted for eye-in-hand by replacing its image encoder with ResNet), ISP gains +12.2% at 100 demos, showing that spherical projection from monocular RGB captures SO(3) structure that EquiDiff's proprioception-only equivariance cannot. Against ACT (a transformer-based behavior cloning method), the gap is largest at +42.2% (100 demos), confirming that equivariant inductive biases are far more effective than attention-based sequence modeling for data-limited manipulation. In the real world, ISP-SO(3) dramatically outperforms Diffusion Policy on tasks requiring 3D geometric reasoning (3D-Pipe: 75% vs. 15%) and fine visual discrimination (Box-Pipe: 80% vs. 10%), while maintaining competitive performance on the long-horizon U-Pipe task (85% vs. 65%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivariant diffusion policy line (EquiDiff, EquiBot, ETSEED) from point-cloud/multi-camera inputs to monocular eye-in-hand RGB by introducing a spherical projection of 2D image features followed by an equivariance correction using the gripper orientation. The spherical lifting technique is borrowed from prior pose-estimation work (Klee et al., Howell et al.), but its combination with a diffusion policy and the formal equivariance-correction mechanism (Proposition 1) are new. The key conceptual departure from prior equivariant policies is that SO(3) symmetry is recovered without any 3D reconstruction: the camera-frame invariance of the projected signal is explicitly compensated by left-multiplying with the end-effector rotation, yielding a world-aligned equivariant representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eye-in-hand RGB is the dominant sensing modality in large-scale robot datasets (RT-1, DROID, Open X-Embodiment), yet prior equivariant policies were incompatible with it; ISP closes this gap with a plug-and-play module that could be integrated into other diffusion or flow-matching policies. The demonstrated data efficiency (100-demo ISP outperforming 200-demo baselines) and large real-world gaps (e.g., 80% vs. 10% on Box-Pipe) suggest equivariance is a practical inductive bias, not just a theoretical nicety, for manipulation. Open questions include extending to translational equivariance, combining with agent-view cameras, scaling to bimanual systems, and reducing the 2.6–5.4× training overhead of equivariant layers.
