## Problem

Jointly adaptive federated optimization (using adaptive updates on both the server and client sides) accelerates convergence and improves accuracy over non-adaptive or server-only adaptive baselines, but its practical deployment in cross-device settings is hindered by two bottlenecks: transmitting global preconditioners from server to clients at every round inflates communication cost, and maintaining local preconditioners on each device imposes significant memory overhead. No prior method retains the full benefits of joint adaptivity while matching the communication complexity of vanilla FedAvg and keeping client memory near the model size.

## Input / Output

Each participating client receives only the current global model parameters x_t (no preconditioners or optimizer states) and draws stochastic gradients from its private local data distribution D_i over K local epochs. The server aggregates the resulting pseudogradients Δ_i^t = x_{i,K}^t − x_{t−1} and applies its own adaptive update (e.g., AdaGrad or Adam with momentum). Communication per round is exactly 2d bits (model up, model down), identical to FedAvg. In the FedAda²++ variant, client-side SM3 compresses second-moment statistics to parameter-group granularity, reducing extra on-device memory from 2d to approximately d (a 99% reduction in the additional memory for a ViT-S model).

## Method

FedAda² eliminates the server-to-client preconditioner transmission by uniformly initializing each client's local preconditioners to zero at the start of every communication round, so the server and client maintain independent gradient-statistic estimates and can even use different adaptive optimizers (e.g., Adam on the server, AdaGrad on the client). FedAda²++ further layers on memory-efficient local optimizers—specifically SM3 adaptations of Adam and AdaGrad—that factorize gradient statistics into low-rank, parameter-group-level approximations, cutting client memory to roughly the model size. The paper proves that under L-smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions, both variants achieve an O(T^{−1/2}) convergence rate for general non-convex objectives, matching the state-of-the-art for federated non-convex optimization, and extends the analysis to delayed preconditioner updates and a broader 'federated blended optimization' framework where per-client optimizer choices can vary dynamically. Empirically, on CIFAR-100, GLD-23K, FEMNIST, and StackOverflow (including a differentially private setting), FedAda²/FedAda²++ match or exceed the accuracy of costly joint-adaptivity baselines that transmit full preconditioners, while using strictly fewer communicated bits and less client memory.

## Results

FedAda² and FedAda²++ achieve the same O(T^{-1/2}) convergence rate as state-of-the-art federated non-convex optimization methods while matching the communication complexity of vanilla FedAvg (2d per round vs. 3d for costly joint adaptivity and 5d for MIME). With SM3 memory-efficient preconditioning, FedAda²++ requires only 0.48% additional client memory compared to a full 1× increase for standard joint adaptivity, representing a 99% reduction in extra on-device memory. Empirically, FedAda²/FedAda²++ match or slightly outperform costly joint adaptivity on CIFAR-100, GLD-23K, and FEMNIST (ViT-S models), and outperform all baselines by a large margin under differential privacy (σ=1, (ε,δ)=(13.1, 0.0025)) on StackOverflow.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), FedAda²/FedAda²++ consistently achieve higher test accuracy and lower test loss across all four datasets (CIFAR-100, GLD-23K, FEMNIST, StackOverflow), with the gap most pronounced on FEMNIST where client heterogeneity is highest. Against server-only adaptive baselines FedAdam and FedAdaGrad (Reddi et al., 2021), FedAda²/FedAda²++ show slightly faster convergence and higher final accuracy, confirming the benefit of joint (server + client) adaptivity over server-only adaptivity. Against Costly Joint Adaptivity (which transmits global preconditioners to clients each round), FedAda² matches or marginally outperforms in accuracy while reducing communication from 3d to 2d per round and eliminating the need to store full global preconditioners on-device. Against MIME and MIMELite (Karimireddy et al., 2021; Ro et al., 2022), FedAda²/FedAda²++ use substantially less communication (2d vs. 5d/4d) and less client memory (~d vs. 4d/3d) while still achieving joint adaptivity, making them far more practical for cross-device deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive federated optimization line initiated by FedAdam/FedAdaGrad (Reddi et al., 2021) and jointly adaptive frameworks like Local Adaptive FedOPT (Wang et al., 2021) and MIME (Karimireddy et al., 2021), which all require transmitting server-side preconditioners to clients at every round. The key departure is the observation that this transmission is unnecessary: simply initializing local preconditioners to zero at each round (FedAda²) preserves convergence while matching FedAvg's communication complexity exactly. FedAda²++ further integrates memory-efficient optimizers (SM3) from the centralized optimization literature (Anil et al., 2019) into the federated client side, a combination not previously explored with convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical deployment of jointly adaptive federated learning in cross-device settings has been bottlenecked by the communication cost of preconditioner transmission and the memory overhead of client-side optimizer states; this paper shows both can be eliminated with minimal performance loss, making joint adaptivity viable at scale. The convergence rate O(T^{-1/2}) matches state-of-the-art federated non-convex methods, and the heavy-tailed noise analysis (Appendix A) provides a theoretical justification for why client-side adaptivity is essential for transformer-based models. Key limitations include the deterministic (full-batch) analysis setting and the restriction to a single client sampling strategy per round; extending to stochastic regimes and exploring heterogeneous client optimizer mixtures via the proposed 'blended optimization' framework are natural next steps.
