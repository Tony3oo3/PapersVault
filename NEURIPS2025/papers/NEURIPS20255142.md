## Problem

Data attribution methods for LLMs lack a systematic, application-driven evaluation framework: existing protocols are either prohibitively expensive (leave-one-out retraining) or suffer from confounding design choices such as lexical overlap in factual tracing and the absence of competitive non-attribution baselines. No unified benchmark exists to fairly compare attribution methods across diverse real-world LLM applications at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255142/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a training set D, a reference set D_ref, a model checkpoint θ, and a user-selected attribution method τ, producing per-sample attribution scores S ∈ R^N that drive subset selection (top-k or Gumbel-top-k) and downstream task evaluation. Metrics vary by task: average accuracy across 7 benchmarks for pre-train selection, AUPRC for toxicity/bias detection, and Recall@50/MRR plus counterfactual rate for factual attribution. The pipeline is method-agnostic—new attribution methods only need to supply scores to plug into the remaining stages.

## Method

DATE-LM is a three-stage modularized pipeline (attribution scoring → subset selection → task evaluation) spanning three application tasks with task-specific robustness designs. For training data selection, it standardizes Gumbel-top-k diversity across all methods and evaluates at two training stages (10k/30k steps) to isolate the scoring function's contribution. For toxicity/bias filtering, it introduces a heterogeneous setting that injects safety-aligned distractors stylistically resembling unsafe data, revealing that attribution methods drop substantially in AUPRC while LLM classifiers remain stable. For factual attribution, it corrupts entities in supporting evidence (e.g., replacing Microsoft with Google) to break surface-level lexical overlap, enabling fair comparison between attribution methods and retrieval baselines like BM25 and Rep-Sim that previously dominated due to lexical shortcuts.

## Results

Across three evaluation tasks, no single data attribution method dominates. In pre-training data selection (30K steps, LAMBADA reference), the non-attribution EDU classifier achieves the highest average accuracy (50.63), outperforming Grad-Sim (50.26) at 11× FLOPS cost and BM25 (50.26) at 1× cost. In toxicity/bias filtering, attribution methods show competitive AUPRC in the homogeneous setting (e.g., Rep-Sim reaches 0.989 on Llama3.1-8B) but suffer a dramatic drop under the heterogeneous setting (Rep-Sim falls to 0.585), while LLM classifiers like Wildguard remain stable (0.827→0.817). In factual attribution, Grad-Sim achieves the best Recall@50 (0.626) and MRR (0.970) on Llama3.1-8B, outperforming BM25 (0.313/0.826) and Rep-Sim (0.625/0.965), and yields the lowest counterfactual rate (0.010) after retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In pre-training data selection, the non-attribution EDU classifier (50.63 avg at 30K steps) and BM25 (50.26) match or exceed the expensive Grad-Sim (50.26, 11× FLOPS) and MATES (50.13, 1.13× FLOPS), indicating that simple baselines are competitive for this task. In fine-tuning data selection, RDS+ (62.4 MMLU) and Rep-Sim (61.2) outperform both Grad-Sim (58.4) and LESS (60.0), with random selection (60.2 avg) also competitive. In toxicity/bias filtering, LLM classifiers (Wildguard 0.827, Llama-Guard 0.782) substantially outperform all attribution methods in the heterogeneous setting, where attribution methods drop 30–50% (e.g., Rep-Sim 0.989→0.585 on 8B); in the homogeneous setting, Rep-Sim and LESS are competitive with or exceed LLM classifiers on 1B models. In factual attribution, Grad-Sim and LESS consistently outperform BM25 and Rep-Sim (e.g., Grad-Sim 0.626 vs. BM25 0.313 Recall@50 on Llama3.1-8B), and achieve the lowest counterfactual rates after retraining (Grad-Sim 0.010 vs. BM25 0.273). Cosine-similarity-based methods (Rep-Sim, Grad-Sim, LESS) consistently outperform dot-product-based methods (Grad-Dot, DataInf, EKFAC) across both toxicity filtering and factual attribution, while DataInf and EKFAC fail to scale to 8B models under the benchmark's setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

DATE-LM unifies and extends the fragmented landscape of data attribution evaluation, which has previously been conducted through either prohibitively expensive leave-one-out retraining or ad-hoc, single-application experiments. It builds on the application-driven evaluation paradigm (e.g., Pan et al. for toxicity detection, Akyurek et al. and Chang et al. for factual attribution) but departs from prior work by standardizing a multi-task pipeline across pre-training data selection, toxicity/bias filtering, and factual attribution under a single framework. The paper also introduces two key design departures: a heterogeneous filtering setting with safety-aligned distractors (absent in prior toxicity detection benchmarks) and a counterfactual factual attribution setup that breaks lexical overlap (addressing a known confound in ROME-based tracing benchmarks).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that simple non-attribution baselines (BM25, Rep-Sim, EDU) can match or outperform expensive gradient-based attribution methods in data selection tasks raises a fundamental open question about when data attribution is truly necessary versus when cheaper heuristics suffice. The dramatic performance drop of attribution methods under the heterogeneous toxicity filtering setting (e.g., Rep-Sim falling from 0.989 to 0.585 AUPRC on Llama3.1-8B) reveals a critical robustness gap that prior benchmarks missed, suggesting attribution methods are overly sensitive to surface-level stylistic similarity. Open questions include whether future attribution methods can be made invariant to stylistic confounds, whether the counterfactual factual attribution setup generalizes beyond ROME-style QA, and how to design evaluation protocols that cleanly isolate the contribution of the attribution signal from auxiliary components like diversity sampling.
