## Problem

Existing video object removal methods fail to eliminate side effects such as shadows, reflections, and lighting changes because paired training data following real-world physical rules is scarce. Most prior works construct artificial pairs by pasting objects or zero-masking, which cannot capture these object-environment interactions, leading to unnatural outputs with residual artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254988/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a full unmasked video clip and a binary object mask sequence as input, deliberately feeding the complete video (rather than the masked version) as reference guidance so the model can attend to side-effect regions via cross-region correlations. It produces an object-erased video at 720×480 resolution (81 frames during training) along with an auxiliary difference mask prediction that localizes all pixel regions altered by the removal. Training data is rendered at 1920×1080 with 90 frames per clip.

## Method

ROSE is a diffusion transformer inpainting model continued from Wan2.1 1.3B, with three key design choices. First, it adopts a reference-based erasing paradigm where the entire unmasked video is concatenated with noisy latents and masks as model input, enabling the transformer's attention mechanism to localize side-effect regions by attending to the object's intrinsic attributes. Second, a difference mask predictor (a two-layer MLP on concatenated features from transformer blocks 5, 15, and 25) is trained with MSE loss against pixel-level differences between paired videos, providing explicit supervision for side-effect localization. Third, five mask augmentation variants (original, point-wise, bounding box, dilated, eroded) are randomly sampled during training to improve robustness to imperfect real-world masks. The model is trained on 16,678 synthetic video pairs generated via an automated Unreal Engine pipeline covering six side-effect categories (common, light source, mirror, reflection, shadow, translucent), with a combined loss of standard diffusion denoising and auxiliary mask prediction.

## Results

On the synthetic paired benchmark (ROSE-Bench), ROSE achieves a mean PSNR of 31.12, SSIM of 0.917, and LPIPS of 0.077, outperforming all five baselines across every side-effect category (Common, Shadow, Light Source, Reflection, Mirror, Translucent). The largest gains appear on the most challenging categories: Light Source (PSNR 30.07 vs. 23.43 for ProPainter) and Mirror (PSNR 28.35 vs. 22.12 for ProPainter). On the realistic paired benchmark, ROSE attains SSIM 0.923 and LPIPS 0.092, though ProPainter edges it on PSNR (32.81 vs. 31.34). On VBench-based unpaired evaluation, ROSE leads in Background Consistency (0.923), Subject Consistency (0.908), and Imaging Quality (0.630).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROSE dominates all five baselines on the synthetic paired benchmark, winning on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across every one of the six side-effect categories, with the most dramatic margins on Light Source (+6.6 dB PSNR over ProPainter) and Mirror (+6.2 dB over ProPainter). On the realistic paired benchmark (DAVIS-based copy-and-paste), ROSE wins on SSIM (0.923 vs. 0.917) and LPIPS (0.092 vs. 0.122), but ProPainter achieves a higher PSNR (32.81 vs. 31.34), suggesting flow-based propagation still excels at pixel-level fidelity in simpler real-world scenes. On the VBench unpaired evaluation, ROSE leads in Background Consistency (0.923 vs. 0.917 ProPainter), Subject Consistency (0.908 vs. 0.903), and Imaging Quality (0.630 vs. 0.626), while ProPainter maintains a slight edge on Temporal Flickering (0.932 vs. 0.936, lower is better) and ties on Motion Smoothness (0.975). Among diffusion-based baselines, DiffuEraser is the closest competitor on the synthetic benchmark but trails ROSE by ~4.6 dB PSNR on average, while FloED and FGT lag further behind. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the reference-based mask guidance (MRG) contributes the largest single gain (+3.56 dB mean PSNR over the base model), followed by the difference mask predictor (DMP) and mask augmentation (MA).

## Relation to Prior Work

ROSE builds on diffusion-transformer video inpainting (Wan2.1, DiffuEraser) but departs from the standard 'mask-and-inpaint' paradigm by feeding the full unmasked video as reference guidance, enabling the model to localize object-correlated side effects (shadows, reflections, illumination changes) that prior methods leave behind. The closest prior work, OmniEraser, addresses side effects only for static-camera image pairs; ROSE extends this to video by generating strictly-aligned paired sequences via an Unreal Engine 3D rendering pipeline, sidestepping the intractability of curating real-world video pairs with physical interactions. An auxiliary difference-mask predictor, trained on pixel-level differences between paired videos, provides explicit supervision for side-effect localization—a component absent from prior inpainting frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine data bottleneck: paired video sequences where object removal physically alters the environment (shadows, reflections, global illumination) are essentially unavailable in the wild, and the 3D-rendering pipeline offers a scalable, controllable solution. ROSE-Bench is the first benchmark to systematically evaluate side-effect removal across five interaction categories, filling a gap where prior benchmarks only test trivial inpainting. Open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real gap persists for more complex interactions (e.g., caustics, multi-bounce reflections), whether the reference-based paradigm generalizes to longer sequences without the observed flickering, and whether the approach can be made real-time for practical video editing pipelines.
