## Problem

ViTs such as DINOv2 repurpose low-informative patch tokens to store global context, creating attention artifacts that degrade dense prediction performance and compromise interpretability—a critical concern in medical imaging where domain shifts compound the issue. Prior mitigations (learnable registers, denoising modules, auxiliary losses) require expensive retraining or lack theoretical grounding, and these artifacts were not well characterized in smaller models like DINOv2-S.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the representation heads (DINO and iBOT) of pre-trained ViT backbones during fine-tuning, consuming RGB images, OCT scans, or fundus photographs and producing class labels, pixel-level segmentation masks, or depth maps depending on the downstream task. For dense prediction, a ViT-UNet hybrid decoder fuses projected patch and class tokens with early UNet encoder features for multi-scale integration. The RMLP itself introduces no new input format or resolution constraint—it modifies only how the backbone's internal representations pass through the projection heads.

## Method

RMLP replaces each linear layer in the DINO and iBOT MLP heads with a randomized, non-trainable operator that adds a Gaussian random projection (i.i.d. entries from N(0, λ/n)) to a truncated or zero-padded residual of the input, effectively acting as a stochastic residual connection. This preserves the topology of the representation space while preventing the heads from exploiting token-level classification shortcuts, forcing the backbone to encode more semantically aligned features. The theoretical analysis shows that with adequate amplitude λ, RMLPs turn point embeddings into probability balls of controlled radius, regularizing contrastive learning by applying cross-entropy over neighborhoods rather than individual points. The approach introduces zero trainable parameters, requires no architectural changes or retraining from scratch, and is a lightweight drop-in regularizer fully compatible with standard DINOv2 fine-tuning protocols and the KoLeo injectivity regularizer.

## Results

On OCT retinal layer segmentation (Eckardt et al. dataset), DINOv2-S fine-tuned with R₅-MLP achieves 0.97±0.03 averaged DICE and 0.72±0.09 DICE on the Outer Nuclear Layer (ONL), substantially outperforming RETFound (0.92±0.06 / 0.59±0.10) and the unfine-tuned DINOv2-S (0.79±0.20 / 0.54±0.20). On natural image tasks, R₅-MLP maintains competitive performance: 0.76±0.01 linear classification accuracy on ImageNet-1k (vs. 0.77±0.01 for DINOv2-S) and 0.80±0.1 mIoU on ADE20k segmentation. For 1-NN pathology classification across seven ophthalmology datasets, R₅-MLP consistently outperforms the MLP-fine-tuned baseline, e.g., 0.78±0.01 on OCTID vs. 0.69±0.02 for DINOv2-S+MLP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RETFound [43], the strongest domain-specific baseline, R₅-MLP wins decisively on OCT segmentation (0.97 vs. 0.92 averaged DICE; 0.72 vs. 0.59 ONL DICE) and on 1-NN pathology classification across most CFP datasets. Against the attention-artifact mitigation methods—Registers [9], Sinder [40], and DVT [42]—R₅-MLP achieves superior OCT segmentation (0.97 vs. 0.92, 0.90, 0.78 respectively) while also preserving natural-image performance better (e.g., 0.80 mIoU on ADE20k vs. 0.72 for Registers and 0.45 for DVT). Compared to the standard MLP fine-tuning baseline (DINOv2-S+MLP), R₅-MLP consistently improves medical task performance (e.g., 0.78 vs. 0.69 1-NN accuracy on OCTID; 0.97 vs. 0.86 DICE on OCT segmentation) while roughly matching on natural-image linear classification (0.76 vs. 0.73). When applied to SwAV [4] (a different self-supervised paradigm), RMLP shows modest gains on OCT segmentation (0.92 vs. 0.88 averaged DICE for R₀.₁) but results are less consistent, suggesting the benefit is strongest when combined with the DINO/iBOT contrastive framework. The unfine-tuned DINOv2-S [30] is outperformed on all medical tasks but slightly leads on natural-image linear classification (0.77 vs. 0.76), which the authors attribute to RMLPs promoting neighborhood-based rather than linearly separable representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on attention artifacts in Vision Transformers (Darcet et al.'s 'registers', Sinder, DVT) by proposing a fundamentally different intervention: replacing the learnable MLP heads (DINO and iBOT) with non-trainable randomized operators (RMLPs) rather than adding tokens, auxiliary losses, or attention-smoothing modules. The theoretical grounding draws on the geometry of contrastive learning (KoLeo injectivity, Gaussian concentration inequalities) to show that RMLPs act as ε-distortion-preserving random projections that turn point embeddings into probability balls. Unlike prior artifact-mitigation methods that require retraining on proprietary data or architectural modifications, RMLP is a drop-in, parameter-free regularization compatible with any contrastive training objective, and the paper demonstrates cross-paradigm applicability by also fine-tuning SwAV under the DINOv2 training protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a lightweight, theory-grounded tool for improving both interpretability and downstream performance of ViTs in domain-adaptation settings, which is particularly relevant for medical imaging where transparency is critical and labeled data is scarce. The topological analysis (Theorems 1–5) provides a formal framework for understanding how ViT embeddings evolve through transformer blocks and how RMLPs regularize the representation space without destroying its structure. Key open questions include: a principled method for selecting the amplitude λ (currently heuristic), whether the approach scales to larger ViT variants and broader medical modalities, and whether the probability-ball interpretation of RMLP outputs can be leveraged for uncertainty quantification in clinical deployment.
