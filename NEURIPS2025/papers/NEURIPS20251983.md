## Problem

Existing multivariate time series anomaly detection methods rely on increasingly complex architectures (Transformers, GANs, dual-axis attention) that detect only fragments of anomalous segments, use bidirectional attention that ignores the unidirectional nature of time, and lack transparent root-cause diagnosis. The paper argues that anomalies inherently manifest through two interconnected signals—prediction errors from temporal causality breakdown and structural deviations in inter-variable relationships—that prior methods fail to explicitly model and jointly exploit.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sliding window of length L=10 over N multivariate time series variables, processing each variable's past L−1 observations independently. It produces a per-timestep anomaly score computed as the product of a prediction score (mean absolute error) and a deviation score (Frobenius norm of the current pairwise dissimilarity matrix minus the Stable Latent Structure), along with an N×N deviation matrix whose high-magnitude rows/columns identify root-cause variables. The compact window length is justified by the observation that anomalies strongly correlate with adjacent time points.

## Method

OracleAD encodes each variable's past window through a dedicated LSTM encoder, applies learnable attention pooling over hidden states to yield a single causal embedding per variable, then fuses all N embeddings via multi-head self-attention into a shared latent space that captures dynamic inter-variable dependencies. A per-variable LSTM decoder jointly reconstructs the input window and predicts the next timepoint, trained with a composite loss combining prediction error, reconstruction error, and a deviation regularizer that penalizes departure from a Stable Latent Structure (SLS)—an epoch-averaged pairwise L2 dissimilarity matrix representing normal-state relational geometry. At inference, the final anomaly score is the product of the prediction score and the SLS deviation score, balancing sharp temporal localization with sustained structural sensitivity across full anomaly segments. The deviation matrix directly exposes which variables disrupt the learned relational structure, enabling unsupervised root-cause identification without any labeled anomaly data.

## Results

OracleAD achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmark datasets. On PSM, it reaches 65.85% F1 and 68.17% VUS-PR, outperforming the best baseline (AutoEncoder) by +19.95 and +15.68 percentage points respectively. On SMD, OracleAD scores 43.03% F1 and 47.52% VUS-PR (+10.87 and +5.92 pts over the best baseline OmniAnomaly). On SWaT, it achieves 76.50% F1 and 74.16% VUS-PR, with AUC-ROC of 82.71% and AUC-PR of 72.39%, matching or exceeding all 11 baselines on most metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OracleAD decisively outperforms all 11 baselines on F1 and VUS-PR across all three datasets, with the largest margins on PSM (+19.95 pts F1 over AutoEncoder) and SMD (+10.87 pts F1 over OmniAnomaly). Transformer-based methods (Anomaly Transformer, iTransformer, PatchTST) and lightweight forecasting models (DLinear, NLinear, TimesNet) perform poorly on anomaly detection despite strong forecasting benchmarks, often scoring near-chance F1 (~8%) on SMD. SARAD is the closest competitor on SWaT, slightly exceeding OracleAD on AUC-ROC (85.40 vs. 82.71) and VUS-ROC (86.30 vs. 82.42), but OracleAD leads on F1 (+19.20 pts) and VUS-PR (+11.44 pts) on that dataset. On PSM, OracleAD's AUC-ROC of 84.78% surpasses the next-best AutoEncoder (66.79%) by 18 points, and its AUC-PR of 68.11% nearly doubles the runner-up (47.27%). The ablation confirms that the multiplicative dual scoring (prediction × deviation) is essential: using either score alone degrades F1 by 4–10 pts depending on the dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

OracleAD departs from the dominant Transformer-based paradigm in MTSAD (Anomaly Transformer, iTransformer, Crossformer) by arguing that bidirectional attention and large receptive fields misalign with the unidirectional, causal nature of time. Instead, it revives the per-variable LSTM encoder-decoder lineage (EncDec-AD, OmniAnomaly) but introduces two key innovations: attention-pooled causal embeddings that condense each variable's history into a single vector, and a Stable Latent Structure (SLS) that serves as a learned, data-driven reference for inter-variable relationships rather than a static graph or manually defined prior. The dual scoring mechanism (prediction error × structural deviation) also distinguishes it from prior methods that rely on a single reconstruction or prediction error signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling case that interpretability and detection accuracy need not be traded off: the SLS deviation matrix directly identifies root-cause variables without post-hoc attribution, which is critical for operational reliability in industrial and cyber-physical systems. It also contributes a methodological critique of MTSAD benchmarks (e.g., Affiliation F1 inflating scores, AUC-ROC being misleading under imbalance) that should influence how the community evaluates future work. Limitations include the assumption of globally consistent inter-variable relationships under normal conditions, which may break down in highly non-stationary or multimodal systems, and the fixed window length that may not suit all temporal scales.
