## Problem

Recent work showed that model-free RL on LLMs can produce 'thinking' behavior (e.g., chain-of-thought reasoning), but no domain-independent theory explains under what conditions thinking emerges as a reward-maximizing strategy. The paper asks: when will model-free RL select thought actions that produce no immediate reward and do not change the environment state, and what role does policy initialization play in this emergence?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250984/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The thought MDP takes a joint state (environment state s, thought state τ) and the agent outputs either an environment action from A or a thought action from C, but not both at the same time-step. Thought actions leave the environment state unchanged and yield zero reward; they only transition the thought state via a deterministic function p_T. In the LLM experiments, input is a five-number arithmetic prompt (with or without partial sums appended as 'thinking tokens') and output is a numerical answer; in the gridworld, input is a 5×5 grid position plus a task label and output is a sequence of cardinal moves interleaved with special thought actions 'A', 'B', or 'C'.

## Method

The paper introduces the thought MDP, a minimal extension of the classical MDP that adds a thought state space T, a set of thought actions C, and a deterministic thought transition function p_T, with the constraint that thought actions neither affect environment transitions nor produce reward. The central theoretical result (Theorem 5) shows that during policy iteration, a thought action is selected at (s, τ) precisely when the value of the sub-policy π(τ′) — triggered by the thought action — exceeds the value of the current sub-policy π(τ), meaning thinking functions as a local policy improvement operator that switches the agent to a better sub-policy already embedded in its policy function. Proposition 4 proves the optimal policy never thinks, so thinking is strictly a transient learning phenomenon whose emergence depends on policy initialization containing heterogeneous sub-policies. Empirically, the authors validate the theory by showing that appending partial sums (forced thinking) increases accuracy across 11 open-source LLMs on arithmetic tasks, and by constructing a 5×5 gridworld where multi-task behavior-cloning pre-training followed by REINFORCE causes a 'Pretrained-Think' agent to discover a think-then-act strategy (output 'A', navigate to bottom-right, output 'B', navigate to top-left) that is significantly more sample-efficient than non-thinking or from-scratch baselines.

## Results

In the LLM validation experiment (Table 1), forcing models to think by appending partial sums increases accuracy on a 5-number addition task across all 11 tested models. The most dramatic improvement is Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, which jumps from 7.20% to 71.20% accuracy (a ~64-point gain), while Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct goes from 5.06% to 96.10%. In the gridworld experiment, the Pretrained-Think agent learns the two-subtask navigation goal significantly faster than Pretrained-NoThink, Scratch-Think, or Scratch-NoThink, with the thinking fraction stabilizing at ~15% of timesteps (matching the theoretical optimum of 2 thought steps in a 14-step episode).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external RL methods but instead runs internal ablations to validate its theoretical predictions. In the LLM experiment, the 'No Thinking' baseline (direct prompt) is consistently outperformed by the 'Thinking' condition (prompt with partial sums appended) across all 11 models, with gains ranging from ~28 points (Tulu-2-7b: 0.30% → 28.50%) to ~64 points (Qwen2.5-1.5B: 7.20% → 71.20%), confirming that thought actions increase v_π(s,τ) as Theorem 5 predicts. In the gridworld, Pretrained-Think (multi-task pre-training + thought actions) learns the two-subtask navigation goal substantially faster than Pretrained-NoThink (pre-training but thought actions masked), which itself outperforms both Scratch-Think and Scratch-NoThink (no pre-training). The Scratch agents fail to learn at all due to sparse reward, confirming that pre-training is a necessary ingredient. The Pretrained-NoThink agent does eventually learn, but the authors note this is because sequences of environment actions can partially trigger pre-trained sub-task behaviors, making discovery harder than a single thought action.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work connecting model-free RL to System 2 reasoning by introducing the thought MDP, a minimal formal extension of the classical MDP that treats thinking as a distinct action type selected by the policy rather than as internal computation (as in Guez et al. 2019) or as decision-time planning with a learned model (as in AlphaGo or the Thinker architecture). It builds on the effective horizon framework of Laidlaw et al. (2023) to derive sample-complexity implications of thought actions, and positions itself against the options framework by noting that thought MDPs naturally model the cost of switching sub-policies over multiple steps. The LLM experiments connect to the DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1 line of work on RL-trained chain-of-thought, but the authors' contribution is a domain-independent theoretical account of *when* such thinking emerges rather than a new training method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, domain-independent formal model that unifies the observation that LLMs 'think' under RL with a general RL principle: thinking emerges when policy initialization contains heterogeneous sub-policies that can be selectively activated. This reframing suggests that the key ingredient for thinking in non-language agents is multi-task pre-training plus a mechanism to manipulate internal state, a hypothesis the authors test in a small gridworld. Major open questions remain: how to define thought states and actions in non-language domains, how to handle dynamic environments where the world changes during thinking, and whether the sampling-error explanation for persistent thought actions at convergence (contradicting Proposition 4) generalizes. The gridworld experiment is a proof-of-concept with limited scale, and the LLM experiments are confirmatory of existing CoT findings rather than demonstrating new capabilities.
