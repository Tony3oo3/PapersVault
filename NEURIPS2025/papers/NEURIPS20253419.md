## Problem

Existing training-time LLM unlearning methods rely on coarse-grained loss combinations that induce uncontrolled gradient dynamics, making it difficult to precisely disentangle forget and retain knowledge while preserving model utility. These approaches also lack principled, interpretable guidance for selecting which layers to intervene on (typically resorting to grid search) and remain vulnerable to knowledge recovery via jailbreaking or probing attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253419/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FALCON takes a pre-trained LLM along with a forget set (harmful or sensitive knowledge to remove) and a retain set (general knowledge to preserve), and produces updated model parameters with the target knowledge selectively removed. The method operates on intermediate hidden activations at a single MI-selected layer rather than across all layers, and the output includes both the unlearned parameters and the identified optimal intervention layer index. Activations are processed as matrices of shape (B·L)×D where B is batch size, L is sequence length, and D is hidden dimension, with PCA reducing dimensionality before KDE-based entropy estimation.

## Method

FALCON combines three mechanisms for fine-grained unalignment. First, it estimates mutual information between forget and retain activations at each layer using KDE with adaptive bandwidth (Scott's rule) after PCA dimensionality reduction, selecting the layer with minimal entanglement for intervention. Second, it constructs Principal Offset Vectors (POVs) via SVD of the frozen model's activation matrix, steering updated representations into subspaces misaligned with the top-K principal directions of undesired knowledge; an InfoNCE contrastive loss then aligns updated activations toward POVs while repelling them from frozen representations, complemented by a cosine-similarity retain loss. Third, when forget and retain gradients exhibit negative cosine similarity (conflict), the forget gradient is projected onto the orthogonal complement of the retain gradient to eliminate interference, and the combined conflict-reduced gradient drives parameter updates via the Sophia second-order optimizer.

## Results

On the WMDP benchmark, FALCON achieves the lowest harmful knowledge scores across all tested models: 26.7 (Bio) and 25.3 (Cyber) on Zephyr-7B, and 27.7 (Bio) and 25.3 (Cyber) on Yi-6B-Chat, while maintaining MMLU scores of 57.4 and 60.3 respectively and PPL at 1.5. On the MUSE copyrighted content benchmark, FALCON achieves forget_knowmem_ROUGE of 0.02 and forget_verbmem_ROUGE of 0.03 while retaining 0.54 ROUGE. On TOFU entity unlearning, FALCON achieves FQ of 0.99 (1% forget) and 0.52 (10% forget) with MU of 0.55–0.60, consistently outperforming all baselines across forget set sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FALCON outperforms all four training-time baselines (LLMU, SCRUB, SSD, RMU) on WMDP scores while preserving MMLU and PPL closest to the base model. On Zephyr-7B, FALCON reduces WMDP-Bio by 9.8 points over the next-best RMU (26.7 vs. 34.5) and by 29.6 points over LLMU (26.7 vs. 36.3), while maintaining MMLU at 57.4 (matching RMU) versus LLMU's 50.3 and SCRUB's 50.0. On Yi-6B-Chat with complex knowledge entanglement, the gap widens: FALCON achieves 27.7 WMDP-Bio versus RMU's 50.8 and LLMU's 56.2, while retaining MMLU of 60.3 versus RMU's 59.6. SCRUB and LLMU suffer severe utility degradation (PPL of 16.5 and 4.8–5.4 respectively), whereas FALCON maintains PPL at 1.5. On MUSE (Table 2), FALCON achieves near-zero forget ROUGE (0.02/0.03) versus NPO (0.56/0.35) and SimNPO (0.54/0.36) while matching the best retention score (0.54). On TOFU (Table 3), FALCON achieves FQ of 0.99 at 1% forget data versus NPO's 0.92 and RMU's 0.16, and maintains MU of 0.60 at 10% forget where GradAscent drops to 0.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

FALCON departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM unlearning that relies on coarse-grained loss combinations (e.g., NPO, SimNPO, LLMU) or simple representation noising (SSD, RMU) by introducing a fine-grained, representation-guided framework. It repurposes contrastive learning (InfoNCE), SVD-based principal direction identification, and gradient orthogonal projection—each drawn from distinct subfields (representation learning, spectral methods, continual learning)—into a unified pipeline where mutual information serves as an information-theoretic selection criterion for intervention layers. Unlike prior training-time methods that treat layer selection as an empirical grid-search problem, FALCON provides a principled, interpretable metric (KDE-estimated MI) to identify where forget and retain representations are least entangled, and unlike inference-time approaches (task arithmetic, in-context unlearning), it achieves permanent parameter-level modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FALCON addresses a practically urgent gap: the need for scalable, interpretable knowledge removal in deployed LLMs under regulatory pressure (GDPR 'right to be forgotten') and safety concerns, without the prohibitive cost of full retraining. The demonstrated resistance to both logit-lens probing and enhanced GCG jailbreaking attacks suggests the unlearning is structural rather than superficial, which is a meaningful differentiator over baselines that can be reversed by adversarial prompts. However, the work is limited to text-only models in the 1B–7B range, and open questions remain about whether MI-guided layer selection and contrastive orthogonal unalignment scale effectively to much larger or multimodal architectures, and whether the KDE-based MI estimation remains reliable in the high-dimensional activation spaces of deeper models.
