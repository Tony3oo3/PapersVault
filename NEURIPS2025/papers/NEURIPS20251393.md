## Problem

In proposal-based line segment detection, confidence scores and line locations are predicted simultaneously, so the confidence may not accurately reflect true prediction quality. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a dedicated learnable ranking and optimization stage that re-scores and refines candidate line segments after initial proposal generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251393/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (resized to 512×512) and produces up to 500 line segments, each parameterized by two 2D endpoints and a confidence score, sorted by confidence with non-maximum suppression applied. Multi-level CNN features are extracted and processed at 128×128 spatial resolution. The model is trained solely on the Wireframe dataset (5,000 images) and evaluated zero-shot on YorkUrban (102 images) and NKL for generalization.

## Method

RANK++LETR builds on the deformable DETR encoder-decoder architecture with a two-stage pipeline: the encoder generates candidate line proposals via spatial-aware assignment (each feature point predicts lines whose centroid falls in its receptive field), and the decoder re-ranks and refines the top-scoring candidates. The decoder employs a novel Line-Aware Deformable Attention (LADA) module where attention positions are interpolated along the candidate line's endpoints rather than clustered around a single reference point, aligning the receptive field with the elongated geometry of lines. For supervision, the decoder is trained with ranking-based losses: an AP-based ranking loss (L_rank) that pushes positive predictions above negatives, and a sorting loss (L_sort) that orders positives by a contiguous quality label derived from endpoint distance to the nearest ground truth. Training uses a warm-up phase (240 epochs, encoder-only supervision) followed by joint encoder-decoder optimization (120 epochs) to ensure the encoder produces meaningful proposals before the decoder's ranking supervision becomes effective.

## Results

On the Wireframe dataset, RANK++LETR achieves 67.9 sAP5, 72.1 sAP10, 68.8 sF10, and 68.3 LAP, outperforming the previous best Transformer-based method RANK-LETR (65.0 sAP5, 69.7 sAP10, 65.6 LAP) by approximately 2.9 points on sAP5. On the YorkUrban dataset, it reaches 28.8 sAP5, 31.2 sAP10, 41.2 sF10, and 34.8 LAP, again leading all baselines. In terms of inference speed, RANK++LETR runs at 12.4 FPS, faster than LETR (5.4 FPS) and RANK-LETR (9.0 FPS) but slower than optimized CNN-based methods like HAWPv2 (45 FPS) and M-LSD† (32.9 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RANK++LETR outperforms all 10 baselines on both Wireframe and YorkUrban across every reported metric. Against the closest Transformer-based competitor RANK-LETR [31], it gains +2.9 sAP5 (67.9 vs 65.0), +2.4 sAP10 (72.1 vs 69.7), and +2.7 LAP (68.3 vs 65.6) on Wireframe, and +1.2 sAP5, +1.1 sAP10, +1.5 sF10, +0.7 LAP on YorkUrban. Versus the strongest CNN-based method HAWPv2 [37], it improves sAP5 by +2.4 (67.9 vs 65.5) and sAP10 by +2.6 (72.1 vs 69.5) on Wireframe, while also surpassing it on all YorkUrban metrics. Compared to LETR [33], the improvement is more substantial: +8.7 sAP5 and +6.5 sAP10 on Wireframe. In terms of speed, RANK++LETR (12.4 FPS) is roughly 2.3× faster than LETR (5.4 FPS) and 1.4× faster than RANK-LETR (9.0 FPS), though it remains slower than CNN-based methods like HAWPv2 (45 FPS) and M-LSD† (32.9 FPS) due to the inherent cost of the Transformer decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer-based line detection lineage initiated by LETR (DETR-style end-to-end detection) and RANK-LETR (matched predicting with re-ranking) by introducing a clear encoder-decoder separation: the encoder generates candidate line proposals while the decoder performs confidence re-ranking and positional refinement. Architecturally, it builds on deformable DETR but replaces the standard deformable attention with a line-aware variant (LADA) whose attention positions are distributed along the candidate segment's elongated geometry rather than around a single reference point. The ranking-based supervision (Rank & Sort Loss, differentiable AP) is adapted from object detection and edge detection literature, with a novel contiguous label definition based on endpoint distance to the nearest ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The encoder-decoder split for proposal generation followed by learnable optimization offers a reusable architectural pattern for other detection tasks where initial predictions benefit from a secondary ranking/refinement stage. The LADA module is a lightweight, task-specific attention design that could transfer to other elongated-structure detection problems (e.g., lane detection, crack detection). However, the method remains slower than optimized CNN-based detectors (12.4 FPS vs. 115.4 FPS for M-LSD), and the generalization to unstructured scenes (NKL) is demonstrated only qualitatively without quantitative metrics. The paper acknowledges that further efficiency gains require lightweight Transformer or hybrid CNN-Transformer designs, leaving the speed-accuracy tradeoff for Transformer-based line detection still open.
