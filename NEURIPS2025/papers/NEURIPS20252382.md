## Problem

Existing estimation theory for dual-type optimal transport map estimators requires (β,b)-smoothness and (α,a)-convexity of the true Brenier potential, along with bounded or sub-exponential distribution supports, excluding many practical scenarios such as transporting from Gaussian to Student-t or from unbounded noise to bounded image data. This paper asks whether statistical and approximation error bounds can be established for general distributions without these restrictive second-order regularity assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. samples X₁,…,Xₙ from a source distribution μ and Y₁,…,Yₘ from a target distribution ν on ℝᵈ, with no bounded-support requirement. It produces an estimated Brenier potential φ̂ and the corresponding OT map estimator ∇φ̂, where the potential is optimized over a chosen function class F (e.g., tanh neural networks, RKHS, wavelets) using a sieved convex conjugate that restricts the Legendre-Fenchel supremum to a data-driven ball of radius R̃. The evaluation metric is the L²(μ) error between the estimated and true OT maps, reported as unexplained variance proportion (UVP).

## Method

The core framework builds on the sieved convex conjugate, which restricts the supremum in the Legendre-Fenchel transform to a bounded region, localizing the approximation problem to compact pseudo-supports of μ and ν. Two new analytical tools are developed: (i) a map stability inequality (Prop. 3.3) that bounds ‖∇φ − ∇φ₀‖²_{L²(μ)} by the truncated excess risk without requiring (β,b)-smoothness of φ₀, relying only on an envelope condition for the OT map and a uniform Hessian bound on the function class; and (ii) an oracle inequality (Prop. 3.4) that decomposes the truncated excess risk into statistical error, sieved bias, and an approximation error measured in the L^∞ norm on a compact set—aligning with classical approximation theory rather than the harder-to-analyze Sobolev gradient norms used previously. For the neural OT estimator, tanh-activated fully connected networks with bounded parameters serve as the function class, and Theorem 3.7 establishes the first non-asymptotic rate E‖∇φ̃ₙ − ∇φ₀‖²_{L²(μ)} ≲ log n · n^(−α/(d+2α)) under only sub-Weibull tails on μ and C^α regularity of φ₀, without compact support, Poincaré inequality, or (α,a)-convexity assumptions.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.7) establishes that the sieved tanh neural network OT estimator between general (unbounded) distributions achieves E‖∇φ̃ₙ − ∇φ₀‖²_{L²(μ)} ≲ log(n) · n^{−α/(d+2α)}, matching the rate of Gunsilius [25] but without requiring compact support, a Poincaré inequality, or Hölder smoothness of the Brenier potential. Numerically, the sieved TNN estimator (with sieve constant C=0 or 1) consistently achieves lower L²-UVP than the original non-sieved dual-type estimator (C=∞) across all four synthetic OT problems in dimensions d=5, 10, 20, with the advantage most pronounced in the small-sample regime (n ≤ 256). The convergence rate is slower than the minimax rate of Hutter & Rigollet [27], which is expected given the substantially weaker regularity assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its sieved TNN estimator against the original (non-sieved) dual-type OT estimators from Hutter & Rigollet [27], Gunsilius [25], and Divol et al. [21], which all correspond to the C=∞ setting in the experiments. Across all four synthetic OT problems (N(0,1)→t₆, Unif(0,1)→N(0,1), t₆→N(0,1), N(0,1)→Unif(0,1)) in dimensions d=5, 10, 20, the sieved estimator with C=0 or C=1 achieves lower L²-UVP than the C=∞ baseline, with the gap largest at small sample sizes (n=64–256) and narrowing as n grows. Theoretically, the paper's rate matches Gunsilius [25] exactly but removes the compact support and Poincaré inequality requirements; it is slower than the minimax rate of Hutter & Rigollet [27] but does not require (α,a)-convexity, (β,b)-smoothness, or bounded supports. The practical takeaway is that a modest sieve constant (C=0 or 1) suffices, and the sieved estimator's performance is stable across a range of C values, while the non-sieved estimator only catches up at very large n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-type OT estimation lineage from Hutter & Rigollet [27] (minimax wavelet estimator under bounded support and strong regularity), Gunsilius [25] (Poincaré-inequality-based analysis), Divol et al. [21] (general function spaces with convexity/smoothness), and Ding et al. [20] (sieved convex conjugate for general distributions). The key departure is twofold: a new map stability inequality that eliminates the (β,b)-smoothness requirement on the Brenier potential for bounding statistical error, and a new oracle inequality that removes the (α,a)-convexity requirement for bounding approximation error, replacing gradient-based L²(μ) errors with a more tractable L∞ norm over a compact set. This L∞ formulation aligns the approximation error with classical neural network approximation theory, enabling the first convergence guarantee for tanh-network OT estimators between unbounded distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing both second-order regularity assumptions (smoothness and convexity) that have constrained the field, this work opens the theoretical door to analyzing OT map estimation in practically important settings previously excluded—e.g., transporting from Gaussian noise to bounded image data in generative modeling, or from uniform to unbounded targets in multivariate quantile regression. The main limitation is that Theorem 3.7 still assumes (β,b)-smoothness for the neural network rate (acknowledged as an analytical convenience), and the rate is not minimax optimal. Open questions include whether the sieve radius choice can be fully justified theoretically, whether the framework extends to ReLU or ICNN architectures, and whether the L∞ approximation error can be tightened to recover minimax rates under additional structure.
