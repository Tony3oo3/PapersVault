## Problem

Existing image-guided composition methods can insert a foreground object into a user-specified region of a background image, but they fail when the task involves human-object interactions: they produce unrealistic or inappropriate human gestures and alter the identities of both the foreground object and the background person. No prior method simultaneously ensures harmonious interaction poses and faithful appearance preservation of both subjects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253263/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a foreground object image (e.g., a product photo) and a background image containing a single human subject as input; an MLLM (GPT-4o) automatically infers the interaction type as a text prompt and the interaction region as a bounding box, eliminating the need for manual region specification. The output is a 512×512 composited image where the object is naturally integrated with the person in a harmonious interaction, with the background person's identity and the object's shape/texture preserved. The framework can also be chained with an Image-to-Video model to produce short demonstration videos.

## Method

HOComp fine-tunes FLUX.1 DiT via LoRA (rank 16) with two core mechanisms. First, MLLM-driven Region-based Pose Guidance (MRPG) uses a three-stage GPT-4o chain-of-thought to generate the interaction type prompt and interaction region, then imposes a fine-grained pose-guided loss (L_pose) computed over DWPose keypoints within that region to constrain the generated human pose. Second, Detail-Consistent Appearance Preservation (DCAP) combines three components: a shape-aware attention modulation that residually adjusts foreground-relevant attention maps in MM-DiT blocks using an object shape mask; a multi-view appearance loss that synthesizes k views via Zero123+ and aligns CLIP features between predicted and input objects; and a background consistency loss that enforces pixel-level matching in an unchanged region mask. The total training objective sums the standard denoising loss with weighted pose, background, and appearance losses, trained on the newly introduced IHOC dataset of 11,700 paired pre/post-interaction images spanning 117 interaction types.

## Results

On HOIBench, HOComp achieves the best scores across all five automatic metrics: FID 9.27, CLIP-Score 30.29, HOI-Score 87.39, DINO-Score 78.21, and SSIM(BG) 96.57. The HOI-Score of 87.39 surpasses the next-best GPT-4o (75.22) by 12.17 points, and the DINO-Score of 78.21 exceeds AnyDoor (58.83) by 19.38 points. In the user study (75 participants, 10 cases), HOComp ranks first in image quality (1.37), interaction harmonization (1.14), and appearance preservation (1.11), with the closest competitor being GPT-4o at 3.10/2.61/5.87 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOComp outperforms all nine baselines on every metric. Against the strongest competitor GPT-4o, HOComp leads by 12.17 on HOI-Score (87.39 vs. 75.22), 12.98 on DINO-Score (78.21 vs. 65.23), and 49.35 on SSIM(BG) (96.57 vs. 47.22), while GPT-4o's background consistency is severely degraded. Among diffusion-based composition methods, OmniGen (HOI 62.33, DINO 43.92) and UniCombine (HOI 58.91, DINO 51.02) are the closest but still trail HOComp by 25+ points on HOI-Score and 27+ on DINO-Score. AnyDoor, despite strong DINO-Score (58.83) among composition methods, produces the worst interaction quality (HOI 25.69) and highest user-study IQ rank (9.72). In the user study, HOComp's appearance-preservation rank of 1.11 is dramatically better than AnyDoor (2.84) and GPT-4o (5.87), confirming that existing methods struggle most with maintaining foreground identity during human-object interactions.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOComp sits at the intersection of image-guided composition (AnyDoor, Paint by Example, FreeCompose), multi-concept customization, and HOI generation, but departs from all three by requiring simultaneous natural interaction and bidirectional appearance preservation. It builds on FLUX.1's DiT architecture as its generative backbone, extending it with MLLM-driven coarse-to-fine pose guidance (using GPT-4o to infer interaction type and region, then DWPose keypoints for fine-grained constraints) and a shape-aware attention modulation mechanism applied to MM-DiT cross-attention maps. Unlike prior HOI generation methods that require explicit pose or reference video inputs at inference, HOComp automates interaction reasoning via MLLMs, and unlike composition methods that focus solely on foreground harmonization, it explicitly preserves background human identity through a dedicated consistency loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of IHOC (11,700 paired pre/post-interaction samples across 117 interaction types) and HOIBench (600 evaluation instances) fills a clear gap: no prior dataset supported training or evaluating interaction-aware composition with both appearance and interaction fidelity. The shape-aware attention modulation and multi-view appearance loss are modular techniques that could transfer to other DiT-based composition or customization pipelines. Key limitations include the 91.33% MLLM region-prediction accuracy (incorrect regions degrade output quality), reliance on a single base model (FLUX.1), and the narrow scope to single-person, single-object interactions. Open questions include extending to physically plausible multi-person interactions, video generation with temporal consistency, and reducing dependence on MLLM inference at test time.
