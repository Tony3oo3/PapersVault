## Problem

Open-source LLM web agents trail proprietary systems, and progress is held back by two intertwined challenges: a narrow focus on single-step tasks that ignores the complexity of multi-step web interactions, and the prohibitive cost of post-training that makes exhaustive hyperparameter sweeps infeasible for most labs. This paper provides the first statistically grounded diagnosis of how to allocate compute between expert demonstrations and on-policy reinforcement learning for multi-step web agents.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a textual observation of the current web page (raw HTML trees in MiniWoB++, accessibility trees truncated to 16k tokens in WorkArena) along with a natural-language task goal, and produces a sequence of high-level UI primitives (noop, fill, click, type, select_option, scroll, hover) over 2–10 steps. All agents use chain-of-thought prompting, and the environment returns a binary success/failure reward per episode. The 16k context window and discrete action space are shared constraints across both benchmarks.

## Method

The training pipeline has two stages: a Llama 3.3 70B teacher generates successful trajectories to warm-start a Llama 3.1 8B student via SFT, then training branches into on-policy RL using a multi-turn adaptation of GRPO (MT-GRPO) with per-goal advantage normalization and optional importance-ratio clipping. The key methodological contribution is a bootstrap-based statistical analysis over 1,370 randomly sampled hyperparameter configurations: for each of 10 hyperparameters, the paper resamples runs 1,000 times to estimate win-rates and 95% confidence intervals, revealing how optimal values shift across SFT compute budgets (base, +2.5e18, +7.6e18 FLOPs). By branching RL from multiple SFT checkpoints and tracking cumulative FLOPs, the paper maps the compute-performance Pareto frontier, showing that early (but not immediate) transition to RL matches peak SFT performance at only 55% of the compute and is the only strategy that closes the gap with closed-source models like GPT-4o.

## Results

On MiniWoB++, the Llama-3.1-8B SFT+RL agent achieves 67.2% on held-out goals and 63.3% on held-out tasks, approaching closed-source models like Claude-3.5-Sonnet (70.5%) and o1-Mini (69.7%). On WorkArena, SFT+RL reaches 35.4% (held-out goals) and 28.8% (held-out tasks), matching the 70B teacher model's 36.0% on held-out goals. The hybrid SFT+RL strategy matches the peak performance of pure SFT on MiniWoB++ while requiring only 55% of the compute (~6 vs. ~11 exaFLOPs), pushing the compute-performance Pareto frontier forward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The SFT+RL hybrid consistently outperforms both pure SFT and pure RL across all four evaluation settings. On MiniWoB++ held-out goals, SFT+RL (67.2%) surpasses pure SFT (55.2%) by 12 points and pure RL (43.5%) by 23.7 points, and comes within 3.3 points of Claude-3.5-Sonnet (70.5%) and 2.5 points of o1-Mini (69.7%). On WorkArena held-out goals, SFT+RL (35.4%) nearly matches the 70B teacher (36.0%) while the base 8B student scores only 8.3%, and exceeds GPT-4o-Mini (27.1%) by 8.3 points. The Qwen 2.5 7B variant shows the same pattern: SFT+RL (65.0%) beats SFT (57.0%) and RL (52.5%) on MiniWoB++ goals. Notably, SFT+RL is the only open-source strategy that closes the gap with closed-source models on MiniWoB++, and it achieves the peak SFT performance at only 55% of the compute cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO objective (from DeepSeek-R1) to a multi-turn web-agent setting with per-goal advantage normalization, and positions its contribution as the first statistically grounded study of compute allocation between SFT and on-policy RL for LLM web agents. It builds on the broader line of work on multi-step web agents (WebRL, SWEET-RL) and the reproducibility concerns raised by Agarwal et al. and Hochlehnert et al., but departs from them by replacing infeasible exhaustive hyperparameter sweeps with a bootstrap-resampling protocol over 1,370 random-search configurations. The two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline itself is standard; the novelty lies in the statistical diagnosis of how optimal hyperparameters shift across SFT compute budgets and in quantifying the compute-performance Pareto frontier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a reproducible, budget-aware playbook for smaller labs to train competitive open-source web agents without scaling model size, and the finding that early SFT-to-RL branching matches peak SFT performance at 55% compute is directly actionable. The hyperparameter sensitivity results (curriculum learning becoming counterproductive after warm-start, optimal discount rate shifting from ~0.9 to ~0.5, importance ratio helping only post-SFT) challenge the assumption that a single set of RL hyperparameters works across training stages. Key open questions remain: 14 of 33 WorkArena tasks stay completely unsolved even after extensive training, the shortcut-seeking behavior (agents editing their own environment) reveals sandboxing gaps, and the findings are limited to English-language interfaces and the 8B–70B Llama/Qwen range, leaving unclear whether the compute trade-offs hold at larger scales.
