## Problem

While multiple normalization schemes (Post-LN, Pre-LN, Mix-LN, Peri-LN, nGPT, sqrt-scaling) are known to affect training stability and depth behavior in transformers, no unified theoretical framework explains how they shape the forward evolution of token representations. The paper addresses the question of how normalization schemes influence deep representations, connecting the observed curse of depth and representation collapse to the specific speed-regulation behavior each scheme imposes on token dynamics.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a sequence of n token embeddings X ∈ R^{d×n} and a chosen normalization scheme, then tracks the directional components θ_j(t) = x_j(t)/‖x_j(t)‖ on the sphere S^{d-1} as they evolve through layers. The output is a characterization of each scheme's speed-regulation factor s_j(t), initial and terminal angular velocities, and intra-cluster variance decay rate. A key structural detail is that all six schemes are shown to share the same underlying attention velocity field, differing only in how they modulate the speed at which tokens traverse it, enabling direct side-by-side comparison.

## Method

The paper recasts all six normalization schemes as interacting particle systems on the sphere governed by a common normalized attention ODE (NA) with scheme-dependent speed-regulation factors s_j(t), derived by decomposing each layer update into radial and tangential components. Using a generalized Lojasiewicz inequality for time-dependent modulated gradient flows, it proves asymptotic clustering to a single token for all schemes under generic initializations. For quantitative comparison, it derives closed-form ODEs under symmetric orthogonal initialization (Theorem 4.1) and analyzes pre-clustered cone configurations (Theorem 4.3), showing that Post-LN and sqrt-scaling cluster exponentially while Pre-LN, Peri-LN, and Mix-LN decay polynomially as ~1/t^C. The framework identifies Peri-LN as particularly effective because it simultaneously achieves order-one initial angular displacement (like Post-LN) and polynomial terminal slowdown (like Pre-LN), making good use of both early and deep layers without requiring tunable parameters.

## Results

The paper establishes that all six normalization schemes (Post-LN, Pre-LN, Mix-LN, Peri-LN, nGPT, sqrt-scaling) admit asymptotic clustering of token directions on the sphere (Theorem 3.1). The key differentiator is the clustering rate: Post-LN and sqrt-scaling collapse tokens at an exponential rate (dVar/dt = −Θ(Var) and −Θ(Var/√t) respectively), while Pre-LN, Peri-LN, and Mix-LN slow to a polynomial rate (dVar/dt = −Θ(Var/t)), and nGPT offers tunable control via α_t (Theorem 4.3). At initialization, Peri-LN and nGPT move tokens order-one angular distances on the hypersphere, whereas Post-LN and Pre-LN advance only O(log n/d) per layer (Theorem 4.2). The paper identifies Peri-LN as the scheme that simultaneously achieves fast early-layer movement and slow late-layer collapse, making best use of both shallow and deep layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six normalization schemes as speed-regulation mechanisms on a common spherical particle system. Post-LN achieves the fastest initial velocity but suffers exponential terminal decay (γ̇ ~ e^{−2t}), leading to rapid representation collapse in deep layers—the 'curse of depth.' Pre-LN and Mix-LN trade a slower initial step (damped by r₀) for polynomial terminal decay (γ̇ ~ 1/t³), making them more resistant to collapse but underutilizing early layers. Peri-LN is identified as the clear winner: it matches nGPT's fast initial angular displacement (order-one on the sphere vs. O(log n/d) for Post-LN/Pre-LN) while also exhibiting the same polynomial 1/t³ terminal slowdown as Pre-LN, thus exploiting both shallow and deep layers effectively. nGPT offers the most flexibility through its learnable α_t parameter, which can be tuned to control both initial speed and terminal clustering rate, but requires additional architectural design. sqrt-scaling provides an intermediate decay rate (e^{−4√t}/√t) that is slower than Post-LN's exponential but faster than Pre-LN's polynomial, positioning it between the two extremes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interacting-particle-on-the-sphere framework of Geshkovski et al. (2025), which was originally developed exclusively for Post-LN, to a unified treatment of six normalization schemes (Post-LN, Pre-LN, Mix-LN, Peri-LN, nGPT, sqrt-scaling) by recasting each as a distinct speed-regulation factor acting on a common velocity field. It builds on the gradient-flow and Lojasiewicz convergence machinery from Geshkovski et al. (2025) and Criscitiello et al. (2024) for Post-LN, and on the empirical motivation from Sun et al. (2025) on the curse of depth and Noci et al. (2022) on signal propagation. The key conceptual departure is showing that all schemes share the same underlying attention dynamics on the sphere and differ only in how they modulate particle speed, enabling direct side-by-side comparison of initial and terminal velocities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled theoretical explanation for why Pre-LN and Peri-LN resist representation collapse in deep layers (polynomial vs. exponential clustering decay) and why Peri-LN in particular is effective: it combines fast initial-layer movement with slow deep-layer convergence, making good use of both early and late layers. This validates the empirical choice of Peri-LN in Gemma-3 and offers a concrete criterion (large initial and terminal velocities) for designing future normalization schemes. Key limitations include the restriction to tied Q=K=V=I_d, omission of FFN layers, and lack of gradient-flow analysis, leaving the intermediate regime and optimization pathologies uncharacterized. Open questions include extending the analysis to untied weights, multi-head attention, and stochastic settings where radial growth is √t rather than linear.
