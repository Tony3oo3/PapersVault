## Problem

Exact recovery of a low-rank matrix from noisy partial observations has been achieved by Abbe et al. (2017) and Bhardwaj et al. (2023), but both require an extra spectral assumption beyond the standard three (low rank, incoherence, sufficient sampling): either a small condition number or large gaps between consecutive singular values. These two assumptions are mathematically contradictory—one demands singular values be close together, the other far apart—making it natural to conjecture neither is necessary. This paper proves that conjecture, showing exact recovery is possible under only the three basic assumptions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input the noisy observed matrix A_{Ω,Z} (entries of A+Z at randomly sampled positions Ω with density p), the discretization unit ε (entries of A must be integer multiples of ε), an upper bound r_max ≥ rank(A), and known parameters K_A, K_Z bounding the signal and noise magnitudes. It outputs the exact recovered matrix A via a rounding step, with the intermediate product being a truncated SVD approximation Â_s whose entries are guaranteed to be within ε/3 of the true entries. The sampling model is independent Bernoulli with density p, and the noise Z has independent mean-zero entries with bounded moments.

## Method

The algorithm (Approximate-and-Round 2) performs three steps: empirical rescaling by the estimated sampling density p̂, a truncated SVD where the truncation index s is chosen as the largest index below r_max where the singular value gap σ̂_s − σ̂_{s+1} exceeds a threshold proportional to (K_A+K_Z)√(r_max(m+n)/p̂), and rounding each entry to the nearest multiple of ε. The core theoretical contribution is a new infinity-norm version of the Davis-Kahan perturbation theorem (Theorem 3.1), proved via a contour integral argument using Stieltjes transforms of the symmetrized matrix, combined with novel semi-isotropic bounds on powers of the random perturbation matrix that exploit entry independence. This approach avoids both the condition number assumption (which arises in Abbe et al.'s block-matrix analysis) and the singular value gap assumption (which is needed for the stronger Davis-Kahan variant used by Bhardwaj et al.), yielding a bound on ‖Ã_s − A_s‖_∞ that depends only on incoherence, rank, and sampling density. The computational cost is O(|Ω|r + mn) FLOPs, dominated by a single truncated SVD.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2.3) guarantees that under only the three basic assumptions (low rank, incoherence, sufficient sampling density), the Approximate-and-Round 2 algorithm recovers every entry of A within absolute error ε/3 with probability 1−O(N⁻¹), requiring sampling density p ≥ C(1/m+1/n) max{log⁴N, r³K²/ε²(1+μ₀²/log²N)} log⁶N. In the base case (r=O(1), K=O(1), μ₀=O(log N)), this reduces to p ≥ C max{log⁴N, ε⁻²}(m⁻¹+n⁻¹) log⁶N, which is optimal in the power of N. The algorithm runs in O(|Ω|r+mn) FLOPs, and in the largest experiment (m=n=2¹⁶≈6000, p=0.1), every entry is recovered to within absolute error 0.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method matches the computational complexity O(|Ω|r+mn) of the two prior exact-recovery methods (Abbe & Fan [24] and Bhardwaj et al. [25]) while eliminating the extra spectral assumptions they require. Abbe & Fan need the condition number κ=σ₁/σ_r to be small (singular values close together), while Bhardwaj et al. need all consecutive singular value gaps to be large (singular values far apart)—assumptions that are mathematically contradictory and both fail on real data like the Yale face database (κ≈10.45 for the first 30 singular values, with some small gaps). The convex optimization approach of Candès & Plan [12] and the gradient-descent method of Keshavan et al. [17] do not achieve entry-wise (exact) recovery at all, only approximate RMSE guarantees, and the latter also requires a small condition number. The paper's key advantage is thus not a numerical improvement in error bounds but the removal of both contradictory spectral assumptions, making the method applicable to a strictly broader class of matrices while retaining the same fast single-step truncated SVD pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exact-recovery-from-noisy-entries line initiated by Abbe et al. [24] and Bhardwaj and Vu [25], both of which achieved entry-wise recovery via truncated SVD plus rounding but required an extra spectral assumption (small condition number or large singular-value gaps, respectively). It also builds on the contour-integral perturbation framework of Tran and Vu [27], adapting it from the spectral norm to the infinity norm through a new semi-isotropic bound on powers of random matrices. Conceptually, the paper departs from the convex-optimization (nuclear norm minimization) and iterative (alternating projection, gradient descent) paradigms by showing that a single truncated SVD step suffices, provided the three basic assumptions (low rank, incoherence, sufficient sampling) hold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a genuine tension in the literature: the two prior exact-recovery methods for noisy data required contradictory spectral conditions (singular values close together vs. far apart), and this paper shows neither is needed, substantially widening the class of matrices to which fast exact recovery applies. The new infinity-norm Davis-Kahan theorem and the contour-integral-plus-semi-isotropic-bounds proof technique are reusable tools likely to find applications beyond matrix completion. Limitations include logarithmic factors in the sample complexity that the authors acknowledge could be tightened, a requirement for bounded (or light-tailed) noise, and the finite-precision (discretization) assumption on the ground-truth entries. Open questions include whether the approach extends to correlated sampling models and whether the log factors can be made optimal.
