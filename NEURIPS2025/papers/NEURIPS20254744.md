## Problem

Current world models operate in separate silos: physical models (MBRL, video generation, 3D occupancy) capture objective dynamics but treat agents as simple reactive entities, while social models (ToM, LLM agents, MBMARL) capture interactions but lack grounding in material reality. This systemic separation creates a fundamental 'integration gap' where neither dimension alone can predict real-world phenomena—such as urban traffic, human-robot collaboration, or crisis response—where physical laws and social agency are inextricably entangled through continuous bidirectional feedback.

## Input / Output

The proposed WM_P-S framework consumes a composite world state s = (s_phy, s_soc), where s_phy decomposes into an environment state s_phy^env and per-agent physical states {s_phy^i}, and s_soc decomposes into individual social attributes {s_soc^i} (beliefs, goals, emotions) and pairwise inter-agent relationships {s_soc^{ij}} (trust, power, norms). The output is a joint state transition T(s'|s) that must inherently model bidirectional coupling—physical evolution is continuously modulated by social state and vice versa—rather than being a separable sum of independent T_phy and T_soc, with the entanglement formalized as I(s_phy; s_soc) > 0.

## Method

As a position paper, the core contribution is a principled conceptual framework rather than a specific trained model. The authors formalize WM_P-S = ⟨N, S, T⟩ with an explicit state-space decomposition and propose three synergistic ACE Principles: (A) multi-level Abstraction of Social Complexity to handle dimensionless, heterogeneous social constructs; (C) Contingent Causality to capture mutable, context-sensitive social causal rules that differ from immutable physical laws; and (E) Entangled System Co-evolution to model how physical affordances shape social cognition and social states drive physical actions in a recursive loop. A three-tier hierarchical evaluation protocol (Perceptual Fidelity → Disentangled Dynamics → Entangled Dynamics) is introduced, with the top tier probing bidirectional socio-physical causality through counterfactuals like 'if anxiety rises in a crowd, how does movement change?' The paper further outlines a research roadmap spanning rich multimodal data curation, hybrid neuro-symbolic architectures with modular physical-social components, meta-learning for non-stationary social rules, and multi-tier evaluation metrics incorporating federated learning and ethical audits.

## Results

This is a position paper and does not report quantitative experimental results. It proposes the ACE Principles (Abstraction, Contingent Causality, Entangled emergence) and a conceptual WM_{P-S} framework for unifying physical and social world models, along with a three-tier hierarchical evaluation protocol (Perceptual Fidelity → Disentangled Dynamics → Entangled Dynamics) and a research roadmap. No benchmarks are evaluated and no performance metrics are reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper carves new conceptual ground by reframing the entire world-modeling literature through a physical-social duality lens, arguing that the field's dominant siloed approach—where MBRL/video-generation models handle physical dynamics and ToM/LLM-agent systems handle social dynamics—constitutes a fundamental incompleteness rather than a complementary division of labor. It draws on cognitive science (Craik's world-model concept), sociology (Bandura's social cognitive theory), and systems theory to motivate the ACE Principles, but does not build on a specific prior method; instead, it reorganizes and critiques existing work (Dreamer, Stable Video Diffusion, ToMnet, Generative Agents, OccWorld, etc.) to expose the integration gap. The proposed WM_P-S formalism and three-tier hierarchical evaluation protocol are novel organizational contributions that repurpose ideas from multi-agent RL, neuro-symbolic AI, and social physics into a unified predictive framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine and underappreciated structural gap: physical world models cannot predict what socially-situated agents will choose to do, while social models lack grounding in material constraints, and neither can capture the bidirectional feedback loops that define real-world complexity. The ACE Principles and WM_P-S formulation provide a concrete vocabulary and mathematical skeleton (joint state space S = S_phy × S_soc, entangled transition T) that could guide the next generation of integrated architectures. However, as a position paper with no empirical instantiation, the central open questions remain: how to learn the entangled transition function T in practice, how to handle the representational chasm between continuous physical quantities and dimensionless social constructs, and how to evaluate integrated models when no suitable benchmark yet exists. The three-tier evaluation protocol is a useful starting point but remains aspirational.
