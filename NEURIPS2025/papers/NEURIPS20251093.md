## Problem

Existing multi-task VRP solvers either require costly retraining per variant or use unified architectures that fail to exploit the compositional structure of VRP variants, each of which derives from a shared set of basis variants. This paper addresses the unexplored potential of reusing specialized basis solvers within a unified solver, arguing that the exponential growth of VRP variants can be mitigated by linearly scaling the number of basis experts rather than training a new solver for every combination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251093/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes VRP instances defined as graphs with N nodes (N=50 or 100), where each node carries coordinates, and customers carry linehaul/backhaul demands, optional time windows, and the instance specifies which of four additional constraints (open route, backhaul, duration limit, time window) are active. The solver autoregressively decodes a feasible tour (a sequence of subtours each starting and ending at the depot) via masked attention, producing a total Euclidean tour cost as the optimization objective. Evaluation uses greedy multi-start rollouts with 8× augmentations over 1K test instances per variant.

## Method

The paper introduces a State-Decomposable MDP (SDMDP) that reformulates VRPs by expressing the state space as a Cartesian product of basis state spaces, yielding optimal basis policies inherently. A Latent Space-based SDMDP (LS-SDMDP) extension then reuses these basis policies in embedding space via a mixture function, with a theorem proving recovery of the optimal unified policy under mild assumptions. For implementation, MoSES realizes basis policies as specialized LoRA experts fine-tuned from a frozen CVRP-pretrained backbone using a proposed Gated-LoRA mechanism that suppresses task-irrelevant features, and implements the mixture function as an adaptive gating mechanism (softmax, norm_softplus, or sigmoid) that computes per-layer, per-token coefficients over the frozen experts plus a trainable residual LoRA module. Training proceeds in three stages: pretraining the backbone on CVRP, fine-tuning four LoRA experts on OVRP/VRPB/VRPL/VRPTW, and training the unified gating on all 16 variants via REINFORCE.

## Results

Across all 16 VRP variants at N=50, MoSES(RF) achieves an average optimality gap of 1.535%, improving over its backbone RF-TE (2.063%) by 25.6% relatively, while MoSES(CaDA) reduces the average gap from 1.715% (CaDA) to 1.515% (11.7% improvement). At N=100, MoSES(RF) achieves 2.782% average gap vs. RF-TE's 3.125% (11.0% relative improvement), and MoSES(CaDA) achieves 2.631% vs. CaDA's 2.766% (4.9% improvement). The time overhead is modest: MoSES(RF) requires 5.8s per 1K instances at N=50 (vs. 2.0s for RF-TE), and MoSES(CaDA) requires 7.4s (vs. 2.3s for CaDA), remaining at the microsecond level per instance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoSES(RF) consistently outperforms its backbone RF-TE across all 16 VRP variants, with the largest gains on complex multi-constraint variants (e.g., OVRPBTW: 3.181% vs. 3.798% gap at N=50). MoSES(CaDA) similarly dominates CaDA on 13/16 tasks at N=100 and all 16 at N=50, achieving the best average gap among all neural solvers. Against the other unified neural solvers (MTPOMO, MVMoE, RF-POMO, RF-MoE), both MoSES variants achieve substantially lower gaps—e.g., on OVRPBTW at N=50, MoSES(CaDA) reaches 3.193% vs. MTPOMO's 4.699% and MVMoE's 4.349%. Traditional solvers (HGS-PyVRP, OR-Tools) produce the best solution quality but require 10.4–20.8 minutes per instance versus seconds for neural methods, making MoSES the best quality-to-speed tradeoff among learned solvers. The key differentiator is that MoSES reuses explicitly specialized basis solvers (via LoRA experts) rather than learning implicit specialization, yielding interpretable gating behavior where the mechanism assigns higher scores to basis solvers matching the active VRP constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-task VRP solver line (MTPOMO, MVMoE, RouteFinder, CaDA) by explicitly incorporating off-the-shelf basis solvers as LoRA experts, rather than learning implicit specialization through mixture-of-experts or attribute composition. It draws on the Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts (MoLE) paradigm from NLP, adapting it to combinatorial optimization with a novel Gated-LoRA mechanism that suppresses task-misaligned backbone features during expert fine-tuning. The State-Decomposable MDP (SDMDP) framework is a new theoretical reformulation that provides the mathematical justification (via Theorems 1–2) for why reusing optimal basis policies in latent space provably recovers the optimal unified policy, a connection not made by prior multi-task VRP work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional structure of VRP variants (16 variants from 5 basis constraints) is a natural property that prior unified solvers underexploited; MoSES's linear scaling of specialized experts versus exponential variant growth offers a practical path to handling many more constraints. The scale-dependent finding that frozen basis experts dominate at N=50 while the trainable residual expert dominates at N=100 suggests a useful inductive bias for when to invest capacity in holistic vs. specialized knowledge. Limitations include the strong bijectivity and policy-equivalence assumptions underlying the theory, the ~3× inference overhead from layer-wise token-wise routing, and evaluation restricted to 16 variants with 5 constraints; extending the framework to broader decision-making settings and designing more efficient aggregation remain open.
