## Problem

Existing RPCA-based LLM compression methods rely on uniform sparsity allocation or manually designed heuristics to distribute pruning across layers, ignoring the significant variation in weight redundancy and rank structure across different layers and matrices. Finding the globally optimal combination of layer-wise sparsity and matrix-wise retained rank is extremely challenging due to the vast combinatorial search space and limited computational resources available post-training. No prior method jointly learns both allocation dimensions within a principled optimization framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (Phi-3-mini, Llama2-7B/13B, Llama3-8B), a small calibration set from C4 (batch size 32, sequence length 256), and a target overall prune rate ρ. It produces compressed weights where each matrix is decomposed into a low-rank factor pair (U, V) and a sparse residual S, along with learned per-layer sparsity ratios s_i and per-matrix rank ratios κ_{i,j}. The final compressed model is obtained by applying RPCA under the converged mean parameters (μ_s, μ_κ) without further sampling, yielding a model that can be deployed with structured low-rank + sparse inference for up to 2× CPU speedup.

## Method

The paper proposes a bilevel optimization framework where the outer loop learns sparsity and rank allocation as a probabilistic optimization problem, and the inner loop solves the resulting RPCA subproblems. For the outer loop, a truncated Gaussian prior N_γ(μ, σ²I) parameterizes the allocation distribution, and a policy gradient estimator with a control variate updates the mean μ without backpropagating through the compressed model, avoiding expensive implicit differentiation. The truncation to [μ−γ, μ+γ] provides bounded support and prevents gradient explosion, while γ is annealed over training to progressively tighten the search. For the inner loop, an adapted QR-based RPCA algorithm replaces costly SVD with QR decomposition of (W−S_t)V_t^T, reducing per-iteration complexity from O(mn·min(m,n)) to O(mr²) and achieving ~75× wall-clock speedup (4 min vs. 5 h for 80 iterations). The two components are integrated in Algorithm 1, where each iteration samples two allocation schemes, compresses the model under both, computes the variance-reduced policy gradient, and projects the updated μ onto the feasible set C = {μ: 1^T μ_s ≥ ρL, μ ∈ [0,1]^{L+LM}}.

## Results

At 50% sparsity, the proposed bilevel framework achieves 9.23 WikiText2 perplexity, 53.79 MMLU, and 67.92% zero-shot accuracy on Llama2-13B, preserving over 95% of the dense model's zero-shot performance (68.72%). On Phi-3-mini at 50% sparsity, it reaches 14.87 PPL and 60.57 MMLU, outperforming the best RPCA baseline OATS (15.18 PPL, 59.99 MMLU). The QR-based inner solver completes 80 iterations in ~4 minutes versus ~5 hours for SVD-based RPCA, a 75× speedup, while the full framework delivers up to 1.99× CPU inference speedup on Llama2-13B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: SparseGPT and Wanda (unstructured pruning), OATS (RPCA with uniform sparsity), and QR (the paper's QR-based inner solver alone, without bilevel allocation). The proposed method wins on all three metrics (PPL, MMLU, zero-shot) across all four models at every prune rate tested (30%, 40%, 50%). Against the closest RPCA competitor OATS, the gains are consistent but moderate: at 50% sparsity on Llama2-13B, Ours improves PPL from 9.49 to 9.23 and MMLU from 52.44 to 53.79. Against unstructured methods, the advantage is larger—on Llama2-7B at 50% sparsity, Ours achieves 46.10 MMLU versus 41.94 (SparseGPT) and 37.16 (Wanda). The QR-only baseline (no bilevel allocation) already matches OATS in quality while running 75× faster (4 min vs. 5 h for 80 iterations), confirming that the bilevel framework's contribution is primarily in adaptive allocation rather than the solver itself. Under high sparsity (60–70%), the learned allocation also outperforms the heuristic OWL method, e.g., reducing Phi-3-mini PPL from 462.67 (OWL) to 208.7 at 70% sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RPCA-based compression paradigm (OATS, LoRAP, LoSparse) that decomposes weight matrices into sparse and low-rank components, and on the growing line of work on non-uniform sparsity allocation (OWL, DSA, ALS, FLAP). It departs from these by replacing heuristic or metric-based allocation rules with a learnable probabilistic formulation: a bilevel optimization where the outer loop uses policy gradient over a truncated Gaussian to jointly learn layer-wise sparsities and matrix-wise rank ratios, while the inner loop solves the resulting RPCA subproblems. The inner solver itself adapts a QR-based low-rank fitting scheme from classical matrix analysis [42], replacing the expensive SVD iterations used by prior RPCA solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the QR-based inner loop is roughly 75× faster than SVD-based RPCA (4 min vs. 5 h for 80 iterations), making iterative allocation search feasible on a single GPU, and the learned allocation consistently outperforms uniform and outlier-based heuristics across Phi-3, Llama-2, and Llama-3 at multiple sparsity levels. The framework's generality—demonstrated by plugging it into Wanda and SparseGPT—suggests it could serve as a reusable allocation layer for diverse compression pipelines. Open questions include scalability to 70B+ models, whether the learned allocation transfers across downstream tasks or requires re-optimization, and how the method behaves under even higher sparsity (>70%) where all methods degrade sharply.
