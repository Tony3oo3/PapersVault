## Problem

Current reasoning-based MLLMs confine their chain-of-thought to the textual domain with only a single static grounding of the visual input at the start. This paradigm fails on tasks requiring dynamic, iterative, and fine-grained interaction with specific visual regions—such as sequentially verifying hypotheses against image details or tracking object states across spatial cues—because the model cannot decide when to look again, where to focus, or how to weave the resulting visual evidence back into its evolving reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (constrained to 3,136–1,605,632 pixels) and a text question. During inference, it generates an interleaved chain-of-thought that alternates between free-form textual reasoning and structured crop commands in JSON format ({"bbox_2d": [x1, y1, x2, y2]}). Each crop command triggers a system-level operation that extracts the specified region, applies a dynamic zoom factor (piecewise-linear from 1.0× to 2.0× based on the bbox-to-image area ratio), re-encodes the result into visual tokens, and appends it to the model's context before generation resumes. The process terminates when the model emits a final answer within <answer> tags.

## Method

VLM-R³ is built on Qwen2.5-VL 7B (frozen vision encoder) and trained in two stages. First, a cold-start supervised fine-tuning on the newly curated VLIR dataset (11,810 samples from GQA, TextVQA, DocVQA, InfographicsVQA, and VSR) provides step-level supervision on region selection and textual justification, with quality filtering via a smaller VLM for semantic-unit validity and a text LLM for logical coherence. Second, Region-Conditioned Reinforcement Policy Optimization (R-GRPO) extends GRPO by masking the policy gradient to only model-generated tokens (text and bbox commands) while excluding environment-injected image tokens from the action space, and by decomposing the trajectory reward into accuracy, format, region-validity (0.5 per valid non-redundant bbox, gated on correct answer), and reasoning-length components. This design lets the model learn a state-dependent policy for when and where to acquire visual evidence, rather than merely imitating fixed crop trajectories.

## Results

VLM-R³ (7B, built on Qwen2.5-VL) achieves 70.4% on MathVista, 30.2% on MathVision, 62.2% on MMMU, 2432 on MME, 87.9% on ScienceQA, 96.8% on DocVQA, and 62.0% on HallusionBench. The largest gain over its base model is on ScienceQA (+14.3 pp, 87.9% vs. 73.6%), followed by MathVision (+5.1 pp) and MathVista (+2.2 pp). On vision-centric benchmarks (Table 2), VLM-R³ reaches an average of 70.1%, surpassing the base model (63.5%) and larger open-source models such as InternVL3-38B and LLaVA-OneVision-72B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its base model Qwen2.5-VL 7B, VLM-R³ improves on every reported benchmark, with the most dramatic gain on ScienceQA (87.9% vs. 73.6%, +14.3 pp) and MathVision (30.2% vs. 25.1%, +5.1 pp). Among open-source reasoning MLLMs, VLM-R³ outperforms Mulberry 7B on MathVista (70.4 vs. 63.1) and HallusionBench (62.0 vs. 54.1), and surpasses R1-OneVision 7B on MathVista (70.4 vs. 64.1) and MathVision (30.2 vs. 29.9); however, Vision-R1 7B edges ahead on MathVista (73.5 vs. 70.4). Compared to closed-source non-reasoning models, VLM-R³ beats GPT-4o on MME (2432 vs. 2328), ScienceQA (87.9 vs. 66.2), DocVQA (96.8 vs. 91.1), and HallusionBench (62.0 vs. 56.2), but trails Gemini-2 Flash on MathVista (70.4 vs. 73.1), MathVision (30.2 vs. 41.3), and MMMU (62.2 vs. 71.7). Against the larger closed-source o1 model, VLM-R³ remains competitive on MathVista (70.4 vs. 71.8) but is substantially behind on MathVision (30.2 vs. 63.2) and MMMU (62.2 vs. 77.6), highlighting the gap that remains with the strongest proprietary reasoning systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLM-R³ extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath [46] to the multimodal setting by conditioning the policy on dynamically acquired visual regions, rather than operating purely on text tokens. It departs from prior visual CoT work (Visual CoT [45], LLaVA-CoT [61]) where bounding boxes are either predefined or used as a single static grounding step, by making region selection a learned, iterative policy that the model invokes mid-reasoning. The VLIR dataset and the interactive crop-and-reinsert pipeline draw conceptual inspiration from O3-style interleaved reasoning [2], but instantiate it with an explicit tool-calling mechanism for spatial grounding rather than free-form image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored gap: current MLLMs perform a single static visual encoding at the start of reasoning and then lose fine-grained spatial attention as the chain grows, a limitation the authors demonstrate mechanistically via attention heatmaps. The 14.3% ScienceQA gain and the ablation showing that removing VLIR cold-start causes a 15.7% drop highlight that the region-selection policy is genuinely learned rather than trivially extractable from the base model. Open questions include whether the interactive multi-turn pipeline (which requires runtime image cropping and re-encoding) can be amortized or distilled into a single-pass model, how the approach scales beyond 7B backbones, and whether the relatively small VLIR corpus (≈12K samples) is sufficient for robust generalization to unseen spatial-reasoning domains.
