## Problem

Existing strategies for curating chain-of-thought training data to stimulate LLM reasoning rely on heuristics (e.g., filtering by CoT length or domain coverage) that lack generalizability and cannot explain fine-grained mechanisms. The cross-domain influence between math and code reasoning—how and why one domain's data improves the other—remains underexplored, and the contribution of specific cognitive behaviors (exploration, verification) and token-level patterns to learned reasoning is unclear.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fine-tuned SFT model, its full training dataset of CoT traces (sequences truncated to 4096 tokens), and a randomly sampled set of n=100 correctly answered evaluation examples used to define the surrogate objective. It produces influence scores at three granularities—instance-level (per training example), sequence-level (per sentence via counterfactual removal), and token-level (per token via autoregressive gradient decomposition)—alongside a derived difficulty-flipping reweighting strategy. The Hessian is approximated via EK-FAC restricted to MLP parameters only, with attention treated as fixed.

## Method

Infra adapts classical influence functions to reasoning-oriented SFT by replacing the non-differentiable task accuracy with a mean log-likelihood surrogate over correctly answered examples, then computing the closed-form influence of each training point on this surrogate via the EK-FAC-approximated Hessian. At the sequence level, it isolates the contribution of individual sentences (e.g., exploration or verification steps) through a counterfactual removal: the influence of sentence y is the difference between the full example's influence and the influence after erasing y. At the token level, it exploits the autoregressive decomposition of the training gradient into per-token log-likelihood terms, yielding a per-token influence score that reveals which specific tokens drive reasoning. The key empirical finding is that high-difficulty math examples boost both math and code reasoning while low-difficulty code tasks most benefit code reasoning, motivating a difficulty-flip reweighting that doubles AIME24 accuracy from 10% to 20% for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, the proposed difficulty-flipped data mixing strategy doubles AIME24 accuracy from 10.0% to 20.0% and improves LiveCodeBench from 33.8% to 35.3%, while also boosting AIME25 from 6.7% to 16.7% and MATH500 from 77.2% to 78.2%. On the larger Qwen2.5-14B model, the same strategy yields AIME24 of 23.0% (vs. 20.0% baseline), AIME25 of 23.3% (vs. 13.3%), and LiveCodeBench of 45.5% (vs. 45.3%). The reverse strategy (simplifying math, complicating code) consistently degrades performance, e.g., dropping LiveCodeBench to 30.0% on 7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but instead contrasts three data-mixing strategies under identical SFT conditions. The proposed Difficulty-Flipped strategy (replacing easy math with hard math from OpenThoughts-114k, and replacing hard code with easy code) outperforms the original Bespoke-Stratos-17k baseline on all four benchmarks for the 7B model, with the largest gain being a 10-point absolute improvement on AIME24 (10.0→20.0) and a 10-point gain on AIME25 (6.7→16.7). The reverse strategy (simplifying math, complicating code) performs worst, dropping LiveCodeBench by 3.8 points on 7B and 1.5 points on 14B, confirming that the direction of difficulty manipulation matters. On the 14B model, gains are smaller but consistent: AIME24 improves by 3 points and AIME25 by 10 points, while MATH500 and LiveCodeBench remain essentially matched or slightly improved. The sequence-level ablation (Table 2) further shows that removing exploration behaviors from CoT drops MATH500 from 77.2 to 73.8 and LiveCodeBench from 33.8 to 32.0, validating the positive role of exploratory reasoning traces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the influence functions framework of Koh & Liang (2017) and its LLM adaptation via EK-FAC (Grosse et al., 2023) from pretraining data attribution to the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase, specifically targeting reasoning capabilities in math and code. It departs from prior heuristic data-curation strategies (s1K, LIMO, Sky-T1) that rely on hand-crafted quality metrics by providing a principled, gradient-based attribution that operates at three granularities: instance, sequence, and token. The sequence-level counterfactual removal and token-level autoregressive gradient decomposition are novel extensions not present in the pretraining-focused influence function literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The difficulty-flipping reweighting strategy offers a simple, interpretable data-mixing recipe that practitioners can adopt without expensive retraining experiments, and the finding that post-correct-answer exploration (previously dismissed as 'overthinking') consistently benefits both math and code reasoning challenges a growing body of work advocating for efficiency-focused CoT pruning. Key limitations include the Hessian approximation restricted to MLP parameters, analysis confined to math and code domains, and single-run experiments without error bars, leaving open whether the cross-domain and difficulty effects generalize to larger models, other reasoning domains (e.g., commonsense, multi-step planning), or RL-based post-training regimes.
