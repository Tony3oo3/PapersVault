## Problem

Group-based RL algorithms like GRPO and RLOO achieve strong results on single-turn tasks (math, code) by computing relative advantages over groups of rollouts, but they collapse all steps within a trajectory into a single episode-level signal. For multi-turn LLM agents operating over tens of decision steps with sparse or delayed rewards, this makes credit assignment across individual actions intractable, undermining policy optimization. The paper asks whether fine-grained per-step credit can be introduced while preserving the critic-free, low-memory, and stable-convergence properties that make group-based RL practical at scale.

## Input / Output

At each decision step, the agent receives a textual environment observation (or an RGB image plus text for VLM agents) along with a task description, and generates a textual action of up to 512 tokens. Training operates on groups of N=8 complete trajectories (up to 50 steps each, ~20k tokens per episode) sampled under identical task and initial-state conditions. The output is updated policy weights; no auxiliary value network or additional rollouts are produced. For search-augmented QA, the agent additionally receives retrieved document snippets as intermediate observations across up to 4 tool-calling turns.

## Method

GiGPO introduces a two-level 'group-in-group' advantage structure. At the episode level, it computes macro relative advantages by normalizing total trajectory returns within the group (as in GRPO). At the step level, it introduces an anchor state grouping mechanism: because all N trajectories share the same task and initial state, many environment states recur across trajectories (e.g., revisiting the same webpage or room); GiGPO retroactively identifies these repeated states via lightweight hashmap lookups and groups all actions taken from the same state together, then computes micro relative advantages using discounted returns within each group. The final per-step advantage is a weighted sum A(a_t) = A^E(τ_i) + ω·A^S(a_t), plugged into a standard clipped policy optimization objective with a KL penalty. Crucially, the step-level grouping is entirely offline—requiring no extra LLM forward passes or auxiliary models—so GiGPO incurs <0.002% additional time cost over GRPO while delivering fine-grained credit signals that sharpen per-step policy learning.

## Results

On ALFWorld, GiGPO_w/o std achieves 86.1% average success rate with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and 90.2% with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, surpassing GRPO (72.8% and 77.6% respectively) by 13.3% and 12.6%. On WebShop, GiGPO_w/o std reaches 67.4% success rate (1.5B) and 75.2% (7B), outperforming GRPO (56.8% and 66.1%) by 10.6% and 9.1%. On search-augmented QA, GiGPO attains 42.1% average (3B) and 47.2% (7B), exceeding the strongest baselines (Search-R1: 32.5%/38.5%; ZeroSearch: 31.7%/39.1%). All gains are achieved with identical GPU memory, identical LLM rollouts, and <0.002% additional time cost over GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GiGPO consistently outperforms all group-based RL baselines (GRPO, RLOO) across both ALFWorld and WebShop at both 1.5B and 7B scales, with the largest margins on ALFWorld (+12.6–13.3% over GRPO) and WebShop (+9.1–10.6% over GRPO). Against PPO (which requires a separate critic network), GiGPO_w/o std surpasses it by 5.7% on ALFWorld (1.5B) and 9.8% on WebShop (7B), while being critic-free and using identical rollout budgets. On search-augmented QA, GiGPO (42.1% at 3B, 47.2% at 7B) outperforms Search-R1 (32.5%/38.5%), ZeroSearch (31.7%/39.1%), and StepSearch (multi-hop only), with particularly large gains on Bamboogle (64.1%/68.9% vs. 11.1%/27.8% for ZeroSearch). GiGPO also exceeds closed-source prompting baselines (GPT-4o: 48.0% ALFWorld, 23.7% WebShop; Gemini-2.5-Pro: 60.3%/35.9%) by wide margins, and on VLM tasks (Sokoban, EZPoints) it achieves 81.0% and 100% success versus GRPO's 67.1% and 86.9%. The only setting where GiGPO does not clearly dominate is the normalization factor choice: F_norm=std performs slightly better on easier ALFWorld subtasks (Pick, Clean) while F_norm=1 is superior on harder tasks (Look, Pick2, WebShop), suggesting task-dependent sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

GiGPO extends the group-based RL lineage (GRPO, RLOO, DAPO) from single-turn tasks to multi-turn LLM agent training, where sparse and delayed rewards make per-step credit assignment the central challenge. It departs from actor-critic methods (PPO, ArCHer, AgentQ) that require auxiliary value networks or MCTS, and from trajectory-level GRPO variants (RAGEN, LOOP) that concatenate full interaction histories, by introducing an anchor-state grouping mechanism that retroactively constructs step-level groups from repeated environment states across trajectories. The approach is explicitly orthogonal to single-turn group-based RL improvements (e.g., DAPO's dynamic sampling), as demonstrated by the GiGPO_dynamic variant, and builds on the verl-agent framework for scalable multi-turn rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GiGPO provides a near-zero-cost (<0.002% overhead) mechanism for fine-grained credit assignment in long-horizon agent training, making group-based RL practically viable for multi-turn settings without the memory and complexity penalties of critic networks. The open question of state-matching robustness in noisy or high-dimensional environments (where exact state identity is hard to detect) limits generalizability, and the authors acknowledge that embedding-based or domain-specific structural equivalence strategies are needed. The released verl-agent framework lowers the barrier for the community to experiment with multi-turn agent RL, and the tool-efficiency side effect (fewer redundant search calls) suggests broader applicability to cost-sensitive agent deployments.
