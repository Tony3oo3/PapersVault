## Problem

Existing zero-shot coordination methods either generate diverse training partners via population-based training but lack explicit test-time reasoning over partner strategy types, or perform online partner modeling but rely on limited hand-defined strategy sets without diverse synthetic training data. A key unresolved challenge is enabling an agent to rapidly adapt within a single episode to a novel partner whose strategy may shift non-stationarily, without having seen that partner during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250047/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The cooperator agent consumes a 12-channel egocentric game-state observation at each timestep along with the partner's actual action (observed one step later) to update its belief. The VAE encoder operates on 26-channel state representations over a 50-step trajectory window and predicts 50 future actions, producing an 8-dimensional latent strategy vector. The output is a discrete action from a 27-action high-level action space, conditioned on the current leading strategy cluster, alongside a continuously updated weight vector over K clusters representing the partner's strategy belief.

## Method

TALENTS trains a sequential VAE on offline trajectories from population agents (FCP, MEP, or Behavior Preference) to learn an 8-dimensional latent strategy space, then partitions it into K discrete clusters via K-Means with silhouette analysis. A cooperator agent is trained with Independent PPO, where the partner's cluster identity is injected as an additive bias vector on the actor's action logits (rather than an embedding concatenation, which proved less effective at learning distinguishable behaviors). At test time, a fixed-share tracking-regret-minimization algorithm treats each cluster as an expert: the VAE decoder predicts the partner's action from each cluster's latent mean, the prediction error against the partner's actual action updates expert weights, and the fixed-share mixing term (α=0.4) allows the belief to track partners who switch strategies mid-episode. This unifies population-based partner generation (for training) with online strategy inference (for adaptation) within a single latent space.

## Results

In agent-agent zero-shot coordination on a modified Overcooked domain, TALENTS achieves the highest team score in 3 of 4 layouts across all three training populations (FCP, MEP, BP). With the BP population, TALENTS scores 842.39 (Open), 642.94 (Hallway), 56.55 (Forced-Coord), and 647.62 (Ring), outperforming GAMMA (573.93, 513.47, 47.52, 387.58) and the population best-response baseline (469.88, 454.26, 78.53, 640.43). In the human-agent study with 119 participants, TALENTS significantly outperforms both baselines in team score (F(2,166)=5.76, p=.003), team fluency (F(2,122)=4.31, p=.02), and perceived trust (F(2,122)=3.23, p=.04).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TALENTS consistently outperforms GAMMA (Liang et al., NeurIPS 2024) across nearly all layouts and populations, with the largest gap on the Ring layout (e.g., 647.62 vs. 387.58 with the BP population, a ~67% improvement). The population best-response (BR) baseline, which trains a dedicated cooperator against each population member, is competitive on the Forced-Coordination layout (78.53 vs. 56.55 with BP) where clear role partitioning makes a single best-response policy effective, but TALENTS dominates on the other three layouts. In the additional baselines (Table A.4), TALENTS (605.88) also outperforms E3T (Yan et al., NeurIPS 2023; 548.66) and PACE (313.34), with the authors attributing PACE's underperformance to its inter-episodic adaptation design being ill-suited to the short time frame needed for high-performance zero-shot coordination. In the human-agent study, TALENTS achieves significantly higher team scores and subjective ratings (fluency, trust) than both GAMMA and BR, confirming that the advantage generalizes to unseen human partners despite training exclusively on agent-agent data.

## Relation to Prior Work

TALENTS unifies two previously separate paradigms in zero-shot coordination: population-based training (PBT) for generating diverse synthetic partners, and online strategy inference for test-time adaptation. It builds directly on GAMMA's use of a VAE to learn a latent strategy space from PBT rollouts, but departs by clustering that latent space into discrete strategy types, training a strategy-conditioned cooperator (via action biasing) rather than a single general cooperator, and replacing static belief updates with a fixed-share tracking-regret algorithm for intra-episodic adaptation. The fixed-share mechanism is borrowed from Herbster & Warmuth's tracking-the-best-expert framework, repurposed here so that each latent cluster acts as an 'expert' whose weight is updated based on prediction loss against the partner's actual actions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that intra-episodic adaptation—where a partner's strategy can shift mid-episode—is meaningfully better than static belief for human-agent coordination, supported by both the ablation (Fig. 4) and the human study showing higher team scores and subjective ratings. However, generalization is bounded by the VAE's interpolation capability: the agent can only adapt to partners whose behaviors fall within the training distribution, and the Forced Coordination layout reveals fragility when reward signals are sparse. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger strategy spaces or more complex multi-agent settings, and whether the VAE latent space can be augmented with human trajectory data at train time to close the remaining gap with human partners.
