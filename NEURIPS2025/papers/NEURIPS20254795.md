## Problem

Existing data selection methods for LLM fine-tuning rely on model-agnostic heuristics (e.g., perplexity filtering), expensive proxy model training, or costly embedding functions, and lack theoretical justification for how sample weights should be assigned relative to a target distribution. This paper addresses the gap by asking: can we derive mathematically optimal, model-specific sample weights directly from the training dynamics (gradients and Hessian) of a pre-trained model, without proxy models, while remaining computationally tractable at the scale of millions of samples?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., Llama2-7B), a large source pool (200k samples drawn from Tulu V2's ~5.8M instruction-tuning examples), and a small target set (8–500 examples from a specific task such as MMLU or GSM8k). It produces per-sample weights over the source pool, from which a fixed-size subset (e.g., 10k) is selected for fine-tuning. Unusually, the weights are computed analytically via a quadratic program rather than learned, and the sparsity level is controlled by binary-searching the L2 regularization coefficient λ to match the desired number of non-zero-weight samples. A brief warm-up phase (10k random samples) is required before gradient computation to stabilize the optimizer state.

## Method

Influence Distillation formulates each sample's influence on target loss via a second-order Taylor expansion of the loss after one gradient step, yielding a quadratic objective f(w) = −pᵀw + (η/2)wᵀQw over sample weights, where p encodes gradient inner products between source and target, and Q is a Hessian-weighted source-gradient Gram matrix. A robust variant adds L2 regularization, non-negativity, and a sum-to-n constraint to prevent overfitting to the current parameters or target set; the solution is obtained via SLSQP. For the Adam optimizer, p and Q are modified to incorporate the first- and second-moment estimates (m, v) after warm-up. To scale to large pools, a landmark-based approximation computes exact gradients for only ℓ ≪ |S| landmark samples and propagates influence to all others via a coefficient matrix C learned by Kernel Ridge Regression on JVP embeddings—projections of intermediate-layer Jacobians onto random Gaussian directions—avoiding full backward passes for every sample. Gradients are further compressed via Randomized Hadamard Transform projections, and in practice the second-order Q term is dropped (first-order approximation) since the learning rate makes it negligible.

## Results

On Llama2-7B, Influence Distillation (InfDist) achieves an average improvement of +2.30 over uniform sampling across six tasks (MMLU, GSM8k, BBH, TyDIQA, Codex, SQuAD), matching full-dataset training (+2.30) while using only 10k of 200k samples. Across four models (Llama2-7B, Llama3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-3B), InfDist outperforms the RDS+ baseline on 3 of 4 models and Mid-PPL on all 4, while requiring 2.9–3.5× less embedding+selection FLOPs (e.g., 872 TF vs. 2800 TF on Llama2-7B). On Qwen2.5-1.5B, InfDist achieves the largest average gain of +6.52 over uniform, approaching full-training performance of +7.00.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RDS+ (the state-of-the-art embedding-based method), InfDist achieves higher average improvement on 3 of 4 models: +2.30 vs. +1.85 on Llama2-7B, +6.52 vs. +4.28 on Qwen2.5-1.5B, and +0.18 vs. −0.28 on Qwen2.5-3B, while using 2.9–3.5× fewer FLOPs for embedding and selection. On Llama3.2-3B, RDS+ edges out InfDist (+1.65 vs. +0.77), though InfDist still requires only 417 TF vs. 1200 TF. Against Mid-PPL (perplexity-based filtering), InfDist wins on all four models, with Mid-PPL actually degrading below uniform on Llama2-7B (−0.65) and Qwen2.5-3B (−0.40). In a direct head-to-head with LESS (which computes exact per-sample gradients), LESS achieves a higher average gain of +3.08 vs. InfDist's +2.30 on Llama2-7B, but at roughly 10× the computational cost (8400 TF vs. 872 TF), making it impractical at scale. Overall, InfDist matches or surpasses full-dataset training on Llama2-7B (+2.30 = +2.30) and Qwen2.5-3B (+0.18 > 0.00) while selecting only 5% of the pool.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-based influence estimation framework of LESS (Xia et al., 2024) by introducing a landmark-based low-rank approximation that avoids computing exact gradients for every training sample, reducing selection cost by roughly 10×. It departs from LESS in three key ways: (1) a second-order (Hessian) formulation with closed-form optimal weights derived for both SGD and Adam, (2) a novel JVP embedding space that outperforms standard hidden-layer embeddings for the landmark coefficient regression, and (3) an L2-regularized robust objective that prevents the unconstrained solution from overfitting to the current parameters. The paper also contrasts with embedding-based methods like RDS+ (Ivison et al., 2025) and heuristic approaches like Mid-PPL (Yin and Rush, 2024), positioning itself as the first method to combine theoretical optimality guarantees with practical scalability for targeted instruction-tuning data selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Influence Distillation offers a principled, 3.5× faster alternative to LESS for targeted instruction tuning, making gradient-influence-based data selection practical at the 200k-sample scale where LESS was previously infeasible. The landmark approximation with JVP embeddings is a reusable technique that could benefit other gradient-based selection or attribution methods. Key open questions remain: the method requires a target dataset (limiting applicability to general pre-training data selection), the single static selection may be insufficient for long pre-training runs where gradients drift, and the warm-up cost—though excluded from reported runtimes—could matter in practice. Extending the framework to multi-phase or online re-selection for pre-training is identified as an important future direction.
