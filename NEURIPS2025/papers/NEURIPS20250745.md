## Problem

Prevailing video diffusion models are constrained to single-shot generation, yet real-world content creation demands multi-shot portrait videos with persistent identity consistency and per-shot content controllability. Existing workarounds—iterative personalized text-to-video or keyframe-to-video pipelines—suffer from adversarial competition between identity preservation and prompt adherence, bottleneck degradation from keyframe quality, and progressive facial drift during temporal expansion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250745/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a sequence of S formatted text prompts (each following an 8-aspect caption covering camera, attributes, hairstyle, apparel, expression, behavior, background, and lighting) along with user-specified frame counts per shot, and optionally a reference face image for personalized generation. Output is a multi-shot portrait video at 832×480 resolution with 125 total frames (~7.8 s), where shot boundaries are determined by the cumulative frame counts. The InfT2V extension fixes the first shot's noise, length, and prompt across repeated generations to produce an unbounded number of identity-consistent shots.

## Method

EchoShot is built on the Wan2.1-T2V DiT backbone and introduces two parameter-free, shot-aware Rotary Position Embedding mechanisms. TcRoPE modulates self-attention by adding a temporal phase shift (t + s·j) at each shot boundary, creating a discontinuity that lets the model recognize inter-shot cuts while supporting variable shot counts and durations. TaRoPE modulates cross-attention with a 1D-RoPE offset (s·k) that preserves full attention between a shot's visual tokens and its own caption while monotonically suppressing attention to other shots' captions, enabling precise per-shot text control without prompt intermingling. The model is trained with rectified flow loss on the newly constructed PortraitGala dataset (650k clips, 400k identities, ~1000 h), using a 1:2 mix of single-shot and multi-shot data. Two extensions—PMT2V (concatenating ArcFace + CLIP-G face embeddings into the cross-attention conditioning) and InfT2V (a RefAttn mechanism that disentangles the first shot's attention to keep it fixed as a reference)—are obtained by fine-tuning from the MT2V weights.

## Results

On the MT2V benchmark, EchoShot-1B achieves the highest identity consistency scores of 73.74% (FaceSim-Arc) and 69.43% (FaceSim-Cur), surpassing the best baseline (StoryDiffusion+Wan-14B at 68.65%/65.29%) by over 5 points. In prompt controllability, EchoShot scores 95.84% (Appearance), 88.86% (Motion), and 94.72% (Background), exceeding the strongest competitor (IC-LoRA+Wan-14B) by 8.25, 5.15, and 19.35 points respectively. Notably, EchoShot achieves these results with only 1B parameters, compared to 13–14B parameters in the baselines, while also attaining top-tier visual quality (88.91% static, 87.85% dynamic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoShot outperforms all three MT2V baselines on identity consistency and prompt controllability. Against the keyframe-based pipelines (StoryDiffusion+Wan-14B and IC-LoRA+Wan-14B), EchoShot gains 5+ points on FaceSim-Arc and 10–20 points on background controllability, while also avoiding the progressive facial degradation and prompt intermingling observed in keyframe-to-video approaches. Compared to HunyuanVideo-13B (limited to 2 shots), EchoShot leads by 8.94 points on FaceSim-Arc and 22.35 points on Background controllability, and supports arbitrary shot counts. In the PMT2V task, both ConsisID and Kling exhibit severe bias toward the reference image and fail to follow prompts (e.g., hairstyle, expressions), whereas EchoShot maintains consistent appearance with precise controllability across shots. The only metric where EchoShot does not lead is dynamic quality (87.85% vs. IC-LoRA+Wan-14B's 87.98%), a negligible 0.13-point difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoShot builds directly on the Wan2.1-T2V DiT architecture and its 3D-RoPE positional encoding, extending the RoPE mechanism with two parameter-free modifications (TcRoPE for shot-boundary delineation in self-attention, TaRoPE for shot-caption alignment in cross-attention). It departs from prior multi-shot video generation approaches—iterative personalized T2V pipelines (ConsisID, Kling) and keyframe-to-video cascades (StoryDiffusion+I2V, IC-LoRA+I2V)—by performing native joint multi-shot denoising rather than sequential or decoupled generation. Among concurrent work, it is closest in spirit to LCT (scene-level attention for multi-shot) and HunyuanVideo's two-shot caption concatenation, but differs by targeting identity-consistent portrait generation with per-shot fine-grained text control rather than general scene coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free RoPE modifications offer a lightweight, architecturally clean recipe for handling discontinuous temporal segments in DiT-based video models, a pattern likely transferable to other multi-segment generation tasks beyond portraits. PortraitGala (650k clips, 400k identities, 1000 hours) fills a concrete gap as the first multi-shot identity-consistent portrait video dataset with fine-grained structured captions. Key limitations remain: the framework cannot extend shots from prior content (no autoregressive continuation), is restricted to single-subject scenes, and the 1B model's advantage over 13–14B baselines may not generalize to multi-subject or non-portrait domains. Open questions include whether the TcRoPE/TaRoPE paradigm scales to arbitrary multi-entity scripts and whether the identity-consistency gains persist at higher resolutions or longer total durations.
