## Problem

Existing traffic signal control methods face a fundamental tension: RL-based policies are fast but rely on simplified vectorized states and static rewards that miss semantically rich cues needed for safety-critical decisions (e.g., emergency vehicle prioritization), while LLM-based approaches offer high-level reasoning but are too slow for real-time control and lack true visual grounding. Additionally, mainstream TSC simulators (SUMO, CityFlow) provide only statistical data without real-time image rendering, preventing fusion of visual perception with language-based reasoning.

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-view intersection images (a bird's-eye view plus directional views from each approach, rendered by a custom vision-enabled simulator built on SUMO) and spatio-temporal traffic state tensors (12 movements × 7 features × 5 timesteps) for the RL branch. The VLM first converts images into natural-language directional descriptions, which are then aggregated into phase-level summaries. The final output is a discrete traffic signal phase selection formatted as a JSON object for downstream execution, accompanied by a textual rationale explaining the decision.

## Method

VLMLight is a four-stage dual-branch architecture: (1) a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) processes multi-view intersection images into structured directional scene descriptions; (2) an LLM meta-controller (Qwen2.5-72B) interprets these descriptions and routes control to either a fast RL policy or a deliberative reasoning branch based on whether safety-critical conditions are detected; (3) the routine RL branch uses a hierarchical Transformer encoder (spatial self-attention over 12 movements, then temporal self-attention over 5 frames) trained with PPO to select signal phases from a discrete action set; (4) the deliberative branch engages three specialized LLM agents—Phase Reasoning (maps directional descriptions to phase-level summaries), Signal Planning (selects the optimal phase with justification), and Rule Verification (checks legality against the feasible phase set, with fallback to RL if invalid). The key architectural insight is that the meta-controller's conditional routing preserves sub-1% performance degradation in routine traffic while enabling up to 65% reduction in emergency vehicle waiting time through structured multi-agent dialogue.

## Results

VLMLight achieves the best emergency vehicle metrics across all three intersections, reducing Average Emergency Waiting Time (AEWT) by up to 65% over the best RL baseline (e.g., 7.48 s vs. 22.04 s on Songdo; 2.17 s vs. 5.17 s on Yau Ma Tei; 2.60 s vs. 7.81 s on Massy). For routine traffic, VLMLight stays within ~1–5% of the best RL methods on Average Travel Time (ATT) and Average Waiting Time (AWT) (e.g., 87.14 s vs. 86.80 s ATT on Songdo). The full deliberative reasoning pipeline completes in 11.48 s, fitting within the standard 13 s signal phase buffer (10 s green + 3 s yellow).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the five RL-based baselines (IntelliLight, UniTSA, A-CATs, 3DQN-TSCC, CCDA), VLMLight wins decisively on emergency vehicle metrics: AEWT drops from 22.04 s (UniTSA) to 7.48 s on Songdo (−66%), from 5.17 s (IntelliLight) to 2.17 s on Yau Ma Tei (−58%), and from 7.81 s (IntelliLight) to 2.60 s on Massy (−67%). On routine traffic (ATT/AWT), VLMLight is within 1–5% of the best RL methods, confirming that the meta-controller successfully defers to the fast RL branch under normal conditions. Against the three rule-based baselines (FixTime, Webster, MaxPressure), VLMLight outperforms all of them on every metric at every intersection, with the largest gaps on emergency metrics (e.g., AEWT 7.48 s vs. 35.38 s for MaxPressure on Songdo). Compared to Vanilla-VLM (which uses VLM descriptions without the dual-branch architecture), VLMLight substantially improves routine ATT (87.14 s vs. 105.48 s on Songdo; 39.80 s vs. 62.45 s on Yau Ma Tei) while also achieving better emergency metrics, demonstrating that the structured multi-agent reasoning pipeline is critical for both efficiency and safety.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLMLight sits at the convergence of three TSC research threads: rule-based heuristics (Webster, MaxPressure), RL-based controllers (IntelliLight, UniTSA, A-CATs, CCDA), and the emerging LLM-based TSC line (LLMLight, LLM-Assisted Light, iLLM-TSC). It departs from prior RL work by replacing vectorized state abstractions with VLM-generated multi-view scene descriptions, and from prior LLM-TSC work by introducing a safety-prioritized meta-controller that routes between a fast RL policy and a multi-agent deliberative reasoning branch, rather than relying on a single monolithic LLM pipeline. The first image-based multi-view TSC simulator is a new infrastructure contribution that enables the vision-language perception layer absent from SUMO/CityFlow-based evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-branch architecture (fast RL for routine traffic, structured LLM reasoning for safety-critical events) offers a practical template for deploying interpretable, safety-aware control in latency-constrained settings, and the 65% reduction in emergency vehicle waiting time is a meaningful operational gain. However, the framework is validated only on single intersections under clear-weather conditions, and the 11.5 s inference latency sits at the edge of the 13 s signal-phase buffer, leaving little margin for network variability or multi-intersection coordination. Open questions include whether the VLM scene-understanding layer is truly necessary versus a lightweight vehicle detector, how the meta-controller generalizes to unseen intersection topologies, and whether the multi-agent deliberation can scale to coordinated network-level control.
