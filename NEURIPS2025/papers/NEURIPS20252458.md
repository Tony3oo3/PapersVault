## Problem

Existing medical LLM benchmarks suffer from data contamination (sourced from public textbooks and exams), lack multi-departmental coverage, rely on multiple-choice formats that diverge from real clinical practice, and provide no end-to-end evaluation of the full patient-visit-to-discharge diagnostic workflow. Consequently, there is no reliable way to measure whether LLMs or medical agents can actually perform the sequential, multi-specialty reasoning that real hospitals require.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252458/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each case is a real de-identified medical record (~1000 tokens) from a Chinese Grade 3A hospital, containing structured fields: chief complaint, medical history, physical examination, imaging reports (CT, MRI, X-ray, ultrasound), and laboratory reports (blood, urine, CSF). The 8 tasks are organized into three functional groups — department guide (selecting from a fixed list of 24 departments), clinical diagnosis (six chain-of-thought sub-tasks from preliminary diagnosis through treatment plan), and imaging diagnosis — all evaluated as generative QA rather than multiple choice. The benchmark spans 150 diseases × 10 cases each (1500 samples) across 24 departments, with a standardized medical-synonym list applied before computing BLEU/ROUGE/BertScore to handle terminology variation.

## Method

ClinicalAgent is a six-stage multi-agent pipeline (departmental guide → preliminary consultation → laboratory examination → imageological examination → final consultation → medical treatment) that dynamically schedules K relevant departments and assigns N clinicians per department, where each clinician is powered by the LLM that empirically performs best in that specific departmental domain (e.g., GPT-4 for pediatrics, InternLM2 for orthopedics, Gemini-Pro for hematology). A dedicated patient-navigator agent (Yi-Chat, chosen for near-perfect instruction-following and minimal hallucination) selects departments from the chief complaint using prior-knowledge rankings from the benchmark. A lead clinician aggregates independent diagnoses, test recommendations, and treatment plans from all team members into a unified output. The key design insight is that no single LLM dominates all 24 departments, so a best-of-breed per-department allocation outperforms any monolithic model, mirroring how real hospitals assemble multidisciplinary teams. Four novel metrics (Department Win Rate, Department Instruction Following Rate, Comprehensive Diagnostic Rate, and Acceptability) quantify performance across both task and department dimensions.

## Results

On ClinicalBench, the best single LLM (InternLM2-20B-Chat) achieves an overall Avg. score of 51.98, DWR of 14.91, and CDR of 31.40, while GPT-4 leads on CDR (33.27) and Acceptability (11.37). The proposed ClinicalAgent variants substantially outperform all individual LLMs: Agent#1@3 achieves the highest Human Score (62.76) and CDR (54.00), while Agent#3@1 reaches the best Acceptability (17.82) and CDR (52.73). Under GPT-4o evaluation, GPT-4 scores highest (90.93) likely due to self-preference bias, but ClinicalAgent (Agent#1@3) remains competitive at 89.40. Notably, general-purpose LLMs consistently outperform specialized medical LLMs (e.g., DISC-MedLLM scores only 12.87 Avg., BianQue-2 scores 4.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 17 LLMs (11 general-purpose and 6 medical-specialized) on ClinicalBench. Among general LLMs, InternLM2-20B-Chat leads overall (Avg. 51.98, DWR 14.91), GPT-4 excels in end-to-end correctness (CDR 33.27, Acceptability 11.37), and Gemini-Pro performs best on diagnostic basis extraction (DB 50.59). Specialized medical LLMs (HuatuoGPT2, WiNGPT2, PULSE-20B, Taiyi-LLM, DISC-MedLLM, BianQue-2) all underperform the top general LLMs, with DISC-MedLLM (Avg. 12.87) and BianQue-2 (Avg. 4.50) scoring near zero on most generative tasks. ClinicalAgent variants outperform every individual LLM on CDR (52.73–54.00 vs. max 33.27 for GPT-4) and Acceptability (17.82–18.22 vs. max 11.37 for GPT-4), with Agent#1@3 achieving the best Human Score (62.76 vs. 58.84 for Gemini-Pro). Under GPT-4o evaluation, GPT-4 scores highest (90.93) due to documented self-preference bias, but Agent#1@3 remains within 1.5 points (89.40). The paper also shows that no single LLM dominates across all 24 departments, motivating the multi-departmental agent design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of medical LLM evaluation, which has relied on multiple-choice questions sourced from public examinations and textbooks (MedQA, CMB-Exam, MMedBench), by introducing a real-case-based, end-to-end, multi-departmental benchmark that eliminates data leakage risk. On the agent side, it extends the multi-agent medical reasoning line (MedAgent, MDAgents, Agent Hospital) — all of which were designed around or validated only on multiple-choice tasks — to a full generative diagnostic pipeline that mirrors actual hospital workflows from patient intake through discharge. The ClinicalAgent architecture repurposes the 'best model per domain' idea from ensemble methods into a department-specific clinician assignment strategy, but grounds it in empirically measured per-department LLM rankings rather than fixed role assignments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ClinicalBench fills a critical gap as the first data-leakage-free, end-to-end, multi-departmental clinical diagnostic benchmark, and is likely to become a standard evaluation tool for medical LLMs given its coverage of 24 departments and 150 diseases. The finding that general-purpose LLMs consistently outperform specialized medical LLMs on real clinical tasks is a sobering result that challenges the assumption that domain-specific fine-tuning is sufficient. Key open questions include whether the China-specific diagnostic protocols generalize to other healthcare systems, how RAG and few-shot techniques might close the performance gap, and whether the multi-agent department-routing strategy scales to more complex multi-morbidity cases.
