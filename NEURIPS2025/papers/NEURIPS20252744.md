## Problem

Diffusion-based relighting methods operate in semantic latent space where proximity does not guarantee physical correctness in the visual domain, producing artifacts like misaligned shadows, overexposed highlights, and incorrect light directions. Additionally, existing approaches lack structured illumination descriptions and dedicated metrics that capture lighting-specific errors such as shadow misalignment or intensity mismatch, making fine-grained controllability and evaluation difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252744/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an input image or video (480p video at 832×480 with 49 frames, or 768×512 images), a target lighting condition expressed as a text prompt, reference image, or reference video, and a binary subject mask specifying the region to relight. It outputs a relit image or video that matches the target illumination while preserving intrinsic scene geometry, reflectance, and content. Notably, the geometry feedback (depth and normal estimation) is used only during training; at inference the pipeline is geometry-free and requires only the standard image/video and lighting condition inputs.

## Method

UniLumos builds on the Wan 2.1 flow-matching video generation backbone (1.3B DiT) and introduces a physics-plausible feedback mechanism: after decoding predicted latents to RGB via the Wan-VAE, a frozen dense estimator (Lotus) extracts depth and surface normal maps, which are compared against reference geometry to compute a masked L2 supervision loss (L_phy) that explicitly aligns illumination with scene structure. To make this RGB-space supervision compatible with efficient few-step inference, the authors adopt path consistency learning, which enforces consistent velocity predictions across larger integration steps and enables reliable geometric feedback under 1–5 step regimes. Training uses a joint objective L = λ₀L₀ + λ₁L_fast + λ₂L_phy with a selective batch allocation strategy (80% flow-matching, 20% path consistency, and 50% of the flow-matching subset additionally receiving physics feedback). A structured six-dimensional lighting annotation protocol (direction, source type, intensity, color temperature, temporal dynamics, optical phenomena) generated by Qwen2.5-VL provides fine-grained conditioning during training and underpins the LumosBench attribute-level evaluation via VLM-based scoring.

## Results

On image relighting, UniLumos achieves PSNR 26.72, SSIM 0.913, LPIPS 0.089, Lumos Score 0.912, and Dense L2 Error 0.103, outperforming the strongest baseline SynthLight (PSNR 25.57, Lumos 0.791, Dense L2 0.214) across all metrics. On video relighting, UniLumos reaches PSNR 25.03, R-Motion 1.436, Lumos Score 0.871, and Dense L2 Error 0.147, surpassing Light-A-Video+Wan2.1 (PSNR 20.78, R-Motion 1.582, Lumos 0.682, Dense L2 0.371). The method also delivers a 20× inference speedup over prior approaches while maintaining physical plausibility, and on the LumosBench attribute-level benchmark, the fine-tuned 1.3B model surpasses much larger general video models (e.g., HunyuanVideo 13B, avg 0.690) on per-attribute lighting controllability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On image relighting, UniLumos wins on every metric against all four baselines; the closest competitor is SynthLight, which trails by 1.15 dB in PSNR, 0.121 in Lumos Score, and 0.111 in Dense L2 Error. IC-Light, despite being the most widely used reference, shows the worst Dense L2 Error (0.447) among image methods, indicating significant geometry-lighting misalignment. On video relighting, UniLumos improves PSNR by over 4 dB and reduces Dense L2 Error by more than half compared to Light-A-Video+Wan2.1 (0.371 → 0.147), while also achieving the best R-Motion (1.436 vs. 1.582) for temporal smoothness. Frame-wise IC-Light performs worst on video (R-Motion 2.437, Dense L2 0.432), confirming the cost of ignoring temporal modeling. On the LumosBench attribute-level benchmark, UniLumos (a fine-tuned 1.3B Wan model) surpasses HunyuanVideo (13B, avg 0.690), CogVideoX (5.6B, avg 0.634), and LTX-Video (1.9B, avg 0.587) on per-attribute lighting controllability, demonstrating that structured relighting supervision on a smaller backbone can outperform much larger general-purpose video generators on lighting-specific tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniLumos builds on the Wan 2.1 flow-matching video generation backbone and incorporates path consistency learning (Frans et al., 2024) to enable few-step inference, but its key conceptual departure from prior diffusion-based relighting methods (IC-Light, SynthLight, Light-A-Video, RelightVid) is introducing RGB-space geometry feedback—depth and surface normal supervision—into the training loop. While prior methods operate entirely in semantic latent space where proximity does not guarantee physical correctness, UniLumos explicitly aligns generated illumination with scene structure using frozen dense estimators (Lotus) as supervision signals. The paper also carves new ground in evaluation by proposing a structured six-dimensional lighting annotation protocol and LumosBench, moving beyond holistic metrics like FID/LPIPS toward disentangled, attribute-level controllability assessment via vision-language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× inference speedup while simultaneously improving physical consistency (shadow alignment, shading coherence) makes physically plausible relighting practical for real-time creative and production workflows, a regime previously inaccessible to diffusion-based methods. LumosBench provides the field with a missing evaluation tool: a disentangled, attribute-level benchmark that can diagnose *which* lighting dimension a model fails on, rather than only reporting aggregate perceptual scores. The paper's stated limitation—that it enforces geometry-aware consistency but does not produce physically quantifiable outputs like radiance or illuminance—leaves open the question of whether truly physically grounded relighting (editable key lights, intensity ramps, environmental reflections) is achievable within a generative framework, or whether a hybrid rendering-plus-generation pipeline is ultimately required.
