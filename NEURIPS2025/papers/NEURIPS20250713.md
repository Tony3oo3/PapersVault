## Problem

Current text-to-video models are trained on open-domain video datasets that do not align with the topics real users actually request, leading to failures on user-focused subjects (e.g., a glowing firefly, a giant squid, Van Gogh paintings). No existing dataset is curated specifically around real user interests, and most recent datasets are reprocessed subsets of HD-VILA-100M with restrictive licenses, offering little new knowledge to models already trained on that source.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 1.09M video clips (720p, ~12.6 s average duration) each paired with a brief caption (~14 words), a detailed caption (~156 words), a topic label, start/end timestamps, and six VBench quality scores (subject consistency, background consistency, motion smoothness, dynamic degree, aesthetic quality, imaging quality). For the companion benchmark BenchUFO, the input is 7,910 real user prompts (791 concrete-noun topics × 10 prompts from VidProM), and the output is a per-topic cosine similarity score between the original prompt and a QWen2-VL-7B description of the generated video, aggregated over the 10/50 worst- and best-performing topics.

## Method

The curation pipeline first extracts 1,291 user-focused topics by embedding 1.67M VidProM prompts with SentenceTransformers, clustering with K-means (2,000 pre-set clusters), and having GPT-4o assign a 1–2-word label per cluster, followed by merging and manual filtering. Videos are then retrieved via YouTube's official API (~500 per topic, filtered for <4 min, ≥720p, Creative Commons license), segmented into semantically consistent clips using the Panda-70M shot-boundary pipeline, and captioned with both Panda-70M's brief-caption model and QWen2-VL-7B (via the Open-Sora-Plan pipeline) for detailed captions. A cost-effective verification step feeds detailed captions into GPT-4o-mini to confirm topic relevance, reducing 3.18M clips to 1.09M. For evaluation, the authors train MVDiT solely on VideoUFO and benchmark it against 16 existing text-to-video models on BenchUFO, showing a +4.2% improvement on the worst-performing topics while maintaining top-topic performance.

## Results

On the proposed BenchUFO benchmark (791 concrete-noun topics × 10 prompts each), MVDiT-VideoUFO achieves the best scores across all four metrics: 0.442 (Low-10), 0.465 (Low-50), 0.619 (Top-50), and 0.651 (Top-10). The headline result is a +4.2% improvement on the worst-performing topics (Low-10) over the previous best model (Pyramidal at 0.400), while simultaneously maintaining the highest Top-10 score. The gap between worst- and best-performing topics is narrowed from 0.233–0.314 (existing models) to 0.209 for MVDiT-VideoUFO, demonstrating more uniform coverage across user-focused topics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVDiT-VideoUFO outperforms all 16 competing text-to-video models on both Low-10 and Low-50 metrics, with the largest margin over Mira (0.236) and Show-1 (0.266) on worst-performing topics. The closest competitors on Low-10 are MVDiT-OpenVid (0.401) and Pyramidal (0.400), both trailing by ~0.04. On Top-10, MVDiT-VideoUFO (0.651) edges out Pyramidal (0.647) and HunyuanVideo (0.645) by a narrow margin, confirming it does not sacrifice strong-topic performance to improve weak ones. Critically, the paper isolates the dataset contribution by training the same MVDiT architecture on OpenVid-1M (Low-10: 0.401) and VidGen-1M (Low-10: 0.382), showing that the improvement stems from VideoUFO's user-focused curation rather than the model architecture. The authors also note that existing models show a consistent bias toward animal-related topics (e.g., seagull, panda, dolphin) in their best-performing sets, while struggling with niche topics like 'giant squid,' 'animal cell,' and 'ancient Egyptian'—gaps that VideoUFO training specifically addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a departure from the dominant paradigm of open-domain video dataset curation (OpenVid-1M, HD-VILA-100M, Panda-70M, etc.), which the authors argue introduces redundant data pipelines rather than genuinely new training content. Conceptually, it builds on Panda-70M's video splitting pipeline and Open-Sora-Plan's QWen2-VL-7B captioning pipeline, but introduces a fundamentally different curation logic: deriving 1,291 topics via K-means clustering of 1.67M real user prompts from VidProM, then targeting YouTube retrieval to those topics. The proposed BenchUFO benchmark also departs from VBench by evaluating on user-relevant concrete nouns (791 topics) rather than a fixed set of 79 common objects, and by using a video understanding model to score semantic alignment rather than relying on fixed object-class labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a concrete gap: no existing video dataset covers more than 63.6% of user-focused topics, and the 0.29% overlap with prior collections means it provides genuinely new training signal. The CC BY 4.0 license (vs. R-UDA restrictions on most competitors) lowers adoption barriers for both academic and commercial use. However, the validation is limited to a single architecture (MVDiT) requiring 32 A100 GPUs, and the +4.2% improvement on worst-performing topics, while meaningful, is modest. An open question is whether integrating VideoUFO into industry-scale foundation model training pipelines would yield proportionally larger gains, and whether the topic distribution derived from VidProM remains stable as user preferences evolve.
