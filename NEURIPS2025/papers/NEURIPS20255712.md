## Problem

Applying GRPO-based reinforcement learning to large vision-language model (LVLM) reasoning faces a fundamental tension: RL-only methods fail to effectively scale reasoning length (only −20% to +10% change), while SFT-RL methods produce overthinking—overly verbose responses across all difficulty levels with only marginal accuracy gains. No existing approach dynamically balances fast and slow thinking in LVLMs, leaving a gap between the need for concise reasoning on simple questions and thorough reasoning on complex ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (up to 1,000,000 pixels) paired with a text question as input and produces a structured output consisting of a chain-of-thought reasoning process enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. The key distinguishing feature is that the reasoning length is adaptive: FAST generates ~189 tokens on easy Geometry3K questions but ~304 tokens on hard ones, a 60% difference, whereas slow-thinking baselines produce uniformly long outputs regardless of difficulty. Training uses 18K curated, rule-based verifiable questions drawn from math, science, VQA, and document understanding sources.

## Method

FAST-GRPO modifies the standard GRPO objective with three coupled mechanisms. First, it estimates question difficulty via a multiplicative score S_difficulty = S_extrinsic × H_image, where S_extrinsic = 1 − pass@k is computed online from rollouts and H_image combines GLCM texture entropy (low-level pixel complexity) with ViT classification entropy (high-level semantic complexity). Second, it introduces a difficulty-aware length reward r_t that, for questions below the 80th-percentile difficulty threshold, rewards shorter-than-average correct responses (encouraging fast thinking), while for harder questions it rewards longer-than-average incorrect responses (encouraging slow thinking), with the reward capped at 1 to prevent unbounded verbosity. Third, it replaces the static KL coefficient with a dynamic β_d = β_min + (β_max − β_min)(1 − S_extrinsic), weakening regularization on hard questions to permit broader exploration and strengthening it on easy questions to maintain stability. A Slow-to-Fast sampling curriculum further shapes training by excluding easy samples in early epochs and hard samples in later epochs, guiding the model from deliberate reasoning toward efficient concise responses.

## Results

FAST-7B achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on MathVista (73.8%) and MathVerse (50.6%), outperforming closed-source GPT-4o (63.8% and 49.9% respectively) on these benchmarks. Compared to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model, FAST delivers an average accuracy improvement of over 10% across seven reasoning benchmarks while reducing average response length by 32.7% (vs. MM-R1) to 67.3% (vs. R1-OneVision). On the most challenging benchmark MM-Math, FAST-7B reaches 44.3% accuracy with only 335.6 tokens, versus 34.1% at 666.7 tokens for the base model. The 3B variant (FAST-3B) also improves its base model by over 14% average accuracy with similar length reductions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest slow-thinking SFT+RL baseline R1-OneVision, FAST-7B wins on 6 of 7 benchmarks (e.g., MathVista 73.8 vs. 64.1, MM-Math 44.3 vs. 34.1) while producing responses ~67.3% shorter (e.g., 120.7 vs. 402.5 tokens on MathVista); it narrowly loses on MM-Vet (71.2 vs. 71.6). Versus the RL-only MM-R1, FAST-7B achieves higher accuracy on all seven benchmarks (e.g., MathVerse 50.6 vs. 49.8, MM-Math 44.3 vs. 41.9) with ~32.7% fewer tokens. Against OpenVLThinker, FAST-7B leads on every benchmark by 1–11 points (e.g., MM-Math 44.3 vs. 33.1) with roughly half the response length. Compared to the closed-source GPT-4o, FAST-7B surpasses it on MathVision (30.6 vs. 30.4), MathVerse (50.6 vs. 49.9), MathVista (73.8 vs. 63.8), and MM-Math (44.3 vs. 31.8), but trails on DynaMath (58.3 vs. 63.7) and MM-Vet (71.2 vs. 80.8). Among fast-thinking reward-shaping methods (Kimi, CosFn, DAST), FAST achieves the best average accuracy (59.0% vs. 55.7–57.8%) while maintaining competitive response lengths (162.2 tokens avg. vs. 75.0–297.7). Against LMM-R1, FAST-7B gains 3–10 points across all benchmarks (e.g., MathVista 73.8 vs. 63.2) with substantially shorter outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1/DeepSeekMath) to the vision-language domain by introducing multimodal-specific difficulty estimation (GLCM texture entropy + ViT classification entropy combined with online pass@k) and difficulty-conditioned reward shaping. It departs from prior slow-thinking LVLM methods (R1-OneVision, Vision-R1, OpenVLThinker) that rely on SFT-RL pipelines and exhibit overthinking, and from RL-only methods (MM-R1, LMM-R1, MM-Eureka) that cannot effectively scale reasoning length. The fast-slow thinking framing draws on LLM-side overthinking remedies (Kimi length penalties, DAST, CosFn) but is the first to adapt these ideas to LVLMs by incorporating visual input complexity into the difficulty signal, which text-only methods ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical inefficiency in LVLM deployment: slow-thinking models produce 2×+ longer responses on easy questions while actually degrading accuracy, wasting inference compute. The empirical finding that reasoning length and accuracy are largely decoupled in LVLMs (±3% accuracy across 180–700 tokens) challenges the implicit assumption that longer chains are always better in multimodal settings. Key open questions include whether the moderate human-alignment of the image complexity metric (SRCC=0.49) limits generalization, how the approach scales beyond 32B parameters, and whether the identified perception bottleneck (over half of errors) can be addressed without further lengthening reasoning chains.
