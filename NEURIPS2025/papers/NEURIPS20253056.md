## Problem

Diffusion transformer inference requires tens to hundreds of sequential denoising steps, creating prohibitive latency for high-resolution image and video generation. While speculative decoding has proven effective for LLMs, adapting it to diffusion models is blocked by two challenges: verifying continuous high-dimensional outputs is computationally infeasible at scale, and the optimal number of speculative steps K must balance acceleration against verification failure rates that vary across denoising phases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253056/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires a text prompt plus a paired draft-target model set (e.g., Flux-SVD 4-bit quantized model with Flux.1-dev 12B for 512×512 images, or Wan2.1-1.3B with Wan2.1-14B for 832×480 videos of 81 frames). It operates over a fixed 50-step denoising schedule and produces the same image or video as the target model would, with the draft model handling most intermediate steps while the target model only executes verification checkpoints and one step per round.

## Method

ASDSV introduces two key mechanisms. First, approximate speculative verification exploits the observed temporal correlation between draft and target model outputs (their step-to-step L1 differences are highly similar after initial warmup steps), so instead of verifying all K intermediate draft outputs, it only checks that the first and last draft outputs align with the target model within a threshold δ, reducing verification from K target-model calls to just 2. Second, a multi-stage speculative strategy assigns a small K (γ₁=2–3) during volatile early denoising and a larger K (γ₂=9) during stable later stages, determined by profiling the verification loss curve for an inflection point. The workflow runs N target-model warmup steps, then alternates: draft model samples K steps, target model verifies first and last outputs in a batched pipeline (generating the current round's last step and next round's first step in parallel), and on failure the target model regenerates from the second step.

## Results

On text-to-video generation (Wan2.1, 81 frames, 832×480), ASDSV-fast achieves a 3.01× speedup (307s vs. 924s latency) with a VBench score of 82.36, compared to 82.69 for the original model (0.4% drop). ASDSV-slow achieves 1.77× speedup with a VBench score of 82.29 (0.5% drop) and the best similarity metrics (PSNR 16.58, SSIM 0.58). On text-to-image generation (Flux.1-dev, 512×512), ASDSV-slow achieves the best FID (3.90) and PSNR (33.14) among all methods with a 1.26× speedup, while ASDSV-fast achieves the lowest FLOPs (206.3T) with FID 4.11 at 1.47× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TeaCache, ASDSV dominates on video generation: ASDSV-fast achieves 3.01× speedup vs. TeaCache-fast's 2.00× while maintaining a higher VBench score (82.36 vs. 81.83), and ASDSV-slow achieves 1.77× vs. 1.47× with better VBench (82.29 vs. 82.17) and substantially better similarity metrics (PSNR 16.58 vs. 15.75, SSIM 0.58 vs. 0.55). On image generation, TeaCache-fast is faster (2.16× vs. 1.47×) but suffers a much larger quality drop (FID 8.16 vs. 4.11 for ASDSV-fast). Against Sparse VideoGen, ASDSV wins decisively on both speed and quality in video: 3.01× vs. 1.21× speedup and VBench 82.36 vs. 78.97 (a 3.7-point gap). Against distillation-based draft models (Flux-lite and Flux-SVD used standalone), ASDSV consistently outperforms: ASDSV with Flux-SVD achieves FID 4.97 vs. Flux-SVD's 5.52 and LPIPS 0.11 vs. 0.19, while ASDSV with Flux-lite achieves FID 6.62 vs. Flux-lite's 9.51, demonstrating that the speculative verification framework adds value beyond simply using a smaller model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the speculative decoding paradigm from LLM inference (Leviathan et al., 2023) to diffusion models, extending the first attempt by De Bortoli et al. (2025) which was restricted to 32×32 CIFAR-10 due to prohibitive verification costs. The key departure is an approximate speculative verification strategy that exploits temporal correlation between draft and target model outputs to check only the first and last of K speculative steps rather than all K, making the approach viable for SOTA 12B–14B diffusion transformers. It also introduces a multi-stage speculative strategy that modulates K across denoising phases, contrasting with both fixed-K speculative decoding and the step-skipping heuristics of caching-based methods like TeaCache.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASDSV demonstrates that speculative decoding can deliver 1.77×–3.01× inference speedup on production-scale multimodal models (Flux.1-dev, Wan2.1) with only 0.3%–0.4% VBench degradation, making near-real-time video generation more practical. However, the approach's effectiveness is bounded by the cost coefficient between draft and target models, the current failure-handling strategy (discarding all subsequent samples on rejection) is suboptimal, and integration with multi-GPU parallelism remains unexplored. Open questions include whether more sophisticated recovery (e.g., binary search for the last acceptable draft step) and hardware-aware draft model selection can close the gap between theoretical and achieved speedups.
