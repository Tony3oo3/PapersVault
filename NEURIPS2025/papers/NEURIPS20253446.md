## Problem

Prior work on LLM knowledge conflicts typically represents model knowledge via a single most-likely response or entropy-based uncertainty metrics, both of which overlook the coexistence of multiple competing modes in the answer distribution. This makes it impossible to distinguish, for example, a model that is genuinely conflicted between two answers from one that has a consistent but wrong belief. The paper asks: can we rigorously characterize *what* an LLM knows about a question by examining the full structure of its response distribution?

## Input / Output

KScope takes a question (with optional supporting context) and produces one of five knowledge status labels: consistent correct, conflicting correct, absent, conflicting wrong, or consistent wrong. To build the empirical distribution, the framework generates M=20 paraphrases of the question and collects N=100 chain-of-thought responses at temperature 1; for open-ended questions, the support set is determined by semantic clustering of the samples rather than predefined options. Invalid responses (hallucinated answers outside the support set or refusals) are explicitly tracked and tested for significance before the remaining distribution is analyzed.

## Method

KScope is a four-step hierarchical statistical testing framework that progressively refines hypotheses about the LLM's knowledge mode set. Step 1 uses a one-sided exact binomial test to detect whether the model predominantly produces invalid answers (absent knowledge). Step 2 applies a two-sided exact multinomial test to check for uniform guessing. Step 3 performs likelihood ratio tests to identify whether multiple options form a statistically indistinguishable plateau (conflicting knowledge), selecting among competing alternatives via BIC after Bonferroni correction. Step 4 uses one-sided exact binomial tests on the reduced two-element mode set to determine whether the model shows a consistent preference (consistent correct or wrong) or remains conflicted. Beyond characterization, the paper applies logistic regression with SHAP-based feature importance across eleven context features (difficulty, relevance, familiarity) to identify what drives successful knowledge updates, and validates that constrained summarization guided by these feature rankings, combined with credibility metadata, improves update success by 4.3% on average across all statuses and generalizes to GPT-4o.

## Results

Applying KScope to nine LLMs across four datasets, the paper finds that supporting gold context substantially increases the proportion of consistent correct knowledge across all models and datasets, with the largest improvement on HotpotQA and the smallest on Hemonc. In the multi-choice setting without context, Llama-70B achieves the highest consistent correct parametric knowledge (53.9% on HotpotQA), while Gemma-2B shows the most absent knowledge (22.0% on HotpotQA). Noisy context (top-10 Wikipedia retrieval) dramatically reduces update success compared to gold context, and the open-ended setting yields lower consistent correct proportions than multi-choice. The combined constrained summarization + credibility augmentation strategy improves knowledge update success by 4.3% on average across all statuses and LLMs, generalizing to GPT-4o which was not part of the feature analysis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a characterization and analysis study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so it does not benchmark KScope against prior knowledge-conflict metrics. Instead, it compares LLMs across three dimensions: (1) model scale within families — larger models consistently show higher consistent correct parametric knowledge (e.g., Llama-70B at 53.9% vs. Llama-3B at 35.4% on HotpotQA), with Llama > Qwen > Gemma in parametric knowledge, though gaps narrow with context; (2) context quality — gold context raises consistent correct knowledge to 88–96% across models, while noisy top-10 retrieval drops it substantially (e.g., Llama-70B falls from 92.0% to 73.1%), and the open-ended setting yields further reductions; (3) augmentation strategies — constrained summarization outperforms naive summarization across all statuses except consistent wrong, where credibility metadata is more effective; the combined strategy (constrained summarization + credibility) achieves the best overall improvement of +4.3% average success rate, and this generalizes to GPT-4o despite it not being included in the feature importance analysis. Naive summarization consistently hurts performance by degrading readability and ROUGE-2 recall while failing to preserve semantic content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant knowledge-conflict literature, which typically represents LLM knowledge via a single most-likely response or entropy-based uncertainty metrics, by instead modeling the full response distribution and its mode structure. It builds on the RAG and knowledge-update line of work (Lewis et al., Xu et al. on credibility) and the statistical-testing tradition (binomial, multinomial, likelihood-ratio tests) to propose KScope, a hierarchical framework that assigns one of five knowledge statuses based on both consistency and correctness of knowledge modes. The feature-importance analysis extends prior findings on context persuasiveness (Du et al. 2024, Wan et al. 2024) by stratifying results per knowledge status rather than treating all questions uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The KScope framework provides a principled, reproducible way to diagnose *why* an LLM fails on a question—distinguishing absent knowledge, internal conflict, and firmly held false beliefs—which has direct implications for designing targeted RAG interventions. The finding that consistent-wrong knowledge requires different context features than other statuses (and that credibility metadata is particularly effective there) offers actionable guidance for practitioners building retrieval-augmented systems. Open questions include generalization to larger support sets, more complex noisy retrieval with genuinely conflicting passages, and the role of finer-grained stylistic features beyond the eleven studied here.
