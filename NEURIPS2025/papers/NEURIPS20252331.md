## Problem

Object detection models trained on large-scale datasets with noisy annotations suffer significant performance degradation, yet prior robust-learning methods address either categorization noise (wrong class labels) or localization noise (inaccurate bounding boxes) in isolation. No existing approach simultaneously handles both types of noise, which frequently co-occur in real-world crowd-sourced annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252331/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images resized to 512×512 and produces bounding boxes with class labels, compatible with both anchor-based (RetinaNet, Faster R-CNN) and anchor-free (FCOS, GFL) as well as transformer-based (Deformable DETR, DINO) detectors. The distinguishing aspect is that training annotations are intentionally corrupted with up to 50% bounding-box coordinate perturbation and/or uniformly sampled incorrect class labels, while evaluation uses clean ground truth. The method is implemented as a plug-in module within MMDetection requiring no architecture-specific modifications.

## Method

ELDET exploits the empirical observation that object detectors memorize localization noise earlier than categorization noise during training. It monitors two task-specific metrics—class-agnostic AP for localization and a Ground-Truth Box Allocation (GTBA) metric for classification—fitting an exponential parametric curve to each to detect the early-learning-to-memorization transition via a gradient-deviation threshold. Once localization memorization begins, the current model is copied as a frozen teacher, and the student continues training with a CrossKD distillation loss that propagates student intermediate features to the teacher's detection heads. The teacher is updated via exponential moving average (EMA) of student weights, with a deliberately small decay rate (α=0.1) applied to the classification head during the window between localization and classification memorization to prevent the teacher from lagging on classification. This self-distillation loop guides the student to retain clean-data knowledge while suppressing overfitting to both noise types simultaneously.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC (AP@50), ELDET consistently outperforms baselines across all noise types and levels. Under the most challenging 40% combined noise, ELDET with RetinaNet achieves 68.82 AP versus 65.63 (baseline), 65.47 (ORSOD), and 65.87 (ADELE). On MS COCO (AP@50), ELDET with GFL reaches 45.58 under 40% combined noise versus 44.81 baseline. On VinDr-CXR (AP@40), ELDET boosts FCOS from 25.32 to 32.74 under 40% combined noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELDET is compared against two baselines: ORSOD (dynamic loss decay targeting categorization noise only) and ADELE (early-learning correction adapted from segmentation to detection). ELDET outperforms both baselines in nearly every setting across all four detectors. The largest margins appear under combined noise: on GFL at 40% combined noise, ELDET gains +7.27 AP over baseline (63.57 vs 56.30) and +2.29 over ADELE (63.57 vs 62.71). ORSOD shows negligible or negative gains on some architectures (e.g., faster R-CNN clean drops from 73.89 to 70.77), indicating poor robustness across detectors. ADELE, designed for pixel-wise segmentation, underperforms on localization noise (e.g., retinanet loc 40%: 71.03 vs ELDET 74.80) because it does not explicitly handle bounding-box coordinate errors. ELDET's advantage is most pronounced when both noise types co-occur, validating its task-specific early-learning detection for localization and classification separately.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the early-learning regularization framework (Liu et al., 2020) and its segmentation adaptation ADELE (Liu et al., 2022) to object detection, where the dual-task structure of classification and localization creates a new challenge: the two tasks memorize noise at different rates. It departs from prior noisy-label detection methods (ORSOD, LLRN) that address only one noise type by simultaneously handling both localization and categorization noise, and from co-teaching approaches by replacing dual-model gradient updates with a single EMA-updated teacher to reduce compute cost. The distillation mechanism leverages CrossKD's cross-head transfer, and the task-specific early-learning detection (class-agnostic metric for localization, GTBA for classification) is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design makes ELDET immediately applicable across anchor-based, anchor-free, and transformer-based detectors without architectural modification, which is practically valuable for practitioners working with imperfect annotations in autonomous driving, medical imaging, or crowd-sourced datasets. Key limitations include the reliance on synthetic uniform noise (not reflecting structured real-world annotation errors), sensitivity to the transition-detection hyperparameter γ, and the absence of a theoretical explanation for why localization is memorized before classification. Open questions include whether the temporal asymmetry holds under more realistic correlated noise, whether it extends to video detection, and whether the EMA-based teacher can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives for very large-scale training.
