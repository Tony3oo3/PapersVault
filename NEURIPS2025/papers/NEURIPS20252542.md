## Problem

Standard online learning assumes the learner has full knowledge of the concept class, but computing the optimal algorithm (SOA) is computationally intractable. Prior oracle-based work studied only a 'restricted' ERM oracle that can query only adversary-generated pairs, leaving open whether a general ERM oracle (querying arbitrary subsets of the domain) or a weaker consistency oracle can circumvent the exponential dependence on the Littlestone dimension in mistake bounds. This paper establishes tight lower bounds for the general ERM setting and explores whether transductive information (knowing all instances in advance) can restore polynomial oracle complexity.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a sequence of T instances (all at once in the transductive setting, one at a time in the online setting) and must output a binary prediction at each step before observing the true label. The learner's only access to the unknown concept class C is through oracle queries: the ERM oracle takes an arbitrary labeled subset S ⊆ X×{0,1} and returns a consistent hypothesis (or 'not realizable'), while the weak consistency oracle returns only a binary realizability signal. Performance is measured jointly by the number of mistakes (or regret in the agnostic case) and the total number of oracle calls, with the goal of polynomial oracle complexity.

## Method

The paper constructs a geometric hard instance using nested hyperrectangles in [0,1]^(T-1) to prove that any algorithm making finitely many ERM queries incurs Ω(2^d_LD) mistakes (realizable) and Ω(√(T·2^d_LD)) regret (agnostic), matching known upper bounds up to constant factors in the exponent. For the weak consistency oracle, a simulation argument shows any deterministic restricted-ERM algorithm can be replicated with an O(T) blowup in oracle calls. In the transductive setting, the key insight is that O(T^(d_VC+1)) weak consistency queries suffice to enumerate all realizable labelings on the known instance set, recovering optimal mistake and regret bounds; conversely, Ω(T) queries are necessary for learnability. For special classes, randomized algorithms exploit structural properties: for thresholds with unknown ordering, random sampling of two points from the uncertainty region yields a balanced ERM partition with constant probability, giving O(log T) queries and mistakes; for k-intervals, a non-realizable-labeling test identifies ordering endpoints to enable recursive halving with O(T^3·2^(2k)) expected queries.

## Results

In the online learning setting, the paper establishes tight lower bounds: Ω(2^{d_LD}) mistakes in the realizable case and Ω(√(T·2^{d_LD})) regret in the agnostic case, both holding for any algorithm making finitely many queries to even the strongest 'agnostic' ERM oracle. In the transductive online setting, the key positive result is that O(T^{d_VC+1}) weak consistency oracle calls suffice to recover the optimal mistake bound (upper bounded by min{d_LD, d_VC·log T}), circumventing the exponential dependence on the Littlestone dimension. For special concept classes, randomized algorithms achieve dramatic improvements: O(log T) ERM calls for thresholds with unknown ordering, and O(T³·2^{2k}) weak consistency calls for k-intervals, both with optimal O(log T) or O(k log T) mistake bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Assos et al. [AAD+23], whose upper bounds give 2^{O(d_LD)} mistakes with 2^{O(d_LD)} ERM calls (realizable) and Õ(√(T·2^{O(d_LD)}) regret with T^{2O(d_LD)} calls (agnostic), this paper proves matching lower bounds of Ω(2^{d_LD}) mistakes and Ω(√(T·2^{d_LD})) regret, confirming the exponential dependence on d_LD is unavoidable even with the strongest agnostic ERM oracle. Compared to Kozachinskiy & Steifer [KS24], whose Ω(3^{d_LD}) lower bound applies only to the restricted ERM (querying only adversary-generated pairs), this paper's Ω(2^{d_LD}) lower bound holds for the general ERM oracle that can query arbitrary subsets of the domain, a strictly stronger oracle. The key differentiator is the transductive setting: while both prior works show exponential mistake bounds in the online setting, this paper demonstrates that O(T^{d_VC+1}) weak consistency calls in the transductive setting recover optimal (linear-in-dimension) mistake bounds, a result unavailable in the online model. For thresholds, the randomized ERM algorithm achieves O(log T) oracle calls, an exponential improvement over the O(T) calls needed by deterministic algorithms from prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the oracle-based online learning framework of Assos et al. [AAD+23] and Kozachinskiy and Steifer [KS24], who studied the 'restricted ERM' oracle (queryable only on adversary-generated pairs), to the more powerful 'general ERM' oracle (queryable on any subset of the domain) and to the weaker 'weak consistency oracle' introduced by Daskalakis and Golowich [DG24] for PAC learning. It also brings these oracle models into the transductive online learning setting, where the instance sequence is known in advance, building on the transductive learning literature of Kakade and Kalai [KK05], Hanneke et al. [HMS23], and Chase et al. [CHMS25]. The key conceptual departure is showing that the exponential-in-Littlestone-dimension hardness persists even with the strongest ERM variant in the online setting, while the transductive setting admits polynomial oracle complexity via the weak consistency oracle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that oracle access fundamentally changes the computational landscape of online learning: even the most powerful ERM oracle cannot escape exponential dependence on Littlestone dimension in the online setting, yet the transductive setting circumvents this with only polynomial weak consistency queries. The negative result for partial concepts (Ω(T) mistakes even with ERM, despite Littlestone dimension ) sharpens the gap between online and PAC learnability. Key open questions include whether randomized algorithms can achieve T^C · 2^{O(d_VC)} oracle calls for general transductive online learning, and whether the current T^{2^{O(d_LD)}} oracle complexity for agnostic online learning can be improved.
