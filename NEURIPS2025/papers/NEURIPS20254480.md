## Problem

Online time series forecasting suffers from unknown distribution shifts driven by unobserved latent variables, yet existing methods that incorporate such shifts into model architectures offer few theoretical guarantees explaining why conditioning on estimated shifts systematically reduces forecasting error. Prior theoretical work either imposes strict data-generation assumptions (e.g., nonstationarity caused solely by interventions on latent variables) or fails to characterize the gap between estimated and ground-truth risk under estimation uncertainty. This paper provides a general theoretical framework with provable risk-reduction bounds and a minimal-data identifiability criterion for the latent variables.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a window of τ+1 historical multivariate observations x_{t-τ:t} ∈ R^{n×(τ+1)} together with estimated latent variables ẑ_t ∈ R^n, and outputs a future segment x̂_{t+1:T} ∈ R^{n×(T-t)}. The latent variables are identified from only four consecutive observations (x_{t-2}, x_{t-1}, x_t, x_{t+1}), making the framework applicable to short-history online settings. The architecture is plug-and-play: the encoder and forecaster blocks can be swapped with any backbone (OneNet, LSTD, Proceed-T, Online-T), while the temporal decoder and two noise estimators remain fixed.

## Method

TOT is a VAE-based, model-agnostic blueprint that introduces latent variables into online forecasting with theoretical guarantees. Theoretically, it proves (i) conditioning on latent variables tightens the Bayes risk, with the reduction persisting under estimation noise and growing with identifiability precision; (ii) the joint block (x_t, z_t) is block-wise identifiable from four consecutive observations via eigenvalue-eigenfunction decomposition of linear operators; and (iii) component-wise identifiability follows under a sparse mixing constraint (empty intimate neighbor set). Architecturally, the model uses a temporal decoder to match the marginal distribution of observations, two independent MLP-based noise estimators—one for latent transitions z_{t-1}→z_t and one for observed transitions (z_t, x_{t-1})→x_t—whose Jacobians enter the ELBO via change-of-variables, and a residual forecaster φ(ẑ_{t+1:T}, η(x_{1:t})) that combines latent and observation features. An L1 penalty on ∂x̂_t/∂ẑ_t enforces sparsity in the mixing procedure, and the total loss balances reconstruction, latent KL, observation KL, and sparsity terms.

## Results

On six real-world benchmarks (ETTh2, ETTm1, Weather, ECL, Traffic, Exchange) with forecast horizons of 1, 24, and 48 steps, TOT plugged into five backbone networks consistently reduces MSE. The largest gains appear on Exchange (e.g., Online-T MSE drops from 0.168 to 0.040 at horizon 48, a 76% reduction) and ECL (Online-T MSE drops from 11.534 to 4.574 at horizon 48). On ETTh2 at horizon 48, OneNet-T improves from 0.806 to 0.773 and Online-T from 1.183 to 0.874. On synthetic data, TOT achieves MCC of 0.9258–0.9324 for latent variable identifiability, far exceeding IDOL (0.3788–0.8593) and TDRL (0.3572–0.8073).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOT is evaluated as a plug-in module atop five backbone networks: LSTD (Cai et al., 2025), Proceed-T (Zhao & Shen, 2024), OneNet/OneNet-T (Wen et al., 2024), and Online-T (Zinkevich, 2003). The method wins on nearly every dataset–horizon combination across all backbones. Gains are largest when the backbone does not already model latent structure: Online-T+TOT reduces MSE by up to 76% on Exchange (0.168→0.040 at horizon 48) and 60% on ECL (11.534→4.574), while OneNet-T+TOT improves ETTh2 from 0.806 to 0.773. In contrast, LSTD+TOT shows the smallest relative gains (e.g., ECL horizon 48: 1.411→1.413, essentially a tie) because LSTD's LSTM architecture already captures some latent-variable-driven shifts. Proceed-T+TOT yields moderate improvements (e.g., ETTh2 horizon 48: 4.056→3.956) but is consistently outperformed by the OneNet and Online-T variants. On the synthetic identifiability task, TOT achieves MCC of 0.93, substantially outperforming IDOL (0.38–0.86) and TDRL (0.36–0.81), confirming that its temporal causal modeling is critical for recovering latent states.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOT builds on the line of online time series forecasting methods that address distribution shifts (fast/slow learning networks, detect-then-adapt, LSTD) and on causal representation learning identifiability results (IDOL, TDRL, Fu et al. 2025). It departs from prior identifiability work in three key ways: it allows noisy (non-invertible) mixing from latent to observed variables, it permits temporal causal edges between consecutive observations (unlike TDRL/LEAP which assume none), and it proves block-wise identifiability from only four consecutive observations by leveraging different transition probabilities rather than conditional independence. The theoretical risk-bound framework (Theorem 1) is novel in explicitly quantifying how estimation uncertainty in latent variables degrades the Bayes risk reduction, a gap the authors identify as unaddressed by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first general theoretical framework with provable risk-reduction guarantees for online time series forecasting under latent-driven distribution shifts, giving practitioners explicit guidance on why and when latent variable conditioning helps. The plug-and-play architecture is practically useful, yielding consistent (if often modest) improvements across six real-world benchmarks and four backbone networks. Key limitations include the assumption of fully observed, discretely sampled data, and the empirical gains over strong baselines like LSTD and OneNet are small, suggesting the theoretical upper bound is not yet fully realized in practice. Extending the framework to irregular/multi-rate sampling and to related tasks like causal discovery on time series or video understanding are natural open directions.
