## Problem

Existing retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods for knowledge-based visual question answering struggle with two issues: (1) full-image visual features are too coarse for fine-grained retrieval in complex scenes (e.g., a small statue is overshadowed by a prominent bell tower), and (2) paragraph-level reranking cannot adequately filter the large volume of irrelevant content in retrieved results. No prior work has applied reinforcement learning to train a VLM for multimodal RAG, leaving tool invocation and relevance filtering unoptimized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253980/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reference RGB image, a natural-language question, and a million-scale Wikipedia knowledge base (articles paired with images). During the processing stage, the image is dynamically transformed via tool calls (cropping via grounding, left-right flipping, captioning) to produce refined multimodal queries. The output is a short text answer generated from a compact, task-oriented knowledge representation F that the RL-trained VLM extracts from the retrieved sections, structured within <answer> tags after a <think> reasoning block.

## Method

Wiki-PRF is a three-stage pipeline (Processing → Retrieval → Filtering) where a single RL-trained VLM (VLM-PRF, based on Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B with LoRA) orchestrates tool selection, retrieval queries, and result filtering. In the processing stage, VLM-PRF reasons in <think> tags and emits <tool> calls (captioning, visual grounding, image flipping) executed by a frozen base VLM to generate precise multimodal queries. The retrieval stage embeds these queries with EVA-CLIP and performs Faiss cosine-similarity search over the knowledge base, retrieving top-k articles and their most relevant sections. The filtering stage has VLM-PRF condense the retrieved sections into a compact task-oriented knowledge F, which a separate VLM then uses to generate the final answer. Training uses GRPO (without KL constraint) with a composite reward combining answer exact-match (α=1), tool-format compliance (β=0.3), and filter-format compliance (γ=0.7), requiring only ~4K training samples and ~15 hours on 8 A800 GPUs.

## Results

Wiki-PRF-7B (VLM-PRF-7B) achieves state-of-the-art performance on both primary benchmarks: 36.0 BEM on E-VQA (All) and 42.8 VQA accuracy on InfoSeek (All), surpassing the previous best methods ReflectiVA (29.2 / 40.1) and MMKB-RAG (35.9 / 36.4). With InternVL3-8B as the base model, the method further reaches 39.2 on E-VQA. On the OK-VQA benchmark, Wiki-PRF-7B sets a new SOTA of 77.8, outperforming KU-RAG (73.1) and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (72.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wiki-PRF-7B outperforms all retrieval-augmented baselines on both E-VQA and InfoSeek. On InfoSeek (All), it leads ReflectiVA [31] by 2.7 points (42.8 vs 40.1) and MMKB-RAG [53] by 6.4 points (42.8 vs 36.4), while on E-VQA (All) it surpasses MMKB-RAG by 0.1 (36.0 vs 35.9) and ReflectiVA by 6.8 (36.0 vs 29.2). Against zero-shot MLLMs, the gap is even larger: GPT-4V [1] scores only 28.1 on E-VQA and 14.6 on InfoSeek, and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B [2] reaches 20.3 / 23.7, confirming that RAG is essential for KB-VQA. On OK-VQA, Wiki-PRF-7B (77.8) beats KU-RAG [54] (73.1) and MMKB-RAG [53] (65.4), establishing a new SOTA. The ablation without RL (VLM-PRF w/o RL, 39.5 on InfoSeek) shows that reinforcement learning contributes an additional 3.3 points, validating the RL training paradigm over standard SFT (41.8 with SFT vs 46.3 with RL in the controlled comparison of Table 6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KB-VQA RAG pipeline (EchoSight, Wiki-LLaVA, ReflectiVA, MMKB-RAG) by inserting two novel stages: a tool-based processing stage that dynamically invokes captioning, grounding, and flipping operations to refine retrieval queries, and an RL-trained filtering stage that condenses retrieved sections into task-oriented knowledge. The RL training itself adapts GRPO from DeepSeekMath (originally for mathematical reasoning) to the multimodal RAG setting, which the authors claim is the first such application. The base VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) and the EVA-CLIP + Faiss retrieval stack are standard components; the contribution lies in how they are orchestrated and trained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that RL with only ~4K training samples (vs. 934K for SFT baselines) yields a model that generalizes better at filtering and tool selection, suggesting RL is a data-efficient path to improving RAG pipelines beyond memorizing QA patterns. The oracle-filtering gap (65.8% vs. 57.6% for ReflectiVA) indicates the filtering stage is the primary bottleneck in current KB-VQA systems, not retrieval alone. Limitations include reliance on a Wikipedia-scale KB, only three tool types, and ~6–9 s per-sample inference time, which constrains real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the RL-trained filtering generalizes to non-Wikipedia knowledge sources and whether additional tools (e.g., OCR, depth estimation) would yield further gains.
