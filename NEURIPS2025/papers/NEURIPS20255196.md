## Problem

Despite the Polyak stepsize achieving near-optimal convergence rates across smooth, non-smooth, and strongly convex functions with a single adaptive rule, no unified explanation exists for why it is adaptive, and its convergence properties and failure modes are scattered across disparate analyses. This paper asks: what geometric property makes the Polyak stepsize adaptive, and when does it genuinely fail to converge?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255196/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a convex function f (or stochastic f(x,ξ) with a transformation h(x,ξ) ≥ 0), an initial point x₁, and subgradients g_t at each iterate. It produces a sequence of iterates x_t where the stepsize is computed entirely from the current gradient norm (η_t = 1/||g_t||², optionally clipped by γ/h(x_t,ξ_t)), requiring no prior knowledge of smoothness or curvature constants. In the stochastic setting, the output includes convergence guarantees on the expected surrogate loss H(x_t) = E[h(x_t,ξ)] rather than directly on F(x_t).

## Method

The core insight is that the Polyak stepsize is equivalent to subgradient descent on the surrogate φ(x) = ½(f(x) − f(x*))², where the stepsize 1/||g_t||² is precisely the inverse of the surrogate's local star upper curvature (LSUC) at x_t. This surrogate is always locally curved regardless of f's own curvature, and inherits additional curvature (quadratic growth) from f when f is sharp or self-bounded. The framework generalizes to ψ(x) = ½h²(x) for any convex h ≥ 0, yielding a family of algorithms (covering SPS_max, SPS_+, ALI-G) with a unified analysis via approximate LSUC. Negative results then show that when the surrogate minimum is strictly positive (h(x*) > 0), the fixed point becomes unstable, iterates can cycle, and convergence fails for a set of initializations of full measure—proving that the neighbourhood-of-convergence terms in existing bounds are fundamental, not artifacts of the proof.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The main positive result (Theorem 2) shows the Polyak stepsize is equivalent to subgradient descent on the surrogate φ(x) = ½(f(x)−f*)² with stepsizes equal to the inverse of the local star upper curvature (LSUC), yielding O(1/T) rates for G-Lipschitz functions, O(1/T²) for L-self-bounded functions, and linear convergence for s-sharp + G-Lipschitz functions. The key negative results (Section 6) prove that when the surrogate minimum h* > 0, the fixed point of the Polyak update is unstable (Proposition 1), iterates can cycle indefinitely (Proposition 2), and for the 1-D quadratic h(x) = x²/2 + a the probability of convergence to the optimum is exactly zero (Proposition 3). In the stochastic setting, Proposition 4 constructs a concrete 2-component quadratic where SPS fails to converge with E[F(x_t)] − min F(x) ≥ 2/3 for all t.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run empirical comparisons but provides a unified theoretical framework that subsumes and extends several existing Polyak stepsize variants. Theorem 3 shows that SPS_max [29], SPS_max^ℓ [35], and SPS_+ [15] are all special cases of the proposed Generalized Polyak Stepsize (Algorithm 1) with different choices of h(x,ξ). The paper's key contribution over these methods is proving that the non-vanishing neighbourhood term (proportional to H(x*)) appearing in their convergence bounds is not an artifact of the analysis but is fundamentally unavoidable (Section 6). Specifically, the negative results show that SPS without interpolation can exhibit a random walk between a finite set of points (Proposition 4), and that even in the deterministic setting with an underestimated optimum, the Polyak update can fail to converge for almost all initializations (Proposition 3). The paper also extends SPS_max's guarantees from the non-smooth setting to the full range of upper curvature (Lipschitz through smooth), which was not previously established.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Polyak's original 1969 stepsize and the extensive line of stochastic variants (ALI-G, SPS_max, SPS_+, SPS_max^ℓ) by reframing them all as gradient descent on a fixed surrogate loss ψ(x) = ½h²(x), where the stepsize is the inverse of a newly defined local star upper curvature (LSUC). It departs from Gower et al.'s earlier surrogate perspective, which used adversarially chosen losses, by showing the surrogate is a single fixed function with guaranteed local smoothness. The subgradient projector literature (Bauschke, Cegielski, Censor) is connected but the paper provides a distinct geometric lens via LSUC and inherits curvature from the original function f.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified geometric explanation for why the Polyak stepsize is adaptive across smoothness regimes, and crucially shows via negative results that the neighbourhood of convergence in stochastic variants is not an analysis artifact but a fundamental instability: for 1D quadratics with h* > 0, the set of converging initializations has measure zero, and iterates can cycle indefinitely. The framework is limited to convex h and it remains open whether non-convex surrogates can admit fast rates with tight convergence neighbourhoods, which would be needed for practical non-convex optimization. The LSUC perspective offers a principled recipe for designing new adaptive-stepsize variants by engineering surrogates with desired curvature properties.
