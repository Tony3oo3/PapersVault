## Problem

Adversarial learning tasks such as Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and GANs are traditionally formulated as min-max problems, but practical implementations must discard second-order (Hessian) terms to remain computationally feasible, creating a gap between theoretical guarantees and actual accuracy. The paper asks whether a unified framework can balance computational cost and learning accuracy without sacrificing the geometric information that drives generalization.

## Input / Output

For the SAM application, the method consumes CIFAR-10 images (32×32 RGB) with labels and a ResNet backbone (18/34/50/101), producing optimized model parameters; the perturbation radius r and penalty parameter μ control the sharpness trade-off. For the GAN application, it takes a latent vector and produces generated samples (2D wheel distributions or StackedMNIST digits). A key implementation detail is that the Hessian-vector product in the SAM update is approximated via Q i.i.d. Gaussian probe vectors, making the method Hessian-free while retaining second-order geometric information.

## Method

The paper reformulates the min-max adversarial problem as a bilevel optimization where the lower-level objective L_ℓ(ω,δ) is chosen to be analytically tractable (e.g., a linear program over the unit ball for SAM, yielding the closed-form solution δ* = ∇L(ω)/‖∇L(ω)‖), so no additional computational burden is introduced. The nonsmooth value function is smoothed via a Moreau envelope with parameter γ, and the resulting equality constraint is handled by a penalty parameter μ, yielding a single-loop stochastic algorithm with three coupled updates per iteration (θ for the Moreau minimizer, δ for the adversarial variable, and ω for the model parameters). For SAM, the gradient of the penalized objective naturally recovers the discarded Hessian term, which is approximated Hessian-free using Q random Gaussian directions. The algorithm achieves a convergence rate of O(1/K^((1−2p)/2)) on the stationarity residual under weak convexity and bounded-variance assumptions, and empirically outperforms standard SAM on CIFAR-10 under label noise while producing flatter loss landscapes (lower Hessian norm, trace, and top eigenvalues).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, the proposed bilevel method achieves 96.34% clean accuracy, 93.41% at 10% label noise, and 91.48% at 20% label noise, consistently outperforming SAM (96.27%, 92.84%, 90.80%) and SGD (95.47%, 89.58%, 82.64%). Across backbones (ResNet-34/50/101), the method reaches 97.75%, 96.50%, and 96.69% respectively, surpassing SAM by up to 1.14 points (ResNet-34). For GAN generation on StackedMNIST, the method captures all 1000 modes with a KL divergence of 0.08, outperforming R3GAN (0.12) and PacGAN (0.28). Sharpness metrics confirm flatter loss landscapes: Hessian norm 3.84 vs. SAM's 4.41, and top eigenvalue 520.9 vs. SAM's 621.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM [16], the bilevel method wins on all noise levels (by 0.07–0.68 points) and on ResNet-34 (by 1.14 points), while roughly matching SAM on ResNet-50 and ResNet-101; it also achieves lower Hessian norm (3.84 vs. 4.41) and top eigenvalue (520.9 vs. 621.3), confirming flatter minima. For GAN generation, the method outperforms DcGAN [54] (99 modes, KL 3.40), VEEGAN [60] (150 modes, KL 2.95), WGAN [3] (959 modes, KL 0.73), PacGAN [34] (992 modes, KL 0.28), and R3GAN [20] (1000 modes, KL 0.12) on both mode coverage and KL divergence, achieving 1000 modes with KL 0.08. In 2D wheel generation (Figure 2), the method captures all 8 modes, whereas SNGAN [49], LCGAN [15], and UGAN [47] exhibit mode collapse. In synthetic min-max convergence (Figure 1), the method converges fastest in both ‖ω−ω*‖ and ‖δ−δ*‖ compared to Hessian-based schemes (RHG [17], CG [53], IAPTT [37]), first-order methods (BOME [35], VPBGD [57]), and the single-loop BAMM [39]. In large-scale computation (Figure 3), the method is 16.8% faster than BAMM at 10,000 samples and substantially outperforms value-function-based schemes (BVFSM [38], BOME, VPBGD) as feature dimensions grow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bilevel optimization perspective for adversarial learning, previously applied to fast adversarial training by Zhang et al. [80], to Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and Generative Adversarial Networks. The algorithmic core is a single-loop stochastic method adopted from Liu et al. [40] (ICLR 2024), which itself builds on the Moreau envelope reformulation of Gao et al. [18]. The key conceptual departure from practical SAM is that the bilevel formulation naturally retains the Hessian-dependent term that standard implementations discard for efficiency, while the closed-form lower-level solution (normalized gradient) ensures no additional computational overhead is introduced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified bilevel framework offers a principled way to recover the geometric (Hessian) information lost in practical SAM without incurring second-order computational costs, and the convergence guarantee (O(1/K^((1-2p)/2))) provides theoretical grounding absent in most practical SAM variants. However, experiments are limited to CIFAR-10 and StackedMNIST with small GANs, leaving open whether the framework scales to ImageNet-scale classification or high-resolution image generation. A key open question is whether the Hessian-vector product approximation (via random Gaussian vectors) remains efficient and accurate for very deep networks, and whether the weak convexity assumptions in the convergence proof hold for modern overparameterized architectures.
