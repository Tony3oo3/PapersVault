## Problem

Supervised deep hashing is highly sensitive to label noise because the discrete nature of binary codes amplifies small annotation errors into large output shifts, leading to fragmented representations. Existing noise-robust hashing methods follow a label-centric paradigm—reweighting, filtering, or correcting labels—but largely ignore the geometric structure of the hash embedding space, which is critical for maintaining stable and semantically consistent codes under noisy supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250149/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed through a VGG-16 backbone with the first seven convolutional layers fine-tuned) and noisy multi-label vectors (one-hot or multi-hot over C semantic categories) as input, and produces L-bit binary hash codes (L ∈ {16, 32, 64, 128}) via sign binarization of continuous embeddings. The noisy labels are not directly discarded but softly calibrated through an uncertainty score, and the training set is dynamically partitioned into clean and noisy subsets each iteration based on prototype alignment scores.

## Method

SEGA shapes the semantic geometry of the hash space through three co-evolving modules: (1) Prototype-Guided Semantic Anchoring maintains learnable class prototypes as structural anchors, supervising clean samples (identified by a percentile threshold on alignment scores) with a softmax prototype loss and regularizing noisy samples with a contrastive loss; (2) Uncertainty-Guided Supervision Calibration computes a unified uncertainty score from energy-based prediction confidence and K-NN structural divergence, then softly reweights labels to attenuate unreliable gradients; (3) Structure-Aware Interpolation generates virtual samples by mixing only semantically similar pairs (sharing at least one label) with different uncertainty levels, transmitting supervision from confident to uncertain samples to regularize decision boundaries. The key insight is that explicitly modeling and reinforcing the geometric structure of the embedding space—rather than merely correcting labels—yields hash codes that are inherently more robust to supervision noise, a claim supported by two theoretical results bounding label-correctness approximation and structure preservation under interpolation.

## Results

Under 60% pairflip label noise, SEGA achieves 73.56 MAP on CIFAR-10, 76.48 on Flickr25K, 64.61 on NUS-WIDE, and 47.58 on MS COCO at 128 bits, outperforming the strongest baseline STAR by 1.7–6.4 points across datasets. Under 60% symmetric noise, SEGA reaches 69.01, 75.04, 64.12, and 47.31 MAP respectively, again leading all competitors. The advantage grows with code length and noise severity: at 80% noise on NUS-WIDE, SEGA maintains MAP above 60 while baselines like CSQ and DPN collapse below 50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEGA outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset, code length, and noise type. Among standard deep hashing methods (DPSH, HashNet, DCH, GreedyHash, WGLHH, CSQ, OrH, etc.), the gap is largest: SEGA exceeds the best standard method (OrH, 58.37 on CIFAR-10 128-bit pairflip) by 15.2 points. General noise-robust methods REL and Jo-SRC are also clearly surpassed (e.g., 73.56 vs. 61.74 on CIFAR-10 128-bit). The closest competitors are the noise-resilient hashing methods DIOR and STAR; SEGA beats STAR by 1.73 points on CIFAR-10 (73.56 vs. 71.83), 4.52 on Flickr25K (76.48 vs. 71.96), 6.44 on NUS-WIDE (64.61 vs. 58.17), and 4.37 on MS COCO (47.58 vs. 43.21) at 128-bit pairflip. The margin over DIOR is even wider, reaching 11.97 points on NUS-WIDE. Under symmetric noise the pattern holds: SEGA leads STAR by 2.25 (CIFAR-10), 3.51 (Flickr25K), 4.23 (NUS-WIDE), and 4.52 (MS COCO) at 128 bits.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEGA departs from the dominant label-centric paradigm in noisy hashing (reweighting, noise transition matrices, sample selection) by reframing the problem as semantic geometry shaping in the hash embedding space. It builds directly on DIOR's prototype alignment loss and dual-partition strategy, extending them with a unified uncertainty score that fuses energy-based prediction confidence and structural neighborhood divergence. The structure-aware interpolation module is a constrained adaptation of Mixup, restricted to pairs that share labels but differ in uncertainty, rather than arbitrary sample mixing. The theoretical grounding (Theorems 3.1–3.2) connects the uncertainty weighting to a probabilistic label-correctness estimate and the interpolation to a cosine-similarity preservation bound, giving the geometric framing a formal basis that prior noisy-hashing work lacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly shaping the geometric structure of the hash space—rather than merely correcting or filtering labels—yields substantially more robust retrieval under extreme (up to 80%) synthetic label corruption, with gaps over the strongest baselines (STAR, DIOR) widening at higher noise rates. This suggests a complementary axis of robustness that label denoising alone cannot capture, potentially applicable to other structure-sensitive tasks like multi-modal retrieval or multi-label classification under weak supervision. A key limitation is the reliance on a static retrieval database and synthetic noise models; adapting SEGA to dynamic, online, or naturally occurring noise distributions remains open. The single-backbone (VGG-16) evaluation also leaves open whether the geometric gains transfer to transformer-based encoders or larger-scale datasets.
