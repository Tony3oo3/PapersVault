## Problem

Modern LLMs and vision transformers are too large for practical deployment, and structured depth pruning—removing entire transformer blocks—is a promising compression strategy. However, existing depth pruning methods (UIDL, LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT, LLM-Streamline) all require a post-pruning healing or fine-tuning phase to recover lost performance, which is computationally expensive and time-consuming. The paper asks whether a contiguous sequence of pruned blocks can be compensated by a single linear transformation estimated from a small calibration set, eliminating the need for any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained transformer model (decoder LLMs like LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Falcon-11B, or encoder ViTs like CLIP-L/14) and a small calibration dataset of a few thousand samples (instruction-tuned text such as SlimOrca, plain web text such as FineWeb, or image-text pairs from MIMIC). It produces a compressed model in which a contiguous sequence of n transformer blocks is removed and replaced by a single d×d linear transformation matrix that is algebraically fused into the down-projection weight of the preceding block's MLP, so the output architecture is identical to the original except for the deleted blocks—no new parameters are introduced.

## Method

ReplaceMe operates in three steps. First, it identifies the optimal cut location by sliding a window of n layers and selecting the position that minimizes the cosine distance between the hidden states immediately before and after the window (Eq. 4). Second, it estimates a linear transformation T that maps the MLP output of the last retained block to the full output of the first removed block, using either a closed-form least-squares solution (L2 objective) or numerical Adam optimization of a per-token cosine distance objective (Eq. 7–8), with the latter simplified to reduce memory by subtracting the attention residual. Third, the estimated T is fused into the MLP down-projection weights of the preceding block via matrix multiplication, preserving the standard transformer architecture with zero additional parameters. The method is entirely training-free—no healing, fine-tuning, or architectural modification is required—and can be extended to multiple non-consecutive linear transforms (Multi_LT_NC) for higher compression ratios. Ablations show that instruction-tuned calibration data outperforms plain text for benchmark accuracy, while self-generated data improves perplexity, and that L1/L2 regularization on T trades off accuracy against perplexity.

## Results

On Llama 2 7B at 25% compression, ReplaceMe (Cosine) achieves 41.9% average accuracy (92.5% relative performance) without any training or healing, outperforming all baselines that require post-pruning fine-tuning. On Llama 3 8B Instruct at 25% compression, ReplaceMe (Cosine) retains 90.9% of the original model's performance with a Lambada OpenAI perplexity of 15.88, compared to 82.5% and 2216.96 for UIDL. The method also generalizes to vision transformers, where CLIP-L/14 pruned by 13% retains near-original accuracy (e.g., 0.939 vs 0.956 on CIFAR-10 zero-shot) without any retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReplaceMe (Cosine) outperforms UIDL [13] on average accuracy (41.9% vs 40.9% on Llama 2 7B; 90.9% vs 82.5% RP on Llama 3 8B) while being entirely training-free, whereas UIDL requires LoRA-based healing on MLP layers. Against LLM-Pruner [29], ReplaceMe achieves a substantial margin of 6.5 percentage points in average accuracy on Llama 2 7B (41.9% vs 35.4%) and 5.6 points in RP on Llama 3 8B (90.9% vs 85.3%), with a dramatically lower perplexity (15.88 vs 12.31 on Lambada). On Llama 3 8B, ReplaceMe also surpasses SVD-LLM [53] by 5.6 RP points (90.9% vs 85.3%) and reduces perplexity from 29.90 to 15.88. LLM-Streamline [3] is the closest competitor on Llama 2 7B (41.6% vs 41.9% avg accuracy), but ReplaceMe still edges it out while requiring no healing step. Across all four baselines, ReplaceMe is the only method that is fully training-free, and it consistently achieves the highest or near-highest average accuracy at 25% compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReplaceMe sits at the intersection of two threads in structured depth pruning: (1) the observation that contiguous transformer blocks can be removed with minimal loss (UIDL, ShortGPT, Shortened LLaMA), which typically requires a post-pruning healing/fine-tuning phase, and (2) the finding that certain transformer blocks exhibit near-linear behavior (Razzhigaev et al., 'Your transformer is secretly linear'). Where LLM-Streamline replaces pruned blocks with a small trained network (FFN or transformer layer), ReplaceMe replaces them with a single linear transformation estimated in closed form (least-squares) or via a few Adam steps on a cosine objective, then fuses it into the existing MLP down-projection. This eliminates both the architectural modification and the healing step, positioning the method as a strictly training-free generalization of UIDL-style layer removal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that practitioners can compress 7B–70B LLMs by 25–37.5% with only a small calibration set and no fine-tuning, which removes the most expensive and time-consuming step in current depth-pruning pipelines. The method's generality across LLMs (Llama, Qwen, Falcon, Mistral) and vision transformers (CLIP-ViT) suggests the linear-approximation hypothesis is broadly applicable. Key open questions include: how far compression can be pushed before the linear approximation fundamentally breaks down (the paper acknowledges healing becomes necessary above ~37.5%), whether task-specific calibration data can close the remaining gap to the full model, and whether the approach extends to encoder-decoder or mixture-of-experts architectures where block redundancy may differ.
