## Problem

Existing post-hoc OOD detection methods either overlook in-distribution statistics or incorporate them ineffectively. The closest prior work, fDBD, regularizes its decision-boundary distance by the feature norm, which the authors show via the law of sines introduces a sin(α) term uncorrelated with ID/OOD separation, degrading performance. The paper asks whether a purely geometric, angle-based score computed relative to the in-distribution mean can capture ID/OOD structure more cleanly.

## Input / Output

Given a pretrained classifier, the method consumes the penultimate-layer feature vector z ∈ ℝ^D, the linear head weights W ∈ ℝ^{|Y|×D} and biases b ∈ ℝ^{|Y|}, and the precomputed mean μ_ID of in-distribution features. It outputs a single scalar ORA score (the maximum relative angle across all class-pair decision boundaries), which is compared against a threshold λ set from a held-out ID validation set to yield an ID/OOD decision. Because the score is scale-invariant, scores from multiple heterogeneous models can be summed directly for ensemble detection without calibration.

## Method

ORA derives closed-form hyperplane equations for every pairwise decision boundary from the classifier head, projects the feature z onto each boundary, then centers both z and its projection relative to μ_ID to form a triangle in feature space. The score is the arccosine of the cosine similarity between the centered feature vector and its centered boundary projection, i.e., the angle θ at the μ_ID origin. The final score takes the maximum θ across all boundaries between the predicted class and every other class, capturing the model's confidence in the least-likely alternative. This design is both translation-invariant (via centering) and scale-invariant (via the angle), which the authors exploit to (i) combine ORA with activation-shaping methods like ReAct and ASH, and (ii) aggregate scores from multiple architectures by simple summation. The authors further show that contrastively learned representations (SCL, CLIP) impose geometric structure that amplifies the angle gap between ID and OOD samples, yielding the largest gains on ResNet-SCL and CLIP backbones.

## Results

On the ImageNet OOD benchmark spanning 9 model backbones, ORA achieves the best average FPR95 of 38.34%, outperforming the second-best method (Maha+ at 42.64%) by 3.18 percentage points. ORA attains the lowest FPR95 in 5 out of 9 model settings, with its single best score of 25.04% on ResNet-50(SCL). On CIFAR-10, ORA reduces average FPR95 to 10.97% (vs. fDBD's 11.85%), and on CLIP it achieves 23.94% FPR95 (linear probe) and 25.85% (zero-shot). The ORA ensemble across ResNet-50, ResNet-50-SCL, and ViT-B/16 further reduces FPR95 to 22.53%, a 2.51-point improvement over the best single model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORA's closest competitor is fDBD (Liu & Qin, ICML 2024), which ORA outperforms by 5.56 points in average FPR95 (38.34% vs. 43.90%) and by 7.74 points on ImageNet ResNet-50-SCL (25.04% vs. 32.78%); the paper shows fDBD's regularization denominator introduces an uninformative sin(α) term that degrades separation. Against Maha+ (Müller & Hein, ICML 2025), the second-best average method at 42.64%, ORA leads by 4.30 points on average and wins decisively on ResNet-50 (43.63% vs. 49.67%) and ResNet-50-SCL (25.04% vs. 45.06%), though Maha+ edges out ORA on Swin (48.21% vs. 51.56%) and DeiT (54.18% vs. 54.16%). Logit-based baselines (MSP, Energy, MaxL) are consistently outperformed by 10–25+ points in FPR95, confirming the advantage of feature-space scoring. Among angle/cosine-based methods, P-Cos (Galil et al.) and R-Cos (Bitterwolf et al.) achieve competitive averages (41.52% and 42.98%) but are surpassed by ORA on 7 of 9 backbones. On the CIFAR-10 benchmark, ORA (10.97% FPR95) beats fDBD (11.85%), KNN+ (13.35%), and SSD+ (18.51%), while on CLIP zero-shot ORA (25.85%) outperforms fDBD/zs (25.23%) and P-Cos/zs (32.46%) but trails R-Mah/zs (22.57%) and fDBD/lp (25.17%). ORA also combines additively with activation shaping (ReAct), dropping FPR95 from 25.04% to 20.36%, a 4.68-point gain over standalone ORA.

## Relation to Prior Work

ORA is a direct geometric refinement of fDBD (Liu and Qin, ICML 2024), which estimates OOD-ness via distance to decision boundaries. The paper shows that fDBD decomposes as sin(θ)/sin(α) in a triangle formed by the feature, its boundary projection, and the ID mean, and that the sin(α) denominator is uninformative for ID/OOD separation—ORA simply drops it and uses the relative angle θ. This places ORA in the post-hoc score-function family alongside KNN, R-Mah, Maha+, and angle-based methods like P-Cos and R-Cos, but distinguishes itself by centering the angle computation at the in-distribution feature mean (incorporating ID statistics) and by being scale-invariant, a property the authors connect to relative-representation results from Moschella et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scale-invariance of the angle score enables a practically simple ensemble strategy—summing ORA scores across heterogeneous architectures (ResNet-50, ResNet-50-SCL, ViT-B/16) yields a 2.51% FPR95 gain over the best single model without any learned combination layer. The strong performance with contrastively learned features (SCL, CLIP) hints at a deeper geometric link between contrastive structure and OOD separability that remains unexplored. Limitations include competitive-but-not-dominant near-OOD performance (NINCO, SSB-Hard) and the requirement of access to classifier head weights for decision-boundary computation, which constrains applicability to models with linear heads or requires the CLIP-style text-embedding workaround. A natural open direction is data-driven per-class weighting of angle scores to further exploit ID statistics.
