## Problem

Standard eluder dimension analysis for generalized linear models inherently introduces a worst-case curvature parameter κ in the leading regret term, which cancels the small-cost factor η(a⋆) and destroys first-order (instance-adaptive) gains. The paper proves this κ-dependence is unavoidable for global eluder dimensions via a lower bound, showing that no first-order regret bound can be obtained without a new complexity measure.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a loss function (log-loss or Poisson loss), a model class (GLM with sigmoid or exponential link over a bounded parameter set), a nondecreasing confidence-width sequence, and sequential action/cost (bandits) or state/action/cost (RL) observations. They output an action per round (bandits) or a deterministic policy per episode (RL), with the theoretical guarantee being a first-order regret bound whose leading term scales with √(n·η(a⋆)) rather than a worst-case κ-dependent quantity. The confidence sets are constructed via excess-loss minimisation rather than squared loss, and the eluder dimension is evaluated only within a small-excess-loss neighbourhood of the true model.

## Method

The core contribution is a localised ℓ₁-eluder dimension that restricts the eluder sequence to a small-excess-loss neighbourhood (bounded by σ) of the optimal model, rather than the full function class. This localisation moves the worst-case curvature κ from the leading regret term into the additive lower-order term Γ′ₙ. The algorithm ℓ-UCB (bandits) and its RL extension ℓ-GOLF implement version-space optimism: at each step they build a confidence set of functions whose empirical excess loss does not exceed the ERM by more than βₜ, then play the most optimistic action/policy within that set. The analysis requires the loss to satisfy three conditions—boundedness, a Bernstein-type variance condition, and a triangular-discrimination triangle condition—which hold for log-loss and Poisson loss but fail for squared loss. A lower bound (Theorem 2) confirms that the global eluder dimension of GLM excess-loss classes grows exponentially in the parameter norm S, making localisation essential rather than merely beneficial.

## Results

The paper establishes a first-order regret bound for ℓ-UCB in stochastic bandits: R_n ≤ inf_σ {3√(n·η(a*)·Γ_n^σ) + 6Γ_n^σ + (4β_n/σ + 1)·dim_elud^σ(σ; Φ̄(F)) + 1}, where the localised eluder dimension dim_elud^σ replaces the global version and removes the κ-dependence from the leading term. A matching lower bound (Theorem 2) shows the global ℓ₁-eluder dimension for GLMs is exponential in the parameter norm S (e.g., exp(Ω(min{|S|, d-1}/4300)) for the logistic model), proving localisation is necessary. For the logistic bandit, Proposition 5 yields R_n ≤ C√(n·η(a*)·d·β_n)·(1+log(1+Sn)) + C·d·β_n·[(1+log(1+Sn))² + e^S·S·log(1+S)] + 12e^S, and Theorem 6 extends this to finite-horizon RL via ℓ-GOLF with the first κ-free first-order bound: R_n ≤ inf_σ {5√(Hn·v₁*(s₁)·Γ_n^σ) + 12H·Γ_n^σ + (4β_n/σ+1)·d̃_n^σ + H}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper makes purely theoretical comparisons to prior first-order regret results. Against Wang et al. (2023, 2024) for online RL, the key distinction is that their bounds scale with the global Bellman eluder dimension, which for logistic linear models carries a hidden κ-dependence in the leading term that cancels the small-cost benefit; the present work's localised dimension moves κ entirely into the additive Γ_n' term, yielding a genuinely instance-adaptive bound. Against Faury et al. (2022) for logistic bandits, the paper's Proposition 5 nearly matches their upper bound R_n ≤ C·S·d·√(n·v(a*))·log(n/δ) + C·S⁶·d·k·(log(n/δ))², with the suboptimality being the use of η(a*) (small-cost) rather than v(a*) = η(a*)(1−η(a*)) (simultaneous small-cost and small-reward); moreover, Faury et al. require an algorithmic warm-up phase while the present work achieves localisation purely in the analysis. The upper bound of Proposition 5 also nearly matches the minimax lower bound of Abeille et al. (2021), R_n ≥ C·d·√(n·v(a*)), confirming near-optimality in the logistic bandit regime. The paper further contrasts its analysis-only localisation with the likelihood-ratio approaches of Lee et al. (2024) and Emmenegger et al. (2024), which are restricted to the MLE (well-specified) setting, whereas the present results hold under the weaker bounded-cost assumption without requiring observations to be generated by a GLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the eluder dimension framework of Russo and Van Roy (2013) and the ℓ₁-eluder dimension of Liu et al. (2022), but identifies a fundamental limitation: in generalised linear model settings, the global eluder dimension necessarily scales with a worst-case curvature parameter κ that cancels any small-cost (first-order) regret improvement. It departs from Faury et al. (2022), who achieve first-order logistic bandit bounds via an algorithmic warm-up (optimal design phase), by instead localising the eluder dimension purely in the analysis, which is essential for the RL extension where random access to state-action pairs makes optimal design infeasible. The RL algorithm ℓ-GOLF generalises the GOLF framework of Jin et al. (2021) to arbitrary loss functions, and the triangular discrimination machinery of Foster and Krishnamurthy (2021) underpins the regret decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first κ-free first-order regret bound for finite-horizon RL with bounded cumulative costs, resolving a gap where prior results (Wang et al., 2023, 2024) had a hidden κ-dependence that silently negated their small-cost claims. A key limitation is that the bound depends on η(a⋆) (small-cost) rather than v(a⋆) (variance-dependent), because the triangle condition is only assumed one-sided; recovering the stronger v(a⋆) bound while retaining generality (e.g., Poisson GLMs) remains open. The analysis-only localisation technique is broadly applicable and suggests that other global complexity measures in RL may similarly benefit from localisation to yield genuinely instance-adaptive guarantees.
