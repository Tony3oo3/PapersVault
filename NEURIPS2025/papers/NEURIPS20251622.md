## Problem

LoRA-based federated fine-tuning methods suffer from accuracy degradation under data heterogeneity because the low-rank constraint cannot capture the higher effective rank of weight updates in non-I.I.D. settings, and existing fixes for exact aggregation (e.g., FedEx-LoRA's residual correction, FFA-LoRA's frozen A) either inflate communication costs or reduce model expressivity. Additionally, prior methods do not accommodate computational heterogeneity, where clients have vastly different hardware budgets for trainable parameters.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained backbones (ViT-B/16 at 85M params, T5-Base at 224M, LLaMA3.2-1B) with LoRA adapters inserted only in self-attention query and value projection matrices. Each client receives the frozen bases B_i, A_i (initialized with orthogonal column/row spaces via Gram-Schmidt or random normal) and trains only the H_i matrices and scalar scaling factors s_i on its local data. Clients upload the products s_i·H_i directly to the server, which averages them per head, so the per-round communication cost matches vanilla LoRA with no additional backbone transmission.

## Method

RAVAN reparameterizes each weight update ΔW as a weighted sum of h augmented LoRA heads s_i·B_i·H_i·A_i, where B_i and A_i are frozen at initialization with mutually orthogonal column and row spaces, and only H_i (an r×r matrix) and the lightweight scalar s_i are trained. This design raises the effective rank of the update from O(1) (as in multi-head vanilla LoRA) to √(Nh) within the same trainable parameter budget N, better approximating the higher-rank updates observed in non-I.I.D. federated settings. For computational heterogeneity, each client selects a subset of heads to fine-tune based on its resource budget using random, weight-based, or gradient-based scoring, while frozen heads simply retain their previous values at aggregation. Exact aggregation is preserved because the server averages the uploaded s_i·H_i products per head and resets s_i=1 each round, avoiding the inexactness of separately averaging B and A. The method requires no full-parameter fine-tuning round for initialization (unlike Fed-SB) and incurs no extra communication overhead relative to standard LoRA.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 50 non-I.I.D. clients (lower parameter budget, N=1.2M), RAVAN achieves 73.80% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline FedIT (68.15%) by 5.65% and FedEx-LoRA (66.58%) by 7.22%. On SVHN under the same setting, RAVAN reaches 89.17% accuracy versus 83.30% for FFA-LoRA (+5.87%). Scaling to LLaMA3.2-1B on GLUE, RAVAN attains 83.90% average accuracy, exceeding the next-best baseline Fed-SB (83.21%) by 0.69%. Across all 16 vision and language configurations, RAVAN achieves the best PEFT performance in 27/32 settings, with the largest margins in the non-I.I.D. regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAVAN outperforms all six baselines across every vision and language configuration tested. Against FedIT [41], the gap widens in the non-I.I.D. regime: on CIFAR-100 with 50 non-I.I.D. clients (lower budget), RAVAN leads by 5.65% (73.80% vs. 68.15%), while the I.I.D. gap is only 2.27%. Against FedEx-LoRA [32], RAVAN's advantage is largest under data heterogeneity, reaching 7.22% on CIFAR-100 (50 non-I.I.D., lower budget) and 19.95% at the higher budget (77.81% vs. 57.86%). FFA-LoRA [34] suffers the most severe accuracy collapse under non-I.I.D. splits (59.98% on CIFAR-100, 50 clients), where RAVAN leads by 13.82%. Fed-SB [33], which requires an expensive full-parameter initialization round, is outperformed by RAVAN in all settings, with the largest margin of 2.69% on CIFAR-100 (50 non-I.I.D., lower budget). In the computational heterogeneity experiments (Figure 3, Figure 6), RAVAN's head-freezing mechanism avoids the per-client SVD (FlexLoRA [4]) and hard-rank truncation (HetLoRA [9]) that cause the strongest baseline to lose 11% accuracy under a skewed-right budget distribution, while RAVAN loses only 2% on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAVAN builds directly on the LoRA-XS formulation (Baalazy et al., 2024), which augments standard LoRA with a trainable middle matrix H while freezing B and A, and extends it to multiple heads with orthogonal frozen bases. It departs from multi-head vanilla LoRA methods (HydraLoRA, LoRAMoE) by showing that the augmented BHA structure yields a fundamentally higher effective rank (√(Nh) vs. N/2d) under the same parameter budget, and from exact-aggregation FL methods (FedEx-LoRA, FFA-LoRA, Fed-SB) by preserving exactness without requiring full-weight communication or expensive SVD-based initialization. For computational heterogeneity, RAVAN replaces the rank-truncation and per-client SVD strategies of HetLoRA and FlexLoRA with a simpler head-freezing mechanism that avoids approximation loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAVAN provides a practical, communication-efficient recipe for federated fine-tuning of LLMs on resource-constrained edge devices, with consistent 2–8% gains over strong baselines across vision and language tasks and model sizes up to 1B parameters. The clean rank analysis (√(Nh) effective rank) gives practitioners a principled way to choose the number of heads, and the head-freezing mechanism offers a straightforward path to handling heterogeneous hardware without the approximation overhead of prior methods. Open questions remain around differential-privacy compatibility, cross-layer head selection, and data-aware initialization of the frozen bases, all of which the authors identify as future directions.
