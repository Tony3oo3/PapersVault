## Problem

Semi-supervised label distribution learning (SSLDL) remains largely unexplored, and existing pseudo-label-based SSL methods (FixMatch, MixMatch) fail in this setting because they minimize only KL divergence, which does not preserve the ranking relationships between labels. This produces skewed pseudo-labels with incorrect label orderings, causing the model to learn wrong semantic relationships. Additionally, forcing exact numeric matching of pseudo-label distributions leads to overfitting, further degrading performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255282/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (from Twitter, Flickr, Emotion6, and RAF datasets) as input and produces a normalized probability distribution over a fixed set of emotion labels (6–8 classes depending on the dataset) as output. The backbone is a ResNet-50 pre-trained on ImageNet, with weak augmentations (flip-and-shift) used for pseudo-label generation via ensemble averaging over H augmentations, and strong augmentations (RandAugment, Cutout) used for the consistency loss. The output distribution satisfies the constraint that all label description degrees sum to 1.

## Method

RankMatch is a two-phase semi-supervised training framework built on a ResNet-50 backbone. In the supervised phase, it combines standard KL divergence with a novel Pairwise Ranking Relationship (PRR) loss that enforces alignment between predicted and ground-truth label rankings while preserving meaningful margins (a threshold t filters out negligible differences caused by annotation noise). In the self-training phase, pseudo-label distributions are generated by averaging model outputs over multiple weak augmentations, and a flexible unsupervised PRR loss is applied that enforces only the relative ranking among pseudo-labels while deliberately ignoring their absolute margins—this prevents overfitting to noisy pseudo-label values. The total loss is L_s + L_uc + λ(L_PRR_L + L_PRR_u), and the paper provides a theoretical proof that incorporating the PRR term tightens the generalization bound compared to KL-only training.

## Results

On the four LDL benchmarks (Emotion6, Flickr-LDL, Twitter-LDL, RAF-LDL) with 10% labeled data, RankMatch achieves the best Canberra distance across all datasets (3.3902, 4.4060, 3.7370, 3.0178 respectively), outperforming the next-best method by 0.09–1.37 points. On Cosine similarity (10% labeled), RankMatch reaches 0.8121 (Emotion6), 0.8489 (Flickr), 0.8544 (Twitter), and 0.7901 (RAF), leading all baselines except DF-LDL on RAF (0.8427). Performance scales with more labels: on Twitter-LDL, Canberra improves from 3.7370 (10%) to 3.2913 (40%), and Kendall tau correlation rises from 0.4927 to 0.5452, confirming that the PRR loss preserves ranking structure even under limited supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RankMatch outperforms all ten baselines on Canberra distance across every dataset and labeled-data proportion, with the largest margins over SSMLL-CAP (e.g., 3.7370 vs. 5.8983 on Twitter-LDL at 10%) and PCLP (4.4060 vs. 5.2781 on Flickr-LDL). Among the deep-learning SSL baselines, FixMatch-LDL and MixMatch-LDL are the closest competitors on Cosine similarity (0.8079/0.7585 vs. RankMatch's 0.8121 on Emotion6), but RankMatch still leads by 0.004–0.054 points; the gap widens substantially on distance metrics (Canberra 3.5080/3.6080 vs. 3.3902). The dual-network GCT-LDL and traditional SA-LDL are consistently weaker, with SA-LDL particularly struggling on Flickr-LDL (Canberra 5.4612 vs. 4.4060). Notably, DF-LDL—a supervised LDL method adapted for SSL—achieves the highest Cosine on RAF-LDL (0.8427 vs. 0.7901), but RankMatch dominates on all distance metrics for RAF (Canberra 3.0178 vs. 3.3281), indicating that the PRR loss better captures distributional shape rather than just angular similarity. On ranking-specific metrics (Table 2), RankMatch achieves the highest Kendall tau (0.5696 on RAF, 0.5535 on Emotion6 at 10%) and Spearman's rho (0.6726, 0.6545), confirming that the PRR loss directly improves label-ordering fidelity beyond what KL-divergence-only training provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the FixMatch/MixMatch pseudo-labeling framework (weak-augmentation ensemble for pseudo-label generation, strong-augmentation consistency loss) to the Label Distribution Learning setting, where the output is a continuous distribution over labels rather than a single class or a binary multi-label vector. The key departure from prior SSL methods is the Pairwise Ranking Relationship (PRR) loss, which explicitly enforces correct inter-label ordering during supervised training to produce pseudo-labels that preserve ranking structure — addressing a failure mode (low KL divergence but inverted label rankings) that KL-only objectives cannot capture. Prior SSLDL methods (SA-LDL, GCT-LDL) are non-end-to-end and rely on manual feature engineering; this work is positioned as the first deep-learning-based SSLDL approach using pseudo-labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSLDL is a practically important but underexplored setting: annotating label distributions (e.g., 315 annotators for RAF-LDL) is far more expensive than single-label annotation, so reducing label demand is high-value. The insight that ranking-only constraints on pseudo-labels outperform strict distribution matching is a useful, generalizable principle for any distributional prediction task. Limitations include reliance on a single random seed with no error bars, baselines partly authored by the same group, and the method still requiring a nontrivial labeled subset. The authors identify LLM-assisted pseudo-label generation as a promising future direction to further reduce annotation cost.
