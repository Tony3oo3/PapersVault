## Problem

Deforming neural signed distance fields under user-specified handle constraints remains an open challenge: evaluating mesh-based energies like ARAP on implicit surfaces requires discretization, and existing neural field deformation methods (e.g., NFGP) are extremely slow (hours per deformation) while explicit mesh-based methods discard the continuous field information. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a local patch meshing representation that enables efficient, resolution-independent computation of the ARAP energy directly on neural SDFs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254158/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a neural SDF (an 8-layer MLP with 256 hidden dims, 6-frequency Fourier features, and Softplus activation, trained via eikonal losses on shapes normalized to [-1,1]³) and a set of user-defined handle pairs (source-target 3D positions) as input. For the neural field pipeline, it outputs a deformed SDF defined as h(x) = f_θ(d_φ⁻¹(x)) via an invertible deformation network; for the mesh pipeline, it outputs deformed vertex positions. Handles can be static (source = target) or moving, and the method supports arbitrary handle configurations without requiring a fixed semantic handle set.

## Method

The core contribution is a local patch meshing scheme that discretizes multiple isosurfaces of the SDF without full mesh extraction: a flat 2D Delaunay triangulation (k=30 points on a disk of radius ρ) is projected onto the surface at each of n=2000 randomly sampled origin points by aligning to the SDF gradient and iteratively applying the closest-surface-point formula. The ARAP energy is then computed in a Monte-Carlo fashion over these patches, using cotangent Laplacian edge weights to penalize deviation from local rigidity. The deformation is encoded as an invertible MLP with 6 coordinate-splitting layers (each a 3-layer MLP with 256 hidden dims and Fourier features), derived from the NICE architecture to preserve volume. Training minimizes L = λ₁·L_handle + λ₂·L_ARAP with ADAM for ~1000 steps, converging in under 3 minutes on consumer hardware (RTX 4070 Ti), compared to ~14 hours for NFGP.

## Results

On the TFD dataset, Implicit-ARAP achieves 0.00% volume error (vs. 5.60–9.40% for all baselines) and 0.85% area error, completing deformation in 2m 48s — roughly 0.75% of the time required by NFGP (14h 26m). On the more challenging DeFAUST dataset, the method achieves 0.00% volume error, 0.63% area error, 2.41% edge-length error, and 3.399° face-angle error in 5m 36s, substantially outperforming NFGP (15.15% vol, 20.02% area, 8.71% EL, 7.577° FA) and SR-ARAP (12.09% vol, 8.67% area, 8.45% EL, 6.833° FA). The invertible MLP architecture is responsible for the exact volume preservation, while the local patch meshing provides robustness to discretization choices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NFGP [57], the closest neural-field baseline, Implicit-ARAP wins decisively on every metric: volume error drops from 6.66% to 0.00% (TFD) and 15.15% to 0.00% (DeFAUST), area error from 5.30% to 0.85% and 20.02% to 0.63%, and runtime from 14h 26m to under 6 minutes (~0.75% of NFGP's time). Against the discrete ARAP [51] and Elastic [9] baselines, Implicit-ARAP loses on local distortion metrics (EL, FA) for TFD — e.g., 3.26% EL vs. 0.33% for ARAP — because these methods solve an exact least-squares problem on a fixed mesh, but it wins on volume (0.00% vs. 9.40%) and area (0.85% vs. 0.29% is a near-tie). Against SR-ARAP [26], the strongest discrete baseline, Implicit-ARAP is competitive on TFD (3.26% vs. 3.44% EL, 4.541° vs. 4.615° FA) but clearly superior on DeFAUST (2.41% vs. 8.45% EL, 3.399° vs. 6.833° FA, 0.63% vs. 8.67% area). Against NSDP [53] on DeformingThings4D, Implicit-ARAP achieves lower volume (0.58% vs. 7.80%), area (2.04% vs. 5.76%), EL (2.03% vs. 3.59%), and FA (4.728° vs. 6.603°) errors, though NSDP is faster at inference (18s vs. 6m 33s) due to its pre-trained data-driven design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical ARAP mesh deformation energy (Sorkine & Alexa, 2007) to the neural field setting by introducing a local patch meshing discretization that projects flat 2D Delaunay triangulations onto multiple isosurfaces of a neural SDF. It builds directly on the NFGP framework (Yang et al., 2021) for neural field deformation via inverse transport, but replaces the continuous thin-shell energy with a discrete, patch-based ARAP loss computed on small local mesh patches. The invertible deformation model reuses the coordinate-splitting architecture from Cai et al. (2022), which inherits volume preservation from the NICE model (Dinh et al., 2015). The key conceptual departure from NFGP is the use of randomly placed, small local patches sampled across multiple isosurfaces rather than a single global discretization or continuous energy formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× speedup over NFGP (2m48s vs 14h26m) makes handle-guided neural field deformation practical on consumer hardware, opening the door to interactive editing workflows for implicit representations. The local patch meshing concept is potentially generalizable to other geometric tasks on neural fields (e.g., curvature estimation, correspondence), though the paper only demonstrates deformation. Key limitations include the lack of exact SDF preservation guarantees, sensitivity to patch radius/density hyperparameters, and the fact that the invertible architecture restricts the deformation space to bijections. An open question the authors highlight is whether one can combine direct SDF output (invertible model) with a fully general deformation space, which would require handling unpredictable SDF changes from unconstrained deformations.
