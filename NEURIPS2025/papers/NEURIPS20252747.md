## Problem

Existing model merging methods require simultaneous access to all expert models, resulting in memory complexity that scales linearly with the number of tasks, and they do not handle the common real-world scenario where models become available sequentially. In a sequential (continual) merging setting, the non-commutative nature of the process introduces unique challenges in preserving previously acquired task knowledge while integrating new models without interference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252747/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of fine-tuned CLIP-ViT model checkpoints (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14) one at a time, along with the shared pre-trained base model, and produces a single merged model checkpoint after each step. Weight matrices in linear layers are processed via SVD-based orthogonal projection, while biases and non-linear layer parameters are handled by direct averaging. The memory footprint remains constant at O(|θ|) regardless of how many tasks have been merged, in contrast to O(T|θ|) for conventional approaches.

## Method

OPCM (Orthogonal Projection-based Continual Merging) is a training-free sequential merging method with two core mechanisms. First, for each incoming task vector ΔW^(t), a full SVD is computed on the previously merged task vector ΔW_merged^(t-1), and the new task vector is projected onto the subspace orthogonal to the top-r_α singular directions (controlled by threshold α), ensuring the projected update is Frobenius-orthogonal to existing merged parameters and thus minimizes task interference. Second, an adaptive time-varying scaling factor λ^(t) normalizes the combined parameter deviation to maintain a stable Frobenius norm distance from the pre-trained model, empirically following a √t trajectory. The general term formula shows the merged model equals the base model plus a scaled sum of all projected task vectors, generalizing Task Arithmetic with orthogonal projections. The method requires no additional training, no hyperparameter search beyond α (robust around 0.5), and achieves 5–8% average accuracy improvement over continual Task Arithmetic and Ties-Merging baselines across 8–20 downstream image classification tasks.

## Results

OPCM achieves 5–8% average accuracy improvement over Continual Task Arithmetic and Continual Ties-Merging across all three CLIP-ViT architectures (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14) and task set sizes (8, 14, 20 tasks). On the most challenging 20-task setting, OPCM reaches 65.7% (ViT-B/32), 70.3% (ViT-B/16), and 76.0% (ViT-L/14) average accuracy, compared to 60.6%, 64.2%, and 70.3% for Continual Task Arithmetic respectively. Backward transfer (BWT) is also improved, with OPCM showing the least forgetting on ViT-L/14 (−2.6% on 8 tasks) versus −7.1% for Continual Task Arithmetic. Results are reported over 10 shuffled task orderings with small standard deviations (≤0.5%), confirming robustness to task ordering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPCM consistently outperforms all four baselines (Continual Fine-Tuning, SWA, Continual Task Arithmetic, and Continual Ties-Merging) on average accuracy across every model size and task set. Against Continual Task Arithmetic, the gap is 5–8% on ACC (e.g., 75.5 vs. 67.5 on ViT-B/32 8-task; 76.0 vs. 70.3 on ViT-L/14 20-task). Against Continual Ties-Merging, the advantage is even larger, reaching 22–27% on 8-task settings (e.g., 87.0 vs. 64.3 on ViT-L/14) and narrowing to 5–8% on 20-task settings. On backward transfer, OPCM shows the best (least negative) BWT on ViT-L/14 (−2.6% on 8 tasks) and ViT-B/16 (−4.8% on 8 tasks), while Continual Task Arithmetic achieves slightly better BWT on some 14-task and 20-task settings (e.g., −1.3% vs. −6.0% on ViT-B/32 14-task). SWA and Continual Fine-Tuning are clearly the weakest baselines, with Continual Fine-Tuning suffering severe forgetting (e.g., 62.6% ACC on ViT-B/32 20-task vs. 89.8% for individual fine-tuned models). The small standard deviations (≤0.5% for OPCM) across 10 shuffled orderings confirm that the method is robust to task ordering, unlike Ties-Merging which shows high variance (e.g., ±10.2 on ViT-B/32 8-task).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weight-interpolation model merging paradigm (Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging) by reframing it as a sequential, continual process where models arrive one at a time rather than all at once. The key conceptual departure is the use of SVD-based orthogonal projection to handle the non-commutativity inherent in sequential merging, drawing on subspace-based ideas from continual learning (orthogonal gradient descent) but applying them to the training-free merging setting. The adaptive √t scaling factor connects to the empirical observation that task vectors are approximately orthogonal, a property noted in prior task arithmetic work but not previously exploited for sequential integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: in real deployments, fine-tuned models become available sequentially (new tasks, new domains), yet all existing merging methods assume simultaneous access. The O(|θ|) memory complexity and training-free nature make this approach immediately deployable without retraining. However, the evaluation is limited to CLIP-ViT image classification, and the SVD computation per weight matrix introduces non-trivial computational cost that scales with matrix dimensions. Open questions include whether the orthogonality assumption on task vectors holds for LLMs or highly correlated task distributions, and whether the method extends to multimodal or generative architectures.
