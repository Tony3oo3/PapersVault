## Problem

Existing model-based RL approaches for mechanism design treat background agents as homogeneous or fail to explicitly represent their unobservable latent traits (skills, preferences, risk attitudes), leading to suboptimal incentive structures in heterogeneous social systems. Compounding this, real-world social interactions are costly and risky, demanding high sample efficiency that standard model-free RL cannot provide.

## Input / Output

The principal receives structured observations consisting of discrete agent states (e.g., grid positions) and a global environment state, along with partial interaction history; agent traits are never directly observed. The mechanism design policy outputs a discrete incentive action (facility placement, team assignment, or tax rate vector), while the SWM simultaneously produces predicted next-state observations and immediate social welfare. The trait trackers output continuous or categorical latent trait estimates, with the posterior tracker consuming full trajectories and the prior tracker operating on partial history for real-time deployment.

## Method

SWM-AP couples a Social World Model with a Mechanism Design Policy via a hierarchical trait-inference architecture. The SWM contains a Posterior Trait Tracker (MLP+LSTM) that infers latent agent traits from complete trajectories and a trait-conditioned dynamics predictor (MLP or GCN+MLP) that forecasts next states and rewards; it is trained by minimizing an ELBO comprising state-prediction MSE and a KL regularization toward a uniform trait prior. The policy embeds a Prior Trait Tracker trained in a supervised fashion to match the posterior estimates from partial history, enabling real-time trait-aware mechanism selection. Policy optimization uses PPO over a combined dataset of real environment rollouts and imagined rollouts generated by the SWM, with the prior trait estimate fed into both the policy network and the world model during imagination. The ELBO derivation formally justifies joint learning of latent traits and dynamics, showing that minimizing the SWM loss maximizes the evidence lower bound under Gaussian dynamics and uniform trait prior assumptions.

## Results

In the 32-agent Facility Location task, SWM-AP achieves the highest final reward (6.62±0.06) and best sample efficiency (274,667 steps to reach MBPO's final performance), outperforming PPO (6.55±0.03, 353,600 steps), MBPO (6.43±0.04, 433,600 steps), and Dreamer (6.57±0.06, 300,800 steps). In the Tax Adjustment (AI-Economist) setting, SWM-AP attains productivity comparable to PPO while significantly improving equality, yielding a higher equality×productivity social welfare metric than both PPO and MBPO. Across all three tasks, SWM-AP's world model achieves lower state and reward prediction losses than the model-based baselines, indicating more accurate dynamics modeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWM-AP consistently outperforms all three baselines on both sample efficiency and final performance in the 32-agent Facility Location task: it requires 22% fewer steps than PPO (274,667 vs. 353,600), 37% fewer than MBPO (274,667 vs. 433,600), and 9% fewer than Dreamer (274,667 vs. 300,800), while also achieving the highest final reward (6.62 vs. 6.55, 6.43, and 6.57 respectively). In the Tax Adjustment task, SWM-AP matches PPO's productivity level but significantly improves equality, whereas MBPO sacrifices substantial productivity in its attempt to increase equality, resulting in lower overall social welfare. In Team Structure Optimization (AdaSociety), SWM-AP again achieves the fewest training steps to target performance and the highest converged group reward, with its trait-conditioned world model producing more accurate next-state predictions than MBPO (e.g., correctly predicting agent movement toward resources where MBPO incorrectly predicts stationary behavior). The consistent advantage across all three diverse tasks suggests that explicit latent trait inference within the world model is the key differentiator, enabling better generalization to heterogeneous agent populations than homogeneous world models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the model-based RL paradigm (PPO for policy optimization, MBPO/Dreamer-style imagined rollouts) and the mechanism design literature in RL, but departs from both by introducing an explicit latent trait inference layer into the world model. Prior multi-agent world models (e.g., COMBO, MABL) simulate joint dynamics but treat agents as homogeneous or lack explicit trait conditioning; classical mechanism design assumes known type distributions or full information. SWM-AP unifies variational inference (via an ELBO-derived objective) with world model learning and policy optimization, using a Posterior Trait Tracker for offline trait estimation and a Prior Trait Tracker for real-time online inference during deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a fundamental gap in AI-driven mechanism design: how to adaptively design incentives for heterogeneous populations whose latent traits are unobservable, without requiring costly real-world trial-and-error. The consistent sample-efficiency and reward gains across three structurally different tasks (facility location, team coordination, taxation) suggest the trait-conditioned world model is a broadly applicable component. Key open questions include scalability to very large agent populations (the 32-agent experiment shows growing memory costs), interpretability of inferred traits beyond confusion-matrix diagnostics, and whether the approach transfers to settings where agent traits are non-stationary or where the principal's own policy influences trait evolution.
