## Problem

Existing red-teaming methods for text-to-image models either require white-box access or assume the attacker knows the specific defense mechanism (e.g., whether a text filter, image classifier, or safety alignment is deployed). In the most realistic and high-risk scenario—commercial black-box APIs like DALL-E 3 or Leonardo.ai—attackers face unknown and diverse defense mechanisms, making it difficult to achieve consistent attack success. The paper tackles how to dynamically adapt to unknown defenses using only coarse-grained feedback (rejection messages or binary NSFW labels) from the target system.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt as input and produces a modified adversarial prompt that, when submitted to the target T2I system, generates images with desired harmful semantics while preserving semantic similarity to the original. The LLM agent (Vicuna-7B) generates N=30 independent modifications per prompt per iteration, and the scoring model operates on CLIP image embeddings (frozen CLIP encoder + learnable single-layer transformation). The framework also extends to text-to-video generation, where individual frames are used during fine-tuning and full videos are generated only at final evaluation.

## Method

RPG-RT operates in iterative cycles: (1) an LLM agent generates multiple prompt modifications, (2) the target T2I black-box system is queried and returns either a rejection or a generated image, and (3) the LLM is fine-tuned via DPO with LoRA using preference pairs constructed from rule-based scoring. The key technical contribution is a scoring model that decouples CLIP representations into a harmful-content component f_n and a benign-semantics component f_s via a learnable transformation, trained with four loss functions (Bradley-Terry ranking for harm severity, invariance for benign semantics, similarity alignment, and reconstruction). Preference rules assign TYPE-3 (NSFW image generated) as most preferred, TYPE-1 (rejected) and TYPE-2 (SFW image) as less preferred, with within-type ordering determined by a composite score combining f_n harm severity and f_s semantic similarity weighted by hyperparameter c. This rule-based approach enables fine-grained control over LLM exploration without requiring knowledge of the target system's specific defenses.

## Results

RPG-RT achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR) on nearly all 19 T2I systems with diverse defense mechanisms, ranging from 32.49% (GuardT2I) to 97.85% (SD v2.1). Against the strongest defense (AdvUnlearn), RPG-RT reaches 40.35% ASR versus a mere 2.04% for the best baseline (P4D-N). On three online commercial APIs, RPG-RT achieves at least 2× the ASR of all baselines, reaching 31.33% on DALL-E 3 where the best competitor (SneakyPrompt) manages only 4.67%. RPG-RT also maintains the lowest Perplexity (PPL) across all methods, indicating the most stealthy prompt modifications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPG-RT outperforms all five baselines on the vast majority of 19 T2I systems. Against white-box methods, MMA-Diffusion and P4D (both K and N variants) are limited by their need for gradient access and fail entirely on SD v2.1/v3; RPG-RT still achieves 97.85% and 97.26% ASR on these models where P4D cannot even run. Among black-box baselines, SneakyPrompt is the strongest competitor on detection-based defenses (e.g., 43.12% on text-cls) but is outperformed by RPG-RT (63.19%) and falls far behind on removal-based defenses (e.g., 8.46% on ESD vs. RPG-RT's 62.91%). Ring-A-Bell is competitive on removal-based defenses (72.46% on SLD-strong) but nearly fails on detection-based systems (0.74% on text-match, 0.00% on GuardT2I), whereas RPG-RT maintains consistent performance across both categories. FLIRT, the most recent black-box baseline, achieves moderate results (e.g., 34.56% on text-match, 41.93% on SLD-strong) but is consistently surpassed by RPG-RT by 15–50 percentage points across most systems. The gap is most dramatic on AdvUnlearn, where RPG-RT achieves 40.35% ASR while the best baseline (FLIRT) reaches only 1.93%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the black-box red-teaming literature for T2I models by removing the assumption that the attacker knows the specific defense mechanism, instead treating feedback from each query as a prior for iterative adaptation. It builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) for fine-tuning an LLM agent and the Bradley-Terry model (1952) for a ranking-based scoring loss, but departs from prior black-box methods (SneakyPrompt, Ring-A-Bell, FLIRT) that are tailored to particular defenses by introducing a rule-based preference modeling framework that dynamically learns latent defense behavior. The scoring model's decoupling of CLIP representations into harmful (f_n) and benign (f_s) components is a novel architectural choice that addresses a specific limitation of using raw CLIP similarity as a reward signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the most realistic and high-risk red-teaming scenario—commercial black-box APIs with unknown, multi-layered defenses—and demonstrates that an iterative LLM+DPO approach can achieve at least 2× the ASR of prior methods on DALL-E 3, Leonardo.ai, and SDXL. The transferability to unseen prompts without re-optimization (single LLM inference) offers a practical efficiency advantage for large-scale red-teaming campaigns. Open questions include how the approach handles adaptive defenses that update over time, whether the single-layer linear scoring model generalizes to more complex semantic spaces, and the computational cost of multiple query iterations versus one-shot methods. The small sample size (10 prompts) for online API experiments limits confidence in the reported ASR gaps.
