## Problem

Existing 3D molecular diffusion models generate high-quality structures but lack effective mechanisms for simultaneously optimizing multiple drug-relevant properties (QED, synthetic accessibility, binding affinity) that are typically predicted by non-differentiable black-box tools. While RL has been widely applied to guide 1D SMILES or 2D graph-based molecular generators, RL-guided optimization of diffusion models for direct 3D de novo molecular generation with multi-objective constraints remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254157/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a condition vector of target property values (QED, SAS, binding affinity thresholds), a number of atoms M, and an initial random noise latent z_T ~ N(0, I). It outputs a full 3D molecular structure consisting of atomic coordinates r ∈ R^{M×3} (constrained to the zero-center-of-gravity subspace) and atom features h ∈ R^{M×d} (one-hot encoded types/charges). The property thresholds are dynamically updated during RL training via a moving average of previously generated molecules, and the generated structures are subsequently scored by RDKit (QED, SAS) and AutoDock Vina (binding affinity to EGFR) for evaluation.

## Method

The framework combines a conditional E(3)-equivariant diffusion model (EDM) backbone with an uncertainty-aware multi-objective RL loop. Surrogate models (Chemprop D-MPNN with evidential learning) predict both the mean and total variance (aleatoric + epistemic) of each molecular property, and the per-property reward is the Gaussian tail probability of exceeding a dynamic threshold, with the multi-objective reward defined as the product of these probabilities (joint satisfaction likelihood). The total reward further incorporates a bonus for validity/uniqueness/novelty and a Tanimoto-based diversity penalty whose weight decays exponentially over episodes to shift from exploration to exploitation. Policy updates use a PPO-style clipped surrogate loss applied to the per-timestep denoising transition probabilities, treating the full reverse diffusion trajectory as a Markov decision process. This design avoids the need for differentiable reward functions and naturally handles conflicting objectives by penalizing molecules with low probability of satisfying any single property.

## Results

On QM9, the proposed method achieves 98.17% validity, 88.90% VUN score, 99.17% molecular stability, and 28.33% top-molecule ratio, improving validity by over 9 percentage points over the best RL baseline (DDPO-IS at 88.82%). On ZINC15, the method reaches near-perfect validity (99.02%) and a 33.40% top-molecule ratio, compared to 30–41% validity and 8–10% top ratio for all baselines. On PubChem, despite the structural complexity of the dataset, the method maintains 100% uniqueness and novelty while achieving 16.23% validity and 88.65% molecular stability, substantially outperforming the best baseline (DDPO-IS: 10.50% validity, 45.37% molecular stability).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four RL-guided diffusion baselines adapted from image generation (SFT-PG, DDPO-SF, DDPO-IS, DPOK) and a vanilla diffusion model (W/O RL) across all three datasets. The proposed method wins decisively on ZINC15, where validity jumps from 30–41% (baselines) to 99.02% and molecular stability from 12–26% to 98.08%, a gap of roughly 58–86 percentage points. On QM9, the method improves validity by ~9 points (98.17% vs. 88.10–88.82%) and molecular stability by up to 13 points over DDPO-IS (99.17% vs. 86.10%), while sacrificing ~6 points in uniqueness (90.90% vs. 96.59–97.57%). On PubChem, the method more than doubles validity (16.23% vs. 7.18–10.50%) and nearly doubles molecular stability (88.65% vs. 33.25–45.37%) over the best baseline. In the ablation study (Table 2), the full framework also outperforms 16 alternative multi-objective strategies spanning scalarization (WS, POO, MMM, LSDW), constraint-based (NMD, NMD-WS, CP, PFM), gradient-based (SVD, PCGrad, CAGrad, GradVac), and uncertainty-based (UCB, EI, MVC, BORE) approaches, with the largest gaps appearing in VUN score (88.90% vs. 69.95–88.75%) and top-molecule ratio (28.33% vs. 13.40–27.02%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Equivariant Diffusion Model (EDM) backbone for 3D molecular generation, extending it with a conditional mechanism and an RL fine-tuning stage. It departs from prior RL-guided diffusion work (DDPO, DPOK, SFT-PG), which was confined to image generation with single-objective rewards, by transplanting PPO-style policy gradients into the 3D molecular domain with multi-objective, uncertainty-aware rewards. It also extends the line of RL-guided molecular generation (previously applied to 1D SMILES via RNNs/VAEs or 2D graphs via Transformers) to native 3D coordinate-space diffusion, and introduces surrogate-model uncertainty (via Chemprop D-MPNN with evidential learning) as the reward signal rather than the scalarization, constraint, or gradient-conflict-resolution strategies used in prior multi-objective molecular optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first end-to-end pipeline that simultaneously optimizes validity, uniqueness, novelty, stability, and multiple drug-relevant properties (QED, SAS, binding affinity) in 3D coordinate space, validated by MD simulations and ADMET profiling against known EGFR inhibitors. A key open question is scalability: the PubChem results (16% validity) reveal that the EDM backbone struggles with large, complex molecules, suggesting the RL framework is only as strong as the underlying diffusion architecture. Future work on scalable 3D diffusion backbones, relaxing the conditional-independence assumption in the multi-objective product reward, and extending to additional targets or property sets would be natural next steps.
