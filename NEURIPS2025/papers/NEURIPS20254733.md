## Problem

Current AI social reasoning benchmarks and training pipelines rely on language-only or language-dominant data, leaving nonverbal social understanding largely unmeasured and underdeveloped in multimodal foundation models. No existing benchmark systematically probes a model's ability to interpret imagined objects, subtle social cues, and theory-of-mind reasoning from purely nonverbal human interaction without spoken dialogue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254733/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question is paired with a mime performance video (1–10 minutes, Creative Commons-licensed from YouTube) and a text prompt that may include start/end timestamps for grounding and scene-level questions. For models without native video support, frames are uniformly sampled with attached timestamps. Answers are open-ended text, scored for semantic equivalence against ground truth using GPT-4o as an automated judge (92% agreement with human graders).

## Method

MIMEQA is a three-level video QA benchmark of 101 mime videos and 806 verified question-answer pairs, organized hierarchically from low-level grounding of imagined objects, through scene-level temporal/affect/intention reasoning, to global-level working memory, social judgment, and theory-of-mind questions. The dataset was constructed by collecting 221 YouTube mime clips, annotating ~10 questions per video with two human annotators, and verifying all annotations with a second reviewer (97.58% agreement), yielding the final 806 pairs. The paper evaluates seven state-of-the-art VideoLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5/2.5-Pro, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-Video, InternVL2.5, VideoLLaMA3) plus a human baseline, and further probes model limitations through text-only ablations, supervised fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-VL on MIMEQA, integration of PoseC3D skeleton-based action labels, and five-fold cross-dataset transfer experiments with Social-IQ 2.0 and IntentQA.

## Results

On MIMEQA, all evaluated VideoLLMs achieve low accuracy, with open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-Video, InternVL2.5, VideoLLaMA3) averaging 19.4–22.2%, GPT-4o reaching 31.3%, and Gemini-2.5-Pro achieving the best score of 38.3%, all far below the human baseline of 86.0%. Performance is weakest on Grounding the Imagined (4.4–28.4% across models) and strongest on Theory of Mind (26.7–54.7%), indicating models struggle most with perceiving imagined objects but handle holistic social reasoning relatively better. Text-only ablations reveal that open-source models gain only 1–7% from video input, while closed-source models show substantial improvements, exposing a strong language bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5-Pro is the strongest model overall at 38.3% average accuracy, outperforming GPT-4o (31.3%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (30.6%) by 7–8 points, and exceeding all open-source models (19.4–22.2%) by roughly 2×. Among open-source models, VideoLLaMA3 (22.2%) and InternVL2.5 (21.6%) slightly edge out Qwen2.5-VL (20.1%) and LLaVA-Video (19.4%), with differences of only 1–3 points. All models show a consistent pattern: global-level questions (ToM, SJ, WM) are answered more accurately than scene-level (I, AR, T) and grounding questions, with Grounding the Imagined being the single weakest category for every model (4.4–28.4%). The gap between the best model (Gemini-2.5-Pro, 38.3%) and humans (86.0%) is 47.7 points, underscoring that no current VideoLLM approaches human-level nonverbal social reasoning. In cross-dataset transfer experiments, models fine-tuned on MIMEQA generalize well to Social-IQ 2.0 (+1.2%) and IntentQA (+2.6%), while the reverse transfer is minimal (+0.4% and +1.1% on MIMEQA), confirming that MIMEQA captures distinct nonverbal reasoning skills not present in conventional social video benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing mime performances—previously used only for narrow action recognition tasks (Mimetics, Cho et al.)—as a holistic benchmark for nonverbal social intelligence in VideoLLMs. It departs from the dominant paradigm of language-dominant social reasoning benchmarks (Social-IQ, IntentQA, MMToM-QA) by removing spoken language entirely, forcing models to rely on gesture, body movement, and imagined objects. The evaluation framework draws on existing VideoLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, GPT-4o, Gemini, LLaVA-Video) and auxiliary perception tools (PoseC3D), but the question hierarchy spanning grounding, scene-level, and global-level nonverbal reasoning is novel in its design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stark 20–38% model accuracy versus 86% human performance reveals a fundamental gap in VideoLLMs' nonverbal social understanding that language-dominant benchmarks mask. The asymmetric transfer finding—MIMEQA-trained models generalize to Social-IQ 2.0 and IntentQA but not vice versa—suggests mime captures a distinct axis of social cognition absent from conventional datasets. Key open questions include how to improve grounding of imagined objects (where even fine-tuning and pose augmentation yield only marginal gains), how to reduce models' over-reliance on language cues, and how to expand cultural diversity beyond the primarily Western context of the current 806-question dataset.
