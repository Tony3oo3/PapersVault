## Problem

Most widely deployed comparison-based preference learning models, including those used in RLHF and DPO for language models, can decrease the score of an item after it is declared preferred over another, violating the intuitive monotonicity property. The only models with a proven monotonicity guarantee (Generalized Bradley-Terry) treat each alternative's score as an independent latent variable and therefore cannot generalize to alternatives that have never been compared. The paper asks whether a generalizing preference learning algorithm can simultaneously guarantee monotonicity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a dataset of pairwise comparison triples (a, b, r) where r ∈ R (e.g., {-1,+1} or [-1,1]), a D-dimensional embedding vector x_a for each alternative a, and an A×A Laplacian matrix L encoding prior similarities between alternatives. It outputs a score vector θ* ∈ R^A computed as x^T β*, where β* is the MAP estimate. The embedding dimension D can be smaller than the number of alternatives A, enabling generalization to unseen alternatives; one-hot encodings of categorical structure (e.g., YouTube channel membership) are a key special case.

## Method

The paper introduces Linear GBT with Diffusion Prior, which extends the Generalized Bradley-Terry model in two ways: scores are parameterized as linear functions of embeddings (θ_a = x_a^T β) rather than free variables, and a graph Laplacian regularization term (1/2)Σ_ab θ_a L_ab θ_b is added to the loss to encourage similar alternatives to receive similar scores. The key theoretical contribution identifies a class of 'diffusion embeddings'—embeddings whose regularized Gram matrices X_λ = x^T x + λI have super-Laplacian inverses—for which monotonicity is provably guaranteed for any root law and any Laplacian prior. The proof proceeds via a differential analysis framework that smooths discrete dataset operations (append, update) into a continuous parameter λ, then shows the score derivative is non-negative by reducing the sign condition to an algebraic property of super-Laplacian matrices. One-hot encodings are shown to be diffusion embeddings, providing a practical and easily verifiable sufficient condition.

## Results

On the Tournesol real-world dataset (1000 YouTube video comparisons, 10-fold cross-validation), the Linear GBT with one-hot channel embeddings achieves an average validation risk of 8.40×10⁻³, compared to 10.1×10⁻³ for the classical GBT without embeddings — a 17% reduction. On synthetic experiments (A=25, N=500), the full embedding [I x]ᵀ outperforms both the classical GBT (embedding I) and the features-only model (embedding x) in normalized MSE across all embedding dimensions D. For the one-hot encoding setting (A=20, D=10), the embedding-based model reaches a target nMSE with substantially fewer comparisons than the classical GBT, demonstrating the generalization benefit of structured embeddings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the classical Generalized Bradley-Terry model (Fageot et al., AAAI 2024), which assigns each alternative an independent latent score with a Gaussian prior and no structural information. On the Tournesol dataset, the proposed Linear GBT with one-hot channel embeddings reduces the average validation risk from 10.1×10⁻³ to 8.40×10⁻³ (a 17% improvement), with the box-plot distribution in Figure 3 confirming consistent gains across all 10 folds. On synthetic data (Figure 2a), the full embedding [I x]ᵀ dominates the classical GBT (embedding I) for all D, combining the stability of the identity component at small D with the feature-based generalization at larger D. In the one-hot encoding experiment (Figure 2b), the embedding-based model requires significantly fewer comparisons N to reach a given nMSE level, highlighting that prior structural knowledge (channel membership) accelerates learning. The paper also shows (Figure 1) that random Gaussian embeddings are rarely 'good' (monotonicity-guaranteeing) unless D ≫ A, whereas concatenation with the identity matrix substantially increases the probability of goodness, consistent with the theoretical Proposition 9.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generalized Bradley-Terry (GBT) model of Fageot et al. (AAAI 2024), which was the only comparison-based preference learning framework proven to guarantee monotonicity, by introducing linear embeddings and a Laplacian-based diffusion prior that enable generalization to previously uncompared alternatives. A key conceptual departure is showing that monotonicity failure is not caused by model nonlinearity (demonstrated with a minimal 2D linear counterexample), but rather by the interaction between the embedding geometry and the comparison structure. The paper connects to the broader monotonicity-violation literature in RLHF/DPO [2, 5, 28, 33] and social choice theory, positioning its contribution as the first model that simultaneously satisfies both monotonicity and generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first preference learning model that provably guarantees monotonicity while also generalizing to unseen alternatives, addressing a fundamental trust issue: users should not see their preferred item's score decrease after reporting a preference. The diffusion-embedding framework (super-Laplacian inverses of Gram matrices) provides a clean algebraic criterion, and the heat-diffusion interpretation (comparisons as heat pumps) offers intuitive understanding. However, the guarantees are restricted to linear models and specific embedding classes (diffusion embeddings, one-hot encodings); checking 'goodness' for arbitrary embeddings remains non-trivial, and whether these results extend to the nonlinear models actually deployed in practice (e.g., LLMs via DPO) is left open.
