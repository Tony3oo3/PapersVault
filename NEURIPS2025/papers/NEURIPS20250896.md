## Problem

Neural world models demand enormous training data and generalize poorly, while existing programmatic world models (e.g., WorldCoder) are limited to simple gridworlds because they perform a discrete combinatorial search for a single monolithic program. This paper tackles the open problem of learning complex, stochastic, partially-observable world models from just a few observations in non-gridworld domains such as Atari games, using symbolic program representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250896/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes object-centric observations parsed by OCAtari (bounding boxes, velocities, and categories per object) rather than raw pixels, along with action sequences and a short (<1000 frame) demonstration trajectory that need not be successful gameplay. It produces a collection of hundreds of small deterministic Python programs (4000+ lines total for Montezuma's Revenge) with learned scalar weights, which together define a probability distribution over next-timestep object attributes. The world model is then used either for hierarchical lookahead planning (producing action sequences) or as a simulator for PPO policy pretraining.

## Method

PoE-World represents the world model as an exponentially-weighted product of programmatic experts, where each expert is a small Python program encoding one causal law (e.g., 'if player touches a skull, player dies'). LLMs (GPT-4o) synthesize these programs from short transition sequences via a multi-module pipeline that first extracts natural-language causal explanations and then converts them into code using helper classes (Obj, ObjList, RandomValues). Programs are interpreted as distributions by placing single-peak distributions on attributes they set and uniform distributions on attributes they leave unset, with a conditional-independence assumption across object features making the product-of-experts normalization tractable per feature. Expert weights are fit by L-BFGS gradient-based maximum likelihood, and low-weight experts are pruned iteratively as new observations arrive. Hard constraints (also LLM-synthesized) are conjoined to the product to rule out physically impossible scenes, and a TAMP-inspired hierarchical planner interleaves high-level abstract graph search (over object-contact states) with low-level MCTS/greedy motion planning to produce executable action sequences.

## Results

On the low-data regime (≤100k environment steps), PoE-World+Planner achieves the best score across all four environments: -12.33±0.88 on Pong, -13.67±0.67 on Pong-Alt, and 100±0.00 on both Montezuma's Revenge and MR-Alt. It is the only method to obtain positive reward on Montezuma's Revenge and its alternative version, where all baselines score 0. On next-observation prediction accuracy (Table 2), PoE-World reaches 0.75 on MR training data and 0.43 on MR-Alt test frames, compared to WorldCoder's 0.36 and 0.12 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO (model-free RL), PoE-World+Planner substantially outperforms in the low-data regime: on Pong, PoE-World scores -12.33 versus PPO@100k's -21.00, and on Pong-Alt, -13.67 versus -18.66. PPO@20M eventually surpasses PoE-World on Pong (17.00 vs. -12.33) but still scores 0 on both Montezuma's Revenge variants, whereas PoE-World achieves 100. Against WorldCoder+Planner (the closest symbolic baseline), PoE-World wins on every environment: -12.33 vs. -17.00 on Pong, -13.67 vs. -19.00 on Pong-Alt, and 100 vs. 0 on both MR variants; the paper attributes this to PoE-World's 4000+ line modular programs versus WorldCoder's <100-line monolithic program, which fails to model platform/ladder constraints and hallucinates abilities. Against LLM-as-Agent (ReAct), PoE-World dominates on all four environments, with ReAct performing at or near random-agent level. On next-observation prediction accuracy, PoE-World also outperforms WorldCoder on all test settings except Pong random frames, where both methods are near chance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-based programmatic world modeling (WorldCoder, CodeWorldModels, Ahmed et al.) by replacing the monolithic single-program representation with an exponentially-weighted product of many small programmatic experts, each encoding a single causal law. The decomposition is motivated by the 'society of mind' perspective from cognitive science and by the practical observation that discrete combinatorial search over a single large program does not scale beyond gridworlds. The hierarchical planner draws on Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) literature, and the object-centric observation interface relies on OCAtari's reverse-engineered Atari RAM parsing rather than raw pixels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a fully symbolic, program-structured world model can be learned for complex, partially-observed, stochastic Atari environments and can generalize zero-shot to novel object arrangements, closing a gap that neural world models (Dreamer, DIAMOND) address only with orders-of-magnitude more data. Key limitations remain: the method requires a separate object detector (no pixel-level modeling), does not address exploration or reward learning, and has been validated on only two games with hand-crafted alternative levels. Open questions include whether the product-of-experts decomposition scales to richer domains (e.g., 3D, multi-agent), whether the conditional-independence assumption across object features holds in practice, and how to integrate program-structured world models with efficient exploration strategies.
