## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of diffusion and flow-based generative models either assume access to a perfect score estimate or provide only sample complexity bounds, leaving the actual shape of the generated density—and how architectural biases distort it—largely uncharacterized. This paper fills that gap by deriving a tight asymptotic description of the generated distribution for a solvable two-layer DAE model, revealing precisely how limited expressivity leads to mode collapse and, upon retraining on synthetic data, to model collapse.

## Input / Output

The generative process starts from a Gaussian noise sample X_0 ~ N(0, I_d) and transports it via a stochastic differential equation over t ∈ [0,1] to produce a high-dimensional sample X_1. The target densities are (possibly infinite) Gaussian mixtures whose centroids lie on a κ-dimensional manifold in R^d with jointly diagonalizable covariances. The characterization is delivered as the law of low-dimensional projections E^T X_t in a fixed reference subspace E of dimension R = Θ_d(1), enabling comparison across training times and against the target density in a τ-independent space.

## Method

The model is a two-layer denoising autoencoder with tied weights, a trainable skip connection b, and a time-encoding vector v: f_{b,w,v}(t,x) = b·x + (w/√d)σ(w^T x/√d + p_t v), trained with online single-pass SGD and weight decay on the stochastic interpolation denoising objective. In the high-dimensional limit (d, n → ∞ with n/d = Θ(1), finite r and κ), the SGD dynamics self-average into a closed system of deterministic ODEs governing low-dimensional summary statistics (Q, Q(c), G, M(c), b) that encode the weights' alignment with the target's centroids and covariance structure. The generative SDE then decomposes exactly into a finite-dimensional nonlinear SDE in the r-dimensional weight column space and an independent isotropic Gaussian evolution in its orthogonal complement, yielding a closed-form characterization of the projected generated density in any fixed reference subspace. This framework is applied to Gaussian mixture targets and MNIST/FashionMNIST covariance-matched Gaussians, and iterated to show how successive retraining on synthetic data compounds variance loss into full model collapse.

## Results

The paper derives a tight asymptotic characterization of the generated density for a two-layer DAE-parametrized diffusion model trained with online SGD in the high-dimensional limit (d, n → ∞ with n/d = Θ(1)). The key quantitative result is that the training dynamics of summary statistics (Q, Q(c), G, M(c), b) concentrate to deterministic ODEs (Result 2.1), and the generative transport reduces to an r-dimensional SDE plus an independent Gaussian in the orthogonal complement (Result 2.2). Numerical validation in d=1000 on a bimodal Gaussian mixture and MNIST-sevens covariance shows excellent agreement between the theoretical ODE/SDE predictions and simulations, with the Hellinger distance between generated and target densities monotonically decreasing over training time. The analysis further reveals that mode collapse (variance loss in generated modes) arises from the skip connection b_τ saturating at the small average eigenvalue Λ, and that re-training on synthetic data leads to progressive model collapse with vanishing variance by the second generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare its method against competing generative models or baselines; it is a theoretical analysis paper whose 'experiments' serve to validate the derived ODE/SDE characterizations against direct numerical simulations in finite dimension (d=1000). The relevant comparison is between the theoretical predictions (blue contours in Figs. 2–4) and Monte Carlo simulations of the full high-dimensional SDE (colormaps), which show close agreement across all tested settings (bimodal Gaussian mixture, MNIST-sevens covariance, FashionMNIST). The paper positions itself relative to prior theoretical works: it generalizes the tight analysis of [21] (limited to binary Gaussian mixtures with isotropic covariances) to generic Gaussian mixtures with non-trivial cluster covariances on a low-dimensional manifold, and extends the DAE learning analysis of [22] from a denoising task to the full generative transport pipeline. No head-to-head performance comparison with other architectures (e.g., U-Nets, deep ReLU networks) is conducted, as the focus is on understanding the inductive bias and failure modes of the simplified DAE architecture rather than benchmarking generative quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tight asymptotic characterization of flow-based generative models initiated by Cui et al. [21] (limited to binary Gaussian mixtures with isotropic covariances) to general Gaussian mixtures with arbitrary cluster covariances supported on low-dimensional manifolds. The technical machinery follows the ODE-based analysis of SGD dynamics pioneered by Saad & Solla [61,62] and adapted to autoencoders by Refinetti & Goldt [56] and Cui & Zdeborová [22], now applied within the stochastic interpolation framework of Albergo et al. [4,5]. Unlike prior sample-complexity results [52,15,13,82] that only bound distances between target and generated densities, this work provides a full low-dimensional description of the generated distribution's shape as a function of training time and sample count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic, closed-form explanation of how mode collapse arises in diffusion/flow-based generative models (via the skip connection strength being bounded by the average covariance eigenvalue), and shows that model collapse under iterative retraining on synthetic data can be analytically tracked generation by generation. The main limitation is the extreme simplification of the architecture (two-layer DAE with tied weights, finite hidden units), which makes direct transfer to practical U-Net-based diffusion models non-trivial. Open questions include extending the analysis to untied weights, deeper networks, and non-Gaussian cluster structures, as well as understanding whether the identified mode-collapse mechanism persists in more expressive architectures.
