## Problem

Existing methods for generating constraint-satisfying text from autoregressive LLMs face a fundamental tradeoff: constrained generation avoids errors with minimal overhead but severely amplifies low-probability tokens, while rejection sampling and ASAp preserve the output distribution but become computationally intractable for dense error sets. The paper asks whether a principled midpoint exists that balances distribution fidelity against inference cost without requiring additional training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt, an autoregressive language model P, and a black-box constraint detector (e.g., a regex matcher, incremental parser, or Pyright type checker) that flags whether the current prefix belongs to the error set B. The error set is assumed prefix-closed: once a violation is detected, all extensions are also violations. Output is a constraint-satisfying token sequence generated via sampling, with the constraint that generation terminates if a maximum number of model invocations is reached, returning the last error-free prefix.

## Method

AprAD's core mechanism repurposes the speculative sampling acceptance/rejection procedure as an adaptive backtracking strategy. When an error is detected at position m, the algorithm computes two probability distributions from the same model: P^B (before removing the violating sequence) and P^{B∪{x}} (after removing it via ASAp's AddBadSample procedure). It then invokes SpecSample with these two distributions to determine how many tokens of the generated prefix to retain, rather than keeping all (constrained generation) or none (ASAp). Because the two distributions differ only in the probability mass of the single violating sequence, the accepted prefix is typically most of the generated length, yielding minimal backtracking in high-entropy regimes. The paper further introduces a hyperparameter h that raises the acceptance ratio to the h-th power, interpolating continuously between constrained generation (h=0) and ASAp (h→∞), and proves the per-iteration probability amplification is bounded by a factor of 2, in contrast to the unbounded amplification of constrained decoding.

## Results

On BigCodeBench (15B Starcoder2), AprAD achieves Pass@1 of 0.26 and Pass@5 of 0.54, matching ASAp exactly while reducing generation ratio from 1.56 to 1.08. AprAD also outperforms constrained generation (Pass@1 0.22, Pass@5 0.51) and reduces hallucinated API calls (!NameErr@1) from 0.93 to 0.98. On the lipogram task, AprAD achieves a quality score of 4.52/5 (near unconstrained 4.68) with a generation ratio of 4.20, compared to ASAp's 1.72/5 quality and 321.00 generation ratio—a ~77× reduction in compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ASAp (Grammar-Aligned Decoding, Park et al. 2024), AprAD matches task performance exactly on BigCodeBench (identical Pass@1, Pass@5, and !NameErr rates) while reducing generation ratio from 1.56 to 1.08 (15B) and 1.47 to 1.06 (7B). On the lipogram task, the gap is dramatic: AprAD's generation ratio of 4.20 is ~77× lower than ASAp's 321.00, and its quality score (4.52) far exceeds ASAp's (1.72), as ASAp often exhausts its 2000-invocation budget producing only a handful of tokens. Against constrained generation (Beurer-Kellner et al. 2024), AprAD achieves higher Pass@1 (0.26 vs 0.22) and Pass@5 (0.54 vs 0.51) on BigCodeBench 15B, and substantially better quality (4.52 vs 3.56) and constraint intent (2.84 vs 2.32) on lipograms, at the cost of a modest generation ratio increase (1.08 vs 1.02, and 4.20 vs 1.00 respectively). In the simulated KL-divergence experiments, AprAD consistently achieves lower divergence than constrained generation (e.g., 0.0046 vs 0.0075 for error set AAA) while maintaining a generation ratio close to 1.0, whereas ASAp achieves near-zero divergence but at higher compute cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

AprAD repurposes the SpecSample prefix-selection algorithm from speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) for a fundamentally different purpose: instead of verifying a draft model's tokens against a target model, it determines how much of a constraint-violating sequence to retain after the probability distribution is marginally adjusted (before vs. after adding one bad sample via ASAp's AddBadSample procedure). This positions AprAD as a principled midpoint between constrained generation (which reuses nearly the entire prefix but causes unbounded probability amplification) and ASAp (which discards everything and becomes intractable for dense error sets). The paper also situates itself relative to posterior-estimation methods (FUDGE, SMC Steering, Ctrl-G) that require task-specific training or expensive Monte Carlo estimation, arguing that AprAD's black-box constraint handling avoids those requirements entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AprAD offers a training-free, black-box method for constrained generation that is practical where ASAp becomes intractable (dense error sets like lipograms, where ASAp's generation ratio reaches 321× vs. AprAD's 4.2×) and where constrained generation produces severely distorted outputs. The proposed hyperparameter h (Section 6.2) suggests a continuous spectrum between the two extremes, though its behavior is left as conjecture. Open questions include whether the per-iteration amplification bound of 2× accumulates problematically over many backtracks in very dense error sets, how AprAD combines with search algorithms (e.g., MCTS for theorem proving), and whether the method extends cleanly to non-sampling decoding strategies like beam search.
