## Problem

LoRA constrains fine-tuning updates to a low-rank space, causing it to underperform full fine-tuning because the joint update of matrices A and B does not properly approximate the full gradient. Existing fixes like LoRA-Pro require a non-unique auxiliary variable whose choice significantly affects performance, and they inflate memory to O(kd) to accommodate momentum—defeating the purpose of parameter-efficient fine-tuning. The open question is how to approximate the full gradient and incorporate momentum within the low-rank space while strictly preserving LoRA's O(kr+dr) memory budget.

## Input / Output

AltLoRA takes a pretrained language model (Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3-8B, or T5-base) and fine-tunes it on text tasks: dialogue generation (WizardLM → MT-Bench), mathematical reasoning (MetaMathQA → GSM8K), code generation (CodeFeedBack → HumanEval), and natural language understanding (GLUE subsets → accuracy). The trainable parameters are only the low-rank matrices A ∈ R^{r×d} and B ∈ R^{k×r} with r ≪ min(k,d), applied to attention layers {Q,K,V,O}. Unlike LoRA-Pro, no full-size k×d momentum or gradient buffer is ever stored; the per-step memory overhead beyond standard LoRA is O(r³) for small matrix inversions.

## Method

AltLoRA replaces LoRA's joint update of A and B with an alternating scheme: at each step, only one matrix is updated. When updating A, it solves min ‖sB·(∇̃_A L) − ∇_W L‖²_F to obtain the optimal gradient approximation ∇̃_A L = (1/s²)(BᵀB)⁻¹∇_A L, which projects the full gradient onto the column space of B. When updating B, it similarly projects onto the row space of the newly updated A. This yields a closed-form solution equivalent to scaled gradient descent in low-rank matrix estimation, requiring only the standard backpropagated gradient and an r×r matrix inverse. For momentum, AltLoRA projects the previous momentum from the old subspace to the new one via M̃ = M·A_old·A_newᵀ(A_new·A_newᵀ)⁻¹, keeping momentum aligned with the evolving low-rank geometry without full-size storage. Theoretically, this design guarantees stable feature learning in the infinite-width regime and transformation invariance (the full-model update is independent of the specific A,B factorization), properties that joint-update variants like LoRA+ and ScaledAdam lack.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B-Base, AltLoRA (rank=8) achieves 6.56 on MT-Bench, 74.49% on GSM8K, and 45.91% on HumanEval, outperforming standard LoRA by +0.5, +8.38, and +3.1 points respectively. The enhanced variant AltLoRA+ further improves GSM8K to 76.91% (exceeding Full FT's 73.31%) and HumanEval to 50.10% (nearly matching Full FT's 50.81%). On the GLUE benchmark with T5-Base, AltLoRA+ achieves the highest average score of 88.30, surpassing Full FT (88.21) and all LoRA variants, with the best result on MRPC (87.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AltLoRA (rank=8) outperforms all nine LoRA variants on MT-Bench, GSM8K, and HumanEval simultaneously, with the largest gains over standard LoRA (+0.5 MT-Bench, +8.38 GSM8K, +3.1 HumanEval) and over LoRA-Pro (+0.37 MT-Bench, +1.37 GSM8K, +2.78 HumanEval). AltLoRA also surpasses LoRA-Rite on all three metrics (6.56 vs 6.10, 74.49 vs 74.10, 45.91 vs 43.12) and LoRA+ (6.56 vs 6.40, 74.49 vs 72.33, 45.91 vs 44.10). Notably, AltLoRA beats Full FT on MT-Bench (6.56 vs 6.31) and GSM8K (74.49 vs 73.31) while using only O(kr+dr) memory versus O(kd) for Full FT. AltLoRA+ further extends the lead on GSM8K to 76.91%, exceeding Full FT by 3.6 points, and nearly matches Full FT on HumanEval (50.10 vs 50.81), though it trails on MT-Bench (6.16 vs 6.31). On the GLUE benchmark, AltLoRA+ achieves the best average (88.30) and top scores on MRPC (87.25) and SST-2 (94.03), while PiSSA edges it out slightly on CoLA (81.90 vs 81.44) and QNLI (93.16 vs 92.99).

## Relation to Prior Work

AltLoRA builds directly on LoRA's low-rank parameterization (W = W₀ + sBA) but departs from the standard joint update of A and B by introducing an alternating projection scheme that sequentially optimizes each factor. It addresses two specific limitations of LoRA-Pro: the non-unique auxiliary variable X that causes significant performance variance, and the O(kd) memory cost of storing full-size momentum. Conceptually, the gradient approximation step connects to scaled gradient descent in low-rank matrix estimation, while the momentum alignment mechanism is novel within the LoRA memory budget, distinguishing it from LoRA-Rite's polar-decomposition-based approach and LoRA-Pro's full-parameter momentum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AltLoRA provides a principled, memory-efficient alternative to LoRA-Pro that matches or exceeds full fine-tuning on mathematical reasoning (76.91 vs. 73.31 on GSM8K) while maintaining O(kr+dr) space complexity, making it practical for resource-constrained settings. The theoretical guarantees—stable feature learning without cross-terms, inherent transformation invariance via projection matrix invariance, and convergence independent of condition number—offer a cleaner optimization landscape than joint-update variants. A key open question is that AltLoRA+ does not preserve transformation invariance under second-order momentum, and the MT-Bench results show AltLoRA+ underperforming AltLoRA, suggesting the adaptive scaling may not universally help. The method's compatibility with Hugging Face and applicability to MoE/RLHF settings broadens its practical relevance.
