## Problem

Existing explainable video action recognition methods produce entangled explanations that conflate motion dynamics with spatial context, making it unclear whether a prediction relies on how a body moves or on surrounding objects and scenes. Language-based approaches offer structure but fail to capture motion effectively because motion is tacit knowledge—intuitively understood but difficult to verbalize. The paper addresses this by proposing a framework that explicitly disentangles temporal dynamics from spatial context in a way aligned with human cognitive processing of actions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip as input, processed through a frozen VideoMAE backbone to produce a video-level feature vector. It outputs an action class label alongside three disentangled concept activation vectors: motion dynamics (visualized as 2D human pose sequences extracted from key clips), objects, and scenes (each represented as text descriptions). The motion dynamics concepts are appearance-agnostic pose sequences rather than pixel-level saliency maps, while object and scene concepts are natural-language phrases generated by GPT-4o and pseudo-labeled via an InternVid vision-language dual encoder.

## Method

DANCE is an ante-hoc concept bottleneck model that inserts a three-part concept layer between a frozen VideoMAE backbone and a linear classifier, partitioning concept parameters into disjoint sub-matrices for motion dynamics, objects, and scenes. Motion dynamics concepts are discovered unsupervised by extracting 2D pose sequences from key clips (identified via pixel-difference keyframe detection), clustering them with FINCH, and assigning binary labels per video. Object and scene concepts are discovered by querying GPT-4o with action-class-specific prompts, then pseudo-labeled using cosine similarity between video embeddings and concept text embeddings from an InternVid dual encoder. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the motion concept layer is trained with binary cross-entropy and the object/scene layers with cosine cubed loss; then, with concept layers frozen, a linear classifier is trained with cross-entropy plus a mixed L1/Frobenius sparsity regularization to encourage interpretable, sparse concept-to-class weight matrices.

## Results

On four video action recognition benchmarks, DANCE achieves top-1 accuracy of 91.1% (KTH), 98.1% (Penn Action), 70.7% (HAA-100), and 87.5% (UCF-101), matching or exceeding the non-interpretable VideoMAE baseline (89.7/97.8/73.5/88.4) on KTH and Penn Action while incurring only a 0.9–2.8 point drop on UCF-101 and HAA-100. In a user study, over 70% of participants rated DANCE's explanations as more interpretable than all three baselines, and the proposed pose-sequence motion dynamics concept scored 4.3/5 in perceived alignment versus 2.3/5 for GPT-4o language concepts and 3.4/5 for UCF-101 attribute concepts. Under severe domain shift (UCF-101 → UCF-101-SCUBA), a single class-level weight edit recovers 4.3 points of accuracy (77.7% → 82.0%) without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-interpretable VideoMAE baseline, DANCE gains +1.4 points on KTH and +0.3 on Penn Action while losing only 0.9 on UCF-101 and 2.8 on HAA-100, showing that the concept bottleneck imposes minimal accuracy cost. Compared to the label-free CBM with GPT-4o-generated entangled language concepts [38], DANCE wins on every dataset by 2.2–5.2 points (e.g., 87.5 vs 85.5 on UCF-101; 70.7 vs 66.5 on HAA-100), and also outperforms the disentangled-language variant by 3.8 points on UCF-101 and 5.4 on HAA-100, indicating that pose-sequence motion concepts are more informative than verbal descriptions. Against the CBM using expert-defined UCF-101 attributes [25], DANCE achieves 87.5% vs 86.8% on UCF-101. In the user study, DANCE is preferred over VTCD [26] (saliency-based), the GPT-4o CBM, and the UCF-101-attribute CBM by more than 70% of participants on a 5-point Likert scale, confirming that the disentangled pose + text format is perceived as substantially more interpretable than unstructured spatio-temporal attributions or purely linguistic explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DANCE extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) framework [Koh et al., ICML 2020] and its label-free variant [Oikarinen et al., ICLR 2023] to the video domain, but departs fundamentally from prior video XAI methods (saliency tubes, VTCD, spatio-temporal concept discovery) that produce entangled, unstructured explanations. The key conceptual departure is defining motion dynamics concepts as clustered 2D human pose sequences—an appearance-agnostic temporal representation—rather than relying on language descriptions or pixel-level attributions. For spatial context, DANCE repurposes the LLM-based concept discovery pipeline from LF-CBM [38] to automatically extract object and scene concepts, combined with a vision-language dual encoder for pseudo-labeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DANCE is the first framework to explicitly disentangle temporal dynamics from spatial context in video action recognition explanations, aligning the explanation structure with how humans cognitively parse actions. The practical utility for model debugging and editing without retraining (e.g., fixing 98 misclassifications via a single weight adjustment under domain shift) opens a path toward interpretable model maintenance. However, the approach is limited to human-centric actions on four relatively small datasets, depends on the quality of 2D pose estimation, and the LLM-generated concept vocabulary may introduce biases. Open questions include scalability to multi-person or non-human actions, robustness to pose estimation failures, and whether the disentanglement principle generalizes to other video understanding tasks beyond action recognition.
