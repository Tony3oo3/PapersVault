## Problem

GRPO and its variant DAPO assign a single rollout-level advantage value to every token within a sequence, ignoring the fact that in mathematical reasoning, individual tokens contribute very differently to the final answer. While process reward models could provide finer-grained signals, they are costly to train, hard to scale, and prone to reward hacking. The paper asks whether token-level advantage can be estimated in a model-free way using only the correctness labels of sampled rollouts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255406/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a group of G sampled rollouts (typically G=16) for a single problem, each labeled with a binary rule-based reward (correct/incorrect). For every unique token across the group, it constructs a 2×2 contingency table counting how many correct vs. incorrect rollouts contain that token, then computes a scalar key-token-value per token. The final output is a per-token advantage vector (one value per token position) that is added element-wise to GRPO's uniform rollout-level advantage, yielding a fine-grained token-level advantage used in the policy gradient loss.

## Method

KTAE quantifies each token's importance through three statistical components computed from the contingency table: (1) association strength via Fisher's exact test p-value (transformed as e^{-2p}) and Information Gain between token occurrence and rollout correctness; (2) association direction via a combination of Cohen's h effect size on the proportion of correct vs. incorrect rollouts containing the token, and a BM25-style standardized term-frequency ratio between concatenated correct and incorrect rollouts; (3) the final key-token-value is the product of weighted strength and direction, passed through a sigmoid and centered at 0.5. This per-token value is added to GRPO's rollout-level advantage to produce the final token-level advantage. The entire computation is model-free, requiring no additional neural network, and is orthogonal to DAPO's clip-higher and dynamic-sampling modifications.

## Results

On the 7B model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B), DAPO+KTAE achieves the highest average score of 52.5 across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, MATH-500, AMC, Minerva, OlympiadBench), outperforming DAPO (51.7), GRPO (50.3), and all other baselines. GRPO+KTAE reaches 51.7 average, improving over vanilla GRPO (50.3) on four of five benchmarks. On the 1.5B model, DAPO+KTAE achieves 43.4 average, surpassing R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (41.5) using the same base model, while also reducing mean response length from 886.8 to 840.4 tokens without any length penalty reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPO+KTAE-7B (avg 52.5) outperforms DAPO-7B (51.7) on MATH-500 (+1.4), AMC (+3.7), and Minerva (+0.8), though it trails slightly on OlympiadBench (43.7 vs. 45.3). GRPO+KTAE-7B (51.7) beats GRPO-7B (50.3) on MATH-500 (+1.4), AMC (+7.3), Minerva (+1.1), and OlympiadBench (+0.5), with only a minor drop on AIME24 (33.3 vs. 36.7, equivalent to one fewer correct answer out of 30 problems). Against external baselines, DAPO+KTAE-7B surpasses Oat-Zero-7B (50.0), SimpleRL-Zero-7B (48.3), Eurus-2-7B-PRIME (47.8), and OpenReasoner-Zero-7B (41.7) on average. On the 1.5B scale, DAPO+KTAE-1.5B (43.4) exceeds R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (41.5) and Oat-Zero-1.5B (41.2), while GRPO+KTAE-1.5B (41.7) edges out GRPO-1.5B (40.6) and DAPO-1.5B (41.2). Notably, KTAE also reduces mean response length by 5–15% across benchmarks without any length penalty, indicating improved reasoning efficiency alongside accuracy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) and its variant DAPO, which eliminate the critic model by computing rollout-level advantages from group statistics. It departs from the uniform-advantage paradigm by introducing a model-free, statistics-based mechanism—combining Fisher's exact test, Information Gain, BM25-inspired term frequency, and Cohen's h effect size—to assign differentiated token-level advantages within each rollout. Conceptually, it occupies the space between coarse outcome-level RL and expensive process reward models (PRMs), offering a middle ground that avoids the cost and reward-hacking vulnerability of PRMs while providing finer-grained signals than vanilla GRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in a plug-and-play module that improves accuracy while simultaneously reducing response length without any explicit length penalty, making it attractive for efficient reasoning deployment. However, the paper is limited to 1.5B and 7B models and binary-reward mathematical reasoning; whether the statistical associations remain informative at larger scales or with continuous/multi-class rewards is unverified. An open question is whether single-token granularity captures enough signal in long reasoning chains, as the authors themselves note that a single token's presence may not be decisive for the final outcome.
