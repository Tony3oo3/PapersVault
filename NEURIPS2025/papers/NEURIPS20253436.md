## Problem

While hierarchical contrastive training with taxonomic labels has shown strong species classification accuracy (BIOCLIP), it remains unclear what representational properties emerge when this training is scaled to hundreds of millions of images. The paper asks whether species-level supervision alone can yield embeddings that generalize to ecological and functional questions never explicitly supervised, and whether intra-species appearance variations (life stages, sexes) are preserved or collapsed under extensive contrastive training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253436/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB images and text prompts consisting of hierarchical taxonomic names (e.g., species, genus, family, order, class, phylum, kingdom) rather than free-form descriptions. It produces paired image and text embeddings in a shared contrastive space; at inference, embeddings support zero-shot species classification via nearest-prototype matching, few-shot classification, and linear probing for downstream tasks like habitat classification, trait prediction, and plant disease detection. The training batch interleaves 69,312 biological images with 8,192 general-domain replay samples from LAION-2B.

## Method

BIOCLIP 2 scales the BIOCLIP architecture from ViT-B/16 to ViT-L/14 and trains on TREEOFLIFE-200M (214M images, 952K taxa curated from GBIF, EOL, BIOSCAN-5M, and FathomNet) using hierarchical contrastive loss that matches each image to its full seven-rank taxonomic hierarchy simultaneously. A key architectural addition is a separate visual projector for experience replay data (26M LAION-2B samples), which prevents optimization conflict between biological taxonomic labels and general-domain text while preserving broad visual understanding. The paper's theoretical contribution (Theorem 5.1) proves that when species prototypes are nearly orthogonal, the contrastive loss's second-order Hessian lies entirely in the species span, so intra-species residuals δ are unconstrained in orthogonal subspaces—formally explaining why life-stage and sex variations are preserved rather than collapsed. Empirically, the authors demonstrate two scale-amplified emergent properties: inter-species embeddings align with ecological/functional relationships (e.g., beak size ordering in Darwin's finches), and intra-species variations become increasingly separable and orthogonal to inter-species distinctions as training data grows from 1M to 214M samples.

## Results

On zero-shot species classification across 10 benchmarks, BIOCLIP 2 achieves a mean top-1 accuracy of 55.6%, surpassing the previous best domain-specific model BIOCLIP (37.6%) by 18.0% and the general-purpose SigLIP (39.6%) by 16.0%. In the 5-shot setting, BIOCLIP 2 reaches 78.3% mean accuracy, outperforming DINOv3 (61.9%) by 16.4% and the original CLIP initialization (58.2%) by 20.1%. On non-species biological visual tasks (habitat, trait, disease), BIOCLIP 2 achieves 57.5% mean accuracy, an 8.9% improvement over DINOv3 (48.6%) and a 14.8% improvement over CLIP (42.7%), despite never being explicitly trained on these tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIOCLIP 2 dominates all six baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the direct predecessor BIOCLIP (ViT-B/16, TREEOFLIFE-10M), BIOCLIP 2 gains 18.0% in zero-shot and 9.8% in 5-shot mean accuracy, with the largest absolute jumps on Fungi (+42.9% zero-shot) and Rare Species (+39.7% zero-shot), reflecting the expanded taxonomic coverage of TREEOFLIFE-200M. Against the general-purpose vision-language models CLIP (ViT-L/14) and SigLIP, BIOCLIP 2 leads by 30.1% and 16.0% respectively in zero-shot species classification, and by 14.8% and 11.7% on non-species biological tasks. Against the vision-only DINOv3, which is commonly used for fine-grained visual features, BIOCLIP 2 still leads by 16.4% (5-shot species) and 8.9% (non-species tasks), confirming that hierarchical contrastive supervision with taxonomic labels provides a stronger inductive bias than self-supervised visual pre-training for biological domains. Against BioTrove-CLIP (162M images, ViT-B/16), BIOCLIP 2 wins by 29.4% zero-shot, demonstrating that taxonomic diversity (952K vs. 366K species) matters more than raw image count. The only task where BIOCLIP 2 does not achieve the top score is Plankton (3.9% zero-shot vs. SigLIP's 2.4% and BIOCLIP's 6.1%), likely due to the extreme fine-grained nature of plankton identification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends BIOCLIP (Stevens et al., 2024), which introduced hierarchical taxonomic labels into CLIP-style contrastive training for species classification on TREEOFLIFE-10M, by scaling both data (214M images, 952K taxa) and model capacity (ViT-L/14) while adding experience replay from LAION-2B. Conceptually, it positions itself within the broader literature on emergent properties in foundation models—drawing parallels to how LLMs acquire in-context learning or DINO learns segmentation—while departing from prior work by providing a formal theorem (Theorem 5.1) explaining why contrastive objectives with near-orthogonal species prototypes preserve intra-species variations in orthogonal subspaces rather than collapsing them. The dataset curation builds on GBIF, EOL, BIOSCAN-5M, and FathomNet, combining museum, camera-trap, and citizen-science sources in a way that prior datasets (BioTrove, TREEOFLIFE-10M) did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that hierarchical contrastive training at scale produces biologically meaningful emergent structure—inter-species embeddings aligning with ecological relationships (e.g., beak size in Darwin's finches) and intra-species variations (life stages, sexes) preserved in orthogonal subspaces—challenges the standard neural-collapse intuition that contrastive training collapses fine-grained differences. This has direct implications for conservation (77.1% IUCN threatened-species coverage), trait prediction, and habitat classification without explicit supervision. Open questions include whether the orthogonality property generalizes beyond biological taxonomies to other hierarchical domains, whether the separation (as opposed to mere orthogonality) of intra-species variants can be formally characterized, and how the long-tail taxonomic imbalance in TREEOFLIFE-200M affects the uniformity of these emergent properties across clades.
