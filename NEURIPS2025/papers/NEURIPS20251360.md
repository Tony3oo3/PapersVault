## Problem

Norm-based OOD detection has proven effective in image domains, but naively transplanting it to graphs fails due to three relational complications: local contamination by anomalous neighbors distorting neighborhood statistics, disparate norm scales across legitimate classes or structural roles, and insufficient one-hop context for boundary or low-degree nodes. No prior work systematically identifies and addresses these graph-specific pitfalls in a unified, post-hoc framework.

## Input / Output

RAGNOR consumes only the node embeddings (h_v ∈ R^k) produced by any pre-trained GNN, the graph adjacency structure for neighborhood queries, and two scalar statistics (μ_ID, σ_ID) estimated from known in-distribution nodes. It outputs a per-node OOD score |Δ_v|, a graph-level score via max or mean aggregation over node scores, and an optional confidence-scaled logit vector z'_v = Ψ(Δ_v)·z_v. The entire pipeline is post-hoc: no architectural modification, retraining, or additional supervision is required, and the computational overhead is limited to median computations over 1-hop and 2-hop neighborhoods.

## Method

RAGNOR refines raw embedding norms through three sequential operations: (1) global Z-score normalization r'_v = (r_v − μ_ID)/(σ_ID + ε) to eliminate class-level norm offsets; (2) median-based local aggregation over the 1-hop neighborhood of normalized norms, which is provably robust to up to 50% OOD contamination (Lemma 6); and (3) optional multi-hop blending r̄_v = λ·r̄_N¹(v) + (1−λ)·r̄_N²(v) to stabilize references for sparse or boundary nodes. The final OOD score is the discrepancy Δ_v = r'_v − r̄_v, and the paper proves (Theorem 8) that under bounded ID norms (|r'_v| ≤ B) and OOD separation (|r'_w| ≥ B + δ₀), ID nodes satisfy |Δ_v| ≤ 2B while OOD nodes satisfy |Δ_w| ≥ δ₀, enabling clean threshold-based detection. The framework is modular—each component can be toggled independently—and requires only O(N·d) time where d is average degree, making it trivially parallelizable across nodes.

## Results

On graph-level OOD detection across 8 datasets, SGOOD+RAGNOR achieves AUROC of 82.1 (ENZYMES), 86.8 (IMDB-M), 88.1 (IMDB-B), 83.0 (REDDIT-12K), 92.0 (BACE), 69.1 (BBBP), 81.3 (DrugOOD), and 68.5 (HIV), consistently outperforming the best baseline SGOOD by 7–8 AUROC points. On node-level detection (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed), NODESAFE++ + RAGNOR reaches AUROC up to 98.8 (Pubmed-S) and reduces FPR95 from 23.34 to 15.0 on Cora-S (no OOD exposure). The method requires no retraining and adds minimal post-hoc overhead to any pre-trained GNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAGNOR (applied post-hoc to SGOOD's GIN backbone) outperforms all 12 graph-level baselines across all 8 datasets on AUROC, AUPR, and FPR95. The closest competitor, SGOOD [5], is surpassed by 7.7–8.1 AUROC points (e.g., 86.8 vs. 78.8 on IMDB-M; 92.0 vs. 84.4 on BACE). General-purpose methods (MSP, ODIN, Energy, Mahalanobis) perform near chance level on most graph datasets (AUROC 40–70), confirming that graph-specific structure is essential. Among graph-specific baselines, OGGTL [34] and AAGOD [11] are competitive on individual datasets (e.g., OGGTL reaches 80.8 AUROC on BACE) but lack consistency across the full suite. On node-level detection, NODESAFE++ + RAGNOR improves over NODESAFE++ by 2.7–5.5 AUROC points (e.g., 98.8 vs. 96.3 on Pubmed-S) and reduces FPR95 by 8.3 points on Cora-S (15.0 vs. 23.34), while also outperforming energy-based (Energy FT), logit-normalization (GNNSAFE++ w/ L_LN), and outlier-exposure (OE) approaches by wide margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAGNOR repurposes the norm-based OOD detection principle well-established in image domains (Park et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2020, Yu et al. 2020) for graph neural networks, where it had not been systematically studied. It departs from the dominant graph OOD detection paradigm—energy-based scoring (GNNSAFE, NODESAFE) and substructure encoding (SGOOD)—by operating purely on embedding norms as a post-hoc signal, requiring no architectural changes or retraining. The paper's conceptual contribution is identifying three graph-specific failure modes (local contamination, class-level norm shifts, boundary/low-degree sparsity) that prevent naive norm transfer from images, and designing targeted remedies (median aggregation, Z-score normalization, multi-hop blending) for each.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The post-hoc, training-free nature of RAGNOR makes it immediately deployable on any existing GNN pipeline, which is practically significant for teams that cannot retrain models. The theoretical guarantee under partial neighborhood contamination (<50% OOD neighbors) provides a principled operating regime, though it leaves open the behavior in dense OOD clusters where the reference norm itself becomes unreliable. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of the underlying GNN embeddings (if norms don't separate ID from OOD, no post-hoc refinement helps), the need for accurate ID statistics, and the restriction to at most 2-hop context, which may be insufficient for very sparse or long-range-dependent graph structures. Whether the approach generalizes to heterogeneous or temporal graphs remains unexplored.
