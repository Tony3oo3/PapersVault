## Problem

Vision Transformers rely on a single fixed patch size and Softmax-based dot-product attention, which limits their ability to simultaneously capture fine-grained local details and long-range global dependencies. Existing multi-scale ViT variants (e.g., MPViT, Focal Transformer) address this with heuristic architectural choices but lack a principled theoretical framework for measuring cross-scale feature correlations, and their fusion strategies often degrade global context or increase computational cost.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images and applies three parallel convolutional tokenizers (3×3 stride-1, 7×7 stride-2, 15×15 stride-1) to produce token sequences at spatial resolutions H×W, H/2×W/2, and H×W respectively, each linearly projected into query/key/value embeddings of dimension d. Depending on the downstream task, the fused multi-scale representation yields class labels (ImageNet-1K), bounding boxes plus instance masks via RetinaNet/Mask R-CNN (COCO), or pixel-level segmentation maps via UPerNet (ADE20K).

## Method

MK-CAViT replaces standard self-attention with a Fast-HGR correlation attention mechanism that scores query-key pairs using batch-averaged cosine similarity (capturing local pairwise dependencies) plus a trace-of-covariance-products term (enforcing global distributional consistency), grounded in Hirschfeld-Gebelein-Rényi maximal correlation theory with an O(1/√N) approximation guarantee. Multi-scale features from the three parallel kernel pathways are combined through a two-stage hierarchical gated fusion: small and medium-kernel features are first merged via learned sigmoid gates on cross-kernel attention, then the result is adaptively mixed with large-kernel global context using a dynamically computed coefficient γ. An adaptive multi-head gating module further modulates each attention head's contribution based on input complexity. Training uses a combined objective of cross-entropy loss minus a weighted F-HGR correlation loss (L = L_CE − μ·L_F-HGR) to simultaneously optimize prediction accuracy and feature-target correlation, with AdamW, cosine LR decay, and mixed-precision FP16.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, MK-CAViT-Base achieves 85.6% Top-1 accuracy with 88.0M parameters and 15.6G FLOPs, surpassing FasterViT-B1 (84.8%), Agent-Swin-Base (84.0%), and ConvNeXt-B (83.8%). At the Large scale, MK-CAViT-Large reaches 86.1% Top-1 with 54% fewer parameters and 21% lower FLOPs than FasterViT-4 (85.4%). On COCO detection (Mask R-CNN), MK-CAViT-Base achieves 50.3 AP^b and 31.9 AP^S (small objects), and on ADE20K segmentation (UPerNet), it reaches 50.8% mIoU, both leading all compared baselines at the same scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MK-CAViT-Base (85.6% Top-1) outperforms all Base-scale baselines: FasterViT-B1 by 0.8%, MPViT-B by 1.3%, Agent-Swin-Base by 1.6%, ConvNeXt-B by 1.8%, and Swin-Base by 2.2%. At the Large scale, MK-CAViT-Large (86.1%) beats FasterViT-4 (85.4%) while using 54% fewer parameters (186M vs. 424.6M) and 21% fewer FLOPs. On COCO detection, MK-CAViT-Base leads with 50.3 AP^b, exceeding Agent-Swin-B (49.0), FasterViT-B1 (49.1), and MPViT-B (48.6); its small-object AP^S of 31.9 also surpasses all baselines (next best: FasterViT-B1 at 30.8). On ADE20K segmentation, MK-CAViT-Base achieves 50.8% mIoU, edging out MPViT-B (50.3%) and FasterViT-4 (49.1%). The multi-kernel ablation (Table 4) confirms that no single kernel size matches the 3/7/15 combination, with the multi-kernel design gaining 2.4–3.0% over the best single-kernel variant on ImageNet and 6.5–7.7% on ADE20K mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

MK-CAViT extends the Vision Transformer lineage by replacing the standard single-patch tokenization with parallel multi-kernel pathways (3×3, 7×7, 15×15), building on the multi-path idea of MPViT but adding explicit cross-scale correlation channels rather than independent streams. The attention mechanism is grounded in Hirschfeld-Gebelein-Rényi maximal correlation theory, with the Fast-HGR approximation derived from the Soft-HGR / I-SoftHGR formulations of Wang et al. (AAAI 2019), replacing expensive covariance whitening with cosine similarity plus trace regularization. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as unifying two previously separate research threads—architectural multi-scale design (MPViT, Focal Transformer, Swin) and information-theoretic correlation measurement—under a single theoretically motivated framework, which neither line of work had attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HGR-based theoretical grounding offers a principled alternative to the heuristic dot-product attention that dominates ViTs, potentially guiding future attention design beyond ad-hoc similarity choices. However, the Fast-HGR approximation (cosine similarity + trace term) is relatively straightforward, and the performance gains over strong baselines like FasterViT and Agent-Swin are consistent but modest (1–2%), limiting the practical urgency of adoption. Open questions include whether the multi-kernel overhead is justified in resource-constrained deployment, how the method behaves on very low-resolution or heavily corrupted inputs, and whether the HGR framework extends naturally to video or 3D vision. The cross-domain generalization experiments (emotion recognition, medical imaging, remote sensing) are encouraging but use only a single model scale and limited baselines per domain.
