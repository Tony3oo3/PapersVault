## Problem

Current video generation models produce characters with arbitrary, unsynchronized lip movements and disconnected expressions, while existing speech-conditioned models are restricted to cropped talking-head regions with static cameras and single speakers. MoCha introduces the novel task of Talking Characters—generating full-body, multi-character talking animations with dynamic camera motion directly from speech and text, without any auxiliary control signals such as reference images, pose skeletons, or facial keypoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252369/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a structured text prompt (specifying environment, character appearance, actions, facing direction, emotion, frame position, camera movement, and shot size, with character tags for multi-clip scenes) and a raw speech audio waveform as the sole inputs. It produces a 128-frame video at 24 fps (~5.3 seconds) at approximately 720×720 resolution with flexible aspect ratios. Multi-clip turn-based dialogue is supported by concatenating speaker segments in the audio, with the model inferring clip boundaries implicitly from speaker changes—no explicit clip tokens or reference frames are required.

## Method

MoCha extends a 30B-parameter MovieGen DiT backbone with a fully tokenized, end-to-end speech conditioning pathway: Wav2Vec2 audio features and tri-encoder text embeddings are projected into 6144-dim tokens and injected via sequential cross-attention (text then audio) after video self-attention in each block, trained with a Flow Matching objective. The key architectural innovation is Localized Audio Attention, which constrains each video latent token to attend only to a window of r+2=6 audio tokens corresponding to its 4×-temporally-downsampled segment, resolving the resolution mismatch between compressed video tokens and full-resolution audio. Training follows a curriculum-based multimodal strategy: 80% speech+text and 20% text-only samples are mixed, with progressive shot-type complexity (close-up → medium/wide → multi-character) to address data scarcity and varying speech relevance across spatial scales. A structured character-tagging prompt template enables consistent multi-clip dialogue generation by assigning unique identifiers to characters, reducing prompt redundancy and improving cross-clip identity coherence.

## Results

On MoCha-Bench, MoCha achieves a lip-sync confidence (Sync-C) of 6.333 and lip-sync distance (Sync-D) of 8.185, outperforming the best baseline Hallo3 (Sync-C 4.866, Sync-D 8.963) by +1.47 and −0.78 respectively. In human evaluation (1–4 scale), MoCha scores approximately 3.8 across all five axes (Lip-Sync 3.85, Expression 3.82, Action 3.82, Text Alignment 3.85, Visual Quality 3.72), approaching the ceiling of 4 (nearly indistinguishable from real cinematic video). The model generates 128-frame, 24 fps clips (~5.3 s) at ~720×720 resolution using a 30B-parameter DiT backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoCha dominates all three baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor Hallo3 (a DiT-based I2V model requiring a reference image), MoCha improves Sync-C by +1.47 and reduces Sync-D by 0.78, while gaining 1.36–1.69 points on all five human-evaluation axes. SadTalker and AniPortrait, which rely on 3DMM coefficients or 2D landmarks respectively, score near the floor (1.00) on Action Naturalness because they only animate the head and do not accept text prompts (Text Alignment is N/A). Notably, MoCha operates in a strictly harder setting: it generates video from raw speech + text alone, whereas all baselines require an auxiliary reference image (I2V setup); the paper mitigates this asymmetry by feeding each baseline MoCha's own first frame as the reference. The consistent 1.4–1.7 point human-evaluation gap over Hallo3 suggests that the end-to-end DiT design with localized audio attention and curriculum training yields substantially more natural, prompt-aligned full-body motion than prior audio-driven portrait methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoCha extends the MovieGen 30B DiT backbone with speech conditioning, departing fundamentally from prior talking-head methods (SadTalker, AniPortrait, Hallo3, EMO) that rely on auxiliary signals—reference images, facial keypoints, or pose skeletons—and are restricted to cropped face regions with static cameras. The key architectural departure is replacing autoregressive U-Net frame-by-frame generation with a fully parallel DiT, which necessitates the proposed localized audio attention to resolve the temporal resolution mismatch between 3D-VAE-compressed video tokens and full-resolution audio. The curriculum-based training strategy that mixes limited speech-labeled data with large-scale text-only video is also novel, addressing data scarcity without sacrificing generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoCha demonstrates that dialogue-centric, full-body, multi-character video generation is feasible end-to-end from speech and text alone, opening a path toward automating film and animation production. However, significant limitations remain: lip-sync degrades in wide shots where the face occupies few pixels, multi-character scenes in a single frame cause speaker confusion, and the baselines compared (talking-head methods) are not directly comparable to the full-body task, weakening the quantitative evidence. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer narratives, more than two characters, or real-time generation, and whether the 30B parameter requirement can be reduced without sacrificing the 'no auxiliary signals' property.
