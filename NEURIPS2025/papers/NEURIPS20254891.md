## Problem

Estimating the population mean of a k-class random variable Y with a limited label budget N is a pervasive problem, but classical approaches like simple random sampling waste the label budget when auxiliary covariates X are informative about Y. While active learning has been extensively studied for classification and for subgroup mean estimation with predefined strata, no prior work has addressed population mean estimation when the optimal stratification scheme is unknown and must be learned adaptively from unlabeled data.

## Input / Output

The analyst observes a large pool of unlabeled covariate instances X and adaptively selects which to label, round by round, subject to a total budget of N label requests. The algorithm also requires a hypothesis class C (e.g., threshold classifiers) and a disagreement-based active learning subroutine S as inputs. The output is an unbiased population mean estimator μ̂_PB constructed by aggregating weighted stratum means, along with the learned stratification scheme G that partitions the covariate space. The stratification is induced by the preimages of the learned classifier's predictions, so the number of strata equals the number of distinct class labels predicted.

## Method

PartiBandits is a two-stage algorithm that bridges disagreement-based active learning and UCB-style adaptive sampling. In Stage 1, it runs a disagreement-based active learning algorithm S (e.g., A²) on the unlabeled pool to learn a classifier ĥ whose excess risk decays at a near-exponential rate; the classifier's preimages define a stratification scheme G that shrinks the average within-stratum variance of Y. In Stage 2, it applies WarmStart-UCB, a UCB-style subroutine that first allocates a fixed fraction τ of the remaining label budget uniformly across all strata (the warm-start), then adaptively requests labels from the stratum with the largest upper confidence bound on its estimated variance. The key insight is that disagreement-based learning rapidly identifies low-variance strata, and the UCB subroutine then concentrates remaining labels where variance is still high, yielding a minimax-optimal rate of Õ((ν + exp(c·(−N/log N)))/N) that is near-exponentially better than SRS when X is predictive of Y.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a near-exponential convergence rate for PartiBandits: the squared estimation error is $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\nu+\exp(c\cdot(-N/\log N))}{N}\right)$, where $\nu$ is the Bayes-optimal classifier risk (Theorem 3). The WarmStart-UCB subroutine achieves $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\Sigma_1(\mathcal{G})}{N}\right)$ when a stratification scheme is given a priori (Theorem 1). Both rates are proven minimax optimal in classical threshold settings (Theorems 2 and 4). Empirically, on the American Family Cohort (6M+ electronic health records), PartiBandits eventually outperforms SRS with fewer labels, with the gap widening as the covariate X (zip-code-derived probability of being Black) becomes more predictive of the outcome Y (hypertension × Black indicator).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline throughout is Simple Random Sampling (SRS), which achieves the classical $\mathcal{O}(\text{Var}(Y)/N)$ rate. In synthetic threshold experiments (Figure 1, left panel), PartiBandits eventually outperforms SRS at label budgets of roughly 30–50, with the performance gap growing as the label-flip rate $\nu$ decreases from 10% to 0%. In the AFC health-records simulation (Figure 2), PartiBandits surpasses SRS at moderate-to-large label budgets, though SRS leads by a small constant factor at very small budgets. The WarmStart-UCB subroutine (Figure 1, right panel) consistently outperforms SRS across all tested stratification schemes, with the advantage increasing as $\Sigma_1(\mathcal{G})$ shrinks. In an additional comparison (Figure 5), Thompson Sampling with a Beta(1,1) prior initially tracks SRS but ceases to improve over time, consistent with known bias results for bandit-based mean estimation (Shin et al., 2019), while PartiBandits continues to reduce error. The paper does not compare against stratified random sampling (StRS) with oracle allocation in the main text, noting that StRS is often the only realistic alternative in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate strands of active learning: disagreement-based theory (Hanneke 2011; Puchkin & Zhivotovskiy 2022), which was designed to achieve exponential savings in classification error, and UCB-style variance-guided sampling (Aznag et al. 2023), which estimates subgroup means under a fixed stratification. The key conceptual move is using a classification active learning algorithm (e.g., A²) in Stage 1 to learn a stratification whose within-group variance is bounded by the Bayes classifier risk plus an exponentially decaying term, then applying a WarmStart-UCB subroutine in Stage 2 to allocate labels adaptively across those learned strata. This repurposing of disagreement-based learning for a mean estimation task—rather than a classification task—is the paper's central novelty, and it also eliminates the counterintuitive inverse dependence on the minimum stratum variance (σ_min) that plagues prior active learning mean estimation frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that near-exponential savings (exp(−cN/log N)/N) are achievable for population mean estimation, not just classification, opens a path for dramatically reducing labeling costs in settings where covariates are informative but the optimal stratification is unknown. The minimax optimality guarantees (Theorems 2 and 4) confirm these rates cannot be improved in classical settings, and the EHR application on 6 million records demonstrates practical relevance for costly demographic or clinical label acquisition. Open questions include how the method extends to high-dimensional or continuous covariates, non-IID data, and settings where the hypothesis class C is poorly specified; the current theory requires C to be well-constructed and relatively small, which may be restrictive in practice.
