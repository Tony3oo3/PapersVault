## Problem

Safety fine-tuning of LLMs on repetitive refusal templates causes false refusals on benign queries, but the root cause in model internals has not been identified. The paper shows that safety data's low lexical diversity (H₁≈9.18 vs. 12.05 for general data) drives a geometric collapse in the residual stream—variance concentrates along a few principal components—correlating with false refusal rates rising from 63% to 84% as safety data increases from 0% to 40%. No prior work had quantified this structural mismatch or proposed a training-time fix that preserves safety while reducing over-caution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (safety and general) and extracts the final-token residual vector at each of L transformer layers, concatenating them into a single high-dimensional vector per prompt for PCA analysis. For training, VCL operates on a window of mid-layer residuals (depth 0.3–0.5) collected from a batch, computing SVD on the centered residual matrix. Outputs include a fine-tuned model with reduced false refusals, PCA-projected residual trajectories visualized in a shared 3D coordinate space (FlowLens), and a scalar variance concentration score tracking geometric stability across training.

## Method

FlowLens concatenates residual vectors from all L layers across N prompts into a matrix X ∈ R^(N·L × d), centers it, and applies PCA with the number of components set by the TwoNN-estimated intrinsic dimension (ID≈3), yielding a stable shared coordinate space that reveals smooth layer-aligned trajectories for general data but irregular, collapsed trajectories for safety data. The Variance Concentration Loss (VCL) is an auxiliary regularizer added to the SFT objective: it computes the SVD of the centered residual matrix from a contiguous window of mid-layer transformers and minimizes the ratio of the sum of top-k squared singular values to total variance, thereby penalizing excessive variance concentration along a few directions. The total loss is L_SFT + γ·L_VCL with γ=50 and k=2–4, applied at residual depth [0.3, 0.5] where the paper shows interventions most effectively reshape downstream geometry. This design avoids the complexity of explicit subspace alignment between safety and general data, requiring only a single batch's residuals and no additional data or inference-time modifications.

## Results

On Llama-3.2-1B, VCL achieves the best safety scores across all benchmarks (DAN 0.89, ToxiGen 1.000, JBB 0.86) while simultaneously attaining the highest false-refusal rates (XSTest 0.86, ORB 0.87, OKTest 0.76), a 35-percentage-point improvement over the SFT baseline (XSTest 0.51). General-capability performance is preserved or slightly improved: MMLU 0.42 (matching SFT), GSM8K 0.51, BBH 0.26, and CodexEval 0.25. On the larger Llama-3.1-8B model, VCL again leads on both safety (DAN 0.91, ToxiGen 1.000) and false refusal (XSTest 0.89, ORB 0.90) while maintaining competitive general scores (MMLU 0.65, GSM8K 0.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCL outperforms all three baselines on both safety and false-refusal metrics simultaneously, which no other method achieves. Against DRO [40], VCL gains +9 points on DAN (0.89 vs 0.80), +8 on ToxiGen (1.00 vs 0.92), and +18 on XSTest false refusal (0.86 vs 0.68), while DRO actually degrades MMLU (0.39 vs 0.42). Against Self-CD [28], VCL leads on DAN by 13 points (0.89 vs 0.76) and on ORB by 44 points (0.87 vs 0.43); Self-CD's inference-time decoding strategy produces unstable ORB scores. Against Vector Ablation [34], VCL wins on XSTest by 28 points (0.86 vs 0.58) and ORB by 42 points (0.87 vs 0.45), though Vector Ablation edges ahead on JBB (0.91 vs 0.86). The key distinction is that VCL operates as a training-time regularizer requiring no inference overhead, whereas Self-CD and Vector Ablation modify the decoding process or inject runtime interventions, making them susceptible to distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads: (1) false-refusal mitigation in safety-aligned LLMs, where prior work relies on sample-based calibration (DRO, Self-CD) or inference-time interventions (vector ablation, system prompting), and (2) residual-stream geometry analysis, where prior studies (Arditi et al., Marks & Tegmark, Shai et al.) typically use per-layer cosine similarity or layer-wise PCA. The paper departs from both: it introduces FlowLens, which concatenates all-layer residuals into a single vector and applies unlayered PCA for a stable global view, and it proposes VCL, a training-time auxiliary regularizer that avoids the data-collection overhead of sample-based methods and the distribution-shift risk of inference-time adaptation. The causal chain linking low lexical diversity in safety completions to geometric variance concentration in the residual stream to elevated false-refusal rates is the paper's core conceptual contribution, connecting a data-level observation to an internal-representation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic, representation-level explanation for false refusals that goes beyond empirical correlation, and VCL offers a drop-in regularizer that practitioners can add to any SFT pipeline without extra data or inference-time changes. The 35-percentage-point reduction in false refusals while maintaining safety and general benchmarks is practically significant for teams deploying safety-tuned models. However, the evidence is concentrated on the LLaMA family (1B and 8B), the analysis focuses only on the top-ID principal components, and the fixed intrinsic dimension across all layers may miss context-specific structure. Open questions include whether the geometric-collapse signature generalizes to non-LLaMA architectures, whether lower-variance PCA directions carry complementary safety signals, and whether adaptive layer-window selection can further improve the regularizer.
