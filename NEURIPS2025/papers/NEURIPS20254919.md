## Problem

Existing jailbreak attacks against LLMs follow a sequential paradigm where each task is understood and answered one by one, but the natural extension of concurrency—interleaving multiple tasks at the word level—has been largely unexplored. The paper investigates whether this concurrent interaction paradigm introduces new safety vulnerabilities, specifically whether embedding a harmful task within a concurrent structure reduces its detectability by guardrails while preserving the LLM's ability to generate the harmful content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254919/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful task and an auxiliary benign task, splits both into word sequences, and interleaves them word-by-word with separators (default: { and }) to form a single concurrent prompt. The target LLM must produce a concurrent answer that maintains the interleaved word-position alignment throughout generation, with words from the harmful task at odd positions and words from the auxiliary task (or blank placeholders) at even positions. The harmful answer is then extracted by stripping the separator-enclosed words, yielding a clean text response that can be evaluated independently.

## Method

JAIL-CON is an iterative black-box attack framework with three steps per iteration: (1) Task Combination, which aligns the word lengths of the harmful and auxiliary tasks by padding with spaces and then interleaves them word-by-word using separators; (2) Concurrent Execution, which queries the target LLM under two variants—CVT (concurrency with valid task, where the LLM answers both tasks in parallel at alternating word positions) and CIT (concurrency with idle task, where the LLM answers only the harmful task while outputting blank placeholders for the auxiliary task); (3) Shadow Judge, which extracts the harmful answer from the concurrent output and scores it with a judge LLM (GPT-4o mini) using a rubric-based template. The iteration continues with a newly selected auxiliary task until the judge score reaches 1 or a maximum of 50 iterations is reached. The key insight is that word-level interleaving fragments the harmful semantics across non-adjacent tokens, significantly reducing the guardrail's filter rate (average FR of 0.33 vs. 0.60+ for sequential attacks) while the LLM retains sufficient context to generate coherent harmful content.

## Results

JAIL-CON achieves an average ASR-O of 0.95 across 6 LLMs (peak 1.00 on LLaMA3-8B), substantially outperforming the second-best method GPTFuzzer (avg. ASR-O 0.71). Under guardrail defense (OpenAI Moderation API), JAIL-CON attains an average ASR-E of 0.64, nearly 2.4× higher than the next-best non-encoding attack (0.27), with a minimum filtering rate of 0.20 on GPT-4o. On utility benchmarks, concurrent task processing (CVT+CIT) recovers most of the sequential performance: e.g., GPT-4o on GSM8K drops from 0.9538 (original) to 0.9272 (CVT+CIT), and on TruthfulQA truthfulness from 0.9339 to 0.8458.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JAIL-CON outperforms all seven baselines on ASR-O across every evaluated LLM, with the largest margins over GCG (avg. 0.95 vs. 0.13), PAIR (0.95 vs. 0.11), and JAM (0.95 vs. 0.14). The closest competitor, GPTFuzzer, reaches an average ASR-O of 0.71 but suffers a much higher filtering rate (avg. FR 0.59 vs. JAIL-CON's 0.33), yielding an ASR-E of only 0.28 compared to JAIL-CON's 0.64. FlipAttack achieves competitive ASR-O on GPT-4o (0.84) and DeepSeek-V3 (0.83) but collapses on LLaMA2-13B (0.05) and LLaMA3-8B (0.10), whereas JAIL-CON maintains ≥0.86 on all six models. Encoding-based attacks (Base64, Combination) achieve near-zero FR but at the cost of very low ASR-O (≤0.26 on most models except GPT-4o/DeepSeek-V3), making their guardrail-bypass advantage unreliable. TAP is the strongest non-iterative baseline, reaching ASR-O of 0.80–0.83 on DeepSeek-V3, Mistral-7B, and Vicuna-13B, yet still trails JAIL-CON by 0.12–0.17 in ASR-O and 0.13–0.15 in ASR-E on those models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a conceptual bridge from operating-system concurrency (time-slicing, interleaved execution) to LLM interaction, proposing word-level task interleaving as a token-level analogue of concurrent processing. Methodologically, it builds on FlipAttack [15] for its prompt-template structure and on the broader iterative black-box jailbreak literature (PAIR, GPTFuzzer, TAP) for its attack loop and evaluation protocol. The key departure is that all prior jailbreak attacks treat each task as a sequential unit, whereas JAIL-CON encodes two divergent intents in adjacent words, exploiting the observation that LLMs can process interleaved tasks while their guardrails degrade in detection accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that embedding a harmful task inside a concurrent (benign + harmful) prompt substantially lowers the OpenAI Moderation API's filter rate (from ~0.71 to ~0.37) reveals a previously unexplored attack surface that is orthogonal to encoding-based or gradient-based evasion. This suggests guardrails may need to be retrained or augmented to parse interleaved multi-intent inputs, and raises open questions about whether the effect generalizes to more than two concurrent tasks, to character-level interleaving, or to LLM-based guardrails (e.g., Llama Guard). Limitations include reliance on a single guardrail for the FR metric, a fixed maximum of 50 iterations, and the observation that separator choice and auxiliary-task distribution still modulate performance, pointing toward future work on automatic separator optimization.
