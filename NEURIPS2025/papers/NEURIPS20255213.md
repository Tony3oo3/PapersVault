## Problem

Prior super-resolution and image compression methods focus on network architecture design while neglecting the underlying image-content representations. Decomposition-based approaches (Fourier/Wavelet bottlenecks) capture periodic patterns but suffer from poor perceptual fidelity, while architecture-heavy methods cannot recover repeating structural elements without explicit frequency modeling. The paper asks whether a unified, learned basis-coefficient representation can replace simple feature-map outputs and simultaneously serve both tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255213/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For super-resolution, the method takes a 64×64 bicubically downsampled RGB crop and reconstructs a 256×256 high-resolution image at 4× scale, using N=6 coefficient-basis pairs modulated by frequency scalars α_j ∈ {1, 4, 16, 64}. For image compression, it takes a full-resolution RGB image and produces a quantized bitstream plus a reconstructed image; in multi-image mode, M images share a single merged basis transmitted alongside per-image quantized coefficients. The Factorized Features representation is a multi-channel tensor of shape (N·K)×H×W that is projected to 3-channel RGB via a final linear layer.

## Method

FIPER represents images via Factorized Features: f(x) = P(Concat_{i,j} {c_ij(x) ⊙ ψ(α_j · b_i(γ_i(x)))}), where spatially variant coefficients c and learned non-uniform basis maps b are generated by a Coefficient Backbone (existing SR networks: HAT, DAT, ATD, SwinIR) and a Basis Swin Transformer, respectively. Two key mechanisms differentiate this from prior decomposition: (1) a sawtooth coordinate transformation γ(x) = x mod k imposes patch-like periodic constraints that force the model to learn repetitive structures at multiple scales, paired with a corresponding sawtooth-aware downsampling in the Basis Transformer; and (2) multi-frequency modulation with periodic functions ψ ∈ {sin, cos} and frequency scalars α compels each basis to fit both high- and low-frequency components, counteracting the spectral bias of neural networks. For compression, the trained SR module replaces the synthesis transform in a TCM-based pipeline, and a Basis Merging Transformer consolidates M per-image bases into one shared basis to exploit cross-image structural redundancy, reducing coding cost in multi-image settings.

## Results

On 4× super-resolution, the best model HAT-L-F† achieves 33.75 dB PSNR on Set5, 29.87 dB on Set14, 28.31 dB on BSD100, 29.51 dB on Urban100, and 33.36 dB on Manga109, representing a 204.4% average relative PSNR gain over the SwinIR baseline toward the HAT-L upper bound. For image compression, TCM-HAT-L-F achieves a −21.09% BD-Rate relative to VTM, surpassing the previous SOTA (LALIC at −15.26%) by 9.35 percentage points. The multi-image variant (M=24) reaches −20.97% BD-Rate while reducing decoding time to 0.084 s per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Every Factorized Features variant consistently outperforms its parameter-matched backbone: HAT-F† beats HAT† by +0.35 dB (Set5) and +0.42 dB (Urban100); DAT-F† beats DAT-L† by +0.12 dB (Set5) and +0.26 dB (Urban100); ATD-F† beats ATD-L† by +0.14 dB (Set5) and +0.28 dB (Urban100). The largest model HAT-L-F† surpasses HAT-L† by +0.45 dB on Set5 and +0.91 dB on Urban100, the most challenging benchmark with repetitive structures. In image compression, TCM-HAT-L-F (−21.09% BD-Rate) outperforms LALIC (−15.26%), TCM (−11.74%), ELIC (−7.24%), and VTM (0%) by wide margins, while also achieving faster encoding (0.109 s) than TCM (0.16 s) and LALIC (0.27 s). The multi-image variant scales gracefully, with M=16 reaching −19.88% BD-Rate and M=24 reaching −20.97%, approaching single-image performance while sharing a single merged basis across all images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Factor Fields [12] basis-coefficient decomposition by replacing fixed sinusoidal or learned-frequency bases with fully learned non-uniform basis maps, augmented by a sawtooth coordinate transformation (γ) that enforces patch-like periodicity and a multi-frequency modulation (ψ, α) that forces each basis to capture both low- and high-frequency content simultaneously. It departs from CosAE-style learnable Fourier series [66] by arguing that uniform harmonic functions are too rigid for local detail, and from architecture-centric SR methods (HAT, DAT, ATD) by treating the representation itself—rather than the network topology—as the primary lever for improvement. For compression, it plugs the trained SR module as a synthesis transform into the TCM [62] pipeline, and introduces a basis-merging transformer for multi-image compression that has no direct analogue in prior learned compression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified Factorized Features representation offers a principled way to share a single image descriptor across SR and compression, reducing the need for task-specific architectural engineering. The -21.09% BD-Rate over VTM and consistent 0.2–0.5 dB PSNR gains across four backbone families (SwinIR, HAT, DAT, ATD) suggest the representation is broadly applicable rather than tied to one architecture. However, the method is compute-heavy (up to 66M params, 240G MACs for the largest SR variant), the multi-image compression at M=1 actually degrades below the VTM anchor, and the authors acknowledge that real-time decoding and semantic integration remain open. The order-dependence finding (coefficient-before-basis vs. basis-before-coefficient) is a useful practical insight but limits the modularity of the pipeline.
