## Problem

Existing contrastive decoding methods for hallucination mitigation rely on a single evil model to suppress hallucinatory tokens, but this approach fails to assign sufficient confidence to the correct factual answer. Moreover, a single factual statement often corresponds to multiple plausible hallucinatory alternatives, yet prior methods only contrast against one negative sample, limiting the model's discrimination capability between factual and hallucinatory content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255513/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt or question as input and produces a factual text response through autoregressive token-by-token generation. During inference, the pretrained LLM (Llama-2-7B-Chat) is augmented with a trained evil model and a truthful model, whose log-probabilities are combined at each decoding step. A dynamic hallucination detection module monitors groups of m newly generated tokens and triggers a tree-based revision mechanism when the factual consistency score falls below an adaptive threshold, resampling and pruning candidate paths until a verified token sequence is selected.

## Method

MCD introduces a new training procedure that constructs multiple positive-negative sample pairs per question (using HaluEval and DeepSeek-V3 to generate varied hallucinatory alternatives) and trains an evil model via Contrary DPO (CDPO, Eq. 8) to fully induce hallucinations, while a truthful model is trained via standard DPO to enhance factuality. At inference, the decoding score combines the base model's log-probability, minus a weighted evil-model log-probability, plus a weighted truthful-model log-probability (Eq. 3), so that a token receives high probability only when deemed factual by the truthful model and unlikely by the evil model. The truthful model's logits are further strengthened via linear-regression extrapolation from its last three Transformer layers, approximating deeper semantic processing without architectural changes. An adaptive plausibility constraint restricts contrastive penalties to a subset of high-probability tokens to preserve generation quality, and a dynamic hallucination detection function with a tree-based revision mechanism identifies and corrects hallucination-prone token groups in real time during generation.

## Results

On TruthfulQA open-ended generation, MCD achieves 53.27% True*Info (vs. 45.83% for Llama-2-7B-Chat baseline), with 74.41% truthfulness and 78.83% informativeness. On TruthfulQA multiple-choice, MCD scores 46.94/70.02/43.17% on MC1/MC2/MC3, improving over the baseline by +12.30/+18.71/+18.07%. On FActScore, MCD reaches 68.1 factual accuracy (vs. 63.8 baseline), and on FACTOR it achieves 69.79/79.66/58.65% on News/Expert/Wiki subsets, outperforming all baselines across every benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCD outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest contrastive decoding baseline ICD, MCD gains +3.48% True*Info (53.27 vs. 49.79) and +1.92/+0.94/+1.92% on MC1/MC2/MC3, attributed to using multiple hallucinatory pairs in CDPO training and the truthful model's confidence boost. CD and ICD perform poorly on FACTOR (22.20/30.89% News vs. MCD's 69.79%), indicating that single-evil-model contrastive decoding is easily misled on long paragraphs without truthful-model guidance. DoLa achieves competitive MC2 (63.80%) but trails MCD on all other TruthfulQA metrics and on FACTOR Expert (67.93% vs. 79.66%). Among representation editing methods, ITI and CSS are substantially outperformed—MCD exceeds ITI by +6.79% True*Info and +16.48% MC2. On FActScore, MCD leads ICD by +1.8 points (68.1 vs. 66.3) while maintaining a higher response ratio (54.7% vs. 36.1%), showing that dynamic hallucination detection preserves generation length while improving accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive decoding lineage (CD → ICD) by introducing a three-model decoding scheme: a base LLM, an evil model trained via Contrary DPO (CDPO) to amplify hallucinations, and a truthful model trained via standard DPO to reinforce factuality. The CDPO objective generalizes DPO's single positive-negative pair to multiple hallucinatory negatives per question, addressing the observation that one factual answer often has many plausible hallucinatory alternatives. The dynamic hallucination detection and tree-based revision mechanism departs from purely logit-level contrast by adding a token-level monitoring and correction loop during generation, drawing on the DoLa-style layer-extrapolation idea to approximate deeper-layer semantics in the truthful model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical insight is that single-model contrastive decoding (CD, ICD) suppresses hallucinations but fails to boost confidence in correct tokens, and the multi-model formulation directly addresses this gap with measurable gains across TruthfulQA, FActScore, and FACTOR. However, the method requires fine-tuning two auxiliary models on synthetic data (HaluEval + DeepSeek-V3-generated hallucinations), incurs a 2.3× inference cost, and is evaluated only on factuality-oriented benchmarks, leaving open whether it transfers to other hallucination dimensions (e.g., self-contradiction, instruction-following). The striking finding that CD and ICD actually degrade below baseline on FACTOR long-paragraph tasks (22–31% vs. 65% baseline) suggests existing contrastive methods are fragile in long-context settings, a limitation the multi-model approach partially overcomes.
