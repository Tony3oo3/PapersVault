## Problem

Preference-based RL (PbRL) for robotics is bottlenecked by the need for extensive human preference labeling, and even when foundation models (FMs) are used as synthetic evaluators, they rely on single-modality assessment (LLM-only or VLM-only), producing incomplete or unreliable feedback. Two intrinsic PbRL challenges remain unaddressed: query ambiguity in early training when trajectory pairs are uniformly low-quality, and the credit-assignment gap between trajectory-level preferences and state-action-level reward signals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes robot trajectories as state-action sequences, a brief task description, and a hybrid-extracted keyframe set (union of near-zero-velocity frames, smoothing-residual peaks, and change-point segments, plus first/last frames). The LLM receives structured textual projections organized by state/action dimensions, while the VLM receives the keyframe image sequence. Outputs include a fused preference label with calibrated confidence per trajectory pair, a learned reward function, an SAC policy, LLM-generated bootstrapped trajectories (foresight), and minimally-edited counterfactual trajectories (hindsight) used for auxiliary reward training.

## Method

PRIMT combines two synergistic modules. First, a hierarchical neuro-symbolic preference fusion: intra-modal fusion queries GPT-4o (LLM) and a VLM multiple times with permuted trajectory orderings, aggregates via majority voting, and calibrates confidence from both mean self-reported certainty and vote agreement; inter-modal fusion then resolves cross-modal conflicts using Probabilistic Soft Logic with four weighted rules (agreement, VLM-priority when visual discriminability is high, LLM-priority when temporal discriminability is high, and indecision when both confidences are low), where discriminability is measured via Wasserstein distance of CLIP keyframe embeddings and trajectory volatility (mean L2 second-order finite differences). Second, bidirectional trajectory synthesis: foresight uses a 3-step CoT code-generation pipeline (plan → executable code → execution under varied initial conditions) to warm-start the trajectory buffer with diverse, task-aligned but non-optimal anchors; hindsight prompts the LLM to perform SCM-based causal reasoning (abduction to identify critical steps, action to minimally edit one step, prediction to verify the counterfactual is sub-preferred), and a causal auxiliary loss enforces reward separation at edited steps while penalizing reward drift in unedited regions, combined with the standard Bradley-Terry preference loss for reward model training.

## Results

PRIMT is evaluated on 2 locomotion tasks (Hopper Stand, Walker Walk from DMC) and 6 manipulation tasks (Button Press, Door Open, Sweep Into from MetaWorld; PickSingleYCB, StackCube, PegInsertionSide from ManiSkill2), reporting success rate for manipulation and episodic return for locomotion. PRIMT consistently outperforms all single-modality FM-based baselines (RL-VLM-F, RL-SaLLM-F, PrefCLM) in both final performance and convergence speed across all 8 tasks. Notably, PRIMT is competitive with the oracle PrefGT (expert-designed reward) and even surpasses it on Sweep Into and Peg Insertion Side, while achieving faster early-stage learning. With GPT-4o-mini as the backbone, PRIMT achieves a 94% cost reduction with only a 16–26% performance drop, yielding a 13× improvement in cost-performance efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRIMT outperforms RL-VLM-F [14] (VLM-only feedback) across all 8 tasks, with the gap most pronounced on high-dimensional ManiSkill tasks where visual-only evaluation struggles with temporal dynamics. Against RL-SaLLM-F [16] (LLM-only with self-augmented trajectories), PRIMT wins on all tasks; RL-SaLLM-F in particular struggles on ManiSkill tasks because it assumes LLM-generated trajectories are always preferred, an assumption that breaks down in high-dimensional state-action spaces. PRIMT also surpasses PrefCLM [15] (crowdsourced LLM feedback), which relies on direct environment code access, confirming that the hierarchical neuro-symbolic fusion and trajectory synthesis provide gains beyond simply increasing the number of LLM queries. Compared to the oracle PrefGT [8] (expert-designed scripted reward), PRIMT narrows the gap substantially and even exceeds it on Sweep Into and Peg Insertion Side, suggesting that the combination of multimodal feedback quality and bidirectional trajectory synthesis can match or exceed hand-crafted reward signals. The naive multimodal baseline PrefMul (feeding all modalities directly to a multimodal FM without structured fusion) performs poorly, validating the necessity of the hierarchical PSL-based fusion design.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRIMT extends the PbRL-with-FM line of work (RL-VLM-F, RL-SaLLM-F, PrefCLM) in two key directions: it replaces single-modality FM evaluation with a hierarchical neuro-symbolic fusion strategy that combines LLM and VLM judgments via Probabilistic Soft Logic, and it repurposes FMs from passive evaluators into active agents that synthesize trajectories (foresight bootstrapping and hindsight counterfactuals). The training backbone is PEBBLE with uncertainty-based sampling, and the counterfactual generation is grounded in Pearl's structural causal model framework. The causal auxiliary loss for credit assignment departs from prior approaches that rely on transformer world models or additional human annotations, instead leveraging minimally-edited counterfactual pairs with a targeted separation-and-consistency objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRIMT addresses the two most practical bottlenecks in deploying PbRL for robotics: the cost of human preference labeling and the difficulty of credit assignment from trajectory-level signals. The finding that naive multimodal fusion (PrefMul) degrades performance while structured PSL-based fusion is critical provides a useful design principle for combining heterogeneous FM signals. Limitations include the computational cost of multimodal FM queries (mitigated by the 94% cost reduction with GPT-4o-mini), evaluation primarily in simulation with limited real-world validation (single Kinova Jaco robot), and single-agent scope. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-agent settings, non-robotic domains, and whether the PSL rules generalize beyond the specific trajectory-context predicates (VDHigh, TDHigh) used here.
