## Problem

Polarization images encode rich physical information (birefringence, surface stress, material properties) absent from standard RGB, but their acquisition requires expensive specialized hardware such as polarization cameras or rotating polarizers. No prior work has explored whether neural networks can estimate polarization information directly from RGB inputs without dedicated sensors. This paper introduces RGB-to-polarization estimation as a new task and establishes the first comprehensive benchmark for sensor-free polarimetric imaging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251130/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a single RGB image (S₀) at 256×256 resolution, normalized to [0,1]. The output is a 9-channel tensor S ∈ ℝ^(H×W×9), where each Stokes component S₁, S₂, S₃ is represented as a 3-channel image (one channel per RGB band), concatenated along the channel dimension. From these Stokes components, physically meaningful derived quantities—degree of linear polarization (DoLP), angle of linear polarization (AoLP), and circular polarization ratio (CoP)—can be computed for downstream polarization-based vision tasks.

## Method

The benchmark evaluates seven models across two architectural families on the Jeon et al. spectro-polarimetric dataset (1,000 training / 200 test pairs). Restoration-based models (Restormer, Uformer) are adapted by replacing the output layer with a 9-channel head and trained with L1 loss; MAE is initialized with self-supervised pre-trained weights, its predictor projection layer is inflated to 9 channels via channel-wise replication, and the full encoder-decoder is fine-tuned end-to-end. Generation-based models include task-specific diffusion models (WDiff, DiT) trained from scratch with RGB as conditioning, and pre-trained Stable Diffusion variants (RealFill, Img2ImgTurbo) fine-tuned via LoRA adapters, where a separate model is trained per Stokes component to reuse the 3-channel UNet decoder. The key finding is that pre-trained restoration models (especially MAE) achieve the best pixel-level accuracy, while diffusion models struggle with structural fidelity, revealing a clear performance gap between restoration and generation paradigms for this task.

## Results

On the Jeon et al. spectro-polarimetric dataset (256×256), MAE achieves the best average PSNR (24.74 dB) and SSIM (0.8876), while Uformer attains the lowest average LPIPS (0.2267). Restoration-based models (MAE, Uformer, Restormer) consistently outperform generation-based models (DiT, WDiff, RealFill, Img2ImgTurbo) across all three metrics. The S1 component is the hardest to estimate (MAE: 22.73 dB PSNR), while S2 and S3 are substantially easier (25.54 and 25.94 dB respectively), reflecting the weaker and more spatially uniform nature of horizontal/vertical polarization differences in natural scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAE (pre-trained self-supervised ViT) achieves the highest average PSNR (24.74 dB) and SSIM (0.8876), outperforming Uformer by 0.40 dB PSNR and 0.015 SSIM, and Restormer by 0.76 dB PSNR. However, Uformer attains the best perceptual quality with the lowest LPIPS (0.2267 vs. MAE's 0.2684), suggesting MAE's outputs, while more pixel-accurate, may contain subtle perceptual artifacts. Among generation-based models, DiT (23.33 dB PSNR) and Img2ImgTurbo (23.33 dB PSNR) are the strongest, both trailing MAE by ~1.4 dB PSNR; WDiff is the weakest overall at 13.11 dB PSNR, indicating that task-specific diffusion from scratch struggles significantly with this task. Pre-trained diffusion models (Img2ImgTurbo, RealFill) outperform from-scratch diffusion (WDiff, DiT) on PSNR/SSIM but suffer from higher LPIPS (0.3542 and 0.2654 vs. 0.2447 for DiT), revealing a trade-off between pixel accuracy and perceptual fidelity in generative approaches. On the cross-dataset generalization test (Table 2), the same ranking holds: MAE and Uformer remain the top performers on Qiu et al. and Kurita et al. datasets, while diffusion-based methods degrade more sharply, confirming that restoration backbones with pre-training generalize better to unseen polarization scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing RGB-to-polarization estimation as a formal task, repurposing existing image restoration architectures (Restormer, Uformer, MAE) and generative diffusion models (WDiff, DiT, RealFill, Img2ImgTurbo) for a problem none of them were originally designed for. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of polarization optics (Stokes parameter representation, Poincaré sphere geometry) and low-level vision, leveraging the spectro-polarimetric dataset of Jeon et al. as its training substrate. The key departure from prior polarization work is the elimination of dedicated hardware: instead of acquiring polarization with specialized sensors, the paper treats it as a pure image-to-image prediction problem from standard RGB inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first benchmark for sensor-free polarization estimation, this paper establishes a performance ceiling and a standardized evaluation protocol that future methods can build upon. The downstream task evaluation revealing that mIoU drops from 92.45 to 45.56 when all polarization channels are estimated highlights a significant gap between current estimation quality and practical utility, making confidence-aware estimation and physically constrained losses critical open problems. The finding that S1 is consistently harder to estimate than S2/S3, and that pre-trained representations (MAE) outperform both task-specific restoration and diffusion models, provides actionable guidance for future architecture design. Limitations include reliance on a single primary dataset with limited material diversity and no evaluation under adverse conditions.
