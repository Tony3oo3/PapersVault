## Problem

Standard dynamic DAG learning methods (e.g., DYNOTEARS, IDYNO) assume stationarity or i.i.d. data, so when time series distributions shift over time—due to policy changes, seasonal effects, or sensor availability—existing approaches must re-learn the graph from scratch, losing historical information and requiring large sample sizes per segment. No prior method combines meta-learning with interventional dynamic DAG discovery in an online, non-stationary setting where both parameters and graph topology can vary across domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes non-stationary time series data pre-partitioned into Q domains (time segments), each with train and validation splits, plus optional binary intervention-target indicators. In the online setting, a sliding window of w previous domains provides meta-training context. The output is a factorized causal graph per domain: a shared weighted adjacency matrix W_s (learned across all domains) combined with a domain-specific private matrix W_{p,m} via Hadamard product to yield the final DAG W_s ∘ W_{p,m}, along with domain-specific SEM parameters (linear or neural network).

## Method

Meta-D²AG formulates dynamic DAG learning as a bilevel meta-optimization: the upper level learns shared parameters W_s that capture invariant causal structure across domains, while the lower level learns private parameters W_{p,m} per domain subject to the NOTEARS acyclicity constraint h(W_s ∘ W_{p,m}) = 0. The key architectural choice is the Hadamard product between shared and private adjacency matrices, which ensures W_s must span all edges present in any domain's graph. To handle the nested constraint, the paper proposes a penalty-based bilevel proximal gradient descent that absorbs both the inner-loop optimality condition and the acyclicity constraint into a single-level penalized objective, solved with a sliding-window average for online efficiency. Theoretical analysis establishes a regret bound of O(m/w²) under proximal PL conditions and extends IDYNO's identifiability theorem to the factorized W_s ∘ W_{p,m} setting with an additional non-sparsity condition on W_s.

## Results

On the Sprites World RL environment, Meta-D²AG achieves the lowest Structural Hamming Distance (SHD) among all methods: 5.2±0.6 for 5 objects and 9.1±1.5 for 10 objects. On synthetic time series datasets (both batch and online settings), Meta-D²AG consistently achieves the lowest SHD across variable sizes d=5 to 20, outperforming all baselines. The method also demonstrates the tightest variance (±0.6 at O=5), indicating more stable graph recovery across repeated runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Meta-D²AG outperforms all five baselines on both object counts in Sprites World. The closest competitor is IDYNO (5.8±1.7 at O=5), which Meta-D²AG beats by 0.6 SHD with notably lower variance (±0.6 vs ±1.7). The gap widens substantially at O=10, where Meta-D²AG (9.1) surpasses IDYNO (12) by 2.9 SHD and GOLEM (14) by 4.9 SHD. DYNOTEARS and J-PCMCI+ degrade severely with more objects (39 and 31 SHD respectively at O=10), while Meta-D²AG scales much more gracefully. On synthetic datasets in both batch and online settings (Figures 2a/2b), Meta-D²AG achieves the lowest SHD across all variable sizes, with the online setting showing particular advantage from leveraging temporal transitions between domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-optimization DAG learning lineage (NOTEARS → DYNOTEARS → IDYNO) by wrapping it in a bilevel meta-learning framework that treats distribution shifts across time segments as distinct domains. The direct predecessor is Meta-DAG [39] (ICASSP 2023), which introduced the shared/private parameter decomposition for causal discovery; the current work generalizes it to the online setting, adds interventional data handling, and introduces a penalty-based proximal gradient solver for the constrained inner loop. The Hadamard-product coupling of shared and private adjacency matrices (W_s ∘ W_p) is a natural extension of the masking idea in [48], while the sliding-window regret analysis draws on online nonconvex optimization tools from [21, 22].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: standard dynamic DAG learners assume stationarity or require re-learning from scratch when distributions shift, losing historical information. The online meta-learning formulation with a provable O(m/w²) regret bound and identifiability guarantee offers a principled way to adapt causal graphs with few new samples per domain. A key limitation is the reliance on predefined domain boundaries, and the authors identify automatic shift detection as an open problem. Broader impact is moderate: the method is well-suited to sensor networks, retail, and public-health monitoring where non-stationarity is the norm, but the evaluation is limited to synthetic data and a single RL environment (Sprites World), leaving real-world validation open.
