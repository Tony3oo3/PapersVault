## Problem

The Pearl Causal Hierarchy treats the counterfactual layer L3 as monolithic, yet recent work showed that some L3 distributions are empirically accessible via counterfactual randomization while others (e.g., PNS) are not. This heterogeneity raises the question of which graphical model assumptions are both sufficient and necessary for counterfactual inference in the region between L2 and full L3 — a gap left unaddressed by existing models, where CBN is too weak to handle counterfactual queries and CTFBN imposes unfalsifiable assumptions beyond what is needed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a causal diagram G, a set of available distributions (observational, interventional, or realizable counterfactual), and a counterfactual query Q, and determines whether Q is identifiable, producing an explicit expression of Q as a function of the available data. A key operational detail is the Counterfactual Reachability Set (CRS) check, which must be applied before each ctf-calculus rule to verify that all intermediate distributions fall within the target layer (L2.25 or L2.5); in L2.25 this check is stricter, requiring examination of descendants of ancestors in addition to the ancestor set itself. The output also includes a formal hierarchy (PCH*) ordering all graphical models by the strength of constraints they encode.

## Method

The paper defines two new distribution collections — L2.25 (where counterfactual randomization on X fixes a single value across all children) and L2.5 (where each child may receive a potentially different randomized value) — and introduces corresponding graphical models CBN2.25 and CBN2.5 that encode independence, exclusion, and consistency constraints restricted to these collections. A sound and complete ctf-calculus is developed by restricting the three standard counterfactual rules (consistency, independence, exclusion) to distributions validated by the CRS check, yielding an identifiability algorithm for each model. The paper then proves a monotonic hierarchy P^L1 ⊆ P^L2 ⊆ P^L2.25 ⊆ P^L2.5 ⊆ P^L3 (Theorem 3) and a corresponding hierarchy of graphical models where each higher model's constraints strictly subsume those below (Theorem 4). The central trade-off is formalized: ascending the hierarchy increases expressive power (supporting more demanding queries) but decreases empirical falsifiability, since higher models encode constraints over distributions that cannot be sampled with available experimental procedures.

## Results

The paper establishes four main theoretical results: (1) Theorem 1 proves that the causal diagram induced by any SCM is a valid CBN2.25 (and CBN2.5) for the corresponding distribution collection; (2) Theorem 2 proves soundness and completeness of the ctf-calculus restricted to L2.25/L2.5 distributions for identifiability; (3) Theorem 3 (PCH*) establishes the inclusion chain P^L1 ⊆ P^L2 ⊆ P^L2.25 ⊆ P^L2.5 ⊆ P^L3, formally inserting two intermediate layers between the interventional and full counterfactual layers; (4) Theorem 4 proves the monotonicity property that constraints encoded by a graphical model at layer i are always a subset of those at any higher layer j. No empirical experiments are conducted.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons. Instead, it positions its contributions relative to existing graphical models in the Pearl Causal Hierarchy: BN (L1), CBN (L2), and CTFBN (L3). The key comparison is structural: CBN2.25 and CBN2.5 are shown to be strictly more expressive than CBN (which cannot represent L2.25/L2.5 queries at all) yet strictly less assumption-heavy than CTFBN (which encodes constraints over all L3 distributions, many of which are empirically unfalsifiable). Table 1 illustrates this trade-off concretely: for an L2.5 query, CBN is insufficient, CTFBN is sufficient but not necessary (it imposes unfalsifiable extra constraints), while CBN2.5 is both sufficient and necessary — the parsimonious match. The paper does not benchmark computational cost or scalability of the ctf-calculus rules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Pearl Causal Hierarchy by inserting two intermediate distribution layers (L2.25 and L2.5) between the interventional (L2) and full counterfactual (L3) layers, building on the counterfactual randomization procedure (Bareinboim et al., 2015) and the characterization of realizable counterfactual distributions (Raghavan & Bareinboim, 2025). The new graphical models CBN2.25 and CBN2.5 are defined by restricting the constraint types of CTFBN (Correa & Bareinboim, 2025) to these narrower distribution collections, and the inferential machinery is a layer-restricted version of ctf-calculus. The work also draws on the foundational PCH framework (Bareinboim et al., 2022) and the broader causal AI program (Bareinboim, 2025), positioning the new models as a refinement of the existing BN → CBN → CTFBN progression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, formal criterion for matching the strength of graphical model assumptions to the empirical accessibility of available data, operationalizing the parsimony principle that one should use the weakest model sufficient for a given query. This is practically important because it delineates which counterfactual queries (e.g., ETT, NDE) can be answered with experimentally accessible data via counterfactual randomization versus those requiring untestable CTFBN-level assumptions. Open questions include the computational complexity of the identification procedure for CBN2.25/CBN2.5, extension to continuous or high-dimensional settings, and whether the hierarchy admits further intermediate layers between L2.5 and L3.
