## Problem

Existing adaptive image tokenizers—whether Matryoshka-style (nested prefix representations) or recurrent/iterative (e.g., ALIT)—require multiple encoder-decoder passes at test time to identify the minimal token count sufficient for a target reconstruction quality. This iterative search is computationally expensive and impractical for latency-sensitive applications like VLMs and video models. The paper asks whether a single forward pass can determine how many tokens an image needs, inspired by the Kolmogorov Complexity principle that the shortest sufficient program should be identifiable without exhaustive search.

## Input / Output

At inference, KARL takes a 256×256 RGB image, a maximum token budget T (up to 256), and a target reconstruction loss threshold ε (default 0.05, measured as pixel-wise ℓ₁). The image is first mapped to a 256-token 2D grid by a frozen VQGAN or VAE; the encoder then distills these into a 1D sequence of at most T tokens, each accompanied by a halting probability in [0,1]. Tokens with halting probability above 0.75 are excluded from decoding, yielding a variable-length 1D token sequence (typically 32–256 tokens) that the decoder uses to reconstruct the image. The loss threshold ε acts as a task-conditioning signal: lower ε demands more tokens, enabling task-aware compression without retraining.

## Method

KARL employs a Perceiver-style transformer encoder that distills 2D VQGAN/VAE tokens into a variable-length 1D latent sequence, with the decoder performing cross-attention between the 1D tokens and a masked 2D positional grid to reconstruct the image. The key training innovation is a loss-conditioned, two-phase procedure per iteration that mirrors Schmidhuber's upside-down RL: in the Estimating Image Complexity (EIC) phase, the model attempts near-lossless reconstruction under a randomly sampled budget T (ε=0), recording the achieved error ε₀; in the Learning to Tokenize (LTC) phase, the encoder is conditioned on ε₀ via a learned loss-embedding token and given an expanded budget T+ΔT, trained to match the same reconstruction quality while assigning high halting probability (supervised by BCE) to the extra ΔT tokens. This induces a self-supervised curriculum—complex images that cannot achieve low error under small budgets are never paired with low-loss/low-token conditions—so the network learns to allocate tokens adaptively in a single pass. At inference, only the LTC-style forward pass runs: the encoder outputs token embeddings and halting probabilities simultaneously, and the decoder reconstructs the image using only non-halted tokens, achieving minimum-program search without iterative refinement.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 with fixed 256 tokens, KARL achieves L1 loss of 0.70, LPIPS of 0.14, SSIM of 0.49, and DreamSim of 0.11, matching or slightly outperforming ALIT (0.76, 0.16, 0.44, 0.10) and clearly beating One-D-Piece (0.94, 0.19, 0.40, 0.12). Under adaptive token allocation targeting ℓ₁ < 0.05, KARL uses ~230 tokens on average in a single 1+1 encoder-decoder pass, versus ~227 tokens requiring ~7×2 passes for ALIT and ~248 tokens requiring ~7.7+1 passes for One-D-Piece, while achieving comparable or better LPIPS (0.15 vs 0.17 vs 0.19) and SSIM (0.47 vs 0.43 vs 0.40). On ImageNet-1K, KARL similarly leads on L1 (0.68 vs 0.75 for ALIT) and SSIM (0.50 vs 0.45) at 256 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ALIT (a recurrent adaptive tokenizer requiring multiple encoder-decoder iterations), KARL matches or slightly exceeds reconstruction quality at fixed token counts—e.g., L1 of 0.70 vs 0.76 and SSIM of 0.49 vs 0.44 at 256 tokens on IN100—while ALIT leads marginally on DreamSim (0.10 vs 0.11). The critical practical advantage is inference cost: under adaptive allocation targeting ℓ₁ < 0.05, KARL completes in 1+1 passes versus ~7×2 passes for ALIT, a ~14× reduction in encoder-decoder rollouts. Against One-D-Piece (a Matryoshka-style prefix-based tokenizer), KARL consistently outperforms on per-image metrics: L1 of 0.68 vs 0.94, LPIPS of 0.14 vs 0.19, and SSIM of 0.50 vs 0.40 at 256 tokens on IN1K, though One-D-Piece produces slightly better FID scores due to its tendency to generate blurrier images that score well on distributional metrics. Under variable token allocation, KARL uses fewer tokens than One-D-Piece (229.6 vs 247.9 at ε=0.05) while achieving better LPIPS (0.15 vs 0.19) and SSIM (0.47 vs 0.40), confirming that the single-pass halting mechanism identifies more compact sufficient representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

KARL departs from two families of adaptive tokenizers—Matryoshka/prefix-based methods (One-D-Piece, FlexTok, ElasticTok) that assume nested representations, and recurrent/iterative methods (ALIT) that require multiple encoder-decoder passes at test time—by learning to predict halting probabilities in a single forward pass. The training strategy repurposes Schmidhuber's upside-down RL paradigm, treating reconstruction loss as a task-conditioning signal rather than a reward to predict, and grounds the token-count objective in Kolmogorov Complexity / Minimum Description Length principles. Architecturally, it builds on Perceiver-style 1D latent distillation over VQGAN/VAE 2D token grids, similar to ALIT and One-D-Piece, but adds per-token halting supervision via a two-phase EIC→LTC procedure within each training iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Single-pass adaptive tokenization removes the iterative search bottleneck that limits prior methods in latency-sensitive settings like VLMs and video models, making variable-length representations practically deployable. The AIT framing—treating active token count as an approximation of Kolmogorov Complexity—offers a principled lens for understanding when and why representations should be shorter, and the observed alignment with human complexity judgments (SAVOIAS) and structural vs. noise distinctions suggests the learned measure captures meaningful signal beyond mere compression ratio. Open questions include whether the KC approximation generalizes to video or multi-modal settings, whether higher-order AIT notions (sophistication, logical depth) can be explicitly modeled, and whether the loss-conditioned training strategy transfers to downstream task-aware tokenization beyond reconstruction.
