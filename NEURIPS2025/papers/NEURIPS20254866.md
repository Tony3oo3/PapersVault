## Problem

Protein fitness optimization requires navigating a combinatorially vast sequence space with only hundreds of wet-lab fitness measurements, yet prior work has largely evaluated steering strategies on computational surrogates or with large labeled datasets, leaving it unclear how different generative priors and guidance methods compare under realistic low-data conditions. This paper asks which steering strategies (classifier guidance, posterior sampling, noise optimization) work best with which types of discrete diffusion models when only ~10² real fitness labels are available, and how adaptive optimization principles like uncertainty-aware exploration can be integrated.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multiple sequence alignment (MSA) of homologous protein sequences to pretrain a generative prior, a small set of labeled sequence-fitness pairs (~200) to train a supervised value function, and a defined design space specifying which residues are allowed to vary (e.g., 15 positions in TrpB, all 119 in CreiLOV, all 56 in GB1). It outputs novel protein sequences sampled from the steered posterior distribution, evaluated for fitness via a computational oracle trained on large real datasets to simulate low-throughput wet-lab assays. In adaptive mode, each round samples a batch of sequences, evaluates them, retrains the value function, and regenerates, mimicking iterative experimental campaigns.

## Method

The core approach pretrains generative priors (discrete diffusion models with uniform or absorbing-state noise, continuous diffusion, and autoregressive language models) on MSA sequences, then steers generation at inference time using plug-and-play guidance without updating prior weights. Three guidance strategies are evaluated: Classifier Guidance (CG), which skews the reverse-time Markov chain rate matrix using a time-dependent value function; Decoupled Annealing Posterior Sampling (DAPS), which uses variable splitting to sample from the posterior with only a clean-data value function; and Noise Optimization Sampling (NOS), which optimizes continuous embeddings. The key finding is that DAPS with discrete diffusion models (MDLM, D3PM) achieves the strongest steerability with few labels, outperforming DPO-based finetuning of language models. For adaptive optimization, the paper introduces a Thompson-sampling-like scheme where an ensemble of 10 neural network regressors is trained on accumulated fitness data, and each new sequence is generated by drawing a different value function from the ensemble, enabling principled exploration-exploitation trade-offs across optimization rounds.

## Results

Using only 200 sequence-fitness pairs for steering, plug-and-play guidance (CG, DAPS) with discrete diffusion models (MDLM, D3PM) consistently generates sequences with higher mean and maximum fitness than the unconditional prior, DPO-finetuned ARLM, and APEXGo across all three proteins (TrpB, CreiLOV, GB1). DAPS with discrete diffusion models achieves the strongest overall steerability, while CG and DAPS perform comparably on discrete models but DAPS outperforms CG on continuous diffusion models. In adaptive optimization (100 sequences/round, ensemble of 10 value functions, Thompson sampling), maximum fitness improves over successive rounds, with ensemble-based Thompson sampling outperforming single-classifier guidance. The UDLM model suffers from mode collapse, and continuous diffusion models underperform due to weaker prior quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SGPO (plug-and-play guidance with diffusion models) against two main baselines: DPO with an autoregressive language model (finetuned ProGen2) and APEXGo (trust-region Bayesian optimization in VAE latent space). SGPO with DAPS or CG guidance outperforms DPO+ARLM on both mean and maximum fitness across all three proteins (TrpB, CreiLOV, GB1), with the paper attributing this to greater steerability of diffusion models and lower computational cost (minutes vs. hours for DPO training). SGPO also outperforms APEXGo in adaptive optimization campaigns, achieving higher mean and maximum fitness over multiple rounds; the authors attribute APEXGo's inferiority to difficulty calibrating the trust region in low-data regimes and reliance on latent space structure that limits extrapolation to high-fitness but unnatural variants. Additionally, sampling from the unconditional generative prior (no guidance) serves as a lower bound, and all guided methods exceed it. The Thompson sampling variant (ensemble of 10 value functions) further improves maximum fitness over single-classifier guidance, suggesting that uncertainty-aware exploration is beneficial in the low-data regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and systematically compares plug-and-play guidance strategies for discrete diffusion models of protein sequences—Classifier Guidance (Nisonoff et al., 2025), Decoupled Annealing Posterior Sampling (Zhang et al., 2025), and Diffusion Optimized Sampling (Gruver et al., 2023)—under realistic low-data conditions (~200 fitness labels), a regime where prior work has largely been absent. It departs from the dominant RL-based alignment paradigm (DPO on protein language models, e.g., Widatalla et al. 2024; Stocco et al. 2024) by showing that frozen-prior guidance is more steerable and computationally cheaper when labels are scarce. The adaptive optimization component adapts Thompson sampling (Kandasamy et al., 2018) to the SGPO setting by ensembling multiple value functions, bridging Bayesian optimization principles with generative model steering in a way not previously explored for protein design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive, side-by-side comparison of generative priors (continuous diffusion, D3PM, MDLM, UDLM, ARLM) and steering strategies (CG, DAPS, NOS, DPO) for protein fitness optimization with real assay data, yielding actionable best practices for practitioners working with hundreds of labels. Key open questions remain: whether findings generalize to non-native fitness objectives where the evolutionary prior is less informative, whether the oracle-based evaluation faithfully captures wet-lab noise and epistasis, and how these methods scale to multi-objective or variable-length sequence design. The finding that DAPS with discrete diffusion is the strongest plug-and-play approach, combined with ensemble-based Thompson sampling for exploration, offers a practical recipe that could accelerate real-world directed evolution campaigns.
