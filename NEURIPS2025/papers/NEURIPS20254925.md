## Problem

No finite-time (non-asymptotic) convergence guarantees exist for average-reward Q-learning with asynchronous updates driven by a single Markovian trajectory. The absence of a discount factor makes the Bellman operator non-contractive in any norm, and the adaptive stepsizes required for correct convergence introduce strong history-dependent correlations that render the algorithm a non-Markovian stochastic approximation, a setting not addressed by existing finite-time SA theory.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a finite MDP (state space S, action space A, unknown transition kernel P, bounded reward R ∈ [-1,1]) and a fully-supported behavior policy π, interacting with the environment one step at a time to collect a single Markovian trajectory. It outputs the sequence of Q-function estimates {Q_k} ∈ R^{|S||A|}, with convergence measured in the span seminorm p_span(·) rather than a standard norm, since the Bellman equation admits the non-unique solution set {Q* + ce | c ∈ R}. An optimal policy is recovered by taking argmax_a Q_k(s,a) at each state.

## Method

The algorithm uses adaptive stepsizes α_k(s,a) = α/(N_k(s,a)+h) inversely proportional to the visit count of each state-action pair, which the paper proves are necessary: with universal stepsizes, the algorithm provably converges to the wrong target (the asynchronous Bellman equation rather than the original Bellman equation). The key insight is that adaptive stepsizes act as implicit importance sampling, reweighting asynchronous updates by empirical visit frequencies to recover the correct Bellman operator. To handle the resulting non-Markovian structure, the paper reformulates the algorithm as a time-inhomogeneous Markovian SA by augmenting the state with the empirical frequency matrix D_k, then applies a Lyapunov-drift analysis using a generalized Moreau envelope of p_span(Q²)/2. The correlation between the time-inhomogeneous noise and the iterates is broken via a two-step argument: (1) establishing almost-sure time-varying bounds on the iterates that grow at most logarithmically, and (2) combining conditioning on the fast-mixing Markov chain with Markov chain concentration inequalities to control the stochastic error, yielding a Õ(1/k) mean-square convergence rate in the span seminorm.

## Results

The paper establishes that the mean-square error in the span seminorm, E[p_span(Q_k − Q*)²], converges at rate Õ(1/k) for the last iterate of average-reward Q-learning with adaptive stepsizes, provided the stepsize parameter α satisfies α(1−β) > 2 where β is the span-seminorm contraction factor of the Bellman operator. The sample complexity to achieve accuracy ε is Õ(|S|³|A|³ D_min⁻²(1−β)⁻⁵ε⁻²). Prior to a burn-in phase of Õ(log k) iterations, the iterates satisfy an almost-sure bound growing at most logarithmically in k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No empirical baselines are compared, as this is a purely theoretical convergence-analysis paper. The key theoretical comparison is between adaptive stepsizes and universal stepsizes (e.g., α_k(s,a) = 1/k for all (s,a)): Proposition 4.1 proves that universal stepsizes cause the algorithm to converge in p_span to a fixed point of the asynchronous Bellman operator H̄(·) whose solution set is provably disjoint from that of the original Bellman operator H(·), except in the degenerate case where the stationary distribution is uniform. Adaptive stepsizes, interpreted as implicit importance sampling with weights estimated from empirical visit frequencies, are shown to be necessary for convergence to the correct target and achieve the Õ(1/k) rate. The paper also notes that its sample-complexity dependence on |S||A| and 1/(1−β) is not tight relative to existing information-theoretic lower bounds derived under a generative (i.i.d.) model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the finite-time analysis of discounted Q-learning (Even-Dar et al. 2003; Chen et al. 2024) to the average-reward setting, where the Bellman operator is only a span-seminorm contraction rather than a norm contraction. It builds on the Lyapunov-drift framework with Moreau envelope Lyapunov functions from Chen et al. (2024) [21] and the seminorm stability theory of Chen et al. (2025) [24], but departs fundamentally by handling a non-Markovian SA structure induced by adaptive stepsizes, requiring a time-inhomogeneous Markovian reformulation and new correlation-breaking techniques. The implicit importance sampling interpretation of adaptive stepsizes (Section 4.2) is a novel conceptual bridge connecting the algorithm to classical off-policy correction, and the proof that universal stepsizes converge to the wrong target (Proposition 4.1) has no analogue in the discounted literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-time last-iterate convergence guarantee for average-reward Q-learning with asynchronous (single-trajectory) updates, filling a gap where only asymptotic results existed. The demonstration that adaptive stepsizes are necessary for correctness—not merely for rate—reframes how practitioners should think about the algorithm and provides a clear diagnostic for why naive extensions of discounted Q-learning fail. Open questions include tightening the sample complexity dependence on |S||A| and 1/(1−β) (currently far from information-theoretic lower bounds), relaxing the seminorm contraction assumption to the more general non-expansive setting, and extending the analysis to other adaptive stepsize variants. The proof techniques for handling time-inhomogeneous Markovian noise in non-Markovian SA are broadly applicable beyond Q-learning.
