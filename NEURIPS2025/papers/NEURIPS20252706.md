## Problem

Prior individual regret bounds for cooperative stochastic MAB over communication graphs include an additive term proportional to the graph's diameter D, which can dominate the regret for practical topologies (e.g., cycles with D=Θ(m) or grids with D=Θ(√m)). This paper is the first to establish an individual regret bound that is entirely independent of the graph's diameter, showing that cooperation can reduce per-agent regret to O(𝓡/m + A² + A√log T) regardless of network topology.

## Input / Output

Each agent operates on an undirected connected communication graph G with m vertices, A actions with reward distributions supported on [0,1], and a time horizon T. Per round, an agent selects one action, observes a scalar reward, and exchanges messages with neighbors containing reward events (timestep, agent ID, action, reward) and elimination events (agent ID, eliminated action). The output is a per-agent action selection policy (round-robin over active actions) with a guaranteed individual regret bound that holds for every agent simultaneously, without requiring knowledge of the graph's diameter or global topology.

## Method

Coop-SE is a fully decentralized variant of Successive Elimination where each agent maintains a set of active actions, computes UCB/LCB confidence bounds from all locally observed reward and elimination messages, eliminates actions whose UCB falls below another action's LCB, and plays active actions in round-robin while forwarding messages to neighbors. The key analytical insight is an implicit synchronization lemma: during the middle half of any sufficiently long stage j, all agents within graph distance τ_j/4 of agent v necessarily share the same active action set, because elimination signals propagate within that radius before the stage ends. This synchronization allows bounding the number of samples v receives from its neighborhood as |N_{≤τ_j/4}|·τ_j/A_j per stage, which combined with standard concentration arguments yields a regret bound depending only on m, A, and T rather than the diameter. The analysis further handles communication constraints via a CONGEST-model variant (O(log(mA))-bit messages over a spanning tree with staggered round-robin scheduling) and a limited-communication variant (O(log T) rounds via hierarchical clustering on a spanning tree).

## Results

The paper's main result is that Coop-SE achieves an individual regret bound of O(ℛ/m + A² + A√(log T)), which is the first individual regret guarantee in cooperative stochastic MAB that is independent of the graph's diameter. In minimax form this is O(√(TA log T)/m + A² + A√(log T)), nearly matching the lower bound of Ω(√(TA/m) + √A). Under the CONGEST model (O(log(mA)) message size), the same regret bound is maintained, and with only O(log T) communication rounds the regret becomes O(log(A)·ℛ/m + A log(A) log T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Coop-UCB2 [21] and DDUCB [29], Coop-SE is the first algorithm to provide an individual (per-agent) regret guarantee rather than only a group/average regret bound; both baselines also require global graph knowledge (eigenvalues of the adjacency or gossip matrix) and their additive terms can scale as large as m on cycle graphs. Against UCB-TCOM [36], which does provide individual regret, Coop-SE eliminates the additive A·D diameter term entirely, replacing it with A² + A√(log T) that is independent of the graph topology; UCB-TCOM also requires knowing D in advance and uses m log(AT) bits per message versus Coop-SE-CONGEST's O(log(mA)) bits. The lower bound Ω(√(TA/m) + √A) confirms that Coop-SE's minimax regret is near-optimal, with the remaining gap being only in the exact dependence on A in the additive term and logarithmic factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Successive Elimination (SE) framework of Even-Dar et al. and prior cooperative SE variants by Yang et al. and Zhang et al., but its core contribution is a new analysis of the Coop-SE algorithm that eliminates the graph-diameter dependency from individual regret bounds. Prior individual-regret results (e.g., Wang et al.'s UCB-TCOM) carried an additive AD term, while consensus-based approaches (Coop-UCB2, DDUCB) required global graph knowledge and only achieved group regret. The key technical departure is the 'implicit synchronization' lemma, which shows that agents within radius τ/4 of a given agent necessarily maintain identical active-action sets over a sub-interval of each stage, enabling a diameter-free sample-count argument.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diameter-free individual regret bound O(R/m + A² + A√log T) is the first of its kind in cooperative stochastic MAB, and with m = R agents the regret becomes O(A² + A√log T), entirely independent of sub-optimality gaps. The result extends to the CONGEST model (log-size messages) and to O(log T) communication rounds, making it practically relevant for bandwidth- and latency-constrained networks. Open questions include closing the gap in the additive A-dependence between the upper and lower bounds, simultaneously achieving both log message size and log communication rounds, and extending the implicit-synchronization technique to UCB or Thompson Sampling where rarely-selected actions break agent synchronization.
