## Problem

Existing LLM safety benchmarks lack medical specificity, graded harmfulness levels, and systematic coverage of jailbreak-style adversarial prompts, while also overlooking over-cautious false refusals of benign queries. No prior medical benchmark jointly evaluates harmful content acceptance, adversarial robustness under rephrased jailbreaks, and false-positive rejection rates in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250671/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a single-turn medical safety prompt (one of 18K prompts spanning 8 safety principles, 4 harm levels 0–3, and 4 prompting styles: direct, indirect, obfuscated, role-play) and the target model's free-text response as input. The GPT-4o-mini judge (temperature 0) classifies each response into one of three exclusive categories—ACCEPT, CAUTION, or REFUSE—and the aggregate Safety Score is computed as a weighted mean over all prompts, where partial credit (e.g., 0.5 for CAUTION on a Level-2 prompt) reflects the appropriateness of the response relative to the prompt's harmfulness grade. A separate fine-tuned classifier outputs a jailbreak-type label (indirect, obfuscation, role-play, or none) used for the mitigation pipeline.

## Method

CARES is constructed by first distilling eight clinically grounded safety principles from AMA ethics, HIPAA, Constitutional AI constitutions, and prior safety rulebases, then generating 6,400 base prompts (4 LLMs × 8 principles × 4 harm levels × 50 queries) via few-shot prompting, deduplicating to 5,340 with MinHashLSH (Jaccard threshold 0.7), and augmenting every prompt with three jailbreak rewrites (indirect/chained questioning, synonym obfuscation with explicit substitution mappings, and role-play disguise from a curated persona list) to yield 21K candidates, of which 18K survive GPT-4 filtering and human review. Evaluation uses a three-way LLM-as-judge protocol (GPT-4o-mini, validated at 98% agreement on 200 manual samples) and a novel Safety Score that assigns graded credit—full credit for REFUSE on harmful prompts and ACCEPT on harmless ones, partial credit (0.5) for CAUTION on moderate-harm prompts, and zero for unsafe acceptances or over-refusals. For mitigation, a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct classifier is fine-tuned (lr 1e-5, 5 epochs, single H100) to identify the jailbreak strategy in a prompt, and its prediction is injected as a reminder conditioning signal to steer the target model toward safer behavior, yielding consistent Safety Score gains especially for weaker models like Claude-3.5-Haiku and Llama-3.1-8B.

## Results

On the 9K CARES safety test set, O4-mini achieves the highest Safety Score (SS) of 0.71, followed by DeepSeek-R1 and Medalpaca-13B at 0.70, while the weakest models (ClinicalCamel-70B, Mixtral-8x7B) score only 0.53. Accuracy and F1 range from 0.58–0.74 and 0.74–0.85 respectively across the 25 evaluated models. Under jailbreak prompting, all models exhibit significant Safety Score drops, with indirect and role-play variants being the most effective at bypassing refusals. The proposed jailbreak-aware classifier (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, 97.7% validation accuracy) yields the largest safety gains for weaker models such as Claude-3.5-Haiku and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct.

## Baselines & Comparisons

O4-mini leads all 25 models with SS 0.71, outperforming the next-best DeepSeek-R1 and Medalpaca-13B (both 0.70) by a narrow margin, while the weakest entries—ClinicalCamel-70B and Mixtral-8x7B—trail by 0.18 in SS. Notably, medically tuned models Medalpaca-13B (SS 0.70) and Meditron-70B (SS 0.67) outperform much larger general-purpose models such as Llama-3.1-70B (SS 0.56) and GPT-4o-mini (SS 0.56), suggesting domain-aligned safety tuning can rival or exceed scale. Among frontier closed-source models, GPT-4o-mini (SS 0.56) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (SS 0.61) underperform relative to smaller open-source Qwen3 variants (SS 0.62–0.63) and the 13B Medalpaca. Under jailbreak conditions, every model's Safety Score drops substantially, with indirect and role-play strategies causing the largest declines; obfuscation-based prompts are comparatively easier to handle. The jailbreak-aware classifier provides the most substantial gains for weaker models (Claude-3.5-Haiku, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.2-3B), while already-strong models like GPT-4o-mini and DeepSeek-V3 see only marginal improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARES extends the medical LLM safety evaluation line (MedSafetyBench, MEDIC, RWE-LLM) by systematically incorporating adversarial jailbreak-style prompt manipulations—indirect, obfuscated, and role-play—into a clinically grounded benchmark, a dimension absent from prior medical safety benchmarks. It also departs from the binary REFUSE/ACCEPT evaluation paradigm common in safety benchmarks (SafeBench, RealToxicityPrompts) by introducing a three-way ACCEPT/CAUTION/REFUSE protocol and a graded Safety Score that penalizes both unsafe acceptances and over-cautious false refusals. The mitigation strategy repurposes a lightweight fine-tuned classifier (Qwen2.5-7B) as a jailbreak detector whose output conditions the target model via reminder prompting, a simple but effective intervention not previously explored in the medical safety context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CARES fills a clear gap by being the first medical benchmark to jointly evaluate harmful content, adversarial robustness, and false-positive refusals across 18K prompts, making it a likely reference standard for clinical LLM safety testing. The finding that jailbreak-style rewriting not only obscures harmful intent but also causes models to over-refuse benign prompts (particularly at Level 0) highlights a previously underappreciated failure mode with real clinical consequences—patients with atypically phrased but safe queries may be denied helpful information. Key limitations include reliance on only three jailbreak strategies (no multi-turn or chain-of-thought attacks), synthetic prompt generation, and single-turn evaluation, leaving open whether findings generalize to interactive, in-the-wild clinical deployments.
