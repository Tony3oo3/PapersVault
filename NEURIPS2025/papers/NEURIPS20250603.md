## Problem

Designing new diffusion transformer architectures requires costly pretraining from scratch to evaluate whether a design choice (e.g., replacing attention with convolution) actually works, making systematic architectural exploration infeasible. The paper asks whether pretrained DiTs can serve as editable scaffolds so that new architectures are materialized and evaluated under small compute budgets (<2% of pretraining compute) rather than requiring full training runs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250603/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The grafting procedure takes a pretrained DiT (DiT-XL/2 for 256×256 class-conditional generation, or PixArt-Σ for 2048×2048 text-to-image generation) and a target replacement operator, and produces a modified model with the new architecture. For class-conditional generation, the grafted model takes a class label and noise as input and outputs a 256×256 image; for T2I, it takes a text prompt and noise and outputs a 2048×2048 image. The grafting itself requires only 8k samples for operator initialization and 10–25% of the original training data for finetuning, with each experiment completing in under 24 hours on 8×H100 GPUs.

## Method

Grafting is a two-stage procedure for editing a pretrained DiT's computational graph. Stage 1 (activation distillation) initializes each new operator by regressing its output to match the original operator's activations on 8k samples, using an operator-specific loss (L1 for MHA, L2 for MLP) chosen based on activation variance analysis. Stage 2 (lightweight finetuning) applies end-to-end training on 10–25% of the data to mitigate cumulative error from multiple simultaneous operator replacements. The paper uses a band-k locality metric on attention matrices to identify which layers are amenable to local-operator replacement, and finds that interleaved (every-other-layer) replacement at 50% ratio outperforms full replacement, yielding FID 2.38–2.64 vs. 2.27 baseline. Beyond operator swapping, grafting also enables architectural restructuring: converting every pair of sequential transformer blocks into parallel blocks reduces depth by 2× (28→14) while achieving FID 2.77, outperforming models trained from scratch at comparable depth.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (256×256, DiT-XL/2), grafted hybrid architectures achieve FID 2.38–2.64 versus the 2.27 baseline, using less than 2% of pretraining compute. On PixArt-Σ (2048×2048 text-to-image), grafting MHA with Hyena-X yields a 1.43× wall-clock speedup with only a 2% GenEval drop (47.78 vs. 49.75). In the depth-to-width case study, the grafted 14-layer model achieves FID 2.77, outperforming all other depth-14 DiTs and even deeper models trained from scratch (e.g., DiT-L/2 at depth 24, FID 3.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DiT-XL/2 baseline (FID 2.27), grafted hybrid designs (e.g., MLP r=6 at 50%: FID 2.38; SWA at 50%: FID 2.67) retain quality within 0.4 FID while reducing operator FLOPs by ~48–50%. In the depth-to-width comparison, the grafted 14-layer model (FID 2.77) clearly outperforms DiT-L/2 (depth 24, FID 3.73) and U-ViT-L (depth 21, FID 3.44) trained from scratch, and beats the best pruning baseline TinyDiT-D14 w/MKD (depth 14, FID 2.86) by 0.09 FID, though with more parameters (712M vs. 340M). Against BK-SDM (depth 14, FID 7.43), the grafted model is substantially better. On PixArt-Σ, the grafted model trades 1.97 GenEval points (47.78 vs. 49.75) for a 1.43× latency reduction (164.58 ms vs. 235.46 ms). On Qwen3-4B (LLM grafting), the grafted SWA model stays within 0.4 points on average benchmark score (68.6 vs. 69.0) while achieving 1.4× decode throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the knowledge distillation framework (Hinton et al., 2015) by repurposing activation regression as an operator initialization mechanism, and extends the line of work on linearizing pretrained models (LoLcats, Mamba-in-Llama, Transformers-to-SSMs) from single-operator replacement in LLMs to multi-operator replacement and architectural restructuring in diffusion transformers. Unlike prior diffusion architecture proposals (SANA, DiffuSSM, DiM, DIG) that require full pretraining to validate new operators, grafting materializes new designs by editing existing pretrained DiTs under <2% pretraining compute. The depth-to-width conversion case study departs from both pruning methods (BK-SDM, TinyDiT) and the FFN Fusion work on LLMs by demonstrating that sequential transformer blocks in a pretrained DiT can be rewired into parallel blocks while preserving generative quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Grafting provides a practical, low-cost pathway for architecture exploration that is currently bottlenecked by pretraining costs, potentially democratizing architectural research for smaller labs. The finding that full self-grafting (replacing all 28 MHA or MLP operators) recovers near-baseline FID with only 10% training data suggests that pretrained DiTs are more robust to operator-level perturbations than previously assumed, opening the door to more aggressive architectural edits. Key limitations include the dependence on a strong pretrained base model, the failure of 100% MHA replacement (indicating some layers require global attention), and the reliance on synthetic data quality for text-to-image grafting. Open questions include whether grafting extends to video DiTs, whether it can guide from-scratch training (e.g., identifying which layers to make local before pretraining), and whether the depth-to-width conversion generalizes beyond 2× reduction.
