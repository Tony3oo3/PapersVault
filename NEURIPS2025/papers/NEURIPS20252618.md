## Problem

Existing pose-free NeRF methods are restricted to a single modality, and the one prior multimodal attempt (Alignmif) uses independent hash grids per modality that fail to converge under ill-conditioned pose-free optimization. A unified framework that jointly reconstructs LiDAR point clouds and camera images while simultaneously estimating poses in large-scale autonomous driving scenes has been notably absent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252618/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes time-synchronized LiDAR point cloud sequences and front-facing camera image sequences, with vehicle poses perturbed by 20° rotation and 3 m translation noise; relative sensor-to-vehicle extrinsics are assumed known. It outputs a single unified neural field (shared multiresolution hash grid) that can render novel-view images (RGB) and novel-view LiDAR range/intensity/ray-drop maps, along with refined vehicle poses parameterized in SO(3)×R³ via Lie algebra updates. Only one front-facing camera is used per sequence, and 768 points are sampled per ray for both modalities.

## Method

MUP employs a single shared i-NGP hash grid and geo-MLP for both modalities, with a lightweight image-specific MLP to reduce modality conflicts. A multimodal-specific coarse-to-fine (MSC2F) strategy activates hash grid layers at different schedules per modality (α_LiDAR: 6–16, α_Camera: 12–16), balancing convergence speeds and preventing the faster-converging LiDAR from dominating the shared features. Pose optimization is both implicit (gradients from rendering loss with modality-specific learning-rate scheduling: LiDAR-weighted early, Camera-weighted later) and explicit via the Multimodal Geometric Optimizer (MMG), which computes an Image-enhanced Chamfer Distance (re-weighting point correspondences by photometric consistency) plus a Point-to-Image regularization term across a multi-frame graph. A consistency constraint further enforces color-depth coherence by back-projecting rendered depth maps onto adjacent frames for photometric error, particularly effective in textureless regions outside the LiDAR FoV.

## Results

On KITTI-360, MUP achieves CD 0.079, F-score 0.942, PSNR 23.46 dB, SSIM 0.759, and ATE 0.187 m, substantially outperforming all baselines. On NuScenes, MUP reaches CD 0.810, F-score 0.656, PSNR 20.83 dB, and ATE 0.176 m, again leading across both LiDAR and image metrics. Pose estimation is particularly dramatic: MUP's RPEt is 1.471 cm on KITTI-360 versus 25–36 cm for the next-best methods, and 4.058 cm on NuScenes versus 14–182 cm for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUP outperforms all five baselines on both datasets across nearly every metric. Against the registration-first Colored-ICP + Alignmif pipeline, MUP reduces CD from 0.492 to 0.079 (KITTI-360) and from 0.930 to 0.810 (NuScenes), while improving PSNR by 2.5 dB and 1.6 dB respectively. The pose-free image-only Nope-NeRF fails to produce LiDAR outputs and suffers severe pose errors (RPEt 83 cm on KITTI-360, 130 cm on NuScenes), confirming its unsuitability for large-scale driving scenes. The pose-free multimodal BA-Alignmif, which uses two independent hash grids, struggles to converge in the ill-conditioned pose-free setting, yielding the worst CD (0.641 on KITTI-360, 1.695 on NuScenes) and the largest ATE (4.266 m on NuScenes). MUP's unified single hash grid with modality-specific coarse-to-fine training and the MMG geometric optimizer are the key factors enabling its dominant pose accuracy (RPEt 1.471 cm vs. 25–182 cm for all baselines on KITTI-360).

## Relation to Prior Work

MUP extends the pose-free NeRF line (BARF, Nope-NeRF, GeoNLF) from single-modality to a unified LiDAR-camera formulation, and departs from the prior multimodal NeRF Alignmif by replacing its two independent hash grids with a single shared hash grid governed by modality-specific coarse-to-fine activation. The key conceptual shift is using continuous Neural LiDAR Fields to provide pixel-wise depth supervision for image reconstruction, rather than projecting discrete point clouds as sparse depth priors as in Nope-NeRF or Alignmif. The MMG module (image-enhanced Chamfer Distance plus point-to-image regularization) draws on classical registration ideas (ICP, CD) but repurposes them as differentiable pose-optimization losses within the NeRF training loop, complementing the implicit gradient-based pose refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly perform pose-free NVS for both LiDAR and camera in large-scale driving scenes with a single compact neural representation, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify as 'notably absent.' The finding that separate per-modality hash grids (Alignmif) fail to converge under ill-conditioned pose-free optimization while a unified grid with modality-specific C2F succeeds offers a practical design principle for future multimodal implicit representations. Key limitations include the inability to handle dynamic scenes and reliance on temporal frame-to-frame correlations, which constrains applicability to longer or more complex driving sequences. Open questions include whether the unified-grid approach generalizes to additional modalities (e.g., radar, semantics) and whether the method can be made real-time for closed-loop simulation.
