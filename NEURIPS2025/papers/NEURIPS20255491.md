## Problem

While numerical reward signals can rank candidate actions, they reduce guidance to scalar scores that overlook context-specific suggestions and avenues for exploration. Natural language feedback offers richer, more actionable guidance, but existing approaches suffer from two key limitations: self-refinement produces weak, unstructured critiques whose quality is bounded by the model's own capacity, and LLM agents struggle to effectively interpret and integrate verbal feedback into their decision-making, often persisting with suboptimal plans despite corrective guidance.

## Input / Output

At each time step, the actor generates M candidate actions conditioned on the interaction history (task instruction plus past actions and observations), and the critic evaluates each candidate against a fixed structured template covering three discrimination dimensions (Contribution, Feasibility, Efficiency) and a revision component (overall grade from Excellent to Very Poor plus a concrete suggested revision). The actor then synthesizes all M critiques to produce a single refined action, which is executed in the environment. Over a full episode, this yields a refined trajectory of critique-conditioned actions that replaces the original unguided trajectory, with the structured critique format ensuring the feedback is both interpretable and actionable at the step level.

## Method

CGI is a two-player framework with a specialized critic model (Llama-3-8B) and an actor model, trained in two stages. In Critique Generation, the critic is fine-tuned via SFT on step-level expert critiques produced by GPT-4o, learning to output structured discrimination (scoring each candidate on contribution, feasibility, and efficiency) paired with actionable revision suggestions. In Action Refinement, the actor undergoes iterative SFT: each iteration alternates between an exploration step (actor interacts with the environment under critic guidance, retaining only trajectories achieving reward 1 along with their critique-action pairs) and a learning step (fine-tuning on a mixture of correct trajectories, critique-action pairs, and general conversational data to prevent overfitting). A key design choice is fine-tuning from the base model each iteration rather than the previous iteration's checkpoint, preventing policy drift that would misalign the actor's learned critique-utilization behavior with its evolving action distribution. This iterative process progressively improves the actor's ability to follow critiques, with the revision ratio in early trajectory stages increasing across iterations, enabling sustained performance gains on long-horizon tasks where vanilla iterative SFT plateaus.

## Results

CGI (Llama-3-8B actor + fine-tuned 8B critic, 3 iterations) achieves an average score of 74.20 across WebShop (76.17), ScienceWorld (78.43), and TextCraft (68.00), outperforming GPT-4o (45.46) by 28.74 points and the base Llama-3-8B (12.66) by 61.54 points. The trained 8B critic model alone surpasses GPT-4o as a feedback provider by +29.16% in average actor performance (61.44 vs. 32.28). Compared to Iterative SFT (58.21), CGI gains an additional +15.99% through critique-guided action refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGI decisively outperforms all closed-source models: GPT-4o (45.46), GPT-3.5-Turbo (22.38), DeepSeek-Chat (16.93), and Claude-3 variants (2.11–14.09) in average score. Among open-source baselines, CGI surpasses Llama-3-70B-Instruct (19.85), AgentLM-70B (21.39), and Agent-FLAN (28.33) by large margins. Against iterative methods, CGI beats Reflexion (11.54) by 62.66 points and Iterative SFT (58.21) by 15.99 points, though Iterative SFT narrowly edges CGI on WebShop alone (78.21 vs. 76.17). In the critic comparison (Table 1), the trained 8B critic outperforms GPT-4o (61.44 vs. 32.28 average), DGAP (23.64), Explicit RM (15.87), and Self-Critique (10.19), demonstrating that a small fine-tuned model provides substantially richer and more actionable feedback than both numerical discriminators and general-purpose LLM judges.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGI extends the iterative SFT paradigm for agentic tasks (ReST meets ReAct) by introducing a dedicated critic model that generates structured natural language feedback, rather than relying solely on correct-trajectory filtering. It departs from the numerical-feedback line (reward models, DGAP discriminators, Best-of-N) by arguing that scalar scores lack the contextual richness needed for effective action revision, and from self-refinement methods (Reflexion, Self-Refine) by separating the critique role into a separately trained model to avoid the quality degradation of self-generated feedback. The data-mixing strategy for maintaining generalization follows AgentTuning, and the overall two-player actor-critic structure is positioned as a novel variation of inference-time sampling that leverages a trained critic rather than a general-purpose LLM-as-judge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that task-specific fine-tuning of a small critic (8B) can substantially outperform GPT-4o as a feedback provider, suggesting that domain-adapted critics are a cost-effective path to improving agent performance without relying on proprietary models. The finding that supervised fine-tuning paradoxically reduces a model's ability to incorporate external critiques (Finding 2) is practically important for anyone building fine-tuned agents, as it identifies a failure mode not previously well-characterized. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to environments without clean expert trajectories, whether the 4× computational overhead is acceptable at scale, and whether the two-player framework can be extended to multi-agent or continuous-action settings.
