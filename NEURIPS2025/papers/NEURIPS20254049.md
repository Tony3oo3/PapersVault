## Problem

Weak-to-strong generalization—where a student trained on imperfect teacher-generated labels surpasses the teacher—has been widely observed but its enabling mechanisms remain poorly understood. Prior theoretical work focused almost exclusively on ridgeless regression with frozen representations, leaving the interplay of teacher and student regularization, the role of model parameterization regimes, and the contribution of nonlinear feature learning largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The teacher is trained on n_t labeled Gaussian samples (x_i, y_i) from a linear or multi-index model, then generates synthetic labels for n_s fresh covariates; the student trains on these synthetic labels with its own regularization. All results are characterized in the high-dimensional proportional limit (d_x/n_s → γ_s, d_x/n_t → γ_t), producing closed-form asymptotic test error expressions in terms of Stieltjes transforms of the Marchenko-Pastur law, along with explicit inequalities delineating the (λ_t, λ_s, γ_s, γ_t) regions where the student outperforms the teacher.

## Method

The paper identifies three distinct mechanisms through random matrix theory analysis. First, in standard ridge regression, the student compensates for the teacher's under-regularization (λ_t < λ_t* = σ²_ε γ_t), with qualitatively different phase transitions depending on whether the student is under-parameterized (γ_s < 1, where any small λ_s suffices) or over-parameterized (γ_s > 1, where λ_s must lie in a non-trivial interval (λ̄_-, λ̄_+)). Second, in weighted ridge regression, a student whose regularization matrix Γ encodes a spiked structure aligned with the target direction (correlation ζ) gains an additional non-positive term −ζ²Δ_Γ in the error difference, enabling outperformance even when the teacher is optimally regularized. Third, in a nonlinear multi-index setting with one-step gradient descent on two-layer networks, the student learns the easy task-specific direction β_e from the teacher's imperfect labels while retaining its pre-trained alignment to the hard cross-domain direction β_h, since the teacher (initialized randomly) can only learn β_e (κ_σe = 1) but not β_h (κ_σh > 1). The proofs rely on the Marchenko-Pastur theorem, asymptotic freeness of independent Wishart matrices, the Hanson-Wright inequality, and the Sherman-Morrison formula for spiked matrix inverses.

## Results

The paper provides exact asymptotic characterizations of the test error difference L_s − L_t in the high-dimensional proportional limit (d_x/n_s → γ_s, d_x/n_t → γ_t). The headline theoretical result (Theorem 5) shows that the student outperforms the teacher if and only if the teacher is under-regularized (λ_t < σ²_ε γ_t), with the precise conditions on λ_s depending on the student's parameterization regime: for γ_s < 1 (under-parameterized), any 0 < λ_s < λ̄ suffices; for γ_s > 1 (over-parameterized), λ_s must lie in a non-trivial interval (λ̄_−, λ̄_+) and an additional inequality (5) on (γ_t, γ_s, λ_t, σ_ε) must hold. In the weighted ridge setting (Theorem 8), the student gains an additional non-positive term −ζ²Δ_Γ, enabling weak-to-strong generalization even when the teacher is optimally regularized, provided the feature-alignment parameter ζ is sufficiently large. Numerical validation (Figures 1–3) confirms that the theoretical contours match simulations closely for moderately large dimensions (d_x = 500, n_t = n_s = 2000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no head-to-head empirical comparisons against competing methods; the 'baselines' are prior theoretical results that the paper extends or refines. Compared to Medvedev et al. [2025], who showed student regularization is necessary but assumed the teacher is optimally trained, this paper explicitly characterizes the role of teacher regularization and shows that an over-regularized teacher (λ_t ≥ σ²_ε γ_t) can never be outperformed by the student via the ridge-regression mechanism. Compared to Ildiz et al. [2025], Dong et al. [2025], and Xue et al. [2025], which focus on ridgeless (λ = 0) regression with frozen features, this paper covers both under- and over-parameterized regimes with explicit ridge penalties and reveals qualitatively different phase transitions (Theorem 5) that are absent in the ridgeless setting. The nonlinear result (Theorem 11) extends Ba et al. [2022, Proposition 2] from random initialization to a spiked (pre-trained) initialization with teacher-generated labels, showing that a single gradient step suffices for the student to acquire the easy direction while retaining the hard pre-trained direction. The weighted ridge analysis (Theorem 8) goes beyond all prior work by showing that architectural/pre-training advantages (captured by the alignment parameter ζ) provide an independent mechanism for weak-to-strong generalization that operates even when the teacher is optimally regularized.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical study of weak-to-strong generalization initiated by Burns et al. (2024) and subsequent analyses (Ildiz et al. 2025, Dong et al. 2025, Xue et al. 2025, Medvedev et al. 2025), which largely focused on ridgeless regression with frozen representations. It departs from this line by (1) analyzing standard and weighted ridge regression to explicitly characterize the interplay of teacher and student regularization, (2) studying both under- and over-parameterized regimes to reveal qualitatively different phase transitions, and (3) moving beyond the linearized regime to a nonlinear multi-index setting with one-step gradient descent, building directly on Ba et al. (2022) for the feature-learning analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified theoretical account of three distinct mechanisms enabling weak-to-strong generalization—compensating under-regularization, leveraging task-aligned regularization structure, and combining teacher-taught easy features with pretrained hard features—offering a conceptual framework that goes beyond the empirical observations of Burns et al. The superalignment implications are significant: if weak models (as proxies for humans) can reliably steer stronger models, this supports the feasibility of human oversight of superhuman systems. Key limitations include the reliance on Gaussian features, one-step gradient descent, and specific spiked-covariance structures; the universality claim is stated but left as future work, and it remains open whether these mechanisms persist in deeper, more realistic architectures or with iterative (rather than single-step) training.
