## Problem

Integrating 3D human motion into foundation language models via instruction tuning causes catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained world knowledge, as the modality gap drives the model to overfit on newly introduced motion tokens. Additionally, prior VQ-VAE-based motion tokenizers encode only temporal structure, ignoring the spatial layout of poses and limiting representation quality for single-frame tasks like pose estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253268/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes task instructions and text prompts (encoded by CLIP for gating), optional RGB images (processed by CLIP ViT-L/14 with a LLaVA projection layer), or 8-frame video clips, and produces discrete motion or pose tokens that are decoded into 3D motion sequences or SMPL pose parameters, or free-text responses for video understanding and dialogue. The human body is partitioned into 5 parts (torso and four limbs), each quantized against its own codebook of 512 vectors with embedding dimension 512; motion sequences additionally undergo temporal convolution with compression ratio 4. These part-specific tokens are appended to the foundation model's vocabulary, preserving original text token ordering.

## Method

HMVLM applies a Mixture-of-Experts LoRA strategy where five LoRA expert pairs (rank 8, applied to all linear modules) are dynamically combined via a 2-layer MLP gating network that reads CLIP-encoded instruction and prompt features. A novel zero expert with non-trainable, zero-initialized matrices allows the model to fall back to the original pretrained weights for motion-unrelated tasks; a dedicated gating loss (L_gat) trained on LMSYS-Chat-1M conversation data explicitly encourages the gate to select the zero expert for general dialogue, preventing catastrophic forgetting. For pose and motion representation, the paper replaces whole-body VQ-VAE with a body-part-aware spatial transformer that uses learnable part parameters and attention masks to pool joint features per body part, then quantizes each part independently against its own codebook, followed by temporal convolution for multi-frame motion. The full model is trained via joint instruction tuning with a combined next-token prediction loss and gating loss, supporting text-to-motion generation, monocular pose estimation, and motion video understanding simultaneously.

## Results

On HumanML3D text-to-motion, HMVLM (single-task) achieves R-precision Top-1 of 0.502, FID of 0.123, and MM-D of 3.039, competitive with the SOTA task-specific MoMask (0.521, 0.045, 2.958). In the multi-task setting, HMVLM retains R-precision Top-1 of 0.463 and FID of 0.156 while simultaneously supporting pose estimation and video understanding. The headline knowledge-preservation result shows only a 3.34% MT-Bench degradation (7.79→7.53) after T2M instruction tuning on Gemma-2, versus a catastrophic 87.16% collapse (7.79→1.00) for MotionAgent under the same foundation model. For pose estimation on Human3.6M, HMVLM (single-task) achieves 92.8 MPJPE and 55.3 PA-MPJPE, outperforming ChatPose (126.0 / 82.4) but trailing the dedicated HMR2.0 (50.0 / 33.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the task-specific SOTA MoMask, HMVLM (single-task) is slightly behind on R-precision (0.502 vs 0.521 Top-1) and FID (0.123 vs 0.045), but competitive on MM-D (3.039 vs 2.958) and diversity. Among foundation-model-based T2M methods, HMVLM (single-task) outperforms MotionGPT-2 on FID (0.123 vs 0.191) and MM-D (3.039 vs 3.080), and substantially outperforms MotionAgent on FID (0.123 vs 0.491) and MotionGPT (Zhang) on all metrics (e.g., R-precision 0.502 vs 0.364, FID 0.123 vs 0.805). The critical differentiator is knowledge preservation: HMVLM on Gemma-2 retains 96.7% of its MT-Bench score after T2M tuning (7.53/7.79), while MotionAgent collapses to 1.00/7.79 and MotionGPT (Llama-2) drops 22.7% (2.73→2.11). For pose estimation, HMVLM (single-task) beats ChatPose by 24.2 mm MPJPE and 27.1 mm PA-MPJPE on Human3.6M, but remains well behind the dedicated HMR2.0 (92.8 vs 50.0 MPJPE), reflecting the inherent trade-off of a unified multimodal framework versus a task-specific model. On KIT-ML, the same pattern holds: HMVLM (single-task) achieves 0.423 R-precision Top-1 and 0.306 FID, outperforming all foundation-model-based baselines but trailing MoMask (0.433 / 0.204).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoE LoRA paradigm introduced by Dou et al. (LoRAMoE, ACL 2024) by repurposing it for multimodal, multitask instruction tuning of a human motion foundation model, and introduces a non-trainable zero expert as a knowledge-preservation mechanism absent from prior MoE LoRA work. For motion tokenization, it departs from the standard VQ-VAE temporal-only pipeline used by T2M-GPT and MotionGPT by adopting a spatial Transformer architecture (from Hu et al.'s motion retargeting work) to produce body-part-specific discrete codes, inspired by patch-based image tokenization. The unified instruction-tuning framework for T2M, pose estimation, and video understanding builds on the MotionGPT/MotionAgent line of work but explicitly addresses the catastrophic forgetting problem that those prior systems left unexamined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practically important and previously underexplored failure mode: naive multimodal instruction tuning on foundation models can catastrophically destroy general linguistic capability (MotionAgent drops from 7.79 to 1.00 on MT-Bench), and the zero-expert gating mechanism offers a lightweight, model-agnostic remedy. However, the multi-task setting still shows meaningful performance degradation relative to single-task fine-tuning on both T2M and pose estimation, suggesting the framework has not fully resolved the multitask trade-off. The authors acknowledge that modality connections remain learned in an independent pairwise manner and that domain discrepancies across datasets limit any-to-any generation, leaving open the question of whether a truly holistic multimodal integration is achievable within this architecture.
