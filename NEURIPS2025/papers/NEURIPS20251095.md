## Problem

Existing data-driven ocean forecasting models are limited to daily temporal resolution and lack mechanisms to adaptively handle the diverse temporal dynamics of ocean variables at sub-daily intervals, where surface processes (e.g., diurnal SST cycles) evolve on fundamentally different timescales than deep-ocean currents. While traditional numerical OGCMs can produce sub-daily eddy-resolving forecasts, they are computationally prohibitive at 1/12° global resolution. No prior data-driven system has achieved six-hourly predictions at eddy-resolving spatial scale with vertical coverage extending to 1500 m.

## Input / Output

The model consumes four consecutive 6-hour ocean state fields (81 channels: T, S, U, V at 20 depth levels from 0–1500 m, plus SSH) on a 2160×4320 global grid at 1/12° resolution, augmented by spatiotemporal context features (diurnal/seasonal encodings, coordinates, bathymetry) that modulate the encoder. It produces the next 6-hour state with identical structure, and during inference operates autoregressively by feeding its own output back as input. Notably, FuXi-Ocean requires only oceanic variables as input—no atmospheric forcing (U10, V10, SST) is needed, unlike competing models such as XiHe and Langya.

## Method

FuXi-Ocean employs a shared convolutional encoder whose weights are modulated by spatiotemporal context features (via element-wise multiplication), followed by stacked Swin-Transformer attention blocks with adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN) and a shared transposed-convolutional decoder with skip connections. The core innovation is the Mixture-of-Time (MoT) module, which maintains a per-channel reliability matrix V ∈ ℝ^(C×4) tracking the spatially-averaged MAE of predictions generated from each of the four temporal windows; at inference, for each channel it selects the top-K (K=1 in practice) most reliable window and uses its prediction, thereby adaptively matching temporal context to each variable's dynamics without increasing model parameters. Training uses a two-stage strategy (single-step pre-training followed by multi-step fine-tuning) with a latitude-weighted Charbonnier loss to correct for grid-cell area distortion, and achieves state-of-the-art performance with only 9 years of HYCOM reanalysis data (2006–2015) on 4×H100 GPUs in ~81 hours.

## Results

FuXi-Ocean achieves 6-hourly SST RMSE below 0.3°C in tropical and subtropical regions, with errors remaining within operational tolerance even at 10-day lead times. In the IV-TT 2022 observational evaluation, FuXi-Ocean's SST RMSE is lower than HYCOM, BLK, and FOAM across all forecast lead times, with significantly smaller cumulative error growth. The Mixture-of-Time module contributes substantially: removing it increases RMSE by nearly 40% for salinity, temperature, and SSH. The model achieves these results using only ~8.5 years of training data and 4 NVIDIA H100 GPUs (~81 hours), with no atmospheric forcing inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three operational numerical systems (HYCOM, BLK, FOAM) evaluated via the GODAE IV-TT Class 4 framework on 2022 SST observations, FuXi-Ocean achieves lower RMSE at every forecast lead time and exhibits markedly slower error growth, despite being trained only through 2014 and using purely oceanic variables as input. For ocean current U/V components, the paper compares against WenHai (Cui et al., 2025) and GLORY12 (Jean-Michel et al., 2021) using buoy data from CMEMS; FuXi-Ocean shows competitive performance, though the authors note the comparison is not entirely fair due to inconsistent testing years. The ablation study confirms that the MoT module is critical: removing it degrades salinity, temperature, and SSH forecasts by up to ~40% RMSE, while ocean current U/V show less sensitivity, suggesting currents are more driven by recent forcing than by their own history. Overall, FuXi-Ocean outperforms all baselines on the primary SST metric and matches or approaches state-of-the-art deep learning models on subsurface variables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive deep learning ocean forecasting line initiated by WenHai (Cui et al., 2025) and the Fuxi weather system (Chen et al., 2023), moving from daily to 6-hourly temporal resolution and from ~700 m to 1500 m depth coverage. Architecturally, it builds on the Swin-Transformer attention blocks and AdaLN modulation used in XiHe (Wang et al., 2024) and Langya (Yang et al., 2024), but departs from them by eliminating atmospheric forcing inputs and introducing the Mixture-of-Time (MoT) module, which performs channel-wise adaptive selection over multiple temporal windows based on learned per-variable reliability. The latitude-weighted Charbonnier loss and two-stage (single-step pre-training → multi-step fine-tuning) training strategy follow established practices from the Fuxi and WenHai pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FuXi-Ocean demonstrates that sub-daily, eddy-resolving, deep-ocean forecasting is feasible with a data-driven model trained on only ~9 years of reanalysis data and no atmospheric inputs, outperforming operational numerical systems (HYCOM, BLK, FOAM) on SST in the IV-TT framework. The MoT module offers a general, lightweight mechanism for handling multi-timescale variables that could transfer to other spatiotemporal forecasting domains. Key open questions include: whether the reanalysis-training bias limits real-world generalization beyond SST, whether the approach extends to abyssal depths or seasonal/interannual timescales, and whether physical conservation constraints can further stabilize long-horizon autoregressive rollouts. The absence of error bars and the SST-only observational evaluation leave the full vertical and variable coverage of the claimed advantage partially unverified.
