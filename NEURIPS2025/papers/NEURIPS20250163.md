## Problem

Transformer-based multi-object tracking methods propagate track queries across frames, but unconditional feature updates at every frame cause noisy observations to accumulate, degrading identity representations and leading to frequent identity switches under occlusion, appearance changes, or detection failures. The paper addresses this by explicitly decoupling dynamic appearance modeling from static identity preservation within the decoder.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250163/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes video frames at 1440×800 resolution, extracting a feature map via a DINO detector (ResNet-50 backbone + transformer encoder) and selecting the top-300 detection candidates as anchor boxes with associated query features. Track queries are propagated from the previous frame, and the decoder outputs updated bounding boxes, confidence scores, and sampling offsets (256 sampling points) for each object. Active tracks are retained when confidence exceeds α=0.6, and tracks lost for more than 60 consecutive frames are terminated.

## Method

The core architecture is a dual-path temporal decoder with L=6 stacked layers, each containing two parallel branches: an appearance-adaptive decoder that refines query features via deformable attention with newly predicted sampling offsets, and an identity-preserving decoder that freezes track query features and reuses historical sampling offsets from the previous frame to maintain temporal consistency. Outputs from both branches are fused via linear projections and an FFN to produce the next layer's query features and bounding box predictions. A confidence-guided update suppression strategy (threshold β=0.4) prevents feature updates for low-confidence tracks, retaining previously reliable representations to suppress identity drift. Training is end-to-end with bipartite Hungarian matching and a weighted sum of classification, bounding box regression, and generalized IoU losses, using a two-stage protocol where the detector is trained first and then only the dual-path decoder is optimized with the backbone frozen.

## Results

On DanceTrack, the proposed method achieves a HOTA of 74.1 (without validation data) and 76.2 (with validation data), setting new state-of-the-art results across all metrics including DetA (83.9), AssA (65.6), MOTA (92.5), and IDF1 (78.6). On SportsMOT, it reaches 73.9 HOTA with 66.6 AssA and 78.7 IDF1, surpassing the previous best (MOTIP) by 1.3 HOTA. On MOT17, the method achieves the best AssA (62.5) and IDF1 (75.1) among all end-to-end approaches, though its MOTA (73.8) trails heuristic-based pipelines like ColTrack (78.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor is ColTrack [18], which the proposed method surpasses by 1.5 HOTA (74.1 vs. 72.6), 3.3 AssA (65.6 vs. 62.3), and 4.6 IDF1 (78.6 vs. 74.0) on DanceTrack, and by 2.4 HOTA (73.9 vs. 71.5) and 4.1 IDF1 (78.7 vs. 74.6) on SportsMOT. MOTIP [11], the previous SOTA on SportsMOT, is outperformed by 1.3 HOTA and 5.5 IDF1. Among tracking-by-detection methods, DiffMOT [20] leads on MOTA (92.8 vs. 92.5 on DanceTrack) but trails significantly on association metrics (IDF1 63.0 vs. 78.6), confirming the paper's claim that detection-centric pipelines underperform on identity consistency. On MOT17, the method achieves the best AssA (62.5) and IDF1 (75.1) among end-to-end trackers, beating MOTRv2 [40] by 1.9 AssA and MOTIP [11] by 5.5 AssA, though it lags behind heuristic pipelines like DeconfuseTrack [16] (HOTA 64.9) and Deep OC-SORT [21] (HOTA 64.9) on overall HOTA due to lower detection accuracy. The method also outperforms all baselines under matched detector settings (Table 6), gaining 3.4 HOTA over MOTRv2 with YOLOX, 4.2 over MOTIP with Deformable DETR, and 7.2 over ColTrack with DINO, demonstrating detector-agnostic robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the end-to-end transformer MOT paradigm established by MOTR and TrackFormer, which propagate track queries across frames within a DETR-style decoder. Its key departure is the dual-path temporal decoder that explicitly decouples appearance adaptation (dynamic query refinement via deformable attention) from identity preservation (frozen track queries with reused historical sampling offsets), a separation absent in prior methods like ColTrack, MOTIP, and MeMOTR that update all queries uniformly. The deformable attention mechanism is inherited from Deformable DETR, and the bipartite matching training objective follows DETR. The confidence-guided update suppression further distinguishes this work by selectively freezing low-confidence track states, addressing a failure mode (unconditional query updates causing identity drift) that prior transformer-based trackers do not explicitly handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly separating dynamic appearance modeling from static identity cues in the decoder is a principled and effective strategy for reducing ID switches, with the dual-path design and confidence suppression together yielding consistent gains across challenging benchmarks (DanceTrack, SportsMOT) where similar appearances and occlusions are prevalent. A notable limitation is that on MOT17, the method's detection accuracy lags behind heuristic-augmented pipelines, suggesting the approach trades some recall for association quality. Open questions include how the dual-path mechanism scales to 3D tracking or extremely dense scenes, and whether the fixed reuse of historical sampling offsets remains beneficial under large appearance changes (e.g., clothing changes) rather than the gradual variations seen in the tested benchmarks.
