## Problem

Human cognition is characterized by abrupt, spontaneous topic shifts driven by associative or emotional cues, yet self-attention-based LLMs follow structured statistical patterns and lack such spontaneity. No prior work has formally characterized when and why attention models preserve or spontaneously change topics, nor has it drawn systematic comparisons to the dynamics of spontaneous human thought. This paper fills that gap by establishing theoretical conditions for topic changes in self-attention and empirically confirming that LLMs behave oppositely to humans: longer contexts and greater topic ambiguity do not increase spontaneous topic shifts.

## Input / Output

Theoretically, the model takes a token sequence X belonging to a topic (defined as a set of Token Priority Graphs) and outputs a predicted next token, which is classified into one of three categories: topic continuity, ambiguous sequence, or topic change. Empirically, the input is the first T words (T ranging from 10 to 150, or up to 1210 for extended experiments) of an arXiv paper's abstract or introduction, augmented via RAG with the top-3 most relevant 800-token excerpts from paper A alone or from papers A and B combined. The output is a 1000-token text continuation generated at temperature 0, and topic continuity is measured as the cosine similarity between the single-topic and mixed-topic continuations.

## Method

The theoretical analysis builds on a single-layer self-attention model with log-loss trained by gradient descent, where topics are formalized as sets of Token Priority Graphs (TPGs) whose strongly-connected components encode token priority hierarchies. The model's convergence to a Graph-SVM solution (inherited from Li et al.) enables three key results: (1) a mixed-topic model preserves the attention priority ordering of the input's original topic; (2) a topic change can occur only if a lower-priority token appears more frequently than every higher-priority token in the input; and (3) longer inputs and greater topic overlap (larger highest-priority SCC) monotonically decrease the probability of a topic change. Empirically, the authors validate these predictions on four frontier LLMs (GPT-4o, Llama-3.3, Claude-3.7, DeepSeek-V3) using 100 unseen arXiv papers from March 2025, showing that cosine similarity between single-topic and mixed-topic continuations increases with input length and does not decrease with topic ambiguity—confirming the theoretical divergence from human spontaneous thought.

## Results

In single-layer self-attention simulations, the proportion of topic changes drops from 0.54% at T=8 to 0.00% at T=512, while topic continuity rises from 98.50% to 100.00%, confirming Theorem 4's prediction that longer inputs suppress spontaneous topic shifts. Across 85,000 test sequences, 99.98% satisfy Theorem 3's necessary condition (a lower-priority token must outnumber all higher-priority tokens for a topic change to occur). In frontier LLM experiments (GPT-4o, Llama-3.3, Claude-3.7, DeepSeek-V3), cosine similarity between single-topic and mixed-topic continuations increases with input length (from 10 to 150 words) and does not decrease with increasing topic ambiguity, mirroring the theoretical prediction and contrasting with human cognition where longer discussions and greater ambiguity promote spontaneous thought.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against traditional ML baselines; instead, its central comparison is between a single-topic self-attention model (W_a) and a mixed-topic model (W_ab) on the same input sequences, as well as across four frontier LLMs (GPT-4o, Llama-3.3, Claude-3.7, DeepSeek-V3) evaluated via RAG-augmented generation. In the simulation setting, the mixed-topic model preserves the single-topic model's attention priority ordering (Theorem 2), with the attention priority similarity converging to 1 relative to W_a but not to W_b after 8,000 gradient descent iterations. In the frontier LLM experiments, all four models show increasing cosine similarity between single-topic and mixed-topic continuations as input length grows from 10 to 150 words, with no model exhibiting a decrease in similarity as topic ambiguity increases. The key qualitative finding is that LLM behavior is the inverse of human cognition: in humans, longer conversations and greater inter-topic connectivity increase spontaneous topic shifts, whereas in all tested LLMs these factors stabilize topic continuity. The paper positions this as a fundamental cognitive discrepancy rather than a performance gap to be closed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-layer self-attention framework and Token Priority Graph (TPG) formalism of Li et al. [25] (AISTATS 2024) and the max-margin token selection architecture of Tarzanagh et al. [49] (NeurIPS 2023) by introducing a graph-based definition of 'topic' as a set of TPGs and analyzing when a model trained on mixed-topic data will spontaneously shift away from the input topic. While prior work on transformers as SVMs established convergence properties and topic-structure learning in attention, no prior study formally characterized the dynamics of spontaneous topic changes or drew explicit comparisons to spontaneous human thought. The empirical extension to four frontier LLMs via RAG-based prompting is a novel application of the theoretical framework to modern architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal characterization of when and why self-attention models spontaneously change topics, revealing a fundamental cognitive divergence: unlike humans, where longer conversations and greater topic interconnectivity increase spontaneous thought shifts, LLMs show the opposite behavior. This has implications for understanding the limits of LLMs in mimicking natural conversational dynamics and for designing systems that need to emulate human-like topic transitions. Key limitations include reliance on a single-layer model for theory (though empirical results on deep LLMs support the findings), use of cosine similarity as a proxy for topic continuity rather than direct measurement, and the narrow experimental scope (100 arXiv papers, abstract-level inputs). Open questions include whether these dynamics persist in longer-context settings, with different loss functions, or in multi-turn conversational settings where human spontaneous thought is most prominent.
