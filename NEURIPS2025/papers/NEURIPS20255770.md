## Problem

RL fine-tuning of LLMs (e.g., GRPO) is computationally expensive, yet prior work has focused almost exclusively on algorithmic improvements while largely overlooking data efficiency. The paper addresses two underexplored dimensions: (1) not all training questions contribute equally to learning progress, and (2) generating fresh rollouts every step is wasteful when recent rollouts could be reused.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255770/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of 8K–10K training questions, a policy model (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B/3B/7B), and a small reference set of K=256 questions per selection step. It produces a fine-tuned policy, per-question adaptive difficulty scores (estimated via a frozen Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct backbone with a trained 3-layer MLP adapter and Platt-scaling calibration head), and a selected batch of B=512 questions weighted toward moderate difficulty. A FIFO replay buffer of capacity C=256/512 stores rollouts, advantages, and old-policy probabilities for reuse.

## Method

The method combines two complementary techniques. First, Difficulty-targeted Online Data Selection (DOTS) estimates each question's adaptive difficulty (average failure rate under the current policy) using an attention-based prediction framework: only K=256 reference questions receive full rollouts, and the remaining questions' difficulties are predicted via softmax-weighted attention over reference embeddings, followed by Platt-scaling calibration. Questions with predicted difficulty closest to 0.5 are prioritized, which Theorem 1 justifies by showing the expected squared gradient norm is maximized at 50% success rate. Second, Rollout Replay (RR) generates fresh rollouts for only a fraction δ=0.5 of the batch and fills the rest from a FIFO buffer of recent rollouts, using a modified GRPO loss with importance sampling ratios relative to the behavior policy that collected each stored rollout. Together, DOTS reduces the number of training steps needed to match GRPO's final performance, while RR cuts per-step rollout cost, yielding 23–62% total training time savings across six LLM-dataset combinations.

## Results

Across six LLM-dataset combinations, DOTS+RR reduces total RL fine-tuning time by 23.30% to 61.65% (average 40.7%) while matching the final performance of original GRPO after 60 training steps. DOTS alone reduces the number of training steps needed by 13.33% to 56.67%, while RR reduces per-step wall-clock time by 11%–13%. The attention-based adaptive difficulty prediction framework achieves Pearson correlation >0.70 with ground-truth difficulty across all settings, and DOTS selects 25.4% more effective (non-degenerate) questions per step than uniform sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is original GRPO with uniform batch sampling. DOTS+RR wins on every reported metric across all six LLM-dataset combinations: it reaches the same final accuracy (averaged over GSM8K, MATH500, Minerva Math, and Olympiad-Bench) with 13.33%–56.67% fewer training steps, and each step is 11%–13% faster due to rollout replay. In wall-clock terms, the combined method saves 23.30%–61.65% total training time (mean 40.7%), with the largest gain on Qwen2.5-3B + DeepMath (61.65%). Throughout training, DOTS+RR maintains higher accuracy than GRPO at nearly every step (Fig. 3), indicating faster convergence rather than merely a time trade-off. The paper also compares DOTS against an external-difficulty-label curriculum baseline (GPT-4o-mini annotations with a staged easy→hard schedule) and shows DOTS consistently outperforms it on both Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Qwen2.5-3B, while requiring no external labeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) as its base algorithm, modifying its objective with importance sampling to support off-policy rollout reuse. The DOTS component draws on curriculum learning in RL and online data selection literature, but introduces the notion of *adaptive* difficulty that co-evolves with the policy, estimated via a lightweight attention-based prediction framework over a small reference set. The Rollout Replay mechanism adapts experience replay from traditional RL to the LLM RL fine-tuning setting, a direction the authors note has not been explored in prior LLM RL work. The paper positions itself against a landscape of algorithmic improvements to GRPO (DAPO, Dr. GRPO) that have largely overlooked data efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 23–62% reduction in total RL fine-tuning time (averaging ~41%) is practically significant for practitioners who find LLM RL training prohibitively expensive, and the techniques are simple enough to adopt with minimal engineering overhead. The adaptive difficulty framework's ability to track policy evolution with ρ > 0.7 correlation using only 256 reference rollouts per selection step is a useful inductive bias for future data-centric RL work. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-verifiable or multi-component reward settings, whether prioritized replay strategies would yield further gains, and how the method scales to much larger model sizes or longer training horizons beyond the 60-step regime tested here.
