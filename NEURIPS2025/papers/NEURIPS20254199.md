## Problem

The number of linear regions is the standard measure of expressive power for ReLU networks, yet the computational complexity of actually counting them remained largely unexplored. Compounding this gap, the literature uses at least six non-equivalent definitions of 'linear region,' leading to inconsistencies and minor errors in prior work. This paper systematically clarifies the definitional landscape and establishes the first comprehensive hardness results for region counting.

## Input / Output

The input is a ReLU network specified by its weight matrices and bias vectors, with all entries encoded as integers or rationals in binary (logarithmic space per entry). The paper studies both a decision version (does the network have more than K regions?) and a function version (compute the exact count), for each of six definitions ranging from activation regions to affine regions. For the one-hidden-layer case, the network has a single weight matrix A^(1) ∈ R^{n×n} and output row A^(2) = (1,…,1); for deeper networks, the number of hidden layers L is a fixed constant ≥ 2.

## Method

The paper's core technique is a family of reductions from classical hard problems. For one-hidden-layer networks, #P-hardness of exact counting is shown by reducing from counting cells of a hyperplane arrangement (itself #P-complete via Linial's reduction from acyclic orientations), constructing a network whose regions correspond exactly to arrangement cells. For networks with two or more hidden layers, NP-hardness of the decision problem follows from a SAT reduction that builds a network with nonzero regions confined to ε-hypercubes around satisfying (0-1) assignments, so the network is identically zero iff the formula is unsatisfiable. Strong inapproximability (no polynomial-factor approximation for L ≥ 3, and 2^{-O(n^{1-ε})} for L = 2) is obtained by amplifying the SAT construction via tensor-product-style copies. On the positive side, the paper shows FPSPACE containment for three definitions by enumerating activation patterns or affine coefficient vectors in polynomial space, improving on prior EXPTIME bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes that counting linear regions in ReLU networks is computationally intractable across all six definitions studied. For one hidden layer, LINEAR REGION COUNTING is #P-hard (Theorem 4.2), while the decision problem 1-REGION-DECISION remains in P (Theorem 4.1). For networks with L ≥ 2 hidden layers, K-REGION-DECISION is NP-hard for Definitions 3–6 (Theorem 5.2), and approximating the region count is NP-hard within a factor of (2ⁿ+1)⁻¹ for L ≥ 3 (Theorem 5.7) and within 2^{−O(n^{1−ε})} for L = 2 (Theorem 5.8). On the positive side, LINEAR REGION COUNTING is shown to be in FPSPACE for Definitions 1, 2, and 6 (Theorem 6.1), improving upon Wang's (2022) EXPTIME bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The main prior work it improves upon is Wang (2022, IJCAI), who showed NP-hardness of K-REGION-DECISION only for networks of depth Θ(log n). The present paper strengthens this to hardness for any constant number of hidden layers L ≥ 2 (Theorem 5.2), and further proves inapproximability results (Theorems 5.7–5.8) that Wang did not address. The #P-hardness for one-hidden-layer networks (Theorem 4.2) is derived from Linial's (1986) #P-completeness of counting cells in hyperplane arrangements, and the reduction is tight enough to imply that a polynomial-factor approximation algorithm would resolve a long-standing open question in that domain. The paper also corrects an error in Wang (2022), whose claimed EXPTIME algorithm actually applies to Definition 4 (open connected regions) rather than Definition 5 (closed connected regions), and shows that no finite algorithm is known for Definition 5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Wang's (2022) initial NP-hardness result for region counting from logarithmically deep networks to any constant number of hidden layers ≥ 2, and adds #P-hardness for the one-hidden-layer case via a reduction from Linial's (1986) #P-completeness of counting hyperplane arrangement cells. It also systematically identifies six non-equivalent definitions of 'linear region' scattered across the literature (Pascanu et al. 2014, Montúfar et al. 2014, Serra et al. 2018, Hanin and Rolnick 2019, etc.), clarifying which prior bounds and algorithms apply to which notion. The inapproximability results build on the SAT-reduction machinery from Lemma 5.3, which itself draws on Froese et al. (2025b) for the one-hidden-layer no-bias special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that counting linear regions is intractable in essentially all practical settings: #P-hard even for a single hidden layer, NP-hard for two layers even at K=1, and inapproximable within exponential factors for three or more layers. This settles a question the authors note was 'surprisingly' unaddressed despite the centrality of region counts as an expressivity measure. Key open problems remain: whether LINEAR REGION COUNTING is in #P for Definitions 5 and 6, whether EXPTIME algorithms exist for Definitions 3 and 5, and whether hardness extends to networks trained by gradient descent (which empirically have fewer regions). The six-definition taxonomy will likely reduce confusion in future work on region bounds and verification.
