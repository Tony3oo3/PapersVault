## Problem

Regression-based physics pose estimators infer joint torques as unconstrained auxiliary predictions, producing kinetically implausible results because the underconstrained problem of estimating kinetics from monocular video (without force sensors) admits many solutions. Existing methods either rely on external physics optimizers that are non-differentiable or too slow for real-time inference, or regress torques directly without enforcing biomechanical constraints, yielding torques that may violate physiological limits.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16-frame monocular video sequences, using initial kinematic estimates from a separate pose estimator (CLIFF) and root trajectory predictions as starting points. Beyond standard 3D joint positions and SMPL mesh, it outputs joint torques computed via two independent pathways (Lagrange's equations and muscle torque generators), per-DoF muscle activation signals in [0,1], detailed per-segment anthropometrics (mass, inertia tensor, center of mass), and ground reaction forces with center-of-pressure locations. The multi-hypothesis torque output is the distinguishing structural feature: two torque estimates are produced simultaneously and constrained to agree.

## Method

MusclePose uses a transformer encoder to produce latent motion features that feed five customizable modules predicting anthropometrics, kinematics, external forces, and two independent torque estimates. The key mechanism is a multi-hypothesis constraint: joint torques are computed analytically via Lagrange's equations (inverse dynamics on a rigid 18-segment, 47-DoF multibody model) and simultaneously via parametric muscle torque generators (MTGs) that model active torque through torque-velocity and torque-angle curves plus passive impedance via a double-exponential function. A torque loss enforces agreement between the two pathways, injecting biomechanical plausibility without an external physics engine. Anthropometrics are predicted as residuals added to literature values (Dumas et al.) scaled by predicted SMPL shape, rather than derived from mesh geometry. Training uses a two-phase curriculum (20 epochs on ground-truth inputs, 5 on model predictions) with AdamW, and the entire pipeline is differentiable and end-to-end trainable.

## Results

On H36M (feet-ground contact sequences), MusclePose achieves the best positional accuracy among all physics-based pose estimators with MJE 48.4 mm and PJE 33.5 mm, outperforming the closest regression-based PHPE baseline PhysPT (MJE 50.6, PJE 35.5) and optimization-based methods SimPoE (MJE 56.7) and DiffPhy (MJE 81.7). On 3DPWoc, MusclePose reports PJE 27.6 mm, slightly behind PhysPT (25.9) and the kinematic CLIFF baseline (24.0). In kinetic biofidelity, MusclePose produces substantially lower residual forces (0.08/W on H36M-Walk vs. 0.22/W without anthropometrics) and median vertical GRF closer to bodyweight (0.98/W vs. 0.74/W without anthropometrics), with joint torques more closely matching published biomechanics reference values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MusclePose outperforms all four other PHPE baselines on H36M: it beats PhysPT by 2.2 mm MJE (48.4 vs. 50.6) and 2.0 mm PJE (33.5 vs. 35.5), D&D by 4.1 mm MJE, SimPoE by 8.3 mm MJE, and DiffPhy by 33.3 mm MJE. On 3DPWoc, MusclePose (PJE 27.6) is 1.7 mm worse than PhysPT (25.9) and 3.6 mm worse than the kinematic CLIFF baseline (24.0), which the authors attribute to the moving-camera setting making root DoF estimation harder. Against purely kinematic methods, MusclePose is 1.9 mm worse than CLIFF on H36M MJE but still competitive, while WHAM (PJE 22.7) and CoMotion (PJE 30.7) on 3DPWoc bracket MusclePose's 27.6. In kinetic plausibility (Table 4), MusclePose achieves lower residual forces (0.1/W vs. 0.4/W for PhysPT on H36M), lower torque variation (2.5 vs. 5.3 N·m/frame), and median GRF closer to 1.0× bodyweight (1.0 vs. 0.4), while also showing less floating (8.0% frames >1 mm vs. 59.0% for PhysPT). The ablation in Table 2 confirms that removing detailed anthropometrics (w/o A) increases residual force from 0.08 to 0.22/W and drops median GRF from 0.98 to 0.74/W, while removing MTGs (w/o τ_MTG) raises residual force to 0.15/W, validating both contributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MusclePose extends the regression-based physics pose estimation (PHPE) line initiated by PhysPT [85], but departs from it by regressing the input parameters of parametric muscle torque generator (MTG) models rather than directly regressing joint torques with a transformer decoder. This design choice mirrors the HPSE paradigm of regressing SMPL parameters instead of mesh vertices, geometrically constraining predictions while reducing complexity. The method also draws on the hybrid residual regression approach from [89] for shape parameters, generalizing it to anthropometrics and ground reaction forces, and incorporates MTG formulations from the biomechanics literature [51, 25] as differentiable, learnable components — a first for a pose estimation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By integrating muscle dynamics modeling and detailed anthropometrics into an end-to-end trainable pose estimator, MusclePose provides a practical alternative to hours-long optimal control simulations for musculoskeletal analysis in sports and rehabilitation. The ablation showing that uniform-density assumptions (common in prior PHPE) inflate residual forces by over 3× highlights a previously underappreciated source of kinetic error. Key limitations include the restriction to feet-ground contact only, the absence of force-annotated training data (forcing qualitative comparison with biomechanics literature), and the underconstrained nature of multi-contact kinetics that MTGs mitigate but do not fully resolve. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to object interactions, non-feet contacts, and whether the predicted muscle activations can be validated against direct EMG recordings in the wild.
