## Problem

Computing the John Ellipsoid of a symmetric polytope defined by a constraint matrix A has a state-of-the-art per-iteration cost of Õ(nd²) from the fixed-point iteration of Cohen et al. (COLT 2019), which is prohibitively expensive when d is large. This paper asks whether structural properties of A—specifically its sparsity pattern and the treewidth of its dual graph—can be exploited to reduce the per-iteration cost while maintaining the same number of iterations and approximation guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255110/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a rank-d constraint matrix A ∈ R^{n×d} defining the symmetric polytope P = {x : −1_n ≤ Ax ≤ 1_n}, along with an approximation parameter ε ∈ (0, 0.1) and failure probability δ. The output is a weight vector v ∈ R^n (normalized so that Σv_i = d) and the associated d×d positive-definite matrix A^T diag(v) A that defines the approximate John Ellipsoid Q, satisfying (1/√(1+ε))·Q ⊆ P ⊆ √d·Q. The first algorithm is randomized with high-probability guarantees, while the second is deterministic.

## Method

The paper develops two algorithms that both build on the fixed-point iteration w_{k+1,i} = w_{k,i}·σ_i(w_k) but replace the exact O(nd²) computation of the quadratic form a_i^T(A^T diag(w)A)^{-1}a_i with structure-aware approximations. The first algorithm (Theorem 1.1) combines leverage-score-based row sampling of B_k = √diag(w_k)·A to produce a sparse (1±ε₀)-approximation of B_k^T B_k, followed by a Gaussian sketch S_k ∈ R^{s×d} to further reduce the dimension for computing the update; a novel telescoping lemma bounds the accumulated error from both sampling and sketching over T = O(ε⁻¹ log(n/d)) iterations, yielding per-iteration cost Õ(ε⁻¹ nnz(A) + ε⁻² d^ω). The second algorithm (Theorem 1.2) exploits small treewidth τ of the dual graph of A: by Lemma 2.7, the Cholesky factor L_k of B_k^T B_k is τ-sparse, so both the factorization and the subsequent leverage-score computation complete in O(nτ²) per iteration, giving total time O(nτ² · ε⁻¹ log(n/d)).

## Results

The paper presents two algorithms that reduce the per-iteration cost of approximating the John Ellipsoid from the prior state-of-the-art O(nd²) to either O~(ε⁻¹nnz(A) + ε⁻²d^ω) (input-sparsity algorithm, Theorem 1.1) or O(nτ²) (treewidth algorithm, Theorem 1.2), while maintaining the same O(ε⁻¹log(n/d)) number of iterations. Both algorithms output an ellipsoid Q satisfying (1/√(1+ε))·Q ⊆ P ⊆ √d·Q. For sparse matrices where nnz(A) ≪ nd, the input-sparsity algorithm achieves a near-linear dependence on the input size; for matrices with small treewidth τ (e.g., τ = 35 in MATPOWER instances with n=20467, d=12659), the treewidth algorithm achieves O(nτ²) per iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the fixed-point iteration algorithm of Cohen, Cousins, Lee, and Yang (COLT 2019), which achieves O~(nd²) per-iteration cost by computing the matrix inverse (A^T diag(w)A)^{-1/2} exactly at each step. The input-sparsity algorithm (Theorem 1.1) strictly improves upon this baseline in all regimes: for sparse matrices (nnz(A) ≪ nd) the improvement is dramatic, reducing the per-iteration cost from O(nd²) to O~(ε⁻¹nnz(A) + ε⁻²d^ω); even for dense matrices (nnz(A) = Θ(nd)), the algorithm is faster when n > d^ω and d > ε⁻¹, since the d^ω term dominates over nd². The treewidth algorithm (Theorem 1.2) provides a complementary speedup for structured matrices: when the dual graph of A has small treewidth τ (e.g., τ = O(d^{1/2}) to O(d^{3/4}) in Netlib LP instances, or τ = 35 in MATPOWER), the per-iteration cost drops to O(nτ²), which can be substantially smaller than both nd² and ε⁻¹nnz(A) + ε⁻²d^ω. Both new algorithms maintain the same iteration count O(ε⁻¹log(n/d)) and the same approximation guarantee (1/√(1+ε))·Q ⊆ P ⊆ √d·Q as the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the fixed-point iteration framework of Cohen et al. (COLT 2019), which reduced John Ellipsoid computation to computing ℓ∞ Lewis weights and achieved Õ(nd²) per iteration using Gaussian sketching. The key departure is that CCLY19 still computed the matrix inverse (Aᵀdiag(w)A)⁻¹/² exactly, which is the bottleneck; this paper adds leverage-score-based row sampling (building on CLM+15 and DSW22) to avoid the full inverse, reducing the per-iteration cost to near input-sparsity time Õ(ε⁻¹nnz(A) + ε⁻²d^ω). The second algorithm exploits the τ-sparsity of Cholesky factors for matrices with small treewidth, a technique drawn from sparse linear algebra (BGHK95, Dav06) but not previously applied to John Ellipsoid computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The John Ellipsoid is a fundamental object in convex geometry with applications spanning high-dimensional sampling, D-optimal experimental design, online learning, and differential privacy, so faster algorithms have broad downstream impact. The input-sparsity result is particularly relevant for large-scale sparse constraint matrices common in network optimization and power systems, where the treewidth algorithm (O(nτ²) per iteration) can be dramatically faster than the dense-matrix baseline. However, the paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the practical gains depend on the matrix being genuinely sparse or having small treewidth; for dense, high-treewidth inputs the improvement over CCLY19 is more modest. An open question is whether the sampling-plus-sketching combination can be further refined to reduce the ε⁻²d^ω term or whether the treewidth approach extends to other structured ellipsoid problems.
