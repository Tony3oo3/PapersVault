## Problem

Existing point mapping frameworks (DUSt3R, MonST3R) are constrained to pairwise frame processing and cannot model complex motion dynamics spanning more than two frames. Moreover, their task definition only accounts for variable camera poses, failing to establish consistent 3D motion trajectories of points over time in dynamic scenes with moving or deforming objects.

## Input / Output

The method takes a W-frame monocular RGB video clip (W=6 in experiments) and produces two semantically distinct outputs per pixel: the first decoder outputs a 3D motion trajectory in a fixed camera coordinate system (i.e., the 3D position of that pixel's content across frame indices), while the second decoder outputs standard 3D point maps in each other frame's camera view. This dual-output design disentangles object motion from camera motion within a single forward pass, replacing the Permutation(W,2) pairwise iterations of vanilla architectures.

## Method

Track3R generalizes the Siamese transformer architecture by inserting a trajectory encoder module that applies factorized temporal attention (trajectory attention) over tokens at the same spatial location across all W frames, using causal masks to encode inter-frame dynamics. To preserve the strong 3D shape prior learned from static scenes, the trajectory attention is wrapped in a layerscale initialized to a near-zero scalar, making the model equivalent to the pairwise architecture at initialization. The key task redefinition repurposes the first decoder's output from '3D coordinate in the other frame's camera view' to '3D coordinate in a fixed camera view indexed by frame,' effectively predicting a motion trajectory. Training initializes from MonST3R pre-trained weights and fine-tunes on SMPL-X/BEDLAM human motion trajectories and Waymo data for 25 epochs, using confidence-aware scale-invariant regression losses for both the trajectory (first decoder) and depth-registered point maps (second decoder).

## Results

Track3R achieves the best 3D motion estimation (EPE_3D) across all three evaluation datasets: 0.431 on iPhone, 0.312 on Sintel, and 0.399 on Point Odyssey, representing a 25.8% relative improvement over the strongest baseline (Align3R, 0.581) on iPhone. Among feed-forward methods, Track3R also achieves the best point mapping accuracy (Abs-rel), e.g., 0.344 on iPhone (15.7% improvement over CUT3R's 0.408). The method runs at 23.81 FPS, substantially faster than test-time optimization baselines (DUSt3R: 0.62 FPS, MonST3R: 0.41 FPS, Align3R: 0.17 FPS) while matching or exceeding their motion estimation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Track3R achieves the best EPE_3D (3D motion estimation) against all seven baselines on every dataset, with the largest gap over DUSt3R (0.431 vs. 0.973 on iPhone) and a 25.8% relative improvement over the strongest motion baseline Align3R (0.581 → 0.431 on iPhone). Among feed-forward methods (Fast3R, Spann3R, CUT3R), Track3R dominates on both EPE_3D and Abs-rel across all three datasets, e.g., beating CUT3R by 38.5% on EPE_3D (0.701 → 0.431) and 15.7% on Abs-rel (0.408 → 0.344) on iPhone. However, the test-time optimization methods (MonST3R, MegaSaM, Align3R) still outperform Track3R on Abs-rel (point mapping accuracy), with MegaSaM achieving 0.211 vs. Track3R's 0.344 on iPhone, suggesting that iterative optimization provides better geometric consistency for static shape recovery. The paper attributes this gap to the fact that test-time methods can inject external motion priors and enforce multi-frame consistency through optimization, whereas Track3R learns these priors end-to-end in a single forward pass. On the downstream camera pose estimation task (Table 2), Track3R achieves the best ATE on iPhone (0.233) and is competitive with CUT3R on Sintel and TUM-dynamics, confirming that the learned joint prior generalizes well to real-world dynamic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Track3R extends the DUSt3R/MonST3R line of Siamese point mapping architectures by introducing a trajectory encoder with factorized temporal attention that lifts the 2-frame constraint to multi-frame processing in a single forward pass. Its key conceptual departure is reinterpreting the two parallel decoders' outputs as two disentangled trajectories per point—one encoding object motion in a fixed camera frame, the other encoding 3D geometry in variable camera views—thereby unifying point mapping and 3D motion estimation into a single joint task. The layerscale initialization (borrowed from video transformer model inflation) is a practical but important design choice that preserves the strong static-scene 3D prior from pre-training while gradually learning dynamic multi-frame dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a concrete gap: existing point mapping methods either assume static scenes or rely on expensive test-time optimization to handle dynamics, and their pairwise architectures cannot model complex motion over extended temporal windows. The finding that a feed-forward model (23.81 FPS) outperforms test-time-optimization baselines (0.17–0.97 FPS) on 3D motion estimation suggests that learning motion priors during training is more effective than injecting them at inference, which could reshape how dynamic 3D reconstruction pipelines are designed. Key limitations include reliance on human-centric synthetic data (SMPL-X/BEDLAM) for trajectory supervision, which may not generalize to arbitrary object dynamics, and the distribution shift introduced by rendered inputs relative to the pre-trained model's real-image prior.
