## Problem

Critically Damped Langevin Diffusions (CLDs) have demonstrated empirical superiority over standard score-based generative models, yet their theoretical guarantees are limited to Kullback-Leibler divergence bounds that fail to capture the computational advantage of kinetic dynamics. No Wasserstein-2 convergence results existed for CLDs, and the degenerate diffusion (noise only on velocity) made standard contraction arguments inapplicable. This paper closes that gap by establishing the first W₂ convergence bound for CLD-based samplers under weaker assumptions than prior work.

## Input / Output

The method operates on an extended position-velocity phase space ℝ^{2d}, taking 2d-dimensional Gaussian noise as input to the backward SDE and a trained score network that approximates the modified score ∇log p̃_t on this extended space. It produces synthetic data samples as the position marginal X_T of the discretized backward process, evaluated against the true data distribution via sliced Wasserstein distance. A key design choice is the additional hyperparameter ε ≥ 0 that injects noise on the position coordinate, controlling sample-path smoothness and restoring ellipticity of the dynamics.

## Method

The paper introduces a generalized CLD dynamics with a position-noise regularization parameter ε that restores ellipticity, enabling analysis via classical VP/VE-style contraction arguments on the extended phase space. The convergence proof decomposes total error into mixing, discretization, and score-approximation components, using a modified score formulation (rescaled density p̃_t = p_t/p_∞) that satisfies a Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation and a synchronous coupling with a Lyapunov-type matrix 𝔐 to handle the hypo-coercive structure. Training employs Hybrid Score Matching on the extended space, where the known Gaussian velocity prior is analytically marginalized to reduce training variance. The discretization error bound explicitly depends on ε, revealing a trade-off: larger ε enhances contractivity but increases path roughness, providing practical tuning guidance. Numerical experiments on Funnel, MG-25, and Diamonds distributions confirm that small positive ε consistently improves generation quality and reduces variance across runs compared to vanilla CLD (ε=0).

## Results

On the 100-dimensional Funnel distribution, introducing a small position-noise regularization ε ∈ [0.1, 0.5] consistently reduces the sliced W₂ distance compared to the vanilla CLD (ε = 0), with the improvement being most pronounced for small drift coefficients a (e.g., a = 0.1, 0.25) where the unregularized error increases sharply. For moderate a values (a = 1, 2), the W₂ error becomes negligible across all ε settings, though ε ∈ [0.1, 0.5] still yields slightly lower mean error and reduced variance across 5 independent runs. In controlled settings (fixing the stationary distribution to N(0, I) via a(ε) = 1 − ε²/2 and σ(ε) = √(4 + ε²)), the regularization benefit persists, and the Leapfrog integrator outperforms Euler-Maruyama while preserving the relative advantage of ε > 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external generative model baselines; instead, it performs ablation-style comparisons within the CLD framework. The primary 'baseline' is the vanilla CLD (ε = 0, a = 2, σ = √2, following Dockhorn et al. 2022), which is outperformed by the regularized variant (ε > 0) in sliced W₂ distance across all tested drift coefficients a ∈ {0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2}. The regularization also reduces run-to-run variance, indicating more stable training. Between integrators, the Leapfrog scheme achieves lower W₂ error than Euler-Maruyama, but the relative benefit of ε > 0 over ε = 0 is maintained under both schemes. Additional experiments on MG-25 (25-mode, 100-dim Gaussian mixture) and Diamonds (2-dim Gaussian mixture) in the appendix confirm the regularization benefit on more challenging multimodal distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Critically-Damped Langevin Diffusion (CLD) framework of Dockhorn et al. (2022) by introducing a position-noise regularization parameter ε that restores ellipticity in the extended phase space, and by adopting the modified score formulation from Conforti et al. (2025). While prior convergence guarantees for CLDs were limited to KL divergence (Conforti et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2023), and Wasserstein convergence was established only for standard VP/VE score-based models (Gao et al., 2025; Strasman et al., 2025; Bruno et al., 2025), this work provides the first Wasserstein-2 convergence bound for CLDs under weaker assumptions than those required by existing KL analyses. The proof technique relies on coupling arguments and hypo-coercive SDE analysis, differing substantially from the entropy-based methods used in prior KL convergence proofs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a notable gap between the empirical superiority of CLD-based samplers and their theoretical understanding, providing practitioners with a principled Wasserstein error bound and practical guidance for tuning the ε regularization parameter. The trade-off identified—where ε enhances contractive behavior but inflates discretization error—offers a concrete knob for balancing sample path smoothness against numerical stability. Open questions include extending the analysis to higher-order integrators like Leapfrog (which empirically outperforms Euler-Maruyama), developing denoiser architectures tailored to the extended position-velocity space, and validating the theory on high-dimensional real-world datasets beyond the toy distributions tested here.
