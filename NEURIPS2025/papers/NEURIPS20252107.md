## Problem

Existing multimodal reasoning benchmarks struggle to simultaneously balance subject diversity, problem complexity, reasoning depth, and language coverage, and have begun exhibiting performance saturation on SOTA models. Additionally, the community lacks a large-scale, multimodal, multi-disciplinary training dataset with curated reasoning paths to support open-source model development beyond the narrow focus on mathematical reasoning.

## Input / Output

Each BMMR instance pairs one or more images (29 image types) with a bilingual (English/Chinese) college-level text question in multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, or open-ended format, spanning 300 UNESCO-defined subfields across 8 disciplines. The BMMR-Verifier additionally consumes a reference solution and a model's full reasoning response, producing a binary correctness label (+/−) for each individual reasoning step and an aggregate response-level score. The BMMR-Train split provides 89k question–reasoning-path pairs formatted for supervised fine-tuning of open-source LMMs.

## Method

The dataset is constructed through a six-step human-in-the-loop pipeline: UNESCO taxonomy-guided collection from print and digital sources, hierarchical discipline classification via GPT-4o, safety/objectivity filtering, format transformation (converting guessable MCQs to open-ended or fact-verification tasks), and a cascade quality-control stage where three models of increasing capability (Qwen2.5-7B → Qwen2.5-72B → GPT-4o) each generate 32 rollouts per instance to compute agreement-rate thresholds, with residual cases resolved by 40 human annotators. The BMMR-Verifier is trained by performing 32 rollouts per sample across multiple models, assigning rule-based correctness labels to each trajectory, then propagating step-level +/− tags following the Math-Shepherd/OVM methodology and optimizing with a per-step cross-entropy loss. At inference, the verifier predicts the probability that the next token is '+' given the question, reference solution, and preceding steps, yielding a continuous score per reasoning step. Fine-tuning experiments use LLaMA Factory and MS Swift with frozen ViT, 1 epoch, and learning rates of 1e-5 (Qwen) or 2e-5 (InternVL), demonstrating that BMMR-Train yields up to 72% relative improvement on specific disciplines.

## Results

On BMMR-Eval, the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves 50.15% overall accuracy (En) and 48.66% (Zh), while the strongest open-source model Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct reaches 38.22% (En) and 39.81% (Zh). Reasoning models o3 and o4-mini score 38.06% and 35.91% respectively, showing strong discipline-specific performance (e.g., o3 reaches 63.89% in ICTs but only 27.78% in Health). Fine-tuning open-source models on BMMR-Train yields substantial gains, with BMMR-InternVL2.5-78B improving by 19.07% overall and BMMR-InternVL2.5-38B surpassing the untrained InternVL2.5-78B in 4 out of 8 disciplines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads with 50.15% overall, outperforming o3 (38.06%), o4-mini (35.91%), Gemini-2.5-Flash-thinking (44.16%), and GPT-4o (25.11%). Notably, GPT-4o underperforms relative to its reputation, scoring below even mid-size open-source models like Qwen2.5-VL-32B (34.09%). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (38.22%) is the strongest, followed by InternVL3-78B (33.76%) and InternVL2.5-78B (28.79%). Reasoning models (o3, o4-mini, QVQ-72B) exhibit pronounced discipline bias: o3 achieves 63.89% in ICTs but drops to 27.78% in Health, while InternVL3-78B scores 41.53% in ICTs versus 16.42% in Social Science. Fine-tuning on BMMR-Train narrows the open-source gap substantially—BMMR-InternVL2.5-78B gains 19.07% overall, and BMMR-InternVL2.5-38B surpasses the untrained InternVL2.5-78B in 4 of 8 disciplines, demonstrating the training set's value for the open-source community.

## Relation to Prior Work

BMMR extends the multi-discipline multimodal evaluation line initiated by MMMU (11k problems, 30 subjects, English-only) to a substantially broader scope: 110k college-level instances across 300 UNESCO-defined subfields, bilingual (English/Chinese), with curated reasoning paths for every instance. For process-based evaluation, the BMMR-Verifier adapts the step-level scoring methodology from Math-Shepherd [25] and OVM [59]—originally designed for text-only mathematical reasoning—to the multimodal, multi-discipline setting, using the same Monte Carlo–derived step labels and cross-entropy training objective. The dataset construction departs from prior multimodal reasoning resources (ScienceQA, MM-Eureka, LLaVA-CoT, MAmmoTH-VL), which are largely K-12-level or single-discipline, by targeting college-level content across 8 high-level disciplines and employing a six-stage human-in-the-loop curation pipeline with cascade-based quality control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BMMR fills a critical gap: the community lacked a large-scale, bilingual, multi-discipline multimodal training set with reasoning paths, and existing evaluation benchmarks were showing saturation. The finding that reasoning-optimized models (o3, o4-mini, InternVL3) exhibit pronounced discipline bias—excelling in ICTs/STEM but underperforming in Health, Agriculture, and Social Science—challenges the assumption that stronger System-2 reasoning uniformly improves all domains, suggesting future post-training must balance specialized reasoning depth with cross-disciplinary breadth. Open questions include whether more advanced post-training (RL, test-time scaling) can close the remaining gap to proprietary models, and whether the BMMR-Verifier's step-level scores can be integrated into training loops (e.g., as process rewards) to directly improve reasoning quality.
