## Problem

Multi-modal image segmentation degrades significantly when modalities are missing due to sensor failures or clinical constraints, yet existing solutions either require separate model parameters per modality combination (inflating deployment cost) or rely on input/feature-level reconstruction that fails to transfer to the spatial precision demands of segmentation. The paper addresses how to build a single unified model that handles all possible modality subsets with minimal performance variance, without requiring modality classification or model selection at inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255634/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts multi-modal volumes or image pairs with arbitrary subsets of modalities present: 3D MRI volumes (T1, T1ce, T2, Flair, each H×W×T slices), or 2D RGB+Depth / RGB+Thermal pairs concatenated into 4-channel tensors. Missing modalities are zero-filled (MRI) or duplicated from an available channel (RGB-D/T). The output is a per-pixel segmentation probability map with class-specific channels (3 tumor sub-regions for BraTS, binary saliency maps, or 37-class semantic labels for SUN-RGBD). A single parameter set serves all 15 MRI, 3 RGB-D, and 3 RGB-T modality combinations simultaneously.

## Method

UniMRSeg employs a three-stage hierarchical self-supervised compensation strategy on a shared 3D U-Net backbone with 3D ASPP. Stage 1 (input level) pretrains via multi-granular modality reconstruction using random modality dropout (50%), channel-wise modality shuffling, and spatial masking, supervised by L1+SSIM loss. Stage 2 (feature level) applies NT-Xent contrastive learning at five encoder levels, pairing complete-modality features with their randomly-dropped counterparts as positives and cross-sample features as negatives, jointly optimized with Dice segmentation loss to keep representations task-aligned. Stage 3 (output level) freezes the encoder and fine-tunes a lightweight reverse attention adapter (containing a 3D Swin Transformer block) that explicitly compensates weak perceptual semantics, guided by feature-level L1 consistency and prediction-level Dice consistency between complete and all 14 incomplete modality variants. The reverse attention mechanism inverts the mutual attention map to highlight differential information between modality-complete and -incomplete representations, enabling targeted correction of regions the earlier stages cannot recover.

## Results

On BraTS2020 brain tumor segmentation (15 modality combinations), UniMRSeg achieves average Dice scores of 80.64% (Whole), 73.33% (Core), and 58.53% (Enhancing), with the lowest standard deviation (8.43/13.04/21.84) among all compared methods. On RGB-D salient object segmentation (STERE), it reaches 0.893 S_m (std 0.047); on RGB-T salient object segmentation (VT1000), 0.899 S_m (std 0.047); and on RGB-D semantic segmentation (SUN-RGBD), 50.7% mIoU (std 3.1). The method uses a single set of parameters across all modality combinations, achieving the best average performance and minimal variance simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On BraTS2020, UniMRSeg outperforms the strongest baseline PASSION [48] by 4.25% on Whole tumor (80.64 vs 76.39) and 7.27% on Core (73.33 vs 66.06), while matching on Enhancing (58.53 vs 58.53); it also reduces the standard deviation on Whole from 10.07 to 8.43, indicating more stable predictions across modality combinations. On RGB-D salient object segmentation (STERE), UniMRSeg surpasses the best baseline PopNet [62] by 6.7% in S_m (0.893 vs 0.826) and reduces std from 0.157 to 0.047. On RGB-T salient object segmentation (VT1000), it beats SSLSOD [80] by 3.0% (0.899 vs 0.869) with a much lower std (0.047 vs 0.076). On SUN-RGBD semantic segmentation, UniMRSeg leads MaskMentor [85] by 2.7 mIoU (50.7 vs 48.0) with the lowest std (3.1 vs 6.1). In the appendix BraTS2018 comparison, UniMRSeg outperforms M3FeCon [69] by 2.58% on Whole (88.90 vs 86.32) and 3.18% on Enhancing (67.02 vs 63.84), again with the lowest standard deviation across all regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three dominant paradigms for incomplete multi-modal segmentation—generalizable architectures (CMX, TokenFusion), projection-based fusion (SFusion, HeMIS), and modality reconstruction (M3AE, MaskMentor, M3FeCon)—by unifying all modality combinations under a single parameter set and replacing input-level reconstruction with a hierarchical self-supervised compensation spanning input, feature, and output levels. It repurposes three SSL paradigms previously studied in isolation (masked autoencoding, NT-Xent contrastive learning, and knowledge distillation) into a progressive three-stage pipeline, and introduces a reverse attention adapter with a 3D Swin Transformer to explicitly compensate for residual representation gaps in a frozen encoder. The modality shuffle augmentation and the deliberate decoupling of modality-agnostic representations from sequential dependencies represent a conceptual departure from prior reconstruction-based methods that treat modalities as fixed channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the deployment bottleneck of maintaining separate models per modality subset—a critical concern in clinical and autonomous-driving settings where sensor failures are routine. The framework's generality across 2D/3D, medical/natural scenes, and four distinct segmentation tasks suggests the hierarchical compensation principle could transfer to other modality-robust perception problems. Open questions include scalability to more than four modalities, performance when training data itself is systematically incomplete (rather than randomly dropped), and whether the three-stage pipeline's necessity persists with stronger pre-trained backbones. The paper acknowledges limitations in Appendix E but does not deeply explore failure modes under extreme corruption or domain shift.
