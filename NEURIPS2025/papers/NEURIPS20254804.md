## Problem

Autoregressive image generation models, despite their success in NLP, struggle to learn high-level visual semantics due to three identified mechanisms: excessive reliance on local and conditional tokens in attention, progressive semantic inconsistency across generation steps, and lack of invariance in discrete visual tokens. This paper provides the first systematic investigation into why next-token prediction underperforms in the visual domain and proposes a unified training solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254804/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (ImageNet) as a conditioning token prepended to a sequence of 256 discrete visual tokens produced by a VQ-GAN tokenizer operating on 256×256 RGB images (16×16 grid of 16×16 patches). During training, each image is also augmented into M=2 views and tokenized separately. At inference, the model generates tokens sequentially in raster order using standard autoregressive sampling, producing a 256×256 RGB image decoded by the VQ-GAN decoder.

## Method

ST-AR augments the standard next-token prediction loss with three self-supervised objectives applied only during training: (1) a MIM loss that randomly masks 25% of attention map entries (setting them to −inf) in the student network and aligns its final-layer features to an EMA teacher, forcing the model to attend beyond spatially adjacent tokens; (2) an inter-step contrastive loss that pulls together student features at different timesteps of the same image against negatives from other images, enforcing semantic consistency across the generation sequence; and (3) an inter-view contrastive loss that aligns features at the same token position across different augmented views of the same image, compensating for VQ-GAN's lack of spatial invariance. The contrastive losses use a SimSiam-style projector on student features and are applied at the mid-depth layer (6th of 12 for LlamaGen-B). The total loss is L_AR + α·L_MIM + β·½(L_step + L_view) with α=1.0, β=0.5, and the EMA teacher is updated with decay 0.9999.

## Results

On ImageNet-256×256 with classifier-free guidance, LlamaGen-XL + ST-AR (300 epochs) achieves FID 2.37, sFID 6.05, and IS 270.59, outperforming the vanilla LlamaGen-XL (FID 2.63) and approaching top diffusion models like SiT-XL (FID 2.15) and DiT-XL (FID 2.27). At just 50 epochs, LlamaGen-XL + ST-AR reaches FID 2.72, a ~49% FID improvement over the vanilla 50-epoch baseline (FID 3.39). Without CFG, LlamaGen-XL + ST-AR (300 epochs) achieves FID 6.20, surpassing LlamaGen-3B (FID 9.38) despite having ~4× fewer parameters. Linear probing accuracy for LlamaGen-B improves from 18.68% to 45.27% with ST-AR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-AR consistently outperforms all vanilla LlamaGen variants across model sizes: LlamaGen-XL + ST-AR (300 ep) achieves FID 2.37 vs. 2.63 for LlamaGen-XL†, and LlamaGen-B + ST-AR reaches FID 4.09 vs. 5.46 for LlamaGen-B. Against other paradigms, ST-AR (775M, 300 ep) beats VAR-d20 (FID 2.57) and MaskGIT-re (FID 4.02) but trails SiT-XL (FID 2.15) and DiT-XL (FID 2.27) by a small margin, while being competitive with StyleGAN-XL (FID 2.30). Notably, LlamaGen-XL + ST-AR at 50 epochs (FID 2.72) already surpasses LlamaGen-XL† trained for 300 epochs (FID 2.63), demonstrating substantial sample efficiency gains. In the no-CFG setting (Table 1), LlamaGen-XL + ST-AR (300 ep) achieves FID 6.20, dramatically outperforming LlamaGen-3B (FID 9.38) despite having ~4× fewer parameters. The method also improves precision over baselines (e.g., 0.83 vs. 0.81 for LlamaGen-XL†) while maintaining comparable recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LlamaGen autoregressive image generation framework by identifying three specific failure modes of next-token prediction in the visual domain (local/conditional attention bias, inter-step semantic drift, and tokenizer invariance deficiency) and addressing them through a self-supervised training paradigm. Conceptually, it forms an iBOT-style framework combining masked image modeling and contrastive learning, but adapts these to the autoregressive setting by masking attention maps rather than input tokens and using an EMA teacher for feature alignment. It departs from REPA and similar approaches that inject pre-trained representation knowledge, instead achieving improved visual understanding entirely through self-guided training objectives during the AR training process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LlamaGen-XL trained with ST-AR for only 50 epochs achieves FID comparable to LlamaGen-3B trained for 300 epochs (despite ~4× fewer parameters) suggests that better visual representation learning can partially substitute for model scale in AR image generation. The systematic diagnosis of why causal attention struggles with global image semantics provides a useful lens for future AR model design. Key limitations include increased training cost from the teacher network and additional losses, evaluation restricted to class-conditional ImageNet-256, and the open question of whether these self-supervised objectives transfer to text-to-image or video generation settings.
