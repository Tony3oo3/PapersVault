## Problem

Existing web agent benchmarks (e.g., VisualWebArena, AgentBench) systematically filter out pages containing CAPTCHAs, leaving no evaluation of whether multimodal LLM agents can handle interactive, multi-step verification puzzles in a closed-loop browser setting. Prior CAPTCHA-solving work treats them as static perception tasks solvable by CNNs, ignoring the sequential planning, fine-grained motor control, and interface state tracking that real CAPTCHAs demand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253006/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Agents receive sequential browser screenshots (RGB) and natural-language CAPTCHA instructions, and must issue discrete browser actions (click, drag, type, press-and-hold) in a closed-loop POMDP until the submit button is clicked. Evaluation is zero-shot across 20 CAPTCHA types (225 puzzles total), reported as pass@1 success rate averaged over three runs per type, plus a novel CAPTCHA Reasoning Depth score (mean 2.94, std 0.92) that counts atomic visual, cognitive, motor, and state-monitoring steps required for a human solution.

## Method

Open CaptchaWorld is a web-based benchmark of 20 diverse CAPTCHA types (image matching, jigsaw alignment, sequence-click, slider, counting, hold-button, etc.) with 225 puzzles generated by GPT-4o or human designers, each annotated with ground-truth solutions and a task-agnostic CAPTCHA Reasoning Depth via a 31-item atomic-step checklist (visual, cognitive, motor, state-reveal categories). The agent is modeled as a finite-horizon POMDP and evaluated using the Browser-Use framework with nine MLLM backbones (Openai-o3, GPT-4.1, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Gemini2.5-Pro, etc.) in a zero-shot, closed-loop browser environment. A key empirical finding is that models systematically over-segment reasoning steps compared to humans (e.g., Openai-o3 assigns depth 5 where humans assign 3), revealing an overthinking bias. Ablations across four agent frameworks (Browser-Use, SeeAct, WebVoyager, OWL) with GPT-4o show that framework choice yields only marginal gains (5.7%–10.0%), confirming that the bottleneck lies in the MLLM's visual-motor reasoning rather than the orchestration layer.

## Results

On Open CaptchaWorld (20 CAPTCHA types, 225 total puzzles), humans achieve a 93.3% Pass@1 success rate, while the best-performing MLLM agent (Openai-o3 via Browser-Use) reaches only 40.0%, a gap of 53.3 percentage points. GPT-4.1 and Gemini2.5-Pro tie at 25.0%, Claude-3.7-Sonnet and DeepSeek-V3 at 20.0%, and the weakest agents (GPT-4o at 5.7%, Openai-o1 at 5.0%) perform near-randomly. Evaluation cost per full CAPTCHA sequence ranges from $7.3 (DeepSeek-V3) to $94.6 (Openai-o1), with Openai-o3 incurring $66.4 for its top accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All evaluated MLLM agents fall substantially short of the 93.3% human baseline. Openai-o3 leads among agents at 40.0% Pass@1 but at a high cost of $66.4 per episode; GPT-4.1 and Gemini2.5-Pro achieve 25.0% at roughly one-third the cost ($16.7 and $18.1), making them the most cost-effective mid-tier options. DeepSeek-V3 matches Claude-3.7-Sonnet at 20.0% but costs only $7.3 versus $18.7, offering the best cost-performance ratio among agents above 15%. At the low end, GPT-4o (5.7%) and Openai-o1 (5.0%) perform near-randomly, with Openai-o1 being the most expensive option at $94.6 despite the worst accuracy. Notably, Claude-3.7-Sonnet is the only model to solve the Hold Button task (100% on that type), while Openai-o3 uniquely succeeds on Geometry_Click, highlighting that no single agent dominates all 20 CAPTCHA types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two prior lines of work: (1) web-agent benchmarks like VisualWebArena and AgentBench that explicitly filter out CAPTCHA-containing pages, and (2) traditional CAPTCHA-solving research (Deep-CAPTCHA, Breaking reCAPTCHA v2) that treats puzzles as static perception tasks solvable by CNNs. By framing CAPTCHA solving as a closed-loop, multi-step POMDP requiring perception, planning, and fine-grained motor control, the paper repurposes the agent-evaluation paradigm (Browser-Use, SeeAct, WebVoyager, OWL) for a task those frameworks were never designed to test. The novel CAPTCHA Reasoning Depth metric extends the idea of task complexity beyond type-based categorization to a task-agnostic, step-counting measure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 53.3% performance gap between humans (93.3%) and the best agent (40%, Openai-o3) provides a concrete, reproducible signal that current MLLM agents are not deployment-ready for real-world web tasks where CAPTCHAs are ubiquitous. The observation that models systematically over-segment reasoning steps compared to humans (overthinking) points to a fundamental inefficiency in how MLLMs decompose interactive tasks, suggesting that future agent architectures need human-like abstraction and experience-based compression. Key limitations include the modest dataset size (225 CAPTCHAs, 20 types), zero-shot-only evaluation, and the absence of fine-tuning or memory-augmented baselines, leaving open whether the gap narrows under adaptation. Cost-performance tradeoffs (o1 at $94.6 yielding only 5% vs. DeepSeek-V3 at $7.3 yielding 20%) raise practical questions about scalable deployment.
