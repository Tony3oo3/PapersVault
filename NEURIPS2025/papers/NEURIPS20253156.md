## Problem

While RadixAttention enables prefix reuse to reduce LLM inference cost, the scheduling problem under online arrival patterns with time-to-first-token (TTFT) constraints was previously uncharacterized. Existing FCFS and LPM strategies can produce large TTFT spikes under burst traffic, and no theoretical framework existed to understand when each strategy fails or to design a principled alternative.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253156/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite query stream of prompts (arbitrary-length strings) paired with non-negative arrival times and produces a non-preemptive processing order. The computational model (Definition 2) captures prefill-dominated inference where each query's cost is proportional to its length minus the prefix overlap with the immediately preceding query, plus a quadratic attention term scaled by c_attn. Processing cannot begin before a query's arrival time, and the objective is to bound per-query TTFT (completion time minus arrival time) under a given constraint T.

## Method

The paper first proves NP-hardness of the TTFT feasibility decision problem via reduction from 3-PARTITION, showing that prefix reuse combined with non-uniform arrival times makes scheduling intractable in general. It then introduces k-LPM (Algorithm 1), which at each scheduling step processes the oldest queued query (an FCFS step) followed by k−1 greedy longest-prefix-match steps, thereby balancing fairness against cache reuse; k=1 recovers FCFS and k=∞ recovers LPM. Under a Regular Arrival Shuffled Queue data generative model (Definition 4) with tree-structured prompts of height two, Theorem 2 establishes that k-LPM achieves a strictly lower worst-case TTFT than both FCFS and LPM with high probability, and Corollary 3 shows this holds for all c_attn ≥ 0. Empirical validation on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct via SGLang with 2000 real prompts from a personalized recommendation use case confirms that k=2 yields the best P99 TTFT across a wide range of request rates.

## Results

The k-LPM algorithm with k=2 achieves reduced P99 TTFT across a wide range of request rates compared to both FCFS (k=1) and LPM (k=∞) on 2000 randomly shuffled prompts from a real industrial use case (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on 8×A100 GPUs via SGLang). Theoretically, Theorem 2 establishes that k-LPM deterministically achieves max TTFT ≤ T + n(u/k + d − s/k), while LPM and FCFS have high-probability lower bounds of T + n(u/k + d) and T + n(u + d − s) respectively. In the varying prefix-ratio experiment (Table 1, 80 req/s, Llama-3.2-1B on H100), k-LPM with k=4 achieves P95 TTFT of 447.37 s at u=5000/d=1000, beating both FCFS (564.44 s) and LPM (565.92 s), while at u=1000/d=5000 FCFS (5843.80 s) outperforms k-LPM (9128.23 s) and LPM (14733.45 s) on P95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares k-LPM against two baselines: FCFS (k=1, processes queries in arrival order) and LPM (k=∞, greedily maximizes KV cache reuse at each step), both from the SGLang framework (Zheng et al., 2024). In the main experiment (Figure 2, P99 TTFT vs. request rate on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), k-LPM with k=2 consistently outperforms both FCFS and LPM across a wide range of request rates, with FCFS winning only at very low rates and LPM winning at the highest rates for median TTFT. In the prefix-ratio experiment (Table 1), k-LPM is never simultaneously worse than both baselines: when the shared prefix is short relative to the unique suffix (u=1000, d=5000), FCFS achieves the best P95 (5843.80 s vs. 9128.23 s for k-LPM and 14733.45 s for LPM); when the prefix dominates (u=5000, d=1000), k-LPM achieves the best P95 (447.37 s vs. 564.44 s for FCFS and 565.92 s for LPM). The theoretical guarantee (Corollary 3) confirms that for any 0 < s < u and k > 1, k-LPM simultaneously beats both FCFS and LPM on maximum TTFT with high probability, and the experiments validate this even when the strict assumptions of the data generative model are relaxed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RadixAttention and LPM scheduler introduced by Zheng et al. (SGLang, 2024), which established offline optimality of LPM for maximizing prefix reuse but left the online scheduling problem under non-uniform arrival times uncharacterized. It departs from prior LLM serving work (Orca, FastSwitch, DistServe, Sarathi-Serve) by isolating the prefix-reuse dimension and providing the first formal computational model and complexity analysis for online query scheduling with KV-cache reuse under TTFT constraints. The k-LPM algorithm generalizes both FCFS (k=1) and LPM (k=∞) as a single parameterized family, filling a gap that prior heuristic schedulers (e.g., Srivatsa et al.'s priority groups) addressed without theoretical grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NP-hardness result (via 3-PARTITION reduction) establishes that prefix reuse fundamentally changes the scheduling complexity landscape—from trivially solvable by FCFS without reuse to intractable with it—providing a clear theoretical boundary for future algorithm design. The k-LPM algorithm offers a practical, drop-in improvement for prefill-dominant workloads (document summarization, coding assistants, test-time compute scaling) with provable TTFT guarantees under a realistic data model. Key open questions include whether a PTAS exists for percentile TTFT constraints, how the results extend to distributional (rather than fixed) query streams and non-constant decoding lengths, and whether the single-model, single-framework experimental validation generalizes across architectures and serving stacks.
