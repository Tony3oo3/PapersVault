## Problem

No public resource pairs single-cell RNA-seq with Cell Painting microscopy under matched chemical treatments, and existing treatment-matched datasets rely on bulk measurements or post-hoc pairings from incompatible experiments, precluding direct comparison of unimodal, multimodal, and multiprofile methods for cellular treatment response modeling. Additionally, the practical utility of recently proposed single-cell and imaging foundation models for treatment identification remains unclear due to the absence of realistic, experiment-aware benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254706/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models receive scRNA-seq gene expression count vectors and/or 224×224 single-cell image crops (5 Cell Painting stain channels processed independently then concatenated) from U2-OS cells treated with one of 28 chemicals. The benchmark supports four input settings: unimodal/multimodal × single/multiprofile (k=32 profiles sampled uniformly without replacement per treatment), with multimodal pairs randomly matched by shared treatment label. Output is a 28-class treatment prediction scored by balanced accuracy and macro-F1, evaluated under both Within-Experiment and Held-out-Experiment test splits that simulate increasing laboratory variability.

## Method

The paper introduces a treatment-identification benchmark that decomposes the task into three stages: feature extraction (ImageNet-pretrained CNNs/transformers, CLIP models, OpenPhenom for imaging; PCA, scVI fit-to-data, and zero-shot foundation models scGPT/Geneformer/UCE/scVI-1/scVI-2 for scRNA-seq), aggregation and integration (AvgPool, DeepSet, Set Transformers for multiprofile; late-fusion concatenation for multimodal), and a trained classification head. The key design contribution is the replicate- and experiment-aware data splitting: Round 1 provides train/validation/WE-test from independent replicate pairs, while Round 2 serves as a held-out experiment test set capturing realistic technical variation from a separate experimental campaign. Across ~24,000 GPU hours of evaluation, the benchmark reveals that fit-to-data scVI outperforms all zero-shot scRNA-seq foundation models, multiprofile aggregation consistently improves unimodal performance, and multimodal late fusion yields inconsistent gains, underscoring the need for careful, realistic benchmarking in ML for biology.

## Results

On the scGeneScope Treatment Identification Benchmark, scVI fit-to-data achieves the strongest unimodal scRNA-seq performance with 51.7% balanced accuracy (WE) and 51.6% (HE), substantially outperforming all zero-shot scRNA-seq foundation models (scGPT: 38.7% WE / 38.1% HE; Geneformer: 28.1% WE / 19.6% HE). The best imaging model (ViT-L) reaches only 27.9% (WE) and 20.8% (HE), and the domain-specific OpenPhenom foundation model performs worst of all at 16.8% (WE) / 14.2% (HE). The overall best configuration, AvgPool+scVI+ViT-L (multimodal multiprofile), achieves 68.2% (WE) but drops to 54.4% (HE), while the best unimodal multiprofile model (DeepSet+scVI) reaches 63.7% (WE) / 58.1% (HE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

scVI fit-to-data (51.7% WE / 51.6% HE balanced accuracy) outperforms every zero-shot scRNA-seq foundation model tested: scGPT (38.7% / 38.1%), Geneformer (28.1% / 19.6%), UCE (22.3% / 20.2%), scVI-1 (31.9% / 28.0%), and scVI-2 (21.8% / 21.1%), with the gap exceeding 10% over scGPT on both splits. Among imaging models, ViT-L leads on WE (27.9%) while ViT-H generalizes best to HE (22.4%), but all imaging models lose 5–7% balanced accuracy from WE to HE, and the domain-specific OpenPhenom model (16.8% / 14.2%) underperforms even ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 (26.2% / 21.3%). Multiprofile aggregation consistently improves unimodal performance: DeepSet+scVI reaches 63.7% WE / 58.1% HE versus 51.7% / 51.6% for single-profile scVI, and AvgPool+ViT-L reaches 47.1% WE versus 27.9% for single-profile ViT-L. However, multimodal late-fusion integration yields inconsistent gains: scVI+ViT-L improves HE (51.7% vs. 51.6%) but not WE, while PCA+ViT-L substantially improves HE (35.7% vs. 20.7%) at the cost of WE parity. The best overall model (AvgPool+scVI+ViT-L, 68.2% WE) does not significantly outperform the best unimodal multiprofile model (DeepSet+scVI, 58.1% HE) on the held-out experiment, suggesting that multimodal integration has not yet delivered reliable cross-experiment gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by providing the first public treatment-matched dataset pairing single-cell RNA-seq with Cell Painting microscopy for the same chemical perturbations, filling a gap left by unimodal repositories (JUMP-CP, RxRx3, CELLxGENE Census) and bulk-expression-only paired resources (Rosetta L1000, toxicological screens). Conceptually, it repurposes the treatment-identification/MoA-prediction task from prior phenomics benchmarks but elevates it through a replicate- and experiment-aware splitting strategy that simulates realistic laboratory variability across independent data generation rounds. The benchmarking framework draws on established methods from both modalities—ImageNet-pretrained CNNs/transformers and CLIP for imaging, PCA/scVI and zero-shot foundation models (scGPT, Geneformer, UCE) for transcriptomics—combined with permutation-invariant aggregation (DeepSet, Set Transformers) and late-fusion multimodal integration, but the key departure is the controlled parallel-experiment design that eliminates the confounding of post-hoc dataset matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scGeneScope dataset and benchmark address a critical infrastructure gap: without treatment-matched single-cell multimodal data, the field cannot rigorously evaluate whether integrating imaging and transcriptomics actually improves treatment response modeling. The finding that a modestly-sized scVI encoder fit from scratch outperforms all zero-shot scRNA-seq foundation models by 10–20% balanced accuracy, and that the only domain-specific imaging foundation model (OpenPhenom) performs worst of all tested models, serves as an important reality check for the biological foundation model community. Key limitations include the single cell line (U2-OS), 28-compound scope, restriction to late-fusion integration, and absence of batch-integration strategies; open questions include whether early/mid fusion, cross-modal attention, or joint generative modeling could close the gap, and whether these findings hold at the scale of hundreds of thousands of perturbations across multiple cell lines.
