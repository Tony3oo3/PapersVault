## Problem

Current text-to-image diffusion transformers (DiTs) condition on complete-text captions in a single pass, which causes a 'complete-text comprehension defect' manifesting as attribute misbinding, semantic entanglement, and style dominance. This stems from two root causes: insufficient semantic analysis due to encoder limitations (text-length bottleneck, softmax competition, positional bias) and premature information exposure, where all semantic primitive types (object, relation, attribute) are modeled simultaneously before primary concepts are established.

## Input / Output

The method takes a complete-text caption as input and internally converts it into a split-text caption—a hierarchical collection of simplified sentences tagged by primitive type ([OBJECT], [RELATION], [ATTRIBUTE])—which is then encoded by three text encoders (CLIP-L/14, CLIP-G/14, T5-XXL) and injected incrementally at different denoising timesteps. The output is a 1024×1024 RGB image generated by the MM-DiT backbone. The input token sequence has length 2L (L≤77) with a hierarchical structure combining split-text tokens and a T5-encoded complete-text supplement to fill unused dimension capacity.

## Method

DiT-ST comprises three components: (1) Caption Parsing, where an LLM (Qwen-Plus) extracts object, relation, and attribute primitives from the caption and assembles them into a hierarchical parsing graph; (2) Hierarchical Caption Input, which constructs the split-text caption from the graph and refines MM-DiT's text encoding by concatenating CLIP-L/14 and CLIP-G/14 split-text tokens with a T5-encoded complete-text token to fully utilize the dimension budget; (3) Incremental Primitive Injection, which determines stage-specific timesteps—object at t=0, relation at the SNR inflection point, and attribute at the cross-attention convergence point—then injects each primitive type's encoded tokens into the input via cross-attention only at its designated stage. Training combines conditional flow matching loss with a composite attention loss that enforces semantic alignment at each injection stage, proper attention convergence timing, and spatial exclusivity between different primitive attention maps.

## Results

On GenEval, DiT-ST Medium (2B) achieves 0.69 overall accuracy, matching DreamEngine and approaching SDv3.5 Large (0.71) despite 4× fewer parameters, while outperforming SDv3 Medium (0.62) by 11.3%. On COCO-5K, DiT-ST Medium records the highest CLIPScore (34.09) and lowest FID (22.11), surpassing SDv3.5 Large by 4.1% on CLIPScore. On COCO-30K, DiT-ST Large (8B) achieves FID 17.16, narrowly beating SDv3.5 Large (17.31). The method also demonstrates robustness to caption length, sustaining CLIPScore above 32 across all length bins and peaking at 35.81 for the longest captions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDv3 Medium (2B, same parameter count), DiT-ST Medium wins on every GenEval subcategory, with the largest gains in two-object reasoning (+0.14) and attribute binding (+0.10), and lifts CLIPScore from 31.31 to 34.09 (+8.9%). Against the larger SDv3.5 Large (8B), DiT-ST Medium trails slightly on GenEval overall (0.69 vs 0.71) and VQAScore (76.12 vs 76.49) but clearly wins on CLIPScore (34.09 vs 32.74, +4.1%), demonstrating parameter efficiency. Against Flux.1 Dev (12B), DiT-ST Medium achieves higher overall GenEval (0.69 vs 0.66) and substantially higher CLIPScore (34.09 vs 31.52), though Flux leads on counting (0.74 vs 0.70) and colors (0.79 vs 0.75). Against PixArt-α (0.6B), DiT-ST dominates across all metrics (GenEval 0.69 vs 0.48, CLIPScore 34.09 vs 32.58). Against DreamEngine, DiT-ST Medium matches the overall GenEval score (0.69) while being more balanced across subcategories, particularly in position (0.36 vs 0.27) and counting (0.70 vs 0.64).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MM-DiT backbone (Esser et al., ICML 2024) and its three-encoder text conditioning pipeline (CLIP-L/14, CLIP-G/14, T5-XXL), refining how text tokens are structured and injected during denoising. It departs from prior methods that mitigate specific manifestations of the complete-text comprehension defect (Attend-and-Excite for attribute misbinding, DeaDiff for style dominance, SCoPE for progressive prompt detailing) by targeting the root cause—syntactic complexity of complete-text captions—through a structural reformulation of the input itself. The incremental injection schedule draws on observations from Choi et al. (2022) about semantic emergence timing and Zhang et al. (2024) about cross-attention convergence, but formalizes these into a principled timestep-selection procedure using SNR inflection points and attention convergence thresholds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The split-text conditioning framework is architecture-agnostic (SDv3 Medium gains the same absolute CLIPScore improvement without fine-tuning), making it a broadly applicable plug-in for existing DiT pipelines. Key open questions include whether batch-level timestep selection can be replaced by sample-level adaptation without prohibitive cost, how the method handles long-range semantic dependencies that splitting may sever (e.g., irony, negation), and whether the LLM-based caption parsing step introduces systematic biases. The reliance on an external LLM (Qwen-Plus) for graph construction adds a dependency that limits deployment in latency-sensitive or offline settings.
