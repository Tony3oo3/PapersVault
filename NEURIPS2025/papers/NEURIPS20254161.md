## Problem

Existing robust boosting methods provide theoretical guarantees only for symmetric and uniform label noise at large training sizes, and can exhibit poor classification accuracy even in noiseless settings. No prior work offers finite-sample performance bounds that account for general (including adversarial) label noise or provides guarantees relative to the Bayes risk. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating boosting as a minimax problem over an uncertainty set defined by base-rules.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n training pairs (x_i, y_i) with x_i ∈ R^d and y_i ∈ {−1, +1}, a library of base-rules H (e.g., decision trees with bounded depth), and a regularization parameter λ (default 1/√n). It outputs a deterministic classifier h(x) = sign(h̄(x)ᵀμ*) assigning binary labels, along with a minimax risk estimate R̄ that serves as a certified upper bound on worst-case error probability. The base-rules are bounded measurable functions in [−1,1] with the symmetry property that −h ∈ H whenever h ∈ H.

## Method

RMBoost learns a linear combination of base-rules by solving a minimax problem that minimizes worst-case 0-1 loss over an uncertainty set of distributions whose base-rule expectations deviate from the empirical average by at most λ in the infinity norm. The key equivalence (Theorem 1) reduces this to a linear program: minimize 1/2 − (1/n)Σ y_i h̄(x_i)ᵀμ + λ‖μ‖₁ subject to |h̄(x)ᵀμ| ≤ 1/2 for all x, which simultaneously maximizes average margin while bounding individual margins. An efficient column-generation algorithm (Algorithm 1) greedily selects base-rules by solving low-dimensional LPs at each round, using dual solutions to generate weighted samples for the base learner. Theoretical guarantees (Theorems 2–4) show finite-sample error bounds that degrade gracefully with general label noise (quantified via P_noise and Var{ρ_y(x)}), establish Bayes consistency when base-rules can approximate the Bayes rule, and provide high-probability bounds scaling as O(√((log n)/n)) with VC-dimension of the base-rule family.

## Results

On 8 UCI datasets, RMBoost achieves competitive noiseless classification error (e.g., 12% on Raisin, 7.5% on Climate, 20% on Blood Transfusion) while exhibiting substantially smaller degradation under 10% uniform/symmetric label noise compared to AdaBoost (e.g., +2% vs +4% on Raisin, +2% vs +4% on Credit-G). The minimax risk optimized during learning closely tracks the actual RMBoost error in practice (e.g., 14% predicted vs 12% actual on Raisin; 9.3% predicted vs 7.5% actual on Climate), validating the theoretical guarantees. Under adversarial noise (flipping labels of the highest-margin samples), RMBoost degrades only mildly while AdaBoost's error increases sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to AdaBoost, RMBoost achieves comparable or better noiseless accuracy on 4 of 8 datasets (Blood, Raisin, Climate, Credit-G) and is within 1–3% on the rest, but shows markedly smaller error degradation under 10% label noise (e.g., +2% vs +4% on Raisin and Credit-G; +2% vs +3.5% on Climate). Against LogitBoost, RMBoost is consistently more robust under noise, with smaller error increases across all 8 datasets (e.g., +2% vs +4% on Raisin, +2% vs +4% on Credit-G). XGBoost with quadratic potential (XGB-Q) is the weakest baseline under noise, degrading by 4–8% on several datasets (e.g., +8% on QSAR, +6% on Credit-G), while RMBoost degrades by only 2–3%. The minimax risk classifier baseline (Minmax, [19]) provides a lower-variance reference but generally achieves higher absolute error than RMBoost in both noiseless and noisy settings (e.g., 20% vs 22% on Titanic noiseless; 24% vs 22% on Titanic noisy). Overall, RMBoost occupies a favorable position on the accuracy–robustness trade-off, matching AdaBoost-level accuracy while approaching the robustness of dedicated noise-tolerant methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' prior minimax classification framework (Mazuelas et al., JMLR 2023; NeurIPS 2020) to the boosting setting, where classification rules are constrained to linear combinations of base-rules. It departs fundamentally from the standard ERM-based boosting paradigm (AdaBoost, LogitBoost) and from existing robust boosting methods (RobustBoost, BrownBoost) that bypass the Long-Servedio negative result by switching to non-convex or unbounded potentials. Instead of selecting a potential function, RMBoost defines an uncertainty set over distributions using the base-rule family and directly minimizes worst-case 0-1 error, connecting distributionally robust optimization with boosting in a way that avoids the potential-function selection entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant theoretical gap: prior robust boosting results only covered symmetric/uniform noise with infinite-sample analysis, while RMBoost provides finite-sample guarantees for arbitrary (including adversarial) label noise and Bayes consistency. The minimax risk obtained during learning also serves as a practical error estimate, which is useful for practitioners. However, the column-generation algorithm scales poorly with training size compared to AdaBoost or LogitBoost, and the empirical gains over strong baselines are modest, leaving the development of more efficient solvers as an important open question for broader adoption.
