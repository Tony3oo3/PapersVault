## Problem

3D gaze estimation methods trained on limited, domain-specific labeled datasets suffer severe performance degradation when deployed in the wild due to domain bias. Existing cross-domain strategies (domain adaptation, domain generalization) and self-supervised pre-training remain constrained by the limited diversity of available labeled data, and no semi-supervised framework has been developed to effectively harness large-scale unlabeled face images for gaze estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253538/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model takes a single 224×224 RGB face image (normalized via 5-landmark alignment and PnP-based head pose estimation) and regresses 3D gaze direction as yaw and pitch angles. During training, the reward model additionally consumes CLIP visual embeddings and scene-specific gaze descriptions generated by InstructBLIP (encoded via CLIP's text encoder) to score pseudo-label reliability. At inference, the reward model is entirely discarded, so deployment incurs no additional architectural or computational overhead beyond the base ViT student.

## Method

OMNIGAZE is a three-phase semi-supervised pipeline: (1) a ViT teacher is trained on labeled gaze data; (2) the teacher generates pseudo-labels for 1.4M diverse unlabeled face images from six sources; (3) a student model is jointly trained on labeled and pseudo-labeled data, guided by a dedicated reward model that scores pseudo-label reliability. The reward model integrates three multimodal cues—CLIP visual features, MLLM-generated scene-specific gaze descriptions (fused via cross-attention), and pseudo-labels interpolated into 3D direction vectors via spherical-to-Cartesian transformation—to produce confidence scores in [0,1]. These scores are used both to filter out low-confidence pseudo-labels (threshold τ=0.5) and to reweight the unsupervised loss, while a periodic teacher refresh every K=10 epochs progressively refines pseudo-label quality. The key insight is that regression-oriented pseudo-label selection (unlike classification thresholding) requires a learned, geometry-aware, and semantically grounded confidence scorer rather than a fixed threshold.

## Results

Under the in-domain setting, OMNIGAZE achieves angular errors of 2.97° (MPIIFaceGaze), 4.07° (EyeDiap), 5.40° (RT-Gene), 9.12° (Gaze360), and 6.72° (IVGaze), outperforming the best prior methods by 0.64°, 0.71°, 1.15°, 0.48°, and 0.34° respectively. In the cross-domain setting (e.g., ETH-XGaze→MPIIFaceGaze), OMNIGAZE reduces error to 5.07° versus 5.91° for the best prior method (AGG). In the zero-shot setting, OMNIGAZE-Base (ViT-B) achieves 3.44° on MPIIFaceGaze and 4.31° on EyeDiap without any fine-tuning on the target dataset, surpassing even in-domain-trained methods like GazeTR (4.00°) and Gaze360 (4.06°) on MPIIFaceGaze.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMNIGAZE outperforms all 22 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. In the in-domain setting, it beats the strongest prior methods—AGE-Net (3.61°) and GazeTR (4.00°) on MPIIFaceGaze, AGE-Net (4.78°) on EyeDiap, GazeTR (6.55°) on RT-Gene, 3DGazeNet (9.60°) on Gaze360, and XGaze (7.06°) on IVGaze—by margins of 0.34°–1.15°. In cross-domain transfer (e.g., ETH-XGaze→EyeDiap), OMNIGAZE achieves 4.84° versus 6.75° for AGG and 6.91° for RAT, a gap of ~2°. In the zero-shot setting, OMNIGAZE-Base (3.44° on MPIIFaceGaze) surpasses DINO-B (6.52°), ViT-B (6.17°), and FaRL-B (6.09°) by over 2.6°, and even exceeds in-domain-trained GazeTR (4.00°) and Gaze360 (4.06°) without any target-domain fine-tuning. The same-backbone comparison (ResNet-18) confirms that OMNIGAZE's gains (3.46° vs. 4.48° baseline on MPIIFaceGaze) stem from the semi-supervised strategy rather than architectural capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the semi-supervised pseudo-labeling paradigm (teacher → pseudo-label → student) from classification tasks (FixMatch, FlexMatch, SemiReward) to the regression setting of 3D gaze estimation, where threshold-based filtering is ill-suited for continuous outputs. It departs from prior cross-domain gaze estimation work (domain adaptation, domain generalization) that remains constrained by labeled data diversity, instead leveraging 1.4M unlabeled face images from six heterogeneous sources. The key methodological departure is a multimodal reward model that jointly reasons over CLIP visual features, MLLM-generated scene-specific gaze descriptions, and spherical-to-Cartesian 3D gaze vectors to score pseudo-label reliability — a design not previously applied to gaze estimation or similar regression SSL problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that semi-supervised training with reward-filtered pseudo-labels can close the gap between in-domain and cross-domain gaze estimation, and even achieve zero-shot performance on unseen datasets that rivals or exceeds prior in-domain results (e.g., 3.44° zero-shot on MPIIFaceGaze vs. 4.00° in-domain for 3DGazeNet). This positions OMNIGAZE as a practical data engine for generating gaze annotations in the wild, addressing the fundamental bottleneck of scarce diverse labeled data. However, the ablation revealing that MLLM-generated gaze descriptions contribute only ~0.4° in isolation (vs. ~2.7° from the full pipeline) suggests the multimodal semantic cue is a modest refinement rather than a critical component, and the approach's dependence on a complex multi-stage training protocol (teacher training, periodic pseudo-label updates, joint reward-model training) limits its ease of adoption. Open questions include whether the reward-model-based pseudo-label scoring generalizes to other regression tasks and whether the 1.4M unlabeled data scale is truly necessary or whether diversity alone suffices.
