## Problem

Deep transformer models suffer from over-smoothing, where token representations converge to near-identical values as layers stack, limiting model depth. Unlike in GNNs where this was first identified and widely studied, the over-smoothing in transformers has not been adequately analyzed from a dynamical-systems perspective. This paper shows that the implicit dynamics of stacked attention layers are equivalent to graph neural diffusion on a complete graph—a dissipative process that monotonically erases high-frequency information—and asks whether replacing this with energy-conserving wave dynamics can mitigate the problem.

## Input / Output

Wavy Transformer is a drop-in replacement for the residual connection inside standard transformer blocks, so it accepts the same inputs (token sequences up to 128 tokens for NLP, 224×224 images for CV, or node feature matrices for sparse graphs) and produces the same outputs (MLM predictions, class labels, or node scores). The only architectural addition is a velocity tensor Y of the same shape as the hidden state X, plus a trainable mixing vector λ ∈ [0,1]^d (or a scalar in the Light Wave variant). The Light Wave variant adds essentially zero parameters beyond a single scalar, while Full Wave adds a velocity-specific FFN and LN branch.

## Method

The paper first proves that the standard residual attention update X^{l+1} = AX^l + X^l is a discrete-time graph neural diffusion on a complete graph, where the attention matrix A defines anisotropic edge weights. It then replaces this first-order dissipative dynamics with a second-order wave equation ∂²X/∂t² = (A−I)X, discretized via a symplectic (energy-conserving) integrator that introduces an explicit velocity variable Y: Y^{l+1} = τ(A−I)X^l + Y^l, X^{l+1} = τY^{l+1} + X^l. To maintain the physical state-velocity relationship Y = ∂X/∂t under the chain rule, the authors design a velocity-specific layer normalization (scaling only, no shift) and a velocity FFN that applies the activation derivative φ′ rather than φ. Two variants are offered: Full Wave (explicit velocity branch with its own FFN and LN) and Light Wave (momentum-only, adding just the term λ(X^l − X^{l−1}) to the standard diffusive update). A trainable mixing coefficient λ = sigmoid(θ) blends the diffusive and wavy paths, allowing the model to learn the optimal balance per feature dimension.

## Results

On ImageNet with DeiT-Tiny (12 layers), the Light Wave residual improves Top-1 accuracy from 72.17% (Diffusion baseline) to 73.09% (+0.92), and the Full Wave variant reaches 72.33% (+0.16). On GLUE, the Mix(+Light) residual attains the best macro average of 66.12, surpassing the Diffusion baseline (64.13) by +1.99, with the largest gain on STS-B (+12.65). On sparse-graph benchmarks, adding the Light Wave residual to DIFFormer at 7 layers on OGBN-Arxiv recovers accuracy from 24.44% to 66.73% (+42.29), and on OGBN-Proteins at 5 layers improves ROC-AUC from 69.42% to 80.14% (+10.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DeiT-Tiny diffusion baseline from Nguyen et al. [29], Wavy Transformer (Light Wave) improves Top-1 accuracy by 0.92 points (73.09% vs. 72.17%) with no additional parameters, and the Full Wave variant adds 0.16 points. When combined with FeatScale [43], the Full Wave residual further lifts accuracy from 72.35% to 72.62% (+0.26), demonstrating that Wavy Transformer is complementary to existing over-smoothing mitigations. On the deeper CaiT-XXS-24 model [40], the Diffuse+Full Wave residual improves Top-1 from 77.6% to 78.6% (+1.0) while reducing parameters from 12.0M to 11.1M. On sparse-graph tasks using DIFFormer [44], the Light Wave residual dramatically mitigates depth collapse: at 7 layers on OGBN-Arxiv, accuracy jumps from 24.44% to 66.73% (+42.29), and on OGBN-Proteins at 5 layers, ROC-AUC rises from 69.42% to 80.14% (+10.72), indicating that the wave residual is most impactful when the diffusive baseline has already collapsed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper establishes a formal equivalence between the residual dynamics of stacked attention layers and graph neural diffusion (GRAND) on a complete graph, then replaces the underlying first-order diffusion PDE with a second-order wave equation to derive the Wavy Transformer update. It departs from prior over-smoothing mitigations for transformers (FeatScale, regularized nonlocal functionals) that inject high-frequency signals externally, and from GNN-based wave/PDE approaches (Graph-CON, PDE-GCN) that operate on sparse graphs, by providing the first intrinsic, physics-grounded mechanism specifically for all-to-all attention. The physically consistent velocity-specific LN and FFN design draws on the chain-rule relationship between state and velocity, extending the standard transformer block in a way that preserves the symplectic (energy-conserving) structure of the discrete wave update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diffusion-to-wave reinterpretation gives a clear physical explanation for why transformers over-smooth (energy dissipation) and why the proposed fix works (energy conservation), which could guide future architecture design beyond this specific block. Practical limitations temper the impact: the Full Wave variant halves inference throughput, the NLP/CV gains are modest (+1.99 GLUE avg, +0.92 ImageNet Top-1), and the causal-attention experiment is inconclusive. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very deep (100+ layer) models, whether the single-scalar mixing coefficient λ suffices for high-dimensional feature spaces, and whether the physical interpretation holds when the attention matrix is time-dependent (as it is in practice).
