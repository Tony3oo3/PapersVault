## Problem

Existing deep anomaly detection methods for tabular data face a trilemma: GAN-based approaches suffer training instability, diffusion-based methods (e.g., DTE) incur prohibitive multi-step inference costs, and most models operate as black boxes without interpretability or theoretical robustness guarantees. Flow matching, a recent generative framework with stable training and efficient sampling, had not been explored for anomaly detection, leaving a gap between its theoretical promise and practical deployment in high-stakes, large-scale tabular settings.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a d-dimensional standardized tabular vector z concatenated with a sinusoidal time embedding of a scalar t ∈ [0,1], and produces a d-dimensional residual vector f_θ([z; Embed(t)]) + z. The anomaly score is the L2 norm of this residual (a single scalar), while the absolute values of its entries directly yield per-feature importance weights without any post-hoc attribution method. Inference requires exactly one forward pass at a fixed evaluation time (default t=1), eliminating the iterative sampling bottleneck of diffusion-based competitors.

## Method

TCCM learns a time-conditioned contraction velocity field f_θ parameterized by a 3-layer MLP (256 hidden units, ReLU) that maps the augmented input [z; Embed(t)] to a vector approximating −z, the direction toward the origin. Training minimizes the L1 loss ‖f_θ([z; Embed(t)]) + z‖₂ over z ~ p_data and t ~ U(0,1), using a constant supervision target −z at all time steps rather than simulating or integrating an ODE trajectory. This design decouples the model from trajectory reconstruction: each time point independently learns how to contract the current state toward the origin, and the sinusoidal time embedding allows the network to modulate contraction magnitude and direction non-linearly over t. At test time, the anomaly score is the residual norm at a single fixed time, and because the residual lives in the original feature space, its entries provide intrinsic feature-wise attribution. Theoretically, the score is (L+1)-Lipschitz continuous when f_θ is L-Lipschitz, yielding certifiable robustness under bounded perturbations.

## Results

On the ADBench benchmark (47 datasets, 5 seeds, 10,575 runs), TCCM achieves the best average rank across all 45 detectors: 5.8 for AUPRC and 5.7 for AUROC, outperforming DTE-NonParametric (rank 2), LUNAR (rank 3), and KDE (rank 4). In inference speed, TCCM is 1,573× faster than DTE-NonParametric, 85× faster than LUNAR, and 4,864× faster than KDE on average; on the largest dataset (census, 299,285×500), TCCM completes inference in 1.50 s versus 48,942 s for DTE. Total end-to-end runtime (training + inference) is 79.5× lower than DTE, 312× lower than KDE, and 50.9× lower than LUNAR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCCM outperforms all 44 baselines in aggregate AUPRC and AUROC ranking across 47 ADBench datasets. The closest competitors are DTE-NonParametric (diffusion-based, rank 2) and LUNAR (GNN-based, rank 3), both of which TCCM surpasses in detection accuracy while being 1,573× and 85× faster in inference, respectively. KDE (rank 4) is 4,864× slower in inference than TCCM. In training, DTE-NonParametric is faster than TCCM (0.06× TCCM's training time), but TCCM still achieves the lowest total runtime among all top-4 methods. Classical baselines (OCSVM, LOF, PCA, IForest, etc.) and deep baselines (GANomaly, DAGMM, DeepSVDD, OneFlow, ICL, MCM, ALAD, DROCC) are all outperformed in aggregate ranking. On the MNIST explainability demo (digit 1 normal vs. digit 7 anomaly), TCCM achieves AUROC 0.76 while producing intrinsic feature-level attributions that highlight the distinguishing horizontal stroke.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCCM is inspired by flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) and its rectified-flow variant (Liu et al., 2022), which learn velocity fields to transport samples between distributions via ODE integration. The key departure is that TCCM abandons full trajectory simulation: instead of integrating a learned velocity field over [0,1], it trains a time-conditioned MLP to predict a single contraction vector toward the origin at each time step, supervised by the constant target −z. This positions TCCM as a deliberate simplification of flow matching for anomaly detection, avoiding the iterative ODE/SDE solvers that make diffusion-based detectors like DTE and Anomaly-DDPM slow, while also sidestepping the adversarial instability of GAN-based methods (AnoGAN, GANomaly) and the invertibility/Jacobian requirements of normalizing flows (OneFlow).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a single forward pass at inference yields a 1,573× speedup over the prior best-accuracy method (DTE-NonParametric) while achieving top-1 AUPRC and AUROC across 47 ADBench datasets, making real-time anomaly detection on large-scale tabular data feasible. The intrinsic feature-wise interpretability (the residual vector lives in input space) and the Lipschitz-continuity guarantee address two long-standing gaps in deep anomaly detection—explainability and certified robustness—that are critical for deployment in finance, healthcare, and cybersecurity. Open questions include whether the one-step scoring loses discriminative information compared to full trajectory integration on more complex distributions, whether the approach extends naturally to non-tabular modalities (time series, graphs), and how performance degrades under significant distributional shift beyond the GMM setting analyzed theoretically.
