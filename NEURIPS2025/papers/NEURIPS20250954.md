## Problem

Integrating dense image segmentation into unified multimodal large language models (MLLMs) remains challenging: existing approaches either discretize masks into boundary point sequences (losing fine-grained detail) or condition dedicated segmentation decoders (e.g., SAM) on MLLM outputs, which prevents the LLM from truly learning pixel-level visual understanding. The paper asks whether segmentation can be achieved entirely through the MLLM's own generative capacity without any task-specific segmentation head.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250954/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at arbitrary resolution along with natural language instructions, and optionally interactive prompts (points and scribbles fed as binary mask images, bounding boxes as text). Outputs are produced as 256×256 pixel images decoded from 680 discrete visual tokens via a frozen VAR multi-scale VQ-VAE, yielding segmentation masks, generated images, or text responses depending on the task. A unified classification head over the extended vocabulary (text tokens + visual codebook) generates all output modalities, with deterministic argmax sampling for segmentation and classifier-free guidance with top-k sampling for image generation.

## Method

ARGenSeg integrates segmentation into an MLLM (InternVL 2.5) by training it to directly predict quantized image tokens from a frozen VAR multi-scale VQ tokenizer, whose codebook is appended to the LLM vocabulary. The key architectural insight is that the MLLM's classification head outputs visual token IDs directly—rather than semantic embeddings routed to a separate decoder—making segmentation fully dependent on the model's pixel-level understanding. For efficiency, a next-scale prediction strategy generates all h_k×w_k tokens at each of K=10 scales in parallel (coarse-to-fine), with a lightweight linear generation projector upsampled from the previous scale serving as the query for the next. Training uses single-stage supervised fine-tuning on 402K segmentation and 1.25M understanding samples with cross-entropy loss, keeping the vision encoder and VQ tokenizer frozen to prevent reliance on external decoders. Ablations confirm that direct token prediction (vs. a DiT diffusion head) is essential for pixel-level accuracy, and that the multi-scale scheme improves both speed (1.28s vs 5.50s) and robustness over single-scale generation.

## Results

On Referring Expression Segmentation, ARGenSeg achieves cIoU of 82.2/77.9/78.4 on RefCOCO/+/g val splits without in-domain fine-tuning, and 86.3/82.3/81.7 after fine-tuning, surpassing the previous SOTA HiMTok (85.0/79.7/80.0 ft) while training on only 402K segmentation samples versus HiMTok's 2.91M. On the more challenging gRefCOCO benchmark, ARGenSeg reaches an average cIoU of 72.4, outperforming the best prior method SAM4MLLM (69.1). Inference takes 1.28s per 256×256 mask on an A100 GPU, achieving >10× speedup over sequential generation (Emu3: 59.4s) and ~2× speedup over VARGPT (2.64s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARGenSeg outperforms the previous generation-based SOTA, HiMTok, on all RefCOCO/+/g splits in both the mixed-training and fine-tuned settings (e.g., 86.3 vs. 85.0 cIoU on RefCOCO val after ft), while using only 402K segmentation samples versus HiMTok's 2.91M. Against dedicated-segmentation-head methods, ARGenSeg (non-ft) surpasses u-LLAVA (83.0→82.2 on RefCOCO val, but 77.9 vs. 77.1 on RefCOCO+ val and 78.4 vs. 77.1 on RefCOCOg val) and PSALM (83.6→82.2 on RefCOCO val, but 77.9 vs. 72.9 on RefCOCO+ val and 78.4 vs. 73.8 on RefCOCOg val), showing the largest gains on the semantically harder RefCOCO+/g. On gRefCOCO, ARGenSeg (72.4 avg cIoU) beats SAM4MLLM (69.1) and HiMTok (66.9) by 3.3 and 5.5 points respectively. In inference speed, ARGenSeg (1.28s) is ~1.5× faster than HiMTok (1.89s) and ~2× faster than VARGPT (2.64s) while producing more tokens (680 vs. 32 for HiMTok), and >10× faster than Emu3 (59.4s). The ablation in Table 10 confirms that the performance gap over HiMTok is not merely due to a stronger backbone: with the same InternVL-2.5 backbone, ARGenSeg's multi-scale VQ generation outperforms HiMTok's single-scale VQ by 1.1–2.6 cIoU points.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARGenSeg departs from the dominant MLLM-segmentation paradigm (LISA, GLaMM, PSALM, SAM4MLLM) that relies on dedicated segmentation decoders conditioned on special tokens or hidden states, and from boundary-point representations (PolyFormer, VistaLLM) that lose fine-grained boundary detail. It also diverges from prior generation-based segmentation attempts: UniGS uses a diffusion U-Net that lacks understanding, and HiMTok requires a task-specific mask tokenizer. The key architectural lineage is VAR's next-scale prediction for efficient autoregressive image generation, combined with a universal VQ-VAE tokenizer (as in Emu3, Janus, VARGPT), but ARGenSeg is the first to have the MLLM directly predict these visual tokens for segmentation without any auxiliary decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating task-specific segmentation heads, ARGenSeg demonstrates that a single unified MLLM can handle segmentation, interactive segmentation, and image generation through the same token-prediction mechanism, simplifying the architecture and enabling natural extension to new tasks (e.g., anomaly detection, depth estimation) with minimal additional data. The practical efficiency gain (1.28s per mask vs. 59.4s for sequential generation) makes autoregressive segmentation viable for real-time applications. Open questions include scaling output resolution beyond 256×256, whether the approach generalizes to higher-fidelity tasks like image editing, and whether the modest understanding degradation on some benchmarks (Table 8) persists with larger training corpora.
