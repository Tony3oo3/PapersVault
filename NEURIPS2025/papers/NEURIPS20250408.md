key_contribution: Introduces T-REG, a regularization that maximizes the length of the Minimum Spanning Tree over learned embeddings (coupled with a soft sphere constraint), with theoretical proofs that it prevents dimensional collapse and promotes uniformity on compact Riemannian manifolds, and extends it to self-supervised learning as T-REGS, achieving competitive or improved results on CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100/1k, and CLIP fine-tuning for image-text retrieval.
limitations: The MST computation via Kruskal's algorithm has O(B²(D·log B)) complexity, which may become a bottleneck at very large batch sizes, and the method requires careful hyperparameter tuning (β ≥ γ ≥ λ) to avoid representation collapse. The theoretical uniformity guarantees are proven for the sphere constraint specifically, and empirical validation is limited to standard vision benchmarks and one multi-modal (CLIP) setting, leaving open questions about generalization to other manifolds and modalities.

surprise_factor: The main conceptual contribution—connecting MST length maximization to entropy maximization and hence to uniformity on arbitrary compact Riemannian manifolds—is elegant but not deeply surprising to readers familiar with TDA and dimension estimation. ## Problem

Self-supervised learning representations suffer from dimensional collapse (embeddings occupying a low-dimensional subspace) and non-uniform distribution, yet existing remedies have notable limitations: contrastive methods require large batch sizes, redundancy-reduction methods only leverage second-order statistics and are blind to density concentration, and the W2-based uniformity metric of Fang et al. is computationally expensive and numerically unstable. The paper asks whether a simpler, more efficient regularization can simultaneously prevent collapse and enforce uniformity with theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two randomly augmented views of an RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) or an image–text pair (for the CLIP fine-tuning experiment), and produces a pair of learned embeddings in a high-dimensional space (typically 1024-d). The key structural detail is that the MST is computed over the entire batch of embeddings (e.g., 256–2048 points), so the regularization operates at the batch level rather than per-sample, and a soft sphere constraint keeps all embeddings near the unit hypersphere rather than enforcing a hard normalization.

## Method

T-REGS regularizes each embedding branch independently by maximizing the length of the Minimum Spanning Tree (MST) over the batch of embeddings (loss L_E) while applying a soft sphere constraint (loss L_S) to prevent trivial dilation; the two terms are combined with hyperparameters γ and λ. This T-REG loss is then added to a view-invariance objective (MSE for standalone use, or the native SSL loss of BYOL, Barlow Twins, etc. for auxiliary use), yielding the full T-REGS objective. Theoretically, the paper proves that for small samples (n ≤ d+1) the MST-length maximum is attained at the vertices of a regular simplex on the sphere, and for large samples the asymptotic MST length is governed by the Rényi (d−1)/d-entropy of the sampling density, which is maximized by the uniform distribution on any compact Riemannian manifold. In practice, the MST is computed via Kruskal's algorithm (GPU-parallelized through torchph), and its gradient is available in closed form: each MST edge exerts a pairwise repulsive force proportional to the unit vector between its endpoints.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k (linear probing, ResNet-50, 100 epochs), T-REGS as a standalone regularizer (L_MSE + L_T-REGS) achieves 68.8% top-1 accuracy at batch size 512, outperforming VICReg (68.3%), SimSiam (68.1%), and MoCo v2 (67.4%), though slightly below INTL (69.5%) and Zero-CL (68.9%). As an auxiliary loss, T-REGS improves BYOL from 66.5% to 67.2% and Barlow Twins from 67.7% to 67.8% on ImageNet-1k. On CIFAR-100, BYOL + T-REGS gains +1.5% over the BYOL baseline (65.2% vs. 63.7%), and on MS-COCO image-text retrieval, CLIP + T-REGS achieves 41.6% R@1 (t→i), surpassing m3-Mix (41.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a standalone regularizer at batch size 512, T-REGS (L_MSE + L_T-REGS) achieves 68.8% on ImageNet-1k, beating VICReg (68.3%), SimSiam (68.1%), MoCo v2 (67.4%), CW-RGP (67.1%), and W-MSE (65.1%), but trailing INTL (69.5%) and Zero-CL (68.9%) which use larger batch sizes (512 and 1024 respectively). As an auxiliary loss, T-REGS consistently improves its host method: BYOL gains +0.7% on ImageNet-1k (66.5→67.2) and +1.5% on CIFAR-100 (63.7→65.2), while Barlow Twins gains +0.1% on ImageNet-1k (67.7→67.8) and +0.3% on CIFAR-100 (68.2→68.5). On CIFAR-10, T-REGS as standalone achieves 91.3%, within 0.1% of the best W2-regularized approach (91.4%). In the multi-modal setting, CLIP + T-REGS outperforms m3-Mix on MS-COCO in both retrieval directions (41.6% vs. 41.0% R@1 t→i; 41.5% vs. 39.9% R@1 i→t) and on Flickr30k i→t (83.2% vs. 82.3% R@1), though it trails m3-Mix on Flickr30k t→i (80.8% vs. 82.7% R@1). The paper also shows T-REGS is competitive with W2-regularized methods from Fang et al. [23] on CIFAR-10/100, matching or exceeding them across all tested base methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

T-REGS is positioned as a computationally simpler and more stable alternative to the W2 optimal-transport uniformity metric of Fang et al. (ICLR 2024), which the authors show shares the same four principled properties (instance permutation, instance cloning, feature cloning, feature baby constraints) but suffers from numerical instabilities in its closed-form SVD-based computation. The MST length maximization loss draws directly on the TDA framework of Carrière et al. (ICML 2021) for differentiable optimization of persistent-homology-based functionals, and on Steele's classical result linking MST growth rates to intrinsic dimension. Within the broader JE-SSL landscape, T-REGS sits alongside redundancy-reduction methods (VICReg, Barlow Twins, INTL) and contrastive methods (SimCLR, MoCo, SwAV), but departs from them by operating on the full geometric structure of the embedding point cloud rather than second-order statistics or pairwise distances to negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical bridge between MST optimization, Rényi entropy maximization, and uniform distribution on compact Riemannian manifolds, giving practitioners a principled, easy-to-implement regularization with provable anti-collapse and uniformity guarantees. Empirically, T-REGS matches or modestly exceeds W2-regularized baselines on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-100/1k, and improves CLIP fine-tuning for cross-modal retrieval, validating its utility as both a standalone and auxiliary loss. Open questions include whether the soft sphere constraint can be generalized to other compact manifolds without re-deriving the theoretical guarantees, how the method behaves at very large batch sizes where MST computation cost grows quadratically, and whether the hyperparameter sensitivity (β ≥ γ ≥ λ required to avoid collapse) can be reduced through adaptive scheduling.
