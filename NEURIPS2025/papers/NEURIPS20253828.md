## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving (E2E-AD) systems are highly sensitive to camera viewpoint changes caused by different vehicle types, sensor mounting positions, and rig configurations, yet no prior work has addressed this robustness gap. Collecting annotated data from every possible vehicle configuration is impractical, and E2E-AD networks trained on a fixed camera setup degrade significantly under even modest viewpoint shifts (e.g., ±10° pitch, ±1 m height).

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images from surround cameras along with their extrinsics as input. During training, novel-view images are synthesized online via feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting from randomly sampled camera poses (pitch ∈ [−10°, 5°], height ∈ [−0.7 m, 1.0 m], depth ∈ [−0.2 m, 1.0 m]), requiring no additional annotations. The perception-planning head is randomly assigned to either the original or a synthesized novel view each training step, and the system outputs an ego trajectory, 3D object detections, a road map, plus auxiliary depth maps and Gaussian primitives.

## Method

VR-Drive integrates feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting as a joint auxiliary task within the E2E-AD pipeline: a shared ResNet-50 encoder extracts image features, a jointly learned depth head provides 3D positions, and a Gaussian network predicts per-pixel scaling, rotation, opacity, and color to enable online novel-view rendering without scene-specific optimization. Two viewpoint-robust modules are inserted into the sparse perception pipeline: a Viewpoint-Mixed Memory Bank that stores instance features from continuously changing novel views and fuses them with current-view features via cross-attention, and a Viewpoint-Consistent Distillation strategy that projects learnable 3D keypoints from anchor boxes onto both original and novel view feature maps, then aligns the sampled features with an MSE loss restricted to high-confidence anchors. A cyclic reconstruction loss (rendering the original view from a novel one) provides rendering supervision when paired ground-truth views are unavailable, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with a unified loss combining detection, mapping, depth, motion, planning, and rendering objectives.

## Results

On nuScenes open-loop planning, VR-Drive achieves 0.60 m average L2 error and 0.06% average collision rate on the original camera view, comparable to DiffusionDrive (0.57 m, 0.08%) and SparseDrive (0.61 m, 0.08%). Under unseen camera viewpoints (pitch, height, depth shifts), VR-Drive maintains 0.68 m average L2 and 0.11% collision rate, substantially outperforming all image-based baselines: DiffusionDrive degrades to 1.17 m / 0.41%, SparseDrive to 0.95 m / 0.31%, and VAD to 0.78 m / 0.42%. In closed-loop CARLA evaluation (Town05-Nov), VR-Drive achieves a Driving Score of 88.25 and Route Completion of 98.28% on unseen viewpoints, versus 48.25 / 94.87% for the ablated baseline and 7.80 / 28.86% for BEV-Planner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among image-based E2E-AD methods, VR-Drive is the only one that maintains near-original performance under unseen camera viewpoints. On the original view, VR-Drive (0.60 m L2, 0.06% collision) is on par with DiffusionDrive (0.57 m, 0.08%) and SparseDrive (0.61 m, 0.08%), and slightly better than VAD (0.72 m, 0.22%). Under unseen viewpoints, the gap widens dramatically: VR-Drive's 0.68 m L2 is 42% lower than DiffusionDrive (1.17 m), 28% lower than SparseDrive (0.95 m), and 13% lower than VAD (0.78 m); its 0.11% collision rate is roughly 3–4× lower than all image-based baselines. BEV-Planner* achieves a lower unseen L2 (0.57 m) but at the cost of a 4.3× higher collision rate (0.47% vs. 0.11%), and it relies on ego-status input. AD-MLP* reports identical numbers across all viewpoints because it consumes ego-status rather than camera images, making it a non-comparable baseline for viewpoint robustness. In closed-loop CARLA, VR-Drive's 88.25 DS on unseen views exceeds the ablated baseline (48.25) by 83% and outperforms BEV-Planner (7.80) by over 10×, confirming that the advantage generalizes beyond open-loop metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

VR-Drive sits at the intersection of two research threads: end-to-end autonomous driving (building on DiffusionDrive's motion planner, Sparse4D's sparse detection architecture, and DETR3D's 3D-to-2D query mechanism) and feed-forward 3D scene reconstruction (building on 3D Gaussian Splatting and pixel-wise feed-forward variants like DrivingForward and MVSplat). The key departure from prior work is twofold: (1) rather than treating novel view synthesis as a separate offline pipeline, VR-Drive integrates feed-forward 3DGS as a jointly-trained auxiliary task within the E2E-AD framework, and (2) it introduces the viewpoint-mixed memory bank and viewpoint-consistent distillation to enforce feature consistency across viewpoints—mechanisms absent from prior viewpoint-robust perception work that focused on 2D/3D detection in isolation. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to address camera viewpoint variation within the E2E-AD paradigm, noting that prior viewpoint-robustness studies targeted perception tasks rather than full planning pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Camera viewpoint variation is an unavoidable real-world deployment challenge (different vehicle types, sensor mounts, and configurations), yet no prior E2E-AD system has been evaluated or designed for it. VR-Drive's new benchmark (Town05-Nov and the nuScenes viewpoint-variation protocol) provides the community with a standardized way to measure this robustness gap, and the finding that existing methods degrade dramatically under modest changes (e.g., DiffusionDrive's L2 error more than doubles under +1m height) underscores the practical urgency. The limitation that performance depends on accurate camera calibration, and the reliance on offline scene optimization to generate test-time viewpoints (rather than truly novel hardware), leave open questions about robustness to calibration drift and extreme viewpoint shifts. The broader implication is that 3D scene understanding as an auxiliary task may serve as a general-purpose regularizer for E2E-AD, a direction worth exploring beyond viewpoint augmentation.
