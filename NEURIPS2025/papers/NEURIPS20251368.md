## Problem

Deep learning methods for medical image reconstruction typically require paired measurement-image data, which is often unavailable in prospective clinical settings. Training on retrospectively simulated pairs leads to performance degradation on real prospective data due to the retrospective-to-prospective gap caused by incomplete imaging knowledge in simulation. This paper addresses the challenge of learning reconstruction from unpaired real measurements while respecting acquisition physics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251368/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unpaired undersampled k-space measurements (MRI at 4× or 10× acceleration) or low-dose CT projections (~10× dose reduction) as input, along with unpaired high-quality reference images from the target distribution. The forward model A (Fourier transform for MRI, Radon transform for CT) must be known and is embedded directly into the transport dynamics. Output is a reconstructed image produced by evolving the measurement along a discrete N-step transport path (N=12 in practice), with the transport network implemented as an unfolding architecture (E2E-VarNet, PromptMR, or MC-CDic depending on modality).

## Method

KIDOT formulates reconstruction as a dynamic optimal transport problem where the path from measurement distribution P to image distribution Q is governed by two physics-informed components: (1) a non-metric data-fidelity cost c(x,y)=‖y−A(x)‖₁ integrated along the path, and (2) a transport ODE that is the gradient flow of the inverse problem, combining the adjoint data-consistency term A*(A(I_t)−I₀) with a learned regularization gradient ∇R(I_t) parameterized by a neural network H_φ. The endpoint distribution constraint I₁~Q is enforced via the Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual of the W₁ distance, parameterized by a potential network φ_θ. Training alternates between updating φ (minimizing transport cost + potential + optional supervised loss on simulated pairs) and θ (maximizing the W₁ dual). The paper proves existence of a minimizer under compactness and continuity assumptions, and validates the framework on simulated MRI, prospective brain MRI, and clinical low-dose CT.

## Results

On the simulated fastMRI knee dataset (4× acceleration), KIDOT achieves the best performance across all four metrics: PSNR 33.31 dB, SSIM 0.8518, FID 10.68, and KID×100 0.83, outperforming the closest unpaired competitor UAR (PSNR 33.01, SSIM 0.8425, FID 19.56, KID×100 1.05) and the supervised E2E-VarNet baseline (PSNR 32.56, SSIM 0.8372, FID 24.35, KID×100 1.29). On the real prospective brain MRI dataset (10× acceleration, T2-FLAIR), KIDOT attains FID 28.26 and KID×100 1.12, surpassing all DDS variants (best: fine-tuned DDS at FID 39.78, KID×100 1.84) and UAR (FID 29.09, KID×100 1.23). On clinical prospective low-dose CT (10-fold dose reduction), KIDOT achieves FID 22.25 and KID×100 1.32, beating UAR (FID 23.82, KID×100 1.45) and the supervised ISTA-Net (FID 24.86, KID×100 1.59).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KIDOT outperforms all ten baselines across all three datasets and all reported metrics. The closest competitor is UAR [37], a variational unpaired method, which KIDOT beats by 0.30 dB PSNR and 0.0093 SSIM on simulated MRI, and by 0.83 FID / 0.11 KID×100 on real prospective MRI and 1.57 FID / 0.13 KID×100 on clinical CT. Among OT-based methods, OT-CycleGAN [35] and FSBM [41] perform substantially worse (FID 156.43 and 113.62 on simulated MRI respectively), indicating that generic or non-imaging-informed transport costs are inadequate for medical reconstruction. The diffusion-based DDS [47] is competitive on simulated data (FID 29.46) but degrades significantly on real prospective MRI even when fine-tuned (FID 39.78), whereas KIDOT's physics-informed dynamics provide a stronger inductive bias under limited real data. Traditional methods (CS-wavelet, BM3D) and supervised deep learning baselines (E2E-VarNet, PromptMR, REDCNN, ISTA-Net) are all consistently outperformed, with KIDOT's advantage being most pronounced on distributional metrics (FID, KID) that capture perceptual quality and anatomical realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic optimal transport framework (Villani; Chen et al.) by replacing the standard metric-based transport cost with an imaging-physics-informed data-fidelity cost and by driving the transport ODE with the gradient flow of the inverse problem itself. Prior OT-based medical imaging methods (OT-CycleGAN, UAR) rely on static OT with generic Euclidean costs disconnected from acquisition physics, while FSBM applies dynamic OT in a Schrödinger bridge setting without modality-specific constraints. KIDOT departs from all of these by embedding the forward operator A into both the instantaneous cost along the path and the governing velocity field, making it the first dynamic OT formulation explicitly consistent with imaging physics throughout the transport trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The retrospective-to-prospective gap is a well-recognized but under-addressed bottleneck in clinical deployment of learned reconstruction; KIDOT offers a principled, physics-grounded route to training from unpaired real data without relying on simulated pairs that may not match prospective acquisition. The theoretical guarantees (existence of minimizer, rationale for the integrated cost via Theorem 3.1) add mathematical rigor uncommon in applied OT-for-imaging work. Key limitations include the hard dependency on an accurate forward model A and the linear computational overhead of multi-step ODE integration, both of which constrain applicability to modalities with well-characterized acquisition physics and to settings where inference latency is tolerable. Open questions include whether the framework can be extended to cases where A is partially unknown or time-varying, and whether the discretization sensitivity (N=12 sweet spot) can be mitigated with higher-order integrators.
