## Problem

Recourse explanations are performative: when many rejected applicants act on recommendations, the resulting distribution shift can cause a refitted model to invalidate the very actions it recommended. Prior robustness work considered only exogenous shifts (temporal drift, geospatial variation) and overlooked this endogenous, recourse-induced shift. The paper asks: under what causal conditions do recourse explanations remain valid under their own performative effects?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254652/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes an applicant's tabular feature vector, a binary predictive model, a structural causal model (SCM) specifying the DAG and structural equations over features and target, and a cost function over interventions. It outputs a causal do-intervention on a subset of features (the recourse action) together with a formal performative validity guarantee: the updated model, trained on a mixture of pre- and post-recourse data, still accepts the applicant. The SCM is essential because the validity characterization depends on distinguishing causes, direct effects, and spouses of the target variable in the causal graph.

## Method

The paper formalizes the post-recourse distribution as a mixture and defines performative validity as the requirement that the optimal updated model never rejects an applicant the original model would have accepted. The central result (Theorem 5.2) uses d-separation to show that performative invalidity can arise from exactly two causal mechanisms: (1) the action being influenced by effect variables of the target, and (2) the action intervening on direct effects of the target. Proposition 5.1 establishes that if the action is conditionally independent of the post-recourse label given post-recourse features, validity is guaranteed. Two sufficient assumptions—resampled unobserved noise (Assumption 5.4) and invertible aggregated noise (Assumption 5.6, satisfied by linear additive and multiplicative SCMs)—restore validity for methods that only intervene on causes. Empirically, counterfactual explanations and causal recourse (which can target effects) exhibit severe performative invalidity across synthetic and real-world settings, while improvement-focused causal recourse (ICR), which exclusively intervenes on causes, remains performatively valid in all tested configurations.

## Results

Across all synthetic settings (LAdd, LMult, NLAdd, NLMult, LCubic) and real-world settings (GPA, Credit), CE and CR cause the conditional probability of a favorable outcome to decrease by 70–100% (e.g., in the nonlinear additive setting) and the post-recourse acceptance rate to drop by nearly 80% (e.g., in the GPA setting). In contrast, ICR maintains unchanged conditional probabilities and acceptance rates across all settings, with the sole exception of the LinCubic setting where the conditional probability increases by 0–60% (a non-problematic shift). All results are averaged over 10 runs with different seeds, and the differences between methods are well outside the reported standard deviations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three recourse methods: Counterfactual Explanations (CE, Wachter et al. 2017), Causal Recourse (CR, Karimi et al. 2021), and Improvement-focused Causal Recourse (ICR, König et al. 2023). CE and CR both fail the performative validity test across every setting: they recommend interventions on effect variables (e.g., GitHub activity), which causes the post-recourse conditional distribution to shift dramatically (70–100% decrease in favorable-outcome probability in NLAdd) and the acceptance rate to drop by nearly 80% in the GPA setting. ICR, which exclusively intervenes on causal variables (e.g., obtaining a master's degree), remains performatively valid in all tested settings, with acceptance rates unchanged after model refitting. The critical distinction is that CE and CR target non-causal variables to flip the prediction, while ICR targets causal variables to genuinely improve the applicant's qualification. Even under the most favorable theoretical conditions (Assumption 5.6, satisfied by linear additive and multiplicative noise models), CE and CR still cause performative invalidity, whereas ICR does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the performative prediction framework (Perdomo et al., 2020) from model retraining to the specific setting of recourse explanations, introducing 'performative validity' as a formal analogue of performative stability. It departs from the robust recourse literature (Rawal et al., 2020; Upadhyay et al., 2021; Nguyen et al., 2023), which studies exogenous distribution shifts (temporal drift, geospatial variation), by identifying an endogenous shift: the recourse recommendations themselves alter the applicant distribution. The causal intervention framework is inherited from Karimi et al. (2021) for causal recourse and König et al. (2023) for improvement-focused causal recourse, and the gaming-vs-improvement distinction from strategic classification (Kleinberg & Raghavan, 2020) informs the theoretical analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: widely deployed counterfactual explanation and causal recourse methods can systematically invalidate themselves, causing applicants to waste time and resources on actions that no longer work after model retraining. The theoretical characterization (Theorem 5.2) gives a clean, actionable criterion—avoid effect variables in both the decision model and the recourse recommendation—that practitioners can use to audit existing systems. Open questions include characterizing the full class of data-generating processes under which ICR guarantees validity, extending to settings with incomplete causal knowledge or violated causal sufficiency, and analyzing multi-step recourse dynamics where applicants repeatedly interact with evolving models.
