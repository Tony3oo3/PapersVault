## Problem

When only a handful (~100) of domain-specific examples are available, LLM-based synthetic data augmentation (e.g., Evol-Instruct, Naive prompting) produces repetitive, low-diversity samples that fail to capture the full complexity of the target domain. Existing retrieval-augmented or in-domain data augmentation methods cannot operate when no relevant domain data exists beyond the tiny seed set, which is the norm for specialized fields like medicine, finance, or niche programming languages.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two inputs: a small domain-specific source dataset D_s (~100 examples, e.g., 100 GSM8K math problems or 100 MedQA clinical questions) and a large general-purpose dataset D_g that is explicitly domain-unrelated (e.g., CoT-Collection with all math/medical/finance entries manually removed). The output is a synthetic domain-specific dataset D_syn at configurable scales (10k, 30k, 120k examples), where each synthetic instance is a domain-formatted question paired with a label generated by the same projection LLM. The critical constraint is that D_g shares no topical overlap with D_s, making the method applicable to domains where no similar public data exists.

## Method

PANGEA is a three-stage LLM pipeline that extracts diversity from irrelevant general data while preserving domain structure from the scarce source set. Stage 1 (Synth Profiling) uses OpenAI o1 to analyze all ~100 source examples and produce a structured domain profile η specifying what informational elements (e.g., equations for GSM8K, clinical timelines for MedQA) should be extracted from general data. Stage 2 (Prompt Writer) uses Llama3.3-70B-Instruct to process each general data instance d_g and, guided by η, distill a structured Synth-Guide Block τ containing domain-relevant building blocks (scenarios, numbers, symbols, clinical events) in a fixed template format. Stage 3 (Projection) uses Llama3.3-70B-Instruct to project each τ onto a source example d_s, generating a synthetic domain-specific question; each d_s is paired with N = |D_g|/|D_s| distinct τ blocks to maximize combinatorial diversity. The key mechanism is that diversity is inherited from the breadth of D_g (many different general passages yield many different τ), while domain fidelity is enforced by η and the source example's structural template, eliminating the need for any in-domain retrieval.

## Results

Using Llama3.2-1B fine-tuned on synthetic data, PANGEA achieves an average accuracy of 44.70% across GSM8K, MedQA, FinQA, and CDSL at the 120k synthetic-data scale, outperforming Evol-Instruct (31.96%) and Naive (30.02%) by 12.7 and 14.7 points respectively. At the 30k scale, PANGEA reaches 34.36% average (GSM8K 38.36, MedQA 39.98, FinQA 41.41, CDSL 17.68), while the instruction-tuned baseline without synthetic data scores only 27.40%. On the extreme CDSL benchmark (a custom domain-specific language where pre-trained models score 0%), PANGEA achieves 35.36% at 120k, surpassing the instruction-tuned Llama3.3-70B (33.62%) and Qwen2.5-72B (23.76%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PANGEA consistently outperforms both the Naive (direct LLM generation from source data) and Evol-Instruct (iterative instruction evolution) baselines across all four benchmarks and all three data scales (10k, 30k, 120k). At the 120k scale, PANGEA leads Evol-Instruct by 12.7 points on average (44.70 vs. 31.96) and Naive by 14.7 points (44.70 vs. 30.02), with the largest margins on FinQA (+16.5 over Evol-Instruct) and CDSL (+22.3 over Evol-Instruct). Against domain-specialized methods, PANGEA surpasses MuMath on GSM8K (32.52 vs. 30.25 at 30k) and UltraMedical on MedQA (37.78 vs. 36.71 at 10k), demonstrating that leveraging entirely unrelated general data can match or exceed purpose-built domain augmentation strategies. On the retrieval-based DataTune method, PANGEA also wins on MedQA at 10k scale (37.78 vs. 34.95), where DataTune's assumption of task-relevant general data breaks down in the extreme scarcity setting. The advantage of PANGEA grows with data volume: at 10k the average gap over Evol-Instruct is 5.6 points, widening to 6.9 at 30k and 12.7 at 120k, indicating that the diversity injected from general data scales effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

PANGEA sits at the intersection of LLM-based synthetic data generation (Self-Instruct, WizardLM/Evol-Instruct, Magpie) and retrieval-augmented data augmentation (DataTune, CRAFT), but departs from both in a key way: it deliberately uses *domain-unrelated* general data as the diversity source rather than domain-relevant examples. Prior retrieval-based methods (DataTune, CRAFT, ELTEX) presuppose that the general corpus contains in-domain signals, while Evol-Instruct-style methods rely solely on the small seed set and suffer from distributional collapse. PANGEA's three-stage pipeline (synth profiling → prompt writer → projection) repurposes the general-data-as-diversity-source idea by explicitly extracting structured 'building blocks' from irrelevant text and projecting them onto the domain-specific format, a conceptual bridge between the two prior lines that neither had explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: domain adaptation when both the seed set is tiny (~100 examples) and no relevant general corpus exists, a setting where RAG and retrieval-based augmentation simply cannot operate. The finding that a 1B model fine-tuned on PANGEA data can outperform 70B/72B instruction-tuned models on the custom CDSL benchmark suggests the approach is particularly valuable for building small, specialized domain models. The main limitation acknowledged is the manual prompt-design overhead across three stages, and the open question of whether the profiling and prompt-writing steps can be further automated or made fully domain-agnostic (the paper shows a small drop with unified templates but does not close the gap). Broader applicability to truly novel or proprietary domains where no analogous public data exists remains the key untested frontier.
