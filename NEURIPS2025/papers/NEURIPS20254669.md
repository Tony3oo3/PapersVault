## Problem

State-of-the-art computer-use agent (CUA) systems remain closed and proprietary, limiting the community's ability to study their capabilities, limitations, and risks. Existing open-source efforts are hampered by the absence of scalable cross-OS annotation infrastructure, GUI datasets that are limited in scope (mobile-only, web-only, or single-domain), and insufficient disclosure of modeling strategies and training recipes, collectively impeding progress toward general-purpose CUAs.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a sequence of computer screenshots (720p–4K resolution) as visual observations, a natural-language task instruction, and a structured textual history (L1 action tokens plus memory components) alongside up to three prior screenshots as visual history. At each step it emits a three-level chain-of-thought (L3 contextual observation → L2 reflective thought with planning/memory/error-correction → L1 concise action) followed by a single executable PyAutoGUI action drawn from a cross-OS action space (click, rightClick, doubleClick, moveTo, dragTo, scroll, write, press, hotkey, terminate). At inference the L2 format is used to maximize reflective reasoning quality.

## Method

OPENCUA introduces a full pipeline from human demonstration to trained agent: (1) AGENTNET TOOL, a cross-OS (Windows/macOS/Ubuntu) annotation application that passively records screen video and high-frequency keyboard/mouse events without disrupting the user; (2) an action-discretization and reflective long-CoT synthesis pipeline where a reflector inspects before/after screenshots to flag errors and generate correction reasoning, a generator conditions on full agent context (history, goal, screenshots, visual coordinate cues) to produce structured L3→L2→L1 reasoning, and a summarizer refines task goals and scores trajectories; (3) a training recipe that mixes all three CoT levels during SFT, uses three-screenshot visual history with L1 textual history for efficiency, and blends CUA trajectories with grounding data (ShowUI, UGround, AXTree-parsed boxes) and general text/vision SFT data. The key insight is that reflective CoT—explicitly teaching the model to detect and recover from its own errors—drives robust performance scaling with both data volume and model size, enabling OPENCUA-72B to reach 45.0% on OSWorld-Verified, a new open-source SOTA.

## Results

OPENCUA-72B achieves 45.0% success rate on OSWorld-Verified (100 steps), establishing a new state-of-the-art among open-source models and outperforming the proprietary OpenAI CUA (31.4%) and Claude 4 Sonnet (41.5%). On GUI grounding, OPENCUA-72B reaches 60.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro and 37.3% (SOTA) on UI-Vision. On the offline AGENTNETBENCH, OPENCUA-32B achieves 79.1% average step success rate, surpassing OpenAI CUA (73.1%). Pass@3 evaluation shows substantial headroom: OPENCUA-72B improves from 45.0% (Pass@1) to 53.2% (Pass@3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPENCUA-72B (45.0% at 100 steps) outperforms all open-source baselines by large margins: Qwen3-VL (38.1%), UI-TARS-72B-DPO (27.1%), and the base Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct (5.0%). It also surpasses the proprietary OpenAI CUA (31.4%) and Claude 4 Sonnet (41.5%) at 100 steps, though it trails the newer Claude Sonnet 4.5 (61.4%) by 16.4 points. Among open-source models, OPENCUA-72B leads by 6.9 points over Qwen3-VL and 17.9 points over UI-TARS-72B-DPO. On GUI grounding, OPENCUA-72B achieves SOTA on UI-Vision (37.3% vs. 25.5% for UI-TARS-72B) and ScreenSpot-Pro (60.8% vs. 38.1% for UI-TARS-72B). On the offline AGENTNETBENCH, OPENCUA-32B (79.1%) outperforms OpenAI CUA (73.1%) and all zero-shot open-source models, with the gap driven primarily by coordinate-action accuracy (81.9% vs. 71.7% for OpenAI CUA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Aguvis [50] and ActRe [52] line of work on pure-vision GUI agents by introducing a reflective long CoT synthesis pipeline (with a dedicated reflector for error detection) and a multi-level CoT training mixture (L1/L2/L3), departing from prior work that used only short or single-level reasoning. It also builds on the two-stage curriculum training paradigm from Aguvis but adds a joint-training strategy and demonstrates that general-domain text data improves agentic performance. The broader positioning is as a comprehensive open-source counterpart to proprietary CUA systems (OpenAI Operator, Claude), filling the gap left by prior open-source efforts that were limited to mobile, web, or single-domain settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPENCUA provides the first fully open pipeline—annotation tool, 22.6K-trajectory desktop dataset spanning 3 OSes, offline benchmark, training recipe, and models—enabling the community to study CUA capabilities, limitations, and risks without relying on closed systems. Key open questions include whether semi-automated or synthetic data can replace the human-annotation bottleneck, whether the 16-point gap to Claude Sonnet 4.5 (61.4%) can be closed through post-training or multi-agent methods (the large Pass@n headroom suggests yes), and whether the reflective CoT mechanism generalizes to truly long-horizon tasks where error compounding remains a core failure mode.
