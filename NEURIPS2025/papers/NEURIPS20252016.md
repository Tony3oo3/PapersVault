## Problem

Existing text-to-motion synthesis methods face a fundamental trade-off: discrete VQ-based autoregressive models introduce quantization errors that disrupt temporal continuity, while continuous diffusion models require slow multi-step sampling and must pre-specify sequence length. This paper demonstrates that vector quantization is not a prerequisite for autoregressive motion modeling, showing that continuous multimodal latent distributions can be generated autoregressively in a single step per token.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252016/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text motion description (e.g., 'A person rolls forward once, then raises their hand and quickly shoots a ball') and produces a variable-length 3D human motion sequence without requiring a pre-specified duration. Unlike diffusion-based baselines that fix maximum sequence length, the autoregressive formulation allows generation to continue until a learned stopping condition, enabling longer sequences for more complex descriptions. Motion is reconstructed via SMPL mesh vertices and joint poses at 20 FPS (HumanML3D) or 12.5 FPS (KIT-ML).

## Method

GMMotion is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 trains a GM-VAE that replaces the standard Gaussian prior with a learnable Gaussian mixture model, regularized by a Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between the empirical latent CDF and the GMM CDF, producing continuous multimodal latent representations that cluster motion data more effectively than VQ codebooks. Stage 2 trains a causal transformer (8 layers, 512 dim, 8 heads) that autoregressively predicts GMM parameters (mixing weights, means, diagonal variances) at each timestep via a single linear head, then samples one latent vector per step from the predicted mixture. Text conditioning is injected through cross-attention with residual connections after each transformer block, and the architecture uses head pre-padding with learnable rotary position encoding for length extrapolation. A Residual-MLP reorganizes sampled latents and a PostNet with residual connections refines them into final motion representations, replacing the multi-step diffusion denoising process with single-step GMM sampling.

## Results

On HumanML3D, GMMotion achieves the best R-Precision across all ranks (Top-1: 0.572, Top-2: 0.761, Top-3: 0.852), the best MM-Dist (2.743), and the highest Diversity (9.792, closest to real motion's 9.503). On KIT-ML, it similarly leads in R-Precision (Top-1: 0.481, Top-2: 0.703, Top-3: 0.819) and MM-Dist (2.604). However, GMMotion trails LaMP on FID (0.086 vs. 0.032 on HumanML3D) and Fg-T2M++ on KIT-ML FID (0.198 vs. 0.135), and diffusion-based methods (MDM, MLD) still achieve higher MultiModality scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMMotion achieves the best R-Precision at all three ranks on both HumanML3D and KIT-ML, outperforming the strongest discrete-AR baseline LaMP by 1.5 points on Top-1 (0.572 vs. 0.557) and 0.9 points on Top-3 (0.852 vs. 0.843) on HumanML3D. It also leads in MM-Dist (2.743 vs. LaMP's 2.759) and Diversity (9.792, closest to real motion) on HumanML3D, and in MM-Dist (2.604 vs. Fg-T2M++'s 2.696) on KIT-ML. On FID, GMMotion (0.086) trails LaMP (0.032) on HumanML3D and Fg-T2M++ (0.135) on KIT-ML, indicating that diffusion-based and LLM-augmented methods still produce slightly more realistic motion distributions. Diffusion models (MDM, MLD) achieve higher MultiModality (2.799, 2.413) than GMMotion (2.033), reflecting their inherent stochastic diversity, though they sacrifice R-Precision and FID substantially. In the user study, GMMotion outperforms both LaMP (visual quality 4.392 vs. 3.962) and MotionLCM (4.392 vs. 3.418) on perceptual quality and text-motion alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMMotion bridges two dominant paradigms in text-to-motion synthesis: discrete autoregressive models (T2M-GPT, MoMask, LaMP) that rely on vector quantization, and continuous diffusion models (MDM, MLD, MotionLCM) that suffer from slow iterative sampling. It builds on the motion-VAE encoder/decoder (Petrovich et al.), the causal transformer backbone of T2M-GPT, and the GMM-VAE prior from Dilokthanakul et al., while departing from both lineages by replacing VQ tokens with a learnable Gaussian mixture distribution and replacing multi-step diffusion with single-step GMM sampling. The KS-distance latent regularization and the adoption of learnable RoPE with head pre-padding (borrowed from LLM practice) are novel architectural choices that enable variable-length generation without pre-specifying sequence duration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating vector quantization, GMMotion removes a source of reconstruction error that has constrained the quality ceiling of AR motion models, while its single-step GMM sampling avoids the iterative cost of diffusion-based approaches. The ability to generate variable-length sequences without a fixed maximum duration addresses a practical limitation of both VQ-AR and diffusion pipelines. However, the sequential nature of AR generation introduces a risk of error accumulation over long horizons, and the paper does not extensively probe generation beyond the ~10-second range of its training data. Open questions include whether the GMM component count and KS regularization weight transfer across motion domains (e.g., dance vs. locomotion) and whether the approach scales to higher-fidelity or longer-horizon generation.
