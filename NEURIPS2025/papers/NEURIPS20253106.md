## Problem

LoRA's nonunique low-rank factorizations induce inconsistent and imbalanced weight updates across equivalent parameterizations, leading to slow, unstable convergence and notable performance gaps relative to full fine-tuning. While the nonuniqueness was previously recognized, its concrete implications for the optimization trajectory remained underexplored, and no principled per-step selection of the optimal factorization existed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253106/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained weight matrix W^pt (of shape m×n) and standard supervised training data, producing updated low-rank factors A_t ∈ R^{m×r} and B_t ∈ R^{n×r} with r ≪ min{m,n}. The per-step refactoring requires computing the matrix geometric mean of (A_t^T A_t)^{-1} and (B_t^T B_t), incurring only O(r²) extra memory and O((m+n+r)r²) extra time—negligible relative to LoRA's Ω(mn) forward/backward cost. A lightweight variant RefLoRA-S reduces this to O(1) extra space by restricting the refactoring to a scalar scale factor.

## Method

RefLoRA characterizes all equivalent LoRA factorizations via an invertible matrix P_t, showing the resulting weight update is fully governed by the SPD matrix S_t = P_t P_t^T. It then derives a quadratic upper bound on the loss as a function of S_t (decoupled from the large gradient ∇ℓ(W_t)) and solves for the global optimum in closed form: S_t* equals the matrix geometric mean (A_t^T A_t)^{-1} # (B_t^T B_t), which guarantees balanced updates A_t^T A_t = B_t^T B_t. In practice, the algorithm 'refactors back' after the gradient step so that the axes of (A_t, B_t) remain unchanged, yielding a preconditioned GD update with matrices S̃_t^{-1} and S̃_t that is directly compatible with Adam/AdamW without modifying moment estimators. This is equivalently interpreted as Riemannian steepest descent on the quotient manifold (R_*^{m×r} × R_*^{n×r})/GL(r) with a congruence-invariant metric, ensuring updates always lie in the horizontal space and never waste effort on gauge directions.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with DeBERTaV3-base (1.33M trainable params), RefLoRA achieves the highest average score of 89.52, outperforming AdaLoRA (89.46), DoRA (88.98), and vanilla LoRA (88.50). On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA3-8B, RefLoRA reaches 85.68% average accuracy versus 85.2% for DoRA, 85.56% for LoRA-RITE, and 80.8% for LoRA. For subject-driven image generation (DreamBooth), RefLoRA reduces fine-tuning loss to 0.086, a 14.0% improvement over LoRA (0.100). In convergence tests, RefLoRA achieves 88.5% of LoRA's throughput with only 132 MB additional GPU memory, while LoRA-Pro and LoRA-RITE drop to 60.2% and 72.6% throughput respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefLoRA consistently outperforms vanilla LoRA across all evaluated tasks: on GLUE it improves the average from 88.50 to 89.52 (+1.02), and on LLaMA3-8B commonsense reasoning it lifts accuracy from 80.8% to 85.68% (+4.88). Against the strongest SOTA baselines, RefLoRA edges out DoRA (85.2%) and LoRA-RITE (85.56%) on LLaMA3-8B, and surpasses AdaLoRA (89.46) on GLUE, though the margins are modest (0.2–0.5 points). The lightweight variant RefLoRA-S remains within 0.33% of full RefLoRA on GLUE and within 0.2–0.3% on commonsense reasoning, while reducing extra memory from 132 MB to <1 MB. On the DreamBooth image generation task, RefLoRA achieves 14.0%, 13.1%, and 9.5% lower loss than LoRA, LoRA-Pro, and LoRA-RITE respectively. In the matrix factorization experiment, RefLoRA converges faster and more stably than both LoRA and ScaledGD, and maintains balanced factor norms (‖A_t‖_F = ‖B_t‖_F) throughout training, unlike LoRA which exhibits severe imbalance in early iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

RefLoRA builds directly on LoRA's low-rank parameterization but reframes its per-step update as a Riemannian optimization problem on the quotient manifold (R^{m×r}_* × R^{n×r}_*)/GL(r), identifying the nonuniqueness of the (A, B) factorization as the root cause of inconsistent and imbalanced weight updates. It departs from gradient-manipulation approaches like LoRA-Pro and LoRA-RITE by adhering to standard backpropagation and instead selecting an optimal SPD preconditioning matrix S_t = P_t P_t^T in closed form (the matrix geometric mean of A_t^T A_t and B_t^T B_t). The connection to ScaledGD for matrix factorization is acknowledged, but RefLoRA generalizes the balanced-update principle to the full fine-tuning setting with a principled loss-bound derivation rather than a task-specific heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, drop-in replacement for LoRA that requires only O(r²) extra memory and O((m+n+r)r²) extra time per step, making it practically attractive for resource-constrained fine-tuning. The balanced-update guarantee (A_t^T A_t = B_t^T B_t) and the quotient-manifold interpretation offer a clean lens for understanding why vanilla LoRA converges slowly, potentially guiding future PEFT designs. Open questions include formal convergence-rate analysis for RefLoRA vs. LoRA, scaling to 30B+ parameter models, and integration with sequential LoRA chaining for pre-training. The simplified RefLoRA-S variant (scalar scaling only) narrows the gap to full RefLoRA to ~0.3% on GLUE, suggesting the full matrix structure may be less critical than the norm-balance it enforces.
