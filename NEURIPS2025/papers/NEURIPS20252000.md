## Problem

Existing guided sampling strategies for diffusion-based offline RL planners—Classifier Guidance, Classifier-Free Guidance, and Monte Carlo Sample Selection (MCSS)—suffer from suboptimal multi-modal action selection, distributional drift, or prohibitive inference-time costs from sampling and scoring multiple candidate trajectories. Directly training a diffusion model with a behavior-regularized objective to generate high-value trajectories is computationally infeasible for planners due to the need for backpropagation through the full iterative denoising process and the intractability of diffusion model density evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252000/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the current environment state s (a low-dimensional state vector) as input and produces a single H-length state trajectory x_0 via the fixed denoising operator, from which an action a is recovered through a separately trained inverse dynamics model. The learnable prior is a GRU conditioned on s that outputs per-timestep mean and log-standard-deviation vectors defining independent Gaussians over the latent noise dimensions. Unlike MCSS, only one trajectory is generated per environment step, and the planner uses DDIM (deterministic) sampling with a planning stride m to produce temporally spaced state sequences.

## Method

Prior Guidance (PG) replaces the standard Gaussian prior N(0,I) of a behavior-cloned diffusion planner with a learnable Gaussian prior p_ψ(x_T|s), optimized to maximize a trajectory critic while penalizing deviation from the standard normal via an f-divergence (KL in practice). Proposition 1 proves that under DDIM's bijective mapping, the intractable behavior-regularized objective over trajectory densities reduces exactly to a closed-form objective over the prior densities, enabling stable regularization. To eliminate backpropagation through the denoising process, PG trains a latent-space value function V̄_φ(x_T) via regression on V(g_s(x_T)), so gradients flow only through the small prior network and the latent critic. At inference, a single x_T is sampled from the learned prior, denoised by the frozen planner g_s, and passed to the inverse dynamics model to yield the action—avoiding the multi-candidate sampling overhead of MCSS entirely.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, Prior Guidance (PG) achieves average normalized scores of 84.6 (MuJoCo), 81.3 (Kitchen), 83.4 (Antmaze), and 164.6 (Maze2D), setting new state-of-the-art results among diffusion planners on all four task families. PG outperforms the strongest diffusion planner baseline, Diffusion Veteran (DV*), in nearly every environment while generating only a single trajectory at inference (N=1) versus DV*'s default N=50 candidates. On long-horizon tasks, the gains are most pronounced: PG scores 81.3 on Kitchen (vs. DV*'s 74.8) and 83.4 on Antmaze (vs. DV*'s 79.9), while on MuJoCo tasks where complex planning is less critical, PG (84.6) is competitive with top diffusion policies like DQL (87.9) and HD (84.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PG outperforms DV* (the strongest diffusion planner baseline using MCSS with N=50) on Kitchen (81.3 vs. 74.8), Antmaze (83.4 vs. 79.9), Maze2D (164.6 vs. 158.5), and MuJoCo (84.6 vs. 83.0), while using only a single trajectory at inference. Against other diffusion planners, PG's advantage is most dramatic on Antmaze, where HD scores only 3.0 and AD 8.0 on average, versus PG's 83.4; on Maze2D, PG (164.6) surpasses AD (144.3), HD (139.9), and Diffuser (119.5). On MuJoCo, PG (84.6) is competitive with the best diffusion policies (DQL 87.9, HD 84.6) but trails them slightly, consistent with the paper's observation that diffusion planners are less advantageous when complex long-horizon planning is not required. PG also outperforms Gaussian policy baselines (CQL, IQL) across all task families, with the largest margins on Antmaze (83.4 vs. 57.1 for IQL) and Kitchen (81.3 vs. 48.7 for IQL). The only environment where PG does not clearly lead is Antmaze-L-D, where HD (83.6) slightly edges out PG (76.0), and HalfCheetah-M where DQL (51.1) and DV* (50.9) both outperform PG (45.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffusion Veteran (DV) [19] as its base diffusion planner and on DDIM [25] for the bijective latent-to-trajectory mapping that underpins the theoretical reformulation. It departs from the dominant guided-sampling paradigm in diffusion planners—Monte Carlo Sample Selection (MCSS)—by shifting the learning burden from inference-time candidate selection to a learnable prior distribution over the diffusion model's latent noise. The behavior-regularized objective connects to the broader offline RL literature (BRAC, AWR, AWAC, SQL/EQL), but the key conceptual departure is making f-divergence regularization tractable in diffusion models by operating on Gaussian priors rather than intractable diffusion densities, a direction previously explored only in conditional generative models (PriorGrad) and skill-based RL (behavior priors, OPAL, FIST).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PG offers a practical efficiency win—replacing N candidate trajectories at inference with a single trajectory from a learned prior—while simultaneously enabling closed-form behavior regularization that was previously infeasible for diffusion models. This is particularly relevant for long-horizon offline RL where MCSS's computational cost and OOD risk are most acute. Open questions include whether the bijective DDIM assumption can be relaxed via flow-matching, whether the approach extends to high-dimensional observations (images), and whether more expressive prior architectures (beyond a GRU-parameterized Gaussian) yield further gains in complex environments. The paper's limitation to low-dimensional state-vector settings and offline-only evaluation constrains its immediate applicability to real-world robotics.
