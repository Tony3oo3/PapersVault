## Problem

Model quantization for multi-frame video enhancement (VFI, VSR, STVSR) remains largely unexplored, and directly applying existing PTQ methods causes severe performance degradation. This stems from two gaps: (a) traditional per-tensor quantization cannot allocate varying representational capacity across frames whose activation distributions differ significantly, and (b) relying solely on full-precision teachers for distillation makes it difficult for low-bit (4/2-bit) students to learn high-quality mappings within their limited capacity.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained Transformer-based video enhancement backbones (RSTT, MIA, EMA-VFI) that process fixed windows of N frames (e.g., 4 or 7 frames at 448×256) at each inference step. Quantization is applied per-frame-slot within this window, learning N distinct sets of clipping bounds for weights and activations in Linear and MatMul layers. The quantized models produce enhanced outputs—upsampled HR frames (×4 spatial), interpolated intermediate frames, or jointly upsampled and interpolated frames—matching the task-specific output of the original full-precision network.

## Method

PMQ-VE is a two-stage coarse-to-fine PTQ framework. In the coarse stage, Backtracking-based Multi-Frame Quantization (BMFQ) assigns frame-specific clipping bounds by initializing from percentiles (p0.1–p10 for lower, p90–p99.9 for upper) to suppress outliers, then performing a recursive backtracking search (BTBI) that explores candidate (lb, ub) pairs with pruning and backtracking to minimize per-frame quantization distortion. In the fine stage, Progressive Multi-Teacher Distillation (PMTD) trains the low-bit student using both a full-precision teacher and intermediate-bit (e.g., 8-bit) teachers, with a linearly increasing coefficient α(t) that gradually shifts supervision weight from intermediate to full-precision teachers, bridging the representational gap. The distillation loss combines L1 output reconstruction and MSE feature-matching across multiple intermediate layers, and the entire process uses fake quantization with STE for gradient-based optimization of clipping bounds while keeping model weights frozen.

## Results

On the STVSR task (RSTT backbone), PMQ-VE achieves 25.42 dB PSNR on Vid4 and 34.69 dB on Vimeo-Fast at 4/4-bit, surpassing the closest competitor 2DQuant (25.04 / 33.59 dB) by ~0.4–1.1 dB. At the extreme 2/2-bit setting, the gap widens to ~0.6–1.0 dB over 2DQuant (e.g., 23.48 vs. 22.91 dB on Vid4). For VSR (MIA backbone) at 4-bit, PMQ-VE reaches 37.34 dB PSNR on Vimeo-90K and 27.64 dB on Vid4, outperforming 2DQuant (36.92 / 27.38 dB). For VFI (EMA-VFI) at 4-bit, the method achieves 29.59 dB PSNR on the [D] variant, beating 2DQuant (28.78 dB) by 0.81 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMQ-VE outperforms all 12 baselines across every task, benchmark, and bit-width tested. The closest competitors are 2DQuant [40] and DBDC+Pac [64], which are specifically designed for pixel-level image super-resolution; PMQ-VE beats 2DQuant by 0.38–1.0 dB PSNR on STVSR (4-bit) and by 0.42–0.81 dB on VSR/VFI (4-bit), with the gap widening at 2-bit (up to ~1.0 dB). Hardware quantization tools (OpenVINO, TensorRT, SNPE) suffer severe degradation at 2-bit (15–20 dB PSNR on Vid4 STVSR) and remain well below PMQ-VE even at 4-bit. Simple calibration methods (Percentile, MinMax) perform poorly, with MinMax dropping to 10.34 dB at 2-bit STVSR. NoisyQuant [42], designed for ViT classification, shows moderate results at 4-bit (24.26 dB on Vid4 STVSR) but degrades sharply at 2-bit (12.06 dB). The full-precision backbones (RSTT-S, MIA, EMA-VFI) serve as upper-bound references; PMQ-VE at 4-bit recovers 96–97% of the FP32 PSNR gap, while at 2-bit it retains roughly 89–90%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends post-training quantization (PTQ) from single-frame image super-resolution (2DQuant, DBDC+Pac) to multi-frame video enhancement, where the key departure is recognizing that activation distributions are not uniform across frames due to frame-dependent attention dynamics. It repurposes standard PTQ machinery—clipping-bound optimization, fake quantization, and STE—into a per-frame setting via a backtracking search (BMFQ), and augments knowledge distillation with intermediate-bit teachers (PMTD) to bridge the representational gap at extreme low-bit widths. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the PTQ-for-low-level-vision line (PAMS, 2DQuant, DBDC+Pac) and the multi-teacher distillation literature, but is the first to explicitly address the inter-frame heterogeneity that makes naive per-tensor quantization suboptimal for video models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Enabling low-bit deployment of Transformer-based video enhancement on edge devices is a practical bottleneck that this paper directly addresses, and the per-frame quantization insight generalizes to any multi-input architecture where input slots have heterogeneous activation statistics. The progressive multi-teacher distillation strategy is a reusable recipe for any setting where a large capacity gap between teacher and student makes direct distillation unstable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to diffusion-based video models (DiT), longer temporal windows, and whether the per-frame overhead becomes prohibitive at scale; the authors themselves note these as future work. The absence of error bars and the reliance on a single training seed limit confidence in the exact magnitude of gains.
