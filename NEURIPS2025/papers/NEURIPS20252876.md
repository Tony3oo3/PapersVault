## Problem

Existing PEFT-based continual learning methods focus exclusively on mitigating catastrophic forgetting by constraining new-task updates to avoid interfering with old tasks, which inherently blocks backward knowledge transfer. When new tasks are positively correlated with old ones, beneficial knowledge could be transferred back to improve old-task performance, but no prior PEFT-based CL method enables this. Two fundamental obstacles prevent this: some LoRA parameters are causally ineffective (constraining the solution space without contributing to performance), and old-task data is inaccessible, making traditional data-based correlation modeling infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text sequences (for NLP benchmarks SuperNI and Long Sequence, with 1,000 training samples per task) or RGB images (for ImageNet-R, with 40/20/10 classes per task) and produces class labels or generated text depending on the task type. LoRA modules are applied to query and value projections in all Transformer attention blocks, with ranks of 4–10 depending on the backbone. A key operational requirement is storing old-task gradient matrices (not raw data) in task memory to compute cross-task correlation and affinity at each training step, which introduces linear memory overhead in the number of tasks.

## Method

CaLoRA combines two mechanisms: (1) Parameter-level Counterfactual Attribution (PaCA) estimates the causal effect of each LoRA parameter via a do-calculus intervention (zeroing the parameter) approximated by a second-order Taylor expansion with a diagonal Hessian, yielding per-parameter effectiveness scores mapped to [0,1] via softmax; (2) Cross-task Gradient Adaptation (CaGA) computes task correlation by projecting the new-task gradient onto the SVD-derived input subspace of each old task, and task affinity by measuring directional cosine similarity between the projected gradient and the old-task gradient column-wise. The corrected gradient is then element-wise modulated by the causal-effect mask and an adaptive term that, depending on the sign of affinity, either removes conflicting gradient components (negative transfer), preserves them (no correlation), or selectively amplifies beneficial components (positive backward transfer), enabling the LoRA update to simultaneously learn the new task and improve old-task performance without data replay.

## Results

On the SuperNI benchmark (15 tasks, T5-Large), CaLoRA achieves 54.42 AP with a forgetting rate of only 0.25, forward transfer of 2.98, and a positive backward transfer of 0.35 — the only method among all baselines to achieve positive BWT. On ImageNet-R (10 tasks, ViT-B/16), CaLoRA reaches 77.72 AP with 2.33 forgetting rate and 0.15 BWT, again the sole method with positive backward transfer. Across all three benchmarks and both task orders, CaLoRA consistently leads on every metric (AP, F.Ra, FWT, BWT), with the most dramatic gains in backward transfer (e.g., +1.10 over SAPT-LoRA on SuperNI Order 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaLoRA outperforms all seven PEFT-based continual learning baselines across every metric on all three benchmarks. Against the strongest baseline SAPT-LoRA, CaLoRA gains +2.66 AP, reduces forgetting by 0.63, and improves BWT by +1.10 on SuperNI (Order 1); on ImageNet-R (10 tasks), the gap is +2.40 AP and +1.40 BWT. Compared to SD-LoRA, the improvements are even larger: +8.51 AP and +2.48 BWT on SuperNI, and +1.48 AP and +1.86 BWT on ImageNet-R. The most distinctive advantage is backward transfer: CaLoRA is the only method achieving positive BWT (0.35 on SuperNI, 0.15 on ImageNet-R-10), while all baselines remain negative (ranging from −0.75 to −28.54). Prompt-based methods (L2P, CodaPrompt, HidePrompt) suffer substantially higher forgetting rates (3.6–7.1 on SuperNI) and negative BWT, confirming that parameter-freezing strategies inherently limit knowledge transfer. InfLoRA and O-LoRA, which enforce orthogonality constraints, show moderate AP but still negative BWT (−2.51 and −24.38 respectively), further validating that CaLoRA's causal-aware gradient adaptation is necessary to unlock positive backward transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaLoRA extends the LoRA-based continual learning line (InfLoRA, SD-LoRA, SAPT) by departing from the dominant paradigm of treating new-task updates as threats to old tasks. While prior PEFT-CL methods enforce orthogonality or parameter freezing to prevent interference, CaLoRA draws on the backward knowledge transfer concept from Lin et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and the gradient projection framework from TRGP (ICLR 2022) to actively identify when new-task training can benefit old tasks. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of parameter-level counterfactual attribution (a causal inference technique) to distinguish effective from ineffective LoRA parameters, which no prior PEFT-CL method attempts. This positions the work at the intersection of causal interpretability and continual learning, rather than purely within the PEFT or CL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first PEFT-based continual learning method to achieve positive backward transfer, demonstrating that new-task training can improve old-task performance rather than merely preserving it. The causal-inference lens on parameter importance offers a principled alternative to heuristic parameter selection in PEFT. However, the method requires storing old-task gradients (memory scales linearly with task count) and assumes clear task boundaries, limiting applicability to online or task-agnostic settings. Open questions include whether the causal attribution approach generalizes to non-LoRA PEFT methods, how it behaves under distribution shift within tasks, and whether the gradient-storage overhead can be eliminated via parameter-space modeling as the authors suggest.
