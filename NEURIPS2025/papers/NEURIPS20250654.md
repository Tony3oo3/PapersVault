## Problem

LLMs frequently exhibit overconfidence, assigning high verbalized confidence to answers that are often incorrect, which undermines their reliability in high-stakes applications. Existing black-box confidence elicitation methods (e.g., Top-K, Self-Random, Misleading) probe LLMs with various prompts but do not explicitly steer the direction of confidence—i.e., they do not control whether the model should be more conservative or optimistic. This paper identifies that LLM confidence is steerable via targeted prompts and exploits this property to improve calibration without any training or access to model internals.

## Input / Output

Given a single text question, the method constructs five steering prompts at levels {very cautious, cautious, vanilla, confident, very confident} and queries the LLM once per level, yielding five (answer, confidence) pairs where confidence is a scalar in [0, 1]. The output is a single calibrated confidence score c(x) ∈ [0, 1] and a final answer selected from the five candidate answers via linear quantization of c(x) onto the observed confidence range. The entire pipeline is zero-shot and black-box, requiring only API access to the LLM with no logits, fine-tuning, or model modifications.

## Method

SteerConf has three components. First, a steering prompt strategy queries the LLM at five intensity levels (ℓ=2, giving 2ℓ+1=5 prompts) that explicitly instruct the model to be more or less cautious, producing steered answers f_l(x) and confidence scores c_l(x). Second, a steered confidence consistency measure κ_conf = 1/(1 + σ_c/μ_c) quantifies how stable the confidence scores are across steering levels, where μ_c and σ_c are the mean and standard deviation of {c_l(x)}; high κ_conf indicates the model's certainty is robust to prompt perturbation. Third, the calibrated confidence is computed as c(x) = μ_c · κ_ans · κ_conf, where κ_ans is the relative frequency of the most common answer among the five steered responses; this product downweights the mean confidence whenever either the answers or the confidence scores are inconsistent. The final answer is selected by linearly quantizing c(x) to a discrete steering index j = ⌊(c(x) − c_min)/(c_max − c_min) · (2ℓ+1)⌋ and returning f_j(x), ensuring the chosen answer aligns with the calibrated confidence level.

## Results

SteerConf achieves an average ECE of 11.7% and AUROC of 75.4% on GPT-3.5 (CoT), and 7.5% ECE / 74.5% AUROC on LLaMA3 (CoT), substantially outperforming all baselines. In the vanilla (non-CoT) setting, GPT-3.5 with SteerConf reduces average ECE from 41.8% to 20.7%, and GPT-4 (CoT) with SteerConf raises average AUROC from 65.5% to 81.1%. The paper reports up to a 39.8% ECE reduction on GSM8K (GPT-3.5) and a 43.3% AUROC improvement on Object Counting (GPT-4, CoT) over vanilla verbalized confidence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SteerConf consistently outperforms all three baselines (Top-K, Misleading, Self-Random) on both GPT-3.5 and LLaMA3 with CoT across every dataset and both metrics. On GPT-3.5 (CoT), SteerConf achieves an average ECE of 11.7% versus 17.3% (Misleading), 17.8% (Top-K), and 18.7% (Self-Random), and an average AUROC of 75.4% versus 69.6%, 65.2%, and 73.0% respectively. On LLaMA3 (CoT), the gap is even wider: SteerConf's average ECE of 7.5% beats Misleading (13.5%), Self-Random (13.9%), and Top-K (18.6%), while its average AUROC of 74.5% surpasses Misleading (70.3%), Self-Random (65.2%), and Top-K (54.1%). Notably, Self-Random achieves the lowest per-dataset ECE on GSM8K (2.2%) but suffers from poor AUROC (79.3%), whereas SteerConf balances both calibration and discrimination more effectively across all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the verbalized confidence elicitation framework established by Xiong et al. (ICLR 2023), which probes LLMs with multiple prompts and aggregates sampled confidence scores using answer consistency. The key departure is introducing a 'steering' dimension: rather than repeating the same prompt (Self-Random) or varying the probing strategy (Misleading, Top-K from Tian et al., EMNLP 2023), SteerConf systematically shifts the LLM's confidence direction via explicit caution/confidence instructions and exploits the stability of confidence scores across these steering levels as an additional calibration signal. The linear-quantization mapping from calibrated confidence back to a steering-indexed answer is a novel aggregation mechanism not present in prior elicitation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SteerConf offers a practical, training-free solution to LLM overconfidence in black-box API settings where logits are inaccessible, which is the dominant deployment scenario for GPT-4 and similar models. However, the method requires 5× the API calls per question, is limited to tasks with discrete or numerical answers where answer consistency is well-defined, and relies on manually designed steering prompts whose effectiveness may not transfer to open-ended generation. Open questions include whether adaptive steering (reducing the number of queries based on early responses) can close the cost gap, and whether the approach generalizes to free-form or multi-turn reasoning tasks where answer consistency is harder to compute.
