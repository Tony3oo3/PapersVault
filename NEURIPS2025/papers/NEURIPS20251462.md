## Problem

Existing automatic concept discovery methods (e.g., CRAFT, ICE) factorize encoder activations via NMF but ignore the downstream classifier, so the extracted concepts may look interpretable yet fail to reflect the model's actual decision-making process. This misalignment undermines explanation faithfulness: the concepts highlighted to a user may not be the features the network truly relies on, producing misleading insights about model behavior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained CNN (ResNet-34 or MobileNetV2) decomposed into a frozen encoder g and a linear classifier head h, along with a set of correctly-classified RGB images for a target class. Images are cropped into 64×64 patches via a sliding window, passed through g to form an activation matrix A ∈ R^{n×p}, which is then factorized. The output is a set of r=25 non-negative concept basis vectors (spatially coherent patches) with per-patch activation coefficients, plus Sobol-index-based importance scores ranking which concepts most drive the model's prediction for that class.

## Method

FACE augments standard NMF with a KL divergence regularization term that penalizes the discrepancy between the classifier head's softmax predictions on the original activations A and on the reconstructed activations UW^T, yielding the joint objective ½‖A − UW^T‖²_F + λ·KL(h(A)‖h(UW^T)). Because the KL term depends on the downstream classifier, multiplicative NMF updates are inapplicable; instead, the method optimizes via projected gradient descent with alternating updates on U and W, enforcing non-negativity by clamping, and initializing both factors with NNDSVD. Theoretically, Pinsker's inequality guarantees that bounding the KL divergence to ε constrains the total variation distance between predictive distributions to √(2ε), providing a formal faithfulness guarantee that reconstruction loss alone cannot offer. The KL regularization also promotes local linearity of the softmax around the concept representation, making concept manipulations produce predictable, interpretable changes in model output. Concept importance is then quantified using Sobol indices over the learned coefficient matrix.

## Results

FACE consistently achieves the highest faithfulness and sparsity scores across all three datasets (ImageNet, COCO, CelebA) and both architectures (ResNet-34, MobileNetV2). On ImageNet with ResNet-34, FACE attains C-Ins 0.969, C-Del 0.891, and C-Gini 0.895, outperforming CRAFT (0.932, 0.752, 0.835) and ICE (0.908, 0.484, 0.537). The most dramatic gains appear in Concept Deletion: FACE's C-Del of 0.891 on ImageNet exceeds CRAFT's 0.752 by 14 points and ICE's 0.484 by 41 points. FACE also achieves the lowest KL divergence (D_KL) between original and reconstructed predictions (e.g., 0.220 vs. 0.240 for CRAFT and 0.359 for ICE on ImageNet/ResNet-34), confirming superior predictive alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FACE outperforms both baselines (ICE and CRAFT) on all three evaluation metrics—Concept Insertion, Concept Deletion, and Gini-index sparsity—across every dataset and architecture tested. The largest margins appear in C-Del: on ImageNet/ResNet-34, FACE scores 0.891 versus CRAFT's 0.752 (+14 pts) and ICE's 0.484 (+41 pts), indicating that removing FACE's top concepts most severely degrades model accuracy, i.e., they are the concepts the model truly relies on. On COCO, FACE's C-Del of 0.894 exceeds CRAFT's 0.691 by 20 points. The gap narrows on CelebA (4 classes), where FACE's C-Del of 0.635 leads CRAFT's 0.604 by only 3 points, which the authors attribute to lower class complexity making fewer features decision-critical. In terms of predictive alignment (D_KL from Table 1), FACE achieves the lowest KL divergence in every setting (e.g., 0.458 vs. 0.600 for CRAFT and 0.596 for ICE on COCO/ResNet-34), while incurring only marginally higher reconstruction MSE—a trade-off the paper argues is necessary for faithfulness.

## Relation to Prior Work

FACE extends the NMF-based concept extraction pipeline established by CRAFT and ICE by adding a KL divergence regularization term that aligns reconstructed activations with the classifier head's predictions. Prior methods (ACE, ICE, CRAFT) operate solely on encoder activations to reconstruct latent geometry, leaving the downstream classifier uninvolved in concept discovery; FACE's key conceptual departure is supervising the factorization with the model's own predictive behavior. The theoretical grounding via Pinsker's inequality connecting KL divergence to total variation distance is a clean but standard information-theoretic tool applied here to formalize what was previously an informal faithfulness concern in the concept-based explanation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: concept-based explanations that look interpretable to humans may misrepresent what the model actually uses for its decision, and FACE provides both a principled fix and a theoretical guarantee for it. However, the method remains class-specific (not instance-level), restricted to CNN architectures, and requires dataset-specific tuning of the KL regularization strength λ. Open questions include whether the approach extends to transformers, whether the learned concepts remain human-interpretable after KL supervision shifts the representation objective, and whether the faithfulness gains persist under distribution shift or adversarial perturbation.
