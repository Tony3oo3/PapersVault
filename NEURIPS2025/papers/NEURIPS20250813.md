## Problem

Squared families of probability distributions have been extensively studied as likelihoods in machine learning, but their potential as conjugate priors in Bayesian inference remains unexplored. Standard conjugate priors (e.g., Gaussian for Gaussian likelihood) are restricted to unimodal beliefs, limiting their expressiveness in few-shot and multi-task settings where the true prior over functions may be multimodal. This paper asks whether squared families can serve as conjugate priors that admit closed-form Bayesian updates while expressing rich multimodal uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method takes a meta-dataset of J tasks {(X_j, Y_j)} for pretraining the prior, then at inference time a support set (X_1*, Y_1*) of N few-shot examples and query inputs X_2*. The prior is parameterised by a PSD matrix M = Θ^TΘ, a Gaussian base measure μ, and a learnable neural-network feature mapping ψ(ω) = σ(Wω + b), alongside a deep feature extractor γ: X → R^{d_1}. The output is a posterior predictive distribution that is itself a GSF (with modified feature mapping ψ' and base measure ν''), yielding multimodal function-value predictions with calibrated uncertainty, evaluated via negative log-likelihood on query sets.

## Method

The core result is that Generalised Squared Families (GSFs), defined as Q(dω|M,μ,ψ) ∝ Tr(M ψ(ω)ψ(ω)^T) μ(dω), form conjugate priors: the posterior after observing data U is again a GSF with the same M and ψ but an updated base measure ν(dω) = p(U|ω)μ(dω). The key enabling mechanism is the parameter-integral factorisation z(M) = Tr(M K_{μ,ψ}), which separates the normalising constant into a parameter factor M and a parameter-independent kernel K_{μ,ψ}, so that the marginal likelihood, posterior, and posterior predictive all reduce to ratios of such traces. Specialising to Gaussian base measures and Gaussian likelihoods yields the GSFP model, where the noise-free posterior predictive is itself a GSF with a modified feature mapping ψ'(f) = ψ((I⊗Γ_*)^† f) and a Gaussian base measure ν''. Training jointly optimises Θ, ψ, μ, and the deep feature extractor γ via type II maximum marginal likelihood over the meta-dataset, with all Bayesian updates computed in closed form.

## Results

GSFP achieves the lowest test NLL on 11 out of 12 evaluation settings across 6 few-shot regression meta-datasets (Sines, Mixed-Noise Sines, QMUL, NDX100, EEG, Power), each with in-distribution and out-of-distribution splits. The only loss is on Mixed-Noise Sines in-distribution (0.46 vs. NGGP NN Linear's 0.24). Gains are most pronounced on QMUL (−1.09 vs. −0.99 best baseline in-distribution) and NDX100 (−2.49 vs. −2.16 in-distribution), where the multimodal prior provides the largest advantage. All results are averaged over 2,500 model evaluations (5 seeds × 500 random shuffles).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GSFP against two GP-based few-shot regression baselines: DKT (Deep Kernel Transfer, Patacchiola et al. 2020) and NGGP (Non-Gaussian Gaussian Processes, Sendera et al. 2021), each tested with RBF, Spectral, and NN Linear kernels. GSFP (using an NN Linear kernel) outperforms the best DKT variant on 11 of 12 settings and the best NGGP variant on 11 of 12 settings, with the sole exception being Mixed-Noise Sines in-distribution where NGGP NN Linear achieves 0.24 NLL versus GSFP's 0.46. The largest margins appear on QMUL (GSFP −1.09 vs. NGGP Spectral −0.99 in-distribution; −1.15 vs. −0.77 out-of-distribution) and NDX100 (−2.49 vs. DKT NN Linear −2.16 in-distribution), where the multimodal GSF prior captures the multi-modal structure of the task distribution more effectively than unimodal Gaussian or normalising-flow-based alternatives. On Sines, GSFP edges out the best baseline (NGGP Spectral) by a small margin (−0.85 vs. −0.84 in-distribution) but shows a clearer advantage out-of-distribution (−0.83 vs. −0.79). The authors note that GSFP training time is slightly longer than DKT but substantially shorter than NGGP, and inference time is comparable to DKT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the squared family density framework (q(ω|Θ,μ,ψ) ∝ ‖Θψ(ω)‖²), previously developed and studied exclusively as a likelihood class in Tsuchida et al. (2023, 2025), by showing it forms a conjugate prior family with closed-form Bayesian updates. The construction generalises deep kernel learning (Wilson et al., 2016) by replacing the Gaussian parameter prior with a generalised squared family (GSF) prior while retaining the same deep neural network feature extractor, yielding the GSFP model. The parameter-integral factorisation (Tr(MK_{μ,ψ})) that makes normalising constants tractable for squared family likelihoods is the key algebraic identity that also makes the marginal likelihood, posterior, and posterior predictive available in closed form when the base measure μ is itself conjugate to the likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical impact is providing a principled, closed-form route to multimodal conjugate priors for Bayesian regression in feature space, addressing a known limitation of Gaussian-process-based deep kernel learning where uncertainty is restricted to unimodal Gaussians. The O(dn²) exact inference complexity (linear in dimension) is a notable advantage over inexact methods that scale exponentially in dimension, though the O(N²) memory for GP-style inference remains a practical constraint. Open questions include establishing asymptotic normality of maximum marginal likelihood estimates, deriving generalisation bounds for the hierarchical few-shot setting, and navigating the trade-off between expressiveness (large n) and computational cost (small n). The framework also suggests broader applicability to any setting where a multimodal prior over parameters is desirable and the base measure admits a closed-form squared family kernel.
