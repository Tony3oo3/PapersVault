## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods focus on designing unsupervised loss functions or sample-filtering heuristics but do not explicitly model the dynamics of model parameters during online adaptation. Under non-i.i.d. data streams, this omission leads to unstable parameter trajectories and eventual model collapse, yet no principled framework exists to diagnose or regulate how parameters evolve over time during adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained vision model (ViT, Swin Transformer, or D2V) and a sequential stream of unlabeled RGB test images drawn from evolving target domains (e.g., 15 corruption types in ImageNet-C presented in fixed order). Only normalization-layer parameters (batch norm or layer norm) are updated via SGD with momentum 0.9 and batch size 64, using very small learning rates (1e-5 to 1e-3). The output is a class prediction at each step plus a dynamically rescaled parameter trajectory, where the effective update interval is modulated per-step by the SIGMA correction mechanism.

## Method

The paper derives a probabilistic framework by approximating SGD with a continuous-time stochastic differential equation (SDE) and discretizing it to obtain a Gaussian transition distribution over parameters, whose variance term σ²Δt² captures local gradient uncertainty. The key diagnostic is the log-variance portrait v_k = log(σ²Δt²), which empirically follows an inverse-gamma (IG) distribution under stable, high-performing adaptation and deviates from it under collapse. SIGMA enforces IG alignment through three per-step operations: (1) an Estimate step that fits an IG distribution to the history of calibrated log-variances via KL minimization using the Nelder-Mead simplex method; (2) an Align step that computes correction coefficients (location shift c_k ≤ 0, scale factor b_k > 1) to map the current log-variance onto the target IG; and (3) a Conjugate step that rescales the effective update interval as Δt_k = (λ r_k T_k) Δt, where r_k corrects location misalignment and T_k dampens high-variance updates, then recursively integrates the modified transition to produce the posterior mean used for prediction.

## Results

On ImageNet-C under the Correlated Input setting (D2V backbone), SIGMA-augmented ROID achieves the best AER of 41.8%±0.14, outperforming the unmodified ROID baseline (44.3%) by 2.5% and the source model (51.8%) by 10.0%. SIGMA yields the largest relative gain when applied to EATA, reducing AER from 50.2% to 44.8% (5.4% improvement). Under the more challenging Correlated Label setting (γ=0.1), ROID+SIGMA reaches 37.7%±0.01 AER, a 3.2% improvement over ROID alone, while TENT+SIGMA recovers 6.1% over TENT, effectively reversing the collapse observed in the unmodified method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIGMA is applied as a plug-in module on top of four of the six baselines (TENT, EATA, DeYO, ROID), consistently improving each. The largest gain is with EATA, where SIGMA reduces AER from 50.2% to 44.8% (5.4% absolute improvement) on Correlated Input, and from 43.3% to 40.9% (2.4%) under Correlated Label (γ=0.1). ROID+SIGMA achieves the best overall AER of 41.8%, beating the unmodified ROID by 2.5% and the next-best baseline (DeYO at 47.1%) by 5.3%. TENT benefits by 1.8% on Correlated Input but shows the most dramatic recovery under Correlated Label, improving by 6.1% (γ=0.1) and 4.9% (γ=0.0), effectively preventing the collapse that drives TENT's AER above the source model. DeYO sees a modest 0.6% gain on Correlated Input, suggesting its sample-filtering mechanism already provides partial IG alignment. RoTTA and SAR are reported as unmodified baselines (51.4% and 51.2% respectively) and are substantially outperformed by all SIGMA-augmented methods, confirming that parameter-dynamics regularization via IG alignment is more effective than student-teacher or sharpness-aware strategies for non-i.i.d. adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant TTA paradigm of designing better unsupervised loss functions or sample-filtering heuristics (TENT, EATA, DeYO, ROID) by instead modeling the parameter trajectory itself through a stochastic differential equation approximation of SGD, building on the SDE framework of Li et al. [33] and its generalizations [35]. The key conceptual shift is treating adaptation as a continuous-time stochastic process governed by the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov equation, then discretizing it to obtain a tractable transition distribution whose log-variance serves as a stability diagnostic. SIGMA is positioned as a plug-in that modulates the effective update interval to preserve inverse-gamma alignment in this log-variance, rather than altering the loss or filtering strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, distributional lens for understanding why TTA methods succeed or collapse under non-i.i.d. streams, and the finding that sample-filtering methods implicitly promote IG alignment offers a unifying explanation for their robustness. However, the practical gains are moderate (1.8–5.4% AER reduction), the framework is validated only on vision classification with normalization-layer adaptation, and the IG alignment is an empirical regularity rather than a proven necessary condition. Open questions include whether the log-variance/IG structure generalizes to other modalities, deeper parameter updates, or supervised online learning, and whether the Nelder-Mead-based estimation introduces meaningful computational overhead at scale.
