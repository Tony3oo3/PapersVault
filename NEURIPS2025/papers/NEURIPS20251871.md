## Problem

Tiny UAVs cannot perform fully autonomous navigation and dynamic obstacle avoidance using only onboard resources because mainstream vision-based approaches (SLAM, object recognition, trajectory estimation) demand hundreds of gigaflops and gigabytes of memory, while existing event-camera methods still process events as dense frames, failing to exploit their inherent sparsity. Neuromorphic control of quadrotors remains largely unexplored, and no prior work achieves real-time avoidance of high-speed obstacles (up to 10 m/s) during active navigation without external infrastructure.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a single monocular event camera stream (asynchronous x, y, polarity, timestamp events) and IMU odometry data, with no depth sensor, RGB camera, or external positioning. The SCNN calibration module takes two 50 ms event-stream windows (one recorded at a snapshot point during outbound flight, one captured near that point during inbound flight) and outputs a 3-DoF relative pose vector (Δx, Δy, Δφ). The Event RF obstacle-avoidance module processes the raw event stream asynchronously and outputs a 2D energy map that is converted into a continuous evasion command (direction and intensity) via gradient descent in an artificial potential field, with a total end-to-end latency of 2.3 ms.

## Method

The pipeline has two co-located modules sharing one event camera on the Speck neuromorphic SoC (327K neurons, ≤8 SNN layers). For navigation, a Siamese SCNN compares outbound and inbound event streams to compute IMU drift correction, enabling visual-homing with periodic recalibration every 7.5 m so that cubic IMU error never exceeds the 60 cm catchment radius. For obstacle avoidance, the Event RF model computationally replicates the frog R3 ganglion cell's ERF–IRF mechanism: each event excites a 2D elliptical Gaussian (ERF) with fast exponential decay (τe = 5 ms) while a delayed, slower-decaying inhibitory field (IRF, τi = 25 ms, Δt = 5 ms) suppresses static backgrounds; the spatial asymmetry (A1/A2 = 1.22) and threshold ratio (Ith/Eth = 1.2) ensure only moving objects sustain high energy. The resulting energy map is binarized, dilated via connected-component analysis to flag dangerous clusters, and fed into a gradient-descent potential-field controller that directly emits evasion maneuvers—completely bypassing object recognition and trajectory prediction. The authors also release the first monocular event-based pose-correction dataset (50,234 paired event streams with 4-DoF ground truth from a 12-camera Vicon system) to train and benchmark the SCNN.

## Results

The proposed neuromorphic system achieves a dynamic obstacle avoidance latency of 2.34 ms, an 88.6% reduction compared to the average latency of competing methods, while maintaining a position error of only 0.02 m and a 94.5% avoidance success rate. In the combined navigation-and-avoidance simulation, the quadrotor achieves 100%, 98%, and 94% success rates across three maps of increasing difficulty (40–130 m flight distance). Real-world indoor experiments confirm 100% avoidance success over 10 trials, and total system energy consumption is reduced to 21% of the traditional architecture baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two RGB-D-based baselines (Xu et al. 2023 RA-L and ICRA), the proposed method dominates on every metric: latency drops from 19.12/39.49 ms to 2.34 ms, position error improves from 0.11 m to 0.02 m, and success rate rises from 89.1% to 94.5% (the RA-L variant is only marginally higher at 96.3%). Compared to Falanga et al. (Science Robotics 2020), the closest event-camera competitor, the proposed system achieves lower latency (2.34 vs 3.56 ms) and substantially lower position error (0.02 vs 0.09 m) while also improving success rate (94.5% vs 86.7%), though Falanga's method lacks a navigation module and thus dedicates all compute to avoidance. Against the LiDAR-based NavRL method (Xu et al. 2025), the proposed approach is faster in both GPU (2.34 vs 14 ms) and onboard (2.34 vs 27 ms) settings, with a success rate within 1.25 percentage points of the GPU variant (94.5% vs 95.75%) and well above the onboard variant (86.5%), while using only a monocular event camera and IMU rather than a LiDAR sensor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads—neuromorphic quadrotor control (Vitale et al., Paredes-Vallés et al.), insect-inspired visual-homing navigation (Cartwright & Collet, Van Dijk et al.), and frog retinotectal ERF/IRF motion detection (Lettvin et al., Hoshino et al.)—into a single end-to-end pipeline on the Speck neuromorphic SoC. It departs from prior event-camera obstacle avoidance work (notably Falanga et al.) by processing raw asynchronous event streams directly rather than binning them into 'event frames,' and from conventional dynamic avoidance methods by entirely bypassing object recognition and trajectory estimation in favor of a bio-inspired static-suppression filter plus potential-field gradient descent. The visual-homing module with SCNN-based pose correction extends the snapshot-homing literature by replacing frame-based snapshots with event streams and introducing the first monocular event-based pose correction dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.3 ms end-to-end avoidance latency (88.6% lower than the next-best method) and 79% energy reduction make real-time dynamic obstacle avoidance feasible on sub-1 kg platforms with no external infrastructure, opening the door to GPS-denied search-and-rescue and inspection missions. Key limitations remain: the monocular sensor provides no depth, the system fails below ~1 lux, and dense-crowd avoidance drops to ~55–62% success rate, suggesting the potential-field approach struggles with many simultaneous obstacles. Future work on stereo event vision and fully unfamiliar environments (without pre-recorded snapshots) would be needed to move from structured return-to-base navigation to open-world exploration.
