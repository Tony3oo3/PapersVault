## Problem

LoRA's two-factor low-rank update (BA^T) does not explicitly optimize the input and output subspaces during training, leaving a persistent performance gap versus full fine-tuning. SVD-based initialization methods (PiSSA, MiLoRA) only guide the starting point but provide no geometric constraint on how subspaces evolve, and the choice of leading vs. trailing singular vectors remains heuristic. The paper asks whether directly learning orthonormal subspaces on the Stiefel manifold throughout training can close this gap in a principled, data-driven way.

## Input / Output

Given a frozen pre-trained weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n}, StelLA learns three adapter parameters per layer: U ∈ St(r,m), V ∈ St(r,n) (orthonormal column matrices), and S ∈ R^{r×r}, producing the adapted weight W̃ = W + (α/r)USV^T. The adapter adds only r(m+n)+r² parameters per layer (r² more than LoRA), and at inference the adapter is merged into W with zero additional latency. The method is applied to linear layers in LLMs (Q, K, V, FFN projections), ViT attention (Q, V), and Stable Diffusion U-Net cross-attention, spanning text and RGB-image modalities.

## Method

StelLA replaces LoRA's BA^T with a three-factor USV^T decomposition where U and V are constrained to the Stiefel manifold (orthonormal columns), mirroring the truncated SVD structure and separating subspace direction from scaling. Optimization proceeds via a modular Riemannian algorithm: Euclidean gradients are converted to Riemannian gradients (Eq. 2), passed through any standard Euclidean optimizer (Adam, SGD) to produce a tentative update, the perturbed direction is projected back onto the tangent space (Eq. 3), and a polar retraction (Eq. 4) maps the result back to the manifold. A gradient-scaling factor √(d/m) and √(d/n) compensates for the different entry magnitudes of U and V when m ≠ n, which is critical in LLM feed-forward layers where dimensions differ by 4×. The polar retraction is computed via batched SVD across all same-shaped U/V matrices, yielding a 15–20× speedup and keeping total training overhead to ~15% over vanilla LoRA.

## Results

On the commonsense reasoning benchmark, StelLA achieves 82.33% average accuracy on LLaMA2-7B and 86.72% on LLaMA3-8B, outperforming the strongest baseline (ScaledAdamW at 80.88% and 85.40%) by +1.3 and +1.3 points respectively, while attaining the top score on all eight sub-tasks for both models. On math and code generation (LLaMA2-7B), StelLA reaches 39.30% average, surpassing LoRA (37.76%), DoRA (36.61%), and PiSSA (37.41%) by up to +2.69 points. For image classification, StelLA achieves 91.20% (ViT-Base) and 92.00% (ViT-Large) average top-1 accuracy, and for text-to-image generation it reduces FID by up to 7.11 points over LoRA on Stable Diffusion 1.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StelLA consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated task domain. Against LoRA [29], StelLA gains +1.57 points on commonsense (LLaMA2-7B), +1.54 on math/code, +0.26 on image classification (ViT-Base), and reduces FID by up to 5.23 on text-to-image. DoRA [40], the strongest single LoRA variant, is surpassed by +1.33 on commonsense, +2.69 on math/code, +0.25 on image classification, and by 2.79 FID points on SD1.5. PiSSA [43] (SVD-based initialization) is beaten by +1.50 on commonsense, +1.89 on math/code, +0.59 on image classification, and by a large margin on text-to-image (PiSSA degrades severely with FID >290). Among the three-factor baselines, TriLoRA [20] and MoSLoRA [66] both underperform StelLA by 5.17 and 1.89 points respectively on commonsense, confirming that the Stiefel manifold constraint (not merely the three-factor form) drives the gain. OLoRA [10] (QR-based orthonormal initialization) and ScaledAdamW [72] (Riemannian preconditioning) are also outperformed, with ScaledAdamW being the closest competitor at 85.40% vs. StelLA's 86.72% on LLaMA3-8B commonsense.

## Relation to Prior Work

StelLA extends LoRA's two-factor BA^T adapter into a three-factor USV^T decomposition analogous to truncated SVD, but unlike SVD-based initialization methods (PiSSA, MiLoRA, OLoRA) that only guide the starting point, it constrains U and V to the Stiefel manifold throughout training so that input and output subspaces are learned end-to-end. It departs from prior three-factor LoRA variants (TriLoRA, MoSLoRA) by enforcing orthonormality via Riemannian optimization rather than leaving the factors unconstrained, and from AdaLoRA/GeoLoRA by using a modular retraction-based optimizer wrapper rather than regularization or gradient-flow tricks for orthogonality. The geometric optimization design—converting any Euclidean optimizer's update into a tangent-space step followed by polar retraction—differs from tightly coupled Riemannian optimizers (Riemannian Adam, Riemannian SGD) by decoupling the manifold constraint from the base optimizer's internal state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StelLA offers a principled, drop-in replacement for LoRA that maintains subspace orthonormality during training, with consistent gains across NLU, NLG, image classification, and text-to-image generation while adding only ~15% training overhead and negligible extra parameters. The modular optimizer interface (pre-hook for Riemannian gradient conversion, post-hook for projection and retraction) makes it easy to integrate into existing PEFT pipelines, and the ablation showing robustness to initialization (random vs. SVD-major vs. SVD-minor) suggests the geometric optimization itself, not a lucky starting point, drives the gains. Open questions include combining StelLA with rank-adaptive strategies (AdaLoRA), extending to tensor-valued layers via product Stiefel manifolds, and validating at very large model scales where the SVD-based retraction cost may become more pronounced.
