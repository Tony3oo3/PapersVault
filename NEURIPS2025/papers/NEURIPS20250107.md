## Problem

Transformers handle diverse data modalities through position embeddings, but extending self-attention to hierarchical, multi-scale data (e.g., documents with paragraph/sentence/token structure, or multi-modal articles with text and images at different granularities) remains unsolved in a principled way. Existing hierarchical and multi-modal transformer architectures rely on ad hoc heuristics that are problem-specific and not seamlessly generalizable, while naively flattening hierarchical structure discards valuable inductive bias and increases computational cost quadratically.

## Input / Output

The method consumes data structured as a 'nested signal'—a tree where each level may occupy a different geometric domain (1D grid for text tokens, 2D grid for image patches, graph for webpage links) with domain-specific position embeddings. For language tasks, input is a 3-level hierarchy (paragraphs → sentences → tokens) with Word2Vec or T5 token embeddings; for multi-modal news, input combines text sub-modalities (headline, abstract, caption, body) and ViT image patches under a shared tree. The output is a block-constrained attention matrix where all leaf pairs within sibling subtrees share a single scalar weight, yielding O(M·b²) parameters instead of O(M²·b²), and ultimately a class label or reduced FLOPs count when used as a zero-shot replacement in pre-trained models.

## Method

The paper derives Hierarchical Self-Attention (HSA) from the first principle of entropy minimization by modeling a finite signal as a statistical mechanical system with N particles, showing that standard Softmax attention emerges as the gradient descent step on a variational entropy upper bound. Generalizing to nested signals, it defines an interaction energy ψ between unrelated tree nodes using the position-embedding dissimilarity of their highest distinct ancestors plus the average Euclidean distance between their leaf nodes, then recursively composes subtree energies via a weighted log-sum-exp. The gradient of the total hierarchy energy yields an attention update whose weight matrix satisfies a 'block constraint' (one shared value per sibling-subtree pair), provably making HSA the closest block-constrained stochastic matrix to flat Softmax attention in total KL-divergence (Theorem 3.2). A two-pass dynamic programming algorithm (bottom-up for sufficient statistics, top-down for attention vectors) computes all leaf gradients in O(M·b²) time. The mechanism can be used to train transformers from scratch with hierarchical inductive bias or to zero-shot replace Softmax layers in pre-trained models (e.g., RoBERTa), cutting self-attention FLOPs by up to 50× with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On sentiment classification, HSA consistently outperforms Flat Self-Attention (FSA) across both datasets and token embeddings: on IMDB, HSA achieves 81.29% accuracy with T5 embeddings vs. 75.77% for FSA (+5.5 pp), and 74.69% vs. 67.39% with Word2Vec (+7.3 pp); on Elec, the gains are 85.21% vs. 82.12% (T5) and 75.49% vs. 71.82% (Word2Vec). On the multi-modal N24News task, HSA reaches 79.52% accuracy and 80.91% F1, surpassing both FSA (79.21% / 79.02%) and DeepSet (75.78% / 75.90%). In the zero-shot RoBERTa approximation setting, replacing selected layers with HSA reduces self-attention FLOPs by up to ~50× (e.g., IMDB: 214.94M → 4.32M MFLOPs) while incurring only a 0.6 pp accuracy drop (95.58% → 94.94%), though QNLI suffers a larger drop (92.67% → 50.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Flat Self-Attention (standard Softmax attention, Vaswani et al. 2017), HSA wins on every sentiment classification configuration: +7.3 pp accuracy on IMDB with Word2Vec, +5.5 pp with T5, +3.7 pp on Elec with Word2Vec, and +3.1 pp with T5. On the multi-modal N24News task, HSA (79.52% Acc, 80.91% F1) edges out FSA (79.21% / 79.02%) and clearly beats the DeepSet multi-modal baseline (Zaheer et al. 2017) by ~3.7 pp accuracy and ~5 pp F1, despite DeepSet also incorporating the image modality. In the zero-shot RoBERTa approximation experiment (Liu et al. 2019), HSA-RoBERTa matches or nearly matches the original model on most benchmarks (e.g., IMDB: 94.94% vs. 95.58%; AGNEWS: 94.22% vs. 94.69%; SST-2: 90.25% vs. 94.03%) while cutting self-attention FLOPs by 10–50×, but QNLI is a notable failure case where accuracy drops from 92.67% to 50.72%, suggesting that certain task types are more sensitive to the block-constraint approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Geometric Deep Learning signal formalism (Bronstein et al.) and the Transformer's self-attention mechanism, but departs from both the heuristic hierarchical transformer architectures (e.g., Hi-Transformer, H-Transformer-1D, Swin) and the ad hoc multi-modal fusion strategies (single-stream, multi-stream, hybrid-stream) by deriving a unified attention mechanism from the first principle of entropy minimization. The statistical-mechanical interpretation of attention connects to prior Potts-model and Hopfield-network views, but the key novelty is generalizing the variational entropy-minimization derivation to nested signals, yielding a block-constrained attention matrix that is provably the closest KL-divergence approximation to flat Softmax attention. The explicit position-embedding treatment is partly inspired by LieTransformer's approach of separating positional similarity from feature interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The optimality guarantee (Theorem 3.2) provides a principled, non-heuristic recipe for incorporating hierarchical and multi-modal inductive biases into transformers, addressing a gap where prior work relied on problem-specific architectural choices. The zero-shot FLOPs reduction (e.g., 214.94M → 4.32M MFLOPs on IMDB) is practically attractive for long-context inference, though the accuracy drops are inconsistent across tasks (QNLI collapses from 92.7% to 50.7%), limiting near-term deployment. Open questions include whether HSA-based training scales to large foundational models, how it extends to autoregressive decoders, and whether the fixed hopping-window hierarchy used in zero-shot experiments can be replaced by learned or task-specific hierarchies to close the accuracy gap.
