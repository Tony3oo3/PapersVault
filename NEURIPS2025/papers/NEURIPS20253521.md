## Problem

Existing unsupervised skill discovery methods—whether mutual-information-based (DIAYN, DADS) or distance-maximizing (CSD, METRA)—encourage state diversity or maximal latent deviation but ignore the temporal periodicity inherent in locomotion and other rhythmic behaviors. No prior method provides a mechanism to discover skills with controllable periods across multiple timescales, leaving the 'how to behave' dimension of skill learning unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a proprioceptive state vector (or 90×90×3 RGB frames for pixel-based settings) together with a scalar period variable L, which is projected through sinusoidal positional encoding before entering the encoder and policy networks. It produces a periodic skill policy π(a|s,L) whose rollouts exhibit a period of exactly 2L timesteps, and a circular latent encoding φ_L(s) in which states are positioned at the vertices of a regular 2L-gon of diameter L centered at the origin. When combined with METRA, the policy additionally takes a continuous skill direction z, yielding π(a|s,z,L) that independently controls both temporal direction and period.

## Method

PSD trains an encoder φ_L that maps states to a circular latent space by maximizing a constrained objective: the L-step latent distance is pushed to its upper bound L (placing states L steps apart at antipodal points), while the 1-step distance is capped at L·sin(π/2L) (the side length of a regular 2L-gon), and a centering term forces opposite points to be symmetric about the origin. The constraint is made tractable via dual gradient descent with Lagrange multipliers and a small relaxation ε. The RL policy is trained with SAC using a single-step intrinsic reward r_PSD = exp(−κΔ²), where Δ measures deviation from the optimal arc length, so the agent is incentivized to trace the circular path and complete a full 2L cycle. An adaptive sampling mechanism dynamically expands or contracts the range [L_min, L_max] based on the policy's cumulative reward at the boundary periods, enabling discovery of dynamically feasible periods without prior knowledge. Theorem 1 proves that the optimal encoder solution is exactly a regular 2L-gon, and the framework composes with METRA by mutually conditioning each encoder on the other's skill variable to prevent period collapse.

## Results

On four downstream locomotion tasks (hurdle and friction variants for HalfCheetah and Walker2D), PSD achieves the highest average return across all settings, evaluated over 10 seeds. The largest gains appear on HalfCheetah-hurdle (3.8 vs. 1.9 for METRA, the strongest baseline) and HalfCheetah-friction (43.4 vs. 30.1 for METRA). PSD also outperforms all baselines on Walker2D-hurdle (5.4 vs. 4.1 for CSD) and Walker2D-friction (8.7 vs. 5.3 for CSD). Beyond downstream performance, PSD uniquely discovers skills spanning a wide range of periods (verified via Fourier analysis), whereas baselines concentrate on narrow frequency bands or produce static/random behaviors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSD outperforms all four baselines on every downstream task. Against the MI-based methods (DIAYN, DADS), PSD's advantage is most pronounced on HalfCheetah-hurdle (3.8 vs. 0.6/0.9), where MI-based skills fail to provide the multi-timescale coordination needed for irregularly spaced hurdles. Against the distance-maximizing methods (CSD, METRA), PSD still leads but by a smaller margin on Walker2D-hurdle (5.4 vs. 4.1 for CSD) and Walker2D-friction (8.7 vs. 5.3 for CSD); METRA is the strongest single baseline, reaching 30.1 on HalfCheetah-friction, yet PSD extends this to 43.4. Qualitatively, Fourier analysis of skill trajectories (Figure 3) shows PSD discovers a broad spectrum of dominant frequencies, while METRA and CSD concentrate on narrow high-frequency bands and DIAYN/DADS produce largely static or random patterns. The paper further demonstrates that combining PSD with METRA (fully unsupervised) yields a richer behavioral repertoire than either method alone, as the two capture orthogonal temporal properties (period length vs. movement direction).

## Relation to Prior Work

PSD carves new ground within unsupervised skill discovery by introducing a circular latent geometry that explicitly encodes temporal periodicity, in contrast to MI-based methods (DIAYN, DADS) that maximize state distinguishability and distance-maximizing methods (CSD, METRA) that maximize latent traversal. Conceptually, it repurposes METRA's temporal-distance encoding but adds a geometric constraint (regular 2L-gon on a circle of diameter L) that links latent geometry directly to period length, enabling a single-step intrinsic reward to produce behaviors of controllable periodicity. The approach also departs from prior periodic-motion learning (CPGs, hand-crafted phase rewards, Fourier autoencoders like PAE/FLD) by being fully unsupervised and requiring no offline data or domain-specific reward engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSD provides a principled, scalable mechanism for discovering multi-timescale periodic behaviors without external rewards, which is directly relevant to legged locomotion and any domain with rhythmic structure. The combination with METRA demonstrates that temporal direction and temporal length are orthogonal axes of skill control, opening a path toward fully unsupervised skill libraries with rich temporal structure. Key limitations include the restriction to locomotion tasks in evaluation, potential underperformance under large persistent disturbances where periodicity is infeasible, and the open question of whether circular geometry generalizes to non-periodic tasks like manipulation. Integrating frequency-domain representations directly into training is identified as a promising future direction.
