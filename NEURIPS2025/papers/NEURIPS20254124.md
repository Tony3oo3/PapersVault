## Problem

In Bayesian Optimization with Preference Exploration (BOPE), the decision maker's utility function is typically monotonic—higher objective values should not decrease utility—yet existing surrogate models (GPs, linear models) do not enforce this constraint, potentially degrading sample efficiency. Additionally, the use of neural networks with uncertainty quantification in pairwise comparison settings remains understudied, with Bayesian Neural Networks via Hamiltonian Monte Carlo facing significant challenges due to scale invariance in preference learning.

## Input / Output

The method alternates between two stages: in Experimentation, a decision variable x is selected and evaluated through an expensive black-box multi-output function f_true to obtain objective values; in Preference Exploration, the experimenter presents a pair of previously observed outputs to the DM, who responds with a binary preference (±1) subject to Gaussian utility noise. The utility surrogate (MoNNE) is trained solely on these pairwise comparisons via Hinge loss, and the acquisition functions (qNEIUU for the next evaluation point, IEUBO for the next comparison pair) are computed by Monte Carlo sampling over the ensemble and the GP output model.

## Method

MoNNE is an ensemble of 8 three-layer neural networks (d×100, 100×10, 10×1 with Swish activation) whose weights are exponentiated to guarantee positivity and thus monotonicity in each input dimension, following Sill's monotonic network construction. Training uses a Hinge loss over pairwise comparisons with a learned margin parameter α, and outputs are normalized across ensemble members to handle the scale invariance inherent in preference data. For uncertainty, the ensemble mean and variance replace the need for full Bayesian inference, which the paper shows is problematic for HMC-trained BNNs under pairwise comparisons due to independent priors conflicting with the required parameter correlations. The acquisition function for preference exploration is modified to IEUBO, which assumes independence between the two utility predictions being compared and computes E[max(X,Y)] analytically for independent Gaussians, making it more robust under utility noise than the correlated EUBO.

## Results

Across six multi-objective test problems (DTLZ2, VLMOP3, ZDT1, OSY, VehicleSafety, CarCabDesign), BOPE-MoNNE consistently achieves the lowest simple regret among all BOPE algorithms, reaching regret below 0.001 in many settings. In terms of surrogate model uncertainty quality (a modified cross-entropy metric), MoNNE scores 0.863–0.951 across problems, substantially outperforming GP (0.622–0.674) and BMNN-VI (0.731–0.911). Prediction accuracy follows the same ordering: MoNNE achieves 86–96% correct preference predictions versus 82–92% for GP and 84–94% for BMNN-VI. The ablation study confirms that both monotonicity constraints and the ensemble component are critical, with the full model outperforming variants where either is removed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOPE-MoNNE outperforms all preference-learning baselines (BOPE-GP, BOPE-BMNN, BOPE-Linear, PBO) and non-preference multi-objective methods (qNParEGO, qNEHVI) across all six test problems in terms of simple regret. Against BOPE-GP [Lin et al., 2022], the primary direct competitor, MoNNE's advantage is consistent and attributed to superior uncertainty estimation (0.86–0.95 vs. 0.62–0.67 uncertainty quality) and the monotonicity constraint, which prevents non-physical utility predictions. Against PBO [Astudillo et al., 2023], which models preferences in the design space rather than the output space, BOPE-MoNNE also wins, confirming that comparing outputs (objective values) is more appropriate for the BOPE setting. BOPE-Linear slightly outperforms MoNNE only when the true utility is linear (as expected), but MoNNE dominates on all non-linear utilities including Cobb-Douglas and LinearExponential. The Known Utility upper bound (Composite BO) remains the best overall, as expected, but BOPE-MoNNE closes the gap substantially, particularly in later iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BOPE framework introduced by Lin et al. (2022), which models DM preferences with a GP utility surrogate and uses EUBO for pairwise comparison selection. The key departure is replacing the GP with a Monotonic Neural Network Ensemble (MoNNE) that enforces monotonicity via positive weight transformations (following Sill, 1997) and quantifies uncertainty through ensembles (following Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017). The paper also introduces IEUBO, an analytically computable acquisition function that assumes independence between utility predictions, and provides the first systematic investigation of why HMC-trained BNNs fail in pairwise comparison settings due to scale invariance conflicting with independent parameter priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Monotonicity is a fundamental economic property of utility functions that prior BOPE methods ignored, and this paper demonstrates that enforcing it yields consistent performance gains across diverse test problems. The finding that HMC-trained BNNs are unsuitable for preference learning (requiring ~2000 samples vs. ~100 for ensembles) is a practical warning for the broader preference learning community. Open questions include whether HMC can be adapted with correlated priors to handle scale invariance, how the approach extends to group decision-making with multiple DMs, and whether the monotonicity constraint remains beneficial in very high-dimensional output spaces.
