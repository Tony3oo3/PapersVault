## Problem

Current automated feature description methods for LLMs assign a single textual explanation per neuron or SAE feature, implicitly assuming monosemanticity. However, growing evidence shows that individual features encode multiple, semantically distinct concepts (polysemanticity), and this single-description constraint limits both the expressiveness of explanations and the ability to capture the full range of behaviors encoded in model internals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252780/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PRISM takes as input a text corpus (C4, processed to 512-token excerpts), a target feature identified by layer and neuron/SAE index, a sentence embedding model (gte-Qwen2-1.5B-instruct), and a labeling LLM (Gemini 1.5 Pro). It produces k=5 natural-language concept descriptions per feature, a scalar polysemanticity score (mean pairwise cosine similarity of description embeddings, where lower values indicate greater conceptual diversity), and per-description quality scores (AUROC and MAD) computed via the adapted COSY protocol using 1,000 Cosmopedia control samples and 10 LLM-generated concept-specific samples.

## Method

PRISM is a three-step pipeline: (1) Percentile Sampling selects 1,000 high-activation text excerpts by drawing one sample per percentile bin across the 99th–100th percentile range (step 1e-05), capturing a broader spectrum of strong activations than top-k sampling; (2) Concept Clustering embeds these excerpts with a sentence transformer and partitions them into k=5 clusters via k-means; (3) Cluster Labeling prompts an LLM with the 20 highest-activation sentences per cluster (with token spans exceeding the 90th-percentile activation threshold highlighted in square brackets) to generate a concise shared-concept summary. For evaluation, PRISM introduces a polysemanticity score based on pairwise cosine similarity among the k description embeddings, and adapts the COSY contrastive protocol to language models, computing AUROC and Mean Activation Difference between activations on concept-specific and control samples. The key distinction from prior work is that the framework is set-valued by design—each feature is mapped to a subset of descriptions rather than a single label—enabling both monosemantic and polysemantic features to be characterized and quantified.

## Results

PRISM (max) achieves the highest AUROC and MAD scores across most model–feature combinations. On GPT-2 XL MLP neurons, PRISM (max) reaches AUROC 0.85 (95% CI 0.78–0.91) and MAD 91.67%, substantially outperforming GPT-Explain (AUROC 0.64, MAD 65.00%). On Llama 3.1 8B Instruct MLP neurons, PRISM (max) attains AUROC 0.71 and MAD 81.67%, compared to Transluce-Explain (AUROC 0.59, MAD 63.33%) and Output-Centric (AUROC 0.55, MAD 58.33%). The sole exception is Gemma Scope SAE features, where Neuronpedia slightly outperforms PRISM (max) on both AUROC (0.62 vs. 0.54) and MAD (63.33% vs. 38.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM (max) decisively outperforms GPT-Explain on GPT-2 XL MLP neurons, improving AUROC from 0.64 to 0.85 and MAD from 65.00% to 91.67%. On Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, PRISM (max) beats both Transluce-Explain (AUROC 0.59 → 0.71; MAD 63.33% → 81.67%) and Output-Centric (AUROC 0.55 → 0.71; MAD 58.33% → 81.67%). For GPT-2 Small SAE features, PRISM (max) edges out Neuronpedia on AUROC (0.57 vs. 0.54) and MAD (28.81% vs. 18.97%), and also surpasses Output-Centric (AUROC 0.57 vs. 0.57; MAD 28.81% vs. 22.03%). The one clear loss is on Gemma Scope SAE features, where Neuronpedia achieves higher AUROC (0.62 vs. 0.54) and MAD (63.33% vs. 38.33%), suggesting that output-space alignment may offer complementary advantages for certain SAE architectures. Overall, neuron-based features (GPT-2 XL, Llama 3.1 8B) yield more reliable and higher-scoring interpretations than SAE-based features across all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM extends the automated feature description line of work initiated by GPT-Explain [21] and continued by Transluce-Explain [23], Neuronpedia [42], and Output-Centric [25], all of which assign a single textual description per neuron or SAE feature. The key conceptual departure is the multi-concept formulation: rather than forcing a single label, PRISM clusters high-activation samples and generates k descriptions per feature, explicitly accommodating polysemanticity. For evaluation, it adapts the CoSy framework [39] (AUROC and MAD) to language models and introduces a novel polysemanticity score based on pairwise cosine similarity among a feature's description embeddings, a dimension absent from prior feature description evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing both a multi-concept description method and a quantitative polysemanticity metric, PRISM gives the interpretability community a systematic way to move beyond the monosemanticity assumption that has constrained prior work. The finding that features previously annotated as 'possibly monosemantic' in OpenAI's automated interpretability release are in fact highly polysemantic underscores how much structure single-label methods miss. Open questions remain: the fixed cluster count (k=5) may under- or over-specify the true number of concepts, textual descriptions cannot capture purely algorithmic or graph-structured computations, and the reliance on LLM-generated labels introduces faithfulness risks that the sanity checks only partially address. The authors also call for standardized human-evaluation benchmarks for multi-concept descriptions, a gap that will grow as interpretability scales to larger models.
