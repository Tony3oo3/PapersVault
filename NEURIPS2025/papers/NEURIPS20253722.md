## Problem

Stochastic convex optimization (SCO) under a variance-bounded gradient oracle is solved by SGD at rate O(R²σ²/ε² + R²L²/ε²), but the R²L²/ε² term is suboptimal at small target precision ε where deterministic cutting plane methods achieve Õ(d). The open question is whether one can achieve the conjectured Õ(R²σ²/ε² + d) rate by exploiting the shape of the noise beyond its variance. This paper introduces isotropic noise—a stronger assumption bounding the noise magnitude in every direction with high probability—as the key structural condition that enables this rate, and provides matching lower bounds.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes query access to a stochastic gradient oracle for a convex L-Lipschitz function f: R^d → R with minimizer x* satisfying ‖x*‖ ≤ R. The oracle can be an ISGO (noise bounded in every unit direction with probability ≥ 1−δ), an ESGO (sub-exponential tails), a VSGO (bounded variance), or a QVSGO (quantum sampling oracle with bounded variance). The output is an ε-optimal point x with f(x) ≤ f* + ε, returned with probability at least 2/3. For the quantum isotropifier subroutine, the output is an unbiased estimate μ̂ of E[X] such that |⟨v, μ̂ − E[X]⟩| ≤ ε for every unit vector v with high probability, i.e., the error is isotropic rather than merely bounded in ℓ₂-norm.

## Method

The core algorithm is a two-stage stochastic cutting plane method. Stage 1 runs a center-of-gravity cutting plane algorithm for the (2R, r)-feasibility problem, where the halfspace oracle at point x is given by −g̃(x, x−x*), a Marginal Approximate Gradient Oracle (MAGO) that only requires tight error η in the direction toward x* while allowing polynomially large ℓ₂-error Γ = η√d. Each MAGO query is implemented by averaging K = O(σ²_I/(dη²)·log(d/ξ)) ISGO samples, exploiting the fact that isotropic noise concentrates in every direction simultaneously, yielding a d-factor savings over VSGO-based mini-batching. Stage 2 identifies the ε-optimal point among the O(d) candidates via iterative binary search on line segments, again using ISGO queries. For the quantum setting, a quantum isotropifier converts a QVSGO into an ISGO by combining a directional quantum mean estimation procedure (building on [22]) with boosted unbiased phase estimation [57] to eliminate the √d per-coordinate error accumulation, followed by a multi-level Monte Carlo debiasing scheme [10,4,53] that preserves the isotropic noise property while removing truncation bias.

## Results

The paper establishes a tight upper bound of $\tilde{O}(R^2\sigma_I^2/\epsilon^2 + d)$ queries for stochastic convex optimization under isotropic noise (Theorem 1), matching a lower bound up to polylog factors (Corollary 2). As a corollary, sub-exponential noise yields the new state-of-the-art rate $\tilde{O}(R^2\sigma_E^2/\epsilon^2 + d)$ (Corollary 3), while variance-bounded noise gives $\tilde{O}(dR^2\sigma_V^2/\epsilon^2 + d)$ (Corollary 4). For quantum SCO, the paper achieves $\tilde{O}(dR\sigma_V/\epsilon)$ queries to a QVSGO (Theorem 7), a polynomial improvement in dimension over the prior state-of-the-art $\tilde{O}(d^{3/2}\sigma_V R/\epsilon)$ from Sidford & Zhang (2024).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's ISGO upper bound $\tilde{O}(R^2\sigma_I^2/\epsilon^2 + d)$ improves upon SGD's $O(R^2\sigma_V^2/\epsilon^2 + R^2L^2/\epsilon^2)$ by a factor of $d$ in the non-stochastic term when $L \gg \max\{\sigma, \epsilon\sqrt{d}/R\}$, capturing high-precision, low-variance regimes. For sub-exponential noise, the $\tilde{O}(R^2\sigma_E^2/\epsilon^2 + d)$ rate (Corollary 3) is shown to be tight up to polylog factors via a matching lower bound (Theorem 5), effectively resolving Open Problem 1 in this special case. Under a general VSGO, the paper's $\tilde{O}(dR^2\sigma_V^2/\epsilon^2 + d)$ rate (Corollary 4) still outperforms SGD when $L \gg \sqrt{d}\cdot\max\{\sigma_V, \epsilon/R\}$, though it carries an extra $d$-factor over the conjectured optimal rate. For quantum SCO, the new $\tilde{O}(dR\sigma_V/\epsilon)$ bound (Theorem 7) achieves a polynomial improvement in dimension over the prior $\tilde{O}(d^{3/2}\sigma_V R/\epsilon)$ result of Sidford and Zhang (2024), which was the best known quantum algorithm for this problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic cutting plane framework of Sidford and Zhang [53] (NeurIPS 2024), which achieved state-of-the-art quantum SCO rates by controlling gradient error in full ℓ₂-norm via mini-batching. The key conceptual departure is showing that it suffices to control gradient error in a single direction (toward the optimum) rather than in norm, enabling a d-factor query savings. The quantum isotropifier subroutine builds on the multivariate mean estimation framework of Cornelissen et al. [22] (STOC 2022) and the boosted unbiased phase estimation of van Apeldoorn et al. [57] (SODA 2023), combined with a modified multi-level Monte Carlo debiasing scheme adapted from [10, 4, 53] to preserve isotropic noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves Open Problem 1 (achieving Õ(R²σ²/ε² + d) queries for SCO) in the affirmative under the new isotropic noise model, with matching lower bounds up to polylog factors, and as a corollary achieves a new state-of-the-art rate for sub-exponential noise. The quantum isotropifier is a potentially reusable subroutine that converts any variance-bounded quantum sampling oracle into one with isotropic error, broadening the applicability of the improved rates. A key limitation is that under the more general VSGO (variance-bounded) model, the achieved rate carries an extra d-factor compared to the conjectured optimum, leaving the full resolution of Open Problem 1 open; the authors also note the quantum rates are dimension-dependent with unclear practical impact.
