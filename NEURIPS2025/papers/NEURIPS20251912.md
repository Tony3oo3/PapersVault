## Problem

Existing image-text datasets either provide short, low-detail captions (e.g., COCO-caption averages 10 words) or rely on unverified VLM-generated descriptions that fall short of human-annotated quality. No prior dataset combines dense, human-verified panoptic segmentation masks with comprehensive scene-level captions, leaving a gap for fine-grained joint understanding and generation tasks that require both pixel-level grounding and rich textual description.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251912/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 118K training and 25K validation RGB images (from COCO2017 and Objects365) paired with detailed captions averaging 203–233 words, each grounded to an average of 13.2 panoptic masks via inline references like <4:a wooden coffee table>. For the PGC task, the model consumes a single RGB image and outputs an interleaved caption with typed segmentation tokens (<SEG_t> for thing instances, <SEG_s> for stuff regions) alongside the corresponding panoptic masks. For T2I generation, text prompts are fed to SD3-medium to produce images evaluated on FID, FD_dinov2, and CLIPScore.

## Method

The annotation pipeline overlays COCONut-S panoptic masks (133 classes) on images using set-of-marks visualization, prompts GPT-4V for region-level caption drafts, and then merges human-edited region captions into a single comprehensive image caption while preserving mask-to-phrase grounding. For the PGC task, PanCaper extends LISA by replacing the vision backbone with a pretrained kMaX-DeepLab encoder, introducing typed <SEG_t>/<SEG_s> tokens for panoptic thing/stuff segmentation, and aligning noun-phrase embeddings to COCO's 133 classes via BERT cosine similarity. A variant, PanCaper-Pro, additionally pools mask-level features into the LLM input to provide region-aware visual cues. For detailed captioning, LLaVA-NeXT-pool augments the standard AnyRes patch features with mask-pooled representations extracted from ground-truth (training) or kMaX-DeepLab proposals (inference), improving dense object description without changing the LLM architecture.

## Results

On the novel PGC (Joint Panoptic Segmentation and Grounded Captioning) task, PanCaper-Pro achieves the best scores across all metrics: CAPTURE 64.3, CIDEr 12.5, BLEU@4 6.4, METEOR 17.9 for captioning, and PQ 0.61 (PQ_thing 0.58, PQ_stuff 0.68) for segmentation. For text-to-image generation, fine-tuning SD3-medium with COCONut-PanCap yields the lowest FID of 21.4 on DOCCI-test and 23.1 on COCONut-PanCap-val, outperforming DOCCI (22.1/24.3) and COCO-caption (27.6/28.0). On VQA benchmarks, incorporating the full 118K COCONut-PanCap data into LLaVA-NeXT instruction tuning improves MM-Vet from 43.5 to 45.5 and MME from 1523 to 1612.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanCaper-Pro (the proposed method) outperforms all baselines on the PGC task: it beats LISA+ (57.9 CAPTURE, 0.50 PQ) by +6.4 CAPTURE and +0.11 PQ, and GLaMM GCG (56.8 CAPTURE, 0.55 PQ) by +7.5 CAPTURE and +0.06 PQ. The advantage is even more pronounced against the GranD-trained variants: LISA+ with GranDf scores only 46.2 CAPTURE and 0.43 PQ, while GLaMM GCG with GranDf drops to 43.2 CAPTURE and 0.27 PQ, attributed to incomplete segmentation annotations in GranD. For T2I generation, COCONut-PanCap training achieves FID 21.4 on DOCCI-test, beating DOCCI (22.1) and COCO-caption (27.6), and on GenEval it reaches 0.68 overall vs. 0.65 (DOCCI) and 0.62 (COCO-caption), with the largest gains on two-object composition (0.89 vs. 0.81/0.78). On referring segmentation, PanCaper+COCONut-PanCap achieves 76.2 on refCOCO-val, slightly trailing GLaMM (77.5) but surpassing LISA-7B (74.1) and PixelLM (73.0); the paper notes GLaMM benefits from additional SA-1B pretraining data, making the comparison uneven. For detailed captioning, LLaVA-NeXT-pool (61.4 CAPTURE) outperforms synthetic-caption variants from GPT-4V (56.2), Qwen2-VL (55.4), and InternVL-2 (53.9), confirming the quality advantage of human-verified annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) VLM-generated detailed caption datasets (DenseFusion1M, ShareGPT4V, Recap-DataComp1B), (2) human-annotated detailed caption datasets (DOCCI, ImageInWords, DCI), and (3) grounded captioning with segmentation masks (GLaMM/GranD, Osprey, MGLMM). It departs from prior work by combining human-in-the-loop annotation with COCONut panoptic segmentation masks to produce captions that are simultaneously detailed, human-verified, and pixel-grounded to both 'thing' and 'stuff' regions. The annotation pipeline leverages GPT-4V as a drafting tool with set-of-marks prompting, but the final captions are human-corrected, distinguishing it from purely synthetic datasets. The PGC baseline (PanCaper) builds on LISA's pixel-grounding architecture and kMaX-DeepLab's panoptic segmentation, extending both to handle multi-mask panoptic output jointly with caption generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COCONut-PanCap provides the first large-scale (118K) human-verified dataset with dense panoptic grounding (avg. 13.2 masks/image), filling a gap between small human-annotated caption sets (DOCCI: 9.6K, IIW: 8.5K) and large synthetic ones that lack quality guarantees. The PGC benchmark is a useful new evaluation setting that tests whether models can simultaneously produce accurate panoptic segmentation and coherent grounded captions. Key limitations include the COCO-centric image source (limiting domain diversity), the relatively small scale compared to billion-image synthetic datasets, and the reliance on COCONut-S masks which may not cover all scene elements. An open question is whether the human-in-the-loop annotation strategy can be scaled beyond COCO to diverse domains, and whether the grounding signal provides complementary benefits when combined with large-scale synthetic pretraining.
