## Problem

Response-level RL algorithms (DPO, GRPO, ReMax) assign a single terminal reward to an entire chain-of-thought, forcing all intermediate reasoning steps to share the same gradient direction and introducing high training variance. Standard actor-critic methods (PPO) attempt to provide step-level credit assignment but suffer from poor value estimation under sparse rewards and training instability from the non-stationary co-training of actor and critic. DAPO addresses both issues by decoupling critic training from policy optimization and providing dense step-level advantage signals.

## Input / Output

DAPO consumes a set of training queries (7.5K math problems from MATH or ~4K code problems from TACO) with only golden answers (no solution traces), a base LLM serving as the reference policy, and a critic initialized from a separate model (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct). For each query, the base model generates 32 solutions, 6 are selected for correct/incorrect balance, and each solution is segmented by line breaks into reasoning steps; the reference policy then produces 16 completions per step for Monte Carlo value estimation. The output is a fine-tuned LLM policy that generates improved chain-of-thought reasoning, evaluated via zero-shot greedy decoding on held-out benchmarks.

## Method

DAPO is a two-stage offline step-level RL algorithm. In Stage 1 (critic training), a generator produces training states, and the reference policy acts as a completer to sample N sub-trajectories from each state; the critic V_φ is trained with binary cross-entropy loss against the empirical Monte Carlo success rate, treating the state value as a probability. In Stage 2 (policy optimization), next-step actions are extracted from the completions, and advantages are computed as A_i = V_φ(s∘a_i) − (1/M)Σ_j V_φ(s∘a_j); the policy is then updated by minimizing a squared loss that fits the log policy ratio to the normalized advantage, i.e., (1/β)A − log(π_θ/π_ref). The key theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.2) proves monotonic improvement: the optimized policy strictly outperforms the reference policy unless the reference is already optimal. Unlike PPO, DAPO removes the critic from the actor training loop entirely, eliminating co-training instability and reducing GPU memory; unlike GRPO/DPO, it provides per-step dense signals rather than a single response-level reward.

## Results

On the in-domain MATH benchmark (5K test, zero-shot greedy decoding), DAPO achieves the largest improvement on OpenO1-Llama-8B-v0.1, boosting accuracy from 52.73% to 60.33% (+7.60 points) using only 7.5K training problems. Across all four tested models, DAPO consistently improves MATH accuracy by +0.95 to +7.60 points. On code generation, DAPO improves Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct by +3.0 on HumanEval, +5.0 on MBPP, and +9.2 on MBPP+, while OpenO1-Llama-8B-v0.1 gains +2.5 on HumanEval and +6.1 on MBPP using only 4K TACO training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPO is compared against two baselines: GRPO (response-level, no critic) and PPO (online actor-critic with GAE). DAPO outperforms both baselines on all four base models on the MATH benchmark. The margin is most pronounced on OpenO1-Llama-8B-v0.1, where DAPO achieves 60.33% versus GRPO's 55.63% (+4.70 gap) and PPO's 54.12% (+6.21 gap). On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, DAPO (53.62%) leads GRPO (52.28%) by 1.34 points and PPO (52.41%) by 1.21 points. On the already-RL-trained Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, DAPO (84.86%) still edges out GRPO (84.33%) by 0.53 points and PPO (83.76%) by 1.10 points. The paper also notes that GRPO exhibits severe overfitting after ~300 training steps with test performance dropping, whereas DAPO improves steadily without overfitting under a comparable compute budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAPO sits at the intersection of two lines of work on LLM reasoning RL: response-level preference optimization (DPO, GRPO, ReMax) and step-level actor-critic methods (PPO, DQO, OREO). It builds on Math-Shepherd's Monte Carlo value estimation for critic training and on the performance difference lemma from Agarwal et al. and Liu et al. as its theoretical backbone. The key departure is a two-stage decoupling: the critic is trained offline to approximate the reference policy's value function before any policy updates, and the actor is then optimized via a squared-loss fit to step-level advantages rather than a clipped policy gradient. This contrasts with PPO's interwoven actor-critic updates and with GRPO's uniform outcome-reward signal across all reasoning steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAPO provides a theoretically grounded (monotonic improvement guarantee) and practically effective recipe for step-level RL on LLMs that requires only ~7.5K training problems and no human annotations, making it accessible for continued-RL on already-trained models. The decoupling of critic and actor training addresses a known instability in PPO-style methods for long CoT, and the empirical demonstration that DAPO outperforms both GRPO and PPO across seven base models strengthens the case for dense step-level signals. The main open limitation is the high compute cost of the critic pre-training stage (32 rollouts per problem plus 16 completions per step), which the authors flag as a barrier to scaling; whether larger query sets or more efficient critic training can close the remaining gap to response-level methods at scale remains to be shown.
