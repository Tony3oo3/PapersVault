## Problem

Existing adverse-weather image restoration models trained on synthetic data fail to generalize to unpredictable real-world degradations due to three gaps: synthetic datasets lack physical fidelity in depth-driven weather effects, static models cannot adapt to novel degradation patterns at deployment, and single-model architectures cannot dynamically coordinate multiple specialized restoration strategies. This paper addresses all three by combining a physics-driven high-fidelity dataset with a dual-level reinforcement learning framework that enables continuous, unsupervised adaptation to real-world conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253759/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single weather-degraded RGB image (rain, snow, haze, or mixed) as input and produces a restored clean image. No paired ground-truth image is required during the reinforcement learning phase; instead, the multi-agent pipeline iteratively applies specialized restoration models guided by CLIP-based weather descriptions and no-reference IQA scores (LIQE, CLIP-IQA, Q-Align, MUSIQ). The global controller limits restoration to at most three sequential agent calls per image and terminates after three consecutive IQA-score drops, returning the best-scoring intermediate result.

## Method

The framework has two levels. At the local level, Perturbation-driven Image Quality Optimization (PIQO) adapts GRPO to image restoration by injecting Gaussian perturbations into DSANet-based model parameters to generate diverse outputs for a single input, scoring them with a composite no-reference reward, filtering out outputs worse than the unperturbed baseline via MUSIQ, computing normalized advantages, and updating parameters with an implicit KL-regularized gradient step. At the global level, a CLIP-based meta-controller generates a semantic weather description, registered agents (derain, dehaze, desnow) bid based on historical success rates, and the top-ranked agent is selected for each restoration round; success is verified by re-running CLIP analysis and checking IQA improvement, with failed agents removed and the next candidate tried. The entire system is cold-started via supervised training on the 1-million-image HFLS-Weather dataset (synthesized with Depth Anything V2 depth maps and an atmospheric scattering model), then refined on real-world data from WReal and URHI without paired supervision.

## Results

On the WReal real-world benchmark, the proposed dual-level RL framework achieves the highest scores across all four IQA metrics (Q-Align, CLIP-IQA, LIQE, MUSIQ) for snow, haze, and rain. Headline numbers include Q-Align of 3.96 (snow), 3.56 (haze), and 4.03 (rain), and MUSIQ of 67.80 (snow), 63.30 (haze), and 64.12 (rain). GPT-4o perceptual evaluation confirms the lead, with the method scoring 4.42/5 on snow artifact removal versus 3.57 for the next-best (JarvisIR). The method also reports 570 ms inference latency, slower than single-model baselines (17–208 ms) but far faster than other multi-agent systems (6,543–15,250 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on every IQA metric across all three weather conditions. The closest competitor is DA-CLIP [33], which the method surpasses by +0.33 Q-Align on snow, +0.43 on haze, and +0.21 on rain, with even larger margins on LIQE (e.g., 3.95 vs 3.24 on snow) and MUSIQ (e.g., 67.80 vs 61.16 on snow). Among the all-in-one restoration baselines, WGWS [69] and OneRestore [18] cluster near the bottom, with OneRestore scoring the lowest Q-Align on haze (2.98) and rain (3.70). The two most recent multi-agent RL methods, DFPIR [45] and JarvisIR [29], are only evaluated via GPT-4o (Table 3); the proposed method leads JarvisIR by +0.85 on snow artifact removal (4.42 vs 3.57) and by +0.42 on haze artifact removal (4.07 vs 3.65). PromptIR [37] and Chen et al. [11] trail the proposed method by roughly 0.3–0.5 Q-Align points across conditions, confirming that the dual-level RL refinement and high-fidelity cold start yield consistent gains over both single-model and prior multi-agent approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) physics-driven synthetic weather dataset construction (building on atmospheric scattering models used in RESIDE, Foggy Cityscapes, and Snow100K, but replacing LiDAR/stereo depth with Depth Anything V2 for scale and fidelity); (2) GRPO-style reinforcement learning from LLM alignment (DeepSeekMath, DeepSeek-R1), which the authors adapt to the one-to-one mapping challenge of image restoration via parameter perturbation (PIQO); and (3) multi-agent image restoration systems (RestoreAgent, AgenticIR, JarvisIR), which the authors extend with a learned meta-controller that dynamically sequences specialized agents based on IQA feedback rather than LLM-based reasoning. The key conceptual departure is treating weather restoration as a sequential decision problem amenable to RL without paired ground truth, rather than a single supervised regression task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1M-image HFLS-Weather dataset and the PIQO training recipe offer a practical path for practitioners who need weather-robust restoration without access to paired real-world data, and the re-training experiments (Table 7) confirm the dataset benefits existing architectures independently of the RL framework. However, the entire evaluation is no-reference (IQA scores + GPT-4o), so the large reported margins cannot be verified against ground truth, and the 570 ms multi-agent latency limits deployment in safety-critical or real-time settings. Open questions include whether PIQO generalizes to other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring), whether the meta-controller's bidding mechanism scales beyond three agents, and whether the RL gains persist when evaluated with full-reference metrics on new paired data.
