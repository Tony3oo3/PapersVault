## Problem

Variational posteriors over the global latent variable in Neural Processes are frequently miscalibrated, producing latent representations that are either overly deterministic or excessively diffuse. This miscalibration persists across different loss functions and model architectures, undermining both predictive accuracy and the reliability of uncertainty estimates—particularly when target inputs deviate from the observed context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251865/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TTSNP takes a pre-trained latent NP model (Simple NP or DANP), a context set of input-output pairs, and target inputs, and produces refined latent posterior samples that yield improved predictive means and variances. The method operates without modifying any pre-trained parameters, instead learning lightweight transition kernels and a pseudo-context generator. It handles varying input and output dimensions (1D–4D GP regression, image completion with different output sizes), and the pseudo-context generator produces synthetic future data points via a permutation-invariant ISAB network conditioned on the observed context and random noise.

## Method

TTSNP refines latent samples at test time using a learned Sequential Monte Carlo Sampler that transports particles from the variational posterior toward the true posterior over T sequential steps. Intermediate distributions are constructed via geometric annealing enriched with a learned pseudo-context term: a permutation-invariant neural network (ISAB) generates synthetic future context points that inject anticipatory uncertainty, inspired by martingale posteriors. Forward and backward transition kernels are Euler discretizations of SDEs whose drifts combine the analytic score of the intermediate distribution with a learned neural network correction, satisfying Nelson's identity. The training objective jointly minimizes a KL divergence between forward and backward path measures (ensuring time-reversibility) and a marginal log-likelihood loss (encouraging diverse posterior mode coverage), with multinomial resampling triggered when the effective sample size drops below 30% of particles.

## Results

On the DANP GP regression task (Table 2), TTSNP trained on 1D data achieves target log-likelihood of 0.661 on in-distribution 1D RBF data, 0.622 under input range shift, and 0.362 under hyperparameter range shift, consistently outperforming all baselines. On the Simple NP RBF task (Table 1), TTSNP reaches 0.430 target log-likelihood vs. 0.352 (Pre-train), 0.382 (Fine-tune), and 0.405 (SMCS). For image completion on EMNIST (Table 3), TTSNP achieves 0.620 target log-likelihood vs. 0.574 (Pre-train) and 0.601 (Fine-tune), and 0.613 on Corrupted EMNIST vs. 0.551 (Pre-train).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTSNP consistently outperforms all three baselines (Pre-train, SMCS with ULA, and Fine-tune) across every evaluation scenario. Against the Pre-train baseline (zero-shot variational posterior), TTSNP improves target log-likelihood by 0.24 on 1D RBF (0.661 vs. 0.419), 0.39 under input range shift (0.622 vs. 0.235), and 0.56 under hyperparameter shift (0.362 vs. −0.197), showing the largest gains under distribution shift. Against the untrained SMCS baseline (ULA transitions without learned kernels), TTSNP gains 0.177 on 1D RBF target (0.661 vs. 0.484) and 0.20 under input shift (0.622 vs. 0.423), confirming that the learned intermediate distributions and pseudo-context mechanism add substantial value over vanilla SMC. Against the Fine-tune baseline (which trains the NP's latent path directly on test-time data), TTSNP wins by 0.151 on 1D RBF (0.661 vs. 0.510) and 0.256 under hyperparameter shift (0.362 vs. 0.108), and also converges faster with fewer training batches (Fig. 6). On the image completion task, TTSNP leads Fine-tune by 0.019 on EMNIST (0.620 vs. 0.601) and 0.030 on Corrupted EMNIST (0.613 vs. 0.583), demonstrating that the advantage extends to high-dimensional output spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of latent Neural Processes (which model functional uncertainty via a global latent variable) and Sequential Monte Carlo Samplers (which sequentially transport particles from a tractable initial distribution toward a target posterior). It builds directly on the optimal-control formulation of continuous-time SMCS from Chen et al. [7], adapting learned drift functions with score-based components and neural network corrections for the NP setting. The pseudo-context intermediate distributions draw conceptual inspiration from Martingale Posterior Neural Processes [32], while the 'test-time scaling' framing borrows the inference-time compute scaling paradigm popularized in LLMs. The key departure from prior NP work is treating the variational posterior not as the final answer but as an initial distribution to be refined at test time via learned particle transport, without modifying the pre-trained encoder-decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and persistent failure mode in latent NPs—miscalibrated variational posteriors that limit both predictive accuracy and uncertainty reliability—and offers a plug-and-play remedy that preserves the pre-trained model. This is practically relevant for any application of NPs where calibrated epistemic uncertainty is critical (e.g., scientific inference, decision-making under limited data). However, the method requires additional training data and introduces per-inference computational overhead (gradient evaluations over T SMC steps), which limits its applicability in compute-constrained or truly zero-shot settings. Open questions include whether the approach extends to local latent variables (as in FNP or ANP), whether the pseudo-context mechanism can be replaced by cheaper alternatives, and how the method scales to very high-dimensional latent spaces or non-Gaussian likelihoods.
