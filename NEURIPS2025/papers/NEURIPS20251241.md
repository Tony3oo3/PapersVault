## Problem

LoRA's low-rank bottleneck forces a trade-off between performance and parameter efficiency: closing the gap with full fine-tuning requires increasing rank, which proportionally increases parameter cost. Recent linear LoRA variants (e.g., MoSLoRA) add extra linear matrices but remain fundamentally linear, offering only marginal gains at very low ranks. The paper asks whether introducing a nonlinear transformation between LoRA's two linear projectors can fundamentally break this bottleneck while simultaneously compressing the rank further.

## Input / Output

AuroRA is applied to frozen pretrained models (RoBERTa, LLaMA3-8B, ViT, SDXL) where only the low-rank adaptation modules are trained. During training, the forward pass is dynamic and input-dependent (B·σ(Ax)), but at inference the update collapses to a static matrix (B·σ(A)) that is merged into W0, incurring zero additional inference latency. The method is evaluated on text inputs (GLUE, commonsense reasoning), RGB images (classification at various resolutions), and text prompts paired with reference images (subject-driven generation via DreamBooth).

## Method

AuroRA inserts an Adaptive Nonlinear Layer (ANL) between LoRA's downward projector A and upward projector B, forming an MLP-like structure with a compressed hidden dimension r̃ = r/k (typically r̃=2, so k=4 or 8). The ANL combines two components: a fixed nonlinearity F(Z) = tanh(H·tanh(Z)) using a learnable self-projection matrix H and tanh activations for coarse fitting, and a learnable nonlinearity L(Z) = w_s·s(Z) using B-spline basis functions with trainable spline weights for fine fitting. This hybrid design achieves a strictly lower approximation error than linear LoRA at the same rank (Proposition 2.1) while maintaining bounded gradients (Proposition 2.2). The key architectural insight is the dynamic-to-static transition: training uses the input-dependent form B·σ(Ax) for flexibility, while inference uses the merged static form (W0 + B·σ(A))x, eliminating all extra computational overhead at deployment.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-Base, AuroRA achieves an average score of 87.1, surpassing Full Fine-Tuning (85.2) and LoRA (85.2) while using only 0.075M trainable parameters (25% of LoRA's 0.3M). On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA3-8B, AuroRA reaches 83.6 average accuracy versus LoRA's 80.8, using just 3.5M parameters (6.25% of LoRA's 56.6M). On ViT-Base image classification across 8 datasets, AuroRA attains 85.1% average accuracy, outperforming LoRA (77.6%) by 7.5% with only 74K parameters (12.7% of LoRA's 581K), and approaching Full Fine-Tuning (86.5%) with 0.086% of its parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AuroRA consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across NLP and CV tasks. Against LoRA, AuroRA gains +1.9% average on GLUE (RoBERTa-Base), +2.8% on commonsense reasoning (LLaMA3-8B), and +7.5% on ViT-Base image classification, while using 6.25%–25% of LoRA's parameters. Against the strongest PEFT baselines, AuroRA beats DoRA by +1.6% (GLUE avg) and FourierFT by +2.1% (GLUE avg) and +4.8% (ViT-Base avg), though it uses more parameters than FourierFT (0.075M vs 0.024M). Against linear LoRA variants (AdaLoRA, DyLoRA, LoRA-drop), AuroRA leads by 1.6–2.6% on GLUE average. Against MoSLoRA (a linear variant with an arbitrary matrix), AuroRA shows substantially larger gains on low-rank settings (e.g., +2.2% on DTD where MoSLoRA only marginally improves over LoRA). Notably, AuroRA matches or exceeds Full Fine-Tuning on most individual datasets (e.g., +6.5% on RTE, +1.5% on CoLA) while using less than 0.1% of total model parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) by inserting an Adaptive Nonlinear Layer (ANL) between its two low-rank projectors, transforming the purely linear two-layer mapping into an MLP-like structure. It departs from linear LoRA variants (AdaLoRA, MoSLoRA, FLoRA) that add extra linear matrices but remain fundamentally linear, and from recent nonlinear variants (LoRAN, SineLoRA, LoDA) that introduce nonlinearity without addressing the rank bottleneck. The key conceptual departure is the simultaneous use of fixed (tanh) and learnable (B-spline) nonlinearities at a compressed hidden dimension (r̃ ≪ r), supported by theoretical guarantees of strictly lower approximation error and bounded gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AuroRA demonstrates that a minimal nonlinear insertion (tanh + B-spline at dimension 2) can break LoRA's low-rank bottleneck, matching or exceeding full fine-tuning with only 6–25% of LoRA's parameters across NLP and CV tasks. The dynamic-training/static-inference strategy eliminates inference overhead, making the method practically deployable. A stated limitation is the absence of evaluation on models larger than 8B parameters, leaving open whether the compressed-rank advantage persists at scale. Future work could explore whether the B-spline component generalizes to higher-order or multi-dimensional spline bases, and whether the approach composes with other PEFT strategies (adapters, prompt tuning).
