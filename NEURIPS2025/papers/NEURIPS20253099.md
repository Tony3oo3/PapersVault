## Problem

For infinite-horizon average reward Constrained MDPs (CMDPs) with general policy parametrization, the best known model-free algorithm achieves a convergence rate of Õ(1/T^{1/5}), far from the theoretical lower bound of Ω(1/√T). This gap arises because the lack of strong convexity in policy parameters prevents standard primal-dual analysis from transferring dual convergence to primal convergence, and the dual learning rate introduces a fundamental tradeoff between constraint violation speed and primal update variance.

## Input / Output

The algorithm interacts with an unknown ergodic MDP by observing state-action transitions (s, a, s'), reward g(s,a) ∈ [0,1], and cost g(s,a) ∈ [-1,1] at each step. The policy is indexed by a d-dimensional parameter θ ∈ R^d (with d ≪ |S||A|), and the critic uses a linear feature map φ_g: S → R^m with ‖φ_g(s)‖ ≤ 1. The output is the policy parameter θ_k and Lagrange multiplier λ_k at each outer-loop iteration, from which the action distribution π_θ(·|s) is read off; the algorithm requires only O(log T_max) samples per inner-loop step on average due to the MLMC estimator.

## Method

The paper proposes a Primal-Dual Natural Actor-Critic (PDNAC) algorithm that solves the saddle-point problem max_θ min_λ L(θ,λ) = J_r(θ) + λJ_c(θ) using a nested-loop structure: an outer loop of K ≈ Õ(T) iterations updates (θ, λ), while inner loops of H ≈ Õ(τ_mix²) steps run a critic subroutine (estimating J_g and value functions via linear TD) and an actor subroutine (estimating the Natural Policy Gradient F(θ)^{-1}∇_θ L via a strongly convex subproblem). The key technical ingredient is a Multi-Level Monte Carlo (MLMC) estimator for both the critic and NPG gradients, which achieves the same bias as averaging T_max samples while requiring only Õ(log T_max) samples and eliminating the need for mixing-time knowledge. By setting H to be nearly constant (rather than Θ(√T) as in unconstrained settings), the algorithm becomes effectively single-timescale, and a sharper analysis of the MLMC-based updates combined with the Fisher non-degeneracy condition yields Õ(1/√T) global convergence and constraint violation rates, matching the MDP lower bound.

## Results

The proposed Primal-Dual Natural Actor-Critic (PDNAC) algorithm achieves global convergence and average constraint violation rates of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})$ over horizon $T$ when the mixing time $\tau_{\text{mix}}$ is known (Theorem 4.9). When $\tau_{\text{mix}}$ is unknown, the rates degrade to $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/T^{0.5-\epsilon})$ provided $T \geq \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{\text{mix}}^{2/\epsilon})$ (Theorem 4.10). These rates match the theoretical lower bound of $\Omega(1/\sqrt{T})$ for MDPs [6], closing the gap from the previous state-of-the-art of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/T^{1/5})$ for general policy parameterization [10].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct comparison is with Bai et al. [10], the only prior model-free algorithm for general policy parameterization in average reward CMDPs, which achieves $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/T^{1/5})$ for both convergence and violation; this paper improves that to $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})$, matching the information-theoretic lower bound. Against tabular model-based methods (Chen et al. [15], Agarwal et al. [2]), the proposed algorithm matches or exceeds their $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})$ rates while operating in a strictly more general setting (arbitrary parametric policies rather than tabular) and without requiring knowledge of the transition kernel. Compared to the linear MDP results of Ghosh et al. [27], the paper's guarantees hold without the restrictive linearity assumption on transition probabilities. Against the model-free tabular Triple-QA [42], which achieves only $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/T^{1/6})$, the proposed method is strictly faster in convergence rate and extends to general parameterization. The paper also provides a setting with unknown mixing time (Theorem 4.10) that no prior general-parameterization CMDP algorithm offers, though at a slightly degraded rate of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/T^{0.5-\epsilon})$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual policy gradient line of work for average reward CMDPs with general parameterization, which previously achieved only O~(1/T^{1/5}) via a two-timescale structure. It departs from that approach by adopting a nested-loop architecture with a nearly constant inner loop H and MLMC-based gradient estimates (borrowed from the unconstrained actor-critic literature), which eliminates the rate gap between the constrained (1/T^{1/5}) and unconstrained (1/T^{1/4}) settings that was previously attributed to the Lagrange dual structure. The natural policy gradient formulation with Fisher information matrix and the Slater-condition primal-dual saddle point are carried forward from prior CMDP work, but the single-timescale parameter regime (K~T, H~const) is the key structural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to close the rate gap between constrained and unconstrained average reward MDPs under general parameterization, matching the O(1/√T) theoretical lower bound for both objective convergence and constraint violation. The unknown-mixing-time setting still carries a horizon requirement T ≥ O~(τ_mix^{2/ε}) that can be large for slowly mixing problems, and the ergTD assumption remains unrelaxed—both are identified as open questions. For practitioners, the practical implication is that the algorithm's nested-loop structure with nearly constant H significantly reduces memory overhead compared to prior two-timescale methods that required storing trajectories of length √T.
