## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks for MLLMs focus on static scene understanding, single-step comparisons, or text-only multi-hop reasoning, leaving a gap in evaluating models on continuous, multi-step spatial transformations governed by precise mathematical constraints. ORIGAMISPACE addresses this by leveraging origami, where each folding step depends on the previous one and all operations must satisfy rigorous geometric theorems (Kawasaki's, Maekawa's, Huzita-Hatori axioms), creating a natural testbed for multi-step spatial reasoning under hard constraints.

## Input / Output

For the three MCQ tasks, inputs are 2D CP diagrams (line drawings with mountain/valley/boundary fold markings), sets of 3–25 shuffled folding-process photographs, or CP diagrams queried for post-fold spatial properties; outputs are single-letter multiple-choice selections. For the code generation task, the input is a compiled flat pattern image plus a folded shape photograph, and the output is a structured CP code specifying vertices_coords, edges_vertices, faces_vertices, and per-edge crease type assignments (M/V/B/F/U) that must satisfy Euler's formula V−E+F=2 and local flat-foldability conditions. The compiler returns one of four typed errors (CSE, GIF, PSI, AFS) or a successful compilation with layering information.

## Method

The benchmark comprises 350 curated origami instances and four tasks: Pattern Prediction (350 MCQs), Multi-step Spatial Reasoning (250 MCQs on shuffled step sequences), Spatial Relationship Prediction (900 MCQs across pose localization, layering, and geometric change), and End-to-End CP Code Generation (120 questions). A key technical contribution is an optimized origami compiler that tracks crease positions, computes paper stacking order, and provides structured four-type error feedback to enable iterative model–environment interaction. For code generation, three learning settings are compared: in-context learning (one-shot generation from examples), environmental learning (up to 10 rounds of generate–compile–revise interaction), and reinforcement learning using TRICO (a PPO-based multi-turn RL algorithm) trained on 471 additional samples with a composite reward combining intermediate compilation-success signals, step-efficiency penalties, and a final 4-dimensional quality score (Topological Structure Similarity, Geometric Similarity via bidirectional Hausdorff distance, Constraint Satisfaction, and Final Folded State). The evaluation framework scores generated CP codes on a weighted 0–1 scale across these four dimensions, with each dimension contributing 25%.

## Results

On ORIGAMISPACE, the best-performing model (Gemini 2.5-pro) achieves 42.68% on Pattern Prediction, 53.45% on Multi-step Spatial Reasoning, and 47.10% on Geometric Change Analysis, while GPT-4o is a close second at 42.71%, 51.81%, and 46.72% respectively. The best open-source model (InternVL2.5-78B) reaches 36.76% on Pattern Prediction and 38.55% on Multi-step Spatial Reasoning, still far below human experts (98.45% and 100%). Even the top models remain well below human common performance (51.18%–88.52%), and weaker open-source models (e.g., MiniCPM-o 2.6 at ~27%) perform near the 25% random-guessing baseline. For the CP code generation task, environmental learning with GPT-4o achieves the highest total score of 52.67, and RL-trained Qwen2.5-VL-32B (42.57) surpasses the untrained 72B variant (32.68 in in-context learning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini 2.5-pro and GPT-4o are the top two models across all spatial reasoning tasks, with Gemini 2.5-pro leading on Multi-step Spatial Reasoning (53.45% vs. 51.81%) and Geometric Change (47.10% vs. 46.72%), while GPT-4o edges ahead on Pattern Prediction (42.71% vs. 42.68%) and Layering Relationship (50.42% vs. 47.68%). Among open-source models, InternVL2.5-78B consistently outperforms all others, achieving 36.76% on Pattern Prediction and 38.55% on Multi-step Spatial Reasoning, surpassing Qwen2.5-VL-72B by ~0.5–1.5 points. Claude-3.5-Sonnet trails GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5-pro by 7–10 points on most tasks, particularly on Multi-step Spatial Reasoning (45.07% vs. 53.45%). Smaller open-source models (MiniCPM-o 2.6, llava-1.5-7b, deepseek-vl2) cluster near the 25% random baseline, with scores between 26–31%, indicating they lack meaningful spatial reasoning capability. For the CP code generation task, environmental learning dramatically improves all models (e.g., GPT-4o jumps from 42.82 to 52.67 total score), and RL-trained Qwen2.5-VL-32B (42.57) exceeds the untrained Qwen2.5-VL-72B in in-context learning (32.68), demonstrating that targeted training can close the gap between model sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against existing spatial reasoning benchmarks (CLEVR, NLVR2, StepGame, LEGO-Puzzles) that the authors argue focus on static scenes, single-step comparisons, or multi-step processes lacking physical/geometric constraints. It draws on computational origami literature (Kawasaki's theorem, Maekawa's theorem, Huzita-Hatori axioms) to introduce a domain where multi-step reasoning and verifiable mathematical constraints are inherently coupled. For the code generation task, the RL training methodology is directly adapted from VAGEN/TRICO [35], a PPO-based multi-turn RL algorithm for VLM agents, repurposed here for iterative CP code refinement through compiler feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ORIGAMISPACE is the first benchmark to jointly evaluate multi-step spatial reasoning and constraint adherence in MLLMs, revealing that even top-tier models (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5-pro) achieve only ~50-53% on multi-step tasks versus 100% for human experts, and that constraint satisfaction remains a persistent bottleneck (~57% even under interactive learning). The interactive compiler environment and preliminary RL results (32B model surpassing 72B) suggest a viable path for training MLLMs on constraint-heavy spatial tasks, though the RL evidence is limited to a single model. Open questions include whether the constraint-satisfaction gap can be closed with more training data or better reward shaping, whether findings generalize beyond origami to other constraint-rich spatial domains (robotics, CAD), and whether the 350-instance scale is sufficient for robust conclusions.
