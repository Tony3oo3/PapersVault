## Problem

Deductive formal problem-solving (D-FPS) in Lean 4 suffers from a granularity mismatch between informal and formal reasoning (e.g., substituting √4 with 2 is trivial informally but requires a multi-line formal proof), and existing FTP paradigms are inefficient for D-FPS because search-based methods waste budget on unnecessary backtracking while whole-proof generation suffers from cascading error propagation. Compounding these issues, there is an extreme scarcity of formal problem-solution pairs to train task-specific models.

## Input / Output

The method takes an informal natural-language mathematical problem (e.g., 'If mn=4 and m+n=5, what is |m−n|?') and produces a formal solution as a sequence of Lean 4 tactic applications that transform the initial proof state—containing typed variables, hypotheses, and a conclusion—into a terminal state where an answer proposition (e.g., a=3) is submitted via the `exact` tactic. Each solution step is a tuple of an informal reasoning step, a formal tactic draft (which may contain `sorry` placeholders), and a set of gap proofs that fill those placeholders, all verified by the Lean 4 kernel.

## Method

HAR (Hierarchical Autoregressive Formal Reasoner) constructs formal solutions as a backtracking-free autoregressive process where each step is hierarchically decomposed: a high-level drafting step generates an informal-aligned reasoning step and a formal tactic draft, then low-level proof searches fill the `sorry` gaps left by the draft. If any component fails Lean 4 type-checking or proof search, the step is rejected and resampled (rejective sampling) without backtracking to earlier states, combining the robustness of search-based methods with the depth-priority of whole-generation. For data bootstrapping, CoPA (Chain-of-Proxy-Autoformalization) cascades statement autoformalization, proof drafting, and proof search as a proxy path to produce formal problem-solution pairs, applying explosion/vacuous checks for problems and deductive checks for solutions, then iteratively trains task-specific models via expert iteration on cumulated data.

## Results

HAR (Cycle 2) achieves 44.09% solving rate on FormalMath500 and 56.58% on MiniF2F-Solving, with a budget of 18,273 and 14,754 steps respectively. This represents a 2.9× improvement over the best prompt-based baseline (Hybrid CoT: 15.50% / 21.60%) and a 1.5× improvement over the best whole-generation baseline (H-WG with K_W=16: 40.04% / 51.08%), while using roughly half the budget. HAR also outperforms H-SA (43.65% / 55.50%), a strong baseline that is given ground-truth informal solutions, demonstrating that the autoregressive hierarchical approach is more effective than simply providing the model with the correct informal reasoning path.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAR consistently outperforms all baselines on both benchmarks across both data cycles. Against the prompt-based baselines from Liu et al. [7] (ICL and Hybrid CoT), HAR achieves roughly 3× the solving rate (44.09% vs. 13.70% on FormalMath500; 56.58% vs. 21.87% on MiniF2F-Solving) while using about 4× less budget. Against the whole-generation baseline WG, HAR (via its AR ablation) nearly doubles the solving rate (34.39% vs. 18.78%) at comparable budget, and the full hierarchical HAR further improves to 44.09%. The most notable comparison is against H-SA, which is augmented with ground-truth informal solutions: HAR still wins by 0.44% on FormalMath500 and 1.08% on MiniF2F-Solving in Cycle 2, suggesting that the autoregressive per-step rejective sampling and hierarchical gap-filling are more effective than simply providing the model with the correct informal reasoning trajectory. Budget-wise, HAR uses the lowest or near-lowest budget among all methods that achieve competitive solving rates, confirming its Pareto-optimality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the D-FPS (Deductive Formal Problem-Solving) framework introduced by the same authors in [7], which formalized math problem-solving as a forward-solving + backward-proving process in Lean 4. For the reasoning pipeline, it departs from both tree-search methods (BFS, MCTS) and whole-proof generation by proposing autoregressive generation with per-step rejective sampling, and introduces a hierarchical decomposition that splits each formal step into an informal-aligned draft plus fine-grained proof searches—adapting the proof-drafting concept from Jiang et al. [38] to the problem-solving setting. For data generation, CoPA repurposes well-studied FTP subtasks (statement autoformalization, proof drafting, proof search) as a cascaded proxy path, sidestepping the need for models with D-FPS-specific priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles the extreme scarcity of formal problem-solution pairs, a fundamental bottleneck that has stalled progress in formal problem-solving, and provides a scalable expert-iteration pipeline (CoPA) that produces process-verified training data. The hierarchical decoupling insight—that aligning informal and formal reasoning granularity via draft-then-fill nearly doubles solving rates—offers a broadly applicable design principle for LLM-based formal reasoning. Open questions include whether expert iteration can be scaled beyond three cycles, whether the approach generalizes to theorem proving and broader mathematical domains (PutnamBench results suggest OOD fragility), and whether the context-length limitation of the 7B base model is the true ceiling for HAR's scaling behavior.
