## Problem

Prior mechanistic analyses of in-context learning treat special attention heads (Previous Token Heads, Induction Heads) and task vectors as separate entities, each studied through end-to-end ablation effects on final predictions. No unified framework links these components to the layer-wise geometric evolution of hidden states that actually determines model outputs. This paper asks what geometric properties of hidden states govern ICL classification accuracy and how specific attention heads and task vectors shape those properties across layers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt with k in-context demonstrations (default k=8) and extracts hidden states at the final token position across all L layers of a pretrained LLM, together with the model's unembedding matrix E. It produces layer-wise trajectories of six geometric measures (separability score, output alignment, singular/variance/mean/composite alignment, effective dimension) comparing ICL versus zero-shot settings, plus head-level ablation effects and task-vector injection results. Evaluation spans binary and multiclass text classification (SUBJ, SST-2, TREC, SNLI, RTE, CB) and an open-ended generation task, across 7 models from 2B to 70B parameters.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which proves that classification accuracy is upper-bounded by the maximum separability S* of query hidden states, with equality achieved only when the optimal separation direction aligns with the label unembedding difference direction—establishing separability and alignment as the two geometric factors governing ICL performance. Empirically, the paper reveals a two-stage phase transition: early layers (driven by Previous Token Heads) rapidly increase separability, while middle-to-late layers (driven by Induction Heads) plateau separability and surge alignment by filtering label-irrelevant semantics and amplifying label-relevant directions. This alignment surge is validated through semantic decoding of SVD directions (label tokens emerge immediately post-transition), low-rank denoising (rank-10 SVD yields >10% accuracy gains), and task-vector injection of PTH/IH outputs into zero-shot hidden states. The framework unifies the attention-head and task-vector perspectives by showing that PTH outputs as task vectors boost separability while IH outputs as task vectors restore the ICL-specific alignment spike, explaining why IH outputs empirically serve as effective task vectors.

## Results

On Llama2-70B across six classification datasets (SUBJ, SST-2, TREC, SNLI, RTE, CB), ICL (8-shot) achieves ~80% accuracy versus a much lower zero-shot baseline, with the accuracy gap driven almost entirely by alignment measures rather than separability (which is already high in zero-shot). The two-stage mechanism is confirmed: separability rises sharply in early layers while all directional alignment measures spike simultaneously in mid-to-late layers. KNN demonstration selection improves accuracy from 80.57% (random) to 88.01%, and replacing labels with uninformative symbols drops accuracy to 59.05%, both effects tracked by alignment. Low-rank (rank-10) SVD denoising of hidden states yields accuracy gains exceeding 10%, particularly in early post-transition layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic analysis paper rather than a method paper, so it does not benchmark a new model against baselines. Instead, it compares ICL against zero-shot and across ICL settings. ICL and zero-shot show only a small gap in separability scores despite an ~80% accuracy difference, establishing alignment as the primary bottleneck. Among ICL settings, kNN-selected demonstrations outperform random selection (88.01% vs. 80.57% accuracy) with consistently stronger alignment and lower effective dimension. Symbol-labeled demonstrations (59.05%) degrade accuracy by flattening the post-transition alignment surge, resembling zero-shot behavior. In ablation experiments, ablating top-10% PTHs substantially reduces separability while leaving most alignment measures intact; ablating top-10% IHs leaves separability largely unchanged but severely disrupts all alignment measures and output accuracy. Random head ablation (matched count) has negligible effects on any measure, confirming the specificity of PTH and IH roles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of ICL mechanistic research: the attention-head circuit perspective (PTH-IH circuits identified by Olsson et al., 2022; Cho et al., 2025a) and the task-vector perspective (Hendel et al., 2023; Todd et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024). Prior work analyzed these components in isolation—either ablating heads to measure end-to-end output effects or injecting task vectors to steer hidden states—without connecting them to the layer-wise geometric evolution of hidden states. The paper's key departure is proposing a geometric framework (separability + alignment with label unembedding vectors) that subsumes both perspectives, showing that PTHs and IHs are the mechanistic drivers of the two distinct geometric phases, and that IH outputs function as task vectors precisely because they align hidden states along label-unembedding directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage phase transition (separability in early layers, alignment in later layers) provides a clear, testable mechanistic account of ICL that explains why IH outputs empirically serve as effective task vectors—a question left open by prior task-vector work. The finding that ICL improves accuracy primarily through alignment rather than separability has practical implications: it suggests that the main bottleneck in zero-shot performance is directional misalignment, not insufficient cluster separation, pointing toward alignment-specific interventions (e.g., low-rank denoising) as more effective than separability-focused ones. Limitations include the preliminary nature of the generation-task analysis, the focus on inference-time behavior in pretrained models (no training dynamics), and the restriction to classification-adjacent tasks for the core geometric analysis.
