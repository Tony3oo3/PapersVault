## Problem

Stochastic simple bilevel optimization requires minimizing an outer objective over the solution set of an inner convex problem, but existing projection-free methods still demand linear optimization over complex derived sets (e.g., intersections of the base domain with a halfspace) rather than over the base set alone. Prior approaches also require a pre-specified tolerance parameter, an expensive initialization procedure to find an ε-optimal starting point, and do not provide anytime guarantees. This paper eliminates all three limitations while maintaining projection-free operation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a compact convex feasible set X, stochastic component functions f(x,θ) and g(x,ξ) (or their finite-sum equivalents), and a linear minimization oracle over X only. It produces a sequence of weighted-average iterates z_t (convex combinations of past x_i with weights tied to the regularization schedule) whose outer and inner optimality gaps converge at provable rates. The algorithm is anytime: it requires no knowledge of the total iteration count T, no preset stepsize schedule tied to T, and no initialization beyond an arbitrary x_0 ∈ X.

## Method

The core mechanism is an iteratively regularized conditional gradient (Frank-Wolfe) scheme: at iteration t, the algorithm performs a linear minimization over X using the composite gradient σ_t·∇F + ∇G, where σ_t = ζ(t+1)^{-p} is a vanishing regularization sequence that progressively shifts emphasis from the outer to the inner objective. Stochastic gradient noise is controlled via the STORM estimator (one-sample setting) or the SPIDER estimator (finite-sum setting with mini-batches of size q), both providing variance reduction through recursive momentum or periodic full-gradient recomputation. The key structural insight is that the LMO is performed exclusively over the base set X, avoiding the halfspace-intersection oracles required by prior projection-free bilevel methods. A weighted-average output z_t is maintained to decouple the outer-level convergence rate from the regularization decay, yielding O(t^{-1/4}) rates (convex) or O(t^{-1/7}) rates (nonconvex outer) in the one-sample setting, and O(t^{-1/2}) or O(t^{-1/4}) respectively in the finite-sum setting.

## Results

On the over-parameterized regression task (Wikipedia Math Essential, n=1068, d=730) within a 4-minute budget, IR-SCG completes 1,182,617 iterations (2.37M oracle calls) versus only 6,727 iterations (7.43M oracle calls) for SBCGI, a ~176× speedup in iterations. In the dictionary learning task (1-minute budget), IR-SCG achieves 32,736 iterations versus 466 for SBCGI (~70× speedup). Theoretically, the methods achieve Ō(t^{-1/4}) convergence for both outer and inner objectives in the convex one-sample setting, Ō(t^{-1/2}) in the finite-sum convex setting, and Ō(t^{-1/7}) / Ō(t^{-1/4}) in the nonconvex one-sample / finite-sum settings, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SBCGI/SBCGF (Cao et al., NeurIPS 2023), the proposed IR-SCG/IR-FSCG methods achieve dramatically more iterations within the same wall-clock time (176× and 70× speedups on the two tasks), because they require only a linear minimization oracle over the base set X rather than over X ∩ {halfspace}. Critically, SBCGI and SBCGF exhibit *degradation* in inner-level optimality gaps over time (consistent with their theoretical guarantee of only ε_g/2-infeasibility), while IR-SCG/IR-FSCG maintain monotonic improvement. Against aR-IP-SeG (Jalilzadeh et al., 2024) and SDBGD (Gong et al., NeurIPS 2021), the paper's methods achieve substantially better outer-level objective values; neither aR-IP-SeG nor SDBGD makes significant progress on the outer-level objective in either experiment. The key practical advantage is that IR-SCG/IR-FSCG are anytime algorithms requiring no pre-specified tolerance ε_g, no expensive initialization to find an ε_g-optimal starting point, and no knowledge of total iterations T in advance, unlike all three baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deterministic projection-free conditional gradient framework of Giang-Tran et al. [20] to the stochastic setting, replacing exact gradients with STORM and SPIDER variance-reduced estimators. It departs from the closest stochastic competitor, Cao et al. [4], by eliminating the need for linear optimization over X ∩ halfspace (replacing it with LMO over X alone), removing the pre-specified tolerance ε_g and its associated expensive initialization, and providing anytime guarantees without requiring knowledge of the total iteration count T. The iterative regularization mechanism itself follows the classical Tikhonov-regularization lineage (Cabot [3], Dutta and Pandit [14], Solodov [49]) but is the first to combine it with stochastic variance reduction in a projection-free bilevel context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in oracle simplicity: many structured feasible sets (e.g., nuclear norm balls, sparsity constraints) admit cheap LMO over X but expensive LMO over X ∩ halfspace, so the proposed method broadens the class of problems where projection-free bilevel optimization is tractable. The anytime property and absence of initialization make the algorithm significantly easier to deploy in practice. Open questions include whether the rate gap relative to Cao et al. (O(t^{-1/4}) vs. O(t^{-1/2}) in the convex stochastic case) can be closed while retaining the simpler oracle, and whether the framework extends to the more general nonconvex-inner-level bilevel setting that remains largely unaddressed in the projection-free literature.
