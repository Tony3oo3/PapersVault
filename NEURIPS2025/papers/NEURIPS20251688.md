## Problem

Key cache quantization in LLMs is hampered by channel-wise outliers that make token-wise quantization infeasible. While KVQuant identifies RoPE as the outlier source and proposes pre-RoPE quantization, this requires on-the-fly RoPE recomputation at every generation step, introducing redundant computational overhead. The paper addresses how to eliminate these outliers without incurring per-step RoPE costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251688/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes post-RoPE key vectors (d-dimensional, where d is the per-head dimension) and current query vectors. Keys are decomposed into d/2 two-dimensional sub-vectors, each converted to a (radius, angle) pair and stored as an r-bit integer and a t-bit integer under asymmetric group-wise quantization (group size g=128). At decode time, the quantized (r, t) indices index into a pre-computed lookup table that yields the dot product with the corresponding query sub-vector, producing the final attention score as a sum over all d/2 pairs.

## Method

PolarQuant exploits the observation that RoPE rotates paired dimensions by the same angle, so outliers in one channel are compensated by the other, yielding smoothly distributed radii and angles in 2D polar space. Each 2D sub-vector (K[2j], K[2j+1]) is converted to polar coordinates (ρ, θ), then quantized asymmetrically with r bits for ρ and t bits for θ using per-group zero-points and scales. For decoding acceleration, PolarQuant pre-computes a lookup table mapping the finite set of 2^(r+t) quantized polar states to their Cartesian dot products with query sub-vectors, replacing the standard QK matrix multiplication with a table lookup. Custom fused Triton kernels perform dequantization and multiplication in a single pass, achieving up to 3.18× throughput speedup over full-precision inference at 32K context length. Unlike KVQuant's pre-RoPE quantization, PolarQuant operates entirely on post-RoPE keys, eliminating the need for RoPE recomputation at each generation step.

## Results

On LongBench, PolarQuant₄₄ achieves 49.39 average on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (128K context), a +0.13 improvement over the Bf16 baseline (49.26), while KIVI-4 scores 49.36 and token-wise Int-4 drops to 49.05. On Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct, where channel-wise outliers are most severe, PolarQuant₄₄ retains 36.41 average (−2.47 from Bf16) versus catastrophic collapse of Int-4 (15.30) and ZipCache-4 (13.61). For reasoning models, PolarQuant₄₄ on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B achieves 62.44 overall (+0.43 over Bf16), substantially outperforming KIVI-4 (59.71, −2.30). In terms of efficiency, PolarQuant delivers up to 3.18× end-to-end throughput speedup and 2.7× QK-multiplication kernel speedup over KIVI at 32K context length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KIVI (the strongest channel-wise quantization baseline), PolarQuant matches or slightly exceeds performance on Llama models: on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct at 3-bit, PolarQuant₃₃ scores 49.53 (+0.27 over Bf16) versus KIVI-2's 48.68 (−0.58), a 0.85-point gap. On Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct, the two methods are nearly tied at 4-bit (36.41 vs. 36.53) but PolarQuant₃₃ edges out KIVI-2 at 3-bit (35.34 vs. 35.64 is actually KIVI winning by 0.30). On reasoning benchmarks, PolarQuant clearly dominates: on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, PolarQuant₄₄ achieves 62.44 overall (+0.43 over Bf16) while KIVI-4 drops to 59.71 (−2.30), a 2.73-point advantage. Against ZipCache, PolarQuant is dramatically superior on Qwen where ZipCache-4 collapses to 13.61 (−25.27) versus PolarQuant's 36.41 (−2.47), a 22.8-point gap. Against QJL (3.13-bit JL-transform method), PolarQuant₃₃ outperforms on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (49.53 vs. 48.61) and Llama-2-7B-Chat (31.20 vs. 30.80). Token-wise Int-N baselines are far inferior on Qwen (15.30 at 4-bit), confirming that channel-wise outliers make per-token quantization infeasible without the polar transformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolarQuant extends the channel-wise group quantization framework introduced by KIVI [19] and the pre-RoPE quantization insight from KVQuant [13], but departs from both by reframing the key cache quantization problem in polar coordinates. Rather than quantizing raw post-RoPE key values (KIVI) or pre-RoPE keys with on-the-fly RoPE recomputation (KVQuant), PolarQuant converts each 2D RoPE-rotated sub-vector into (radius, angle) and quantizes those two scalars independently. The LUT-based decoding acceleration is conceptually inspired by KVQuant's lookup-table approach, but PolarQuant eliminates the RoPE recomputation overhead by pre-computing query-key dot products directly in polar space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory dominates long-context LLM inference, and key cache quantization has been the harder sub-problem due to channel-wise outliers; PolarQuant's polar-coordinate perspective offers a clean geometric resolution that avoids both the precision loss of token-wise quantization and the computational overhead of pre-RoPE quantization. The 3.18× decoding speedup from the fused LUT kernel makes this practically relevant for production long-context serving. However, the method is restricted to RoPE-based decoder-only Transformers, and its compatibility with alternative position encodings (ALiBi, learned embeddings) or newer architectures (MLA, hybrid attention) remains untested. The modest performance gap over KIVI (often <1 point on LongBench) suggests the primary value is in efficiency rather than accuracy.
