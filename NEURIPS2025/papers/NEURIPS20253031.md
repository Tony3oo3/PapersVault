## Problem

Coordinating large teams of heterogeneous mobile agents to perform tasks under temporal constraints (time windows, order dependencies) is NP-hard, and existing approaches—MILP solvers, metaheuristics, and sequential GNN-based policies—either fail to scale beyond modest problem sizes or require iterative per-decision recomputation that becomes prohibitively slow. Prior Heterogeneous Graph Transformers lack native support for edge features, which are essential for encoding agent-specific travel times and heterogeneous task execution durations in MATAS problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253031/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a heterogeneous graph with Agent, Task, Assignment, State, and Task Order nodes, where edges encode precomputed RRT-based travel times (distinct per agent due to heterogeneous velocities), task execution times, inter-task distances, and required wait times. A single forward pass simultaneously outputs a task allocation (agent-task pairing matrix of size |A|×|T| derived from Assignment node features) and a per-agent schedule (ordered task sequence derived from Task Order node outputs), with no iterative environment interaction. The model is trained exclusively on 10-agent/20-task instances but generalizes zero-shot to 40-agent/200-task problems.

## Method

TARGETNET augments the standard Heterogeneous Graph Transformer with a novel edge-attention mechanism: each message-passing step combines node-level attention (Att_N) and edge-level attention (Att_E) to update target node and edge features, leveraging the distributive property of attention so that relational information in edge features (travel times, execution durations) directly modulates how much each edge contributes to the target. The graph contains O(|A||T|) Assignment nodes with O(|A||T|²) inter-assignment edges encoding agent-specific travel times, and graph-raw residual connections append initial features at every layer to mitigate vanishing gradients. Training uses REINFORCE with a hybrid reward combining dense per-step feasibility signals (+1/−1) and a sparse terminal reward that penalizes makespan and counts feasible assignments, all computed from a single forward pass. This simultaneous formulation reduces inference complexity from O(|A||T|³) (sequential) to O(|A||T|²), enabling 20×–250× speedup over sequential GNN baselines while maintaining near-optimal feasibility and optimality.

## Results

On small-scale problems (10 agents, 20 tasks), TARGETNET achieves a 13.27% improvement in feasible task assignments over CA-EDF and 36.35% over HetGAT, generating schedules in under 0.20 seconds. At extra-large scale (40 agents, 200 tasks), where the MILP solver fails to produce fully feasible solutions within 12 hours, TARGETNET generates schedules with 70.80% more feasible tasks than the solver and 47.05% more than HetGAT in only 0.37% of the computation time. The model delivers a 20×–250× speedup over sequential GNN-based methods and 3.90×–26.18× speedup over heuristics at medium to extra-large scales, while being trained exclusively on small-scale instances (10 agents, 20 tasks) and generalizing without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Gurobi MILP exact solver, TARGETNET produces 231.73% more feasible tasks in 0.03% of the solver's time at small scale, and at extra-large scale (40 agents, 200 tasks) the solver times out after 12 hours with only partial schedules while TARGETNET completes in under 12 seconds with 70.80% more feasible assignments. Compared to the EDF and CA-EDF heuristics, TARGETNET assigns 164.10% more feasible tasks in 3.83% of the heuristic's runtime, and is 3.90×–26.18× faster at medium to extra-large scales. Against the Genetic Algorithm metaheuristics (GenRandom, Gen-EDF), TARGETNET outperforms by 201.54% in 0.01% of the computation time. Versus the HetGAT sequential GNN baseline, TARGETNET achieves 36.35% higher feasibility at small scale and 47.05% at extra-large scale, while running 538.56× faster due to its single-pass simultaneous architecture versus HetGAT's per-step environment updates. The ablation variants (TARGETNET\E, TARGETNET\R, TARGETNET\ER) confirm that both edge features and residual connections contribute to robustness, with the full model outperforming ablations by 16.91%–34.75% on average across three seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Heterogeneous Graph Transformers (HGT) [40] by augmenting their node-type-specific attention with an edge-feature attention mechanism, addressing a gap the authors identify: standard HGTs cannot natively process relational information encoded in edge features, which is critical for MATAS where travel times and execution durations live on edges. It also incorporates graph-raw residual connections [41] to stabilize deep GNN training. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior GNN-based MATAS methods (HetGAT [61], Wang & Gombolay [33]) that perform sequential, one-assignment-at-a-time decision-making, instead proposing a simultaneous (single-forward-pass) policy that jointly outputs both task allocation and scheduling, trained via REINFORCE for the simultaneous variant and SAC for the sequential baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the 20×–250× inference speedup over sequential GNN baselines while matching or exceeding their solution quality, and in the demonstrated inductive generalization from 10-agent/20-task training to 40-agent/200-task deployment without retraining—something exact solvers and metaheuristics cannot achieve at scale. Key limitations include the static-environment assumption (no dynamic obstacles or inter-agent collision avoidance), the lack of interpretability in the learned graph policy, and the observation that sequential models still yield superior quality at the largest scales, suggesting a hybrid architecture as a natural next step. Open questions include whether the edge-attention mechanism transfers to other combinatorial optimization problems with rich edge features, and how the approach performs under truly dynamic or stochastic environments.
