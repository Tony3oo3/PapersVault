## Problem

Competitive programming benchmarks increasingly contain duplicate or highly similar problems, inflating LLM pass rates and undermining contest fairness, yet no dedicated benchmark exists for problem-level retrieval in this domain. Existing code-centric retrieval benchmarks (e.g., CoIR, APPS) suffer from severe train-test leakage due to lack of temporal separation, and they overlook the critical task of retrieving similar problems themselves. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a temporally separated, multi-task retrieval benchmark and specialized models for competitive programming.

## Input / Output

The system consumes three types of text: full natural-language problem statements (avg. ~1000 tokens), multi-language solution code (C++, Python, Java, etc., avg. ~1200 tokens), and user-generated simplified/paraphrased problem descriptions (avg. ~226 tokens). During training, non-essential portions of problem descriptions (I/O format, sample I/O, explicit constraints) are randomly masked to force the model to focus on core algorithmic intent. The output is a single dense embedding vector per input, enabling cosine-similarity ranking over corpora of up to ~42K problems or ~2.9M code solutions across four distinct retrieval tasks.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage training pipeline built on SFR-Embedding-Code-2B_R (Gemma-2-2B). Stage 1 (CPRetriever-Code) introduces Group-InfoNCE, a contrastive loss that treats all m correct solutions of a problem as a positive group: it maximizes the query-to-group similarity (mean over positives) while contrasting against other problems and groups, and adds a variance-based penalty that regularizes the spread of similarities within the positive set, preventing collapse of distinct algorithmic approaches. Stage 2 (CPRetriever-Prob) fine-tunes the Stage-1 model with a triplet margin loss on Problem-to-Duplicate and Simplified-to-Full pairs, using hard negatives mined via a pretrained retriever and verified by Qwen-2.5-Max to exclude false matches. The pipeline is validated on both the 2B base and Qwen3-Embedding-4B, with ablations confirming that Group-InfoNCE outperforms standard InfoNCE and naive multi-positive averaging, and that the two-stage design yields complementary specializations: CPRetriever-Code excels on code-centric tasks while CPRetriever-Prob dominates problem-centric tasks.

## Results

On the CPRet benchmark (NDCG@10), CPRetriever-Prob-Qwen3-4B achieves the best overall average of 84.60 across four retrieval tasks, with standout scores of 96.15 on Simplified-to-Full and 74.33 on Problem-to-Duplicate. At the 2B scale, CPRetriever-Code achieves 70.40 on Text-to-Code and 70.59 on Code-to-Code, substantially outperforming its base model SFR-Embedding-Code-2B (39.60 and 68.05 respectively). CPRetriever-Prob (2B) reaches 60.06 on Problem-to-Duplicate and 90.74 on Simplified-to-Full, surpassing the strongest code-specific baseline SFR-Emb.-Code-2B (45.26 and 86.43) by 14.8 and 4.3 points. The 4B-scale models further extend these gains, with CPRetriever-Code-Qwen3-4B reaching 86.22 on T2C and 86.70 on C2C.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 17 baselines spanning general-purpose (e.g., Qwen3-Embedding, E5, GTE, BGE, NV-Embed, Linq-Embed) and code-specific (e.g., CodeSage, SFR-Embedding-Code, Qodo-Embed) embedding models. At the 2B scale, CPRetriever-Code nearly doubles the T2C score of its base SFR-Emb.-Code-2B (70.40 vs. 39.60) and slightly improves C2C (70.59 vs. 68.05), while CPRetriever-Prob gains 14.8 points on P2Dup (60.06 vs. 45.26) and 4.3 points on S2Full (90.74 vs. 86.43) over the same base. At the 4B scale, CPRetriever-Prob-Qwen3-4B outperforms the strongest general-purpose baseline Qwen3-Embedding-4B by 13.45 points in average NDCG@10 (84.60 vs. 71.15), with the largest gap on P2Dup (74.33 vs. 56.59). Code-specific baselines consistently outperform general-purpose ones of comparable size, but the proposed models lead all code-specific baselines on their respective target tasks. The only notable trade-off is that CPRetriever-Prob sacrifices ~14 points on T2C relative to CPRetriever-Code (56.50 vs. 70.40 at 2B), reflecting the inherent tension between code-level and problem-level retrieval objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the competitive programming retrieval line initiated by CoIR (which defined Text-to-Code and Code-to-Code tasks on APPS/CodeTransOcean) by introducing two problem-centric retrieval tasks—Problem-to-Duplicate and Simplified-to-Full—that were previously undefined in the literature. Methodologically, it builds on the SFR-Embedding-Code-2B_R architecture and the standard InfoNCE contrastive framework, proposing Group-InfoNCE as a principled extension that models multiple correct solutions as a structured positive set with variance-based regularization rather than treating them as independent positives. The temporal-separation strategy for test construction draws on LiveCodeBench's contamination-aware design but applies it more rigorously, using only post-2023 problems to avoid the data leakage effects the paper documents in earlier benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most impactful finding is empirical: problems with high retrieval similarity to prior data systematically inflate LLM pass rates and compress performance gaps between model variants, suggesting that current competitive programming benchmarks may overestimate genuine algorithmic reasoning. This has direct implications for how the field constructs and interprets LLM coding evaluations. Open questions include whether the 700 annotated duplicate pairs capture the full scope of redundancy (especially on decentralized platforms), whether the temporal separation will hold as training corpora expand, and whether the similarity-aware evaluation principle generalizes to other domains like mathematical competitions. The practical tool (CPRetriever online demo) and the 50% duplicate rate detected in a real 2025 contest underscore the urgency of the problem.
