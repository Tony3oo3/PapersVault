## Problem

Despite numerous AIGI detectors reporting over 95% accuracy in controlled settings, their real-world reliability remains unverified. Existing benchmarks fail to systematically assess generalization to unseen generators, robustness to common image degradations, sensitivity to data augmentation choices, or the impact of test-time preprocessing—four critical factors that determine practical deployment. This paper asks whether AIGI detection is truly a solved problem by constructing a realistic evaluation framework that exposes these gaps.

## Input / Output

Each detector receives a single RGB image (potentially degraded by JPEG compression, Gaussian noise, or up-down sampling, and pre-processed via crop or resize) and outputs a binary real/fake decision with a confidence score. The benchmark decomposes overall accuracy into R.Acc. (correctly identifying real images) and F.Acc. (correctly identifying fake images), revealing that high aggregate accuracy often masks near-zero fake detection under perturbations. Evaluation spans 25 test subsets with balanced real/fake pairs drawn from FFHQ, CelebA-HQ, Open Images V7, and 23 synthetic sources.

## Method

AIGIBench is a comprehensive benchmark rather than a new detector. It constructs 23 diverse fake-image subsets covering GAN-based noise-to-image (ProGAN, StyleGAN3, StyleGAN-XL, etc.), diffusion text-to-image (SD-XL, SD-3, DALLE-3, Midjourney-v6, FLUX.1-dev, Imagen-3, GLIDE), GAN deepfakes (BlendFace, E4S, FaceSwap, InSwap, SimSwap), diffusion personalized generation (InstantID, Infinite-ID, PhotoMaker, BLIP-Diffusion, IP-Adapter), and in-the-wild collections from social media (SocialRF) and AI art communities (CommunityAI). Eleven SOTA detectors are re-trained under two settings (ProGAN-only vs. ProGAN+SD-v1.4) and evaluated across four tasks: multi-source generalization, multi-degradation robustness, data augmentation variation, and test-time preprocessing (crop vs. resize). The key finding is that all detectors suffer severe F.Acc. collapse under JPEG compression and Gaussian noise (often dropping below 5%), while R.Acc. remains artificially high near 100%, exposing a strong 'predict-real' bias that aggregate accuracy metrics conceal.

## Results

Under Setting-II (trained on 144K SD-v1.4 + ProGAN images), SAFE achieves the highest mean accuracy (79.9%) and AIDE the highest mean A.P. (82.7%) across 25 test subsets. However, all 11 detectors suffer severe degradation on real-world data: mean F.Acc. drops below 20% on SocialRF and CommunityAI, and traditional CNNs (ResNet-50, CNNDetection, Gram-Net) achieve near-zero F.Acc. (<5%) on most OOD generators despite >95% R.Acc. Under JPEG compression (quality=50), F.Acc. collapses to 0–2% for nearly all detectors while R.Acc. remains above 94%, revealing a strong 'predict-real' bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFE [58] leads in overall accuracy (79.9% mean Acc., 96.8% R.Acc.) and maintains the most balanced F.Acc./R.Acc. trade-off across GAN and diffusion generators, but still drops to 16.4% F.Acc. on SocialRF. AIDE [20] achieves the highest mean A.P. (82.7%) and the best F.Acc. among Setting-I-trained models (69.0%), excelling on OOD GANs (99.0% on R3GAN) yet failing on deepfakes (14.3% on FaceSwap). CLIPDetection [19] and FreqNet [57] show the strongest robustness under degradation (maintaining 33–59% F.Acc. under JPEG/noise vs. <2% for CNNs), but sacrifice R.Acc. (73.3% and 65.9% respectively). Traditional CNNs (ResNet-50 [65], CNNDetection [64], Gram-Net [66]) achieve >95% R.Acc. in-distribution but collapse to <5% F.Acc. on nearly all OOD generators, confirming severe overfitting. NPR [23] and DFFreq [68] occupy a middle ground with moderate F.Acc. (41.9% and 51.9%) but remain vulnerable to deepfake and in-the-wild content. No single detector dominates all 25 subsets, and all methods degrade substantially on SocialRF and CommunityAI, confirming that AIGI detection is far from solved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new detection method but instead carves new ground as a comprehensive evaluation benchmark (AIGIBench) for the AIGI detection task. It departs from prior benchmarks (GenImage, DeepfakeBench, DF40, WildFake, Chameleon) by being the first to simultaneously evaluate all four pipeline components—multi-source generalization, multi-degradation robustness, data augmentation sensitivity, and test-time preprocessing—under a unified protocol. It also distinguishes itself by incorporating the most recent generative models (FLUX.1-dev, Imagen-3, SD-3, Midjourney-v6) and real-world in-the-wild data from social media and AI art communities, which earlier benchmarks largely omitted. The 11 detectors evaluated (ResNet-50 through SAFE) serve as subjects of study rather than as methodological contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that detectors reporting >95% accuracy in controlled settings suffer catastrophic F.Acc. drops (often below 5%) under common degradations like JPEG compression, while R.Acc. remains artificially high near 100%—challenges the field's assumption that AIGI detection is approaching a solved state. The observation that no single detector dominates across all 25 subsets, and that standard data augmentation provides limited or even negative benefits, points to a fundamental gap between benchmark performance and real-world reliability. Open questions include whether incorporating deepfake-style manipulations during training can close the generalization gap, whether frequency-domain or CLIP-based features offer a path to true robustness, and how detectors should handle the growing diversity of 2024+ generative models. The benchmark's limitation to two training settings (ProGAN-only and ProGAN+SD-v1.4) and its planned leaderboard make it a living resource likely to shape future evaluation standards.
