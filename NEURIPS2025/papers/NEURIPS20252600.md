## Problem

LLM-based robot planners produce overconfident yet potentially unsafe plans due to hallucination and instruction ambiguity, yet existing uncertainty estimation methods treat planning uncertainty as a monolithic quantity without distinguishing between reducible epistemic uncertainty (stemming from model ignorance) and irreducible intrinsic uncertainty (stemming from environmental randomness). This conflation leads to misattribution of uncertainty sources and inaccurate confidence calibration, limiting the ability to safely halt execution or request human help.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural language instruction, a scene description listing available objects, and the LLM-generated task plan, which are jointly fed into Llama3.2-8B to extract the hidden activation of the last token from the final layer as a fixed-dimensional task encoding vector T. The output is a scalar composite uncertainty score U in [0,1] that decomposes into three interpretable sub-scores: a task familiarity metric A_sim (from RND prediction error), a task clarity score A_amb (binary or continuous), and an expected success rate p. The final score drives a tri-state decision: proceed autonomously, halt, or request human clarification.

## Method

CURE decomposes planning uncertainty into epistemic (task familiarity + task clarity) and intrinsic (expected success rate) components, each estimated by a dedicated module. Task familiarity is assessed via Random Network Distillation: a frozen random target network and a trained predictor network both consume the task encoding vector T, and their output Euclidean distance serves as an unfamiliarity signal. Task clarity is evaluated by two parallel paths—a slow LLM-query-based binary ambiguity check and a fast MLP regression head—and the expected success rate is predicted by a second MLP head within the same Uncertainty Assessment Network (UAN). The UAN is trained with a cross-entropy loss where the success-rate branch is gated by an indicator that only activates on unambiguous tasks, preventing the model from learning spurious success signals from inherently unclear instructions. The final uncertainty U is a weighted combination of all three sub-scores via tunable parameters α₁, α₂, α₃, and the entire pipeline is plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modification to the underlying LLM planner.

## Results

On the kitchen manipulation task (Table 1), CURE-Ambiguity achieves the highest Spearman correlation of 0.466 (p = 1.46×10⁻¹⁷) and SR-HR-AUC of 0.547, outperforming the best baseline KnowNo (Spearman 0.336, SR-HR-AUC 0.395) by 38% in correlation and 38% in AUC. On the tabletop rearrangement task (Table 3), CURE achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.635 (p = 2.83×10⁻³⁵) and SR-HR-AUC of 0.732, nearly doubling the best baseline (Vanilla: Spearman 0.328, SR-HR-AUC 0.293). The CURE w/o sim variant (without RND-based familiarity) still achieves Spearman 0.610 and SR-HR-AUC 0.703 on tabletop, confirming the UAN network's core contribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CURE outperforms all three baseline families across both evaluation metrics. Against KnowNo [47] (the strongest robotics-specific baseline), CURE-Ambiguity improves Spearman correlation from 0.336 to 0.466 (+38.7%) and SR-HR-AUC from 0.395 to 0.547 (+38.5%) on kitchen tasks; on tabletop rearrangement, CURE improves from 0.302 to 0.635 (+110%) in Spearman and from 0.351 to 0.732 (+108%) in SR-HR-AUC. Against IntroPlan [48], the advantage is dramatic: IntroPlan achieves a near-zero Spearman of 0.030 (p = 0.60, not significant) and SR-HR-AUC of 0.005 on kitchen tasks, while CURE reaches 0.454 and 0.534 respectively. Against the LLM confidence-elicitation methods from Xiong et al. [38] (Vanilla, CoT, Self-probing, Top-k, Multi-step), CURE consistently achieves the highest Spearman correlation (0.454 vs. best baseline 0.254 for Top-k) and SR-HR-AUC (0.534 vs. best 0.297 for Multi-step) on kitchen tasks, with even larger margins on tabletop (0.635 vs. 0.328 for Vanilla). The ablation variant CURE w/o sim (removing RND familiarity) still substantially outperforms all baselines, indicating the UAN network alone provides most of the gain, while RND adds a further ~2–4% improvement in Spearman correlation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the epistemic/aleatoric uncertainty decomposition from reinforcement learning and applies it to LLM-based robot task planning, a setting where prior work (KnowNo, IntroPlan) treated uncertainty as a monolithic signal. It further subdivides epistemic uncertainty into task clarity and task familiarity, borrowing Random Network Distillation from RL exploration to quantify familiarity and introducing MLP regression heads trained on LLM hidden states for clarity and success-rate prediction. The approach is positioned as a plug-and-play module orthogonal to the planner itself, contrasting with KnowNo's conformal-prediction framing and IntroPlan's knowledge-base retrieval, while also subsuming the various LLM confidence-elicitation strategies (vanilla, CoT, self-probing, top-k, multi-step) from Xiong et al. as baselines rather than building on any single one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By separating the sources of uncertainty, CURE enables a robot to distinguish 'I don't understand the instruction' from 'the instruction is clear but the environment is unpredictable,' which has direct safety implications for halting execution versus requesting clarification. The plug-and-play design means existing LLM planners can adopt the module without architectural changes, lowering the barrier to deployment. However, the method requires task-specific pretraining of the UAN and RND networks, limiting generalizability to unseen task distributions, and the uncertainty scores are not yet calibrated to actual success probabilities—both gaps the authors flag for future work. Open questions include whether dynamic, online-updated familiarity models can eliminate the need for per-domain retraining and whether incorporating physical priors would improve transfer across environments.
