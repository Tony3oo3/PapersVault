## Problem

Existing 3D vision datasets lack the critical combination of ground-truth 3D geometry, full day-to-night lighting variation, and natural egocentric 6DoF motion needed to evaluate robust all-day perception. Driving datasets are limited to planar motion, handheld datasets offer limited lighting diversity, and prior egocentric datasets (e.g., LaMAR) rely on grayscale imagery unsuitable for color-dependent applications. This paper introduces Oxford Day-and-Night to fill this gap with a large-scale, lighting-diverse egocentric benchmark.

## Input / Output

For NVS, the pipeline consumes 1408×1408 RGB fisheye frames (110° FOV) at 20 FPS from Meta ARIA glasses, MPS-estimated camera poses, and semi-dense point clouds filtered to depth std < 0.4 m; it produces novel view renderings and 3D geometry as Gaussian primitive centers evaluated against TLS ground truth. For relocalization, a single query image is matched against a database of daytime images (with a pre-built COLMAP/SfM model) to recover a 6DoF pose. The dataset provides both original fisheye and two undistorted pinhole variants (Max FOV with mask, All Valid), and the relocalization split comprises 5,466 database, 2,819 daytime query, and 7,197 nighttime query images across five Oxford scenes.

## Method

The dataset is built by recording 2–10 minute egocentric videos at five Oxford locations between 4–10 pm (capturing day-to-night transitions) using Meta ARIA glasses (Profile 2: dual IMUs at 1000/800 Hz, RGB and global-shutter grayscale SLAM cameras). Multi-session SLAM via Meta's cloud-based MPS fuses all recordings into a single global coordinate frame, yielding per-frame 6DoF poses and semi-dense point clouds. These are aligned to Oxford Spires TLS ground truth (Leica RTC360, mm-level) through an automated AprilTag + ICP pipeline achieving 6.7 cm mean point-to-point error. Two dataset variants are derived: an NVS variant (5× temporal subsampling, fisheye undistortion, uncertainty-based point cloud filtering) and a relocalization variant (spatial filtering with scene-specific position/orientation thresholds, 2:1 day database/query split, all night frames as queries). Benchmarking evaluates Splatfacto-W and Gaussian-Wild for NVS, and the HLoc pipeline with seven sparse/dense matchers plus three SCR methods for relocalization, revealing substantial day-to-night performance drops and identifying retrieval as the key bottleneck for matching-based approaches.

## Results

On the Oxford Day-and-Night visual relocalization benchmark, feature-matching (FM) methods dramatically outperform scene-coordinate-regression (SCR) methods, especially at night. Among FM methods, RoMA achieves the best night-query accuracy (e.g., 57.91% at (0.5 m, 5°) on H.B. Allen Centre), while SIFT drops from ~90% (day) to ~10% (night) on Bodleian Library. SCR methods ACE and GLACE essentially fail at night (0.00% at the strictest threshold across all outdoor scenes), whereas R-SCoRe retains modest performance (2–6% at (0.25 m, 2°)). For NVS, both Splatfacto-W and Gaussian-Wild achieve PSNR > 25 dB but LPIPS > 0.56, indicating poor perceptual quality in large-scale, day-to-night scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This dataset paper benchmarks 10 relocalization methods (7 FM + 3 SCR) and 2 NVS methods on the new Oxford Day-and-Night benchmark. Among FM methods, RoMA [61] consistently outperforms all others at night, achieving 57.91% (0.5 m, 5°) on H.B. Allen Centre versus 44.32% for SP+SG [49] and 41.20% for LoFTR [50]; SIFT [58] and DISK+LG [60] degrade most severely (≤10% on most outdoor scenes). Among SCR methods, R-SCoRe [63] is the only one retaining non-trivial night performance (up to 23.61% on HBAC), while ACE [51] and GLACE [52] collapse to near-zero accuracy across all outdoor night scenes. A retrieval ablation (Table 3) shows that pairing RoMA with MegaLoc [68] retrieval boosts Bodleian night accuracy from 25.24% to 70.25% at (0.25 m, 2°), revealing retrieval as the primary bottleneck. For NVS, Gaussian-Wild [47] outperforms Splatfacto-W [46] on 4 of 5 scenes in PSNR (e.g., 28.38 vs. 25.98 on Bodleian), but both suffer LPIPS > 0.56, and Splatfacto-W produces degenerate point clouds (<2000 Gaussians) on Bodleian and Keble College.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining three elements that no prior dataset has simultaneously provided: natural egocentric head motion (via Meta ARIA glasses), high-fidelity 3D ground-truth geometry (via Oxford Spires TLS scans), and continuous day-to-night lighting variation at city scale. It departs from driving datasets (Oxford RoboCar, KITTI) that offer scale but only planar motion, from handheld relocalization benchmarks (Aachen Day-Night, Cambridge Landmarks) that provide pose diversity but limited lighting range, and from egocentric datasets (Ego4D, LaMAR) that capture first-person video but lack 3D geometry or color. The multi-session SLAM pipeline (Meta MPS) serves as the enabling infrastructure, automating camera pose annotation across sessions with dramatic illumination changes, while AprilTag-based alignment to TLS maps provides the geometric ground truth that prior egocentric datasets have lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical gap: current state-of-the-art methods degrade severely under night conditions, with scene-coordinate-regression approaches (ACE, GLACE) failing almost entirely (near 0% accuracy) and feature-matching methods dropping from 90%+ to 20–60% at night. The retrieval-bottleneck finding (Table 3) is practically important—pairing RoMA with ground-truth retrieval recovers 80%+ accuracy, showing that image retrieval, not dense matching, is the primary accuracy limiter under day-night shifts. For NVS, the degeneration of 3DGS in-the-wild methods (fewer than 2000 surviving Gaussians in two scenes) and the PSNR/LPIPS discrepancy highlight that existing metrics and methods are inadequate for large-scale, lighting-diverse scenes. Open questions include how to build retrieval systems robust to extreme photometric change, how to make 3DGS handle city-scale scenes with day-night transitions, and whether the 6.7 cm SLAM pose accuracy is sufficient for all downstream tasks.
