## Problem

Existing temporal reasoning benchmarks for LLMs (e.g., TimeBench, TRAM) focus on simplified scenarios such as basic temporal commonsense and short-text relationships, neglecting three critical real-world challenges: the density of temporal information in world knowledge, the rapid evolution of event details over time, and the complex temporal dependencies in multi-session social interactions. Additionally, temporal reasoning is a hierarchical framework of fine-grained abilities, yet prior benchmarks either target only specific aspects (e.g., neuro-symbolic reasoning in TReMu) or overlook fundamental temporal concept understanding, leaving a significant gap in comprehensive evaluation.

## Input / Output

Each QA instance pairs a text context with a temporal reasoning question. Contexts vary by sub-dataset: TIME-WIKI uses LLM-generated narratives around interconnected entities from Wikidata; TIME-NEWS uses RAG-retrieved segments (top-3 articles) from very long temporal complex events exceeding 500k tokens; TIME-DIAL preserves original multi-session dialogs averaging over 15k tokens. Questions come in three formats—free-form, single-choice, and multiple-choice (the latter synthesized via the STARC framework with misleading options to reduce calibration errors from synonymous expressions). Evaluation uses token-level F1 for free-form answers, Exact Match for the Timeline task, and option-level macro F1 for choice-based tasks.

## Method

TIME is a multi-level benchmark with 38,522 QA pairs organized into three progressive levels (basic temporal retrieval, temporal expression reasoning, complex temporal relationship reasoning) spanning 11 fine-grained subtasks. Construction follows a unified pipeline: temporal facts are collected (via SLING-parsed Wikidata for TIME-WIKI, pre-existing time points from TCELongBench for TIME-NEWS, and LLM-extracted event graphs from LOCOMO/REALTALK for TIME-DIAL), timelines are generated by chronologically organizing these facts, and QA pairs are synthesized using rule-based templates combined with DeepSeek-V3/R1 for natural language refinement. For TIME-NEWS, a RAG framework with BM25, Vector, and Hybrid retrievers handles the impractical full-text evaluation of 500k+ token articles. A 943-instance human-annotated subset (TIME-LITE) is curated through multi-round expert review, achieving 89.13% consistency with the full synthesized data, enabling efficient and reliable evaluation across 24 models.

## Results

Across 24 models evaluated on TIME, the strongest advanced model (Deepseek-R1) achieves 96.67% on Extract and 93.33% on Order Compare (TIME-LITE-WIKI), yet still scores only 33.33% on the Timeline task. The Timeline subtask is the most challenging across all scenarios: even o3-mini reaches at most 33.33% (TIME-WIKI) and 27.59% (TIME-NEWS), while small vanilla models fall below 10%. On TIME-DIAL, the hardest scenario for retrieval, even Deepseek-R1 achieves only 48.56% on Localization and 65.00% on Extract, substantially lower than its performance on other datasets. Test-time scaling (R1-Distill vs. vanilla Qwen2.5-14B) yields a 24.44% gain on Order Compare and 12.0% on Counterfactual (TIME-DIAL), but causes an 8.16% drop on Localization (TIME-WIKI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 24 LLMs across three model tiers (non-reasoning, reasoning/distilled, and advanced) on all 11 temporal reasoning subtasks. Among non-reasoning models, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct leads on basic retrieval (81.70% Extract, 83.84% Localization) but lags reasoning models on logical tasks (e.g., 66.64% DC vs. 88.20% for QwQ-32B). Test-time scaling consistently benefits logical reasoning: Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B outperforms Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct by 24.44% on Order Compare and 12.0% on Counterfactual (TIME-DIAL), yet underperforms by 8.16% on Localization (TIME-WIKI), revealing that extended chain-of-thought can introduce overthinking errors in retrieval. Among advanced models, Deepseek-R1 and o3-mini are the strongest overall, with o3-mini achieving the best Duration Compare (92.86%) and Co-temporality (54.34%) on TIME-WIKI, while Deepseek-R1 leads on Order Reasoning (57.09%) and Counterfactual (55.71%). GPT-4o achieves the highest Extract score (98.89%) but is outperformed by both Deepseek-R1 and o3-mini on most Level-2 and Level-3 tasks. The Timeline task remains the universal bottleneck: no model exceeds 33.33% on any dataset, and small vanilla models (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B) score below 1.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the temporal reasoning benchmark lineage (TimeBench, TRAM, TReMu, TCELongBench) by unifying three previously separate real-world scenarios—knowledge-intensive facts (via Wikidata/SLING), rapidly evolving news events (via TCELongBench's temporal complex events), and very-long multi-session dialogs (via LOCOMO/REALTALK)—into a single hierarchical framework with 11 fine-grained subtasks across 3 progressive levels. Where prior benchmarks either focused on simplified commonsense or targeted only one temporal aspect (e.g., TReMu on neuro-symbolic reasoning, TCELongBench on event ordering), TIME deliberately covers the full spectrum from basic temporal retrieval to counterfactual multi-event reasoning. The QA synthesis pipeline borrows from STARC for multiple-choice generation and from TempLAMA's SLING-based Wikidata parsing for structured temporal fact extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIME provides the first unified, multi-level evaluation that captures the full complexity of real-world temporal reasoning, and its results reveal that even frontier models (o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1) achieve below 55% on complex temporal relationship tasks and below 30% on Timeline ordering—highlighting a substantial gap between current LLM capabilities and human-like temporal understanding. The finding that test-time scaling consistently improves logical reasoning but can degrade retrieval performance (e.g., 12.49% Localization drop on TIME-DIAL) challenges the assumption that extended chain-of-thought universally benefits all sub-competencies. Open questions include whether the extremely low Timeline performance reflects a fundamental architectural limitation in sequence modeling, and whether the TIME-LITE subset (943 instances) will become a standard lightweight evaluation protocol for rapid model comparison.
