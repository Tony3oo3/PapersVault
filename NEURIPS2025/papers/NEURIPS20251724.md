## Problem

Active inference improves over uniform sampling by prioritizing uncertain instances for labeling, but its reliance on independent sampling induces variance inflation and produces imbalanced labeled sets that poorly represent the population distribution. This paper addresses how to further improve statistical efficiency under a fixed labeling budget by enforcing structural balance in the selected samples without increasing annotation costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251724/cropped_page7_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small labeled dataset (features and labels), a large unlabeled dataset (features only), and a labeling budget n_b specifying how many unlabeled instances to query. It requires a predictive model and an uncertainty model trained on the labeled data, with task-specific uncertainty measures (absolute residual for regression; 1 minus max class probability for classification). The output is a population mean estimate with a confidence interval, along with binary selection indicators determining which unlabeled instances receive labels.

## Method

The core contribution is integrating the cube method—a two-phase balanced sampling algorithm (flight phase for iterative probability adjustment, landing phase for resolving residual imbalances)—into active inference by using model uncertainty estimates as the balancing auxiliary variable. The balancing constraint requires the weighted sum of uncertainties among selected instances to match the population total, preventing oversampling from specific uncertainty regions. A τ-mixed sampling rule blends uncertainty-proportional and uniform inclusion probabilities to guard against instability from misspecified uncertainty. The resulting estimator is a generalized difference (GD) estimator combining model predictions with inverse-probability-weighted correction terms, and the paper proves it achieves asymptotic normality with strictly lower variance than traditional active inference, since the balancing constraint eliminates the additional variability from estimation error interacting with non-uniform sampling.

## Results

Under a 0.1 labeling budget, the proposed cube-active method achieves the narrowest 90% confidence interval widths across 11 datasets, reducing CI width by approximately 25%–85% compared to traditional active inference (independent sampling) and by at least 30% compared to uniform (PPI) sampling. For example, on the Bike Sharing dataset, cube-active yields a CI width of 0.3316 versus 2.2052 for traditional-active and 2.8213 for uniform. In terms of label budget efficiency, cube-active requires only 33%–93% of the labeling budget needed by traditional-active to match its precision at a 0.2 budget, with the Energy dataset requiring less than 10% of the benchmark budget. Empirical coverage rates remain close to the nominal 0.9 level across most datasets, confirming valid inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares cube-active against three baselines: (i) classical simple random sampling with a sample mean estimator, (ii) prediction-powered inference (PPI) with uniform random labeling [Angelopoulos et al., 2023], and (iii) traditional active inference with independent uncertainty-based sampling [Zrnic & Candès, 2024]. Cube-active dominates all three baselines on every dataset in terms of CI width. Against traditional-active (the closest competitor), cube-active narrows CIs by roughly 25%–85% (e.g., 0.0722 vs. 0.1443 on Linear synthetic; 0.3316 vs. 2.2052 on Bike Sharing). Against uniform PPI, the improvement is at least 30% across all tasks. In the budget-saving analysis (Table 2), cube-active requires only 33%–93% of the labeling budget that traditional-active needs to reach the same precision benchmark, with the Energy dataset requiring less than 10%. The classical baseline is consistently the worst, requiring >150% of the benchmark budget in most settings. Coverage rates remain near the nominal 0.9 level for cube-active on most datasets, though slight under-coverage appears on Energy (0.803) and Credit Fraud Detection (0.870), likely due to the extreme class imbalance and small effective sample sizes in those settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of active statistical inference (Zrnic & Candès, ICML 2024), prediction-powered inference (Angelopoulos et al., Science 2023), and the cube method for balanced sampling (Deville & Tillé, Biometrika 2004). The key conceptual departure is reconceptualizing model uncertainty estimates as dynamic auxiliary covariates for a balancing constraint, rather than merely as sampling weights in an independent-sampling scheme. This transforms the active inference problem from one of variance-inflated unequal-probability sampling into a covariate-balanced design problem, leveraging the GD estimator framework (Cassel et al., 1976) with a structural constraint that the weighted sum of uncertainties in the labeled subset matches the population total.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical resolution to a known weakness of active inference—variance inflation from independent sampling—by showing that balanced sampling yields a strictly lower asymptotic variance (Theorem 1a vs. 1b) without requiring the predictive model to be unbiased. The 25–85% confidence-interval width reductions across 11 datasets are practically significant for label-constrained settings (medical diagnostics, remote sensing, census). Open questions include extending the framework to sequential/online settings where auxiliary variables update dynamically, and understanding how robust the variance-reduction guarantee is when Assumption 5 (uncertainty model quality) is mildly violated, which the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
