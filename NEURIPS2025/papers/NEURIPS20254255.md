## Problem

Current egocentric vision benchmarks focus on physical activities, hand-object interactions, and attention modeling while implicitly assuming affect-neutral and behaviorally uniform participants. This gap prevents vision systems from capturing the internal emotional and dispositional states that shape human decisions, social interaction, and intent in real-world settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254255/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are synchronized multimodal streams from Meta's Project Aria glasses (per-eye tracking video at 640×480/90 fps, 1408×1408 POV RGB at 10 fps, dual IMUs at 1000/800 Hz, nosepad PPG at 128 Hz) supplemented by external ECG (1024 Hz), EDA (256 Hz), and respiratory (400 Hz) sensors, all recorded over 16 tasks spanning induced video stimuli and naturalistic activities. Outputs are three prediction targets: binarized arousal/valence/dominance (low/high via median split), a 9-class discrete emotion label derived from the dominant emotion in a weighted Mikels' Wheel self-report (100% distributed across nine emotions in 10% increments), and binarized Big Five personality traits. All predictions are evaluated per-task using leave-one-subject-out cross-validation across 43 participants.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is the egoEMOTION dataset (50+ hours, 43 participants, 16 tasks) paired with three benchmark tasks and classical ML baselines. For feature extraction, 612 hand-crafted features are computed per task: 15 statistical descriptors (mean, std, percentiles, RMS, skewness, kurtosis, line integral, etc.) applied to pupil size, pixel intensity, Fisherface projections, eye gaze yaw/pitch, blink counts, LBP-TOP micro-expression features, and acceleration magnitude, plus 77 ECG/PPG HRV features, 31 EDA features, and 14 respiratory features. Continuous affect uses an SVM with RBF kernel on standardized features; discrete emotion and personality use Random Forest classifiers with SelectKBest mutual-information feature selection (top 10 features). Deep learning baselines (a CNN and the WER transformer) are also evaluated on raw filtered signals. The key empirical finding is that egocentric signals—particularly eye-tracking features and head IMU—outperform traditional physiological sensors (ECG, EDA, RSP) across all three tasks, with the full egocentric-only fusion reaching F1=0.75 for affect, 0.46 for emotion, and 0.57 for personality versus 0.70, 0.24, and 0.50 for wearable-only signals.

## Results

Using classical ML (SVM for affect, Random Forest for emotions and personality) with leave-one-subject-out cross-validation, the paper achieves mean F1 scores of 0.75 (continuous affect), 0.46 (9-class discrete emotion), and 0.59 (Big Five personality) when fusing all modalities. Notably, signals from the egocentric Aria glasses alone (F1 = 0.74, 0.46, 0.57) match or exceed traditional wearable physiological sensors (F1 = 0.70, 0.24, 0.50), with pupil size and IMU acceleration being the strongest individual egocentric features. All results substantially exceed random baselines (0.59, 0.11, 0.53 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's classical ML baselines (SVM with RBF kernel for affect; Random Forest with SelectKBest for emotions and personality) outperform both deep learning baselines across all three tasks. Against the CNN [55], the classical method leads by 7 F1 points on continuous affect (0.75 vs. 0.68), 24 points on discrete emotion (0.46 vs. 0.22), and 18 points on personality (0.59 vs. 0.41). Against the transformer-based WER [65], the margins are even larger: 15 points on affect (0.75 vs. 0.60), 25 points on emotion (0.46 vs. 0.21), and 15 points on personality (0.59 vs. 0.44). A key finding is that the egocentric glasses modality alone (F1 = 0.46 for emotion) dramatically outperforms wearable physiological signals alone (F1 = 0.24), suggesting that eye-tracking video and head motion capture richer affective information than ECG/EDA/RSP in this setting. The deep learning models, trained with 5-fold CV on filtered continuous signals, appear underfit relative to the hand-crafted feature pipeline, which the authors attribute to limited data (43 subjects) and the need for larger-scale pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two well-established but previously separate lines of work: affective computing datasets (DEAP, MAHNOB-HCI, ASCERTAIN, AMIGOS) that rely on dedicated physiological sensors in controlled labs, and egocentric vision benchmarks (Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS, Ego-Exo4D) that model observable behavior while assuming affect-neutral participants. It repurposes the Project Aria smart glasses—previously used for activity recognition and gaze prediction—as a primary affective sensing platform, combining eye-tracking video, head-mounted PPG, and IMU data with traditional ECG/EDA/RSP sensors. The weighted Mikels' Wheel annotation scheme extends prior binary emotion tagging (MAHNOB-HCI, AMIGOS) to capture mixed-emotion intensity, and the inclusion of Big Five personality labels alongside affect follows ASCERTAIN but in a more ecologically valid, multi-task setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that hand-crafted features from egocentric eye-tracking video (pupil size, pixel intensity, Fisherface projections) outperform dedicated physiological sensors for both continuous affect and discrete emotion prediction, the paper challenges the field's default assumption that ECG/EDA are the gold-standard channels for affect detection. This opens a practical path toward affect-aware egocentric systems using only commercially available smart glasses, without additional wearable sensors. Key open questions include whether deep learning with proper pretraining can close the gap with classical methods (the current DL baselines underperform, likely due to small dataset size and borrowed architectures), whether the findings generalize beyond young adult participants, and how the system performs in truly uncontrolled, long-duration real-world settings rather than the ~105-minute lab protocol used here.
