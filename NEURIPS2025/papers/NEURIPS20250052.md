## Problem

Finite-time generalization of unregularized gradient descent in multiclass separable classification was largely unexplored, with prior risk bounds existing only for the binary case where performance depends solely on the loss decay rate. This paper asks whether the same decay-rate-only characterization extends to k-class settings and reveals that it does not: the p-norm geometry of the loss template fundamentally changes how risk scales with the number of classes.

## Input / Output

The method operates on i.i.d. labeled pairs (x, y) with x ∈ R^d, ||x||_2 ≤ 1, and y ∈ [k], under a separability assumption with margin γ and a separating matrix W* of Frobenius norm ≤ 1. GD is initialized at W_1 = 0 with a geometry-dependent step size η = 1/(6k^{2/p}β) for T iterations, and the output is a linear classifier W_T ∈ R^{k×d} accompanied by a high-probability population risk bound of the form Õ(βk^{2/p}ρ^{-1}(ε/k)² / (γ² min{T,n})), where ρ is the tail function characterizing the loss decay.

## Method

The analysis rests on two new technical pillars. First, a Rademacher complexity bound for vector-valued linear predictors whose loss templates are β-smooth with respect to the L_p norm (2 ≤ p ≤ ∞), yielding a k^{1/p} factor in the complexity and k^{2/p} in the generalization gap—improving as p grows. Second, a smoothness transfer lemma showing that the empirical risk is 3βk^{2/p}-smooth in the Frobenius norm of W, so the optimal GD step size scales as 1/k^{2/p}, giving faster optimization convergence for larger p. Combining these yields the upper bound in Theorem 1. To establish tightness, the paper constructs hard instances where the loss decomposes into k independent binary sub-problems (via a sum-of-univariate-φ template), proving a matching Ω(βk·ρ^{-1}(ε/k)²/(γ² min{T,n})) lower bound for p = 2, demonstrating that linear k-dependence is unavoidable in the Euclidean-smooth regime while L_∞-smooth losses like cross-entropy achieve only logarithmic k-dependence.

## Results

The paper establishes that for multiclass separable classification, the risk of unregularized GD depends crucially on the L_p-smoothness geometry of the loss template, not just its decay rate. For exponentially-tailed losses, the upper bound scales as Õ(k^(2/p)/(γ² min{T,n})) for general p, yielding Õ(1/(γ² min{T,n})) for p=∞ (cross-entropy) but Õ(k/(γ² min{T,n})) for p=2. A matching lower bound of Ω̃(k/(γ² min{T,n})) for p=2 proves the linear dependence on the number of classes is unavoidable in the Euclidean-smooth regime, establishing a strict separation from the p=∞ case where the risk is independent of k up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between loss templates smooth with respect to different L_p norms, revealing that geometry matters as much as decay rate. Cross-entropy (p=∞ smooth) achieves a risk bound of Õ(1/(γ² min{T,n})), matching the binary-case bound of Schliserman and Koren [19] up to logarithmic factors and showing no polynomial dependence on k. In contrast, L_2-smooth losses (p=2) suffer an unavoidable linear dependence on k, as proven by the tight lower bound in Theorem 3. This separation contrasts with the asymptotic implicit-bias results of Ravi et al. [15], which suggest all exponentially-tailed losses behave similarly in the limit; the paper shows that finite-time generalization distinguishes them sharply. For polynomially-tailed losses (ρ(x)=x^{-α}), the upper bound becomes Õ(k^(2/(α+2))(1+α/p)/(γ²T)^(α/(2+α))), again improving as p increases. The Rademacher complexity bound (Lemma 1) also improves from O(k) for p=2 (recovering Li et al. [7]) to O(1) for p=∞ (recovering Reeve and Kaban [16]), unifying prior results under a single template-based framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the binary-case risk bounds of Schliserman and Koren [19] to the multiclass setting, using the loss template framework of Wang and Scott [24] to characterize multiclass losses. The Rademacher complexity analysis adapts the covering-number techniques of Lei et al. [6] and Reeve and Kaban [16], but crucially shifts the smoothness assumption from the full loss function to its template. The key departure from prior work is that while Ravi et al. [15] showed all exponentially tailed losses converge to the same max-margin solution asymptotically, this paper reveals that finite-time generalization bounds depend on the p-norm geometry of the template, not merely its decay rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-time population risk bound for unregularized GD in multiclass separable classification, resolving open problems posed by Ravi et al. [15]. The finding that L∞-smooth templates (e.g., cross-entropy) yield logarithmic dependence on k while L2-smooth templates incur unavoidable linear dependence provides a theoretical explanation for why cross-entropy is preferred in large-scale classification. Limitations are significant: the analysis is restricted to linear predictors on separable data with single labels, so direct applicability to deep networks or noisy settings is unclear. Future work on extending these geometric insights to nonlinear and nonseparable regimes, particularly classifier-head fine-tuning, is a natural next step.
