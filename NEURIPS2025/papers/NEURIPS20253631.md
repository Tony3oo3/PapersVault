## Problem

Spectral features (eigenvectors, eigenvalues) are widely used to enhance GNN expressivity, but prior analyses via the WL hierarchy and homomorphism counting align poorly with graph spectra and yield limited insight into what spectral information actually drives distinguishability. This paper shows that EPNN—which generalizes many popular spectral GNNs including Random Walk, resistance, and shortest-path distance encodings—is incomplete even on graphs with pairwise distinct eigenvalues (simple spectrum), exposing a fundamental limitation of current spectral invariant architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the eigendecomposition of a graph matrix (adjacency or Laplacian): a matrix V of K eigenvectors (n×K) and a vector of K distinct eigenvalues, plus optional node features. The core challenge is that eigenvectors carry a sign ambiguity (each column can be negated) and a permutation ambiguity (node ordering), so the model must produce outputs invariant under S_n × {−1,1}^K. Outputs include a graph-level scalar or class prediction, canonicalized eigenvectors that resolve the sign ambiguity, and unique per-node identifiers that break symmetries without violating equivariance.

## Method

The paper introduces equiEPNN, which augments EPNN's invariant message-passing with a parallel sign-equivariant feature update, inspired by rotation-equivariant point cloud networks. At each layer, the invariant feature h_i is updated using elementwise products of equivariant features (v_i ⊙ v_j) as edge messages, while the equivariant feature v_i is updated by adding a weighted sum of neighbor equivariant features, with weights computed from invariant features. This dual update is proven to be strictly more expressive than EPNN: it separates the paper's 12-node counterexample in just 2 iterations, whereas EPNN cannot separate it at any depth. The paper further establishes completeness conditions for EPNN on simple spectrum graphs with sparse eigenvectors (at least one zero-free row), and proves that equiEPNN achieves perfect eigenvector canonicalization on ZINC by producing equivariant outputs that avoid the self-symmetry obstruction.

## Results

On ZINC (graph property regression, MAE↓), equiEPNN achieves 0.2805±0.019 with a ~30K parameter budget, outperforming GNNML1 (0.3140) and ChebNet (0.3569) among spectral methods, though trailing PPGN (0.1589). On MNIST-Superpixel (image classification, Acc↑), equiEPNN reaches 90.32%±0.7, surpassing GNNML1 (84.21%) and slightly edging out PPGN (90.04%), while ChebNet leads at 92.08%. With a larger ~500K parameter budget on ZINC, equiEPNN achieves 0.099±0.001 MAE, the best among spectral methods and beating EPNN (0.103±0.006). Additionally, equiEPNN achieves perfect eigenvector canonicalization on ZINC, reducing uncanonicalizable eigenvectors from 10.93% to 0.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPGN (Maron et al., NeurIPS 2019), equiEPNN loses on ZINC (0.2805 vs. 0.1589 MAE) but slightly wins on MNIST-Superpixel (90.32% vs. 90.04% accuracy), while PPGN has cubic complexity. Against EPNN (Zhang et al., ICML 2024), equiEPNN achieves a clear win on ZINC with the larger parameter budget (0.099 vs. 0.103 MAE), confirming the theoretical result that equiEPNN is strictly more expressive than EPNN. Among spectral methods specifically, equiEPNN is the best performer on ZINC in both parameter regimes, beating GNNML1 by ~3.4 MAE points (0.2805 vs. 0.3140) and ChebNet by ~7.6 points (0.2805 vs. 0.3569) at the smaller budget. On MNIST-Superpixel, equiEPNN outperforms GNNML1 by 6.1% accuracy but trails ChebNet by 1.8%, which the authors attribute to ChebNet's handcrafted polynomial features. The paper also proves that popular distance-based spectral GNNs (Graphormer-GD, PRD-WL, DiffWire, Random-Walk GNNs) are all incomplete on simple spectrum graphs, placing them at or below EPNN's expressivity ceiling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EPNN architecture (Zhang et al., ICML 2024) by importing the parallel equivariant+invariant update mechanism from geometric point cloud networks (Joshi et al., ICML 2023; Sverdlov & Dym, ICLR 2025), adapting rotation equivariance to the sign-equivariant graph spectrum setting. It departs from prior expressivity analyses of spectral GNNs, which relied on WL-test hierarchies and homomorphism counting, by introducing a new framework based on maximal eigenvalue multiplicity that is more naturally aligned with the spectral structure these networks exploit. The paper also builds on the canonicalization literature (Ma et al., NeurIPS 2023/2024) to demonstrate a practical application of the equivariant features for resolving eigenvector sign ambiguity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that EPNN and by extension many popular distance-based spectral GNNs (Graphormer-GD, PRD-WL, DiffWire, Random-Walk GNNs) are incomplete even on graphs with distinct eigenvalues—challenges the implicit assumption that the simplest spectral case is the easiest for spectral methods. The eigenvalue multiplicity hierarchy provides a more natural and actionable lens for understanding SGNN expressivity than WL-based comparisons. However, equiEPNN itself remains incomplete (Theorem 4), leaving open the fundamental question of whether any spectral GNN can achieve completeness on simple spectrum graphs, and the practical gains over EPNN are modest (e.g., 0.099 vs 0.103 MAE on ZINC).
