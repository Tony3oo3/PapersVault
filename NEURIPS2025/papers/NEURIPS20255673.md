## Problem

While Mamba's linear-complexity SSM module is well understood, the specific architectural reasons for its limitations relative to Transformers—particularly in handling symmetrical patterns and reversed sequences—remained unexplored. This paper asks whether the nonlinear convolution preceding the SSM, rather than the SSM itself, is the source of Mamba's inability to recognize symmetric relationships and match reversed token orders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255673/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Both tasks use short synthetic token sequences: the composite function task uses fixed-length-8 sequences containing two anchor tokens and a key token, with the model predicting the final-token label (the function output); the inverse sequence matching task uses variable-length sequences (e.g., 29 tokens for a 2-layer model) built from five permutations of three numbers plus a reversed query, with the model predicting the position index of the matching unreversed sequence. Loss is computed exclusively on the final token, and the inverse task inserts separator tokens equal to Mamba's pure convolutional receptive field to prevent convolution-based shortcuts.

## Method

The paper employs two synthetic diagnostic tasks—composite function and inverse sequence matching—combined with mechanistic interpretability tools (information flow visualization, causal intervention by zeroing SSM matrix entries, and information substitution) to isolate the role of Mamba's nonlinear convolution. The key finding is that Mamba's Conv1d+SiLU layer fuses token representations asymmetrically because its kernel weights remain non-uniform and near-orthogonal throughout training, so reversed sequences produce different fused inputs to the SSM. Ablations confirm the diagnosis: replacing the convolution with an all-ones kernel, or adding a residual connection from the pre-convolution input to the SSM together with positional encoding, restores Mamba's ability to learn symmetric solutions and match reversed sequences. The paper further shows that inserting the same nonlinear convolution into a Transformer reproduces the asymmetry bias, confirming convolution as the causal factor rather than the SSM.

## Results

On the inverse sequence matching task, standard Mamba achieves 100% training accuracy but degrades to random-guess level test accuracy (both in-distribution and OOD), while the Transformer and the modified Mamba (with residual connection + positional encoding) both achieve high test accuracy. On the composite function task, Mamba with small initialization (large γ) consistently biases toward the composite solution across all depths, failing to learn the symmetric solution; removing convolution asymmetry (all-one kernel) or adding positional encoding shifts Mamba toward the symmetric solution. The modified Mamba substantially surpasses both standard Mamba and the Transformer on the inverse sequence matching task, including on OOD generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three architectures on the inverse sequence matching task: standard Mamba, a matched Transformer, and a modified Mamba with a residual connection bypassing the nonlinear convolution plus positional encoding. Standard Mamba fails catastastically on test (random-guess accuracy despite 100% training accuracy), confirming that its nonlinear convolution prevents it from matching reversed sequences. The Transformer handles the task with ease, as expected from its symmetric attention mechanism. The modified Mamba not only recovers the Transformer-level performance but substantially surpasses it, particularly on OOD generalization, demonstrating that the SSM module itself is capable of the task once the asymmetric convolution bottleneck is removed. On the composite function task, adding nonlinear convolution to a Transformer reproduces Mamba's asymmetry bias (the Transformer either fails to generalize or fits by the symmetric solution rather than the composite one), confirming that convolution—not the SSM—is the root cause of the asymmetry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the composite function task framework from Zhang et al. (2024b), which was previously applied only to Transformers, to the Mamba architecture, and introduces a novel inverse sequence matching task to probe symmetry sensitivity. It draws on interpretability techniques from the LLM mechanistic interpretability literature (causal intervention, information flow analysis) to diagnose which internal Mamba components are actually responsible for information propagation. The work positions itself within the growing body of literature identifying Mamba's limitations (retrieval, in-context learning, long-context) but offers a structural, component-level explanation rooted in the nonlinear convolution rather than the SSM itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By isolating the nonlinear convolution as the source of Mamba's asymmetry bias and showing that a simple residual connection with positional encoding restores symmetry-matching ability, the paper provides a concrete architectural prescription for future SSM designs. However, all experiments use small models (32–128 hidden dimensions) on synthetic data, leaving open whether the observed bias persists or is mitigated at the scale of production Mamba models, and whether hybrid Mamba-Transformer architectures inherit or dilute this limitation. The finding that the SSM module is effectively bypassed in favor of convolution for information retrieval also raises questions about the actual role of the SSM in Mamba's success on natural language tasks.
