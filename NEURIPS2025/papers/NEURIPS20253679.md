## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for diffusion models calibrate quantization parameters independently at each denoising step, ignoring the fact that quantization errors accumulate and propagate through the sequential sampling process. The paper shows analytically and empirically that this accumulated error grows drastically over steps while the per-step error remains relatively constant, making it the dominant source of quality degradation—especially at low bit-widths.

## Input / Output

AccuQuant takes a pre-trained full-precision diffusion model (DDIM on CIFAR-10 at 32×32, LDM on LSUN/ImageNet at 256×256, or Stable Diffusion v1.4 on MS-COCO at 512×512) along with a small calibration set (256 samples per group) and produces a quantized model with reduced bit-widths for both weights and activations (e.g., 3/6, 4/8, 6/6 bits). The calibration process operates on grouped denoising steps (typically 5–25 steps per group), and the resulting quantized model generates images of the same resolution as the full-precision counterpart during inference.

## Method

AccuQuant groups M consecutive denoising steps and minimizes the MSE between the full-precision model's output and the quantized model's output over the entire group, rather than calibrating each step independently. This explicitly accounts for accumulated quantization errors within the group. To make this tractable, the paper derives a gradient approximation showing that the scalar noise-scheduling coefficient term dominates the Jacobian term in the inter-step gradient, reducing memory complexity from O(M) to O(1). A novel loss function with stop-gradient operators (Eq. 18) is designed so that its gradient matches the approximated expression, enabling stable calibration without storing intermediate activations. The calibration proceeds sequentially across groups, using the full-precision model's output at each group boundary as the starting point, and only the quantization step-sizes are optimized (no weight retraining).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (DDIM, 100 steps), AccuQuant achieves FID2FP32 of 3.30 at 6/6 bits, 1.15 at 4/8 bits, and 6.69 at 3/6 bits, substantially outperforming Q-Diffusion (35.24, 3.26, 42.74) and TFMQ-DM (3.64, 1.52, 27.11) on the FID2FP32 metric. On ImageNet (LDM-4, 20 steps), AccuQuant reaches FID2FP32 of 0.65 at 4/8 bits and 6.31 at 3/6 bits, beating PTQD (0.84, 13.85) and TFMQ-DM (1.25, 10.06). On MS-COCO text-to-image (SD v1.4, 50 steps), AccuQuant achieves FID2FP32 of 10.99 at 4/8 bits, outperforming PCR (14.25 at 4/8.4 mixed-precision) and Q-Diffusion (20.42 at 4/8), while also attaining the highest CLIP score (26.85 vs. 26.48 for PCR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AccuQuant consistently outperforms all six baselines on FID2FP32, the metric measuring fidelity to the full-precision model. Against Q-Diffusion [25], the gains are most dramatic at low bit-widths: at 3/6 bits on CIFAR-10, FID2FP32 drops from 42.74 to 6.69, and on LSUN-Churches from 42.27 to 4.84 at 4/6 bits. Against TFMQ-DM [19], AccuQuant achieves lower FID2FP32 across all settings (e.g., 1.15 vs. 1.52 at 4/8 on CIFAR-10; 0.65 vs. 1.25 at 4/8 on ImageNet) while also improving FID. Against TAC [54], which uses per-step correction parameters, AccuQuant matches or beats FID (e.g., 4.75 vs. 4.89 at 4/8 on CIFAR-10) and reports FID2FP32 values that TAC does not provide. Against PTQD [14] on ImageNet, AccuQuant wins on FID2FP32 (0.65 vs. 0.84 at 4/8; 6.31 vs. 13.85 at 3/6), LPIPS, PSNR, and SSIM. Against PCR [50] on MS-COCO, AccuQuant achieves FID2FP32 of 10.99 at 4/8 bits, beating PCR's 14.25 at 4/8.4 mixed-precision bits, while also attaining the highest CLIP score (26.85 vs. 26.48), demonstrating that fewer bits with multi-step simulation outperforms more bits with single-step calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the line of post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for diffusion models, which itself extends general PTQ techniques (BRECQ, PD-Quant) to the sequential denoising setting. Prior diffusion-specific PTQ methods (Q-Diffusion, TFMQ-DM, PTQ4DM) calibrate each denoising step independently, while error-correction approaches (PTQD, TAC) add per-step correction parameters, and PCR uses a single-step progressive scheme. AccuQuant departs from all of these by explicitly simulating multiple consecutive denoising steps with separate inputs for the full-precision and quantized models, and introduces a gradient approximation that reduces memory from O(n) to O(1), making multi-step calibration practical for large models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The error accumulation problem is fundamental to all multi-step diffusion sampling, and AccuQuant's FID2FP32 improvements (e.g., 35→3 on CIFAR-10 at 6/6 bits) demonstrate that prior single-step calibration leaves substantial room for improvement. The gradient approximation is a practical contribution that makes the method scalable to large models on a single GPU. However, the method's efficacy is limited for few-step (1–2 step) diffusion models, and the group-size hyperparameter currently requires manual tuning; adaptive group sizing and extension to distilled/few-step samplers remain open directions.
