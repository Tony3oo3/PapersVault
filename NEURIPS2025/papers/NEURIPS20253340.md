## Problem

Fitting and interpreting low-rank RNNs that implement target dynamical systems remains an open problem: existing training methods (FORCE, backpropagation) require large networks, multiple epochs, and produce weights that are difficult to interpret. No principled approach existed for determining the minimal network size needed for a given ODE, for choosing the distribution of nonlinear basis functions, or for selecting the most informative training points in state space.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a closed-form target ODE g(z) (offline setting) or teacher trajectories z_t generated by simulating the ODE (online setting), along with an input signal u that drives the network. Outputs include the learned linear weight vector n over a random nonlinear basis, the minimal number of neurons d' identified via OMP, optimized GP kernel hyperparameters (σ_m², σ_b², σ_mb) for principled initialization, and in the active-learning setting, a sequence of maximally informative (z, v) query points. The network state is constrained to evolve in an r + d_in dimensional subspace, with the output z identified with the latent recurrent activity κ.

## Method

The core insight reframes low-rank RNN training as least-squares regression over random nonlinear basis functions (shifted, scaled tanh or ReLU units), formalizing the connection to the Neural Engineering Framework. Offline, the weight vector n is solved in closed form via ridge regression on a grid of z values; online, a recursive least-squares (RLS) update tracks target trajectories with O(d) per-step cost, outperforming FORCE and BPTT in both accuracy and convergence speed. For minimal networks, a variant of orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP) greedily selects the smallest subset of basis functions, with a continuous extension (COMP) that refines each selected basis's (m, b) parameters via gradient descent. In the dual (kernel) space, the infinite-width limit of the RNN converges to a Gaussian Process with an arcsin kernel parameterized by the basis distribution; maximizing the GP marginal likelihood over these hyperparameters yields data-adaptive initializations that improve finite-network training. Finally, an active learning rule selects the next measurement point by maximizing the predictive variance (top eigenvector of the posterior covariance), reducing the number of samples needed to fit the dynamics.

## Results

On the binary decision-making task, the proposed RLS method achieves lower test error than both full-rank and low-rank BP-trained networks at comparable sizes, while training in 0.069 s versus ~29 s (full-rank BP) or ~62 s (low-rank BP). On the sine-wave generation task, rank-2 networks trained with the proposed method consistently outperform FORCE-trained full-rank networks across all tested sizes, achieving lower MSE with fewer neurons. On the Lorenz attractor, the method produces accurate reconstructions at N=16–64 neurons, whereas FORCE fails to reliably capture the signal until N=1024. For the minimal-RNN problem, OMP recovers a near-perfect fit to a 1D ODE with just 5 neurons, and the continuous-OMP (COMP) variant achieves sharper MSE reductions with even fewer neurons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FORCE [9,10], the proposed RLS method achieves lower MSE with substantially fewer neurons on both the sine-wave task (rank-2 networks beat FORCE full-rank at all sizes) and the Lorenz attractor (accurate at N=16–64 vs. FORCE needing N=1024). Against backpropagation-through-time (BPTT) [12], the method achieves lower test error on the binary decision-making task at comparable network sizes and trains ~400–900× faster (0.069 s vs. 29–62 s). The paper also compares GP-optimized basis initialization against standard-normal initialization: in both BPTT and RLS training, GP-optimized bases yield lower training MSE, demonstrating that the dual-space (GP) formulation provides a principled initialization advantage. Overall, the proposed framework wins on all three comparisons (FORCE, BPTT, and initialization quality) in terms of accuracy-per-parameter and training efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the Neural Engineering Framework (NEF) with the low-rank RNN literature by showing that a low-rank RNN is equivalent to a single-hidden-layer neural ODE whose basis functions are random shifted/scaled nonlinearities. From this geometric view, the authors derive an online recursive least-squares (RLS) training rule that replaces the iterative FORCE or BPTT procedures, an OMP/COMP-based search for the minimal number of neurons needed to embed a target ODE, and a Gaussian Process equivalence for infinite-width low-rank RNNs that enables principled initialization via marginal-likelihood optimization of the basis distribution. The active learning component draws on classical Bayesian experimental design (MacKay 1992; Mania et al. 2022) but applies it to the kernel-space posterior of the RNN's linear readout weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a closed-form, interpretable alternative to the dominant BP/FORCE training paradigm for low-rank RNNs, with the practical consequence that networks as small as 5 neurons can faithfully embed bistable-attractor or limit-cycle dynamics that prior work typically required 512+ neurons to learn. The GP-based initialization (optimizing the distribution over basis slopes and offsets rather than fixing it to a standard normal) offers a principled, data-adaptive starting point that improves downstream gradient-based training even for finite networks. Key limitations include the teacher-training assumption (the target ODE must be known), the active-learning requirement of arbitrary latent-space sampling, and the GP analysis being restricted to erf/ReLU nonlinearities; open directions include inferring low-rank networks from noisy neural recordings and developing control-theoretic strategies for informative input selection.
