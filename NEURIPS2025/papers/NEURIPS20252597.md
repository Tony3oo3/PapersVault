## Problem

Neural operator surrogates for PDEs require large labeled datasets that are prohibitively expensive to obtain from high-fidelity simulations. While semi-supervised learning can exploit abundant unlabeled inputs, conventional pseudo-labeling and self-training methods are vulnerable to noisy pseudo-labels that mislead training, and existing meta pseudo-labeling approaches (e.g., MPL, Noisy TS) are either limited to classification or apply uniform, spatially inconsistent feedback that fails to generalize to high-dimensional PDE regression on nonuniform meshes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small labeled set and a much larger unlabeled set, where each input is a tuple of geometry (spatial coordinates on a mesh or point cloud), associated physical quantities, and physical parameters. The student model outputs the PDE solution field (steady-state or autoregressive transient) together with a point-wise heteroscedastic variance field, while the teacher additionally produces an epistemic uncertainty estimate via EMC Dropout. The framework is model-agnostic and operates on both mesh-based (MGN, AeroGTO) and point-based (DeepONet, Transolver) representations, requiring aligned output shapes between teacher and student.

## Method

UMPL employs a teacher-student architecture with asymmetric uncertainty roles: the teacher estimates epistemic uncertainty via EMC Dropout (an EMA-enhanced MC Dropout that incrementally updates predictive mean and variance without repeated stochastic forward passes) and generates pseudo-labels perturbed by a spatially coherent Gaussian-smoothed random field over the mesh connectivity. The student learns from these pseudo-labels using a heteroscedastic regression loss that down-weights high-uncertainty samples, while a dedicated uncertainty decoder predicts input-dependent aleatoric variance. The key mechanism is an uncertainty-informed feedback loop derived via a REINFORCE-style gradient: the student's performance change on labeled data after pseudo-label training (h) scales a negative log-likelihood term, causing the teacher to increase the likelihood of pseudo-labels that improve the student and suppress those that do not. Two stabilizing components—Progressive Uncertainty Filtering (masking top-k% most uncertain predictions early in training) and Supervised Anchoring (updating the student exclusively on labeled data each step)—prevent error accumulation and model collapse under distribution shift.

## Results

Under the 10% labeled data setting, UMPL achieves an average 14.18% improvement over the best baseline across all tasks. On steady-state benchmarks (Darcy Flow, Ahmed, NSM2D), UMPL yields a 12.13% average improvement over the second-best method (Noisy TS); on out-of-distribution scenarios (Stationary Lid, Black Sea), the gains reach 20.19% (spatial OOD) and 11.37% (temporal OOD). The largest single improvement is 24.39% (Transolver on Stationary Lid w/o OOD), and UMPL is the only method that converges on the Ahmed 3D benchmark where Pseudo Label and Mean Teacher fail entirely. Results are consistent across all four architectures (DeepONet, MGN, Transolver, AeroGTO) and seven task settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMPL outperforms all three baselines on every benchmark and every model architecture tested. Against Pseudo Label [31] (fixed teacher, no feedback), UMPL achieves 10–24% lower L2 error, with the largest gap on Ahmed (22.9% for AeroGTO) where Pseudo Label fails to converge. Against Mean Teacher [54] (EMA-updated teacher), UMPL improves by 10–24%, demonstrating that uncertainty-informed feedback is substantially more effective than simple parameter averaging for refining pseudo-label quality. Against Noisy TS [57] (the closest competitor, a meta pseudo-labeling method with scalar noise feedback), UMPL still wins by 10–24% on all tasks; the paper attributes this to Noisy TS's uniform scalar feedback being spatially incoherent, whereas UMPL's point-wise aleatoric-uncertainty feedback provides structured, location-aware guidance. Figure 2 further shows that UMPL's feedback loss correlates more tightly (higher Kendall-τ) with the true pseudo-label error than Noisy TS's, confirming more reliable teacher updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta pseudo-labeling paradigm (MPL, Pham et al. 2021) from classification to high-dimensional PDE regression, and draws on Sequential MC Dropout (Carreno et al. 2023) to design an efficient EMA-based epistemic uncertainty estimator (EMC Dropout) for the teacher. It departs from prior semi-supervised PDE work (e.g., Noisy TS, Wang et al. 2024) by replacing scalar noise injection with structured, point-wise aleatoric-uncertainty feedback, and by assigning distinct uncertainty roles (epistemic to teacher, heteroscedastic aleatoric to student) rather than treating both models symmetrically. The framework is explicitly model-agnostic, wrapping around existing neural operator architectures (DeepONet, Transolver, MGN, AeroGTO) rather than proposing a new backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in scientific computing: simulation-generated labels are expensive, yet unlabeled inputs are abundant, and conventional SSL methods introduce noise that is particularly harmful under the strict physical constraints of PDE systems. The consistent 10–24% improvements across four architectures and seven tasks (including OOD and distribution-shift settings) with only 10% labeled data make the framework immediately useful for practitioners who cannot afford full simulation campaigns. Key limitations remain: the i.i.d. assumption degrades under severe distribution shifts, EMC Dropout adds computational overhead, and the loss requires aligned output shapes (ruling out adaptive meshes). Open questions include whether the uncertainty-informed feedback mechanism scales to 3D flow fields and whether the Rademacher-based generalization bound (Theorem 3.2) can be tightened for the non-convex neural operator setting.
