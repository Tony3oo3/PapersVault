## Problem

Existing multi-frame scene flow estimation methods either concatenate voxel features along the channel dimension or stack them in an explicit temporal dimension, both of which cause feature size and computational cost to grow linearly with the number of input frames, making scalable multi-frame reasoning impractical. Additionally, prior supervised approaches do not explicitly address severe class imbalances (e.g., cars vs. pedestrians) or enforce motion consistency across points belonging to the same object instance.

## Input / Output

The method takes N consecutive LiDAR point clouds (typically 2–15 frames at 10 Hz) along with ego-vehicle odometry, which is used to compensate for sensor motion before the network predicts the residual per-point 3D flow vector. Point features are extracted via PointPillars and aggregated into sparse 3D voxels at 0.15 m (Argoverse 2) or 0.2 m (Waymo/nuScenes) resolution; the Δ scheme ensures the resulting feature tensor has a fixed spatial-channel shape (V×C) independent of N, so the backbone-decoder network processes a constant-size input regardless of how many past frames are supplied.

## Method

The core contribution is a temporal Δ scheme that computes voxel-wise differences between the current frame and each past frame, applies a geometric decay factor λ to weight more recent frames more heavily, and sums the weighted differences into a single sparse feature map of constant size. This Δ feature is fed into a MinkowskiNet sparse 3D backbone followed by a GRU-based per-point decoder (inherited from DeFlow) to produce the residual scene flow. Training uses a composite loss combining the DeFlow motion-awareness loss, a proposed Category-Balanced Loss that assigns higher weights to underrepresented classes (pedestrians, VRUs), and an Instance Consistency Loss that penalizes per-point flow variance within each moving object instance (speed > 0.4 m/s), thereby enforcing rigid-body motion coherence. The sparse Δ operation is implemented with CUDA memory-coalescing and bank-conflict-avoidance techniques, achieving up to 10× speedup and 100× memory reduction over dense equivalents.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 test set, ΔFlow (5 frames) achieves a mean Dynamic Bucket-Normalized EPE of 0.113 and a mean Three-way EPE of 2.11 cm, setting a new state-of-the-art among all methods. On Waymo, it reaches 1.64 cm mean Three-way EPE, and on nuScenes, 0.216 mean Dynamic Bucket-Normalized EPE. In terms of efficiency, ΔFlow runs in 8 s per sequence (Argoverse 2) versus 15 s for Flow4D (5 frames) and 24 h for EulerFlow, while maintaining a constant feature size regardless of the number of input frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ΔFlow outperforms the strongest multi-frame supervised competitor, Flow4D [18], by 22% in mean Dynamic Bucket-Normalized EPE (0.113 vs. 0.145) and 5.8% in mean Three-way EPE (2.11 vs. 2.24 cm) on Argoverse 2, while running 2× faster (8 s vs. 15 s per sequence). On Waymo, ΔFlow reduces mean Three-way EPE by 19% (1.64 vs. 2.03 cm) and is 45% faster than Flow4D; on nuScenes, it cuts mean bucket-normalized EPE by 39% (0.216 vs. 0.279). Against two-frame supervised methods, ΔFlow (2 frames) already matches or beats Flow4D (2 frames) on bucket-normalized EPE (0.145 vs. 0.174) and SSF (0.145 vs. 0.181), and substantially outperforms DeFlow (0.145 vs. 0.276) and FastFlow3D (0.145 vs. 0.532). Even the offline method EulerFlow [35], which processes all frames in a sequence over 24 hours, is surpassed by ΔFlow in mean bucket-normalized EPE (0.113 vs. 0.130) while being thousands of times faster. The largest per-category gains appear for pedestrians (PED: 0.149 vs. 0.216 for Flow4D-5F) and VRUs (0.096 vs. 0.127), attributed to the proposed Category-Balanced and Instance Consistency losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DeFlow's GRU-based voxel-to-point decoder and motion-awareness loss, while departing from the dominant multi-frame strategies of prior work. Existing approaches (FastFlow3D, DeFlow, SSF) concatenate voxel features along the channel dimension, and Flow4D introduces an explicit 4D temporal convolutional dimension; both cause feature size and parameter count to grow with the number of input frames. Δ Flow replaces these with a simple subtraction-based Δ scheme (frame differencing weighted by a temporal decay factor) that maintains a constant feature size regardless of frame count, and adds two new loss terms (Category-Balanced and Instance Consistency) that address class imbalance and per-object motion coherence beyond what DeFlow's speed-bucketed loss provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Δ scheme offers a practically important efficiency win: multi-frame scene flow estimation becomes scalable without the quadratic memory/speed degradation that plagues 4D or concatenation-based approaches, making long temporal context feasible for real-time autonomous driving. The strong cross-domain generalization (trained on one LiDAR configuration, tested on another) suggests the learned motion representations are sensor-agnostic, which is valuable for deploying a single model across vehicle fleets. The main limitation is the continued reliance on supervised ground-truth annotations; the authors identify self-supervised integration as a key future direction. An open question is why performance plateaus or slightly declines beyond ~10 frames, suggesting that very long temporal context introduces noise rather than signal for current motion prediction.
