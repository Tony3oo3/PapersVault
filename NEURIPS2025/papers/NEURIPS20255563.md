## Problem

Decision trees suffer from non-differentiability (preventing gradient-based optimization) and lower test accuracy compared to ensemble methods. Existing differentiable tree approaches either use straight-through estimators that discard critical gradient information or introduce soft probabilistic routing that fundamentally alters the hard-split, deterministic nature of classic trees. The paper asks: can one make an oblique decision tree fully differentiable for end-to-end gradient optimization while preserving exact hard splits and unique deterministic decision paths?

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data with feature vectors x_i ∈ [0,1]^p and target values y_i (continuous for regression, discrete class labels for classification). It produces a single oblique decision tree of user-specified depth D whose leaf predictions are either constant values or linear combinations of input features (k_t^T x_i + h_t). At inference, each sample follows a unique deterministic path to exactly one leaf via hard ReLU-based branching tests, yielding a single point prediction rather than a probability distribution over leaves.

## Method

The core reformulation replaces each branch node's binary split with a ReLU-based violation measure: v_{i,j} = ReLU(a_j^T x_i − b_j) for left-ancestors and ReLU(b_j − a_j^T x_i) for right-ancestors, producing exact zero (correct direction) or positive (violation) values. Cumulative violations U_{i,t} across all ancestor nodes identify the unique zero-violation path, and a one-hot Argmin over {U_{i,t}} deterministically assigns each sample to its leaf. To enable gradient backpropagation, Argmin is approximated during training by a scaled softmin S(U_{i,t}) = exp(−αU_{i,t}) / Σ exp(−αU_{i,t}), while inference reverts to exact Argmin. A multi-run warm-start annealing strategy progressively increases α across successive optimization runs (log-spaced from α_min to α_max), warm-starting each run with the previous solution to balance approximation fidelity against numerical stability. The entire tree (all split weights A, thresholds b, and leaf parameters θ) is optimized simultaneously via gradient descent in PyTorch, with multiple random initializations and margin-based initialization adjustments to avoid near-zero ReLU outputs that destabilize softmin.

## Results

On 17 medium-scale regression datasets, RADDT achieves 82.39% R² test accuracy, outperforming CART (74.85%) by 7.54%, ORT-LS (78.67%) by 3.72%, and HHCART (76.75%) by 5.64%. With linear leaf predictions, RADDT-Linear reaches 84.63% R², surpassing Random Forest (82.62%) by 2.01%, XGBoost (83.51%) by 1.12%, and TEL (83.87%) by 0.76%. RADDT also scales to 12-depth trees on million-sample datasets where GradTree, SoftDT, DGT, and LatentTree all fail, achieving a 432× speedup over ORT-LS at depth 12 on the ailerons dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RADDT outperforms all greedy oblique tree baselines: HHCART by 5.64%, OC1 by 9.08%, and RandCART by 11.19% in R², while also beating the foundational orthogonal CART by 7.54%. Against gradient-based trees, RADDT surpasses GradTree by 18.09% and SoftDT by 9.67%; it also outperforms DGT by 2.66% and LatentTree by 6.24% in test accuracy (evaluated at fixed depths where those methods are feasible). Against the local search method ORT-LS, RADDT leads by 3.72% in test accuracy while using a shallower average depth (7.29 vs. 6.24). In same-dataset comparisons on baselines' original data, RADDT beats GradTree by 4.99% on 27 classification datasets, soft trees by 6.88% on 4 regression datasets, and outperforms DGT-Linear/TAO-Linear/DTSemNet on 4 of 5 shared regression datasets. Against tree ensembles, RADDT-Linear (84.63%) exceeds RF (82.62%), XGBoost (83.51%), and TEL (83.87%), though the constant-prediction RADDT (82.39%) is slightly below them. On million-scale datasets at depth 12, RADDT is the only method that successfully trains, outperforming CART by 3.15% in test R² while being 42× faster than DGT at depth 4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two main lines of prior work on oblique decision trees: (1) MIP-based optimal tree formulations (Bertsimas & Dunn 2017, ORT-LS) that suffer from scalability, and (2) gradient-based trees that introduce softness via sigmoid/smooth-step approximations (SoftDT, TEL) or straight-through estimators (DGT, GradTree, DTSemNet). The key conceptual departure is using ReLU's exact zero/non-zero output to enforce hard splits and Argmin to select a unique decision path, then approximating only the Argmin with a scaled softmin for gradient flow—preserving deterministic branching and prediction rather than probabilistic routing. The ReLU-as-decision-boundary idea echoes Lee & Jaakkola (2020), but here it is applied directly to reformulate and optimize a tree rather than to implicitly mimic one within a neural network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for applications requiring interpretable, differentiable, and scalable tree models (e.g., reinforcement learning policies, explicit model predictive control). The finding that a single oblique tree with linear predictions (RADDT-Linear) outperforms XGBoost and Random Forest is notable, though the authors rightly caution that comparing a single tree to ensembles is inherently asymmetric. Key open questions include: the lack of theoretical guarantees for the warm-start annealing strategy, the overfitting gap at depth 12 (training +5.43% but testing −4.78%), and the unresolved corner case where a_j=0 and b_j=0 simultaneously. Future work on dedicated regularization (e.g., hierarchical shrinkage) and extending the optimization strategies to other gradient-based trees like SoftDT could close these gaps.
