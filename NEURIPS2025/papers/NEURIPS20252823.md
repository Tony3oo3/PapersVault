## Problem

Existing pattern recognition benchmarks for LLMs (ARC, WILT, DEER) are limited to few-shot settings (<10 examples), while many-shot ICL benchmarks focus almost exclusively on classification tasks. No benchmark evaluates whether LLMs can aggregate hundreds to thousands of diverse input-output examples to recognize complicated underlying functions — a critical capability for long-context reasoning and future generalist agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252823/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The LLM receives a concatenated prompt containing 4 to 2048 input-output example pairs (shots) generated by an unknown function, followed by a new target input. Shots have highly diverse I/O formats (str→str, int→int, list[float]→list[float], int,int→int, etc.) and are drawn from 20,000 generated pairs per function. The model must output the predicted result for the target input; answers are extracted via rule-based regex and scored with exact match. Test cases are designed to be slightly harder (larger numbers, longer lists) than the shots.

## Method

The benchmark is built via a four-stage pipeline: (1) collect introductory-level solution functions from HumanEval+, MBPP+, and APPS; (2) prompt GPT-4o-0806 to write Python data generators for each function, then execute them to produce 20,000 input shots and 10 test cases; (3) run the ground-truth function on generated inputs to obtain outputs; (4) stitch prompts and filter out unsolvable or low-diversity problems. From the resulting MIR-Extended (693 functions × 10 test cases = 6,930 problems), MIR-Core (300 problems) is selected using a metric D that quantifies performance growth from few-shot to many-shot, with LLM-labeled difficulty as the dominant predictive factor. The paper then evaluates 21 LLMs across 4–2048 shots and conducts ablations on inductive vs. transductive reasoning, robustness to erroneous shots, RAG-based shot selection, the SolverLearner coding paradigm, and out-of-domain meta-shots.

## Results

On MIR-Extended (6930 problems), the best model o1-mini-0912 achieves <0.7 accuracy, while GPT-4o-0806 reaches <0.4; no model saturates. On MIR-Core (3000 problems), the gap between few-shot and many-shot is more pronounced, but all models still saturate around 256 shots due to attention dispersion. A key finding is that transductive reasoning (direct answer without CoT) unanimously outperforms inductive reasoning (with CoT) across all 21 evaluated models — e.g., o1-preview-0912 scores 0.797 without CoT vs. 0.588 with CoT, and DeepSeek-R1 scores 0.757 vs. 0.298. LLMs are also robust to erroneous shots, maintaining decent accuracy even at 3/4 error rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline comparison is against SolverLearner (Cheng et al., 2024), which proposes a 'first-code-then-execute' paradigm for inductive reasoning. On MIR-Core, SolverLearner does NOT consistently outperform standard in-context reasoning: gains are marginal and model-dependent (e.g., +0.022 for DeepSeek-R1 at 16-shot, but -0.067 for Gemini-1.5 Pro at 256-shot), and the performance curves are flatter across shot counts, indicating poor utilization of additional examples. In the broader model comparison, o1-mini-0912 and o1-preview-0912 clearly lead all other models on both MIR-Extended and MIR-Core, followed by Claude-3.5-Sonnet and GPT-4o-0806; however, even the best model stays below 0.7 accuracy, confirming the benchmark's difficulty. Notably, embedding-based RAG (selecting 64 nearest shots via all-MiniLM-L6-v2) shows no significant improvement over random shot selection, and out-of-domain meta-shots provide only marginal gains that weaken with more in-distribution shots.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two communities that have not previously overlapped: pattern recognition / inductive reasoning benchmarks (ARC, WILT, DEER, MIRAGE), which are limited to few-shot (<10 examples), and many-shot ICL / long-context benchmarks (HELMET, LongICLBench, ManyICLBench, NIAH), which focus on classification or retrieval rather than complex rule induction. Rather than building on a single prior method, the authors repurpose introductory-level coding functions from HumanEval+, MBPP+, and APPS as underlying patterns, using GPT-4o to auto-generate 20,000 input-output shots per function — a pipeline that scales far beyond the manual or few-shot data construction used in prior pattern recognition work. The paper also engages with the SolverLearner 'code-then-execute' paradigm [10] and embedding-based RAG as potential solutions to the many-shot saturation problem, positioning its negative results against these intuitive approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIR-Bench fills a genuine evaluation gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously tests many-shot (up to 2048 examples) and complex, non-classification pattern recognition with diverse I/O modalities. The finding that transductive reasoning (direct answer, no CoT) unanimously outperforms inductive reasoning (forced CoT) across all 21 models challenges the prevailing assumption that CoT universally aids reasoning, and the demonstration that embedding-based RAG provides no benefit over random shot selection rules out an obvious engineering fix for the saturation problem. Many-shot in-context pattern recognition remains an open problem — even the strongest models (o1-1217, o3-mini) saturate around 256 shots — and the authors identify supervised fine-tuning or RL with ICL data as a promising but unexplored direction. Limitations include text-only evaluation, potential non-uniqueness of underlying functions given finite shots, and the heuristic (quadratic-fit) selection of MIR-Core problems.
