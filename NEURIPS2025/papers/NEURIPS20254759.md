## Problem

While TMLE's statistical guarantees (double robustness, semi-parametric efficiency) are well-established, its behavior as an iterative optimization procedure remains largely unexplored: convergence is typically assumed rather than proven, stopping criteria are applied inconsistently in practice, and no optimization-theoretic foundation exists to guide implementation choices like iteration limits or tuning parameters in finite-sample settings.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n i.i.d. observations, the canonical gradient (influence function) D*Ψ(p) of a d-dimensional target functional Ψ, and an initial density estimate p_n^0 (typically from a machine-learning procedure). At each iteration it produces an updated density along a parametric fluctuation submodel, and the final output is the plug-in estimate Ψ(p_n^*) evaluated at the converged density. Beyond the point estimate, the analysis yields geometric objects: a smooth homotopy path in the probability simplex per iteration, a solution submanifold of the estimating equation whose codimension equals d, and explicit probabilistic bounds on overshooting events.

## Method

The paper recasts TMLE's iterative updates as an explicit optimization scheme over the probability simplex and proves global convergence without requiring convexity of the submodel objective. The key geometric insight is that each fluctuation submodel (linear, exponential, or logistic reparameterization) defines an injective C¹ embedding of R^d into the simplex — a smooth, non-self-intersecting homotopy path — which precludes cycling and enables a telescoping argument: squared step sizes form a summable series via Lipschitz-in-path bounds, forcing ε_n^k → 0. The solution set of the estimating equation is shown to be a smooth submanifold of infinite dimension with codimension exactly d, using the infinite-dimensional implicit function theorem and surjectivity of the Fréchet derivative of the influence function. As a by-product, the analysis reveals an overshooting phenomenon where the loss-minimizing update can pass through a feasible root of the estimating equation without stopping, with probability bounded by an exponential in n that degrades with dimension d.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The main results are six theorems: (1) a necessary-and-sufficient characterization of TMLE stopping criteria (Theorem 1); (2) a non-self-intersection guarantee for homotopy paths in the probability simplex (Theorem 2); (3) a proof that the solution set of the estimating equation forms a smooth submanifold whose codimension equals the target parameter dimension (Theorem 3); (4) the first rigorous global convergence proof for TMLE iterates (Theorem 4); (5) explicit sufficient conditions for one-step termination (Theorem 5); and (6) an exponential-in-n probabilistic bound on the overshooting probability, P[overshoot] ≲ 2d·exp(−nλ_o²C_o²μ̃/(2B_o²)) (Theorem 6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper contains no empirical baselines or head-to-head comparisons, as it is a theoretical contribution. In terms of prior theoretical work, the paper positions itself against the classical TMLE literature (van der Laan & Rubin [5], van der Laan et al. [6]), which established asymptotic efficiency but assumed convergence without proof. Theorem 4 provides the first rigorous convergence guarantee, going beyond the existing Lemma 1 (convergence of empirical risk only). Theorem 5 unifies and extends scattered one-step convergence conditions previously found in [42, 43, 25, 44] into a single theorem with four distinct sufficient conditions. The non-self-intersection result (Theorem 2) is novel relative to EM-algorithm convergence analyses [22–24], which rely on convexity assumptions that TMLE's simplex-embedded homotopies do not satisfy. The overshooting phenomenon (Theorem 6) is identified as a new failure mode not previously characterized in the TMLE literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the TMLE framework of Van Der Laan & Rubin [5] and the foundational regularity conditions of van der Laan et al. [6], recasting the iterative debiasing procedure as an explicit optimization problem over the probability simplex. It extends the one-step convergence results scattered across Rosenblum & Van Der Laan [42], Rytgaard & Van Der Laan [44], and Díaz & Rosenblum [25] into a unified set of sufficient conditions (Theorem 5), while drawing conceptual parallels to EM algorithm convergence analysis [22, 23, 24] but highlighting the unique challenges posed by influence-function-driven fluctuations within simplex-embedded homotopies. The paper explicitly departs from prior work by providing the first rigorous global convergence proof for TMLE as an iterative scheme, rather than treating convergence as an implementation detail or assuming it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a longstanding theoretical gap: TMLE's asymptotic guarantees (double robustness, semi-parametric efficiency) have been well-established, but the finite-sample convergence behavior of its iterative updates was assumed rather than proven, leaving practitioners without principled guidance on stopping criteria, iteration limits, or tuning parameters. The discovery of the overshooting phenomenon (Theorem 6) is practically significant, as it reveals that standard TMLE implementations can silently bypass valid solutions, motivating new safeguard strategies like step-size control or root tracking. Open questions include how approximate subproblem solutions (as in numerical practice) affect convergence guarantees, whether the framework extends to higher-order TMLE variants, and whether the geometric insights (non-self-intersection, submanifold structure) can be leveraged to design accelerated or regularized debiasing algorithms.
