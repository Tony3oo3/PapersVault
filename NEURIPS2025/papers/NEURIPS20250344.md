## Problem

Protein structure prediction models like AlphaFold3 and Boltz-1 are trained to map a sequence to a single most-likely structure, failing to capture the conformational diversity of dynamic biomolecular complexes. While cryo-EM can image this heterogeneity, it only outputs density maps rather than atomic models, and existing model-building methods produce incomplete structures especially at lower resolutions (>4 Å). The paper bridges this gap by using experimental cryo-EM data to guide a pretrained diffusion model toward conformational states consistent with the observed density.

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein sequence (up to ~2,200 residues for Boltz-1 compatibility) and a cryo-EM density map (a 3D array representing electron scattering potential, tested at resolutions from 2 Å to 6.5 Å) as input, and produces 25 atomic coordinate samples per replicate across 3 model replicates. The density map is consumed in two forms: a low-resolution point cloud (via weighted k-means) for global guidance and the full-resolution voxel grid for local guidance. No retraining is required; the method operates entirely at inference time on top of the pretrained Boltz-1 score model.

## Method

CryoBoltz applies a multiscale guidance mechanism to the reverse diffusion process of Boltz-1, structured as four sequential phases: an unguided warm-up (T_w steps), global guidance (T_g steps), local guidance (T_l steps), and an unguided relaxation (T_r steps). During global guidance, the density map is converted to a point cloud via weighted k-means clustering, and the guidance term is the negative gradient of the Sinkhorn divergence (entropy-regularized Wasserstein distance) between the Tweedie-estimated clean sample and the point cloud, pulling the structure toward the correct global shape. During local guidance, the guidance term is derived directly from the physics-based cryo-EM forward model (sum of Gaussian form factors convolved with B-factor blurring plus Gaussian noise), fitting higher-resolution details. This global-to-local strategy regularizes the rugged, multimodal posterior in early diffusion steps before introducing high-frequency constraints, and the entire procedure requires no additional training.

## Results

On real cryo-EM maps of four dynamic biomolecular systems (P-glycoprotein, Pma1, CYP102A1, YbbAP), CryoBoltz achieves all-atom RMSD of 1.31–4.17 Å and TM scores of 0.957–0.997 across 10 conformational states, substantially outperforming unguided Boltz-1 (RMSD 2.93–8.78 Å) and AlphaFold3 (RMSD 2.69–8.02 Å). On synthetic 2 Å maps of STP10, CryoBoltz reaches sub-1 Å all-atom RMSD (0.89–1.06 Å) for both inward and outward conformations, while unguided Boltz-1 only samples the outward state (RMSD 2.66–3.82 Å) and AlphaFold3 only the inward state. ModelAngelo, the state-of-the-art model-building method, produces highly incomplete models (2.3%–91.5% residue coverage) with TM scores as low as 0.010 on P-glycoprotein maps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CryoBoltz outperforms unguided Boltz-1 on every metric across all 10 experimental conformational states, with the largest gains on P-glycoprotein (e.g., apo state RMSD drops from 6.99 Å to 1.38 Å) and CYP102A1 closed state (8.78 Å to 2.00 Å). Against AlphaFold3, CryoBoltz also wins on all systems, though the margin is smaller on P-glycoprotein inward state (1.35 vs. 2.69 Å RMSD) where AF3 is relatively competitive. Critically, CryoBoltz recovers conformational states that neither unguided baseline can sample at all: Boltz-1 only predicts the outward STP10 conformation and only the active Pma1/bound YbbAP states, while AlphaFold3 only predicts the inward STP10 state. ModelAngelo, the dedicated model-building method, is dramatically outperformed by CryoBoltz on TM score (e.g., 0.989 vs. 0.361 on Pgp apo) and produces severely incomplete models (as low as 2.3% residue coverage on Pgp occluded/collapsed states, and 0% on the 6.5 Å CYP open map). The ablation in Table 1 confirms that the multiscale (global + local) guidance strategy is necessary: global-only guidance achieves competitive TM scores but higher RMSD, while local-only guidance fails to drive large conformational changes (e.g., STP10 inward RMSD of 3.86 Å vs. 1.06 Å with full guidance).

## Relation to Prior Work

CryoBoltz builds on the Boltz-1/AlphaFold3 diffusion-based structure prediction framework and the DPS (Diffusion Posterior Sampling) guidance algorithm, combining them in a novel way: it uses cryo-EM density maps as the likelihood signal to steer the reverse diffusion process in atomic coordinate space. Conceptually, it departs from prior conformational-diversity approaches (MSA subsampling, MD-based training data) by requiring no retraining and no heuristic sequence manipulation, and from prior cryo-EM model-building methods (ModelAngelo, EMBuild) by leveraging a pretrained generative prior rather than de novo tracing. The multiscale global-to-local guidance strategy (Sinkhorn divergence on a point cloud, then physics-based density-map fitting) is a practical innovation addressing the multimodality of the cryo-EM forward model that simpler single-scale guidance cannot handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that experimental cryo-EM data can be integrated into structure prediction at inference time without any retraining, enabling recovery of conformational states that unguided models completely miss (e.g., inward vs. outward transporter states) and improving accuracy on functionally critical regions like antibody CDR loops. The practical impact is significant: it can replace hours of manual model building with minutes of automated guided sampling, particularly for low-resolution maps where existing tools like ModelAngelo produce highly incomplete models. Key limitations include dependence on the base model providing a reasonable initialization, optimization instability from the multimodal likelihood, and current restriction to small complexes (<2,200 residues). Open questions include principled validation of models from low-resolution maps, stabilizing guidance to avoid off-manifold drift, and extending to larger multi-state systems with shared deformation models.
