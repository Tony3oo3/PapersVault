## Problem

Existing autoregressive multimodal pipelines train vision tokenizers separately for low-level pixel reconstruction and then freeze them during downstream training, using only discrete codebook indices. This decoupled paradigm creates a representation bottleneck: the tokenizer is agnostic to downstream tasks requiring varied semantics (e.g., text recognition in images), and the discrete indices block gradient propagation, making joint optimization infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251589/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes RGB images (processed at 512×512 for downstream tasks; 256×256 for tokenizer pre-training) and text prompts as input, producing either text captions/answers for multimodal understanding or 512×512 generated images for text-to-image synthesis. The IBQ-based tokenizer encodes images into a spatial grid of quantized embeddings (downsampling factor s=16, codebook size 131,072, code dimension D=256), which are projected into the LLM's hidden space via a lightweight MLP rather than being reduced to discrete indices.

## Method

ETT replaces the conventional use of discrete codebook indices with the continuous codebook embeddings themselves, connecting them through a GeLU-activated MLP projector to a pretrained LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B), thereby making the entire computation graph differentiable. The training objective jointly optimizes a token-level caption cross-entropy loss and the original VQ reconstruction loss (weighted by α=0.25), so the tokenizer learns semantically meaningful representations while preserving image reconstruction fidelity. Training follows three stages: (1) alignment with frozen tokenizer and LLM, training only the projector; (2) semantic learning where all components (tokenizer, projector, LLM) are unfrozen and co-optimized; (3) post-training with the tokenizer frozen to specialize for understanding (ETT-Chat) or generation (ETT-Gen). This design requires no modification to the LLM's text codebook, embedding layers, or classification heads, making it a drop-in enhancement over existing autoregressive pipelines.

## Results

On multimodal understanding benchmarks, ETT (1.5B LLM) achieves 58.8 on MMBench-EN, 66.6 on SEED-Bench-Img, 59.4 on GQA, 91.7 on ScienceQA-Img, and 56.8 on TextVQA, outperforming all discrete-tokenizer baselines (Chameleon, LWM, Liquid, Show-o) despite using fewer parameters and less data. The 3B variant further improves to 61.3 MMBench, 67.8 SEED-Bench, and 93.3 ScienceQA. On text-to-image generation, ETT (1.5B) attains 0.63 overall GenEval score and 81.03/58.19/72.14 on T2I-CompBench (color/shape/texture), surpassing SDXL (0.55 GenEval) and all discrete autoregressive baselines while using only 30M training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against discrete-tokenizer baselines, ETT (1.5B) dominates across the board: it outperforms Chameleon (7B) by +27.7 on MMBench (58.8 vs 31.1), +36.0 on SEED-Bench (66.6 vs 30.6), and +44.5 on ScienceQA (91.7 vs 47.2), while using roughly half the parameters and less data. ETT also surpasses Liquid (7B) on MME (1532.6 vs 1119.3), GQA (59.4 vs 58.4), and TextVQA (56.8 vs 42.4), and beats Show-o (1.3B) on POPE (82.4 vs 80.0) and GQA (59.4 vs 58.0). Compared to the strongest discrete baseline Emu3 (8B), ETT (3B) wins on MMBench (61.3 vs 58.5), GQA (60.5 vs 60.3), and ScienceQA (93.3 vs 89.2), but trails on SEED-Bench (67.8 vs 68.2), MM-Vet (32.2 vs 37.2), and TextVQA (61.1 vs 64.7). Against continuous-encoder VLMs, ETT (3B) is competitive with LLaVA-1.6 (7B) on MMBench (61.3 vs 67.4) and GQA (60.5 vs 64.2) while using far less pre-training data (32M vs 0.4B+), and matches Janus (1.3B) on GQA (60.5 vs 59.1) and SEED-Bench (67.8 vs 63.7). On generation, ETT (1.5B) achieves 0.63 GenEval overall, beating SDXL (0.55), LlamaGen (0.32), and Show-o (0.53), and reaches 81.03 on T2I-CompBench color—surpassing Emu3 (79.13)—while using only 30M training samples versus Emu3's undisclosed (larger) scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on IBQ as its vision tokenizer backbone but departs fundamentally from the standard autoregressive multimodal pipeline (Emu3, Show-o, Janus, Chameleon, LWM) where the tokenizer is frozen and only discrete codebook indices are fed to the LLM. The key conceptual departure is inspired by continuous-encoder VLMs (EVE, LLaVA) that update their vision encoders during training: ETT applies the same principle to discrete tokenizers by passing codebook embeddings (rather than indices) through a projector into the LLM, making the entire pipeline differentiable. This enables joint optimization of the tokenizer and LLM under a combined caption + reconstruction objective, something that was infeasible under the index-only paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ETT offers a minimal, drop-in modification to existing AR multimodal pipelines that challenges the widely-held assumption that a frozen, reconstruction-optimized tokenizer is sufficient for downstream understanding and generation. The ablation revealing that tuning the tokenizer solely for understanding catastrophically destroys reconstruction (rFID 1.03 → 45.70) while a small reconstruction weight (α=0.25) recovers it is practically important for anyone building unified multimodal models. Open questions include whether this paradigm can train a tokenizer from scratch (rather than fine-tuning IBQ), whether it extends to video and audio modalities, and whether the gains scale with larger data and model capacity.
