## Problem

As LLM training scales to tens of thousands of GPUs, hardware failures become frequent and non-negligible, yet existing fault-tolerant methods (checkpointing, rescheduling, redundant computation) introduce substantial memory and compute overhead that degrades throughput even under fault-free conditions. The paper identifies that these approaches are fundamentally algorithm-agnostic, insisting on exact step-by-step replication of computations, which is unnecessarily restrictive for stochastic optimization where gradient noise is already inherent. The key question is whether fault-tolerant training can strategically sacrifice computation precision to reduce overhead while still achieving strong model performance.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a distributed cluster (32 A100 GPUs, 4 nodes × 8 GPUs) with |DP|=4 and |PP|=8, consuming initial LLaMA model weights and C4 pre-training data. A critical dynamic input is the node availability status: upon detecting a failure, the neighbor node within the same DP rank fetches the failed node's weights and optimizer states from a corresponding device in another DP group. Outputs include the final trained weights, validation perplexity on held-out data, and downstream zero-shot (BoolQ, ARC-Easy, PIQA, TruthfulQA-MC2) and fine-tuned GLUE scores, all measured under three simulated failure frequencies (low/medium/high) with specific failure intervals and recovery times.

## Method

MeCeFO employs a Neighbor-Do-Both (NDB) strategy where a failed node's workload is absorbed by a neighboring node in the same DP rank, then applies three algorithmic techniques to minimize the neighbor's extra burden: (i) skip-connection, which deterministically drops the MHA module during backpropagation so the neighbor only propagates activation gradients without computing MHA Wgrad/Dgrad; (ii) selective activation recomputation for FFN modules, where the neighbor stores only FFN input activations and recomputes intermediate activations during backprop; and (iii) low-rank gradient approximation, which uses a rank-r SVD of FFN weight matrices (updated every τ=100 iterations) to approximate Wgrad via G_y(x^T V_1)V_1^T, reducing FLOPs from 2bmn to 2brn+2brm+2rmn. The theoretical contribution proves convergence at O(1/√(nT)) under a gradient-error assumption (δ-bounded relative error), matching standard distributed SGD. Empirically, MeCeFO incurs only 4.18% throughput drop for LLaMA-7B under high-frequency failures while maintaining perplexity within 1.6% of fault-free training, achieving 5.0×–6.7× greater resilience than Bamboo and Oobleck.

## Results

Under high-frequency hardware failures, MeCeFO incurs only a 4.18% throughput drop when pre-training LLaMA-7B (111.12k → 106.47k tokens/s), compared to 20.84% for Bamboo and 28.09% for Oobleck. Model quality is well preserved: validation perplexity increases by just 1.61% for LLaMA-7B (14.92 → 15.16) and 2.19% for LLaMA-1B (15.49 → 15.83) under high-frequency faults. Zero-shot and GLUE fine-tuning scores remain within 0.1–0.7 points of the fault-free baseline, confirming that the efficiency sacrifices do not degrade final model performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeCeFO outperforms both baselines on both throughput and throughput degradation across all model sizes and fault frequencies. Compared to Bamboo (redundant computation), MeCeFO achieves 2.76× to 13.9× less throughput drop under faults; for LLaMA-7B under high-frequency failures, MeCeFO degrades by 4.18% versus Bamboo's 20.84%. Bamboo also suffers from inherently low fault-free throughput (e.g., 438k vs. 1199k tokens/s for LLaMA-350M) due to its always-on redundancy overhead. Compared to Oobleck (pipeline template rescheduling), MeCeFO achieves 3.71× to 28.0× less degradation; the gap widens with model size and fault frequency, reaching 28.09% vs. 4.18% for LLaMA-7B under high-frequency faults. Critically, MeCeFO also maintains higher absolute throughput even in the fault-free setting (1199k vs. 704k for 350M; 111k vs. 67k for 7B), since its efficiency techniques are only activated during failures. On model quality, MeCeFO's validation perplexity and downstream GLUE/zero-shot scores remain within ~2% of the fault-free baseline, confirming that the relaxed computation does not compromise final model performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in fault-tolerant distributed training (Bamboo, Oobleck, checkpointing-based methods), which treats fault recovery as an exact-computation problem inherited from general distributed systems. Instead, MeCeFO repurposes techniques from the memory/computation-efficient training literature—skip-connections (inspired by DropBP), activation recomputation, and low-rank gradient approximation (related to GaLore)—but applies them selectively and only during fault recovery rather than throughout training. The neighbor-do-both (NDB) failover strategy is a natural extension of standard DP redundancy, while the convergence analysis builds on standard momentum SGD theory with an added gradient-error assumption (Assumption 3) to account for the approximations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale LLM training where hardware failures are frequent and costly: a 4.18% throughput drop under high-frequency failures (vs. 20–28% for baselines) translates to substantial compute savings at cluster scale. However, the experimental validation is limited to a 32-GPU cluster and models up to 7B parameters, leaving open whether the approach maintains its advantages at the 100K+ GPU scale it targets. Key open questions include: whether Assumption 3 (bounded relative gradient error) holds universally across architectures and training stages, how MeCeFO interacts with tensor parallelism and more complex failure modes (switch-level, communication disruptions), and whether the per-iteration failure model adequately captures real-world failure dynamics.
