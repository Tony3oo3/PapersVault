## Problem

Reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) has proven effective for language and image reasoning in LLMs/MLLMs, but extending it to video reasoning faces two critical gaps: the absence of large-scale, high-quality video chain-of-thought (CoT) datasets with sufficient complexity and granularity, and the lack of explicit mechanisms to ensure that generated reasoning traces are faithfully grounded in visual evidence rather than hallucinated from text-only priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250155/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips (16 frames at 128×28×28 patch resolution during training; 32 frames at 256×28×28 during inference) paired with a text question, and produces a structured output consisting of a chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in <think>... tags followed by a final answer in <answer>...</answer> tags. The reasoning trace is internally organized into three consecutive segments—question parsing, video describing, and abstract reasoning—of which only the video-describing segment is subject to the semantic-consistency reward during RL.

## Method

VIDEORFT follows a two-stage RFT pipeline. In the SFT stage, a cognitively-inspired CoT curation pipeline first uses GPT-4o-mini to produce structured per-frame video representations, then invokes DeepSeek-R1 with five sub-prompts (simulated observation, task understanding, selective focus, visual reasoning, reflective answering) to generate initial CoTs via blind text-only reasoning, and finally refines those CoTs with Qwen2.5-VL conditioned on the actual video to eliminate visual hallucinations, yielding VideoRFT-CoT-102K. In the RL stage, GRPO is applied over VideoRFT-RL-310K with a composite reward R = R_f + R_a + 𝟙[R_a>0]·R_s, where the novel semantic-consistency reward R_s computes the cosine similarity between SigLIP text embeddings of the video-description span and averaged SigLIP image embeddings of uniformly sampled frames, explicitly incentivizing visual grounding of the reasoning trace while the accuracy gate prevents reinforcement of factually incorrect but semantically plausible outputs.

## Results

VIDEORFT achieves state-of-the-art performance on 4 out of 6 video reasoning/understanding benchmarks (VSI-Bench, MMVU, TempCompass, VideoMME), with average accuracy of 36.8, 68.5, 73.7, and 59.8 respectively. Compared to its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VIDEORFT gains +5.0% on VSI-Bench, +7.2% on MMVU, and +7.0% on VideoMME. It surpasses the proprietary GPT-4o on VSI-Bench by +2.8% (36.8 vs. 34.0) and outperforms all concurrent R1-based video reasoning models (Video-R1, TinyLLaVA-Video-R1, VideoChat-R1) in overall ranking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VIDEORFT improves on all six benchmarks, with the largest gains on MMVU (+7.2%) and VideoMME (+7.0%), confirming the effectiveness of the RFT pipeline. Compared to the closest concurrent R1-based competitor Video-R1, VIDEORFT wins on VSI-Bench (+1.0%), MMVU (+4.7%), TempCompass (+0.5%), and VideoMME (+0.5%), but trails on VideoMMMU (51.1 vs. 52.3) and MVBench (62.1 vs. 63.9). Against the proprietary GPT-4o, VIDEORFT surpasses it on VSI-Bench (36.8 vs. 34.0) but falls short on VideoMMMU (51.1 vs. 61.2), MMVU (68.5 vs. 75.4), and VideoMME (59.8 vs. 71.9). Among all non-RL open-source MLLMs (LLaVA-OneVision, VILA, LongVA, mPLUG-Owl3, etc.), VIDEORFT consistently achieves the highest scores, with margins of 4–18% depending on the benchmark. The 3B variant (Appendix A.1) also outperforms TinyLLaVA-Video-R1-3B by +8.2% on MMVU and +11.5% on TempCompass, demonstrating scalability to smaller models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage Reinforced Fine-Tuning (RFT) paradigm—originally established for LLMs by ReFT [27] and popularized by DeepSeek-R1's GRPO algorithm [34]—to the video domain for MLLMs. It departs from prior template-based video reasoning frameworks (VoT, STEP) that impose rigid multi-step structures, and from concurrent video RFT efforts (Video-R1, VideoChat-R1, TinyLLaVA-Video-R1) that rely on simplistic CoT prompting without explicit visual grounding. The key methodological departures are a cognition-inspired multi-expert CoT curation pipeline (combining a reasoning LLM for blind reasoning with an MLLM for cross-modal revision) and a semantic-consistency reward that selectively aligns the video-description segment of reasoning traces with visual features via SigLIP embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized bottleneck in video RFT—the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality video CoT data—by releasing two substantial datasets (VideoRFT-CoT-102K and VideoRFT-RL-310K) that are likely to become useful resources for the community. The semantic-consistency reward offers a principled, lightweight mechanism for enforcing visual grounding during RL without requiring a separate reward model, which could be adopted in other multimodal RL settings. However, the approach inherits limitations from its reliance on existing LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, GPT-4o-mini) for CoT generation, and the paper does not report error bars or multiple runs, leaving the robustness of the reported gains somewhat uncertain. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to longer videos, higher frame rates, or domains where the structured representation step (GPT-4o-mini) is less reliable.
