## Problem

Dexterous (especially bimanual) garment manipulation remains unsolved due to three compounding gaps: no simulation environment supports realistic dexterous hand–garment interactions at scale, data collection relies on labor-intensive teleoperation or expert RL, and existing imitation-learning policies fail to generalize across the diverse shapes and deformations of unseen garments. This paper tackles all three simultaneously by building a dedicated environment, an automated data pipeline, and a hierarchical generalizable policy.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes garment point clouds (2048 points, no color), environment point clouds (2048 points, with color), optional interaction-object point clouds (2048 points), robot joint states (60-dim vector covering 2×6 arm joints + 2×24 hand joints), and GAM-generated left/right affordance similarity scores (2048×2, normalized to [0,1]). It outputs a 60-DOF joint action vector per timestep (6 DoF per UR10e arm + 24 DoF per Shadow Hand) over a 4-step action horizon, with the diffusion process using 10 denoising steps. Data is recorded every 5 simulation timesteps to reduce density without sacrificing performance.

## Method

HALO decomposes manipulation into two hierarchical stages. First, the Garment Affordance Model (GAM) uses a PointNet++ encoder trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss (leveraging Skeleton Merger-derived correspondences across flat garments and point-tracing for deformed states) to transfer demo grasp points onto novel garments via feature dot-product similarity, enabling category-level generalization with translation/scale normalization. Second, the Structure-Aware Diffusion Policy (SADP) follows the Diffusion Policy framework but conditions denoising on a fused scene representation: garment features (PointNet++ over garment point cloud concatenated with affordance features), interaction-object features (MLP), full environment point cloud (MLP), and robot state (MLP), producing 60-DOF joint actions. The automated data collection pipeline requires only a single expert demonstration (hand poses via LeapMotion, grasp points, and task sequence) and uses GAM to retarget grasp points onto new garments, with trajectories adapted to garment geometry (e.g., lifting height scaled to sleeve length), generating 100 diverse demonstrations per task in 30–80 seconds each.

## Results

HALO achieves an average success rate of 84.6% across 14 dexterous garment manipulation tasks in simulation, ranging from 72% (Wear Bowl Hat) to 92% (Hang Tops). In contrast, Diffusion Policy (DP) averages 54.6% and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) averages 55.4%, with per-task success rates between 41% and 67%. HALO outperforms both baselines on every single task, with the largest margins on Hang Tops (+47 pp over DP) and Wear Bowl Hat (+31 pp over DP). In real-world evaluation, HALO achieves 13/15 on Fold Tops and 14/15 on Wear Hat, versus 9/15 and 10/15 for DP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HALO consistently outperforms both Diffusion Policy (DP) and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) across all 14 simulation tasks. Against DP, HALO gains an average of ~30 percentage points (84.6% vs. 54.6%), with the largest advantage on Hang Tops (92% vs. 45%) and Wear Bowl Hat (72% vs. 41%). Against DP3, the average gap is ~29 percentage points (84.6% vs. 55.4%), with DP3 performing slightly better than DP on a few tasks (e.g., Fling Trousers 58% vs. 54%, Hang Trousers 59% vs. 56%) but still trailing HALO by 24–31 pp on those tasks. The paper attributes DP/DP3's weakness to their inability to accurately locate manipulation points on novel garment shapes and to generate trajectories adapted to garment-specific geometry, which HALO addresses through its two-stage GAM + SADP architecture. In real-world settings, HALO also leads: 13/15 on Fold Tops vs. 9/15 (DP) and 8/15 (DP3), and 14/15 on Wear Hat vs. 10/15 (DP) and 9/15 (DP3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on UniGarmentManip's dense visual correspondence framework for the Garment Affordance Model (GAM) and on Diffusion Policy's action-generation paradigm for the Structure-Aware Diffusion Policy (SADP), combining them into a hierarchical architecture for garment manipulation. It departs from prior garment manipulation environments (Softgym, GarmentLab, FluidLab) by introducing the first simulation specifically designed for dexterous bimanual hands, replacing the attach-block hack with physically grounded adhesion/friction parameters in PBD. The automated data collection pipeline, which exploits category-level structural correspondence to generate diverse demonstrations from a single expert demo, represents a departure from the teleoperation- or RL-expert-dependent data collection that has dominated the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DexGarmentLab provides the first dedicated benchmark for dexterous bimanual garment manipulation with 15 tasks across 8 garment categories, filling a clear gap between rigid-object dexterous manipulation benchmarks and the simpler parallel-gripper garment simulators. The automated data collection pipeline (30–80 s per demo, matching teleoperation quality) significantly lowers the barrier to scaling garment manipulation research. Key open questions remain: the environment only supports single-garment tasks, PBD/FEM simulation still exhibits penetration and instability artifacts, and the policy struggles with highly occluded or non-standard geometries (e.g., asymmetrical designs). Extending to multi-garment tasks, mobile robot platforms, and more complex actions like knot-tying are natural next steps.
