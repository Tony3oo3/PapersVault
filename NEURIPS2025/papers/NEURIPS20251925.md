## Problem

Existing first-order methods for bilevel optimization with coupled lower-level constraints either require costly projection oracles, rely on double or triple-loop structures with suboptimal convergence rates, or fail to rigorously connect the stationary points of their reformulated problems to the original hyper-objective. This paper addresses the gap by developing a single-loop, Hessian-free algorithm for the important special case of coupled linear constraints, while establishing a rigorous theoretical bridge between the penalty-reformulated objective and the original bilevel problem.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input the upper-level objective f(x,y), the strongly convex lower-level objective g(x,y), the coupled linear constraint h(x,y) = Bx + Ay − b ≤ 0, and a penalty parameter δ = Θ(ε). It produces an ε-stationary point of the penalty-reformulated function Φ_δ(x), whose value and gradient are provably O(δ)-close to those of the original hyper-objective Φ(x) under LICQ and strict complementarity. The method is entirely first-order—no Hessian or Jacobian of the lower-level problem is computed—and operates in a single loop over all primal and dual variables simultaneously.

## Method

SFLCB reformulates the bilevel problem as the minimax penalty function Φ_δ(x) = min_y max_z [f(x,y) + (1/δ)(g(x,y) − g(x,z))] over the coupled constraint set, then converts inequality constraints to equalities via slack variables and constructs an augmented Lagrangian K that is strongly convex in y and strongly concave in z. A single-loop gradient descent-ascent scheme is applied to the resulting minimax problem over K, updating x, y, z, and the dual multipliers u, v simultaneously at each iteration with projections onto the slack-variable set. The convergence analysis introduces a novel potential function V_t that combines the augmented Lagrangian value with dual-distance terms, enabling a descent lemma that yields O(ε^{-3}) iteration complexity—improving over the O(ε^{-3}log(ε^{-1})) rate of prior double-loop methods. The full row rank of A (or LICQ at initialization in the decoupled case) is leveraged to bound dual variable errors without requiring access to exact optimal duals.

## Results

The primary theoretical contribution is an improvement in iteration complexity for finding an ε-stationary point of the reformulated function Φ_δ: from O(ε^{-3} log(ε^{-1})) to O(ε^{-3}) for coupled linear constraints with full row rank A, and from O(ε^{-3} log(ε^{-1})) to O(ε^{-3}) for decoupled constraints Ay ≤ 0 with LICQ at initial points. For the general case Ay ≤ 0 without LICQ, the complexity is O(ε^{-4}). Empirically, on the Diabetes dataset for SVM hyperparameter optimization, SFLCB converges significantly faster than all four baselines (GAM, LV-HBA, BLOCC, BiC-GAFFA), and on the 3-node and 9-node transportation network design problems, SFLCB substantially outperforms BLOCC in UL utility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFLCB improves upon BLOCC [16] (Jiang et al., 2024) in both theory and practice: theoretically, SFLCB reduces the complexity from O(ε^{-3} log(ε^{-1})) to O(ε^{-3}) for coupled linear constraints and eliminates the need for double/triple loops; empirically, SFLCB converges significantly faster on both the SVM hyperparameter optimization (Diabetes dataset) and the transportation network design tasks. Compared to GAM [46] (Xu & Zhu, AAAI 2023), which uses Clarke subdifferential approximation and only provides asymptotic convergence guarantees, SFLCB offers non-asymptotic rates and is Hessian-free. Against LV-HBA [50] (Yao et al., 2024), which requires a projection operator onto the coupled constraint set C = {(x,y) | h(x,y) ≤ 0} at each iteration, SFLCB avoids this costly projection by using slack variables and augmented Lagrangian. Versus BiC-GAFFA [49] (Yao et al., 2024), which reformulates via a doubly regularized gap function but only provides an asymptotic relationship between the reformulated and original problems, SFLCB establishes a rigorous non-asymptotic O(δ) bound on the gradient gap (Theorem 4.9), providing stronger theoretical justification for the reformulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the penalty-based reformulation of bilevel optimization introduced by Kwon et al. [21] and Jiang et al. [16], who both recast the original bilevel problem as a single-level minimax problem with a penalty parameter δ. The key departure is twofold: (1) the authors provide the first rigorous non-asymptotic bound on the gradient gap between the reformulated function Φ_δ and the original hyper-objective Φ under *coupled* constraints Y(x) (extending [21]'s result, which only covered decoupled constraints), and (2) they replace the double- or triple-loop algorithms of [16] with a single-loop, Hessian-free gradient descent-ascent method (SFLCB) that leverages an augmented Lagrangian to enforce strong convexity/concavity in the minimax subproblem. The existence of ∇Φ(x) under strict complementarity and LICQ is inherited from Xu and Zhu [46].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-loop, Hessian-free design makes SFLCB substantially easier to implement at scale compared to prior implicit-gradient or multi-loop methods, which is important for applications like hyperparameter optimization and adversarial training where Hessian computations are prohibitive. The removal of the log(ε^{-1}) factor in the convergence rate, while modest, eliminates a practical bottleneck for high-precision solutions. However, the analysis is restricted to deterministic, linearly constrained settings with LICQ, and the authors explicitly flag stochastic extensions and the question of whether the optimal O(ε^{-2}) rate (known for unconstrained BLO) is achievable under constraints as open problems. The practical impact is real but bounded by these structural assumptions.
