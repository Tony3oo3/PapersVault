## Problem

Bayesian DAG learning requires posterior distributions over graph structures, but existing approaches either use Gibbs-type priors with continuous acyclicity penalties that do not place mass on exact zeros (and thus do not yield exact DAG posteriors), or rely on augmented discrete spaces (e.g., topological orderings) that complicate inference. No prior work provides provably valid probability distributions whose support lies directly on the space of sparse acyclic adjacency matrices, making principled uncertainty quantification over DAG structures an open challenge.

## Input / Output

The method takes an observational data matrix X ∈ R^{n×p} and a sparsity parameter λ ≥ 0, and produces a full posterior distribution over weighted acyclic adjacency matrices W ∈ R^{p×p} (with zero diagonal). The posterior is represented as a projected distribution: a multivariate Gaussian on an unconstrained matrix W̃ is mapped through a minimal-distance projection onto the set of l1-constrained acyclic matrices, yielding exact zeros for absent edges. For nonlinear SEMs, the input is the same data matrix but the output is a posterior over neural network weights whose first-hidden-layer norms form an acyclic adjacency matrix. The method operates in an unsupervised setting with no labels or interventions required.

## Method

ProDAG defines a new family of distributions over DAGs by projecting a continuous distribution P onto the space of sparse acyclic adjacency matrices via pro_λ(W̃) = argmin_{W∈DAG, ||W||_{l1}≤λ} ||W̃−W||_F. The combinatorial projection is reformulated as a continuous optimization using DAGMA's log-determinant acyclicity constraint h(W) = −log det(I−W∘W) = 0, solved by a two-step procedure: a path-following algorithm projects W̃ onto the acyclic set, then a non-iterative soft-thresholding step enforces the l1 ball. Both the prior and the variational posterior are projected distributions, with variational parameters (means and covariances of a Gaussian on W̃) optimized by maximizing the ELBO via gradient descent. Gradients of the projection with respect to W̃ and λ are computed analytically through the implicit function theorem applied to KKT conditions, avoiding backpropagation through the iterative solver. The framework extends to nonlinear SEMs by constraining the Jacobian of a neural network to be acyclic, reducing the projection to the same matrix problem on the norms of first-hidden-layer weight vectors.

## Results

On the Sachs flow cytometry dataset (p=11, n=7466), ProDAG achieves the best expected SHD (18±0.2), expected F1 score (24±0.6%), and AUROC (60±1.6%), while tying DiBS on Brier score (16±0.2). On synthetic linear Erdős-Rényi DAGs (p=20, s=40), ProDAG outperforms all Bayesian baselines across most metrics for moderate-to-large sample sizes (n≥100), approaching the accuracy of frequentist DAGMA and BOSS at n=1000. On the large MUNIN semi-synthetic network (p=186, n=100), ProDAG achieves a Brier score of 146±4.0 and expected SHD of 201±4.9, substantially outperforming BayesDAG (Brier 279, SHD 557) while DAGMA scores better on F1 (67% vs 37%) and AUROC (89% vs 87%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProDAG outperforms DAGMA on all four metrics on the Sachs dataset (Brier 16 vs 36, SHD 18 vs 29, F1 24% vs 17%, AUROC 60% vs 49%), demonstrating the Bayesian advantage over the shared continuous acyclicity formulation. Against DiBS, ProDAG ties on Brier score (16 vs 16) but wins decisively on structure recovery (SHD 18 vs 22, F1 24% vs 16%) and discriminative power (AUROC 60% vs 55%). ProDAG dominates DiBS+ across all metrics (Brier 16 vs 26, SHD 18 vs 24, F1 24% vs 15%, AUROC 60% vs 51%). Compared to BayesDAG, ProDAG wins on Brier (16 vs 17), SHD (18 vs 25), and F1 (24% vs 20%), with AUROC essentially tied (60% vs 59%). Against the discrete-search method BOSS, ProDAG wins on every metric (Brier 16 vs 34, SHD 18 vs 28, F1 24% vs 15%, AUROC 60% vs 49%). In the MCMC comparison (Table 3, p=20, n=100), ProDAG substantially outperforms Gadget on all metrics (Brier 13 vs 52, SHD 14 vs 37, F1 78% vs 17%, AUROC 91% vs 65%), highlighting the scalability advantage of variational inference over MCMC for DAG learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProDAG departs from the two dominant paradigms for Bayesian DAG learning—Gibbs-type priors with continuous acyclicity penalties (DiBS, BayesDAG) and state-augmentation over topological orderings (Charpentier et al., Bonilla et al.)—by instead constructing distributions that natively have support on the space of sparse DAGs via a minimal-distance projection from a continuous distribution. The projection leverages DAGMA's log-determinant acyclicity characterization (Bello et al., 2022) and the l1-ball projection algorithm of Duchi et al. (2008), but combines them into a nonconvex combinatorial problem that is nonetheless shown to yield a unique, measurable mapping (Theorem 1). Conceptually, the approach extends the l1-ball projection distributions of Xu and Duan (2023) from sparse regression to the nonconvex setting of acyclic matrices, where the projection is no longer a proximal mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProDAG provides the first provably valid Bayesian distributions over DAGs that place exact mass on zeros and guarantee acyclicity without relaxations or discrete approximations, addressing a long-standing gap in uncertainty quantification for causal structure learning. The GPU-parallelizable projection and the reduction of the nonlinear case to the same projection as the linear case (Proposition 1) make the framework practically scalable to p=100+ variables. Key limitations include cubic worst-case complexity, the nonconvexity of the acyclicity constraint (solutions are only stationary points), and the inherent approximation error of variational inference. A natural open direction is extending the framework to interventional data, where the projected distributions could remain unchanged while the likelihood is adapted.
