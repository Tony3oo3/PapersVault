## Problem

Existing pancreatic tumor CT datasets are small (≤2,238 scans), drawn from few centers, and lack comprehensive anatomical annotations (pancreas subregions, key vessels, surrounding organs) needed for clinically meaningful tumor analysis. This data limitation prevents AI models from generalizing to diverse clinical settings and makes it impossible to properly evaluate false-positive rates, since most public datasets contain almost no normal scans.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 36,390 abdominal CT volumes in unified NIfTI format, each paired with metadata (age, sex, contrast phase, in-plane spacing, slice thickness, diagnosis). Annotations are voxel-wise masks over 28 classes: pancreatic tumor, pancreas head/body/tail, and 24 surrounding structures (SMA, celiac artery, portal vein, bile ducts, duodenum, kidneys, liver, lungs, femurs, etc.). The 9,901-case training set is publicly released (CC BY-NC-SA), while the 26,489-case test set from three unseen centers plus RATIC is reserved for third-party evaluation to ensure out-of-distribution generalization testing.

## Method

The core contribution is the PanTS dataset itself, built through a multi-annotator protocol where 23 radiologists (specialists, general radiologists, residents) performed slice-by-slice voxel-wise annotation via MONAI-Label, with a human-in-the-loop workflow using an AI anatomy segmentator to pre-label non-tumor structures that were then manually verified. Empirical validation uses standard nnU-Net under two key experiments: (1) a scaling study training on MSD-Pancreas (n=281), PANORAMA (n=2,238), and PanTS (n=9,901), showing AUC rising from 0.810 to 0.959 on the held-out OOD test set; and (2) a 2-class vs. 28-class ablation demonstrating that joint supervision on 24 surrounding structures improves tumor DSC by +10.3% and NSD by +9.7%, with t-SNE analysis revealing better feature separation. Annotation quality was verified via a 300-scan inter-annotator agreement study yielding median DSC of 86.1%, with a 20% DSC threshold flagging ambiguous cases for senior review.

## Results

On the held-out PanTS test set (out-of-distribution, 145 centers), nnU-Net trained on PanTS (n=9,901) achieves an AUC of 0.959 for pancreatic tumor detection, compared to 0.810 for MSD-Pancreas (n=281) and 0.819 for PANORAMA (n=2,238). On the official MSD-Pancreas test set (third-party evaluated), the PanTS-trained nnU-Net outperforms all prior baselines by at least +4.9% DSC and +3.1% NSD, securing the top-1 position on the MSD leaderboard. In the 28-class vs. 2-class ablation, adding 24 surrounding anatomical structures improves tumor segmentation DSC from 57.4% to 67.7% (+10.3%) and NSD from 56.8% to 66.5% (+9.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares nnU-Net trained on PanTS against two prior pancreatic tumor datasets. Against MSD-Pancreas (Antonelli et al., 2022; n=281, 1 center), the PanTS-trained model achieves AUC 0.959 vs. 0.810 on the PanTS out-of-distribution test set, and on the official MSD-Pancreas test set it surpasses all existing leaderboard entries by at least +4.9% DSC and +3.1% NSD, becoming the new top-1 model. Against PANORAMA (Alves et al., 2024; n=2,238, 7 centers), the PanTS-trained model improves AUC from 0.819 to 0.959 on the same out-of-distribution test set, demonstrating that the 16× larger annotation scale and 20× greater institutional diversity yield substantially better generalization. The modest gain from MSD to PANORAMA (0.810→0.819) suggests scale alone is insufficient; the large jump to PanTS is attributed to both the 4.4× increase in annotated scans and the comprehensive 28-class annotation scheme covering 24 surrounding anatomical structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the pancreatic CT dataset space by combining unprecedented scale (36,390 scans from 145 centers) with clinically targeted anatomical detail (pancreas subregions plus 24 surrounding structures) that no prior dataset—PANORAMA, MSD-Pancreas, or general abdominal benchmarks like AbdomenCT-1K—has offered simultaneously. Methodologically, it repurposes the human-in-the-loop annotation workflow established in AbdomenAtlas-8K and ScaleMAI to scale voxel-wise labeling, and uses nnU-Net as a standard baseline to isolate the effect of data scale and anatomical context rather than proposing a new architecture. The paper's conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit demonstration that both tumor annotation volume and surrounding-structure supervision are independently necessary for robust out-of-distribution pancreatic tumor segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pancreatic cancer remains the third leading cause of cancer death in the U.S., and the 80–85% late-stage diagnosis rate underscores the need for AI tools that generalize across institutions and imaging protocols—precisely the gap PanTS addresses with its 145-center, multi-protocol design. The finding that 89% normal scans enable meaningful specificity and PPV evaluation (a gap in all prior pancreatic tumor datasets) is practically critical for any future screening deployment. Open questions include whether the +10.3% DSC gain from anatomical context transfers to other architectures beyond nnU-Net, whether the 86.1% median inter-annotator DSC ceiling limits further model improvement, and whether the substantial distributional shift between training and test sets (HU values, contrast phase mix) reflects realistic clinical variation or an overly conservative benchmark.
