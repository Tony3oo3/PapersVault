## Problem

In off-dynamics RL, an agent trained in a source domain must deploy in a target domain with shifted transition dynamics. Existing distributionally robust MDP frameworks define uncertainty sets as balls around the transition kernel at each state-action pair, which can be overly conservative when the dynamics are known to follow a linear mixture structure. This paper identifies that when the nominal dynamics are linear mixtures of known basis modes, the natural uncertainty should be placed on the mixture weighting parameter rather than the kernel itself, and provides the first finite-sample analysis of this new framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of K trajectories collected from the source domain by a behavior policy, a known d-dimensional feature mapping φ(·|s,a) whose components are probability distributions (basis latent modes), and a robustness radius ρ specifying the divergence-ball size around the true mixture weights. It outputs a deterministic stationary robust policy π̂ along with a high-probability suboptimality upper bound. A key structural constraint is that the basis modes must be known a priori while the weighting parameter θ⁰ ∈ Δ^{d-1} is unknown and estimated from data; the uncertainty set is a divergence ball in the parameter simplex rather than in the kernel space.

## Method

The core contribution is a new uncertainty set design: instead of a ball around the transition kernel P⁰(·|s,a), the linear mixture uncertainty set is defined as {⟨φ(·|s,a), θ⟩ : θ ∈ Δ^{d-1}, D(θ‖θ⁰) ≤ ρ}, placing the perturbation on the mixture weights. The paper proves the robust Bellman equation holds under this rectangular-type structure, enabling dynamic programming. The algorithm (Algorithm 1) proceeds in three stages: (1) estimate θ⁰ via transition-targeted ridge regression over a constructed feasible set (chosen over value-targeted regression to avoid data coverage issues), (2) build confidence ellipsoids for θ using a concentration lemma tailored to the dependent error structure, and (3) apply the double pessimism principle to define a doubly pessimistic value estimator J_Pess²(π) = inf over confidence region × inf over robust uncertainty set, then maximize it to obtain π̂. Theoretical guarantees yield suboptimality bounds of Õ(dH²C^{π*}/√K) for TV, Õ(dH²C^{π*}e^{H/λ}/(ρ√K)) for KL, and Õ(d(√ρH³+H²)C^{π*}/√K) for χ² divergences.

## Results

The paper establishes the first finite-sample guarantees for the linear mixture DRMDP framework. Under TV-divergence, the suboptimality bound is Õ(dH²C^{π*}/√K); under KL-divergence, it is Õ(dH²C^{π*}e^{H/λ}/(ρ√K)); and under χ²-divergence, it is Õ(d(√ρH³+H²)C^{π*}/√K). The corresponding sample complexity to learn an ε-optimal robust policy is Õ(d²H⁴(C^{π*})²/ε²) for TV, Õ(d²H⁴(C^{π*})²e^{2H/λ}/(ρ²ε²)) for KL, and Õ(d²(√ρH³+H²)²(C^{π*})²/ε²) for χ². These bounds match the non-robust linear mixture MDP results of Ayoub et al. [2] in terms of dimension d and horizon H when the robustness radius ρ is negligible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper that introduces a new framework rather than benchmarking against empirical baselines. The paper provides conceptual (geometric) comparisons showing that the linear mixture uncertainty set is strictly smaller than both the (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty set (Lemma 3.4, Figure 1a) and the d-rectangular uncertainty set (Figure 1b) in specific instances, implying less conservative robust policies. The authors explicitly note (Remark 3.6) that direct comparison with other DRMDP settings is not fair because each requires different prior information. The theoretical bounds are compared to existing results: the TV-divergence bound matches Theorem 1 of Ayoub et al. [2] for standard linear mixture MDPs in d and H dependence, while the KL and χ² bounds carry additional terms (e^{H/λ}/ρ and √ρH³ respectively) that reflect the inherent difficulty of robust learning under those divergences, consistent with prior tabular DRMDP results [5, 41, 42].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: distributionally robust MDPs (Iyengar 2005; Nilim & El Ghaoui 2005) and linear mixture MDPs (Ayoub et al. 2020; Jia et al. 2020). Prior DRMDP work defines uncertainty sets directly on transition kernels—either per state-action pair ((s,a)-rectangular) or on factor distributions (d-rectangular, Goyal & Grand-Clement 2023; Ma et al. 2022). The key departure here is defining the uncertainty set on the mixture weighting parameter θ rather than on the kernel itself, yielding a fundamentally different geometric structure. The algorithm design leverages the double pessimism principle (Blanchet et al. 2024) and transition-targeted ridge regression (Li et al. 2024), adapting them to the new parameter-space uncertainty geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-sample analysis of robust policy learning under linear mixture uncertainty sets, establishing that the framework is statistically learnable under TV, KL, and χ² divergences. The geometric comparisons showing the linear mixture set is strictly contained in (s,a)-rectangular and can be smaller than d-rectangular provide a principled justification for when parameter-space uncertainty modeling is preferable. However, the meta-algorithm relies on an intractable planning oracle, and the practical algorithms in the appendix introduce a theory-practice gap. Open questions include online learning extensions, the effect of basis-mode misspecification, and whether computationally efficient algorithms can close the gap between the theoretical guarantees and tractable implementations.
