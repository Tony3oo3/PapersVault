## Problem

Dynamic pricing with resource constraints (e.g., finite inventory) requires balancing revenue maximization against irreversible resource depletion, yet prior algorithms either assume non-degeneracy conditions that fail in practice or ignore the intermediate case where a machine-learned initial price estimate is available. This paper addresses all three information regimes—full, no, and informed price—under hard resource constraints, removing the non-degeneracy requirement and providing the first rigorous analysis of how an informed price with known error bound improves regret.

## Input / Output

At each period the algorithm receives the remaining resource capacity vector c^t ∈ R^m_+ and a known consumption matrix A ∈ R^{m×n}, and must output a continuous price vector p^t ∈ [L,U]^n for n products. In the full-information setting the demand function f(p) = α + Bp is given; in the no-information setting it must be learned online from observed (p^s, d^s) pairs; in the informed-price setting an initial pair (p⁰, d⁰) with known error bound ε⁰ is provided. Performance is measured as regret against a deterministic fluid benchmark, and the algorithm must never violate capacity constraints (demand exceeding remaining capacity is curtailed).

## Method

The core mechanism is a Boundary Attracted Re-solve: at each period the algorithm solves the fluid LP for the optimal price, then rounds any demand component below a dynamic threshold ζ(T−t+1)^{−1/2} to zero, pushing prices toward the upper bound and preemptively avoiding the ill-conditioned near-degenerate region where dual variables blow up. For the no-information setting, this re-solve is combined with linear regression (using a pseudo-inverse of a moment matrix P^t) and controlled Gaussian perturbations decaying at rate t^{−1/4} to ensure sufficient price variance for estimation, yielding the optimal O(√T) regret. For the informed-price setting, an estimate-then-select algorithm anchors regression at (p⁰, d⁰) and checks whether (ε⁰)²T exceeds a tolerance ρ√T: if so it falls back to the no-information algorithm, otherwise it exploits the informed pair to achieve O((ε⁰)²T + log T) regret, exhibiting a phase transition between near-full-information and worst-case learning performance.

## Results

The paper establishes three theoretical regret bounds: O(ζ²n²‖B⁻¹‖₂ log T) for full information (Theorem 2.1), O((ζ² + ‖B⁻¹‖₂)√T) for no information (Theorem 3.1, matching the known lower bound), and O(min{ρ√T, (ε⁰)²T + C′log T}) for the informed-price setting (Theorem 4.2). In large-scale experiments (m=100 resources, n=200 products), boundary attraction (ζ=1) reduces regret by 30–45% over the classical re-solve baseline (ζ=0) across all three information regimes. For example, in the full-information setting at T=1000, regret drops from 13,100±655 (baseline) to 6,689±201 (proposed), and in the learning setting from 91,578±4,579 to 56,163±1,685.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical comparison is against the classical re-solve baseline (ζ=0, i.e., no boundary attraction / no demand thresholding). The proposed boundary-attraction mechanism (ζ=1) consistently outperforms this baseline across all three information settings and all time horizons tested, with regret reductions of 30–45% (full information), 32–39% (learning), and 27–45% (informed price). The absolute gap widens with T (e.g., in the learning setting, from 4,513 at T=200 to 35,415 at T=1000), and confidence intervals are substantially tighter for the proposed method (e.g., ±201 vs. ±655 at T=1000 in full information), indicating greater stability. Theoretically, the paper's full-information result improves upon Jasin (2014) and Wang and Wang (2022), which require a non-degeneracy condition (unique optimal dual solution) that often fails in practice; the proposed method removes this requirement entirely. The no-information O(√T) bound matches the known lower bound of Keskin and Zeevi (2014), confirming optimality. The informed-price result exhibits a phase transition: when ε⁰ is small, regret approaches O(log T) (near full-information performance), while for large ε⁰ it gracefully degrades to O(√T) (matching the no-information worst case).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fluid re-solving framework for dynamic pricing with resource constraints (Jasin 2014; Wang & Wang 2022) by introducing a boundary attraction mechanism that eliminates the non-degeneracy condition previously required for logarithmic regret guarantees. For the learning setting, it builds on the regression-based exploration of Keskin & Zeevi (2014) and Simchi-Levi & Xu (2022), adapting their parameter estimation to handle hard resource constraints. The informed-price setting draws on the learning-augmented algorithms paradigm (Lykouris & Vassilvitskii 2021; Bhaskara et al. 2020–2023), but is the first to combine it with irreversible resource consumption and degenerate fluid solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-degeneracy condition is a significant practical limitation in revenue management—degenerate fluid solutions are common when resources become scarce—so removing it makes logarithmic-regret guarantees applicable to realistic instances. The informed-price phase transition result (regret O(min{√T, (ε⁰)²T + log T})) provides a clean theoretical characterization of when offline data is worth leveraging under resource constraints, with the notable negative result that knowing the error bound ε⁰ is necessary to beat the √T baseline. Key limitations include the linear demand model, the requirement that ε⁰ be known a priori, and the single-informed-pair setting; extending to nonlinear demand, multiple informed prices, or online estimation of ε⁰ are natural open directions.
