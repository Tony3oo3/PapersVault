## Problem

Experience Replay (ER) in Online Continual Learning implicitly solves an ε-constraint problem with a fixed, post-hoc slack defined by its weighting parameter λ, which can drive updates into regions of negative generalization and overfit to the limited memory buffer. The paper identifies that this fixed trade-off between plasticity and stability is suboptimal and proposes a method that explicitly and dynamically manages the forgetting constraint.

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming 32×32×3 RGB image mini-batches (batch size 32) from sequential class-incremental tasks, interleaved with reservoir-sampled memory buffer samples (0.6k–5k depending on dataset). At each step it computes a look-ahead gradient on task-specific memory subsets before the outer-loop constraint evaluation, and calibrates the slack tolerance ε̄ dynamically at task boundaries using a 5% degradation margin over the recorded reference loss. Output is the updated Reduced ResNet-18 parameter vector, used for class prediction without task identity at inference.

## Method

GEC formulates each OCL update as an explicit ε-constraint quadratic program: minimize the distance between the candidate update direction v and the current-task gradient ∇ℓ_c, subject to a dynamic barrier constraint ⟨∇ℓ̄_m, v⟩ ≥ φ_h(θ_k) where φ_h = α·(ℓ̄_m(θ_k) − ℓ̄_m(θ_{k−1}) − ε̄). When the memory-loss increase exceeds the slack tolerance ε̄ (constraint violated), the QP forces v to project positively onto the memory gradient, prioritizing constraint satisfaction; when the constraint is satisfied, the barrier becomes non-positive, permitting aggressive current-task optimization while bounding the rate of memory-loss increase. The closed-form solution v_k = ∇ℓ_c + λ_k ∇ℓ̄_m uses a dynamically computed coefficient λ_k = max((φ_h − ⟨∇ℓ_c, ∇ℓ̄_m⟩)/(‖∇ℓ̄_m‖² + δ), 0), and in practice extends to a multi-constraint QP with per-task barriers when task labels are available in the buffer. Theoretical analysis via a continuous-time analogue proves that the thresholded constraint violation [h(θ_t)]₊ is non-increasing and that the current-task loss is non-increasing whenever the constraint is satisfied.

## Results

GEC achieves the best Average Anytime Accuracy (AAA) and Average Accuracy (Acc) across all three OCL benchmarks and all memory buffer sizes. On Seq-CIFAR10 (|M|=1k), GEC reaches 69.42 AAA / 59.12 Acc, surpassing the strongest baseline POCL (66.23 / 58.50) by 3.2 and 0.6 points respectively. On the more challenging Seq-CIFAR100 (|M|=5k) and Seq-TinyImageNet (|M|=5k), GEC achieves 38.11 / 34.22 and 27.66 / 20.25, outperforming POCL by 1.77 and 2.18 AAA points. GEC also attains the best (least negative) Forgetting Measure, e.g. −15.12 on Seq-CIFAR100 (|M|=5k) vs. −17.55 for POCL, while training in 147.6 s—roughly 3.2× faster than POCL (474.3 s) and 1.9× faster than CBA (274.3 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEC outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and memory configuration for both AAA and Acc. The closest competitor is POCL (ICML 2024), which GEC surpasses by 3.19 AAA on Seq-CIFAR10 (1k), 1.77 AAA on Seq-CIFAR100 (5k), and 2.18 AAA on Seq-TinyImageNet (5k); the gap widens on Forgetting Measure (−15.12 vs. −17.55 on CIFAR100-5k). Among rehearsal-based methods, GEC beats CLSER by 6.15 AAA on CIFAR10-1k and 8.78 AAA on CIFAR100-5k, and outperforms CBA by 3.95 AAA on CIFAR10-1k. Hard-constraint gradient projection methods (GEM, A-GEM) and regularization (EWC) are substantially weaker, with GEC exceeding GEM by over 32 AAA on CIFAR10-1k. DER++ and DER, which augment replay with logit distillation, remain far behind GEC (e.g., 69.42 vs. 61.01 AAA on CIFAR10-1k). In terms of efficiency, GEC trains in 147.6 s on Seq-CIFAR10—only 1.7× slower than ER (86.3 s) but 3.2× faster than POCL (474.3 s) and 1.9× faster than CBA (274.3 s)—while delivering the best accuracy, indicating a favorable performance–efficiency trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets Experience Replay (ER) through the lens of ε-constraint optimization, showing that ER's fixed weighting parameter λ implicitly defines a post-hoc slack on the stability constraint — a perspective that bridges the gap between ER's soft-constraint behavior and the hard-constraint formulations of GEM/A-GEM. It departs from POCL, which also frames OCL as multi-objective optimization but via Pareto steering, by instead using a dynamic gradient-guided barrier that explicitly modulates the constraint based on whether forgetting exceeds a tolerance. The QP-based update mechanism draws on the dynamic barrier gradient descent framework of Gong and Liu (2021), adapted here to the continual learning setting with per-task constraints and a look-ahead gradient strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ε-constraint reframing provides a principled explanation for why rehearsal-based methods (ER) consistently outperform gradient-projection methods (GEM/A-GEM) in OCL — mild constraint violations can be beneficial for generalization, and fixed weighting can cause overfitting to the memory buffer. GEC's dynamic mechanism addresses both limitations while remaining computationally lighter than POCL (~147s vs ~474s training time). Open questions include whether the ε-constraint perspective generalizes beyond image classification to language or multimodal OCL, whether the dynamic threshold calibration strategy is optimal, and whether the QP overhead can be further reduced for truly resource-constrained streaming scenarios.
