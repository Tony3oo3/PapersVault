## Problem

Existing agent-based systems for long video understanding face two critical limitations: they model individual frames in isolation, failing to capture temporal context across consecutive frames, and they rely on sparse frame sampling to reduce captioning costs, inevitably discarding crucial fine-grained information needed for accurate answers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250864/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an arbitrarily long video (from minutes to multi-hour recordings, with EgoMem videos averaging 6.33 hours) and a free-form text question. The video is consumed hierarchically: first as coarse clips (e.g., 100s segments at 1 FPS), then progressively finer segments (10s at 2 FPS, 1s at 2 FPS), with the depth of exploration determined dynamically by the question. The output is a confident text answer accompanied by an explainable, step-by-step reasoning trace showing how the system localized and integrated relevant evidence across temporal scales.

## Method

VideoLucy employs a three-level hierarchical memory structure (coarse, fine, ultra-fine) where each level explicitly defines a temporal scope and detail granularity, enabling multi-level representation with comprehensive information coverage. Four specialized agents—Captioning (MLLM-based visual-to-text conversion), Localization (LLM-based temporal relevance filtering), Instruction (generates targeted caption prompts for missing information), and Answering (decides confidence and produces final answers)—operate within an iterative backtracking loop. The loop starts from sparse coarse memory, then at each iteration identifies the single most question-relevant time period, generates a question-guided caption instruction, and explores both deeper memory (finer temporal resolution of that period) and broader memory (additional periods), updating the current memory list until the answering agent can produce a confident answer. This coarse-to-fine, question-guided exploration mimics human recollection and avoids the computational cost of exhaustive dense captioning while preserving temporal context and fine details.

## Results

On LVBench (ultra-long video QA), VideoLucy achieves 58.8% overall accuracy, surpassing the previous best open-source MLLM AdaReTake-72B (53.3%) by 5.5% and GPT-4o (48.9%) by 9.9%, with a particularly strong 75.6% on key information retrieval. On Video-MME (w/o subtitles), VideoLucy reaches 72.5% average accuracy (66.8% on the long split), outperforming the best agent-based system MemVid (64.0%) by 8.5% and matching Gemini 1.5 pro (75.0% overall) on the long-video subset. On the proposed EgoMem benchmark (6.33-hour egocentric videos), VideoLucy achieves 56.7% average accuracy, exceeding the best MLLM baseline VideoChat-Flash-7B (46.4%) by 10.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models, VideoLucy (72.5% Video-MME avg; 58.8% LVBench) surpasses GPT-4o (71.9%; 48.9%) and Gemini 1.5 Flash (70.3%) but remains below Gemini 1.5 pro (75.0%) on Video-MME overall, though it leads on the long-video split (66.8% vs. 67.4%). Among open-source MLLMs, VideoLucy outperforms all 72B-scale models on LVBench (58.8% vs. AdaReTake-72B's 53.3%) and on the Video-MME long split (66.8% vs. AdaReTake-72B's 65.0%), despite using only a 7B MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) as its captioner. Against agent-based systems, VideoLucy achieves the largest margins: 8.5% over MemVid (64.0%) on Video-MME, 5.5% over AdaReTake-72B (53.3%) on LVBench, and 10.3% over VideoChat-Flash-7B (46.4%) on EgoMem. On the Needle-in-a-Video-Haystack test, VideoLucy's accuracy remains nearly flat across video lengths from 400 s to 4000 s, whereas all MLLM baselines degrade substantially with length. The only consistent loss is to Gemini 1.5 pro on Video-MME short/medium splits, where the proprietary model's larger context window provides an advantage for shorter videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoLucy sits within the agent-based long video understanding paradigm (VideoAgent, DrVideo, VideoTree, MemVid) but departs from these systems in two key ways: it replaces frame-level captioning with a hierarchical memory structure that captures temporal context across consecutive frames at multiple granularities, and it replaces static sparse sampling with an iterative backtracking mechanism that dynamically deepens memory only where the question demands it. The design is explicitly inspired by human recollection (coarse-to-fine), drawing a conceptual parallel to cognitive science rather than to prior video architectures. It also introduces EgoMem, a new benchmark built on EgoLife that targets cross-temporal event understanding and instantaneous detail perception in multi-hour egocentric videos, filling a gap not addressed by existing benchmarks like Video-MME or LVBench.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a 7B open-source MLLM paired with an LLM agent can match or exceed proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro) on long video QA, which is practically significant for cost-sensitive deployments. The EgoMem benchmark (42 videos averaging 6.33 hours, 504 QAs) provides the community with a rigorous testbed for extremely long video understanding that prior benchmarks do not cover. However, the approach inherits limitations from its constituent MLLM/LLM (hallucination, missed details in coarse memory), the iterative loop introduces non-trivial inference latency, and the absence of error bars or multi-seed evaluation makes it difficult to gauge the robustness of the reported margins. Open questions include how the backtracking mechanism scales to real-time or streaming video, and whether the hierarchical structure can be learned end-to-end rather than relying on hand-designed temporal scopes.
