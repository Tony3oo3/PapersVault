## Problem

Existing algorithms for computing equilibria in Mean-Field Games either require strict monotonicity (which excludes common symmetric-transition cases) or only guarantee convergence of time-averaged iterates, as in Fictitious Play. This paper closes the gap by establishing last-iterate convergence (LIC) of a discrete-time algorithm under the weaker, more realistic Lasry-Lions-type (non-strict) monotonicity assumption, without requiring time-averaging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250611/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fully specified discrete-time, finite-state MFG model (state space S, action space A, horizon H, transition kernels P_h, reward functions r_h, initial distribution μ₁) together with a full-support initial policy π⁰ and hyperparameters (KL regularization λ, inner learning rate η, outer iterations N, inner iterations τ). It outputs the last-iterate policy σ^k (not a time average) that converges to the unregularized mean-field equilibrium π*, along with the induced state distribution μ^k = m[σ^k]. The full-support requirement on π⁰ ensures the KL divergence is well-defined throughout the iteration.

## Method

The core algorithm (APP) is a two-level scheme: an outer Proximal Point (PP) loop where each update solves σ^{k+1} = argmax_π {J(μ^{k+1}, π) − λD_{m[π]}(π, σ^k)}, and an inner Regularized Mirror Descent (RMD) loop that approximates this PP step by running τ iterations of a KL-regularized MD update on the associated regularized MFG. The key theoretical insight is that each PP iteration is exactly equivalent to finding the equilibrium of a KL-regularized MFG, and RMD converges to that equilibrium at an exponential rate (Theorem 4.3), overcoming the non-Lipschitz continuity of the Q-function via the regularizing effect of KL divergence. The PP method itself achieves LIC under non-strict monotonicity (Theorem 3.1), proven by applying the Łojasiewicz inequality to the real-analytic objective to handle the iteration-dependent mean field μ^k. Updating the base distribution σ after each outer PP step is essential: without it, the algorithm converges only to a regularized equilibrium (O(λ) away from the true equilibrium), whereas with the update it converges to the unregularized mean-field equilibrium.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: Theorem 3.1 establishes last-iterate convergence (LIC) of the Proximal Point method under non-strict Lasry-Lions monotonicity (previously requiring strict monotonicity), and Theorem 4.3 proves that Regularized Mirror Descent (RMD) converges to the regularized equilibrium at an exponential rate D_{μ*}(ϖ*, π^t) ≤ D_{μ*}(ϖ*, π⁰)·e^{−ληt/2}, improving over prior polynomial bounds. The numerical experiment on the Beach Bar Process benchmark confirms that the proposed APP algorithm monotonically decreases exploitability across outer iterations and converges to the unregularized mean-field equilibrium (vertices of the probability simplex), whereas RMD alone stalls in the interior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main empirical comparison is between APP (Algorithm 1) and RMD (Algorithm 2, from F. Zhang et al. 2023) on the Beach Bar Process. RMD alone, which solves only the KL-regularized MFG, shifts the equilibrium to the interior of the probability simplex and thus fails to recover the unregularized mean-field equilibrium, which in this benchmark lies at the simplex vertices. APP, which wraps RMD inside an outer PP update that periodically refreshes the base distribution σ, successfully converges to the vertices and drives exploitability to zero (Figure 2b). Theoretically, the paper also improves on F. Zhang et al. (2023) and Dong et al. (2025), which established only polynomial convergence rates (O(λ log²T/√T) for time-averaged policies and O(H³/(λt)) for last-iterate) under monotonicity, whereas Theorem 4.3 yields an exponential last-iterate rate under the same assumptions. The PP method (Theorem 3.1) further extends LIC guarantees to non-strictly monotone MFGs, a setting where prior discrete-time results (Pérolat et al. 2022) required strict monotonicity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the Lasry-Lions monotonicity framework for MFGs, the Proximal Point method from convex optimization, and the regularized Mirror Descent line of work (Cui & Koeppl 2021; F. Zhang et al. 2023). It departs from prior LIC results (Pérolat et al. 2022; Hadikhanloo & Silva 2019) that required strict monotonicity or contraction, by establishing LIC under the weaker non-strict monotonicity assumption via a novel use of the Lojasiewicz inequality to handle the policy-dependent mean field. The key conceptual bridge is showing that one PP iteration is equivalent to solving a KL-regularized MFG, which then admits an exponentially convergent RMD solver — a connection not previously exploited in the MFG literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is significant because non-strict monotonicity is the realistic assumption for many MFG instances (e.g., symmetric transitions), and LIC is essential for deep-learning-based MFG solvers where time-averaging is infeasible. However, several important gaps remain: the analysis is model-based (no sample complexity or function-approximation error), the step-size upper bound η* can be small in practice, and LIC for the combined APP algorithm is still conjectural. Extending these guarantees to model-free settings with neural function approximators, and to μ-dependent transition kernels, are natural next steps that would broaden practical applicability.
