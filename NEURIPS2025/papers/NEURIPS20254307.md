## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs for video temporal understanding either rely solely on visual inputs or fuse audio and speech in rigid, non-adaptive ways that degrade when modalities are missing or irrelevant. No prior dataset provides large-scale, long-form video annotations with explicit modality dropout, and no architecture dynamically reweights modality contributions based on the query context, limiting robustness in real-world scenarios where audio, visual, and speech signals are unevenly available.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 64 uniformly sampled video frames, a ±1-second audio segment centered on each frame's timestamp, and a free-form text query, producing either a timestamped segment caption or a moment-retrieval interval. A special <sync> token appended to the vocabulary triggers switching between a dedicated Time Head (for numeric timestamp prediction) and the standard LM Head (for free-form caption text), enabling a single generation pass to interleave temporal and linguistic outputs. During training, videos are resampled at 1 fps to reduce redundancy, while inference retains full frame fidelity.

## Method

TriSense's central mechanism is a Query-Based Connector that first applies Cross-Attention between each modality's compressed features (16 tokens per modality via slot-based compression) and the encoded query, then computes adaptive softmax weights over globally-pooled modality vectors through a single-layer MLP, allowing the model to emphasize the most query-relevant modality and suppress missing or irrelevant ones. The fused representation is combined with a Time Encoder (tokenizing timestamps into 6 character tokens per frame) and fed into a Mistral-7B backbone initialized from TRACE, which performs causal event prediction over a sequence of (timestamp, caption) pairs. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) feature alignment on single-modality data with only the connector and LM head trainable, (2) connector generalization on mixed-modality TriSense-2M data with the Time Encoder and Time Head unfrozen, and (3) instruction tuning on 1.5M mixed samples with the LLM backbone unfrozen while the connector is fixed. The supporting TriSense-2M dataset (2M samples from ~38K long-form videos averaging 905 s) is generated by a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-72B Generator that fuses unimodal captions into omni-modal ones, filtered by a Judger model scoring semantic alignment on a 0–5 scale.

## Results

On the proposed TriSense-2M benchmark, TriSense achieves 1.12 R@IoU=0.5 and 0.42 R@IoU=0.7 on AVS Moment Retrieval, nearly doubling the best omni-modal baseline Qwen2.5-Omni (0.61/0.21) and nearly tripling TRACE (0.39/0.12). For Segment Captioning under the same AVS setting, TriSense scores CIDEr 8.3 and ROUGE-L 20.1, substantially outperforming LongVALE (4.9/16.7) and all vision-only baselines. In zero-shot evaluation on Charades-STA, TriSense reaches 42.3 R@0.5 and 27.6 R@0.7 with only 64 frames, matching or exceeding TRACE-uni (43.7/21.0) while using fewer frames. On the LongVALE benchmark, TriSense's zero-shot R@0.5 of 9.3 slightly surpasses LongVALE's 8.6 despite the latter being trained on that same dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriSense decisively outperforms all baselines on the TriSense-2M benchmark across AVS, VS, and AV modality settings for both Moment Retrieval and Segment Captioning. On AVS-MR, TriSense (1.12 R@0.5) nearly doubles Qwen2.5-Omni (0.61) and nearly triples TRACE (0.39), while on AVS-SC it achieves CIDEr 8.3 versus LongVALE's 4.9. The sole loss is on Visual-Only Moment Retrieval, where TRACE-uni (0.48 R@0.5) slightly edges TriSense (0.43), which the authors attribute to TriSense's optimization for multimodal settings and its use of only 64 frames versus 128 in TRACE. In zero-shot evaluation on Charades-STA, TriSense (42.3 R@0.5, 27.6 R@0.7) is competitive with TRACE-uni (43.7/21.0) and clearly wins on the stricter IoU=0.7 threshold. On the LongVALE benchmark, TriSense's zero-shot R@0.5 of 9.3 marginally exceeds LongVALE's 8.6 (trained on the same data), while on VideoMME general understanding TriSense scores 48.7 versus TRACE-uni's 49.6 using only 55% of the general understanding training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

TriSense builds directly on TRACE's causal event modeling framework and time encoder, reusing its Mistral-7B backbone initialization and temporal prediction paradigm, while incorporating VTG-LLM's slot-based compression for token efficiency. It departs from prior omni-modal temporal models (LongVALE, Qwen2.5-Omni) by introducing a Query-Based Connector that adaptively reweights modality contributions per query, rather than compressing all modalities into a fixed representation or using static positional encodings. The TriSense-2M dataset extends the omni-modal annotation line (VAST, VALOR, LongVALE) by explicitly including modality dropout scenarios and long-form videos (~905s average), addressing a gap those datasets leave unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TriSense-2M dataset (2M samples, 38K long-form videos, explicit modality dropout) provides a much-needed resource for training and evaluating models that must handle real-world scenarios where audio, speech, or visual signals are partially or fully absent. The adaptive modality weighting mechanism is a clean, well-ablated architectural contribution that generalizes across eight modality configurations. However, the model's reliance on only 64 input frames limits its performance on long-video moment retrieval relative to vision-only SOTA, and the LLM-generated caption pipeline (Generator/Judger) may introduce systematic biases that are difficult to fully audit. Open questions include whether the adaptive weighting scales gracefully to more than three modalities and whether the dataset's automated construction pipeline generalizes to domains beyond the InternVid/VAST source material.
