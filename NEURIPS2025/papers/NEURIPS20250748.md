## Problem

Feature-level dropout is the dominant perturbation strategy in semi-supervised medical image segmentation for consistency learning, but its effectiveness hinges on a sensitive dropout-rate hyperparameter: low rates yield negligible regularization while high rates (≥0.7) cause rapid performance collapse. The paper seeks a more stable, interpretable, and controllable perturbation mechanism that avoids this brittle hyperparameter dependency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250748/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D medical image slices cropped to 128×128 pixels (chest CT for the LC dataset, short-axis cardiac cine-MRI for ACDC) along with ground-truth segmentation masks available only on the labeled subset (5% or 10% of the data). It produces two outputs from a shared post-quantization feature space: a per-pixel segmentation mask (primary task) and a reconstructed image (auxiliary self-supervisory signal), both decoded from the same discrete VQ representation.

## Method

VQ-Seg encodes input images into continuous latent features, quantizes them into a large codebook (K=16,384) via vector quantization, and then applies a Quantized Perturbation Module (QPM) that probabilistically transitions each codeword to a neighbor based on learned inter-codeword distances, controlled by a single strength parameter ε. A dual-branch decoder architecture shares the post-VQ space between an image-reconstruction head (L1 loss) and a segmentation head (cross-entropy), using reconstruction as a self-supervisory signal to preserve fine-grained visual detail lost during discretization. A Post-VQ Feature Adapter (PFA) further aligns the quantized features with frozen DINOv2 foundation-model embeddings through patch-wise contrastive learning, recovering high-level semantic information. Training follows a teacher-student paradigm with EMA (α=0.99) and pseudo-labeling, where the teacher generates pseudo-labels on unlabeled data and the student is trained on QPM-perturbed quantized representations; gradients flow through the non-differentiable VQ step via the Straight-Through Estimator. Theoretically, the paper shows via KL-divergence analysis that dropout's perturbation radius grows unboundedly with the dropout rate, whereas QPM's perturbed distribution remains strictly bounded for all ε∈[0,1], guaranteeing numerical stability.

## Results

On the Lung Cancer (LC) dataset with 10% labeled data, VQ-Seg achieves the best Dice of 0.7852, Jaccard of 0.6731, HD95 of 11.6179, and ASD of 4.2094, outperforming the second-best MCNet (Dice 0.7555) by 2.97%. Under the more challenging 5% labeled setting, VQ-Seg still leads with Dice 0.6643 and Jaccard 0.5257, surpassing Unimatch (Dice 0.6493) by 1.5%. On the ACDC cardiac MRI dataset, VQ-Seg reaches Dice 0.9103 and Jaccard 0.8327 at 10% labeled data, approaching the fully supervised nnUNet-F (Dice 0.9185) while using only 10% of the annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQ-Seg outperforms all nine semi-supervised baselines on both the LC and ACDC datasets across all four metrics at both 5% and 10% labeled settings. On LC at 10% labeled, the largest margin is over MCNet (+2.97% Dice) and Unimatch (+3.41% Dice), while the closest competitor on HD95 is UA-MT (11.6724 vs. 11.6179, a narrow 0.0545 gap). At 5% labeled on LC, VQ-Seg leads Unimatch by 1.5% Dice and ABD by 2.29% Dice, with the most substantial boundary-accuracy gain over MCNet (ASD 4.2276 vs. 4.9231). On ACDC at 10% labeled, VQ-Seg surpasses ARCO (Dice 0.9103 vs. 0.9026) and Unimatch (0.9103 vs. 0.8978) by 0.77% and 1.25% respectively, and at 5% labeled it beats Unimatch by 1.42% Dice (0.9057 vs. 0.8915). The fully supervised nnUNet-F still leads on LC (Dice 0.8259) and ACDC (Dice 0.9185), but VQ-Seg at 10% labeled narrows the gap to just 0.73% on LC and 0.82% on ACDC, demonstrating strong annotation efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

VQ-Seg builds directly on the Unimatch v2 architecture (same encoder-decoder backbone) and the vector quantization framework from VQ-VAE, incorporating a codebook mapping mechanism for stable codebook learning. It departs from the dominant consistency-learning paradigm in semi-supervised medical segmentation—where feature-level dropout (as in UA-MT, MCNet, and others) is the standard perturbation mechanism—by replacing stochastic dropout with a structured, distance-aware perturbation over a discrete codebook (QPM). The dual-branch reconstruction-segmentation design and the foundation-model-guided alignment (PFA with DINOv2) are novel combinations not previously explored in the semi-supervised medical segmentation literature, drawing on ideas from self-supervised reconstruction and contrastive alignment in natural image processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-documented pain point—dropout rate sensitivity in consistency learning—with a theoretically grounded alternative (bounded KL divergence in discrete space) that is more interpretable and stable. The new 828-case Lung Cancer CT dataset fills a gap for central-type carcinoma segmentation benchmarks. However, the perturbation is restricted to the discrete VQ space, limiting direct transfer to continuous-feature semi-supervised frameworks, and the foundation model integration adds computational overhead. Open questions include whether the QPM idea can be generalized to continuous latent spaces and whether more efficient (e.g., distilled) foundation model priors can match DINOv2's alignment quality at lower cost.
