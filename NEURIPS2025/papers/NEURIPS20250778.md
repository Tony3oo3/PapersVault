## Problem

Pessimistic Bilevel Optimization (PBO) models adversarial worst-case follower responses but has a complex min-max-min structure that prevents direct application of gradient-based methods developed for optimistic bilevel optimization. The only existing gradient-based PBO method (AdaProx) requires a double-loop procedure and second-order derivative information. This paper asks whether a fully first-order, single-loop gradient-based algorithm for PBO can be designed, and answers affirmatively.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes the upper-level objective F(x,y), lower-level objective f(x,y), and closed convex constraint sets X⊆ℝⁿ and Y⊆ℝᵐ, and produces iterates (xᵏ, yᵏ, zᵏ) converging to a relaxed stationarity point of the PBO value function φ(x) = max_{y∈S(x)} F(x,y). The key challenge is that φ(x) is generally non-smooth, so the algorithm operates on a smooth surrogate φ_{ρ,σ}(x) with dynamically updated penalty ρₖ→∞ and regularization σₖ→0. Convergence is measured via the relaxed stationarity residual ‖xᵏ − Proj_X(xᵏ − αₖ∇φₖ(xᵏ))‖/αₖ and the saddle-point error ‖(yᵏ,zᵏ) − (yₖ*(xᵏ), zₖ*(xᵏ))‖.

## Method

The core idea is to construct a smooth approximation of the non-smooth PBO value function by reformulating φ(x) as a constrained minimax problem, then applying penalization (parameter ρ) to relax the constraint and adding a regularization term (σ/2)‖z‖² plus a coupling term −σ⟨y,z⟩ to ensure strong convexity-concavity in (z,y). This yields φ_{ρ,σ}(x) = min_{z∈Y} max_{y∈Y} ψ_{ρ,σ}(x,y,z), which is continuously differentiable with an explicit gradient formula. SiPBA then performs a single projected gradient ascent-descent step on the minimax subproblem to obtain an inexact saddle point (yᵏ⁺¹, zᵏ⁺¹), which substitutes for the exact saddle point in the gradient formula to produce an inexact descent direction for x. The entire procedure is single-loop with no inner iterations and no Hessian computations, using only first-order gradients of F and f. Theoretical analysis establishes non-asymptotic rates of O(1/K^{1−2s}) for x-stationarity and O(1/K^{1−6p−7q}) for saddle-point error under polynomial parameter schedules, plus a modified ε-subdifferential stationarity guarantee.

## Results

On the synthetic PBO (n=100), SiPBA achieves a minimum relative error of 1.22×10⁻⁶ with 10/10 valid runs in 1.03 s, compared to AdaProx-PD (1.79×10⁻⁷ min error but 87.44 s), AdaProx-SG (3.80×10⁻⁶, 4.07 s), and Scholtes-C/D (1.12×10⁻⁵ / 9.60×10⁻⁵, only 1/10 valid runs). On spam classification across four corpora, SiPBA-trained PBO models achieve the best average accuracy (85.5% Acc / 84.7% F1 when trained on TREC06) and F1 score, outperforming both SQP-trained PBO and single-level baselines. On deep hyper-representation (MNIST/FashionMNIST), SiPBA achieves the highest test accuracy among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdaProx-PD [31], SiPBA is approximately 85× faster (1.03 s vs. 87.44 s) while achieving a comparable minimum relative error (1.22×10⁻⁶ vs. 1.79×10⁻⁷) and a substantially better worst-case error (1.45×10⁻⁶ vs. 1.53×10⁻⁵). Against AdaProx-SG [31], SiPBA is ~4× faster (1.03 s vs. 4.07 s) with 10/10 valid runs versus 9/10 and a tighter max error (1.45×10⁻⁶ vs. 1.02×10⁻⁴). Against the Scholtes-C and Scholtes-D MPCC relaxations [12], SiPBA is ~22–23× faster and achieves 10/10 valid runs versus only 1/10, with max errors orders of magnitude smaller (1.45×10⁻⁶ vs. 0.10 and 1.06). On spam classification, SiPBA-trained PBO models outperform the SQP-solved PBO from [16] in average accuracy and F1 (e.g., 85.5/84.7 vs. 85.1/83.3 when trained on TREC06), and both PBO variants substantially outperform single-level scikit-learn baselines [59] (e.g., 85.5/84.7 vs. 81.0/76.2 for Hinge). On deep hyper-representation, SiPBA achieves the highest test accuracy on both MNIST and FashionMNIST, surpassing the optimistic bilevel baselines AID-FP, AID-CG [29], and PZOBO [62].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the constrained minimax reformulation of the PBO value function introduced by Zeng [71], extending it with a penalization-regularization scheme that yields a smooth surrogate amenable to first-order methods. It departs from the only prior gradient-based PBO algorithm, AdaProx [31], which required a double-loop structure and second-order derivative information; SiPBA eliminates both requirements through a single projected gradient ascent-descent step per iteration. The broader OBO algorithmic toolkit (KKT/MPCC reformulations, value-function constraints) does not transfer to PBO due to the min-max-min structure, which motivates the novel smooth approximation approach rather than a direct adaptation of existing bilevel methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully first-order, single-loop gradient algorithm for PBO, filling a notable gap in the bilevel optimization literature where PBO had lagged far behind OBO in algorithmic development. The smooth approximation framework (penalty + regularization + coupling term) is general and could potentially extend to other non-smooth value function problems. Key limitations include the restriction to deterministic settings (stochastic PBO is left as future work), the requirement that X be compact, and sublinear convergence rates. Open questions include whether the single-loop structure extends to non-convex lower-level problems and whether the approach scales to the very large-scale PBO instances arising in modern ML applications.
