## Problem

In multimodal federated learning, clients often observe different subsets of modalities and may also have missing input features within each modality, causing local models to learn incompatible representation spaces that degrade upon aggregation. Prior work addresses either modality-level heterogeneity or feature-level missingness in isolation, but the combined setting—where both events co-occur across heterogeneous clients—remains largely unaddressed, leaving no mechanism to align or reconfigure representations for clients with arbitrary data-missing patterns.

## Input / Output

Each client receives multimodal signal data (12 ECG modalities for PTB-XL, 5 polysomnographic modalities for Sleep-EDF) where a binary missing matrix φ(D_k) zeros out unavailable modalities and features, parameterized by (p_m, p_s) controlling the fraction of affected samples and the fraction of modalities missing within them. The method outputs a 5-class label per instance and, as an intermediate artifact, a client-specific data-missing profile of τ embedding controls that is communicated to the server for probabilistic aggregation. The Inception Network encoder processes each available modality independently before the reconfiguration and fusion stages.

## Method

PEPSY decomposes each modality's representation into three components—modality-specific (shared learnable embeddings), data-specific (instance features with mean-imputation for missing modalities, regularized by a contrastive loss L_ds that pulls same-instance features together), and missing-pattern (selected from a client's data-missing profile via query-key cosine-similarity matching, constrained to κ relevant embeddings per modality). These are concatenated into a data-complete representation, further regularized by a reconfiguration contrastive loss L_rc that aligns projected representations with full-modality features, and fused via attention-weighted cross-modal combination before a prediction head. On the server side, because data-missing profiles have variable sizes and arbitrary ordering across clients, aggregation is framed as a non-parametric clustering problem solved with PFPT, which dynamically adapts the number of clusters to the system's missingness complexity. A theoretical bound (Theorem 3.1) shows the expected prediction deviation under missing modalities scales as O(μ|S|√(L_ds/(|M|−|S|)² + log|M|²/(|M|−|S|)²)), directly linking the contrastive training loss to robustness.

## Results

On PTB-XL (12 ECG modalities) and Sleep-EDF (5 EEG modalities), PEPSY achieves the highest accuracy in 40/40 IID test cases, outperforming the strongest baseline FedMAC by up to 11.67% on EDF and maintaining a 7.91% lead under severe missing (p_m=0.8, p_s=0.8) on PTB-XL (75.08% vs. 67.17%). Under Non-IID conditions with extreme missingness (p_m=0.8, p_s=0.8), PEPSY reaches 60.40% on PTB-XL versus 45.90% for FedMAC and 27.32% for FedProx, demonstrating up to 36.45% absolute improvement over the weakest baseline. The method also generalizes to mismatched train/test missing statistics, surpassing baselines by up to 14% when client and server missing patterns differ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEPSY outperforms all five baselines across the vast majority of settings. Against FedMAC (the strongest baseline, which handles intra-client feature missing via cross-modal aggregation), PEPSY leads by 1.74–14.49% on PTB-XL and 4.99–11.67% on Sleep-EDF, with the gap widening as missingness increases. FedProx, which ignores missing modalities entirely, degrades most severely under Non-IID conditions (e.g., 27.32% on PTB-XL at p_m=0.8, p_s=0.8) while PEPSY retains 60.40%. MIFL and FedMSplit, designed for inter-client modality heterogeneity, show high variance (±15–22%) and collapse under combined missingness. FedInMM, targeting intra-client feature missing, plateaus near 40% on Sleep-EDF regardless of missing level, indicating it cannot exploit cross-client collaboration. The consistent advantage of PEPSY is attributed to its data-missing profile that reconfigures representations per client and its probabilistic synchronization (PFPT-based) for server-side profile aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEPSY extends the multimodal federated learning (MMFL) literature by unifying two previously separate data-missing events—missing modalities across clients and missing input features within modalities—into a single framework. It builds on PFPT (Weng et al., NeurIPS 2024) for non-parametric server-side aggregation of client profiles, and draws on contrastive learning and query-key attention mechanisms for representation alignment. The key conceptual departure from prior work (FedMSplit, MIFL, FedInMM, FedMAC) is the introduction of a learnable data-missing profile with embedding controls that encode each client's specific missing patterns as reconfiguration signals, rather than attempting imputation or assuming a shared modality set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a realistic and previously unaddressed gap in MMFL where both modality heterogeneity and intra-modality feature missingness co-occur, a common scenario in wearable health monitoring and distributed sensing. The theoretical bound (Theorem 3.1) linking the data-specific contrastive loss to prediction deviation under missing modalities provides a principled justification for the design. However, evaluation is limited to two signal-based datasets (PTBXL, SleepEDF) with a fixed Inception encoder, leaving open questions about scalability to higher-dimensional modalities (images, text), larger client populations, and real-world deployment where missingness patterns are non-stationary over time.
