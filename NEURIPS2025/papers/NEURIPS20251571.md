## Problem

Existing linear CMDP algorithms either allow constraint violations during exploration (e.g., Ghosh et al. [18] achieves sublinear violation regret but not zero episode-wise safety) or incur exponential computational costs. Tabular methods achieve episode-wise zero-violation with Õ(√K) regret but scale with state-space size, making them impractical for large-scale problems. The fundamental open question is whether a computationally efficient linear CMDP algorithm can simultaneously achieve sublinear regret and zero episode-wise constraint violation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a finite-horizon CMDP with a known feature map φ(s,a) ∈ R^d (‖φ‖₂ ≤ 1), known linear reward and utility functions r_h(s,a) = (θ_h^r)ᵀφ(s,a) and u_h(s,a) = (θ_h^u)ᵀφ(s,a), and a strictly safe policy π^sf with margin ξ > 0. Unlike bandit settings where a safe action vector a^sf ∈ R^d suffices for feasibility, here the safe policy is a full policy object, and the constraint is imposed on the expected cumulative utility over the entire episode rather than per-step. The output is a per-episode policy π^(k) computed via backward value iteration with a composite softmax, guaranteed to satisfy V_{P,1}^{π^(k),u}(s_1) ≥ b with high probability.

## Method

OPSE-LCMDP constructs clipped optimistic value functions for reward, pessimistic value functions for utility, and a compensating value function, all estimated via regularized least-squares on linear features. The key policy is a composite softmax π^(k),λ that blends optimistic reward Q̄, compensating Q̄†, and pessimistic utility Q̲ weighted by a Lagrange multiplier λ, with entropy regularization κ for analytical tractability. A bisection search over λ ∈ [0, C_λ] finds the smallest λ satisfying the pessimistic constraint, yielding polynomial per-episode cost independent of |S|. A novel π^sf deployment trigger—deploying the safe policy only when the algorithm is 'unconfident' in its safety (i.e., the bonus-return V_{P,1}^{π^sf,β^(k)}(s_1) exceeds ξ/(4C_u))—ensures the number of safe-policy episodes is logarithmic in K via the elliptical potential lemma. The regret analysis uses a mixture policy (1−α)π^sf + απ^★ to bridge the gap between the pessimistic constraint and the optimistic objective, adapted from tabular CMDP techniques to the linear setting.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4) establishes that OPSE-LCMDP achieves a regret bound of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(H^2\sqrt{d^3K}) + \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^3H^4\xi^{-2}) + \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(H^4\xi^{-1}\sqrt{d^5K})$ while guaranteeing zero episode-wise constraint violation for all episodes. This is the first linear CMDP algorithm to simultaneously achieve sublinear regret, episode-wise safety, and polynomial computational cost (independent of state-space size $|\mathcal{S}|$). Numerical experiments in Appendix F confirm sublinear regret with zero violation across multiple linear CMDP environments, while the prior state-of-the-art (Ghosh et al. [18]) exhibits positive violation regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ghosh et al. [18] (the prior state-of-the-art linear CMDP algorithm), OPSE-LCMDP achieves zero episode-wise constraint violation, whereas Ghosh et al. [18] only guarantees sublinear *violation regret* (i.e., cumulative violations grow sublinearly but are not zero in every episode). However, OPSE-LCMDP pays a price in the regret exponent: $\sqrt{d^5H^8}$ versus $\sqrt{d^3H^4}$ for Ghosh et al. [18], an extra $dH^2$ factor. Crucially, OPSE-LCMDP runs in polynomial time per episode (via bisection search over $\lambda$), while Ghosh et al. [18] incurs an exponential $K^H$ computational cost. Against DOPE [8] (tabular), both methods achieve zero episode-wise violation, but DOPE's LP-based approach scales with $|\mathcal{S}|$, making it infeasible for large state spaces, whereas OPSE-LCMDP's cost is polynomial in $d$, $H$, and $A$ and independent of $|\mathcal{S}|$. The paper notes that the $\xi^{-1}$ dependence in the regret is shared by all existing safe RL algorithms and whether it can be improved to $\xi^0$ remains open.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimistic-pessimistic exploration framework from tabular CMDPs (Liu et al. 2021, Bura et al. 2022) to the linear function approximation setting, where LP-based methods become computationally infeasible. It builds on the softmax policy and dual λ-search machinery of Ghosh et al. (2022, 2024) but introduces two key departures: a confidence-triggered safe-policy deployment rule (replacing the infeasibility-based trigger) and a bisection search over λ that exploits monotonicity of the softmax value function to avoid the exponential K^H cost of prior linear CMDP methods. The linear constrained bandit warm-up (Section 2) adapts the safe bandit framework of Pacchiano et al. (2021) to handle policies rather than single safe actions, which is the critical conceptual shift needed for episode-wise safety.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to simultaneously achieve sublinear regret, zero episode-wise constraint violation, and polynomial computational cost in linear CMDPs — a combination that was previously thought to require either constraint violations (Ghosh et al. 2024) or exponential computation. The ξ^{-1} dependence in the regret bound remains unresolved across all safe RL literature, and extending to multi-constraint settings (where λ becomes a vector and monotonicity breaks) is identified as a major open problem. The d^5 H^8 dependence is notably looser than the d^3 H^4 of unconstrained LSVI-UCB, and whether this gap is fundamental or an artifact of the analysis is unclear. For practitioners, the result validates that safe exploration in large state spaces is theoretically tractable without sacrificing regret guarantees.
