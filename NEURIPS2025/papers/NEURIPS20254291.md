## Problem

Expressive variational families like normalizing flows lack practical convergence guarantees—their theoretical assurances hold only at the intractable global optimum—while MCMC is asymptotically exact but not a variational family. MixFlows bridged this gap by averaging pushforwards under an ergodic, measure-preserving map, but constructing such exact maps for continuous targets proved intractable: ODE discretization destroys measure preservation, Metropolis-Hastings corrections break invertibility, and ergodicity is nearly impossible to prove. This paper resolves these limitations by deriving exact, invertible, measure-preserving flow maps directly from general involutive MCMC kernels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unnormalized target density π(x), a reference distribution q₀ (a mean-field Gaussian trained for 10K Adam steps), and standard MCMC kernel hyperparameters (e.g., leapfrog step size and number of steps for HMC, or step size for RWMH). It produces a variational density q_T(x) evaluable in closed form via the simplified ratio π(x)·(1/T)Σ(q₀/π)(f⁻ᵗ(x)), i.i.d. samples from q_T, and downstream quantities including ELBO estimates and importance-sampling estimates of the normalization constant log Z. A key practical detail is that density evaluation avoids explicit Jacobian tracking by exploiting the measure-preserving property, and the frozen random parameter sequences (θ₁,…,θ_T) are cached once per flow construction.

## Method

The core construction augments the involutive MCMC state (x, v) with two uniform variables u_v ∈ [0,1]ᵈ and u_a ∈ [0,1] that explicitly encode the randomness in auxiliary-variable sampling and the accept/reject decision, yielding an invertible, exactly π-measure-preserving map f_θ for each random parameter θ = (θ_v, θ_a) ~ Unif[0,1]ᵈ⁺¹. Invertibility of the accept/reject step is achieved by exploiting the self-inverse property of the involution g to recover the MH ratio from the output state alone. From this single IRF, four variational families are derived: (i) improved homogeneous MixFlows (fixing θ to a constant), (ii) IRF MixFlows (mixture of pushforwards through a frozen i.i.d. sequence, with O(T²) density cost), (iii) backward IRF MixFlows (reversing the composition order to reduce density cost to O(T)), and (iv) ensemble IRF MixFlows (averaging endpoints of M independent T-step trajectories, trading bias in T against variance in M for potentially geometric convergence). All four families converge to π in total variation under Assumption 2.2—that the induced Markov kernel P admits π as its unique invariant distribution—which is substantially weaker than the pointwise ergodicity of individual flow maps required by prior MixFlow theory.

## Results

On four synthetic 2D targets (Banana, Neal's funnel, Cross, Warped Gaussian), the IRF flows achieve ELBO and log Z estimates near 0 (the perfect value for normalized targets) consistently across 32 runs, while RealNVP and NSF exhibit high variability with extreme outliers; on the Funnel target, 10 of 15 RealNVP runs and all NSF runs diverged during training. Per-sample importance sampling ESS is orders of magnitude higher for IRF flows than for either normalizing flow. On four real-data benchmarks (TReg d=4, Brownian d=32, SparseReg d=83, LGCP d=1600), the IRF flows match or modestly improve upon the best-tuned RealNVP and NSF in both ELBO and log Z accuracy, while normalizing flow training is orders of magnitude more expensive (LGCP normalizing flows did not finish within 48 hours). Against NUTS (10K iterations, 5K warmup), IRF flows outperform on two models and are slightly worse on the other two, at generally faster computation time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **RealNVP** (6 layers, 50K Adam steps), the IRF flows win decisively on all four metrics (ELBO, log Z, ESS, computation time) across all eight benchmarks. RealNVP shows high run-to-run variability and training instability (10/15 runs diverged on Funnel), while IRF flows remain stable and near-optimal. Against **Neural Spline Flow** (NSF), the gap is even wider: all NSF runs diverged on the Funnel target, and NSF consistently produces worse ELBO and log Z estimates with lower ESS than both RealNVP and the IRF flows. Against **NUTS** (10,000 iterations with 5,000 warmup), the comparison is more nuanced: IRF flows outperform NUTS on two of four real-data models (lower maximum absolute error in coordinate-wise posterior standard deviations) and are slightly worse on the other two, but do so at generally faster wall-clock time. The paper explicitly notes the goal is not to outperform MCMC but to construct a variational family with MCMC-like asymptotic exactness, which the IRF flows achieve while retaining tractable density evaluation and i.i.d. sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MixFlow framework (Xu et al. 2022), which required a single deterministic ergodic and measure-preserving map to guarantee asymptotic exactness—a condition that is extremely difficult to satisfy for continuous targets. The key departure is replacing this single map with an iterated random function (IRF) system derived from general involutive MCMC kernels (HMC, RWMH, MALA), where the randomness is made explicit via uniform auxiliary variables and the Metropolis-Hastings accept/reject step is rendered invertible by exploiting the self-inverse property of the involution. This connects the variational flow literature to the IRF/random dynamical systems tradition (Diaconis & Freedman 1999) and the earlier work on driving MCMC with dependent random streams (Murray & Elliott 2012), while also subsuming DAIS/DSMC methods as a special case. The theoretical analysis leverages the Birkhoff ergodic theorem and the random ergodic theorem to establish total variation convergence under the significantly weaker assumption of a unique invariant distribution rather than ergodicity of a specific flow map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves the central practical limitation of MixFlow—no general construction of exact e.m.p. maps existed for continuous targets—by providing a generic recipe: any involutive MCMC kernel yields an exact, invertible, measure-preserving IRF that can serve as a variational flow layer. The four resulting flow families (homogeneous, IRF, backward IRF, ensemble) offer practitioners a spectrum of bias-variance-cost tradeoffs, all with provable TV convergence and no requirement for optimal tuning. Open questions include characterizing convergence rates beyond the O(1/T) ergodic-averaging bound, pairing the framework with recent auto-tuning MCMC (AutoStep, autoMALA, GIST) for truly tuning-free exact flows, and understanding how performance scales in very high dimensions (the 1600-dim LGCP experiment is encouraging but limited). The ensemble variant's geometric-rate potential (O(ρ^T ∨ 1/√M)) is promising but only heuristically characterized.
