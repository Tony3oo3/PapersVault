## Problem

Existing video temporal grounding models treat all text tokens uniformly during cross-modal attention, causing them to over-rely on the [EOS] token's global sentence semantics while underutilizing word-level cues essential for fine-grained temporal alignment. The paper demonstrates empirically that attention maps concentrate almost entirely on [EOS], suppressing localized word signals (e.g., 'red jacket'), and that no prior architecture explicitly disentangles global and local textual semantics for structured video-text grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253094/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted video clip features (from CLIP+SlowFast, InternVideo2, or I3D encoders, without fine-tuning) and a natural language query tokenized with [SOS]/[EOS] markers. It produces two outputs jointly: normalized start/end timestamps for moment retrieval (predicted at multiple temporal resolutions via a 1D convolutional feature pyramid) and per-clip saliency scores for highlight detection. The text side is split into the single [EOS] embedding and the remaining word-token sequence, which are routed through separate branches rather than processed as a flat token list.

## Method

DualGround is a dual-branch architecture that structurally separates sentence-level and phrase-level semantics. The sentence-level path uses only the [EOS] token with Adaptive Cross Attention, where learnable dummy tokens act as attention sinks for irrelevant clips, allowing semantically aligned clips to attend sharply to the [EOS] key. The phrase-level path clusters word tokens into N semantically coherent phrases via a Recurrent Phrase Generator (conditioned on [EOS] and prior phrases) refined by Slot Attention, then computes dense phrase-clip context embeddings via Hadamard product of projected phrase and video features, aggregated by a reconstructed global token P_[EOS]. The two branch outputs are fused by simple addition and decoded through a multi-scale temporal feature pyramid (inherited from FlashVTG/R2-Tuning) for moment boundary prediction. Training combines focal+L1 moment loss, ranking+contrastive highlight loss, and two phrase-level regularizers: a Distinct Query Attention loss enforcing orthogonality across phrase attention distributions, and an InfoNCE-based [EOS] reconstruction loss aligning the phrase-derived global token with the original [EOS] embedding.

## Results

On QVHighlights validation with InternVideo2 features (fair comparison), DualGround achieves R1@0.5 = 73.48, R1@0.7 = 58.97, and mAP = 53.26 for moment retrieval, and VG-mAP = 44.12 / VG-Hit@1 = 71.62 for highlight detection, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. On Charades-STA with IV2, it reaches R1@0.5 = 70.67 and R1@0.7 = 50.33. On TVSum highlight detection, it attains an average mAP of 88.1, matching the best prior method (TR-DETR) and slightly exceeding FlashVTG (88.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualGround outperforms all four baselines on QVHighlights validation (IV2 backbone): it beats FlashVTG by +2.0 on R1@0.5, +2.91 on R1@0.7, and +0.65 on mAP; beats TR-DETR by +1.76 on R1@0.5 and +3.04 on R1@0.7; and beats CG-DETR by +3.42 on R1@0.5 and +3.10 on R1@0.7. The gains are most pronounced at the stricter IoU threshold (R1@0.7), indicating improved temporal precision rather than merely broader predictions. On Charades-STA (IV2), DualGround edges out FlashVTG by +0.35 (R1@0.5) and +0.46 (R1@0.7), and surpasses CG-DETR by +0.27 and +1.93 respectively, confirming cross-domain generalization. On TVSum highlight detection, DualGround ties TR-DETR at 88.1 average mAP and marginally exceeds FlashVTG (88.0), while clearly outperforming CG-DETR (86.8) and earlier methods (UMT 83.1, QD-DETR 85.0). The consistent but modest margins over the strongest baseline (FlashVTG) suggest the dual-path phrase modeling provides a reliable but incremental architectural improvement over the multi-scale decoding framework it builds upon.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualGround builds directly on the multi-scale temporal pyramid decoding of FlashVTG and R2-Tuning, and adopts the Adaptive Cross Attention (ACA) mechanism with learnable dummy tokens from CG-DETR for its sentence-level path. Its key departure from prior VTG models (CG-DETR, TR-DETR, FlashVTG) is the structural disentanglement of text tokens: rather than treating all tokens uniformly in cross-modal attention, it routes the [EOS] token through a dedicated sentence-level branch and clusters word tokens into phrase-level units via Recurrent Phrase Generation and Slot Attention. This positions the work at the intersection of the DETR-based VTG lineage and the text-centric grounding line (e.g., Keyword-DETR, LGI), but introduces a principled two-level semantic hierarchy that prior methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that current VTG models exhibit near-perfect correlation (~0.97 Pearson) between [EOS] and word-token attentions, and that [EOS]-only inputs can match or exceed full sequences under strong backbones like InternVideo2, challenges the implicit assumption that word tokens contribute meaningfully to fine-grained grounding in flat-attention architectures. As vision-language models scale with richer text encoders, the [EOS] token will likely become even more dominant, making explicit token-role separation increasingly critical. Open questions include whether the fixed phrase count (N=3–4) can be made adaptive to query structure, whether the approach extends to audio-visual grounding, and whether the phrase-level path's benefit persists as backbone text encoders become stronger.
