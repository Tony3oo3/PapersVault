## Problem

Reconstructing millimeter-level 3D geometry from radio frequency signals is fundamentally harder than from RGB or LiDAR because RF sensing is lensless (no physical filter excludes irrelevant signals), reflections are dominated by specular rather than diffuse scattering, and volumetric rendering requires full 3D space sampling with cubic complexity. Prior neural RF reconstruction methods either relied on simulated data or targeted far-range large-scale scenes, leaving near-range high-resolution object reconstruction unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw FMCW chirp signals from a 77 GHz TI 1843BOOST mmWave radar (3.59 GHz bandwidth, 0.04 m range resolution) collected across 17–68 scanning planes (each 0.14 m × 0.25 m, antenna spacing ~λ/4–λ/2) positioned by a Franka Research 3 robotic arm, along with the 3D positions of all antennas. It outputs a learned signed distance function (SDF) representing the object's surface geometry, from which meshes can be extracted, and can additionally render matched filter heatmaps at novel viewpoints for view synthesis evaluation.

## Method

GeRaF introduces three key mechanisms to make differentiable RF volumetric rendering tractable: (1) a differentiable matched filter that correlates received signals with ideal reference chirps to suppress noise while preserving informative reflections; (2) a physics-based rendering pipeline modeling round-trip specular reflections via a shifted Lambertian model, free-space path loss (1/u⁴ power decay), and SDF-derived surface normals; and (3) a lensless sampling strategy that computes opaque density and transmittance once along a shared primary ray direction and reuses them across all antennas, with a proven correction (Eq. 7) that adjusts transmittance using only the sigmoid-SDF values at ray origins. A Signal Tracing Bank further reduces cost by processing antenna patches per iteration and caching results. The neural model comprises an 8-layer SDF MLP, a 4-layer reflectivity MLP, and a single global trainable power parameter, trained end-to-end with L2 loss on rendered vs. ground-truth MF heatmaps and a dynamic loss masking strategy that excludes points where low signal power is due to geometric misalignment rather than absent geometry.

## Results

GeRaF outperforms the Matched Filter (MF) baseline across all 10 test objects on both F1-Score and Chamfer Distance (CD). On non-occluded objects, F1 improves from 0.44–0.80 (MF) to 0.71–0.97 (GeRaF), with the largest gain on the Bottle (0.44→0.97) and Wrench (0.52→0.88). Chamfer Distance drops substantially, e.g., from 0.29 mm to 0.07 mm on the Wrench and from 0.35 mm to 0.05 mm on the Bottle. Under occlusion, GeRaF still improves F1 (e.g., Knife*: 0.45→0.83) and CD (e.g., Knife*: 0.84→0.26 mm), though the margin narrows for the Elephant* (F1 0.42→0.52). Novel view synthesis achieves ~30 dB PSNR on held-out radar views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Matched Filter (MF) summation: the 3D MF heatmap is thresholded to produce a point cloud, which is then converted to a mesh via Poisson Surface Reconstruction. GeRaF wins on every object and every metric. On F1-Score, GeRaF improves by 0.11–0.53 points over MF (e.g., Bottle: 0.44→0.97; Wrench: 0.52→0.88; Knife*: 0.45→0.83). On Chamfer Distance, GeRaF reduces error by 0.01–0.58 mm (e.g., Wrench: 0.29→0.07 mm; Bottle: 0.35→0.05 mm; Knife*: 0.84→0.26 mm). The advantage is most pronounced for non-occluded objects with strong specular reflections (Bottle, Wrench) and remains consistent under occlusion, though the gap narrows for the Elephant* where F1 only improves from 0.42 to 0.52. No other learned or classical 3D reconstruction baselines are included, as the paper positions itself as the first method to perform millimeter-level RF geometry reconstruction in real-world settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeRaF adapts the NeuS [55] SDF-based neural implicit surface framework and the NeRF [39] differentiable volumetric rendering paradigm to the fundamentally different physics of radio frequency sensing. It departs from prior RF reconstruction work (Radar Fields, DART, NeRF2, SPINR) that targets far-field or large-scale scenes using beamforming approximations, by operating in the near-field where wave propagation is non-parallel and requires full matched-filter processing. The key conceptual departure from vision-based methods is the replacement of lens-based 1D ray sampling with lensless 3D volumetric sampling, necessitating new strategies for alpha blending and computational tractability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that millimeter-level 3D surface geometry can be reconstructed from raw mmWave radar signals via end-to-end neural optimization, opening applications like non-invasive package inspection and through-occlusion sensing that are impossible with RGB or LiDAR. However, significant limitations remain: evaluation is limited to a handful of metal objects scanned on a single pitch axis, the reflection model relies on a simplified shifted-Lambertian approximation rather than a full RF BRDF, and training requires 32 hours on an H100 GPU. Open questions include extending to non-metallic materials, handling multi-layer surfaces, improving the RF reflection model, and reducing computational cost to practical levels.
