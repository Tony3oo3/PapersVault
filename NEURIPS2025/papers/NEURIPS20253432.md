## Problem

Local PPR methods like APPR achieve O(1/(αε)) time complexity, but the underlying optimization problem is strongly convex with condition number 1/α, suggesting an O(1/√α) accelerated bound should be achievable. However, standard acceleration techniques introduce momentum terms that break the monotonically decreasing ℓ1-norm of the gradient—a property on which all existing local algorithms critically depend to maintain sublinear runtime. This paper resolves an open conjecture by Fountoulakis and Yang [19] and Zhou et al. [52] on whether a provably accelerated local method with O(1/√α) dependence exists.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an undirected, connected graph via its adjacency and degree matrices, a single source node s, a damping factor α ∈ (0,1), and a precision parameter ε. It produces a sparse ε-approximation π̂ satisfying ‖D⁻¹(π̂ − π)‖∞ ≤ ε, where D is the degree matrix. Crucially, the algorithm is local: at any point it only accesses nodes within a dynamically maintained active set (via a queue), never requiring a full pass over the graph. The method is parameter-free—no user-tuned hyperparameters beyond α and ε are needed—and the number of outer iterations is bounded by Õ(1/√α).

## Method

The AESP framework wraps a Catalyst-style accelerated outer loop around a local inexact proximal point inner solver. At each outer iteration t, the algorithm solves a shifted linear system (Q + ηI)z = b⁽ᵗ⁻¹⁾ using either LocGD (batch gradient descent on active nodes) or LocAPPR (per-coordinate online updates), where the shift η = 1 − 2α reduces the per-solve condition number from 1/α to a constant (2/3 for α < 0.5). The outer loop maintains momentum via y⁽ᵗ⁾ = x⁽ᵗ⁾ + βₜ(x⁽ᵗ⁾ − x⁽ᵗ⁻¹⁾), and the inner solver is guaranteed to return a point in the φₜ-ball of the proximal objective, preserving the monotonic decrease of the D^{1/2}-scaled gradient ℓ1-norm. This yields Õ(1/√α) outer iterations, each performing a short evolving set process with per-round cost Õ(vol(Sₜ)/γ̄ₜ) ≤ Õ(min{R²/ε², 2m}), giving an overall bound of Õ(R²/(√α·ε²)) that is independent of graph size when 1/ε² ≪ m.

## Results

The paper evaluates AESP-LOCAPPR and AESP-LOCGD on four large-scale real-world graphs (com-dblp, ogb-mag240m, ogbn-papers100M, com-friendster, wiki-en21) with up to n=244M nodes and m=1.728B edges, at α=0.01 and ε=10^{-6}. AESP-LOCAPPR achieves the fastest convergence in the early stages due to its online per-coordinate updates, significantly outperforming APPR, APPR-opt, and LocGD baselines. When α is varied from 10^{-3} to 10^{-1} on com-dblp (ε=0.1/n), both AESP variants reduce the number of operations and running time substantially compared to non-accelerated methods, with the speedup growing as α decreases. The theoretical guarantee of Õ(1/√α) outer iterations (vs. O(1/α) for standard methods) is empirically validated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AESP-LOCAPPR and AESP-LOCGD against three baselines: APPR [4], APPR-opt (APPR with optimal step size 2/(1+α)), and LocGD (with optimal step size 2/(1+α)). AESP-LOCAPPR is the most efficient variant overall, benefiting from its online per-coordinate updates that avoid redundant gradient computations. All AESP-based methods achieve much faster convergence in the early stages than the baselines, a direct consequence of solving shifted linear systems with a constant condition number (τ=2/3 when α<0.5 and η=1-2α) rather than the original ill-conditioned system. The speedup over standard local solvers is most pronounced when the damping factor α is small (e.g., α=10^{-3}), where the Õ(1/√α) vs. O(1/α) iteration gap translates into the largest absolute gains in operations and wall-clock time. The paper does not report a single unified numerical table; instead, results are presented as convergence curves (Fig. 4) and speedup plots (Fig. 5), showing consistent wins for AESP methods across all tested graphs and α values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: local PPR algorithms (APPR by Andersen et al., the Fountoulakis variational formulation), first-order acceleration (Catalyst by Lin et al.), and the locally evolving set process framework (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2024). The key departure from prior work is that standard accelerated methods (Nesterov momentum, Chebyshev iterations) break the monotonically decreasing ℓ₁-gradient property that local algorithms rely on, yielding only Õ(m/√α) global bounds. AESP resolves this tension by embedding a Catalyst-style outer loop around short local evolving set processes that solve shifted linear systems with constant condition number, thereby preserving monotonicity at each stage while accumulating acceleration across stages. This directly resolves the open conjecture from Fountoulakis & Yang [19] and Zhou et al. [52] that Õ(1/√α) local complexity is achievable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that an ε-approximation of PPR can be computed in Õ(R²/(√α·ε²)) time, independent of graph size when 1/ε² ≪ m, which is a meaningful sublinear guarantee for the regime where local methods are most useful. Two limitations temper the impact: the constant R (bounding the ratio of initial gradient norms across outer iterations) is not universally bounded across all graph configurations, and the 1/ε² dependence remains a gap from the conjectured 1/ε. Open questions include whether R can be eliminated via adaptive restarts or simplex constraints, whether the 1/ε² can be tightened, and whether the framework extends cleanly to the ℓ₁-regularized variational form without non-negativity assumptions. The framework also suggests improvements to bidirectional PPR and other single-source graph diffusion problems.
