## Problem

Existing long-context LLM approaches (input compression, architectural modifications, external retrieval) overlook a fundamental internal limitation: transient FFN activations cause information to decay over long sequences, and unstructured FFN weights fragment semantics across tokens. No prior method targets these two FFN-specific failure modes with a principled, training-free mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252869/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained LLM (e.g., Qwen-2-7B-Instruct, Llama-2-7B-chat) and a long-context text sequence (up to 200K tokens). The AMB consumes intermediate FFN activation tensors of shape (B×L×d_ff) at selected layers and produces enhanced activations of the same shape, while the CE module takes the pretrained FFN weight matrices W1 and W2 and outputs in-place reordered versions with identical dimensions. The final output is a standard generated text response; no architectural shape changes or additional parameters are introduced.

## Method

PaceLLM introduces two training-free, brain-inspired mechanisms targeting FFN layers. First, the Activation Memory Bank (AMB) emulates prefrontal cortex persistent activity by maintaining a bounded key-value store of historical FFN activations with usage counters; at each step it computes cosine similarity between current activations and stored keys, retrieves top-k similar and bottom-k' dissimilar entries, and fuses them into the output via a three-tier threshold strategy (direct reuse, averaging, or passthrough), updating memory via LRU eviction for low-similarity cases. Second, Cortical Expert (CE) Clustering emulates cortical modularity by applying KMeans-Constrained clustering on L2-normalized rows of the gated projection matrix W1 to partition neurons into K equal-sized expert clusters, then reordering W1 by rows and W2 by columns according to cluster assignments to create a structured, expert-specialized FFN layout. Both components are model-agnostic, require no retraining, and are applied sparsely across selected layers to balance performance and computational overhead.

## Results

In the low-cost fine-tuning setting on LongBench (Qwen-2-7B-Instruct), PaceLLM (Activation Beacon+CE+PA) achieves 42.62 on Single-Document QA, 46.55 on Multi-Document QA, 28.74 on Summarization, 70.56 on Few-Shot Learning, and 67.52 on Code, outperforming all baselines. On ∞-Bench, the method yields 12.5% and 17.5% absolute gains on En.Dialogue (15.5 vs. 3.00) and En.Multi-Choice (64.19 vs. 46.72) over Activation Beacon. In the Needle-In-A-Haystack test, PaceLLM extends reliable retrieval to 200K tokens, surpassing Activation Beacon's 128K limit. On MMLU (short-context), performance is maintained at 70.51 average, confirming no degradation in general language understanding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LongLLMLingua [16], PaceLLM (AB+CE+PA) achieves substantially higher scores across all LongBench categories, with the largest gap on Multi-Document QA (46.55 vs. 20.30, a 26-point margin) and Single-Document QA (42.62 vs. 24.70). Against SnapKV [20], PaceLLM leads on every task, most notably on MQA (46.55 vs. 37.60) and FSL (70.56 vs. 67.10), while the Code gap is smaller (67.52 vs. 60.30). Against the closest baseline, Activation Beacon [45], PaceLLM improves SQA by 2.12 points, MQA by 6.25 points (the headline 6% gain), Summarization by 1.94 points, FSL by 2.16 points, and Code by 1.12 points. On ∞-Bench, the advantage over Activation Beacon is even more pronounced: 12.5 points on En.Dialogue and 17.5 points on En.Multi-Choice. In the NIAH test, PaceLLM extends the measurable context window from 128K to 200K tokens, a 56% increase in reliable retrieval range. On MMLU, both methods perform comparably (70.51 vs. 70.25), confirming that the long-context gains do not come at the cost of short-context generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaceLLM carves new ground at the intersection of brain-inspired interpretability and long-context LLM optimization. While prior brain-inspired work (HippoRAG, HMT, Larimar) introduces external memory modules or attention-level mechanisms, and prior long-context methods (Activation Beacon, SnapKV, LongLLMLingua) operate at the token or KV-cache level, this paper targets the FFN layer specifically—treating it as an underexploited site for both persistent activation caching (analogous to prefrontal working memory) and weight reorganization (analogous to cortical modularity). The FFN-as-key-value-memory insight from Geva et al. provides the theoretical grounding, but the activation-level memory bank combined with constrained KMeans expert clustering applied to FFN weights is a novel combination not present in prior long-context or neuro-inspired literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature makes this practically accessible: practitioners can apply it to any pretrained model without retraining, and the 200K-token NIAH result (vs. 128K for Activation Beacon) demonstrates tangible context-length extension. However, the AMB introduces non-trivial storage and compute overhead (×1.32–1.37 over Activation Beacon), and the gains, while consistent, are moderate (1–6% on most LongBench subtasks). Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether the brain-inspired framing yields genuinely new design principles beyond the memory-bank-plus-reordering mechanism, and whether the method transfers to multi-modal or embodied settings as the authors speculate.
