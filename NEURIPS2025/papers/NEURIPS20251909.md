## Problem

Most inference-time scaling methods for diffusion models (Best-of-N, Beam Search, SMC, Search-over-Path) execute a fixed number of denoising steps regardless of instance difficulty, making it impossible to allocate more computation to harder inputs or stop early on easier ones. This paper formalizes the challenge of adaptive inference-time scaling—dynamically adjusting computational effort per instance during inference—and identifies the rigidity of unidirectional, fixed-schedule denoising as the core limitation.

## Input / Output

The method requires a pre-trained diffusion model and a task-specific reward/verifier function defined on the clean-sample space x₀; no retraining is needed. It operates across diverse modalities and resolutions: 512×512 text-conditioned images (Stable Diffusion v1.5), 9×9 Sudoku grids with 17–28 givens, pixel mazes of sizes 11×11 to 15×15 (trained only on 4×4 to 6×6), 3D molecular conformations with up to 9 heavy atoms (QM9), and 1000+ step navigation trajectories (OGBench PointMaze). The output is a single refined sample in the respective domain, with the number of refinement cycles (the 'thinking time') varying per instance.

## Method

ABCD reframes diffusion inference as a bi-directional search process with three coupled components. (1) Cyclic Diffusion Search alternates fast DDIM denoising (T→0), reward-based top-K selection, J-fold replication, re-noising to go-back timesteps, and re-denoising, enabling the model to escape local minima by revisiting earlier diffusion stages. (2) Automatic Exploration-Exploitation Balancing replaces a single fixed go-back step with a 'temperature pool' of M uniformly spaced go-back levels; replicas are distributed across all levels in parallel, and suboptimal temperatures are naturally pruned by the selection step, so the system implicitly discovers the right exploration depth per instance and per cycle. (3) Adaptive Thinking Time provides a principled stopping rule: inference terminates when all top-K particles originate from the lowest (zero) temperature for κ consecutive cycles, signaling that global exploration no longer yields improvements and only local refinement remains. The key distinction from prior work is that both the exploration depth and the total number of cycles are determined adaptively per instance rather than set as fixed hyperparameters.

## Results

On the Sudoku harder test set (17–28 givens), ABCD achieves 99.6% accuracy at 0.92 s wall-clock time, surpassing SoP (95.8% at 1.33 s), BS (84% at 0.54 s), SMC (74% at 0.32 s), and BoN (66% at 1.32 s). On OGBench Point Maze Giant, ABCD is the only method to reach 100% success (at 202 s), while SoP peaks at 96%, BS at 72%, SMC at 28%, and BoN at 76%. For 3D molecule generation on QM9, ABCD attains ~0.99 molecular stability in ~20 s versus SoP's peak of 0.94, and for text-to-image generation it reaches a human-preference score of 0.2764 in under 81 s compared to SoP's >300 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABCD outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated task. Against SoP [16] (the closest competitor, which also uses bi-directional noising), ABCD gains +3.8 pp on Sudoku (99.6% vs 95.8%), +4 pp on OGBench Giant (100% vs 96%), and +5 pp on molecule stability (0.99 vs 0.94), while also reaching target quality in substantially less wall-clock time (e.g., <81 s vs >300 s for HPS 0.2764 on text-to-image). Against Diffusion Beam Search [17], the gap is even wider: ABCD reaches 100% on OGBench Giant in 202 s whereas BS plateaus at 72% even after 1014 s. Sequential Monte Carlo [24, 31] and Best-of-N [36] are the weakest baselines, peaking at 28% and 76% respectively on OGBench Giant, and at 74% and 66% on Sudoku—far below ABCD's near-perfect scores. The consistent pattern is that ABCD's adaptive exploration (multiple go-back temperatures) and adaptive stopping (ATT) allow it to allocate more compute to hard instances while terminating early on easy ones, a flexibility that fixed-schedule baselines cannot replicate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inference-time scaling literature for diffusion models (Best-of-N, Beam Search, SMC/FKD, Search-over-Path) by reframing inference as a bi-directional cyclic search rather than a fixed unidirectional denoising trajectory. While SoP [16] already introduces limited backward (noising) steps, ABCD generalizes this into a full cyclic process with a pool of go-back temperatures (AEEB) and a principled convergence-based stopping rule (ATT), making both exploration depth and total compute instance-adaptive. The reward-guided generation objective (p(x) ∝ exp(r(x)/α)·p_pre(x)) is shared with prior work, but the search structure and termination logic are the core departures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: fixed-budget inference methods either waste compute on easy instances or under-allocate on hard ones, and the adaptive stopping criterion (monitoring which temperature level top-K particles originate from) offers a simple, parameter-light solution. Limitations include untested scalability to very high-dimensional outputs (high-res video), absence of theoretical guarantees, and reliance on a task-specific verifier. A promising open direction the authors identify is amortizing the search into a learned policy that predicts adaptive strategies from input context, which could eliminate online search cost entirely.
