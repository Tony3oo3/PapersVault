## Problem

In standard residual connections, the module output is added directly to the input stream, but this output contains a component parallel to the stream that merely rescales (or can anti-align with) the existing direction, potentially wasting module capacity on modulation rather than learning novel features. The paper asks whether explicitly discarding this parallel component and updating only with the orthogonal part can improve generalization and training stability without altering the identity skip path.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252409/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images at dataset-specific resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) and outputs a class label via a final linear head. The orthogonal projection is computed at every residual connection: in ViTs it is applied feature-wise over the model dimension d (per token), while in ResNetV2 it can be applied either feature-wise over the channel dimension C or globally over all spatial-channel dimensions, with the choice controlled by a reduction set D in the projection formula.

## Method

The Orthogonal Residual Update replaces the standard x_{n+1} = x_n + f(σ(x_n)) with x_{n+1} = x_n + f_⊥(x_n), where f_⊥(x_n) = f(σ(x_n)) − s_n·x_n and s_n = ⟨x_n, f(σ(x_n))⟩ / (‖x_n‖² + ε) is the scalar projection of the module output onto the stream. This Gram-Schmidt-style projection costs only O(sd) FLOPs per block (≈6sd for the projection plus ≈2s for normalization), adding ≤2% throughput overhead on ViTs and ~3–13% on ResNetV2. Geometrically, the update is a first-order Taylor approximation of the exponential map on the learned feature manifold, and from a Neural ODE perspective it yields a flow that (in the ε→0 limit) preserves the stream norm, acting as near-rotations rather than radial expansions. The identity gradient path is preserved because the Jacobian of the update equals I + ∂f_⊥/∂x_n, maintaining the same gradient-flow guarantee as standard residual networks. Empirically, the orthogonal update stabilizes the stream norm after an early 'Transition Point,' prevents the progressive suppression of novel (orthogonal) directions observed under linear updates, and yields +3.78 pp Acc@1 for ViT-B on ImageNet-1k with modest overhead.

## Results

The headline result is a +3.78 pp Acc@1 gain for ViT-B on ImageNet-1k (77.05% vs. 73.27% for the linear baseline), with a +1.77 pp gain for ViT-S (72.53% vs. 70.76%). On CIFAR-100, ViT-B improves by +6.82 pp (75.07% vs. 68.25%) and ViT-S by +1.94 pp (73.86% vs. 71.92%). Gains on ResNetV2 are more modest, ranging from +0.26 pp (ResNetV2-34, CIFAR-10) to +2.01 pp (ResNetV2-101, TinyImageNet), which the authors attribute to the lower width-to-depth ratio γ of basic-block ResNets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard linear residual connection (x_{n+1} = x_n + f(σ(x_n))) as in ResNetV2. The orthogonal update consistently outperforms this baseline across all four datasets and both architecture families. The largest gains appear in ViT models: ViT-B improves by +3.78 pp on ImageNet-1k, +6.82 pp on CIFAR-100, and +4.65 pp on CIFAR-10, while ViT-S gains +1.77 pp on ImageNet-1k and +1.94 pp on CIFAR-100. On ResNetV2, improvements are positive but smaller (typically +0.2 to +2.0 pp), with ResNetV2-101 on TinyImageNet showing the largest CNN gain (+2.01 pp). The authors hypothesize that the weaker ResNetV2 gains relate to the lower width-to-depth ratio γ (≈14.75 for ResNetV2-34 vs. ≈64 for ViT-S/B), leaving less directional redundancy for the orthogonal projection to exploit. A learning-rate robustness sweep (Table 3) further confirms that the orthogonal update maintains its advantage even at aggressive LRs (e.g., 5×10⁻³) where the linear baseline degrades sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the pre-activation residual connection of ResNetV2 (He et al., ECCV 2016) and the linear residual stream paradigm now standard in Transformers (Vaswani et al., 2017; Dosovitskiy et al., 2020). It departs from prior work that modifies the skip path itself (e.g., orthogonal or entangled transforms replacing the identity) by preserving the identity skip and instead projecting the module's additive output to be orthogonal to the input stream. The orthogonality principle connects to a broader lineage including orthogonal weight initialization, Stiefel-manifold optimization, and the Muon optimizer, but applies it to the geometry of the residual update direction rather than to parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is a few lines of code with <2% throughput overhead on ViTs, yet yields a +3.78 pp Acc@1 gain on ViT-B/ImageNet-1k, making it a low-cost, high-impact modification for practitioners. The internal-dynamics analysis revealing that standard linear updates actively suppress the parallel component (parallel suppression) provides a mechanistic explanation for why the orthogonal variant stabilizes stream norms and preserves representational diversity. Key open questions include whether the gains generalize to LLMs, diffusion models, and substantially deeper/wider architectures; the paper's γ (width-to-depth ratio) hypothesis for explaining architecture-dependent efficacy remains unproven; and a rigorous theoretical account of when and why orthogonal updates help is still lacking.
