## Problem

Diffusion samplers trained to sample from unnormalized energy distributions without data suffer from poor sample efficiency in high dimensions, as their early random proposals are misaligned with the energy landscape, and from mode collapse driven by primacy bias during self-training. Existing remedies such as replay buffers and local energy-guided refinements yield only marginal gains and fail to overcome the fundamental exploration deficit of the initial diffusion policy.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized energy function E(x) (or, for molecular systems, a bond graph plus 3D atomic coordinates evaluated via a TorchANI potential) and produces samples from the target Boltzmann distribution p(x) ∝ exp(−E(x)) along with a learned log partition function estimate. For molecular conformer generation, the output is a set of 3D atomic-coordinate conformations that must be topology-aligned and chirality-corrected against the target bond graph before evaluation. The energy function is queried only during training (by the Searcher and for TB loss computation), while the trained Learner generates samples evaluation-free at inference time.

## Method

SGDS alternates between a gradient-guided MCMC Searcher (AIS, MALA, or underdamped Langevin dynamics) and a neural diffusion Learner trained via the trajectory balance objective. The Searcher collects off-policy samples into a replay buffer, and from the second round onward its energy landscape is augmented with a random-network-distillation (RND) intrinsic reward that steers exploration toward modes the Learner has not yet visited. The Learner is trained on a mixture of on-policy trajectories (from its own forward SDE) and off-policy trajectories (backward-kernel rollouts from replayed Searcher samples), with a rank-based sampling distribution prioritizing lower-energy buffer entries. A periodic partial re-initialization of the Learner network—resetting parameters while retaining the learned log Z and the replay buffer—counters primacy bias and prevents premature mode locking. The key mechanism is that the trajectory balance criterion guarantees unbiased density matching even when trained on biased MCMC samples, allowing the expensive gradient evaluations of the Searcher to be amortized across many Learner updates.

## Results

On Manywell-64, SGDS achieves an EUBO–ELBO gap of 16.26±0.22 with only 20M energy calls, compared to the best baseline TB+LS+LP (26.68±3.37 at 320M calls) and PIS (1704.91±408.49 at 100M calls). On Manywell-128, SGDS reaches a gap of 70.35±4.31 at 20M calls, outperforming TB+Expl+LP (93.37±1.22 at 240M calls) and iDEM (323.04±5.69 at 300M calls). On LJ-55 (d=165), SGDS achieves a gap of 33.01±0.93 at 45K calls versus PIS (45.53±5.58 at 45K) and TB+Expl+LS (123.02±21.72 at 1M). On Alanine Tetra-peptide, SGDS (26.60±0.21) outperforms MLE (29.07±0.56) while TB and TB+Expl+LS diverge entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGDS dominates all baselines on the EUBO–ELBO gap metric across every benchmark while using 5–18× fewer energy calls. Against the strongest local-search baseline TB+LS+LP (Sendera et al.), SGDS achieves a 16.26 gap on Manywell-64 versus 26.68 (a 39% reduction) at 16× fewer calls, and 70.35 versus 101.81 on Manywell-128 at 16× fewer calls. Against PIS and plain TB, the gap advantage is dramatic: SGDS closes the gap to 16.26 on Manywell-64 where PIS leaves it at 1704.91 and TB at 544.36, all with 5× fewer energy evaluations. GAFN (intrinsic-reward injection) and AT+LP (adaptive teacher) both suffer from instability or high energy costs, yielding gaps of 252.80 and 72.48 on Manywell-64 respectively, far above SGDS's 16.26. On LJ-55, SGDS (gap 33.01 at 45K calls) outperforms PIS (45.53 at 45K) and TB+Expl+LS (123.02 at 1M), while TB diverges entirely. On Alanine Tetra-peptide, SGDS (26.60) beats MLE (29.07) and is the only method that converges alongside MLE, whereas TB and TB+Expl+LS both diverge.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGDS sits at the intersection of GFlowNet-based diffusion samplers (trajectory balance objective from Malkin et al. 2022, continuous GFlowNet theory from Lahlou et al. 2023) and off-policy training frameworks for diffusion samplers (Sendera et al. 2024, Lemos et al. 2024). It extends Lemos et al.'s multiple-round MCMC-to-diffusion pipeline by replacing ad-hoc exploration with RND-based intrinsic rewards and by introducing periodic re-initialization to counter primacy bias. The paper explicitly positions itself as a 'deeper but shorter-cycle alternative' to Sendera et al.'s frequent diffusion-based re-initializations, and as a more stable replacement for the adversarial adaptive-teacher Searcher of Kim et al. 2025, using classical MCMC (AIS, MALA, MD) rather than additional neural networks for exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5–15× reduction in energy calls (20M vs. 100–330M for baselines) while matching or exceeding ELBO/EUBO performance across Manywell-64/128, LJ-55, and real peptide systems makes high-dimensional Boltzmann sampling substantially more tractable. The identification of primacy bias as the root cause of mode collapse in iterative diffusion-sampler training, and the simple periodic re-initialization fix, offer a transferable diagnostic for any RL-style amortized inference loop. Open questions include whether the framework scales to even higher-dimensional systems (beyond d=165), whether multi-agent cooperative search can further reduce the MCMC budget, and whether the RND-based exploration signal remains effective when the energy landscape is extremely rugged or has very sparse high-reward regions.
