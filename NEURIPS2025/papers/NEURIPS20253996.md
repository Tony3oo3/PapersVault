## Problem

Existing logit-based model fusion methods (e.g., ULD, FuseLLM) align vocabulary dimensions independently via KL divergence or Wasserstein distance, overlooking the semantic co-activation dependencies among token dimensions that encode a model's internal reasoning structure. This token-level treatment limits the ability to fuse models with divergent generation behaviors, particularly on tasks requiring multi-step reasoning or fine-grained relational inference where inter-token interactions matter.

## Input / Output

The method consumes instruction-response pairs from a 130k multi-task dataset (general reasoning, math, code) along with logits from up to three source models (Qwen2.5-14B, Qwen2.5-Coder-14B, Mistral-24B) and a Phi-4-14B pivot model. Logits of shape [B, L, d] are sparsified to top-k dimensions (k=10) per position, then aggregated via outer products across sequence positions into d'×d' co-activation graphs. The output is a single 14B fused checkpoint that matches the pivot's inference cost while inheriting complementary reasoning strengths from all sources.

## Method

InfiGFusion introduces a Graph-on-Logits Distillation (GLD) loss that models logit vectors as feature-level graphs and aligns their structures across models using Gromov-Wasserstein distance. Specifically, top-k sparsified logits are converted into dynamic co-activation graphs via dot-product similarity across sequence positions (C = Z^T Z), where nodes represent vocabulary channels and edges quantify joint activations. To make GW tractable at LLM vocabulary scale (n > 10^5), the paper derives a closed-form approximation that compresses each d'×d' similarity matrix into scalar node-level degree features, then aligns them via sorting-based matching, reducing complexity from O(n^4) to O(n log n) with a provable error bound of (n-1)/n² + (m-1)/m². The overall training objective combines GLD (structure-aware), ULD (token-level Wasserstein-1), and SFT losses, with ablations confirming that ULD provides coarse distributional alignment while GLD captures higher-order relational dependencies that token-level objectives miss.

## Results

InfiGFusion achieves an average score of 83.85 across 11 benchmarks (reasoning, math, coding, text reasoning, instruction following), outperforming the Phi-4 pivot (79.54), SFT (81.36), and all fusion baselines (MiniLogit 81.43, FuseLLM 81.46, FuseChat 82.12, InfiFusion 82.20). On complex reasoning subtasks (Table 3), InfiGFusion reaches 84.16 average over 18 BBH/MMLU tasks, with standout gains of +35.6 on Multistep Arithmetic and +37.06 on Causal Judgement over SFT. The method requires only 195 GPU hours versus 650 for FuseChat, while maintaining the same 14B parameter count as the pivot model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfiGFusion outperforms all four fusion baselines on average: +2.42 over MiniLogit (81.43), +2.39 over FuseLLM (81.46), +1.73 over FuseChat (82.12), and +1.65 over InfiFusion (82.20). The largest margins appear on structurally demanding tasks: BBH (85.62 vs. 82.68 MiniLogit, 77.62 FuseLLM, 83.37 FuseChat) and MBPP (85.2 vs. 78.49 MiniLogit, 79.28 FuseLLM, 81.80 FuseChat), confirming the benefit of structure-aware graph alignment over token-level objectives. InfiGFusion also surpasses the SFT baseline (81.36) by +2.49 on average, with particularly large gains on complex reasoning subtasks (+35.6 on Multistep Arithmetic, +37.06 on Causal Judgement). The method is slightly behind FuseChat on GSM8K (90.45 vs. 91.21) and behind InfiFusion on MMLU (85.24 vs. 85.81), suggesting token-level factual recall remains a minor weakness. In terms of efficiency, InfiGFusion uses 195 GPU hours—substantially less than FuseChat (650) and comparable to InfiFusion (160)—while delivering the best overall score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the InfiFusion framework [16] by adding a structure-aware Graph-on-Logits Distillation (GLD) loss on top of the existing Universal Logit Distillation (ULD) [15] and SFT objectives. The core mathematical tool is the Gromov-Wasserstein distance [20], which the authors approximate via a novel sorting-based closed-form reducing complexity from O(n⁴) to O(n log n). Conceptually, the paper departs from prior logit-based fusion methods (FuseLLM, FuseChat, MiniLogit) that treat vocabulary dimensions independently, instead modeling inter-token co-activation patterns as graph structures to capture semantic dependencies during distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(n log n) GW approximation with a provable O(1/n) error bound makes structure-aware logit alignment practical for LLM-scale vocabularies (n > 10⁵), opening a path for relational distillation that was previously infeasible. However, the paper acknowledges that GLD offers marginal or even negative benefits on factoid/knowledge-centric tasks and lacks mechanisms for resolving factual conflicts between source models. Open questions include adaptive dependency modeling (when to weight structural vs. token-level alignment), source reliability estimation, and whether the graph-on-logits formulation generalizes beyond the 14B-scale setting tested here.
