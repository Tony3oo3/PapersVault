## Problem

Minimax regret lower bounds for succinct linear bandits—where the unknown parameter has structure such as entry-sparsity, group-sparsity, or low rank—have been largely underdeveloped, with existing results limited to the entry-sparse vector setting and often relying on informal reductions. This paper asks whether a unified, general framework can produce sharp lower bounds across multiple succinct structures simultaneously, and whether it can tighten the one existing bound for entry-sparse vectors.

## Input / Output

The framework operates in an abstract inner-product vector space V (instantiated as R^d for vectors or R^{d1×d2} for matrices) and takes as input a fixed action set A ⊆ V, a succinctness structure parameterized by s (number of non-zero entries, non-zero rows, or matrix rank), dimension parameters (d, d1, d2), and horizon n. It produces a minimax regret lower bound of the form Ω(min(C_min^{-1/3}(A)·s^α·n^{2/3}, √(d·s·n))) together with an explicit construction of a hard action set and s-succinct parameter θ that forces any policy to incur that regret. The quantity C_min(A) measures how well the action set supports a well-conditioned exploration distribution, and the exponent α on s varies by structure (2/3 for entry-sparse and group-sparse, 1/3 for low-rank).

## Method

The paper introduces a general succinctness model in which an s-succinct vector decomposes over a 'support' of s mutually orthogonal unit vectors from a predefined succinct unit set U, and defines two semi-norms Q(·) and R(·) that behave as a primal–dual norm pair on s-succinct vectors. The main theorem (Theorem 3.8) constructs a hard action set A = H ∪ S and a parameter set Θ, then establishes a lower bound via an information-regret trade-off: H-based actions are informative for distinguishing parameters but incur constant per-round regret, while S-based actions are near-optimal but sparse, providing limited information. The proof combines Le Cam's Lemma with KL-divergence decomposition over Gaussian reward distributions and Jensen's inequality to bound the agent's ability to identify the correct unit in each of b parallel sub-problems. Applying the framework with minimal customization yields a sharpened entry-sparse bound (improving the s^{1/3} to s^{2/3} dependence of [7]) and, to the authors' knowledge, the first lower bounds for group-sparse matrix and low-rank matrix bandits.

## Results

The paper establishes minimax regret lower bounds for three succinct linear bandit settings. For entry-sparse vectors, the new bound Ω(min(C_min^{-1/3} s^{2/3} n^{2/3}, √(dsn))) sharpens the prior result of [7] by improving the sparsity dependence from s^{1/3} to s^{2/3} and the dimension dependence from √(dn) to √(dsn). For group-sparse matrices, the paper provides the first lower bound Ω(min(C_min^{-1/3} s^{1/3} n^{2/3}, √(d₁d₂n))). For low-rank matrices, the first lower bound Ω(min(C_min^{-1/3} s^{1/3} n^{2/3}, √(d₁d₂n))) is also established, where C_min(A) measures the well-conditionedness of the action set's exploration distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the prior lower bound of Hao, Lattimore, and Wang [7] for entry-sparse vector bandits, where the new framework improves the sparsity exponent from s^{1/3} to s^{2/3} in the data-poor regime and adds a factor of √s in the data-rich regime (√(dsn) vs. √(dn)), yielding a strictly tighter bound. For group-sparse and low-rank matrix bandits, no prior lower bounds existed, so the paper's contributions are entirely novel in those settings. The new entry-sparse lower bound nearly matches the upper bound O(C_min^{-2/3} s^{2/3} n^{2/3}) of the explore-sparsity-then-commit algorithm in [7] in the data-poor regime, closing the gap from s^{1/3} to s^{2/3}. In the data-rich regime, the Ω(√(dsn)) term matches the classical Ω(√(sdn)) lower bound of [11] up to the constant factor, confirming optimality. For low-rank matrices, the lower bound Ω(√(d₁d₂sn)) in the data-rich regime is consistent with the upper bound O((d₁+d₂)^{3/2} √(sn)) of [9,8], though a gap in the dependence on d₁+d₂ remains. The unified framework of [12] provides upper bounds of O(s^{1/3} n^{2/3} polylog) across all three settings, and the new lower bounds show that the s^{1/3} n^{2/3} rate is essentially tight in the data-poor regime for group-sparse and low-rank cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the information-regret trade-off technique introduced by Hao, Lattimore, and Wang [7] for entry-sparse linear bandits, which used Le Cam's Lemma and KL-divergence decomposition to construct a lower bound in the data-poor regime. It generalizes that single-setting construction into a unified 'succinctness' framework defined over abstract vector spaces, parameterized by a set of succinct units and two semi-norms (Q and R) that play the role of a norm and its dual. The framework subsumes entry-sparse vectors, group-sparse matrices, and low-rank matrices as special cases, whereas prior lower bound work [11, 7] was confined to the entry-sparse setting and upper bound work [8, 9, 12] addressed the other structures without matching lower bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first minimax lower bounds for group-sparse and low-rank matrix bandits, and sharpens the entry-sparse lower bound from s^{1/3} to s^{2/3} in the data-poor regime, narrowing the gap with known upper bounds. The general framework is designed to extend to other structured settings (tensors, kernel-based bandits, nonparametric bases), making it a reusable tool for the bandit theory community. However, significant gaps remain between the derived lower bounds and existing upper bounds in all three settings, and the framework requires the action set to admit well-conditioned exploration (C_min > 0), leaving the data-poor regime without such structure still open.
