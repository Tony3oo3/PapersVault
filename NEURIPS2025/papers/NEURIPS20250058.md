## Problem

Negative Preference Optimization (NPO) is a leading LLM unlearning method, but it suffers from an unacknowledged 'reference model bias': by anchoring the unlearning objective to the pre-unlearning model's predictions, NPO allocates optimization power uniformly by deviation from the reference rather than by true sample-specific difficulty. This causes under-forgetting of strongly-memorized or short-response data and ineffective gradient smoothing in early training, ultimately limiting unlearning quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-2 7B-chat, ICLM-7B, Zephyr-7B-beta), a forget set of (prompt, response) text pairs to be erased, and a retain set to preserve general utility. It outputs updated model parameters via a single fine-tuning pass—no reference model checkpoint is required at inference or training time, distinguishing it from NPO. The forget loss is length-normalized per response, and the retain loss is standard token-level cross-entropy, combined with a regularization weight λ.

## Method

SimNPO replaces NPO's reference-model-dependent reward β·log(π_θ(y|x)/π_ref(y|x)) with SimPO's reference-free, length-normalized reward (β/|y|)·log π_θ(y|x), yielding a bounded forget loss that drives the model to suppress token prediction on forget data without ever consulting the pre-unlearning model. The resulting gradient decomposes into a data-aware weight w'_θ(x,y) = 2(π_θ(y|x))^(β/|y|)/(1+(π_θ(y|x))^(β/|y|)) · 1/|y| times the gradient-ascent direction, where the 1/|y| factor allocates more optimization power to shorter (harder-to-forget) responses and the sigmoid-shaped term provides smooth, reference-free divergence control. This design simultaneously fixes NPO's uneven power allocation across memorization levels and response lengths, and eliminates the early-stage weight collapse (w≈1) that made NPO behave like uncontrolled gradient ascent. Theoretical analysis via a mixture-of-Markov-chains synthetic setup and a divergence-rate argument (Appendix G) confirm that SimNPO unlearns all samples in O(log(1/ε)/η) steps regardless of response length, whereas NPO's step count scales with |y|.

## Results

On TOFU Forget05 (LLaMA-2-7B-chat), SimNPO achieves a forget quality (FQ) of 0.99 and model utility (MU) of 0.58, closely approaching the Retrain gold standard (FQ=1.00, MU=0.62) while substantially outperforming NPO (FQ=0.79, MU=0.57). On MUSE News, SimNPO attains a PrivLeak of 72.93 (closer to 0 is better) and KnowMem on retain data of 39.65, compared to NPO's 108.91 and 37.58 respectively. On MUSE Books, SimNPO achieves KnowMem_Dr of 48.27 versus NPO's 23.71, while both achieve zero KnowMem on the forget set. SimNPO also demonstrates superior robustness against relearning attacks, maintaining higher forget quality over more epochs than NPO, particularly under shortest-response relearning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimNPO decisively outperforms all optimization-based baselines on TOFU Forget05. Against GA (Thudi et al., 2022) and GradDiff (Yao et al., 2023), SimNPO achieves FQ of 0.99 versus near-zero (1.9e-9 and 3.6e-9), while also preserving utility (MU 0.58 vs. 0.00 for GA and 0.56 for GradDiff). Against IDK (Maini et al., 2024), SimNPO wins on FQ (0.99 vs. 1.9e-9) with comparable MU (0.58 vs. 0.57). Against RKLD (Wang et al., 2024), SimNPO improves FQ from 0.79 to 0.99 and MU from 0.56 to 0.58. The primary comparison is with NPO (Zhang et al., 2024): SimNPO improves FQ from 0.79 to 0.99 (a 25% relative gain) while maintaining or slightly improving MU (0.58 vs. 0.57). On MUSE, SimNPO outperforms the Task Vector method (Shi et al., 2024) on both PrivLeak (72.93 vs. −100.00 for News; −19.82 vs. −83.78 for Books, where closer to 0 is better) and retain utility KnowMem_Dr (39.65 vs. 47.94 for News; 48.27 vs. 62.55 for Books), though Task Vector retains an edge on utility in some settings. SimNPO is the closest approximate method to the Retrain gold standard across all benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NPO (Negative Preference Optimization) framework for LLM unlearning, which itself was inspired by DPO for aligning LLMs with human preferences. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'reference model bias'—the reliance on the pre-unlearning model as an anchor for the unlearning objective—and replacing it with SimPO's reference-free, length-normalized reward formulation. This positions SimNPO as a natural successor in the lineage GA → GradDiff → NPO (DPO-inspired) → SimNPO (SimPO-inspired), drawing from the broader family of reward-free offline preference objectives (IPO, KTO, ORPO, SimPO) to solve an unlearning problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of reference model bias in what was considered the state-of-the-art unlearning method (NPO) provides a concrete, actionable diagnosis for why unlearning performance plateaus, and the fix is remarkably simple—swap one preference optimization variant for another. However, both NPO and SimNPO still rely on promoting divergence from the pre-trained state, which inherently causes utility loss, particularly in knowledge unlearning scenarios (WMDP). Open questions include establishing theoretical guarantees for SimNPO, whether divergence-based unlearning can be replaced by more targeted mechanisms, and whether the length-normalization principle generalizes beyond the specific benchmarks tested.
