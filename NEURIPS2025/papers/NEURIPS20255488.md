## Problem

Training LLMs on long reasoning sequences (8k–100k+ tokens) is bottlenecked by GPU memory consumed during backpropagation, specifically the storage of intermediate activations and full logits. Even with gradient checkpointing, the reforwarded layer activations and the T×C logits matrix remain in memory simultaneously, capping the maximum trainable sequence length. Existing solutions either reduce parameter/optimizer memory (LoRA, GaLore) or address only part of the activation/logit cost (MsT, BPT), leaving a gap for a unified, exact, and FLOPs-efficient BP method.

## Input / Output

StreamBP consumes the same inputs as standard training: a long text sequence (up to 100k+ tokens), transformer LLM weights in BF16/FP32, and a loss objective (SFT cross-entropy, GRPO clipped policy loss, or DPO log-sigmoid preference loss). It outputs mathematically exact parameter gradients identical to standard BP, while peak activation memory scales as 1/D of the full-sequence cost (D = number of sequence partitions). The method integrates as a drop-in replacement within HuggingFace Transformers/TRL pipelines and is compatible with flash attention, LoRA, and Deepspeed ZeRO distributed training.

## Method

StreamBP applies a linear decomposition of the chain rule along the sequence dimension: the gradient ∂L/∂W is expressed as a sum over D sequence partitions, each computed and accumulated sequentially so that only one chunk's intermediate activations reside in memory at a time. For the language modeling head, logits are partitioned into D chunks of size (T/D)×C, and per-chunk gradients are accumulated in-place; for DPO, a post-hoc scalar correction (σ(βℓ)−1) recovers the exact gradient despite the non-separable log-sigmoid. For transformer layers, the key structural insight is Property 3.1: computing ∂H_out^(i)/∂W requires only Q^(i), K^(1:i), and V^(1:i), so the causal mask means each partition's attention score S^(i)=Q^(i)K^(1:i)ᵀ uses a triangular sub-matrix rather than the full T×T product, cutting attention FLOPs by roughly half. K and V are computed once and cached across partitions, and the partitioned reforward is compatible with flash attention tiling. A distributed variant defers gradient all-reduce until after per-layer accumulation and caches gathered parameters locally to avoid (D−1)× redundant all-gather calls under ZeRO-3.

## Results

StreamBP scales the maximum backpropagation sequence length by 2.8–5.5× over gradient checkpointing on a single A800-80GB GPU (e.g., 200k vs. 36.5k for SFT on Qwen 3-4B full-parameter, 16.3k vs. 5.9k for 32B LoRA). In distributed ZeRO-2 training, StreamBP reaches 131.8k sequence length versus 23.7k for checkpointing (~5.6×). StreamBP also achieves faster BP time than checkpointing, with acceleration growing from 0.4% at 9k to 12.9% at 24k sequence length, and beats MsT by up to 12.9% in BP time. Gradient correctness is verified with ≤0.04% relative deviation from standard BF16 backpropagation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against gradient checkpointing (Chen et al., 2016), StreamBP wins decisively on both memory and speed: it supports 2.8–5.5× longer sequences on a single GPU and 5–5.6× longer sequences under ZeRO-2 distributed training, while simultaneously reducing BP time by up to 12.9% at 24k sequence length (Qwen 3-4B). Against the long-sequence SFT baseline MsT (Luo et al., 2024), StreamBP achieves approximately 1.7× larger maximum sequence length (e.g., 25.8k vs. 31.8k at 27k in Table 1's memory profile, and ~1.7× in Figure 4 for Qwen 3-8B LoRA) and substantially lower BP time (e.g., 13.2s vs. 15.2s at 18k, 17.2s vs. 20.3s at 21k). StreamBP's advantage over both baselines grows with sequence length, consistent with its exploitation of the causal attention structure to halve the FLOPs of pre-attention score computation. In distributed settings, StreamBP's ability to use larger batch sizes (enabled by its lower per-sample memory) further reduces per-sample BP time and communication overhead relative to checkpointing.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamBP carves new ground in memory-efficient backpropagation by applying a linear decomposition of the chain rule along the sequence dimension in a layerwise manner, rather than recomputing activations (gradient checkpointing) or distributing sequences across GPUs (sequence parallelism). It is conceptually adjacent to MsT (which partitions logits for SFT) and BPT (which partitions forward across sequence), but StreamBP unifies both the logits and transformer-layer activation memory reduction under a single framework and extends it to RL and preference-optimization objectives. The exploitation of the causal attention structure to reduce FLOPs (using K^(:i) instead of full K) is a departure from prior memory-saving methods that typically trade speed for memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StreamBP directly addresses the dominant memory bottleneck in training reasoning models on long sequences, enabling 2.8–5.5× longer sequences on the same hardware while being faster than gradient checkpointing—a combination that makes long-sequence SFT, GRPO, and DPO training significantly more accessible. The sequence-length scaling transfers directly to batch-size scaling, providing an additional training-speed lever. Open questions include extension to MoE and multimodal architectures, optimal partition-size selection under fused backward operators, and whether the HBM overhead from repeated weight loading can be further mitigated at very large D.
