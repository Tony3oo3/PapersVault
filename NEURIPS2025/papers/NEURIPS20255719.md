## Problem

Directly applying R1-style reinforcement learning (GRPO) to multimodal retrieval MLLMs is infeasible due to prohibitive token consumption from multiple candidates combined with fine-grained CoT reasoning, and training instability that yields incorrect reasoning processes. Prior retrieval MLLMs either rely on imprecise embedding similarity or perform direct QA without explicit reasoning, limiting their ability to handle complex retrieval cases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255719/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query of any modality (text, image, or interleaved image-text) and a top-K candidate set (K=50) preselected by a first-stage embedding model, where each candidate is compressed into exactly two tokens (a content token and a query-candidate relationship token) by the ICM before entering the language model. The output is a retrieval result index accompanied by a structured CoT reasoning process that may include special <inspection-index-start>/<inspection-index-end> tokens to selectively expand challenging candidates back to their full uncompressed token sequences mid-reasoning.

## Method

Retrv-R1 introduces an Information Compression Module (ICM) that uses two attention layers to compress each candidate into a content token and a relationship token, pretrained via a self-alignment objective that forces compressed tokens to reproduce the LM's descriptions of the full candidate. A Details Inspection Mechanism allows the MLLM to autonomously flag challenging candidates during CoT and retrieve their full token sequences as supplementary context, mitigating compression-induced information loss. Training follows three stages: (1) ICM pretraining with frozen LM, (2) SFT on a 100K synthetic 4-stage CoT dataset (speculation → quick negative verification → refined positive verification with inspection → result generation) generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B, and (3) GRPO-based RL on 10K hard samples with a curriculum reward that gradually increases the efficiency penalty λ on inspection frequency, balancing accuracy gains against token consumption.

## Results

On the M-BEIR benchmark (16 sub-tasks across 8 query/candidate format combinations), Retrv-R1-7B achieves an average Recall@K of 69.2, outperforming the previous best method LamRA-7B (63.7) by 5.5 points and the strongest R1-style MLLM Vision-R1-7B (50.2) by 19.0 points. Even the smaller Retrv-R1-3B (65.5 avg) surpasses the 7B-parameter LamRA-7B. In terms of efficiency, Retrv-R1-7B at K=50 achieves an inference time ratio (ITR) of 1.00 and GPU memory ratio (GMR) of 1.00, while competing methods at the same K require 4.8–7.7× more inference time and 2.4–3.5× more GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Retrv-R1-7B (69.2 avg) decisively outperforms the strongest retrieval-specific baseline LamRA-7B (63.7) by 5.5 points, with the gap widening on challenging composed-image tasks (e.g., CIRR: 72.3 vs. 63.3; OVEN: 86.6 vs. 79.2). Against R1-style reasoning MLLMs not designed for retrieval, the advantage is even larger: Retrv-R1-7B beats Vision-R1-7B (50.2) by 19.0 points and VLM-R1-7B (50.0) by 19.2 points. The 3B variant (65.5) already surpasses the 7B LamRA-7B, demonstrating strong parameter efficiency. On efficiency (Table 2), Retrv-R1-7B at K=50 requires only 1.00× the inference time and 1.00× the GPU memory of itself as reference, while Qwen2.5-VL-7B at K=50 needs 4.79× time and 2.44× memory, and Vision-R1-7B at K=50 needs 7.23× time and 3.28× memory. On out-of-domain generalization (Table 3), Retrv-R1-7B leads on all unseen datasets (CIRCO MAP@5: 47.8 vs. LamRA 42.8; GeneCIS R@1: 29.2 vs. 24.8; VisD R@1: 74.2 vs. 70.9; VIST R@1: 30.9 vs. 28.6; MT-FIQ R@5: 69.6 vs. 63.9). On the multimodal recommendation task (Table 5), even without fine-tuning Retrv-R1-3B outperforms all task-specific recommenders (e.g., Sports HR@10: 7.13 vs. ICSRec 5.65), and with fine-tuning achieves SOTA (Sports HR@10: 9.95, Beauty HR@10: 12.71, Toys HR@10: 12.89).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage universal multimodal retrieval paradigm established by LamRA (embedding-based coarse retrieval followed by MLLM-based fine selection) by replacing the SFT-trained selection model with an R1-style reasoning MLLM trained via GRPO, following the RL-for-reasoning recipe popularized by DeepSeek-R1. It departs from prior R1-style MLLMs (Vision-R1, VLM-R1) that target general visual reasoning by introducing retrieval-specific architectural and training adaptations: an Information Compression Module that reduces each candidate to two tokens, a details inspection mechanism for selectively expanding challenging candidates, and a curriculum-based efficiency reward. The paper also builds on BLIP-2-style self-alignment for pretraining the compression module and on synthetic CoT generation (via Qwen2.5-VL-72B) to bootstrap reasoning before RL, a pattern shared with Reason-RFT and Light-R1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Retrv-R1 demonstrates that step-by-step reasoning can substantially improve multimodal retrieval accuracy while simultaneously reducing inference cost, addressing a key practical bottleneck (token explosion from many candidates) that has limited MLLM-based retrieval to small candidate sets. The framework's strong generalization to unseen datasets, held-out tasks, and out-of-domain recommendation suggests a path toward a single reasoning MLLM serving diverse retrieval needs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large candidate pools (K >> 50), whether the synthetic CoT quality from a 72B teacher limits the reasoning ceiling, and whether the ICM's two-token compression remains sufficient for fine-grained or video retrieval tasks.
