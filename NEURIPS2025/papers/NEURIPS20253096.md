## Problem

Existing entity alignment methods fall into two camps: symbolic models that provide interpretable, precise inference but fail on low-degree entities without aligned neighbors, and neural models that achieve high recall but lack interpretability and cannot quantify uncertainty. Prior neuro-symbolic approaches treat the two components as separate modules (neural as similarity scorer, symbolic as pseudo-label refiner) rather than jointly optimizing them, and the exponential search space over long relational rules limits symbolic reasoning to short paths. The paper also notes that generating interpretable, confidence-quantified explanations for alignment decisions remains underexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two knowledge graphs represented as sets of entities, relations, and directed relational triples, along with a sparse set of observed (seed) aligned entity pairs. It produces three types of output: (1) binary alignment predictions for all hidden entity pairs, (2) ranked candidate lists per entity for hit@k/MRR evaluation, and (3) interpretable supporting rule path pairs with quantified confidence scores generated by an explainer that operates in hard-anchor (pre-aligned pairs only) or soft-anchor (including inferred pairs) mode. The one-to-one matching constraint during pseudo-label generation ensures each entity appears in at most one positive prediction.

## Method

NeuSymEA models the joint probability of truth-score assignments for all entity pairs in a Markov random field regulated by weighted cross-KG logic rules, optimized via a variational EM algorithm. In the E-step, a neural model (Dual-AMN or LightEA) parameterizes the variational distribution q_θ over hidden alignment variables, trained on both observed pairs and symbolically-inferred pseudo-labels selected by a threshold δ. In the M-step, rule weights are updated by maximizing the log-likelihood over observed and neural-predicted pseudo-labels, where the key insight is a logic deduction mechanism that decomposes any L-length rule into L unit-length sub-rules, reducing the search space from exponential to linear in L. Rule weight updates simplify to updating sub-relation probabilities p_sub(r⊆r′) via a closed-form ratio (Equation 10), while relation uniqueness scores η(r) remain fixed. After convergence, an explainer reverses the decomposition to reconstruct long supporting rules with confidence computed as the product of unit-rule confidences, providing interpretable evidence for both aligned and non-aligned pairs.

## Results

On the condensed DBP15K dataset, NeuSymEA-L achieves the best Hit@1 of 0.858 on FR-EN and 0.811 on JA-EN, while NeuSymEA-D achieves 0.815 on ZH-EN, outperforming the strongest neural baseline LightEA (0.827/0.778/0.770) and the closest neuro-symbolic baseline EMEA (0.773/0.736/0.748). On the full DBP15K (which includes low-degree entities), NeuSymEA-D achieves a 7.6% Hit@1 improvement over LightEA on ZH-EN (0.801 vs. 0.725). In the extreme low-resource setting with only 1% seed alignments, NeuSymEA-L reaches 0.737 Hit@1 on FR-EN, rivaling or surpassing models trained on 20% of the data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuSymEA consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across both full and condensed DBP15K splits. Against the strongest neural baselines, NeuSymEA-L gains +3.3% Hit@1 over LightEA on JA-EN (0.811 vs. 0.778) and +3.1% on FR-EN (0.858 vs. 0.827), while NeuSymEA-D gains +4.5% on ZH-EN (0.815 vs. 0.770). Against the closest neuro-symbolic competitor EMEA, NeuSymEA achieves substantially larger margins: +7.5% Hit@1 on JA-EN (0.811 vs. 0.736), +8.5% on FR-EN (0.858 vs. 0.773), and +6.7% on ZH-EN (0.815 vs. 0.748), which the authors attribute to the unified joint-probability objective versus EMEA's separate-component design. On the full (sparser) DBP15K, the gap widens further: neural models degrade significantly (e.g., Dual-AMN MRR drops from 0.815 to 0.717 on JA-EN) while NeuSymEA remains stable, confirming that the symbolic path-level matching component compensates for the entity-level matching weakness of pure neural models under sparsity. In the 1%-seed low-resource setting, NeuSymEA-L achieves 0.737 Hit@1 on FR-EN, exceeding LightEA's 0.430 and EMEA's 0.480 by a wide margin, and even surpassing some models trained on 20% data.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuSymEA extends the variational EM framework from probabilistic logic neural networks (Qu & Tang, NeurIPS 2019) and the one-step inference mechanism from PARIS (Suchanek et al., VLDB 2012) to the cross-KG entity alignment setting, where prior neuro-symbolic KG completion methods only model single-KG structures. It departs from existing neuro-symbolic EA methods (PRASE, EMEA) that treat symbolic and neural components as separate modules by unifying them under a joint Markov random field objective optimized via variational EM. The key technical departure is the logic deduction mechanism that decomposes long rules into unit-length sub-rules, avoiding the exponential search space that limits prior symbolic EA approaches to short rules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, unified framework that resolves the long-standing tension between interpretable symbolic reasoning and scalable neural embeddings in entity alignment, with the variational EM offering a coherent optimization objective rather than ad-hoc coupling. The demonstrated robustness in low-resource settings (73.7% hit@1 with only 1% seed pairs, rivaling models trained on 20% data) has practical significance for real-world KG integration where aligned pairs are scarce. An open question is scalability to multi-KG alignment, which the authors acknowledge would require a more sophisticated optimization paradigm beyond iterative pairwise alignment. The finding that symbolic models improve on sparser full datasets while neural models degrade also suggests complementary failure modes that future hybrid methods should exploit more explicitly.
