## Problem

Origin-destination (OD) travel demand estimation in large urban road networks is a high-dimensional, stochastic, non-differentiable, and computationally expensive inverse optimization problem, yet no standardized benchmark exists to connect Bayesian optimization research with realistic urban mobility settings. Existing BO benchmarks (analytic test functions, hyperparameter optimization) lack the noise, constraints, and scale (up to 10,100 dimensions) inherent in real traffic simulation, making it impossible to systematically evaluate whether and how BO methods scale to transportation-scale problems.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a high-dimensional OD demand vector (3 to 10,100 continuous variables bounded in [1, 2000]) as the optimization input, a SUMO freeway network topology (10 to 2,173 links) as the simulation environment, and 5-minute-interval main-line traffic counts from Caltrans PeMS sensors (3 to 219 sensors) as ground-truth observations. Each candidate OD vector is evaluated by running a full mesoscopic SUMO simulation (0.8 s to 40,099 s per evaluation), producing simulated link traffic counts that are compared against sensor observations via normalized RMSE. The output is the OD demand vector minimizing NRMSE, with performance reported as percentage improvement over a shared random initialization.

## Method

BO4Mob is a benchmark framework rather than a new algorithm: it constructs five progressively complex freeway subnetworks from the San Jose, CA network (Simple Ramp → Full Region) with TAZ-based OD pair generation, real PeMS sensor data filtered for physical consistency (traffic count conservation at ramps) and TAZ granularity, and SUMO mesoscopic simulation as the stochastic black-box oracle. The optimization objective is the sum of squared errors between ground-truth and simulated link traffic counts (Eq. 4), evaluated under a fixed random seed per run. Five methods are benchmarked under identical initialization and budgets: Random Search, SPSA (simultaneous perturbation gradient approximation), Vanilla BO (GP with Matérn 5/2 ARD + qLogEI), SAASBO (sparse axis-aligned subspace GP with horseshoe prior), and TuRBO (adaptive trust-region BO with anisotropic regions and Thompson sampling). Key design choices include scaling simulation time, batch size, and epoch count with network complexity, and verifying genuine high-dimensionality by showing >95% of OD variables are dominant (not sparse) in the Small Region network.

## Results

Across four scalable networks (Simple Ramp through Small Region), BO methods consistently outperform non-BO baselines. TuRBO achieves the lowest NRMSE on Simple Ramp (0.001, 99.47% improvement) and Small Region (0.258, 69.54% improvement), while SAASBO leads on One-Way Corridor (0.07, 68.56% improvement). SPSA and random search show minimal gains (2.6–29.4% improvement), confirming the value of surrogate modeling. On the Full Region (10,100 dimensions), no method achieved any improvement over the initial solution, with all NRMSE values remaining near 0.78.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TuRBO [Eriksson et al., 2019] delivers the strongest overall performance, achieving the best NRMSE on Simple Ramp (0.001 vs. 0.003 for Vanilla BO) and Small Region (0.258 vs. 0.437 for Vanilla BO), with near-ties on Junction. SAASBO [Eriksson & Jankowiak, 2021] is the top method on One-Way Corridor (0.070 vs. 0.090 for TuRBO) but degrades on more complex networks, ranking last among BO methods on Small Region (0.477). Vanilla BO [Jones et al., 1998] is competitive on mid-scale networks (Junction: 0.233, Small Region: 0.437) and outperforms SAASBO there, but is outpaced by TuRBO on the simplest and most complex cases. SPSA [Spall, 1992], the standard non-BO baseline in transportation, consistently underperforms all BO methods, achieving only 2.6–27.1% improvement versus 40–99% for BO approaches, with particularly poor results on Small Region (0.825, barely above the initial 0.847). Random search serves as a lower bound and confirms that the observed gains are attributable to the optimization strategy rather than favorable initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by bridging two communities that have largely developed in parallel: high-dimensional Bayesian optimization (TuRBO, SAASBO, Vanilla BO) and transportation engineering's OD demand calibration (Osorio 2019, Dantsuji et al. 2022). Rather than proposing a new optimization algorithm, it repurposes existing BO methods as baselines and introduces a realistic, simulation-based benchmark (BO4Mob) that exposes the gap between the analytic/hyperparameter benchmarks (BBOB, HPoBench, LassoBench) typically used in BO papers and the stochastic, non-differentiable, high-dimensional problems encountered in urban mobility. The benchmark's design draws on the SUMO simulator and PeMS sensor data, integrating them into a standardized evaluation protocol that neither community had previously provided.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a concrete gap: prior BO evaluations relied on noiseless analytic functions or low-dimensional hyperparameter tuning, neither of which captures the stochastic, expensive, and truly high-dimensional nature of OD estimation. The most important open question the paper surfaces is scalability — at the Full Region scale (10,100 dimensions, ~11 hours per simulation), all four methods failed to improve over initialization, highlighting that current high-dimensional BO is not yet ready for urban-scale problems. The paper also identifies promising directions (physics-informed kernels, uncertainty quantification for under-determined OD, multi-output formulations for dynamic OD) that remain unexplored, and the open-source release lowers the barrier for the transportation community to adopt BO tools.
