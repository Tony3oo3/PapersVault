## Problem

Fine-tuning vision-language models like CLIP requires backpropagation, making them infeasible on memory-constrained, inference-only edge devices. Existing backpropagation-free (BP-free) methods relying on evolutionary strategies or zeroth-order (ZO) optimization suffer from high gradient estimation variance and premature convergence, and prior attempts to reduce this variance by limiting trainable parameters offer limited benefit in the already low-dimensional prompt-tuning setting. The paper identifies that the sharpness of the loss landscape and initialization quality are the key unaddressed factors governing ZO optimization performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images paired with class-specific text prompts (constructed by concatenating a fixed template embedding like 'a photo of a' with a class label embedding) and produces a class label via the CLIP model's classification head. The only trainable parameter is a low-dimensional latent vector w ∈ R^512, projected into the 512-dimensional text embedding space through a fixed random projection matrix, so the entire optimization operates on 512 scalar parameters using exclusively forward passes—no gradient computation or backpropagation graph is ever constructed.

## Method

SharpZO is a two-stage forward-only optimizer. Stage 1 performs a sharpness-aware CMA-ES warm-up where the worst-case perturbation ε* = ρ∇L(θ)/‖∇L(θ)‖₂ is added to the CMA-ES sampling distribution, simultaneously smoothing the loss landscape and producing a strong initialization; the gradient needed for ε* is estimated via coordinate-wise finite differences (CGE) since backpropagation is unavailable. Stage 2 performs sparse ZO-SGD using randomized gradient estimation (RGE) with a novel Z-pruning mask Ω applied to the perturbation vector u (rather than to weights), where Ω is computed from a second-order Taylor expansion of the loss and Z-score-normalized CGE gradient estimates to suppress outlier gradient components. The theoretical analysis shows that the sharpness-aware warm-up effectively clips the L-smoothness constant with ρ, yielding a convergence rate of O((1/ημ) log(L³η²ρ²/ε)) for Stage 2, and empirically SharpZO achieves up to 7% average accuracy gain over prior BP-free baselines while requiring only 2 forward queries per step versus 10 for ZIP.

## Results

On the ViT-B/16 backbone, SharpZO achieves 75.64% average accuracy across 11 few-shot classification datasets (16-shot, 20K training steps), outperforming the strongest ZO baseline ZIP (70.98%) by 4.7% and zero-shot CLIP (65.23%) by 10.4%. On the ResNet-50 backbone, SharpZO reaches 69.76% average, a 5.7% gain over ZIP (64.08%). In terms of training efficiency (time-to-test-accuracy), SharpZO converges in 15.3 minutes on ImageNet and 2.4 minutes on OxfordPets, compared to 19.0 and 126.3 minutes for ZIP, and 172.6 and 714.8 minutes for BlackVIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SharpZO outperforms all BP-free baselines across all 11 tasks on both backbones. Against the strongest ZO baseline ZIP, SharpZO gains 4.7% average accuracy on ViT-B/16 (75.64% vs. 70.98%) and 5.7% on RN50 (69.76% vs. 64.08%), winning on every individual dataset. Against BlackVIP, the gap is even larger: 7.4% on ViT-B/16 and 8.9% on RN50, with particularly dramatic improvements on Flowers102 (+15.0% ViT) and EuroSAT (+5.2% ViT). Against BBT (CMA-ES-based), SharpZO leads by 3.2% (ViT) and 4.4% (RN50). Compared to the BP-based CraFT method (Table 4), SharpZO achieves higher accuracy on all four tested tasks (e.g., 71.60% vs. 68.21% on ImageNet) while using less training memory (3132 MB vs. 3298 MB on ImageNet). On distribution-shift robustness (Table 2), SharpZO improves over ZIP by 2.0% (RN50) and 2.8% (ViT-B/16) averaged across ImageNet-V2, -Sketch, -A, and -R, and approaches the BP-based CoOp method (43.3% vs. 42.4% avg on RN50).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: CMA-ES-based black-box optimization (Hansen 2001), sharpness-aware minimization (Foret et al. 2021), and zeroth-order stochastic gradient descent (Ghadimi & Lan 2013). It departs from prior BP-free VLM fine-tuning methods (BBT, BlackVIP, ZIP) that primarily reduce ZO gradient variance by limiting trainable parameter dimensionality through low-rank decomposition or magnitude-based pruning. Instead, SharpZO introduces a conceptual shift: it addresses ZO optimization difficulty through loss-landscape smoothing via a sharpness-aware CMA-ES warm-up stage, and through a novel Z-pruning criterion that leverages gradient information and Z-score normalization rather than raw weight magnitudes. The hybrid two-stage design (global ES exploration followed by local ZO exploitation) is, to the authors' knowledge, the first to incorporate sharpness-aware initialization into a ZO fine-tuning pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SharpZO provides a practical path toward deploying VLM fine-tuning on memory-constrained edge devices (IoT, wearables) where backpropagation is unavailable, achieving near-first-order accuracy (approaching CoOp) with only forward passes. The finding that a single ZO query (q=1) suffices after sharpness-aware warm-up—compared to q=5 in prior methods—has direct implications for reducing inference cost on resource-limited hardware. However, the method is currently limited to low-dimensional prompt-tuning settings; the CGE-based gradient estimation required for the sharpness term and Z-pruning metrics scales poorly with parameter dimension, leaving open the question of whether SharpZO extends to full-model or multimodal LLM fine-tuning. Future work on adapting the sharpness-aware warm-up to higher-dimensional ZO settings would be a natural and impactful next step.
