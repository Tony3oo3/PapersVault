## Problem

Online learning of neural networks was not extensively explored in the literature, despite its natural fit for adversarial and streaming prediction tasks where i.i.d. assumptions fail. The paper asks what the optimal mistake bound is for online learning of feedforward networks with sign activations, and under what structural assumptions dimension-free bounds become possible. The characterization reveals that the answer is governed by a geometric quantity (the totally-separable packing number) that is exponential in dimension, making additional restrictions essential for practical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a stream of instances x_t ∈ B(ℝ^d) (unit ball) one at a time, predicts a label ŷ_t ∈ 𝒴 = {1,…,Y} before observing the true label, and incurs 0/1 loss. The target is an unknown feedforward network with sign activations and unit-norm weight vectors; a critical constraint is that every first-hidden-layer neuron classifies all instances with margin γ₁ bounded away from zero. In the agnostic variant, the adversary may flip labels in unknown rounds, and the learner must randomize to achieve Õ(√(MT)) regret.

## Method

The core technique is a meta-learner that runs a multiclass Weighted Majority algorithm over a class of experts, where each expert simulates a subset of g neurons (via perceptron-like updates steered by binary 'manual' vectors specifying update rounds) combined with a labeling function mapping region-specifying vectors to labels. The key insight is that at most TS(d, γ₁) neurons are 'important' for partitioning the input sequence into regions, which bounds the expert class size and yields the mistake bound Õ(TS(d,γ₁)/γ₁²). Under the multi-index model (target depends on only k ≪ d orthonormal directions), the effective dimension drops to k, giving Õ(TS(k,γ₁)/γ₁²). Under the extended margin assumption (all neurons in all layers have margin γ), VC-theory and the uniform convergence theorem reduce the number of needed neurons, producing a dimension-free bound of Õ(log|𝒴|/γ^{O(L)}) where L is network depth.

## Results

The paper establishes that the optimal mistake bound for online learning a sign-activation neural network is characterized by the TS-packing number: the upper bound is Õ(TS(d, γ₁)/γ₁²) and a matching lower bound of Ω(TS(d, ε) + 1/ε²) holds for some networks. In the multi-index model (k ≪ d relevant directions), the bound improves to Õ(TS(k, γ₁)/γ₁²), yielding poly(1/γ₁) mistakes for constant k. Under the extended margin assumption (all neurons in all layers have margin γ), the bound becomes dimension-free: Õ(log|Y|/γ^(4L+2)). The TS-packing number itself satisfies TS(d, ε) ≥ max{1/(γ√d)^d, d}, implying exponential-in-d mistakes are unavoidable in the worst case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is with Rakhlin et al. (2015), who gave regret bounds for online learning of neural networks with Lipschitz activations and convex losses. Under the extended margin assumption with depth L and binary output, this paper achieves a regret bound of Õ(√T/γ^O(L)) (via Theorem 2.3 + Proposition 2.5), while Rakhlin et al. obtain Õ(C_ℓ√(T log d)·(B/γ)^O(L)), where C_ℓ is the loss Lipschitz constant and B bounds the ℓ₁-norm of weight vectors. The paper's bound has four stated advantages: (1) no dependence on input dimension d or weight-norm B, (2) an explicit algorithm rather than a minimax analysis, (3) applicability to the non-convex 0/1 loss, and (4) a finite (T-independent) mistake bound in the realizable case. The paper also notes its connection to the pruning literature, identifying a small set of 'important' neurons (of size at most TS(d, γ₁)) sufficient to compute the target function, which parallels but differs from standard pruning that retrains after removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online learning of neural networks initiated by Rakhlin et al. (2015), who provided regret bounds under Lipschitz activations and convex losses, to the sign-activation and 0/1-loss setting that is natural for classification and binarized networks. The proof architecture is built on the multiclass Weighted Majority algorithm (Littlestone and Warmuth, 1994) as a meta-learner, with expert classes constructed via a perceptron-with-manual-updates technique inspired by Ben-David et al. (2009), and the agnostic-to-realizable reduction of Hanneke et al. (2023a). A key conceptual departure is the identification of the optimal mistake bound with the totally-separable packing number from discrete geometry (Goodman and Goodman, 1945; Tóth and Tóth, 1973), a connection not previously exploited in online learning of neural networks. The multi-index model draws on the high-dimensional inference literature (Goldt et al., 2020; Arous et al., 2021), and the extended-margin proof leverages VC-theory and uniform convergence (Vapnik and Chervonenkis, 1971) in a novel neuron-pruning argument.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the optimal mistake bound for online learning of sign-activation networks is governed by the TS-packing number, which is exponential in the input dimension d for small margins — a fundamental barrier showing that dimension-free mistake bounds are impossible without additional structure. The two natural restrictions studied (multi-index model and extended margin) provide clean polynomial or dimension-free bounds, offering a principled path to tractable online learning. Open questions include whether the exponential dependence on network depth L in the extended-margin bound is tight, whether the polynomial gap between upper and lower bounds in the general characterization can be closed, and whether computationally efficient algorithms exist (the current meta-learner maintains more than (1/γ)^(1/γ) experts). The connection to neural network pruning — identifying a small set of 'important' neurons sufficient to compute the target function — suggests a new theoretical lens on a widely used practical technique.
