## Problem

On-policy RL algorithms for LLM post-training (PPO, RLOO, GRPO) require sequential data generation and policy updates, creating bottlenecks that waste GPU compute and limit scalability. While asynchronous off-policy data generation can parallelize rollout production, standard on-policy objectives are not robust to the resulting stale trajectories, and prior work shows increasing off-policyness degrades performance. The paper asks whether a principled off-policy objective can enable stable, high-performance training on asynchronously generated data at scale.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM as the initial policy, a frozen reference policy, a task-specific reward model (binary correctness for GSM8K, a learned 6.7B preference model for TL;DR summarization, or a toxicity classifier for red-teaming), and a dataset of prompts. SEARCHER nodes generate K responses per prompt (with S > K total samples to ensure diversity beyond K unique sequences), and the TRAINER samples batches of B prompts × K responses from a shared replay buffer using a mixed recency/reward-prioritized strategy. The output is post-trained model weights, evaluated via pass@1 accuracy (GSM8K), win-rate against a gold reward model (TL;DR), or attack success rate and pairwise cosine diversity (red-teaming).

## Method

TBA integrates the VarGrad Trajectory Balance loss—a GFlowNet off-policy objective—into a distributed asynchronous RL framework with separate SEARCHER and TRAINER nodes that run continuously and independently. SEARCHER nodes generate off-policy trajectories via vLLM and stash them in a shared replay buffer, while the TRAINER node optimizes the policy using the TB loss without pausing for generation, syncing weights to searchers only every k steps. The TB gradient (Eq. 7) reduces to mean-baseline REINFORCE with a KL-regularized reward for on-policy data but remains well-defined off-policy without importance sampling ratios, making it naturally compatible with stale trajectories. A key design choice is the sampling strategy: with probability m the TRAINER draws from the most recent sync step (most on-policy data), and with probability 1−m it samples using reward-prioritized (softmax or uniform) weighting over the entire buffer, balancing exploration against exploitation. The paper shows that even 15+ steps of staleness maintains or improves performance, and that scaling SEARCHER count consistently improves diversity and reward discovery, yielding 4×–50× wall-clock speedups over synchronous baselines.

## Results

On GSM8K (RhoMath-1B), TBA achieves 54.6% pass@1 accuracy, outperforming VinePPO (~52.8%) and GRPO (49.05%) while delivering a ~50× wall-clock speedup over VinePPO and 1.5× over speed-optimized async DPO. On TL;DR summarization (Pythia 410M), TBA reaches 0.86 win rate with ~15 steps of off-policyness, surpassing Online DPO (0.82), PPO (0.76), and RLOO (0.77) at the same off-policy level, with ~5× speedups over async DPO. On MATH-500 (Qwen 2.5 7B), TBA' matches or exceeds Dr. GRPO, particularly in highly off-policy settings. For automated red-teaming, TBA achieves comparable or better diversity-toxicity tradeoffs with ~7× speedups over the synchronous GFlowNet baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TBA consistently outperforms all baselines at matched or higher off-policyness levels. On TL;DR PFT, TBA (0.86 win rate) beats Online DPO (0.82), PPO (0.76), and RLOO (0.77) at ~15 steps off-policy, while maintaining comparable KL/perplexity (1.13 vs. 1.10–1.12). On GSM8K, TBA (54.6%) surpasses VinePPO (~52.8%) by 1.8% and GRPO (49.05%) by 5.5%, with a ~50× speedup over VinePPO. For automated red-teaming, TBA reaches the diversity-toxicity Pareto frontier (cosine distance 0.42–0.49, 94.5–97.6% toxic prompts) in 1.7–2.9 hours on 4–64 V100s, versus 11.9 hours for the synchronous GFlowNet baseline [34], a ~7× speedup. On MATH-500 with Qwen 2.5 7B, TBA' performs competitively with Dr. GRPO [37], with the gap widening in favor of TBA' under higher off-policyness. Notably, TBA also outperforms the on-policy Online DPO setting (0.85 win rate) despite using off-policy data, demonstrating that the trajectory balance objective effectively handles distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate threads: the Trajectory Balance (TB) objective from GFlowNets [40, 23, 34], which provides a principled off-policy training signal for sequence generation, and asynchronous distributed RL architectures [46, 26] that decouple data generation from policy updates. Prior async RLHF work (Noukhovitch et al. [46]) applied on-policy or near-on-policy objectives (PPO, RLOO, Online DPO) in asynchronous settings and found that increasing off-policyness degrades performance. TBA's key conceptual departure is arguing that TB's inherent off-policy compatibility (inherited from GFlowNet theory) makes it the natural objective for truly asynchronous training, where replay buffer data can be arbitrarily stale. The paper also positions TB as off-policy amortized variational inference (forward KL) in contrast to on-policy RL's reverse-KL optimization [32], and builds its red-teaming experiments directly on Lee et al.'s [34] GFlowNet-based TB trainer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TBA's 4×–50× wall-clock speedups in RL post-training address a practical bottleneck that limits how quickly and cheaply LLMs can be aligned, and the finding that scaled search (more searcher nodes) consistently improves performance opens a path to leveraging large GPU clusters for exploration in sparse-reward settings. The paper's central empirical claim—that off-policy data does not harm (and can help) performance when paired with TB, directly contradicting concurrent findings from Noukhovitch et al. [46]—is well-supported across three tasks and model scales from 410M to 7B. Limitations include sensitivity to the KL coefficient β (requiring annealing schedules), high gradient variance at the trajectory level, and the fact that the largest model tested (Qwen 2.5 7B) used a simplified variant (TBA') without the full replay-buffer sampling strategy. Open questions include whether adding clipped importance-sampling ratios to the TB gradient would further improve robustness, whether partial energy functions can reduce variance, and whether the approach scales to much larger models or longer contexts.
