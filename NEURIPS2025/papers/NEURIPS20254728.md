## Problem

Always-on smart glasses need to know when a user is reading to trigger heavier models (OCR, VLMs) only when relevant, but no large-scale in-the-wild dataset or efficient method exists for this task. Prior reading detection studies relied on handcrafted gaze features in constrained lab settings, and OCR-based approaches are impractical on wearables due to power, bandwidth, and the fundamental ambiguity that text in view does not imply reading.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three optional modalities over a 2-second window: a 3D gaze ray sequence at 60 Hz, a 64×64 RGB crop (5° FoV, only 1/484 of the full 1408p frame) centered on the current gaze point, and a 6-DoF IMU/VIO head-pose sequence at 60 Hz. The primary output is a scalar reading confidence in [0,1]; extension heads produce 7-way reading-mode labels (not reading, walking, out loud, engaged, scanning, writing/typing, skimming) and 4-way reading-medium labels (not reading, print, digital, objects). Because of modality dropout, any subset of the three inputs is valid at inference time.

## Method

The architecture is a 137k-parameter multimodal transformer: modality-specific 3-layer CNN encoders (1D convolutions for gaze and IMU, 2D convolutions for the RGB crop) tokenize each signal into feature vectors, which are concatenated and processed by a 3-layer transformer encoder with a linear [CLS] head. The key training trick is modality dropout—each batch randomly uses one, two, or all three modalities with equal probability—so the learned representations remain useful when sensors are unavailable at inference. The central empirical insight is that the three modalities are complementary: gaze excels at detecting saccadic reading patterns even when text is visually occluded, RGB handles short-text and sign-reading where gaze patterns are ambiguous, and IMU disambiguates head-turning from reading. The model is explicitly designed for on-device real-time inference on Aria Gen 2 glasses, running comfortably within a 4-hour battery budget without offloading to a server.

## Results

On the Seattle test set, the combined gaze + RGB + IMU model achieves 86.9% accuracy and 88.1% F1 (P at 0.9 recall: 88.2%), outperforming the best single-modality model (gaze: 82.3% acc, RGB: 82.2% acc) by +4.6%. The model is extremely lightweight at 137k parameters and runs in real-time on-device on Aria Gen 2 glasses. Zero-shot transfer to the Columbus subset maintains reasonable performance, and the model generalizes to left-to-right non-English scripts (Bengali: 93.0% acc) but requires gaze augmentation for vertical (Chinese: 35.5% → 85.1% with rotation) and right-to-left (Arabic: 21.0% → 51.5% with flip) scripts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external reading-detection methods but instead ablates modality combinations as its primary comparison. Gaze and RGB individually achieve similar accuracy (~82%) but with complementary failure patterns: gaze handles low-light and distance cases where RGB fails, while RGB excels on short texts and signs where gaze patterns are ambiguous. Adding IMU as a secondary signal provides a consistent +2.6% (over gaze) or +1.3% (over RGB) improvement by disambiguating head-turning from reading. In cross-dataset evaluation, training on the in-the-wild Seattle data and testing on EGTEA Gaze+ yields 87.7% accuracy (only −1.9% from in-domain), whereas the reverse (EGTEA→Seattle) drops to 62.9% (−16.4%), demonstrating that constrained-environment training does not transfer to real-world reading scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the reading detection literature (Kelton et al. 2019, Landsmann et al. 2019) that relied on handcrafted gaze features (fixations, saccades) in constrained lab settings, showing that a learned transformer on diverse in-the-wild data makes such feature engineering unnecessary. It also extends egocentric activity recognition datasets (Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, EGTEA Gaze+) where reading is a tiny, restricted subset, by introducing the first large-scale (100-hour) multimodal reading-specific dataset with 60 Hz eye tracking, hard negatives, and diverse real-world scenarios. The method itself is a lightweight multimodal transformer that repurposes the foveal-crop idea from gaze-guided attention in action recognition, combined with modality dropout for flexible inference on wearable hardware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first practical, on-device (137k params, 4+ hr battery on Aria Gen 2) reading recognition system that can serve as an efficient gate for invoking heavier OCR/VLM pipelines on always-on smart glasses, addressing a key bottleneck in contextual wearable AI. The dataset extends reading-comprehension research from controlled cognitive labs to unconstrained, multimodal, in-the-wild settings, opening new avenues for studying reading behavior at scale. Open questions include personalization for individual reading speed/style, optimal dynamic modality activation for power savings, and robust generalization to vertical and right-to-left scripts without inference-time augmentation.
