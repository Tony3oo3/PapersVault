## Problem

Existing unsupervised adversarial fine-tuning methods for visual encoders (e.g., FARE) suffer from two critical limitations: training instability in early stages that causes sharp drops in clean data accuracy, and a suboptimal robustness–accuracy trade-off that lacks principled control. No prior method provides a structured mechanism to explicitly balance improving adversarial robustness while preserving nominal performance on clean inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252752/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

LORE takes standard RGB images (no labels or text prompts required) and produces a fine-tuned vision encoder whose embeddings remain close to the frozen pretrained reference on clean inputs while resisting adversarial perturbations. The proximity constraint is scale-invariant via a per-sample margin m(x) = ‖φ_θ₀(x)‖², so the allowable deviation adapts to each sample's embedding magnitude. The fine-tuned encoder is then used for zero-shot classification, OOD evaluation, and embedding interpretability assessment against GPT-4-generated text templates.

## Method

LORE formulates adversarial fine-tuning as a semi-infinite constrained optimization problem: minimize the worst-case embedding distance under ℓ∞-bounded PGD perturbations, subject to a per-sample constraint that clean embeddings stay within ρ·‖φ_θ₀(x)‖² of the frozen reference. The constraint is solved via Lagrangian duality, where the dual variable λ_ω(x) is parameterized as a lightweight two-layer MLP (hidden dim 512, Softplus activation) that takes the reference model's embeddings as input, yielding input-dependent constraint weighting. Training alternates K=5 primal gradient steps on the encoder θ with a single dual gradient step on ω, using the clean loss to guide λ_ω updates. The hyperparameter ρ provides a single, interpretable knob controlling the robustness–accuracy frontier, and the scale-invariant constraint provably bounds cosine-similarity deviation by 2√ρ, preserving image–text alignment in vision-language models.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification with ViT-B/32 CLIP (trained on ImageNet), LORE consistently outperforms FARE across all perturbation budgets. At ε=2, LORE² achieves 57.0% average zero-shot clean accuracy vs. FARE²'s 54.1% (+2.9), and 42.5% adversarial accuracy vs. FARE²'s 39.4% (+3.1). At ε=4, the gap widens: LORE⁴ reaches 50.2% clean and 22.5% adversarial accuracy vs. FARE⁴'s 44.7% and 18.2% respectively. The unmodified CLIP baseline has near-zero adversarial robustness (<0.1% at all ε), while LORE maintains clean accuracy within 1–4% of the original model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FARE (Schlarmann et al., ICML 2024), the primary unsupervised baseline, LORE wins on every metric across all perturbation budgets: at ε=4 training, LORE⁴ achieves 50.2% average zero-shot clean accuracy vs. FARE⁴'s 44.7% (+5.5) and 22.5% adversarial accuracy at ε=4 vs. FARE⁴'s 18.2% (+4.3). Against the unmodified CLIP baseline (Radford et al., 2021), LORE sacrifices at most ~4% clean accuracy (50.2% vs. 64.0% at ε=4 training) but gains from near-zero to 22.5% adversarial robustness at ε=4, a dramatic improvement. In the supervised extension (Table 5), applying LORE's ℓ₂ proximity constraint to TeCoA (Mao et al., 2023) yields massive clean-accuracy gains: TeCoA⁴+ℓ₂ reaches 73.1% clean vs. TeCoA⁴'s 49.6% (+23.5) while also improving robust accuracy from 19.8% to 49.1%. LORE also outperforms FARE on OOD robustness (ImageNet-C) and embedding interpretability (cosine similarity with GPT-4 text templates), where FARE² degrades under corruptions but LORE² maintains or even exceeds the original CLIP's performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

LORE extends the unsupervised adversarial fine-tuning framework of FARE (Schlarmann et al., ICML 2024), which minimizes the distance between clean and perturbed CLIP embeddings, by replacing its implicit regularization with an explicit Lagrangian dual constraint on embedding-space proximity to a frozen reference model. The constrained optimization machinery is borrowed from Robey et al. (2021) and Chamon et al. (2020, 2021), who applied semi-infinite constrained learning with Lagrangian duality to supervised adversarial robustness; LORE's key conceptual move is transplanting this framework to the label-free, embedding-space setting where the constraint becomes a per-sample proximity bound rather than a classification-loss threshold. This positions LORE as a principled upgrade over both FARE (which lacks any explicit trade-off control) and naive additive regularization (which the paper shows yields a strictly worse Pareto frontier).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners fine-tuning foundation visual encoders (CLIP, DINOv2) for deployment in adversarial environments, LORE provides a single interpretable hyperparameter (ρ) that directly controls the robustness–clean-accuracy trade-off, avoiding the trial-and-error of tuning regularization weights. The paper's limitation that the method is bounded by the quality of the frozen reference model, and its heuristic neural-network parameterization of Lagrange multipliers, leave open questions about whether better dual variable representations or adaptive anchors could push the frontier further. A deeper theoretical treatment of the non-convex duality gap and the generalization properties of the constrained encoder family would strengthen the current empirical-only guarantees.
