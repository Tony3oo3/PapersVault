## Problem

Existing TTS benchmarks rely on restricted text domains, limited linguistic diversity, and costly human evaluations that are non-reproducible and struggle with multilingual or prosodically rich content. There is no systematic, scalable framework that both generates progressively harder test cases targeting nuanced challenges (emotions, paralinguistics, code-switching, complex pronunciation, syntactic complexity, questions) and automatically evaluates the subjective dimensions of speech quality that traditional metrics like WER and MOS cannot capture.

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds 1,645 text prompts (organized into 6 categories × 4 complexity depths) to candidate TTS systems, producing ~420 minutes of synthesized audio per system. For evaluation, the LALM judge receives two audio clips (randomly labeled T1/T2 to avoid positional bias), the original text, the category label, and a structured rubric prompt specifying the target dimension (prosody, emotion, pronunciation, etc.) with chain-of-thought and timestamp-based reasoning instructions. The judge outputs a structured JSON containing natural-language justifications per system, a comparative analysis annotated as subtle or significant, scalar scores in [0,3] per system, and a winner label (0=tie, 1, or 2), which are aggregated into a win-rate metric where 0.5 indicates parity with the baseline.

## Method

The framework has two core components: (1) an iterative LLM-based test-generation pipeline that starts from 140 human-curated BASE-TTS seed prompts, expands breadth via LLM curation (adding 50 diverse samples per category), then applies three rounds of category-specific depth refinement (e.g., appending sequential questions, expanding isolated foreign words into idiomatic phrases, adding nested paralinguistic cues) to produce progressively harder utterances; and (2) a model-as-a-judge evaluation protocol where a Large Audio Language Model (Gemini 2.5 Pro as primary judge) performs A/B comparison of two TTS outputs against a strong reference baseline, using category-specific rubrics and inference-time scaling. The approach is zero-shot with respect to the judge—no fine-tuning is required—and the win-rate metric is validated against human preferences (Spearman ρ up to 90.5%) and shown to be stable across multiple LALM judges (Kendall's W = 0.97). The paper also ablates text normalization strategies and voice sensitivity, showing that LLM-based normalization (GPT-4.1-mini) substantially improves complex pronunciation performance (51.7% → 76.7% win-rate) while basic rule-based TN can degrade quality.

## Results

On EmergentTTS-Eval (1,645 test cases across 6 categories), model-as-a-judge win-rates against the gpt-4o-mini-tts (Alloy) baseline range from 8.90% (Suno Bark) to 65.17% (GPT-4o-audio-preview with Ballad voice and strong prompting). GPT-4o-Audio (Ballad) achieves the strongest expressiveness scores at 88.84% in Emotions and 82.14% in Paralinguistics, while HumeAI ranks as the second-best closed-source system at 42.73% overall. Among open-source models, Orpheus-TTS leads at 30.12% overall, with all open-source systems performing very poorly on Complex Pronunciation (≤10.61% win-rate). Strong prompting consistently improves performance, e.g., GPT-4o-mini-tts rises from the 50% baseline to 56.32% overall win-rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All seven open-source baselines fall well below the 50% parity threshold against gpt-4o-mini-tts (Alloy). Suno Bark is the weakest at 8.90% overall, with near-zero win-rate in Emotions (0.00%) and very low scores in Paralinguistics (6.60%) and Complex Pronunciation (8.36%). Sesame1B (15.96%) and Zyphra/Zonos (16.55%) show similar deficits, with Sesame1B particularly struggling in Emotions (7.32%) due to flat intonation and random interjections. Tortoise-TTS (17.67%) and MiniCPM (22.36%) perform marginally better but still trail the baseline substantially, with MiniCPM failing completely on Complex Pronunciation (1.84%). Qwen 2.5 Omni (28.77% with strong prompting) and Orpheus-TTS (30.12%) are the strongest open-source systems, with Orpheus-TTS showing particular strength in Paralinguistics (39.82%) and Syntactic Complexity (38.92%). In contrast, closed-source systems like HumeAI (42.73%), ElevenLabs (33.89%), and Deepgram Aura-2 (32.44%) approach or exceed parity in several categories, while GPT-4o variants with strong prompting and the Ballad voice dramatically surpass all baselines, reaching 65.17% overall and 88.84% in Emotions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends BASE-TTS (EmergentTTS), which introduced 20 hand-crafted prompts per category across seven linguistically motivated TTS phenomena, by automating both test-case generation (via iterative LLM refinement) and evaluation (via LALM-as-judge), scaling from 140 to 1,645 samples and adding a new 'Complex Pronunciation' category. The model-as-a-judge paradigm draws on the broader LLM-as-judge trend in NLP and prior audio-specific work (SALMONN, WavReward, Chen et al.), but is the first to apply out-of-the-box LALMs like Gemini 2.5 Pro as reward models for subjective TTS dimensions such as expressiveness, prosody, and pronunciation accuracy. The iterative depth-refinement strategy is conceptually analogous to instruction-tuning difficulty escalation (e.g., WizardLM), repurposed here to generate progressively harder TTS stimuli.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a critical gap: traditional TTS metrics (WER, AutoMOS) show only 21–31% Spearman correlation with human preference, while the proposed LALM win-rate reaches 90.5%, making it a practical, reproducible, and scalable alternative to costly human MOS panels. Open questions remain around evaluation cost (~$50 per system with Gemini 2.5 Pro), LALM biases toward literary phrasing, the limitation to Latin-script foreign words rather than native character sets, and occasional judge hallucinations on subjective dimensions. The open-sourced, extensible framework positions itself as a standard stress-test for next-generation TTS systems, particularly as they are deployed in increasingly complex real-world scenarios (code-switching, technical notation, emotional narration).
