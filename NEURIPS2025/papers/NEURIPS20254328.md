## Problem

Most counterfactual inference frameworks assume acyclic structural causal models (DAGs), yet many real-world systems—gene regulatory networks, hormonal feedback axes, market equilibria—contain cycles that violate acyclicity. For cyclic SCMs, it is unclear whether structural equations admit unique solutions, and the theoretical foundations for soft (parametric) interventions such as shift-scale changes remain largely undeveloped, leaving counterfactual queries in such settings ill-posed.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a cyclic SCM specified by structural equations f: X×E→X over closed coordinate domains, a product exogenous noise distribution P_E, and shift-scale intervention parameters (scale a_j, shift b_j) applied to a chosen subset of variables. It produces the joint counterfactual distribution P_{(X,X')} over the twin network, individual counterfactual outcome mappings (e.g., (c,i)↦(C',I')), and sub-Gaussian concentration bounds for 1-Lipschitz functionals of the counterfactual. A key structural constraint is the global ℓ^p-contraction constant κ<1 on f and the bounded-gain condition |a_j|≤1 on all scale coefficients, which together guarantee well-posedness.

## Method

The paper establishes that a global ℓ^p-contraction condition (‖f(x,e)−f(y,e)‖_p ≤ κ‖x−y‖_p with κ<1) on the structural mechanism implies the cyclic SCM is 'simple'—uniquely solvable with respect to every subset of endogenous variables—via the Banach fixed-point theorem applied to coordinate-wise restriction maps. It then proves that shift-scale interventions with bounded scale coefficients (|a_j|≤1) preserve the contraction constant on the twin SCM, so the intervened model remains simple and the counterfactual distribution is well-defined. The class of such interventions is shown to be closed under composition, with the composed scale being a product of individual scales (hence still ≤1 in magnitude). Under an additional 1-Lipschitz-in-noise assumption and Gaussian exogenous variables, the paper derives explicit sub-Gaussian tail bounds for counterfactual functionals with proxy 2(1−κ)^{−2}σ², showing sharp concentration around the mean. The twin-network construction is shown to be equivalent to Pearl's abduction–action–prediction procedure under unique solvability, grounding the counterfactual semantics.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical benchmarks. The main quantitative contributions are: (1) a global ℓ^p-contraction condition with constant κ < 1 guarantees unique solvability (simplicity) of cyclic SCMs for every subset of variables (Theorem 1); (2) shift-scale interventions with bounded scale coefficients |a_j| ≤ 1 preserve the contraction constant κ, ensuring the intervened twin SCM remains simple (Theorem 2); (3) under 1-Lipschitz noise and Gaussian exogenous variables with Σ ⪯ σ²I, counterfactual functionals satisfy a sub-Gaussian tail bound P(h(X,X′) − E[h] ≥ t) ≤ exp(−t²/(4(1−κ)⁻²σ²)) (Proposition 2). A worked linear example (Section 4) shows a shift-scale intervention on income (α=0.8, β=1.0) raises consumption by ~29% and income by ~82%, while reducing the C–I correlation from 0.75 to 0.69.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is purely theoretical and does not compare against any empirical baselines or competing methods. The contributions are foundational: it establishes that the class of globally contractive cyclic SCMs is simple (uniquely solvable), that shift-scale interventions with |a_j| ≤ 1 preserve simplicity of the twin SCM, and that the class is closed under composition. The paper positions itself relative to Bongers et al. (2021), whose Proposition 8.2 presupposes simplicity and gives closure under do-interventions and marginalization but does not provide sufficient analytic conditions for simplicity; Theorem 2 here fills that gap by giving a concrete contraction-based criterion. No empirical comparisons, ablations, or benchmark evaluations are reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the formal framework of Bongers et al. (2021) for cyclic SCMs, adopting their definitions of simple SCMs, unique solvability, and twin networks, and on Mooij et al. (2013) for the ODE-based semantics that give cyclic structural equations causal meaning. It departs from the dominant counterfactual inference literature (Pearl's do-operator, deep SCMs, diffusion-based counterfactuals) by replacing the acyclicity assumption with a global ℓᵖ-contraction condition and by studying soft shift-scale interventions rather than hard do-interventions. The key conceptual move is showing that the Banach fixed-point theorem, applied to the structural equations, simultaneously guarantees unique solvability of the base model, the intervened twin model, and compositional stability of sequential interventions—thereby making the entire twin-network counterfactual machinery well-posed in the cyclic setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a genuine theoretical gap: prior counterfactual frameworks (deep SCMs, diffusion causal models, backtracking counterfactuals) all presuppose DAGs, yet many important systems (gene regulatory networks, hormonal feedback axes, market equilibria) are inherently cyclic. The contraction-based sufficient condition is clean and verifiable, and the sub-Gaussian concentration result provides the first tail bounds for counterfactual functionals in cyclic models. However, the global contraction requirement is restrictive—many realistic feedback systems are only locally contractive—and the concentration result depends on Gaussian noise, leaving heavy-tailed or bounded-moment settings open. Extending the framework to nonlinear, stochastic, or unbounded-gain interventions, and to data-driven certification of contraction constants from black-box simulators, are the most pressing open questions.
