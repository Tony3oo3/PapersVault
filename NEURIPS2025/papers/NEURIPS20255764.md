## Problem

Existing text-to-audio-video (T2AV) generation methods assume full correspondence between audio and visual features, but real-world audiovisual scenes exhibit only partial and asymmetric alignment—visual elements like roads or buildings may produce no sound, while audio events like wind may lack visual presence. Naively enforcing full cross-modal alignment under these conditions injects mismatched information into joint representations, degrading both semantic consistency and temporal synchronization in generated outputs.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single natural language text prompt and produces a 10-second synchronized video clip (16 fps) and audio waveform (48 kHz). Inference follows a cascaded structure: video is first generated from text via a pretrained CogVideoX1.5 diffusion model, and the resulting visual latent is then projected through a lightweight adapter to condition a separately fine-tuned AudioLDM-based audio generator, ensuring the soundtrack is grounded in the actual generated visual content rather than only the text. The aligned latent dimensionality for audio is fixed at 512, and both single-modal (T2A, T2V) and multi-modal (TV2A, TA2V) diffusion backbones are independently fine-tuned before the cascaded pipeline is assembled.

## Method

SAVA introduces a masked latent adaptation module that learns to isolate only the shared latent dimensions between video and audio. Learnable adapter networks reparameterize frozen pretrained encoder outputs, and two mask networks—each conditioned on both modalities' embeddings—produce binary supports that filter out modality-specific noise before cross-modal reconstruction (audio decoded from masked video latent, video from masked audio latent). An L1 sparsity penalty on the masks, combined with a direct alignment loss, drives the model to retain only the minimal set of jointly predictive dimensions; the paper proves this objective recovers exactly the minimal Markov blankets of the shared latent factors under standard causal and universality assumptions. In Stage II, LoRA fine-tuning adapts the diffusion backbones to consume the aligned cross-modal latent alongside the text embedding, and Stage III performs cascaded diffusion inference where the video latent is projected into an audio-guiding representation that conditions audio generation.

## Results

On VGGSound, the unidirectional SAVA setting achieves the best FVD (662.9), FAD (5.49), AVHScore (0.206), and CAVPSIM (0.165) across all baselines, with a 6.5% relative FVD reduction and 16.0% FAD reduction over the strongest baseline (CoDi). On AudioCaps, SAVA attains the highest AVHScore (0.142) and CAVPSIM (0.230), confirming superior cross-modal semantic and temporal alignment. The bidirectional variant further improves alignment (AVHScore 0.217, CAVPSIM 0.183 on VGGSound) at a modest FVD cost. In the V2A sub-task, SAVA achieves the lowest KL (2.128) and FAD (6.155), outperforming SpecVQGAN and SeeHear.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAVA outperforms TAVDiffusion [33] on every metric on both datasets, with particularly large gaps in FVD (662.9 vs. 956.3 on VGGSound) and AVHScore (0.206 vs. 0.162), indicating that TAVDiffusion's two-stream cross-attention alignment introduces more semantic noise. Against CoDi [41], SAVA reduces FAD by 16% (5.49 vs. 8.36) and improves AVHScore by nearly 2× (0.206 vs. 0.108) on VGGSound, showing that composable diffusion's shared prompt space is less effective at isolating cross-modal shared factors. JavisDiT [29] is the closest competitor on generative fidelity, achieving a slightly lower FVD on AudioCaps (801.2 vs. 817.6), but SAVA still leads on all alignment metrics (AVHScore 0.142 vs. 0.104, CAVPSIM 0.230 vs. 0.207), confirming that masked latent adaptation yields better temporal and semantic synchronization. In the V2A sub-task, SAVA beats SpecVQGAN [20] on KL (2.128 vs. 3.290) and FAD (6.155 vs. 7.736), and outperforms SeeHear-Full [52] on KL (2.128 vs. 2.619) and FAD (6.155 vs. 7.316), validating the alignment module's transferability to video-conditioned audio synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAVA departs from prior T2AV methods (TAVDiffusion, CoDi, JavisDiT) that assume full cross-modal alignment by introducing a learnable masking mechanism grounded in causal inference, specifically the recovery of minimal Markov blankets. It builds on the SmartCLIP observation that full alignment in shared embedding spaces introduces semantic noise, and repurposes the adapter/LoRA paradigm for lightweight adaptation of frozen pretrained diffusion encoders (CogVideoX, AudioLDM). The theoretical framing via d-separation and mutual information on latent DAGs is borrowed from causal discovery literature but applied here for the first time to selective cross-modal reconstruction in generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underappreciated failure mode in multimodal generation: forcing alignment on modality-specific features degrades both semantic consistency and temporal synchronization. The provable recovery guarantee (under standard universality assumptions) gives the masking mechanism a principled justification beyond heuristic sparsity. However, the cascaded unidirectional inference limits expressiveness for tightly coupled audio-visual content, generation quality remains bounded by frozen pretrained backbones, and the approach requires high-quality paired training data—extending to weakly supervised or truly bidirectional co-generation remains open.
