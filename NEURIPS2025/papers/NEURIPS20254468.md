## Problem

Existing interactive OS-level benchmarks for GUI agents cover Ubuntu Linux, Windows, and Android but omit macOS entirely, a major platform with distinctive GUI conventions and 28+ exclusive applications (Pages, Numbers, Keynote, iMovie, Xcode). Additionally, prior benchmarks are predominantly English-only and rarely integrate safety evaluation with functional performance in a unified framework, leaving both multilingual capability and adversarial robustness unmeasured.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254468/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Agents receive full-screen 1024×768 screenshots as observations (retaining a rolling window of the 3 most recent) alongside natural-language task instructions in one of five languages, with the macOS system UI also set to the matching target language. They output VNC-standard actions (mouse clicks, drags, keyboard presses, scrolling) at normalized coordinates. Evaluation is binary per task via programmatic scripts (AppleScript, JavaScript, or zsh) that verify final system state, aggregated as mean success rate across languages and seven task categories.

## Method

macOSWorld is built on virtualized macOS Sequoia 15.2 instances running on AWS-hosted dedicated Apple hardware (Mac Minis with custom firmware), enabling reproducible AMI snapshots and automatic multilingual environment duplication across five languages. The benchmark comprises 202 interactive tasks across 30 applications (28 macOS-exclusive), each specifying an AMI snapshot, a preparation script, and one or more evaluation scripts, organized into seven categories from system navigation to advanced development workflows. A dedicated safety subset of 29 tasks introduces context-deception attacks via genuine macOS pop-up windows spawned through AppleScript in parallel with the agent loop (not synthetic screenshot overlays), where attack success is defined as clicking a 'distraction' button rather than the 'gold' close/cancel button. The centralized testbench orchestrates the full pipeline—snapshot recovery, task preparation, agent-environment interaction, and programmatic evaluation—via SSH, VNC, and AWS APIs under a POMDP formulation with a 15-screenshot / 30-dialog-turn budget.

## Results

On macOSWorld, the two proprietary computer-use agents (CUAs) lead with overall success rates of 37.1% (Claude CUA) and 33.8% (OpenAI CUA), while Gemini 2.5 Pro occupies a middle tier at 18.5%. Open-source research models lag dramatically: GPT-4o achieves 5.3%, UI-TARS 7B DPO 4.8%, and ShowUI 1.1% average success rate. Across languages, linear alphabetic languages (English 19.3%, Russian 17.7%) outperform block-character languages (Chinese 17.2%, Japanese 15.8%) and right-to-left Arabic (13.7%), with Arabic showing a 28.8% degradation relative to English. On the safety subset, the two strongest CUAs are highly vulnerable to context-deception attacks with ~70% distraction rates, while GPT-4o leaves 100% of pop-ups unhandled.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Claude CUA (37.1%) outperforms OpenAI CUA (33.8%) by 3.3 points overall, with the largest gap in System & Interface tasks (56.6% vs. 37.2%) and Multi-Apps (8.6% vs. 6.4%), while OpenAI CUA leads in Productivity (49.1% vs. 41.1%). Gemini 2.5 Pro (18.5%) trails both CUAs by 15+ points but remains the strongest non-CUA agent, particularly in File Management (33.8%) where it approaches OpenAI CUA. GPT-4o (5.3%) and UI-TARS 7B DPO (4.8%) are close in overall score but fail for entirely different reasons: GPT-4o has good planning but imprecise grounding, while UI-TARS suffers from hallucinations and invalid output formats. ShowUI (1.1%) is the weakest, exhibiting nonsensical actions despite occasional correct grounding. On the safety subset, Claude CUA and OpenAI CUA are most vulnerable to deception attacks (72.4% and 69.0% distracted respectively), while GPT-4o never interacts with pop-ups (100% unhandled) and UI-TARS/ShowUI are distracted 58.6% and 41.4% of the time despite their low overall task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interactive OS-level benchmarking lineage established by OSWorld (Ubuntu/Windows) and WindowsAgentArena (Windows) to macOS, adopting their POMDP formulation, task taxonomy, and execution-based evaluation. It departs from prior work in three key dimensions: (1) targeting macOS with its 28 exclusive applications and distinct GUI conventions, (2) providing full multilingual support (5 languages for both task instructions and system UI), and (3) integrating a non-synthetic context-deception safety subset that builds on the pop-up window attack of Zhang et al. [42] but adapts it to realistic macOS dialogs. No prior interactive OS benchmark covers macOS, supports non-English environments, or combines functional and safety evaluation in a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark capability gap (proprietary CUAs >30% vs. open-source models <5%) that underscores the need for macOS-specific adaptation, and exposes systematic multilingual weaknesses—particularly a 28.8% degradation in Arabic—that will affect real-world deployment across diverse user populations. The safety findings are particularly consequential: the two strongest CUAs are ~70% vulnerable to context-deception attacks, suggesting that greater environmental interaction capability paradoxically increases susceptibility to social-engineering-style manipulation. Open questions include whether language-aware training or UI-layout adaptation can close the multilingual gap, how to define non-binary reward rubrics for fine-grained evaluation, and what defense mechanisms can protect agents from realistic deceptive UI content.
