## Problem

Foundation models have transformed computer vision and NLP, but no general-purpose pre-training framework exists for combinatorial optimization. Existing ML4CO approaches are task-specific (branching, cutting, LNS) and cannot transfer across problem types, while the only prior 'foundation model' attempt (Li et al.) focuses on sample generation within a single problem domain. The core challenge is learning robust representations for variables, constraints, and entire instances across diverse MILP structures, distributions, and scales without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a MILP instance encoded as a bipartite graph with variable nodes (features: type, objective coefficient, bounds, reduced cost), constraint nodes (features: cosine similarity with objective, normalized bias), and edges representing non-zero constraint coefficients. For graphs exceeding a size threshold (g_size=20,000), adaptive partitioning splits them into balanced sub-graphs preserving the variable-to-constraint ratio. Outputs are node-level embeddings (64-dim per variable/constraint) and instance-level graph embeddings, which are fed directly into downstream heads (GBDT for solution prediction, MLP for LNS policy, KNN for solver configuration) without any fine-tuning of the pre-trained encoder.

## Method

OPTFM introduces a multi-view graph transformer that achieves O(N) attention complexity by removing softmax and using Frobenius-normalized linear projections, while directly incorporating the sparse adjacency matrix into each attention layer (unlike Wu et al. who fuse a separate GNN at the output). The attention is decomposed into self-attention within variable and constraint sets plus two half cross-attentions (C→V and V→C), respecting the bipartite structure and semantic asymmetry of MILPs. Pre-training is hierarchical: first, node-level graph reconstruction masks a% of edges and trains the model to predict variable-constraint connectivity via cross-entropy; second, with the node encoder frozen, instance-level contrastive learning treats different random partitions of the same graph as positive pairs and uses ProxyAnchorLoss to learn discriminative graph embeddings. This decoupled pipeline bounds memory by sub-graph size rather than full graph size, enabling training on instances with tens of millions of nodes.

## Results

On the LNS downstream task (Table 2), OPTFM achieves the best primal gap among all learning-based methods across four problem families: 0.93% on Set Covering, 0.05% on Maximum Independent Set, 1.93% on Combinatorial Auction, and 3.02% on Maximum Cut, with corresponding Primal Integrals of 16,782, 178.55, 2,099.5, and 3,845. On the MIPLIB 2017 real-world benchmark (Table 4), OPTFM achieves a 2.92% average gap and wins on 171/240 instances, substantially outperforming RL-LNS (8.07% gap, 114/240 wins) while approaching Gurobi (1.98% gap, 203/240 wins). On the solver configuration task (Table 5), OPTFM yields a 3.24% average objective improvement on Item Placement with 74/100 wins, far exceeding SGFormer (2.55%, 18 wins) and MILPTune (0.40%, 3 wins).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPTFM outperforms all learning-based baselines (RL-LNS, Branching, CL-LNS, Fast-T2T, AnySCP) and the SCIP solver on every problem family in the LNS task, with the largest margins on Combinatorial Auction (1.93% vs. 2.05% for CL-LNS) and Maximum Cut (3.02% vs. 3.03% for CL-LNS). Against the commercial Gurobi solver, OPTFM is competitive on Set Covering (0.93% vs. 0.75%) and Combinatorial Auction (1.93% vs. 1.44%) but trails on MIS (0.05% vs. 0%) and Maximum Cut (3.02% vs. 0.62%). Among pre-training baselines, OPTFM consistently beats SGFormer [41] (e.g., 1.93% vs. 2.59% gap on CA) and GCN [12] (1.93% vs. 3.11% on CA), confirming the benefit of the multi-view transformer architecture. On the MIPLIB 2017 real-world set, OPTFM (2.92% gap, 171/240 wins) more than doubles the win count of RL-LNS (114/240) and approaches Gurobi (203/240). On solver configuration, OPTFM achieves 74/100 wins on Item Placement versus 18 for SGFormer and 3 for MILPTune, demonstrating the value of the instance-level contrastive pre-training. The ablated variants (OPTFM-Nocross, OPTFM-WGNN) are consistently weaker than full OPTFM, validating the cross-attention and integrated edge-matrix design.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPTFM builds directly on the O(N) linear attention mechanism of Wu et al. (SGFormer, NeurIPS 2024) and the bipartite graph encoding of MILP problems from Gasse et al. (NeurIPS 2019), but departs from both in key ways: it integrates the sparse adjacency matrix directly into each attention layer rather than fusing a separate GNN at the output, and it decomposes all-pair attention into semantically structured self-attention (within variables/constraints) and cross-attention (between them) to respect the bipartite structure. Conceptually, it positions itself against the task-specific ML4CO literature (GNN-GBDT, LIGHT-MILPOPT, GOAL, MTL) and against Li et al.'s (ICLR 2025) 'foundation model' that only addressed sample generation for policy transfer within a single problem domain, arguing that a truly general representation learning framework for variables, constraints, and instances is still missing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single pre-trained model, used without fine-tuning, can generate embeddings that outperform task-specific end-to-end approaches across solution prediction, large neighborhood search, and solver configuration—suggesting that general-purpose representation learning is viable for combinatorial optimization. However, the scope is limited to MILP instances encoded as bipartite graphs with a single-layer, single-head attention, and the pre-training data (MIPLIB 2017 with perturbations) is relatively small (1,065 base instances). Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-MILP combinatorial problems (e.g., TSP, scheduling), whether deeper or multi-head architectures would further improve representations, and whether the contrastive graph-level pre-training can be replaced by more informative objectives.
