## Problem

Standard offline imitation learning assumes access to expert or near-expert demonstrations, but in many practical domains (autonomous driving, healthcare) only undesirable or sub-optimal demonstrations are available. The key gap is learning a desirable policy that actively avoids bad behaviors from a small set of negative examples, without expert data, while also leveraging a much larger unlabeled dataset of unknown quality. Prior approaches like SafeDICE mix undesirable and unlabeled data and imitate the resulting mixed policy, which can be low-quality and lead to incorrect learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes two datasets: a small set of undesirable demonstration trajectories (D^UN, e.g., 5–100 trajectories in Mujoco, 100 in Safety-Gym) and a much larger unlabeled dataset (D^MIX) of mixed quality containing random, undesirable, and expert trajectories. The output is a state-to-action policy extracted via advantage-weighted behavioral cloning (WBC) from the learned Q-function. A key property is that the training outcome is theoretically independent of the quality of the unlabeled dataset, thanks to the occupancy correction mechanism, making the method robust to unknown data composition.

## Method

UNIQ formulates avoidance learning as a cooperative (non-adversarial) MaxEnt IRL objective that minimizes expected reward on undesirable state-action pairs while maximizing it elsewhere, in contrast to the adversarial min-max structure of standard imitation learning. The objective is transformed from reward space to Q-space via inverse Q-learning, but this yields a non-convex objective in Q (due to the log-sum-exp value function appearing with a negative sign). To handle this, the paper leverages Extreme Q-Learning to decompose training into alternating convex updates: a Q-update minimizing a linear-plus-regularizer loss, and a V-update minimizing an exponential Extreme-V loss. For sample efficiency, an occupancy correction ratio τ(s,a) = ρ^UN(s,a)/ρ^MIX(s,a) is estimated via a discriminator trained to separate undesirable from unlabeled data, allowing the expectation over the small undesirable dataset to be unbiasedly approximated using the larger unlabeled dataset. The final policy is extracted via weighted behavioral cloning using the advantage function A(s,a) = (Q(s,a) − V(s))/β.

## Results

On Mujoco (unconstrained RL), UNIQ achieves returns close to the expert baseline across all four tasks: 105.9±4.0 on Walker2d (vs. 107.9 expert), 104.4±10.5 on Ant (vs. 117.5 expert), 75.7±6.8 on HalfCheetah (vs. 84.6 expert), and 73.5±20.6 on Hopper (vs. 110.8 expert), while all baselines fall far short. On Safety-Gym (constrained RL), UNIQ achieves the lowest cost among all methods in every task (e.g., 23.0±4.7 on Point-Button vs. 91.0 for SafeDICE and 120.2 for BC-mix), with returns within 1–2 points of the expert policy. The occupancy correction mechanism is critical: using a converged τ yields substantially better results than a uniform τ=1 (e.g., 105.9 vs. 12.8 on Walker2d).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIQ decisively outperforms all four baselines on Mujoco return: it achieves 75.7 on HalfCheetah vs. 3.1 (SafeDICE), 2.2 (DWBC), 3.2 (IPL), and −2.8 (LS-IQ), and 105.9 on Walker2d vs. 41.7 (SafeDICE), 38.8 (DWBC), 11.8 (IPL), and −0.1 (LS-IQ). In Safety-Gym, UNIQ achieves the lowest cost in every task, with the largest margins over SafeDICE (e.g., 23.0 vs. 91.0 cost on Point-Button; 148.6 vs. 201.3 on Car-Button) and BC-mix (e.g., 27.1 vs. 48.8 on Point-Goal). SafeDICE is the closest competitor in the safety setting but still trails UNIQ by 2–4× in cost on the harder Button tasks. LS-IQ and IPL, designed for expert or preference-based imitation, degrade severely in the avoidance setting (often negative returns on Mujoco), confirming that standard imitation objectives are ill-suited for learning from bad demonstrations. DWBC is competitive on Mujoco Ant (84.6 vs. UNIQ's 104.4) but struggles on Safety-Gym costs, while BC-mix (plain behavioral cloning on the full unlabeled set) performs poorly across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNIQ extends the inverse Q-learning (IQ-Learn) framework from the standard 'imitate expert' setting to a fundamentally different 'avoid undesirable' setting, where the max-min adversarial objective becomes a cooperative min-min problem. It borrows the Extreme Q-Learning (XQL) machinery to handle the resulting non-convexity in Q-space, and adapts the DICE-style occupancy correction to unbiasedly estimate expectations over the undesirable policy using a large unlabeled dataset. The most direct prior work, SafeDICE, also targets learning from undesirable demonstrations but relies on imitating a negatively-weighted mixture of undesirable and unlabeled policies, which the authors argue is unreliable when unlabeled data quality is unknown. UNIQ's occupancy-ratio correction decouples the learning objective from the quality of the unlabeled data, a key conceptual departure from SafeDICE and from standard DICE methods that match the learner's distribution to the expert's.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: in domains like autonomous driving and healthcare, one often has abundant records of bad outcomes but few (or no) expert demonstrations, making 'learn to avoid' the more realistic learning target. The occupancy-correction mechanism is a clean, theoretically grounded tool that could be adapted to other settings where one must weight samples by an unknown density ratio. Open questions remain around handling multiple quality tiers of undesirable data, extracting partially-good actions from bad trajectories, and scaling beyond the relatively low-dimensional Mujoco and Safety-Gym benchmarks. The assumption that an entire trajectory is uniformly undesirable is a simplification that limits real-world applicability.
