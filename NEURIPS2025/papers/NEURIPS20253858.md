## Problem

While GRPO and DPO have been applied to LLM reasoning, their relative strengths in autoregressive image generation remain unexplored. Image generation introduces domain-specific challenges absent in text—text-image consistency, aesthetic quality, and the need for learned reward models rather than rule-based verification—yet prior work lacks a systematic comparison of these two RL algorithms under matched conditions, nor an analysis of how reward model choice and scaling strategies differentially affect in-domain performance versus out-of-domain generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253858/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes free-form text prompts as input and produces RGB images via sequential autoregressive prediction of discrete visual tokens (quantized by a VQ autoencoder). In-domain evaluation uses long, detailed compositional prompts from T2I-CompBench (5.6k prompts describing multi-object scenes with attributes and spatial relations), while out-of-domain evaluation uses short templated prompts from GenEval (e.g., 'a photo of a red car'), creating a substantial domain gap in prompt granularity that tests generalization robustness.

## Method

The paper conducts a controlled empirical study using Janus-Pro as the autoregressive baseline, training both GRPO (on-policy, group size 4, no value critic) and DPO (off-policy, highest/lowest-scoring image pairs per prompt) under matched computational budgets and identical training prompts. It then varies three axes: (1) reward model type (HPS, ImageReward, Unified Reward, Fine-tuned ORM, Metric Reward), (2) scaling of sampled images per prompt (4→16), in-domain data volume (1×→3× via GPT-4o-generated prompts), and iterative training cycles (0→3), and (3) evaluation on both in-domain (T2I-CompBench) and out-of-domain (GenEval) benchmarks. Key findings: DPO outperforms GRPO in-domain by ~11.5% on average, while GRPO generalizes better out-of-domain by ~1.1%; DPO is more sensitive to reward model choice; and the intrinsic generalization capacity of a reward model (measured via best-of-4 selection) predicts the RL algorithm's generalization performance.

## Results

On the in-domain T2I-CompBench benchmark, DPO consistently outperforms GRPO across all five reward models, with an average in-domain advantage of 11.53% (e.g., DPO 57.81 vs. GRPO 50.01 with Metric Reward; DPO 55.67 vs. GRPO 49.76 with ImageReward). On the out-of-domain GenEval benchmark, GRPO reverses this trend, surpassing DPO by an average of 1.14% (e.g., GRPO 80.99 vs. DPO 80.03 with Unified Reward; GRPO 79.26 vs. DPO 77.67 with ImageReward). Both methods substantially improve over the untrained Janus-Pro baseline (38.56 on T2I-CompBench, 78.04 on GenEval), with the best in-domain gain reaching +19.25% (DPO with Metric Reward) and the best out-of-domain gain reaching +2.95% (GRPO with Unified Reward).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GRPO and DPO against the untrained Janus-Pro autoregressive baseline. Both RL methods substantially outperform the baseline on T2I-CompBench (e.g., DPO with Metric Reward: 57.81 vs. 38.56, a +19.25% gain) and on GenEval (e.g., GRPO with Unified Reward: 80.99 vs. 78.04, a +2.95% gain). On in-domain T2I-CompBench, DPO wins over GRPO across all reward models, with the largest margin of 7.80% under Metric Reward (57.81 vs. 50.01) and a consistent 4–6% gap under HPS, ImageReward, and Unified Reward. On out-of-domain GenEval, GRPO wins over DPO across all reward models, with the largest margin of 2.42% under HPS (79.18 vs. 77.31) and a peak of 0.96% under Unified Reward (80.99 vs. 80.03). Notably, DPO's out-of-domain performance is more sensitive to reward model choice (range of 2.72) compared to GRPO (range of 1.81), and the reward model with the strongest intrinsic generalization (Unified Reward, best-of-4 score 84.49) yields the best RL generalization for both algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the preliminary work of 'Image Generation with CoT' [18], which first demonstrated DPO's feasibility for autoregressive image generation, by conducting the first systematic head-to-head comparison with GRPO [43] in this domain. It builds on the iterative DPO paradigm [37] and the group-relative advantage estimation from DeepSeekMath [43], adapting both to the discrete-token autoregressive setting of Janus-Pro. Unlike prior explorations [23, 48, 58] that applied individual RL algorithms without controlled comparison, this work isolates the effects of reward model choice, sampling scale, data diversity, and iterative training under matched computational budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a reward model's intrinsic generalization capability (measured via best-of-N) predicts the RL algorithm's out-of-domain performance offers a practical criterion for reward model selection that goes beyond in-domain accuracy. The non-monotonic scaling behavior of DPO—where excessive sampling degrades both in-domain and out-of-domain performance—challenges the common assumption that more preference data is always better. However, the study is limited to a single autoregressive architecture (Janus-Pro), a single in-domain benchmark, and lacks error bars, leaving open whether these findings transfer to diffusion-based generators or larger model scales.
