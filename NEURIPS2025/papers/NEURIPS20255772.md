## Problem

Existing online conformal prediction methods require access to true outcome labels at each time step to verify coverage, which is incompatible with privacy requirements in sensitive streaming applications. Meanwhile, prior private conformal prediction approaches are limited to offline, centralized settings and do not address the risk of data exposure at acquisition time. This paper fills the gap by providing a dynamic, model-free, one-pass online conformal prediction framework under local differential privacy that requires no trusted curator and no access to raw user data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of feature vectors X_t and, at each step, a single-bit privatized response from the user generated via the Locally Randomized Binary Response (LRBR) mechanism, which compares the user's locally computed non-conformity score S_t against the current quantile threshold q_t. For regression, the output is a prediction interval [Ŷ_t − q_t, Ŷ_t + q_t]; for classification, it is a prediction set of all labels whose non-conformity score (1 − predicted probability) falls below q_t. The entire pipeline operates in O(1) space with one-shot interaction per user, meaning each user is inquired exactly once and no historical data is stored or re-accessed.

## Method

The core mechanism is the Locally Randomized Binary Response (LRBR): the user locally computes S_t, compares it to the server's current quantile estimate q_t, and returns the truthful binary indicator with probability r or a fair coin flip otherwise, yielding an (ε,0)-LDP randomizer with ε = log((1+r)/(1−r)). This privatized bit replaces the deterministic subgradient in the quantile pinball loss update, producing a modified subgradient g_t whose expectation still equals the true subgradient scaled by r. The quantile q_t is then updated via a coin-betting online convex optimization framework with the Krichevsky-Trofimov estimator, providing parameter-free, adaptive learning with O(√(T log T)) regret. The key insight is that the binary structure of the conformal subgradient (whether S_t ≤ q_t) is naturally compatible with LDP's randomized response, enabling privacy protection without additional noise injection. When the underlying predictive model is itself trained under LDP, sequential composition yields end-to-end privacy guarantees across the full pipeline, and the method achieves asymptotic long-run coverage at the nominal 1−α level regardless of the predictive model.

## Results

On synthetic regression data (10,000 samples, 90% target coverage), the proposed method achieves long-run coverage of 0.890 with interval widths of 3.26–4.46 across four drift scenarios, compared to DPCP's widths of 3.29–9.00. Under the strongest privacy setting (ε=0.5), coverage drops modestly to 0.850–0.853 while widths remain stable (3.11–3.83), whereas DPCP's widths inflate by up to 40% (e.g., 8.95 in Case A). Computationally, the proposed method runs in 0.167 s for T=25,000, roughly 10× faster than DPCP (1.378 s) and ~116× faster than DtACI (19.34 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPCP (Angelopoulos et al., 2022), an offline private conformal method, the proposed method trades a small coverage deficit (~1 percentage point below target) for substantially narrower prediction intervals: in the abrupt-shift Case A, widths are 3.43 vs. 8.29 (no-privacy) and 3.28 vs. 8.95 (ε=0.5), a reduction of over 60%. DPCP's higher coverage (0.900–0.922) comes at the cost of conservative, non-adaptive intervals that cannot respond to distributional shifts. Against DtACI (Gibbs and Candès, 2024), an online adaptive method without privacy, the proposed method achieves comparable coverage (0.890 vs. 0.897 in Case A) with slightly narrower widths (3.43 vs. 3.47) while being approximately 100× faster computationally (0.167 s vs. 19.34 s at T=25,000). DtACI is also sensitive to initialization and requires multiple expert feedback queries per step, which the authors note could cause user fatigue in privacy-sensitive settings. In the no-drift Case D, DPCP's higher coverage becomes more favorable since there is no distributional shift to adapt to, narrowing the practical advantage of the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of online conformal prediction (Gibbs & Candès 2021; Podkopaev et al. 2024) and local differential privacy, combining the coin-betting quantile estimation framework of Orabona & Pal (2016) with a classic randomized-response LDP mechanism. Prior private conformal methods (Angelopoulos et al. 2022, DPCP) operate offline under central DP, while online CP methods (DtACI, Gibbs & Candès 2024) lack any privacy protection. The key departure is recognizing that the binary subgradient signal in online CP is naturally amenable to a one-shot LDP randomizer, enabling a trusted-curator-free, single-pass pipeline that no prior work achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap: no prior method provides distribution-free, model-agnostic prediction sets under LDP in a streaming, one-pass setting with O(1) memory. This is practically important for real-time applications (health monitoring, algorithmic trading) where raw data cannot be centrally stored. Key limitations include only asymptotic (not finite-sample) coverage guarantees, and the use of standard non-conformity scores that may yield conservative intervals. Open directions include non-asymptotic bounds under LDP, data-adaptive scores (e.g., CP-Gen), and multivariate extensions.
