## Problem

Existing Wasserstein convergence analyses for score-based diffusion models rely almost exclusively on Euler discretization, leaving a quantitative comparison of alternative schemes (exponential integrators, midpoint randomization) unexplored. Moreover, while accelerated samplers leveraging Hessian information have been analyzed in KL or TV distance, no Wasserstein convergence guarantees exist for such second-order methods, leaving their practical advantage in this more informative metric unquantified.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained score function s_*(t,x) (obtained via score matching on the data), an initial Gaussian sample from p̂_T = N(0, (1−e^{−T})I_d), and for the second-order (SO) sampler, estimates of the Hessian of the log density (L_n) and its temporal derivative (M_n), which in practice are computed via Hessian-vector products rather than explicit Hessian formation. The output is a single sample from the target data distribution p_0 after N discrete steps of size h = T/N. The analysis assumes the target density is m_0-strongly log-concave with L_0-Lipschitz score, and the forward process is the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck SDE dX_t = −X_t/2 dt + dB_t.

## Method

The paper establishes Wasserstein-2 convergence bounds for four discretization schemes of the backward SDE: Euler-Maruyama (EM), Exponential Integrator (EI), Randomized Midpoint (REM), and a hybrid Exponential Integrator with Midpoint Randomization (REI). All four achieve the same rate Õ(√(dh)) in discretization error, differing only in constants, confirming that midpoint randomization offers no convergence improvement over EM/EI in this setting (though it enables parallelization). The core novelty is a second-order (SO) accelerated sampler built on the local linearization method: the drift term γ(T−t, x) is approximated via its Itô expansion over each step, introducing the Hessian of the score (L_n) and a temporal-curvature term (M_n), which yields a closed-form matrix-exponential update within each interval. This second-order discretization reduces the per-step error from O(√h) to O(h), improving the overall iteration complexity from Õ(d/ε²) to Õ(√d/ε) in Wasserstein-2 distance, validated on synthetic penalized logistic regression posteriors and MNIST image generation.

## Results

The paper establishes that all four first-order discretization schemes (Euler-Maruyama, Exponential Integrator, Randomized Midpoint, and Exponential Integrator with Midpoint Randomization) achieve a Wasserstein-2 convergence rate of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d/\varepsilon^2)$ iterations, while the proposed second-order (SO) Hessian-based sampler achieves an improved rate of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon)$ iterations. Empirically, on a 20-dimensional penalized logistic regression posterior, the SO method consistently achieves lower W2 distance across all step sizes compared to the four first-order schemes. On MNIST, the SO algorithm produces visibly sharper and more realistic digit samples than EM, EI, REM, or REI at the same number of sampling steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed second-order (SO) Hessian-based sampler against four first-order discretization schemes: Euler-Maruyama (EM), Exponential Integrator (EI), Randomized Midpoint (REM), and Exponential Integrator with Midpoint Randomization (REI). Theoretically, all four first-order methods share the same $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d/\varepsilon^2)$ iteration complexity, with EM and EI having comparable constants (consistent with prior KL-divergence comparisons in [5]), while REM and REI introduce a $\sqrt{d/3}$ factor in their discretization constants due to the variance from randomization but offer no asymptotic improvement. The SO method achieves a strictly better rate of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon)$, requiring only $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\varepsilon)$ iterations versus $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\varepsilon^2)$ for all first-order schemes, and permits a larger step size $h = \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon/\sqrt{d})$ versus $h = \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2/d)$. Empirically on the 20-dimensional synthetic posterior, SO achieves lower W2 distance at every tested step size, and on MNIST it produces visibly higher-quality samples than all four baselines at matched iteration counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads in diffusion model theory: Wasserstein convergence analysis (Gao et al. [13]) and accelerated sampling via higher-order information (Shoji [33], Liang et al. [27], Li et al. [24]). It extends the SDE-based score matching framework of Song et al. [36] by providing the first systematic quantitative comparison of four discretization schemes (Euler-Maruyama, exponential integrator, randomized midpoint, and their hybrid) under Wasserstein-2 distance, and by transplanting Shoji's 1998 local linearization method into the diffusion sampling context to obtain the first Wasserstein convergence guarantee for a Hessian-accelerated sampler. Prior acceleration results (Liang et al. [27], Li et al. [24]) were expressed in KL or TV distance; this work fills the gap by analyzing the same class of methods in the more practically relevant Wasserstein metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Õ(√d/ε) rate for the Hessian-based sampler (versus Õ(d/ε²) for vanilla methods) is a meaningful theoretical improvement that could guide practitioners toward second-order samplers when Hessian information is accessible. However, the analysis requires strong log-concavity of the target, accurate Hessian estimates, and a specific Ornstein-Uhlenbeck forward process, limiting immediate applicability to complex real-world distributions. Open questions include extending the framework to general (f,g) pairs, non-log-concave targets, and probability flow ODEs, as well as understanding whether the midpoint randomization's parallelization advantage can be combined with the Hessian acceleration to yield both speed and efficiency gains.
