## Problem

Existing generative methods for protein conformational ensembles (diffusion-based or flow-matching) produce structurally independent snapshots that fail to capture the temporal coherence and causal chain of conformational changes inherent in real protein dynamics. While MD simulations naturally model these dynamics, their computational cost limits them to microsecond timescales. The paper addresses the gap of generating physically realistic, temporally coherent protein trajectories that respect the hierarchical multi-scale organization of molecular motions.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial protein backbone structure (represented as 13D per-residue vectors combining SE(3) frames via unit quaternions and sin/cos-encoded torsion angles) along with the amino acid sequence as input. It produces a full backbone trajectory of 400 frames at 1ns resolution, generated hierarchically: a slow-scale model first produces 20 anchor conformations at 20ns intervals, which then condition the fast-scale model to fill in intermediate frames. The representation is rotation- and translation-equivariant, and only backbone atoms (N, Cα, C, O) are modeled rather than full-atom structures.

## Method

TEMPO models protein dynamics as coupled stochastic differential equations at two timescales, parameterized by a shared spatiotemporal encoder that combines Invariant Point Attention (for inter-residue spatial interactions) with GRU layers (for frame-to-frame temporal dependencies). The low-resolution model (Δt=20ns) learns drift dynamics for slow collective motions, while the high-resolution model (Δt=1ns) learns fast local fluctuations conditioned on the slow-scale trajectory. Training is supervised with an MSE reconstruction loss plus a steric clash penalty (1.2Å threshold), and the model uses a two-frame input window to capture short-range memory effects. At inference, the hierarchical strategy first autoregressively generates the sparse slow-scale trajectory, then uses each slow-scale state as an initialization anchor for the fast-scale model to produce the complete fine-grained trajectory, ensuring physical consistency across temporal scales.

## Results

On ATLAS, TEMPO achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.91 for pairwise RMSD and 0.89 for global RMSF, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: AlphaFlow at 0.48/0.60). It captures 76% of principal components with similarity >0.5 (vs. 44% for AlphaFlow, 10% for MDGen) and achieves the lowest MD PCA Wasserstein distance of 0.60. On mdCATH, TEMPO generates 400-frame trajectories in ~22 seconds (0.006 h), compared to 4.5–4.7 h for AlphaFlow/ESMFlow, while achieving a backbone RMSD error of 1.78 Å versus MDGen's 3.76 Å. The up-sampling variant (TEMPO-Up) reaches near-perfect correlations (r=0.99) and 95.31% PC-similarity >0.5 on mdCATH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BioEMU, TEMPO dominates on virtually every metric: pairwise RMSD correlation of 0.77 vs. −0.02, RMSF of 1.60 vs. 10.08 (GT 1.64), and W₂ of 4.21 vs. 10.70, indicating BioEMU's independent-sampling approach produces physically implausible conformations. Against AlphaFlow and ESMFlow (both flow-matching ensemble generators), TEMPO achieves substantially higher RMSD/RMSF correlations (0.77/0.67 vs. 0.41/0.41 and 0.26/0.34) and is ~750× faster at inference (0.006 h vs. 4.5–4.7 h), though these methods achieve higher %PC-sim>0.5 (21.88–25.00 vs. 7.81) and slightly better transient contact Jaccard scores, reflecting their broader but less temporally coherent sampling. Against MDGen (the only other trajectory-generating baseline), TEMPO wins decisively on trajectory accuracy (RMSD Error 1.78 vs. 3.76 on mdCATH; 1.83 vs. 3.20 on ATLAS) and RMSD correlation (0.77 vs. 0.71; 0.91 vs. 0.48 on ATLAS), while MDGen retains advantages in absolute conformational spread (pairwise RMSD 1.11 vs. 2.78) and clash ratio (0.42% vs. 4.75%), suggesting TEMPO explores a wider conformational space at the cost of slightly more steric conflicts. On ATLAS specifically, TEMPO's advantage is most pronounced, with 76% PC-similarity >0.5 versus 44% (AlphaFlow) and 10% (MDGen), and MD PCA W₂ of 0.60 versus 1.52 and 1.89 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEMPO carves new ground by synthesizing three previously separate research threads—SDE-based Langevin dynamics modeling, autoregressive sequence generation, and biophysical multi-scale decomposition—into a unified hierarchical framework for protein trajectory generation. It departs from diffusion-based ensemble generators (AlphaFlow, ESMFlow, BioEMU) that treat conformations as independent samples, and from trajectory-focused methods like MDGen that operate at a single temporal resolution, by explicitly coupling a slow-scale drift model (20 ns) with a fast-scale model (1 ns) in a causal, top-down generation pipeline. The spatiotemporal encoder repurposes Invariant Point Attention from AlphaFold for geometric encoding and adds a GRU for temporal memory, but the architectural novelty lies primarily in the two-scale SDE formulation and the hierarchical inference strategy rather than in any single component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical middle ground between computationally prohibitive MD simulations and static ensemble generators that lack temporal coherence, with a 22-second inference time for 400 frames versus hours for flow-matching baselines. However, the current scope is limited to single-protein backbone dynamics, and the generalization gap between training (46.7% PC-sim > 0.5) and test (7.81%) sets is substantial, suggesting the model struggles with unseen energy landscapes. Open questions include extending to side-chain and multi-molecular dynamics, scaling to longer timescales (beyond 400 ns), and developing evaluation metrics that directly correlate with experimental observables rather than structural proxies.
