## Problem

Prior characterizations of stable (flat) minima for two-layer ReLU networks were restricted to either the interpolation regime or univariate inputs, leaving open how these minima behave in the high-dimensional, non-interpolation setting most relevant to practical gradient-descent training. This paper asks how well stable minima of overparameterized two-layer ReLU networks generalize when inputs are multivariate and the network has not interpolated the training data. The surprising answer is that while flatness does guarantee generalization, the sample complexity grows exponentially with input dimension, yielding an exponential separation from low-norm (weight-decay) solutions that are known to avoid the curse of dimensionality.

## Input / Output

The analysis assumes inputs drawn i.i.d. uniformly from the unit ball B_1^d with labels bounded in [-D, D], and considers two-layer ReLU networks of width K trained by gradient descent with step size η < 2. The central output is a data-dependent weighted variation norm |f|_{V_g} that characterizes the function space of stable minima, where the weight g(u,t) is computed from the empirical data distribution and governs how much norm budget each neuron direction (u,t) can consume. The paper produces upper bounds on generalization gap and MSE at rate Õ(n^{-1/(2d+4)}), a minimax lower bound at rate Ω(n^{-2/(d+1)}), and a Radon-domain analytic characterization linking the weighted norm to a weighted second-order Radon-domain total variation.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a data-dependent weighted variation norm V_g on functions, where the weight g(u,t) is derived from the loss Hessian structure and quantifies how many training points a ReLU neuron in direction (u,t) activates on. For uniform data on the ball, g(u,t) ≈ (1-|t|)^(d+2), which vanishes exponentially near the boundary—this is the mechanism enabling 'neural shattering,' where neurons drift to activate on only a tiny fraction of data while retaining large weight magnitudes without violating the flatness constraint. Upper bound proofs decompose the domain into an inner core (where g is bounded below and standard metric-entropy arguments apply) and a boundary annulus (where the weight is small and error is controlled by volume), balancing the two to obtain the final rate. The minimax lower bound uses a novel spherical-cap packing on S^{d-1} with exp(Ω(d)) disjoint caps, placing boundary-localized ReLU atoms on each cap to construct a family of hard-to-learn functions that are simultaneously in the stable-minima function class and exponentially separated in L², directly mirroring the empirical neural shattering phenomenon.

## Results

The paper establishes that stable (flat) minima of two-layer ReLU networks generalize at a rate of $\tilde{O}(n^{-1/(2d+4)})$ in MSE, while a minimax lower bound of $\Omega(n^{-2/(d+1)})$ certifies this rate is unavoidable for the induced function class. This creates an exponential-in-dimension separation from low-norm (weight-decay) solutions, which achieve $\tilde{O}(n^{-1/4})$ independent of $d$. Empirically, on a 10-dimensional linear target with 512 noisy samples, stable minima (η=0.9, no weight decay) yield MSE ≈ 1.105 with neurons activating on ≤10% of data, while weight decay (η=0.01, λ=0.1) achieves MSE ≈ 0.055 with all neurons fully active. The log-log MSE-vs-n slope for vanilla GD drops to ~0.1 at d=5, whereas weight decay maintains slopes above 0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between stable (flat) minima and low-norm solutions induced by $\ell^2$ weight decay. Stable minima suffer an exponential curse of dimensionality: their MSE rate $n^{-1/(2d+4)}$ degrades rapidly (e.g., from $n^{-1/6}$ at $d=1$ to $n^{-1/14}$ at $d=5$), while weight-decay solutions maintain a dimension-independent $\tilde{O}(n^{-1/4})$ rate. This constitutes an exponential separation in sample complexity. Empirically, on the 10D linear target, stable minima overfit (MSE ≈ 1.105, matching noise level) due to the 'neural shattering' phenomenon where neurons develop large weights while activating on very few data points, whereas weight decay prevents this and achieves MSE ≈ 0.055. The paper also contrasts with Qiao et al. [2024], who showed matching $n^{-4/5}$ rates for univariate stable minima but only on an interval strictly inside the data support; this paper's $n^{-1/6}$ upper and $n^{-1/2}$ lower bounds for $d=1$ hold over the full domain, capturing extrapolation behavior and revealing a gap not present in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minima-stability implicit bias line of work (Mulayoff et al. 2021 for univariate interpolation, Nacson et al. 2023 for multivariate interpolation, Qiao et al. 2024 for univariate non-interpolation) to the multivariate, non-interpolation regime, which had been left open. It introduces a data-dependent weighted variation norm V_g that unifies and generalizes the prior function-space characterizations, and connects to the broader flat-minima-generalization literature (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, Keskar et al.) and the edge-of-stability dynamics (Cohen et al. 2020). The key conceptual departure is providing matching upper and lower bounds that reveal an exponential-in-dimension gap between flat (stable) solutions and low-norm (weight-decay) solutions, a separation not present in any prior stability analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers a clear negative result for the widely-held 'flat minima generalize well' narrative: while flatness does imply generalization, the sample complexity grows exponentially with input dimension, in stark contrast to weight-decay solutions that avoid the curse of dimensionality. The 'neural shattering' mechanism—where neurons satisfy the stability constraint by minimizing activation frequency rather than shrinking weights—provides a concrete, geometrically grounded explanation for this failure. Limitations are significant: the theory covers only two-layer ReLU networks with inputs drawn uniformly from a ball, and the empirical validation is entirely synthetic. Extending the analysis to deeper architectures, more general data distributions, and adaptive optimization algorithms remains open and would be needed to assess practical relevance.
