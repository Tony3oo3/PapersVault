## Problem

Current sequence-to-label methods for protein mutation stability (ΔΔG) prediction require computing a separate latent representation for each mutant site, making them computationally expensive, prone to overfitting, and architecturally rigid. Additionally, existing ΔΔG datasets suffer from significant train-test redundancy and sequence-mutation misalignment, undermining fair evaluation of generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254581/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single wild-type protein sequence (and optionally a predicted structure for structure-aware PLMs like ESM-IF or ProSST) and produces a full n×20 mutation landscape matrix in one forward pass, where each entry L_{i,j} is the predicted ΔΔG for mutating position i to amino acid j. Only experimentally measured entries (tracked by a binary mask) contribute to the training loss, so the landscape is inherently sparse. This contrasts with sequence-to-label baselines that must run n×20 separate forward passes, one per mutant sequence.

## Method

Venus-MAXWELL reformulates the PLM's native zero-shot log-likelihood scoring as a differentiable matrix operation: the PLM outputs a log-probability matrix Q, the wild-type log-probabilities are extracted via Hadamard product with the one-hot sequence encoding and broadcast back to a matrix, and their difference yields the landscape L = Q − V_wt^rep. Crucially, no task-specific regression head is added; the PLM's output logits are fine-tuned directly, so the model's initial (pre-fine-tuning) performance equals the PLM's zero-shot prediction. Training uses a combined objective of a ranking loss (negative Pearson correlation between predicted and measured ΔΔG over masked positions) and an MSE loss through a small two-layer MLP that rescales log-probability scores to the experimental ΔΔG range. This matrix-based paradigm is universal across PLM types (masked LMs, inverse folding models, structure-aware models) and reduces the cost of predicting all single-site mutations for a 200-residue protein from 3,800 separate computations to a single forward-backward pass.

## Results

On the curated Test12K benchmark (12,443 mutations across 308 proteins), Venus-MAXWELL(ESM-IF) achieves a mean per-protein Spearman correlation of 0.517 and Pearson correlation of 0.542, surpassing the SOTA ThermoMPNN (Spearman 0.508) with statistical significance (p < 0.01). Venus-MAXWELL(ProSST) reaches Spearman 0.479 without any architectural modification to the base PLM. In terms of inference speed, Venus-MAXWELL(ESM-IF) predicts 20,050 mutants per second versus 2,088 for ThermoMPNN, a >10× speedup despite having 50× more parameters (142M vs 2.68M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Venus-MAXWELL(ESM-IF) outperforms ThermoMPNN (the primary SOTA baseline) by 0.009 in Spearman correlation (0.517 vs 0.508, p < 0.01) and by 0.056 in F1 (0.427 vs 0.371), while being >10× faster in inference (20,050 vs 2,088 mutants/s). Against the physics-based methods, Venus-MAXWELL(ESM-IF) substantially exceeds FoldX (Spearman 0.517 vs 0.410) and Rosetta (0.517 vs 0.368). The embedding transfer baselines (ESM-IF+MLP at 0.302, ProSST+MLP at 0.234) perform poorly under the strict low-similarity evaluation protocol, confirming that the sequence-to-label paradigm struggles to generalize when train/test sequence identity is below 30%. On individual public benchmarks (p53, s669, Myoglobin, Ssym), Venus-MAXWELL(ESM-IF) also consistently outperforms both ThermoMPNN and Stability Oracle, e.g., achieving 0.751 vs 0.702 vs 0.727 on p53.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant sequence-to-label paradigm for ΔΔG prediction (exemplified by ThermoMPNN and Stability Oracle), which encodes each mutant sequence individually through a PLM and regresses a scalar from the latent representation. Instead, Venus-MAXWELL reformulates the problem as sequence-to-landscape: it applies matrix operations (Hadamard product, row-wise subtraction) directly on the PLM's output log-probability matrix to produce the full mutation landscape in a single forward pass, making the fine-tuning process differentiable and compatible with any PLM that outputs per-position amino acid probabilities. The approach is conceptually grounded in the zero-shot log-likelihood scoring already available in PLMs, but elevates it from a per-mutant heuristic to a matrix-level training objective with ranking and MSE losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup over ThermoMPNN (despite 50× more parameters) makes high-throughput single-site saturation screening practical for directed evolution workflows, where thousands of mutants must be evaluated rapidly. The curated Train226K/Test12K dataset with strict <30% sequence identity separation addresses a persistent data-leakage problem in the field, enabling more honest generalization benchmarks. Key limitations remain: the framework is restricted to single-site mutations and single-amino-acid vocabulary tokens (excluding BPE or structure-aware tokenizers), and extending it to combinatorial multi-site mutations is an open challenge that the authors explicitly flag for future work.
