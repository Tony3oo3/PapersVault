## Problem

Linear attention methods achieve O(N) complexity but consistently underperform standard softmax attention. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) softmax attention produces convex combinations that can only blend information additively, preventing a single layer from expressing subtractive or contrastive operations, and (2) the uniform 1/t component in the softmax Taylor expansion introduces a persistent averaging bias that dilutes focused attention in long contexts. These limitations stem from the constant zero-order term that enforces non-negativity for stability but suppresses high-order token interactions.

## Input / Output

ZeroS consumes the same inputs as standard Transformer attention: token embedding sequences (length N, dimension d) for text, image patch embeddings for vision, or time series values, projected through learned W_q, W_k, W_v matrices. The unusual structural detail is that the radial logits s_i depend only on step i (the negative inner product between each token's projection and its running cumulative average), while all t-dependent interactions are handled through learned sigmoid gates σ_t^1 and σ_t^h applied at readout time. The output is a standard attention vector o_t ∈ R^d that plugs into the residual stream, but the internal weights r_{t,i}·cos θ are zero-sum (sum to zero over i), permitting negative contributions that enable contrastive operations within a single layer.

## Method

ZeroS removes the constant 1/t zero-order term from the softmax Taylor expansion and reweights the remaining first-order (δ_{t,i}/t) and higher-order (ε_{t,i}) residuals with independent learned sigmoid gates, producing zero-sum weights w_{t,i} that admit both positive and negative values while maintaining O(1/t) magnitude bounds independent of sequence length. The method decouples radial and angular components: the radial part captures length-dependent interactions through i-only logits s_i = -(1/√d) u_i ū_i^⊤ (deviation from cumulative average with a trainable smoothing prior), while the angular part uses normalized direction vectors q̂_t k̂_i^⊤ (with RoPE for relative position) multiplied directly onto the zero-sum radial weights without positivity constraints. Linear-time computation is achieved by maintaining three O(d²) prefix-sum state matrices (F_t, G_t, H_t) that accumulate e^{s_i} k̂_i^⊤ v_i, s_i k̂_i^⊤ v_i, and k̂_i^⊤ v_i respectively, then reading them out with the current step's angular vector and gate scalars in O(d²) per step, yielding O(Nd²) total time and O(d²) memory. Theoretically, the paper proves that zero-sum weights strictly enlarge the set of representable deviation vectors compared to convex combinations (Proposition 3.1), that stacking a full-softmax first layer with zero-sum subsequent layers recovers the full affine hull (Proposition 3.3), and that the output is uniformly Lipschitz with a 1/√t decay factor ensuring training stability independent of sequence length (Proposition 3.5).

## Results

On the MAD benchmark, ZeroS (linear-time) achieves a 73.5% average across six in-context tasks, outperforming all linear-time baselines (Mamba 69.3%, DeltaNet 71.8%, GLA 60.0%, LinAttn 62.3%) and approaching the Transformer's 74.5%, while ZeroS-SM (quadratic-time) reaches 75.4%, exceeding the Transformer. On WikiText-103, ZeroS attains 23.91 val / 24.61 test PPL, beating the Transformer (24.4 / 24.78) and all other linear methods, with ZeroS-SM further improving to 23.62 / 24.17. On ImageNet-1k with DeiT-Tiny, ZeroS achieves 75.51% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing DeiT (72.20%), TNN (72.29%), HGRN1 (74.40%), and HGRN2 (75.39%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroS (linear) outperforms all linear-time baselines on the MAD benchmark: it beats Mamba by 4.2 points (73.5 vs 69.3), DeltaNet by 1.7 (73.5 vs 71.8), GLA by 13.5 (73.5 vs 60.0), and LinAttn by 11.2 (73.5 vs 62.3), while remaining within 1.0 point of the Transformer (73.5 vs 74.5). ZeroS-SM exceeds the Transformer by 0.9 points on MAD and by 0.78 PPL on WikiText-103 (23.62 vs 24.40 val), confirming that zero-sum reweighting benefits even quadratic-time attention. On WikiText-103, ZeroS (23.91 val PPL) beats the Transformer (24.40) and all linear baselines except Mamba (22.58) and TNN (23.98), with the gap to Mamba reflecting Mamba's stronger language-modeling prior. On ImageNet, ZeroS (75.51% Top-1) surpasses HGRN2 (75.39%), HGRN1 (74.40%), TNN (72.29%), and the DeiT baseline (72.20%) at comparable parameter counts (~6M). On time series forecasting, ZeroS achieves the best or near-best MSE/MAE across Weather, Solar, and ETT datasets, outperforming GLA, AFT, iTransformer, PatchTST, and DLinear on most splits. On RegBench and MQAR, ZeroS matches or exceeds GLA, RetNet, and RWKV, and performs comparably to vanilla softmax attention on MQAR induction-head tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroS departs from the dominant linear-attention paradigm of approximating softmax via kernel feature maps (Katharopoulos et al., 2020; Qin et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2024), which inherently forces non-negative weights and restricts angular interactions to (0, 90°). Instead, it modifies the softmax mechanism itself by removing the zero-order 1/t term from the Taylor expansion, yielding zero-sum weights that permit negative values while preserving O(N) scan structure. The radial-angular decoupling—separating magnitude (via i-dependent deviation logits) from direction (via normalized q·k cosine with RoPE)—is a novel architectural choice not present in prior linear attention, which couples both through a single kernel product. The paper also contrasts with hybrid approaches (Griffin, Lite Transformer) and state-space models (Mamba, RWKV) that address attention dilution through engineering solutions rather than modifying the weight structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result—that zero-sum weights strictly enlarge the set of representable deviations compared to convex combinations while maintaining O(1) output norm independent of sequence length—provides a principled explanation for why linear attention underperforms softmax and a minimal fix. The practical implication is significant: a single-term removal plus gating yields O(N) attention that matches or exceeds O(N²) softmax across diverse benchmarks, potentially eliminating the expressivity-efficiency tradeoff that has constrained long-context modeling. Key limitations include evaluation only at small scale (GPT-2 768-dim, DeiT-Tiny) without large-LLM validation, absence of a fused GPU kernel, and focus on autoregressive tasks; whether the approach retains its advantages at billions of parameters and on non-causal settings remains open.
