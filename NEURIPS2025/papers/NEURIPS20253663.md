## Problem

Existing generative time series forecasting models rely on recurrent autoregressive decoding or multi-step denoising (e.g., diffusion), making long-horizon inference computationally expensive. Most have been evaluated only on short-term settings, leaving their practical advantage over deterministic methods unclear for long-term forecasting. This paper asks whether a generative model can achieve one-step full-horizon generation while matching or exceeding state-of-the-art deterministic accuracy across diverse forecast lengths.

## Input / Output

The model takes a historical multivariate time series window x ∈ R^{C×L} (C channels, L lookback steps) and produces a full-horizon forecast ŷ ∈ R^{C×H} in a single forward pass. Because the model is generative, any number of samples can be drawn from the prior at inference time; point estimates (MSE/MAE) are computed from the median of samples, while probabilistic quality (CRPS) is estimated via a quantile-based approximation over the sample set. During training, the target series y is additionally available to the encoder and flow modules, but inference uses only x.

## Method

TARFVAE is a conditional VAE whose Gaussian posterior q(z₀|x,y) is refined by a Transformer-based autoregressive flow (TARFLOW) into a flexible, non-Gaussian posterior z, breaking the expressiveness limitation of standard VAEs. Only the forward (encoding) direction of TARFLOW is used—its autoregressive inverse is deliberately omitted—so generation remains a single decode step: sample z from the prior p(z|x) and pass it through the VAE decoder. The decoder augments z with a full-attention query over x's embeddings before MLP blocks produce the reconstruction. TARFLOW's coupling functions s and t are implemented as causal Transformers over channel-partitioned latent variables with dimension-reversing permutations between blocks. The entire architecture rests on simple MLP blocks (no dedicated temporal modules), and the training objective combines a Gaussian reconstruction term, the flow log-determinant, and a KL divergence between the flow-transformed posterior and the prior.

## Results

On the deterministic forecasting benchmark (L=96, H∈{96,192,336,720}), TARFVAE achieves the best average MSE on 6 of 7 datasets, with headline values of 0.427 (ETTh1), 0.355 (ETTh2), 0.271 (ETTm2), and 0.168 (Electricity), outperforming the strongest deterministic baseline DUET by 4.3% on ETTh2 and 4.8% on Weather. Against generative baselines (Table 3), TARFVAE secures top-1 MSE/MAE on ETTh1, ETTm1, and Exchange, with an average rank of 2.1 across 10 metrics. Inference time is ~3 ms regardless of horizon or sample size (1–200 samples), reducing latency by 85.6% versus mr-Diff at H=720 on ETTh1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against deterministic baselines, TARFVAE achieves the best average MSE on 6 of 7 datasets (losing only to DUET on Exchange: 0.349 vs 0.318 MSE) and the best MAE on 6 of 7 (losing to DUET on Solar: 0.248 vs 0.233 MAE). The margin over the strongest Transformer-based baselines (DUET, iTransformer, PatchTST) is consistent at 1–5% MSE reduction, while the gap over older architectures (Crossformer, FEDformer) is substantially larger (e.g., 0.427 vs 0.529 MSE on ETTh1). Among generative methods, TARFVAE outperforms mr-Diff on ETTh1 (0.405 vs 0.411 MSE) and ETTm1 (0.321 vs 0.340 MSE), ties on Exchange MSE (both 0.016), but trails on Weather (0.309 vs 0.296 MSE) and Electricity (0.150 vs 0.155 MSE, a narrow 3.2% gap). TARFVAE dominates all diffusion-based baselines (TimeGrad, CSDI, SSSD, TimeDiff) by wide margins—e.g., 0.405 vs 0.993 MSE on ETTh1 versus TimeGrad. In inference speed, TARFVAE (~3 ms) is 85.6% faster than mr-Diff (21.6 ms at H=720) and over 200× faster than TimeGrad (6724 ms), while maintaining stable latency across 1–200 samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

TARFVAE sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: variational autoencoders for probabilistic time series forecasting (TimeVAE, D3VAE) and normalizing flows for density estimation (TARFLOW, Transformer-MAF). Its key architectural departure is using only the *forward* process of TARFLOW to refine a Gaussian posterior into a richer distribution, deliberately omitting the autoregressive inverse that would be needed for full flow-based generation. This preserves VAE's one-step decoding while gaining the expressiveness of a flow-shaped latent space. The paper also departs from the prevailing Transformer-heavy LTSF literature by grounding the encoder, decoder, and prior in simple MLP blocks, echoing the 'simple models suffice' argument from DLinear and SOFTS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TARFVAE is notable as the first generative forecasting model to deliver competitive long-horizon (up to 720-step) predictions on both deterministic (MSE/MAE) and probabilistic (CRPS) metrics while being over 85% faster than diffusion-based alternatives at inference. This addresses a practical gap: prior generative methods were either too slow for long horizons (diffusion) or relied on autoregressive decoding that eroded VAE's speed advantage. Open questions include whether the MLP backbone is truly sufficient for complex, high-dimensional series (the authors acknowledge this limitation), and whether the flow-enhanced posterior can be further strengthened without reintroducing autoregressive cost. The framework's modularity suggests it could absorb stronger temporal encoders in future work.
