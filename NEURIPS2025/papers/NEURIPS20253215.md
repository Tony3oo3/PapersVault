## Problem

The k-out-of-t random allocation sampling scheme, where each user's data is used in exactly k randomly chosen steps out of t, has been adopted in DP-SGD and private aggregation but lacks tight theoretical privacy guarantees. Existing analyses either reduce to shuffling (yielding overly conservative bounds with an extra √(ln(1/δ)) factor) or rely on Monte Carlo simulations that are computationally infeasible for small δ and do not provide provable guarantees or asymptotic characterizations.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a t-step algorithm M (e.g., the Gaussian mechanism with noise parameter σ) and parameters t (total steps) and k (allocations per element), and reduces the problem to a single non-adaptive dominating randomizer R: {*, ⊥} → Y via the hockey-stick divergence. The output is a privacy profile δ_A(ε) (the hockey-stick divergence between the allocation outputs on neighboring datasets), from which (ε,δ)-DP guarantees and integer-order Rényi DP parameters are derived. The bounds are expressed analytically in terms of the RDP parameters of R and the partition structure of α, avoiding numerical PLD computation.

## Method

The paper provides three complementary analyses. The main result (Theorem 4.1) decomposes random allocation into sequential posterior sampling steps, truncates the posterior probability at a threshold η ≈ 1/t, and bounds the truncation error via Azuma-Hoeffding concentration, showing the privacy is upper bounded by Poisson subsampling at rate (1+γ)k/t plus a vanishing lower-order term. A second technique (Theorem 4.3) observes that Poisson sampling is a mixture of random allocations with varying k, proves monotonicity of privacy in k, and derives a universal constant-factor (~1.6×) upper bound on ε relative to Poisson. The third technique (Theorem 4.4) gives an exact closed-form expression for the Rényi divergence of the remove direction as a sum over integer partitions of α weighted by the RDP of the base randomizer, with a separate approximate bound for the add direction. All three analyses first reduce general k to k=1 via a composition decomposition (Lemma 3.2) and general adaptive algorithms to a single dominating randomizer (Theorem 3.1).

## Results

The paper's main quantitative result (Theorem 4.1) shows that the privacy of k-out-of-t random allocation is upper bounded by Poisson subsampling with rate (1+γ)k/t plus a vanishing lower-order term, where γ = O(e^{ε₀}√(k·ln(k/δ′)/t)). Numerical evaluations (Figures 1–2) with the Gaussian mechanism (δ=10⁻¹⁰, t=10⁶) show that the combined random allocation bounds are typically within ~10% of the Poisson PLD bounds across a wide range of σ values. In the large-ε regime, the direct RDP analysis of allocation (Theorem 4.4) actually yields tighter bounds than the Poisson PLD-based bounds (Figure 5). The asymptotic scaling of ε matches Poisson: ε = O(k√(ln(t/δ))/(σ√t)), avoiding the extra √(ln(1/δ)) factor incurred by shuffling-based analyses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Poisson subsampling (λ=1/t), the paper's random allocation bounds are within ~10% in most practical regimes (Figures 1–2), and in some large-ε regimes the RDP-based allocation bounds are actually tighter than the Poisson PLD bounds. Against the shuffling scheme, random allocation is significantly better: shuffling-based analysis incurs an additional √(ln(1/δ)) factor (giving ε ≥ C′·k·ln(1/δ)/(σ√t)), which is substantially worse numerically (visible in Figure 1 where the shuffling curve is well above the allocation and Poisson curves). Against Dong et al. [2025] (DCO25, 'Balanced Iteration Subsampling'), the paper's combined bounds are comparable or better, and often significantly tighter, particularly in the k≪t regime where DCO25's loose upper bound for α>2 divergences becomes a bottleneck. Against the lower bound from Chua et al. [2024a] (CGHKKLMSZ24), the paper's upper bounds are close, confirming near-tightness of the analysis. Overall, the paper demonstrates that random allocation can replace Poisson subsampling with only minor privacy loss while offering utility advantages (exact sum computation, no data dropping, bounded participation).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical privacy guarantees for the k-out-of-t random allocation sampling scheme, which was previously analyzed only via Monte Carlo simulation (Chua et al. 2024a, Choquette-Choo et al. 2025) or reduced to shuffling-based bounds (Balle et al. 2020). The analysis builds on the hockey-stick divergence and privacy profile framework of Balle et al. (2018), the posterior sampling decomposition from the shuffling literature (Erlingsson et al. 2019), the dominating-pair reduction of Zhu et al. (2022), and the RDP framework of Mironov (2017). A key departure from prior work is the use of a truncated posterior sampling argument to show random allocation is asymptotically equivalent to Poisson subsampling at rate k/t, combined with a direct RDP analysis that yields exact integer-order divergences for the remove direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a long-standing practical discrepancy in DP-SGD where implementations use shuffling or random allocation but analyses assume Poisson subsampling, by showing that random allocation achieves nearly identical privacy guarantees to Poisson while offering utility advantages (exact sum computation, no dropped samples). The bounds are within ~10% of Poisson across most parameter regimes and even exceed Poisson in some, making random allocation a practical drop-in replacement. Limitations include the ~10% gap in some regimes, lossy RDP-to-(ε,δ) conversion, and the fact that the (ε,δ) bounds do not support tight composition accounting (addressed in the authors' follow-up work on PLD approximation). Open questions include whether the PLD-based approach in the follow-up can close the remaining gap to Poisson and whether these techniques extend to non-Gaussian mechanisms.
