## Problem

Prior neural model checking learns ranking functions to verify liveness properties of reactive systems but cannot construct inductive invariants, leaving safety properties and combined safety-liveness specifications out of reach. This paper closes that gap by showing that a single neural network can simultaneously represent both an inductive invariant (proof of safety) and a ranking function (proof of liveness), enabling formal verification of arbitrary LTL properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252509/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a SystemVerilog hardware design (a finite-state reactive system with word-level registers) and an LTL formula over Boolean observables, which is compiled via Spot into a non-deterministic Büchi automaton recognizing violations. The hardware and automaton are composed synchronously, and the method produces a single neural partially-ranking function with per-automaton-state parameters that maps register valuations to integer ranks; a global threshold κ on this network defines the invariant boundary, and the resulting certificate is formally checked via one-step BMC queries over the full state space to yield a sound proof or a concrete counterexample.

## Method

The core architecture is a neural partially-ranking function with two branches: a left branch of fully connected layers with sign activations that acts as a binary mask, and a right branch of linear functions; their element-wise product yields a piecewise-linear integer-valued output with 2^m configurations. Training uses a counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS) loop where the learning phase solves a mixed-integer linear program (via Gurobi) over sample transitions to find network parameters satisfying initiation and ranking constraints, and the checking phase poses one-step BMC queries (via Bitwuzla/EBMC) to verify the certificate over the entire state space, feeding counterexamples back into the dataset. The sign-activation structure is key: it permits an MILP encoding with only linear constraints (avoiding bilinear neuron-weight products), making the network trainable with numerically stable constraint solvers rather than gradient descent. The architecture starts as a per-state linear model and escalates to deeper networks only when the linear model is infeasible, yielding compact, naturally quantised certificates.

## Results

On the 634-task SystemVerilog benchmark, the proposed neural partially-ranking function completes 190/206 (92%) pure-safety tasks, 192/219 (88%) pure-liveness tasks, and 172/209 (82%) combined safety-liveness tasks within a 5-hour timeout. On pure liveness, the method is faster than the per-task fastest competitor on 66% of tasks, with speedups of 10× on 46%, 10³× on 11%, and 10⁴× on 4%. On combined safety-liveness properties, it is faster on 61% of tasks, achieving 10²× speedups on 43%, 10⁴× on 27%, and 10⁵× on 6% of tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior neural model checking approach NMC'24 [63] (liveness only), the proposed method completes 20 more pure-liveness tasks (192 vs. 172) and is faster on 88% of shared tasks with speedups of at least 10×, reaching 10⁵× on 5% of tasks. Against the leading symbolic model checker nuXmv [28], the method wins on pure liveness (88% vs. 82% completion) and combined safety-liveness (82% vs. 74%), while matching it on pure safety (92% vs. 100%, with 80% of tasks finishing in under 1 s for both). Against rIC3 [119], the method dominates on liveness (88% vs. 50%) and safety-liveness (82% vs. 61%), while slightly trailing on pure safety (92% vs. 95%). Against ABC/Super-Prove [24], the gap is largest on liveness (88% vs. 36%) and safety-liveness (82% vs. 46%), with ABC retaining a small edge on pure safety (100% vs. 92%). Overall, the method is the only approach that handles all three property classes, and it achieves the highest completion rate on the two classes that require ranking functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2024 neural model checking framework (Giacobbe et al., NeurIPS 2024), which learned ranking functions via gradient descent to verify liveness properties only, by introducing the neural partially-ranking function that jointly encodes inductive invariants (safety proofs) and ranking functions (liveness proofs) in a single network. The training paradigm shifts from stochastic gradient descent to MILP/SMT solvers, building on prior results showing that sign-activated feed-forward networks admit linear encodings amenable to constraint solving (Icarte et al., CP 2019; Thorbjarnarson & Yorke-Smith, 2020). The overall learn-check loop follows the CEGIS paradigm (Abate et al., CAV 2018), where a constraint solver proposes parameters and a BMC-based checker either certifies or produces counterexamples to feed back into training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying safety and liveness in a single neural certificate, this work closes a significant gap: prior neural model checking could not verify the majority of practical LTL specifications that combine both property types, while symbolic tools (IC3, k-liveness BMC) struggle with liveness at scale. The 10^5× speedups on combined safety-liveness tasks suggest neural certificates may become the default approach for word-level hardware verification of arbitrary LTL. Key limitations include restriction to sign-activated architectures, hardware/reactive systems without arrays or dynamic data structures, and evaluation on textbook-level designs rather than industrial-scale RTL; generalization to other activation functions and compositional verification of large designs remain open.
