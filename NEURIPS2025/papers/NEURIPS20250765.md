## Problem

Existing debiasing methods for text-to-image diffusion models either couple multiple model components for full-parameter training (incurring heavy computational cost and risk of over-debiasing) or rely on auxiliary networks at inference time (adding sampling overhead and inheriting hidden biases from third-party models). The text encoder's role as a primary source of attribute bias in Stable Diffusion remains underexplored, and no lightweight, targeted approach exists to correct it without auxiliary references.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250765/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (e.g., 'Photo portrait of a CEO, a person') and produces a 512×512 RGB image via Stable Diffusion's latent denoising pipeline. During fine-tuning, the input is a set of attribute-conditioned prompts paired with their generated images (encoded by CLIP's image encoder); during inference, the two-stage strategy switches between the original and debiased text encoder at timestep τ = 3T/4, so the user-facing interface remains a single prompt with no additional inputs or reference images required.

## Method

LightFair performs LoRA fine-tuning on the CLIP text encoder alone (≈123M vs. ≈860M U-Net parameters) using a collaborative distance-constrained loss that simultaneously enforces equalized odds (ℓ_o: equal cosine distance from the neutral text embedding to each attribute's image-embedding centroid), equalized quality (ℓ_q: equal distance between each attribute's text and image embeddings), and a regularization term (ℓ_reg) to prevent semantic drift. An Adaptive Foreground Extraction module applies cross-attention between the attribute text embedding and the image embedding to suppress background interference before computing centroids. A theoretical result (Thm. 4.1) establishes equivalence between equalized generation probability and equalized embedding distance under mild independence assumptions. At inference, a two-stage text-guided sampling strategy (motivated by a frequency-domain analysis showing low-frequency/main-word information emerges early while high-frequency/attribute information emerges late) uses the original text encoder for t ≥ τ and the debiased encoder only for t < τ, adding virtually no sampling overhead while preserving generation quality.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.5, LightFair reduces gender Bias-O from 0.73 (SD) to 0.30 and gender Bias-Q from 1.90 to 0.99, while simultaneously improving CLIP-T from 29.31 to 30.57. For race debiasing on SD v1.5, it cuts Bias-Q from 0.54 to 0.18 and raises CLIP-T to 31.34. On SD v2.1, gender Bias-O drops from 0.85 to 0.33 and race Bias-Q from 0.63 to 0.40. The paper reports achieving SOTA debiasing at approximately 1/4 the training burden of full-parameter fine-tuning methods, with virtually no increase in sampling cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightFair achieves the lowest gender Bias-O (0.30) and Bias-Q (0.99) on SD v1.5, outperforming the next-best FinetuneFD (0.38 / 2.31) and Balancing Act (0.41 / 1.70) by substantial margins. For race debiasing on SD v1.5, LightFair attains the best Bias-Q (0.18 vs. 0.20 for FinetuneFD) and the highest CLIP-T (31.34 vs. 29.34 for Balancing Act), while also achieving the lowest Bias-O (2.60). On SD v2.1, LightFair reduces gender Bias-O from 0.85 (SD) to 0.33, beating Debias VL (0.43) and UCE (0.90), and achieves the best race Bias-Q (0.40 vs. 0.49 for Debias VL). The main trade-off is a drop in Inception Score (e.g., 233.53 vs. 278.21 for FinetuneFD on SD v1.5 gender) and CLIP-I, which the authors attribute to the expected effect of debiasing altering image attributes. Overall, LightFair is the only method that simultaneously improves both fairness metrics and text-image alignment (CLIP-T) across all settings, while requiring only LoRA fine-tuning of the text encoder (~123M params) rather than full U-Net training (~860M params).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion's architecture (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) but departs from both major debiasing paradigms: full-parameter fine-tuning that couples the U-Net and text encoder (FinetuneFD, UCE), and inference-time correction relying on auxiliary networks or external references (FairD, FairMapping, Balancing Act, Debias VL). Instead, it isolates the CLIP text encoder as the primary bias source and applies LoRA fine-tuning with a distance-constrained loss in the joint text-image embedding space, avoiding complex gradient chains and auxiliary models. The two-stage sampling strategy, grounded in a frequency-domain analysis of the denoising process, is a novel mechanism for limiting when the debiased encoder intervenes, drawing on spectral properties of natural images rather than learned schedules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain (1/4 training burden, negligible sampling overhead) makes fair T2I generation more accessible, and the theoretical link between equalized distance and equalized odds (Thm. 4.1) provides a principled optimization target that avoids needing ground-truth attribute distributions. However, the method still requires generating training images with the model itself to compute semantic centroids, creating a mild circularity; it has been validated only on U-Net-based SD (v1.5, v2.1) and two/three attributes, leaving open whether the approach transfers cleanly to DiT-based architectures or to more complex intersectional bias scenarios. The frequency-domain timing insight, while well-motivated, is validated primarily on gender attributes and may not generalize identically to all attribute types.
