## Problem

Existing world simulators for LLM/VLM agent development fall short in three critical ways: they rely on limited hand-crafted scenes (e.g., 15 in CARLA, 211 in Habitat 3.0), simulate simplified game-like physics and social rules rather than realistic dynamics, and lack native interfaces for language-model-driven agents to perceive, reason, and act. This gap prevents systematic evaluation and training of agents in the noisy, open-ended physical and social environments where real-world deployment ultimately demands.

## Input / Output

Agents receive multi-modal observations including RGB screenshots at 90° horizontal FOV, depth maps, segmentation masks, structured semantic scene graphs, and environment feedback (e.g., collision events, updated scene state). They also receive task metadata such as relative distance and angle to the goal, action history (past 3 actions), and waypoint information from the local action planner. Outputs are open-vocabulary natural language actions at varying abstraction levels (e.g., 'go to the nearest chair and sit down'), which the simulator's local action planner decomposes into executable low-level action sequences (navigate, sit_down, step, turn, etc.) before the agent receives updated observations in a closed loop.

## Method

SIMWORLD uses a two-tier architecture: an Unreal Engine 5 backend providing real-time 3D rendering and continuous physics (gravity, momentum, inertia), and a Python layer handling procedural generation, agent I/O, and scenario construction. The procedural generation pipeline uses a quad-tree data structure to hierarchically build city layouts in three stages (road network → building placement → street-level details), enabling effectively infinite scene diversity. A retrieval-augmented LLM scene agent supports language-driven editing by grounding natural language commands against the scene graph, retrieving assets via CLIP-based matching, and falling back to text-to-3D generation (Hunyuan3D) for novel objects. The local action planner bridges high-level LLM intentions and low-level execution through an LLM-based parser that decomposes instructions into atomic actions, plus a pluggable executor (rule-based with Dijkstra routing, or VLM-based for perception-sensitive scenarios). A hierarchical waypoint system (coarse intersection nodes plus ~70 fine-grained interpolated waypoints per sidewalk) provides spatial abstraction for navigation, while a modular traffic system (VehicleManager, PedestrianManager, IntersectionManager) simulates dynamic urban mobility with traffic signals and crosswalk rules.

## Results

In the navigation task (Case Study 1), GPT-4o achieves the highest consistent success rate (93.33% across Easy/Medium/Hard) with the lowest collision count (1.73–5.10), while Claude-3.7-Sonnet reaches 96.67% SR on Medium tasks. However, all VLM agents exhibit high red-light violation rates (46–100%), revealing a critical gap between visual perception and active attention. In the multi-agent delivery task (Case Study 2), DeepSeek-V3 and Claude-3.5-Sonnet earn the highest average profits (69.48 and 69.07 respectively), but with high variance (std 16.77 and 20.69), whereas Gemini-2.5-Flash achieves more stable but lower returns (42.42, std 3.10). GPT-4o-mini fails entirely across all delivery metrics (0.00 profit, 0.00 successful orders).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIMWORLD is the only simulator in Table 1 that simultaneously supports procedural generation, language-controllable editing, open-vocabulary action space, and the highest physical/social realism rating (+++), while also accommodating humanoid, robot, and vehicle agents on Unreal Engine. Compared to CARLA [10] and EmbodiedCity [13], which offer high realism but lack procedural generation and language-based control, SIMWORLD adds open-ended world expansion and LLM-native interfaces. Against Minecraft-based platforms (Minedojo [12], Mindcraft [50]), SIMWORLD provides realistic continuous physics (gravity, momentum) rather than discrete block mechanics. In the agent evaluations, no single LLM dominates: GPT-4o leads in navigation consistency (93.33% SR across all difficulties), Claude-3.7-Sonnet achieves the lowest red-light violation rate (46.43%) and highest Medium-difficulty SR (96.67%), and Gemini-2.5-Pro shows the best obstacle sensitivity (lowest CC-S of 3.08 on Hard). In the delivery task, DeepSeek-V3 and Claude-3.5-Sonnet outperform GPT-4o (43.91) and Gemini-2.5-Flash (42.42) in average profit, but with substantially higher variance, while GPT-4o-mini and QwQ fail to generate meaningful economic activity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIMWORLD extends the embodied agent evaluation line of work by integrating three capabilities that no prior simulator combines: realistic UE5-based physical and social dynamics, open-ended procedural world generation with language-driven editing, and a native open-vocabulary LLM/VLM agent interface. It builds conceptually on LLM-Planner's high-to-low action decomposition and CoELA's modular executor design, but generalizes both by supporting rule-based and VLM-based executors, multi-modal inputs, and city-scale environments. The paper positions itself against game-like platforms (Minecraft, Pokemon) that lack physical realism, domain-specific simulators (CARLA, AI2-THOR) with narrow scope, and social sandboxes (OASIS, Virtual Village) that lack open-endedness and physical grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SIMWORLD provides a much-needed open-source platform for evaluating LLM/VLM agents in realistic, open-ended physical and social environments, filling a gap between simplified game worlds and narrow domain simulators. The case studies reveal a fundamental limitation: current VLMs cannot function as truly embodied agents because they fail to actively adjust their visual attention (e.g., looking up at traffic signals), exposing a disconnect between passive perception and active sensor control. Open questions include whether the platform can support large-scale online RL training, how findings generalize beyond the two case studies, and whether the observed personality-driven behavioral differences in multi-agent settings are robust across scales and task types.
