## Problem

Existing rendering-based pre-training methods for autonomous driving (e.g., UniPAD, ViDAR, GaussianPretrain, VisionPAD) operate on dense BEV or volumetric representations, making them fundamentally incompatible with Sparse Perception Models (SPMs) that rely on implicit sparse queries lacking explicit spatial positions. No pre-training scheme existed that could effectively transfer geometric knowledge to these sparse query-centric architectures, leaving a significant gap in leveraging abundant unlabeled data for SPMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255026/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes 6 surround-view camera images (704×256 or 1408×512) with their intrinsics and extrinsics, plus LiDAR point clouds used exclusively as depth ground truth at valid measurement pixels. It produces 25,600 predicted 3D Gaussians (position, 3D scale, 4D rotation, opacity, spherical harmonics coefficients) that are splatted to render multi-view RGB images and depth maps. During fine-tuning, the plug-in module outputs task-specific predictions: a 200×200×16 voxel grid with 18 semantic classes for occupancy, or 3D bounding boxes with translation, scale, orientation, velocity, and attribute attributes for detection.

## Method

SQS introduces learnable Gaussian queries (paired anchor vectors and query features) processed by a Gaussian Transformer Decoder that combines self-encoding, 3D sparse convolution on a voxelized grid, and deformable cross-attention with multi-scale FPN image features to iteratively refine Gaussian parameters via MLP heads. Pre-training is self-supervised: rendered RGB and depth images are compared against ground truth using weighted L1 loss (ω_rgb=1.0, ω_depth=0.05), with depth supervised only at LiDAR-observed pixels. For fine-tuning, a plug-in Query Interaction module freezes the pre-trained model, filters low-opacity anchors, and applies k-nearest-neighbor spatial attention to fuse pre-trained Gaussian query features into task-specific queries, enabling seamless integration into heterogeneous sparse decoders (e.g., SparseBEV's 2D BEV queries or PETR's 3D queries) without architectural modification.

## Results

On the SurroundOcc validation set for 3D semantic occupancy prediction, GaussianFormer+SQS achieves 31.52 IoU and 20.40 mIoU, improving over the GaussianFormer baseline by +1.69 IoU and +1.30 mIoU, and surpassing the dense-method SurroundOcc (20.30 mIoU) and OccFormer (19.03 mIoU). For 3D object detection on nuScenes, SQS pre-training boosts SparseBEV† from 55.8 to 56.6 NDS (+0.8) and Sparse4Dv3† from 56.1 to 56.9 NDS (+0.8) with ResNet50, and from 59.2 to 60.2 NDS (+1.0) and 62.3 to 63.1 NDS (+0.8) with ResNet101. Data efficiency analysis shows the benefit amplifies with less labeled data: fine-tuning on only 10% of annotations yields a +3.7 mIoU gain over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SQS is a plug-in pre-training method applied on top of existing sparse perception models, so the most direct comparisons are GaussianFormer vs. GaussianFormer+SQS (occupancy) and SparseBEV†/Sparse4Dv3† vs. their SQS-enhanced versions (detection). On occupancy, SQS lifts GaussianFormer from 19.10 to 20.40 mIoU, surpassing the dense-method baselines SurroundOcc (20.30 mIoU) and OccFormer (19.03 mIoU), and substantially outperforming BEVFormer (16.75 mIoU), TPVFormer* (17.10 mIoU), Atlas (15.00 mIoU), and MonoScene (7.31 mIoU). On 3D detection with ResNet50, SparseBEV†+SQS (56.6 NDS) and Sparse4Dv3†+SQS (56.9 NDS) both exceed StreamPETR (55.0 NDS), Sparse4Dv2 (53.9 NDS), SOLOFusion (53.4 NDS), BEVPoolv2 (52.6 NDS), BEVStereo (50.0 NDS), and PETRv2 (45.6 NDS). With the larger ResNet101 backbone, Sparse4Dv3†+SQS reaches 63.1 NDS / 54.4 mAP, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses SparseBEV† (59.2 NDS), SOLOFusion (58.2 NDS), Sparse4D (55.0 NDS), BEVDepth (53.5 NDS), BEVFormer (51.7 NDS), and DETR3D (43.4 NDS). The consistent gains across both sparse detection architectures (SparseBEV and Sparse4Dv3) and two backbone scales validate the generality of the query-based splatting pre-training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) pre-training line (GaussianPretrain, VisionPAD, UniPAD) by adapting it to the sparse query-centric paradigm, which prior rendering-based pre-training methods were fundamentally incompatible with. While dense BEV-centric pre-training reconstructs scenes from volumetric or BEV representations, SQS instead predicts 3D Gaussians from a small set of learnable Gaussian queries and uses self-supervised splatting to reconstruct RGB and depth, then transfers knowledge to task-specific queries via a spatial-aware local attention module. The approach also builds on the sparse perception model family (SparseBEV, Sparse4D, GaussianFormer) as target architectures, and on deformable cross-attention mechanisms for efficient 3D-to-2D feature sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPMs are increasingly preferred for real-world autonomous driving deployment due to their computational efficiency, yet they lacked a dedicated pre-training strategy; SQS fills this gap with a plug-and-play design that works across heterogeneous sparse architectures. The data-efficiency results (+3.7 mIoU with only 10% labeled data) suggest practical value in annotation-scarce settings. Key limitations include the extra inference overhead from the frozen plug-in model and the lack of semantic disambiguation among pre-trained queries for different downstream tasks, leaving open how to incorporate semantic information during pre-training and whether the approach extends to end-to-end driving pipelines.
