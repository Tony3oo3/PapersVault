## Problem

FFT-based operators in neural network training (e.g., circulant-structured adapters) require intermediate complex-valued tensors that consume extra memory and break in-place computation. Standard rFFT maps N real inputs to N+2 real outputs, creating a dimensional mismatch that forces additional allocation, while existing libraries like FFTW and cuFFT lack bfloat16 support and cannot preserve the original input buffer. No prior method achieves a true real-domain, fully in-place FFT with consistent input-output memory for deep learning training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a real-valued tensor of size N (the signal to transform) and a circulant weight vector of the same size, both in bfloat16 or float32. The forward FFT produces the full frequency spectrum encoded within the same N real-valued slots—real parts of y_k at index k and imaginary parts at the conjugate-symmetric index r−k—rather than the N+2 slots required by rFFT. The inverse IFFT recovers the original real-valued signal in-place, and the elementwise product of two such spectra (for circulant matrix multiplication) preserves the symmetric layout, so the entire forward and backward pass never allocates auxiliary complex buffers.

## Method

rdFFT exploits two structural properties of the Cooley-Tukey decomposition on real-valued inputs: (1) conjugate symmetry y_{r−k} = conjugate(y_k) is preserved at every recursive sub-FFT level, and (2) the DC term y_0 and Nyquist term y_{r/2} are always real. These observations allow an interleaved memory layout where each conjugate pair's real and imaginary parts occupy two real-valued slots (indices k and r−k), squeezing the N+2 real storage of rFFT down to exactly N. A formal proof (Proposition 1) shows that butterfly operations on conjugate-symmetric pairs produce symmetric four-element groups, guaranteeing the layout remains valid across all recursion stages and enabling fully in-place execution. For the inverse transform, the butterfly graph is traversed in reverse with symmetric/anti-symmetric decomposition to recover real-valued outputs without auxiliary complex arrays. The operator is integrated into model training via block-circulant parameter matrices, where the linear transformation y = Cx is computed as IFFT(FFT(c) ⊙ FFT(x)), and gradients follow the same in-place pattern, achieving zero intermediate tensor allocation throughout forward and backward passes.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B (GSM8K), rdFFT with p=4096 reduces peak GPU memory to 17.91 GB, compared to 20.71 GB for standard FFT, 19.16 GB for rFFT, and 26.90 GB for full fine-tuning. On RoBERTa-large (MRPC), rdFFT with p=1024 achieves 5.77 GB peak memory versus 6.72 GB (FFT), 6.09 GB (rFFT), and 6.15 GB (full fine-tuning). In single-layer experiments (D=4096, B=1), rdFFT with p=4096 uses only 0.09 MB peak memory—a 1,531× reduction over full fine-tuning (144.33 MB). Downstream MRPC accuracy is maintained at 90.0–90.7%, on par with rFFT and full fine-tuning (90.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to PyTorch's standard complex FFT (torch.fft.fft), rdFFT reduces peak memory by 1.8–2.8 GB on LLaMA2-7B and by ~0.95 GB on RoBERTa-large, primarily by eliminating intermediate complex-valued tensor allocations during forward and backward passes. Against rFFT (torch.fft.rfft), rdFFT saves an additional 1.2–1.3 GB on LLaMA2-7B and ~0.32 GB on RoBERTa-large, because rFFT still requires N+2 real-valued output buffers that mismatch the N-element input. Against LoRA (r=32), rdFFT with p=4096 uses 17.91 GB versus LoRA's 18.96 GB on LLaMA2-7B, and 5.77 GB versus 6.24 GB on RoBERTa-large, despite having fewer trainable parameters (0.75 MB vs. 48 MB). On MRPC accuracy, rdFFT (90.0–90.7%) matches rFFT (90.3–90.7%) and is within 0.9% of full fine-tuning (90.9%), confirming no quality degradation. The trade-off is throughput: rdFFT runs at ~0.93 k tokens/sec versus 1.45–1.77 k for FFT/rFFT, due to CUDA synchronization overhead in the in-place butterfly operations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Cooley-Tukey recursive FFT decomposition and the conjugate-symmetry property exploited by rFFT, but departs from existing in-place FFT libraries (FFTW, cuFFT) that still require N+2 real slots for a length-N real input, creating a dimensional mismatch. It also extends the line of in-place operations in deep learning (e.g., in-place BatchNorm, in-place distillation) to the Fourier-transform operator, and is motivated by the memory overhead in circulant-matrix-based fine-tuning methods such as Block Circulant Adapter (BCA) and FourierFT. The key conceptual departure is an implicit complex encoding that packs the N/2+1 complex rFFT output into exactly N real-valued slots, enabling true in-place forward and backward passes with bfloat16 support that prior libraries lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners running FFT-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning (circulant adapters, FourierFT-style methods) on memory-constrained GPUs, rdFFT eliminates all intermediate tensor allocations and supports the bfloat16 precision that modern training pipelines require, yielding up to ~2× memory reduction over rFFT and orders-of-magnitude reduction over full fine-tuning in single-layer settings. The main limitation is that the implicit real-valued encoding precludes direct access to the complex spectrum, so any workflow requiring explicit frequency-domain manipulation (spectral filtering, custom frequency operations) must decode back to complex form, breaking the in-place guarantee. Open questions include extending the approach to non-power-of-two sizes without zero-padding overhead, supporting 2D/3D FFTs, and whether the throughput penalty (~50% slower than rFFT at large sizes) can be mitigated with better CUDA kernel design.
