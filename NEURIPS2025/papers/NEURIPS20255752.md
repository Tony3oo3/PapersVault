## Problem

Existing SD-based Real-VSR methods face a fundamental conflict: jointly optimizing spatial detail and temporal consistency in a single model yields suboptimal trade-offs, where improving one objective harms the other. Additionally, the inherent temporal consistency preserved in degraded LQ videos is largely ignored, missing a strong prior that could anchor detail generation on a stable temporal basis. The paper asks how to simultaneously achieve realistic frame details and temporal coherence in a single diffusion step.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of adjacent LQ video frames (current and preceding) at 512×512 resolution in a sliding-window fashion, with the first frame handled via self-replication. LQ inputs are degraded using the RealESRGAN pipeline (blur, noise, compression) with identical parameters within a video and random parameters across videos. Output is a ×4 super-resolved HQ video frame produced in a single diffusion step, with both LoRA branches merged into the frozen SD UNet at inference for efficient deployment.

## Method

DLoRAL decouples temporal consistency and spatial detail learning into two specialized LoRA branches (C-LoRA and D-LoRA) within a shared one-step residual diffusion UNet (SD V2.1 backbone). A Cross-Frame Retrieval (CFR) module warps adjacent latent features via SpyNet and performs top-k attention with a learnable per-position threshold to selectively fuse complementary information from the previous frame, providing degradation-robust temporal priors. Training alternates between a consistency stage (CFR + C-LoRA trainable, optimized with pixel, LPIPS, and optical-flow losses) and a detail enhancement stage (D-LoRA trainable with an added classifier score distillation loss, while CFR and C-LoRA are frozen), with smooth loss interpolation between stages to prevent instability. At inference, both LoRA modules are merged into the UNet and a single diffusion step refines the LQ latent to HQ, achieving ~10× speedup over multi-step diffusion-based VSR methods.

## Results

On the UDM10 benchmark, DLoRAL achieves the best MUSIQ (65.62), CLIPIQA (0.652), MANIQA (0.373), LPIPS (0.327), and DOVER (42.87) scores among all compared methods. On the real-world RealVSR dataset, DLoRAL attains the highest DOVER (49.65), CLIPIQA (0.617), and DISTS (0.150) while maintaining the lowest warping error (17.30) among diffusion-based VSR methods. On VideoLQ, DLoRAL leads in MUSIQ (63.85), CLIPIQA (0.567), MANIQA (0.344), and DOVER (38.51). Inference takes only 346 s for 50 frames of 512×512 ×4 VSR on an A100 GPU, roughly 10× faster than Upscale-A-Video (3640 s) and MGLD (4146 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Real-ISR baselines, DLoRAL substantially outperforms RealESRGAN and StableSR on all perceptual metrics (e.g., MUSIQ 65.62 vs. 49.84 and 47.81 on UDM10) while also achieving better temporal consistency (E_warp 4.72 vs. 7.58 and 8.44). OSEDiff, the one-step Real-ISR method, is the closest competitor on DOVER for UDM10 (48.40 vs. 42.87) but suffers from significantly worse warping error (5.22 vs. 4.72) and lower CLIPIQA (0.574 vs. 0.652), confirming that frame-independent generation sacrifices temporal coherence. Among diffusion-based Real-VSR methods, DLoRAL consistently surpasses Upscale-A-Video, MGLD, and STAR on perceptual quality: on UDM10 it leads in MUSIQ (65.62 vs. 61.05/61.31/40.79), CLIPIQA (0.652 vs. 0.445/0.453/0.267), and MANIQA (0.373 vs. 0.318/0.291/0.244). On the real-world RealVSR benchmark, DLoRAL achieves the best DOVER (49.65 vs. 36.14/42.04/30.21) and CLIPIQA (0.617 vs. 0.436/0.530/0.532) while maintaining the lowest warping error (17.30) among all diffusion-based VSR methods. The user study further confirms these quantitative gains, with DLoRAL receiving 93 out of 120 votes versus 14 (MGLD), 8 (STAR), and 5 (Upscale-A-Video). In terms of efficiency, DLoRAL's single-step inference (346 s) is approximately 10× faster than Upscale-A-Video (3640 s) and MGLD (4146 s), and 8× faster than STAR (2830 s), while using fewer parameters (1300 M vs. 14442 M for Upscale-A-Video).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-step residual diffusion framework from OSEDiff (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2024) from image to video super-resolution, and adapts the dual-LoRA decoupling idea from PiSA-SR (Sun et al., 2024) to separate temporal consistency learning from spatial detail enhancement. Unlike prior diffusion-based Real-VSR methods (Upscale-A-Video, MGLD, STAR) that jointly optimize detail and consistency in a single multi-step pipeline, DLoRAL introduces a Cross-Frame Retrieval module to extract degradation-robust temporal priors directly from LQ inputs and trains two specialized LoRA branches in alternating stages. The key conceptual departure is treating the LQ video's inherent inter-frame consistency as a learnable prior rather than something to be enforced purely through loss functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× inference speedup over existing diffusion-based VSR methods (346s vs. 2830–4146s for 50 frames) makes high-quality video restoration practically deployable, and the decoupled dual-LoRA strategy offers a general recipe for resolving conflicting objectives in generative restoration. However, the inherited 8× VAE downsampling limits recovery of very fine details (e.g., small text), and the heavy VAE compression may itself disrupt temporal coherence—addressing this would require a VAE purpose-built for Real-VSR. An open question is whether the alternating training schedule generalizes to longer video sequences or more complex degradation distributions beyond the RealESRGAN pipeline used here.
