## Problem

Despite LLMs' ability to solve multi-step reasoning problems, it remains unclear whether their internal computations are modular and localized to specific components or distributed opaquely across all parameters. Prior circuit analysis was limited to small models (3-layer transformers, GPT-2) or provided only partial evidence, leaving open whether frontier-scale LLMs (7B–27B) employ identifiable, interpretable sub-circuits for propositional logic that involves distracting clauses and multi-step inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes few-shot prompted propositional logic problems (five boolean variables, two rules with a logical operator and a linear chain, three fact assignments, and one query variable) and generates counterfactual prompt variants by flipping the QUERY token, swapping rule positions, or altering fact truth values. It produces a sparse set of attention heads partitioned into four functional families—queried-rule locating, queried-rule mover, fact-processing, and decision heads—along with calibrated indirect-effect scores quantifying each head's causal contribution to the model's first answer token prediction.

## Method

The core technique is Causal Mediation Analysis applied at the attention-head level: flipping the QUERY token generates a counterfactual prompt that forces the model to execute an entirely different causal chain (QUERY → Relevant Rule → Relevant Facts → Decision), and the logit difference between the two answers serves as the outcome. Necessity is established by patching individual head outputs with counterfactual activations and measuring the resulting logit shift; sufficiency is verified by freezing the complement of the identified circuit to counterfactual values and confirming the output is preserved (~94% logit-difference recovery for Gemma-2-9B). Fine-grained sub-component patching (query, key, value vectors) with targeted counterfactuals—such as swapping rule locations or flipping fact values—disambiguates each head family's specific role. A key architectural finding is 'lazy reasoning': the circuit remains largely dormant until the Question token is encountered, after which the four head families activate sequentially. Gemma-2-27B additionally exhibits dedicated logical-operator heads (attending to 'and'/'or' tokens) and a more parallel information flow compared to the strictly sequential 9B circuit.

## Results

The paper identifies four families of attention heads (queried-rule locating, queried-rule mover, fact-processing, and decision heads) that form a sparse reasoning circuit in Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B, and Gemma-2-27B. The sufficiency test (Table 1) shows the full 13-head circuit preserves 94% of the logit difference for Gemma-2-9B and 98% for Mistral-7B, while removing any single head family drops the metric to near zero or negative. Model proof accuracies on the logic problem are ~83% (Gemma-2-9B, disjunction), ~70% (Mistral-7B, disjunction), and 86% (Gemma-2-27B, full problem). Attention statistics confirm specialized roles: the locating head places >85% weight on the queried rule's conclusion, the mover head allocates ~50% to the QUERY token (5× the second-largest), and decision heads focus >80% on the correct answer token.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability paper rather than a benchmarking study, so comparisons take the form of circuit ablations and cross-model/cross-scale contrasts. Ablating any of the four head families from the full circuit collapses the preserved logit difference from 0.94 to between −1.11 and 0.17, confirming all four families are necessary. Across scales, Gemma-2-27B's circuit is more parallel than Gemma-2-9B's (fact-processing heads bypass decision heads directly) and additionally contains 'logical-operator' heads that attend to 'and'/'or' tokens—components absent or causally inert in the 9B model. Compared to a 3-layer transformer trained from scratch on the same task (which achieves 100% accuracy), the pre-trained LLMs exhibit far more modular and specialized attention-head roles, whereas the small model's heads show situation-dependent, unstructured attention patterns. The paper also notes that swapping the prompt format (Rules/Facts order) leaves the locating and decision heads nearly unchanged while the intermediate mover and fact-processing heads shift, indicating partial robustness of the circuit to surface-level format changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the circuit-analysis methodology of Wang et al. (2023a) and the causal mediation analysis framework of Pearl (2001) from small models (GPT-2, 3-layer transformers) to frontier-scale LLMs (7B–27B parameters) on a multi-step propositional logic problem. Prior mechanistic interpretability work on reasoning has focused on single-hop tasks (token copying, addition, indirect object identification) or small task-trained models; this paper is the first to characterize full reasoning circuits in pre-trained LLMs for a problem requiring multi-step inference with distracting clauses. It also contrasts its findings with small-model studies (Brinkmann et al. 2024; Guo et al. 2025b) that show intermingled, less modular reasoning, highlighting a qualitative shift in mechanism with scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that three architecturally distinct LLMs (Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B, Gemma-2-27B) all employ analogous four-family modular circuits for propositional logic suggests that specialized reasoning components organically emerge from pre-training, providing a concrete mechanistic account of in-context logical reasoning. The 'lazy reasoning' finding—that circuits primarily activate only after the query token is seen—challenges the intuition that models pre-process all context uniformly. Key open questions include whether these modular circuits persist for longer reasoning chains, how they interact with chain-of-thought generation in 'thinking' models, and whether the scale-dependent differences (e.g., logical-operator heads appearing only at 27B) generalize beyond this narrow problem.
