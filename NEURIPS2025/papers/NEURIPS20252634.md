## Problem

Non-smooth non-convex finite-sum coupled compositional optimization (FCCO) arises in group DRO, X-risk minimization, and constrained learning, but existing methods face two limitations: a high O(ε^{-6}) iteration complexity under mean Lipschitz continuity assumptions, and reliance on vanilla SGD-type updates that are poorly suited for deep learning. This paper closes both gaps by introducing stochastic momentum methods with a new state-of-the-art O(ε^{-5}) rate.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite-sum compositional objective F(w) = (1/n)Σ f_i(g_i(w)) where f_i are non-smooth (e.g., hinge) and g_i are smooth or weakly convex, along with minibatch samples for stochastic estimation of g_i. For constrained applications, it additionally takes inequality constraint functions g_i(w) ≤ 0 and optimizes a smoothed hinge penalty. The output is a nearly ε-stationary point of the original non-smooth objective (or an ε-KKT point for constrained problems), with the smoothing parameter set to λ = ε/C_f to control the approximation gap between the smoothed and original objectives.

## Method

The key insight is a two-level smoothing strategy: first, each non-smooth outer function f_i is replaced by its Moreau envelope f_{i,λ} (equivalently, Nesterov smoothing when f_i is convex), converting the problem into a smooth FCCO; second, when inner functions g_i are weakly convex, an additional Moreau envelope is applied to the already-smoothed objective, yielding a fully smooth problem amenable to momentum-based optimization. SONEX is a single-loop algorithm that tracks each g_i(w_t) via coordinate-wise MSVR-style updates and applies a stochastic momentum (or Adam) gradient step, achieving O(ε^{-5}) complexity without requiring mean Lipschitz continuity of the inner gradient. ALEXR2 is a double-loop algorithm that reformulates the smoothed problem as a strongly-convex-strongly-concave minimax via the conjugate of f_{i,λ}, solves the inner minimax with a Bregman-proximal update on the dual variable, and uses the resulting inexact gradient for an outer momentum update on w. For constrained optimization, the method optimizes a smoothed hinge penalty Φ_λ(w) = g_0(w) + (1/m)Σ f_λ(g_i(w)), where f_λ is the Moreau envelope of the hinge function, yielding O(ε^{-5}) complexity for finding an ε-KKT solution.

## Results

The paper establishes a new state-of-the-art iteration complexity of O(ε⁻⁵) for non-smooth non-convex FCCO, improving over the prior O(ε⁻⁶) of SONX. Empirically, SONEX outperforms SONX and PrimalDual (ALEXR) on GDRO with CVaR across Camelyon17, Amazon, and CelebA in both training loss and test accuracy. ALEXR2 with smoothed hinge penalty achieves higher AUC values than SOX (squared-hinge), SONX (hinge), and ICPPAC on Adult and COMPAS while maintaining comparable constraint satisfaction. On BDD100K continual learning, the modified SONEX achieves higher target-task accuracy than SOX with similar constraint violation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SONEX (single-loop, momentum/Adam) improves over SONX [6] (single-loop, SGD) by reducing complexity from O(ε⁻⁶) to O(ε⁻⁵) and empirically achieves lower training loss and higher test accuracy on all three GDRO datasets (Camelyon17, Amazon, CelebA). Against PrimalDual (ALEXR [5]), SONEX also shows superior GDRO performance, particularly on the large-scale Amazon dataset with 1252 groups. For constrained optimization, ALEXR2 with smoothed hinge penalty outperforms SOX with squared-hinge [7], SONX with hinge [8], and the double-loop ICPPAC [10] on AUC maximization with ROC fairness constraints, achieving higher AUC on both Adult and COMPAS while maintaining similar constraint violation levels. In continual learning on BDD100K, the modified SONEX with smoothed square penalty beats SOX with squared hinge on target-task accuracy improvement while retaining comparable constraint satisfaction. The key practical advantage is that momentum/Adam updates (used by SONEX and ALEXR2) are more suitable for deep learning than the vanilla SGD updates required by prior methods like SONX.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-smooth FCCO line initiated by Hu et al. (SONX, NeurIPS 2023), which first addressed non-smooth outer functions with weakly convex inner functions using MSVR-based variance reduction and SGD updates. The key departure is the introduction of stochastic momentum methods (the first for non-smooth FCCO), enabled by a two-layer smoothing strategy: Moreau envelope smoothing of the outer functions (equivalent to Nesterov smoothing when convex) and, when inner functions are weakly convex, a nested Moreau envelope on the resulting objective. The inner-function tracking mechanism in SONEX is inherited from MSVR [16], while the double-loop ALEXR2 builds on the ALEXR primal-dual algorithm [5] for the inner minimax subproblem, and the momentum gradient estimation follows the SOX structure [2].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(ε^{-5}) complexity improvement over the prior O(ε^{-6}) state-of-the-art is practically meaningful for deep learning applications where momentum and Adam updates are standard, and the extension to non-convex inequality-constrained optimization yields a new best rate for finding ε-KKT solutions. A notable negative finding is that the STORM variance-reduction technique (used in MSVR) does not improve the convergence rate because the bottleneck is the O(1/ε) smoothness of the smoothed outer functions, not gradient variance. Open questions include whether the EVLB assumption (Assumption 4.3) can be weakened or removed, whether the gap between SONEX's and ALEXR2's dependence on inner batch size can be closed, and whether the smoothing-based approach extends to settings where the proximal mapping is not easily computable.
