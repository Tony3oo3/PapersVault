## Problem

Existing crystal generation models face a fundamental trade-off: LLMs excel at predicting discrete atomic types (high compositional validity) but struggle with continuous variables like coordinates and lattice parameters (low structural validity), while equivariant diffusion models handle continuous geometry well but produce inaccurate atomic compositions. No prior framework had combined these complementary strengths into a single generative pipeline for periodic materials.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255635/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts natural-language prompts specifying either unconditional generation, a target atomic composition (e.g., a chemical formula), or a space group number. The LLM component consumes these prompts and emits a CIF-format text sequence where fractional coordinates are rounded to two decimal places, lattice lengths to one decimal, and angles as integers. The final output is a complete crystal structure M = (A, X, L) — atom type one-hot vectors, fractional coordinates in the quotient space R^{N×3}/Z^{N×3}, and a 3×3 lattice matrix — where A comes directly from the LLM and X, L are refined by the diffusion model.

## Method

CrysLLMGen trains two components independently on the same dataset: a LLaMA-2-7B fine-tuned via LoRA to generate crystal CIF text, and a CSPNet-based equivariant diffusion model (EGNN with Fourier-transform message passing) trained for structure prediction given fixed atom types. At sampling time, the LLM produces an intermediate (Â, X̂, L̂); the atom types Â are retained as the final composition, while X̂ and L̂ are injected at an intermediate diffusion timestep τ (0 ≤ τ ≤ T) rather than starting from pure noise, and the diffusion model denoises from τ down to 0. Fractional-coordinate diffusion uses a Wrapped Normal forward process with a truncation function to respect periodicity, while lattice diffusion follows standard DDPM with a cosine schedule. The key design insight is that the LLM's pre-trained chemical prior biases the composition toward thermodynamically stable formulas (reducing ΔE_chem), while the equivariant diffusion network corrects geometric errors (reducing ΔE_struct), yielding a 68% improvement in stability over the best denoising baseline and 90% over the LLM baseline on S.U.N. metrics.

## Results

On MP-20, CrysLLMGen(7B) achieves 99.94% structural validity and 93.55% compositional validity, simultaneously matching the best denoising models on structure and the best LLM on composition. On the S.U.N. stability benchmark (MP-20), CrysLLMGen generates 16.79% stable and 9.21% S.U.N. materials, outperforming the best denoising baseline SymmCD (9.99% stable, 6.76% S.U.N.) by 68% and 36% respectively, and the LLM baseline Llama-2-7B (12.67% stable, 4.84% S.U.N.) by 33% and 90%. On the CSP task, CrysLLMGen achieves the highest match rate across all four datasets (e.g., 74.24% on MP-20, 42.16% on MPTS-52) with the lowest RMSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrysLLMGen achieves the best compositional validity (93.55%) among all models on MP-20, matching the LLM baseline Llama-2-7B while surpassing all denoising models (best: UniMat at 89.40%) by over 4 points. On structural validity, CrysLLMGen (99.94%) ties with CDVAE, DiffCSP, MatterGen, CrysBFN, and FlowLLM, and clearly outperforms UniMat (97.20%), SymmCD (92.30%), FlowMM (96.85%), and Llama-2-7B (97.70%). In the S.U.N. stability evaluation, CrysLLMGen dominates all baselines: it generates 62.02% metastable structures vs. 56.60% for Llama-2-7B and 40.01% for SymmCD, and 9.21% S.U.N. vs. 4.84% (Llama-2) and 6.76% (SymmCD). On the CSP task, CrysLLMGen outperforms all four baselines (CDVAE, DiffCSP, FlowMM, Llama-2-7B) on both match rate and RMSE across Perov-5, Carbon-24, MP-20, and MPTS-52, with the largest gains over diffusion/flow models on MP-20 (74.24% vs. 61.39% for FlowMM). For text-conditional generation, CrysLLMGen matches Llama-2-7B on atomic composition accuracy but outperforms it by 42% on space group prediction, attributable to the diffusion model's structural refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of crystal generation research: LLM-based autoregressive generation (Gruver et al. [7], which fine-tunes LLaMA-2 on CIF text) and equivariant diffusion models (DiffCSP [10], CDVAE [8], MatterGen [11]). The closest prior work is FlowLLM [15], which also pairs an LLM with a generative module, but CrysLLMGen departs in three ways: it uses diffusion rather than flow matching, trains both components in parallel rather than sequentially, and injects LLM outputs at an intermediate denoising timestep τ rather than treating them as a base distribution. The diffusion component extends DiffCSP's CSPNet/EGNN architecture but repurposes it for a structure-prediction task (given atom types, predict coordinates and lattice) rather than joint generation of all three components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid framework offers a clean, architecture-agnostic recipe for combining discrete and continuous generative modeling in crystal design, and the decomposition of E_hull into compositional (ΔE_chem) and structural (ΔE_struct) terms provides a principled rationale for why freezing the LLM's atom types is effective. Key limitations include the lack of any interaction or feedback between the LLM and diffusion modules during training or sampling, reliance on a 7B LLaMA-2 backbone, and the use of a vanilla (non-latent) diffusion model. Open questions include whether mutual guidance between the two components could further improve quality, whether the approach scales to larger LLMs or latent diffusion, and whether the intermediate-timestep injection strategy generalizes to other multimodal generative tasks beyond crystals.
