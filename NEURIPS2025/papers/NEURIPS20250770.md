## Problem

Memory-based dynamic graph learning methods suffer from vanishing gradients and information forgetting in RNN updaters, limiting their ability to capture long-range temporal dependencies. Existing approaches also fail to adaptively balance long-range and short-range temporal correlations, and naively fusing structural (topological) features with temporal encodings introduces modality conflicts that degrade performance. SALoM addresses all three gaps simultaneously within a unified memory-based framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250770/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a continuous stream of timestamped interaction events (u, v, t) with associated edge features and node features, processed in chronological order with a temporal encoder for time intervals. It maintains per-node temporal memory (dimension 172) and dual hash-based structural memory tables (long-range dimension 64, short-range dimension 16) that are updated incrementally as events arrive. The output is a stochastic unified node embedding z_u^t produced by an information-bottleneck decoder (sampled from a learned Gaussian), which is then used for binary link prediction via a scoring function.

## Method

SALoM builds on TGN's memory-based architecture but replaces the standard GRU memory updater with a Long-Short Memory Updater (LSMU) that employs a Sparse Mixture-of-Experts controller to dynamically route each event among CFC cells (closed-form continuous-time Neural ODE updaters for long-range dependencies) and GRU cells (for short-range patterns), with top-r gating to prevent over-generalization. For structural encoding, a Co-occurrence Neighbor Encoder computes long- and short-range co-occurrence counts between nodes using dual hash tables, capturing relative topology without iterative message passing. Temporal and structural encodings are fused through an Information Bottleneck module: a linear projection followed by variational reparameterization (Gaussian sampling) regularized by KL divergence against a standard normal, which resolves modality conflicts and produces distinguishable node representations. The training objective combines binary cross-entropy for link prediction with a β-weighted KL term (β=1e-3) to enforce the information bottleneck constraint.

## Results

SALoM achieves the best average rank (1.07) on transductive AP across 13 dynamic graph datasets, outperforming the closest competitors DyGFormer (3.15) and CNE-N (3.61). The most dramatic gains appear on political/diplomatic datasets: US Legis. (92.27% AP vs. 72.58% for CNE-N), UN Trade (93.86% vs. 77.97%), and UN Vote (86.81% vs. 58.10%), representing improvements of 14.18%, 15.89%, and 32.48% over the nearest competitor. On inductive AP, SALoM similarly leads with an average rank of 1.53, with particularly large margins on Can. Parl. (96.20% vs. 87.74% for DyGFormer) and UN Trade (85.46% vs. 69.84% for CNE-N).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALoM wins on 11 of 13 datasets for transductive AP, tying with DyGFormer on Wikipedia (99.03) and Social Evo. (94.73). The largest margins are on political/diplomatic graphs: US Legis. (+19.69 pp over CNE-N), UN Trade (+15.89 pp over CNE-N), and UN Vote (+28.71 pp over CNE-N), where structural co-occurrence patterns are most informative. Against the strongest overall competitor DyGFormer, SALoM leads by 2.26 pp on MOOC, 1.75 pp on Enron, and 1.75 pp on Can. Parl., while trailing slightly on Wikipedia Trans-AUC (98.87 vs. 98.91) and LastFM Trans-AUC (92.32 vs. 93.05). Against CNE-N, the closest structural-encoding competitor, SALoM leads on every dataset, with the gap widening from ~0.2 pp on Reddit to ~34 pp on UN Vote. Memory-based baselines (JODIE, DyRep, TGAT, TGN) are consistently outperformed by 5–20+ pp, confirming that the LSMU's adaptive long-short balancing and the IB-based structural fusion provide substantial gains over fixed RNN memory updates. On inductive settings, SALoM maintains its lead, with the most notable advantage on Can. Parl. (96.20% vs. 87.74% for DyGFormer) and UN Trade (85.46% vs. 69.84% for CNE-N), though it trails DyGFormer slightly on Wikipedia (98.49 vs. 98.59) and Social Evo. (92.84 vs. 93.14).

## Relation to Prior Work

SALoM extends the memory-based temporal graph network lineage (JODIE → DyRep → TGN) by replacing the standard GRU memory updater with a hybrid architecture that combines closed-form continuous-time cells (CFC, from Hasani et al.) for long-range dependencies and GRUs for short-range patterns, routed through a sparse mixture-of-experts controller. It also adopts the co-occurrence neighbor encoding scheme introduced by DyGFormer and CNE-N as its structural encoder, but departs from prior work by fusing temporal and structural representations through an information bottleneck variational framework rather than simple concatenation or linear projection. The paper explicitly positions itself against both memory-based methods (which suffer from gradient vanishing and information forgetting) and neighbor-based sequential methods (which face quadratic complexity and limited neighbor sampling), arguing that neither paradigm alone resolves the long-short temporal balancing and structural integration challenges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses three concrete and well-motivated gaps in dynamic graph learning—long-range temporal capture, adaptive long-short balancing, and conflict-free structural-temporal fusion—achieving substantial gains (up to 32.48% AP) on politically structured datasets where prior methods struggle. The leakage-free evaluation protocol is a methodologically important contribution, revealing that prior results on datasets like USLegis may have been inflated by intra-timestamp information leakage. Open questions remain around the computational cost of the dual hash-based structure memory at scale, the sensitivity of the MoE routing to dataset characteristics, and whether the IB fusion generalizes beyond binary link prediction to multi-relational or attributed settings.
