## Problem

Despite rapid advances in multimodal AI for Sound Source Localization (SSL), it remains unknown whether these models exhibit modality biases (e.g., favoring vision over audio) or how they resolve cross-modal conflicts when visual and auditory cues disagree or are absent. Existing SSL datasets further compound this problem by featuring large, centrally placed sounding objects that encourage visual shortcut learning, and by relying on mono audio that lacks the spatial cues (ITD/ILD) essential for accurate localization.

## Input / Output

EchoPin takes a static RGB image (from MSCOCO, with one target sounding object) paired with a 10-second, 16 kHz two-channel stereo audio waveform synthesized via Unity 3D simulation using depth maps from DepthAnything and HRTF-filtered through KEMAR dummy-head impulse responses. The stereo waveform is converted into a cochleagram tensor of shape 66×160,000×2 (ERB filters × temporal samples × binaural channels), with the two channels merged via 1D convolution before feeding the audio encoder. The model outputs a spatial similarity heatmap whose peak activation indicates the predicted sound source location, evaluated against ground-truth bounding boxes using A-Acc (spatial) and V-Acc (semantic) metrics.

## Method

EchoPin is a neuroscience-inspired dual-encoder model built on the IS3 architecture, where the key innovation lies in its biologically grounded audio preprocessing pipeline: stereo waveforms are first filtered with pre-measured HRTFs (KEMAR, 710 spatial positions) to emulate pinna, head, and torso spectral shaping, then decomposed into cochleagrams via 66-channel ERB filterbanks that capture tonotopic and temporal resolution more faithfully than conventional mel-spectrograms. The model is trained on the newly curated AudioCOCO dataset (28,224 pairs), which eliminates visual shortcut biases by controlling object sizes (three bins: 0–5%, 5–15%, 15–30%), semantics, and spatial positions, and by synthesizing depth-aware stereo audio through a 3D physics simulator. Training initializes from pre-trained IS3 weights and optimizes end-to-end with a triplet loss for audio-visual semantic alignment and a CIoU loss for spatial localization. Ablation variants (EchoPin-M for mono, EchoPin-S for mel-spectrogram) confirm that both stereo structure and cochlear-inspired frequency decomposition are critical, and the full model spontaneously exhibits a human-like horizontal-over-vertical localization asymmetry attributable to the interaural spacing and HRTF-shaped stereo input.

## Results

On the AudioCOCO benchmark (Size2, Congruent condition), EchoPin achieves 50.9% A-Acc, substantially outperforming IS3 (16.5%), CAVP (15.2%), and AVSegformer (15.5%), though still below human performance (79.3%). Under the challenging ConflictVCue condition, EchoPin retains 21.9% A-Acc while IS3 drops to 12.3% (near the 9.5% chance level), and under AbsVCue, EchoPin reaches 54.0% versus IS3's 13.5%. In the Multi-Instance Localization task, EchoPin achieves 24.1% A-Acc (Size2) compared to 7.9% for IS3 and 36.4% for humans. On standard SSL benchmarks (CIoU), EchoPin also leads: 45.02 on VGG-SS and 85.87 on Flickr-SoundNet, surpassing IS3 (42.96 / 84.40) and CAVP (43.58 / 85.03).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoPin outperforms all eight baselines across every experimental condition and object size. Against IS3 (the base architecture it builds upon), EchoPin gains approximately 34 points in Congruent (50.9% vs 16.5%), 9.6 points in ConflictVCue (21.9% vs 12.3%), and 40.5 points in AbsVCue (54.0% vs 13.5%), with the largest gap in AbsVCue reflecting IS3's heavy reliance on visual cues. CAVP and AVSegformer, trained on large-scale audio-visual datasets (AVSBench, VPO), achieve only 15.2% and 15.5% in Congruent respectively, and collapse to near-chance (7.0–7.1%) under ConflictVCue, confirming their visual shortcut bias. FNAC, LVS, and SSLTI all fall below 13% in Congruent and drop to 6.5–7.2% under ConflictVCue, performing at or below chance in AOnly. The large-scale multimodal models ImageBind (5.8%) and LanguageBind (4.6%) perform worst overall, barely exceeding chance even in the Congruent condition, suggesting their generic contrastive pretraining does not transfer well to fine-grained spatial localization. On standard SSL benchmarks (Table S3), EchoPin also leads IS3 and CAVP by 2–3 CIoU points on both VGG-SS and Flickr-SoundNet, validating that the AudioCOCO training data and stereo cochleagram pipeline generalize beyond the proposed benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on IS3 (Senocak et al., 2023), a dual-stream CNN architecture for sound source localization, extending it with HRTF-based spatial filtering and cochleagram representations inspired by auditory neuroscience. It departs from the broader SSL literature (CAVP, AVSegformer, LVS, FNAC) in two fundamental ways: replacing monaural mel-spectrogram inputs with stereo audio synthesized via 3D physical simulation (Unity + KEMAR HRTFs), and introducing a controlled benchmark protocol (AudioCOCO) that systematically probes modality conflict, absence, and multi-instance scenarios—dimensions prior datasets and evaluations largely ignored. The human psychophysics comparison further distinguishes this work from purely computational SSL studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic demonstration that current multimodal SSL models are heavily vision-biased—dropping to near-chance under conflicting visual cues while humans remain robust—provides a clear diagnostic for why these models fail in real-world deployment. The emergent horizontal-vertical localization asymmetry in EchoPin, confirmed by the interaural-axis rotation ablation, offers a concrete example of how biologically grounded sensory preprocessing can produce human-like perceptual structure without explicit behavioral supervision. Key limitations include the restriction to static images (no temporal dynamics), simulation-based audio that omits real-world effects like refraction and reverberation, and the persistent gap to human performance, especially for small objects and multi-instance scenes. Future work on video-based SSL, more physically complete sound propagation, and scaling the AudioCOCO pipeline to larger scenes would address these gaps.
