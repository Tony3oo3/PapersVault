## Problem

Existing panoptic symbol spotting methods for CAD drawings rely on rasterization, graph construction, or point-based representations that lose geometric continuity and primitive-level structural awareness, leading to blurred symbol boundaries, poor handling of overlapping symbols, and high computational costs on large-scale drawings. The key gap is the absence of a compact, type-agnostic representation that preserves the geometric structure of vector primitives while remaining efficient for Transformer-based architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes CAD drawings as sets of heterogeneous vector primitives (lines, arcs, circles, ellipses, splines, etc.), optionally augmented with layer IDs as prior information. Each primitive is converted into a sequence of sampled line segments, and the model produces per-primitive predictions that are fused into a unified panoptic output: instance class labels with binary masks for countable things (30 classes such as windows, doors, furniture) and dense semantic labels for uncountable stuff (5 classes such as walls, railings). When layer priors are unavailable, primitive IDs substitute for layer IDs in the position encoding, enabling robust operation in real-world settings where detailed annotations are missing.

## Method

VecFormer introduces a line-based representation where each primitive is uniformly or dynamically sampled into K line segments, each encoded with a 7-dimensional feature vector (segment length, unit direction, midpoint, and primitive geometric centroid) and a 3D position (midpoint coordinates plus normalized layer ID). A Point Transformer V3 backbone extracts line-level features, which are aggregated into primitive-level features via combined max-and-average Line Pooling, then enhanced by a Layer Feature Enhancement module that fuses intra-layer context through attention pooling. A six-layer dual-branch Transformer decoder (inspired by OneFormer3D) with Query Selection jointly produces instance proposals (class logits + einsum-based masks) and per-primitive semantic logits. A training-free Branch Fusion Refinement post-processing step resolves instance-semantic inconsistencies through three operations: confidence-based Overriding, majority Voting on instance labels, and Remasking to remove label-contaminated primitives, yielding a coherent panoptic prediction.

## Results

On the FloorPlanCAD dataset, VecFormer achieves a Panoptic Quality (PQ) of 91.1 with prior information and 88.4 without, setting a new state-of-the-art in panoptic symbol spotting. The most striking gain is in the Stuff category: VecFormer improves Stuff-PQ by 21.2 points over the second-best method (DPSS, 64.7) without prior information, and by 9.6 points over SymPoint-V2 (80.8) with prior information. At the primitive level, VecFormer also leads in semantic quality with F1 of 93.8 and length-weighted F1 of 92.2, surpassing the previous best (SymPoint-V2) by 4.3 and 3.9 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VecFormer outperforms all seven baselines across every reported metric in both evaluation settings. Without prior information, it surpasses the strongest competitor DPSS [40] by +2.2 PQ (88.4 vs. 86.2) and by a dramatic +21.2 Stuff-PQ (85.9 vs. 64.7), indicating that prior methods struggle severely with uncountable stuff regions when layer annotations are unavailable. With prior information, VecFormer edges out SymPoint-V2 [32] by +1.0 PQ (91.1 vs. 90.1) and by +9.6 Stuff-PQ (90.4 vs. 80.8), while also exceeding CADSpotting [9] by +2.2 PQ and +9.8 Stuff-PQ. Earlier methods such as PanCADNet [1], CADTransformer [4], GAT-CADNet [6], and SymPoint [8] trail by 15–30 PQ points, reflecting the generational gap between rasterization/graph/point-based approaches and the proposed line-based Transformer. The consistent advantage in Stuff-PQ over Thing-PQ across all baselines highlights that VecFormer's line-based representation is particularly effective at capturing the elongated, structurally continuous regions characteristic of stuff categories like walls and railings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant point-based representation paradigm in panoptic symbol spotting (SymPoint, CADSpotting) by proposing a line-based representation that encodes each vector primitive as a sequence of line segments with intrinsic geometric attributes. It builds on OneFormer3D's dual-branch Transformer decoder and Query Selection strategy for joint instance-semantic prediction, and adopts SymPoint-V2's Layer Feature Enhancement module for intra-layer context. The backbone choice of Point Transformer V3 for handling unordered spatial data is a natural fit for the line-segment tokenization, while the Branch Fusion Refinement post-processing is a novel contribution not present in prior CAD spotting methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 21.2-point Stuff-PQ improvement without prior information is practically significant for real-world CAD deployment where layer annotations are often unavailable, suggesting that geometric continuity in the representation itself can substitute for structural priors. The line-based representation's compactness (~15% fewer tokens at equal sampling density, ~8× shorter than CADSpotting at comparable density) makes it more scalable for large drawings. A key open question is whether the uniform sampling strategy generalizes to highly complex geometric regions, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation and propose geometry-aware dynamic sampling as future work. The method's reliance on a single benchmark (FloorPlanCAD) leaves open whether the line-based advantage holds across other vector graphic domains.
