## Problem

Flow-based latent generative models like Stable Diffusion 3 produce photorealistic images, yet existing methods that use them as priors for inverse imaging problems (super-resolution, inpainting, deblurring) fail to match that fidelity. The core obstacles are the intractable data likelihood in VAE latent space, conflicting optimization objectives between the data term and the generative prior, and reconstructions that drift away from the observed measurements. No prior work had unified flow matching, variational inference, and hard data consistency into a single training-free framework for latent inverse problems.

## Input / Output

FLAIR takes a degraded observation (bicubic-downsampled low-res image, motion-blurred image, or masked image) at 768×768 resolution, a text prompt (e.g., 'A high quality photo of a face' or a DAPE-generated caption), and the forward operator A. It operates entirely in the latent space of the SD3.5 Medium VAE and produces one or multiple posterior samples of the clean image. The method is training-free: the flow model and VAE are frozen, and all optimization is performed at inference time over the variational mean μ_x in latent space, with 50 network function evaluations per reconstruction.

## Method

FLAIR reformulates the standard variational posterior-sampling objective by replacing the score-matching regularizer with a flow-matching loss that directly compares the learned velocity field v_θ(x_t, t) against a reference velocity u_t derived from the variational distribution (Proposition 1). A deterministic trajectory adjustment (Proposition 2) reparameterizes the noisy latent as a linear interpolation between the posterior mean and a single-step velocity-based endpoint prediction, replacing pure random noise with a model-guided direction that keeps samples on high-density regions of the prior. Data fidelity is enforced via hard data consistency: after each regularizer gradient step, the current estimate is projected onto the measurement manifold by minimizing ‖y − A(μ_x)‖², decoupling the two objectives. Finally, a time-dependent calibration weight λ_R(t) is computed offline from the flow model's conditional flow-matching error at each timestep, down-weighting the regularizer where the model is least reliable (t < 0.2 is zeroed out entirely).

## Results

On FFHQ (768×768, 50 NFE), FLAIR achieves LPIPS of 0.213 (SR×8), 0.271 (SR×12), 0.236 (motion deblurring), and 0.184 (inpainting), with patch FID scores of 13.3, 16.2, 10.7, and 8.7 respectively — substantially outperforming all baselines on both perceptual metrics. On DIV2K, FLAIR similarly leads with LPIPS of 0.353 (SR×8) and 0.163 (inpainting) and FID of 26.5 and 11.0. The method also demonstrates high sample diversity (posterior variance concentrated in high-frequency regions) and can produce accurate MMSE estimates via 8-sample ensembling, improving PSNR by ~1 dB while confirming samples are distributed around the posterior mean.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLAIR consistently outperforms all four latent-space baselines (ReSample, FlowDPS, RSD, FlowChef) on LPIPS and FID across all four inverse problems and both datasets. The largest margins appear in inpainting, where FLAIR achieves FID 8.7 on FFHQ versus 42.5 (FlowDPS) and 70.8 (ReSample), and in motion deblurring (FID 10.7 vs. 40.2 for FlowChef). On PSNR, the picture is more nuanced: FlowDPS and RSD occasionally match or slightly exceed FLAIR on SR×8/×12 (e.g., RSD 29.69 vs. FLAIR 29.54 on FFHQ SR×8), but FLAIR wins decisively on inpainting PSNR (23.69 vs. 21.97 for RSD) and motion deblurring PSNR (29.61 vs. 27.67 for RSD). In pixel space (CelebA-HQ 256×256), FLAIR also beats DDNM, DPS, Moment Matching, and IGDM on both LPIPS and FID for inpainting and SR×8. The paper notes that FlowDPS tends to produce blurry textures, FlowChef loses semantic coherence, and RSD suffers from mode collapse without its repulsive term.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLAIR extends the variational posterior sampling framework for inverse problems (Mardani et al. 2024; Zilberstein et al. 2025/RSD) by reformulating the score-based regularizer into a flow-matching velocity-field objective, making it the first scheme to unify latent generative modeling, flow matching, and variational inference for inverse problems. It adopts the hard data consistency principle from ReSample (Song et al. 2024) but applies it within a variational distribution rather than a single trajectory, and departs from ad-hoc flow-guidance methods (FlowDPS, FlowChef) by grounding the guidance in a principled variational lower bound. The deterministic trajectory adjustment and accuracy calibration are novel contributions not present in any prior variational or flow-based inverse problem solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLAIR provides a practical, training-free recipe for leveraging any pretrained flow-based model (e.g., SD3) as a prior for diverse inverse problems, with a clean ablation showing that deterministic trajectory adjustment is the single most critical component for avoiding mode collapse in low-density regions of the prior. The accuracy calibration insight—that the flow-matching loss actually increases near t=0, necessitating a time-dependent weight—offers a general diagnostic for any flow-based generative model. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or video, how it handles out-of-distribution content where the prior assigns near-zero density, and whether the additional DTA hyperparameters (α, δt) can be eliminated or learned automatically.
