## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods produce biased soft labels when applied to long-tailed datasets, causing significant tail-class performance degradation. While prior work like LTDD addresses parameter-level distribution mismatch, the bias encoded in soft labels—an increasingly critical component in modern distillation pipelines—remains unaddressed. This paper identifies two entangled sources of soft-label bias (the imbalanced distillation model and the distilled images) and provides both a theoretical generalization bound and an empirical perturbation analysis to characterize the problem.

## Input / Output

ADSA operates as a post-hoc module on the output of any existing distillation pipeline: it consumes the distilled synthetic images (e.g., 224×224 RGB for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR), the raw logits from a pretrained distillation model evaluated on those images, and the empirical class frequencies π_y from the original long-tailed training set. It produces a single calibrated soft-label vector per distilled image (K-dimensional probability distribution) by applying a per-image logit shift parameterized by one scalar τ. No modification to the distilled images, the distillation model, or the evaluation training loop is required.

## Method

ADSA applies logit adjustment (f_y(x) − τ·log π_y) to the distillation model's raw logits for each distilled image, where τ is a single calibration strength and π_y is the class frequency in the original long-tailed training set. The key insight is that the distilled images themselves serve as a hold-out validation set: because they exhibit a distribution shift relative to the original data, the class-wise average confidence computed over them reveals the model's output imbalance. ADSA then optimizes τ to minimize the variance of these class-wise average confidences (Eq. 10), effectively equalizing confidence across all classes while preserving the inter-class relational structure of the soft labels. The search is performed over τ ∈ (0, 3) and requires no additional training, making the module hyperparameter-free at deployment and seamlessly pluggable into SRe2L, EDC, GVBSM, MTT, and DREAM pipelines.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k-LT with EDC at IPC=50, ADSA raises overall top-1 accuracy from 38.6% to 41.4% and tail-class accuracy from 12.4% to 24.2% (+11.8%). On CIFAR-10-LT at IPC=50 and IF=100, EDC+ADSA achieves 74.8% top-1 accuracy versus 56.0% for EDC alone (+18.8%), and SRe2L+ADSA reaches 45.3% versus 34.6% for SRe2L (+10.7%). On CIFAR-100-LT at IPC=50 and IF=100, EDC+ADSA improves from 40.9% to 43.5%. Gains are largest under extreme imbalance (IF=100) and larger IPC budgets, confirming that ADSA's benefit grows with the severity of the long-tail distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADSA consistently outperforms all baselines it is integrated with. Against EDC (the strongest baseline), ADSA yields the largest absolute gains: +18.8% on CIFAR-10-LT (IPC=50, IF=100: 74.8% vs 56.0%), +11.8% tail accuracy on ImageNet-LT (IPC=50: 24.2% vs 12.4%), and +2.6% on CIFAR-100-LT (IPC=50, IF=100: 43.5% vs 40.9%). Against SRe2L, improvements reach +10.7% on CIFAR-10-LT (IPC=50, IF=100) and +6.6% tail accuracy on ImageNet-LT (IPC=10: 14.2% vs 7.6%). Against GVBSM, gains are +16.0% on CIFAR-10-LT (IPC=50, IF=100: 46.9% vs 30.9%). LTDD, which addresses long-tailed distillation via parameter-level matching, is outperformed by EDC+ADSA on CIFAR-10-LT (74.8% vs 64.0% at IPC=50, IF=100) and on ImageNet-LT (41.4% vs not directly reported but SRe2L+ADSA at 37.0% already exceeds LTDD's parameter-matching approach). Notably, ADSA also outperforms its own uncalibrated soft-label variants (Table 3: DREAM+ADSA 59.9% vs DREAM+soft label 41.0% at IPC=10), confirming that the calibration step, not merely the presence of soft labels, drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the D3S domain-shift generalization bound (Loo et al., ICML 2024) to the long-tailed setting, deriving an imbalance-aware upper bound that explicitly separates posterior alignment from feature-distribution alignment. It departs from LTDD (Zhao et al., 2025), which addresses long-tailed dataset distillation through parameter-level trajectory matching, by identifying that the increasingly critical soft-label component in modern pipelines (SRe2L, EDC, GVBSM) carries its own entangled bias. The calibration mechanism in ADSA is borrowed directly from logit adjustment (Menon et al., ICLR 2021), repurposed from an inference-time correction for long-tail classifiers into a post-hoc soft-label debiasing step for distilled datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that soft labels — a component now standard in large-scale distillation pipelines — silently degrade under class imbalance, and that a single hyperparameter-free logit adjustment can recover up to ~12% tail-class accuracy on ImageNet, provides a practical, drop-in fix for a previously overlooked failure mode. The post-hoc design means existing distillation codebases need no architectural changes, making adoption low-cost. Key limitations include the assumption of shared class-conditional distributions (p_tr(x|y)=p_te(x|y)), dependence on known class-frequency statistics (ruling out online/streaming settings), and the additive bias decomposition that the authors acknowledge may not hold strictly. Open questions include frequency-agnostic calibration, extension to domain-shift scenarios, and whether the same bias structure appears in generative-based distillation methods.
