## Problem

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which improves reasoning in text-based LLMs, often degrades performance in video understanding by inducing a failure mode the authors term 'Visual Thinking Drift'—where reasoning traces become unmoored from actual video content, hallucinating visual details and overriding correct perceptual intuitions. This paper provides a systematic empirical study across 10 benchmarks and 20 subtasks showing the problem is widespread (especially in open-source MLLMs), offers a Bayesian explanation rooted in the norm asymmetry between language and visual projection matrices, and proposes a targeted solution.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip sampled at 2 FPS (4 FPS for Vinoground) with up to 16 frames during training and 32 at inference, alongside a natural-language question. It outputs a free-form chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer, which is either a multiple-choice selection or a numerical value depending on the benchmark. The video token budget is 128×28×28 pixels during training and 256×28×28 at inference, and subtitles are excluded from Video-MME evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is Visual Evidence Reward (VER), a binary reward signal integrated into the GRPO reinforcement learning objective that explicitly incentivizes reasoning traces which cite verifiable, question-relevant visual facts from the video. An auxiliary LLM judge (Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct) evaluates each generated response against pre-computed visual evidence snippets, assigning a reward of α=0.3 when the trace properly references grounded visual details. The visual evidence itself is generated offline via 'inverted prompting': an external MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) is given the (question, ground-truth answer) pair and asked to produce minimal supporting visual cues, reducing the search space and enforcing question-specific alignment. Training follows a two-stage pipeline—SFT on Video-R1-COT-165k for cold-start, then 2,000 iterations of GRPO with VER on a Reversed-in-Time + Video-R1-260k mixture—applied to a Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model, yielding the Video-VER model that achieves up to +9.0% absolute accuracy over its base with CoT prompting.

## Results

Video-VER (7B, CoT) achieves top-1 accuracy of 64.1% on MVBench, 59.3% on Video-MME, 52.7% on VideoMMMU, 65.1% on MMVU, 53.1% on VideoHallucer, 70.0% on EventHallusion, 34.6% on VSI-Bench, 74.0% on TempCompass, 52.8% on TVBench, and 14.4% (Group) on Vinoground. It ranks first among open-source models on 9 of 10 benchmarks, with the sole exception being VSI-Bench where Video-R1 leads (35.8 vs. 34.6). Compared to its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B under CoT prompting, Video-VER yields consistent absolute gains ranging from +1.6% (Vinoground) to +9.0% (VideoHallucer), averaging +4.0% across all 10 benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest open-source video reasoning competitor, Video-R1 (7B, CoT), Video-VER wins on 9 of 10 benchmarks, with the largest margin on EventHallusion (+18.6 pp: 70.0 vs. 51.4) and VideoHallucer (+8.9 pp: 53.1 vs. 44.2); it trails only on VSI-Bench (34.6 vs. 35.8). Compared to its own base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B under CoT prompting, Video-VER improves every benchmark by +1.6 to +9.0 pp, confirming that the Visual Evidence Reward specifically rescues the performance drop that CoT typically causes. Against non-reasoning 7B baselines (LLaVA-OneVision, AoTD, Video-UTR, Kangaroo), Video-VER leads on all benchmarks where they report results, e.g., 64.1 vs. 57.1 on MVBench and 59.3 vs. 57.7 on Video-MME over LLaVA-OneVision. Against proprietary models, Video-VER remains behind GPT-4o (CoT) and OpenAI-o3 on most benchmarks (e.g., 64.1 vs. 65.1 on MVBench, 59.3 vs. 67.0 on Video-MME), but narrows the gap substantially compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and notably surpasses GPT-4o on EventHallusion (70.0 vs. 83.6 is GPT-4o's advantage, but Video-VER beats GPT-4o DA on VideoHallucer: 53.1 vs. 61.8 is still GPT-4o's lead). The paper also reports results on NExT-QA (81.0) and EgoSchema (66.2), where Video-VER outperforms agentic-workflow systems like SeViLA (73.8) and VideoAgent (71.3/54.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based reinforcement learning post-training paradigm (from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1) to video MLLMs, building directly on Video-R1's framework for video reasoning with rule-based rewards. It departs from prior video CoT work (Video-R1, AoTD, VITED, TinyLLaVA-Video-R1) by identifying a systematic failure mode—'Visual Thinking Drift'—where reasoning traces become unmoored from visual evidence due to language-prior dominance in the decoder, and by introducing a Visual Evidence Reward that explicitly supervises grounding via an LLM-as-a-judge. The inverted prompting strategy for generating question-specific visual evidence is a novel data-construction technique that contrasts with both standard CoT annotation and generic video captioning approaches used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CoT prompting often degrades video understanding—contrary to its success in text-based reasoning—challenges a broadly held assumption and redirects attention toward grounding rather than verbosity as the key to video intelligence. The VER mechanism is a simple, lightweight addition to existing RL pipelines that practitioners can adopt without architectural changes, though its current scope is limited to closed-ended (MCQ) tasks where rule-based reward computation is tractable. Open questions include extending VER to open-ended video QA, reducing dependence on a large external MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) for evidence generation, and whether the norm-asymmetry explanation generalizes to newer architectures with more balanced vision-language integration.
