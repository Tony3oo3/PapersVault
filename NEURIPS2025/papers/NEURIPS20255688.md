## Problem

Generating high-resolution 3D shapes with explicit volumetric representations is bottlenecked by the quadratic cost of full attention in Diffusion Transformers, making training at 1024³ resolution computationally prohibitive. Prior explicit-latent methods are limited to 256³ resolution and require 32+ GPUs, while implicit vecset-based methods suffer from asymmetric VAE architectures that compromise training efficiency and geometric fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255688/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (processed at 518×518 through DINO-v2) as conditioning and produces a watertight 3D mesh at up to 1024³ SDF resolution. Internally, the pipeline operates on sparse SDF volumes where only voxels with |SDF| < 1/128 are retained, and the VAE maintains this sparse volumetric format consistently across input, latent, and output stages—eliminating the cross-modality translation (e.g., point cloud → 1D vecset → dense volume) common in prior 3D VAEs.

## Method

Direct3D-S2 combines a symmetric sparse SDF VAE (SS-VAE) with a Diffusion Transformer (SS-DiT) that employs a novel Spatial Sparse Attention (SSA) mechanism. SSA partitions sparse 3D tokens into spatially coherent blocks based on their 3D coordinates (rather than 1D indices) and computes attention through three gated modules: sparse 3D compression (block-level global context via sparse 3D conv + mean pooling), spatial blockwise selection (top-k block retrieval using GQA-aggregated compression scores), and sparse 3D window (local feature interaction). A custom Triton GPU kernel handles the variable-length sparse block structure, achieving 3.9× forward and 9.6× backward speedup over FlashAttention-2 at 128k tokens. The generative model is trained with rectified flow (conditional flow matching) using a progressive multi-resolution strategy from 256³ to 1024³, and a sparse conditioning mechanism extracts only foreground DINO-v2 tokens to reduce cross-attention overhead.

## Results

On the image-to-3D benchmark, Direct3D-S2 achieves ULIP-2 of 0.3111, Uni3D of 0.3931, and OpenShape of 0.1752, outperforming all compared methods across all three shape-image alignment metrics. For VAE reconstruction at 1024³ resolution, the SS-VAE achieves a Chamfer Distance of 7.91 and F1(0.001) of 70.19, substantially surpassing Trellis (CD 28.60), XCube (CD 15.42), and Dora (CD 15.12). The method trains at 1024³ resolution on only 8 A100 GPUs over 7 days, whereas prior state-of-the-art methods require 32+ GPUs even at 256³ resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Direct3D-S2 outperforms all four image-to-3D baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor TripoSG [17], it gains +0.0485 on ULIP-2 (0.3111 vs 0.2626), +0.0061 on Uni3D (0.3931 vs 0.3870), and +0.0024 on OpenShape (0.1752 vs 0.1728). The margin over Trellis [39] is larger: +0.0286 ULIP-2 and +0.0176 Uni3D. Hunyuan3D 2.0 [49] and Hi3DGen [44] trail by the widest gaps, with ULIP-2 scores of 0.2535 and 0.2725 respectively. On the VAE reconstruction front (Table 4), the SS-VAE at 1024³ achieves a Chamfer Distance of 7.91 and F1(0.001) of 70.19, roughly doubling the F1(0.001) of XCube [31] (31.85) and Dora [6] (31.86), while Trellis [39] lags far behind at CD 28.60 and F1(0.001) 11.57. Notably, Direct3D-S2 achieves these gains while training on 8 GPUs at 1024³, compared to 32+ GPUs at 256³ for prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Native Sparse Attention (NSA) mechanism—originally designed for 1D sequences in LLMs—to unstructured sparse 3D volumetric data by redesigning block partitioning around 3D spatial coherence and modifying the compression, selection, and windowing modules for irregular token sets. It also departs from prior 3D VAEs (CLAY, Trellis, XCube) that use heterogeneous representations (point cloud input → 1D vecset latent → dense volume output) by enforcing a unified sparse SDF format across encoder, latent, and decoder stages. The DiT backbone and rectified flow objective follow the Trellis/Direct3D lineage, while the sparse conditioning mechanism builds on DINO-v2 feature extraction with foreground masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The headline result—training at 1024³ resolution on 8 GPUs versus 32+ GPUs for 256³ in prior methods—makes gigascale 3D generation practically accessible to smaller labs, which is a meaningful shift for the field. The SSA mechanism is a general-purpose attention acceleration technique that could transfer to other sparse 3D tasks (e.g., scene generation, 4D content). Limitations include the modest quality margins over SOTA (Table 1), the absence of error bars or multi-seed runs, and the acknowledged gap between forward (3.9×) and backward (9.6×) speedups due to top-k sorting overhead. Open questions include whether SSA generalizes beyond SDF volumes to other sparse 3D representations and whether the approach scales beyond 1024³.
