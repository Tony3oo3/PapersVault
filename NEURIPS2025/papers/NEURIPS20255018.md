## Problem

Most neural operators (FNO, CNO, transformers) assume Cartesian grids, making them impractical for PDEs on arbitrary or complex geometries common in engineering. Graph-based approaches like MeshGraphNet handle unstructured domains but lack multi-scale capability and fail to achieve space-time resolution invariance, meaning they cannot generalize to unseen spatial or temporal resolutions. RIGNO asks whether a multi-scale, graph-based neural operator can simultaneously learn on arbitrary point clouds and remain invariant to changes in input/output resolution.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an unstructured point cloud (no grid or mesh structure required) with per-point solution values, spatially varying coefficients, and scalar time/lead-time inputs, and produces solution values at the same coordinates. A key design constraint is that the physical mesh can be at any resolution during training or inference; the model internally constructs a coarser 'regional mesh' by random subsampling of physical nodes, so the same architecture handles inputs from 42² to 128² grids without retraining. For time-dependent problems, the operator is time-continuous: given a solution at time t and a lead time τ, it predicts the solution at t+τ, enabling arbitrary autoregressive rollout strategies at inference.

## Method

RIGNO follows an encode-process-decode GNN paradigm where the encoder aggregates information from physical nodes onto a coarser regional mesh via single-step message passing, the processor performs P stacked message-passing steps on multi-scale regional edges (Delaunay triangulation at multiple subsampling levels to capture long-range interactions), and the decoder projects back to the physical mesh. Three novel mechanisms drive its performance: (1) edge masking, where a random subset of edges is disabled at each message-passing step during training, forcing the model to learn weaker spatial correlations and achieving approximate resolution invariance; (2) temporal fractional pairing, a fine-tuning strategy that uses the pretrained model's own output at an intermediate time as input to create training pairs with lead times smaller than the training time resolution, unlocking generalization to arbitrarily small time steps; and (3) lead-time conditioning embedded in normalization layers at every hidden layer. Training uses an all2all strategy that pairs every time snapshot with every later snapshot, quadratically increasing effective training pairs.

## Results

On 13 unstructured/point-cloud datasets spanning 8 PDEs, RIGNO-18 achieves the lowest median relative L1 error on all 13 benchmarks, with errors ranging from 0.01% (ACE) to 6.90% (CE-Gauss). Gains over the strongest baselines are often an order of magnitude: e.g., on NS-Gauss, RIGNO-18 achieves 2.29% vs. 76.4% (MeshGraphNet) and 92.5% (UPT). On 9 Cartesian-grid datasets, RIGNO-18 is best on 8 of 9, outperforming state-of-the-art Cartesian operators (CNO, scOT, FNO) by factors of 2–10× on most problems (e.g., 1.12% vs. 5.03% for CNO on NS-PwC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIGNO-18 dominates all five arbitrary-domain baselines (MeshGraphNet, Geo-FNO, FNO DSE, GINO, UPT) across all 13 unstructured datasets, with the largest margins on advection-dominated problems (NS-Gauss: 2.29% vs. 76.4% for MGN; NS-PwC: 1.58% vs. 33.1% for MGN). Among the arbitrary-domain baselines, GINO is consistently the strongest competitor (e.g., 1.19% on NS-SVS, 4.38% on Elasticity), while UPT and MeshGraphNet degrade severely on complex dynamics (errors exceeding 50–100% on several NS and CE datasets). On Cartesian grids, RIGNO-18 outperforms CNO, scOT, and FNO on 8 of 9 datasets; the sole exception is Poisson-Gauss where scOT achieves 0.68% vs. RIGNO-18's 1.80%, reflecting scOT's advantage on elliptic problems with global interactions. The paper attributes RIGNO's edge in time-dependent and advection-dominated settings to the expressive power of local message passing combined with multi-scale edges for long-range interactions, while noting that gains on purely elliptic problems are less pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

RIGNO extends the MeshGraphNet lineage of GNN-based PDE simulation by introducing a two-level (physical/regional) mesh architecture inspired by weather-forecasting graph networks, where processing occurs on a downsampled regional mesh rather than directly on the input point cloud. It departs from Cartesian neural operators (FNO, CNO, scOT) by operating entirely on graphs, enabling arbitrary domain geometries without domain masking or learned diffeomorphisms. The all2all training strategy and lead-time conditioning are inherited from Poseidon, while the multi-scale regional mesh with adaptive support radii and the edge-masking mechanism are novel contributions that address the resolution-sensitivity weakness identified in prior graph-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RIGNO provides the first GNN-based neural operator that simultaneously achieves spatial and temporal resolution invariance on arbitrary domains, closing a gap that has limited the practical deployment of graph-based PDE surrogates in engineering. The edge-masking technique's dual role as both a resolution-invariance regularizer and a free uncertainty-quantification mechanism is a practical contribution likely to be adopted in other GNN-based scientific ML settings. Key limitations include the restriction to 2D, the absence of theoretical generalization or approximation guarantees, and the observation that large training sets are still needed for small errors—issues the authors flag for future work alongside 3D extension and foundation-model integration.
