## Problem

No public benchmark existed that combined high-fidelity musculoskeletal digital twins of amputees with bionic prosthetic limbs and modern learning-based control, leaving the question of whether human-level coordination between biological and mechanical limbs is achievable untested. MyoChallenge 2024 addresses this by establishing a reproducible, open-source testbed for joint control of integrated musculoskeletal and prosthetic systems across both manipulation and locomotion tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252100/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The manipulation track consumes a 222-dimensional observation (joint positions/velocities of the 27-DOF myoArm and 26-DOF MPL, 6-DOF object state, 63 muscle activations, 5 binary contact labels, and start/goal pillar positions) and outputs an 80-dimensional continuous action vector (63 muscle stimulations in [-1,1] plus 17 MPL joint position targets). The locomotion track consumes a 365-dimensional observation including a 10×10 terrain height map over a 2×2 m area, socket interaction forces, and full muscle state, and outputs 55 actions (54 muscle stimulations plus a discrete selection among 5 predefined OSL controller parameter sets). Notably, the prosthetic leg's internal state is unobservable—only the socket force on the femur is available—mimicking real-world sensing limitations.

## Method

The benchmark is built on MyoSuite (MuJoCo-based), providing physiologically realistic musculoskeletal models with up to 4000× speedup over prior simulators and full contact dynamics. Two tracks are defined: a bimanual passover manipulation task (myoMPL: biological arm + Modular Prosthetic Limb) and a 100 m varied-terrain locomotion task (myoOSL: bilateral biological leg with transfemoral amputation + Open Source Leg). Winning solutions span distinct methodological families: Muscle Heads used muscle synergies (9 synergies coordinating 63 muscles via learned controlled variables) with SAC and a 3-stage curriculum; LNS Group applied DynSyn (K-Medoids clustering of muscle length correlations) with trajectory interpolation for the MPL; MSKBioDyn combined PPO with AMP adversarial imitation learning from the Gait120 motion-capture dataset (120 subjects) and a VAE-compressed terrain encoder; Loco UCSD employed TD-MPC2 model-based RL with a decoder-free latent world model and MPPI planning. All top solutions relied on curriculum learning to progressively increase difficulty, and the benchmark uniquely incorporates physiological metrics (joint overextension torque as a pain proxy, total muscle activation as metabolic cost) alongside task success and time.

## Results

In the manipulation track, the winning team (Muscle Heads) achieved a score of 0.26 (26% success rate), a 44% improvement over the second-place score of 0.18, while the provided baseline achieved only ~3% success rate. The winning solution completed episodes in 5.9 seconds with 0.034 muscle activation units and a peak contact force of 481 N. In the locomotion track, the winning team (MSKBioDyn) achieved a score of 0.72, walking up to 71.6 m across terrains including rough ground (9 cm), stairs (7 cm steps), and hills (24 cm). Across both tracks, only 3 of 53 teams in manipulation and 2 in locomotion surpassed the provided baselines, with top solutions outperforming the baseline by a factor of approximately 10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper, so comparisons are between competing team solutions and the provided baselines rather than between proposed methods. In the manipulation track, the provided baseline (PPO with a three-stage curriculum, ~3% success rate) was surpassed by three teams, with the winning Muscle Heads solution (SAC + muscle synergies + inverse kinematics) achieving 0.26 — roughly an 8.7× improvement over baseline. The second-place LNS Group (DynSyn dynamical synergistic representation + trajectory interpolation) scored 0.18. In the locomotion track, no baseline was provided for the amputee myoOSL model (only intact myoLeg baselines via DEP-RL and reflex-based controllers exist in MyoSuite), so the two qualifying teams (MSKBioDyn at 0.72 and Loco UCSD at 0.19) are compared only against each other. MSKBioDyn (PPO + AMP imitation learning on Gait120) outperformed Loco UCSD (TD-MPC2 model-based RL) by a factor of ~3.8 in score, primarily due to superior terrain generalization enabled by the large motion-capture dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MyoChallenge competition series (2022, 2023) and the MyoSuite simulation framework, but introduces a fundamentally new problem formulation: the co-control of a biological musculoskeletal limb and a robotic prosthetic limb as a unified task. Prior benchmarks in this space either focused exclusively on prosthetic control (osim-rl NIPS 2018) or on purely biological motor control (Learning to Run, MyoChallenge 2022/2023), leaving the integration gap unaddressed. The winning solutions draw on diverse methodological lineages—muscle synergies from neuroscience (Tresch et al.), adversarial motion priors from stylized character control (AMP), and model-based RL (TD-MPC2)—but no single prior work addresses the specific challenge of coordinating overactuated musculoskeletal actuators with a separately-controlled prosthetic device.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial gap between current AI capabilities and human bionic coordination: the best manipulation score was only 26%, and only 5 of 53 teams surpassed baselines, underscoring that the co-control problem remains largely unsolved. The physiological evaluation metrics (joint overextension torque as a pain proxy, total muscle activation as metabolic cost) introduce a dimension absent from pure robotics benchmarks, pushing the field toward biologically plausible rather than merely task-achieving controllers. Key open questions include whether the oracle-information assumption between biological and prosthetic limbs (unrealistic in practice) can be relaxed, how prosthetic gait asymmetries affect long-term tissue health, and whether the simulation's rigid-body approximation of socket interaction captures clinically relevant dynamics.
