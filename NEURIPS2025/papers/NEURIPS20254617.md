## Problem

Standard off-policy deep RL algorithms like DQN implicitly assume no unobserved confounders (NUC) between actions and subsequent outcomes, an assumption that fails when learning from a demonstrator with richer observations than the learner. Under such confounding, the true transition and reward dynamics are not identifiable from the observed data, causing standard DQN to converge to ineffective policies even with powerful neural network backbones. This paper asks how to learn a robust, effective policy from confounded off-policy data in high-dimensional (image) domains where unobserved confounders cannot be ruled out a priori.

## Input / Output

The learner receives 64×64 masked RGB game frames as state observations (with specific regions—e.g., opponent position, score, radar—hidden) and passively observes the demonstrator's action, immediate reward, and next masked state at each timestep; the demonstrator operates on the full unmasked screen, making the masked region an unobserved confounder. The output is a CNN-parameterized Q-network that maps (state, action) pairs to lower-bounded action-value estimates, from which a conservative policy is extracted via argmax. Unlike standard DQN, the Q-network is trained to approximate a pessimistic lower bound rather than the true Q-value, and the agent never actively intervenes in the environment—it only queries the demonstrator for transitions.

## Method

The core contribution is the Causal Bellman Optimality Equation (Prop. 3.1), which derives a closed-form lower bound on the optimal Q-value by decomposing the update into two cases: when the observed action matches the target action, the update follows the standard Bellman equation using the sampled next state; when it does not match, the update substitutes the minimum reward bound and the worst-case (minimum over next states) Q-value, effectively optimizing against the worst CMDP instance compatible with the observations. This bound is implemented as Causal-DQN, a Double DQN variant that uses experience replay and minibatch SGD but replaces the standard Bellman target with the causal target (Eq. 11), where the worst-case next state is estimated empirically by sampling candidate next states and selecting the one with the smallest Q-value. The algorithm is purely off-policy: at each step it queries the demonstrator for a confounded transition rather than acting in the environment, and the Q-network is trained over all actions simultaneously so that the causal update can leverage information from mismatched actions. Across 12 confounded Atari games, Causal-DQN consistently outperforms standard DQN, LSTM-DQN, and interventional DQN baselines, recovering or surpassing the full-observation demonstrator's performance despite using only masked inputs and a shallow CNN.

## Results

Causal-DQN achieves a normalized mean return of 1.04 and normalized IQM of 1.02 across 12 confounded Atari games, surpassing the full-observation demonstrator (normalized mean 1.00). In 7 out of 12 games, Causal-DQN exceeds the demonstrator's performance despite using only masked observations and a simpler CNN architecture. All three non-causal DQN baselines (Interv. DQN, Conf. DQN, Conf. LSTM-DQN) achieve normalized means of only 0.09–0.13, failing to learn effective policies under confounding. Causal-DQN converges in under 1M environment steps across all games.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Causal-DQN dominates all three DQN baselines across all 12 confounded Atari games. Compared to the Interv. DQN (CNN DQN trained directly under masked observations without demonstrator data), Causal-DQN achieves a 8x higher normalized mean (1.04 vs. 0.13); the interventional baseline barely learns within 1M steps, with the paper noting it only begins to improve after ~3M steps. Against Conf. DQN (CNN DQN fed confounded demonstrator trajectories), Causal-DQN's normalized mean is 10.4x higher (1.04 vs. 0.10), showing that naively using confounded data without causal correction actively harms learning. The Conf. LSTM-DQN baseline (normalized mean 0.09) performs worst among the three, indicating that adding temporal memory does not compensate for the absence of causal reasoning. Against the full-observation Demonstrator (Alonso et al., NeurIPS 2024, a diffusion-based actor-critic with residual blocks and LSTMs), Causal-DQN matches or exceeds its performance in 7/12 games (e.g., Gopher: 7327 vs. 5481; KungFuMaster: 44196 vs. 35400; RoadRunner: 27414 vs. 16561), while trailing in 5 games (e.g., Boxing: 71.5 vs. 89.0; MsPacman: 1748 vs. 2317). The paper attributes the demonstrator's suboptimal performance in some games to observational overfitting on spurious visual features that are masked in the confounded setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the Causal Bellman Optimality Equation from Zhang & Bareinboim (2025), which provides a closed-form lower bound on the optimal Q-value under unobserved confounding, by embedding it into a DQN training loop for high-dimensional pixel-based environments. It departs from standard off-policy learning (which assumes no unobserved confounders) and from robust RL (which requires a pre-specified uncertainty set) by constructing the ignorance region directly from confounded observational data via partial identification. The empirical evaluation builds on the Nature DQN architecture and the DIAMOND actor-critic demonstrator, extending both into a confounded off-policy learning setting that neither was designed for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that standard DQN variants (CNN, LSTM, with or without confounded demonstrations) fail catastrophically under even mild unobserved confounding, while a causally-aware Bellman update recovers or surpasses expert performance in 7/12 games using a simpler architecture and masked observations. The broader implication—that scaling models and data on confounded observations amplifies rather than corrects bias—challenges the prevailing scaling-law narrative in RL and has direct relevance to RLHF, robotic pretraining from video, and any off-policy setting where the behavioral policy differs from the learner's. Open questions include extending the causal Q-update to policy-gradient methods, continuous action spaces, and multi-agent systems, as well as understanding how the pessimistic bound behaves as the confounding structure becomes more complex.
