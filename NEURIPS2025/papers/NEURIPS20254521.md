## Problem

Existing temporal graph learning research trains and evaluates models on a single network in isolation, implicitly assuming temporal dynamics are unique and non-generalizable. This leaves within-domain and cross-domain transferability largely unaddressed, particularly in zero-shot settings where no labeled data exists for new graphs. The paper asks whether temporal graph models can learn shared temporal structures across multiple networks and generalize to entirely unseen ones.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of discrete-time graph snapshots (DTDGs) from independent temporal networks, where each snapshot contains nodes (e.g., Ethereum addresses), weighted directed edges (transactions), and node/edge features. The model produces a binary graph-level prediction of whether the number of edges will increase or decrease over a future window (δ₁=3 to δ₂=10 days), computed by an MLP decoder that mean-pools node embeddings and concatenates four graph-level snapshot features (mean in-degree, weighted in-degree, out-degree, weighted out-degree). A 70%-15%-15% temporal split is used, and the test set exhibits high structural novelty (average surprise score 0.82), making prediction genuinely challenging.

## Method

MiNT is a multi-network pre-training framework that trains any DTDG-compatible TGNN backbone (HTGN or GC-LSTM) across multiple independent temporal graphs simultaneously. Two key mechanisms ensure network-agnostic learning: (1) order shuffling randomizes the sequence of networks each epoch to preserve the IID assumption and prevent spurious order-dependent correlations, and (2) context switching resets all historical embeddings at the start of each network, preventing bias carryover between structurally distinct graphs. Model selection uses network-agnostic validation by averaging AUC across all training networks. For zero-shot inference (NTI), the frozen pre-trained model performs a single forward pass over the test network's train and validation splits to initialize historical embeddings specific to that network, then predicts on the held-out test split without any fine-tuning. The framework is architecture-agnostic and scales linearly in training time with the number of networks.

## Results

On 20 unseen Ethereum token networks, MiNT-64 (HTGN backbone) achieves the best average rank of 2.80 (lower is better) and wins top-1 AUC on 6 networks, outperforming all single-model baselines. The scaling effect is pronounced: average rank improves from 6.15 (MiNT-2) to 2.15 (MiNT-64) when comparing against single models only, with a win ratio of 0.80. In the cross-domain setting, MiNT-12 Mix (6 token + 6 social networks) achieves the best average rank of 1.91 across 20 mixed test networks, matching or exceeding standard HTGN on social datasets like MathOverflow (0.782 vs 0.788) and RedditB (0.695 vs 0.656). MiNT also achieves over 180× faster inference compared to training a single model per network.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiNT-64 (HTGN) achieves the best average rank (2.80) across all 20 test networks, beating GraphPulse (3.30), single HTGN (4.10), WinGNN (4.95), GC-LSTM (5.35), EvolveGCN (5.5), ROLAND (5.85), and Persistent Forecasting (8.35). However, GraphPulse wins on 8 individual networks (top rank) versus 6 for MiNT-64, excelling on topology-heavy datasets like BAG (0.934 vs 0.931), XCN (0.821 vs 0.851), and REPv2 (0.830 vs 0.789). MiNT-64 dominates on networks where single models struggle with variance, e.g., MIR (0.836 vs 0.750 for HTGN) and GLM (0.831 vs 0.830). Single HTGN retains an edge on SDEX (0.762 vs 0.614) and DINO (0.730 vs 0.779, though GC-LSTM single reaches 0.874). The key advantage of MiNT is consistency: it ranks in the top two on 7 of 20 networks without any per-dataset training, whereas single models require full supervised training on each network and show high variance (e.g., ROLAND ranges from 0.228 to 0.815).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant single-network paradigm in temporal graph learning, where methods like HTGN, GC-LSTM, EvolveGCN, ROLAND, and GraphPulse are each trained and evaluated on one temporal graph in isolation. It repurposes the pre-train-and-transfer paradigm well-established in NLP and CV (and recently explored for static graphs via GraphAny and AnyGraph) to the temporal graph setting, introducing MiNT as the first algorithm to train TGNNs across multiple independent dynamic graphs. The key technical departures—context switching (resetting historical embeddings between networks) and order shuffling—are lightweight modifications to standard single-network training that make the framework network-agnostic. The paper also extends the temporal graph property prediction task (introduced by GraphPulse, a prior work by the same first author) from a single-network to a multi-network zero-shot transfer setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MiNT establishes the first large-scale benchmark (92 temporal graphs) specifically designed for studying transferability and neural scaling in temporal graph learning, filling a gap left by existing benchmarks (TGB, OGB, EdgeBank) that focus on within-network tasks. The demonstrated scaling trend—performance improving roughly 50% from 2 to 64 training networks—provides empirical grounding for the emerging direction of Temporal Graph Foundation Models. Key limitations include the DTDG-only formulation (excluding continuous-time dynamics), the restriction to 64 networks due to compute constraints, and the primary focus on Ethereum transaction graphs, leaving open whether scaling continues beyond this range and whether the approach generalizes to fundamentally different temporal graph structures (e.g., biological or communication networks at scale).
