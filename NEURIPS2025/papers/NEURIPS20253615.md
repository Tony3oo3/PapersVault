## Problem

Relighting a single image or video requires both accurate scene intrinsic understanding and faithful light transport synthesis, but existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: end-to-end models are constrained by the scarcity of paired multi-illumination data, while two-stage inverse-then-forward rendering pipelines accumulate errors and fail to capture complex material effects like transparency and subsurface scattering. The paper asks whether a single generative model can jointly estimate scene intrinsics and synthesize relit output in one pass, avoiding the discrete intermediate representations that limit two-stage methods.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image or video (57 frames at 704×1280) and a target HDR environment map as input, producing a relit video and a corresponding albedo map simultaneously. Because standard VAEs cannot encode HDR intensities, the environment map is decomposed into three complementary buffers (Reinhard-tonemapped LDR panorama, normalized log-intensity, and per-pixel directional vectors) that are VAE-encoded and concatenated along the channel dimension with the relit latent. The albedo and relit latents are concatenated along the temporal (frame) dimension, effectively treating them as a single extended video clip for the diffusion model.

## Method

UniRelight fine-tunes a Cosmos-Predict1-7B DiT video diffusion model to jointly denoise the albedo and relit video latents in a single forward pass, inspired by VideoJAM's joint denoising but replacing its linear-layer latent compression with direct temporal concatenation, which the authors found yields better quality. Learnable type embeddings and binary condition masks distinguish the three modalities (input, albedo, relit) within the concatenated token sequence, enabling full self-attention across all frames for cross-modal information exchange. Training combines 108k synthetic multi-illumination videos rendered with an OptiX path tracer, the MIT multi-illumination dataset (with pseudo-albedo from a separate inverse renderer), and 150k auto-labeled real-world video-albedo pairs, using a multi-conditioning strategy where the input video is randomly dropped (12%), both input and GT albedo are provided (18%), or only the input video conditions the model (70%). This hybrid data strategy and joint formulation allow the model to implicitly reason about scene structure rather than relying on explicit G-buffers, improving generalization to unseen domains and complex materials.

## Results

On the SyntheticScenes dataset, UniRelight achieves PSNR 26.97, SSIM 0.847, and LPIPS 0.190, outperforming the strongest baseline DiffusionRenderer (Cosmos) (PSNR 26.61, SSIM 0.841, LPIPS 0.222). On the more challenging MIT multi-illumination benchmark, UniRelight achieves PSNR 20.76, SSIM 0.749, and LPIPS 0.251, substantially surpassing the best baseline (DiffusionRenderer Cosmos at PSNR 17.29, SSIM 0.622, LPIPS 0.355). In a user study on MIT multi-illumination, UniRelight is preferred over all baselines 84–96% of the time. For albedo estimation on SyntheticScenes, UniRelight (PSNR 28.07) is slightly behind DiffusionRenderer (Cosmos) (PSNR 28.56) but well ahead of IntrinsicImageDiffusion (PSNR 16.41).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniRelight outperforms all five baselines on both relighting datasets across all metrics. Against DiLightNet [68] and Neural Gaffer [26], the gains are large (e.g., +11.2 dB PSNR over DiLightNet on MIT), with the user study confirming strong perceptual superiority (92% and 84% preference, respectively). Against the original DiffusionRenderer [39], UniRelight improves PSNR by 2.8 dB on SyntheticScenes and 3.8 dB on MIT, with the gap widening on MIT due to DiffusionRenderer's inability to handle complex materials (glass, anisotropic surfaces) via explicit G-buffers. The strongest comparison is against the re-implemented DiffusionRenderer (Cosmos) [39], which narrows the gap on SyntheticScenes (PSNR 26.97 vs. 26.61) but still trails by 3.5 dB on MIT multi-illumination, where complex real-world materials expose the limitations of the two-stage inverse-then-forward pipeline. For albedo estimation, UniRelight is slightly behind DiffusionRenderer (Cosmos) (PSNR 28.07 vs. 28.56) but substantially ahead of IntrinsicImageDiffusion [32] (PSNR 16.41). Against IC-Light [73] on object-centric synthetic data, UniRelight achieves PSNR 23.19 vs. 18.08, though the two methods use different conditioning formulations (full HDR environment map vs. background context only).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the joint latent denoising paradigm from VideoJAM (which jointly predicts appearance and motion) to the relighting domain, concatenating albedo and relit-video latents along the temporal axis for single-pass DiT denoising. It departs from the dominant two-stage inverse-then-forward rendering pipeline (DiffusionRenderer, RGB↔X) by eliminating explicit G-buffer intermediates in favor of implicit scene understanding through cross-modal self-attention. The backbone is the Cosmos-Predict1 DiT video model, and the data strategy extends DiffusionRenderer's synthetic rendering pipeline with a novel auto-labeling approach for real-world videos using a separately trained inverse renderer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint formulation addresses a core practical limitation of two-stage relighting pipelines—error accumulation and inability to model complex materials (transparency, subsurface scattering, anisotropy) that G-buffers cannot encode—while the auto-labeling strategy for real-world data mitigates the chronic scarcity of multi-illumination supervision. Open questions remain around emissive objects, text-conditioned relighting, and whether incorporating explicit 3D geometry (e.g., traced shadow buffers) could further improve physical accuracy. The data augmentation application for driving/robotics scenes suggests broader utility beyond creative editing.
