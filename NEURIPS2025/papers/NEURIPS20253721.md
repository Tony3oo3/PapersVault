## Problem

Molecular structure elucidation from mass spectrometry is bottlenecked by the scarcity of annotated experimental spectra and the intrinsic heterogeneity of raw spectral signals, which vary substantially with collision energy, adduct type, and instrument settings. Existing de novo methods treat spectra and molecular structures as separate modalities, leading to semantic mismatches and molecular hallucinations where generated structures are chemically valid but deviate from the true molecule. Adapting the large-scale pretraining paradigm from NLP to mass spectrometry has been hindered by the lack of a reliable, condition-invariant spectral representation suitable for building large pretraining corpora.

## Input / Output

The model takes tandem mass spectra (variable-length m/z–intensity peak lists) as input, which are first converted into 4096-bit Morgan fingerprints by the pretrained MIST model conditioned on the molecular formula, then tokenized into <fpXXXX> tokens. Output is a ranked list of candidate molecular structures in SELFIES format (185-token vocabulary guaranteeing chemical validity); at inference, 100 candidates are sampled via beam-search multinomial decoding and re-ranked by formula distance followed by log-probability to yield top-k predictions. The chemical formula is used only for re-ranking, not as a direct model input during training.

## Method

MS-BART adopts a BART-BASE backbone with a unified token vocabulary that interleaves SELFIES tokens and fingerprint tokens separated by a special <fps_sep> token, enabling cross-modal sequence-to-sequence generation. Training follows a three-stage pretraining–finetuning–alignment paradigm: (1) multi-task pretraining on 4M RDKit-computed fingerprint–molecule pairs using SELFIES denoising, fingerprint-to-molecule translation, and hybrid denoising objectives; (2) finetuning on experimental spectra where MIST-predicted fingerprints serve as input, bridging the distributional gap between computed and real-world fingerprints; (3) contrastive alignment via a chemical-feedback rank loss that penalizes the model for assigning higher log-probability to candidates with lower Tanimoto similarity to the ground truth, thereby mitigating molecular hallucination. The key architectural insight is that molecular fingerprints provide a condition-invariant, reliably computable intermediate representation that makes large-scale pretraining feasible without simulating spectra under diverse experimental settings.

## Results

On NPLIB1, MS-BART achieves Top-1 Tanimoto similarity of 0.44 and Top-10 Tanimoto of 0.51, surpassing the second-best method (DiffMS) by 25.7% and 8.5% respectively, while also achieving the best Top-1 MCES (9.66) and Top-10 MCES (8.31). On MassSpecGym, MS-BART achieves Top-1 MCES of 16.47 (best among all methods) but trails DiffMS on accuracy (1.07% vs 2.30% Top-1) and Tanimoto similarity. With gold fingerprints, MS-BART reaches 73.50% Top-1 accuracy on NPLIB1 and 47.56% on MassSpecGym, demonstrating the model's upper bound. Inference is ~53× faster than DiffMS (~3 s vs ~160 s per spectrum on an RTX 4090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MS-BART outperforms all fingerprint-based and retrieval-based baselines (Spec2Mol, MIST+NeuralDecipher, MIST+MSNovelist, MADGEN, and the MassSpecGym Transformer baselines) on every metric across both datasets, with the largest gaps on MassSpecGym where most baselines achieve near-zero accuracy. Against the strongest competitor, DiffMS, the picture is mixed: on NPLIB1, MS-BART wins on all four similarity metrics (Top-1 MCES 9.66 vs 11.95; Top-1 Tanimoto 0.44 vs 0.35; Top-10 MCES 8.31 vs 9.23; Top-10 Tanimoto 0.51 vs 0.47) but trails on exact-match accuracy (7.45% vs 8.34% Top-1; 10.99% vs 15.44% Top-10), which the authors attribute to stricter pretraining data filtering. On MassSpecGym, DiffMS leads on accuracy and Tanimoto similarity, while MS-BART retains the best Top-1 MCES (16.47 vs 18.45). Overall, MS-BART achieves SOTA on 5 of 12 key metrics (all four NPLIB1 similarity metrics plus MassSpecGym Top-1 MCES) and is ~53× faster at inference than DiffMS.

## Relation to Prior Work

MS-BART extends the fingerprint-based structure elucidation line (CSI:FingerID, MSNovelist) by reframing the problem as a unified language modeling task: both spectral fingerprints and SELFIES molecular strings are tokenized into a shared vocabulary and processed by a BART denoising seq2seq backbone. The pretraining-finetuning-alignment paradigm is borrowed directly from NLP, with the alignment step adapting the contrastive rank loss from Fang et al. (ICLR 2024) to mitigate molecular hallucination. The key departure from prior de novo methods (Spec2Mol, MADGEN, DiffMS) is the use of large-scale pretraining on 4M RDKit-computed fingerprint-molecule pairs to bridge the data scarcity gap, rather than training end-to-end on limited experimental spectra.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 53× speed advantage over diffusion-based DiffMS makes MS-BART practically deployable for high-throughput metabolomics workflows, and the unified vocabulary design offers a clean template for extending to other spectral modalities. However, the stark gap between MIST-predicted fingerprints (7.45% Top-1 on NPLIB1) and gold fingerprints (73.5%) reveals that the generation model is far from the bottleneck — the fingerprint prediction step is. Open questions include whether end-to-end joint training of spectrum-to-fingerprint and fingerprint-to-molecule can close this gap, how to incorporate chemical formula information during training (currently used only for re-ranking), and whether the approach generalizes to [M+Na]+ adducts and other ionization modes that were excluded during training.
