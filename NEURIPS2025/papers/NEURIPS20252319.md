## Problem

Existing evaluations of LLMs on table tasks are narrowly focused on NL-to-SQL and Table-QA, overlooking the broader spectrum of expert-level tasks that professional data engineers, analysts, and database administrators face daily in spreadsheets, databases, and computational notebooks. This gap limits understanding of model capabilities in structured data processing and hinders progress toward general-purpose table reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252319/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question follows a standardized triplet format of (Instruction, Input-table(s), Ground-truth answer), where tables are serialized in markdown, CSV, JSON, or HTML. Tables average 2,659 rows and 11 columns (up to 126K tokens in the longest quartile), and questions may involve 1 to 3+ tables. Outputs are open-ended and task-specific—SQL queries, Pandas transformation programs, spreadsheet formulas, entity/schema match labels, predicted cell values, or fact-verification verdicts—evaluated via a lightweight framework supporting both execution-based and structured-output comparison.

## Method

MMTU is constructed through a systematic curation pipeline: a literature survey across two decades of data management (SIGMOD/VLDB), programming languages (PLDI/POPL), and web data (WWW/WSDM) publications yields 25 user-facing tasks from 52 expert-labeled datasets, filtered for objective evaluability and real-world data. Questions are standardized into a uniform triplet format, quality-checked with an LLM (removing ~8% ambiguous or incorrect items), and verified by domain experts. The evaluation framework supports diverse metrics including execution-based evaluation for code generation and structured comparison for matching tasks. The paper benchmarks 26 frontier models (reasoning and chat) and performs detailed sensitivity analyses on long table context, row/column permutation robustness, and format variation, alongside a manual error taxonomy covering table understanding (38%), reasoning/coding (28%), knowledge (18%), and other (15%) failure modes.

## Results

On MMTU (28,136 questions across 25 table tasks), the best-performing model is OpenAI GPT-5 (reasoning) at 69.6% accuracy, followed closely by o3 at 69.1% and Gemini-2.5-pro at 66.5%. Among open-source reasoning models, DeepSeek-R1 achieves the highest score at 57.9%, while the best chat-oriented model (GPT-5-Chat) reaches 57.7%. Reasoning models outperform chat models by over 10 percentage points on average, and even the top model leaves ~30% of questions unanswered, indicating substantial room for improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, MMTU evaluates 26 frontier models rather than proposing a new method. The key comparison is between reasoning and chat model families: GPT-5 (69.6%) and DeepSeek-R1 (57.9%) lead among reasoning models, while GPT-5-Chat (57.7%) and DeepSeek-V3 (55.5%) lead among chat models, with reasoning models winning by 11.9 and 2.4 percentage points respectively. DeepSeek-R1, the strongest open-source reasoning model, trails GPT-5 by 11.7 points, revealing a notable proprietary-vs-open-source gap. Llama-4-Maverick (49.0%) and Llama-4-Scout (39.3%) fall well behind both top reasoning and chat models, indicating that open-weight models still lag on complex table tasks. Cost-efficiency analysis shows that lightweight reasoning models like GPT-5-mini (66.7% at $0.0028/question) and Gemini-2.5-Flash (62.5% at $0.0020/question) offer competitive quality at a fraction of GPT-5's cost ($0.0173/question). The paper's detailed error analysis focuses on four models (GPT-5, DeepSeek-R1, GPT-5-Chat, DeepSeek-V3), finding that table understanding errors (38%) and reasoning/coding errors (28%) dominate, with long-context tables and column-direction reasoning being the hardest sub-problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMTU carves new ground by unifying 25 table tasks drawn from decades of data management (SIGMOD/VLDB), programming languages (PLDI/POPL), and web data (WWW/WSDM) research into a single LLM evaluation framework. It departs from prior table benchmarks that narrowly focused on NL-to-SQL and Table-QA, and from spreadsheet-centric benchmarks (Spreadsheet-Bench, SheetCopilotBench) that are tied to specific file formats and limited in scale. Conceptually, it positions table understanding as a first-class evaluation domain comparable in scope to MMLU or MMMU, repurposing expert-labeled datasets from the data management community that had never been used to evaluate foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With even GPT-5 scoring only 69.6%, MMTU establishes a clear ceiling that motivates continued research on table reasoning, coding, and long-context understanding for structured data. The finding that models remain sensitive to semantically-invariant table permutations (especially column shuffling) and that table-based needle-in-a-haystack performance drops sharply relative to standard text NIH reveals fundamental limitations in how LLMs process 2D tabular structure. Key open questions include whether multi-modal (visual) table input can overcome text-serialization bottlenecks, whether subjective table tasks (summarization, enrichment) can be reliably evaluated, and whether the literature-sampling bias in task selection omits important real-world scenarios like multi-turn table manipulation.
