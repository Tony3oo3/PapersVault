## Problem

Existing 2D adversarial defenses against diffusion-based image editing fail when transferred to 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes for two reasons: their pixel-level perturbations do not encode 3D spatial correlations and thus degrade across viewpoints, and they are easily erased during the iterative multi-round optimization inherent to 3D editing. No dedicated defense framework for malicious 3D editing had been proposed prior to this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253912/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3DGS scene parameterized by Gaussian attributes (position μ, covariance Σ, opacity α, color c) and a library of 415 text prompts (from DreamFusion) to introduce semantic diversity during training. It outputs a perturbed 3DGS scene where a bounded perturbation Δ = {Δ_μ, Δ_ε, Δ_α, Δ_c} is added to the original parameters, constrained by ‖Δ‖ < ξ. The perturbation must remain visually imperceptible (high PSNR relative to the original) while disrupting editing across all sampled viewpoints.

## Method

DEGauss optimizes 3D spatial perturbations through two collaborative mechanisms. First, a View-Focal Gradient Fusion (VFGF) strategy performs farthest-point sampling in camera translation-rotation space to select N=6 widely distributed viewpoints, then weights each view's gradient by a normalized power of its total loss (with exponent γ and stabilization τ), biasing updates toward the most vulnerable views. Second, a Dual Discrepancy Optimization strategy jointly maximizes a Feature Discrepancy Loss (L2 distance in the InstructPix2Pix encoder's latent space between perturbed and original rendered views) and a Guidance Discrepancy Loss (L2 distance between the diffusion model's noise predictions for the perturbed scene versus the original scene under the same text prompt and timestep). The total loss combines a rendering fidelity term with weighted FD and GD terms (λ_FD = λ_GD = 1e-5), optimized over 2000 steps on a single RTX 4090, producing perturbations that stably disrupt editing trajectories throughout iterative 3D editing while preserving photorealistic rendering.

## Results

On the four evaluated 3DGS scenes (face, girl, bear, bicycle), DEGauss achieves the highest PSNR (33.92, 35.59, 32.20, 32.84 dB respectively), indicating the most imperceptible perturbations among all methods. Simultaneously, DEGauss attains the lowest CLIP similarity (0.886, 0.869, 0.895, 0.936), CLIP-T (0.219, 0.238, 0.294, 0.234), and CLIP-D (0.033, 0.045, 0.026, 0.054) scores, demonstrating the strongest disruption of malicious editing trajectories. The method is evaluated using GaussianEditor as the editing pipeline with 6 sampled views and 2,000 training steps on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEGauss outperforms all four 2D-defense baselines (AdvDM, Mist, SDS, AdvPaint) on every metric across all four scenes. AdvDM and Mist, which optimize noise-prediction loss in 2D space, show the weakest defense with CLIP scores of 0.87–0.97 and CLIP-D of 0.04–0.11, indicating that editing largely succeeds despite the perturbation. SDS (score distillation) provides the least suppression overall, with CLIP-D reaching 0.107 on the girl scene. AdvPaint is the closest competitor, leveraging attention disruption to achieve CLIP-D of 0.031–0.058, yet DEGauss still leads by 0.003–0.004 on CLIP-D and by 1–2 dB on PSNR, meaning DEGauss disrupts editing more effectively while keeping perturbations more imperceptible. The generalization experiments (Table 2) further confirm that DEGauss maintains its advantage across GaussianEditor, DGE, DreamCatalyst, and EditSplat, with CLIP similarity dropping to 0.82–0.96 depending on the editor and scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEGauss extends the line of adversarial defense against diffusion-based image editing (AdvDM, Mist, SDS, AdvPaint) from the 2D single-image setting to the 3D Gaussian Splatting domain. The key conceptual departure is that 2D pixel-level perturbations cannot capture 3D spatial correlations across viewpoints and are erased during iterative 3D optimization, so DEGauss instead optimizes perturbations directly on the 3D Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, color) and introduces a view-focal gradient fusion mechanism to handle multi-view consistency. The dual-discrepancy loss (feature + guidance) draws on the noise-prediction objective of AdvDM but reformulates it in latent space for more stable gradients, while the wide-view sampling strategy is a novel geometric component with no direct 2D analogue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3D editing tools (GaussianEditor, DGE, DreamCatalyst, EditSplat) become increasingly accessible, the risk of unauthorized modification of 3D digital assets and personal portraits grows, making this the first systematic defense framework for that threat. However, the defense is per-scene (perturbations must be optimized for each specific 3DGS model), limiting its practicality for large-scale asset protection, and it has only been validated on a handful of simple scenes with a single diffusion backbone (InstructPix2Pix). Open questions include whether the defense generalizes to more complex scenes, whether more sophisticated editors could learn to bypass the perturbation, and whether a universal (scene-agnostic) 3D defense is feasible.
