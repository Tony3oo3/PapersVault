## Problem

Existing hyperparameter selection methods with formal reliability guarantees (LTT, PT) either ignore structural relationships among candidate hyperparameters or restrict them to a single linear ordering, while many practical settings—such as prompt templates of varying lengths—exhibit DAG-structured reliability hierarchies. This paper asks whether such structured knowledge can be incorporated into FDR-controlling multiple hypothesis testing to enable more efficient exploration of the hyperparameter space without sacrificing statistical validity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251324/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a discrete candidate hyperparameter set Λ (e.g., 100 prompt templates or 32 grid points), a calibration dataset Z that must be split into Z_OPT (for Pareto estimation and graph learning) and Z_MHT (for hypothesis testing), optional prior pairwise reliability preferences η_ij (e.g., from an LLM-as-a-judge), and user-specified FDR level δ and reliability thresholds α_l. It outputs a subset Λ̂_Z of hyperparameters whose average unreliability fraction is bounded by δ, from which a single configuration is selected by minimizing auxiliary risk estimates (e.g., prompt length, negative ROUGE-L). The data split is essential: p-values for testing are computed on Z_MHT, while all graph-construction statistics use Z_OPT, ensuring valid finite-sample guarantees.

## Method

RG-PT constructs a directed acyclic graph (the Reliability Graph) over the Pareto-optimal subset of hyperparameters, where edges encode expected reliability dominance. Depth assignment is performed by scoring each hyperparameter via the Bradley-Terry model—combining data-driven pairwise p-value comparisons with optional prior preferences through a pseudocount mechanism—and then clustering scores into D levels via agglomerative hierarchical clustering. Directed edges between consecutive levels are learned by solving a non-negative Lasso regression that predicts each child's risk vector from its potential parents' risk vectors, ensuring only positive (monotonic) reliability influences are retained. FDR-controlling testing is then executed by DAGGER, which traverses the DAG level-by-level, adaptively adjusting per-node thresholds based on the subgraph structure and prior rejection counts, and pruning entire subtrees when a parent is deemed unreliable. The key distinction from PT is that the DAG allows multiple hyperparameters at the same depth to be tested in parallel, increasing statistical power while preserving the FDR guarantee regardless of the graph's correctness.

## Results

Across five tasks (prompt engineering, object detection, language translation, image classification, and radio access scheduling), RG-PT achieves FDR values of 0.084–0.095, all below the target δ=0.1, while LTT and PT achieve 0.071–0.097. In prompt engineering, RG-PT consistently identifies shorter reliable prompt templates than both LTT and PT (Fig. 1b, 3a, 3b), with average FDR of 0.089, 0.095, and 0.092 on sentiment analysis, STS, and WIC respectively. In sequence-to-sequence translation, RG-PT maintains higher ROUGE-L scores than LTT and PT across all BLEU reliability thresholds (Fig. 4). Bayesian optimization and Thompson Sampling baselines violate the FDR target, achieving 0.14 and 0.21 respectively, while all three MHT-based methods (RG-PT, LTT, PT) satisfy the constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RG-PT outperforms both LTT [4] and PT [5] on the primary optimization objective: in prompt engineering, RG-PT selects significantly shorter reliable prompts (lower average prompt length in the boxplots of Fig. 1b, 3a, 3b), and in translation it achieves higher ROUGE-L scores at every BLEU threshold (Fig. 4). On FDR control, all three MHT-based methods satisfy the δ=0.1 target, though LTT and PT tend to be slightly more conservative (lower FDR), e.g., 0.071 vs. 0.084 on image classification. The key advantage of RG-PT is that it achieves this with a more efficient exploration of the hyperparameter space by exploiting the DAG structure, rather than a linear ordering (PT) or no structure (LTT). Against non-MHT baselines, Bayesian optimization (UCB) and Thompson Sampling (MAB) both violate the FDR constraint (0.14 and 0.21 vs. target 0.1), confirming that only the MHT-based approaches provide the required statistical guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learn-Then-Test (LTT) framework for FDR-controlled hyperparameter selection and its multi-objective extension, Pareto Testing (PT), by replacing the linear testing order with a directed acyclic graph (DAG) structure. The DAG is constructed using the Bradley-Terry ranking model (to assign depth levels incorporating prior information) and non-negative Lasso (to learn parent-child edges), and the FDR-controlling test is executed via the DAGGER algorithm from the graphical multiple-testing literature. The key conceptual departure from PT is the move from a total ordering to a partial ordering, allowing multiple hyperparameters at the same reliability level to be tested in parallel rather than sequentially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RG-PT offers a principled way to incorporate structured domain knowledge (e.g., longer prompts are more reliable) into hyperparameter selection while preserving formal FDR guarantees, which is valuable for safety-critical LLM deployment and multi-objective ML pipelines. The paper's limitations are notable: it applies only to discrete hyperparameter spaces, provides no theoretical results on statistical power or sample efficiency, and the practical gains over PT are modest (shorter prompts, slightly higher ROUGE-L). Open questions include whether the RG structure can be optimized to maximize power, whether synthetic data can substitute for held-out data in RG construction, and how the framework integrates with sequential testing via e-processes.
