## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) like LLaDa and Dream achieve competitive language generation quality but suffer from a critical efficiency bottleneck: existing samplers unmask only one token per function evaluation, or unmask a fixed number (Top-k) at the cost of significant quality degradation from joint dependence error. No prior sampler could adaptively decide both which and how many tokens to unmask per step while preserving performance, leaving the parallelism advantage of MDMs largely unrealized.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained MDM (LLaDa 8B with max sequence length 4096, or Dream 7B with 2048) given a text prompt padded with mask tokens to the model's maximum length. Generation is restricted to the index range [len(prompt), len(prompt)+max_gen_len], and a generate_until stopping criterion (e.g., detecting a '[DONE]' phrase with all preceding tokens unmasked) terminates sampling early. The output is a fully unmasked token sequence evaluated on code generation (HumanEval, MBPP), math reasoning (GSM8K, MATH), and logic puzzles (10×10 mazes, 9×9 Sudoku).

## Method

EB-Sampler is a drop-in adaptive sampler that, at each step, sorts all masked tokens by a model-error proxy (confidence, entropy, or margin) and then greedily selects the largest prefix of this sorted list whose accumulated prediction entropies (minus the maximum) stay below a user-defined threshold γ. The key insight is that tokens predicted with both low model error and low entropy have bounded joint mutual information in the data distribution, so they can be safely unmasked in parallel without incurring substantial joint dependence error. The paper formalizes this within a general family of adaptive multi-token samplers parameterized by a partitioning function φ, and proves a KL-divergence error decomposition into model error and joint dependence error, showing that the entropy bound in Equation (2) approximates an upper bound on the joint dependence term. This yields 2–3× speedups over Top-1 sampling on standard benchmarks with no performance loss, and the approach extends to smaller reasoning tasks like maze navigation and Sudoku where ARMs typically struggle.

## Results

EB-Sampler achieves 2–3× speedups over Top-1 sampling on code and math reasoning benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K, MATH) with LLaDa 8B and Dream 7B, while maintaining equivalent pass@1 accuracy. On MBPP with Dream 7B, EB-Sampler (entropy, γ=0.1) requires only 25.49 NFE under generate_until logic versus 101.71 for Top-1 (≈4× speedup) at comparable accuracy (58% vs. 58.8%), and 21.19 NFE under semi-AR block generation versus 64.59 (≈3× speedup). On logic puzzles (10×10 mazes, 9×9 Sudoku), EB-Sampler retains near-full accuracy down to ~10–15 NFEs, whereas Top-k baselines degrade sharply already at ~10 NFEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares EB-Sampler against Top-k samplers (k ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}) using three error proxies (confidence, entropy, margin) as baselines, evaluated on LLaDa 8B and Dream 7B. EB-Sampler consistently dominates the accuracy–NFE Pareto frontier across all four benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K, MATH) and both models, achieving 2–4× fewer function evaluations at matched accuracy compared to Top-1. The margin error proxy generally yields inferior accuracy and is relegated to the appendix; confidence works best for LLaDa 8B while entropy works best for Dream 7B. On the smaller logic-puzzle tasks (maze navigation, Sudoku), EB-Sampler degrades more gracefully than Top-k as NFE decreases, retaining most accuracy down to ~10–15 NFEs while Top-k baselines show a steeper decline starting around 10 NFEs. A comparison to naive per-token thresholding (Appendix D.3) shows EB-Sampler's accumulated entropy bound provides a meaningful advantage on GSM8K, though the gap narrows on MBPP where predictions are uniformly high-confidence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the greedy ordered-unmasking samplers introduced for MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022) and adopted by LLaDa and Dream, which select a fixed number k of lowest-error-proxy tokens per step. The key departure is replacing this fixed-k schedule with an adaptive, entropy-bounded criterion that dynamically determines how many tokens to unmask based on their cumulative prediction entropy, grounded in a KL-divergence error decomposition into model error and joint dependence error. The approach is complementary to speculative decoding and to remasking/predictor-corrector methods, and borrows the generate_until stopping logic from autoregressive generation practices to make MDM efficiency measurement more meaningful.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–3× speedup without performance loss is practically significant for MDM deployment, and the drop-in simplicity lowers adoption barriers. A notable practical finding is that standard MDM evaluation overestimates efficiency gains because models waste function evaluations on tokens after the stopping phrase; the generate_until and semi-AR analyses reveal this bias and suggest MDM evaluation protocols need rethinking. Open questions include whether the adaptive sampler can be learned end-to-end, whether it composes with remasking or predictor-corrector iterations, and whether the time-independent analysis extends to time-dependent discrete diffusion models.
