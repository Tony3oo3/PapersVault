## Problem

Transferring appearance (texture and fine geometric detail) to a 3D asset fails when the input and appearance objects have significantly different geometries. Multi-view 2D-diffusion pipelines produce geometrically inconsistent results, and direct application of 3D generative models yields poor generalization and limited control under large shape discrepancies. No existing method robustly disentangles style from structure to transfer appearance across substantially dissimilar 3D shapes while preserving the input's global geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252154/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an input 3D mesh (whose voxel positions are fixed to enforce global geometric consistency) and an appearance object in one of three forms: an image + textured mesh pair, a text prompt, or a standalone image (from which a mesh can be generated via Trellis). The output is a textured 3D mesh decoded from structured latents (SLAT) that carries the appearance object's texture and fine geometric details while retaining the input mesh's coarse shape. Only the latent codes z_i are optimized during generation; the active voxel positions p_i remain fixed throughout.

## Method

GuideFlow3D is a training-free framework that steers a pretrained rectified flow model (Trellis) at inference time by interleaving flow sampling steps with latent-space optimization guided by differentiable losses. The core update rule combines the learned flow vector field v_θ(z,t|c) with the gradient of a guidance objective ∇_z L(z|c), extending universal guidance to rectified flow. Two guidance objectives are proposed: (1) a part-aware appearance loss that co-segments input and appearance shapes using PartField features and k-means clustering to establish approximate part correspondences, then minimizes L2 distance between matched structured latents; and (2) a self-similarity contrastive loss that encourages intra-part feature consistency and inter-part separability when no appearance mesh is available. The rectified flow model enforces the generative prior (realism and distributional validity), while the guidance losses inject appearance or structure constraints the base model was not trained for, enabling flexible conditioning on image, text, or mesh inputs without retraining.

## Results

Using GPT-5-based pairwise ranking metrics (lower is better) on the simple-complex intra-category set, GuideFlow3D achieves the best Overall ranking of 2.12 under image-mesh conditioning (L_appearance), outperforming Trellis (2.62), EasiTex (3.81), Cross-Image Attention (3.59), and MambaST (4.87). Under text conditioning (L_structure), GuideFlow3D achieves an Overall ranking of 1.95 versus Trellis at 2.39 and SDXL+Cross-Image Attention at 2.98. The advantage is consistent across all six criteria (Style Fidelity, Structure Clarity, Style Integration, Detail Quality, Shape Adaptation, Overall Quality) and extends to inter-category and complex-complex settings (Table 2), where GuideFlow3D maintains Overall rankings of 2.45 (simple-complex inter-category) and 2.56 (complex-complex inter-category) with image conditioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GuideFlow3D consistently outperforms all four baselines across every criterion and both conditioning modalities. Against Trellis [72] (the unguided rectified flow baseline), GuideFlow3D improves Overall ranking from 2.62 to 2.12 (image) and 2.39 to 1.95 (text), with the largest gains in Style Fidelity (2.51→1.89) and Style Integration (2.53→2.07), confirming that the guidance losses are the primary source of improvement. EasiTex [52] performs competitively on Structure Clarity (4.30) but lags significantly on Fidelity (3.18) and Overall (3.81), struggling under large geometric deviations due to its ControlNet-based edge/depth conditioning. Cross-Image Attention [2] achieves the best Clarity among 2D-style baselines (3.48) but fails to maintain consistent 3D surface mapping, yielding Overall 3.59. MambaST [8] produces globally smooth textures but blends input and appearance colors (residual gray tones), resulting in the worst Overall ranking (4.87) despite reasonable Clarity (3.55). In the inter-category setting (Table 2), GuideFlow3D's advantage over Trellis narrows slightly (2.45 vs. 2.76 Overall for simple-complex inter-category) but remains clear, while 2D-based baselines degrade more sharply under large shape discrepancies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal guidance framework [6]—originally formulated for 2D diffusion models—to the rectified flow setting and applies it to 3D structured latents from Trellis [72], enabling training-free conditioning on objectives the base model was never trained for. It departs from prior 3D style transfer methods (StyleRF, StyleGaussian, Mesh2Tex, EASI-Tex) that operate on implicit representations or require task-specific training, by working directly in a structured latent space with explicit mesh output. The part-aware appearance loss draws on PartField [41] for geometric co-segmentation, while the self-similarity loss connects to classical structure-based descriptors [59, 34], bridging geometric and perceptual style transfer under a single guided-sampling framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, optimization-guided approach offers a practical path to 3D appearance transfer that handles large geometric discrepancies between input and appearance objects—a failure mode of direct generative application—while supporting flexible conditioning (mesh, image, or text). Key limitations include the 96-second per-sample runtime (not real-time), dependence on Trellis and PartField quality, and the assumption of noiseless input meshes. Open questions include whether learned feed-forward guidance can replace iterative optimization, how to extend the framework to other flow-based 3D generators, and whether the GPT-based evaluation protocol generalizes beyond the furniture-centric benchmark.
