## Problem

Existing CZSL methods freeze model parameters and class prototypes after training, causing performance degradation when the test-time label distribution includes unseen attribute-object compositions not encountered during training. This label space distribution shift has not been effectively addressed because prior approaches cannot leverage the unlabeled test data to adapt the model toward the new composition distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253165/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (processed by CLIP ViT-L/14) as the primary input, augmented by a dynamic priority queue of up to K=3 high-confidence historical test images per composition class that provides visual knowledge. At test time, each image is consumed exactly once in an online streaming fashion; the KAM parameters are updated via backpropagation only after the prediction is emitted to minimize per-sample latency. The output is a composition label (attribute-object pair) with a full probability distribution over all feasible compositions, alongside continuously updated textual and visual prototype matrices.

## Method

TOMCAT introduces Knowledge Accumulation Modules (KAMs)—per-class learnable offset matrices initialized to zero—that progressively adjust both textual and visual prototypes at test time without modifying the frozen base model. A dynamic priority queue stores the K=3 lowest-entropy (highest-confidence) images per class to construct visual prototypes, complementing the text-encoder-derived textual prototypes. An adaptive update weight, computed as a sigmoid of the negative cosine similarity between the incoming image feature and each prototype, controls how strongly the KAM shifts each prototype: images closely matching a prototype receive minimal adjustment, while dissimilar images (likely unseen compositions) trigger stronger updates. The KAMs are optimized by minimizing a combined loss of multimodal prediction entropy and a contrastive multimodal collaborative representation learning term that aligns textual and visual prototypes of the same composition while pushing apart non-corresponding ones.

## Results

In the closed-world setting, TOMCAT achieves state-of-the-art AUC and HM on UT-Zappos (48.3 / 60.2), C-GQA (16.0 / 34.0), and Clothing16K (99.5 / 98.4), while ranking second on MIT-States (AUC 22.6 / HM 39.5) behind ClusPro. In the open-world setting, TOMCAT leads on UT-Zappos (AUC 43.7 / HM 57.9), C-GQA (AUC 4.2 / HM 14.2), and Clothing16K (AUC 95.8 / HM 95.0), with margins of 4.2% and 4.0% AUC over the second-best on UT-Zappos and C-GQA respectively. The method also outperforms OTTA baselines (online-TPS, TDA, DPE) by up to 3.7% AUC under fair test-time adaptation conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOMCAT outperforms the strongest prior CZSL method ClusPro (ICLR'25) on UT-Zappos closed-world by 1.7% AUC (48.3 vs. 46.6) and 1.7% HM (60.2 vs. 58.5), and on C-GQA by 1.1% AUC (16.0 vs. 14.9) and 1.2% HM (34.0 vs. 32.8). However, TOMCAT trails ClusPro on MIT-States (AUC 22.6 vs. 23.8, HM 39.5 vs. 40.7), which the authors attribute to ~70% label noise in that dataset. Against other recent CLIP-based CZSL methods (Troika, CDS-CZSL, IMAX, PLID), TOMCAT leads by 3.5–6.6% AUC on UT-Zappos and 3.2–4.9% AUC on C-GQA. In the open-world setting, the advantage widens: TOMCAT beats ClusPro by 4.2% AUC on UT-Zappos (43.7 vs. 39.5) and 1.2% AUC on C-GQA (4.2 vs. 3.0). Under fair OTTA comparison (Table 9), TOMCAT surpasses online-TPS, TDA, and DPE by 3.7%, 6.7%, and 5.0% AUC on UT-Zappos respectively, confirming that the gains are not merely from test-time adaptation but from the specific multimodal knowledge accumulation design.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOMCAT sits at the intersection of two research threads: CLIP-based CZSL methods (CoOp, Troika, CDS-CZSL) that fine-tune vision-language models with prompt and adapter tuning, and online test-time adaptation (OTTA) methods (Tent, TPT, TDA, DPE, TPS) that exploit unlabeled test data to counter distribution shift. The key departure is that prior OTTA work targets feature-domain shift, whereas TOMCAT addresses the label-space shift unique to CZSL—where unseen compositions are recombined from seen primitives and were entirely absent during training. The method repurposes entropy minimization and prototype updating from OTTA, but introduces CZSL-specific mechanisms: a dynamic priority queue for visual prototypes, adaptive update weights that modulate adjustment based on seen/unseen familiarity, and multimodal collaborative representation learning to align text and visual prototypes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that test-time unsupervised data can meaningfully improve CZSL, opening a practical direction for deployed compositional recognition systems that encounter new attribute-object combinations post-deployment. The notably larger gains in the open-world setting (up to 4.2% AUC) suggest the approach is most valuable when the label space is genuinely open, which is the more realistic scenario. Two acknowledged limitations remain open: knowledge is accumulated only at the composition level rather than exploiting primitive-level structure, and the priority queue's cold-start bias toward early-encountered compositions could be addressed with better initialization strategies. The method's efficiency (only lightweight KAM parameters updated, ~10ms additional latency) makes it practical for interactive applications.
