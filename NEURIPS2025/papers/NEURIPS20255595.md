## Problem

Existing anti-mimicry defenses (Glaze, AntiDreamBooth, MetaCloak, SimAC) are vulnerable to diffusion-based purification attacks such as DiffPure and Noise Upscaling, which strip protective perturbations before fine-tuning. Additionally, these methods exhibit limited cross-model transferability, making them ineffective when the attacker's text-to-image model differs from the one used during defense training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255595/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of clean artist images (e.g., 20 paintings per artist from WikiArt or 10 face images per identity from CelebA) along with target images of a deliberately different style, and produces perturbed protected images under an L∞ budget of 8/255. During training, the method consumes an ensemble of image encoders (VAE, OpenCLIP-ViT-H-14, OpenCLIP-ViT-bigG-14) and an ensemble of diffusion-based purifiers/upscalers (SD-x4-upscaler, Guided Diffusion, DDPM) to compute the style and upscale losses. The output perturbations are designed to be nearly imperceptible to human observers while disrupting style extraction by downstream text-to-image models.

## Method

StyleGuard formulates protection as a bilevel optimization problem solved via alternating updates: a surrogate diffusion model is fine-tuned for K₁ steps on the current protected images using DreamBooth loss, then the protected images are updated for K₂ PGD steps using a composite loss. The composite loss combines three terms: (1) a denoise loss that maximizes denoising error across an ensemble of LDMs for cross-model transferability; (2) a novel style loss that aligns the mean and variance of latent features (AdaIN-style representation) of protected images toward target images while pushing them away from clean images, perturbing global style features rather than local L2 distances; and (3) a novel upscale loss that, via a meta-learning approach, maximizes the denoising error on an ensemble of purifiers and upscalers so that purification amplifies rather than removes the perturbation. Expectation-over-transformation (EoT) with random cropping, resizing, and Gaussian noise is incorporated to ensure robustness to common preprocessing. The total loss is L_denoise + η·L_upscale + λ·L_style with η=1 and λ=10, optimized over N=100 iterations on 8× NVIDIA 3090 GPUs.

## Results

On WikiArt with DreamBooth fine-tuning, StyleGuard achieves the highest FID and lowest Precision across all five transformation settings. Under the most challenging purification attacks, StyleGuard reaches FID 418.33 (Precision 0.03) under DiffPure and FID 401.80 (Precision 0.00) under Noise Upscaling, compared to the unprotected baseline of FID 237.89/0.68 and FID 236.58/0.60 respectively. On CelebA for personalization attacks, StyleGuard maintains IMS_CLIP of 0.685 and IMS_VGG of 0.120 under Noise Upscaling, while baselines degrade to IMS_CLIP 0.725–0.780 and IMS_VGG 0.354–0.379. Cross-model transferability (FID ratio) reaches 92.4–96.5% for StyleGuard versus 73.5–85.5% for Anti-DreamBooth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StyleGuard outperforms all five baselines (Glaze, Mist, Anti-DreamBooth, MetaCloak, SimAC) across every transformation setting in Table 1. The gap is most dramatic under purification attacks: under DiffPure, StyleGuard achieves FID 418.33 vs. SimAC's 290.15 and MetaCloak's 318.87, while under Noise Upscaling it reaches FID 401.80 vs. SimAC's 284.52 and Mist's 256.65. SimAC, the strongest prior method under simple transformations (Crop+Resize: FID 365.47, Precision 0.06), degrades sharply under DiffPure (Precision jumps to 0.38) and Noise Upscaling (Precision 0.45), whereas StyleGuard maintains Precision ≤ 0.05 in all settings. Glaze is the weakest baseline under purification, with Precision recovering to 0.60 under Noise Upscaling (nearly matching the unprotected 0.60). On the Textual Inversion task (Table 4), StyleGuard again leads with FID 425.76 and Precision 0.07 under Noise Upscaling, while SimAC (FID 335.79, Precision 0.30) and MetaCloak (FID 308.42, Precision 0.72) are substantially weaker.

## Relation to Prior Work

StyleGuard extends the adversarial-perturbation defense line initiated by Anti-DreamBooth (alternating bilevel optimization), MetaCloak (EoT for transformation robustness), and SimAC (timestep selection), but departs from them in two key ways. First, it replaces the denoising-loss-only objective with a style loss based on AdaIN-style mean/variance statistics of latent features, targeting global style representations rather than local pixel-level disturbances. Second, it introduces an upscale loss that meta-learns against an ensemble of diffusion-based purifiers (DiffPure) and upscalers (SD-x4/x2), directly addressing the purification vulnerability that defeats prior methods. The style representation draws from the style-transfer literature (AdaIN, feature-space adversarial attacks), repurposing it as a defense objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: existing anti-mimicry defenses are broken by diffusion-based purification (DiffPure, Noise Upscaling), which is a realistic attacker capability. StyleGuard's ensemble-based upscale loss provides a principled way to harden perturbations against such purification, and the style-loss component improves cross-model transferability, making the defense more deployable in the wild where the attacker's model is unknown. However, the authors acknowledge reduced efficacy against commercial black-box upscalers (FIE) and LoRA-based fine-tuning, and the method requires ~20 minutes per image with 8 GPUs, limiting scalability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video diffusion, 3D generation, or more sophisticated purification pipelines, and whether the style-feature disruption can be made imperceptible at larger perturbation budgets.
