## Problem

Existing mixed-precision quantization (MPQ) methods face a fundamental trade-off between search efficiency and cross-dataset generalization: iterative search-based approaches (e.g., HAQ, GMPQ) require thousands of policy evaluations and are tied to the search dataset, while fast single-pass critics (e.g., OMPQ) rely on random data distributions that differ across datasets, preventing generalization. No prior method simultaneously achieves sub-second policy derivation and a quantization policy that transfers from one dataset to another without re-searching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253979/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single minibatch of 128 images sharing one class label (e.g., 'cat') passed through a pre-trained network, along with a target model size budget T. It produces an integer bit-width vector (one positive integer per layer) that assigns higher precision to layers with greater topological entropy, subject to the constraint that total model size does not exceed T. Notably, the input requires no random or diverse data—only same-label images—and the resulting policy is dataset-agnostic, meaning a policy derived on CIFAR-10 transfers directly to ImageNet or PASCAL VOC without re-computation.

## Method

GMPQ-TE measures per-layer quantization sensitivity via Topological Entropy (TE): each layer's output feature maps are treated as 2D grids, converted to weighted graphs, and subjected to clique filtration (sublevel-set construction from max to min edge weight). The 1st Betti number is tracked across the filtration to produce a Betti curve per image, from which a birth time (the threshold at which the first cycle appears) is extracted; TE is then the weighted Shannon entropy of the birth-time distribution across the same-label minibatch. Because TE captures how stably a layer preserves common spatial features within a class, it is provably independent of input resolution and label, yielding cross-dataset consistency. The MPQ problem is formulated as a single-pass linear program that minimizes the sum of TE_i × bit-width_i under the size constraint, with the ordering constraint that higher-TE layers receive strictly higher bit-widths; this LP is solved in under 15 seconds on a single CPU. Theoretical analysis further proves that the TE ranking is preserved throughout quantization-aware training (entropy drift is bounded by half a quantization step), so the bit-width assignment remains optimal without re-solving.

## Results

On ImageNet with MobileNet-V2 (PTQ), GMPQ-TE achieves 71.8% Top-1 accuracy at 33.4% compression (10.1 BOPs) with a search cost of only 15 s, versus 71.5% for GMPQ (6.1K s) and 71.7% for R-GMPQ (6.8K s). At higher compression (~74%), GMPQ-TE reaches 69.8% Top-1 in 11 s. On PASCAL VOC with SSD & VGG-16, GMPQ-TE attains 71.1% mAP at 36.6% compression (27 s search cost), outperforming R-GMPQ (70.8%, 6.4K s) and EdMIPS (69.4%, 5.4K s). On FPGA hardware (XC7Z045), GMPQ-TE delivers the highest throughput (413 GOP/s) and lowest latency (9.4 ms) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMPQ-TE consistently outperforms the generalizable baselines GMPQ and R-GMPQ on both accuracy and search cost: at ~46% compression on MobileNet-V2, GMPQ-TE achieves 71.2% Top-1 versus 70.4% (GMPQ) and 70.9% (R-GMPQ), while reducing search time from ~9–10K s to 14 s (~700× speedup). Against OMPQ, GMPQ-TE trades a small accuracy gap at the highest compression level (69.8% vs. 70.39% Top-1) for a substantially higher compression ratio (74.8% vs. 27.3%) and comparable cost (11 s vs. 9 s). On object detection (PASCAL VOC, SSD & VGG-16), GMPQ-TE surpasses EdMIPS by 1.7% mAP (71.1% vs. 69.4%) at similar parameter budgets while cutting search cost from 5.4K s to 27 s. Against iterative search methods (HAQ, DJPQ, HMQ, DQ, BP-NAS), GMPQ-TE matches or exceeds their accuracy at comparable compression while reducing search cost by 2–4 orders of magnitude (e.g., 14 s vs. 18.3K s for HAQ). On FPGA hardware, GMPQ-TE achieves the best throughput (413 GOP/s on XC7Z045) and lowest latency (9.4 ms), outperforming R-GMPQ (398 GOP/s, 10.4 ms) and GMPQ (367 GOP/s, 12.3 ms). Overall, GMPQ-TE wins or ties on accuracy across all 14 baselines at comparable compression levels, with the sole exception being a 0.6% Top-1 gap versus OMPQ at the most aggressive compression setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes topological entropy (TE) from Zhao and Zhang (ICLR 2022), originally designed to evaluate individual CNN unit effectiveness, as a layer-wise quantization sensitivity metric for mixed-precision quantization. It combines this with the single-pass linear programming formulation introduced by OMPQ (Ma et al., AAAI 2023) and LIMPQ (Tang et al., ECCV 2022), but replaces their random-minibatch-based sensitivity measures (orthogonality, layer-wise importance) with TE computed from same-label minibatches. The key conceptual departure from prior generalizable MPQ methods (GMPQ, R-GMPQ) is the claim that TE's focus on preserving common features within a label class—rather than representing arbitrary random data—yields cross-dataset consistency without requiring expensive iterative search or attribution-based evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing quantization policy search from hours (GMPQ: 2.8 GPU-hours) to seconds (~11s on MobileNet-V2) while claiming generalization from CIFAR-10 to ImageNet and PASCAL VOC could substantially lower the barrier to deploying quantized models on new datasets. However, the cross-dataset generalization claim rests on strong theoretical assumptions (class-conditional distribution invariance within receptive fields, Lipschitz bounds on entropy drift during QAT) that are difficult to verify empirically, and the evaluation covers only three datasets and a limited set of architectures. Open questions include whether TE-based policies remain valid for generative models, video understanding, or tasks where same-label feature sharing is weaker, and whether the LP formulation's assumption of stable TE ranking during fine-tuning holds under aggressive compression (e.g., 2-bit).
