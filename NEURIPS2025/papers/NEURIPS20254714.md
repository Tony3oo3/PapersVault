## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for speech foundation models rely on backpropagation, incurring high GPU memory overhead that limits deployment on resource-constrained devices. Backpropagation-free TTA methods exist but were designed for vision tasks and perform poorly on speech due to fundamental differences in architecture (CNN+Transformer vs. pure CNN/Transformer), task formulation (sequence-to-sequence vs. classification), and noise characteristics (temporally varying vs. spatial). No efficient, backpropagation-free TTA framework tailored specifically for speech foundation models had been proposed.

## Input / Output

The method processes one noisy speech utterance at a time (batch size 1) in an online, sequential stream. Each utterance is a variable-length sequence of frame-level feature vectors (x_t^i ∈ R^d_in) fed through a frozen pre-trained SFM (Wav2Vec2-Base or HuBERT-Large with CTC head). The output is a frame-level posterior distribution over 32 CTC token classes (26 letters + 6 special tokens), decoded into a text transcription. Pre-computed source-domain statistics (per-layer centroids and per-token-class means/covariances from clean LibriSpeech) are stored as lightweight references (order L×d and 2×L×32×d parameters) and used during adaptation without accessing individual source samples.

## Method

E-BATS introduces a lightweight prompt adaptation (LPA) module that adds a single learnable prompt vector s_t ∈ R^d to the CNN encoder's latent features (Z_t + s_t·1^T), exploiting the observation that mean shift dominates covariance shift (up to 7.8×) in speech domain shifts. The prompt is optimized per-utterance using CMA-ES, a derivative-free evolutionary strategy that samples J=50 candidate prompts from a multivariate normal and iteratively refines the distribution parameters (mean, covariance, step size) over K iterations without any gradient computation. A multi-scale loss guides optimization: entropy minimization (excluding blank-token frames to avoid trivial collapse), utterance-level latent embedding alignment to source centroids across all transformer layers, and token-level distribution alignment with adaptive confidence weighting that down-weights unreliable pseudo-labels under high shift. A test-time exponential moving average (T-EMA) module smooths CMA-ES parameters across consecutive utterances (γ=0.8–0.9), stabilizing adaptation in dynamic streams while preventing both catastrophic forgetting and overfitting to individual utterances.

## Results

On Wav2Vec2ForCTC-Base, E-BATS achieves an average WER of 15.6% on Gaussian noise (σ=0.0–0.02), 24.0% on CHiME-3 single-domain, 24.3% on CHiME-3 mixed, 12.5% on TED, and 30.6% on CommonVoice. Compared to the strongest BP-free baseline (FOA), E-BATS delivers 4.1%–13.5% WER reductions across conditions, with the largest gap (20.0%) at the highest noise level (σ=0.02). Against the best BP-based method (DSUTA), E-BATS matches or beats it on 3 of 5 datasets while using 2.0×–6.4× less peak GPU memory. On the larger HuBERTForCTC-Large backbone, E-BATS achieves 6.4% avg WER on Gaussian noise and 14.0% on CHiME-3, with 2.4×–6.8× memory savings over BP-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-BATS outperforms all three BP-free baselines across every dataset: FOA (the strongest BP-free method) is beaten by 4.1%–13.5% WER, while T3A and LAME actually degrade the source model (e.g., T3A scores 35.9% vs. 34.2% source on CHiME-3 Single), confirming that classifier-only updates are insufficient for speech. Among BP-based methods, E-BATS matches or beats the best speech-specific baselines (DSUTA, CEA, SUTA) on most conditions—e.g., 24.0% vs. 24.0% on CHiME-3 Single and 30.6% vs. 36.1% on CommonVoice—while using 2.8×–3.3× less GPU memory. General-purpose BP methods (TENT, SAR, CoTTA, EATA) perform near or below the source model on speech, with EATA and CoTTA degrading significantly on CommonVoice (61.3% and 36.9% respectively), indicating their vision-oriented strategies do not transfer. The key differentiator is that E-BATS updates only a single prompt vector per utterance (preserving the frozen backbone), whereas BP-based methods like DSUTA update many parameters and suffer catastrophic forgetting on diverse domains like CommonVoice (36.1% vs. E-BATS's 30.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

E-BATS sits at the intersection of prompt tuning (Lester et al., 2021) and derivative-free evolutionary optimization (CMA-ES, Hansen 2016), repurposing both for a setting where neither was previously applied: single-utterance, backpropagation-free test-time adaptation of hybrid CNN-Transformer speech foundation models. It departs from the dominant BP-based TTA line (TENT, SUTA, DSUTA) by eliminating gradient computation entirely, and from prior BP-free TTA (FOA, T3A, LAME) by recognizing that speech-specific architectural and task differences—sequence-to-sequence mapping, temporally varying noise, batch-size-1 processing, and LayerNorm rather than BatchNorm—render vision-derived BP-free strategies ineffective. The multi-scale loss design (entropy + utterance-level + token-level alignment) is novel in combining global and local distribution matching with adaptive confidence weighting, going beyond the entropy-only or classifier-only objectives used in prior BP-free work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–6.4× GPU memory reduction while matching or exceeding BP-based SOTA makes TTA viable on edge devices and in continuous-inference pipelines where automatic differentiation is infeasible, opening a practical path for deploying speech foundation models in real-world noisy environments. The finding that mean shift dominates covariance shift (up to 7.8×) provides a clean geometric justification for the simple additive-prompt mechanism, suggesting that many acoustic domain shifts are approximately translational in latent space. Key limitations include the CMA-ES iteration latency (sequential, not fully GPU-parallelized), which constrains real-time use, and the current restriction to CTC-based ASR with two specific backbones. Open questions include whether the approach extends to autoregressive or non-CTC speech tasks, whether GPU-parallel CMA-ES can close the latency gap, and whether prompt-based adaptation can be extended to transformer layers without the accuracy drop observed in ablations.
