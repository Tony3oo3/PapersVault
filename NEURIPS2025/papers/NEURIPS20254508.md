## Problem

Long-tailed class distributions cause deep models to overfit majority classes while degrading on tail classes, and existing remedies (data augmentation, loss rebalancing, decoupled training) fail to deliver consistent gains across broader scenarios such as multi-label recognition and foundation-model finetuning. No prior method directly manipulates the model's internal parameter space to reallocate representational capacity toward minority classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254508/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (evaluated at 112×112 and 224×224 resolutions) and produces either a single class label (multi-class tasks on CIFAR-100-LT, Places-LT) or a set of active labels (multi-label tasks on MIML, Pascal-VOC, NUS-WIDE-SCENE, MS-COCO). At inference the low-rank tail-specific parameters are fused into the main weights, so the forward pass is computationally identical to the unmodified backbone with no extra storage or latency.

## Method

MORE decomposes every convolutional weight matrix as W = W^g + B^t A^t, where the low-rank component W^t (rank r ≈ 0.1) is reserved for minority-class expertise. A discrepancy-based loss computes the squared L2 distance between the full-model logits and the main-parameters-only logits, weighted by the empirical class prior π_y so that majority-class samples receive stronger penalties, thereby forcing θ^t to minimize its influence on head predictions while amplifying tail-class representations. A sinusoidal schedule α(τ) = A·sin(πτ/T) modulates this loss over training—starting low to let θ^g establish generalizable features, peaking mid-training to drive tail-specific learning, and tapering to prevent overfitting. The total objective is L_base + α(τ)·L_MORE, making MORE a plug-and-play regularizer compatible with any base loss (CE, LA, ProCo, BCE, Focal, ASL). A Rademacher-complexity analysis (Theorem 1) shows that this space partitioning yields a tighter balanced-risk generalization bound than the undecomposed baseline.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), MORE combined with ProCo achieves 52.9% overall top-1 accuracy (vs. 51.9% for ProCo alone), with the Few-class accuracy improving from 33.4% to 34.0%. On the severely imbalanced Places-LT (IF=996), LA+MORE improves Few-class accuracy by 3.2% (27.4%→30.6%) and Medium by 1.7% (40.3%→42.0%). For multi-label recognition, MORE yields up to 3.8% overall mAP gains on MIML (BCE: 80.8%→84.6%) and up to 8.3% Few-class mAP improvement on PASCAL-VOC when finetuning CLIP (82.2%→90.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MORE is designed as a plug-and-play module that consistently improves every baseline it is combined with. On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=10), ProCo+MORE (65.9% All) outperforms BCL (64.2%), LA (63.7%), CB (62.1%), and CE (61.4%), with the largest gains on Few classes (60.2% vs. 58.7% for ProCo alone). On the extreme-imbalance Places-LT (IF=996), LA+MORE improves Few accuracy by 3.2% over LA alone (30.6% vs. 27.4%), while ProCo+MORE achieves the best overall accuracy of 40.8%. For multi-label tasks, MORE adds 3.2–4.0% overall mAP over BCE/Focal/ASL baselines on MIML, and the gains grow with dataset severity: on PASCAL-VOC (IF=20.9), ASL+MORE lifts Few mAP by 2.9% (52.9%→55.8%), and on MS-COCO (IF=352.9), ASL+MORE improves Few mAP by 0.8% (27.2%→28.0%). When finetuning CLIP, the improvements are most dramatic on PASCAL-VOC where ASL+MORE achieves a 8.3% Few-class mAP gain (82.2%→90.5%), demonstrating that MORE's parameter-space rebalancing is particularly effective when the base model is already strong.

## Relation to Prior Work

MORE borrows the low-rank factorization structure from LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) but repurposes it fundamentally: rather than enabling parameter-efficient fine-tuning, the decomposition W = W_g + B^t A^t is used to partition model capacity between majority and minority classes, guided by a novel discrepancy-based loss. The theoretical grounding extends the class-wise Rademacher complexity framework of Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2023) to show that such a partition tightens the balanced-risk generalization bound. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant long-tailed learning paradigms (data resampling, logit adjustment, decoupled training, contrastive learning) by operating at the parameter-space level, positioning itself as a complementary plug-and-play regularizer rather than a replacement for existing rebalancing strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature (no inference overhead, compatible with CE, LA, ProCo, BCE, Focal, ASL, and CLIP finetuning) makes MORE a practical add-on for practitioners facing long-tailed distributions across single-label, multi-label, and foundation-model finetuning settings. The ablation showing that LoRA-like decomposition alone is ineffective without the tailored loss clarifies that the mechanism is the class-weighted discrepancy signal, not the low-rank structure per se. Open questions include whether stricter decoupling (e.g., orthogonality constraints between θ^g and θ^t) would further improve modularity, whether the approach extends to few-shot or domain-adaptation scenarios, and how it behaves when tail classes are semantically disparate rather than merely underrepresented.
