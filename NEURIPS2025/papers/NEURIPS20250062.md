## Problem

Collecting pseudo-malicious instructions—semantically harmless queries that trigger unnecessary LLM refusals—is essential for evaluating and mitigating over-refusal, but existing curation methods either lack scalability (manual creation) or fail to produce sufficiently diverse and consistently refusal-inducing prompts (rewriting, gradient-based search). No prior approach explicitly optimizes for LLM refusal probability while simultaneously maintaining linguistic diversity and semantic safety across diverse models.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes seed instructions sampled from TRIDENT-CORE and a frozen target LLM (LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct) whose token logits provide response confidence scores for fitness evaluation. GPT-4O serves as the mutator, recombinator, and safety judge, generating modified instructions with harmlessness justifications that are verified before scoring. The outputs are two datasets: EVOREFUSE-TEST (582 pseudo-malicious instructions for benchmarking over-refusal across 9 LLMs) and EVOREFUSE-ALIGN (3,000 instruction pairs with GPT-4O-generated chosen/rejected responses for SFT and DPO fine-tuning).

## Method

EVOREFUSE formulates pseudo-malicious instruction generation as maximizing an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) on LLM refusal probability, derived via Jensen's inequality to avoid the numerical instability of direct marginal likelihood estimation (where response log-probabilities underflow to ~10^{-203}). The ELBO decomposes into response confidence and refusal log-probability terms, with the decoding entropy treated as a near-constant offset (accounting for only 0.4% of variance). An evolutionary loop iteratively applies nine strategy-guided GPT-4O mutators (deceptive context, sensitive words, extreme emotion), recombines top-L high-fitness candidates, and uses simulated annealing with linear cooling to balance exploration and exploitation. Fitness is a Monte Carlo estimate over K=10 sampled responses combining a refusal classifier's log-probability and length-normalized token log-probabilities, with a GPT-4O safety judge filtering out any semantically harmful variants at every generation step.

## Results

EVOREFUSE-TEST achieves the highest average refusal-triggering rate across 9 LLMs, surpassing the next-best benchmark (PH-GEN) by 85.34% without a safety-prior system prompt and by 140.41% over SGTEST with one enabled. It also yields 34.86% greater lexical diversity (MSTTR 0.54 vs. 0.48) and 40.03% higher response log-probability (−43.55 vs. −103.08) than the second-best dataset. For mitigation, fine-tuning LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct on EVOREFUSE-ALIGN reduces over-refusals by 29.85% (SFT) and 45.96% (DPO) relative to the best fine-tuning baseline, while preserving safety on jailbreak benchmarks (e.g., 0.97 PRR on ADVBENCH with DPO vs. 1.00 for SFT baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVOREFUSE-TEST dominates all eight prior over-refusal benchmarks on refusal-triggering rate: it outperforms XSTEST (0.05→0.24 on DeepSeek, 0.13→0.65 on LLaMA), OKTEST (0.09→0.24), SGTEST (0.18→0.24), HITEST (0.08→0.24), OR-BENCH (0.14→0.24), and PHTEST (0.10→0.24) on DeepSeek-7B, with the largest gap on LLaMA-3.1-8B (the target model, 364% higher). Among automatically generated baselines, EVOREFUSE-TEST also beats OR-GEN (0.16→0.24) and PH-GEN (0.19→0.24), confirming that evolutionary search explores more effective refusal-triggering variants than rewriting or gradient-based pipelines. For mitigation (Table 3), EVOREFUSE-ALIGN with SFT achieves the lowest over-refusal PRR of 0.32 on EVOREFUSE-TEST, beating OR-BENCH SFT (0.45), PHTEST SFT (0.39), PROMPTAGENT SFT (0.43), Few-Shot Prompting (0.48), and DRO (0.56); DPO further reduces this to 0.30 while maintaining 0.97 PRR on ADVBENCH (vs. 1.00 for SFT baselines), indicating a modest 4.82% safety trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on LLM over-refusal evaluation (XSTEST, OKTEST, OR-BENCH, PHTEST) by replacing manual curation, keyword embedding, or single-objective gradient search with an evolutionary prompt optimization framework guided by a variational ELBO objective. Conceptually, it repurposes techniques from evolutionary prompt search (EvoPrompt, GPS) and variational inference for the safety-evaluation domain, where prior methods either lacked an explicit optimization signal (OR-BENCH rewriting) or explored too narrow a search space (PHTEST gradient descent). The ELBO formulation—deriving a numerically stable lower bound on refusal probability to sidestep the underflow problem of direct marginal likelihood estimation—is a novel technical bridge between variational inference and LLM safety testing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two released datasets (EVOREFUSE-TEST with 582 instructions, EVOREFUSE-ALIGN with 3,000 pairs) provide the LLM safety community with a substantially harder evaluation benchmark and a practical alignment resource that reduces over-refusals by ~30–46% without degrading safety. The interpretability finding that over-refusals arise from shortcut learning on sensitive keywords concentrated in early transformer layers offers a mechanistic explanation that could inform targeted safety-alignment interventions. Open questions include whether the evolutionary approach generalizes to multimodal or non-English settings, whether the GPT-4O-dependent mutation/judge pipeline introduces circularity in safety verification, and whether the ELBO-based fitness can be extended to multi-objective safety criteria beyond binary refusal.
