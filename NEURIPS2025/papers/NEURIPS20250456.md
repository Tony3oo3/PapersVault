## Problem

Prior work on early-stage hardware design quality prediction operates only at the module level and relies on intermediate representations like AIGs that lose rich semantic information present in Verilog source code. The question of whether timing violations and routing congestion can be predicted at the individual line-of-code level directly from RTL has not been addressed in the literature, despite being critical for designers who need to identify specific code responsible for quality issues without running hours-long synthesis and PnR flows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250456/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw Verilog RTL source code at two granularities simultaneously: each individual line is tokenized and embedded independently, while the entire module is also embedded as a whole. Outputs include binary line-level classifications (congestion-causing or timing-violating), continuous line-level WNS regression values, and a derived module-level WNS obtained by selecting the minimum across all line-level predictions rather than being predicted directly from module features alone.

## Method

VeriLoC extracts embeddings from the penultimate layer of CL-Verilog (a 13B LLaMA-2 variant fine-tuned on Verilog), computing both module-level embeddings via attention-weighted pooling over all token hidden states and line-level embeddings via the same mechanism applied to individual lines. These are concatenated, projected to a lower dimension through a trained autoencoder (4096→1024→128), and further augmented with embeddings from up to p=5 neighboring lines to capture local sequential context. A gradient-boosted tree classifier/regressor (XGBoost or LightGBM, chosen for robustness on imbalanced data where only a small fraction of lines cause issues) is then trained on the augmented embeddings with ground-truth labels from Synopsys RTL Architect. The key architectural insight is that combining local line context, neighboring-line context, and global module context in a single concatenated representation is essential for accurate line-level predictions, and that module-level WNS can be improved by aggregating line-level predictions rather than predicting directly from module features.

## Results

VeriLoC achieves F1-scores of 0.86 for line-level congestion detection and 0.95 for line-level timing detection (both with LightGBM and context window p=5). At the module level, VeriLoC predicts worst negative slack (WNS) with R² = 0.94 and MAPE = 6% averaged across 14 OpenABCD designs, substantially outperforming prior SOTA methods that report MAPE of 12–17%. The method also delivers a 14× average (61× median) speedup over Synopsys RTL Architect for the same predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VeriLoC outperforms all four baselines on both R² and MAPE for module-level WNS prediction. Against the strongest prior method RTL-Timer [1] (R²=0.86, MAPE=12%), VeriLoC improves R² by 8 percentage points and halves MAPE to 6%. MasterRTL [4] (R²=0.74, MAPE=15%) and the GNN-based netlist predictor [10] (R²=0.69, MAPE=17%) are further behind, with VeriLoC achieving 20+ point R² gains over the GNN baseline. VeriDistill [11], which combines LLM RTL embeddings with GNN-based LUT embeddings, achieves a competitive MAPE of 7.6% but lags significantly on R² (0.728 vs. 0.94), suggesting its multimodal fusion does not capture timing semantics as effectively. An ablation (VeriLoC-Mod) using only module-level embeddings without line-level context yields R²=0.91 and MAPE=8%, confirming that the line-level embeddings contribute meaningfully even to module-level predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes decoder-only Verilog code-generation LLMs (specifically CL-Verilog) as embedding extractors for downstream quality prediction, building on recent evidence that penultimate-layer activations from such models yield effective representations. It departs from prior RTL quality-prediction work (MasterRTL, RTL-Timer, VeriDistill, CircuitFusion) in two key ways: (1) it operates directly on Verilog source rather than intermediate representations like AIGs or synthesized netlists, preserving semantic information that is lost in graph conversion; and (2) it introduces line-of-code-level granularity, whereas all prior methods predict only at the module level. The architecture draws on the software code-quality literature (CodeBERT, bug detection via LLMs) but adapts it to the hardware domain where training data is scarce and concepts like routing congestion are absent from general-purpose LLM training corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VeriLoC enables designers to receive line-level timing and congestion feedback in seconds rather than the hours-to-days required by full synthesis-and-PnR flows, potentially compressing the iterative design-closure loop that currently takes weeks to months. The demonstration that a 7B CL-Verilog variant achieves 22× speedup over RTL Architect with only a modest F1 drop (0.93 timing, 0.84 congestion) suggests practical deployability. Key limitations include evaluation on a single open-source dataset (OpenABCD) with leaf modules only, no inter-module dependency modeling, and reliance on a single commercial EDA tool (Synopsys RTL Architect) for ground-truth labels. Open questions include generalization to industrial multi-million-gate designs, extension to additional QoR metrics (power, area), and whether the embeddings can drive automated RTL optimization rather than mere prediction.
