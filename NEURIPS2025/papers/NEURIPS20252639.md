## Problem

Language models can generate correct responses among repeated samples, but weak verifiers (LM judges, reward models) are noisy, poorly calibrated, and produce inconsistent scores, creating a generation-verification gap. Naive unweighted averaging of verifier scores underperforms because individual verifier accuracies vary by up to 37.5%, yet learning proper weights typically requires large labeled datasets that are expensive to obtain. The paper asks: how can we effectively combine multiple heterogeneous weak verifiers into a strong ensemble with minimal labeled data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252639/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query, K repeatedly sampled candidate responses (K up to 100) from a generator LM, and scores from m verifiers (33 reward models of 8B–72B producing continuous scores, plus LM judges from Chatbot Arena producing discrete {0,1} or [0,1] scores). It outputs a selected response index and a posterior correctness probability Pr(y=1|s₁,…,sₘ). A key practical detail is that only 5–10 labeled development samples (≤1% of the evaluation set) are needed to estimate the class balance prior and adaptive binarization thresholds; the full test set remains unlabeled. As an efficiency extension, a distilled 400M ModernBERT-Large cross-encoder replaces the full verifier ensemble, accepting a concatenated query-response pair and outputting a single scalar score.

## Method

WEAVER adapts weak supervision to verification by first binarizing continuous reward-model scores using adaptive thresholds learned from a tiny labeled dev set, then filtering out verifiers whose positive rates fall outside a class-balance-dependent range (e.g., dropping verifiers with >80% or <20% positive rates when the dataset is roughly balanced). The remaining binary votes are modeled as conditionally independent observations of a latent correctness variable Y, and verifier accuracy parameters (true positive and true negative rates) are estimated via moment matching: pairwise joint probabilities and marginal frequencies of verifier outputs are equated to model-implied expressions, yielding a quadratic system solved by gradient descent on a matrix objective. The posterior Pr(Y=1|S=s₁,…,sₘ) is then computed with a Naive Bayes-style formula using the estimated parameters, and the response with the highest posterior is selected. As a distillation step, a 400M cross-encoder is trained on WEAVER's pseudo-labels (the posterior scores), retaining 98.7% of the ensemble's accuracy while reducing verification compute by 99.97%.

## Results

Using Llama 3.3 70B Instruct as the generator with K=100 repeated samples, WEAVER achieves 86.2% average success rate across MATH500 (93.4%), GPQA Diamond (66.4%), MMLU College (94.9%), and MMLU Pro (90.2%). This outperforms majority voting by 14.0 percentage points and comes within 0.5% of OpenAI's o3-mini (86.7%) and within 5.7% of the Pass@100 oracle upper bound (91.9%). The distilled 400M cross-encoder retains 98.7% of WEAVER's full accuracy while reducing verification compute by 99.97%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WEAVER (86.2% avg) decisively outperforms all verification baselines: majority voting (72.2%, +14.0 pts), the best single reward model from RewardBench (72.7%, +13.5 pts), naive ensemble of top-10 reward models (69.1%, +17.1 pts), self-verification (66.9%, +19.3 pts), and multi-agent verification (71.6%, +14.6 pts). Against frontier single-sample LMs, WEAVER surpasses GPT-4o (69.0%, +17.2 pts), Claude 3.7 Sonnet (70.4%, +15.8 pts), and Llama 4 Maverick (82.2%, +4.0 pts), while coming within 0.5% of o3-mini (86.7%). The gap to the Pass@100 oracle (91.9%) is only 5.7 points, indicating WEAVER recovers most of the verification signal available in the generated candidates. Notably, WEAVER's gains are largest on GPQA Diamond (+19.0 pts over majority voting), suggesting the ensemble is most beneficial on harder reasoning tasks where individual verifiers are noisiest.

## Relation to Prior Work

WEAVER adapts the weak supervision framework (Snorkel, Data Programming) from Ratner et al. to the LM verification setting, treating binary verifier outputs as noisy labels in a latent variable model. It departs from prior verification work (self-verification, multi-agent verification, majority voting) by introducing a principled weighted ensemble that estimates verifier accuracies via moment matching rather than requiring large labeled datasets. The paper also extends the repeated-sampling literature by adding verifier count as a new scaling axis and demonstrating that weighted aggregation substantially outperforms naive averaging when verifier quality varies widely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 400M cross-encoder distilled from WEAVER's ensemble retains 98.7% of the accuracy while reducing verification compute by 99.97%, making strong verification feasible on a single GPU rather than an 8-GPU node per 70B verifier. This opens the door to on-device verification and broader deployment of inference-time scaling. Open questions include whether the conditional independence assumption holds for correlated verifiers, how WEAVER extends to multimodal or process-level verification, and whether the framework can provide supervision signals for RLHF training rather than just test-time selection.
