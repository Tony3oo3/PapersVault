## Problem

Posterior sampling with discrete diffusion models in finite state spaces remains challenging because likelihood functions defined on discrete supports lack the gradient information that continuous diffusion posterior sampling methods (e.g., DPS) fundamentally rely on. Existing discrete approaches such as SVDD-PM approximate value functions in a reinforcement-learning style that is difficult to tune, particularly when the guidance signal is complex. This paper addresses the gap by developing a principled, plug-and-play posterior sampling algorithm that requires no gradient of the likelihood and provides rigorous convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method operates on data in finite discrete spaces (binary strings for MNIST images, 4-ary sequences for DNA, 129-ary sequences for music) and takes as input a pretrained SEDD model with a uniform transition kernel, a measurement y or reward function r(x), and a geometric annealing schedule {η_k} decaying from η_max to η_min ≈ 0. The likelihood sampling step is implemented via Metropolis-Hastings MCMC because the unnormalized posterior density over the finite support is directly accessible, while the prior step simulates a partial reverse continuous-time Markov chain starting from noise level η_k. The output is a single sample x^(K) drawn from the target posterior p(x|y) ∝ p(x)p(y|x).

## Method

SGDD introduces an auxiliary variable z and a Hamming-distance-based potential function D(x,z;η) = d(x,z)·log((1+(N−1)e^{−η})/((N−1)(1−e^{−η}))) that decouples the prior and likelihood in the augmented distribution π(x,z;η) ∝ exp(−f(z;y) − g(x) − D(x,z;η)). The key insight is that this potential, when substituted into the prior sampling step, exactly recovers the conditional distribution p(x_0|x_t) of a discrete diffusion model with uniform kernel at noise level η, so the prior step reduces to running a partial reverse CTMC from z^(k) starting at σ_t = η_k. The likelihood step samples z^(k) from a distribution proportional to exp(−f(z;y) − D(x^(k),z;η)) using MCMC, requiring no gradient of f. An annealing schedule {η_k} gradually reduces the regularization strength, and the paper proves convergence to the true posterior via a Fisher-information-based analysis for discrete Markov chains, with error bounds accounting for both score approximation and Euler discretization.

## Results

On discretized MNIST XOR inverse problems, SGDD achieves 20.17 dB PSNR and 91.2% class accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (SVDD-PM) by 8.36 dB and 39.8 percentage points. On guided DNA enhancer design, SGDD (β=50) reaches 9.14 median predicted activity and 93.0% ATAC accessibility, a 42% improvement in median reward over the previous state-of-the-art (DRAKES w/o KL at 6.44). On monophonic music infilling at 40% masking, SGDD reduces Hellinger distance to 0.126 versus 0.465 for SVDD-PM (a 2× reduction). On synthetic posterior sampling (D=10), SGDD achieves 0.334 Hellinger distance versus 0.410–0.455 for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGDD decisively outperforms all six baselines across every task. On MNIST XOR/AND, SGDD surpasses SVDD-PM [27] by 8.36 dB (PSNR) and SMC [50] by 10.12 dB, while DPS [7] (adapted to discrete via a guidance rate matrix) performs worst at 9.04 dB. On DNA enhancer design, SGDD (β=50) achieves 9.14 median Pred-Activity versus DRAKES w/o KL [48] at 6.44 and SVDD-PM at 5.41; DRAKES w/ KL is competitive on ATAC-Acc (92.5% vs. 93.0%) but lags on reward. Classifier guidance (CG) [32] and Feynman-Kac MAX (FK-MAX) [33] both underperform SGDD on DNA, with CG collapsing to 0% ATAC-Acc. On music infilling, SGDD halves the Hellinger distance of SVDD-PM (0.126 vs. 0.465 at 40% masking) and reduces outlier notes from 11.2% to 0.3%, while SMC [50] performs comparably to SVDD-PM. The consistent margin across all domains and task types (inverse problems, reward-guided generation) indicates that the split Gibbs formulation with the Hamming-distance potential is fundamentally better suited to discrete state spaces than gradient-based or value-function-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the split Gibbs posterior sampling framework (Vono et al. [47], Wu et al. [51]) from continuous Euclidean spaces with ℓ2 regularizers to discrete state spaces by designing a Hamming-distance-based potential function that exactly matches the SEDD discrete diffusion transition kernel (Lou et al. [29]). Unlike prior discrete diffusion posterior sampling methods (SVDD-PM [27], SMC [50]) that rely on value function approximation or particle-based sequential sampling, SGDD provides a Gibbs-based approach with rigorous convergence guarantees. The theoretical analysis generalizes the Fisher information-based convergence framework from continuous SDEs (Sun et al. [43], Wu et al. [51]) to continuous-time Markov chains using the FIR inequality (Hilder et al. [16]).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGDD is the first principled plug-and-play posterior sampling algorithm for discrete diffusion models with provable convergence to the true posterior, filling a gap where prior methods required derivative-free value function approximation or large particle counts. The potential function design (Eq. 10) is a clean conceptual bridge: it makes the prior sampling step equivalent to running a partial discrete diffusion reverse process, enabling direct reuse of pretrained discrete diffusion models. Open questions include scalability to very high-dimensional discrete spaces (the synthetic study shows degradation at D=10), generalization beyond uniform transition kernels, and whether the MCMC-based likelihood step can be replaced by learned samplers for more complex likelihoods.
