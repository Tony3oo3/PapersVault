## Problem

Long video understanding in VLMs is bottlenecked by the sheer token count: each frame expands to tens or hundreds of tokens, so processing more than ~1k frames exceeds LLM context limits and forces sparse sampling that loses temporal information. Existing compression methods rely on training-free heuristics (pooling, attention-based token selection) that degrade severely at the extreme compression ratios needed for hour-long videos, and frame-level selection suffers from the LLM's 'lost in the middle' position bias in long contexts.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a video sampled at up to 8 FPS (128–1024 frames during training; up to 2048 frames at inference for videos over an hour) and a text question. Each 8-frame clip is encoded by UMT-L into 128 visual tokens (16 per frame) via token merging and an MLP projector. At inference, QC-Comp first selects a subset of top-k relevant frames (256–2048 depending on benchmark) before the LLM generates a text answer, typically a multiple-choice selection.

## Method

XComp combines two compression mechanisms. At the token level, LP-Comp supervises the LLM layers to progressively compress each frame from 16 tokens to 1 token using a cosine schedule across all transformer layers, with suffix-preservation (keeping the last N tokens per frame) to remain compatible with causal attention; this is learned via a data-efficient supervised compression tuning stage requiring only 2.5% of VideoChat-Flash's SFT data. At the frame level, QC-Comp computes question-conditioned relevance scores from internal LLM attention between question tokens and video tokens, but mitigates the 'lost in the middle' position bias by splitting the video into 64-frame segments and computing local attention within each segment independently, then aggregating scores to select the top-k most relevant frames globally. The key insight is that heuristic compression fails at extreme ratios because the LLM layers were never trained to condense dropped-token information into retained tokens, so learnable progressive compression across all layers is necessary.

## Results

XComp (2B) achieves 59.7 on LongVideoBench, 66.7 on MLVU, 45.6 on VideoMME (Long), and 46.2 on LVBench, consistently outperforming its base model VideoChat-Flash-2B (58.3, 65.7, 44.9, 42.9) across all four long-video benchmarks. The most notable gain is on LVBench (+3.3 points), where the extreme compression enables processing 2,048 frames versus the baseline's limited capacity. XComp also reduces LLM inference TFLOPs by 53–58% and latency by 45–58% at 1k–4k frames, while uniquely maintaining or improving accuracy as input frame count increases (unlike VideoChat-Flash, which degrades beyond a certain frame threshold).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct base model VideoChat-Flash-2B, XComp wins on all four benchmarks: +1.4 on LongVideoBench (59.7 vs 58.3), +1.0 on MLVU (66.7 vs 65.7), +0.7 on VideoMME-Long (45.6 vs 44.9), and +3.3 on LVBench (46.2 vs 42.9), using only 2.5% of the SFT data. Against InternVL3-2B, XComp leads by +4.3 on LongVideoBench and +2.5 on MLVU. Among 7–9B models, XComp (2B) trails VideoChat-Flash-7B (64.7/74.7/55.4/48.2) and Eagle2.5-8B (66.4/77.6) on most metrics, but surpasses mPLUG-Owl3-7B on LVBench (46.2 vs 43.5) and is competitive with Kangaroo-8B and TimeMarker-8B. Against proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro), XComp remains behind on LongVideoBench and VideoMME but notably exceeds GPT-4o on LVBench (46.2 vs 30.8). When applied to LLaVA-Next-Video, XComp improves VideoMME-Long from 62.7 to 63.2 and LVBench from 40.6 to 43.1, outperforming both the original model and a same-data fine-tuning baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VideoChat-Flash (Li et al., 2024), adopting its UMT-L encoder, token-merging projector, and Qwen2-1.5B LLM backbone, but departs fundamentally from its heuristic (training-free) token compression by introducing supervised, progressive compression learned end-to-end within the LLM layers (LP-Comp). It also extends the frame-selection literature by addressing the 'lost in the middle' position bias of LLM attention through segmented local attention (QC-Comp), a design inspired by local-attention work in long-context NLP but applied here to video frame relevance scoring. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against both context-extension approaches (LongVA, LongVILA) and prior compression methods (Llama-Vid, MovieChat, Video-XL) by pushing token compression to the extreme of one token per frame while simultaneously performing question-conditioned frame selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: XComp reduces LLM inference TFLOPs by 53–58% and latency by 45–58% while enabling 2×–4× denser frame sampling, making hour-long video understanding feasible on modest hardware. The finding that heuristic compression degrades performance at high ratios (Table 2) challenges the widespread assumption that training-free token selection is sufficient, and the data-efficiency of the compression tuning (2.5% of SFT data, ~24 GPU-hours on 8×H100) lowers the barrier to adoption. Key limitations include evaluation only on 2B-scale models, absence of multi-seed statistical analysis, and a moderate performance drop on fine-grained OCR tasks (MME-VideoOCR), suggesting that extreme compression still sacrifices some detail. Open questions include whether the learnable compression can be integrated into pre-training, whether joint optimization of frame selection and token compression yields further gains, and how the approach scales to 7B+ models.
