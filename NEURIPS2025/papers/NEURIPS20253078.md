## Problem

Standard Transformers lack an explicit mechanism for recursion and structured state transitions, causing them to fail on tasks requiring recognition of regular expressions and deterministic context-free grammars, particularly when input lengths exceed those seen during training. Prior stack-augmented approaches (e.g., stacked attention) restructure the attention computation itself, creating entanglement and reducing modularity, rather than introducing a separate, reusable recursive state mechanism that preserves the integrity of the Transformer layers.

## Input / Output

For natural language pretraining, the model consumes token sequences (up to 4096 positions) and produces next-token predictions via standard autoregressive decoding. For formal language evaluation, input strings are uniformly sampled from length 1–40 during training but tested up to 500 tokens to measure length generalization; the model decodes an output sequence (e.g., a classification label or transformed string). The stack operates on intermediate hidden states between Transformer layers rather than on the raw input, so the external I/O interface remains identical to a standard Transformer.

## Method

STACKTRANS inserts differentiable hidden state stacks between standard Transformer layers, leaving the attention mechanism untouched and preserving flash-attention compatibility. At each decoding step, a learned linear projection followed by softmax produces a probability distribution over three soft operations (push, pop, no-op), and the stack is updated by a continuous interpolation of the three candidate transitions (Eq. 2), making the entire process end-to-end differentiable. A differentiable stack mask tracks which positions are active, and a global reading mechanism—implemented as a learnable query-over-stack attention (Eq. 4)—replaces the standard top-only peek to stabilize training and enrich gradient flow. To improve expressiveness at low cost, the hidden state is down-projected into H independent low-rank subspaces (each of dimension d_s), each hosting its own stack, and the per-head read-outs are concatenated and up-projected back to the full hidden dimension. Training parallelism is maintained by processing the per-token layer sequence [h_{t,0}, …, h_{t,L}] independently across tokens, and an entropy-based regularization term prevents the operation probabilities from collapsing to a uniform distribution.

## Results

STACKTRANS-360M (0.36B params, ~1T tokens) achieves an average score of 44.3 across 9 natural language benchmarks, outperforming all baselines including models with 2–3× more parameters (OLMo-1.1B: 38.7, TinyLLaMA-1.1B: 37.9). The most dramatic gains appear on reasoning-heavy tasks: GSM8K (33.6 vs. 1.6–33.4 for baselines) and ARC (59.0 vs. 43.4–53.0). On formal language tasks, STACKTRANS reaches 100% accuracy on all three regular-expression tasks and 92–100% on deterministic context-free tasks, compared to 20–55% for the standard Transformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STACKTRANS-360M (0.36B, 1T tokens) achieves the highest average (44.3) among all six models, beating SmolLM (38.2), SmolLM2 (40.3), Qwen2.5-0.5B (40.1), OLMo-1.1B (38.7), and TinyLLaMA-1.1B (37.9). The largest margins are on compositional/reasoning tasks: GSM8K (33.6 vs. 1.6 for SmolLM and 1.8 for OLMo) and ARC (59.0 vs. 43.4 for TinyLLaMA). However, STACKTRANS does not win every individual benchmark: OLMo-1.1B edges it on HellaSwag (60.7 vs. 59.5), PIQA (75.2 vs. 71.7), CommonsenseQA (40.3 vs. 37.1), and OpenBookQA (38.0 vs. 37.5), while TinyLLaMA leads on Winogrande (55.7 vs. 52.8) and SmolLM2 on TriviaQA (16.9 vs. 11.2). On formal language tasks (Table 1), STACKTRANS achieves 100% on all RE tasks and 92–100% on DCF tasks, substantially outperforming the standard Transformer (20–55%) and matching or exceeding StackRNN and StackAttn. The key takeaway is that the 360M-parameter STACKTRANS, trained on only 1T tokens, matches or surpasses 1.1B-parameter models trained on 2–3T tokens on average, with particularly strong gains on tasks requiring recursive or hierarchical reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of stack-augmented neural networks (Joulin & Mikolov 2015; Grefenstette et al. 2015) from RNNs to the Transformer setting, and departs from recent stack-attention approaches (DuSell & Chiang 2024; Li et al. 2024) that embed stack operations within the attention computation. Instead, STACKTRANS inserts differentiable hidden-state stacks *between* standard Transformer layers, preserving the attention mechanism and maintaining compatibility with flash-attention. The design is directly inspired by pushdown automata from formal language theory, and the pretraining pipeline follows the OLMo framework using Dolma and Smoll corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STACKTRANS addresses a well-known theoretical gap in Transformers (inability to recognize regular expressions and deterministic context-free grammars) with a modular, low-overhead (~10%) architectural addition that does not require modifying attention. The claim that a 360M-parameter STACKTRANS outperforms open-source LLMs with 2–3× more parameters is practically significant if it holds at scale, but the evidence is limited to a single 360M model against a specific set of baselines. Open questions include whether the training-parallelism approximation (breaking temporal dependencies between tokens) degrades performance at larger scales, whether the benefits persist beyond 7B parameters, and whether the stack mechanism interacts favorably with other recent architectural innovations (e.g., MoE, longer contexts).
