## Problem

High-dimensional online calibration over arbitrary convex sets and norms lacked a unified algorithmic framework: prior algorithms either had exponential dependence on dimension (Foster-Vohra) or were tailored to specific settings like ℓ₁-calibration over the simplex (Peng 2025). This paper asks whether a single, norm-agnostic algorithm can achieve near-optimal calibration rates across all convex sets and norms simultaneously, and whether the analysis can be simplified by connecting calibration to swap regret minimization.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a sequence of adversarial outcomes y_t ∈ P sequentially, where P is any bounded convex set in ℝ^d, and outputs at each round a uniform distribution over L past empirical averages (one per level of an H-ary tree). Notably, the algorithm is completely identical regardless of the norm ‖·‖ or the convex set P — it requires no knowledge of the optimal regularizer, the dual norm, or any online linear optimization subroutine. The guarantee is that the ‖·‖-calibration error is at most εT after T = (diam(P)/ε)^{O(ρ/ε²)} rounds, where ρ is the optimal FTRL regularizer range.

## Method

The core insight is that the TreeCal algorithm (which assigns each node of an H-ary depth-L tree the average of past outcomes in its parent interval, and plays the uniform mixture over the root-to-leaf path) is exactly an instantiation of the TreeSwap swap-regret minimization algorithm with Follow-The-Leader as the subroutine. The analysis reduces calibration error to full swap regret via Bregman divergences (using a 1-strongly-convex regularizer R), then applies the TreeSwap bound by analyzing the unimplementable Be-The-Leader variant (which has zero external regret) and showing that FTL's predictions are close to BTL's in squared norm. This yields a squared-norm calibration bound of (diam(P)/√ε)^{O(ρ/ε)}, from which the linear-norm bound follows by Cauchy-Schwarz. The paper also proves a matching lower bound showing exponential dependence on 1/ε is necessary, via the classic reduction from calibration to swap regret.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.1) shows that TreeCal guarantees squared-norm calibration error Cal_T^{||·||²} ≤ εT for T ≥ (diam(P)/√ε)^{O(Rate(P,||·||)/ε)}. For the d-simplex with the ℓ₁ norm, this yields ε-calibration in d^{O(1/ε²)} rounds (recovering [Pen25]); for the ℓ₂ ball with the ℓ₂ norm, the bound is (1/ε)^{O(1/ε²)}, independent of dimension. The lower bound (Theorem 4.3) shows that any algorithm requires T ≥ exp(c·min{d^{1/14}, ε^{-1/6}}) rounds to achieve ℓ₁-calibration error εT on the simplex, strengthening the prior d^{Ω(log 1/ε)} lower bound of [Pen25] and establishing that exponential dependence on 1/ε is necessary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The primary comparison is to [Pen25] (Peng, 2025), which achieved ℓ₁-calibration on the simplex in d^{O(1/ε²)} rounds using a KL-divergence-based analysis tailored to that specific setting. TreeCal recovers this exact rate but via a unified swap-regret analysis that simultaneously handles arbitrary convex sets and norms, without requiring any OLO subroutine or knowledge of the optimal regularizer. On the lower bound side, [Pen25] showed d^{Ω(log 1/ε)} rounds are necessary; this paper strengthens that to exp(Ω(min{d^{1/14}, ε^{-1/6}})), proving that exponential dependence on 1/ε is unavoidable. The algorithm itself is nearly identical to [Pen25]'s (both use a tree of sub-forecasters with uniform mixtures), but the analysis via TreeSwap [DDFG24, PR24] and the Be-The-Leader trick is novel and enables the generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions TreeCal as a specific instantiation of the TreeSwap swap-regret minimization algorithm [DDFG24, PR24] with Follow-The-Leader as the subroutine, providing a unified analysis that recovers and generalizes the l1-simplex result of [Pen25] to arbitrary convex sets and norms. The calibration-to-swap-regret reduction via Bregman divergences generalizes ideas from [LSS25, FKO+25], while the lower bound leverages the classic FV98 reduction from calibration to swap regret combined with the swap-regret lower bound of [DFG+24]. The key conceptual departure from [Pen25] is that the analysis does not require any online linear optimization subroutine or knowledge of the optimal regularizer/rate ρ; the algorithm is identical for every (P, ||·||) pair, and the rate emerges purely from the analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, unified framework connecting high-dimensional calibration rates to online linear optimization rates, showing that the optimal calibration rate for any (P, ||·||) is governed by the OLO rate of the corresponding problem. The strengthened lower bound (exponential in 1/ε, versus the polynomial-in-log(1/ε) bound of [Pen25]) establishes that the exponential dependence on 1/ε is fundamentally necessary, closing a gap in the understanding of what is achievable. Open questions include whether the gap between the upper bound (exponential in ρ/ε²) and lower bound (exponential in ε^{-1/6}) can be closed, and whether the analysis extends to settings where the learner must play pure actions rather than distributions.
