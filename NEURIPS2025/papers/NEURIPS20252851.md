## Problem

The dominant backbone-plus-aggregator paradigm in visual place recognition (VPR) introduces structural complexity, performs one-shot aggregation without opportunity for refinement, and requires carefully designed aggregators (NetVLAD, SALAD, BoQ) that may lose positional or contextual information. This paper argues that in the transformer era, a dedicated aggregator is unnecessary because the intrinsic self-attention mechanism of the backbone can implicitly aggregate patch features into robust global descriptors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (224×224 for training, 322×322 for inference) and produces a 6144-dimensional global descriptor vector formed by concatenating 8 aggregation tokens (each 768-dim) from the final transformer block output, followed by L2 normalization. Unlike prior methods that append an explicit aggregation layer after the backbone, ImAge requires no additional network modules beyond the 8 learnable tokens (0.006M parameters), and the descriptor dimension is directly controlled by the number of aggregation tokens.

## Method

ImAge prepends M learnable aggregation tokens (M=8) to the patch token sequence at the junction between frozen and trainable transformer blocks (the 4th-to-last block for DINOv2-base-register), rather than at the input. In subsequent blocks, self-attention enables agg-patch interactions that progressively aggregate global context into the aggregation tokens, while agg-agg interactions refine their own representations. The tokens are initialized with L2-normalized k-means cluster centers computed from training descriptors, injecting a data-driven visual prior. Training uses multi-similarity loss with only the last four transformer blocks fine-tuned, avoiding the GPU memory overhead of backpropagating through frozen blocks that would occur if tokens were inserted at the first block. The final global descriptor is simply the L2-normalized concatenation of the 8 aggregation tokens from the last block output, eliminating any explicit aggregator entirely.

## Results

ImAge achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five main VPR benchmarks: 94.1% R@1 on Pitts30k, 94.5% R@1 on MSLS-val, 84.5% R@1 on MSLS-challenge (1st on the official leaderboard), 97.1% R@1 on Tokyo24/7, and 97.7% R@1 / 99.3% R@5 on Nordland. In the controlled comparison (Table 3, same DINOv2-base-register backbone, GSV-Cities training, and resolution), ImAge outperforms NetVLAD, SALAD, and BoQ on every dataset while using zero aggregator parameters (vs. 0.012M–8.6M), a smaller descriptor (6144-dim vs. 8448–12288-dim), and the fastest inference time (14.8 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImAge outperforms all 11 single-stage baselines on every dataset in the comprehensive comparison (Table 2). Against the closest competitors using DINOv2 backbones, ImAge leads BoQ by 0.4% R@1 on Pitts30k (94.1 vs. 93.7) and by 1.8% on MSLS-val (94.5 vs. 93.8); it surpasses EDTformer by 0.7% on Pitts30k (94.1 vs. 93.4) and matches it on Tokyo24/7 (97.1%); and it edges out SALAD-CM by 1.4% on MSLS-val (94.5 vs. 94.2) and by 1.8% on MSLS-challenge (84.5 vs. 82.7). In the controlled same-setup comparison (Table 3), ImAge beats NetVLAD by 1.2% R@1 on Pitts30k (94.0 vs. 92.8), SALAD by 1.5% on Pitts30k (94.0 vs. 92.5), and BoQ by 0.9% on Pitts30k (94.0 vs. 93.1), while also achieving the largest margin on Nordland (93.2 vs. 90.5/86.5/87.0). CricaVPR and SuperVLAP report slightly higher Pitts30k R@1 (94.9/95.0) but rely on a cross-image encoder that correlates multiple images from the same place, making them not directly comparable to single-image methods like ImAge. Overall, ImAge achieves the best or tied-best R@1 on all five main benchmarks without any explicit aggregator, using fewer parameters (0M aggregator params vs. 0.012M–8.6M) and the fastest inference (14.8 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant backbone-plus-aggregator paradigm in VPR (NetVLAD, SALAD, BoQ, EDTformer) by arguing that a dedicated aggregation module is unnecessary when using a transformer backbone. It draws conceptual inspiration from DINOv2-register tokens (which buffer global context but are discarded) and prompt tuning (which adds learnable tokens for adaptation), but repurposes the idea: a small set of aggregation tokens prepended mid-backbone directly form the global descriptor via the backbone's inherent self-attention. The k-means initialization strategy is borrowed from NetVLAD's cluster-center initialization, and the insertion-at-the-frozen/trainable-boundary strategy is a novel empirical finding specific to VPR fine-tuning practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that transformer self-attention subsumes the role of an explicit aggregator—simplifies the VPR pipeline and reduces parameter count and inference latency while matching or exceeding SOTA. This could influence how retrieval tasks more broadly are designed in the transformer era, potentially eliminating a whole class of hand-crafted aggregation modules. Key limitations include reduced effectiveness with weaker backbones (ViT-ImageNet, CLIP) and the requirement to fine-tune at least the last few blocks, which precludes fully frozen-model use cases like AnyLoc. The paper leaves open whether the approach generalizes to other modalities and retrieval tasks beyond VPR.
