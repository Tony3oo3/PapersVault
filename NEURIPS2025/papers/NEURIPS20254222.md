## Problem

While RL with verifiable rewards has proven effective for enhancing reasoning in LLMs and individual vision-language tasks, it remains unclear whether VLMs can compose independently acquired skills across modalities (text-to-visual transfer) and across reasoning domains (e.g., geometric + spatial) under out-of-distribution conditions. This paper systematically probes this compositional ability gap and finds that current post-training strategies, including RL, leave VLMs significantly underperforming on compositional multimodal tasks despite near-perfect individual task accuracy.

## Input / Output

Each diagnostic task exists in paired pure-text and multimodal variants with matched semantics: geometric reasoning takes either a text description of shapes with labeled dimensions or an image showing those shapes, and spatial reasoning takes either textual grid-position descriptions or a grid image with embedded colored shapes. The model must produce a reasoning trace (in a think block), optionally a visual caption (in a caption block for RL-Ground), and a final answer (area value or grid index) in an answer block. Compositional variants require integrating both skills in a single query, and OOD variants alter the objective (e.g., largest area instead of total area, farthest shape instead of nearest).

## Method

The paper introduces ComPABench, a controlled diagnostic benchmark with 4K training and 500 evaluation samples per task type, designed to isolate cross-modal transfer, cross-task skill composition, and OOD generalization. For training, it compares SFT, GRPO-based RL (following DeepSeek-R1), and SFT-initialized RL on Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct. The key proposed method, RL-Ground, adds two mechanisms to standard RL: (1) a mandatory <caption> block that forces the model to explicitly describe visual content in natural language before entering the reasoning stage, promoting early visual-to-text alignment; and (2) a fine-grained progress reward that provides dense supervision at intermediate vision-grounded subgoals (e.g., correct shape area computation or distance estimation) rather than relying solely on sparse final-answer correctness. This combination yields substantial gains in compositional accuracy (52.8% vs. 31.2% for standard RL on 7B) and OOD robustness, with ablations confirming that grounding fidelity is tightly correlated with compositional performance.

## Results

On the multimodal compositional task (7B), RL-Ground achieves 52.8% accuracy, substantially outperforming RL (31.2%), SFT (7.2%), SFT-init-RL (1.0%), and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (13.8%). On visual grounding subtasks, RL-Ground reaches 96.2% on Shape Area Grounding and 88.6% on Grid Position Grounding, the highest among all methods. For OOD compositional generalization, RL-Ground achieves 52.8% (7B) and 38% (3B), matching or exceeding its in-distribution performance, while SFT drops to 2.8% (7B). On the zero-shot Geometry3K benchmark, RL-Ground (trained only on synthetic data) achieves 21.2%, outperforming the base model (14.8%), SFT (9.2%), and RL-only (17.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four post-training strategies (SFT, RL via GRPO, SFT-initialized RL, and the proposed RL-Ground) against the unmodified Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct base model. RL-Ground decisively wins on the multimodal compositional task (52.8%), beating RL by 21.6 points, SFT by 45.6 points, and SFT-init-RL by 51.8 points. On Grid Position Grounding, RL-Ground (88.6%) surpasses RL (74.8%) and SFT (65.4%), confirming that explicit caption-before-thinking plus progress reward improves visual grounding. However, on Shape Area Grounding, standard RL (96.6%) marginally edges out RL-Ground (96.2%), suggesting the grounding gain is task-dependent. SFT-init-RL performs worst on compositional reasoning (1.0%), indicating that SFT initialization actively harms compositional flexibility by imposing rigid reasoning paths that RL cannot correct. On the zero-shot Geometry3K transfer test, RL-Ground (21.2%) outperforms all other strategies, demonstrating that the gains are not overfit to the synthetic ComPABench tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional-ability probing paradigm established for LLMs (Zhao et al. 2024, Xu et al. 2024b) to the vision-language setting, introducing cross-modal and cross-task dimensions that have no direct LLM analogue. Methodologically, it builds on the DeepSeek-R1 GRPO training recipe and the SFT-initialized RL hybrid strategy, adapting them to VLMs following the trajectory set by Vision-R1, MM-Eureka, and VLM-R1. However, it deliberately departs from prior VLM RL work—which has focused on individual task gains (visual math, object localization)—by isolating and measuring whether independently acquired skills actually compose, a question those papers leave unanswered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic diagnostic of where VLM compositional reasoning breaks down, showing that the gap is not merely a matter of training more data but a structural limitation of current post-training objectives. RL-Ground (caption-before-thinking + progress reward) is a lightweight, model-agnostic intervention that practitioners can adopt immediately, and its transfer to Geometry3K suggests broader applicability. Key limitations temper the impact: all experiments use synthetic geometric/spatial tasks on a single model family (Qwen2.5-VL) at 3B/7B scale, leaving open whether the findings hold for more complex real-world composition or larger models. The catastrophic SFT-induced forgetting of compositional ability and the counterintuitive failure of SFT-init RL on composition are important negative results that should reshape how the community sequences post-training stages.
