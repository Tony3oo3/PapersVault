## Problem

Current large vision-language models treat perception and generation as separate concerns: they either perform isolated object segmentation or use precomputed masks as mere conditional prompts for local editing, typically stitching together multiple disjointed models. No existing framework jointly optimizes segmentation-aware perception and region-controllable generation in a single end-to-end architecture, leaving a gap between high-level semantic understanding and fine-grained pixel-level editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251123/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FOCUS consumes RGB images or video frames at two simultaneous resolutions (256×256 for global semantics and 768×768 for fine-grained detail), natural-language instructions, and optional spatial annotations (clicks, boxes, scribbles, or masks); for video, annotations on a single frame suffice to drive full-clip editing. The model produces binary segmentation masks with category scores, high-fidelity edited or generated images (up to 1024×1024 via bucketed aspect-ratio cropping), free-text responses, and autoregressively predicted discrete visual tokens (semantic + pixel) that condition the diffusion decoder.

## Method

FOCUS couples a dual-branch visual encoder (QwenViT at 256×256 for global semantics and ConvNeXt-L at 768×768 for multi-scale local detail, fused via cross-attention) with a MoVQGAN-based tokenizer that discretizes images into semantic tokens (28× downsampling, 32K codebook) and pixel tokens (16× downsampling, 98K codebook). A Qwen2.5-3B LLM receives continuous pre-quantization features to mitigate information loss, and a gated cross-attention adapter injects three ConvNeXt scales (f2, f3, f4) into learnable queries for multi-scale perception. A Mask2Former decoder predicts masks that are downsampled and injected as spatial cross-attention conditions into an SDXL-initialized diffusion UNet, enabling region-level control during denoising. Training follows a progressive four-stage pipeline—tokenizer/diffusion pretraining, visual-language adapter warmup, multimodal pretraining with segmentation supervision, and instruction tuning for region-controlled editing—gradually increasing resolution from 256 to 1024 to balance generation quality against language understanding.

## Results

On multimodal understanding, FOCUS (3B) achieves 88.0 on POPE, 81.5 on MMBench, 73.9 on SEED, and 1570.3 on MME-P, matching or exceeding the larger Janus-Pro-7B (79.2 MMBench, 72.1 SEED, 1567.1 MME-P) while outperforming the same-scale ILLUME+ across all benchmarks. On image generation, FOCUS attains the best GenAI-bench Basic score (0.83) and GenEval Overall (0.75) among unified models, with an MJHQ-30K FID of 6.05 comparable to ILLUME+ (6.00). On referring segmentation, FOCUS reaches 86.3 mIoU on RefCOCO testA and 79.8 on RefCOCOg testA, competitive with the specialist HyperSeg (85.7/78.9). On the Emu Edit benchmark, FOCUS achieves the best DINO (0.831) and CLIP-T (0.278) scores among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among understanding-only models, FOCUS (3B) surpasses all baselines including LLaVA-NeXT (67.4 MMBench), Emu3-Chat (58.2 MMBench), and Qwen-VL-Chat (60.6 MMBench), confirming that the dual-branch encoder and segmentation-aware features boost perception even at a smaller parameter scale. Against unified understanding-and-generation models, FOCUS outperforms ILLUME+ (same 3B Qwen2.5 backbone) on every reported metric (e.g., 81.5 vs. 80.8 MMBench, 1570.3 vs. 1414.0 MME-P) and matches or exceeds the 7B Janus-Pro-7B on MMBench (81.5 vs. 79.2) and MMMU (50.2 vs. 50.0). On generation, FOCUS achieves the highest GenAI-bench Basic (0.83) and Advanced (0.72) scores, beating Janus-Pro-7B (0.80/0.69) and ILLUME+ (0.72/0.71), and leads on GenEval Overall (0.75) and Color Attribute (0.57) over all unified baselines. For referring segmentation, FOCUS is competitive with the specialist HyperSeg (84.1 vs. 84.8 RefCOCO Val mIoU) while outperforming PSALM (83.6) and LISA-7B (74.9) by a wide margin, and it leads on RefCOCOg testA (79.8 vs. 78.9 HyperSeg). On the Emu Edit editing benchmark, FOCUS achieves the best DINO (0.831) and CLIP-T (0.278) scores, edging out ILLUME+ (0.826/0.275) and substantially outperforming editing-only methods like Emu Edit (0.819/0.231) and MagicBrush (0.776/0.222), though Emu Edit retains a slight edge on CLIP-DIR (0.109 vs. 0.105).

## Relation to Prior Work

FOCUS extends the unified vision-language model line (Emu3, Janus, ILLUME+, VILA-U) by integrating a jointly-optimized segmentation decoder (Mask2Former-based) whose masks serve as spatial conditions for a diffusion decoder (initialized from SDXL), rather than treating segmentation as a separate preprocessing step or relying solely on text-driven control. The visual tokenizer draws on MoVQGAN's dual-branch quantization design, and the dual-branch encoder (QwenViT + ConvNeXt-L with gated cross-attention) addresses the resolution gap between global semantics and fine-grained spatial detail that prior unified models handle with a single encoder. The key conceptual departure from prior unified models is that segmentation masks are co-optimized with generation in an end-to-end pipeline, making pixel-level perception a first-class signal for spatially controllable editing rather than an auxiliary output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 3B-parameter model can match or exceed 7B unified models (Janus-Pro-7B, ILLUME+) across understanding, generation, and editing simultaneously, FOCUS suggests that architectural design (dual-branch encoding, continuous token injection, mask-conditioned diffusion) can partially substitute for parameter scale in unified multimodal systems. The ablation revealing that 1024-resolution training degrades language understanding (POPE drops ~10 points) due to increased hallucination highlights an open tension between visual fidelity and linguistic robustness that the field has not yet resolved. Limitations include the high computational cost (13 days for the 3B LLM training), reliance on SDXL initialization for the diffusion decoder, and the fact that video editing is demonstrated but not quantitatively benchmarked against dedicated video editing systems.
