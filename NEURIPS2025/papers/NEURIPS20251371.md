## Problem

Existing large-scale video diffusion models are too computationally expensive for real-time interactive applications that require responding to user inputs with low latency while generating coherent video over extended durations. Prior streaming approaches (diffusion forcing, step distillation) still require multiple denoising steps per frame chunk and suffer from error accumulation beyond a few seconds, limiting their use as interactive game engines or world simulators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251371/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a user-provided first frame (independently VAE-encoded), a text prompt, and optional per-frame interactive conditions (pose keypoints or Plücker camera embeddings). It autoregressively produces one latent frame (corresponding to 4 video frames) per forward pass at 24 fps, streaming results in real time at 736×416 on a single H100 or 1280×720 on 8×H100, with a latency of ~0.16 s per frame and support for continuous generation up to 60 s (1440 frames) without stopping or reprocessing context.

## Method

AAPT transforms a pre-trained 8B MMDiT video diffusion model into a one-step autoregressive generator via three sequential stages: (1) diffusion adaptation, which replaces bidirectional attention with block causal attention (visual tokens attend to text + past/current frames within a sliding window of N=30 latent frames) and adds a recycled-frame input channel; (2) consistency distillation to obtain a one-step initialization; and (3) adversarial training using an R3GAN/relativistic objective with a same-architecture discriminator that outputs per-frame logits for multi-duration discrimination. The key training insight is student-forcing: the generator recycles its own generated frames as inputs during training (matching inference exactly), which is shown to be critical for mitigating error accumulation over long videos. A segment-based long-video training strategy lets the discriminator evaluate overlapping 10-s segments of a 60-s generated video, bypassing the scarcity of long single-shot training data and enabling minute-long coherent generation.

## Results

On VBench-I2V 1440-frame (60-second) generation, AAPT achieves the best temporal quality (89.79) and frame quality (62.16) among all compared streaming methods, outperforming SkyReel-V2 (82.19/53.67) and MAGI-1 (80.79/60.01) by a wide margin. On 120-frame generation, AAPT achieves the best frame quality (66.58) and best I2V conditioning scores (98.60/99.36). In terms of speed, the 8B model generates 24fps at 736×416 on a single H100 with 0.16s latency (vs. CausVid's 9.4fps at 640×352 with 1.30s latency), and 24fps at 1280×720 on 8×H100, while supporting continuous 1440-frame streaming via KV cache without reprocessing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CausVid (the prior SOTA for fast streaming), AAPT is dramatically faster (0.16s vs 1.30s latency; 24.8 vs 9.4 fps) at higher resolution (736×416 vs 640×352), though CausVid reports a higher 120-frame temporal quality score (92.00 vs 89.51) which the authors attribute to its 12fps training data inflating the dynamic degree metric. On 1440-frame long-video generation, AAPT substantially outperforms both SkyReel-V2 and MAGI-1 in temporal quality (89.79 vs 82.19 and 80.79) and frame quality (62.16 vs 53.67 and 60.01), with the gap widening over time as baselines suffer error accumulation after 20–30 seconds. For pose-conditioned human video generation, AAPT ranks second in pose accuracy (AKD 2.740) behind OmniHuman-1 (2.136) but ahead of CyberHost (3.123), and is competitive in visual quality (FID 22.43, FVD 11.78). For camera-controlled world exploration, AAPT achieves new SOTA in FVD (61.33), translational error (0.1185), and appearance consistency (0.9012), while closely following CameraCtrl2 on movement strength and geometric consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Adversarial Post-Training (APT) [49] from one-shot video generation to the autoregressive, per-frame streaming setting, combining it with consistency distillation [83] and R3GAN [31] objectives within a block-causal DiT/MMDiT architecture [68]. Conceptually, it departs from the diffusion-forcing paradigm (CausVid [117], MAGI-1 [75], SkyReel-V2 [9]) by replacing multi-step denoising with a single forward pass per latent frame, and from token-level autoregressive video models (VideoPoet [43]) by generating an entire frame of tokens at once. The LLM-inspired causal architecture with KV cache is adapted from language modeling, but the key architectural distinction is frame-level (not token-level) autoregression with noise-sampled outputs rather than softmax token probabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time interactive video generation (game engines, world simulators, virtual humans) has been bottlenecked by the computational cost of diffusion models; achieving 24fps streaming at 736×416 on a single H100 with 0.16s latency opens the door to practical deployment. The student-forcing finding—that teacher-forcing adversarial training catastrophically fails at inference while student-forcing is essential—provides important guidance for future autoregressive generative models. Open questions include improving long-range subject/scene consistency (the sliding window and segment-based discriminator limit global coherence), reducing one-step generation artifacts that persist in the scene, and extending beyond the demonstrated 60-second limit (5-minute generation still produces artifacts).
