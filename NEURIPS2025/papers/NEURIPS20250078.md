## Problem

Prior work on Two-Stage Submodular Maximization (2SSM) assumes the collection of second-stage objectives is known a priori, leaving the online variant—where objectives are revealed sequentially and the restricted ground set must adapt without hindsight—as an open question. The central difficulty is that the first-stage objective (the expected second-stage reward) is neither submodular nor efficiently evaluable, so standard online submodular maximization techniques are inapplicable.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the algorithm receives a Weighted Threshold Potential (WTP) function f_t over a ground set of n elements (with at most G terms and parameters bounded by M) and must commit to an integral restricted ground set x_t of size at most ℓ before observing f_t. After f_t is revealed, a second-stage solution y_t is selected from the matroid M restricted to x_t (e.g., a uniform matroid of rank k < ℓ for cardinality constraints). The algorithm's only information at decision time is the history of previously revealed functions and past ground-set choices; it has no oracle access to the first-stage objective F_t(·), which is NP-hard to compute even after f_t is known.

## Method

RAOCO (Rounding-Augmented OCO) operates in two stages per round: (1) an OCO policy (FTRL or OGA) maintains a fractional restricted ground set x̃_t over the convex hull X̃_ℓ, using a concave relaxation F̃ of the first-stage objective whose supergradients are computable in polynomial time via a linear program for WTP functions; (2) Randomized Pipage Rounding converts x̃_t to an integral ground set x_t. The key technical contribution is proving that the distribution over second-stage solutions induced by pipage rounding satisfies submodular dominance, which—combined with state-of-the-art contention resolution schemes—yields the guarantee E[F(Ξ(x̃))] ≥ c_M(1−1/e)·F̃(x̃). This bridges the fractional OCO regret (O(ℓGM²√(nT))) to the integral setting, giving sublinear (1−1/e)²-regret under general matroids and the improved (1−1/e)(1−e^{−k}k^k/k!)-regret under uniform matroids of rank k.

## Results

Theoretically, RAOCO achieves sublinear (1−1/e)² ≈ 0.40 α-regret under general matroid constraints and sublinear (1−1/e)(1−e^{−k}k^k/k!) α-regret under cardinality constraints (Theorem 5.1), with regret scaling as O(ℓGM²√(nT)). Empirically, across all seven datasets (Wikipedia, Images, MovieRec, TeamFormation, Influence, Coverage, HotpotQA), the two-stage RAOCO variants (OGA, FTRL-L2, FTRL-H) outperform or match the one-stage baseline (1S-OGA) in cumulative average reward C_t. The performance gap is most pronounced on Influence, Images, and Coverage, where RAOCO achieves nearly double the average reward of 1S-OGA on Coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RAOCO against two offline baselines and one online baseline. Against OFLN-RGR (Stan et al. 2017, Replacement-Greedy), which achieves the state-of-the-art offline approximation of ½(1−1/e²) ≈ 0.43, RAOCO's online guarantee of (1−1/e)² ≈ 0.40 under matroid constraints is slightly weaker, but RAOCO operates online without prior knowledge of the objective sequence. Against OFLN-CO (Balkanski et al. 2016, Continuous-Optimization), RAOCO improves the offline approximation for cardinality constraints from 1−1/e−Ω(1/√k) to (1−1/e)(1−e^{−k}k^k/k!), which is strictly better for all finite k and converges to 1−1/e as k→∞. Against the one-stage online baseline 1S-OGA (which ignores the two-stage structure by setting k=ℓ), RAOCO consistently outperforms it across all seven datasets, with the gap being most dramatic on Coverage (nearly 2× reward), Influence, and Images. Notably, OFLN-RGR coincides with OPT in all experimental datasets, confirming that the offline state-of-the-art is essentially optimal on these instances, while RAOCO's online performance remains competitive without any lookahead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the Online Two-Stage Submodular Maximization (02SSM) problem, a natural online extension of the offline 2SSM formulation of Balkanski et al. [2016] and Stan et al. [2017], which they explicitly identify as an open question. Technically, the algorithm (RAOCO) builds on Si Salem et al. [2024]'s reduction of online submodular maximization to OCO for WTP functions, but departs critically because the first-stage objective F is neither submodular nor efficiently evaluable (it is NP-hard), ruling out standard online submodular maximization techniques. The rounding analysis combines Randomized Pipage Rounding [Chekuri et al., 2009] with contention resolution schemes under non-product distributions [Dughmi, 2020], exploiting a submodular dominance property of the induced distribution to handle dependencies introduced by pipage rounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first no-regret online algorithm for 2SSM, enabling adaptive ground-set selection in streaming recommender systems, team formation, and influence maximization without prior knowledge of the objective distribution. The improved offline approximation for cardinality constraints, (1−1/e)(1−e^{−k}k^k/k!), strictly dominates Balkanski et al.'s bound for all finite k and converges to the 1−1/e hardness threshold as k→∞. Key limitations are the restriction to WTP objectives (a proper subclass of monotone submodular functions) and full-information feedback; extending to general submodular functions or bandit settings remains open, as does closing the gap to the state-of-the-art offline matroid guarantee (0.40 vs. 0.43).
