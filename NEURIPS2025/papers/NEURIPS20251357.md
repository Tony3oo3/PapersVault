## Problem

Recursive training of diffusion models on their own synthetic outputs causes model collapse, but prior characterizations via variance shrinkage or distribution shift progress so slowly that they miss the rapid practical degradation in visual quality and diversity. This paper identifies a generalization-to-memorization transition as the operative collapse mechanism and shows it is driven by the declining entropy of the synthetic training data at each iteration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251357/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a candidate image pool (either 2N synthetic images from the previous iteration under the replace paradigm, or all accumulated real and synthetic images under the accumulate-subsample paradigm) and a target subset size N. Images are encoded via a frozen DINOv2 model into a feature space where pairwise L2 distances are computed. The output is a selected subset of N images with maximized nearest-neighbor distances (i.e., high KL-estimated entropy), which is then used to train the next-iteration UNet-based DDPM at 32×32 resolution.

## Method

The paper first establishes empirically that the generalization score (average nearest-neighbor distance between generated and training images) correlates linearly with the log-entropy of the training set (Pearson r = 0.91), and that entropy is the key driver of the generalization-to-memorization transition rather than variance or dataset size alone. Motivated by this, the selection objective is to maximize the Kozachenko-Leonenko entropy estimate of the chosen subset, which reduces to maximizing the sum of log nearest-neighbor distances. Two greedy approximations are proposed: (1) Greedy Selection, a farthest-point-sampling procedure that iteratively adds the candidate with the largest minimum distance to the current set, and (2) Threshold Decay Filter, a soft variant that admits all points exceeding a distance threshold τ and decays τ by a factor α when no point qualifies, providing tunable selection strength. Both operate in DINOv2 feature space and serve as plug-in data curation steps within the self-consuming loop, requiring no changes to the diffusion model architecture or training procedure.

## Results

Under the accumulate-subsample paradigm on CIFAR-10, Greedy Selection reduces the FID at iteration 8 from 75.7 (vanilla) to 44.7, a 41% improvement. On MNIST with classifier-free guidance (scale=2), augmenting CFG with the data selection filter drops FID from 66.2 to 22.4 at iteration 8, while also preserving image diversity. The Pearson correlation between training-set entropy and log(generalization score) is 0.91 (p ≈ 0), confirming entropy as a strong predictor of model generalizability. Greedy Selection selects 65% real images at iteration 8 on FFHQ versus only 12.5% under vanilla uniform subsampling, demonstrating that the method preferentially retains boundary (real) samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the vanilla replace and accumulate-subsample paradigms against their variants augmented with Greedy Selection or Threshold Decay Filter. Greedy Selection consistently outperforms both vanilla paradigms on generalization score across all three datasets (CIFAR-10, FFHQ, MNIST) and both paradigms. On FID under accumulate-subsample (CIFAR-10, iteration 8), Greedy Selection (44.7) beats Threshold Decay Filter and the vanilla baseline (75.7). However, on FFHQ under the replace paradigm, Threshold Decay Filter achieves better FID than Greedy Selection, suggesting adaptive selection strength can be advantageous in certain settings. For classifier-free guidance on MNIST, the filter-augmented CFG (FID 22.4) dramatically outperforms vanilla CFG (FID 66.2) and unconditional generation (FID 74.4), showing the method mitigates the well-known diversity collapse of CFG. In the ablation (Table 1), an intermediate filter ratio of 0.6–0.8 yields the best FID (38.2–40.0), while using all 2N images without filtering (ratio 1.0) degrades to FID 49.5, confirming that selective filtering is essential rather than simply increasing data volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the model collapse literature by reframing the problem from variance shrinkage or distribution shift (the dominant lenses in prior work by Shumailov et al., Dohmatob et al., and Kazdan et al.) to a generalization-to-memorization transition driven by declining training-set entropy. It builds on the generalization/memorization regime analysis of Zhang et al. (2024) and Yoon et al. (2023) for diffusion models, but extends it into the recursive self-consuming setting where dataset size is fixed yet information content decays. The proposed mitigation repurposes classical farthest-point sampling and threshold-based filtering for the specific objective of maximizing dataset-level entropy, distinguishing it from per-sample data pruning methods (Sorscher et al., Kolossov et al.) that target classification accuracy rather than generative diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically urgent concern: as AI-generated data inevitably contaminates future training sets, models that memorize training images raise copyright and diversity issues long before the theoretically predicted variance collapse manifests. The entropy-based selection method is a simple, plug-in technique that practitioners can adopt immediately, and the finding that it preferentially retains real images (65% vs. 12.5% baseline) offers an interpretable mechanism. However, the lack of a rigorous theoretical explanation for why entropy collapses, the computational cost of the greedy selection, and the limited experimental scale (32×32 images, ~16M parameters, ≤10 iterations) leave important open questions about whether these findings hold at the scale of modern foundation models or in the language modality.
