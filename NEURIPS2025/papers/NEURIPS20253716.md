## Problem

LVLMs built on discrete image tokenizers (e.g., Chameleon, Emu3, Janus-Pro) remain prone to object hallucination, yet existing mitigation methods such as contrastive decoding and latent editing perform poorly or inconsistently on these architectures. The paper identifies a previously unexplored root cause: co-occurrence patterns among discrete image tokens encode visual priors that cause the model to evoke tokens absent from the current image but frequently co-occurring with present ones, thereby hallucinating objects that are not visually grounded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253716/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an image quantized into a sequence of discrete tokens from a VQ-VAE/VQGAN codebook (≈8,192 entries) concatenated with a text prompt, and produces a hallucination-reduced text description via autoregressive decoding. CGC is computed offline once per model using COCO 2017 Panoptic segmentation masks, while VTD modifies only the hidden representations of image-token positions (not text tokens) at a single intermediate transformer layer (21–27 depending on model) during generation. The editing is parameterized by a layer index l and a magnitude coefficient γ, both tuned per model via grid search on an AMBER subset.

## Method

The framework comprises two components: Context-Guided Clustering (CGC) and Visual Token Decontamination (VTD). CGC builds a co-occurrence graph over codebook entries where edge weights combine spatial proximity (tokens within 3×3 grid cells) and semantic coherence (tokens sharing a panoptic segmentation mask), retaining only the top 10% of connections; a 2-layer GNN is then trained with an InfoNCE contrastive loss weighted by edge strength plus a positive-pair similarity loss that enforces absolute similarity thresholds, and the resulting node embeddings are clustered via K-means into ~10 groups. VTD, applied at inference, identifies the dominant clusters in the input image, selects tokens from those clusters that are absent from the image, and subtracts their projected embeddings from the hidden representations of all present image tokens at layer l, scaled by γ (Eq. 4), directly suppressing the latent influence of hallucination-prone tokens. The key empirical finding motivating this design is that absent tokens from dominant clusters show 5–10% higher HitRate@K correlation with hallucinated objects than present tokens or tokens from non-dominant clusters, and CGC outperforms naive K-means clustering on this metric. The method is complementary to existing approaches (OPERA, SID, PROJECTAWAY) and achieves the best efficiency among all baselines—5× faster than OPERA with the lowest GPU memory footprint.

## Results

CGC+VTD achieves the best performance in 22 out of 27 metrics (top-2 in 25) across three LVLMs (Chameleon-7B, Emu3-13B, Janus-Pro-7B) and three benchmarks. On Object HalBench with Janus-Pro-7B, CHAIR-s drops from 19.33 (Nucleus Sampling) to 10.43 and CHAIR-i from 10.68 to 5.81. On AMBER with Chameleon-7B, CHAIR improves by 5.46 points and discriminative accuracy by 2.98 points over the baseline. The method is also the most efficient: 188s inference time (5× faster than OPERA at 941s) and 14,478 MB GPU memory (lowest among all methods).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGC+VTD outperforms all four baselines on Object HalBench across all three models. On Chameleon-7B, it reduces CHAIR-s from 39.56 (OPERA, the strongest baseline) to 38.58 and CHAIR-i from 25.26 to 23.69. The gap is most dramatic on Janus-Pro-7B, where CGC+VTD achieves CHAIR-s of 10.43 versus 18.52–19.90 for all baselines, and CHAIR-i of 5.81 versus 10.50–10.68. Contrastive decoding methods (VCD, SID) consistently underperform even Nucleus Sampling on generative tasks, confirming their incompatibility with discrete image token spaces. PROJECTAWAY and OPERA show moderate improvements over the baseline but are significantly outperformed by CGC+VTD, particularly on instance-level hallucination (CHAIR-i). The Cluster-Base ablation (K-means without CGC) shows no consistent improvement, validating that context-guided clustering is essential for capturing meaningful visual priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept of object co-occurrence priors—previously studied in the language modality (LURE [15], [16])—to the visual modality of discrete image tokenizers, hypothesizing that codebook token co-occurrence patterns create analogous 'visual priors' that drive hallucination. The latent editing mechanism in VTD is directly inspired by PROJECTAWAY [6], which subtracts projections of hallucinative text token embeddings from intermediate hidden states; here, the subtraction targets visually absent image tokens identified via a novel GNN-based clustering pipeline (CGC). The paper explicitly departs from contrastive decoding methods (VCD, SID) and OPERA, showing empirically that these approaches are incompatible or suboptimal for discrete-tokenizer LVLMs where visual and text tokens share a unified vocabulary space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to identify and quantify visual priors from discrete image token co-occurrence as a distinct hallucination source, providing a mechanistic explanation for why these models hallucinate that is orthogonal to language-modeling biases. The method is practical: it requires no retraining, operates at inference time, and is 5× faster than OPERA while achieving better hallucination reduction across three models and three benchmarks. Key limitations include applicability only to discrete-tokenizer LVLMs (not continuous-feature models like LLaVA), a slight trade-off in object coverage, and the need for per-model hyperparameter tuning of editing layer and magnitude. An open question is whether the co-occurrence graph construction (currently based on COCO Panoptic segmentation) generalizes to open-world or non-COCO visual domains.
