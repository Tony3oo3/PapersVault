## Problem

LLM API providers face a diverse model zoo where large or reasoning-heavy models are often overkill for simple queries, inflating costs unnecessarily. Existing routing approaches either require pre-inference on all candidate models (introducing latency), fix the model zoo during training (preventing adaptation to new models), or tightly couple router parameters to specific experts (requiring costly retraining). The paper asks whether LLM routing can be reframed as a recommendation system problem that learns a generalizable suitability signal without any pre-inference overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251127/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The router consumes a user instruction (natural language text), a user-specified inference mode (direct, self-consistency, CoT, or Tree-of-Thought), and a cost threshold discretized into five levels, alongside a candidate zoo of 52 LLMs spanning <10B to ~70B parameters. It outputs a single routed LLM selection per query, with the cost threshold treated as a dynamic input feature rather than a hard constraint, so the same trained router adapts to different budget levels without retraining. Model representations combine intrinsic attributes (architecture, scale, training details) with capability distributions from both published benchmarks and a 1,415-instruction online coreset, while query representations fuse three semantic encoders (GTE_Large, Qwen2.5-0.5B, RoBERTa-Large) with meta-dimensional annotations (difficulty, reasoning level, domain) extracted via Qwen2.5-7B.

## Method

LLMRec reframes LLM routing as a RecSys ranking task where queries map to users and LLMs to items, with the core insight that an LLM's suitability for a query is a complex latent signal analogous to user-item preference. The framework engineers four feature groups—model (intrinsic + capability), query (semantic + meta-dimensional), context (inference type + cost level), and behavioral sequences (model performance on the coreset and on top-k nearest training neighbors of the current query)—then learns their high-order interactions using RecSys kernels such as Wide&Deep, DeepFM, AFM, and DIN. The DIN-inspired component is particularly important: it applies attention over a model's historical performance sequences, dynamically weighting which past behaviors are most relevant to the current query to produce a query-aware capability representation. Training is supervised on over 1 billion model-query pairs from 50+ LLM families across 30+ benchmarks, with ground-truth labels generated by a Pareto-based ranking that sorts correct-answer models by ascending cost and shuffles incorrect ones last, conditioned on the cost threshold context. This design enables zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets and seamless onboarding of new LLMs via lightweight coreset evaluation rather than full retraining.

## Results

On general and commonsense reasoning tasks (Table 1), LLMRec with the DIN kernel achieves a mean accuracy of 75.52 across MMLU, TruthfulQA, ARC-Challenge, and MMLU-stem, surpassing the best large-scale LLM (Mixtral-8x22B at 72.56) and the best small-scale LLM (Phi-3 at 70.17) while routing to an average ~31B model. On math and symbolic reasoning (Table 2), DIN reaches 78.20 mean accuracy with 473 average output tokens, compared to 79.39/564 tokens for the best large-scale LLM, yielding roughly 30% fewer tokens. Across all six evaluation datasets, LLMRec achieves an average cost reduction of over 38% while maintaining or exceeding the accuracy of the strongest single LLM in the zoo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLMRec (DIN) decisively outperforms both embedding-matching and classical-ML baselines: GTE_Large achieves only 68.64 mean accuracy (vs. 75.52 for DIN, a 6.9-point gap) while routing to a larger average model (~55B vs. ~31B), and LR trails at 66.76 mean (an 8.8-point gap). Among RecSys-kernel variants, DIN leads with 75.52, narrowly ahead of Deep & Wide (74.70) and DeepFM (74.69), while AFM lags at 72.82. On math and symbolic reasoning (Table 2), DIN reaches 78.20 mean accuracy with 473 average tokens, beating GTE_Large (71.57/554) and LR (70.71/495) by 6.6 and 7.5 points respectively, while also reducing token usage by ~15–17%. Critically, the best routing method (DIN at ~31B average scale) surpasses the best single large-scale LLM (Mixtral at 72.56 mean on Table 1) by nearly 3 points, demonstrating that intelligent routing to a mix of smaller models can outperform always using the largest available model.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLMRec departs from prior LLM routing work (AutoMix, RouteLLM, Hybrid LLM, GraphRouter) that either requires pre-inference on all candidate models, fixes the model zoo during training, or tightly couples the router to model parameters (as in MoE). Its core conceptual move is to import the full RecSys feature-engineering and interaction-learning paradigm—Wide & Deep, DeepFM, AFM, and especially DIN's attention-based behavioral sequence modeling—into the routing setting, treating queries as users, LLMs as items, and suitability as a latent preference signal. The DIN-based behavioral sequence (model performance on a coreset and on top-k nearest training neighbors of the current query) is the most direct architectural inheritance, repurposing user-history attention to capture query-aware model capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 38% average cost reduction while preserving accuracy is practically significant for LLM API providers managing multi-million-dollar monthly inference bills, and the Pareto-frontier-under-cost-budget metric offers a more decision-relevant evaluation than accuracy-only comparisons. However, the paper approximates cost via parameter scale rather than actual API pricing, and the cold-start problem for newly released models (requiring coreset evaluation before routing) remains an open practical challenge. The RecSys framing also raises questions about how well it extends to agentic or multi-turn settings where the 'query' is a trajectory rather than a single instruction, and whether the feature interactions learned on 2024-era model zoos transfer as the landscape shifts toward reasoning-heavy RLVR models.
