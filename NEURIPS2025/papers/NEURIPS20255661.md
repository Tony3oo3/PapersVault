## Problem

Pose-free novel view synthesis from monocular videos of dynamic scenes remains unsolved: existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods depend on pre-computed camera poses from COLMAP or multi-view rigs that fail under significant motion, and naively pairing MonST3R's pose predictions with 4D-GS yields poor reconstruction because dynamic objects dominate the RANSAC inlier set. The paper identifies that motion segmentation and scene reconstruction must be jointly optimized rather than treated as isolated stages to handle scenes where moving objects occupy a large image fraction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single monocular RGB video sequence (no pre-computed poses, intrinsics, or multi-view captures) and produces a full 4D scene representation (512 control points + Gaussian primitives), per-frame camera extrinsics, dense depth maps, and binary dynamic-object masks. Novel views are rendered at 960×540 resolution at 45 FPS on a single RTX 3090, with the entire scene stored in 80 MB. The pipeline is fully pose-free, eliminating the COLMAP dependency that causes failures in 5 out of 14 MPI Sintel sequences.

## Method

4D3R is a three-module pipeline. First, a MonST3R-based ViT encoder-decoder extracts per-pixel scene coordinate maps and confidence maps, from which high-confidence 3D points are filtered; these points serve as K-point prompts for SAM2 to produce pixel-precise dynamic masks. Second, the Motion-Aware Bundle Adjustment (MA-BA) module restricts PnP-RANSAC to static-region points only (mask = 0), then refines poses via a differentiable dense bundle adjustment layer that incorporates SEA-RAFT optical flow with static-region weighting. Third, the Motion-Aware Gaussian Splatting (MA-GS) representation uses 512 sparse control points with RBF kernels and a deformation-field MLP that outputs per-time SE(3) transforms; Gaussian positions and orientations are deformed via Linear Blend Skinning with normalized exponential weights, and a gradient-detached path prevents competing optimization between the MLP and Gaussian parameters. Training is two-stage: control points in dynamic regions are optimized first with a masked photometric loss, then Gaussian parameters are refined under ARAP and static-rigidity regularizers, with adaptive control-point densification guided by gradient magnitude.

## Results

On the HyperNeRF dataset, 4D3R achieves 25.6 dB PSNR and 0.844 MS-SSIM without pre-computed camera poses, outperforming the best COLMAP-dependent method SC-GS (25.3 dB / 0.841) and 4DGS (25.2 dB / 0.845) while training in only 50 minutes versus 4–5 hours. On the more challenging DyNeRF dataset, the method reaches 19.6 dB PSNR and 0.755 MS-SSIM, surpassing all baselines including pose-free RoDynRF (17.8 dB) and the MonST3R+SC-GS combination (16.4 dB). For camera pose estimation on MPI Sintel, 4D3R achieves the best ATE of 0.086 and RPE trans of 0.035, matching or exceeding specialized pose estimation methods like LEAP-VO (ATE 0.089) and MonST3R (ATE 0.108). The method also reduces storage to 80 MB and maintains 45 FPS rendering, compared to 153–200 MB and <1 FPS for competing pose-free approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against COLMAP-dependent dynamic scene methods, 4D3R (25.6 dB) surpasses SC-GS (25.3 dB) and 4DGS (25.2 dB) on HyperNeRF PSNR while being 4–6× faster to train. Against pose-free baselines, the advantage is more pronounced: RoDynRF (23.8 dB) and MonST3R+SC-GS (20.4 dB) trail by 1.8 dB and 5.2 dB respectively, with the gap widening on DyNeRF (19.6 vs. 17.8 and 16.4 dB). For pose estimation on MPI Sintel, 4D3R achieves the lowest ATE (0.086) and RPE trans (0.035), beating LEAP-VO (0.089 / 0.066), MonST3R (0.108 / 0.042), and RoDynRF (0.089 / 0.073), while also outperforming traditional SfM-based methods like DROID-SLAM (ATE 0.175) and BARF (ATE 0.447). Notably, COLMAP itself fails to produce poses in 5 out of 14 MPI Sintel sequences, underscoring the practical advantage of the pose-free pipeline. The method matches SC-GS in rendering speed (45 FPS) and storage (80 vs. 85 MB) while eliminating the COLMAP dependency entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: pose-free neural rendering (NeRF-, BARF, InstantSplat, ZeroGS), dynamic scene reconstruction via 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS, SC-GS), and transformer-based geometric estimation (DUSt3R, MonST3R). It departs from prior pose-free methods by extending them from static to dynamic scenes, and from prior 4D-GS methods by eliminating the COLMAP dependency. The key architectural departure is the joint optimization of motion segmentation (via MonST3R + SAM2) and camera pose refinement through a Motion-Aware Bundle Adjustment module, rather than treating pose estimation and scene reconstruction as sequential, independent stages. The MA-GS representation builds on SC-GS's sparse control points but adds motion-mask-guided initialization and gradient-detached LBS to decouple deformation learning from Gaussian parameter optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that truly pose-free dynamic scene reconstruction from a single monocular video is practically achievable with competitive quality, removing the COLMAP bottleneck that has limited real-world deployment of 4D-GS methods. The 5× training speedup and 80MB storage footprint make the approach viable for longer sequences and resource-constrained settings. However, the method still requires textured frames with sufficient static regions and distinguishable dynamic objects, and struggles with complex non-rigid deformations. Open questions include whether temporal consistency constraints can further improve long-sequence rendering, whether self-supervised segmentation can reduce dependence on SAM2, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-object or occluded dynamic scenes.
