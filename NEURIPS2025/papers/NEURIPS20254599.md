## Problem

Offline Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning struggles to estimate accurate Goal-Conditioned Value Functions (GCVFs) from datasets with limited state-action coverage, leading to poor generalization to unseen state-goal pairs and failure in long-horizon or large-scale navigation tasks. Existing value learning methods lack structural inductive biases that encode the geometric cost-to-go structure inherent in navigation problems, causing learned value functions to ignore obstacle constraints and maze topology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254599/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The value function network takes a state vector s and a goal vector g (both continuous, with goal space equal to state space) and outputs a scalar GCVF estimate. The gradient ∇_s V_θ(s,g) is computed via automatic differentiation through the network. The hierarchical actor then consumes the GCVF to produce a high-level subgoal (a state prediction) and a low-level continuous action, with the full pipeline operating entirely on offline trajectory data without environment interaction.

## Method

The core contribution is an Eikonal PDE-derived regularizer, (‖∇_s V_θ(s,g)‖ · S(s) − 1)², added to the standard expectile regression loss for GCVF estimation. This regularizer enforces a distance-like (1-Lipschitz) structure on the value function, grounded in the connection between the Eikonal PDE and the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation established via Proposition 4.1, which shows the Hamiltonian is upper-bounded by an Eikonal-like residual. The method is integrated into HIQL's action-free implicit Q-learning framework, forming Eik-HIQL, where the regularized GCVF drives a hierarchical actor (subgoal predictor + low-level policy) trained via advantage-weighted regression. The speed profile S(s)=1 is used uniformly, requiring no privileged obstacle information, and the gradient is computed via JAX's automatic differentiation. The regularizer is broadly compatible with any temporal-difference-based value learning algorithm, as demonstrated with IQL and IVL variants.

## Results

On the OGbench benchmark (31 tasks), Eik-HIQL achieves the highest success rate in the most challenging settings, with gains exceeding 100% over HIQL in 7 of 31 tasks. Headline results include antmaze-stitch-giant (48±11% vs. HIQL 3±3%, QRL 2±2%, CRL 0±0%), humanoidmaze-navigate-giant (68±5% vs. HIQL 18±5%, QRL 1±1%, CRL 4±2%), and pointmaze-stitch-giant (22±10% vs. HIQL 1±4%). In contact-rich domains (antsoccer, manipulation), Eik-HIQL performs comparably to or slightly below baselines, e.g., antsoccer-navigate-arena (19±2% vs. HIQL 60±4%) and cube-single-play (25±1% vs. HIQL 31±4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HIQL (the direct ablation baseline), Eik-HIQL wins decisively on large-scale navigation and stitching tasks: antmaze-stitch-giant improves from 3±3% to 48±11% (16×), humanoidmaze-navigate-giant from 18±5% to 68±5% (3.8×), and pointmaze-stitch-giant from 1±4% to 22±10%. However, Eik-HIQL underperforms HIQL on antsoccer (e.g., 19±2% vs. 60±4% on navigate-arena) and cube manipulation (25±1% vs. 31±4%), where contact-rich, non-Lipschitz dynamics violate the smoothness assumption underlying the Eikonal regularizer. Against QRL, Eik-HIQL dominates on high-dimensional and stitched tasks (antmaze-stitch-giant: 48% vs. 2%; humanoidmaze-stitch-giant: 19% vs. 0%) but QRL retains an edge on simpler pointmaze variants (pointmaze-navigate-large: 90% vs. 83%; pointmaze-stitch-large: 85% vs. 73%). Against CRL, Eik-HIQL is broadly superior, with CRL competitive only on a few navigate variants (e.g., pointmaze-navigate-teleport: 50% vs. 47%; antmaze-navigate-teleport: 55% vs. 52%) but collapsing to near-zero on most stitching and high-dimensional tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends HIQL (Park et al., 2024), a state-of-the-art offline GCRL algorithm combining hierarchical actors with action-free implicit Q-learning, by adding an Eikonal PDE-based regularizer to the GCVF estimation loss. The theoretical grounding draws on the connection between the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation and the Eikonal PDE (Proposition 4.1), positioning the regularizer as a model-free simplification of the HJB-based approach of Lien et al. (2024) that avoids requiring explicit dynamics. Unlike prior gradient-norm penalties used in GANs or model-based RL for training stability, the Eikonal residual here is explicitly designed as a geometric inductive bias that enforces a distance-like cost-to-go structure on the value function, and its application to offline GCRL is, to the authors' knowledge, the first.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Eikonal regularizer offers a simple, broadly compatible drop-in modification for any temporal-difference-based GCRL pipeline, with demonstrated gains across IQL, IVL, and HIQL backbones. Its practical impact is strongest in large-scale navigation and trajectory stitching, where it yields >100% improvements over unregularized HIQL, addressing a key bottleneck in offline GCRL generalization. However, the regularizer's reliance on Lipschitz continuity assumptions limits its effectiveness in contact-rich, interactive tasks (antsoccer, manipulation), where non-smooth dynamics violate the underlying smoothness premise. Open questions include designing task-adaptive speed profiles for safe GCRL, extending to multi-agent settings, and developing representation learning mechanisms that can handle the hybrid dynamics of object interaction.
