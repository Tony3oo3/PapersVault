## Problem

Prior work on episodic MDPs with aggregate bandit feedback (where only the cumulative episode loss is observed, not per-step losses) has focused exclusively on worst-case adversarial regret bounds of O(√T). No algorithm was known to achieve instance-dependent O(polylog T) regret in stochastic environments under this feedback model, leaving a gap in best-of-both-worlds (BOBW) guarantees for this practically important setting.

## Input / Output

The learner interacts with a layered MDP (L layers, |S| states, |A| actions) and at each episode observes only a single scalar aggregate loss c_t ∈ [0,1] equal to the sum of per-step losses along the trajectory, plus the state-action trajectory itself. The output is a policy π: S→A selected each episode, with the guarantee that regret is O(log T) in stochastic regimes (with gap-dependent constants) and Õ(√T) in adversarial regimes, without the algorithm needing to know which regime it is in. For unknown transitions, the algorithm also maintains empirical transition estimates and confidence bounds updated in an epoch-based fashion.

## Method

The algorithm applies FTRL over the occupancy-measure polytope (the convex hull of all policy-induced occupancy measures) rather than over policies directly, using two regularizer choices: a Tsallis-entropy hybrid with a small log-barrier term, and a pure log-barrier with adaptive per-coordinate learning rates. The key technical contribution is a novel loss estimator, inspired by Maiti et al.'s shortest-path estimator, that constructs an unbiased estimate of the per-state-action loss from only the scalar aggregate feedback, with a second moment bounded by (1−π(a|s))/q(s,a) — the (1−π(a|s)) factor is critical for self-bounding. For unknown transitions, the paper designs an optimistic variant of this estimator that remains valid despite containing negative terms, avoiding the loss-shifting trick required by prior work and yielding improved dependence on the horizon L. The stochastic regret bound follows from a self-bounding argument that converts the gap-dependent upper bound into O(U + √(UC)) form, where U is the gap-dependent quantity and C is the corruption level.

## Results

For episodic MDPs with known transitions, the proposed Tsallis-entropy algorithm achieves gap-dependent stochastic regret of $\sum_{s\neq s_L, a\neq\pi^*(s)} \frac{\log T}{\Delta(s,a)} + \frac{L|S|\log T}{\Delta_{\min}}$ and adversarial regret of $\sqrt{|S||A|LT}$, while the log-barrier variant achieves stochastic regret of $\sum_{s\neq s_L, a\neq\pi^*(s)} \frac{\log T}{\Delta(s,a)}$ and adversarial regret of $\sqrt{|S||A|LT\log T}$. Both bounds are shown to be tight: the log-barrier stochastic bound matches the new individual-gap-dependent lower bound, and the adversarial bound matches the known lower bound of $\sqrt{|S||A|LT}$. For unknown transitions, the algorithm achieves stochastic regret of $\frac{(|S||A|)^{O(1)}\log^2\iota}{\Delta_{\min}}$ and adversarial regret of $\sqrt{(|A|+L)|S|^2|A|LT\log^2\iota}$, all with efficient (polynomial-time) computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Bubeck et al. [2012] and Lancewicki & Mansour [2025], which both achieve only worst-case $\sqrt{T}$-type regret in both stochastic and adversarial settings, this paper's algorithms achieve gap-dependent $O(\log T)$ stochastic regret, a fundamental improvement when the environment is stochastic. Against Dann et al. [2023] and Ito et al. [2024], which provide a gap-dependent stochastic bound of $\frac{|S|^2|A|\log|A|\log T}{\Delta_{\min}}$ (depending only on the minimum gap) and adversarial regret $\sqrt{|S|^2|A|T\log|A|}$, this paper improves the stochastic bound to a per-state-action sum $\sum \frac{\log T}{\Delta(s,a)}$ (tighter when gaps are heterogeneous) and removes the $\log|A|$ factor from the adversarial bound, achieving the matching lower bound $\sqrt{|S||A|LT}$. The log-barrier variant's stochastic bound is provably tight, matching the new lower bound established in this paper. All proposed algorithms are computationally efficient (solvable via convex optimization), unlike the linear-bandit reduction approaches of Bubeck et al. and Dann et al. which involve exponentially many arms. For unknown transitions, this is the first BOBW algorithm in the aggregate bandit feedback setting, improving upon Efroni et al. [2021] and Cohen et al. [2021] which only provide worst-case $\sqrt{T}$ bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BOBW framework from Jin et al. [2021] (episodic MDPs with individual feedback and unknown transitions) to the more challenging aggregate bandit feedback setting, where only a single scalar loss is observed per episode. The core loss estimator is adapted from Maiti et al. [2025]'s online shortest path algorithm, and the self-bounding analysis draws on Zimmert and Seldin [2021] (Tsallis-INF) and Wei and Luo [2018] (adaptive log-barrier). A key departure from prior BOBW MDP work is that the proposed estimator naturally carries a (1−π(a|s)) factor in its second moment, eliminating the technically involved loss-shifting trick required by Jin et al. [2021, 2023].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to achieve instance-dependent O(log T) stochastic regret and Õ(√T) adversarial regret simultaneously for episodic MDPs with aggregate bandit feedback, with matching lower bounds in the known-transition case. The practical motivation (healthcare treatment sequences, educational curricula) is compelling, but the tabular MDP assumption and per-round convex optimization limit immediate applicability. Open questions include extending to linear MDPs or function approximation, replacing FTRL with policy optimization for better scalability, and generalizing beyond layered MDPs to stochastic shortest path problems.
