## Problem

Graph Transformers have achieved strong node classification results through two dominant strategies—integrating GNN blocks or modulating node attributes with positional encodings—but the shared mechanism underlying their effectiveness remained unexplored. This paper asks what unifying principle drives these diverse architectures and how to design a GT that is both effective and computationally efficient without inheriting GNN limitations or incurring the overhead of positional encodings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254566/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse graph adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} and a node attribute matrix X ∈ R^{n×f}, processing them through separate MLP projection layers to produce initial topology and attribute representations of shape R^{n×d}. The final output is a per-node class probability vector obtained by softmax over an MLP applied to a weighted combination (controlled by scalar α) of the updated topology and attribute representations. Training operates under a semi-supervised paradigm where only a subset of node labels is available, and the loss combines cross-entropy on labeled nodes with a consistency regularization term.

## Method

UGCFormer implements a cross-aggregation mechanism via a linearized Dual Cross-Attention (DCA) module that interactively updates topology and attribute representations. The DCA module reorders attention computation to Q(K^T V) for linear complexity, shares parameters between Query and Key (both derived from the same source space to ensure cross-space interaction), and normalizes via Frobenius norms. Topology representations are updated by blending a spectral-clustering term (Ã V) with the cross-attention output, while attribute representations blend their initial MLP projection with the reciprocal cross-attention output, each governed by learnable Tanh-scaled scalars. A consistency constraint—sharpening pseudo-labels derived from averaging the two representations and penalizing L2 deviation—prevents overfitting from the dual-channel design. Theoretically, the DCA update is shown to be equivalent to solving a convex objective combining a spectral clustering term, an attribute fidelity term, and an HSIC-based dependence measure between topology and attribute representations.

On homophilic graphs (Table 1), UGCFormer achieves an average accuracy of 86.66% across 7 datasets, outperforming the best baseline Polynormer (85.85%) by 0.81% and attaining the best rank (1.14 vs. 4.71). On heterophilic graphs (Table 2), UGCFormer reaches 61.83% average accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline SGFormer (60.29%) by 1.54%. On large-scale graphs (Table 3), UGCFormer achieves 79.95% ROC-AUC on ogbn-proteins and 74.02% accuracy on ogbn-arxiv, both state-of-the-art among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGCFormer outperforms all 15 baselines (7 GNNs + 8 GTs) on 6 of 7 homophilic datasets, with the largest margin on PubMed (+2.55% over Polynormer) and CiteSeer (+1.10% over Polynormer). Among GT baselines, Polynormer and SGFormer are the closest competitors, but UGCFormer still leads by 0.81% and 1.52% in average accuracy respectively. On heterophilic graphs, UGCFormer wins on 5 of 7 datasets, with a notable +1.96% advantage over GOAT on Cornell; however, SGFormer and Polynormer remain competitive on smaller graphs like Texas and Wisconsin. On large-scale ogbn-arxiv, UGCFormer achieves 74.02% accuracy, exceeding Polynormer (73.46%) and SGFormer (72.63%), while on ogbn-proteins it reaches 79.95% ROC-AUC vs. SGFormer's 79.53%. Compared to LINKX—which also processes topology and attributes separately but without cross-interaction—UGCFormer consistently wins across all 14 small/medium datasets, validating the cross-aggregation mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two dominant Graph Transformer (GT) design strategies: integrating GNN blocks (as in GraphGPS, NAGphormer, SGFormer) and modulating node attributes with Positional Encodings (as in NodeFormer, Exphormer, GOAT). Its key conceptual departure is showing that both strategies share a unified underlying mechanism—cross-aggregation between topology and attribute representations—thereby reframing the design space of GTs around this single principle. Architecturally, UGCFormer builds on linearized attention (Katharopoulos et al., ICML 2020) and the single-layer self-attention design of SGFormer (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2023), but replaces self-attention with a parameter-shared dual cross-attention module that explicitly models inter-source interactions rather than intra-source ones. Unlike LINKX, which also processes topology and attributes separately, UGCFormer introduces an explicit interaction mechanism (cross-attention) plus a consistency constraint to prevent representation distortion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-aggregation framework provides a unifying theoretical lens that could guide future GT design beyond the two currently dominant paradigms, potentially simplifying the architecture search space. Practically, UGCFormer offers a linear-complexity GT that consistently outperforms 14 baselines across 16 benchmarks (including large-scale ogbn-arxiv and ogbn-proteins), making it a strong default choice for node classification. However, the paper acknowledges that extensive per-dataset hyperparameter tuning (layers, dimensions, α, β, weight decay) remains necessary, limiting plug-and-play applicability. Open questions include whether the cross-aggregation mechanism generalizes to other graph tasks (link prediction, graph classification) and whether adaptive hyperparameter selection can reduce the tuning burden.
