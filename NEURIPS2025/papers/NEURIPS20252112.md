## Problem

Existing drug recommendation systems either model full patient histories (incurring high memory/computation costs and ignoring the clinical reality that similar diagnoses lead to similar prescriptions) or decompose molecules via rule-based methods like BRICS that disrupt functional-group connectivity and ignore patient-condition context. No prior framework simultaneously extracts condition-specific core substructures from drug molecules and compresses heterogeneous patient-drug interaction patterns into a reusable, discrete representation for efficient and interpretable recommendation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes longitudinal EHR sequences (multi-hot diagnosis and procedure vectors across all prior visits up to time t), molecular graphs for every candidate drug (node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F} and binary adjacency A ∈ R^{N×N}), and a precomputed DDI relation matrix D. It produces a multi-label binary drug combination vector ŷ ∈ {0,1}^{|M|} (thresholded at 0.5), condition-specific core substructure embeddings z_d per drug, and a learned discrete codebook W of S (patient-representation, drug-vector) prototype pairs. The codebook is updated online during training via a VQ loss, and at inference the nearest codebook entry provides a prior drug-category signal that is fused with attention over substructure representations.

## Method

SubRec integrates four modules: (1) a single-layer Transformer encoder over diagnosis and procedure embedding sequences to produce a patient representation e^(i); (2) a GIN-based molecular graph encoder whose node embeddings are fused with e^(i) via an MLP, followed by a Conditional Information Bottleneck (CIB) that uses Gumbel-Sigmoid noise injection to stochastically mask less-informative nodes, yielding a compact, condition-specific subgraph whose pooled embedding is the drug substructure vector z_d; (3) an adaptive vector-quantization codebook that clusters all observed (e^(i), v_m^(i)) pairs into S discrete prototypes, updated with a four-term VQ loss (embedding + commitment for both patient and drug vectors); and (4) a prediction head that computes attention-weighted drug substructure features o_m and codebook-retrieved drug features o_d, concatenates them with e^(i), and applies a sigmoid to produce the multi-label output. The total loss jointly optimizes prediction (BCE + multi-label margin), CIB compression, VQ codebook alignment, and a DDI penalty with dynamically adjusted weighting to balance accuracy and safety.

## Results

On MIMIC-III, SubRec achieves the best Jaccard (0.5585), PRAUC (0.7927), and F1 (0.7016) among all 10 methods, with a DDI rate of 0.0623. On MIMIC-IV, SubRec similarly leads with Jaccard 0.4635, PRAUC 0.7023, and F1 0.6216, while maintaining a DDI rate of 0.0674. The average number of recommended drugs (#MED) is 19.50 on MIMIC-III and 14.06 on MIMIC-IV, comparable to other methods. SubRec's advantage is most pronounced on PRAUC, where it outperforms the next-best baseline by up to 0.267 (vs. GAMENet on MIMIC-IV).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubRec outperforms all nine baselines on Jaccard, PRAUC, and F1 across both MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV. On MIMIC-III, it beats the strongest longitudinal-history baseline VITA by +0.017 Jaccard and +0.021 PRAUC, and the best substructure-aware method MoleRec by +0.028 Jaccard and +0.013 PRAUC. On MIMIC-IV, the margin over MoleRec narrows to +0.006 Jaccard but SubRec still leads by +0.016 PRAUC and +0.018 F1. In terms of DDI safety, SubRec's rate (0.0623 on MIMIC-III, 0.0674 on MIMIC-IV) is competitive but not always the lowest—GAMENet achieves a lower DDI of 0.0502 on MIMIC-IV, though at the cost of substantially worse PRAUC (0.4353 vs. 0.7023). The ablation study confirms that removing either the substructure capture module or the auxiliary codebook degrades Jaccard by ~0.03–0.04, validating that both components contribute meaningfully to the overall gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

SubRec sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) longitudinal EHR-based drug recommendation (COGNet, RETAIN, VITA, GAMENet), (2) molecular substructure-aware recommendation that uses rule-based BRICS decomposition (SafeDrug, MoleRec), and (3) Graph Information Bottleneck methods for subgraph extraction (PGIB, VGIB). The key departure is replacing fixed rule-based substructure splitting with a Conditional Information Bottleneck that treats the patient's health representation as a conditioning variable, so that the extracted substructures are condition-specific rather than drug-invariant. Additionally, the paper introduces an adaptive vector quantization codebook to compress heterogeneous patient-drug interaction patterns into a small set of discrete prototypes, a mechanism not previously applied to drug recommendation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses two practical pain points in clinical drug recommendation: the sparsity and heterogeneity of longitudinal patient histories, and the need for interpretable, condition-aware molecular representations. The finding that a codebook of only 32 vectors performs comparably to 20,000 suggests that the space of clinically meaningful patient-drug patterns is far more compact than the raw data implies, which could reduce deployment costs. However, the DDI rate is competitive but not dominant (VITA and GAMENet achieve lower rates on MIMIC-III), and the method requires full molecular graph information for all candidate drugs, limiting applicability in settings where structural data is incomplete. Open questions include whether the discrete codebook can be dynamically extended for out-of-distribution patients and whether the CIB-Graph extraction generalizes beyond the MIMIC critical-care setting to broader outpatient or polypharmacy scenarios.
