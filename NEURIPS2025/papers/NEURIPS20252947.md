## Problem

While reinforcement learning has proven effective at incentivizing reasoning in LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1), its potential for image quality assessment remains largely unexplored. Existing RL-based IQA methods like Q-Insight treat visual quality as an absolute perceptual quantity, framing NR-IQA as a regression task that requires dataset-specific reward calibration and perceptual scale realignment. This paper addresses the gap by exploiting the intrinsically relative nature of visual quality through a ranking-based RL formulation.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single RGB image paired with a fixed structured text prompt instructing it to rate quality on a 1–5 scale. It produces a two-part output: a step-by-step reasoning process enclosed in  tags, followed by a single float score (rounded to two decimal places) in <answer> tags. During training, K=6 candidate responses are generated per image, and the model is evaluated zero-shot across eight datasets spanning imaging-related distortions (blur, noise, compression) and processing-related distortions (deblurring, super-resolution, dehazing, image generation).

## Method

VisualQuality-R1 fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B via a novel RL2R algorithm that embeds the Thurstone model within Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). For each minibatch of B images, GRPO generates K quality predictions per image; the asymmetric comparative probability between images x_i and x_j is computed by subtracting the mean score of x_j from each individual score of x_i, normalized by the sum of their sample variances. The reward for each prediction is a continuous fidelity measure (rather than a discretized binary label) averaged over all B−1 pairwise comparisons, providing fine-grained policy-gradient signals. Unlike prior ranking-based IQA models that fix the Thurstone variance to one, RL2R leverages the sample variances from GRPO to dynamically capture predictive uncertainty, stabilizing reward computation and improving generalization across distortion types without perceptual scale realignment.

## Results

On the single-dataset (KADID-10K-trained) setting, VisualQuality-R1 achieves an average SRCC of 0.777 and PLCC of 0.814 across eight zero-shot test datasets spanning imaging and processing distortions. With multi-dataset training (KADID-10K + SPAQ), performance improves to 0.791 SRCC and 0.831 PLCC without requiring perceptual scale realignment. The method outperforms the closest RL-based competitor Q-Insight (0.766 SRCC / 0.803 PLCC) and all SFT-based VLMs (DeQA-Score: 0.731/0.772; Q-Align: 0.632/0.679) by a clear margin, while the untrained Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone already reaches 0.708/0.752.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest RL-based competitor Q-Insight [21], VisualQuality-R1 wins on 6 of 8 test datasets in SRCC (e.g., KonIQ: 0.830 vs 0.806; Super-Res: 0.756 vs 0.724; Image Gen: 0.775 vs 0.749) but trails slightly on CLIVE (0.750 vs 0.761) and De-hazing (0.598 vs 0.601), yielding a 0.011 average SRCC advantage. In the multi-dataset setting, the gap widens substantially: VisualQuality-R1† reaches 0.791 average SRCC while Q-Insight† drops to 0.759, because Q-Insight's regression-based reward cannot handle the perceptual scale mismatch between KADID-10K (1–5) and SPAQ (0–100). Against SFT-based VLMs, VisualQuality-R1 outperforms DeQA-Score by 0.046 SRCC and Q-Align by 0.145 SRCC on average, confirming that RL2R produces more generalizable quality judgments than supervised fine-tuning. All discriminative deep-learning baselines (UNIQUE, MUSIQ, MANIQA) and handcrafted methods (NIQE, BRISQUE) are surpassed by large margins (≥0.18 SRCC), while even the untrained Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone (0.708) is exceeded by 0.069 SRCC, demonstrating that the RL2R training loop adds meaningful IQA-specific capability on top of general VLM knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-based NR-IQA line from SFT-based scoring (Q-Align, DeQA-Score) and RL-based regression (Q-Insight) to RL-based ranking, integrating the Thurstone comparative-judgment model within GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) and using the continuous fidelity loss as a reward signal. The key conceptual departure from Q-Insight is reframing visual quality as an intrinsically relative quantity rather than an absolute one, which eliminates the need for dataset-specific reward calibration and perceptual scale realignment. The approach also inherits the ranking-based IQA tradition (Ma et al., Zhang et al.) that models quality via pairwise comparisons, but realizes it through a generative VLM with explicit reasoning traces rather than a discriminative network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to train across multiple IQA datasets without perceptual scale realignment is a practical advantage that regression-based VLM methods (including Q-Insight) cannot match, making VisualQuality-R1 more directly applicable to real-world pipelines spanning diverse distortion types. The generated reasoning traces offer interpretability and targeted feedback for downstream tasks like super-resolution and image generation, though the high inference cost (2.34 s, 17.6 GB) limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to reference-based settings, whether application-aware prompting can improve task-specific accuracy, and whether distillation or self-consistency sampling can reduce the test-time compute overhead without sacrificing the ranking fidelity.
