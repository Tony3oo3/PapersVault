## Problem

Audio captioning models trained with teacher forcing suffer from exposure bias, causing caption degeneration at inference. Existing contrastive remedies rely on cosine similarity over pooled representations, which discards the temporal structure critical for aligning acoustic and linguistic sequences, while DTW-based alternatives impose overly strict monotonic alignment and full Wasserstein distance suffers from the curse of dimensionality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw audio clip as input and produces a single natural-language caption as output. At inference, the encoder-decoder model (Enclap or ACT backbone) first generates approximately 30 candidate captions via nucleus sampling (p=0.7), which are then reranked by a combined likelihood-plus-kernel score to select the final caption. The framework is also applied to audio reasoning tasks where the input is an audio clip paired with a question and the output is a free-form reasoning answer.

## Method

The core contribution is the Unbiased Sliced Wasserstein RBF (USW-RBF) kernel, which defines similarity between two empirical distributions of hidden representations as the expectation of exp(−γ·W₂²) over random 1-D projections, rather than exp(−γ·SW₂²). This reordering of expectation and exponential makes the Monte Carlo estimator unbiased with O(L^{−1/2}) convergence, enabling direct use in stochastic gradient optimization. Rotary positional embeddings are concatenated to each hidden vector before projection so that the sliced Wasserstein distance captures both feature-space and temporal-position discrepancies. The ACUS framework jointly optimizes the standard MLE loss with the USW-RBF kernel during training, and at inference combines stochastic decoding with USW-RBF-based reranking to select the best caption from a candidate pool. The kernel is proven positive definite for p=2 and shown to be an upper bound on the standard SW-RBF kernel via Jensen's inequality.

## Results

On AudioCaps, ACUS achieves the best scores across most metrics: METEOR 0.262, CIDEr 0.807, SPICE 0.192, and SPIDEr 0.50, outperforming the strongest baseline (Enclap+CL) by 0.005 on METEOR and 0.039 on CIDEr. On Clotho, ACUS similarly leads with METEOR 0.186, CIDEr 0.419, SPICE 0.133, and SPIDEr 0.275. For audio reasoning, the USW-RBF kernel improves GAMA's average accuracy on MMAU test mini from 30.1% to 34.10% (+4.0%) and raises the CompA-R GPT4-o average score from 4.0 to 4.3. In text-to-audio retrieval, ACUS-generated captions achieve R@1 of 32.2 on AudioCaps, approaching the human caption level of 35.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On AudioCaps, ACUS outperforms all 10 baselines on METEOR, CIDEr, SPICE, and SPIDEr, with the largest margin over WavCaps (CIDEr 0.807 vs. 0.787) and Enclap+CL (CIDEr 0.807 vs. 0.768, SPIDEr 0.500 vs. 0.481). The only metric where ACUS does not lead is ROUGE-L, where AutoCap scores 0.517 versus ACUS's 0.509. On Clotho, ACUS edges out Enclap (METEOR 0.186 vs. 0.182, SPICE 0.133 vs. 0.130) and Enclap+CL (CIDEr 0.419 vs. 0.405, SPIDEr 0.275 vs. 0.271), with gains being smaller due to higher reference-caption diversity. For audio reasoning, the USW-RBF kernel improves GAMA's MMAU test mini accuracy from 30.1% (base) to 34.10%, surpassing the contrastive-learning variant (30.49%) by 3.6 points, and raises CompA-R average GPT4-o score from 4.0 to 4.3. The contrastive-learning baseline (Enclap+CL) consistently underperforms ACUS on both captioning datasets, confirming that cosine-similarity-based reranking is inferior to the temporal-aware USW-RBF score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) contrastive regularization for audio captioning (CL4AC, CLIP-AAC), which uses cosine similarity but discards temporal structure through pooling; (2) optimal transport for sequence matching (order-preserving Wasserstein distance, soft-DTW), which handles temporal distortion but suffers from the dimensionality curse in high-dimensional embedding spaces; and (3) sliced Wasserstein kernels (Carrière et al., Kolouri et al., Nguyen et al.), which avoid the dimensionality curse but were previously only available in biased Monte Carlo form. The key departure is the unbiased formulation—placing the expectation outside the exponential—combined with rotary positional embedding to encode temporal order into the transport cost, and its use as a reranking criterion alongside stochastic decoding rather than as a pure training regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ACUS framework provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to cosine-similarity-based contrastive reranking for audio captioning, with a clean O(L^{-1/2}) convergence guarantee that makes it compatible with standard SGD. The demonstrated generalizability to audio reasoning (GAMA on CompA-R and MMAU) suggests the USW-RBF kernel could serve as a general cross-modal alignment tool beyond captioning. However, the inference cost (RTF ~0.81 vs. 0.33 for beam search, requiring ~30 candidate generations) limits real-time deployment, and the improvements over strong baselines like Enclap+CL are modest (e.g., CIDEr 0.807 vs. 0.768 on AudioCaps). Open questions include whether the kernel can be made cheaper via quasi-Monte Carlo or control variates in practice, and whether the temporal alignment benefit persists for longer-form audio descriptions.
