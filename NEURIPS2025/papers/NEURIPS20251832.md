## Problem

Existing perturbation-based explainability methods for time series models are restricted to regularly sampled data with fixed timestamps and no missing values. Irregular time series—dominant in healthcare and other critical domains—have informative missingness and variable sampling intervals that current saliency methods completely ignore, making it impossible to detect whether a model relies on the structure of observed data (e.g., which tests a physician ordered) rather than the actual values.

## Input / Output

The method takes an irregular time series as a triplet (t_n, x_n, d_n) of sorted unique timestamps, observed values (with NaN for missing entries), and a binary observation mask, along with a black-box model f that maps this triplet to a prediction. It produces a saliency mask m(t): ℝ → [0,1]^C defined as a continuous-time function (not a fixed-length tensor), which can be evaluated at any time point to determine per-channel importance. The mask is applied via perturbations to generate counterfactual examples whose effect on f's output quantifies feature importance.

## Method

Contimask parameterizes the saliency mask as a small feedforward network (MLP-F) with exponentially spaced Fourier feature transformations, operating in continuous time rather than on a fixed grid. The key technical contribution is a Deletion perturbation (Del) that simulates data had not been observed by setting values to NaN and the observation mask to 0, which is non-differentiable with respect to the mask. Because Del (and any structure-altering perturbation) breaks the computation graph, the mask is trained via NeuroEvolution using the PGPE algorithm (EvoTorch with ClipUp optimizer) instead of gradient descent. The optimization objective balances prediction divergence, mask coverage (penalizing deviation from a target proportion of observed data), and mask smoothness in continuous time. Fourier feature encodings enable the small network (~3,400 parameters) to learn sharp, localized masks that outperform both plain MLPs and full tensor parameterizations under NeuroEvolution.

## Results

On the value-based Rare Feature benchmark, the proposed Deletion (Del) perturbation achieves F1=0.891, outperforming FMA (F1=0.638) and GB (F1=0.556). On the temp-based Rare Feature setting, Del achieves F1=0.982 with Recall=1.000, while FMA and GB completely fail (F1=0.000), demonstrating that only structure-altering perturbations can detect timing-based saliency. For real-world sepsis prediction with the mTAN model, Del produces the largest log-odds change (7.88) for sepsis-predicted patients compared to FMA (4.16) and GB (4.41), confirming its superiority on informative-missingness signals. MLP-F masks trained via NeuroEvolution outperform tensor masks (MT) under the same optimizer (F1=0.622 vs 0.219 on Rare Feature).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three perturbations—Gaussian Blur (GB) and Fading to Moving Average (FMA) adapted from DynaMask, and the proposed Deletion (Del) perturbation—across value-based and temp-based synthetic benchmarks and a real-world sepsis task. Del decisively wins on all temp-based settings (F1=0.982 on Rare Feature, F1=0.248 on Rare Time) where FMA and GB score exactly 0.000, since only Del alters the data structure (mask d) rather than just observed values. On value-based Rare Feature, Del also leads with F1=0.891 versus FMA's 0.638 and GB's 0.556, though the gap is smaller. On the real-world sepsis prediction task (mTAN model, sepsis-predicted patients), Del produces the largest average log-odds change (7.88) compared to FMA (4.16) and GB (4.41), indicating it captures more predictive signal from informative missingness. For sepsis-negative predictions, value-altering perturbations (FMA: 7.26, GB: 6.29) slightly outperform Del (4.57), suggesting the two perturbation types capture complementary aspects of saliency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Contimask extends the perturbation-based explanation framework established by Fong & Vedaldi (2017) and DynaMask (Crabbe & Van Der Schaar, 2021) from regular to irregular time series, where observations arrive at non-uniform intervals with informative missingness. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a non-differentiable Deletion perturbation that simulates data points never having been observed, addressing a structural dimension of saliency that all prior value-altering perturbations (Gaussian blur, moving average) ignore. To handle this non-differentiability, the paper replaces gradient descent with NeuroEvolution (PGPE via EvoTorch/ClipUp) and parameterizes masks as small MLPs with Fourier feature transformations operating in continuous time, rather than as fixed-size tensors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first black-box method to explain irregular time series models, and its case study on sepsis prediction reveals a practically dangerous finding: the mtan model is heavily influenced by structural biases introduced by treating physicians' decisions about which features to monitor, meaning the model may simply confirm clinical decisions rather than provide independent predictive value. Key limitations include the computational cost of NeuroEvolution (4–5× slower than gradient descent), the particular difficulty of explaining NCDE models due to repeated spline fitting (~30s per evaluation), and the inability to incorporate learned perturbations that improve explanations in the regular time series setting. Open questions include developing differentiable alternatives to structure-altering perturbations, combining value- and structure-altering perturbations in a unified framework, and improving NeuroEvolution strategies for 2D mask optimization.
