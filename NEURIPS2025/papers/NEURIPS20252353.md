## Problem

Existing lifelong multi-view clustering methods rely on feature library mechanisms to propagate knowledge from historical views to newly-arrived data, which overlooks the basis embedding information within each view. Additionally, the mapping paradigm used for knowledge transfer inevitably distorts learned landmarks and established affinities due to the uninterruption nature, disrupting anchor topology and hierarchical cluster structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252353/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method receives incomplete multi-view data D_t ∈ R^{d_t × n_t} arriving sequentially (one view at a time in a lifelong stream), where each view may have a different dimension d_t and a different subset of observed samples n_t, specified by an index vector w_t. It produces cluster assignments via spectral partitioning on the final shared bipartite graph Q̃_t, while simultaneously maintaining a unified anchor matrix B̃_t and the bipartite graph as persistent memory structures that are updated and transferred to subsequent views without recomputing historical view data.

## Method

BSTM introduces a twin-memory paradigm where a unified anchor matrix (UAM) and a shared bipartite graph (SBG) collectively transfer knowledge across views, avoiding redundant view-data recalculating and mitigating fusion-induced representation distortion. To handle anchor mismatch between the current view and historical views while preserving numerical values, the method designs a bit-swapping scheme: a learnable permutation matrix Ŝ_{t-1} with one-hot encoded rows and columns (operating exclusively on {0,1}) that rearranges anchor topologies and correspondingly reorders graph structures to reach a matched configuration. Indicator matrices H_t and Ē_{t-1} resolve size incompatibilities caused by missing samples across views. All components—view-specific projectors G_t, unified anchors B̃_t, shared bipartite graph Q̃_t, and the swapping matrix Ŝ_{t-1}—are jointly optimized in an end-to-end framework via a four-step alternating scheme with linear complexity in the number of samples, where the swapping matrix is solved as an integer linear program.

## Results

On PROKARYO at 10% missing ratio, BSTM achieves 74.95% ACC, 82.21% PUR, and 64.79% FSC, outperforming the strongest baseline AGCMC (70.13% ACC, 77.19% PUR) by approximately 4.8 and 5.0 percentage points respectively. At 40% missing ratio on the same dataset, BSTM maintains 72.78% ACC and 79.13% PUR, extending the gap to AGCMC (65.65% ACC, 69.95% PUR) to over 7 points. On the large-scale CALTEALL dataset (9,144 samples, 5 views), BSTM reaches 74.14% ACC and 79.56% PUR at 70% missing ratio, surpassing PSMVC (73.77% ACC, 78.49% PUR). BSTM is the only method that successfully processes all 8 datasets including the large-scale YOUTUTWE (63,896 samples) and FAMNISIX (70,000 samples), where several baselines fail entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSTM consistently outperforms all 12 baselines on the majority of dataset–metric combinations. On PROKARYO (the clearest separation), BSTM beats the next-best AGCMC by 4.8% ACC and 5.0% PUR at 10% missing, and the gap widens to 7.1% ACC and 9.2% PUR at 40% missing. On WIKIFEA, MKKMC edges out BSTM by 0.12% in ACC (54.34 vs 54.22) but BSTM leads in PUR (59.74 vs 57.44) and FSC (47.23 vs 46.27); on CALTEALL at 40% missing, PSMVC slightly exceeds BSTM in FSC (68.32 vs 67.54) while BSTM wins in ACC (73.23 vs 71.56) and PUR (78.94 vs 77.35). A critical practical advantage is that GSRMC, FLSD, MVTSC, AGCMC, NGSPL, and PIMVC all fail to run on the large-scale YOUTUTWE (63,896 samples) and FAMNISIX (70,000 samples) datasets, whereas BSTM processes them normally thanks to its linear-complexity solver. On PROTEINF, BSTM leads in ACC across all missing ratios (36.26/33.57/30.07) but the PUR advantage over LRGMV is modest (41.70 vs 41.28 at 10%), which the authors attribute to the spectral partitioning step potentially degrading data diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

BSTM departs from the dominant feature-library paradigm in lifelong multi-view clustering (Wan et al. 2022, 2024; Sun et al. 2020; Qu et al. 2024), which propagates knowledge by storing and mapping historical view features, by instead maintaining a unified anchor matrix and a shared bipartite graph as twin memory components that jointly transfer knowledge across incoming views. The bit-swapping strategy draws conceptual inspiration from orthogonal permutation matrices used in prior incremental MVC work (Qu et al. 2024; Wan et al. 2024) but restricts the transformation to the binary {0,1} space, ensuring value invariance during anchor reordering. The shared bipartite graph construction via indicator matrices also distinguishes BSTM from per-view architectures that recompute similarities independently for each view, and the linear-complexity solver builds on the anchor-based efficiency principles established in large-scale MVC (Wang et al. 2022; Li et al. 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The twin-memory + bit-swapping formulation offers a principled way to handle the two failure modes (basis-embedding neglect and mapping-induced distortion) that plague existing lifelong MVC methods, and the linear complexity makes it applicable to large-scale datasets (up to 70K samples) where many competitors simply fail. However, the method still relies on two hyperparameters (λ, β) requiring manual tuning, and the spectral-partitioning pipeline may degrade data diversity compared to direct discrete label optimization—both acknowledged as open directions. The broader question of whether binary-space permutation is the optimal mechanism for maintaining numerical consistency under anchor reordering, or whether continuous relaxation could yield further gains, remains unexplored.
