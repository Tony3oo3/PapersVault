## Problem

Existing MNL contextual bandit algorithms either assume linear utilities (limiting expressiveness for real-world user behavior) or, when extended to general non-linear utilities, face a fundamental tradeoff: computationally tractable methods yield suboptimal regret (Õ(T^{2/3}) or Õ(T^{5/6})), while statistically efficient methods are computationally intractable due to non-convex optimization. No prior algorithm simultaneously achieves both polynomial-time computation and the sharp Õ(√T) regret bound under non-linear parametric utilities.

## Input / Output

At each round, the agent observes d-dimensional context feature vectors for all N items (bounded in ℓ₂ norm) and a revenue vector, then selects an assortment of at most K items. The agent receives only a single choice observation (which item or the outside option was selected), not direct utility feedback, making parameter estimation indirect through the MNL probability model. The utility function is parameterized by a d_w-dimensional vector w in a potentially highly non-convex function class (e.g., a two-layer neural network), and the algorithm must handle the equivalence set W* arising from parameter symmetries such as neuron permutations.

## Method

ONL-MNL operates in two phases: a uniform exploration phase of t₀ = Õ(κ^{-3/2} d_w √T) rounds to compute a pilot estimator via empirical risk minimization, followed by an optimistic UCB phase. In Phase II, the algorithm minimizes a regularized negative log-likelihood where the MNL probability is linearized via a first-order Taylor approximation of the non-linear utility around the current estimate, and regularization is centered at the pilot estimator rather than the origin. The optimistic utility z_ti = f_{ŵ_t}(x_ti) + √β_t ‖∇f_{ŵ_t}(x_ti)‖_{V_t^{-1}} + β_t C_h/λ includes an extra item-independent residual term from the Taylor approximation that does not vanish over time. A key technical contribution is a new concentration inequality for the MNL parameter estimation that is independent of the regularization parameter λ (up to logarithmic factors), enabling the confidence radius to scale as Õ(1) rather than Õ(√λ + d/√λ). The analysis relies on a generalized geometric condition (Assumption 4) on the expected squared loss that permits multiple global minima due to parameter symmetries, strictly weakening prior assumptions that required a unique global minimum.

## Results

Theoretically, ONL-MNL achieves a cumulative regret of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{-2}\mu^{-1}d_w\sqrt{T})$, the first computationally tractable $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})$ regret bound for MNL contextual bandits with non-linear (e.g., neural network) utilities, independent of the total number of items N. In experiments (N=100, K=5, d=3, T=1000, 30 seeds), ONL-MNL maintains low and stable cumulative regret under both realizable (two-layer sigmoid NN) and misspecified (cosine utility) settings, while all linear-utility baselines exhibit steadily growing regret. When N increases from 100 to 800, ONL-MNL's Phase II regret slope remains stable, whereas ε-greedy-MNL's regret grows noticeably, consistent with its polynomial dependence on N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ONL-MNL is compared against four baselines: three linear-utility MNL bandit algorithms (UCB-MNL [29], TS-MNL [28], OFU-MNL+ [20]) and one general-utility method (ε-greedy-MNL [41]). Under non-linear utilities, all three linear methods fail to capture the true utility structure and exhibit steadily increasing cumulative regret, while ONL-MNL maintains low regret in both realizable and misspecified settings. Against ε-greedy-MNL [41], which is the only prior tractable method for general utilities, ONL-MNL achieves substantially lower cumulative regret and, critically, its regret bound is independent of N, whereas ε-greedy-MNL's regret scales polynomially with N (confirmed experimentally: regret grows visibly as N increases from 100 to 800 for ε-greedy-MNL but remains stable for ONL-MNL). The intractable methods from [41] (log-barrier, Feel-Good TS) achieve $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})$ regret but require super-linear dependence on K ($\mathcal{O}(K^2)$) and polynomial dependence on N, and cannot be solved in polynomial time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MNL contextual bandit framework from linear to non-linear parametric utilities (e.g., neural networks), building directly on Zhang and Luo [41] who first considered general utility classes but faced a fundamental tradeoff between computational tractability and statistical efficiency. The UCB-based exploration strategy generalizes the linear-utility UCB-MNL algorithm of Oh and Iyengar [29], while the concentration inequality for parameter estimation adapts self-normalized martingale techniques from Dani et al. [7] to handle the λ-dependent regularization regime. A key departure is the introduction of a generalized geometric condition (Assumption 4) that explicitly permits multiple global minima due to parameter symmetries, going beyond the unique-minimum assumptions in prior parametric bandit analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first computationally tractable algorithm achieving O(√T) regret for MNL contextual bandits with non-linear utilities, breaking the tractability–efficiency tradeoff identified in prior work. The regret bound's independence from the number of items N is practically significant for large-scale assortment optimization (e.g., e-commerce with thousands of SKUs). Open questions include reducing the dependence on the instance factor κ, extending the framework to MNL MDPs with non-linear transition kernels, and validating performance at larger scales (deeper networks, longer horizons) where the current experiments use only 3–15 hidden neurons and T ≤ 1000.
