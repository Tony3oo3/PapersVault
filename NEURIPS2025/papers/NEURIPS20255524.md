## Problem

Existing certified defenses for time-series anomaly detection provide robustness guarantees only under ℓp-norm constraints, which fail to capture the intrinsic temporal structure of time-series data—small temporal shifts or rescalings can dramatically inflate ℓp-norm distance without changing semantics. Although Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is the natural similarity metric for time series and DTW-based adversarial attacks have been demonstrated, no certifiable robustness result existed under the DTW metric. This paper introduces the first DTW-certified robust defense for time-series anomaly detection.

## Input / Output

The method operates on sliding windows of size T×C (default T=50) from multivariate or univariate time series, and is model-agnostic: it wraps any pre-trained anomaly detector's scoring function without requiring retraining. At inference, it injects n=1,000 Gaussian noise samples (σ=0.5) to estimate the percentile-smoothed anomaly score via Monte Carlo, then computes a per-instance certified DTW radius using the Keogh Lower Bound with a Sakoe-Chiba wrapping window w=4. The output is a binary anomaly decision (score vs. threshold γ) paired with a guaranteed DTW radius e within which no adversarial perturbation can flip the prediction with probability ≥ 1−α (α=1e-3).

## Method

The core mechanism adapts percentile randomized smoothing to anomaly detection and bridges ℓp-norm certification to DTW distance via a lower-bound transformation. Specifically, the percentile-smoothed score h_p(x) is bounded within an ℓ2-ball of radius r (Lemma 3.1), and Lemma 3.2 shows that this ℓ2 certificate automatically holds for all points within a DTW ball of radius e = inf{LB_Keogh(x,x′) : ‖x−x′‖ > r}. Theorem 3.3 then exploits the envelope structure of the Keogh Lower Bound to derive a closed-form expression for e: when the ℓ2 radius r exceeds the total envelope slack R, the certified DTW radius is √(M² + r² − R²) − M, where M is the maximum per-step slack. This yields a model-agnostic, training-free defense that can be applied to any pre-trained detector (COUTA, TimesNet, DeepSVDDTS) and generalizes to arbitrary ℓp norms by swapping the noise distribution and CDF.

## Results

Under DTW-based adversarial attacks (budget e_att=1.0), the DTW-certified defense consistently outperforms both the undefended base model and the ℓp-norm certified defense across all four evaluated datasets. The headline improvement is up to 18.7% in F1-score over the ℓp-norm certified baseline (e.g., UCR-1: 0.948 vs. 0.761; NIPS-TS-WATER: 0.525 vs. 0.423). In terms of certified robustness, the method certifies up to 99.46% of test inputs with a mean DTW radius of 0.189 (NIPS-TS-WATER, DeepSVDDTS), while maintaining or even improving detection F1-score and ROC AUC relative to the unsmoothed base model on several datasets (e.g., SMAP COUTA: 0.961 F1 vs. 0.794 base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed DTW-certified defense against the ℓp-norm certified defense (Cohen et al., randomized smoothing [18]) under a strong DTW-based adversarial attack with budget e_att=1.0, using the COUTA model. The DTW-certified defense wins on all four datasets in both F1-score and ROC AUC. The F1-score advantage over the ℓp-norm baseline ranges from 7.2 percentage points on SMD (0.464 vs. 0.392) to 18.7 percentage points on UCR-1 (0.948 vs. 0.761). The gap is particularly pronounced on datasets with strong temporal distortions under attack (SMD, NIPS-TS-WATER), where the ℓp-norm certificate provides only partial protection. On UCR-1, the DTW-certified defense nearly recovers the clean-model F1 (0.948 vs. 0.682 base), whereas the undefended model collapses to 0.084, demonstrating that the DTW-aligned certificate is essential for effective defense against temporally-aware adversaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the randomized smoothing paradigm for certified robustness (Cohen et al., 2019) to the time-series anomaly detection setting by adopting percentile smoothing (Chiang et al., 2020) to handle unbounded anomaly scores. The key conceptual departure is bridging ℓp-norm certificates to DTW distance via the Keogh Lower Bound (Keogh & Ratanamahatana, 2005), yielding the first closed-form DTW-certified radius. Prior certified defenses for time-series anomaly detection (Cao et al., 2024; Franco et al., 2023) operated exclusively under ℓp norms, which the authors argue are misaligned with temporal structure, while DTW-based adversarial attacks (Belkhouja et al., 2022) had no corresponding certified defense.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a concrete gap: DTW-based attacks on time-series anomaly detectors were demonstrated to be effective, yet no provable defense existed under that metric. The model-agnostic, training-free nature of the approach makes it immediately applicable to deployed detectors, and the 18.7% F1 improvement over ℓp-certified baselines under DTW attacks validates the metric alignment. However, the Keogh lower bound is conservative (certified proportions drop to ~10–15% on high-dimensional datasets like NIPS-TS-SWAN), Monte Carlo sampling introduces inference overhead, and the attack comparison uses only the COUTA model. Open questions include tightening the DTW relaxation, reducing sampling cost, and extending the framework to time-series classification.
