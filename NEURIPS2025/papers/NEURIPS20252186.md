## Problem

Local search is a well-established framework for point-based k-means clustering with strong approximation guarantees, but extending it to the k-means of lines problem is nontrivial: the standard capture relation fails because point-to-line distance lacks the triangle inequality, and the infinite extent of lines makes it difficult to identify effective swap points. This paper presents the first single-swap local search algorithm for k-means of lines with provable approximation guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a set L of n pairwise non-parallel lines in R^d and an integer k, and outputs a set C of k points in R^d that minimizes the sum of squared distances from each line to its nearest center, where the distance from a line to a point is the shortest squared Euclidean distance from any point on the line to that point. The algorithm runs in polynomial time O(n²d² + 9^d · ndk²ζ log ρ) for low-dimensional Euclidean space, with the 9^d factor arising from the CrossLine discretization in d dimensions.

## Method

The SLS-k-ML algorithm introduces two key components to overcome the geometric obstacles of line clustering. First, a proportional capture relation replaces the standard nearest-center capture: an optimal center is captured by a current center if that current center receives the largest fraction of lines from the optimal center's cluster, enabling cost analysis without the triangle inequality. Second, a CrossLine structure discretizes the geometric space around two sampled lines by constructing 3^d parallel shifts of each line along coordinate axes and taking their pairwise intersections, guaranteeing that at least one grid point lies within distance r of any point in the enclosed region. The algorithm initializes via the CENTROID-SET procedure (collecting closest-point projections between all line pairs), then iteratively samples two lines with probability proportional to their current clustering cost, constructs CrossLine candidate points at multiple scales, and performs a single-swap if any candidate reduces the total cost. This yields a (500+ε)-approximation in expectation.

## Results

On the real-world OpenStreetMap datasets (RE 1, n=476, d=2 and RE 2, n=418, d=2), SLS-k-ML achieves average clustering costs of 1.01E-07 (k=10) and 1.35E-04 (k=3) on RE 1, and 1.60E-06 (k=10) and 1.39E-03 (k=3) on RE 2, all within 1–2 seconds. On the synthetic dataset SYN 1 (n=5000, d=10, k=10), SLS-k-ML achieves an average cost of 1.86E+04 in 456 seconds, compared to 4.24E+04 in 20,400 seconds for the coreset+sampling baseline. The coreset+exhaustive search baseline exceeds 12 hours on all datasets with n≥418 and k=10, making it impractical.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SLS-k-ML against two variants of the coreset-based method from Marom & Feldman (NeurIPS 2019): coreset+sampling and coreset+exhaustive search. SLS-k-ML dominates on every dataset and k value where a comparison is possible. On RE 1 (k=10), SLS-k-ML achieves an average cost of 1.01E-07 versus 6.24E-03 for coreset+sampling (≈61,000× lower) while running 5.8× faster (1.05 s vs. 6.05 s). On the larger synthetic dataset SYN 1 (n=5000, d=10, k=10), SLS-k-ML reduces the average cost by roughly 2.3× (1.86E+04 vs. 4.24E+04) and is approximately 45× faster (456 s vs. 20,400 s). The coreset+exhaustive search baseline is intractable for k=10 on all datasets (exceeding 12 hours) and for k=3 on SYN 2, whereas SLS-k-ML completes in under 20 minutes even on the largest instance. The consistently low standard deviation across 10 runs (e.g., 2.73E-07 on RE 1 k=10) further indicates that SLS-k-ML's advantage is not due to favorable random initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local search framework for point-based k-means (Lattanzi & Sohler 2019; Huang et al. 2023) to the k-means of lines problem, adapting the CENTROID-SET initialization from Marom & Feldman (2019). The key departure is that the standard capture relation—where an optimal center is 'captured' by its nearest current center—fundamentally breaks down for lines because point-to-line distance lacks the triangle inequality. To overcome this, the paper introduces a proportional capture relation (based on assignment fractions rather than geometric proximity) and a CrossLine discretization structure that provides a finite candidate set guaranteeing coverage near optimal centers, neither of which has a direct analogue in the point-based literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first local search algorithm with provable approximation guarantees for k-means of lines, filling a gap where prior work offered only heuristics (Ommer et al.) or coreset-based methods with exponential dependence on k (Marom & Feldman). The CrossLine structure and proportional capture relation are reusable geometric tools that could inform future work on clustering other structured objects (e.g., higher-dimensional affine subspaces). However, the (500+ε) approximation is substantially looser than what is achievable for point-based k-means (constant or (1+ε) factors), and the running time has a 9^d dependence that limits practicality to very low dimensions. Open questions include tightening the approximation, removing the d-dependence, and extending to multi-swap strategies.
