## Problem

Existing methods for simultaneous scene understanding and 3D reconstruction rely on a 2D-to-3D feature alignment paradigm that extracts features from pre-trained 2D vision-language models (e.g., CLIP, LSeg) and compresses them into 3D representations. This approach suffers from two fundamental limitations: it cannot support instance-level understanding tasks (instance, panoptic segmentation) because the source 2D models lack such capabilities, and the necessary feature compression (e.g., 512-dim to 64-dim) discards fine-grained semantics, degrading 3D understanding accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes V≥2 sparse unposed RGB images (256×256) with normalized camera intrinsics and optional text prompts as input, and produces pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians (one per pixel per view) for reconstruction alongside a shared set of 100 learnable queries that decode multi-view 2D mask logits and class labels for understanding. The critical design constraint is that both the 3D Gaussians and 2D masks are pixel-aligned: the Gaussian at pixel (i,j) in view v corresponds directly to the mask prediction at the same pixel, enabling a direct 2D-to-3D lifting without any feature compression or alignment step. The entire pipeline operates in a single feed-forward pass (~0.07s inference), requiring no per-scene optimization.

## Method

SIU3R replaces 2D-to-3D feature alignment with a pixel-aligned 2D-to-3D lifting strategy: a ViT-based Image Encoder (initialized from MASt3R) simultaneously extracts geometry-focused and semantic-focused features, which feed into a Gaussian Decoder (ViT + DPT head) predicting per-pixel 3D Gaussians and a Unified Query Decoder (100 shared learnable queries with stacked cross-/self-attention) predicting cross-view consistent masks for semantic, instance, panoptic, and text-referred segmentation. A Mutual Benefit Mechanism couples the two tasks bidirectionally: Multi-View Mask Aggregation (R→U) rasterizes 2D semantic logits onto the 3D Gaussians and projects them back to all views to enforce cross-view mask consistency (training-free at inference), while Mask-Guided Geometry Refinement (U→R) applies a depth-continuity loss within each predicted mask to tighten intra-instance 3D geometry. The final SIU3R field is obtained by lifting 2D masks to 3D via the pixel-aligned Gaussians, supporting multi-task 3D understanding and versatile 3D editing (removal, replacement, recoloring) without any alignment with external 2D models.

## Results

On ScanNet, SIU3R achieves state-of-the-art performance across all tasks simultaneously: for 3D reconstruction, it reaches AbsRel 0.0742, RMSE 0.2081, PSNR 25.96 dB, SSIM 0.8220, and LPIPS 0.1841; for scene understanding on context views (2D-only), it achieves mIoU_s 0.5922, mAP 0.2817, PQ 0.6612, and mIoU_t 0.5273; and for 3D-aware understanding on novel views, it reaches mIoU_s 0.5920, mAP 0.2714, PQ 0.6495, and mIoU_t 0.5270. The method runs in 0.13s per scene with ~4GB inference VRAM, making it the fastest among all compared simultaneous methods. Ablations confirm that the Mutual Benefit Mechanism (R→U and U→R modules) contributes meaningful gains in both reconstruction and understanding without additional training cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LSM (the only other feed-forward simultaneous method), SIU3R achieves a dramatic improvement in understanding: mIoU_s jumps from 0.2745 to 0.5922 (+31.8 pts) and mIoU_t from 0.1925 to 0.5273 (+33.5 pts), while also surpassing LSM in reconstruction (PSNR 25.96 vs 21.88, LPIPS 0.1841 vs 0.3035). Against reconstruction-only baselines, SIU3R matches or exceeds NoPoSplat on depth (AbsRel 0.0742 vs 0.0944) and NVS (PSNR 25.96 vs 25.91) while additionally providing full multi-task segmentation. Against understanding-only baselines, SIU3R outperforms Mask2Former on semantic mIoU (0.5922 vs 0.5466) and instance mAP (0.2817 vs 0.2486), and dramatically surpasses LSeg on text-referred mIoU (0.5273 vs 0.2127). In Appendix B, SIU3R also outperforms per-scene optimization methods Feature-3DGS (mIoU 0.5922 vs 0.3965) and NeRF-DFF (mIoU 0.5922 vs 0.3410) while being orders of magnitude faster (0.13s vs 145.30 min and 2.71 min respectively). The ablation rows confirm that removing R→U drops 3D-aware understanding to zero (no novel-view masks) and reduces 2D mIoU_s by 4.1 pts, while removing U→R degrades depth (AbsRel 0.0962 vs 0.0742) and NVS quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 2D-to-3D feature alignment paradigm (used by LSM, Feature-3DGS, NeRF-DFF) that embeds off-the-shelf 2D vision-language model features into 3D representations via per-scene or feed-forward rasterization. Instead, it inherits the pose-free feed-forward reconstruction paradigm from DUSt3R/NoPoSplat and the query-based segmentation architecture from Mask2Former, but recombines them through a pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian representation that enables explicit 2D-to-3D lifting without any feature compression or alignment loss. The mutual benefit mechanism (bidirectional information flow between reconstruction and understanding) is a novel conceptual contribution not present in prior simultaneous reconstruction-understanding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependency on 2D model feature alignment, SIU3R removes two fundamental bottlenecks: the inability to perform instance-level and panoptic segmentation (which CLIP/LSeg-based methods cannot support) and the semantic degradation from feature dimension compression. The practical impact is significant—real-time (0.07s) generalizable 3D scene understanding from just two unposed images without per-scene optimization. However, the method is currently validated only on ScanNet with limited training data (~2 hours on 8×RTX4090), and the authors acknowledge that noisy projected 3D-to-2D ground-truth labels and restricted training scale limit generalizability to broader visual domains. Open questions include whether the alignment-free paradigm scales to outdoor scenes, dynamic content, and whether the mutual benefit mechanism generalizes beyond indoor ScanNet-style scenes.
