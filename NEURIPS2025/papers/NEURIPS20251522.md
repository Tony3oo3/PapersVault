## Problem

RAG-based web search systems are vulnerable to adversarial corruption of the retrieval corpus (prompt injection, corpus poisoning), yet existing robustness defenses like RobustRAG suffer from significant information loss in aggregation, struggle with long-form generation, and critically overlook the built-in reliability signals (document rank, PageRank-like scores) that search engines already provide. This paper asks: can we design a provably robust RAG defense that explicitly leverages document reliability to filter out malicious documents while preserving utility?

## Input / Output

The method takes a user query q and an ordered list of k retrieved documents D = (x_1, …, x_k) where x_1 is the most reliable, optionally augmented with cardinal reliability weights w(x_i) ∈ [0,1] summing to 1. It produces a filtered document subset S* (the 'consistent majority') and a final LLM-generated answer grounded only in S*. In the ordinal setting, only the rank ordering is used; in the cardinal setting, weights modulate sampling probabilities. The contradiction graph is built from pairwise NLI judgments on isolated per-document answers, with a fixed threshold β = 0.5 for the CONTRADICTION label.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs a contradiction graph where vertices are retrieved documents and edges connect pairs whose isolated LLM answers are judged contradictory by an NLI model (DeBERTa-v3-large). The defense then finds the Maximum Independent Set (MIS) of this graph, selecting the largest mutually consistent subset of documents; when multiple MIS of equal size exist, the one with the smallest lexicographic index order is chosen, explicitly prioritizing higher-ranked (more reliable) documents. For scalability beyond k ≈ 20, a weighted sample-and-aggregate framework samples T smaller contexts of size m with replacement proportional to document weights, runs the MIS-based selection on the T intermediate answers, and aggregates the results. Theoretical analysis proves that under bounded corruption (k′ ≤ k/5) and NLI error bounds ε_1, ε_2, the probability of including any malicious document in the selected set is at most e^{−O(k)}, yielding (1 − e^{−O(k)})-robustness.

## Results

On the primary k=10 evaluation (Table 1), MIS with GPT-4o-mini achieves 76% RQA accuracy, 66% NQ accuracy, and 80.1/100 Bio LLM-judge score under benign conditions, and retains 76% RQA / 65.4% NQ / 79 Bio under prompt injection at the lowest-ranked position (Pos 10). Under attack at the highest-ranked position (Pos 1), MIS drops only modestly (70% RQA, 59.6% NQ, 77.9 Bio). The scalable Sampling+MIS variant (k=50, Table 2) with GPT-4o-mini reaches 77% RQA, 68.6% NQ, and 81.3 Bio benign, and maintains 79% RQA / 67.4% NQ / 76.7 Bio when the attack targets the middle-ranked document (Pos 25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RobustRAG (Keyword), the other robustness-focused baseline, MIS wins on nearly every metric across all three models and datasets. The gap is most pronounced on the long-form Biography task, where MIS scores 80.1 (GPT-4o-mini) vs. 61.2 for RobustRAG under benign conditions, and 79 vs. 61.4 under attack at Pos 10—a roughly 19-point advantage that highlights RobustRAG's known weakness on complex generation. Against AstuteRAG and InstructRAG (designed for benign utility rather than adversarial robustness), MIS matches or exceeds their benign accuracy while substantially outperforming them under attack (e.g., 76% vs. 61% RQA @Pos10 for GPT-4o-mini). The rank-aware nature of MIS is evident: accuracy at Pos 10 consistently exceeds Pos 1, a property none of the baselines exhibit. In the scalable k=50 setting (Table 2), Sampling+MIS with GPT-4o-mini achieves the highest scores across all four datasets in both benign and mid/low-rank attack positions, surpassing RobustRAG by 11–16 points on RQA and 15–16 points on Bio under attack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the keyword/next-token majority-voting strategy of RobustRAG [61] by reframing document selection as a Maximum Independent Set problem on an NLI-based contradiction graph, and further distinguishes itself from AstuteRAG [58], InstructRAG [60], and TrustRAG [69] by explicitly incorporating ordinal or cardinal document-reliability signals (search rank, PageRank-like weights) that prior robust RAG frameworks treat as an unordered set. The weighted sample-and-aggregate framework draws on randomized-smoothing and reservoir-sampling ideas from the robustness literature [4, 9] but applies them to the RAG document-selection setting with reliability-weighted sampling. Conceptually, the paper repurposes a classical graph-theoretic tool (MIS) and a standard NLI model as a pre-generation filter, positioning itself as a complementary upstream layer rather than a replacement for downstream aggregation or rationale-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing provable robustness guarantees (Theorem 1) under bounded corruption and bounded NLI error, the paper moves RAG defense from purely empirical heuristics toward a theoretically grounded framework, and the rank-aware MIS selection offers a practical, interpretable mechanism that leverages signals already available in production search engines. The notable preservation of long-form generation quality (Bio scores ~72 vs. ~52 for RobustRAG) addresses a key practical limitation of prior robustness methods. Open questions include whether the bounded-corruption assumption (k' ≤ k/5) holds under adaptive adversaries, how the approach scales beyond k=50 without the sampling approximation, and whether the NLI error bounds (ε₁, ε₂) remain tight under increasingly sophisticated prompt-injection payloads that mimic benign content.
