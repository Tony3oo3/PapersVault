## Problem

Despite the empirical success of diffusion-based generative imputation for time series, the theoretical understanding of how well these models capture complex spatio-temporal dependencies between missing and observed values remains limited. In particular, it is unclear how the statistical efficiency of imputation depends on the missing pattern, and how to construct confidence regions for imputed values with formal coverage guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254111/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series of length H with d-dimensional observations under a block-missing pattern (entire time frames unobserved), consuming the observed partial sequence x_obs and the missing index set. It produces a point estimate (mean of B independently generated samples from the learned conditional distribution) and a confidence region defined as a Euclidean ball around the point estimate, with radius set to the (1−α)-quantile of distances from generated samples. The theoretical analysis assumes Gaussian process data where temporal correlation is governed by a kernel function over positional embeddings and spatial dependence is captured by a shared covariance matrix Λ.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a novel score representation theory that reformulates the conditional score function as the solution to a nested quadratic optimization problem, then unrolls the resulting nested gradient descent algorithm into a transformer architecture where each attention block approximates one gradient step and attention heads separately capture different covariance components (Σ_obs, Σ_cor, Σ_miss). Training uses a score matching loss with a proposed mixed-masking strategy that blends different missing patterns (random, weakly grouped, moderately grouped, strongly grouped) to reduce distribution shift between training and test. The paper establishes a sample complexity bound of Õ(√(Hd²κ⁵(Σ_cond)κ²(Σ_obs))/√n) for total variation distance to the true conditional distribution, where κ is the condition number induced by the missing pattern, and shows confidence region coverage converges to the desired level at Õ(n^{−1/2}) rate with a class-dependent distribution shift coefficient.

## Results

On latent Gaussian process data (H=96, d=8, |I_miss|=16), DiT achieves MSE of 0.53–0.70 across four missing patterns and four training strategies, substantially outperforming CSDI (0.58–0.75) and GPVAE (4.39–5.45). For 95% confidence region coverage, DiT reaches 36–81% depending on pattern difficulty, while CSDI achieves 54–73%. The mixed-masking training strategy (S4, blending random, weakly/moderately/strongly grouped masks) consistently improves both MSE and CR coverage over purely random masking (S1), e.g., on the hardest pattern P1, DiT+S4 achieves MSE 0.67 vs. 0.70 for DiT+S1, and CR coverage 56.4% vs. 36.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiT consistently outperforms both CSDI and GPVAE on MSE across all four missing patterns and all four training strategies. Against CSDI (a convolutional conditional diffusion model), DiT achieves lower MSE by 0.02–0.05 on average, with the gap widening on harder clustered patterns (e.g., 0.67 vs. 0.68 on P1/S4). Against GPVAE (a variational autoencoder with GP prior), DiT is dramatically superior, achieving MSE roughly 7–8× lower (e.g., 0.53 vs. 4.45 on P4/S4). On CR coverage, DiT with mixed-masking (S4) surpasses CSDI on the harder patterns P1–P3 (e.g., 56.4% vs. 55.7% on P1/S4; 73.4% vs. 67.2% on P3/S4), while CSDI edges ahead on the easiest pattern P4 (72.9% vs. 80.6%—actually DiT wins here too at 80.6%). GPVAE's CR coverage is omitted due to its poor point-estimation quality. The mixed-masking strategy (S4) improves DiT over random masking (S1) on all patterns, confirming the theoretical prediction that reducing distribution shift between training and test improves imputation reliability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional diffusion imputation framework of CSDI (Tashiro et al., 2021) by replacing the convolutional backbone with a Diffusion Transformer (Peebles and Xie, 2022) and, crucially, provides the first statistical efficiency analysis for this specific architecture in the imputation setting. The theoretical machinery builds on prior score-approximation and distribution-recovery results for diffusion models (Chen et al., 2023; Oko et al., 2023; Mei and Wu, 2025), but introduces a novel algorithm-unrolling technique that represents the conditional score function as a nested gradient descent, enabling a constructive transformer approximation with explicit width/depth bounds. The paper also departs from prior imputation theory by explicitly connecting the missing pattern to the condition number of the conditional covariance, a structural insight absent from earlier analyses of diffusion-based imputation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The condition-number-to-missing-pattern link gives practitioners a concrete, interpretable diagnostic for why certain imputation tasks are harder than others, and the mixed-masking training strategy (which reduces the distribution-shift coefficient by ~48× for clustered patterns) is a simple, broadly applicable training recipe that improves both DiT and CSDI. However, the theoretical guarantees are restricted to Gaussian process data under MCAR block-missingness, and the empirical CR coverage rates remain well below the nominal 95% level even at n=10⁵, suggesting the bounds are loose in practice. Open questions include extending the analysis to heavy-tailed or non-Gaussian data, characterizing optimal mixing ratios for the masking strategy, and understanding whether the condition-number bottleneck persists in more realistic, non-block missingness regimes.
