## Problem

Irregular multivariate time series have uneven sampling intervals and misaligned observations across variables, which degrade standard deep learning classifiers. Existing attention-based methods fail to fully exploit the missingness patterns that encode the underlying data-generating process, while methods that capture intervariable dependencies rely on computationally expensive graph neural networks. No prior approach efficiently addresses both challenges simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253407/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes irregular multivariate time series where each of V variables has its own set of observed time points and values with potentially different counts and uneven intervals; missing values are zero-filled with binary mask indicators concatenated to the observations. The encoder produces a fixed-length representation of shape V×K (where K is the number of learnable reference points), and the classifier outputs C-dimensional softmax probabilities. The representation dimension is deliberately set equal to V to enforce intervariable consistency between input and latent space.

## Method

ATENet introduces Adaptive Time Encoding (ATE), which learns a globally shared set of K reference time points end-to-end as queries in a multi-time attention mechanism, interpolating irregular observations onto these points to produce fixed-length representations that capture missingness patterns without manual tuning. Two lightweight consistency regularization terms enhance the representations: a temporal consistency term uses instance-wise and point-wise contrastive losses on randomly masked views to capture intricate temporal dynamics, and an intervariable consistency term applies binary cross-entropy between outer-product matrices of the input and the representation to efficiently encode cross-variable structural relations without graph neural networks. The classifier is a simple GRU followed by two fully connected layers, and the entire framework is trained end-to-end with a combined loss of cross-entropy (with label smoothing), temporal consistency, and intervariable consistency terms.

## Results

ATENet achieves the best average rank of 2.00 across all four datasets and both metrics (AUROC and AUPRC), outperforming the next-best method MTSFormer (rank 2.38). Headline AUROC scores are 85.54 on P12-M, 79.64 on P12-L, 84.02 on P19, and 99.18 on PAM; corresponding AUPRC scores are 53.31, 97.70, 41.16, and 97.61. ATENet also requires at least 10× fewer parameters and runs at least 3× faster per epoch than Raindrop, the most parameter-heavy baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATENet outperforms all nine baselines on P12-M (AUROC 85.54 vs. MTSFormer 84.11, mTAND 84.18), P12-L (AUROC 79.64 vs. MTSFormer 75.17, Warpformer 74.57), and PAM (AUROC 99.18 vs. Raindrop 98.73, MTSFormer 98.39), with the largest margins on P12-L where it leads the runner-up by over 4.5 AUROC points. On P19, however, ATENet (AUROC 84.02, AUPRC 41.16) is outperformed by MTSFormer (88.96 / 57.96), GRU-D (87.16 / 47.37), and Raindrop (85.93 / 50.63), making P19 the only dataset where ATENet does not rank first. Among graph-based methods, MTGNN and Raindrop show the weakest robustness when variables are removed, with Raindrop dropping ~40% AUROC on P19 at 40% variable removal, while ATENet degrades far less. In terms of efficiency, ATENet uses at least 10× fewer parameters and is at least 3× faster per epoch than Raindrop and MTGNN, which rely on graph neural networks for intervariable modeling. Statistical paired t-tests confirm ATENet is significantly better than most baselines on most dataset–metric combinations, with the notable exceptions being P19 where MTSFormer and GRU-D remain significantly ahead.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATENet builds directly on the multi-time attention (MTA) mechanism of Shukla and Marlin (mTAND, ICLR 2021), which interpolates irregular sequences at fixed reference points, and on the instance-wise and point-wise contrastive losses of TS2Vec (Yue et al., AAAI 2022). The key departure is making the reference time points learnable end-to-end rather than fixed, and replacing the graph neural networks used by Raindrop, MTGNN, and MTSFormer to capture intervariable dependencies with a lightweight outer-product-based consistency regularization. The temporal consistency term further extends the masked-modeling idea from semi-supervised time-series work into the irregular-sequence setting via random masking of observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple GRU classifier combined with an efficient encoder can match or beat graph-based and multi-scale attention methods on irregular multivariate time-series classification while requiring 10× fewer parameters and 3× less processing time than Raindrop, making it attractive for resource-constrained clinical deployments. The robustness to missing variables (up to 50% dropped) is practically important for real-world sensor and EHR data. Open questions include generalization under temporal distribution shifts between training and deployment, performance in extremely sparse observation regimes, and whether more expressive classifiers can close the remaining gap on noisy datasets like P19 without sacrificing the efficiency advantage.
