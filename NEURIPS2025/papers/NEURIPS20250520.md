## Problem

Stellarator design is a high-dimensional, constrained optimization problem that requires expensive physics simulations and deep domain expertise, yet the community lacks standardized optimization benchmarks with strong baselines and large-scale datasets of quasi-isodynamic (QI) configurations with computed ideal-MHD equilibria. This gap bottlenecks progress for optimization and machine learning researchers who want to engage with stellarator design without first mastering the full physics toolchain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250520/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The optimization problems take a plasma boundary parameterized by a truncated Fourier series in cylindrical coordinates (up to 4 poloidal and toroidal modes, yielding 80 degrees of freedom under stellarator symmetry with R_{0,0}=1 fixed) and produce scored configurations via VMEC++ vacuum ideal-MHD equilibria. The dataset of ~158k configurations includes equilibria at five volume-averaged beta levels (1–5%) alongside 12 performance metrics. The generative model operates in a PCA-reduced latent space of boundary coefficients and outputs new Fourier-coefficient vectors that are validated against the VMEC++ oracle.

## Method

The dataset is generated by sampling diverse target QI fields from the omnigenous field parameterization of Dudt et al., then searching for matching plasma boundaries via three strategies: physics-informed heuristics, near-axis expansion (pyQSC), and stage-one optimization using DESC (with automatic differentiation) or VMEC++ in the loop (with NGOpt from Nevergrad). For the optimization benchmarks, the primary baseline is an augmented Lagrangian method with a non-Euclidean (trust-region) proximal regularization, where each subproblem is solved by the NGOpt gradient-free meta-algorithm; this is compared against scipy-trust-constr and scipy-COBYQA. For generative modeling without oracle access, the pipeline reduces boundary coefficients via PCA, trains a Random Forest classifier to estimate per-constraint feasibility, fits a Gaussian Mixture Model on the feasible subset as a prior, and uses adaptive MCMC (Random-walk Metropolis-Hastings) to sample from the posterior defined by the classifier quasi-likelihood times the GMM prior, yielding novel feasible configurations from as few as 41–52 training examples.

## Results

Across three optimization benchmarks of increasing complexity, the ALM-NGOpt baseline is the only method that produces feasible solutions (all constraint violations ≤ 0.009). On the geometric problem, it achieves a normalized score of 0.969 with ε_max = 1.27 and constraint violation of 0.0002. On the simple-to-build QI problem, it reaches a score of 0.431 with L̃_∇B = 8.61. For the multi-objective MHD-stable problem, it achieves a hypervolume indicator of 130.0 across four aspect-ratio-constrained instances (A* ∈ {6, 8, 10, 12}), with the A ≤ 10 instance being fully feasible (constraint violation = 0.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three optimization approaches: scipy-trust-constr (gradient-based trust-region interior point), scipy-COBYQA (gradient-free), and ALM-NGOpt (augmented Lagrangian with NGOpt gradient-free meta-algorithm). ALM-NGOpt is the only method that satisfies all constraints in both the geometric and simple-to-build problems, with normalized constraint violations of 0.0002 and 0.009 respectively, compared to 0.301–3.25 for the SciPy-based methods. On the geometric problem, ALM-NGOpt and scipy-COBYQA achieve the same ε_max of 1.27, but ALM-NGOpt reduces constraint violation by a factor of ~4,700 (0.0002 vs. 0.953). On the simple-to-build problem, scipy-trust-constr reports a lower L̃_∇B (2.10 vs. 8.61), but its solution is infeasible (constraint violation 3.25) and was computed at reduced fidelity; scipy-COBYQA is both infeasible and achieves a worse objective (14.4). The paper notes that SciPy-based optimizers ran for ~40 hours on 4 vCPUs, while ALM-NGOpt required 18–34 hours on a 96-vCPU machine, suggesting the feasibility advantage comes at comparable or higher compute cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established two-stage stellarator design pipeline (field optimization followed by coil design) and leverages existing physics solvers (VMEC++, DESC) and optimization frameworks (SIMSOPT, Nevergrad) that have made stellarator design more accessible in recent years. It departs from prior large-scale stellarator datasets (QUASR, Landreman's QA/QH collections) by focusing specifically on quasi-isodynamic configurations and, crucially, by including computed ideal-MHD equilibria rather than near-axis expansions. The generative modeling component (PCA + Random Forest + GMM + MCMC) repurposes standard statistical tools for the novel purpose of identifying feasible regions in a high-dimensional constrained design space without querying expensive physics oracles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale (~158k) QI-focused stellarator dataset with full MHD equilibria and the first set of standardized optimization benchmarks with strong baselines for stage-one stellarator design, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify as a bottleneck for cross-disciplinary progress. The demonstration that a simple generative pipeline can produce oracle-validated feasible configurations from only 41–52 training examples is encouraging for data-driven fusion design, though the evidence is limited to two relaxed problems. Key limitations include the approximate (finite-budget) nature of the QI fields, the restriction to plasma boundaries without coil design, and the narrow mode-number range (≤4), leaving open whether these approaches scale to reactor-relevant configurations with more complex geometries.
