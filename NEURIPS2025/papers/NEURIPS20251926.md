## Problem

Existing aerodynamic and engineering datasets are confined to 2D configurations, RANS-based simulations, or limited geometric variations, and none provide a reproducible end-to-end pipeline for large-scale 3D multiphysics (fluid-structure interaction) data with multi-modal annotations. This paper addresses the absence of a unified resource that combines broad parametric design diversity, high-fidelity LES-FEA co-simulation, and structured text-to-geometry pairings for automotive component design and surrogate modeling.

## Input / Output

The surrogate models consume point-cloud coordinates, surface normals, and mesh connectivity from ~400K-cell surface meshes and regress per-node normalized velocity (Ux, Uy, Uz), pressure (p), and displacement (Dx, Dy, Dz) fields at eight temporal snapshots corresponding to a 0.07 s impulsive acceleration pulse applied at 30° to the flow. The generative-AI modality pairs structured natural-language prompts (specifying cutout count, inter-curve spacing, and center-plane offset) with 3D point-cloud hood geometry for supervised fine-tuning of LLMs. The dataset is organized in a curriculum-learning sequence from fewest to most cutout curves across three tiers (≈4500, 12000+, and 10000+ randomized geometries).

## Method

The dataset is generated through a fully automated pipeline: base hood shells are reconstructed via convex-hull extrusion from 100 inner-hood CADs, cutout boundaries are extracted using SAM-2 segmentation on 2D surface projections, and ~1750 unique cutout profiles are clustered into nine groups via K-Means on (perimeter, area) before being re-embedded into 3D shells under bilateral-symmetry and user-defined spacing constraints. Each geometry is simulated with a one-way coupled LES (Spalart-Allmaras DDES, ~750K polyhedral cells) and linear-elastic FEA (~400K cells) co-simulation via preCICE nearest-projection pressure mapping, consuming ~5000 CPU-core-hours across 16,000+ variants. For benchmarking, five encoder-core-decoder architectures (MLP, PointNet, GraphSAGE, Graph U-Net, PointGNNConv) are trained with AdamW and MSE loss on the 4500-geometry subset, revealing that simple point-wise models achieve lower in-distribution MSE while graph-based models with tuned neighborhood size (mNN) generalize better to unseen geometries, quantifying a speed-accuracy-generalization trade-off across model classes.

## Results

On the in-distribution (ID) test, MLP achieves the lowest MSE for Ux (0.25×10⁻²), Uz (0.44×10⁻²), and pressure p (0.37×10⁻²), while PointNet provides the best overall balance across all seven fields. On the out-of-distribution (OOD) test, the trend reverses: Graph U-Net attains the best pressure MSE (4.05×10⁻²) and PointGNNConv achieves the lowest transverse displacement errors (Dy = 4.78×10⁻², Dz = 13.67×10⁻²). Inference latency ranges from ~9.7 ms (MLP, GraphSAGE) to ~249 ms (Graph U-Net), quantifying the speed–accuracy trade-off across architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five architectures (MLP, PointNet, GraphSAGE, Graph U-Net, PointGNNConv) on the same AutoHood3D dataset, so there is no single 'proposed method'—the contribution is the benchmark itself. In-distribution, MLP and PointNet decisively outperform all three graph-based models: MLP leads on Ux, Uz, and p, while PointNet leads on all three displacement components (Dx = 0.29×10⁻² vs. 2.80–14.88×10⁻² for the others). GraphSAGE and PointGNNConv suffer the worst ID errors, with PointGNNConv reaching 15.41×10⁻² on Dz. Out-of-distribution, the ranking inverts: Graph U-Net achieves the best pressure MSE (4.05×10⁻² vs. 4.96 for MLP and 15.45 for PointNet), and PointGNNConv wins on Dy (4.78×10⁻²) and Dz (13.67×10⁻²), indicating that graph message-passing captures global flow structure and localized cutout-induced deformation more robustly on unseen geometries. The ablation in Appendix B attributes MLP's ID advantage to high-capacity point-wise memorization (hidden width 256) rather than spatial aggregation, confirmed by an ablation showing OOD error improves as hidden dimension shrinks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of large-scale CFD/FSI datasets (Airfrans, DrivAerNet++, WindsorML, PDEBench) by introducing the first open-source 3D fluid-structure interaction benchmark with multi-modal annotations (STL meshes, point clouds, time-resolved LES/FEA fields, and natural language prompts). It builds directly on OpenFOAM's pimpleFoam and solidDisplacementFoam solvers, coupled via preCICE, and leverages SAM-2 for automated cutout segmentation and Gemma3 for text-to-geometry corpus generation. The base hood geometries are drawn from the CarHoods10K dataset [43], which the authors extend from inner-hood-only designs to full dual-shell models with engineered cutouts. Unlike prior datasets that are confined to 2D, RANS-based, or single-physics, AutoHood3D unifies LES turbulence modeling, one-way FSI coupling, and generative-AI text annotations in a single reproducible pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoHood3D fills a concrete gap: no prior public resource offers a reproducible end-to-end 3D FSI dataset with sufficient geometric diversity (16,000+ variants), multi-modal representations, and structured text annotations for text-to-geometry synthesis in an engineering context. The benchmarking results provide practical guidance—MLP/PointNet are faster and better in-distribution, while Graph U-Net and PointGNNConv generalize better to unseen geometries—quantifying the speed-accuracy-generalization trade-off for FSI surrogates. Key open questions remain: whether one-way coupling suffices for all manufacturing scenarios, whether 2D-projected cutouts adequately capture 3D geometric complexity, and whether physics-informed loss functions (enforcing momentum/force conservation at the interface) can close the systematic displacement prediction errors observed across all architectures. The modular workflow also positions the dataset as a template for generating new FSI benchmarks in other engineering domains.
