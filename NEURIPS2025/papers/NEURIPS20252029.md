## Problem

Existing DPO-based preference alignment for text-to-motion generation (e.g., MoDiPO) suffers from two fundamental limitations: offline DPO overfits to a single fixed unpreferred motion group per text condition, while online DPO is biased toward high-probability motions and ignores low-probability but high-preference samples. This paper theoretically characterizes both failure modes and proposes a unified solution that exploits the complementary strengths of offline and online data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt c, a set of offline preferred motions x^w from human-annotated datasets (HumanML3D, KIT-ML), and K=2–4 dynamically generated candidate motions from the current policy model π_θ. A reward model (TMR) scores all candidates, and a threshold τ=0.45 partitions them into valuable unpreferred (score < τ) and high-preference unpreferred (score ≥ τ) subsets. The output is a fine-tuned diffusion or autoregressive text-to-motion model that produces 3D joint-angle motion sequences aligned with human preference.

## Method

SoPo trains on 'semi-online' pairs: high-quality preferred motions from offline datasets paired with diverse unpreferred motions generated online by the policy model. The core loss (Eq. 9) is a DPO objective where x^w comes from the offline set and x^l is the lowest-rewarded online sample. Two key mechanisms address practical challenges: (1) a threshold-based distribution separation splits online samples into valuable unpreferred (r < τ, used in standard DPO contrast) and high-preference unpreferred (r ≥ τ, where the loss reduces to a single-sided log-sigmoid on the preferred motion only, avoiding penalizing good samples); (2) an offline reweighting β_w(x^w) = β(C − S(x^w)) based on minimum cosine similarity between the preferred motion and all online candidates prioritizes preferred motions that are far from the generated distribution, widening the effective preference gap. The full objective is adapted to diffusion models by replacing log-ratio terms with per-timestep denoising loss differences (Eq. 16), and the entire procedure is summarized in Algorithm 1, requiring only ~20× the base generation time versus ~121K× for MoDiPO.

## Results

On HumanML3D, MLD*+SoPo achieves the best R-Precision (0.528/0.722/0.827 for Top-1/2/3), MM Dist (2.939), and FID (0.174) among all compared text-to-motion methods, improving over the MLD* baseline by +4.76% R-Precision, +3.70% MM Dist, and +61.3% FID. On KIT-ML, MoMask+SoPo achieves the best Top-1 R-Precision (0.446), MM Dist (2.783), and FID (0.176). For preference alignment specifically (Table 1), MDM(fast)+SoPo gains +5.27% R-Precision and +2.91% MM Dist over the MDM(fast) baseline, while MLD+SoPo gains +3.25% MM Dist over MLD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLD*+SoPo outperforms all 15 compared baselines on R-Precision (all top-k), MM Dist, and FID on HumanML3D. The closest competitor, Motion Mamba [7], achieves 0.502/0.693/0.792 R-Precision and 3.060 MM Dist, while SoPo reaches 0.528/0.722/0.827 and 2.939 respectively; however, Motion Mamba has slightly higher Diversity (9.871 vs. 9.584). Against the direct preference-alignment baseline MoDiPO-T [9], SoPo improves R-Precision Top-1 from 0.455 to 0.528 (+16%) and reduces MM Dist from 3.267 to 2.939 (−10%). On KIT-ML, MoMask+SoPo achieves the best Top-1 R-Precision (0.446), MM Dist (2.783), and FID (0.176), surpassing MoMask alone (0.433, 2.779, 0.204) and all other methods. In the text-to-image experiment (Table S1), SoPo applied to Flux-Dev achieves the best HPS (0.321), CLIP (0.396), and Image Reward (1.194) scores while requiring only 32 GPU hours versus 316 for online DPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al.) to text-to-motion generation, building directly on MoDiPO (Pappa et al.), the first work to apply DPO-based preference alignment to motion synthesis. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical analysis showing that offline DPO overfits to a one-point preference distribution while online DPO suffers from biased sampling where low-probability high-reward motions receive vanishing gradients. The proposed semi-online loss (SoPo) hybridizes both paradigms—using offline preferred motions for a clear preference gap and online-generated unpreferred motions for diversity—drawing on the diffusion-specific DPO formulation from Wallace et al. (Diffusion-DPO).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Preference alignment for text-to-motion was essentially unaddressed before MoDiPO, and this paper provides the first systematic theoretical characterization of why existing DPO variants fail in this setting, offering a principled fix. The practical impact is notable: SoPo achieves the best R-Precision and MM Dist among all compared methods on HumanML3D and KIT-ML, and extends to text-to-image (Flux-Dev) showing generality. A key open question is the dependence on reward models (TMR) whose generalization across diverse, open-domain motion scenarios remains limited, as the authors acknowledge. Future work could explore reward-model-free variants or scaling the semi-online approach to longer, more complex motion sequences.
