## Problem

Existing methods for multimodal sentiment analysis under missing modalities rely on explicit modality reconstruction or teacher-student distillation, both of which require fully observed training data for supervision—data that is often scarce in practice. Additionally, these approaches treat each sample independently, overlooking the potential of cross-sample semantic information to enrich representations and improve robustness when modalities are incomplete.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254754/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-extracted features from up to three modalities (BERT contextual embeddings for text, MTCNN+OpenFace facial features for video, and COVAREP MFCC/pitch features for audio), with missing modalities zero-padded. It produces a sentiment prediction: a continuous regression score (range −3 to 3) for MOSI/MOSEI or a four-class emotion label (neutral, happy, sad, angry) for IEMOCAP. Uniquely, the method also consumes representations from other samples in the current batch as auxiliary context, making its effective input a set of B samples rather than a single instance.

## Method

HME avoids explicit modality reconstruction by enriching each observed modality with semantically relevant cues retrieved from other samples in the batch. A Modality Enhancement step uses cosine similarity with a threshold to select related samples, averages their representations, and refines them via a Perceiver network with learnable prompts (both modality-specific and shared). A Hyper-Modality Generation step then performs cross-attention between modality-specific and shared prompts to distill cross-sample sentiment signals into a unified representation per modality. A Variational Information Bottleneck compresses these representations, filtering noise and redundancy while producing a per-modality standard deviation. Finally, an uncertainty-aware fusion module uses this standard deviation (inverted and clamped) as adaptive weights to balance original and enhanced representations, ensuring that noisier or less reliable enhanced features contribute less to the final prediction.

## Results

Under the fixed missing protocol, HME achieves an average ACC/F1 of 76.4/76.2 on MOSI and 79.0/77.4 on IEMOCAP, outperforming the best baselines (IMDer at 75.5/75.3 on MOSI and LNLN at 77.0/74.6 on IEMOCAP). Under the random missing protocol, HME reaches 80.0/79.7 on MOSI and 79.3/78.2 on IEMOCAP, yielding average improvements of 2.0 ACC and 2.3 F1 on MOSI, and 1.9 ACC and 3.9 F1 on IEMOCAP over the strongest competing method. On MOSEI, HME similarly leads with 76.7/75.6 (fixed) and 78.9/78.0 (random) average ACC/F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HME consistently outperforms all five baselines (GCNet, MPLMM, DiCMoR, IMDer, LNLN) across every missing-modality scenario on both MOSI and IEMOCAP under both fixed and random missing protocols. On MOSI (fixed missing), HME's average of 76.4/76.2 exceeds the closest competitor IMDer (75.5/75.3) by ~0.9 points, with the largest margin over LNLN (70.8/73.8) and MPLMM (71.7/65.9). On IEMOCAP (fixed missing), HME improves ACC by 2.0 and F1 by 2.8 over the best baseline (LNLN at 77.0/74.6), and the gap widens to 3.9 F1 under the random missing protocol. Under the random missing protocol on MOSI, HME leads IMDer by 2.0 ACC and 2.3 F1 on average, with the advantage growing as the missing rate increases (e.g., at MR=0.7, HME scores 73.5/71.7 vs. IMDer's 67.7/67.1). The only notable exception is on MOSEI (fixed missing), where LNLN reports an anomalously high F1 of 77.2 for the V and A-only scenarios (likely a reporting artifact), but HME still leads on average (76.7/75.6 vs. LNLN's 75.1/81.4 inflated by those outliers).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant reconstruction-based paradigm for MSA with missing modalities (IMDer, DiCMoR, GCNet) and teacher-student distillation approaches (HRLF, LNLN) by instead enriching observed modalities with cross-sample semantic cues retrieved from the current batch. It builds on the Perceiver architecture (Jaegle et al.) for prompt-based representation refinement and the Variational Information Bottleneck (Alemi et al.) for denoising, but repurposes both within a novel cross-sample retrieval pipeline. Unlike prior hyper-modality representation work (ALMT, LNLN, MPLMM) that assumes a specific anchor modality (typically language) is always present or requires pretraining on complete data, HME generates enhanced representations from any available modality without these prerequisites.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-sample enhancement strategy offers a practical alternative to modality reconstruction for real-world MSA systems where full-modality training data is scarce, and the uncertainty-aware fusion provides a principled mechanism for weighting unreliable enhanced representations. The consistent 1–3 point gains across all missing-rate levels and both protocols on three benchmarks demonstrate robustness, though the authors acknowledge that VIB-generated representations can still overlap across modalities and that noise within the original (non-enhanced) representations is not addressed. Open questions include how the method scales with batch size (the retrieval pool), whether the similarity-threshold-based sample selection generalizes to out-of-distribution or adversarial inputs, and whether the approach extends beyond sentiment to other multimodal tasks.
