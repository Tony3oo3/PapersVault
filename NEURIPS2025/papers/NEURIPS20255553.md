## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction (HOI) synthesis methods operate on closed-set objects and predefined tasks, failing to generalize to unseen object categories or interpret open-vocabulary, free-form language instructions. No prior framework unifies 3D spatial affordance reasoning with long-horizon, physically plausible motion generation for novel objects, leaving a critical gap between LLM-based text-to-motion approaches (which lack 3D grounding) and MLLM-based 3D perception methods (which cannot synthesize continuous interaction dynamics).

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object point cloud (upsampled for fine-grained affordance inference, then downsampled for training efficiency) and a free-form natural-language instruction (e.g., 'I'm feeling thirsty, could you find a water bottle and take a sip') as input. It produces three structured outputs: a spatial affordance map identifying geometrically plausible interaction regions on the object, a temporally ordered sub-task sequence of atomic actions, and a long-horizon HOI sequence comprising per-frame hand joint positions and object 6-DoF poses. The sub-task sequence can span multiple distinct manipulation phases (e.g., grasp → twist → lift), with the diffusion model generating continuous motion including in-between transitions between sub-sequences.

## Method

OpenHOI is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 fine-tunes a 3D MLLM (ShapeLLM backbone: ReCon++ point-cloud encoder + LLaMA language model) via a coarse-to-fine strategy—first on large-scale static affordance datasets to instill open-vocabulary affordance priors, then on dynamic HOI data for fine-grained spatial grounding. The model uses a dedicated <AFF> segmentation token (following Lisa) to emit dense per-point affordance masks alongside an autoregressive sub-task decomposition, trained with cross-entropy for task tokens and Dice+BCE for affordance masks. Stage 2 is a transformer-based HOI diffusion model conditioned on the affordance map, CLIP-encoded sub-task text, and object point cloud, trained with denoising loss plus distance-map and relative-orientation losses, and steered by classifier-free guidance (10% condition masking, scale 2.5). During inference, a training-free physics refinement stage applies three loss-guided corrections—affordance contact alignment, penetration prevention, and motion in-between smoothing—via a DSG-inspired Spherical Gaussian Constraint that blends steepest-descent gradient steps with random sampling directions to enforce physical plausibility without distribution shift.

## Results

On GRAB (seen/unseen), OpenHOI achieves MPJPE of 47.64/51.34 mm, FOL of 0.26/0.27, FID of 26.43/28.29, and MModality of 24.59/19.91, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On ARCTIC (seen/unseen), it reaches MPJPE of 45.15/47.25 mm, FOL of 0.25/0.28, FID of 19.74/20.05, and MModality of 15.25/12.66. The largest margins appear on unseen objects, where OpenHOI reduces MPJPE by 9–41 mm and FID by 8–47 points relative to the strongest baseline (Text2HOI), confirming strong open-world generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenHOI consistently outperforms all four baselines (MDM, TM2T, MotionGPT, Text2HOI) across every metric on both GRAB and ARCTIC, for both seen and unseen splits. The closest competitor is Text2HOI, which also uses a diffusion model for text-guided HOI; on GRAB-unseen, OpenHOI reduces MPJPE from 60.67 to 51.34 mm (−15.4%) and FID from 36.96 to 28.29 (−23.5%), while also improving MModality from 10.98 to 19.91, indicating substantially richer multi-modal generation. On ARCTIC-unseen, the gap widens further: MPJPE drops from 57.83 (Text2HOI) to 47.25 mm, and FID from 25.22 to 20.05. LLM-based baselines (MotionGPT, HOIGPT) suffer the largest degradation on unseen objects (MPJPE 66–93 mm on GRAB-unseen), confirming that discrete-token LLM architectures lack the 3D grounding needed for open-world generalization. Diversity scores also favor OpenHOI on unseen splits (3.61 on GRAB, 2.49 on ARCTIC), whereas baselines collapse toward near-zero diversity, suggesting they produce mode-collapsed outputs for novel objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: LLM-based HOI generation (HOIGPT, MotionGPT), which operates on discrete motion tokens without 3D spatial grounding, and 3D MLLM-based affordance understanding (ShapeLLM, LASO, 3D-AffordanceLLM), which perceives object functionality but does not synthesize continuous interaction sequences. OpenHOI unifies these by fine-tuning ShapeLLM to jointly produce affordance maps and decomposed sub-task sequences, then feeding those as conditions into a diffusion-based HOI generator. The training-free physics refinement stage draws directly on the Spherical Gaussian Constraint (DSG) framework for loss-guided sampling, and the <AFF> segmentation token design follows Lisa's approach to dense prediction within autoregressive language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenHOI establishes a practical pipeline for open-vocabulary, long-horizon HOI synthesis that generalizes to unseen object categories, which is a prerequisite for deploying manipulation in AR/VR and dexterous robotics. However, the paper acknowledges significant limitations: performance degrades noticeably after roughly three consecutive actions (>450 frames), the model cannot target specific instances in a scene (e.g., 'the second cabinet'), and fine-grained dynamics such as fluid simulation remain out of reach. The extreme-case H2O results (MPJPE jumping from ~51 to ~76–81) reveal that 'open-world' generalization is still fragile under full distribution shift, leaving compositional long-horizon tasks like 'cook a meal' as an open challenge that may require hierarchical or chain-of-thought reasoning.
