## Problem

Existing multi-modal LLM benchmarks (MMBench, SEED-Bench, MME) cover only a narrow slice of face and human understanding—typically celebrity recognition, action recognition, or social relation—leaving critical abilities like deepfake detection, face anti-spoofing, cross-pose/cross-age face recognition, and crowd counting unexplored. No dedicated, comprehensive benchmark exists to systematically evaluate how well MLLMs understand faces and humans across the full spectrum of perception and reasoning tasks, nor to identify which specialist models are still needed to supplement MLLM capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250322/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each problem presents one or two RGB images (single face crops, full-body images, or image pairs for recognition/re-identification tasks) alongside a multiple-choice text question with 2–4 options. The model must output a single option letter; answers are extracted via regex with a ChatGPT fallback. The benchmark comprises 1,800 problems each in a development set and a test set, available in both English and Chinese, with options shuffled per problem to prevent letter-position bias. Scoring uses weighted accuracy where each L2 ability carries equal weight, and L3 sub-abilities share their parent's weight equally.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-level hierarchical ability taxonomy (L1: face vs. human × perception vs. reasoning; L2: 10 abilities; L3: 18 fine-grained dimensions) and builds Face-Human-Bench by collecting images and annotations from 16 public face/human datasets, then transforming them into multiple-choice problems via a semi-automatic pipeline. The image processing step applies task-specific operations (cropping, concatenating, adding bounding boxes) while the text processing step converts original labels into one correct option plus distractors, with ChatGPT assisting in generating plausible incorrect options and fixing grammar. Human reviewers verify unambiguity and single-answer correctness before inclusion. The evaluation protocol assesses 25 MLLMs from 13 families under zero-shot settings, then probes ability correlations (Pearson coefficients), relative-position sensitivity (a new RPSS metric measuring score deltas between original and cropped/boxed image versions), and the effect of hints and 1-stage/2-stage task-specific Chain-of-Thought prompting, finally comparing MLLM performance against early specialist models on 13 L3 tasks using a normalized relative score to flag tasks where specialist models remain necessary.

## Results

On Face-Human-Bench (English, zero-shot), overall scores range from 27.9% (MiniGPT-4-7B) to 76.4% (InternVL-Chat-v1.2-Plus). The best open-source model, InternVL-Chat-v1.2-Plus (76.4%), outperforms the best closed-source model, GPT-4o (70.0%), by 6.4 points. Adding hints and 1-stage task-specific CoT instructions improves GPT-4o's overall score from 70.0% to 79.9% (+9.9), while having negligible or slightly negative effects on InternVL-Chat-v1.2-Plus (76.4% → 75.0%). On specialist-model comparison, MLLMs significantly underperform on deepfake detection (relative score 0.17), crowd counting (−0.06), and challenging face recognition scenarios (cross-pose 0.48, cross-age 0.39, similar-looking 0.42, occluded 0.26).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 25 evaluated MLLMs, InternVL-Chat-v1.2-Plus achieves the highest overall score (76.4%), narrowly edging out LLaVA-NeXT-34B (76.3%) and InternVL-Chat-v1.5 (74.1%) among open-source models, and surpassing the best closed-source model GPT-4o (70.0%) by 6.4 points. GPT-4V (69.3%) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (67.5%) trail GPT-4o, while Gemini-1.5-Pro (60.3%) underperforms despite strong face recognition (85.6%, best among all models). At the L2/L3 level, GLM-4V-9B leads in compound expression recognition, facial expression recognition, and action recognition, while CogVLM2-19B-Chat excels in relative position understanding. In the specialist-model comparison (Table 3), MLLMs match or exceed early specialist models on age estimation (relative score 1.01), basic expression (1.06), face anti-spoofing (1.24), action recognition (−0.06, near parity), and person re-identification (0.86), but fall significantly short on deepfake detection (0.17), crowd counting (−0.06), and all four challenging face recognition scenarios (0.26–0.48), indicating that specialist models remain necessary for these tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground as the first benchmark dedicated exclusively to face and human understanding in MLLMs, departing from general-purpose MLLM benchmarks (MMBench, SEED-Bench, MME, LAMM) that include only a handful of scattered face/human abilities (celebrity recognition, action recognition, social relation). Rather than collecting new data, it repurposes 16 established specialist-model datasets (CelebA, UTKFace, RAF-DB, FaceForensics++, LFW variants, Market-1501, etc.) through a semi-automatic pipeline that converts specialist-model annotations into multiple-choice MLLM problems. The hierarchical three-level ability taxonomy (2 L1 → 10 L2 → 18 L3 dimensions) provides a structured evaluation framework that prior general benchmarks lack, and the introduction of the Relative Position Sensitivity Score (RPSS) as a diagnostic metric is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the face/human community with a concrete map of where MLLMs succeed and fail, revealing that specialist models remain essential for deepfake detection (relative score 0.17), crowd counting (worse than random), and challenging face recognition scenarios (cross-pose, cross-age, similar-looking, occluded). The finding that CoT prompting yields a ~10% improvement for GPT-4o but zero benefit for the best open-source model (InternVL-Chat-v1.2-Plus) highlights a critical reasoning-capability gap that limits the practical deployment of open-source MLLMs in face/human-centric applications. Open questions include whether the CoT gap can be closed through better instruction tuning or larger open-source models, and whether the specialist-model augmentation strategy demonstrated in the paper scales to production multi-modal agents.
