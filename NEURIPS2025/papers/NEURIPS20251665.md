## Problem

Existing tensor compressed sensing (TCS) methods rely on global sensing (measuring the entire tensor) or slice-wise sensing (measuring full lateral slices), which is impractical in distributed or streaming settings where only individual tubes (mode-3 fiber vectors) are accessible. This paper introduces the tube-wise TCS model, where each tube is measured independently by its own sensing matrix, and provides the first provable exact-recovery guarantees under this more fine-grained local sensing paradigm.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a third-order tensor X* ∈ R^{n×n×n3} of known tubal rank r, observed through tube-wise Gaussian measurements: each tube X*(i,j,:) ∈ R^{n3} is projected by an independent matrix Z_ij ∈ R^{m×n3}, yielding m scalar measurements per tube (total n²m measurements). The output is the recovered tensor X = A * B^T via Burer-Monteiro factors A ∈ R^{n×r×n3} and B ∈ R^{n×r×n3}. A key structural property is that the per-tube sample size m required for exact recovery scales as Õ(max{r⁵n3/n, 1}), decreasing linearly with the spatial dimension n — a property unique to this local model.

## Method

The paper parameterizes the low-tubal-rank tensor via Burer-Monteiro factorization X = A * B (tensor product) and minimizes a non-convex quadratic loss that includes a balancing term ‖A^T * A − B^T * B‖_F² to resolve the non-uniqueness of the factorization. The loss is solved by gradient descent with a spectral initialization obtained from a truncated t-SVD of the adjoint-map reconstruction. The central theoretical contribution is proving linear convergence of GD under a tube-wise sample complexity that decreases linearly with spatial dimension n, achieved by showing (via a leave-one-out analysis with 2n auxiliary sequences) that GD iterates are implicitly regularized to maintain bounded tensor incoherence throughout optimization. This incoherence preservation, combined with a tensor Restricted Isometry Property that exploits the tube-wise independence structure, ensures the empirical Hessian concentrates on its population counterpart, yielding the linear rate.

## Results

The proposed tube-wise TCS method achieves exact recovery (relative Frobenius error ≤ 10⁻³) with dramatically fewer tube-wise samples than all baselines: e.g., only m=6 samples per tube for n₃=30, r=4, n=50, versus 13,500 (FGD), 24,000 (TNN), 16,500 (Schatten-2/3 TNN), and 268 (Alt-PGD-Min). Runtime is also substantially lower (18.9 s vs. 1,626–2,396 s for global methods). A key finding is that increasing the spatial dimension n from 20 to 50 *reduces* the required tube-wise sample size (e.g., from m=12 to m=6 for n₃=30, r=4), a property unique to the tube-wise model and confirmed by phase-transition diagrams (Fig. 2). The method converges linearly in O(κ² log(1/ε)) iterations with spectral initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four t-SVD-based TCS baselines: FGD [26] (global, non-convex, linear rate), TNN [28] (global, convex), Schatten-2/3 TNN [20] (global, non-convex), and Alt-PGD-Min [64] (slice-wise local, non-convex, linear rate). The proposed tube-wise method wins decisively on both sample efficiency and runtime across all tested configurations. Against the closest competitor Alt-PGD-Min (the only other local sensing method), the proposed method requires 44× fewer samples (m=6 vs. 268) and runs 8× faster (18.9 s vs. 155.9 s) in the hardest setting (n₃=30, r=4, n=50). Against global methods (FGD, TNN, Schatten-2/3 TNN), the gap is orders of magnitude: the proposed method needs 2,250×–4,000× fewer samples. A distinctive advantage is that the tube-wise sample complexity *decreases* with spatial dimension n (e.g., m drops from 12 to 6 as n goes from 20 to 50), whereas all baselines require more samples as n grows. IR-tTNN [60] is compared only theoretically in Table 1 (sub-linear convergence, no sample complexity guarantee) and is not included in the empirical comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local tensor compressed sensing (TCS) line from slice-wise sensing [64] to a finer-grained tube-wise sensing model, where each mode-3 fiber vector is measured independently. The optimization framework adapts the Burer-Monteiro factorization [5] and gradient descent with spectral initialization from non-convex matrix recovery to the t-SVD tensor setting, while the leave-one-out (LOO) analysis technique is borrowed from matrix completion [8, 32] and generalized to tensors. The tensor incoherence condition and tensor RIP are new formulations tailored to the tube-wise sensing structure, distinguishing this work from global TCS methods (TNN [28], FGD [26]) that require full-tensor measurements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that tube-wise sample complexity decreases linearly with spatial dimension n, meaning larger tensors actually require fewer measurements per tube—a property absent from all prior TCS results and nearly matching the information-theoretic lower bound in total samples. However, the r^5 rank dependence (versus r degrees of freedom) is acknowledged as an analysis artifact, the quadratic condition-number dependence limits convergence on ill-conditioned tensors, and the framework is restricted to third-order tensors with known tubal rank and Gaussian sensing. Extending to over-parameterized factorizations, unknown rank, and higher-order tensors under general t-SVD transforms are identified as important open directions.
