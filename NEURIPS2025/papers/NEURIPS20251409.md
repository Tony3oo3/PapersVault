## Problem

Speculative decoding for on-device LLMs typically requires a draft model that is pretrained or distilled offline for a specific target model family, creating tight coupling that limits model flexibility and incurs distillation overhead. Two practical challenges remain unsolved: (1) vocabulary mismatch between draft and target models from different tokenizer families breaks the standard rejection-sampling formulation, and (2) the drafter must adapt online as target models change due to user personalization or task switching, rather than being frozen after offline alignment.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt and operates a small draft model (Llama-68M, 2 layers, 768 hidden) alongside a large target model (7B–32B) with potentially different vocabularies (e.g., 32K vs. 152K tokens). The drafter proposes k tokens autoregressively; these are translated into the target vocabulary space via direct mapping or n-gram cache lookup, verified in parallel by the target, and either accepted or corrected via residual resampling. The n-gram cache (typically 0.5–4.5 MB) stores cross-vocabulary sub-token-to-merged-token mappings and grows sub-linearly with processed tokens, making it feasible for on-device memory budgets.

## Method

OmniDraft introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, a cross-vocabulary n-gram cache stores mappings from sequences of draft sub-tokens to single target merged tokens, with a probability mapping rule that assigns the product of sub-token draft probabilities to the n-gram and subtracts that mass from the prefix sub-token to maintain approximate distributional correctness for the residual correction step. Second, a hybrid online distillation loss combines reverse KL divergence on direct-mapping tokens (computed in target vocabulary space) with negative log-likelihood on n-gram constituent tokens (computed in drafter vocabulary space), updating the drafter on-policy using accepted and corrected outputs from the target. Third, an acceptance prediction head (following SpecDec++) dynamically truncates the draft length based on cumulative rejection probability, trained either jointly with distillation or in an interleaved schedule that buffers acceptance labels across multiple distillation updates to mitigate distribution shift from the continuously adapting drafter.

## Results

On cross-vocabulary speculative decoding with Llama-68M as drafter, the full hybrid distillation loss (L_DM + λL_N-gram) achieves acceptance rates of 0.37–0.42 and speedups of 1.61x–1.70x on GSM8K with Llama3-8B and Qwen2-7B targets, compared to 0.10–0.14 acceptance and 0.94x–1.04x speedup for the direct-mapping-only baseline (SpD_DM). On same-vocabulary adaptive drafting with Vicuna-7B, the interleaved distill + adapt variant reaches up to 2.15x speedup and 0.52 acceptance rate on GSM8K, while the joint variant achieves the highest acceptance rate of 0.61 with 2.08x speedup. Overall, OmniDraft delivers 1.5–2x wall-time speedup across math reasoning, coding, and text generation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) SpD_DM, the direct-mapping-only cross-vocabulary speculative decoding (equivalent to UAG's translation layer), and (2) vanilla SpD (same-vocabulary speculative decoding without distillation or adaptive drafting). Against SpD_DM, OmniDraft's hybrid distillation (L_DM + λL_N-gram) consistently wins on both acceptance rate and speedup across all four tasks and both target models — e.g., on GSM8K with Llama3-8B, acceptance improves from 0.10 to 0.42 and speedup from 0.94x to 1.70x. Against vanilla SpD (same-vocabulary, Vicuna-7B target), the joint distill + adapt variant achieves 0.61 acceptance and 2.08x speedup on GSM8K versus SpD's 0.21 and 1.44x, while the interleaved variant reaches 2.15x speedup. The LoRA variant of the full method slightly underperforms full fine-tuning (e.g., 0.37 vs. 0.42 acceptance on GSM8K/Llama3-8B) but still substantially outperforms both baselines, confirming feasibility for on-device deployment with parameter-efficient adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) along three axes simultaneously: cross-vocabulary operation (building on UAG's direct-mapping translation layer but adding an n-gram cache to handle merged-token mismatches), online distillation (extending offline draft-target alignment à la DistillSpec/OSD to a continuous, on-policy setting inspired by GKD), and adaptive draft length (adopting SpecDec++'s acceptance-prediction head). The unifying contribution is the 'one drafter for all' paradigm, where a single 68M-parameter model pairs with arbitrary target models via dynamic LoRA adapters and a shared n-gram cache, rather than requiring per-target distilled drafters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in simplifying on-device LLM deployment: instead of maintaining a separate distilled drafter per target model and per task, a single lightweight model with swappable LoRA adapters and a sub-5MB n-gram cache can serve multiple targets across multiple tasks. Open questions include cache eviction under tight memory budgets, handling special/multi-modal tokens without direct mapping, and whether the online single-pass training is stable enough for truly long-running deployment. The paper also notes that adaptive drafting underperforms distill-only on GSM8K, suggesting the acceptance-prediction head struggles with non-stationary labels in online settings.
