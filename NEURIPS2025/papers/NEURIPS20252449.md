## Problem

Existing differentially private SVM methods are limited to binary classification. Extending them to multi-class via one-vs-rest or one-vs-one ensembles requires each training sample to be accessed once per binary classifier, multiplying the privacy budget consumption by the number of classes and forcing each sub-classifier to operate with only ε/c of the total budget. This paper asks whether multi-class SVMs can be built under DP with a single data access per sample, preserving the full privacy budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes d-dimensional tabular feature vectors with integer class labels (c classes) from UCI benchmark datasets. For weight perturbation, inputs are min-max normalized to unit norm to bound sensitivity; for gradient perturbation, per-sample gradients are clipped to a fixed norm R=1 before noise injection. The output is a class label produced by argmax over c class-wise linear scores, with the entire multi-class decision function inheriting the (ε,δ)-DP guarantee via the post-processing property.

## Method

PMSVM builds a multi-class classifier by solving a single all-in-one SVM optimization (primarily Crammer-Singer or M³-SVM formulations) rather than an ensemble of binary SVMs, so each sample is accessed exactly once and the full privacy budget ε is available. Two noise-injection strategies are provided: (i) Weight Perturbation (PMSVM-WP) solves the unified dual QP, recovers the primal weight matrix, and adds isotropic Gaussian noise calibrated by a new multi-class leave-one-out sensitivity bound Δ_w = (2C/n)√(λ_max(G)), which for CS-SVM reduces to √2·(2C/n) — only a √2 factor over the binary case while eliminating c-fold data access; (ii) Gradient Perturbation (PMSVM-GP) replaces the non-smooth hinge loss with a smooth approximation, then runs DP-SGD with per-sample gradient clipping and Gaussian noise, with an adaptive variant (PMSVM-AGP) using momentum. A utility-advantage theorem (Theorem 3) shows the excess loss from noise scales as O(dσ²(1−τ²)log T/(λT)), where τ<1 is the noise ratio relative to OvR/OvO, formally proving that the single-access all-in-one formulation yields smaller privacy-induced error.

## Results

On six UCI multi-class datasets (Cornell, Dermatology, HHAR, ISOLET, USPS, Vehicle), PMSVM-WP achieves accuracy gains of 0.30–0.50 over the best weight-perturbation baseline (OPERA) at ε=1, e.g., 0.599 vs. 0.244 on Cornell and 0.884 vs. 0.236 on USPS. For gradient perturbation, PMSVM-AGP reaches 0.929 on HHAR and 0.897 on USPS at ε=1, surpassing GRPUA (0.851, 0.747) and the DP-SGD linear baseline (0.887, 0.875). The advantage is most pronounced under tight privacy (ε=1) and narrows at ε=8, where all methods converge. PMSVM-GP also reduces per-iteration training time from 43.34 ms (GRPUA) to 9.27 ms on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PrivateSVM [11] (OvR weight perturbation), PMSVM-WP wins on 5 of 6 datasets with large margins at ε=1 (e.g., +0.35 on Cornell, +0.65 on USPS), losing only on Vehicle where the all-in-one SVM baseline itself underperforms. Against OPERA [9] (analytic Gaussian mechanism for OvR), PMSVM-WP similarly dominates at low ε (e.g., 0.711 vs. 0.296 on Dermatology, 0.262 vs. 0.046 on ISOLET) but the gap narrows at ε=8. For gradient perturbation, PMSVM-GP and PMSVM-AGP consistently outperform GRPUA [9] (OvR DP-SGD) across all six datasets and all ε values, with the largest gap on ISOLET (0.501 vs. 0.060 at ε=1). Compared to the DP-SGD linear-layer baseline [20], PMSVM-AGP wins on 5/6 datasets at ε=1 (e.g., 0.929 vs. 0.887 on HHAR, 0.897 vs. 0.875 on USPS) but is slightly behind on Dermatology (0.905 vs. 0.911), confirming that margin-maximizing gradients are competitive with cross-entropy loss under DP. Computationally, PMSVM-GP runs ~4.7× faster per iteration than GRPUA (9.27 vs. 43.34 ms), since it updates all class weights jointly rather than training c separate binary classifiers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends binary DP-SVM methods (Rubinstein et al. 2012, Chaudhuri et al. 2011) to the multi-class setting by leveraging all-in-one SVM formulations (Crammer-Singer, Weston-Watkins, M³-SVM) that solve a single joint convex optimization rather than c separate binary problems. The key departure from prior multi-class DP-SVM work (which relies on OvR/OvO and thus consumes the privacy budget c times via the composition theorem) is the observation that all-in-one SVMs access each sample only once, enabling the full ε to be allocated to a single mechanism. The gradient perturbation method builds directly on Nie et al.'s (2024) smoothed M³-SVM objective and the DP-SGD/moments-accountant framework of Abadi et al. (2016), while the weight perturbation sensitivity analysis generalizes the leave-one-out bound of Zhang (2001) to the multi-class dual formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for privacy-preserving multi-class classification with SVMs: reducing data accesses from O(c) to O(1) means the privacy budget is no longer diluted by the number of classes, which is critical for high-cardinality problems (e.g., 26-class ISOLET). The theoretical contribution—a tight sensitivity bound of (2C/n)√λ_max(G) that reduces to √2·(2C/n) for CS-SVM—provides a clean, implementable noise calibration. However, the evaluation is limited to six UCI tabular datasets with linear kernels, and the Vehicle dataset reveals that the all-in-one SVM baseline itself can underperform, suggesting the approach may not universally dominate OvR when the joint QP is poorly conditioned. Open questions include extension to kernel (non-linear) all-in-one SVMs, scalability to very high-dimensional or image data, and whether the single-access advantage persists under adaptive or sequential query settings.
