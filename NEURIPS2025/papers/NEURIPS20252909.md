## Problem

Model-agnostic data valuation methods that score training points without fitting a model remain inadequate: Wasserstein-based approaches like LAVA rely on non-unique dual potentials (yielding non-deterministic scores) and expensive Sinkhorn approximations whose rankings drift substantially from true leave-one-out orderings. The paper asks whether a distributional distance exists that admits a closed-form, deterministic influence function scalable to web-scale datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy training set, a small clean validation set (as few as 30 samples suffice for reliable scores), and a classifier trained only on the validation set to estimate P(y|x) for each training point. It outputs a single scalar net influence score per training example, combining a feature-level marginal MMD term and a label-level conditional MCMD term weighted by a fixed balancing factor λ=0.03. No model is trained on the training data itself; the Gaussian kernel bandwidth is set via the median heuristic on sampled pairwise distances.

## Method

KAIROS derives a closed-form influence function for MMD (Proposition 1): the influence of point x equals E_P[k(x',x)] − E_Q[k(x',x)], which avoids the intractable critic-shift term that makes Wasserstein-based scores non-deterministic and biased. For label-aware valuation, the paper extends to Expected Maximum Conditional Mean Discrepancy (E-MCMD), yielding a conditional influence proportional to −‖μ_{Y|X}^P(x) − φ(y)‖, which for categorical labels reduces to the L2 distance between the predicted class-probability vector and the one-hot label. The net score linearly combines both terms, and an efficient two-phase algorithm precomputes per-point kernel means (A_i, B_i) and label residuals (R_i) offline, then updates all scores in O(mN) time when a new batch of m points arrives—versus O(N²) recomputation in prior work. Theoretical guarantees include symmetry (equal marginal contributions yield equal scores) and density separation (a near-zero threshold cleanly partitions clean from corrupted points).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, KAIROS achieves 0.857 AUC for feature-noise detection and 0.791 AUC for label-noise detection, outperforming all baselines. On ImageNet (1.28M images), KAIROS reaches 0.869 AUC (feature noise) and 0.861 AUC (label noise) in ~8 minutes, versus SAVA's 0.817 and 0.484 in ~2 hours—a 15× speedup with substantially better label-noise detection. In the online streaming setting, KAIROS achieves up to 50× speedup over LAVA and 28× over KNN Shapley. KAIROS also requires only 30 validation samples to reach 0.77 detection accuracy, compared to 9,000 for LAVA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KAIROS outperforms LAVA [30] on both noise types (0.857 vs 0.837 feature; 0.791 vs 0.529 label AUC on CIFAR-10) and dramatically on ImageNet label noise (0.861 vs 0.484), while running 15× faster; LAVA's Wasserstein-based scores also require 9K validation samples to match KAIROS's 30-sample accuracy. Against Data-OOB [36], KAIROS wins on feature noise (0.857 vs 0.727) and label noise (0.791 vs 0.784), and is ~100× faster offline. KNN Shapley [27] is beaten on both noise types (0.857 vs 0.723; 0.791 vs 0.751) and by 28× in online runtime. DVRL [76] performs poorly across most tasks and is 10× slower offline. On ImageNet, SAVA [32] (LAVA's scalable variant) is outperformed on both AUC and runtime. Among model-based methods, KAIROS beats TRAK [46] (0.857 vs 0.638 feature; 0.791 vs 0.743 label) and LOGRA [8] (0.857 vs 0.565; 0.791 vs 0.663) without requiring any model training. The one comparison where KAIROS trails is against MAPLE [78] on label noise (0.791 vs 0.828), though KAIROS dominates on feature noise (0.857 vs 0.347) and offers broader task coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

KAIROS sits at the intersection of three research threads: the influence function framework from robust statistics (Law, 1986), kernel two-sample testing via MMD (Gretton et al., 2012), and conditional mean embeddings (Ren et al., 2016). It departs most sharply from the Wasserstein-based model-agnostic valuation line (LAVA, SAVA), arguing that the non-uniqueness of Kantorovich dual potentials and the bias of Sinkhorn regularization make those methods fundamentally unreliable for leave-one-out ranking. The key conceptual move is substituting MMD for Wasserstein as the distributional distance, which converts an intractable optimization-based influence into a closed-form kernel expression, and augmenting it with E-MCMD to capture label-specific corruptions that marginal MMD alone misses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 50× online speedup and 15× ImageNet speedup over the fastest prior method make model-agnostic data valuation feasible at web scale, directly addressing EU AI Act compliance requirements that mandate data assessment independent of any specific model. The density-separation theorem provides a clean theoretical guarantee that a single threshold can partition clean from corrupted data, a property Wasserstein-based scores provably lack. Limitations include the fixed Gaussian kernel and balancing coefficient (λ=0.03) across all tasks, the assumption of a clean validation set, and the absence of regression extensions. Open questions include whether learned or adaptive kernels can further improve detection, and how the method behaves under substantial validation-set noise or in fully unsupervised settings.
