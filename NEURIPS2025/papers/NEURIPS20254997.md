## Problem

Converting coarse-resolution gridded weather forecasts to station-level predictions introduces substantial spatial biases, particularly in complex terrains and under extreme conditions. Two core challenges remain unaddressed by existing interpolation methods: station-level heterogeneity (site-specific temporal and spatial dynamics that uniform architectures cannot capture) and oversmoothing in graph-based models, which blurs fine-scale variability and suppresses local extremes critical for extreme event detection.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a triplet: (i) multichannel gridded forecast fields X^(t) ∈ R^{C×H×W} over T time steps (e.g., 721×1440 at 0.25° global resolution), (ii) irregularly distributed station coordinates P = {p_j ∈ R²} for M stations (2288 CMA surface stations in the main experiments), and (iii) historical station observations H over T₁+1 past time steps. It produces a single scalar prediction ŷ_j at each station for the target future time step t=+T₂ (24-hour accumulated precipitation), operating natively off-grid without requiring grid-aligned station placement.

## Method

DiffLiG is a three-module GNN framework. First, GeoLiquidNet encodes station-specific temporal dynamics via a parameterized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process whose time constant τ(p) is learned from station coordinates, functioning as a spatially-conditioned low-pass filter that adaptively controls memory horizon per site. Second, a Spatial Message Passing Network (Dynamic Edge Modulator) constructs adaptive graph edges by jointly learning edge existence (neural discriminator with binary gating), distance-aware Gaussian decay on Haversine distance, and multi-head directional attention, enabling asymmetric, context-sensitive aggregation across two propagation stages (grid-to-station, then station-to-station). Third, a Probabilistic Forecast Diffuser generates N diverse correction candidates by injecting Gaussian noise into node features and denoising through a graph U-Net with residual blending, and a Multi-Criteria Score Selector evaluates each candidate on semantic consensus (self-attention agreement), spatial coherence (lightweight GCN smoothness), and physical plausibility (extreme-value penalty MLP), then fuses or hard-selects the best output before a final station bias correction MLP.

## Results

On the primary 24-hour precipitation interpolation benchmark, DiffLiG achieves the lowest RMSE across all four forecast sources: 5.09 (IFS-HRES), 4.80 (ERA5), 4.72 (IFS-ENS), and 5.10 (FourCastNet), while also attaining the highest ACC (0.74–0.77) and TS@2mm (0.46–0.53). For oversmoothing mitigation on IFS-HRES, DiffLiG reaches TS@20mm = 0.3122 and TS@100mm = 0.0202, outperforming the next-best GAT (0.2986 / 0.0162) by ~5% and ~24% respectively. In a cross-dataset transfer test (trained on IFS-HRES, evaluated on GraphCast without fine-tuning), DiffLiG still achieves the best RMSE (5.52) and TS@2mm (0.5218), confirming robustness to input distribution shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffLiG outperforms all traditional interpolation methods (Nearest, Linear, Kriging, IDW) by a wide margin on every metric and source; for example, on IFS-HRES it reduces RMSE from 5.74–6.10 to 5.09 and lifts TS@2mm from 0.39–0.41 to 0.46. Among GNN baselines, DiffLiG beats GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, and KCN on RMSE and ACC across all four sources; the closest competitor is KCN (RMSE 5.39 on IFS-HRES vs. 5.09 for DiffLiG). The physics-inspired MeshGraphNet is the strongest single baseline on TS@2mm for FourCastNet (0.499 vs. DiffLiG's 0.494), but DiffLiG still leads on RMSE (5.10 vs. 5.26) and ACC (0.730 vs. 0.693) for that source. MGNN, which fuses ERA5 and MADIS in a multimodal graph, is competitive on ERA5 RMSE (4.99 vs. 4.80) but lags substantially on IFS-HRES TS@2mm (0.364 vs. 0.462) and IFS-ENS TS@2mm (0.436 vs. 0.529). The standalone Diffusion baseline and ViT are consistently the weakest learning-based methods, confirming that raw generative or attention-only architectures without spatial graph structure are insufficient for station-level correction. In the cross-dataset transfer experiment (Table 5), DiffLiG trained solely on IFS-HRES still achieves the best RMSE (5.52) and TS@2mm (0.522) on GraphCast, surpassing GAT (5.58 / 0.525) and GCN (5.55 / 0.499) without any fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffLiG draws on two distinct lineages: Liquid Time-Constant Networks (LTC) for its site-aware OU-based temporal encoding, and denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM) for its ensemble generation mechanism. It departs from prior grid-to-station interpolation work (IDW, kriging, MGNN, MeshGraphNet) by operating natively off-grid with a learned, spatially adaptive graph rather than fixed connectivity, and by explicitly targeting two failure modes—station heterogeneity and oversmoothing—that prior GNN-based correctors address only implicitly. The Dynamic Edge Modulator and Multi-Criteria Score Selector are novel architectural contributions that combine directional attention, learnable edge gating, and physics-informed ensemble selection in a way not seen in prior spatial interpolation or weather post-processing literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: operational users need station-level forecasts, yet both NWP and AI weather models output coarse grids that introduce systematic spatial biases, especially in complex terrain. DiffLiG's lightweight design (0.19 MB, ~7.5 h training on a single GPU) makes it deployable as a post-processing module without retraining upstream models, which is significant for operational meteorology. Key limitations include reliance on historical station observations (limiting applicability to new or sparse stations), testing only on precipitation and temperature, and the absence of explicit physical consistency constraints. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to vector fields (wind), multi-variable coupling, and truly data-sparse regions.
