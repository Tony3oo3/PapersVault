## Problem

Siegel spaces, a versatile family of Riemannian symmetric spaces generalizing both hyperbolic spaces and SPD manifolds, remain largely unexplored for discriminative neural network construction. While multiclass logistic regression (MLR) and fully-connected (FC) layers have been developed for hyperbolic and SPD settings, no effective framework existed for building such layers on Siegel spaces. Prior RSS-based approaches relied heavily on maximal abelian subspaces, potentially limiting the networks' ability to learn rich representations and complex decision boundaries.

## Input / Output

For radar clutter classification, the input is a point in the product space Sym_m^+ × SH_m^{q-1}, where the SPD component is the real part of the zeroth-order AR coefficient and the Siegel components are higher-order AR coefficients mapped via the inverse Cayley transform from the Siegel disk. For node classification, the input consists of 6-dimensional node embeddings on the Siegel upper space, learned by optimizing a distance-based loss that matches pairwise Riemannian distances to graph distances. In both settings, the output is a class label over M classes.

## Method

The paper constructs MLR layers via two complementary formulations. The first exploits the quotient structure SH_m ≅ Sp_{2m}/SpO_{2m}, defining a custom inner product ⟨log(gg^T), log(hh^T)⟩ on the group that agrees with the Riemannian distance, yielding a closed-form point-to-hyperplane distance (Theorem 3.5) extendable to product spaces (Theorem 3.8). The second uses the vector-valued distance on RSS to parameterize hyperplanes by a point and a boundary direction, producing a more compact MLR layer via an upper bound on the point-to-hyperplane distance (Proposition 3.11). Two FC layer variants are introduced: an AFC layer that applies the symplectic group action φ(a+ib)[x] as a translation, and a DFC layer that performs dimensionality reduction via Stiefel-manifold parameters. The AFC layer generalizes affine-invariant translation and the DFC layer generalizes the Bimap layer from prior SPD work, while both guarantee outputs remain on the Siegel space.

## Results

On radar clutter classification (4 simulated AR-Gaussian datasets), SiegelNet-AFC-QMLR achieves mean accuracies of 80.94%, 96.50%, 91.00%, and 85.60%, outperforming the second-best method (kNN) by 4.71%, 3.50%, 14.25%, and 12.39% respectively. On node classification (Glass, Iris, Zoo), SiegelNet-AFC-QMLR_SH_m reaches 45.79%, 38.20%, and 53.37% mean accuracy, surpassing all baselines including the prior Siegel-space method SiegelNet-BFC-BMLR (41.12%, 37.26%, 48.12%). The DFC variant underperforms the AFC variant on radar clutter (e.g., 40.78% vs. 80.94% on Dataset 1), highlighting the importance of the group-action FC layer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven baselines across two tasks. On radar clutter classification, SiegelNet-AFC-QMLR decisively outperforms all SPD-based methods (SPDNet, SPDNetBN, MLR-AI, GyroSpd++) by margins of 15–55 percentage points, demonstrating that the full complex Siegel representation captures structure invisible to real SPD inputs. It also beats the kNN baseline (the only other method operating on the full product space H_m^+ × SD_m^{q-1}) by 3.5–14.25%. On node classification, SiegelNet-AFC-QMLR_SH_m wins on all three datasets (Glass, Iris, Zoo) against kNN (by 16–20 pts), LogEig [27] (by 4–10 pts), and the prior Siegel-space network SiegelNet-BFC-BMLR [35] (by 4.6–5.3 pts). The vector-valued distance variant (VMLR) is more compact (~2× smaller model) but slightly less accurate than the quotient-structure variant (QMLR), and the DFC layer consistently underperforms the AFC layer, suggesting the symplectic group action is a stronger inductive bias than dimensionality reduction via Stiefel matrices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gyrovector-space and quotient-structure frameworks developed for hyperbolic and SPD neural networks (Ganea et al. 2018; Nguyen et al. 2023, 2024) to Siegel spaces, a family of Riemannian symmetric spaces that had previously been used only for embedding and visualization (López et al. 2021; Taha et al. 2023) rather than discriminative classification. The key departure from the closest prior work (Nguyen et al. 2025, ICLR) is the introduction of a novel inner product on the quotient space and a second MLR formulation based on vector-valued distance (Kapovich et al. 2017), both of which avoid the reliance on maximal abelian subspaces that the authors argue constrains representational capacity. The product-space extension (SPD × Siegel) for radar clutter is a new setting not covered by prior RSS neural network work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first paper to build end-to-end discriminative neural networks on Siegel spaces, opening a largely unexplored family of Riemannian symmetric spaces to representation learning. The practical impact is demonstrated by substantial gains over SPD-based baselines on radar clutter classification (up to ~14% over kNN), suggesting that the richer geometry of Siegel spaces captures structure that SPD manifolds miss. However, significant open questions remain: the DFC layer underperforms the AFC layer dramatically (40.8% vs 81.0% on one dataset), computational cost is high due to eigenvalue decompositions, and critical building blocks (convolutions, batch normalization, attention) are absent. The non-positive curvature constraint also limits applicability to graphs with mixed or positive curvature.
