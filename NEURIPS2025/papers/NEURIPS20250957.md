## Problem

Existing VLMs for spatial referring face two critical gaps: (1) integrating depth via shared RGB encoders causes modality interference that degrades pretrained image understanding, while costly 3D reconstruction introduces modality gaps; and (2) multi-step spatial reasoning to resolve complex, compositional spatial references (up to 5 steps) remains largely unexplored due to the absence of datasets with explicit reasoning annotations and training strategies that go beyond SFT memorization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250957/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB or RGB-D sensor observations (depth optionally inferred via DepthAnything V2) together with a spatially constrained text instruction encoding multiple spatial relations (e.g., 'the third cup from the left, closest to the camera'). It produces a single 2D point coordinate in image space rather than a bounding box or region, which naturally maps to 3D via depth and handles occlusion better for robotic grasping/placement. The output also includes an explicit multi-step reasoning chain where each step contains a structured perception annotation (type, target object, measured value) before the final point prediction.

## Method

RoboRefer builds on NVILA with a dedicated depth encoder and projector (initialized from RGB counterparts) that is updated independently during joint RGB/RGB-D training, preserving the image encoder's general VQA performance while enhancing 3D spatial perception without modality interference. Training proceeds in two stages: first, SFT with depth alignment followed by spatial understanding enhancement on RefSpatial (2.5M samples, 20M QA pairs, 31 spatial relations from 2D web images, 3D embodied videos, and simulated scenes); second, RFT using GRPO with metric-sensitive process reward functions that score intermediate perception steps (e.g., L1 distance for position predictions) in an order-invariant manner, rather than rewarding only the final answer. The process reward design, combined with the SFT-provided spatial understanding as a strong prior, enables the model to refine step-wise perception accuracy and generalize to unseen spatial relation combinations, outperforming SFT-only training by 9.1% on unseen compositions.

## Results

On the new RefSpatial-Bench (multi-step spatial referring, up to 5 reasoning steps), RoboRefer-2B-RFT achieves 52.0% (Location), 54.0% (Placement), and 41.6% (Unseen relations), surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro by 17.4% in average accuracy. On single-step spatial understanding benchmarks (CV-Bench, BLINK, RoboSpatial, SAT, EmbSpatial), RoboRefer-8B-SFT attains an average success rate of 89.6%, exceeding Gemini-2.5-Pro by 5% absolute. On the RoboRefIt location task, RoboRefer-2B-RFT scores 74.2% versus 49.8% for RoboPoint and 61.9% for Gemini-2.5-Pro on Where2Place placement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboRefer outperforms all 14 baselines across the evaluated benchmarks. Against proprietary models, RoboRefer-2B-RFT surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro by 17.4% on RefSpatial-Bench (the new multi-step benchmark) and by 5% on average single-step spatial understanding, while GPT-4o and Claude-3.7-Sonnet trail by even larger margins (e.g., 96.9% vs. 84.6% on CV-Bench 2D-Relation). Among open-source VLMs, RoboRefer-2B-SFT exceeds its NVILA-2B base by 21.7% absolute and outperforms Qwen-2.5-VL-72B on all spatial benchmarks. Spatial specialist models (SpatialRGPT-8B, SpaceLLaVA-13B, RoboPoint-13B, SpatialBot-3B) are consistently outperformed, with RoboPoint scoring only 49.8% on RoboRefIt versus 74.2% for RoboRefer-2B-RFT. On the unseen-relations split of RefSpatial-Bench, RoboRefer-2B-RFT (41.6%) leads Molmo-72B (21.2%) by 20.4%, demonstrating that RFT generalizes better than SFT to novel spatial compositions. In real-world robot evaluation, only RoboRefer achieves non-zero success rates (80% manipulation, 60% navigation) on long-horizon tasks with dynamic spatial constraints, while Octo, OpenVLA, and SoFar all fail (0%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on NVILA as its base VLM architecture and adopts GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the RL training algorithm, but departs sharply from prior spatial VLMs (SpatialRGPT, SpatialBot, SpaceLLaVA) that treat depth as an RGB-like modality through a shared image encoder. The key architectural departure is a dedicated depth encoder and projector that avoids modality interference without requiring extensive RGB co-training. Conceptually, the paper extends the spatial referring task from single-step perception (the focus of prior work like RoboPoint and SpatialRGPT) to multi-step compositional reasoning, and is the first to apply reinforcement fine-tuning with metric-sensitive process rewards specifically to spatial point prediction rather than VQA or segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: multi-step spatial referring with explicit reasoning has no prior benchmark or large-scale training data, and the 20M-QA RefSpatial dataset plus RefSpatial-Bench fill that void. The practical robotics demonstrations (UR5 manipulation, G1 humanoid navigation) show the point-prediction formulation unifies manipulation and navigation under one interface, which is architecturally clean for embodied systems. Key limitations remain: the model predicts 2D image-plane points requiring depth-based 3D conversion, relies on qualitative spatial relations (left/right) rather than quantitative geometry, and the headline 17.4% improvement over Gemini-2.5-Pro is measured on the authors' own benchmark. Open questions include whether direct 3D point prediction and intent-aware instruction parsing can be integrated without sacrificing the current precision.
