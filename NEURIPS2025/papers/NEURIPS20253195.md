## Problem

Existing multi-task neural VRP solvers rely on heavy encoder–light decoder architectures that can only be trained via RL on small-scale instances and exhibit poor generalization to large-scale problems. Heavy decoder architectures offer strong scale generalization but are impractical to train with RL due to prohibitive memory/compute costs, and supervised learning is infeasible because labeled solutions for multiple VRP variants are unavailable. This paper addresses the training bottleneck by introducing a knowledge-distillation-based multi-task training method that enables label-free training of a heavy decoder model across diverse VRP variants.

## Input / Output

The model takes a VRP instance defined by node features (2D coordinates, demands, service times, time-window bounds), a pairwise distance matrix, and task-specific constraint parameters (vehicle capacity, duration limit, open-route flag, backhaul proportions). At each autoregressive decoding step, the decoder also consumes dynamic state features—remaining vehicle load, current time, remaining route duration, and an open-route indicator—alongside the last-visited and depot node embeddings. The output is a complete multi-vehicle routing solution (ordered node-visit sequence per vehicle) with the total tour distance as the objective; during inference, the R3C strategy iteratively re-optimizes randomly sampled segments over 200 iterations to refine the initial greedy solution.

## Method

The MTL-KD framework first trains six single-task POMO teacher models (heavy encoder–light decoder) independently via RL on 100-node instances, then distills their per-step output distributions into a single multi-task student model with a light encoder (1 Transformer layer) and heavy decoder (6 Transformer layers) using KL divergence at every decoding step, enabling label-free multi-task training without the compute burden of RL on the heavy decoder. The heavy decoder re-encodes all unvisited nodes together with dynamic state and depot/last-node context through L Transformer layers at each step, using single-head attention with padding and feasibility masks to select the next node. At inference, the proposed Random Reordering Re-Construction (R3C) strategy decomposes the initial solution into subtours, randomizes their external sequence order, samples a random-length contiguous segment ending at the depot, and re-optimizes it with the model—accepting the result only if it improves the objective—thereby increasing sampling diversity and escaping local optima across all 16 VRP variants.

## Results

On 6 seen VRP variants at n=1000, MTL-KD(R3C200)_{128} achieves gaps of 1.5%–11.4% relative to HGS-PyVRP (e.g., 2.10% on CVRP, 1.56% on VRPL, 9.24% on VRPTW), while competing multi-task neural solvers degrade severely (MVMoE: 45%–136%, MT-POMO: 13%–66%). On 10 unseen variants at n=1000, MTL-KD(R3C200) maintains gaps of 4.5%–12.2%, whereas MVMoE reaches 49%–136% and MT-POMO reaches 30%–77%. On real-world Set-X (CVRPLIB) and Solomon (VRPTW) benchmarks, MTL-KD consistently outperforms all multi-task neural baselines, with the largest improvements on large-scale instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the heuristic baseline HGS-PyVRP, MTL-KD(R3C200)_{128} achieves gaps of 1.5%–11.4% on seen tasks and 4.5%–12.2% on unseen tasks at n=1000, remaining competitive but not surpassing the heuristic on most variants; however, it does beat OR-Tools on VRPB (−5.55%) and OVRPB (−4.48%). Against the strongest multi-task neural baselines, MTL-KD(R3C200) dramatically outperforms MVMoE (M+aug8) at n=1000, reducing gaps from 45%–136% to 1.5%–11.4% on seen tasks and from 49%–136% to 4.5%–12.2% on unseen tasks. MT-POMO (M+aug8) shows similar degradation at scale (13%–66% gap on seen, 29%–77% on unseen), which MTL-KD reduces by roughly 5–10×. RouteFinder variants (RF-MVMoE) achieve 12.8%–23.8% gaps on seen tasks at n=1000, still 2–6× worse than MTL-KD. CaDA performs catastrophically at large scale (182%–569% gaps), making it non-competitive. On real-world Set-X and Solomon benchmarks, MTL-KD outperforms POMO, POMO-MTL, MVMoE/4E, and MVMoE/4E-L across nearly all instances, with the largest margins on harder instances (e.g., X-n219-k73: 0.77% gap vs. 1.95% for POMO-MTL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads in neural combinatorial optimization: (1) multi-task VRP solvers (MT-POMO, MVMoE, RouteFinder, CaDA) that use heavy encoder–light decoder architectures and are trained via RL, and (2) heavy decoder architectures (Luo et al., NeurIPS 2023) that demonstrate strong scale generalization but are impractical to train with RL due to computational cost. The key departure is using knowledge distillation from multiple single-task POMO teachers to train a unified heavy decoder student, bypassing the need for labeled data or expensive RL on the heavy decoder. The R3C inference strategy extends RRC from the heavy decoder paper by adding random reordering of subtour external order to increase sampling diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, label-free training recipe for multi-task heavy decoder models, which were previously infeasible to train at scale. The finding that a student distilled at scale 100 inherits the heavy decoder's scale generalization up to 1,000 nodes—while the POMO teachers themselves do not generalize across scales—suggests that architectural inductive bias (heavy decoder) can be decoupled from training scale via distillation. The main limitation is the high inference cost of the heavy decoder, and the paper acknowledges that more efficient architectures for multi-task settings remain an open problem. The R3C strategy, while simple, offers a reusable inference-time improvement for any autoregressive VRP solver.
