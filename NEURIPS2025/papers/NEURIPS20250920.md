## Problem

RDP coding theorems establish that optimal compressors require both VQ-like space-packing efficiency and infinite shared randomness between encoder and decoder, yet existing neural compressors either lack the packing efficiency of lattice quantization or rely on high-complexity reverse channel coding that cannot operate under limited or zero shared randomness. This paper asks what low-complexity architectural components are necessary and sufficient for RDP optimality, and how performance degrades gracefully when shared randomness is finite or absent.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a source vector (28×28 grayscale MNIST images, 16-dim physics measurements, or 33-dim audio signals) and, for SD/QSD-LTC, a shared dither vector drawn uniformly from the lattice fundamental cell (continuous for SD-LTC, discrete over nested-lattice cosets for QSD-LTC). The decoder additionally receives a private continuous dither (for PD-LTC and QSD-LTC) and outputs a randomized reconstruction. The compressed bitstream is the entropy-coded lattice-quantized latent, with rate given by H(c|u) for shared-dither schemes or H(ŷ) for private-dither schemes. Latent dimensions are 8 (MNIST, Physics) or 16 (Speech), with the E₈ or Λ₁₆ lattice used in place of the integer lattice for LTC variants.

## Method

The paper proposes three lattice transform coding (LTC) architectures that inject randomness via dithered lattice quantization in the latent space: SD-LTC (shared continuous dither, infinite shared randomness), PD-LTC (private decoder-only dither, zero shared randomness), and QSD-LTC (discrete shared dither from nested lattices Λ ⊃ Λ_f, finite shared randomness R_c = log|Λ/Λ_f| bits/dim). The key insight is that dithered LQ simultaneously provides VQ-like packing efficiency (addressing distortion) and randomized reconstructions with the correct marginal distribution (addressing perception), at far lower complexity than reverse channel coding. For SD-LTC, the additive channel equivalence makes dithered LQ behave like an AWGN channel in high dimensions, and the proof of RDP optimality on Gaussian sources leverages the sphere-bound-achieving property of lattice cells. For PD-LTC, a new proof technique based on lattice Gaussian coding (flatness factors, MAP decoding) shows achievability of R(D/2,∞) under perfect perception, which is the fundamental limit without shared randomness. All three schemes are trained end-to-end with an RDP objective combining entropy-coded rate, MSE distortion, and a Wasserstein-based perception loss, using either the straight-through estimator or the noisy proxy for the quantizer.

## Results

The paper establishes theoretical optimality: SD-LTC achieves the full RDP function R(D,P) on the Gaussian source (Thm. 4.3), while PD-LTC achieves R(D/2,∞) under perfect perception P=0 (Thm. 4.5), which is the fundamental limit without shared randomness. Empirically, on MNIST, Physics, and Speech datasets, SD-LTC consistently achieves the best RDP tradeoff, and QSD-LTC with a nesting ratio of 3 (R_c = log 3 ≈ 1.58 bits/dim) nearly matches SD-LTC's performance despite using finite rather than infinite shared randomness. Performance monotonically improves with better lattice packing (integer → E8 → Barnes-Wall) and more shared randomness (PD → QSD → SD), confirming the theoretical predictions on both synthetic and real-world sources.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against reverse channel coding (RCC, Li & El Gamal 2018), SD-LTC outperforms 8-dimensional RCC on the Gaussian source at P=0 while having significantly lower complexity (RCC is exponential in dimension and rate, whereas LTC uses polynomial-time closest-vector search). Against deterministic NTC (Ballé et al. 2020), the key finding is that at low rates, deterministic NTC cannot enforce the perception constraint at all regardless of the perception weight λ₂, whereas SD-LTC and PD-LTC maintain perceptual quality; at higher rates, deterministic NTC's performance converges to that of SD-NTC. The paper further shows that for a fixed lattice, increasing shared randomness from zero (PD-LTC) to finite (QSD-LTC, R_c = log 2 or log 3) to infinite (SD-LTC) strictly improves the RDP tradeoff, and for a fixed amount of shared randomness, better lattice packing (e.g., E8 over the integer lattice) also strictly improves performance. QSD-LTC with R_c = log 3 nearly closes the gap to SD-LTC (R_c = ∞) on all three real-world datasets, demonstrating that a small amount of structured shared randomness captures most of the benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Lattice Transform Coding (LTC) from Lei et al. (2025), which replaced scalar quantization with lattice quantization in the latent space for rate-distortion optimality, by introducing dithered lattice quantization to handle the perception constraint in the RDP framework. It departs from the high-complexity Reverse Channel Coding (RCC) approach of Theis et al. (2022) by showing that dithered LQ with shared or private randomness achieves the same fundamental limits at dramatically lower complexity. The work also connects to classical dithered quantization (Ziv, 1985) and lattice Gaussian coding (Ling and Belfiore, 2014), unifying these tools within a neural compression architecture that handles zero, finite, and infinite shared randomness through a single nested-lattice framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first constructive, low-complexity scheme proven RDP-optimal on the Gaussian source, bridging a gap between non-constructive RDP coding theorems and practical neural compressors. The finding that QSD-LTC with only ~1.58 bits/dimension of shared randomness nearly matches infinite shared randomness has direct practical implications for systems where truly infinite shared randomness is infeasible. Open questions include theoretical analysis of QSD-LTC (which requires new tools for dither vectors over nested lattices), extension to SOTA image compression architectures with hyperprior models, and whether the Gaussian-source optimality results extend to non-Gaussian sources with nonlinear transforms.
