## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) such as DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1 generate extended Chain-of-Thought traces that often exhibit overthinking—verbose repetition, unnecessary self-verification, and continued exploration after reaching correct answers—leading to 50–80% redundant tokens without accuracy gains. Existing efficient-reasoning methods either impose user-specified length budgets or compare against reference models, but none let the model autonomously discover its own optimal reasoning length, which depends jointly on problem difficulty and model capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251302/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text reasoning prompt (math or coding problem) with no special length instructions or format constraints, and produces a concise CoT trace (typically enclosed in  tags) followed by a final answer. During training, n=8 rollouts are generated per prompt to compute the Sample Optimal Length; during inference, a single response is produced. The approach is fully prompt-agnostic, requiring no user-specified token budgets or reference model outputs at test time.

## Method

ShorterBetter introduces the Sample Optimal Length (SOL)—the length of the shortest correct response among n sampled generations—as a self-supervised proxy for the formally defined Optimal Reasoning Length (OL). The reward function r(y_j) = α·𝕀(y_j = y_i*) − β·|ℓ(y_j) − SOL| combines a correctness term with a length-penalty term anchored to SOL, which naturally adapts to problem difficulty and updates as the model improves. When no correct response exists in a group, SOL defaults to the average length, providing a neutral baseline that avoids biasing toward over- or under-thinking. The policy is optimized via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which normalizes rewards within each group of n samples to compute advantage estimates and applies clipped policy updates with a KL penalty against a frozen reference policy. Applied to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B, this single unified RL procedure achieves 50–80% output length reduction while maintaining or improving accuracy across in-domain math and out-of-domain benchmarks.

## Results

ShorterBetter-1.5B achieves 77.6%–79.2% output length reduction on in-domain math tasks (AIME, AMC, Olympiad, Minerva) with +2.5% to +3.2% accuracy gains, and 75.5%–77.0% reduction on out-of-domain benchmarks (MathQA, BBH, MMLU, LiveCodeBench) with only −0.8% to −1.6% accuracy change. The 7B variant (α=2) attains a 62.1% length reduction in-domain with a +7.1% accuracy improvement and a 62.3% reduction out-of-domain with just −0.48% accuracy change. Across all in-domain and out-of-domain tasks, ShorterBetter-7B achieves the highest Accuracy-Efficiency (AE) Score among all length-reduction baselines, e.g., 1.89 on AIME vs. 1.31 for Training Efficient and 0.28 for O1-Pruner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen baselines (Distill-1.5B/7B), ShorterBetter reduces output length by 50–80% while matching or exceeding accuracy (e.g., 53.3% vs. 36.7% on AIME-7B; 75.9% vs. 66.3% on AMC-7B). Compared to the non-reasoning Qwen2.5-Instruct models, ShorterBetter achieves substantially higher accuracy (e.g., 53.3% vs. 16.7% on AIME-7B) while still producing outputs far shorter than the Distill models. Against Training Efficient (Arora & Zanette, 2025), ShorterBetter-7B achieves higher AE Scores on every benchmark (e.g., 1.89 vs. 1.31 on AIME; 0.79 vs. 0.34 on MathQA) and shorter outputs at comparable or better accuracy. Against O1-Pruner (Luo et al., 2025a), ShorterBetter-7B dominates on AE Score across all tasks (e.g., 1.89 vs. 0.28 on AIME; 0.73 vs. 0.31 on MMLU) and maintains much higher accuracy on in-domain math (e.g., 53.3% vs. 6.7% on AIME), though O1-Pruner produces shorter absolute outputs on some out-of-domain tasks. Overall, ShorterBetter consistently wins on the composite AE metric, confirming that its SOL-based reward yields a superior accuracy–efficiency trade-off relative to all four baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its policy optimization backbone and is motivated by the overthinking phenomenon documented in recent work (Chen et al., 2025; Su et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025). It departs from explicit length-budget methods (L1, DAST) and reference-model comparison approaches (O1-Pruner) by introducing Sample Optimal Length (SOL) — the length of the shortest correct response among multiple sampled generations — as a self-supervised, data-driven reward signal. This places it in the same family as Training Efficient (Arora and Zanette, 2025) and Kimi 1.5, which also leverage sampled responses, but distinguishes itself by anchoring the reward to the shortest *correct* response rather than the shortest or average response overall.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50–80% output length reduction while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy is practically significant for deployment cost and latency, and the SOL formulation is simple enough to be readily adopted by practitioners working with reasoning models. However, the method is validated only on 1.5B/7B Qwen-based models and verifiable-answer tasks (math, code), leaving open whether it scales to larger models or generalizes to open-ended reasoning with non-binary correctness. The reasoning trace analysis revealing structural compression (reduced self-verification, fewer alternative explorations) suggests the method changes *how* models reason, not just *how long*, but more rigorous quantitative metrics for reasoning structure remain an open question.
