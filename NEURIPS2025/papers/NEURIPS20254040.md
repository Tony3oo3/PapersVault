## Problem

Physics-Informed ML was designed for domains with well-known governing equations, full observability, and clean structured data, yet biological modeling systematically violates all three assumptions: prior knowledge is uncertain and fragmented across databases, data is sparse and heterogeneous across individuals and perturbations, key species remain unobserved, and networks are high-dimensional. No established benchmarks exist to evaluate PIML under these biological conditions, leaving the field unable to assess whether its methods transfer or how they should be adapted.

## Input / Output

The proposed BIML framework consumes multi-source, semi-structured biological knowledge (curated pathway databases like KEGG and Reactome, literature annotations, expert input) alongside sparse, noisy time-series measurements collected across diverse individuals, cell lines, and perturbations, with LLMs and foundation models generating priors, context embeddings, and candidate mechanisms. Outputs are ODE-based dynamical system models carrying explicit uncertainty distributions over both structure and parameters, interpretable network topologies (gene regulatory networks, reaction networks), and calibrated predictions under unseen conditions or interventions. Unlike PIML, which takes a known governing equation as a fixed input, BIML treats the equation structure itself as an uncertain, partially known quantity to be jointly inferred with the data.

## Method

This position paper proposes Biology-Informed Machine Learning (BIML) as a principled extension of PIML organized around four methodological pillars: uncertainty quantification via Bayesian and probabilistic frameworks that treat model structure and parameters as distributions; contextualization through mixed-effects and hierarchical models that disentangle shared mechanisms from context-specific variation; constrained latent structure inference where latent variables represent biologically motivated hidden components grounded by prior knowledge rather than abstract statistical constructs; and scalability via structured priors, sparse or low-rank representations, and modular chemical-reaction-network-based decomposition. Foundation Models and LLMs serve as an integrative layer across all pillars, formalizing uncertain priors, disambiguating heterogeneous contexts, proposing plausible latent components, and constraining combinatorial search spaces. The paper further argues that the field needs new epistemic stress-test benchmarks reflecting biological realism (uncertain priors, partial observability, heterogeneity) and an application-driven evaluation culture that values domain relevance and failure-mode exposure over idealized performance metrics.

## Results

This is a position paper and does not report quantitative experimental results, benchmarks, or performance metrics. Instead, it presents a conceptual framework (Biology-Informed Machine Learning, or BIML) with four methodological pillars—uncertainty quantification, contextualization, constrained latent structure inference, and scalability—and provides two illustrative use cases (gene regulatory network inference and generalization to unseen interventions) as qualitative sketches rather than evaluated experiments. The paper's primary contribution is a roadmap and set of community recommendations rather than empirical findings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No baselines or head-to-head method comparisons are presented, as this is a position paper proposing a new paradigm (BIML) rather than evaluating a specific algorithm. The paper does note, in passing, that recent benchmarking work (e.g., PertEval-scFM [117], Wong et al. [119]) shows simple baselines can match or exceed complex deep models for post-perturbation RNA-seq prediction, and that on several tasks LLMs still underperform simple baselines [117, 119]. These observations are cited as motivation for the BIML framework's emphasis on structured priors and uncertainty rather than scale, but no direct method-vs-method comparison is conducted in this paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper positions Biology-Informed Machine Learning (BIML) as a principled evolution of Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML), building directly on the PIML paradigm (Karniadakis et al. 2021; Raissi et al. 2019) while arguing that its core assumptions—clean governing equations, full observability, low-noise structured data—systematically fail in biological settings. It draws on a broad methodological lineage including SINDy for sparse dynamics discovery, Neural ODEs and Gaussian Processes for probabilistic dynamical modeling, and Bayesian/mixed-effects frameworks for uncertainty, while proposing Foundation Models and LLMs (ODEFormer, LLM-Lasso, scPRINT, LangPert) as a new integrative layer for encoding uncertain biological priors. The paper departs from prior PIML work by reframing biology's 'deficiencies' (uncertain knowledge, partial observability, heterogeneity, high dimensionality) not as obstacles but as the necessary catalysts for methodological innovation, and by explicitly calling for new biological benchmarks to replace the physics-dominated evaluation landscape (ODEBench, SRBench).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and underappreciated gap: PIML methods have been validated almost exclusively on idealized physics benchmarks, leaving their applicability to real biological dynamical systems untested and unbenchmarked. By proposing four methodological pillars (uncertainty quantification, contextualization, constrained latent structure inference, scalability) and a concrete role for FMs/LLMs as knowledge-integration infrastructure, it provides a structured roadmap that could redirect PIML research toward high-impact biological and translational problems. Key open questions remain: how to systematically integrate LLM-generated priors without amplifying hallucination, what new abstractions enable scalable uncertainty-aware inference in high-dimensional biological networks, and how to build community norms (benchmarks, review practices, co-review) that reward biological realism over algorithmic novelty. As a position paper with no empirical results, its impact hinges on whether the community adopts the BIML framing and invests in the proposed benchmarks and infrastructure.
