## Problem

HJB-based reinforcement learning for continuous-time systems (ODEs/SDEs) encodes dynamics continuity in the equation's structure, but existing methods require explicit knowledge of the drift and diffusion coefficients, restricting them to model-based settings. Standard model-free TD methods lose this continuity information because the dynamics are encoded in the transition kernel (the expectation subscript), which is discarded under sample-based approximation. The paper asks: can one target the HJB equation in a fully model-free way, preserving continuity information even when approximating expectations with samples?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous state vector s_t ∈ R^n (dimensionality ranges from 11 for Hopper to 244 for Humanoid) and an action a_t sampled from a stochastic policy, along with the next state s_{t+Δt} obtained from environment interaction. It produces a scalar value function estimate V(s_t) parameterized by a neural network. Unusually, the TD update does not use V(s_{t+Δt}) as the target; instead it uses the finite-difference state increments Δs/Δt and Δs·Δs/Δt multiplied by the gradient and Hessian of V at s_t, requiring the value network to be twice differentiable and the time step Δt to be sufficiently small.

## Method

The key insight (Proposition 1) is that the drift term μ(s,a) and diffusion term σσᵀ(s,a) in the HJB equation can be equivalently expressed as limits of sample-based finite differences: E[μ] = lim E[(S_{t+Δt}−s_t)/Δt] and E[σσᵀ] = lim E[(ΔsⁱΔsʲ)/Δt]. Substituting these into the fixed-policy HJB equation yields the dTD update (Definition 1), whose expectation argument no longer depends on μ or σ, enabling direct sample-based approximation. The critical design choice is treating the derivative-based terms (∇V·Δs/Δt + ½∇²V:ΔsΔs/Δt) as the prediction and the value-based terms (−ρ + γV(s_{t+Δt})) as the target, which empirically outperforms the naive reverse assignment. For practical stability under function approximation, β-dTD linearly combines the classical TD error with the dTD error using a tunable weight β. Convergence is established via elliptic PDE theory: the fixed-point equation (γI − L^π)V = ρ̄ is shown to admit a unique weak solution (Lax-Milgram), and the induced continuous-time dynamics ∂_t V = TV − V exhibit exponential stability with rate λ = (1/γ)(γ − ‖b‖²_∞/α) under uniform ellipticity and bounded-domain assumptions.

## Results

The paper evaluates TD, β-naive-dTD, and β-dTD on four MuJoCo/Brax environments (Hopper, HalfCheetah, Ant, Humanoid) at three noise levels (coef = 0.00, 0.01, 0.05) using PPO and A2C. β-dTD consistently outperforms β-naive-dTD across all environments and noise levels, and matches or exceeds standard TD in final performance while showing faster learning rates. The tuned β values for β-dTD are substantial (0.57, 0.74, 0.24, 0.33 for PPO; 0.24, 0.60, 0.75, 0.47 for A2C across Hopper, HalfCheetah, Ant, Humanoid), confirming that the dTD term retains meaningful influence on learning. Under high process noise (coef = 0.05), both β-dTD and TD show comparable performance degradation, particularly in Ant and HalfCheetah.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three methods: standard TD, β-naive-dTD, and β-dTD, using both PPO and A2C as the policy optimization backbone. β-dTD outperforms β-naive-dTD in every environment and noise setting; the optimized β for β-naive-dTD is very small (0.08, 0.07, 0.23, 0.02 for PPO), making its effective update nearly identical to plain TD, yet it still underperforms TD, likely due to residual interference from the naive-dTD term and suboptimal tuning of other hyperparameters. β-dTD matches or slightly exceeds TD in final reward across all settings, with the most notable advantage being a faster rate of improvement during training, attributed to the gradient and Hessian information injected by the dTD term. In HalfCheetah, β-dTD achieves the largest β (0.74 for PPO, 0.60 for A2C), indicating the strongest reliance on the differential update in that environment. Under high noise (coef = 0.05), the advantage of β-dTD over TD narrows, and both methods degrade similarly, suggesting the continuity assumption is less informative when stochastic perturbations dominate the dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time TD lineage initiated by Doya (2000) and Munos (1997) for ODE systems to stochastic (SDE) dynamics, while simultaneously removing the model-based requirement that has characterized prior HJB-based RL (Munos & Bourgine 1997; Jia & Zhou 2022a,b, 2023). The key conceptual departure is Proposition 1, which shows that the drift and diffusion coefficients in the HJB equation can be replaced by limits of sample-based finite differences, thereby preserving continuity information under sample approximation without requiring explicit knowledge of μ or σ. This positions the work between the standard Bellman-equation TD (which loses continuity under sampling) and the classical HJB framework (which demands a known model), carving out a genuinely model-free HJB-based formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper bridges two communities—stochastic control (HJB/PDE) and model-free RL—that have largely developed in parallel, offering a principled target for continuous-time policy evaluation that exploits the structure of SDE dynamics. However, the convergence analysis is restricted to an idealized function-space setting with a bounded domain and uniform ellipticity, leaving the practical stability of dTD with neural function approximation unproven; the need for the β-dTD hybrid (mixing in standard TD) is an implicit acknowledgment of this gap. Open questions include extending the method to off-policy settings, tightening the convergence theory to cover function approximation, and reducing the variance of the second-order (Hessian) estimator in high-dimensional state spaces.
