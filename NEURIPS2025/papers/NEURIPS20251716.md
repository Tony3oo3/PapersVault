## Problem

SGD-based solvers are the standard practical tool for semi-discrete optimal transport in high-dimensional and sample-based settings, yet no convergence guarantees existed for their ability to approximate the OT map. Prior theoretical results (Pooladian et al., 2023) established minimax rates only for the compact-support, quadratic-cost case and required solving a batched empirical problem. This paper closes the gap by proving that online, non-regularized SGD can estimate the OT map at the minimax rate O(1/√n) under MTW costs on compact domains and the quadratic cost on unbounded domains.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes i.i.d. samples from the continuous source measure μ one at a time in an online fashion (no storage required), alongside the full discrete target measure ν = Σ w_i δ_{y_i} (M support points with known weights) and a cost function c. It outputs the Brenier potential g*, the OT cost, and the OT map estimate T(g)(x) = x − ∇g^c(x). A practical requirement is knowledge of a compact set K with μ(K) ≥ 1 − (1/2)min_j w_j, which can be estimated from a preliminary sample via a quantile bound with dimension-free sample complexity.

## Method

The core method is Projected Stochastic Gradient Descent (PSGD) applied to the convex semi-dual formulation min_g H(g) = E_{X~μ}[h(g,X)]. The key technical contribution is identifying a compact L∞-ball projection set C that provably contains a minimizer of H even when μ has unbounded support (Lemma 3.1), which was previously unknown. This projection step yields a Restricted Strong Convexity (RSC) property of H on C (Lemma 4.2), enabling convergence analysis without knowing the strong convexity constant. For the non-averaged iterates, PSGD achieves O(1/n^b) in potential error for b ∈ (1/2,1); for the averaged iterates with α-Hölder density, the rate improves to O(1/n) for the potential and O(1/√n) for the OT map, which is shown to be minimax optimal (Theorem 5.3). The analysis extends from the compact MTW-cost setting to the non-compact quadratic-cost setting by establishing new Hessian regularity and local strong convexity results via a partition-of-unity decomposition over annular regions.

## Results

The paper establishes that Projected SGD (PSGD) achieves a minimax-optimal O(1/√n) convergence rate for OT map estimation under both MTW costs (compact support) and the quadratic cost (including non-compact support), where n is the number of samples from the source measure. For the OT cost and Brenier potential, PSGD achieves a faster O(1/n) rate under MTW and quadratic costs, improving upon the O(1/√n) rate available in the general (non-MTW) setting. Theorem 5.3 proves a matching minimax lower bound of Ω(1/√n) for OT map estimation, confirming optimality. Numerical experiments across three settings (non-quadratic cost, non-compact support, non-smooth source) confirm the predicted rates, with the OT map estimator exhibiting O(1/√n) behavior in Examples 1–2 and O(1/n^0.45) in Example 3 (consistent with b=0.9 for a non-Hölder density).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are to prior theoretical guarantees rather than empirical baselines. Compared to Pooladian et al. [31], who established the O(1/√n) minimax rate for OT map estimation in the compact quadratic setting, this paper extends the result to (i) all MTW costs on compact support and (ii) the quadratic cost on non-compact support, while also achieving the rate in an online (non-batched) setting without requiring knowledge of the total number of samples in advance or a regularization parameter. Compared to Genevay et al. [17], who introduced SGD for the semi-dual but relied on entropic regularization (yielding prohibitive constants in ε⁻¹), this paper provides guarantees for the non-regularized problem. Compared to Newton-type methods of Kitagawa et al. [21] and combinatorial methods [1,2], which require full knowledge of the source density and suffer from the curse of dimensionality, PSGD operates purely from samples with O(M) per-iteration cost and no mesh construction. The key novel contribution over all prior work is the identification of a compact projection set (Lemma 3.1) valid even when μ has unbounded support, which enables the restricted strong convexity argument underpinning the O(1/n) potential rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimax estimation framework for semi-discrete OT maps established by Pooladian et al. [31], who proved O(1/√n) rates only for compactly supported sources with quadratic cost using batched empirical OT. It also builds on the local strong convexity and Hessian regularity results of Kitagawa, Mérigot, and Thibert [21] (compact case) and the quantitative Laguerre cell stability of Bansil and Kitagawa [6], extending both to the non-compact quadratic setting. The key methodological departure is the introduction of a projection set (a ‖·‖∞-ball) that localizes the Brenier potential even when the source measure has unbounded support, enabling convergence analysis of Projected SGD for the non-regularized semi-dual — a setting where prior work [17, 33] relied on entropic regularization with prohibitive constants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This closes a fundamental theoretical gap: SGD-based solvers are the standard practical tool for semi-discrete OT in generative modeling and high-dimensional applications, yet lacked any convergence guarantee for OT map estimation. The minimax optimality result (Theorem 5.3) confirms that the O(1/√n) rate is the best achievable, and the extension to MTW costs on compact domains and quadratic cost on unbounded domains broadens the scope well beyond prior work. Open questions include whether the RSC structure can be exploited by adaptive methods (S-Adam, S-Adagrad) for faster practical convergence, whether the non-compact analysis extends to broader MTW costs, and whether the conservative M²/λ dependence in the map rate can be tightened.
