## Problem

In classical PAC learning, generalization rates must hold uniformly over both target concepts and marginal distributions. Benedek and Itai's concept-nonuniform model relaxes this by allowing rates to depend on the target concept, but the complementary marginal-nonuniform variant—where rates may depend on the marginal distribution but must hold uniformly over all concepts—lacked a complete characterization of achievable learning rates. This paper fills that gap by providing a full trichotomy of rates in the marginal-nonuniform regime, and additionally improves the known rate for concept-nonuniform learning from log(n)/n to 1/n.

## Input / Output

The learner receives an i.i.d. labeled sample S_n = ((x_i, y_i))_{i=1}^n drawn from a fixed marginal distribution P over the domain X, where labels are generated by an unknown target f* ∈ H. The output is a hypothesis ĥ_n ∈ H whose expected error under P is bounded uniformly over all f* ∈ H, with the bound's constants C, c permitted to depend on P but not on f*. The key structural constraint is that a single algorithm must work for every labeling under a given P, distinguishing this from concept-nonuniform learning where the algorithm may be tailored per concept.

## Method

The main result is a trichotomy governed by the VC-eluder dimension VCE(H): finite classes (VCE=0) achieve exact exponential rate e^{-n}; classes with 1 ≤ VCE(H) < ∞ achieve exact fine-grained linear rate VCE(H)/n; and classes with VCE(H) = ∞ require arbitrarily slow rates. The upper bound for the linear regime uses a two-sample strategy: the first half of the sample is shown (via König's lemma applied to a Littlestone tree) to reduce the version space's VC dimension to at most VCE(H) on the second half with probability 1 − e^{-κ(P)·n}, after which the one-inclusion graph algorithm yields the d/n rate. The matching lower bound constructs a marginal distribution over an infinite d-VC-eluder sequence so that any learner must err on a point it has not seen. For concept-nonuniform learning, the paper replaces the standard ERM-on-each-subclass approach with one-inclusion graph predictors aggregated via a weighted majority algorithm (online-to-batch conversion), eliminating the log(n) overhead and achieving the optimal 1/n rate.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3) establishes a complete trichotomy for marginal-nonuniform PAC learning rates: (1) exponential rate e^{-n} arises iff the hypothesis class is finite (VCE(H)=0); (2) linear rate 1/n arises iff the VC-eluder dimension is finite and positive (1 ≤ VCE(H) < ∞, equivalently finite VC dimension); (3) arbitrarily slow rates are required iff VCE(H) = ∞ (infinite VC dimension). Theorem 8 sharpens the linear case to the fine-grained rate VCE(H)/n, where VCE(H) ≤ VC(H) and the gap can be arbitrarily large. For concept-nonuniform learning, Theorem 16 improves the previously known log n/n rate [Lugosi and Zeger, 1996] to the optimal linear rate 1/n for all countable unions of VC classes.

| Condition on H | VCE(H) | VC(H) | Achievable Rate | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Finite class | 0 | < ∞ | e^{-n} (exact) | Any finite H with \|H\| ≥ 3 |

| Infinite VC dim. | ∞ | ∞ | Arbitrarily slow | All open sets on [0,1] |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The key comparison is against the prior state of the art in nonuniform learning theory. For concept-nonuniform learning, the paper improves the best previously known rate from log n/n [Lugosi and Zeger, 1996] to the optimal 1/n, using a one-inclusion graph predictor combined with an online-to-batch weighted majority conversion. For marginal-nonuniform learning, the paper provides the first complete characterization of achievable rates, going beyond the partial examples given by Ben-David, Benedek, and Mansour [1995]. A notable structural finding is that the 1/n-marginal-nonuniform family coincides exactly with the 1/n-uniform (PAC-learnable) family, but the fine-grained constant is governed by VCE(H) rather than VC(H), and since VCE(H) ≤ VC(H) with arbitrarily large gaps (e.g., VCE=1 but VC unbounded), this yields meaningfully sharper rates for specific classes. The paper also identifies strict inclusions and separations across all four learning settings (uniform, universal, concept-nonuniform, marginal-nonuniform), with one open case remaining (a class that is universally linear-learnable and marginal-nonuniformly learnable at arbitrarily slow rates but not concept-nonuniformly learnable).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper completes the rate characterization for marginal-nonuniform PAC learning, a setting defined by Ben-David, Benedek, and Mansour (1995) as one of four quantifier-order variants of PAC learnability, for which only partial examples were previously known. The central technical tool is the VC-eluder dimension introduced by Hanneke and Xu (2024), which had previously characterized universal ERM rates but is here shown to also govern the exact fine-grained rate d/n in the marginal-nonuniform regime. The proofs draw on the one-inclusion graph algorithm (Haussler et al., 1994), König's lemma for the key concentration argument, and the fine-grained rate framework of Bousquet et al. (2023), while the concept-nonuniform improvement leverages an online-to-batch conversion via the weighted majority algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean trichotomy (exponential / linear / arbitrarily slow, governed by VCE = 0, finite, or infinite) gives a complete and parameter-sharp picture of what is achievable when the learning rate may depend on the data distribution. A notable conceptual finding is that the family of classes learnable at linear rate in the marginal-nonuniform setting coincides exactly with the uniformly PAC-learnable classes, yet the governing constant is VCE(H) rather than VC(H), which can be arbitrarily smaller. Open questions include whether the universal Glivenko-Cantelli property can distinguish marginal-nonuniformly learnable classes requiring arbitrarily slow rates from non-learnable ones, and whether the weak-realizability variant changes the landscape for concept-nonuniform learning.
