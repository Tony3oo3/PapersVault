## Problem

LLMs exhibit logical inconsistency across multi-turn inference, producing outputs that are plausible but factually incorrect. Existing self-verification approaches fail systematically: single-agent reflection suffers from confirmation bias (inconsistent self-verification), while multi-agent debate leads to collusive reinforcement where agents converge on shared errors. The paper asks how to enable LLMs to self-check through strategic interactions that overcome both single-agent inconsistency and multi-agent collusion without human feedback or additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query x and a finite candidate answer set Y (directly from given options for multiple-choice tasks, or constructed by sampling from the LLM's distribution P_LLM(y|x, correct) using nucleus and top-k sampling for open-ended tasks). It produces a ranked candidate list representing the optimal preference ordering at separating equilibrium, where the generator's and verifier's orderings coincide. The output is a consensus ranking that encodes both correctness and consistency, rather than a single selected answer.

## Method

The Bayesian Decoding Game (BDG) models LLM decoding as a multistage signaling game between a Generator (operating under partial information about correctness) and a Verifier (assessing outputs without observing original signals). The key innovation is the σ_i-Separated Equilibrium (σ_i-SE), which enforces a minimum L1 distance between the generator's action distributions under correct vs. incorrect signals, preventing the collusive Nash equilibria that plague prior consensus games like ECG. Convergence is achieved through a Markovian (memoryless) strategy update schedule with no-regret guarantees, where each player updates based only on the opponent's previous-round action rather than full history, yielding O(n log n) complexity versus ECG's O(n²) and 30× faster convergence. The algorithm iterates until both σ-separation conditions are satisfied and the generator's and verifier's preference orderings align, at which point the consensus ranking is returned. The framework is training-free and model-agnostic, requiring only access to the LLM's probability estimates for generation and verification prompts.

## Results

On the average across MMLU, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, and RACE-High, BDG achieves 60.1% accuracy (20.2% improvement over the single-agent Generative Ranking baseline of 50.6%), outperforming ECG (59.0%, 18.2% improvement). The headline result is LLaMA-13B with BDG reaching 78.1% on ARC-Easy, surpassing the much larger PaLM-540B (76.6%). On MMLU with DeepSeek-7B, BDG lifts accuracy from 44.07% (G) to 49.52%, a 12.4% absolute gain. BDG also converges ~30× faster than ECG's Average Recall update to its separating equilibrium.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDG consistently outperforms ECG [31] (Equilibrium Consensus Game) on 7 of 8 domain-model combinations, with the sole exception being RACE-High with LLaMA-13B where ECG scores 62.8 vs. BDG's 60.3. On MMLU, BDG gains 0.6 points over ECG (7B) and 1.8 points (13B); on ARC-Easy the gap is larger (3.8 and 1.7 points respectively). Against the Mutual Information Ranking baseline [39], BDG achieves substantially higher accuracy across all domains (e.g., 40.5 vs. 33.1 on MMLU-7B; 78.1 vs. 71.5 on ARC-Easy-13B). Self-Contrastive Decoding [42] is also outperformed by BDG on most settings (e.g., 46.9 vs. 41.7 on MMLU-13B; 59.6 vs. 56.5 on ARC-Challenge-7B), though SCD occasionally matches or slightly exceeds BDG on RACE-High-13B (58.9 vs. 60.3). The paper also reports that BDG's LLaMA-13B (78.1, ARC-Easy) surpasses PaLM-540B (76.6) and its MMLU score (46.9) exceeds zero-shot GPT-3-175B (37.7), demonstrating that the game-theoretic mechanism enables smaller models to outperform much larger ones without additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Equilibrium Consensus Game (ECG) framework of Jacob et al. [31], which first formalized LLM decoding as a signaling game, by replacing the Average Recall update with a Markovian strategy update and enforcing a σ-Separated Equilibrium instead of a Nash Equilibrium to prevent collusive convergence. It departs from Prover-Verifier Games [27] by being training-free and cooperative rather than RL-based and zero-sum, and from multi-agent debate systems [20, 12] by structuring verification as a formal two-player signaling game with explicit separation constraints rather than unstructured iterative discussion. The theoretical contribution—showing that Nash equilibria in the decoding signaling game admit collusive solutions (Thm. 1) and that separating equilibria with σ-separation eliminate this pathology—provides a principled game-theoretic justification absent from prior consensus-based decoding methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-motivated failure mode (collusive reinforcement in multi-agent debate and confirmation bias in single-agent reflection) with a clean theoretical fix, and the training-free, model-agnostic design makes it broadly applicable. However, the evaluation is concentrated on multiple-choice benchmarks (MMLU, ARC, RACE) with LLaMA-7B/13B, and the headline comparisons against PaLM-540B and GPT-3-175B rely on zero-shot settings that may not reflect the full capability gap. Open questions include whether the σ-separation constraint generalizes to open-ended generation where candidate sets must be constructed, how the method scales with candidate set size, and whether the game-theoretic framing provides advantages over simpler ensemble or self-consistency baselines in more diverse task settings.
