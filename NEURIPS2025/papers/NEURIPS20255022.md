## Problem

Learning rate schedules for budgeted-iteration training—achieving optimal performance under a fixed number of iterations—remain largely heuristic, lacking rigorous theoretical grounding and requiring extensive per-network trial-and-error to select. No unified schedule exists that is both theoretically justified and robustly superior across diverse architectures, tasks, and training budgets without case-by-case tuning.

## Input / Output

At each training step, the schedule consumes the current iteration index t, the total budget T (or phase boundaries T_k, T_{k+1}), a single hyperparameter φ, and the learning rate bounds (η_min, η_max), then outputs a scalar learning rate η_t via a closed-form cosine-modulated rational expression. No per-iteration numerical optimization is required; the formula is evaluated in O(1) per step and is plug-and-play with any PyTorch optimizer (SGD, AdamW, etc.). The multi-phase variant (K > 2) additionally requires phase boundaries, but the paper's main experiments use the single-phase case (K = 2).

## Method

The paper formulates learning rate design as a min-max optimization: minimize the worst-case quadratic loss (bounded by Hessian eigenvalues λ_l, λ_u around local optima) over the iteration budget, subject to SGD dynamics. By invoking the Flanders–Shortley theorem on Chebyshev polynomials, they show the exact minimax-optimal solution has a closed-form structure, then fit the numerical solutions across nine (λ_l, λ_u) configurations with a single-parameter family η_t = (η_max − η_min) · 2[1 + cos(·)] / [2φ + (2−φ)(1 + cos(·))] + η_min. The hyperparameter φ is theoretically linked to the condition number (φ = 2κ in the normalized special case), giving it an interpretation as an optimization-difficulty knob: larger φ accelerates decay for ill-conditioned landscapes, while φ ≈ 2 recovers the standard cosine schedule. Convergence bounds are proven for both φ > 2 and φ < 2 under stochastic Hessian noise, and the schedule is shown to approximate step decay, exponential, REX, cyclic, and OneCycle schedules through simple parameter adjustments.

## Results

On vision classification, UBA achieves 95.65% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ResNet18), 78.95% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet34), and 79.39% on ImageNet (ResNet50) at full training budget, outperforming all six baselines at 25%, 50%, and 100% iteration budgets. On language modeling with OLMo (36M–300M parameters), UBA achieves state-of-the-art results on approximately 50% of 10 benchmarks (PIQA, HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, SciQ, ARC-E/C, COPA, SocialIQA, SOC) while delivering the best average score across all model scales. Notable gains include +1.7 on SciQ-73M and +2.63 on ARC-E-300M over the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UBA consistently outperforms all six baselines on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet across every budget level, with the largest margins over Step decay (e.g., +7.71 on CIFAR-100 at 25% budget) and Cosine (e.g., +4.66 on CIFAR-100 at 25%). On CIFAR-10 at full budget, Budgeted Training (BT) edges UBA by a narrow 0.03 points (95.68 vs. 95.65), but UBA leads at 25% and 50% budgets. REX is the strongest single baseline on CIFAR-10 (95.45 at 100%) yet trails UBA by 0.20 points; on ImageNet, Cosine is the closest competitor (79.07 vs. UBA's 79.39). For language tasks (OLMo 36M–300M), UBA wins on roughly half the 10 benchmarks while the best baseline varies per benchmark (REX dominates at 300M, Cosine at 150M, CLR at 36M), but UBA achieves the highest average score at every scale. The key differentiator is consistency: UBA is the only schedule that never drops below the second-best result by more than ~1 point across all 27 vision settings and all language scales, making it a reliable default without per-task tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper derives a learning rate schedule from a min-max optimization framework that approximates the loss landscape via quadratic expansion around local optima, then connects the resulting closed-form solution to Chebyshev polynomial minimax theory (Flanders & Shortley, 1951). It departs from the heuristic lineage of cosine annealing, step decay, REX, and cyclical schedules by grounding the schedule in worst-case curvature robustness rather than empirical tuning. The single hyperparameter φ is shown to relate to the Hessian condition number, linking the schedule to local optimization difficulty in a way prior schedules (Defazio et al., Pan et al.) only partially addressed through linear decay or eigencurve analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UBA offers practitioners a theoretically justified, single-parameter default schedule that consistently (if modestly) outperforms common alternatives across vision and language tasks, multiple architectures, and varying iteration budgets—reducing the trial-and-error cost of schedule selection. The key open question is the explicit, quantifiable mapping between φ and a general optimization-difficulty metric; the paper acknowledges no established metric exists, and the multi-phase (K>2) formulation remains underexplored. The language-model results are less decisive (UBA wins on roughly half of benchmarks), suggesting the schedule's advantage may be more pronounced in vision or smaller-scale settings.
