## Problem

The Proximal Stochastic Gradient Langevin Algorithm (PSGLA) is widely used for sampling composite potentials V = f + g where g is non-smooth, but no convergence guarantees existed for non-convex (non-log-concave) potentials. Additionally, prior stability results for the inexact Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm (iULA) contained unavoidable discretization error terms that limited their applicability. This paper fills both gaps by proving drift stability without discretization error and deriving the first convergence proof of PSGLA for non-log-concave sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded linear observation y = Ax + n (e.g., an image with 50% masked pixels and Gaussian noise σ = 1/255), a degradation matrix A, and a pretrained denoiser D_√γ trained at a fixed noise level √γ (which fixes the step-size γ). It outputs posterior samples from which a restored image (mean of iterates) and an uncertainty map (standard deviation of iterates) are computed. The denoiser must be firmly non-expansive or a proximal operator of a weakly convex function for the theory to apply; DnCNN trained at a single noise level satisfies this, while multi-noise-level denoisers like GSDRUNet do not directly.

## Method

The key insight is reformulating PSGLA as a two-point algorithm where a shadow sequence Y_k follows an iULA with drift b^γ(y) = ∇f(y − γ∇g^γ(y)) + ∇g^γ(y), where g^γ is the Moreau envelope of g. The paper proves a drift stability theorem for iULA in W_p and TV metrics without discretization error terms (Theorem 1), then shows that under weak convexity of g and strong convexity at infinity of g^γ, the drift b^γ satisfies the weak dissipativity condition needed for geometric ergodicity. Combining this stability result with the discretization error bound (Theorem 2) and the convergence of the Moreau envelope to g as γ → 0 (Proposition 1) yields the first convergence proof of PSGLA for non-convex potentials (Theorem 3). The Plug-and-Play variant (PnP-PSGLA) replaces the proximal operator with a pretrained denoiser, and empirically achieves faster mixing than PnP-ULA by rigidly projecting iterates onto the clean-data manifold rather than softly guiding them.

## Results

On the CBSD68 dataset for 50% masked-pixel inpainting, PnP-PSGLA with DnCNN achieves the best PSNR (30.81 dB), best LPIPS (0.05), and tied-best SSIM (0.92) among all compared methods. It requires only 10,000 iterations and 120 seconds per image, making it 10× faster than PnP-ULA (100,000 iterations, 1,200 s) while substantially outperforming it in quality. The TV-denoiser variant of PnP-PSGLA runs in just 25 s with 1,000 iterations at a modest quality drop (PSNR 29.24).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PnP-PSGLA with DnCNN outperforms all five baselines on PSNR and LPIPS. Against PnP-ULA [59], the improvement is dramatic: PSNR rises from 27.89 to 30.81 dB (+2.92 dB), LPIPS drops from 0.12 to 0.05, and runtime decreases by 10× (1,200 s → 120 s), because PnP-ULA requires ~5×10⁵ iterations to converge while PnP-PSGLA converges in ~10⁴. Against the optimization-based baselines PnP [92] and RED [81], PnP-PSGLA gains 0.29–0.31 dB in PSNR and 0.01–0.02 in LPIPS, while also providing posterior samples (and thus uncertainty estimates) that these point-estimation methods cannot. Against DiffPIR [101], PnP-PSGLA achieves higher PSNR (30.81 vs 29.99) and lower LPIPS (0.05 vs 0.06), though DiffPIR is far faster (1 s vs 120 s) and lacks a convergence guarantee. The TV-denoiser variant of PnP-PSGLA [33] trades quality (PSNR 29.24) for speed (25 s, 1,000 iterations), serving as a lightweight baseline within the same algorithmic family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stability analysis of the inexact Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm (iULA) by removing the discretization error term present in prior work [75], yielding cleaner drift-stability bounds in W_p and TV metrics. It then leverages these improved stability results together with properties of the Moreau envelope to prove the first convergence guarantee for the Proximal Stochastic Gradient Langevin Algorithm (PSGLA) in the non-convex setting, where prior analyses [84, 33] were restricted to convex potentials. The paper also builds on the Plug-and-Play framework of [92] and the PnP-ULA algorithm of [59], positioning PSGLA as a semi-implicit discretization of the same Langevin SDE that ULA discretizes explicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convergence proof for PSGLA under non-convex potentials fills a significant theoretical gap that has limited the adoption of proximal Langevin methods in practical Bayesian imaging, where potentials are routinely non-convex and non-smooth. Empirically, the 100× reduction in mixing time relative to PnP-ULA (10,000 vs. 100,000+ iterations) makes posterior sampling with uncertainty quantification more tractable for image restoration. Key open questions include obtaining theoretical explanations for the observed PSGLA-over-ULA speedup, tightening the partially implicit constants in the convergence bounds, and reducing the overall computational cost of the MCMC procedure, which remains a bottleneck for high-dimensional problems.
