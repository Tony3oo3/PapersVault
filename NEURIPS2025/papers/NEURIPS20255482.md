## Problem

Prior egocentric video prediction models, such as Navigation World Models, condition on low-dimensional controls (velocity, heading) that neglect the agent's own body dynamics. This paper tackles the harder problem of predicting first-person visual outcomes from full 72-degree-of-freedom whole-body motion, where the same joint movement can produce different visual results depending on environment and body state, and where visual consequences of actions are often delayed and subtle.

## Input / Output

The model takes a context window of 3–15 past egocentric frames (224×224, encoded to latents via a frozen Stable Diffusion VAE) and a 72-dimensional action vector per timestep (3 root translation + 23 joints × 3 relative Euler angles in Xsens skeleton ordering, representing the delta from the previous frame, normalized to [−1,1] for translation and [−π,π] for rotation). It outputs the next predicted egocentric frame in latent space, decoded to pixels at inference. During training, random timeskips sample 16 frames from a 32-second window, and the timeskip magnitude is included as an additional action signal.

## Method

PEVA is an autoregressive conditional diffusion transformer (CDiT) that predicts each next frame via a DDPM denoising process conditioned on k past latent frames and the current action through AdaLN layers. Three key architectural choices distinguish it from prior CDiT-based world models: (1) random timeskips during training, where the model learns to predict across variable temporal gaps to capture both short-term dynamics and multi-second activity patterns; (2) sequence-level training with causal masking, enabling parallelized learning over all sequence prefixes in a single forward pass via spatial-only attention within the current frame and cross-attention to clean past-frame tokens; and (3) a structured 72-D action representation preserving the kinematic tree hierarchy, fed to AdaLN via simple concatenation rather than learned embeddings. The training objective is a reweighted ELBO (simple noise-prediction loss plus a VLB covariance term) applied per transition under a Markov assumption, trained on the Nymeria dataset at 4 FPS with AdamW (lr=8e-5, batch 512).

## Results

On single-step prediction 2 seconds ahead (Nymeria validation set), PEVA achieves LPIPS 0.303, DreamSim 0.193, ATE 0.274, RPE(Rotation) 13.53°, PCK@0.2 0.791, and FID 62.29, outperforming both baselines across all metrics. The most dramatic gain is in camera rotation consistency (RPE Rotation: 13.53° vs. 26.06° for CDiT and 36.51° for DF*), indicating that whole-body pose conditioning substantially improves spatial grounding. PEVA also achieves a PCK@0.2 of 0.85 on wrist position accuracy compared to 0.22 for the text-conditioned Cosmos model, demonstrating far finer control over hand placement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEVA is compared against two baselines: CDiT (Navigation World Models, Bar et al. 2025), which conditions on low-dimensional velocity/heading controls, and DF* (Diffusion Forcing, Chen et al. 2024), which applies full-sequence diffusion to the latent sequence. PEVA wins on every reported metric against both baselines. Against CDiT, the largest relative gains are in RPE(Rotation) (13.53° vs. 26.06°, a 48% reduction) and ATE (0.274 vs. 0.386, a 29% reduction), showing that whole-body pose conditioning dramatically improves camera trajectory fidelity. Against DF*, PEVA improves FID by 15% (62.29 vs. 73.05) and PCK@0.2 by 5.5 percentage points (0.791 vs. 0.750), indicating better generative quality and hand-position accuracy. The paper also reports that PEVA's wrist PCK@0.2 of 0.85 far exceeds Cosmos (Agarwal et al. 2025) at 0.22, highlighting the advantage of structured 3D pose over text conditioning for fine-grained body control.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEVA extends the Navigation World Models (NWM) framework, which used conditional diffusion transformers to predict egocentric video from low-dimensional controls (velocity, heading), by conditioning on full 72-DoF whole-body pose structured by the kinematic tree. It also builds on the CDiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and the broader world-model literature (Dreamer, DIAMOND), but reverses the typical human motion generation setup by treating 3D pose as input rather than output. The paper draws on the Nymeria dataset for synchronized egocentric video and motion capture, and positions itself against text/image-conditioned video generation (Make-a-Video, Tune-A-Video) by grounding prediction in physically realizable body motion rather than abstract prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is a foundational step toward embodied video prediction that captures how full-body movement shapes first-person perception, with implications for humanoid robotics, VR/AR, and world-model-based planning. Key open questions include: whether the preliminary CEM-based planning (limited to single-arm, 12-DoF actions) scales to full-body closed-loop control; how to incorporate semantic/task-level conditioning beyond image similarity; and how to mitigate long-horizon error accumulation in open-loop rollouts. The finding that non-visible body joints (torso, legs) meaningfully affect egocentric dynamics through head stabilization and balance is a useful insight for future embodied systems.
