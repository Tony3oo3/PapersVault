## Problem

Generating realistic time series samples that satisfy hard domain-specific constraints (e.g., OHLC bounds for stock prices, peak demand times for power grids) remains challenging: existing training-based methods lack scalability to new constraint sets, projection-based approaches degrade sample quality by pushing noisy latents off the denoiser's training manifold, and guidance-based methods require intractable weight tuning when hundreds of constraints are present. The paper asks whether one can guarantee constraint satisfaction while preserving sample quality without additional training or external realism enforcers.

## Input / Output

CPS takes a pre-trained diffusion denoiser (Time Weaver-CSDI backbone), a constraint set expressed as up to ~100 linear inequalities (e.g., o−h≤0, c−h≤0, mean-equality constraints, argmax/argmin value constraints), and optionally contextual metadata for conditional generation. It produces a single time series sample x^gen ∈ R^{K×L} (K channels × L timesteps) that satisfies all constraints within a tolerance δ=0.01 while remaining in the support of the data distribution. Constraints are extracted from held-out test samples, so the method is evaluated on constraint sets never seen during training.

## Method

CPS modifies the DDIM reverse sampling loop by replacing the posterior mean estimate ẑ₀(z_t;ε_θ) with a projected version ẑ₀,pr(z_t;ε_θ) obtained by solving an unconstrained optimization: minimize ½(||z − ẑ₀||² + γ(t)·Π(z)), where Π(z) is the constraint violation function and γ(t)=e^{1/(1−ᾱ_{t−1})} is a time-varying penalty that is small in early (noisy) steps and grows to ~10⁵ at the final step. The key insight is that projecting the posterior mean (which lives at the same noise level as the constraint set) rather than the noisy intermediate latents avoids pushing samples off the denoiser's training manifold, and subsequent denoising steps naturally correct any projection artifacts without needing an external critic. This eliminates the need for constraint-specific training, guidance weight tuning, or additional realism-enforcing models. Under a linear constraint set with Gaussian prior, the paper proves O(1/T) convergence of the deterministic variant, showing that partial constraint enforcement in early steps suffices for the final sample to satisfy all constraints.

## Results

CPS achieves the lowest Frechet Distance (FD) across all six datasets, reaching 0.0023 on Stocks (vs. 0.0711–1.2575 for baselines) and 0.0234 on Air Quality (vs. 0.0473–0.7457). On the Discriminative Score (DS), CPS attains 0.006±0.004 on Stocks, a ~70% improvement over the best baseline (COP at 0.09±0.04). On Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), CPS achieves 0.20±0.71 on Stocks and 2.35±1.48 on Air Quality, representing a ~22% reduction over the best baseline. CPS also achieves zero constraint violation magnitude across all datasets, matching the best constraint-satisfying baselines while substantially outperforming them on sample quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the guidance-based baselines (Guided DiffTime and Diffusion-TS from [12, 22]), CPS dominates on all metrics: while these methods achieve competitive FD on some datasets, they suffer severe constraint violations (e.g., 1128.22 on Stocks for Guided DiffTime, 40.51 for Diffusion-TS) and substantially higher DTW (7.84 vs. 0.20 on Stocks). Against the post-processing baselines COP and COP-FT [12], which do achieve zero constraint violation, CPS provides a 30× reduction in FD and 15× reduction in DS on Stocks (0.0023 vs. 0.0711 FD; 0.006 vs. 0.09 DS), demonstrating that iterative projection-and-denoising avoids the quality degradation of one-shot projection. Against the projection-based methods from other domains, PDM [11] and PRODIGY [20], CPS achieves a 6× FD reduction on Air Quality (0.0234 vs. 0.1503) and a 10× reduction on Stocks (0.0023 vs. 0.0368), attributed to projecting the posterior mean estimate rather than noisy intermediate latents. CPS also outperforms the training-based Loss-DiffTime [12] on Stocks (FD 0.0023 vs. 0.9897, DTW 0.20 vs. 7.75) while matching it on Air Quality, all without constraint-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPS builds directly on the DDIM deterministic sampling framework, modifying the reverse update by replacing the posterior mean estimate with its projection onto the constraint set after each denoising step. It departs from prior training-free approaches (PDM, PRODIGY) that project noisy intermediate latents, and from guidance-based methods (Guided DiffTime, Diffusion-TS) that lack constraint satisfaction guarantees, by instead projecting the clean-sample estimate and relying on subsequent denoising steps to correct projection artifacts. The approach is conceptually related to RB-modulation for image personalization but provides convergence guarantees for linear constraint sets, and it avoids the constraint-specific training required by Loss-DiffTime or the external realism enforcers used by COP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPS offers a practical, training-free, and hyperparameter-free solution for constrained time series generation that scales to ~100 constraints, which is important for stress-testing and synthetic data generation in safety-critical domains like power grids and finance. The convergence analysis (Theorem 4.4) provides a principled penalty schedule and reveals that expensive constrained optimization is only needed at the final denoising step for large T, offering a practical efficiency insight. Open questions include extending guarantees beyond Gaussian priors and linear constraints, handling dynamic/non-convex constraints (e.g., robotics trajectories), and applying the framework to style transfer between time series. The projection step adds inference cost, and the convergence results require a unique solution assumption that may not hold in practice.
