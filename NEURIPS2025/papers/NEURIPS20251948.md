## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has driven major gains in LLM reasoning (math, code), but extending this to visual perception in VLMs is blocked by a fundamental tension: tasks demanding full-scene perception (e.g., 200-word captioning) produce outputs too long and open-ended to grade deterministically, while easily verifiable tasks (VQA) probe only fragments of a scene. No existing vision-centric task simultaneously forces exhaustive visual perception and yields a concise, unambiguously checkable answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251948/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a ~200-word human-annotated caption (from PixMo-Cap) in which exactly one 2–5-word noun phrase has been surgically replaced by a visually similar but incorrect alternative (e.g., 'red sedan' → 'blue sedan', 'three chairs' → 'four chairs', or a scene-text character swap). The model must output only the hallucinated span as a short token-level string; correctness is determined by exact string match against the injected error, with a small relaxation allowing the model to include adjacent verbatim caption words around the span. The final reward is 0.9 × answer-correctness + 0.1 × format-compliance (proper use of  and \boxed{} delimiters).

## Method

ViCrit reframes visual perception as a span-localization problem: GPT-4 is prompted to select one object description within a 200-word PixMo-Cap caption and perturb it into a visually plausible hallucination (object substitution, attribute flip, count error, spatial-relation swap, scene-text corruption, material/shape/condition change), yielding 875K image–corrupted-caption training pairs. The VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B/72B-Instruct) is fine-tuned end-to-end with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), where the deterministic binary reward from exact string matching provides a noise-free signal that scales without learned reward models. This design forces the model to cross-check every noun phrase against the image during RL exploration, internalizing a 'verify-then-respond' perceptual strategy rather than memorizing object lists. The paper further introduces ViCrit-Bench (607 manually curated samples spanning four image domains and eight hallucination types) as a zero-shot diagnostic benchmark whose scores correlate monotonically (r = 0.96) with average VLM performance across eight general benchmarks.

## Results

ViCrit-RL-72B achieves an average accuracy of 63.16% across eight general VL benchmarks, up from 59.78% for the base Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct (+3.38), and reduces CHAIRs from 26.4 to 21.0 while improving MMHal from 3.82 to 3.91. At the 7B scale, ViCrit-RL-7B improves average accuracy from 50.61% to 53.01% (+2.40) and reduces CHAIRs from 28.0 to 25.2. On the proposed ViCrit-Bench, ViCrit-RL-72B reaches 43.0% accuracy (best open-source), while the strongest closed-source model (OpenAI-o3) achieves only 47.7%, confirming the benchmark's difficulty. Cross-domain gains are notable: +4.9% on MathVision, +4.5% on VLMsAreBlind, and +3.9% on ChartXiv for the 72B model, despite these domains comprising <10% of training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base Qwen2.5-VL models, ViCrit-RL consistently wins: the 72B model gains +3.38 average accuracy and reduces CHAIRs by 5.4 points, while the 7B model gains +2.40 average and reduces CHAIRs by 2.8. Compared to Molmo (7B/72B), ViCrit-RL dominates across all metrics, with the 72B model outperforming Molmo-72B by +16.78 average accuracy (63.16 vs 46.38). Against LLaVA-OneVision, ViCrit-RL-72B leads by +11.87 average (63.16 vs 51.29) and achieves substantially lower hallucination rates. The closest competitor is InternVL2.5-78B (58.75 avg), which ViCrit-RL-72B surpasses by +4.41 on average; however, InternVL2.5-78B retains a slight edge on MMMU (68.7 vs 66.0). Among closed-source models, ViCrit-RL-72B (63.16 avg) outperforms GPT-4o (57.10 avg) by +6.06, though o1 achieves higher scores on MathVision (58.2) and MMMU (78.2) where ViCrit-RL-72B scores 40.1 and 66.0 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the RL-with-verifiable-rewards paradigm that has driven recent LLM reasoning progress (DeepSeek-R1, math/code RL) into the visual perception domain, where no comparable verifiable task previously existed. It departs from supervised captioning (which the authors argue leads to surface-level object memorization) and from VQA-style perception probes (which test only scene fragments) by reframing perception as a 'find the one injected error' critic task over 200-word captions, yielding a binary exact-match reward. The ViCrit-Bench extends prior hallucination evaluation metrics (CHAIR, MMHal) from aggregate scoring into a fine-grained, category-balanced diagnostic with eight hallucination types across four image domains, and the SFT comparison in Appendix C explicitly positions RL as superior to caption-supervised fine-tuning for generalizable perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, scalable recipe for RL training on visual perception that was previously blocked by the lack of verifiable tasks, and the cross-domain transfer (natural-image training → math, abstract, chart improvements) suggests the model learns a general 'how to perceive' strategy rather than memorizing object lists. The r=0.96 correlation between ViCrit-Bench scores and general VLM accuracy positions the benchmark as a lightweight diagnostic proxy for overall perception capability. Key limitations include the modest 72B improvement (data may be insufficiently challenging for stronger models), drops in spatial and text hallucination detection after RL (attributed to training data imbalance), and evaluation on a single model family (Qwen2.5-VL), leaving open whether the recipe transfers across architectures. Future work could explore harder hallucination injection, video perception, and multi-error variants.
