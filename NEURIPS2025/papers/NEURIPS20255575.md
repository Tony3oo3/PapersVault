## Problem

Discrete diffusion model inference is bottlenecked by a trade-off between exact simulation methods (unpredictable runtime, redundant score evaluations) and first-order approximate methods like τ-leaping (small step sizes needed to control discretization error). No high-order numerical schemes had been developed for the discrete diffusion setting, leaving a gap between the rich literature on high-order ODE/SDE solvers and the practical needs of discrete generative modeling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained neural network score function and an initial sample drawn from the stationary distribution (uniform over [S]^d or an absorbing/masked state), then evolves the backward continuous-time Markov chain over a discretized time grid with early stopping at T−δ. Outputs are discrete data samples: 1024-token text sequences (S=50258), 256×256 images represented as 256 VQ-GAN tokens (S=1025), or 256-token math-reasoning responses. Computational cost is measured in number of score function evaluations (NFE), and the method requires only a single forward pass per stage per step, making it directly comparable to τ-leaping under matched NFE budgets.

## Method

The paper introduces two two-stage high-order solvers for the backward CTMC: θ-RK-2 (a direct analog of classical second-order Runge-Kutta using Poisson random measures) and θ-Trapezoidal (which splits each interval at a θ-section point, runs τ-leaping for the first sub-step, then applies an extrapolation of intensities with coefficients (α₁, −α₂) for the second sub-step). The key design choice in θ-Trapezoidal is using extrapolation rather than interpolation, which renders the scheme unconditionally second-order for all θ∈(0,1], whereas θ-RK-2 requires θ∈(0,1/2] for the same guarantee. Theoretical analysis via stochastic integral formulations and Dynkin's formula establishes KL-divergence error bounds of O(κ²T) for both methods, improving on the O(κT) of τ-leaping. Empirically, θ-Trapezoidal outperforms τ-leaping, Euler, Tweedie τ-leaping, FHS, and Semi-AR across text (RADD/GPT-2), image (MaskGIT/ImageNet), and math-reasoning (LLaDA-8B/GSM8K) benchmarks at matched NFE, with robustness to θ in the range [0.3, 0.5].

## Results

On text generation (RADD, OpenWebText, 1024 tokens), the θ-Trapezoidal method achieves a generative perplexity of 49.05 at NFE=128 and 27.55 at NFE=1024 (evaluated with GPT-2 Large), outperforming τ-leaping (52.37 / 28.80) and Euler (86.28 / 44.69) by large margins. On ImageNet 256×256 image generation (MaskGIT), θ-Trapezoidal consistently achieves lower FID than Euler and τ-leaping across all NFE budgets from 4 to 64. On GSM8K math reasoning with the 8B LLaDA-Instruct model, θ-Trapezoidal reaches 35.1% accuracy at NFE=64, surpassing the Semi-AR sampler (33.6–33.8%) in the low-NFE regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The θ-Trapezoidal method outperforms all six baselines on text generation: it beats τ-leaping [22] by ~3.3 perplexity points at NFE=128 (49.05 vs 52.37) and by ~1.2 at NFE=1024 (27.55 vs 28.80), and dominates Euler [33] and Tweedie τ-leaping [30] by over 36 points at NFE=128. Against the exact First-Hitting Sampler [21], θ-Trapezoidal achieves substantially lower perplexity (49.05 vs ≤122.73 at NFE=128), demonstrating that freedom from discretization error does not guarantee better sample quality. On image generation (Figure 3), θ-Trapezoidal achieves lower FID than both Euler and τ-leaping at every NFE from 4 to 64, while Parallel Decoding [63] and FHS only lead at extremely low NFE (≤8) before saturating. On GSM8K with the 8B LLaDA-Instruct model, θ-Trapezoidal (35.1%) outperforms Semi-AR [64] (33.6–33.8%) at NFE=64, with the gap narrowing at NFE=256 (39.7% vs 40.3% for Semi-AR Rand.), confirming that the high-order advantage is most pronounced under tight computational budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts high-order numerical integration techniques—specifically the two-stage Runge-Kutta and trapezoidal methods developed for ODEs (Butcher), SDEs (Anderson & Mattingly; Burrage), and chemical reaction systems (Hu et al.)—to the discrete diffusion model setting for the first time. The direct algorithmic predecessor is the first-order τ-leaping method (Campbell et al., NeurIPS 2022), which the authors extend from O(κT) to O(κ²T) discretization error in KL divergence. The key technical departure from the SDE/chemical-reaction literature is handling both time- and state-inhomogeneous Poisson intensities simultaneously, which requires careful design of the extrapolation coefficients (α₁, α₂) and a positivity condition on the extrapolated intensity that is a long-standing open problem inherited from the SDE trapezoidal method literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Discrete diffusion models are becoming a primary generative framework for text, images, molecules, and DNA sequences, but their inference cost (number of score evaluations) has been a practical bottleneck; a provably second-order solver that requires no retraining and works across 200M–8B parameter models is a practically important efficiency gain. The positivity assumption on the extrapolated intensity remains an open theoretical issue (the paper shows it holds with >95% probability empirically but cannot prove it unconditionally), and the authors identify higher-order schemes, adaptive step sizes, and parallel sampling as natural next steps. The finding that approximate high-order methods can outperform exact simulation (FHS) at high NFE budgets challenges the implicit assumption that unbiased sampling is always preferable when score estimation error is nonzero.
