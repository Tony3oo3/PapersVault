## Problem

The classical Black-Box Shift Estimator (BBSE) recovers target class priors by inverting an empirical confusion matrix, but produces a brittle point estimate that amplifies finite-sample noise and treats all classes independently. In high-class-count regimes with semantically related labels, this leads to fragile estimates for rare classes and provides no calibrated uncertainty. The paper asks whether a fully probabilistic model can simultaneously regularize both the confusion matrix and the target prior while exploiting label-similarity structure.

## Input / Output

The method is a pure post-processing layer requiring three inputs: (i) a frozen classifier's predictions on n' unlabelled target instances, (ii) a small labelled source-validation set (e.g., 5,000 points) used to form the empirical confusion matrix, and (iii) a pre-computed label-similarity graph whose edges come from CLIP text embeddings (k-NN with k=4 for K=10, k=8 for K=100) or penultimate-layer features for MNIST. The output is a full joint posterior over the target prior vector q and the confusion matrix C, from which per-class credible intervals and posterior predictive distributions are read off directly. No retraining of the classifier is needed.

## Method

GS-B³SE places a joint Bayesian hierarchical model on both the target log-odds vector θ and each confusion-matrix column φ_i, coupling them through a shared Laplacian-Gaussian Markov random field on the label-similarity graph. The confusion-matrix columns are parameterized via a Dirichlet-log-normal hierarchy (log-odds → softmax → simplex), and Gamma hyper-priors on the shrinkage strengths τ_q and τ_C make the model fully hierarchical. Posterior inference uses either Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (NUTS, 4 chains × 1,000 draws) or a fast block Newton-CG scheme exploiting the sparse Laplacian Hessian. Through information geometry, the negative log-posterior decomposes into an m-convex KL-divergence term (data fit) and an e-convex Laplacian quadratic form (graph prior), making the objective geodesically convex under the Fisher-Rao metric with strong-convexity modulus α = n'λ_min(F₀) + τ_q λ₂(L). The natural-gradient flow reduces to a replicator-Laplacian dynamical system, and the paper proves identifiability, N^{-1/2} posterior contraction, class-wise variance bounds O(1/(λ₂(L)·N)), and N^{-1} bias under Laplacian misspecification.

## Results

GS-B³SE achieves the lowest ℓ₁ prior-estimation error across all three datasets: 0.002 on MNIST (K=10), 0.025 on CIFAR-10 (K=10), and 0.22 on CIFAR-100 (K=100). After Saerens post-correction, it attains 98.6% accuracy on MNIST, 84.4% on CIFAR-10, and 78.3% on CIFAR-100. The advantage over the strongest point-estimator baseline (MLLS) widens with the number of classes, from a 5× error reduction on MNIST to a 3.2× reduction on CIFAR-100, confirming that graph smoothing is most beneficial in high-class-count, low-per-class-count regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-B³SE outperforms all four baselines on both ℓ₁ prior error and post-correction accuracy across every dataset. Against the strongest point-estimator MLLS, it reduces ℓ₁ error from 0.010 to 0.002 on MNIST (5×), from 0.052 to 0.025 on CIFAR-10 (2.1×), and from 0.71 to 0.22 on CIFAR-100 (3.2×), with corresponding accuracy gains of +2.3 pp, +3.2 pp, and +4.9 pp. The original BBSE and Saerens EM are substantially worse, particularly on CIFAR-100 where BBSE's ℓ₁ error (1.62) is 7.4× that of GS-B³SE. RLLS, which adds an ℓ₂ penalty to the normal equations, improves over BBSE but still trails GS-B³SE by a factor of 8 on CIFAR-100 ℓ₁ error (0.92 vs. 0.22), highlighting that hand-tuned scalar regularization cannot match structured graph-based smoothing when per-class counts are scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Black-Box Shift Estimator (BBSE) framework of Lipton et al. (2018) from a deterministic matrix inversion to a fully Bayesian hierarchical model, and generalizes the Saerens EM re-weighting procedure by showing its fixed point emerges as a special case of the proposed natural-gradient flow. The graph-structured smoothing draws on Laplacian-Gaussian Markov random fields from spatial statistics and recent graph-Dirichlet-multinomial models (Kołodziejek et al., 2023), but is the first to couple both the target prior and every confusion-matrix column to the same label-similarity graph within a single joint posterior. The information-geometric interpretation positions GS-B³SE as a Riemannian penalised likelihood on the simplex, unifying the m-convex data-fit term and the e-convex graph regulariser under the Fisher-Rao metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method delivers calibrated credible intervals for target class priors—something no prior point estimator (BBSE, RLLS, MLLS, EM) provides—making it directly useful for downstream decision-making under distribution shift where over-confident but wrong priors are costly. The variance bound scaling inversely with the graph's algebraic connectivity λ₂(L) gives practitioners a principled diagnostic: denser label-similarity graphs yield tighter estimates, and the robustness result (bias decays as N⁻¹ under Laplacian misspecification) means the method degrades gracefully with imperfect graphs. Open questions include whether the graph can be learned jointly with the posterior, whether structured variational approximations can handle extreme label counts (K > 1000), and whether the information-geometric framing extends to covariate-shift or mixed-shift settings.
