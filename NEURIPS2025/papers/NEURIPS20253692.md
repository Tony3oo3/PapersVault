## Problem

While deep networks have demonstrated cross-dataset transfer for neural decoding, it remains unclear whether the foundation-model paradigm of large-scale pretraining on diverse data can productively improve intracortical motor decoding, and what fundamental data barriers (sensor variability, output stereotypy) ultimately cap the benefits of scaling. This paper quantifies the premise by pretraining an autoregressive Transformer on 2000 hours of paired neural and behavioral data from over 30 subjects and systematically probing where and why scaling helps or fails.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253692/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input is intracortical spiking activity tokenized as binned spike counts at 20 ms temporal resolution, spatially patched into 32-channel vectors (e.g., a 100-channel array yields 4 tokens per timestep), alongside low-dimensional behavioral covariates (kinematics, EMG, forces) assigned one token per dimension per timestep. BCI-controlled data additionally carries Phase and Return tokens. At inference, behavior tokens are zero-masked and Phase/Return tokens are omitted, so the model predicts behavior purely from the neural token stream in a causal, autoregressive fashion.

## Method

NDT3 is an autoregressive Transformer (45M or 350M parameters) with modality-specific linear readin/readout layers, following the GATO/TDM template. All modalities are flattened into a single token stream ordered to respect a canonical causal sequence within each real-world timestep, with rotary embeddings tracking timesteps and learned modality-specific position embeddings distinguishing tokens within a timestep. Pretraining uses MSE loss for behavioral prediction and categorical cross-entropy for spike-count and return prediction over ~2000 hours of data from motor cortex in monkeys and humans; fine-tuning retains the same objectives and updates all parameters. The key empirical finding is that joint scaling of data (1.5 hr → 2 khr) and model size (45M → 350M) yields consistent downstream gains, but only when the target dataset contains fewer than ~1.5 hours of data; beyond that threshold, irreducible sensor variability and attractor-like output stereotypy prevent further improvement.

## Results

NDT3 is pretrained on ~2000 hours of paired neural spiking and behavioral data from 30+ monkeys and humans, in 45M and 350M parameter autoregressive Transformer variants. Across 31 evaluation settings spanning 8 motor decoding tasks, the 350M-parameter model pretrained on 2000 hours achieves the best aggregate behavior-prediction R², significantly outperforming all other pretrained NDT3 variants (Wilcoxon signed-rank test) except the 350M 200-hour model. Critically, pretraining gains over from-scratch NDT3 are concentrated at downstream data scales below ~1.5 hours; beyond this threshold, the distinction between pretrained and from-scratch models vanishes after normalizing for dataset difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDT3 outperforms both baselines across all 31 evaluation settings. Against the Wiener Filter (ridge regression with multi-timestep history), all NDT3 variants (scratch and pretrained) achieve higher aggregate R², with the gap widening as downstream data scale decreases. Against NDT2 (the prior MAE-self-supervised Transformer that leads the FALCON benchmark), from-scratch NDT3 already outperforms both the from-scratch and the 100-hour-pretrained NDT2 variants, and pretrained NDT3 extends this lead. Within the NDT3 family, the key finding is that scaling pretraining data from 1.5 hr to 2000 hr at 45M parameters yields diminishing returns (saturating near the 200-hr level), but jointly scaling to 350M parameters at 2000 hr produces a significant further gain. The 350M 2khr model is the only variant that significantly outperforms all other pretrained NDT3s (FDR-corrected pairwise tests), though it does not significantly beat the 350M 200-hr model. Importantly, all pretraining advantages over from-scratch NDT3 disappear once downstream fine-tuning data exceeds ~1.5 hours, indicating that the practical benefit of pretraining is confined to low-data regimes typical of early BCI calibration sessions.

## Relation to Prior Work

NDT3 is the direct successor to NDT2 (Ye et al., NeurIPS 2023), replacing its masked autoencoder self-supervision with an autoregressive Transformer architecture inspired by GATO and Decision Transformers. The autoregressive design enables a unified token stream over neural spiking activity and heterogeneous behavioral covariates (kinematics, EMG, force), with Decision Transformer-style Phase and Return tokens to handle BCI-driven behavior. The paper positions itself within the broader foundation-model movement applied to neural data, scaling from NDT2's ~100 hours/10M parameters to 2000 hours/350M parameters, while explicitly distinguishing its approach from other scaled spiking-activity Transformers (POYO, NDT-MTM) and from NeuroAI efforts that use pretrained language/vision models to predict neural activity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that pretraining gains saturate at roughly 1.5 hours of downstream data and that further scaling without increased model capacity actively degrades performance—provides a concrete, well-evidenced diagnosis of why neural data scaling differs fundamentally from NLP/CV scaling. The paper isolates two structural barriers (sensor variability causing input-order sensitivity, and output stereotypy preventing angular extrapolation) that future neural foundation models must overcome, serving as minimal benchmarks for the field. Practically, the released 350M/2khr model reduces iBCI calibration burden for users with limited data, but the narrow benefit window means the broader promise of 'scale to solve everything' remains unfulfilled for intracortical decoding.
