## Problem

Existing machine unlearning and certified data removal methods assume that the data distribution remains similar before and after removal, relying on gradient-based parameter updates that degrade under out-of-distribution (OOD) shifts. When samples are removed, the intrinsic correlations between feature representations and labels transform, weakening removal accuracy and generalization to unseen data. No prior method explicitly addresses the feature-correlation shifts induced by removal while maintaining certified removal guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained neural network split into a feature-extraction layer (w_extr) and a linear classification layer (w_clf), along with the full training dataset and the specific sample (x_n, y_n) to be removed. Input features are first projected through a Random Fourier Feature mapping into a higher-dimensional space (dimension m_z, typically up to 1000), where sample weights are optimized to minimize pairwise cross-covariance. The output is a localized Newton-update adjustment to the classifier weights (w_clf^-) plus the decorrelated feature representations, avoiding full retraining while preserving the ε-certified removal guarantee.

## Method

DecoRemoval integrates two modules: (1) a discriminative-preserving factor decorrelation module that applies Random Fourier Features to map input features into a higher-dimensional space, then optimizes sample weights to minimize the Frobenius norm of pairwise partial cross-covariance matrices in RKHS, reducing inter-feature statistical dependence while preserving class-discriminative structure; and (2) a smoothed data removal module that injects a random linear perturbation term (b^T w_clf) into the training loss, which shifts the gradient by a constant vector but leaves the Hessian unchanged, thereby preserving the validity of the Newton-update removal mechanism while obfuscating gradient signals associated with removed samples. The key theoretical insight is that the linear perturbation's zero second derivative ensures the certified removal update w_clf^- = w_clf* + H^{-1}Δ remains structurally identical to the unperturbed case, providing an information-theoretic safeguard against data leakage without sacrificing removal accuracy. The RFF mapping achieves dimensionality reduction at linear computational complexity, and the overall algorithm requires only a lightweight fine-tuning of the linear classifier rather than full model retraining.

## Results

On five benchmark datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, SST-2, ESS, CGSS), DecoRemoval consistently achieves near-retraining accuracy across all removal scales (1,000–10,000 samples). At 1,000 removed samples, DecoRemoval reaches 48.97% ACC / 0.482 F1 on MNIST (vs. 51.75% retraining), 90.45% ACC / 0.827 F1 on SST-2 (vs. 91.76% retraining), and 54.97% ACC / 0.520 F1 on ESS (vs. 55.43% retraining). Even at 10,000 removed samples, DecoRemoval retains 48.34% ACC on MNIST and 50.10% ACC on CGSS, outperforming the strongest baseline (SSD) by 3.3 and 2.9 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecoRemoval outperforms all five baselines (CR, SISA, DP-SGD, SSD, CU) on every dataset and removal scale. The closest competitor is SSD (Foster et al., AAAI 2024), which uses Fisher-information-based parameter dampening; DecoRemoval exceeds SSD by 3.5 pp ACC on MNIST (48.97 vs. 45.45), 3.5 pp on CIFAR-10 (48.56 vs. 45.06), and 4.8 pp on ESS (54.97 vs. 50.15) at 1,000 removed samples. Certified Removal (Guo et al., ICML 2020) and SISA (Qu et al., 2024) are the weakest baselines, trailing DecoRemoval by 5–9 pp ACC on image datasets. On the noisy survey datasets (ESS, CGSS), where feature correlation is most severe, DecoRemoval's advantage is largest: at 10,000 removed samples it achieves 54.64% ACC on ESS versus 49.19% for SSD and 48.40% for CR. On SST-2, DecoRemoval matches DP-SGD in ACC (90.45%) but surpasses it in F1 (0.827 vs. 0.825) while maintaining better efficiency (0.097 s vs. 0.124 s per removal). In the MIA privacy evaluation (Table 3), DecoRemoval achieves the lowest attack success rate (50.2%) on CGSS with LSTM while retaining higher accuracy (56.8%) than CR (55.4%), confirming a favorable privacy–utility trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

DecoRemoval builds directly on the certified data removal framework of Guo et al. (2020), which uses a Newton update on the linear classifier to remove a sample's influence without retraining. The key departure is the insertion of a Random Fourier Feature (RFF) decorrelation module—borrowed from stable learning (Stablenet, Zhang et al. 2021) and kernel independence testing—between the feature extractor and the linear head, motivated by the observation that inter-feature correlation shifts under OOD conditions degrade the Newton update's accuracy. A second departure is the addition of a random linear loss perturbation (b^T w_clf) that the authors prove leaves the Hessian unchanged, thereby preserving the removal update while obscuring per-sample gradient signals. The method is positioned against recent retraining-free unlearning approaches (SSD via Fisher information, CU via Newton bounding) and privacy-focused baselines (SISA, DP-SGD), but unlike those it explicitly targets distributional shift rather than merely privacy guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most certified unlearning methods assume the pre- and post-removal distributions are similar, yet real-world deletion requests (GDPR, financial compliance) inevitably shift the data distribution. By showing that RFF-based decorrelation plus a provably safe loss perturbation can close much of the accuracy gap to full retraining across images, text, and survey data, the work offers a lightweight, retraining-free recipe for OOD-robust unlearning. However, the Newton update is restricted to the linear classification layer, so the method does not yet handle deep non-linear heads; the authors themselves flag integration with data augmentation, adversarial training, and large-model settings as open directions. The moderate (1–3 point) accuracy gains over strong 2024 baselines (SSD, CU) and the comparable efficiency to certified removal suggest a useful but incremental step rather than a paradigm shift.
