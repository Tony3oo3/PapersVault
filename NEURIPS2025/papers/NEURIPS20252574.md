## Problem

Existing 3D HOI motion generators depend on predefined object meshes and limited mocap data, constraining generalization, while video generation models achieve visual fidelity but produce physically implausible hand-object interactions. The paper identifies a methodological contradiction: motion systems model physical constraints but lack data scale, whereas video models leverage massive visual priors but lack explicit motion dynamics. SViMo resolves this by arguing that appearance and motion co-evolve under shared physical laws and should be denoised jointly.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (416×624×3) and a text prompt (max 226 tokens) as input, requiring no predefined object models, pose sequences, or motion trajectories. It simultaneously produces a 49-frame HOI video at 416×624 resolution and explicit 3D motion sequences comprising 42 hand keypoints and 298 object point cloud nodes per frame. Internally, SViMo represents motion as a 2D 'rendered motion video' (the 3D interactions projected onto the image plane) to bridge the representation gap with the video latent space, while VID recovers the explicit 3D geometry.

## Method

SViMo extends the CogVideoX-5B DiT backbone (42 blocks, 6.02B params) into a joint video-motion denoiser where video and motion latents are diffused and denoised synchronously within a single architecture. A triple-modality adaptive modulation mechanism learns per-modality scaling, shifting, and gating parameters from the timestep embedding to align heterogeneous text, video, and motion feature spaces, while 3D full-attention captures inter- and intra-modal dependencies across the concatenated 26,590-token sequence. A separate Vision-aware 3D Interaction Diffusion (VID) module generates explicit 3D hand poses and object point clouds from the denoised video and motion latents via dual-stream 3D convolutional encoding and cross-attention. A closed-loop feedback mechanism feeds VID's 3D outputs back into SViMo through two pathways: (1) interaction guidance, where predicted 3D motions are re-projected to 2D rendered motion videos and concatenated with the noised motion latent, and (2) gradient constraints, where VID's loss backpropagates through SViMo. Training proceeds in two phases—VID warmup followed by joint optimization with weighted losses (ω₁=1, ω₂=0.05)—on the TACO dataset using 4×A800 GPUs with DeepSpeed ZeRO-3.

## Results

On the TACO benchmark, SViMo achieves the highest overall video quality score of 0.8785 (VBench), surpassing CogVideoX-5B (0.8727), Easy Animate (0.8297), and Animate Anyone (0.8172). For 3D motion generation, SViMo dramatically outperforms MDM and EMDM, reducing MPJPE from 0.3382/0.3255 to 0.1087, Chamfer distance from 0.7915/0.7788 to 0.1577, and HOI FID from 0.4056/0.3681 to 0.1050. User studies confirm these gains, with 78.42% preference for SViMo videos and 97.56% for its motions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SViMo wins on overall video quality against all six video baselines. Against the native 3D video models (Hunyuan-13B, Wan-14B), SViMo achieves a substantially higher overall score (0.8785 vs. 0.4493 and 0.7675) because those models produce near-static or hallucinated outputs with poor dynamic degree. Against the 2.5D image-animation methods (Animate Anyone, Easy Animate), SViMo improves subject and background consistency by ~3–4% while maintaining comparable dynamic degree. Against CogVideoX-5B (the closest competitor, fine-tuned on TACO), SViMo gains a small but consistent edge across all four VBench sub-metrics. For motion generation, SViMo dominates both MDM and EMDM by large margins: MPJPE is reduced by ~67% (0.1087 vs. 0.3382/0.3255), Chamfer distance by ~80% (0.1577 vs. 0.7915/0.7788), and HOI FID by ~72% (0.1050 vs. 0.4056/0.3681), attributed to the vision-aware 3D interaction diffusion and closed-loop feedback that MDM/EMDM lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

SViMo sits at the intersection of two established but largely separate research lines: 3D hand-object interaction motion generation (MDM, EMDM, Text2HOI, CG-HOI), which relies on mocap data and predefined object meshes, and large-scale video generation (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, Animate Anyone), which leverages massive visual priors but lacks explicit physical dynamics. The paper departs from both by extending a pretrained image-to-video DiT (CogVideoX-5B) into a unified synchronized diffusion framework that jointly denoises video latents and 'rendered motion video' latents, then couples this with a separate vision-aware 3D interaction diffusion (VID) module via a closed-loop feedback mechanism. This contrasts with prior joint-generation attempts like Vlogger (two-stage pose-then-video) and VideoJAM (video + 2D optical flow), as SViMo produces explicit 3D hand trajectories and object point clouds within a single end-to-end denoising pipeline without requiring pose sequences or object meshes as inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental tension in HOI generation: motion models capture physical plausibility but generalize poorly due to limited mocap data, while video models have rich visual priors but produce physically implausible interactions. The closed-loop co-evolution between SViMo and VID, where 3D motion guidance refines video denoising and video latents in turn constrain motion, is a principled architectural answer to this tension. However, the study is limited to a single training dataset (TACO, 2.5k sequences), the video quality improvement over the CogVideoX-5B backbone is modest (0.8785 vs. 0.8727 overall VBench), and the 6B-parameter model requires 4×A800 GPUs. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences, multi-object or articulated interactions beyond the point-cloud representation, and whether the synchronized diffusion paradigm generalizes to whole-body human-object interaction or other embodied generation tasks.
