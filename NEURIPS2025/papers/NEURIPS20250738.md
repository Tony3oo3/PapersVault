## Problem

Solving sparse-reward RL tasks in high-dimensional, long-horizon settings requires directed exploration toward the target goal, yet prior goal-selection strategies either pursue undirected novelty (MEGA, DISCERN) or always target the final goal (HER), making them intractable when the goal space is large. The missing ingredient is a principled mechanism that simultaneously balances achievability, novelty, and relevance to the target, without relying on hand-designed reward shaping or external models.

## Input / Output

At each episode, DISCOVER receives the target goal g*, the current initial state s0, the set of previously achieved goals G_ach (maintained as a replay buffer of observed states), and mean/variance estimates from an ensemble of critic networks. It outputs a single exploratory goal g_t that the goal-conditioned policy π_θ(s, g_t) pursues for the duration of the episode, followed by a random-exploration phase. All relevance and direction estimates are bootstrapped entirely from the agent's own critic ensemble—no distance metrics, generative models, or human-specified reward shaping are required.

## Method

DISCOVER selects exploratory goals by maximizing a UCB-style objective (Eq. 3) that combines three terms estimated by an ensemble of critics: achievability V(s0,g), novelty σ(s0,g), and relevance V(g,g*) + βσ(g,g*), weighted by a tunable coefficient α_t. The key insight is that the critic ensemble's mean provides a bootstrapped sense of direction toward the target, while its variance quantifies uncertainty, enabling principled exploration-exploitation trade-offs at the goal-selection level. An online adaptation rule (Eq. 4) adjusts α_t to maintain a target goal-achievement rate of ~50%, preventing the agent from selecting goals that are too easy or too hard. The connection to UCB sampling in linear bandits yields a theoretical bound on episodes to reach the target that is linear in the initial-to-target distance D and independent of the volume of the full goal space, avoiding the curse of dimensionality that plagues undirected methods. The method is instantiated on top of TD3 with hindsight experience replay for goal relabeling, and is evaluated on antmaze, arm manipulation, and n-dimensional pointmaze environments.

## Results

In pointmaze navigation, DISCOVER is the only method that scales beyond 2D: it reaches 10% target goal achievement in 2.9M steps (2D), 3.1M (3D), 7.4M (4D), 5.4M (5D), and 18.7M (6D), while all undirected baselines (MEGA, Achievability+Novelty) fail entirely beyond 2D and HER never succeeds in any dimension. In the antmaze and arm manipulation environments, DISCOVER consistently achieves higher success rates faster than all goal-selection baselines, with the gap widening in the harder configurations. Standard RL methods without explicit goal selection (TD3, SAC, Bootstrapped DQN, RND, MaxInfoRL) fail to reach the target task even in the 'simple' environment settings within 50M steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISCOVER decisively outperforms all goal-selection baselines. MEGA [Pitis, ICML 2020] and the undirected Achievability+Novelty ablation both solve 2D pointmazes (4.8M and 5.2M steps respectively) but completely fail in 3D and above, while DISCOVER solves up to 6D in 18.7M steps. HER [Andrychowicz, NeurIPS 2017] never reaches 10% success in any pointmaze dimension and underperforms in antmaze/arm, likely because it always targets the final goal without intermediate skill-building. DISCERN (uniform) [Warde-Farley, 2018] samples goals uniformly from achieved states and also fails to scale with dimension. The standard RL baselines—TD3 [Fujimoto, ICML 2018], SAC [Haarnoja, ICML 2018], Bootstrapped DQN [Osband, NeurIPS 2016], RND [Burda, ICLR 2019], and MaxInfoRL [Sukhija, ICLR 2025]—all fail to achieve the target task even in the simplest environment configurations within 50M steps, demonstrating that intrinsic curiosity and reward-shaping alone are insufficient for long-horizon sparse-reward tasks without directed goal selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

DISCOVER extends the goal-conditioned RL exploration framework of Pitis et al. (MEGA, ICML 2020), which selects goals balancing achievability and novelty, by adding a bootstrapped relevance term V(g, g*) that directs exploration toward the target goal. The goal relabeling mechanism from Andrychowicz et al. (HER, NeurIPS 2017) remains a core algorithmic component for training. The key conceptual departure is connecting the goal selection objective to UCB sampling (Srinivas et al., ICML 2010), yielding a theoretical bound on time-to-solve that depends only on the initial distance to the target rather than the volume of the goal space—a property absent from prior multi-goal exploration bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that undirected goal selection (MEGA, uniform sampling) completely fails in 3D+ pointmazes while DISCOVER scales to 6D highlights that relevance/direction is not merely a marginal improvement but a prerequisite for solving high-dimensional sparse-reward tasks. The method's simplicity—adding a single critic-based relevance term to an existing objective—makes it broadly applicable to any goal-conditioned RL setup. Open questions include whether the approach extends to goal generation (rather than selection), hierarchical planning, and domains with large language model priors where the ensemble overhead may be prohibitive. The reliance on bootstrapped value estimates for direction means performance is bounded by the critic's generalization ability, a limitation the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
