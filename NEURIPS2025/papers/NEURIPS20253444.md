## Problem

Prior LLM-based time-series anomaly detection (TSAD) work has shown limited success, often performing worse than trivial baselines, yet the fundamental question of *why* LLMs struggle with TSAD remained unexplored. This paper diagnoses two distinct failure modes: LLMs fail to detect subtle anomalies (trend shifts, frequency changes) when complex normal patterns like overlapping seasonality obscure them, and they fail to accurately localize anomaly intervals because existing prompts implicitly require token counting—a well-documented weakness of LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw univariate time series and first applies de-seasonalization (removing only the seasonal component via ACF-based period discovery and additive decomposition), then feeds the resulting de-seasonalized sequence to the LLM in two simultaneous modalities: (1) a text representation where each numerical value is explicitly paired with its positional index (e.g., '1: 0.32, 2: 0.41, …'), and (2) a line-plot image of the same de-seasonalized series. The LLM outputs a set of anomaly intervals as (start_index, end_index) pairs, which are post-processed from free-form text. The explicit indexing in the text input is the key structural difference from prior AnomLLM prompts, which presented bare value lists without positional cues.

## Method

The proposed method is a zero-shot, tool-augmented pipeline with two complementary components targeting the two diagnosed failure modes. First, a lightweight de-seasonalization module computes the sample autocorrelation function to identify the dominant period P*, performs classic additive decomposition (x_t = s_t + τ_t + r_t) with window length P*, and passes only the de-seasonalized sequence x̃_t = x_t − s_t to the LLM—removing stable recurring seasonality while preserving subtle trend and frequency deviations that constitute the anomalies. Second, index-aware prompting embeds each value's positional index directly in the text input, transforming the LLM's localization task from fragile internal token counting into a simple index lookup. The LLM receives both the indexed text and the de-seasonalized image simultaneously, integrating textual and visual cues for detection and localization. No fine-tuning or domain-specific training is required; the entire approach operates in a single zero-shot inference call per time series, with the statistical decomposition implemented via FFT at O(T log T) cost.

## Results

On the AnomLLM benchmark, the proposed method (de-seasonalization + index-aware prompting) achieves F1 scores of 70.04 (GPT-4o), 67.56 (Qwen-2.5-VL-72B), 63.31 (Gemini-1.5-Flash), and 34.66 (InternVL2-LLaMA3-76B) under standard metrics, surpassing all 21 existing AnomLLM prompt variants. The average F1 improvement over the best AnomLLM baseline is 39.9%, with a maximum gain of 66.6% (GPT-4o: 70.04 vs. 43.56). On the TSB-AD-U benchmark, the LLM-based method achieves the highest average F1 among 16 non-LLM baselines (ML, DL, and foundation models), though at significantly higher inference latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 21 AnomLLM prompt variants [Zhou & Yu, ICLR 2025], the proposed method wins on every LLM tested, with the largest gains on GPT-4o (+28.01 F1 over the best AnomLLM prompt) and Qwen-2.5 (+20.91 F1), while InternVL2-LLaMA3-76B shows only marginal improvement (+0.99 F1), correlating with its lower MMLU-Pro/MMMU reasoning scores. On the TSB-AD-U benchmark, the LLM-based method achieves the highest average F1 among 16 non-LLM baselines spanning ML (SR, IForest, Sub-PCA, KMeansAD, MatrixProfile), DL (CNN, USAD, AnomalyTransformer, OmniAnomaly, AutoEncoder, TimesNet, FITS), and foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM, Lag-Llama, MOMENT), though its inference speed is substantially slower due to text decoding overhead. The ablation confirms that index-aware prompting contributes the largest single gain (−23.36 F1 when removed), followed by de-seasonalization (−5.64 F1), indicating that localization is the dominant bottleneck. The paper also demonstrates that LLMs can perform context-aware anomaly filtering (excluding known/explainable anomalies via natural language context), a capability not available to any of the 16 non-LLM baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a diagnostic follow-up to the AnomLLM benchmark (Zhou & Yu, ICLR 2025) and a broader line of LLM-based TSAD work (Alnegheimish et al., Dong et al., Xu et al., Liu et al.) that evaluated downstream detection accuracy without explaining failure mechanisms. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes classical statistical tools (autocorrelation-based period discovery, additive decomposition) as a lightweight preprocessing step and identifies a specific prompting deficiency—implicit token counting for localization—that prior methods overlooked. The paper also departs from prior LLM-TSAD studies by benchmarking against 16 non-LLM baselines (ML, DL, and foundation models) on TSB-AD-U, a comparison largely absent from the LLM-TSAD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition of LLM TSAD failures into 'understanding' vs. 'localization' provides a clear diagnostic framework that prior work lacked, and the finding that explicit index-aware prompting is the single most impactful intervention (23.4 F1 drop in ablation, vs. 5.6 for de-seasonalization) is a practical, low-cost insight for anyone prompting LLMs on sequential data. The context-aware filtering demonstration (excluding explainable anomalies via natural language) highlights a capability genuinely unique to LLMs that conventional score-thresholding methods cannot replicate. Key limitations remain: inference latency is orders of magnitude slower than non-LLM methods, the approach is univariate-only, and the large headline improvements (up to 66.6% F1) are partly relative to very weak baselines where many AnomLLM prompts score below the always-normal predictor.
