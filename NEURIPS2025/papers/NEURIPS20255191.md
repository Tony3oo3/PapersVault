## Problem

Existing diversified recommendation methods rely solely on observed user-item interactions, which suffer from inherent exposure and selection biases that cause underexplored user preferences to be overwhelmed or never manifested. This leads to narrow recommendations that cater only to dominant interests, placing users in 'rabbit holes.' The paper asks whether LLMs' world knowledge can be leveraged to systematically uncover these hidden preferences and use them to improve diversity without sacrificing relevance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes user textual attributes (name, bio, location), user interaction history (likes, retweets, ratings), and item textual attributes (content, category, brand, description), all of which are first refined into LLM-generated summaries to reduce noise. Beyond the final top-k diversified recommendation list, the method produces synthetic user-item interactions that augment the training set; these are generated by matching items to uncovered preference leaf nodes and scoring them with a weighted diversity-relevance criterion. The Tree of Preferences serves as a global, one-time intermediate structure shared across all users, with items pre-assigned to leaf nodes via LLM.

## Method

ToP-Rec constructs a hierarchical Tree of Preferences (ToP) via LLM, progressively partitioning the item preference space from coarse to fine (e.g., travel → nature tours → hiking), with items pre-assigned to leaf nodes. Given a user's history, the LLM performs a breadth-first search on ToP to identify the coarse-to-fine path explaining observed behavior, then re-evaluates to surface unobserved preferences not reflected in the interaction data. Candidate items matching these uncovered preferences are selected using a combined score balancing diversity (inversely proportional to preference frequency in user history) and relevance (cosine similarity between user and item BERT embeddings), and the selected items are added as synthetic interactions. A general recommender (LightGCN) is then trained on the combined original and synthetic data with BPR loss. To reduce LLM inference cost, the method dynamically selects influential users for augmentation based on k-step gradient alignment between each user's local gradient and the model's parameter trajectory, with gradient dimension reduction for scalability.

## Results

On the three real-world datasets (Twitter, Weibo, Amazon), ToP-Rec achieves the best or near-best performance across both relevance (Recall@k) and diversity (Category-Entropy@k) in nearly all settings. On Twitter, ToP-Rec attains R@50=0.0586, R@100=0.0841, CE@50=1.3275, and CE@100=1.3852, all the best among 10 methods. On Amazon, it reaches R@50=0.1380, R@100=0.2120, CE@50=0.5298, and CE@100=0.5902, again dominating all baselines. The single suboptimal result is R@100 on Weibo (0.1667 vs. backbone 0.1669), while CE@100 on Weibo (1.1369) is the best. Inference latency is comparable to traditional methods (CDM, LCD-UC) and significantly faster than LLM-based reranking (LLM4Rerank).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToP-Rec outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of metric-dataset combinations, with only one suboptimal result (R@100 on Weibo, 0.1667 vs. backbone 0.1669). Among heuristic methods, MMR and DPP improve diversity over the backbone but sacrifice relevance (e.g., DPP drops R@50 from 0.0567 to 0.0467 on Twitter), whereas ToP-Rec improves both simultaneously. Conventional diversity-enhancing methods (Box, LCD-UC, CDM) show moderate diversity gains but consistently underperform ToP-Rec on relevance by 1–4% in R@50 across datasets. LLM-based baselines (LLM4Rerank-A/AD, LLMRec-MMR) achieve competitive relevance but limited diversity improvement, likely due to coarse-grained preference analysis; for instance, LLM4Rerank-AD reaches R@50=0.1044 on Weibo but only CE@50=1.0001, while ToP-Rec achieves both R@50=0.1054 and CE@50=1.0333. The diversity-relevance trade-off analysis (Figure 2) further confirms that ToP-Rec dominates the Pareto frontier, achieving higher diversity with a larger margin while maintaining or improving relevance over the LightGCN backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: diversified recommendation (MMR, DPP, Box embedding, CDM), LLM-based recommendation (LLM4Rerank, DLCRec, LLMRec), and influential data selection (LESS). It departs from prior diversified recommendation work by arguing that observed interactions alone are insufficient due to exposure and selection bias, and instead leverages LLM world knowledge to uncover underexplored user preferences. The Tree of Preferences structure is inspired by Tree of Thoughts for hierarchical reasoning, but is applied here as a global preference-space partition rather than a per-query search. The data-centric strategy of generating synthetic interactions to train a standard recommender backbone (LightGCN, MF, NGCF) distinguishes it from LLM-based reranking or embedding-modification approaches, while the gradient-alignment-based user selection directly extends LESS to the recommendation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated gap: existing diversity methods are constrained by the same biased data they aim to diversify, creating a ceiling on achievable diversity without sacrificing relevance. The data-centric pipeline is backbone-agnostic and avoids the high inference latency of LLM-at-serving-time approaches, making it deployable. Open questions include how well the approach scales to very large item catalogs (the LLM-based item-to-leaf assignment is a one-time cost but still non-trivial), how sensitive results are to the quality of LLM-generated user/item summaries, and whether the assumption that LLM world knowledge reliably maps to individual user preferences holds across diverse domains and populations. The reliance on a 32B-parameter LLM for all reasoning steps also raises questions about cost-effectiveness in production settings.
