## Problem

Gating mechanisms are ubiquitous in sequence models (LSTMs, state-space models, linear attention) yet their specific effects within standard softmax attention remain poorly understood. Existing works that incorporate gating (e.g., Switch Heads, NSA) do not disentangle the contribution of the gate itself from other architectural components, leaving it unclear whether and why gating provides intrinsic value beyond routing or sparsity selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The gated attention layer takes the standard transformer input X ∈ R^{n×d_model} (a token sequence of length n with model dimension d_model) and produces a modulated attention output of the same shape. The gating score is computed as σ(XW_θ) where W_θ is a small learned projection (e.g., d_model × q×d_k for elementwise head-specific gating), yielding a sigmoid score in [0,1] that is element-wise multiplied with the SDPA output. The added parameter overhead is less than 2M for the 15B MoE model and wall-time latency increase is under 2%.

## Method

The paper introduces a head-specific sigmoid gate applied after the Scaled Dot-Product Attention output (position G1): Y' = Y ⊙ σ(XW_θ), where Y is the per-head SDPA output and X is the current query's hidden state. Through 30 variants across 15B MoE and 1.7B dense models trained on 3.5T tokens, the authors attribute the gains to two mechanisms: (1) the gate breaks the low-rank linear composition W_V·W_O by inserting non-linearity between the value and output projections, and (2) the resulting gating scores are strongly sparse (mean ≈ 0.116) and query-dependent, introducing input-dependent sparsity that filters irrelevant context. This sparsity eliminates both massive activations (reducing max hidden-state values from 1053 to 94) and attention sinks (reducing first-token attention from 46.7% to 4.8%), which in turn improves training stability, enables larger learning rates, and facilitates context-length extension via YaRN (gaining >10 points on RULER at 128k).

## Results

On the 15A2B MoE model (400B tokens), SDPA elementwise sigmoid gating (G1) reduces average PPL from 6.026 to 5.761 (−0.265) and improves MMLU from 58.79 to 60.82 (+2.03), HellaSwag from 73.07 to 74.64, and GSM8k from 52.92 to 55.27, outperforming parameter-expansion baselines (e.g., adding 4 experts) that add 400M parameters versus gating's 201M. On the 1.7B dense model trained on 3.5T tokens, SDPA gating improves PPL from 6.180 to 6.130 and HumanEval from 34.15 to 37.80, while also enabling stable training at 8e-3 max LR where the baseline diverges. The gate eliminates attention sink (first-token attention drops from 46.7% to 4.8%) and massive activations (max hidden-state value drops from 1053 to 94), and at 128k context length (YaRN-extended) the gated model scores 58.82 on RULER versus 31.65 for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against parameter-expansion baselines on the 15A2B MoE model, SDPA elementwise gating (G1, +201M params) achieves PPL 5.761, substantially beating k=8 (+50M, PPL 5.979), q=48 (+201M, PPL 5.953), and Add 4 Experts (+400M, PPL 5.964) on both PPL and MMLU, demonstrating that the gating mechanism is more effective per parameter than simply widening the model. Against Switch Heads [20], the paper shows in Appendix A.1 that the best Switch Head configuration (Switch v, 1top1, +3M params) is functionally equivalent to a value-layer headwise gate and achieves PPL 5.808, while the full SDPA elementwise gate (G1) further improves to 5.761, isolating the intrinsic value of query-dependent SDPA output gating beyond expert routing. Against Sandwich Norm [44] for training stability at 8e-3 max LR on the 48-layer 1.7B model, the baseline diverges entirely, Sandwich Norm recovers to PPL 7.407, and SDPA elementwise gating achieves a lower PPL of 7.325 with better benchmark scores (MMLU 54.47 vs. 52.07, GSM8k 36.62 vs. 32.90), indicating gating provides both stability and a genuine performance advantage over normalization-based stabilization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long lineage of gating mechanisms (LSTMs, Highway Networks, Mamba, linear attention) into standard softmax attention, but its key departure is a systematic, controlled disentanglement of gating's contribution from other architectural changes. It builds directly on the Scaled Dot-Product Attention formulation of Vaswani et al. [22] and positions against Switch Heads [20] and NSA [21], showing that the sigmoid gate itself—not the expert routing or sparse attention design—drives most of the gains. The closest prior work is Quantizable Transformers [69], which also observes that attention gating reduces outliers, but this paper provides a far more comprehensive variant comparison (30 configurations) and mechanistic explanation via non-linearity and input-dependent sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a head-specific sigmoid gate after SDPA (adding <2M parameters to a 15B model) eliminates attention sinks, reduces massive activations, and enables stable training at higher learning rates is practically significant—this exact configuration is already deployed in Qwen3-Next. The paper also reveals that the 'routing' component of Switch Heads contributes little beyond the gate itself, reframing how the community should interpret sparse-attention gains. Open questions remain: no theoretical account explains why eliminating attention sinks aids long-context extrapolation, and the interplay between sparsity, massive activations, and training stability in deeper/larger models is still under-explored.
