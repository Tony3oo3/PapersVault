## Problem

While machine learning (ELBO maximization) and neuroscience (free energy minimization) share an identical variational objective, neither has yielded prescriptive algorithms derived from first principles—effective models like VAEs and predictive coding were discovered empirically and justified post-hoc. The paper asks whether a top-down framework can derive brain-like inference dynamics directly from free energy minimization, producing architectures that are simultaneously biologically plausible and empirically competitive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255465/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes images (16×16 natural patches, MNIST digits, or color images up to CelebA resolution) through an iterative recurrent process: the input is held fixed while membrane potentials evolve over T_test=1,000 discrete time steps before converging to an attractor state. The output is a sequence of integer-valued Poisson spike count vectors z_t (not continuous latents), from which reconstructions are computed as x̂=Φz. This spike-count representation is hardware-efficient and biologically grounded, in contrast to the continuous Gaussian latents of standard VAEs.

## Method

FOND (Free energy Online Natural-gradient Dynamics) prescribes three fixed rules—natural gradient descent on free energy, online rolling-update priors, and iterative recurrent refinement—applied after the modeler chooses distributions and parameterizations. For iP-VAE, Poisson posteriors/priors with log-rate parameterization (r=exp(u)) yield the update u_{t+1}=u_t+Φ^T x−Φ^T Φ z_t, where z_t~Poisson(exp(u_t)); the Fisher information matrix is diagonal (exp(u)), making the natural gradient computationally trivial. The recurrent term Φ^T Φ z_t produces emergent divisive normalization and lateral competition, while the exponential nonlinearity and stochastic Poisson sampling provide effective stochastic depth. Training accumulates gradients across T_train inference steps (backpropagation through time) with a single weight update, and the β-VAE coefficient controls the reconstruction-sparsity trade-off.

## Results

On 16×16 natural image patches (van Hateren), iP-VAE achieves R² = 0.83 reconstruction with 77% sparsity (proportion of zero spike counts), while iG-VAE reaches R² = 0.87 but with 0% sparsity, and iGrelu-VAE achieves R² = 0.82 with 58% sparsity. iP-VAE uses 25× fewer parameters than amortized counterparts with deep convolutional encoders, and its integer-valued spike count representations enable hardware-efficient deployment. On MNIST, the Poisson VAE family reaches ~98% classification accuracy, and iP-VAE with nonlinear decoders outperforms hybrid iterative-amortized VAEs on out-of-distribution reconstruction and classification. The model scales to color image datasets including CelebA, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet32.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iP-VAE and LCA [39] are statistically indistinguishable in reconstruction-sparsity performance, though iP-VAE exhibits lower variability across hyperparameter settings and adds stochastic spiking dynamics. All three iterative VAEs (iP-VAE, iG-VAE, iGrelu-VAE) consistently outperform their amortized counterparts (P-VAE [50], G-VAE [18], Grelu-VAE) in Euclidean distance to the reconstruction-sparsity optimum, despite the amortized models using deep convolutional encoders with orders of magnitude more parameters. On MNIST, iterative VAEs outperform both standard predictive coding (PC [22]) and incremental PC (iPC [90]) in reconstruction metrics, with iP-VAE achieving the best reconstruction-sparsity trade-off. For out-of-distribution generalization, iP-VAE with nonlinear decoders consistently outperforms hybrid iterative-amortized VAEs (Marino et al. [91], Kim et al. [92]) on both within-dataset perturbations and cross-dataset settings. In downstream classification on MNIST, the amortized P-VAE reaches ~98% accuracy comparable to supervised predictive coding networks, while iP-VAE's advantage is primarily in reconstruction quality, sparsity, and OOD robustness rather than raw classification accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Bayesian Learning Rule (BLR; Khan & Rue, 2023), which recasts diverse learning algorithms as natural-gradient variational inference, and extends it to the sequential/online setting with a rolling prior-update scheme. It also unifies predictive coding (Rao & Ballard, 1999), sparse coding (Olshausen & Field, 1996), and the Locally Competitive Algorithm (Rozell et al., 2008) as special cases of free-energy minimization, but departs from the field's typical post-hoc justification pattern by adopting a prescriptive, top-down derivation: FOND's three fixed prescriptions (natural gradients, online updates, iterative refinement) applied to Poisson distributions yield the iP-VAE spiking architecture without engineering any of its emergent properties (lateral competition, divisive normalization, V1-like Gabor filters). The Poisson VAE (Vafaii et al., 2024) by the same first author provides the distributional foundation, while the iterative-amortized inference literature (Marino et al., 2018; Kim et al., 2018) supplies the closest ML baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a purely derived spiking architecture with integer-valued spike counts and 25× fewer parameters can match or exceed amortized deep convolutional VAEs on reconstruction-sparsity trade-offs, challenging the ML community's default assumption that amortized inference is strictly superior. The emergent cortical properties (contrast-dependent latency, Gabor-like filters, divisive normalization) arising from first-principles derivation rather than architectural engineering offer a concrete, testable bridge between the free-energy principle and circuit-level neuroscience. Open questions include whether biologically plausible local learning rules can replace backpropagation-through-time, how the framework extends to hierarchical and non-stationary settings, and whether the OOD generalization advantage persists at ImageNet scale. The main empirical results rely on 16×16 patches and MNIST, with larger-dataset evidence deferred to appendices, leaving the practical ceiling of the approach somewhat untested.
