## Problem

Existing discrete diffusion language models suffer from semantically empty noise: masked diffusion uses a single mask token with no meaning and cannot self-correct decoded tokens, while uniform diffusion introduces random tokens that are semantically inconsistent with clean tokens. This limits the progressive refinement and arbitrary-order generation that make diffusion attractive over autoregressive models. The paper asks whether introducing intermediate semantic levels between clean words and the mask can resolve these limitations while preserving the flexibility of the diffusion framework.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a fixed-length sequence of 512 tokens (GPT-2 tokenizer) where, at any denoising timestep, each position independently occupies one of three hierarchy levels: a specific word token, a cluster token (coarse-grained semantic group), or the mask token. The DiT-based network (170M or 425M parameters) outputs a word-level probability distribution for every position at every step, from which cluster-level predictions are derived via the Bayesian posterior Γx_θ rather than a separate head. Generation proceeds over 512 denoising steps, progressively resolving all positions to their final word tokens.

## Method

HDLM introduces a hierarchical vocabulary (word → cluster → mask) with a surjective mapping Γ, and formulates the forward process as a CTMC where each token independently transitions to its higher-level ancestor according to a time-varying scheduler, so that different tokens in a sentence can occupy different hierarchy levels at the same timestep. The reverse process performs 'next semantic scale prediction': the model predicts word tokens from cluster tokens (restricted within-cluster via force transition) and cluster tokens from mask tokens, enabling progressive self-correction. The paper derives a closed-form continuous-time ELBO that decomposes into two weighted cross-entropy losses—a token-level loss within the known cluster and a cluster-level loss—showing that MDLM is the special case of a single cluster. A stochastic perturbation mechanism (ξ<1) corrupts cluster tokens during training to improve robustness to inaccurate contexts, and the framework generalizes to arbitrary numbers of intermediate hierarchies.

## Results

On OpenWebText, HDLM-small-64 (170M params, 131B tokens) achieves a validation perplexity of 23.36 and generative perplexity of 144.2, outperforming all discrete diffusion baselines. The larger HDLM-base-128 (425M params) reaches a validation perplexity of 19.22 and generative perplexity of 139.9, surpassing GPT-2 (23.40) and approaching Llama-1 110M (16.11, trained on 2× more tokens). Across all model sizes, HDLM consistently achieves the lowest generative perplexity among discrete diffusion methods, with the base model reducing Gen. PPL by ~15% over MDLM-small.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDLM-small-64 outperforms MDLM-small on both validation perplexity (23.36 vs. 27.39) and generative perplexity (144.2 vs. 163.7), a ~15% reduction in Gen. PPL. Against GIDD⁺-small, HDLM-small-64 also wins on both metrics (23.36 vs. 25.82 Valid. PPL; 144.2 vs. 170.2 Gen. PPL), showing that the hierarchical semantic structure is more effective than GIDD's uniform-noise mixing for self-correction. Compared to SEDD (≤24.10 Valid. PPL, no Gen. PPL reported), HDLM-small-64 achieves a lower validation perplexity at the same 131B-token budget. At the base scale, HDLM-base-128 (19.22 Valid. PPL) surpasses GPT-2 (23.40) by ~4.2 points, though it remains behind Llama-1 110M (16.11), which was trained on 262B tokens—twice the data. The paper also verifies that HDLM with n=1 cluster recovers MDLM's performance (25.72 vs. 27.39 reported), confirming the theoretical special-case relationship.

## Relation to Prior Work

HDLM extends the discrete diffusion language modeling line (D3PM, SEDD, MDLM, MD4) by replacing the binary word-to-mask transition with a hierarchical word-to-cluster-to-mask process, drawing the 'next-scale prediction' concept from visual autoregressive models (VAR). It subsumes GIDD's unified masked-plus-uniform framework by showing that the mixing distribution π_t can be data-dependent (a function of the clean token's cluster), yielding fundamentally different CTMC properties. The paper formally proves MDLM is the n=1-cluster special case, positioning HDLM as a strict generalization that adds design space (number of clusters, perturbation probability, schedule shape) while preserving compatibility with prior training and sampling procedures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical formulation addresses two core weaknesses of masked diffusion (no self-correction, semantically empty mask tokens) and uniform diffusion (semantic inconsistency between noise and signal), offering a principled path toward diffusion language models that can revise earlier decisions. The stochastic perturbation trick (ξ<1) is practically impactful—reducing generative PPL by over 60%—but comes at the cost of worse validation PPL, raising open questions about the validation-vs-generation tradeoff and whether the effect persists at larger scale. Experimental validation is limited to 170M–425M parameters on OpenWebText with a single intermediate hierarchy; scaling to multi-hierarchy structures, larger models, and other domains (image, protein) remains future work.
