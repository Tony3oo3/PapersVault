## Problem

Spectral decomposition methods for learning POMDPs have been limited to either non-adaptive policies (uniform, round-robin) or a restricted class of adaptive memoryless policies where all samples must come from a single stochastic policy with a minimum action probability. This creates a data-inefficiency bottleneck: samples collected under older or different policies cannot be reused for model estimation. The paper addresses the open question of whether spectral techniques can be applied to samples collected from multiple adaptive belief-based policies simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observation-action trajectories (o_t, a_t) collected under potentially different belief-based policies π_l, along with a known reward function r: O → [0,1]. It operates in the undercomplete regime (S ≤ O) with discrete observation and action spaces. The output consists of an estimated observation matrix Ô ∈ R^{O×S} and per-action transition matrices {T̂_a} ∈ R^{S×S}, which together define an estimated POMDP model used to compute the optimal belief-based policy via an optimization oracle. A key structural constraint is that the third view matrix V_3 = O T_a^T is policy-independent, which is what makes cross-policy sample combination valid.

## Method

The core contribution is Mixed Spectral Estimation, which constructs weighted covariance matrices K_{ν,ν'}^{(a,L)} by averaging per-policy covariances weighted by the number of samples n_l^{(a)} for each action a across L trajectories. The key insight (Theorem 5.3) is that when views are modified using these mixture covariance matrices, the resulting second- and third-order moments still admit a spectral decomposition whose columns correspond to the policy-independent third view matrix V_3^{(a)} = O T_a^T, with only the mixing weights ω^{(a,L)} changing. This allows tensor decomposition to recover the same model parameters regardless of which policies generated the data. Building on this, Mixed Spectral UCRL (Algorithm 2) uses an optimistic UCRL structure where each episode re-estimates the model from all accumulated trajectories, constructs a confidence set, and selects the most optimistic policy. A novel episode-stopping rule terminates an episode when any action's within-episode count exceeds its total historical count, ensuring sufficient per-action samples without requiring a fixed exploration schedule. Under one-step reachability, the algorithm achieves Õ(√T) regret against the optimal belief-based policy, improving over the prior Õ(T^{2/3}) bound.

## Results

The Mixed Spectral UCRL algorithm achieves a regret bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})$ against the optimal belief-based policy, improving over the state-of-the-art $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{2/3})$ of SEEU. The estimation procedure (Theorem 5.4) provides finite-sample guarantees with error scaling as $1/\sqrt{N_L}$ for the observation model and $1/\sqrt{N_L^{(a)}}$ for each transition matrix. Numerical experiments on POMDPs with S=4, A=3, O=4 confirm that Mixed Spectral UCRL achieves lower cumulative regret than both SEEU and SM-UCRL across the full horizon, with the gap widening over time consistent with the theoretical rate improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior spectral-based POMDP learning algorithms. Against SM-UCRL [Azizzadenesheli et al., COLT 2016], Mixed Spectral UCRL wins decisively: SM-UCRL requires stochastic policies with a minimum action probability $\iota > 0$, does not reuse samples across episodes, and only competes against the weaker class of memoryless policies, yielding an $\mathcal{O}(T)$ regret bound. Against SEEU [Xiong et al., 2021], Mixed Spectral UCRL improves the regret rate from $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{2/3})$ to $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})$ by eliminating the need for separate exploration and exploitation phases and by reusing all collected samples (including those from exploitation) in the spectral estimation. In the numerical regret experiment (Figure 2, S=4, A=3, O=4), Mixed Spectral UCRL consistently achieves lower cumulative regret than both baselines, with the advantage over SEEU growing with horizon length, matching the predicted $\sqrt{T}$ vs. $T^{2/3}$ scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral decomposition (SD) framework for POMDP learning, building directly on the tensor decomposition techniques of Anandkumar et al. (2014) and the adaptive-policy extension of Azizzadenesheli et al. (2016, SM-UCRL). The key departure is from both SM-UCRL (which requires all samples from a single stochastic policy with minimum action probability ι>0) and SEEU (which alternates between exploration and exploitation phases, discarding exploitation-phase data). By proving that the third view matrix is policy-independent, the authors show that weighted covariance matrices across multiple belief-based policies still admit a valid spectral decomposition, enabling sample reuse that neither prior approach permits. The optimistic UCRL framework of Jaksch et al. (2010) provides the algorithmic template for converting the estimation procedure into a regret-minimization algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to achieve Õ(√T) regret against the optimal belief-based policy in infinite-horizon average-reward POMDPs, improving over the Õ(T^{2/3}) bound of SEEU and removing the need for stochastic exploration policies. The data-efficiency gain—reusing samples across episodes and policies—has practical implications for settings where interaction is expensive. However, the result relies on the one-step reachability assumption (all transition matrix entries bounded below by ε>0) and assumes access to an optimization oracle for the belief MDP, both of which limit immediate applicability. The authors explicitly flag relaxing Assumption 6.1 as a key future direction, and the computational intractability of solving the belief MDP Bellman equation remains an orthogonal challenge.
