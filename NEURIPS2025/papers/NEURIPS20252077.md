## Problem

Existing LVLM-based geo-localization methods face two persistent gaps: reasoning-oriented training data is dominated by street-view imagery with limited scene diversity and fixed viewpoints, and supervised fine-tuning encourages pattern replication rather than grounded visual-geographic reasoning. This paper addresses both by constructing a diverse, reasoning-annotated dataset from social media images and replacing SFT with reinforcement learning that explicitly rewards visual grounding and localizability assessment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252077/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input, prompted with a standardized geolocation template that instructs the model to analyze visual cues (architecture, signage, vegetation, terrain) and reason step-by-step. Output is structured into a chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory enclosed in think tags followed by an answer block containing discrete country and city names; these place names are geocoded via Microsoft Azure Maps to representative GPS coordinates for distance-threshold evaluation (1 km through 2500 km). The model also implicitly produces a localizability judgment as part of its reasoning, which feeds into the reward signal during training.

## Method

GLOBE is built on a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone and trained via a two-stage pipeline. First, MP16-Reason (33K training / 12K test samples) is curated from the MP-16 social-media dataset by distilling localizability judgments, reasoning trajectories, and geolocation predictions from three strong VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, InternVL3-78B, GeoCLIP), then filtering through multi-dimensional verification: localizability thresholding, cross-model self-verification for consistency, and semantic-segmentation-based entity grounding to ensure reasoning references actual visual elements. Second, the model is fine-tuned with GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) using a composite reward of three task-specific signals: a localizability reward (LLM-based classifier on image-reasoning pairs), a visual grounding consistency reward (fraction of reasoning entities matched to segmented visual elements), and a hierarchical geo-localization accuracy reward (country-then-city with partial credit). Group-normalized advantages within each prompt's candidate set guide policy updates, while a KL penalty constrains divergence from the reference policy, enabling the model to develop structured, visually grounded reasoning rather than memorizing coordinate patterns.

## Results

On MP16-Reason-Test, GLOBE-7B achieves 62.85% at City (25 km), 86.68% at Country (750 km), and 92.52% at Continent (2500 km), outperforming all open-source LVLMs including Qwen2.5-VL-72B (59.30% City) and InternVL3-78B (52.46% City) using only 33K training samples. On the public IM2GPS3K benchmark, GLOBE reaches 40.18% City and 71.45% Country, surpassing Qwen2.5-VL-72B (35.77% City) and GeoReasoner-7B (26.94% City). GLOBE trails closed-source systems GPT-4.1 (66.76% City) and Doubao1.5-VL (64.02% City) on MP16-Reason-Test, but remains the strongest open-source method across all evaluated benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLOBE-7B outperforms all open-source LVLMs on MP16-Reason-Test, beating its own backbone Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +10.13 pp at City (62.85 vs 52.72) and the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B by +3.55 pp (62.85 vs 59.30), demonstrating that GRPO-based reasoning training extracts and refines knowledge beyond what the teacher model provides. Against task-specific reasoning baselines, GLOBE dominates GeoReasoner-7B by +22.41 pp at City (62.85 vs 40.44) and +18.67 pp at Country (86.68 vs 68.01), attributed to scene diversity in MP16-Reason versus street-view-only training data. On IM2GPS3K, GLOBE surpasses GeoReasoner-7B by +13.24 pp at City (40.18 vs 26.94) and GaGA by +7.18 pp (40.18 vs 33.00). GLOBE remains behind closed-source GPT-4.1 (−3.91 pp City) and Doubao1.5-VL (−1.17 pp City) on MP16-Reason-Test, a gap the authors attribute to undisclosed training scale. Notably, GLOBE achieves these results with only 33K curated samples, compared to 4M+ for image-only methods like GeoCLIP and 48M for RFM-YFCC, highlighting the data efficiency of reasoning-driven supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geo-localization literature from classification/retrieval paradigms to a reasoning-driven LVLM formulation, building directly on GRPO (originally proposed for mathematical reasoning in DeepSeekMath) as the training algorithm. It departs from prior LVLM-based geo-localization work (e.g., GeoReasoner, G3, GaGA) that relied on supervised fine-tuning or street-view-centric reasoning datasets, instead introducing multi-model knowledge distillation with cross-model verification for data curation and task-specific reward signals (localizability, visual grounding, geo-accuracy) for RL-based fine-tuning. The multi-dimensional verification pipeline—combining localizability filtering, self-verification across VLMs, and semantic-segmentation-based visual grounding checks—repurposes ideas from hallucination-mitigation literature into a geo-localization-specific data quality framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 33K high-quality reasoning-annotated samples can match or exceed models trained on millions of image-coordinate pairs, suggesting a data-efficient path for LVLM adaptation to spatial reasoning tasks. The GRPO-based training paradigm, with its structured multi-reward design, is potentially generalizable to other multimodal reasoning tasks beyond geo-localization. However, a key open question remains: reasoning-driven approaches fundamentally lack the spatial precision for fine-grained (street-level or coordinate-level) localization, pointing toward hybrid architectures that combine semantic reasoning for region constraint with local feature retrieval for pinpoint accuracy. The paper also raises important privacy concerns about inferring precise locations from user-shared images at scale.
