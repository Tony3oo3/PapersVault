## Problem

Two-stage image generation involves a fundamental trade-off: compressing images more aggressively in the tokenizer (stage 1) makes the latent distribution easier for the generative model (stage 2) to learn, but worsens reconstruction quality. The paper asks whether and when 'worse' reconstruction is actually 'better' for generation, and how this trade-off interacts with model scale and training compute. Prior work had not systematically characterized this rate-distortion-usefulness trade-off through the lens of scaling laws.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a class label (ImageNet, 1000 classes) or a text prompt (BLIP-3o, 27M image-text pairs) as conditioning input and produces a 256×256 RGB image. The image is first encoded into 256 discrete VQ tokens (a 16×16 spatial grid from a 16× downsample factor) over a 16k-entry codebook, then decoded by a ResNet-based VQGAN decoder. The stage 2 autoregressive model is a Llama 2 transformer (52M–775M parameters) that predicts tokens in raster-scan order, with classifier-free guidance at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is Causally Regularized Tokenization (CRT), which adds an L2 next-token prediction loss—computed by a small 2-layer causal transformer on the pre-quantized encoder latents—directly into the VQGAN training objective. This loss is backpropagated through the encoder, embedding an autoregressive inductive bias into the token representations so that each token becomes more predictable given all preceding tokens, without changing the compression rate (bits per pixel). The authors specifically use L2 on pre-quantized latents rather than cross-entropy on quantized tokens, because L2 is similarity-aware and avoids the discrete jumps that damage reconstruction fidelity. The causal regularizer uses the same architecture as the stage 2 model (minus embedding and classification layers) to ensure inductive bias transfer, and is trained with a separate AdamW optimizer at a weight of λ=4.0. This single intervention improves stage 2 compute scaling by 2–3×, enabling a 775M-parameter model with 256 tokens/image to match LlamaGen-3B (3.1B parameters, 576 tokens/image) at 2.18 FID—an 8× reduction in inference compute.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, the optimized CRT tokenizer (CRT_opt) with a 775M autoregressive model achieves 2.18 gFID using only 256 tokens per image, matching LlamaGen-3B (3.1B params, 576 tokens/image) with 4× fewer parameters and half the tokens (8× less inference compute). The unoptimized CRT variant achieves 2.35 gFID at the same scale. Across compute scaling, CRT improves the scaling law slope from α=−0.65 to α=−0.73, yielding 1.5–3× compute efficiency gains over the VQGAN baseline at all model scales from 111M to 775M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The headline result is that CRT_opt-AR-775M (2.18 FID, 256 tokens) exactly matches LlamaGen-3B (2.18 FID, 576 tokens, 3.1B params) while using 4× fewer parameters and half the tokens per image, an 8× reduction in inference compute. At the same 775M parameter scale, CRT_opt (2.18 FID) substantially outperforms LlamaGen-XL with 576 tokens (2.62 FID) and LlamaGen-XL with 256 tokens (3.39 FID). Against diffusion baselines, CRT_opt beats DiT-XL/2 (2.27 FID, 675M) but trails MAR-H (1.55 FID, 943M, no VQ) and VAR-d24 (2.09 FID, 1.0B, 680 tokens). In compute-controlled comparisons (Table 1b), CRT uniformly outperforms the tuned VQGAN baseline at every model scale from 111M to 775M, with the gap widening at smaller scales (e.g., 4.34 vs 4.90 gFID at 111M). On LSUN Horses, CRT achieves a dramatic 2.56 gFID at 111M versus 4.98 for the baseline, though on LSUN Bedrooms the advantage is smaller (2.57 vs 2.62 at 111M). On the BLIP-3o text-to-image benchmark, CRT improves gFID from 4.61 to 4.15 and CLIPScore from 0.33 to 0.36 over the baseline tokenizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VQGAN tokenizer (Esser et al., 2021) and the LlamaGen autoregressive generation pipeline (Sun et al., 2024), introducing Causally Regularized Tokenization (CRT) as a minimal intervention: a 2-layer causal transformer loss applied to pre-quantized latents during stage 1 training. It departs from the '1D tokenizer' line of work (VAR, VQVAE-2, SEED) that modifies tokenizer architecture to capture global semantics, instead keeping the standard VQGAN architecture and embedding the autoregressive inductive bias through a simple regularization term. The paper also extends the rate-distortion-usefulness trade-off framework from Tschannen et al. (2018) by rigorously connecting it to compute scaling laws across five orders of magnitude of training FLOPs, going beyond the qualitative observations in prior work that larger codebooks or more aggressive compression can hurt stage 2 performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–3× compute efficiency gain from CRT and the ability to match LlamaGen-3B (2.18 FID) with 775M parameters and 256 tokens per image (vs. 3.1B and 576) represent a practically significant reduction in both training and inference cost for autoregressive image generation. The systematic scaling-law framework provides a principled way to choose tokenizer compression rate based on available generation model capacity, which is broadly applicable to any two-stage generation pipeline. Key limitations include the restriction to autoregressive (not diffusion) generation, a maximum model size of 775M parameters, and evaluation primarily on class-conditional ImageNet; open questions include whether CRT generalizes to diffusion-based decoders, video tokenization, and whether the gains persist at much larger scales where data becomes the bottleneck rather than compute.
