## Problem

Existing structured layer pruning methods for LLMs suffer from three key limitations: direct layer removal discards meaningful intermediate feature mappings (no layer is truly redundant), linear weight aggregation in feed-forward networks causes over-smoothing because channel-wise alignment does not hold across FFN layers, and post-training recovery mechanisms fail to exploit the structural correspondences created during pruning. The paper asks whether a pruning strategy can preserve the mapping capacity of removed layers while enabling efficient knowledge transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250235/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-2-7b with 32 transformer blocks) and a small calibration set (as few as 128–256 samples from WikiText-2 or C4) as input, and produces a compressed model at a target sparsity (10%, 20%, or 30%) along with an explicit layer-correspondence mapping P = [{a₁,b₁}, …, {a_N,b_N}] linking each fused layer in the pruned model to its original layer group. The merged layers maintain the same hidden-state dimension as the original module via constrained concatenation, and the calibration data is consumed only during the pruning phase for channel importance scoring and SBI computation, not during post-training.

## Method

CoMe operates in two phases. In the pruning phase, a channel sensitivity metric scores each weight channel by the expected ℓ₁-norm of its output perturbation (|x_i|·Σ|w_{i,k}|), and a Skip-Block Influence (SBI) score identifies adjacent layer groups that minimally alter hidden features. Within each group, the top-k channels from every layer are concatenated column-wise (rather than linearly summed) to form a compact fusion layer, with the per-layer parameter preservation ratio allocated proportionally to BI scores raised to a skewness power p. In the post-training phase, a hierarchical distillation protocol exploits the layer-correspondence mapping P to align teacher and student hidden features via symmetric KL divergence, either sequentially (CoMe-mp, one merged layer per iteration with decaying learning rates) or jointly (CoMe-sp, all merged layers optimized simultaneously), enabling efficient recovery of the mapping capacities lost during pruning.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7b with 30% parameter pruning, CoMe-sp (with hierarchical distillation post-training) achieves 52.58% average accuracy across seven NLP benchmarks, retaining 82.98% of the dense model's 62.11% average accuracy. After post-training, CoMe-sp surpasses LLM-Streamline by 2.4% in average accuracy and achieves lower perplexity (11.45 C4 / 8.54 Wiki-2 vs. 18.60 / 19.75). On Qwen-3-4b at 30.1% sparsity, CoMe-sp reaches 47.22% average accuracy (71.30% retained), outperforming LLM-Streamline by 5.2%. The concatenation-based merge alone (without post-training) improves average accuracy by over 2% and reduces perplexity by over 4.7 compared to linear weight aggregation methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMe consistently outperforms all eight baselines across the seven NLP benchmarks. Among DLP methods (Mag+, Taylor+, ShortGPT, SLEB), CoMe achieves the highest average accuracy at every sparsity level; for instance, on LLaMA-2-13b at 30% sparsity, CoMe surpasses all DLP methods by at least 4% in average accuracy. Among WSLP methods, CoMe's concatenation-based merge improves average accuracy by over 2% and reduces perplexity by over 4.7 compared to FuseGPT-MI+F, MKA, and LaCo; notably, removing the concatenation step from CoMe drops accuracy by 2.12% and increases PPL by 4.73, confirming the merge's critical role. Against the strongest post-training baseline LLM-Streamline, CoMe-sp wins by 2.4% average accuracy on LLaMA-2-7b (52.58 vs. 50.13) and by 5.2% on Qwen-3-4b (47.22 vs. 41.96), while also achieving substantially lower perplexity (11.45 vs. 18.60 on C4). CoMe-mp (sequential layer-wise distillation) performs comparably to LLM-Streamline, indicating that joint multi-layer optimization (CoMe-sp) is essential for effective recovery. On Qwen-3-4b, LLM-Streamline suffers severe perplexity degradation (200.93 C4) after pruning, whereas CoMe maintains 56.13, highlighting CoMe's robustness across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMe extends the structured layer pruning line of work by building on ShortGPT's Block Influence (BI) score, generalizing it to a Skip-BI metric for multi-layer groups, and departing fundamentally from the Weighted Sum-based Layer Pruning (WSLP) paradigm used by MKA, LaCo, and FuseGPT. The key conceptual departure is replacing linear weight aggregation with channel-level concatenation, motivated by the observation that FFN modules lack the channel-wise alignment that residual connections provide for MHA and normalization layers. For post-training, CoMe introduces a hierarchical distillation protocol that exploits the layer correspondences established during pruning, contrasting with LLM-Streamline's single-layer mapping and global-alignment methods like MiniLLM and DistiLLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that linear weight aggregation in FFN modules consistently worsens perplexity compared to simply retaining one layer challenges a core assumption underlying several popular WSLP methods, suggesting the field may have been applying an inappropriate fusion strategy to a significant portion of transformer parameters. Practically, CoMe's 83% accuracy retention at 30% sparsity on LLaMA-2-7b offers a viable path for deploying LLMs in resource-constrained settings. However, the method's reliance on a heuristic parameter preservation ratio (p) and model-specific minimum retention thresholds (ρ) limits its plug-and-play applicability, and the authors acknowledge that adaptive, architecture-aware merging remains an open problem. Extending the approach to MoE expert merging and multi-layer simultaneous fusion are identified as future directions.
