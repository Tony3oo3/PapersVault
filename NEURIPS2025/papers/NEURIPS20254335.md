## Problem

Supervised optical flow models are trained on synthetic datasets with narrow motion statistics, and self-supervised methods rely on photometric or smoothness heuristics that fail under complex real-world dynamics like non-rigid deformation, motion blur, and textureless regions. This paper asks whether frozen, self-supervised generative video models can be prompted zero-shot—without any flow-specific fine-tuning—to extract accurate optical flow, and systematically identifies which model architectural properties make this possible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254335/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB video frames (F1, F2) and a query point location in F1, and produces the 2D displacement vector of that point in F2 along with an occlusion flag. Frame 2 is provided only as a sparse set of masked patches (not the full frame), and the query point is encoded by injecting a small white Gaussian bump (σ=2.0, amplitude 255) at its location in F1. The model is run twice—once clean and once perturbed—and the flow is read out from the difference between the two prediction distributions, so each query point requires two forward passes through the frozen model.

## Method

KL-tracing is a test-time inference procedure built on the LRAS autoregressive video model, which uniquely combines three properties: distributional (non-blurry) prediction, a local tokenizer that encodes each spatio-temporal patch independently, and random-access decoding that allows conditioning on any arbitrary subset of target-frame patches. At test time, a small Gaussian perturbation is injected at the query point in frame 1, and the frozen model is rolled out once conditioned on frame 1 and masked patches of frame 2, both with and without the perturbation. Rather than computing the noisy RGB difference between the two sampled predictions, KL-tracing computes the patch-wise Kullback-Leibler divergence between the clean and perturbed prediction logits, which bypasses sampling randomness and yields a crisp divergence map that peaks at the perturbation's destination. An arg-max over this map gives the flow vector, and a threshold on the KL value flags occlusion. The paper also systematically demonstrates why deterministic models (CWM), global-latent diffusion models (SVD), and raster-order autoregressive models (Cosmos) all fail at this task, isolating the three architectural properties above as necessary for precise zero-shot flow extraction.

## Results

On the real-world TAP-Vid DAVIS benchmark, LRAS with KL-tracing (zero-shot, no flow-specific training) achieves the best Average Distance (AD = 11.18 px), best <δx_avg (65.20%), best Average Jaccard (AJ = 44.16%), and best Occlusion Accuracy (OA = 74.58%), outperforming all supervised (RAFT, SEA-RAFT) and unsupervised (Doduo, SMURF, CWM) baselines. On the synthetic TAP-Vid Kubric benchmark, it achieves the best AD (5.06 px) but trails SEA-RAFT on AJ (65.49 vs. 75.06) and <δx_avg (81.66 vs. 84.63), indicating that the advantage is most pronounced on challenging real-world dynamics. In Table 2, KL-tracing with LRAS reduces endpoint error from 8.48 (RGB difference) to 5.08, and dramatically outperforms SVD (74.80) and Cosmos (35.43–66.55) on the DAVIS subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DAVIS (real-world), LRAS with KL-tracing dominates all baselines: it halves the endpoint error of SEA-RAFT (11.18 vs. 20.18 px) and RAFT (11.18 vs. 25.33 px), and improves <δx_avg by 6.5 points over SEA-RAFT (65.20 vs. 58.69) and 10.8 points over RAFT (65.20 vs. 54.37). Against unsupervised methods, it substantially outperforms SMURF (AD 11.18 vs. 27.28; <δx_avg 65.20 vs. 44.18), Doduo (AD 11.18 vs. 13.41; <δx_avg 65.20 vs. 48.50), CWM (AD 11.18 vs. 23.53; <δx_avg 65.20 vs. 26.30), and DiffTrack (<δx_avg 65.20 vs. 46.90). On Kubric (synthetic), the picture is more mixed: SEA-RAFT leads on AJ (75.06 vs. 65.49) and <δx_avg (84.63 vs. 81.66), while LRAS+KL-tracing achieves the lowest AD (5.06 vs. 6.54 for SEA-RAFT, 5.60 for RAFT). In the two-frame multi-frame tracker comparison (Table 5), LRAS+KL-tracing (AJ 44.2, <δx_avg 65.2) beats CoTracker-v3 (AJ 39.9, <δx_avg 58.0) but trails AllTracker (AJ 54.9, <δx_avg 66.0), which benefits from global multi-frame refinement. The key takeaway is that a completely training-free, zero-shot procedure matches or exceeds supervised flow models on the most challenging real-world benchmark, while remaining competitive on synthetic data where supervised methods have a training-data advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Counterfactual World Model (CWM) perturb-and-track paradigm—originally designed for deterministic next-frame predictors—to generative video models, and introduces KL-tracing, a test-time procedure that computes flow from logit-space KL divergence rather than RGB differences. It systematically departs from prior work on extracting visual intermediates (depth, illumination) from video generators, which required supervised fine-tuning, by demonstrating that zero-shot prompting is viable when the base model has three specific architectural properties: distributional prediction, local tokenization, and random-access decoding. The paper positions LRAS as the first architecture satisfying all three, and contrasts it against CWM (blurry deterministic outputs), Stable Video Diffusion (coarse global latent conditioning), and Cosmos (raster-order-only decoding), each of which fails for a distinct architectural reason.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a completely training-free, zero-shot procedure can match or beat supervised flow methods (RAFT, SEA-RAFT) on real-world DAVIS and synthetic Kubric benchmarks challenges the assumption that optical flow requires task-specific training data. The three-property framework (distributional prediction, local tokenization, random-access decoding) provides a concrete design checklist for future video models to become visually promptable. Key limitations include the method's speed (point-by-point inference is much slower than dense flow networks), its dependence on a single architecture (LRAS), and evaluation limited to two-frame flow rather than dense or long-horizon tracking. Open questions include whether the properties can be retrofitted into other architectures, whether KL-tracing extends to other visual intermediates (segments, material properties), and whether distilling sparse KL-tracing labels into faster networks can close the efficiency gap.
