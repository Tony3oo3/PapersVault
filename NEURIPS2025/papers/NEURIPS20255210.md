## Problem

Spatio-temporal forecasting models suffer performance degradation under non-stationary distribution shifts (e.g., sensor aging, seasonal pattern changes) during real-world deployment. Existing remedies—OOD-robust architectures, data augmentation, and continual fine-tuning—require complex training-stage modifications or period-specific labeled data, making them computationally expensive and inapplicable in data-scarce streaming settings. The paper asks: can we achieve effective, lightweight, and timely adaptation purely at test time without altering the backbone or requiring additional training data?

## Input / Output

At each streaming time step t, the method receives a sliding-window spatio-temporal tensor X_t ∈ R^{N×T^h×C} (plus an optional spatial graph) and produces a calibrated prediction ŷ_t^cal ∈ R^{N×T^f×C}. A key operational detail is that true labels for test samples become available only after a delay of T^h + T^f − 1 steps due to label autocorrelation in STF; the method exploits this delayed label availability through a FIFO queue of size T^f, ensuring the calibrator is updated only on samples whose ground truth is already known, thereby avoiding information leakage while respecting the sliding-window stride latency constraint.

## Method

ST-TTC appends a lightweight Spectral Domain Calibrator (SD-Calibrator) after a frozen pre-trained backbone. The calibrator applies a per-node rFFT along the time dimension, decomposes the spectrum into amplitude and phase, and applies group-wise per-node modulation offsets (λ^α, λ^φ ∈ R^{G×N×1}, initialized to zero) to correct periodic structural biases, then reconstructs the time-domain signal via irFFT—requiring only 2NG parameters versus 2NM for full-spectrum parameterization. For test-time learning, a streaming memory queue (FIFO, size T^f) stores recent input-label pairs; when the queue overflows, the oldest dequeued sample triggers a single gradient descent step (flash update) on the calibrator parameters only, with the backbone frozen. This single-sample, single-step update is provably controlled (bounded parameter change ηG_max) and avoids overfitting to transient noise, while the spectral-domain formulation provides a theoretical perturbation bound ensuring calibrated outputs remain close to the original prediction.

## Results

ST-TTC consistently improves all six backbone models (STAEformer, STTN, GWNet, STGCN, STID, ST-Norm) across six datasets, with relative MAE reductions ranging from 0.3% to 2.4% (e.g., GWNet on PEMS-03: 16.73→16.42, −1.85%). On the METR-LA benchmark in the online setting (20/5/75 split), ST-TTC achieves MAE 3.77±0.07 and RMSE 7.75±0.13, outperforming the strongest baseline DOST (MAE 4.38, RMSE 8.26) by 14% and 6% respectively. In the continual learning setting, ST-TTC with EAC yields up to 32.6% MAE reduction on Energy-Stream (5.15→3.47). The method adds at most 3.82 minutes of inference time on LargeST while requiring only 2NG learnable parameters (G=4 groups).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the regular setting on METR-LA, ST-TTC (MAE 3.46, RMSE 7.21) ties CompFormer on MAE and slightly trails on RMSE (7.21 vs 7.19), but does so without requiring additional training data or backbone modification, whereas CompFormer uses extra training information. ST-TTC clearly beats TTT-MAE (MAE 3.47, RMSE 7.43) and TENT (MAE 4.84, RMSE 8.53) in the regular setting. In the more challenging online setting (20/5/75 split), ST-TTC dominates all baselines: it outperforms DOST by 14% in MAE (3.77 vs 4.38) and 6% in RMSE (7.75 vs 8.26), beats OneNet by 24% in MAE (3.77 vs 4.94), OnlineTCN by 21% (3.77 vs 4.78), and FSNet by 35% (3.77 vs 5.79). All online baselines require backbone architecture modifications (marked ✗), while ST-TTC remains a plug-and-play module. In the continual learning setting (Table 4), ST-TTC with EAC achieves up to 32.6% MAE reduction on Energy-Stream, and STKEC+ST-TTC narrows the gap to EAC, confirming that the calibration module is complementary to existing training paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new paradigm called 'test-time computing' (ST-TTC) that sits between test-time training (TTT-ST) and online continual learning (DOST) for spatio-temporal forecasting. It departs from TTT-ST by freezing the backbone entirely and instead appending a lightweight spectral-domain calibrator (SD-Calibrator) that performs phase-amplitude modulation via rFFT, rather than attaching self-supervised heads to the network. It also diverges from DOST and CompFormer by requiring no architectural modifications and no access to additional training data, relying solely on a streaming memory queue of historical test labels. Conceptually, the method repurposes Fourier-domain calibration (related to FITS, FourierGNN, PastNet) not as a feature extractor but as a post-hoc correction module updated via single-step gradient descent, formalizing a 'calibrate rather than retrain' philosophy for test-time adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering a plug-and-play, architecture-agnostic calibration layer that meets the strict timeliness constraints of real-world spatio-temporal deployment (inference overhead < sliding-window stride), which prior test-time adaptation methods fail to satisfy. The consistent 1–3% gains across six backbone families, six domains, and scales up to 8,600 nodes demonstrate broad applicability, while the 32.6% MAE reduction on Energy-Stream highlights potential under severe distribution shift. Open questions include whether the spectral calibration generalizes to non-periodic or aperiodic spatio-temporal signals, whether the single-step flash update remains sufficient under rapidly evolving (non-gradual) shifts, and how the approach extends to foundation-model-scale spatio-temporal architectures where internal test-time compute might be more impactful than output calibration.
