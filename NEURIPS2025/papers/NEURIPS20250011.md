## Problem

Fast adversarial training (FAT) methods that use data filtering operate at the batch level, treating all samples within a selected batch identically. This ignores the fact that individual samples have different distances to the decision boundary and thus different vulnerability to adversarial attacks, causing vulnerable samples in 'less representative' batches to be skipped while non-vulnerable samples in 'more representative' batches are learned, limiting both efficiency and robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250011/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images with class labels (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and produces a trained CNN (ResNet or WideResNet) that outputs class predictions. Unusually, each input sample is first perturbed via FGSM to generate an adversarial counterpart, and the pair is scored for vulnerability before being probabilistically assigned to either the natural-training or adversarial-training subset, so the effective training set composition changes at every filtering interval T during training.

## Method

VDAT introduces two core components. First, a margin-based vulnerability calculation measures each sample's vulnerability as the negative absolute difference between soft margins (log-sum-exp over all non-target classes) of the natural image and its FGSM-perturbed adversarial example, capturing decision-boundary shift information while remaining robust to targeted attacks. Second, a vulnerability-aware data filtering step normalizes these vulnerability scores into per-sample probabilities that determine whether each sample is converted to an adversarial example for training; more vulnerable samples receive higher probability of adversarial treatment. The overall training loss combines a natural loss on the remaining samples and an adversarial loss on the filtered adversarial subset, with filtering re-applied every T epochs to adapt to the evolving decision boundary. This sample-wise, probability-based filtering replaces the batch-wise selection used by prior data-filtering FAT methods, achieving up to 76% efficiency gains while improving both natural and adversarial accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WideResNet34-10, VDAT_FGSM achieves 92.34% natural accuracy and 54.49% AutoAttack accuracy in only 3.41 GPU hours, compared to 85.17%/51.67% in 31.90 GPU hours for standard PGD-AT. On CIFAR-100 with the same model, VDAT_FGSM reaches 72.91% natural and 31.65% AA accuracy in 3.44 GPU hours. On ImageNet-1K with ResNet50, VDAT_FGSM attains 67.47% natural and 38.69% PGD-50 accuracy in 38.41 GPU hours, outperforming all FGSM-based FAT baselines by at least 9.6% in efficiency while achieving the highest accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDAT_FGSM outperforms all FGSM-based FAT baselines (FGSM-SDI, FGSM-PGK, TDAT) on both natural and adversarial accuracy across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-1K, while reducing training cost by 59–93% compared to standard PGD-AT. On WideResNet34-10/CIFAR-10, VDAT_FGSM achieves 92.34% natural accuracy (vs. 86.40% for FGSM-SDI and 83.32% for FGSM-PGK) and 54.49% AA accuracy (vs. 51.17% and 53.28%), at only 3.41 GPU hours (vs. 9.40 and 8.30). Against data-filtering-based FAT methods, VDAT_FGSM surpasses DFEAT by 6.68% natural and 1.72% AA accuracy on CIFAR-10 while using 67% less compute, and outperforms AdvGradMatch by 7.80% natural and 6.66% AA accuracy with 80% less cost. VDAT_PGD achieves the best PGD-50 accuracy (60.26% on WRN34-10/CIFAR-10) but at higher cost than VDAT_FGSM, and VDAT_FGSM generally wins on natural accuracy and weaker attacks while VDAT_PGD wins on stronger attacks (C&W, AA) due to stronger adversarial examples. In the robust NAS setting, VDAT_FGSM consistently achieves the lowest search cost (e.g., 0.34 GPU days vs. 0.80 for vanilla DSRNA on CIFAR-10) while improving both natural and adversarial accuracy of the derived architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fast adversarial training (FAT) literature by shifting the data filtering paradigm from batch-wise to sample-wise selection. It builds directly on the soft-margin decision boundary formulation from Xu et al. (ICLR 2023) to define a per-sample vulnerability metric, and departs from prior batch-level filtering methods (AdvGradMatch, DFEAT) that treat all samples within a selected batch identically. The vulnerability-aware probabilistic filtering mechanism is a novel recombination of margin-based vulnerability scoring with stochastic sample selection, positioned as a complementary axis to the dominant FAT direction of improving adversarial example generation efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 76% training cost reduction while simultaneously improving both natural and adversarial accuracy across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-1K makes VDAT a practically attractive drop-in replacement for standard FAT pipelines, and its demonstrated compatibility with robust NAS and alternative training frameworks (TRADES, AWP) broadens its applicability. The paper explicitly acknowledges the lack of theoretical guarantees for why margin-difference-based vulnerability is a valid proxy for adversarial sensitivity, leaving this as a key open question. A practical limitation is that the vulnerability calculation itself introduces periodic overhead (mitigated by the interval hyperparameter T), and the method's reliance on a single FGSM perturbation for vulnerability estimation may not capture vulnerability under all attack geometries.
