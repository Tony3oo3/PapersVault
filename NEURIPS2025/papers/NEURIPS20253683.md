## Problem

Existing energy-driven neural samplers (e.g., PIS, iDEM, DDS) are restricted to diffusion processes on continuous state spaces, leaving discrete and hybrid multimodal settings unaddressed. While generator matching (GM) recently unified data-driven training across diffusion, flow, and jump processes on general state spaces, no energy-driven counterpart existed. This paper closes that gap by enabling training of arbitrary continuous-time Markov processes from an energy function alone, without any equilibrium data.

## Input / Output

The method requires only oracle access to an unnormalized energy function E(x) (equivalently, the unnormalized density exp(-E(x))) with no pre-computed samples from the target Boltzmann distribution. It produces independent samples from p_target(x) ∝ exp(-E(x)) by simulating the trained CTMP after training. The framework accommodates continuous (R^d), discrete (finite state space), and mixed discrete-continuous state spaces, with the energy function defining the target over whichever space is appropriate.

## Method

EGM estimates the marginal generator of a CTMP via self-normalized importance sampling (SNIS), replacing the intractable posterior p_{1|t}(x_1|x) with a tractable proposal q_{1|t} and unnormalized weights proportional to exp(-E(x_1)). To reduce the high variance of these importance weights, the paper introduces a bootstrapping trick: instead of sampling from the target at time 1, it samples from a nearby intermediate time r > t where the posterior p_{r|t} closely matches the backward transition kernel, yielding lower-variance weights. The intermediate unnormalized density p̃_r is intractable, so a surrogate energy model E_r^φ is trained via a noised energy matching (NEM) objective. Training follows a bi-level scheme: an outer loop refreshes a replay buffer with samples from the current sampler, while an inner loop minimizes the EGM loss (a Bregman divergence between the SNIS-estimated generator and the neural network's generator parametrization) with the buffer held fixed. The framework is modality-agnostic, with concrete instantiations for conditional OT paths (flow), variance-exploding paths (diffusion), and masked diffusion paths (discrete jump).

## Results

On the multimodal benchmarks, EGM with bootstrapping achieves E-W1 of 0.20 on GB-RBM (vs. 0.77 for Gibbs), 1.65 on JointDW4 (vs. 4.80), and 1.20 on JointMoG (vs. 7.76), demonstrating substantial improvements over the Gibbs sampler. On the 10×10 Ising model at critical temperature (β=0.4), bootstrapping reduces E-W1 from 19.94 (EGM alone) to 2.51, beating Gibbs (5.29). On GB-RBM, EGM+BS also achieves x-W2 of 0.61 versus 5.12 for Gibbs, a roughly 8× improvement in continuous-dimension fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is a parallel Gibbs sampler (4 chains × 6000 steps), chosen because no prior jump-based or hybrid discrete-continuous neural sampler baselines exist. On the multimodal tasks, EGM+bootstrapping decisively outperforms Gibbs: E-W1 drops from 7.76 to 1.20 on JointMoG (≈6.5×), from 4.80 to 1.65 on JointDW4 (≈2.9×), and from 0.77 to 0.20 on GB-RBM (≈3.9×). On the discrete Ising model, EGM without bootstrapping underperforms Gibbs at critical temperature (β=0.4, 10×10: E-W1 19.94 vs. 5.29), but bootstrapping closes and reverses the gap (2.51 vs. 5.29). At high temperature (β=0.2), EGM+BS matches or slightly beats Gibbs on both grid sizes. Overall, bootstrapping is the critical component that makes EGM competitive with or superior to the Gibbs baseline across all tested settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Generator Matching (GM, Holderrieth et al., ICLR 2024), which provides a unified data-driven training framework for arbitrary continuous-time Markov processes (flow, diffusion, jump) on general state spaces, to the energy-only setting where no equilibrium samples are available. It builds on the bi-level replay-buffer training scheme from iDEM (Akhound-Sadegh et al., ICML 2024) and the bootstrapped noised energy matching (BNEM, OuYang et al., 2024) for learning intermediate energy surrogates. The key departure is replacing the intractable posterior expectation in GM's marginalization trick with self-normalized importance sampling, and introducing a bootstrapping trick that estimates the generator via a nearby future time step to reduce importance-weight variance. This yields the first energy-driven training framework that simultaneously covers continuous, discrete, and mixed state spaces across diffusion, flow, and jump process types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EGM fills a notable gap: prior energy-driven neural samplers (PIS, iDEM, DDS) are restricted to continuous state spaces and diffusion processes, while discrete and multimodal energy-based sampling has relied on traditional MCMC or ad-hoc methods. The bootstrapping variance-reduction trick and the forward-looking parametrization for graphical models are practical contributions that could accelerate training of discrete and hybrid samplers. However, validation is limited to small synthetic benchmarks (≤100 dimensions) with Gibbs sampling as the sole baseline, and no comparison to other energy-driven methods on discrete or multimodal tasks exists. Open questions include unbiased generator estimation, learned proposal distributions, connections to PINN objectives, and whether the framework unifies with the GFN literature.
