## Problem

Cross-modal retrieval models (e.g., CLIP) suffer from a modality gap that causes degraded performance when retrieval keys are composed of fused image-text modality (e.g., Wikipedia pages containing both images and text). Previous approaches to address this, such as VISTA, require generating and carefully curating new triplet datasets for specific retrieval scenarios, which is expensive and fails to generalize to unseen modality combinations. The paper asks whether multimodal retrieval can be improved across all modality combinations using only existing image-caption paired data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251812/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes standard image-caption pairs (LCS-558K) and extracts three embeddings per sample: an image embedding e_i, a text embedding e_t, and a fused embedding e_it (obtained either via VISTA's specialized architecture that appends visual tokens to text tokens, or as the sum e_i + e_t for CLIP-based models). At inference, queries and candidates can each be text-only, image-only, or fused text-image, and the model retrieves ranked candidates from either a shared global database or a task-specific local database. For video retrieval (CoVR), 15 uniformly sampled frames are averaged into a single visual embedding.

## Method

GCL extends the standard two-modality contrastive loss to a three-modality formulation by integrating image, text, and fused text-image embeddings into a single mini-batch. For N image-caption pairs, the loss computes contrastive objectives across all six positive modality pairs — (i,t), (i,it), (t,i), (t,it), (it,t), (it,i) — while treating all other embeddings in the batch as negatives and masking out same-modality self-pairs. This is applied as a fine-tuning stage on pretrained cross-modal retrieval models (VISTA, CLIP, TinyCLIP) using off-the-shelf image-caption data, eliminating the need for newly generated triplet datasets. The key insight is that by forcing contrastive alignment across all three embedding types simultaneously, the model learns a unified representation space that supports retrieval across every modality combination, not just the specific scenarios encoded in curated triplets.

## Results

On the global M-BEIR benchmark (Recall@50), GCL achieves an average score of 34.06 with VISTA, outperforming the pretrained VISTA (21.18), VISTA fine-tuned with generated triplets (25.28), and standard contrastive learning on paired data (24.65). For CLIP-SF, GCL raises the average from 14.92 (pretrained) to 21.89. On MMEB (Recall@1), GCL+VISTA reaches 44.9 average, surpassing CL+Triplet (37.7) and pretrained VISTA (40.3). On the CoVR video retrieval benchmark, GCL improves VISTA's R@1 from 31.22 to 37.52 and CLIP-SF's R@1 from 37.32 to 37.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCL consistently outperforms all baselines on the global M-BEIR average: VISTA+GCL (34.06) beats pretrained VISTA (21.18) by +12.88, VISTA+CL+Triplet (25.28) by +8.78, and standard CL+Pairwise (24.65) by +9.41. For CLIP-based models, GCL lifts CLIP-SF from 14.92 (pretrained) to 21.89 (+6.97) and TinyCLIP-SF from 17.36 to 22.71 (+5.35), with TinyCLIP-SF+GCL even surpassing the larger VISTA model (21.18) despite having fewer parameters (120M vs. 196M). Against AlignCLIP's intra-modality separation loss (30.45 average on global M-BEIR), GCL achieves 34.06, showing a +3.61 advantage, particularly on tasks involving fused modality queries (q_it→c_i, q_it→c_t). The one area where CL+Triplet retains an edge is on specific scenario-matched tasks (e.g., q_t→c_it on WebQA: 80.88 vs. 64.00), but GCL generalizes far more broadly across all 8 task types without requiring task-specific triplet curation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive learning framework established by CLIP to the multimodal retrieval setting, where retrieval keys can be text-only, image-only, or fused image-text. It builds directly on VISTA's architecture for extracting fused embeddings and UniIR's score-fusion strategy (e_it = e_i + e_t), but departs from the dominant paradigm of generating specialized triplet datasets (as in VISTA's IT2I/T2IT or MegaPairs) by instead reformulating the contrastive loss to incorporate all three embedding types within a single mini-batch. The approach also subsumes AlignCLIP's intra-modality separation loss, which addresses the modality gap only for image-text pairs, by generalizing the contrastive objective to include fused-modality queries and candidates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GCL offers a practical, cost-efficient path to improving multimodal retrieval without the labor-intensive curation of generated triplet datasets, and it generalizes to unseen modality combinations that triplet-based fine-tuning leaves unlearned. The consistent gains across VISTA, CLIP, and TinyCLIP on M-BEIR, MMEB, and CoVR suggest broad applicability, and the TinyCLIP result (120M params outperforming 196M and 427M baselines) highlights its value for edge deployment. Open questions include whether GCL can be integrated with multimodal LLMs for retrieval-augmented generation, and whether the slight performance drops on domain-specific tasks (CIRR, FashionIQ) can be recovered through task-specific fine-tuning on top of GCL.
