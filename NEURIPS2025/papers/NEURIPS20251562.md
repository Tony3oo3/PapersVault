## Problem

Existing feed-forward large reconstruction models (LRMs) are restricted to static scenes and cannot predict the motion of dynamic objects, while optimization-based deformable Gaussian splatting methods require hours of computation and multiple pre-trained priors. No prior method achieves real-time, generalizable dynamic scene reconstruction from a single monocular video in a single feed-forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251562/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 24-frame posed monocular video (256×256 or 512×512) with per-frame camera intrinsics/extrinsics encoded as 7-dimensional Plücker rays (6D ray + normalized timestamp), plus 4 temporally distant reference frames sampled at ±48 and ±96 frames to provide larger camera baselines. It outputs per-pixel deformable 3D Gaussian splats at every 4th keyframe (temporal downsampling rate l=4), where each pixel carries depth, RGB, quaternion rotation, scale, opacity, and a set of 3D translation-only deformation vectors to all 24 timestamps. These deformable Gaussians support rendering novel views at arbitrary timestamps and, when chained across overlapping video segments via nearest-neighbor matching, yield long-range 3D point tracking.

## Method

DGS-LRM uses a 24-layer transformer with weight normalization that processes temporally tokenized input: instead of patchifying each frame independently (as in GS-LRM), it groups s×s×l pixel cubes across space and time into single tokens, reducing token count by 4× and making large-window training feasible. The key representation is a per-pixel deformable Gaussian at each keyframe, where deformation is modeled as pure 3D translation (rotation and opacity deformation were found unnecessary or harmful). Training is supervised end-to-end on a 40,000-scene Kubric synthetic dataset with 4 synchronized cameras, using a combination of photometric (MSE + LPIPS), pixel-aligned depth, and dense 3D scene flow losses, with a dual-view sampling strategy that draws two output views at the same timestamp from synchronized sequences to eliminate motion ambiguity. For long-range tracking, predicted scene flows from consecutive 24-frame segments are chained by matching nearest neighbors in a 6-dimensional space (deformed Gaussian position distance + momentary flow direction) using Faiss GPU-accelerated L1 search.

## Results

On DyCheck (monocular dynamic view synthesis), DGS-LRM achieves mPSNR 14.89 and mLPIPS 0.42 in 0.495 seconds, matching the quality of optimization-based methods (D3DGS: 11.92 mPSNR; PGDVS: 15.88 mPSNR) that require 1–3 hours of computation. On PointOdyssey (3D tracking), DGS-LRM with flow chaining achieves ATE-3D of 0.21 m and δ₀.₁ of 0.57, comparable to SpatialTracker (0.22 m, 0.59), while the native 24-frame variant reaches ATE-3D 0.11 m and δ₀.₁ 0.72. The method also outperforms the prior feed-forward dynamic reconstruction method L4GM by 6+ mPSNR points on DyCheck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest feed-forward dynamic method L4GM [67], DGS-LRM wins decisively: 14.89 vs. 5.84 mPSNR (full scene) and 11.97 vs. 5.84 mPSNR (dynamic mask), indicating far superior generalization on real-world DyCheck videos. Compared to the optimization-based D3DGS [97], DGS-LRM improves mPSNR by ~3 points (14.89 vs. 11.92) and mLPIPS by 0.24 (0.42 vs. 0.66) while being roughly 7,000–21,000× faster (0.495 s vs. 1–3 hours). Against PGDVS [106], DGS-LRM is slightly worse in quality (14.89 vs. 15.88 mPSNR) but runs in under 0.5 s versus 3 hours. For 3D tracking on PointOdyssey, DGS-LRM with flow chaining (ATE-3D 0.21 m) matches SpatialTracker [90] (0.22 m) and substantially outperforms Chained RAFT-3D [76] (0.70 m) and Lifted CoTracker [37] (0.77 m); the native 24-frame variant (ATE-3D 0.11 m) exceeds SpatialTracker. Finally, the ablation against static GS-LRM [102] confirms that modeling deformation is essential: DGS-LRM achieves 14.89 PSNR vs. 13.02 for the static baseline on DyCheck.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGS-LRM extends the feed-forward large reconstruction model (LRM) paradigm—established for static scenes by GS-LRM and LRM—to dynamic scenes by predicting per-pixel deformable 3D Gaussians with explicit 3D scene flow. It departs from the closest prior predictive method, L4GM, which models time-dependent Gaussians without explicit deformation vectors, and from optimization-based deformable Gaussian splatting methods (D3DGS, PGDVS) that require hours of per-scene optimization. The architecture borrows temporal tokenization from video generation (MovieGen), reference-frame sampling from BTimer, and scene-flow extraction/normalization from MegaSaM, unifying these into a single end-to-end transformer trained on multi-view synthetic data with ground-truth 3D flow supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single feed-forward transformer can produce physically grounded deformable 3D reconstructions from monocular video in under a second, matching quality that previously required hours of optimization and multiple pre-trained priors. The dual capability—high-quality novel view synthesis and competitive 3D tracking from the same predicted deformation field—suggests a unified representation for dynamic scene understanding. Key open questions include handling longer videos without flow-chaining artifacts, generalizing to non-rigid and topologically changing deformations, and closing the remaining sim-to-real gap for complex real-world dynamics.
