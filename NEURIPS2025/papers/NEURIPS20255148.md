## Problem

Comparisons of foundation language-vision models (e.g., CLIP vs. MaMMUT) and training datasets are typically performed at only one or a few isolated scales without aligning pre-training compute, making it impossible to disentangle whether observed differences stem from architecture, data quality, or simply different compute budgets. This paper addresses the gap by showing how full scaling law derivation with dense measurements across a wide span of model sizes and samples-seen scales enables robust, reproducible comparison of both models and datasets on a common compute axis.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the models consume image-text pairs (1.4B unique samples per dataset) at resolutions of 224×224 or 256×256 with patch sizes of 32, 16, or 14. For downstream evaluation, the trained encoders produce joint image-text embeddings used for zero-shot classification (top-1 accuracy on 35 tasks including ImageNet-1k and robustness suites), zero-shot retrieval (Recall@5 on MS-COCO), and after fine-tuning, pixel-level segmentation masks on ADE20K. The paper's primary 'output' is the fitted scaling law parameters (A_c, B_c, α_c, E_c) with 95% confidence intervals, which enable prediction of performance at unseen compute budgets up to ~2.6×10^12 GFLOPs.

## Method

The paper trains 15 ViT model configurations (S/32 through H/14) at 11 samples-seen scales (1.28M–3B) for both CLIP (contrastive InfoNCE loss only) and MaMMUT (contrastive + captioning loss with cross-attention between image and text tokens) on three open datasets, then fits a double-saturation scaling law L(C) = A_c·(C+B_c)^(−α_c) + E_c to the compute-optimal Pareto frontier points. Model and dataset comparison is performed by examining where scaling curves cross at aligned compute budgets, validated via held-out point prediction with MSE and confidence interval checks. The key finding is that MaMMUT consistently outperforms CLIP above a crossover threshold of 10^10–10^11 GFLOPs across all datasets and tasks, while DFN-1.4B consistently yields stronger scalability than DataComp-1.4B and Re-LAION-1.4B. The authors further show that constant learning rate schedules (saving 98% compute) reproduce the same scaling trends, and derive samples-seen-based scaling laws demonstrating MaMMUT's superior data efficiency.

## Results

OpenMaMMUT-L/14 trained on 12.8B samples from DataComp-1.4B achieves 80.34% zero-shot ImageNet-1k accuracy and 71.19% / 85.88% MS-COCO T→I / I→T Recall@5, outperforming openCLIP (75.24%) and DataComp CLIP (79.19%) trained on the same dataset. Scaling law analysis reveals a consistent crossover between CLIP and MaMMUT at approximately 10^10–10^11 GFLOPs, beyond which MaMMUT's stronger scaling exponent (α_c = 0.233 vs 0.227 for IN1k) yields superior performance. DFN-1.4B consistently provides stronger scalability than DataComp-1.4B and Re-LAION-1.4B for both architectures across all downstream tasks. Scaling law predictions for unseen compute scales (e.g., 2.59×10^12 GFLOPs) estimate MaMMUT ViT-L/14 at 82.0% IN1k accuracy (95% CI: 81.5–82.6%) versus 79.6% for CLIP ViT-L/14 at 2.14×10^12 GFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard CLIP [7] and openCLIP [10], openMaMMUT achieves substantially higher ImageNet-1k accuracy (80.34% vs 75.24–75.54%) at the same 12.8B sample budget, with the scaling law crossover confirming MaMMUT's advantage grows with compute. Compared to MetaCLIP [43] (79.19%) and DataComp CLIP [19] (79.19%), openMaMMUT leads by ~1.15 points on IN1k while using the same DataComp-1.4B dataset. Against SigLIP [18] (80.44%) and SigLIP2 [14] (82.35%), openMaMMUT is competitive or slightly behind, but those models are trained on 40B samples from the closed WebLI-10B dataset versus 12.8B from open DataComp-1.4B; the scaling law analysis in Fig. 12 further shows SigLIP provides no scalability benefit over standard CLIP. DFN [20] (81.41%) slightly outperforms openMaMMUT on IN1k, but DFN uses an ImageNet-finetuned model for data filtering (marked with *), whereas openMaMMUT uses purely open data. The scaling law comparison across datasets shows DFN-1.4B consistently yields stronger scalability than DataComp-1.4B and Re-LAION-1.4B for both CLIP and MaMMUT, and CoCa [16] is predicted to be less scalable than MaMMUT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reproducible scaling law framework for CLIP established by Cherti et al. (CVPR 2023) in two key directions: (1) incorporating the MaMMUT architecture (Kuo et al., TMLR 2023) with its contrastive-plus-captioning objective alongside standard contrastive CLIP, and (2) using scaling laws as an explicit comparison tool across multiple open datasets (DataComp-1.4B, Re-LAION-1.4B, DFN-1.4B) rather than a single-dataset prediction exercise. It builds on the compute-optimal training framework of Hoffmann et al. (NeurIPS 2022) for estimating optimal dataset sizes, and departs from prior scaling law studies by performing dense measurements (15 model configurations × 11 sample scales) with held-out validation, enabling robust cross-model and cross-dataset comparison that prior works with sparse measurements could not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first fully reproducible, systematic framework for comparing foundation model training procedures (architecture, loss function, dataset) on a common compute axis, which is critical as the community scales up open vision-language models. The finding that the CLIP-vs-MaMMUT crossover occurs consistently between 10^10 and 10^11 GFLOPs across all datasets, tasks, and learning rate schedules means that conclusions drawn from single-scale comparisons can be actively misleading. Key open questions include: whether these trends hold with larger open datasets (>1.4B) needed to validate predictions at 12.8B+ sample scales without heavy repetition, whether additional loss types (masking, diffusion) or architectural components (patch size, context length) alter the scaling picture, and how to efficiently incorporate more factors into scaling law-based comparison given the high computational cost.
