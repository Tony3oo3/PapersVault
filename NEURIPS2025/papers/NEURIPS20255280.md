## Problem

In hybrid model parallelism combining tensor parallelism (TP) and pipeline parallelism (PP), TP introduces significant All-Reduce communication bubbles that grow with TP size (e.g., 27.5% of total time at TP=8), while PP suffers from pipeline bubbles due to stage synchronization. Existing works address these two types of bubbles in isolation—either overlapping TP communication at the kernel/hardware level or designing flexible PP schedules—without a unified approach that simultaneously minimizes both.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Transformer-based LLM or MLLM (e.g., Qwen2, Qwen2-VL) with a specified parallelism configuration (TP size, PP size, 2 virtual stages per device), microbatch count, sequence length, and hardware topology (GPU count, interconnect type such as NVLink or PCIe). It produces a concrete execution schedule consisting of braided forward-backward computation blocks arranged in a V-shape dataflow across PP stages, yielding improved training throughput (up to 12% for LLMs, 16% for MLLMs) at the cost of slightly higher peak activation memory, with an optional offloading variant for memory-constrained settings.

## Method

The core mechanism decouples each Transformer layer's forward and backward passes into fine-grained units (Pre-Attn, Attn, Pre-MLP, MLP) and further splits backward units into activation-gradient and weight-gradient components following Zero Bubble. These units are then braided—interleaving forward units of one microbatch with backward units of another—to form execution blocks where TP All-Reduce communication is hidden behind PP computation, achieving near-zero TP idle time. Residual connections are fused into the Attn and MLP units (with a detach-and-divide-by-t trick) to eliminate extra data dependencies that would otherwise block overlap. On top of these blocks, a V-shape PP schedule with warm-up, steady, and cool-down phases is designed to balance peak memory across stages and minimize PP bubbles, with weight-gradient separation selectively activated to align block durations. An enhanced variant offloads long-lifespan chunk-0 activations to CPU during the steady phase to reduce peak memory by 10–19% with negligible throughput loss on high-bandwidth interconnects.

## Results

On the 12.1B Qwen2 LLM with TP=8, PP=2, and sequence length 6144, the proposed schedule achieves 3.49 samples/s throughput, a 12.2% improvement over 1F1B-I (3.11) and 11.2% over ZB-V (3.13). For the 13.2B Qwen2-VL MLLM with TP=8, PP=2, the method reaches 2.87 samples/s versus 2.46 for 1F1B-I, yielding a 16.7% throughput gain. On the 26.3B LLM with TP=8, PP=4, throughput improves from 8.68 (1F1B-I) to 9.61 samples/s (10.7% gain). The theoretical analysis shows the proposed schedule reduces non-overlapped TP communication from 2m·T_AR (1F1B-I) or 4m·T_AR (ZB-V) to a constant (2p+1)·T_AR, independent of microbatch count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: 1F1B-I (interleaved 1F1B from Megatron-LM) and ZB-V (Zero Bubble V with V-shape dataflow). The proposed method consistently outperforms both across all tested LLM and MLLM configurations, with the largest gains at TP=8 where TP communication bubbles are most severe (up to 12.2% over 1F1B-I on LLMs and 16.7% on MLLMs). At TP=4, improvements are more modest (2–5%), confirming that the benefit scales with TP size. Notably, ZB-V sometimes underperforms 1F1B-I (e.g., 6.46 vs 6.60 samples/s on 12.1B LLM, TP=8, PP=2) because its decoupling of the backward pass exposes previously hidden TP communications, increasing total non-overlapped TP time from 2m·T_AR to 4m·T_AR. The proposed schedule avoids this by braiding forward and backward units within the same chunk, reducing non-overlapped TP communication to a constant (2p+1)·T_AR regardless of microbatch count. The trade-off is higher peak activation memory (3p·M_a vs 2p·M_a for ZB-V), which the authors address with an enhanced offloading variant that recovers 10–19.2% of peak memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate optimization threads in hybrid model parallelism: TP communication overlap (addressed at the kernel/hardware level by Flux [2], T3 [25], and Megatron's decomposition [37]) and PP bubble reduction (addressed at the scheduling level by GPipe [7], 1F1B-I [23], and Zero Bubble [30]). The key conceptual departure is treating PP's abundant fine-grained computation as the resource to hide TP communication at the *scheduling* level, rather than requiring kernel fusion or hardware triggers. The braided execution block draws on Zero Bubble's activation/weight gradient decoupling [30] and the V-shape dataflow from ZB-V [29], but recombines them into a composite structure that simultaneously addresses both bubble types—a synergy neither prior line of work attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12–16% throughput gain is practically significant for large-scale LLM/MLLM training, and the scheduling-level approach (no kernel modifications) makes it far more accessible than prior TP-overlap methods that require custom CUDA kernels or hardware support. A notable limitation is the higher peak activation memory (3p·Ma vs. 2p·Ma for ZB-V), which the authors partially address with an offloading variant, though the benefit is hardware-dependent (diminishing on high-bandwidth H20 GPUs). Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully to TP=16+, how it interacts with expert parallelism in MoE models, and whether the memory overhead can be further reduced without sacrificing the braiding structure.
