## Problem

Existing Genomic Foundation Models (GFMs) suffer from four interrelated limitations: (i) no systematic, task-specific search over architectural choices (normalization, positional encoding) tailored to genomic inductive biases; (ii) inability to simultaneously achieve state-of-the-art on both short-context classification and ultra-long-context tasks like enhancer-target prediction over 450 kbp; (iii) restricted vocabularies limited to four nucleotides, preventing natural-language annotation of DNA sequences; and (iv) underexploited multi-task synergies across biologically related downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254432/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, DNA sequences are BPE-tokenized (4096 initial vocab) into 250-token contexts (~1 kbp). For downstream classification, a single DNA window is encoded and a linear head predicts a regulatory-element class; for variant-type classification, both reference and alternate sequences are independently encoded and their hidden-state difference (Δh = h_alt − h_ref) is fed to a classifier. For enhancer-target prediction, 450 kb windows are compressed via SEQPACK to fit within the model's native context. For the SEQ2FUNC generation task, the vocabulary is expanded to 8173 tokens (adding 4077 wordpieces) and the model autoregressively emits a natural-language functional description following the DNA sequence.

## Method

Omni-DNA is a decoder-only Transformer (20M–1.1B params) pretrained with causal next-token prediction on 300 billion nucleotides from RefSeq. An extensive architecture sweep identifies RoPE positional encoding combined with bias-free LayerNorm as the optimal configuration, outperforming ALiBi and RMSNorm variants on both convergence speed and downstream accuracy. For long-context modeling, SEQPACK introduces a position-aware learnable sampling operator that uses Gumbel-Softmax over a trainable temperature vector to select K informative tokens from N, combined with a geometric splitting scheduler that partitions the sequence into exponentially growing segments, reducing compression cost from O(N log K) to Θ(N) while preserving attention-sink tokens at both sequence boundaries. Multi-task fine-tuning groups 10 biologically related histone-modification tasks under shared linear heads or autoregressive label tokens, consistently outperforming single-task baselines. Vocabulary expansion for text generation is stabilized via NEFTune (isotropic embedding noise) and key-token replication (α=5) to counteract the softmax probability shrinkage caused by adding unseen tokens.

## Results

On the 18-task Nucleotide Transformer downstream benchmark, Omni-DNA (1B) achieves the best average score of 0.767, outperforming NT2.5B (0.709), Caduceus-PH (0.715), HyenaDNA (0.707), and DNABERT-2 (0.679), winning 13 of 18 individual tasks. On the 8-task Genomic Benchmark, Omni-DNA (116M) attains the highest average of 0.879. For the long-context enhancer-target interaction task (450 kb windows), Omni-DNA with SEQPACK reaches AUROC 0.895, surpassing HyenaDNA (0.828) and Caduceus-PH (0.826) while approaching the assay-rich ABC upper bound (0.926). On the novel SEQ2FUNC text-generation task, Omni-DNA@FT achieves weighted F1 0.730 and MCC 0.367, outperforming GPT-4o zero-shot (F1 0.659, MCC −0.015) by 7.1% and 38.2% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni-DNA (1B) outperforms all compared GFMs on 13 of 18 NT downstream tasks, with the largest margins on histone modification tasks (e.g., H3K4ME3: 0.642 vs. NTv2's 0.410, a 54% relative gain) and enhancer-type classification (0.492 vs. HyenaDNA's 0.386). On the three splice-site tasks, Omni-DNA trails NTv2/NT2.5B (e.g., Splice All: 0.941 vs. 0.983) but still surpasses Caduceus, DNABERT-2, and HyenaDNA. On the Genomic Benchmark, Omni-DNA (116M) beats DNABERT-2 (same size) on 7 of 8 tasks and achieves the highest average (0.879 vs. Caduceus-PH's 0.866). For the 450 kb enhancer-target task, Omni-DNA with SEQPACK (AUROC 0.895) substantially outperforms HyenaDNA (0.828) and Caduceus-PH (0.826), closing 75% of the gap to the expert ABC model (0.926); NTv2 and DNABERT-2 cannot run at this context length due to OOM. On SEQ2FUNC, Omni-DNA@FT surpasses GPT-4o zero-shot by 7.1% weighted F1 and 38.2% MCC, demonstrating that a fine-tuned 116M genomic model can generate more accurate functional annotations than a general-purpose LLM without any genomic pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

Omni-DNA extends the decoder-only, causal-language-modeling paradigm that recent GFMs (HyenaDNA, DNAGPT, MegaDNA) have adopted away from the earlier BERT-style masked-LM models (DNABERT-2, Nucleotide Transformer), but departs from them by conducting a systematic architecture sweep (RoPE vs. ALiBi, RMSNorm vs. bias-free LayerNorm) specifically tuned to genomic inductive biases rather than transplanting NLP defaults. For long-context modeling, it diverges from state-space and convolution-based approaches (Caduceus, HyenaDNA, Evo) by introducing SEQPACK, a learnable position-aware token-sampling operator that exploits the sparsity of genomic sequences (junk vs. functional regions) rather than replacing the attention mechanism itself. The multimodal DNA-to-text capability extends beyond ChatNT's two-stage NTv2+Vicuna architecture by unifying vocabulary expansion and instruction-following within a single autoregressive model, and the SEQ2FUNC dataset is the first large-scale corpus pairing raw DNA sequences with natural-language functional descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic empirical evidence that pre-training loss is a poor proxy for downstream biological accuracy (Finding 2), cautioning the GFM community against relying solely on scaling-law loss curves to guide model selection. SEQPACK's ability to maintain AUROC > 0.89 at 220× compression while a comparable pruning method (DCP) collapses to chance level suggests that learned, position-aware compression is a viable path to whole-genome-scale reasoning without abandoning transformer architectures. Open questions include whether the multi-task synergy generalizes beyond histone-mark tasks, whether SEQPACK's geometric splitting scheduler is optimal for non-uniform genomic architectures, and whether the architecture-sweep conclusions (RoPE + bias-free LN) hold at scales beyond 1B parameters or on non-human genomes. The authors acknowledge that the architecture search was computationally expensive and that reinforcement-learning-based post-training could further improve generalizability.
