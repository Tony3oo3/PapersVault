## Problem

Current MLLMs, trained primarily on 2D data emphasizing pixel-level continuity, rely on superficial 2D visual cues rather than leveraging the underlying 3D consistency of objects when performing spatial reasoning across multiple views. This paper asks whether targeted training on a foundational viewpoint task can activate the latent 3D spatial perception in MLLMs and transfer to broader out-of-domain spatial reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252813/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of real-world object-centric images (horizontally separated by 20–100°) alongside a multiple-choice text question asking about camera translation (left/right/none) or rotation (left/right/none) from either an ego-centric or object-centric reference frame. The model outputs a single option letter as the viewpoint direction label; during the cold-start phase it additionally produces a structured reasoning chain describing the camera trajectory before answering. The Viewpoint-100K dataset spans 10,813 objects across 205 classes, with ground-truth angles computed from MVImgNet camera parameters.

## Method

The core approach is a two-stage fine-tuning strategy on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. Stage 1 (Knowledge Injection) applies SFT on Viewpoint-100K with a hybrid cold-start initialization that interleaves plain QA pairs with Gemini-generated pseudo chain-of-thoughts at a 0.9:0.1 ratio, simultaneously teaching viewpoint representations and preserving coherent reasoning behavior. Stage 2 (Generalization Enhancement) applies GRPO reinforcement learning on the SAT synthetic dataset, using result-based rewards (format + correctness, each weighted 0.5) to broaden spatial generalization while retaining the injected viewpoint knowledge. The key insight is that direct RL fails due to entrenched 2D biases (manifested as high KL divergence), so explicit SFT supervision must first shift the model's reasoning from 2D cues to 3D consistency before RL can effectively refine and generalize the learned spatial skills.

## Results

On CV-Bench, Actual-7B achieves an average score of 83.5, a +12.3 improvement over the Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct baseline (71.2) and surpassing GPT-4o (79.4) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (77.7). On BLINK, the model reaches 87.6 average (vs. 73.4 baseline), with the MultiView subtask jumping from 53.3 to 99.2, demonstrating near-perfect viewpoint understanding. On 3DSRBench, Actual-7B improves from 45.8 to 47.7 average, though it remains below QwenVLMax (52.4) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (50.3). On the in-domain Viewpoint-100K test set, SFT alone lifts accuracy from 12.9% (baseline) to 92.2%, with human annotators achieving 97.67%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct baseline, Actual-7B wins on all three main benchmarks: CV-Bench (+12.3 avg), BLINK (+14.2 avg), and 3DSRBench (+1.9 avg). Compared to GPT-4o, Actual-7B outperforms on CV-Bench (83.5 vs. 79.4) and BLINK (87.6 vs. 62.7), and also edges ahead on 3DSRBench (47.7 vs. 44.6). Against Gemini-1.5-Pro, Actual-7B wins decisively on CV-Bench (83.5 vs. 77.7) and BLINK (87.6 vs. 59.2) but trails on 3DSRBench (47.7 vs. 50.3). Versus the larger QwenVLMax, Actual-7B loses on 3DSRBench (47.7 vs. 52.4) but surpasses it on BLINK (87.6 vs. 71.1). On the additional MMSI-Bench, Actual-7B (28.9 avg) slightly outperforms InternVL2.5-78B (28.5) and GPT-4o (30.3 is close), while the ablation without GRPO drops to 27.2, confirming the value of the two-stage strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of MLLM spatial reasoning evaluation (VSI-Bench, 3DSRBench, BLINK) and targeted training strategies, departing from prior work that augments MLLMs with external 3D features or visual prompts (e.g., coarse correspondences in [21], bounding boxes in [33]) by instead attempting to 'activate' latent spatial reasoning through internal training. It repurposes the well-established CV task of relative camera pose estimation into a simplified multiple-choice training signal for MLLMs, drawing on MVImgNet for data and the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeekMath for the generalization phase. The hybrid cold-start initialization with pseudo chain-of-thoughts borrows from the RL-for-reasoning paradigm (DeepSeek-R1) but applies it in a novel way to preserve instruction-following during spatial knowledge injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a very constrained training task (horizontal viewpoint MCQs on object-centric pairs) can produce measurable out-of-domain gains across diverse spatial benchmarks, suggesting that foundational spatial skills are a bottleneck that targeted training can address. However, the scope is narrow: all data is object-centric, only horizontal transformations are considered, results are reported for a single 7B model, and code is not yet released. Open questions include whether this 'activation' paradigm scales to full 6-DoF pose estimation, multi-object scenes, or larger models, and whether the GRPO phase's occasional degradation of previously injected knowledge (observed on 3DSRBench height/location) can be mitigated with better reward shaping.
