## Problem

Transformer-based world models achieve strong prediction accuracy but fail to capture the sparse, state-dependent causal structure of object interactions, limiting their ability to adapt efficiently to environmental changes and making them vulnerable to distractors. Prior local causal discovery methods are restricted to simple scalar-state settings and cannot scale to object-centric image embeddings with variable scene composition. The paper asks whether sparsity-regularised hard attention in a transformer can learn accurate local causal graphs from observation-based environments.

## Input / Output

The model takes object-factored latent states (32-dim VAE embeddings for Pong, 64-dim for CREATE, or tokenised vehicle trajectories and map lines for Traffic) as input, with an environment index appended during training. It outputs predicted next-step object embeddings, a binary local causal adjacency matrix (the learned interaction graph at each timestep), and intervention target indicators. During few-shot adaptation, the model consumes a small set of observed trajectories (5 sequences) without the environment index and optimises a continuous intervention token in embedding space to match the new dynamics.

## Method

SPARTAN replaces standard soft attention in a stacked transformer encoder with hard attention: at each layer, a binary adjacency matrix is sampled from Bernoulli(σ(q_i^T k_j)) via the Gumbel-softmax trick, and this matrix masks the scaled dot-product attention so information flows only along active edges. To handle multi-hop information flow across layers, the model computes a path matrix (product of per-layer adjacency matrices plus identity for residual connections) and applies an L0 sparsity penalty on it, ensuring the learned graph is truly sparse. Training uses Lagrangian relaxation to schedule the sparsity weight, initially prioritising prediction accuracy before progressively pruning redundant edges. For interventions, a learnable codebook of intervention tokens is appended to the object tokens; at test time, adaptation to unknown environments is performed by gradient-descent optimisation of a single continuous intervention token, enabling generalisation to unseen compositions of previously seen dynamics changes.

## Results

SPARTAN achieves the lowest Structural Hamming Distance (SHD) to ground-truth causal graphs across all three domains: 1.51 on Interventional Pong, 1.17 on CREATE, and 6.84 on Traffic, compared to 6.37, 6.29, and 10.6 for the Transformer baseline respectively. On prediction accuracy, SPARTAN matches or slightly outperforms the Transformer (8.60 vs 8.83 rollout error on Pong; 246.6 vs 265.5 on CREATE; 0.52 vs 0.51 on Traffic). In robustness tests, removing non-causal entities causes only 24.5% error change for SPARTAN versus 1140.2% for the Transformer on Pong, and 13.9% versus 32.8% on Traffic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Transformer baseline (an instantiation of SlotFormer with one-step context), SPARTAN achieves dramatically lower SHD—1.51 vs 6.37 on Pong and 1.17 vs 6.29 on CREATE—while matching prediction accuracy, demonstrating that sparsity regularisation with hard attention is necessary for accurate local causal discovery beyond what soft attention thresholding provides. Against the Global Graph baseline (based on VCD/AdaRL fixed-graph masked MLPs), SPARTAN wins on both prediction error (8.60 vs 11.36 on Pong; 246.6 vs 277.4 on CREATE) and SHD (1.51 vs 5.42; 1.17 vs 8.77), confirming that state-dependent local graphs outperform fixed global graphs for temporally sparse physical interactions. Against Amortized Causal Discovery (ACD) in Appendix C, SPARTAN achieves SHD of 1.50 vs 10.27 on Pong and 1.17 vs 39.77 on CREATE, outperforming ACD even when ACD is given the true sparsity level as a prior. In few-shot adaptation (5 trajectories, unknown environment), SPARTAN consistently achieves the lowest prediction error across seen and unseen interventions, while the Transformer requires full gradient fine-tuning and the Global Graph cannot handle varying scene compositions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPARTAN extends the SlotFormer line of transformer-based object-centric world models by replacing standard soft attention with sparsity-regularised hard attention, drawing on the causal discovery literature's use of sparsity as an inductive bias (VCD, AdaRL). It departs from CoDA's claim that soft attention with thresholding suffices for local causal graph recovery, arguing instead that explicit hard masking plus a sparsity penalty is necessary beyond simple state-based settings. The method also generalises prior local causal discovery approaches (CAI, ELDEN, FCDL), which operate on scalar states with fixed object counts, to variable-composition scenes with learned object embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPARTAN is the first method to learn state-dependent local causal graphs from image-based observations with variable scene composition, bridging a gap between causal discovery theory and practical world modelling. The striking robustness gap (24% vs. 1140% error change under distractor removal) provides strong empirical evidence that spurious attention connections in standard transformers are a major vulnerability. Open questions include identifiability guarantees when scene composition varies, whether sparsity can jointly induce disentangled representations, and whether the intervention-token adaptation space covers truly novel dynamics beyond compositions of seen interventions.
