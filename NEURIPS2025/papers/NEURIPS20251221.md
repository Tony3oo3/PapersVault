## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting relies on photometric loss for geometry optimization, which produces ambiguous and inaccurate surfaces in indoor scenes dominated by large, low-texture planar regions such as walls and floors. Existing approaches either yield locally smooth but globally curvy surfaces instead of true planes, or depend on task-specific plane detectors trained on limited data that fail to generalize to novel planar objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251221/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images, user-specified language prompts (e.g., 'wall, floor, door, ceiling'), and a COLMAP SfM sparse point cloud as input. The prompts are scene-adaptable—additional keywords like 'blackboard' or 'sofa' can be added for atypical environments. Outputs include the optimized 3D Gaussian representation, a high-fidelity 3D mesh recovered via TSDF fusion from rendered depth maps, novel view renderings, and per-pixel depth and normal maps. DUSt3R depth maps are generated in groups of 40 images to ensure multi-view geometric consistency.

## Method

PlanarGS introduces two complementary supervision mechanisms on top of standard 3DGS. First, the LP3 pipeline generates reliable planar priors by combining GroundedSAM vision-language segmentation with cross-view fusion (back-projecting plane masks from neighboring frames using DUSt3R depth to recover missed planes) and geometric inspection (K-means clustering on prior normal maps plus plane-distance outlier detection to split merged or non-planar regions). Second, during Gaussian optimization, a planar prior supervision term enforces co-planarity by fitting least-squares planes to back-projected rendered depth within detected plane masks and penalizing deviations, while a geometric prior supervision term constrains rendered depth and normals on low-texture regions to match scale-and-shift-aligned DUSt3R predictions. Gaussians are additionally flattened (minimizing the smallest scale factor) and densely initialized by back-projecting plane-region pixels, and a depth-normal consistency regularizer aligns Gaussian orientations with surface normals to prevent roughness.

## Results

On the MuSHRoom dataset, PlanarGS achieves a Chamfer Distance of 4.49 cm and F1 score of 77.14% for surface reconstruction, outperforming the closest competitor DN-Splatter (CD 5.77 cm, F1 61.86%) by 22% in CD and 15.3 points in F1. On ScanNet++, PlanarGS reaches CD 3.66 cm and F1 82.78%, and on Replica it achieves CD 4.13 cm and F1 81.90%, consistently leading all baselines. For novel view synthesis on MuSHRoom, PlanarGS attains the best PSNR (26.42 dB) and SSIM (0.8874) while maintaining competitive LPIPS (0.2141), confirming that geometric improvements do not degrade rendering quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PlanarGS outperforms all eight baselines across all three datasets on every surface reconstruction metric. The closest competitor, DN-Splatter [46], which also uses multi-view depth and normal priors from DUSt3R, is surpassed by PlanarGS by 22% in CD (4.49 vs 5.77 cm) and 15.3 points in F1 (77.14% vs 61.86%) on MuSHRoom, with similar margins on ScanNet++ (CD 3.66 vs 4.16, F1 82.78 vs 75.86) and Replica (CD 4.13 vs 5.60, F1 81.90 vs 68.12). PGSR [4], which introduces multi-view geometric consistency, achieves F1 of 59.11% on MuSHRoom but still trails PlanarGS by 18 points, and its completion error (13.50 cm) is notably worse. The original 3DGS [18] baseline is the weakest, with CD of 11.92 cm and F1 of only 38.53% on MuSHRoom, highlighting the severity of the texture-less plane problem. 2DGS [15] and GOF [63] improve over 3DGS but remain 54–63% worse in CD than PlanarGS. QGS [69] achieves the best LPIPS (0.1994) among baselines but sacrifices geometric accuracy (CD 9.28 cm). DUSt3R [50] used directly for mesh generation (via TSDF) yields CD of 8.17 cm on ScanNet++, confirming that Gaussian splatting with planar supervision substantially improves upon raw depth-prior meshes. FDS [7] is compared qualitatively in the appendix (Fig. 10), where PlanarGS produces visibly smoother planar regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

PlanarGS extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework by integrating two types of foundation-model priors: semantic planar priors from a vision-language segmentation model (GroundedSAM) and geometric depth/normal priors from a multi-view 3D model (DUSt3R). It departs from prior Gaussian surface reconstruction methods (PGSR, 2DGS, DN-Splatter) that rely on monocular depth/normal cues or local multi-view consistency, which the authors argue produce 'locally smooth and globally curvy' surfaces rather than true planes. The key conceptual departure is treating planar region detection as a language-prompted, cross-view-fused pre-processing step (the LP3 pipeline) rather than a learned task-specific detector, and then enforcing a holistic co-planarity constraint during optimization — a mechanism not present in prior 3DGS-based surface reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The language-prompted design offers practical flexibility: adapting to new indoor environments (e.g., adding 'blackboard' for a classroom) requires only swapping keywords rather than retraining a detector, which is a meaningful advantage over task-specific plane detectors like ZeroPlane or Manhattan-SDF's MLP-based fusion. However, the method is explicitly limited to scenes dominated by large flat surfaces and offers no improvement for curved walls or outdoor scenes lacking man-made planar structures. An open question is whether the co-planarity constraint can be generalized to piecewise-planar or weakly-planar surfaces, and whether the reliance on DUSt3R's view-consistent depth (requiring an A6000 GPU with 48GB VRAM for 40-image groups) limits deployment in resource-constrained settings.
