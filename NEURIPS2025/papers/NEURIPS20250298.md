## Problem

Natural-gradient variational inference (the Bayesian Learning Rule) is computationally efficient only for exponential-family posteriors, yet many practically important heavy-tailed distributions such as the Student-t lie outside this family. Existing extensions (e.g., Lie-group BLR) handle some non-EF cases but have not been generalized to multivariate settings. This paper asks whether the marginalization relationship between a minimal exponential family and its non-EF marginal can be exploited to recover the full Fisher-Rao geometry and hence a parameterization-invariant natural-gradient update for the marginal.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a dataset of tabular observations, a prior specified as a representative point in the lifted joint exponential-family space (e.g., Normal-Wishart natural parameters), and a likelihood function. Optimization is performed entirely in the higher-dimensional joint natural-parameter space, but the output is a marginal variational posterior (e.g., a multivariate Student-t) obtained by projecting the converged joint parameters back through the marginalization map. The key structural constraint is that the joint lift must be a minimal, regular exponential family and the marginal must be moment-parametrized (finite, smooth, injective moment map), which excludes distributions like the Cauchy whose first moments are undefined.

## Method

The paper proves that when a distribution arises as the marginal of a minimal regular exponential family, its parameter space is a Riemannian quotient manifold whose induced metric is exactly the Fisher information metric of the marginal family (Theorems 1–2). The resulting Quotient Natural Gradient (QNG) algorithm computes the natural gradient in the joint space using the expectation-parameter duality trick (avoiding any full Fisher-matrix inversion), then projects the gradient onto the horizontal subspace—the Fisher-orthogonal complement of the fibre tangent space—via a rank-r projector that requires inverting only an r×r matrix. For the Normal-Wishart-to-Student-t lift the vertical space is one-dimensional, reducing the projection to a single scalar division. The projected step is taken in the joint natural parameters, and the final marginal posterior is recovered by applying the marginalization map, guaranteeing that the trajectory is invariant to the choice of representative within each fibre.

## Results

On four UCI/OpenML Bayesian logistic-regression benchmarks, NG-Ours (the proposed quotient natural-gradient optimizer) achieves the highest posterior-mean test accuracy on Breast Cancer (97.1%) and Sonar (91.0%), and the highest sample-based accuracy on Diabetes (77.9%) and Spambase (90.6%). It also maintains the highest predictive entropy across all datasets (e.g., 0.186 nats on Sonar vs. 0.091 for NG-LIN and 0.000 for BBVI-NS), indicating substantially better uncertainty quantification. The paper reports that NG-Ours requires roughly one-tenth as many optimization iterations as BBVI-NS to converge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BBVI-NS (Roeder et al., 2017), NG-Ours wins on posterior-mean accuracy for Breast Cancer (97.1% vs. 93.1%) and Sonar (91.0% vs. 79.5%), and on sample-based accuracy for Diabetes (77.9% vs. 74.9%) and Spambase (90.6% vs. 88.9%). Critically, BBVI-NS collapses to near-point posteriors (entropy ≈ 0 on all datasets), so it provides no meaningful uncertainty quantification, whereas NG-Ours maintains the highest entropy everywhere. Against NG-LIN (Lin et al., 2020a), NG-Ours achieves a large margin on Sonar sample accuracy (91.4% vs. 81.5%) and Breast Cancer mean accuracy (97.1% vs. 89.2%), while the two methods are closer on Diabetes and Spambase. The paper attributes the Sonar advantage to the richer Normal-Wishart marginal representation (per-direction matrix kurtosis) combined with the geometry-aware horizontal projection, and notes that NG-Ours converges in roughly one-tenth the iterations of BBVI-NS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian Learning Rule (BLR) of Khan and Rue [2023] and the exponential-family gradient identity of Khan and Nielsen [2018] to distributions outside the exponential family. Where Lin et al. [2020a] handle Student-t via a curved (non-minimal) exponential family and Kiral et al. [2023] use Lie-group actions, this work instead exploits the fact that many heavy-tailed distributions are marginals of minimal exponential families, showing via quotient-manifold theory that the marginal parameter space inherits a unique Fisher-Rao metric. The key algorithmic departure is a horizontal-space projection in the covering (joint) space that replaces the expensive Fisher-matrix inversion in the marginal coordinates, building on the scale-mixture representations of Andrews and Mallows [1974] and Barndorff-Nielsen et al. [1982].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quotient-manifold construction provides a unified, representation-invariant template for natural-gradient variational inference across any distribution that arises as a marginal of a minimal exponential family, potentially covering Laplace, exponential-power, and other heavy-tailed families. However, the paper itself flags important open questions: whether every curved exponential family can be 'uncurved' by adding free parameters (the Laplace example shows this is not guaranteed), and how the method scales computationally in high dimensions where the Fisher metric becomes expensive. Empirical validation is limited to four small UCI classification datasets with Bayesian logistic regression, so broader applicability to regression, time-series, or deep generative models remains to be demonstrated.
