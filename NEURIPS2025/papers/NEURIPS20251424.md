## Problem

Existing provably secure linguistic steganography methods rely on autoregressive language models whose sequential generation causes error propagation: a single tampered token corrupts the conditional distribution of all subsequent tokens, rendering message extraction infeasible under active tampering. Directly porting these methods to diffusion language models does not resolve the issue, since each denoising step's distribution still depends on previously sampled tokens, and the tight coupling between distributions and positions makes DLM-based steganography even more fragile to position shifts from insertions, deletions, or tokenization ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The sender provides a text prompt (truncated to two sentences from IMDb), a secret message bitstring, and a shared PRNG seed, while the receiver additionally needs the tampered stegotext. Both parties must share an identical diffusion language model (Dream 7B), sampling parameters (temperature, top-p), and the PRNG key. The output is a natural-language stegotext of 512 tokens that is computationally indistinguishable from the model's random sampling, and the receiver recovers the embedded message bits with error correction. Embedding capacity is constrained by the min-entropy of each denoising position's categorical distribution and the requirement of at least 3 robust positions per step for repetition coding.

## Method

STEAD exploits the DLM's parallel denoising to identify 'robust positions'—tokens unmasked simultaneously and independently within a single timestep—where message-driven pseudo-random number sampling can embed bits without cross-position entanglement. At each denoising step with ≥3 robust positions, the same message bits are redundantly embedded across all positions using a repetition code, with per-position capacity set to the floor of the min-entropy of the least informative distribution in the batch. Non-robust positions use the shared PRN directly as a pseudo-random error-correcting code for substitution detection. During extraction, a μ-nearest-neighbor search (with μ = max(2, |L−L′|)) corrects position offsets caused by insertions and deletions, while repetition-code voting recovers the message from partially corrupted robust batches. The paper proves computational indistinguishability of stegotext from model output (reducing to PRNG security) and derives a closed-form robustness bound: 2(α+β+γ) < min_s(N_s/L) and β+γ < μ/L guarantee correct extraction under F_{α,β,γ}-bounded tampering.

## Results

STEAD achieves an embedding capacity of 84.08 bit/10³ tokens (top-p=1.00) with the Dream diffusion model, roughly 6× higher than PSARS (13.81 bit/10³ tokens) under comparable sampling settings. Steganalysis detection error rates (P_E) remain at ~50% across three DNN-based steganalyzers (FCN, R-BiLSTM-C, LSTMATT) on both IMDb and C4, confirming computational indistinguishability from covertext. Under token-level attacks, STEAD sustains >80% correct extraction rate at α=0.2 substitution, β*=10 insertions, and γ*=10 deletions, and maintains >80% correction rate under 10% word-level synonym substitution where non-robust baselines fail entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PSARS [23] (the only prior method combining provable security with robustness), STEAD achieves ~6× higher embedding capacity (84.08 vs. 13.81 bit/10³ tokens at top-p=1.0) and faster encoding/decoding per bit, while also demonstrating superior robustness under mixed token-level attacks (substitution + insertion + deletion). Against Discop [16], the comparison is stark: directly migrating Discop to the Dream diffusion model collapses its success rate to 0.00% under token ambiguity (vs. 95.50% on Qwen), while STEAD on Dream retains 99.49% success. Even when a (20,1) repetition code is bolted onto Discop on Qwen to address robustness, the correct rate is only 2.55% under strong mixed attacks, compared to STEAD's 83.43%—confirming that ARM-based error propagation fundamentally limits the viability of adding redundancy to sequential-generation steganography.

## Relation to Prior Work

STEAD extends the provably secure linguistic steganography (PSLS) lineage initiated by Meteor, Discop, and SparSamp, which all rely on autoregressive models (ARMs) and their sequential sampling. The key conceptual departure is replacing ARMs with masked diffusion language models (DLMs), whose parallel denoising positions provide natural 'robust positions' where repetition error-correcting codes can be applied without the error propagation that plagues ARMs. The message-driven pseudo-random number sampling mechanism is inherited from Discop/SparSamp, while the pseudo-random error correction for non-robust positions draws on Christ & Gunn's (CRYPTO 2024) framework. Unlike concurrent work PSARS (which adds robustness to ARMs) or GTSd (a coverless DLM steganography without provable security), STEAD is the first to combine provable security with robustness in a DLM setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical vulnerability: ARM-based PSLS methods are completely broken under even mild active tampering, which is a realistic threat in censorship-resistant communication scenarios. The 6× capacity improvement over PSARS (84 vs 14 bits per 10³ tokens) and the demonstration that naive ARM-to-DLM migration catastrophically fails (Discop drops from 96% to <1% correct rate) validate the DLM-based approach. However, the provable robustness bound is extremely strict (≈0.3% tampering threshold for 512-token sequences), meaning the theoretical guarantee covers only very light attacks; practical robustness beyond this bound is empirical rather than guaranteed. Open questions include whether more sophisticated ECC (beyond repetition codes) can improve the capacity-robustness tradeoff, and whether the approach generalizes to other DLM architectures beyond Dream.
