## Problem

While convergence rates for local SGD in decentralized federated learning are well understood, sharp statistical guarantees beyond the central limit theorem—specifically Berry-Esseen error bounds and time-uniform Gaussian approximations over the entire iterate trajectory—were entirely absent. This gap blocks two critical applications: (1) valid multiplier bootstrap inference on final iterates, which requires explicit finite-sample error control, and (2) statistically calibrated detection of adversarial model-poisoning attacks, which demands uniform control over the full trajectory rather than just the endpoint.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full local SGD trajectory {Y_t}_{t=1}^n produced by K clients running in parallel with periodic synchronization every τ steps via a symmetric connection matrix C, under a polynomially decaying step-size schedule η_t = η_0(t+k_0)^{-β} with β ∈ (1/2,1). Outputs include explicit Berry-Esseen error bounds (e.g., d_C ≲ n^{1/2−β}√K for Polyak-Ruppert averages), two constructed Gaussian approximation processes (Aggr-GA and Client-GA) that track the entire trajectory with quantified coupling error, and a CUSUM-type statistic R_t = max_{s≤t} s|Ȳ_s − Ȳ_t| whose null distribution is calibrated via the Gaussian coupling to yield valid attack-detection thresholds. A key structural detail is that Client-GA exactly replicates the local SGD synchronization pattern (applying C_t only at synchronization steps), making it directly deployable in privacy-sensitive settings where full covariance sharing is infeasible.

## Method

The paper establishes two families of results. First, it derives the first Berry-Esseen bounds for local SGD by applying the Lindeberg method (via Shao and Zhang 2022) with a novel coupling that accounts for the connection matrix C and periodic synchronization, yielding explicit three-term error rates involving n, K, and β. Second, it constructs two time-uniform Gaussian approximations for the entire trajectory: Aggr-GA, a single aggregated Gaussian process Y_{t,1}^G = (I−η_t A)Y_{t−1,1}^G + η_t Z_t K^{−1/2} with rate O_P(n^{1−β}) + o_P(n^{1/p}K^{−1/2} log n), and Client-GA, a client-level process that mimics the local SGD update rule including the connection matrix, with a slightly worse rate but O(Kd²) per-iteration cost and no full covariance sharing. Both are covariance-matching approximations (extending Bonnerjee et al. 2024 to multivariate, non-stationary, long-range-dependent settings) rather than generic functional CLT / Brownian-motion limits. A key theoretical insight is a computation-communication phase transition: for K = o(√n), β = 3/4 minimizes the Berry-Esseen error, while for K ≳ √n no choice of β ∈ (1/2,1) drives the error to zero, revealing a fundamental hardness barrier as the number of clients grows.

## Results

The paper establishes the first Berry-Esseen bound for local SGD in decentralized federated learning, showing the Kolmogorov distance for Polyak-Ruppert averaged iterates scales as $n^{1/2-\beta}\sqrt{K}$ (Theorem 2.1). A key phase-transition result (Theorem 2.3, Remark 2.2) reveals that for $K=o(\sqrt{n})$, the optimal step-size exponent is $\beta=3/4$, while for $K\gtrsim\sqrt{n}$, no $\beta\in(1/2,1)$ drives the Berry-Esseen error to zero. For time-uniform Gaussian approximation of the full trajectory, Aggr-GA achieves error $O_P(n^{1-\beta})+o_P(n^{1/p}K^{-1/2}\log n)$ (Theorem 3.1), while the more privacy-preserving Client-GA achieves $O_P(n^{1-\beta}+(n/K)^{1/4+1/2p}(\log n)^{3/2})$ (Theorem 3.2). Simulations confirm that both proposed approximations substantially outperform a standard functional CLT (Brownian motion) approximation in finite samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the functional central limit theorem (f-CLT) / Brownian motion approximation for local SGD from Li et al. [2022], which provides only an asymptotic covariance-matching guarantee. In the Q-Q plot comparisons (Section 4.3, Figure 3), both Aggr-GA and Client-GA track the empirical quantiles of the maximum partial sums $U_n$ far more closely than the f-CLT approximation across all tested configurations ($K\in\{10,25,50\}$, $N=500$, $\tau=20$). Aggr-GA consistently achieves the tightest fit, while Client-GA is slightly looser but still substantially better than f-CLT. The f-CLT approximation is consistently the worst performer, confirming that the covariance-matching structure of the proposed Gaussian processes (which exactly replicates the non-stationary, polynomially-decaying step-size dynamics) captures the trajectory distribution far more accurately than a generic Brownian bridge. The trade-off is that Aggr-GA requires full covariance sharing at each step (hurting privacy), while Client-GA mimics the local SGD communication pattern exactly at the cost of a slightly larger error term involving $(n/K)^{1/4+1/2p}$. Additionally, the Berry-Esseen results (Theorems 2.1–2.3) explicitly clarify the $K=o(n^{2\beta-1})$ condition previously stated without justification in Gu and Chen [2024], and reveal a new computation-communication phase transition at $K\asymp\sqrt{n}$ that is empirically validated in Section 4.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Berry-Esseen line of work for SGD (Shao and Zhang 2022, Samsonov et al. 2024, Sheshukova et al. 2025) from single-client or linear SA settings to the multi-client decentralized federated learning (local SGD) setting, providing the first explicit finite-sample error bounds for local SGD iterates. It builds on the CLT results of Gu and Chen (2024) and Li et al. (2022) by going beyond asymptotic normality to sharper, step-by-step and time-uniform Gaussian approximations. The time-uniform (KMT-type) Gaussian coupling is drawn from the classical time-series strong approximation literature (Komlós et al. 1975, Sakhanenko, Götze and Zaitsev 2008, Berkes et al. 2014) and the covariance-matching framework of Bonnerjee et al. (2024), but is introduced here for the first time in a stochastic approximation context with polynomially decaying step-sizes and multivariate, non-stationary dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results enable statistically valid bootstrap procedures (multiplier and Gaussian) for decentralized federated learning, which were previously inaccessible due to the lack of finite-sample Gaussian approximation guarantees. The phase-transition finding—that for K ≳ √n no step-size β ∈ (1/2,1) yields a vanishing Berry-Esseen error—reveals a fundamental computation-communication trade-off that constrains how many clients can participate while maintaining inference quality. The time-uniform approximations open a path to provably valid sequential attack detection (model poisoning, LIE, MITM) in federated systems, a practically critical but theoretically underdeveloped area. Open questions include the role of the synchronization parameter τ in the derived rates, extension to non-convex or locally strongly convex settings, and practical deployment of the CUSUM-based detection framework.
