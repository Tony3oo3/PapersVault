## Problem

Estimating the spectral density of extremely large Hermitian matrices becomes intractable when the matrix is too large to form, too ill-conditioned for matrix-vector products, or distributed across systems that only expose small submatrices. Existing tools (Nyström, stochastic Lanczos quadrature, Krylov methods) either require full matrix-vector access or introduce bias toward large eigenvalues, leaving a gap for what the authors term 'impalpable' matrices where only masked snapshots are available.

## Input / Output

The method takes a randomly sampled n_s × n_s submatrix of a large n × n Hermitian matrix (with n_s ≪ n), along with user-specified spectral smoothing parameters (Jacobi/Chebyshev polynomial degree, kernel type for density estimation) and a gluing function (low-degree Padé approximant) for analytic continuation. It produces estimated spectral density values ρ̂(x) at user-chosen points x on the real line for the full n × n matrix. The submatrix must be obtained via random row/column sampling to satisfy the asymptotic freeness assumption underlying the free-probability framework.

## Method

Free decompression reverses the Nica-Speicher free-compression theorem: the R-transform of a submatrix of size n_s relates to that of the full n × n matrix by a simple argument scaling R_{n_s}(z) = R_n(z·n_s/n), which is inverted to recover R_n from R_{n_s}. In the Stieltjes-transform domain, this scaling corresponds to a first-order quasilinear PDE (∂m/∂t = −m + m⁻¹·∂m/∂z) solved via the method of characteristics, yielding the explicit solution m(t,z) = m₀(φ(t,z))·e^{−t}. Two key technical challenges are addressed: (1) the characteristic curves cross the spectral support where the Stieltjes transform is discontinuous, requiring a secondary analytic branch constructed via a Padé-approximant 'gluing function' that cancels the jump; and (2) the initial Stieltjes transform is approximated from submatrix eigenvalues using Jacobi polynomial expansions with Gauss-Jacobi quadrature, rather than the raw empirical sum which has poles on the support. Error bounds show polynomial growth in the decompression ratio n/n_s under mild regularity assumptions.

## Results

On the 50,000×50,000 Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) dataset, free decompression from a 1024×1024 submatrix achieves a total variation distance of 5.94%, Jensen-Shannon distance of 8.33%, and Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance of 3.02% from the true spectral density, with first-moment relative error of 0.17% and second-moment relative error of 0.49%. The computational speedup is dramatic: direct eigendecomposition of the full 50K matrix requires 67,331 seconds, while free decompression completes in 3.6 + 0.8 seconds (approximately 18,000× faster). On synthetic Marchenko-Pastur benchmarks (decompressing from n_s=1000 to n=32,000), the average TV distance is 0.2%, the average JS distance is 1.867%, and the log-determinant relative error is 1.78%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other spectral estimation methods, as it explicitly positions free decompression as the first method for the impalpable matrix setting where neither the full matrix nor matrix-vector products are available. Instead, validation is performed against two references: (1) known analytic limiting distributions from random matrix theory (Marchenko-Pastur, Wigner semicircle, Kesten-McKay, Wachter, and free Meixner laws), where free decompression from a 1000×1000 submatrix to 32,000×32,000 achieves TV distances of ~0.2% and JS distances of ~1.9% for the Marchenko-Pastur case; and (2) direct eigendecomposition of the full 50,000×50,000 NTK matrix, where free decompression from a 1024×1024 submatrix reproduces the spectral density with TV distance of 5.94% and moment errors below 0.5%, while reducing computation time from ~19 hours to under 5 seconds. The authors note that the only prior method for impalpable matrices is FLODANCE (Ameli et al., 2025), which targets log-determinants specifically rather than the full spectral density, and no head-to-head comparison is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the Nica-Speicher (1996) free compression theorem—which relates the R-transform of a submatrix to that of the full matrix—to create 'free decompression,' a procedure that extrapolates spectral information from small submatrices to the full matrix. It sits at the intersection of free probability theory, random matrix theory (Marchenko-Pastur, Wigner, Kesten-McKay, Wachter, Meixner laws), and PDE-based numerical methods, formulating the decompression as a first-order quasilinear PDE for the Stieltjes transform. The paper explicitly positions itself beyond the four-tier taxonomy of matrix accessibility (explicit → implicit → out-of-core → impalpable), targeting the most extreme case where even matrix-vector products are unavailable, a regime where prior tools like stochastic Lanczos quadrature, Nyström methods, and the FLODANCE algorithm (for log-determinants only) do not apply.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method for spectral density estimation of impalpable matrices, filling a gap the authors identify as having 'very few tools currently available.' The practical demonstration on a 50,000×50,000 NTK (ResNet-50/CIFAR-10) achieving ~6% TV distance from a 1024×1024 submatrix in under 5 seconds (vs. ~67,000 seconds for direct computation) is compelling for ML practitioners working with enormous kernel matrices. However, the authors acknowledge significant limitations: the method is sensitive to the quality of spectral density approximation and the gluing function, and highly ill-conditioned matrices remain out of reach without a log-scale Stieltjes transform formulation. Open questions include whether the polynomial error growth (Proposition 4) becomes prohibitive at extreme decompression ratios, and whether the method can be extended to non-Hermitian or structured matrices.
