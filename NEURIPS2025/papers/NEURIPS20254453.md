## Problem

Existing one-step diffusion distillation methods for real-world image super-resolution (e.g., VSD, knowledge distillation) rely on a multi-step teacher model, which imposes a hard upper bound on the student's generative quality. Moreover, VSD does not directly exploit high-resolution training data, depending solely on the teacher's embedded prior, which can lead to unrealistic textures if the teacher's fitted distribution deviates from true HR image statistics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254453/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image as input, encodes it into the latent space via a frozen VAE encoder, and performs a single denoising step at a fixed intermediate timestep T_L to produce a high-resolution latent, which is then decoded back to pixel space. At inference, only the generator (SD 2.1-base UNet with LoRA) is needed; the large-scale SDXL discriminator is used exclusively during training. A fixed text embedding serves as the generation condition, eliminating the need for CLIP or ControlNet modules at test time, and the model is evaluated at ×4 super-resolution on 512×512 outputs.

## Method

D³SR replaces the conventional teacher-student distillation paradigm with an adversarial framework where a larger-scale diffusion model (SDXL) acts as a discriminator in the latent space. The discriminator extracts UNet mid-block features from forward-diffused versions of both real and generated latents at random timesteps, then passes them through an MLP head to produce patch-level realism scores. The generator (SD 2.1-base with LoRA on the UNet) is trained with a combined loss of MSE, a novel Edge-Aware DISTS (EA-DISTS) perceptual loss that computes DISTS on both the images and their Sobel-extracted edges, and the adversarial loss from the diffusion discriminator. Because the discriminator is larger than the generator and directly perceives the true HR data distribution, it breaks the performance ceiling imposed by a same-scale or smaller teacher model. The discriminator itself is fine-tuned with LoRA on its downsample and middle blocks, while the MLP head is randomly initialized.

## Results

On the RealSR benchmark (×4), D³SR achieves the best no-reference metrics among all one-step diffusion methods: LPIPS 0.2961, DISTS 0.1782, NIQE 3.899, MUSIQ 68.23, MANIQA 0.6383, and CLIPIQA 0.5647. On RealSet65, D³SR leads all one-step methods on every NR metric (NIQE 3.998, MUSIQ 70.25, MANIQA 0.6298, CLIPIQA 0.5481). In terms of efficiency, D³SR requires only 0.11 s per 512×512 image on a single A100 GPU—roughly 7× faster than multi-step methods like ResShift (0.71 s) and 105× faster than StableSR (11.50 s)—while using 33% fewer parameters than OSEDiff (966.3M vs. 1400M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against multi-step diffusion baselines, D³SR trades PSNR/SSIM (24.11/0.7152 vs. StableSR's 26.28/0.7733) for substantially better perceptual quality: it outperforms StableSR on all four NR metrics (e.g., MUSIQ 68.23 vs. 60.53, CLIPIQA 0.5647 vs. 0.4310) while running ~105× faster. Compared to DiffBIR and SeeSR (both 50-step), D³SR achieves lower NIQE (3.899 vs. 3.947/4.540) and higher MUSIQ (68.23 vs. 68.02/66.37) at 50–100× lower inference cost. Among one-step methods, D³SR consistently wins on every NR metric: versus OSEDiff it improves LPIPS (0.2961 vs. 0.3036), NIQE (3.899 vs. 4.344), MUSIQ (68.23 vs. 67.31), and CLIPIQA (0.5647 vs. 0.5524) while being 3.2× faster (0.11 s vs. 0.35 s) and using 33% fewer parameters. Against SinSR, D³SR gains 1.87 dB in MUSIQ (68.23 vs. 61.62) and 0.0955 in CLIPIQA (0.5647 vs. 0.4691). Versus ADDSR (1-step), D³SR leads on LPIPS (0.2961 vs. 0.2990), DISTS (0.1782 vs. 0.1852), NIQE (3.899 vs. 5.223), MUSIQ (68.23 vs. 63.08), and CLIPIQA (0.5647 vs. 0.4498). The only metrics where D³SR trails are PSNR and SSIM, which the authors attribute to the generative nature of diffusion-based SR versus reconstruction-oriented baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant one-step diffusion distillation paradigm (VSD, KD, DMD) that relies on a multi-step teacher model to guide a one-step student, instead repurposing a larger-scale pre-trained diffusion model (SDXL) as an adversarial discriminator in latent space. Conceptually, it bridges diffusion-GAN methods (Diffusion-GAN, ADD, LADD) with the scaling literature on diffusion models, but differs by using a frozen pre-trained diffusion backbone as the discriminator rather than a randomly initialized one, and by explicitly studying how discriminator scale affects generator quality. The EA-DISTS perceptual loss is a straightforward extension of DISTS with Sobel edge maps, building on the line of work using deep feature-based perceptual losses (LPIPS, DISTS) for image restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a 3× inference speedup over other one-step diffusion SR methods and 7× over multi-step methods, with 30%+ fewer parameters, making diffusion-based Real-ISR viable for real-time applications. The finding that a larger discriminator (SDXL) improves generator quality beyond what a same-scale teacher could achieve in standard distillation opens a path to iteratively improving one-step generators by simply upgrading the discriminator. However, the approach still lags GAN-based methods on PSNR/SSIM, and the paper acknowledges (in supplementary material) that limitations exist; it remains open whether the discriminator scaling property holds beyond the SD 2.1 → SDXL comparison or generalizes to other restoration tasks.
