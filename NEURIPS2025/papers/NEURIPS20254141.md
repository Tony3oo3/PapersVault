## Problem

Prior work showed that diffusion models can be repurposed as zero-shot classifiers that outperform CLIP on a few compositional benchmarks, but these results were preliminary, relying on limited benchmarks and lacking systematic analysis of the conditions under which they succeed. This paper asks: under what conditions do diffusion classifiers exhibit compositional understanding, and what factors (domain gap, timestep selection, model architecture) determine their discriminative performance?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254141/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The classifier takes an RGB image (typically 512×512 for SD1.5/2.0, 1024×1024 for SD3-m) encoded into a latent representation via a pretrained autoencoder, along with a set of candidate text prompts (positive and negative captions). It outputs a class label by selecting the prompt that minimizes the weighted noise-prediction error, effectively performing image-to-text retrieval. The evaluation protocol varies by benchmark: some use paired image-text inputs (two images, two prompts) while others use a single image with multiple candidate prompts, all scored by retrieval accuracy.

## Method

The paper's core technical contributions are threefold. First, it is the first to use SD3-m (a rectified flow transformer) as a zero-shot classifier by reparameterizing its conditional flow matching objective as a noise-prediction loss, enabling the same ELBO-based classification framework used for SD1.5/2.0. Second, it introduces SELF-BENCH, a diagnostic benchmark of diffusion-model-generated images (from GenEval prompts) with human-filtered quality subsets, to isolate domain effects by comparing in-domain vs. cross-domain classification accuracy. Third, it proposes a low-shot timestep reweighting strategy that learns task-specific weighting functions (piecewise-constant or polynomial) using only 5% of the target dataset, which significantly mitigates SD3-m's domain-gap sensitivity by emphasizing the intermediate timesteps where the model makes meaningful prompt-conditioned edits without overwriting the original image structure.

## Results

Across 10 compositional benchmarks (33 tasks), diffusion classifiers achieve ~43% average discriminative accuracy for SD1.5/SD2.0, while the more capable SD3-m drops to ~39%. Diffusion classifiers outperform all CLIP variants only on Position tasks; on Object, Attribute, and Counting tasks, CLIP consistently wins. Low-shot timestep reweighting (5% data) yields substantial gains for SD3-m: 98% on CLEVR binding (vs. 63% baseline) and 42% on Whatsup spatial (vs. 30% baseline), while SD1.5/SD2.0 show negligible improvement. On SELF-BENCH, in-domain discrimination correlates positively with generation accuracy (r = 0.77), but cross-domain evaluation causes up to 38% accuracy loss for SD3-m on two-object tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and OpenCLIP (Ilharco et al., 2021) variants (RN50x64, ViT-B/32, ViT/L14, ViT-H/14, ViT-g/14), diffusion classifiers lose on Object, Attribute, and Counting tasks across all three SD versions, but SD3-m uniquely outperforms all CLIP models on Position tasks. The paper notes this contradicts prior work (Li et al. 2023; Clark et al. 2023) that reported diffusion classifiers beating CLIP on CLEVR and Winoground; the broader 10-benchmark evaluation reveals the advantage is task-specific rather than general. Against Discffusion (He et al., 2024), the vanilla zero-shot Diffusion Classifier (Li et al., 2023) performs significantly better on SD1.5 and comparably on SD2.0/SD3-m in the SELF-BENCH in-domain setting (Figure A.1), contradicting Discffusion's claims of superior accuracy. Notably, SD3-m's superior generative compositionality does not translate to better discrimination—earlier, less capable models (SD1.5, SD2.0) often outperform it, a finding the authors attribute to domain gap and timestep sensitivity rather than architectural superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion classifier line of work initiated by Li et al. (2023) and Clark et al. (2023), which showed that pretrained text-to-image diffusion models can be repurposed for zero-shot classification via their denoising objective. It departs from prior studies (Krojer et al. 2023, Clark et al. 2023) that evaluated on only a handful of compositional benchmarks (CLEVR, Winoground) by scaling to 10 benchmarks and 33 tasks, and by introducing SELF-BENCH to isolate the domain gap as a confounding factor. The paper is the first to adapt SD3-m (a rectified flow / flow-matching model) to the classifier setting, requiring reparameterization of the CFM objective into a noise-prediction loss, and it introduces low-shot polynomial timestep reweighting as a practical adaptation strategy not explored in prior diffusion classifier work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the most comprehensive diagnostic to date of when and why diffusion classifiers succeed or fail on compositional tasks, identifying domain gap and timestep sensitivity as the two decisive factors. The finding that SD3-m's superior generative compositionality does not translate to better discrimination (and in fact correlates with worse cross-domain performance) tempers the optimistic narrative that stronger generators are automatically better discriminators, and the low-shot timestep reweighting offers a lightweight, practical fix. Open questions remain: whether the domain gap can be fully closed with more aggressive adaptation, whether the findings generalize beyond the Stable Diffusion family to other architectures (e.g., DiT-based models like FLUX), and whether the generative-discriminative misalignment is fundamentally a diversity problem or a representation problem.
