## Problem

Existing layout-to-image (L2I) generation methods produce artifacts such as object fusion, spatial ambiguity, and distortion when multiple bounding boxes overlap, particularly when the overlapping instances are semantically similar. Current L2I benchmarks are heavily skewed toward low-overlap layouts, leaving model performance under dense, realistic overlap conditions largely unmeasured.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251187/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The L2I task takes a layout of normalized bounding boxes (1–10 objects), a global image caption, and per-instance local captions as input, and produces a single RGB image. OverLayBench additionally annotates each sample with pairwise relationship phrases between overlapping instances (e.g., 'behind', 'holding'), which are used to compute the relationship-level success rate (SR_R). The OverLayScore metric, computed from IoU and CLIP cosine similarity across all overlapping box pairs, stratifies samples into simple, regular, and complex difficulty tiers.

## Method

The paper introduces three contributions. First, OverLayScore (Eq. 1) quantifies layout difficulty as the sum over all overlapping box pairs of their IoU weighted by the CLIP cosine similarity of their instance captions. Second, OverLayBench is curated in three stages: Flux generates ~86k reference images from COCO-derived captions, Qwen2.5-VL-32B performs grounding, captioning, and relationship extraction, and human curation filters invalid cases and balances the final 4,052 samples across difficulty levels. Third, CreatiLayout-AM fine-tunes CreatiLayout-SD3 on a 67.8k-image amodal-mask dataset by adding two auxiliary losses to the standard denoising objective: a token-level alignment loss (Eq. 2) that measures the fraction of attention mass falling outside the ground-truth amodal mask, and a pixel-level cross-entropy loss (Eq. 3) that directly supervises the attention map against the binary mask, encouraging the diffusion model to allocate spatial attention to the full (occluded) object shape rather than only the visible portion.

## Results

On OverLayBench-Complex (the most challenging split), CreatiLayout-Flux achieves the best mIoU (54.50%) and O-mIoU (28.97%), while DreamRender attains the highest SRE (77.87%) and SRR (84.93%). All methods show substantial degradation as difficulty increases: CreatiLayout-Flux's mIoU drops from 71.17% (Simple) to 54.50% (Complex), and O-mIoU drops from 49.80% to 28.97%. The proposed CreatiLayout-AM improves O-mIoU by +15.90% over CreatiLayout on Simple and +5.42% on Regular, with FID also improving for EliGen-AM (−8.45% on Simple). Training-free methods (e.g., RegionalPrompting) remain significantly below training-based methods, with best mIoU of only 28.35% on Complex versus 54.50% for CreatiLayout-Flux.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiT-based methods (CreatiLayout-Flux, EliGen, DreamRender) consistently outperform all U-Net-based baselines (GLIGEN, InstanceDiff, MIGC, HiCo, 3DIS) across every metric and difficulty level. CreatiLayout-Flux leads in mIoU and O-mIoU on all three splits (e.g., 54.50% vs. 53.68% for InstanceDiff on Complex), while DreamRender achieves the best SRE on Regular (83.52%) and Complex (77.87%), and EliGen leads in SRR on Simple (89.67%). GLIGEN is the weakest baseline overall, with SRE dropping to 41.70% on Complex and FID reaching 57.32. Among training-free methods (Table 4), RegionalPrompting is the strongest but still trails the best training-based method by ~26 points in mIoU on Complex (28.35% vs. 54.50%). The proposed CreatiLayout-AM improves over CreatiLayout-SD3 by +4.05% mIoU and +15.90% O-mIoU on Simple, and EliGen-AM improves over EliGen by +2.24% mIoU and −8.45% FID on Simple, though gains diminish on the Complex split due to distribution shift from the training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the layout-to-image (L2I) generation line of work by identifying dense bounding-box overlap as a specific, underexplored failure mode. It builds directly on CreatiLayout (a Siamese multimodal DiT) and EliGen (entity-level regional attention) as base architectures, and borrows the token-level attention alignment idea from TokenCompose, repurposing it with amodal mask supervision to address occlusion artifacts. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior L2I benchmarks (COCO, HiCo-7k, LayoutSAM) by arguing they are biased toward low-overlap layouts, and introduces OverLayScore—a joint spatial-semantic difficulty metric—as the organizing principle for both evaluation and data curation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OverLayBench fills a genuine evaluation gap: by balancing samples across OverLayScore difficulty levels and providing dense instance-level captions plus relationship annotations, it enables the first systematic measurement of how L2I models degrade under realistic overlap conditions. However, the methodological contribution (CreatiLayout-AM) is preliminary—gains are meaningful on Simple/Regular splits (+15.9% / +5.4% O-mIoU) but essentially vanish on the Complex split, suggesting amodal mask supervision alone is insufficient for the hardest cases. Open questions include whether richer occlusion-aware training signals (e.g., depth, explicit occlusion reasoning) or larger-scale amodal datasets could close the remaining gap, and whether the Flux-generated benchmark images are sufficiently representative of real-world overlap distributions.
