## Problem

Conformal prediction under covariate shift requires estimating the full likelihood ratio function r(x) = dP_{2,X}/dP_{1,X}(x) to reweight calibration scores, but this estimation is unreliable for high-dimensional features such as images or text, causing coverage guarantees to break down. The paper asks whether one can construct prediction sets with valid marginal coverage in the target domain without directly estimating r.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250543/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three data splits: n₁ labeled source points (features + labels), n₂ unlabeled target points, and n₃ unlabeled source points, along with a pretrained predictor f and a nonconformity score S(x,y). It outputs a threshold function q(x) = ĥ(x) over a linear hypothesis class (e.g., a linear head on pretrained features), which defines prediction sets C(x) = {y : S(x,y) ≤ q(x)} with asymptotic (1−α) coverage in the target domain. The regularization term is computed from one-dimensional projections of the likelihood ratio via sample averages over the source and target splits, avoiding any explicit estimation of r as a function.

## Method

LR-QR solves a regularized quantile regression: minimize E₁[ℓ_α(h(X), S(X,Y))] + λ(E₁[β²h(X)²] + E₂[−2βh(X)]) over h in a linear class and scalar β. The regularization is derived by expanding E₁[(h−r)²] and applying the change-of-measure identity E₁[r·h] = E₂[h], so the objective implicitly aligns h with the true likelihood ratio without ever estimating it. The key theoretical insight is that the first-order conditions of the pinball loss, when the hypothesis class contains r, yield exact coverage under the induced covariate shift; the regularization ensures the empirical minimizer inherits this property up to a controllable error. The optimal regularization λ* ∝ n₁^{-1/3}(1/n₂+1/n₃)^{-1/3} balances estimation error in the pinball term against the regularization term, and the finite-sample coverage guarantee is proved via a novel stability-bound analysis that handles the data-dependent regularizer through intermediate losses and a conditioning argument.

## Results

On the MMLU benchmark (nominal 90% coverage), LR-QR achieves 89.6±1.2% coverage with an average prediction set size of 3.38±0.15, the smallest set size among all methods that reach approximately 90% or higher coverage. On the Communities and Crime regression task (127-dim features), LR-QR tracks the nominal coverage level most closely across four covariate-shift scenarios, while split conformal undercovers in two setups and overcovers in the other two. On the RxRx1 image classification task (WILDS), LR-QR adheres most closely to the 0.9 nominal coverage across 14 test conditions, whereas split conformal, weighted CP, and 2R-CP deviate substantially from the target.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LR-QR outperforms all six baselines on coverage accuracy (closeness to the nominal 90% level) across all three datasets. On MMLU, split conformal (SCP) undercovers severely at 78.1%, weighted conformal prediction (WCP) undercovers at 86.5%, while the four methods that overcover—DRO-CP (99.7%), DR-iso (96.3%), Robust-CP (95.8%), and 2R-CP (96.9%)—all inflate their prediction set sizes to 3.56–3.92, compared to LR-QR's 3.38. On Communities and Crime, methods that explicitly estimate the likelihood ratio (WCP, 2R-CP) and DRO-CP fail to track nominal coverage, particularly in the first shift scenario, whereas LR-QR remains closest to the target. On RxRx1 (high-dimensional image data), the paper notes that directly estimating the likelihood ratio and conditional quantile (as in WCP and 2R-CP) is insufficient to correct coverage violations, and LR-QR's superior coverage is not due to inflated prediction sets. Overall, LR-QR is the only method that simultaneously achieves near-nominal coverage and competitive set size across all three tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantile regression framework for conformal prediction introduced by Gibbs et al. [15], which minimizes the pinball loss over a linear hypothesis class to achieve conditional coverage guarantees. It departs from the dominant line of covariate-shift conformal methods (Tibshirani et al., Lei & Candès, Park et al., Qiu et al., Yang et al.) that all require explicit estimation of the likelihood ratio function r, by replacing that step with a data-dependent regularization term built from one-dimensional projections of r. The regularization is conceptually related to the one-step covariate shift adaptation of Zhang et al. [66], but is specifically designed so that the first-order conditions of the pinball loss yield valid test-time coverage, a property that [66] does not guarantee. The theoretical analysis introduces a novel use of stability bounds from learning theory to handle the data-dependent regularizer, which does not vanish asymptotically as in classical settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck: explicit likelihood ratio estimation becomes unreliable in high-dimensional feature spaces (images, text embeddings), and the resulting estimation error directly degrades coverage. By avoiding this estimation step while retaining theoretical coverage guarantees with controllable error terms, LR-QR offers a more robust path to valid conformal prediction under realistic covariate shift. The cross-modal validation (tabular regression, image classification, LLM question-answering) demonstrates broad applicability. Open questions include tightening the coverage slack in structured settings, understanding how the hypothesis class geometry interacts with the regularization in practice, and extending the framework to other shift types (label shift, concept shift) where the conditional P_{Y|X} is not preserved.
