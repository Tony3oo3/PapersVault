## Problem

Structured sparsity penalties such as the L_{2,1} or non-convex L_{2,2/D} norms are non-differentiable at zero, causing standard SGD to oscillate or converge to dense suboptimal solutions rather than exact group sparsity. Existing remedies—proximal optimizers, iterative pruning-and-retraining pipelines, or post-hoc magnitude pruning—either abandon standard SGD, require architecture-specific routines, or decouple the sparsification step from training without formal guarantees. The paper asks whether a modular, fully differentiable structured sparsity method compatible with off-the-shelf SGD and applicable to arbitrary architectures can be designed with provable equivalence to the original non-smooth penalty.

## Input / Output

The method takes a differentiable neural network whose target weights are partitioned into J groups (e.g., convolutional filters, attention heads, neurons, or decision trees), a gating depth D ≥ 2, and a regularization strength λ. During training, each group's weight vector is replaced by a primary weight vector plus D−1 scalar gating factors (a negligible parameter overhead, e.g., <0.1% for VGG-16), and standard SGD with weight decay is applied to all factors. After training, the gates are collapsed multiplicatively into the original weight tensor, groups whose norm falls below a tiny threshold are zeroed out, and the resulting sparse weights are loaded into a compact architecture with the pruned components removed, yielding a directly deployable model with reduced FLOPs.

## Method

D-Gating decomposes each group weight w_j as ω_j · ∏_{d=1}^{D−1} γ_{j,d}, where ω_j is the primary weight vector and γ_{j,d} are D−1 scalar gating factors, then applies smooth L2 regularization (i.e., standard weight decay) uniformly across all D factors. The key theoretical insight is that at any stationary point, the AM-GM inequality forces the D factors to be balanced (‖ω_j‖² = γ_{j,1}² = … = γ_{j,D−1}² = ‖w_j‖^{2/D}), at which point the D-Gated objective simplifies exactly to the non-smooth L_{2,2/D}-regularized loss, establishing a bijection between local minima of the two problems. Under continuous-time gradient flow, pairwise factor imbalances decay exponentially at rate 4λ/D, so the D-Gated loss converges to the L_{2,2/D} loss at least exponentially fast; under discrete SGD, geometric decay holds up to second-order terms and balanced zero representations are absorbing states. The approach is fully modular: it requires no specialized optimizer, no post-hoc pruning step, and can be applied to any groupable substructure (filters, heads, neurons, trees, modalities) within any architecture using only standard SGD.

## Results

On filter sparsity (Table 1), D-Gating with D=4 achieves up to 541.65× FLOPs speedup on VGG-16/SVHN within 10% of max accuracy, compared to 18.88× for L₂,₁+magnitude pruning and 7.53× for Network Slimming. On ResNet-18/CIFAR-100, D=4-Gating reaches 41.04× speedup versus 14.01× (L₂,₁+pruning) and 3.56× (Network Slimming). In feature selection across six datasets, 3- and 4-Gating dominate LassoNet and HSIC for nearly all sparsity levels. In language modeling (NanoGPT/TinyShakespeare), D-Gating achieves substantially higher attention-head sparsity before accuracy degrades, whereas direct L₂,₁ optimization fails to produce structured sparsity at all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LassoNet [37] and HSIC [66] on six feature-selection datasets, 2-Gating is often inferior, but 3- and 4-Gating dominate both baselines for almost all input-sparsity configurations, making non-convex D-Gating the preferred option. In filter sparsity, D-Gating (all D values) outperforms L₂,₁+magnitude pruning [62] and Network Slimming [40] on both accuracy-sparsity tradeoff and FLOPs speedup; the gap is most dramatic on SVHN where D=4-Gating reaches 541.65× speedup versus 18.88× and 7.53× respectively. For the group lasso baseline [67] in sparse linear regression, D=2-Gating exactly matches the group lasso solution (confirming theoretical equivalence), while D>2-Gating achieves better test RMSE at comparable sparsity. In language modeling, direct L₂,₁ optimization (with or without post-hoc pruning) fails to induce structured head sparsity, whereas D-Gating produces a smooth accuracy-sparsity tradeoff with much higher sparsity before degradation. On the Wine dataset with NODE [47], 2-Gating at tree level shows that fewer than 5 trees (out of 500) suffice to match the full model's RMSE, suggesting the default 2048-tree configuration is heavily overparameterized. In the appendix, D-SNAMs outperform sparse NAMs (SNAMs) [65] in predictive performance for shape-function sparsity without requiring post-hoc pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-Gating extends the factorization principle from Grandvalet (1998) and Hoff (2017)—where smooth L2 penalties on multiplicative factors induce non-smooth sparsity on the product—to arbitrary gating depth D and arbitrary structured groupings, generalizing the L_{p,q} framework of Hu et al. (2017) and the group lasso of Yuan & Lin (2006). Unlike prior differentiable sparsity methods that were restricted to unstructured sparsity or exactly two factors, D-Gating provides a modular overparameterization applicable to any architecture and group structure. It departs from the dominant paradigm of iterative pruning-and-retraining pipelines and from specialized proximal optimizers by showing that standard SGD on the gated objective provably recovers the same local minima as the original non-smooth L_{2,2/D} problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

D-Gating offers practitioners a drop-in, SGD-compatible mechanism for structured sparsity that requires no post-hoc pruning, no custom optimizers, and negligible parameter overhead, with formal guarantees of equivalence to the non-smooth penalty. The consistent empirical advantage of D>2 (non-convex) over D=2 (convex group lasso) across vision, language, and tabular tasks suggests that the non-convex regime is practically important and previously underexploited in deep learning. Open questions include how discrete-time SGD with large or scheduled learning rates affects the imbalance dynamics, whether D-Gating integrates cleanly into the complex training pipelines of foundation models where sparse training from scratch is impractical, and how it interacts with sophisticated pruning-and-retraining schedules.
