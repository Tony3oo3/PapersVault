## Problem

For fully observable LTI systems, state-of-the-art learn-to-stabilize methods achieve sample complexity depending only on the number of unstable modes k, independent of state dimension n. However, partially observable systems do not admit a uniquely identifiable unstable subspace (any similarity transformation yields the same input-output behavior), and require dynamic controllers, making it unclear whether dimension-free sample complexity is achievable. This paper answers affirmatively, establishing both the algorithm and the fundamental limits of stabilizability for partially observable unstable LTI systems.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes M rollouts of length T = 3m+2, where each rollout applies i.i.d. Gaussian inputs u_t ~ N(0, σ_u²) starting from x_0 = 0 and collects noisy outputs y_t = Cx_t + v_t with v_t ~ N(0, σ_v²I). The number of unstable modes k must be known a priori (or inferred from a spectral gap in the Hankel matrix singular values). The output is a stabilizing H∞ controller K(z) for the full system, together with a state-space realization (N̂₁, Ĉ, B̂) of the unstable component, which is determined only up to an unknown similarity transformation S. The total sample complexity is MT = O(k²), independent of the full state dimension n.

## Method

LTS-P decomposes the open-loop transfer function into an unstable component F(z) = CQ₁(zI−N₁)⁻¹R₁B and a stable component Δ(z) = CQ₂(zI−N₂)⁻¹R₂B, then estimates only F(z) via compressed SVD on a 'lifted' Hankel matrix whose (i,j)-th block is CA^{m+i+j−2}B. The lifting by m powers of A causes stable modes to decay as O(|λ_{k+1}|^m) while unstable modes grow, yielding a rank-k approximation H̃ = ÕC̃ with exponentially decaying error. From the SVD factorization H̃ = ÛΣ̂V̂*, the unstable subsystem is recovered up to a similarity transformation by solving shift equations on the observability and controllability blocks. A robust H∞ controller is then designed for the estimated unstable component to satisfy the small gain condition ‖K(z)F_K(z)‖_{H∞} < 1/‖Δ(z)‖_{H∞}, which is shown to be a necessary and sufficient condition for stabilizing the full system. The sample complexity scales as O(k²) through the controllability/observability index α = O(k), making it the first result achieving dimension-free stabilization for partially observable LTI systems.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5.3) establishes that LTS-P stabilizes a partially observable LTI system with total sample complexity MT = O(k²), where k is the number of unstable eigenvalues, and is independent of the full state dimension n. In simulations (Appendix A) with k=5 fixed and n varied, LTS-P requires the shortest rollout length and fewest number of trajectories across all noise levels (σ = 0.4, 0.6, 0.8), with its sample requirements remaining O(poly(k)) regardless of n. The stabilization probability (Figure 3) improves most significantly with additional trajectories, consistent with the theoretical bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LTS-P against two state-of-the-art system identification baselines: nuclear norm regularization (Sun et al., CoLDC 2020) and the Ho-Kalman algorithm (Zheng and Li, IEEE Control Systems Letters 2020). In the rollout-length experiment (Figure 1), LTS-P requires the shortest rollouts to stabilize the system, while Sun et al. maintains roughly constant rollout length across dimensions (since it only uses the last data point per rollout) and Zheng and Li grows linearly with n. In the number-of-trajectories experiment (Figure 2), LTS-P requires the fewest trajectories overall; when n ≳ k it performs comparably to Zheng and Li (as expected, since LTS-P reduces to the full-system identification when k = n), but when n ≫ k it clearly outperforms both baselines. The key advantage is that LTS-P's sample complexity scales only with k (the unstable dimension) rather than n, making it substantially more data-efficient for high-dimensional systems dominated by stable dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unstable/stable subspace decomposition framework of Hu et al. [19] and its noisy extension by Zhang et al. [50] from fully observable to partially observable LTI systems, where the unstable subspace is no longer uniquely identifiable. The key technical departure is the introduction of a 'lifted' Hankel matrix (raising A to power m to separate stable from unstable modes) combined with compressed SVD, drawing on classical model reduction ideas but adapted to the unstable setting where Hankel matrices grow unboundedly. The robust controller design leverages the small gain theorem from H∞ control theory, treating the stable component as an adversarial modeling error, and builds on the Hankel matrix estimation techniques of Zheng and Li [51] for the multi-trajectory setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result achieving dimension-free sample complexity (O(k²) in the number of unstable modes, independent of state dimension n) for stabilizing partially observable LTI systems, substantially reducing data requirements for high-dimensional systems dominated by stable dynamics. The characterization of the H∞ norm condition on the stable component as both necessary and sufficient for stabilization reveals a fundamental limit of the unstable+stable decomposition approach: if the stable component is too large in H∞ norm, no controller can stabilize the system even with perfect knowledge of the unstable component. Open questions include handling process noise in the transfer function, tightening the O(k²) bound, and extending to non-diagonalizable unstable subsystems.
