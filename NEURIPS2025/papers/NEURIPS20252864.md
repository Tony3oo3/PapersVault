## Problem

Existing Graph Prompt Learning (GPL) methods lack a unified understanding of how different prompt mechanisms (input-level, layer-wise, representation-level) interact with pretrained models, and they fail to generalize across diverse downstream scenarios, especially under distribution shifts such as homophilic-to-heterophilic graphs. The paper further reveals through theoretical analysis that representation-level prompts are fundamentally equivalent to linear probing, meaning they merely adapt a classifier rather than unleashing pretrained model capabilities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}, node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F}), a frozen pretrained graph encoder f_θ, and a small set of labeled nodes V_L as input. It produces node class labels via a lightweight classifier and a learnable prompt adjacency matrix Ã that modifies the graph topology. Unlike feature-level prompts, the prompt operates purely on edge structure, and the original graph is preserved through iterative bootstrapping rather than being replaced, which is critical for preventing model collapse in few-shot settings.

## Method

UniPrompt is an input-level topological prompt that constructs a kNN graph from cosine similarity of node features as the initial prompt topology, then assigns learnable scalar weights to each prompt edge via a scaled-and-shifted ELU gating mechanism that can adaptively prune or amplify edges. A bootstrapping integration rule Â^(t) = τÂ^(t-1) + (1-τ)Ã iteratively blends the prompt graph with the original adjacency matrix, where τ ∈ [0,1] controls the balance and prevents overfitting. The prompt parameters Ψ and a lightweight projection head g_φ are jointly optimized on labeled nodes while the pretrained encoder remains frozen. The key design principle, grounded in the theoretical equivalence of representation-level prompts to linear probing, is that the prompt should modify input topology to unleash pretrained model capabilities while the classifier handles downstream adaptation—clearly separating the roles of the two components.

## Results

On 1-shot cross-domain node classification (Table 2), UniPrompt achieves the best accuracy on 5 out of 6 datasets: Cora (45.37%), CiteSeer (43.25%), PubMed (55.01%), Cornell (51.58%), and Squirrel (25.29%), outperforming the strongest GFM baseline MDGFM by up to 10.81 points on Cornell. On 3/5-shot cross-domain classification (Table 3), UniPrompt leads on 5 of 6 datasets, reaching 65.64% on Cora(5-shot), 65.09% on PubMed(5-shot), and 52.09% on Cornell(3-shot). In in-domain 1-shot settings across DGI, GRACE, and GraphMAE pretrained models, UniPrompt consistently outperforms GPL baselines (GPPT, GraphPrompt, GPF, EdgePrompt) on heterophilic datasets like Cornell, Texas, and Wisconsin, while showing more modest gains on homophilic datasets like Cora and CiteSeer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPrompt outperforms all GPL baselines (GPPT, GPF, EdgePrompt) by large margins in cross-domain settings: on Cornell 1-shot, UniPrompt reaches 51.58% vs. GPF's 34.54% and GPPT's 25.09%. Against the strongest multi-domain GFM baseline MDGFM, UniPrompt wins on 5 of 6 cross-domain datasets (Cora +0.54, CiteSeer +1.07, PubMed +8.17, Cornell +10.81, Squirrel +0.99) but loses on Chameleon (25.14 vs. 28.36), where dense inter-class connectivity makes topological prompting less effective. In in-domain 1-shot experiments, UniPrompt surpasses GPPT and GraphPrompt on heterophilic datasets (Cornell, Texas, Wisconsin) where those methods suffer from model collapse, while on homophilic datasets (Cora, CiteSeer) all methods perform comparably. The semi-supervised baselines (GCN, GAT) and self-supervised baselines (DGI, GraphCL) are consistently outperformed by UniPrompt across all cross-domain settings, confirming the advantage of prompt-based adaptation over training from scratch. At 3/5-shot, UniPrompt extends its lead over MDGFM on Squirrel (26.70 vs. 23.00) and Chameleon (31.38 vs. 30.54), narrowing the gap on CiteSeer (59.37 vs. 61.24) where higher homophily benefits the GFM's multi-domain pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the Graph Prompt Learning (GPL) literature, which includes input-level methods (GPF, EdgePrompt), representation-level methods (GPPT, GraphPrompt), and layer-wise methods (All-in-one, MultiGPrompt). Its key conceptual departure is a theoretical proof that representation-level prompts are functionally and optimization-equivalent to linear probing, thereby reframing a large class of GPL methods as merely classifier adaptation rather than genuine 'prompting.' Building on ideas from Graph Self-Supervised Learning (bootstrapping) and Graph Structure Learning, UniPrompt proposes an input-level edge prompt initialized via kNN with a bootstrapped integration strategy, combined with a lightweight classifier, as a universal recipe compatible with any frozen pretrained encoder (DGI, GRACE, GraphMAE, FUG).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The equivalence result (representation-level prompt ≡ linear probe) provides a clear design principle for the GPL field: prompts should modify the input to unleash pretrained model capability, while a simple classifier handles downstream adaptation. This reframing could redirect future GPL research away from representation-level token designs toward input-level structural interventions. Limitations include the theoretical proof assuming linear prompts and classifiers, the added hyperparameter τ for bootstrapping, and evaluation primarily on small-to-medium graphs (≤2708 nodes) with node classification as the sole task. Open questions include whether the input-level advantage persists at scale (e.g., OGBN-arXiv with 110K nodes), for non-linear prompt functions, and for tasks beyond node classification such as link prediction or graph-level tasks.
