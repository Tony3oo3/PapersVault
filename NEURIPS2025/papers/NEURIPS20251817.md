## Problem

Message passing in GNNs was designed under the homophily assumption, yet it remains effective on heterophilic graphs for reasons that are not well understood from a model perspective. Prior analyses focused on which data characteristics make vanilla message passing work, leaving unexplored how heterophilic message-passing (HTMP) mechanisms actually improve node discriminability. This paper asks: can we explicitly enhance the Compatibility Matrix (CM) through model design to produce better embeddings on heterophilic graphs?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph with adjacency matrix, node feature matrix, and partial node labels (48%/32%/20% train/val/test splits). Beyond standard node embeddings and class predictions, CMGNN internally maintains a K×K compatibility matrix estimated from confidence-weighted pseudo labels, which is updated only when validation performance improves to ensure stability. Virtual prototype nodes (one per class, constructed as mean features of training-set nodes) are appended to the graph, so the effective input graph is augmented with K additional nodes connected to all real nodes.

## Method

CMGNN introduces three coupled mechanisms to explicitly increase Compatibility Matrix Discriminability (CMD) while addressing sparsity and noise. First, a virtual neighborhood of K class-prototype nodes is added so every node—regardless of degree—can receive class-level messages, mitigating sparsity. Second, a confidence metric derived from information entropy of soft pseudo labels down-weights uncertain predictions when estimating the CM, reducing noise from incorrect labels. Third, CM-aware message passing collects three message streams (ego, raw neighborhood, and CM-weighted supplement neighborhood via Hadamard product of soft pseudo labels with the CM), combined with per-node adaptive weights and multi-layer concatenation. A discrimination loss penalizes cosine similarity between class-aggregated prototype embeddings, simultaneously sharpening the CM rows and separating node embeddings across classes.

## Results

On 8 small-scale datasets, CMGNN achieves the best average rank of 1.5, winning on 6 of 8 datasets. Headline results include 84.35% on Roman-Empire (vs. 82.88% for OrderedGNN), 52.13% on Amazon-Ratings (vs. 51.15% for OrderedGNN), and 45.70% on Chameleon-F (vs. 45.20% for ACM-GCN). On 5 large-scale datasets, CMGNN again achieves the best average rank of 1.6, with a particularly striking 59.86% on Snap-Patents (vs. 42.24% for GCNII, a 17.6-point margin). CMGNN also maintains competitive performance on homophilic graphs (e.g., 97.00% on BlogCatalog, 89.99% on Pubmed).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMGNN outperforms all 20 baselines on 6 of 8 small-scale datasets, with the largest margins on Roman-Empire (+1.47 over M2MGNN), Amazon-Ratings (+0.98 over OrderedGNN), and Squirrel-F (+0.99 over ACM-GCN). On Actor, OrderedGNN edges out CMGNN by 0.25 points (37.07 vs. 36.82), and on Pubmed the two are essentially tied (89.99 vs. 90.01). Among the strongest heterophilic baselines, ACM-GCN and OrderedGNN are the closest competitors, but CMGNN surpasses both on the majority of heterophilic datasets (homophily < 0.5). On large-scale graphs, CMGNN's advantage is most dramatic on Snap-Patents (59.86% vs. 42.24% for GCNII, the next best), while on Twitch-Gamer it is slightly behind N² (65.18 vs. 65.76) and on Genius behind GCNII (85.19 vs. 86.45). The CM-based baselines (CLP, EPFGNN, CPGNN) all rank well below CMGNN (avg. ranks 11.8, 15.0, 15.6 vs. 1.5), confirming that naively using the CM without addressing sparsity and noise is insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Compatibility Matrix (CM) concept introduced by Zhu et al. (CPGNN, AAAI 2021) and the ENKL/CSBM-H theoretical framework from Luan et al. (NeurIPS 2023), connecting them to explain why heterophilic message passing (HTMP) works. While prior analyses (Ma et al. 2022, Zhu et al. 2023, Luan et al. 2023) studied which data characteristics make vanilla message passing viable on heterophilic graphs, this paper shifts to a model-level perspective: it shows that existing HTMP methods (GPR-GNN, ACM-GCN, GloGNN) succeed by implicitly increasing the Compatibility Matrix Discriminability (CMD). The proposed CMGNN departs from prior CM-based methods (CLP, EPFGNN, CPGNN) by addressing sparsity and noise through virtual neighborhood supplementation and confidence-based CM estimation, rather than naively reweighting edges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CMD-ENKL negative correlation theorem provides a unifying theoretical lens for understanding why diverse HTMP architectures work, offering a principled design criterion (maximize CMD) for future heterophilic GNNs. Practically, CMGNN's virtual neighborhood and confidence-based estimation address real deployment challenges (imbalanced degrees, semi-supervised label scarcity) that prior CM-based methods ignore. However, the theoretical analysis is restricted to two-class CSBM-H with equal degrees, and the method only targets the message-passing component, leaving spectral and graph-transform approaches unanalyzed. Open questions include whether the CMD principle generalizes to multi-class, transductive, or fully-supervised settings, and whether the modest performance gains over strong baselines (e.g., OrderedGNN, ACM-GCN) persist under stricter evaluation protocols.
