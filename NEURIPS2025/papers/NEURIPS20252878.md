## Problem

Existing AEOS constellation scheduling methods either operate on small, simplified scenarios with abstracted constraints or lack a common benchmark for fair comparison, making it difficult to evaluate real-world performance under large-scale, dynamic, and physically constrained conditions. No prior benchmark combines a high-fidelity physics simulator, large-scale diverse scenarios, and ground truth scheduling annotations for multi-satellite scheduling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252878/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep, the model receives concatenated static and dynamic satellite matrices (orbital elements, mass, current MRP attitude, battery state) and task matrices (release/due times, target lat/lon, progress), plus a sinusoidal timestep embedding. The output is a per-satellite assignment vector where each entry is either 0 (idle) or a task index; the simulator then converts these high-level assignments into low-level power and attitude-pointing commands, enforcing five physical constraints (dynamics, energy, FOV, continuity, time window) and rejecting infeasible actions.

## Method

AEOS-Former is a Transformer encoder-decoder in which a dedicated internal constraint module (a 2-layer MLP) predicts, for every satellite-task pair, a feasibility logit and an estimated attitude-adjustment time. These predictions generate a constraint-driven cross-attention mask (M_ij = w·ŝ_ij + b) that gates the decoder's attention over task features, so infeasible pairs are suppressed during both training and inference. The model is trained with a three-term loss (binary cross-entropy on feasibility, MSE on timing, cross-entropy on assignment) and then refined through a simulation-based iterative learning loop: after supervised pretraining on AEOS-Bench annotations, the model proposes schedules in the Basilisk simulator, and only trajectories exceeding a performance threshold are merged back into the training set for additional supervised rounds, enabling the model to discover strategies beyond the original expert annotations.

## Results

On the val-unseen split, AEOS-Former achieves a comprehensive score (CS) of 4.43, a completion rate (CR) of 35.42%, and a power consumption (PC) of 68.99 Wh, outperforming the strongest baseline MSCPO-SHCS (CS 5.21, CR 35.35%, PC 140.83 Wh). On the test split with real satellite data, AEOS-Former reaches CS 6.28 and PC 40.91 Wh, surpassing MSCPO-SHCS by 16.7% in CS and reducing power consumption by over 72%. Across all three splits, AEOS-Former attains the lowest CS while maintaining competitive or superior CR, demonstrating a better balance between task completion and energy efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AEOS-Former is compared against three baselines: HAAL (handover-aware task allocation, optimization-based), REDA (multi-agent reinforcement learning), and MSCPO-SHCS (stochastic hill-climbing, optimization-based). Against the strongest baseline MSCPO-SHCS, AEOS-Former wins on CS across all splits (e.g., 4.43 vs. 5.21 on val-unseen, 6.28 vs. 7.33 on test) and dramatically reduces power consumption (68.99 Wh vs. 140.83 Wh on val-unseen; 40.91 Wh vs. 149.20 Wh on test), while matching or slightly exceeding CR (35.42% vs. 35.35% on val-unseen). On the test split, AEOS-Former's CR (19.25%) is marginally below MSCPO-SHCS (19.44%), but its substantially lower PC and TAT yield a better overall CS. Both HAAL and REDA are far outperformed, with HAAL achieving only ~1% CR and REDA only ~3–5% CR, reflecting their inability to scale to the large, constraint-rich scenarios in AEOS-Bench. The constraint-aware attention module and simulation-based iterative learning are the key differentiators, as confirmed by ablation studies showing that removing either component degrades CS by 0.27–0.85.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of realistic physics simulation and learned scheduling for agile satellite constellations. Prior benchmarks (REDA, SatNet, EOSSP-RCS) either use fewer than 10 scenarios, omit physics simulation, or lack ground-truth annotations, while prior methods (HAAL, MSCPO-SHCS, REDA) operate under simplified or abstracted constraints. AEOS-Former repurposes the Transformer encoder-decoder paradigm with a novel constraint-aware attention mask derived from a dedicated feasibility predictor, and couples it with a simulation-based self-training loop—an approach not previously applied to constellation scheduling at this scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AEOS-Bench fills a critical gap as the first large-scale, physics-grounded benchmark with ground-truth annotations, enabling fair and realistic comparison of scheduling methods that prior small-scale or constraint-free benchmarks could not support. The ~50% reduction in power consumption at comparable completion rates highlights that constraint-aware learning can yield substantial operational savings beyond what optimization-based baselines achieve. Key limitations include single-point task representation (no area-based coverage), absence of error bars, and baselines adapted from simplified settings, leaving open whether the gains persist under more complex task geometries or truly adversarial dynamic environments.
