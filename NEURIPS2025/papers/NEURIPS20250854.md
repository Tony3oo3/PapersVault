## Problem

Existing novel-object 6D pose estimation methods require expensive prerequisites—precise CAD models, multi-view template libraries, neural field training, or sensor-based depth that fails on transparent and reflective surfaces (over 85% failure rate on ClearPose). The paper asks whether robust pose estimation is possible with only a single RGB reference image, eliminating all of these costly dependencies while maintaining accuracy under challenging lighting and material conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250854/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query RGB image and a single reference RGB image (both at 640×480) containing the same object, plus a segmentation mask (e.g., from SAM) to localize the target. No CAD model, multi-view collection, depth sensor, or novel-view synthesis is required. It produces the 6D relative pose T_{q→r} between query and reference, with intermediate outputs of metric depth maps (valid for all pixels including transparent regions) and dense 2D correspondences used for point-cloud registration.

## Method

The pipeline has two core innovations. First, a token-scaler mechanism is inserted into DPAv2's top-to-bottom fusion layers: efficient attention modules re-weight low/mid-level features while InceptionConv-based scalers modulate high/global-level features, with a dynamic scale layer predicting a global scalar from pooled multi-level features. This is trained with a composite loss combining SSI+BerHu (global), scale alignment, edge-emphasized gradient matching, and surface normal consistency terms. Second, a fine-tuning-free depth-aware matching module extends LoFTR by additively fusing normalized depth features into both coarse (1/8 resolution) and fine (1/2 resolution) RGB feature maps in the latent space, keeping all LoFTR parameters frozen. Only the lightweight token scaler is trained (supervised on labeled depth); the final pose is solved via PointDSC registration on depth-projected point clouds.

## Results

On metric depth estimation, SingRef6D achieves a 14.41% improvement in δ₁.₀₅ over DPAv2 (fine-tuned head) on REAL275 (44.28% vs. 29.87%), a 23.07% improvement on ClearPose (54.30% vs. 31.23%), and a 65% improvement on Toyota-Light (80.09% vs. 14.64%). For 6D pose estimation, the method achieves a 6.1% average AR improvement over Oryon across three benchmarks using predicted depth (REAL275: 28.7 vs. 20.4; Toyota-Light: 31.7 vs. 24.1; ClearPose: 19.4 vs. 17.1). The depth-aware LoFTR-based matcher with the proposed token-scaler depth model also reduces computational cost to 11.6M parameters and 13.9 GFLOPs, compared to 264.3M/120.1 for Oryon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPAv2 (fine-tuned head) as a depth source, SingRef6D's token-scaler fine-tuning yields dramatic gains: +14.41% δ₁.₀₅ on REAL275, +23.07% on ClearPose, and +65% on Toyota-Light, directly translating to +14.4% and +20.3% AR improvements in pose estimation with Oryon and LoFTR matching respectively. Against UniDepth (fine-tuned), SingRef6D outperforms on all three depth benchmarks (e.g., 44.28% vs. 33.81% δ₁.₀₅ on REAL275). In 6D pose estimation with predicted depth, SingRef6D surpasses SIFT by +12.6% average AR and Oryon by +6.1% average AR across the three datasets; on Toyota-Light specifically, it also edges out RoMA (31.7 vs. 30.9 AR). The only case where SingRef6D trails a baseline is Toyota-Light with DPAv2 depth, where Oryon's VLM-based textual matching provides a slight advantage (24.1 vs. 24.1 AR), highlighting the sensitivity of pose accuracy to depth prediction quality. Computationally, SingRef6D uses only 11.6M parameters and 0.74 GB memory versus 264.3M/5.90 GB for Oryon and 111.3M/5.10 GB for RoMA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Depth Anything V2 (DPAv2) for monocular metric depth estimation and LoFTR for detector-free feature matching, but departs from both by introducing a lightweight token-scaler fine-tuning mechanism (a ControlNet-like structure that re-weights hierarchical transformer features) and a depth-aware matching module that fuses normalized depth features additively into LoFTR's latent space. Conceptually, it positions itself against two dominant paradigms in novel-object 6D pose estimation: (1) CAD/sensor-depth-based methods (MatchU, Oryon) that fail on transparent or reflective surfaces, and (2) multi-view synthesis or neural-field-based methods (NOPE, FoundationPose, Zero123-6D) that require expensive generative model training. The key departure is the strict single-RGB-reference constraint, which eliminates all forms of novel-view synthesis, 3D reconstruction, or per-instance training while still achieving competitive pose accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in dramatically reducing the reference requirement for 6D pose estimation to a single RGB image plus a lightweight fine-tuned token scaler (~11.6M params, 13.9 GFLOPs), making the pipeline deployable in resource-constrained settings where depth sensors, multi-view rigs, or GPU-heavy generative models are unavailable. The 85% sensor-depth failure rate on transparent objects (ClearPose) and the resulting 23% depth accuracy improvement over DPAv2 on that dataset highlight a real-world gap that prior methods could not address. Limitations remain: the method requires object segmentation masks for localization, generalization is bounded by DPAv2 and LoFTR pre-training, and performance degrades in extremely low-light conditions. Open questions include whether the token-scaler paradigm transfers to other ViT-based backbones and whether depth-aware matching can extend to broader applications like multi-view scene reconstruction.
