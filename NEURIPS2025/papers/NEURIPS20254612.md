## Problem

Existing 3D human reconstruction methods either require dense multi-view inputs or time-consuming per-subject optimization, and combining them with semantic segmentation typically relies on a two-stage pipeline (reconstruct then segment) that breaks 3D consistency across viewpoints. No unified, feed-forward framework existed that simultaneously produces high-fidelity 3D human geometry and 3D-consistent body-part segmentation from a single image without per-instance optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254612/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image as input; a pre-trained SV3D diffusion model synthesizes additional posed views (typically 2) in ~6 seconds, which are then processed by the reconstruction network in ~0.2 seconds. The output is a set of pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian primitives augmented with learnable semantic feature embeddings, enabling both real-time novel-view rendering (via alpha-blended color/opacity/SH) and 28-class human-part segmentation (via feature-field rasterization) from arbitrary viewpoints without re-optimization.

## Method

HumanCrafter employs a dual-Transformer architecture: a Feature Aggregation Transformer (Grouped Query Attention with RMS Pre-Norm) ingests diffusion-generated multi-view images concatenated with SMPL-rendered side-view normals and Plücker camera embeddings, predicting per-pixel depth and 3D offsets to unproject pixels into initial Gaussian positions. A second Pixel-align Aggregation Transformer then reuses the attention weights from the first Transformer to perform weighted cross-attention over frozen DINOv2 features, propagating 2D semantic priors into a 3D-consistent feature field without requiring labeled 3D annotations. The 3D Gaussians carry both appearance attributes and a shared semantic embedding, so rendering and segmentation are jointly optimized via a multi-task loss combining LPIPS-weighted rendering error, cosine-similarity feature distillation on novel views, and cross-entropy segmentation on a small set of annotated viewpoints (500 scans × 8 views from a curated dataset of 40,000 images).

## Results

On the 2K2K dataset (512×512), HumanCrafter achieves PSNR 23.489, SSIM 0.916, LPIPS 0.045, and 6453.23 COLMAP points, outperforming the strongest baseline Human3Diffusion (PSNR 22.323, SSIM 0.882, LPIPS 0.053) by +1.17 dB in PSNR. On THuman2.1, it reaches PSNR 23.186 and SSIM 0.907, surpassing PSHuman (PSNR 20.853) by +2.33 dB. For 3D human-part segmentation on 2K2K (single-view), HumanCrafter achieves mIoU 0.801 and mAcc 0.882, beating the Human3Diffusion+Sapiens pipeline (mIoU 0.781, mAcc 0.851) while running ~3.7× faster (6.24 s vs. 23.21 s per object).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HumanCrafter outperforms all nine baselines on both reconstruction and segmentation metrics. In reconstruction (Table 2), it leads Human3Diffusion by +1.02 dB PSNR on THuman2.1 and +1.17 dB on 2K2K, while also achieving the highest SSIM (0.907/0.916) and lowest LPIPS (0.046/0.045), indicating superior perceptual quality. The general-purpose reconstruction models (LGM, GRM, InstantMesh, LaRa) trail by 2–5 dB in PSNR, confirming that human-specific geometric priors (SMPL normals) are critical for faithful human novel-view synthesis. In segmentation (Table 1), HumanCrafter achieves mIoU 0.840 with two GT views, surpassing LSM* (0.724) by +11.6 points and Sapiens (0.823) by +1.7 points; in the single-view setting it reaches mIoU 0.801 vs. 0.781 for the Human3Diffusion+Sapiens two-stage pipeline, while reducing per-object runtime from 23.21 s to 6.24 s. The 3D-consistency metric (#Points from COLMAP) further validates geometric fidelity, with HumanCrafter producing 6453 points on 2K2K versus 5134 for Human3Diffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation by augmenting each Gaussian primitive with learnable semantic feature embeddings, building directly on the feature-field loss formulation from LSM [31] and the pixel-wise 3DGS regression paradigm of LGM/GRM/GS-LRM. It departs from prior human-specific 3DGS methods (GPS-Gaussian, Human3Diffusion, PSHuman) that focus solely on appearance by introducing a joint reconstruction-and-segmentation objective, and from two-stage pipelines (reconstruct-then-segment with Sapiens) by unifying both tasks in a single feed-forward Transformer. The key architectural departure is the Pixel-Align Aggregation module, which reuses attention weights from the geometry Transformer to weight frozen DINOv2 features, effectively lifting a 2D self-supervised model into a 3D-consistent semantic field without per-scene optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework enables real-time (126 ms/object) 3D human reconstruction with body-part semantics from a single image, which is directly applicable to AR/VR character creation, semantic 3D editing, and cinematic production where per-scene optimization is infeasible. The demonstrated cross-task synergy—where the segmentation loss measurably improves reconstruction PSNR and vice versa—suggests that multi-task 3D representations can be more efficient than task-specific pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic 4D humans, whether the SMPL geometric prior limits generalizability to non-standard body shapes, and whether the 28-class taxonomy scales to finer-grained or open-vocabulary segmentation.
