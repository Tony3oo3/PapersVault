## Problem

The 1-peer exponential graph achieves finite-time convergence (maximizing the expected spectral gap) only when the number of nodes n is a power of two, and its efficiency for arbitrary n and the commutativity of its mixing matrices remain unexplored. The base-(k+1) graph extends finite-time convergence to any (n,k) but suffers from non-uniform node degrees and lacks commutativity, requiring strict synchronization. This paper addresses both gaps by revisiting the underlying principles of the exponential graph and designing new dynamic graphs that achieve finite-time convergence for any n while preserving commutativity and uniform degree.

## Input / Output

Given a number of nodes n and a maximum communication degree k (k≥2 for the null-cascade graph), the method produces a periodic sequence of τ doubly stochastic circulant mixing matrices, each specifying which k peers a node communicates with and the associated mixing weights. The periodic interval τ depends on the factorization of n: for the k-peer exponential graph τ=⌊log_{k+1}(n)⌋, while for the null-cascade graph τ=κ+Σ⌊ν_i/2⌋ over factors exceeding k. In the DSGD training loop, these matrices are applied cyclically to local model parameters (d-dimensional vectors) to produce the global averaged parameter used for evaluation.

## Method

The core insight is that finite-time convergence of a cascade of τ circulant mixing matrices is equivalent to isolating the DC component (zero frequency) and nullifying all non-zero frequency components, which can be analyzed via the DFT of the filter sequences defining each circulant matrix. The k-peer exponential graph generalizes the 1-peer case by using Moving Average Filters (MAFs) with (k+1) uniform weights and exponential expansion orders m_ℓ=(k+1)^ℓ, yielding a closed-form expected spectral gap p=1−max|sin(πi(k+1)^τ/n)/((k+1)^τ sin(πi/n))| that approaches 1 for any n. The null-cascade graph extends this by factorizing n=∏(ν_i+1) and applying MAFs (for factors ν_i≤k) followed by Steerable Nulling Filters (SNFs) with non-uniform weights derived from conjugate-pair polynomial roots (for factors ν_i>k), ensuring all non-zero frequencies are nullified while maintaining circulant structure, uniform degree, and commutativity for any n with k≥2.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (α=0.1, ResNet-18), the null-cascade graph achieves the highest test accuracy across all three (n,k) configurations: 86.60% at (15,2), 88.17% at (17,2), and 87.84% at (30,2). On CIFAR-100 under the same settings, it reaches 72.13%, 72.03%, and 73.29% respectively. The null-cascade graph achieves finite-time convergence (p=1) with periodic intervals τ=3, 8, and 4 for the three configurations, compared to τ=4, 5, and 5 for the base-(k+1) graph. In consensus error tests, both the null-cascade and base-(k+1) graphs drive error to zero after τ iterations, while the k-peer exponential graph shows small but non-zero residual error when n is not a power of k+1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The null-cascade graph (P2) outperforms all four baselines on test accuracy in 5 out of 6 configurations. Against the base-(k+1) graph (C2), which also achieves finite-time convergence, the null-cascade graph gains +1.91% on CIFAR-10 (15,2) (86.60% vs 84.69%) and +0.72% on CIFAR-100 (30,2) (73.29% vs 72.75%), attributed to its uniform degree property that avoids unbalanced parameter mixing. Against the (k+1)-partite random match graph (C4), the null-cascade graph leads by +1.04% on CIFAR-10 (15,2) and +0.38% on CIFAR-100 (30,2). The ring graph (C1) is consistently the weakest structured baseline, trailing the null-cascade by 4.5–8.8% on CIFAR-10. The random graph (C3) performs poorly, especially on CIFAR-100 where it drops below 20% accuracy. The k-peer exponential graph (P1) is competitive but never surpasses the null-cascade, confirming that finite-time convergence (p=1) provides a measurable advantage over near-optimal spectral gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 1-peer exponential graph [Ying et al., NeurIPS 2021]—which achieves finite-time convergence only when n is a power of two—by generalizing it to k-peer communication and providing a unified DSP-based analysis via Moving Average Filter frequency responses. It also builds on the base-(k+1) graph [Takezawa et al., NeurIPS 2024], which achieves finite-time convergence for any (n,k) but suffers from non-uniform degrees and non-commutative mixing matrices. The key conceptual departure is reframing finite-time convergence as a cascade of spatial filters that isolate the DC component while nullifying all non-zero frequencies, enabling the design of the null-cascade graph that combines MAFs with Steerable Nulling Filters to satisfy all three desirable properties (finite-time convergence, commutativity, uniform degree) simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The null-cascade graph provides the first dynamic graph construction that simultaneously guarantees finite-time convergence, commutativity, and uniform degree for any number of nodes n with k≥2, addressing a gap left by both the 1-peer exponential graph (limited n) and the base-(k+1) graph (non-uniform degree, non-commutativity). The DSP-based analytical framework (Theorem 2) gives practitioners a closed-form expression for the expected spectral gap of the k-peer exponential graph for any n, enabling informed graph selection. Limitations include the requirement k≥2 and degraded efficiency (larger τ) when n contains large prime factors, which remains an open design challenge. The practical impact is most relevant to settings with multicasting support where k-peer communication is feasible.
