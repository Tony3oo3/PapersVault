## Problem

Full-face appearance-based gaze estimation suffers from poor cross-domain generalization because the eye region occupies only a small fraction of the image, while the remaining pixels introduce domain-specific noise (lighting, skin tone, expression) that drives overfitting. Existing remedies operate at the 2D image-feature level and do not exploit the structured geometric decomposition of the face, leaving gaze entangled with appearance factors that vary across domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252255/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 224×224 RGB facial crop (normalized to [0,1]) and produces a unit 3D gaze vector as the final output. Internally, a multi-task encoder regresses five FLAME parameter groups — shape β∈ℝ¹⁰⁰, head pose θ∈ℝ⁶, expression ψ∈ℝ⁵⁰, a 256-dim gaze code γ∈ℝ²⁵⁶, and lighting l∈ℝ²⁷ — from which a 3D mesh is reconstructed to extract N 3D facial landmarks. Text prompts describing gaze direction and domain factors (derived from the extracted FLAME parameters) are fed to a frozen CLIP text encoder to supply semantic supervision.

## Method

The 3DPE-Gaze framework couples two complementary modules. The 3D Geometric Prior Module (3DGP) reuses the DECA ResNet encoder with all non-gaze branches (β, θ, ψ, l) frozen at their pre-trained weights, while a trainable MLP head learns the gaze code γ; the FLAME decoder then reconstructs a 3D mesh whose landmarks provide anatomically grounded structural constraints. The Semantic Concept Alignment Module (SCAM) programmatically generates CLIP text prompts for gaze direction and for each domain factor (lighting, expression, shape, pose) extracted by 3DGP, then applies a bidirectional contrastive loss — a gaze-concept alignment term pulling γ toward gaze descriptions and a domain-repulsion term pushing γ away from interference descriptions — to carve an explicit semantic boundary. A cross-attention predictor uses γ as Query and the 3D landmarks as Key/Value, followed by an MLP that outputs the normalized gaze vector. Training is staged: first the frozen FLAME branches are held fixed while γ is pre-trained with angular loss, then SCAM contrastive losses are introduced and the full model is jointly optimized with Adam (lr 10⁻⁴, batch 256) on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On four cross-domain gaze estimation tasks (ETH-XGaze→MPIIFaceGaze, ETH-XGaze→EyeDiap, Gaze360→MPIIFaceGaze, Gaze360→EyeDiap), 3DPE-Gaze achieves the lowest average angular error of 6.43°, outperforming the best SOTA method LG-Gaze (6.84°) by 0.41° and the CNN baseline (8.86°) by 2.43°. The method wins on 3 of 4 individual tasks, with the largest margin on ETH-XGaze→EyeDiap (6.13° vs. 6.96° for CLIP-Gaze). In-domain performance remains competitive, achieving 4.03° on MPIIFaceGaze and 5.06° on EyeDiap, matching or exceeding prior SOTA in those settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DPE-Gaze achieves the best average cross-domain error (6.43°), beating all seven compared methods. Against the closest SOTA, LG-Gaze [35] (6.84° avg), it improves by 0.41° on average, winning on three of four tasks (E→D: 6.13 vs 7.22; G→M: 6.71 vs 6.83; G→D: 6.23 vs 6.86) but trailing on E→M (6.66 vs 6.45). The method also outperforms CLIP-Gaze [36] (6.96°) and Liang et al. [18] (6.95°) on average, though Liang et al. retains a notable advantage on E→M (5.79° vs 6.66°), which the authors attribute to unstable 3D geometric priors from the MPIIFaceGaze dataset. Against earlier baselines—PureGaze [7] (8.28°), CDG [30] (7.25°), and Xu et al. [33] (7.63°)—the gains are more substantial (0.8–1.9°). In in-domain evaluation (Table 2), 3DPE-Gaze matches or exceeds GazeTR-Hybrid [5] on MPIIFaceGaze (4.03° vs 4.00°) and EyeDiap (5.06° vs 5.17°), confirming that the cross-domain gains do not come at the cost of within-domain accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FLAME parametric face model (Li et al., 2017) and the DECA encoder (Feng et al., 2021) to reframe gaze estimation as a 'structure-to-gaze' prediction problem rather than the conventional 'pixel-to-gaze' regression. It departs from prior cross-domain gaze methods (PureGaze, CDG, De-confounded Gaze) that treat geometric cues like head pose as auxiliary inputs, instead making a full parametric 3D decomposition the core representation. It also differs from CLIP-based gaze estimators (CLIP-Gaze, LG-Gaze) by applying semantic contrastive purification to an already geometrically decoupled gaze code, rather than to raw 2D image features — a 'secondary purification' strategy the authors explicitly contrast with prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled recipe for cross-domain gaze estimation that requires no target-domain data, which is practically important for deploying gaze trackers across diverse user populations and environments. The finding that a simple 3D landmark representation outperforms the full FLAME parameter set for generalization is a useful design insight: more detailed geometric parameters can reintroduce domain-specific noise. Key limitations remain — FLAME cannot model fine eyeball rotation, the semantic concept set is predefined rather than adaptive, and the method has not been validated for real-time or mobile deployment — leaving open questions about whether specialized parametric eye models and learned concept generation can close the remaining gap.
