## Problem

Existing self-knowledge distillation (SKD) methods rely on fixed or heuristically defined weights to prioritize features during knowledge transfer, which remain static throughout training and fail to adapt to the evolving importance of features as the model learns. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a mechanism that dynamically identifies and re-weights task-critical features in real time using gradient information, eliminating the need for auxiliary architectures or external teachers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253975/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet, and various sizes for fine-grained and segmentation datasets) and produces class labels, bounding boxes, or pixel-level segmentation masks depending on the downstream task. Unusually, the model also internally consumes its own previous mini-batch predictions as soft targets and the gradient magnitudes of intermediate feature maps as a dynamic weighting signal, meaning no external teacher network or additional data augmentation pipeline is required at inference time.

## Method

SDPGO combines two modules within a single self-distillation framework. The Sequential Iterative Learning Module splits each mini-batch into two halves aligned with the previous and next iteration, then computes a KL-divergence consistency loss between the current predictions and the softened predictions from the prior iteration, treating the evolving model as its own teacher. The Proximal Weight Assignment Module computes per-channel gradient magnitudes of intermediate feature maps, applies a proximal (soft-thresholding) operator with an adaptively updated threshold to enforce sparsity, normalizes the result via Z-score, and aggregates it into a dynamic scalar α that modulates the distillation loss weight in the total objective L_Task + α·L_SIL. This gradient-driven α shifts emphasis toward high-gradient (task-critical) features during high-gradient phases and recalibrates toward under-optimized features during low-gradient phases, providing real-time adaptation without additional parameters or architectural changes.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SDPGO achieves 96.44% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-32, surpassing the best baseline (MiSKD at 95.59%) by 0.85% and the strongest auxiliary-architecture method (FRSKD at 94.78%) by 1.66%. On CIFAR-100, SDPGO reaches 79.36% with WRN-20-8, outperforming FRSKD (78.95%) and DLB (79.21%). On ImageNet with ResNet-18, SDPGO attains 72.47% top-1 and 92.56% top-5 accuracy, exceeding the best feature-based KD method DCCD (71.95%) by 0.52% and the best SKD method FASD (71.70%) by 0.77%. On fine-grained CUB200, SDPGO achieves 78.06% top-1 with ResNet-18, a 2.63% gain over FASD and 6.95% over MiSKD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDPGO consistently outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every dataset and architecture tested. Against auxiliary-architecture SKD methods, SDPGO surpasses BYOT by 2.87% on CIFAR-10 (R-32) and 4.68% on CUB200 (R-18), and exceeds EFWSNet by 2.47% on CIFAR-10 (R-32) and 0.11% on ImageNet. Among data-augmentation-based SKD methods, SDPGO leads MiSKD by 0.85% on CIFAR-10 (R-32) and 6.95% on CUB200 (R-18), and FASD by 0.99% on CIFAR-10 (R-32) and 2.63% on CUB200. For historical-prediction-based methods, SDPGO beats DLB by 2.29% on CIFAR-10 (R-32) and 1.96% on CUB200, and PS-KD by 2.40% on CIFAR-10 (R-32) and 0.88% on ImageNet. On ImageNet, SDPGO (72.47% top-1) also exceeds conventional teacher-student KD methods including DCCD (71.95%), FitNet (71.61%), CAT-KD (71.22%), CRD (71.17%), SSKD (71.62%), and Review (70.81%), confirming its advantage over both offline and online distillation paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDPGO extends the self-knowledge distillation (SKD) lineage that began with BYOT and FRSKD (auxiliary-architecture approaches) and PS-KD/DLB (historical-prediction approaches), but departs from all of them by replacing static or heuristic feature weights with a proximal-gradient-driven dynamic weighting scheme. It also builds on recent gradient-guided distillation work [25, 63], which used gradients to assess feature importance, but introduces a soft-thresholding proximal operator and an adaptive sparsity threshold to make the weighting both sparse and temporally stable. The sequential iterative learning module (KL divergence between adjacent mini-batch halves) is conceptually related to DLB's last-mini-batch distillation but is reframed as a consistency-regularization loop that feeds back into a dynamic loss-balancing hyperparameter α.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SDPGO achieves consistent accuracy gains (up to ~6% on CIFAR-100, ~2.7% on ImageNet) with zero additional inference cost and no auxiliary networks, making it attractive for resource-constrained deployment. Its demonstrated robustness to 40% symmetric label noise and to 25% data scarcity suggests the gradient-weighted distillation signal acts as a form of implicit regularization beyond what standard KD provides. Open questions include whether the convexity/L-smoothness assumptions in the convergence proof can be relaxed for non-convex deep networks, how the method behaves under very small batch sizes where gradient estimates become noisy, and whether the gains persist at the scale of billion-parameter models where gradient magnitudes may be less discriminative.
