## Problem

Existing methods for distilling softmax attention into linear attention fix the feature dimension uniformly across all layers, ignoring the diverse functional roles and input distributions of different attention layers. The paper addresses the unresolved question of how to choose the feature dimension that governs approximation quality in a data-adaptive, layer-specific manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254615/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Transformer (GPT-2 or Pythia-1B), a set of text sequences (Wikipedia), a tolerance parameter λ, and a total computational budget C (set to the head size, 64 or 128). It produces layer-specific feature dimensions M_s that vary substantially across layers (e.g., ranging from 9 to 277 in Pythia-1B), trained PRF-based nonlinear feature maps with per-layer scaling coefficients, and a fully distilled linear attention model. The dimensions are allocated proportionally to each layer's estimated degrees of freedom while preserving the average inference cost equal to C.

## Method

The core insight is that the optimal feature dimension for approximating the attention kernel K(x,y)=exp(xᵀy/√d) is governed by the statistical degrees of freedom N*λ = tr(Σ(Σ+λI)⁻¹), which captures the effective dimensionality of the input distribution. Theorem 3 provides an L²(ρ⊗ρ) error bound showing that the required number of features M scales as N*λ log N*λ when sampling from the DoF-optimal density q_λ, rather than the ambient dimension d. Practically, Algorithm 1 estimates DoF per layer from a Gram matrix of sampled queries and keys, then allocates dimensions proportionally to DoF while matching the total budget. For feature learning, the paper introduces a layerwise training strategy using either L² kernel-approximation loss or softmax attention-weight-matching loss, which trains each layer's PRF features independently and reduces training cost by ~45% compared to end-to-end direct loss, while achieving comparable downstream accuracy.

## Results

On GPT-2, the proposed DoF-based dimension selection with softmax loss achieves 0.4190 average accuracy across seven downstream tasks, outperforming the fixed-dimension baseline (Fix: 0.4141 with direct loss) and both prior linear-attention baselines (DiJiang: 0.3409, Performer: 0.3952). The DoF+Clip variant with direct loss reaches 0.4223, nearly matching the original GPT-2 (0.4266) while maintaining the same inference cost. On Pythia-1B, DoF reduces the average performance degradation from the original model by 39% (−0.0161 vs. −0.0265 for Fix). Layerwise training with softmax loss is ~17% faster per sample than end-to-end direct training (90.4 vs. 109.4 ms/sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior linear-attention distillation methods: DiJiang (Chen et al., 2024), which uses quasi-Monte Carlo feature sampling with fixed dimensions, and Performer (Choromanski et al., 2021), which uses fixed-dimension PRF features. On GPT-2, the proposed DoF method (0.4190 average with softmax loss) substantially outperforms DiJiang (0.3409) by ~7.8 percentage points and Performer (0.3952) by ~2.4 points. Against the fixed-dimension baseline (Fix, 0.4141 with direct loss), DoF achieves a modest but consistent improvement, with the largest gains on ARC-E (+3.2 pts) and WSC (+2.9 pts). The DoF+Clip variant (capping dimensions at head size) further narrows the gap to the original GPT-2, reaching 0.4223 vs. 0.4266. On Pythia-1B, DoF (0.4118) outperforms Fix (0.4014) by 1.04 points on average, reducing the degradation from the original model by 39%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of kernel approximation theory (Bach, 2017) and the growing line of work distilling softmax attention into linear attention (Chen et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2024). While prior distillation methods fix the feature dimension uniformly across all layers, this paper introduces a data-driven, layer-specific dimension selection grounded in the statistical degrees of freedom of the attention kernel, building directly on Bach's (2017) optimal sampling density result. The feature representation uses Positive Random Features from the Performer (Choromanski et al., 2021), and the layerwise training strategy departs from the end-to-end distillation paradigm by training each layer's features independently with kernel-matching losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded recipe for allocating a fixed computational budget across attention layers, which is a practical concern for deploying linear attention in production. The empirical finding that effective dimensionality varies by an order of magnitude across layers (and even across heads within a layer) offers a concrete diagnostic for understanding where a Transformer's complexity is concentrated. Key limitations include the shared dimension across heads within a layer (despite large head-level DoF variation), evaluation only on moderate-scale language models (GPT-2, Pythia-1B), and the absence of statistical significance reporting. Open questions include whether head-level adaptive allocation or head pruning can further improve efficiency, and whether the approach scales to larger models and non-text modalities.
