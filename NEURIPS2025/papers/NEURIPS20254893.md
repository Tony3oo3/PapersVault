## Problem

State-of-the-art solutions for generative modeling (diffusion/flow models), unsupervised representation learning (contrastive loss), and classification (cross-entropy) remain largely disjoint, each with its own architecture, objective, and latent-space conventions. The paper asks whether a single principled framework can unify all three tasks, enabling them to share representations and benefit from one another rather than being trained in isolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254893/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 256×256 AFHQ-Cat/CelebA-HQ/LSUN-Bedroom) and discrete class labels, with Gaussian noise as the generative prior. Each data type is mapped into a shared Gaussian latent space via a type-specific encoder that produces a disjoint 'latent zone' (a subset of the Gaussian space), and a type-specific decoder maps latents back to data. Depending on the encoder-decoder composition, the system outputs a generated image (decoder alone or label encoder + image decoder), an image embedding for downstream tasks (image encoder alone), or a predicted class label (image encoder + label decoder).

## Method

LZN maintains a single shared Gaussian latent space partitioned into disjoint zones, one per sample, via two atomic operations. Latent computation maps each sample through a deterministic encoder to an anchor point, then applies flow matching (FM) to integrate from the anchor back to the Gaussian prior, yielding a latent zone whose support is disjoint from all other zones and whose marginal distribution is N(0,I). Latent alignment couples zones across data types by maximizing a soft assignment probability—computed from the Gaussian mixture components of the intermediate FM distribution—at a truncated set of late time steps, making the otherwise non-differentiable discrete zone assignment tractable. Tasks are expressed as compositions of these encoders and decoders (e.g., label encoder + image decoder for conditional generation, image encoder + label decoder for classification), and the entire system is trained end-to-end with the original generative loss plus the alignment objective, without introducing auxiliary contrastive or cross-entropy terms.

## Results

On unconditional image generation, RF+LZN improves FID on CIFAR-10 from 2.76 to 2.59 and reduces reconstruction error from 0.83 to 0.41, while also improving FID on AFHQ-Cat (6.08→5.68) and LSUN-Bedroom (6.25→5.95). On unsupervised representation learning (ImageNet linear probe with ResNet-50), LZN achieves 69.5% Top-1 and 89.3% Top-5 accuracy, outperforming MoCo (60.2%) by 9.3% and SimCLR (69.3%) by 0.2%. For joint conditional generation and classification on CIFAR-10, RF+LZN achieves 2.40 FID (vs. 2.47 for RF) and 94.47% classification accuracy, with both tasks improving over training each in isolation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In unsupervised representation learning (Table 2), LZN (69.5% Top-1) outperforms all contrastive baselines up to SimCLR (69.3%), beating MoCo by 9.3% and SimCLR by 0.2%, but trails the stronger MoCo v2 (71.7%), BYOL (74.3%), and DINO (75.3%) by 2.2–5.8%. The authors note this gap is expected since LZN training was not fully converged and used ResNet-50 rather than ViT. In unconditional generation (Table 1), RF+LZN consistently improves over RF: FID drops from 2.76→2.59 on CIFAR-10, 6.08→5.68 on AFHQ-Cat, and 6.25→5.95 on LSUN-Bedroom, with reconstruction error halved across all datasets. In conditional generation (Table 4), RF+LZN improves FID from 2.47 to 2.40 and reconstruction error from 0.69 to 0.38 on CIFAR-10. For classification (Table 3), RF+LZN achieves 94.47% on CIFAR-10, competitive with dedicated classifiers like MobileNetV2 (94.43%) and ResNeXt29 (94.73%), though slightly below the best DLA (95.47%); notably, joint training with generation (94.47%) outperforms classification-only training (93.59%), demonstrating cross-task synergy.

## Relation to Prior Work

LZN builds on flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as its core mechanism for computing latent zones, and on rectified flow (Liu et al., 2022) as the generative decoder. Conceptually, it departs from three separate lineages: contrastive representation learning (MoCo, SimCLR, BYOL, DINO), which imposes no distributional constraint on embeddings; standard generative models, which treat conditions as separate inputs to the decoder; and classification, which treats labels as model outputs rather than data. The paper positions LZN as orthogonal to AR transformers/LLMs, arguing that LZN's unified latent space offers a complementary formulation where all data types (images, labels, text) are connected through a single Gaussian-prior space partitioned into disjoint zones, rather than being tokenized into a single sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core contribution is a principled unification: if LZN scales, it could replace three separate training pipelines (generative, contrastive, classification) with one shared latent space, enabling cross-task synergy by design. The demonstration that joint generation+classification training improves both tasks over isolated training is a concrete validation of this synergy hypothesis. However, significant open questions remain: training efficiency is a bottleneck (backprop through FM trajectories), representation learning lags SoTA (DINO) by ~6 points, only the image domain and at most two tasks are tested, and the quadratic growth in supported applications with more encoder-decoder pairs is unverified. Whether the framework scales gracefully to multi-modality and many simultaneous tasks is the key unresolved question.
