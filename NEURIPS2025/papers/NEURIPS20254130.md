## Problem

Existing real-world cooperative perception datasets are constrained to a single intersection or a single vehicle, preventing fair assessment of how cooperative perception algorithms generalize across diverse urban layouts. Without multi-vehicle, multi-infrastructure data spanning multiple intersections, models risk overfitting to specific geometry and traffic patterns, yielding misleadingly high performance that does not transfer to new environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254130/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each 20-second sequence provides temporally aligned and spatially calibrated data from two vehicles (6 FHD RGB cameras at 20 FPS, one 128-ray LiDAR at 20 FPS, RTK-corrected IMU at 1 cm accuracy) and up to three infrastructure poles (1–3 VGA thermal cameras at 30 FPS, 64-ray and 32-ray LiDARs at 20 FPS per pole), all referenced to a shared global ENU coordinate frame. Annotations are 3D bounding boxes at 10 Hz across 13 object classes with per-sequence tracking IDs and six attribute types, yielding ~712k instances. For the cooperative detection benchmark, one ego agent's LiDAR is combined with collaborator agents' LiDAR via fusion strategies, and detection is evaluated at IoU thresholds of 0.3 and 0.5 within a ±100 m × ±40 m range, requiring at least 5 LiDAR points per object.

## Method

The core contribution is a large-scale real-world dataset (34 sequences, 3 intersections, 2 vehicles + up to 3 infrastructure poles) with rigorous UTC/PTP synchronization, phase-locked LiDAR, hardware-triggered camera capture, and cone-target-based extrinsic calibration minimizing reprojection error. For benchmarking, the paper introduces two novel split strategies: Equal Intersection Split (EIS), which distributes sequences proportionally across all three intersections with three randomized non-overlapping splits, and Separate Intersection Split (SIS), a leave-one-out scheme where an entire intersection is held out for testing to measure true generalization to unseen geometry. Five SOTA cooperative perception methods (F-Cooper, AttFuse, V2X-ViT, Where2Comm, CoBEVT) with a PointPillars backbone are evaluated under no-fusion, early-fusion, late-fusion, and intermediate-fusion strategies. The key empirical finding is a 14.0 mAP@0.5 drop from EIS to SIS, exposing a significant generalization gap that existing cooperative perception architectures fail to bridge across unseen intersection layouts.

## Results

On the SIS_{1/2 vs.3} split (unseen intersection), AttFuse achieves the best overall mAP@0.5 of 25.5, followed by CoBEVT (24.9) and F-Cooper (24.2). All intermediate fusion methods substantially outperform no fusion (18.3 mAP@0.5), early fusion (22.8), and late fusion (11.1). The generalization gap is stark: on known intersections (EIS_avg), CoBEVT reaches 38.2 mAP@0.5, but drops to 24.2 mAP@0.5 on average across unseen-intersection splits—a 14.0 mAP@0.5 degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttFuse [30] achieves the best overall mAP@0.5 (25.5) on the unseen-intersection split, edging out CoBEVT [28] (24.9) by 0.6 points and F-Cooper [6] (24.2) by 1.3 points. Where2Comm [13] (23.8) and V2X-ViT [29] (22.5) trail further behind, with V2X-ViT underperforming even early fusion (22.8) on mAP@0.5. All five intermediate fusion methods clearly surpass no fusion (18.3) and late fusion (11.1), confirming that feature-level cooperation is essential; late fusion's poor performance highlights the difficulty of associating agent-specific object lists. On the known-intersection EIS split (Table 6), V2X-ViT [29] reverses its ranking and achieves the highest mAP@0.5 (33.8), while CoBEVT [28] is second (33.2), suggesting method rankings are sensitive to intersection familiarity. The 14.0 mAP@0.5 gap between EIS_avg (38.2) and average SIS (24.2) for CoBEVT underscores that no current method generalizes well to entirely unseen intersection layouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the cooperative perception dataset landscape by being the first real-world V2X dataset to simultaneously feature multiple vehicles, multiple infrastructure sensor poles, and multiple distinct intersection layouts. Prior datasets each cover only a subset of these dimensions: DAIR-V2X-C and V2X-Seq offer many intersections but only one vehicle; V2X-Real has two vehicles but a single intersection; TUMTraf-V2X provides infrastructure data at one crossing. UrbanIng-V2X repurposes the existing SOTA cooperative detection methods (F-Cooper, AttFuse, V2X-ViT, Where2Comm, CoBEVT) as benchmark baselines rather than proposing new architectures, and introduces novel evaluation splits (Equal Intersection Split and Separate Intersection Split) to properly stress-test generalization across unseen intersection geometries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.0 mAP@0.5 performance drop when evaluating on an unseen intersection (SIS) versus known intersections (EIS) exposes a critical generalization gap in current cooperative perception methods that single-intersection benchmarks have masked. This finding is practically important because real-world deployment requires robustness across diverse urban layouts, and the dataset provides the first controlled setting to measure this. Key limitations include the restriction to three intersections in one German city, absence of adverse weather conditions, and a relatively short total recording duration (~11 minutes per agent). Open questions include whether the generalization gap can be closed with domain adaptation, synthetic-to-real transfer (supported by the provided CARLA digital twin), or data-efficient learning approaches.
