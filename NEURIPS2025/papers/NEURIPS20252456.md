## Problem

In positive-unlabeled (PU) learning, the widely used non-negative risk estimator (nnPU) clips the negative-class empirical risk at zero, causing the negative class to be over-emphasized during training. This leads to consistently lower false positive rates than false negative rates, producing imbalanced classification error rates between the two classes. No existing PU risk estimator explicitly enforces balance between positive and negative class errors.

## Input / Output

The method operates under the two-sample PU setting: positive labeled samples drawn from the positive-class conditional distribution and unlabeled samples drawn from the full population, along with a known class prior π. Inputs are grayscale images (Fashion-MNIST, 28×28), RGB images (CIFAR-10, 3×32×32; Alzheimer, 3×224×224), and the output is a binary class label (+1/−1) for each test sample. The classifier is trained via stochastic mini-batches drawn jointly from the positive and unlabeled sets.

## Method

DC-PU replaces nnPU's non-negativity constraint max{0, R̂⁻ᵤ − πR̂⁻ₚ} with a dynamic lower bound max{ω, R̂⁻ᵤ − πR̂⁻ₚ}, where ω is an exponential moving average of (1−π)R̂⁺ₚ that tracks the current positive-class risk, ensuring the negative-class risk cannot fall below the positive-class risk. Additionally, DC-PU imposes an explicit equality constraint R̂⁺ₚ = (R̂⁻ᵤ − πR̂⁻ₚ)/(1−π) to force the two class risks to match. Training is performed via the augmented Lagrange multiplier framework: the equality constraint is incorporated as a quadratic penalty with a Lagrange parameter Θ updated each iteration, while the max operator is handled with a rollback gradient strategy. Theoretical analysis shows DC-PU is a positively biased but minimax-optimal estimator with convergence rate O(1/√nₚ + 1/√nᵤ), and the bias term decays exponentially with sample size.

## Results

DC-PU achieves the best final (last-5-epoch) Micro-F1 across all four benchmark datasets: 88.46% on CIFAR-10-1, 87.03% on CIFAR-10-2, 89.97% on F-MNIST-1, and 86.44% on F-MNIST-2, outperforming the next-best baseline (nnPU) by 2.2–2.7 points. On the real-world Alzheimer dataset, DC-PU reaches 70.55% Micro-F1 and 69.99% Macro-F1 (last), while also achieving the lowest GAP between FNR and FPR (10.79 vs. 16.49 for nnPU), confirming its class-balance advantage. With the Dist-PU-inspired regularization added (DC-PU+), the method reaches 92.73% Micro-F1 on F-MNIST-1, the highest among all compared PU learning methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-PU outperforms all five risk-estimator baselines (uPU, nnPU, abs-PU, Dist-PU*, FOPU*) on every "last" Micro-F1 and Macro-F1 metric across all four benchmark datasets, with margins of 1.7–2.7 points over nnPU and 2.4–4.8 points over Dist-PU*. uPU is the weakest baseline, collapsing to 41–61% Micro-F1 (last) due to overfitting from its unconstrained negative-risk term. Among practical PU methods (Table 5 with regularization), DC-PU+ achieves the highest Micro-F1 on CIFAR-10-1 (89.54%), CIFAR-10-2 (88.99%), and F-MNIST-1 (92.73%), but is marginally outperformed by P3Mix-E (89.50% vs. 89.41%) on F-MNIST-2. LaGAM shows high variance (±1.4 on CIFAR-10-2) and drops to 80.88% on F-MNIST-2, while MIXPUL and PULNS trail DC-PU+ by 2–5 points on most datasets. The GAP metric on Alzheimer further confirms DC-PU's balance advantage: 10.79 (last) vs. 16.49 for nnPU and 17.55 for uPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PU learning risk estimator lineage from uPU (unbiased) through nnPU (non-negative constraint) to a dual-constrained formulation. The key departure from nnPU is replacing the fixed lower bound of 0 on the negative-class risk term with a dynamically updated lower bound ω tied to the positive-class risk, and adding an explicit equality constraint between the two class risks, solved via an augmented Lagrange multiplier framework. The theoretical analysis (bias, consistency, estimation error via McDiarmid's inequality and Rademacher complexity) follows the proof structure established in nnPU but adapts it to the new constrained objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete imbalance problem in nnPU where the negative class is systematically over-played, which is practically relevant for PU applications like outlier detection where both error types matter. However, the absolute F1 improvements over nnPU are modest (1–3 points), and the method introduces additional hyperparameters (τ, β, γ) and training complexity. Open questions include whether the balancing constraint is always desirable when the true class priors are highly imbalanced, how DC-PU interacts with the one-sample PU setting, and whether the equality constraint generalizes to multi-class or multi-label PU formulations.
