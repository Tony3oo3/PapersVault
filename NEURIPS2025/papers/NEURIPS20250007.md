## Problem

Existing text-based 3D editing methods apply 2D diffusion models independently to each rendered viewpoint, producing multi-view inconsistencies such as the Janus problem (e.g., a panda's face appearing on the bear's back). These artifacts originate in the diffusion model's intermediate features, meaning that enforcing consistency only at the final image level is insufficient. The paper addresses the lack of a mechanism to inject 3D-consistent structural guidance directly into the 2D editing process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250007/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting scene and a free-form text editing instruction (e.g., 'Turn a stone bear into a panda'). It renders 20 views of the source scene, edits them via InstructPix2Pix with the 3D feature field injected at intermediate layers, and then re-optimizes the 3DGS parameters (1000 iterations, LPIPS + L1 loss) against the edited images to produce the final edited 3DGS. The 3D diffusion feature field itself is trained on the source scene before editing begins, using diffusion features extracted at multiple timesteps.

## Method

DFFSplat builds a 3D-consistent diffusion feature field on 3DGS that stores only view-independent structural information, avoiding the blurriness caused by averaging view-dependent appearance across viewpoints. A dual-encoder autoencoder decomposes timestep-conditioned diffusion features into a structure latent (E_str) and an appearance latent (E_app); the structure encoder is regularized with DINOv2 self-similarity map matching (L_str) and a time-invariance loss (L_t-sim) that enforces consistency across diffusion timesteps. During editing, the rendered 3D structural features are fused with the appearance encoder's output from the in-progress edit and decoded back into diffusion features, which replace the model's native features at selected intermediate layers of InstructPix2Pix. An additional 'view-consistency guidance' term is appended to the standard classifier-free guidance, weighting the structural condition separately from the image and text conditions to jointly enforce geometric coherence and prompt fidelity.

## Results

On 5 scenes from IN2N and DreamEditor, DFFSplat achieves a CLIP similarity of 25.11 and CLIP directional similarity of 18.15, outperforming all four baselines on both text-fidelity metrics. The method also matches the fastest editing time (~4 min) shared with DGE, while VcEdit requires ~20 min. In correspondence matching (lower is better), DFFSplat achieves the lowest distortion scores of 3.93 (Bear) and 5.53 (Dog), indicating the best preservation of source geometry and multi-view consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFFSplat outperforms all four baselines on both CLIP similarity and CLIP directional similarity. The closest competitor is DGE (24.66 CLIP, 17.21 CLIP Dir.), which DFFSplat surpasses by 0.45 and 0.94 points respectively, while matching its ~4 min editing time. GaussianEditor (24.40, 16.67) and VcEdit (23.18, 14.03) trail further behind, with VcEdit also being the slowest at ~20 min. GaussCtrl lags the most on text fidelity (22.87, 13.17). In the correspondence matching evaluation (Table 3), DFFSplat achieves the lowest distortion on both the Bear (3.93) and Dog (5.53) scenes, confirming superior structural preservation compared to DGE (4.14, 7.36) and all other baselines, directly addressing the Janus problem that plagues the other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DFFSplat extends the text-based 3D editing pipeline (InstructPix2Pix + 3DGS optimization) by introducing a 3D-consistent diffusion feature field, building on the feature-field training paradigm from LangSplat, Feature 3DGS, and Feature Splatting, and the feature-injection mechanism from Plug-and-Play diffusion. The key departure from prior 3D editing methods (GaussianEditor, DGE, GaussCtrl, VcEdit) is that instead of editing each view independently and hoping for consistency, DFFSplat renders a shared 3D structural feature into the 2D diffusion model's intermediate layers, explicitly enforcing geometric and semantic coherence. The dual-encoder decomposition (structure vs. appearance) and the time-invariance loss are novel architectural choices not present in prior feature-field or 3D editing work, and the view-consistency guidance term extends the standard classifier-free guidance formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Janus problem—frontal-view semantics leaking onto non-frontal views—is a persistent and visually egregious failure mode in 3D editing that prior methods have only partially addressed (e.g., DGE limits consistency to adjacent views, GaussCtrl/VcEdit enforce geometric but not semantic consistency). DFFSplat's correspondence-matching results (notably 5.53 vs. 7.36 on the dog scene) suggest a meaningful reduction in structural distortion, which matters for downstream applications like film and game production. However, the method is tightly coupled to InstructPix2Pix's editing scope and is explicitly unsuitable for large geometric transformations, leaving open the question of whether the 3D feature-field injection strategy generalizes to more flexible or geometry-aware editing backbones.
