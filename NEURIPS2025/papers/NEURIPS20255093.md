## Problem

Prior theoretical characterizations of low-rank gradient structure in two-layer networks relied on idealized assumptions— isotropic or well-conditioned data covariance, independent data and weight matrices, and a single rank-one dominant direction. This paper asks how the low-rank gradient structure changes under realistic anisotropic, ill-conditioned spiked data, and how common regularizers (weight decay, input noise, Jacobian penalty) selectively modulate the resulting gradient components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255093/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a two-layer network f(x) = γ_m a^T σ(Wx) trained on n samples from a spiked Gaussian model Σ = Σ̂ + n^{2ν} qq^T (with bulk spectral decay λ_k = k^{-α}), and produces an explicit decomposition of the gradient G = ∇_W L into three rank-one terms (S1 residue-aligned, S2 data-spike-aligned, S12 interpolant) plus a bulk error E, with high-probability norm bounds that determine which terms dominate. The framework covers both NTK (γ_m ~ 1/√m) and MF (γ_m ~ 1/m) scalings, arbitrary C² or ReLU activations, and allows W to depend on X to track training dynamics beyond initialization.

## Method

The core result (Theorems 3.1–3.2) shows that the gradient is generically well-approximated by a rank-two matrix S1 + S12 + S2, where S1 ∝ X_B^T r (a∘μ)^T captures the bulk data-residue direction and S2 ∝ q·z^T[(ra^T)∘σ_⊥'(XW^T)] captures the data-spike direction, with S12 interpolating between them. The relative prominence of each component is governed by the spike magnitude ν, bulk decay α, residue-spike alignment β, and the activation function: for ν < 1/4 the gradient is approximately rank-one (residue-dominated), for ν ∈ [1/4, 1/2) it becomes rank-two, and for ν ≥ 1/2 the data spike dominates. The paper further shows that ReLU's non-smoothness inflates the bulk error and suppresses S1, isotropic input noise (τ² = n^ρ) promotes S1 while suppressing S2, and Jacobian penalization suppresses S1 while promoting a new data-aligned spike S3. These theoretical predictions are validated on synthetic spiked data and real datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 embeddings) by examining the singular value spectrum of the gradient.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result (Theorems 3.1 and 3.2) shows that the inner-layer gradient G of a two-layer network on spiked data is generically well-approximated by a rank-two matrix S₁ + S₁₂ + S₂, where S₁ is aligned with the bulk data-residue X_B^T r and S₂ is aligned with the data spike direction q. For small spikes (ν < 1/4), the gradient collapses to approximately rank-one dominated by S₁; for moderate spikes (ν ∈ [1/4, 1/2)) it is rank-two; and for large spikes (ν ≥ 1/2) it is rank-one aligned with the data spike. On real data, MNIST (ν ≈ 0.784 > 1/2) exhibits a data-spike-dominated gradient as predicted by Theorem 3.2, while CIFAR-10 embeddings (ν ≈ 0.3572 < 1/2, α ≈ 0.6) show a prominent residue-aligned spike S₁, with Jacobian regularization (λ = 10⁵) suppressing S₁ and promoting the data-aligned component.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a standard empirical benchmark comparison; the `compares_to` list is empty. Instead, the paper positions its contributions as generalizations of prior theoretical results: Theorem 3.1 extends Ba et al. (NeurIPS 2022) [3] by relaxing isotropic data/weight assumptions to anisotropic, ill-conditioned spiked covariances and covering both MF and NTK scalings; Theorem 3.2 generalizes Ba et al. (NeurIPS 2023) [4] by removing the requirement that targets align with the spike direction q. The paper also identifies what it considers an error in Moniri et al. (2024) [28], whose NTK-scaling prediction of gradient alignment with X_B^T y or ω is contradicted by both theory (Theorem 3.1 predicts alignment with X_B^T r) and empirical measurements (Figure 4). Empirically, the paper validates its predictions against synthetic spiked data across multiple activations (Tanh, Swish, Softplus, ReLU, ELU, Sigmoid) and losses (MSE, BCE, Hinge), and confirms the regularization effects (weight decay, input noise, Jacobian penalty) on both synthetic and real datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 embeddings).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on low-rank gradient structure in two-layer networks initiated by Ba et al. [3] (rank-one under isotropic data) and Ba et al. [4] (spiked covariance with single-index targets), by relaxing isotropy to allow anisotropic, ill-conditioned bulk covariances and by removing the independence requirement between data and weights. It also generalizes the NTK-scaling analysis of Moniri et al. [28], which the authors argue contains an erroneous alignment prediction. The central conceptual departure is the identification of a generically rank-TWO gradient structure (residue spike S₁ plus data-spike S₂ connected by an interpolant S₁₂), rather than the rank-one structure found in all prior specialized settings, along with a systematic analysis of how activation functions and regularizers (weight decay, input noise, Jacobian penalty) selectively modulate these components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rank-two gradient structure provides a more realistic and nuanced account of feature learning than the rank-one narrative dominant in prior theory, and the regularization analysis reveals that common techniques like input noise can rescue a suppressed residue spike under ReLU while Jacobian penalties promote data-aligned learning — practical insights for practitioners tuning training. The MF-versus-NTK distinction in gradient alignment (target-aligned vs. residue-aligned) has direct implications for understanding why different scaling regimes produce different learned features. Open questions include extending the analysis beyond two layers, characterizing the phase transition from residue to data-spike alignment observed during training at intermediate spike sizes, and understanding how these gradient structures evolve over longer training horizons rather than a single step.
