## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods for X-ray imaging (X-Gaussian, R2-Gaussian) inherit representations from visible-light 3DGS, where Gaussian ellipsoids have density that fades toward the periphery and rendering accumulates multi-directional contributions. This fails to capture the fundamental physics of X-ray imaging, where rays penetrate objects in straight lines and attenuate proportionally to material density times path length through each homogeneous substance. The result is an inability to faithfully model distinct material compositions and their boundaries within internal structures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 5 or 10 sparse X-ray projections (512×512, captured over 0°–180° via the TIGRE toolbox with minor scatter and electronic noise) and produces novel X-ray views at arbitrary angles, which can then be fed into ASD_POCS (100 views) to reconstruct a full CT volume. All geometric computations are performed in Normalized Device Coordinates (NDC) space, where the orthographic projection property allows the 3D ellipsoid to be reduced to a 2D ellipse for segment-length evaluation. Training takes approximately 4 minutes on a single RTX 6000 Ada GPU with up to 500,000 ellipsoids.

## Method

X-Field replaces the Gaussian-fading density of 3DGS with homogeneous ellipsoids, each assigned a single non-negative attenuation coefficient σᵢ, so that pixel intensity is computed as a finite Riemann sum of σᵢ·lᵢ over the ray path per the Beer-Lambert law. The key technical contribution is an explicit closed-form derivation of segment length lᵢ for each ellipsoid along the view direction, expressed as l_max × √(1 − (C−B²)/A) where A, B, C are quadratic forms of the covariance inverse and displacement vector. A first-pass precedence strategy (Algorithm 1) resolves overlapping ellipsoids by assigning shared regions to the nearer ellipsoid in depth order, and an Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) via eigenvalue decomposition replaces the coarse AABB for pixel-ellipsoid association. Initialization uses a hybrid progressive pipeline (CGLS → SART → TV regularization) to produce a geometrically informed point cloud, and a material-based optimization step splits ellipsoids along boundaries identified by k-nearest-neighbor density and local attenuation gradient analysis.

## Results

On the X3D dataset, X-Field achieves PSNR of 35.71 dB and SSIM of 0.980 on Human Organ 10-view NVS, surpassing the SOTA R2-Gaussian (33.72 dB, 0.967) by 1.99 dB in PSNR. On Daily Object 10-view, X-Field reaches 42.80 dB PSNR and 45.64 LPIPS*, outperforming R2-Gaussian (41.93 dB, 54.31 LPIPS*). For sparse-view CT reconstruction from 10 input views, X-Field combined with ASD_POCS achieves 33.26 dB PSNR, a 3.98 dB improvement over R2-Gaussian (29.28 dB). The method trains in ~4 minutes on a single RTX 6000 Ada GPU and renders at 45 fps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-Field outperforms the closest SOTA competitor R2-Gaussian on PSNR across all four NVS settings (e.g., 35.71 vs 33.72 dB on Human Organ 10-view; 37.41 vs 34.52 dB on Daily Object 5-view) and on LPIPS* in every setting, though R2-Gaussian retains a slight SSIM edge on Daily Object 10-view (0.986 vs 0.983). Against the 3DGS-based X-Gaussian, X-Field gains 12.83 dB PSNR on Human Organ 10-view (35.71 vs 22.88) and 17.10 dB on Daily Object 5-view (37.41 vs 20.31), reflecting the benefit of explicit segment-length attenuation modeling over simple Gaussian superposition. NeRF-based methods (SAX-NeRF, NAF, NeAT, TensoRF) trail by 13–20 dB in PSNR, confirming that explicit ellipsoid representations are substantially more effective for X-ray than implicit neural fields. For CT reconstruction from 10 sparse views, X-Field achieves 33.26 dB PSNR versus R2-Gaussian's 29.28 dB (a 3.98 dB gain), though R2-Gaussian reports higher SSIM (0.946 vs 0.911), suggesting X-Field trades some structural similarity for higher peak fidelity. Traditional methods FDK and SART are far behind all learning-based approaches, with PSNR below 20 dB in all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-Field extends the 3DGS-based X-ray reconstruction lineage (3DGS → X-Gaussian → R2-Gaussian) by replacing the Gaussian density falloff with homogeneous ellipsoids carrying constant attenuation coefficients, grounded in the Beer-Lambert law. The critical departure is the explicit segment-length computation that tracks how far an X-ray traverses each material, a factor absent in prior Gaussian-splatting X-ray methods that simply superimpose ellipsoids. The hybrid progressive initialization (CGLS → SART → TV) also departs from the SfM-based or random initialization strategies used by X-Gaussian and R2-Gaussian, which fail under X-ray's attenuation-dependent appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling high-quality reconstruction from as few as 5–10 projections, X-Field directly addresses the clinical need to reduce patient radiation exposure while maintaining diagnostic fidelity. The ablation isolating segment length as the dominant component (7.6 dB PSNR drop when removed) validates the physical motivation over purely geometric optimization. Open questions remain: whether ellipsoids are the most parameter-efficient representation for heterogeneous materials, whether large medical model priors could further accelerate convergence, and whether the framework generalizes to polyenergetic or spectral CT settings.
