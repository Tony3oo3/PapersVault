## Problem

In realizable continual linear regression under random task orderings, the worst-case expected loss after k iterations has a known lower bound of Ω(1/k), but the best prior upper bound using an unregularized scheme is only O(1/k^{1/4}), leaving a significant gap. This paper asks whether standard regularization techniques—explicit isotropic ℓ₂ penalties or implicit regularization via finite step budgets—can close this gap, and if so, what regularization schedules are needed.

## Input / Output

The method takes M jointly realizable linear regression tasks (X_m, y_m) with data radius R = max_m ‖X_m‖₂, a random with-replacement task ordering τ, and either a sequence of regularization coefficients (λ_t) or inner step counts and sizes (N_t, γ_t). It produces a weight vector w_k after k iterations, with the primary analytical output being a bound on the expected average loss E_τ L(w_k). The analysis also extends to the seen-task loss L_{1:k}(w_k), which measures performance on previously encountered tasks rather than the full task collection.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a unified reduction showing that both explicit ℓ₂ regularization (solving a proximal subproblem per task) and implicit regularization (performing N_t gradient steps on the unregularized task loss) are equivalent to a single Incremental Gradient Descent step on a quadratic surrogate loss f^{(t)}(w; m) = ½‖√A_m^{(t)}(w − X_m⁺y_m)‖², where the matrix A_m^{(t)} encodes the regularization strength. This reduction makes the surrogate's smoothness tunable via the regularization parameter, which is the key lever for tightening last-iterate SGD analysis. For a fixed coefficient λ = R²(ln k − 1), the paper recovers a near-optimal O(log k / k) rate by instantiating existing SGD bounds. For the optimal O(1/k) rate, the paper introduces a novel last-iterate bound for SGD on time-varying objectives (Lemma 4.7) and applies it with an increasing schedule λ_t = (13R²/3)·(k+1)/(k−t+2), which progressively reduces parameter updates and matches the Ω(1/k) lower bound.

## Results

Under random with-replacement task orderings over jointly realizable linear regression tasks, the paper proves that a fixed regularization strength (λ = R²(ln k − 1)) yields a near-optimal worst-case expected loss of O(log k / k), improving upon the previous best unregularized bound of O(1/k^{1/4}) from Evron et al. [14]. More importantly, an increasing regularization schedule (λ_t = (13R²/3)·(k+1)/(k−t+2)) achieves the optimal rate of O(1/k), matching the known Ω(1/k) lower bound for any continual learning scheme. The same O(1/k) rate extends to the seen-task loss metric, and a matching lower bound is shown for isotropic regularization under any coefficient sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against Evron et al. [14], which established the best prior dimensionality-independent upper bound of O(1/k^{1/4}) for unregularized continual linear regression under random orderings. The paper's fixed-regularization result (O(log k / k)) and increasing-regularization result (O(1/k)) both strictly improve upon this, with the latter closing the gap to the Ω(1/k) lower bound. Against Cai & Diakonikolas [5], which provides O(M√(log(k/M))/k) for cyclic orderings with fixed explicit regularization, the paper's results are complementary: they target random orderings (where [5] has no guarantee) and achieve a rate independent of M. Against Evron et al. [12] and Kong et al. [26], which provide unregularized bounds under cyclic orderings, the paper's random-ordering results are in a different regime but demonstrate that regularization is the key ingredient for optimal rates. The paper also establishes a new lower bound (Lemma 4.9) showing that no isotropic regularization schedule can beat O(1/k) for seen-task loss, confirming the optimality of their increasing schedule in that metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Evron et al. [14], which reduced unregularized continual linear regression to SGD on a fixed-smoothness surrogate and achieved O(1/k^{1/4}) under random orderings. The key departure is showing that both explicit L2 regularization and implicit regularization via finite step budgets reduce to incremental gradient descent on *tunable-smoothness* surrogate losses, giving the analyst a new degree of freedom. Prior theoretical work on regularized continual regression was restricted to two tasks (Li et al. [28,29]), required commutativity of task covariances (Zhao et al. [48]), or analyzed only cyclic orderings (Cai & Diakonikolas [5]); this paper handles arbitrary task collections under random orderings. The paper also introduces a new last-iterate SGD bound for time-varying objectives (Lemma 4.7), which is the analytical engine behind the optimal-rate result.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes the gap between the O(1/k^{1/4}) upper bound and the Ω(1/k) lower bound for realizable continual linear regression under random orderings, showing that a simple increasing regularization schedule achieves the optimal O(1/k) rate. This provides a clear theoretical prescription: regularization strength should increase over time (or equivalently, the step budget per task should decrease), which aligns with biological observations of attenuating synaptic plasticity. Key open questions remain: whether regularization is fundamentally necessary for optimal rates or merely a convenient analytical device, whether the results extend to nonlinear or non-realizable settings, and whether the predicted schedules actually help on standard continual learning benchmarks (the paper provides no such experiments). The purely theoretical scope—linear regression, joint realizability, random orderings—limits immediate practical impact but establishes a clean baseline for future work.
