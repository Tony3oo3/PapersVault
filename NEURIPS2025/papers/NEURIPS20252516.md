## Problem

In source-free domain adaptation, the pre-trained source model produces unreliable pseudo labels on target domain data, especially in early training, leading to noisy supervision and unstable convergence. Existing methods that leverage CLIP for pseudo label refinement (e.g., DIFO) are limited by CLIP's frozen vision encoder, which yields low-quality image features with poor inter-class separability under large domain gaps, and by standard mutual information losses that fail to dynamically reflect the model's changing prediction quality during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained source model (ResNet-50 or ResNet-101), unlabeled target domain RGB images, and a CLIP ViT-B/32 model whose text encoder is fixed with prompts in the format 'a photo of [class]'. Training is organized into cycles: at the cycle level, pseudo labels are generated and the CLIP vision encoder is fine-tuned (≤8 update steps per cycle); at the iteration level, the target model is trained with fixed hard pseudo labels over multiple iterations. The output is an adapted target model producing per-class probability predictions on target domain images.

## Method

DUET introduces a dual-perspective pseudo labeling strategy that assigns hard pseudo labels only to target samples where the task-specific target model and the domain-invariant CLIP model agree on their argmax predictions, while averaging their soft predictions to form a mixed label for CLIP optimization. For CLIP vision encoder adaptation, the standard MI loss is replaced with a Tsallis mutual information (TMI) whose entropy index ψ is dynamically regulated by an Uncertainty-based Adaptation Index (UAI) computed via EMA of the batch-normalized CLIP prediction entropy: ψ > 1 flattens the entropy curve to encourage exploration of uncertain samples early in training, while ψ < 1 sharpens it to promote exploitation of confident samples later. The target model is trained via a PLMatch framework combining cross-entropy on hard pseudo labels, consistency training (KL divergence between weakly and strongly augmented views), and CLIP-guided knowledge distillation (KL divergence between target model and CLIP predictions), with all three losses weighted by fixed coefficients (α=0.4, β=0.2, γ=0.4).

## Results

DUET achieves 84.7% average accuracy on Office-Home (ResNet-50) and 91.4% on VisDA-C (ResNet-101), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art DIFO by +1.6% and +1.4% respectively. On DomainNet-126 (ResNet-50), the method reaches 82.9% average accuracy, a +2.9% improvement over DIFO (80.0%). Gains are most pronounced in visually challenging domains such as Clipart (A→C: 73.6% vs. DIFO's 70.6%) and Sketch (C→S: 79.0% vs. 74.9%), where large domain gaps most severely degrade prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUET outperforms all seven compared baselines across every domain adaptation scenario on all three benchmarks. Against the closest competitor DIFO (CVPR 2024), which also leverages CLIP for pseudo-label refinement, DUET improves average accuracy by +1.6% on Office-Home (84.7% vs. 83.1%), +1.4% on VisDA-C (91.4% vs. 90.0%), and +2.9% on DomainNet-126 (82.9% vs. 80.0%). The margin over DIFO is largest in Clipart-target scenarios (e.g., A→C: 73.6% vs. 70.6%; P→C: 73.7% vs. 70.1%) and Sketch-target scenarios on DomainNet (C→S: 79.0% vs. 74.9%), confirming the benefit of the TMI-based vision encoder optimization under large domain gaps. Earlier baselines such as SHOT (71.9%), GKD (72.4%), and TPDS (73.5%) on Office-Home are surpassed by 11–13% in average accuracy, while AdaCon (65.0%) and SCLM (64.7%) trail by over 20%, reflecting the substantial advantage of incorporating a vision-language model (CLIP) into the SFDA pipeline. On VisDA-C, DUET also surpasses AdaCon (86.8%) and TPDS (87.6%) by 4.6% and 3.8% respectively, demonstrating consistent gains in the simulation-to-real setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DIFO (CVPR 2024), which introduced the use of CLIP as a domain-invariant reference for pseudo label refinement in source-free domain adaptation. DUET departs from DIFO in two key ways: it replaces CLIP's prompt tuning with direct vision encoder fine-tuning guided by a Tsallis mutual information (TMI) loss, and it introduces a dual-perspective pseudo labeling mechanism that only assigns hard labels when the task-specific target model and CLIP agree, applying consistency training to the remaining samples. The broader SFDA lineage (SHOT, NRC, GKD, AdaCon, TPDS) provides the self-supervised pseudo-labeling paradigm that DUET refines, while the Tsallis entropy framework is borrowed from statistical physics and adapted here as a dynamic exploration-exploitation controller.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-perspective pseudo labeling strategy offers a practical, threshold-free mechanism for balancing pseudo label quality and coverage, which is a persistent challenge in SFDA. The TMI-based optimization with an uncertainty-adapted index provides a principled way to modulate training intensity over the course of adaptation, potentially transferable to other semi-supervised or test-time adaptation settings. However, the computational overhead of fine-tuning CLIP's vision encoder (even if limited to fewer than eight update steps) remains a concern for resource-constrained deployments, and the paper does not explore whether the TMI mechanism generalizes beyond the specific CLIP-vision-encoder optimization context. The modest but consistent gains (+1.4–2.9%) over a strong recent baseline (DIFO) suggest the approach is effective but not transformative for the field.
