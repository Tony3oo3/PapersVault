## Problem

Subject-agnostic seizure prediction (SASP) remains unsolved because existing models suffer from three coupled limitations: (1) spectral bias toward low frequencies that obscures transient high-frequency oscillations critical as preictal biomarkers, (2) failure to jointly model phase-amplitude coupling dynamics that distinguish ictal from interictal states, and (3) inability to handle heterogeneous electrode montages across patients and species. These constraints prevent a single model from generalizing across recording modalities, implantation strategies, and individual anatomical variability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254215/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multichannel neural recordings (scalp EEG, ECoG, SEEG, or LFP) from humans, rats, dogs, or macaques, which are resampled, segmented into fixed-duration non-overlapping patches, and harmonized to 64 effective channels via channel rejection/duplication before being projected into a shared embedding space by a channel-independent linear layer. The output is a binary preictal/interictal label per segment, evaluated under subject-agnostic nested cross-validation where test subjects are entirely unseen during training. The architecture is inherently agnostic to the original electrode count and spatial arrangement, requiring no per-patient retraining.

## Method

FAPEX integrates three novel modules. First, the Fractional Neural Frame Operator (FrNFO) performs adaptive time-frequency decomposition via a learnable bank of Weyl-Heisenberg frame filters whose window kernels are generated by an implicit MLP parameterized with Hermite polynomial basis functions and sine activations; a per-channel learnable fractional order θ governs the time-frequency interpolation, and the output is computed through the fractional convolution theorem, yielding complex-valued representations that naturally separate into amplitude and phase while provably preserving high-frequency content. Second, Amplitude-Phase Cross-Encoding (APCE) uses two sequential bidirectional Mamba (selective state-space) blocks in a cross-attention-like fashion: the phase BSSM conditions its state-space parameters on the amplitude embedding, and the amplitude BSSM conditions on the phase-informative output, capturing time-varying cross-frequency dependencies. Third, Spatial Correlation Aggregation (SCA) applies linear attention over the channel dimension with a depthwise-convolution-gated mechanism to model inter-electrode dependencies at linear complexity. The model is trained end-to-end under supervised, self-supervised (pretrained on TUEG/CCEP/PPE then finetuned), and transfer-learning protocols across 12 benchmarks spanning four species and four recording modalities.

## Results

FAPEX-Base achieves top-1 F1 on all 12 benchmark datasets under supervised training, with median F1 scores ranging from 72.5 (HUP) to 94.6 (RESPECT) and SEN from 73.7 (HUP) to 92.3 (RESPECT). With self-supervised pretraining on TUEG/CCEP/PPE, FAPEX-Base (SSL) further improves, reaching 94.9% F1 on CANINE, 94.6% F1 on RESPECT, and 95.0% F1 on PCS. The paper reports approximately 10% improvement in F1 and sensitivity over the strongest supervised baselines, and up to 15% sensitivity gains in cross-domain transfer scenarios (e.g., LPIRE→KAIME). Across 22 supervised and 10 self-supervised baselines evaluated under nested subject-agnostic cross-validation, FAPEX-Base (SSL) attains the highest median F1 on 11 of 12 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAPEX-Base (SL) outperforms the strongest supervised baselines (TSLANet, ModernTCN, Medformer) by 1–6 points in F1 across most datasets, with the largest gaps on RESPECT (92.3 vs. 78.7 for TSLANet) and CTLE-RATLFP (83.2 vs. 78.3 for Omni-Scale). On CANINE, Omni-Scale achieves higher SEN (91.4 vs. 88.8) but FAPEX-Base (SL) leads substantially in F1 (90.7 vs. 84.1) and ROC (97.2 vs. 83.7), indicating better precision-recall balance. Among self-supervised baselines, FAPEX-Base (SSL) surpasses Neuro-BERT by 2.1 F1 on BEIRUT (87.3 vs. 85.2) and by 21.2 F1 on HUP (77.2 vs. 54.4), and outperforms CBraMod by 4.3 F1 on CANINE (94.9 vs. 90.6). COMETS is the closest SSL competitor on BEIRUT (86.0 F1) and CANINE (93.4 F1), but FAPEX-Base (SSL) still leads by 1.3 and 1.5 F1 respectively. In cross-domain transfer (Fig. 4), FAPEX-Base achieves relative F1 gains exceeding 30% over both Neuro-BERT and CBraMod under source-only transfer, with consistent positive Δ% across all six source-target pairs tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAPEX builds on three distinct mathematical and architectural lineages: (1) fractional Fourier transform and fractional convolution theory, which provides the continuous time-frequency interpolation framework; (2) nonstationary Weyl-Heisenberg frame theory, which supplies the redundant basis structure for adaptive signal decomposition; and (3) selective state-space models (Mamba) with cross-attention mechanisms, which underpin the amplitude-phase cross-encoding module. The key departure from prior work is the integration of implicit neural representations (MLPs with Hermite polynomial bases) to generate learnable window functions for the fractional frame filters, combined with per-channel learnable fractional orders—something absent from both classical FrFT implementations and prior neural time-frequency architectures. Unlike conventional CNNs and Transformers that exhibit documented spectral bias toward low frequencies, FrNFO explicitly addresses this by preserving high-frequency oscillatory content through fractional-order operations, and unlike prior seizure predictors that process amplitude or phase in isolation, FAPEX jointly encodes both through bidirectional cross-SSM interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAPEX establishes the first model to demonstrate consistent superiority in subject-agnostic seizure prediction across 12 benchmarks spanning four species and four recording modalities, addressing a long-standing scalability limitation in the field where prior approaches required per-patient training. The finding that a compact architecture outperforms large foundation models (CBraMod, Neuro-BERT) pretrained on the same data suggests that inductive bias from fractional-order signal processing matters more than parameter scale for neuroelectrophysiological tasks. Open questions include whether the FrNFO's fractional-order decomposition generalizes to other nonstationary signal domains beyond epilepsy, how the model performs in truly closed-loop clinical settings with real-time latency constraints, and whether the channel-alignment preprocessing step (harmonizing to 64 channels) limits applicability to very sparse or very dense montages. The paper also notes that limitations are discussed in supplementary materials, and future work targets wearable deployment and multi-modal integration.
