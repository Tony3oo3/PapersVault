## Problem

Current image generation models produce visually appealing images but their 3D spatial awareness—understanding object placement, spatial relationships, and quantitative measurements—remains largely unexplored and unevaluated. Standard VLM-based evaluation pipelines fail to capture fine-grained spatial errors, making it difficult to assess whether models can truly plan scenes the way human photographers do. GenSpace addresses this by introducing a dedicated benchmark and a specialized evaluation pipeline that reconstructs 3D scene geometry to measure spatial faithfulness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251820/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the input is a natural-language prompt specifying spatial conditions (pose, relation, or measurement) over 50 object categories; for image editing, the input is a source image paired with a spatial editing instruction. The evaluation pipeline consumes a single generated RGB image and produces a unified spatial correctness score in [0, 100], decomposed into orientation difference (degrees), relation correctness (binary), and relative distance error (percentage). The benchmark comprises 3,600 samples total (1,800 per task), organized as 3 dimensions × 3 sub-domains × 4 prompt templates × 50 samples each.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a Spatial Expertise Pipeline that reconstructs 3D scene geometry from a single generated image by combining multiple visual foundation models: Grounded-SAM for 2D object localization, Depth Anything V2 for metric depth, OrientAnything for 3D object orientation, and WildCamera plus PerspectiveFields for camera intrinsic calibration. These estimates are unprojected into a canonical 3D point cloud to recover absolute object and camera poses. Scoring proceeds in three steps: (1) an object presence check via Grounded-SAM detection, (2) spatial difference analysis comparing extracted 3D states against the prompt-specified target (orientation in degrees, relation as binary, distance as relative percentage), and (3) a piecewise-linear mapping to [0, 100] with sub-domain-specific thresholds (e.g., ≤30° orientation error scores 100, ≤33% distance error scores 100). For multi-condition sub-domains, individual scores are multiplied. This pipeline achieves 76.22% agreement with human labels versus 56.44% for the best general-purpose VLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro), demonstrating that specialized 3D reconstruction outperforms holistic VLM judgment for spatial evaluation.

## Results

On the GenSpace text-to-image benchmark (Table 4), GPT-4o achieves the best average rank (1.8) with scores of 59.41 (Camera Pose), 62.72 (Object Pose), 94.55 (Egocentric Relation), and 41.33 (Object Distance), while even the top model scores below 25 on Complex Pose and below 35 on all Spatial Measurement sub-domains. In instruction-based image editing (Table 5), GPT-4o again leads (rank 1.8) but specialized editing models like InstructPix2Pix and ICEdit score near 0 on pose sub-domains. For the evaluation metric itself (Table 3), the proposed spatial expertise pipeline achieves 76.22% average human alignment, substantially outperforming the best VLM evaluator Gemini-2.5-Pro (56.44%), GPT-o3 (55.78%), and GPT-4o (53.22%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's evaluation pipeline is validated against three state-of-the-art VLM evaluators (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-4o, GPT-o3) on 900 manually annotated samples. The proposed spatial expertise pipeline wins on every sub-domain, achieving 76.22% average human alignment versus 56.44% (Gemini-2.5-Pro), 55.78% (GPT-o3), and 53.22% (GPT-4o) — a gap of roughly 20 percentage points. The largest VLM weaknesses are in Allocentric Relation (42–50% vs. 73% for the pipeline) and Intrinsic Relation (44–48% vs. 56%), confirming that general-purpose VLMs struggle with perspective transformation and view-agnostic spatial reasoning. Among the 9 image generation models benchmarked, GPT-4o dominates across nearly all sub-domains, but even it scores only 21.21 on Allocentric and 35.19 on Camera Distance, revealing that no current model handles allocentric transformation or metric measurement reliably.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image generation benchmarking lineage (GenEval, T2I-CompBench, PhyBench, Commonsense-T2I) by introducing a dedicated spatial awareness dimension grounded in real-world photographic composition, organized into three hierarchical levels (pose → relation → measurement). It departs from prior evaluation approaches that rely on general-purpose VLMs for scoring, instead proposing a pipeline that composes specialized visual foundation models (Grounded-SAM, Depth Anything, OrientAnything, WildCamera, PerspectiveFields) to reconstruct 3D scene geometry and compute quantitative spatial differences. The work also connects to the 3D spatial understanding literature (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, 3DSR-Bench, COMFORT) by identifying the same egocentric-allocentric and metric measurement failures in generative models that those benchmarks expose in VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest unified model (GPT-4o) achieves only ~60% on basic object pose and ~25% on complex multi-object pose, while metric measurement adherence is near-universal failure across all models—highlighting a fundamental gap between visual plausibility and 3D spatial correctness. The evaluation pipeline's 76% human alignment versus 56% for the best VLM demonstrates that spatial evaluation requires purpose-built tooling rather than general VLM judgment, a finding with implications for all spatial evaluation tasks. Open questions include whether the identified limitations (camera location confusion, egocentric-allocentric inversion, metric insensitivity) stem from training data composition or architectural constraints, and whether the evaluation pipeline's reliance on multiple foundation models introduces compounding errors that limit its own accuracy ceiling.
