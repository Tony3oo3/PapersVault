## Problem

Existing generalization bounds for score-based generative models rely on approximation-theory arguments that assume the score network is a good approximation of the true score, thereby neglecting the impact of the training dataset and the optimization algorithm. This makes the bounds overly pessimistic and unable to explain why hyperparameters like learning rate and batch size empirically affect generation quality. The paper provides the first algorithm- and data-dependent generalization analysis for SGMs, explicitly accounting for optimization dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples from a data distribution μ with bounded support (‖x‖ ≤ D) and trains a score network s_θ(t,x) via denoising score matching under an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck forward process with a cosine noise schedule. At inference, sampling starts from standard Gaussian noise γ^d and proceeds via the exponential integrator (EI) discretization of the backward SDE. The theoretical output is a high-probability KL divergence bound between μ and the generated distribution ν_T^(n), decomposed into the empirical ESM loss, a data-dependent diffusion gap of order O(1/√n), and a score generalization gap that depends on the optimizer's trajectory.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a decomposition of the score approximation error ε_s^(n)(θ) into three terms: the explicit score matching loss L_ESM^(n)(θ) (the quantity actually optimized during training), a data-dependent diffusion gap Δ̂_T^(n) measuring the discrepancy between forward diffusions initialized from the empirical vs. true distribution, and a score generalization gap G_λ(Z^(n),θ) quantifying the difference between population and empirical DSM risk. The diffusion gap is bounded via concentration inequalities and smoothed Wasserstein distance, yielding O(1/√n) + E_d. The generalization gap is then controlled using two existing learning-theoretic tools: (i) for SGLD, a gradient-norm-based bound from Mou et al. (2018) showing that averaged gradient norms upper-bound the gap; and (ii) for ADAM, topological complexity measures (weighted lifetime sum E¹ and positive magnitude PMag) of the training trajectory from Andreeva et al. (2024). Combining these yields a full KL bound of the form L_ESM^(n)(θ) + O(1/√n) + E_i + E_d, where E_i and E_d are initialization and discretization errors independent of the training procedure.

## Results

The paper establishes the first algorithm- and data-dependent generalization bound for score-based generative models, decomposing the score approximation error as ε_s^(n)(θ) = L_ESM^(n)(θ) + Δ_s^(n) + G_1^(n)(θ), yielding a high-probability KL bound of the form L_ESM^(n)(θ) + O(1/√n) + E_i + E_d. Empirically, on the butterflies and flowers datasets, the gradient-norm-based quantity b⟨‖ĝ_k‖²⟩ and the topological complexity measures E¹(W^(n)) and PMag(√n·W^(n)) all correlate strongly with the observed score generalization gap across varying ADAM learning rates and batch sizes. For SGLD on a 4D Gaussian mixture, the theoretical bound B(n,η) = √(ηβ⟨‖ĝ_k²‖⟩/n) matches the order of magnitude of the generalization gap, with the tightest correlation at high inverse temperature β.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare its generative model against competing architectures or training methods; rather, it validates its theoretical generalization bounds by correlating different complexity measures with the empirical score generalization gap. Among the three topological/gradient-based measures tested on ADAM (b⟨‖ĝ_k‖²⟩, E¹(W^(n)), and PMag at two scales), all three show strong correlation with the generalization gap on butterflies and flowers, with PMag(10⁻²·W^(n)) performing slightly worse than the others, consistent with the theory in [ADS+24]. The gradient-norm bound from [MWZZ18] (adapted for SGLD) provides an upper bound on the generalization gap whose order of magnitude matches observations, though the absolute values are larger due to the unknown subgaussian constant τ. The paper notes that correlations improve near convergence, suggesting these measures are most informative when the model has settled into a local minimum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the KL convergence framework of Conforti et al. (CDS25), which decomposes the generative error into initialization, score approximation, and discretization terms, but treats the score approximation error as a monolithic quantity. The key departure is a further decomposition of that score error into an explicit score matching loss, a data-dependent diffusion gap, and a score generalization gap, then applying existing learning-theoretic tools (SGLD gradient-norm bounds from Mou et al., topological bounds from Andreeva et al.) to the gap term. This contrasts with prior approximation-theory approaches (OAS23, ADR24, CLL23) that establish existence of good score estimators without accounting for the actual training algorithm, and with information-theoretic analyses (CZS25) that capture algorithm dependence only implicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm- and data-dependent generalization analysis for score-based generative models, providing practitioners with concrete, measurable quantities (gradient norms, weighted lifetime sum, positive magnitude of training trajectories) that predict generation quality without requiring a full retraining. The finding that the data-dependent diffusion gap is negative in expectation is a useful structural insight, though the high-probability bounds remain coarse with constants that can grow rapidly as the discretization step shrinks. Open questions include whether the subgaussian loss assumption holds for practical architectures, whether the bounds tighten under entropy-based noise schedules, and whether the empirical correlations observed on small-to-medium datasets (MNIST, butterflies, flowers) persist at the scale of modern large-scale diffusion training.
