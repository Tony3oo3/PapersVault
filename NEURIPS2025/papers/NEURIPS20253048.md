## Problem

Conventional visual prompting (VP) methods, which adapt frozen pre-trained vision models by learning additive noise on input images, suffer from two critical limitations: the restricted expressivity of simple additive transformation, and a tendency to overfit when the prompt parameter count increases. The paper asks how to expand VP's transformation space to improve accuracy while simultaneously mitigating the overfitting that accompanies additional parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images at varying resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR/SVHN/GTSRB, 128×128 for most natural-image datasets, 480×320 for CLEVR) and produces class labels for image classification, or bounding boxes and segmentation masks for the secondary detection/segmentation tasks. All input images are resized to 256×256 and center-cropped to 224×224 before the prompting transformations are applied. The recognition model (CLIP ViT-B/32, ResNet50, etc.) remains entirely frozen; only the prompt parameters are optimized, and the learned prompt is applied at inference with negligible overhead (~6% of model inference time).

## Method

ACAVP transforms the input image through three sequentially applied, jointly optimized operations: (1) an affine transformation (rotation, translation, shearing, scaling) with learnable parameters constrained by tanh/sigmoid activations to prevent extreme distortions, which creates task-specific prompt regions while preserving original image content; (2) a color transformation via a learnable per-pixel multiplicative factor (constrained to [0, R_σ]) that adjusts brightness and contrast to emphasize task-relevant features; and (3) the traditional additive prompt applied only to the zero-valued regions created by the affine transformation. Overfitting is mitigated using TrivialAugment data augmentation, which the paper shows is substantially more effective than dropout, MSE regularization, or weight decay for VP training. A PAC-learning theoretical analysis proves that ACAVP's hypothesis space strictly contains those of EVP and AutoVP, guaranteeing a smaller approximation error, while TrivialAugment controls the corresponding increase in estimation error.

## Results

Using CLIP ViT-B/32, ACAVP achieves an average accuracy of 83.18% across 12 image classification datasets, outperforming VP (78.39%), EVP (78.86%), and AutoVP (81.54%), and surpassing Linear Probing (82.59%) in average accuracy. On the Flowers dataset, ACAVP reaches 89.36%, a 6.67-point gain over AutoVP and 21.53 points over VP. On corrupted datasets (CIFAR-10-C / CIFAR-100-C), ACAVP achieves 83.98% and 58.68% respectively, outperforming all baselines by 3–8 points. With ResNet50 (ImageNet-pretrained), ACAVP attains 30.67% average accuracy without label mapping and 42.45% with label mapping, again the best among VP methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACAVP wins on 9 out of 12 datasets against all three VP baselines (VP, EVP, AutoVP) with CLIP ViT-B/32, achieving the highest average accuracy (83.18%) and surpassing Linear Probing (82.59%). The largest margins are on Flowers (+6.67 pp over AutoVP, +21.53 pp over VP) and UCF (+5.23 pp over AutoVP), where the affine and color transformations capture fine-grained visual features. ACAVP trails AutoVP slightly on CLEVR (77.19 vs 79.19), GTSRB (92.04 vs 92.50), and SVHN (91.81 vs 92.94), but these gaps are within 1.2 pp. Against VPT (Jia et al., 2022), ACAVP outperforms on 8 of 10 datasets, with the most notable win on Flowers (89.36 vs 76.2). In the generative-model transferability experiment (Table 14), ACAVP beats TVP (Zhang et al., 2024) on both CIFAR-10 (98.85 vs 98.78) and CIFAR-100 (88.72 vs 83.10) with InstructBLIP, and also on BLIP2 (85.32 vs 85.15 on CIFAR-10; 66.67 vs 62.80 on CIFAR-100). Computationally, ACAVP adds only 6% overhead relative to model inference, far less than Coordinator (685%) or AutoVP (60%), while VP and EVP are near-zero but less accurate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual prompting (VP) lineage from adversarial reprogramming (Elsayed et al., 2019) through VP (Bahng et al., 2022), EVP (Wu et al., 2024), and AutoVP (Tsao et al., 2024) by replacing the single additive transformation with a composite of affine, color, and additive operations, and by formally proving hypothesis-space containment (EVP ⊆ AutoVP ⊆ ACAVP) under a PAC-learning framework. It departs from prior VP work in two key ways: (1) the transformation space is expanded beyond pixel-level addition to include geometric and photometric modifications, and (2) overfitting is identified as a first-class problem in VP training, with TrivialAugment shown to be the most effective mitigation—benefiting not only ACAVP but also existing baselines. The approach remains firmly in the task-level (non-sample-specific) VP paradigm, deliberately avoiding the inference-time overhead of neural-network-based prompt generators like Coordinator or SMM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most broadly useful finding is that TrivialAugment yields up to 12 percentage-point gains on existing VP methods (e.g., standard VP on Flowers), suggesting that overfitting—not limited expressivity—has been the dominant bottleneck in VP training, a diagnosis that should influence how the community designs and regularizes future VP variants. ACAVP's ability to surpass linear probing in average accuracy while remaining a black-box, parameter-free adaptation method strengthens the case for VP in settings where model internals are inaccessible. Open questions include whether non-linear or frequency-domain transformations could further expand the transformation space without prohibitive cost, and whether the modest ImageNet gains (0.16 pp over zero-shot) indicate a ceiling for input-only adaptation at scale.
