## Problem

Vision Language Models are highly sensitive to visually dominant but semantically irrelevant elements in images, causing them to generate task-irrelevant reasoning or hallucinations on complex tasks like math, chart understanding, and geolocation. Existing approaches either perform one-time visual search without iterative refinement or rely on text-only reasoning that cannot adaptively re-focus on image regions as reasoning progresses, leaving a gap in dynamically coupling visual attention with multi-step reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253544/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text question as input and iteratively produces a reasoning chain where each step is accompanied by a dynamically updated visual focus image — a cropped and magnified sub-region of the original image selected by a sliding window. Unlike standard VLM inference that processes the full image throughout, CoFFT's visual input changes at every iteration, switching between global views and local details. The process terminates when the model outputs a completion signal, yielding a final answer.

## Method

CoFFT is a training-free iterative framework that emulates human visual cognition through three stages per cycle: (1) Diverse Sample Generation produces k candidate multi-step reasoning samples at varying temperatures; (2) Dual Foresight Decoding scores each sample using a weighted combination of visual focus (cosine similarity and IoU of relative attention maps between question and sample) and reasoning progression (mean log-probability improvement over the current chain), then commits the first step of the best sample; (3) Visual Focus Adjustment computes a composite attention map combining question relevance (penalized by already-processed regions) and future-reasoning relevance from the selected sample, then applies a dynamic sliding window with adaptive thresholding to crop and magnify the most informative region. A key enabling mechanism is a relative attention formulation that normalizes question-attention against a generic descriptive-attention baseline, suppressing salient-but-irrelevant regions. The three stages cycle iteratively, creating a feedback loop where reasoning directs visual focus and refined visual focus improves subsequent reasoning, without any model retraining or architectural modification.

## Results

CoFFT achieves consistent Pass@1 accuracy improvements of 3.1–5.8% over the baseline across seven visual reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, MMStar, M3CoT, Charxiv, SeekWorld-China, SeekWorld-Global) using four VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B/32B, InternVL-2.5-8B, LLaVA-Next-7B). On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, CoFFT reaches 48.19% average accuracy versus 42.72% for the baseline; on Qwen2.5-VL-32B, it reaches 52.96% versus 47.05%. The largest absolute gains appear on fine-grained tasks: SeekWorld-China improves by up to +13.7% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 35.12 vs. 21.45) and Charxiv by up to +4.7% (Qwen2.5-VL-32B: 50.9 vs. 44.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoFFT outperforms all four baselines (MCTS, Predictive Decoding, ICoT, DyFo) on every benchmark and every VLM tested. Against the strongest text-based reasoning baselines (MCTS and Predictive Decoding), CoFFT gains +2.3 to +3.9% average accuracy, with the largest margins on fine-grained visual tasks (e.g., +8.5% on SeekWorld-China for Qwen2.5-VL-7B over Predictive Decoding). Against DyFo, the closest visual-search competitor, CoFFT leads by +1.9 to +3.2% on average, with particularly large gaps on MathVision (+4.6 to +5.3%) and SeekWorld-Global (+1.6 to +2.2%). ICoT (interleaved chain-of-thought prompting) is the weakest baseline, underperforming even the unmodified baseline on most benchmarks (e.g., 34.91% vs. 42.72% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B), suggesting that text-only interleaved reasoning without visual focus adjustment is counterproductive. Notably, CoFFT's advantage scales with model size: the 32B Qwen2.5-VL gains +5.9% over baseline versus +5.5% for the 7B variant, indicating the method leverages stronger base models more effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoFFT sits at the intersection of multi-modal chain-of-thought reasoning (MCTS, Predictive Decoding, ICoT) and training-free visual search (DyFo, SEAL), but departs from both by making them mutually iterative rather than sequential or one-shot. Prior visual search methods like DyFo perform a single static crop before reasoning, while reasoning-enhancement methods like Predictive Decoding operate on a fixed image throughout; CoFFT instead creates a closed loop where each reasoning step re-evaluates and re-crops the visual input. The relative attention mechanism (normalizing question attention against a descriptive baseline) is a lightweight but novel component that enables the visual focus scoring without additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play method that consistently improves multiple VLMs (7B–32B) by 3–6% across math, chart, and geolocation benchmarks, CoFFT offers a practical route to better fine-grained visual reasoning without retraining or architectural changes. The scaling property—larger models benefit more—suggests the technique will become increasingly valuable as VLMs grow. However, the authors acknowledge that CoFFT can introduce errors on problems the base VLM already solves correctly, and the computational overhead (~28× baseline FLOPS) remains a deployment concern. Open questions include whether the iterative cycle can be made more robust (reducing the 'helps on hard, hurts on easy' pattern) and whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-image settings.
