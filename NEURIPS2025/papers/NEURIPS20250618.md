## Problem

Standard weak-to-strong generalization, a key superalignment paradigm, fails to transfer adversarial robustness to strong vision-language models even when guided by an adversarially robust weak teacher. The paper identifies the root cause as a mismatch in learning objectives: adversarial examples are absent from the alignment data, so the strong student never encounters worst-case inputs during distillation. This leaves large-scale VLMs vulnerable to imperceptible perturbations that propagate catastrophic errors through multimodal reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a fully unsupervised regime: it consumes RGB images (224×224 for ViT-L/14) paired with fixed text prompts (e.g., 'This is a photo of [CLASS]') for zero-shot classification, or question strings for VQA, without any ground-truth labels during training. Adversarial perturbations (ℓ∞-bounded, ε=2/255, 10-step PGD) and inverse adversarial perturbations are generated on-the-fly from the teacher's pseudo-labels. Outputs are class probabilities via cosine similarity between image and text embeddings, generated captions (evaluated by CIDEr), or VQA answers, with the robust vision encoder serving as a plug-and-play replacement in downstream VLMs like LLaVA 1.5 7B and OpenFlamingo 9B.

## Method

Adv-W2S reformulates weak-to-strong alignment as a min-max objective where the student is trained on adversarial inputs (to elicit robustness) while the teacher's guidance is refined via inverse adversarial examples that maximize teacher confidence in the predicted class. Two complementary mechanisms mitigate unreliable weak-teacher supervision: (1) entropy-guided uncertainty re-weighting, which adaptively shifts each sample's loss weight from teacher-student alignment toward student self-refinement based on the teacher's prediction entropy (using a soft normalization w(x)=H_T(x)/(H_T(x)+κ_H)); and (2) inverse adversarial refinement, which generates benign perturbations that push the teacher's prediction toward a one-hot distribution, providing higher-confidence guidance. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–3) establishes that low prediction entropy enforces larger vision feature norms, which expand classification margins and yield certifiable robustness radii under Lipschitz continuity, while the inverse-adversary cross-entropy inequality guarantees that aligning adversarial student predictions with refined teacher outputs simultaneously improves clean-sample generalization.

## Results

On the primary zero-shot classification benchmark (14 datasets, Auto-Attack ε=2/255), Adv-W2S achieves the best average clean accuracy of 68.75% and average robust accuracy of 48.38%, outperforming the strongest baselines (FARE: 65.50% clean / 42.97% robust; PMG: 64.46% clean / 45.74% robust) by approximately 3–5% in clean accuracy and 2.6–5.4% in robust accuracy. On ImageNet specifically, Adv-W2S reaches 75.84% clean and 58.30% robust accuracy, slightly below TeCoA (80.00% / 61.74%) in-distribution but leading on out-of-distribution generalization. The method also extends to downstream VLM tasks (captioning, VQA, hallucination, CoT reasoning) via plug-and-play vision encoder replacement, consistently achieving the best robust scores across LLaVA 1.5 and OpenFlamingo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adv-W2S outperforms all four adversarial fine-tuning baselines (TeCoA, PMG, FARE, TGA) on average zero-shot clean accuracy (68.75% vs. 61.56–65.50%) and average robust accuracy (48.38% vs. 42.97–45.74%) across 14 datasets. The largest margin is over TeCoA (+7.19% clean, +5.12% robust) and FARE (+3.25% clean, +5.41% robust). On the in-distribution ImageNet test set, Adv-W2S trails TeCoA by ~4% clean and ~3.5% robust, reflecting the cost of the unsupervised scheme; however, this gap narrows substantially when an auxiliary supervised branch is added (80.56% / 64.08%). Across diverse CLIP backbones (ResNet101, ViT-B/16) and perturbation radii (ε=3/255, 4/255), Adv-W2S consistently leads in both clean and robust accuracy. In downstream VLM tasks (captioning CIDEr, VQA accuracy, POPE hallucination F1, ScienceQA CoT), the plug-and-play robust encoder from Adv-W2S achieves the best robust scores across LLaVA 1.5 and OpenFlamingo, e.g., 55.3 CIDEr on COCO captioning vs. 52.8 (PMG) and 50.6 (TGA), and 83.8 POPE F1 vs. 80.8 (FARE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weak-to-strong generalization paradigm (Burns et al., ICML 2024) to the adversarial robustness setting for vision-language models, identifying that the standard formulation fails to transfer robustness even when the teacher is adversarially trained. It departs from prior adversarial fine-tuning methods for VLMs (TeCoA, FARE, PMG, TGA) by framing robustness elicitation as an unsupervised alignment problem guided by a weak teacher rather than supervised minimax optimization. The two proposed mechanisms—entropy-guided uncertainty re-weighting and inverse adversarial example refinement—draw on ideas from knowledge distillation reliability analysis and inverse adversarial training, respectively, but are combined here for the first time in a weak-to-strong VLM context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic analysis of why robustness fails to transfer under weak-to-strong generalization, offering a clear diagnostic (absence of adversarial examples in the alignment objective) and a practical fix that is plug-and-play for large VLMs like LLaVA and OpenFlamingo. The unsupervised nature of the scheme is significant for the superalignment narrative, where ground-truth labels may be unavailable or unreliable. Open questions include the inherent trade-off between in-distribution and zero-shot performance, whether the approach scales to even larger or non-CLIP architectures, and whether the entropy-based re-weighting generalizes to settings where the teacher is substantially weaker than assumed.
