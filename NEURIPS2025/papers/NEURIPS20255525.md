## Problem

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), the dominant post-training method behind DeepSeek-R1, suffers from training instability due to sensitivity to clip thresholds and KL coefficients, and its off-policy variant relies on importance sampling that risks gradient explosion. More fundamentally, existing methods like DPO may fail to converge to the true KL-constrained optimal policy because of inherent flaws in the Bradley-Terry model, leaving a gap between practical post-training algorithms and the theoretically optimal solution.

## Input / Output

Given a prompt x and a group of k sampled responses {y_i} drawn from an arbitrary sampling distribution π_s (not necessarily the current policy), along with scalar reward scores R(x, y_i) and a reference policy π_θ' (typically the previous step's policy), GVPO computes per-response gradient weights and updates the policy in a single step. The key constraint is that the sum of weights within each prompt group equals zero, which is what cancels the intractable partition function. The output is an updated policy π_θ that provably converges to the KL-constrained reward-maximizing optimum regardless of the choice of π_s, enabling off-policy training with reusable or heterogeneous data without importance sampling ratios.

## Method

GVPO's core insight is that when response weights in a prompt group sum to zero, the partition function Z(x) in the closed-form optimal policy becomes invariant and cancels out, eliminating the need for intractable expectation over all responses. The gradient weight for each response is defined as the difference between the central distance of implicit rewards (β log π_θ/π_θ' minus its group mean) and that of actual rewards (R minus its group mean), yielding a loss equivalent to the mean squared error between these two central distances. This loss decomposes into three interpretable components: a group-relative advantage term (R − R̄) that promotes reward maximization, a variance term Var(log π_θ) that balances exploration and exploitation without ad-hoc entropy tuning, and a covariance term Cov(log π_θ, log π_θ') that acts as a trust-region regularizer. Theorem 3.1 proves the unique global optimum is exactly the KL-constrained reward-maximizing policy, and Theorem 3.2 extends this guarantee to any sampling distribution satisfying a mild support condition, decoupling the sampling policy from the learned policy and avoiding both on-policy requirements and importance sampling instability.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B post-trained with the Competition Math dataset, GVPO achieves the best scores across all five math benchmarks: 20.72 (avg@32) on AIME2024, 62.65 on AMC, 83.80 on MATH500, 45.95 on Minerva, and 46.96 on OlympiadBench (all pass@1 except AIME2024). The largest gains over the strongest baseline (Remax) appear on AIME2024 (+3.53) and AMC (+2.41), while MATH500 shows a more modest +1.80 improvement. GVPO also generalizes to Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, outperforming GRPO on all five benchmarks (e.g., 11.56 vs 8.54 on AIME2024, 56.60 vs 52.60 on MATH500).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GVPO outperforms all four math-reasoning baselines (GRPO, Dr.GRPO, Remax, Reinforce++) on every benchmark. Against GRPO [Shao et al., 2024], the closest direct predecessor, GVPO gains +5.93 on AIME2024 (20.72 vs 14.79) and +4.89 on Minerva (45.95 vs 41.17). Against Remax [Li et al., 2023], the strongest single baseline, GVPO leads by +3.53 on AIME2024 and +2.41 on AMC, while the gap narrows to +1.80 on MATH500. Dr.GRPO [Liu et al., 2025] is competitive on Minerva (44.48) but trails GVPO by +1.47, and Reinforce++ [Hu et al., 2025] is consistently outperformed by 2–5 points across benchmarks. On the summarization alignment task (Appendix C), GVPO surpasses DPO [Rafailov et al., 2023] in reward (5.75 vs 4.83), win rate against human demonstrations (79.49% vs 68.28%), preference accuracy (64.93% vs 60.43%), and LLM-judge scores (e.g., 69% vs 31% from GPT-4o), supporting the claim that GVPO's unique-optimum guarantee translates into better alignment quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

GVPO sits at the intersection of two influential lines of work: the group-relative policy optimization framework (GRPO/DeepSeekMath) that eliminates the value function by standardizing rewards across sampled responses, and the closed-form optimal policy under KL-constrained reward maximization (Jaques et al., 2017) that DPO exploits for pairwise preference learning. The key conceptual departure is extending DPO's partition-function cancellation—achieved there via the Bradley-Terry pairwise model—to a group-wise zero-sum property (sum of weights equals zero), which eliminates the intractable partition function in multi-sample settings while preserving the analytical link to the optimal policy. Unlike GRPO, which relies on importance sampling and gradient clipping for off-policy stability, GVPO decouples the sampling distribution from the learned policy entirely, and unlike DPO, it guarantees a unique global optimum rather than admitting multiple minimizers due to Bradley-Terry model limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GVPO offers a theoretically grounded drop-in replacement for GRPO in LLM post-training pipelines, with the practical advantage of avoiding importance-sampling instability and supporting flexible (including off-policy and mixed) sampling distributions without biased clipping. The ablation showing complete training collapse (0.00 scores) when either the variance or covariance regularization term is removed underscores that these analytically-derived terms are not optional heuristics but structurally necessary, which is a useful diagnostic for practitioners tuning RL-based post-training. However, the experimental validation is limited to math reasoning on 1.5B/7B models and a single summarization task, leaving open questions about scalability to larger models, broader task diversity (code, dialogue, multi-turn), and whether the theoretical guarantees translate to consistent gains in production-scale training with heterogeneous data sources.
