## Problem

The best-known policy for Online Convex Optimization with adversarial constraints (COCO) achieves O(√T) regret and Õ(√T) cumulative constraint violation (CCV), but it was open whether CCV could be substantially improved under natural assumptions. This paper shows that in the fixed-dimensional setting (d ≪ T), one can achieve CCV as small as Õ(T^{1−β}) by trading off regret to Õ(√(dT) + T^β), a trade-off that is critical for safety-critical applications where constraint satisfaction takes precedence over cost minimization.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner selects an action from a convex decision set X ⊂ R^d (diameter D) before observing the adversarial convex cost function f_t and constraint function g_t, both bounded in [0,1]. The output is a sequence of actions with a tunable parameter β ∈ [0,1] that explicitly controls the regret–CCV trade-off: smaller β yields lower CCV at the cost of higher regret. The only structural requirement beyond boundedness is a feasibility assumption (at least one action satisfies all constraints), and the method extends to a relaxed long-term budget feasibility setting where the benchmark may violate constraints up to a prescribed budget B_T.

## Method

The core mechanism is a regret decomposition using an exponential Lyapunov function Φ(x) = e^{λx} that constructs surrogate cost functions combining the original cost with a dynamically weighted constraint violation, so that minimizing surrogate regret simultaneously controls both regret and CCV. The key technical ingredient is a new adaptive small-loss regret bound for the EXPERT problem (Theorem 1) that handles potentially unbounded losses via a self-confident learning rate η_t = (1/√G_{t−1})·√(ln N / (L̃_{t−1} + γG_{t−1})), which is essential because the surrogate costs grow with the Lyapunov term. For the CONSTRAINED EXPERT problem (simplex decision set, linear functions), the adaptive Hedge algorithm is applied directly to the surrogate costs; the general convex case is reduced via a δ-covering argument with δ = 1/T; and for M-smooth functions, OGD with adaptive step sizes replaces the covering approach for O(1) per-round complexity. The parameter β tunes the trade-off: β = 0 recovers O(√T) regret with Õ(T) CCV, while β = 1 yields O(T) regret with Õ(1) CCV (logarithmic in the Online Constraint Satisfaction special case where all costs are zero).

## Results

For the CONSTRAINED EXPERT problem, the proposed adaptive Hedge-based policy achieves O(√(T ln N) + T^β) regret and Õ(T^(1-β) ln N) CCV, where β ∈ [0,1] is a tunable parameter. In the general d-dimensional convex setting, the bounds become Õ(√(dT) + T^β) regret and Õ(dT^(1-β)) CCV, and under M-smoothness, a first-order OGD-based policy attains O(√(MT) + T^β) regret and Õ(MT^(1-β)) CCV. In the special case of Online Constraint Satisfaction (all costs zero, β=1), the CCV reduces to O(ln T), a logarithmic improvement over the previous Õ(√T) bound. The key trade-off is that Regret_T × CCV_T = Õ(T), allowing the learner to sacrifice regret for substantially smaller constraint violation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the OGD-based policy of Sinha and Vaze [2024], which achieves O(√T) regret and Õ(√T) CCV. The proposed method strictly improves upon this by introducing a tunable parameter β: setting β < 1 yields CCV of Õ(T^(1−β)), which is asymptotically smaller than Õ(√T) for any β > 0, at the cost of an additional T^β term in the regret. In the experimental evaluation (N=20 experts, T=5000 rounds), the adaptive Hedge-based policy with β=0.75 achieves significantly lower cumulative constraint violation while maintaining sub-linear regret compared to the OGD baseline. The expert selection frequency plots further show that the proposed policy quickly identifies and locks onto the best feasible expert, whereas the OGD-based policy initially selects infeasible experts and takes considerably longer to converge. The theoretical improvement is most dramatic in the OCS special case (β=1, zero costs), where CCV drops from Õ(√T) to O(ln T).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the regret decomposition framework of Sinha and Vaze (NeurIPS 2024), which achieved O(√T) regret and Õ(√T) CCV via an OGD-based policy, and extends the classical small-loss (L*) regret bounds of Cesa-Bianchi and Lugosi (2006) to handle potentially unbounded loss vectors through a new adaptive Hedge policy (Theorem 1). The key conceptual departure is leveraging these small-loss bounds on surrogate cost functions—whose scale grows with the Lyapunov function—to trade regret for substantially smaller CCV, a technique not previously exploited in the COCO literature. For the smooth convex case, the paper repurposes Orabona's (2019) OGD small-loss bound as the core analytical tool, yielding a computationally efficient first-order algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is particularly relevant to safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, AI safety, budget-constrained advertising) where constraint violation is non-negotiable and the Õ(√T) CCV bound is too loose. The tunable parameter β provides a principled trade-off: practitioners can set β=1 to achieve O(ln T) CCV in the pure constraint-satisfaction case, or balance regret and CCV as needed. Key open questions include establishing matching lower bounds, designing efficient algorithms for the fixed-dimensional setting without the smoothness assumption (the covering-based reduction has O(T^d) complexity), and extending to non-convex or stochastic settings.
