## Problem

Photorealistic Codec Avatar (PCA) decoders rely on transposed convolutions that produce extreme activation outliers and checkerboard sparsity, making standard post-training quantization methods (SmoothQuant, GPTQ, rotation-based approaches) inapplicable because non-linearities and 4-D kernel structures break their algebraic assumptions. Simultaneously, VR headsets like Meta Quest 3 lack dedicated compute for the decoder, yielding only ~25 FPS on the Snapdragon XR2 Gen 2 SoC—far below the 90 FPS threshold for comfortable VR. No prior work addresses both the algorithmic quantization gap and the hardware underutilization caused by transposed-convolution sparsity in a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The decoder receives a compact latent code (a low-dimensional vector produced by an encoder from an inward-facing camera capture of the user's face) and reconstructs a 1024×1024 UV-mapped facial texture map plus a 3D mesh for rendering. The texture is sampled during the rendering stage to produce the final photorealistic avatar image. All quantization and acceleration targets the decoder stage specifically, operating at W4A4 or W8A8 precision, with the full pipeline (sensoring → encoding → transmission → decoding → rendering) required to sustain 90+ FPS end-to-end on an edge VR SoC.

## Method

ESCA combines four co-designed components. First, Input Channel-wise Activation Smoothing (ICAS) adapts SmoothQuant's activation-to-weight migration to transposed convolutions by applying per-channel scale factors and fusing them into adjacent layer weights (exploiting LeakyReLU's positive-homogeneity) so no runtime multiplication is needed. Second, Facial-Feature-Aware Smoothing (FFAS) computes activation variance within predefined facial region masks (eyes, mouth) and exempts the top-k% most detail-sensitive channels from smoothing to preserve high-frequency textures. Third, UV-Weighted PTQ rasterizes the 3D face mesh onto the feature-map grid to build a per-pixel importance map, then uses this map to weight the Hessian matrix in a GPTQ-style greedy column-by-column quantization, concentrating bit precision on perceptually critical UV regions. Fourth, a custom 16×16 weight-stationary systolic-array accelerator employs an input-combining mechanism that tiles the im2col-transformed activation into 4×4 blocks, discards fully-zero tiles, and stacks the remainder—reducing redundant MAC operations by up to 75%—while a modified PE with dual-weight/dual-activation multiplexers bypasses zero entries at cycle granularity. An optimized pipeline overlaps transmission and decoding across frames to achieve 100 FPS end-to-end.

## Results

At 4-bit (W4A4) quantization, the full ESCA pipeline (FFAS-UV) achieves FovVideoVDP scores of 5.8541 (front), 4.9795 (left), and 4.9605 (right), surpassing the best baseline GPTQ (5.4980/4.5868/4.5729) by +0.36/+0.39/+0.39 VDP respectively. At 8-bit (W8A8), FFAS-UV reaches 6.5241/5.8589/5.8071 VDP, nearly matching the FP32 full model (6.5364/5.9480/5.8625). On the hardware side, the input-combining accelerator reduces decoder latency from 42.04 ms to 12.51 ms (3.36× speedup) at INT8, and the optimized pipeline sustains 100 FPS end-to-end on a VR headset, exceeding the 90 FPS real-time requirement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESCA (FFAS-UV) outperforms all six baselines at 4-bit across every viewpoint. Against the strongest baseline GPTQ, it gains +0.36 VDP on the front view, +0.39 on left, and +0.39 on right, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.21746 vs. 0.22048 front). POCA, the only prior method specifically designed for codec avatars, trails FFAS-UV by +0.62 VDP on the front view, and 2DQuant (adapted from super-resolution) lags by +0.56 VDP. AdaRound+LSQ, a generic weight-only PTQ scheme, suffers the largest degradation (−2.28 VDP front vs. FP32), confirming that naive quantization is inadequate for transposed-convolution-heavy decoders. At 8-bit, the quality gap narrows: FFAS-UV edges out POCA by +0.04 VDP front and +0.01 VDP right, while the ICAS-only variant actually underperforms all baselines (5.6007 front), showing that aggressive smoothing is counterproductive when quantization pressure is low. The paper also reports that FFAS-UV reduces the front-to-left view consistency gap to 0.88 VDP versus 1.21 for GPTQ, an important property for VR where users frequently shift gaze.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) lineage from SmoothQuant, GPTQ, and rotation-based methods (QuaRot, DuQuant, SpinQuant) into the domain of Codec Avatar decoders, where transposed convolutions followed by LeakyReLU break the linear-algebra assumptions those methods rely on. It adapts channel-wise activation smoothing to the 4-D convolutional setting and introduces a UV-mapping-weighted Hessian that is specific to face texture rendering, departing from the generic layer-wise error minimization of GPTQ. On the hardware side, it builds on prior systolic-array and sparse-convolution acceleration work (Kung et al. 2019) by introducing an input-combining mechanism tailored to the checkerboard sparsity pattern that transposed-convolution im2col produces, and co-designs the quantized model with a dedicated 16×16 PE accelerator for VR SoCs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESCA demonstrates that photorealistic Codec Avatar inference can run at 100 FPS on a resource-constrained VR headset, removing the cloud dependency that previously made real-time telepresence impractical for portable devices. The 3.36× decoder latency reduction from exploiting transposed-convolution sparsity is a concrete, reusable hardware technique for any deconvolution-heavy generative model on edge platforms. Key limitations include reliance on accurate UV priors, evaluation on a single dataset (MultiFace), and acceleration of only the decoding stage; extending co-optimization to the full sensor-to-render pipeline and reducing UV-map dependence are natural next steps.
