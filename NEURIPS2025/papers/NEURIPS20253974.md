## Problem

Classical zeroth-order methods for convex-concave minimax problems require O(dε^{-1}) function value queries, and it remained an open question whether quantum computing could provide a speedup for general minimax problems. While quantum gradient estimators (based on the quantum Fourier transform) can approximate gradients in Õ(1) queries, it was unclear whether one could go beyond gradient estimation to design Hessian-aware quantum zeroth-order algorithms that improve the dependence on both ε^{-1} and d simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume only a quantum function evaluation oracle U_f (a unitary mapping |z⟩|v⟩ → |z⟩|v⊕f(z)⟩) along with Lipschitz constants L0, L1, L2, an initial point z0, and a target accuracy ε. The output is an ε-saddle point (defined via a restricted gap function) for minimax problems or an ε-stationary point (‖∇f(z)‖ ≤ ε) for non-convex problems, obtained as a weighted average of intermediate iterates z_{t+1/2} with weights 1/λ_t. No gradient or Hessian oracle access is required—only function values are queried.

## Method

The paper constructs a quantum Hessian estimator by applying finite differences to quantum gradient estimates (each costing Õ(1) oracle queries), yielding a full Hessian approximation in Õ(d) queries with spectral-norm error O(d²L₁^{1/4}L₂^{1/2}ε_H^{1/4}). This estimator is embedded in a Newton proximal extragradient (NPE) framework to form HAQZO, which achieves Õ(dε^{-2/3}) queries by exploiting second-order convergence. A double-loop variant HAQZO⁺ reuses the Hessian estimate every m=d iterations (lazy Hessian), reducing the complexity to Õ(d + d^{2/3}ε^{-2/3}) and improving the d-dependency by a factor of d^{1/3}. For strongly-convex-strongly-concave problems, a restart strategy (Restart-HAQZO⁺) yields Õ(d + d^{2/3}(L₂/μ)^{2/3}), and the same Hessian-aware design extends to non-convex optimization via a quantum cubic-regularized Newton method (QCNM) achieving Õ(d + d^{1/2}ε^{-3/2}).

## Results

For the convex-concave minimax problem, HAQZO+ achieves a query complexity of Õ(d + d^{2/3}ε^{-2/3}) to the quantum function evaluation oracle, improving upon the classical zeroth-order bound of O(dε^{-1}) by a factor of d^{1/3}ε^{-1/3}. For the strongly-convex-strongly-concave setting, Restart-HAQZO+ achieves Õ(d + d^{2/3}(L₂/μ)^{2/3}), outperforming the classical Õ(dL₁/μ) by a factor of d^{1/3}μ^{-1/3}. For non-convex optimization, QCNM achieves Õ(d + d^{1/2}ε^{-3/2}) to find an ε-stationary point, improving over the best classical zeroth-order method Õ(d² + d^{3/2}ε^{-3/2}) and the prior quantum method Õ(ε^{-7/4}) when d = O(ε^{-1/2}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical zeroth-order baselines for convex-concave minimax, HAQZO+ (Õ(d + d^{2/3}ε^{-2/3})) dominates both ZOSPA (O(dε^{-2})) and ZOVIA (O(dε^{-1})), achieving a speedup of d^{1/3}ε^{-1/3} over the best classical method. For the strongly-convex-strongly-concave case, Restart-HAQZO+ (Õ(d + d^{2/3}(L₂/μ)^{2/3})) improves over ZOVIA (Õ(dL₁/μ)) by a factor of d^{1/3}μ^{-1/3}. In the non-convex setting, QCNM (Õ(d + d^{1/2}ε^{-3/2})) outperforms all classical zeroth-order methods: GFM (O(dε^{-7/4})), DF-CNM (Õ(d²ε^{-3/2})), and Zero-Order CNM (Õ(d² + d^{3/2}ε^{-3/2})), primarily by reducing the polynomial dependence on d. Compared to the prior quantum method Q-Perturbed-AGD (Õ(ε^{-7/4})), QCNM achieves a better ε-dependence (ε^{-3/2} vs. ε^{-7/4}) and is superior when d = O(ε^{-1/2}), demonstrating the advantage of Hessian-aware quantum design over gradient-only quantum approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantum zeroth-order optimization line initiated by Jordan's fast quantum gradient estimator [26] and its applications to convex [8, 2] and non-convex [19] settings, by constructing the first efficient quantum Hessian estimator via finite differences and embedding it into a Newton proximal extragradient (NPE) framework [37]. The double-loop lazy-Hessian structure in HAQZO⁺ is directly inspired by recent classical lazy-Hessian methods [14, 12, 10, 34], while the non-convex extension (QCNM) replaces classical estimators in the zeroth-order cubic-regularized Newton method of Doikov and Grapiglia [13]. The key departure from prior quantum optimization work is moving beyond first-order (gradient) estimation to second-order (Hessian) estimation, which unlocks the superior ε-dependence of second-order methods while retaining the d-reduction from quantum access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that quantum speedups for minimax optimization can improve both the dimension dependence (d^{1/3} factor) and the accuracy dependence (ε^{-1/3} factor) simultaneously, going beyond the d-only speedups achievable with quantum gradient estimators alone. The quantum Hessian estimator is a reusable building block that also yields improvements for non-convex optimization, suggesting a broader template for Hessian-aware quantum algorithms. However, the query complexity still scales with d (as d^{2/3} or d^{1/2}), and the quantum lower bound for this problem remains unknown, leaving open whether the achieved rates are optimal. Practical impact is currently limited by the absence of fault-tolerant quantum hardware, but the theoretical framework provides clear targets for future quantum optimization research.
