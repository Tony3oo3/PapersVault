## Problem

Existing diffusion-based recommenders optimize position-wise (point-wise) preference signals that treat each item independently during denoising, neglecting the joint dependencies and relative ordering among items in a predicted list. This limitation is especially harmful for multi-step trajectory prediction, where user preferences evolve causally across future positions and the model must generate a coherent, ordered sequence rather than independent per-position predictions. The paper formalizes this gap as the User Behavior Trajectory Prediction (UBTP) task and shows that listwise supervision is essential for capturing global preference structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user's observed interaction history H_u = (i_{u,1}, …, i_{u,n}) as input and produces an ordered sequence of k future items, where for each future position j a ranked Top-K candidate list A_{u,j} is generated in parallel (non-autoregressive). The trajectory length k is dataset-dependent (3 for Amazon Beauty, 5 or 10 for MovieLens-1M and LastFM), and the concatenated sample X_0 = concat(H, z_0) is denoised in continuous latent space before being mapped back to discrete item IDs via cosine similarity scoring against the full item embedding table.

## Method

LPDO injects a Plackett-Luce listwise ranking signal directly into the diffusion ELBO, replacing the independent per-position reconstruction objective with a joint likelihood over the entire ordered trajectory. The denoising backbone is a causal transformer that processes the concatenated history-and-target embedding with cross-attention conditioning on the clean history, capturing directional preference evolution across positions. The key training loss L_ListPref is derived from Soft-ListMLE (a relaxed ListMLE with penalty factor γ that avoids overly peaked distributions), and the full objective L_LPDO = λ·L_Simple + (1−λ)·L_ListPref + L_Reg jointly optimizes reconstruction fidelity, listwise ranking utility, and regularization. A tight variational lower bound is derived by introducing the forward noising process as a variational posterior, yielding a tractable objective that couples KL divergence terms (denoising) with the expected Plackett-Luce log-likelihood (ranking). At inference, the model iteratively denoises from Gaussian noise over T steps (flexibly adjustable from 1 to 50), then scores all items per position in parallel to select Top-K candidates.

## Results

LPDO achieves consistent state-of-the-art performance across all three datasets (Amazon Beauty, MovieLens-1M, LastFM) and trajectory lengths (3–10), with relative improvements of 6–37% over the strongest baseline (DCRec) on SeqMatch@50. On LastFM (len=5), LPDO reaches SeqHR@5 of 0.2507 and SeqMatch@50 of 0.2357, surpassing DCRec (0.1931 and 0.1723) by 29.83% and 36.81% respectively. LPDO also achieves the lowest Perplexity across all settings (e.g., 27.28 on LastFM len=5 vs. 29.28 for DCRec), indicating superior probabilistic fidelity. Diffusion-based methods without ranking loss (DreamRec, PreferDiff) collapse to near-zero SeqMatch and PPL > 500, confirming the critical role of listwise preference supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPDO outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset, trajectory length, and metric. The strongest competitor is DCRec (the most recent diffusion-based recommender with dual conditioning), which LPDO surpasses by 6.92–36.81% on SeqMatch@50 and 1.66–8.21% on PPL; the gap is largest on LastFM (len=5) where LPDO's listwise Plackett-Luce objective captures preference evolution most effectively. DiffuRec, the second-strongest baseline, is consistently outperformed by LPDO by 10–30% on SeqHR@5, confirming that listwise ranking supervision adds substantial value over pointwise cross-entropy ranking. Non-diffusion methods (FPMC, SASRec, SASRec-AR, STOSA) are uniformly weaker than diffusion-based approaches, with SASRec-AR (autoregressive extension) only marginally improving over vanilla SASRec. Notably, DreamRec and PreferDiff—diffusion models without explicit ranking loss—suffer severe embedding collapse, yielding near-zero SeqMatch@50 and PPL exceeding 500, which the authors attribute to the absence of a ranking signal to anchor the denoised embeddings to the item space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DCRec diffusion framework (which conditions on user history via concatenation) by replacing its pointwise cross-entropy ranking loss with a Plackett-Luce listwise objective derived from ListMLE, embedded directly into the diffusion ELBO. It departs from prior diffusion-based recommenders (DiffuRec, DreamRec, PreferDiff) that treat each position independently, arguing that coherent multi-step trajectory generation requires modeling joint dependencies across the entire predicted sequence. The causal transformer backbone is adopted to capture directional preference evolution within the trajectory, and the SeqMatch metric is introduced to fill the evaluation gap left by position-wise HR/NDCG metrics that cannot assess sequence-level consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing UBTP as a distinct task and showing that listwise preference supervision is essential (not merely beneficial) for diffusion-based trajectory generation, the paper provides a principled recipe for coupling generative denoising with ranking objectives that could transfer to other sequential decision settings. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets with modified baselines, the hyperparameters λ and γ require manual tuning per dataset, and the offline metrics (SeqMatch, PPL) may not capture real-world user engagement. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much longer trajectories, whether the listwise signal remains critical when richer context features are available, and whether online A/B validation confirms the offline gains.
