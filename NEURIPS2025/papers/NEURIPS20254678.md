## Problem

Existing gait recognition networks treat gait as either shape/motion (appearance-based) or structure (model-based), leaving the concept ambiguous and making it difficult for researchers to verify whether a network truly captures intended gait semantics. Moreover, learning solely from gait modalities on limited databases produces features that lack universality under real-world covariates such as clothing changes, occlusions, and view variations. This paper asks: what is a gait, and how can human vocabulary knowledge from VLMs guide gait networks toward more universal, interpretable feature learning?

## Input / Output

The method takes a gait silhouette sequence of 30 frames at 64×44 resolution as the visual input, combined with 8 fixed vocabulary embeddings (one per gait cycle phase) extracted offline from a frozen text encoder (CLIP, LLaMA-3-8B, or DeepSeek-R1-Distill). The vocabulary features are shared across all body parts and all samples since gait cycle phases are an inherent attribute of walking. The output is a per-part gait feature embedding (C_g-dimensional) fed into a Gait Head with separate FCs and BNNeck for identity classification via triplet and cross-entropy losses.

## Method

α-Gait introduces two modules that bridge the modality gap between frozen VLM vocabulary embeddings and learned gait features. The Vocabulary Relation Mapper (VRM) first projects vocabulary features into the gait channel space via a linear transition, then applies a self-attention mechanism (with the linear mapping removed to preserve original vocabulary distribution) to establish inter-vocabulary relations, producing a normalized query vector Q_V. The Gait Fine-grained Detector (GFD) then performs DETR-style cross-attention where Q_V acts as the object query and the temporally-resolved gait part feature O (from a ResBlock/P3D encoder with horizontal partitioning) acts as the key/value, yielding a semantically-guided gait feature F. The key design insight is that VLMs are sensitive to global walking patterns (gait cycle phases) but not local silhouette details, so the 8 clinically-defined gait cycle phases serve as the minimal, complete vocabulary base that bridges the VLM and gait spaces. The text encoder remains frozen throughout training and inference, adding negligible runtime overhead.

## Results

On CASIA-B, α-Gait-T achieves 94.8% mean Rank-1 accuracy (98.9% NM, 96.8% BG, 88.6% CL), matching CLTD and approaching VPNet (94.9%). On the in-the-wild Gait3D benchmark, α-Gait-M reaches 76.3% Rank-1 / 67.8% mAP, surpassing VPNet (75.4%) and GaitMoE (73.7%). On GREW, α-Gait-L attains 81.2% Rank-1 / 92.7% Rank-10, exceeding VPNet (80.0%) and DeepGaitV2 (77.7%). On CCPG, α-Gait-S leads with 87.3% mean gait accuracy and 95.1% mean ReID accuracy, outperforming DeepGaitV2 by 4.0% and 1.4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

α-Gait consistently outperforms all 22 baselines across the five benchmarks. On Gait3D, it surpasses the strongest prior methods—VPNet (75.4%), DeepGaitV2 (74.4%), and GaitMoE (73.7%)—by 0.9–2.6% in Rank-1, and leads in mAP (67.8% vs. GaitMoE's 66.2%). On GREW, α-Gait-L beats VPNet by 1.2% Rank-1 and DeepGaitV2 by 3.5%, while also achieving the best Rank-10 (92.7% vs. DeepGaitV2's 91.8%). On CASIA-B, α-Gait-T (94.8%) ties CLTD and is within 0.1% of VPNet (94.9%), while clearly exceeding GaitGCI (93.1%), HSTL (94.3%), and DyGait (94.1%). On CCPG, the gap widens: α-Gait-S exceeds DeepGaitV2 by 4.0% in mean gait accuracy and 1.4% in ReID, and outperforms GaitBase by 11.8%. Model-based baselines (GaitGraph2, GaitTR, GPGait, SkeletonGait) are substantially weaker, with GaitGraph2 scoring only 11.2% on Gait3D and 5.0% on CCPG, confirming that vocabulary-guided appearance-based learning dominates in these settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both established gait recognition paradigms—appearance-based (silhouette/CNN) and model-based (skeleton/GCN)—by introducing a third paradigm, Gait-World, that uses VLM-extracted vocabulary features as semantic priors to guide gait network learning. The Gait Fine-grained Detector directly adapts DETR's query-based detection mechanism, repurposing vocabulary embeddings as object queries and gait part features as regions of interest. While text-guided recognition exists in face recognition (CFAM, CaptionFace, TGFR) and open-vocabulary detection (ViLD, Detic, OV-DETR), this is the first application of vocabulary-guided learning to gait, motivated by the observation that VLMs can recognize gait cycle phases from silhouettes despite failing on fine-grained local details.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Gait-World paradigm offers a lightweight, interpretable complement to existing gait methods: only eight frozen vocabulary embeddings are shared across all inputs, adding negligible runtime overhead while providing human-understandable semantic guidance. The consistent gains across appearance (+2.6%), model (+2.4%), and multimodal (+2.1%) backbones on Gait3D suggest the vocabulary signal is genuinely informative rather than an architectural artifact. However, the paper explicitly positions α-Gait as an initial attempt with only eight universal embeddings, and open questions remain about whether richer per-input language descriptions, expanded vocabularies (view, clothing, carrying), or true multimodal integration could unlock substantially larger benefits. The broader implication is that VLM vocabulary spaces may serve as a universal, covariate-robust prior for biometric recognition beyond gait.
