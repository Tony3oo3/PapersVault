## Problem

Existing synthetic data evaluation frameworks (FID, precision/recall, k-way marginals) cannot capture the structural properties and inter-field correlations of datasets that combine structured relationships with natural language fields. The paper demonstrates that synthetic data violating all structural constraints can still achieve high precision and recall, exposing a critical blind spot in current evaluation. Struct-Bench fills this gap by providing a structure-aware, composite benchmark for DP synthetic data derived from such hybrid datasets.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a real dataset, a corresponding synthetic dataset, a user-authored context-free grammar (CFG) encoding node types and structural relations, and an optional set of key nodes specified via Tregex tree patterns. It produces three metric families: structural metrics (CFG pass rate, key node dependency measured as Wasserstein-2 distance over cosine-similarity distributions of node-pair embeddings, and attribute match using Wasserstein-2 or total variation distance), non-structural metrics (KNN-Precision and KNN-Recall on sample embeddings), and downstream task accuracy via a train-synthetic-test-real protocol with LLM-generated labels and Longformer fine-tuning.

## Method

Struct-Bench parses each sample with the user-provided CFG to construct a parse tree, from which it programmatically extracts nodes and their attributes. Structural evaluation proceeds at three granularities: whole-sample (CFG-PR), node-pair (KND, where Tregex-specified key node pairs are scored by embedding cosine similarity and compared via distributional distance), and individual-node/sample (AM, comparing attribute distributions between real and synthetic data). Non-structural evaluation follows the standard KNN-Precision/Recall protocol on sentence embeddings. Downstream evaluation uses GPT-4o to generate labels, fine-tunes a Longformer on the synthetic data, and measures accuracy on a held-out real test set. The paper further demonstrates two algorithmic improvements to Private Evolution: LLM-assisted reformatting (prompting the model to fix CFG-invalid samples before or after private voting) and node extraction with auto-generation (extracting specific node types for blank-filling while conditionally generating remaining nodes), which together raise CFG-PR to ~94% while improving semantic diversity.

## Results

On ShareGPT (ε=4), PE with GPT-4o achieves the highest CFG-PR (0.86) but KNN-Recall drops to 0.15, while non-private FT (ε=∞) achieves CFG-PR of 0.53 with KNN-Recall of 0.66. On the more complex ICLR dataset, all baselines score below 0.2 on CFG-PR, and DP-FT achieves 0 on all datasets. The case study demonstrates that combining LLM-assisted reformatting with node extraction and auto-generation improves PE's CFG-PR to ~94% on ShareGPT while also boosting KNN-Recall and KNN-Precision over vanilla PE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four DP synthetic data generation approaches: Private Evolution (PE), Instruction Following (IF, a zero-shot PE variant with ε=0), DP Fine-Tuning (DP-FT via DP-SGD), and non-private Fine-Tuning (FT, ε=∞) as an upper bound. PE clearly dominates on structural validity: at ε=4, PE achieves CFG-PR of 0.57 (GPT-2) and 0.86 (GPT-4o) on ShareGPT, while DP-FT scores 0 on all datasets and FT only reaches 0.53. However, PE's structural advantage comes at a severe semantic diversity cost—KNN-Recall drops to 0.15 (PE+GPT-4o) versus 0.66 (FT) on ShareGPT, and to 0.02 versus 0.47 on ICLR. Instruct DP-FT narrows the gap with PE on ShareGPT (CFG-PR 0.55 vs. 0.57) when both use GPT-2, suggesting instruction-guided generation partially compensates for the lack of iterative private voting. The case study shows that augmenting PE with LLM-assisted reformatting and node extraction pushes CFG-PR to ~94% while simultaneously improving KNN-Recall, demonstrating that the structural-semantic tradeoff is not inherent to PE but can be mitigated with targeted post-processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP synthetic data evaluation landscape (which has prior benchmarks for images, unstructured text, and tabular data) to the underexplored setting of structured text containing natural language fields. It draws conceptual inspiration from the GEM NLG benchmark [12] and repurposes context-free grammars as a lightweight formalism for encoding dataset structure, departing from the k-way marginal comparisons used in tabular evaluation and the FID/precision-recall metrics used for unstructured data. The case study builds directly on Private Evolution (PE) [60], modifying its Random and Variation APIs with LLM-assisted reformatting and node extraction, while also benchmarking against DP-SGD fine-tuning [63, 59, 65] as a training-based alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a fundamental gap: no existing SOTA DP synthetic data generator can simultaneously capture structural constraints and semantic diversity, with DP-FT achieving zero CFG pass rate on most datasets and PE/IF with frontier models sacrificing KNN-Recall to near-zero while achieving high structural compliance. This structure-semantics tradeoff is a concrete open problem that the field has not previously been able to measure or articulate. Limitations include the reliance on CFG (less expressive than context-sensitive grammars), the use of LLM-based labeling for downstream tasks, and the fact that the case study improvements (reformatting + node extraction) address structure and diversity but leave key node dependency (KND) largely unresolved.
