## Problem

RLVR training for LLM reasoning requires high-quality problem sets with verifiable answers, but existing datasets are either scarce (human-labeled), lack rigorous answer verification (distillation-oriented synthetic sets), or expand indiscriminately without considering the model's current capabilities. This leads to problems that are either too trivial or too hard, causing gradient vanishing in group-level RL algorithms like GRPO and wasting training signal. The paper asks: can consistently failed problems be systematically leveraged to synthesize targeted training data that addresses the model's specific reasoning deficiencies?

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes an initial math problem set (MATH-12k for models ≤7B; a 17.5k-sample DAPO+LightR1 combination for 14B/32B) paired with ground-truth answers, plus a pre-trained LLM policy model. During preliminary RL training, per-problem accuracy trajectories are tracked across epochs to identify weaknesses. The output is 40k synthetic problems per model, constrained to single-answer format (no multiple-choice, multi-part, or proof-based questions), with reference answers verified via self-consistency (≥50% agreement across multiple rollouts) and difficulty-filtered to a 25%–75% accuracy window for the initially trained model. The final augmented training set concatenates the original problems with the retained synthetic ones.

## Method

SwS is a three-stage framework. First, Self-aware Weakness Identification runs preliminary GRPO training and flags problems whose maximum accuracy never reaches 50% and whose accuracy trend has a negative slope across epochs. Second, Targeted Problem Synthesis extracts underlying mathematical concepts from the failed problems, groups them by category, and recombines them using co-occurrence probabilities and embedding similarities to ensure semantic coherence; the augmentation budget is allocated proportionally to each category's failure rate. A strong instruction model (LLaMA-3.3-70B) generates problems from the recombined concepts, multiple LLMs verify quality (concept coverage, factual accuracy, solvability), a reasoning model (QwQ-32B) labels answers via self-consistency with Math-Verify equivalence checking, and difficulty filtering retains only problems in the learnable accuracy range. Third, Augmented Training continues RLVR for 600 steps on the combined set, using dynamic sampling that only updates on prompts with 10%–90% online accuracy, clip-higher (ε=0.20, ε^h=0.28), and no KL penalty. The key distinction from prior synthesis work is that problem generation is explicitly conditioned on the model's own failure patterns rather than random concept sampling or rephrasing, and the difficulty filter ensures synthetic problems remain in the informative gradient region for GRPO.

## Results

SwS achieves average absolute improvements of +10.0% (56.7 vs. 46.7) for the 7B model and +7.7% (68.4 vs. 60.7) for the 32B model over their respective BaseRL counterparts across eight mathematical reasoning benchmarks. The largest gains appear on competition-level tasks: AIME24 improves by +16.7% (7B) and AIME25 by +33.3% (32B). On the math-specialized 7B variant, SwS-Math-7B reaches 58.3% average, surpassing Oat-Zero-7B (56.8%) and PRIME-RL-7B (53.3%). The framework also yields consistent gains on smaller models: +2.5% for 3B and +2.3% for LLaMA-8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SwS-7B (56.7 avg) outperforms Open-Reasoner-7B (53.3) by 3.4 points and SimpleRL-Base-7B (49.2) by 7.5 points, with the largest margin on AMC23 (+22.5 over BaseRL) and AIME24 Avg@1 (+16.7). At 32B scale, SwS-32B (68.4) surpasses Open-Reasoner-32B (58.5) by 9.9 points and SimpleRL-Base-32B (59.4) by 9.0 points, with a striking +33.3 gain on AIME25 Avg@1. On the math-specialized track, SwS-Math-7B (58.3) beats Oat-Zero-7B (56.8) by 1.5 and PRIME-RL-7B (53.3) by 5.0, confirming that weakness-driven synthesis generalizes beyond base-model training. Across all four compared baselines, SwS wins on the average metric for every model size tested (3B, 7B, 8B, 32B), with no benchmark where it underperforms its direct BaseRL counterpart by more than 0.5 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO/DAPO RLVR training pipeline (Shao et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025) and the concept-extraction-and-recombination paradigm from KP-Math (Huang et al., 2024), but departs from prior problem synthesis work (MetaMath, MathScale, PromptCoT, WizardMath) by making synthesis explicitly driven by the model's own failure cases identified during RL training rather than by indiscriminate expansion or human-curated concept pools. The key conceptual shift is treating the RL training process itself as a diagnostic tool: problems the model consistently fails to learn (never reaching 50% accuracy with a negative trend) become seeds for targeted augmentation, closing a loop between training dynamics and data construction that prior synthesis pipelines lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in RLVR—scarcity of verifiable-answer problems matched to model difficulty—by generating 40k targeted synthetic problems whose total synthesis cost (~8,300 GPU-hours) is comparable to the augmented training itself (~9,500 GPU-hours). Open questions remain: the self-evolving setting (using the policy model itself for generation) yields only +1.2% improvement, suggesting current open-source instruction models cannot generate sufficiently hard problems for competition-level benchmarks; the framework is restricted to RL with verifiable rewards and has not been extended to SFT/distillation or non-mathematical tasks; and the reliance on a strong external reasoning model (QwQ-32B) for answer labeling limits applicability to frontier models where no stronger teacher exists, though the weak-to-strong extension partially addresses this.
