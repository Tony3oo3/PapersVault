## Problem

Existing LLM-generated text detectors frame detection as binary classification, implicitly assuming human-written texts form a single coherent distribution. The paper argues this assumption is fundamentally flawed: human texts are inherently heterogeneous across authors, styles, and domains, causing classifiers to overfit to limited observed samples and fail on unseen human inputs. The paper provides a theoretical proof (via Pearson χ² divergence) that binary classifiers can achieve near-optimal training loss while suffering arbitrarily large real-world error, motivating a reformulation as an out-of-distribution detection problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single candidate text as input, encodes it via a RoBERTa backbone into a d-dimensional embedding, and produces a continuous OOD score whose interpretation depends on the chosen detector: a hypersphere distance (DeepSVDD), a sigmoid probability (HRN), or a log-sum-exp energy value (Energy-based). At inference, the score is thresholded to yield a binary human/machine label. The framework is model-agnostic in the sense that any of the three OOD scoring heads can be swapped in without changing the encoder or pipeline.

## Method

The core reformulation treats machine-generated text as the in-distribution (ID) class and human text as out-of-distribution (OOD), so the model only needs to characterize the compact LLM-generated cluster rather than model the open-ended human distribution. Concretely, a RoBERTa encoder maps text to embeddings, and one of three OOD losses is applied: DeepSVDD minimizes the mean squared distance from embeddings to a learned center; HRN trains a one-class head with an H-regularization term that penalizes excessive input sensitivity; and the energy-based method learns a contrastively shaped energy surface with separate margins for ID and OOD samples. A SimCLR-style contrastive loss is added to all variants to sharpen the human-machine separation in embedding space. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 2) shows that when the χ² divergence between the training human distribution and the true open-world human distribution is large, any binary classifier achieving suboptimality Δ on training data incurs loss at least 0.9·q_H·(D+1)·Δ on real data, formally justifying the OOD formulation.

## Results

On the DeepFake dataset, the proposed DeepSVDD-based OOD detector achieves 98.3% AUROC and 98.3% AUPR with only 8.9% FPR95, outperforming the strongest training-based baseline DeTeCtive (95.6% AUROC, 97.1% AUPR, 16.1% FPR95) by 2.7 points in AUROC and 7.2 points in FPR95. On M4-multilingual, all three OOD variants (DeepSVDD, HRN, Energy) reach 96.2–96.4% AUROC, surpassing DeTeCtive's 93.1% AUROC, while the Energy-based method achieves a notably low FPR95 of 10.7% versus DeTeCtive's 52.4%. On the adversarial RAID benchmark, the Energy-based variant attains 99.7% AUPR and 28.3% FPR95, and DeepSVDD achieves the highest AUROC of 94.7%, both substantially exceeding DeTeCtive (85.1% AUROC, 55.7% AUPR, 74.3% FPR95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed OOD framework wins across all three benchmarks and all three metrics against every one of the 12 baselines. Against the strongest training-based baseline DeTeCtive, the DeepSVDD variant gains +2.7 AUROC and −7.2 FPR95 on DeepFake (98.3 vs. 95.6 AUROC; 8.9 vs. 16.1 FPR95), +3.3 AUROC and −34.2 FPR95 on M4-multilingual (96.4 vs. 93.1 AUROC; 18.2 vs. 52.4 FPR95), and +9.6 AUROC and −46.0 FPR95 on RAID (94.7 vs. 85.1 AUROC; 38.3 vs. 74.3 FPR95). Among zero-shot methods, the best performer Glimpse reaches only 69.5 AUROC on DeepFake, trailing DeepSVDD by 28.8 points; Fast-DetectGPT and Binoculars similarly lag by 20–35 AUROC points. Among other training-based baselines, BiScope (79.2 AUROC on DeepFake) and RADAR (61.2 AUROC) are outperformed by 19 and 37 AUROC points respectively, while GhostBuster (52.9 AUROC) and GPTZero (59.3 AUROC) show the largest gaps. The HRN variant achieves the lowest FPR95 of 4.4% on DeepFake, and the Energy variant achieves the best AUPR of 99.7% on RAID, demonstrating complementary strengths across the three OOD formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant binary classification paradigm in LLM-generated text detection (exemplified by DeTeCtive, GPTZero, RADAR, and zero-shot methods like DetectGPT) by reframing the problem as out-of-distribution detection. It repurposes well-established OOD techniques from computer vision—DeepSVDD, HRN, and energy-based scoring—into a text detection pipeline, combined with a SimCLR-style contrastive loss for representation learning. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 2) formally proves that binary classifiers trained on limited human-text samples will generalize poorly when the true human distribution is heterogeneous, providing a principled justification for the OOD formulation that prior detection work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing from binary classification to OOD detection offers a more principled and generalizable framework for AI-text detection, with the practical benefit that the detector only needs to model the compact LLM distribution rather than the open-ended human distribution. This could reduce the need for ever-larger and more diverse human-text training sets as new LLMs and domains emerge. Open questions include whether the assumption that LLM outputs form a coherent in-distribution cluster holds as model families diversify, and how the approach performs when the detection target shifts from 'any LLM' to a specific model. The paper does not report error bars or statistical significance, and the energy-based method's superior AUPR on RAID (99.7) versus its weaker AUROC on unseen models (90.2) suggests the method choice is scenario-dependent rather than universally dominant.
