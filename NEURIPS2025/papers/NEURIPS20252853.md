## Problem

Current digital twin models of mouse visual cortex accurately predict single-neuron responses but fail to capture population-level representational transformations across the visual hierarchy, particularly the progressive emergence of invariant object recognition from V1 to higher areas. While these models reproduce low-level properties such as receptive field size gradients, they do not reproduce the experimentally observed monotonic increase in rotation-invariant object decoding or object discriminability along the cortical hierarchy (V1 < LM < RL < AL).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252853/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes grayscale video clips (36×64 pixels at 30 fps) alongside behavioral signals (pupil size, pupil center coordinates, running speed) and produces frame-by-frame predicted firing rates for individual neurons across four visual areas (V1, LM, RL, AL), trained on ~115,000 units from the MICrONS two-photon calcium imaging dataset. Evaluation extends beyond single-neuron correlation to population-level metrics: receptive field sizes via spike-triggered averaging on white noise, rotation-invariant object classification accuracy using linear SVMs on PCA-projected responses (92 object classes, 15 rotations), and object discriminability quantified by mutual information from one-vs-rest logistic regression on 128-neuron subsets.

## Method

The core is a 3D CNN with factorized spatial-temporal convolutions, but the key contribution is a hierarchical readout scheme in which each visual area's neurons are projected from a distinct depth of the shared core network, aligned with anatomical hierarchy scores (V1 from layer 6, LM/RL from layer 7, AL from layer 8 of an 8-layer core). This contrasts with the standard baseline where all areas read from the final layer, and introduces an inductive bias that forces the network to progressively transform representations in a manner consistent with cortical anatomy. Training uses a Poisson loss with area-specific readout modules (learnable spatial masks + linear feature weights), a behavioral MLP, and a shifter module for gaze-related receptive field repositioning. The authors demonstrate that anatomical alignment is the critical factor: permuting area-to-layer assignments systematically degrades population-level performance, while alternatives including reweighted losses, unconstrained multi-layer readouts, increased depth (6–8 layers), and the large-scale Wang et al. (2025) foundation model all fail to recover the hierarchical gradient in invariant object recognition.

## Results

The baseline 4-layer digital twin captures the receptive field size gradient across visual areas (Kendall's τ = 0.50) but fails on both population-level object recognition tasks, yielding negative Kendall's τ for the Hoeller rotation-invariance task and the Froudarakis object-discriminability task. The proposed hierarchical readout model (8-layer core with area-specific readout depths: V1→layer 6, LM/RL→layer 7, AL→layer 8) achieves positive Kendall's τ on all three metrics, reproducing the experimentally observed monotonic increase in RF size, rotation-invariant decoding accuracy, and object discriminability from V1 to AL. At the single-neuron level, the hierarchical model matches or exceeds all other variants, with notably improved prediction accuracy in AL compared to the 8-layer baseline. The state-of-the-art foundation model of Wang et al. (2025) achieves median CC_norm of 0.58–0.76 across sessions but predicts AL, RL, and LM as nearly identical, failing to recover fine-scale hierarchical distinctions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed hierarchical readout against four categories of alternatives: (1) the standard baseline (all areas read from the final core layer), (2) a reweighted-loss variant that balances area contributions, (3) an all-layer readout variant giving each neuron access to all core layers, (4) deeper baselines (6- and 8-layer cores), and (5) the large-scale foundation model of Wang et al. (2025). The hierarchical readout outperforms all alternatives on the two population-level object recognition tasks (Hoeller and Froudarakis), where every other variant either shows flat or reversed area orderings (negative Kendall's τ). The 6-layer baseline partially recovers the Hoeller ordering (assigning higher discriminability to LM, RL, AL over V1) but still misranks AL below LM and fails on Froudarakis. The Wang et al. (2025) foundation model, despite having ~4.5M core parameters and 54M readout parameters (vs. 3.4M and 17.4M for the hierarchical model), captures only the broad V1-vs-higher distinction and predicts AL, RL, and LM as nearly interchangeable. The hierarchical model also improves single-neuron CC_norm in AL relative to the 8-layer baseline, suggesting that anatomically aligned readout depths facilitate learning of area-specific representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Sensorium 2023 3D-CNN architecture (Turishcheva et al., 2024) and the Gaussian spatial readout of Lurz et al. (2020) to construct a digital twin of the mouse visual cortex. It departs from prior digital twin work (Cadena et al., 2019; Nayebi et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025) in two key ways: (1) it introduces a hierarchical readout that assigns each cortical area to a distinct depth in the shared core, aligned with anatomical hierarchy scores, rather than reading all areas from a single final layer; and (2) it shifts evaluation from single-neuron prediction accuracy to population-level representational geometry, specifically testing whether models reproduce experimentally observed gradients in receptive field size and invariant object recognition across V1, LM, RL, and AL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a digital twin can reproduce the progressive emergence of invariant object representations along the mouse visual cortical hierarchy, and it shows that anatomical alignment of readout depths—not merely increased model scale or depth—provides the critical inductive bias. The finding that the 54M-parameter Wang et al. (2025) foundation model fails to capture fine-grained inter-area distinctions while a smaller hierarchically-constrained model succeeds challenges the assumption that scaling alone resolves representational geometry. Open questions include whether the model preserves equivariant representations alongside invariance, why area RL shows contradictory performance across the Hoeller and Froudarakis paradigms, and whether explicit inter-area feedback or recurrent connections would further improve biological fidelity.
