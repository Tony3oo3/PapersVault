## Problem

In sparse-reward, partially observable MARL without communication, existing curiosity-driven exploration methods suffer from two critical failures: they confuse environmental stochasticity with meaningful novelty (the Noisy-TV problem), and they treat all unexpected observations uniformly, overlooking the socially informative novelty encoded in peers' behaviors. This results in agents being persistently distracted by others' actions rather than leveraging them to infer task dynamics, leading to suboptimal exploration in decentralized settings.

## Input / Output

CERMIC operates on low-dimensional state embeddings s_t and s_{t+1} (projected from raw observations via shared-structure encoders with momentum-updated targets), the current action a_t, the received extrinsic reward r_t^e, and an inferred multi-agent context feature f_n^o produced by a GNN over a dynamically updated intention graph. The intention graph is only advanced when an MLP-based agent detector confirms the presence of other agents, ensuring efficient computation under partial observability. The module outputs a theoretically grounded intrinsic reward r_t^i (a Bayesian Surprise computed as the square root of a KL divergence between conditional and marginal posteriors of CERMIC parameters), a calibrated curiosity latent x_t, and combined exploration/exploitation loss signals for self-training.

## Method

CERMIC is built on the Information Bottleneck principle, learning a Gaussian latent x_t ~ g_φ(s_t, a_t) that maximizes I(X_t; S_{t+1}) (predictive novelty for exploration) while minimizing I(X_t; [S_t, A_t]) (compression). The key innovation is replacing the standard KL-based compression with a distributionally robust chance constraint conditioned on the inferred multi-agent context f_n^o, which is converted via Cantelli's inequality into a tractable second-order cone loss (ReLU of μ_Ψ(f_n^o) + β√Σ_Ψ − c̄) that adaptively dampens exploration when peer context is unreliable. A task-adaptive calibration factor γ, estimated via InfoNCE bounds on the mutual information between the current inferred intention and the preceding context (previous intention + extrinsic reward), automatically down-weights unreliable intention inferences early in training or when task dynamics are misaligned. The intrinsic reward is derived from Bayesian Surprise over CERMIC's own parameters, and the paper proves it is tightly bounded by the LSVI-UCB exploration bonus in linear MDPs, ensuring it naturally decays as uncertainty diminishes.

## Results

MAPPO-CERMIC achieves state-of-the-art performance on 12 of 15 evaluated tasks across VMAS, MeltingPot, and SMACv2 under sparse-reward settings. Headline gains include 67.3 mean episodic reward on Balance* (vs. 63.2 for QPLEX-ICES), 10.03 on ChiGam (vs. 9.07), 6.74 on PriDil (vs. 5.42), and 1.06 on Flocki* (vs. 0.63). On MeltingPot, where rewards depend on emergent inter-agent dynamics, CERMIC shows its largest margins, e.g., 78.3 on CleanUp vs. 76.7 for the best baseline. QMIX-CERMIC also improves over QMIX on several tasks (e.g., Transp* 0.84 vs. 0.42) but is generally outperformed by its MAPPO counterpart.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAPPO-CERMIC outperforms the strongest curiosity baseline QPLEX-ICES on 9 of 15 tasks, with the largest margins on Flocki* (1.06 vs. 0.63), PriDil (6.74 vs. 5.42), ChiGam (10.03 vs. 9.07), and Balance* (67.3 vs. 63.2), while trailing slightly on GiveWa* (3.94 vs. 3.96), Wheel* (-1.47 vs. -1.44), StaHun (7.11 vs. 7.20), and pro5v5* (0.70 vs. 0.74). Against MACE, CERMIC wins on most tasks but loses on Sampli* (25.4 vs. 28.1) and Passag* (172 vs. 166 is actually a win; MACE leads on Sampli*). Compared to the standard MARL baselines MAPPO and QMIX, CERMIC consistently improves, e.g., Balance* 67.3 vs. 60.3 (MAPPO) and 48.5 (QMIX). Against the SoTA methods CPM and QMIX-SPECTRA, MAPPO-CERMIC achieves superior results on the majority of tasks, particularly in sparse-reward settings (e.g., Passag* 172 vs. 162 for CPM). The single-agent curiosity method MAPPO-DB is consistently outperformed, and Table 2 further shows its per-agent contribution degrades with more agents (12.1 at 8 agents) while CERMIC improves (17.3), confirming the value of multi-agent contextual calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

CERMIC extends the Information Bottleneck framework for curiosity-driven exploration established by Dynamic Bottleneck (Bai et al., NeurIPS 2021) and the original IB principle (Tishby et al.), which formulate exploration as maximizing mutual information with next states while compressing the current context. The key departure is the introduction of a distributionally robust, multi-agent contextual calibration of the compression term: rather than treating all surprise uniformly (as in MAPPO-DB, MACE, or ICES), CERMIC conditions the chance constraint on a graph-neural-network-encoded intention model of peer agents, using Cantelli's inequality to convert the DRO formulation into a tractable second-order cone loss. The task-adaptive calibration factor γ, estimated via InfoNCE mutual information between consecutive intention states and extrinsic rewards, further distinguishes CERMIC from prior single-agent or centralized curiosity methods by automatically down-weighting unreliable peer inferences early in training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CERMIC addresses a practically important gap: robust curiosity-driven exploration in decentralized, communication-free MARL under severe partial observability, where prior methods either rely on centralized training signals or treat all novelty uniformly. The plug-and-play design (integrating with MAPPO and QMIX) and consistent SOTA gains across VMAS, MeltingPot, and SMACv2 make it immediately applicable. However, the intention modeling from local observations alone remains a bottleneck—pre-trained detectors and graph encoders are needed for best performance, and the authors acknowledge that scaling to higher-dimensional or multi-modal agent interactions will require hierarchical latent models. Open questions include whether LLM-based intention bootstrapping can replace the ad-hoc GNN module, and whether the calibration mechanism generalizes to adversarial or mixed-motive settings beyond the cooperative benchmarks tested.
