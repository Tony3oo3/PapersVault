## Problem

In two-sided matching markets with tied worker preferences, no single stable matching is simultaneously optimal for all workers, and existing bandit learning approaches break down when utility differences are statistically indistinguishable. The paper asks whether distributions over matchings can approximate a stable solution while guaranteeing every worker a fair fraction of their optimal stable share (OSS), and how to extend this to online learning with only partial reward observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253233/cropped_page22_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The offline oracle takes an N×K utility matrix U with entries in [0,1] (encoding potentially tied worker preferences) and strict job-side preference profiles P_a, and outputs a uniform distribution over m = ⌈log₂N + 2⌉ internally stable matchings, where each worker is matched in exactly one matching. In the bandit setting, the algorithm receives 1-sub-Gaussian reward observations only for matched worker-job pairs over T rounds and produces a sequence of matchings with per-worker regret guarantees. The robustness variant accepts a rectangular uncertainty set U and adds an ε-additive error term to the guarantee.

## Method

The core offline algorithm (Algorithm 1) duplicates each job m times, runs Gale-Shapley on the expanded instance with a tie-breaking rule that prioritizes lower duplication indices, then extracts m internally stable matchings from the result. The key technical insight is a directed forest construction where nodes are workers who prefer some stable matching over the algorithm's output, and edges encode conflicts over shared job copies; the tree structure combined with stability constraints yields an inductive O(log N) upper bound on the OSS-ratio. For online learning, ETCO (Explore-then-Choose-Oracle) performs round-robin exploration to build confidence intervals, then adaptively switches between Gale-Shapley (when all top-N CIs are disjoint, indicating strict preferences) and the approximation oracle (when CIs overlap, indicating statistical ties). The lower bound proof constructs two 4×4 serial dictatorship instances differing in a single utility entry, using an information-theoretic argument to establish a fundamental trade-off: sublinear standard regret in large-gap regimes necessitates Ω(T^{1-2δ}) approximation regret in zero-gap regimes.

## Results

The paper establishes tight approximation ratios for stable matching with ties: restricting to stable matchings yields a linear Ω(N) OSS-ratio (Theorem 1), while allowing all matchings gives a logarithmic Ω(log N) lower bound (Theorem 2) that is matched by an algorithm over internally stable matchings achieving O(log N) (Theorem 3). In the bandit learning setting, the ETCO algorithm achieves O(K ln T / Δ_min²) standard regret when Δ_min = Ω(√(K ln T / T₀)) and O((K ln T)^{1/3} T^{2/3}) approximation regret when Δ_min = Õ(T^{-1/3}) (Corollary 1). A fundamental trade-off lower bound (Theorem 8) shows no algorithm can simultaneously achieve sublinear regret in both large-gap and small/no-gap regimes, with approximation regret of Ω(T^{1-2δ}) in the tied regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines, so comparisons are between matching classes and theoretical regimes. The key comparison is between restricting to stable matchings (Ω(N) loss) versus allowing internally stable matchings (O(log N) loss), showing that relaxing stability from weak to internal yields an exponential improvement in the approximation ratio. In the bandit setting, ETCO subsumes the prior ETGS algorithm [37] in the large-gap regime (matching the Ω(N ln T / Δ_min²) lower bound of [52]) while extending to the tied regime where prior methods [42, 8, 37] provide no guarantees. Theorem 8 establishes that this extension comes at a provable cost: any algorithm achieving sublinear standard regret for all large-gap instances must incur Ω(T^{1-2δ}) approximation regret on some tied instance, a trade-off not present in standard combinatorial bandit literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gale-Shapley stable matching framework and the multi-player bandit matching literature (Liu et al. 2020; Kong & Li 2023) to handle one-sided indifference, a setting where prior bandit results become ill-defined. While earlier work on stable matching with ties (Irving 1994; Halldórsson et al. 2003) focused on maximizing matching size, this paper introduces the OSS-ratio as a fairness metric and the duplication-index technique (Algorithm 1) to achieve a tight O(log N) approximation over internally stable matchings. In the online setting, the paper departs from the explore-then-commit paradigm of Kong & Li by adding an adaptive oracle-switching mechanism that detects statistical ties via confidence intervals and falls back to an approximation oracle, bridging the gap between strict-preference and tied-preference regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first instance-independent worker-optimal stable regret bound for bandit matching with ties and proves a fundamental trade-off (Theorem 8) showing that no algorithm can simultaneously achieve optimal regret in both large-gap and small-gap preference regimes — a structural limitation that goes beyond computational intractability. The tight O(log N) OSS-ratio with incentive compatibility provides a practical fairness guarantee for real-world markets (e.g., conference reviewer assignment, crowd-sourcing platforms) where discrete ratings create natural ties. Key open questions include extending guarantees to decentralized settings where workers cannot coordinate, and validating the algorithms on real-world datasets, both of which the authors identify as important future directions.
