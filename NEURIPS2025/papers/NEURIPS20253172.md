## Problem

Geometric diffusion models achieve strong task-specific performance but lack a mechanism to transfer across downstream tasks with different geometric control signals (e.g., changing the number of conditioning frames, adding a global class label, or conditioning on a protein pocket). Full fine-tuning and prompt-based adaptation methods suffer from overfitting on limited downstream data and catastrophic forgetting of the pretrained task, and no prior framework provides a parameter-efficient, equivariance-preserving adapter for geometric diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noised geometric graph (nodes with 3D coordinates x_i ∈ R³ and feature vectors h_i ∈ R^H, plus edges), a control signal of one of three types—frame sequences (additional T̃ frames per node), a global type vector c̃ ∈ R^K, or a subgraph (Ṽ, Ē)—and a diffusion timestep τ. The output is a conditional score ε_θ(G_τ, τ) + s_{θ',φ}(G_τ, τ, C), where the adapter term s is added to the frozen denoiser's score; this composite score drives the reverse transition kernel at sampling time. The control is injected via type-specific coupling operators (node-feature addition for global, supergraph union for subgraph, temporal concatenation for frames) and the adapter output is decoupled back to the original node/frame subset before being added.

## Method

GeoAda inserts B lightweight equivariant adapter blocks into a frozen pretrained geometric diffusion denoiser, each block comprising a coupling operator f, a trainable copy of one selected pretrained layer (initialized from the pretrained checkpoint), a decoupling operator g, and an equivariant zero-convolution. The zero-convolution (z_φ) scales coordinates relative to the center-of-mass by a scalar φ_x and scales node features element-wise by φ_h, both initialized to zero, so the adapter contributes exactly zero at initialization—preserving the pretrained score while remaining differentiable. Only the trainable layer copies and zero-convolution parameters are optimized via the standard denoising loss on the downstream dataset, while the entire pretrained backbone stays frozen. The paper proves (Proposition 4.1) that the composition g ∘ ε_θ ∘ f is SE(3)-equivariant for all three control types, guaranteeing that the adapted model retains the geometric inductive bias and SE(3)-invariant marginals of the original diffusion model. Empirically, selecting the first layer from every K consecutive pretrained layers as the trainable copy outperforms naive initial/final layer choices under the same parameter budget.

## Results

On the Charged Particles dataset, GeoAda achieves ADE 1.119 and FDE 2.669 on the downstream task (15-frame conditioning), matching Full FT (1.093/2.676) while tuning only half the parameters, and critically preserves pretrain performance (ADE 1.175 vs. 1.177 for the original model) whereas Full FT degrades to ADE 5.998 and most other baselines collapse to NaN. On MD17 molecular dynamics, GeoAda achieves the best ADE across all five molecules (e.g., 0.891 on Aspirin vs. 0.929 for Full FT) while retaining pretrain accuracy, whereas Full FT and prompt-based methods show NaN or inf on the original task. On CMU Mocap, GeoAda achieves the best short-term prediction (e.g., 8.92 ms at 80 ms horizon for walking) and long-term prediction (e.g., 34.52 ms at 560 ms for walking) across all action categories while preserving pretrain performance. On CrossDocked2020 molecule generation, GeoAda (QM9-pretrained) achieves a Vina Score of −5.54, 57.4% high-affinity rate, and 0.74 diversity, matching or surpassing TargetDiff (−5.47, 58.1%, 0.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoAda consistently outperforms all fine-tuning and prompt-based baselines on downstream tasks while uniquely preserving pretrain performance. Full FT achieves comparable downstream ADE/FDE (1.093/2.676 vs. 1.119/2.669 on Charged Particles) but catastrophically forgets the pretrain task (ADE jumps from 1.177 to 5.998, and unconditional generation collapses to NaN). PARTIAL-k [12] and GPF/GPF-plus [6] are 35–40% worse on downstream ADE (1.6–1.7 vs. 1.1) and also collapse on pretrain evaluation. MLP-k (EGTN head-only) is the weakest baseline, with ADE 6.258 on downstream and complete pretrain failure. On molecule generation (CrossDocked2020), GeoAda matches or exceeds TargetDiff [10] on Vina Score (−5.54 vs. −5.47) and diversity (0.74–0.76 vs. 0.72), while substantially outperforming liGAN [25], GraphBP [19], AR [21], and Pocket2Mol [24] on binding affinity and drug-likeness metrics. The key differentiator is that GeoAda is the only method that avoids catastrophic forgetting across all four experimental domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoAda directly adapts the ControlNet paradigm (lightweight trainable branches on a frozen diffusion backbone) to the geometric diffusion setting, where the critical additional constraint is preserving SE(3)-equivariance throughout fine-tuning. It builds on the geometric diffusion model lineage (EDM, GeoDiff, GeoTDM, TargetDiff) and the parameter-efficient fine-tuning literature for GNNs (AdapterGNN, GPF), but is the first to provide a principled, equivariance-preserving adapter design with coupling/decoupling operators for three distinct geometric control types (frame, global, subgraph). The equivariant zero-convolution is a geometric analogue of ControlNet's zero-conv that explicitly enforces rotation-equivariance and translation-invariance of the adapter output, going beyond the standard zero-initialization trick.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in geometric ML: pretrained geometric diffusion models are expensive to train, yet downstream tasks (e.g., pocket-conditioned ligand generation, trajectory prediction with different observation lengths) require adaptation to new control signals. GeoAda's ability to match full fine-tuning quality while preserving pretrain-task performance (avoiding the catastrophic forgetting that causes baselines to produce NaN/inf outputs) makes it a practical tool for labs with limited compute. Open questions include whether the adapter approach scales to multi-task or multi-control settings simultaneously, whether the sudden convergence phenomenon (68–73% loss drop over ~200 steps) can be better understood or controlled, and whether the method extends to higher-dimensional symmetry groups (e.g., SE(4) for protein backbone dynamics).
