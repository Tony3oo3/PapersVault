## Problem

Existing few-shot optimization methods either employ meta-learning models not designed for optimization (performing data adaptation rather than model parameter adaptation) or focus on meta-learning acquisition functions specific to Bayesian optimization, limiting their applicability to surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms (SAEAs). Moreover, few-shot optimization for expensive multi-objective problems (EMOPs) has been largely unexplored despite EMOPs imposing stricter surrogate modeling requirements than single-objective settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250781/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes small datasets (|D_m| samples each) collected from N related expensive tasks for meta-learning, plus a target task with a limited evaluation budget. A key design choice is that only 1d samples (vs. the conventional 10d) are drawn from the target task for surrogate initialization, with the remaining budget spent on evolutionary search. The output is an archive of evaluated solutions forming an approximate Pareto front, alongside the adapted MDKL surrogate whose task-specific kernel parameters (θ*, p*) encode the target task's unique features on top of the meta-learned experience.

## Method

The core contribution is Meta Deep Kernel Learning (MDKL), which constructs a deep kernel by passing inputs through a neural network φ(w,b) into a GP base kernel with parameters θ and p (Eq. 2). The architecture explicitly separates task-independent parameters γ^e = {w, b, θ^e, p^e}, meta-learned across related tasks via gradient descent on negative log-likelihood over small support sets, from task-specific increments Δθ*, Δp* that are adapted on the target task's growing archive. This separation enables continual parameter adaptation during optimization—unlike prior meta-learning surrogates whose parameters are frozen after meta-training. The FSEO framework embeds the MDKL surrogate into a regression-based SAEA (instantiated as MOEA/D-FS on MOEA/D-EGO), with an MSE-based update strategy that accepts or rejects each surrogate adaptation to prevent overfitting on small archives.

## Results

On 7 DTLZ multi-objective problems (10 decision variables, 3 objectives), MOEA/D-FS (FSEO instantiated on MOEA/D-EGO) uses only 10 samples (1d) for surrogate initialization versus 100 samples (10d) for baselines, saving 90 expensive evaluations. MOEA/D-FS achieves competitive or better IGD+ values on 6 out of 7 DTLZ problems, with IGD+ dropping rapidly in the first few evaluations. In the 'out-of-range' ablation (excluding the most similar related task), performance remains competitive (e.g., DTLZ2: 0.141 vs. 0.143 IGD+), confirming robustness to task similarity. On a real-world NAS problem (Transformer architecture, 2 objectives), MOEA/D-FS saves 82 evaluations while reaching competitive Hypervolume within 50 additional evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MOEA/D-EGO [58] (the base SAEA without meta-learning), MOEA/D-FS wins on 4 of 7 DTLZ problems, ties on 2, and loses on 1 (DTLZ7, attributed to its discontinuous Pareto front), while using 90 fewer evaluations for surrogate initialization. Against 9 other comparison SAEAs and BO-based methods, MOEA/D-FS achieves better/competitive/worse IGD+ on 30/15/18 (in-range) and 31/13/19 (out-of-range) test instances, indicating consistent superiority across the majority of problems. On the real-world NAS problem, MOEA/D-FS is grouped with qLogEHVI [10] and K-RVEA [9] as the top-performing methods in terms of Hypervolume, while saving 82 evaluations compared to these baselines that require 100 samples for initialization. The 'out-of-range' ablation confirms that even when the most similar related task is excluded, MOEA/D-FS still outperforms MOEA/D-EGO on 6 of 7 DTLZ problems, demonstrating that the meta-learned experience generalizes beyond near-duplicate tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot optimization (FSO) line of work, which has been dominated by single-objective Bayesian optimization (FSBO/MBO), to the multi-objective surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithm (SAEA) setting. It builds directly on Deep Kernel Learning (Wilson et al., AISTATS 2016) as the surrogate architecture and on meta-learning for DKL (Wistuba & Grabocka, ICLR 2021), but departs from prior FSO in two key ways: (1) the MDKL model introduces explicit task-specific kernel parameters (Δθ, Δp) that are continually adapted during optimization, unlike prior meta-learning surrogates whose parameters are frozen after meta-training; and (2) the FSEO framework is designed to plug into any regression-based SAEA rather than being tied to a specific BO acquisition function. The base SAEA instantiation is MOEA/D-EGO (Zhang et al., 2010), and the comparison set includes other expensive multi-objective methods such as K-RVEA and qLogEHVI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to reducing expensive evaluation budgets in multi-objective optimization by 90% (from 10d to 1d initialization samples) while maintaining or improving solution quality, which is directly relevant to domains like engine calibration and network architecture search where each evaluation costs hours or days. Two open questions remain: there is no formal definition or metric for task relatedness, making it unclear how to select or weight related tasks in practice, and the framework is currently restricted to regression-based SAEAs, leaving classification- or ranking-based surrogates unaddressed. The ablation showing that excluding the most similar related tasks ('out-of-range') barely degrades performance is a useful robustness signal but also suggests the learned experience may be relatively generic rather than task-specific.
