## Problem

DiT-based video generation is severely bottlenecked by two orthogonal factors: the O(N²) self-attention complexity over ~115K tokens at 720P, and the T-fold overhead of multi-step diffusion sampling. Existing training-free acceleration methods (fixed sparsity patterns like CLEAR/SVG, feature caching like TeaCache) achieve only 1.5–2× speedup, while distillation-based approaches require costly retraining and often degrade quality. The paper addresses the gap of achieving substantial, practical speedup (minutes → seconds) as a plug-and-play inference pipeline without any model retraining.

## Input / Output

For text-to-video, the method takes a text prompt and produces a 5-second 720P video (125×720×1280 pixels, corresponding to a 32×45×80 patchified latent of ~115K tokens); for image-to-video, it additionally accepts a reference first-frame image. The pipeline is applied post-hoc to any trained DiT video model (HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1, AccVideo) and outputs a standard denoised video latent decoded by the model's VAE. No architectural modification or retraining of the base model is required—Jenga operates entirely at inference time by modifying the attention computation and the resolution schedule across diffusion timesteps.

## Method

Jenga combines two complementary, training-free techniques. First, Block-Wise Attention Carving (AttenCarve) reorders 1D vision tokens into 3D-local blocks via Generalized Hilbert space-filling curves, then computes a per-head block selection mask as the union of an Importance Mask (top-k block selection with a cumulative-probability cutoff to preserve global context), a Condition Mask (full attention for text/condition tokens), and an Adjacency Mask (26-neighborhood in 3D to fix border artifacts), reducing attention from O(N²) to O(N′N). Second, Progressive Resolution (ProRes) decomposes the T-step denoising into S stages of increasing latent resolution, predicting the clean latent at each stage boundary, upscaling via area interpolation, and re-noising; a text-attention amplifier with a resolution-dependent log-bias β counteracts the field-of-view narrowing observed at lower resolutions. A fixed 23-step case-agnostic timestep schedule (matching TeaCache-fast) further reduces NFE. Together these yield 4.68–8.83× speedup on HunyuanVideo with only 0.01% VBench degradation, and extend to I2V (4.43×), distilled models (3.16×), and 8-GPU parallel inference (23× over single-GPU baseline).

## Results

On HunyuanVideo (50-step, 720P, ~115K tokens), Jenga-Flash achieves an 8.83× speedup (1625s → 184s DiT time) with only a 0.01% VBench drop (82.73% vs. 82.74%), while Jenga-Base achieves 4.68× speedup (347s) with a VBench score of 83.34%—actually exceeding the baseline. Jenga-Turbo reaches 7.22× speedup (225s) with VBench 83.07% and a notable semantic improvement (VBench-S: 77.48% vs. 72.84% baseline). Across other models, Jenga delivers 4.43× on HunyuanVideo-I2V, 3.16× on the distilled AccVideo, and 6.52× on Wan2.1-1.3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against attention-only baselines, AttenCarve outperforms CLEAR (2.17× vs. 0.89× speedup; CLEAR actually slows down due to kernel overhead), MInference non-causal (2.17× vs. 1.99×), and SVG (2.17× vs. 1.64×), while also achieving the highest VBench (83.42%) and VBench-S (75.85%) among all attention methods. Against the pipeline-level baseline TeaCache-fast (2.31×, VBench 82.39%), Jenga-Base (4.68×, VBench 83.34%) more than doubles the speedup while improving VBench by ~1%. The combined Jenga-Flash (8.83×) substantially outperforms the best single-technique baseline (ProRes+TimeSkip at 3.28×) by 2.7× in speed while maintaining comparable VBench (82.73% vs. 82.57%). On Wan2.1-1.3B, Jenga-Turbo achieves 6.52× speedup (17s) versus TeaCache-fast's 3.48× (34s) with a negligible VBench drop (82.52% vs. 82.63%). On the distilled AccVideo model, Jenga-Turbo delivers 3.16× speedup (51s vs. 161s) with VBench 83.29% vs. 83.82% baseline. On HunyuanVideo-I2V, Jenga achieves 4.43× speedup (338s vs. 1499s) with VBench-I2V 87.75% vs. 87.49% baseline, slightly exceeding the original.

## Relation to Prior Work

Jenga sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) sparse attention for efficient transformer inference, drawing on MInference's block-wise key-value selection (adapted from LLMs to 3D video via space-filling curve reordering) and building beyond static-pattern methods like CLEAR, SVG, and STA that achieve only 1.5–2× speedup; and (2) progressive/bottleneck resolution strategies for diffusion sampling, extending Bottleneck Sampling's variable-resolution idea with a text-attention amplifier to correct field-of-view degradation at low resolutions. The key conceptual departure is treating attention sparsity and token-count reduction as orthogonal, composable axes rather than competing optimizations, and using a dynamic, input-dependent block selection (top-K + probability cutoff) instead of fixed spatial-temporal windows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

An 8.83× training-free speedup that reduces 720P video generation from ~27 minutes to ~3 minutes on a single H800 is a practically transformative result for real-world deployment of DiT-based video models, and the plug-and-play nature (no retraining, works across HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1, AccVideo, and I2V variants) broadens its applicability. Open questions include whether the non-adaptive SFC partitioning can be made semantic-aware, whether learnable carving strategies during training could push sparsity further, and whether latent-alignment artifacts at resolution transitions (currently mitigated by prompt engineering) can be eliminated architecturally. The finding that sparse attention *improves* semantic coherence (VBench-S: 72.84%→77.48%) suggests that full attention may introduce noise that dilutes text conditioning, an insight worth exploring further.
