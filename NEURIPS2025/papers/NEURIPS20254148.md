## Problem

Optimizing multi-agent LLM systems is difficult because (1) acquiring appropriate training signals for each specialized agent is non-trivial, and (2) credit assignment across agents is ambiguous in unstructured language-based interactions. Existing approaches rely on fragile, manually designed prompts and heuristics, making systematic optimization of the full multi-agent pipeline challenging.

## Input / Output

In problem-solving settings, each agent receives the original question plus the concatenated outputs of all predecessor agents in a directed communication graph (e.g., Physicist → Mathematician → Summarizer), producing a chain of text responses whose final output is the answer. In competitive settings, input is a game state with initial resources and the full history of both players' moves, and output is a structured trade proposal or accept/reject decision per turn over up to 8–10 rounds. The PubMedQA variant adds a long context passage that the Context Analyst must summarize before the Problem Solver generates a yes/no answer.

## Method

SIRIUS iteratively fine-tunes each agent in a multi-agent system via supervised fine-tuning on a self-constructed experience library of successful reasoning trajectories. At each iteration, agents generate responses conditioned on predecessor outputs; trajectories yielding correct final answers are retained per-agent, while failed trajectories undergo augmentation: an external critic agent (grounded in the ground truth) provides feedback, the agent regenerates a corrected response, and the result is rephrased to appear as a direct solution. The key insight is that successful multi-agent interaction trajectories contain useful collaboration patterns even without pinpointing which specific steps were decisive, sidestepping the need for fine-grained intermediate supervision. This bootstrapping loop—sample, evaluate, augment failures, fine-tune—progressively refines each agent's policy to maximize joint system performance across T iterations.

## Results

SIRIUS achieves the best accuracy across all three problem-solving tasks (College Physics, College Chemistry, PubMedQA) for every backbone model tested. With GPT-3.5-turbo, SIRIUS reaches 33.96% on College Physics, 55.90% on College Chemistry, and 75.33% on PubMedQA; with GPT-4o-mini, it reaches 47.35%, 56.41%, and 73.67% respectively. The paper reports overall improvements of 2.86% to 21.88% over the strongest baseline, with the largest gains on College Chemistry (e.g., +8.21% over COMM for GPT-3.5-turbo) and consistent but smaller gains on PubMedQA (+3.06% to +3.40% over COMM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIRIUS outperforms all four baselines (STaR, COMM, TextGrad, DSPy) on every task and model where a direct comparison is available. Against STaR (single-agent self-taught reasoning), SIRIUS gains 3.1–11.2 percentage points, confirming that multi-agent collaboration plus joint fine-tuning surpasses single-agent bootstrapping. Against COMM (training-free multi-agent prompting), SIRIUS leads by 2.4–8.2 points, showing that fine-tuning each agent's role-specific policy adds substantial value over prompt-only collaboration. Against TextGrad (gradient-based prompt optimization), SIRIUS wins by 1.9–6.7 points; notably, TextGrad could not be applied to PubMedQA with GPT-3.5-turbo due to instruction-parsing failures on long contexts. Against DSPy/MIPROv2 (joint instruction and few-shot optimization), SIRIUS leads by 1.2–16.4 points, with the largest margin on College Chemistry where DSPy underperforms even the single-agent baseline. The advantage is most pronounced for weaker backbones (Llama-3.2-3B) and on long-context tasks (PubMedQA), where structured role decomposition and role-specific fine-tuning provide the greatest benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIRIUS extends the bootstrapping paradigm of STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022) from single-agent reasoning to multi-agent settings, where the credit assignment problem makes step-level supervision intractable. Rather than optimizing prompts (as in TextGrad or DSPy) or relying on training-free collaboration (as in CoMM or Mixture-of-Agents), it fine-tunes individual agents via supervised learning on trajectory-level rewards, sidestepping the need to attribute success to specific intermediate decisions. The trajectory augmentation pipeline (feedback → regeneration → rephrasing) is a novel mechanism for converting failed trajectories into usable training signal, and the competitive setting builds on the NegotiationArena platform to test generalization beyond cooperative problem-solving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SIRIUS offers a practical, scalable recipe for improving multi-agent LLM systems without requiring fine-grained human annotation of intermediate steps, and the resulting experience library is reusable for future self-correction and self-play. Key limitations include dependence on backbone LLM quality, unresolved credit assignment in unstructured language interactions, and the need to manually design agent topologies and roles. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger agent counts, more complex (non-sequential) communication graphs, and open-source models where fine-tuning is more accessible but base capability is lower.
