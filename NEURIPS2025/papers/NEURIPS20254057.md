## Problem

While the minimum width of deep narrow MLPs for approximating continuous functions under the uniform norm has been extensively studied, the corresponding question for continuously differentiable (C¹) functions under derivative-sensitive norms such as the Sobolev W^{1,∞} norm remained largely open. This gap is significant because many practical deep learning techniques (Sobolev training, PINNs, gradient-penalized GANs) directly penalize differences in derivatives, making the W^{1,∞} topology the natural setting. The paper determines the optimal minimum width in a broad range of input/output dimension pairs, including notable cases like (8,8) and (16,8).

## Input / Output

The method takes the input dimension n, output dimension m, and a choice of activation function σ (Leaky-ReLU, ReLU, or a general non-polynomial C^{1,1} function) as parameters, and produces a closed-form expression for the optimal minimum width w_min^{W^{1,∞}}(n,m,σ). The approximation target is any C¹ function f: R^n → R^m, measured in the local Sobolev norm W^{1,∞} which simultaneously controls both the function values and their first-order partial derivatives. The result is the expression min(n+m, max(2n+1, m)) in cases where the lower and upper bounds coincide, with an additional +1 for ReLU or general C^{1,1} activations compared to Leaky-ReLU-like ones.

## Method

The proof proceeds in two main steps. First, leveraging control theory, the paper shows that any diffeomorphism can be represented as a flow map of an ODE (via Caponigro's theorem), and that this flow can be approximated step-by-step by deep narrow MLPs of width d + α(σ), where α(σ) = 0 for Leaky-ReLU-like activations and 1 for ReLU or general C^{1,1} activations. Second, the paper introduces a purely geometric quantity Ω(n,m) — the minimum dimension l such that projections of C¹-embeddings from [0,1]^n into R^l cover all C¹ functions into R^m — and proves via Palais's extension theorem that Ω(n,m) exactly characterizes the minimum width. For the lower bound, the paper constructs C¹ functions whose self-intersection has the structure of a sphere or real projective space, then applies the Borsuk-Ulam theorem to show that any function with a smaller codomain must fail to be injective, thereby forcing Ω(n,m) ≥ n+m or Ω(n,m) ≥ 2n+1 in specific dimension regimes determined by the number of linearly independent vector fields on projective spaces.

## Results

The paper establishes that the optimal minimum width for approximating C¹ functions by deep narrow MLPs under the W^{1,∞} Sobolev norm is min(n+m, max(2n+1, m)) in a broad range of cases. Specifically, it proves Ω(8,8) = 16 and Ω(16,8) = 24, and more generally that Ω(n,m) = n+m when 2^{4a+b} | n and m ≤ 8a+2^b, while Ω(n,m) = 2n+1 when (3n+3)/2 < m ≤ 2n and n+1 ≡ 0 (mod c_{2n-m+1}). The upper bound Ω(n,m) ≤ min(n+m, max(2n+1, m)) holds universally, and the lower bound matches it for infinitely many (n,m) pairs, yielding exact optimal widths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. Instead, it builds on and extends prior theoretical bounds: the upper bound max(2n+1, m) was previously established by Hwang (2023) under the uniform norm, and the n+m bound traces back to Kidger & Lyons (2020). The paper's key contribution is showing these upper bounds also hold under the finer W^{1,∞} Sobolev topology, and that they are tight (i.e., also lower bounds) for infinitely many dimension pairs. Prior lower bounds from Johnson (2018), Cai (2022), and Kim et al. (2023) gave only n+1 or m+1 in various settings; this paper closes the gap to the exact optimal value in the cases covered by Theorem 5.9, including the notable (8,8) and (16,8) configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied problem of determining the minimum width for universal approximation by deep narrow MLPs from the uniform norm to the Sobolev W^{1,∞} norm for C¹ functions. It builds on the upper bounds of Hanin & Sellke (2017), Kidger & Lyons (2020), and Hwang (2023) for continuous functions, and on the lower bounds of Johnson (2018), Cai (2022), and Kim et al. (2023), showing that the same upper bounds n+m and max(2n+1, m) carry over to the finer Sobolev topology. The key methodological departure is the use of control theory (Caponigro 2011) to approximate diffeomorphisms via ODE flows, combined with algebraic-topological tools (Borsuk-Ulam theorem, Palais extension theorem, projective space submersions) to establish matching lower bounds—technologies not previously applied to the neural network width problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The W^{1,∞} norm is directly relevant to practical deep learning techniques that penalize derivative differences, including Physics-Informed Neural Networks, Sobolev training, and GANs with gradient penalty, so having a theoretical characterization of the minimum width in this setting provides architectural guidance that was previously unavailable. However, the results are non-constructive (no approximation rates or depth bounds), apply only to specific (n,m) pairs where the topological lower bound matches the upper bound, and leave the intermediate regime (3n ≥ 2m) open. The parity-dependent behavior of Ω(k, 2k−1) (2k for even k, 2k+1 for odd k) suggests a deeper structural property of the embedding problem that remains to be fully understood.
