## Problem

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has driven major advances in LLM reasoning, but its progress is bottlenecked by the scarcity of high-quality training data: existing benchmarks are fixed in size, quickly memorized, or rely on unreliable internet-scraped content. This paper addresses the need for an open-ended, procedurally generated reasoning environment that provides unlimited training instances with controllable difficulty and fully automatic, unambiguous reward signals.

## Input / Output

Each task is a text-formatted reasoning problem (e.g., a 4×4 Sudoku grid, a polynomial equation, a knights-and-knaves puzzle) generated on-the-fly by a parameterized algorithm. Difficulty is controlled via three parameter types: difficulty parameters (node counts, polynomial degrees, word lengths), structural parameters (dimensionality, constraint types, proof depth), and stylistic parameters (variable names, number formats). The model produces a free-text reasoning solution, which is scored by an algorithmic verifier that checks both answer correctness (accuracy reward) and output formatting (auxiliary reward), yielding a scalar reward in [0, 1.2] used for RLVR training.

## Method

REASONING GYM is a library of over 100 procedurally generated reasoning environments spanning algebra, arithmetic, geometry, graph theory, logic, algorithmic tasks, and games, each equipped with an automatic verifier and configurable difficulty parameters. The key design principles are algorithmic verifiability (no human judgment), large solution spaces (to reward generalizable strategies over memorization), and parametric difficulty control (enabling dynamic curricula). For training, the authors use GRPO (a PPO variant) via the verl library on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, sampling tasks uniformly across a composite of generators with a combined accuracy-and-format reward. A curriculum learning variant progressively increases task difficulty when the model exceeds a 70% success threshold over 20 steps, outperforming fixed-difficulty sampling. The paper demonstrates intra-domain transfer, cross-domain transfer (e.g., algorithmic training improving algebra and geometry), and external benchmark gains on MATH, GSM8K, Big-Bench Hard, and MMLU-Pro.

## Results

On zero-shot RG evaluation, reasoning models (o3-mini 63.5%, DeepSeek-R1 59.5%) outperform the best non-reasoning models (Llama 4 Maverick 41.5%, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 40.3%) by ~22%, while even frontier models struggle with hard configurations (e.g., o3-mini drops 71.9% on code tasks from easy to hard). RLVR training on RG tasks with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct yields consistent intra-domain gains (e.g., algebra +11.7%, games from 0% to 3.3%) and notable cross-domain transfer (algorithmic training improves algebra by +29.1% and geometry by +22.3%). On external benchmarks, the RG-Math model improves MATH by +9.7% (48.5→58.2) and Big-Bench Hard by +7.7% (8.68→16.34), with smaller gains on GSM8K (+0.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external RLVR methods but instead benchmarks the base model (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct) against its RLVR-trained variants across multiple settings. Intra-domain, RLVR consistently improves over the baseline in all five tested categories, with the largest gains in algebra (+11.7%) and a notable bootstrap from 0% to 3.3% on Games. Cross-domain, algorithmic training produces the broadest transfer, improving algebra (+29.1%), geometry (+22.3%), and arithmetic (+15.6%), while logic training transfers to cognition (+13.3%) and graphs (+9.1%); the only negative transfers are minor (ARC −2.3%, Games −0.8%). On external benchmarks, RG-Math outperforms the baseline on MATH (+9.7%) and Big-Bench Hard (+7.7%) but shows only marginal GSM8K improvement (+0.5%), suggesting the gains are concentrated in harder reasoning tasks. For curriculum learning, the adaptive-difficulty approach outperforms fixed-difficulty training across all three tested environments (Spell Backwards, Mini Sudoku, Count Primes), with the largest margin being +40.67% on 4-letter words in Spell Backwards.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the procedural generation paradigm from Cobbe et al. (2020) to LLM reasoning, but at a scale and breadth not seen in prior efforts. It positions itself against fixed reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, Big-Bench Hard) that suffer from memorization, and against RLVR pipelines like Tulu 3 and DeepSeek-R1 that depend on finite, human-curated or internet-scraped data. Closest concurrent work includes TextArena (text games), Logic-RL (logic puzzles), KORGym (games), and Reasoning Core (formal domains), but RG distinguishes itself by covering 100+ tasks across algebra, algorithms, logic, games, geometry, and cognition in a unified library with integrated curriculum learning and verifiable reward infrastructure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RG addresses the fundamental data scarcity bottleneck in reasoning research by providing unlimited, difficulty-controllable training instances, making it a practical resource for the growing RLVR community. The demonstrated cross-domain transfer (algorithmic training improving algebra by +29% and geometry by +22%) suggests RLVR may develop general reasoning primitives rather than narrow heuristics, though this is tested on a single 3B model. Key limitations include single-turn text-only tasks, uniform data sampling, and the absence of multi-turn or multimodal reasoning; open questions include how curriculum strategies interact with non-stationary data streams and catastrophic forgetting in continual learning settings.
