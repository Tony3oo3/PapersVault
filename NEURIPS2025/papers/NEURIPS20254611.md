## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods assume a static scenario where each test batch originates from a single distribution, but real-world data streams often contain samples from multiple distinct distributions simultaneously (dynamic scenario). Under this setting, global batch normalization strategies compute statistics holistically across all features, causing cross-distribution interference and significant accuracy degradation (average 15% drop). No prior method provides a mechanism to separately normalize features belonging to different distributions within the same batch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254611/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming batch of 64 unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) processed by a pre-trained model with batch normalization layers (ResNet-50 or EfficientViT-M5). At each time step, the model must adapt and predict without retaining or accessing any historical batches, reflecting memory-constrained deployment. The output is a class label prediction per image plus updated per-layer, per-subset BN statistics (mean and variance) that replace the original source-domain statistics for the current forward pass.

## Method

FIND implements a divide-and-conquer normalization strategy with three components. First, Layer-Wise Feature Disentanglement (LFD) computes instance-level feature means at each BN layer, builds a first-neighbor graph via cosine similarity, and extracts connected components to partition the batch's feature maps into distribution-specific subsets without requiring domain labels or a predefined cluster count. Second, Feature-Aware Batch Normalization (FABN) computes subset-specific statistics (TFN) and blends them with source-domain statistics (SBN) via a weighted combination (α=0.8 favoring SBN) to compensate for the small-sample bias in subset statistics while retaining domain-specific adaptation. Third, Selective FABN (S-FABN) computes a per-layer sensitivity score from the KL divergence between source and target distributions (weighted by channel-wise variance) and applies partitioning only to layers exceeding a threshold γ, reducing inference overhead by 17–32% while preserving accuracy.

## Results

On the primary ResNet-50 evaluation (severity-5 corruptions, batch size 64), FIND* achieves 48.50% average accuracy across all three dynamic scenarios (CrossMix, Random, Shuffle), outperforming the strongest baseline α-BN (43.98%) by 4.5 points and the source model (37.25%) by 11.25 points. In the most challenging CrossMix scenario, FIND* reaches 47.19% average accuracy versus 39.18% for α-BN and 39.47% for DeYO, a gain of ~8 points. On the EfficientViT-M5 backbone, FIND* further extends the lead to 53.07% Avg-All, surpassing α-BN (50.07%) by 3 points. FIND* also demonstrates strong efficiency: 0.15 s latency and 1.21 GB memory per batch, ranking top-3 in speed among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIND* outperforms all 13 baselines across every dynamic scenario and dataset. Among test-time fine-tuning methods, the gap is largest against RoTTA (32.14% vs. 48.50%, a 16.4-point margin) and NOTE (39.39%, a 9.1-point margin), while the closest fine-tuning competitor DeYO (43.48%) is still surpassed by 5 points. Among test-time normalization methods, α-BN (43.98%) is the strongest baseline, yet FIND* exceeds it by 4.5 points on average and by 8 points in the CrossMix scenario; TBN (41.43%) and IABN (33.38%) trail further behind. Against the four additional 'in-the-wild' baselines (COME, UnMix-TNS, REM, CoLA) evaluated in Appendix D, FIND* leads by 9–13 points on CIFAR10-C, 7–10 points on CIFAR100-C, and 10–12 points on ImageNet-C under CrossMix. Notably, all fine-tuning baselines degrade or show inconsistent gains in dynamic settings (e.g., RoTTA drops below the source model in CrossMix), whereas FIND* maintains stable improvements, confirming that precise normalization—not parameter updates—is the critical lever in multi-distribution batches.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIND extends the test-time normalization line of work (TBN, α-BN, IABN) by introducing a layer-wise divide-and-conquer strategy for handling multi-distribution batches, departing from the static single-distribution assumption that underpins most prior TTA methods. Conceptually, it repurposes first-neighbor graph clustering (from FINCH) and the domain-relevant/class-relevant feature decomposition insight (from Neyshabur et al. and Lee et al.) to partition feature maps at each BN layer rather than operating on raw inputs. The method is backward-free and explicitly positions itself against both test-time fine-tuning approaches (TENT, SAR, DeYO, etc.) and prior normalization-only methods, arguing that precise normalization—not parameter updates—is the critical lever in dynamic scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a realistic deployment gap: real-world data streams often contain mixed distributions within a single batch, yet prior TTA methods assume homogeneous batches and degrade substantially (15% average accuracy drop) under this condition. The finding that fine-tuning actively harms performance in dynamic settings while normalization-based correction helps reframes where adaptation effort should be directed. Limitations include reliance on BN layers (restricting applicability to LayerNorm-only architectures), evaluation limited to corrupted-image benchmarks, and a cold-start phase that requires initial batches. Open questions include whether the layer-wise partitioning strategy transfers to non-BN normalization, video/multimodal streams, and whether the SBN-TFN blending ratio can be learned rather than fixed.
