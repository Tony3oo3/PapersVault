## Problem

Existing medical visual grounding benchmarks are limited to single-image scenarios, yet real clinical workflows inherently require sequential image analysis—comparing pre- and post-treatment scans, tracking lesion evolution across longitudinal series, and aligning findings across views and modalities. No benchmark had systematically evaluated fine-grained cross-image grounding capability in MLLMs for these clinically essential tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252502/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample consists of a medical image sequence of 2–6 images (resized to 336×336 px, min-max normalized, stored as lossless PNG) drawn from 10 modalities (CT, MRI, X-ray, US, endoscopy, fundus, etc.), paired with a VQA-style text prompt. The model must output bounding-box coordinates for the target region; performance is scored via mean IoU and ACC@0.5. 3D volumes are converted to 2D by slicing along anatomical axes, and surgical videos are sampled at fixed frame intervals, so all inputs are flat 2D RGB sequences regardless of original acquisition format.

## Method

MedSG-Bench defines eight VQA-style tasks organized into two paradigms: Image Difference Grounding (registered and non-registered, where the latter adds up to 20-px random translations to simulate misalignment) and Image Consistency Grounding (multi-view, object tracking, visual concept, visual patch, cross-modal, and referring grounding). The benchmark is constructed from 76 public datasets (9,630 QA pairs, 24,341 images) with controlled perturbations (CutPaste, generative anomaly synthesis) and manual curation to ensure semantic alignment. For the model, MedSeq-Grounder is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and instruction-tuned via LLaMA-Factory on MedSG-188K (188,163 samples from 324,359 images), where diverse instruction templates are expanded using GPT-4, Claude, and DeepSeek to mitigate phrasing bias. Training uses a global batch size of 64 over 15,000 steps at lr 5e-6 on 4×A40-48G GPUs, and all baseline evaluations are conducted in a zero-shot setting.

## Results

On MedSG-Bench (9,630 VQA pairs across 8 sequential grounding tasks), the proposed MedSeq-Grounder (7B) achieves an average IoU of 72.55% and Acc@0.5 of 79.71%, dramatically outperforming all baselines. The best proprietary model, Gemini 2.5 Pro, reaches only 20.66% IoU and 15.61% Acc@0.5 in zero-shot, while the strongest general-purpose baseline (Migician, 7B) achieves 20.29% IoU and 11.39% Acc@0.5. Medical-domain specialized models perform worst overall, with HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B scoring just 8.97% IoU and 2.36% Acc@0.5, even below the smaller Qwen2.5-VL-3B (10.94% IoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedSeq-Grounder (7B) outperforms every baseline by a wide margin: it exceeds the best proprietary model Gemini 2.5 Pro by ~52 IoU points (72.55 vs. 20.66) and GPT-4o by ~55 points (72.55 vs. 17.70). Among general-purpose MLLMs, the multi-image grounding specialist Migician (7B) is the closest competitor at 20.29% IoU, still trailing MedSeq-Grounder by over 52 points; scaling Qwen2.5-VL from 3B to 72B yields only marginal gains (10.94→13.35% IoU), confirming that model size alone does not improve sequential grounding. Notably, medical-domain specialized models underperform their general-purpose counterparts: HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B (8.97% IoU) lags behind Qwen2.5-VL-7B (12.31%) by 3.34% IoU, and even falls below the smaller Qwen2.5-VL-3B, which the authors attribute to catastrophic forgetting of spatial grounding during medical instruction tuning. Within the InternVL family, the 78B model (6.44% IoU) performs worse than the 8B variant (9.26%), further illustrating that grounding ability does not scale with parameter count in current MLLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-context visual grounding paradigm (introduced by MC-Bench and Migician for natural images) into the medical domain, where no prior benchmark has addressed sequential image grounding. It departs from existing medical grounding benchmarks (MS-CXR, MeCoVQA-G), which are restricted to single-image scenarios, and from multi-image medical understanding benchmarks (Medical-Diff-VQA, MMXU), which evaluate comprehension rather than explicit spatial localization. The construction pipeline repurposes 76 publicly available segmentation datasets and applies controlled perturbations (CutPaste, generative anomaly synthesis, spatial shifts) to create grounding pairs, while the model (MedSeq-Grounder) is built on Qwen2.5-VL and trained via instruction tuning with LLaMA-Factory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even frontier proprietary models (Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-4o) achieve only ~20% IoU on medical sequential grounding, exposing a substantial capability gap that current MLLMs are not designed to address. The finding that medical-domain specialized models (e.g., HuatuoGPT-Vision) underperform same-size general-purpose models suggests catastrophic forgetting of spatial grounding during domain-adaptive instruction tuning—a concern with broad implications for medical MLLM development. Key limitations include the absence of real longitudinal clinical data (all sequences are synthetically constructed from single-patient datasets) and the task-specific nature of MedSeq-Grounder, which may sacrifice general VQA capability. Open questions include whether grounding supervision can be integrated into medical pretraining without degrading other skills, and how to incorporate authentic multi-visit patient trajectories.
