## Problem

Existing data consumer (DC) bidding strategies in Auction-based Federated Learning (AFL) are static—represented as fixed non-linear or linear functions with parameters fitted from historical auction data—making them poorly suited to the highly dynamic, non-stationary AFL market where DO arrivals, competitor behaviors, and budget states shift continuously. This paper asks whether a reinforcement learning agent can learn an adaptive, sequential bidding policy that outperforms these static methods in competitive multi-DC auction settings.

## Input / Output

At each auction round i, the agent observes a triple (remaining budget B^i, remaining DO count C−i, DO utility v^i), where v^i is computed via GTG-Shapley contribution scores fed into a Beta Reputation System. This observation is expanded into a sequential state window of W=40 past observations, which is then soft-clustered by a GMM (K=10 components) into a lower-dimensional cluster-affinity vector Γ(s_i). The agent outputs a discrete action selecting an adjustment rate λ_a, which updates the scaling factor ω via ω_i = ω_{i−1}×(1+λ_a); the actual bid price is then b^i = v^i/ω_i and submitted to the SPSB auctioneer.

## Method

RLB-AFL frames DC bidding as a POMDP in which a DQN agent sequentially controls the ω scaling factor to maximize cumulative utility under a budget constraint (γ=1, undiscounted). The key architectural contribution is a GMM-based soft clustering module applied to a sliding window of W=40 historical states: the EM algorithm learns cluster parameters (u_k, μ_k, Σ_k) that map the continuous, high-dimensional sequential state space into a K-dimensional affinity vector, mitigating state sparsity and easing Q-value approximation. An online variant of the M-step (with learning rate κ=0.5) allows the GMM to adapt dynamically as new states arrive, avoiding the need for a pre-collected state corpus. A second contribution is an enhanced ε-greedy policy that monitors whether the Q-value distribution across the discrete action space is unimodal; if the distribution deviates from unimodality (indicating abnormal Q-estimation), ε is increased to encourage exploration, providing an automatic exploration-exploitation balance beyond standard linear annealing. The DQN uses three fully connected hidden layers of 64 nodes, trained with RMSprop (lr=0.0001), a replay buffer of 6,000 transitions, batch size 32, and a target network synchronized every 30 steps.

## Results

Under the IID scenario with budget=100, RLB-AFL achieves the highest total utility across all six datasets, e.g., 18.62 on MNIST, 16.84 on CIFAR-10, and 17.68 on Fashion-MNIST, while also attaining the best FL model accuracy (85.86%, 41.83%, and 76.82% respectively). Averaged over all datasets and budget settings, RLB-AFL outperforms the best baseline (RLB) by 10.56% in total utility and 3.15% in model accuracy. Under the Non-IID scenario, RLB-AFL improves test accuracy by 3.19% on average over the best baseline. Ablation studies confirm that both the GMM-based state clustering and the enhanced ε-greedy policy contribute meaningfully to the gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLB-AFL consistently outperforms all eight baselines on both total utility and FL model accuracy across all six datasets and three budget levels. Against the strongest baseline RLB (a generic RL bidding method not tailored to AFL), RLB-AFL gains approximately 10.56% in utility and 3.15% in accuracy on average, with the gap widening at higher budgets (e.g., 22.06 vs. 19.83 utility on MNIST at budget=400). The AFL-specific static methods FBs and FBc (Fed-Bidder variants) trail RLB-AFL by roughly 15–20% in utility, confirming that static bidding functions struggle in dynamic auction environments. Simpler heuristics (Const, Rand, Bmub, Lin) and the Bidding Machine (BM) are substantially outperformed, with BM lagging RLB-AFL by about 25–30% in utility. Ablation results show that removing GMM state clustering (w/o c) reduces utility by ~1–2 points, while replacing the enhanced ε-greedy with standard ε-greedy (w/o ε) causes a larger drop of ~2–3 points, indicating the exploration policy is the more critical component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ω-control bidding formulation from Fed-Bidder [15] and the RL-based bidding framework from RLB [29] by making the approach specifically tailored to the dynamic, multi-DC competitive setting of auction-based federated learning. The key methodological departure is the integration of Gaussian Mixture Model soft clustering over sequential state windows to combat state-space sparsity, combined with an enhanced ε-greedy policy that monitors Q-value distribution unimodality to adaptively modulate exploration. Unlike prior DC-side methods that are static functions (linear, non-linear, or heuristic) trained on historical auction data, RLB-AFL frames bidding as a POMDP solved by DQN, positioning it as the first cluster-based RL approach for multiple DCs competing over a shared DO pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap: static bidding strategies degrade in the inherently non-stationary AFL market where DO arrivals, competitor behavior, and budget depletion create shifting dynamics. The GMM-based state clustering offers a reusable technique for any RL agent operating in sparse, high-dimensional continuous state spaces, potentially applicable beyond AFL. However, the evaluation is entirely simulation-based with synthetic auction dynamics, and the paper acknowledges it overlooks inter-DC collaboration, which could significantly alter optimal bidding behavior. Open questions include whether the approach scales to markets with thousands of concurrent DCs, how sensitive performance is to the GMM cluster count in truly non-stationary environments, and whether the unimodality assumption on Q-value distributions holds under adversarial or cooperative multi-agent dynamics.
