## Problem

The sampling dynamics of diffusion models remain a black box: each reverse step blends thousands of values in ways that are hard to anticipate, and small hyperparameter changes can turn a perfect sample into a failure. No principled diagnostic exists for identifying *when* during sampling the paths to different outcomes (e.g., different classes or styles) become distinguishable, making it impossible to target interventions at the right timesteps without expensive grid searches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained diffusion model (DiT-XL/2, DDPM, or Stable Diffusion) together with a data distribution partitioned into disjoint events (object classes, bird species, artistic styles) and a user-specified goal. It outputs discrete transition indices (convergence index i*, per-class merger times t_merge,k) that parameterize the reverse sampler, and downstream artifacts: accelerated generated images, class-conditional samples with guidance windows, zero-shot class predictions, and style-transferred images. A key design choice is that all fluctuation statistics are computed on the *forward* diffusion trajectory (cheap Monte-Carlo sweep over the data), not on the expensive reverse sampling path, so the diagnostic cost is a single forward pass per event pair.

## Method

The paper introduces cross-fluctuations—centered-moment tensor inner products borrowed from statistical physics—as a probe for discrete phase transitions in diffusion sampling. For two disjoint events Ω₁ and Ω₂, the n-th order normalised cross-fluctuation M_ρ^(n) measures the cosine similarity between their conditional n-th moment tensors; for n=2 this reduces exactly to Centred Kernel Alignment (CKA) between conditional covariance matrices, making it efficiently computable. A 'merger' is declared when M_ρ^(n) reaches 1 (or when the within-event fluctuation magnitudes fall below a threshold ε), and the earliest such index i* generalises the coupling time of a finite Markov chain. These merger times then drive five practical interventions: early stopping of the reverse ODE (saving 25–40% of steps), class-specific guidance windows that replace the grid search in Interval Guidance, merger-aware timestep weighting for zero-shot classification, corrupted-exemplar ILVR for rare-class generation, and Fourier-regularity-based transfer of merger schedules to style variants for zero-shot style transfer—all without retraining or new hyperparameters.

## Results

On class-conditional generation, the fluctuation-driven guidance window outperforms the Interval Guidance (IG) baseline across all metrics: on ImageNet with DiT-XL/2, FID drops from 3.22 to 2.86, Precision rises from 0.78 to 0.83, and Coverage from 0.35 to 0.39. For zero-shot classification, truncated inverse-SNR weighting with merger-aware bounds achieves 65.28% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet (vs. 54.96% for the uniform-weight baseline of Li et al.), 88.38% on CIFAR-10, and 89.15% on Oxford-IIIT Pets. In zero-shot style transfer, the method improves PSNR from 25.67 to 28.71 (Ghibli, Oxford-IIIT Pets) and from 26.16 to 28.65 (van-Gogh, Oxford-IIIT Pets) over SDEdit, while reducing MSE by roughly 3×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Interval Guidance (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2024), the paper's fluctuation-derived guidance windows win on every reported metric across ImageNet, MNIST, and CIFAR-10 (e.g., FID 2.86 vs. 3.22 on ImageNet; Coverage 0.39 vs. 0.35), while eliminating the expensive grid search that IG requires to find its optimal (t_start, t_end) interval. Against the zero-shot classifier of Li et al. (2023), the merger-aware truncated inverse-SNR weighting improves ImageNet top-1 accuracy from 54.96% to 65.28% (+10.3 pp) and CIFAR-10 from 84.67% to 88.38% (+3.7 pp), with the gains attributed to excluding timesteps beyond the first class-merger event. Against SDEdit (Meng et al., 2021) for style transfer, the fluctuation-based start time yields substantially higher PSNR (28.71 vs. 25.67 for Ghibli on Oxford-IIIT Pets; 28.65 vs. 26.16 for van-Gogh) and lower MSE (0.03 vs. 0.09), all without the dataset-wide grid search that SDEdit's protocol requires. In rare-class generation (Table 3), the merger-aware IG + corrupted exemplar variant further extends the IG gains (e.g., CLIP similarity 0.27 vs. 0.21 on iNaturalist), confirming that the fluctuation signal generalises to under-represented tail classes without re-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts centered-moment (fluctuation) statistics from statistical physics to diffusion model sampling, showing that the n=2 cross-fluctuation between class-conditional covariance matrices is exactly Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), thereby unifying a physics-inspired diagnostic with a well-known representation-similarity metric. Practically, it replaces the expensive grid searches in Interval Guidance (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2024), the uniform/inverse-SNR weighting in zero-shot diffusion classification (Li et al., 2023), and the noising-time search in SDEdit (Meng et al., 2021) with a single forward Monte-Carlo sweep that yields class-specific merger times. The merger-time concept is shown to generalise the coupling time of finite Markov chains (Aldous & Fill, 2002; Levin & Peres, 2017) to continuous SDE dynamics, and the rare-class variant builds on ILVR (Choi et al., 2021) by restricting its application to the fluctuation-derived interval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, low-cost diagnostic that tells practitioners exactly when different data categories become indistinguishable during diffusion, enabling targeted interventions (early stopping, interval guidance, merger-aware weighting) without retraining or hyperparameter search. However, the individual performance gains are modest (a few FID points, a few percent accuracy), and the analysis is restricted to variance-preserving schedules; extending to EDM-style non-VP schedules, anisotropic diffusions, and higher-order fluctuations for complex data remains open. The conceptual unification of Markov-chain coupling, statistical-physics phase transitions, and diffusion sampling is elegant but its practical impact will depend on whether the fluctuation-based time selection generalises beyond the relatively clean class-conditional settings tested here.
