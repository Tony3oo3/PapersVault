## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D/4D generation methods, including recent feed-forward models like L4GM, TRELLIS, and Hunyuan3D, fail to preserve the semantic identity of a specific subject across unobserved viewpoints, often hallucinating incorrect colors and textures on side and back views. Subject-driven (personalized) 3D/4D generation that faithfully reproduces a given subject's appearance from a single image or video remains largely underexplored, with existing approaches either too slow (score distillation) or still unable to maintain identity consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255088/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image or video of a subject along with a rough 3D/4D asset already produced by an existing generative model (e.g., LGM for 3D, L4GM for 4D). Multi-view observations are rendered from azimuth angles spanning ±180° at elevation 0°, and the pipeline progressively modifies these 2D renderings before unprojecting them back. The output is an identity-preserving 3D Gaussian asset (for image input) or a dynamic 3D Gaussian sequence (for video input), and the approach is model-agnostic, applicable to any underlying 3D representation beyond Gaussians.

## Method

TIRE (Track, Inpaint, Resplat) is a three-stage progressive texture infilling pipeline. Track uses CoTracker video tracking in a backward direction (from target views toward the source view) to produce clean inpainting masks identifying occluded regions, which yields more accurate masks than intuitive forward tracking. Inpaint personalizes a Stable Diffusion inpainting model via LoRA fine-tuning on the source image with augmentation, then progressively infills viewpoints in order of increasing angular distance (±20° sweet spot → ±90° → remaining views up to ±180°), using backward tracking from each queried view to the nearest anchor to minimize the inpainting area; only the first 30% of the denoising schedule is applied to preserve original structure. Resplat refines cross-view consistency with a mask-aware multi-view diffusion denoising step (ImageDream) that updates only the novel-view latents while keeping the source view fixed, then resplats the refined multi-view observations back to 3D Gaussians via LGM/L4GM.

## Results

On the DreamBooth-Dynamic benchmark, TIRE achieves a DINO (ViT-S/16) similarity of 0.5665 and DINO (ViT-B/16) of 0.5815 for video-to-4D generation, outperforming L4GM (0.5506/0.5694) and STAG4D (0.5287/0.5592), though trailing Customize-It-3D (0.5773/0.6087) on this metric which the authors argue is unreliable for this task. On the more comprehensive VLM-based evaluation for image-to-3D, TIRE achieves the best average score of 1.854 (out of 4) across six VLMs, surpassing Hunyuan3D-v2.5 (1.703) and TRELLIS (1.476). A user study with 18 participants (540 total scores) confirms TIRE is subjectively preferred over L4GM and Customize-It-3D on overall quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIRE outperforms L4GM (DINO ViT-S/16: 0.5665 vs. 0.5506) and STAG4D (0.5665 vs. 0.5287) on DINO similarity for video-to-4D, and also beats SV4D (0.5665 vs. 0.5213). On the VLM-based image-to-3D evaluation, TIRE leads TRELLIS by 0.378 average points (1.854 vs. 1.476) and Hunyuan3D-v2.5 by 0.151 (1.854 vs. 1.703), winning on every individual VLM except Gemma3 27B where it edges out by only 0.005. TIRE loses to Customize-It-3D on DINO similarity (0.5665 vs. 0.5773), but the authors demonstrate this is a metric artifact—Customize-It-3D produces geometrically incorrect views that merely resemble the reference in color/texture, and qualitative comparisons and the user study clearly favor TIRE. For the image-to-3D baselines SV3D, Wonder3D, LGM, and MeshFormer, comparisons are qualitative only (Fig. 4), where TIRE shows superior identity preservation and fewer ghosting artifacts across all four.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIRE positions itself as an orthogonal, post-hoc refinement pipeline that sits on top of existing feed-forward 3D/4D generation models (LGM, L4GM, TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D-v2.5) rather than replacing them. Its three stages repurpose 2D tools for 3D problems: CoTracker for occlusion-aware mask extraction, a LoRA-finetuned Stable Diffusion inpainter (inheriting the subject-driven personalization paradigm from RealFill and DreamBooth) for progressive texture completion, and multi-view diffusion (ImageDream/Wonder3D) for cross-view consistency before resplatting to 3D Gaussians. This departs from SDS-based optimization (too slow, mode-averaging) and from multi-view diffusion hallucination (systematic color/appearance bias) by grounding novel-view appearance in tracked correspondences to the source view rather than relying on the generative model to invent unseen content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even the most recent native 3D generation models (TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D-v2.5) still produce assets whose side and back views deviate significantly from the reference subject, confirming that identity preservation is an unsolved problem orthogonal to generation speed and fidelity. TIRE's model-agnostic design means it can be layered onto any future 3D/4D generator, making it a potentially widely adoptable post-processing step. Key limitations include the ~100-minute per-asset runtime (inherited from RealFill's per-subject fine-tuning), the absence of temporal-specific personalization for 4D, and the acknowledged inadequacy of DINO similarity as a quantitative metric for subject fidelity in 3D/4D. Open questions include designing proper evaluation metrics that jointly assess geometry and appearance, extending personalization to temporal patterns in 4D, and replacing per-subject fine-tuning with feed-forward personalization models.
