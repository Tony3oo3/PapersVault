## Problem

Traditional multi-objective Bayesian optimization seeks a single Pareto-optimal solution, but when objectives are highly conflicting, no single solution can perform acceptably on all of them. The coverage optimization problem—finding a small set of K < T solutions such that each of the T objectives is well-optimized by at least one solution—had not been addressed within the BO framework, with prior work relying on gradient-based or evolutionary methods that do not scale to high-dimensional black-box settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes T black-box objective functions sharing a common input domain, a user-specified covering set size K < T, and a search space that may be continuous or structured (molecules, peptides). For structured spaces, a pre-trained VAE maps inputs to a continuous latent space where optimization is performed. The output is a set of K solutions maximizing the coverage score c = Σ_t max_k f_t(x_k), which credits only the best solution per objective, making the objective submodular and the exact solution NP-hard.

## Method

MOCOBO introduces an Expected Coverage Improvement (ECI) acquisition function that measures the expected gain in coverage score from evaluating a new candidate, estimated via Monte Carlo sampling from the GP posterior. The best covering set among observed data is constructed at each step using a greedy submodular maximization algorithm (Algorithm 1) with a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee and O(nKT) runtime. The algorithm employs K parallel trust regions (following TuRBO-M) centered on the current best covering set, each proposing q candidates via ECI (or its batch extension q-ECI) within its rectangular region. For high-dimensional problems, an approximate GP surrogate (PPGPR) is used, and for structured search spaces, MOCOBO operates in the latent space of a pre-trained VAE with end-to-end surrogate updates during optimization.

## Results

Across four high-dimensional tasks (peptide design, molecule design, rover navigation, and image tone mapping), MOCOBO consistently achieves the highest coverage scores among all methods, with K < T solutions nearly matching the performance of T individually optimized solutions (the ceiling baseline). For the peptide design task, K=4 peptides cover T=11 target bacteria, with in vitro validation confirming high potency (MIC ≤ 16 μmol/L) against 9 of 11 drug-resistant pathogens. For molecule design, K=3 molecules cover T=6 element objectives, each achieving a max score >0.9. The ablation study shows MOCOBO achieves the same final coverage as an oracle partitioning strategy with only minimal loss in optimization efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOCOBO outperforms all five baselines (CluSO, TuRBO, ROBOT, MORBO, and LOL-BO) across all four tasks in terms of coverage score. CluSO [Liu et al., PPSN 2024], the only prior method explicitly designed for black-box coverage optimization, shows substantially lower performance than even the non-coverage baselines, as it was developed for low-dimensional problems and does not scale to the high-dimensional and structured settings studied here. TuRBO [Eriksson et al., NeurIPS 2019] and LOL-BO [Stanton et al., ICML 2022] require T independent single-objective runs and then post-hoc select the best K covering solutions, which MOCOBO surpasses by directly optimizing the coverage objective. MORBO [Daulton et al., UAI 2021], a strong multi-objective BO method, also falls short of MOCOBO because it seeks Pareto-optimal single solutions rather than a covering set. ROBOT [Maus et al., AISTATS 2023], which seeks diverse solutions per objective, similarly underperforms MOCOBO. The key quantitative finding is that MOCOBO's K < T solutions nearly match the 'T Individually Optimized Solutions' ceiling, demonstrating that explicit coverage optimization recovers most of the performance that would require T separate solutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOCOBO extends the Bayesian optimization framework—specifically TuRBO's trust-region scheme and the expected improvement acquisition function—to a new problem setting: coverage optimization, where K < T solutions must collectively cover T objectives. The coverage optimization problem was previously addressed only via gradient-based methods (Ding et al., Li et al., Lin et al.) and a single evolutionary algorithm (CluSO, Liu et al.), none of which scale to high-dimensional or structured search spaces. The method's core novelty is the Expected Coverage Improvement (ECI) acquisition function, which naturally generalizes EI by replacing single-objective improvement with improvement in a submodular coverage score, combined with a greedy (1-1/e)-approximation for the NP-hard covering set construction. For structured domains, it builds on latent-space BO with VAEs (LOL-BO, SELFIES-VAE) and PPGPR surrogate models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that K << T solutions found by MOCOBO nearly match the coverage of T individually optimized solutions, with in vitro validation showing potent antimicrobial peptides covering 9 of 11 drug-resistant pathogens—providing a practical pathway for multi-target drug discovery under synthesis constraints. Key limitations include the need for domain knowledge to choose K, the requirement of pre-trained generative models for structured domains, and the NP-hardness of exact covering set construction (mitigated by the greedy approximation). Open questions include simultaneous optimization of K and solutions, threshold-based coverage variants, and whether lazy greedy strategies can further reduce the computational cost of the submodular maximization step.
