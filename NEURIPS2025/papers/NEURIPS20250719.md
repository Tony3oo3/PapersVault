## Problem

Existing animal video datasets are too small (at most 2.4K clips of 15 frames), lack object-centric crops and essential annotations (masks, keypoints, depth), and no benchmark or baseline exists specifically for 4D quadruped reconstruction from in-the-wild video. This leaves the field without scalable data, a standardized evaluation protocol, or a method that produces temporally coherent 3D reconstructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250719/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes object-centric RGB video clips (512×512, 10 fps) together with per-frame auxiliary annotations: binary mask silhouettes, 2D keypoints from ViTPose++, DINOv2 image features, SEA-RAFT optical flow, and Depth Anything V2 depth maps. It outputs a temporally coherent sequence of posed 3D meshes, with per-frame articulation parameters, camera poses, and instance-specific shape deformations. The Animal-in-Motion benchmark comprises 230 curated sequences (11,061 frames) across 23 quadruped categories, each validated for clean silhouettes and smooth keypoints.

## Method

4D-Fauna adapts the feed-forward 3D-Fauna model with per-sequence test-time optimization: it computes a mean DINO feature across all frames to predict a single consistent canonical shape, then directly optimizes per-frame articulation and camera pose parameters (initialized from pretrained predictors) rather than fine-tuning the predictor networks. Two new supervision terms are introduced: a 2D keypoint reprojection loss on 10 aligned joints to resolve leg-ordering ambiguity, and temporal smoothness losses (first- and second-order differences) on articulation angles, posed bone coordinates, and camera pose to enforce motion coherence. The full objective combines the original 3D-Fauna losses (mask, image, feature reconstruction, adversarial, Eikonal, and regularization) with these additions, optimized via Adam at lr=0.1 over 25 epochs in sliding windows of 8 frames on a single L40 GPU. The supporting data pipeline automates YouTube scraping (GPT-generated queries), shot-based splitting, CLIP-score filtering, Grounded-SAM-2 tracking with multi-stage quality filtering (overlap, resolution, truncation, identity consistency), and GPT-verified object-centric cropping.

## Results

On the Animal-in-Motion benchmark (230 sequences, 11,061 frames), 4D-Fauna achieves IoU 0.814, PCK@0.1 0.664, PCK@0.05 0.317, KT-PCK@0.1 0.418, KT-PCK@0.05 0.193, and MPJVE 0.044. The model-based SMALify attains the highest 2D scores (IoU 0.867, PCK@0.1 0.954, MPJVE 0.023) but produces unrealistic 3D shapes due to depth ambiguity. 4D-Fauna substantially outperforms the original 3D-Fauna (IoU 0.670, PCK@0.1 0.470, MPJVE 0.058) on every metric while maintaining natural, temporally coherent 3D reconstructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SMALify (model-based), 4D-Fauna trails on all 2D projection metrics (e.g., IoU 0.814 vs. 0.867, PCK@0.1 0.664 vs. 0.954, MPJVE 0.044 vs. 0.023), but the paper argues SMALify's higher scores come at the cost of unrealistic 3D shapes—arbitrarily elongated limbs, sideways-bent legs, and distorted frontal-view configurations that exploit depth ambiguity. Against AniMer (model-based feed-forward), 4D-Fauna wins on every metric, with notable gains in IoU (0.814 vs. 0.677), PCK@0.1 (0.664 vs. 0.537), and MPJVE (0.044 vs. 0.038). Against the original 3D-Fauna (model-free feed-forward), 4D-Fauna improves substantially across the board: IoU +0.144, PCK@0.1 +0.194, PCK@0.05 +0.140, KT-PCK@0.1 +0.089, KT-PCK@0.05 +0.063, and MPJVE −0.014, demonstrating that sequence-level optimization with keypoint supervision and smoothness losses is critical for coherent 4D reconstruction. The paper highlights that 2D metrics alone are insufficient: model-based methods score higher yet produce perceptually worse 3D results, exposing a gap between quantitative evaluation and reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D animal reconstruction literature (model-based: SMAL, SMALify, AniMer; model-free: MagicPony, 3D-Fauna, Lassie, Dove) to the temporal/4D setting by introducing sequence-level optimization on top of the feed-forward 3D-Fauna architecture. The data pipeline draws on the broader web-scraping tradition (Common Crawl, LAION, YouTube-8M) but is the first to automate the full chain from raw YouTube video to object-centric, feature-annotated animal clips suitable for 3D/4D reconstruction. Conceptually, 4D-Fauna bridges the model-based and model-free paradigms: it retains the learned shape prior of model-free methods while adding per-sequence test-time optimization with keypoint and smoothness losses reminiscent of model-based fitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Animal-in-Motion benchmark and the 30K-video / 2M-frame dataset fill a critical gap: prior animal video datasets were an order of magnitude smaller and lacked object-centric crops and auxiliary annotations needed for 4D reconstruction. The finding that model-based methods dominate 2D metrics (IoU, PCK) while producing visibly unrealistic 3D shapes, whereas model-free methods yield more natural reconstructions at lower scores, exposes a fundamental limitation of current evaluation practice and motivates 3D-aware metrics. Open questions include whether more expressive temporal models (e.g., autoregressive or video-diffusion-based) can close the remaining gap, and whether the 2D-projection-based metrics can be replaced with truly 3D-grounded evaluation once multi-view or 3D-scanned ground truth becomes available at scale.
