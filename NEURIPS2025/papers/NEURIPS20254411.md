## Problem

Modern ANNs rely on predefined architectures with fixed neuron counts and dense connectivity, making them overparameterized and vulnerable to complex inputs, whereas biological brains continuously self-assemble through growth, competition, and apoptosis. Prior self-assembly approaches like Neural Developmental Programs (NDP) either neglect neuronal apoptosis or incur prohibitive computational costs (over 12 hours per run), preventing practical use. This paper asks whether a perceptron can fully self-assemble—dynamically growing, competing, and pruning its own neurons and synapses—while remaining compatible with standard deep learning training pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254411/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SAGP consumes standard feature vectors (text, audio MFCC/Mel spectrograms, image pixels, or graph node features) as input neurons and produces class labels or classification scores at output neurons. Uniquely, the network begins with zero hidden neurons and only direct input-to-output synapses, then autonomously grows and prunes its internal topology during training. A secondary output is the out-degree of each input neuron after training, which serves as a learned feature-importance score usable for interpretability visualization and feature selection without any additional training objective.

## Method

SAGP generalizes the MLP into a Graph Perceptron (GP) where information flows via L steps of message-passing on an arbitrary directed graph, enabling non-layered topologies such as residual, Jordan, Elman, and fully connected patterns. Self-assembly is achieved through learnable neuron-level and synapse-level binary masks (parameterized via Gumbel-softmax and the Straight-Through Estimator) combined with a novel Competition Loss that drives masks toward 0 or 1, simulating biological neuron and synaptic competition. Two discrete rules govern structural change: an Apoptosis Rule removes hidden neurons whose soft mask falls below β times the population mean, and a Growth Rule adds new neurons (with all possible synapses) when no pruning occurs for N consecutive epochs. The total training objective is the downstream task loss plus an auxiliary loss summing the Competition Loss over all masks, so the entire assembly process is differentiable and integrated into standard backpropagation without requiring reinforcement learning or explicit genome encoding, yielding a >10,000× speedup over NDP.

## Results

SAGP achieves performance ranging from 96% to 110.42% of MLP while using only 0%–10.41% of hidden neurons and 0.49%–9.86% of synapses across six datasets. On Photo, SAGP-b reaches 0.8704 F1-micro (100.20% of MLP) with just 0.20% of hidden neurons and 1.09% of synapses. On ESC-50, SAGP-l surpasses MLP by 4.28% in F1-micro (0.3438 vs 0.3297) while using only 10.41% of hidden neurons. On the Digit toy dataset, SAGP achieves 99.8% accuracy with 0 hidden neurons in 0.0136 s/epoch, compared to NDP's 93.0% accuracy with 48 neurons in 158 s/epoch—a >10,000× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MLP (the standard multi-layer perceptron), SAGP matches or slightly underperforms on F1-micro (96–100% of MLP) but uses fewer than 10% of the parameters; on ESC-50 it actually exceeds MLP by 4.28% in F1-micro. Against NDP (Neural Developmental Program), SAGP achieves 99.8% vs 93.0% accuracy on the Digit dataset while running >10,000× faster per epoch (0.0136 s vs 158 s) and ultimately requiring 0 hidden neurons vs NDP's 48. As a drop-in replacement for the MLP head in GAMLP, LSTM, and LeNet, SAGP preserves or slightly improves F1-micro (e.g., 0.8620 vs 0.8546 on Computers with GAMLP; 0.3445 vs 0.3321 on ESC-50 with LSTM) while reducing hidden neurons by 88–99%. For feature selection, SAGP's out-degree ranking outperforms XGBoost (e.g., 0.8490 vs 0.8137 at 5% features with GAT) and GradEnFS (0.8517 vs 0.8359 at 10% with GAT), and is competitive with LassoNet (0.8490 vs 0.8473 at 5% with GAT), despite SAGP not being explicitly designed for feature selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGP extends the classical MLP by reformulating its computation as message-passing on a general graph (Graph Perceptron), drawing on the message-passing framework of Gilmer et al. (2017). The self-assembly mechanism departs from the closest prior work, Neural Developmental Programs (NDP, Najarro et al. 2023), in two key ways: it replaces explicit genome encoding optimized by reinforcement learning with learnable Gumbel-softmax masks driven by a competition loss, and it introduces neuronal apoptosis (a mechanism NDP neglected) alongside growth. This positions SAGP within the broader plastic-neural-network and neuroevolution literature while aligning more closely with standard backpropagation-based training than prior developmental approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully self-assembling perceptron can match or exceed MLP performance with roughly 5% of the parameters, and does so at a 10,000× speed advantage over the only prior self-assembling model (NDP), making the paradigm potentially practical. The out-degree of input neurons emerging as a competitive feature-importance signal (beating XGBoost and GradEnFS, rivaling LassoNet) opens an interpretability angle that is largely absent in standard MLPs. Key open questions include whether the message-passing overhead and the three-stage assembly dynamics (rapid growth → intense pruning → stabilization) hold at the scale of modern deep models (e.g., ImageNet, transformers), and whether the fixed hyperparameters (L, α, γ₁, γ₂, β, N) transfer across problem domains without task-specific tuning.
