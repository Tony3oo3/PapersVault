## Problem

Existing continuous-optimization structure learning methods integrate imprecise prior knowledge (which specifies only edge existence, not exact weights) by forcing edge weights toward non-zero values, but this introduces a quantitative deviation that propagates to other edges and distorts the learned DAG. The paper identifies and formalizes this overlooked failure mode, showing that the deviation creates characteristic spurious graph patterns whose prevalence grows with the magnitude of the deviation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255312/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an n×d observational data matrix X (rows are i.i.d. samples) and a set of qualitative prior directed edges Π = {(X_i → X_j)} indicating which edges should exist in the true DAG. It outputs a weighted adjacency matrix W whose non-zero entries define the learned DAG structure. The priors carry no numerical weight specifications—only directional existence—which is precisely the source of the deviation problem the method addresses. The framework operates under a linear SEM with diagonal noise covariance.

## Method

PGAP is a two-stage adaptive framework grounded in a theoretical analysis of how edge weight deviation γ on a prior-enforced edge propagates linearly to other incoming edges (Lemma 2), with propagation governed by whether the two parent nodes share a direct edge or a common child (Lemma 3). This propagation mechanism gives rise to two detectable structural signals: triangular patterns and double collision patterns, whose expected counts under an Erdős-Rényi model scale as (2/3)dp and (1/6)(dp)² respectively, far exceeding their natural occurrence rates. In the first stage, a base structure learning algorithm (e.g., NOTEARS, GOLEM, DAGMA) learns an initial DAG with prior knowledge incorporated via a penalty term. In the second stage, PGAP counts the number of deviation-induced patterns involving each prior edge and adaptively reduces that prior's loss weight by a fixed step size per detected pattern, then re-optimizes to produce the final DAG, thereby down-weighting priors that cause structural distortion.

## Results

On ER2 graphs with 2d samples and 40% prior proportion (Gauss-NV noise), PGAP reduces SHD from 35.7 to 26.7 (−25%) and improves F1 from 48.9 to 57.8 (+9 pts) over NOTEARS-Softmax. For 20d samples at the same prior proportion, SHD drops from 34.7 to 26.3 (−24%) and F1 rises from 48.3 to 56.4 (+8 pts). Across all tested settings (ER2/ER4, 2d/20d, 20/40/60 nodes, Gaussian/Exponential/Gumbel noise), PGAP-enhanced methods consistently outperform their unmodified baselines in both F1 and SHD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGAP is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement on five baseline structure learning methods: NOTEARS (Zheng et al., 2018), GOLEM (Ng et al., 2020), DAGMA (Bello et al., 2022), NT-LogLL (Deng et al., 2024), and VARSORT (Reisach et al., 2021). In all comparisons, the PGAP-enhanced version outperforms the original baseline on both F1 and SHD. The most substantial gains appear with NOTEARS-Softmax at 40% prior proportion, where SHD drops by ~9 points (35.7→26.7) and F1 rises by ~9 points (48.9→57.8) on ER2/2d. For NOTEARS-ReLU, SHD improves by ~7.5 points (60.5→53.0) at 40% prior. On denser ER4 graphs, improvements are more modest (e.g., NOTEARS-Softmax SHD: 60.3→57.0 at 2d, 57.8→50.8 at 20d), consistent with the paper's observation that deviation-induced patterns become harder to distinguish from natural structure in dense graphs. PGAP-enhanced GOLEM, DAGMA, and NT-LogLL all show consistent but smaller improvements over their unmodified counterparts across prior proportions and noise types (Figure 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NOTEARS continuous optimization framework for DAG structure learning and the broader literature on integrating prior knowledge (hard constraints, soft penalty terms) into such frameworks. Its key departure is identifying a previously overlooked side effect: enforcing qualitative priors (edge existence) in a quantitative linear SEM model induces edge weight deviation that propagates linearly to other edges, as formalized in Lemmas 1–3. Rather than proposing a new optimization objective or acyclicity constraint, PGAP operates as a post-hoc adaptive adjustment layer that detects two theoretically-derived graph patterns (triangular and double collision) as structural signals of deviation and down-weights offending priors in a second optimization pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap that practitioners of prior-guided structure learning have not formally confronted: imprecise domain knowledge, when forced into precise weight parameters, can silently corrupt the learned graph beyond the prior edge itself. The theoretical characterization of deviation propagation and the identification of two exhaustive pattern types provide a principled diagnostic tool. However, the analysis is confined to linear SEM under faithfulness, no formal consistency guarantees are provided for the two-stage estimator, and empirical gains are modest (a few F1/SHD points), particularly in denser graphs where the target patterns occur more frequently by chance. Extending the deviation analysis to nonlinear models and establishing convergence/consistency properties remain open.
