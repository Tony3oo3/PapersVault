## Problem

In clustering-based approximate MIPS, the routing step—deciding which shards to probe—has received far less attention than scoring, yet it directly controls how many points must be examined and how much data must be fetched from storage. The authors observe that the widely-used NORMALIZEDMEAN router implicitly applies an optimistic boost whose magnitude correlates with shard variance, but this behavior is ad hoc and unpredictable. The paper asks whether a statistically principled formulation of optimism in routing can yield more accurate shard selection, particularly on datasets with variable point norms.

## Input / Output

At query time, the router receives a unit-norm query vector q and, for each shard, a compact set of precomputed statistics: the shard mean μ_i, the diagonal D of the shard covariance, and t eigenvector/eigenvalue pairs (Q_t, Λ_t) of the normalized off-diagonal residual. It outputs a ranked list of the top-ℓ shards by an optimistic score θ_i, which serves as a probabilistic upper bound on the maximum inner product achievable within that shard. The routing is fully decoupled from scoring: the selected shards can subsequently be searched by linear scan, Product Quantization, or graph-based ANN, independent of the router used.

## Method

OPTIMIST formalizes routing as finding the smallest threshold θ_i ≥ ⟨q, μ_i⟩ such that at least (1+δ)/2 of the inner-product distribution in shard i falls below θ_i, where δ ∈ [0,1] controls the degree of optimism. The authors solve this via one-sided Chebyshev's inequality, yielding θ_i = ⟨q, μ_i⟩ + √((1+δ)/(1−δ)) · q^T Σ_i q, which requires the shard covariance. To avoid storing the full d×d covariance, they introduce a Masked Sketch: decompose Σ = D + R, eigendecompose the normalized residual R_∘ = D^{−1/2}RD^{−1/2}, and retain only the top t components, requiring just t+2 vectors per shard (typically t ≤ 10). A theoretical result (Lemma 2) shows this sketch dominates the standard Eckhart–Young low-rank approximation under a mild diagonal-balance assumption. The resulting router is unsupervised, independent of the clustering algorithm, and reduces the number of points probed by up to 50% at fixed recall compared to NORMALIZEDMEAN on benchmark datasets.

## Results

OPTIMIST achieves the same recall as state-of-the-art routers while probing up to 50% fewer points per query. At 95% top-100 recall, OPTIMIST saves 54% of points probed on Music (6,666 vs. 14,463 points), 22% on Text2Image, 20% on NQ-ADA2, 7.7% on MS MARCO-MiniLM, and 5.5% on GLOVE. On DeepImage, OPTIMIST is slightly worse (+10% more points), attributed to the dataset's PCA-based construction. End-to-end latency savings mirror the routing savings, reaching 55% on blob storage for Music and 24.8% for Text2Image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPTIMIST outperforms NORMALIZEDMEAN (the strongest baseline) on 5 of 6 datasets, with the largest gains on Music (54% fewer points at 95% recall) and Text2Image (22% fewer). The only exception is DeepImage, where OPTIMIST probes 10% more points, likely due to the dataset's PCA-based feature construction producing unusual partition statistics. Compared to MEAN and SCANN, OPTIMIST consistently achieves higher recall at the same probe budget; NORMALIZEDMEAN itself generally dominates MEAN and SCANN except on Music. The SUBPARTITION baseline (KRT) becomes competitive at small k values but is outperformed by OPTIMIST as k grows, particularly when the covariance sketch rank t is small (t ≤ 10). OPTIMIST's advantage is most pronounced when data points have varying norms and when shards reside on high-latency storage (blob), where the reduced I/O volume translates directly into lower end-to-end latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of clustering-based MIPS routing and the sequential decision-making literature, specifically the 'optimism in the face of uncertainty' principle from bandit algorithms. It departs from the standard MEAN and NORMALIZEDMEAN routers (inherited from spherical KMeans) by identifying that NORMALIZEDMEAN's empirical superiority over MEAN stems from an implicit, uncontrolled optimism that correlates with shard variance. The practical algorithm draws on concentration inequalities (one-sided Chebyshev) and matrix sketching (Woodruff) to produce a compact, assumption-free covariance approximation, while the Masked Sketch of Rank t is a novel construction that provably outperforms the standard Eckhart-Young-Mirsky low-rank approximation under mild diagonal-balance conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In storage-backed ANN systems—now common in production vector databases—routing accuracy directly governs I/O volume, cache pressure, and retrieval throughput, making even modest routing improvements translate into large end-to-end latency savings. The paper's 50% reduction in points probed (e.g., on Music) is practically significant for operators managing billion-scale indexes on blob storage. Open questions include: whether higher-order moment approximations (the general framework of Section 2.2) can be made computationally tractable, whether the diagonal-balance assumption of Lemma 2 can be removed via random rotations, and whether the method's advantage persists when shards reside entirely in memory rather than external storage.
