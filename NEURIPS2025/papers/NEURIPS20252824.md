## Problem

MCTS-based chain-of-thought data generation selects a single high-reward path from the search tree and discards all sibling nodes, even though these unselected branches contain partially correct reasoning, recurring error patterns, and alternative strategies. This unconditional rejection wastes informative structural signals that could improve the quality of the retained path without requiring additional rollouts or ground-truth labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252824/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a math problem statement and an MCTS search tree in which, at each depth, the selected node is paired with up to two sibling nodes sharing the same parent (ensuring identical local context). The critique model receives the selected step text alongside sibling texts and outputs a natural-language critique; the revision model then takes the selected step plus that critique and produces a refined step. The final output is a refined chain-of-thought path of depth D, and at scale, the SIGMA-15K/30K/60K training datasets used for full fine-tuning of 7B base models.

## Method

SIGMA operates in two stages. First, MCTS with UCT selection explores a branching tree of reasoning steps (n=3 candidates per node, k=5 log-probability-ranked samples, max depth 16) and extracts the highest-cumulative-Q path. Second, a sibling-guided refinement applies Textual Gradient Descent (TGD) step-by-step: at each depth d, a Critique LLM (GPT-4o-mini) compares the selected node to its same-parent siblings and emits a natural-language 'symbolic gradient' identifying overlooked strengths, weaknesses, and ambiguities; a Revision LLM then uses this feedback to rewrite the step, analogous to one coordinate-wise descent update. The process iterates sequentially over all D depths, keeping other steps fixed, so the full path undergoes one pass of coordinate descent in text space. No additional rollouts, external reward models, or access to internal model gradients are required, making the refinement model-agnostic and directly pluggable into existing MCTS data pipelines.

## Results

On the MATH benchmark, DeepSeekMath-SIGMA-7B-30K achieves 54.9% exact-match accuracy using only 30K training samples, outperforming DART-Math trained on 590K samples (53.6%) and MathFusion-60K (53.4%). Across six benchmarks (MATH, GSM8K, CollegeMath, DeepMind, OlympiadBench, TheoremQA), SIGMA-30K attains a 48.2 average on DeepSeekMath-7B, exceeding MathFusion-30K (45.7) by +2.5 and MathFusion-60K (47.5) by +0.7. Scaling to 60K samples yields 49.3 average, setting a new state-of-the-art among all tested 7B models. On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, SIGMA-60K reaches 65.6 average, surpassing DART-Math-60K (56.5) by +9.1 and MathFusion-60K (48.3) by +17.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIGMA outperforms all 16 baselines across every model backbone and data budget tested. Against the closest competitor MathFusion [37], SIGMA-30K wins by +2.5 average on DeepSeekMath (48.2 vs 45.7), +2.1 on LLaMA3 (37.7 vs 35.6), and +5.0 on Mistral (34.9 vs 29.9); at 60K, SIGMA extends the lead to +1.8, +1.2, and +0.8 respectively. DART-Math [46] at 60K is surpassed by SIGMA-60K on DeepSeekMath (49.3 vs 47.4) and LLaMA3 (40.2 vs 37.6), though MathFusion-60K edges SIGMA-60K on Mistral's Olympiad sub-score. Large-scale baselines (MetaMath [56] at 400K, MMIQC [31] at 2.3M, WizardMath [33] at 418K, RefAug [60] at 60K) are all outperformed by SIGMA using 15K–60K samples. On Qwen2.5-Math-7B (Table 4), SIGMA-60K (65.6 avg) beats Rstar [18] (747K), Eurus-2-7B-PRIME [14] (230K), OpenMath-Nemotron-7B [10] (3.2M), and AceMath-7B-Instruct [32] (2.3M) by margins of +9.1 to +31.3 points. On commonsense reasoning (Table 5), SIGMA surpasses RevThink [8] (the strongest baseline) by +0.66 on Mistral-7B and +2.36 on Gemma-7B, and outperforms SKD [51,27], Distill Step-by-Step [21], and Question Aug [26] by 5–12 points on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIGMA sits at the intersection of two research threads: MCTS-based reasoning path selection (Alphamath Almost Zero, REST-MCTS*, Mulberry) and language-model feedback optimization (TextGrad, Self-Refine, Math-Shepherd). Prior MCTS methods select a single high-reward path and discard all sibling branches, while prior feedback methods (e.g., TextGrad) backpropagate natural-language critiques through generative pipelines but lack access to the structured variation produced by tree search. SIGMA's core departure is treating discarded sibling nodes as localized contrastive references that generate 'symbolic gradients' for a textual gradient descent step, thereby converting the search tree's full structure into dense supervision without additional rollouts or external reward models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 30K-sample SIGMA dataset outperforms 590K-sample DART-Math on DeepSeekMath-7B (54.9% vs. 53.6% on MATH), and the total synthesis cost drops from ~$3,456 to ~$106, making high-quality CoT data generation accessible to small labs. The framework is model-agnostic and plugs into existing MCTS pipelines without architectural changes. Key limitations include reliance on GPT-4o-mini (or a comparable teacher) for critique and revision, evaluation restricted to 7B-scale models, and the fact that the largest gains appear on out-of-domain benchmarks (DeepMind, TheoremQA) while in-domain MATH improvements over same-budget baselines are more modest (~2–5 points). Open questions include whether sibling-guided refinement scales to 70B+ models, whether the approach transfers to non-mathematical reasoning without task-specific prompting, and whether iterative multi-pass TGD (beyond the single coordinate-descent pass used here) yields further gains.
