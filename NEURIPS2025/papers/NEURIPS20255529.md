## Problem

Visual autoregressive (VAR) modeling has shown strong potential for image generation but remains unexplored for segmentation, a task demanding precise low-level spatial perception. Existing discriminative segmentation methods treat the task as parallel pixel classification, struggling to model iterative, context-dependent spatial relationships, while prior generative segmentation approaches (e.g., GSS) fail to disambiguate overlapping instances or preserve fine-grained positional cues due to their simple mask-to-latent transformations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image (H×W×3) as input and produces segmentation masks in any of three formats: semantic (per-pixel class labels), instance (binary masks with instance IDs), or panoptic (unified thing/stuff masks). Internally, the image is encoded into a grid of latent tokens (H/d × W/d), and the output is generated as a 'seglat'—an RGB image where each instance is assigned a unique color determined by its spatial centroid within an a×a grid partition—before being decoded back into pixel-level masks. Two special tokens ([CLS] for semantic context and [TYP] for task type) condition the autoregressive generation to switch between segmentation settings.

## Method

Seg-VAR reformulates segmentation as a conditional autoregressive mask generation problem using VAR's hierarchical next-scale prediction. The key innovation is the spatial-aware seglat encoding: a colormap encoder assigns each instance a unique RGB value based on its centroid's position in a spatial grid (6^3−1 = 215 colors from {0,51,102,153,204,255}^3), enabling the transformer to distinguish overlapping objects through positional cues rather than arbitrary color labels. The architecture comprises an image encoder (backbone + multi-scale fusion) producing latent priors, a seglat encoder/decoder (adapted from ControlVAR with shared tokenizer and full cross-attention between image and seglat tokens at each scale), and latent encoder/decoder modules bridging the two. Training follows a three-stage strategy: (1) joint image-seglat training to learn seglat representations, (2) latent encoder/decoder optimization for mask-to-seglat transformation, and (3) image encoder alignment minimizing KL divergence between image-derived latents and seglat distributions. The overall objective is an ELBO combining mask reconstruction loss, KL divergence for latent alignment, and cross-entropy for token prediction, with top-k/top-p sampling (k=900, p=0.96) at inference.

## Results

On COCO panoptic segmentation (val2017, 133 categories), Seg-VAR with Swin-L backbone achieves 59.7 PQ, surpassing Mask2Former (57.8 PQ) by 1.9 PQ and the generative baseline GSS (44.9 PQ) by 14.8 PQ. On COCO instance segmentation, Seg-VAR reaches 52.7 AP (Swin-L), outperforming Mask2Former by 2.6 AP and 3.2 AP^boundary. For semantic segmentation, Seg-VAR attains 85.82 mIoU on Cityscapes (+2.52 over Mask2Former) and 54.90 mIoU on ADE20K (+7.1 over Mask2Former), setting new state-of-the-art across all three segmentation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seg-VAR outperforms all 21 compared baselines across every task and backbone. The primary discriminative baseline, Mask2Former [14], is consistently beaten: by 1.9 PQ on COCO panoptic (Swin-L), 2.6 AP on COCO instance, 2.52 mIoU on Cityscapes, and 7.1 mIoU on ADE20K. The closest generative competitor, GSS [5], is surpassed by a large margin of 14.8 PQ on panoptic and 6.4 mIoU on ADE20K, confirming that GSS's simple MLP-based maskige transformation fails to disambiguate instances. Among instance segmentation baselines, Seg-VAR exceeds Mask R-CNN [27] by 2.8 AP (R101) and 3.2 AP (Swin-L), QueryInst [24] by 3.8 AP, and Swin-HTC++ [6] by 3.2 AP. On semantic segmentation, Seg-VAR dominates the discriminative family (FCN [46], PSPNet [78], DeepLab-v3+ [8], CCNet [30], SETR [79], UperNet [71], SegFormer [72], DANet [25], OCRNet [75]) by 2.5–13.5 mIoU depending on dataset and backbone, and also surpasses the generative UViM [36] by 15.05 mIoU on Cityscapes and 11.19 mIoU on ADE20K. Notably, even at matched parameter counts (Table 8), Seg-VAR with R50 still edges out Mask2Former by 0.4 mIoU, indicating the gains are architectural rather than purely capacity-driven.

## Relation to Prior Work

Seg-VAR extends the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) modeling paradigm—originally designed for image generation via next-scale prediction—into the segmentation domain, building directly on the ELBO-based generative formulation of GSS and the joint image-control modeling of ControlVAR. It departs from GSS by replacing the simple MLP mask-to-RGB transformation with a location-sensitive color mapping (seglat) that assigns spatially-unique RGB values to instances via grid-based centroid assignment, inspired by UniGS. The framework also diverges from the dominant discriminative paradigm (Mask2Former, Mask R-CNN) by reframing segmentation as a conditional autoregressive token generation problem rather than parallel pixel-wise classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Seg-VAR demonstrates that a single autoregressive architecture can achieve state-of-the-art results across semantic, instance, and panoptic segmentation simultaneously, suggesting VAR's sequential, context-accumulating nature may be inherently well-suited to the hierarchical spatial reasoning segmentation demands. The 14.8 PQ gap over GSS highlights that spatial-aware tokenization is critical for generative segmentation to handle instance disambiguation. However, the substantially higher compute cost (~3× FLOPs and ~40% slower inference vs. Mask2Former) limits practical deployment, and the authors note that extension to video segmentation remains unexplored. An open question is whether the autoregressive inductive bias provides genuine advantages in occluded or small-object scenarios beyond what larger discriminative models could achieve with equivalent compute.
