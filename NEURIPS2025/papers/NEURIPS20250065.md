## Problem

As LLMs produce increasingly human-like text, the feature distributions of AI-generated and human-written content overlap substantially, causing classification boundaries used by existing training-based and training-free detectors to blur. This paper addresses the need for a detection approach that captures the intrinsic generative-process differences between AI and human writing in a direct, interpretable manner rather than relying on separable feature-space boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250065/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sequence (capped at 1024 tokens) and two language models—a reference model M1 (e.g., Falcon-7B-Instruct) for scoring and an observer model M2 (e.g., Falcon-7B) for cross-perplexity computation. It outputs a scalar repair score R(s) and a binary label (AI-generated if R(s) ≤ τ, human-written otherwise), where τ is a calibrated threshold. No training data or labeled examples are required at inference time, making it fully zero-shot.

## Method

DNA-DetectLLM draws an analogy between DNA replication and text generation: the ideal AI-generated sequence (constructed by greedy argmax decoding under M1) serves as the 'template strand,' while the input text is a potentially 'mutated' strand where tokens deviate from optimal choices. The method defines a repair score R(s) = ½(σ(s) + σ(ŝ|s)), where σ is the ratio of log-perplexity to cross-perplexity, quantifying the cumulative difficulty of restoring the input to its ideal AI form. A key theoretical result shows that averaging over random repair orders converges to this closed-form midpoint, eliminating the need for iterative token-by-token repair and reducing computation by ~20×. Human-written texts exhibit larger deviations from the ideal sequence (higher repair scores) than AI-generated texts, enabling reliable zero-shot classification via a single threshold comparison.

## Results

On the three self-constructed datasets (XSum, WritingPrompts, Arxiv) across GPT-4 Turbo, Gemini-2.0 Flash, and Claude-3.7 Sonnet, DNA-DetectLLM achieves an average AUROC of 98.30%, outperforming the best baseline Binoculars (97.39%) by 0.93% relatively. On four public benchmarks (M4, DetectRL Multi-LLM, DetectRL Multi-Domain, RealDet), DNA-DetectLLM attains an average AUROC of 90.86% and F1 of 87.02%, yielding relative improvements of 5.55% in AUROC and 2.08% in F1 over the strongest competitor. The gains are most pronounced on the challenging DetectRL Multi-Domain setting, where DNA-DetectLLM reaches 88.23% AUROC versus 77.45% for Binoculars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNA-DetectLLM outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and LLM combination. The closest competitor is Binoculars [14], which DNA-DetectLLM surpasses by 4.78 AUROC points on average (90.86 vs. 86.08) and 1.77 F1 points (87.02 vs. 85.25); the gap widens dramatically on DetectRL Multi-Domain (88.23 vs. 77.45) and DetectRL Multi-LLM (88.97 vs. 83.21). Fast-DetectGPT [4] is the second-strongest baseline at 85.07 average AUROC, trailing DNA-DetectLLM by 5.79 points. Training-based methods (Biscope, OpenAI-D, R-Detect) lag substantially, with Biscope reaching only 82.28 average AUROC and R-Detect just 68.61, confirming the paper's argument that training-based approaches overfit to in-distribution features. Among classic zero-shot detectors, Entropy, Likelihood, and LogRank all fall below 73 average AUROC, while DetectGPT [22] performs worst at 59.02, particularly collapsing on DetectRL Multi-Domain (34.67). The additional baselines in Appendix E (GPTZero at 95.11, ImBD at 91.20, DNA-GPT at 73.28) are also consistently outperformed by DNA-DetectLLM's 97.56 average AUROC across five datasets including PubMedQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

DNA-DetectLLM is built directly on the Binoculars scoring framework [14], which established the ratio of log-perplexity to cross-perplexity (σ) as an effective zero-shot detection signal. The paper's conceptual departure is reframing detection as a DNA-inspired mutation-repair process: rather than computing a single static score, it constructs an ideal AI-generated sequence via greedy decoding and measures the cumulative effort to 'repair' the input toward that ideal. The theoretical analysis in Section 3.6 shows this repair score simplifies to the midpoint of the initial and final Binoculars scores, R(s) = ½(σ(s) + σ(ŝ|s)), positioning the method as a principled extension of the dual-model scoring paradigm rather than a fundamentally new scoring mechanism. It also situates itself within the broader training-free detection lineage (DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT, Lastde++) while explicitly arguing that static feature-space boundaries are increasingly blurred by modern LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, zero-shot detector with consistent SOTA performance across diverse domains and strong robustness to paraphrasing and token-editing attacks, making it relevant for real-world deployment where training data is unavailable and adversarial evasion is a concern. However, the core scoring mechanism reduces to averaging two Binoculars scores, so the practical gain over Binoculars is moderate (~1% AUROC on their own data, ~5.5% on public benchmarks where baselines are weaker). Open questions include whether the repair paradigm generalizes beyond English text or to multimodal generation, whether the two-LLM requirement (reference + observer) limits scalability, and whether the DNA-inspired framing can yield genuinely new detection signals beyond the simplified midpoint formula. The paper also notes memory constraints prevented large-batch efficiency evaluation, leaving real-time monitoring performance unverified.
