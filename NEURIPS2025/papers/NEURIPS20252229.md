## Problem

Existing table recognition methods predominantly target HTML output, which lacks the structural expressiveness needed for complex tables with nested headers, merged cells, and mathematical content. Direct table-image-to-LaTeX generation has received limited attention, and current evaluation protocols (token-level TEDS/BLEU or pixel-level visual similarity) fail to jointly capture both structural correctness and rendered visual fidelity, particularly for large, deeply nested tables.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252229/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single table image (rendered from a LaTeX tabular environment in a scientific paper) as input and produces the corresponding LaTeX table code as output. Tables are stratified into three complexity levels based on cell count and the presence of \multirow/\multicolumn commands: simple (default), medium (≥2 merge commands and 100–160 cells), and complex (>160 cells). The generated LaTeX is evaluated both as a structural tree (via HTML conversion for TEDS-Structure) and as a rendered image (via CW-SSIM against the ground-truth rendering).

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning of a pre-trained MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B or InternVL2-1B) on 1.2M table-LaTeX pairs scraped from arXiv, and (2) reinforced fine-tuning via VSGRPO, a dual-reward extension of GRPO applied to 5,936 complex tables. The key innovation is a visual-in-the-loop reward design: each generated LaTeX output is compiled into a rendered image, and a CW-SSIM-based visual reward (threshold 0.6) is computed against the ground-truth rendering, while a structure reward (TEDS-Structure, threshold 0.9) is computed by converting both predicted and ground-truth LaTeX to HTML. Both rewards are binary (0/1), and their combination in the GRPO advantage term jointly optimizes structural accuracy and rendered appearance—something neither reward achieves alone. The KL penalty (β=0.02) and PPO clipping (ε=0.2) stabilize training, and ablations confirm that SFT initialization, complex-table-only RL data, and both reward signals are each necessary for peak performance.

## Results

On the complex table subset, Qwen2.5-VL-3B-VSGRPO achieves a TEDS score of 0.8673 and TEDS-Structure of 0.9218, surpassing the next-best model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) by 0.1225 in TEDS and 0.0884 in TEDS-Structure. On CW-SSIM, the same model reaches 0.6145 on complex tables (+0.0903 over Qwen2.5-VL-72B) and 0.7236 on medium tables (+0.1113), while maintaining a 0.9917 compile success rate that exceeds Mathpix (0.9889). Intern2-VL-1B-VSGRPO sets a new record on simple tables with CW-SSIM 0.8201, surpassing the previous best by 0.049. On the external LATTE benchmark, Qwen2.5-VL-3B-VSGRPO achieves CW-SSIM 0.8225 and TEDS-Structure 0.9461, outperforming all baselines including the task-specific LATTE model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2.5-VL-3B-VSGRPO (3B parameters) decisively outperforms all larger general-purpose VLMs: it beats Qwen2.5-VL-72B by 0.1225 TEDS on complex tables (0.8673 vs. 0.7448) and by 0.0884 TEDS-Structure (0.9218 vs. 0.8334), while also surpassing Intern2.5-VL-78B by a margin of 0.5294 TEDS on complex tables (0.8673 vs. 0.3379), where the 78B model collapses. Against the commercial tool Mathpix, VSGRPO leads on all three complexity levels in both TEDS and TEDS-Structure, with the largest gap on complex tables (0.8673 vs. 0.7176 TEDS). The expert LaTeX model Nougat is far behind, achieving only 0.0424 TEDS on complex tables, indicating severe structural generalization failure. On the external LATTE benchmark, VSGRPO also outperforms the task-specific LATTE model (CW-SSIM 0.8225 vs. 0.7615; TEDS-Structure 0.9461 vs. 0.9445), confirming strong cross-dataset generalization. In human evaluation, Qwen2.5-VL-3B-VSGRPO receives the most votes at every difficulty level (42/37/70 for simple/medium/complex), clearly preferred over GPT-4o, Mathpix, and its own SFT-only variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath [8] by introducing a dual-reward mechanism that jointly optimizes structural accuracy (TEDS-Structure) and rendered visual fidelity (CW-SSIM), going beyond the purely text-level rewards used in prior RL-for-generation work. It departs from the dominant table recognition literature (TableFormer, VAST, DRCC) that targets HTML output, instead focusing on LaTeX generation where prior approaches (Nougat, LATTE, LaTeXNet) either train from scratch or lack explicit reinforcement learning. The visual-in-the-loop design—compiling generated LaTeX into images and using them as reward signals—is the key conceptual departure, addressing the non-differentiability of the rendering step that prevents gradient-based optimization of visual quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The released 1.2M table-LaTeX dataset from arXiv fills a notable gap in publicly available resources for this task, and the complexity-based evaluation split (simple/medium/complex) provides a more realistic benchmark than prior uniform evaluations. The finding that a 3B model with targeted RL on complex tables can outperform 72B/78B general-purpose VLMs suggests that task-specific reinforcement is a viable path for small models in structured code generation. However, the computational bottleneck of rendering LaTeX to images for reward computation limits scalability, the RL training set is small (5,936 tables), and the absence of error bars or multiple seeds weakens confidence in the reported margins. Open questions include whether the dual-reward approach transfers to other code-generation tasks (e.g., formulas, figures) and whether the reward thresholds (0.6 CW-SSIM, 0.9 TEDS-Structure) are robust across domains.
