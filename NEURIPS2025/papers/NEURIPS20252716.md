## Problem

Prior work on learning to bid in repeated multi-unit auctions focused on adversarial opponents, leaving open how the inherent difficulty of the auction format itself (uniform vs. discriminatory pricing) affects learning when opponents are stochastic. This paper asks whether the richer bandit feedback in uniform-price auctions (which partially reveals opposing bids) can yield fundamentally faster learning rates than discriminatory auctions, and characterizes exactly when such a separation occurs.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a bidder's non-increasing marginal valuations (v_1,...,v_K) in [0,1], the number of items K, and the time horizon T. Each round it outputs a non-increasing bid vector (b_1,...,b_K) in [0,1]^K; the bid space is continuous and the algorithm operates without discretization. Feedback is either the full opposing bid vector β^t (full-information) or only the allocation count and per-unit price (bandit), with the uniform auction's bandit feedback additionally revealing certain components of β^t that fall within the bidder's bid intervals.

## Method

The core approach is a deterministic online algorithm that estimates marginal CDFs of the stochastic adversary's bids and uses concentration bands (DKW inequality, Natarajan dimension for the uniform auction) to select bids directly in the continuous [0,1]^K space, avoiding the discretization used in prior bandit-based methods. For bandit feedback, the algorithm exploits the richer uniform-auction feedback (Lemma 2: partial observation of opposing bids within bid intervals) via a round-robin exploration schedule that ensures each marginal CDF receives sufficient samples, followed by a commit phase. An improved successive-elimination variant (Algorithm 3) uses sequentially shrinking estimation intervals per CDF, yielding instance-dependent √T regret when the optimal bid set has a positive gap Δ, while retaining the worst-case T^{2/3} guarantee. For i.i.d. unit-demand adversaries, a new concentration inequality for partially observed order statistics (built on DKW and the polynomial structure of order-statistic CDFs) recovers full-information-type rates in the uniform auction.

## Results

Under full-information feedback, both uniform and discriminatory auctions achieve a tight worst-case regret of Θ̃(K√T). Under bandit feedback, worst-case regret is Θ̃(T^{2/3}) for both formats, but the uniform auction admits faster instance-dependent rates: 0 regret for unit-demand bidders, Θ̃(√T) for two-unit-demand, and Θ̃(√T) for Δ-separated or i.i.d. symmetric adversaries, while the discriminatory auction remains at Θ̃(T^{2/3}) in all these settings. The paper also provides the first matching lower bound Ω(T^{2/3}) for the uniform price auction under bandit feedback, completing the characterization of Potfer et al.'s upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Galgana & Golrezaei (2025) for discriminatory auctions, the paper matches their Θ̃(T^{2/3}) bandit-feedback regret rate but strengthens their lower bound from a discretized-bid result to one holding for any algorithm in continuous bid spaces. Against Brânzei et al. (2023) for uniform-price auctions under full-information feedback, the paper improves the regret from Θ̃(K^{3/2}√T) to Θ̃(K√T), a factor-of-√K improvement achieved by exploiting negative correlation between CDF estimates via the Natarajan dimension. Against Potfer et al. (2024) for uniform-price auctions under bandit feedback, the paper provides the first matching lower bound Ω(T^{2/3}) (requiring v₃ > 0), completing the tight characterization of their Θ̃(K^{5/3}T^{2/3}) upper bound. The key separation result is that the uniform auction's richer bandit feedback (revealing opposing bid values when they fall in specific intervals) enables √T regret on structured instances where the discriminatory auction's feedback provides no such advantage, maintaining T^{2/3} regret universally.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of work on learning to bid in repeated multi-unit auctions (Brânzei et al. 2023, Galgana & Golrezaei 2025, Potfer et al. 2024) by shifting from adversarial to stochastic opposing bids, which isolates the inherent learning difficulty of each auction format from the difficulty of facing strategic agents. Methodologically, it departs from prior approaches that discretize the bid space and apply randomized bandit algorithms, instead using deterministic CDF estimation with DKW-type concentration bands and Natarajan-dimension-based uniform convergence to operate in continuous bid spaces. The successive-elimination framework from Audibert & Bubeck (2010) is repurposed to design an adaptive algorithm (Algorithm 3) that achieves instance-dependent regret without prior knowledge of the instance's difficulty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first regret-based comparative framework for uniform vs. discriminatory multi-unit auctions, revealing that while worst-case rates are similar (Θ̃(T^{2/3}) under bandit feedback), the uniform auction's richer feedback structure enables √T regret on broad families of instances where the discriminatory auction remains at T^{2/3}. The concentration inequality for partially observed ordered statistics (Lemma 5) is noted as potentially of independent interest beyond auctions. Key limitations include the stochastic adversary assumption (strategic adversaries remain open), the requirement of known valuations, and the single-bidder perspective; extending to multiple learning bidders and unknown valuations are identified as important future directions.
