## Problem

Existing zero-shot object navigation methods either rely on planar BEV value maps with ambiguous positional distinctions or on LLM/VLM spatial reasoning that is fundamentally limited in 3D understanding. Both approaches lack a structured global spatial representation that integrates semantic priors with real-time observations, and their greedy navigation strategies cause frequent backtracking that severely degrades search efficiency in unbounded 3D environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252782/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives a 640×480 RGB-D observation and its 6-DoF pose (position + SO(3) rotation) from odometry, along with a free-form text target description (e.g., 'find the red sofa'). The system outputs a discrete navigation action from {MOVE FORWARD 0.25 m, TURN LEFT/RIGHT 30°, LOOK UP/DOWN 30°, STOP}, with success defined as issuing STOP within 0.1 m of the target within 500 steps. Internally, the system maintains a 45,000-voxel 3D belief map at 0.25 m resolution, a visibility map encoding per-voxel detection likelihood, and a fused posterior belief map that drives frontier selection.

## Method

BeliefMapNav constructs a 3D voxel-based belief map by projecting multi-scale CLIP image features (extracted from patches at k=3 scales) and SAM instance counts, back-projected via depth into a hierarchical semantic voxel map at scene/region/object levels, then weighting per-voxel cosine similarities against LLM-inferred hierarchical landmarks (room/region/object) with relevance scores to form a prior belief. A visibility map encodes per-voxel detection confidence (decreasing near image boundaries and at extreme distances, with optimal range queried from GPT-4o), and the posterior belief is the element-wise product of prior and visibility. Frontier observation beliefs are computed by ray-casting from each frontier position in four cardinal directions, summing posterior beliefs over visible voxels plus a constant for unobserved voxels. The planner then optimizes a frontier visiting permutation via GPU-accelerated simulated annealing (swap/shift/reverse moves) to minimize the expected cumulative A* path distance weighted by observation beliefs, selecting the first frontier in the optimized sequence as the next navigation goal and replanning at every step.

## Results

On HM3D, BeliefMapNav achieves 61.4% SR and 30.6 SPL, surpassing the previous best zero-shot SR (InstructNav, 58.0%) by +3.4% and improving SPL by +9.7 over InstructNav (20.9). On HSSD, it delivers 65.2% SR and 32.1 SPL, a +14.2% SR and +7.2 SPL gain over the prior best (ImagineNav, 51.0% SR / 24.9 SPL). On MP3D, BeliefMapNav reaches 37.3% SR and 17.6 SPL, slightly below UniGoal (41.0% SR) but exceeding it in SPL (+1.2), with the SR gap attributed to UniGoal's object-verification mechanism targeting MP3D's poor mesh quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BeliefMapNav outperforms all zero-shot baselines on HM3D in both SR and SPL, achieving 61.4% SR (vs. InstructNav 58.0%, SG-Nav 54.0%, UniGoal 54.5%) and 30.6 SPL (vs. VLFM 30.4%, GAMap 26.0%, InstructNav 20.9%). The SPL advantage over InstructNav is particularly large (+9.7), reflecting the benefit of the distance-cost-aware planner over InstructNav's dense search strategy. On HSSD, BeliefMapNav dominates with 65.2% SR and 32.1 SPL, exceeding the runner-up ImagineNav by +14.2% SR and +7.2 SPL. On MP3D, BeliefMapNav's SR (37.3%) trails UniGoal (41.0%) and SG-Nav (40.2%) by 3.7 and 2.9 points respectively, which the authors attribute to those methods' object-verification mechanisms compensating for MP3D's poor mesh quality; however, BeliefMapNav still leads in SPL (17.6 vs. 16.4). Against supervised methods like SGM (60.2% SR on HM3D) and ProcTHOR (54.4% SR), BeliefMapNav matches or exceeds their SR while remaining fully zero-shot, and its SPL of 30.6 is competitive with SGM's 30.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines in zero-shot object navigation: (1) BEV value-map methods (GAMap, InstructNav, VLFM) that provide dense spatial estimates but lack high-level semantic reasoning, and (2) LLM/VLM-based approaches (SG-Nav, L3MVN) that offer commonsense reasoning but are limited in spatial understanding. BeliefMapNav unifies these by constructing a 3D voxel-based belief map that encodes hierarchical spatial-visual features (scene/region/object levels via CLIP and SAM) alongside LLM-generated landmarks with relevance scores, then fuses this prior with a visibility map for posterior updates. The local navigation planner is inherited from VLFM, while the path-level optimization via GPU-accelerated simulated annealing over frontier visiting sequences is a new contribution not present in prior greedy or single-goal selection methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9.7 SPL gain over the highest-SR zero-shot method (InstructNav) on HM3D demonstrates that structured 3D belief representations can substantially improve path efficiency without sacrificing success rate, which is critical for real-world deployment where energy and time budgets matter. The error analysis reveals that 48% of remaining failures stem from open-vocabulary detector errors and 37% from multi-floor navigation, pointing to clear future directions. The authors note the 3D voxel map could extend to mobile manipulation, but validation is limited to simulated object navigation on three benchmarks, and the local planner (inherited from VLFM) remains a bottleneck in narrow spaces. The finding that LLM choice has negligible impact (≤1% SR across 8B–trillion-parameter models) suggests the framework's robustness but also implies the LLM's role is currently narrow.
