## Problem

Driving world models trained exclusively on real-world expert trajectories struggle to reliably simulate hazardous or non-expert behaviors (collisions, off-road deviations) that are severely underrepresented in training data, leading to hallucinations when exposed to out-of-distribution actions. This limitation undermines their utility for policy evaluation, reward estimation, and closed-loop simulation, where the model must faithfully predict consequences of suboptimal driving decisions.

## Input / Output

ReSim takes 9 historical front-view frames at 10 Hz, an optional high-level text command (e.g., 'Turning left'), and an optional ego trajectory of 8 waypoints at 2 Hz as input, and generates a 4-second future video at 10 Hz and 512×896 resolution. A separate Video2Reward module (frozen DINOv2 + prediction head) converts the simulated video into a scalar reward, and an inverse dynamics model (IDM) with an XVO visual-odometry backbone translates the predicted video into an executable ego trajectory of 8 waypoints at 2 Hz. The model supports both action-conditioned and action-free prediction modes, with NAVSIM trajectories randomly masked at p=0.5 during training to enable both.

## Method

ReSim adapts the 2B-parameter CogVideoX diffusion transformer into a controllable driving world model via a three-stage curriculum: (1) visual+text conditioning on 4M OpenDV clips, (2) joint training on OpenDV, NAVSIM (85K), and CARLA (88K) at 256×448 with a frozen backbone and LoRA adapter to learn action conditioning via a lightweight trajectory encoder (two attention blocks + linear head), and (3) full fine-tuning at 512×896. Two key training innovations distinguish the approach: a dynamics consistency loss that supervises latent motion discrepancies across multiple timestep intervals (K=4) to enforce spatiotemporal coherence, and an unbalanced noise sampling strategy that increases the frequency of high-noise timesteps [500,1000] from 1/2 to 2/3 to prevent the model from taking shortcuts on low-noise steps. The heterogeneous data compilation is central—CARLA provides 88K samples of both expert (PDM-Lite) and non-expert (rule-based exploration with random steering, hard turns, lane changes, tailgating) behaviors that are absent from real-world logs, enabling reliable simulation of hazardous outcomes.

## Results

On nuScenes (zero-shot, no training data), ReSim achieves FID 5.2 and FVD 50.4, outperforming the best in-distribution baseline Vista (FID 6.9, FVD 89.4) by 44% in FVD. On Waymo action controllability, ReSim reduces Trajectory Difference to 0.86 under expert action conditioning (vs. Vista's 1.89, a 54% improvement). For reward-guided policy selection on NAVSIM, ReSim + Video2Reward achieves PDMS 74.1, surpassing the uniform average ensemble (66.8) and Vista-based selection (59.2). On NAVSIM planning, the video-prediction-based policy reaches PDMS 86.6 using only front-view video, exceeding LAW (84.6) and TransFuser (84.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReSim outperforms all 13 baselines across every evaluated metric. On nuScenes prediction fidelity (Table 2), ReSim achieves the best FID (5.2) and FVD (50.4) in a zero-shot setting, beating the strongest in-distribution baseline Vista (FID 6.9, FVD 89.4) by 44% in FVD, and older methods like DriveGAN (FVD 390.8) and WoVoGen (FVD 417.7) by over 7×. On Waymo action controllability (Table 1), ReSim reduces Trajectory Difference to 0.86 under expert action conditioning versus Vista's 1.89 (54% improvement) and 1.13 under action-free versus Vista's 5.68 (80% improvement). For reward-guided policy selection (Table 3), ReSim + Video2Reward reaches PDMS 74.1, outperforming Vista-based selection (59.2), the uniform average ensemble (66.8), and individual policies TransFuser (47.7) and LTF (47.2). On NAVSIM planning (Table 4), the ReSim-based video prediction policy achieves PDMS 86.6, surpassing world-model planners LAW (84.6) and DrivingGPT (82.4), end-to-end methods UniAD (83.4) and TransFuser (84.0), and the VO planner baseline (78.4), while requiring only front-view video without multi-sensor input, ego status, or extra annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReSim builds directly on CogVideoX's diffusion transformer architecture and Vista's clean-latent conditioning strategy for visual context, but departs from the entire line of driving world models (GAIA-1, DriveDreamer, GEM, Vista) by introducing heterogeneous data compilation that mixes real-world expert logs with CARLA-simulated non-expert trajectories. The Video2Reward module also breaks from prior reward formulations: Drive-WM relies on rule-based 3D perception models sensitive to sensor configuration, while Vista uses uncertainty-based rewards that cannot capture specific hazard types; ReSim instead learns a generalizable video-to-reward mapping from simulator infraction scores. The dynamics consistency loss and unbalanced noise sampling are new training strategies not present in prior driving world models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental data-bias problem in driving world models: real-world logs are overwhelmingly safe expert demonstrations, making models unreliable precisely when they are needed most (hazardous or non-expert scenarios). By showing that a modest 88K-sample CARLA corpus can substantially improve both controllability and reward estimation in real-world settings, the work provides a practical recipe for closing the simulation-to-reality gap. Key open questions remain: inference is slow (~2 min per 4s clip on one A100), agents cannot yet be trained within ReSim's synthesized world, and fair closed-loop planning benchmarks are still lacking. The Video2Reward paradigm of learning rewards from a unified video interface rather than hand-crafted 3D perception rules is a direction likely to be adopted more broadly.
