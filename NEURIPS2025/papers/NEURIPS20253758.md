## Problem

While it was known that padded transformers remain within TC^0, it was an open question whether they could actually solve all problems in TC^0 (or even the smaller class AC^0). This paper answers that question with an exact characterization and further extends the analysis to looped+padded transformers, revealing how padding (width) and looping (depth) systematically expand expressive power while preserving parallelism as alternatives to sequential chain-of-thought decoding.

## Input / Output

The transformer receives an input string w over alphabet Σ, prepended with a beginning-of-sequence token $, and appended with O(n^k) blank padding tokens (□ ∉ Σ) that serve as parallelizable storage. A linear head on the final layer produces a binary output (0 or 1) indicating language membership. The model uses causal masking, masked pre-norm, and averaging hard attention (AHAT) with no positional encodings, and the number of padding tokens scales polynomially with input length n while loop depth scales as O(log^d n).

## Method

The key insight is that n^k padding tokens provide enough 'storage space' to store all n^k variable assignments of an FO+M^2 formula, with each padding token holding the truth value of a subformula under a specific assignment via the layer-norm hash representation. Attention heads then implement quantifiers by averaging over relevant subsets of padding tokens and thresholding, inductively building up the full formula in O(ℓ) layers for nesting depth ℓ. For looped transformers, the paper introduces the wide-TC^d circuit evaluation problem and proves it is complete for FO-uniform TC^d under L-reductions, then shows that O(log^d n) looping suffices to evaluate such circuits, yielding the exact characterization AHAT_*^d = FO-uniform TC^d. A novel uniformity collapse result (FO-uniform TC^d = L-uniform TC^d for d ≥ 1) further strengthens the characterization and is of independent interest in circuit complexity.

## Results

The paper provides exact characterizations of transformer expressive power via three main theorems. Theorem 1 shows that fixed-depth (d=0) polynomially-padded AHATs recognize exactly FO-uniform TC^0, resolving an open question by proving the matching lower bound (the upper bound was previously known). Theorem 2 extends this to show that for any d ≥ 1, O(log^d n)-looped polynomially-padded AHATs recognize exactly FO-uniform TC^d. Corollary 2.1 then implies that polylog-looped poly-padded transformers recognize exactly FO-uniform NC, the largest class of parallelizable problems. Additionally, Theorem 3 proves a novel circuit complexity result: for d ≥ 1, FO-uniform TC^d = L-uniform TC^d = NL-uniform TC^d (uniformity collapse).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or baseline comparisons. The key 'comparison' is between the paper's exact characterizations and prior partial results: the TC^0 upper bound for padded transformers was established by Pfau et al. (2024) and Merrill & Sabharwal (2023a), but the matching lower bound (i.e., that padded transformers can solve all of TC^0) was previously open and is the paper's first main contribution. For looped transformers, the paper builds on Merrill & Sabharwal (2025) who showed log-depth transformers can solve graph connectivity (an NL-complete problem), and extends this to show that log^d-looping with padding captures exactly TC^d. The paper also positions its results relative to chain-of-thought (CoT), noting that CoT can solve problems outside TC^0 (e.g., in P) but at the cost of sequential decoding, whereas padding and looping preserve parallelism up to FO-uniform NC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads: (1) the circuit-complexity characterization of transformer expressive power, initiated by Merrill & Sabharwal (2023a) showing AHATs are in TC^0 and by the FO+M^2 logic framework (Merrill & Sabharwal, 2023c; Mix Barrington et al., 1990), and (2) the padding-token mechanism introduced by Pfau et al. (2024), which established an upper bound but left the matching lower bound open. The looped-transformer model and the graph-connectivity result from Merrill & Sabharwal (2025) provide the depth-scaling machinery that the paper composes with padding to obtain its main theorems. The paper's key methodological departure is importing the classical complexity-theoretic apparatus of complete problems and reductions (FO-reductions, L-reductions, wide-TC^d circuit evaluation) into the transformer analysis, which prior work had not done.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact characterization (Theorem 1) resolves a concrete open question from Pfau et al. (2024) and shows that padding tokens—despite being a trivially parallelizable mechanism—close the entire gap to TC^0, meaning no further architectural change is needed at fixed depth. The extension to looped transformers (Theorem 2) and the uniformity collapse (Theorem 3) together suggest that padding + polylog looping is a principled, parameter-free way to scale transformer expressivity up to FO-uniform NC while preserving parallelism, offering a theoretical justification for exploring these as alternatives to sequential chain-of-thought. Open questions include a finer-grained characterization of AHAT_k^d for fixed k, the practical learnability of looped layers, and whether the AHAT idealization (hard attention, masked pre-norm) is essential or merely convenient.
