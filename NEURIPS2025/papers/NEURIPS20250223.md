## Problem

Existing transport-based generative models (diffusion, flow matching) are trained for a single predetermined task and cannot be repurposed for inpainting, denoising, posterior sampling, or other objectives without retraining or task-specific architectural modifications. The paper asks whether a single pretrained model can natively support an arbitrary collection of generative tasks by generalizing the interpolation structure itself rather than adding task-specific guidance or conditioning at inference.

## Input / Output

The model is trained on pairs (x_0, x_1) where x_0 is Gaussian noise and x_1 is data, with operator parameters (α, β) sampled from a connected set S during training. At inference, the task is selected entirely by choosing a path (α_t, β_t) through S: for inpainting, α is a diagonal matrix with entries 0 at observed pixels and 1−t at masked ones; for multichannel denoising, α and β are linear combinations of Fourier-domain corruption operators; for posterior sampling, the reward parameters (A, b) determine a shifted operator pair (α_r, β_r) and state x_r; for maze planning, α_i = 0 pins the trajectory at specified waypoints. Outputs are full images (28×28 to 256×256), denoised images, lattice field configurations (32×32), or 50-point trajectories, all produced by the same trained U-Net drift estimator.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the scalar time variable in stochastic interpolants with bounded linear operators, defining I(α, β) = αx_0 + βx_1 over a connected set S of operator pairs. Two drifts η_0(α,β,x) = E[x_0 | I(α,β)=x] and η_1(α,β,x) = E[x_1 | I(α,β)=x] are learned jointly via least-squares regression over the operator space, and samples are transported along any differentiable path (α_t, β_t) via a probability flow ODE or an SDE with tunable diffusion coefficient. For the Hadamard-product (diagonal) case, this yields a universal inpainting model where arbitrary masks are handled zero-shot by setting the corresponding α entries. For quadratic-reward posterior sampling, the paper derives an exact analytical relation (Proposition 3.1) expressing the posterior drifts in terms of the prior drifts evaluated at a transformed operator pair (α_r, β_r) and shifted state x_r, eliminating the need for retraining. The drift estimator is a U-Net that takes the operator parameters as additional conditioning input, trained with a progressive curriculum from the diagonal of the hypercube to full per-pixel or block-wise operator variation.

## Results

On CelebA (128×128) random-mask inpainting, the method achieves the best PSNR of 33.76 dB and SSIM of 0.967, outperforming the strongest baseline PnP-Flow (33.54 / 0.953). On AFHQ-Cat (256×256), it leads on both random-mask (PSNR 33.11, SSIM 0.945) and block-mask (PSNR 26.96, SSIM 0.914) settings. On CelebA block-mask inpainting, it ranks second in PSNR (29.98 vs. PnP-Flow's 30.59) but remains competitive in SSIM (0.938 vs. 0.943). Beyond inpainting, the same pretrained model performs zero-shot sequential generation, posterior sampling in the φ⁴ lattice model, and maze path planning without any retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against four state-of-the-art flow/diffusion-based inpainting methods: OT-ODE (Pokle et al., 2024), D-Flow (Ben-Hamu et al., 2024), Flow-Priors (Zhang et al., 2024), and PnP-Flow (Martin et al., 2025). The proposed method achieves the best PSNR and SSIM on three of four settings: CelebA random-mask (33.76/0.967 vs. PnP-Flow's 33.54/0.953), AFHQ-Cat random-mask (33.11/0.945 vs. 32.98/0.930), and AFHQ-Cat block-mask (26.96/0.914 vs. 26.87/0.904). On CelebA block-mask, PnP-Flow edges ahead in both PSNR (30.59 vs. 29.98) and SSIM (0.943 vs. 0.938), though the gap is small. OT-ODE is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing the top methods by 4–5 dB in PSNR across all settings. A key distinction is that the proposed method achieves these results zero-shot with arbitrary masks chosen at inference time, whereas the baselines are typically tailored to specific corruption types or require task-specific guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the stochastic interpolant framework (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden 2022; Albergo et al. 2023a) and flow matching (Lipman et al. 2022) by replacing the scalar time variable with bounded linear operators, yielding 'operator-based interpolants' I(α,β) = αx₀ + βx₁. Where prior flow-matching and diffusion models are trained for a single predetermined transport (noise→data), and guidance-based approaches (DDPM, DPS) require task-specific trajectory modifications, this work learns a single drift over a rich operator space S, so that different generative tasks (inpainting, denoising, posterior sampling, path planning) correspond to different operator paths chosen at inference time. The probability flow ODE and SDE generation results (Propositions 2.5–2.6) are direct generalizations of the standard stochastic interpolant transport theorems, and the posterior sampling result (Proposition 3.1) extends the quadratic-reward guidance literature by deriving exact drift transformations rather than approximate guidance terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The operator-based interpolant provides a clean, unified mathematical language in which diverse generative tasks are different traversals of the same learned space, potentially enabling a single pretrained model to serve as a general-purpose generative engine without task-specific retraining. Key limitations temper the impact: posterior sampling is restricted to quadratic rewards, the empirical validation uses small datasets (MNIST 28×28, CelebA 128×128, AFHQ-Cat with only 5,000 training images), and the training cost increases with the dimensionality of the operator space. Open questions include whether the multitask advantage scales to high-resolution image generation or language modeling, how to handle non-quadratic rewards, and whether the connectedness requirement on S limits practical operator choices.
