## Problem

Standard gradient guidance for diffusion-based test-time planning performs well only under convex, differentiable reward landscapes, but real-world planning tasks involve non-convex objectives, non-differentiable constraints (e.g., intermediate waypoints), and multi-reward structures where naive gradient methods converge to local optima. Moreover, recent supervised planning approaches require task-specific training or value estimators, undermining zero-shot generalization to unseen test-time objectives.

## Input / Output

TDP consumes a pretrained diffusion planner (trained on offline demonstrations), a user-defined guide function J(τ) that may be non-convex or non-differentiable, and an initial state s₀. It outputs a complete action trajectory τ̂ = â₁:ₜ̂ that maximizes J within a planning horizon T_pred determined by the planner architecture. The method is fully zero-shot: no expert demonstrations, value estimators, or task-specific fine-tuning are required at test time, and the state decomposition (observation vs. control features) is inferred autonomously from the guide function's gradient structure.

## Method

TDP formulates test-time planning as a tree search with a bi-level sampling process. In the first level (Parent Branching), a gradient-based state decomposition (Algorithm 1) autonomously splits state features into observation states (non-zero ∂J/∂sᵢ) and control states (zero gradient); fixed-potential particle guidance (PG) then applies repulsive RBF-based forces among control states to generate diverse parent trajectories, while gradient guidance simultaneously steers observation states toward task-relevant regions. In the second level (Sub-Tree Expansion), each parent trajectory is perturbed at a random branch site and refined via fast conditional denoising (N_f ≪ N steps) to produce child trajectories that improve local alignment with the guide signal while restoring dynamic feasibility. The two guidance components are unified into a single integrated guidance term g_TDP = g_gg + g_pg within one conditional distribution. Proposition 1 provides theoretical justification: initializing guided sampling from on-subspace parent trajectories (rather than standard Gaussian noise) prevents convergence to off-subspace local optima, enabling recovery of the global optimum under multi-component guide functions.

## Results

On the KUKA robot arm PNWP task (non-concave guide function), TDP achieves 66.81±0.17, nearly doubling MCSS (35.69±0.07) and outperforming AdaptDiffuser (39.72±0.08) by 27 points. On Maze2D gold-picking, TDP scores 43.7 (single-task) and 72.4 (multi-task) average, versus 21.1 and 44.9 for MCSS. On AntMaze multi-goal exploration, TDP finds 66.1±1.9 goals with a sequence match score of 33.8±1.4, improving ~11% over MCSS (61.3 goals, 30.2 sequence match) while also reducing timesteps per goal from 612.1 to 558.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDP consistently outperforms all five baselines across all three task suites. Against Diffuser [1] (single-sample gradient guidance), TDP achieves roughly 2× the score on PNWP (66.81 vs 31.13) and 4× on Maze2D single-task (43.7 vs 6.8). Against TAT/Diffuser^γ [29], which aggregates trajectories into a tree but still relies on standard diffusion sampling, TDP gains 32 points on PNWP (66.81 vs 34.72) and 28 points on Maze2D multi-task (72.4 vs 15.9). Against MCSS [43] (sample-and-select), TDP improves by 31 points on PNWP (66.81 vs 35.69) and 22 points on Maze2D single-task (43.7 vs 21.1). Against MCSS+SS [13] (MCMC-based stochastic sampling with 4× more computation), TDP still leads by 30 points on PNWP and 28 points on Maze2D single-task. Against AdaptDiffuser [2] (fine-tuning with synthetic expert demos), TDP surpasses it on PNWP (66.81 vs 39.72) and PNP average (49.06 vs 48.36), demonstrating that zero-shot test-time planning can match or exceed per-task training approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

TDP builds on the Diffuser framework (Janner et al., 2022) for trajectory generation and the gradient guidance formalism (Guo et al., 2024) for test-time steering, but departs from the single-pass denoising paradigm by introducing a tree-search structure with bi-level sampling. It repurposes fixed-potential particle guidance (Corso et al., ICLR 2024) — originally designed for diverse image generation — as an exploration mechanism over control states, combined with gradient-based exploitation over observation states. The paper explicitly positions itself against the dominant trend of improving pretrained model quality (sequential, hierarchical, fine-tuning approaches), arguing that the guidance algorithm itself is the underexplored bottleneck for complex test-time objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TDP provides a practical, training-free solution to a well-identified failure mode of diffusion planners: gradient guidance collapses on non-convex, non-differentiable, or multi-reward objectives that are common in real-world planning. The Proposition 1 result gives a clean theoretical explanation for why initialization from on-subspace (unconditional) samples is critical, which could inform broader diffusion guidance research beyond planning. The main open question is computational cost — the pairwise distance calculations and expanded search add overhead that may limit scalability to very high-dimensional or long-horizon settings, and the authors note that learned priors or more efficient search strategies are needed to close this gap.
