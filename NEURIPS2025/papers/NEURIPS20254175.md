## Problem

Existing categorical frameworks for causality (Markov categories, simplicial sets) treat causal models as objects in symmetric monoidal categories but lack a unified structure that simultaneously provides a sheaf-theoretic mechanism for composing local causal mechanisms into global functions, a generic categorical definition of interventions, and an internal logical language for counterfactual reasoning. This paper identifies the topos as the category that naturally supplies all three properties at once.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254175/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a causal model specified as a triple ⟨U, V, F⟩ (exogenous variables, endogenous variables, and a global function) or, more generally, a causal functor F: C → E from a symmetric monoidal structure category (e.g., a Markov category) to a cocomplete semantic category (e.g., Prob). It produces a topos C_TCM with a non-Boolean subobject classifier Ω carrying three truth values {0, ½, 1}, a Heyting algebra of subobjects generated by interventions, and an internal Mitchell-Benabou language with Kripke-Joyal forcing semantics. The key output is the universal factorization: any causal functor mapping decomposable structure to probabilistic semantics factors uniquely through the TCM representation via the Yoneda embedding.

## Method

The paper defines topos causal models (TCMs) as a strict generalization of SCMs where objects are triples ⟨U,V,F⟩ and arrows are commutative diagrams between models. It proves C_SCM is a topos by explicitly constructing a subobject classifier Ω with three truth values (an element x is classified as 1 if x∈U, ½ if x∉U but g(x)∈V, and 0 otherwise), showing this is non-Boolean and induces intuitionistic logic. The framework is then generalized to functor categories via the Yoneda embedding, where a Grothendieck topology on the causal category ensures local functions 'collate' into a unique global function (sheaf condition). The central universal property (Theorem 7) states that any causal functor from a structural category to a cocomplete semantic category factors uniquely through the TCM, with the left adjoint L to the right Kan extension R defined as a colimit over the category of elements. Finally, the paper defines the Causal Mitchell-Benabou Language (CMBL), a typed local set theory whose formulas are interpreted as arrows into Ω, with Kripke-Joyal forcing semantics replacing classical truth by local truth over generalized elements.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental results or quantitative benchmarks. The main contributions are eight theorems establishing that (1) the category of SCMs forms a topos (Theorem 3), (2) any causal functor from a structural category to a cocomplete semantic category factors uniquely through a TCM representation via the Yoneda embedding (Theorem 7), and (3) the subobject classifier for causal interventions forms a Heyting algebra with intuitionistic rather than Boolean semantics (Theorem 8). The subobject classifier Ω for C_SCM is shown to have three truth values {0, 1/2, 1} rather than the Boolean {0, 1}, reflecting the three-way classification of exogenous variable realizations relative to a submodel.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include empirical comparisons or baselines, as it is a foundational theoretical contribution. Its implicit comparison is against the standard SCM framework [Pearl, 2009], which it strictly generalizes: TCMs subsume SCMs as a special case while adding sheaf-theoretic composition of local mechanisms, a generic subobject-classifier-based definition of interventions, and an internal intuitionistic logical language (Mitchell-Benabou language with Kripke-Joyal semantics). The paper also positions itself relative to prior categorical causality work (Markov categories [Fritz, 2020], string diagram surgery [Jacobs et al., 2018], and universal causality [Mahadevan, 2023]), arguing that the topos framework uniquely provides all three universal properties (sheaf gluing, subobject classifier interventions, and internal logic) simultaneously. No quantitative performance comparison is made or applicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of categorical approaches to causality (Markov categories, string diagrams, simplicial sets) by recasting the entire framework in topos theory, building directly on Mahadevan's earlier Universal Causality framework (2023) and Pearl's SCMs as the base case. The key departure from prior categorical work is the use of toposes specifically, which brings three structural tools not available in symmetric monoidal or simplicial formulations: sheaf-theoretic gluing of local causal mechanisms, a subobject classifier for interventions, and an internal intuitionistic logical language. The paper positions TCMs as a strict generalization of SCMs, with the universal property (Theorem 7) showing that any causal functor from a structural category to a semantic category factors uniquely through the TCM representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central conceptual contribution is showing that the subobject classifier for causal models is non-Boolean (a Heyting algebra), meaning causal interventions and counterfactuals are inherently governed by intuitionistic rather than classical logic — a structural insight that reframes how one should reason about do-calculus and potential outcomes. The universal factorization theorem provides a clean categorical criterion for when a causal model is well-posed. However, the paper is entirely theoretical with no experiments, and the practical implications (e.g., the j-do-calculus and j-stable causal discovery mentioned as future work) remain unvalidated. Open questions include whether the intuitionistic logic induced by the topos structure yields computationally tractable inference procedures and how the framework handles stochastic (non-deterministic) causal mechanisms beyond the deterministic SCM case.
