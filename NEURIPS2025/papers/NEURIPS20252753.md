## Problem

In heterogeneous federated learning, clients with diverse model architectures and non-IID data face two persistent knowledge-sharing barriers: aggregating local knowledge into a global model without proxy datasets (which risk privacy leakage), and distributing global knowledge back to clients in a way that respects each client's specific architecture. Existing approaches either rely on proxy data for knowledge distillation, share only coarse-grained prototypes or logits, or perform complex layer matching, all of which lose fine-grained, layer-specific knowledge.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a private non-IID subset of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) and a distinct CNN architecture ranging from a 5-layer CNN (~3.2M params) to ResNet-152 (~58.2M params). Rather than uploading full models, each client transmits only the top-k=2 critical layers (selected by relative L2 parameter change) to the server, which generates Gaussian pseudo-data X~N(0,I) to probe layer responses. The output is a set of personalized local models that retain each client's original architecture and produce class predictions, with dimensional mismatches between global and local layers resolved via partial parameter-space projection (zero-padding beyond the minimum shared dimension).

## Method

FedFree operates through three modules. (1) Knowledge Extraction: each client identifies critical layers by computing the relative L2-norm parameter change Δθ_{i,l}^t = ‖θ_{i,l}^t − θ_{i,l}^{t−1}‖²/‖θ_{i,l}^{t−1}‖² and uploads only the top-k layers. (2) Knowledge-Sharing (local-to-global): the server generates Gaussian pseudo-data and performs a reverse layer-wise distillation, updating each global layer g_j by minimizing the squared output difference between g_j X and the corresponding uploaded client layer θ_{i,l} X, thereby transferring functional knowledge without any real data. (3) Knowledge Alignment (global-to-local): the server computes Knowledge Gain Entropy (KGE) = H(g_j^t) − H(θ_{i,l}^t) on 256-bin quantized weight distributions for same-type layer pairs, and sends back only the global layer with the maximum positive KGE to each client, which replaces its corresponding local layer. This targeted, entropy-guided distribution ensures each client receives only updates that encode broader feature diversity than its current state, with O(1/T) convergence guarantees under both strongly convex and non-convex objectives.

## Results

FedFree achieves average test accuracy of 95.81% on CIFAR-10 and 76.83% on CIFAR-100 with 10 heterogeneous clients, outperforming the best HtFL baseline (FedGH) by 1.67 and 15.92 percentage points respectively. The gains are most dramatic at scale: with 100 heterogeneous clients on CIFAR-100, FedFree reaches 49.20% versus 33.63% for the best baseline (FedKD), a 46.3% relative improvement. In the homogeneous setting, FedFree also leads, achieving 59.34% on CIFAR-100 (10 clients) versus 42.70% for FedProx, a 38.97% relative gain. On TinyImageNet (100 clients), FedFree attains 23.31% accuracy, surpassing all four HtFL baselines (13.28–17.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedFree outperforms all eight baselines across every setting. Among HtFL baselines, FedGH is the strongest competitor at 10–20 clients (94.24% and 91.85% on CIFAR-10), but FedFree still leads by 1.67 and 3.81 points; the gap widens substantially on CIFAR-100 (15.92 and 10.19 points). At 100 clients, FedKD becomes the best HtFL baseline (76.43% CIFAR-10, 33.63% CIFAR-100), yet FedFree surpasses it by 13.87 and 46.30 points respectively. Among HmFL baselines, pFedLA is competitive at 10–20 clients on CIFAR-10 (90.94%, 88.36%) but collapses to 16.63% at 100 clients, while FedFree maintains 86.08%. FedProx is the strongest HmFL baseline on CIFAR-100 (42.70% at 10 clients), but FedFree exceeds it by 38.97 points. FedAvg, FedProto, and FedTGP are consistently the weakest baselines, with FedTGP dropping to 38.04% on CIFAR-10 (20 clients, HtFL) and FedProto to 28.03% (100 clients, HtFL CIFAR-10).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedFree carves new ground at the intersection of knowledge distillation and heterogeneous federated learning by inverting the standard distillation direction: rather than a server-side teacher (built on proxy data) distilling to clients, local critical layers serve as the knowledge source and a server-side global model is updated to match their functional outputs on Gaussian pseudo-data. This departs from proxy-data-dependent KD methods (FedMD, FedDF, FedKD) and from prototype/representation-sharing approaches (FedProto, FedTGP) that operate only at the output level. The Knowledge Gain Entropy mechanism for global-to-local distribution is a novel selection criterion not seen in prior HtFL work, which typically broadcasts aggregated parameters or prototypes uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedFree addresses two persistent barriers in HtFL—privacy-preserving local-to-global aggregation without proxy data and targeted global-to-local distribution—that have limited the practical deployment of heterogeneous federated learning on resource-constrained edge devices. The framework's simplicity (Gaussian pseudo-data, entropy-based layer selection) makes it attractive for real-world adoption, though the paper acknowledges that physical deployment and extremely large models remain untested. Open questions include whether KGE's heuristic link between weight-distribution entropy and genuine knowledge gain holds under more extreme heterogeneity, and whether the reverse-transfer mechanism scales to transformer-based or multimodal architectures beyond the CNNs evaluated here.
