## Problem

Existing spatiotemporal reasoning benchmarks (GeoQA, TemporalQA, STBench, Open3DVQA) offer fragmented views that collapse tasks into multiple-choice, ignore sensor-grounded geometric computation, and do not evaluate the full reasoning hierarchy from state estimation through relational inference to world-knowledge-aware decision-making. No prior benchmark assesses Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) or the critical capability of invoking external tools (e.g., a Python code interpreter) that real-world Cyber-Physical System agents require.

## Input / Output

Inputs span five simulated sensor modalities (range, bearing, range+bearing, binary proximity, time-of-arrival events) with modality-specific uniform noise (e.g., 1% for range), geometric object coordinate pairs, time-interval pairs, and natural-language landmark descriptions paired with OpenStreetMap coordinates. Outputs are open-ended numeric estimates (e.g., [x,y] coordinates, event times in seconds, field variable values) or binary true/false judgments, formatted between [RESULTS_START] and [RESULTS_END] markers. A key design choice is that every task is evaluated in two modes: Direct Answering (DA), where the model reasons purely in text, and Code Interpreter (CI), where the model writes and executes Python (with numpy, scipy, shapely, filterpy) in a sandbox, enabling separate measurement of reasoning ability versus tool-usage competency.

## Method

STARK organizes 26 tasks across a three-tier hierarchy: Tier 1 (state estimation) simulates four-sensor localization/tracking in a 10×10 grid with partial observability via random sensor dropout, and field-variable prediction from real PurpleAir/CalTrans PeMS data; Tier 2 (reasoning over states) generates spatial relationship instances from 35 geometry-predicate combinations (DE-9IM/ArcGIS) and temporal relationship instances from Allen's 13 interval relations, including compound spatiotemporal tasks that require localizing events in time via spatial constraints; Tier 3 (world-knowledge-aware reasoning) uses ArcGIS and OpenStreetMap for landmark direction, proximity, route planning, ETA, and FSM-simulated user trajectories for POI/intent prediction. The benchmark is evaluated on 8 LLMs and 3 LRMs (o3, o3-mini, o4-mini) in both DA and CI modes, with first-principle baselines (nonlinear least-squares multilateration, triangulation, SCAAT-style EKF, distance-weighted interpolation) for Tier 1 and a random-guess baseline (0.5 accuracy) for Tiers 2–3 where deterministic GIS tools would be uninformative. Metrics are trimmed RMSE (excluding top/bottom 10%) for coordinate/time tasks and trimmed RMSPE for scale-varying field predictions, with CI performance reported as relative change in error versus DA.

## Results

On STARK-S (1.3k samples, direct answering), o3 achieves the lowest RMSE across nearly all Tier 1 and Tier 2 tasks: 0.036 on range localization (vs. 0.563 for the best LLM, Llama-4), 0.669 on range tracking (vs. 2.028 for Llama-4), and 0.000 on spatial and temporal relationship reasoning. Among LLMs, GPT-4.5 leads in localization (0.056 RMSE on range-bearing) and spatial relationships (0.040), while deepseek-chat excels in field variable prediction (0.002 RMSPE on S-impute) and range-bearing tracking (0.118 RMSE). On Tier 3 world-knowledge tasks, the LRM advantage narrows: o3-mini and o4-mini are outperformed by GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 on intent prediction and POI prediction, while full o3 maintains near-perfect scores (0.000 on landmark direction, POI prediction, route planning, and ETA). Using a Python code interpreter (CI) reduces localization error by ~80% for LLMs like GPT-4o and GPT-4.1, but degrades tracking performance and can increase o3-mini's error by up to +86%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LLMs and LRMs against first-principle baselines (multilateration, triangulation, EKF) on Tier 1 tasks and a random-guess baseline (0.5 accuracy) on Tier 2/3 tasks. For range-only localization, the multilateration baseline generally outperforms LLMs, but o3 matches or surpasses it (0.036 RMSE), while the baseline falters under poor sensor geometry due to Geometric Dilution of Precision. For tracking, classical EKF baselines outperform most LLMs, yet LRMs (especially o3) frequently surpass EKF in range and bearing tracking by discarding unreliable initial estimates and adapting to sensor dropout—something the fixed EKF recursion cannot do. On Tier 2 spatial/temporal relationship tasks, o3 and o3-mini achieve near-perfect accuracy (0.000 RMSE), far exceeding all LLMs (best: Llama-4 at 0.029 for spatial, 0.046 for temporal). On Tier 3 world-knowledge tasks, the performance gap narrows substantially: GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 achieve 0.000 on POI prediction and route planning, matching o3, while smaller LRMs (o3-mini, o4-mini) are outperformed by several LLMs on intent prediction and landmark proximity, suggesting that model scale is critical for LRMs to leverage world knowledge effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of spatiotemporal reasoning benchmarks (GeoQA, TemporalQA, STBench, Open3DVQA) by introducing a hierarchical three-tier structure grounded in classical spatial logic (DE-9IM) and temporal logic (Allen's interval algebra), while departing from prior work in three key ways: open-ended numeric answers instead of multiple choice, diverse sensor modalities (range, bearing, proximity, event-based) rather than coordinates or text alone, and explicit evaluation of code interpreter (CI) tool usage. For baselines, it draws on established first-principle methods—multilateration, triangulation, and SCAAT-style Extended Kalman Filters—positioning LLMs/LRMs against the classical signal-processing and estimation literature rather than against other language models. The world-knowledge tier (Tier 3) repurposes ArcGIS and OpenStreetMap services to create grounded navigation and intent-prediction tasks that no prior LLM benchmark has attempted in a sensor-fusion context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STARK provides the first structured, multi-tiered benchmark for evaluating LLMs and LRMs on CPS-relevant spatiotemporal reasoning, filling a gap that prior flat or single-modality benchmarks left open. The finding that the o3 LRM achieves 3×–10× lower error than LLMs in localization/tracking and can match or surpass EKF/multilateration baselines in certain configurations is practically significant for CPS designers considering LLM-based perception stacks. However, the paper reveals important caveats: smaller LRMs (o3-mini) underperform LLMs on world-knowledge tasks, and CI can degrade tracking performance by up to 86%, suggesting that guardrails and feedback mechanisms are still needed before LLMs can be reliably deployed as CPS agents. Open questions include whether RL fine-tuning can close the LLM–LRM gap for geometric reasoning and whether the benchmark's 10×10 grid and four-sensor setup generalize to larger, more complex deployments.
