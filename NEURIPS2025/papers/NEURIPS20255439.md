## Problem

Sliced optimal transport methods like min-SWGG provide explicit transport plans at low computational cost, but they are constrained to linear projections and require a number of slices that grows exponentially with ambient dimension. This limits their applicability to high-dimensional data and to non-linear structures such as data on manifolds, where linear projections lose critical geometric information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255439/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two empirical point clouds {x_i} in R^d (or on a Riemannian manifold such as the Poincaré ball) with arbitrary weights, and produces an admissible coupling matrix π_ε^θ ∈ U(a,b) along with an upper-bound Wasserstein cost. The projection map φ: R^d × R^q → R is parameterized by θ and can be a simple linear projection (q = d) or a multi-layer neural network; for the CFM application, inputs are batches of 32×32 CIFAR-10 images and Gaussian prior samples, and the output coupling is used to define straightened flow trajectories for few-step image generation.

## Method

The paper reformulates min-SWGG as a bilevel optimization problem where the outer level minimizes the true OT cost ⟨C_{μν}, π^θ⟩ over the projection parameter θ, and the inner level solves a 1D OT problem to obtain π^θ. To make the outer problem differentiable despite the piecewise-constant value function h(θ), the authors apply Stein's lemma with Gaussian perturbations: the smoothed objective h_ε(θ) = E_Z[h(θ + εZ)] is differentiable, and its gradient is estimated via a control-variate Monte-Carlo scheme ∇h_ε(θ) ≈ (1/εN) Σ_k (h(θ+εz_k) − h(θ)) z_k, requiring only N+1 1D OT solves per step at O(N(n+m)log(n+m)) cost. The generalized extension replaces linear projections with an arbitrary (potentially neural-network) map φ, and for hyperbolic manifolds uses horosphere projections with von Mises-Fisher perturbations and Riemannian gradient descent. The resulting smoothed plan π_ε^θ = E_Z[argmin g(π, θ+εZ)] remains an admissible coupling, guaranteeing an upper bound on the true Wasserstein distance while enabling end-to-end GPU-optimized training of the projector.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 image generation, DGSWP-CFM with a neural network projector achieves the best FID of 3.56 with 100-step Euler sampling, outperforming I-CFM (4.63) and OT-CFM (4.82). With the adaptive Dormand-Prince solver, DGSWP-CFM (NN) reaches FID 3.87 with 120.04 NFE, comparable to OT-CFM (3.86, 132.90 NFE) but with ~10% fewer function evaluations. In gradient flow experiments, DGSWP is the only slicing-based method that produces satisfactory transport plans in high-dimensional settings (d ≥ 2n), where min-SWGG and SWD baselines fail to converge. On the 8-Gaussians-to-Two-Moons toy problem, DGSWP's transport cost is notably closer to the true squared Wasserstein distance than min-SWGG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against min-SWGG [37], DGSWP consistently produces lower transport costs in the 8-Gaussians-to-Two-Moons example and is the only slicing method that converges in high-dimensional gradient flow experiments (Fig. 4), where min-SWGG's optimization-based variant still fails. In gradient flow, DGSWP outperforms both SWD [48] and Augmented Sliced Wasserstein Distance [12] across all tested target distributions and dimensions. On hyperbolic spaces, DGSWP converges faster than HHSW [9] and standard SWD on the Poincaré ball (Fig. 5). For image generation, DGSWP-CFM (NN) beats I-CFM [34] by 1.07 FID points and OT-CFM [54] by 1.26 FID points under 100-step Euler sampling; under the adaptive DoPri5 solver, I-CFM retains a slight FID edge (3.65 vs. 3.87) but DGSWP-CFM requires 13% fewer NFE (120.04 vs. 138.16), and OT-CFM is essentially tied on FID (3.86 vs. 3.87) while using 11% more NFE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of sliced-OT research: min-SWGG (Mahey et al., 2024), which lifts 1D plans to the original space but is restricted to linear projections, and Generalized Sliced Wasserstein (Kolouri et al., 2019), which allows non-linear projections but only computes distances without explicit transport plans. The key conceptual move is reformulating min-SWGG as a bilevel optimization problem and applying Stein's lemma to obtain a differentiable, GPU-friendly surrogate that supports arbitrary (including neural network) projection maps. The CFM application extends the OT-CFM framework (Tong et al., 2024) by replacing mini-batch OT with the proposed DGSWP plans, and the hyperbolic experiments build on horospherical sliced Wasserstein (Bonet et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first differentiable, scalable method for optimizing the projection direction in sliced OT plans, removing the exponential-in-dimension bottleneck of random slicing and the linearity constraint of min-SWGG. The demonstration that sliced OT plans can replace mini-batch OT in conditional flow matching is a notable first, suggesting that approximate couplings from efficient slicing can outperform exact but small-batch couplings in generative modeling. Open questions include: (i) characterizing injectivity conditions on the projection map φ to guarantee a valid distance, (ii) extending statistical guarantees (sample complexity) from SWD to the plan-based setting, and (iii) whether the CFM advantage persists on larger, more diverse image benchmarks beyond CIFAR-10.
