## Problem

NeRF reconstructions produce 'floater' artifacts—spurious density accumulations in regions unobserved by training cameras—that severely degrade novel view synthesis. Existing cleanup methods either require expensive learned 3D diffusion priors (Nerfbusters, ~20 min cleanup) or add per-frame inference overhead for uncertainty filtering (BayesRays, 2.5× slower rendering), leaving a gap for a fast, architecture-agnostic post-hoc cleanup that modifies the NeRF itself without extra memory or inference cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained NeRF (Nerfacto, 30k steps) and its original training camera poses, then fine-tunes the same network for 1,000 iterations using 2^17 randomly sampled 3D points plus the original training rays. The output is the modified NeRF density field with the same architecture and memory footprint, rendered via standard NeRF ray marching at 205 ms/frame—identical to the uncleaned baseline. No additional networks, uncertainty maps, or inference-time post-processing are introduced.

## Method

The core contribution is a Free Space Prior (FSP) loss that enforces a global zero-density assumption across the entire 3D volume, implemented by randomly sampling N=2^17 points in 3D space (not just along training rays) and applying a sigmoid-softened penalty: L_FSP = Σ(1/(1+e^{-σ(x_i)}))². The total optimization objective is L_rec + λ·L_FSP (λ=0.1), where the photometric reconstruction loss preserves scene structure along training rays while the FSP loss drives density to zero in unobserved regions. The critical design choice is sampling in full 3D space rather than along rays, which is what allows the prior to reach truly unseen regions that ray-based regularization cannot access. The interplay between the two losses naturally partitions the scene into three regimes: unseen regions receive only FSP (density → 0), empty seen regions receive both losses agreeing on zero, and surface regions receive competing signals that settle at a reduced-but-preserved density, yielding a bimodal density histogram without erasing scene geometry.

## Results

On the Nerfbusters benchmark, Free Space Prior (λ=0.1) achieves PSNR 17.60, SSIM 0.60, LPIPS 0.32, Coverage 89.88%, and Dice 0.88, outperforming the Nerfacto baseline (PSNR 16.44, Dice 0.83) and Nerfbusters (PSNR 17.01, Coverage 68.13%). In runtime, the method completes cleanup in 25 seconds (vs. 1200 s for Nerfbusters and 37 s for BayesRays) and renders at 205 ms/frame (vs. 506 ms for BayesRays), a 2.5× inference speedup. Geometrically, it matches BayesRays on Chamfer Distance (0.011) while achieving lower depth RMSE (3.80 vs. 3.91) and MAE (1.02 vs. 1.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Nerfbusters, Free Space Prior wins decisively on every metric: PSNR 17.60 vs. 17.01, Coverage 89.88% vs. 68.13%, and Dice 0.88 vs. 0.73, while being 48× faster in cleanup (25 s vs. 1200 s). Against BayesRays (T=0.9), the method achieves higher PSNR (17.60 vs. 17.49) and lower LPIPS (0.32 vs. 0.36), and is 2.5× faster at inference (205 ms vs. 506 ms) and 1.5× faster in cleanup (25 s vs. 37 s); BayesRays retains a slight edge on Coverage (93.23% vs. 89.88%) and Dice (0.90 vs. 0.88). Compared to the uncleaned Nerfacto baseline, Free Space Prior improves PSNR by +1.16 dB, SSIM by +0.07, and LPIPS by −0.13, confirming meaningful artifact removal. Against Gaussian Splatting and L2 regularization, the method leads on PSNR (17.60 vs. 15.13 and 17.10), SSIM, and LPIPS, demonstrating that simple weight-level or splat-level regularization is insufficient for floater removal. Overall, Free Space Prior achieves the best PSNR among all cleanup methods while maintaining competitive coverage, and is the fastest in both cleanup and inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NeRF formulation (Mildenhall et al., ECCV 2020) and positions itself within the NeRF artifact-removal line initiated by Nerfbusters (ICCV 2023) and BayesRays (CVPR 2024). It departs from both by replacing their complex data-driven 3D priors (diffusion models) or inference-time uncertainty filtering with a simple global MAP prior: a sigmoid-softened zero-density loss applied via random 3D sampling. Conceptually, it draws on the regularization literature (sparsity priors in image denoising, TV regularizers in NeRF variants) but applies the prior to the density output rather than network weights, and crucially samples across the full 3D volume rather than only along training rays.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practically significant efficiency win—25-second cleanup versus 20 minutes for Nerfbusters and 2.5× faster inference than BayesRays—while matching or exceeding their quality, making NeRF cleanup accessible without specialized hardware or additional model weights. Its architecture-agnostic design (validated across Nerfacto, Instant-NGP, LeRF, Seathru-NeRF, Open-NeRF) suggests broad adoptability. A key limitation is that it cannot correct artifacts stemming from noisy or inconsistent input images, only under-optimization in unseen regions. An open question is whether the 'empty unseen space' assumption can be relaxed for physically complex media (e.g., underwater scattering) without degrading cleanup quality, though the paper shows the method degrades gracefully in such cases.
