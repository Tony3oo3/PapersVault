## Problem

Consistency Models achieve one-step image generation by mapping PF-ODE trajectory points to the data endpoint, but their training critically depends on the discretization step Δt that selects adjacent trajectory points. Existing discretization strategies are manually designed, requiring empirical tuning for each noise schedule and dataset, and suboptimal choices create an imbalance between trainability (too-small Δt causes instability) and stability (too-large Δt makes the objective intractable). No unified framework existed to automatically and adaptively determine the optimal discretization step during training.

## Input / Output

The model takes a random Gaussian noise vector z ~ N(0, I) and, for conditional generation, a class label, and produces a single RGB image in one forward pass (1 NFE). For CIFAR-10 the output is 32×32; for ImageNet 64×64 it is 64×64; for ImageNet 512×512 the model operates in the SD-VAE latent space and decodes to 512×512. The training uses the Consistency Training (CT) paradigm, so no pretrained teacher trajectory is needed—adjacent points are constructed directly from the forward diffusion process x_t = α_t x_0 + β_t z.

## Method

ADCMs formulates discretization as a constrained optimization: minimize local consistency (the squared distance between f_θ⁻(x_t) and f_θ⁻(x_{t−Δt})) subject to a bound on global consistency (the denoising error ‖f_θ⁻(x_{t−Δt}) − x_0‖²). A Lagrange multiplier λ relaxes the constraint into a weighted sum, and the Gauss-Newton method yields a closed-form solution Δt* = [λ/(1+λ)] · E[vᵀ(f_θ⁻(x_t)−x_0)] / E[vᵀv], where v is the total time derivative of the network output computed via a Jacobian-vector product. This makes Δt* inversely proportional to the Jacobian magnitude (smaller steps where the network output changes rapidly) and proportional to the current denoising error (larger steps where the target is less reliable). The time segmentation T is re-solved from t=T down to t=ε every 25,000 gradient updates, and an adaptive weighting function w(t) = 1/L_global combined with a Pseudo-Huber distance metric further stabilizes training.

## Results

On unconditional CIFAR-10, ADCMs achieves a 1-step FID of 3.16 with only 12.8M training images, outperforming ECM (FID 3.60 at 51.2M images) and iCT (FID 2.83 at 409.6M images). At 76.8M images, ADCMs reaches a 1-step FID of 2.80, surpassing iCT's 2.83 which required 409.6M images. On class-conditional ImageNet 64×64, ADCMs achieves a 1-step FID of 3.49 with a 2× model at 12.8M images and 3.04 at 51.2M images, substantially reducing training overhead compared to prior CMs. On Flow Matching, ADCMs achieves FID 5.14 without manual adjustments, outperforming ECM (5.82) and sCT (88.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADCMs outperforms iCT [36] on both datasets (FID 2.80 vs 2.83 on CIFAR-10; 3.04 vs 4.02 on ImageNet 64×64) while using only 19% and 3% of the training budget, respectively. Against ECM [7], ADCMs achieves a substantially better CIFAR-10 FID (3.16 vs 4.54 at 12.8M images) but trails on ImageNet 64×64 (3.04 vs 2.49*), where ECM benefits from additional training costs indicated by the asterisk. Compared to sCT [23], ADCMs achieves a better CIFAR-10 FID (2.80 vs 2.85) but a worse ImageNet FID (3.04 vs 2.04*), again with sCT incurring extra training overhead. On Flow Matching (Table 4), ADCMs (FID 5.14) clearly beats ECM (5.82) and dramatically outperforms sCT (88.52), demonstrating strong adaptability without manual discretization tuning. On ImageNet 512×512 (Table 5), ADCMs with a 2× model achieves FID 10.53 at 6.4M images, improving over ECM (13.55) at the same budget and approaching sCT (5.84) which required 32× more data (204.8M images). Overall, ADCMs trades a small FID gap to the best CMs (which use extra training costs) for a 4–32× reduction in training budget, positioning it as the most training-efficient one-step CM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Consistency Model training framework (Song et al. 2023; Song & Dhariwal 2024; Geng et al. 2024) by reframing the discretization step selection — previously handled through manually designed schedules (iCT, ECM) or iterative PSNR-based solving (CCM) — as a constrained optimization problem. It draws on the JVP-based velocity computation from sCM (Lu & Song 2024) and unifies prior discretization strategies (DMs, discrete-CMs, continuous-CMs) as special cases of a single Lagrangian formulation. The key departure is replacing heuristic or iterative discretization with a closed-form Gauss-Newton solution that adaptively balances local consistency (trainability) and global consistency (stability) at each time step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADCMs reduce the training budget for competitive one-step generation by roughly 4× on CIFAR-10 (12.8M vs 51.2M images for ECM) and eliminate the need for manual discretization tuning across different noise schedules and datasets, which is a practical pain point for practitioners. The method's adaptability to Flow Matching without re-engineering the discretization schedule suggests broader applicability to emerging DM variants. Open questions include whether the approach extends to Consistency Distillation (where estimating L_global requires iterative PF-ODE solving, increasing cost), whether the single scalar λ can be made fully adaptive, and how the method behaves at much larger scales (e.g., video or 3D generation) where the JVP computation may become prohibitive.
