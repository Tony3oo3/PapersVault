## Problem

Existing text-to-image benchmarks remain confined to single-dimensional assessments (e.g., semantic alignment only, or text rendering only), failing to provide a unified framework that simultaneously evaluates reasoning-driven generation, stylization, multilingualism, and diversity. As state-of-the-art T2I models increasingly incorporate rich knowledge modeling and reasoning capabilities, no prior benchmark adequately measures these frontier abilities alongside traditional prompt-following metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255330/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark accepts 2,440 prompts (1,120 English + 1,320 Chinese) across six categories, with prompts structured into three length tiers (short <30 words, medium 30–60, long >60) at roughly a 1:2:1 ratio, and supports natural-language, tag-based, and phrase-based prompt formats. For stylization evaluation, curated reference images per style are additionally provided. Outputs are five composite scores per model: semantic alignment (0–1 via DSG-style question graphs), a text-rendering score combining edit distance, completion rate, and word accuracy, a knowledge-and-reasoning score, a style-similarity score, and a diversity score. Users can flexibly evaluate only a subset of dimensions by generating images solely for the relevant prompt category.

## Method

OneIG-Bench is constructed through a four-stage pipeline: (1) curating prompts from real-world user inputs and public datasets, (2) clustering and deduplicating via cosine similarity to cluster-center embeddings to balance category distribution, (3) rewriting prompts with GPT-4o under a Beta(2.37, 2.86) length-sampling scheme to enforce the 1:2:1 short/medium/long ratio, and (4) manual review for sensitivity and semantic conflicts. Evaluation metrics are dimension-specific: semantic alignment uses GPT-4o-generated question dependency graphs answered by Qwen2.5-VL-7B with conditional leaf-node validation; text rendering combines Levenshtein edit distance, completion rate, and word accuracy into a single composite score with a normalization constant φ; knowledge and reasoning uses GPT-4o textual answers scored against generated images via LLM2CLIP cosine similarity; style similarity employs a dual-encoder framework (CSD + a CLIP fine-tuned on CSGO-generated images) taking the maximum cosine similarity to reference images; and diversity is computed as the mean pairwise DreamSim dissimilarity within each prompt's generated set. The key design principle is modular decomposition—each dimension can be evaluated independently, enabling targeted diagnosis of model bottlenecks without requiring full-pipeline image generation.

## Results

On OneIG-Bench, no single model dominates all five evaluation dimensions. GPT-4o achieves the highest Reasoning score (0.345) and Style score (0.462), while Imagen4 leads in Alignment (0.857) and Seedream 3.0 leads in Text Rendering (0.865). Among open-source models, HiDream-I1-Full performs best overall with Alignment 0.829, Text 0.707, and Reasoning 0.317. Kolors 2.0 is highlighted as the best diversity performer (0.300) that does not compromise alignment (0.820), whereas Stable Diffusion 1.5's high diversity (0.429) is attributed to poor semantic alignment (0.565).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads in Reasoning (0.345 vs. Imagen4's 0.338 and Recraft V3's 0.323) and Style (0.462 vs. Seedream 3.0's 0.413), but has the lowest Diversity (0.151). Imagen4 achieves the best Alignment (0.857) and second-best Reasoning (0.338), while Seedream 3.0 dominates Text Rendering (0.865 vs. GPT-4o's 0.857 and Imagen4's 0.805) with a large gap in edit distance on short prompts (7.2 vs. 14.5 for HiDream). Among open-source models, HiDream-I1-Full is the strongest all-rounder (Alignment 0.829, Text 0.707, Reasoning 0.317), outperforming Flux.1-dev (0.786/0.523/0.253) and Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large (0.809/0.629/0.294). Kolors 2.0 stands out for combining strong Alignment (0.820) with the best Diversity among high-alignment models (0.300), whereas Stable Diffusion 1.5's top Diversity (0.429) is undermined by its poor Alignment (0.565). Unified multimodal models (BAGEL, Show-o2, OmniGen2) show competitive Alignment (0.77–0.82) but generally lag behind dedicated diffusion models in Text Rendering and Reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the T2I evaluation lineage from single-dimensional benchmarks (T2ICompBench, GenEval, DPG-Bench for alignment; WISE, WorldGenBench for reasoning) into a unified multi-dimensional framework. It directly builds on DSG's question-dependency-graph approach for semantic alignment scoring and on Gandikota & Bau's DreamSim-based pairwise similarity for diversity measurement, while introducing novel composite metrics for text rendering (ED/CR/WAC) and a dual-encoder (CSD + fine-tuned CLIP) style similarity protocol. The key departure from prior work is the simultaneous coverage of six evaluation axes—alignment, text rendering, reasoning, style, diversity, and multilingualism—within a single benchmark, rather than the fragmented, one-dimension-per-benchmark landscape that previously existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OneIG-Bench fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously covers text rendering, reasoning, style, and diversity, making cross-model comparisons on these axes impossible without stitching together incompatible evaluation protocols. The finding that even the strongest models (GPT-4o, Imagen4) score below 0.37 on knowledge-and-reasoning generation suggests this capability remains largely unsolved, pointing to a concrete research frontier. The authors acknowledge that their reasoning metric (GPT-4o answer + LLM2CLIP cosine similarity) is provisional and that aesthetic evaluation is underdeveloped, leaving open questions about more principled reasoning and perceptual-quality metrics. As a publicly released benchmark with 2,440 prompts and a leaderboard, it is positioned to become a standard reference for T2I model comparison.
