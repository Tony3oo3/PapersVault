## Problem

Training dexterous manipulation policies requires either expensive human demonstrations or hand-crafted, task-specific reward functions, both of which limit scalability and generalization. Prior trajectory-tracking approaches re-introduce the demonstration dependency they sought to avoid, since accurate reference trajectories must be collected for every new task or scene. The paper asks whether coarse, semantically informed motion plans from a VLM are sufficient to scaffold RL exploration without any human data or manual reward engineering.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB-D image (800×800), a natural language instruction, and the current wrist pose as input. The VLM first detects task-relevant 2D keypoints in the image, which are lifted to 3D world coordinates via depth back-projection; it then generates n coarse waypoints (n < T) for each keypoint and the wrist, which are linearly interpolated to horizon T. The low-level policy consumes proprioceptive state (wrist pose, finger joint positions/velocities), estimated 3D keypoint positions from a pose tracker, and the remaining plan steps τ_{t:T}, producing absolute finger joint targets and a residual wrist offset Δw at each control step (60 Hz).

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level hierarchy where an off-the-shelf VLM (Gemini 2.5 Flash Thinking) generates coarse 3D keypoint-and-wrist trajectory 'scaffolds' from a language command and scene image, and a low-level residual RL policy (3-layer MLP, PPO with 2048 parallel ManiSkill3 environments) learns to track those scaffolds. The VLM operates in three phases—semantic keypoint detection, spatial trajectory generation, and linear interpolation—and its plans are refined iteratively by feeding successful rollouts back as few-shot in-context examples. The low-level policy predicts only a residual wrist offset Δw (clipped) on top of the VLM's wrist trajectory plus absolute finger positions, with a dense reward combining exponential keypoint-tracking error and a contact-maintenance term, eliminating the need for task-specific reward design. A curriculum on the tracking-error termination threshold (linearly annealed to half its initial value) guides exploration from broad to precise. For sim-to-real transfer, the policy is trained with domain randomization on a digital twin and deployed zero-shot using FoundationPose for closed-loop keypoint estimation.

## Results

Across 8 simulated dexterous manipulation tasks (semantic understanding, unstructured motion, articulated objects, precise manipulation), the few-shot VLM-scaffold method achieves an average success rate of 72%, closely approaching the oracle with perfectly scripted plans. After three iterations of in-context refinement, the overall success rate improves to 81%. In real-world deployment on a 16-DoF Allegro hand mounted on a KUKA LBR iiwa 14 arm, the system achieves 90% success on Place Bottle onto Plate, 85% on Slide Box to Bottle, and 65% on Hammer Three Times, all without human demonstrations or handcrafted rewards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against an oracle baseline with manually defined keypoints and hard-coded trajectories, which represents the upper bound on performance. The few-shot VLM-scaffold method (72% average) closely approaches this oracle across all 8 tasks, demonstrating that VLM-generated coarse plans are nearly as effective as perfectly scripted ones. Against the IKER baseline [50], which uses VLMs to generate reward-function parameters rather than trajectories, the proposed method outperforms across tasks, as trajectory-based scaffolding provides denser and more structured guidance than scalar reward shaping. The pre-recorded trajectory baseline performs poorly on all tasks except Drawer (where the wide handle makes exact trajectory matching less critical), confirming that adaptability to novel initial conditions is essential. Ablations show that reducing waypoints from 20 to 3 degrades performance, but most tasks saturate by 10 waypoints, indicating that planning fidelity is rarely the bottleneck compared to low-level tracking (which accounts for 26% of failures). The most dramatic improvement from few-shot prompting occurs on the Fridge task, where the VLM commonly flips world-coordinate axes in zero-shot mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the trajectory-tracking framework for dexterous manipulation established by Chen et al. (CoRL 2024) and the residual RL formulation of Haldar et al. (RSS 2023) and Johannink et al. (ICRA 2019), where a low-level policy learns to track reference trajectories with dense, task-agnostic rewards. The key departure is replacing human teleoperated demonstrations as the source of reference trajectories with VLM-generated coarse 'scaffold' plans, shifting the burden from data collection to high-level spatial-semantic reasoning. This positions the work at the intersection of VLM-based planning (MOKA, REKEP) and dexterous RL, but unlike open-loop VLM planners, the VLM output here serves only as a training-time supervision signal for a closed-loop residual policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that neither human demonstrations nor handcrafted reward functions are necessary for dexterous manipulation, provided a VLM can supply sufficiently coarse motion plans — a practical simplification that could significantly lower the barrier to deploying dexterous hands on new tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond rigid-body interactions, how to close the loop between low-level controller capabilities and VLM planning, and whether faster VLMs can reduce the current 1–2 minute reasoning latency to enable real-time operation. The sim-to-real transfer on three physical tasks is encouraging but limited in scope, and the method's dependence on a specific proprietary VLM (Gemini 2.5 Flash Thinking) raises questions about robustness and accessibility.
