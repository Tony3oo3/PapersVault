## Problem

Existing hallucination detection benchmarks fail to capture the specific challenges of financial long-context QA: high numerical precision, domain-specific terminology, and the 'lost in the middle' degradation when relevant information is buried in documents exceeding 100,000 tokens. No prior benchmark simultaneously addresses hallucination detection, long-context evaluation (up to 30,000 tokens), and the financial domain, leaving a critical gap for evaluating LLM reliability in high-stakes financial applications.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample consists of a financial query, a context drawn from SEC filings (ranging from 500 to 30,000 tokens), and a candidate answer to be classified. The context is constructed by extending a validated 500-token seed chunk with contiguous text from the original filing, with the seed chunk positioned at the beginning, middle, or end of the extended context. The model outputs a binary hallucination label (hallucination or not hallucination) along with a structured reasoning trace that identifies specific unsupported or contradictory claims relative to the context.

## Method

PHANTOM is built via a two-stage pipeline: first, a seed dataset of 3,962 query-answer-context triplets is generated using Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct on 500-token chunks from four SEC filing types (10-K, 8-K, 497K, DEF 14A), with 38.4% human-validated (98.6% accuracy); second, each seed chunk is expanded to 2K–30K tokens by extracting contiguous surrounding text from the original filing, creating three positional variants (beginning, middle, end) while preserving the original query and answer. This design enables controlled, apples-to-apples comparison of model sensitivity to context length and information placement without additional human annotation. For the fine-tuning experiment, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is trained via LoRA (rank 64, alpha 128) on reasoning chains generated by Llama-3.3-70B with rejection sampling, using only 8-K and 497K data to test generalization to unseen 10-K and DEF 14A filing types.

## Results

On the PHANTOM seed dataset (500-token context), Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct achieves the highest F1 score of 0.916, though this is inflated as it was the model used to generate the dataset. Among unbiased models, Phi-4 (F1=0.885) and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking (F1=0.882) lead, both outperforming all closed-source models including GPT-4o (F1=0.867), Gemini-2.0-flash (F1=0.874), and o3-mini (F1=0.818). After fine-tuning Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on PHANTOM (trained on 8-K/497K, tested on 10-K/DEF-14A), the model reaches F1=0.933 on 10-K and F1=0.923 on DEF-14A, matching or exceeding Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. Across long contexts (up to 30K tokens), open-weight models show near-total recall collapse beyond 20K tokens, while closed-source models degrade more gracefully.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-weight models, Phi-4 (F1=0.885) and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking (F1=0.882) outperform all closed-source baselines, beating GPT-4o (0.867), Gemini-2.0-flash (0.874), Gemini-1.5-flash-002 (0.867), and o3-mini (0.818). Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct achieves the top F1 of 0.916 but is confounded by its role in dataset generation. The Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct baseline performs poorly (F1=0.708, near random), while the fine-tuned PHANTOM-Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct surges to F1=0.933 on 10-K and 0.923 on DEF-14A, matching Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct on 10-K (0.933 vs. 0.932) and exceeding it on DEF-14A (0.923 vs. 0.880). DeepSeek-R1-Distill models show a precision-recall trade-off: they improve precision over their base Qwen models (e.g., 0.895 vs. 0.816 for 14B) but sacrifice recall (0.824 vs. 0.938), resulting in lower overall F1. The Qwen3 'Thinking' variant outperforms its 'Instruct' counterpart by 3.4 F1 points (0.882 vs. 0.848), confirming the benefit of reasoning-focused tuning for hallucination detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to simultaneously address hallucination detection, long-context processing, and the financial domain in a single benchmark. Prior hallucination detection benchmarks (HaluEval, ARES, RAGEval) either focus on short contexts, general domains, or require extensive human labeling, while financial benchmarks (AlphaFin, FinMTEB, OmniEval) do not specifically target hallucination detection or long-context degradation. The methodology of expanding validated 500-token seed chunks using contiguous text from original SEC filings—rather than concatenating unrelated documents—repurposes the standard seed-and-expand data augmentation idea to preserve topical coherence and enable controlled positional experiments, departing from the synthetic concatenation approach used in prior long-context benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PHANTOM provides the first systematic quantification of how context length and information placement degrade hallucination detection specifically in financial QA, revealing that open-weight models suffer near-total recall collapse beyond 20k tokens while closed-source models degrade more gracefully—a gap with direct implications for deploying LLMs in regulatory and compliance settings. The demonstration that a 7B model fine-tuned on PHANTOM (trained on 8-K/497K) can match or exceed a 70B model on unseen filing types (10-K/DEF 14A) suggests domain-specific fine-tuning is a viable path to cost-effective hallucination detection. Key open questions include whether the synthetic hallucination patterns generalize to real-world LLM failures, whether the contiguous-text expansion adequately captures multi-document RAG scenarios, and how the benchmark extends to multimodal financial documents (charts, tables).
