## Problem

Existing targeted adversarial attacks on MLLMs align only global features (e.g., CLIP [CLS] tokens) between adversarial and target samples, ignoring the rich local patch-token representations that encode fine-grained spatial and semantic details. This neglect leads to weak local-level alignment, causing adversarial perturbations to overfit surrogate models and exhibit poor transferability—particularly to closed-source commercial MLLMs like GPT-4o and Claude.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251944/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean natural image (224×224×3) and a target image (from MSCOCO validation) as inputs, and produces a single adversarial image constrained to an ℓ∞ perturbation budget of 16/255. The adversarial image is evaluated by prompting an MLLM with 'Describe this image' and measuring caption similarity to the target image's caption via GPTScore (threshold 0.5 for success). Optimization uses an ensemble of three CLIP variants (ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, ViT-g-14-laion2B) as surrogates over 300 PGD iterations with step size 1/255 and random cropping augmentation.

## Method

FOA-Attack introduces a two-level feature optimal alignment loss: at the global level, a cosine-similarity loss aligns [CLS] tokens of the adversarial and target images; at the local level, patch-token features are first compressed via K-means clustering (n=3 or 5 centers) to reduce redundancy, then aligned through an optimal transport formulation solved with the Sinkhorn algorithm, yielding a local clustering OT loss. A dynamic ensemble weighting strategy adaptively balances the three surrogate CLIP encoders by monitoring each model's per-step loss reduction rate—models converging faster receive lower weights, preventing optimization from overemphasizing easily-aligned objectives. The total loss per encoder is L_coa + η·L_fin (η=0.2), and the ensemble loss is the weighted sum across models. A progressive strategy increases cluster centers from 3 to 5 upon attack failure, and the entire pipeline is built atop M-Attack's random-cropping PGD framework.

## Results

On closed-source MLLMs (threshold 0.5), FOA-Attack achieves 75.1% ASR and 0.59 AvgSim on GPT-4o, 77.3% ASR and 0.62 AvgSim on GPT-4.1, and 53.4% ASR and 0.50 AvgSim on Gemini-2.0, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline M-Attack (60.3%, 60.8%, 44.8% ASR respectively). On open-source MLLMs, FOA-Attack reaches 79.6% ASR on LLaVa-1.5-7B and 70.7% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, surpassing M-Attack by 11.3 and 18.1 percentage points. On reasoning-enhanced models, FOA-Attack achieves 81.0% ASR on GPT-o3, exceeding M-Attack's 67.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOA-Attack consistently outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated model. Against the strongest baseline M-Attack, FOA-Attack gains +14.8 pp ASR on GPT-4o (75.1% vs 60.3%), +16.5 pp on GPT-4.1 (77.3% vs 60.8%), and +8.6 pp on Gemini-2.0 (53.4% vs 44.8%), while also improving AvgSim by 0.09–0.09 across these models. Against AnyAttack, the gap is even more dramatic: 75.1% vs 8.2% ASR on GPT-4o and 53.4% vs 6.1% on Gemini-2.0. SSA-CWA and AdvDiffVLM perform poorly on closed-source models (ASR ≤ 2.5% on GPT-4o), while AttackVLM's best variant (Laion encoder) reaches 39.7% on GPT-4o but still trails FOA-Attack by 35.4 pp. On open-source models, FOA-Attack similarly dominates: 79.6% ASR on LLaVa-1.5-7B vs M-Attack's 68.3%, and 70.7% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B vs 52.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on M-Attack [33], which established the recipe of random cropping/resizing plus an ensemble of CLIP encoders with PGD-style optimization for targeted adversarial attacks on MLLMs. It departs from the entire line of prior targeted attacks (AttackVLM, SSA-CWA, AdvDiffVLM, AnyAttack) that align only the global [CLS] token by introducing a fine-grained local feature alignment objective: K-means clustering of patch tokens followed by an optimal-transport (Sinkhorn) matching loss. The dynamic ensemble weighting strategy further differentiates the work from prior ensemble attacks that assign uniform weights to all surrogate models, instead adapting weights based on per-model convergence speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results demonstrate that even the most commercially deployed and safety-aligned MLLMs (GPT-4o/4.1, Claude-3.5/3.7, Gemini-2.0) remain highly vulnerable to targeted adversarial perturbations, with ASR reaching 75–77% on GPT-4o/4.1. Notably, reasoning-enhanced variants (GPT-o3, Claude-3.7-thinking) show no meaningful robustness gain, suggesting that chain-of-thought alignment does not protect the shared visual encoder. Open questions include whether the OT-based local alignment generalizes beyond CLIP-family surrogates, whether the attack transfers to video or multi-turn settings, and whether any practical defense (beyond input transformations) can close the gap.
