## Problem

Single-image 3D human reconstruction remains ill-posed for in-the-wild photos due to three compounding challenges: varying body proportions (full-body, half-body, headshots), diverse personal belongings and loose clothing that parametric models like SMPL cannot capture, and the scarcity of high-quality multi-source human training data. Existing template-based methods accumulate errors from inaccurate pose/shape estimation, while template-free methods lack sufficient human-specific priors to produce consistent geometry and realistic textures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252759/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single in-the-wild RGB photo of a person at arbitrary framing (full-body, half-body, or headshot) and produces a textured 3D human mesh with a UV texture map. No parametric model (e.g., SMPL) is required as input; the only auxiliary signals extracted from the reference image are depth and normal maps via the Sapiens foundation model. The full optimization pipeline runs in approximately 1.4 hours on a single NVIDIA A100 80G GPU, producing a mesh at 512-resolution DMTet with 1024×1024 UV textures.

## Method

GeneMAN is a template-free, optimization-based pipeline built on two human-specific prior models finetuned on a multi-source dataset of 52K+ 3D instances and 100K 2D images: a 2D prior (finetuned Stable Diffusion V1.5) for texture realism and a 3D prior (finetuned Zero-1-to-3) for multi-view geometric consistency. Geometry is recovered in two stages: first, a template-free NeRF (Instant-NGP) is initialized via hybrid 2D/3D SDS losses plus reference-view RGB, mask, depth, and normal supervision; then the NeRF mesh is converted to DMTet at 512 resolution and sculpted with normal- and depth-adapted diffusion guidance (from HumanNorm) and an SDF regularization loss. Texture is refined in two spaces: a coarse UV map is optimized in latent space using the same hybrid SDS losses with a training-free multi-view consistency trick (identical Gaussian noise across concatenated views), followed by pixel-space refinement where SDEdit-based denoising with a ControlNet conditioned on the GeneMAN 2D prior generates refined multi-view images that supervise the UV map via MSE and LPIPS losses.

## Results

On in-the-wild images, GeneMAN achieves 32.24 PSNR, 0.013 LPIPS, and 0.730 CLIP-Sim, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., +5.3 dB PSNR over PIFu, +11.8 dB over SiTH). On the CAPE dataset, GeneMAN records 28.49 PSNR, 0.015 LPIPS, and 0.838 CLIP-Sim, leading in LPIPS and CLIP-Sim while trailing GTA (30.38) on PSNR. In the geometric evaluation on CAPE, GeneMAN achieves the best Chamfer Distance (1.87 cm), Point-to-Surface distance (1.95 cm), and Normal Consistency (0.0681) across all eight compared methods. A user study with 40 participants confirms that 73.08% prefer GeneMAN's reconstructions over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeneMAN dominates all four primary baselines (PIFu, GTA, TeCH, SiTH) on in-the-wild images across every metric, with the largest gaps over SiTH (+11.8 dB PSNR, 10× lower LPIPS) and GTA (+7.2 dB PSNR). On CAPE, GeneMAN wins on LPIPS (0.015 vs. 0.019 for GTA) and CLIP-Sim (0.838 vs. 0.826 for TeCH) but trails GTA by 1.9 dB on PSNR, likely because GTA's template-based SMPL fitting benefits from the controlled capture conditions. In the geometric evaluation (Table 4), GeneMAN outperforms all eight baselines including template-based methods (PaMIR, ICON, ECON, SIFU) on Chamfer Distance, Point-to-Surface distance, and Normal Consistency, with the closest competitor being SiTH (CD 1.92 vs. GeneMAN's 1.87). The paper notes that template-based methods in prior work used ground-truth SMPL parameters from CAPE, giving them an unfair advantage; when re-evaluated under inference mode without GT poses, their performance drops significantly, further widening GeneMAN's lead. Qualitatively, template-based methods (GTA, TeCH, SiTH) exhibit 'bent-leg' artifacts and inconsistent back-view textures, while GeneMAN's template-free design handles loose clothing, personal belongings, and varying body proportions (head-shot, half-body, full-body) more robustly.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeneMAN departs from the dominant template-based paradigm (SMPL/SMPL-X conditioned methods like PaMIR, ECON, GTA, SiTH, TeCH) by adopting a fully template-free reconstruction pipeline, while also going beyond prior template-free methods (PIFu, HumanLRM) that suffer from insufficient geometric and textural priors. Its core architectural lineage traces through the text-to-3D SDS framework (DreamFusion, Magic3D) and the view-conditioned diffusion prior from Zero-1-to-3, but it specializes both the 2D and 3D diffusion priors to the human domain via fine-tuning on a curated multi-source dataset. The two-stage geometry (NeRF initialization → DMTet sculpting) and two-stage texture (latent-space SDS → pixel-space ControlNet/SDEdit refinement) pipeline draws on components from HumanNorm, DreamGaussian, and the broader ThreeStudio ecosystem, but their specific combination for human reconstruction with part-aware camera sampling is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeneMAN addresses a practical gap: template-based methods fundamentally break down on in-the-wild portraits with varying body proportions (headshots, half-body), loose garments, and personal belongings, while prior template-free methods lack the priors to produce convincing geometry and consistent textures. The multi-source human dataset (50K+ multi-view instances spanning 3D scans, videos, 2D photos, and synthetic augmentations) is a reusable asset for the community. Key limitations include the ~1.4-hour per-sample optimization time (vs. feed-forward alternatives like IDOL or HumanLRM), reliance on Sapiens for depth/normal estimation, and the fact that the approach remains per-instance rather than amortized. Open questions include whether the human-specific priors can be distilled into a feed-forward model, how the method handles severe occlusions or extreme poses, and whether the multi-source dataset construction strategy generalizes to other object categories.
