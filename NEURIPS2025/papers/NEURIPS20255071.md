## Problem

Existing peer prediction mechanisms guarantee truthfulness only in expectation (Bayesian Nash equilibrium on expected scores), which assumes agents have linear utility in their scores. In practice, non-linear payment rules (threshold bonuses, letter grades) and inherently non-linear intrinsic utilities are common, breaking the equilibrium guarantee. This paper introduces stochastically dominant truthfulness (SD-truthfulness)—requiring the truth-telling score distribution to first-order stochastically dominate all deviations—and shows that no existing mechanism naturally satisfies this stronger criterion.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes two agents' discrete signal reports over n i.i.d. questions (signals drawn from a finite space Σ), the joint signal distribution Pr(X_a, X_b) (or its marginals), and for EA a target enforcement distribution Φ = (n_0, ..., n_{c-1}) specifying the desired empirical count of each signal. The output is a single numerical score per agent, structured as an average of Bernoulli or binomial random variables. The SD-truthfulness guarantee applies to the full score distribution (not just its mean), and sensitivity is measured as the derivative of expected score with respect to effort, normalized by score standard deviation.

## Method

The paper proposes two rounding reductions that convert any truthful mechanism into an SD-truthful one: direct rounding (normalize score to [0,1] and use as a Bernoulli lottery probability, which trivially satisfies SD-truthfulness but destroys sensitivity by a factor of √n) and partition rounding (split n questions into K disjoint subsets, apply direct rounding to each subset's score, and average the K rounded Bernoulli scores, recovering a √K sensitivity factor). The main contribution is the Enforced Agreement (EA) mechanism, which commits to a target empirical distribution Φ on Alice's reports and randomly flips excess reports to enforce Φ before scoring via Output Agreement. In the binary-signal setting with self-predicting signals, EA is provably SD-truthful for any Φ without any rounding step, because the enforcement eliminates the variance-reduction strategies (e.g., always reporting the majority signal) that break SD-truthfulness in OA. The paper further derives a closed-form sensitivity-maximizing Φ and shows empirically that even a prior-free uniform enforcement outperforms all other SD-truthful mechanisms in most binary-signal settings.

## Results

In the binary-signal setting, the Enforced Agreement (EA) mechanism achieves the highest sensitivity among all SD-truthful mechanisms across most prior distributions (Pr[X_a=0] ∈ [0.1, 0.9]) and task counts (n = 10 to 100), with the sole exception being a near-uniform prior where PTS-partition-round edges ahead. Notably, even EA-uniform—a prior-free implementation that simply enforces a uniform distribution—consistently outperforms PTS-partition-round, which requires knowledge of the signal prior. The sensitivity ratio between a mechanism and its direct rounding is √n (e.g., √100 = 10 for n=100 questions), while partition rounding recovers √K times the sensitivity of direct rounding, partially restoring the original mechanism's performance.

**Table 1: Comparison of SD-truthful peer prediction mechanisms, ordered by sensitivity (high to low).**

| Mechanism | Requirements for SD-truthfulness | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Output Agreement (OA) | Self-dominating signal | No rounding needed; highest sensitivity but strong assumption |
| Enforced Agreement (EA) [This paper] | Binary and self-predicting signal | No rounding needed; prior-free variant available |
| Peer Truth Serum (PTS) | Self-predicting signal | Requires partition rounding; sensitive to prior bias |
| Correlated Agreement (CA) | Any signal | Requires partition rounding; uses 2 questions per score |
| Matching Agreement (MA) | Any signal | Requires partition rounding; coincides with CA in binary setting |

## Baselines & Comparisons

EA outperforms all other SD-truthful mechanisms in the binary-signal setting except when the signal prior is nearly uniform (|Pr(X_a=0) − 0.5| < 0.03), where PTS-partition-round achieves comparable or slightly higher sensitivity. Against OA, EA matches its high sensitivity while relaxing the strong self-dominating assumption to the weaker self-predicting condition. Against PTS-partition-round, EA-uniform (which requires no prior knowledge) consistently wins once the prior deviates by more than 3% from uniform, and EA-prior/EA-optimal further widen the gap. Against CA-partition-round and MA-partition-round, EA dominates across all settings because these mechanisms require two questions per individual score (halving K to n/2) and use penalty questions that do not contribute to sensitivity, effectively reducing only 1/4 of questions to count toward sensitivity. In the non-binary (|Σ|=4) Weather Sentiment setting, EA is no longer SD-truthful, and PTS-partition-round emerges as the most sensitive SD-truthful mechanism, though all partition-rounded mechanisms show lower sensitivity than their original (non-SD-truthful) implementations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-task peer prediction literature (Dasgupta & Ghosh 2013, Faltings et al. 2017, Shnayder et al. 2016) by introducing stochastic dominance-truthfulness (SD-truthfulness) as a stronger incentive guarantee than the standard Bayesian Nash equilibrium truthfulness that assumes linear utility. The rounding-into-binary-lottery idea was previously proposed by Miller et al. (2005) for risk-averse agents, but this paper generalizes it to arbitrary monotone utility functions and introduces a partition rounding reduction that partially recovers the sensitivity lost by naive rounding. The novel Enforced Agreement (EA) mechanism builds directly on Output Agreement's scoring but adds a report-flipping enforcement step to eliminate the variance-manipulation strategies that break SD-truthfulness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that no existing peer prediction mechanism is naturally SD-truthful is a meaningful negative finding: it shows the standard BNE guarantee is fundamentally insufficient when agents face non-linear payment rules (threshold bonuses) or non-linear intrinsic utilities (letter grades), which are common in practice. The EA mechanism's prior-free variant (EA-uniform) outperforming prior-dependent mechanisms like PTS-partition-round is a practical win for deployment. Key open questions include extending SD-truthfulness to non-binary signal spaces (where EA is only truthful, not SD-truthful), exploring approximate SD-truthfulness for higher sensitivity, and designing mechanisms that remain incentive-compatible under tournament (rank-order) reward structures where score correlations matter.
