## Problem

Current online VFM-assisted 3D segmentation pipelines concatenate global point features across scans without instance-level temporal modeling, which exacerbates the over-segmentation and fragmentation inherent to VFMs like SAM. This absence of temporal reasoning means the model cannot maintain consistent object identities across frames or recover from partial visibility caused by viewpoint changes in embodied robotics, forcing reliance on brittle heuristic post-processing such as non-maximum suppression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251641/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming sequence of RGB-D frames processed online (one frame at a time), where each frame is first segmented by a frozen 2D VFM (SAM or FastSAM) to produce 2D masks that are lifted into 3D via recorded depth and camera intrinsics. The output is a set of 3D instance segmentation masks with temporally consistent identities, per-instance embeddings, and axis-aligned 3D bounding boxes, delivered at 0.7 FPS (SAM) or 10.1 FPS (FastSAM) on a single A100 GPU. The system maintains a bounded track bank of constant size, ensuring fixed computational overhead regardless of sequence length.

## Method

The paper recasts online 3D instance segmentation as continuous instance tracking with three synergistic modules. Long-Term Memory (LTM) maintains a bounded track bank and performs confidence-gated Hungarian assignment using fused appearance (embedding dot-product) and geometric (IoU of 3D boxes) affinities, with a fixed-capacity recall queue that reactivates stale tracks after prolonged occlusion. Short-Term Memory (STM) refines each instance embedding via distance-aware cross-frame attention that subtracts a per-query Euclidean centroid-distance penalty (scaled by learned receptive-field parameters) from the attention logits, suppressing noisy associations from spatially distant instances. Spatial Consistency Learning (SCL) addresses VFM over-segmentation through two complementary mechanisms: at inference, Learning-Based Mask Integration merges high-affinity fragments via hierarchical clustering and re-pools features; during training, Instance Consistency Mask Supervision applies one-to-many loss across a dual-branch decoder (one branch with self-attention for fragment selection, one without for cross-fragment robustness), yielding coherent queries that feed both LTM and STM.

## Results

On ScanNet200 (class-agnostic), AutoSeg3D achieves 45.5 AP / 66.7 AP50 / 81.0 AP25 with SAM and 46.2 AP / 67.9 AP50 / 81.7 AP25 with FastSAM, surpassing the prior best online method ESAM by 3.3 AP (SAM) and 2.8 AP (FastSAM) while maintaining comparable FPS (0.7 and 10.1 respectively). On ScanNet, the method reaches 43.4 AP / 62.5 AP50 / 77.4 AP25, outperforming ESAM by 1.8 AP. Zero-shot transfer to SceneNN and 3RScan yields consistent gains of 0.9–1.9 AP over ESAM across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct competitor is ESAM (ICLR'25), the prior state-of-the-art online VFM-assisted method. AutoSeg3D outperforms ESAM by 3.3 AP on ScanNet200 (45.5 vs. 42.2 with SAM) and by 2.8 AP with FastSAM (46.2 vs. 43.4), while matching its 0.7 FPS throughput. Against offline baselines, AutoSeg3D surpasses Open3DIS (34.6 AP) and SAI3D (28.2 AP) by a wide margin, and exceeds SAMPro3D (18.0 AP) by 27.5 AP. On ScanNet, the method beats the online baseline TD3D-MA (39.0 AP) by 4.4 AP and ESAM (41.6 AP) by 1.8 AP, though it remains below the offline Oneformer3D (59.3 AP) and TD3D (46.2 AP), which benefit from full-sequence access. In zero-shot transfer (ScanNet200→SceneNN/3RScan), AutoSeg3D consistently leads ESAM by 0.9–1.9 AP, confirming generalization beyond the training domain. The FastSAM variant (AutoSeg3D-E) achieves 10.1 FPS, slightly below ESAM-E's 10.6 FPS, while delivering 2.8 AP higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ESAM (ICLR 2025), the current state-of-the-art for online VFM-assisted 3D segmentation, which lifts SAM masks into 3D and uses a dual-layer decoder with auxiliary merging tasks but omits instance-level temporal modeling. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the long-term/short-term memory architecture from multi-object tracking (MOTR, TrackFormer) and video instance segmentation (VisTR), recasting each VFM-derived mask as a persistent track query rather than a per-frame detection. This reframing is motivated by complementary learning systems theory from neuroscience, where hippocampal (fast, episodic) and neocortical (slow, consolidated) memory are mapped to STM and LTM respectively. Spatial Consistency Learning further departs from prior heuristic post-hoc NMS by learning mask-merging affinities end-to-end with one-to-many fragment supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that identity-aware temporal reasoning—a component long standard in 2D tracking and video segmentation but absent from 3D VFM pipelines—yields consistent gains across four benchmarks, validating that over-segmentation from VFMs is better addressed through learned temporal association than through handcrafted post-processing. The practical relevance is high for embodied robotics, where real-time, streaming 3D perception with stable object identities is essential for navigation and manipulation. The acknowledged limitation that neither this nor prior methods model relative motion of dynamic objects leaves a clear gap for future work, particularly in scenes with pedestrians or mobile robots. The approach's reliance on SAM/FastSAM as a 2D front-end also means it inherits their failure modes on novel or heavily occluded objects.
