## Problem

Developing and validating intelligent schedulers for geo-distributed data centers is hampered by the absence of benchmarks that simultaneously model time-varying environmental signals (grid carbon intensity, electricity prices, weather), detailed DC physics (IT power, HVAC, thermal dynamics), and realistic network transmission costs and delays. Existing studies rely on abstracted physical models or optimize local controls in isolation, creating a gap between algorithmic theory and the operational reality of multi-objective, spatio-temporal scheduling across globally distributed sites.

## Input / Output

At each 15-minute timestep, the agent receives a variable-length observation of k_t per-task feature vectors, each concatenating 4 global time features, 5 task-specific features (CPU, GPU, memory, SLA, bandwidth), and 5×N per-DC state features (resource availability, carbon intensity, price, temperature, utilization). The action is a corresponding list of k_t discrete integers from {0..N}, where 0 means defer and j>0 assigns the task to datacenter j, incurring transmission penalties (cost, energy, carbon, delay) for remote placements. The LLM controller additionally emits a structured natural-language explanation citing specific input values (e.g., carbon intensity per DC, ambient temperature) to justify each routing decision.

## Method

DCcluster-Opt is a Gymnasium-compatible simulation environment that integrates the Alibaba GPU cluster trace (extended to one year) with real-world electricity prices, grid carbon intensity, and weather for 20+ global regions, coupled with a physics-informed DC model (CPU/GPU/memory power, CRAC/chiller/cooling-tower HVAC with EnergyPlus-derived curves, optional Heat Recovery Units) and empirical network delay/cost parameters. Baseline controllers include rule-based heuristics and RL agents (custom SAC with Geo+Time ablations, plus PPO/APPO/IMPALA via Ray RLlib) trained on a configurable multi-objective reward over cost, CO₂, energy, water, and SLA violations. The paper's novel agentic controller distills the trained SAC policy into an LLM through a three-stage pipeline: (1) executing the expert policy to collect state-action trajectories, (2) textualizing numerical states and actions into structured human-readable prompts, and (3) fine-tuning a base LLM on this corpus. The resulting LLM is wrapped in a six-agent LangGraph workflow (Sensor → Analyst → Planner → Validator → Executor → Monitor) that produces auditable, explainable scheduling decisions and generalizes to unseen cluster sizes (5 and 10 DCs) without retraining.

## Results

On a 5-DC, 30-day simulation (15-min timesteps, 10 seeds), the SAC (Geo+Time) agent achieves the lowest total operational cost ($92,401 ± $4,134) and lowest CO₂ emissions (308 ± 7.6 t) among all controllers, but at the expense of a 25.47% SLA violation rate due to 474 deferred tasks. Rule-based controllers (e.g., Round Robin, Lowest Price) maintain near-zero SLA violations (0.00–1.15%) but incur 2–15% higher costs. Adding an RL-controlled HVAC layer on top of SAC (Geo+Time) further reduces energy by 11.2% (to 921.3 MWh) and CO₂ by 11.5% (to 273.1 t), and a Heat Recovery Unit pushes CO₂ down to 268.9 t with 12.0% cost savings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Round Robin RBC [41], the SAC (Geo+Time) agent reduces total cost by ~8.4% ($92,401 vs. $100,896) and CO₂ by ~6.7% (308 t vs. 329 t), but trades off a 25.47% SLA violation rate versus Round Robin's 0.00%. The RLlib-based PPO [43], APPO [44], and IMPALA [45] agents (with default hyperparameters) all underperform the custom SAC: PPO incurs higher costs and emissions, exhibits a 29.6% SLA violation rate and >3,500 task deferrals, and shows lower resource utilization. The paper notes that achieving competitive performance with standard RLlib algorithms requires careful tuning of both hyperparameters and reward structure, highlighting the benchmark's ability to differentiate algorithmic choices. Overall, the SAC agent wins on cost and carbon but loses on SLA compliance to all RBCs, illustrating the inherent multi-objective trade-off the benchmark is designed to expose.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SustainDC benchmark (Naug et al., NeurIPS 2024) for its physics-informed data center model, extending it from local single-DC control to a geo-distributed multi-DC scheduling problem. It integrates thermal and energy modeling approaches from Sun et al. (2021) and Breen et al. (2010), and builds on the authors' prior RL-based carbon-aware DC control work (Sarkar et al., AAAI 2024; IEEE CASE 2023). The key departure from prior geo-distributed scheduling research (Convolbo et al. 2018, Hogade et al. 2022, Zhang et al. 2023) and sustainability benchmarks (Vessim, CloudSim) is the unified integration of real-world geo-temporal data streams, full-stack IT+HVAC physics, transmission-aware network dynamics, and a modular multi-objective reward system into a single Gymnasium-compatible environment specifically targeting AI/GPU workload scheduling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCcluster-Opt fills a critical gap by providing the first open-source, high-fidelity testbed that simultaneously models the full spatio-temporal complexity of sustainable geo-distributed AI scheduling, enabling fair comparison of diverse controllers under realistic conditions. The LLM distillation approach (Section 7) introduces an interpretable, auditable alternative to black-box RL for critical infrastructure control, though the evidence is limited to a 3-DC in-distribution setting with modest out-of-distribution validation. Key limitations include reliance on a single 2020 workload trace, a 15-minute timestep that may miss fast thermal dynamics, and the absence of reactive pricing models (e.g., LMP). Open questions include whether the LLM controller's apparent generalization to unseen cluster sizes holds under more rigorous evaluation, and how these findings transfer to production-scale systems with hundreds of DCs.
