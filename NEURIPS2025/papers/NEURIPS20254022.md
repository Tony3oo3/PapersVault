## Problem

Mamba's S6 unit, despite being derived from state-space architectures designed for long-range dependencies, consistently underperforms on long-range sequential benchmarks like LRA. The paper identifies three root causes through theoretical analysis: (i) shared B and C matrices across channels reduce effective width and expressiveness, (ii) the input-dependent sampling interval creates an overly aggressive inductive bias that can erase long-term memory, and (iii) the selection mechanism becomes increasingly difficult to optimize as sequence length grows, causing training instability.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of d-dimensional vectors (u_1, ..., u_L) where d = h × p is partitioned into h blocks of p dimensions each; each block is processed by an independent recurrent unit with its own selective dynamics. For LRA tasks, inputs are flattened pixel arrays (Image), path coordinate sequences (Pathfinder, Path-X up to length 16384), or text tokens; for language modeling, inputs are token sequences from SlimPajama. Outputs are d-dimensional vectors at each position, decoded into task predictions (classification, retrieval) or next-token distributions for perplexity computation.

## Method

B2S6 extends the S6 unit with two key modifications: (1) a block structure that partitions the d-dimensional input into h sub-vectors of size p, each governed by an independent recurrent unit with its own selection vector w, input matrix B_weight, output matrix C, and bias b, enabling selective behavior across multiple subspaces; and (2) a channel-specific, input-independent bias term B_bias^(i) added to the input matrix computation, which restores per-channel capacity lost in S6's shared-parameter design. The paper proves that S6 is not a universal approximator (Theorem 1) while B2S6 regains this property via either the block or bias component alone (Theorem 4), that S6 imposes an exponentially large inductive bias under large inputs (Theorem 2) while B2S6 exhibits only polynomial decay (Theorem 5), and that S6's training instability grows with sequence length (Theorem 3). Complex-valued parameters for A, B_weight, and B_bias are used, and reduced learning rates are applied to the selection parameters w and b to further stabilize training on long sequences.

## Results

On the Long-Range Arena (LRA) benchmark, B2S6 achieves an average accuracy of 87.71%, outperforming all selective SSM baselines (Mamba at 66.59%, Mamba2 at 70.54%, S7 at 71.82%) and non-selective baselines (S4 at 86.09%, S4D at 84.89%, S5 at 87.46%). B2S6 is the first selective SSM to reach state-of-the-art on LRA, with particular strength on Path-X (97.90%) and Pathfinder (95.93%). On language modeling (SlimPajama-6B, ~250M params), B2S6 achieves a final perplexity of 20.98, closely matching S6/Mamba (21.08) and Mamba2 (20.82), while S4D lags at 25.67.

## Baselines & Comparisons

B2S6 dramatically outperforms the original Mamba/S6 (66.59% avg) by 21.12 points, confirming that the shared B/C matrices and input-dependent dynamics severely limit long-range modeling. It also beats Mamba2 (70.54%) by 17.17 points and S7 (71.82%) by 15.89 points, both of which are other selective SSM variants. Against non-selective baselines, B2S6 edges out S5 (87.46%) by 0.25 points and S4 (86.09%) by 1.62 points, while surpassing S4D (84.89%) by 2.82 points. On the language modeling task (SlimPajama-6B), B2S6 matches S6/Mamba perplexity (20.98 vs. 21.08 at 28,830 steps) and is within 0.15 of Mamba2 (20.82), while S4D trails by ~4.7 points, demonstrating that the block-bias design preserves Mamba's language modeling strength without sacrificing long-range capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the S4/S4D line of state space models (Gu et al., 2022) and the selective S6/Mamba architecture (Gu & Dao, 2023), which introduced input-dependent dynamics to replace the fixed recurrence of S4D. The proposed B2S6 unit combines the multihead block structure previously suggested in Mamba2 (Dao & Gu, 2024) with a novel channel-specific, input-independent bias term in the B matrix. Conceptually, the paper departs from the prevailing narrative that input-dependent selectivity is strictly beneficial by proving that S6's shared B and C matrices across channels actually reduce expressiveness below S4D (a non-universal approximation result), and that the input-dependent sampling interval introduces an exponentially aggressive inductive bias and training instability that S4D does not suffer from.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical explanation for why Mamba underperforms on long-range sequence tasks despite being derived from architectures designed for long-range dependencies, and offers a simple architectural fix (block structure + channel bias) that restores and exceeds S4D/S4 performance on LRA while preserving language modeling quality. Open questions include whether the reduced learning rate on Δ-related parameters is the optimal stability fix, whether B2S6 scales to billion-parameter language models, and whether the theoretical results extend to the w≠0 case in the training stability analysis. The preliminary language modeling evaluation (250M params, one epoch) leaves open whether the 2.7% training overhead and slightly higher perplexity persist at scale.
