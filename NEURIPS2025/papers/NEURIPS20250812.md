## Problem

Establishing object-level correspondence between egocentric and exocentric views is critical for AR guidance and robot skill acquisition, but existing approaches struggle with extreme viewpoint variations, occlusions, and small objects. When SAM 2 is applied directly to this task, it fails because (1) it simply adds memory-aware and prompt features, ignoring the distribution gap between views, and (2) its FIFO memory bank discards long-term information, causing degradation on long videos.

## Input / Output

The method consumes temporally synchronized ego-exo video pairs resized to 480×480, along with a sequence of ground-truth object masks in one view (the query view), and produces per-frame segmentation masks for the same object in the other view. It supports two symmetric settings—Ego2Exo and Exo2Ego—and operates autoregressively with a fixed memory bank of 6 frames per view, processing 8 consecutive frames per training sample. Inference runs at approximately 8.4 FPS on a single V100 GPU.

## Method

LM-EEC builds on SAM 2 and introduces two key modules. First, a Memory-View Mixture-of-Experts (MV-MoE) module treats the memory-aware feature and the view-specific (prompt) feature as two complementary experts, applying a dual-branch routing mechanism—channel-wise weights from a two-layer MLP with global average pooling, and spatial-wise weights from a Conv-ReLU-Conv-Sigmoid branch—each applied via residual modulation before summing the two refined features. Second, a dual-memory bank system stores ego and exo features separately and applies a view-specific compression strategy: when the bank exceeds a threshold, it identifies the most temporally redundant feature point (minimum Euclidean distance between adjacent frames at each spatial location) and averages it with its neighbor, reducing redundancy while preserving long-term discriminative content. The full model is trained jointly from the pre-trained SAM 2 checkpoint for 60 epochs on 8 A100 GPUs, with memory compression applied only at inference time.

## Results

On the EgoExo4D test set, LM-EEC achieves an IoU of 54.98 (Ego2Exo) and 65.77 (Exo2Ego), substantially outperforming the best prior baselines (SimVOS at 38.26 and Cutie at 47.52, respectively). The method also attains the best Location Error (0.017 / 0.031) and Contour Accuracy (0.778 / 0.774) across both correspondence directions. In the Exo2Ego setting, LM-EEC trails Cutie on Balanced Accuracy (58.14 vs. 70.71), a limitation the authors attribute to the inherited difficulty of handling object disappearance from the SAM 2 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LM-EEC decisively outperforms all six baseline families on IoU, Location Error, and Contour Accuracy in both Ego2Exo and Exo2Ego settings. Against the strongest prior method SimVOS [40], LM-EEC gains +16.7 IoU (54.98 vs. 38.26) in Ego2Exo and +25.1 IoU (65.77 vs. 40.67) in Exo2Ego. The EgoExo4D-native baselines XSegTx and XView-XMem (+XSegTx) [1] trail by 20–52 IoU points, confirming that task-specific matching or XMem-style cross-view tracking is insufficient for this task. Among the general VOS backbones, Cutie [41] is the closest competitor in Exo2Ego (47.52 IoU) but still lags LM-EEC by 18.25 IoU; however, Cutie achieves a notably higher Balanced Accuracy (70.71 vs. 58.14), indicating it handles object disappearance better. STCN [37], QDMN [38], and GSFM [39] are all surpassed by 27–62 IoU points, with GSFM performing particularly poorly (3.98 IoU in Ego2Exo). The ablation in Appendix F further shows that the MV-MoE module transfers to STCN and QDMN backbones, adding +3.1 and +1.7 IoU respectively, underscoring its generality beyond the SAM 2 architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM 2's promptable video segmentation architecture, adapting it for the ego-exo correspondence task introduced by EgoExo4D. It departs from SAM 2's naive additive fusion of memory-aware and prompt features by introducing a lightweight Memory-View Mixture-of-Experts module that performs dense, feature-level expert routing along both channel and spatial dimensions. The dual-memory compression strategy extends the XMem-inspired memory bank concept from a single unified store to view-specific banks, replacing SAM 2's FIFO eviction with a simple point-wise averaging of the most temporally redundant adjacent frames. Unlike prior ego-exo methods (XSegTx, XView-XMem) that adapted older VOS architectures, this work leverages SAM 2's strong generalization as a starting point and focuses on fixing its specific failure modes in cross-view settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ego-exo correspondence is a foundational capability for AR coaching, robot skill acquisition, and multi-view scene understanding, yet SAM 2—the current state-of-the-art video segmentation model—degrades significantly on this task due to ineffective cross-view fusion and limited long-term memory. The paper's finding that a trivially simple memory compression (averaging the most similar adjacent frame pair) outperforms clustering and IoU-based selection strategies suggests that feature-level redundancy removal may be more effective than frame-level selection for long-video segmentation. A key open limitation is the model's inability to handle object disappearance (reflected in lower BA scores), which the authors acknowledge stems from SAM 2's lack of explicit presence/absence prediction—a gap that could be addressed with targeted augmentation or an auxiliary visibility branch.
