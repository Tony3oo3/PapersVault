## Problem

In-context RL (ICRL) faces two structural challenges that limit transformer-based decision models: the intrinsic multi-modality of state-action-reward sequences (where states are continuous, actions are high-frequency, and rewards are sparse scalars with large semantic gaps), and the heterogeneous task distribution that causes gradient conflicts during multi-task training. No prior ICRL method has leveraged mixture-of-experts architectures to address these bottlenecks from an architectural perspective.

## Input / Output

T2MIR-AD consumes a long autoregressive history of (s, a, r) transitions as a prompt and predicts the next action; T2MIR-DPT takes a query state paired with a sampled trajectory prompt and predicts the optimal action for that state. The multi-modal input sequence uses shared positional embeddings across the three modalities to preserve temporal consistency, and the output is a continuous action vector (via linear + Tanh) or discrete action distribution depending on the environment. At test time, prompts are built incrementally from online interactions without any parameter updates.

## Method

T2MIR replaces the feedforward layer in each transformer block with two parallel MoE layers whose outputs are concatenated before the next layer. The token-wise MoE routes individual hidden states to top-k experts via a learned router, automatically separating tokens by semantic modality; it is regularized with a combined importance and load-balancing loss (coefficient-of-variation penalties) to prevent expert collapse. The task-wise MoE routes entire sequences to experts at the task level using the mean hidden state as the routing signal, and is trained with an InfoNCE contrastive loss that maximizes a lower bound on mutual information between the task and its router representation, with MoCo-style momentum updates for key-router consistency. The framework is instantiated on both AD (causal transformer with Flash Attention) and DPT (supervised pretraining with optimal-action labels) backbones, trained end-to-end with the imitation loss plus the two auxiliary losses.

## Results

On the Mixed dataset across six environments, T2MIR-AD and T2MIR-DPT achieve the top two test returns in most settings. Headline numbers include T2MIR-DPT reaching -43.2±0.8 on Cheetah-Vel (vs. -119.2±30.4 for AD), 492.8±29.3 on Walker-Param (vs. 395.2±7.1 for AD), and T2MIR-AD reaching 823.9±7.1 on Reach (vs. 790.4±20.2 for AD) and 665.8±13.9 on Push (vs. 604.7±6.3 for AD). On DarkRoom, T2MIR-AD achieves 90.9±0.0, tying with DICP. The metric is episodic test return (higher is better; negative values indicate cost).

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2MIR consistently outperforms AD and DPT (its direct architectural backbones) across all six environments, with the largest gains on Cheetah-Vel where T2MIR-DPT improves from -56.1 (DPT) to -43.2 and T2MIR-AD from -119.2 (AD) to -68.9. Against DICP, T2MIR ties on DarkRoom (both 90.9) but clearly wins on Cheetah-Vel (-43.2 vs. -74.9), Walker-Param (492.8 vs. 403.7), and Push (665.8 vs. 560.0); the paper notes DICP's model-based planning is advantageous in the small DarkRoom state-action space but degrades in larger spaces like Reach and Push. T2MIR outperforms IDT on every environment, with the gap most pronounced on Cheetah-Vel (-43.2 vs. -82.8) and Push (665.8 vs. 620.6). UNICORN, the context-based offline meta-RL baseline, is outperformed on all environments where it is available, with particularly large margins on Cheetah-Vel (-43.2 vs. -80.6) and Push (665.8 vs. 547.4). Overall, the two T2MIR variants occupy the top two positions in 5 out of 6 environments, with the exception being DarkRoom where DICP matches T2MIR-AD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the first MoE-based architectural enhancement for in-context reinforcement learning, building directly on two mainstream ICRL backbones: Algorithm Distillation (AD, Laskin et al., ICLR 2023) and Decision Pretrained Transformer (DPT, Lee et al., NeurIPS 2023). The sparse top-k MoE design and load-balancing regularization follow Shazeer et al. (ICLR 2017), while the task-wise routing loss is derived from the InfoNCE mutual-information lower bound (van den Oord et al., 2018) with a MoCo-style momentum update (He et al., CVPR 2020). Unlike prior MoE-in-RL work that applies MoE to value functions or visual encoders, T2MIR targets the transformer's feedforward layer specifically to address the two ICRL-specific challenges of state-action-reward multi-modality and heterogeneous task distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

T2MIR offers a simple, drop-in architectural upgrade that consistently improves in-context learning speed and asymptotic performance across diverse environments, with the advantage being most pronounced in harder problems where baselines struggle. The ablation revealing that token-wise MoE dominates for long-context AD while task-wise MoE dominates for short-prompt DPT provides actionable guidance for practitioners choosing ICRL backbones. Key open questions include whether the scaling benefits of MoE (parameter growth with fixed compute) materialize at the dataset scale of XLand-MiniGrid or VLA tasks, and whether the contrastive routing loss remains effective when the number of tasks grows to thousands.
