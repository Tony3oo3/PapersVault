## Problem

Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) models predict Dirichlet distributions over class probabilities to quantify both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, yet they are trained with level-0 one-hot labels that implicitly assert full certainty. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that under standard empirical risk minimization with convex level-1 losses, the optimal level-2 prediction is always a Dirac measure, collapsing both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty to zero. This supervision mismatch between deterministic labels and distributional predictions remains unaddressed by existing EDL methods, which either ignore the issue or require expensive human-annotated soft labels.

## Input / Output

The model takes 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) as input and outputs a non-negative evidence vector via a ResNet-18 backbone with Softplus activation, which is converted to Dirichlet parameters α = e + 1. From these parameters, the method derives class probabilities (α/S), aleatoric uncertainty via conditional entropy (Eq. 64), and epistemic uncertainty via Dirichlet differential entropy (Eq. 67) or vacuity (K/S). During training, the supervision target is not the one-hot label but a vicinally interpolated level-1 categorical distribution, making the loss a level-2-to-level-1 comparison rather than the standard level-2-to-level-0 comparison.

## Method

The paper proposes a vicinal risk minimization (VRM) training paradigm for EDL that replaces hard labels with level-1 soft labels generated by strong Mixup (β ≫ 1, e.g., β = 10) between randomly paired training samples, producing continuous label distributions that encode aleatoric uncertainty. A second component augments the vicinal interpolation with Gaussian noise samples (x^(m) ~ N(0, σ²I)) whose labels are set to the uniform distribution, simulating epistemic uncertainty and preventing Dirichlet concentration from growing unboundedly. The total loss is the expected cross-entropy between Dirichlet-sampled probabilities and the vicinal soft labels, optimized jointly. Theoretically, the paper shows that strong mixing reduces the test risk lower bound by a factor of 1/(2β+1) + 1/2 < 1 (Theorem 2) and that the mixing parameter λ monotonically controls Dirichlet concentration growth, with smaller λ suppressing evidence accumulation (Theorem 3).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed method achieves 96.18% classification accuracy and the best OOD detection AUROC across all six OOD datasets (e.g., 97.32 on MNIST, 96.20 on SVHN, 91.81 on Tiny ImageNet). On CIFAR-100, it reaches 78.29% accuracy with top AUROC on all OOD sets (e.g., 86.17 on MNIST, 85.07 on SVHN). For selective classification on CIFAR-10-C, the method achieves a mean E-AURC of 29.40, substantially lower than the next-best I-EDL (47.92). On OOD generalization, it attains 88.73% mean accuracy on CIFAR-10-C and 61.35% on CIFAR-100-C, outperforming the strongest baseline H-EDL (73.85% and 49.59% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 15 baselines on every OOD detection AUROC metric for both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 as the in-distribution set. On CIFAR-10, the closest competitor is H-EDL (NeurIPS 2024), which achieves 92.07 AUROC on Places365 versus 91.61 for the proposed method (a narrow margin), but the proposed method leads by 1.72 points on MNIST (97.32 vs 95.60) and 3.21 points on SVHN (96.20 vs 92.99). On CIFAR-100, the gap widens substantially: the proposed method beats RED (ICML 2023) by 7.72 points on MNIST (86.17 vs 78.45) and 3.28 points on SVHN (85.07 vs 80.98). For selective classification, the method reduces mean E-AURC from 47.92 (I-EDL, the best baseline) to 29.40, a 38% relative improvement. On OOD generalization, the advantage is most dramatic: 88.73% mean accuracy on CIFAR-10-C versus 73.85% for H-EDL (a 14.9-point gap), and 61.35% on CIFAR-100-C versus 49.59% for H-EDL (a 11.8-point gap). The method also integrates additively with existing EDL variants (EDL, H-EDL) and a standard softmax classifier, consistently improving OOD accuracy by 13–15 points over their unmodified versions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) and Vicinal Risk Minimization (VRM). It builds directly on the Dirichlet-based EDL framework of Sensoy et al. [45] and the VRM/Mixup principle of Chapelle et al. [5] and Zhang et al. [51], but departs from standard EDL by replacing level-0 hard-label supervision with vicinally generated level-1 soft labels. This addresses a known limitation identified by Bengs et al. [2, 3] and Jürgens et al. [22]—that ERM with hard labels drives Dirichlet posteriors to collapse into Dirac deltas—without requiring the expensive human-annotated soft labels (e.g., CIFAR-10H) used by Javanmardi et al. [20]. The paper extends the theoretical analysis by showing that aleatoric uncertainty also vanishes under level-0 supervision (Theorem 1), not just epistemic uncertainty as previously established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a simple modification to the EDL training loss—replacing one-hot targets with Beta-mixed vicinal labels—yields large, consistent gains in OOD generalization (~15 points on CIFAR-10-C), selective classification (E-AURC roughly halved), and OOD detection, without any architectural changes or extra annotation. The theoretical results (Theorems 2–3) provide a clean explanation for why strong mixing reduces noise sensitivity and slows Dirichlet concentration growth. Key limitations include reliance on two manually tuned hyperparameters (β and β_noise), evaluation restricted to CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-18, and the open question of whether adaptive or data-driven strategies can replace the fixed Beta parameters. Scaling to larger datasets (ImageNet), deeper architectures, and non-image modalities remains untested.
