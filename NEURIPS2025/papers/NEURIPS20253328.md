## Problem

Existing membership inference attacks for large vision-language models rely on white-box or gray-box access to internal features such as token logits and likelihoods, which are unavailable in the dominant generation-as-a-service deployment paradigm. This paper addresses the open problem of detecting whether a visual sample was included in an LVLM's training set using only the model's generated textual outputs, a setting that is both practically critical and technically challenging due to the entanglement of memorization and generalization in large models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a suspected RGB image as input, which is first segmented (via SAM) and then transformed into masked or grayscale probe images paired with multiple-choice textual instructions. The target LVLM receives these probe images and instructions and returns only free-text answers; no logits, confidence scores, or internal representations are accessed. The final output is a scalar detection score (mean prediction accuracy across R repeated trials and N retained tasks) that is thresholded to produce a binary membership prediction.

## Method

KCMP constructs semantically confounding mask prediction tasks by segmenting objects with SAM and generating deceptive alternatives (K plausible objects or colors) via GPT-4o, so that the correct answer cannot be inferred from general world knowledge alone. A prior-knowledge calibration step then filters out tasks that a proxy LLM (GPT-o1) can solve through textual reasoning, using CLIP-based object-to-caption relevance and LLM-estimated rationality scores to rank and retain only the most memory-dependent probes. The target LVLM is prompted to answer the surviving tasks, and its prediction accuracy is averaged over R repetitions and N tasks to yield a detection score; samples with abnormally high scores are flagged as training members. The key design insight is that by making each probe individually infeasible from prior knowledge, high accuracy becomes a reliable signal of memorization rather than generalization, enabling effective inference in a strict black-box setting.

## Results

On VL-MIA/Flickr (K=10), KCMP achieves an average AUC of 0.695, substantially outperforming the only other black-box baseline Image Infer (0.514 Rouge / 0.509 MPNet) while approaching gray-box methods such as MaxRényi-K% (0.820) and Min-K% (0.812). On VL-MIA/DALL-E (K=10), KCMP reaches 0.673 average AUC, surpassing MaxRényi-K% (0.612) and Min-K% (0.584) among gray-box baselines. As K increases to 30, KCMP's average AUC rises to 0.823 (Flickr) and 0.795 (DALL-E), narrowing or closing the gap with the strongest gray-box methods. On the DAM-based benchmark, KCMP achieves 0.584 AUC, exceeding Image Infer (0.502/0.489) but trailing Min-K% (0.743) and Max-Prob-Gap (0.622).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KCMP is compared against six baselines spanning gray-box and black-box settings. Against the sole other black-box method, Image Infer [39] (which measures output stability via Rouge/MPNet similarity across repeated queries), KCMP wins decisively: on VL-MIA/Flickr at K=10, KCMP achieves 0.695 average AUC versus 0.514 (Rouge) and 0.509 (MPNet) for Image Infer, a gap of roughly 18 percentage points. Against gray-box likelihood-based methods, KCMP generally trails at small K but converges as K grows: at K=30 on Flickr, KCMP (0.823) approaches MaxRényi-K% (0.904) and Min-K% (0.927), while on DALL-E at K=30, KCMP (0.795) actually exceeds MaxRényi-K% (0.718) and Min-K% (0.619). On the DAM-based benchmark, KCMP (0.584) beats Image Infer (0.502/0.489) but falls short of Min-K% (0.743) and Max-Prob-Gap (0.622). The Perplexity and Aug-KL baselines [11, 44] show highly variable performance across models (e.g., Perplexity drops to 0.535 on DALL-E), whereas KCMP maintains more consistent gains across all three target LVLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to formulate and solve black-box membership inference for LVLMs, a setting where only generated text is accessible. Prior MIA work for LVLMs (Li et al. 2024, Hu et al. 2025) and for LLMs (Shi et al. 2024, Yeom et al. 2018) all rely on likelihood-based or logit-based features that are unavailable in generation-as-a-service deployments. Rather than building on a single prior method, KCMP repurposes existing tools—SAM for segmentation, CLIP for relevance scoring, and commercial LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-o1) for generating confounding alternatives and reasoning about knowledge leakage—into a novel three-stage probing pipeline that isolates memorization signals from generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: all major commercial LVLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude) operate as black-box APIs, making existing gray-box MIA methods inapplicable to the systems that pose the greatest real-world privacy risk. The finding that a text-output-only method can approach gray-box AUC (0.82 vs. 0.90 at K=30 on Flickr) suggests that internal model access is not strictly necessary for effective auditing. However, the closed-source results remain modest (~0.57 AUC), the method is limited to natural images with visually distinct objects (medical scans, documents are out of scope), and no defense mechanism is proposed—leaving open whether model providers can mitigate this attack through output perturbation or rate limiting.
