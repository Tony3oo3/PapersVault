## Problem

Existing few-shot federated learning methods for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) face a persistent tradeoff: they require training a large number of parameters, incurring substantial communication overhead and local computational costs, while still delivering suboptimal accuracy under non-IID user data distributions. No prior approach simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and drastically reduced communication/computation budgets in this setting.

## Input / Output

Each user provides a small set of labeled RGB images (capped at 16 per class, partitioned non-IID via a Dirichlet distribution with β=0.1) paired with textual prompts of the form 'a photo of a [class]'. The frozen CLIP text encoder processes the prompts while the vision encoder processes the images; the output is a top-1 class label evaluated against a global test set. The method operates on the standard CLIP ViT-B/32 architecture with 100 federated users, each holding a local copy of the model.

## Method

NormFit selectively fine-tunes only the Pre-LayerNorm parameters (scale and shift, ~1.5K total) at the input stage of CLIP's vision encoder, freezing the entire remainder of the vision encoder and the full text encoder. This minimal update rapidly realigns feature statistics with each user's local data distribution, reducing per-user communication to 4.1 KB and computation to 85 GFLOPs—orders of magnitude below competing methods. Theoretically, the paper proves that fine-tuning only the Pre-LayerNorm yields a generalization gap up to ~30× smaller than fine-tuning all LayerNorms (Theorem 1), and that the Pre-LayerNorm's position at the network's input grants it uniquely amplified influence on the global Lipschitz bound through both direct and cascading terms (Theorem 2). NormFit requires no architectural changes, no new external parameters, and no algorithmic modifications, and can be deployed standalone or layered on top of existing prompt/adapter tuning methods to further boost their accuracy.

## Results

NormFit achieves an average top-1 accuracy of 73.72% across 10 datasets in non-IID few-shot FL (β=0.1), outperforming the strongest FL baseline FLoRA (72.64%) and the standard VLM few-shot method TIP-Adapter (70.81%). The method uses only 1.5K trainable parameters (Pre-LayerNorm of the vision encoder), resulting in just 4.1 KB of per-user communication and 85 GFLOPs of computation—roughly 100× less communication and 10–100× less computation than competing methods. When integrated as an add-on, NormFit further boosts CoOp from 62.08% to 73.90% and TIP-Adapter from 70.81% to 73.27% average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NormFit outperforms all four FL-specific baselines on average: FLoRA (72.64%), FedTPG (70.37%), FedDAT (70.11%), and FedCLIP (69.33%), with the largest gap over FedCLIP (+4.39%) and the smallest over FLoRA (+1.08%). Against the standard VLM few-shot methods adopted in FL, NormFit surpasses TIP-Adapter (70.81%), CoCoOp (64.71%), and CoOp (62.08%) by 2.91, 9.01, and 11.64 percentage points respectively. The gains are most pronounced on fine-grained and texture datasets (Aircraft: +10.34% over FedCLIP; DTD: +1.87% over FLoRA) and on Food-101 (+4.61% over FLoRA), where minority-class representation is most challenging. In terms of efficiency, NormFit transmits only 4.1 KB per user per round versus 68–410 KB for baselines, and requires 85 GFLOPs versus 230–850K GFLOPs, making it 100× more communication-efficient and up to 10× more computation-efficient than the closest competitor FLoRA.

## Relation to Prior Work

NormFit carves new ground at the intersection of few-shot VLM fine-tuning and federated learning by identifying the Pre-LayerNorm of the vision encoder as the single optimal parameter subset to adapt, rather than following the dominant paradigm of adding external modules (adapters, prompts, LoRA) or fine-tuning large parameter subsets. It departs from prior FL-VLM methods (FedCLIP, FedTPG, FedDAT, FLoRA) that all introduce new trainable components, and from centralized few-shot methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, TIP-Adapter) that optimize prompts or adapter weights. The conceptual leap is treating a normalization layer—typically considered a fixed, uninformative component—as the primary and sufficient adaptation point, supported by a generalization-gap analysis showing that fewer trainable parameters yield provably tighter bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: NormFit reduces per-user communication from hundreds of KB to 4.1 KB and computation from ~10^5 GFLOPs to 85 GFLOPs while achieving SOTA accuracy across 10 datasets, making few-shot FL viable for bandwidth-limited and resource-constrained edge devices. The method's trivial implementation (no architecture changes, no new parameters, no algorithmic modifications) and its compatibility as an add-on to existing methods (CoOp+NormFit, TIP-Adapter+NormFit) lower the adoption barrier considerably. Open questions include whether the Pre-LayerNorm strategy generalizes to extreme domain shifts, whether per-user personalization of the norm parameters could further improve non-IID performance, and whether the approach remains competitive in high-data regimes where full fine-tuning becomes feasible.
