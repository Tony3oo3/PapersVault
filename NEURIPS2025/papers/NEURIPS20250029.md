## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting-based localization methods predominantly use 3D GS only for pose refinement, making them heavily dependent on accurate initial pose estimates that come from scene-dependent or feature-matching pipelines. When the initial pose is inaccurate or fails, the refinement process becomes ineffective. This paper addresses the gap by leveraging 3D GS for both initial pose estimation and refinement within a unified, scene-independent framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250029/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB image (resized to 256×256) and a pre-trained 3D Gaussian scene model as input, from which 4096 Gaussians are spatially uniform-sampled across a 0.1m grid. Each Gaussian contributes position, opacity, covariance, and spherical harmonic coefficients as features, supplemented by frozen DINOv2 embeddings and a depth estimation head. The output is a 6-DoF camera pose (rotation matrix and translation vector), obtained via PnP+RANSAC in both the initial and refinement stages, with the refinement network operating on 128×128 real and rendered views to produce a residual coordinate map.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage pipeline that fully exploits 3D GS. Stage 1 (GS-RelocNet) regresses a confidence matrix between image pixels and 3D Gaussians using a Swin Transformer image encoder and a Point Transformer V3 Gaussian encoder, connected by a unidirectional feature fusion module that lets 3D model features guide RGB descriptor learning without the reverse dependency—enabling pre-computation of model descriptors for faster inference. Stage 2 renders the view with 3D GS at the initial pose, then a refinement network with a bidirectional feature fusion module between real and rendered images predicts a residual coordinate map to solve the relative pose via PnP+RANSAC. Training uses a Gaussian-distribution-based confidence loss for GS-RelocNet and an L1 coordinate map loss plus an auxiliary pose loss (with learned balancing coefficients) for the refinement network, all under supervised learning with scene-independent transfer from ScanNet/MegaDepth to target scenes.

## Results

On 7 Scenes (scene-dependent), the full framework achieves a mean median position/rotation error of 0.71 cm / 0.23°, outperforming the best 3D GS baseline STDLoc (0.76 cm / 0.24°) and ACE+GS-CPR (0.8 cm / 0.25°). In the scene-independent setting, it reaches 1.4 cm / 0.82°, reducing position error by 46.2% over the previous best (Pixloc, 2.9 cm / 0.98°). On Cambridge Landmarks, the method achieves 7 cm / 0.16° mean error (scene-dependent) and 9 cm / 0.21° (scene-independent, trained on MegaDepth), surpassing DUSt3R (17 cm / 0.39°) and Reloc3R (48 cm / 0.58°). On dynamic TUM RGB-D and Bonn, the framework attains 1.4 cm and 4.3 cm mean RMSE of ATE respectively, outperforming all compared SLAM systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the scene-dependent 7 Scenes setting, the proposed method (0.71 cm / 0.23°) narrowly beats STDLoc (0.76 cm / 0.24°) and ACE+GS-CPR (0.8 cm / 0.25°), while substantially outperforming SCR methods like ACE (1.1 cm / 0.34°) and GLACE (1.2 cm / 0.36°). In the scene-independent 7 Scenes setting, the method achieves 1.4 cm / 0.82°, a 46.2% position-error reduction over Pixloc (2.9 cm / 0.98°) and a 76.7% reduction over DUSt3R (6 cm / 1.53°), establishing it as the first scene-independent 3D GS-based relocalization method. On Cambridge Landmarks, the scene-independent result (9 cm / 0.21°, trained on MegaDepth) surpasses DUSt3R (17 cm / 0.39°) by 47% in position and Reloc3R (48 cm / 0.58°) by 81%. On dynamic TUM RGB-D, the framework achieves 1.4 cm mean RMSE ATE, outperforming LC-CRF SLAM (2.5 cm) and DynaSLAM (2.0 cm); on Bonn, it reaches 4.3 cm versus 6.8 cm for LC-CRF SLAM and 23.8 cm for ReFusion. On 12 Scenes, the method attains 98.7% at the 2 cm / 2° threshold, exceeding Marepo+GS-CPR (90.9%) by 7.8 percentage points. The method runs at 65 ms/frame (15.4 FPS) on an RTX 4090, more than 2× faster than ACE+GS-CPR (190 ms) and STDLoc (143 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D GS) localization line by using 3D Gaussians for both initial pose estimation and refinement, whereas prior 3D GS methods (GS-CPR, STDLoc) only employ them for refinement and depend on accurate initial poses from separate SCR or APR pipelines. The initial stage draws on the SCR paradigm (ACE, GLACE, DUSt3R) but replaces point-cloud scene representations with 3D Gaussians, and the refinement stage adapts the residual coordinate map formulation from Wang & Qi (2023a). Architecturally, it combines Swin Transformer image encoders with Point Transformer V3 3D Gaussian encoders, linked by a novel unidirectional feature fusion module (model→image) that enables pre-computation of scene descriptors, and a bidirectional fusion module in the refinement network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first scene-independent 3D GS-based relocalization framework, it removes the need for scene-specific pre-training, making 3D GS localization applicable to unseen environments—a practical gap that prior 3D GS methods (GS-CPR, STDLoc) cannot fill. The 46% position-error reduction over the previous best scene-independent method (Pixloc) on 7 Scenes and the 2× speed advantage over competing 3D GS approaches make it a strong candidate for real-time AR/robotics deployment. Key open questions include robustness to environmental changes (lighting, weather), scalability to very large scenes (the paper sketches but does not fully validate a coarse-to-fine strategy), and dependence on high-quality 3D GS models, which remain a practical bottleneck.
