## Problem

Remote physiological sensing has advanced toward clinical precision for vital signs like HR and RR, but resources for higher-order states such as cognitive load remain scarce, especially across diverse remote modalities. No existing dataset combines more than two remote sensing modalities with contact-based ground truth for cognitive workload assessment, leaving a critical gap for non-intrusive cognitive monitoring in scenarios where contact sensors are impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250028/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes five remote modalities simultaneously: RGB stereo video (Zed2i), NIR video at 940 nm, two thermal videos (Boson 640, positioned above and below the face), and mm-wave radar (AWR6843ISK), alongside contact-based pulse oximeter PPG and BioHarness chest band ECG/respiratory signals as ground truth. Camera frames are downsized to 128×128, cardiac waveforms resampled to 30 Hz, and respiratory signals to 15 Hz; radar data is beamformed and Fourier-transformed along the fast-time axis before feeding a 1D CNN. The pipeline outputs PPG and respiratory waveforms, blink waveforms (via mediapipe eye-landmark tracking), derived vital signs (HR, HRV, RR, BR, BRV), and ultimately a binary cognitive load label (high/low) per 2-minute recording, binarized using participant-specific NASA-TLX median thresholds.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage pipeline: (1) remote vital sign estimation and (2) cognitive load classification from the estimated signals. For rPPG, a Siamese Contrast-Phys+ fusion network shares the first two convolutional blocks across RGB and NIR streams, with a pretraining strategy that trains RGB first, transfers weights to NIR, then initializes the fusion network from the NIR model to prevent RGB dominance; this achieves <3 BPM HR error. For respiration, a Siamese thermal-camera fusion model is combined with a 1D CNN on beamformed radar via late waveform fusion (concatenating the two output waveforms and processing with a 1D CNN). Cognitive load classification then operates on engineered features (HRV time/frequency/geometric indices, respiratory spectral features, blink rate and variability) extracted from the full 2-minute estimated waveforms, benchmarking ML models (RF, GB, SVM, LR, LDA, KNN, DT, MLP) against DL models (1D CNN, LSTM, ResNet1D). The key finding is that the full remote multimodal combination (rPPG + remote respiratory + blink markers) with Gradient Boosting reaches 86.49% accuracy, approaching the 87.5% contact-based upper bound, validating non-intrusive cognitive monitoring.

## Results

For remote HR estimation, the RGB+NIR fusion network (Siamese Contrast-Phys+) achieves 2.94 ± 0.27 BPM MAE, outperforming all unimodal baselines (best unimodal: Contrast-Phys+ RGB at 3.75 BPM). For respiratory rate, the waveform fusion (thermal cameras + radar) achieves 2.25 ± 0.17 RPM MAE. For the primary cognitive load classification task, Gradient Boosting with the full remote multimodal set (rPPG + Remote Respiratory + Blink Markers) achieves 86.49% accuracy (F1: 0.878), approaching the contact-based upper bound of 87.5% (MLP, F1: 0.884).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks six rPPG/RR estimation methods (PhysNet, RythmFormer, PhysFormer, FactorizePhys, PhysMamba, Contrast-Phys+) on the CogPhys dataset. For HR estimation, the proposed RGB+NIR fusion network (built on Contrast-Phys+) achieves 2.94 BPM MAE, beating the best unimodal method (Contrast-Phys+ RGB at 3.75 BPM) by ~0.8 BPM and PhysMamba (4.08 BPM) by ~1.1 BPM. For RR estimation, the waveform fusion (thermal + radar) achieves 2.25 RPM MAE, marginally outperforming the radar-only RF-Net (2.32 RPM) and the best unimodal thermal model (Contrast-Phys+ Below at 2.27 RPM). In cognitive load classification, the full remote multimodal pipeline (GB, 86.49%) nearly matches the contact-based upper bound (MLP, 87.5%), while unimodal remote PPG alone drops to 69.23% (RF), demonstrating that multimodal fusion is essential. DL models (1D CNN, LSTM, ResNet1D) consistently underperform ML models on this task, with the best DL result (1D CNN on Blink Markers at 68%) trailing the best ML result by ~18.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Contrast-Phys+ backbone (Sun et al., TPAMI 2024) for its Siamese RGB-NIR fusion network and adapts the rPPG-Toolbox (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023) for thermal-camera respiratory estimation, but its core contribution is a dataset and pipeline that bridges two previously separate research threads: remote vital sign estimation (rPPG, radar respiration) and cognitive workload assessment (traditionally via EEG or contact PPG/ECG). Prior remote-sensing datasets (UBFC, MMPD, iBVP, EquiPleth) target HR/RR ground truth but lack cognitive-state labels, while prior cognitive-load studies rely on contact-based biosignals or single-modality eye tracking. CogPhys departs from both by introducing five concurrent remote modalities (RGB stereo, NIR, two thermal cameras, mm-wave radar) alongside contact references and NASA-TLX-derived cognitive load labels, creating a two-stage pipeline from raw sensor data to abstract cognitive state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-parity between the best remote multimodal configuration (86.49% with rPPG + remote respiration + blink markers) and the contact-based upper bound (87.5%) provides the first empirical evidence that non-intrusive sensing can practically replace contact wearables for cognitive monitoring, with implications for driver monitoring, telemedicine, and at-home rehabilitation. Key limitations temper the impact: the study is confined to a controlled lab with 37 participants, uses only binary (high/low) load labels from four task types, and lacks direct neural signals (EEG/fNIRS) to disentangle cognitive load from stress. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to real-world, uncontrolled environments, whether multi-class or continuous load estimation is feasible, and whether the strong ML-over-DL result for feature-based classification reflects a dataset-size limitation or a genuine architectural gap.
