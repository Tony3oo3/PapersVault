## Problem

State-of-the-art protein generative models are trained on AlphaFold2-predicted structures, but low-confidence (low-pLDDT) predictions are typically filtered out, introducing a bias toward shorter, simpler folds and discarding valuable structural information from longer, more complex proteins. This filtering limits the model's ability to generate diverse, designable structures at biologically relevant lengths (700+ residues), where AF prediction quality degrades most severely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252197/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a target sequence length (300–800 residues for long-protein generation, 50–256 for short-protein) and optionally a fixed 3D motif for scaffolding tasks, and outputs a 3D protein backbone as a point cloud of SE(3) reference frames. Training consumes AlphaFold-predicted structures annotated with their average pLDDT score; the model is a 16.7M-parameter SE(3)-equivariant transformer (Genie architecture with 8 triangle layers) trained on ~290K geometrically reclustered AFDB representatives. At inference, 1000 diffusion steps with a tunable noise scale γ control the designability–diversity trade-off.

## Method

Ambient Protein Diffusion treats low-confidence AlphaFold structures as corrupted data rather than discarding them. The key insight is that adding Gaussian noise contracts the KL divergence between the AF-predicted distribution and the true structural distribution, so each protein can be assigned a minimum diffusion time (its 'merging time') below which its prediction errors are effectively erased. Concretely, the dataset is partitioned by average pLDDT into three quality regimes (high >90, medium 80–90, low 70–80), each protein is pre-noised to its assigned level during a one-time annotation step, and training uses a modified diffusion loss (generalizing Daras et al.'s ambient objective) that only evaluates each protein at timesteps at or above its merging time, with a critical EDM-derived rescaling weight to prevent vanishing gradients. Training samples the diffusion time first and then selects an eligible protein, ensuring balanced coverage across the noise trajectory. The approach is combined with a geometrically reclustered AFDB dataset (TM-Align threshold 0.5, coverage 0.75) that maximizes structural diversity independent of evolutionary relationships, yielding a 17M-parameter model that outperforms the 200M-parameter Proteina baseline in both diversity and designability on long proteins.

## Results

On long-protein generation (300–800 residues), Ambient Protein Diffusion (17M params) achieves 86% designability and 86% diversity at 700 residues, compared to Proteina's (200M params) 68% and 45% respectively — a 26-point gain in designability and a 91% relative gain in diversity. At 800 residues, Ambient maintains 68% on both metrics versus Proteina's 55% designability and 47% diversity. On short-protein generation (≤256 residues), Ambient dominates the designability–diversity Pareto frontier, reaching 98.6% designability with 0.781 diversity (γ=0.55) and 93.0% designability with 0.867 diversity (γ=0.65), surpassing all baselines including Proteina and Genie2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Proteina (200M params, flow-matching), Ambient Protein Diffusion (17M params, diffusion) wins decisively on diversity at every length ≥400 residues (e.g., 86 vs 45 at 700 residues; 68 vs 47 at 800) and on designability at 700 (86 vs 68) and 800 (68 vs 55), though Proteina edges ahead on designability at 300 residues (93 vs 90). Against Genie2 (the architectural base), Ambient wins on both metrics at all lengths ≥400, with the gap widening sharply for longer proteins (e.g., 87 vs 2 designability at 600 residues). Against Proteus, Ambient dominates on both metrics at every length tested. On short-protein generation (≤256 residues), Ambient establishes a new Pareto frontier, outperforming Proteina (FS γ=0.5: 91.4% designability, 0.71 diversity) and Genie2 (95.2%, 0.59) simultaneously on both axes. On TM-novelty (lower is better), Ambient achieves 0.773 PDB / 0.848 AFDB, beating Genie2 (0.789/0.862) by ~2% and Proteina (0.831/0.893) by ~7%/5%. On motif scaffolding, Ambient generates 1,923 unique single-motif scaffolds (vs Genie2's 1,445 and Proteina's 2,094) and 89 multi-motif structures across 5/6 tasks (vs Genie2's 40 across 4/6), matching Proteina's single-motif performance while being 12× smaller.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ambient diffusion framework (Daras et al. [14, 16, 18]), originally developed for image tasks with known corruption (Gaussian noise, masking), to the setting of arbitrary, unknown, non-parametric corruption processes in a scientific domain. It builds on the Genie2 SE(3)-equivariant architecture for protein backbone generation and adopts the EDM loss-weighting scheme [30] for stable training across heterogeneous noise levels. The key departure from prior protein generative models (Genie2, Proteina) is the rejection of the standard practice of filtering low-pLDDT AlphaFold structures; instead, each structure is assigned a corruption-aware diffusion time based on its pLDDT, and the diffusion objective is adjusted per-sample. Additionally, the paper re-derives the AFDB cluster dataset using FoldSeek hyperparameters optimized for geometric diversity rather than evolutionary similarity, producing a more balanced training set for generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 17M-parameter model trained on ~290K structures outperforms the 200M-parameter Proteina model trained on ~780K structures across designability, diversity, and novelty for long proteins, suggesting that better use of available data can substitute for brute-force scale. The ambient diffusion framework is generalizable to other scientific domains where training data is inherently noisy and the corruption mechanism is unknown. Key open questions include: (i) whether per-residue (rather than per-protein) quality-aware corruption handling could recover the pLDDT < 70 structures that are still discarded, (ii) whether the pLDDT-to-merging-time mapping can be learned rather than hand-tuned, and (iii) whether the generated structures hold up under experimental validation, which the authors acknowledge as a critical but unaddressed gap.
