## Problem

Despite the growing availability of open-source SFT datasets for LLM post-training, no systematic side-by-side comparison has been conducted to understand how dataset composition, sample quality, and task distribution affect downstream model performance. Existing post-training corpora lack transparent documentation of their curation processes, making it difficult to determine which data characteristics drive performance gains and how to optimally combine complementary datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253776/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw SFT samples from Tulu (0.94M pairs, 95% single-turn, STEM-heavy) and SmolTalk (~1.04M examples, 70% multi-turn, conversation-centric), annotating each sample via Magpie with Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct across dimensions including 12-class task category, 5-level input quality, instruct reward (0–5 discrete for multi-turn, continuous for single-turn), safety, language, and turn structure. The output is TuluTalk, a curated mixture of 808k samples—14% smaller than Tulu and 23% smaller than SmolTalk—alongside fully annotated versions of both source datasets, all publicly released for reproducibility.

## Method

The annotation pipeline extends Magpie in two ways: an error-tolerant JSON parser with in-context examples (reducing post-processing errors by up to 15%) and adapted prompts that ingest full multi-turn conversation histories rather than only the first user-assistant exchange, keeping annotation failure below 3%. Curation proceeds in two stages: a strict quality filter selects multi-turn samples with excellent input quality and maximum reward (5) plus single-turn samples with excellent input quality and above-median reward, followed by a task-aware augmentation that adds ~3k samples from underrepresented instruction-following categories (advice seeking, information seeking, creative writing, reasoning) with slightly relaxed thresholds (good input quality, above-third-quantile reward). This two-stage recipe was validated through ablations on 10% stratified subsamples before being applied to the full datasets, and the resulting TuluTalk is evaluated via SFT on Llama-3.1-8B, SmolLM2-1.7B, Qwen2.5-0.5B/3B, and SmolLM3-3B across 14 benchmarks, consistently outperforming both source datasets while using fewer samples.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B, TuluTalk achieves an overall average of 51.62% across 14 benchmarks, outperforming SmolTalk (51.38%), Tulu (50.32%), and Orca (47.72%). On SmolLM2-1.7B, TuluTalk reaches 35.89% overall, again leading SmolTalk (35.49%), Tulu (35.16%), and Orca (32.42%). TuluTalk contains 808k samples—14% fewer than Tulu (940k) and 23% fewer than SmolTalk (1.04M)—while matching or exceeding their performance. Notable individual gains include IF-Eval at 74.84% (Llama) and GSM8K at 74.84% (Llama), both the highest among all compared datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Tulu (Lambert et al., 2025), TuluTalk wins on overall average for both Llama (51.62% vs. 50.32%) and SmolLM (35.89% vs. 35.16%), with the largest margins on WinoGrande (+2.6% for Llama), HellaSwag (+2.1%), and IF-Eval (+0.75%). Tulu retains a slight edge on MATH (12.31% vs. 11.96%) and HumanEval (58.54% vs. 56.49%) for Llama. Against SmolTalk (Allal et al., 2025), TuluTalk achieves a narrow but consistent overall lead (51.62% vs. 51.38% for Llama; 35.89% vs. 35.49% for SmolLM), winning on WinoGrande, IF-Eval, GSM8K, and both Open LLM Leaderboards, while SmolTalk edges ahead on TruthfulQA (55.74% vs. 53.16%) and ARC-C (59.04% vs. 57.42%) for Llama. Against Orca/AgentInstruct (Mitra et al., 2024), TuluTalk dominates across every benchmark and both models, with overall margins of +3.9% (Llama) and +3.5% (SmolLM), confirming that the more recent Tulu and SmolTalk curation strategies substantially outperform the earlier Orca pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Magpie framework (Xu et al., ICLR 2025) for LLM-as-judge data annotation, extending it with error-tolerant parsing and multi-turn conversation handling to systematically label every sample in two major open SFT corpora (Tulu-3-SFT-Mix and SmolTalk). It departs from prior post-training dataset papers (Tulu, SmolTalk, Orca) that typically describe curation pipelines only at a high level, by making the annotation-driven curation process fully transparent and reproducible. The task-aware curation recipe extends simple quality-threshold filtering by explicitly restoring underrepresented task categories, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior work where mixture ratios were set without documented ablations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, annotation-driven comparison of two flagship open SFT datasets and demonstrates that a 14–23% smaller mixture (TuluTalk) can match or exceed both sources across 14 benchmarks and multiple model architectures, offering a concrete, reproducible recipe for quality-aware post-training data curation. The publicly released Magpie annotations for Tulu and SmolTalk create a reusable resource for the community to study data quality without re-running expensive annotation pipelines. Open questions include whether the curation recipe generalizes to other dataset pairs or to RL/DPO stages beyond SFT, and whether the modest per-benchmark gains (often <1%) would persist with larger training budgets or different base models. The single-run experimental protocol (no error bars) limits confidence in small differences.
