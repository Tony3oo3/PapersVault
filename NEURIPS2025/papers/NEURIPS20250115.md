## Problem

Existing video tokenizers operate under a Frame-Proportional Information Assumption, providing fixed temporal compression rates that cause the number of latent tokens—and thus diffusion model compute—to scale linearly with frame rate. This makes high-frame-rate video generation prohibitively expensive, even though the observable information in a video is bounded by its duration rather than its frame count.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250115/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VFRTok accepts video clips at any frame rate (12–120 FPS) at 256×256 resolution and 2/3-second duration, producing a fixed-length latent representation (512×32 for VFRTok-L or 128×128 for VFRTok-S) whose token count is independent of the input frame rate. The decoder can reconstruct or generate video at a different frame rate than the encoder, enabling native frame interpolation (e.g., 12→120 FPS) without retraining.

## Method

VFRTok is a query-based ViT autoencoder where fixed-length learnable latent tokens query variable-length spatio-temporal patch tokens (patch size 4×8×8) in the encoder, and the process is reversed in the decoder. Two key mechanisms enable duration-proportional compression: (1) timestamp-based 3D RoPE replaces frame-index-based positional encoding so that equal-duration videos at different frame rates share the same maximum rotation angle, and (2) asymmetric frame-rate training, where the encoder and decoder randomly process different frame rates of the same video, forcing the latent to encode continuous spatio-temporal information. Additionally, Partial RoPE applies rotary embeddings to only 50% of attention heads (position heads) while leaving the remaining heads (content heads) unrotated, decoupling positional priors from content modeling and improving generation fidelity.

## Results

VFRTok-L (512 tokens, 32-dim) achieves state-of-the-art generation FVD of 124.78 on K600 and 71.34 on UCF101 (with CFG), while requiring only 0.49 TFLOPs — just 8.4% of the compute of OmniTokenizer or Cosmos-L (5.82 TFLOPs). On the 60 FPS LAVIB benchmark, VFRTok-L achieves gFVD 148.68 with 512 tokens and 0.49 TFLOPs, compared to 375.47 (Omni) and 1552.04 (Cosmos-L) at 10,240 tokens and 22.67 TFLOPs. For reconstruction, VFRTok-L matches Cosmos-M (2048 tokens) in PSNR (31.63 vs 31.66) on K600 while using only 1/4 the latent capacity, and achieves substantially better LPIPS (0.0394 vs 0.0710) and rFVD (4.64 vs 6.77).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OmniTokenizer [33] (4096 tokens, 5.82 TFLOPs), VFRTok-L achieves 49% lower gFVD on K600 (124.78 vs 242.54) and 20% lower on UCF101 (71.34 vs 88.89) with 11.9× less compute. Against Cosmos-L [1] (4096 tokens, 5.82 TFLOPs), VFRTok-L reduces gFVD by 59% on K600 (124.78 vs 302.58) and 5% on UCF101 (71.34 vs 75.11) at the same 11.9× compute reduction. Against Cosmos-M [1] (2048 tokens, 2.37 TFLOPs), VFRTok-L matches gFVD on K600 (124.78 vs 125.02) while using 4× fewer tokens and 4.8× less compute. Against LTX-VAE [11] (128 tokens, 0.12 TFLOPs), VFRTok-S (same token count) dramatically outperforms on generation: gFVD 131.34 vs 358.48 on K600 and 129.55 vs 345.82 on UCF101, despite identical compute budget. On the 60 FPS LAVIB benchmark, VFRTok-L (512 tokens) achieves gFVD 148.68, beating Omni (375.47) by 60% and Cosmos-L (1552.04) by 90% while using 20× fewer tokens and 46× less compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

VFRTok extends the query-based visual tokenizer paradigm (TiTok, SoftVQ-VAE, MAETok) from images to video, replacing the grid-based 3D compression used by Cosmos, LTX-VAE, and OmniTokenizer with a fixed-length latent representation that decouples token count from frame rate. The key conceptual departure is the Duration-Proportional Information Assumption, which reframes video compression from a frame-count-dependent to a duration-dependent problem, grounded in interpolation error theory. The Partial RoPE mechanism is a targeted modification of 3D RoPE that addresses a diagnosed failure mode—positional priors dominating content modeling in attention—without altering the overall ViT architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1/8 token reduction with competitive reconstruction and SOTA generation quality directly addresses a core bottleneck in video diffusion: quadratic scaling of DiT compute with token count. The variable frame rate capability (12→120 FPS interpolation) opens a practical path for frame-rate-agnostic video generation pipelines. However, dense attention limits scalability to long videos, and the evaluation is confined to 256×256 resolution and 2–3 second clips, leaving open whether the Duration-Proportional assumption and the efficiency gains hold at higher resolutions and longer durations. The acknowledged limitation of segmenting into temporal slices with causal window attention is a natural but non-trivial next step.
