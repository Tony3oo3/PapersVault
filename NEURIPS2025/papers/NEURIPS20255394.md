## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for MLLMs select tokens purely by attention-based saliency, which concentrates on a small subset of dominant patches and discards semantically important contextual information. Moreover, the attention distribution is highly skewed—only a few tokens receive substantial attention while the rest are nearly flat—making it difficult to distinguish informative tokens from truly redundant ones. No prior method jointly optimizes both saliency and semantic coverage of the retained token set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255394/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the full set of visual tokens produced by a CLIP ViT vision encoder (576 tokens for LLaVA-1.5, 2,880 for LLaVA-Next, 2,048 for Video-LLaVA) together with a text prompt. It outputs a pruned subset of K tokens (K ≪ N) that replaces the full token sequence before the LLM's self-attention layers. The pruning is applied post-encoder and is entirely train-free; saliency scores are extracted from CLS-token attention at the second-to-last vision encoder layer, and pairwise cosine similarities among all tokens are precomputed.

## Method

SCOPE formulates token selection as a greedy submodular optimization that jointly maximizes saliency and semantic coverage. It first defines a set-coverage function f(S) as the sum, over all tokens u, of the maximum cosine similarity between u and any token in the selected set S. The marginal token-coverage gain Δ(v;S) = f(S∪{v}) − f(S) quantifies how much additional semantic coverage token v contributes. The SCOPE score multiplies this gain by the token's attention score raised to a scaling factor α (default 1.0): Δ(v, A_v^α; S) = Δ(v;S) · A_v^α. Tokens are then selected iteratively—each step picks the unselected token with the highest SCOPE score and updates the coverage scores—until the target budget K is reached. This greedy procedure ensures the retained tokens are both highly salient and broadly representative of the full semantic space, preserving completeness under aggressive compression (e.g., 9× token reduction with 96% performance retention on LLaVA-1.5 7B).

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 7B with 64 retained visual tokens (88.9% reduction from 576), SCOPE achieves 96.0% of the full-model average performance, outperforming VisionZip (93.5%), SparseVLM (85.1%), FastV (74.9%), and PDrop (73.5%). Under milder compression (192 tokens, 66.7% reduction), SCOPE reaches 99.5% relative accuracy, surpassing VisionZip by 1.5% and SparseVLM by 3.0%. On LLaVA-Next 7B with 160 tokens (94.4% reduction from 2,880), SCOPE retains 95.1% performance versus 92.5% for VisionZip and 86.9% for SparseVLM. On Video-LLaVA with only 136 tokens (93.3% reduction from 2,048), SCOPE achieves 100.5% of the original performance, nearly matching the unpruned model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCOPE consistently outperforms all five baselines across every token budget and model architecture tested. Against the strongest baseline VisionZip (CLS-attention-based pruning), SCOPE gains +2.5% average relative accuracy at 64 tokens on LLaVA-1.5 7B (96.0% vs. 93.5%) and +2.6% at 160 tokens on LLaVA-Next 7B (95.1% vs. 92.5%). Against SparseVLM (text-guided selection), the margin is larger: +10.9% at 64 tokens on LLaVA-1.5 and +8.2% at 160 tokens on LLaVA-Next. FastV (early-layer text-to-vision attention) and PDrop (pyramid redundancy reduction) show the largest gaps, with SCOPE exceeding them by 21–23% at 64 tokens on LLaVA-1.5. On the video benchmark (Video-LLaVA, 136 tokens), SCOPE achieves 100.5% of the unpruned model, beating VisionZip by 7.3% and SparseVLM by 14.0%. On Qwen2-VL at 10% token ratio, SCOPE surpasses DivPrune (diversity-based pruning) by 3.7% in average score (89.6% vs. 85.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the submodular set-cover framework (Iyer et al., 2021) to formalize a token-coverage gain, then departs from the dominant saliency-only pruning paradigm (FastV, SparseVLM, VisionZip, PDrop) that selects tokens purely by top-k attention scores. While DivPrune considers diversity among selected tokens, SCOPE explicitly couples per-token saliency (CLS attention) with a geometric coverage objective over the full token set, using an iterative greedy selection that balances both criteria. The method is positioned as a train-free, plug-and-drop module inserted after the vision encoder, inheriting the integration pattern established by prior pruning works on LLaVA-family models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for MLLM deployment: a 9× token reduction with ~96% performance retention (and 3.2× inference speedup) makes high-resolution and video MLLMs viable on resource-constrained hardware. However, the paper acknowledges that aggressive pruning may still discard fine-grained or rare semantic content, and generalizability beyond the LLaVA family (though partially validated on Qwen2-VL) remains an open question. The observation that pruning can *improve* performance over the full-token baseline on certain benchmarks (e.g., MMVet, POPE) suggests that redundant tokens introduce interference, opening a broader question about optimal token budgets that may be model- and task-dependent.
