## Problem

Existing contrastive learning methods for sequential recommendation suffer from two fundamental flaws in contrastive pair construction: random perturbations (masking, dropout) can completely alter a sequence's semantic meaning, and collaborative-signal-based clustering produces unreliable pairs under data sparsity. Moreover, these methods rely on rigid, predefined selection rules that prevent the model from autonomously learning which pairs are most informative, limiting the full potential of contrastive learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes user interaction sequences (item IDs, max length 20) along with item textual attributes (category, brand, description) that are processed by an LLM (DeepSeek-V3) to produce semantic embeddings via SimCSE-RoBERTa. These embeddings are computed once, cached, and frozen during training. At inference, only the standard transformer backbone is used to produce next-item scores over the full item vocabulary, yielding top-k recommendations with no additional LLM latency.

## Method

SRA-CL introduces a semantic retrieval-augmented contrastive learning framework with two retrieval mechanisms: (1) inter-user retrieval, where LLM-generated preference embeddings of user sequences are used to retrieve top-k semantically similar users as a candidate pool, and (2) intra-user retrieval, where LLM-generated context-aware item embeddings (incorporating both item attributes and co-occurring user sequences) retrieve top-k semantically similar items for substitution-based augmentation. A learnable sample synthesizer (attention over the candidate pool with a learnable adapter) dynamically constructs positive samples for inter-user contrastive learning, jointly optimized with the recommendation objective. For intra-user contrastive learning, 20% of items in a sequence are replaced with semantically similar items from the retrieved pool to produce two augmented views. The total training loss combines the standard next-item cross-entropy with weighted inter-user and intra-user contrastive losses, and the entire framework is plug-and-play across different backbone architectures.

## Results

SRA-CL achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four datasets, with HR@20 of 0.1282 (Yelp), 0.0823 (Sports), 0.1314 (Beauty), and 0.1702 (Office), and NDCG@20 of 0.0533, 0.0347, 0.0568, and 0.0725 respectively. The method outperforms the strongest baselines by up to 11.82% in HR@20 (on Sports) and 9.12% in NDCG@20 (on Sports), with improvements of 3.59%–9.29% in HR@20 and 5.07%–7.68% in NDCG@20 across all datasets. Results are confirmed by a paired t-test at significance level 0.01, with each experiment conducted five times.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRA-CL outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and metric. Among classical methods, BERT4Rec is the strongest (e.g., HR@20 = 0.0913 on Yelp), but SRA-CL surpasses it by 40.5% on Yelp HR@20. Among contrastive learning baselines, MCLRec and ICSRec are the closest competitors: on Sports, MCLRec achieves HR@20 = 0.0736 vs. SRA-CL's 0.0823 (+11.8%), and on Office, ICSRec achieves HR@20 = 0.1643 vs. SRA-CL's 0.1702 (+3.6%). The LLM-based baselines (LRD, RLMRec, LLM-ESR) generally underperform the strongest contrastive learning methods, with RLMRec being the best LLM baseline (HR@20 = 0.1125 on Yelp) yet still trailing SRA-CL by 14.1%. The paper attributes SRA-CL's advantage to its semantic retrieval mechanism, which produces higher-quality contrastive pairs than the random perturbations (CL4SRec, CoSeRec) or collaborative-signal-based clustering (ICLRec, ICSRec) used by existing methods, particularly in data-sparse settings where collaborative signals degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of contrastive learning for sequential recommendation (CL4SRec, CoSeRec, ICLRec, DuoRec, MCLRec, ICSRec) and LLM-based semantic understanding (LRD, RLMRec, LLM-ESR), but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than using random perturbations or collaborative-signal clustering to build contrastive pairs, it uses LLM-generated semantic embeddings as a retrieval mechanism to construct candidate pools, then applies a learnable synthesizer to select positive samples. Unlike LLM-based recommenders that require LLM inference at serving time, SRA-CL uses the LLM only as a one-time preprocessing step to produce frozen semantic embeddings, keeping the inference path identical to a standard ID-based sequential recommender. The learnable sample synthesizer (an attention-based weighted combination over retrieved candidates) is a novel component not present in prior contrastive sequential recommendation work, which typically relies on hard rules like same-cluster membership or shared next-item.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-identified weakness in contrastive sequential recommendation—low-quality positive pairs from random augmentation or sparse collaborative clustering—and shows that semantic retrieval from LLMs can substantially improve pair quality, especially in data-sparse regimes where collaborative signals are weakest. The plug-and-play, inference-free design makes the approach practically deployable, which is a meaningful advantage over LLM-in-the-loop recommenders. Open questions include: how the method behaves when item text attributes are unavailable or of low quality, whether the fixed top-k retrieval (k≈10) generalizes to much larger catalogs, and whether the one-time LLM embedding step remains valid as user preferences or item catalogs evolve over time.
