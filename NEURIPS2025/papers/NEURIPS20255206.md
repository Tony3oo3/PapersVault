## Problem

Existing camera location estimators in SfM (LUD, BATA, ShapeFit, FusedTA) rely on IRLS-type reweighting that does not fully exploit cycle-consistency information, making them vulnerable to cycle-consistent corruption where corrupted edges still satisfy geometric consistency. Additionally, these methods struggle with large variations in inter-camera distances: LUD-type objectives overweight long edges while BATA-type objectives underutilize clean long edges. The paper also targets the elimination of bundle adjustment, which is computationally expensive and ubiquitous in current global SfM pipelines.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes undistorted RGB images as input, extracts keypoint matches (SIFT or LoFTR), and computes pairwise essential matrices via RANSAC to obtain relative rotations and unit direction vectors. These define a viewing graph G([n], E) where each edge carries a noisy direction vector γ_ij and relative rotation R_ij. The method outputs absolute camera rotations {R_i} (up to a global rotation) and absolute camera locations {t_i} (up to global translation and scale), with no bundle adjustment step. The location solver operates purely on the direction vectors and the graph topology, requiring no inter-camera distance measurements.

## Method

Cycle-Sync introduces a Welsch-type robust loss ρ(x) = 1 − e^(−a|x|) that inherits L1's non-smoothness at the origin (enabling exact recovery under corruption) while suppressing the influence of large residuals (handling variable edge distances). The solver is an MPLS framework adapted for location estimation: since inter-camera distances are unavailable, cycle inconsistencies d_ijk are redefined using distances estimated in previous iterations, and edge weights are updated via a message-passing rule that aggregates 3-cycle information with a parameter λ_t → 1 so cycle-consistent evidence progressively dominates over individual residuals. Initialization uses a truncated AAB (T-AAB) scheme that estimates angular corruption from well-shaped triangles. The full pipeline also replaces REAPER with STE for robust direction estimation and uses MPLS-cycle (MPLS with λ_t fixed at 1) for rotation averaging, eliminating the need for bundle adjustment. The paper proves the strongest known deterministic exact-recovery guarantee under adversarial corruption, showing cycle consistency alone suffices for the lowest known sample complexity.

## Results

On ETH3D (13 scenes), Cycle-Sync achieves an average median translation error of 0.01, reducing error by 60.9% over LUD (0.04), 66.1% over BATA (0.05), 89.8% over ShapeFit (0.16), and 90.0% over FusedTA (0.16) under identical preprocessing. As a full pipeline without bundle adjustment, Cycle-Sync (median 0.01) outperforms GLOMAP+BA (0.30) and Theia+BA (0.18) on average. On synthetic data, Cycle-Sync achieves exact recovery (error < 10⁻⁴) up to 80% corruption, versus only 30% for most baselines, and remains robust under adversarial cycle-consistent corruption up to the theoretical limit of q < 0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cycle-Sync consistently outperforms all four location estimation baselines (LUD, BATA, ShapeFit, FusedTA) on 10 out of 13 ETH3D scenes under identical STE + MPLS-cycle preprocessing, with the largest margins against ShapeFit and FusedTA (which fail catastrophically on facade and relief_2 with errors > 0.8). Against the full SfM pipelines, Cycle-Sync (without bundle adjustment) achieves a lower average median error than both GLOMAP+BA (0.01 vs 0.30) and Theia+BA (0.01 vs 0.18), though Theia wins on the majority of individual scenes (e.g., courtyard, delivery_area, electro) while Cycle-Sync avoids the large failure cases that inflate GLOMAP's and Theia's averages. On the IMC-PT dataset (9 city-scale scenes with LoFTR matches), Cycle-Sync achieves the best average median error (0.29) but with smaller margins over baselines (0.30–0.31), which the authors attribute to LoFTR's tendency to overfit repetitive architectural structures. In synthetic experiments, Cycle-Sync extends the exact-recovery phase transition from q ≤ 0.3 (most baselines) to q ≤ 0.8 under uniform corruption, and remains accurate under adversarial cycle-consistent corruption where all baselines rapidly deteriorate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Message-Passing Least Squares (MPLS), originally developed for rotation synchronization over SO(3), to the fundamentally different problem of camera location estimation, which lacks group structure and inter-camera distance measurements. It generalizes the LUD/ShapeFit/BATA optimization family by introducing a Welsch-type loss that retains L1's non-smoothness at the origin (enabling exact recovery) while suppressing the influence of long edges. The initialization leverages a truncated variant of AAB's cycle-consistency measure, and a plug-and-play outlier rejection module based on the subspace-constrained Tyler's estimator (STE) is integrated as a preprocessing step. The key conceptual departure from prior IRLS-based methods is the full integration of cycle-consistent message passing into the reweighting loop, rather than treating cycles only as a heuristic for outlier detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating bundle adjustment while outperforming full SfM pipelines that rely on it, Cycle-Sync offers a simpler, more robust global pose estimation pipeline that could reduce computational cost and failure modes in downstream applications (NeRF, Gaussian splatting, robotics). The strongest known deterministic exact-recovery guarantee under adversarial corruption, with improved sample complexity over all prior work, provides a theoretical foundation that previous methods lacked. However, the guarantee covers only the initialization phase rather than full convergence of the nonconvex optimization, and performance may degrade on sparse graphs lacking well-shaped 3-cycles. Open questions include extending guarantees to the full iterative procedure, developing adaptive reweighting schedules, and validating on larger-scale or more diverse real-world datasets.
