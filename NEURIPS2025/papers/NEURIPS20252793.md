## Problem

Despite being pretrained on datasets 100× larger than domain-specific benchmarks, 7B+ LVLMs consistently underperform much smaller feature-based models (e.g., 9M parameters) on Temporal Video Grounding. The paper identifies the root cause as over-penalization of false negatives during supervised fine-tuning: autoregressive loss imposes disproportionately high penalties on reasonable timestamp predictions (e.g., [1.9s, 3.9s] vs. ground truth [2s, 4s]), leading to overfitting and poor generalization. The paper asks whether reinforcement learning with verifiable task-specific rewards can replace SFT as a more effective post-training paradigm for TVG.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of video frames sampled at 2 FPS (adaptively resized to ~2.8M total pixels, e.g., 100 frames at ~96×96×3 for a 50-second video) and a natural language query as input. It produces a structured two-part output: a chain-of-thought reasoning block containing timestamped video segment captions (e.g., `<t_s1 to t_e1: C_1; t_s2 to t_e2: C_2>`), followed by a final answer block with the predicted temporal boundaries in the format `<answer>t_s to t_e</answer>`. The visual encoder is frozen during training; only the LLM parameters are updated.

## Method

Time-R1 post-trains LVLMs via GRPO (using DAPO's token-level loss) with a composite verifiable reward: a timestamp-aware IoU (tIoU) that multiplies standard IoU by penalties on start/end timestamp deviations relative to video duration, plus a binary format reward enforcing the thinking-then-answer template. The key insight is that RL directly optimizes the task metric (IoU) rather than token-level cross-entropy, avoiding SFT's rigid penalties on near-correct predictions. TimeRFT further improves data efficiency by selecting only 2.5K moderately difficult samples (Gaussian-filtered around IoU 0.3) from 339K candidates, training over multiple epochs with dynamic hard sampling that removes samples exceeding IoU 0.7 each epoch, and a cold-start SFT phase with 150 CoT-caption examples to stabilize early reasoning. This framework is model-agnostic, yielding consistent gains across Qwen2.5-VL, MiMo-VL, and InternVL3 at 2B–8B scales.

## Results

In zero-shot settings, Time-R1 (trained on only 2.5K RL samples) achieves R1@0.3 of 78.1 on Charades-STA, 58.6 on ActivityNet, and 41.8 on TVGBench, surpassing all prior SFT-based LVLMs and the closed-source Gemini-2.5-Pro (39.1 on TVGBench). When fine-tuned on downstream benchmarks, Time-R1* reaches R1@0.7 of 50.1 on Charades-STA and R1@0.5 of 55.6 on ActivityNet, outperforming both SFT-based LVLMs (e.g., TimeSuite*: 43.0 R1@0.7) and feature-based VLP models (e.g., EaTR*: 44.9 R1@0.7). The framework also improves general video QA: on VideoMME, RL boosts accuracy from 53.0 to 54.2, while SFT-LoRA degrades it to 51.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In zero-shot TVG, Time-R1 (2.5K RL samples) outperforms all SFT-based LVLMs: on Charades-STA it leads VideoChat-Flash (74.5→78.1 R1@0.3) and VideoMind (73.5→78.1) by 3.6–8.2 points, and on ActivityNet it surpasses VideoMind (48.4→58.6 R1@0.3) by 10.2 points. On the proposed TVGBench, Time-R1 (41.8 R1@0.3) beats both TRACE (37.0) and the closed-source Gemini-2.5-Pro (39.1). After fine-tuning, Time-R1* achieves 50.1 R1@0.7 on Charades-STA, exceeding TimeSuite* (43.0) by 7.1 points and VideoChat-TPO* (40.7) by 9.4 points, while using far fewer training samples (2.5K vs. 349K for TimeSuite*). On ActivityNet, Time-R1* (55.6 R1@0.5) surpasses the best VLP model SSRN* (54.5) and EaTR* (37.6 R1@0.7 vs. 34.0). The paper also shows RL preserves general video QA ability (VideoMME: 54.2 vs. 51.7 for SFT-LoRA), whereas SFT degrades it, confirming RL's superior generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward) paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and DAPO for LLM post-training to the domain of temporal video grounding in LVLMs. Prior TVG work falls into two camps: feature-based video-language pretraining (VLP) methods that regress timestamps from fused encoder features, and SFT-based LVLMs that autoregressively generate timestamp tokens. The key conceptual departure is the argument that SFT's autoregressive loss over-penalizes near-correct predictions (e.g., [1.9s, 3.9s] vs. ground truth [2s, 4s]), whereas RL with a tIoU-based verifiable reward directly optimizes the task metric and tolerates reasonable temporal variation. The paper also introduces TVGBench, a balanced 800-sample evaluation set, to address the mismatch between existing large-scale TVG benchmarks (designed for small models) and the slower inference of LVLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 2.5K RL samples outperform 339K SFT samples on TVG while simultaneously preserving (and slightly improving) general video QA performance challenges the prevailing assumption that large-scale supervised data is necessary for LVLM specialization. This has practical implications for practitioners who want to adapt LVLMs to temporal tasks without the compute cost of massive SFT or the generalization degradation it causes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to ultra-long videos (a stated limitation), whether the data-efficiency gap holds as model scale increases beyond 8B, and whether RLVR can be applied to other long-form video tasks such as dense captioning or multi-event localization.
