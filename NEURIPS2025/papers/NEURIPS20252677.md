## Problem

Sparse IMU-based motion capture estimates joint positions through double integration of acceleration, causing quadratic error accumulation from drift and sensor noise that is especially severe during slow, low-velocity motions where the motion-signal-to-noise ratio is minimal. Existing software-only solutions (RNNs, attention models) can only mitigate but not eliminate this fundamental limitation of indirect measurement. The paper addresses this gap by introducing direct distance measurements from body-mounted Time-of-Flight sensors as learnable positional constraints on limb-end joints.

## Input / Output

The system consumes 6-DoF IMU signals (3-axis acceleration + 3×3 orientation matrix) from six body nodes (left/right forearms, left/right lower legs, pelvis, head) plus 4×4 ToF depth maps from four of those nodes (forearms and lower legs only; head and pelvis have zero-filled depth). Outputs are per-frame estimates for 18 tracked joints: velocity (J×3), position (J×3), and rotation (J×6), plus a global root translation computed by fusing forward-kinematics-based displacement from stationary endpoints with the network-predicted root velocity. The ToF depth maps are low-resolution (4×4) SPAD-based measurements capturing distances from distal joints to nearby body parts and the ground.

## Method

The core contribution is a Transformer-based multi-sensor integration framework with two key mechanisms. First, Node-Centric Data Integration (NCI) replaces the conventional flatten-then-FCN approach by treating each sensing node as an independent token: intra-node integration encodes the co-located ToF depth, IMU acceleration, and orientation into a single token via a tokenize function, and inter-node integration uses Transformer self-attention to compute dynamic cross-node interaction weights, preserving the hierarchical sensor structure. Second, Dynamic Spatial Positional Encoding (Dyn-PE) models each node's spatial position as a learnable 2-layer MLP function of all six nodes' motion signals, producing time-varying sinusoidal encodings that reflect the continuously evolving body configuration rather than fixed index-based positions. These node embeddings feed three sequential LSTMs that regress joint velocity, position, and rotation in a cascade, trained with L2 loss against optical mocap ground truth. Global translation is obtained by fusing FK-based displacement (computed from stationary endpoint detection via velocity thresholding) with the network's root velocity prediction.

## Results

On the proposed ToF-IP-DB dataset, ToF-IP achieves 17.26° SIP error, 12.09° angular error, 6.31 cm positional error, and 11.41 cm endpoint positional error, outperforming the strongest baseline PNP (18.52°, 13.23°, 6.86 cm, 12.39 cm) across all metrics. On the DIP dataset (with synthesized ToF), ToF-IP reaches 13.62° SIP error and 6.75° angular error, substantially beating PNP's 13.71° and 8.75° respectively, while also achieving the lowest endpoint positional error of 6.65 cm (vs. PNP's 7.49 cm). The method maintains the lowest jitter (0.12 km/s³) on ToF-IP-DB, matching PNP and PIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToF-IP consistently outperforms all five baselines (Transpose, TIP, PIP, DynaIP, PNP) on every metric across both datasets. The strongest baseline is PNP (SIGGRAPH 2024), which ToF-IP beats by 1.26° in SIP error and 1.14 cm in endpoint positional error on ToF-IP-DB, and by a notable 2.0° in angular error on DIP (6.75° vs. 8.75°). DynaIP (CVPR 2024) is the second-strongest baseline, trailing ToF-IP by 1.78° in SIP error on ToF-IP-DB and 0.16° on DIP. The older baselines TIP (2017) and Transpose (2021) show substantially larger errors (20+° SIP on ToF-IP-DB), highlighting the cumulative progress in the sparse-IMU literature. The largest relative gains from ToF-IP appear in endpoint positional error (the joints directly equipped with ToF sensors), confirming that direct distance measurements most benefit the limb-end joints where inertial integration error accumulates most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse-IMU motion capture lineage (DIP, TIP, PIP, DynaIP, PNP, Transpose) by addressing the fundamental error-accumulation problem at the hardware level rather than purely through software. It retains the standard 6-IMU layout and the cascade-of-LSTM motion estimator architecture from Transpose/PIP/PNP, but departs from the FCN-based flattening of multi-node sensor data by introducing a Transformer encoder with node-level tokenization (Node-Centric Data Integration) and a motion-conditioned positional encoding (Dyn-PE) tailored to the continuously evolving spatial configuration of wearable sensors. The integration of SPAD-based ToF sensors for direct inter-limb distance measurement is the key conceptual departure from all prior inertial-only methods, which rely on indirect time-based acceleration integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing direct distance constraints that bypass double-integration, ToF-IP tackles a long-standing and fundamental limitation of sparse IMU systems, with particular gains on slow, low-velocity motions (Tai Chi, Baduanjin) where the motion-signal-to-noise ratio is lowest. The 3% volume increase makes the hardware practically deployable, and the 208-minute ToF-IP-DB dataset with synchronized IMU-ToF-optical ground truth is a valuable resource for the community. However, the ToF sensors are low-resolution (4×4), performance is sensitive to occlusions and field-of-view limitations, and evaluation is confined to controlled laboratory settings with 10 participants, leaving open questions about robustness in diverse real-world environments and generalization to different body morphologies.
