## Problem

A well-known impossibility result by Mannor et al. (2009) shows that no algorithm can simultaneously achieve sublinear regret and sublinear constraint violation in CMDPs with adversarially selected rewards and constraints. This paper bridges the gap between fully stochastic and fully adversarial CMDPs by introducing adversarial corruption C as a measure of how much reward and constraint distributions shift across episodes, and shows that performance degrades smoothly from √T (stochastic) to linear (fully adversarial) as C grows.

## Input / Output

The learner interacts with an episodic loop-free CMDP over T episodes of length L, receiving only bandit feedback: at each step it observes the reward and all m constraint costs for the single state-action pair actually visited, not the full transition or reward tables. The algorithm outputs a policy (action distribution per state) at each episode. A key input distinction is whether the corruption value C is known (NS-SOPS) or unknown (Lag-FTRL), and the performance guarantee is expressed as Õ(√T + C) for both regret and positive constraint violation, where positive violation means per-episode violations are summed without cancellation.

## Method

NS-SOPS (known C) augments standard UCB confidence bounds with two additional terms: C/N_{t}(x,a) to absorb per-episode distributional shifts and C/T to bound the gap between the time-averaged mean and the fictitious non-corrupted counterpart, yielding enlarged bounds that 'boost' optimism in both reward maximization and constraint satisfaction. Lag-FTRL (unknown C) instantiates ⌈log₂T⌉ stabilized copies of NS-SOPS, each with a different corruption guess C=2^j, and a master FTRL procedure with log-barrier regularization selects which instance to follow at each episode. The FTRL losses are constructed from the Lagrangian of the offline CMDP program, modified with a [·]⁺ operator to handle positive constraint violation; a key theoretical contribution is proving that the positive Lagrangian with multiplier L/ρ retains a strong-duality property under Slater's condition, which is essential to prevent the FTRL from selecting instances that trade large rewards for large violations.

## Results

The paper provides purely theoretical guarantees with no empirical experiments. The headline result is that both NS-SOPS (known C) and Lag-FTRL (unknown C) achieve Õ(√T + C) regret and Õ(√T + C) positive constraint violation under bandit feedback, where C is the adversarial corruption of rewards and constraints. In the fully stochastic case (C = 0), this reduces to the state-of-the-art Õ(√T) bound matching Efroni et al. [2020]. In the worst-case adversarial setting (C = Θ(T)), the bounds become linear in T, which is coherent with the impossibility result of Mannor et al. [2009] that rules out simultaneously sublinear regret and sublinear constraint violation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experiments. The comparisons are entirely at the level of theoretical guarantees against prior work. Compared to Stradi et al. [2024b, 2025c] (adversarial constraints), which achieve sublinear non-positive constraint violation but linear regret, this paper achieves sublinear regret AND sublinear positive constraint violation whenever C is sublinear in T, and handles the stricter positive violation metric. Compared to Efroni et al. [2020] (stochastic CMDPs), the bounds match in the C=0 case but extend to non-stationary settings. Compared to Wei et al. [2023] and Ding and Lavaei [2023] (non-stationary CMDPs), this paper uses a static regret baseline (easier) but handles positive constraint violation (stricter) and does not require large-T assumptions. The key distinction from corruption-robust RL works (Lykouris et al. [2021], Chen et al. [2021], Wei et al. [2022], Jin et al. [2024]) is the dual objective of simultaneously controlling regret and constraint violation, which prevents direct application of standard corralling techniques.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the corruption-robust reinforcement learning line (Lykouris et al. 2021, Chen et al. 2021, Wei et al. 2022, Jin et al. 2024) from unconstrained MDPs to constrained MDPs, where the dual objective of minimizing regret while controlling constraint violation breaks standard corralling techniques. The NS-SOPS algorithm builds on the optimistic policy search of Efroni et al. (2020) for stochastic CMDPs and the confidence-set construction of Jin et al. (2020) for adversarial MDPs, while the Lag-FTRL meta-procedure adapts the multi-instance selection framework of Agarwal et al. (2017) and the optimistic loss estimators of Neu (2015) to the constrained setting. Crucially, the paper departs from prior adversarial CMDP work (Stradi et al. 2024b, 2025c), which could only achieve linear regret due to the Mannor et al. (2009) impossibility result, by showing that sublinear regret and violation are simultaneously attainable when the adversarial corruption C is accounted for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first unified framework that smoothly interpolates between stochastic and adversarial CMDPs, resolving a long-standing gap: the Mannor et al. (2009) impossibility result is bypassed not by weakening the objective but by quantifying non-stationarity through corruption. The treatment of positive constraint violation (no cross-episode cancellations) is practically essential for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving, where a past violation cannot be 'repaid.' Open questions include whether the Õ(√T + C) bounds are tight, whether the approach extends to continuous action spaces or partially observable settings, and whether the corruption measure can be estimated adaptively without the logarithmic number of instances used here.
