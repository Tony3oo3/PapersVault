## Problem

Direct numerical simulation of multiscale PDE systems is computationally infeasible because it requires resolving the finest scales, and existing data-driven approaches either implicitly parameterize subgrid effects via closure models or sacrifice spatial structure through global reduced-order projections. No prior framework explicitly learns the coupled stochastic dynamics of both macroscale and microscale latent states from observational data while enforcing a principled scale hierarchy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251836/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is trained on noisy spatiotemporal trajectories of a fully-resolved PDE solution (e.g., 1000-point 1D grids or 320×80 2D grids) and at test time requires only a single initial observation y(t₀) on the fine grid. It produces a posterior predictive distribution p(y(t)|y(t₀)) as a Gaussian mixture over N Monte Carlo samples of the latent SDE, where each component is centered at an adaptively weighted combination of the decoded macroscale and microscale states. The macroscale state lives on a coarse grid (e.g., 20 points in 1D, 32×8 in 2D) while the microscale state is a compact global latent vector of dimension n_η ∈ {1,…,5}, and the decoder maps both back to the full-resolution observation space.

## Method

The framework decomposes the high-dimensional state into a macroscale latent ζ on a coarse grid and a microscale latent η, whose joint evolution is governed by a learned coupled system of Itô SDEs with coupling functions mediating inter-scale interactions. A Product of Experts (PoE) likelihood enforces scale separation: a macroscale Gaussian expert scores the full observation while a microscale expert scores only the residual after subtracting the macroscale prediction, and the normalization constant acts as an adaptive regularizer that penalizes microscale contributions in directions already well-captured by the macroscale model. Training maximizes a reparametrized ELBO via amortized variational inference, partitioning trajectories into short segments and using a linear variational SDE whose parameters are expressed through ODEs on the mean and covariance, thereby eliminating the need for an SDE solver in the optimization loop. The encoder applies a learned smoothing operator followed by separate macroscale and microscale encoders, while the decoder uses a prolongation operator for ζ and a learned nonlinear map for η, with the PoE structure yielding a closed-form Gaussian conditional for y|z whose mean adaptively weights the microscale contribution by the relative precision matrices.

## Results

The paper evaluates on four PDE systems (1D advection, 1D KdV, 2D cylinder flow, 2D shallow water) using normalized relative error ε = ‖y − ŷ‖/‖y‖. The best multiscale model (n_η=5) achieves errors of 0.079 (Wave 1D), 0.042 (KdV 1D), 0.009 (Cylinder 2D), and 0.152 (SWE 2D), representing 64–87% error reductions over the strongest baseline in each case. The method consistently outperforms all baselines across all four test problems, with the largest gains on advection-dominated and slowly-decaying Kolmogorov n-width systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: coarse DNS, DMD, POD-SINDy, and an implicit-scale model (n_η=0, i.e., their framework without the microscale latent state). The multiscale model (n_η=5) wins on all four test cases against every baseline. On Wave 1D, it achieves 83% lower error than the best baseline (POD-SINDy, 0.469 → 0.079). On KdV 1D, it beats POD-SINDy (0.057) by 36% and the implicit-scale model (0.324) by 87%. On Cylinder 2D, it outperforms DMD (0.016) by 44% and the implicit-scale model (0.063) by 86%. On SWE 2D, it reduces error by 64% over the best baseline (implicit scale, 0.426 → 0.152), while coarse DNS and ROM methods all cluster around 0.53–0.86. The performance gap is most pronounced on problems with slowly-decaying Kolmogorov n-width (Wave 1D, SWE 2D), where linear projection and global ROM methods struggle most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the closure modeling paradigm (Mori-Zwanzig, LES, data-driven closure) by introducing an explicit microscale latent state with its own learned SDE dynamics, rather than parameterizing subgrid effects as corrections to the macroscale alone. The variational inference machinery is inherited from Course and Nair's amortized reparametrization framework for latent SDEs (NeurIPS 2023, Nature 2023), while the multiscale likelihood draws on Hinton's Product of Experts to enforce scale separation. Unlike standard ROM methods (DMD, POD-SINDy) that learn a global low-dimensional representation, the approach maintains spatial structure on a coarse grid, positioning it closer to LES-style coarse-grained modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a simulator-free, probabilistic alternative to closure modeling that naturally propagates uncertainty and enables full-resolution prediction through a decoder—capabilities that conventional closure models lack. The ~80–87% error reductions over baselines across four diverse PDE systems demonstrate practical value, though the current global compression of the microscale state may be suboptimal for problems dominated by local subgrid structures (e.g., 3D turbulence). Open questions include scaling to chaotic systems with long-term stability requirements, spatially-aware microscale representations, and multi-level nested coarse grids for systems with more than two dominant scales.
