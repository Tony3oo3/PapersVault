## Problem

Decoding high-fidelity visual stimuli from single-cell neural activity in V1 is severely constrained by data scarcity: biological recordings provide only a few thousand neurons and limited training samples per subject. Training decoders from scratch on such data yields low-fidelity reconstructions, while GenAI-based methods (diffusion models, pre-trained generators) produce deceptively realistic but spatially inaccurate images with hallucinated content, because their priors are misaligned with the low-level features actually encoded by V1 neurons.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a vector of z-scored spike responses from n neurons (ranging from ~8,000 to ~47,000 depending on dataset) and precomputed Most Exciting Inputs (MEIs) — one grayscale image per neuron that maximizes its response under a CNN encoder. Images are small grayscale patches (36×64 px for BRAINREADER, 22×36 px for SENSORIUM 2022, 20×20 px for SYNTHETIC CAT V1), and the output is a reconstructed grayscale image at the same resolution. MEIs are generated once per dataset via gradient ascent on a trained encoder and are not learned during decoding.

## Method

MEIcoder uses a two-part architecture: a subject-specific 'readin' module and a shared 'core' CNN. In the readin, each neuron's scalar response is combined with a learnable neuron embedding via a one-layer network to produce a spatial context map, which is then pointwise-multiplied with that neuron's precomputed MEI to form a 'neural map'; a pointwise convolution compresses all n neural maps into a fixed channel count. The core is a six-layer CNN (with batch norm, ReLU, dropout) that reconstructs the image from these compressed neural maps. Training uses a combined objective of negative log-SSIM reconstruction loss (weight 0.9) and an auxiliary adversarial loss (weight 0.1) from a separate discriminator CNN, which pushes reconstructions toward natural image statistics without the semantic bias of GenAI priors. The key insight is that MEIs encode each neuron's receptive field as a dense spatial template, providing a biologically grounded prior that dramatically improves data efficiency and prevents hallucination compared to generic generative priors.

## Results

MEIcoder achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three datasets and all four metrics (SSIM, PixCorr, Alex(2), Alex(5)). On the data-scarce BRAINREADER dataset (8,587 neurons, 4,500 training samples), MEIcoder reaches SSIM 0.400, PixCorr 0.679, Alex(2) 0.998, and Alex(5) 0.990, substantially outperforming the best baseline InvEnc (SSIM 0.321, Alex(5) 0.896). On SENSORIUM 2022 (8,372 neurons), MEIcoder achieves SSIM 0.331 and Alex(5) 0.896 versus InvEnc's 0.288 and 0.720. On the larger SYNTHETIC CAT V1 dataset (46,875 neurons, 45,000 training samples), MEIcoder attains SSIM 0.774 and Alex(5) 0.987, matching or exceeding InvEnc (SSIM 0.771, Alex(5) 0.978).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEIcoder outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The largest gains are over the encoder-inversion methods: InvEnc (best baseline) is surpassed by +0.079 SSIM and +0.094 Alex(5) on BRAINREADER, and by +0.043 SSIM and +0.176 Alex(5) on SENSORIUM 2022. EGG (Energy Guided Diffusion) shows the weakest overall performance, with Alex(5) scores of only 0.659 (BRAINREADER) and 0.755 (SENSORIUM 2022), roughly 0.2–0.3 points below MEIcoder. Among direct pixel decoders, MonkeySee and CAE lag significantly on SSIM (0.232 and 0.256 vs. 0.400 on BRAINREADER), while MindEye2, despite its GenAI components, produces spatially inaccurate reconstructions with high variance and hallucinated content, yielding SSIM 0.277 and Alex(5) 0.878 on BRAINREADER. The multi-subject fine-tuned variant MEIcoder (FT) further improves SSIM to 0.424 on BRAINREADER, demonstrating that the core/readin architecture enables effective transfer across subjects.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEIcoder departs from the dominant GenAI-based decoding paradigm (MindEye2, diffusion-model-guided inversion) by replacing semantic priors with biologically grounded ones: neuron-specific Most Exciting Inputs (MEIs) derived from the visual system's own coding properties. It also moves beyond prior neural-population decoders (InvEnc, EGG, MonkeySee, CAE) that either invert pre-trained encoders in response space or use simple feed-forward readins, instead injecting MEIs as dense spatial templates into a shared CNN core with an SSIM-based loss and auxiliary adversarial training. The architecture's core/readin split for multi-subject transfer is a practical departure from prior methods that are not designed to integrate heterogeneous single-subject datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first clear scaling characterization for V1 decoding, showing that 1,000–2,500 neurons and under 1,000 training samples suffice for high-fidelity reconstructions with digit-level discrimination, which directly informs the cost-benefit tradeoff for invasive recording in neuroprosthetic applications. The unified 160K-sample benchmark across mouse and synthetic cat V1 data addresses a critical gap in reproducibility for this subfield. Key open questions include whether MEIcoder generalizes to higher visual areas (V4, IT) with more complex receptive fields, whether the synthetic spiking model's scaling behavior faithfully predicts biological performance, and whether the mixed transfer-learning results (gains on BRAINREADER but not SENSORIUM 2022) can be resolved.
