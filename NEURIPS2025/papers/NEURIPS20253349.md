## Problem

Existing Text-to-Cypher systems rely on supervised fine-tuning or few-shot prompting, which struggle with complex schemas and ambiguous queries, and treat schema selection as a separate preprocessing step rather than part of the reasoning process. No prior work uses reinforcement learning to jointly learn schema selection and Cypher generation, leaving models without an explicit, trainable mechanism to identify relevant schema components during query synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253349/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a natural language question and a simplified graph schema containing only entity and relation names (no properties). The output is a structured multi-step rollout: an initial reasoning block, a JSON-formatted schema selection (parsed to retrieve the full schema with properties from an external dictionary), a second reasoning block, and a final Cypher query. If the JSON is unparseable or references non-existent labels, the full schema is returned as a fallback, making the pipeline robust to malformed selections.

## Method

Cypher-RI trains Qwen2.5-Coder-7B from scratch using GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with a rollout that interleaves schema selection into the reasoning chain: think → emit JSON schema selection → retrieve and append the full selected schema → think → emit Cypher. A loss-masking mechanism (M_{i,t} ∈ {0,1}) ensures only LLM-generated tokens contribute to the policy gradient, excluding externally retrieved schema tokens from backpropagation. The reward is a sum of three rule-based components: format compliance (+1/−1), schema selection correctness against gold labels (+2/−1.5/−2), and Cypher execution accuracy against the gold answer (+2/−1.5/−2). Training data is filtered by generating 8 rollouts per prompt and discarding instances where all rollouts are uniformly correct or incorrect, preventing vanishing advantages in GRPO. No annotated reasoning traces are provided; the model autonomously discovers reasoning strategies that integrate schema selection through the RL signal alone.

## Results

On CypherBench, Cypher-RI achieves 69.59% Execution Accuracy (EX), surpassing GPT-4o (60.18%) by 9.41 percentage points, and attains a near-perfect 99.28% Executable Percentage (Exec.), the highest among all evaluated models. On PSJS, GPT-4o retains a slight edge (76.87% vs. 75.21%), but Cypher-RI's superior EX indicates it retrieves correct data through equally valid but structurally different query patterns. On Neo4j-Text2Cypher, Cypher-RI achieves 30.61% EX and 60.13% Google-BLEU, closely matching GPT-4o (31.73% EX, 62.93% Google-BLEU) while outperforming all other baselines. Compared to its base model Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (13.12% EX), Cypher-RI delivers a 56.47-point absolute improvement in Execution Accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cypher-RI decisively outperforms GPT-4o on CypherBench Execution Accuracy (69.59% vs. 60.18%, +9.41 pp) and Executable Percentage (99.28% vs. 94.90%), but trails slightly on PSJS (75.21% vs. 76.87%). On Neo4j-Text2Cypher, GPT-4o maintains a narrow lead in both EX (31.73% vs. 30.61%) and Google-BLEU (62.93% vs. 60.13%), while Cypher-RI still surpasses all other baselines. Against the domain-specific Text2Cypher-Gemma-3-27B (38.71% EX), Cypher-RI leads by 30.88 pp on CypherBench EX, demonstrating that RL-based schema-integrated training on a 7B model substantially outperforms supervised fine-tuning on a 27B model. The general-purpose baselines (Gemma-2-9B, Llama3.1-8B, GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-1.5-Pro) all fall well below Cypher-RI, with the largest gap observed against the base Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (13.12% EX), confirming the transformative effect of the RL training paradigm. The R3-NLGQL multi-model pipeline (23.94% EX) is also significantly outperformed, suggesting that unified RL training with schema selection is more effective than decomposing the task across multiple specialized models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-reasoning paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1 to the Text-to-Cypher domain, building on GRPO as the optimization algorithm. It departs from prior RL-based query generation work (SQL-R1, Reasoning-SQL) by embedding schema selection directly into the reasoning trajectory rather than treating it as a separate preprocessing step, and from prior Text-to-Cypher systems (R3-NL2GQL, Text2Cypher) by eliminating the need for supervised fine-tuning on annotated reasoning chains. The loss-masking mechanism for externally retrieved schema tokens is a practical contribution not seen in prior RL-for-LLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 7B RL-trained model outperforms GPT-4o by 9.41% on CypherBench while achieving 99.28% executability demonstrates that domain-specialized RL training can rival much larger general-purpose models at a fraction of the inference cost, which is practically significant for deployment. The ablation confirming that SFT memorizes (85.66% train / 44.34% test) while RL generalizes (84.59% train / 69.59% test) reinforces the broader 'SFT memorizes, RL generalizes' narrative. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to other graph query languages (Gremlin, G-CORE), whether the schema-selection-as-reasoning-step design transfers to other structured-output tasks, and how the method scales to much larger or more heterogeneous schemas.
