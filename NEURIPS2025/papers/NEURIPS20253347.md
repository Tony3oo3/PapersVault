## Problem

Existing video prediction methods face a fundamental efficiency-accuracy trade-off: global-attention transformers capture long-range temporal dependencies but scale quadratically with sequence length and spatial resolution, while convolutional and recurrent networks suffer from limited receptive fields and vanishing gradients over extended horizons. No prior architecture has adapted the gradient-driven Titans memory—originally designed for language modeling—to the dense, high-dimensional video forecasting domain, where redundant frames dominate and critical events are sparse.

## Input / Output

The model takes T observed video frames of shape (B, T, C, H, W) and predicts T' future frames at the same spatial resolution. Each frame is decomposed into 16×16 pixel patches via a convolutional embedding, so the effective token grid is (H/16)×(W/16) per frame; spatial dimensions range from 64×64 (Moving MNIST) to 128×128 (Human3.6M). The output is reconstructed from patch-level representations through a transpose-convolutional decoder back to full-resolution frames.

## Method

VideoTitans integrates three components coordinated by a single gating mechanism (Memory-as-a-Gate): (i) a sliding-window attention core that models short-term temporal dependencies with linear complexity in sequence length and spatial resolution; (ii) a gradient-based episodic memory that updates its state via M_t = (1−α_t)M_{t-1} + S_t, where the surprise signal S_t = η_t S_{t-1} − θ_t ∇ℓ(M_{t-1}; x_t) selectively encodes only tokens whose loss gradient indicates substantial novelty; and (iii) a small set of persistent tokens (P=3–4) concatenated to the input that encode input-agnostic, task-specific priors. The encoder uses 16×16 conv patches with positional encoding, reshapes tokens so the temporal axis carries T×embed_dim features, and the decoder mirrors this with transpose convolutions. Training uses Adam with MSE loss, EMA (decay 0.995), and a max gradient norm of 1.0 to prevent the numerical instabilities observed when memory depth exceeds two layers.

## Results

On Moving MNIST, VideoTitans achieves the lowest MSE (21.33) and lowest FLOPs (13.33G) among all compared methods, outperforming MIM (MSE 22.55, 179.2G) and PhyDNet (MSE 28.20, 15.3G). On TrafficBJ, VideoTitans attains the best MSE (0.3099), SSIM (0.9898), and FLOPs (1.90G), surpassing TAU (MSE 0.3108, SSIM 0.9849, 2.49G). On Human3.6M, VideoTitans achieves the highest SSIM (0.9871) versus TAU (0.9839), though SimVP-gSTA edges it on MSE (108.07 vs. 109.48). On WeatherBench, VideoTitans delivers the best uv10 MSE (1.4056) and tcc MSE (0.0554) with the lowest FLOPs (4.92G) across all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoTitans achieves the best MSE on Moving MNIST (21.33 vs. MIM's 22.55) and TrafficBJ (0.3099 vs. TAU's 0.3108), while also delivering the lowest FLOPs on both (13.33G vs. PhyDNet's 15.3G; 1.90G vs. TAU's 2.49G). On TrafficBJ, it substantially outperforms all baselines in SSIM (0.9898 vs. TAU's 0.9849, a 0.5% gap). On Human3.6M, VideoTitans leads in SSIM (0.9871 vs. TAU's 0.9839) but trails SimVP-gSTA on MSE (109.48 vs. 108.07) and FLOPs (128.52G vs. 74.6G). On WeatherBench, VideoTitans achieves the best uv10 MSE (1.4056 vs. SimVP-Uniformer's 1.4781) and tcc MSE (0.0554 vs. SimVP-Uniformer's 0.0561) with the lowest FLOPs (4.92G vs. SimVP-Poolformer's 5.61G), though SimVP-gSTA edges it on t2m MSE (1.1523 vs. 1.1852). Recurrent baselines (PredRNN, E3D-LSTM, ConvLSTM) are consistently outperformed across all datasets by large margins in both accuracy and efficiency, with VideoTitans using 10–100× fewer FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoTitans adapts the Titans gradient-driven episodic memory module (Behrouz et al., 2024), originally designed for language modeling, to the dense video prediction domain for the first time. It departs from the dominant recurrent (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, E3D-LSTM) and efficient-transformer (SimVP variants, TAU, MAU) lines of work by replacing quadratic global attention with linear-complexity sliding-window attention and augmenting it with a gradient-based surprise mechanism that selectively encodes surprising tokens into long-term memory. The persistent-token component draws on the broader idea of input-agnostic learned priors, while the overall gating-based integration (Memory as a Gate) is chosen from among three Titans integration strategies after empirical comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a language-modeling memory mechanism can be transplanted to high-dimensional video prediction without incurring quadratic cost, offering a principled alternative to both RNNs and full-attention transformers for long-horizon forecasting. However, the practical gains are modest: VideoTitans achieves best or near-best metrics on most benchmarks with 10–30% lower FLOPs, but the margins over strong baselines like TAU and SimVP-gSTA are small, and the neural memory module is limited to depth 2 due to training instability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full-resolution WeatherBench2, whether it generalizes beyond prediction to other video understanding tasks (recognition, segmentation), and whether the hyperparameter sensitivity (gradient norm clipping, memory depth) can be alleviated architecturally rather than through manual tuning.
