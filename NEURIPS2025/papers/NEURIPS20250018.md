## Problem

Deep-learning regressors lose accuracy in data-scarce regimes where acquiring ground-truth labels is costly and slow, yet expert knowledge in the form of pairwise comparisons (which item has a higher property value) remains underutilized. Existing post hoc refinement methods either require retraining, are tied to specific architectures, or do not exploit the natural ease with which humans and general-purpose LLMs can make relative judgments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250018/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, RankRefine takes a query sample, a small reference set of k labeled items (typically 10–30, drawn from training data), the base regressor's point estimate and variance, and k pairwise rankings from an external ranker (a general-purpose LLM queried with SMILES strings, or human experts). It produces a single refined scalar prediction and a post-fusion variance estimate. The method is plug-and-play: no retraining or architectural changes are needed, and the base regressor only needs to expose both a point estimate and an uncertainty measure (e.g., random forests, Gaussian processes).

## Method

RankRefine derives a rank-based property estimate by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of a Bradley-Terry model over the pairwise comparisons, treating the query's true property value as the sole free parameter; its variance is approximated via the inverse observed Fisher information. This rank-based estimate is then fused with the base regressor's Gaussian prediction using inverse-variance weighting, which the paper proves (Theorem 3.1) yields the minimum-variance unbiased estimator under independence. A key theoretical result (Corollary 3.2.1) shows that any ranker with finite variance strictly reduces expected MAE, and a closed-form bound (Equation 4) quantifies the required ranker accuracy for a target improvement factor. To guard against over-confident or biased rankers, the ranker variance is floored at a user-chosen fraction of the regressor variance, and the framework remains model-agnostic, requiring only that the base regressor provide calibrated uncertainty.

## Results

Using ChatGPT-4o as a zero-shot pairwise ranker with 20 comparisons per query, RankRefine achieves β (post-refinement MAE / base MAE) of 0.895–0.971 across six TDC ADME molecular datasets, corresponding to up to ~10.5% relative MAE reduction (e.g., VDss: β = 0.895 ± 0.053). With an oracle ranker at just 55% pairwise accuracy and k = 10 references, RankRefine still consistently lowers error across all nine molecular and three tabular benchmarks. In a human-in-the-loop age-estimation study (UTKFace), pairwise self-correction with 15 references reduces MAE from 6.343 to ~6.05 (β = 0.954 ± 0.046).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RankRefine is compared against two post-hoc refinement baselines: the projection-based method of Yan et al. (2024) and Regression by Re-ranking (RbR) of Goncalves et al. (2023). Against RbR, RankRefine generally outperforms across all molecular datasets (Figure 5b shows β_ours − β_reranking < 0 in most configurations). Against the projection baseline, RankRefine wins whenever the ranker accuracy falls in the realistic 0.50–0.95 range; the projection method only dominates when the ranker is nearly perfect (>0.95), because in that regime it can strictly enforce all pairwise constraints without inflating absolute error. The paper notes that such near-perfect rankers are rare in practice, making RankRefine's robustness to moderately accurate rankers (as low as 55%) the key practical advantage. On the tabular datasets (Smart Farming, Student Performance, International Education Cost), the same pattern holds: RankRefine improves over the base regressor with ranker accuracy as low as 0.55.

## Relation to Prior Work

RankRefine sits at the intersection of two classical statistical tools — the Bradley-Terry model for pairwise comparisons and inverse-variance weighting for combining independent estimates — and applies them in a post-hoc, model-agnostic setting. It departs from joint regression-ranking training objectives (Huang et al., 2024) and pairwise-difference regressors (Tynes et al., 2021; Fralish et al., 2023) by requiring no retraining, and from projection-based consolidation (Yan et al., 2024) by using a likelihood-based rank estimate rather than hard constraint enforcement. The paper's distinctive contribution is demonstrating that off-the-shelf, un-fine-tuned LLMs can serve as sufficiently accurate pairwise rankers for molecular properties, extending the LLM-for-chemistry line of work (Guo et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2025) from classification and fine-tuned generation to a zero-shot ranking role.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In data-scarce scientific domains where ground-truth labels require costly experiments, RankRefine offers a practical, plug-and-play way to squeeze additional accuracy from existing regressors using only a handful of pairwise comparisons — obtainable from LLMs or human experts without retraining. The theoretical guarantee that any finite-variance ranker reduces expected MAE is clean, but the practical ceiling is bounded by the ranker's accuracy and the Gaussian/independence assumptions, which may not hold under heavy-tailed or correlated noise. Open questions include whether richer stochastic-transitivity models or Bayesian calibration layers can close the gap between the Bradley-Terry approximation and true label scales, and whether the approach extends naturally to multivariate or structured targets such as full pharmacokinetic profiles.
