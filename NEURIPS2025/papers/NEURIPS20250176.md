## Problem

Vision-based 3D Panoptic Scene Completion (PSC) remains largely unexplored, with existing Transformer-based methods relying on a fixed set of learned instance queries that, while updated with image context during training, remain static and shared across all inputs at test time. This static nature limits the model's ability to dynamically adapt to the specific scene being observed, degrading instance identification and geometric completion. The paper addresses this by introducing context-adaptive instance proposals that are initialized from scene-specific image context at both train and test time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single forward-facing RGB image (1226×370) as input, from which it estimates a depth map via a pretrained MobileStereoNet and extracts image features via an EfficientNetB7 backbone. It produces a full 3D panoptic voxel grid at 256×256×32 resolution (0.2 m voxels, 20 semantic classes) where every voxel is assigned both a semantic class label and an instance ID, directly predicting the complete panoptic scene without post-hoc clustering. Internally, the network operates at half voxel resolution (128×128×16) and upsamples via trilinear interpolation.

## Method

IPFormer lifts 2D image features into a probabilistic 3D volume using per-pixel depth distributions, then samples only visible voxels (determined by inverse-projecting the depth map) to initialize context-adaptive instance proposals via 3D deformable self-attention, ensuring each proposal is grounded in actual scene content rather than being a static learned embedding. A dual-head, two-stage training strategy first trains a voxel-proposal head on the SSC objective to establish strong geometric and semantic representations, then introduces the instance-proposal head that attends over the pretrained voxel features via cross-attention followed by self-attention among instances. Decoding aligns instance features with voxel features through a parameter-free dot-product affinity, converts to probabilities via sigmoid, and aggregates a complete panoptic scene by argmax over class and instance dimensions. This visibility-based, context-adaptive initialization yields a 14× runtime reduction (0.33 s vs. 4.51 s) over baselines that require post-hoc DBSCAN clustering, while achieving state-of-the-art in-domain PSC metrics and superior zero-shot generalization to KITTI-360.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI validation set, IPFormer achieves the best overall Panoptic Quality (PQ†=14.45, PQ=6.30) and Recognition Quality (RQ-All=9.75) among all compared methods. It also leads on Stuff-specific metrics (PQ-Stuff=9.35, RQ-Stuff=14.43) while placing second on SQ-All (41.95 vs. CGFormer's 48.14) and SSC metrics (IoU=40.90, mIoU=15.33). A standout advantage is runtime: IPFormer completes inference in 0.33 s versus 4.51–4.70 s for all baselines, a >14× speedup, because it directly predicts a panoptic scene without post-hoc DBSCAN clustering. On the out-of-domain SSCBench-KITTI360 test set, IPFormer also outperforms CGFormer+DBSCAN in zero-shot generalization (PQ†=9.41 vs. 8.44, SQ=24.68 vs. 17.82).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPFormer is compared against four state-of-the-art vision-based SSC methods (MonoScene, Symphonies, OccFormer, CGFormer), each augmented with DBSCAN clustering to approximate instance predictions, since no established vision-based PSC baselines exist. Against the strongest baseline CGFormer+DBSCAN, IPFormer wins on PQ† (14.45 vs. 14.39), PQ (6.30 vs. 6.16), RQ-All (9.75 vs. 9.48), PQ-Stuff (9.35 vs. 9.03), and RQ-Stuff (14.43 vs. 13.86), but trails on SQ-All (41.95 vs. 48.14), PQ-Thing (2.09 vs. 2.20), SQ-Thing (42.67 vs. 44.46), and SSC metrics (IoU 40.90 vs. 45.98, mIoU 15.33 vs. 16.89). Against Symphonies, OccFormer, and MonoScene, IPFormer dominates across all PSC metrics by wide margins (e.g., PQ† 14.45 vs. 11.69/11.25/10.12). The most striking practical advantage is runtime: IPFormer's 0.33 s total inference time is over 14× faster than all baselines (4.51–4.70 s), which include ~4.43 s of DBSCAN clustering overhead. On zero-shot out-of-domain transfer to SSCBench-KITTI360, IPFormer again outperforms CGFormer+DBSCAN on PQ† (9.41 vs. 8.44), SQ (24.68 vs. 17.82), and RQ-All (2.16 vs. 1.87), with smaller relative performance drops across most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

IPFormer extends the Transformer-based SSC/PSC lineage (VoxFormer, Symphonies, CGFormer, OccFormer) by replacing their static or context-aware-but-non-adaptive instance queries with context-adaptive instance proposals initialized from visible voxels sampled via depth. The two-stage training strategy (SSC first, then PSC) and dual-head architecture draw from CGFormer's TPV+voxel encoder and PaSCo's instance-voxel alignment, but the visibility-based proposal initialization is the core conceptual departure. The paper positions itself against PanoSSC (the only prior camera-based PSC method) by noting PanoSSC's reliance on non-public data and static queries, and against LiDAR-based PSC (PaSCo) by addressing the unexplored vision-based setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14× runtime reduction (4.51 s → 0.33 s) is practically significant for real-time autonomous driving, and the zero-shot generalization to KITTI-360 suggests the context-adaptive proposal mechanism transfers across sensor configurations. However, the evaluation is limited to KITTI-family datasets with forward-facing single images, no temporal or multi-view information, and the baselines are adapted SSC methods with post-hoc DBSCAN clustering rather than true PSC systems, making the SOTA claim somewhat qualified. Open questions include whether the approach scales to surround-view or multi-frame settings, whether the moderate SSC performance (second-best on IoU/mIoU) can be closed without sacrificing PSC gains, and how the method behaves on more diverse urban or indoor scenes.
