## Problem

Current RLHF pipelines train a single scalar reward model via Bradley-Terry, collapsing diverse human preferences into a majority signal and marginalizing minority perspectives. This one-size-fits-all objective leads to preference collapse, where majority views are amplified and response diversity is reduced. The paper asks whether a small distribution over reward functions can faithfully capture the full spectrum of human preference frequencies without requiring annotator identities or predefined demographic groups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251940/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise preference data where each comparison is annotated by multiple annotators, retaining the soft label (fraction of annotators preferring one response) rather than collapsing to a binary majority vote. No annotator identifiers or group labels are required. The output is a weighted ensemble of k reward functions (each a linear head on a frozen SFT Llama 3-8B embedding), which induces a corresponding mixture of aligned policies deployable in balanced, steerable, or distributional pluralism modes at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is pairwise calibration: for every context-response pair, the fraction of reward functions in the ensemble preferring one response must match the observed annotator preference fraction. To construct such ensembles, the paper proposes a Forward Stagewise Additive Modeling (FSAM) procedure that iteratively trains a new reward model on the current residual calibration error (using MSE loss on soft labels with a sigmoid proxy for the binary vote indicator), then re-optimizes mixture weights. Each reward model is initialized from the same SFT checkpoint with a freshly initialized linear head, keeping all other parameters frozen. Theoretically, the paper proves that finding a perfectly calibrated ensemble is NP-hard (via reduction from Minimum Feedback Arc Set), yet an O(1/ε)-sized ensemble suffices for ε-calibration, extreme outliers can be pruned with bounded calibration loss, and the approach generalizes with finite VC-dimension reward classes.

## Results

On all four datasets (MultiPref, PersonalLLM, HelpSteer2, Reddit TL;DR), an ensemble of just 2–4 weak reward models trained via FSAM achieves lower MSE calibration error on held-out prompts than the theoretically optimal single deterministic reward model (which always selects the majority-preferred answer, with a floor of Σᵢ min{pᵢ², (1−pᵢ)²}). Gains in calibration accuracy taper off around 6 ensemble members. For diversity, pairwise Kendall-τ rank correlations between reward models within each ensemble are well below 1 (ranging roughly 0.3–0.7 depending on dataset), confirming that the ensemble members capture distinct preference patterns rather than duplicating one another.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against a theoretically optimal single deterministic reward model that always picks the majority-preferred response for every pairwise comparison. This single-model baseline minimizes binary classification error and its MSE calibration loss is bounded below by Σᵢ min{pᵢ², (1−pᵢ)²}. The FSAM ensemble of k=8 weak reward models (each initialized from the same Llama 3-8B SFT checkpoint with a freshly initialized linear reward head) beats this single-model floor on all four datasets, with the crossover occurring at k=2–4. No other named baselines (e.g., BT-trained single reward, label-smoothed variants, or cluster-based multi-reward methods) are directly benchmarked in the experiments; the comparison is specifically designed to show that a small mixture of weaker models can outperform the best possible single deterministic model on the calibration objective. The paper notes that reporting standard accuracy is not meaningful here because the task is not majority classification and annotator identities are unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard RLHF pipeline (Ouyang et al., 2022) and Bradley-Terry reward modeling (Bradley & Terry, 1952) by replacing the single scalar reward with a distribution over reward functions, trained on soft preference fractions rather than binary majority labels. It departs from prior pluralistic alignment work that relies on predefined demographic groups or clustering (Chakraborty et al., 2024; Sorensen et al., 2025) by learning the ensemble purely from aggregate pairwise preference data without annotator identities. The construction algorithm draws on forward stagewise additive modeling (Hastie et al., 2009), adapting it to the reward-calibration setting with MSE loss and sigmoid-smoothed binary indicators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal criterion (pairwise calibration) and matching theoretical guarantees—NP-hardness of exact calibration, O(1/ε) small-support sufficiency, outlier pruning, and generalization bounds—for learning a distribution over rewards from aggregated preferences without group labels. This gives practitioners a principled, identity-free path to pluralistic alignment that avoids the data-sparsity and echo-chamber risks of per-group or per-user models. Key open questions include whether pairwise calibration extends to higher-order (multi-response) preferences, how the approach behaves on highly contested topics where refusal may be preferable, and whether the FSAM heuristic's empirical success generalizes beyond the 8B-parameter scale tested here.
