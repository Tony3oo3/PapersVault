## Problem

Discrete Flow-based Models (DFMs) produce high-quality discrete data but require slow multi-step iterative decoding, making few-step and one-step generation impractical. The root cause is the factorization approximation across dimensions that becomes increasingly inaccurate for large time steps, and existing remedies (distillation methods like SDTT, Di4C) require teacher-student architectures and specialized training objectives. This paper identifies that the factorization error depends on the coupling between source and target distributions, opening a path to reduce it without modifying the training procedure.

## Input / Output

For image generation, 256×256 RGB images are tokenized into 16×16 grids of VQGAN tokens (vocabulary size 1024), and the model takes a class label plus a random or masked initial token grid to produce the full 16×16 token sequence in 1, 4, or 8 decoding steps. For text generation, sequences of 1024 tokens (GPT-2 tokenizer, vocabulary 50257) are generated from a uniform random initial state. The rectification process itself consumes a pretrained DFM and a small set of generated (source, target) pairs (50k for images, 20k for text) to define the next coupling, rather than requiring the full training dataset.

## Method

ReDi characterizes the DFM factorization error as Conditional Total Correlation (TC) and shows it depends on the coupling π between source and target distributions. The rectification loop trains a DFM on the current coupling π_k, then samples (X₀, X₁) pairs from the trained model to define a new coupling π_{k+1} = p(X₀)·p_θ(X₁|X₀), which provably monotonically decreases Conditional TC (Theorem 1, via the Pythagorean inequality for KL divergence). For high-cardinality data like text, a perturbed rectification variant samples clean targets and perturbs them to intermediate states before generating, preventing marginal drift across iterations. The final rectified coupling is then used to train a one-step generative model that directly maps source to target in a single forward pass, achieving FID 11.68 on ImageNet versus 90+ for prior one-step distillation baselines, without any teacher-student architecture or specialized loss terms.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, ReDi³-distill achieves an FID of 11.68 and IS of 182 in a single step, dramatically outperforming SDTT† (FID 90.40, IS 14) and Di4C (FID 90.32, IS 13) at the same 1-step setting. At 4 steps, ReDi¹ achieves FID 7.58 and IS 228, and ReDi² achieves FID 7.86 and IS 240, both surpassing the MaskGIT teacher (FID 10.90, IS 184) and matching or exceeding Di4C (FID 6.20, IS 216) and SDTT† (FID 8.97, IS 205). On OpenWebText, iterative rectification (ReDi¹, ReDi², ReDi³) matches the 1024-step DUO+DCD teacher's generative perplexity at 16 and 8 steps respectively, yielding 64× and 128× decoding speedups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MaskGIT teacher, ReDi¹ and ReDi² clearly win at 4-step generation (FID 7.58/7.86 vs. 10.90; IS 228/240 vs. 184), and ReDi³-distill nearly matches the teacher's 4-step quality in a single step (FID 11.68 vs. 10.90). Against Di4C, the comparison is mixed at 4 steps: Di4C achieves a lower FID (6.20 vs. 7.58) but ReDi¹ achieves a higher IS (228 vs. 216) and comparable Precision/Density/Coverage; however, at 1 step ReDi³-distill overwhelmingly dominates (FID 11.68 vs. 90.32, IS 182 vs. 13). Against SDTT†, ReDi¹ wins on both FID (7.58 vs. 8.97) and IS (228 vs. 205) at 4 steps, and again the 1-step gap is enormous (FID 11.68 vs. 90.40). On OpenWebText, ReDi variants consistently outperform the DUO+DCD teacher at matched step counts and match the teacher's 1024-step perplexity (36.21) at 16 steps (ReDi¹: 37.13) and 8 steps (ReDi³: 36.33), while the hybrid Di4C+ReDi¹ further improves over Di4C alone at every step count (e.g., 4-step PPL 47.50 vs. 55.42).

## Relation to Prior Work

ReDi adapts the rectification principle from continuous Rectified Flows (Liu et al., 2023; InstaFlow; PeRFlow) to the discrete domain, but identifies a fundamentally different error to minimize: rather than path non-straightness, the relevant quantity is the factorization approximation error, which the paper characterizes via Conditional Total Correlation. This departs from prior DFM distillation methods (SDTT, Di4C, DUO) that modify training objectives or require teacher-student architectures; ReDi instead iteratively refines the coupling between source and target distributions using only a single model and the standard DFM training objective. The iterative training-and-sampling loop also draws conceptual parallels to Iterative Markovian Fitting procedures in Schrödinger bridge matching, positioning ReDi as a discrete-domain instance of broader coupling-refinement principles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-step generation result (FID 11.68 vs. ~90 for SDTT/Di4C) is a large practical win, and the method's simplicity—no specialized losses, no dual-model training—makes it broadly applicable as a plug-in for existing DFM pipelines and distillation frameworks. Open questions include the theoretical connection to continuous rectification (the paper acknowledges this gap), whether the approach extends to autoregressive models within the DFM framework, and how error accumulation from model-generated pairs behaves at scale in very high-cardinality settings. The perturbed rectification strategy for text generation, while effective, introduces an additional hyperparameter (perturbation time) that may need careful tuning across domains.
