## Problem

Modern long-sequence vision models (Transformer-XL, Longformer, Mamba) break the permutation equivariance of full self-attention through sparse attention, recurrence, or state-space updates, making their performance sensitive to the order in which image patches are linearized. This sensitivity has been largely overlooked, with the conventional row-major raster scan assumed to be inconsequential. The paper asks whether patch order measurably affects accuracy and whether an optimal, task-specific ordering can be learned.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 224×224 RGB image split into a 14×14 grid of 16×16 patches (196 tokens) plus a fixed [CLS] token at position 0. The Plackett-Luce policy outputs a single 196-dimensional logit vector (one scalar per patch), from which a permutation is sampled during training via Gumbel-top-k and taken deterministically at test time. Only the 196 patch tokens and their positional embeddings are permuted; the [CLS] token remains fixed, yielding a class label as the final output.

## Method

REOrder is a two-stage framework. First, an information-theoretic prior is derived by discretizing images with a VQ-VAE and measuring LZMA compression ratios across six fixed scan orders (row-major, column-major, Hilbert, spiral, diagonal, snake); the least compressible ordering provides a warm-start initialization for the policy. Second, a Plackett-Luce distribution over permutations—parameterized by a 196-dim logit vector—is optimized with REINFORCE, where the reward is negative cross-entropy loss and permutations are sampled efficiently via the Gumbel-top-k trick in O(n log n). A three-stage curriculum separates representation learning (15 epochs of standard row-major training) from policy exploration (30 epochs with a triangular Gumbel temperature schedule peaking at τ=0.2), after which the temperature collapses to zero and the backbone fine-tunes under the frozen deterministic permutation. The approach adds negligible overhead (196 parameters) and acts as a drop-in modification to existing long-sequence model training.

## Results

REOrder improves top-1 accuracy over the best fixed patch ordering by up to 3.01% (±0.23 pp) on ImageNet-1K and 13.35% (±0.21 pp) on Functional Map of the World. Mamba (ARM) benefits most, with average gains of 2.20% (±0.22 pp) across orderings on IN1K and 9.32% (±0.22 pp) on FMoW. Transformer-XL shows more modest improvements of 0.70% (±0.22 pp) average on IN1K and 1.10% (±0.21 pp) on FMoW, while Longformer—whose sliding-window attention most closely approximates full self-attention—shows no measurable gain from REOrder on either dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViT serves as a control: it is permutation-equivariant and achieves consistent ~37.5% (IN1K) / ~46.5% (FMoW) regardless of patch order, confirming that the observed gains are specific to architectures that break equivariance. Against Mamba/ARM, REOrder wins decisively—improving the Hilbert-curve baseline by 3.01% on IN1K and the diagonal baseline by 13.35% on FMoW—because ARM's fixed four-directional causal scans are highly sensitive to input ordering. Against Transformer-XL, REOrder wins by 0.70% average on IN1K (peaking at 1.50% with Hilbert initialization) and 1.10% on FMoW, confirming that even segment-recurrence models benefit from learned ordering. Against Longformer, REOrder ties (no improvement on either dataset), which the authors attribute to Longformer's near-full attention approximation making it the least order-sensitive of the three long-sequence models. The random-permutation baselines (53.10% static, 50.23% per-batch) are 7–11 pp below row-major, demonstrating that the structured REINFORCE exploration—not merely any non-standard ordering—is what drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the long-sequence vision model literature (Longformer, Transformer-XL, Mamba/ARM) by identifying that the architectural approximations these models use to avoid O(n²) attention—sliding windows, segment recurrence, and state-space scans—break the permutation equivariance that full self-attention guarantees. It departs from prior scan-order work (e.g., ARM's fixed row-wise clusters) by treating patch ordering as a learnable variable rather than a fixed design choice, and repurposes the Plackett-Luce + REINFORCE framework from learning-to-rank to search over the combinatorial permutation space of 196 patches. The information-theoretic compressibility prior (LZMA on VQ-VAE tokens) is a novel but ultimately weak initialization signal, motivating the RL stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that patch ordering is a free, drop-in hyperparameter that can yield meaningful accuracy gains (up to 3% on ImageNet-1K, 13.35% on FMoW) without architectural changes, which is valuable for practitioners deploying long-sequence vision models. Key limitations include evaluation only on Base-scale models across two datasets, a single global ordering shared across all images (rather than per-image dynamic ordering), and a very short RL exploration window (30 epochs, with peak noise for ~1 epoch). Open questions include whether the learned orderings transfer across model scales, whether per-image dynamic policies (conditioned on patch embeddings) yield further gains, and how REOrder behaves at ultra-high-resolution sequence lengths where the combinatorial space becomes even more intractable.
