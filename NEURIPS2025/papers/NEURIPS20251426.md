## Problem

Existing heterogeneous LLM fusion methods rely on real data from a few fixed domains, preventing the target LLM from acquiring knowledge across diverse domains, and use rigid pre-specified data proportions that cannot adapt to the target LLM's varying capabilities, causing a 'bucket effect' where improvements in some capabilities come at the expense of others.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251426/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K source LLMs of differing architectures and sizes (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B, Mistral-Small-24B, Phi-4) and a single target LLM (e.g., Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Gemma2-9B-IT) as input, and produces a fused target LLM that performs no worse than the best source LLM across all domains. Notably, no real training data is used; all question-answer pairs are synthetically generated through multi-model collaboration on a dynamically expanding hierarchical knowledge tree, with only ~1.7K synthetic samples needed versus ~90K for baselines.

## Method

Bohdi organizes knowledge domains into a three-level hierarchical tree and formalizes both domain exploration and cross-domain sampling proportion allocation as a Hierarchical Multi-Armed Bandit (HMAB) problem. The Sprout operation enables dynamic domain expansion by having all source LLMs collaboratively propose new child domains via majority vote, while the Harvest operation generates synthetic QA pairs by having a randomly selected Leader model pose questions and judge the best answer among all models. DynaBranches employs Thompson Sampling with Beta(1,1) priors over Bernoulli reward parameters to adaptively tune sampling proportions, and the Introspection-Rebirth (IR) mechanism uses a Sliding Window Binomial Likelihood Ratio Test (SWBLRT) to detect distribution shifts in reward signals as the target LLM updates, resetting posterior parameters to avoid over-reliance on stale observations. The overall process alternates between a Meditation phase (domain exploration, data collection, proportion adjustment) and an Enlightenment phase (supervised fine-tuning of the target LLM on collected data) over R iterations.

## Results

On Gemma2-9B-IT, Bohdi achieves an average score of 58.90 across 8 benchmarks, a +4.16 improvement over the base model (54.74), while using only 1,756 training samples (~1.8% of the 90K–95K used by baselines). The most striking gain is on TheoremQA, where Bohdi lifts performance from 15.62 to 26.88 (+72%), surpassing the larger Qwen2.5-14B source model. On Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, Bohdi delivers a +4.12% average improvement with consistent gains across all 8 benchmarks, whereas SFT and FuseChat-3.0 cause drops of over 30% on TheoremQA and BBH. Bohdi also achieves the best or second-best score on the majority of individual benchmarks for both target models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bohdi outperforms all four baselines on average for both target models. On Gemma2-9B-IT, Bohdi (AVG 58.90) beats FuseChat (55.80) by +3.10, SFT (52.14) by +6.76, FuseChat-3.0 (53.16) by +5.74, and Condor (54.95) by +3.95. The advantage is most pronounced on reasoning and math benchmarks: Bohdi leads on TheoremQA by +10.26 over Condor and +16.88 over SFT, and on MATH by +3.12 over Condor. A critical differentiator is capability balance—SFT and FuseChat-3.0 cause severe drops (up to >30%) on TheoremQA and BBH for Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, while Bohdi maintains or improves every benchmark simultaneously. Despite using only ~1.8% of the training data (1.7K vs. 90–95K samples), Bohdi achieves the highest average, demonstrating substantially superior data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the heterogeneous LLM fusion line (FuseChat, FuseChat-3.0) by eliminating the dependence on real data and fixed domain proportions that constrain all prior methods. It draws on Condor's tree-structured knowledge organization but departs fundamentally by making domain expansion dynamic (via Sprout/Harvest multi-model collaboration) and by formalizing the domain selection and data allocation problem as a Hierarchical Multi-Armed Bandit, solved with Thompson Sampling. The Introspection-Rebirth mechanism with SWBLRT is a novel addition that enables online tracking of the target model's shifting capabilities during iterative training, a dimension absent from all prior fusion approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Bohdi demonstrates that effective heterogeneous LLM fusion is possible with only ~1.8% of the training data used by baselines (1.7K vs. 90K), while simultaneously eliminating the capability imbalance that plagues fixed-allocation methods. The HMAB formulation provides a principled, theoretically grounded framework for adaptive data allocation that could transfer to other iterative training settings. Open questions include scalability to deeper knowledge trees or more source models, the computational overhead of multi-model Sprout/Harvest collaboration at scale, and whether the approach generalizes beyond text to multimodal fusion. The absence of error bars and the limited number of target architectures (two) leave some uncertainty about the robustness of the reported gains.
