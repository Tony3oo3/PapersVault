## Problem

Existing AI-generated video detectors exhibit poor generalization across unseen generative models because they overfit to generator-specific semantic flaws or compression artifacts rather than intrinsic low-level traces shared by all synthesis architectures. The paper identifies that mid-high diagonal frequency components are both discriminative and robust to video compression, yet no prior training strategy explicitly guides detectors toward exploiting these cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250891/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector processes up to 64 frames per video, each center-cropped to 504×504 pixels (36×36 patches of 14×14) and fed frame-by-frame into a DINOv2 backbone with registers. The video-level prediction is obtained by averaging the 64 frame-level logit scores, yielding a single binary probability for real vs. AI-generated. All test videos are H.264-compressed to match real-video codec conditions and avoid format bias.

## Method

The core contribution is a forensic-oriented training paradigm built on two augmentation strategies applied to a DINOv2 backbone trained end-to-end. First, real videos from Panda70M are reconstructed through the Pyramid Flow VAE to create visually identical fakes that embed architecture-specific artifacts while eliminating semantic content bias. Second, a novel wavelet-band replacement (WaveRep) augmentation uses a 3-level Fully Separable Wavelet Transform (Haar) to decompose each fake frame into 16 frequency bands; the baseband, horizontal, and vertical bands of the fake are swapped with those of its real counterpart, leaving only the diagonal mid-high frequency bands as the sole discriminative signal. This forces the network to learn compression-robust, generator-agnostic forensic traces rather than spurious low-frequency or axis-aligned cues. The detector is trained on a single generator (Pyramid Flow) plus its VAE reconstructions, yet generalizes to 15+ unseen generators including autoregressive models like NOVA, achieving ~12% average accuracy improvement over state-of-the-art.

## Results

The proposed method achieves an average balanced accuracy of 94.3% across 15 different video generators (7 recent 2024–25 models and 8 from the GenVideo benchmark), outperforming the second-best method DeMamba (84.0%) by over 10 percentage points. When all baselines are retrained on the same Pyramid Flow training data, the proposed method still leads at 94.3% versus RINE* (85.2%) and DMID* (83.8%). The method also attains an average AUC of 98.5% and a binary ECE of 0.046, indicating strong calibration. Across 15 generators, the approach yields an average improvement of approximately 12% in balanced accuracy over the state of the art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 8 baselines across every generator and every metric (bAcc, AUC, Pd@5, NLL, ECE). Against the strongest video-specific baseline DeMamba, it gains ~10 points in average balanced accuracy (94.3% vs. 84.0%), with the gap widening to ~15 points on recent generators like Sora (98.5% vs. 56.5%) and Flux (98.0% vs. 56.8%). The video-oriented AIGVDet lags by nearly 20 points on average, while MM-Det performs below random guessing (42.4% avg bAcc) due to format bias in its original DVF training data. Among image-based detectors, RINE (74.1%) and DMID (67.0%) are the closest but still trail by 20+ points. When all methods are retrained on the same Pyramid Flow data (Table 2), the proposed method retains a 9–31 point advantage, and notably image-based methods (RINE* 85.2%, DMID* 83.8%) surpass video-based ones (DeMamba* 78.1%, AIGVDet* 65.2%), suggesting current video detectors under-exploit 3D information. The ablation confirms the wavelet-band replacement augmentation contributes an additional ~3–5 points over reconstruction-only training, driving AUC to ~99% and bAcc past 90%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant trend in AI-generated video detection of designing novel architectures (3D CNNs, Mamba-based models, multi-branch spatial-temporal networks) and instead focuses on the training paradigm, specifically a forensic-oriented data augmentation. It builds on the frequency-domain analysis literature showing that generative architectures leave quasi-periodic spectral peaks (from upsampling operations) and on the image-forensics practice of using VAE reconstruction to create semantically neutral fake training data. The key conceptual departure is the identification of diagonal mid-high wavelet bands as the compression-robust, cross-generator discriminative cue, and the use of Fully Separable Wavelet Transform band replacement to force the detector to rely on those specific frequencies rather than on horizontal/vertical components that overlap with codec artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple, well-motivated augmentation strategy can outperform complex architectural innovations by ~12% in average balanced accuracy across 15 generators, including very recent models (NOVA, FLUX, Sora), and won the SAFE Synthetic Video Detection Challenge 2025. This shifts the research emphasis from architecture design to training data curation and domain-informed augmentation in forensic detection. A key limitation is that the method does not explicitly model temporal artifacts, and its reliance on the Pyramid Flow VAE for reconstruction may limit generalization to entirely different synthesis paradigms. Open questions include whether the diagonal-frequency insight holds for future autoregressive or non-diffusion generators and how to systematically characterize temporal-domain forensic cues.
