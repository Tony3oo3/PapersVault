## Problem

Existing online nonparametric regression methods either restrict competitors to smooth (Lipschitz) or RKHS functions, or handle general Banach spaces without exploiting smoothness structure, and none provide minimax-optimal regret rates against general Besov spaces B^s_pq via a constructive algorithm. Furthermore, no prior method simultaneously adapts to both the unknown regularity parameters (s, p, q) and spatially inhomogeneous smoothness across the domain, leaving a gap in handling realistic functions with varying local regularity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a stream of input points x_t from a compact hypercube X ⊂ R^d and, after each prediction, first-order gradient feedback from convex G-Lipschitz losses. It produces sequential real-valued predictions f̂_t(x_t) with regret guarantees that depend on the Besov norm of the comparator. Notably, the method is parameter-free: it requires no prior knowledge of the smoothness s, integrability p, or summability q, and the adaptive version additionally requires no knowledge of the spatial partition or local smoothness exponents, instead discovering them data-drivenly through the expert-aggregation mechanism.

## Method

The core algorithm (Algorithm 1) maintains a wavelet expansion of the predictor truncated at a data-dependent level J, updating only the coefficients whose basis functions are active at the current input x_t via a parameter-free online optimization rule (e.g., coin-betting or self-tuned mirror descent) that satisfies a second-order regret bound. The regret analysis decomposes into an estimation term (controlled by the parameter-free subroutine over active coefficients) and an approximation term (controlled by a nonlinear best-N-term wavelet oracle), yielding minimax-optimal rates T^{1-s/d} or √T depending on the regime. For local adaptivity (Algorithm 2), a dyadic partition tree is constructed where each node n at level l(n) runs an independent wavelet decomposition starting at scale j_0 = l(n), and predictions are aggregated via a sleeping-expert mechanism with second-order regret guarantees. This allows the algorithm to automatically allocate finer resolution to regions of lower regularity, achieving regret bounds that depend on local Besov norms and smoothness exponents s_n rather than a single global worst-case value.

## Results

The paper establishes minimax-optimal regret bounds for online nonparametric regression against general Besov spaces B^s_{pq} with 1 ≤ p,q ≤ ∞ and s > d/p. For the high-regularity regime (s ≥ d/2 or p < 2), Algorithm 2 achieves regret O(G‖f‖_{B^s_{pq}} √T); for the low-regularity regime (p > 2, s < d/2), it achieves O(G‖f‖_{B^s_{pq}} T^{1−s/d}). The locally adaptive extension (Theorem 2) further refines these bounds by replacing the global smoothness s with per-region local smoothness s_n, yielding improved rates in spatially inhomogeneous settings (e.g., O(T^{1/5}) instead of O(T^{1/(2s+1)}) for f(x)=x^s with s∈(1,2) under exp-concave losses).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Algorithm 2 strictly dominates all four baselines in generality and rate optimality. Compared to Vovk [39], which achieves T^{1−s/(s+d)} and requires knowing s, p, and B, this work achieves the faster minimax-optimal rate T^{1−2s/(2s+d)} (for s ≥ d/2) without requiring the user to specify s or p—only an upper bound S ≥ s and a small ε. Against Vovk [40], which is restricted to p ≥ 2 and yields the suboptimal T^{1−1/p} rate (independent of smoothness s), this work covers all p,q ≥ 1 and recovers the full smoothness-dependent rate. Compared to Gaillard & Gerchinovitz [17] and Zadorozhnyi et al. [42], both of which are limited to Sobolev spaces W^s_p with p ≥ 2, this work extends to the full Besov scale B^s_{pq} for all 1 ≤ p,q ≤ ∞ and eliminates the ε loss present in [42]. Crucially, none of the prior constructive methods achieve the minimax-optimal rate for generic Besov spaces; this paper provides the first such constructive and adaptive algorithm, and additionally offers local adaptivity to spatially inhomogeneous smoothness that no baseline provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the locally-adaptive online nonparametric regression framework of Liautaud et al. [29] (restricted to Lipschitz functions with s≤1) to the full generality of Besov spaces B^s_pq for all 1≤p,q≤∞ and s>d/p, by replacing the chaining-based construction with a multiscale wavelet decomposition. The coefficient updates leverage parameter-free online optimization (Mhammedi & Koolen [32]), while the local adaptivity mechanism builds on the sleeping expert aggregation framework [18]. The work also generalizes Vovk's defensive forecasting [40], which competed with Banach-space prediction rules but yielded rates depending only on integrability p≥2, by incorporating smoothness structure through wavelet coefficients. Conceptually, the paper bridges classical batch wavelet approximation theory (Donoho, DeVore) with online adversarial learning, a combination the authors note remains largely unexplored in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first constructive algorithm achieving minimax-optimal regret over general Besov spaces in the online adversarial setting, removing the need to know (s,p,q) in advance and unifying previously fragmented results for RKHS, Sobolev, and Hölder spaces under a single framework. The local adaptivity result (Theorem 2) is practically significant: in spatially inhomogeneous environments, the algorithm can substantially outperform globally-tuned methods by exploiting higher local smoothness in regular regions while tolerating lower regularity elsewhere. Key limitations include the exponential dimensional complexity in the wavelet regularity parameter S, and the restriction to s>d/p (i.e., L^∞-embedded functions); extending to general L^p competitors with s≤d/p is identified as an open direction requiring new analytical tools.
