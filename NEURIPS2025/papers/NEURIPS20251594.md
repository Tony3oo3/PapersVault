## Problem

Existing 3D semantic occupancy prediction methods face an efficiency-expressiveness tradeoff: dense voxel grids waste computation on empty space, while Gaussian-based object-centric representations are constrained by an inherent ellipsoidal shape prior that forces dense packing of many primitives to approximate diverse real-world geometries (cuboids, cylinders, irregular shapes). No prior representation can model complex 3D structures with both few primitives and high geometric fidelity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images (6 cameras at 900×1600 for nuScenes, or a single front camera at 376×1408 for KITTI-360) and produces a dense 3D semantic occupancy grid (200×200×16 or 256×256×32 voxels) with per-voxel class labels. Internally, the scene is represented by a sparse set of P learnable superquadrics (1600–12800), each parameterized by position, 3D scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, semantic probability vector, and two shape exponents (ε₁, ε₂). Occupancy and semantics at any query point are computed analytically from the superquadric mixture rather than from a learned voxel head.

## Method

QuadricFormer replaces ellipsoidal Gaussians with superquadrics as scene primitives, exploiting the two shape exponents (ε₁, ε₂) to natively parameterize cuboids, cylinders, spheres, and intermediate shapes with only 11 parameters per primitive. A probabilistic superquadric mixture model converts each superquadric into a local occupancy probability distribution via p_o(x;Q) = exp(−f(x_Q)), where f is the superquadric surface equation evaluated in the primitive's local frame; final occupancy is the complement of the product of per-primitive non-occupancy probabilities, and semantics are inferred by opacity-weighted aggregation. The architecture iteratively refines randomly initialized superquadrics through B quadric-encoder blocks, each combining a quadric encoder, 3D sparse convolution for self-interaction, deformable attention for image–primitive cross-attention, and a residual decoder. A post-training pruning-and-splitting module discards small-scale superquadrics (likely in empty regions) and splits large-scale ones (likely in occupied regions), then refines with two additional blocks, concentrating primitives where they matter. Training uses cross-entropy plus Lovász-softmax loss with AdamW and cosine LR decay over 20 epochs.

## Results

On nuScenes, QuadricFormer with 1600 superquadrics achieves 20.12 mIoU and 31.22 IoU, while scaling to 12800 superquadrics reaches 21.11 mIoU and 32.13 IoU — state-of-the-art among all compared methods. In the efficiency comparison (Table 3), QuadricFormer at 1600 primitives runs at 162 ms latency and 2554 MB memory, roughly 2× faster and 20% less memory than GaussianFormer-2 at the same primitive count (341 ms, 3075 MB). On SSCBench-KITTI-360 (monocular), QuadricFormer with 12800 primitives achieves 38.89 IoU and 13.63 mIoU, matching GaussianFormer-2's 38.37 IoU / 13.90 mIoU while using 3× fewer primitives (12800 vs. 38400).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuadricFormer (12800 superquadrics) achieves the highest mIoU (21.11) and IoU (32.13) on nuScenes, outperforming GaussianFormer-2 (20.82 mIoU, 31.74 IoU) by +0.29 mIoU and +0.39 IoU with the same number of primitives. Against GaussianFormer (144000 Gaussians), QuadricFormer (1600 superquadrics) surpasses it by +1.02 mIoU (20.12 vs. 19.10) while using 90× fewer primitives. On KITTI-360, QuadricFormer (12800 prims) slightly trails GaussianFormer-2 (38400 prims) in mIoU (13.63 vs. 13.90) but leads in IoU (38.89 vs. 38.37) with 3× fewer primitives. In the efficiency table, QuadricFormer at 1600 primitives (162 ms, 2554 MB) is roughly 2× faster and uses 20% less memory than GaussianFormer-2 at the same count (341 ms, 3075 MB), and even at 12800 primitives (179 ms, 2563 MB) it remains faster and lighter than GaussianFormer-2 at just 1600 primitives. QuadricFormer also outperforms all voxel-based and planar baselines (OccFormer, SurroundOcc, TPVFormer, BEVFormer, MonoScene, Atlas) by a wide margin in mIoU, confirming the advantage of object-centric superquadric representations over dense grids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the probabilistic primitive-mixture framework of GaussianFormer-2 [15] and the iterative quadric-encoder architecture of GaussianFormer [18], replacing 3D Gaussian primitives with superquadrics. The key conceptual departure is that Gaussians impose a fixed ellipsoidal iso-probability surface (Eq. 3), whereas superquadrics introduce two shape exponents (ε₁, ε₂) that allow continuous interpolation between cuboids, cylinders, spheres, and star-like profiles (Eq. 4), enabling complex geometries to be captured with far fewer primitives. The overall training pipeline—image backbone, deformable attention for cross-modal interaction, 3D sparse convolution for self-encoding, and residual attribute updates—remains structurally identical to the Gaussian-based predecessors, making the contribution primarily a representation swap within an established architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency–accuracy trade-off: 1600 superquadrics match or exceed the accuracy of 12800 Gaussians while running at roughly half the latency (162 ms vs. 341 ms) and lower memory, which matters for real-time autonomous driving deployment. The pruning-and-splitting module further concentrates primitives in occupied regions, addressing the random-initialization limitation the authors acknowledge. Open questions include whether depth- or detection-based initialization could eliminate the wasted primitives in empty space, whether the approach generalizes to 4D occupancy forecasting or end-to-end planning pipelines, and whether the superquadric shape prior is sufficient for highly irregular or deformable objects (e.g., pedestrians in diverse poses) that may still require many primitives.
