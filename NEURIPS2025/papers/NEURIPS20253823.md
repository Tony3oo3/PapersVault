## Problem

Existing vision-language models produce coarse-grained, unstructured chains of thought that are disorganized and difficult to evaluate at intermediate steps, making it hard to provide fine-grained reward signals for reinforcement learning or to select high-quality reasoning paths during inference. The paper addresses two sub-problems: how to decompose reasoning into logically coherent steps, and how to score each step's quality to enable effective RL training and inference-time scaling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253823/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a structured reasoning chain followed by a final answer. The reasoning chain is delimited by 11 special tokens and decomposed into a variable number of self-generated steps, each containing three sub-components (Name, Thought, Reflection) that are individually parseable. This rigid token-based structure—rather than prompt-based formatting—ensures reliable step splitting for downstream PRM scoring and step-level beam search.

## Method

The framework has three stages: (1) SFT on ShareGPT-Step-300K, a 300K-sample dataset of GPT-4o-generated step-by-step reasoning across 17 multimodal tasks, which teaches the model the structured Name/Thought/Reflection format; (2) training a Process Reward Model (InternVL-2.5-38B) on 200K step-annotated examples produced by both Monte Carlo estimation (Math-Shepherd) and LLM-as-Judge (GPT-4o), enabling per-step correctness scoring; (3) iterative DPO where 16 sampled reasoning paths per question are scored by the PRM using a weighted combination of step scores and answer scores, and positive-negative pairs are selected with a margin threshold to drive preference optimization over multiple rounds. At inference time, a step-level beam search leverages the structured output to sample and score steps sequentially, achieving the same sample budget as Best-of-N while exploiting intermediate structure for more accurate path selection.

## Results

On six multimodal reasoning benchmarks, CoS (built on InternVL-2.5-MPO-8B) achieves an average score of 73.4, with standout results of 81.0 on M3CoT, 87.4 on ChartQA, and 67.8 on MathVista. Applied to LLaVA-NeXT-8B, the method yields a 4.9% average improvement (64.2 vs. 52.1 baseline), while on InternVL-2.5-MPO-8B it delivers a 3.2% average gain (73.4 vs. 69.4). The iterative DPO stage contributes the majority of the improvement, particularly for stronger base models where SFT alone yields only marginal gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoS (73.4 avg) outperforms all 15 baselines across every benchmark where scores are reported. Against the strongest full-benchmark baselines, CoS leads InternVL-2.5 (68.0) by 5.4 points, Insight-V (65.7) by 7.7, Qwen2-VL (65.1) by 8.3, and LLaVA-OneVision (64.6) by 8.8 on average. The largest single-benchmark gains appear on M3CoT, where CoS reaches 81.0 versus 62.4 for InternVL-2.5 and 61.5 for Insight-V, reflecting the benefit of fine-grained step-level rewards for multi-step reasoning. On MathVista, CoS achieves 67.8, surpassing R1-Onevision (64.1) and R1-VL (63.5) by 3.7 and 4.3 points respectively. The ablation in Table 4 further confirms that the structured CoS reasoning pattern with PRM rewards (+4.0 over SFT) substantially outperforms both no-reasoning (+2.1) and direct-prompting (+2.7) baselines under identical training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NLP process-reward-model and iterative DPO paradigm (Math-Shepherd for MC step annotation; iterative DPO for preference learning) to vision-language models, but introduces a key structural departure: a fine-grained Chain-of-Step template (Name/Thought/Reflection with special tokens) that makes individual reasoning steps explicitly parseable and scorable. Unlike prior VLM reasoning work (LLaVA-CoT, Insight-V, Visual-CoT) that produces unstructured, coarse-grained thought chains, CoS enforces discrete, evaluable steps, enabling both PRM-based fine-grained rewards during training and step-level beam search at inference. The approach also builds on the inference-time scaling literature (best-of-N, self-consistency) by showing that structured step-level search achieves the same sampling budget as best-of-N while yielding consistent gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, reproducible framework (300K structured dataset, trained PRM, iterative DPO pipeline) that sets strong baselines for VLM reasoning and demonstrates that fine-grained step rewards consistently outperform outcome-only rewards across multiple architectures. A notable open question is whether the reliance on GPT-4o and Monte Carlo estimation for process labels (acknowledged as imperfect) limits the ceiling of the approach, and whether human-quality process annotations would yield further gains. The finding that step-wise DPO (constructing preference pairs at every step) actively harms performance, despite being the natural fine-grained extension, highlights that the gap between step-level evaluation and step-level training remains unresolved.
