## Problem

Existing unsupervised 3D representation learning for LiDAR relies on masked autoencoding or contrastive learning, both of which implicitly select nuisance invariants through handcrafted augmentations or masking strategies. These approaches neglect the temporal LiDAR sequence, which naturally encodes object motion, semantics, and ego-vehicle interactions. The paper asks whether temporal forecasting of future LiDAR observations can serve as a superior unsupervised pre-training signal that lets the data itself determine which nuisance variables to suppress.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a current LiDAR point cloud (xyz coordinates plus modality-specific features such as intensity for Once or intensity+elongation for Waymo) and ego-vehicle actions (relative Δx, Δy translation and Δθ rotation between consecutive frames) as input. During pre-training it produces forecasted point clouds at future timestamps as range and intensity values via differentiable rendering; the final output is a pre-trained sparse-convolution 3D encoder (128-channel dense voxel features at D×H×W resolution) that initializes downstream detectors or segmenters. All point clouds are aligned to the t₀ coordinate frame, and ground points are filtered by a z-threshold before ray sampling.

## Method

TREND combines two novel components: (1) a Recurrent Embedding scheme that sinusoidally encodes ego-vehicle actions, projects them through a shared MLP, concatenates with the previous timestamp's 3D embedding, and applies a shared 3D dense convolution to propagate features forward in time—explicitly modeling ego-motion effects on scene dynamics; and (2) a Temporal LiDAR Neural Field that takes trilinearly-interpolated 3D features, sinusoidally-encoded timestamps, and query point positions to predict geometry features and signed distance values via MLPs. Differentiable rendering integrates SDF-derived occupancy along sampled rays to predict range, while a separate intensity network fuses ray direction, geometry features, and queried features to predict LiDAR intensity. Training uses a curriculum that gradually extends the forecasting horizon (1→2 frames over 48 epochs) and an L1 loss on range, intensity, and SDF at observed points. Unlike prior forecasting work (4DOCC, Uno, T-MAE), TREND incorporates ego actions and models LiDAR-specific characteristics (intensity, surface geometry) rather than treating the scene as a camera-oriented radiance field.

## Results

On the Once dataset with 5% labeled data for fine-tuning, TREND achieves 47.84 mAP, a +1.77 improvement over random initialization (46.07) and up to 400% more gain than the previous SOTA pre-training method UniPAD (+0.16). On NuScenes, TREND reaches 33.17 mAP and 46.21 NDS, improving by +2.11 mAP and +1.46 NDS over random initialization—91% and 94% more improvement than UniPAD respectively. On SemanticKITTI, TREND achieves 31.12 mIoU (+2.89) and 79.82% accuracy (+9.14) over the from-scratch baseline, outperforming all other pre-training methods. In cross-dataset transfer (Once→Waymo), TREND yields a +0.77 average gain in mAP/mAPH over random initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TREND outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and fine-tuning ratio tested. On Once (5% labels), TREND's +1.77 mAP gain is roughly 11× larger than UniPAD's +0.16 and dramatically exceeds the negative gains of ALSO (−1.38), Occ-MAE (−1.64), 4DOCC (−5.23), and T-MAE (−0.95), which all degrade performance below random initialization. On NuScenes, TREND achieves 33.17 mAP vs. UniPAD's 32.16, T-MAE's 30.53, and 4DOCC's 26.99, with the gap over UniPAD representing a 91% relative improvement in pre-training benefit. On SemanticKITTI segmentation, TREND leads UniPAD by 1.13 mIoU (31.12 vs. 29.98) and Occ-MAE by 3.30 mIoU. In the cross-dataset transfer experiment (Once→Waymo), TREND gains +0.77 average mAP/mAPH while UniPAD is essentially at parity with random initialization (−0.03). The only category where TREND shows a slight degradation is Pedestrian at 5% and 20% on Once, which the authors attribute to the cylinder-like geometry of pedestrians in LiDAR being confusable with poles and bins.

## Relation to Prior Work

TREND repurposes LiDAR point cloud forecasting—previously studied as a primary perception task (4DOCC, Uno, Copilot4D)—as an unsupervised pre-training signal for 3D encoders, departing from the dominant masked-autoencoding and contrastive-learning paradigms. It adapts the differentiable rendering pipeline from NeuS to a temporal, LiDAR-specific neural field that models both signed distance and intensity, and introduces a recurrent embedding scheme that conditions temporal feature evolution on ego-vehicle actions, a factor absent from prior forecasting and pre-training work. Conceptually, the paper frames temporal dynamics as a data-driven alternative to handcrafted nuisance-variability sets used in contrastive and MAE-based pre-training, arguing that predicting scene evolution implicitly encodes object semantics and interaction biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 400% relative improvement over prior SOTA pre-training on Once (1.77% mAP) and consistent gains across four datasets and two downstream tasks demonstrate that temporal forecasting is a substantially more informative pre-training signal than reconstruction or contrast for LiDAR, while incurring only ~8% additional compute and no extra memory. However, the absolute gains remain modest (1–2% mAP), and the method shows class-dependent behavior—pedestrian detection degrades in low-data regimes due to cylinder-like geometric ambiguity in LiDAR. Open questions include whether the ego-action conditioning is truly critical beyond providing a temporal inductive bias, whether the ~2-second forecasting horizon can be extended without representation degradation, and whether the approach generalizes to non-autonomous-driving LiDAR scenarios.
