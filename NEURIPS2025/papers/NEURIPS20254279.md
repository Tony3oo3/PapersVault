## Problem

Despite a growing number of backdoor attack and defense methods for diffusion models, no comprehensive benchmark exists to enable fair comparison, systematic taxonomy, or standardized evaluation. Unlike discriminative models where backdoor targets are limited to a single class label, DMs expose multiple attack vectors (noise and text triggers) and diverse target types (object replacement, style modification, patch insertion), making existing conclusions from classification-based backdoor research inapplicable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254279/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds text prompts and Gaussian noise (optionally with injected triggers) into unconditional DDPM pipelines (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, CelebA-HQ at 256×256) or conditional Stable Diffusion v1.5/v2.0 (LAION-Aesthetics 40k for training, MS-COCO 10k for evaluation). Outputs are generated images scored by a unified GPT-4o-based evaluator that performs visual question answering in a single inference run to produce ASR, PSR, and ACC, supplemented by traditional metrics (MSE, FID, LPIPS, TCS, BCS) and attack-efficiency measures (runtime, poisoning ratio).

## Method

The paper proposes a unified loss formulation (Eq. 1) with hyperparameters α, β, γ that subsumes all nine implemented attacks as special cases, distinguishing text-trigger loss, noise-trigger loss, and utility preservation. A systematic taxonomy classifies attacks along two axes: trigger modality (noise vs. text, with or without an external target image set S) and target type (ImageFix, ImagePatch, ObjectAdd, ObjectRep, StyleAdd). The key evaluation novelty is an MLLM-based protocol where GPT-4o extracts key objects from prompts, generates binary Yes/No questions, and computes ASR (target presence), PSR (non-target content preservation), and ACC (clean-input fidelity) from the fraction of positive answers—eliminating the need for task-specific classifiers. Three visualization tools (cross-attention assimilation, activation norm divergence, pre-activation bimodality) are adapted to reveal internal backdoor mechanisms in both unconditional and T2I DMs.

## Results

Across five backdoor target types, no single attack method dominates all metrics. For ImageFix, VillanDiffusion achieves the best balance (MSE 0.0300, FID 13.50, runtime 4017s), while VillanCond achieves the lowest MSE (0.0010) at the cost of FID 28.81. For ImagePatch, Pixel-Backdoor (BadT2I) overwhelmingly outperforms BiBadDiff, achieving ASR_GPT 99.6% vs. 34.10% and FID 21.34 vs. 88.50. For ObjectRep, EvilEdit achieves the highest PSR_GPT (85.25%) and TCS (26.68) with the fastest runtime (16.59s, zero data), while TPA (RickRolling) achieves the highest ASR_GPT (96.80%) but the lowest PSR_GPT (5.50%). For StyleAdd, both methods achieve high ASR_GPT (>91%) and ACC_GPT (>84%), with Style-Backdoor (BadT2I) preserving more non-target content (PSR_GPT 90.68% vs. 65.92%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 9 attack methods and 4 defense methods across 5 target types. For ImageFix, VillanDiffusion [14] outperforms BadDiffusion [4] (MSE 0.0300 vs. 0.0200, FID 13.50 vs. 18.21) and InviBackdoor [15] (MSE 0.0300 vs. 0.0950, FID 13.50 vs. 58.19), while TrojDiff [13] is the slowest (83197s vs. ~4000s for others). For ImagePatch, Pixel-Backdoor (BadT2I) [9] dominates BiBadDiff [38] on every metric (ASR_GPT 99.6% vs. 34.10%, FID 21.34 vs. 88.50, runtime 25266s vs. 62421s). For ObjectRep, the five methods trade off differently: TPA (RickRolling) [5] wins on ASR (96.80%) but fails on PSR (5.50%), EvilEdit [7] wins on PSR (85.25%) and runtime (16.59s, zero data) but has moderate ASR (61.10%), and TI (PaaS) [16] achieves the best LPIPS (0.0055) with only 6 poisoned samples. For StyleAdd, Style-Backdoor (BadT2I) [9] outperforms TAA (RickRolling) [5] on PSR (90.68% vs. 65.92%) and TCS (27.48 vs. 24.02) but is 55× slower (30170s vs. 543s). Among defenses, input-level methods (Textual Perturbations [24], TERD [25]) prove more effective than model-level methods (Elijah [27], T2IShield [29]) for both unconditional and T2I backdoors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established backdoor learning paradigm from discriminative models (where attacks are limited to single-modality inputs and a single target type—misclassification) to the substantially more complex setting of diffusion models, which involve multiple input modalities, a time-step dimension, and diverse target types. It unifies nine previously independent attack methods (BadDiffusion, TrojDiff, VillanDiffusion, BadT2I, EvilEdit, RickRolling, PaaS, InviBackdoor, BiBadDiff) under a single optimization formulation (Equation 1), showing they are all special cases parameterized by hyper-parameters α, β, γ and the target mapping π_S. The MLLM-based evaluation approach repurposes the T2I faithfulness evaluation paradigm (TIFA, LLMScore) for backdoor assessment, replacing the brittle practice of training separate binary classifiers per target type.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first comprehensive benchmark for DM backdoors, BackdoorDM provides the standardized pipeline and fair comparison infrastructure that the field has lacked, directly addressing the authors' observation that the absence of such a benchmark 'hinders future research progress.' Key findings—particularly that model-level defenses (Elijah, T2IShield) show limited effectiveness while input-level defenses are more effective, and that non-conflicting targets (ImageFix, StyleAdd) are systematically easier to attack than conflicting ones (ObjectRep)—provide actionable guidance for both attack and defense research. The benchmark is currently limited to unconditional and T2I diffusion models; extending it to TTS, T2V, and other generative modalities remains an open challenge, as does developing defenses that can handle the full diversity of backdoor target types.
