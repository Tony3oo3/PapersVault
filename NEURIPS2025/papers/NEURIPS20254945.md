## Problem

Counterfactual identification for high-dimensional multivariate outcomes (e.g., images) from observational data remains an open problem. Prior work established identifiability for bijective causal mechanisms only in the d=1 monotone case or for d>1 under the Backdoor Criterion, leaving the Markovian multivariate setting unresolved. Existing deep generative models for counterfactual inference produce promising estimates but lack identification guarantees, undermining their causal validity.

## Input / Output

The method takes an observed image (192×192 chest X-ray from MIMIC-CXR) along with parent variable values (sex, race, age, disease) and an intervention specification (e.g., do(RACE=r)) as input, and produces a counterfactual image at the same resolution. Counterfactual inference is computed by solving the learned ODE backwards in time for abduction (recovering the exogenous noise u) and forwards for prediction under the intervened parents. In the controlled ellipse experiment, inputs are 2D Cartesian coordinates with an angular parent parameter, and outputs are the full set of counterfactual points tracing the ellipse.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution shows that parameterising the causal mechanism as the time-1 map of a dynamic optimal transport flow (Benamou-Brenier formulation) yields a unique, monotone, and rank-preserving counterfactual transport map T*(pa*, pa, x) = T(T⁻¹(x; pa); pa*), by invoking Brenier's theorem (T = ∇φ for convex φ) and Caffarelli regularity conditions. Practically, the flow is a continuous normalizing flow (neural ODE) with a UNet-parameterised velocity field, trained via flow matching with linear interpolation paths. A key architectural contribution is a bespoke Markovian Batch-OT coupling: rather than naively pairing exogenous noise samples with observations (which implicitly entangles U and PA), the method solves the OT problem separately for each fixed parent value, preserving the U ⊥ PA independence required for valid abduction. This yields a family of conditional OT couplings π^(m)(u,x|pa) that are integrated over the parent distribution during training. For non-Markovian settings, the framework extends to Instrumental Variable, Backdoor, and Frontdoor criteria, where bijectivity alone (without monotonicity) suffices under the stated variability conditions.

## Results

On the controlled counterfactual ellipse task (where ground-truth counterfactuals are known), the proposed OT-FLOW achieves a mean average percentage error (μ_APE) of 0.76% in the Markovian setting with 50 function evaluations, compared to 607% for the previous Markovian baseline (Nasr-Esfahany et al., 2023) and 1.0% for their Backdoor-Criterion baseline. On MIMIC-CXR chest X-rays (192×192), the method achieves |Δ_AUC| of 0.050% for RACE interventions (vs. 8.640% baseline) and 0.023% for DISEASE interventions (vs. 2.490% baseline), demonstrating substantial improvements in counterfactual effectiveness without requiring counterfactual fine-tuning or classifier-free guidance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Nasr-Esfahany et al. (2023), the proposed OT-FLOW dramatically outperforms the Markovian baseline (0.76% vs. 607% μ_APE) and matches or slightly beats their Backdoor-Criterion result (0.76% vs. 1.0%), while requiring no confounder variable. On MIMIC-CXR, the method substantially outperforms Ribeiro et al. (2023) on RACE interventions (|Δ_AUC| = 0.050% vs. 8.640%) and DISEASE interventions (0.023% vs. 2.490%), with modest gains on SEX (0.173% vs. 0.370%). However, on AGE interventions the proposed method is slightly worse (0.333% vs. 0.288% Δ_MAE), and the authors note that OT-FLOW underperforms plain FLOW on disease interventions, suggesting non-Markovian interaction effects or subpar OT approximation in that regime. Overall, the paper demonstrates that the Markovian OT coupling is essential: the naive Batch-OT flow (which violates U⊥PA) performs comparably to non-OT baselines, while the corrected coupling yields near-exact ground-truth counterfactuals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Nasr-Esfahany et al. (2023), who established counterfactual identifiability for bijective causal mechanisms but explicitly left open the multivariate (d>1) Markovian case, stating 'it is not clear how to generalise the monotonicity condition to multi-dimensional variables.' The key conceptual move is importing Brenier's theorem and the Benamou-Brenier dynamic OT formulation into the SCM framework, showing that the time-1 dynamic OT map provides a natural, coordinate-free multivariate generalisation of scalar monotonicity. On the methodological side, the paper builds on flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and Batch-OT flow matching (Pooladian et al., 2023), but introduces a bespoke Markovian coupling that preserves the U⊥PA independence constraint, which standard Batch-OT implicitly violates. The IV and Frontdoor identifiability results for d>1 also extend and complement Nasr-Esfahany et al.'s earlier d=1 IV and d≥1 BC results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first counterfactual identifiability guarantees for high-dimensional Markovian SCMs from purely observational data, filling a gap that undermines the causal validity of all prior deep generative counterfactual methods (Pawlowski et al., 2020; Ribeiro et al., 2023; Sanchez and Tsaftaris, 2021). Without such identification, observationally equivalent models can yield different counterfactual answers, making causal claims unjustifiable. A key open question is scaling: the Markovian Batch-OT coupling requires solving OT per parent value, which becomes prohibitive in high dimensions, and the MIMIC-CXR results hint that non-Markovian interaction effects (e.g., disease interventions) may violate the assumptions in practice. The paper also cautions that axiomatic soundness metrics (composition, effectiveness, reversibility) do not constitute evidence of identification, urging practitioners to verify identifiability conditions before making causal claims.
