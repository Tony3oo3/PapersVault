## Problem

Existing few-shot learning methods that address Support-Query Shift (SQS) only handle inter-set distribution shifts between support and query sets, while overlooking intra-set shifts where instances within the same set originate from different domains. This paper introduces the more general and challenging Dual Support-Query Shift (DSQS) setting, where both inter-set and intra-set shifts occur simultaneously during meta-testing, blurring decision boundaries and degrading classification accuracy far beyond what prior SQS mitigation methods can handle.

## Input / Output

The framework takes RGB images from support and query sets (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for mini-ImageNet, 84×84 for Tiered-ImageNet) that may carry arbitrary pixel-level shifts such as blur, fog, noise, or device-specific distortions, and outputs class labels for query samples via a model-agnostic comparison module (e.g., ProtoNet or MatchingNet). The method operates in a 16-way 1-shot or 5-shot episodic setting, where the support set provides 1 or 5 labeled examples per class and the query set contains 16 unlabeled instances to be classified. A key design choice is that the repairer and embedding function are trained on predefined shifts during training but applied to arbitrary unknown shifts at inference, making the framework bias-agnostic.

## Method

DUAL combines two complementary mechanisms to obtain clean features and perform reliable distribution alignment. First, Dual Adversarial Training (Dual AT) trains a pixel-level repairer R (a REDNet-like encoder-decoder) to denoise shifted images by minimizing feature-space distance to the original, and a robust embedding function φ via a semantic-aware generator G that produces hard examples—perturbed samples that are maximally dissimilar in embedding space yet retain correct class labels, enforced through a KL-divergence constraint. Second, Dual Regularized Optimal Transportation (Dual OT) performs a two-stage alignment: an intra-set plan π′ transports support instances toward their class-wise centroids to reduce intra-class variance, and an inter-set plan π* aligns the query distribution to the transported support set, both using negative-entropy regularization solved via Sinkhorn iterations. The theoretical analysis proves that the repairer contracts feature noise linearly (σ′ ≤ Lε_p), the adversarial training enforces a deterministic margin κ that shrinks class-conditional covariances, and together these effects reduce the 2-Wasserstein distance between support and query distributions, yielding a provably lower classification risk bound.

## Results

On the three main FSL benchmarks (CIFAR-100, mini-ImageNet, Tiered-ImageNet), DUAL-P achieves top-1 accuracy of 38.93% / 53.00% / 34.29% in the 1-shot setting and 54.47% / 67.83% / 47.81% in the 5-shot setting, outperforming all 10 baselines. The paper reports an average improvement of 25.66% over state-of-the-art methods across the three datasets. In the cross-domain Meta-Dataset evaluation (Table 3), DUAL-P also leads on all 10 out-of-domain datasets, e.g., reaching 50.71% on Omniglot and 60.45% on VGG Flower in the 1-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUAL-P outperforms the strongest robust FSL baseline RAS [35] by +2.60% on mini-ImageNet 1-shot (53.00 vs. 50.40) and +3.88% on mini-ImageNet 5-shot (67.83 vs. 63.95), and by +4.45% on CIFAR-100 5-shot (54.47 vs. 50.02). Against the best support-query shift baselines, DUAL-P beats PGADA [21] by +2.63% on mini-ImageNet 1-shot and +6.74% on mini-ImageNet 5-shot, and surpasses TP [3] by +7.34% on mini-ImageNet 1-shot (53.00 vs. 45.66). The gap is most pronounced against AQP [1] and SSL-ProtoNet [28], where DUAL-P leads by over 20% on mini-ImageNet 1-shot (53.00 vs. 30.59 and 28.59, respectively). On Tiered-ImageNet, DUAL-P achieves the best 1-shot accuracy (34.29%) and 5-shot accuracy (47.81%), exceeding the next-best AQP by +3.89% and +2.47% respectively. In the cross-domain Meta-Dataset evaluation, DUAL-P dominates all 10 out-of-domain datasets, e.g., +17.90% over PGADA on Omniglot (50.71 vs. 32.81) and +10.52% on VGG Flower (60.45 vs. 49.93), confirming that the dual alignment strategy generalizes beyond the three primary benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Support-Query Shift (SQS) line of work in few-shot learning (TP, PGADA, AQP), which previously addressed only inter-set distribution shifts between support and query sets, by introducing the Dual Support-Query Shift (DSQS) formulation that additionally accounts for intra-set shifts within each set. Methodologically, it combines a pixel-level repairer (inspired by image restoration networks like REDNet) with adversarial training of the embedding function (building on robust FSL methods like AQ and RAS), then applies a two-stage entropy-regularized optimal transport scheme for alignment. The key conceptual departure from prior SQS work is the decoupled design: the repairer handles pixel-level noise while the generator challenges the encoder at the feature level, rather than conflating both roles in a single adversarial loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DSQS formulation is more faithful to real-world deployment scenarios where both device/environment differences (inter-set) and within-batch variability (intra-set) coexist, and the 25.66% average improvement over 10 baselines across three datasets demonstrates practical value. However, the repairer requires predefined shift types during training, limiting its applicability to truly unknown corruptions, and the computational overhead (up to 18.5 hours for mini-ImageNet training) is non-trivial. Open questions include whether the framework extends to video or multi-modal FSL, whether more sophisticated OT formulations (e.g., unbalanced or entropic variants) could further reduce the transport cost, and whether the decoupled repairer-generator design generalizes beyond the specific shift types tested.
