## Problem

State-of-the-art diffusion-based video generation models for autonomous driving achieve high visual realism (e.g., low FVD) yet produce subtle geometric distortions—shifted vanishing points, inconsistent lane topology, and incorrect depth—that cause a significant drop in downstream 3D object detection (mAP 25.7 vs. 35.5 on real data). No prior work systematically quantifies these distortions or provides a mechanism to correct them during generation, as standard pixel-level MSE training objectives ignore higher-order geometric-semantic relationships.

## Input / Output

The diffusion model takes driving scene conditions (3D bounding boxes, road layout) and generates multi-camera driving video. During RLGF fine-tuning, the reward is computed on noisy latent features z_k at intermediate denoising steps (not the final decoded video), compared against VAE-encoded latents of the corresponding real video. A lightweight micro-decode module (shallow VAE decoder layers) converts noisy latents into per-frame features suitable for perception, avoiding costly full decoding. The reward signal is hierarchical: point-level (vanishing point L2 distance), line-level (lane F1-score), plane-level (masked depth RMSE for road and vehicle regions), and scene-level (KL divergence on occupancy features + 3D occupancy IoU).

## Method

RLGF fine-tunes a frozen video diffusion model via LoRA (rank 16 on DiT attention layers) using a reinforcement learning objective where rewards come from two pre-trained latent-space perception models: a multi-task geometry model (DINOv2 backbone predicting VP heatmap, lane segmentation, and depth) and an occupancy prediction model (predicting 3D occupancy grids and intermediate features). The key efficiency mechanism is Latent-Space Windowing Optimization: instead of unrolling the full T-step diffusion chain, rewards are applied to noisy latents within a randomly positioned sliding window of w=5 steps (start sampled from [8,30] out of T=30), reducing backpropagation memory while still providing targeted corrective signals at both coarse-structure and detail-refinement stages. The Hierarchical Geometric Reward combines five components—VP consistency, lane topology F1, masked depth RMSE, occupancy feature alignment via KL divergence, and 3D occupancy IoU—each weighted to balance geometric fidelity across scales. Perception model weights remain frozen throughout training, ensuring a consistent evaluation standard as the LoRA parameters adapt.

## Results

On nuScenes validation, RLGF applied to DiVE boosts 3D object detection mAP from 25.75 to 31.42 (+5.67) and NDS from 33.61 to 36.07, while reducing VP error by 21% (0.086→0.068) and depth RMSE by 57% (1.822→0.772). Applied to MagicDrive-v2, RLGF improves mAP from 18.95 to 23.21 (+4.26) and NDS from 21.10 to 27.80 (+6.70). Visual quality is preserved (FVD: 68.4→67.6 for DiVE; 101.2→99.8 for MagicDrive-v2), and the gap to real-data 3D detection performance (mAP 35.53) narrows substantially, from ~10 points to ~4 points for DiVE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLGF is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on two baselines: DiVE and MagicDrive-v2. Against DiVE, RLGF wins decisively on all metrics: +5.67 mAP, +2.46 NDS, 21% lower VP error, 11% higher Lane F1, and 57% lower depth RMSE, while maintaining comparable FVD (67.6 vs. 68.4). Against MagicDrive-v2, RLGF also wins across the board: +4.26 mAP, +6.70 NDS, 14% lower VP error, 8.5% higher Lane F1, and 43% lower depth RMSE. Compared to the other standalone generators (Panacea, Drive-WM), DiVE+RLGF achieves the best 3D detection mAP (31.42 vs. 20.66 and 11.58) and the best GeoScores, while also having the lowest FVD. The remaining gap to real data (mAP 35.53) shrinks from 9.78 points (DiVE alone) to 4.11 points (DiVE+RLGF), confirming that geometric fidelity is the primary bottleneck for synthetic-data 3D perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-diffusion paradigm introduced by VADER (Black et al., ICCV 2023), which demonstrated that LoRA-based reinforcement learning can fine-tune diffusion models using reward signals, by replacing generic preference rewards with domain-specific geometric feedback from pre-trained AD perception models. It operates within the latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), leveraging the VAE latent space for both generation and reward computation. The key departure from prior AD video generation work (Panacea, DriveWM, MagicDrive, DiVE) is the explicit targeting of geometric fidelity—vanishing point consistency, lane topology, depth coherence, and 3D occupancy—rather than the pixel-level visual realism (FVD) that those methods optimize. The Latent-Space Windowing Optimization further differentiates from full-rollout RL-diffusion by applying rewards at intermediate noisy latents within a sliding window, reducing memory cost while enabling targeted feedback at different stages of structure formation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a critical gap that the AD video generation community has largely overlooked: visually realistic synthetic videos (high FVD) can contain pervasive geometric distortions that severely degrade 3D perception (mAP drops from 35.5 to 25.7) while leaving 2D detection nearly unaffected (43.8 vs 44.7). RLGF's plug-and-play nature—requiring only LoRA fine-tuning with frozen perception models—makes it immediately applicable to existing video generation pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (nuScenes) and two baselines, the latent-space depth estimation is notably weaker than its pixel-space counterpart (RMSE 2.596 vs 1.798), and the reward weights are manually tuned. Open questions include generalization to adverse weather, night scenes, and other datasets, as well as whether jointly adapting perception models during RL could further improve results.
