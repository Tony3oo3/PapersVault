## Problem

Existing gradient coding schemes for distributed learning either assume homogeneous straggler probabilities across all workers or rely on excessive data replication to tolerate a known number of stragglers, both of which are unrealistic in heterogeneous systems. Moreover, prior methods that minimize residual error often lack rigorous convergence guarantees, while those ensuring unbiasedness (e.g., SGC) use binary decoding coefficients and impose pairwise distribution constraints that can become infeasible. This paper bridges these gaps by designing an optimally structured approximate gradient code that simultaneously minimizes residual error, ensures an unbiased estimator, and explicitly accounts for per-worker straggler heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

The method takes n data partitions, per-worker straggler probabilities p_i (modeled via shifted-exponential distributions with parameters ψ_i sampled from a uniform range), and current model parameters β_t as input. It produces a sparse encoding matrix A (k×n) and decoding vector w (k×1) that are fixed across iterations, yielding an estimated gradient sum ĝ^(t) used for parameter updates. A key design constraint is that the average computation load d = 1 + (k−1)/n remains strictly below 2, meaning each data partition is replicated to at most two workers on average, and the worst-case per-worker load is bounded by n−k+2.

## Method

The core contribution is a convex reformulation of the gradient code design problem: by substituting α_{i,j} = (1−p_i)·w_i·a_{i,j}, the non-convex joint optimization of encoding and decoding coefficients becomes a convex problem (P3) solvable via KKT conditions. Theorem 1 characterizes the optimal structure—each row i of α must sum to Y_i = δ_i^{-1}·n/Σ_j δ_j^{-1} (where δ_i = p_i/(1−p_i)) and each column must sum to 1—directly linking the code to straggler heterogeneity. Two closed-form constructions (Scheme I: centralized sharing with one common partition; Scheme II: sequential adjacent sharing) realize this structure with d < 2. Convergence is proven for λ-strongly convex losses (O(1/T) rate), μ-smooth losses (average gradient norm → 0), and the combined case, with error bounds depending only on straggler probabilities rather than computation load.

## Results

On the COCO dataset with MobileNetV3 (k=10 workers, τ_th=1.1), the proposed Schemes I and II achieve convergence rates closely matching centralized GD, while all other baselines (BGC, EHD, OD, IS-SGD) suffer significant convergence degradation due to stragglers. The proposed method operates at a computation load of d = 1 + (k-1)/n < 2, compared to d ≈ 2 for all benchmark gradient coding methods. Theoretical analysis guarantees O(1/T) convergence for λ-strongly convex losses with an error bound scaled by 1/Σᵢδᵢ⁻¹, and convergence to a stationary point for μ-smooth losses. Qualitative detection results (Figure 5) confirm the proposed method identifies the same objects as straggler-free GD (3 objects), while SGC duplicates detections and EHD/BGC/OD miss objects entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four gradient coding baselines (BGC, EHD, OD, SGC) in convergence speed across all tested configurations (k=10 and k=100, τ_th=1.1 and 1.5). BGC [8], EHD [9], and OD [11] all suffer from poor convergence under straggler conditions because they do not enforce unbiasedness of the gradient estimator; the paper notes that 'except for SGC, all benchmark methods suffer from poor convergence due to the adverse impact of stragglers.' SGC [12] is the only baseline that guarantees unbiasedness and thus avoids the worst convergence failures, but it still converges more slowly than the proposed schemes because it does not minimize the residual error (variance) of the estimator. The proposed method also achieves this at a strictly lower computation load (d < 2 vs. d ≈ 2 for all benchmarks), as shown in Figure 4 where convergence is normalized by computation load. In the object-detection qualitative comparison (Figure 5), the proposed method matches GD's detection quality exactly, while SGC produces a duplicated parking meter, EHD/BGC/OD each detect only one object, and IS-SGD misidentifies objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines of gradient coding research: (1) residual-error-minimizing schemes (Raviv et al., Charles et al., Glasgow et al.) that lack rigorous convergence guarantees, and (2) unbiasedness-preserving schemes like SGC (Bitar et al.) that use binary decoding and impose pairwise distribution constraints that can be infeasible. The key departure is formulating a joint optimization that simultaneously minimizes residual error and enforces unbiasedness under a heterogeneous straggler model (non-uniform p_i per worker), yielding closed-form encoding/decoding coefficients via Lagrangian duality. The resulting Schemes I and II achieve computation load d < 2, well below the d ≥ s+1 requirement of exact gradient coding (Tandon et al.) and the d ≈ 2 of prior approximate methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving GD-level convergence with less than 2× data replication in realistic heterogeneous clusters, where prior methods either required knowing exact straggler counts or assumed uniform straggling probabilities. The convergence bound depending only on straggler probabilities (not computation load) is a clean theoretical result that decouples robustness from redundancy. Key limitations include the need to know p_i a priori (addressed only heuristically via MLE or parametric fitting), initial data-distribution communication overhead, and the acknowledged difficulty of extending the unbiasedness guarantee to adaptive optimizers like Adam (partially addressed via a two-track decoder). Open questions include validating the scheme on larger, more diverse workloads and developing principled online estimation of heterogeneous straggler statistics.
