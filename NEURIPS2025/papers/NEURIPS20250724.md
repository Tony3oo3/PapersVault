## Problem

Standard contrastive learning treats all augmented views of the same instance as equally similar, enforcing a fixed maximum similarity regardless of augmentation strength. However, strong augmentations (e.g., heavy cropping) can introduce significant semantic degradation or even content distortion, making the uniform similarity assumption a source of misguided supervision that degrades learned representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224 at evaluation) along with their augmentation parameters (e.g., crop ratio, color jitter magnitude, blur kernel size) as auxiliary inputs to an augmentation-aware module. It produces d-dimensional feature embeddings from a ResNet-50 or ViT-Small encoder, which are then used for downstream linear probing, Faster R-CNN detection, logistic-regression few-shot classification, and cosine-similarity retrieval. The augmentation parameters are not part of the encoder input but are fed separately to the auxiliary module to estimate per-view-pair similarity targets.

## Method

CLVS introduces an augmentation-aware module φ (a set of per-augmentation-type MLPs) that maps encoder embeddings into augmentation-sensitive latent spaces, from which a variable similarity is computed as the minimum cosine similarity across all augmentation types—focusing on the transformation causing the greatest semantic shift. Three auxiliary losses are added to the base contrastive objective: (1) a parameter-prediction loss L_d that trains φ to recover augmentation parameter differences, (2) an alignment loss L_ali that pulls the embedding cosine similarity toward the predicted variable similarity via an ℓ₂ objective, and (3) a relative similarity constraint L_re (a hinge loss) that penalizes cases where a strongly augmented view receives higher similarity than a weakly augmented one. A theoretical analysis shows that the variable similarity reduces the statistical variance of pairwise similarities, tightening the generalization error bound. The method is plug-and-play, demonstrated on MoCo, SimSiam, SimCLR, BYOL, SwAV, and MoCo-v3 (ViT) backbones.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 (ResNet-50, 200 epochs), SimSiam+CLVS achieves 86.0% top-1 linear evaluation accuracy, surpassing the best baseline (EquiMod/INTL at 83.9%) by 2.1% and the SimSiam baseline by 5.9%. MoCo+CLVS reaches 84.9%, a +6.1% gain over MoCo. On ImageNet-1k, SimSiam+CLVS achieves 71.4% top-1 accuracy (+1.4% over SimSiam), while MoCo+CLVS reaches 69.9% (+2.4% over MoCo). The method also improves transfer learning across 7 downstream datasets, object detection (VOC/COCO), and few-shot classification (FC100, CUB200, Plant).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimSiam+CLVS (86.0%) outperforms all 8 baselines on ImageNet-100, with the largest margins over MoCo (+7.2%), SimSiam (+5.9%), and AugSelf (+5.5%), and a 2.1% lead over the strongest baselines EquiMod and INTL (both 83.9%). MoCo+CLVS (84.9%) similarly beats every baseline, including EquiMod and INTL by 1.0%. On ImageNet-1k, SimSiam+CLVS (71.4%) leads all methods by 1.4%, while MoCo+CLVS (69.9%) ties with INTL (69.9%) but still surpasses SimSiam by 2.4%. In transfer learning (Table 3), SimSiam+CLVS achieves the best accuracy on all 7 datasets, with gains of +5.42% on CIFAR-100 and +7.02% on Flowers over SimSiam. On object detection (Table 4), MoCo+CLVS leads on COCO (39.35 AP) and VOC (52.22 AP), though CoCor slightly edges it on VOC AP (54.31). For few-shot classification (Table 5), SimSiam+CLVS achieves the best 5-way 1-shot accuracy on FC100 (45.39%) and CUB200 (47.33%), outperforming EquiMod and INTL by 0.4–2.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the augmentation-aware self-supervision line initiated by AugSelf [35], which introduced an auxiliary task to predict augmentation parameter differences. The key departure is that AugSelf only preserves augmentation information in the forward pass, whereas CLVS explicitly modulates the contrastive supervision signal itself by introducing a variable similarity target that dynamically adjusts based on augmentation extent. The method also differentiates from stronger-augmentation approaches (CLSA, RényiCL, CoCor) by explicitly modeling per-augmentation-type similarity rather than treating augmentation strength as a monolithic scalar, and from equivariance-based methods (EquiMod) by using a minimum-similarity aggregation across augmentation types to focus on the most semantically disruptive transformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The variable similarity mechanism is a general-purpose, plug-and-play module that consistently improves a wide range of contrastive learning frameworks (MoCo, SimSiam, SimCLR, BYOL, SwAV, MoCo-v3) across linear evaluation, object detection, few-shot classification, and retrieval, suggesting broad practical utility for practitioners. The theoretical result linking variable similarity to a tighter generalization error bound via reduced similarity variance provides a principled justification beyond empirical gains. However, the method is currently limited to positive pairs and does not extend the variable similarity mechanism to negative samples, which the authors identify as an important open direction. The core intuition (stronger augmentation → lower similarity target) is fairly natural, and the theoretical proof relies on somewhat loose constants, leaving room for tighter analysis.
