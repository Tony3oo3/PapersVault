## Problem

Cross-modal contrastive learning for Cell Painting high-content screening data faces three unresolved challenges: microscopy channels capture semantically independent structures (unlike correlated RGB channels), each perturbation label maps to many images creating false negatives in standard CL, and prior methods (CLOOME, MolPhenix) rely on perturbation-class-specific encoders (e.g., Morgan fingerprints for chemicals) that cannot unify heterogeneous perturbation types like CRISPR knockouts and ORFs in a single model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-channel Cell Painting images (5–8 channels, each highlighting a distinct cellular component such as nuclear DNA or mitochondria) grouped by perturbation, alongside natural language prompts that encode cell type, perturbation name, and class-specific identifiers (SMILES strings for compounds, gene symbols for CRISPR). Output is a pair of aligned embeddings in a shared latent space: a per-perturbation image embedding from CrossChannelFormer and a text embedding from BERT, enabling bidirectional retrieval (perturbation-to-profile and profile-to-perturbation) as well as intra-modal similarity search for biological relationship recovery.

## Method

CellCLIP's image encoder, CrossChannelFormer, operates in three stages: (1) a frozen DINOv2 extracts per-channel embeddings treating each microscopy channel as an independent grayscale image; (2) ABMIL gated-attention pooling aggregates all images within a perturbation into a single per-channel profile, resolving the many-to-one structure and filtering false-positive cells; (3) a compact 12-layer transformer with learnable channel embeddings reasons across the C+1 pooled channel tokens (rather than spatial patches), capturing inter-channel relationships at a fraction of the cost of channel-agnostic ViTs. Perturbations are encoded by a pretrained BERT over templated text prompts, providing a class-agnostic representation that unifies chemicals, CRISPR, and ORFs. Training uses an asymmetric objective: a Continuously Weighted Contrastive Loss (CWCL) for the profile-to-perturbation direction, where soft labels are computed from channel-wise cosine similarity between pooled profiles to preserve biological structure, combined with a standard CLIP loss for the perturbation-to-profile direction.

## Results

On the Bray et al. cross-modal retrieval benchmark, CellCLIP achieves Recall@1 of 1.18% (perturb-to-profile) and 1.25% (profile-to-perturb), substantially outperforming CLOOME (0.27%/0.07%) and MolPhenix* (0.56%/0.66%). On intra-modal tasks (CPJUMP1), CellCLIP attains 0.663 mAP for replicate detection and 0.413 mAP for within-class sister perturbation matching, compared to 0.575/0.245 for CLOOME and 0.531/0.222 for MolPhenix*. For zero-shot gene-gene relationship recovery on RxRx3-core, CellCLIP achieves the highest recall across all five pathway databases (e.g., 0.714 on CORUM, 0.778 on HuMAP), surpassing both cross-modal and unimodal baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CellCLIP outperforms all three baselines across every reported metric. Against CLOOME [38], CellCLIP more than doubles Recall@1 on both retrieval directions (1.18% vs. 0.27% perturb-to-profile; 1.25% vs. 0.07% profile-to-perturb) and achieves ~3× higher Recall@10. Against MolPhenix* [16] (the strongest prior cross-modal method), CellCLIP improves Recall@1 by ~2.1× (1.18% vs. 0.56%) and Recall@10 by ~1.7× (7.37% vs. 4.30%). On intra-modal tasks, CellCLIP also leads: replicate detection mAP of 0.663 vs. 0.575 (CLOOME) and 0.531 (MolPhenix*), and within-class sister perturbation matching mAP of 0.413 vs. 0.245 and 0.222 respectively. Against the unimodal OpenPhenom-S/16 [27] baseline, CellCLIP achieves higher replicate detection mAP (0.663 vs. 0.357) and gene-gene recovery recall (e.g., 0.714 vs. 0.649 on CORUM), though OpenPhenom remains competitive on some gene-gene databases (e.g., HuMAP 0.723 vs. 0.778). Notably, CellCLIP also trains ~3.9× faster than CLOOME and ~6.7× faster than instance-level training, thanks to per-perturbation pooling and the compact C+1 token CrossChannelFormer design.

## Relation to Prior Work

CellCLIP extends the CLIP-style cross-modal contrastive learning paradigm to high-content screening data, departing from prior Cell Painting CL methods (CLOOME, MolPhenix) in three key ways: (1) replacing perturbation-class-specific encoders (Morgan fingerprints + MLP, GNNs) with a unified text encoder (BERT) that generalizes across chemical, CRISPR, and ORF perturbations; (2) introducing CrossChannelFormer, a lightweight transformer that reasons over pooled per-channel embeddings (C+1 tokens) rather than the spatial patches used by channel-agnostic ViTs, achieving cross-channel reasoning at a fraction of the computational cost; and (3) incorporating ABMIL-style gated attention pooling to handle the many-to-one image-to-perturbation structure and mitigate false negatives/positives. The training objective adapts CWCL to preserve biologically meaningful similarity structure in the profile space, going beyond the binary positive/negative formulation of standard CLIP loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CellCLIP provides the first open-source, multi-class perturbation contrastive learning framework for Cell Painting, addressing a practical gap where prior state-of-the-art methods (MolPhenix) relied on closed-source components and non-public datasets. The substantial efficiency gains (6.7× from pooling, 3.9× over CLOOME) make the approach accessible to labs without large GPU clusters. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to previously published datasets without wet-lab validation, and the observation that cross-class perturbation matching (e.g., CRISPR vs. compound targeting the same gene) remains poorly solved, suggesting morphological signatures are highly class-specific. Future directions include conditional phenotypic generation from text and systematic analysis of individual channel contributions.
