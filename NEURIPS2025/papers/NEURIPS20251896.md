## Problem

Existing FP8 LLM training recipes either rely on high-overhead fine-grained scaling kernels or fall back to BF16 for sensitive GEMMs such as attention score computation, limiting throughput gains. No architecture has been demonstrated to support FP8 computation for all GEMMs within transformer blocks—including the dot-product attention—during both forward and backward passes at scale while maintaining training stability and matching BF16 downstream quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251896/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text tokens from the FineWeb-Edu corpus (131K-vocabulary BPE tokenizer, 4096 context length) and produces next-token predictions via a standard language modeling objective. All GEMMs inside transformer blocks (QKV projections, QK^T attention scores, PV attention-value product, and FFN linear layers) are executed in FP8 (E4M3 forward, E5M2 backward) using low-overhead tensorwise delayed scaling, while the final LM head projection remains in BF16. Training uses a WSD (Warmup-Steady-Decay) learning rate schedule with AdamW.

## Method

FOG (Fast and Outlier-Guarded) is a family of transformer architectures designed to suppress large activation outliers that cause FP8 divergence. The design removes pre-normalization layers and replaces them with post-normalization (Post-RMSNorm or LayerScale) before residual connections, adds QK entropy regularization with frozen gains (RMSNorm or tanh activation) to prevent attention entropy collapse, and scales the first block's input by σ^{-1} to maintain unit variance at initialization. By using non-gated pointwise activations (xIELU, GeLU) instead of SwiGLU, FOG avoids the quadratic outlier amplification inherent to gated linear units. The combination of these guards keeps activation kurtosis at sub-linear-to-logarithmic growth over training, enabling stable FP8DPA training with simple delayed scaling (history length 1024, zero margin) rather than expensive blockwise scaling. Three variants—FOG-max (xIELU + RMSNorm), FOG-opt (GeLU + RMSNorm), and FOG-flash (GeLU + tanh)—offer different throughput/quality trade-offs, achieving up to 43% throughput gains over BF16 at 8B scale while matching or exceeding baseline downstream accuracy.

## Results

FOG achieves up to 43% throughput improvement over the BF16 Llama3 baseline at 8B scale (FOG-flash: 12,764 vs 9,105 tokens/s/GPU), while matching or slightly exceeding downstream quality (FOG-max 1.5B: 47.1 avg vs Llama3 1.5B: 46.1 avg across 9 benchmarks). At 1.5B scale, FOG variants gain 15–18% throughput over BF16. All previously proposed architectures (Llama3, OLMo2, OP, Llama+SmoothSwiGLU) diverge under FP8DPA training, while FOG trains stably up to 450B tokens (15× Chinchilla-optimal budget).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Llama3 BF16 baseline, FOG-max at 1.5B slightly exceeds the baseline average (47.1 vs 46.1) while training 15.2% faster, and at 390M it also wins (40.8 vs 39.8). Against Llama+SmoothSwiGLU (the only other architecture shown to train stably in FP8), all FOG variants outperform it in throughput at 8B scale (e.g., FOG-flash +40.2% vs SmoothSwiGLU +34.3% over BF16), and the gap widens at longer contexts (e.g., +7.7% speed-up gap at 16K context). Against the OP architecture (from which FOG is derived), FOG resolves two critical instabilities: freezing the QK RMSNorm gains prevents early divergence, and adding post-RMSNorm prevents late divergence during learning-rate cooldown. Against OLMo2, both architectures diverge under FP8DPA, but OLMo2 diverges later (15.9B tokens at 1.5B vs 3.3B at 390M), confirming the paper's observation that larger models diverge later under FP8DPA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two converging threads: (1) FP8 training recipes for LLMs that use delayed or blockwise scaling to manage the narrow dynamic range of E4M3/E5M2 formats (DeepSeek-V3, FP8-LM, Fishman et al. ICLR 2025 with SmoothSwiGLU), and (2) outlier-mitigation architectures that remove pre-normalization and gated activations to suppress large activation features (OP by He et al. NeurIPS 2024, OLMo2). The key departure is that all prior FP8 approaches either kept the dot-product attention GEMMs in BF16 or relied on expensive fine-grained (blockwise) scaling kernels. FOG instead achieves stable FP8 for *all* GEMMs including attention (FP8DPA) using only low-overhead tensorwise delayed scaling, by combining post-normalization, frozen QK RMSNorm gains, and non-gated pointwise activations into a single architectural recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 35–43% throughput gain at 8B scale with no quality loss is a substantial practical win for large-scale LLM pre-training, and the architectural changes (post-norm, frozen QK gains, non-gated activations) are simple enough to be adopted in existing training stacks. The kurtosis-based early-divergence signal—detecting instability ~12B tokens before loss spikes—offers a broadly useful diagnostic for any low-precision training regime, reducing the cost of validating new architectures. The main open limitation is that the LM head still runs in BF16, and the observation that larger models diverge later under FP8DPA (Table 2) raises unresolved questions about scale-dependent stability that the authors explicitly leave for future work.
