## Problem

Task-trained RNNs can achieve identical training loss yet exhibit qualitatively different internal dynamics, weight configurations, and out-of-distribution behavior—a phenomenon called solution degeneracy that undermines conclusions drawn from single model instances. While the Contravariance Principle (Cao & Yamins, 2024) predicts that harder tasks should yield less dispersed solutions, this principle has remained purely theoretical with no systematic empirical validation. No unified framework existed to simultaneously quantify and control degeneracy across behavioral, dynamical, and weight-space levels.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 50 independently initialized RNNs (128 hidden units, tanh nonlinearity, trained via BPTT with Adam to a task-specific MSE threshold) and produces three scalar degeneracy scores per condition: behavioral degeneracy (standard deviation of OOD losses under a temporal-generalization condition that doubles the delay or trial length), dynamical degeneracy (mean pairwise Procrustes distance between linear dynamic operators extracted via DMD from delay-embedded hidden-state trajectories), and weight degeneracy (permutation-invariant Frobenius norm on recurrent weight matrices, normalized by parameter count for cross-size comparison). The DSA metric is invariant to orthogonal transformations of the state space, and a null distribution from same-network trajectory splits confirms that observed cross-network distances are statistically significant.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified three-level degeneracy framework and applies it to 3,400 RNNs across four neuroscience tasks (N-Bit Flip-Flop, Delayed Discrimination, Sine Wave Generation, Path Integration), systematically varying four factors: task complexity (via input-output channel count, memory demand, or auxiliary loss), feature-learning strength (via μP parameterization where a single hyperparameter γ interpolates between lazy and rich regimes), network width (32–512 units under fixed γ), and structural regularization (nuclear-norm and L1 penalties on recurrent weights). The central empirical finding is a double dissociation: task complexity and feature learning are contravariant (reducing dynamical degeneracy while increasing weight degeneracy, consistent with the Contravariance Principle), whereas network size and regularization are covariant (reducing degeneracy at both dynamical and weight levels). μP is used to disentangle feature learning from width effects, and the framework is validated on GRUs and chaotic Lorenz-96 dynamics to confirm generality beyond vanilla RNNs and stable attractors.

## Results

Across 3,400 RNNs trained on four neuroscience-relevant tasks, the paper identifies a double dissociation in how four factors shape solution degeneracy. Task complexity and feature learning produce contravariant effects: dynamical degeneracy (measured by DSA distance) decreases while weight degeneracy (measured by permutation-invariant Frobenius norm) increases, with mixed effects on behavioral degeneracy (OOD loss std). In contrast, larger network size and structural regularization (low-rank or L1) produce covariant effects, reducing degeneracy at all three levels simultaneously. The paper empirically validates the Contravariance Principle [24], showing that harder tasks constrain the space of viable dynamical solutions while dispersing weight configurations, and that stronger feature learning (higher γ under μP) amplifies this weight-space dispersion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods in the traditional sense; instead, it systematically contrasts four factors' effects on three degeneracy levels. The central comparison is between contravariant factors (task complexity, feature learning) and covariant factors (network size, regularization): the former reduce dynamical degeneracy while increasing weight degeneracy, whereas the latter reduce both. A key dissociation emerges between dynamical and behavioral degeneracy: stronger feature learning makes neural dynamics more consistent across networks but increases OOD behavioral variability (likely due to overfitting), while higher task complexity reduces both. The paper also contrasts DSA-based dynamical degeneracy with SVCCA-based representational degeneracy, finding that the four factors do not impose the same constraints on static representations as they do on temporal dynamics, supporting the use of DSA over geometry-based metrics for RNNs. Effects are confirmed to generalize beyond vanilla RNNs to GRUs (Appendix H) and to chaotic RNNs trained on Lorenz 96 prediction (Appendix E).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Dynamical Similarity Analysis (DSA) for comparing neural dynamics, the μP parameterization for isolating feature-learning strength, and SVCCA for representational comparison, but unifies these into a single multi-level framework for quantifying solution degeneracy across behavior, dynamics, and weights simultaneously. It extends the Contravariance Principle of Cao and Yamins (2024) from a theoretical conjecture to an empirically validated finding, and goes beyond prior single-level studies (e.g., Turner et al. 2021 on dynamics, Maheswaranathan et al. 2019 on representational geometry) by systematically sweeping four controlling factors—task complexity, feature learning, network size, and regularization—across all three levels in a coordinated design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive empirical map of what controls solution degeneracy in task-trained RNNs, offering practitioners concrete knobs (task design, μP γ, width, regularization) to tune consistency versus diversity for specific research goals in neuroscience and ML. The double dissociation—contravariant effects of task complexity/feature learning versus covariant effects of size/regularization—reframes how one should interpret single-network reverse-engineering: harder tasks make dynamics more reliable but weights less comparable. Key open questions include the observed decoupling between dynamical and behavioral degeneracy (stronger feature learning improves dynamics consistency but worsens OOD behavioral consistency, likely via overfitting), and whether the framework extends to tasks with multiple qualitatively distinct solution basins.
