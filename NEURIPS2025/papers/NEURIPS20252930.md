## Problem

Vision Transformers rely on fixed square patch tokens that fragment objects without regard for semantic boundaries, unlike text tokenizers that align with meaningful subword units. While superpixel-based subobject tokenizers improve semantic coherence, none are fully end-to-end differentiable, preventing the tokenizer itself from being trained jointly with the backbone. This paper addresses the gap by unifying precise semantic alignment, differentiability, and adaptive granularity in a single modular tokenizer.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (224×224 for classification, 512×512 for segmentation) and produces a variable number of adaptive superpixel tokens per image (e.g., ~240 tokens vs. 197 for 16×16 patches at 224×224), with each token grounded in original pixel positions. Tokens are extracted at pixel-level granularity and fed into any standard ViT backbone, yielding class labels, per-superpixel segmentation predictions via a simple MLP head, or vectorized paths via potrace tracing. The tokenizer is modular and can be retrofitted onto pretrained ViTs without architectural modification.

## Method

∂HT constructs a full hierarchy of connected superpixel partitions by iteratively merging each vertex with its most similar neighbor under a positive semi-definite kernel (Gaussian, d=8), then selects the optimal partition level using information criteria (AICC) where degrees of freedom are estimated via lattice-theoretic properties of the partition space (inversely proportional to internal edge count per region). Differentiability is achieved through a mean-injection trick that replaces each superpixel's pixel-mean with a learnable linear mapping of the tokenized feature, enabling gradient flow from the ViT loss back through the tokenization process. A dynamic background masking mechanism blends foreground and background features via a learnable parameter λ and shared background features β to mitigate sparsity when irregular regions are interpolated to fixed patch sizes. For retrofitting pretrained ViTs, the tokenizer is first aligned via a reconstruction pretraining objective that minimizes the L2 distance between original pixels and the linearly mapped token features, ensuring the injected features preserve the original image's local texture properties.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k classification trained from scratch, ∂HT with ViT-B16 achieves 83.2% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the patch-based DEiT3 baseline (82.6%) and all other superpixel tokenizers (SPFormer 82.7%, SuiT 82.1%, SPiT 80.4%). When retrofitted onto pretrained DEiT3 ViT-B16, ∂HT reaches 83.9% top-1 accuracy (vs. 82.6% patch baseline) and improves linear-probing accuracy on fine-grained benchmarks (Caltech256: 91.2%, CUB200: 74.4%). For dense prediction, ∂HT-ViT-B achieves 48.9% mIoU on COCO-Stuff164k without a dense decoder, and zero-shot salient segmentation Fmax of 92.4/77.9/71.9 on ECSSD/DUTS/DUT-OMRON, surpassing all adaptive-tokenization baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against patch-based DEiT3 [37], ∂HT wins on from-scratch ViT-B16 (83.2% vs. 82.6%) and on retrofitting (83.9% vs. 82.6%), with the largest gains on fine-grained linear-probing tasks (CUB200: 74.4% vs. 72.1%). Against SPFormer [12], ∂HT achieves higher Base-capacity accuracy (83.2% vs. 82.7%) while being both modular and differentiable, whereas SPFormer is neither. Against SuiT [16], ∂HT outperforms on Base capacity (83.2% vs. 82.1%) and is fully differentiable, while SuiT operates on downsampled images and lacks end-to-end learning. Against SPiT [15] (the closest prior work), ∂HT wins decisively on both Small (80.0% vs. 75.0%) and Base (83.2% vs. 80.4%) capacities and adds differentiability that SPiT lacks. On zero-shot salient segmentation (Table 4), ∂HT-B surpasses DINO-B+ [58], SPiT-B [15], and SuiT-B [16] on Fmax across all three benchmarks (e.g., 92.4 vs. 90.3 on ECSSD). On raster-to-vector conversion (Table 5), ∂HT in zero-shot mode (MSE 0.00178, PSNR 27.50, SSIM 0.8541) outperforms DiffVG-AP [59], which was specifically trained for the task (MSE 0.00265, PSNR 26.22, SSIM 0.8494).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own SPiT [15] framework for modular superpixel tokenization in ViTs by making the entire pipeline end-to-end differentiable, addressing the key limitation that prior subobject tokenizers (SuiT, EPOC, SPiT) rely on non-differentiable grouping steps. It departs from dynamic merging approaches (ToMe, Slot Attention) that remain tethered to grid primitives, and from deformable sampling methods (DPT, DAT) that are still fundamentally patch-based, by introducing true off-grid hierarchical partitioning with pixel-level granularity. The use of information criteria (AIC, BIC, etc.) for automatic partition selection from a lattice-structured hierarchy is a novel formulation that replaces manual threshold tuning or learned gating mechanisms used in prior adaptive tokenization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, plug-and-play design enables retrofitting pretrained ViTs with adaptive tokenization without retraining from scratch, which is practically valuable as the field accumulates large pretrained models. The out-of-the-box raster-to-vector conversion capability (outperforming DiffVG without task-specific training) suggests the learned superpixel partitions capture genuinely useful structural information beyond what was explicitly optimized for. Key limitations include roughly 2× throughput reduction versus patch-based ViTs, degraded performance at very low resolutions, and reliance on Gaussian distributional assumptions for the information criteria. Open directions include extending to self-supervised learning, video transformers, and vision-language models where adaptive token budgets could address redundancy in high-dimensional inputs.
