## Problem

High-quality long-context instruction data is essential for aligning long-context LLMs, yet the instruction datasets behind open-source models like Qwen and Llama remain proprietary. Human annotation of long-context instructions is prohibitively expensive, and existing template- or seed-based synthesis methods struggle to achieve the scale, diversity, and quality needed for effective long-context training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250434/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes documents from FineWeb-Edu (averaging ~1.6k tokens) and appends model-specific pre-query template tokens (e.g., `<|start_header_id|>user` for Llama-3, `<|im_start|>user` for Qwen-2.5) to form the input sequence. It produces complete instruction triplets (document, query, response); in the multi-document variant, the document field concatenates up to n randomly sampled distractor documents with a special separator token, yielding cross-document reasoning tasks. The p-Mix strategy then wraps each triplet into a hybrid training sequence by prepending a short-context instruction and probabilistically appending long- or short-context segments until the sequence reaches the 64K token limit.

## Method

LongMagpie exploits the observation that instruction-tuned long-context LLMs, when presented with a document followed only by the special tokens that precede a user turn, auto-regressively generate contextually relevant queries about that document—leveraging document-query relationship patterns internalized during their own instruction tuning. The pipeline has two stages: (1) query generation by sampling completions after the document+pre-query prefix, followed by response generation to form (D, Q, R) triplets, with rule-based filtering (question-mark heuristic) and length-based filtering to discard continuations; and (2) multi-document extension where x random documents (x drawn uniformly from 0 to n, optimal at n=10) are concatenated with the target document to create harder cross-document reasoning tasks. The p-Mix strategy addresses the known degradation of short-context performance by prepending one short-context instruction to every training sequence, then iteratively appending either a LongMagpie long-context instruction (with probability P_L=0.4) or another short-context instruction (with probability 1−P_L) until approaching the maximum sequence length, effectively balancing both regimes within a single training pass.

## Results

On long-context benchmarks, LongMagpie achieves leading performance: 62.10 on HELMET, 91.17 on RULER, and 34.4 on LongBench v2, yielding a LongAVG of 62.56 — the best among all long-instruction data methods. With the p-Mix strategy (LongMagpie + UltraChat), the model retains a HELMET score of 62.11 and RULER of 89.70 while recovering short-context performance to 64.10 ShortAVG, only 0.33 below the overall best. The method also demonstrates strong sample efficiency, averaging just 1.6K tokens per instruction versus 10–13× more for ChatQA and LongAlign.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongMagpie outperforms both long-context baselines across all three long-context benchmarks: it beats ChatQA by +1.87 on HELMET, +1.35 on RULER, and +3.6 on LongBench v2 (+2.28 LongAVG), and surpasses LongAlign by a wider margin of +4.31, +5.09, and +9.9 respectively (+6.44 LongAVG). Against short-instruction baselines (Tulu, Magpie, UltraChat), LongMagpie also leads on long-context metrics — e.g., +3.25 RULER over Tulu and +7.32 RULER over UltraChat — while sacrificing only 1–2 points on ShortAVG. The p-Mix variant (LongMagpie+UltraChat) recovers most of the short-context gap (64.10 vs. 64.43 best) while maintaining the top HELMET score (62.11) and a competitive RULER (89.70), confirming that the self-synthesized long-context signals are complementary to existing short-instruction data. In the sample-count-controlled comparison (190k samples), LongMagpie still leads ChatQA (+1.06 HELMET, +0.83 RULER) and LongAlign (+0.66 HELMET, +3.29 RULER), and the gap widens at 450k samples, indicating better scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

LongMagpie directly extends Magpie's self-synthesis mechanism—prompting aligned LLMs with only special tokens preceding a user turn to elicit auto-regressive instruction generation—by prepending a document before those tokens, thereby exploiting the document-query relationship patterns internalized during instruction tuning. This departs from prior long-context instruction synthesis methods (ChatQA, LongAlign, WildLong) that rely on predefined templates, seed questions, or multi-stage pipelines, eliminating those components entirely. The multi-document extension and the p-Mix probabilistic mixing strategy are novel additions that go beyond the single-document, single-regime setting of both Magpie and prior long-context data construction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete barrier to open-source long-context model development: the proprietary nature of long-context instruction data behind models like Qwen and Llama. The demonstrated sample efficiency (1.6K tokens per instruction versus 10–13× more for ChatQA and LongAlign) makes large-scale synthesis practically accessible, and the p-Mix strategy offers a simple recipe for avoiding the well-known short-context degradation. Key limitations include inheriting biases from the source LLM and inadequate coverage of long-form output tasks, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to non-Qwen/Llama model families or to tasks requiring extended reasoning and generation beyond document-query patterns.
