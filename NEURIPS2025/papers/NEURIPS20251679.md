## Problem

3D Gaussian diffusion models (e.g., DiffSplat) suffer from time-intensive inference due to the massive number of Gaussian primitives in the Recon-Gen-Render pipeline, making inference-time scaling via multiple sampled trajectories impractical. Unlike 2D diffusion, the unstructured 3DGS representation and the sequential reconstruction-generation-rendering stages prevent direct application of existing efficient inference or best-of-N selection strategies, leaving a gap in scalable, quality-preserving acceleration for 3D diffusion.

## Input / Output

For text-to-3D, the method takes a text prompt and produces a 3D Gaussian splat representation renderable from arbitrary viewpoints; for image-to-3D, it takes a front-facing RGB image (center-cropped to 256×256) and reconstructs the 3D object. Internally, the method operates on a 16×16 latent patch grid (SD-3.5-Medium backbone) where the trajectory selector evaluates intermediate latents at timestep T/2, and the instance mask partitions foreground instance tokens from background tokens before feeding them into the denoising transformer. The final output is a set of Gaussian primitives with explicit position, scale, rotation, color, and opacity parameters, rendered via Gaussian splatting with a camera matrix.

## Method

TRIM combines two complementary trimming strategies applied as a post-training framework to an off-the-shelf 3D Gaussian diffusion model. (1) Trajectory Reduction: a lightweight latent selector (single conv layer + 2-layer MLP) is trained via offline pairwise knowledge distillation—distilling from a Decoder-Renderer-Evaluator joint model—using binary cross-entropy on {latent pair, quality score} triplets synthesized from 64 trajectories per prompt. At inference, N trajectories are denoised to the midpoint (T/2), then a tournament selection retains only the highest-potential trajectory, reducing total denoising steps from NT to NT−(N−1)t and cutting post-denoising (VAE decode + rendering) cost by N×. (2) Instance Mask Denoising: a training-free corner-reference attention mechanism (inspired by DINO's [CLS]-patch attention) detects background regions by comparing each patch's similarity to an aggregated [REF] token from the four grid corners; a progressive expansion scheduler gradually masks from the outer boundary inward across four phases. All background tokens are merged into a single [BG] token to reduce transformer sequence length, and a post-denoising correction step zeros out background Gaussian opacities to eliminate rendering artifacts. The entire framework is model-agnostic and requires no retraining of the diffusion backbone.

## Results

On T³ Bench (text-to-3D), TRIM achieves the best CLIP Similarity across all three sub-categories (31.58% single object, 31.48% single object with surroundings, 30.11% multiple objects) and is the only method to produce a positive ImageReward score (0.12 on single object). On the GSO dataset (image-to-3D), TRIM improves reconstruction fidelity over DiffSplat with PSNR 16.78 vs. 16.20, SSIM 0.82 vs. 0.79, and LPIPS 0.17 vs. 0.19. TRIM also reduces inference FLOPs from 195.68T to 106.31T (45.7% reduction) and runtime from 8.64s to 5.24s while improving quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRIM outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor DiffSplat (the backbone model), TRIM gains +0.63 to +1.28 in CLIP Similarity, +0.42 to +7.50 in CLIP R-Precision, and +0.60 to +0.61 in ImageReward across the three T³ Bench sub-categories, while simultaneously reducing FLOPs by 45.7%. Against LGM, the next-strongest baseline, TRIM leads by 1.62–3.69 in CLIP Similarity and 7.5–33.25 in CLIP R-Precision, with a substantially higher ImageReward (e.g., 0.12 vs. −0.72 on single object). GVGEN and Direct-3D trail significantly on all metrics, with CLIP Similarity 7–10 points lower and ImageReward scores 2+ points worse. On image-to-3D (GSO), TRIM beats InstantMesh by +1.25 PSNR and +0.05 SSIM, and DiffSplat by +0.58 PSNR, confirming consistent gains under image conditioning where trajectory diversity is inherently lower.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRIM builds directly on DiffSplat, which repurposes 2D image diffusion backbones (SD-3.5, PixArt-Σ) for 3D Gaussian splat generation via a Recon-Gen-Render pipeline. It adapts inference-time scaling ideas from LLMs (best-of-N sampling) and 2D diffusion (SANA 1.5, trajectory scoring) to the 3D setting, where the additional rendering stage makes naive step scaling prohibitively expensive. The spatial trimming component draws on token merging (ToMe, DiffCR) but departs by being entirely training-free and leveraging corner-reference attention for instance detection rather than learned compression ratios. The key conceptual departure is replacing costly end-to-end trajectory scaling with a lightweight latent selector that prunes trajectories mid-denoising, combined with progressive background masking that requires no backbone retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical 40% FLOPs reduction (195T→106T) and runtime drop (8.6s→5.2s) make 3D Gaussian diffusion inference substantially more accessible for interactive applications, and the training-free, model-agnostic design means it can be plugged into any transformer-based 3D diffusion backbone. The finding that trajectory diversity scaling consistently outperforms denoising-step scaling in 3D (while step scaling degrades quality) provides a clear practical guideline for compute allocation. However, spatial trimming is currently confined to the denoising transformer blocks because the pipeline relies on repurposed 2D backbones with 3D-to-2D structural transformations; a natively 3D-structure-aware diffusion model would be needed for end-to-end spatial efficiency. The observed narrowing of output diversity under trajectory reduction also raises open questions about the quality-diversity trade-off in best-of-N selection for 3D generation.
