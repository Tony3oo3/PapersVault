## Problem

Spiking Neural Networks are highly sensitive to distribution shifts due to their sparse, temporal spiking dynamics, yet no source-free, single-instance test-time adaptation method exists specifically for SNNs. Existing ANN-oriented TTA methods either require batch data, source data, or operate on output probabilities and BN statistics that are ill-suited to SNNs' discrete spike trains and architectures that often omit normalization layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251437/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single test sample (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR/Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet, or event streams for DVS Gesture) and produces a class label prediction. No source data, target batches, or ground-truth labels are required at test time; the model is updated exactly once per incoming sample. Internally, the method generates an augmented batch of 32–64 views via AugMix from the single input, extracts spike-count feature maps at the deepest layer retaining spatial support (C×H×W), and aggregates spikes over the full temporal window (T steps) into per-channel spatial count vectors.

## Method

SPACE performs test-time adaptation by enforcing consistency of spike-behavior-based local feature maps across augmented views of a single test sample. For each augmented view, it computes total spike counts over time at a chosen extractor layer, converts per-channel spatial count vectors into probability distributions via softmax, and measures pairwise similarity as the average channel-wise dot product of these distributions. The adaptation loss sums (1 − similarity) over all augmented-view pairs and is minimized with a single SGD step on the extractor parameters, after which the adapted model predicts the label. The key distinction from prior work is operating on intermediate spike dynamics rather than output probabilities (MEMO) or BN statistics (SITA), and using total spike counts rather than stepwise matching to smooth the loss landscape and reduce computational cost while capturing the rate and spatial-support changes that distribution shift induces in SNNs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C (SNN-VGG9, severity 5), SPACE achieves 71.03% average top-1 accuracy, outperforming MEMO (69.20%), DeYO (67.99%), RoTTA (67.79%), and No Adapt (66.57%). On Tiny-ImageNet-C (SNN-VGG11), SPACE reaches 16.48% average accuracy versus 14.67% for MEMO and 14.47% for No Adapt. On the ImageNet series (Spike-driven Transformer V3), SPACE attains 43.37% average accuracy across V2/R/A, a +1.80% gain over No Adapt (41.57%). On the neuromorphic DVS Gesture-C benchmark (SNN-ConvLSTM), SPACE achieves 65.91% average accuracy, a +6.06% improvement over No Adapt (59.85%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPACE consistently outperforms all four baselines in average accuracy across every evaluated dataset and architecture. Against MEMO (the strongest single-instance baseline), SPACE gains +1.83% on CIFAR-10-C (VGG9), +1.48% on CIFAR-10-C (ResNet11), +1.81% on Tiny-ImageNet-C, +1.81% on the ImageNet series, and +5.68% on DVS Gesture-C; MEMO retains a narrow edge only on JPEG corruption (85.05% vs 82.88% on CIFAR-10-C). SITA is effectively inapplicable to SNNs: it performs worse than No Adapt on CIFAR-10-C (66.41% vs 66.57%) because directly adjusting BN parameters without gradient guidance disrupts spike information, and it is entirely absent for architectures without BN layers (ResNet, ConvLSTM). RoTTA's memory-bank mechanism cannot be leveraged in the single-sample regime, yielding only marginal gains (+1.22% over No Adapt on CIFAR-10-C). DeYO, which relies on smooth probability outputs for confidence comparison, is incompatible with SNNs' discrete temporal spike outputs and also operates only on normalization layers, resulting in performance near or below No Adapt on most corruptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPACE extends the single-instance, source-free TTA paradigm established by MEMO (output-consistency across augmentations) and SITA (BN-statistics adaptation) by replacing both with a spike-behavior-based feature map alignment objective that is native to SNNs. Conceptually, it departs from MEMO's coarse output-probability consistency by operating on intermediate temporal spike counts, and from SITA's BN-moment updates by being architecture-agnostic (no BN required). The method also inherits the AugMix augmentation strategy from MEMO but applies it to a fundamentally different signal: per-channel spatial distributions of aggregated spike activity rather than softmax class probabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a clear gap: SNNs are increasingly deployed in edge and neuromorphic settings where distribution shift is a deployment prerequisite, yet no prior TTA method was designed for their discrete, temporal computation. The finding that BN-based adaptation (SITA) actively degrades SNN performance is practically important, as it cautions against porting ANN TTA heuristics to spiking architectures. A key open question is whether the backpropagation requirement limits true neuromorphic deployment; the authors acknowledge this and suggest future work on gradient-free or local optimization alternatives. The method's generality across CNN, Transformer, and ConvLSTM backbones suggests broad applicability, but validation on larger-scale or multi-modal SNN deployments remains to be seen.
