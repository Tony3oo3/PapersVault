## Problem

Computer-aided diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis in wrist conventional radiographs is severely limited by the absence of publicly available, high-quality annotated datasets. The wrist's anatomical complexity—numerous small carpal bones with narrow joint spaces, frequent overlaps, and RA-induced morphological alterations such as bone erosion and bony ankylosis—makes expert annotation extremely challenging, and existing datasets either target pediatric bone age, lack pixel-level masks, or remain private. This paper introduces RAM-W600, the first public multi-task dataset combining wrist bone instance segmentation and Sharp/van der Heijde (SvdH) bone erosion scoring, along with standardized benchmarks.

## Input / Output

For the segmentation task, input is a wrist-region crop from a PA conventional radiograph (0.15 mm/pixel resolution) resized to 512×512 pixels, and output is a pixel-level instance segmentation mask for 14 distinct wrist bones (5 metacarpals, 7 carpal bones with pisiform and triquetrum merged, distal radius, and distal ulna), stored as NumPy arrays. For the BE classification task, input is a 224×224 pixel ROI crop of one of six SvdH joint surfaces (Metacarpal 1st, Trapezium, Scaphoid, Lunate, Distal Radius, Distal Ulna), and output is a binary label indicating presence (SvdH score > 0) or absence (score = 0) of bone erosion. The full dataset comprises 1048 radiographs from 388 patients across six institutions, with 618 images carrying segmentation masks and 800 images carrying 4800 joint-level SvdH BE scores.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a multi-institutional dataset with expert-verified annotations produced by a multidisciplinary team (radiological technologist, board-certified radiologist with 25 years' experience, and orthopedic doctor), followed by a comprehensive benchmark of 13 supervised architectures spanning CNNs (U-Net, DeepLabV3+, PSPNet, SegResNet, U-Net++), vision transformers (SegFormer, TransUNet), and state-space/Mamba-based models (UKAN, UMambaBot, UMambaEnc, SwinUMamba), plus three foundation-model configurations (SAM with box/point prompts, MedSAM with box prompts). All supervised models are trained with AdamW (weight decay 1e-2), cosine annealing from 1e-4 (segmentation) or 1e-6 (classification), 100 epochs, batch size 8 or 16, and five fixed random seeds for reproducibility on a single RTX 4090 GPU. Key findings: SwinUMamba achieves the best segmentation (DSC 97.75%, NSD 90.71%), but NSD remains substantially lower than DSC across all models, revealing persistent difficulty in boundary delineation under bone overlap and erosion; foundation models (SAM, MedSAM) underperform supervised models by 9–13 DSC points. For BE classification, all models achieve near-chance balanced accuracy (best 52.64% for MobileViT), with extreme class imbalance (majority of joints scored 0) and subtle radiographic features rendering the task highly challenging.

## Results

On the RAM-W600 wrist bone instance segmentation task, SwinUMamba achieves the best overall DSC of 97.75% and NSD of 90.71%, with the lowest VOE (4.35%) among supervised models. TransUNet is a close competitor, reaching 97.62% DSC and the best MSD of 1.05 pixels, but with substantially more parameters (105.91M vs. 59.89M). Foundation models (SAM, MedSAM) lag far behind, with DSC capped at 88.74% (SAM box) and 85.07% (MedSAM box). For the secondary BE classification task, all models perform poorly, with the best balanced accuracy reaching only 52.64% (MobileViT) and F1-score of 12.40% (EfficientFormer), underscoring the extreme difficulty of detecting subtle bone erosion under severe class imbalance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 13 supervised segmentation baselines, Mamba-based architectures (SwinUMamba, UMambaEnc, UMambaBot) consistently outperform all CNN- and Transformer-based models on both DSC and NSD, with SwinUMamba leading by 0.13–0.42% DSC over the next-best TransUNet and by 1.2–6.2% NSD over CNN baselines like U-Net and DeepLabV3. TransUNet achieves the best MSD (1.05 pix) but at 105.91M parameters and 22.05 ms inference, making it less efficient than SwinUMamba (59.89M, 38.52 ms) or the lightweight UMamba variants (~4.5M params). Foundation models SAM and MedSAM are substantially outperformed by all supervised methods: SAM (box) trails SwinUMamba by 9.01% DSC and 26.31% NSD, while MedSAM (box) lags by 12.68% DSC and 51.90% NSD, confirming that general-purpose segmentation priors are inadequate for the fine-grained, overlap-heavy wrist bone task. For BE classification, no model achieves meaningful performance: the best BACC is 52.64% (MobileViT) and best F1 is 12.40% (EfficientFormer), with MedMamba showing the highest DOR (5.89) but extreme variance (±9.66). The paper also reports that BE-affected samples cause statistically significant DSC degradation (p < 0.05–0.001) for most segmentation models, while NSD shows no significant group difference, highlighting that erosion primarily disrupts boundary precision rather than global overlap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground as the first public multi-task dataset for wrist bone instance segmentation and SvdH BE scoring in conventional radiography for rheumatoid arthritis. Prior CR wrist segmentation efforts (Kang 2022, Du 2024, Lee 2023) relied on private datasets and targeted bone mineral density or bone age assessment rather than RA pathology, while RA-specific scoring datasets (Sun 2022, Bo 2024) provided severity labels without pixel-level anatomical annotations. The paper positions itself at the intersection of these two lines of work, unifying anatomical segmentation and clinical scoring into a single benchmark, and evaluates a broad spectrum of architectures from classical CNNs (U-Net, DeepLabV3) through Transformers (TransUNet, SegFormer) to state-space models (UMamba, SwinUMamba) alongside foundation models (SAM, MedSAM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that wrist bone segmentation in CR remains challenging at the boundary level (NSD peaks at ~90.7% despite DSC reaching 97.75%), and that SvdH BE classification is far from solved, with the best model achieving only 52.64% balanced accuracy and 12.40% F1-score, underscoring the extreme difficulty of detecting subtle erosive changes under class imbalance. Foundation models (SAM, MedSAM) underperform supervised architectures by a wide margin, suggesting that general-purpose segmentation priors are insufficient for fine-grained anatomical delineation in pathological radiographs. Key limitations include the single-geographic-region origin of RA cases and the severe underrepresentation of high-grade BE scores, leaving open questions about cross-population generalizability and whether targeted augmentation or weak-signal extraction architectures can close the BE detection gap.
