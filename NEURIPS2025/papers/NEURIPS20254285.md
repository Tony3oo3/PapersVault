## Problem

While VLMs are known to be vulnerable to adversarial examples, no prior work has demonstrated the ability to precisely control every output token of a VLM through imperceptible image perturbations. This paper fills that gap by providing a theoretical proof (via certified radius analysis) that visual perturbations induce strictly smaller robustness radii than textual perturbations, and by empirically showing that a single unified attack can subvert safety alignment, hijack task outputs, and force arbitrary token sequences across multiple VLMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image, a text prompt (token sequence t_{1:n}), and a desired target output token sequence t_{n+1:n+l} as inputs, and produces a single adversarial image x^{adv} whose perturbation from the original is bounded by an ℓ∞-norm budget ε (tested at 4/255, 8/255, 16/255). The target sequence can specify the full output or only specific segments—e.g., the initial tokens for jailbreaking or the EOS token for sponge examples—giving the attacker fine-grained control over which parts of the response are manipulated while leaving others unconstrained.

## Method

VMA formulates the attack as maximizing the conditional probability of a target token sequence over the adversarial image, subject to an ℓ∞-norm constraint. Rather than using standard PGD (which fails due to the deep, iteratively computed autoregressive objective and the non-differentiable projection step), VMA introduces two key modifications: (1) a differentiable sigmoid-based transformation Γ(z, x̲, x̄) = x̲ + σ(z)·(x̄ − x̲) that maps an unconstrained variable z into the ε-ball, eliminating gradient discontinuities; and (2) Adam-style first-order and second-order momentum (β₁, β₂) to stabilize convergence over the multi-step autoregressive probability landscape. The optimization is performed entirely in the transformed z-space, making the objective differentiable everywhere. The theoretical contribution proves (Corollary 1) that the certified ℓ₁-radius for visual perturbations is strictly smaller than that for textual synonym-substitution perturbations, explaining why imperceptible image noise can precisely flip individual output tokens while text modifications cannot.

## Results

VMA achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 90.25% for precise output manipulation across four VLMs (LLaVA, Qwen2-VL, Phi-3, DeepSeek-VL) with arbitrary prompts and images. For jailbreaking on AdvBench, VMA reaches ASRs of 97.12–99.81% at ε=4/255 and up to 99.81% at ε=16/255, compared to only 0.58–42.77% without perturbation. Hallucination and privacy breach attacks achieve ASRs of at least 98.97% and 93.38% respectively at ε=4/255, while the Denial-of-Service attack reaches 88.20–96.89% at the same budget. The sponge example attack increases average inference time by over 200× on DeepSeek-VL and 400× on other models with ε=4/255.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VMA is presented as a novel attack with no direct prior baseline achieving precise token-level manipulation of VLM outputs; the paper explicitly states it is 'the first work that demonstrates the potential of precise manipulation of each token in a VLM's output using imperceptible perturbation.' The only implicit baseline is the no-perturbation setting (ε=0/255), where ASRs range from 0% to 42.77% depending on the model and task, confirming that the high attack success rates are attributable to the adversarial perturbation rather than inherent model failures. Compared to prior image-based jailbreaking methods (e.g., FigStep, ImgJP) that target only the initial affirmative response, VMA achieves full-sequence control, enabling it to bypass safety alignment even when models initially refuse. The ablation in Table 11 shows that the differentiable sigmoid transformation alone improves ASR from 71% to 79%, momentum alone from 71% to 88%, and their combination reaches 96% with 4× fewer iterations (26 vs. 104), validating both design choices over the vanilla PGD baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PGD adversarial attack framework (Madry et al., 2017) by replacing its projection-based updates with Adam-style first- and second-order momentum and a differentiable sigmoid transformation, specifically to handle the deep, iteratively-computed conditional probability objective of autoregressive VLMs. Conceptually, it departs from prior VLM adversarial work (Qi et al. 2024, Bailey et al. 2023, Zhao et al. 2023) that focused on inducing unsafe content or wrong answers, by formulating the problem as precise per-token sequence manipulation and unifying it as a single optimization objective applicable to jailbreaking, hijacking, hallucination, DoS, sponge examples, and watermarking. The theoretical analysis draws on randomized smoothing (Cohen et al. 2019, Kumar et al. 2020) and text robustness via staircase distributions (Zhang et al. 2024) to prove that visual perturbations yield strictly smaller certified radii than textual perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a fundamental vulnerability class in VLMs: a single imperceptible image perturbation (ε=4/255) can precisely control the entire output token sequence, bypassing safety alignment and enabling a broad spectrum of attacks from one mechanism. This has direct implications for the deployment of VLMs in safety-critical or commercial settings, where even a 4/255 perturbation is visually undetectable. Key limitations constrain near-term impact: the attack is white-box only, transferability across architectures is poor, and difficulty scales with target sequence length. Open questions include whether black-box variants exist, whether adversarial training can close the gap between visual and textual certified radii, and whether the dual-use nature (attack vs. watermarking) can be leveraged for practical copyright protection.
