## Problem

Existing adaptive clipping methods for DP-SGD (e.g., AdaClip, quantile-based clipping) operate in the standard coordinate system, treating each gradient coordinate independently. This ignores correlations across gradient dimensions, causing redundant noise injection that degrades utility without improving privacy. GeoClip addresses this gap by clipping and perturbing gradients in a transformed basis aligned with the geometry of the gradient distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-sample gradients from a supervised training loop and produces updated model parameters with a formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantee computed via the Connect-the-Dots accountant. The transformation matrix M_t and centering vector a_t are estimated exclusively from previously released noisy gradients using exponential moving averages, incurring zero additional privacy cost. For high-dimensional models, a streaming rank-k PCA variant replaces the full d×d covariance with a d×k eigenspace, reducing per-step cost to O(dk²+k³).

## Method

GeoClip transforms each gradient into a decorrelated basis via ω_t = M_t(g_t − a_t), clips to unit norm, adds isotropic Gaussian noise, and maps back to the original space. The optimal transformation M_t is derived in closed form (Theorem 2) by solving a convex program that minimizes the noise-injection term Tr[(M_t^T M_t)^{-1}] subject to a clipping-probability constraint Tr(M_t^T M_t Σ_t) ≤ γ, yielding M_t* ∝ (Σ_t)^{-1/4} in the eigenbasis of the gradient covariance. In practice, Σ_t is tracked via exponential moving averages of the already-privatized gradients, so no raw data or extra privacy budget is needed. For large models, a Streaming Rank-k PCA algorithm maintains a low-rank eigendecomposition of the covariance, projecting gradients into a k-dimensional subspace for clipping and noise injection before mapping back to full dimensionality.

## Results

GeoClip consistently outperforms all baselines across tabular and image datasets under matched (ε,δ)-DP budgets. On the Breast Cancer dataset (d=62), GeoClip achieves 93.63% test accuracy at ε=0.87, compared to 87.71% for AdaClip, 92.28% for quantile-based clipping, and 85.95% for standard DP-SGD. On the Android Malware dataset (d=484), GeoClip reaches 92.67% at ε=0.67 versus 90.23% (AdaClip) and 81.86% (Quantile). In the Fashion-MNIST final-layer fine-tuning setting (510 parameters), GeoClip attains 73.09% at ε=1, outperforming AdaClip (69.24%) and DP-SGD (69.83%) by roughly 4 points, while also exhibiting the smallest standard deviation across seeds in every experiment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoClip is compared against three baselines: AdaClip (coordinate-wise adaptive clipping), quantile-based clipping (DP quantile of per-sample gradient norms), and standard DP-SGD (fixed clipping threshold). GeoClip wins on every dataset and every privacy budget tested. Against AdaClip, the margin is largest on Breast Cancer (up to 5.9 points at ε=0.87) and Malware (up to 2.4 points at ε=0.67), and on Fashion-MNIST fine-tuning (3.85 points at ε=1). Against quantile-based clipping, GeoClip leads by 1.35 points on Breast Cancer at ε=0.87 but by a much wider 10.81 points on Malware at ε=0.67, where quantile-based clipping degrades severely. Against standard DP-SGD, GeoClip gains 7.68 points on Breast Cancer (ε=0.87) and 2.10 points on Malware (ε=0.67). Notably, GeoClip also achieves the lowest standard deviation in nearly all settings, indicating more stable training under privacy constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoClip generalizes AdaClip (Pichapati et al., 2019), which restricts the gradient transformation to a diagonal matrix for per-coordinate scaling, by allowing an arbitrary full-rank transformation matrix that captures inter-coordinate correlations. It also positions itself against quantile-based adaptive clipping (Andrew et al., 2021) and standard DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016), all of which operate in the standard coordinate system and treat gradient dimensions independently. The paper distinguishes its approach from correlated-noise methods (Denisov et al., 2022; Choquette-Choo et al., 2024), which modify the noise structure rather than the gradient space, and notes that combining both directions remains unexplored. The convergence analysis extends Theorem 2 of Pichapati et al. to the general transformation setting, providing the theoretical basis for the optimal transformation derivation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoClip addresses a fundamental limitation of DP-SGD—redundant noise injection along correlated gradient directions—by adapting the clipping and perturbation geometry to the data, without consuming additional privacy budget. The practical impact is a consistent utility improvement across regression and classification tasks, with notably faster convergence that reduces total privacy expenditure. However, the method introduces additional hyperparameters (notably h₂), requires eigendecomposition overhead, and has been validated primarily on small-to-medium datasets (up to ~484 parameters) and linear models, leaving open whether the gains persist for deep networks with millions of parameters. The interaction between geometry-aware clipping and correlated-noise mechanisms, and the behavior under non-convex loss landscapes typical of deep learning, remain important open questions.
