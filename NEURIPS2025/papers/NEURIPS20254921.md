## Problem

Existing cluster schedulers for deep learning use bin-packing strategies that are unaware of the sparse, high-volume communication patterns inherent in LLM pre-training, where GPUs exchange data exclusively within specific DP and PP groups. This misalignment with multi-tier data center topology causes significant cross-switch bandwidth loss, and the fundamental trade-off between aligning DP and PP groups simultaneously remains unaddressed at the scale of thousands of GPUs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254921/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The scheduler consumes a job specification (total GPU count, DP/TP/PP degrees), the physical CLOS-like network topology (minipod structure with leaf/spine/core switches), and live cluster state including available nodes and the job queue. It produces a discrete placement assignment mapping each communication group to a specific minipod, solved as a mixed-integer program, along with a resource reservation plan that blocks incoming jobs from occupying reserved nodes until the LPJ arrives. The communication matrix representation (rows = PP groups, columns = DP groups) encodes per-GPU weight sizes and DP/PP communication volumes computed from an analytical model.

## Method

Arnold formulates LPJ scheduling as a mixed-integer program that minimizes the weighted sum of the maximum spread across DP and PP communication groups, where spread is the number of distinct minipods a group spans. A key domain-specific simplification exploits the homogeneity and synchrony of LPJ communication groups to coarsen the scheduling unit from individual GPUs to entire communication groups, transforming the problem into a bin-packing-like MIP solvable in seconds by off-the-shelf solvers (SCIP). The DP/PP trade-off is balanced via affinity parameters α and β, derived by matching the job's computation-to-communication and DP-to-PP volume ratios against a pre-characterized database of placement experiments. A queuing policy with an ML-driven job-completion-time predictor (GBM) opportunistically schedules short-lived jobs into reserved zones, balancing resource utilization against guaranteed LPJ placement.

## Results

In simulation experiments across three cluster topologies (3 to 11 minipods), Arnold reduces the weighted maximum spread of communication groups by up to 1.67× compared to the best baseline, with an average 1.2× reduction. In production, Arnold achieves a 5.7% throughput speedup at 208 GPUs and a 10.6% end-to-end improvement at 9600+ GPUs over MegaScale, reducing the maximum spread of DP and PP groups by 1.5× and 1.3× respectively at full scale. Scheduling latency remains low even for 512-node jobs in 1000+ node clusters, whereas the enumeration baseline exceeds 100 s for just 10 nodes in a medium topology.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Arnold consistently outperforms all four simulation baselines (Best-fit, Random-fit, GPU-packing, and Topo-aware) on medium and large cluster settings, achieving up to 1.67× lower weighted spread; on the simplest topology (3 minipods, 18 nodes) it ties with Best-fit, GPU-packing, and Topo-aware because the search space is too small for differentiation. In the production cluster experiment, Arnold surpasses MegaScale by 5.7% at 208 GPUs and 10.6% at 9600+ GPUs in average throughput, with the gap widening as model size and node count increase. On the open-source Llama3 8B benchmark (12 L20 GPUs, RoCE), Arnold achieves 3.86 PetaFlops versus 3.82 for GPU-pack and 3.80 for the bin-packing strategy used by MegaScale, confirming generalizability across hardware and network types. The paper notes that as the DP affinity parameter α increases toward 1, Arnold's advantage over bin-packing baselines shrinks, since the objective reduces to standard bin-packing; in practice α is kept below 0.5 based on characterization results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of GPU cluster scheduling work (Gandiva, AntMan, MLaaS, Topo-aware scheduling) by introducing a communication-group-aware formulation specifically for LLM pre-training jobs. While prior schedulers apply bin-packing to maximize GPU locality, they lack awareness of the hybrid parallelism communication structure (DP and PP groups) that governs inter-node traffic in LLM training. The paper builds on Megatron-LM as the training framework and formulates the placement problem as a mixed-integer program over a communication matrix, departing from the graph-partitioning approach of Topo-aware scheduling by exploiting the homogeneous, synchronous structure of LLM communication groups to enable efficient online solving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.6% end-to-end speedup on 9600+ GPUs is practically significant for production LLM training pipelines, where even single-digit percentage improvements translate to substantial GPU-hour savings. The finding that topology-optimized scheduling can inadvertently slow computation kernels through GPU stream interference broadens the scope of topology-aware scheduling beyond communication efficiency. Open questions include how the approach handles failure recovery (re-solving MIP and migrating is expensive), whether characterization results transfer across fundamentally different network architectures beyond CLOS topologies, and how the DP/PP trade-off shifts as model architectures evolve (e.g., longer contexts, more MoE layers).
