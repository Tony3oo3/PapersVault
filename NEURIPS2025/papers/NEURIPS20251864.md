## Problem

Existing DNN-based image watermarking methods are designed for low-resolution images (typically <512×512) and require full-image processing, making them infeasible for ultra-high resolution (UHR) images due to prohibitive memory and computational costs. Moreover, these methods lack robustness to the extreme cropping and scaling distortions that are prevalent during large-image transmission, where only a small fraction of the image may survive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251864/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image of arbitrary resolution (tested from 128×128 up to 4096×4096) and a binary message (default 30 bits, up to 100 bits) as input, and produces a watermarked image at the same resolution along with an extracted binary message. Crucially, the watermark is a resolution-independent template generated from normalized coordinates, so decoding requires only a 128×128 patch regardless of the original image size—achieving >98% accuracy with just 0.4% of a 2K image area. The embedding strength γ (default 0.02) controls the watermark amplitude added to the host image.

## Method

The core insight is to parameterize a template watermark using Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) that map continuous normalized pixel coordinates directly to RGB watermark values, eliminating the fixed-resolution constraint of CNN-based methods. Three key components enable this: (1) a resolution-independent coordinate sampling strategy that normalizes pixel positions to [-1,1] and samples a fixed 128×128 grid, (2) a hierarchical multi-scale coordinate embedding that combines bilinear interpolation over four learnable feature grids at increasing resolutions with Fourier feature mapping to capture both low- and high-frequency structure, and (3) a low-rank watermark injection module that reformulates the tensor fusion network's Cartesian product of position and message features via Canonical Polyadic Decomposition (rank 32), reducing parameters while preserving cross-modal interaction. The INR renders the watermark pixel-wise, which is added to the host image; a CNN decoder with squeeze-and-excitation blocks extracts the message from a 128×128 patch. Training is supervised with MSE loss on both visual fidelity and bit accuracy, using randomly scaled and cropped 128×128 patches from DIV2K with a composite noise layer simulating real-world distortions.

## Results

At 2K resolution (2048×2048), the proposed method achieves 98.74% decoding accuracy under extreme random cropping (128×128 patch, ~0.4% of image area) on DIV2K, far exceeding all baselines which drop to ~50%. At 4K (4096×4096), it still maintains 85.94% cropping accuracy using only 0.1% of the image area. At the standard 128×128 resolution, the method achieves 100% identity accuracy and >99% under Gaussian noise, rotation, translation, and color perturbations, outperforming the best low-resolution baseline MBRS on 6 of 8 distortion types. Embedding speed is 4 ms per 2K image (CPU-only), versus 74 ms (DWSF), 308 ms (TrustMark), and >20 min (RAIMARK).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the best low-resolution baseline MBRS at 128×128, the proposed method wins on 6 of 8 distortions (e.g., Identity 100% vs 99.18%, Gaussian Noise 99.83% vs 98.06%, Rotation 99.73% vs 95.37%) but trails slightly on JPEG compression (93.36% vs 96.56%) and Dropout (95.13% vs 95.75%). The decisive advantage appears at high resolutions: under 2K random cropping, the proposed method achieves 98.74% (DIV2K) while DWSF, TrustMark, and RAIMARK all collapse to ~50%, indicating they cannot localize or recover the watermark from a small patch of a large image. Under 2K scaling, the gap narrows but remains significant (98.66% vs 80.70% for DWSF, 76.07% for TrustMark, 62.33% for RAIMARK). At 4K, the proposed method still exceeds 83% for cropping and 92% for scaling, whereas all baselines remain near or below 65%. In terms of embedding speed, the proposed method (4 ms, CPU-only) is ~18× faster than DWSF (74 ms), ~77× faster than TrustMark (308 ms), and orders of magnitude faster than RAIMARK (>20 min), which requires per-image INR fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CNN-based DNN watermarking paradigm (HiDDeN, StegaStamp, MBRS) by replacing full-image convolutional processing with an INR-based template watermark generator that maps normalized coordinates to watermark pixel values, achieving resolution independence with fixed memory. It builds on the Tensor Fusion Network (Zadeh et al., 2017) by reformulating the Cartesian-product feature interaction into a low-rank CPD decomposition, enabling per-pixel watermark injection without prohibitive compute. Among high-resolution watermarking methods, it contrasts with block-based DWSF (vulnerable to misalignment), interpolation-based TrustMark (vulnerable to cropping), and per-image INR fine-tuning RAIMARK (extremely slow), positioning itself as a pre-computable, resolution-agnostic template approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 4ms CPU embedding for 2K images versus 20+ minutes for RAIMARK, and >98% decoding accuracy from just 0.4% of image area under extreme cropping, making UHR watermarking feasible for real-time pipelines in digital media and medical imaging. Key limitations include lower visual quality than content-dependent methods (PSNR ~37 vs 40+), limited watermark capacity (30–100 bits), and the acknowledged weakness of INR feature representation compared to CNNs. Open questions include whether hybrid CNN+INR architectures can close the quality gap, how to increase payload without sacrificing the redundancy that provides cropping/scaling robustness, and whether the template-based paradigm generalizes to video or 3D content.
