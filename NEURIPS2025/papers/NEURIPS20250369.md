## Problem

REPA, which aligns Diffusion Transformer hidden states with ViT visual encoders to accelerate training, has not been validated on the canonical U-Net architecture that empirically converges faster than DiTs. Adapting REPA to U-Nets is non-trivial because skip connections redistribute semantic density to intermediate layers, progressive downsampling creates spatial dimension mismatches with ViT's fixed-resolution tokens, and the architectural gap between U-Net and ViT makes strict token-wise cosine similarity an ineffective alignment metric.

## Input / Output

The model operates in the latent space of the sd-vae-ft-ema VAE, taking a random Gaussian noise tensor and an ImageNet class label as input, and denoising it over a flow-matching trajectory to produce a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image. During training, the alignment signal is computed against DINOv2 patch embeddings (16×16 spatial grid, 1024-dim) extracted from the corresponding clean image, while the U-Net's intermediate-stage features are at a reduced 8×8 spatial resolution before upsampling.

## Method

U-REPA builds on SiT↓, a flow-matching transformer with U-Net-style skip connections and 2× progressive downsampling/upsampling across three stages. Three key design choices address the U-Net-specific alignment challenges: (1) alignment is performed at the middle stage (layer 18 of 36 for XL), where skip connections concentrate semantic density, rather than at early layers as in DiT-based REPA; (2) U-Net features are first projected through a trainable MLP and then upsampled 2× to match DINOv2's native resolution, preserving the ViT encoder's fine-grained information; (3) a manifold loss is added alongside the standard token-wise REPA loss, which regularizes the pairwise cosine-similarity structure among tokens (Frobenius-norm distance between the two similarity matrices) rather than enforcing direct token-to-token correspondence, accommodating the larger feature-space gap between U-Net and ViT. The total loss is L_velocity + λ(L_REPA + w·L_ML) with w=3 optimal.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with classifier-free guidance, SiT↓-XL/2+U-REPA achieves FID 1.48 in just 200 epochs and FID 1.41 in 400 epochs, outperforming the REPA baseline (FID 1.42 at 800 epochs) with half the training time. At 512×512 resolution, U-REPA reduces FID from 2.44 to 2.21 and raises IS from 247.3 to 274.7 compared to SiT-XL/2+REPA. In terms of energy cost, U-REPA at 2M iterations consumes 1.96×10⁹ J versus 3.25×10⁹ J for REPA at 4M iterations, a 40% reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

U-REPA outperforms all ten baselines on FID. The most direct comparison is against REPA (Yu et al., 2024): U-REPA achieves FID 1.41 at 400 epochs versus REPA's 1.42 at 800 epochs, halving training time while slightly improving quality. Against MDTv2-XL/2 (Gao et al., 2024), the strongest masked-diffusion baseline, U-REPA improves FID from 1.58 to 1.41 with 63% fewer epochs. Compared to the flow-matching SiT-XL/2 (Ma et al., 2024) and the transformer-based DiT-XL/2 (Peebles et al., 2023), U-REPA reduces FID from 2.06/2.27 to 1.41 with roughly 70% fewer epochs. Against pixel-space diffusion methods (ADM-U, VDM++, Simple Diffusion) and latent-space alternatives (U-ViT, DifiT, MaskDiT), U-REPA achieves substantially lower FID (1.41 vs. 1.73–3.94), confirming that the U-Net architecture combined with representation alignment provides a clear convergence advantage over both isotropic transformer and pixel-space approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the REPA representation alignment framework (Yu et al., 2024), which aligns DiT hidden states with ViT encoder features, to the U-Net diffusion architecture. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that U-Net's skip connections fundamentally alter block functionality—shifting the optimal alignment position from early layers (as in DiT) to middle layers—and that progressive downsampling creates spatial dimension mismatches requiring explicit upsampling. The manifold loss, inspired by manifold knowledge distillation (Hao et al., 2022), is introduced to handle the larger feature-space gap between U-Net and ViT that makes strict token-wise cosine similarity insufficient. The base architecture SiT↓ is constructed by adding U-Net components (skip connections, downsampling) to the SiT flow-matching framework (Ma et al., 2024), with FLOPs aligned to DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

U-REPA achieves FID 1.41 on ImageNet-256 in 400 epochs versus REPA's 1.42 in 800 epochs, with ~40% lower energy consumption, establishing a new efficiency frontier for class-conditional image generation. The finding that U-Net architectures inherently converge faster than DiTs (even without alignment) and that this advantage compounds with representation alignment suggests the field may have over-invested in pure transformer backbones for diffusion. Open questions include whether U-REPA transfers to U-Net-heavy downstream tasks (low-level vision, video), whether more complex U-Net topologies (multi-stage, non-integer scaling) benefit further, and whether the manifold loss generalizes to other cross-architecture alignment settings beyond U-Net-to-ViT.
