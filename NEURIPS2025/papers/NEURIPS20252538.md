## Problem

Cryo-EM reconstruction of biomolecules with both conformational flexibility (non-rigid deformation) and compositional variation (parts present or absent) remains challenging because existing methods are limited in expressiveness, interpretability, or efficiency. Deformation-based methods like 3DFlex cannot handle missing parts, while latent-variable methods like CryoDRGN lack explicit motion models and part-based interpretability. The core difficulty is regularizing high-dimensional structural variability from extremely noisy 2D projections without overfitting, while recovering biologically meaningful part decompositions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D cryo-EM particle images (128×128 or 320×320 pixels, SNR < 5%) with pre-estimated poses (rotation R∈SO(3), translation t∈R³) and a crude initial 3D rigid reconstruction used to seed Gaussian components. It produces a hierarchical GMM with per-image latent codes (D=4) that generate 3D density maps supporting both amplitude modulation (compositional variability) and two-level deformation (rigid anchor motion + local Gaussian displacement). The part-based segmentation is an emergent output: clustering on learned per-Gaussian features (E=24) yields anchors that define interpretable structural parts without manual masks.

## Method

CryoSPIRE is a two-stage unsupervised framework. Stage 1 (part discovery) optimizes a coarse GMM (G=2048) where each Gaussian carries a learnable feature vector; an MLP generates per-Gaussian center offsets and amplitude modulations conditioned on image latent codes and features. After optimization, k-means++ clustering on the learned features naturally groups Gaussians into coherent parts that move or appear/disappear together, requiring no supervision. Stage 2 (part-aware hierarchical GMM) introduces one anchor per part, each with a rigid-body transformation (rotation + translation) predicted by an MLP from the anchor feature and latent code, plus per-Gaussian local displacements and sigmoid-gated amplitude modulations from separate MLPs. This two-level motion hierarchy—rigid anchor motion plus fine local deformation—enables piecewise smooth motion where nearby parts can move independently. Training minimizes L2 image reconstruction loss with a zero-mean Gaussian prior on latent codes and an L2 penalty on feature offsets, all optimized jointly on a single GPU.

## Results

On the CryoBench benchmark, CryoSPIRE achieves a mean Per-Conformation FSC AUC of 0.402 on IgG-1D, 0.386 on IgG-RL, and 0.427 on Ribosembly. It outperforms the strongest baseline RECOVAR (0.391, 0.372, 0.430) on the two IgG datasets by a clear margin, while on Ribosembly the difference from RECOVAR is not statistically significant. CryoSPIRE is the first GMM-based method to be successfully evaluated on CryoBench, surpassing all neural-field and linear-subspace baselines (CryoDRGN, DRGN-AI, 3DFlex, 3DVA) by 0.01–0.04 AUC across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CryoSPIRE outperforms RECOVAR, the strongest linear-subspace baseline, by 0.011 AUC on IgG-1D (0.402 vs 0.391) and 0.014 on IgG-RL (0.386 vs 0.372), where conformational flexibility dominates. On Ribosembly, which involves compositional heterogeneity with minimal conformational change, RECOVAR (0.430) marginally edges out CryoSPIRE (0.427), a difference the authors note is not statistically significant. Against neural-field methods, CryoSPIRE leads CryoDRGN by 0.036 (IgG-1D) and 0.037 (IgG-RL), and CryoDRGN-AI by 0.036 and 0.031 respectively. 3DFlex, which cannot handle compositional heterogeneity, is not evaluated on Ribosembly and trails CryoSPIRE by 0.066 on IgG-1D. 3DVA and 3D Classification are consistently the weakest baselines, with 3D Classification achieving only 0.297 on IgG-1D versus CryoSPIRE's 0.402.

## Relation to Prior Work

CryoSPIRE sits at the intersection of GMM-based cryo-EM reconstruction (E2GMM, DynaMight) and the hierarchical anchor-based structure of Scaffold-GS from 3D Gaussian Splatting. It departs from prior GMM methods by replacing positional encodings with learnable per-Gaussian features that naturally encode structural regularities, and from deformation-based methods like 3DFlex by jointly modeling conformational and compositional heterogeneity. The two-stage design—coarse part discovery followed by hierarchical part-aware refinement—provides inductive bias that linear subspace methods (3DVA, RECOVAR) and neural field approaches (CryoDRGN, DRGN-AI) lack, making the representation interpretable as biologically meaningful parts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CryoSPIRE is the first GMM-based method to be successfully benchmarked on CryoBench, establishing a new SOTA and demonstrating that sparse, part-based representations can match or exceed dense neural field and linear subspace models for heterogeneous reconstruction. The automatic part discovery without manual masks or supervision is practically significant for handling real datasets where part boundaries are unknown. Key open questions remain: validation of estimated structures on experimental data, the relationship between the learned latent space and biophysical energy landscapes, and the need for more principled clustering (beyond k-means++ with manual cluster count). The method also assumes initial structure and pose estimates, leaving fully ab initio heterogeneous reconstruction unresolved.
