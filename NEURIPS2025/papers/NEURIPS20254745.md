## Problem

Existing k-anonymity algorithms with provable guarantees face a stark tradeoff: O(k)-approximation requires Ω(n²k) time, while O(log k)-approximation demands O(n^{2k}) worst-case time. No prior work achieves nearly-linear runtime with a provable guarantee, and k-anonymity has not been studied in the massively parallel computation (MPC) model. The paper asks whether linear-in-n algorithms with provable approximation exist, whether efficient MPC algorithms are possible, and whether sub-k approximation is achievable in polynomial time.

## Input / Output

The input is a table T of n records, each a d-tuple over a finite alphabet Σ, plus an anonymity parameter k. Each record is one-hot encoded into a d|Σ|-dimensional binary vector, then projected to O(log n) dimensions via a Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform (exploiting d-sparsity for O(d log n) per-point cost). The output is a partition of records into groups of size at least k, where each group's attributes are suppressed (replaced with '*') to make all members indistinguishable, minimizing total suppressed attribute count. The algorithm also produces a C-approximate (R,k)-near-neighbor graph as an intermediate structure for the MPC variant.

## Method

The key reduction shows that an O(k)-approximation to k-anonymity follows from a minimum-size constrained clustering solution with an O(1) pointwise guarantee under squared ℓ₂ distance: if every point is within O(1)·ρ_k(p) of its cluster center, the resulting suppression is O(k)-optimal. To solve this clustering in nearly-linear time, the algorithm samples O(n log n / k) anchor points, uses LSH to compute approximate k-th nearest neighbors for anchors (reducing k-NN to 1-NN via multi-scale distance thresholds R_j = 3^j), and greedily assigns all points to clusters via a maximal independent set construction over the anchor neighborhood graph. The MPC extension builds C-approximate (R,k)-near-neighbor graphs in O(1) rounds using parallel LSH and sorting, then applies the [EMMZ22] β-ruling-set clustering framework across O(log d) distance scales, yielding Õ(log^{1+ε} n) rounds and O(n^{1+γ}(d+k)) total space.

## Results

On the Adult dataset (48,842 records, 8 quasi-identifier attributes), the proposed Ours (Heuristic) method achieves a ~51× speedup over the [ZWL+18] clustering baseline at k=10 (17.4s vs. 894.2s), ~67× at k=15 (5.9s vs. 394.4s), and ~66× at k=20 (3.4s vs. 222.0s), while producing a comparable number of hidden attributes across the full range k=5–30. The theoretical algorithm achieves an O(k)-approximation in Õ(nd + n·(n/k)^(1/C²+o(1))) time, and the MPC variant runs in Õ(log^(1+ε) n) rounds with O(n^(1+γ)(d+k)) total space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against the clustering-based heuristic of Zheng et al. [ZWL+18, ICA3PP 2018], which iteratively selects the point with the highest average distance from existing centers and assigns its k−1 nearest neighbors to the same cluster. In terms of anonymization quality (number of hidden attributes, Figure 1), the Ours (Heuristic) variant closely matches [ZWL+18] across the entire range k=5–30, while the purely theoretical algorithm performs comparably at small k but degrades as k increases. In terms of runtime (Table 2), Ours (Heuristic) is dramatically faster—achieving 51× to 67× speedups—by replacing the O(n²) nearest-neighbor computation with LSH-based approximate k-NN search and random sampling for center selection. The paper does not compare against other baselines such as Mondrian [LDR06] or the O(log k)-approximation of [PS07, KT12], limiting the empirical comparison to a single prior method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MPC algorithm for minimum-size constrained clustering from Epasto et al. (SODA 2022), adapting it to the k-anonymity setting via a reduction that maps records to one-hot binary vectors and uses Johnson-Lindenstrauss dimensionality reduction plus locality-sensitive hashing to achieve nearly-linear runtime. On the hardness side, it leverages the dense-vs-random conjecture framework from Chlamtac et al. (SODA 2017) to establish conditional lower bounds for a newly introduced single-point k-anonymity sub-problem. The paper departs from prior k-anonymity algorithms (which achieved O(k) approximation in O(n²k) time or O(log k) approximation in O(n^{2k}) time) by showing that the O(k) approximation can be obtained in nearly-linear time, closing a significant gap between the two regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first nearly-linear-time algorithm for k-anonymity with a provable approximation guarantee, and the first to address the problem in the Massively Parallel Computation model, making k-anonymity practical for large-scale distributed settings. The conditional hardness result (Theorem 1.3) provides evidence that the O(k) approximation ratio may be near-optimal, suggesting that the O(log k) approximation of Park and Shim (2007) likely cannot be achieved in polynomial time for general d and k. A key open question is whether the single-point hardness lower bound extends to the full k-anonymity problem. Empirically, the algorithmic ideas yield a ~50× speed-up over the best clustering-based heuristic on the Adult dataset while preserving comparable privacy-utility trade-offs.
