## Problem

Generic coder LLMs lack sufficient understanding of assembly code syntax and semantics for binary code similarity detection (BCSD), which is harder than source-code similarity due to information sparsity, lost structure, and diverse compiler settings. Existing binary-specific models are small and fail to generalize across optimization levels, architectures, and obfuscation techniques, while larger LLM-based approaches rely on closed-source models or excessive compute. The paper asks how to efficiently uptrain a small generic LLM into a specialized binary code embedding and matching expert.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255681/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a single binary function in assembly form, preprocessed by replacing arbitrary addresses, strings, and bytes with special tokens (addr, byte, str), flattening the basic-block hierarchy into one sequence with BLK separator tokens, and optionally prepending language tokens indicating compiler settings. The model produces a fixed-dimensional embedding per function; retrieval is performed by cosine similarity against a pool of 1,000 (custom dataset) or 10,000 (BinaryCorp) candidate functions, returning a ranked list. Evaluation spans cross-optimization (O0–O3), cross-architecture (x86, PowerPC, Arm, MIPS), and cross-obfuscation (substitution, flattening, bogus control flow) settings.

## Method

EBM is a four-phase training pipeline applied to Qwen2.5-Coder-0.5B: (1) data augmentation adds BLK structure tokens and compiler-setting language tokens to cleaned assembly sequences; (2) causal uptraining treats cross-architecture matching as a translation task, concatenating semantically identical function pairs and fine-tuning with next-token prediction so the model learns to 'translate' between assembly dialects; (3) LLM2Vec converts the causal decoder into a bidirectional encoder by enabling full attention and fine-tuning with masked next-token prediction, yielding BERT-like embeddings without retraining from scratch; (4) a cumulative GTE (cGTE) loss extends InfoNCE by adding query-query, key-key, and cross-pair contrastive terms and by accumulating embeddings across distributed batches before backpropagation, increasing effective negative pairs under limited GPU memory. The ablation shows LLM2Vec contributes the largest MRR gain (2–4×), causal training is critical for cross-architecture generalization, and cGTE helps most on complex tasks like cross-obfuscation.

## Results

EBM (0.5B) achieves an average MRR of 0.916 and Recall@1 of 0.872 on cross-optimization retrieval (pool size 1,000), outperforming the next-best baseline CodeGemma (2B) by 6% MRR and 6.4% Recall@1. On the more challenging cross-architecture task, EBM reaches 0.811 MRR and 0.721 Recall@1, surpassing CodeGemma (0.474 MRR) by over 33 points. On BinaryCorp (pool size 10,000), EBM attains 0.872 MRR and 0.849 Recall@1, edging out CLAP (0.860 MRR, 0.827 Recall@1) while using a model 26–60× smaller (0.5B vs. 13B/30B backbone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBM (0.5B) outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluation setting. Against non-LLM methods (SAFE, PalmTree, Asm2Vec, OrderMatters), EBM leads by 40–90 MRR points, confirming that LLM-based approaches have decisively surpassed traditional binary similarity techniques. Among LLM baselines, EBM beats the larger Qwen2.5-Coder (1.5B) by 6.7 MRR points (0.916 vs. 0.849) and CodeGemma (2B) by 6 MRR points (0.916 vs. 0.856) on cross-optimization, despite being 3–4× smaller. The gap widens dramatically on cross-architecture: EBM achieves 0.811 MRR versus 0.474 for CodeGemma and 0.428 for Qwen2.5-Coder, a margin of 33–38 points attributable to the causal translation training. On BinaryCorp (pool 10,000), EBM (0.872 MRR) narrowly surpasses CLAP (0.860 MRR) while avoiding CLAP's dependency on GPT-3.5 for natural-language supervision and its 13B/30B backbone. The zero-shot Qwen2.5-Emb (1.5B) baseline (0.694 MRR) already exceeds all non-LLM methods, but EBM's fine-tuning adds a further 22-point gain, validating the multi-phase training pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLM2Vec (BehnamGhader et al., 2024), which converts causal LLMs into bidirectional encoders via masked next token prediction, and the GTE contrastive loss (Li et al., 2023), extending it into a cumulative variant (cGTE) that aggregates embeddings across distributed batches. It departs from prior binary code similarity detection (BCSD) work in two key ways: rather than training small models from scratch (SAFE, PalmTree, Asm2Vec) or relying on large/closed-source LLMs (CLAP uses GPT-3.5 + Llama 13B/30B), it uptrains a compact 0.5B generic coder model through a multi-phase pipeline. The translation-style causal learning is inspired by cross-lingual pretraining (Conneau & Lample, 2019), reframing cross-architecture assembly matching as a language translation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that effective binary code similarity detection becomes accessible on modest hardware (4× RTX 6000) with a 0.5B model, removing the dependency on closed-source APIs or 13B+ parameter models that limit scalability for small labs and companies. Cross-architecture detection, which the authors note has been largely unstudied, is addressed with strong results, opening the door to cross-platform vulnerability and malware matching. Key limitations include the absence of evaluation on larger LLMs, relatively small training data compared to frontier coder models, and no demonstration on downstream tasks like malware or vulnerability detection, leaving open whether the learned embeddings transfer to those settings.
