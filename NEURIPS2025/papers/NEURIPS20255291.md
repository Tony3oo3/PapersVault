## Problem

While MLLM-based embedding approaches outperform CLIP-style models on complex cross-modal tasks (multilingual retrieval, document understanding, interleaved encodings), the underlying reasons for this superiority remain unexplored. Specifically, it is unclear whether MLLMs possess inherent cross-modal alignment that can be unlocked with minimal additional training, and how a model's generative capability relates to its representational potential after contrastive refinement.

## Input / Output

The model accepts arbitrary combinations of text, RGB images, video, and audio as input and produces a single embedding vector per input for downstream similarity matching (retrieval, classification, clustering). The text-only variant is trained exclusively on ~276k NLI sentence triplets (premise-entailment-contradiction), while the multimodal variant adds ~94k synthetic multimodal pairs (visual documents, image-text, interleaved formats) for a total of ~370k triplets. Embeddings are extracted from the language decoder's hidden representations, with modality-specific encoders and projectors frozen during training.

## Method

LCO-EMB exploits the key insight that MLLMs implicitly achieve cross-modal alignment during generative pretraining, where the language decoder learns to integrate multimodal signals into a shared representation space for text generation. The framework applies LoRA-based contrastive fine-tuning to only the language decoder using text-only NLI data, treating CL as a lightweight post-hoc refinement that projects pre-aligned generative embeddings into a similarity-matching subspace rather than as the primary alignment mechanism. This minimal perturbation preserves both the model's generative capabilities and the latent cross-modal alignment structure. The paper further identifies a Generation-Representation Scaling Law (GRSL) showing that post-CL representational quality scales positively with the MLLM's pre-CL generative capability, and provides a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound formally linking the generative prior's mutual information to an upper bound on contrastive performance. Empirically, LCO-EMB achieves state-of-the-art on MIEB-Lite using ~21× less training data than GME, and the GRSL is validated on SeaDoc, a new low-resource Southeast Asian visual document retrieval benchmark.

## Results

On MIEB-Lite (51 tasks), LCO-EMB-Omni(7B) achieves a mean score of 68.8 (multilingual) and 67.6 (English), setting a new state-of-the-art using only ~370k training triplets—approximately 21× less data than GME (~8M). The text-only variant LCO-EMB-VL(7B) reaches 60.4 (multilingual mean) without any multimodal supervision, already surpassing the proprietary Voyage-Multimodal-3 (58.1). On the GRSL validation task SeaDoc, continual OCR-intense pretraining before contrastive learning further improves retrieval performance, confirming that generative capability sets an upper bound on representational quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCO-EMB-Omni(7B) outperforms all seven baselines on MIEB-Lite Mean(m), leading GME(7B) by +4.3 points (68.8 vs 64.5) while using ~21× less training data (370k vs 8M pairs). Against the strongest MLLM baseline mmE5(11B), LCO-EMB gains +7.0 points (68.8 vs 61.8), and against the proprietary Voyage-Multimodal-3, the margin is +10.7 points (68.8 vs 58.1). Notably, even the text-only LCO-EMB-VL(7B) (60.4) surpasses Voyage-Multimodal-3 (58.1) and E5-V (51.9) without any multimodal training data. On CLIP-style encoder baselines, LCO-EMB-Omni(7B) leads SigLIP-so400m by +15.3 and CLIP-ViT-bigG by +17.5 on Mean(m). The largest relative gains appear on tasks leveraging MLLM reasoning: cross-lingual Visual STS (82.4 vs GME's 75.4), compositionality (50.1 vs mmE5's 27.8), and multilingual retrieval (64.3 vs GME's 62.4). LCO-EMB also achieves competitive results on clustering and linear probing—areas where MLLM-based models typically lag behind CLIP-style encoders—narrowing the gap to within 2–3 points of SigLIP on clustering (80.0 vs 75.9) and matching or exceeding it on linear probing (74.1 vs 78.8 for SigLIP, but 72.5 for LCO-EMB-VL(7B) multi).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the CLIP-style paradigm where large-scale multimodal contrastive learning is the primary mechanism for cross-modal alignment, and from prior MLLM-based embedding work (E5-V, VLM2Vec, GME, mmE5) that still relies on substantial multimodal paired data for CL fine-tuning. It repurposes the MLLM's generative pretraining as the source of cross-modal alignment, reframing CL as a lightweight post-hoc refinement rather than the alignment engine itself. Theoretically, it inverts the 'Representation-Generation Scaling Law' studied by Cambrian-1 and Yang et al. (where generation scales with encoder quality) by proposing a 'Generation-Representation Scaling Law' where representation quality scales with the MLLM's own generative capability, formalized via a PAC-Bayesian bound. The language-centric data strategy (text-only NLI pairs plus minimal multimodal calibration) draws on E5-V's text-only generalization but extends it to omnimodal settings with a principled LoRA-based preservation of latent alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that text-only contrastive learning generalizes to improve embeddings across image, audio, and video modalities—without any multimodal supervision—challenges the assumption that cross-modal alignment requires cross-modal data, and provides a practical efficiency path (orders of magnitude less training data and compute) for building strong omnimodal embedders. The GRSL, if it generalizes beyond the tested Qwen/LLaVA families, suggests that the most effective route to better multimodal representations is investing in generative pretraining quality rather than scaling contrastive data, which could reshape how labs allocate compute. Open questions include whether the latent alignment hypothesis holds for non-autoregressive or non-decoder-based MLLM architectures, whether joint generative-contrastive training (acknowledged as future work) could further close the gap, and whether the GRSL bound is tight enough to be practically predictive for model selection.
