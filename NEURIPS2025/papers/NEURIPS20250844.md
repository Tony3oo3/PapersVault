## Problem

Pre-Norm transformers offer stable training via a strong identity path but consistently underperform Post-Norm in final accuracy, while Post-Norm delivers better generalization yet suffers from gradient vanishing and can diverge in deep architectures. No existing normalization placement resolves this stability-versus-performance trade-off within a single transformer block.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250844/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text token sequences with a fixed context length of 4096 tokens, producing next-token probability distributions for autoregressive language modeling. Models are trained from scratch on the OLMoE Mix dataset and evaluated on a suite of 13+ downstream benchmarks (ARC, HellaSwag, PIQA, MMLU, etc.) using the LM Eval Harness. The architectural change is entirely internal to each transformer block's normalization placement, so the external I/O interface is identical to any standard decoder-only LLM.

## Method

HybridNorm replaces the uniform Pre-Norm or Post-Norm placement with a per-sublayer hybrid: QKV normalization (RMSNorm applied independently to Q, K, and V before the scaled dot-product) is used inside the multi-head attention block, while Post-Norm (normalization after the residual addition) is applied in the feed-forward network block. A variant, HybridNorm*, additionally treats the first transformer block with Pre-Norm on both MHA and FFN to stabilize early-layer gradient flow. Theoretically, the paper proves that QKV-Norm decouples the gradient of each attention weight matrix from the others (reducing coupling from O(‖W_K‖‖W_V‖‖W_O‖) to O(‖W_O‖)), preventing the vicious cycle of weight-norm growth that causes Pre-Norm collapse. Empirically, across dense models (151M–7B) and MoE models (1B active / 7B total), HybridNorm* achieves lower training loss, lower perplexity, and higher downstream accuracy than Pre-Norm, Post-Norm, QK-Norm, and Mix-LN, while remaining stable at 29 layers where Post-Norm diverges.

## Results

On the 7B dense model (150B tokens), HybridNorm* achieves 63.06% average accuracy across 10 downstream benchmarks, outperforming Pre-Norm (60.61%) by 2.45 points, with the largest gain on Basic Arithmetic (+7.17, from 43.50% to 50.67%). Training loss drops from 2.469 to 2.430, and C4 perplexity improves from 15.32 to 14.83. On the 1.2B dense model (1T tokens), HybridNorm* reaches 64.15% average vs. Pre-Norm's 62.99%, and on MoE-1B-7B (500B tokens), HybridNorm* achieves 66.12% vs. Pre-Norm's 64.95%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HybridNorm* consistently outperforms Pre-Norm (Xiong et al., ICML 2020) across every evaluated task and model scale: on the 7B model it gains +2.45 average accuracy, on 1.2B dense +1.16, and on MoE-1B-7B +1.17. Against Post-Norm (Vaswani et al., NeurIPS 2017), HybridNorm achieves lower training loss (2.74 vs. 2.76) and higher HellaSwag accuracy (53.35 vs. 51.20) on the 550M model; critically, Post-Norm diverges entirely at 29 layers while HybridNorm remains stable. Compared to Mix-LN (Li et al., ICLR 2025), which applies Post-Norm to early layers and Pre-Norm to deeper layers, HybridNorm achieves lower loss (2.74 vs. 2.76), lower C4 perplexity (20.00 vs. 20.43), and higher HellaSwag (53.35 vs. 51.29). Against QK-Norm (Henry et al., EMNLP Findings 2020), which normalizes only Query and Key, HybridNorm's additional Value normalization yields further gains in loss (2.74 vs. 2.75) and HellaSwag (53.35 vs. 52.29), confirming that normalizing all three QKV components is more effective than QK alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

HybridNorm sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: the Pre-Norm/Post-Norm trade-off analysis (Xiong et al., 2020) and QKV normalization for attention stability (Rybakov et al., 2024; Menary et al., 2024). It departs from Mix-LN (Li et al., 2025), which applies Post-Norm to shallow layers and Pre-Norm to deep layers, by instead integrating both normalization philosophies *within* each transformer block—QKV-Norm in the MHA and Post-Norm in the FFN. The theoretical gradient-coupling analysis extends the Jacobian framework of Noci et al. (2022) and Ormaniec et al. (2025) to formally characterize how QKV-Norm decouples weight gradients relative to Pre-Norm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HybridNorm offers a simple, drop-in normalization strategy that consistently improves both training stability and downstream accuracy across dense (550M–7B) and MoE (1B–7B) models, making it a practical candidate for standard LLM training pipelines. The clean theoretical result—that gradient coupling follows Pre-Norm > QK-Norm > QKV-Norm—provides a mechanistic explanation that goes beyond empirical tuning. Open questions include whether the approach composes well with other attention variants (e.g., MLA, GQA with different head configurations), whether the first-block special treatment (HybridNorm*) generalizes to architectures without a distinct embedding layer, and how it interacts with other stabilization techniques like DeepNorm or Sandwich-LN at extreme depths.
