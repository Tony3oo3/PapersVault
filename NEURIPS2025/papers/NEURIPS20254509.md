## Problem

Transformer-based stereo super-resolution models suffer from attention noise—non-negligible attention weights assigned to irrelevant context that dilute key feature representations and cause over-smoothing in reconstructed images. Additionally, existing stereo cross-attention modules (SCAM) rely on strict horizontal epipolar alignment, which degrades performance under calibration errors or local distortion. The Differential Transformer, designed to suppress attention noise in LLMs, fails to transfer directly to visual tasks: simply swapping it into SwinFIRSSR actually reduces PSNR across all benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of low-resolution RGB stereo images (left and right views) produced by bicubic downsampling at ×2 or ×4 scale, and outputs the corresponding high-resolution stereo pair. During training, inputs are cropped into 32×96 patches with stride 16; the reconstruction module uses a 1×1 convolution followed by pixel-shuffle upsampling to restore full resolution. A residual connection adds the shallow 3×3 convolution features back to the deep features before reconstruction, so the network learns only the high-frequency detail residual.

## Method

DIFFSSR stacks 13 Diff Cross-Attention Blocks (DCABs, 180 channels) between a shallow 3×3 conv encoder and a pixel-shuffle decoder. Each DCAB contains three Diff Cross-Attention Layers, each combining a Window-based Multi-head Differential Attention (W-MDA) module, a Sliding Stereo Cross-Attention Module (SSCAM), and a two-layer MLP. W-MDA splits queries and keys into two halves, computes two softmax attention maps, and subtracts the second (scaled by a learnable λ) from the first, thereby amplifying relevant context while canceling noise; it uses Relative Positional Encoding and operates within local non-overlapping windows for efficiency. SSCAM replaces the fixed horizontal epipolar search of SCAM with an Unfold-based sliding window (size 3) that lets each query attend to a small vertical neighborhood in the opposite view, making cross-view fusion robust to local misalignment. The model is trained for 500K iterations with Charbonnier L1 loss, cosine-annealed learning rate (2e-4 → 1e-7), and data augmentation on 4× RTX 3090 GPUs.

## Results

DIFFSSR achieves state-of-the-art stereo image super-resolution across all four benchmark datasets. At ×4 scale, it reaches 24.40 dB PSNR / 0.7722 SSIM on Flickr1024, 27.15 dB / 0.8201 on KITTI 2012, 26.23 dB / 0.8092 on KITTI 2015, and 30.55 dB / 0.8723 on Middlebury (left view), surpassing SwinFIRSSR by 0.11 dB on Flickr1024 and 0.22 dB on Middlebury. At ×2 scale, DIFFSSR attains 30.27 dB / 0.9301 on Flickr1024 and 36.61 dB / 0.9609 on Middlebury. The model uses 19.86M (×2) and 20.01M (×4) parameters, roughly 17% fewer than SwinFIRSSR (23.94M / 24.09M) while delivering higher quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFSSR outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and scale. Against the strongest prior method SwinFIRSSR [4], DIFFSSR gains +0.13 dB PSNR on Flickr1024 (×2: 30.27 vs 30.14; ×4: 24.40 vs 24.29) and +0.20 dB on Middlebury (×4: 30.64 vs 30.44), while using ~17% fewer parameters. Against NAFSSR-L [6], the gap is larger at ×4, with DIFFSSR achieving 30.64 vs 30.20 dB on Middlebury (+0.44 dB) and 24.40 vs 24.17 dB on Flickr1024 (+0.23 dB). Among lightweight stereo SR methods, iPASSR [5] (1.37M params) trails DIFFSSR by ~1.6 dB on Flickr1024 (×2), and PASSRNet [12] by ~1.9 dB. SISR-only baselines (EDSR [15], RDN [29], RCAN [2]) are consistently 1.5–2.5 dB below DIFFSSR, confirming the benefit of stereo cross-view fusion. SCGLANet [34] (25.29M params) and Steformer [19] (1.34M params) are both outperformed at ×4, with SCGLANet lagging by 0.10 dB on Flickr1024 and Steformer by 0.82 dB. MSSFNet [32] (1.82M params) is surpassed by 0.41 dB on Flickr1024 (×4), and SRRes+SAM [33] by 0.81 dB on the same dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Differential Transformer (Ye et al., 2024), originally designed to suppress attention noise in large language models, to the visual domain of stereo image super-resolution. It builds on the SwinFIRSSR architecture (Zhang et al., 2022) as its primary framework and redesigns the Stereo Cross-Attention Module (SCAM) from NAFSSR (Chu et al., 2022) into a sliding-window variant (SSCAM). A key departure from prior work is the finding that directly substituting the DIFF Transformer into SwinFIRSSR degrades performance, necessitating a full architectural redesign (DCAB) with domain-specific modifications such as replacing RoPE with RPE and SwiGLU with a standard MLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first exploration of the Differential Transformer in visual tasks, establishing that the mechanism requires careful adaptation (window-based attention, relative positional encoding, simpler FFN) before it can benefit dense spatial data. The SSCAM's robustness to epipolar misalignment addresses a practical limitation of existing stereo SR methods, and the training-free window-size generalization (Table 8) offers deployment flexibility. However, the PSNR gains over SwinFIRSSR are modest (≈0.1–0.2 dB), no error bars are reported, and the approach has only been validated on stereo SR—whether differential attention transfers to other restoration or generation tasks remains an open question.
