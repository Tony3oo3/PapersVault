## Problem

Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) and domain-generalized semantic segmentation (DGSS) address complementary but separate challenges: OVSS recognizes unseen classes via text but degrades sharply under domain shift, while DGSS maintains robustness across domains but cannot handle novel categories. No prior work unifies both capabilities in a single framework, leaving a gap for real-world scenarios (e.g., autonomous driving in adverse conditions) that require simultaneously segmenting arbitrary queried objects and generalizing to unseen environments.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resolutions range from 1042×1042 on DELIVER to 2048×1024 on Cityscapes) and an arbitrary set of K text class labels as input. The image is processed in parallel by a frozen VFM encoder and a frozen DepthAnything V2 depth encoder, while the text labels are encoded once by a frozen CLIP text encoder into shared semantic embeddings. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask M ∈ R^(H×W×K) assigning each pixel to one of the K open-vocabulary classes, combining fine-grained spatial boundaries with open-vocabulary class assignment.

## Method

Vireo is a single-stage framework that keeps the VFM, DepthAnything V2, and CLIP text encoders fully frozen, training only ~3.8M parameters across three modules. The Tunable Vireo module injects layer-specific GeoText Queries into the frozen VFM, where each query fuses depth features and text embeddings via cross-attention to progressively refine visual representations across encoder layers, exploiting the domain-invariance of geometric structure. The Coarse Mask Prior Embedding (CMPE) generates weakly supervised coarse masks from multi-scale gated features, injecting dense gradient signals back through the frozen encoder to accelerate convergence and strengthen textual influence. The Domain-Open-Vocabulary Vector Embedding Head (DOV-VEH) uses a pixel decoder and Transformer decoder where the refined GeoText Queries act as learnable queries, producing mask and classification embeddings whose dot product yields the final open-vocabulary segmentation prediction.

## Results

Under the Cityscapes → ACDC + BDD100K + Mapillary + GTA5 setting, Vireo achieves 60.6% mIoU on Night-ACDC, 82.3% on Fog-ACDC, 76.3% on Rain-ACDC, and 76.2% on Snow-ACDC, outperforming the strongest DGSS baseline FADA by 3.2, 2.1, 1.3, and 2.7 points respectively. Under the GTA5 → Cityscapes + BDD100K + Mapillary setting, Vireo reaches 70.69% on Cityscapes, 62.91% on BDD100K, and 69.63% on Mapillary. On the open-vocabulary DELIVER benchmark (Cityscapes → DELIVER), Vireo attains 35.73% mIoU in sunny conditions and 27.51% at night, surpassing the best OVSS baseline (CAT-Seg) by 7.5 and 7.0 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vireo outperforms all 17 baselines across every target domain in both evaluation settings. Against the strongest VFM-based DGSS baselines, Vireo surpasses FADA (NeurIPS'24) by 3.2 mIoU on Night-ACDC (60.6 vs. 57.4), 2.1 on Fog-ACDC (82.3 vs. 80.2), and 4.1 on GTA5 (67.86 vs. 63.78); it also beats REIN (CVPR'24) by 4.7 on Night-ACDC and 5.5 on GTA5. Against the best OVSS baselines, Vireo leads SED (CVPR'24) by 21.9 mIoU on Night-ACDC (60.6 vs. 38.7) and 13.3 on Fog-ACDC (82.3 vs. 69.0), and outperforms FC-CLIP (NeurIPS'23) by 19.8 on Night-ACDC. On the open-vocabulary DELIVER benchmark, Vireo exceeds the best OVSS method CAT-Seg by 7.5 mIoU in sunny conditions (35.73 vs. 28.21) and 7.0 at night (27.51 vs. 20.56), while also achieving the highest ADE847 score (7.31 vs. 6.95). In the GTA5 → Cityscapes transfer, Vireo gains 2.46 mIoU over FADA on Cityscapes (70.69 vs. 68.23) and 1.54 over FADA on Mapillary (69.63 vs. 68.09). The consistent margins across all 10 target-domain columns confirm that Vireo's depth-geometry and text-alignment design yields simultaneous gains in both domain robustness and open-vocabulary recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vireo unifies two previously separate research threads—open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS, e.g., FC-CLIP, SED, CAT-Seg) and domain-generalized semantic segmentation (DGSS, e.g., REIN, FADA)—into a single framework for the first time. It builds directly on REIN's paradigm of injecting learnable prompts into a frozen VFM encoder, but extends it by coupling each prompt layer with depth features from DepthAnything V2 and CLIP text embeddings via the GeoText Query mechanism. The depth-integration strategy is motivated by DepthForge's finding that geometric cues are largely invariant to illumination and texture shifts, and the overall design exploits the observed complementarity between DGSS (encoder-centric) and OVSS (decoder-centric) paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first single-stage OV-DGSS framework, Vireo addresses a practical gap in autonomous driving perception where a system must simultaneously recognize novel object categories and remain robust under adverse weather or lighting. The parameter-efficiency result—outperforming FADA (11.65M params) with only 3.78M trainable parameters across four different VFMs—suggests that geometric-textual prompt design can substitute for heavier adapter modules. Key limitations include the assumption of a reliable RGB stream (no fallback for occlusion, glare, or sensor failure) and evaluation restricted to driving-centric scenes; extending the framework to multi-sensor fusion and non-driving open-vocabulary domains remains open.
