## Problem

Existing multi-modality object ReID methods assume all modalities (RGB, NIR, TIR) are present during both training and inference, yet real-world deployments frequently encounter modality-missing inputs due to sensor failures, privacy constraints, or security requirements. Prior reconstruction-based approaches (DENet, TOP-ReID) handle missing modalities only at inference while still requiring fully observed data for training, leaving a critical gap for scenarios where data incompleteness affects the training phase as well.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255454/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts triplets of RGB, NIR, and TIR images where any subset may be absent (represented as zero-pixel tensors), with missing rates controlled by a probability tuple η during training. At inference, missing visual modalities are replaced by compensatory textual embeddings generated through a frozen CLIP text encoder, and the final retrieval feature is a concatenation of available visual embeddings and generated text embeddings. The output is a standard ReID ranking list matching query identities against a gallery, evaluated via mAP and Rank-1 accuracy across seven modality-availability scenarios.

## Method

Miss-ReID comprises three collaborative modules operating on a frozen CLIP backbone. (1) M-bHIPR builds per-modality memory banks storing identity prototypes updated via EMA, with intra-modality ClusterNCE contrastive loss enforcing identity-invariant features within each modality. (2) M-iOSM uses learnable structure-aware query vectors to perform multi-head attention over local patch embeddings from all available modalities, distilling modality-invariant object structural representations that are fused across modalities. (3) L-dMMC inverts the structural representation into a pseudo-word token via an MLP, embeds it with modality-specific learnable prompts into a crafted text template, and passes it through the frozen CLIP text encoder to produce compensatory textual embeddings aligned to the visual feature space via a memory-based text-vision contrastive loss. The key insight is leveraging VLMs' inherent vision-text alignment so that text-derived semantic features can substitute absent visual cues, and the entire pipeline is trained under simulated modality dropout (random zeroing with probability η) so the model never assumes data completeness.

## Results

On RGBNT201 (person ReID), Miss-ReID achieves the best mean performance across six modality-missing inference scenarios: 54.6% mAP and 55.7% R-1, surpassing the second-best DeMo by 3.9% mAP and 4.9% R-1. In the most challenging scenario where both RGB and TIR are missing (RNT), Miss-ReID reaches 34.5% mAP / 33.3% R-1, outperforming DeMo (26.3/24.9) and TOP-ReID (26.2/26.0). On RGBNT100 (vehicle ReID), Miss-ReID attains 65.6% mean mAP and 86.8% mean R-1, with the lowest average degradation rates (20.5% mAP, 10.2% R-1) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Miss-ReID outperforms all five baselines on the mean mAP and R-1 across the six modality-missing scenarios on RGBNT201. Against the strongest competitor DeMo (AAAI 2025), Miss-ReID gains +3.9% mAP (54.6 vs 50.7) and +4.9% R-1 (55.7 vs 50.8), with the largest margin in the most challenging RNT scenario (+8.2% mAP, +8.4% R-1). Compared to TOP-ReID (AAAI 2024), the improvement is even more pronounced: +10.2% mAP and +10.3% R-1 on average. IDEA (CVPR 2025) reports only mAP for missing scenarios (50.5% mean), still trailing Miss-ReID by 4.1%. PCB (ECCV 2017) serves as a weak historical baseline, with Miss-ReID exceeding it by over 34% mean mAP. On the vehicle ReID benchmark RGBNT100 (Table 4), Miss-ReID similarly dominates: 65.6% mean mAP vs DeMo's 66.3% (nearly tied) but 86.8% mean R-1 vs DeMo's 82.8%, and substantially outperforms TOP-ReID (55.4/77.8) and DENet (50.1/74.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multi-modality object ReID (established by PFNet, EDITOR, DeMo, IDEA) by addressing a practical gap: prior methods assume modality completeness during both training and inference, while earlier missing-modality attempts (DENet, TOP-ReID) handle incomplete inputs only at inference while still requiring fully observed training data. Miss-ReID departs from this lineage by introducing modality-missing simulation during training and, more notably, by repurposing the textual inversion paradigm (Gal et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2024) within a VLM (CLIP) framework to generate compensatory text embeddings that substitute absent visual features. The memory-bank prototype mechanism draws from ClusterNCE-style contrastive learning, while the structure-aware query design follows standard multi-head attention patterns, making the architectural novelty primarily in the integration strategy rather than in individual components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a genuinely underexplored deployment gap: real-world multi-spectral surveillance systems face sensor failures, privacy restrictions, and security constraints that make modality completeness an unrealistic assumption. By demonstrating that VLM-derived textual features can partially substitute missing visual modalities with significantly smaller performance degradation than reconstruction-based baselines, the paper opens a pathway toward more resilient multi-modality ReID in practice. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (RGBNT201, RGBNT100) and three modalities, and the authors acknowledge that extreme cases (complete modality collapse) and expanded modality integration (LiDAR, event cameras, audio) remain open challenges. The reliance on a frozen CLIP text encoder also raises questions about scalability to more complex or domain-specific visual structures.
