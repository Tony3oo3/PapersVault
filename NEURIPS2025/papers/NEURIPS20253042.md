## Problem

Existing SOC-based diffusion samplers (PIS, DDS) learn controlled dynamics from stationary or uninformative reference processes, failing to leverage annealed reference dynamics that progressively guide trajectories toward the target. Methods that do employ annealed references (PDDS, CRAFT, SCLD) rely on importance-weighted sampling during training, limiting solver flexibility. NAAS is the first to integrate the SOC perspective with annealed reference SDEs while remaining free of IWS.

## Input / Output

The method requires a twice-differentiable target energy U_1(x) (for Hessian-vector products in the adjoint ODE) and a tractable prior energy U_0(x), typically a Gaussian. It outputs a two-stage controlled SDE: a Brownian-motion-driven process over [-1,0] that generates samples from a learned prior μ, followed by an annealed Langevin process over [0,1] whose terminal distribution matches ν ∝ exp(-U_1). For the molecular benchmark, the state space is 60-dimensional internal coordinates of alanine dipeptide, and evaluation uses KL divergence on torsion angles and Wasserstein distance on energy values.

## Method

NAAS formulates a SOC problem (Theorem 3.1) with annealed reference dynamics dX_t = -(σ_t²/2)∇U_t dt + σ_t dW_t and running cost ∂_t U_t, proving via Hopf-Cole transform that the optimal marginal satisfies p_t* ∝ exp(-U_t - V_t), which equals the target at t=1 since V_1=0. Because the required prior μ ∝ exp(-U_0 - V_0) is intractable, a second SOC problem over [-1,0] learns a control v to generate samples from μ, yielding a unified two-control formulation (Theorem 3.4). The controls u and v are optimized alternately: u via adjoint matching (requiring a backward ODE for the lean adjoint involving Hessian-vector products of U_t), and v via reciprocal adjoint matching (exploiting the vanishing drift to sample intermediate states from a conditional Gaussian given only endpoints). Replay buffers cache state-adjoint pairs every 200–500 iterations to avoid recomputing the full forward-backward system at each step.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmarks, NAAS achieves Sinkhorn distance 0.10±0.0075 on MW54 (5d), 496.48±27.08 on GMM40 (50d), and 394.55±29.35 on MoS (50d), outperforming the second-best method (SCLD) by over 75% on MW54 and GMM40 and by ~40% on MoS. On the Alanine Dipeptide molecular generation task (60d), NAAS achieves E(·)W₂ = 1.076, a 5× improvement over PIS (5.918) and DDS (5.467), while also achieving the best KL divergence on φ (0.260) and γ₁ (0.272). The annealed reference dynamics provide a strong initialization: NAAS begins training on MoS with MMD ≈ 0.22 and Sinkhorn ≈ 2600, already competitive with the final results of SMC, PIS, and DDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAAS outperforms all eight baselines on MW54 (Sinkhorn 0.10 vs. SCLD's 0.44, a 77% improvement) and GMM40 (Sinkhorn 496.48 vs. SCLD's 3787.73, a 87% improvement), and on MoS (Sinkhorn 394.55 vs. SCLD's 656.10, a 40% improvement). On Funnel, SMC-ESS (117.48) marginally beats NAAS (132.30) on Sinkhorn, though NAAS is competitive with CRAFT (134.34) and SCLD (134.23). Among the SOC-based diffusion samplers (PIS, DDS, AS, SCLD), NAAS dominates on every benchmark where they are directly comparable, with the largest gaps on the high-dimensional GMM40 and MoS tasks. On the Alanine Dipeptide molecular generation task, NAAS substantially outperforms both PIS and DDS on γ₁ (0.272 vs. 0.453 and 4.095) and energy Wasserstein distance E(·)W₂ (1.076 vs. 5.918 and 5.467), while achieving the best φ KL divergence (0.260 vs. 0.597 and 0.493). The paper also shows that the alternating optimization scheme outperforms joint optimization (Table 4: MW54 0.10 vs. 0.20; MoS 394.55 vs. 597.99), confirming the benefit of decoupling the prior-learning and annealed-transport stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAAS extends the stochastic optimal control (SOC) framework for diffusion sampling by replacing the stationary or memoryless reference SDEs used in PIS (Zhang and Chen, 2022) and DDS (Vargas et al., 2023) with annealed Langevin dynamics that progressively guide trajectories toward the target. The paper proves that DDS is a special case of its formulation (Corollary 3.2) and that the prior-learning subproblem reduces exactly to PIS (Lemma 3.3), unifying these methods under a single SOC objective. For solving the resulting SOC problem, NAAS adopts Adjoint Matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2025) and its reciprocal variant from Adjoint Sampling (Havens et al., 2025), deliberately avoiding the importance-weighted sampling (IWS) required by competing annealed samplers such as PDDS, CRAFT, and SCLD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that the annealed reference dynamics provide a remarkably strong initialization: on the 50d MoS benchmark, NAAS begins training with an MMD (~0.22) and Sinkhorn distance (~2600) already competitive with or superior to the final performance of SMC, PIS, and DDS, meaning the learned control only needs to correct residual mismatches rather than build the distribution from scratch. This suggests a general principle that informative reference paths can dramatically reduce the learning burden in diffusion-based samplers. However, the method requires computing the Hessian of the energy function during sampling, needs two separate control networks, and has only been validated on problems up to 60 dimensions, leaving open whether the approach scales to the thousands-of-dimensions regime relevant to molecular and protein applications. The trade-off between the two alternating optimization stages and the choice of annealing schedule remain unexplored.
