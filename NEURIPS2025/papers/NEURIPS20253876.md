## Problem

Existing low-light image enhancement methods rely on deterministic pixel-level mappings between paired low/normal-light images, neglecting the continuous physical process of luminance transitions governed by scene radiance and sensor responses. This causes severe performance degradation when normal-light references are unavailable, as models overfit to static domain relationships rather than capturing the context-dependent, hierarchical nature of real-world illumination dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253876/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light RGB image (H×W×3) as input and produces an enhanced normal-light RGB image of the same resolution. Internally, the image is converted to YUV color space and a smoothed illumination map is computed via guided filtering to drive the hierarchical luminance adaptation. In the optional LASQ++ mode, unpaired normal-light images (not pixel-aligned) are additionally used during training to train an adversarial discriminator, but no paired ground truth is ever required.

## Method

LASQ reformulates LLIE as a statistical sampling process over hierarchical power-law luminance distributions rather than a deterministic mapping. A Luminance Adaptation Operator (LAO) is defined as a power-law function whose base and exponent are computed from local luminance statistics, and a hierarchical MCMC scheme samples LAO sets from a truncated Gaussian distribution, progressively generating coarse-to-fine spatially-varying correction patterns (2^(n-1) patches at level n). These sampled luminance-enhanced images are encoded into latent features and aligned with diffusion timesteps via a temporal mapping, so the forward diffusion process injects noise guided by hierarchical illumination semantics from global to local scales. A U-Net denoising network is trained with a noise-prediction loss plus a global L1 consistency loss against the coarsest luminance reference, and inference uses DDIM sampling conditioned on the low-light latent. An optional GAN discriminator (LASQ++) adds adversarial refinement when unpaired normal-light references are available.

## Results

On paired benchmarks, LASQ achieves 20.375 PSNR / 0.814 SSIM / 0.191 LPIPS on LOLv1 and 18.137 PSNR / 0.547 SSIM / 0.308 LPIPS on LSRW, matching or exceeding the closest unsupervised competitor LightenDiffusion (20.453 / 0.803 / 0.192 and 18.555 / 0.539 / 0.311) on SSIM and LPIPS. On no-reference datasets, LASQ attains the best NIQE scores across DICM (3.715), NPE (3.571), and VV (2.777), outperforming all 12 baselines including supervised methods. LASQ++ (with unpaired normal-light references) further improves paired-metric fidelity (e.g., 18.584 PSNR on LSRW) at a slight cost to cross-domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LASQ's closest competitor is LightenDiffusion (the only other diffusion-based unsupervised method). On LOLv1, LASQ trails by 0.078 dB PSNR (20.375 vs 20.453) but leads on SSIM (0.814 vs 0.803) and LPIPS (0.191 vs 0.192); on LSRW the PSNR gap widens to 0.418 dB (18.137 vs 18.555) though LASQ still wins SSIM and LPIPS. On all three no-reference datasets (DICM, NPE, VV), LASQ achieves the best NIQE, surpassing LightenDiffusion by 0.009, 0.047, and 0.164 respectively, and beating all supervised methods by large margins (e.g., 2.45 NIQE lower than SMG on DICM). The sole exception is VV-PI, where LightenDiffusion edges out LASQ (2.558 vs 2.623). Among supervised baselines, SMG and PyDiff dominate paired-metric PSNR on LOLv1 (23.814 and 23.275) but generalize poorly to no-reference sets, where LASQ outperforms them by 1.5–2.5 NIQE points. LASQ++ narrows the paired-metric gap (18.584 PSNR on LSRW, matching LightenDiffusion) but slightly degrades no-reference scores, confirming a fidelity–generalization trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

LASQ departs from the dominant paradigm in low-light image enhancement—deterministic pixel-level mappings learned via supervised or cycle-consistency training—by reformulating the problem as stochastic sampling over hierarchical power-law luminance distributions. It builds on diffusion models (DDIM inference, U-Net denoiser) and Retinex-inspired illumination decomposition, but introduces a key conceptual shift: a Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling scheme over luminance adaptation operators (parameterized by power-law exponents) is embedded into the forward diffusion process, replacing fixed gamma curves or learned illumination maps. The optional adversarial discriminator (LASQ++) extends the framework toward a hybrid diffusion-GAN, drawing on LSGAN-style training when unpaired normal-light references are available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LASQ demonstrates that physics-informed statistical priors (power-law luminance transitions) can be integrated into diffusion training to achieve competitive reference-free enhancement with strong cross-dataset generalization, reducing reliance on paired data that limits most supervised LLIE methods. However, the computational cost (≈220 GFLOPs, ~214 ms inference) remains in the same range as other diffusion-based LLIE methods, and the framework's sensitivity to hierarchy depth N and diffusion steps T limits practical deployment. Open questions include whether dynamic power-law parameterization can handle time-varying scenes (video) and whether hardware-aware co-design can reduce the inference overhead to match lightweight non-diffusion methods.
