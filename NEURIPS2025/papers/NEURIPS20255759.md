## Problem

Existing multimodal recommendation systems rely on item-based interaction histories (e.g., past purchases) that are not always available and do not generalize across domains. This paper asks whether a user's task-agnostic visual history—diverse, noisy photos from daily life—can serve as a rich personalization signal for recommendation, and identifies the absence of benchmarks to evaluate such systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255759/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At runtime, the system takes a text recommendation query, a user's visual history of up to w=64 retrieved images (encoded via CLIP ViT-L/14@336px), and a candidate set of 30–100 items each with a name, text description, and optional images. The retrieved images are gridified into a single 8×8 composite image (112×112 per sub-image for PaliGemma) to fit within the MLLM's context window, accompanied by numbered captions (≤30 words each) and aspect words. The output is a ranked candidate list produced by scoring each candidate's special-token probability in the first output position.

## Method

VISUALLENS builds an offline 'spectrum user profile' where each image is augmented with a caption (LLaVA-v1.6-8B) and aspect words (iteratively refined over ~4 rounds using Llama-3.1-70B as a judge), spanning from raw visual content to clean semantic cues. At inference, a CLIP-based retrieval step selects the top-w images most relevant to the query category via cosine similarity against category-averaged embeddings, and these are packed into a d×d grid treated as a single image for the MLLM. The backbone (PaliGemma 3B or MiniCPM-V2.5 8B) is trained via three stages: LoRA continual pretraining on DOCCI dense captions to learn grid grounding, iterative aspect-word refinement with cross-entropy loss, and joint multi-task fine-tuning that simultaneously optimizes aspect generation and candidate matching (BCE loss) under a weighted combined objective. This joint training lets the model learn which aspect words are actually predictive of user preference, closing the loop between profile quality and recommendation accuracy.

## Results

VISUALLENS (MiniCPM-V2.5, 8B) achieves 82.3% Hit@10 and 38.9% Hit@3 on Google Review-V, and 91.0% Hit@10 and 59.1% Hit@3 on Yelp-V, outperforming the strongest direct-inference baseline GPT-4o by 1.6% and 4.6% on Hit@3 respectively. The 3B PaliGemma variant also surpasses GPT-4o on Yelp-V Hit@3 (58.8% vs. 54.5%) despite being substantially smaller. Compared to the random baseline, VISUALLENS improves Hit@3 by 18% on Google Review-V and 26% on Yelp-V, filling approximately 75% of the gap to human-annotated performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISUALLENS (8B) outperforms the state-of-the-art multimodal recommender UniMP [46] by 4.8% on Hit@3 for Google Review-V (38.9 vs. 34.1) and by 10.6% for Yelp-V (59.1 vs. 48.5), despite UniMP being a purpose-built multi-task personalization model. Against the text-only Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct baseline, VISUALLENS gains 2.6% and 6.9% on Hit@3 for the two datasets, confirming the value of visual history over text-only profiles. The 8B VISUALLENS also beats the much larger Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (text-only) by 3.0% and 5.9% on Hit@3, and surpasses GPT-4o (multimodal, direct inference) by 1.6% on Google Review-V and 4.6% on Yelp-V Hit@3. The 3B PaliGemma variant of VISUALLENS already exceeds GPT-4o on Yelp-V Hit@3 (58.8 vs. 54.5) and MRR (44.3 vs. 41.7), demonstrating that the framework's gains are not solely attributable to model scale. A notable finding is that the simple 'rank by rating' heuristic underperforms even the random baseline on Hit@1 and Hit@3, indicating that aggregate popularity signals are poorly aligned with individual user preferences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in multimodal recommendation (UniMP, COURIER, PC²L) that relies on item-based interaction histories, instead proposing to use task-agnostic visual history—photos from a user's daily life—as the personalization signal. Conceptually, it bridges the personalized-conversation line (ONCE, which uses task-agnostic text) with multimodal recommendation by replacing textual logs with visual ones, and repurposes off-the-shelf MLLM components (CLIP for encoding, LLaVA for captioning, PaliGemma/MiniCPM-V2.5 as backbones) within a novel spectrum-profile pipeline. The grid-based multi-image encoding and iterative aspect-word refinement are practical contributions that address the context-window bottleneck of current MLLMs, but the overall architecture is a composition of existing modules rather than a new model family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two new benchmarks (Google Review-V, Yelp-V) fill a genuine gap—no prior dataset evaluates personalization from task-agnostic visual history—and will likely become standard references for this emerging direction. The spectrum user profile (raw image → caption → aspect words) offers a useful, interpretable abstraction that practitioners can adopt or extend. Key open questions remain: the framework handles only static images (no video, audio, or temporal narratives), restricts to a QA recommendation format, and raises significant privacy concerns when modeling preferences from daily-life photos. The modular design means each component (encoder, captioner, aspect extractor) can be independently upgraded, suggesting substantial headroom for future improvements.
