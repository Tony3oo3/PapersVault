## Problem

Long-form video understanding (hour-long or longer) remains challenging because even million-token context windows are insufficient for the information density of such videos, and existing video agents rely on manually prescribed, rigid search workflows that fail to adapt to diverse query types. The paper asks whether an LLM can autonomously plan and orchestrate an adaptive, multi-granular search strategy over a segmented video to answer complex questions without a fixed pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253005/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an ultra-long video (segmented into 5-second clips at 2 fps, frames at 720p) and a multiple-choice user query, optionally augmented with WhisperX transcripts that guide segmentation and enrich captions. Before answering, the video is offline-converted into a structured database containing a global subject registry, per-clip captions with dense embeddings, and the original decoded frames. The agent outputs a single answer after up to 15 iterative reason-act steps, with an average of ~7.3 steps in practice.

## Method

DVD comprises two stages. First, a multi-granular video database is built by uniformly segmenting the video into 5-second clips, progressively extracting a subject-centric global registry and per-clip captions via a VLM (GPT-4.1), embedding captions for retrieval, and retaining decoded frames for pixel-level inspection. Second, an agentic search-and-answer loop (ReAct-style) equips a reasoning LLM (OpenAI o3) with three search-centric tools—Global Browse (subject/event summaries over the whole video), Clip Search (cosine-similarity retrieval of top-k clip captions), and Frame Inspect (open-ended VQA on raw frames within a specified time range)—and lets the LLM autonomously decide which tool to invoke, what parameters to synthesize, and when to answer, rather than following a fixed workflow. The key design choice is to avoid prescribing any search order or tool-combination pattern, instead relying entirely on the LLM's reasoning to adaptively chain tools per query, which ablations show outperforms hand-crafted workflows by 4–7.5% accuracy at fewer steps.

## Results

On the primary benchmark LVBench (1,549 questions over 103 hour-long videos), DVD achieves 74.2% overall accuracy, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art MR.Video (60.8%) by 13.4 percentage points, and further improves to 76.0% with auxiliary transcripts. On LongVideoBench, DVD reaches 71.6% overall and 68.6% on the Long subset (900s–3600s), outperforming the prior best by 4.1% and 7.0% respectively. On EgoSchema, DVD attains 76.6%, exceeding the reported human-level accuracy of ~76%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVD outperforms all 14 baselines across every benchmark where comparisons are available. On LVBench, the 13.4-point margin over the previous SOTA MR.Video (60.8%) is the largest gap, while the 17.1-point gain over the base VLM OpenAI o3 (57.1%) highlights the value of the agentic search layer. Among video agents, DVD surpasses VCA (41.3%) by 32.9 points and VideoTree (28.8%) by 45.4 points on LVBench. On LongVideoBench, DVD leads the prior best (MR.Video at 61.6% on Long) by 7.0 points. On Video MME Long, DVD (67.3%) beats AdaRETAKE (65.0%) by 2.3% and MR.Video (61.8%) by 5.5%, approaching Gemini-1.5-Pro (67.4%). On EgoSchema, DVD (76.6%) exceeds VCA (73.6%) by 3.0 points and surpasses the reported human-level accuracy of ~76%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ReAct-style iterative observe-reason-act paradigm (Yao et al., ICLR 2023) to long-form video understanding, drawing conceptual inspiration from the Deep Research and Deep Search agentic systems that decompose complex information-gathering tasks into modular tool-use chains. It departs from prior video agents (VideoTree, VCA, VideoAgent, MR.Video) that prescribe fixed, human-designed search workflows (e.g., tree-based navigation or sequential predict-reflect-search loops) by instead providing a multi-granular video database and a small set of search-centric tools (Global Browse, Clip Search, Frame Inspect) that the reasoning LLM orchestrates autonomously and adaptively per query. The multi-granular database construction—progressive subject registry, clip-level caption embeddings, and preserved pixel frames—serves as a structural contribution that enables the three tools to operate at complementary levels of detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13.4% absolute improvement over prior SOTA on LVBench (74.2% vs. 60.8%) and the demonstration that an adaptive workflow outperforms a hand-crafted one by 7.5% while using fewer steps provide strong evidence that LLM reasoning quality, not workflow rigidity, is the bottleneck in video agents. The behavioral taxonomy (Global Browse Only, Simple Action, Iterative Search, Frame Inspect Trap, Clip Search Trap) and the finding that GPT-4o collapses into premature Simple Action for 91.4% of queries offer actionable design insights for future video agents. Key limitations include heavy reliance on proprietary APIs (GPT-4.1, o3) at ~$2 per question, Azure content filtering that corrupts 10–25% of queries, and the open question of whether the approach's gains transfer to fully open-source model stacks (DeepSeek-R1 achieves 68.5%, still strong but notably below o3's 76.0%).
