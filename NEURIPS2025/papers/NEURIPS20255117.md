## Problem

EEG-based models trained on static datasets generalize poorly to unseen individuals due to substantial inter-individual variability, yet real-world deployment requires continual adaptation as new subjects arrive sequentially. Existing continual learning approaches for EEG either depend exclusively on the most recent model state (making them vulnerable to outlier-induced error accumulation) or lack a principled mechanism to balance plasticity for new individuals against stability of prior knowledge. The paper asks how to simultaneously enhance adaptation to each emerging individual and suppress catastrophic forgetting over long-term continual decoding.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes unlabeled EEG recordings from new individuals one at a time in a sequential stream, with each individual's raw signals segmented into task-specific epochs (30s for sleep staging, 10s for emotion, 4s for motor imagery). A pre-trained source model (CNN + Transformer + classifier) is initialized on a labeled source subset, and the synaptic network is seeded with handcrafted initial features extracted per-channel across time, frequency, and time-frequency domains. Beyond task-specific class predictions, the system outputs a dynamically expanding synaptic network where each node stores an individual's pseudo-labeled samples, fine-tuned model, and weighted synaptic connections to other nodes.

## Method

SPICED replaces the conventional sequential model-update paradigm with a bio-inspired synaptic network that dynamically expands as new individuals arrive. For each new individual, a synaptic importance coefficient (a weighted combination of inter-node feature similarity and the node's average synaptic connection strength) ranks all existing nodes, and the top-K nodes are selected for both data replay and weighted model fusion—initializing the current model as a convex combination of top-K node models rather than the immediately preceding model. Self-supervised training via Contrastive Predictive Coding generates high-confidence pseudo-labels, which are jointly trained with replayed samples under a cross-entropy loss. After adaptation, synaptic consolidation selectively amplifies the connection strengths of the top-K activated nodes (capped at 3× baseline to mimic LTP saturation), while synaptic renormalization applies a global Ebbinghaus-curve-inspired exponential decay to all connections, with the decay rate modulated by each node's activation history (frequently activated nodes forget slower). This consolidation–renormalization interplay progressively strengthens task-discriminative memory traces while suppressing redundant noisy connections, maintaining homeostatic balance across the expanding network.

## Results

SPICED achieves the best or near-best accuracy across all three EEG datasets and all source-target splits. On ISRUC (sleep staging), SPICED reaches 75.6% ACC at 30% source proportion, outperforming the strongest baseline BrainUICL (74.8%) and EWC (71.3%). On FACED (emotion recognition), SPICED achieves 43.7% ACC at 30% source, surpassing UCL-GV (41.4%) by 2.3 points. On Physionet-MI (motor imagery), SPICED attains 48.7% ACC at 30% source, marginally ahead of BrainUICL (48.3%). Notably, SPICED exhibits the lowest standard deviations (e.g., ±0.13 on ISRUC 30%, ±0.17 on Physionet-MI 30%), indicating superior robustness to input order variations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPICED outperforms all six baselines on 8 out of 9 evaluation settings, with the sole exception being Physionet-MI at 50% source where BrainUICL edges ahead by 0.1 points (52.5 vs. 52.4). The most substantial gains appear on FACED: at 30% source, SPICED (43.7%) beats UCL-GV (41.4%) by 2.3 points and BrainUICL (33.8%) by nearly 10 points, while at 50% source it surpasses EWC (48.7%) and BrainUICL (20.2%) by 0.2 and 28.7 points respectively. On ISRUC, SPICED's advantage is most pronounced at 10% source (62.6% vs. EWC's 60.6%), where the few-shot pre-training regime amplifies the benefit of holistic synaptic network replay over sequential model chaining. On Physionet-MI, SPICED consistently leads or ties BrainUICL (the strongest EEG-specific baseline) across all splits, with margins of 0.3–0.4 points. A key differentiator is robustness: SPICED's standard deviations are consistently the lowest (e.g., ±0.13 on ISRUC 30%, ±0.17 on Physionet-MI 30%), whereas BrainUICL shows high variance on FACED (±2.90 at 30%, ±3.46 at 50%), suggesting that SPICED's synaptic homeostasis mechanism better suppresses error accumulation from outlier individuals in the continual stream.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPICED builds directly on BrainUICL [51], which established the unsupervised individual continual learning (UICL) paradigm for EEG, adopting the same source-pretraining-plus-incremental-adaptation setting and CPC-based pseudo-label generation. The key departure is the introduction of an auxiliary synaptic network that models synaptic homeostasis (consolidation via LTP-like strengthening and renormalization via LTD-like global downscaling) as a structurally independent module, rather than modifying the core model architecture as prior synaptic-inspired CL methods [35–42] require. This positions SPICED at the intersection of bio-inspired continual learning and practical BCI deployment, where the synaptic network's dynamic expansion and homeostatic balance replace the sequential model dependency (M_i from M_{i-1}) with holistic network-wide memory retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, biologically grounded mechanism for the stability-plasticity tradeoff in continual learning, showing that the interplay between selective consolidation and periodic renormalization can simultaneously enhance adaptation to novel individuals and suppress error accumulation over long sequences. However, the framework only approximates fundamental synaptic homeostasis principles without modeling deeper neurobiological dynamics, and the unified initial feature extraction across tasks limits the precision of inter-individual similarity quantification. Open questions include whether the synaptic homeostasis paradigm generalizes beyond EEG to other sensorimodal continual learning settings, and whether task-specific feature extraction or richer neurobiological models (e.g., sleep-stage-dependent consolidation) could yield further gains.
