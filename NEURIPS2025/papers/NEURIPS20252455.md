## Problem

Gradient descent with large stepsizes on linearly separable data was previously shown to converge only for losses satisfying the self-bounding property (e.g., logistic loss), where the analysis hinges on a modified descent lemma. Pilot studies reveal that losses lacking this property (e.g., Tsallis q-loss with q > 1) also attain arbitrarily small loss, raising the question of what structural property of a loss function actually governs large-stepsize GD convergence. This paper identifies the separation margin property—loss vanishing for sufficiently large positive prediction margins—as the key factor, and establishes convergence rates for a broad class of Fenchel-Young losses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a bounded linearly separable binary dataset (‖x_i‖ ≤ 1, margin γ > 0), a Fenchel-Young loss ℓ(z) = φ*(−z) generated by a twice-continuously-differentiable convex potential φ satisfying symmetry and zero-entropy conditions, and an arbitrary constant stepsize η > 0 with no upper bound. It produces a linear classifier w with guaranteed ε-optimal empirical risk after T steps, where the rate T = Ω(ε^{−α}) depends on φ through the exponent α = limsup_{μ↓0} [φ′(μ)/(μφ″(μ))][1 − φ(μ)/(μφ′(μ))]. Losses with separation margin yield α < 1, beating the classical T = Ω(ε^{−1}) rate, and the stepsize η can be arbitrarily large without affecting convergence.

## Method

The proof replaces the standard descent lemma with a classical perceptron argument: it tracks the growth of parameter alignment ⟨w_t, w_*⟩ with the optimal separator, showing each GD step improves a lower bound on this alignment while the parameter norm remains bounded. The separation margin property is critical—it ensures ‖w_t‖ stays bounded via the split optimization technique, preventing the unbounded norm growth that would otherwise break the perceptron inequality. The convergence rate exponent α is characterized through an order-evaluation technique from functional analysis, applied to the gradient function g(ℓ^{-1}(ε)) to extract its asymptotic power-law behavior as ε → 0. This yields concrete rates: Tsallis 2-entropy achieves T = Ω(ε^{−1/2}) and Rényi 2-entropy (collision entropy) achieves T = Ω(ε^{−1/3}), both strictly better than the classical stable-regime rate and the logistic loss rate after the edge-of-stability phase transition.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 5) establishes that GD with arbitrary constant stepsize η > 0 achieves ε-optimal loss on linearly separable data under Fenchel-Young losses, with iteration complexity T = Ω(ε^{−α}) where α is determined by the potential function φ. The headline rates are T = Ω(ε^{−1/2}) for the Tsallis 2-entropy and T = Ω(ε^{−1/3}) for the Rényi 2-entropy (collision entropy), both strictly better than the classical GD rate T = Ω(ε^{−1}) for convex smooth objectives. Notably, these improved rates hold without the self-bounding property that was previously considered essential for large-stepsize convergence, and instead rely on the separation margin property of the loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the classical GD convergence rate T = Ω(ε^{−1}) for convex smooth objectives under the stable regime (η < 2/β). The Tsallis q-loss (q > 1) and Rényi q-loss (q > 1) both achieve strictly better rates: T = Ω(ε^{−1/q}) for q ∈ (1,2) and T = Ω(ε^{−1/2}) or Ω(ε^{−1/3}) at the boundary, all outperforming the classical rate. Against Wu et al. [63], who showed large-stepsize logistic regression converges at rate T = Ω̃(ε^{−1}) after an edge-of-stability phase, the Tsallis and Rényi losses with separation margin achieve faster convergence without requiring the self-bounding property. The semi-circle loss (α = 2) is a negative comparison, yielding T = Ω(ε^{−4}) without separation margin, which is worse than the classical rate. The paper also contrasts with the perceptron argument used in [63], showing that the self-bounding property is not necessary for large-stepsize convergence, and that separation margin is the key structural property enabling improved rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the large-stepsize GD convergence analysis of Wu et al. [63]—which was restricted to logistic regression and relied on the self-bounding property to establish a modified descent lemma—to the broad class of Fenchel-Young losses [11]. The key departure is methodological: instead of the modified descent lemma, the proof leverages the classical perceptron argument [44] combined with an order-evaluation technique from convex analysis [6] to characterize the convergence rate exponent. The paper identifies separation margin (a notion from SVM theory) as the property that actually governs large-stepsize convergence behavior, rather than the self-bounding property previously considered essential.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that losses without the self-bounding property (e.g., Tsallis q-loss for q>1, Rényi 2-entropy) can achieve ε-optimal loss at rates of O(ε^{-1/2}) or O(ε^{-1/3})—strictly better than the classical stable-regime rate O(ε^{-1})—challenges the prevailing narrative that the self-bounding property is a cornerstone of large-stepsize GD convergence. Open questions include whether implicit bias toward the max-margin direction persists under separation-margin losses (as opposed to the logistic loss), whether finite-time convergence to exact zero is achievable, and whether the n-dependency in the rate is truly necessary. The analysis is limited to linearly separable data and linear models, and the non-separable case remains largely open beyond one dimension.
