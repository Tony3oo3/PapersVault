## Problem

Chain-of-Thought reasoning has shown strong gains in static VLM tasks like VQA, but its role in long-horizon, action-centric Vision-Language Navigation remains unexplored. This paper conducts the first systematic comparison of No-Think, Pre-Think, and Post-Think reasoning strategies for VLN and reveals a 'Test-time Reasoning Collapse' phenomenon where explicit CoT at test time consistently degrades navigation accuracy due to training-testing mismatch in partially observable, dynamic environments.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives a monocular RGB frame, 8 uniformly sampled historical frames (always including the first), and a natural-language instruction. The model outputs a discrete navigation action from a fine-grained space: move forward (25/50/75 cm), turn left or right (15°/30°/45°), or stop. During testing the model predicts a short horizon of n=3 actions but executes only the first, enabling reactive re-planning at every step without incurring CoT generation overhead.

## Method

Aux-Think decouples reasoning from action execution by using CoT exclusively as auxiliary supervision during training while maintaining direct action prediction (No-Think) at test time, inspired by dual-process theory of human learning. Training co-optimizes three prompt-switched tasks on the NVILA-lite 8B backbone: (1) CoT-based reasoning that generates stepwise reasoning traces, (2) instruction-based reasoning that reconstructs the navigation instruction from the observation sequence, and (3) Receding-Horizon Action Planning that predicts the next 3 actions as the primary objective. The authors also release R2R-CoT-320k, the first large-scale CoT-annotated VLN dataset (320K+ traces generated by Qwen-2.5-VL-72B), which provides the reasoning supervision signal. This design internalizes structured reasoning patterns into the model's representations during training, so that at test time the agent acts directly without generating intermediate reasoning, eliminating the hallucination and error-cascade risks that cause Test-time Reasoning Collapse.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val-Unseen, Aux-Think achieves 54.8% SR and 46.9 SPL using only 1600K extra training data, surpassing NaVILA (54.0% SR, 49.0 SPL with 2770K data) on success rate while using ~42% less data. Without any extra data (0K), Aux-Think still reaches 46.0% SR and 40.5 SPL, dramatically outperforming NaVid° (24.7% SR, 23.6 SPL) under the same data constraint. On RxR-CE Val-Unseen, Aux-Think achieves 52.2% SR and 61.9% OSR with 1920K data, beating Uni-NaVid (48.7% SR, 55.5% OSR with 5900K data) and NaVILA (49.3% SR with 3100K data) on success rate while using roughly one-third the training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among monocular large-model baselines, Aux-Think (1600K extra data) achieves the highest SR (54.8%) on R2R-CE, beating NaVILA (54.0%, 2770K) by 0.8 points and Uni-NaVid (47.0%, 5570K) by 7.8 points, while using substantially less training data. On RxR-CE, Aux-Think (52.2% SR, 61.9% OSR) outperforms both Uni-NaVid (48.7% SR, 55.5% OSR) and NaVILA (49.3% SR) on success rate, though it trails NaVILA on SPL (40.2 vs. 44.0), which the authors attribute to broader exploration behavior induced by CoT training. Against the zero-extra-data NaVid° baseline, Aux-Think° more than doubles SR (46.0 vs. 24.7) and SPL (40.5 vs. 23.6) using only the R2R-CE training split. Traditional non-LLM methods (BEVBert, ETPNav, ENP-ETPNav) achieve higher SR (57–59%) but rely on panoramic observations and waypoint predictors, making them incomparable in the monocular action-prediction setting. In the head-to-head reasoning strategy comparison (Table 3), Aux-Think (41.3% SR, 35.8 SPL) outperforms No-Think (35.1% SR, 30.2 SPL), Post-Think (29.0% SR, 23.8 SPL), and Pre-Think (11.4% SR, 8.6 SPL) by a wide margin, confirming the Test-time Reasoning Collapse finding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NVILA 8B vision-language model as its base architecture and operates within the established VLN-CE (continuous environment) setting introduced by Krantz et al. (2020). It departs from recent VLN methods (NaVid, Uni-NaVid, NaVILA) that all employ No-Think testing by systematically investigating whether Chain-of-Thought reasoning—successful in static VLM tasks like VQA—transfers to long-horizon embodied navigation. The Aux-Think framework draws on dual-process theory of human cognition, using CoT as auxiliary training supervision while maintaining direct action prediction at test time, and introduces receding-horizon action planning as a primary training task not present in prior VLN-CE methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Test-time Reasoning Collapse finding challenges the prevailing assumption that explicit CoT reasoning improves performance in sequential, partially observable embodied tasks, with direct implications for how reasoning is integrated into robot navigation and potentially other long-horizon control problems. The R2R-CoT-320k dataset fills a critical gap as the first CoT-annotated VLN resource, enabling future research on reasoning-aware navigation. Key limitations include evaluation on a single model family (NVILA 8B) and SFT-only training, leaving open whether TRC generalizes across architectures, whether reinforcement learning or larger-scale training mitigates it, and whether the Aux-Think paradigm extends to other embodied tasks like manipulation or locomotion.
