## Problem

Score-based diffusion models assume Euclidean data space, yet many scientific applications (molecular conformers, protein-ligand docking, rotational alignment) involve distributions on curved Lie group manifolds where standard denoising score-matching is intractable for non-Abelian groups. Existing Riemannian diffusion approaches require explicit manifold projections and trajectory simulation, and recent findings show that unconstrained Cartesian-coordinate models outperform equivariant ones. The paper asks whether one can construct a generative process directly in the flat representation space of a Lie group that models any distribution on the group while retaining the practical advantages of Euclidean diffusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes data points in Euclidean space (3N-dimensional molecular coordinates, 28×28 MNIST images, or compound+protein pocket pairs) and produces generated samples as products of finite group element actions applied to an initial point. Unusually, the learned score is not a standard gradient but a vector of directional derivatives along fundamental flow coordinates (e.g., a single scalar for SO(2) rotation of MNIST, or 3N components for per-atom SO(3)×R₊ on molecules). The forward corruption is exactly solvable in closed form as a product of exponentials of Lie algebra generators, so no numerical trajectory simulation is needed during training.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the gradient operator in score matching with the linear operator L = Π(x)ᵀ∇, where Π(x) is the matrix of fundamental vector fields induced by the Lie algebra action of G on X. This yields a new class of paired SDEs: the forward process is exactly solvable as x(t) = (∏ᵢ exp(τᵢ(t)Aᵢ))x(0), with flow coordinates τ(t) following a standard Gaussian SDE; the reverse process includes an additional quadratic Casimir term ρ_X(Ω) that compensates for the curvature of flow coordinates, plus a divergence term Π(x)∇ᵀ·Π(x). The Langevin update decomposes as a direct sum of independent infinitesimal Lie algebra actions, so each component of the generalized score is learned along a single orbit direction. Standard score matching is recovered as the special case G = T(n) where Π = I and the Casimir vanishes. Training uses a time-dependent neural network to predict the generalized score, with the loss computed in flow coordinates where the conditional distribution is Gaussian.

## Results

On rotated MNIST, the proposed GSM model achieves 0.96±0.02 classification accuracy and 85.8±15.7 FID, substantially outperforming the Brownian Bridge Diffusion Model (BBDM) baseline (0.80±0.10 accuracy, 133.4±19.0 FID). On CrossDocked2020 molecular docking, the SE(3)-guided Lie algebra diffusion achieves a mean RMSD of 2.91±1.0 Å, compared to 5.6±1.2 Å for Riemannian Score-based Generative Modeling (RSGM) and 2.92±1.57 Å for BBDM. On QM9 conformer generation, the SO(3)×R+ group yields a mean energy difference of −0.2159 vs −0.2144 for standard Euclidean diffusion, a marginal but consistent improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BBDM on rotated MNIST, the proposed GSM method wins decisively on both metrics: 0.96 vs 0.80 classification accuracy and 85.8 vs 133.4 FID, with the paper attributing the gap to BBDM operating in the full 28×28 pixel space where intermediate states produce implausible mixed-digit interpolations. On CrossDocked2020 molecular docking, GSM achieves a mean RMSD of 2.91±1.0 Å, dramatically outperforming RSGM (5.6±1.2 Å) while matching BBDM (2.92±1.57 Å) on RMSD; however, GSM and RSGM both achieve zero MAE on rigid-body consistency by design, whereas BBDM yields 0.43±0.21 due to unphysical coordinate trajectories. On QM9 conformer generation, the SO(3)×R+ Lie group formulation provides only a marginal energy improvement (−0.2159 vs −0.2144 mean Δ) over standard T(3)^N diffusion, suggesting the benefit is small for single-conformer tasks but more pronounced in the docking setting where global SE(3) structure is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Generalized Score Matching (Lyu, 2009; Lin et al., 2016) from Euclidean spaces to the representation space of Lie groups, where the linear operator L is constructed from fundamental vector fields of the Lie algebra action. It positions itself as an alternative to Riemannian score-based generative modeling (De Bortoli et al., 2022), which requires explicit projection onto the manifold and trajectory simulation, by instead performing all computation in flat Cartesian space while the dynamics follow curved Lie group orbits. Standard score matching is recovered as the special case of the translation group T(n), and the framework is shown to extend naturally to flow matching. The introduction of a new class of exactly-solvable paired SDEs involving the quadratic Casimir element is a theoretical contribution that goes beyond existing score-based diffusion formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap highlighted by AlphaFold 3 (Abramson et al., 2024), where unconstrained Cartesian-coordinate diffusion outperformed equivariant torsion-space approaches, suggesting that manifold-based diffusion's limitations are technical rather than fundamental. By working in representation space, the method retains the geometric inductive bias of the Lie group while avoiding projection steps and enabling simulation-free training. Key open questions include: whether the commutativity condition (Condition 3) is satisfiable for all practically relevant Lie groups, how the framework scales to very high-dimensional groups, and whether the extension to flow matching (sketched only in Appendix F) yields practical gains. The QM9 results show only marginal energy improvement over standard diffusion, suggesting the advantage is most pronounced in bridging/alignment tasks rather than unconstrained generation.
