## Problem

No existing bias amplification metric simultaneously satisfies three desirable properties: (1) identifying the direction of bias amplification (A→T vs. T→A), (2) correctly measuring amplification in both balanced and unbalanced datasets, and (3) distinguishing positive from negative (i.e., bias-reducing) amplification. Co-occurrence metrics like BA→ and BA_MALS fail on balanced datasets, Multi→ cannot capture negative amplification, and the predictability-based leakage amplification (LA) lacks directionality, forcing practitioners to evaluate multiple metrics to verify all three aspects.

## Input / Output

Given a classification dataset with protected attribute labels A and task labels T, a trained model M producing task predictions T̂ and attribute predictions Â, and a user-chosen attacker function (any supervised model, e.g., MLP, SVM, decision tree), DPA outputs two bounded scores in [-1, 1]: DPA_{A→T} and DPA_{T→A}. A key preprocessing step equalizes dataset and model accuracy by randomly flipping a fraction of ground-truth labels to match the model's prediction accuracy, preventing prediction errors from being conflated with bias. The attacker function's quality is measured by a user-specified quality function Q (e.g., accuracy, F1), which must map 0 to worst performance and its upper bound to best performance.

## Method

DPA measures directional bias amplification via predictability: for the A→T direction, it trains an attacker f_A^T to predict true task labels T from true attribute A (yielding dataset bias Ψ_A^D = Q(f_A^T(A), T)) and a second attacker f_A^T̂ to predict model task predictions T̂ from the same true attribute A (yielding model bias Ψ_A^M = Q(f_A^T̂(A), T̂)). The DPA score is the relative change (Ψ_A^M − Ψ_A^D)/(Ψ_A^M + Ψ_A^D + ε), which bounds the output to [-1, 1], accounts for pre-existing dataset bias (unlike LA's absolute difference), and is robust to attacker hyperparameters because the ratio cancels out scale differences in attacker performance. The T→A direction is symmetric, swapping the roles of A and T as the fixed prior variable, following Wang et al.'s definition of directionality. This single metric thus replaces the need to combine multiple metrics to verify directionality, balanced-dataset applicability, and sign of amplification.

## Results

On the COCO progressive-masking experiment, DPA correctly reports increasing T→A bias amplification from −0.015 (original) to 0.809 (bounding-box masked) on the unbalanced sub-dataset, and from 0.023 to 0.104 on the balanced sub-dataset, while BA→ and BA_MALS report zero on the balanced set and LA/Multi→ fail to track the trend. On ImSitu (unbalanced), DPA's model ranking by A→T bias amplification matches the ground-truth Sen_{A→T} ranking with only a one-position swap (rank 5 vs. 6), whereas all other metrics show large ranking mismatches. On ImSitu (balanced), DPA's ranking exactly matches the Sen_{A→T} ranking, while BA→ and BA_MALS report zero for all models. On COMPAS, DPA correctly identifies negative A→T bias amplification (−0.040) in the unbalanced case, which Multi→ misreports as positive (+0.038), and detects positive bias amplification in the balanced case where BA→ reports zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPA outperforms all four baselines on the three key properties: directionality, balanced-dataset support, and negative-amplification detection. BA_MALS [2] and BA→ [3] both report exactly zero bias amplification on balanced datasets (COCO balanced, ImSitu balanced, COMPAS balanced), making them useless in those settings; DPA correctly reports non-zero, increasing scores in all cases. Multi→ [4] works on balanced datasets and is directional, but cannot capture negative bias amplification — on COMPAS (unbalanced, A→T) it reports +0.038 when the true amplification is negative (DPA reports −0.040), and on ImSitu its model rankings diverge substantially from ground-truth Sen scores. LA [7] handles balanced and unbalanced datasets and captures negative amplification, but lacks directionality entirely; on COCO it fails to track the increasing T→A model bias (scores go 0.037→0.072→0.094→0.027, non-monotonic) and on ImSitu its model rankings differ by up to 7 positions from the true bias ranking. DPA is the only metric that simultaneously satisfies all three properties, as confirmed by Table 1 in the paper and by the experimental results across COMPAS, COCO, and ImSitu.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPA sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work on bias amplification: co-occurrence-based metrics (BA_MALS, BA→, Multi→) that measure directional bias but fail on balanced datasets or lose sign information, and the predictability-based Leakage Amplification (LA) from Wang et al. 2019 that handles balanced datasets but lacks directionality. The paper departs from LA by normalizing the predictability change to a relative (rather than absolute) quantity, which yields a bounded [-1,1] range and reduces sensitivity to attacker hyperparameters. It also departs from BA→ and Multi→ by replacing conditional-probability comparisons with attacker-function predictability, enabling it to work on balanced datasets while preserving directionality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPA offers practitioners a single metric that simultaneously provides directionality, balanced-dataset compatibility, and sign-aware amplification scores—properties that previously required evaluating multiple metrics and manually reconciling their outputs. The controlled experiments (one-pixel shortcuts on balanced COCO, CMNIST) concretely demonstrate that balanced datasets can still amplify bias from unlabeled elements, reinforcing that surface-level balancing is insufficient. A key open limitation is that DPA reports only aggregate A→T and T→A scores and cannot disaggregate amplification to individual attribute-task pairs, which limits its utility for targeted intervention; the authors suggest feature attribution or causal attackers as future directions.
