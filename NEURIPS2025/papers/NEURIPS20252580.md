## Problem

While prior work has shown that adversarial examples crafted on ViTs transfer more effectively than those from CNNs, the specific role of computational redundancy inherent in ViTs' architecture has not been systematically identified or exploited. This paper investigates how data-level redundancy (redundant tokens and attention patterns) and model-level redundancy (over-parameterized FFNs, redundant attention heads) can be actively amplified to boost adversarial transferability, rather than merely being a side effect of ViT design.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image from ImageNet-1K, a target class label, and a surrogate ViT model (ViT-B/16, PiT-B, or Swin-T) as input, and produces an adversarial perturbation constrained to an ℓ∞-norm ball of radius ε=16/255. The perturbation is generated via 10-step MI-FGSM iterations with the redundancy operations applied at each step. The resulting adversarial example is then evaluated for transferability on black-box victim models including CNNs (ResNet-50, VGG-16, MobileNetV2, Inception-v3), other ViT variants, and Vision-Language Large Models (LLaVA, Qwen 2.5-VL, InternVL, DeepSeek-VL).

## Method

The core contribution is a suite of five redundancy-exploiting operations applied within the surrogate ViT during adversarial optimization: (1) random binary masking of attention logits to amplify sparsity, (2) random permutation of attention head QK projections to capture invariant features, (3) appending clean tokens from benign samples as regularization anchors, (4) a ghost MoE that averages FFN outputs under different random dropout masks to diversify feed-forward paths, and (5) optimizable robust tokens appended to the input sequence that are adversarially trained at test time to robustify the surrogate. A REINFORCE-based policy gradient then learns a per-block operation selection matrix M ∈ ℝ^{L×O}, where each transformer block stochastically samples a subset of operations from the pool, maximizing expected adversarial loss in an online, block-specific manner. This dynamic online learning strategy allows the method to adaptively compose redundancy operations across layers rather than applying them uniformly.

## Results

Using ViT-B/16 as the surrogate model, the proposed method achieves an average black-box fooling rate of 86.9% across 8 target models, surpassing the strongest baseline PGN (77.8%) by 9.1 points and MI-FGSM (54.1%) by 32.8 points. With PiT-B as surrogate, the average fooling rate reaches 87.4% (vs. PGN's 75.1%), and with Swin-T as surrogate it reaches 88.9% (vs. PGN's 85.3%). On individual targets, the method achieves near-perfect transferability on same-architecture models (99.7% on ViT-B/16, 99.7% on PiT-B, 98.2% on Swin-T) and strong cross-architecture transfer to CNNs (77.7% on ResNet-50, 90.6% on VGG-16 with ViT-B/16 surrogate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 9 baselines across every surrogate–target combination. The closest competitor is PGN (flat local maxima), which the method surpasses by 9.1 points (ViT-B/16 surrogate), 12.3 points (PiT-B), and 3.6 points (Swin-T) in average fooling rate. Among gradient-based baselines, EMI-FGSM is the strongest (69.8% avg with ViT-B/16), yet the proposed method still leads by 17.1 points. ViT-specific attacks TGR, GNS, and FPR (which exploit token-level or frequency-domain properties) achieve 68.4%, 61.9%, and 65.6% respectively with ViT-B/16 surrogate, all trailing the proposed method by 18.5–25.0 points. DTA (direction tuning) performs poorly in transfer settings (59.8% avg), and the basic momentum methods MI-FGSM and NI-FGSM remain the weakest at 54.1% and 55.2%. The gap is most pronounced on CNN targets: on ResNet-50, the method achieves 77.7% vs. PGN's 68.9% and MI-FGSM's 39.4%, indicating that redundancy-driven operations particularly benefit cross-architecture transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the well-documented computational redundancy in ViTs—previously studied for inference efficiency (token pruning, head pruning, MoE sparsification)—as an active resource for adversarial transferability. It builds on MI-FGSM as the iterative attack backbone and on the adversarial game formulation of Bose et al. (2020) for the robust-token component, but departs from prior ViT-specific attacks (TGR, GNS, FPR) by modifying the surrogate's forward pass rather than the optimization or input. The REINFORCE-based policy that dynamically selects which redundancy operations to apply per transformer block is a novel integration layer not present in prior transferability work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a design principle long treated as a waste to be minimized (redundancy) can be deliberately amplified to produce more transferable adversarial perturbations, offering a new axis for both attack design and, by implication, defense. Practical relevance is broadened by evaluation on VLLMs (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, InternVL, DeepSeek-VL), though improvements there are modest (~2–5%). Open questions include whether the redundancy-exploitation principle transfers to non-ViT architectures, how the added RL training overhead scales, and whether defenders can neutralize attacks by reducing the very redundancy the method exploits.
