## Problem

Off-policy continuous-control RL algorithms (e.g., TD3) rely on target network soft updates via Polyak averaging, which assumes small parameter changes produce smooth output transitions. Spiking neural networks violate this assumption because their binary, non-differentiable spike outputs cause abrupt discontinuous jumps under the same update rule, destabilizing the critic's optimization and degrading performance. Prior SNN-RL work simply retrofitted SNNs into ANN-centric frameworks without adapting the algorithm to discrete spiking dynamics.

## Input / Output

The spiking actor network receives a state vector from the MuJoCo environment, normalized to (−1, 1) via tanh, and encodes each dimension into a population of 10 soft-reset IF neurons with Gaussian receptive fields. Over 5 internal simulation steps, LIF neurons process the spikes, and a decoder reads the membrane potentials of non-firing output neurons to produce a continuous action vector, which is tanh-normalized and linearly scaled to the environment's action limits. The proxy target network (a standard ReLU MLP) consumes the same state vector and outputs a continuous action used only for computing TD targets during training.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the discrete SNN target actor with a continuous ANN proxy network that is updated implicitly in output space rather than explicitly via Polyak parameter averaging. Specifically, the proxy is trained by gradient descent to minimize the L2 distance between its output and the online SNN actor's output on minibatches sampled from the replay buffer (K=3 iterations per update), acting as a form of implicit imitation. This ensures the proxy's outputs transition smoothly during soft updates, eliminating the discrete jumps that destabilize the critic's TD targets. The target critic remains a standard ANN updated by explicit Polyak averaging, and the proxy is discarded at deployment so the deployed SNN incurs no additional inference cost. A theorem guarantees that as the proxy learning rate approaches zero, output changes become arbitrarily small, ensuring stable optimization.

## Results

On five MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks (IDP, Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d), the proposed Proxy Target framework with simple LIF neurons (PT-LIF) achieves a 5.84% average performance gain (APG) over the ANN(TD3) baseline, making it the first SNN with basic LIF neurons to surpass its ANN counterpart in continuous control. Compared to the vanilla LIF baseline (−10.54% APG), PT-LIF delivers a 32.15% relative improvement in average returns. The framework also reduces training variance substantially and achieves the lowest inference energy consumption (11.09 nJ average) among all tested methods, versus 281.78 nJ for the ANN baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PT-LIF outperforms all five baselines on the APG metric: it beats ANN(TD3) by 5.84 percentage points, ANN-SNN conversion by 26.95 pp, vanilla LIF by 16.38 pp, pop-SAN by 12.50 pp, MDC-SAN by 5.47 pp, and ILC-SAN by 1.20 pp. Notably, PT-LIF is the only method that simultaneously surpasses the ANN baseline across all neuron types tested (LIF, CLIF, DN), with APG values of 5.84%, 5.46%, and 5.06% respectively. Against the strongest SNN baseline (ILC-SAN, 4.64% APG), PT-LIF achieves a modest but consistent edge, while the gap over earlier SNN methods (pop-SAN, MDC-SAN) is more pronounced. In terms of energy, PT-LIF consumes 11.09 nJ per inference on average—roughly 25× less than the ANN baseline (281.78 nJ)—while also achieving a slightly lower average firing rate (32% vs. 33%) than vanilla LIF, further improving efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the TD3 off-policy algorithm (Fujimoto et al., 2018) and the population-coded spiking actor network architecture (Tang et al., 2021), but departs from the prevailing approach of retrofitting SNNs into unmodified ANN-centric RL frameworks. Prior SNN-RL works (pop-SAN, MDC-SAN, ILC-SAN) focus on improving the spiking network itself—via dynamic neurons, lateral connections, or bio-plausible topologies—while leaving the RL algorithm untouched. The key conceptual shift here is identifying the target network soft update mechanism as the source of instability and proposing a continuous proxy network to mediate between discrete SNN outputs and the Polyak-averaging requirement, effectively treating the RL algorithm as something that must be adapted to SNN dynamics rather than the other way around.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a path toward SNN-tailored RL algorithms, arguing that the algorithm itself—not just the network architecture—must account for discrete, non-differentiable dynamics. The finding that simple LIF neurons outperform more complex models once the algorithm is fixed challenges the field's assumption that richer neuron dynamics are necessary for competitive performance. However, the work remains at the simulation level; deployment on actual neuromorphic hardware, handling of non-stationary environments, and extension to multi-agent or hierarchical settings remain open. The modest ~6% APG over ANN (rather than a large margin) suggests the proxy target addresses a real but bounded bottleneck, and further SNN-specific algorithmic adaptations will likely be needed for larger gains.
