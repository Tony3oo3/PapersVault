## Problem

Despite being one of the simplest generative neural networks, the learning dynamics of Restricted Boltzmann Machines remain poorly understood: the likelihood is intractable, exactly solvable cases reduce to SVD, and existing statistical-physics analyses use Bayesian posterior sampling rather than the maximum-likelihood objective actually used in practice. This paper provides the first rigorous high-dimensional asymptotics of RBM training under the standard likelihood objective, bridging the gap between RBM theory and the well-developed multi-index/spiked-covariance literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an n×d data matrix generated from a spiked covariance model (with d→∞, n=αd, and r=Θ(1) latent features) and a fixed number k of hidden units, producing the learned d×k weight matrix W. The key structural constraint is that the effective objective depends only on k projections of the data (x_μ^T W/√n) plus a non-separable regularization η₂ that depends solely on the hidden-unit prior and the Gram matrix W^T W/d. Outputs include closed-form state-evolution recursions for both AMP and GD, a characterization of all stationary points, and the exact weak recovery threshold.

## Method

The central result (Theorem 1) proves that in the high-dimensional limit with fixed k, the RBM log-likelihood is asymptotically equivalent to an unsupervised multi-index model with a non-separable regularization term η₂, enabling the import of AMP and dynamical mean-field theory tools. To handle the non-separable penalty, the authors introduce AMP-RBM, a variant of low-rank AMP that uses a Lagrange multiplier Q̂ to decouple the joint optimization over W and Q, yielding alternating updates with separable denoisers. They prove that AMP-RBM's fixed points coincide exactly with the stationary points of the effective objective (Theorem 2), derive its state evolution (Theorem 3), and show that linearization around zero reduces to the power method, giving the BBP threshold α·λ_max⁴ = 1 as the weak recovery threshold. For gradient descent, they provide a rigorous DMFT description (Theorem 5) with a low-dimensional recursion tracking the full training trajectory, generalizing prior DMFT results that were limited to multi-index and tensor problems.

## Results

The paper's headline result is that the weak recovery threshold of RBMs trained via maximum likelihood (analyzed through AMP-RBM or GD) exactly matches the Baik-Ben Arous-Peché (BBP) transition at λ_max = α^(-1/4), i.e., the information-theoretic limit for detecting signals in the spiked covariance model. Rigorous state evolution equations (Theorem 3) and GD asymptotics (Theorem 5) are derived, showing that both AMP-RBM and GD converge to the same stationary points characterized by Theorem 4. Numerical verification (Figure 1) with r=k=2, n=8000, d=4000, and Rademacher priors confirms that the overlap between the true signal W* and the AMP-RBM/GD output tracks the state evolution predictions and crosses zero precisely at the BBP threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AMP-RBM and GD against the Bayes-optimal performance for constrained low-rank matrix estimation from Lesieur et al. [2017]. In Figure 1 (right), all three methods (AMP-RBM, GD, and Bayes optimality) are plotted as overlap vs. SNR for r=k=2 with Λ=diag(λ, 0.5λ). AMP-RBM and GD (both with random initialization) achieve the same weak recovery threshold as the Bayes-optimal bound, confirming that RBMs are computationally optimal for signal detection at the BBP transition. The state evolution curves for AMP-RBM and GD almost perfectly overlap, indicating qualitatively identical asymptotic behavior. This contrasts with prior teacher-student RBM analyses (Thériault et al. [2024], Manzan and Tantari [2024]) that use a different loss function (zero-temperature limit) and predict a spin-glass phase, whereas the present likelihood-maximization framework yields the clean BBP threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: high-dimensional statistical inference (spiked covariance models, AMP, BBP transition) and RBM learning theory. Prior RBM analyses either reduce to SVD (Gaussian-Gaussian RBMs), rely on non-rigorous replica methods focused on Bayesian posterior sampling rather than the practical MLE objective (Huang & Toyoizumi, Thériault et al., Manzan & Tantari), or provide only heuristic early-training dynamics (Bachtis et al., Harsh et al.). The key conceptual move is proving that the RBM MLE objective in the high-dimensional, fixed-hidden-units limit is asymptotically equivalent to an unsupervised multi-index model with non-separable regularization, thereby importing the full AMP/state-evolution and DMFT machinery developed for multi-index and tensor learning (Montanari & Venkataramanan, Gerbelot et al., Troiani et al.) into the RBM setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous asymptotic characterization of RBM training via maximum likelihood in high dimensions, establishing that RBMs achieve the information-theoretically optimal BBP weak recovery threshold — a result that contrasts sharply with prior replica-method analyses predicting spin-glass phases under a different (Bayesian posterior) objective. The multi-index equivalence provides a reusable theoretical bridge that could extend to more complex generative architectures (e.g., diffusion models, deep Boltzmann machines). Key limitations include the fixed number of hidden units, factorizable bounded priors, and the unproven conjecture that the characterized stationary points are global optima for k > 1; extending beyond these constraints and to overparameterized regimes (k growing with d) remains open.
