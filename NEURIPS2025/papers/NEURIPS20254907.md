## Problem

Existing driving benchmarks for VLMs operate on single-view images or monocular videos, focus predominantly on ego-vehicle actions, and are tightly coupled to specific datasets through manual annotation. There is no benchmark that evaluates whether driving expert models can perform holistic spatio-temporal reasoning over multi-view video or LiDAR, including interactions among third-party agents that do not directly affect the ego vehicle.

## Input / Output

The benchmark presents 971 multiple-choice questions (5 options, 1 correct) spanning 43 scenario types across four categories: ego-vehicle, agent, ego-to-agent, and agent-to-agent. Input format is adapted per model class: LLMs receive GPS positions and agent trajectories as text; off-the-shelf VLMs receive a sequence of selected camera-view images (one per timestep, chosen to match the relevant viewpoint) plus a text prompt; driving expert VLMs receive full multi-view image sequences. The output is a single letter identifying which scenario occurs in the scene.

## Method

STSBench is a dataset-agnostic framework that automatically mines predefined traffic scenarios from ground-truth annotations (3D bounding boxes, tracking IDs, ego-motion data, HD maps) using hand-crafted heuristics per scenario type. Each scenario in the catalog is paired with a list of semantically related negative scenarios that serve as distractors in question generation. A lightweight verification interface lets human inspectors simply confirm or reject mined scenarios and remove invalid negatives, reducing annotation to ~14.5 seconds per sample rather than full manual labeling. The framework was instantiated on the nuScenes validation set (150 scenes, 4790 mined scenarios sub-sampled to 1188, verified to 971 final questions) to produce STSnu. Evaluation covers three model families—text-only LLMs, off-the-shelf VLMs, and driving expert VLMs—revealing that even the best visual model (DriveMM, 44.07%) trails GPT-4o with trajectory text (55.27%), and that all models struggle most with agent-to-agent interaction scenarios.

## Results

On STSnu (971 multiple-choice questions from 43 traffic scenarios on nuScenes validation), GPT-4o (text-only LLM with ground-truth trajectories) achieves the highest average accuracy of 55.27%, outperforming the best visual model DriveMM (44.07%) by over 11 points. Among driving expert VLMs, DriveMM leads at 44.07%, narrowly ahead of the off-the-shelf InternVL 2.5 8B (43.50%), while Senna-VLM (27.70%) and OmniDrive (29.33%) trail significantly. The gap between LLMs and VLMs is most pronounced for ego-vehicle scenarios (GPT-4o: 58.93% vs. DriveMM: 47.79%) and ego-to-agent scenarios (75.15% vs. 61.83%), while all models struggle with agent-to-agent interactions (best: GPT-4o at 42.97%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o (text-only, 55.27%) dominates all visual models by a wide margin, particularly on ego (58.93% vs. 47.79% for DriveMM) and ego-to-agent (75.15% vs. 61.83%) scenarios, suggesting that trajectory-based reasoning is far more reliable than visual spatio-temporal inference. Among driving expert VLMs, DriveMM (44.07%) outperforms Senna-VLM (27.70%) and OmniDrive (29.33%) by 15–16 points on average, with the largest gap in ego scenarios (47.79% vs. 8.81% for Senna-VLM), likely because DriveMM processes multi-view video directly rather than projecting into BEV. The off-the-shelf InternVL 2.5 8B (43.50%) nearly matches DriveMM overall and even leads on agent scenarios (47.19% vs. 38.70%), indicating that general-purpose VLMs can rival specialized driving models on certain interaction types. DeepSeek V3 (48.96%) as a text-only LLM also surpasses all visual models, reinforcing that current VLMs have not yet learned to extract spatio-temporal reasoning from pixels as effectively as from structured trajectory data. Llama 3.2 (22.07%) performs near chance level, confirming that smaller LLMs lack the reasoning capacity for traffic scenario identification even with full trajectory access.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing driving VLM benchmarks (DriveMLLM, DriveLM, NuInstruct, OmniDrive) that primarily evaluate off-the-shelf VLMs on single images or monocular videos with a focus on ego-centric actions. STSBench carves new ground by targeting driving expert VLMs operating on multi-view video or LiDAR, and by extending the evaluation scope from ego-vehicle actions to include agent-to-agent interactions that do not directly affect the ego vehicle. The automatic scenario-mining pipeline from ground-truth annotations (3D boxes, tracks, HD maps) contrasts with the manual annotation approach used by most prior benchmarks, enabling generalization across datasets with different sensor configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that state-of-the-art driving expert VLMs (Senna-VLM, OmniDrive, DriveMM) have fundamentally limited spatio-temporal reasoning, with the best visual model (DriveMM) achieving only 44% average accuracy and struggling most on agent-to-agent scenarios. The finding that a text-only LLM with ground-truth trajectories (GPT-4o, 55%) outperforms all visual models suggests current visual encoders may not adequately support holistic scene reasoning. Key limitations include the reliance on clean nuScenes data (lacking edge cases) and heuristic-based mining that may miss rare scenarios, leaving open the question of whether models can generalize to safety-critical, long-tail driving situations.
