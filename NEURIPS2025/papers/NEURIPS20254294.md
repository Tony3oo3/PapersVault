## Problem

While infinite-width limits of standard neural networks (dense, CNN, transformer) are well-established as Gaussian processes, it was unclear whether this convergence extends to Neural Operators, which map between infinite-dimensional function spaces. The paper addresses two open questions: under what conditions do arbitrary-depth NOs converge to function-valued GPs, and how can the limiting covariance function be computed in closed form for practical use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254294/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on band-limited functions defined on a d_x-dimensional flat torus T^{d_x}, with inputs represented by their finite Fourier series coefficients. In the regression setting, given n input-output function pairs sampled on a regular grid, the model produces a posterior mean function and pointwise variance over the output domain. The band-limit B of the integral kernel is a hyperparameter controlling the effective resolution of the learned operator, and the toroidal domain enables all covariance computations to reduce to finite Fourier sums.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 3.1) shows that NOs with independent GP priors on convolution kernels and Gaussian priors on pointwise weights (both scaled by 1/J_{ℓ-1}) converge in distribution to a function-valued GP as all hidden widths go to infinity. The key technical tool is a compositionality lemma (Lemma 3.2) proving that the covariance of a composition of random operators equals the composition of their individual covariance functions, enabling an inductive closed-form expression for the limiting covariance c_∞. The paper derives explicit covariance formulas for two kernel parametrizations: (1) the Fourier Neural Operator, where band-limited Fourier coefficients are drawn i.i.d. from a complex Gaussian, yielding a finite-sum covariance; and (2) a novel toroidal Matérn operator using tensor-product Matérn spectral densities, which captures non-shift-invariant structure. In regression, the posterior is computed via standard GP machinery on the function-valued kernel, with hyperparameters optimized by L-BFGS.

## Results

The paper validates its theoretical results empirically: the total variation distance (TVD) between the empirical output distribution of finite-width FNOs and the derived infinite-width GP distribution approaches zero as hidden channel width J increases (Fig. 1), and Monte Carlo variance estimates converge to the analytical covariance computation (Fig. 2). In regression experiments (Fig. 3), the ∞-FNO (trained via L-BFGS on GP hyperparameters) and finite-width FNOs (trained with Adam, lr=0.001) are evaluated on test L² loss across widths J and kernel band-limits B∈{1,5,20} on a synthetic operator task and 1D Burgers' equation (ν=0.002, t=0→t=2). In the synthetic setting, models achieve low L² loss only when their band-limit exceeds the ground-truth operator's band-limit (B=5); for Burgers' equation, ∞-FNO with B=5 and B=20 produce overlapping predictions. The paper explicitly notes that close numerical agreement between the Bayesian ∞-FNO and Adam-trained finite-width FNOs is not expected, as they optimize different objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard finite-width Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) from Kovachki et al. (2023), trained with Adam (step size 0.001) at increasing widths J, compared against the proposed ∞-FNO (infinite-width GP trained via L-BFGS on hyperparameters). The paper does not frame this as a head-to-head performance contest: it explicitly states that the ∞-FNO is a Bayesian posterior-mean predictor while finite-width FNOs minimize empirical risk, so close agreement is not expected. What is shown is qualitative consistency: within each model class, increasing the kernel band-limit B from 1 to 5 to 20 monotonically improves test L² loss, and the ∞-FNO with B=5 and B=20 overlap on both tasks. In the synthetic regression task, both ∞-FNO and finite-width FNOs fail to predict accurately when B=1 (below the ground-truth band-limit of 5), confirming that the band-limit is the critical capacity parameter. On Burgers' equation (n=100 samples, m=103 grid points, 5-fold CV), the ∞-FNO and the widest finite-width FNOs achieve comparable L² loss magnitudes, though the Bayesian model provides additional uncertainty quantification (posterior variance) that the FNO baseline does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established infinite-width Bayesian neural network → Gaussian process connection (Neal, 1995; Novak et al., 2019; Matthews et al., 2018) to neural operators (Kovachki et al., 2023), which learn mappings between function spaces via kernel integral operators. The key technical tool is the operator-valued kernel framework of Kadri et al. (2016) combined with Hilbert space-valued GPs, and the Matérn GP construction on Riemannian manifolds from Borovitskiy et al. (2020) is adapted to the flat torus. The paper departs from prior Bayesian neural operator work (Magnani et al., 2022, 2024), which used last-layer Laplace approximations in the finite-width regime, by providing an exact infinite-width characterization. It also complements kernel methods for operator learning (Batlle et al., 2024) by offering a principled way to construct operator-valued kernels from neural operator architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first rigorous characterization of when and how infinite-width neural operators converge to function-valued GPs, unlocking GP machinery (posterior inference, uncertainty quantification) for operator learning. The closed-form covariance functions for FNOs and the proposed toroidal Matérn operator enable practical kernel-based operator learning without training. However, the theory applies only at initialization (not after training), the experiments are limited to 1D problems with small datasets, and the computational cost scales cubically in grid size and number of training functions. Bridging the gap between SGD-trained finite-width NOs and their infinite-width GP counterparts (via a neural tangent kernel extension to Hilbert space-valued functions) remains a key open question.
