## Problem

Existing video reward models for text-to-video generation provide only a single overall alignment score, lacking the ability to decompose judgments into distinct aspects like safety, coherence, bias, and visual fineness. This coarse-grained evaluation fails to capture scenario-specific priorities (e.g., safety matters more for cartoon videos, coherence for sports videos) and there is no large-scale benchmark with fine-grained annotations to train or evaluate such models.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text prompt paired with a video clip, which is encoded by the InternVL2-2B VideoLLM backbone into a hidden state from the last token. The output is a hierarchical score vector: 28 fine-grained criterion scores (grouped into 5 aspects), 5 aspect-level preference scores produced by the gating network, and a single scalar overall preference score computed as a weighted sum of aspect scores. In the preference-tuning application, the video is generated at 512×320 resolution with 8 frames per sample.

## Method

MJ-VIDEO stacks two Mixture-of-Experts layers on top of a frozen InternVL2-2B VideoLLM. The first layer (Aspect MoE) uses a shallow MLP gating network to produce softmax-normalized routing weights over five predefined aspects (Alignment, Safety, Fineness, C&C, B&F), dynamically determining which aspects matter for a given prompt-video pair. The second layer (Criteria MoE) applies a per-aspect gating mechanism that selects and weights the relevant criteria within each aspect, combined with a regression head that projects the hidden state into 28 criterion scores. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) MSE regression on 28 criterion-level annotations, (2) Bradley-Terry ranking loss on aspect-level preference pairs, and (3) joint optimization with an overall preference ranking loss, with earlier-stage losses retained at reduced weight (λ=0.3) to prevent catastrophic forgetting. This decomposition allows the model to assign different weights to different evaluation dimensions depending on the input, rather than relying on a fixed aggregation.

## Results

On overall video preference (strict metric), MJ-VIDEO achieves 68.75% on MJ-BENCH-VIDEO, 64.16% on Safesora-test, and 70.28% on GenAI-Bench, outperforming the best LVLM baseline (MiniCPM at 30.51%) and the specialized VideoScore model (58.47%) by 17.58% relative improvement on MJ-BENCH-VIDEO. On fine-grained aspect-level evaluation, MJ-VIDEO achieves 78.41% accuracy and 79.05% strict preference on Alignment, 87.50% accuracy on Safety, and 95.36% accuracy on Coherence & Consistency. When used as a reward model for preference fine-tuning of VideoCrafter2, MJ-VIDEO improves human-evaluated video quality from 56.30 to 69.90 and text-video alignment from 68.80 to 79.20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MJ-VIDEO (a 2B MoE model) outperforms all 11 baselines across all three evaluation datasets. Against the strongest LVLM baselines, it beats MiniCPM-8B (30.51%) by 38.24 points and GPT-4o (35.35%) by 33.4 points on MJ-BENCH-VIDEO strict preference, while also surpassing the 26B InternVL2 (22.88%) by 45.87 points. Against the specialized VideoScore reward model (58.47%), MJ-VIDEO gains 10.28 absolute points (17.58% relative) on MJ-BENCH-VIDEO and 8.83 points on Safesora-test, though the margin narrows to 1.14 points on GenAI-Bench. On fine-grained aspect evaluation (Table 1), MJ-VIDEO leads on 4 of 5 aspects in accuracy, with the sole exception being Fineness where InternVL2-26B edges ahead (69.81% vs. 68.60%), consistent with larger visual encoders capturing pixel-level details better. In the preference fine-tuning experiment (Table 3), MJ-VIDEO as reward model improves VideoCrafter2's human-evaluated quality (69.90 vs. 64.50 for VideoScore) and alignment (79.20 vs. 74.80), while VBench automated scores show modest gains in image quality (67.89 vs. 65.03) and human action (94.00 vs. 92.00). On generalization to external benchmarks (Table 11), MJ-VIDEO (68.75%) substantially outperforms OpenAI o1 (43.77%) and Claude 3.7 Sonnet (48.62%) on MJ-BENCH-VIDEO, and also leads on GenAI-Bench (70.28% vs. 63.31% for Claude).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MJ-Bench line of work from text-to-image preference evaluation to video, and adapts the MoE-style scalarization framework from Wang et al. [44] (originally for text multi-objective reward modeling) to the video domain. It departs from prior video reward models like VideoScore and CLIP-based approaches, which provide only a single overall scalar score, by decomposing video preference into five aspects with 28 fine-grained criteria and routing inputs through specialized expert branches. The RLAIF pipeline for video generation alignment follows the VADER framework, with MJ-VIDEO replacing VideoScore as the reward signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MJ-BENCH-VIDEO provides the first large-scale, fine-grained video preference benchmark, filling a gap that has limited the development of video reward models. The demonstration that a 2B MoE model outperforms 26B general-purpose LVLMs and GPT-4o on video preference judgment suggests that task-specific decomposition is more effective than raw model scale for this task. Key limitations include reliance on open-source (lower-quality) video generators, focus on short videos, and inability to test against proprietary models like Sora. Open questions include whether the fine-grained MoE approach scales to longer videos and more diverse generation scenarios, and whether the benchmark's annotation protocol generalizes beyond the five chosen aspects.
