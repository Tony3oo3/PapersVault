## Problem

Existing multi-agent imitation learning methods face two fundamental challenges: Behavioral Cloning suffers from compounding error, while Adversarial Imitation Learning is prone to training instability due to its adversarial optimization. The single-agent IQ-Learn framework, which reformulates imitation as a non-adversarial Q-function optimization, cannot be directly extended to multi-agent settings because treating all agents as one entity precludes decentralized execution, and the closed-form optimal policy requires a logsumexp over the joint action space that is intractable for continuous control.

## Input / Output

MAFIS consumes expert demonstration trajectories (100 trajectories for discrete tasks, 20 for continuous tasks) collected by QMIX or HASAC-trained experts, along with each agent's local observation-action history τ^i = (o_0^i, a_0^i, ..., o_t^i). The output is a factorized joint policy where each agent independently produces its action: for discrete control, the agent selects from a finite action set via the softmax of its individual Q-values; for continuous control, the agent samples N actions via SGLD from an energy-based distribution and executes the one with the highest Q-value. The factorized structure ensures decentralized execution without requiring access to global state at test time.

## Method

MAFIS represents the joint soft Q-function as a weighted sum of individual Q-functions using a QMIX-style mixing network, then proves (Proposition 4.1) that the optimal joint policy factorizes into independent per-agent policies of the form π_{Q^i}(a^i|τ^i) ∝ exp{(1/α)k^i(s)Q^i(τ^i,a^i)}, reducing the adversarial min-max objective to a single non-adversarial maximization over Q^{tot}. For continuous control, where computing the logsumexp normalizer Z_{τ^i} is intractable, the key insight is that the optimal policy is an Energy-Based Model with energy function −(1/α)k^i(s)Q^i(τ^i,·), enabling gradient estimation via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics sampling (Proposition 4.2) rather than explicit normalization. This yields a unified, non-adversarial training objective for both discrete and continuous multi-agent imitation, with a target Q-network, gradient penalty, and L2 output regularization for stable continuous-control training.

## Results

On the offline setting (Table 1), MAFIS consistently outperforms all baselines across 15 tasks spanning four benchmarks. On SMACv2, MAFIS achieves 18.35 on terran_5_vs_5 (exceeding the expert's 17.55) versus 7.64 for BC and 15.57 for MIFQ. On the continuous-control MaMuJoCo benchmark, MAFIS reaches 3694.13 on Ant(2x4) compared to 1547.32 for BC (a 2.4× improvement), while MIFQ is not applicable. On Gold Miner, MAFIS scores 4004.02 on the Easy variant versus 2395.90 (BC) and 3252.17 (MIFQ), closing the gap to the expert (4810.23) to within ~17%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAFIS outperforms all three baselines on every task where they are applicable. Against BC, MAFIS achieves substantially higher returns: e.g., 3694.13 vs. 1547.32 on Ant(2×4) and 4004.02 vs. 2395.90 on GoldMiner_Easy, confirming that the non-adversarial factorized objective mitigates the compounding-error problem inherent to supervised cloning. Against MIFQ (discrete tasks only), MAFIS wins on all 12 discrete tasks; the gap is largest on MPE simple_spread (−18.77 vs. −39.95, a 2.1× improvement in return) and on SMACv2 terran_5_vs_5 (18.35 vs. 15.57), which the authors attribute to MIFQ's incorrect representation of the optimal soft value function. Against MA-GAIL (online adversarial method), MAFIS is consistently superior on challenging tasks such as terran_10_vs_11 and simple_spread, while MA-GAIL's adversarial training instability causes it to underperform even BC on continuous-control tasks (e.g., Ant(2×4)). Notably, MAFIS exceeds the expert's return on terran_5_vs_5 (18.35 vs. 17.55) and approaches within 17% of the expert on GoldMiner_Easy, though it still lags behind the expert on MaMuJoCo continuous tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAFIS extends the single-agent IQ-Learn framework (Garg et al., 2021) to multi-agent imitation learning by introducing a QMIX-style value decomposition that factorizes the joint soft Q-function into per-agent components, enabling scalable training and decentralized execution. It departs from the closest prior work, MIFQ (Bui et al., 2024), in two key ways: a corrected derivation of the optimal soft value function (placing the mixing coefficient inside the logsumexp rather than outside) and a novel use of stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics to sample from the optimal policy as an energy-based model, thereby handling continuous action spaces without adversarial optimization. The method also contrasts with adversarial imitation learning (MA-GAIL) by eliminating the generator-discriminator game in favor of a non-adversarial objective, and with behavioral cloning by avoiding compounding error through the IRL formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAFIS provides a unified, non-adversarial framework for multi-agent imitation learning across both discrete and continuous control, addressing two practical pain points: training instability from adversarial optimization and the intractability of logsumexp over continuous action spaces. The EBM perspective on the optimal policy offers a clean theoretical lens that could inform future work on policy representation in imitation learning. However, the method still lags behind expert performance—particularly in continuous control (e.g., ~3700 vs. ~5700 on Ant)—and the reliance on multiple Langevin steps and parallel samples introduces computational overhead that may limit scalability to very large action spaces or many agents.
