## Problem

Existing neural network-based BIP solvers (e.g., MIP-GNN, DiffILO) are limited to linear objectives, while state-of-the-art solvers like Gurobi and SCIP handle nonlinearities only through linearization, which introduces exponentially many auxiliary variables and becomes intractable at scale. This paper addresses the gap of solving general nonlinear BIPs (with sin, log, exp terms) directly, without linearization, in a scalable and differentiable manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a BIP problem with polynomial objective and constraints, which is first reformulated into PUBO form via polynomial fitting of nonlinear terms and penalty-based constraint elimination. The actual network input is the hypergraph incidence matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{m×n} (where each hyperedge encodes one polynomial monomial) and the PUBO coefficient vector Q ∈ R^n, while a randomly initialized feature matrix X^(0) ∈ R^{m×d} provides node embeddings. The output is a relaxed vector x ∈ [0,1]^m that, through continuous relaxation annealing, converges to a discrete binary assignment solving the original BIP.

## Method

BIPNN exploits a one-to-one mapping between polynomial BIP objectives and hypergraph structures: each monomial (e.g., x_i·x_j·x_k) becomes a hyperedge, and the entire PUBO objective is encoded in the incidence matrix and coefficient vector. A two-layer HGNN+ network is trained unsupervised with the PUBO objective as the loss, where the loss is computed via a GPU-accelerated matrix formulation (Eq. 5) that replaces per-term polynomial evaluation with a single Hadamard product and column-wise multiplication over the incidence matrix, reducing complexity to O(mn/T) on T GPU cores. Nonlinear terms (sin, log, exp) are exactly fitted by polynomials over {0,1}^m using an inclusion-exclusion coefficient formula, and constraints are converted to polynomial penalty terms by enumerating minimal violation subsets. A continuous relaxation annealing schedule (γ from negative to positive) first smooths the landscape to escape local optima, then enforces binary discreteness of the output.

## Results

On real-world hypergraph max-cut benchmarks (Table 1), BIPNN achieves the best number of cuts on 4 of 10 datasets (UAT: 8,463; AmzPhoto: 83,446; Primary: 8,516; High: 5,252), while Gurobi leads on 5 datasets and ties on EAT (3,997). On synthetic large-scale BIP instances (Fig. 4), BIPNN's solving time grows linearly with problem size whereas SCIP and Gurobi exhibit exponential growth; BIPNN surpasses SCIP in solution quality once variables exceed 2,500 (d=4) or 1,000 (d=6), and surpasses Gurobi beyond 4,300 (d=4) or 3,500 (d=6) variables. On the largest instances (20,000 variables, 40,000 monomials, Table 5), both SCIP and Gurobi fail to return any solution within 30 minutes, while BIPNN achieves an objective of −5,039.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SCIP, BIPNN shows a clear scaling advantage: on synthetic BIP instances with polynomial degree 6, BIPNN's solution quality ratio to SCIP drops below 1 (i.e., BIPNN is better) once the number of variables exceeds 1,000, and the gap widens rapidly with size. On real-world max-cut, BIPNN beats SCIP on all 10 datasets in Table 1. Against Tabu search, BIPNN achieves comparable performance when variables exceed 1,000 and significantly outperforms it beyond 2,500 variables (Fig. 8). Against Gurobi, results are mixed on real-world datasets—Gurobi wins on 5 of 10 (BAT, DBLP, CiteSeer, Cora, PubMed) while BIPNN wins on 4 (UAT, AmzPhoto, Primary, High) with one tie (EAT)—but on large-scale synthetic problems BIPNN decisively outperforms Gurobi, which fails to return any solution at 20,000 variables / 40,000 monomials within the 30-minute budget (Table 5). The paper attributes BIPNN's edge at scale to its GPU-accelerated matrix-based PUBO loss computation, which avoids the exponential blowup of linearization-based branch-and-cut.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIPNN builds on HGNN+ as its core hypergraph convolutional architecture and adopts continuous relaxation annealing (CRA) from Ichikawa (2024) to enforce discrete solutions during training. It departs from prior neural BIP solvers—MIP-GNN, DiffILO, HypOp, and NeuralQP—which are either supervised, restricted to linear/quadratic objectives, or model only constraints rather than the full objective. The key conceptual departure is the observation of a one-to-one mapping between polynomial BIP objectives and hypergraph structures, which enables an entirely unsupervised, end-to-end training pipeline where the PUBO loss is computed via GPU-parallel matrix operations on the incidence matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Nonlinear BIPs are NP-complete, and state-of-the-art solvers (Gurobi, SCIP) rely on linearization that introduces exponentially many auxiliary variables, making large-scale instances intractable. BIPNN demonstrates that an unsupervised HyperGNN can outperform these solvers beyond ~1,000–2,500 variables on polynomial BIPs, offering a scalable alternative for domains like quantum computing, materials science, and operational optimization. Limitations include reliance on polynomial reformulation (which can become combinatorially complex for high-order nonlinearities), a real-world benchmark limited to max-cut, and the need for careful penalty coefficient tuning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-polynomial nonlinearities that resist exact polynomial fitting, and whether hybrid strategies combining BIPNN with commercial solvers can close the gap on smaller instances where Gurobi still dominates.
