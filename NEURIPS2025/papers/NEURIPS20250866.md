## Problem

Standard autoregressive image generation models produce pixels or patches in fixed spatial orders (e.g., raster scanning) that ignore the natural hierarchical structure of images in the frequency domain, where low frequencies encode global structure and high frequencies encode local details. This misalignment forces the model to spend many tokens establishing global context before adding detail, leading to inefficient inference and suboptimal capture of long-range dependencies. No prior AR method has explicitly ordered the generation sequence by frequency content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250866/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an ImageNet class label (1 of 1,000 categories), NFIG produces a 256×256 RGB image through 10 sequential frequency-band prediction steps. The image is tokenized into 680 discrete tokens distributed across 10 frequency bands at resolutions determined by scaling factors [1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,13,16], with lower-frequency bands using fewer tokens at lower spatial resolution. Inference uses classifier-free guidance (CFG=4.5) and top-k sampling (k=990) over a codebook of 4096 entries.

## Method

NFIG introduces a Frequency-guided Residual-quantized VAE (FR-VAE) that decomposes the VAE latent feature map into frequency bands via FFT with binary masks, then applies residual quantization where each band is represented at a resolution proportional to its token budget (lower frequencies → fewer tokens at lower resolution). The autoregressive generator replaces VAR's next-scale (spatial resolution) prediction with next-frequency prediction: a decoder-only transformer with block-wise causal attention sequentially predicts token matrices T₁, T₂, …, Tₙ ordered from low to high frequency, where each Tᵢ is conditioned on all previously generated lower-frequency bands. Frequency band boundaries are set proportionally to the token count at each resolution level, ensuring information-theoretic alignment between frequency content and representational capacity. The tokenizer is trained with a DINOv2-initialized encoder and DINO discriminator, achieving a reconstruction FID of 0.85, while the 310M-parameter generator achieves a generation FID of 2.81 on ImageNet-256 with a 1.25× speedup over VAR-d20.

## Results

On ImageNet-256×256, NFIG (310M params, 10 steps) achieves a generation FID of 2.81 and Inception Score of 332.42, the best among all autoregressive models tested. It also attains the best reconstruction FID (0.85) among AR methods and a 1.25× speedup over the strongest AR baseline VAR-d20 (600M). NFIG outperforms VAR-d16 (same 310M size) by 0.74 FID and 21% IS, and matches or exceeds VAR-d20 (twice the parameters) while using 25% less inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NFIG achieves the best gFID (2.81) and IS (332.42) among all autoregressive baselines, outperforming VAR-d16 (same 310M size) by 0.74 FID and 21% IS, and VAR-d20 (600M) by 0.14 FID while being 25% faster. Against XQGAN-310M, NFIG wins on gFID (2.81 vs. 2.96) but trails on reconstruction FID (0.85 vs. 0.78), indicating the gain comes from the frequency-guided generation paradigm rather than tokenizer quality alone. Among non-AR methods, NFIG's gFID of 2.81 is competitive with StyleGAN-XL (2.30) and DiT-XL/2 (2.27), though it falls short of the largest diffusion models (L-DiT-3B at 2.10). However, NFIG's IS of 332.42 surpasses all compared models including L-DiT-3B (304.4) and DiT-XL/2 (278.2), and its recall (0.59) is the highest among all methods, suggesting better diversity coverage. The frequency-domain analysis (Appendix B.2) further shows NFIG preserves mid- and high-frequency content more faithfully than VAR-16 (FKS 87.6% vs. 79.5%; high-frequency retention 66.7% vs. 48.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

NFIG directly extends VAR (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2024), which introduced next-scale prediction using a Laplacian Pyramid to order autoregressive generation from coarse to fine spatial resolutions. The key conceptual departure is replacing VAR's spatial-resolution ordering with a frequency-band ordering: instead of predicting at increasing image resolutions, NFIG predicts at increasing frequency bands (low to high), using FFT-based decomposition in a novel Frequency-guided Residual-quantized VAE (FR-VAE) built on the VQGAN framework. This draws an analogy to diffusion models that progressively denoise from low to high frequencies, but applies the principle to the autoregressive setting. The tokenizer design also incorporates ideas from XQGAN (residual quantization, DINO discriminator) and initializes the encoder with DINOv2 weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NFIG provides a principled, interpretable ordering for autoregressive image generation grounded in the well-known 1/f power spectrum of natural images, and demonstrates that this frequency prior yields both quality gains (FID 2.81, IS 332.42) and efficiency gains (25% faster than VAR-d20) with fewer parameters. However, the frequency band division strategy is acknowledged as simplistic (proportional to token count), and the evaluation is limited to ImageNet-256 with a single training run and no error bars. Open questions include whether the frequency-ordered paradigm generalizes to video (temporal frequency), 3D generation, or higher resolutions, and whether more rigorous frequency decomposition (e.g., wavelet-based or statistically motivated) would further improve performance.
