## Problem

Existing non-Euclidean attention mechanisms (e.g., MAtt for SPD under LEM, GDLNet for Grassmannian) are handcrafted for specific manifolds and metrics, limiting their generalizability across geometries. Moreover, their transformation layers (BiMap, FrMap) are numerical approximations that only partially respect the underlying Riemannian geometry. The paper asks whether a single, principled attention framework can be built that unifies operations across multiple matrix manifolds by exploiting their shared gyrovector space structure.

## Input / Output

The network consumes raw multichannel EEG time-series signals, which are first processed by two convolutional blocks and then segmented into non-overlapping temporal subparts, each yielding a covariance matrix. Depending on the chosen geometry, these matrices are used directly as SPD points, decomposed into (U, S) pairs for SPSD, or projected to orthonormal basis matrices for Grassmannian. The final output is a discrete class label (e.g., motor imagery direction, SSVEP frequency, or correct/error trial) produced by a fully connected head applied to flattened manifold-valued representations.

## Method

GyroAtt generalizes self-attention to gyrovector spaces by replacing the three standard attention operations with their gyrovector analogues: (1) linear projections are replaced by gyro homomorphisms—structure-preserving maps that generalize linear maps and are explicitly derived for each geometry (e.g., OPO^T for SPD-AIM, expm(M logm(P) M^T) for SPD-LEM, OU for Grassmannian); (2) attention scores are computed from gyro distances between query and key points, mapped through a softmax of an inverse-log transform; (3) value aggregation uses the Weighted Fréchet Mean (WFM), the manifold analogue of the weighted average, computed via closed-form expressions (LEM, LCM) or Karcher flow (AIM, Grassmannian). A learnable gyro bias and matrix-power nonlinearity are appended after aggregation to increase expressiveness. The framework is instantiated on seven matrix gyrovector spaces—three SPD (AIM, LEM, LCM), three SPSD (product of Grassmannian and SPD metrics), and one Grassmannian—enabling direct comparison of geometries within a single architecture.

## Results

On BNCI2014001 (inter-session), GyroAtt-SPD-AIM achieves 75.4% balanced accuracy, outperforming the best SPD baseline TSMNet (69.0%) by 6.4% and the manifold-attention baseline MAtt (66.5%) by 8.9%. On MAMEM-SSVEP-II, GyroAtt-SPSD-LCM reaches 68.7% accuracy, surpassing GDLNet (65.5%) by 3.2% and MAtt (65.2%) by 3.5%. On BCI-ERN, GyroAtt-SPSD-LEM achieves 79.1% AUC, exceeding GDLNet (78.2%) by 0.9%. GyroAtt-Gr consistently ranks second across all four datasets and outperforms GDLNet in every evaluation setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GyroAtt-SPD-AIM dominates on BNCI2014001 and BNCI2015001, beating the strongest SPD baseline TSMNet by 6.4% (inter-session) and 1.5% (inter-subject) on BNCI2014001, and by 0.4% and 0.9% on BNCI2015001. Against the dedicated manifold-attention baselines, GyroAtt-SPD-AIM outperforms MAtt by 8.9% on BNCI2014001 inter-session and by 5.4% on BNCI2015001 inter-session, while GyroAtt-Gr surpasses GDLNet by 14.4% on BNCI2014001 inter-session (72.5% vs. 58.1%). On MAMEM-SSVEP-II, GyroAtt-SPSD-LCM achieves the best result (68.7%), exceeding GDLNet (65.5%) by 3.2% and MAtt (65.2%) by 3.5%. On BCI-ERN, GyroAtt-SPSD-LEM leads with 79.1% AUC, edging out GDLNet (78.2%) by 0.9% and MAtt (75.7%) by 3.4%. Notably, GyroAtt-Gr consistently ranks second across all four datasets, outperforming GDLNet in every single evaluation setting, demonstrating the generality of the gyrovector-space formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the gyrovector space formulations for matrix manifolds established by Nguyen et al. (ICML 2023, arXiv 2024), which defined gyro addition and gyro distance operations on SPD, Grassmannian, and SPSD manifolds. It departs from prior manifold-attention work (HAN for hyperbolic, MAtt for SPD-LEM, GDLNet for Grassmannian) by replacing geometry-specific handcrafted layers (BiMap, FrMap, ReOrth) with a unified algebraic primitive—gyro homomorphisms—that generalizes linear transformations to any gyrovector space. The key conceptual shift is from designing separate attention pipelines per manifold to a single framework where the underlying geometry is a swappable parameter, with the WFM serving as the manifold analogue of weighted averaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework enables practitioners to systematically compare geometries (AIM vs. LEM vs. LCM, SPD vs. SPSD vs. Grassmannian) under an identical architecture, which prior geometry-specific designs made impractical. The finding that the optimal geometry varies by EEG task (SPD-AIM for motor imagery, SPSD-LCM for SSVEP and ERN) is practically useful for BCI system design. However, the framework is limited to manifolds admitting gyrovector structures, and the performance gains over strong baselines (1–3%) are moderate. Open questions include whether the gyro homomorphism concept extends to non-gyrovector manifolds and whether the framework scales to larger sequence lengths or other modalities beyond EEG.
