## Problem

One-stream Transformer trackers like LoRAT rely on standard full self-attention, whose quadratic token complexity makes real-time inference prohibitively expensive at high resolutions or with extended temporal sequences. Simply extending these trackers to multi-frame inputs with bidirectional attention yields only marginal gains, likely due to rank collapse in deep Transformers, leaving a gap in how to model temporal dependencies efficiently without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253997/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker takes a 224×224 template image (encoded once and cached) plus one or more search region crops (224×224 for the past frame, 378×378 for the current frame in the multi-frame variant) and produces a target classification score and a bounding box via an anchor-free MLP head applied to the search-region output embeddings. During online tracking, the template's key/value embeddings are cached permanently, and the past search frame's KV cache is conditionally refreshed only when the current prediction exceeds a 0.9 confidence threshold, keeping the temporal context warm without full re-encoding.

## Method

LoRATv2 replaces full self-attention in a frozen DINOv2-pretrained ViT backbone with frame-wise causal attention, where tokens attend fully within their own frame but only causally to preceding frames, combined with KV caching so past frames are never re-encoded. To handle the information asymmetry this causal structure introduces across streams, Stream-Specific LoRA Adapters (SSLA) assign a dedicated low-rank LoRA module (rank 64) to each input stream (template, search₁, search₂) while the shared backbone remains frozen, allowing each stream to specialize without additional inference cost. Training follows a two-phase progressive curriculum: Phase 1 trains only the template and single-search LoRA adapters on (z, x¹) pairs, then Phase 2 freezes those and introduces a new LoRA adapter plus prediction head for the second search frame, reducing peak memory by ~11% and training time by 40% compared to one-phase multi-frame training while achieving higher final accuracy.

## Results

LoRATv2-L378 achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmarks: 76.1% SUC on LaSOT, 62.4% SUC on TNL2K, 78.2% AO on GOT-10k, and 44.2% SUC on VastTrack. In terms of efficiency, LoRATv2-L378 runs at 202 FPS with 268G MACs, substantially outperforming its predecessor LoRAT-L378 (167 FPS, 325G MACs) while delivering higher accuracy. The smallest variant, LoRATv2-B224, reaches 72.0% SUC on LaSOT at 713 FPS with only 25G MACs, demonstrating a superior performance-to-FLOPs ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRATv2-L378 outperforms its direct predecessor LoRAT-L378 on all four benchmarks (LaSOT SUC +1.0, TNL2K SUC +0.1, GOT-10k AO +0.7, VastTrack SUC +0.3) while also being faster (202 vs. 167 FPS) and lighter (268G vs. 325G MACs). Against the strongest autoregressive tracker ARTrackV2-L384, LoRATv2-L378 leads by a large margin on LaSOT (+2.5 SUC) and TNL2K (+0.8 SUC), though it trails slightly on GOT-10k AO (78.2 vs. 79.5). Compared to ODTrack-L384, LoRATv2-L378 gains +2.1 SUC on LaSOT and +0.7 SUC on TNL2K. Against the one-stream tracker OSTrack384, the improvement is substantial at +5.0 SUC on LaSOT. The efficiency gains are consistent across model sizes: LoRATv2-B224 achieves 713 FPS at 25G MACs versus LoRAT-B224's 546 FPS at 30G MACs, while maintaining higher accuracy (72.0 vs. 71.7 SUC on LaSOT).

## Relation to Prior Work

LoRATv2 extends LoRAT (ECCV 2024), which introduced LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning for one-stream Transformer trackers, by replacing standard full self-attention with frame-wise causal attention and KV caching to enable efficient multi-frame temporal modeling. The causal attention design draws from autoregressive LLM architectures (Transformer-XL, GPT-style KV caching) and applies them to the tracking domain, while the Stream-Specific LoRA Adapters address a novel asymmetry problem that arises when causal masking is introduced into the one-stream paradigm. The two-phase progressive training strategy is inspired by curriculum learning and builds on the observation that simply extending one-stream trackers to multi-frame inputs with bidirectional attention yields only marginal gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for reducing the quadratic attention cost in Transformer trackers while simultaneously improving accuracy, which is important for real-time deployment on resource-constrained systems. The ablation revealing that shared LoRA fails to converge under causal attention in multi-frame settings highlights a previously unaddressed asymmetry problem that any future causal-attention tracker must handle. Open questions include how far the temporal window can be extended before KV cache memory becomes prohibitive, whether the autoregressive constraint fundamentally limits expressiveness compared to bidirectional attention for certain tracking scenarios, and whether the progressive training strategy generalizes beyond two search frames.
