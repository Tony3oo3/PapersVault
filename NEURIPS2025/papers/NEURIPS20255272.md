## Problem

Identifying the quantum system least affected by noise is critical for near-term quantum computing and communication, yet no dedicated framework existed for selecting the purest state from a set of unknown quantum states. Existing tools like quantum state tomography incur prohibitive sampling costs, and quantum state certification targets a fixed reference rather than a relative comparison. This paper introduces the purest quantum state identification (PQSI) problem and provides the first adaptive algorithms with matching upper and lower bounds on error probability.

## Input / Output

The learner receives copy access to K unknown n-qubit quantum states and must distribute a fixed budget of N total copies adaptively across rounds, choosing at each step both which state to sample and which POVM to apply (the state is destroyed after measurement). The output is the index of the identified purest state, accompanied by a theoretical error probability bound that depends on the purity gaps Δ_(i), the number of states K, the system dimension d = 2^n, and the total sample budget N. The key structural constraint is that the learner must simultaneously optimize over the exponentially large POVM space and the state-selection strategy.

## Method

The paper develops two adaptive elimination algorithms. The incoherent algorithm (IM-PQSI) constructs random measurement bases from Haar-random unitary matrices (approximable by unitary 4-designs), estimates purity via a collision estimator on repeated single-copy measurements, and eliminates the least-pure candidate each round while dynamically allocating more samples to surviving states; its error probability scales as exp(−Ω(NH₁/(log K · 2^n))). The coherent algorithm (CM-PQSI) replaces the collision estimator with a SWAP test on two copies of the same state, directly estimating Tr(ρ²) and achieving exp(−Ω(NH₂/log K)), eliminating the exponential 2^n factor. A lower bound is established by reducing PQSI to a purest random quantum state identification problem over Haar-rotated states, showing that any fixed two-outcome incoherent POVM yields Bernoulli outcomes that are inherently hard to distinguish, matching the upper bound up to a log K factor.

## Results

The paper establishes three key theoretical bounds for the Purest Quantum State Identification (PQSI) problem. The incoherent (single-copy) measurement algorithm IM-PQSI achieves error probability exp(−Ω(NH₁/(log K · 2ⁿ))), while the coherent (multi-copy) SWAP-test algorithm CM-PQSI achieves exp(−Ω(NH₂/log K)), eliminating the exponential 2ⁿ dimension factor. A matching lower bound of exp(−O(NH₁/2ⁿ)) is proven for any algorithm using fixed two-outcome incoherent POVMs, confirming the incoherent upper bound is tight up to the log K factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The central comparison is between two measurement paradigms: incoherent (single-copy) and coherent (multi-copy SWAP test) measurements. When purity gaps satisfy Δ₍ᵢ₎ > 1/d², the coherent algorithm achieves the same error probability with only O(1/2ⁿ) of the sample complexity required by the incoherent algorithm, demonstrating an exponential separation in sample efficiency. When purity gaps are very small (Δ₍ᵢ₎ ≤ 1/d²), both algorithms yield similar asymptotic bounds of the form exp(−Ω(NH₂/log K)), though the SWAP-test variant achieves a smaller constant factor in the exponent. The lower bound (Theorem 5.6) confirms that the incoherent upper bound is essentially tight, establishing that the 2ⁿ penalty is inherent to single-copy measurement strategies and cannot be circumvented by algorithmic improvements alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the purest quantum state identification (PQSI) problem, explicitly positioning it as the first dedicated study of 'best quantum state identification.' It draws on three distinct lineages: classical best arm identification (Audibert & Bubeck, COLT 2010) for the adaptive elimination framework and lower-bound construction; quantum inner product estimation via Haar-random measurements (Anshu et al., STOC 2022) for the collision-estimator analysis under random unitary bases; and the sample-complexity framework for purity estimation (Gong et al., 2024) for the two-outcome POVM lower-bound definition. The paper departs from quantum state tomography and certification by framing the objective as identifying the argmax of purity rather than reconstructing or verifying a state, and from classical bandit problems by adding the exponential-dimensional POVM basis selection as an additional decision variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal quantification of the coherent-versus-incoherent measurement gap (a 2^n factor in sample complexity) provides a clean theoretical benchmark for when quantum memory investment is justified in near-term hardware. The matching upper and lower bounds for incoherent measurements (up to log factors) establish that the proposed adaptive algorithm is essentially optimal within that model, closing a gap that prior quantum property-testing work left open for this specific objective. Open questions include extending the lower bound beyond two-outcome POVMs to all measurement strategies, formulating a fixed-confidence variant, and generalizing to nearest-state identification under trace distance—each of which would broaden the framework's applicability to practical quantum error mitigation and channel benchmarking.
