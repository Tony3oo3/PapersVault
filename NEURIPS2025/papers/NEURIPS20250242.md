## Problem

Off-policy evaluation (OPE) in high-dimensional, long-horizon environments is intractable with existing methods: importance sampling suffers exponential variance growth with horizon, and model-based autoregressive rollouts accumulate compounding errors in learned dynamics. No prior approach simultaneously handles both high state/action dimensionality and long evaluation horizons with reliable accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250242/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline behavior trajectories (sliced into sub-trajectories of fixed window length w), the score functions of both the target policy π and behavior policy β, and an initial state s₀. It produces synthetic target-policy trajectories of arbitrary length T by stitching w-length guided sub-trajectories end-to-end, from which the expected return J(π) is estimated via a learned reward function. A key design property is that the diffusion model is trained once on behavior data and reused for any target policy at inference time, requiring only the target's score function—making it compatible with diffusion policies that lack explicit action densities.

## Method

STITCH-OPE trains a conditional denoising diffusion model on short behavior sub-trajectories of length w, where each sub-trajectory is conditioned on its starting state s_t. At inference, it generates target-policy trajectories via guided diffusion, shifting the backward denoising mean by a guidance function g(τ) = α∇_τ Σ log π(a_t|s_t) − λ∇_τ Σ log β(a_t|s_t) with α > λ > 0. The negative behavior score term (the key novelty over prior policy-guided diffusion) prevents the sampling distribution from collapsing to high-density behavior regions, thereby correcting distribution shift. Sub-trajectories are stitched semi-autoregressively: each new w-length chunk is conditioned on the terminal state of the previous chunk, balancing compositionality (higher entropy than full-trajectory conditioning) against error compounding (shorter than full-horizon rollouts). Theorem 3.3 provides an MSE bound depending on w rather than T, yielding an exponential variance reduction over both importance sampling and full-trajectory diffusion.

## Results

On the D4RL and OpenAI Gym benchmarks (Table 2), STITCH-OPE outperforms all baselines in 11 out of 15 environment-metric instances. Headline Log RMSE values (lower is better) include -2.33 on Hopper, -1.33 on Walker2d, and -2.34 on Pendulum, compared to the next-best baselines (PGD at -1.22, -0.32, -0.91 respectively). STITCH-OPE achieves the highest Spearman rank correlation on 4 of 5 environments (0.76 Hopper, 0.87 HalfCheetah, 0.96 Pendulum, 0.82 Acrobot) and the lowest Regret@1 on Walker2d and Pendulum (<0.01). Across varied evaluation horizons (T=128 to T=1000, Table 4), STITCH-OPE consistently achieves the best Log RMSE, with performance degrading gracefully as T increases, validating the theoretical O(T²) bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STITCH-OPE most decisively outperforms the full-trajectory diffusion baseline PGD [16], which it directly extends: on Log RMSE, STITCH-OPE improves from -1.22 to -2.33 on Hopper and from -0.91 to -2.34 on Pendulum, confirming that sub-trajectory stitching avoids the compositionality failures of full-length diffusion. Against the single-step model-based baseline MB [19], STITCH-OPE wins on Log RMSE in 4/5 environments (e.g., -2.33 vs -1.70 on Hopper) but loses on Regret@1 for Hopper (0.11 vs 0.04), suggesting MB's simpler rollout is occasionally sufficient for policy ranking in that specific setting. Among model-free estimators, IS [33] and DRE [31] suffer most on long-horizon D4RL tasks (IS Log RMSE of -0.48 on Hopper vs STITCH-OPE's -2.33), consistent with the curse of horizon; DRE is competitive on HalfCheetah Log RMSE (-1.19 vs -0.85) but degrades on other environments. FQE [22] and DR [37] are consistently outperformed across nearly all metrics and environments. An additional comparison against Diffusion World Models (DWM) [10] in Appendix L.6 further confirms STITCH-OPE's advantage, and when target policies are diffusion policies (Table 3), STITCH-OPE outperforms all baselines in 6 of 9 instances, leveraging the fact that it only requires the target policy's score function rather than an explicit action distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

STITCH-OPE extends the policy-guided diffusion (PGD) framework of Jackson et al. (2024) by introducing two key modifications: sub-trajectory stitching via conditional diffusion (replacing full-horizon generation) and a negative behavior-policy guidance term that prevents over-regularization. The guided diffusion mechanism itself builds on Dhariwal & Nichol (2021), while the use of diffusion models trained on behavior data for trajectory generation follows Janner et al. (2022). The paper identifies a conceptual gap in prior work: the importance-sampling likelihood ratio can be interpreted as a classification density in diffusion guidance, and the negative behavior score term that naturally arises from this interpretation was overlooked in PGD. Compared to model-free OPE (IS, DR, FQE, DRE), STITCH-OPE avoids exponential variance blowup; compared to autoregressive model-based OPE, it avoids compounding dynamics errors by generating sub-trajectories in a single diffusion pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STITCH-OPE is the first method to demonstrate robust off-policy evaluation in high-dimensional, long-horizon tasks (T=768+), a regime where both model-free estimators (exponential variance) and autoregressive model-based estimators (compounding errors) fail. The theoretical guarantee of exponential MSE reduction in the sub-trajectory window w (rather than full horizon T) provides a principled basis for the stitching design. The method's reliance only on the target policy's score function makes it naturally compatible with diffusion policies, broadening its applicability to modern robotics. Open questions include whether the bounded likelihood ratio assumption (Assumption 3.1) holds in practice for severely shifted policies, whether benefits extend beyond robotics to other domains, and whether the approach can be adapted for offline policy optimization rather than just evaluation.
