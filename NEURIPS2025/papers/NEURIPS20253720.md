## Problem

Spiking neural networks (SNNs) still lag behind ANNs in classification accuracy, and existing knowledge distillation approaches to close this gap require large external teacher models or introduce additional training overhead (e.g., extra classifiers, extended timesteps, or label-dependent correctness checks). The paper asks how to distill a high-performance SNN without any such additional cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253720/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either static RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) or neuromorphic event streams (128×128 downsampled to 48×48, integrated into T event frames via SpikingJelly). The SNN processes each input over T timesteps, producing T per-timestep logit vectors that are averaged for the final class prediction. The deconstruction into T submodels is purely logical—no architectural change is made—and the method is architecture- and neuron-agnostic (LIF, CLIF, VGG, MS-ResNet, SEW-ResNet, Spike-driven Transformer, QKFormer).

## Method

The core insight is that an SNN with T timesteps can be logically deconstructed into T submodels sharing identical parameters but producing different outputs due to evolving membrane potential states. Each submodel's output confidence (maximum softmax probability, averaged over a batch) is used to dynamically identify the strongest and weakest submodels per iteration, requiring no labels. Two complementary distillation losses are then applied: Strong2Weak uses KL divergence (temperature α=2) to pull the weakest submodel's logits toward the strongest, while Weak2Strong reverses the direction so the weakest submodel's dark knowledge regularizes the strongest. The total training loss is the standard cross-entropy plus the distillation term (coefficient λ=1 by default), optimized with a rectangular surrogate gradient. Flexible variants—ensemble teacher/student, simultaneous bidirectional, and cascade (adjacent-confidence-pair) distillation—further extend the scheme without any extra modules or pre-trained teachers.

## Results

On ImageNet, Strong2Weak with SEW-ResNet34 at T=4 achieves 70.53% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best non-KD baseline STAA-SNN (70.40%) and the best KD baseline TKS (69.60%). On CIFAR-10/100, Weak2Strong with ResNet-19 reaches 96.66% and 82.02% respectively, outperforming TKS (96.35%/79.89%) and QKFormer (96.18%/81.15%). On neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS, Weak2Strong with VGGSNN at T=10 achieves 86.70%, exceeding TKS (85.30%) by 1.40%. The method also improves adversarial robustness, with Weak2Strong boosting FGSM robust accuracy by 2.23% over RAT on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 26 baselines spanning non-KD SNN training methods, KD-based SNN methods, and robustness methods. On ImageNet, Strong2Weak (70.53%) outperforms TKS (69.60%) by 0.93%, MPS (69.03%) by 1.50%, and the best non-KD method STAA-SNN (70.40%) by 0.13%. On CIFAR-100, Weak2Strong with ResNet-19 (82.02%) surpasses QKFormer (81.15%) by 0.87% and TKS (79.89%) by 2.13%, while on CIFAR-10 it leads TKS (96.35%) by 0.31%. On neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS, Weak2Strong with VGGSNN T=10 (86.70%) beats TKS (85.30%) by 1.40% and EnOF (80.50%) by 6.20%. For adversarial robustness on CIFAR-100, both schemes improve upon RAT: Weak2Strong gains +2.23% on FGSM (21.23% vs 19.00%) and +1.49% on CW (24.08% vs 22.59%), while Strong2Weak gains +2.15% on FGSM and +0.81% on CW. The method also outperforms TSSD (72.90%) and SSCL (78.50%) on CIFAR10-DVS, confirming consistent gains over prior self-distillation and contrastive-learning approaches for SNNs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-distillation paradigm (Zhang et al., ICCV 2019) and the dark-knowledge insight from weak models (Yuan et al., CVPR 2020) into the spiking neural network setting, but departs from prior SNN distillation work that relies on external teacher models (KDSNN, EnOF), label-based correctness checks (TKS), or added auxiliary classifiers (TSSD). The key conceptual departure is exploiting the inherent temporal structure of SNNs—each timestep instance is a submodel sharing the same parameters—to create teacher-student pairs with zero additional overhead, using output confidence rather than accuracy or loss to identify strong and weak submodels. The distillation loss itself is standard KL divergence with temperature softening from Hinton et al. (2015), so the novelty lies entirely in the deconstruction strategy and the bidirectional (Strong2Weak and Weak2Strong) distillation design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-overhead property makes this method immediately applicable to any SNN training pipeline without architectural modifications, which is practically significant for the neuromorphic computing community where deployment constraints are tight. The notably larger gains on neuromorphic datasets (up to 5.36% on CIFAR10-DVS) versus static images (≤1.75%) suggest the approach is particularly well-suited to temporal tasks, opening a path toward more efficient training of SNNs for event-based vision and sequential learning. Open questions include how to resolve the diversity-similarity tradeoff that limits simultaneous distillation, whether confidence-based identification generalizes to non-classification tasks, and whether the adversarial robustness gains can be further exploited through dedicated robustness-aware distillation designs.
