## Problem

Normalization layers in transformers (LayerNorm, RMSNorm) stabilize training but introduce additional hyperparameters (weight decay, learning rate warm-up) and computational overhead. The normalized transformer (nGPT) eliminates these hyperparameters by enforcing strict hypersphere constraints, but adds even more normalization layers, increasing both training and inference runtime. The paper asks whether the benefits of consistent normalization—stable input scales, prevention of the 'Curse of Depth,' and faster convergence—can be achieved with cheaper approximate operations that do not add normalization layers.

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of up to 2048 text tokens from a 50,304-token vocabulary (GPT-NeoX tokenizer) and produces next-token logits over the full vocabulary. A key architectural detail is that all internal representations are bounded to have L2 norm ≤ 1 (compact space) rather than strictly normalized, and the constant normalization factors ν are precomputed scalars (not learned parameters) that can be absorbed into weight matrices at inference time, yielding the same inference cost as a standard GPT.

## Method

anGPT replaces all normalization layers with approximate scalar multiplications using constant factors ν ≈ ||x||₂⁻¹, justified by the concentration of measure phenomenon: in high dimensions, the norm of a random vector concentrates tightly around its mean, so a fixed scalar suffices. For linear maps, ν is derived analytically (e.g., ν_qkv = √(d_m/d_h)); for activation functions, it is estimated via Monte Carlo (≈3.74 for SiLU). Parameters are bounded (||w||₂ ≤ 1) rather than normalized, creating 'compact' representations that eliminate the need for weight decay. Classic residual updates are replaced with LERP (h ← h + α(x−h)) with a learnable interpolation parameter α, and the residual normalization factor ν(α) = 1/√(1−2α+2α²) is computed per step. This decoupling of stabilization (handled by ν) from loss minimization (handled by remaining parameters) removes the need for learning rate warm-up while achieving up to 40% faster convergence than GPT+ with only ~3% additional training runtime.

## Results

anGPT achieves an average 1.4× convergence speedup over GPT+ (with QK normalization) and 2.0× over GPT+ without QK norm, with only ~3% additional training step runtime (0.1455s vs 0.1416s per step on 4×A100). On downstream benchmarks, the 1B anGPT model outperforms GPT+ across all six tasks, with gains ranging from 3% (WinoGrande) to 22% (LAMBADA perplexity, 14.68 vs 16.73 at 42B tokens). Scaling law exponents for anGPT and GPT+ are nearly identical (α_D ≈ 0.47 for both), confirming that the convergence speedup does not alter fundamental scaling behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against nGPT [5], anGPT achieves a slightly higher convergence speedup (1.4× vs 1.29× over GPT+) and substantially lower runtime overhead (~3% vs ~9% per step), while matching or exceeding nGPT's validation loss at larger token budgets (nGPT is marginally better at smaller budgets). Against the LN-scaling fix from Sun et al. [6], anGPT shows a 1.3% improvement over GPT+ while LN scaling yields 0.0% (no improvement), indicating that depth-specific scaling alone does not address the underlying norm growth. Against the Dynamic Tanh (DyT) normalization replacement from Zhu et al. [32], anGPT clearly wins: DyT degrades performance by 6.5% relative to GPT+, while anGPT improves it by 1.3%. Overall, anGPT dominates all three baselines on both convergence speed and final validation loss, with the added benefit of eliminating weight decay and learning rate warm-up as hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends nGPT (Loshchilov et al., ICLR 2025), which enforces strict hypersphere normalization on all representations and parameters, by replacing exact normalization operations with cheap scalar multiplications justified by the concentration of measure phenomenon in high dimensions. It also adapts the constrained parameter regularization framework of Franke et al. (NeurIPS 2024) by bounding (rather than normalizing) weight norms to ≤ 1, yielding 'compact' rather than strictly normalized representations. The key conceptual departure from nGPT is that anGPT decouples stabilization (handled by fixed normalization factors ν) from loss minimization (handled by remaining parameters), whereas nGPT conflates both roles in its learnable scaling vectors. The architecture retains nGPT's LERP residual updates and reparameterization scheme but removes all classical normalization layers (RMSNorm, QK-norm) in favor of post-L2-normalization and constant factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating weight decay and learning rate warm-up reduces the hyperparameter search space, while the 40% convergence speedup at only 3% runtime overhead makes anGPT a near drop-in replacement for standard GPT architectures with favorable scaling law exponents matching GPT. The finding that anGPT tolerates larger batch sizes than GPT+ at larger model scales could be particularly valuable for distributed pretraining. However, open questions remain: results are limited to ≤1B parameters, ≤7× Chinchilla optimal tokens, a single dataset (primarily SlimPajama), and single-seed runs without error bars, leaving unclear whether the speedup persists at frontier scale or with different data distributions. The paper also notes the method's compatibility with reduced-precision (FP8) training as a promising but unexplored direction.
