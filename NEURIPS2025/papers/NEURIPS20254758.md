## Problem

Existing privacy defenses in federated learning apply uniform protection across all modalities, ignoring that different modalities (e.g., text vs. image) carry different amounts of recoverable information and thus require different protection strengths. Additionally, inter-modal correlations in multimodal federated learning create a 'barrel effect' where breaching the most vulnerable modality accelerates leakage across all modalities, a risk that unimodal information-theoretic frameworks do not capture.

## Input / Output

The method operates on paired image-text data from distributed clients in a federated setting. Images are encoded via LeNet-5 (CIFAR) or ResNet-50 (Hateful-Memes/CrisisMMD), and text via TextCNN or BERT, with features fused either by concatenation (early fusion) or logit averaging (late fusion). The output is a globally trained classification model; the privacy mechanism acts on per-modality gradients before they are transmitted to the server, adjusting Gaussian noise scales independently for each modality's encoder.

## Method

Sec-MMFL introduces two information-theoretic mechanisms. First, it computes the conditional mutual information I(G; D^i | D^{-i}) for each modality i to quantify how much information that modality leaks through the gradient given all other modalities are known, then assigns per-modality noise scales σ_i = c·(1/√w_i) where w_i is a softmax-normalized risk weight, with the global factor c determined by binary search over Rényi-DP composition to meet a target (ε, δ) budget. Second, it adds a cross-modality mutual information reduction loss L_MI = Σ I(G^i; G^j) to the training objective, explicitly minimizing the statistical dependence between gradients of different modality encoders to mitigate the barrel effect. This dual approach—adaptive per-modality noise allocation plus inter-modality gradient decorrelation—achieves more balanced privacy protection across modalities while maintaining model utility.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 at ε=1, Sec-MMFL achieves 86.24% accuracy while simultaneously reducing image PSNR to 5.142 and text recovery rate (TRR) to 0.51, outperforming all baselines on accuracy and providing the most balanced protection across modalities. On CIFAR-100 at ε=5, Sec-MMFL reaches 80.28% accuracy with PSNR 10.136 and TRR 0.64, again leading in both utility and privacy. On the real multimodal benchmarks, Sec-MMFL achieves the best LPIPS (0.975 on Hateful-Memes, 0.801 on CrisisMMD) and lowest TRR (0.47 and 0.39 respectively), while maintaining competitive AUC (0.564) and F1 (0.385).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sec-MMFL consistently outperforms all three baselines on accuracy across every ε setting tested. On CIFAR-10 at ε=1, it achieves 86.24% accuracy versus 73.36% (DP-FedAvg), 80.31% (NbAFL), and 42.13% (LDP-FL), a 12.9-point gain over the strongest baseline. In terms of privacy, Sec-MMFL achieves the lowest PSNR (5.142 vs. 8.166 for DP-FedAvg and 8.998 for NbAFL) and lowest TRR (0.51 vs. 0.61 and 0.72), indicating stronger protection for both image and text modalities simultaneously. The key differentiator is balance: baselines like NbAFL over-protect images (high PSNR) while under-protecting text (high TRR=0.72), whereas Sec-MMFL's conditional mutual information–driven noise allocation keeps both metrics low and comparable. DP-FedAvg-MI, which uses simple mutual information rather than conditional mutual information, narrows the gap (PSNR 7.394, TRR 0.54) but still fails to match Sec-MMFL's accuracy (71.48% vs. 86.24%) or its balanced protection profile. On the real multimodal datasets (Hateful-Memes, CrisisMMD), Sec-MMFL achieves the best LPIPS and lowest TRR while maintaining the highest or near-highest task performance (AUC/F1), confirming the advantage generalizes beyond synthetic CIFAR-text pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information-theoretic privacy assessment framework of Tan et al. [41] from unimodal federated learning to the multimodal setting, replacing simple mutual information with conditional mutual information to capture inter-modal dependencies in gradient leakage. It builds on the standard DP-FedAvg and LDP-Fed defense paradigms but departs from their uniform-noise assumption by allocating modality-specific noise scales via Rényi DP, and introduces a cross-modality mutual information reduction loss term that has no direct precedent in prior FL privacy work. The 'barrel effect' framing—where the most recoverable modality accelerates leakage across all modalities—connects to the cross-modal gradient inversion attack of Liu et al. [29] but reframes it as a design principle for defense rather than merely an attack vector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As multimodal federated learning expands into healthcare, IoT, and social media, the assumption that uniform privacy budgets suffice across modalities becomes increasingly untenable; this paper provides a principled, information-theoretic mechanism for calibrating per-modality protection. However, the evaluation is limited to image-text pairs with specific encoder architectures (LeNet-5/TextCNN, BERT/ResNet-50), reports no error bars across runs, and the cross-modality MI reduction loss relies on neural MI estimators whose accuracy at small batch sizes is not analyzed. Open questions include whether the conditional MI framework generalizes to three or more modalities (e.g., audio, video), whether the RDP-based noise allocation holds under non-i.i.d. client participation, and whether the approach integrates cleanly with secure aggregation or homomorphic encryption layers.
