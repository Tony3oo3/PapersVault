## Problem

While empowerment (an agent's capacity to influence its observable future) has a well-established information-theoretic formulation, plasticity (an agent's capacity to be shaped by what it observes) lacks a universal, agent-centric definition that places it on equal footing with empowerment. The two concepts have remained conceptually distant despite both being foundational to agency, and no formal connection or tension between them had been established.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255481/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on a minimal agent-environment interface of two finite sets (actions and observations) with no Markov assumption, taking as input an agent function (history → action distribution), an environment function (history → observation distribution), and arbitrary time intervals [a:b] and [c:d] that need not start at time 1. The output is a scalar plasticity value (GDI from observations to actions, maximized over a set of environments) and a scalar empowerment value (GDI from actions to observations, maximized over a set of agents), both defined over the chosen intervals. The maximization over environment or agent sets encodes potential rather than actual influence, analogous to the distinction between potential and actual information flow in empowerment literature.

## Method

The paper introduces the Generalized Directed Information (GDI), a new information-theoretic quantity that strictly generalizes Massey's directed information by allowing sequences of arbitrary length that need not begin at the start of time, with conditioning on pre-interval history to remove confounders. The GDI is defined as a sum of conditional mutual information terms I(X_{a:min(b,i)}; Y_i | X_{1:a-1}, Y_{1:i-1}) over the overlap of the two intervals, and the paper proves a conservation law (Theorem 3.5) showing that conditional mutual information decomposes exactly into forward and backward GDI terms. Plasticity is then defined as the GDI from observations to actions (maximized over environments), and empowerment as the GDI from actions to observations (maximized over agents), making them exact mirrors of each other via the agent-environment symmetry (Proposition 4.6). The main result (Theorem 4.8) establishes a tight upper bound m = min{(b-a+1)log|O|, (d-c+1)log|A|} on the sum of plasticity and empowerment over the same intervals, proving a fundamental tension: an agent cannot simultaneously maximize both, and extreme values of one force the other to zero.

## Results

The paper's central result (Theorem 4.8) establishes a tight upper bound: for any agent-environment pair and intervals [a:b]_n, [c:d]_n, the sum of empowerment and plasticity is bounded by m = min{(b−a+1)log|O|, (d−c+1)log|A|}, and this bound is achievable in extreme cases where one quantity equals m and the other is zero. Proposition 4.6 proves that plasticity is the exact mirror of empowerment: the agent's empowerment equals the environment's plasticity, and vice versa, under the same GDI measure with reversed influence direction. Theorem 3.5 extends the conservation law of directed information to the new Generalized Directed Information (GDI), showing that all information exchanged between agent and environment decomposes into either plasticity or empowerment. Lemma 4.2 provides necessary and sufficient conditions for non-zero plasticity: an agent has positive plasticity if and only if there exists a time-step where conditioning on observations reduces the entropy of its actions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or benchmark comparisons. Instead, it positions its contributions as strict generalizations of prior theoretical constructs: the GDI (Definition 3.1) strictly generalizes Massey's (1990) directed information (Proposition 3.2), recovering it exactly when both sequences start at time 1 and have equal length. The GDI-based empowerment (Definition 4.4) subsumes both Klyubin et al.'s (2005) mutual-information-based definition for open-loop agents and Capdepuy's (2011) directed-information-based definition for closed-loop agents (Proposition 4.5). The conservation law (Theorem 3.5) extends Massey and Massey's (2005) conservation of directed information to arbitrary intervals with conditioning on prior history. The paper does not compare against competing plasticity measures from the neuroscience or continual learning literature, instead arguing that its definition satisfies a set of structural and semantic desiderata that prior formulations do not jointly address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Massey's directed information (1990) and its conservation law (Massey & Massey, 2005) into a new quantity—the Generalized Directed Information (GDI)—that handles sequences of arbitrary length and offset, enabling measurement over arbitrary time windows. It builds directly on Capdepuy's (2011) directed-information formulation of empowerment and Klyubin et al.'s (2005) original mutual-information definition, showing both are special cases of the GDI-based empowerment. The key conceptual departure is from the neuroscience and continual-learning literatures on plasticity (Carpenter & Grossberg, 1988; Dohare et al., 2024), which treat plasticity informally or in narrow settings; here, plasticity is given a universal, agent-centric, information-theoretic definition that places it on exact structural parity with empowerment via the bidirectional communication symmetry of Marko (1973).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight upper bound (Theorem 4.8) establishing a fundamental tension between an agent's plasticity and empowerment over the same interval is a clean structural result with direct implications for agent design: over-optimizing adaptability can sacrifice control, and vice versa. The GDI itself is of independent interest to information theory, and the conservation-law extension (Theorem 3.5) provides a new decomposition tool for any agent-environment interaction. Key open questions include how to incorporate goals/reward into the framework, how to efficiently estimate GDI in practice, and whether the plasticity-empowerment tension manifests in concrete learning algorithms (e.g., loss of plasticity in deep RL). The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation beyond a brief appendix experiment, so practical impact remains speculative.
