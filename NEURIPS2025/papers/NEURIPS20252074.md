## Problem

In real-world multi-agent systems, each agent's observation comprises components from multiple entities (allies, opponents, environment) that arrive with different, stochastic delays, violating the Markov assumption and degrading MARL performance. Prior work addressed only single-agent or fixed-delay settings, leaving the fundamental challenge of stochastic, per-entity observation delays in multi-agent environments unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252074/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The compensator receives an extended observation sequence (current delayed observation plus T steps of history) concatenated with past action sequences and a delay-value vector (one entry per entity, indicating how many timesteps stale each component is). For Echo mode, delay values are converted to binary presence/absence vectors to maintain input consistency across autoregressive steps. The compensator outputs a reconstructed delay-free observation via a dual-head design (cross-entropy for discrete features, MSE for continuous features), which then feeds the actor to produce agent actions.

## Method

RDC is a training framework wrapping standard MARL algorithms (FT-QMIX, FT-VDN) with four modules: (1) a delay compensator in two modes—Flash (single-pass reconstruction) and Echo (autoregressive, T-step incremental reconstruction)—implemented with GRU or Transformer and trained online with a hybrid CE+MSE loss; (2) a delay-reconciled critic that receives delay-free global states during centralized training under CTDE; (3) a curriculum-learning actor that linearly anneals from delay-free ground-truth observations to fully compensated observations; and (4) knowledge distillation where a low-delay teacher model guides the student's hidden representations and action outputs via an annealed auxiliary loss. The framework's key insight is that observation reconstruction alone is insufficient—convergence and policy quality must be jointly addressed through the training-side modules, and the compensator must be trained synchronously with the RL policy to track shifting data distributions.

## Results

On SMAC 5m_vs_6m (win rate), the Oracle (delay-free FT-QMIX) drops from 84.8% at delay 0 to 0.0% at delay ≥2, while the full RDC method (Echo+DR+H+KD) achieves 86.3% at delay 0 and retains 51.8% at delay 8. On MPE SPREAD (reward), Echo+DR+H+KD reaches −31.8 at delay 0 (exceeding Oracle's −33.1) and −33.2 at delay 12 (vs. Oracle's −67.2). On MPE REFERENCE, the same configuration achieves −16.7 at delay 0 (vs. Oracle −17.6) and −18.7 at delay 12 (vs. Oracle −43.7), demonstrating near-ideal delay-free performance even under substantial stochastic delays.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is FT-QMIX (Hu et al. [17]), evaluated in three configurations: Oracle (delay-free training, no delay at test), Base (trained and tested with delay, no compensation), and Base+C+DR (with curriculum learning and delay-reconciled critic but no observation compensation). The full RDC method (Echo+DR+H+KD) decisively outperforms Base across all six scenarios: on SMAC 5m_vs_6m, Base achieves 0.5% win rate at delay 0 versus 86.3% for RDC; on 6h_vs_8z, Base reaches 0.6% versus 94.1%. Even Base+C+DR, which adds curriculum learning and a delay-reconciled critic, still degrades severely (e.g., 3.0% at delay 8 on 5m_vs_6m vs. 51.8% for RDC), confirming that observation compensation is the critical missing component. RDC matches or slightly exceeds Oracle at delay 0 in most scenarios (e.g., 99.8% vs. 99.7% on 3s_vs_5z; 86.3% vs. 84.8% on 5m_vs_6m), with the marginal gain attributed to additional training steps from the knowledge distillation teacher. Under unfixed (stochastic) delays, RDC maintains near-Oracle performance with only marginal degradation, while Base and Base+DR show no meaningful delay resistance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on delayed observation in reinforcement learning from single-agent settings (Wang et al. 2023's delay-reconciled training, Walsh et al. 2009's CDMDP) and fixed-delay MARL approaches (DAMARL, TimeNet) to the more general case of stochastic, per-entity observation delays in multi-agent systems. It builds directly on QMIX/VDN value-factorization baselines (FT-QMIX/FT-VDN) and repurposes knowledge distillation and curriculum learning—techniques well-established in other contexts—as training-time scaffolds for the compensator-actor-critic pipeline. The key conceptual departure is the DSID-POMDP formulation, which models each observation component (self, allies, enemies, environment) as having its own independent delay distribution, rather than treating delay as a single scalar applied uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RDC framework provides a modular, plug-and-play solution for a problem (stochastic observation delay) that is ubiquitous in real-world MAS deployments—swarm robotics, networked control, multi-agent games—yet has been largely overlooked in the MARL literature. The finding that neither curriculum learning nor delay-reconciled critic alone suffices, and that the full four-component pipeline is needed to approach delay-free performance, gives practitioners a clear recipe. Key open questions include: whether the compensator can generalize to relay/hopping communication topologies (violating the independent-entity assumption), whether better architectures can close the remaining gap on complex SMAC scenarios, and whether the framework extends to continuous action spaces and policy-based algorithms beyond value factorization.
