## Problem

Existing Byzantine-robust federated learning aggregation methods (Krum, Median, Geometric Median, MCA, CClip) impose significant computational overhead on the central server—ranging from O(pM log M) to O(pM²)—while also struggling with non-IID data heterogeneity. Moreover, no prior Byzantine-robust FL method has guaranteed convergence to a true stationary point (zero optimality gap) for non-convex loss functions. This paper seeks to reduce aggregation cost to O(pM) while simultaneously handling both Byzantine attacks and data heterogeneity, and to establish the first zero-optimality-gap convergence guarantee in this setting.

## Input / Output

Each honest client receives the current global model parameter w^t (a p-dimensional vector), computes a local stochastic gradient on a minibatch of its private non-IID data (MNIST 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10 32×32 RGB, or Tiny-ImageNet 64×64 RGB), and uploads that gradient to the server. Byzantine clients may upload arbitrary p-dimensional vectors instead. The server normalizes every uploaded vector to unit norm, computes their weighted mean (weights proportional to local dataset sizes), and broadcasts the updated global model parameter w^{t+1} = w^t − η^t · g^t back to all clients. The final output is a trained image classifier (MLP, LeNet, or MobileNetV3) evaluated on held-out test sets.

## Method

Fed-NGA's core mechanism is to divide each client's uploaded gradient vector by its own L2 norm before aggregation, then take the weighted mean of these unit-norm vectors to form the global update direction. Because normalization caps every client's contribution to unit magnitude regardless of the vector's original scale, a Byzantine client sending an arbitrarily large or adversarially crafted vector can no longer dominate the aggregate—its influence is bounded identically to that of any honest client. The paper proves that under Lipschitz continuity, unbiased stochastic gradients, and bounded inner-error/heterogeneity assumptions, Fed-NGA converges at rate O(1/T^{1/2−δ}) to a neighborhood of stationary points (Type I bounds), and—under the milder Type II bounds that constrain only the *directional* deviation of normalized gradients—achieves exact convergence to a stationary point with zero optimality gap, a result not previously established for Byzantine-robust FL. The aggregation step requires only O(pM) operations (one norm computation and one division per client, plus a weighted sum), making it the most computationally efficient robust aggregator compared to all baselines.

## Results

On TinyImageNet (MobileNetV3, C_α=0.2, β=0.6), Fed-NGA achieves 56.95% top-1 accuracy without attacks and maintains 45.23–55.82% under five Byzantine attack types, outperforming the best baseline (GM at 55.68% no-attack, 42.88% FoE). On CIFAR-10 (LeNet, C_α=0.3), Fed-NGA reaches 54.48% no-attack and 51.16% under FoE, while baselines like MCA and CClip collapse to ~10%. On MNIST (MLP, C_α=0.4), Fed-NGA attains 96.72% no-attack and 94.71% under FoE. In terms of aggregation time, Fed-NGA requires only ~0.05 s per round on CIFAR-10 versus ~30 s for Median and up to ~1369 s for MCA under sign-flip attacks, reflecting its O(pM) complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fed-NGA consistently outperforms all five baselines in both accuracy and aggregation time. Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), Fed-NGA is dramatically more robust: under FoE on CIFAR-10, FedAvg drops to 10.00% while Fed-NGA retains 51.16%. Compared to Median (Xie et al., 2018) and Krum (Blanchard et al., 2017), Fed-NGA achieves 5–15 percentage-point accuracy gains under sign-flip and FoE attacks on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 51.16% vs. 38.87% and 39.67% for sign-flip), while also being ~600× faster in aggregation time (~0.05 s vs. ~30 s). Against MCA (Luan et al., 2024) and CClip (Karimireddy et al., 2021), Fed-NGA shows the largest margins: both baselines collapse to ~10% under FoE on CIFAR-10 and MNIST, whereas Fed-NGA maintains 51.16% and 94.71% respectively. In the no-attack setting, Fed-NGA also leads or matches the best baseline (GM at 55.68% on TinyImageNet, MCA at 48.67% on CIFAR-10), confirming that normalization does not sacrifice clean-data performance. On running time, Fed-NGA is the fastest across all datasets, with aggregation costs of 0.04–0.34 s versus 1–1369 s for the other robust methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied FL subproblems: Byzantine-robust aggregation (Krum, trimmed mean, geometric median, MCA, CClip) and data heterogeneity mitigation (FedBN, SCAFFOLD-style corrections). Prior robust aggregation methods handle Byzantine faults but impose O(pM log M) to O(pM²) server-side complexity, while normalization-based approaches (FedBN, Wang et al. 2020) address heterogeneity but were not designed for adversarial robustness. Fed-NGA repurposes the simple operation of dividing each client's gradient by its norm as a unified aggregation rule, drawing on the known role of normalization in stabilizing training under heavy-tailed noise (Jakovetić et al., 2023) and in FL heterogeneity (Li et al., 2021), but is the first to prove it provides Byzantine robustness with O(pM) complexity and to establish zero optimality gap convergence under mild conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(pM) aggregation complexity is a genuine practical win over all prior Byzantine-robust methods (which require at least O(pM log M)), making robust FL feasible in latency-sensitive edge settings. The zero optimality gap result under Assumptions 3.5–3.6 is a theoretical first for Byzantine-robust FL, though the conditions (uniformly small normalized gradient deviations throughout training) may not always hold in practice, as the authors themselves acknowledge. Open questions include whether the approach extends to partial client participation, adaptive multi-round attacks where Byzantine clients observe prior normalizations, and whether the mild conditions for exact convergence can be relaxed to cover more realistic non-IID regimes.
