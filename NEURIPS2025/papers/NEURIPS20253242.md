## Problem

The existing high-probability regret bound for GP-UCB in the Bayesian setting, Õ(T^((ν+d)/(2ν+d))) for Matérn and O(√(T ln^(d+2) T)) for SE, is strictly worse than the O(√T ln T) bound achieved by Scarlett's successive elimination algorithm. This paper asks whether GP-UCB itself can achieve near-optimal regret, and answers affirmatively by showing Õ(√T) for Matérn (under a smoothness condition) and O(√(T ln²T)) for SE.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a known zero-mean GP with either a squared exponential or Matérn kernel (with known lengthscale ℓ and smoothness ν > 2), a compact input domain [0,r]^d, and confidence width parameters β_t, and at each step selects a query point x_t maximizing the posterior mean plus β_t^{1/2} times the posterior standard deviation. The output is a high-probability cumulative regret bound that depends on sample-path regularity constants (c_gap, c_sup, c_quad, ρ_quad) whose explicit dependence on the confidence level δ_GP is not characterized, limiting the result to high-probability rather than expected regret guarantees.

## Method

The key insight is that the information gain I(X_T) of the realized GP-UCB input sequence is much smaller than the worst-case MIG γ_T(X) when inputs concentrate near the unique maximizer x*. The proof decomposes regret into a lenient-regret term R_T^(1) (bounded by Õ(1) via Cai et al.'s technique) and a near-maximizer term R_T^(2), which is analyzed via a dyadic decomposition: at each scale, the MIG is evaluated over a shrinking L2-ball around x* whose radius decreases as the time index grows. Under the Matérn condition 2ν+d ≤ ν², the polynomial growth in MIG is exactly canceled by the shrinking domain, yielding Õ(√T); for SE, a careful choice of the threshold T̄ gives O(√(T ln²T)). Supporting this, the paper proves new MIG upper bounds with explicit radius dependence by reducing the problem from a d-dimensional ball to a (d+1)-dimensional hypersphere and leveraging spherical harmonics eigenvalue decay, avoiding the uniform boundedness assumption on eigenfunctions required by prior work.

## Results

The paper establishes that GP-UCB achieves Õ(√T) cumulative regret with high probability under a Matérn kernel with smoothness parameter ν satisfying 2ν+d ≤ ν², and O(√T ln²T) regret under a squared exponential (SE) kernel. These bounds strictly improve upon the existing GP-UCB guarantees of Õ(T^((ν+d)/(2ν+d))) (Matérn) and O(√T ln^(d+2) T) (SE) from Srinivas et al. [51]. The Matérn result is comparable to the state-of-the-art O(√T ln T) bound of Scarlett [46] up to a polylogarithmic factor, while the SE result eliminates the dimension-dependent exponent d in the logarithmic term.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the existing GP-UCB regret bound from Srinivas et al. [51], which the new analysis strictly improves for both the Matérn and SE kernels. For the Matérn kernel, the exponent on T drops from (ν+d)/(2ν+d) (which is > 1/2 for all finite ν,d) to 1/2 (up to polylog factors), representing a fundamental improvement in the polynomial dependence. For the SE kernel, the logarithmic exponent improves from d+2 to 2, eliminating the dimension dependence in the log factor. Compared to Scarlett [46], which achieves O(√T ln T) for a successive-elimination algorithm in one dimension, the new GP-UCB bound is Õ(√T) — matching up to polylog factors — but crucially does not require prior knowledge of sample-path regularity constants (c_gap, c_quad, etc.) that Scarlett's algorithm needs for its construction. The paper also notes that its technique does not directly extend to the frequentist setting or to other algorithms like Thompson sampling, as those analyses lack the required sub-linear regret on index subsets and lenient regret properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the regret analysis of GP-UCB (Srinivas et al., 2010) by exploiting the algorithm's own concentration behavior around the maximizer, rather than treating the input sequence adversarially as in the original proof. The high-level strategy builds on Scarlett (2018), who achieved O(√T ln T) regret via a successive elimination algorithm, and on Cai et al. (2021), whose lenient regret decomposition is adapted here to bound the 'far from optimum' component. The key technical novelty is a dyadic partitioning argument that bounds the information gain over shrinking neighborhoods of x*, inspired by Janz et al. (2020), combined with a new MIG upper bound on bounded domains proved via reduction to spherical harmonics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result shows that GP-UCB—the most widely deployed BO algorithm—achieves near-optimal Õ(√T) regret in the Bayesian setting, closing the gap with Scarlett's algorithm that required prior knowledge of sample-path constants. This validates GP-UCB's practical dominance from a theoretical standpoint. Open questions include extending the analysis to expected regret (blocked by unknown dependence of sample-path constants on confidence level), removing the smoothness constraint 2ν+d ≤ ν² for Matérn, and adapting the concentration-based technique to Thompson sampling or information-directed sampling. The MIG bound via spherical harmonics reduction is also a standalone contribution that avoids the uniform boundedness assumption on eigenfunctions.
