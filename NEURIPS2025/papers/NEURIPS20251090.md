## Problem

Existing 3D scene reconstruction methods cannot simultaneously achieve complete geometry in occluded regions, physically stable inter-object interactions, photorealistic real-time rendering, and simulation-ready physical properties. Prior Real2Sim approaches produce incomplete geometry or unstable physics, amodal reconstruction methods focus on single objects or rely on asset retrieval, and physically plausible reconstruction is limited to simple object-ground contacts or requires full observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251090/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single video of a static indoor scene along with camera poses (inferred or ground truth), instance masks (from SAM or dataset annotations), and monocular depth/normal priors (from Marigold). It produces an interactive 3D scene graph where each node carries a per-object neural SDF, a mesh extracted via marching cubes, Gaussian-on-Mesh appearance for real-time rendering, and rigid-body physical parameters (mass, friction, damping, restitution), while edges encode hierarchical support, beside, and collide relationships. The resulting assets are directly importable into Isaac Sim and Unreal Engine for interactive simulation and gaming.

## Method

HoloScene formulates scene-graph recovery as a structured energy minimization that jointly optimizes observation fidelity (RGB, mask, monocular geometry), generative completeness (virtual views synthesized by Wonder3D after LaMa inpainting of occluded regions), geometric non-penetration (SDF and mesh intersection penalties), and physical stability (simulator-in-the-loop energy via Isaac Sim measuring post-gravity displacement). Inference proceeds in three stages: (1) gradient-based optimization of per-instance SDFs and Gaussians against observations; (2) sampling-based tree search where Wonder3D generates multiple diverse shape candidates per object, and a breadth-first traversal over the support tree selects the combination minimizing physics energy while enforcing non-penetration through SDF pruning and mesh vertex optimization; (3) gradient-based refinement of Gaussians for final appearance. The key insight is that the combinatorial physics-plausibility problem over inter-object interactions is tractable via the scene graph's tree topology, enabling a divide-and-conquer strategy that avoids exhaustive search while guaranteeing global stability along every support chain.

## Results

HoloScene achieves 100% object reconstruction ratio (OR%) across all three datasets (Replica, ScanNet++, iGibson), compared to 56.3–98.6% for baselines. On physical stability, HoloScene reaches 81.7% Stable(All)% on Replica, 70.6% on ScanNet++, and 71.1% on iGibson, dramatically outperforming PhyRecon (5.2–5.6%) and DP-Recon (4.1–9.4%). For geometry, HoloScene is competitive (e.g., CD 4.05, F1 83.21 on Replica) though DP-Recon achieves slightly better scene-level geometry (CD 3.45, F1 87.66) at the cost of physical plausibility. HoloScene is the only method combining real-time rendering with high physical stability, achieving PSNR 27.82 and LPIPS 0.304 on Replica while maintaining 95.7% ground-contact stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoloScene decisively outperforms all three baselines on physical stability: on Replica, Stable(All)% is 81.7% vs. 39.4% (ObjSDF++), 5.6% (PhyRecon), and 8.5% (DP-Recon); on iGibson it reaches 71.1% vs. 36.1%, 5.2%, and 4.1% respectively. HoloScene also achieves 100% object reconstruction ratio across all datasets, whereas DP-Recon drops to 56.3% on Replica and PhyRecon to 77.5%, indicating that baselines lose small objects during optimization. On scene-level geometry, DP-Recon achieves slightly better Chamfer Distance and F1 on Replica (CD 3.45, F1 87.66 vs. HoloScene's 4.05, 83.21), but this comes at the cost of severe physical instability (8.5% vs. 81.7% stable). On object-level geometry (iGibson), HoloScene wins all metrics (CD 3.17, F1 81.31, NC 78.13), validating its amodal completion via Wonder3D. For rendering, HoloScene matches or exceeds DP-Recon (the only other real-time method) on PSNR and LPIPS across all datasets, while ObjSDF++ and PhyRecon lack real-time rendering entirely. The failure-rate analysis (Table 4) confirms HoloScene's advantage: 0 invalid geometry failures vs. 25–38 for baselines, and 18.3% physics failure rate on Replica vs. 60.6–94.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

HoloScene unifies three previously separate research threads—instance-level neural SDF reconstruction (ObjectSDF++, PhyRecon), Gaussian-on-Mesh rendering (SUGAR, Drawer), and generative shape completion (Wonder3D, diffusion SDS)—into a single energy-minimization framework over an interactive scene graph. It departs from prior physically-plausible reconstruction (PhyRecon, CAST) by replacing object-ground-only differentiable physics with a hierarchical scene-graph topology and simulator-in-the-loop (Isaac Sim) stability verification, and from amodal reconstruction (DP-Recon, asset-retrieval methods) by using asset-free generative sampling with structured tree search over the support hierarchy. The divide-and-conquer inference (gradient init → sampling + tree search → gradient refinement) is the paper's core algorithmic contribution, addressing the mixed discrete/continuous, non-differentiable nature of the joint objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical pipeline for turning a single video into a simulation-ready, interactable digital twin with complete geometry, stable physics, and real-time rendering—addressing a gap that has limited Real2Sim applications in robotics and gaming. The scene-graph representation with explicit support/beside/collide edges is a reusable abstraction for downstream tasks (gaming, VFX, 6D pose replay). Key limitations include restriction to static indoor scenes, ~12 hours of GPU compute per scene, and an acknowledged trade-off between pixel-level fidelity and physical stability. Open questions include extension to dynamic/outdoor scenes, articulated and deformable objects, and relightable reconstruction.
