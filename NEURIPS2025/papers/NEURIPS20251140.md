## Problem

Vision-language models struggle with quantitative spatial reasoning (e.g., measuring object sizes, distances, and physical dimensions) because 2D images lack explicit 3D structural information and existing spatial datasets rely on model-estimated annotations rather than precise physical measurements. The paper identifies two root causes: a scarcity of large-scale datasets with metrically accurate spatial annotations, and the absence of depth information in standard VLM inputs that prevents the model from recovering 3D geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251140/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image, a depth map (ground-truth during training, estimated via Depth-Anything-V2 at inference), and a text question as input. The depth map is downsampled via adaptive mean pooling to match the CLIP patch grid, then encoded into sinusoidal positional embeddings that are element-wise added to the image feature map before being flattened and fed to the LLM. Outputs are free-form text answers spanning eight task types: scale estimation, object counting, 2D grounding, existence detection, relative position, absolute distance, scale comparison, and reference-object estimation.

## Method

SD-VLM introduces two complementary contributions. First, the MSMU dataset provides 700K QA pairs with 2.5M precise numerical annotations generated from 2K real 3D scenes (ScanNet/ScanNet++) via a pipeline that builds scene graphs, rasterizes 3D instances to 2D, and uses template-based generation plus LLM-assisted chain-of-thought augmentation (10K CoT samples). Second, Depth Positional Encoding (DPE) encodes per-patch mean depth values using sinusoidal functions of varying frequencies (analogous to Transformer positional encodings but along the z-axis), producing depth embeddings that are simply added to the CLIP image embeddings (E_vision = E_image + E_depth), upgrading the model's spatial awareness from 2D to 3D without any architectural changes or additional learnable modules. The full model is trained via LoRA fine-tuning on LLaVA-1.5-7B (frozen CLIP-ViT/14 vision encoder) for one epoch on 8 V100 GPUs, with the key insight that abundant precise physical measurements enable VLMs to implicitly learn camera intrinsics and the 2D-to-3D mapping.

## Results

On MSMU-Bench, SD-VLM achieves a 56.31% average success rate across eight quantitative spatial tasks, outperforming GPT-4o (32.28%) by 24.03 points and Intern-VL3-78B (33.63%) by 22.68 points. With chain-of-thought augmentation (MSMU-CoT), the average improves to 59.19%, with particularly strong gains in scale comparison (55.66% → 69.81%) and absolute distance (40.00% → 50.00%). On Q-Spatial++, SD-VLM reaches 56.2% success rate, surpassing GPT-4o (52.0%) and InternVL-3-78B (53.6%), and on SpatialRGPT-Bench it achieves 33.3% (quantitative) and 65.5% (qualitative), both state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD-VLM outperforms all ten baselines on MSMU-Bench, with the largest margins over text-only LLMs (GPT-4-Turbo: 15.66%, Qwen2.5: 4.63%, DeepSeek-V3: 7.39%) and the LLaVA-1.5-7B base model (19.45%). Among proprietary VLMs, SD-VLM beats GPT-4o (32.28%) by 24.03 points and Gemini-2 (35.17%) by 21.14 points, with the most dramatic gap in scale estimation (51.35% vs. 3.86% for GPT-4o) and reference object estimation (46.07% vs. 2.09%). Against open-source VLMs, SD-VLM surpasses Intern-VL3-78B (33.63%) by 22.68 points and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (30.82%) by 25.49 points. Among depth-encoded VLMs, SD-VLM leads SpatialRGPT (28.98%) by 27.33 points and SpatialBot (29.17%) by 27.14 points, demonstrating that the depth positional encoding approach is substantially more effective than depth-as-image, depth-as-prompt, or depth-as-token alternatives. On external benchmarks, SD-VLM also achieves the best Q-Spatial++ score (56.2% vs. 53.6% for InternVL-3-78B) and the best SpatialRGPT-Bench quantitative score (33.3% vs. 28.7% for SpatialRGPT), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of depth-augmented VLMs (SpatialRGPT, SpatialBot) by replacing their more complex depth integration strategies (vision-encoder-based depth features, API-based textual depth prompts, token concatenation) with a minimal sinusoidal depth positional encoding added directly to CLIP image features. Conceptually, it draws on the Transformer positional embedding paradigm and repurposes it for the depth axis, while its dataset contribution departs from prior spatial datasets (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, Q-Spatial) that rely on model-estimated annotations by instead deriving metrically precise labels from real 3D scene scans (ScanNet, ScanNet++). The paper's core insight—that sufficient physical distance annotations can implicitly teach a VLM the camera intrinsics needed for 2D-to-3D mapping—provides a principled justification for why depth plus precise measurements, rather than explicit 3D structure, is the right input formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DPE module is a drop-in, parameter-free technique that any depth-aware VLM could adopt, and the MSMU dataset (700K QA pairs, 2.5M numerical annotations) fills a critical gap in large-scale, metrically accurate spatial training data. The finding that even fine-tuning on general (non-spatial) data with DPE yields a ~25% relative improvement on spatial benchmarks suggests depth encoding implicitly unlocks latent spatial reasoning, which could influence how future VLMs are designed. Key limitations include the indoor-only scene coverage, dependence on an external depth estimator at inference, and evaluation on a single 7B architecture; open questions include whether DPE transfers to larger backbones (e.g., Qwen-VL), outdoor/dynamic scenes, and whether the implicit intrinsics learning generalizes beyond the training distribution.
