## Problem

While Transformers' in-context learning (ICL) mechanism is well understood as emulating gradient descent on the latent function, Mamba's ICL mechanism remains largely unexplored theoretically. Even the canonical linear regression ICL task, a standard benchmark for analyzing Transformer ICL, has not been rigorously analyzed for Mamba, leaving open the question of how Mamba's sequential, recurrent architecture enables in-context learning and how its mechanism differs from attention-based models.

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequential stream of N context tokens e_i = (x_i^T, y_i)^T followed by a query token e_q = (x_q^T, 0)^T, where x_i, x_q ~ N(0, I_d) and y_i = w^T x_i for a randomly drawn w ~ N(0, I_d). Unlike Transformers that process all tokens in parallel via global attention, Mamba consumes tokens one-by-one through its recurrent hidden state, and the scalar prediction ŷ_q is extracted from the (d+1)-th channel of the output at the final (query) position, corresponding to the zero placeholder in e_q.

## Method

The paper analyzes the training dynamics of a single-layer Mamba (S6) model with Gaussian-initialized W_B, W_C trained via gradient descent on the population loss. The key insight is that the trained Mamba's hidden state update h_l = α h_{l-1} + (1-α)β y_l x_l emulates a variant of online gradient descent with fixed step size (1-α), progressively aligning the projected hidden state with the latent w as tokens are processed sequentially — a mechanism fundamentally different from Transformers' single-step GD emulation via global attention. To prove exponential convergence despite the non-convex landscape induced by Gaussian initialization, the authors develop three techniques: Vector-coupled Dynamics (tracking inner-product dynamics between decomposed columns of B and C), Negative Feedback Convergence (exploiting self-damping terms to derive exponential decay rates), and Fine-grained Induction (simultaneously bounding squared norms, target terms, and cross-interactions to avoid saddle points). The resulting population loss bound of O(d(d+1)/N) matches Transformer's sample complexity, and the analysis shows the selection mechanism (input-dependent B_l, C_l) is essential — static S4 cannot adapt to task-specific w and thus cannot perform true ICL.

## Results

The paper establishes that a randomly initialized Mamba (S6) trained with gradient descent on in-context linear regression converges exponentially to an ICL solution, with a population loss upper bound of 3d(d+1)/(2N), matching the O(1/N) sample complexity of Transformers (Zhang et al., 2024). The trained Mamba's hidden state update rule h̃_l = α·h̃_{l-1} + (1-α)β·y_l·x_l is shown to emulate a variant of online gradient descent with fixed step size (1-α), distinct from Transformer's single-step GD emulation. Experiments (d=4, d_h=80, N=50) confirm that C^T B converges to a diagonal matrix, the projected hidden state aligns with w as more tokens are processed, and the empirical test loss tracks the theoretical 3d(d+1)/(2N) bound across N ∈ [4, 80].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is theoretical rather than empirical, contrasting Mamba's ICL mechanism against two baselines. Against Transformers (Zhang et al., 2024; Von Oswald et al., 2023), Mamba achieves the same O(1/N) loss scaling but through a fundamentally different mechanism: Mamba's linear recurrence imposes position-dependent geometric weights (α^i) on context tokens, whereas Transformer's global attention assigns approximately uniform 1/N weights to all y_i·x_i pairs. Against S4 (Gu et al., 2022), the paper demonstrates that the selection mechanism (input-dependent B_l, C_l) is essential for ICL; S4's static B and C matrices produce task-agnostic linear combinations that cannot adapt to varying latent w, rendering S4 incapable of true in-context learning. The paper also notes empirical evidence (Park et al., 2024) that Mamba and Transformers have complementary strengths: Transformers handle vector-valued MQAR tasks that Mamba cannot, while Mamba succeeds on sparse-parity ICL where Transformers fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical ICL analysis lineage—dominated by Transformer studies (Garg et al. 2022; Von Oswald et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024)—to the Mamba/S6 selective state-space architecture, building directly on Mamba's ZOH discretization and input-dependent B_l, C_l, Δ_l structure (Gu and Dao, 2024) and its S4 predecessor (Gu et al. 2022). It departs from prior SSM-ICL work (Yang et al. 2024, 2025; Behrouz et al. 2025a,b) that leveraged SSM–online-learning connections to design new architectures, instead providing a rigorous training-dynamics analysis of vanilla Mamba from Gaussian initialization. Unlike Sushma et al. (2024), which augmented SSMs with local self-attention to inherit Transformer ICL, this paper analyzes Mamba's native selective recurrence alone, showing the selection mechanism is necessary and sufficient for ICL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous convergence and loss-bound analysis of Mamba's ICL for linear regression, revealing that Mamba emulates a variant of online gradient descent (sequential, position-dependent weighting) rather than the single-step gradient descent (global, uniform weighting) characteristic of Transformers. This mechanistic distinction helps explain empirical capability differences (e.g., Mamba failing on MQAR but succeeding on sparse parity) and clarifies why S4 without selection cannot perform ICL. Key limitations include the single-layer scope, linear-regression-only setting, and strong assumptions (A = −I, fixed Δ, Gaussian initialization, d_h = Ω(d²)), leaving open whether the online-GD mechanism persists in multi-layer Mamba or for nonlinear tasks.
