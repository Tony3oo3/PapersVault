## Problem

Existing neural operators for PDEs on arbitrary domains face a fundamental accuracy-efficiency tradeoff: message-passing models like RIGNO achieve high accuracy but suffer from poor training throughput and inference latency, while more efficient models like GINO sacrifice accuracy. No prior method simultaneously delivers state-of-the-art accuracy, robustness across diverse PDE types, and the computational efficiency and scalability needed for large-scale industrial 3D simulations.

## Input / Output

GAOT consumes unstructured point clouds where the domain geometry varies across samples (e.g., different airfoil shapes or car bodies), with inputs including PDE coefficients, boundary conditions, and a domain parameterization χ_D. For time-dependent problems, the current solution snapshot, time t, and lead-time τ are appended as additional inputs. The output is a neural field: the model can be queried at any point x in the domain to produce the PDE solution, enabling evaluation at resolutions different from training (sub- or super-resolution) without retraining.

## Method

GAOT follows an encode-process-decode architecture. The encoder is a Multiscale Attentional Graph Neural Operator (MAGNO) that aggregates input data at multiple radii using attention-based quadrature weights (replacing fixed weights in standard GNOs), fuses each scale with local statistical geometry embeddings (neighbor count, distance statistics, centroid offset, PCA anisotropy), and combines scales via a learned softmax weighting. The processor is a patch-based Vision Transformer with RoPE positional encodings, RMSNorm, and long-range skip connections, enabling efficient global information exchange. The decoder mirrors the MAGNO encoder in reverse, mapping processed latent tokens back to the original domain to produce the solution at any query point. For time-dependent PDEs, time and lead-time are added as inputs and a flexible time-stepping strategy (output, residual, or time-derivative) is applied. Key implementation tricks—precomputing and caching graphs, processing encoder/decoder sequentially per sample while batching the transformer, and edge-dropping for very large meshes—ensure high training throughput and scalability to 9M-point 3D industrial datasets.

## Results

GAOT achieves the best or second-best median relative L¹ error on 14 out of 15 2D PDE benchmarks (7 time-independent, 8 time-dependent), being best on 10 and second-best on 4. On time-independent tasks, GAOT is ~50% more accurate on average than the second-best model (RIGNO-18), and on the DrivAerNet++ 3D industrial benchmark it sets a new SOTA with Pressure MSE of 4.27×10⁻² and WSS Mean AE of 1.54×10⁻¹, improving WSS MAE by ~30% over the previous leaderboard leader (TripNet). In terms of efficiency, GAOT achieves the highest training throughput (97.5 samples/s) and lowest inference latency (6.97 ms) among all compared models, roughly 50% faster in throughput than GINO and 2.5× faster than RIGNO-18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RIGNO-18 (the strongest graph-based message-passing baseline), GAOT wins on 10 of 15 benchmarks and is narrowly second on 4 (NACA0012, RAE2822, NS-Gauss, CE-RP), with the largest gaps on Poisson-C-Sines (3.10 vs 6.83%) and Elasticity (1.34 vs 4.31%). Against GINO (the closest encode-process-decode baseline), GAOT is more accurate on 13 of 15 tasks and also faster in both throughput (97.5 vs 60.4 samples/s) and latency (6.97 vs 8.46 ms). Transolver and GNOT (end-to-end transformer baselines) are significantly less accurate on most tasks (often >10× worse) and scale poorly with input/model size, making them infeasible for the large time-dependent datasets. UPT is competitive on a few tasks (e.g., Poisson-Gauss at 48.4% vs GAOT's 0.83%) but degrades severely on others. On the 3D industrial DrivAerNet++ benchmark, GAOT outperforms all leaderboard entries (FIGConvNet, TripNet, RegDGCNN) on both Pressure and WSS metrics, with the most notable gain being ~30% lower WSS MAE than TripNet. On the extreme-resolution DrivAerML (9M points) and NASA-CRM datasets, GAOT roughly halves the MSE of GINO on wall shear stress (16.98 vs 28.48 ×10⁻²) and pressure (7.72 vs 10.57 ×10⁻²).

## Relation to Prior Work

GAOT extends the encode-process-decode paradigm of MeshGraphNets [38] and the GNO encoder/decoder framework of GINO [29], but departs from both in three key ways: (1) replacing single-scale kernel integration with a multiscale attentional graph neural operator (MAGNO) that adaptively fuses information across radii, (2) introducing local statistical geometry embeddings (neighbor count, distance variance, centroid offset, PCA anisotropy) in lieu of signed distance functions or node/edge feature augmentation, and (3) substituting the FNO processor of GINO with a patch-based ViT processor using RoPE and RMSNorm. The time-stepping strategy for time-dependent PDEs is inherited from RIGNO [36], and the all2all training protocol follows Poseidon [22].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical claim—that the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff observed across existing neural operators (accurate RIGNO is slow; fast GINO is less accurate) can be broken simultaneously—has direct practical implications for industrial CFD, where GAOT achieves SOTA on three large-scale 3D benchmarks (DrivAerNet++, DrivAerML, NASA-CRM) with 4–9 orders of magnitude speedup over classical solvers. The neural-field decoder (query at arbitrary points) and the demonstrated transfer-learning capability (near order-of-magnitude gain from fine-tuning vs. training from scratch) open pathways for foundation-model-style PDE surrogates. Open questions include whether the structured-stencil latent grid remains optimal for highly anisotropic 3D meshes, whether the bidirectional graph-building strategy generalizes beyond the tested industrial domains, and whether the approach extends to problems with moving or topologically changing boundaries.
