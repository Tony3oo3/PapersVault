## Problem

Matrix-preconditioned optimizers like Shampoo, SOAP, and Muon have shown promising speedups over AdamW in small-scale experiments, but reported gains are inconsistent and often vanish at larger model sizes. The root cause is the absence of principled hyperparameter scaling rules for these optimizers: without knowing how learning rate and weight decay should co-vary with model width and depth, tuning becomes infeasible at scale and comparisons between optimizers become unreliable.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the model architecture (embedding dimension D, number of layers L, per-layer input/output dimensions d_in and d_out), the optimizer variant (including whether blocking with block size b or grafting is used), and produces closed-form power-law scaling exponents for the per-layer learning rate, the regularization parameter ε, and the independent weight decay. The outputs are structured as a lookup table (Table 1) mapping each optimizer configuration to its scaling rule, with separate entries for hidden layers, embedding, and readout layers, and with L=1 for non-residual parameters.

## Method

The paper derives μP scaling rules for matrix-preconditioned optimizers by reducing the infinite-width condition to a single algebraic equation ηQ(G)x′ = Θ(1), where G = δxᵀ is a rank-1 gradient with known scaling (δ = Θ(1/d_out), x = Θ(1), xᵀx′ = Θ(d_in)). For each optimizer, the derivation tracks how the preconditioner's eigenvalues scale with width, yielding closed-form learning rate exponents that account for blocking (which fixes the preconditioner's operating dimension) and grafting (which inherits the reference optimizer's scaling). The key empirical finding is that μP alone can still suffer finite-width deviations for expressive preconditioners (e.g., SOAP, Shampoo with RMS normalization), which are mitigated by fixed-size blocking or explicit spectral normalization of updates to exactly √(d_out/d_in). For compute-optimal training, the paper further shows that scaling independent weight decay as 1/width is near-optimal across all tested optimizers, and that combining μP learning rate scaling with 1/D weight decay yields a consistent ~1.4× speedup over AdamW from 190M to 1.4B parameters.

## Results

Using μP-scaled learning rates and 1/D-scaled independent weight decay, Muon, SOAP, and Shampoo each achieve a stable ~1.4× compute-efficiency speedup over well-tuned AdamW when training Llama-architecture language models from 190M to 1.4B parameters on FineWeb (20 tokens per parameter, context length 1024). Under the Standard Parameterization (SP, i.e., constant learning rate and weight decay), the speedup rapidly diminishes with scale, dropping below 1.1× for the 1.4B model. The compute-optimal tokens-per-parameter ratio is 7.4 for Muon versus 9.6 for AdamW, indicating that matrix-preconditioned optimizers reach diminishing returns earlier. Ablations confirm that both μP and 1/D weight decay are individually necessary: scaling both as 1/width (AdamW's effective rule) reduces Muon's speedup to below 1.1× at 1.4B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is AdamW (with μP and 1/D-scaled weight decay as its own optimal configuration). With the paper's proposed scaling rules, all three matrix-preconditioned optimizers—Muon, SOAP, and Shampoo—consistently outperform AdamW by approximately 1.4× in compute efficiency across the full 190M–1.4B parameter range on FineWeb. This stands in contrast to prior work by Wen et al. [49], which reported that Muon's advantage over well-tuned AdamW shrinks to ~1.1× at 1.2B parameters, and Semenov et al. [39], which found speedups decreasing with scale. The paper attributes these discrepancies to the absence of principled hyperparameter transfer: under SP (constant LR and WD), all three optimizers rapidly lose their advantage, with Muon's speedup falling below 1.1× at 1.4B. Among the tested optimizers, Muon achieves the best absolute performance while being the simplest to tune, owing to its small number of hyperparameters and low overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Maximal Update Parameterization (μP) framework from Yang & Hu (ICML 2021) and its depth-scaling generalization CompleteP (Dey et al., 2025) from SGD/Adam to matrix-preconditioned optimizers (Shampoo, SOAP, Muon, AdaMuon), accounting for practical techniques like blocking and grafting that prior μP derivations (Ishikawa & Karakida, 2023) did not cover. It also incorporates explicit spectral normalization from Large et al. (NeurIPS 2024) as a finite-width correction where μP's infinite-width asymptotics fail. The empirical contribution positions the work against recent large-scale optimizer comparisons (Wen et al., Liu et al., Semenov et al.) that reported inconsistent or vanishing speedups, attributing the discrepancy to incorrect hyperparameter scaling rather than fundamental optimizer limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves a key open question in the optimizer literature: matrix-preconditioned optimizers do maintain their advantage at scale, but only under correct hyperparameter transfer, providing practitioners with concrete, per-layer scaling rules (Table 1) that make adoption of Muon, SOAP, or Shampoo feasible without per-model tuning. The finding that both μP learning-rate scaling and 1/D independent weight decay are necessary (ablations show either alone degrades the speedup below 1.1×) is a practical recipe. Open questions include why 1/D weight decay is near-optimal across optimizers, whether μP needs power-law corrections for width ranges beyond 10×, and whether the 1.4× speedup persists at frontier scales (10B+) where batch sizes and training horizons differ substantially.
