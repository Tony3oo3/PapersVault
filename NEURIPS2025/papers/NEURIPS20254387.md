## Problem

Open-source image-editing models lag behind proprietary systems like GPT-4o-Image due to three compounding gaps: existing datasets suffer from low resolution, simplistic prompts, negligible edit regions, and lack of novel task types (identity-preserving extraction, hybrid multi-object edits, multi-turn interactions); current benchmarks do not stratify task difficulty or evaluate multi-turn capability; and no open-source evaluation model aligned with human preferences exists for scalable scoring.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254387/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The editing model (ImgEdit-E1) takes an original RGB image (short side ≥1280 px in the training set) and a natural-language editing instruction—either a single prompt or a three-turn dialogue—and produces a single edited image at the model's native resolution (1024×1024 for ImgEdit-E1 and GPT-4o-Image; 512×512 for UltraEdit and AnySD). Multi-turn inputs are structured as sequential instructions where later turns may reference earlier objects via pronouns, impose persistent global constraints, or request rollback to a prior version. The benchmark outputs per-sample scores on three dimensions (instruction adherence, editing quality, detail preservation) plus a FakeShield forensic recall score.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a unified pipeline that constructs 1.3M high-quality edit pairs and trains a competitive editing model. The data pipeline chains LAION-Aesthetics filtering (score >4.75, short side >1280 px), YOLO-World open-vocabulary detection, SAM2 segmentation, GPT-4o prompt generation with spatial grounding, task-specific inpainting using FLUX/SDXL with ControlNet, IP-Adapter, Canny LoRA, and FLUX-Redux for identity-consistent extraction, followed by GPT-4o rubric-based post-processing that discards ~20% of candidates. ImgEdit-E1 fuses Qwen2.5-VL-7B (text+image encoder, 8k context) and SigLIP (low-level vision encoder) via MLP projections into the text branch of a FLUX DiT backbone, trained in two stages: first freezing all backbone parameters and training only the MLPs (50k steps, Prodigy optimizer, batch 128), then jointly fine-tuning the FLUX image branch and both MLPs (10k steps). For evaluation, ImgEdit-Bench comprises a 734-case basic suite, a 50-case UGE suite with occluded/camouflaged/multi-instance scenes, and a 30-case multi-turn suite, scored by GPT-4o or by ImgEdit-Judge (a Qwen2.5-VL-7B fine-tuned on 200k balanced rating records that achieves ~70% agreement with human annotators).

## Results

On ImgEdit-Bench (GPT-4o scored, 1–5 scale across instruction adherence, editing quality, and detail preservation), ImgEdit-E1 achieves an overall score of 3.21, outperforming all other open-source models: Step1X-Edit (3.06), UltraEdit (2.70), AnyEdit (2.45), InstructPix2Pix (1.88), and MagicBrush (1.83). The closed-source GPT-4o-Image leads with 4.20, while the strongest recent open-source model UniWorld-V2 reaches 4.49. ImgEdit-E1 is particularly strong in object extraction (2.55) and hybrid edit (2.87), tasks where most baselines score below 2.0, reflecting the targeted high-quality data in the ImgEdit dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImgEdit-E1 (3.21 overall) outperforms all open-source baselines: Step1X-Edit (3.06), UltraEdit (2.70), AnyEdit (2.45), InstructPix2Pix (1.88), and MagicBrush (1.83). The advantage is most pronounced in object extraction (2.55 vs. 1.76 for Step1X-Edit and 1.88 for AnyEdit) and hybrid edit (2.87 vs. 2.64 for Step1X-Edit and 1.56 for AnyEdit), tasks where ImgEdit's targeted data provides a clear edge. Against the closed-source GPT-4o-Image (4.20), ImgEdit-E1 trails by roughly 1.0 point overall but narrows the gap on style transfer (4.38 vs. 4.93) and addition (3.90 vs. 4.61). In human preference evaluation (top-2 pick ratio), ImgEdit-E1 is selected 51% of the time versus 46% for Step1X-Edit and 91% for GPT-4o-Image, confirming the quantitative rankings. Gemini-2.0-Flash, evaluated only on the multi-turn suite, shows content memory and understanding capabilities but lacks version backtracking, whereas GPT-4o-Image handles all three multi-turn dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FLUX diffusion transformer as its generative backbone and follows the emerging VLM-as-text-encoder paradigm pioneered by Step1X-Edit, replacing traditional T5/CLIP encoders with Qwen2.5-VL for richer instruction comprehension. It departs from Step1X-Edit by using SigLIP (rather than a VAE) for low-level structural features, and from prior editing datasets (MagicBrush, InstructPix2Pix, AnyEdit, UltraEdit, SEED-Data-Edit) by introducing a GPT-4o-driven pipeline with SAM2 segmentation, YOLO-World grounding, and strict forensic-based post-processing to achieve substantially higher data quality. The multi-turn editing formulation (content memory, content understanding, version backtracking) and the object-extraction task are positioned as absent from all prior datasets and benchmarks, extending the instruction-based editing task into interactive, multi-turn settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete bottleneck in the open-source image-editing ecosystem: the quality and coverage gap between proprietary models (GPT-4o-Image, Gemini) and open-source alternatives, which the authors attribute primarily to data quality rather than architecture. The 1M-pair dataset and three-tier benchmark (basic, UGE, multi-turn) provide the community with tools to diagnose specific weaknesses (grounding, instruction adherence, detail preservation) that prior single-metric benchmarks could not isolate. Limitations include English-only prompts, mask-based inpainting that fails on shape-mismatched replacements, no detailed architectural ablation, and the observation that all models still produce images easily flagged by forensic detectors, suggesting non-edited-region preservation remains an open problem.
