## Problem

Existing hybrid-action RL algorithms (e.g., HPPO, PDQN) maximize reward in parameterized action spaces but routinely violate safety constraints, while standard constrained RL methods (Lagrange multipliers, penalty functions) are unstable and hyperparameter-sensitive when extended to discrete-continuous hybrid actions. No prior work simultaneously handles parameterized action spaces and cost constraints in a principled, stable manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state-vector observation from the environment and outputs a parameterized action: a discrete action selected from a finite set (e.g., turn, accel, break) paired with a continuous parameter (e.g., acceleration magnitude, steering angle) specific to that discrete choice. The policy is decomposed into a discrete actor π_d and a continuous actor π_c, and the safety check uses the undiscounted cumulative cost return rather than the discounted cost state-value, to avoid missing unsafe episodes.

## Method

CHPO introduces the CPMDP framework (Constrained Parameterized-action MDP) that unifies CMDP cost constraints with PAMDP parameterized actions, and builds a constrained hybrid-action actor-critic architecture with separate reward and cost critics plus discrete and continuous actors. The key mechanism replaces Lagrange multipliers with a sign-function switch: when the current policy satisfies cost constraints, the PPO-style clipped objective maximizes reward advantage; when violated, it minimizes cost advantage, inspired by CRPO's primal stochastic approximation. The discrete and continuous policy components are updated jointly within this single clipped surrogate, avoiding the instability of auxiliary dual variables. Theoretical analysis proves that after K updates the policy gap and cost violation both converge at O(1/√K) rates, with bounds depending on state/action space dimensions and the discount factor.

## Results

At the standard cost limit c̄ᵢ=1, CHPO achieves the highest reward across all four tasks while keeping average costs within the safety constraint: Moving (1.41±0.55), Sliding (−0.37±0.52), HardMove (0.89±0.59), and Parking (37.63±3.04). CHPO also exhibits the lowest cost on Moving (0.22±0.99) and Parking (0.93±3.17), and the most stable reward variance on Parking (±3.04 vs. ±25.21 for HPPO-Lag). Across relaxed cost limits (c̄ᵢ=1.5, 2), CHPO consistently delivers the best reward among all baselines that satisfy the constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHPO outperforms all three baselines on reward across all four tasks at c̄ᵢ=1. On Moving, CHPO (1.41) beats PADDPG-Lag (1.30) by ~8% and HPPO-Lag (−0.43) by a wide margin, while also achieving the lowest cost (0.22 vs. 0.36 and 0.17 with much higher variance). On HardMove, the gap is most dramatic: CHPO achieves a positive reward of 0.89 while all baselines remain negative (PADDPG-Lag −0.29, HPPO-Lag −0.51, PDQN-Rco −0.88). On Parking, CHPO (37.63±3.04) surpasses PDQN-Rco (32.72±7.44) by ~15% with substantially lower variance, and outperforms PADDPG-Lag (26.66) and HPPO-Lag (20.01) by large margins. PDQN-Rco achieves the lowest cost on Sliding (0.12) and Parking (0.21) but at the expense of the worst rewards (−0.99 and 32.72 respectively), confirming the paper's observation that reward-constrained methods drive hybrid-action policies toward local optima. PADDPG-Lag violates the cost constraint on Sliding (1.09 > 1.0) at c̄ᵢ=1, while CHPO stays within bounds (0.92).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed but previously separate lines of work: parameterized-action RL (PAMDP, HPPO, PADDPG, PDQN) and constrained RL (CMDP, CPO, RCPO, CRPO). It introduces the CPMDP formulation, which augments the PAMDP tuple with a cost set, and adopts the hybrid actor-critic architecture of HPPO with separate discrete and continuous policy heads. The key methodological departure from standard constrained RL is the rejection of Lagrange multiplier / primal-dual approaches in favor of a direct primal policy gradient strategy inspired by CRPO, where the policy alternates between reward maximization and cost minimization based on whether the current policy satisfies the cost threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap: no prior algorithm simultaneously handles parameterized (discrete-continuous) action spaces and cost constraints, a combination that arises naturally in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and robotic manipulation. However, the evaluation is limited to four relatively simple 2D simulation tasks from DI-engine plus one custom Parking task, all with a single cost constraint, and the method is online-only with no discussion of sample efficiency or real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-constraint settings with complex hybrid action structures, whether offline or model-based variants are feasible, and how the C/A ratio scheduling generalizes beyond the heuristic used here.
