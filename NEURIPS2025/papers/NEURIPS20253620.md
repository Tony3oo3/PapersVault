## Problem

Existing cross-modal hashing methods assume fully accurate supervision or only handle label noise by replacing correct labels with incorrect ones, but real-world annotations are often redundant—each sample carries a candidate label set mixing ground-truth and spurious labels. No prior CMH method explicitly identifies the true label subset within redundant annotations while simultaneously addressing cross-modal semantic divergence, leading to overfitting on noisy labels and distorted hash-space alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes image-text pairs (VGG19 features for images, Doc2Vec features for text) each annotated with a multi-label candidate vector containing both true and redundant noisy labels, parameterized by a redundant rate (ratio of noisy to true labels) ranging from 1.0 to 2.5. It produces L-bit binary hash codes (L ∈ {32, 64, 128}) for each modality, enabling cross-modal retrieval via Hamming distance. A linear classifier with sigmoid activation on the continuous hash representations yields per-class prediction probabilities used throughout the confidence-reconstruction pipeline.

## Method

NACD comprises two coupled modules. The Neighbor-aware Confidence Reconstruction (NACR) module iteratively refines per-sample label confidence via a momentum-based update (γ decaying 0.95→0.8) and then aggregates confidence from C=20 cross-modal nearest neighbors weighted by fused image-text similarity, mixing the neighbor consensus with self-prediction through a coefficient λ to produce a reconstructed confidence vector. The Class-aware Robust Contrastive Hashing (CRCH) module computes a dynamic class-wise threshold t_k as the mean reconstructed confidence per class, binarizes confidences into pseudo-labels, and builds an IoU-based label-similarity matrix to define positive and negative contrastive pairs; a margin-based thresholding mechanism (inherited from UCCH) further suppresses overly easy negatives. A quantization loss penalizes deviation of continuous hash values from ±1 to reduce binary quantization error. Training uses a 10-epoch warm-up (disabling CM and threshold updates), RMSprop at lr=1e-5, batch size 128, and 100 total epochs on 4×V100 GPUs.

## Results

NACD reports average MAP (I2T + T2I) across three datasets (MIRFlickr-25k, NUS-WIDE, MS-COCO) at hash code lengths of 32/64/128 bits under redundant rates of 1.0–2.5. At the most challenging setting (128 bits, redundant rate 2.5), NACD achieves 75.3% on Flickr, 69.3% on NUS, and 68.8% on COCO, exceeding the second-best method by 3.3%, 1.7%, and 2.6% respectively. A key finding is that while all supervised baselines degrade sharply as the redundant rate increases (e.g., MIAN drops from 73.0% to 60.2% on Flickr), NACD remains remarkably stable (77.1% → 75.3%), confirming its robustness to noisy supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NACD is compared against 11 baselines: five unsupervised (DJSRH, DGCPN, PIP, CIRH, UCCH) and six supervised (CMMQ, MIAN, LtCMH, DHRL, NRCH, RSHNL) cross-modal hashing methods. NACD outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset, bit length, and redundant rate combination. Among supervised methods, NRCH (ACM MM 2024) is the closest competitor, but NACD leads it by 1.4–3.3 points at 128 bits under high redundancy (e.g., 75.3% vs. 63.9% on Flickr at RR=2.5). The gap widens substantially as the redundant rate increases: at RR=1.0, NACD leads NRCH by ~2.7 points on Flickr, but at RR=2.5 the margin grows to 11.4 points, demonstrating that NACD's neighbor-aware disambiguation becomes increasingly critical under heavier noise. Unsupervised methods (notably UCCH) remain flat across redundant rates since they ignore labels, but they are consistently 3–13 points below NACD, confirming that even under heavy annotation noise, leveraging (disambiguated) supervision is far more effective than discarding it entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: supervised cross-modal hashing (CMH) and partial multi-label learning (PML). Prior supervised CMH methods (MIAN, RSHNL, NRCH) assume either fully accurate labels or simple label-flip noise, while PML methods handle redundant candidate label sets but operate in a shared feature space and ignore cross-modal divergence. NACD bridges this gap by adapting PML-style neighbor-based confidence reconstruction to the cross-modal hashing setting, and borrows the margin-based thresholding mechanism from UCCH [22] for robust contrastive pair construction. The key conceptual departure is treating redundant annotations as a first-class supervision signal in CMH rather than reducing them to a simpler noise model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and previously unformalized gap: real-world multimodal annotations are often redundant (candidate label sets mixing true and spurious labels), yet no prior CMH method explicitly handles this setting. The modular design (NACR for disambiguation, CRCH for robust pair construction) could be adapted to other noisy-supervision retrieval tasks. However, the authors acknowledge two important limitations: the method does not account for semantic similarity between true and noisy labels (e.g., 'car' vs. 'truck'), and experiments are restricted to image-text pairs on three datasets, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to additional modalities or to settings where noisy labels are semantically close to ground truth.
