## Problem

Existing generative robotic manipulation frameworks synthesize scenes and rewards but overlook tool-use scenarios, and simply retrieving human-designed tools (e.g., a rolling pin) often fails because such tools are poorly suited for robotic grasping and control. Prior tool-design methods either optimize within rigid predefined templates or apply generic text-to-3D generation that lacks functional grounding for physically realizable, robot-compatible tools. The paper addresses the need for a fully automated pipeline that designs task-specific tools from scratch and simultaneously plans how a robot should use them.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language task description, a 3D scene configuration specifying objects with mesh files, positions, and Euler angles, and a task-specific scalar reward function. It produces a parameterized 3D tool mesh (assembled from geometric primitives and text-to-3D generated components via a CSG-like tree of operations), the tool's initial placement as a position and Euler-angle pair, and a manipulation trajectory expressed as a sequence of three high-level API calls (grasp, release, move). The tool is specified in a JSON format with per-part geometry type, size parameters, a graspability flag, and executable Python assembly and placement functions, making the output both editable and directly simulatable.

## Method

RobotSmith is a three-stage pipeline that couples VLM-based design with physics-based optimization. First, a dual-agent Critic Tool Designer (Proposer and Critic, both o3-mini) iteratively proposes and refines tool geometries: the Proposer outputs a JSON tool spec with parts and a CSG-like assembly function, renders multi-view images, and the Critic evaluates structural validity and task fit until returning a termination signal. Second, a Tool User agent generates an initial trajectory using three minimal APIs (grasp with sampling-based pose search, release, and move with IK + motion planning). Third, a Joint Optimizer applies CMA-ES (population 20, 50 iterations) to simultaneously fine-tune tool shape parameters (bounded to [0.5s₀, 2.0s₀]) and trajectory waypoints (±0.2 m translation, ±π rotation) by executing candidates in the Genesis physics simulator and maximizing the task reward. The key architectural choice is a modular, parameterized tool representation combining procedural primitives with text-to-3D generated meshes, which enables precise geometric refinement without full redesign and supports both simple and semantically complex tool geometries.

## Results

Across 9 diverse manipulation tasks (rigid, deformable, and fluid objects), RobotSmith achieves an overall best performance score of 0.94 and a 50.0% success rate, substantially outperforming the 3D generation baseline Meshy (0.72 / 21.4%) and the tool retrieval baseline (0.53 / 11.1%). The no-tool baseline achieves only 0.24 / 2.8%, confirming that purposeful tool design is essential for these tasks. Per-task, the method reaches 1.00 best score on Lift a Bowl, Lift a Piggy, and Flatten Dough, with success rates up to 87.5% (Lift a Piggy) and 62.5% (Flatten Dough).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobotSmith outperforms all four baselines on the overall success rate (50.0% vs. 21.4% for Meshy, 11.1% for Retrieval, and 2.8% for No Tool). Against Meshy (text-to-3D generation), the proposed method wins on 7 of 9 tasks in success rate, with the largest gaps on Reach (50% vs. 0%), Lift a Bowl (50% vs. 0%), and Transport Water (25% vs. 0%); Meshy only edges ahead on Hold a Phone (67.5% vs. 75% is actually Ours winning) — in fact Ours wins or ties on every task. Against the Retrieval baseline (BlenderKit semantic search), RobotSmith dominates on nearly all tasks, as retrieved tools designed for human use (e.g., rolling pins, hammers) are poorly suited for robotic grasping. The No Tool baseline confirms that without a purpose-built tool, the robot cannot complete any task above the 0.8 success threshold except Cut Dough (25%), and even that success is often accidental. ShapeTalk (3D editing via natural language), tested qualitatively, fails on all tasks due to incoherent edits from its implicit representation, further validating the need for the paper's parameterized, part-based tool representation with explicit assembly logic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of VLM-based reasoning, text-to-3D generation, and physics simulation for robotic tool design. Prior tool-design work (Liu et al. 2023, Guo et al. 2024, Li et al. 2023) is constrained to predefined templates with limited parameter tuning, while 3D generative approaches (Wu et al. 2019, Ha et al. 2021, Wang et al. 2023) optimize in latent spaces not designed for functional tool geometry. RobotSmith departs from both by using a dual-agent VLM loop (o3-mini) to propose and critique tool designs in a modular CSG-like representation, then jointly refining geometry and trajectory via CMA-ES in the Genesis physics simulator—effectively repurposing VLM commonsense priors as a design oracle grounded by simulation feedback rather than as a direct geometry generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that fully autonomous, template-free tool design and use is feasible, lifting task success from 2.8% (no tool) to 50% across diverse rigid, deformable, and fluid manipulation tasks, with real-world 3D-printed validation on an XArm7. A key open question is scalability: the current optimizer only adjusts scale, translation, and rotation, precluding topology changes or part reconfiguration that more complex tools would require. The reliance on a single VLM (o3-mini) and a single text-to-3D model (Meshy) also raises questions about robustness across model generations and whether the dual-agent design loop generalizes to tools with more intricate functional requirements.
