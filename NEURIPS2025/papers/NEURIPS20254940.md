## Problem

Drone visual active tracking (VAT) via reinforcement learning lacks a unified, high-fidelity benchmark that captures open-world complexity (diverse scenes, human-like target behaviors, full drone dynamics). Additionally, existing distance-based rewards fail under the tilted top-down viewpoints typical of air-to-ground tracking, because affine projection distorts the relationship between physical distance and image-plane position, providing misleading feedback to the agent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254940/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step the agent receives a single 84×84 RGB frame from the drone's downward-facing camera (DJI Matrice 100 with 3-axis gimbal) and outputs one of seven discrete maneuvers: forward, backward, leftward, rightward, turn left, turn right, or stop. The observation is purely visual with no explicit target coordinates at test time; during training the simulator provides privileged information (target position, camera-to-world transform) exclusively for reward computation, not as agent input.

## Method

GC-VAT replaces the standard Euclidean-distance reward with a Goal-Centered Reward built on a deviation metric φ(P_g, C_g) that normalizes the target's ground-plane distance to the image-center projection by the distance to the projected image boundary, making the reward invariant to the affine distortion of tilted viewpoints. The reward is r_gc = tanh(α(1−φ)³) inside a truncated image region (λ_clip=0.7) and zero outside, and the paper proves (Proposition 1) that any distance-based reward satisfying the monotonicity principle will assign higher rewards to farther targets under non-horizontal viewpoints. Training uses PPO with a CNN(C8×8-16S4)→GRU(256)→FC(200) backbone, a two-stage Curriculum-Based Training strategy (linear trajectories/no obstacles → diverse behaviors/occlusions), and domain randomization of initial drone position, orientation, and gimbal pitch to encourage exploration. The DAT benchmark provides 24 city-scale scenes (6 base scenes × 4 weather conditions) generated via a digital-twin tool from OpenStreetMap, full Webots drone dynamics, and SUMO-driven human-like target behaviors across five target categories.

## Results

On the DAT benchmark, GC-VAT achieves a within-scene Tracking Success Rate (TSR) of 72% (avg across 6 city-scale scenes) and a Cumulative Reward (CR) of 242, substantially outperforming D-VAT (19% TSR, CR 35) and AOT (22% TSR, CR 49). In cross-scene generalization, GC-VAT reaches 57% TSR and CR 176, while in cross-domain (night/foggy/snow) it achieves 67% TSR and CR 213. On real-world deployment, GC-VAT attains a zero-shot TSR of 88.4% on a DJI Mini 3 Pro drone and an 81.0% Correct Action Rate on VOT, DTB70, and UAVDT video sequences (vs. 42% for a random policy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GC-VAT decisively outperforms both baselines across all evaluation settings. Against D-VAT [19], GC-VAT improves within-scene TSR by 279% (0.19→0.72), cross-scene TSR by 200% (0.19→0.57), and cross-domain TSR by 253% (0.19→0.67). Against AOT [39], the within-scene TSR gap is from 0.22 to 0.72 (a 3.3× improvement), and cross-domain TSR rises from 0.22 to 0.67. The paper attributes the baselines' failure to their use of Euclidean distance-based rewards, which the authors prove (Proposition 1) to be misaligned with the true deviation metric when the camera is not at a fixed horizontal forward viewpoint — a condition that is always violated in air-to-ground drone tracking. Ablation confirms that replacing the Goal-Centered Reward with D-VAT's distance-based reward drops TSR from 0.68 to 0.06 (an ~800% degradation), validating the theoretical analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of reinforcement learning-based Visual Active Tracking (VAT) initiated by AOT [Luo et al., TPAMI 2019], AD-VAT+ [Zhong et al., TPAMI 2019], and D-VAT [Dionigi et al., RA-L 2024], which model drone tracking as an MDP with end-to-end policy learning. It departs from these methods in three key ways: (1) replacing distance-based rewards with a Goal-Centered Reward grounded in a deviation metric that accounts for affine projection distortion, backed by a theoretical proof that Euclidean-distance rewards are provably misaligned under non-horizontal camera viewpoints; (2) introducing a Curriculum-Based Training strategy to handle the vastly larger and more complex open-world scenes; and (3) constructing the DAT benchmark with full-physics drone dynamics (Webots), human-like target behaviors (SUMO), and a digital twin tool for unlimited scene generation, in contrast to the 2–8 manually built scenes with rule-based or policy-based targets in prior benchmarks. The network architecture (CNN + GRU) and PPO training algorithm are standard and shared with prior VAT work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DAT benchmark is the first to combine 24 city-scale scenes, full drone dynamics, human-like target behaviors, and a digital twin generation tool, addressing the long-standing lack of a unified, scalable evaluation platform for drone VAT. The theoretical proof that distance-based rewards fail under tilted top-down viewpoints provides a principled justification for the Goal-Centered Reward design and explains why prior methods plateau at low performance in realistic air-to-ground settings. Open questions remain: the benchmark covers only 6 base scenes × 4 weather conditions, the method uses discrete actions limiting control precision, and the sim-to-real validation, while promising (88.4% TSR on a DJI Mini 3 Pro), is limited to zero-shot transfer on a single drone and a small number of video sequences. The digital twin tool's ability to generate unlimited scenes from OpenStreetMap data positions DAT as a potentially widely adopted platform for future drone tracking research.
