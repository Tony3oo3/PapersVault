## Problem

Large foundation models struggle to acquire transferable, structured thinking abilities because existing instruction data is either labor-intensive to curate, cognitively shallow, or modality-inconsistent. Self-supervised synthesis methods like MMEvol and MMInstruct produce data with limited reasoning depth and high quality variance, while RL-based approaches incur prohibitive computational costs. The paper asks what specifically matters in question synthesis design for enabling thinking-centric fine-tuning with minimal data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255676/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a meta-topic sampled from eight cognitive categories (quantitative, causal, temporal, social-ethical, etc.) and a VLM as the sole generation engine, requiring no external knowledge sources. In the multimodal extension, anchor image-text pairs from ScienceQA or OK-VQA provide visual grounding. The output is 400 structured Chinese multi-hop QA samples, each containing a Question, Answer, and explicit reasoning trace (T_i), which are then used for LoRA fine-tuning under a four-phase progressive curriculum (guided answering → reason reconstruction → paired reasoning → autonomous solving). The final output is a fine-tuned VLM evaluated on six reasoning benchmarks.

## Method

MINDGYM is a four-stage self-synthesis pipeline that injects structured cognitive priors (P_cog) — adapted from the Thinking-Claude protocol with modular operations like hypothesis testing, multi-angle interpretation, and self-verification — into every generation stage. Stage 1 generates a 150–200-word background passage from a meta-topic; Stage 2 produces up to five logically independent single-hop seed questions with reject sampling (cosine similarity threshold) to enforce semantic diversity; Stage 3 composes these seeds into a multi-hop question using adaptive cognitive operators (Bridging, Comparison, Temporal) while the model verbalizes its full thinking trace; Stage 4 extracts the final schema-aligned (Question, Answer, Rationale) triple. The key insight is that both high mean quality and low quality variance are critical for stable fine-tuning — larger models (32B/38B) produce data with 67.91% lower variance than baselines, and the progressive four-phase training curriculum (from externally guided to autonomous solving) outperforms uniform supervision by 1.33 points. With only 400 text-only Chinese samples and frozen vision encoders, the method achieves up to 16% gains on MathVision, demonstrating that cognitively structured self-challenging data transfers across modalities without any visual supervision.

## Results

Using only 400 synthetic Chinese text samples, MINDGYM achieves the best OVERALL score across all four VLM backbones: 57.84 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (+1.00 over raw), 68.06 on Qwen2.5-VL-32B (+0.48), 64.95 on InternVL-8B (+2.49), and 66.23 on InternVL-38B (+1.07). The headline gain is on MathVision-Mini, where Qwen2.5-VL-7B jumps from 24.67 (raw) to 28.62, a 16% relative improvement, while InternVL-8B improves from 33.22 to 35.53. Data-Juicer analysis shows MINDGYM data achieves +16.7% average quality and 67.91% lower quality variance compared to baselines on Qwen2.5-VL-32B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MINDGYM-Text(400) achieves the highest OVERALL score across all four VLMs, outperforming every baseline. Against OpenO1-sft(400), MINDGYM wins by +0.80 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), +0.48 (Qwen2.5-VL-32B), +0.75 (InternVL-8B), and +0.46 (InternVL-38B) on OVERALL. The advantage is most dramatic against the 4k-sample baselines: OpenO1-sft(4k) collapses to 50.48 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (−6.36 vs. raw) while MINDGYM reaches 57.84, and MMEvol-DvQA(4k) drops to 63.99 on InternVL-38B while MINDGYM scores 66.23. Against LIMO(817), MINDGYM leads by +0.74, +1.81, +1.13, and +1.07 on OVERALL for the four models respectively. On the headline MathVision benchmark, MINDGYM surpasses the strongest baseline (LIMO) by 2.72 points on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (28.62 vs. 25.90) and by 2.63 on InternVL-8B (35.53 vs. 33.88 for MMEvol-SciQA). Notably, MINDGYM achieves these gains with only 400 samples, matching or exceeding baselines trained on 4,000+ samples, demonstrating superior data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

MINDGYM extends the self-instruction data synthesis lineage (Self-Instruct, WizardLM, MMEvol) by embedding structured cognitive priors—breadth, depth, and progression—directly into the generation prompt, drawing on the Thinking-Claude protocol for modular cognitive operations. It departs from prior multimodal synthesis methods (MMInstruct, MMEvol) that suffer from modality inconsistency, and from RL-based or iterative-prompting approaches (RL4F, self-correction) that incur prohibitive compute costs, by treating data quality and variance as first-class design objectives rather than post-hoc filtering criteria. The progressive training pathway (guided answering → reason reconstruction → paired reasoning → autonomous solving) is a novel organizational choice for leveraging the synthesized thinking traces, distinguishing it from uniform SFT on CoT or ToT data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 400 cognitively structured Chinese text samples can outperform 4,000+ samples from stronger baselines across six reasoning benchmarks, and that reducing quality variance is as critical as raising mean quality for stable fine-tuning—a finding with broad implications for data-centric model development. Cross-modal transfer (text-only training improving MathVision and MathVista) suggests that thinking-centric supervision strengthens intrinsic reasoning that generalizes beyond the training modality. Key limitations include the small sample size (400), reliance on a single language (Chinese) for main results, and preliminary-only multimodal synthesis; open questions include whether fully generative image+reasoning pipelines can scale this approach and whether the cognitive-prompt design transfers to domain-specific settings like medicine or finance.
