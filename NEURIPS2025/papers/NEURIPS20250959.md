## Problem

Existing retrieval-augmented image generation methods perform a single, static retrieval of entire reference images before decoding begins, conditioning the whole generation on fixed visual cues. This leads to over-copying of irrelevant details, stylistic bias, and hallucination of unrelated elements because the retrieved references cannot adapt to the evolving generation context. The paper addresses this by asking whether retrieval can be made dynamic, fine-grained, and context-aware at the patch level throughout autoregressive generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250959/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a pre-constructed patch-level retrieval database (built from ~13.6M images from CC12M, JourneyDB, and DataComp, encoded via a quantized autoencoder into 576 patches per image). At each autoregressive step, the already-generated surrounding h-hop patches serve as retrieval queries against database keys (concatenated neighbor embeddings), retrieving top-K patch values. The output is a full generated image produced token-by-token, with retrieval signals integrated at every step rather than once at the start.

## Method

AR-RAG performs k-nearest-neighbor retrieval at the image patch level at every autoregressive generation step, using the surrounding already-generated patches as localized queries to fetch contextually relevant patches from a pre-built database. Two parallel implementations realize this: DAiD is a training-free decoding strategy that constructs a sparse retrieval distribution over the codebook (softmax over l2 distances scaled by temperature τ) and merges it with the model's native output distribution via a weighted average (weight λ); FAiD is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning approach that inserts lightweight modules at every L/b decoder layers, each performing multi-scale convolutional smoothing (2×2 through Q×Q kernels) on retrieved patch features to ensure spatial coherence with the local context, followed by compatibility-scored blending of refined features into the hidden state. FAiD's progressive design forwards each module's output to the next deeper module, enabling iterative refinement as representations evolve through the network. The approach is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on both Janus-Pro (strict left-to-right AR) and Show-o (masked token decoding).

## Results

On GenEval, FAiD on Janus-Pro achieves 0.78 overall score, outperforming the base Janus-Pro (0.71) and all retrieval-augmented baselines (RA-CM3: 0.65, ImageRAG: 0.24, RDM: 0.36). On Midjourney-30K, FAiD reduces FID from 14.33 (Janus-Pro) to 6.67, CMMD from 0.12 to 0.07, and FWD from 28.41 to 9.40. On DPG-Bench, FAiD achieves 79.36 overall versus 77.26 for Janus-Pro and 73.76 for RA-CM3. DAiD, the training-free variant, also improves over the base model on all benchmarks with minimal overhead (0.22% inference time increase).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAiD on Janus-Pro (1.2B params) outperforms the base Janus-Pro (1.0B) by +0.07 on GenEval overall and +2.10 on DPG-Bench, while reducing FID on Midjourney-30K from 14.33 to 6.67. Against image-level retrieval baselines, FAiD substantially outperforms RA-CM3 (0.78 vs. 0.65 GenEval; 79.36 vs. 73.76 DPG-Bench), RDM (0.78 vs. 0.36 GenEval), and ImageRAG (0.78 vs. 0.24 GenEval), demonstrating that patch-level autoregressive retrieval avoids the over-copying and stylistic bias problems of static image-level retrieval. Notably, the 1.2B FAiD model matches or exceeds much larger non-retrieval models: it surpasses DALL-E 3 (0.67), FLUX.1-dev (0.67), and SDXL (0.55) on GenEval overall. On T2I-Bench, FAiD improves Numeracy from 0.251 (Janus-Pro) to 0.537 and Color from 0.652 to 0.703, though RDM (diffusion-based) retains a slight edge on Texture (0.538 vs. 0.483), reflecting the inherent information loss in discrete tokenization. DAiD, the training-free variant, provides consistent but smaller gains (e.g., +0.01 GenEval, +0.62 DPG-Bench) with only 0.22% inference overhead, while FAiD adds 36% overhead for its feature blending operations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) from the NLP domain into image generation, but departs fundamentally from prior image-level RAG methods (RDM, Re-Imagen, KNN-Diffusion, ImageRAG, RA-CM3) that perform a single static retrieval of entire reference images before decoding begins. Instead, AR-RAG performs fine-grained, step-wise k-NN retrieval at the patch level, using already-generated surrounding patches as dynamic queries rather than the text prompt. The two implementations—DAiD (training-free distribution merging) and FAiD (parameter-efficient fine-tuning with multi-scale convolution and compatibility-based blending)—are built on autoregressive discrete-token backbones (Janus-Pro, Show-o), positioning the work at the intersection of VQ-VAE tokenization, transformer-based generation, and retrieval-augmented decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that prior image-level retrieval methods (RDM, ImageRAG) actually degrade performance below their non-retrieval baselines on GenEval and DPG-Bench, providing strong empirical motivation for the patch-level approach. AR-RAG's ability to selectively incorporate relevant visual elements while avoiding over-copying of irrelevant context addresses a practical failure mode that limits the adoption of retrieval in image generation. Key limitations include restriction to discrete-token autoregressive models (not continuous diffusion), a retrieval database of ~13M images (far below billion-scale), and 2D-only scope, leaving open whether the patch-level retrieval principle transfers to latent diffusion or 3D generation. The training-free DAiD variant with only 0.22% inference overhead makes the technique immediately deployable on existing autoregressive image generators.
