## Problem

State-of-the-art video-to-audio generation methods rely on classifier-free guidance (CFG), which randomly drops conditioning signals during training to simulate both conditional and unconditional objectives. This multi-task setup can dilute model capacity and cause mismatched sampling behavior at inference. The paper asks whether a model-guided training objective—where the generative model supervises itself—can replace CFG during training while still achieving (or exceeding) the same generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251216/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a silent video clip of ~8 seconds (L_V frames at 224×224 RGB) and produces a corresponding audio waveform. Internally, the video is encoded by a CAVP video encoder into frame-level embeddings that are temporally aggregated into a single global conditioning vector (1×768). The audio target is a 64×816 log-mel spectrogram, mapped to an 8×16×204 latent via a pretrained AudioLDM VAE, patchified into 816 tokens of dimension 768 for the Transformer. At inference, the denoised latent is decoded back to a mel-spectrogram and a neural vocoder reconstructs the waveform.

## Method

MGAudio comprises three components: (1) a Flow-Based Denoising Transformer (FBDT) built on the SiT architecture that learns a conditional flow field via flow matching; (2) a Dual-Role Audio-Visual Encoder (DRAVE) that uses the CAVP video encoder for conditioning (injected via AdaLN) and the CAVP audio encoder for intermediate representation alignment (a REPA-style cosine-similarity loss between clean CAVP audio features and noisy transformer intermediates); and (3) an Audio Model-Guidance (AMG) objective that replaces CFG training with a self-distilled target u′ = u + w·sg(u_θ(x_t, v, t) − u_θ(x_t, ∅, t)), where the stop-gradient and EMA of the online model stabilize the guidance term. A key empirical finding is that AMG-trained models still benefit from CFG at inference (unlike the vision domain), yielding a FAD of 0.40 on VGGSound with only 131M parameters—substantially outperforming CFG-trained baselines of similar or larger size.

## Results

On VGGSound, MGAudio (131M params, CFG=1.45) achieves a state-of-the-art FAD of 0.40, substantially outperforming the second-best MMAudio (FAD 0.71) and all other baselines. It also attains the best FD (6.16) and IS (12.82) among all methods, with an alignment accuracy of 95.65%. On the UnAV-100 generalization benchmark, MGAudio leads with FAD 0.54, FD 5.40, and IS 13.90, while maintaining strong alignment accuracy (97.54%). Notably, training on only 10% of VGGSound still yields FAD 0.73, surpassing full-data baselines like FRIEREN and MDSGen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGAudio decisively outperforms all CFG-trained baselines on the primary FAD metric: 0.40 vs. MMAudio (0.71), FRIEREN (1.38), MDSGen (1.40), Diff-Foley (6.25), and See and Hear (5.51). Against the closest competitor MMAudio (157M params), MGAudio (131M params) wins on FAD (0.40 vs. 0.71), FD (6.16 vs. 6.97), IS (12.82 vs. 11.09), and alignment accuracy (95.65% vs. 92.28%), though MMAudio achieves a lower KL divergence (2.07 vs. 2.76). Compared to FRIEREN, MGAudio improves FAD by 3.4× and FD by 2×, but FRIEREN edges ahead on alignment accuracy (97.25% vs. 95.65%) and inference speed (0.20s vs. 0.31s). Against the SiT baseline with CFG (FAD 1.52), MGAudio achieves a 3.8× FAD reduction, confirming the benefit of model-guided training over standard CFG training. On UnAV-100, MGAudio leads all methods on FAD (0.54), FD (5.40), and IS (13.90), while FRIEREN maintains a slight alignment accuracy advantage (98.14% vs. 97.54%) and MMAudio achieves the lowest KL (1.62 vs. 2.00). The ablation in Table 4 further shows that MG alone (FAD 2.13) underperforms CFG (FAD 1.52), but combining MG training with CFG at inference (FAD 1.19) and adding dual-role alignment (FAD 1.14) yields the best results, demonstrating the complementary nature of the two mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

MGAudio extends the Model-Guidance (MG) framework of Tang et al. [16], originally designed for discrete class-conditional image generation on ImageNet, to the continuous video-conditioned audio domain. It builds on the flow-matching Transformer backbone of SiT [35] and the CAVP contrastive audio-visual pretraining from Diff-Foley [9], while drawing on REPA [25] for intermediate feature alignment. The key departure from prior V2A methods (FRIEREN, MDSGen, MMAudio) is replacing the standard classifier-free guidance training objective with a self-distilled AMG loss and introducing a dual-role encoder that simultaneously conditions the denoiser and aligns intermediate audio representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that AMG training with only 10% of VGGSound outperforms full-data CFG baselines suggests a practical path toward data-efficient audio generation, particularly relevant as text-to-video models produce ever more silent footage needing Foley. The modality-specific observation that AMG alone underperforms CFG in audio (unlike vision) while AMG+CFG at inference yields the best results opens questions about whether audio's temporal sensitivity fundamentally requires dual supervision. Limitations include weak performance on human vocalizations and reliance on iterative sampling, pointing toward future work on phonetic-aware generation and acceleration via consistency models or distillation.
