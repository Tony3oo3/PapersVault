## Problem

In-context Ranking (ICR) with LLMs processes a query and all candidate documents in a single prompt, but the quadratic attention complexity makes inference prohibitively expensive as the candidate list grows. Prior listwise re-rankers treat the LLM as a black box, failing to exploit the inherent structure of the ICR task where documents are largely independent and conditioned on a shared instruction, and rely on autoregressive decoding that is poorly calibrated for producing diverse ranked lists.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query and a list of N candidate documents (N = 10–500 in experiments) formatted as a single prompt with a query-conditioned instruction prefix, structured document segments (ID + content + END markers), and a trailing query section. Output is produced in two modes: (1) autoregressive decoding of document identifiers, or (2) direct attention-based inference where relevance scores S(q, d_k) are read from signal-carrier query tokens at a fixed middle layer (l* = 20), bypassing iterative decoding entirely. The attention-based mode scales to ~500 documents (~100K context) within a second on TPU hardware.

## Method

BlockRank introduces two architectural modifications to a fine-tuned Mistral-7B. First, it enforces structured sparse attention: document tokens attend only causally within their own document chunk and to the instruction chunk (not to other documents), while query tokens attend to the full prompt, reducing per-layer attention complexity from O(N²·L²·d) to O(N·L²·d) where N is the number of documents and L is the chunk length. Second, it adds an auxiliary InfoNCE contrastive loss at a middle layer (l* = 20) that explicitly trains specific 'signal-carrier' query tokens (delimiters like ':' and '[[') to concentrate attention mass on the ground-truth relevant document over all candidates. A permutation-invariant position embedding assigns all documents the same local position space (starting after the instruction) and places query tokens at a large fixed offset (8192), decoupling document representations from their list position. The combined training objective is L_NTP + 0.1·L_aux, and the optimized attention scores enable a single-pass inference mode that reads relevance directly without autoregressive generation.

## Results

On MSMarco (in-domain, N=100), BlockRank Mistral achieves P@1 of 76.2 and MRR@10 of 42.0, outperforming the Full-FT Mistral baseline (75.5 P@1, 38.3 MRR@10) and all traditional retrieval baselines including monoT5-XL (41.2 MRR@10) and ColBERTv2 (39.7 MRR@10). On the BEIR zero-shot generalization benchmark (top-100 Contriever candidates), BlockRank Mistral achieves an average nDCG@10 of 54.8, surpassing the prior SOTA listwise ranker FIRST (54.3), RankZephyr (53.7), and RankVicuna (50.7). At N=100 documents, BlockRank is approximately 4.7× faster than Full-FT at inference, and scales to ~500 documents (~100K context) within 1.15 seconds while maintaining P@1 of 28.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlockRank Mistral outperforms the directly comparable Full-FT Mistral baseline by +0.7 P@1 and +3.7 MRR@10 on MSMarco, with the MRR gap indicating substantially better calibration across the full ranked list. Against traditional retrieval baselines, BlockRank's 42.0 MRR@10 exceeds monoT5-XL (41.2), ColBERTv2 (39.7), GTR-XXL (38.8), monoBERT (38.2), and Sentence-transformer (37.2), while its 76.2 P@1 far surpasses BM25 (29.7) and Sentence-transformer (58.8). In the zero-shot BEIR generalization setting, BlockRank (54.8 avg nDCG@10) beats the prior SOTA FIRST (54.3), RankZephyr (53.7), and RankVicuna (50.7), as well as the cross-encoder baseline (50.7) and the Contriever retrieval-only baseline (45.9). Among zero-shot LLMs, BlockRank's fine-tuned P@1 of 76.2 substantially exceeds Gemini-2.0-flash (65.1) and Mistral-7B-instruct (43.5) on MSMarco, demonstrating that task-specific fine-tuning with structured attention yields large gains over prompting alone. The key efficiency advantage is that BlockRank processes all 100 candidates in a single forward pass with linear attention complexity, whereas competing listwise rankers (FIRST, RankZephyr) require multiple sliding-window passes or auto-regressive decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the In-context Ranking (ICR) paradigm introduced by Lee et al. (2024), which demonstrated that long-context LLMs can perform retrieval by processing candidate documents in a single prompt, and on the listwise LLM re-ranking line of work (RankVicuna, RankZephyr, FIRST) that fine-tunes open-source LLMs for this task. It departs from these methods by treating the LLM's attention mechanism as a first-class architectural object rather than a black box: it identifies task-specific sparsity in attention (documents need not attend to each other) and query-token retrieval signals in middle layers (building on retrieval-head findings from Wu et al. 2024 and Chen et al. 2025), then enforces the sparsity architecturally and optimizes the signals via a contrastive auxiliary loss. The structured sparse attention draws conceptually from Longformer/BigBird-style efficient attention but is semantically informed by the ICR prompt structure rather than generic sequence-length heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.7× inference speedup at N=100 and linear (vs. quadratic) scaling to ~500 documents within a second make LLM-based reranking practically deployable in retrieval pipelines where latency is critical, and the attention-based inference path eliminates autoregressive decoding entirely. The finding that inter-document attention is largely dispensable for ICR is a useful architectural insight that could inform future efficient LLM designs for listwise tasks. Key limitations include demonstration on a single model family (Mistral-7B), reliance on a specific prompt format with query in the prefix, and unverified robustness of the learned attention signals across highly diverse task distributions. Open questions include whether the structured attention assumption holds when documents are topically interdependent, and whether the approach transfers to other base architectures or multi-modal settings.
