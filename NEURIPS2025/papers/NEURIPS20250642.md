## Problem

Prior work (DEER, DeepPCR) showed that nonlinear state space models can be parallelized by recasting sequential evaluation as a Gauss-Newton optimization problem, but the factors governing how many optimization steps are needed remained unclear. This paper asks: what property of the underlying dynamics determines whether the resulting optimization problem is well-conditioned and thus amenable to efficient parallelization?

## Input / Output

The method takes a D-dimensional nonlinear state space model defined by a sequence of functions f_1,...,f_T, an initial state s_0, and sequence length T, and produces the full state trajectory (s_1,...,s_T) by minimizing a residual-based merit function. Each Gauss-Newton step solves a linear recursion with block bidiagonal Jacobian structure in O(log T) time via parallel associative scan, so total cost is O(log T) per step times the number of steps. The theoretical output is a bound on the Polyak-Lojasiewicz constant μ of the merit function, which directly predicts the number of optimization steps required.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical characterization linking the Largest Lyapunov Exponent (LLE) λ of the dynamics to the conditioning of the merit function. Theorem 2 proves that the PL constant μ satisfies bounds that degrade exponentially with T when λ > 0 (unpredictable/chaotic) but remain bounded away from zero when λ < 0 (predictable/contracting), yielding O((log T)²) total parallel evaluation time for predictable systems. The proof exploits the nilpotent structure J = I − N where N collects Jacobian products along subdiagonals, so that J⁻¹ = Σ N^k and the norms of N^k are governed by Jacobian product growth. Theorem 4 establishes global linear convergence of DEER (Gauss-Newton) by exploiting the lower block-triangular structure of J and J⁻¹, and Theorem 5 characterizes the basin of quadratic convergence as a function of both the LLE and the Lipschitz constant of the dynamics Jacobian.

## Results

The paper establishes that for predictable nonlinear state space models (LLE λ < 0), DEER (Gauss-Newton) converges in O((log T)²) total computational time, where T is the sequence length, versus O(T) for sequential evaluation. On the dysts benchmark (T=30,000), chaotic systems require 697–30,000 DEER steps to converge, while their stable observers (all with negative LLE) require only 2–14 steps—a reduction of 3–4 orders of magnitude. Experiments on a parametric family of RNNs confirm a sharp threshold in convergence steps at λ=0, precisely matching the theoretical prediction from Theorem 2 that the PL constant degrades exponentially with T when λ > 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method baselines; the central comparison is between predictable (λ < 0) and unpredictable (λ > 0) dynamics under the same DEER optimizer. On the dysts benchmark, all nine chaotic systems (positive LLE) hit the 30,000-step cap or require thousands of steps, while their corresponding stable observers (negative LLE) converge in 2–14 steps, confirming the theory that predictability is the determining factor for parallelizability. In the RNN family experiments (Figure 2), DEER shows a sharp threshold: for λ < 0 the number of steps grows sublinearly with T, while for λ > 0 it grows exponentially, matching the theoretical PL constant bound from Theorem 2. The paper also notes that prior DEER/DeepPCR work [1,2,3] did not establish global convergence guarantees or connect conditioning to system dynamics, whereas this paper proves global linear convergence (Theorem 4) and characterizes the basin of quadratic convergence in terms of LLE and Jacobian Lipschitzness (Theorem 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DEER/DeepPCR line of work (Lim et al. 2024; Danieli et al. 2023; Gonzalez et al. 2024), which recast sequential nonlinear state evaluation as a parallelizable Gauss-Newton optimization problem, by providing the first precise theoretical characterization of when this parallelization is actually efficient. The key conceptual departure is importing the Largest Lyapunov Exponent from nonlinear dynamical systems theory (contraction analysis, Lyapunov exponent methods) to bound the Polyak-Lojasiewicz constant of the merit function, thereby linking dynamical predictability directly to optimization conditioning. The proof machinery also reveals a structural connection to Mamba2's semiseparable matrices and to classical parallel-in-time methods (multiple shooting, MGRIT), positioning the work at the intersection of dynamical systems, optimization, and parallel computing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, a priori criterion (negative LLE) for determining whether optimization-based parallelization of a given nonlinear SSM will outperform sequential evaluation, which is critical as SSMs (Mamba, LrcSSM, Pararmn) scale to large language models. It also yields a concrete architecture design principle: parameterizing models to be contractive guarantees parallelizability throughout training. Open questions include extending the theory to quasi-Newton methods (which trade memory for more steps), characterizing predictability for MCMC chains to justify parallel MCMC, and connecting the LLE-based conditioning analysis to the CFL-number framework used in parallel-in-time PDE solvers. A practical limitation is DEER's significant memory footprint, which quasi-Newton variants alleviate at the cost of more iterations.
