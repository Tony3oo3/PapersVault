## Problem

Prior non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for BFGS require strong convexity, Lipschitz-continuous gradients, and Lipschitz-continuous Hessians—assumptions that fail for common objectives like logistic regression and log-barrier functions. Moreover, all previously established rates depend on condition-number-type parameters (μ, L, K) that change under coordinate transformations, contradicting BFGS's inherent affine invariance. This paper closes both gaps by deriving global convergence rates under the strictly weaker assumption of strong self-concordance, with rates that are fully affine-invariant.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a twice-differentiable, strictly convex, M-strongly self-concordant objective f: R^d → R, an arbitrary initial point x_0 ∈ R^d, and an arbitrary positive-definite initial Hessian approximation B_0 ∈ S_{++}^d, with step sizes selected via the Armijo-Wolfe line search (parameters α, β). It produces explicit global linear and superlinear convergence rate bounds of the form (1 - c/(1+D_0)²)^t and (C/t)^t, where D_0 = 2ω⁻¹(M²(f(x_0)-f(x_*))/4) encodes initial suboptimality and the strongly self-concordant constant M, and C depends on Ψ(B_0) (a trace-log-det measure of initial Hessian quality). All rate expressions are invariant under linear reparameterizations of the variables.

## Method

The key technical mechanism replaces the uniform bounds available under strong convexity and Lipschitz Hessian with a reweighting strategy: by conjugating all quantities with P = (1+D_0)∇²f(x_*)^{1/2}, the analysis shows ‖ŷ_t‖²/ŝ_t^T ŷ_t ≤ 1 under strong self-concordance alone, and establishes the time-dependent lower bound ‖g_t‖²/(f(x_t)-f(x_*)) ≥ 1/(1+D_t) in place of the uniform 2μ bound. Suboptimality is measured via the self-concordant potential ω(x) = x - log(x+1) and its inverse, which naturally captures the geometry of self-concordant functions. For superlinear convergence, the paper proves that the unit step size η_t = 1 satisfies the Wolfe conditions once C_t ≤ δ_1 and ρ_t ∈ [δ_2, δ_3], and shows the latter condition is violated for only finitely many iterations (bounded by a constant times Ψ(B̃_0) + D_0(Ψ(B̄_0) + (1+D_0)²)), yielding the (C/t)^t rate. The entire analysis is conducted in the weighted norm induced by ∇²f(x_*), ensuring all constants are affine-invariant and depend only on M, D_0, and the initial Hessian quality.

## Results

The paper establishes global linear convergence of BFGS at rate (1 − α(1−β)/(1+D₀)²)ᵗ for strictly convex, strongly self-concordant functions, and global superlinear convergence at rate (C/t)ᵗ where C depends on the initial suboptimality D₀ and the initial Hessian approximation B₀. Numerical experiments on the hard cubic function and logistic regression (d = 100 and d = 1000) confirm that BFGS initially lags behind gradient descent (GD) and accelerated gradient descent (AGD) but significantly outperforms both after approximately d iterations. Under an ill-conditioned linear transformation, BFGS maintains identical convergence behavior (affine invariance) while GD and AGD degrade substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares BFGS (with B₀ = I and B₀ = cI) against gradient descent (GD) and accelerated gradient descent (AGD) on two test problems: the hard cubic function and logistic regression, at dimensions d = 100 and d = 1000. On the hard cubic function, BFGS with both initializations performs nearly identically and overtakes GD and AGD after roughly d iterations, achieving substantially lower suboptimality. On logistic regression, BFGS with B₀ = cI outperforms BFGS with B₀ = I, and both BFGS variants surpass first-order methods after a short transient. Under an ill-conditioned transformation matrix A, BFGS convergence is unchanged (affine invariance), while GD and AGD degrade significantly, confirming that first-order methods lack this property. Theoretically, the paper's framework removes the strong convexity, Lipschitz gradient, and Lipschitz Hessian assumptions required by prior non-asymptotic BFGS analyses [38, 41, 42], replacing them with the weaker and affine-invariant strong self-concordance condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-asymptotic global convergence analysis of BFGS established by Jin, Jiang, and Mokhtari [41, 42] and Jin and Mokhtari [38], which required strong convexity plus Lipschitz continuity of the gradient and Hessian. The key departure is replacing these assumptions with strict convexity and strong self-concordance, a strictly weaker condition that covers important examples like logistic regression and log-barrier functions where prior analyses do not apply. The proof technique is fundamentally different: rather than bounding ‖y_t‖²/s_t^T y_t by a Lipschitz constant and ‖g_t‖²/(f(x_t)−f(x_*)) by a strong-convexity constant, the authors work in a weighted norm induced by the Hessian at the optimum and exploit self-concordance inequalities to obtain time-dependent bounds that still yield linear and superlinear rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first set of non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for BFGS that are affine-invariant, meaning the rates depend only on the strongly self-concordant parameter (which is invariant under linear coordinate changes) rather than on condition numbers that vary with the chosen basis. This aligns the theory with the well-known affine invariance of BFGS itself, closing a gap where prior rates were inconsistent with the method's structural properties. A key limitation is the reliance on strong self-concordance rather than standard self-concordance; extending the results to the broader self-concordant class (e.g., without the uniform Hessian-difference bound) is identified as an open direction. For practitioners, the practical impact is moderate: BFGS remains less commonly deployed than first-order methods in large-scale deep learning, but the results strengthen the theoretical foundation for quasi-Newton methods in settings where strong convexity is absent.
