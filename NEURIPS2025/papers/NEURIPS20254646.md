## Problem

Instruction-based image editing faces a fundamental precision-efficiency tradeoff: fine-tuning-based methods require massive datasets (450K–10M pairs) and architectural modifications to bridge the gap between descriptive prompt spaces and editing instruction spaces, while training-free approaches struggle with complex instructions and layout preservation. The paper asks whether the inherent generation fidelity and contextual awareness of large-scale Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) can be directly leveraged for instruction-based editing without external complexity, achieving state-of-the-art results with a fraction of the training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image and a natural-language editing instruction, which are combined into a 512×1024 diptych (source on the left, blank region on the right) with a fixed binary mask covering the right half. The instruction is embedded in a structured in-context prompt ('A diptych with two side-by-side images of the same scene. On the right, the scene is exactly the same as on the left but {instruction}') rather than fed as a raw command. The output is the edited image extracted from the right panel of the generated diptych, produced via 50-step rectified-flow denoising with a guidance scale of 50.

## Method

ICEdit exploits the diptych structure and bidirectional attention of FLUX.1 Fill (a 12B inpainting DiT) to perform editing in-context: the left panel reconstructs the source image while the right panel generates the edit, requiring no position or condition encoders beyond the base architecture. To overcome the model's lack of learned image-to-image editing priors, the paper applies minimal parameter-efficient fine-tuning (0.2B trainable parameters, 50K samples) using a Mixture-of-Experts LoRA design—four rank-32 LoRA experts in the multi-modal attention output projection with TopK=1 sparse routing—outperforming a single LoRA by 13% in GPT-based editing success. At inference time, an Early Filter Inference-Time Scaling strategy samples 6 noise candidates, runs only 10 denoising steps for each, uses Qwen2.5-VL 72B to pairwise-rank the preliminary outputs for instruction compliance, and then refines the best candidate with full 50-step denoising, yielding a 16% boost in overall VIE-Score at modest additional compute (110 NFE vs. 50).

## Results

On the Emu Edit test set, ICEdit achieves the best CLIP-I (0.907) and DINO (0.866) scores among all compared methods, with a GPT-4o score of 0.68, while using only 0.05M training samples and 0.2B trainable parameters (vs. 10M samples and 2.8B params for EmuEdit). On MagicBrush, ICEdit attains the lowest L1 (0.060) and highest CLIP-I (0.928) and DINO (0.853) scores. In VIE-Score evaluation, ICEdit reaches 78.2 overall, surpassing the closed-source SeedEdit (75.7) after applying the Early Filter Inference-Time Scaling strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICEdit outperforms all eight baselines on both CLIP-I and DINO (source-image preservation) on the Emu Edit test set, with the largest margins over FLUX.1 Fill (+0.113 CLIP-I, +0.207 DINO) and ACE++ (+0.116 CLIP-I, +0.179 DINO). On GPT-4o editing success, ICEdit (0.68) trails only the closed-source EmuEdit (0.72) but does so with 200× less training data (0.05M vs. 10M) and 14× fewer trainable parameters (0.2B vs. 2.8B). On MagicBrush, ICEdit achieves the best L1 (0.060), CLIP-I (0.928), and DINO (0.853), beating the closest competitor UltraEdit (L1 0.066, CLIP-I 0.904) and MagicBrush itself (L1 0.074, CLIP-I 0.908). Against DiT-based baselines specifically—FluxEdit, FLUX.1 Fill, RF-Solver Edit, and ACE++—ICEdit leads by 0.09–0.12 on CLIP-I and 0.11–0.21 on DINO, demonstrating that the in-context editing paradigm with minimal fine-tuning substantially surpasses both training-free and heavily fine-tuned DiT approaches. In VIE-Score (human-preference-aligned metric), ICEdit reaches 78.2 overall, exceeding SeedEdit's 75.7, though SeedEdit retains a slight edge in perceptual quality (PQ) while ICEdit excels in source-content preservation (SC).

## Relation to Prior Work

ICEdit carves new ground by repurposing the in-context generation capability of large-scale DiTs (specifically FLUX.1 Fill) for instruction-based image editing, a task previously addressed either through massive fine-tuning on 450K–10M editing pairs (InstructPix2Pix, Emu Edit, UltraEdit) or through training-free inversion/attention manipulation (RF-Solver, StableFlow) that struggles with complex instructions. The diptych framing—source image on the left, edited output on the right—borrows from in-context generation work on reference-guided synthesis (OminiControl, In-Context LoRA) but applies it to editing with a fixed inpainting mask rather than architectural modifications. The Mixture-of-LoRAs module draws on MoE architectures from LLMs (Switch Transformers, Mixtral), and the VLM-based inference-time scaling extends recent work on golden noise and inference-time scaling for diffusion models, but the specific combination of in-context editing paradigm + minimal MoE-LoRA fine-tuning + early-filter VLM selection is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.1% data efficiency (50K vs. 10M samples) and 1% trainable parameters (0.2B vs. 12B) establish a compelling efficiency-precision tradeoff that could make high-quality instruction-based editing accessible to smaller research groups and production systems with limited compute. However, open questions remain: the method struggles with spatial relocation (object movement) and polysemous instructions due to T5's limited semantic disambiguation, and the inference-time scaling relies on a 72B VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) that is itself computationally expensive, partially offsetting the training efficiency gains. Whether the in-context editing paradigm generalizes beyond the diptych format to more complex multi-region or sequential editing workflows remains unexplored.
