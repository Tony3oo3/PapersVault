## Problem

Wasserstein gradient flow (WGF) is the standard method for optimizing functionals over probability measures, but for non-convex objectives it can converge to saddle points rather than second-order optimal solutions. While perturbed gradient descent methods have been well-studied in finite-dimensional Euclidean space, no algorithm with polynomial-time guarantees for second-order optimality existed in the infinite-dimensional Wasserstein space. This paper asks whether a perturbative modification of WGF can efficiently escape saddle points in measure space.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an initial probability measure μ₀ ∈ P₂(ℝᵈ) and a sufficiently smooth objective functional F whose Wasserstein gradient ∇_μ F and Hessian ∇²_μ F are available. It outputs a probability measure that is an (ε,δ)-second-order stationary point (i.e., ‖∇_μ F‖_{L²(μ)} ≤ ε and λ_min H_μ ≥ −δ), or for strictly benign objectives, a measure within W₂-distance α of a global optimum. The Hessian H_μ is an integral operator on L²(μ)ᵈ, and the perturbation step requires sampling a Gaussian process with a measure-dependent kernel, which in practice is approximated via N-particle ensembles.

## Method

PWGF alternates between standard WGF evolution and Hessian-guided perturbations: when the Wasserstein gradient is small (indicating proximity to a stationary point), the measure is pushed forward along a random vector field ξ ~ GP(0, K_μ) where the kernel K_μ(x,y) = ∫ ∇²_μ F(μ,x,z)∇²_μ F(μ,z,y) μ(dz) is the squared Wasserstein Hessian operator H_μ². This construction is the key insight: unlike isotropic noise, the Hessian-derived kernel 'directs' the perturbation toward the smallest-eigenvalue (unstable) direction of H_μ, guaranteeing objective decrease with high probability. Saddle detection is handled practically by checking whether the objective drops by a threshold F_thres after a fixed WGF evaluation window following each perturbation. The main theorem establishes that PWGF reaches an (ε,δ)-second-order stationary point in Õ(ΔF(1/ε² + 1/δ⁴)) time with probability 1−ζ, and for strictly benign objectives this implies polynomial-time global convergence.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5.2) establishes that PWGF reaches an (ε, δ)-second-order stationary point in Õ(ΔF(1/ε² + 1/δ⁴)) time steps with probability 1−ζ, where ΔF is the initial objective gap. The discrete-time version (Theorem 5.5) achieves the same Õ(ΔF(1/ε² + 1/δ⁴)) step complexity. For strictly benign objectives (Corollary 5.6), PWGF converges to an α-close global optimum in the same polynomial time. The key hyperparameters scale as η_p = Õ(δ^{3/2} ∧ δ³/ε), T_thres = Õ(1/δ), and F_thres = Õ(δ³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baseline comparisons; all results are stated as convergence theorems. The paper improves upon three prior approaches in a theoretical sense: (1) standard WGF (Jordan et al., 1998) only guarantees convergence to a first-order stationary point and can get stuck at saddle points, whereas PWGF guarantees second-order optimality; (2) the isotropic Gaussian process perturbation of Kim & Suzuki (2024) was conjectured to help escape saddles but lacked theoretical guarantees, while PWGF's Hessian-directed kernel K_μ = H_μ² provides a provable escape mechanism (Proposition 5.4); (3) finite-dimensional perturbed gradient descent (Jin et al., 2017; Li, 2019) achieves analogous Õ(1/ε² + 1/δ⁴) complexity in Euclidean space, and PWGF extends this to the infinite-dimensional Wasserstein space using Gaussian process theory and optimal transport geometry. No numerical benchmarks against these baselines are reported in the main text; toy simulations in Appendix F illustrate the algorithm qualitatively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the perturbed gradient descent (PGD) framework of Jin et al. (2017) and Ge et al. (2015) from finite-dimensional Euclidean space to the infinite-dimensional Wasserstein space of probability measures. It builds directly on the Wasserstein gradient flow (WGF) of Jordan et al. (1998) as the base dynamics, and improves upon Kim & Suzuki (2024), who proposed isotropic Gaussian process perturbations for WGF without theoretical guarantees. The key departure is constructing the GP kernel from the Wasserstein Hessian (K_μ = H_μ²), which 'directs' the perturbation toward the smallest-eigenvalue direction of the Hessian operator rather than exploring all directions uniformly, thereby providing the first rigorous saddle-escape guarantee in measure space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence guarantee for second-order optimality in non-convex probability measure optimization, closing a significant gap: WGF was known to converge only to first-order stationary points, and the non-convex regime (e.g., 3+ layer networks, MMD) had no rigorous escape-from-saddles theory. The strict benignity corollary gives polynomial-time global convergence for important problems like matrix factorization and 3-layer network training. Key limitations include the need to compute the full Wasserstein Hessian (an integral operator), strong Lipschitz smoothness assumptions on the Hessian, and the fact that experiments are limited to toy simulations. Open questions include stochastic Hessian approximations for scalability and broader benignity characterizations for distributional objectives.
