## Problem

Joint-embedding SSL methods face a fundamental trade-off: representations optimized for invariance (e.g., classification) lose fine-grained transformation information needed for equivariant tasks, and vice versa. Existing equivariant SSL approaches resolve this by adding explicit equivariance losses or dual predictors, but these impose rigid objectives that limit flexibility. The paper asks whether a single predictive architecture can learn both invariant and equivariant representations simultaneously without any equivariance-specific loss terms.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a short sequence of M+1 image views (e.g., 128×128 crops from 3DIEBench, 32×32 foveated patches from STL-10, or augmented 32×32/64×64 images) along with M relative transformation vectors (actions) between consecutive views. Actions are encoded via a learnable linear projector (default 128-d) and can represent 3D rotations (quaternions), augmentation parameters (crop coordinates, color jitter, blur sigma), or 2D saccade offsets. The model produces two architecturally distinct outputs: per-view encoder representations that are equivariant to the transformation group, and a single aggregate representation (from a learnable [AGG] token) that is invariant and used for downstream classification. The predictor additionally outputs a predicted latent representation of the next view in the sequence.

## Method

seq-JEPA encodes each view with a shared ResNet-18 backbone, concatenates each encoded view with its corresponding action embedding, and passes the resulting sequence through a 3-layer, 4-head transformer encoder that outputs an aggregate representation via a learnable [AGG] token. This aggregate is concatenated with the final action and fed to a 2-layer MLP predictor that predicts the next view's representation, which is compared against an EMA target encoder's output using a cosine-similarity loss. No equivariance loss, dual predictor, or explicit transformation-decomposition objective is used; instead, action conditioning in both the transformer and predictor implicitly forces the encoder to preserve transformation structure (equivariance), while the transformer's sequence aggregation naturally filters out transformation-specific variability (invariance). This emergent architectural disentanglement means the same backbone simultaneously serves equivariant tasks (via individual view representations) and invariant tasks (via the aggregate token), and the autoregressive formulation extends naturally to path integration over action sequences.

## Results

On 3DIEBench, seq-JEPA (M_tr=3, M_val=5) achieves 87.41% top-1 classification accuracy and 0.71 R² for relative rotation prediction, simultaneously outperforming the best invariant method (BYOL: 82.90% acc, 0.12 R²) and the best equivariant method (ContextSSL: 80.40% acc, 0.74 R²). On STL-10 via predictive learning across saccades (no augmentations), seq-JEPA reaches 83.44% top-1 accuracy with 0.80 R² positional equivariance, approaching SimCLR's 85.23% which uses full-resolution images with strong augmentations. Ablating action conditioning drops equivariance R² from 0.71 to 0.29 while preserving classification accuracy, confirming the architectural disentanglement of invariance and equivariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

seq-JEPA (3,5) dominates all eight baselines on 3DIEBench: it exceeds the best invariant method BYOL by 4.51 points in classification (87.41% vs 82.90%) and by 0.59 in rotation R² (0.71 vs 0.12). Against equivariant baselines, it outperforms SIE by 9.92 points in classification (87.41% vs 77.49%) and by 0.13 in rotation R² (0.71 vs 0.58), and beats EquiMod by 3.12 points in classification (87.41% vs 84.29%) and by 0.39 in rotation R² (0.71 vs 0.32). The only near-tie is with ContextSSL on rotation R² (0.71 vs 0.74), where seq-JEPA still leads by 7.01 points in classification (87.41% vs 80.40%). On CIFAR-100 with hand-crafted augmentations, seq-JEPA achieves the highest equivariance R² across all three transformations (crop 0.79, jitter 0.64, blur 0.92) while remaining competitive in classification (58–60%). On STL-10 saccades, seq-JEPA (83.44%) trails SimCLR with full augmentations (85.23%) by 1.79 points but matches Conv-JEPA's positional equivariance (0.80 R²) while surpassing it in classification (83.44% vs 80.04%).

## Relation to Prior Work

seq-JEPA extends the joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) framework of LeCun (2022) and I-JEPA (Assran et al., 2023) by replacing the single masked-view prediction with autoregressive prediction over sequences of action-observation pairs. It departs from the dominant two-view SSL paradigm (BYOL, SimCLR, VICReg) and from equivariant SSL methods (SIE, EquiMod, SEN, ContextSSL) that rely on explicit equivariance loss terms or dual predictors. The key conceptual departure is that equivariance emerges from action-conditioned sequential aggregation through a transformer encoder, rather than being enforced by a dedicated objective, while invariance emerges from the aggregate [AGG] token representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented invariance-equivariance trade-off in SSL with a clean architectural solution that requires no additional loss terms, making it a potentially simpler and more general recipe for representation learning. The emergent dual-structure (equivariant encoder outputs + invariant aggregate) also enables sequential tasks like path integration and predictive learning across saccades without hand-crafted augmentations. Key limitations include poor OOD generalization (all methods fail on unseen rotation ranges), dependence on known transformation groups, and validation only on small-to-medium datasets (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, STL-10, 3DIEBench); scaling to ImageNet-scale or video/multi-modal settings remains open.
