## Problem

Existing adversarial patch attack methods simplify the inherently combinatorial optimization by addressing patch factors—shape, location, number, and content—independently, typically fixing shapes to basic geometric forms and optimizing only one or two factors at a time. This decoupling severely restricts the exploitable solution space and limits both attack effectiveness and physical applicability. The paper tackles the open problem of jointly optimizing all critical patch factors simultaneously under a black-box, gradient-free setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252999/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image, a score-based black-box target model (providing output probabilities), the true label, and hyperparameters controlling patch count (k), mini-patch count (n), and iteration budgets. It produces an adversarial example constructed by compositing an irregular multi-patch binary mask with a continuous RGB perturbation via element-wise blending. The mask is encoded at 4×4 block granularity (reducing a 224×224 mask to 56×56), and the perturbation is initially defined per-block before pixel-level refinement, yielding locally connected, physically printable irregular patches rather than fixed rectangles or crosses.

## Method

IMPACT is a two-phase, gradient-free framework built on evolutionary algorithms. Phase 1 uses Differential Evolution to jointly optimize a binary mask encoding (each bit representing a 4×4 block) and a 3-channel content matrix (one RGB entry per active block), with a novel dimensionality reduction encoding that compresses the search space while preserving expressiveness. A random aggregation algorithm then consolidates scattered active blocks into k contiguous, irregular patches via K-Means clustering followed by stochastic local movement, ensuring physical realizability. Phase 2 applies (1+1) Evolution Strategy to refine the full-resolution perturbation at the pixel level, transitioning from coarse block-level exploration to fine-grained exploitation. The key distinction from prior work is that shape, location, number, and content are all encoded as decision variables and optimized simultaneously, rather than being fixed or optimized in isolation.

## Results

On ImageNet with a 10,000-query budget and 2% perturbation area, IMPACT achieves 96.4% untargeted ASR against ResNet50 (vs. 93.6% for Patch-RS and 57.0% for TPA), with an average query count of 1,044. For targeted attacks on the same model, IMPACT reaches 57.8% ASR compared to 20.0% for Patch-RS and 15.4% for TPA. Across VGG16 and ViT-B, IMPACT consistently leads by 2–3% on untargeted ASR and by 15–30% on targeted ASR over Patch-RS, while TPA lags by 35–40% on untargeted attacks across all three architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMPACT outperforms both baselines across all three models, both attack types, and both perturbation areas. Against Patch-RS (which optimizes rectangular patch content via random search), IMPACT gains 2.8–3.2 percentage points on untargeted ASR at 2% area (e.g., 96.4% vs. 93.6% on ResNet50) and a dramatic 37.8-point margin on targeted ASR (57.8% vs. 20.0%). Against TPA (reinforcement-learning-based patch optimization), the gap is even wider: IMPACT leads by roughly 39 points on untargeted ASR (96.4% vs. 57.0%) and by 42 points on targeted ASR. IMPACT also requires fewer queries on average (AQ 1,044 vs. 1,408 for Patch-RS on ResNet50 untargeted 2%), indicating that joint optimization of shape, location, number, and content is both more effective and more query-efficient than optimizing these factors independently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of adversarial patch attacks and evolutionary optimization, departing from prior work that typically optimizes patch factors in isolation—content (LaVAN, Patch-RS), location (Rao et al., TPA), or shape (deformable patches, cross-shaped patches)—by formulating and solving the full joint optimization of mask and perturbation simultaneously. The dimensionality reduction encoding (4×4 tiling with binary mask + continuous content) is novel in that it unifies shape, location, number, and content into a single searchable representation, whereas prior EA-based patch methods (CamoPatch, AdvIB, Tang et al.) either fixed the mask to rectangular shapes or only optimized content. The two-phase design (DE for global exploration, (1+1)-ES for pixel-level refinement) repurposes standard evolutionary operators with a custom binary/continuous hybrid mutation and a K-Means-based random aggregation step to produce physically coherent irregular patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint-optimization framing closes a genuine gap: prior methods that decoupled mask from content left substantial adversarial potential on the table, and the ~3× targeted-ASR improvement over Patch-RS (57.8% vs 20.0% on ResNet50) quantifies that gap convincingly across three architectures. The random aggregation algorithm producing locally connected, irregular, physically printable patches addresses a practical limitation of prior EA-based methods that yielded fragmented or fixed-geometry patches. Key limitations include restriction to score-based (not decision-based) black-box settings and the absence of theoretical guarantees; extending IMPACT to decision-based attacks and providing convergence or robustness analysis are natural open questions.
