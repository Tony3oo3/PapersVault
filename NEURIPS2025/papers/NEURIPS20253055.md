## Problem

Prior scaling-law studies for low-bit LLM quantization reached conflicting conclusions about the optimal bit-width (4-bit vs. 1.58-bit) because each bit-width was evaluated with different training schemes and quantization functions, making cross-bit comparisons unreliable. No unified framework existed to perform apples-to-apples comparisons across 1-bit, 1.58-bit, 2-bit, 3-bit, and 4-bit settings, leaving the accuracy-size trade-off landscape unclear.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained full-precision LLM weights (MobileLLM 125M–1.5B, LLaMA-3 1B/3B/8B) and produces quantized model weights at a chosen bit-width, with all non-embedding/output weights quantized while those layers remain at 8-bit or 16-bit. Training uses a fixed total token budget (e.g., 100B tokens) split ~90% for full-precision pretraining and ~10% for QAT fine-tuning, with the quantization scale α initialized from weight statistics and then learned end-to-end. The quantized model then generates text under standard zero-shot evaluation on commonsense reasoning and language modeling benchmarks.

## Method

ParetoQ unifies training and quantization design across five bit-widths by (1) establishing that ~90% full-precision pretraining followed by ~10% QAT fine-tuning is optimal for all bits, and (2) assigning a bit-specific quantization function: Elastic Binarization for 1-bit, a novel Stretched Elastic Quant (SEQ) for 1.58-bit and 2-bit, and standard LSQ for 3-bit and 4-bit. SEQ amends LSQ by shifting the rounding grid so that output levels are symmetrically balanced (e.g., {−1.5, −0.5, 0.5, 1.5} for 2-bit) and the full-precision weight span is evenly divided, which is critical at sub-3-bit precision. All variants use learnable per-channel scales optimized via the final loss (rather than fixed min-max statistics) and backpropagate through quantization with a straight-through estimator. A key empirical finding is a sharp learning transition between 2-bit and 3-bit: at 3-bit and above, QAT fine-tuning acts as 'compensation' (10–20% weight change, staying near the pre-trained distribution), while at 2-bit and below it acts as 'reconstruction' (~40% weight change, forming new representations), which explains why lower-bit models require roughly 3× more fine-tuning tokens to saturate.

## Results

On LLaMA-3 8B, ParetoQ 2-bit achieves 71.2 average accuracy across five zero-shot reasoning tasks with WikiText2 perplexity of 8.0, narrowing the gap to full precision (74.6 avg, 6.15 ppl) to just 3.4 points — a 5.7-point improvement over the best prior QAT method (EfficientQAT, 65.5 avg). At 1.58-bit (ternary), ParetoQ reaches 69.0 avg accuracy and 8.6 ppl, outperforming 1-bit Era (65.6 avg, 11.7 ppl) by 3.4 points while using only 30% of the training tokens. At 1-bit, ParetoQ achieves 67.8 avg and 9.5 ppl, dramatically surpassing BiLLM (39.3 avg, 38.5 ppl) and ARB-LLM (27.4 ppl). Across all eight models (125M–8B) and five bit-widths, ParetoQ consistently resides on the Pareto frontier, with 1.58-bit, 2-bit, and 3-bit quantization generally outperforming 4-bit in the accuracy–size trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParetoQ decisively outperforms all 14 baselines at every bit-width tested. In the 2-bit regime on LLaMA-3 8B, ParetoQ (71.2 avg, 8.0 ppl) beats the best QAT method EfficientQAT (65.5, 9.6) by 5.7 points and the vector-quantization method AQLM (70.2) by 1.0 point, while all PTQ methods (GPTQ 35.9, AWQ 36.1, SpinQuant 38.1, OmniQ 34.8) collapse to near-random accuracy. At 1.58-bit, ParetoQ (69.0, 8.6) surpasses 1-bit Era (65.6, 11.7) by 3.4 points in accuracy and 3.1 in perplexity, and outperforms TernaryLLM (11.2 ppl) by 2.6 ppl. At 1-bit, ParetoQ (67.8, 9.5) achieves a 28.5-point accuracy margin over BiLLM (39.3, 38.5) and reduces WikiText2 perplexity from 27.4 (ARB-LLM) to 9.5. Across the full model suite (125M–8B) in Tables 3–5, ParetoQ consistently leads or ties with SpinQuant at 4-bit, leads LLM-QAT by 5–15 points at 3-bit, and leads all PTQ/QAT baselines by 10–35 points at 2-bit, confirming that the advantage is most pronounced in the extreme low-bit regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParetoQ builds directly on Learned Step Size Quantization (LSQ) for 3/4-bit and Elastic Binarization for 1-bit, introducing Stretched Elastic Quant (SEQ) as a new quantization function for 1.58-bit and 2-bit. It departs from prior scaling-law studies (Dettmers & Zettlemoyer for 4-bit, Ma et al. for 1.58-bit) by elevating the quantization function and training scheme to first-class variables in the scaling equation, rather than treating them as fixed hyperparameters. The paper also challenges the from-scratch training paradigm of 1-bit Era and Spectra, demonstrating that fine-tuning from full-precision initialization consistently outperforms training from scratch even at binary and ternary precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 2-bit and ternary quantization outperform 4-bit in the accuracy-size Pareto trade-off challenges the widespread assumption that 4-bit is the practical sweet spot, with direct implications for on-device deployment where memory and latency are critical. However, widespread adoption of 2-bit remains gated by hardware support (e.g., INT2 in NVIDIA tensor cores), and the paper acknowledges that optimal bit-width selection may vary across use cases and data distributions. The unified framework provides a much-needed apples-to-apples baseline that the field lacked, making it a likely reference point for future low-bit quantization research.
