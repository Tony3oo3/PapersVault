## Problem

While spatial location and molecular interactions are known to shape neural circuit connectivity, the interplay between spatial position, gene expression, and functional connectivity at the macroscale of human brain networks remains incompletely understood. Prior approaches relied on linear or bilinear models that may not capture inherent non-linearities, and spatial autocorrelation—a major confound in neurogenomic studies—was not rigorously controlled. This paper presents the first deep learning approaches for predicting whole-brain functional connectivity from gene expression and regional spatial coordinates, alongside a novel spatiomolecular null mapping technique to ensure learned relationships are not artifacts of spatial proximity.

## Input / Output

The method takes regional gene expression profiles (7,380 genes per region from the Allen Human Brain Atlas, sampled at ~500 spatial locations) and 3D MNI spatial coordinates as input, and produces edge-wise predicted functional connectivity values (Pearson correlations of BOLD signals) reconstructing the full symmetric connectome matrix. Gene expression is consumed as a sequence of 123 tokens (bins of 60 genes sorted by transcription start site on the reference genome) per region, with an optional [CLS] token initialized from 3D coordinates. Evaluation is performed on population-averaged connectomes from UK Biobank (456 regions, n=1814), HCP-YA (456 regions, n=1065), and MPI-LEMON (183/391/729 regions, n=136), using both random and spatially constrained 4-fold train-test splits.

## Method

The Spatiomolecular Transformer (SMT) is a BERT-style encoder that tokenizes each region's gene expression vector into 123 contiguous bins of 60 genes sorted by transcription start site (TSS) on the human reference genome, projected into 128-dim embeddings. Multi-head self-attention (2–4 heads, up to 4 layers) incorporates ALiBi positional biases to encourage hierarchical, multi-scale genomic representations, while a dedicated [CLS] token initialized from 3D MNI coordinates conditions the molecular encoding on anatomical location. A 2–3 layer MLP decoder predicts connectivity from concatenated region-pair embeddings. To counteract the limited sample size of human transcriptomic data, a distributional target-side augmentation strategy injects individual-level connectome targets from the full population during training. A parallel single-gene-resolution MLP (2×7380 input) serves as a high-performance baseline, and a novel spatiomolecular null mapping procedure—using 10,000 spatial spins with rejection sampling to preserve correlated gene expression vs. distance structure—provides a stringent benchmark confirming that performance exceeds spatial autocorrelation alone.

## Results

On the UKBB dataset (456 regions), SMT w/[CLS] achieves a Pearson-r of .84±.02 (random split) and .70±.12 (spatial split) for whole-brain functional connectivity prediction, with MSE of .010 and geodesic distance of 9.8. The single-gene MLP w/coords achieves comparable performance (.83 random, .74 spatial), while the best linear baseline (Bilinear Low-rank) reaches .77 random and .66 spatial. Crucially, all non-linear models significantly exceed their spatiomolecular null baselines (e.g., SMT w/[CLS] null: .73 random, .49 spatial), confirming genuine transcriptomic signal beyond spatial autocorrelation. Cross-dataset, SMT w/[CLS] trained on UKBB predicts MPI-LEMON 183 at r=0.78, well above null.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the rules-based CGE(PCA) baseline [Richiardi 2015], SMT w/[CLS] improves Pearson-r from .23 to .84 (random) and .31 to .70 (spatial), a gain exceeding 0.5. The distance-based exponential decay model [Shinn 2023] achieves .35 (random) and .44 (spatial), substantially below all learned methods. The original Bilinear Connectome Model [Kovács 2020] reaches .62 (random) and .44 (spatial), while its PLS-embedded variant improves to .72 and .58 respectively. The Bilinear Low-rank model [Qiao 2024] is the strongest linear competitor at .77 (random) and .66 (spatial), but SMT w/[CLS] still leads by 0.07 and 0.04 Pearson-r respectively. Notably, under the spatial split, MLP w/coords (.74) slightly outperforms SMT w/[CLS] (.70), suggesting the [CLS] token introduces mild overfitting when test regions are spatially distant from training regions; however, the true-vs-null gap remains large for both, confirming genuine molecular signal capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts transformer architectures from single-cell genomics (scBERT, TOSICA, spaCI) to the whole-brain transcriptome-to-connectome prediction problem, introducing TSS-based tokenization with ALiBi positional biases to capture multi-scale genomic organization. It positions itself as a non-linear successor to established bilinear connectome models (Kovács et al., Qiao) and PLS-based imaging transcriptomics methods, which the authors argue cannot adequately capture inherent non-linearities. A key methodological departure is the spatiomolecular null mapping, which extends traditional spatial spin tests (Váša et al.) by additionally preserving transcriptomic autocorrelation structure (CGE-vs-distance curves), addressing a confound that prior null approaches did not control for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a rigorously validated framework showing that gene expression and spatial coordinates jointly predict whole-brain functional connectivity well beyond spatial autocorrelation, opening a path toward decoding the molecular basis of brain network architecture. However, the modest performance gap between the biologically-informed SMT and a simple MLP, combined with the finding that spatial coordinates alone achieve Pearson-r > 0.7, raises open questions about how much genuine molecular signal is captured versus spatial structure. Key limitations include reliance on only six AHBA donors for transcriptomic data and population-level (not individual-level) prediction. Future directions include scaling to larger transcriptomic atlases, integrating polygenic risk scores, and extending to patient-specific connectome prediction for clinical applications.
