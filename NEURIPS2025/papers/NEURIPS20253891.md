## Problem

Despite rapid progress in MLLMs for single-view video understanding, no benchmark has systematically evaluated whether these models can integrate information across first-person (egocentric) and third-person (exocentric) viewpoints — a capability fundamental to human cross-view reasoning but entirely unexplored in the MLLM literature.

## Input / Output

Each question presents one or more paired video clips (egocentric and/or exocentric, from synchronized or asynchronous recordings) alongside a 4-way multiple-choice text question. The model receives the question stem, labeled options, and the video inputs simultaneously, and must return a single answer letter. All 7,330 questions are evaluated in a zero-shot setting with no fine-tuning, and accuracy is the sole metric. A preliminary experiment confirms models can distinguish multiple video streams as separate inputs before the main evaluation.

## Method

EgoExoBench is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) video collection from six publicly available ego-exo datasets (Ego-Exo4D, EgoExoLearn, LEMMA, EgoMe, TF2023, CVMHAT) spanning synchronized and asynchronous recordings; (2) QA construction via three complementary strategies — annotation-derived templates for structured tasks, LLM-generated questions (using Qwen2.5-32B and DeepSeek-V3) for open-ended reasoning, and human annotation for fine-grained spatial tasks; and (3) quality assurance through LLM-based consistency verification and vision-grounded filtering that discards any question answerable from text alone. The 7,330 MCQs are organized into 11 subtasks under three dimensions: Ego-Exo Matching (semantic alignment across views), Ego-Exo View Transition (spatial correspondence and coordinate-frame translation), and Ego-Exo Temporal Reasoning (sequence alignment and prediction across asynchronous streams). The paper then evaluates 13 open- and closed-source MLLMs zero-shot, and conducts ablations on CoT prompting, cross-perspective reference videos, and ego-vs-exo viewpoint substitution to characterize where and why current models fail.

## Results

On EgoExoBench (7,330 MCQs across 11 subtasks), GPT-o4-mini achieves the highest closed-source accuracy at 48.0%, while Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads open-source models at 44.7%. In the supplementary evaluation, Gemini 2.5 Pro reaches 51.7% overall, the best among all tested models. Human evaluators achieve 90.1% accuracy under a 3-minute deliberation condition, revealing a ~42% gap over the best model. Performance drops sharply on cross-view tasks: even the best models score below 55% on most subtasks, with Skill Evaluation (SE) being the hardest at ~30% for all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, GPT-o4-mini (48.0%) outperforms GPT-4o (38.5%) by 9.5% and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (31.3%) by 16.7%, with the largest advantage on Task Matching (65.3% vs. 52.9% vs. 33.4%). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (44.7%) leads InternVL3-78B (41.4%) and all 7–14B models by 3–15%, showing consistent scaling benefits. EgoGPT-7B, despite being fine-tuned on a large egocentric corpus (EgoIT-99K), scores only 29.6%—comparable to generic 7B models like LLaVA-OV-7B (29.5%)—indicating that egocentric pretraining alone does not confer cross-view reasoning ability. In the supplementary evaluation, Gemini 2.5 Pro (51.7%) surpasses GPT-o4-mini by 3.7%, primarily on Matching subtasks (e.g., 67.3% on Object Matching vs. 56.8%). Across all models, the Ego-Exo View Transition subtask Egocentric Wearer Identification (EWI) shows the largest human–model gap (100% human vs. 76% best model), while Skill Evaluation (SE) is the hardest subtask for both humans (68.9%) and models (~30%), suggesting domain-specific expertise remains a bottleneck even for the strongest systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first benchmark that explicitly evaluates cross-view (egocentric-exocentric) video reasoning in MLLMs, a capability that prior video benchmarks (EgoSchema, Video-MME, MLVU, CG-Bench) never assess because they operate on single-view data in isolation. It repurposes six existing paired ego-exo datasets (Ego-Exo4D, EgoExoLearn, LEMMA, EgoMe, CVMHAT, TF2023) that previously served recognition or pose-estimation tasks but lacked any downstream QA or reasoning evaluation. Conceptually, it departs from prior ego-exo video understanding methods (view-invariant learning, domain adaptation, knowledge distillation) that target recognition accuracy rather than the higher-level semantic, spatial, and temporal reasoning required to bridge perspectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark 42-point gap between the best MLLM (48%) and human performance (90%), exposing that current models cannot perform the cross-view integration that is fundamental to embodied agents and human-robot collaboration. The finding that CoT prompting degrades rather than improves performance on most subtasks challenges the assumption that textual reasoning scaffolding transfers to interleaved visual-linguistic tasks, pointing toward a need for new architectures that natively interleave visual and linguistic inference across viewpoints. Limitations include the MCQ format (which may understate open-ended reasoning difficulty) and the fact that the benchmark may not capture the full breadth of real-world ego-exo scenarios, leaving room for future expansion.
