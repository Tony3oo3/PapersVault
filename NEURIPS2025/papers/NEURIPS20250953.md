## Problem

While the generative exponent provides a tight sample complexity characterization for Gaussian single-index models, the multi-index setting (r > 1) introduces a sequential 'leap' structure where the index space is revealed incrementally along different subspaces at different timescales, and no analogous tight characterization existed. This paper introduces the generative leap exponent to capture this sequential structure and proves it determines the exact sample complexity for efficient subspace recovery.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n iid samples (x_i, y_i) with x_i ~ N(0, I_d) and y_i depending on x_i only through a hidden r-dimensional projection, along with the Hermite moment order k at each leap step and a PSD kernel K over the (augmented) labels. The output is the full index subspace S ⊂ R^d, recovered sequentially leap-by-leap, with a guarantee that the operator-norm distance between the estimated and true projection matrices is at most ε. Notably, the algorithm requires no prior knowledge of the link function P, only the kernel K and the leap parameters.

## Method

The core algorithm is a sequential spectral procedure that recovers the index space along a 'leap decomposition' — a canonical flag of subspaces where each step identifies the lowest-order Hermite tensor carrying information given previously recovered directions. At each step, it constructs a matrix U-statistic U_n = (1/n(n-1)) Σ_{i≠j} φ(x_i)φ(x_j)^T K(y_i, y_j), where φ(x) is the flattened k-th order Hermite tensor and K is a generic PSD kernel (e.g., RBF) over labels; the U-statistic formulation summing only over disjoint pairs is critical because it removes diagonal terms that would otherwise prevent concentration at the optimal n ~ d^{k/2} threshold. The kernel trick over labels eliminates the need to know the specific link function, and the algorithm iterates by augmenting the label with the projection onto the previously recovered subspace, with a Lipschitz continuity argument controlling error accumulation across leaps. A matching lower bound is established via the low-degree polynomial framework, showing no degree-D polynomial test can detect the planted structure with n < d^{k*/2} samples.

## Results

The paper establishes a tight sample complexity of n = Θ(d^{k*/2}) for recovering the index subspace of any Gaussian multi-index model, where k* is the generative leap exponent. The lower bound (Theorem 1) shows n = Ω̃(d^{k*/2}) is necessary under the Low-Degree Polynomial framework, while the upper bound (Theorem 3) shows n = O(d^{k*/2}/ε + d/ε²) is sufficient via a sequential spectral U-statistic algorithm. For specific model classes: r-Gaussian Parity requires n = Θ(d^{r/2}) (first result achieving this rate), while intersection of half-spaces, polynomials, and piecewise-linear (ReLU) functions all have k* ≤ 2, yielding linear sample complexity n = Θ(d).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The results are positioned against prior theoretical guarantees: for polynomials, the n = Θ(d) rate matches Chen and Meka [2020]; for intersection of half-spaces, it matches Vempala [2010] and Klivans et al. [2024]; for ReLU networks, it improves upon Chen et al. [2022] by allowing non-zero biases. The key novelty is the r-Gaussian Parity result (n = Θ(d^{r/2})), which is the first to achieve the optimal LDP rate for this model. Compared to Diakonikolas et al. [2025a,b], the proposed algorithm achieves the tighter n = Θ(d^{k*/2}) rate versus their n = d^{O(k*)}, though their method provides explicit dependence on r, ε, and Lipschitz constants. The generative leap exponent k* is always ≤ the information leap exponent l* (Proposition 3), meaning the proposed algorithm can require fewer samples than gradient-based methods governed by l*.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative exponent framework of Damian et al. [2024] from single-index to general multi-index Gaussian models, introducing the 'generative leap exponent' and an associated 'leap decomposition' that captures the sequential, saddle-to-saddle structure of multi-index estimation first observed in gradient-based learning (Abbe et al. 2023, Bietti et al. 2025). The algorithmic core draws on tensor PCA unfolding (Montanari & Richard 2014, Zheng & Tomioka 2015) but departs from prior spectral estimators by using a kernel U-statistic over disjoint sample pairs, which is essential to avoid diagonal-term domination and achieve the optimal d^{k*/2} threshold. The concentration analysis relies on Gaussian universality results from Brailovskaya and van Handel [2024] to handle the heavy tails of high-order Hermite tensors, and the lower bound extends the LDP framework of Damian et al. [2024] to a detection problem adapted to the leap decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish a tight (matching upper and lower) sample complexity n = Θ(d^{k*/2}) for learning the index subspace of an arbitrary Gaussian multi-index model without prior knowledge of the link function, resolving a key open question in the computational-statistical gap literature. The kernel U-statistic approach is practically appealing because it eliminates the need for non-parametric regression over the link function, and the result that piecewise linear functions (including ReLU networks with biases) have k* ≤ 2 implies linear-in-d sample complexity for a broad class of models. Open questions include extending the framework to non-Gaussian data distributions, controlling the hidden constant C(P) which may be exponential in the intrinsic dimension r, and whether the zero-measure exclusions in the linear-transformation results (Proposition 6) are truly necessary.
