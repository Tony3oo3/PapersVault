## Problem

Many successful representation learning methods (e.g., Deep InfoMax) maximize Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD) between joint and marginal distributions via discriminative losses, yet the theoretical connection between this JSD-based objective and mutual information (MI), defined via Kullback-Leibler divergence (KLD), remained unestablished. This paper derives a new, optimal, and tractable lower bound on KLD as a function of JSD, providing the first rigorous guarantee that maximizing JSD provably increases a lower bound on MI.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired samples (u, v) drawn from the joint distribution p_UV and scrambled pairs drawn from the product of marginals p_U ⊗ p_V, feeding them into a binary discriminator that outputs a scalar probability. The MI lower bound is computed as Ξ(log 2 − L_CE), where Ξ is a strictly increasing function defined only implicitly through its inverse Ξ⁻¹(y) = D_JS[B(1)‖B(exp(−y))], requiring either a numerical root-finder or a differentiable logit-based approximation (1.15·Logit(0.5(x/log 2 + 1))) for practical use. In the Information Bottleneck experiments, the input is a 28×28 MNIST grayscale image with a digit label, and the output is a learned latent representation T used for classification.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution leverages the joint range of f-divergences (Harremoës–Vajda theorem) to fully characterize the achievable (JSD, KLD) pairs by restricting to Bernoulli distributions, then identifies the lower envelope of this range as the image of the boundary segment μ=1, yielding the optimal bound Ξ(D_JS[p‖q]) ≤ D_KL[p‖q]. The second key result shows that the f-divergence variational lower bound on JSD is exactly equivalent to minimizing the cross-entropy loss of a binary classifier trained to distinguish joint from marginal pairs, giving the chain Ξ(log 2 − L_CE) ≤ Ξ(I_JS) ≤ I[U;V]. This unifies the GAN discriminator objective with an information-theoretic interpretation and makes the gap due to suboptimal discriminators explicit via a first-order Taylor expansion. For MI estimation, a two-step procedure trains the discriminator and then computes E_pUV[Logit(q_θ(z=1|u,v))], equivalent to GAN-DIME. The approach is end-to-end differentiable (unlike two-step estimators that require retraining when the joint changes), uses a single discriminator, and avoids the high variance of Donsker–Varadhan-based bounds like MINE and NWJ.

## Results

On MNIST Information Bottleneck experiments, JSD-LB achieves 98.8% test accuracy (vs. 98.2% DisenIB, 97.6% VIB), 99.5% adversarial robustness at ε=0.1 (vs. 94.3% DisenIB), and 100.0% AUROC for OOD detection (vs. 99.4% DisenIB). In MI estimation benchmarks across Gaussian, cubic, asinh, half-cube, Student, and uniform distributions, JSD-LB consistently provides tighter and lower-variance lower bounds than MINE, NWJ, and CPC, particularly in high-MI regimes where MINE's bound can exceed the true MI. The derived two-step MI estimator (equivalent to GAN-DIME) achieves the most accurate MI estimates across all tested settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In MI estimation benchmarks, JSD-LB outperforms MINE [3] and NWJ [16] in both tightness and variance, with MINE's bound sometimes exceeding the true MI in high-dependence regimes while JSD-LB remains a valid lower bound that closely tracks it. Compared to CPC/InfoNCE [18], JSD-LB is not upper-bounded by log(batch size), enabling it to capture higher MI values without requiring large batches. The two-step estimator derived from the paper (equivalent to GAN-DIME [12]) surpasses all differentiable VLBs in estimation accuracy. In the Information Bottleneck setting on MNIST, JSD-LB outperforms VIB [1] (98.8% vs. 97.6%), NIB [9] (98.8% vs. 97.2%), Squared-VIB [21] (98.8% vs. 96.2%), Squared-NIB [21] (98.8% vs. 93.3%), and DisenIB [19] (98.8% vs. 98.2%) on generalization, with the largest margins in adversarial robustness (99.5% vs. 94.3% at ε=0.1) and OOD detection (100.0% vs. 99.4% AUROC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges a long-standing gap between two threads in the MI estimation literature: KLD-based variational lower bounds (MINE, NWJ) and JSD-based discriminative objectives (Deep InfoMax, GAN discriminators). It draws on the classical joint range theory of f-divergences (Harremoës-Vajda 2011) — a tool from information theory that had not previously been applied to representation learning — to derive an optimal lower bound on KLD as a function of JSD. The discriminator-cross-entropy connection to JSD, originally noted in the GAN literature (Goodfellow 2014, Nowozin 2016), is re-derived here with an explicit information-theoretic interpretation that quantifies the gap from suboptimal discriminators. The IB experiments build directly on the Disentangled Information Bottleneck setup (Pan 2021), replacing its MI estimator with the proposed JSD-LB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical justification for why JSD-based discriminative objectives (widely used in practice via Deep InfoMax) provably increase a lower bound on true mutual information, resolving a question that had only been addressed empirically. The tightness of the general JSD-KLD bound when restricted to the MI setting (joint vs. product of marginals) is a notable result, demonstrated both analytically for discrete distributions and empirically across continuous benchmarks. Key limitations include the reliance on a numerically-verified conjecture (Conjecture B.5) for the main theorem's proof, the lack of a closed-form expression for the Xi function, and evaluation limited to MNIST for the IB experiments. Open questions include whether the bound's tightness extends to high-dimensional continuous settings and whether the conjecture admits an analytical proof.
