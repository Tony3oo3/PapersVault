## Problem

Policy optimization in continuous POMDPs requires agents to balance exploration (reducing state uncertainty) against exploitation (maximizing immediate reward), but existing deep-RL methods rely on the QMDP approximation, which assumes the latent state becomes fully observable after one step and thereby ignores the value of future observations. This makes information gathering incidental rather than intentional, causing methods to fail on tasks requiring deliberate exploration such as light-dark navigation and active triangulation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observation-action histories (z_{0:t}, a_{0:t-1}) and maintains belief states as weighted particle sets approximating p(s_t|z_{0:t}, a_{0:t-1}), with oracle access to the observation likelihood g(z|s). The policy can be history-dependent (a GRU encoder over the full observation-action sequence) or belief-dependent (a set transformer over the particle set, ensuring permutation invariance). Output is a diagonal Gaussian action distribution N(m, diag(σ²)) parameterized by the learned policy, with the training objective being the risk-sensitive log-marginal likelihood of optimality events.

## Method

P3O casts POMDP policy learning as probabilistic inference in a non-Markovian Feynman-Kac model over observation-action trajectories, where potential functions encode the expected reward under the belief and the transition kernel is the belief-space predictive process. A nested SMC algorithm (Algorithm 2) samples from the reward-weighted trajectory distribution Ψ_T: an outer particle filter targets the FK model with resampling, mutation (policy sampling), and reweighting by exp(η·ℓ_t), while an inner particle filter at each history particle tracks the belief via standard bootstrap filtering. The policy gradient is estimated via Fisher's identity under Ψ_T, yielding E_{Ψ_T}[Σ_t ∇_φ log π_φ(a_t|z_{0:t}, a_{0:t-1})], which has lower variance than REINFORCE because trajectories are drawn from the reward-weighted posterior rather than the uninformative prior. The risk-sensitive objective B_η(φ) = (1/η) log p_φ(O_{1:T}) is maximized by gradient ascent, with η controlling the bias-variance tradeoff: larger η reduces gradient variance but introduces risk-sensitivity bias, while η→0 recovers the unbiased REINFORCE gradient.

## Results

The paper evaluates P3O against three deep-RL baselines (SLAC, DVRL, DualSMC) and a closed-form LQG baseline across five continuous POMDP benchmarks, reporting average return over 1024 trajectory rollouts with standard error over 10 seeds. On the LQG benchmark, P3O converges to performance comparable to the analytically optimal LQG controller without exploiting any analytic structure. On the Light-Dark and Triangulation tasks, which require deliberate information gathering, P3O and DVRL consistently outperform the QMDP-based solvers SLAC and DualSMC, which fail to learn effective policies. On the Pendulum and Cart-Pole swing-up tasks (where active exploration is not critical), all methods achieve comparable returns, with differences in convergence speed reflecting the frequency of gradient updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P3O is compared against three deep-RL baselines and a closed-form LQG reference. Against SLAC (QMDP + SAC, Lee et al. 2020) and DualSMC (QMDP + SAC + planning, Wang et al. 2020), P3O clearly wins on the Light-Dark and Triangulation tasks, where the QMDP approximation's disregard for future information causes persistently high-variance observations and stalled learning; on the Pendulum and Cart-Pole tasks, all methods achieve comparable final returns. Against DVRL (Igl et al. 2018, retrained with SAC), P3O performs comparably on all tasks, as both methods operate in the belief space without the QMDP shortcut, though P3O's nested SMC sampling provides a principled lower-variance gradient estimator. Against the LQG closed-form solution (Aoki, 1967), P3O converges to similar cumulative reward on the linear-Gaussian quadratic benchmark, validating the method under general assumptions without exploiting analytic structure. The paper also shows that increasing the number of Feynman-Kac particles N and belief particles M improves performance with diminishing returns, and that the temperature η trades off gradient variance against bias in the risk-sensitive objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: the control-as-inference framework (Toussaint & Storkey 2006, Levine 2018), which casts policy optimization as probabilistic inference; nested sequential Monte Carlo (Chopin et al. 2013, Crisan & Miguez 2018), originally developed for online parameter estimation in state-space models; and Thrun's (1999) belief-space generative process for POMDPs. The key departure is from the dominant QMDP-based paradigm (SLAC, DualSMC) and from methods that sample from state-space dynamics rather than belief-space dynamics. By constructing a non-Markovian Feynman-Kac model over observation-action trajectories and sampling from it via a nested particle filter, the paper avoids both the QMDP approximation and the need to learn explicit value functions, instead deriving a policy gradient via Fisher's identity under the reward-weighted posterior distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, approximation-free framework for policy optimization in continuous POMDPs that explicitly captures the value of information gathering, addressing a fundamental limitation of QMDP-based methods that underpin many popular deep RL algorithms. The empirical demonstration that QMDP methods completely fail (not merely degrade) on dual-effect tasks like light-dark and triangulation is a clear and useful diagnostic for practitioners. Key open questions include scaling beyond low-dimensional 2D settings (the bootstrap particle filter is known to suffer in high dimensions), removing the oracle observation model requirement, quantifying the bias introduced by the nested SMC structure, and developing principled strategies for tuning the risk parameter η.
