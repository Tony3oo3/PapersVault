## Problem

Model-based RL agents are highly sensitive to small dynamics changes, sensor noise, and Sim2Real gaps, yet existing robust RL methods (adversarial perturbations, CVaR constraints, domain randomization) do not exploit the trajectory-level generation capability of diffusion models. The paper asks whether diffusion-based world models can be steered to produce worst-case trajectories in a principled way, thereby enabling CVaR-optimal policy learning without the compounding errors of autoregressive models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252309/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment state-action-reward trajectories (H=10 steps in experiments) collected online from MuJoCo environments, along with a learned cumulative reward function Z_φ that maps noisy trajectory vectors to scalar returns. It produces two outputs: (1) a Gaussian policy π_ω(s) that maps states to actions, and (2) adversarial synthetic trajectories drawn from the worst α-percentile of the return distribution, generated by a guided diffusion model that denoises full H-step trajectory vectors in N reverse steps. Unlike autoregressive world models, the diffusion model generates the entire trajectory simultaneously, and inpainting fixes the starting state s_0 at every denoising step to anchor generation to a real initial condition.

## Method

AD-RRL's core mechanism is Adversarially Guided Diffusion (AD), which implements the CVaR dual formulation as a classifier-guided diffusion process. The key insight is that the CVaR risk envelope perturbation ξ*(τ) — the bounded density that minimizes expected return — is proportional to the conditional distribution p_θ(τ | τ ∈ C_α), so steering the diffusion toward the worst α-percentile set C_α is equivalent to applying the adversarial perturbation. Practically, the denoising mean at each step i is shifted by −c_i Σ_i g_i, where g_i = ∇_τ Z_φ(τ) is the gradient of the learned cumulative reward, biasing generation toward low-return trajectories. The paper proves (Proposition 4.3) that choosing c_i ≤ √(2 log η_i(α,N) / g_i^T Σ_i g_i) with ∏η_i = 1/α guarantees the induced perturbation stays within the CVaR risk envelope. AD-RRL wraps this in a Dyna-style A2C loop: collect real trajectories, train the diffusion model and reward model, sample adversarial trajectories via the guided process (with PolyGRAD-style policy guidance for action consistency), and update the policy on the synthetic worst-case data.

## Results

On the nominal MuJoCo training environments, AD-RRL achieves the best mean return on 4 of 5 tasks: Cheetah (4126.11), Walker (4357.80), Reacher (−3.97), and InvertedPendulum (1000.00), with only PolyGRAD slightly ahead on Hopper (3346.99 vs. 3280.23). Under perturbed physics parameters (mass, friction, gravity, actuator gear), AD-RRL consistently outperforms all robust and non-robust baselines, demonstrating that its CVaR-guided adversarial diffusion training does not sacrifice nominal optimality for robustness. In an ablation where model-free baselines receive twice as many samples (3M vs. 1.5M), AD-RRL still matches or exceeds their performance on both nominal returns and robustness under parameter shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PolyGRAD (the direct predecessor), AD-RRL trades a small nominal Hopper deficit (3280 vs. 3347, within overlapping confidence intervals) for substantially better robustness under perturbed mass, friction, and gravity, as shown in Figures 2a, 2b, and 2d. Against the robust baselines, AD-RRL dominates: M2TD3 achieves only 361.73 on Hopper (vs. 3280 for AD-RRL) and 634.76 on InvertedPendulum (vs. 1000), while CPPO and DR-PPO trail by 700–2000+ points on Cheetah and Walker. Against the strong model-free baselines PPO and TRPO, AD-RRL matches or surpasses nominal returns on 4 of 5 tasks and clearly outperforms them under all tested physics perturbations. In the 3M-sample ablation, even when model-free methods receive double the data, AD-RRL (1.5M samples) remains on par or better, and some baselines (DR-PPO, TRPO) actually degrade in robustness with more training, suggesting overfitting to nominal dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PolyGRAD [38], an online Dyna-style model-based RL method that uses diffusion models for trajectory generation, and on Diffuser [17] for the guided-diffusion planning framework. The key conceptual departure is inverting the standard use of classifier-guided diffusion: whereas prior diffusion-based RL methods condition generation on high-reward trajectories for planning, AD-RRL conditions on low-reward (worst-α-percentile) trajectories to train robustness. The theoretical bridge connecting CVaR's dual formulation (an adversarial density perturbation over trajectories) to adversarial diffusion guidance is the paper's main novelty, unifying two previously separate lines of work—risk-sensitive RL [5, 34, 35] and diffusion-based trajectory generation [17, 38].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical equivalence between CVaR's risk envelope and adversarial diffusion guidance provides a principled mechanism for sampling worst-case trajectories without explicit adversarial agents, which could simplify robust RL training pipelines. However, the method inherits the computational overhead of diffusion models (roughly 3× slower than model-free baselines in wall-clock time), assumes smooth dynamics for gradient-based guidance, and has only been validated on simulated MuJoCo tasks without Sim2Real deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-smooth or discrete dynamics, whether alternative guidance objectives beyond CVaR (e.g., entropy-regularized risk) yield better robustness, and whether the computational cost can be reduced via fewer denoising steps or distillation.
