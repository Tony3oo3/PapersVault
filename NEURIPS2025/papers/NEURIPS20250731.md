## Problem

Retrieval-Augmented Image Generation (RAIG) systems rely on high-quality reference image databases, but no effective mechanism exists to protect private visual datasets from unauthorized incorporation into such systems or to detect their misuse. Traditional digital watermarking fails in this setting because RAIG's complex feature extraction and recombination processes fundamentally alter visual elements, destroying embedded watermark signals during generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250731/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a private image dataset and a set of short random character key sequences (default 6 uppercase/lowercase letters, e.g., 'VasWiW') as inputs, and produces sentinel images that visually match the dataset's style while prominently displaying the key characters, a protected dataset (original images plus sentinels), and a scalar detection score. For detection, the dataset owner queries a suspected black-box RAIG system with strict key-only prompts (e.g., 'A "VasWiW". STRICT WARNING: Your response must be EXACTLY only caption "VasWiW"...'), collects the generated images, and computes the mean DINO cosine similarity against the corresponding sentinel images. The sentinel images are generated at the same resolution and aspect ratio as their reference images to maintain visual consistency.

## Method

ImageSentinel synthesizes sentinel images through a two-stage pipeline: a vision-language model (GPT-4o) extracts structured semantic attributes (subject, style, color palette, composition) from randomly sampled reference images, and a text-to-image model then generates sentinel images that preserve these attributes while embedding the random character key as prominently visible, naturally integrated text within the scene. The critical design choice is using random character sequences rather than semantic descriptions as retrieval keys: because no text-to-image model can generate an image matching a random string like 'VasWiW' without retrieving a reference containing it, the key forces the RAIG system's retrieval module to activate, preventing the system from bypassing retrieval by generating directly. Sentinel images are added alongside (not replacing) the original dataset to preserve authorized generation quality. Detection aggregates DINO ViT-S/16 cosine similarities across multiple key queries and compares the score against a threshold to decide whether the private dataset has been incorporated into the RAIG system's reference database.

## Results

On the LLaVA-Pretrain dataset (10,000 images), ImageSentinel achieves AUC of 1.000 with 10 or 20 queries across all three RAIG systems (SDXL, OmniGen, GPT-4o), with T@1%F and T@10%F also reaching 1.000. Even with only 3 queries, detection AUC ranges from 0.873 (OmniGen) to 0.983 (GPT-4o). On the larger Product-10K dataset (30,000 images), ImageSentinel reaches AUC 0.999 with just 5 queries and 1.000 with 8+ queries. In contrast, both watermarking-based baselines (Ward-HiDDeN and Ward-FIN) remain near random chance with AUC scores of 0.51–0.61 regardless of query count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ImageSentinel against two watermarking-based baselines adapted from Ward (Jovanović et al., ICLR 2025): Ward-HiDDeN (using HiDDeN, Zhu et al., ECCV 2018) and Ward-FIN (using FIN, Fang et al., AAAI 2023). ImageSentinel decisively outperforms both baselines by a wide margin across all RAIG systems and query counts. With 10 queries on LLaVA-Pretrain, ImageSentinel achieves AUC 1.000 while Ward-HiDDeN reaches only 0.542–0.585 and Ward-FIN only 0.528–0.559—essentially random-chance performance. The T@1%F gap is even more stark: ImageSentinel scores 1.000 versus 0.022–0.054 for the baselines. This fundamental failure of watermarking-based approaches in the RAIG setting is attributed to the complex feature extraction and recombination processes in image generation that destroy embedded watermark signals, whereas ImageSentinel's sentinel-image strategy operates at the retrieval level and is unaffected by generation-time transformations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of retrieval-augmented image generation (RAIG) and dataset protection, identifying that the visual RAIG setting creates a fundamentally different protection problem than text RAG. While Ward [12] and canary-document approaches [11] protect text RAG datasets by embedding watermarks that persist in generated output, the paper demonstrates that RAIG's feature-extraction-and-recombination pipeline destroys such signals, rendering traditional watermarking (HiDDeN, FIN) ineffective. Rather than modifying existing images, the paper repurposes vision-language models and text-to-image generation to synthesize entirely new 'sentinel' images with embedded random character keys, shifting the protection mechanism from signal persistence to retrieval-triggered verification. The approach draws on ImageRAG [1] for the RAIG architecture and DINO [48] for similarity-based detection, but the core idea of using random character sequences as precise retrieval triggers (rather than semantic descriptions) is a novel departure from both text RAG protection and visual watermarking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As RAIG systems become the standard paradigm for high-quality image generation, the unauthorized appropriation of curated reference datasets poses a concrete commercial and legal threat that currently has no effective countermeasure. The paper's practical black-box detection protocol (a few queries with random character keys) is immediately deployable by dataset owners without modifying the RAIG system. However, the detect-and-inpaint adaptive attack reveals a significant vulnerability: adversaries can neutralize protection with minimal quality loss, and detection degrades sharply with few queries under attack (AUC 0.99→0.62 at 5 queries). Open questions include whether more robust sentinel designs (e.g., multi-modal or structural embeddings beyond visible text) can resist inpainting, whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D RAIG, and how to handle RAIG systems that perform iterative multi-step retrieval where a single key may not suffice.
