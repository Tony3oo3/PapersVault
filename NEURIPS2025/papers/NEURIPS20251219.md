## Problem

Continual Reinforcement Learning agents suffer from catastrophic forgetting and inefficient knowledge utilization when adapting to sequential tasks. Existing CRL methods either fail to transfer knowledge across tasks without interference, exhibit substantial performance degradation on earlier tasks as the sequence lengthens, or face scalability issues where memory and computational costs grow linearly or quadratically with the number of tasks.

## Input / Output

The agent receives either 39-dimensional state vectors (Meta-World robotic manipulation) or 210×160×3 RGB frames (SpaceInvaders, Freeway) and outputs 4-dimensional continuous actions or discrete actions (6 for SpaceInvaders, 3 for Freeway). A key structural constraint is that the policy parameter is always constructed as a fixed base parameter plus a bounded linear combination of at most K_max knowledge vectors, so the effective model size remains constant regardless of how many tasks have been encountered. The critic is reinitialized per task while only the actor undergoes continual adaptation.

## Method

CKA-RL decomposes the policy into a frozen base parameter θ_base (learned on the first task) and task-specific knowledge vectors v_k that capture incremental adaptations, inspired by task arithmetic. For each new task τ_k, the policy is constructed as θ_k = θ_base + Σ_j α_j^k v_j + v_k, where the adaptation weights α_j^k come from a softmax over learnable parameters β_k, allowing the agent to selectively reuse or ignore historical knowledge; v_k is initialized to zero and only β_k and v_k are optimized during training. To prevent unbounded memory growth, an Adaptive Knowledge Merging step triggers when the vector pool exceeds K_max: the most cosine-similar pair of vectors is replaced by their average, keeping the pool size constant. Theoretical analysis shows the parameter drift is bounded by the norms of reused vectors, knowledge vectors remain nearly orthogonal (off-diagonal cosine similarity in [−0.24, 0.12]), and the merging perturbation is O(√δ) where δ measures the gap from perfect alignment.

## Results

CKA-RL achieves an average performance of 0.7498 and forward transfer of 0.5049 across three benchmarks (Meta-World, SpaceInvaders, Freeway), outperforming the strongest baseline CompoNet (0.7196 perf, 0.4674 FWT) by 4.20% in performance and 8.02% in forward transfer. On individual environments, CKA-RL reaches 0.9928 performance on SpaceInvaders and 0.7923 on Freeway, both setting new state-of-the-art. The method also maintains a low forgetting rate of 0.45 while achieving the highest overall performance, demonstrating a favorable stability-plasticity trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CKA-RL outperforms all nine baselines on both average performance and forward transfer across all three environments. The closest competitor is CompoNet (ICML 2024), which CKA-RL surpasses by 4.20% in average performance (0.7196→0.7498) and 8.02% in forward transfer (0.4674→0.5049); on SpaceInvaders specifically, CKA-RL gains 11.29% in forward transfer over CompoNet (0.6963→0.7749). CbpNet (Nature 2024), the second-strongest baseline, is beaten by 6.27% on Meta-World performance (0.4368→0.4642) and by 18.30% on SpaceInvaders performance (0.8392→0.9928). CReLUs (CoLLAs 2023) is the best plasticity-enhancement method but trails CKA-RL by 1.12% on Freeway performance and 1.73% on Freeway forward transfer. Architecture-based methods (ProgNet, PackNet, MaskNet) and fine-tuning baselines (FT-1, FT-N) are substantially outperformed, with PackNet and MaskNet showing negative forward transfer, indicating severe catastrophic forgetting that CKA-RL effectively mitigates.

## Relation to Prior Work

CKA-RL repurposes the task arithmetic / model editing paradigm (Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023) from NLP and vision into the continual reinforcement learning setting, representing each task's adaptation as a low-rank knowledge vector added to a frozen base policy. It departs from architecture-based CRL methods (CompoNet, ProgNet, PackNet) that grow or mask the network, and from plasticity-enhancement methods (CReLUs, CbpNet) that modify activation dynamics, instead treating knowledge transfer as a linear combination problem over a bounded vector pool. The Adaptive Knowledge Merging mechanism, which consolidates the most cosine-similar vectors when the pool exceeds a capacity threshold, is a novel scalability addition not present in prior task-arithmetic or continual RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constant-memory, constant-latency property of CKA-RL (via bounded pool size and fixed-size policy construction) addresses a practical bottleneck in continual RL where most competing methods see linear or quadratic growth in parameters and inference cost. However, the method has been validated only on small-scale MLPs and standard benchmarks (Meta-World, two Atari games); its applicability to deeper architectures, high-dimensional visual perception, or long-horizon robotic control remains unverified. Open questions include whether the near-orthogonality of knowledge vectors persists in larger networks, whether the softmax adaptation factors can be replaced by learned gating for more complex task relationships, and how the method behaves under truly non-stationary (rather than sequential) task distributions.
