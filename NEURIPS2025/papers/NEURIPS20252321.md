## Problem

Existing AI-generated image (AIGI) detectors struggle to generalize across diverse generative models and content types because they lack source-invariant, content-agnostic features. Prior approaches that disrupt semantics via patch shuffling (e.g., 32×32 patches) or pixel-difference features still retain residual semantic artifacts that bias detection. The paper identifies local pixel entropy—an unexplored statistical measure of pixel randomness—as a promising discriminative cue that captures generation artifacts while inherently suppressing semantic content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252321/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image resized to 224×224 and produces a binary real/fake classification score. Internally, the image is transformed into Multi-granularity Local Entropy Pattern (MLEP) feature maps of shape (H−1)×(W−1)×(C·K), where each channel encodes Shannon entropy over a 2×2 pixel window at a given resampling scale. Because the 2×2 window contains only four pixels, entropy values are restricted to five discrete levels {0, 0.8, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0}, yielding a compact, interpretable representation that is fed to a standard CNN classifier.

## Method

MLEP is computed in three stages: (1) the image is partitioned into small L×L patches (L=2) and randomly permuted per channel to destroy semantic structure; (2) the scrambled image is downsampled and upsampled at multiple scale factors (e.g., {1, 1/2, 1/4}) using bilinear interpolation, and the results are concatenated along the channel dimension to form a multi-scale resampling image; (3) Shannon entropy is computed over a 2×2 sliding window with stride 1 across all channels, capturing intra-patch, inter-patch, and inter-scale pixel randomness simultaneously. The resulting MLEP feature maps are fed into a standard ResNet-50 classifier trained with binary cross-entropy loss. The key insight is that AI-generated images exhibit systematically lower local entropy (a 'glossy and smooth' texture) than real photographs, and the combination of fine-grained shuffling, multi-scale resampling, and overlapping 2×2 windows amplifies this difference while eliminating content bias. Trained solely on four ProGAN categories from ForenSynths, the detector generalizes to 32 GAN- and diffusion-based generative models.

## Results

On the mean across all 32 generative models (16 GAN-based, 16 Diffusion-based), MLEP achieves 97.1% accuracy and 98.9% A.P., outperforming the strongest baseline D³ (92.8% Acc, 98.2% A.P.) by 4.3% in accuracy. On the Diffusion-Set specifically, MLEP reaches 97.8% Acc and 99.6% A.P., surpassing NPR (94.2% Acc) and D³ (91.1% Acc) by 3.6% and 6.7% respectively, despite being trained exclusively on ProGAN data. On the GAN-Set, MLEP scores 96.4% Acc and 98.2% A.P., trailing only FatFormer (99.0% Acc) which benefits from a transformer architecture but degrades sharply on diffusion models (84.8% Acc).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLEP achieves the best mean accuracy (97.1%) and A.P. (98.9%) across all 32 datasets, outperforming the closest competitor D³ [20] by 4.3% in accuracy and 0.7% in A.P. On the Diffusion-Set, MLEP dominates with 97.8% Acc, beating NPR [7] (94.2%), D³ (91.1%), and FatFormer [17] (84.8%) by 3.6%, 6.7%, and 13.0% respectively, demonstrating strong cross-architecture generalization from GAN-only training. On the GAN-Set, FatFormer [17] achieves a higher 99.0% Acc than MLEP's 96.4%, but this advantage is entirely offset by FatFormer's 84.8% on diffusion models, yielding a lower overall mean. Compared to the spatial-domain baselines NPR [7] and Zheng [8], MLEP gains 3.1% and 16.3% in mean accuracy respectively, validating the entropy-based feature design over pixel-difference and coarse patch-shuffling approaches. Frequency-domain methods (F3Net [10], FreqNet [21]) and CLIP-based methods (CLIPping [16], Ojha [15], VIBAIGC [23]) all trail MLEP by 9–16% in mean accuracy, with the largest gaps on diffusion-generated images. The only per-dataset losses for MLEP are on a few GAN models (e.g., BigGAN 87.1% vs. FatFormer 99.5%), where the entropy signal is weaker, but these are more than compensated by near-perfect scores on most other generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two recent AIGI detection approaches: NPR (Tan et al., CVPR 2024), which uses local pixel differences to capture upsampling artifacts, and Zheng et al. (NeurIPS 2024), which disrupts semantic artifacts via 32×32 patch shuffling. The key departure is replacing pixel-difference features with Shannon entropy computed over 2×2 windows, and replacing coarse 32×32 shuffling with fine-grained (L=2) patch permutation, arguing that both prior methods retain residual semantic content that limits cross-domain generalization. A multi-scale downsample-then-upsample resampling step is added to expose generation artifacts at multiple resolutions, producing the Multi-granularity Local Entropy Patterns (MLEP) fed to a standard ResNet classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a conceptually simple, interpretable, and content-agnostic feature (discrete entropy on tiny shuffled patches) can match or exceed complex learned representations (CLIP-based, diffusion-reconstruction, transformer-based) across 32 generative models, offering a lightweight and transparent alternative for AIGI detection. However, the authors acknowledge a significant robustness gap: detection accuracy drops 17–45% under common post-processing (JPEG compression, blurring, noise), limiting real-world deployment. Open questions include whether entropy-based features can be made robust to such degradations, whether the 2×2 window constraint (which limits entropy to five discrete values) can be relaxed without prohibitive computational cost, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D content.
