## Problem

Self-supervised video trackers rely predominantly on intra-frame appearance features and fail when multiple objects look visually similar, because they neglect the inter-frame temporal dynamics that humans use to disambiguate identity. This paper asks whether pretrained video diffusion models—trained only to generate coherent video—already encode the motion representations needed to resolve such ambiguities, without any tracking-specific supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251341/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence and a ground-truth segmentation mask for the first frame as input. The video is split into overlapping 16-frame clips (matching I2VGen-XL's maximum input length) with a configurable overlap (default l=2 frames). Each clip is passed through the 3D UNet of a pretrained video diffusion model at a chosen high-noise timestep, while each individual frame is independently fed into a 2D image diffusion model at a low-noise timestep. The output is a per-frame pixel-level segmentation mask propagated from the first frame to all subsequent frames via top-K label aggregation in a spatially local neighborhood.

## Method

The key insight is that video diffusion models encode coarse motion signals (e.g., object position changes across frames) at high-noise denoising stages, while fine appearance details emerge only at low-noise stages. TED extracts motion-aware features R_m from the 3rd decoder block of I2VGen-XL's 3D UNet at high noise levels (τ=300–900, dataset-dependent), where temporal attention and 3D convolutions process the entire 16-frame clip jointly, and appearance features R_a from the 8th block of ADM's 2D UNet at low noise (τ=51), where each frame is processed independently. These are fused via L2-normalized concatenation with a tunable weight λ (0.4–1.0 depending on how visually ambiguous the scene is). Tracking then proceeds via standard recurrent label propagation: for each query pixel, the top-K most similar pixels in reference frames are identified by dot-product similarity in the fused representation space, and their labels are aggregated by weighted sum. No parameters are trained or fine-tuned; the entire pipeline is inference-time feature extraction from off-the-shelf pretrained diffusion models.

## Results

On the standard DAVIS-2017 benchmark, TED achieves J&Fm of 77.6, outperforming the strongest prior self-supervised methods (Spa-then-Temp 74.1, DIFT 75.7, SMTC 73.0) by up to 3.5 points. On the newly introduced Youtube-Similar dataset with real-world similar-looking objects, TED reaches J&Fm of 66.0, a 6.4-point gain over Spa-then-Temp (59.6). On the controlled Kubric-Similar benchmark with two identical balls, TED achieves Jm of 87.2 while most baselines hover near 50% (random-guess level), demonstrating the method's ability to track objects that are visually indistinguishable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TED outperforms all 17 baselines across every dataset. On DAVIS-2017, TED (J&Fm 77.6) beats the strongest image-based method DIFT (75.7) by 1.9 points and the strongest video-based method Spa-then-Temp (74.1) by 3.5 points. The gap widens substantially on similar-looking object benchmarks: on Youtube-Similar, TED (66.0) surpasses DIFT (60.7) by 5.3 and Spa-then-Temp (59.6) by 6.4; on Kubric-Similar with identical objects, TED (90.2) dominates SMTC (68.6) by 21.6 points and DIFT (55.1) by 35.1 points, while most baselines remain near the 50% random-guess floor. Among image-only methods, SFC (71.2 on DAVIS) and DIFT (75.7) are the strongest, but both rely solely on appearance and fail to distinguish similar objects. Among video-based methods, SMTC (73.0) and Spa-then-Temp (74.1) are the closest competitors on DAVIS, yet both process frames independently at inference with 2D encoders, limiting their temporal modeling. TED's advantage is most pronounced precisely where prior methods fail—tracking visually similar or identical objects—confirming that motion-aware representations from video diffusion models address a fundamental weakness of appearance-only trackers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of self-supervised tracking (DIFT, Spa-then-Temp, SFC, CRW, SMTC), which learns representations from scratch via pretext tasks but processes frames independently at inference, and from prior diffusion-based trackers (Track4Gen, Diff-Tracker, VidSeg) that require tracking-specific training or auxiliary modules. Instead, it repurposes the internal feature activations of pretrained video diffusion models (I2VGen-XL, SVD) at high-noise denoising stages as motion-aware representations, requiring no tracking-specific objectives or additional training. The method fuses these motion features with appearance features from a pretrained image diffusion model (ADM), following the two-stream philosophy of Simonyan & Zisserman, and applies standard label propagation for inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that video diffusion models encode coherent inter-frame motion at high-noise stages—where appearance is nearly destroyed—provides a new lens on what generative models learn internally and opens a path toward leveraging pretrained generative models for perception tasks without task-specific supervision. However, the practical adoption is constrained by significant computational overhead (682 ms/frame, 15.3 GB memory vs. 360 ms and 1.9 GB for SFC), and the method's reliance on 3D UNet architectures limits generality. Key open questions include whether motion intelligence can be distilled into lightweight models, whether the high-noise motion encoding generalizes to non-UNet video diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based), and whether the approach extends to longer temporal contexts beyond the 16-frame window.
