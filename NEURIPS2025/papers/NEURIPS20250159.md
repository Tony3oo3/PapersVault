## Problem

Existing PID estimators (CVX, BATCH) are limited to discrete or low-dimensional features, making them inapplicable to continuous, high-dimensional multimodal data. The Gaussian PID (GPID) formulation by Venkatesh et al. restricted the optimization to joint Gaussians without proving this restriction was lossless, and its algorithm required expensive eigenvalue decompositions scaling cubically in the sum of feature dimensions. This paper resolves the optimality question and proposes a substantially more efficient algorithm for GPID, then extends it to arbitrary input distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes two multimodal feature vectors X1 ∈ R^{d_X1} and X2 ∈ R^{d_X2} (of arbitrary dimensionality and distribution) along with a target variable Y ∈ R^{d_Y}, and outputs four non-negative PID components: redundancy R, unique information U1 and U2, and synergy S. For non-Gaussian inputs, the method first learns invertible normalizing flow encoders to map each modality into a latent Gaussian space before computing PID; for pre-extracted features (audio, time-series), pretrained encoders are used upstream. The total information R+U1+U2+S provides an upper bound on achievable multimodal model performance for the task.

## Method

The paper makes two core technical contributions. First, Thin-PID reformulates the GPID synergy optimization as minimizing a log-determinant objective log|HΣ_Y H^T + Σ_{n1n2}| / |Σ_{n1n2}| over only the off-diagonal noise covariance block Σ_{n1n2}^off ∈ R^{d_X1×d_X2}, solved via projected gradient descent with RProp updates and SVD-based projection onto the spectral norm ball ‖Σ_{n1n2}^off‖_2 ≤ 1. This reduces the dominant cost from O((d_X1+d_X2)^3) eigenvalue decomposition (as in Tilde-PID) to O(min(d_X1,d_X2)^3) SVD, yielding >10× speedup when dimensions are high. Second, Flow-PID learns a Cartesian product of normalizing flows f1×f2×fY that transforms arbitrary input distributions into pairwise Gaussian marginals while provably preserving total mutual information (Theorem 4.1), trained with a Gaussian marginal loss that minimizes KL divergence between transformed marginals and variational Gaussians. The paper also proves (Lemma 3.2) that a jointly Gaussian optimizer always exists for GPID, resolving an open problem from Venkatesh et al. that their restricted formulation might only provide a lower bound.

## Results

On synthetic Gaussian examples, Thin-PID recovers ground-truth PID values with error < 10⁻¹², while Tilde-PID has absolute error > 10⁻⁸. On non-Gaussian multi-dimensional examples (up to 100×60×2 dimensions), Flow-PID aligns with the exact PID truth in relative values, whereas Tilde-PID produces distorted interaction degrees. On real-world MultiBench datasets, Flow-PID assigns substantially higher total information (R+U₁+U₂+S) than CVX/BATCH (e.g., 1.24 vs 1.18 on AV-MNIST; 4.50 vs 1.02 on MOSI Audio+Text), and achieves 96.72%–100% of best-model accuracy in model selection across six datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Tilde-PID (Venkatesh et al., NeurIPS 2023), Thin-PID achieves a 10× speedup when min(d_X₁, d_X₂) > 100 and reduces numerical error from > 10⁻⁸ to < 10⁻¹² on canonical Gaussian examples. On non-Gaussian multi-dimensional data (Table 2), Flow-PID recovers the correct relative PID values matching the Gaussian ground truth, while Tilde-PID (which estimates covariance directly from non-Gaussian samples) produces qualitatively wrong interaction patterns (e.g., reporting R=0.18 vs. truth 0.79 at dimension 2×2×2). Against BATCH/CVX (Liang et al., NeurIPS 2023), Flow-PID assigns significantly higher total mutual information on real-world datasets (e.g., 4.50 nats on MOSI Audio+Text vs. 0.54 for BATCH), which the authors argue better reflects the actual information content given that multimodal models achieve >75% accuracy. In model selection (Table 6), Flow-PID matches or exceeds BATCH on 5 of 6 datasets, with the only loss being UR-FUNNY (96.72% vs. 98.58%), attributed to the high unique information in the text modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Bertschinger et al. PID definition and the Gaussian broadcast channel interpretation of GPID introduced by Venkatesh and Schamberg, resolving an open optimality question left by Venkatesh et al.'s ~G-PID: the authors prove that restricting the optimizer to jointly Gaussian distributions incurs no loss of optimality when pairwise marginals are Gaussian. Methodologically, Thin-PID departs from Tilde-PID by optimizing only the off-diagonal noise covariance block via projected gradient descent with SVD-based projection, rather than performing full eigenvalue decomposition on the joint covariance. Flow-PID then extends the framework to arbitrary input distributions by learning normalizing-flow encoders that map modalities into a latent Gaussian space while preserving mutual information, a natural but previously unexploited connection between flow-based density estimation and information decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes PID estimation tractable for continuous, high-dimensional multimodal data—a regime where prior methods (CVX, BATCH) were either inapplicable or prohibitively expensive—by combining a 10× computational speedup with a principled non-Gaussian extension. This opens the door to using PID as a practical diagnostic tool for modality importance and model selection in large-scale multimodal benchmarks. Key limitations include the approximation error introduced by the Gaussian marginal assumption in Flow-PID, the restriction to two source modalities, and the difficulty of validating PID values on real-world data where ground truth is unknown. Future work could extend the framework to more than two modalities, multi-task settings, and large-model pretraining guided by information decomposition.
