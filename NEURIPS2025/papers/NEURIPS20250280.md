## Problem

Chain-of-thought reasoning in Vision-Language Models is confined to textual space, preventing the model from directly interacting with visual inputs—such as zooming into image regions or selecting specific video frames—during the reasoning process. This textual-only paradigm limits VLMs' ability to capture fine-grained visual details like tiny objects, small embedded text, and nuanced temporal actions in videos. The paper asks whether VLMs can perform reasoning steps directly within the visual modality, using computational visual manipulations as integral actions in the reasoning chain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250280/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a vision-language query consisting of an image or a 16-frame video sequence paired with a textual question. During reasoning, it can invoke two visual operations: `crop_image` (specifying a 2D bounding box to zoom into a region of an image) and `select_frames` (specifying up to 8 frame indices from the 16-frame video). The output is an iterative reasoning trajectory that interleaves textual thinking segments with visual operation calls and their execution results (cropped images or selected frames fed back as visual tokens), concluding with a final answer in a boxed format.

## Method

Pixel-Reasoner, built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, is trained in two phases to overcome a 'learning trap' where the model's nascent pixel-space reasoning receives more negative feedback than its established textual reasoning, causing premature abandonment of visual operations. The first phase is a warm-start instruction tuning on 7,500 GPT-4o-synthesized trajectories (single-pass and error-induced self-correction) that establish foundational proficiency in visual operations and self-correction, with loss masks applied to execution outputs and deliberately erroneous operations. The second phase is a curiosity-driven RL phase using GRPO with selective sample replay, where the reward function—derived via Lagrangian relaxation of a constrained optimization—incorporates a curiosity bonus that rewards pixel-space reasoning when the Rate of Pixel-space Reasoning (RaPR) falls below a threshold H, and an efficiency penalty for exceeding N visual operations. This dynamic incentive naturally diminishes as the policy explores more, preventing reward hacking while sustaining exploration of the novel reasoning path.

## Results

Pixel-Reasoner (7B, built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 84.3% accuracy on V* Bench, 73.8% on TallyQA-Complex, 67.8% on MVBench, and 84.0 ANLS on InfographicsVQA, setting the best known open-source results across all four benchmarks. Notably, the 7B model surpasses proprietary models including GPT-4o (62.8% on V*) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (79.2% on V*) by 5.1 percentage points on V* Bench. The ablation 'Warm-Start Model (w/o RL)' scores only 79.0% on V* and 74.3 ANLS on InfoVQA, confirming that the RL phase is critical for unlocking pixel-space reasoning gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models without tools, Pixel-Reasoner (7B) outperforms GPT-4o by 21.5 points on V* (84.3 vs 62.8) and by 3.2 points on MVBench (67.8 vs 64.6), while matching Gemini-2.5-Pro on TallyQA-Complex (73.8 vs 74.0) and InfographicsVQA (84.0 vs 84.0) and exceeding it by 5.1 points on V*. Against tool-augmented baselines specialized for V*, Pixel-Reasoner surpasses IVM-Enhance/GPT-4V (84.3 vs 81.2), Visual Sketchpad/GPT-4o (84.3 vs 80.4), PaLI-X-VPD/55B (84.3 vs 76.6), and SEAL/7B (84.3 vs 74.8), all without requiring external tool APIs at inference time. Against open-source VLMs, the 7B Pixel-Reasoner dominates the 27B Gemma3 by 22 points on V* and 19.5 points on TallyQA, and improves over its own base Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 13.9 points on V* and 3.4 on TallyQA. The only benchmark where a proprietary model retains an edge is InfographicsVQA, where Gemini-2.0-Flash scores 86.5 ANLS versus Pixel-Reasoner's 84.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B as its base VLM and adopts GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) with selective sample replay (from VL-Rethinker) as its RL backbone. It departs from the dominant textual CoT paradigm in VLMs by proposing that reasoning steps can directly manipulate visual inputs via operations like zoom-in and select-frame, rather than solely translating visual observations into text. Unlike prior tool-augmented approaches (Visual Sketchpad, VPD, IVM) that rely on external tool calls or distill tool-based reasoning into closed-source models, Pixel-Reasoner trains an open-source VLM to natively interleave visual operations within its reasoning chain through a two-phase pipeline of synthesized-trajectory instruction tuning and curiosity-driven RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'learning trap'—where RL causes a model to abandon a nascent capability in favor of a stronger existing one—provides a general diagnostic for anyone attempting to instill new reasoning behaviors via RL. The curiosity-driven reward scheme, which dynamically decays as the model's pixel-space reasoning rate increases, offers a principled and reusable mechanism for balancing exploration of new skills against exploitation of established ones. However, the approach is currently limited to two visual operations and a single 7B model, and the paper acknowledges that broader task coverage and additional operations (depth maps, image search) remain open. Whether the pixel-space reasoning paradigm generalizes beyond fine-grained visual detail tasks to more abstract multimodal reasoning is an important open question.
