## Problem

Existing deep learning Arctic sea ice concentration (SIC) forecasting methods operate at a single fixed temporal granularity (e.g., only daily or only monthly), overlooking the inter-granularity correlations where short-term fluctuations inform long-term trends and vice versa. Additionally, the dominant U-Net architecture implicitly models temporal sequences through channel-wise convolutional fusion, which is ill-posed for explicit sequential modeling of spatially correlated time series.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254630/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three SIC sequences simultaneously: 7 days of daily SIC, 8 weeks of weekly-average SIC, and 6 months of monthly-average SIC, each at 448×304 pixels (25 km × 25 km grid) from the NSIDC G02202 V4 dataset. For each granularity, the input length L equals the forecasting lead time P, so the model produces three simultaneous forecasts (daily, 8-week average, and 6-month average SIC maps) in a single forward pass. Only SIC data is used as input—no additional atmospheric or oceanic variables are required.

## Method

SIFusion replaces the U-Net channel-wise fusion with independent spatial tokenization: a shared Swin Transformer V2 encoder partitions each SIC map into 2×2 patch tokens and hierarchically encodes them into compact 1D spatial tokens, which are then sequentially concatenated within each granularity to form 'granularity variates.' An encoder-only Transformer backbone (inspired by iTransformer) applies multi-head self-attention across the three granularity variate tokens to capture inter-granularity correlations, while per-variate FFNs extract intra-granularity sequential information. Two skip connections preserve information: a spatial skip from the last Swin encoder block to the first decoder block, and a cross-attention sequential skip where pre-projection sequence tokens serve as queries against predicted SIC features as keys/values. A symmetric Swin Transformer V2 decoder with patch-expanding operations restores the tokenized features back to full-resolution SIC maps for all three granularities simultaneously.

## Results

On the NSIDC G02202 dataset, SIFusion achieves the best 7-day SIC forecasting with RMSE 0.0429, MAE 0.0096, R² 0.987, and IIEE 926, outperforming the closest baseline SICNet (RMSE 0.0490, MAE 0.0100). For 6-month seasonal forecasting, SIFusion reaches RMSE 0.0692 and R² 0.917, substantially surpassing IceNet (RMSE 0.1820, R² 0.567) and MT-IceNet (RMSE 0.0777, R² 0.915). For the 8-week average granularity, SIFusion achieves RMSE 0.0625 and IIEE 1600, establishing a new state-of-the-art for sub-seasonal weekly-scale prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIFusion outperforms all directly comparable baselines across every reported metric. For 7-day forecasting, SIFusion reduces RMSE by 12.4% over SICNet (0.0429 vs. 0.0490) and by 37–38% over ConvLSTM, PredRNN, and SimVP, while also achieving the lowest IIEE (926 vs. 976 for SICNet) and SIE_dif (0.0380 vs. 0.0718). For 6-month seasonal forecasting, SIFusion dramatically outperforms IceNet (RMSE 0.0692 vs. 0.1820; R² 0.917 vs. 0.567) and edges out MT-IceNet (RMSE 0.0692 vs. 0.0777; MAE 0.0166 vs. 0.0197). Notably, SIFusion uses only SIC as input, whereas several baselines (IceNet, MT-IceNet, IceFormer) incorporate additional atmospheric and oceanic variables, underscoring the value of the multi-granularity fusion approach. The 45-day (IceFormer) and 90-day (SICNet_90) baselines are not directly comparable since SIFusion does not report those specific lead times, but SIFusion's 7-day and 8-week results suggest competitive or superior performance at intermediate sub-seasonal scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIFusion departs from the dominant U-Net-based paradigm in Arctic SIC forecasting (SICNet, IceNet, MT-IceNet), which performs implicit sequence modeling through channel-wise convolutional fusion, by instead adopting independent spatial tokenization via Swin Transformer V2 followed by explicit sequential modeling. The sequential backbone directly inherits the iTransformer paradigm [33], where multi-head self-attention operates over granularity variate tokens (treating daily, weekly, and monthly SIC as distinct variates) rather than over temporal tokens, and FFN processes each variate independently. The key conceptual departure is the reframing of multi-granularity SIC as correlated variates in a unified encoder-only Transformer, rather than as separate single-scale forecasting problems as in all prior deep learning approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a model using only SIC data with multi-granularity fusion outperforms baselines that incorporate additional atmospheric and oceanic variables (e.g., SIFusion R²=0.973 vs. IceNet R²=0.567 for 6-month forecasting) suggests that inter-granularity correlations in sea ice dynamics are a previously underexploited signal. The framework is generic and extensible: the authors note that climate variables and pre-trained weather foundation models could be incorporated, and the multi-granularity design positions SIFusion as a candidate for sub-seasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) sea ice forecasting. Open questions include whether the 8-week granularity (not aligned with standard weekly or monthly scales) is optimal, whether performance gains persist when climate covariates are added, and whether the iTransformer-style variate attention generalizes to other spatiotemporal forecasting domains beyond sea ice.
