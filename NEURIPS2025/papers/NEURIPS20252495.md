## Problem

GNNs suffer from over-smoothing (node features collapsing as depth increases) and over-squashing (inability to propagate information across distant nodes), yet no unified theoretical framework explains why these problems arise or why certain fixes work. This paper shows both phenomena share a common root cause—extreme gradient vanishing driven by the contractive spectrum of the normalized adjacency matrix—and bridges the recurrent and graph learning literatures to address it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252495/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a graph adjacency matrix and per-node feature vectors, processing them through K layers of a state-space GNN update. The fixed state transition matrix Λ (constructed from a random unitary matrix scaled so eig(Λ)≈1) and input matrix B are shared across layers and not trained; only the coupling function F_θ (an arbitrary MPNN layer, e.g., k-hop aggregation) is optimized. Outputs are node embeddings used for node classification (Cora, LRGB) or graph-level regression (GPP tasks, RingTransfer).

## Method

The paper first proves that the layer-wise Jacobian of a linear GCN has squared singular values λ_i²μ_j, where λ_i are eigenvalues of the normalized adjacency (all |λ_i|≤1) and μ_j come from the weight matrix, making the Jacobian inherently more contractive than in RNNs or MLPs (Theorem 3.2). To counteract this, GNN-SSM rewrites the layer update as H^(k+1)ᵀ = Λ H^(k)ᵀ + B F_θ(H^(k),k)ᵀ, where Λ is a fixed matrix with eigenvalues near 1, placing the Jacobian at the edge of chaos at zero extra trainable-parameter cost (Proposition 3.3). The paper then shows via the Banach fixed-point theorem that contractive GNN layers drive all node features to the zero fixed point, explaining over-smoothing as zero-norm collapse rather than mere similarity, and that Dirichlet energy decreases as a direct consequence. For over-squashing, the authors argue that both the topological term (graph connectivity) and the model-dynamics term in the sensitivity bound must be addressed simultaneously, leading to kGNN-SSM, which combines k-hop rewiring with the non-dissipative SSM dynamics to achieve state-of-the-art long-range performance.

## Results

On the GPP graph-property-prediction tasks (lower log₁₀(MSE) is better), the full kGNN-SSM model (GCN + SSM + eig(Λ)≈1 + k-hop) achieves −3.075 (Diameter), −3.604 (SSSP), and −4.265 (Eccentricity), substantially outperforming DRew-GCN+delay (−2.402, −1.602, −2.029) and vanilla GCN (0.742, 0.950, 0.847). On the LRGB Peptides-Func task, kGCN-SSM with increased hidden dimension reaches 72.12% AP, compared to 68.05% for DRew-GCN with the same dimension increase. On Peptides-Struct, kGCN-SSM achieves 27.01 MAE (×10⁻²) versus 27.64 for DRew-GCN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla GCN, kGNN-SSM improves log₁₀(MSE) by roughly 3.8–5.1 points across all three GPP tasks, confirming that the base architecture is severely limited by extreme gradient vanishing. Against the strongest baseline DRew-GCN+delay, kGNN-SSM wins on every GPP task with large margins: 0.67 on Diameter, 2.00 on SSSP, and 2.24 on Eccentricity (in log₁₀(MSE)), indicating that the state-space formulation with edge-of-chaos dynamics is more effective than delay-based rewiring alone. On LRGB Peptides-Func, kGCN-SSM+d↑ (72.12% AP) outperforms DRew-GCN+d↑ (68.05% AP) by over 4 points, while on Peptides-Struct the gap is smaller (27.01 vs. 27.64 MAE). GIN and Gated-GCN are compared qualitatively via Jacobian eigenvalue histograms and Dirichlet energy curves (Figure 4): GIN shows occasional |λ|>1 outliers and Gated-GCN has substantial spectral mass above the unit circle, both indicating unstable or collapsing dynamics that the GNN-SSM avoids by design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of GNN research—over-smoothing (Oono & Suzuki, Cai & Wang) and over-squashing (Di Giovanni et al., Topping et al.)—under a single mechanistic explanation rooted in vanishing gradients and the contractive spectral structure of normalized adjacency matrices. It draws directly on the state-space model literature for sequences (Mamba, S4, Orvieto et al.) to propose GNN-SSM, a parameter-free architectural modification that places the layer-wise Jacobian at the edge of chaos, and on the dynamical isometry literature (Pennington et al., Saxe et al.) for the theoretical tools (Lipschitz constants, Banach fixed-point convergence) that link Jacobian contraction to feature collapse. The paper departs from the prior over-smoothing narrative, which attributed collapse to iterative aggregation converging to a rank-one subspace, by showing the dominant mechanism is actually norm contraction driving all features to a zero fixed point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified Jacobian-spectrum lens gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic (eigenvalue histogram of the layer-wise Jacobian) to predict whether a given GNN will over-smooth, over-squash, or diverge, and the GNN-SSM construction is a drop-in, zero-extra-parameter modification applicable to any MPNN. The finding that over-squashing mitigation requires *both* graph rewiring and non-dissipative dynamics (rather than rewiring alone, as much prior work assumed) reframes how long-range graph modeling should be approached. Open questions include whether the fixed, non-trained state-transition matrix Λ is always optimal versus learned variants, how the approach scales to very large or heterophilic graphs, and whether the clean spectral analysis extends beyond the GCN/GAT setting where the math is most tractable.
