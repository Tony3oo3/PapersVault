## Problem

While information-theoretic generalization analysis has been well-developed for supervised encoder-decoder models (e.g., via the information bottleneck hypothesis), rigorous theoretical characterizations of how latent variables (LVs) govern generalization in unsupervised models like VQ-VAEs remain largely absent. Existing PAC-Bayes analyses either focus on encoder/decoder parameter complexity without isolating the role of LVs, or rely on impractical assumptions such as a fixed, untrained decoder. This paper addresses the gap by providing the first decoder-independent, information-theoretic generalization bounds for VQ-VAEs that explicitly quantify the contribution of discrete latent variables.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on bounded image data (MNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10 32×32) processed through a VQ-VAE with a discrete codebook of K entries in a d_z-dimensional latent space. The theoretical outputs are closed-form upper bounds on the reconstruction-loss generalization gap and on the 2-Wasserstein distance between the true data distribution and the generated-data distribution. A key structural property is that all bounds depend only on encoder parameters (codebook e, encoder φ) and the induced LV distribution, remaining entirely independent of decoder complexity θ — a property empirically validated by showing that adding ~74,000 decoder parameters has negligible effect on the generalization gap.

## Method

The paper extends the supersample framework of information-theoretic analysis to VQ-VAEs by constructing a data-dependent prior over discrete LVs (codebook indices) that decouples LV complexity from decoder parameters, yielding a generalization bound characterized by two KL divergence terms (Theorem 2). However, the empirical KL term in this bound does not vanish asymptotically, so the authors introduce a novel permutation symmetric setting: instead of the standard n-bit supersample index U, they use a uniform random permutation T of all 2n data points, defining a new data-dependent prior that simultaneously satisfies the two necessary conditions (shuffle-invariance and train/test swap) without requiring a data-independent prior. This yields a bound (Theorem 3) that converges to zero as n→∞ and depends only on I(e,φ;S) and I(J̃;T|e,φ,X̃). Additionally, a metric-entropy-based uniform bound (Theorem 4) avoids specifying the training algorithm, and a 2-Wasserstein distance bound (Theorem 5) formalizes how KL regularization on LVs improves data generation quality.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with experiments in Appendix G serving to validate the derived bounds. Key empirical findings on MNIST (K=128, d_z=64) include: (1) adding a single ResBlock (~74,000 additional decoder parameters) has a negligible effect on the generalization gap, confirming decoder independence; (2) the second KL term KL(Q_{J̃,U}||Q_{J̃})/n exhibits a consistent positive correlation (r≈0.46–0.60) with the generalization gap across decoder complexities (2–5 ResBlocks) and sample sizes (n=250–4000), while the first KL term KL(Q_{J,U}||P)/n does not decrease with n, validating Lemma 3's O(1) prediction; (3) the proposed data-dependent prior (Eq. 9) consistently improves data generation test performance over the standard uniform prior (Table 4, Appendix G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing generative or compression methods; its contribution is theoretical. The experiments validate internal theoretical predictions rather than benchmarking against external baselines. Specifically, the decoder-independence claim is validated by showing that increasing decoder capacity from 2 to 5 ResBlocks leaves the generalization gap essentially unchanged while training loss decreases (Table 3, Appendix G). The non-convergence of the first KL term (Lemma 3) is confirmed empirically, motivating the permutation symmetric setting of Theorem 3. The only 'comparison' is between the standard uniform prior and the proposed data-dependent prior for data generation, where the latter consistently yields lower test loss (Table 4, Appendix G), supporting the theoretical recommendation in Theorem 5. The paper positions itself relative to Mbacke et al. [46] (who assumed an untrained decoder) and Sefidgaran et al. [57] (supervised setting), but does not run head-to-head numerical comparisons with these prior bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information-theoretic generalization analysis framework of Sefidgaran et al. (NeurIPS 2023), who showed decoder-independent bounds for supervised representation learning via data-dependent priors over latent variables, to the unsupervised VQ-VAE setting with discrete latent spaces. It builds on the supersample IT analysis of Hellström & Durisi (NeurIPS 2022) and Steinke & Zakynthinou (COLT 2020), and incorporates the data regeneration technique from Mbacke et al. (NeurIPS 2023), who provided PAC-Bayes guarantees for VAEs under the restrictive assumption of an untrained decoder. The key departure is the discovery that the standard supersample setting fails to yield asymptotically vanishing bounds for VQ-VAEs, motivating the novel permutation symmetric prior that resolves this gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous decoder-independent generalization analysis for VQ-VAEs that isolates the role of latent variables, providing a formal justification for KL-based regularization on discrete LVs in both generalization and data generation (via a 2-Wasserstein bound). The permutation symmetric prior is a potentially reusable technique for other unsupervised settings where the standard supersample shuffling breaks symmetry. Key open questions include extending the analysis to continuous latent spaces (where the discrete codebook structure is unavailable) and developing computable versions of the Theorem 3 bound, which currently relies on a 2n-dimensional dependent random variable that resists standard CMI estimation.
