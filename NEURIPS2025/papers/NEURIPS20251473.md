## Problem

Deploying large robot policies in the real world requires steering them to match scenario-specific needs, but existing approaches like goal-conditioning demand that the policy be trained with a distribution of test-time objectives in mind, requiring foresight over all future steering needs. This paper seeks a steering method that works with off-the-shelf pretrained diffusion policies, supports multiple positive and negative objectives, and remains robust to lower-quality guidance conditions without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251473/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a current RGB observation, robot proprioception, and a set of guidance condition images (desired and/or undesired outcomes) that are projected into frozen DinoV2 patch-embedding space (256×384). It produces a 16-step action chunk by modifying the standard DDIM denoising loop of a pretrained diffusion policy (ResNet-18 + U-Net, ~18M params). The guidance conditions need only be partial-state images of target scenes rather than full trajectory demonstrations, and the system accepts any number of positive and negative conditions simultaneously.

## Method

DynaGuide decouples steering from the base policy by training a separate 6-layer transformer dynamics model (~16M params) in DinoV2 latent space that predicts future observation embeddings from current observation, proprioception, and a noisy action sequence. A guidance metric (Eq. 2) computes a log-sum-exp over squared latent distances between the predicted future state and each guidance condition, treating positive and negative objectives symmetrically. During inference, the gradient of this metric with respect to the noisy action is subtracted from the policy's noise prediction at each DDIM step (Eq. 3), directly steering the denoising trajectory toward desired outcomes. Stochastic sampling (repeating each denoising step M=4 times via MCMC) stabilizes the guidance signal, allowing higher guidance strengths without trajectory incoherence. The dynamics model is trained with 50% noise-augmented actions (matching the DDIM scheduler) so it remains in-distribution when queried with noisy actions during the guidance loop.

## Results

On the CALVIN ArticulatedParts benchmark, DynaGuide achieves an average target behavior success of 70%, boosting the base policy's behavior frequency by 8.7×. On the UnderspecifiedObjectives experiment (lower-quality guidance conditions), DynaGuide outperforms the goal-conditioned baseline by 5.4× on average (e.g., 0.33 vs. 0.023 on button_on). On MultiObjectives, DynaGuide reaches up to 80% success across multiple target behaviors with nearly zero full behavior failures. On the real robot, DynaGuide achieves 72.5% success on CupPreference and 80% on HiddenCup, and doubles interactions with a novel object (mouse) not present in the base policy's training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unsteered Base Policy (Diffusion Policy [7]), DynaGuide consistently and significantly increases target behavior frequency across all CALVIN experiments (8.7× on ArticulatedParts). Against the sampling-based GPC baseline [39], DynaGuide wins on MovableObjects (where GPC degrades to base-policy level due to high action variance), on UnderrepresentedBehaviors (40% success at 1% data vs. lower for GPC), and on MultiObjectives (fewer undesired behaviors and fewer 'no behavior' failures). Against Position Guidance (ITPS) [50], DynaGuide outperforms on all ArticulatedParts tasks, benefiting from the dynamics model's reasoning over fixed coordinate targets. Against Goal Conditioning, DynaGuide is dramatically superior on lower-quality objectives (5.4× on average on UnderspecifiedObjectives), while GC performs comparably only on the clean, in-distribution ArticulatedParts setup. Against Classifier-Free Guidance [20] (Appendix A.5), DynaGuide achieves higher total positive-guidance success and substantially fewer 'No Behavior' failures (CFG fails >10% of the time vs. near-zero for DynaGuide), attributed to the log-sum-exp latent selection mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaGuide sits at the intersection of three established lines: diffusion policy learning [7], classifier guidance for diffusion processes [9], and latent dynamics models for planning [56]. It departs from goal-conditioned policies by fully separating the steering signal (a DinoV2-space dynamics model) from the base policy, and from sampling-based steering (GPC [39]) by actively modifying the denoising trajectory rather than selecting among samples. The stochastic sampling stabilization from ITPS [50] is adopted to keep high guidance strengths tractable, and the log-sum-exp combination of multiple latent distances is a design choice motivated by the need to handle mutually exclusive or partially attainable objectives simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is that any pretrained diffusion policy can be steered at inference time toward multiple positive and negative objectives without retraining, which is a significant deployment advantage over goal-conditioned or fine-tuned approaches. The 5.4× robustness gap over goal-conditioning on underspecified objectives and the ability to elicit novel behavior (mouse-grabbing from a cup-only policy) suggest the dynamics model carries useful world knowledge beyond the base policy's training distribution. Key limitations remain: guidance conditions are restricted to visual outcome images (no language or kinesthetic specification), the method cannot control the trajectory path—only the endpoint—and the approach has only been validated on relatively short-horizon manipulation tasks. Open questions include multi-modal guidance, long-horizon composition, and whether the dynamics model's additional knowledge can be systematically leveraged for behavior synthesis beyond the base policy's support.
