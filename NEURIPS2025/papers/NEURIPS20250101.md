## Problem

Classification-based VPR methods assign place labels based on fixed geographic grids and view-direction rules, but images from the same location captured under different viewpoints or partial occlusions can look drastically different, creating supervision signals that contradict true visual similarity. Existing remedies—manually defined orientation labels (CosPlace) or SVD-based cropping (EigenPlaces)—still misalign class boundaries with actual scene semantics, degrading descriptor quality and retrieval robustness.

## Input / Output

At training, the method consumes RGB panoramic images from SF-XL (~0.9M images) cropped at 504×504 pixels, partitioned into UTM grid cells (margin M=10 m) and further subdivided into K=3 view-direction clusters. At inference, a single RGB query image is encoded into a 512-dimensional descriptor and matched against a geo-tagged reference database via nearest-neighbor search, with correct localization defined as a 25-meter geographic radius. Multi-angle cropping (every 60° with starting angles at 0° and 30°) generates 12 views per panorama to increase semantic continuity between adjacent classes.

## Method

MutualVPR builds on a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 backbone with a trainable MulConv bottleneck adapter (three parallel convolutional branches at different receptive fields) followed by GeM pooling and a linear projection to 512 dimensions. The core contribution is a mutual learning loop: place labels are first initialized by coarse UTM grid partitioning, then iteratively refined within each grid by K-means clustering on the current descriptors, while the encoder is trained with Large Margin Cosine Loss (LMCL) against those cluster assignments. Each training iteration alternates between (1) using updated clusters to supervise feature learning and (2) using improved features to re-cluster, so that visually similar views—regardless of orientation or occlusion—progressively converge into the same class. This co-evolution eliminates the need for orientation labels and corrects early misassignments, yielding semantically consistent supervision that is robust to both viewpoint variation and occlusion.

## Results

MutualVPR (512-dim, trained on SF-XL) achieves R@1 of 92.4 on Tokyo 24/7, 92.6 on Pitts250k, 90.9 on Pitts30k, 89.2 on MSLS-val, and 80.8 on SF-XL-test v1, outperforming all classification-based baselines trained on the same data. On the challenging SF-XL-Occlusion benchmark, MutualVPR reaches 47.4% R@1 and 65.8% R@5, surpassing the next-best method (SALAD+CM at 40.8% R@1) by over 6 points. The method uses only a 512-dimensional descriptor, matching or exceeding higher-dimensional contrastive methods (e.g., BoQ at 512-dim, SALAD+CM at 512+32-dim) on most benchmarks while requiring no hard sample mining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CosPlace (same SF-XL training data, orientation-label supervision), MutualVPR wins on every benchmark, with the largest gap on Tokyo 24/7 (92.4 vs. 76.5 R@1, +15.9) and SF-XL-test v1 (80.8 vs. 64.8 R@1, +16.0), confirming that adaptive clustering resolves the supervision inconsistencies inherent in fixed view-direction labels. Against EigenPlaces (also SF-XL-trained), MutualVPR wins on Tokyo 24/7 (+7.6 R@1) and MSLS (+1.1 R@1) but trails on Pitts30k (90.9 vs. 92.3), Pitts250k (92.6 vs. 93.5), and SF-XL-test v1 (80.8 vs. 83.8), where EigenPlaces' geometry-driven viewpoint-invariance prior is better suited. Among contrastive-learning methods trained on GSV-Cities, MutualVPR (trained only on SF-XL) matches or exceeds BoQ on Tokyo 24/7 (92.4 vs. 91.9) and SF-XL-test v1 (80.8 vs. 79.6), and surpasses SALAD+CM on SF-XL-test v1 (80.8 vs. 78.4), though it lags on Pitts30k and Pitts250k where GSV-Cities' consistent-viewpoint training data gives contrastive methods an edge. On the SF-XL-Occlusion stress test, MutualVPR leads all baselines by a wide margin: 47.4% R@1 vs. 40.8% for SALAD+CM and CricaVPR₁, 38.2% for BoQ, and 36.8% for EigenPlaces, demonstrating that iterative feature-aware clustering substantially improves robustness under heavy occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

MutualVPR extends the classification-based VPR lineage initiated by CosPlace (UTM grid partitioning + LMCL) and EigenPlaces (SVD-based view grouping), but departs fundamentally by replacing their fixed, handcrafted view-direction labels with an iterative K-means clustering that co-evolves with the feature encoder. The MulConv adapter from CricaVPR is adopted as the lightweight fine-tuning module on top of a frozen DINOv2 backbone, while the mutual learning loop—where updated clusters supervise feature learning and improved features guide re-clustering—is the paper's core conceptual departure from both contrastive-learning VPR (which relies on hard sample mining) and prior classification-based methods (which rely on static labels).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode in classification-based VPR—supervision inconsistency from view-direction changes and occlusions—that prior methods handle only heuristically. The ~10-point R@1 gap on SF-XL-Occlusion (47.4% vs. 36.8% for EigenPlaces) demonstrates that semantically consistent supervision, rather than geometric invariance priors, is the key to robustness under missing visual cues. A notable open question is the fixed cluster number K, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation; the finding that DINOv2 peaks at K=3 while ResNet50 peaks at K=6 suggests no universal optimal granularity exists, pointing toward adaptive or per-grid K as a natural next step.
