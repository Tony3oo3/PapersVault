## Problem

Existing MLIP benchmarks rely on static DFT references and error-based regression metrics (MAE, RMSE) that are vulnerable to data leakage, become outdated as new datasets emerge, and fail to capture physical consistency, generalization to out-of-distribution structures, or practical utility in downstream scientific tasks. This paper identifies that top-ranked models on benchmarks like Matbench Discovery can exhibit broken crystal symmetry, rough PES derivatives, and poor reactivity despite low regression errors, exposing a gap between benchmark scores and real-world atomistic modeling performance.

## Input / Output

The platform takes MLIP model checkpoints (14 open-source models spanning equivariant and non-equivariant architectures) and applies them to atomic structures across four benchmark categories. Outputs are DFT-reference-agnostic physical and geometric metrics: tortuosity and Spearman's coefficients on equation-of-state and potential-energy curves, MD stability scores (fraction of valid runs and steps-per-second scaling exponent), energy conservation deviation and rotational equivariance MAE binned by QUESTS differential entropy, and task-specific quantities like vacancy migration barriers, CO₂ adsorption heats via Widom insertion, 2D material dynamical stability F1 scores, and Landau-like phase transition PES profiles. All workflows are defined as Prefect tasks for parallel HPC execution with ASE as the simulation backend.

## Method

MLIP Arena introduces four complementary benchmark categories that replace regression-error evaluation with physics-aware probes. (1) Asymptotic analysis fits Birch-Murnaghan EOS on 1,000 WBM crystal structures (21,000 ionic relaxation trajectories per model) and computes PECs for all homonuclear diatomics (0.9 r_cov to 3.1 r_vdw), quantifying smoothness via tortuosity (arc-chord ratio), short-range repulsion via Spearman's monotonicity coefficients, and conservative-field deviation via central-difference force-energy consistency. (2) MD stability tests run NVT (300→3000 K) and NPT (0→500 GPa) Nosé-Hoover simulations on 1,000 random mixture structures (RM24), plus a 1 ns hydrogen combustion annealing test to probe chemical reactivity. (3) Distribution-shift robustness uses QUESTS differential entropy to quantify structural 'surprise' relative to training data, then measures energy drift in 5 ps NVE windows and rotational equivariance MAE across 50 angles, revealing that direct-force models increasingly violate conservation and symmetry as OOD-ness grows. (4) Thermodynamic property benchmarks include CI-NEB vacancy migration in 114 FCC/HCP elements, Widom-insertion CO₂ adsorption in 20 MOFs, phonon-based dynamical stability screening of 505 C2DB 2D materials, and octahedral-tilting PES in BaZrO₃ to detect Landau-like second-order phase transitions.

## Results

On the EOS benchmark (1,000 WBM structures), MACE-MPA achieves the best derivative flips (1.037 ± 0.283) and tortuosity (1.005 ± 0.054), while ORBv2 performs worst on both (1.316 ± 0.870 and 1.037 ± 0.215). For 2D material dynamical stability (C2DB), MACE-MP(M) achieves the highest macro F1 score of 0.420, with CHGNet, ORBv2, and ALIGNN scoring below 0.30 and failing to identify any stable structures. In the hydrogen combustion reactivity test, only MACE-MP(M) and EquiformerV2(OC20) ignite within the experimental adiabatic flame temperature range (2380–3000 K), while ORB/ORBv2 produce zero water molecules and positive reaction enthalpies. ORBv2 is the fastest model in MD (best SPS scaling exponent) but cannot sustain 500 GPa compression due to the absence of an explicit short-range repulsive core.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACE-MPA and MACE-MP(M) consistently rank at or near the top across EOS quality (derivative flips, tortuosity, Spearman's coefficients) and PEC smoothness, with MACE-MPA achieving the best conservation deviation (0.077 eV/Å) on homonuclear diatomics. MatterSim, sharing M3GNet's architecture, is significantly more stable and performant in MD simulations, demonstrating that architecture alone does not determine runtime or stability. ORBv2 is the fastest in MD (best SPS scaling exponent) but ranks worst on EOS quality and fails under 500 GPa compression; it also produces zero water in hydrogen combustion and shows large energy conservation violations under distribution shift. SevenNet and CHGNet occupy the middle tier on EOS but underperform on 2D dynamical stability (macro F1 < 0.30) and fail to complete 1 ns hydrogen combustion trajectories. EquiformerV2(OC20) is the only non-MACE model that ignites hydrogen within the experimental temperature window, yet it exhibits the worst tortuosity (8.669) and conservation deviation (21.385 eV/Å) on PECs, revealing a stark disconnect between reactivity and pairwise interaction quality. The paper's central finding is that no single MLIP dominates all benchmark categories, and top Matbench Discovery rankings do not transfer to physical consistency, reactivity, or OOD robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of MLIP benchmarking (Matbench, Matbench Discovery, and DFT-reference-based evaluations) by introducing physics-aware, reference-agnostic metrics that probe asymptotic behavior, energy conservation, rotational equivariance, and practical utility under extreme conditions. It builds conceptually on prior work showing that low force-error does not guarantee energy conservation (Bigi et al., Fu et al.) and on the QUESTS information-theoretic descriptors for quantifying distribution shift, but repurposes these ideas into a unified, reproducible benchmark platform. The RM24 random-mixture dataset and the four-category evaluation structure (asymptotics, MD stability, distribution-shift robustness, thermodynamic properties) are novel contributions that carve out a new evaluation space orthogonal to static DFT regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MLIP Arena exposes critical failure modes of current foundation MLIPs—short-range PES holes, broken crystal symmetry in NEB paths, non-conservative force drift under distribution shift, and inability to capture chemical reactivity—that are invisible to standard regression benchmarks. The finding that top Matbench Discovery models perform poorly on pairwise diatomic interactions suggests that apparent bulk accuracy may mask fundamentally incorrect physics through many-body error cancellation. Open questions include whether these physics-aware metrics can be integrated into training objectives (via RL, implicit differentiation, or test-time training), whether the benchmark scales to new model architectures as they emerge, and whether the DFT-agnostic evaluation can be made as easily comparable as the DFT-reference approach it replaces.
