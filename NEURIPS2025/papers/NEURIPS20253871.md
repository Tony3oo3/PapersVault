## Problem

Bounding the Blackwell threshold α_Bw and the d-sensitive threshold α_d is fundamental for computing Blackwell and d-sensitive optimal strategies in perfect-information stochastic games via reduction to discounted games. Prior work provided bounds only for the mean-payoff case (d = −1) in deterministic games and for the one-player (MDP) case, leaving no general bound on α_d for d > −1 and suboptimal bounds on α_Bw for two-player stochastic games.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three game parameters under Assumption 1.1: the number of states n, an upper bound W on absolute integer rewards, and the common denominator M of rational transition probabilities. It produces explicit upper bounds on α_Bw and α_d, expressed as bounds on −log(1−α) (i.e., the gap 1−α is lower-bounded by e^{−L}). The bounds are regime-dependent: for deterministic games (M=1) three complementary techniques yield different tightness depending on the relative size of log(W) versus n, while for stochastic games the d-sensitive bound additionally requires a unichain structural assumption.

## Method

The core technique studies the zeros of Δ(α), the numerator polynomial of the difference v_i^{σ,τ}(α) − v_i^{σ',τ'}(α) between discounted value functions of two stationary strategy pairs. By bounding the degree (≤ 2n−1) and coefficient magnitudes of Δ via graph-path analysis (deterministic case) or cofactor-matrix expansions (stochastic case), the paper then applies three algebraic root-separation results to guarantee no zero lies in (α_threshold, 1): (1) the Lagrange bound on the modulus of non-zero roots, applied after the change of variable ε = 1−α; (2) a Mahler-measure-based separation theorem of Dubickas (building on Mignotte–Waldschmidt) that lower-bounds |z−1| for algebraic numbers of large degree; and (3) a multiplicity bound of Borwein–Erdélyi–Kós on the order of 1 as a root, which yields a smaller d than n−2 for which d-sensitive optimality implies Blackwell optimality in deterministic games. The three techniques are complementary—neither dominates the others across all regimes of n, W, and M—and together they improve prior bounds by a factor of Ω(n).

## Results

For deterministic perfect-information SGs, the paper provides three complementary bounds on the Blackwell threshold: the Lagrange-based bound gives -log(1-α_Bw) ≤ O(n + log(W/√n)), the Mahler-measure bound gives O(√(n log(n) log(√n W))), and the multiplicity bound gives O(√(n log(W)) log(n/log(W)) + log(W)). The Lagrange bound also yields the first bound on the d-sensitive threshold α_d beyond d=-1: -log(1-α_d) ≤ O(log(W) + (d+4)(1 + log(2n/(d+4)))). For stochastic SGs, the Lagrange bound gives -log(1-α_Bw) ≤ O(n(1+log(M)) + log(W)), improving upon prior work by a factor of Ω(n).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper improves upon [AM09] by a factor of Ω(n) on -log(1-α_Bw) for both deterministic and stochastic SGs: where [AM09] gives O(n log(n) + n² log(W)) for deterministic games, the Lagrange bound (Th. 1.2) achieves O(n + log(W/√n)). Compared to [GCP23], which is restricted to MDPs and gives O(n² + n log(W)), the paper extends to two-player SGs while also improving the exponent of n from 2 to 1. The multiplicity-based bound (Th. 1.5) recovers the result of [MK25] (O(√(n log(W)) log(n/log(W)) + log(W))) but extends it from deterministic MDPs to deterministic two-player SGs. The paper also recovers the tight bound α_{-1} ≤ 1 - 1/(8Wn³) of [ZP96] for d=-1, and provides the first bound on α_d for d > -1 in the deterministic setting. The three new bounds (Lagrange, Mahler, multiplicity) are complementary: for W = O(1), the Mahler and multiplicity bounds (O(√n log n)) dominate the Lagrange bound (O(n)), while for W = exp(Θ(n)), the Lagrange and multiplicity bounds (O(n)) dominate the Mahler bound (O(n√log n)).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the root-separation approach to bounding the Blackwell threshold from prior work on MDPs (Grand-Clément & Petrik 2023) and stochastic games (Andersson & Miltersen 2009) to the two-player perfect-information setting, while replacing weaker Cauchy-type separation bounds with stronger Lagrange and Mahler-measure-based results. It also generalizes the multiplicity-theorem approach of Mukherjee & Kalyanakrishnan (2025) from deterministic MDPs to deterministic two-player SGs, and introduces the first bounds on the d-sensitive threshold α_d for d > -1, building on Veinott's original d-sensitive optimality framework. The key methodological departure is exploiting the path-then-circuit structure of deterministic value functions to obtain tighter coefficient bounds, and using separation from 1 specifically (rather than from all conjugates) to gain a factor of Ω(n).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These bounds control the computational complexity of the main known method for computing Blackwell and d-sensitive optimal strategies (solving discounted games at α > threshold), and the Ω(n) improvement over prior work is the first such gain in this area. However, the bounds remain exponentially close to 1, so they do not yet yield polynomial-time algorithms, and the multiplicity approach—which gives the strongest deterministic bounds—provably fails for stochastic games, leaving a gap. Open questions include obtaining α_d bounds without the unichain assumption for stochastic games, understanding whether the exponential gap between the bounds and 1 is inherent or an artifact of the separation techniques used, and deriving lower bounds on α_Bw and α_d to assess tightness.
