## Problem

Evaluating LLM-generated text requires measuring semantic similarity to references, but existing semantic similarity benchmarks (STS, Winograd, MRPC, BIOSSES) are expensive to produce via human annotation, limited in domain coverage, and lack clear operational definitions of semantic equivalence. There is no scalable, domain-agnostic method for generating semantic similarity benchmarks with controlled, interpretable semantic variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250906/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes knowledge graph triples (source-node, relation, target-node) from domain-specific KGs (Codex, Oregano, FinDKG, Globi) and produces binary-labeled pairs of natural-language statements: similar pairs (label 1) generated from two independent LLM renderings of the same subgraph, and dissimilar pairs (label 0) generated from an original subgraph and a perturbed version, further stratified into four perturbation sub-types (node removal, node replacement, edge removal, edge replacement). Each statement is validated by requiring exact reconstruction of the source subgraph via LLM-based entity and KG extraction, with post-processing (lowercasing, stop-word removal, lemmatization) before comparison.

## Method

The Semantic-KG framework generates benchmarks through four stages: (1) BFS-based subgraph sampling from a KG with type-diversity constraints, (2) targeted perturbation of the subgraph using one of four operations—node/edge removal or replacement—with domain-specific edge-replacement mappings ensuring semantic meaning is altered (e.g., 'increases effect' → 'decreases effect'), (3) LLM-based natural-language generation from the triples using few-shot prompting, and (4) validation via LLM-driven KG reconstruction where a statement is retained only if the reconstructed triples exactly match the original. The key insight is that KG perturbations provide fine-grained, interpretable control over semantic variation without human annotation, enabling scalable benchmark creation for any domain with an available KG. A random number of perturbations (1–70% of nodes) is applied per subgraph to vary the degree of semantic dissimilarity.

## Results

The paper evaluates 15 semantic similarity methods (LLMs, embedding models, and NLP metrics) on the Semantic-KG benchmark across 4 domains (Codex, FinDKG, Globi, Oregano) and 4 perturbation types, reporting F1 scores. No single method is consistently superior: LLMs outperform traditional methods on edge perturbations (GPT-4o x edge-replacement interaction: beta=0.184, p=0.031), while Sentence-T5-Base outperforms most LLMs on node perturbations (node-removal: beta=0.260, p=0.002; node-replacement: beta=0.216, p=0.011). Domain effects are also significant, with Sentence-T5-Base showing a dramatic drop on Globi (beta=-0.413, p=0.002) and ROUGE-1 underperforming on FinDKG (beta=-0.236, p=0.081, not significant). F1 scores range from 0.061 (ROUGE-1 on FinDKG edge-replacement) to 1.000 (Gemini-1.5 Pro on FinDKG edge-replacement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LLM-as-a-judge methods (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5 Pro, Llama-3.1-70B/8B, GPT-3.5), embedding models (Text-embedding-3-Large, ada-002, Sentence-T5-Base, DeBERTa), and NLP metrics (ROUGE-1/2/L, BLEU, BERTScore) on the Semantic-KG benchmark. On edge-replacement perturbations, LLMs clearly dominate: Gemini-1.5 Pro achieves 0.778 (Codex), 1.000 (FinDKG), and 0.974 (Oregano), while ROUGE-1 collapses to 0.061 on FinDKG edge-replacement. Conversely, on node-removal, Sentence-T5-Base (0.971 on Codex, 0.914 on FinDKG) and ROUGE-2 (0.969 on Codex) outperform GPT-4o (0.730 on Codex, 0.857 on FinDKG) and most other LLMs. Text-embedding-3-Large is the most consistent method overall, maintaining F1 above 0.70 across nearly all dataset-perturbation combinations. Llama-3.1-70B tracks closely behind GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5 Pro on edge perturbations but falls behind on node perturbations. The key takeaway is that no method wins across all conditions: the best choice depends on whether the application requires detecting entity-level changes (favoring traditional methods) or relationship-level changes (favoring LLMs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the idea of using knowledge graphs as validation tools for LLMs (as in RAG systems and factual-accuracy benchmarks like [16]) into a benchmark-generation framework, where controlled KG perturbations (node/edge removal and replacement) create semantically similar and dissimilar text pairs without human annotation. It departs from existing STS benchmarks (SemEval, Winograd, MRPC, BIOSSES, MedSTS) that rely on costly and subjective human judgment, and from automated perturbation pipelines ([7], [27], [32]) that operate on surface-level text modifications rather than structured semantic relationships. The key conceptual move is treating the KG as a ground-truth semantic specification, so that perturbation type directly encodes the type of semantic variation being tested.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a scalable, domain-agnostic path to generating semantic-similarity benchmarks for any domain with an available KG, addressing the cost and subjectivity barriers of human-annotated STS datasets. The empirical finding that no single method is consistently superior—LLMs excel at detecting relationship (edge) changes while traditional embedding methods can outperform them on entity (node) changes—has direct practical implications for choosing evaluation metrics in high-stakes LLM deployments. Open questions include whether the closed-world assumption of KGs limits the benchmark's realism, whether more complex perturbations (implicature, tone, context-dependent meaning) would yield different method rankings, and whether the binary task setup adequately captures graded semantic similarity.
