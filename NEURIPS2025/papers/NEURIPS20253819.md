## Problem

Standard generative diffusion bridge models are driven by Brownian motion, which is Markovian with independent increments and cannot capture the long-range temporal dependencies, memory effects, and anomalous diffusion phenomena present in real-world data such as protein conformations and natural images. While fractional Brownian motion (fBM) naturally models these effects via its Hurst index, its bridge drift is intractable, preventing direct use in learning-based bridge construction. This paper bridges that gap by constructing tractable diffusion bridges driven by a Markov approximation of fBM.

## Input / Output

In the paired setting, the method consumes aligned data pairs (x₀, x₁) — e.g., apo and holo 3D Cα atomic coordinates from the D3PM protein dataset, or source/target image pairs — and learns a stochastic SDE whose solution transports x₀ to x₁ while preserving the training coupling. In the unpaired setting, it takes separate samples from source and target distributions (e.g., cat and wild images from AFHQ at 32×32, 256×256, or 512×512 resolutions in pixel or latent space) and produces translated samples. The Hurst index H ∈ (0,1) and the number of OU processes K (fixed at 5 in main experiments) are the key hyperparameters controlling trajectory roughness and long-range correlation structure.

## Method

FDBM replaces the Brownian motion driving noise in diffusion bridges with a Markov-approximate fractional Brownian motion (MA-fBM), defined as a weighted superposition of K Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes all driven by the same standard BM, with L²-optimal coefficients computed in closed form for a given Hurst index H. The non-Markovian reference process X = √ε B̂ᴴ becomes Markovian only after augmenting with the OU state vector Y, yielding the augmented process Z = (X, Y) with linear drift matrix F and diffusion vector G. For paired data, the paper proves existence of a coupling-preserving SDE (generalizing ABM) whose intractable drift is approximated by a time-dependent neural network, trained via a KL-divergence loss computed by sampling from the partially pinned MA-fBB. For unpaired data, it formulates a lifted Schrödinger bridge problem on the augmented space and trains via a generalized α-IMF procedure with a forward-forward strategy. The neural network architecture (DiT for images) is identical to the BM-based baselines; only the input/output scaling via the ζₖ(t,1) coupling functions changes, making the fractional extension computationally nearly free relative to the Brownian baseline.

## Results

On the D3PM protein conformational change task (paired), FDBM with H=0.2 achieves a median RMSD of 2.12 Å and 48% of predictions below the 2 Å threshold, outperforming the ABM baseline (2.40 Å, 43%) and SBALIGN (3.80 Å, 0%). On unpaired image translation (AFHQ-32, DiT-L/2), FDBM achieves a pretraining FID of 35.99 versus SBFlow's 50.68 for cats→wild, and after online finetuning reaches 20.26 versus SBFlow's 33.92. On AFHQ-256 (DiT-L/2), FDBM attains a pretraining FID of 11.62 compared to SBFlow's 16.62 for cats→wild, demonstrating consistent gains across resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SBALIGN (Somnath et al., UAI 2023), FDBM substantially outperforms on all D3PM metrics: median RMSD drops from 3.80 Å to 2.12 Å (H=0.2), and the fraction of predictions below the 2 Å structural-discernibility threshold rises from 0% to 48%. Against Sesame (Miñan et al., ICLR 2025 Workshop), FDBM (H=0.2) achieves a lower median RMSD (2.12 vs. 2.87 Å) and a higher %RMSD<2 (48% vs. 38%), while Sesame retains a slight edge on ΔRMSD median (2.15 vs. 2.44). Against the ABM baseline (Bortoli et al., 2023), FDBM (H=0.2) improves median RMSD from 2.40 to 2.12 Å and %RMSD<2 from 43% to 48%, while FDBM (H=0.3) achieves the best ΔRMSD median (2.52 vs. 2.43), indicating better relative improvement over the starting apo conformation. Against SBFlow (Bortoli et al., 2024) on unpaired image translation, FDBM consistently achieves lower FID: on AFHQ-32 (DiT-L/2) the pretraining FID improves from 50.68 to 35.99 (cats→wild) and the finetuning FID from 33.92 to 20.26; on AFHQ-256 the pretraining FID improves from 16.62 to 11.62. The paper notes that online finetuning for FDBM is reliable only near H=0.5 due to asymmetry between forward and backward bridges, a limitation not present in the BM-based SBFlow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion bridge framework by replacing the standard Brownian motion driving noise with a Markov approximation of fractional Brownian motion (MA-fBM), building directly on Augmented Bridge Matching (ABM) for paired data and Schrödinger Bridge Flow (SBFlow) for unpaired data. The MA-fBM construction follows Daems et al. and Harms & Stefanovits, who showed that fBM can be approximated by a finite superposition of Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes driven by a shared BM, making the augmented process Markovian and the bridge drift computable in closed form. The key departure from prior bridge work is that the Hurst index H becomes a tunable hyperparameter controlling path roughness and long-range temporal correlation, whereas all existing bridge methods (ABM, SBFlow, SBALIGN) are restricted to the memoryless H=0.5 case. The paper also generalizes the reciprocal class and Markovian projection machinery from Peluchetti and Shi et al. to the augmented fractional setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FDBM is the first framework to incorporate fractional (non-Markovian) noise into generative diffusion bridges, opening a principled route to modeling long-range dependencies and anomalous diffusion in generative tasks. The practical gains are modest but consistent: in protein conformation prediction, rougher dynamics (H=0.2) improve the fraction of near-native predictions (RMSD<2Å) from 43% to 48% over ABM, and in unpaired image translation, FDBM with DiT-L/2 achieves substantially lower FID than SBFlow on AFHQ-32. Key limitations include the absence of convergence guarantees for the fractional Schrödinger bridge, the observation that online finetuning only works reliably near H=0.5 (where the forward and backward bridges nearly coincide with the Brownian case), and the fact that MA-fBM with H=0.5 and K>1 does not exactly recover BM. Open directions include theoretical guarantees for fractional Schrödinger bridges, principled finetuning of asymmetric bridges, and extensions to manifold-valued processes.
