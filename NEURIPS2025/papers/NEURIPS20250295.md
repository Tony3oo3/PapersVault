## Problem

Existing approaches to inferring animal learning rules assume fixed parametric forms (e.g., Q-learning, REINFORCE) that may not capture the complex, history-dependent learning dynamics animals actually employ during de novo task acquisition. Prior flexible neural-network-based methods have focused on bandit-style tasks with fixed structure, leaving the inference of nonparametric, non-Markovian learning rules from scratch in de novo settings largely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250295/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of trial-by-trial behavioral observations—signed stimulus contrast s_t ∈ [-1,1], binary wheel-turn choice y_t ∈ {0,1}, and binary reward r_t—along with the current GLM weight vector w_t ∈ R^d (initialized from psychometric curve estimates). It outputs a learned weight-update function Δw_t that, when iteratively applied, produces the full GLM weight trajectory over trials and, through the sigmoid GLM, predicted choice probabilities for held-out animals. The DNN variant takes only current-trial covariates as input, while the RNN variant additionally integrates arbitrary-length trial history through a GRU hidden state.

## Method

The framework separates the decision policy (a dynamic Bernoulli GLM with interpretable weights w_t) from the learning rule (the mapping from task covariates to per-trial weight updates Δw_t), parameterizing the latter with a feedforward DNN for Markovian rules or a GRU-based RNN for non-Markovian, history-dependent rules. In the DNN variant, a two-hidden-layer network maps (w_t, x_t, y_t, r_t) directly to Δw_t; in the RNN variant, a GRU first encodes the full trial history into a hidden state h_t, which a feedforward head then maps to Δw_t. Both models are trained end-to-end by maximizing the log-likelihood of observed binary choices (equivalently, minimizing binary cross-entropy) via gradient descent, with the GLM weights evolving as w_{t+1} = w_t + Δw_t. This modular design preserves interpretability of the decision policy while allowing the learning rule to take arbitrary data-driven forms, and the recurrent architecture specifically captures multi-trial reward-history dependencies that memoryless DNNs cannot represent.

## Results

On the IBL mouse learning dataset (N=12 animals), RNNGLM achieves the best held-out log-likelihood of −59892, outperforming DNNGLM (−60360, p=1.79e-8), REINFORCE (−60576, p=1.57e-2), and tinyRNN (−60564, p=2.12e-3). In simulations, DNNGLM recovers the ground-truth REINFORCE learning rule with reconstruction error (log₁₀ RMSE) decreasing monotonically with dataset size, while RNNGLM uniquely captures non-Markovian reward-history dependencies that DNNGLM cannot represent. The inferred learning rules reveal a 'negative baseline' (weight decreases after errors) and multi-trial reward-history sensitivity, both absent from classical parametric rules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two primary baselines: the classical REINFORCE policy-gradient rule [Williams 1992] and the tinyRNN architecture [Li et al. 2023]. Both DNNGLM and RNNGLM significantly outperform REINFORCE on held-out log-likelihood (−60360 and −59892 vs. −60576, respectively), with RNNGLM gaining an additional ~468 LL points over DNNGLM (p=1.79e-8). RNNGLM also outperforms tinyRNN (−59892 vs. −60564, p=2.12e-3), which directly models behavior via RNNs rather than parameterizing GLM weight updates. Notably, RNNGLM also beats history-augmented variants of both baselines (REINFORCE-history: −60504; DNNGLM-history: −60168), indicating that the recurrent architecture captures multi-trial dependencies that simple one-trial history extensions cannot. Enforcing a non-negativity constraint on the baseline (removing the 'negative baseline' effect) degrades performance to −68532, confirming the functional importance of the inferred suboptimal learning dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on inferring learning rules from animal behavioral data (Ashwood et al. 2020; Geadah & Pillow 2025), which previously assumed fixed parametric forms such as REINFORCE or Q-learning. It departs from prior RNN-based cognitive model discovery (Li et al. 2023; Miller et al. 2023) by targeting de novo task learning rather than bandit-style adaptation, and by keeping an interpretable Bernoulli GLM for the decision policy while using neural networks solely to parametrize the weight-update function. The GLM decision framework and weight-tracking philosophy build directly on Roy et al. (Neuron 2021) and PsyTrack (Roy et al. 2018), and the non-Markovian RNN variant is motivated by eligibility-trace-like dynamics in the RL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first nonparametric, non-Markovian learning-rule inference for de novo tasks, revealing reward-history-dependent dynamics (larger updates after rewarded sequences) and a 'negative baseline' that deviate from classical policy-gradient assumptions. Key limitations include pooling across animals (obscuring individual variability), a deterministic and static update function, evaluation on a single relatively simple task (IBL sensory discrimination), and the under-constrained nature of inferring learning rules from binary choices alone. Open questions include whether the inferred inductive biases generalize across tasks and architectures, how to jointly infer the decision model and learning rule, and how to capture stochasticity and nonstationarity in learning.
