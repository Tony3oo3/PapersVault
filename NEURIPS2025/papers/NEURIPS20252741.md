## Problem

Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA) provides superior KV cache compression and inference speed compared to GQA, but most model providers have heavily invested in GQA-based architectures and lack incentive to retrain MLA models from scratch. The key obstacle to conversion is that the Absorb operation—critical for inference acceleration—is blocked because every query/key head carries its own RoPE, preventing the low-rank factorization that MLA relies on. This paper asks whether existing GQA models can be seamlessly converted to MLA while inheriting their pretraining effort.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252741/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained GQA model checkpoint (LLaMA, Qwen, Gemma, Mistral/Mixtral) and a small calibration dataset used to compute PCA on key/value activations. It produces a DeepSeek-compatible MLA model checkpoint whose KV cache is compressed by up to 93%, directly loadable into inference engines like vLLM and SGLang without custom kernels. The output model supports the Absorb operation, enabling seamless switching between compute-intensive (MHA-like) and memory-efficient (MQA-like) execution modes during inference.

## Method

TransMLA converts GQA to MLA through three key techniques. First, RoRoPE applies PCA on key activations across all g grouped heads for each RoPE frequency subspace, computing orthogonal rotation matrices that concentrate the dominant positional signal into the first head while provably preserving the attention inner product (via invariance under orthogonal transforms applied identically to real and imaginary RoPE components). Second, FreqFold exploits the similarity of adjacent RoPE frequencies (θ_l ≈ θ_{l+1}) to group nearby subspaces, enabling PCA over a larger combined latent space for sharper concentration. Third, Balanced Key-Value (BKV) rescales K_nope and V to matched ℓ₂-norms before joint PCA, preventing the key-dominated subspace from starving the value representation during low-rank compression. The merged latent head structure makes the model structurally compatible with DeepSeek's Absorb operation, and only 0.5–6B tokens of fine-tuning are needed to recover near-original performance.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B, TransMLA compresses the KV cache by 68.75% with only a 1.65% average benchmark drop (58.20 vs. 59.85 original) in the training-free setting, compared to MHA2MLA's 21.85% drop (37.90) at the same compression ratio. After fine-tuning on 6B tokens at 92.97% KV compression, TransMLA recovers to 59.19 average (0.66% below original). On SmolLM-1.7B, TransMLA at 87.50% compression with 1B tokens achieves 54.01 average, surpassing MHA2MLA's 54.76 at 68.75% compression with 6B tokens. Inference speedup reaches 10× at 8K context length on a 165 TFLOPS / 24 GB accelerator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is MHA2MLA [Ji et al., 2025], which also converts MHA/GQA models to MLA. In the training-free setting, TransMLA dramatically outperforms MHA2MLA: on LLaMA-2-7B at 68.75% KV compression, TransMLA scores 58.20 average versus MHA2MLA's 37.90 (a 20.3-point gap), and on SmolLM-1.7B at the same ratio, 51.95 vs. 40.97. After 6B tokens of fine-tuning, TransMLA at 92.97% compression (59.19 avg) nearly matches MHA2MLA at only 68.75% compression (59.51 avg), meaning TransMLA achieves equivalent quality at roughly 3× higher compression. The paper also reports that TransMLA at 7.03% remaining KV cache outperforms MHA2MLA at 31.25% remaining cache in the training-free setting. Crucially, TransMLA reports actual inference speedups (up to 10× at 8K context via vLLM), whereas MHA2MLA reports only compression ratios without demonstrating end-to-end speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

TransMLA builds directly on DeepSeek-V2/V3's MLA architecture—specifically its Absorb operation and Decoupled RoPE scheme—and on the GQA formulation, merging grouped KV heads into a single latent representation. It departs from the concurrent MHA2MLA work by replacing norm-based RoPE dimension selection with a PCA-driven orthogonal rotation (RoRoPE) plus frequency-similarity grouping (FreqFold), and by introducing a norm-balancing step (BKV) before joint low-rank compression of K_nope and V. Unlike post-training KV compression methods (Duo-Attention, KiVi, H2O, Palu) that require custom kernels, TransMLA produces a checkpoint that loads directly into DeepSeek's existing inference stack (vLLM, SGLang), inheriting all their optimizations without architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first practical, low-cost migration path for the large ecosystem of GQA-based models (LLaMA, Qwen, Gemma, Mistral) to benefit from DeepSeek's highly-optimized MLA inference tooling without retraining from scratch—only ~6B tokens of fine-tuning are needed to recover near-original performance after 93% KV cache compression. The theoretical proof that MLA strictly subsumes GQA in expressiveness at equal KV budget gives a principled rationale for the migration. Open questions include: MLA's weaker compatibility with tensor parallelism (requiring data parallelism instead), error accumulation on very long contexts, and whether the norm-disparity between keys and values can be resolved more elegantly to enable truly training-free conversion.
