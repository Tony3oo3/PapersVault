## Problem

Federated Learning suffers from data heterogeneity (label skew and domain shift) across clients, leading to inconsistent local objectives, slow convergence, and degraded global model accuracy. Existing model-driven methods cannot fundamentally eliminate the bias caused by heterogeneous local data, while data-driven generative approaches either expose sensitive client information to the server or are constrained by limited client-side computational resources and knowledge scope.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250046/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Clients provide a small set of loss-selected representative images (224×224×3) that are captioned locally via BLIP into token sequences, which are the only data transmitted to the server—no raw images or latent features are shared. The server produces synthetic images via Stable Diffusion, which are distributed back to clients for local dataset augmentation and simultaneously used to fine-tune the aggregated global model (ResNet-18/10). A server-maintained dynamic data pool (size 25 per class) stores generated samples with their text prompts for cross-round retrieval.

## Method

Flick introduces two phases into the FL workflow: (1) a local summary phase where each client selects representative samples via a loss-based criterion (highest-loss sample for above-average classes, lowest-loss for below-average classes) and captions them with a pre-trained VLP model, converting sensitive visual information into low-sensitivity text tokens; and (2) a server-side data generation phase where an out-of-the-box LLM (GPT-4o-mini) analyzes the pooled cross-client captions through a designed prompt template, fusing client-specific insights with inherent commonsense knowledge to produce class-specific text prompts. These prompts drive a latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5) to synthesize images, with a Sentence-BERT-based retrieval mechanism reusing historical samples to reduce generation cost. A decision matrix, built from per-class validation accuracy of local models on the server-held pool, determines which clients receive which synthetic classes. Generated samples serve dual purposes: compensating local data distributions (progressively approaching IID) and fine-tuning the aggregated global model, with the server pool updated dynamically each round.

## Results

On PACS (domain shift + label skew, 20 clients), Flick achieves 94.49% top-1 accuracy, improving over vanilla FedAvg (83.06%) by 11.43% and reducing convergence from 142 to 11 rounds (12.9× faster). On Office-Caltech (8 clients), Flick reaches 78.51% accuracy (+7.67% over FedAvg's 70.84%) with 37 rounds vs. 135. On the large-scale DomainNet benchmark (100 clients, 20% participation per round), Flick attains 85.76% accuracy (+11.63% over FedAvg) in just 11 rounds, outperforming all 11 baselines across every dataset and baseline combination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flick dominates all 12 baselines across both PACS and Office-Caltech under the combined domain-shift + label-skew setting. The closest competitor is FGL (also a server-side generative method using client captions), which Flick outperforms by 4.65% on PACS (94.49% vs. 89.84%) and 4.69% on Office-Caltech (78.51% vs. 73.82%), while also requiring roughly half the rounds (11 vs. 22 on PACS). Model-driven methods (FedBN, FedHEAL, FedProx, FedDyn, FedNAR) consistently trail Flick by 4–11% in accuracy; FedHEAL is the strongest model-driven baseline at 87.88% on PACS but still lags Flick by 6.61%. Server-side generative methods DynaFed and FedFTG show high variance and even degrade below the FedAvg baseline on Office-Caltech (−2.92% and −1.76% respectively), whereas Flick maintains consistent gains. Against client-side generative methods (FRAug, Gen-FedSD, AutoAugment), Flick achieves higher accuracy (94.49% vs. 93.73% for Gen-FedSD on PACS) with fewer rounds (11 vs. 19) and no client-side generation overhead. On DomainNet with 100 clients, Flick extends its lead, improving accuracy by up to 11.63% and accelerating convergence by up to 10.36× over the respective baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flick carves new ground at the intersection of data-driven heterogeneous FL and LLM-assisted generation. Prior data-driven methods either generate samples locally on clients (FRAug, Gen-FedSD) or on the server using knowledge distilled from the global model (DynaFed, FedFTG), while FGL is the closest server-side generative approach but requires clients to caption all local images, incurring high overhead and privacy risk. Flick departs by introducing a privacy-conscious local summary phase (selective loss-based captioning) that feeds cross-client-specific knowledge into an out-of-the-box LLM, which then fuses this with its inherent commonsense to produce text prompts for a server-side diffusion model. The retrieval-based caching of historical synthetic samples via SBERT similarity is an additional efficiency mechanism not present in prior generative FL frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Flick offers a practical recipe for FL practitioners facing both label skew and domain shift: it offloads computationally intensive generation to the server while keeping client-side overhead minimal (only a few captioned samples per round), and the 12.9× convergence speedup makes it attractive for communication-constrained settings. However, the approach depends on cloud-based LLM API access (GPT-4o-mini), which introduces its own privacy and cost considerations that the paper acknowledges but does not fully resolve. Open questions include whether the commonsense-knowledge infusion generalizes beyond image classification to other modalities or tasks, whether the privacy guarantees hold under adversarial caption-inversion attacks, and whether the framework remains effective when the LLM's commonsense is misaligned with the target domain.
