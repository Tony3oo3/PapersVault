## Problem

Existing multimodal dataset distillation (MDD) methods suffer from Modality Collapse, where distilled image-text pairs exhibit over-concentrated intra-modal representations and an enlarged inter-modal gap, degrading cross-modal retrieval. The paper identifies this as stemming from a fundamental conflict between the over-compressive nature of dataset distillation and the cross-modal contrastive supervision, which mutually reinforce representation concentration. Additionally, prior MDD approaches impose asymmetric supervision across modalities, with the image branch receiving significantly weaker update signals than the text branch.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale image-text dataset (e.g., Flickr-30K with ~31k pairs, MS-COCO with ~123k pairs, or LLaVA-cc3m with ~558k pairs) and a CLIP-style architecture with frozen pretrained encoders (NFNet/RegNet/ResNet-50/ViT for images; BERT/DistilBERT for text) plus randomly initialized projection heads. It also requires 20 pre-collected expert training trajectories (10 epochs each) over the projection heads. The output is a compact synthetic dataset of 100–800 image-text pairs with learned soft correspondence labels, where the synthetic images are raw pixel tensors and synthetic texts are token-level representations, both optimized jointly under a weighted binary cross-entropy contrastive loss.

## Method

RepBlend introduces two key mechanisms. First, Representation Blending replaces random noise perturbation with in-distribution mixing of representations from different synthetic instances in the embedding space (analogous to MixUp but applied post-encoder, pre-projection), which weakens the overly dominant cross-modal supervision signal and restores intra-modal diversity without introducing semantically meaningless perturbations. Second, Symmetric Projection Trajectory Matching replaces the asymmetric protocol (matching the full image encoder trajectory against the text projection head trajectory) with matching both image and text projection head trajectories, ensuring balanced optimization dynamics and stronger cross-modal alignment. Theoretically, the paper proves that cross-modal contrastive gradients guide updates along directions determined by positive-pair text embeddings, reinforcing intra-modal concentration; blending disrupts this alignment. The entire pipeline freezes the encoders and optimizes only the projection heads and synthetic data, yielding a 6.7× distillation speedup over LoRS while achieving +9.4 IR@10 and +6.3 TR@10 under the 100-pair setting on Flickr-30K.

## Results

On Flickr-30K under the 100-pair (0.3%) setting, RepBlend achieves IR@1 = 11.5% and TR@10 = 55.5%, surpassing the SOTA baseline LoRS (8.3% IR@1, 49.2% TR@10) by +3.2 and +6.3 points respectively. At the 500-pair budget, gains widen to IR@10 = 55.9% (vs. 41.6% for LoRS) and TR@10 = 66.7% (vs. 53.7%). On MS-COCO (100 pairs), RepBlend reaches IR@10 = 22.3% and TR@10 = 28.0%, nearly doubling LoRS's 12.2% and 19.6%. Additionally, the projection-based design yields a 6.7× distillation speedup (1.71 s/iter vs. 11.5 s/iter) and a 2.14× reduction in peak GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RepBlend consistently outperforms all five baseline families across every distillation budget and both datasets. Against the primary SOTA competitor LoRS (ICML 2024), RepBlend gains +3.2 IR@1 and +6.3 TR@10 on Flickr-30K at 100 pairs, and +14.3 IR@10 and +13.0 TR@10 at 500 pairs; on MS-COCO the 100-pair gap is +10.1 IR@10 and +8.4 TR@10. MTT-VL (TMLR 2024) and TESLA-VL (ICML 2024) are substantially weaker, with TESLA-VL sometimes underperforming even random sampling (e.g., 0.1 IR@1 vs. 0.6 for Random on MS-COCO at 200 pairs). Coreset selection methods (Random, Herding, K-Center, Forgetting) serve as lower bounds and are outperformed by 5–20× in retrieval accuracy. Beyond accuracy, RepBlend also achieves a 6.7× distillation speedup and 2.14× memory reduction over LoRS by replacing full-encoder trajectory matching with lightweight projection-head matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal dataset distillation (MDD) line initiated by MTT-VL (Wu et al., TMLR 2024) and advanced by LoRS (Xu et al., ICML 2024), which adapted trajectory-matching-based distillation to CLIP-style image-text pairs. The key conceptual departure is a diagnostic reframing: rather than treating MDD as a straightforward extension of unimodal DD, the authors identify a fundamental conflict between the over-compressive nature of distillation and the cross-modal contrastive supervision, formalizing it as 'modality collapse.' Methodologically, RepBlend introduces representation-space blending (analogous to MixUp but applied to encoder outputs) and symmetric projection-head trajectory matching, both of which modify the LoRS optimization protocol rather than its loss formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of modality collapse as a structural optimization pathology—rather than a mere tuning issue—gives the MDD community a concrete diagnostic and a principled remedy, potentially guiding future work beyond the specific RepBlend recipe. The 6.7× distillation speedup from switching to projection-based matching is a practically significant efficiency gain that lowers the barrier to MDD experimentation. However, the authors acknowledge that current MDD remains limited to pair-level modeling without fine-grained token-object alignment, and insufficient cross-instance interaction constrains further compression gains—open questions that will require instance-aware or relation-enhanced strategies.
