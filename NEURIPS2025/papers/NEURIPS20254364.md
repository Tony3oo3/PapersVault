## Problem

Designing protein sequences that are both high-fitness and novel beyond wild-type neighborhoods is hampered by surrogate misspecification in out-of-distribution regions and the risk of generating biologically implausible sequences. Local methods like PEX avoid these issues but miss distant high-fitness regions, while global exploration strategies (RL, GFNs) degrade when the surrogate loses fidelity. Incorporating pre-trained generative models with rich biological priors into iterative optimization workflows has been impractical due to the need for task-specific fine-tuning on limited data.

## Input / Output

PROSPERO takes an initial dataset of protein sequences with oracle-assigned fitness scores (ranging from ~2,600 to ~15,000 sequences of length 75–439), a frozen pre-trained EvoDiff-OADM generative model (38M parameters), and a black-box oracle (TAPE or FLEXS). Each of N=10 active learning rounds evaluates K=128 candidate sequences against the oracle, with the surrogate (an ensemble of three 1D CNNs) retrained on the growing dataset each round. The output is a set of novel protein sequences ranked by oracle fitness, with the top 100 used to compute maximum/mean fitness, novelty (Hamming distance from wild-type), and diversity (pairwise Hamming distance).

## Method

PROSPERO formulates iterative protein design as inference-time guidance of a frozen pre-trained generative model (EvoDiff) by a surrogate updated in an active learning loop, eliminating the need for task-specific fine-tuning. Two key innovations drive the approach: (1) targeted masking, inspired by alanine scanning, which constructs batches of alanine-substituted variants of the current best sequence, scores them with the surrogate's UCB acquisition function to identify fitness-relevant yet mutation-tolerant positions, and masks only those positions for completion; and (2) biologically-constrained Sequential Monte Carlo sampling, which restricts EvoDiff's unmasking proposals to amino acids in the same charge class as the wild-type residue (positive: R/K/H, negative: D/E, neutral: all others), then computes importance weights by rolling out each partial sequence with the constrained model, scoring it with the surrogate, and correcting for the constraint via the unconstrained model's perplexity. Resampling at each unmasking step progressively concentrates the particle population on high-fitness, biologically plausible sequences, enabling robust exploration even under surrogate misspecification.

## Results

PROSPERO achieves the highest maximum fitness on 5 out of 8 protein engineering tasks (E4B: 8.114, AAV: 0.720, GFP: 3.617, UBE2I: 2.993, LGK: 0.043) and ranks second on the remaining 3, improving over wild-type fitness on every task. In terms of novelty (average Hamming distance from the starting sequence), PROSPERO ranks first on 6 out of 8 tasks, achieving 2–9× greater novelty than PEX while matching or exceeding its fitness. Only PROSPERO and PEX achieve fitness improvements over the wild-type across all eight landscapes; four baselines (CMA-ES, DyNaPPO, CbAS, GFN-AL) fail to improve on any task. Under severe covariate shift (75 mutations from wild-type), PROSPERO attains the highest mean pTM (0.599) versus the next-best MLDE (0.572), demonstrating robustness to surrogate misspecification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROSPERO outperforms all nine baselines on maximum fitness for 5 of 8 tasks and is competitive (within 0.01–0.05) on the remaining 3. PEX [6] is the closest competitor on fitness (second on AMIE, TEM, Pab1) but achieves only 1–9 mutations of novelty versus PROSPERO's 3–74, confirming that local exploration cannot match PROSPERO's fitness-novelty trade-off. AdaLead [7] wins on Pab1 (1.978 vs. 1.527) but trails on every other task and generates far less novel sequences. GFN-AL-δCS [12] and LatProtRL [10] are competitive on fitness for several tasks but produce substantially lower novelty (e.g., LatProtRL: 1.09 on AMIE vs. PROSPERO's 20.99). MLDE [13] matches PROSPERO on LGK fitness (0.038 vs. 0.043) but lags on novelty (50.09 vs. 74.33). CMA-ES [16], DyNaPPO [8], CbAS [14], and GFN-AL [9] fail to improve over wild-type on most or all tasks, with CbAS and GFN-AL producing negative fitness on several landscapes. Under the severe covariate shift setting (75 mutations from wild-type), PROSPERO's advantage widens: it achieves 0.672 max pTM versus 0.652 for MLDE and 0.630 for GFN-AL-δCS, while also exploring the most novel region (22.03 novelty vs. 21.68 for MLDE). The ablation confirms that the charge-class RAA constraint is the primary source of robustness at low SNR, while SMC guidance and targeted masking drive gains at higher SNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROSPERO sits at the intersection of active learning for protein design (FLEXS, PEX, AdaLead), inference-time guidance of generative models (classifier guidance, SMC-based decoding), and reduced amino acid alphabets for biological constraints. It departs from prior approaches in two key ways: rather than training new policies (as in DyNaPPO, LatProtRL, or GFN-AL-δCS) or retraining generative models each cycle (as in CbAS), it keeps a pre-trained generative model (EvoDiff) frozen and steers it at inference time via a surrogate-updated SMC procedure. The targeted masking strategy, inspired by alanine scanning, replaces the random masking used in GFN-AL-δCS and MLDE, while the charge-class constraint on SMC proposals introduces an explicit biological prior absent from prior surrogate-guided exploration methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PROSPERO addresses a core tension in protein engineering: exploring beyond wild-type neighborhoods for novelty while maintaining biological plausibility and robustness to surrogate misspecification. The framework's reliance on a frozen pre-trained model makes it straightforward to adopt without task-specific fine-tuning, and the charge-class constraint provides a simple, interpretable mechanism for robustness that could be extended to other physicochemical groupings. Limitations include the computational cost of deterministic runs (~30 hours vs. ~3 hours), the inherent diversity risk of SMC resampling, and the fact that the charge-based grouping, while universal, may be suboptimal for specific proteins. Open questions include whether adaptive alphabet reduction or structure-informed masking (e.g., integrating 3D alanine scanning) could further improve performance in lab-in-the-loop settings.
