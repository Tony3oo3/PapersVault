## Problem

Existing fMRI-to-text decoding frameworks process the entire fMRI sequence in a single step, causing memory overload and semantic drift as input length grows. These methods also ignore the human brain's dual cognitive strategy of incremental word-by-word processing combined with periodic segmental integration, which is critical for managing working memory during language comprehension.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255015/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes cortical-surface fMRI time series (T frames, each TR seconds) recorded during naturalistic auditory language comprehension and produces open-vocabulary natural language text. The fMRI sequence is segmented into fixed-length chunks of 20 TRs (each covering multiple words), and text is generated segment-by-segment with a semantic summary propagated between consecutive segments, rather than decoding the full sequence in one pass.

## Method

CogReader comprises two stages: (A) a text-guided masked autoencoder for fMRI representation learning, and (B) a brain-inspired sequential decoder. In stage A, a spatial-temporal Transformer encoder is pretrained on HCP with random 75% masking, then fine-tuned on Narratives using a text-guided masking strategy where BERT attention scores over stimulus words determine which fMRI frames are semantically salient—top-40% frames receive 75% vertex masking while the rest receive only 25%, forcing the model to reconstruct neural activity at key semantic moments. In stage B, the fMRI sequence is divided into 20-TR segments; each segment is encoded and decoded incrementally by a BART model, and after each segment a BERT-based wrap-up module summarizes the decoded text into a compact semantic vector (via an MLP) that is injected into the next segment's fMRI representation, enabling cross-segment semantic continuity and reducing the effective memory burden of long-form decoding.

## Results

On the Narratives dataset at 60 TR decoding length, CogReader achieves BLEU-1 of 36.2%, BLEU-4 of 12.1%, ROUGE-F of 36.2%, and BERTScore-F of 53.5%, outperforming all baselines across every metric and decoding length. The performance advantage grows substantially with sequence length: at 20 TR the BLEU-1 gap over the best baseline (PREDFT, 24.3%) is ~11 points, while at 60 TR it widens to ~10 points over PREDFT (26.4%) and ~18 points over UniCoRN (18%). On the Huth dataset at 60 TR, CogReader similarly leads with BLEU-1 of 35.2% and BERTScore-F of 53.9%, versus the next-best EEG-Text at 23.6% and 47.2% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogReader outperforms all four baselines (UniCoRN, EEG-Text, BP-GPT, PREDFT) on every metric at every decoding length on both the Narratives and Huth datasets. The most notable finding is that while baselines degrade as decoding length increases from 20 to 60 TR (e.g., UniCoRN's BLEU-1 drops from 22.9 to 18.0; BP-GPT's BERTScore-F drops from 44.1 to 41.6), CogReader's performance improves (BLEU-1 rises from 25.4 to 36.2; BERTScore-F from 46.3 to 53.5), demonstrating that the sequential wrap-up mechanism effectively mitigates the memory-overload and semantic-drift problems. At 60 TR on Narratives, CogReader leads the closest competitor PREDFT by 9.8 BLEU-1 points (36.2 vs. 26.4) and 5.4 BERTScore-F points (53.5 vs. 48.1), and leads EEG-Text by 14.1 BLEU-1 points. On the Huth dataset at 60 TR, the margin is even larger: CogReader achieves 35.2 BLEU-1 versus EEG-Text's 23.6 and PREDFT's 22.6. The noise-data experiment (Table 5) further confirms that CogReader's gains are not merely due to LLM memorization, as performance drops sharply when real fMRI is replaced with noise input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fMRI-to-text decoding line initiated by UniCoRN (which treats fMRI as a 'foreign language' for BART-style translation) and BP-GPT (prompt-based LLM conditioning), but departs from their single-step whole-sequence decoding paradigm by introducing a brain-inspired sequential framework with incremental per-segment decoding and a wrap-up summarization mechanism. On the representation learning side, it modifies the standard MAE framework (He et al., 2022) by replacing random masking with a text-guided strategy that uses BERT attention scores to selectively mask fMRI frames at semantically salient time points. The cognitive motivation—mimicking human incremental processing and segmental integration during language comprehension—distinguishes it conceptually from prior work that treats fMRI-to-text as a cross-modal translation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated limitation: existing fMRI-to-text decoders degrade on long sequences due to memory overload and semantic drift, and the sequential wrap-up approach offers a principled solution grounded in cognitive science. The noise-data experiment (Table 5) provides useful validation that the model genuinely extracts semantic information from fMRI rather than relying on LLM memorization, directly responding to concerns raised by Jo et al. (2024). Key limitations include the static (non-adaptive) segmentation strategy, evaluation on only two datasets, and the inherent low temporal resolution of fMRI; future work on content-adaptive segmentation and fMRI-EEG multimodal integration are natural next steps.
