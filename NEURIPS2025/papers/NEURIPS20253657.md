## Problem

Autoregressive pre-training of medical MLLMs like LLaVA-Med is highly data-hungry: reducing instruction-following data from 100% to 10% causes a dramatic performance drop (72.64% → 52.39% on VQA-RAD), revealing weak vision-language alignment that depends on large volumes of costly curated data. Existing approaches address this by scaling model size or data volume, but the paper shows the alignment mechanism itself is the bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253657/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes medical images (chest X-ray, CT, MRI, histopathology, gross pathology) processed through CLIP-ViT-L-Patch14 into patch embeddings, paired with text questions tokenized for LLaMA-7B. During pre-training, GPT-4 generates extended context captions from original answers, which are used only to guide latent-space alignment and are excluded at inference. The model outputs free-text answers for VQA (open- and closed-ended) and class labels for zero-shot classification across 23 datasets.

## Method

EXGRA-MED constructs three modality-specific graphs (visual, original-answer text, GPT-4-extended text) from batch embeddings, with edges formed via k-nearest neighbors and node features enriched by a 2-layer GCN message-passing step. A barycenter graph—defined directly from known triplet pairs—reformulates the K-way alignment into K separate pairwise quadratic assignment problems, each solved with Lagrange decomposition heuristics. Gradients flow through the discrete solver via IMLE (Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation), which estimates gradients by differencing solutions of noise-perturbed alignments, enabling end-to-end training of the full LLaMA-7B backbone. The alignment loss (Hamming distance between solver output and ground-truth triplet mapping) is jointly optimized with the standard autoregressive language modeling loss, weighted by a coefficient α. The paper also proves that the resulting SGA distance defines a valid metric on the space of structured graphs and that this space is geodesic.

## Results

EXGRA-MED pre-trained on only 10% of LLaVA-Med's instruction-following data achieves 72.52% average on VQA-RAD, essentially matching LLaVA-Med trained on 100% data (72.64%) while LLaVA-Med at 10% drops to 52.39%. At full (100%) pre-training, EXGRA-MED reaches 74.91% (VQA-RAD), 85.46% (SLAKE), and 63.87% (PathVQA), yielding a 74.75% overall score. The enhanced EXGRA-MED (DCI) variant further improves to 75.25% on VQA-RAD and 64.82% on PathVQA (75.1% overall), outperforming the 40B-parameter Med-Dr across all three VQA benchmarks despite using only 7B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EXGRA-MED (100%) outperforms LLaVA-Med by +2.01% on VQA-RAD, +2.03% on SLAKE, and +0.76% on PathVQA, and surpasses the 40B Med-Dr by a wide margin (+16.7% on VQA-RAD, +6.7% on SLAKE, +2.0% on PathVQA) despite being 5.7× smaller. Against contrastive pre-training baselines at 10% data, EXGRA-MED leads InfoNCE (72.52 vs 68.48 on VQA-RAD), SigLIP (72.52 vs 67.47), VLAP (72.52 vs 66.98), GeoCLAP (72.52 vs 68.03), PAC-S (72.52 vs 65.26), and IMAGEBIND (72.52 vs 66.53), while PLOT collapses to 21.67%. At 100% data, EXGRA-MED also beats GPT-4 (200B) on VQA-RAD (74.91 vs 59.2) and SLAKE (85.46 vs 38.6), and outperforms Med-MoE (Phi2, 3.6B) by +4.3% on VQA-RAD and +0.1% on SLAKE. The DCI variant further extends the PathVQA lead to 64.82% vs LLaVA-Med's 64.06% and BioMed-CLIP's unreported score. Notably, EXGRA-MED's advantage is most pronounced on open-ended questions (e.g., 66.35 vs 63.65 on VQA-RAD open), suggesting the graph alignment improves semantic grounding beyond simple pattern matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA-Med architecture (LLaMA-7B + CLIP-ViT + MLP projector) and its two-stage training pipeline, but departs from the standard autoregressive-only pre-training objective by introducing a multi-graph alignment loss. The alignment mechanism draws on the Wasserstein barycenter concept to reformulate K-graph matching into K separate pairwise alignments, solved via Lagrange decomposition heuristics, with gradients backpropagated through the discrete solver using Implicit MLE (IMLE). Unlike prior vision-language alignment methods (BLIP-2, LLaVA) that train only lightweight projectors between frozen encoders, EXGRA-MED trains the LLM itself through the graph alignment objective, and unlike contrastive multi-modal methods (PAC-S, GeoCLAP, ImageBind) that extend InfoNCE to multiple modalities, it imposes structural graph constraints (k-NN edges + message passing) that capture relational consistency beyond pairwise feature similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's key practical finding—that autoregressive pre-training for medical MLLMs suffers a ~20% performance drop when data is reduced to 10%, while multi-graph alignment recovers most of this gap—offers a concrete data-efficiency lever for medical AI practitioners who cannot afford to curate massive instruction-following datasets. The theoretical contributions (proving the SGA distance is a valid metric and that the structured graph space is geodesic) provide mathematical grounding for the alignment objective. However, limitations remain: the method is validated only on the LLaVA architecture, relies on GPT-4 for extended caption generation (introducing cost and potential hallucination), and the generalization to other architectures (Flamingo, medical-specific vision encoders) or to chain-of-thought medical reasoning is left as future work.
