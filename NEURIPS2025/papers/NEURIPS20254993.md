## Problem

Dual encoders (DEs) are the standard architecture for scalable information retrieval, but their Euclidean geometry imposes constraints that make hierarchical retrieval (HR)—where relevant documents are all ancestors of a query in a hidden DAG—particularly challenging. The paper addresses two gaps: (1) proving that asymmetric DEs can in principle solve HR despite these geometric constraints, and (2) identifying and fixing the 'lost-in-the-long-distance' phenomenon, where learned embeddings systematically fail to retrieve documents further up the hierarchy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text queries and a document set whose underlying hierarchical structure (a DAG) is unobserved; training data consists of matching query-document pairs sampled from the hierarchy without explicit path-length labels. The output is a set of d-dimensional query and document embeddings whose inner products yield relevance scores, from which a ranked list is produced. A key constraint is that the same node is embedded differently by the query encoder and document encoder (asymmetric DE), and the relevant set for a query includes not just its exact match but all ancestors up to a bounded distance.

## Method

The paper first proves via a constructive algorithm (Algorithm 1) that asymmetric DEs solve HR with embedding dimension O(max{s log m, (1/ε²) log m}), where s is the max number of relevant documents per query and m is the document set size. For practical training, the authors identify that standard softmax-loss training with in-batch negatives suffers from lost-in-the-long-distance: recall degrades sharply for query-document pairs separated by more edges in the hierarchy. Their pretrain-finetune recipe first trains on regular-sampled data (all distances) to establish a good baseline, then finetunes exclusively on heavy-tail-sampled data where long-distance pairs are upsampled proportionally to their distance, with a reduced learning rate and increased temperature. This two-stage approach preserves short-distance recall near 100% while boosting long-distance recall (e.g., from 19% to 76% on WordNet), whereas naive rebalanced sampling trades short-distance quality for long-distance gains without achieving both simultaneously.

## Results

On WordNet (82,115 noun synsets) with 64-dimensional embeddings, the pretrain-finetune recipe achieves 92.3% overall recall, compared to 71.4% for regular sampling and 41.8–40.7% for rebalanced sampling. The headline improvement is on long-distance pairs: recall at distance 8 jumps from 19.4% (regular) to 75.7% (pretrain-finetune), while distance-0 recall is preserved at 100%. On the HyperLex benchmark, pretrain-finetune achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.415, outperforming the best prior method (WN-Euclidean, 0.389). On the ESCI shopping dataset, pretrain-finetune outperforms joint training on both Exact and Substitute recall@k for k ∈ {100, 1000, 10000}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against regular sampling on WordNet (d=64), pretrain-finetune improves overall recall from 71.4% to 92.3% and long-distance (dist 8) recall from 19.4% to 75.7%, while maintaining 100% recall at distance 0. Rebalanced sampling (p=0.01 or 0.03) improves long-distance recall to ~33–37% but catastrophically degrades short-distance recall to 0.6–2.8%, confirming the trade-off identified in the toy setup. On HyperLex, pretrain-finetune (ρ=0.415) outperforms WN-Euclidean / Poincaré embeddings [Nickel & Kiela, 2017] (ρ=0.389), WN-Basic [Vulić et al., 2017] (ρ=0.240), and OrderEmb [Vulić et al., 2017] (ρ=0.195). In Appendix D, 16-dimensional hyperbolic embeddings [Dhingra et al., 2018] achieve 45.4% overall recall on WordNet, still well below pretrain-finetune's 60.1% at the same dimension. On ESCI, pretrain-finetune (pretrain on Exact, finetune on Substitute) consistently beats joint training on both Exact and Substitute recall@k across all k values tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of dual-encoder retrieval and graph embedding literature. It departs from hyperbolic and box-embedding approaches (Nickel & Kiela, Dhingra et al.) that model hierarchies in non-Euclidean spaces by proving that standard Euclidean dual encoders are sufficient for hierarchical retrieval given adequate dimension, and by providing a constructive algorithm (Algorithm 1) with a tight O(s log m) dimension bound. The pretrain-finetune recipe repurposes the standard transfer-learning paradigm but applies it to a novel sub-task: treating long-distance ancestor retrieval as the 'downstream' task. The theoretical analysis builds on and generalizes the multi-label embedding bounds of Guo et al. [15] to the asymmetric, ancestor-retrieval setting with an arbitrary gap parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both a theoretical guarantee (DEs can solve HR) and a practical recipe (pretrain-finetune) that directly addresses a failure mode practitioners would encounter when deploying dual encoders on hierarchical document structures. The discovery that the intuitive fix of rebalanced sampling fails—improving long-distance recall while catastrophically degrading short-distance recall—is a useful negative result that justifies the two-stage approach. Open questions include: how the recipe performs when no reliable proxy for query-document distance exists, whether it extends to multi-hop or non-tree DAGs with high branching, and whether the pretraining stage can be replaced by a smaller, more targeted initialization when short-distance data is scarce.
