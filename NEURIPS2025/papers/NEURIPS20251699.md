## Problem

Polyak-Ruppert averaging is the standard technique for achieving optimal asymptotic variance in stochastic approximation, yet sharp finite-time high-probability bounds for the averaged iterates in general settings remain largely unexplored. Prior analyses either provide bounds only for unaveraged iterates, require step sizes that depend on the target confidence level δ (yielding only pointwise control of one quantile), or are restricted to specific operator classes. This paper fills the gap by establishing a general-purpose theorem that converts any high-probability bound on unaveraged SA iterates into a tight concentration bound on the averaged iterates, with a step size independent of δ.

## Input / Output

The framework requires as input a high-probability bound of the form ||x_i − x*||²_c ≤ α_i f_ξ(δ′, k) for all 0 ≤ i ≤ k (holding with probability ≥ 1−δ′), together with structural assumptions on the operator (a fixed point, invertibility of J_F̄(x*)−I, and local pseudo-smoothness with constant N) and subgaussian noise at the fixed point. The output is a non-asymptotic bound on ||y_k − x*||²_c that controls the entire tail of the error distribution as a function of δ, rather than a single quantile. The leading term scales as O(log(1/δ)/k)·σ̄², and the step-size exponent ξ is chosen independently of δ, which is the key distinction from prior δ-dependent analyses.

## Method

The core theorem (Theorem 4.1) decomposes the averaged error y_k − x* into an early-time component (iterates before the SA enters the local smoothness radius R) and a late-time component, then bounds each separately. The early-time term is controlled directly by the assumed iterate bound and decays as 1/k² once k exceeds a threshold k₀. For the late-time term, the proof applies a Taylor expansion around x* to express (k+1)(J_F̄(x*)−I)·ỹ_k as a sum of four terms (noise at x*, a telescoping sum of iterate differences, a local smoothness residual, and a Jacobian-noise term), then uses an exponential supermartingale argument with a carefully chosen λ_k and three applications of Cauchy-Schwarz to bound the joint moment generating function. The resulting bound is shown to be tight up to a universal constant 2√6 via a constructed Gaussian example. The framework is then instantiated for contractive SA with additive and multiplicative noise, and applied to tabular TD(n)-learning, Q-learning, and off-policy TD-learning with linear function approximation, yielding the first full-distribution concentration bounds for averaged Q-learning and off-policy TD-learning.

## Results

The main theorem (Theorem 4.1) establishes that for averaged SA iterates, ‖y_k − x*‖²_c ≤ O(1/k)·O(log(1/δ)) + o(1/k)·Õ(f_ξ(δ,k)²), where the leading term is sub-exponential in δ. A constructed example (Proposition 4.2) shows this bound is tight up to a universal constant factor of 2√6. For contractive SA with additive noise, the leading constant improves from 32aσ² (unaveraged, [14]) to 24σ̄² (averaged), and the dimension-dependent term improves from 128ec_dσ² to 3(d + 8log 2)σ̄². Applications yield Õ(1/k^{5/4})·O(log(1/δ)) higher-order terms for tabular TD-learning and Q-learning with step size ξ = 1/2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include empirical comparisons; all comparisons are between theoretical bounds. Against the unaveraged SA bound of [14] (Chen et al., Annals of Applied Probability 2025), Polyak averaging improves the leading constant: the log(1/δ) coefficient drops from 32a (a > 1, so ≥ 32) to 24, and the dimension-dependent term drops from 128ec_dσ² to 3(d + 8log 2)σ̄², where σ̄² ≤ σ² since the averaged bound only requires subgaussianity at the fixed point. Unlike prior high-probability results for averaged SA (e.g., [31, 46, 29]) that use step sizes depending on the target confidence δ (giving only pointwise quantile control), this paper's step size α_k = α/(k+h)^ξ is δ-independent, yielding a bound on the entire error distribution. The paper also establishes, to the authors' knowledge, the first high-probability bounds on the full error distribution for averaged Q-learning (Theorem 6.2) and averaged off-policy TD-learning with linear function approximation (Theorem 6.3), settings where no prior distributional guarantees existed. For multiplicative noise, Theorem 5.2 proves an impossibility result: no sub-Weibull tail bound of the form c₀(1 + (log(1/δ))^m)/(k+1) can hold for averaged SA, in contrast to the sub-Weibull tail achievable for unaveraged SA under the same noise model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the concentration framework for unaveraged contractive SA established by Chen, Maguluri, and Zubeldia [14] (additive noise) and Chen et al. [12] (multiplicative noise), using their high-probability bounds on individual iterates as the input assumption to a new general averaging theorem. It departs from prior averaged-SA analyses (e.g., [36, 18, 31, 39, 46]) in two key ways: the step size is independent of the target confidence δ, and the resulting bound controls the entire tail of the error distribution rather than a single quantile. The Polyak-Ruppert averaging scheme itself [40, 43] is classical, but no prior work provided a general-purpose theorem that converts arbitrary unaveraged concentration bounds into sharp averaged bounds for nonlinear operators with both additive and multiplicative noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework yields the first full-distribution high-probability bounds for averaged TD-learning, Q-learning, and off-policy TD-learning with linear function approximation, filling a gap where prior results either required δ-dependent step sizes or only controlled one point of the error distribution. The impossibility result (Theorem 5.2) showing that under multiplicative noise, averaged SA necessarily has heavier tails than any sub-Weibull distribution is a fundamental finding that clarifies a key asymmetry between additive and multiplicative noise settings. A notable limitation is that the proof technique introduces an artifact where the averaged error's tail appears heavier than the unaveraged error's (addressed by Lemma 4.1 at the cost of a suboptimal rate), and the tightness gap of 2√6 in the leading constant, while small, leaves room for improvement. Open questions include whether the general framework can be extended to Markovian (non-i.i.d.) noise and two-time-scale SA.
