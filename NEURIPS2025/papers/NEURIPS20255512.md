## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for voxel-based 3D object detection are limited to INT8 and suffer severe accuracy collapse when naively extended to INT4, which is already supported by modern edge hardware. Prior approaches like LiDAR-PTQ also rely on time-consuming distillation-based calibration and lack generalization beyond 3D detection, hindering rapid deployment on resource-constrained devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255512/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained FP32 point cloud detector (e.g., CP-Voxel, VoxelNeXt, PillarNeXt) and a small unlabeled calibration set (as few as 64–256 frames, ~0.9% of training data) to produce per-layer quantization parameters: weight scales/zero-points and piecewise activation scales for foreground and background regions. The first and last network layers are kept in full precision. The resulting quantized model consumes standard LiDAR point clouds and outputs 3D bounding boxes (detection), class labels (classification), or per-point semantic labels (segmentation) at INT4 or INT8 precision.

## Method

Point4bit is a fully post-training 4-bit quantization framework with two complementary components. FA-PAQ identifies foreground voxels in BEV feature maps via unsupervised Top-K activation selection (top 20% non-empty voxels align with GT boxes), then applies CDF-based piecewise quantization to those regions, dividing the activation range into m equal-probability intervals so that quantization resolution adapts to the skewed foreground distribution. G-KWQ computes per-channel gradient sensitivity of the detection loss, selects the top m₂% most task-critical weight channels, and adds a gradient-weighted rounding-error penalty to the quantization loss to preserve high-fidelity representations for those weights. Both components are optimized layer-wise via grid search over candidate scales, requiring no retraining or labeled data, and the full calibration completes in ~39 minutes on a single V100 GPU—approximately 6× faster than LiDAR-PTQ.

## Results

On CP-Voxel (nuScenes val), Point4bit achieves 58.48% mAP / 66.21% NDS under W8A8, matching the FP baseline (58.45/66.22) and outperforming all PTQ baselines. Under the ultra-low-bit W4A4 setting, it reaches 56.97% mAP / 64.88% NDS, a drop of less than 1.5% from FP, while naive RTN collapses to 29.46% mAP. The method generalizes across architectures: VoxelNeXt W4A4 yields 58.97% mAP / 65.20% NDS and PillarNeXt W4A4 yields 60.89% mAP / 66.93% NDS. Quantization deployment time is ~39 min, roughly 6× faster than LiDAR-PTQ (224 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under W8A8 on CP-Voxel, Point4bit (58.48 mAP) marginally outperforms LiDAR-PTQ (58.34), QDrop (57.98), and PD-Quant (58.06), with the gap being small but consistent across NDS as well. The advantage becomes dramatic under W4A4: Point4bit achieves 56.97 mAP versus 39.40 for RTN+GS (+17.6) and 29.46 for plain RTN (+27.5), while LiDAR-PTQ and other baselines are not evaluated at 4-bit due to their known degradation. On VoxelNeXt W4A4, Point4bit (58.97 mAP) surpasses RTN+GS (52.71) by +6.3 mAP; on PillarNeXt W4A4, it reaches 60.89 mAP versus 36.20 for RTN+GS (+24.7). In terms of deployment efficiency, Point4bit completes quantization in ~39 min versus 224 min for LiDAR-PTQ and 650 min for PD-Quant, making it the fastest method while delivering the highest accuracy at 4-bit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends LiDAR-PTQ (ICLR 2024), the first PTQ framework for point cloud 3D detection, which was limited to INT8 and relied on time-consuming distillation-based calibration. Point4bit departs from LiDAR-PTQ in two key ways: it replaces the uniform activation treatment with a foreground-aware piecewise strategy (FA-PAQ) that exploits the sparsity and geometric structure of point clouds, and it introduces gradient-guided weight sensitivity scoring (G-KWQ) to protect task-critical weights under extreme bit-width constraints. The broader PTQ literature (RTN, PD-Quant, QDrop) provides the baseline quantization primitives, but none of these methods were designed for the sparse, structure-sensitive nature of LiDAR-based 3D perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By pushing point cloud quantization from INT8 to INT4 with less than 1.5% accuracy loss, this work opens a practical path toward deploying 3D detectors on resource-constrained edge hardware (e.g., Jetson AGX Orin) where 4-bit inference is natively supported. The method's generality across voxel-based, pillar-based, and sparse architectures, as well as across detection, classification, and segmentation tasks, suggests it could become a standard tool in the point cloud deployment pipeline. Key open questions remain: actual W4A4 acceleration is not yet demonstrated due to immature spconv 4-bit support, ultra-low-bit (≤2-bit) quantization is still out of reach, and the foreground-aware mechanism relies on the assumption that high-activation voxels align with foreground objects, which may not hold in all sensor configurations or adversarial scenarios.
