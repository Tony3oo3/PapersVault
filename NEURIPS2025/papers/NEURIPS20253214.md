## Problem

Most temporal causal discovery methods infer static causal graphs that aggregate or time-average dependencies, obscuring the fact that real-world systems (climate, biology, biomechanics) exhibit dynamic causality where active causal edges shift over time. No existing method has been rigorously evaluated on datasets with known time-varying ground-truth causal structures, leaving a critical gap in capturing evolving cause-effect relationships.

## Input / Output

UnCLe takes a multivariate time series matrix x ∈ R^{N×T} (N variables, T timesteps) and produces two complementary outputs: a dynamic causal graph Ĝ^Pert consisting of T time-resolved N×N adjacency matrices where each entry quantifies the causal strength from variable j to i at a specific timestep, and a static summary graph Ĝ^Agg (a single N×N matrix) obtained by pooling learned dependency coefficients across C semantic channels. The method scales to high-dimensional inputs (up to 325 variables in traffic data) thanks to parameter sharing across all N series.

## Method

UnCLe uses a parameter-sharing TCN autoencoder (Uncoupler/Recoupler) to disentangle each univariate series into C-channel latent representations, then models inter-variable dependencies via C lightweight auto-regressive Dependency Matrices Ψ that perform linear prediction in the learned latent space. Training is two-stage: first pretraining the autoencoder on reconstruction loss, then jointly optimizing reconstruction, prediction, and L1 sparsity on Ψ. For dynamic causal discovery, UnCLe applies temporal permutation to individual series and measures the datapoint-wise increase in prediction error for all other series, yielding time-resolved causal strengths. For static discovery, it aggregates the L2-norm of Ψ coefficients across channels. The key insight is that the TCN autoencoder learns a coordinate transformation where complex non-linear dynamics become approximately linear, and that disrupting temporal structure via permutation selectively impairs prediction of true effects while preserving marginal distributions.

## Results

On static causal discovery, UnCLe(P) achieves the best AUROC and AUPRC across four of five benchmarks: Lorenz#1 (0.999/0.996), Lorenz#2 (0.940/0.804), Lorenz#3 (0.922/0.636), and NC8 (0.975/0.835), with GVAR leading only on FINANCE (0.999/0.990 vs. UnCLe's 0.987/0.933). On the dynamic TVSEM benchmark, UnCLe(P) achieves a perfect 1.000 AUROC/AUPRC, compared to GVAR's 0.733/0.400 and the theoretical Static Best of 0.467/0.300. On the harder ND8 dynamic dataset, UnCLe(P) reaches 0.921 AUROC, surpassing both GVAR (0.723) and the Static Best upper bound (0.905). On the MoCap human motion task, UnCLe preserves only 20% of skeletal joint connections (missing rate) versus 62.2% for GVAR and 60.0% for JRNGC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnCLe(P) outperforms all nine baselines on Lorenz#1, Lorenz#2, Lorenz#3, and NC8, with the largest margins on the high-dimensional Lorenz#3 (0.922 AUROC vs. next-best CUTS+ at 0.863) and the chaotic Lorenz#2 (0.940 vs. CUTS+ at 0.894). On FINANCE, GVAR achieves the highest AUROC (0.999) with UnCLe(P) a close second (0.987), while TCDF and VARLiNGAM trail further behind. For dynamic causal discovery on TVSEM, UnCLe(P) achieves a perfect 1.000 AUROC/AUPRC, while GVAR—the only other baseline capable of producing dynamic graphs—reaches only 0.733/0.400, and the best possible static graph (Static Best) is limited to 0.467. On the more challenging ND8 dataset with multiple simultaneous direction switches, UnCLe(P) (0.921 AUROC) surpasses GVAR (0.723) by 20 points and even exceeds the Static Best upper bound (0.905), confirming the advantage of dynamic modeling. On the MoCap human motion task, UnCLe's inferred causal graphs preserve 80% of anatomically adjacent joint connections (20% missing rate), substantially outperforming GVAR (62.2% missing) and JRNGC (60.0% missing), and produce phase-specific structures (crouch, flight, touchdown) that align with biomechanical expectations.

## Relation to Prior Work

UnCLe sits at the intersection of neural Granger causality (NeuralGC, TCDF, GVAR, JRNGC, CUTS+) and TCN-based autoencoder representation learning, but departs from prior work in two key ways. First, while component-wise neural GC methods (NeuralGC, TCDF) suffer O(N²) parameter scaling, UnCLe achieves scalability through parameter-sharing Uncoupler/Recoupler networks across all variables. Second, and more fundamentally, UnCLe is the first method to combine semantic disentanglement in latent space with perturbation-based, datapoint-wise error analysis to produce time-resolved causal graphs; GVAR is the only other method capable of dynamic output, but it lacks the disentanglement mechanism and has not been rigorously evaluated on dynamic ground truths. The auto-regressive Dependency Matrices draw on the linearization principle (Socha 2007; Brunton 2017) that a suitable coordinate transform can render nonlinear dynamics approximately linear.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: most temporal causal discovery methods produce a single static graph that obscures how causal relationships evolve, yet many real systems (seasonal ecology, developmental biology, biomechanics) have time-varying causal structure. The dramatic performance gap on TVSEM (UnCLe 1.000 vs. Static Best 0.467 AUROC) and the biomechanically interpretable phase-specific MoCap graphs (arms dominant in crouch, legs in flight, whole-body in touchdown) make a compelling case that dynamic causal discovery is not merely a technical refinement but a qualitatively different lens. The primary open question, acknowledged by the authors, is the absence of identifiability guarantees: under what conditions does the learned latent linearization provably recover the true dynamic causal graph rather than a predictive surrogate? Extending evaluation beyond the simple 2-variable TVSEM and single MoCap motion to more complex, confounded dynamic systems would strengthen the contribution considerably.
