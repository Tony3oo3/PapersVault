## Problem

Existing text-to-sketch generation methods lack precise control over sketch styles, and prior exemplar-based style transfer approaches that directly substitute K/V matrices in self-attention suffer from content leakage and structural incoherence, particularly when reference and target sketches have low structural similarity. No prior method supports controllable multi-style sketch synthesis where users can blend and weight multiple reference styles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251261/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'a sketch of a deer standing in a meadow') and one or two reference style sketches as input, producing a single sketch image in bitmap format (black strokes on white background). Generation is training-free on pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.5 or SDXL backbones, using 100 DDIM sampling steps (~40s on SD v1.5, ~70s on SDXL per sketch on an A100 40GB GPU). For multi-style generation, a continuous parameter η ∈ [0,1] controls the relative dominance of each reference style, and the method also supports a style-oriented mode (reduced content guidance, increased style guidance) for users prioritizing stylistic fidelity over text alignment.

## Method

M3S is a training-free framework built on pre-trained diffusion models that replaces direct K/V substitution with feature concatenation: reference K/V features are appended to target K/V in self-attention layers rather than replacing them, and a linear smoothing operation (K̂_ref = λK_tar + (1−λ)K_ref) blends target content into reference features to mitigate content leakage. A Joint AdaIN module modulates the latent noise at each denoising step by interpolating between AdaIN transformations toward each reference, with parameter η controlling style tendency. A separated style-content guidance mechanism splits classifier-free guidance into two terms—one conditioned on text plus injected features (content direction, scale ω₁) and one conditioned only on injected features with null text (style direction, scale ω₂)—enabling independent control of text alignment versus stylistic expression. For abstract freehand sketches, an optional counter-based regularizer uses Tweedie's formula to estimate the denoised image, extracts Sobel gradients, and applies gradient descent on the latent to suppress shadow artifacts and sharpen strokes.

## Results

M3S (SDXL) achieves the best average CLIP-T score of 0.3514 across six sketch styles, demonstrating superior text-to-sketch alignment. On style consistency (DINO), M3S (SDXL) scores 0.6545 on Style 1 and 0.6922 on Style 6, slightly trailing AttentionDistillation (0.7738 and 0.7027) but achieving the lowest VGG Gram-matrix distance (0.0165 on Style 1, 0.0259 on Style 6). In human preference evaluation, M3S (SDXL) achieves the highest average rating of 6.19/8, significantly outperforming InstantStyle (5.08) with p-value = 1.06×10⁻⁵ when both use the SDXL backbone. Each sketch takes ~40 s (SD v1.5) or ~70 s (SDXL) on an A100 40 GB GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M3S (SDXL) outperforms all six quantitative baselines on CLIP-T (text alignment) in 5 out of 6 styles, with the largest margins over StyleAligned (+0.047 on Style 1) and RB-Modulation (+0.043 on Style 4). On style consistency (DINO), AttentionDistillation [63] retains a lead (e.g., 0.7738 vs. 0.6545 on Style 1), but M3S achieves substantially lower VGG Gram-matrix distance (0.0165 vs. 0.0930), indicating better structural style matching without content leakage. InstantStyle [48] is the closest competitor on CLIP-T (0.3512 vs. 0.3607 on Style 1) but trails M3S by 0.16 in DINO on Style 1, reflecting weaker style fidelity. CSGO [52] and StyleStudio [21] produce aesthetically plausible outputs but show the highest VGG distances (0.0571–0.1873), confirming their outputs drift toward natural-image appearance rather than sketch style. In human preference, M3S (SDXL) scores 6.19 vs. InstantStyle's 5.08 (p = 1.06×10⁻⁵) and AttentionDistillation's 4.28. CLIPasso [46], DiffSketcher [53], and SketchRNN [11] are compared only qualitatively (Appendix F); all three produce stylistically homogeneous outputs, and CLIPasso generates chaotic strokes when its foreground segmentation fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant K/V substitution paradigm in diffusion-based style transfer (Alaluf et al. 2024, Cao et al. 2023, Chung et al. 2024) by treating reference features as auxiliary inputs via concatenation rather than replacement, motivated by the domain gap between natural images and sparse sketches. It further distinguishes itself from StyleAligned (Hertz et al. 2024), which applies rigid AdaIN statistical alignment to Q/K features, by showing that such constraints actively degrade sketch quality and instead using linear smoothing to blend target content into reference K/V. The multi-style extension via joint AdaIN modulation on latent noise draws from the style-tendency control idea in Alaluf et al. but generalizes it to interpolate between two reference styles with a single parameter η.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, multi-style sketch generation framework offers practitioners a practical tool for artist-style sketch synthesis without task-specific training, with the η parameter providing intuitive creative control over style blending. The finding that rigid feature-statistics alignment (effective for natural images) harms sketch generation highlights an important domain-specific caveat for applying image style transfer techniques to sparse, stroke-based representations. Key open questions include handling extremely sparse references (where AdaIN modulation breaks down), achieving localized/regional style assignment rather than global blending, and whether the SDXL incompatibility with abstract datasets like QuickDraw reflects a fundamental fidelity-quality trade-off in high-resolution diffusion backbones.
