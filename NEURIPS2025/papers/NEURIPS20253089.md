## Problem

In many-shot in-context learning (50+ demonstrations), performance varies significantly with demonstration order (ICL-DOI), yet existing order-optimization methods fail in this regime for two reasons: heuristic quality metrics (GlobalE, LocalE, PDO) lack theoretical grounding and become unreliable when subtle performance differences between orders are hard to distinguish, and the n! permutation space makes exhaustive evaluation computationally infeasible. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a principled order-quality metric and an efficient hierarchical search strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of n demonstrations formatted as (query, single-token answer) pairs, an LLM (tested on GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-4o-Mini, SciPhi-Mistral-7B, Zephyr-7B, and LlaMa-3-8B), and text embeddings for clustering. It outputs a permutation of the demonstrations that maximizes ICL accuracy on held-out queries. The demonstrations are concatenated into a single prompt context (e.g., 150 shots), and the LLM is queried with this context plus a test query to produce a logit vector over answer choices; the optimized order is selected to maximize the ICD-OVI score estimated via LLM-generated probing samples.

## Method

The paper introduces ICD-OVI, an information-theoretic metric derived from V-usable information that quantifies the expected log-probability gain an LLM obtains from a query when a specific demonstration order is provided as context, relative to predicting from the answer sequence alone. A point-wise V-information (PVI) threshold handles unreliable LLM-generated labels by replacing their contribution with an expectation over all possible labels. For search, HIDO clusters demonstrations via K-means on text embeddings (typically ≤4 clusters), then alternates between intra-cluster optimization (random neighborhood search flipping 10% of positions, guided by rank-correlation bounds) and inter-cluster optimization (exhaustive evaluation of all cluster permutations). The score function is dynamically refined across iterations by regenerating probing sets from the current top-k orders, progressively reducing distribution discrepancy between probing and true data samples.

## Results

HIDO achieves the highest accuracy across nearly all dataset–LLM combinations in Table 2. The largest gains appear on TREC (82.29% with GPT-3.5T vs. 77.47% GlobalE, 78.65% LocalE, 76.69% PDO) and CommitmentBank (51.19% vs. 46.43% for both GlobalE and LocalE). On DBPedia, HIDO reaches 97.92% with GPT-3.5T, outperforming the best baseline (PDO, 96.22%) by 1.7 points. Across all five LLMs and nine datasets, HIDO wins or ties on the majority of settings, with the most consistent margins on TREC, CB, and SST-5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares HIDO against three existing demonstration-order metrics: GlobalE and LocalE (Lu et al., ACL 2022) and PDO (Xu et al., Findings of NAACL 2024). HIDO outperforms all three baselines on 8 out of 9 datasets with GPT-3.5T, with the largest margins on TREC (+4.8 to +5.6 points over the best baseline) and CommitmentBank (+3.0 to +4.8 points). With GPT-4o-Mini, HIDO again leads on most datasets, achieving 89.45% on AGNews versus 84.77% (LocalE) and 83.07% (GlobalE). The only settings where HIDO does not clearly win are RTE (tied with GlobalE at 81.90% on GPT-3.5T) and a few high-ceiling datasets like CR where all methods cluster within 1 point. Across the smaller open-source models (SciPhi-Mistral, Zephyr, LlaMa-3), HIDO maintains its lead, confirming the method is model-agnostic. The consistent advantage over PDO is notable because PDO relies on a human-specified prior distribution, which the paper argues is unreliable in many-shot settings where LLMs struggle to distinguish subtle order differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot ICL demonstration order optimization literature (Lu et al. 2022's GlobalE/LocalE, Xu et al. 2024's PDO) to the many-shot regime (50+ demonstrations), where the super-exponential permutation space (n!) renders exhaustive search infeasible and heuristic metrics become unreliable due to subtle performance differences. The key conceptual departure is replacing heuristic order-quality metrics with ICD-OVI, an information-theoretic score derived from V-usable information theory (Xu et al., ICLR 2020) and the PVI threshold technique (Lin et al., EACL 2023), providing a theoretically grounded measure of how much usable information an ordered demonstration set enables the LLM to extract from queries. The hierarchical optimization framework (K-means clustering followed by alternating intra- and inter-cluster search) draws on decomposition strategies from combinatorial optimization (e.g., hierarchical TSP solvers) to make the vast permutation space tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM context windows continue to expand, many-shot ICL becomes a practical deployment strategy, but the persistent order instability (ICL-DOI) undermines reliability; this paper provides the first theoretically justified metric and a computationally feasible optimization procedure for the many-shot setting. The consistent (if moderate) gains across five LLMs and nine datasets, along with lower variance than baselines, suggest the approach is robust and model-agnostic. Limitations include reliance on K-means clustering that may miss complex inter-demonstration dependencies, the computational overhead of iterative probing-set updates, and evaluation restricted to text classification tasks. Open questions include whether the information-theoretic guarantees hold under distribution shift, whether the optimal number of clusters can be learned automatically, and whether the framework extends to generative or multi-turn ICL settings.
