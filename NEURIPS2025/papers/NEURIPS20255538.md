## Problem

Current mathematical benchmarks evaluate LLMs only on end-to-end problem-solving accuracy, leaving it unclear whether models genuinely acquire mathematical concepts and reasoning principles or merely memorize training patterns through long chain-of-thought sequences. This paper asks whether mathematical reasoning can be decomposed into minimal, disentangled 'atomic capabilities' and how these atoms interact, providing a finer-grained diagnostic than existing benchmarks offer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255538/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs span four formats: natural-language math problems (tagged by field and difficulty level), fill-in-the-blank definition/property statements, Lean-formal-language proof problems, and CounterMath-style propositions requiring truth-value judgment. All outputs are constrained to a \boxed{} answer for exact-match scoring, except backward reasoning which additionally requires an explicit counterexample; counterexample quality is scored via GPT-4o-judged Example, Strict, and Loose alignment metrics against a reference example rather than exact string match.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-axis atomic capability taxonomy: four mathematical fields (algebra, geometry, analysis, topology) each split into two difficulty levels, and three logical reasoning levels (conceptual understanding via definition/property completion, forward multi-step reasoning in formal Lean language, and counterexample-driven backward reasoning). For each atomic unit, the authors construct balanced train/test splits sourced from MATH, GSM8K, OlympiadBench, NaturalProofs, LeanWorkbook, and CounterMath, then evaluate 17 open-source and commercial LLMs under a unified CoT prompting protocol. To probe interactions, they fine-tune Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct-7B (and InternLM2-math-plus-7B) with LoRA on a single atomic capability's training set and measure transfer effects on all other capabilities, revealing that algebra training yields the largest cross-field gains and that conceptual-understanding training alone outperforms direct field-specific training for stimulating higher-level reasoning.

## Results

On field atomic capabilities (Table 1), DeepSeek-R1 achieves the strongest overall commercial performance, scoring 83.6/80.9 (low/high) on Algebra, 76.8/78.0 on Geometry, 70.3/68.2 on Analysis, and 77.7/82.7 on Topology. OpenAI o1 leads in Algebra (93.6/87.1) but drops sharply in Geometry (66.3/62.3) and Analysis (59.3/46.9). Among open-source 7B models, Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct-7B dominates with 80.5/65.2 on Algebra, outperforming larger models like InternLM2-Math-Plus-20B. On logical reasoning (Table 2), DeepSeek-R1 scores 84.5 on definition recognition and 80.7 F-1 on counterexample judgment, while Gemini2.5-pro leads backward reasoning with 90.8% example rate and 75.7% loose alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an evaluation paper that benchmarks 17 models (12 open-source, 5 commercial) on decoupled atomic capabilities rather than proposing a new method. DeepSeek-R1 is the strongest overall commercial model, leading or near-leading in all four fields (e.g., 77.7/82.7 Topology vs. o1's 52.9/49.4 and GPT-4o's 53.0/49.4). OpenAI o1 achieves the highest Algebra scores (93.6/87.1) but underperforms DeepSeek-R1 by 10.5–33.3 points in Geometry, Analysis, and Topology. Among open-source 7B models, Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct-7B substantially outperforms all others (e.g., 80.5 Algebra Low vs. MetaMath-Mistral-7B's 59.6 and InternLM2-math-plus-7B's 49.2), even surpassing the 20B InternLM2-Math-Plus. On logical reasoning, DeepSeek-R1 leads definition recognition (84.5) and counterexample F-1 (80.7), while Gemini2.5-pro dominates backward reasoning example construction (90.8% example rate, 75.7% loose alignment vs. o1's 55.8%/40.9%). A notable finding is that training on Algebra data transfers positively to Geometry (+13.6 points at Level 1) and Analysis (+8.1), exceeding in-field training gains, whereas Topology training causes negative transfer to Analysis (−7.3 at Level 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of evaluating LLM mathematical reasoning via end-to-end accuracy on benchmarks like MATH, GSM8K, and OlympiadBench, which treat problem-solving as an undifferentiated skill. Drawing on cognitive science's 'atomic thinking' concept, it reclassifies existing math datasets into four field-specific atomic units (algebra, geometry, analysis, topology) and three logical reasoning units (conceptual understanding, forward reasoning with formal language, backward reasoning with counterexamples), creating a decoupled evaluation framework. It builds on formal-math-language work (Lean4, Lean-STaR) and counterexample-driven reasoning (CounterMath) but repurposes them as isolated capability probes rather than full problem-solving pipelines. The interaction experiments (LoRA fine-tuning on Qwen2.5-Math-7B and InternLM2-math-plus-7B) extend prior transfer-learning observations into a structured cross-ability analysis that no prior benchmark has attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides practitioners with a diagnostic lens to identify *which* specific mathematical sub-skill a model lacks, rather than only knowing it fails on a problem. The finding that training solely on definition-completion tasks improves forward reasoning by ~19 points and backward reasoning by ~10 points challenges the prevailing assumption that complex problem-drilling is necessary for high-level math ability, suggesting a more efficient and cognitively grounded training strategy. Key limitations include training experiments restricted to two 7B base models, no exploration of curriculum learning or RL for targeted atomic stimulation, and the topology difficulty anomaly remaining only hypothesized. Open questions include how to compose multiple atomic abilities for genuinely complex multi-field problems and whether the observed cross-field transfer (especially algebra→geometry) holds at larger scales or with different architectures.
