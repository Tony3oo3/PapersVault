## Problem

Paired RGB-thermal image data is scarce and expensive to collect, yet essential for visual-thermal sensor fusion and cross-modality tasks. Existing RGB-to-thermal translation methods (GAN- and diffusion-based) are trained on narrow, domain-specific datasets and fail to generalize across diverse viewpoints, thermal sensor types, and environmental conditions. No prior model can synthesize thermally faithful images that reflect significant variations in all three factors simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251112/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 RGB image (encoded to a latent via a pretrained KL-VAE) and a learnable dataset-specific style embedding of dimension 1024, and produces a single-channel 256×256 thermal image decoded from the generated latent. The style embedding is a discrete, per-dataset vector (not a continuous parameter), and an additional unconditional embedding enables classifier-free guidance. Thermal images are normalized to 8-bit grayscale, and the latent space reduces spatial dimensions by a factor of f with C latent channels.

## Method

ThermalGen formulates thermal synthesis as flow matching in a latent space: a SiT (Scalable Interpolant Transformer) backbone predicts the velocity field v(z_t, t, z_RGB, y) that integrates from pure noise to the thermal latent over T=50 ODE steps. A style-disentangled mechanism introduces learnable per-dataset style embeddings injected via adaLN-Zero conditioning, so a single model handles all RGB-T relationships without retraining; an unconditional embedding supports classifier-free guidance. RGB conditioning is achieved either through multi-head cross-attention (RGB latent as query, noised thermal latent as key/value) or direct channel concatenation with the thermal latent. The thermal encoder/decoder is a KL-VAE trained with L1+LPIPS reconstruction and optional GAN fine-tuning, while the flow model is trained jointly across 17+ datasets via random batch sampling with AdamW at lr=1e-4 for 200k steps.

## Results

ThermalGen-L/2 achieves the best FID and LPIPS on 7 out of 9 evaluated datasets, with standout results on NII-CU (FID 69.30, LPIPS 0.21), AVIID (FID 29.05, LPIPS 0.13), M3FD (FID 35.82, LPIPS 0.14), and MSRS (FID 52.31, LPIPS 0.21). On the newly introduced satellite-aerial datasets, it achieves FID 76.91 on Bosonplus-day and 75.80 on Bosonplus-night, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline (BBDM at 137.68 and 101.08 respectively). Performance degrades on Boson-night (FID 161.22, low-contrast thermal imagery), LLVIP (FID 238.60, distribution shift), and FLIR (FID 70.09, extreme lighting), where fine-tuned DiffV2IR variants remain competitive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ThermalGen consistently outperforms all GAN-based baselines (pix2pix, CycleGAN, pix2pixHD, VQGAN) on FID and LPIPS across nearly all datasets, with the largest margins over CycleGAN (e.g., FID 35.82 vs. 171.37 on M³FD) and DDIM (FID 35.82 vs. 229.24 on M³FD). Against the strongest diffusion baseline BBDM, ThermalGen achieves substantially lower FID on M³FD (35.82 vs. 120.79), MSRS (52.31 vs. 145.86), and Bosonplus-day (76.91 vs. 137.68). The closest competition comes from fine-tuned DiffV2IR variants: DiffV2IR* (fine-tuned on M³FD) achieves FID 37.74 on M³FD vs. ThermalGen's 35.82, and DiffV2IR+ (fine-tuned on FLIR) achieves FID 37.83 on FLIR vs. ThermalGen's 70.09, where ThermalGen loses. On the aerial datasets, ThermalGen dominates NII-CU (FID 69.30 vs. best baseline 118.59) and AVIID (FID 29.05 vs. 51.61), while DiffV2IR† (trained on LLVIP test set) retains an unfair advantage on LLVIP (FID 50.10 vs. 238.60). Overall, ThermalGen wins on 7 of 9 datasets across all four metrics simultaneously, with losses confined to Boson-night, LLVIP, and FLIR where domain-specific challenges (low contrast, distribution shift, extreme lighting) limit generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

ThermalGen builds directly on the SiT flow-matching architecture and the latent diffusion (KL-VAE) framework, repurposing them for the RGB-to-thermal translation task where prior work relied on GAN-based methods (pix2pix, CycleGAN, pix2pixHD, VQGAN) or diffusion-based approaches (DDIM, BBDM, DiffV2IR). The key architectural departure is the style-disentangled conditioning mechanism: rather than retraining for each dataset's RGB-T style as DiffV2IR requires, ThermalGen uses learnable dataset-specific style embeddings injected via adaLN-Zero (borrowed from DiT), enabling a single model to generate target-style thermal images across heterogeneous sensors, viewpoints, and environments. The RGB conditioning via latent concatenation or cross-attention extends standard conditional generation to the cross-modal setting where direct weight sharing between RGB and thermal domains is infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scarcity of synchronized, calibrated RGB-T pairs is a persistent bottleneck for cross-modality tasks (alignment, retrieval, detection), and ThermalGen's ability to synthesize thermal images from abundant RGB data across diverse conditions addresses this at scale. The style-disentangled design means new datasets can be incorporated by simply appending a new style embedding without retraining, offering a practical path toward ever-larger training corpora. However, performance still degrades on low-contrast (Boson-night) and distribution-shifted (LLVIP) datasets, and the lack of error bars or multi-seed evaluation limits confidence in the reported margins. Open questions include whether synthesized thermal data can fully substitute for real paired data in safety-critical downstream tasks, and whether the flow-matching formulation offers meaningful advantages over well-tuned diffusion baselines beyond the architectural novelty.
