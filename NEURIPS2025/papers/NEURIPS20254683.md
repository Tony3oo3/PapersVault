## Problem

Existing audio-visual event parsing methods process entire videos offline with large models, making them unsuitable for real-time streaming applications. This paper introduces Online Audio-Visual Event Parsing (On-AVEP), a new paradigm requiring models to accurately parse audio, visual, and audio-visual events using only past and current information while maintaining low computational cost — a setting where the absence of future context makes event disambiguation particularly challenging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254683/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted audio and visual feature vectors (768-d from CLIP/CLAP or 1536-d from OnePeace) as a streaming sequence, processing a fixed sliding window of L_c=10 timesteps at each inference step with stride 1. At each time step T, it outputs a multi-label binary event vector for audio-visual events (100 classes on UnAV-100) or separate audio-only and visual-only event vectors (25 classes on LLP). The model also internally generates pseudo-future predictions over L_f=5 future timesteps, which are supervised during training but discarded at inference.

## Method

PreFM's core mechanism is Predictive Multimodal Future Modeling: a Universal Hybrid Attention (UHA) block first fuses current audio-visual features cross-modally, then learnable query tokens attend to these fused features to generate initial pseudo-future sequences, and a Temporal-Modality Cross Fusion stage mutually refines current and pseudo-future representations through combined self-attention, cross-modal, and cross-temporal interactions to reduce predictive noise. During training, two auxiliary designs enhance the model: Modality-agnostic Robust Representation distills modality-agnostic event features from a frozen OnePeace teacher (via text-prompted encoding of ground-truth labels) using cosine similarity loss, and Focal Temporal Prioritization applies Gaussian-decay weights centered at the current time step to emphasize predictions closest to T. The full training objective combines BCE losses on both current and pseudo-future windows with the distillation term, optimized via random segment sampling that provides diverse fixed-length history windows across the video.

## Results

On the On-AVEL task (UnAV-100), PreFM achieves 59.1 F1, 70.1 mAP, and 46.3 Avg F1-score, surpassing the previous best online method CCNet (54.8 F1, 62.3 mAP, 37.0 Avg F1) by +9.3 in Avg F1 while using only 2.7% of its parameters (6.5M vs. 238.8M) and 0.6% FLOPs (0.4G vs. 72.1G). The enhanced PreFM+ variant further reaches 51.5 Avg F1 with 13.8M parameters. On the On-AVVP task (LLP), PreFM achieves 53.3 F1_av and 41.2 Avg F1_av, outperforming the next-best online method MM-CSE (48.9 F1_av, 36.2 Avg F1_av) by +5.0 in Avg F1_av with fewer parameters (3.3M vs. 6.2M). PreFM runs at 51.9 FPS with 19.3 ms latency on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreFM decisively outperforms all three online baselines on UnAV-100: it beats CCNet [78] by +9.3 Avg F1 (46.3 vs. 37.0) and +7.8 mAP (70.1 vs. 62.3), UniAV [14] by +16.0 Avg F1 (46.3 vs. 30.3), and UnAV [13] by +17.7 Avg F1 (46.3 vs. 28.6), while using 2.7%–47% of their parameters. The efficiency gap is dramatic: PreFM requires 0.4G FLOPs versus 72.1G for CCNet and 52.4G for UnAV, and runs at 51.9 FPS versus 7.5–15.6 FPS for baselines. On LLP for On-AVVP, PreFM surpasses MM-CSE [71] by +5.0 Avg F1_av (41.2 vs. 36.2), NREP [27] by +7.7, LEAP [76] by +6.9, CoLeaf [49] by +11.5, and VALOR [29] by +6.0, while using fewer parameters than all except VALOR. Notably, even the offline (full-context) versions of baselines (marked *) are only marginally ahead of PreFM+ (58.3 vs. 51.5 Avg F1 for CCNet* vs. PreFM+), suggesting the predictive future modeling effectively compensates for the absence of future frames. The PreFM+ variant narrows the online-to-offline gap further, achieving 51.5 Avg F1 online versus 58.3 for the best offline method, a gap of only 6.8 points with 94% fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining Online Audio-Visual Event Parsing (On-AVEP) as a streaming counterpart to the well-studied offline tasks of audio-visual event localization (AVEL) and audio-visual video parsing (AVVP). Conceptually, it repurposes ideas from online action detection and anticipation (e.g., MAT, JOADAA) to the multimodal audio-visual domain, introducing a predictive future modeling mechanism to compensate for the absence of future context. The training strategy draws on knowledge distillation from frozen pre-trained multimodal encoders (OnePeace, CLIP, CLAP) and a focal temporal reweighting scheme, combining these in a way not previously applied to audio-visual event parsing. The Universal Hybrid Attention block is a novel architectural primitive that unifies self-attention, cross-modal, and cross-temporal interactions in a single operation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work is practically significant because it demonstrates that a 6.5M-parameter model can outperform 238M-parameter offline SOTA methods by +9.3 Avg F1 while running at 52 FPS with 19ms latency, making real-time multimodal event understanding feasible on resource-constrained hardware (wearables, autonomous driving, HRI). Key limitations remain: pseudo-future predictions introduce irreducible noise that degrades performance in dense multi-event scenes (Avg F1 drops from 0.54 to 0.13 as event count exceeds 6), and the framework lacks explicit contrastive mechanisms to separate acoustically/visually similar events. Open questions include whether the predictive future modeling paradigm generalizes to longer-range temporal reasoning, open-vocabulary settings, or integration with large language models for richer semantic understanding.
