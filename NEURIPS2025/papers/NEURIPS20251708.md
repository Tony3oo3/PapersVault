## Problem

Deep survival models learn rich covariate-risk relationships but remain purely frequentist, offering no principled uncertainty quantification over survival functions. No existing method combines the representational power of deep neural networks with full Bayesian posterior inference in a scalable, continuous-time survival framework, leaving a gap in high-stakes settings like medicine where calibrated uncertainty estimates are essential for trust and adoption.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251708/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular covariate vectors x ∈ R^p, observed times y_i = min(T_i, C_i), and binary event indicators δ_i under non-informative right-censoring. Rather than producing a single point-estimate survival curve, NeuralSurv outputs a full posterior distribution over the survival function S(t|x) and hazard function λ(t|x), summarized as median curves with 90% Bayesian credible intervals that narrow as data accumulates. The model operates in continuous time without discretization, and the normalization factor Z(t,x) ensures the prior mean of the sigmoidal hazard matches the baseline hazard profile.

## Method

NeuralSurv models the hazard as a Weibull baseline λ_0(t;φ) modulated by a sigmoid of a Bayesian neural network output, λ(t|x) = λ_0(t,x;φ)·σ(g(t,x;θ)). A two-stage data augmentation handles the two intractabilities: Pólya-Gamma latent variables linearize the sigmoid nonlinearity, and marked Poisson processes convert the continuous-time integral in the likelihood into a product over random points, for which the paper provides the first rigorous validity guarantee (Theorem 3.1). To make posterior inference tractable, the BNN is locally linearized around its MAP estimate, inducing a Gaussian process prior that restores full conjugacy with the augmented likelihood. This yields closed-form coordinate-ascent variational updates (CAVI) for all parameters—φ (Gamma), θ (Gaussian), ω (Pólya-Gamma), and Ψ (marked Poisson)—that scale linearly in network size via the Woodbury identity, avoiding both time discretization and Monte Carlo gradient estimation.

## Results

On three representative real-world datasets (COLON, METABRIC, GBSG), NeuralSurv achieves the best IPCW IBS (calibration) on all three: 0.218 (COLON), 0.212 (METABRIC), and 0.188 (GBSG), outperforming the next-best methods (Sumo-Net at 0.241/0.223 and DQS at 0.229). On discriminative performance (C-index), NeuralSurv achieves 0.671 (COLON), 0.584 (METABRIC), and 0.657 (GBSG), ranking first on COLON and GBSG and second on METABRIC (behind BCESurv at 0.616). Across all eight real datasets, NeuralSurv attains the best IPCW IBS on seven and the best C-index on four, with second-best C-index on three. In the synthetic experiment, NeuralSurv consistently delivers the lowest IPCW IBS across all training set sizes (N=25 to 150) while providing 90% credible intervals that appropriately narrow with more data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuralSurv dominates on calibration (IPCW IBS), achieving the best score on all three displayed datasets and 7 out of 8 total real datasets, with margins of 0.023–0.041 over the next-best competitors (Sumo-Net on COLON/METABRIC, DQS on GBSG). On discriminative performance (C-index), NeuralSurv wins on COLON (0.671 vs. DQS 0.635) and GBSG (0.657 vs. PMF 0.655) but trails BCESurv on METABRIC (0.584 vs. 0.616), where BCESurv's competing-risks formulation gives it an edge. Among the deep survival baselines, DySurv performs worst overall (IPCW IBS 0.465–0.536), while Sumo-Net is competitive on IPCW IBS but consistently weak on C-index (0.447–0.485). DQS is the strongest C-index competitor (0.635 on COLON, 0.611 on GBSG) yet NeuralSurv surpasses it on IPCW IBS by 0.028–0.041. Traditional models (Cox PH, Weibull AFT, Random Survival Forest, Survival SVM) are included for reference but are not designed for high-dimensional feature representations, and NeuralSurv's Bayesian framework provides credible intervals and well-calibrated uncertainty estimates that none of the frequentist deep baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuralSurv unifies three previously separate threads: Pólya-Gamma augmentation for sigmoid nonlinearity (Polson et al., 2013), marked Poisson process augmentation for intractable continuous-time integrals (Donner & Opper, 2018; Zhou et al., 2020), and local linearization of Bayesian neural networks to recover Gaussian-process conjugacy (Immer et al., 2021). The sigmoidal hazard structure and normalization factor follow Fernandez et al. (2016) for GP survival models, but the paper is the first to apply this combined augmentation scheme to survival analysis and to provide a formal validity guarantee (Theorem 3.1) for the data-augmentation step. The key departure from prior deep-survival work (DeepSurv, DeepHit, MTLR, etc.) is the shift from point-estimate frequentist training to full posterior inference via coordinate-ascent variational updates in the augmented space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In high-stakes clinical settings where data is scarce and miscalibrated predictions can be dangerous, NeuralSurv's Bayesian credible intervals and superior IPCW IBS / D-Calibration scores offer a principled path toward trustworthy deep survival models. However, the mean-field factorization ignores posterior correlations between φ and θ, the MAP-centered linearization may miss multimodal posteriors, and the full-dataset CAVI passes limit scalability to large cohorts—open questions that stochastic or mini-batch extensions would need to address. The sigmoidal hazard assumption, while standard, constrains the class of risk dynamics the model can capture, and extending the framework to handle multimodal or high-dimensional clinical data remains future work.
