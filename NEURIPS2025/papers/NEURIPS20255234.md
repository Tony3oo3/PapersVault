## Problem

Transferring texture from a single 2D reference image to a 3D object remains challenging: 2D editing methods applied frame-by-frame produce view-inconsistent results, while text-driven 3D editing methods rely on coarse prompts that fail to preserve fine-grained texture characteristics from the reference image. This paper tackles both view consistency and texture fidelity simultaneously for 3D Gaussian Splatting objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255234/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D reference image (either user-provided or generated via depth-conditioned diffusion from the object's depth map), a fixed 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, and a text prompt. Backgrounds are removed from both the reference and unedited renderings before encoding into latent space for k-step partial diffusion. The output is a finetuned 3DGS model whose texture matches the reference image, produced through iterative cycles of multi-view image editing followed by 3D Gaussian fine-tuning.

## Method

3DOT combines three mechanisms: (1) progressive generation, which starts editing from the reference view and propagates to adjacent views using weighted fused cross-attention over a sparse reference set (reference view, previous edited view, and optionally a horizontally flipped reference), maximizing overlap to enforce local consistency; (2) view-consistency gradient guidance, which runs two parallel denoising passes—one conditioned on reference views via cross-attention and one without—and injects the difference as a scaled gradient signal to amplify cross-view coherence, analogous to classifier-free guidance; (3) prompt-tuning-based gradient guidance, which learns a single token by aligning it with the CLIP-space difference between the unedited rendering and the reference image, then further refines it in diffusion feature space, and uses the resulting token as an additional guidance term during denoising to preserve texture identity in seen views and enforce style consistency in unseen views. The final denoising prediction (Eq. 8) sums four terms: unconditional, text-guided, reference-conditioned, and prompt-tuning-guided components, each with its own scaling factor.

## Results

On the Mip-NeRF 360 and NeRF-Art datasets, 3DOT achieves a CLIP score of 0.9333, substantially outperforming the best text-driven baseline IN2N (0.8917) by ~4.6%. LPIPS scores (lower is better) drop to 0.1166 (Alex) and 0.1247 (VGG), representing ~31% and ~22% reductions over the best baselines (IGS2GS and GaussCtrl, respectively). Vision-GPT faithfulness scores reach 76/100 versus 54 for the best baseline (DGE), and user-study ratings hit 4.58/5 compared to 2.44 for the best baseline (IGS2GS), indicating a near-doubling of perceived quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DOT outperforms all four text-driven baselines on every reported metric. Against IN2N [15] (NeRF-based, instruct-pix2pix guided), 3DOT improves CLIP score by 0.042 and reduces Alex-LPIPS by 0.054, while Vision-GPT jumps from 45.5 to 76. Against IGS2GS [37] (GS-based, instruct-pix2pix guided), the gains are similar in CLIP (0.9333 vs 0.8908) and LPIPS, with user-study ratings nearly doubling (4.58 vs 2.44). Against GaussCtrl [41] (GS-based, multi-view consistent editing via unedited-view conditioning), 3DOT achieves a 0.070 CLIP improvement and 0.034 LPIPS reduction; the paper attributes GaussCtrl's weakness to excessive preservation of the original texture, limiting editability. Against DGE [8] (GS-based, epipolar cross-attention for consistency), 3DOT leads by 0.076 in CLIP and 0.055 in Alex-LPIPS; DGE's independent per-view editing stage causes its epipolar mechanism to break down under large semantic changes, yielding near-unchanged outputs. The paper notes that all baselines are inherently text-driven and were adapted to image-based editing by feeding captioned descriptions as prompts, which inherently limits their ability to preserve fine-grained texture characteristics from a reference image.

## Relation to Prior Work

3DOT positions itself at the intersection of 2D diffusion-based editing (Plug-and-Play, SwapAnything) and 3D text-driven editing (IN2N, IGS2GS, GaussCtrl, DGE), arguing that neither paradigm alone suffices: 2D methods lack multi-view coherence, while text-driven 3D methods lose fine-grained texture identity. Its view-consistency gradient guidance directly extends classifier-free guidance [16] by replacing the unconditional branch with a cross-attention-free branch, and its prompt-tuning mechanism adapts textual inversion [13] and visual instruction inversion [26] to encode texture-difference tokens in CLIP and diffusion feature spaces. The iterative edit-then-finetune loop follows IN2N [15], and the latent-space initialization follows GaussCtrl [41], but the progressive single-reference-to-neighborhood generation strategy is the paper's core architectural departure from prior 3D editing pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a single 2D reference image can drive high-fidelity, view-consistent 3D texture transfer on 3DGS objects, opening a practical path for content creators to customize 3D assets without manual UV mapping or multi-view capture. However, the method's reliance on language-based segmentation (LangSAM) introduces dark-border artifacts, and its robustness degrades under significant geometric mismatches between reference and object. Open questions include whether the three-component pipeline can be simplified or distilled, how it scales to multi-object scenes or dynamic content, and whether the iterative two-pass editing can be reduced to a single pass without quality loss.
