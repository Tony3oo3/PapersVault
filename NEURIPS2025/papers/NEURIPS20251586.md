## Problem

Training equivariant diffusion models for symmetric data (molecules, crystals, proteins) relies on either equivariant architectures or data augmentation, both of which produce high-variance gradient estimators that slow convergence and destabilize optimization. No principled variance-reduction technique existed that was compatible with both strategies while preserving equivariance guarantees.

## Input / Output

Orbit Diffusion is a training-time modification, not a new architecture: it plugs into existing diffusion or flow-matching pipelines (ETFLOW, DiffCSP, PROTEINA, TGDMat) without changing their input/output interfaces. During training, for each noisy sample x_t it additionally draws N group elements (e.g., 200 SO(3) rotations and 50 graph automorphisms for molecules, 10,000 SO(3) rotations for proteins) to compute an orbit-weighted denoising target, then backpropagates a single MSE loss. The forward pass and backward pass count per sample remain unchanged; the extra cost is only the group-element sampling and averaging, adding ~20% overhead for small models and ~0.1% for 200M-parameter models.

## Method

The key insight is to reinterpret data augmentation as a Monte Carlo estimator of the gradient of a fully symmetrized diffusion loss, then apply Rao-Blackwellization: since the denoiser φ(x_t, t) does not depend on x_0, the gradient decomposes into an outer expectation over x_t and an inner conditional expectation E[x_0 | x_t], and replacing the single-sample target x_0 with this conditional mean yields a provably lower-variance unbiased estimator (Theorem 1). Because E[x_0 | x_t] is intractable, the paper estimates it via self-normalized importance sampling (SNIS) with a proposal that samples group elements g from the orbit of the generating x_0, so that the intractable orbit-integral terms in the importance weights cancel analytically. The practical variant, Orbit Diffusion (OrbDiff), fixes x_0 to the example that produced x_t and samples N group elements (optionally from a time-dependent distribution ν_t(g) that concentrates near the identity at low noise), computing the orbit-weighted target φ*(x_0, x_t, t) = Σ w^(i) g^(i)∘x_0 / Σ w^(i) with weights w^(i) = q_t(x_t | g^(i)∘x_0)/ν_t(g^(i)). Theorem 2 proves that despite φ* ≠ E[x_0|x_t] pointwise, the gradient of the resulting OrbDiff loss exactly matches the ideal Rao-Blackwellized gradient, and that the target is itself equivariant, ensuring the loss admits equivariant minimizers.

## Results

On GEOM-QM9 molecular conformer generation, ETFLOW + OrbDiff achieves 85.4% Recall Cov@0.1 (vs. 81.4% for reproduced ETFLOW) and the lowest Recall AMR (0.074 mean, 0.027 median), while also improving Precision Cov@0.1 from 74.6% to 80.2%. On crystal structure prediction, OrbDiff_WN improves DiffCSP's Match Rate from 51.49% to 55.70% on MP-20 and reduces RMSE from 0.063 to 0.053; for text-guided CSP, it boosts TGDMat(L) Match from 61.91% to 66.74% on MP-20. For protein structure generation, OrbDiff finetuning raises PROTEINA's designability from 93.8% to 95.6% and lowers scRMSD from 1.00 to 0.93, while maintaining competitive diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GEOM-QM9, OrbDiff (integrated into ETFLOW) clearly outperforms GEOMOL (28.4% vs. 85.4% Recall Cov@0.1) and Torsional Diffusion (37.7% vs. 85.4%), and surpasses MCF on Recall (85.4% vs. 81.9% Cov@0.1; 0.074 vs. 0.103 AMR) while MCF retains a slight edge on Precision Cov@0.5 (93.9% vs. 91.9%). Compared to the reproduced ETFLOW baseline, OrbDiff yields a 4-point gain in Recall Cov@0.1 and a 5.6-point gain in Precision Cov@0.1. On crystal structure prediction, OrbDiff_WN consistently beats DiffCSP (55.70% vs. 51.49% Match on MP-20; 0.053 vs. 0.063 RMSE) and substantially outperforms CDVAE and P-cG-SchNet; for text-guided CSP, it improves TGDMat(L) Match from 61.91% to 66.74% on MP-20. For protein generation, OrbDiff finetuning improves designability over naive finetuning (95.6% vs. 93.8%) but remains below the larger M21M model (99.0%) and FoldFlow(OT) (97.2%), though it achieves better diversity (Cluster 0.52 vs. 0.30 for M21M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate strategies for learning G-invariant distributions—equivariant network architectures (Hoogeboom et al., 2022; Klein et al., 2024) and data augmentation (Geffner et al., 2025; Abramson et al., 2024b)—under a single probabilistic framework by reinterpreting augmentation as a Monte Carlo estimator of the symmetrized diffusion loss gradient. The key departure is applying Rao-Blackwellization to this estimator, yielding the Orbit Diffusion loss (Eq. 12) with an orbit-weighted target that provably reduces variance while requiring only one forward/backward pass per sample. The method is demonstrated as a drop-in modification to existing equivariant (ETFLOW, DiffCSP) and non-equivariant (PROTEINA) diffusion/flow-matching pipelines, building directly on the denoising diffusion framework of Ho et al. (2020) and the equivariant diffusion setting of Hoogeboom et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Orbit Diffusion offers a practical, low-overhead variance-reduction technique (≈20% for small models, 0.1% for 200M-parameter models) that stabilizes training and improves generalization across molecular, crystal, and protein generation without architectural changes. The theoretical guarantee that the symmetrized loss admits equivariant minimizers, combined with the proof that data augmentation provides no benefit for already-equivariant denoisers, clarifies a previously underappreciated distinction in the literature. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned proposal distributions over group elements could further improve the efficiency-accuracy trade-off, and whether the orbit-sampling restriction generalizes well to highly non-uniform or discrete symmetry groups where the orbit structure is less regular.
