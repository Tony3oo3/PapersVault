## Problem

Most contemporary video representations are biased toward static properties (objects, scenes) and poorly encode visual change over time. The paper identifies this gap by introducing chiral action recognition—distinguishing temporally opposite actions like 'opening vs. closing a door'—as a diagnostic task, and shows that even large video models (VideoJEPA, VideoMAE, InternVideo2.5) struggle with it, revealing a lack of genuine time-sensitivity in existing video embeddings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253102/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video as 16 linearly sampled frames at 224×224 resolution, each independently encoded by a frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 backbone to yield a 384-dim CLS token per frame. The output is a compact 768-dim descriptor formed by concatenating a static vector z_s and a dynamic vector z_d (each 384-dim), which can be used directly for linear-probe classification or concatenated with features from other video models to improve their performance on standard benchmarks.

## Method

LiFT (Linearized Feature Trajectories) is a self-supervised autoencoder that adapts frozen DINOv2 per-frame features into a time-aware video descriptor, inspired by the Perceptual Straightening hypothesis that visual change maps to straight lines in perceptual space. The encoder is a 4-layer, 8-head Transformer that processes the projected frame-feature sequence along with two learnable tokens (z_s for static content, z_d for dynamic change), while the decoder enforces a linearity constraint by computing z_t = z_s + (t/T)·z_d and reconstructing each frame's feature via a 2-layer MLP. Training uses a standard L2 reconstruction loss plus an orthogonality regularizer between z_s and z_d, all in an unsupervised setting on ~240K Kinetics-400 videos with pre-computed features. The entire trainable module has only 8.7M parameters beyond the frozen 22M DINOv2 encoder, making it far more compact than native video models (e.g., VideoJEPA's 305M parameters) while outperforming them on chiral action recognition by over 7%.

## Results

On the proposed chiral action recognition benchmark, LiFT achieves 86.6% on SSv2, 75.5% on EPIC-Kitchens, and 69.5% on Charades, outperforming all baselines while using a compact 768-dim descriptor. The closest competitor, VideoMAE with time-concatenation pooling (8192-dim), reaches 85.7/75.0/66.1, trailing LiFT by 0.9/0.5/3.4 points respectively. On standard action recognition benchmarks (Table 5a), concatenating LiFT with VideoJEPA lifts top-1 accuracy by +3.9 on K400, +1.3 on UCF-101, +1.9 on HMDB-51, and +2.7 on SSv2, while combining with VideoMAE yields gains of +8.6/+5.2/+6.1/+6.0 across the same four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiFT outperforms all 16 baselines on all three chiral datasets. Against the strongest image-model baselines, LiFT beats DINOv2 concatenation (79.7/74.1/65.8) by 6.9/1.4/3.7 points and SigLIP2 concatenation (76.8/74.7/67.8) by 9.8/0.8/1.7 points, while using a 768-dim descriptor versus 6144–12288 dims. Against native video models, LiFT surpasses VideoMAE with time concatenation (85.7/75.0/66.1) by 0.9/0.5/3.4 points, VideoJEPA with time concatenation (85.4/70.8/57.1) by 1.2/4.7/12.4 points, and InternVideo2.5 with time concatenation (80.0/70.9/62.8) by 6.6/4.6/6.7 points. Parameter-efficient adaptation methods (ST-Adapter at 50.5/63.7/54.4 and DiST at 52.1/59.9/55.9) perform near chance level, confirming that supervised dataset-specific tuning does not yield general time-aware descriptors. Notably, even the 6B-parameter InternVideo2.5 with average pooling (55.8/66.1/55.4) is far outperformed by LiFT's 30M-parameter model, highlighting that model scale alone does not guarantee temporal sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiFT repurposes the Perceptual Straightening hypothesis (Henaff et al., 2019) — that biological visual systems transform complex pixel dynamics into straighter trajectories — by learning an autoencoder that maps frozen DINOv2 feature sequences to ordered points on lines in a latent space. This departs from the dominant paradigm of training large multi-frame video models (VideoJEPA, VideoMAE, InternVideo2.5) from scratch on millions of videos, and from PEFT methods (ST-Adapter, DiST) that require dataset-specific supervised fine-tuning. The paper also carves out a new evaluation axis: while prior work on temporal ordering, arrow of time, and reversible actions studies specific temporal properties, chiral action recognition is framed as a general linear-separability test of time-awareness in any video descriptor, positioned as complementary to (not subsumed by) those formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CiA benchmark provides a simple, interpretable probe for time-awareness that is missing from standard action recognition suites, which are known to be biased toward static/spatial understanding. The finding that a 30M-parameter adapter on frozen image features outperforms 10× larger video models on temporal discrimination, and that concatenating LiFT descriptors consistently lifts VideoJEPA/VideoMAE/InternVideo2.5 on four benchmarks, suggests that temporal information is underexploited in current video representations and can be recovered cheaply. Key limitations include reliance on CLS tokens only (missing spatial detail, especially for horizontal motion) and the relatively small scale of the chiral benchmark; open directions include combining the linearization recipe with masked modeling or autoregressive pre-training, and using dense feature maps to address the horizontal-motion gap.
