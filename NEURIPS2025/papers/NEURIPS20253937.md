## Problem

Existing approaches to cultural awareness in language models treat culture as static background knowledge—either encoded as fixed labels during training or imposed as external prompts at inference—overlooking its dynamic, evolving nature. This limits reliability in downstream tasks requiring genuine cultural sensitivity, as neither data-centric nor prompt-based paradigms integrate culture as an internal, adaptive reasoning state within the model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253937/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CALM takes a single text sequence (cultural statement, social media post, or QA pair) and internally disentangles it into three parallel representations—task semantics, explicit cultural concepts, and latent cultural signals—via a shared LLM backbone (Qwen3-32B). The final output is a task-specific prediction (binary/multi-class label or culturally grounded response), but the intermediate cultural identity representation can trigger a reflective re-reasoning cycle if the generated output's reverse-inferred cultural profile falls below a cosine-similarity threshold with the model's internal identity state.

## Method

CALM is a four-stage closed-loop architecture: (1) an Abstract Cognitive Space disentangles input into task semantics, explicit cultural concepts (trained via span infilling), and latent cultural signals (trained via style classification); (2) a Contrastive Window applies type-specific SimCLR-style contrastive learning to organize each cultural stream into structured clusters; (3) an Identity Alignment Pool performs Gumbel-Softmax soft clustering on both streams, aligns them via multi-head cross-attention from latent to explicit clusters, and routes the aligned representation through a Mixture-of-Experts with expert-choice routing organized along three theory-grounded dimensions (Contextuality, Interpersonality, Normativity), with residual fusion preserving original cultural signals; (4) a Reflective Reasoning Loop generates a culturally conditioned self-prompt, performs reasoning, then runs an inverse identity calibration head to detect and correct cultural misalignment in a single corrective cycle. The key distinction from prior work is that culture is modeled as a dynamic internal reasoning state with self-correction, rather than a static attribute or external prompt modifier.

## Results

On CultureAtlas (cultural commonsense reasoning), CALM achieves 89.1% F1 (All Culture), outperforming all open-source baselines by 15+ points over Vicuna-13B (73.7%) while approaching GPT-4 (93.1%). On CREHate (cross-cultural hate speech), CALM reaches 83.66% average accuracy, exceeding GPT-4 by 5.5 points. On UniVaR (value reasoning), CALM attains 23.04% Acc@1 and 65.41% Acc@10, surpassing the UniVaR baseline by 4.4 and 3.7 points respectively. On EMGSD (social bias detection), CALM achieves 85.3% Macro F1, outperforming BERT (82.8%) and GPT-4o (64.8%) by 2.5 and 20.5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CALM outperforms all open-source LLM baselines (LLaMA-2-7B/13B, Vicuna-7B/13B) on CultureAtlas by 15–33 F1 points, with the largest gap on low-resource cultures where Vicuna-13B scores 74.3% versus CALM's 88.6%. Against the proprietary GPT-4, CALM trails by 4.0 F1 points on CultureAtlas but surpasses it by 5.5 points on CREHate (83.66% vs. 78.20% accuracy) and by 20.5 points on EMGSD (85.3% vs. 64.8% Macro F1). On UniVaR, CALM beats all embedding-based baselines (GloVe, BERT, RoBERTa, XLM-R, MPNet, LaBSE) by 19–22 Acc@1 points and outperforms the purpose-built UniVaR model by 4.4 Acc@1 points. On CREHate, CALM exceeds GPT-3.5 (71.34%), Orca-2 (69.26%), Flan-T5 (68.17%), and OPT (66.25%) by 12–17 accuracy points, maintaining balanced performance across all five English-speaking regions. On EMGSD, CALM surpasses fine-tuned BERT (82.8%) and ALBERT-V2 (81.5%) while matching DistilBERT's carbon footprint (158.42 gCO2e vs. 156.48 gCO2e).

## Relation to Prior Work

CALM repurposes two well-established mechanisms—SimCLR-style contrastive learning for representation structuring and expert-choice MoE routing for conditional specialization—into a unified pipeline for cultural awareness, a task where prior work (CultureBank, CulturePark, SeaLLMs) has treated culture as static background knowledge or external prompt-level constraints. The key conceptual departure is modeling culture as an internal, dynamic reasoning state that is disentangled, clustered, aligned, and self-corrected within the model, rather than encoded as fixed labels or injected via demographic priming. The reflective reasoning loop, inspired by metacognitive self-monitoring, is a novel addition that has no direct analogue in prior cultural-alignment literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CALM addresses a persistent gap in LLM research: the assumption that culture is static and fully specifiable at training time. By demonstrating that a structured cultural reasoning architecture (not raw model scale) drives performance, the paper offers a practical path toward culturally adaptive systems without requiring ever-larger backbones. However, significant open questions remain: the three predefined communicative dimensions (Contextuality, Interpersonality, Normativity) risk essentialist oversimplification, the framework inherits bias from its training corpora, and no universal cross-cultural evaluation standard yet exists. Future work on temporal cultural dynamics, multimodal cultural grounding, and genuinely open-ended cultural identity (beyond fixed dimension axes) would be needed to move beyond the current structured-abstraction paradigm.
