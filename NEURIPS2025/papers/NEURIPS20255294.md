## Problem

Existing unified multimodal LLMs rely on separate or mismatched representations for visual understanding (continuous semantic features like CLIP) and generation (pixel-level discrete tokens like VQVAE), which limits unified reasoning, complicates interleaved tasks, and breaks the autoregressive paradigm. No prior work achieves a fully discrete, semantic, and shared visual representation that simultaneously supports strong understanding and high-fidelity generation within a single next-token-prediction framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255294/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For understanding, a 384×384 RGB image is encoded by TA-Tok into 729, 169, or 81 discrete tokens (depending on scale factor) and fed alongside a text prompt to the LLM, which produces a text response. For generation, a text prompt is autoregressively decoded into a sequence of discrete visual tokens, which are then passed to either an autoregressive de-tokenizer (256/512/1024 px) or a diffusion de-tokenizer (512 px) to produce the final image. Both modalities share a single expanded vocabulary of 65,536 visual tokens plus the original LLM text tokens, so the model treats images and text identically at the token level.

## Method

The core contribution is the Text-Aligned Tokenizer (TA-Tok), which discretizes SigLIP2 image features via a codebook C = EW, where E is a frozen subset of LLM token embeddings and W is a learnable projection matrix, ensuring visual tokens are semantically grounded in the LLM's latent space from initialization. Scale-Adaptive Pooling and Decoding let the model trade off token count (729/169/81) to match task demands for detail versus efficiency. Two complementary Generative De-Tokenizers decode the discrete semantic tokens into pixels: an autoregressive model (trained from scratch on VQVAE latents) for fast, unified-AR inference, and a diffusion model (initialized from SANA-0.6B) for higher fidelity. The unified MLLM (Tar) simply expands the LLM vocabulary with the TA-Tok codebook and trains with standard cross-entropy next-token prediction over mixed text–image sequences, augmented with novel I2I and TI2I pre-training tasks that empirically accelerate convergence and improve generation quality.

## Results

On visual understanding, Tar-7B achieves 87.8 POPE, 1571 MME-P, 355 MME-C, 74.4 MMB, 73.0 SEED, 61.3 GQA, and 39.0 MMMU, matching or exceeding Janus-Pro-7B (the strongest unified baseline) on most benchmarks. On visual generation, Tar-7B reaches 0.84 GenEval overall and 84.19 DPG Bench overall, surpassing all unified models on GenEval (vs. 0.80 for Janus-Pro-7B) and tying Janus-Pro-7B on DPG. The smaller Tar-1.5B model achieves 88.4 POPE (best among 1.5B-scale models) and 0.76 GenEval / 82.96 DPG, outperforming Janus-Pro-1B on both generation metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On visual understanding, Tar-7B (87.8 POPE, 1571 MME-P, 355 MME-C) matches or slightly exceeds Janus-Pro-7B (87.4, 1567, 260) on POPE and MME, but trails it on MMB (74.4 vs. 79.2) and MMMU (39.0 vs. 41.0). Tar-1.5B achieves the best POPE score (88.4) among all 1.5B-scale models, surpassing Janus-Pro-1.5B (86.2) and Harmon-1.5B (87.6), while also leading on MME-C (342 vs. 321 for Harmon). On visual generation, Tar-7B achieves 0.84 GenEval overall, outperforming Janus-Pro-7B (0.80), ILLUME-7B (0.61), and Transfusion-7B (0.63), and even exceeding the generation-only SANA-1.5 (0.81) and SD3-Medium (0.74). On DPG Bench, Tar-7B (84.19) ties Janus-Pro-7B (84.19) and slightly trails SANA-1.5 (84.70), while Tar-1.5B (82.96) edges out Janus-Pro-1B (82.63). The key finding is that Tar's fully discrete, text-aligned representation (D,S) achieves competitive understanding performance without a continuous semantic encoder, while simultaneously delivering the strongest generation results among unified models.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tar positions itself against the growing family of unified MLLMs (Emu3, Chameleon, Janus, Show-o, Transfusion, ILLUME, VILA-U, UniTok) that attempt to handle both visual understanding and generation in one model. Its key departure is the Text-Aligned Tokenizer (TA-Tok), which discretizes SigLIP2 semantic features through a codebook initialized from frozen LLM token embeddings, thereby eliminating the separate alignment stage used by Janus-style methods and avoiding the pixel-level quantization errors of VQVAE-based approaches (Emu3, Chameleon). For decoding, it decouples the semantic token space from pixel synthesis via two complementary generative de-tokenizers (autoregressive and diffusion-based, the latter initialized from SANA), rather than forcing the LLM itself to produce pixel-level tokens or breaking the autoregressive paradigm with diffusion objectives as in Show-o and Transfusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully discrete, text-aligned semantic representation can match continuous-semantic models (e.g., Janus-Pro with SigLIP) on understanding benchmarks while simultaneously enabling competitive generation, challenging the prevailing assumption that understanding requires continuous features. The text-aligned codebook initialization is a practical contribution: it outperforms both random initialization and data-hungry pre-alignment stages with less training data, offering a cleaner recipe for building unified MLLMs. Open questions include whether the reliance on external de-tokenizers (AR or diffusion) limits the model's ability to perform fine-grained editing or multi-turn image manipulation, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or higher-resolution generation without the current resolution ceiling imposed by the de-tokenizer.
