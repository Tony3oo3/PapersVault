## Problem

Reconstructing undistorted underwater scenes from aerial views is hindered by refraction at the wavy air-water interface, and supervised methods fail to generalize because real ocean waves are superpositions of many wave types with no available large-scale ground truth. Prior unsupervised approaches (e.g., NDIR) model distortion fields without explicitly recovering the water surface shape, missing a physically grounded signal that could improve reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253206/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short sequence of 10 distorted RGB frames (50 fps on Real1, 125 fps on TianSet) captured by a stationary orthographic camera above a static planar underwater scene, and is trained per-sequence rather than across a dataset. It outputs a single undistorted image of the scene plus a per-frame 2D water surface height map, the latter being a byproduct of the physics-based distortion model rather than a separate prediction head.

## Method

The architecture uses two SIREN neural-field networks: a time-conditioned surface-height network H_θ(x,t) that predicts the water surface elevation at every spatial position and time, and a spatial image network I_φ(x) that predicts the underlying scene color. The key mechanism is that the spatial gradient ∇h(x,t) is obtained by backpropagating through H_θ, and Snell's-law first-order approximation converts this gradient into a per-pixel distortion map d(x,t) = (1−1/n)·h₀·∇h(x,t), which warps the image network's input coordinates to synthesize each observed distorted frame. Training is fully unsupervised with a single L1 reconstruction loss over all frames, preceded by a lightweight initialization stage that drives distortion to zero and fits the mean image. The choice of SIREN is central: its sinusoidal activations make the network's output and its spatial derivatives naturally compatible, so the gradient needed for refraction is computed in the same forward pass without auxiliary supervision, and the simplified one-term loss outperforms NDIR's three-term objective.

## Results

On the Real1 dataset, the proposed method achieves average PSNR 19.78, SSIM 0.61, and LPIPS 0.12, outperforming the unsupervised baseline NDIR (19.44 / 0.59 / 0.14) and the supervised method Li et al. (17.14 / 0.53 / 0.20). On TianSet, it reaches PSNR 18.50, SSIM 0.40, and LPIPS 0.17, again leading NDIR (18.22 / 0.37 / 0.20) and Li et al. (17.12 / 0.39 / 0.22). On the simulated dataset, the method surpasses NDIR across all three wave types, with the largest gain on Ripple waves (PSNR 23.07 vs 21.50, LPIPS 0.05 vs 0.07). The method additionally outputs a water-surface height estimate with RMSE 0.115 and Abs Rel 0.0635 on the synthetic data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary unsupervised baseline NDIR [11], the proposed method wins on average PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on both Real1 and TianSet, with the most consistent advantage in LPIPS (0.12 vs 0.14 on Real1; 0.17 vs 0.20 on TianSet). NDIR edges out the proposed method only on the Math sequence (PSNR 24.07 vs 23.98, SSIM 0.62 vs 0.60), while the two are essentially tied on the Eye sequence. Against the supervised method Li et al. [12], the proposed method dominates across all metrics on both datasets, improving average PSNR by roughly 2.6 dB on Real1 and 1.4 dB on TianSet, while also being unsupervised. Compared to NeRT [10], the proposed method achieves substantially lower LPIPS (0.12 vs 0.29 on Real1; 0.17 vs 0.29 on TianSet), indicating markedly better perceptual fidelity, though NeRT is occasionally competitive on PSNR and SSIM (e.g., 18.60 vs 18.50 PSNR on TianSet). The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the single-term reconstruction loss (Eq. 4) outperforms NDIR's three-term loss (Eq. 5) for this architecture, yielding PSNR 19.78 vs 19.42 and LPIPS 0.121 vs 0.13.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unsupervised refractive-distortion removal framework of NDIR (Li et al., CVPR 2021), which uses a grid-deformation network to predict per-pixel warping, and on SIREN (Sitzmann et al., NeurIPS 2020) for modeling continuous signals and their derivatives. The key departure is replacing the learned distortion field with a single time-conditioned SIREN that predicts water surface height, from which distortions are computed analytically via Snell's law—grounding the warping in physical wave dynamics rather than learning it end-to-end. This also simplifies the training loss from NDIR's three-term objective to a single reconstruction term, and yields a direct surface-height estimate as a byproduct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is attractive for aerial ocean monitoring (coral surveys, fish-farm inspection, drowning detection) because it is fully unsupervised and requires no paired ground-truth of surface geometry, which is essentially unavailable for real open-water conditions. However, the assumptions of a static planar scene, orthographic viewing, and small-amplitude waves limit direct applicability to complex 3D underwater scenes or oblique camera angles, as acknowledged in the paper's own real-world coral-tank test. Open questions include whether the restoration can serve as a reliable pre-processing step for downstream tasks like monocular SLAM or feature matching, and how the method behaves under strong non-planar geometry or large wave amplitudes where the first-order Snell approximation breaks down.
