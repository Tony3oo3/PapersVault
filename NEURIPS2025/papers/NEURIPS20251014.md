## Problem

In entropy-regularized RL, the unique τ-optimal policy converges to a well-defined limit only in tabular MDPs; in general continuous MDPs, the vanishing-temperature limit of policies and their derived objects (value functions, return distributions) is not well understood. Moreover, existing distributional RL methods produce non-convergent iterates in the control setting, leaving no principled way to estimate the return distribution of an optimal policy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a discounted MDP (X, A, P, r, γ) with a fixed reference policy π^ref and a temperature schedule τ→0 paired with a decoupled temperature σ(τ) satisfying σ/τ→0. It produces the optimality-filtered reference policy π^{ref,★} (the restriction of π^ref to expected-value-optimal actions per state), its associated return distribution function ζ^{π^{ref,★}}, and the reference-optimal action-value function q_ref^★. The framework handles both discrete and continuous action spaces, with convergence in total variation for discrete A and weakly for continuous A.

## Method

The core mechanism is the temperature decoupling gambit: at each τ, the policy is constructed as π^{τ,σ} = G_τ q_σ^★, where the potential q_σ^★ is computed at a smaller (amplified) temperature σ while the Boltzmann-Gibbs policy temperature remains τ. This decoupling ensures that the TV distance between G_τ q_σ^★ and G_τ q_ref^★ vanishes as τ→0 (bounded by (σ/τ)·log p_ref), yielding convergence to the interpretable optimality-filtered reference policy π^{ref,★}. For distributional RL, the paper introduces soft distributional Bellman operators T_τ^π and T_τ^★ that incorporate KL regularization penalties into the return distribution update, proving they are γ-contractions in Wasserstein distance—unlike the standard distributional optimality operator whose iterates diverge in the control setting. The resulting two-phase algorithm (control at σ, then evaluation at τ with the decoupled policy) provides the first convergent iterative scheme for approximating the return distribution of a reference-optimal policy to arbitrary accuracy.

## Results

The paper establishes that under the temperature decoupling gambit (σ/τ→0), policies π^{τ,σ} converge to the optimality-filtered reference policy π^{ref,★} in total variation (discrete A) or weakly (continuous A) as τ→0 (Theorem 3.9). Return distribution functions ζ^{τ,σ} converge to those of π^{ref,★} in Wasserstein distance d_{p;p',ω} (Theorem 3.10). For the soft distributional Bellman operator T_τ^★, iterates converge at rate d_p(ζ̄^n, ζ̄^{τ,★}) ≤ C_{p,τ,γ}·n·γ^{n/p}·d_p(ζ̄^0, ζ̄^{τ,★}) (Theorem 4.5), in contrast to the standard distributional optimality operator T^★ whose iterates diverge. Numerical demonstrations on small MDPs confirm that π^{τ,σ} recovers the uniform-on-optimal-actions policy while π^{τ,★} collapses to a single action, and that 32-bit precision soft distributional dynamic programming with σ=τ² and 1000 iterations accurately approximates the reference-optimal return distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two policy schemes: standard ERL (π^{τ,★} = G_τ q_τ^★) and the temperature decoupling gambit (π^{τ,σ} = G_τ q_σ^★ with σ=τ²). On a three-state MDP (Figure 3.1), both converge to optimal policies as τ→0, but they converge to *different* optimal policies: π^{τ,★} collapses to δ_{a₁} at state x₀, while π^{τ,σ} samples all optimal actions uniformly at x₀, matching the interpretable π^{ref,★}. For distributional RL, the paper compares the standard distributional optimality operator T^★ (from Bellemare et al. [5]) against the proposed soft operator T_τ^★. Figure 4.1 shows that T^★ iterates diverge (oscillate) while T_τ^★ iterates converge stably to the soft return distribution. The paper's Theorem 4.7 further shows that combining T_σ^★ (control) with T_τ^{π} (evaluation) at decoupled temperatures yields a convergent, reference-optimal return distribution estimate, which the standard T^★ cannot provide in the control setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the entropy-regularized RL (ERL) framework of Neu et al. [28] and Ziebart et al. [48]—which guarantees a unique Boltzmann-Gibbs optimal policy at any fixed temperature—by analyzing the vanishing-temperature limit in general (continuous) MDPs, a regime where prior convergence results were limited to tabular settings. It also builds on the distributional Bellman operators of Bellemare et al. [5], introducing soft (entropy-regularized) distributional Bellman operators that, unlike their unregularized counterparts, produce convergent iterates in the control setting. The key conceptual departure is the 'temperature decoupling gambit,' which deliberately uses a suboptimal policy (at an amplified temperature σ) for evaluation while optimizing at temperature τ, yielding a limiting policy (the optimality-filtered reference policy) that is distinct from the standard ERL vanishing-temperature limit even in tabular MDPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that in continuous action spaces, even state-of-the-art methods can at best achieve a 'reference-optimal' value q_ref^★ that is strictly below the classical optimal value q^★, reframing what is achievable in practice. The temperature decoupling gambit provides a principled, interpretable mechanism for recovering a diversity-preserving optimal policy and, via the new soft distributional Bellman operators, the first convergent iterative scheme for estimating the return distribution of such a policy—critical for safety-critical applications where return variance matters. Key limitations include the qualitative (non-quantitative) nature of the convergence guarantees, making it unclear a priori which temperatures suffice for ε-approximation, and the empirical validation is restricted to small tabular MDPs without deep RL experiments.
