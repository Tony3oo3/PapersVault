## Problem

Generative super-resolution models can hallucinate textures and details, yet existing uncertainty quantification methods either produce non-intuitive interval-valued pixels or continuous confidence scores lacking formal probabilistic guarantees. This paper asks: can we produce a simple binary confidence mask that tells users exactly which regions of a super-resolved image to trust, with distribution-free statistical guarantees, while remaining agnostic to the underlying black-box generative model?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254488/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image X ∈ [0,1]^{w×h×3} and a black-box generative super-resolution model μ (e.g., a diffusion model, possibly API-locked) as inputs, and produces a binary confidence mask M_α(X) ∈ {0,1}^{kw×kh} indicating which pixels of the predicted high-resolution image are trustworthy at fidelity level α. Calibration requires only unlabelled high-resolution images not present in the model's training set; all color comparisons are performed in perceptually uniform Lab space. The indecision score σ is computed from M stochastic realizations of μ(X), and the threshold t_α is found via dynamic programming in O(nd log d) time.

## Method

The core mechanism is a conformal risk control calibration: a model indecision score σ (a smoothed pixel-wise variance over M generated images, computed via a kernel-based second-moment decomposition to avoid undoing the super-resolution) is thresholded at t_α to yield the binary mask M_α(X) = {p : [σ(X)]_p ≤ t_α}. The threshold is calibrated on unlabelled hold-out data so that the expected fidelity error E[sup_{p∈M_α(X)} D_p(μ(X), Y)] ≤ α, where D_p is a user-chosen local similarity metric (pointwise L1, neighborhood-averaged, or human-annotated semantic). The paper proves two additional guarantees beyond the base conformal fidelity bound: a lower bound on PSNR within the trusted region (Proposition 3.1) and a worst-case miscoverage bound under data leakage where some calibration samples overlap the training distribution (Proposition 3.2). The approach is fully model-agnostic—no access to model internals is needed—and the choice of D_p and the low-pass smoothing kernel K provide practical knobs that significantly affect mask size and interpretability.

## Results

On the Liu4K dataset with SinSR as the base model, the method achieves an average PSNR of 32.75±1.55 (semantic D_p) and 30.23±1.12 (non-semantic D_p) at fidelity level α=0.075, compared to 26.83±1.06 without any uncertainty quantification. The fidelity error is tightly controlled at 0.03±0.01 (semantic) and 0.07±0.01 (non-semantic) at α=0.075, well below the 0.14±0.01 baseline. Under distribution shift (calibrated on Liu4K, evaluated on DIV2K), PSNR drops to ~13.3±6.9 but still exceeds the 11.73±0.39 no-method baseline. The conformal mask size at α=0.100 is 0.73±0.07 (semantic) and 0.23±0.06 (non-semantic), indicating the method retains meaningful trusted regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior uncertainty quantification methods: Angelopoulos et al. (2022b), which produces interval-valued pixels, and Kutiel et al. (2023), which produces continuous per-pixel confidence scores. Direct quantitative comparison is infeasible due to output modality differences (binary masks vs. intervals/continuous scores), so the comparison is qualitative (Figure 5). The authors argue that Angelopoulos et al.'s lower+upper image pair is difficult to interpret visually, while Kutiel et al.'s continuous masks closely mirror the original image structure rather than isolating meaningful uncertainty regions. Critically, the authors prove that Kutiel et al.'s calibration procedure does not actually satisfy its stated probabilistic guarantee (Appendix B, with a counterexample), whereas the proposed method has a proven conformal fidelity guarantee (Theorem 2.1). Against the 'no uncertainty quantification' baseline (trusting the entire image), the method improves PSNR by ~5.9 dB (semantic, α=0.075: 32.75 vs. 26.83) while reducing fidelity error from 0.14 to 0.03, demonstrating that the confidence mask meaningfully identifies trustworthy regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal risk control framework (Angelopoulos et al., 2022a) and split conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005) to the setting of generative image super-resolution, producing binary confidence masks rather than the interval-valued images of Angelopoulos et al. (2022b) or the continuous (but unguaranteed) scores of Kutiel et al. (2023). It is closely related in structure to conformal approaches for semantic segmentation (Csillag et al., 2023; Mossina et al., 2024), but adapts the fidelity metric to a local, pixel-neighborhood image similarity that is interpretable to end users. The method is model-agnostic and operates atop any black-box diffusion-based super-resolution model (evaluated with SinSR), requiring only unlabelled calibration data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first conformal-prediction-based UQ method for generative super-resolution that yields interpretable binary trust masks with provable fidelity-error, PSNR, and data-leakage-robustness guarantees, making it directly applicable to API-locked foundation models. Key limitations include the need for multiple stochastic generations per input (computational cost), the exchangeability assumption restricting guarantees to in-distribution behavior, and the reliance on a local similarity metric that may not capture all perceptual differences. Open questions include jointly estimating uncertainty with the upscaled image, extending guarantees under significant distribution shift, and adapting the framework to other restoration tasks beyond super-resolution and colorization.
