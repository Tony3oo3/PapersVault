## Problem

Link sign prediction in signed bipartite graphs faces a fundamental scalability bottleneck: feature-based SGRL methods require GPU memory that grows at least linearly with dataset size (making large-scale training infeasible), while subgraph-based methods like SELO incur prohibitive per-query inference costs. No existing approach simultaneously achieves sublinear memory scaling during training and fast inference on graphs with tens of millions of edges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253286/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a signed bipartite graph G = (U, V, E⁺, E⁻) and a queried edge (u, v) ∉ E⁺ ∪ E⁻, and outputs a binary sign prediction (positive/negative) via a sigmoid head. During training, a topological subgraph of at most T=800 nodes and K=4 layers is extracted per query; during inference with the distillation module, only the pre-computed node embeddings Z_u and Z_v are needed, combined via element-wise product and a small MLP. The distilled node embeddings also serve as general-purpose representations for downstream tasks like node clustering and classification.

## Method

SBSN comprises three modules operating in two training stages. First, a Topological Subgraph Extractor iteratively samples intermediate node layers so that every node in the subgraph lies on a simple path between u and v (unlike standard k-hop neighbor sampling), with the optimal layer count derived theoretically as K_opt = max{2, ⌊Tp/e⌋} + 2. Second, a Directed SignFlow Aggregator performs edge-granularity message passing: node features from consecutive layers are transformed into edge features via an MLP, edge features are updated by pooling over neighboring edges, and attention weights derived from edge features govern weighted aggregation of source-layer node features—this edge-based mechanism captures richer structural patterns than node-feature-only GCN-style aggregation. Third, a Node Feature Distillation Module (trained in a second stage) learns to approximate the subgraph embedding F_sub(u,v) from the element-wise product Z_u ⊙ Z_v via a linear layer, using a cosine similarity loss plus binary cross-entropy, reducing per-query inference from O(T²(log N + d²)) to O(d²) with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On medium-scale datasets, SBSN achieves the best AUC across all four benchmarks: 92.0±1.4 on Bonanza (vs. 59.6 for the best baseline SGA), 88.0±0.4 on U.S. House (vs. 84.8 for SBGNN), 74.5±0.1 on Amazon-Book (vs. 65.2 for SELO), and 74.3±0.2 on ML-1M (vs. 73.5 for SELO), representing improvements of 28.5%, 3.2%, 9.3%, and 0.8% respectively. On large-scale datasets (ML-10M, ML-32M, Amazon-Book-51M), SGRL-based methods and SELO fail due to OOM or timeout, while SBSN achieves AUC of 75.2, 78.3, and 73.1 respectively, outperforming Node2vec by 20+ points. The distilled variant SBSN-node retains most of the subgraph-based performance (e.g., 92.5 vs. 92.0 AUC on Bonanza) while reducing inference time by a factor of ~618,000×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBSN outperforms all seven baselines on AUC across every medium-scale dataset. The largest margin is on Bonanza, where SBSN (92.0 AUC) surpasses the best baseline SGA (59.6) by 28.5 points, attributed to the highly imbalanced label distribution (98% positive). On U.S. House, SBSN (88.0) beats the closest competitor SBGNN (84.8) by 3.2 points; on Amazon-Book, it leads SELO (65.2) by 9.3 points; and on ML-1M, it edges out SELO (73.5) by 0.8 points. Among SGRL-based methods (SGCN, SGCL, SGA, SBGNN, SBGCL), SBGNN is consistently the strongest on U.S. House but underperforms SBSN by 3–10 AUC points on other datasets. SELO, the only other subgraph-based method, is competitive on ML-1M (73.5 vs. 74.3) but suffers from prohibitive inference time (354,353 s vs. 2,658 s for SBSN on ML-1M) and OOM on large-scale datasets. On large-scale datasets (ML-10M, ML-32M, Amazon-Book-51M), all SGRL methods and SELO fail (OOM or timeout), leaving only Node2vec as a viable baseline, which SBSN outperforms by 15–22 AUC points.

## Relation to Prior Work

SBSN sits at the intersection of two established lines of work on signed bipartite graphs: full-graph signed GNNs (SGCN, SBGNN, SBGCL, SGA) that suffer from linear GPU memory growth, and subgraph-based encoders (SELO, SUBGNN) that offer local structural advantages but incur prohibitive inference costs and lack reusable node features. Rather than extending either family directly, SBSN repurposes the subgraph extraction paradigm with a key architectural departure: a directed, edge-feature-based SignFlow Aggregator that propagates information only along paths connecting the queried pair, combined with a post-hoc node feature distillation stage that converts subgraph embeddings into lightweight node representations. The theoretical result on optimal topological layer count (Theorem 1) provides a principled design choice absent from prior subgraph methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine scalability bottleneck: SGRL methods hit OOM on datasets with ~10M+ edges, while subgraph methods like SELO require ~354,000 seconds for inference on ML-1M. SBSN's sublinear memory scaling (demonstrated up to 51M edges on a single 80GB GPU) and the 618,000× inference speedup from distillation make signed bipartite link prediction viable in production-scale e-commerce and social network settings. Open questions remain around dynamic/evolving signed graphs, the heuristic nature of the subgraph sampling strategy (random vs. degree-based choices are dataset-dependent), and whether the distillation module's approximation loss grows with graph density. The method is also restricted to bipartite structures, limiting direct transfer to general signed graphs.
