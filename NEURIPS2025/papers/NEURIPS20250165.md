## Problem

Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) models require multiple iterative sampling steps over a fixed target space, making them unsuitable for real-time or streaming audio generation. While streaming generation has been explored in video diffusion, streaming audio generation from discrete neural codec tokens within a flow matching framework had not been addressed. Existing causal GAN-based decoders (e.g., Mimi in Moshi) suffer from perceptual degradation when reconstructing high-resolution waveforms from highly compressed low-bitrate tokens due to the absence of progressive refinement.

## Input / Output

The model takes discrete audio tokens from neural codecs (EnCodec at 75 Hz or Mimi at 12.5 Hz, using 8 RVQ quantizers) as conditioning, along with a context prompt of previously generated waveform frames (ratio γ=3, i.e., 24 prompt tokens per 8 stream tokens for EnCodec). The output is a raw 24 kHz waveform signal. For streaming, the input waveform is reshaped from 1D to 2D (height T/h, width h=160) via linear-reshape transformation rather than STFT, avoiding the need for future frames and enabling frame-level causal generation with 160 ms latency per token.

## Method

StreamFlow introduces Streaming Flow Matching (SFM), which partitions the sequence into data streams each containing a fixed context prompt (held at t=1) and a streaming window whose noise level decreases monotonically from t=1 to 0, enabling simultaneous multi-time vector field estimation in a single forward pass. The architecture uses Scale-DiT, a modified Diffusion Transformer that computes the difference between the residual stream and the MHSA/FFN output, applies layer normalization to this difference, and scales it by a learnable adaptive rate ρ (clamped to [10⁻⁴, 1]) before the skip connection, improving training stability without adding parameters. Training is two-stage: SFM pre-training with causal noising and condition dropout, followed by adversarial fine-tuning using MPD, MS-STFTD, and MS-SB-CQTD discriminators. Inference uses the Euler ODE solver with 8 steps for EnCodec streaming (4 for parallel) and 2 steps for Mimi, with generated frames prepended as in-context prompts for temporal coherence.

## Results

On EnCodec token reconstruction (universal speech test set), the non-streaming StreamFlow (170M) achieves M-STFT 0.997, PESQ 3.473, UTMOS 3.450, and MOS 4.03±0.02, outperforming all parallel baselines. The streaming StreamFlow-Base (175M) achieves M-STFT 1.061, PESQ 3.335, UTMOS 3.325, and MOS 4.03±0.02, surpassing even the non-streaming RFWave (M-STFT 1.280, PESQ 3.020) and MBD (M-STFT 1.612, PESQ 2.645). On Mimi token reconstruction (Nq=8), StreamFlow improves WER from 6.93 to 6.16 and UTMOS from 3.506 to 3.910 over the original Mimi decoder, while reducing latency from 80 ms to 160 ms with only 2 ODE steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-streaming baselines, StreamFlow-Base (streaming) outperforms Vocos on M-STFT (1.061 vs 1.074), PESQ (3.335 vs 3.051), and UTMOS (3.325 vs 3.100), and beats RFWave on M-STFT (1.061 vs 1.280), PESQ (3.335 vs 3.020), and UTMOS (3.325 vs 2.988), though RFWave retains a slight edge on periodicity error (0.078 vs 0.102). The much larger MBD (411M params) is clearly dominated by StreamFlow-Base on every metric, with M-STFT improving from 1.612 to 1.061 and PESQ from 2.645 to 3.335. Against the streaming EnCodec decoder, StreamFlow-Base achieves substantially higher quality (PESQ 3.335 vs 2.643, UTMOS 3.325 vs 2.542, MOS 4.03 vs 3.74) while using 175M vs 15M parameters. On Mimi token reconstruction, StreamFlow improves WER (6.16 vs 6.93), M-STFT (1.217 vs 1.352), PESQ (2.306 vs 2.266), and UTMOS (3.910 vs 3.506) over the original Mimi decoder, though Mimi retains a small advantage on CER (3.05 vs 3.26) and pitch error (50.7 vs 45.6). When integrated into the Moshi full-duplex speech language model, replacing the Mimi decoder with StreamFlow reduces CER from 7.59 to 7.19 and raises UTMOS from 3.610 to 3.847.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) from its established parallel, fixed-length generation paradigm to a streaming, token-wise setting by introducing causal noising along the time axis and simultaneous multi-time vector field estimation. It draws on the linear-reshape transformation from WaveNeXt to avoid STFT/iSTFT overhead in streaming, and adapts the nGPT-inspired normalization into Scale-DiT for improved DiT training stability. The work also builds on the two-stage training recipe (flow matching pre-training followed by adversarial fine-tuning) popularized by PeriodWave-Turbo, and positions itself against both GAN-based streaming decoders (causal EnCodec, Mimi) and parallel CFM vocoders (RFWave, MBD, Vocos).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StreamFlow demonstrates that flow matching can be made genuinely streaming without sacrificing quality, closing a gap that has kept CFM-based vocoders out of real-time speech language model pipelines. The successful drop-in replacement of the Mimi decoder in Moshi with measurable UTMOS and WER gains validates the practical utility of the approach for full-duplex dialogue systems. Key limitations remain: adversarial fine-tuning is still required for high fidelity (adding training complexity), the quality-latency trade-off is only partially addressed (two-step inference for Mimi), and the evaluation is confined to English speech on LibriTTS and a small universal test set. Open questions include whether the framework scales to music or general audio, whether the adversarial stage can be eliminated, and how the approach behaves at higher token rates or with single-quantizer codecs.
