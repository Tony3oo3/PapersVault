## Problem

Distilled small language models (SLMs) are far more efficient than large language models (LLMs) for chain-of-thought reasoning, but they diverge from LLM reasoning paths during generation, causing severe accuracy drops (e.g., 4.8× reduction for R1-1.5B vs. R1-32B on AIME). The paper identifies that only a small fraction of tokens (~5%) genuinely alter the reasoning path, while most SLM-LLM differences are neutral (e.g., abbreviations), and asks whether SLMs can follow LLM reasoning paths by selectively replacing only these path-divergent tokens.

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive decoding step, the SLM produces a next-token prediction along with its top-100 output logits, last-layer hidden states, and the predicted token's embedding. The 56M-parameter router consumes these three SLM-side signals to emit a binary divergence probability; if it exceeds a user-set threshold p_th, the LLM is invoked to overwrite that single token. The final output is a full text response (up to 32K tokens) that tracks the LLM's reasoning path, with the routing threshold providing post-training control over the accuracy-efficiency trade-off without retraining.

## Method

R2R combines three components: (1) a sentence-level path-following labeling pipeline that, for each SLM-LLM token mismatch, continues generation from both candidate tokens using the LLM until sentence end, then uses a verifier LLM (Qwen2.5-72B) to classify the difference as neutral or divergent, producing 7.6M token-level routing labels; (2) a lightweight 6-layer FFN router (56M params, hidden size 1024) trained with class-weighted BCE loss on SLM logits, token embeddings, and hidden states, exploiting the finding that divergent tokens exhibit 3.8× higher SLM entropy and are disproportionately low-frequency; and (3) an immediate-correction routing scheme that invokes the LLM via parallel prefilling to fix only the current divergent token in-place, eliminating the rollbacks and mid-draft invalidations inherent to speculative decoding. This design advances the test-time scaling Pareto frontier, achieving 92% of R1-32B accuracy with only 5.6B average activated parameters and a 2.8× wall-clock speedup.

## Results

On the AIME, GPQA-Diamond, and LiveCodeBench benchmarks, R2R (routing between R1-1.5B and R1-32B) achieves 46% average accuracy with only 5.6B average activated parameters and a total cost of 103 (K tokens × B params), surpassing R1-7B (28% acc., 7B params) by 1.6× and R1-14B (43% acc., 14B params) by 1.1× in average accuracy. R2R retains 92% of R1-32B's accuracy (46% vs. 50%) while using the LLM for only 11–15% of tokens, delivering a 2.8× wall-clock speedup (84.3 tok/s vs. 30.5 tok/s) on A800 GPUs. The router's 56M-parameter FFN introduces only 5.96% latency overhead, and R2R reduces per-token memory access by 5.4× compared to R1-32B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DeepSeek-R1 distilled family, R2R at 5.6B average parameters outperforms R1-7B by 1.6× in average accuracy (46% vs. 28%) and surpasses R1-14B by 1.1× (46% vs. 43%), while using less than half the parameters of R1-14B and only 17% of R1-32B's parameters. Compared to RouteLLM query-level routing methods (QR-SW, QR-MF, QR-BERT, QR-LLM), R2R achieves 1.4–2.4× higher accuracy at the 7B parameter level and 1.2–1.4× higher accuracy at the 14B level, with 1.5–1.6× lower total cost, because token-level routing avoids the suboptimal all-or-nothing model assignment per query. Against speculative decoding baselines EAGLE-2 and HASS (both using R1-32B), R2R delivers 1.18× and 1.15× higher throughput (84.3 vs. 71.4 and 73.3 tok/s) while requiring ~17× less total computation, since immediate per-token correction eliminates the rollback and redundant prefilling inherent in periodic draft-verify cycles. On the Qwen3 family (0.6B/8B/32B), R2R generalizes across architectures, consistently outperforming both the base models and query-level routing, and a router trained on one SLM-LLM pair transfers acceptably to a different LLM without retraining (e.g., 0.6B+32B router applied to 0.6B+8B retains 53% AIME accuracy vs. 65% when retrained).

## Relation to Prior Work

R2R departs from two established lines of work on efficient LLM inference. Query-level routing methods (RouteLLM, GraphRouter) select a single model per response based on learned preferences, while speculative decoding (EAGLE, HASS) requires exact token-level agreement between draft and target models, leading to low acceptance rates and costly rollbacks. R2R introduces a middle ground: token-level routing with a relaxed path-following objective that tolerates neutral SLM-LLM differences and only invokes the LLM for genuinely divergent tokens, eliminating the need for periodic verification and rollback. The path-following labeling strategy and the observation that only ~5% of tokens are truly divergent are novel contributions not present in either prior line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

R2R delivers a 2.8× wall-clock speedup over R1-32B at comparable accuracy and a 4.6× accuracy boost over R1-1.5B with under 15% LLM usage, making long CoT reasoning substantially more deployable. The finding that performance gaps between SLMs and LLMs are concentrated in a small fraction of path-divergent tokens reframes how the community thinks about distillation failure modes. Key limitations include reliance on greedy sampling (nucleus sampling is only briefly validated), the need for a separate LLM verifier during data labeling, and the absence of system-level optimizations that could further reduce the 6% router overhead. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-turn dialogue, agentic settings, or non-reasoning tasks where divergence patterns may differ.
