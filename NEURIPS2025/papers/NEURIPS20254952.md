## Problem

Gradient-based ODE solvers for diffusion model inference (e.g., DPM-Solver++, UniPC) accelerate denoising but lack an information-theoretic foundation, treating inference purely as numerical trajectory approximation while neglecting information transmission efficiency. This paper asks: what principle actually governs effective denoising, and can it be exploited to design better inference without reference trajectories?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254952/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EVODiff is a training-free inference-time method that takes a noisy latent vector x_T, a pre-trained diffusion model x_θ (data-prediction parameterization), and a discrete time schedule {t_i}, then iteratively produces a clean image x_0. It operates on both pixel-space models (CIFAR-10, FFHQ-64, ImageNet-64/256) and latent-space models (Stable Diffusion v1.4/v1.5, LSUN-Bedrooms), with the number of function evaluations (NFE) as the primary efficiency knob. For text-to-image generation, a text prompt is additionally consumed by the underlying diffusion model, and the method is applied as a drop-in replacement for the ODE solver.

## Method

EVODiff reframes diffusion inference through conditional entropy: under the Gaussian reverse-transition assumption, minimizing conditional entropy is equivalent to minimizing conditional variance, which in turn decomposes reconstruction error into variance and bias terms. The key algorithmic insight is that entropy-reduction (RE) based multi-step iterations—using model predictions from low-variance regions—outperform traditional finite-difference (FD) based gradient estimation. The method refines two parameters per step with closed-form solutions: ζ_i balances the variance between the gradient term and the first-order term by minimizing the difference between forward and inverse state estimations (evolution state differences), and η_i balances gradient errors between forward and backward finite differences. This yields a reference-free, second-order globally convergent solver (local error O(h³)) that requires no auxiliary reference trajectory, distinguishing it from methods like DPM-Solver-v3 that rely on empirical model statistics.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (50k samples, logSNR schedule), EVODiff achieves FID 2.78 at 10 NFE, a 45.5% reduction over DPM-Solver++ (5.10) and a 30.2% reduction over UniPC (3.98). On ImageNet-256 (10k samples, uniform schedule), EVODiff reaches FID 7.25 at 20 NFE, matching DPM-Solver-v3's 7.42 while using a reference-free approach, and reduces the NFE cost by 25% (from 20 to 15) for high-quality samples. On LSUN-Bedrooms (latent diffusion, 50k samples), EVODiff achieves FID 7.91 at 5 NFE, a 43.4% improvement over UniPC (13.97), while also reducing generation time by up to 5.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVODiff consistently outperforms all five baselines across all evaluated datasets. Against DPM-Solver++ (the primary baseline), EVODiff reduces FID by 45.5% on CIFAR-10 at 10 NFE (2.78 vs. 5.10) and by 22.0% on FFHQ-64 at 10 NFE (5.31 vs. 6.81). Against UniPC, EVODiff achieves a 30.2% FID reduction on CIFAR-10 at 10 NFE and a 43.4% reduction on LSUN-Bedrooms at 5 NFE (7.91 vs. 13.97). On ImageNet-256, EVODiff matches DPM-Solver-v3 at 10 NFE (both 8.14 FID) but surpasses it at 20 NFE (7.25 vs. 7.42), while being reference-free (DPM-Solver-v3 requires a reference trajectory). Against the Heun solver from EDM, EVODiff achieves dramatically lower FID (e.g., 2.78 vs. 22.82 on CIFAR-10 at 10 NFE). For text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion v1.4/v1.5, EVODiff achieves higher CLIP scores (33.79 vs. 33.07 average at 10 NFE on v1.4) and Aesthetic scores (5.77 vs. 5.71) compared to DPM-Solver++, with visual artifacts notably reduced at low NFE settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-based ODE solver lineage for diffusion inference (DPM-Solver, DPM-Solver++, UniPC, DPM-Solver-v3, EDM/Heun) by introducing an information-theoretic lens: successful denoising is reframed as conditional entropy reduction, and the solver's role becomes optimizing conditional variance rather than merely approximating an ODE trajectory. It departs from prior work by providing the first theoretical proof that data-prediction parameterization outperforms noise prediction (previously supported only empirically by [13, 20]) and by showing that DPM-Solver and Heun iterations are specific instances of a unified entropy-reduction framework. Unlike DPM-Solver-v3, which relies on a reference trajectory (bias term), EVODiff achieves variance optimization in a reference-free manner through closed-form solutions for interpolation and gradient-balancing parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable theoretical gap in the ODE-solver-for-diffusion literature by grounding inference efficiency in information transmission (conditional entropy) rather than purely numerical approximation, offering a principled explanation for why certain solvers outperform others. Practically, the 45.5% FID reduction on CIFAR-10 at 10 NFE and 25% NFE savings on ImageNet-256 are substantial for practitioners seeking faster, higher-quality sampling without retraining. Key limitations include the current restriction to data-prediction parameterization and the independence assumption across timesteps; open questions include whether information-theoretic principles can be extended to optimize information flow more broadly (e.g., in latent-space or multi-modal settings) and whether the framework generalizes to noise-prediction or v-prediction parameterizations.
