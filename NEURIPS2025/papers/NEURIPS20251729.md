## Problem

Existing embodied AI simulators for robotics are confined to indoor household scenes or autonomous driving, lacking the combination of photorealism, procedural city-scale generation, dynamic pedestrian/traffic systems, and flexible multi-agent control needed to study outdoor urban navigation. No prior platform simultaneously supports robots, humans, and vehicles with rich action spaces in procedurally generated, visually realistic cities, leaving a gap in evaluating critical urban robot capabilities such as social navigation, traffic rule compliance, and multi-robot collaboration.

## Input / Output

The robot receives an egocentric RGB image, a semantic segmentation map (color-coded: green=trees, purple=buildings, yellow=sidewalks, black=driveways), a depth map, a natural-language subtask instruction, a rendered visual hint image showing the expected view upon subtask completion, the current compass heading, and a textualized action history. The output is a variable-length sequence of discrete actions (move 5 m forward/backward/left/right, rotate ±90°, stay still, or signal subtask completion), with the model also predicting intermediate reasoning targets (distance to goal, expected orientation, remaining action plan) during fine-tuning. The simulator itself outputs procedurally generated 2 km² city maps with dynamic agents at 60 FPS.

## Method

SWR is built on Unreal Engine 5 with a four-stage procedural city generation pipeline (priority-queue road growth, collision-aware building placement, contextual street element scattering, and waypoint-based traffic population) that produces infinite photorealistic urban environments. The simulator uses an asynchronous multi-agent control framework where robots, pedestrians, and vehicles act independently through a centralized buffer, with vehicle motion governed by a PID controller and pedestrians following probabilistic routing at intersections. For evaluation, VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Flash, QwenVL, etc.) are deployed via a two-module ReAct pipeline: a context-aware perception module compares the current view against the visual hint, and a text-domain ReAct module reasons over the description, memory, and orientation to emit action sequences. The fine-tuned baseline (QwenVL2.5-7B with LoRA rank 8) is trained on SimWorld-20K—20K steps from 200 A*-oracle trajectories across 100 training maps—using multi-task supervision on action prediction, distance estimation, orientation prediction, and CoT action planning. A multi-robot search benchmark additionally employs RoCo-style dialectic communication where a map-aware main robot localizes a follower robot from its verbal description and coordinates a rendezvous plan.

## Results

On the SIMWORLD-MMNAV easy benchmark, all zero-shot non-reasoning VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Flash, QwenVL, Gemma 3, InternVL) achieve 0% Success Rate, with Subtask SR ranging from 15.36% (Gemma 3 27B) to 37.06% (Gemini 2.5 Flash). Reasoning models GPT-o3 and GPT-o3-pro achieve 5.0% and 8.3% SR respectively, the only zero-shot models to complete any full task. After fine-tuning on SimWorld-20K, QwenVL 2.5 7B reaches 4.0% SR, 52.45% Subtask SR, and 53.63% Distance Progress—the best Subtask SR and DP across all models—outperforming proprietary GPT-4o (33.07% Subtask SR, 15.60% DP) and Gemini 2.5 Flash (37.06%, 31.29%). On the harder setting with traffic and pedestrians, even the best model (GPT-4o) drops to 2.08% SR with 10.37 dynamic collisions per episode.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The fine-tuned QwenVL 2.5 7B (SimWorld-20K) is the only model to achieve non-zero full-task SR among open-source models and surpasses both GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Flash on Subtask SR (52.45% vs. 33.07% and 37.06%) and Distance Progress (53.63% vs. 15.60% and 31.29%), despite being a 7B model. Among zero-shot models, GPT-o3-pro leads with 8.3% SR and 46.35% Subtask SR, but the fine-tuned 7B model still exceeds it on both Subtask SR (+6.1 pts) and Distance Progress (+14.2 pts). The RL baseline VLA-RL (28.37% Subtask SR) underperforms all zero-shot VLMs, indicating that sparse-reward RL with standard vision encoders struggles with the spatial reasoning demands of city-scale navigation. On the multi-robot search benchmark (SIMWORLD-MRS), GPT-4o with an oracle A* planner achieves 65% CSR, but degrades to 33.33% CSR when using the RoCo dialogue-based collaboration protocol, showing that converting plans into natural language introduces significant execution noise. Gemini 2.5 Flash with oracle planner (54.55% CSR) trails GPT-4o by ~10 points, while QwenVL 2.5 72B with RoCo achieves only 11.11% CSR, highlighting the gap between proprietary and open-source models on grounded multi-agent communication.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SimWorld simulator [44] with procedural city generation, a PID-based traffic system, and quadruped robot support, positioning it against both indoor household simulators (Habitat, RoboTHOR, BEHAVIOR) and outdoor driving-focused simulators (CARLA, AirSim, MetaUrban). The evaluation pipeline builds on the ReAct framework [57] for zero-shot VLM-based navigation, while the multi-robot search benchmark draws on the RoCo collaboration paradigm [33]. The key conceptual departure is unifying photorealism, procedural scalability, dynamic traffic/pedestrian simulation, and multi-agent control in a single platform, then using it to create benchmarks that jointly stress-test perception, spatial reasoning, social navigation, and grounded communication—capabilities that prior benchmarks evaluate in isolation or in static, indoor settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmarks expose a stark capability gap: even GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Flash achieve 0% full-task success on the obstacle-free 'easy' setting, and fine-tuning QwenVL-2.5-7B on 20K steps yields only 4% SR, suggesting that current VLMs lack the robust 3D spatial reasoning, long-horizon planning, and pragmatic instruction interpretation needed for real urban navigation. The open-source UE5-based simulator and the SimWorld-20K dataset provide a scalable, reproducible platform for the community to develop and evaluate embodied agents at city scale. Key limitations include the outdoor-only scope, limited human action space (26 actions), and the absence of indoor scenes; future work on scaling human motion generation, incorporating indoor environments, and applying RL or corrective demonstrations to close the performance gap are natural next steps.
