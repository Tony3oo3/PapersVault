## Problem

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) suffer from slow convergence and limited accuracy when solving complex PDEs, particularly in fluid dynamics. While gradient magnitude imbalances across loss terms have been studied, the equally critical issue of directional gradient conflicts—where gradients from different physical constraints (e.g., momentum, continuity, boundary conditions) point in opposing directions—remains largely unexplored and is identified here as a fundamental bottleneck that forces inefficient zigzagging optimization trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PDE system with initial and boundary conditions along with collocation points sampled (fixed or random) across the space-time domain, and produces a neural network approximating the PDE solution. Unusually, it also outputs diagnostic gradient alignment scores—both intra-step (measuring directional agreement among individual loss-term gradients within a single update) and inter-step (measuring consistency between consecutive update directions)—computed from the post-optimizer-transformed gradients to capture actual update directions. The multi-term loss structure (residual, boundary, initial condition) means the alignment score must handle more than two vectors simultaneously, extending beyond standard pairwise cosine similarity.

## Method

The paper introduces a gradient alignment score that generalizes cosine similarity to n vectors, quantifying directional conflicts among multiple loss-term gradients. Theoretically, it proves that at small-weight initialization, the intra-step alignment score converges to a zero-mean binary random variable for all first-order optimizers (GD, Adam, Shampoo, SOAP), showing conflicts are intrinsic to PINNs rather than optimizer-specific. The key insight is that quasi-second-order optimizers implicitly precondition the loss landscape to promote alignment: the paper establishes that SOAP approximates the Newton preconditioner (H⁻¹), which eliminates dependence on the Hessian condition number and enables larger effective learning rates while maintaining trajectory consistency. Empirically, SOAP achieves 2–10× accuracy improvements over Adam across 10 PDE benchmarks using the PirateNet architecture with random weight factorization, including the first successful PINN solution of turbulent Kolmogorov flow at Re=10,000.

## Results

Across 10 PDE benchmarks, SOAP achieves relative L² errors ranging from 8.05×10⁻⁶ (Wave) to 3.99×10⁻² (Lid-driven cavity, Re=5000), consistently outperforming the Adam baseline by 2–19×. The most dramatic gains appear on coupled fluid-dynamics problems: 8.1× on lid-driven cavity flow, 6.4× on turbulent Kolmogorov flow (Re=10,000), and 14× on Rayleigh-Taylor instability (Ra=10⁶). On the 2D Poisson and Heat benchmarks (Table 2), SOAP with a [2,256,256,256,1] MLP achieves 1.35×10⁻⁶ and 2.65×10⁻⁶ relative L² error in float32, respectively, far surpassing NGD's best of 3.84×10⁻⁷ (Poisson, float64) and 7.68×10⁻⁶ (Heat, float64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOAP dominates the Adam baseline on all 10 benchmarks, with improvements from 2× (Burgers, KdV) to 19× (Grey-Scott), and 6–14× on the three most challenging fluid-dynamics problems. Against the other quasi-second-order methods, SOAP wins on 8 of 10 benchmarks; Muon narrowly edges SOAP on Kuramoto-Sivashinsky (3.51×10⁻² vs. 3.86×10⁻²) and Ginzburg-Landau (4.58×10⁻³ vs. 4.78×10⁻³), while Kron is consistently outperformed by both SOAP and Muon. On the 2D Poisson and Heat benchmarks (Table 2), SOAP in float32 achieves 1.35×10⁻⁶ and 2.65×10⁻⁶ relative L² error, beating NGD's best float64 results (3.84×10⁻⁷ and 7.68×10⁻⁶) by roughly 3× and 3× respectively, while also being more stable across random seeds. Adam+L-BFGS (Urbán et al. 2025) provides no improvement over plain Adam on the four benchmarks where both are reported, and is not applicable to the more complex fluid-dynamics problems. Overall, SOAP's advantage is most pronounced on high-Reynolds-number and multi-scale problems, where gradient conflicts are most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient pathology analysis of Wang et al. [21], which identified magnitude imbalances between PINN loss terms, by introducing a second, previously underexplored mode: directional gradient conflicts where gradients from different losses point in opposing directions. The authors import quasi-second-order optimizers (SOAP, Muon, Kron) from the deep learning optimization literature and establish a formal connection between SOAP and Newton's method, providing theoretical grounding for why these methods resolve directional conflicts through implicit Hessian preconditioning. The experimental backbone builds on the PirateNet architecture [27] and the training pipeline of Wang et al. [72], positioning the contribution squarely within the PINN optimization subfield rather than introducing a new network architecture or PDE formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first successful PINN simulation of turbulent Kolmogorov flow at Re=10,000 represents a genuine capability breakthrough, opening a class of multi-scale fluid dynamics problems previously considered beyond PINN reach. The 2–10× error reductions across 10 diverse PDE benchmarks, combined with the gradient alignment score as a diagnostic tool, give practitioners both a practical optimizer choice and a way to monitor training health. Key limitations include the ~2× computational overhead of SOAP, the theoretical analysis being restricted to 1D Laplace with small initialization, and the open question of whether more efficient preconditioners can match SOAP's alignment properties at lower cost. Broader implications extend to any multi-objective deep learning setting where competing loss gradients create directional conflicts.
