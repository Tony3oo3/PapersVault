## Problem

Existing federated LoRA fine-tuning methods either share a single LoRA module across all clients (which degrades under task heterogeneity) or assign one expert per client (which is redundant and computationally wasteful), and fixed top-k Mixture-of-Experts selection is suboptimal because different clients need different numbers of experts. The paper formalizes two unresolved questions: how to determine the optimal number and allocation of LoRA experts given heterogeneous client distributions, and how to enable each client to dynamically select the appropriate number of experts for its data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (RoBERTa-Large 355M for NLU, LLaMA-2-7B 8-bit quantized for NLG) and local text datasets held by distributed clients. A brief initialization phase produces LoRA B matrices that serve as the clustering signal; the final outputs are M fine-tuned LoRA expert pairs (A, B) plus per-client adaptive router matrices of dimension (2M−1)×d. For NLU tasks the model outputs class labels (accuracy metric); for NLG tasks it outputs generated text (ROUGE-1 metric).

## Method

FedLEASE operates in two phases. In the initialization phase, each client trains a LoRA module locally for E epochs; the server then computes pairwise cosine-similarity distances over the flattened LoRA B matrices across all layers, applies Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering for every candidate k, and selects the optimal number of experts M via the silhouette coefficient, initializing each expert by averaging the LoRA parameters of its assigned cluster. In the iterative training phase, each client updates only its assigned expert and a local router whose output space is expanded from the standard R^{M×d} to R^{(2M−1)×d}: the first M outputs are independently learned routing scores all connected to the client's own expert (enabling multiple internal components), while the remaining M−1 outputs connect to the other experts. A top-M selection over these 2M−1 scores dynamically determines how many unique experts (from 1 up to M) participate in each forward pass, guaranteeing the assigned expert is always included while eliminating the need for a manually tuned fixed k. Server aggregation averages expert parameters and router networks within each cluster per communication round.

## Results

On the GLUE NLU benchmark (RoBERTa-Large), FedLEASE achieves 87.76% average accuracy across SST-2, QNLI, MRPC, and QQP, outperforming the strongest baseline (FedSA at 84.60%) by 3.16%. On the FLAN NLG benchmark (LLaMA-2-7B), FedLEASE achieves 61.70 average ROUGE-1, surpassing the best baseline (FedSA at 60.20) by 1.50%. FedLEASE uses only 0.2075% of parameters on NLU and 0.0584% on NLG, comparable to or lower than most baselines, while maintaining the lowest standard deviation (±0.78 on NLU) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedLEASE consistently outperforms all five baselines on both NLU and NLG tasks. Against FedIT [45] (single shared LoRA with FedAvg), FedLEASE gains 5.53% on NLU average and 4.37% on NLG average, demonstrating that a single LoRA module is insufficient for heterogeneous clients. Against FFA-LoRA [35], the gap is largest (6.70% on NLU, 5.68% on NLG), as FFA-LoRA uses fewer parameters (0.1107%) but lacks any expert specialization. Against FedDPA [44] (dual global-local adapter), FedLEASE leads by 3.27% on NLU and 1.89% on NLG, showing that the binary global-local design cannot capture nuanced client similarities. Against FedSA [17] (selective aggregation for LoRA), FedLEASE wins by 3.16% on NLU and 1.50% on NLG; FedSA is the strongest baseline but still relies on a fixed aggregation strategy. Against IFCA+LoRA [16] (clustered federated learning adapted with LoRA), FedLEASE gains 3.28% on NLU and 1.97% on NLG; while IFCA+LoRA also clusters clients, it lacks cross-cluster knowledge transfer, resulting in isolated learning. Notably, FedLEASE achieves these gains with fewer parameters than most baselines (0.2075% vs. 0.2213% on NLU) and the lowest variance across runs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated LoRA fine-tuning line initiated by FedIT (Zhang et al., 2024) and subsequent works (FFA-LoRA, FedSA, FedDPA), which predominantly rely on a single shared LoRA module or a binary global-local adapter design. It draws on the centralized LoRA-MoE integration explored by HydraLoRA (Tian et al., 2024) and the classical Mixture-of-Experts routing paradigm (Jordan & Jacobs, 1994), but departs from both by introducing a data-driven clustering step (based on LoRA B-matrix cosine similarity) to determine the optimal number of experts and their client allocation, and by replacing fixed top-k routing with an adaptive top-M mechanism that expands the router output space from R^{M×d} to R^{(2M-1)×d}. Unlike IFCA-based clustered FL, FedLEASE enables cross-cluster knowledge transfer through the shared expert pool, and unlike FedDPA's dual-personalizing design, it supports a variable number of experts per client rather than a fixed global-local pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedLEASE addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying federated LLM fine-tuning across organizations with genuinely heterogeneous data: the question of how many domain-specific adapters to maintain and how each client should combine them. The adaptive top-M routing mechanism is a reusable architectural component that could benefit other MoE-based PEFT settings beyond federated learning. Key limitations include the assumption of a static client population and fixed expert assignments, the evaluation on relatively modest model sizes (RoBERTa-Large 355M, LLaMA-2-7B with 8-bit quantization), and the reliance on a one-time clustering step that may not adapt to evolving data distributions. Open questions include dynamic re-clustering under non-stationary conditions, scalability to 70B+ models, and whether the silhouette-based expert count selection generalizes to settings with more than a handful of distinct domains.
