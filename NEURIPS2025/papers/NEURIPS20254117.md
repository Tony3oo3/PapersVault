## Problem

Constrained expected improvement (CEI) is one of the most widely adopted acquisition functions for constrained Bayesian optimization, yet its theoretical convergence rate has never been rigorously established under either frequentist or Bayesian settings. The multiplicative structure between the objective EI and the probability of feasibility, combined with the algorithm's need to explore infeasible regions to learn the constraint boundary, makes the analysis substantially more complex than the unconstrained EI case analyzed by Bull (2011).

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a compact domain C ⊂ ℝ^d with a single noise-free black-box objective f and constraint c (extendable to m constraints under conditional independence). GP surrogate models with SE or Matérn kernels are maintained for both f and c, and the CEI acquisition selects the next point by maximizing the product of EI and the probability of feasibility Φ(−μ_t^c/σ_t^c). The theoretical output is a simple regret upper bound r_t = f_t^+ − f(x*) expressed in terms of the RKHS norm bounds B_f, B_c, the posterior standard deviation σ_t^f, and the maximum information gain γ_t^f, yielding explicit convergence rates for each kernel family.

## Method

The core analysis exploits CEI's multiplicative structure: the regret at x* is bounded by inserting and dividing by the probability of feasibility P_{t_k}(x*), converting the problem into bounding EI at the chosen sample point and lower-bounding POF at x* via the RKHS confidence interval on c. Under the frequentist RKHS setting, this yields O(t^{−1/d}) and O(t^{−min{ν,1}/d} log^η(t)) rates matching Bull's unconstrained results; an improved bound using maximum information gain γ_t^f to control σ_{t_k}^f(x_{t_k+1}) gives O(t^{−1/2} log^{(d+1)/2}(t)) for SE and O(t^{−ν/(2ν+d)} log^{ν/(2ν+d)}(t)) for Matérn, improving upon Bull for d≥3. Under the Bayesian GP-prior setting, the key new ingredient is a high-probability bound on |I_t^f(x) − EI_t^f(x)| ≤ √β σ_t^f(x), derived via a novel analysis of the improvement function's distribution, which then feeds into the same regret decomposition to yield analogous rates with an extra log^{1/2}(t) factor.

## Results

Under frequentist (RKHS) assumptions, CEI achieves a simple regret upper bound of O(t^{-1/2} log^{(d+1)/2}(t)) for the squared exponential kernel and O(t^{-ν/(2ν+d)} log^{ν/(2ν+d)}(t)) for Matérn kernels (ν > 1/2) (Theorem 3.7). Under Bayesian (GP prior) assumptions, the rates are O(t^{-1/2} log^{(d+2)/2}(t)) for SE and O(t^{-ν/(2ν+d)} log^{(2ν+0.5d)/(2ν+d)}(t)) for Matérn, with probability ≥ 1−δ (Theorem 3.11). These are the first established convergence rates for CEI. Numerical experiments on 8 synthetic problems and 5 benchmark CBO problems confirm sublinear regret convergence consistent with the theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not perform empirical comparisons against other CBO algorithms; its contribution is purely theoretical. The relevant theoretical 'baseline' is the convergence rate for unconstrained EI established by Bull [2011], which gives O(t^{-1/d}) for SE and O(t^{-min{ν,1}/d} log^η(t)) for Matérn kernels under the same RKHS assumptions. The improved rates in Theorem 3.7 (using maximum information gain instead of Taylor expansion of the kernel) strictly dominate Bull's rates for SE with d ≥ 3 and Matérn with d ≥ 3, ν ≥ d/(d−2). The Bayesian-setting rates (Theorem 3.11) differ from the frequentist rates only by an additional log^{1/2}(t) factor, which the authors attribute to the high-probability confidence interval constants. No prior work had established any convergence rate for CEI under either setting, so all results are novel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bull's (2011) convergence rate analysis for unconstrained Expected Improvement to the constrained setting, addressing the unique challenge posed by CEI's multiplicative acquisition structure (EI × probability of feasibility). While prior CBO convergence work (Lu & Paulson 2022, Zhou & Ji 2022) focused on UCB-type methods with additive acquisition functions, CEI's product form requires new techniques to bound the feasibility probability term. The improved rates in Theorem 3.7 additionally draw on information-theoretic bounds from Lyu et al. (2019) for noise-free UCB, replacing Bull's Taylor-expansion-based kernel bounds with maximum information gain arguments to achieve faster convergence in higher dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous convergence guarantee for CEI, one of the most widely deployed CBO algorithms in practice (default in BoTorch and other packages), providing practitioners with theoretical assurance that the method converges at the same rate as its unconstrained counterpart. The constraint introduces only a constant factor 1/Φ(−B_c) rather than changing the rate, which is reassuring but also means the analysis doesn't capture potential practical slowdowns from small feasible regions. Open questions include extending to noisy observations (where the definition of feasibility becomes ambiguous), resolving Vakili's (2022) open problem on noise-free kernel bandits for further rate improvements, and whether the Bayesian-setting log^{1/2}(t) gap can be closed.
