## Problem

Orient Anything V1 estimates object orientation by assuming a single unique front face, which fails for rotationally symmetric objects (e.g., a cylinder has two valid front faces) and cannot estimate relative rotation between two views without error accumulation from independent absolute predictions. Additionally, its training data from real 3D assets suffers from severe category imbalance and inconsistent quality, limiting generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251754/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts one or two RGB images (resized to 518 px), each tokenized by DINOv2 into K patch tokens plus a learnable token. For a single frame, the output is three discrete probability distributions over angle bins (360-bin azimuth, 180-bin polar, 360-bin in-plane rotation); the azimuth distribution is modeled as a periodic Gaussian whose periodicity parameter α ∈ {0,1,2,4} directly encodes the number of valid front faces. For two frames, the second frame's learnable token additionally predicts a relative rotation distribution (azimuth, polar, in-plane) with respect to the first frame, without symmetry modeling.

## Method

The architecture follows VGGT's multi-frame transformer: DINOv2 encodes each input image into tokens that are jointly processed by unified transformer blocks, with the first frame's learnable token predicting absolute orientation and subsequent frames' tokens predicting relative rotation. The key training objective replaces V1's unimodal circular Gaussian with a symmetry-aware periodic Gaussian distribution over azimuth, where the periodicity parameter α captures 0 to N valid front faces, eliminating the need for a separate confidence score. Training data is a 600K synthetic 3D asset dataset generated via a Class Tag → Caption (Qwen-2.5) → Image (FLUX.1-Dev) → 3D Mesh (Hunyuan-3D-2.0) pipeline, annotated through model-in-the-loop ensemble pseudo-labels fitted to periodic distributions with cross-asset consistency calibration. The model is initialized from VGGT (1.2B parameters, pre-trained on 3D geometry), repurposing its camera token for orientation/rotation prediction, and trained with BCE loss on distribution fitting for 20k iterations with random 1–2 frame sampling per batch.

## Results

On zero-shot absolute orientation estimation, Orient Anything V2 achieves a median 3D angle error of 26.00° on SUN-RGBD (vs. 33.94° for V1) and 86.4% accuracy on Ori_COCO (vs. 72.4%), with the largest relative gain on ARKitScenes (Med 36.48° vs. 77.58°). For zero-shot relative rotation estimation, V2 achieves 98.07% Acc30 on LINEMOD and 99.7% Acc30 on OnePose under POPE's sampling, and maintains 51.6% Acc30 on LINEMOD under random sampling (avg. 78.22° rotation) where POPE drops to 10.3%. On zero-shot rotational symmetry recognition, V2 reaches 65.2% accuracy on Omni6DPose, outperforming the strongest VLM baseline (GPT-4o at 62.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct predecessor Orient Anything V1, V2 wins on 5 of 6 orientation benchmarks, with the most dramatic improvement on ARKitScenes (Med 36.48° vs. 77.58°, a 53% reduction) and Pascal3D+ (Acc30 72.7% vs. 55.0%); V1 retains a narrow edge on ImageNet3D (Med 13.34° vs. 15.26°) where it was fine-tuned. In zero-shot relative rotation estimation, V2 decisively outperforms all three pose-estimation baselines (Gen6D, LoFTR, POPE) across LINEMOD, YCB-Video, OnePose++, and OnePose; under POPE's small-rotation sampling (avg. 14.85°), V2 achieves 98.07% Acc30 on LINEMOD versus POPE's 77.0%, and under random sampling (avg. 78.22°) the gap widens to 51.6% vs. 10.3%, highlighting the fragility of feature-matching methods under large viewpoint changes. For rotational symmetry recognition on Omni6DPose, V2 (65.2%) surpasses all four VLM baselines: GPT-4o (62.5%), Qwen2.5VL-72B (55.8%), GPT-o3 (53.7%), and Gemini-2.5-pro (44.4%), demonstrating that a dedicated orientation model captures 3D spatial structure more reliably than general-purpose vision-language models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to Orient Anything V1, extending its single-front-face orientation estimation to handle rotational symmetry (0 to N valid front faces) and relative rotation between views. Architecturally, it repurposes VGGT's multi-frame transformer and DINOv2 encoder, replacing the unimodal circular Gaussian fitting objective with a symmetry-aware periodic distribution that natively encodes rotational symmetry. On the data side, it departs from V1's VLM-annotated real Objaverse assets in favor of a fully synthetic pipeline (FLUX.1-Dev → Hunyuan-3D-2.0) with model-in-the-loop ensemble annotation, and contrasts with prior 6DoF pose methods (POPE, LoFTR, Gen6D) by replacing explicit feature matching with implicit learned rotation prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying absolute orientation, relative rotation, and symmetry recognition in a single zero-shot model, this work removes the need for task-specific pipelines and provides a practical foundation for robotics, AR/VR, and spatial understanding applications. The dramatic performance gap over feature-matching methods under large rotations (e.g., 51.6% vs. 10.3% Acc30 on LINEMOD at ~78°) suggests that implicit semantic understanding can fundamentally outperform correspondence-based approaches in challenging regimes. Key limitations include the two-frame cap (precluding video-level tracking) and reduced accuracy under severe occlusion or low-information views; extending to multi-frame sequences and handling ambiguous monocular cues remain open directions.
