## Problem

In heterogeneous collaborative perception, agents with different fixed sensors and perception encoders produce intermediate features with significant domain gaps, degrading fusion quality. Existing common-representation methods designate one specific agent's representation as the shared space, making alignment difficult for agents whose representations differ substantially from that designated agent. This paper asks whether a common representation can instead be *negotiated* from all participating modalities to minimize the domain gap with every agent simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252916/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent ingests either a LiDAR point cloud (16-, 32-, or 64-channel) or a monocular camera image (resized to 384 px or 336 px height), encoded by a fixed perception encoder (PointPillars, SECOND, or Lift-Splat with EfficientNet/ResNet50) into a BEV feature map. The sender-receiver pairs operate on these intermediate BEV features, transforming them between the local representation space and the negotiated common representation space; the fused feature is then passed to a task head that outputs 3D bounding boxes. During training, paired observations from the same scene perspective are fed to all modality encoders to enable cycle-consistency and alignment supervision.

## Method

NegoCollab introduces a negotiator built on a feature pyramid network that fuses the standardized local representations of each modality into a common representation, with per-level estimators weighting each modality's contribution. A plug-and-play sender (ConvNeXt recombiner + fused axial attention aligner) maps local features into the common space for sharing, while a receiver (fused axial attention converter guided by a local prompt + ConvNeXt recombiner) projects received features back to the local space. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a cyclic distribution consistency loss enforces that the receiver can reconstruct the original local feature from the common representation, while a multi-dimensional alignment loss (distribution matching, structural consistency via 9-keypoint relative relation matrices, and pragmatic consistency via a shared 2D occupancy prediction head with focal loss) distills multimodal knowledge from the negotiated common representation into each sender; second, receivers are fine-tuned with the downstream collaborative detection loss. New agents join by training only their own sender-receiver pair against the frozen pre-negotiated common representation, requiring no retraining of existing agents.

## Results

On the OPV2V-H dataset, NegoCollab achieves AP@0.5 = 0.745 and AP@0.7 = 0.555 across all four agent types (m1–m4), substantially outperforming the best common-representation baseline STAMP (0.382 / 0.286) and even surpassing one-to-one adaptation methods MPDA (0.512 / 0.435) and PnPDA (0.494 / 0.424) in the all-agent collaboration setting. On real-world datasets V2V4Real and DAIR-V2X, NegoCollab similarly leads all baselines (e.g., AP@0.5 = 0.605 on V2V4Real vs. 0.613 for MPDA, and AP@0.5 = 0.397 on DAIR-V2X vs. 0.443 for PnPDA). The ablation confirms that the negotiator plus multi-dimensional alignment loss (structural + pragmatic) lifts AP@0.5 from 0.617 (distribution loss only, no negotiator) to 0.711.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the one-to-one adaptation baselines MPDA and PnPDA, NegoCollab achieves a large margin in the all-agent collaboration setting (AP@0.5: 0.745 vs. 0.512 and 0.494; AP@0.7: 0.555 vs. 0.435 and 0.424), but trails them slightly in the m1m3 (LiDAR–LiDAR) and m2m4 (camera–camera) pairwise scenarios where the domain gap is smaller. Against the common-representation baselines STAMP, MPDA-P, and PnPDA-P, NegoCollab dominates across every evaluation column, with the largest gap over STAMP (AP@0.5 All: 0.745 vs. 0.382, a +95% relative improvement). The paper also shows that NegoCollab's negotiated common representation generalizes to new agents (m3, m4) that did not participate in the negotiation, achieving competitive performance (AP@0.5 m1m3 = 0.911, m2m4 = 0.512) without retraining the existing alliance. On real-world datasets (V2V4Real, DAIR-V2X), NegoCollab remains the top common-representation method and is within 1–5% of the best one-to-one adaptation method, while being significantly more robust to localization noise (maintaining >0.48 AP@0.5 at σ=0.6 where late fusion drops to 0.278).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the common-representation paradigm for heterogeneous collaborative perception introduced by STAMP (Gao et al., 2025), which designates a specific agent's representation as the shared space and trains adapter-reverter pairs for bidirectional transformation. The key departure is replacing this fixed designation with a negotiator (an FPN-based module with per-level estimators) that synthesizes the common representation from all participating modalities' local representations, thereby reducing the inherent domain gap. The multi-dimensional alignment loss (distribution + structural + pragmatic) draws on knowledge distillation techniques from cross-modal learning, while the sender-receiver architecture with ConvNeXt and fused axial attention modules builds on the plug-and-play domain adaptation lineage (MPDA, PnPDA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negotiated common representation offers a more flexible and scalable path for heterogeneous collaborative perception in real deployments where perception models are frozen for safety reasons, and the finding that the choice of initial alliance significantly affects generalization to new agents provides practical guidance for system design. A key open question is how to make the common representation adapt dynamically as new agents join, since the current fixed negotiation inevitably increases information loss for agents not involved in the original negotiation. The paper also highlights that intermediate feature-level fusion (as in NegoCollab) is substantially more robust to localization errors than late fusion, reinforcing the practical value of feature-space collaboration over result-space merging.
