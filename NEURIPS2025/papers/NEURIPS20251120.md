## Problem

Existing 3D indoor scene datasets for embodied AI suffer from three interrelated limitations: limited scale and diversity, sanitized layouts that omit the small items prevalent in real environments, and severe object collisions that render them incompatible with physics-based simulation engines. No prior dataset simultaneously achieves large scale, realistic cluttered layouts with hundreds of object categories, and simulation-ready physical plausibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251120/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests three heterogeneous scene sources—real-world RGB-D scans (EmbodiedScan), procedurally generated layouts (Infinigen Indoors), and designer-created 3D scenes—along with 80M CAD models from Objaverse and interactive assets from PartNet-Mobility. The output is a structured hierarchy of Scene → Regions → Instances → Parts, yielding ~40,000 scenes with 48K regions across 15 types, 1.96M objects spanning 288 classes, and an average of 41.5 objects per region. Roughly 20% of objects are replaced with interactive (articulated) counterparts, and all scenes are collision-free after physics optimization, making them directly loadable into SAPIEN or IsaacSim.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage multi-source processing pipeline. In Stage 1, source-specific extraction routines handle each data type: for real scans, GPT-4o maps Cap3D captions to 288 categories with InternVL-based verification, a context-driven fuzzy label replacement resolves ambiguous 'object' annotations using spatial position rules, and canonical pose correction aligns object orientations; for procedural scenes, object-number-aggressive rules are applied to Infinigen to maximize clutter; for designer scenes, custom BEV polygon annotation tools and instance splitting/merging scripts restore a clean hierarchical structure, with InternVL generating semantic labels from six-view renderings. In Stage 2, a three-term loss (IoU collision penalty, ground-alignment term, and position regularization) optimizes large-furniture oriented bounding boxes, after which all objects are imported into SAPIEN with COACD convex decomposition (including cavity-aware segmentation of furniture) and gravity/repulsion simulation to eliminate residual floating and interpenetration of small objects.

## Results

On the full-version InternScenes (with all small objects), all three scene generation baselines show degraded performance: FID ranges from 66.59 (PhyScene, Living) to 133.20 (ATISS, Dining), and SCA scores are far from the ideal 50% (94.97–99.44%), indicating poor layout fidelity. On the simplified version (large furniture only), FID drops to 22.88–30.89 and SCA improves to 57.70–70.95%, confirming that small-object placement is the primary challenge. For point-goal navigation, NavDP-FT achieves 51.0% success on InternScenes-Real2Sim and 63.6% on InternScenes-Gen, substantially outperforming DD-PPO (23.6% and 45.0% respectively), yet still falls well short of the near-perfect rates reported on simpler benchmarks like Habitat-Sim.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For scene generation, PhyScene outperforms DiffuScene on the full (complex) version, particularly on the Living Region where PhyScene achieves FID 66.59 versus DiffuScene's 107.49, suggesting its physics-based guidance helps with complex multi-object layouts. On the simplified version, DiffuScene and PhyScene are largely comparable (FID within ~1–2 points), while ATISS is consistently slightly worse. For navigation, NavDP-FT (fine-tuned on 118,784 InternScenes trajectories) improves over the pretrained NavDP by ~2.7% success on Real2Sim (51.0% vs 48.3%) and ~1.7% on Gen (63.6% vs 61.9%), demonstrating that the dataset's diversity aids generalization. DD-PPO, trained in Habitat-Sim with discrete actions, performs worst across all settings (23.6% success on Real2Sim), highlighting the sim-to-real and action-space gap. Overall, all baselines struggle significantly on the full InternScenes compared to their performance on simpler datasets like 3D-FRONT, underscoring the dataset's challenging nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate lines of 3D indoor scene data—real-world scans (EmbodiedScan, ScanNet, MP3D), designer-created scenes (3D-FRONT, Structured3D), and procedurally generated scenes (Infinigen Indoors)—into a single simulatable dataset. It departs from prior real-to-sim work (OpenRooms, MetaScenes) by preserving small items and using a GPT-4o + InternVL-based retrieval pipeline over Objaverse assets rather than Scan2CAD alignment, and from designer-created datasets by explicitly retaining cluttered small-object layouts and resolving collisions via SAPIEN physics simulation. The physics-aware composition pipeline (OBB optimization with IoU/ground/regularization losses followed by convex-decomposition-based simulation) extends the collision-resolution approach of BEHAVIOR-1K to a much larger and more diverse scene collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InternScenes addresses a critical bottleneck for embodied AI and 3D AIGC: the absence of large-scale, simulation-ready scenes with realistic object density (41.5 objects/region, ~3× prior datasets). The benchmark experiments reveal that current SOTA scene generation methods degrade substantially when small objects are included, and that navigation policies struggle in cluttered environments even after fine-tuning on 118K trajectories—suggesting that scaling data alone is insufficient without architectural advances. Key open questions include how to reduce reliance on manual annotation for region and instance labeling, how to improve 3D asset quality and diversity, and whether physics-based guidance (as in PhyScene) can be generalized to handle the full complexity of real-world cluttered layouts.
