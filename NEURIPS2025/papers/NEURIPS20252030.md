## Problem

Existing 3D part datasets like PartNet rely on untextured, remeshed geometries and expert-dependent annotation workflows that limit scalability and strip away visual cues essential for part recognition. Beyond data quality, there is no large-scale benchmark for class-agnostic fine-grained part segmentation on textured meshes, nor any evaluation of 3D language models on part-centric reasoning tasks such as counting, naming, and grounding individual parts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252030/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 23,519 textured 3D meshes (sourced from Objaverse, ABO, and 3D-FUTURE) annotated at the face level with 350,187 hierarchical part instances across 50 categories, preserving original geometry and texture without remeshing. The segmentation benchmark consumes textured meshes (or derived colorized point clouds with normals) and evaluates per-face part masks against leaf-node ground truth using mIoU. The part-centric QA benchmark feeds 8,192-point clouds plus text queries to 3D LLMs, expecting outputs of integer counts, part name labels, or eight-vertex bounding box coordinates; a separate Qwen3-14B model post-processes raw LLM text into structured JSON for scoring.

## Method

PartNeXt's construction pipeline combines CLIP-based text-similarity filtering (using Cap3D captions, threshold 0.75) to curate category-consistent models from Objaverse, and GPT-4o to bootstrap per-category part hierarchies guided by five formalized design criteria (functionality-aware, hierarchical, exhaustive variants, atomicity, consistency), with rendered images provided to the model to capture diverse part variants. Annotation is performed on a fully web-based dual-panel interface where the left panel shows unsegmented mesh faces and the right panel shows already-labeled parts, enabling annotators to work around occlusion for interior components; three face-level selection tools (connected-component, bounding-box, and per-face) replace PartNet's remeshing-and-cutting workflow. The paper introduces two benchmarks—class-agnostic part instance segmentation (250 objects, leaf-node GT, mIoU) and part-centric 3D QA (counting, classification, grounding)—and trains Point-SAM on PartNeXt versus PartNet to demonstrate that the dataset's texture-preserving, face-level annotations yield consistent IoU gains across both in-domain and zero-shot transfer settings.

## Results

On the class-agnostic part segmentation benchmark (250 objects, 50 categories), SAMesh achieves the highest mIoU of 51.57%, followed by PartField (50.22%) and SAMPart3D (36.78%), revealing that even SOTA methods struggle with fine-grained leaf-level parts. On the 3D part-centric QA benchmark, all three 3D LLMs show limited performance: part counting MAE ranges from 1.72 (ShapeLLM, with category) to 2.46 (3DLLM, without category), classification accuracy peaks at only 25% (ShapeLLM), and grounding IoU reaches just 33% (ShapeLLM). Training Point-SAM on PartNeXt alone yields IoU@1 of 44.3% versus 39.9% for PartNet-trained models on the PartNeXt test set, with the mixture achieving 45.3%, confirming the dataset's superior quality and diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For class-agnostic segmentation, SAMesh [39] leads with 51.57% mIoU but tends to over-segment, while PartField [25] (50.22%) occasionally fails to separate adjacent connected regions; SAMPart3D [48] trails significantly at 36.78% mIoU, struggling with continuity in weakly-textured areas and inconsistent granularity. On the 3D part-centric QA benchmark, ShapeLLM [35] achieves the best part counting MAE (1.72 with category) and grounding IoU (0.33), but PointLLM [46] edges it out on counting without category (1.79 vs. 1.85); 3DLLM [11] performs worst on counting (2.16/2.46 MAE) and fails to produce valid bounding boxes for grounding entirely. All three LLMs show very low classification accuracy (≤25%), indicating a fundamental gap in fine-grained part-level semantic understanding. For the Point-SAM training comparison, PartNeXt-trained models consistently outperform PartNet-trained counterparts across all IoU@k metrics (e.g., 44.3% vs. 39.9% at IoU@1), and the mixture of both datasets yields the best results (45.3% IoU@1), validating PartNeXt's annotation quality and category diversity as a stronger training foundation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartNeXt directly extends the PartNet lineage (Mo et al., 2019) by addressing its two core limitations: the loss of texture and original geometry due to remeshing, and the expert-dependent annotation interface that hindered scalability. It sources models from Objaverse, ABO, and 3D-FUTURE, and replaces PartNet's mesh-cutting workflow with a web-based, face-level selection system on textured meshes, aided by CLIP-based filtering and GPT-4o-generated hierarchies. For benchmarking, it builds on the class-agnostic part segmentation evaluation protocol from PartField and extends the 3D LLM evaluation landscape (PointLLM, ShapeLLM, 3D-LLM) with a dedicated part-centric QA benchmark that no prior work has provided.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PartNeXt provides the first large-scale, textured, hierarchically annotated 3D part dataset at PartNet-comparable scale but with double the category coverage (50 vs. 24), making it a likely successor benchmark for fine-grained 3D part understanding. The benchmarks reveal that even SOTA segmentation methods achieve only ~50% mIoU on fine-grained leaf-level parts, and 3D LLMs are essentially non-functional for part-level reasoning (classification accuracy ~0.2, grounding IoU ~0.3), highlighting a substantial gap. Key limitations include the need for predefined per-category hierarchies (blocking open-vocabulary annotation), the absence of part-level captions or physical attributes, and a model count (23.5K) that is still modest relative to Objaverse-XL's 10M+ objects.
