## Problem

Accurate graphon estimation typically requires large networks, yet many practical settings (e.g., protein structures with ~25 nodes) only provide small target graphs. The only prior transfer learning method for graphon estimation requires known node correspondences between source and target, which is unrealistic in most applications. This paper addresses the more practical and unsolved setting where no node correspondences are available, introducing both an alignment challenge and the difficulty of formulating transfer objectives without labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes two unlabelled adjacency matrices: a larger source graph A_s ∈ {0,1}^{n_s×n_s} and a smaller target graph A_t ∈ {0,1}^{n_t×n_t} with n_s > n_t, with no assumed node correspondence between them. It outputs a target edge probability matrix P̂_t ∈ [0,1]^{n_t×n_t} estimated under the graphon model. A key design choice is that the optimal transport alignment is computed on neighborhood-smoothed probability estimates rather than the raw Bernoulli-noisy adjacency matrices, and the transport plan is column-normalized before being used as a projection operator.

## Method

GTRANS operates in three stages. First, neighborhood smoothing (NS) produces initial graphon estimators for both source and target. Second, Gromov-Wasserstein (or entropic GW) optimal transport is solved on these smoothed estimates to obtain an alignment matrix π̂, which is column-normalized and used to project the source estimator into the target latent space via P̂_t^trans = π̃ᵀ P̂_s^ini π̃, followed by a second NS smoothing pass. Third, an adaptive debiasing step is triggered when the GW distance between the two smoothed estimates exceeds a threshold δ: the residual R_t = P̂_t^ini − P̂_t^trans2 is smoothed via NS to extract target-specific structure while filtering small-sample noise, and the smoothed residual is added back to the transferred estimate. The paper provides a stability theorem bounding ‖π̂ − π*‖_F in terms of the estimation errors of the initial graphon estimators, and proves that the empirical GW distance concentrates around the population GW distance, validating the alignment strategy.

## Results

On IMDB-Binary, GTRANS-EGW achieves 77.50% test accuracy (transferring from COLLAB) and 76.80% (from Reddit-Binary), outperforming all baselines by 2–5 percentage points. On IMDB-Multi, GTRANS-EGW reaches 51.27% (Reddit-Binary source) and GTRANS-GW reaches 50.23% (COLLAB source), again leading all competitors. On PROTEINS-Full, GTRANS-GW achieves 69.33% (D&D source), outperforming NS, USVT, SAS, ICE, GWB, and IGNR, though the Graphlet kernel baseline slightly edges it at 70.11%. In synthetic experiments (Table 1), GTRANS consistently achieves 20–50% lower MSE than target-only methods for similar graphon pairs, while matching NS performance for dissimilar pairs thanks to the adaptive debiasing mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTRANS outperforms all four target-only graphon estimators (NS, USVT, SAS, ICE) across every dataset by 2–6 percentage points in classification accuracy, confirming that transfer from a larger source graph provides substantial gains when the target is small. Against the graphon-learning baselines GWB, IGNR, and SIGL, GTRANS wins on IMDB-Binary (by ~1–4 points) and IMDB-Multi (by ~2–7 points), but is slightly outperformed on PROTEINS-Full, where SIGL (67.13%) and IGNR (65.87%) are closer competitors and the Graphlet kernel (70.11%) narrowly beats GTRANS-GW (69.33%). The Graphlet baseline, which uses a completely different paradigm (subgraph kernel + SVM), is the only method that surpasses GTRANS on any dataset, suggesting that for protein structure graphs the local topological features captured by graphlets may complement or exceed the global graphon-based transfer signal. In synthetic MSE experiments (Table 1), GTRANS achieves the lowest error for all similar graphon pairs (e.g., 0.9 vs. 2.1 for NS on 7→6), while for dissimilar pairs it degrades gracefully to match NS performance, validating the adaptive debiasing mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three pillars: neighborhood smoothing (NS) for initial graphon estimation [Zhang et al., 2017], Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport for graph alignment without known correspondences [Mémoli, 2011], and entropic regularization for computational tractability [Solomon et al., 2016]. The key departure from prior work is the transfer learning setting: the only previous transfer method for graphon estimation [Jalan et al., 2024] requires the target to be a known subset of the source, whereas GTRANS handles the more practical case of unknown node correspondences. The theoretical contribution extends EGW stability analysis to arbitrary (non-Euclidean) cost matrices, filling a gap left by prior analyses that assumed inner-product structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: graphon estimation degrades sharply for small networks (e.g., 25-node protein graphs), yet larger related graphs are often available in the same domain. The adaptive debiasing mechanism is a useful design choice that prevents negative transfer when source and target graphons diverge, making the method robust across domain shifts. Limitations include the single-source assumption, restriction to static graphs, and lack of node covariates; extending to multiple sources, dynamic settings, and covariate-augmented alignment are natural next steps. The method's practical impact is demonstrated through improved G-Mixup data augmentation for graph classification and link prediction, suggesting broader utility in graphon-based downstream pipelines.
