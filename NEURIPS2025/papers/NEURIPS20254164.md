## Problem

Causal mediation analysis is fragmented: structure discovery methods learn causal graphs but cannot interpret specific mediation pathways, while classical mediation analysis requires pre-specified paths and assumes a known structure. No existing framework propagates structural uncertainty from graph learning through pathway identification to effect estimation, leaving a gap between discovering causal structure and quantifying mechanistic mediation effects under uncertainty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254164/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SIGMA consumes observational tabular data with mixed variable types (continuous, binary, categorical) organized as treatment A, outcome Y, k mediators M, and covariates C; in the HPP application this comprises 62 domain-informed features aggregated from 11 body systems over 6,000+ individuals. The method outputs a posterior ensemble of 1,000 sampled DAGs, a ranked set of stable mediation pathways selected by Path Stability Score (threshold τ=0.15), and for each pathway, BMA-aggregated NDE, NIE, and TE point estimates with 95% confidence intervals that decompose variance into within-DAG estimation uncertainty and between-DAG structural uncertainty.

## Method

SIGMA is a three-stage end-to-end pipeline. First, a differentiable Flow-SEM models each variable as a neural-network function of its parents plus heteroskedastic noise, trained by maximizing log-likelihood under a NOTEARS acyclicity penalty and L1 sparsity on a continuous weight matrix W_θ; edge inclusion probabilities are extracted via sigmoid(|W_θ|) and 1,000 DAGs are sampled with cycle removal to approximate the structural posterior. Second, the Path Stability Score computes the empirical frequency of each directed A-to-Y path across the DAG ensemble, providing an unbiased Monte Carlo estimate of posterior path inclusion probability and enabling automatic pathway selection without manual pre-specification. Third, for each stable path, structure-guided nuisance functions (propensity scores, conditional outcome expectations) are fit via K-fold cross-fitting using parent sets from each supporting DAG; 3-node paths use Efficient Influence Functions while longer paths use plugin estimators, and final estimates are aggregated via Bayesian Model Averaging with the law of total variance combining within-DAG and between-DAG variance components.

## Results

On the LowDim-L synthetic dataset, SIGMA achieves the lowest empirical bias across all mediation effects: for Pathway 1 (V5→V19→V16), NDE bias is −0.0066 and NIE bias is 0.0811; for Pathway 2 (V12→V10→V6), NDE bias is 0.0007 and NIE bias is −0.0231, substantially outperforming DeepMed, Lasso, RF, and GBM. In causal discovery, SIGMA achieves an average 42.1% improvement in orientation F1 over baselines, though DECI retains a slight adjacency F1 edge on the highest-dimensional datasets (0.3814 vs. 0.3741 on HighDim-D). On the 2000-dimensional scalability test, SIGMA maintains Path F1 of 0.429 and Adjacency F1 of 0.467 with SE ≤ 0.083. On the real-world HPP cohort, SIGMA identifies multiple physiologically meaningful mediation pathways (e.g., sleep HRV → PWV → carotid IMT) with an overall TE error of 0.2734.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In causal discovery (Figure 2), SIGMA is compared against PC, linear NOTEARS, ICALiNGAM, Grandag, and DECI across ten synthetic datasets. SIGMA achieves a 42.1% average improvement in orientation F1 over all baselines, which is critical for downstream pathway identification; however, DECI retains a marginal adjacency F1 advantage on the two highest-dimensional settings (0.3814/0.4869 vs. 0.3741/0.4754 on HighDim-D/P), likely due to its global variational inference approach. In mediation effect estimation (Table 1), SIGMA dominates all four baselines (DeepMed, Lasso, RF, GBM) on empirical bias: for Pathway 1 NDE, SIGMA's bias of −0.0066 is roughly 10× smaller than DeepMed's (−0.0637) and 30× smaller than RF's (−0.2263); for Pathway 2 NIE, SIGMA's bias of −0.0231 is about 6× smaller than DeepMed's (0.1397). Lasso and GBM show the largest biases on NIE (up to 0.4347 for RF), indicating that standard regression and tree-based methods struggle with counterfactual mediation estimation. SIGMA's advantage is attributed to its BMA aggregation over the DAG ensemble, which propagates both within-structure and between-structure uncertainty into the final estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIGMA unifies three previously disconnected research threads: differentiable Flow-SEM structure learning (building on Voelkle et al. and the NOTEARS acyclicity penalty), semiparametric mediation effect estimation via Efficient Influence Functions (Bickel et al.; Tchetgen Tchetgen & Shpitser), and Bayesian Model Averaging for model selection (Raftery et al.). It departs from DECI, which performs end-to-end causal inference but targets interventional distributions rather than pathway-specific mediation effects, and from DeepMed, which estimates NDE/NIE but assumes a known mediation structure. The key conceptual departure is the Path Stability Score, which bridges structure discovery and pathway identification by quantifying marginal path inclusion probability across a posterior DAG ensemble — a step absent from both constraint-based and score-based causal discovery methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in high-dimensional biomedical research where causal structures are unknown and mediation pathways cannot be manually pre-specified, as in the Human Phenotype Project with 60+ features across 11 body systems. The HPP validation revealing a previously unspecified sleep–gut microbiota–cardiovascular pathway suggests the approach can surface clinically actionable mechanisms invisible to pre-specified analyses. Key limitations remain: reliance on faithfulness and causal sufficiency assumptions (acknowledged with sensitivity analysis frameworks but not empirically stress-tested), a single real-world dataset for validation, and the 22–27% performance degradation at 2000 dimensions that limits applicability to very high-dimensional omics settings. Open questions include whether the uniform BMA weighting can be improved with learned structure priors and whether the framework extends to time-varying or longitudinal mediation.
