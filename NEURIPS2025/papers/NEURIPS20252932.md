## Problem

Existing video concept transfer methods treat videos as a monolithic entity, making it difficult to independently manipulate foreground subjects, backgrounds, or motion dynamics. Additionally, they apply a single static prompt across all denoising timesteps, ignoring the progressive coarse-to-fine nature of diffusion generation, which limits fine-grained controllability and visual fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252932/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image (segmented into foreground and background via a pretrained segmentation model), a source video, a text description, and optionally optical flow extracted from a driving video via RAFT. It produces a 49-frame video at 720×480 resolution where the specified concept (character, garment, background, or motion) has been transferred from the reference while preserving temporal consistency. The foreground and background are processed through separate branches, and motion is injected as a conditioning signal with random noise augmentation for robustness.

## Method

UniTransfer employs a dual-to-single-stream DiT architecture (built on CogVideoX-5B) with three collaborative branches: a conditional foreground branch (no noise input), a generative background branch, and a motion fusion branch. Foreground features are injected into the background stream via zero-initialized convolutional projections at each DiT block (ControlNet-inspired), while optical flow is fused through element-wise addition with adaptive normalization. A self-supervised pretraining phase uses random rectangular masking on 12M OpenVid videos to learn disentangled foreground-background representations without annotations. The key timestep decomposition introduces a Chain-of-Prompt (CoP) mechanism where Qwen-QWQ-32B generates three hierarchical prompts (coarse, mid, fine) that are applied to different denoising stage intervals, aligning prompt granularity with the model's progressive generation focus from global structure to fine texture.

## Results

On the TikTok dataset, UniTransfer achieves LPIPS 0.152, PSNR 26.78, SSIM 0.803, FID 46.74, and FVD 345 for video character transfer, outperforming the best baseline Animate Anyone (LPIPS 0.198, FID 79.18, FVD 398) by a wide margin. On UBC Fashion, the method records LPIPS 0.125, FID 39.73, and FVD 312, surpassing Champ (FID 41.02, FVD 324). On VBench consistency metrics, UniTransfer attains the highest Subject Consistency (0.945 on TikTok, 0.939 on UBC), Aesthetic Quality (0.651/0.664), and Dynamic Degree (0.903/0.911) across all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTransfer outperforms all quantitative baselines on both TikTok and UBC. Against character animation methods (Animate Anyone, Champ, UniAnimate), it achieves 41% lower FID (46.74 vs. 79.18) and 13% lower FVD (345 vs. 398) on TikTok, with the largest gap over UniAnimate (FID 58.19). On VBench consistency metrics (Table 2), it surpasses CogVideoX (SubC 0.927, AesQ 0.557) and Mofa-Video (SubC 0.923, AesQ 0.593) on Subject Consistency and Aesthetic Quality, while matching Mofa-Video on Motion Smoothness (0.962). Text-guided editing baselines (Video-P2P, RF-Editor) trail significantly on Subject Consistency (0.842, 0.911) and Aesthetic Quality (0.443, 0.423). For motion transfer, qualitative comparisons show Motion-I2V suffers temporal drift and AnyV2V fails to preserve reference appearance, while UniTransfer maintains both identity fidelity and coherent motion. The paper does not report quantitative results for Motion-I2V or AnyV2V in the main tables; their comparison is limited to visual inspection in Figure 5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CogVideoX DiT architecture as its generative backbone and adapts the random-masking self-supervised pretraining strategy from LMD to the video domain. It departs from prior image-guided video editing methods (Animate Anyone, MIMO, VideoSwap, AnyV2V) that either treat the video as a monolithic entity or decompose only along the spatial axis for human-centric tasks. The key conceptual departure is the joint progressive decomposition along both spatial (foreground/background/motion) and temporal-denoising (coarse/mid/fine via Chain-of-Prompt) dimensions, with the latter drawing an explicit analogy to Chain-of-Thought reasoning in LLMs applied to diffusion timestep scheduling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying character, background, garment, and motion transfer under a single decomposed framework, this work addresses a practical gap where prior methods required separate pipelines for each editing sub-task. The dramatic ablation showing ~3× LPIPS degradation without self-supervised pretraining underscores that disentangled spatial representations are hard to learn from small annotated sets alone, a finding relevant to broader video generation research. Open questions include whether the three-stage CoP decomposition generalizes to longer videos or more complex multi-entity interactions, and whether the reliance on external segmentation (SAM2) and optical flow (RAFT) as conditioning signals limits end-to-end learnability.
