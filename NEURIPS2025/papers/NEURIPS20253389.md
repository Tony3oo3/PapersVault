## Problem

Existing scaling laws for LLM pre-training predict only the final-step loss and leave open whether the full loss trajectory obeys similar relations. Moreover, while learning rate schedules (LRSs) are known to be critical in practice, their precise role in shaping scaling exponents and optimal resource allocation remains theoretically uncharacterized. This paper addresses both gaps by establishing a functional-level scaling law that captures the entire loss dynamics under arbitrary LRSs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253389/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The FSL takes as input a continuous learning-rate schedule γ(t) (expressed in intrinsic time), model size M, capacity exponent β > 1, task difficulty s > 0, and label-noise level σ, and produces the expected excess risk E[R(ν_t)] as a closed-form expression in intrinsic time t. The schedule enters exclusively through a convolutional integral with a power-law forgetting kernel K(t) = (1+t)^{-(2-1/β)}, so different LRSs are distinguished only by how they weight this kernel. From the FSL, the paper derives explicit final-step scaling laws (power-law exponents in data D or compute C) and optimal hyperparameters—peak learning rate, decay fraction, and data-vs-model compute split—for each schedule in both data-limited and compute-limited regimes.

## Method

The key technical move is an intrinsic-time reparametrization of the SGD SDE: physical time τ is rescaled by the accumulated learning rate t = ∫₀^τ φ(r)dr, which absorbs the LRS from the drift term and retains it only in the diffusion coefficient γ(t) = hφ(T⁻¹(t))/b(T⁻¹(t)). This decoupling yields a Volterra integral equation for the expected loss whose solution is the Functional Scaling Law: E[R(ν_t)] − σ²/2 ≈ M^{−sβ} + (1+t)^{−s} + ∫₀ᵗ (1+t−z)^{−(2−1/β)}[(1+z)^{−s}+σ²]γ(z)dz. Each term has a clear interpretation—approximation error, signal learning, and noise accumulation/dissipation—so the LRS's effect is isolated in the convolution. The paper then instantiates the FSL for constant, exponential-decay, and WSD schedules, deriving explicit scaling exponents and optimal resource allocations that recover and extend prior results. Finally, a discretized FSL ansatz is fitted to LLM pre-training loss curves (0.1B–1B, dense and MoE) to predict unseen schedules and to numerically optimize the LRS, with the FSL-optimal schedule being WSD-like and driving the learning rate below the conventional 0.1·η_max threshold.

## Results

The paper establishes a Functional Scaling Law (FSL) that characterizes the full loss trajectory of SGD under arbitrary learning rate schedules in power-law kernel regression. Table 1 summarizes the resulting scaling exponents: WSD achieves the best data-optimal scaling (D^{-sβ/(1+sβ)}(log D)^{sβ/(1+sβ)} in the easy regime) and compute-optimal scaling (C^{-sβ/(2+sβ)}(log C)^{sβ/(2+sβ)}), strictly outperforming exponential decay and constant schedules. In LLM pre-training experiments (0.1B–1B parameters), the FSL-optimal LRS—discovered to be WSD-like with the decay phase driving the learning rate far below the conventional 0.1·η_max threshold—achieves a strictly lower final loss than cosine, WSD, and 8-1-1 baselines on a 1B QwenMoE model trained on 20B tokens.

| LRS | Data-Optimal (Easy) | Data-Optimal (Hard) | Compute-Optimal (Easy) | Compute-Optimal (Hard) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Constant | D^{−s/(s+1)} | D^{−s/(s+1)} | C^{−sβ/(1+sβ+β)} | C^{−sβ/(1+sβ+β)} |
| Exponential decay | D^{−sβ/(1+sβ)}(log D)^s | D^{−s}(log D)^s | C^{−sβ/(2+sβ)}(log C)^{sβ/(2+sβ)} | C^{−sβ/(1+β)}(log C)^{sβ/(1+β)} |
| WSD | D^{−sβ/(1+sβ)}(log D)^{sβ/(1+sβ)} | D^{−s} | C^{−sβ/(2+sβ)}(log C)^{sβ/(2+sβ)} | C^{−sβ/(1+β)} |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three canonical LRSs—cosine [37], WSD [69, 21], and the 8-1-1 multi-step schedule [5]—both theoretically and empirically. Theoretically, WSD achieves the best scaling efficiency in both data- and compute-limited regimes, followed by exponential decay, with the constant schedule performing worst; the key advantage of WSD is that its stable phase boosts intrinsic training time, removing logarithmic factors in the signal-learning term without altering the noise term's order. In LLM pre-training experiments on a 1B QwenMoE model (20B tokens, 33,907 steps), the FSL-optimal LRS (which is WSD-like with a deeper decay than standard WSD) achieves a strictly lower final loss than all three baselines. The FSL also accurately fits the 8-1-1 trajectory and generalizes to predict the unseen cosine and WSD loss curves for both 400M and 1B LLaMA models. Across additional experiments on GPT-2 (100M) and LLaMA (1B) at varying training durations (17k–134k steps), the FSL-optimal schedule consistently outperforms all WSD variants with different decay ratios (3:1, 4:1, 5:1) by approximately 0.01 in final loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of PLK regression scaling-law analyses (Bordelon et al. [7, 8], Paquette et al. [50], Lin et al. [35]) by introducing an intrinsic-time SDE formulation that unifies the treatment of arbitrary learning-rate schedules, building on the stochastic-modified-equation framework of Li et al. [31, 32]. Prior work analyzed only specific schedules (gradient flow, constant, or exponential decay) and focused on final-step loss; here the Functional Scaling Law captures the full loss trajectory under any LRS, with the schedule entering through a convolutional 'forgetting kernel.' The paper also provides a theoretical derivation of the empirical Multi-Power Law (MPL) fitting model of Luo et al. [38], connecting a heuristic LLM loss-curve predictor to first-principles kernel regression dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers the first principled explanation for why WSD schedules outperform pure decay in LLM pre-training, showing that this advantage already emerges in a simple quadratic (kernel regression) model—removing the need to invoke deep-network-specific mechanisms. The FSL framework is practically useful as a surrogate for fitting and predicting loss trajectories across model sizes (0.1B–1B) and architectures (dense LLaMA, GPT-2, QwenMoE), and the numerically derived FSL-optimal schedule is WSD-like with a decay far below the conventional 0.1·η_max floor. Open questions include whether the FSL characterization extends to the easier-task regime (s > 1) for random features, whether the kernel-regression insights transfer quantitatively to deep nonlinear networks, and how the framework generalizes to momentum-based optimizers or non-quadratic losses.
