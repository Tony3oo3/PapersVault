## Problem

Existing data attribution methods for text-to-image models—whether influence-function-based or unlearning-based—require minutes to hours per query and tens to hundreds of gigabytes of storage, making them orders of magnitude more expensive than the image generation they are meant to explain. No prior method achieves attribution at a latency comparable to the generation itself (tens of seconds) while retaining competitive counterfactual accuracy, preventing real-world deployment on platforms that charge users only 5–10 cents per image.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single synthesized image and its conditioning text prompt, encodes both through off-the-shelf DINO (image) and CLIP (text) backbones followed by a small learned MLP, and retrieves the top-K most influential training images from a dataset of up to 400 million via cosine-similarity search over precomputed embeddings. The output is a ranked list of training image indices; the method does not produce absolute influence magnitudes, only relative ordering. At training time, the supervision signal comes from AbU+ attribution scores computed on a two-stage subset: first 10k–100k nearest neighbors are retrieved with a CLIP index, then only a 20% random subsample of those neighbors receives full unlearning-based scoring.

## Method

The core contribution is distilling the slow Attribution-by-Unlearning-plus (AbU+) teacher into a fast embedding space via a pointwise learning-to-rank objective. The rank function is parameterized as r_ψ(ẑ, z_i) = cos(f_ψ(ẑ), f_ψ(z_i)), where f_ψ = g_ψ ∘ φ composes a pretrained encoder φ (concatenated DINO + CLIP-Text features) with a 3-layer MLP g_ψ trained to map toward attribution-relevant directions. Training uses binary cross-entropy on normalized rank labels (1/K for most influential, 1 for least), chosen over MSE regression (fails to converge at 10⁴ candidates) and ordinal loss (incompatible with single-vector cosine search at inference). Two sampling strategies improve efficiency and accuracy: with probability 0.1, a random non-neighbor image is injected and assigned the worst rank to preserve global distance structure, and only M ≈ 0.1K of the K neighbors are scored per iteration to reduce curation cost. At deployment, the entire attribution query reduces to one forward pass through the encoder and an ANN search, taking ~19 ms versus 2.28 hours for AbU+, while achieving counterfactual leave-K-out performance within one standard error of the unlearning-based teacher on MSCOCO and superior rank-prediction accuracy on Stable Diffusion trained on LAION-400M.

## Results

On MSCOCO (100K training images, 110 test queries), the tuned DINO+CLIP-Text embedding achieves a loss deviation of 5.28×10⁻³ at k=500 and 9.35×10⁻³ at k=4000, approaching the AbU+ teacher (5.83 and 10.70) while running in 18.7 ms versus 2.28 hours — a ~437,000× speedup. Storage drops from 1.9 GB (AbU+) to 354 MB. On Stable Diffusion (LAION-400M), the same tuned feature achieves competitive mAP(L) rank-prediction accuracy against AbU+ ground truth over 100K candidates, with text features proving strictly necessary for good performance (unlike MSCOCO where image features dominate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AbU+ teacher (2.28 hr/query), the tuned embedding achieves 91% of the loss-deviation signal at k=500 (5.28 vs 5.83) and 87% at k=4000 (9.35 vs 10.70), while being ~437,000× faster and using 5× less storage. Compared to D-TRAK (46.7 s, 30 GB), the tuned method is ~2,500× faster and 85× smaller in storage, with comparable loss deviation at k=500 (5.28 vs 5.44) and slightly lower at k=4000 (9.35 vs 9.59). Versus TRAK and JourneyTRAK (both ~3.7 min, 290 GB), the tuned method is ~12,000× faster and 820× smaller; TRAK edges ahead at k=500 (5.18 vs 5.28) but the tuned method surpasses JourneyTRAK (4.44) and is competitive with TRAK within one standard error. Among fast embedding baselines, the tuned DINO+CLIP-Text clearly outperforms untuned DINO (4.76→5.28 at k=500), CLIP-Text (3.82→5.28), DINOv2 (3.89→5.28), and AbC-DINO (4.75→5.28), confirming that attribution-specific tuning is essential. On Stable Diffusion, text features become strictly necessary (DINO/CLIP-Image alone underperform), and the tuned combined feature achieves the best mAP(L) across all thresholds, with performance still improving at 5000 queries (101M attribution ranks), indicating headroom with more compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper distills the slow but accurate Attribution by Unlearning (AbU) method into a fast feature embedding space via learning-to-rank, building directly on Wang et al.'s AbU (NeurIPS 2024) as the teacher signal and improving it with EKFAC (George et al., 2018) to create AbU+. Conceptually, it repurposes the knowledge-distillation paradigm—typically applied to compress model predictions—toward compressing an expensive per-query attribution procedure into a one-time-trained similarity search. It positions itself against influence-function methods (TRAK, D-TRAK, JourneyTRAK) that trade accuracy for speed, and against off-the-shelf feature similarity (CLIP, DINO) that is fast but not attribution-calibrated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing attribution from hours to ~19 ms with sub-1 GB storage, this work makes data attribution practically deployable on real-world text-to-image platforms, opening the door to compensation models, copyright provenance, and per-query transparency that were previously infeasible due to cost. Key limitations remain: the method distills only relative ranking (losing absolute influence magnitude), inherits the teacher's failure modes, and counterfactual validation was only feasible on MSCOCO, not Stable Diffusion. Open questions include whether influence-based attribution signals remain valid at LAION-400M scale, how to characterize the degree and concentration of influence, and extending the approach to flow-matching and one-step generative models.
