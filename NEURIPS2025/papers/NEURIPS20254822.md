## Problem

Most existing event-based object detection methods apply temporal modeling modules only at high-level, low-resolution features, where the inherently sparse and localized temporal structure of event streams has already been degraded by repeated downsampling. This paper argues that fine-grained temporal cues are most informative at early, high-resolution stages and that placing recurrent modules before significant spatial abstraction is essential for capturing the dense temporal patterns that drive accurate detection of slow-moving or partially occluded objects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams discretized into 5-channel voxel grids over a 50 ms window (B=5 temporal bins), processed as temporal sequences of length 21 (Gen1, 304×240) or 5 (1 Mpx at 640×360, eTram at 640×360). It outputs per-frame bounding boxes with class labels via a YOLOv6 detection head operating on multi-scale features fused by an FPN, evaluated with mAP and AP50 across vehicle, pedestrian, and two-wheeler categories.

## Method

The architecture is a CNN-RNN hybrid that places ConvLSTM-based recurrent blocks at low spatial scales (downsampling factors 2, 4, 8) before major downsampling, preserving fine-grained temporal information. The core innovation is the Decoupled Deformable-enhanced Recurrent Layer (DDRL), which decouples per-pixel motion estimation from feature fusion: current event features and the previous recurrent state are concatenated and compressed, then a modulated deformable convolution with learned offsets (Δx_k) and modulation scalars (Δm_k) adaptively resamples the compressed representation to align with scene motion, followed by an SE block over 8 grouped deformable channels. Three parallel branches perform independent temporal downsampling at different resolutions, and their outputs are hierarchically fused via an FPN feeding a YOLOv6 head. Training uses ADAM with a OneCycle scheduler and a mixed BPTT/TBPTT strategy, achieving 52.7 mAP on Gen1 with 26.4 M parameters—outperforming transformer-based baselines like SAST-CB (48.2) and S5-ViT-B (47.7) at comparable or lower inference cost.

## Results

The proposed CNN-RNN hybrid architecture (SATE) achieves 52.7 mAP on Gen1, 49.1 mAP on 1 Mpx, and 33.0 mAP on eTram, setting new state-of-the-art results on all three benchmarks. With only 26.4M parameters, the model runs at 8.80 ms per frame on Gen1 and 13.3 ms on 1 Mpx, matching or exceeding the inference speed of transformer-based competitors. On Gen1, it surpasses the previous best CNN-RNN method (ASTMNet, 46.7 mAP) by 6.0 mAP and the best transformer-based method (SAST-CB, 48.2 mAP) by 4.5 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Gen1, SATE (52.7 mAP) outperforms all 16 baselines, with the largest margins over CNN-RNN methods: +6.0 mAP over ASTMNet (46.7), +5.5 over RVT-B (47.2), and +4.5 over SAST-CB (48.2). It also surpasses the strongest transformer-based competitor ERGO-12 (50.4) by 2.3 mAP while using fewer parameters (26.4M vs. 59.6M) and running 8× faster (8.80 ms vs. 69.9 ms). On 1 Mpx, the advantage narrows to +0.4 mAP over SAST-CB (48.7) and +0.8 over ASTMNet (48.3), but SATE still leads all methods. On the sparser eTram traffic-monitoring dataset, SATE achieves 33.0 mAP, beating SAST-CB (30.0) by 3.0 mAP, RVT-B (29.5) by 3.5, and S5-ViT-B (29.3) by 3.7, while maintaining competitive inference speed (13.05 ms). Notably, SATE is trained from scratch without pre-trained weights, whereas several transformer baselines benefit from ImageNet pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on ConvLSTM for temporal recurrence and Deformable ConvNets v2 for adaptive spatial sampling, but departs sharply from both the Transformer-based detection line (RVT, GET, SAST, S5-ViT) and the earlier CNN+RNN line (RED, ASTMNet, DMANet) by relocating recurrent modules to low spatial scales—before significant downsampling—rather than at high-level features. The key conceptual departure is a 'divide-and-conquer' design that decouples per-pixel motion estimation (via learned deformable offsets) from feature fusion, combined with a multi-branch backbone that performs independent temporal downsampling at three resolution levels. This positions the work as a deliberate return to convolutional architectures with targeted recurrent augmentation, arguing that the field's shift toward global attention has overlooked fine-grained temporal cues that are most informative at early, high-resolution stages of sparse event streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a prevailing assumption in event-based detection that temporal modeling is best performed at high-level, semantically rich features, showing instead that low-dimensional recurrent encoding preserves critical motion information that is lost through successive downsampling. The 4–5 mAP gap over the strongest Transformer baselines with fewer parameters suggests that architectural simplicity and precise temporal placement can outcompete global attention for sparse, asynchronous data. However, the reliance on a basic voxel-grid representation and the absence of statistical significance testing limit the generality of the claims, and the authors themselves note that richer event encodings and sparse convolutional variants remain open directions that could further reduce the dependency on recurrent depth.
