## Problem

Training-free guided generation for flow/diffusion models has produced two seemingly separate families of techniques—posterior guidance (estimating gradients via the target prediction model) and end-to-end guidance (backpropagating through the full ODE solve)—without a unifying theoretical framework connecting them. This paper asks whether these families are actually related, and if so, what the precise relationship is and what it implies for the compute-accuracy trade-off in guidance gradients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253797/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained flow/diffusion model with affine probability paths (e.g., a diffusion model on 256×256 FFHQ images or an EquiFM model on QM9 molecules), a differentiable guidance function L defined on the data space, and optionally a noisy measurement y (for inverse problems) or target property values (for molecule generation). At each ODE timestep t, it consumes the current state x_t and the model's target prediction x_{1|t}^θ(x_t) to compute a local guidance gradient, producing a guided sample x_1 that minimizes L while remaining on the learned data manifold. The key operational detail is that the guidance is applied as a short inner optimization loop (k steps of a Runge-Kutta solver) at each of the N outer ODE steps, rather than as a single gradient evaluation or a full backpropagation through all N steps.

## Method

The core insight is that the standard posterior guidance gradient ∇_x L(x_{1|t}^θ(x)) is exactly an explicit Euler discretization (within DTO) or implicit Euler discretization (within OTD/continuous adjoint) of the true end-to-end gradient, with step size h = γ_1 − γ_t where γ_t is the signal-to-noise ratio. Using exponential integrators, the paper rewrites the affine probability path flow in a semi-linear form that makes this Euler interpretation transparent (Propositions 4.2–4.3). Theorem 5.1 further shows the flow Jacobian is an integral of covariance projections, explaining why the greedy step naturally steers the state along the data manifold, and Theorem 5.3 bounds the greedy-vs-ideal gradient error by O(h²). Motivated by Theorem 6.1—which shows that replacing the single Euler step with an α-th order Runge-Kutta solver yields O(h^{α+1}) error—the paper introduces a family of 'greedy(k-step)' methods that take k inner solver steps (midpoint, 2-step Euler, 5-step Euler) at each ODE timestep, creating a continuous design axis between cheap posterior guidance and expensive full end-to-end backpropagation.

## Results

On FFHQ inverse problems, the Greedy(Euler) posterior guidance strategy achieves PSNR 30.87 / SSIM 0.823 / LPIPS 0.141 / FID 40.73 on random-mask inpainting and PSNR 28.01 / SSIM 0.766 / LPIPS 0.182 / FID 57.04 on Gaussian deblurring, performing close to the SOTA DAPS (PSNR 31.12 / FID 32.17) and substantially outperforming DPS (PSNR 25.46 / FID 69.20). On the non-linear HDR reconstruction task, Greedy(5-step Euler) reaches PSNR 28.27 / FID 35.35, surpassing DAPS (PSNR 27.12 / FID 42.97). For QM9 molecule generation, the single-step Greedy(Euler) is unstable (MAE 11.28 on polarizability, worse than unguided EquiFM at 9.525), while Greedy(5-step Euler) achieves MAE 4.177, and full DTO(50-step) reaches the best MAE of 1.404, approaching the theoretical lower bound of 0.10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPS (Chung et al., ICLR 2023), the greedy posterior guidance methods clearly win on all metrics: on random-mask inpainting, Greedy(Euler) improves PSNR from 25.46 to 30.87 and FID from 69.20 to 40.73; on Gaussian deblurring, PSNR improves from 25.87 to 28.01 and FID from 79.75 to 57.04. Against DAPS (Zhang et al., CVPR 2025), the greedy methods are competitive but slightly behind on linear tasks (e.g., PSNR 30.87 vs. 31.12 on inpainting), yet the 5-step Euler variant actually surpasses DAPS on the non-linear HDR reconstruction task (PSNR 28.27 vs. 27.12, FID 35.35 vs. 42.97). Against RED-diff (Mardani et al., ICLR 2024), the greedy methods substantially outperform on HDR reconstruction (PSNR 25.07–28.27 vs. 22.16). For molecule generation, the unguided EquiFM baseline (Song et al., NeurIPS 2023) is beaten by higher-order greedy variants (e.g., Greedy(5-step Euler) MAE 4.177 vs. 9.525 on polarizability), but the single-step Greedy(Euler) is unstable and underperforms the unguided model; full DTO(50-step) achieves the best results (MAE 1.404) but at much higher compute cost, confirming the paper's central trade-off between gradient accuracy and computational budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate families of training-free guided generation for flow/diffusion models: posterior guidance (DPS and its variants, which use Tweedie's formula to estimate a local gradient) and end-to-end guidance (D-Flow, FlowGrad, and adjoint-based methods that backpropagate through the full ODE solve). The key conceptual move is showing that the posterior guidance gradient is exactly a first-order Euler discretization of the continuous adjoint equations, making the two families points on a single accuracy-compute spectrum parameterized by the order of the numerical solver. The theoretical machinery draws on exponential integrators for affine probability paths (building on DPM-Solver and related work) and the Jacobian structure analysis from D-Flow, but the unification itself and the resulting design space of intermediate Runge-Kutta guidance schemes are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(h²) error bound between greedy and ideal gradients gives practitioners a principled, task-dependent knob for trading compute against guidance accuracy, and the empirical demonstration that 5-step Euler greedy guidance can beat SOTA DAPS on HDR reconstruction validates the design space. However, the molecule generation results reveal a significant limitation: the single-step greedy (Euler) is unstable and worse than unguided generation, requiring many steps to approach DTO performance, which undermines the 'greed is good' narrative for non-smooth or high-dimensional guidance landscapes. Open questions include whether hybrid or adaptive step-size schemes (as hinted at via Moufad et al.) can resolve the instability, and whether the unification extends beyond affine probability paths to more general flow models.
