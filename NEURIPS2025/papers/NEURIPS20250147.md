## Problem

State Space Models (SSMs) such as VMamba suffer significant performance degradation under distribution shifts due to their fixed directional traversal bias and accumulation of domain-specific artifacts in recurrent hidden states. While test-time adaptation (TTA) methods are well developed for CNNs and ViTs, no prior approach explicitly exploits the unique architectural properties of SSMs—specifically their multi-directional scan mechanism—for adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250147/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single corrupted or domain-shifted RGB image as input, which is internally reshaped into a sequence of non-overlapping patches and traversed along four canonical spatial directions (left-to-right, top-to-bottom, right-to-left, bottom-to-top). The output is a class label over the same label space as the source domain. Adaptation is fully self-supervised: no ground-truth labels or source data are required at test time, and only the Mamba-specific state-space parameters within the SS2D blocks are updated while the rest of the network remains frozen.

## Method

TRUST generates multiple traversal permutations by reordering VMamba's four directional scans, exposing the model to diverse causal perspectives of the same image. In an offline phase, permutations are ranked by prediction entropy and the top-K lowest-entropy (most confident) permutations are selected. During adaptation, each selected permutation reorders the cross-scan traversals, and the SS2D parameters are updated via one step of gradient descent on a cross-entropy loss against the model's own pseudo-labels. The resulting K adapted parameter sets are then averaged in weight space, drawing on the flat-minima principle to converge toward a smoother, more robust region of the loss landscape. The final averaged model is evaluated using the default traversal order, yielding a single consolidated model that suppresses artifact-induced bias without requiring external augmentations or auxiliary models.

## Results

TRUST achieves top-1 accuracy of 77.5% on CIFAR10-C, 54.3% on CIFAR100-C, and 56.1% on ImageNet-C (all at severity level 5), surpassing the strongest baseline SHOT by 10.7–14.4 percentage points. On domain generalization benchmarks, TRUST reaches 41.5% on ImageNet-S, 64.0% on ImageNet-V2, 44.3% on ImageNet-R, and 69.9% on PACS, with the largest gains on ImageNet-R (+13.0% over source-only) and ImageNet-S (+10.1% over source-only). The method consistently outperforms all six baselines across all seven benchmarks without requiring source data, external augmentations, or auxiliary models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRUST outperforms all six baselines on every benchmark. Against the strongest general-purpose TTA methods, SHOT and Tent, TRUST gains 10.7–14.4 points on corruption datasets (CIFAR10-C, CIFAR100-C, ImageNet-C) and 8.9–12.4 points on domain shift benchmarks (ImageNet-S, ImageNet-R), while the margin narrows to 1.6–2.5 points on ImageNet-V2 and PACS where the source model is already relatively robust. SAR and ETA, which incorporate sharpness-aware or sample-weighted entropy minimization, show only marginal improvements over source-only (≤3 points) and are consistently outperformed by TRUST by 10–17 points. LAME, a parameter-free Laplacian-based method, essentially matches source-only performance on most datasets, highlighting its limited adaptability. The adapted ViT-based method FOA [34], when ported to the VMamba backbone, actually degrades performance by 8.2 points on ImageNet-C, underscoring that architecture-agnostic TTA strategies are poorly suited to SSMs, whereas TRUST's exploitation of the SS2D traversal mechanism yields a 17.4-point gain over source-only on the same benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VMamba's SS2D module, which processes image patches along four canonical spatial directions (left-to-right, top-to-bottom, right-to-left, bottom-to-top). It departs from architecture-agnostic TTA methods (Tent, SHOT, SAR, LAME) that typically update only normalization-layer affine parameters via entropy minimization, by instead adapting the Mamba-specific state-space parameters within SS2D blocks. The weight-averaging component draws on the flat-minima literature (SWAD, Model Stock), but replaces the usual source of diversity (data augmentation) with traversal permutations that exploit VMamba's internal causal ordering, yielding structurally diverse model variants without external augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRUST is the first TTA method that explicitly leverages the architectural properties of SSMs (directional traversal, recurrent hidden states) for adaptation, opening a path for architecture-aware test-time strategies as SSM-based vision models gain traction. A key limitation is that the method is specific to VMamba's four-directional Cross-Scan design and has not been validated on other SSM variants or at scale beyond ImageNet-1000; the authors also note it does not yet generalize to the medical domain. The large performance gap over baselines is partly attributable to adapting SS2D parameters rather than BN layers (the 'naive' variant already gains ~8%), so the incremental value of the permutation-and-averaging mechanism, while clean, is more modest (~3–4%). Open questions include whether the approach extends to other SSM architectures with different scanning strategies and whether it scales to video or 3D settings.
