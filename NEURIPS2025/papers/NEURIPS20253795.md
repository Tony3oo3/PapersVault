## Problem

Existing temporal tensor decomposition methods assume discrete, finite-dimensional indexes in all non-temporal modes, making them unsuitable for data with continuous spatial coordinates (e.g., longitude, latitude, depth in ocean or climate data). Additionally, automatic rank determination—a critical hyperparameter selection problem—remains largely unexplored in functional temporal tensor models, where classical discrete-factor sparsity priors cannot be directly applied.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253795/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of observed tensor entries, each specified by a tuple of continuous spatial indexes (one per non-temporal mode) and a continuous timestamp, with no requirement for a regular grid. It outputs predicted entry values at arbitrary continuous index-timestamp combinations, accompanied by a closed-form Student's t-distribution for uncertainty quantification, the learned R-dimensional factor trajectories for each mode, and an automatically revealed effective rank (components pruned to zero). Unlike fixed-rank baselines, the user specifies only an upper bound R (e.g., 10) and the model determines the active rank during training.

## Method

CATTE encodes each continuous spatial index via learnable Fourier features (cos/sin with a learnable frequency vector), feeds them through an MLP encoder to produce the initial state of a per-mode neural ODE, and integrates the ODE forward in time to obtain time-varying latent factor trajectories, which are decoded back to R-dimensional factors. For automatic rank determination, it places a dimension-wise Gaussian-Gamma sparsity prior over the entire family of factor trajectories (rather than static factors), so that redundant components are driven to zero across all time and space. The key technical contribution is a fully analytical ELBO derived from the conjugate Gaussian-Gamma structure, eliminating the need for sampling-based approximation and enabling efficient gradient-based joint optimization of the ODE parameters, Fourier frequencies, and variational parameters. The resulting predictive distribution for new entries follows a Student's t-distribution with closed-form mean, scale, and degrees of freedom.

## Results

CATTE with its Functional Automatic Rank Determination (FARD) mechanism achieves the best RMSE across all three real-world datasets without manual rank tuning: 0.284 on CA Traffic, 0.078 on Server Room, and 0.373 on SSF. The closest competitor, LRTFR at R=7, achieves 0.365, 0.156, and 0.502 respectively. On the SSF dataset (which has continuous spatial coordinates), methods that do not model continuous-indexed modes (NONFAT, DEMOTE, THIS-ODE) fail catastrophically with RMSE > 1.6, while CATTE achieves 0.373. CATTE also reveals the underlying tensor ranks automatically (5, 7, 7 for the three datasets) and provides uncertainty quantification via a closed-form Student's-t predictive distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CATTE outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor LRTFR [17] (a deterministic low-rank functional Tucker model), CATTE reduces RMSE by 22% on CA Traffic (0.284 vs 0.365), 50% on Server Room (0.078 vs 0.156), and 26% on SSF (0.373 vs 0.502), while also providing uncertainty quantification that LRTFR lacks. The temporal tensor methods THIS-ODE [6], NONFAT [15], and DEMOTE [28] all assume discrete spatial indexes and fail dramatically on the SSF dataset (RMSE 1.69–9.79) because they cannot exploit the continuous spatial coordinates (latitude, longitude, depth). FunBaT [19], which does handle continuous indexes via Gaussian process priors, performs reasonably on SSF (RMSE ~0.64–0.69) but still trails CATTE by ~40–45% because it treats temporal dynamics as an independent mode rather than learning time-varying factor trajectories. The ablation (CATTE w.o. FARD) confirms that the automatic rank mechanism contributes an additional 5–10% RMSE improvement over a fixed-rank variant, and CATTE is more robust to added Gaussian noise (RMSE 0.305 at noise variance 0.5 vs 0.356 without FARD).

## Relation to Prior Work

CATTE unifies two previously separate lines of work: temporal tensor decomposition (which models continuous timestamps via neural ODEs, GPs, or splines but assumes discrete indexes in other modes) and functional tensor decomposition (which handles continuous spatial indexes but treats time as just another mode). The automatic rank determination mechanism (FARD) extends the classical Bayesian Gaussian-Gamma prior framework of Zhao et al. (2015) from static factor matrices to families of latent functions, requiring a novel analytical ELBO derivation that avoids sampling. The use of learnable Fourier features (Tancik et al., 2020) as initial states for the neural ODE encoder is a repurposing of a technique originally designed for image synthesis, here applied to encode arbitrary continuous spatial coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CATTE addresses a practical gap for spatiotemporal scientific data (ocean, climate, traffic) where multiple modes are naturally continuous and the underlying rank is unknown a priori. The sampling-free analytical ELBO makes the Bayesian rank-selection mechanism computationally tractable in the functional setting, which prior methods could not achieve. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the absence of explicit physical constraints, limiting long-range extrapolation accuracy; integrating domain-specific PDEs or conservation laws is identified as the natural next step. The framework also opens the question of whether the CP-format factorization is the most expressive structure for highly anisotropic spatiotemporal fields, where Tucker or tensor-train cores might be preferable.
