## Problem

Existing deepfake detectors are typically designed to identify artifacts specific to particular forgery techniques, making them impractical to extend to the ever-growing diversity of generators. The paper asks whether a universal detector can be built by focusing on a small set of generalizable artifacts shared across all deepfakes, rather than enumerating technique-specific cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252330/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector consumes RGB facial images (or 32 uniformly sampled frames from a video) at standard resolution and outputs a binary real/fake classification score. During training, pseudo-fake samples are synthesized on-the-fly via the FIA-USA pipeline using 81 facial landmarks for mask generation, an autoencoder, and a super-resolution model, so no real deepfake data is needed at training time. The backbone (EfficientNet-B4) feeds a Feature Pyramid Network whose highest-resolution layer is used for the contrastive regularization.

## Method

The framework rests on three components. First, FIA-USA is a data augmentation strategy that simultaneously injects Face Inconsistency Artifacts (via multi-type masks covering both full-face convex-hull boundaries and localized eye/mouth regions) and Up-Sampling Artifacts (via autoencoder and super-resolution reconstruction of the target face), then blends the reconstructed target with a source face using a randomly selected mask. Second, Automatic Forgery-aware Feature Selection (AFFS) pre-computes a per-channel ranking by measuring how well each channel's real-vs-fake feature difference aligns with the forgery mask, retaining only the top-m channels per layer to build a compressed FPN. Third, Region-aware Contrastive Regularization (RCR) applies two contrastive losses on the FPN features: a patch-level loss that pulls real-region features together while pushing forged-region features apart, and an image-level loss that aligns the unmanipulated regions of real and fake faces while separating the manipulated regions. The overall training objective combines binary cross-entropy with weighted PCR and ICR terms (λ₁=1, λ₂=2.5, λ₃=0.25, τ=0.7), optimized with SAM for 50 epochs.

## Results

On the DeepfakeBench cross-dataset evaluation (frame-level AUC), the proposed method achieves an average of 85.2%, outperforming LSDA (84.8%) and ProDet (84.3%) across CDF-v1, CDF-v2, DFD, and DFDCP. On the DF40 generative deepfake benchmark, the method reaches 87.8% average AUC across six face-swapping types, surpassing the next-best ProgressiveDet (78.7%) by 9.1 points. On the DiFF diffusion-based forgery dataset, it achieves 86.05% (T2I), 84.95% (I2I), 89.42% (FS), and 72.73% (FE) frame-level AUC, exceeding SBI by 5.8–5.9 points across all subsets. At the video level, the method attains 94.1% AUC on CDF-v2 and 86.6% on DFDCP, both leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best average frame-level AUC (85.2%) on DeepfakeBench, edging out LSDA (84.8%) and ProDet (84.3%), though it trails ProDet on CDF-v1 (90.1 vs. 90.9) and DFD (82.1 vs. 84.8). On the DF40 generative benchmark, it dominates all 30+ baselines by a wide margin, reaching 87.8% average AUC versus ProgressiveDet's 78.7% and SBI's 64.4%, with the largest gap on e4s (87.5 vs. 71.0) and uniface (91.8 vs. 85.4). On DiFF, it outperforms SBI by 5.8–5.9 points across all four forgery subtypes (T2I, I2I, FS, FE) and surpasses the diffusion-specific DIRE detector by 41.8 points on T2I. At the video level, it leads on CDF-v2 (94.1 vs. SBI's 92.8) and DFDCP (86.6 vs. SBI's 85.5), but falls short of LSDA on DFDC (73.2 vs. 77.0). The method also demonstrates superior robustness to Gaussian blur, JPEG compression, contrast changes, and block-wise perturbations compared to FWA, Face X-ray, SBI, and PatchForensics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the blending-based pseudo-fake augmentation paradigm pioneered by Face X-ray [40] and refined by SBI [63], but departs from prior work by systematically identifying two universal artifact categories (Face Inconsistency Artifacts and Up-Sampling Artifacts) that all deepfakes must exhibit, and designing a dual-artifact augmentation (FIA-USA) that simultaneously injects both at face-level and region-level. Unlike prior methods that target isolated artifact types or operate in frequency/latent domains, the paper argues that a standard RGB-level image classifier trained on pseudo-fakes containing only FIA and USA can generalize broadly, complemented by AFFS for feature compression and RCR for region-aware contrastive learning. The work also positions itself against recent latent-space augmentation methods (e.g., LSDA [79]) by demonstrating that properly designed RGB-level artifacts retain critical discriminative signals even against generative deepfakes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FIA/USA taxonomy provides a principled, generator-agnostic lens for understanding why deepfakes are detectable, potentially guiding future detector design toward universal rather than method-specific artifacts. The finding that a simple image-level pseudo-fake pipeline with a standard CNN backbone outperforms more complex frequency-domain and latent-space approaches across 7 datasets (including the very recent DF40 and DiFF benchmarks) challenges the field's recent trajectory toward increasingly sophisticated augmentation strategies. Open questions include whether the two-artifact taxonomy remains exhaustive as generative models improve, how the approach scales to video-level temporal reasoning, and whether the AFFS initialization step (requiring a pre-trained model) limits practical deployment. The lack of open-sourced code also limits immediate reproducibility.
