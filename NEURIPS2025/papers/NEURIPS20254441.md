## Problem

Existing noise-robust GNN methods inherit detection mechanisms designed for i.i.d. data and fail to explicitly exploit the node-neighbor interdependencies inherent in graph structure for identifying mislabeled nodes. This paper asks whether the semantic discrepancy between a node's individual features and its structural context can serve as an intrinsic, graph-specific signal for label noise detection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph G=(V,E) with node feature matrix X∈R^{|V|×d}, adjacency matrix A, and noisy one-hot labels Ỹ over a training split (10% of nodes). It produces final node class predictions from the mixed-view classifier, which integrates both node features and neighborhood structure via standard GCN message passing with self-loops. The framework does not require knowledge of the noise rate or transition matrix.

## Method

GD² decouples node representations into an ego-view (GCN layers using only the identity matrix, i.e., no message passing) and a structure-view (GCN layers aggregating neighbor features via the adjacency matrix), each with a separate linear classifier. The L2 prediction discrepancy between the two views is modeled with a two-component GMM to partition training nodes into clean and noisy subsets. A view-specific training strategy then amplifies this discrepancy: the ego-view is supervised on all noisy labels, the structure-view on clean labels plus high-confidence pseudo-labels from a mixed-view, and the mixed-view on clean labels only. A theoretical concentration bound (Theorem 1) shows that clean-labeled nodes exhibit bounded cross-view representation deviation that tightens with node degree, while mislabeled nodes violate this bound because their ego-view overfits corrupted supervision independently of the stable structural context.

## Results

GD² achieves the best or second-best node classification accuracy across all six datasets (Computer, Photo, CS, WikiCS, Roman-Empire, Amazon-Ratings) under both Uniform and Pair noise settings. Under the most challenging Pair-0.4 noise, GD² reaches 83.16% on Computer, 89.00% on Photo, 84.68% on CS, 75.88% on WikiCS, 62.16% on Roman-Empire, and 38.28% on Amazon-Ratings. The largest margin over the strongest graph-specific baseline (ERASE) is 3.03% on Computer (Pair-0.4), while on heterophilous graphs GD² leads GCN by up to 2.54% (Roman-Empire, Uniform-0.2: 66.19% vs 64.65%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GD² outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and noise setting. Among graph-specific methods, the closest competitor is ERASE (label propagation + coding rate), which GD² surpasses by 1.42–3.03% depending on dataset and noise level (e.g., 83.16% vs 80.13% on Computer Pair-0.4). NRGNN (structure refinement) and TSS (topological sample selection) trail GD² by 1–4% on homophilous graphs and by up to 4.5% on heterophilous graphs (Roman-Empire). RTGNN and PI-GNN (auxiliary supervision) are consistently outperformed, with GD² leading by 2–6% under high noise. General i.i.d. noisy-label methods (SCE, APL, Co-teaching, JoCoR, ProMix) degrade substantially on graph data—e.g., Co-teaching drops to 48.53% on Roman-Empire Uniform-0.6 while GD² maintains 51.24%—confirming that graph-specific relational modeling is essential for effective noise-robust node classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in noise-robust graph learning, where mislabeled nodes are identified via loss-based or confidence-based metrics inherited from i.i.d. settings (e.g., NRGNN, ERASE, TSS). Instead, GD² repurposes the structural decomposition of GNN message passing into an ego-view (node features only) and a structure-view (neighborhood aggregation) as an explicit noise detection signal, modeling their prediction discrepancy with a GMM. The view-specific training strategy, which assigns asymmetric supervision (noisy labels to ego-view, pseudo-labels to structure-view), is a novel design choice that amplifies the discrepancy for mislabeled nodes. The theoretical analysis builds on standard concentration-of-measure arguments for GCN representations, extending them to bound cross-view discrepancies for clean nodes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GD² provides a principled, graph-native mechanism for noise detection that explicitly exploits node-neighbor interdependencies rather than treating graph nodes as i.i.d. samples, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior work. The framework is relatively lightweight (GCN encoder + linear heads + GMM) and achieves consistent gains across both homophilous and heterophilous graphs, suggesting broad applicability. However, the approach is limited to static, homogeneous graphs, and its extension to dynamic or heterogeneous settings—where structural context is relation-specific and temporally evolving—remains an open question. Additionally, the method's reliance on a GMM for discrepancy modeling and two hand-tuned thresholds (τ, γ) introduces practical sensitivity, as shown in the hyperparameter analysis.
