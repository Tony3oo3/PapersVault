## Problem

Existing expansion-based continual learning methods primarily target single-domain class-incremental learning with frozen pre-trained backbones, which fails to handle the more realistic multi-domain continual learning (MDCL) scenario where tasks span diverse domains (natural, aerial, medical) with both severe domain shifts and underlying semantic similarities. Frozen backbones lack sufficient plasticity for challenging domains, passive parameter freezing leads to forgetting in long task sequences, and no mechanism exists to leverage previously learned parameters when analogous domains reappear.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from heterogeneous domains (CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet, CUB-200, MNIST, ImageNet-R, EuroSAT, RESISC45, CropDiseases, ChestX-ray8) and outputs a class label per image. During training, each task provides only its own domain's data with no access to prior task data (rehearsal-free). During testing, the model operates in a task-agnostic manner: the Expert Selection Mechanism routes each sample to the most suitable expert based on Mahalanobis distance to stored Gaussian memory distributions, requiring no task identity at inference time.

## Method

LEAR introduces a collaborative backbone of two pre-trained ViTs (global and local), fine-tuning only their last three layers, which jointly feed a dynamically created lightweight expert (feature transformation module + linear classifier) per task. Three alignment losses regulate optimization: MIBPA minimizes mutual information between current and frozen-backbone predictions to preserve historical expert prediction patterns; KLDBFA constrains the local backbone via KL divergence between current and frozen feature distributions modeled as Gaussians; and HSICBCO minimizes the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion between global and local features to enforce complementary, disentangled representations. An Expert Selection Mechanism models each expert's knowledge as a Gaussian memory distribution and selects the most relevant prior expert via Mahalanobis distance to initialize new experts (accelerating learning on analogous domains) and to route test samples without task labels.

## Results

On the CDM multi-domain continual learning sequence (10 domains), LEAR achieves an average accuracy of 84.61%, outperforming the strongest baseline RanPAC (71.01%) by 13.6 percentage points. On the reshuffled ETI sequence, LEAR reaches 84.90% average accuracy, again surpassing RanPAC (71.08%) by 13.8 points. LEAR also achieves the lowest GPU memory usage (2,926 MB) and CPU memory (9,526 MB) among all methods while maintaining 42.54M trainable parameters, demonstrating that its selective fine-tuning of only the last three ViT layers reduces computational overhead compared to full-backbone backpropagation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAR dominates all eight baselines across both CDM and ETI sequences. Against the strongest competitor RanPAC (NeurIPS 2024), LEAR gains +13.6% (CDM) and +13.8% (ETI) in average accuracy, with particularly large margins on challenging domains like TinyImageNet (+9.8% on CDM) and ChestX-ray8 (+6.3% on CDM). The MoE-adapter method (CVPR 2024) shows highly inconsistent performance (e.g., 92.74% on C10 but only 6.12% on TImg in CDM), yielding a 57.33% average that LEAR surpasses by 27.3 points. Prompt-based methods (L2P, DAP, D-Prompt, C-Prompt) all remain below 31% average accuracy in CDM, with C-Prompt collapsing to 12.42%, indicating that frozen-backbone prompting is insufficient for multi-domain shifts. Rehearsal-based DER++(Re) and the dual-branch CLS-ER achieve only 29.22% and 30.34% respectively, confirming that memory-buffer approaches struggle when domains span natural, aerial, and medical imagery simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEAR extends the expansion-based method (EBM) lineage for continual learning by departing from the standard practice of freezing pre-trained ViT backbones. While prior EBMs (RanPAC, MoE-adapters) and prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) allocate task-specific capacity on top of a frozen backbone, LEAR introduces a collaborative dual-backbone architecture (global + local) where both are incrementally fine-tuned under three distinct alignment constraints (mutual information, KL divergence, HSIC). The paper also carves new ground by formulating Multi-domain Continual Learning (MDCL) as a scenario that deliberately mixes semantically similar and dissimilar domains within a single task sequence, going beyond the single-domain CIL or the simpler DIL settings evaluated in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MDCL formulation is a useful and more realistic generalization that captures the coexistence of related and unrelated domains in real deployment, and the rehearsal-free design with task-agnostic test-time expert routing has practical appeal. The dramatic ablation showing that removing the Expert Selection Mechanism collapses performance from 84.90% to 23.71% underscores how critical structured knowledge transfer is in multi-domain settings. However, the acknowledged parameter growth (42.54M trainable parameters, comparable to full fine-tuning) limits scalability to long task sequences, and the authors themselves flag expert merging with self-distillation as needed future work. Open questions include whether the Gaussian distribution assumption for feature alignment holds under extreme domain shifts and whether the approach generalizes beyond ViT-based backbones.
