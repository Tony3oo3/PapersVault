## Problem

Autoregressive speech language modeling in continuous latent space lacks an efficient per-step generative module: existing continuous approaches rely on diffusion or flow matching (requiring iterative sampling per step) or regression losses that fail to capture the underlying distribution. The paper asks how to construct a lightweight, single-pass conditional generator that is training-stable, inference-efficient, and capable of modeling the full per-step distribution without discretization or hierarchical architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253417/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes BPE-tokenized text (vocab 16,384) and a reference speech prompt (either a 3-second prefix for continuation or a full utterance for voice cloning), both encoded into a shared sequence. Speech is represented as continuous latent vectors at 75 Hz with dimensionality 128, obtained by summing the eight Encodec codebook embeddings per frame. Output is a speech waveform decoded from the generated latent sequence via the Encodec decoder; in streaming mode, text and speech positions are interleaved at a configurable n:m ratio so that m speech vectors are produced for every n text subwords received.

## Method

SLED pairs a 12-layer LLaMA-style Transformer (0.2B params) for autoregressive dependency modeling with a lightweight 6-block MLP (~35M params) as the per-step conditional generator, which maps the AR condition vector z_t and a random noise vector ε through AdaLN-modulated residual blocks to produce the next latent vector in a single forward pass. Training minimizes the Generalized Energy Distance (with L1 norm, β=1) between model samples and ground-truth latents per step; the critical repulsive term E[‖h_t − h_t′‖₁] between two model samples is what elevates the objective from a mere regression loss (RMSE) to a strictly proper scoring rule that captures distributional structure. At inference, classifier-free guidance (λ=2.0) interpolates conditional and unconditional AR outputs to improve text-speech alignment, and a binary sigmoid head on the AR output predicts termination. The purely autoregressive design with no post-AR refinement enables native streaming generation by interleaving text and speech positions in the sequence.

## Results

On the LibriSpeech-PC test-clean set, SLED (0.2B params) achieves WER-C of 1.59% (3s prefix) and 1.51% (reference utterance), both surpassing the ground-truth Encodec reconstruction (1.79%). Speaker similarity (SIM) reaches 0.515 and 0.664 under the two prompting settings, respectively. In streaming mode (5:20 text-to-speech interleaving), SLED maintains a DNSMOS of 3.59, nearly matching offline synthesis (3.58), while incurring only a modest WER-C increase (2.18 vs. 1.67). SLED achieves an RTF of 0.8 with 280G FLOPs for 10 s of audio, using roughly 10× fewer FLOPs than DiTAR (2750G) at comparable RTF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the discrete hierarchical baseline VALL-E [72], SLED wins on every reported metric: WER-H drops from 3.8 to 1.99 (3s prefix) and from 5.9 to 1.97 (reference), while SIM improves from 0.508 to 0.515 and 0.580 to 0.664, despite using a lighter generative head (~35M vs. ~159M NAR module). Compared to VALL-E 2 [5], SLED achieves lower WER-C (1.59 vs. 1.6; 1.51 vs. 1.5) and WER-H (1.99 vs. 2.32; 1.97 vs. 2.44) but trails slightly on SIM (0.515 vs. 0.529; 0.664 vs. 0.678). Among continuous-latent baselines, SLED matches MELLE [46] on reference-utterance SIM (0.664) and WER-C (1.51 vs. 1.47) while improving WER-H (1.97 vs. 2.10); against FELLE [73], SLED substantially outperforms on the reference setting (WER-C 1.51 vs. 2.20, WER-H 1.97 vs. 2.89) but is marginally behind on 3s-prefix SIM (0.515 vs. 0.539). In the efficiency comparison with DiTAR [26], SLED achieves a comparable RTF (0.8 vs. 0.66) while requiring only 280G FLOPs versus 2750G (~10× reduction) and 0.2B versus 0.6B parameters. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that replacing the energy-distance objective with plain RMSE causes catastrophic failure (WER-C jumps from 1.59 to 40.60), underscoring the necessity of the repulsive term for distributional modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLED sits at the intersection of two lines of work: discrete speech language models (VALL-E, VALL-E 2) that rely on RVQ tokenization and hierarchical autoregressive architectures, and continuous speech LM approaches (MELLE, FELLE, DiTAR) that attempt to model latent representations directly but use regression losses, per-token diffusion, or flow matching. The paper's key departure is replacing these per-step generative objectives with generalized energy distance (GED), a specialized MMD that serves as a strictly proper scoring rule, paired with a lightweight MLP-based implicit generative module using AdaLN noise injection (borrowed from Shao et al. [60]). It also adopts the binary stop-prediction head from MELLE [46] and the interleaved text-speech scheduling from CosyVoice 2 [8] for streaming, but eliminates the post-AR refinement and multi-step sampling that those methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing RVQ, hierarchical architectures, and post-AR refinement, SLED substantially simplifies the speech LM pipeline while enabling natural streaming generation—a property that is architecturally difficult to achieve in discrete hierarchical or diffusion-based continuous models. The clean theoretical grounding (energy distance as a proper scoring rule) provides a principled alternative to ad-hoc regression or diffusion objectives for continuous autoregressive generation. However, the work is limited to English audiobook speech at 0.2B scale, voice cloning still lags behind traditional TTS systems, and the latent encoder (Encodec) was not co-trained for this purpose. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multilingual or general-purpose speech LMs, and whether a purpose-built continuous latent encoder would close the remaining quality gap.
