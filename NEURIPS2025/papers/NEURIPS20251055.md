## Problem

Large-scale monocular depth estimation foundation models (e.g., Depth Anything) suffer severe accuracy degradation under aggressive 4-bit post-training quantization, while quantization-aware training requires labeled data that is unavailable for zero-shot foundation models. No prior method successfully applies 4-bit quantization across all layers of such models without ground-truth depth annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251055/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single RGB image as input and produces a relative depth map as output, operating in a zero-shot setting without task-specific fine-tuning. Quantization calibration uses only 16 unlabeled images, and SCA module training uses a 5% unlabeled subset of the training set. The SCA modules themselves are stored in FP16 precision to minimize the parameter overhead (~0.44M additional parameters), while the rest of the network operates at 4-bit for both weights and activations.

## Method

QSCA inserts lightweight Self-Compensating Auxiliary (SCA) modules—linear layers in transformer encoder blocks and convolutional layers in DPT decoder blocks—into each quantized block in a residual fashion, adding a correction term to the quantized output without modifying quantized weights. SCA parameters are initialized via a closed-form ridge-regression solution that projects the input onto the residual between full-precision and quantized block outputs. The SCA modules are then fine-tuned through self-supervised distillation: the full-precision model serves as a teacher, and the quantized model with SCA is trained to match intermediate block features (L1 loss) and final depth maps (SILog loss) using only unlabeled calibration data. This eliminates the need for ground-truth depth labels, enabling rapid adaptation (~210s for ViT-S) compared to reconstruction-based PTQ methods that require thousands of seconds.

## Results

On NYUv2 under 4-bit weight/activation quantization (W4A4), QSCA achieves δ₁ of 0.8097 (ViT-S) and 0.6845 (ViT-B) for Depth Anything v1, and 0.8151 / 0.6845 for Depth Anything v2, compared to full-precision δ₁ of 0.9720–0.9791. Under the less aggressive W4A6 setting, QSCA reaches δ₁ of 0.9333–0.9468, recovering over 95% of full-precision accuracy. On KITTI (W4A4), QSCA achieves δ₁ of 0.7273 (ViT-S, DA v1) and 0.8857 (W4A6), consistently outperforming all PTQ baselines by 10–40 percentage points in δ₁.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QSCA outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and configuration. Against the strongest prior PTQ method QDrop, QSCA gains +9.3 δ₁ points on NYUv2 (0.8097 vs. 0.7166, DA v1 ViT-S, W4A4) and +11.7 points on KITTI (0.7273 vs. 0.3234). The closest competitor is QwT [31], which requires labeled data for its compensation modules; QSCA still edges ahead by +0.9 δ₁ on NYUv2 (0.8097 vs. 0.8007) and +4.1 points on KITTI (0.7273 vs. 0.6862) under W4A4, while operating entirely without ground-truth labels. Reconstruction-based methods (BRECQ, QDrop) are also 15–15× slower to calibrate (~3100–5800 s vs. ~210 s for QSCA). Simpler PTQ baselines (MinMax, Percentile, PTQ4ViT, RepQ-ViT, ERQ) all fall 10–30 δ₁ points below QSCA under W4A4, with MinMax and BRECQ occasionally collapsing to near-random performance (δ₁ < 0.2) on ViT-B models.

## Relation to Prior Work

QSCA directly adapts the lightweight compensation module idea from QwT (Quantization without Tears), which used closed-form initialization to recover quantization-induced loss, but re-purposes it for a self-supervised setting where the full-precision model serves as its own teacher, eliminating the need for ground-truth depth labels. This departs from reconstruction-based PTQ methods (BRECQ, QDrop, AdaRound) that require task-specific loss functions and extensive per-block optimization, and from QAT which demands labeled data and full retraining. The SCA module's residual design (linear in transformer blocks, convolutional in DPT decoder blocks) is a targeted architectural choice for the ViT-encoder/DPT-decoder structure of Depth Anything, rather than a generic quantization fix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to successfully apply 4-bit quantization across all layers of large-scale monocular depth estimation foundation models, opening a path toward deploying Depth Anything-class models on resource-constrained edge hardware for robotics and autonomous driving. The self-supervised formulation is practically significant because foundation MDE models are typically trained on proprietary or unlabeled data, making labeled-data-dependent methods (QAT, supervised QwT) inapplicable. Key limitations include reliance on fake quantization (no hardware-verified inference), evaluation restricted to Depth Anything v1/v2 with ViT-S and ViT-B backbones, and the absence of error bars or multi-seed runs. Open questions include whether the SCA approach generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (segmentation, optical flow) and whether true integer arithmetic on target hardware preserves the gains observed under simulated quantization.
