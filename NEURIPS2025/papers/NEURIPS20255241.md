## Problem

Video LLMs face quadratic computational growth with long visual token sequences, and existing token compression methods apply uniform per-frame compression ratios, ignoring that frames vary in query relevance. Binary keyframe selection is suboptimal because it irrevocably discards useful cues in unselected frames while retaining redundancy in selected ones, and pre-encoding frame selection introduces additional computational overhead that undermines efficiency gains.

## Input / Output

The method takes uniformly sampled video frames (e.g., 32 or 64 frames, each encoded into 196 patch tokens by a vision encoder) and a text query. It produces a variable-length compressed visual token sequence where each frame retains a different number of tokens (determined by its estimated importance), which is then fed to the LLM backbone to generate a text answer. The per-frame token count is bounded by a configurable upper limit T_max to suppress temporal attention outliers, and the total retained tokens must equal a user-specified global budget T_total.

## Method

DyToK is a training-free, two-stage framework that dynamically allocates per-frame token budgets using the keyframe prior inherent in VLLM attention. First, a lightweight assistant model (e.g., 0.5B guiding a 7B model) computes cross-modal attention scores from the last query token to visual tokens, aggregated exclusively from the deepest one-third of layers, yielding calibrated per-frame importance weights. Second, the global token budget is distributed proportionally to these weights via a largest-remainder allocation scheme with a per-frame cap to mitigate temporal attention outliers. The actual within-frame token pruning is delegated to a modular Compression function, making DyToK plug-and-play compatible with encoder feature-based methods (VisionZip, DyCoke-encoder) and LLM attention-based methods (FastV, DyCoke-LLM) without requiring any retraining.

## Results

On LLaVA-OneVision with 32 frames (6272 total tokens), DyToK integrated with VisionZip† achieves 53.9 average score at 50% token retention (99.6% of vanilla), surpassing the unpruned baseline of 54.1 by 0.2 points at 25% retention (54.4, 100.6%). At extreme 90% compression (448 tokens), DyToK+VisionZip† reaches 48.8 average (90.2% of vanilla) versus VisionZip alone at 38.6 (71.3%), a 18.9-point gain. On Qwen2.5-VL with FastV at 25% retention, DyToK achieves 52.2 average (96.0% of vanilla) versus FastV's 45.5 (83.6%), a 12.4-point improvement. The method attains 4.3× faster inference while preserving or exceeding uncompressed accuracy across VideoMME, LongVideoBench, and MLVU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyToK consistently outperforms all three baselines across every compression ratio tested. Against VisionZip (encoder feature-based), DyToK yields +4.2% average accuracy at 50% retention and a dramatic +18.9% at 90% retention (48.8 vs. 38.6), showing that dynamic per-frame allocation is critical under aggressive pruning. Against FastV (LLM attention-based), DyToK improves by +1.8% at 50% retention and +6.1% at 85% retention (50.2 vs. 46.9), with the gap widening as compression becomes more severe. Against DyCoke (hybrid pruning), gains are more modest (+1.2% to +1.5% at moderate compression) since DyCoke already incorporates some dynamic behavior, but DyToK still provides consistent improvements. Notably, at 25% retention, DyToK+DyCoke (54.4) actually exceeds the unpruned vanilla model (54.1), demonstrating that intelligent token allocation can surpass full-token processing. On Qwen2.5-VL, DyToK+FastV at 25% retention (52.2) outperforms the vanilla model (54.4) by a smaller margin but still beats FastV alone by 12.4 points, confirming cross-model generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work on VLLM efficiency: LLM attention-based token pruning (FastV, PruneVid) and encoder feature-based token selection (VisionZip, DyCoke). It departs from both by introducing a dynamic, per-frame token budget allocation driven by a newly identified 'keyframe prior' embedded in VLLM cross-modal attention, rather than applying a uniform compression ratio across all frames. The use of a lightweight assistant model (0.5B) to extract deep-layer attention as a proxy for the full model's keyframe perception is a practical repurposing of the 'small model guides large model' idea from prior VLM acceleration work, but applied specifically to temporal importance estimation for video. The paper also identifies and characterizes a 'Temporal Attention Outlier' phenomenon (disproportionate attention to edge and mid-sequence frames) that connects to the broader attention-sink and register-token literature in transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and 4.3× inference speedup with minimal accuracy loss make DyToK immediately useful for practitioners deploying VLLMs on long videos, and the keyframe prior observation provides a new lens for understanding how VLLMs allocate attention across temporal context. The Temporal Attention Outlier finding is a useful diagnostic that explains why naive uniform compression fails and motivates the per-frame upper-limit mechanism. A key open question the authors acknowledge is whether the auxiliary assistant model can be eliminated entirely, as its presence, while lightweight, adds deployment complexity. The method's reliance on cross-modal attention from a specific architectural family (decoder-only LLMs with standard attention) also raises questions about generalizability to architectures with sliding-window or 3D-convolutional attention (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL), where the paper shows more modest gains.
