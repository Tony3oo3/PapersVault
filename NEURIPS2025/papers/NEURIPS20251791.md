## Problem

Training high-performing Small Language Models (SLMs) via knowledge distillation from larger teachers remains inefficient due to three core limitations: hard pruning permanently discards teacher knowledge, feature-based distillation requires costly alignment modules that become stale as student representations evolve, and information-rich FFN activations are largely overlooked. These issues collectively limit distillation efficiency, forcing SLMs to consume trillions of tokens to match the quality of larger models.

## Input / Output

LRC consumes a frozen pre-trained teacher model (e.g., Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B/7B-Instruct) and a mixed text corpus (~10–20B tokens from Fineweb-Edu, DCLM, CosmopediaV2, and OpenHermes). It produces a standalone student model whose weights are generated on-the-fly as W^S = W^T · W^p, where only the low-rank projection matrices W^p and RMSNorm parameters (<1% of total parameters) are trainable. After training, the teacher is discarded and the student performs standard forward passes without any modification, enabling immediate SFT and inference.

## Method

LRC unifies soft pruning and activation distillation in a single training stage through two coupled mechanisms. First, Low-Rank Projection applies trainable matrices W^p ∈ R^{d^T × d^S} to every teacher weight (q, k, v, o, up, gate, down, embedding, LM head), directly generating the student's parameters as a compressed linear image of the teacher's. Second, Activation Clone aligns the student's intermediate activations—specifically the q/k/v/up/gate linear projections and the attention and FFN module outputs—with the teacher's counterparts via MSE loss, with a key emphasis on FFN signals that prior methods ignore. A central design insight is the alignment-free property (Lemma 1): because student weights are linear projections of teacher weights, the same W^p matrices that compress weights can be reused to project teacher activations into student space, eliminating the need for separate alignment modules. The total objective combines KL divergence on logits, next-token prediction loss, and a weighted clone loss (α·L_clone), and the entire procedure requires training fewer than 1% of the student's parameters, achieving over 1000× token efficiency compared to standard SLM pre-training.

## Results

LRC-1.7B, distilled from Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct using only 20B tokens, achieves an average zero-shot score of 64.98 across 9 benchmarks, surpassing Qwen3-1.7B (63.17, trained on 36T tokens) by 1.81 points with over 1,000× fewer training tokens. LRC-1.5B (10B tokens, Llama-3.2-3B teacher) reaches 63.48, outperforming SmolLM2-1.7B (60.50, 11T tokens) and MiniCPM-1.2B (60.42, 1T tokens). At the 4B scale, LRC-4B (18B tokens, Qwen2.5-7B teacher) matches Qwen3-4B (70.30 vs. 70.29, 36T tokens) and outperforms Minitron-4B (68.31, 94B tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRC-1.7B outperforms all sub-2B baselines: it beats Qwen3-1.7B (64.98 vs. 63.17 avg) by 1.81 points despite using 1,800× fewer tokens, and surpasses InternLM2-1.8B (62.60) and SmolLM2-1.7B (60.50) by 2.4 and 4.5 points respectively. LRC-1.5B similarly dominates MiniCPM-1.2B (63.48 vs. 60.42) with 10× fewer training tokens. At the 4B scale, LRC-4B edges out Qwen3-4B (70.30 vs. 70.29) and Gemma3-4B (69.43) while using only 18B tokens versus 36T and 4T, and clearly surpasses Minitron-4B (68.31) by 2.0 points with 5× less data. In the controlled comparison against Sheared-Llama-2.7B-B (same Llama2-7B teacher, same Redpajama data), LRC-2.7B-B achieves a marginal win (52.67 vs. 52.55) while using 5× fewer tokens (10B vs. 50B). In the ablation against TinyBERT-style distillation, LRC reaches LM loss 3.0 approximately 2.7× faster, confirming the advantage of weight-level projection over training from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

LRC extends the knowledge distillation paradigm (Hinton et al., 2015) and the structured-pruning-plus-distillation pipeline of Minitron and Sheared Llama by replacing hard pruning with trainable low-rank projections that simultaneously compress teacher weights and serve as implicit alignment layers. It departs from feature-based distillation methods (TinyBERT, MiniLM) in two key ways: it eliminates separate alignment matrices via an alignment-free design (Lemma 1), and it elevates FFN activations to first-class distillation signals rather than focusing primarily on attention. The method is conceptually closest to SliceGPT's use of linear projections for weight compression, but replaces PCA's fixed linear assumption with learnable low-rank matrices and couples them with activation-level behavioral cloning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1,000× reduction in training tokens (20B vs. 36T for Qwen3-1.7B) is a substantial practical win for resource-constrained groups aiming to build competitive SLMs, and the alignment-free design simplifies the training pipeline. However, the performance ceiling under larger-scale training remains unexplored, and the current implementation retains the teacher's FFN intermediate dimension, limiting architectural compression. Open questions include whether LRC scales to 70B+ teachers, how it interacts with reducing d_mid, and whether the FFN-activation emphasis generalizes beyond the Llama/Qwen architecture family.
