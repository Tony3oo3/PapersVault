## Problem

Existing OT-based DRO methods rely on static uncertainty sets with hand-crafted reference distributions and fixed transport costs, leading to either overly conservative worst-case distributions that include unrealistic perturbations or overly restrictive sets that miss plausible shifts. Conventional transport costs (e.g., pixel-wise distance) fail to encode semantic similarity in domains like computer vision, and heuristic constructions of the reference distribution ν (mixup, noise injection) can corrupt the uncertainty set. No prior method dynamically co-adapts both the semantic transport costs and the uncertainty set geometry during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with class labels (training) and standard data-augmentation pairs (original + augmented, e.g., crop/flip) to define ground-truth semantic couplings for IOT calibration. Beyond the standard class prediction, the algorithm internally produces a worst-case distribution Q^λ (via semi-relaxed OT) and a dynamically filtered reference distribution ν̂ that shrinks or expands the uncertainty set each iteration. The transport costs C_θ^X and C_θ^Y are learned parameters rather than fixed metrics, and the filtering threshold τ(q) is computed per-target from the current coupling, making the output distribution shift-dependent.

## Method

AdaDRO recasts Sinkhorn DRO as a bilevel optimization where the lower level is a semi-relaxed OT that explores the worst-case distribution Q^λ, and the upper level is an inverse OT that aligns model-induced couplings with ground-truth semantic matchings. Two IOT objectives drive semantic calibration: a feature-level IOT (Eq. 12) learns kernel-based transport costs C_θ^X by matching augmented pairs, and a label-level IOT (Eq. 13) calibrates inter-class costs C_θ^Y via the reproducing property of the linear kernel on classifier weights. An OT-feedback filtering mechanism then refines the reference distribution ν by retaining only samples whose coupling confidence γ_θ^X(p,q) exceeds a dynamic threshold τ(q) that combines global average confidence with target-specific local matching capacity, yielding a data-driven, iteration-dependent uncertainty set. Convergence to an ε-stationary point in O(ε^{-4} log(1/ε)) iterations is guaranteed using MLMC-RT to handle the nested-expectation gradient of the evolving worst-case objective.

## Results

On Colored MNIST under high spurious correlation, AdaDRO achieves 87.2% average accuracy and 79.9% worst-group accuracy, outperforming the next-best method (IRM at 79.5%/75.3%) by 7.7 and 4.6 points respectively. On Waterbirds, AdaDRO reaches 87.1% average and 80.5% worst-group accuracy, matching GroupDRO on worst-group while improving average accuracy by 0.8 points. Under 50% label noise on CIFAR-100, AdaDRO achieves up to 57.2% accuracy (at λ=2.0), surpassing SDRO's best of 44.8% by over 12 points. On the strong spurious correlation setting of Colored MNIST, AdaDRO reaches 57.2% versus 45.1% for IRM and 44.8% for SDRO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaDRO consistently outperforms all six baselines across the evaluated distribution shift scenarios. Against the closest DRO competitor SDRO [62], AdaDRO improves worst-group accuracy by 7.3 points on Colored MNIST (79.9 vs 72.6) and by 5.1 points on Waterbirds (80.5 vs 75.4), while also achieving higher average accuracy in both settings. Compared to GroupDRO [50], AdaDRO wins on average accuracy (87.2 vs 78.4 on Colored MNIST; 87.1 vs 86.3 on Waterbirds) and worst-group accuracy on Colored MNIST (79.9 vs 73.0), though it ties GroupDRO on Waterbirds worst-group (both 80.5). Against IRM [2], AdaDRO gains 7.7 points in average accuracy and 4.6 points in worst-group accuracy on Colored MNIST. The divergence-based methods KL-DRO [46] and WDRO [9] trail AdaDRO by 10+ points in worst-group accuracy on Colored MNIST, and UniDRO [11] performs substantially worse (47.8% worst-group), suggesting its unified formulation struggles under strong spurious correlation. Under 50% label noise, AdaDRO's label-agnostic semantic calibration provides a 12+ point advantage over SDRO, demonstrating robustness to corrupted supervision that label-dependent baselines lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaDRO extends Sinkhorn DRO (Wang et al., 2023) by making both the transport cost and the uncertainty set dynamic during training, rather than fixed as in all prior DRO formulations. The key conceptual move is recasting the Sinkhorn DRO objective as a bilevel problem whose lower level is a semi-relaxed OT (distribution exploration) and whose upper level is an inverse OT (semantic alignment), drawing on the IOT framework of Stuart & Wolfram (2020) and the cross-entropy-as-IOT equivalence shown by Shi et al. (ICML 2023). This OT/IOT unification then naturally yields two practical components—semantic calibration via IOT and coupling-based filtering of the reference distribution—that have no direct analogue in static DRO methods like KL-DRO, WDRO, or GroupDRO. For gradient estimation of the resulting nested-expectation objective, the paper adapts MLMC-RT (Hu et al., NeurIPS 2021) to the setting of a dynamically evolving uncertainty set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core practical limitation of DRO—the difficulty of choosing an uncertainty set that is neither overly conservative nor too restrictive—by letting the set co-evolve with the model, and provides convergence guarantees despite this dynamism. A notable emergent property is robustness to label noise (Table 3), arising because the feature-level IOT calibration is unsupervised and the coupling-based filter can discard mislabeled samples. However, the method still requires domain priors to construct the initial reference distribution ν, and the empirical evaluation, while spanning several shift types, is limited to relatively small-scale benchmarks (MNIST, Waterbirds, CIFAR-LT); scalability to large-scale vision or language tasks remains an open question. The OT/IOT lens also opens a path for incorporating richer transport tools (unbalanced OT, learned metrics) into robust learning, but the current framework's dependence on data augmentation for semantic calibration may limit its applicability to modalities where augmentation is less natural.
