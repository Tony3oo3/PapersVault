## Problem

Generating 4D scenes (dynamic 3D content) from text prompts requires both producing synchronized multi-view videos and reconstructing explicit 3D representations. Existing 4D video diffusion architectures introduce extra parameters through separate cross-view/cross-time attention layers or synchronization modules, limiting capacity and generalization on scarce 4D training data. Meanwhile, reconstruction from multi-view videos typically relies on slow per-scene optimization, making it impractical for interactive or large-scale use.

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a text prompt plus optional reference videos: a fixed-view video (specifying scene content and motion from one viewpoint) and a freeze-time video (capturing the scene from multiple angles at one timestep). The 4D video model outputs a grid of synchronized frames organized by viewpoint (rows) and timestep (columns), operating on latent tokens compressed 8× spatially and 4× temporally. The feedforward reconstruction model then consumes only the RGB frames (no camera parameters) and produces per-timestep 3D Gaussian splats with predicted camera intrinsics and extrinsics, all in the coordinate frame of the first input view.

## Method

The 4D video model fuses cross-view and cross-time attention into a single masked self-attention layer where each token attends to all tokens sharing the same viewpoint or the same timestamp, implemented via FlexAttention for efficient sparse computation. This design introduces zero additional parameters beyond the pretrained 11B DiT video backbone, and 4D positional coordinates (v,t,x,y) are collapsed to 3D via (v·T_max+t, x, y) to preserve compatibility with the pretrained RoPE. For reconstruction, the model extends VGGT with zero-initialized temporal attention layers for cross-timestep information sharing, a camera token replacement step that enforces consistent camera predictions across time, and a DPT-based Gaussian head that predicts opacities, scales, rotations, and color/pose residuals on top of unprojected depth centroids. Training uses rectified flow with progressive temporal duration, fine-tuning only self-attention layers, and the reconstruction model is trained in two stages (static then dynamic) with the VGGT backbone frozen.

## Results

On the 4D video generation benchmark (generated video dataset), the full method achieves the best multi-view consistency (Met3R 0.173) and top VBench scores for Subject (97.7) and Background (98.4) consistency, outperforming all baselines. For feedforward reconstruction on static scenes (Tanks & Temples, 16 views), the method reaches PSNR 20.85 / LPIPS 0.1464 / SSIM 0.6057 without requiring input camera poses or manual scale tuning, surpassing BTimer (20.45 / 0.1633 / 0.5771) which uses both. On dynamic scenes (Neural3DVideo), it achieves PSNR 21.63 / LPIPS 0.1200, marginally beating BTimer multi-view (21.55 / 0.1213) under the same no-pose, no-tuning protocol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 4D video generation (Table 3), the proposed method achieves the lowest Met3R multi-view inconsistency score (0.173), beating the closest competitor 4Real-Video (0.192) and ReCamMaster-V2 (0.194), while TrajectoryCrafter (0.324) and SynCamMaster/ReCamMaster-V1 (0.530) are far worse. The only metric where a baseline wins is VBench Image quality, where TrajectoryCrafter scores 67.6 vs. 66.2 for the full method, though TrajectoryCrafter suffers from point-cloud artifacts and much lower multi-view consistency. For static NVS (Table 6), the method outperforms GSLRM, BTimer, and Splatt3r on both Tanks & Temples and LLFF across all three metrics, despite not receiving camera poses or manual scale tuning that the baselines require. On dynamic NVS (Table 7), it edges out BTimer multi-view (PSNR 21.63 vs. 21.55; LPIPS 0.1200 vs. 0.1213) while trailing by a negligible 0.004 on SSIM. In the system-level diagnosis (Table S6), the full two-stage pipeline (video gen + feedforward recon) achieves PSNR 19.14, matching the grid-completion + 4DGS baseline (19.05) while reducing reconstruction time from over an hour to under 3 seconds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends 4Real-Video [15], which used a parallel two-stream architecture with a lightweight synchronization module to combine cross-view and cross-time attention. The key departure is replacing both sequential interleaving (SynCamMaster, SV4D) and parallel-with-sync (4Real-Video) designs with a single fused self-attention layer that uses a sparse mask to let tokens attend across views or time simultaneously, requiring zero additional parameters beyond the pretrained 11B DiT video backbone. On the reconstruction side, the paper extends VGGT [23]—a static-scene feedforward 3D reconstruction model—into the dynamic regime by adding temporal attention layers, a camera token replacement mechanism for temporal consistency, and a DPT-based Gaussian head, building on the Gaussian splatting representation from 3DGS [22] and the pose-refinement strategy of Splatt3r [69].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first fully feedforward pipeline from text/video prompts to dynamic Gaussian splats, reducing 4D reconstruction from hours of per-scene optimization (4DGS) to under 3 seconds while matching or exceeding quality. The fused sparse attention design is a practical recipe for scaling multi-view video diffusion to modern 11B+ DiT backbones without parameter bloat, and the finding that 2D homographic-transformed videos are critical training data for dynamic scene generation is a useful augmentation strategy for the field. Key limitations include the absence of full 360° coverage, residual layering artifacts in Gaussian renderings, and ~4-minute inference for 8×29 frames on a single A100, leaving open questions about distillation for faster inference and whether the fused attention pattern generalizes to even larger view counts or longer temporal extents.
