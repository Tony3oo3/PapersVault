## Problem

Existing video anomaly analysis methods are fragmented: temporal detection outputs only frame scores without spatial or semantic context, while spatial localization and textual understanding methods are task-specific and typically require fine-tuning or prompt-tuning on annotated data. No prior approach unifies all three sub-tasks (when, where, what/why) under a single fully zero-shot, training-free framework, leaving a gap in holistic, interpretable anomaly analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250996/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes an untrimmed video as input, subsampled at a frame stride of 16, with 10-frame clips centered on each target frame fed to a frozen VLM (VideoLLaMA3-7B) for captioning. Outputs are structured as a cascaded chain: frame-wise anomaly scores in [0,1], a video-level surrogate probability, an extracted anomaly tag list (concise phrases), spatial bounding boxes with confidence scores for anomalous frames, and a final natural-language description of the anomaly. The tag list and scores from the VAD stage are reused as conditioning signals for the downstream VAL and VAU stages.

## Method

The core contribution is a chained test-time reasoning pipeline with two components. Intra-Task Reasoning (IntraTR) first produces frame-wise scores via VLM captioning plus LLM scoring, then slides a window to find the most suspicious segment and extracts anomaly tags from it; a score-gated mechanism triggers a second reasoning pass only when the surrogate video-level score falls within [0.5±m], injecting the extracted tags into the scoring prompt to refine low-confidence predictions without inducing over-thinking hallucinations. Inter-Task Chaining (InterTC) propagates the tag list and scores into downstream tasks: for spatial localization, tags are prepended to the localization prompt to guide bounding-box prediction; for textual understanding, tags enrich the description prompt and a score-gated overlay draws detected bounding boxes onto frames (only when the anomaly score exceeds 0.5) to provide visual grounding. The entire pipeline uses frozen VLMs and LLMs with dynamically refined prompts at each stage, requiring no gradients, no additional data, and no fine-tuning.

## Results

On UCF-Crime, the proposed framework achieves 84.28% AUC (fixed m) and 84.08% AUC (adaptive m), outperforming the best prior zero-shot method LAVAD (80.28%) by ~4%. On XD-Violence, it reaches 91.34% AUC and 68.07% AP, surpassing LAVAD (85.36% AUC, 62.01% AP) by ~6%. On UBNormal, the method achieves 68.98% AUC vs. LAVAD's 51.06%, a ~18% absolute gain. For VAL, the InterTC-refined Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves 25.17% TIoU, beating supervised STPrompt (23.90%) and VadCLIP (22.05%). For VAU, InterTC-refined VideoLLaMA3-7B reaches 0.345 BLEU and 0.721 GPT-D on UCF-Crime/XD-Violence, approaching or surpassing instruction-tuned Hawk and HolmesVAU on several metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest zero-shot baseline LAVAD [Zanella et al., 2024], the proposed framework wins by 4.0% AUC on UCF-Crime (84.28 vs. 80.28), 5.98% AUC on XD-Violence (91.34 vs. 85.36), and a dramatic 17.9% AUC on UBNormal (68.98 vs. 51.06), confirming that the IntraTR score-gated reasoning step is most beneficial on ambiguous/synthetic content. Compared to other zero-shot VLM baselines, the gap is even larger: CLIP (53.16), LLaVA-1.5 (72.84), and the unrefined VideoLLaMA3+Llama3.1 pipeline (78.7 on UBNormal) all trail by 6–31 points. Against supervised methods, the framework is competitive but not always superior: STPrompt (88.08) and Holmes-VAU (88.96) still lead on UCF-Crime AUC, while VERA (88.26) leads on XD-Violence AUC; however, the proposed method requires no training data, no fine-tuning, and no domain-specific prompts. On the VAL task, the InterTC-refined zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-7B (25.17% TIoU) outperforms both supervised VadCLIP (22.05%) and STPrompt (23.90%), and nearly matches the oracle-class-name upper bound (25.21%). On VAU, the InterTC-refined VideoLLaMA3-7B (0.345 BLEU, 0.721 GPT-D on UCF-Crime) surpasses zero-shot baselines VideoChat-Flash-2B (0.165 BLEU) and InternVideo2.5-8B (0.159 BLEU) by large margins, and approaches instruction-tuned Hawk (0.379 BLEU) and HolmesVAU (0.435 BLEU), even exceeding them on GPT-D (0.428 vs. 0.580/0.356) and GPT-C metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LAVAD (Zanella et al., 2024), which established a training-free VLM+LLM pipeline for temporal video anomaly detection, by adding two structured test-time reasoning layers: a score-gated intra-task refinement that extracts anomaly tags from the most suspicious window and uses them to re-score ambiguous samples, and an inter-task chaining mechanism that propagates those tags and spatial bounding boxes into downstream localization and textual understanding prompts. It departs from prompt-tuning methods (STPrompt, VERA, AnomalyRuler) and instruction-tuned MLLMs (Hawk, HolmesVAU) by requiring no annotated data or gradient updates whatsoever, and from prior zero-shot baselines (CLIP, LLaVA, LAVAD) by unifying temporal, spatial, and textual outputs in a single cascaded inference pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first strictly zero-shot, training-free framework that simultaneously handles temporal detection, spatial localization, and textual explanation for video anomalies, addressing a practical gap where prior methods were either task-specific or data-dependent. The score-gated reasoning design offers a principled way to control test-time compute and avoid the 'over-thinking' hallucinations documented in LLM literature, making the approach more robust than unrestricted chain-of-thought. Open questions include whether the chaining generalizes beyond anomaly detection to other multi-stage video understanding tasks, whether the marginal gain from the localization overlay (which the authors attribute to frozen VLMs not being trained on annotated frames) can be closed with better visual prompting, and how the framework scales to longer videos or higher-resolution inputs given the multi-pass VLM/LLM cost.
