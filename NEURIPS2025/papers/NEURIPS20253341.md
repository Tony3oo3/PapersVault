## Problem

Existing text-to-motion datasets provide only brief, general descriptions (e.g., 12 words in HumanML3D) that cannot capture the complexity of multi-second motions, limiting fine-grained controllability and generalization to unseen prompts. Additionally, prior generative masked modeling approaches like MoMask apply residual quantization at a single full temporal scale, producing tokens with highly uneven information content and necessitating separate models for different token layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253341/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a textual description (averaging 48 words in SnapMoGen) or a casual user prompt rewritten by an LLM into the dataset's expressive narration style, processed through T5-base for word-level features. It outputs a 3D human pose sequence of 4–12 seconds at 30 fps with 296-dimensional per-frame features (root velocity, height, 6D joint rotations, positions, and velocities across 24 joints), directly convertible to standard BVH mocap formats. Generation proceeds iteratively over 10–18 steps from a fully masked token sequence, with classifier-free guidance applied at inference.

## Method

MoMask++ introduces two key changes over MoMask. First, a multi-scale residual VQ assigns each quantization layer a progressively coarser temporal resolution (n/2^V through n/2^0) while sharing a single codebook across all layers; quantized features at coarse scales are bilinearly upsampled to full resolution before residual computation, so each layer captures meaningful semantics at its own temporal granularity rather than diminishing marginal detail. Second, all tokens from all scales are concatenated along the temporal axis and generated by a single bidirectional masked transformer (8 layers, 13.5M–18.3M params) trained with a cosine-scheduled variable masking rate, a replacing-and-remasking strategy, and 10% text dropout for classifier-free guidance. Two text-conditioning variants are explored—concatenating text and motion embeddings (in-context) or injecting text via cross-attention—with the cross-attention variant showing better robustness on long prompts. This design achieves state-of-the-art results on both HumanML3D and SnapMoGen using only a quarter of MoMask's token count.

## Results

On the new SnapMoGen benchmark, MoMask++ (cross-attention variant) achieves the best FID of 15.06, CLIP Score of 0.685, and R-Precision Top-1 of 0.802, outperforming the prior SOTA MoMask (FID 17.40, CLIP 0.664, R-Precision Top-1 0.777) while using only a quarter of the token count (2 VQ layers vs. 6). On HumanML3D, MoMask++ (in-context) attains R-Precision Top-1 of 0.528 and FID of 0.072, surpassing MoMask (0.521, 0.045) on alignment metrics while MoMask retains a slight FID edge. The gap over diffusion-based baselines is particularly large on SnapMoGen: MDM scores FID 57.78 and CLIP 0.481, while MoMask++ achieves FID 15.06 and CLIP 0.685.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoMask++ consistently outperforms all baselines on SnapMoGen across every metric. Against the closest competitor MoMask (the prior SOTA), MoMask++ improves FID from 17.40 to 15.06 (cra) and CLIP Score from 0.664 to 0.685, while using only 2 VQ layers versus MoMask's 6 (a quarter of the tokens). Diffusion-based methods (MDM, StableMoFusion, MARDM) are substantially weaker on the expressive-text benchmark: MDM trails by over 42 FID points and 0.20 CLIP score, while StableMoFusion and MARDM lag by roughly 12 FID points. The autoregressive T2M-GPT also underperforms significantly (FID 32.63, CLIP 0.573), confirming that discrete-token masked generation better handles long, detailed prompts. On HumanML3D, MoMask++ (in-context) edges out MoMask on R-Precision (0.528 vs. 0.521 Top-1) and MM Distance (2.912 vs. 2.958), though MoMask retains a small FID advantage (0.045 vs. 0.072); the cross-attention variant achieves the best MModality (1.192). Overall, the in-context variant excels on HumanML3D while the cross-attention variant is more robust on the longer-text SnapMoGen prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MoMask (CVPR 2024), the prior state-of-the-art generative masked model for 3D human motion, by replacing its full-scale residual VQ with a multi-scale variant that assigns each quantization layer a distinct temporal resolution and shares a single codebook across all layers. This design eliminates MoMask's need for a two-model hierarchy (primary + secondary transformer) in favor of a single masked transformer operating on concatenated multi-scale tokens. On the data side, SnapMoGen departs from prior motion-text datasets (HumanML3D, BABEL, Motion-X) by providing substantially longer, more expressive annotations (48 vs. 12 words average) and preserving temporal continuity across clips, drawing on the broader observation from text-to-image/video literature that descriptive prompts improve generation quality and controllability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a genuine bottleneck in text-to-motion research: existing annotations are too brief to capture fine-grained motion details, limiting both controllability and generalization to unseen prompts. The multi-scale VQ finding (45% fewer tokens with better reconstruction) and the LLM-based prompt rewriting for casual user inputs offer practical tools for deployment. However, open questions remain: the model still struggles with rare motion patterns, does not enforce physical plausibility (e.g., foot contacts), and the inertial mocap source introduces residual jitter. The temporally continuous segments open the door to long-horizon motion generation and blending research that prior fragmented datasets could not support.
