## Problem

Existing scene text retrieval methods rely on costly bounding box annotations for training and adopt task-specific retrieval strategies that cannot unify diverse query types (word, phrase, combined, semantic) within a single model. Additionally, box-free vision-language models tend to overlook detailed, insalient text instances in natural scenes, leading to high miss rates for small text. The paper addresses both the annotation cost and the multi-query unification gap simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB scene text image (processed at progressive resolutions from 512×512 up to 800×800 during training) and a text query of one of four styles—single word, continuous phrase, discontinuous combined keywords, or free-form semantic description. It produces a ranked list of images scored by cosine similarity between multi-grained vision embeddings (T+1 iterations × 64 query tokens) and text embeddings, with an optional re-ranking stage that feeds the top 2% of candidates back through the multi-modal encoder for one-to-one matching scores.

## Method

MSTAR is built on SigLIP's ViT-Base-512 vision encoder and BLIP-2's multi-modal encoder, and introduces three key modules. The Progressive Vision Embedding (PVE) iteratively applies a Salient Attention Shift (SAS) step that binarizes the cross-attention map of the multi-modal encoder (via thresholding + watershed + connected components), inverts it into a mask, and uses it in a masked self-attention layer to suppress previously attended salient features, thereby forcing the model to capture insalient text regions across T iterations. Style-aware text instructions are prepended to each query before encoding, steering the multi-modal encoder to place different query types in distinct representation subspaces. The Multi-Instance Matching (MIM) module then aligns the resulting multi-grained vision and text embeddings via Hungarian assignment for single-word queries and a lightweight cross-attention aggregation for multi-word queries, trained jointly with a dual contrastive loss and a cross-entropy image-text matching loss—no positional or box supervision is required at any stage.

## Results

On the newly introduced MQTR benchmark (16k images, 4 query types), MSTAR achieves 66.78% average MAP, surpassing the best box-based method TDSL (58.25%) by 8.53% and the best prior box-free method BLIP-2(FT) (58.11%) by 8.67%. On six public word-retrieval datasets, MSTAR reaches 82.56% average MAP (84.18% with re-ranking), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art box-based method TDSL (74.16%) by 8.40% and FDP (79.18% on Total-Text) by 6.37%, all without requiring any bounding-box annotations. MSTAR also runs at 14.2 FPS, more than twice the speed of TG-Bridge (6.7 FPS) due to the absence of a text detection module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSTAR outperforms all 15 baselines on the MQTR benchmark. Against box-based text spotters, MSTAR beats TG-Bridge [11] by 3.38% in word retrieval and Deepsolo [49] by 5.73%, while also surpassing them on phrase and semantic queries where box-based methods struggle due to their inability to leverage linguistic semantics. Among box-based retrieval methods, MSTAR exceeds TDSL [38] by 8.53% in average MAP on MQTR and by 8.40% on the six public word-retrieval datasets, though it underperforms TDSL on the CTR dataset (60.13% vs. 66.45%) due to the absence of precise box supervision for extremely small text. Against box-free VLMs, MSTAR outperforms fine-tuned BLIP-2 [19] by 8.67% in average MAP on MQTR and SigLIP [52] by 11.34% on phrase retrieval, demonstrating that the Progressive Vision Embedding and Multi-Instance Matching modules effectively address the salient-region bias of pre-trained VLMs. On the PSTR phrase-retrieval dataset, MSTAR achieves 95.71% MAP, surpassing FDP [51] (92.28%) and TDSL (89.56%). In inference speed, MSTAR (14.2 FPS) is more than twice as fast as TG-Bridge (6.7 FPS) and comparable to TDSL (12.0 FPS), while the re-ranking variant (6.9 FPS) further boosts MAP by 1.56% on word retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSTAR departs from the dominant scene text retrieval paradigm that relies on explicit text localization (detection-based methods like TDSL, FDP, or text spotters like ABCNet and MaskTextSpotter) by leveraging pre-trained VLMs (BLIP-2 as the multi-modal encoder, SigLIP as the vision encoder) in a fully box-free framework. The key architectural departure is the Progressive Vision Embedding module, which iteratively shifts attention from salient to insalient regions using masks derived from the model's own cross-attention maps, addressing the well-known tendency of VLMs to overlook fine-grained text. The Multi-Instance Matching module with Hungarian assignment provides explicit one-to-one vision-language alignment, contrasting with the implicit matching in prior contrastive approaches. The MQTR benchmark extends the evaluation landscape beyond single-type (word or phrase) retrieval to a unified four-query-type setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating bounding box annotations entirely, MSTAR significantly reduces the annotation cost that has been a major bottleneck for scene text retrieval research, while still matching or exceeding box-based SOTA on most benchmarks. The MQTR benchmark is the first to evaluate multi-query retrieval (word, phrase, combined, semantic) in a unified setting, filling a clear gap for real-world applications like document indexing and search disambiguation. The acknowledged limitation—underperforming box-based methods on extremely small and dense text—highlights an open question about whether box-free approaches can fully close the fine-grained perception gap without any positional supervision. The iterative attention-shifting mechanism (PVE/SAS) may have broader applicability to other tasks where VLMs need to attend to subtle, non-salient visual details.
