## Problem

Vision Transformers face quadratic computational and memory costs when processing high-resolution medical images (up to 64K² pixels), because capturing spatial hierarchy requires small patches that explode sequence length. Existing remedies—sub-quadratic attention schemes or multi-resolution hierarchical transformer stacks—either alter the attention mechanism or add complex interacting models, limiting retrofitting to pretrained encoders. The paper asks whether a data-driven patching strategy can encode spatial hierarchy into the input embedding without any architectural modification, and whether the resulting structure can also eliminate the convolution decoder entirely.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D pathology whole-slide images (512² to 65,536² pixels) or 3D CT/MRI volumes (512³) as input. Before entering the transformer, each image is passed through a randomly parameterized Gaussian blur and Canny edge detector, then adaptively partitioned into a fixed-length sequence of variable-size patches (sequence lengths of 1,024–16,384) via a quadtree or octree. The output is a full-resolution segmentation mask (or class label for the classification variant) reconstructed by linearly upscaling the encoder's patch-level predictions back to pixel space using the same tree geometry—no learned decoder is involved.

## Method

SHF builds a recursive quadtree (2D) or octree (3D) over the Canny edge image, always splitting the child region with the highest summed edge value until a target sequence length N is reached; this yields small patches in detail-rich areas and large patches in smooth regions, increasing per-token information density. The resulting patches are ordered along a Z-order space-filling curve and fed into a standard pretrained ViT encoder (SAM or SAM 2) whose convolution decoder has been removed. Training minimizes a combined Dice + BCE loss directly on the encoded (patched) mask sequence, avoiding the memory cost of materializing full-resolution masks during backpropagation. At inference, the identical tree structure is reused to 'depatch' the encoder output—each predicted patch is linearly upscaled to its spatial location—exploiting the observation that mask information is far sparser than image content, so tree-structured reconstruction reaches up to 99.5% recovery accuracy without any learned upsampling. Because SHF operates purely as input pre-processing and output post-processing, it can be retrofitted onto any ViT without modifying the self-attention or encoder weights.

## Results

On the PAIP high-resolution pathology dataset (512² to 64K² pixels), SHF+SAM achieves a 2.95%–7.03% Dice score improvement over SAM at the same computational budget, with speedups ranging from 3.86× to 32.3×. On 3D medical datasets, SHF-SAM2 achieves 89.71% Dice on BTCV (vs. 82.77% for SAM2) with a 7.15× training speedup, and 87.25% Dice on KiTS19 (vs. 81.35% for SAM2) with a 2.58× speedup. For classification on PAIP at 16K² resolution, SHF-ViT with 2×2 patches reaches 80.14% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing HIPT (72.69%) by over 8% while using 16× fewer GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHF+SAM outperforms all baselines on the PAIP segmentation task across every resolution tested. At 64K², SHF+SAM achieves 82.96% Dice, beating SAM (61.68%) by 21.28 points, UNETR (75.31%) by 7.65 points, TransUNet (67.67%) by 15.29 points, and UNet (59.69%) by 23.27 points, while using 4–8× fewer GPUs. On 3D BTCV, SHF-SAM2 (89.71% Dice) surpasses SAM2 (82.77%), Swin UNETR (89.5%), UNETR (89.1%), U-Mamba (83.51%), TransUNet (83.8%), and U-Net (80.2%), with a 7.15× training speedup over SAM2. On KiTS19, SHF-SAM2 (87.25%) outperforms SAM2 (81.35%), Swin UNETR-V2 (84.14%), UNETR (86.45%), U-Mamba (86.22%), and nnFormer (75.85%). For classification on PAIP at 16K², SHF-ViT-2 (80.14% Top-1) exceeds HIPT (72.69%) by 7.45 points and vanilla ViT (68.97%) by 11.17 points, using only 8 GPUs versus 128 for both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two dominant strategies for handling long sequences in ViTs: model-hierarchical approaches (HIPT, CrossViT) that train multiple transformers at different resolutions, and attention-hierarchical approaches (Swin, MViT) that modify the self-attention mechanism with shifted windows or multi-scale token fusion. SHF departs from both by operating entirely as a pre/post-processing layer on an intact ViT encoder: it uses Canny edge detection and quadtree/octree partitioning to create adaptive input patches, then applies the same tree structure for depatching at inference, thereby eliminating the convolution decoder used in SAM/SAM 2. The method also builds on the authors' own prior adaptive patching work [43], which they argue suffers from hyperparameter sensitivity, reframing the problem as structured hierarchical feature extraction rather than parameter tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for high-resolution medical imaging: a 32× training speedup at 64K² resolution with a 75% reduction in GPU count, and the complete elimination of convolution decoders that otherwise require >20GB of memory for mask activations. The model-agnostic design (retrofittable to any ViT without architectural changes) makes it broadly applicable, and the extension to 3D (octrees for BTCV/KiTS) demonstrates generality. However, the approach relies on edge-detection quality for spatial hierarchy extraction, the depatching step assumes mask sparsity (validated at 99.5% recovery but not stress-tested on dense masks), and all experiments are confined to medical imaging, leaving open whether the method transfers to natural-image or multimodal settings. The authors note future work on materials discovery, neuron detection, and multimodal problems.
