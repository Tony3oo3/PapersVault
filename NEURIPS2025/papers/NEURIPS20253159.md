## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods for large vision-language models retain the same number of KV vectors in every layer, ignoring the markedly heterogeneous importance distributions across layers. This uniform strategy causes significant contextual information loss in layers with dispersed importance and wastes budget in layers with concentrated importance, leading to notable generation quality degradation under aggressive compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253159/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard LVLM inference: an RGB image (encoded into visual patches) and a text instruction are fed into models like LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B or Qwen-VL. The user specifies a compression ratio budget (e.g., 20%–90% of the full KV cache), and the method produces autoregressive text output while maintaining the KV cache at that budget. A key practical detail is that the optimal layer-wise cache size ratios are estimated offline from as few as 1–10 random training samples, so no per-inference search overhead is incurred at deployment time.

## Method

PrefixKV reframes layer-wise KV cache sizing as finding an optimal 'global prefix configuration': for each layer, the retained KV vectors are the top-ranked ones by normalized attention-score importance (the 'prefix' of the priority sequence), and the per-layer prefix length is the decision variable. The paper characterizes each layer's importance distribution via its Lorenz curve (cumulative priority) and Gini coefficient, revealing that layers differ substantially in concentration. A binary search over a scalar information-retention threshold p is then used to find the unique value where the sum of per-layer prefix lengths exactly meets the compression budget, guaranteeing that every layer retains the maximal amount of contextual information under that budget. Because the cumulative priority curves are stable across samples, the resulting configuration can be precomputed offline with a handful of random inputs and reused at inference, eliminating online search cost. During decoding, the method follows Elastic Cache's fixed-distance pruning to maintain the derived layer-wise ratios as new tokens are generated.

## Results

On MM-Vet with LLaVA-1.5-7B, PrefixKV achieves a PPL of 5.97 at 20% KV cache budget, compared to 10.4 (PyramidKV), 21.0 (Elastic), 120 (H2O), and 90.0 (StreamingLLM), while the uncompressed model scores 5.28. At 50% budget, PrefixKV reaches PPL 5.50 vs. 5.63 (PyramidKV), 9.06 (Elastic), 18.6 (H2O), and 66.0 (StreamingLLM). On LLaVA-Description, the advantage is even more pronounced: at 20% budget, PrefixKV achieves PPL 3.69 vs. 48.3 (PyramidKV), 14.0 (Elastic), 105 (H2O), and 105 (Local). The method also delivers 1.8× inference speedup at 20% compression for LLaVA-1.5-7B while maintaining near-uncompressed generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrefixKV consistently outperforms all five baselines across every compression budget and both model scales. Against StreamingLLM (Local) and H2O, the gap is dramatic at aggressive compression: at 20% budget on MM-Vet-7B, PrefixKV achieves PPL 5.97 versus 90.0 and 120 respectively, a gap of over 15×. PyramidKV, which uses a manually designed pyramidal allocation (larger cache in shallow layers), is the closest structural competitor but still trails PrefixKV by 4.4 PPL at 20% budget (10.4 vs. 5.97) and by 0.13 at 50% (5.63 vs. 5.50). Elastic Cache, the primary baseline the paper builds upon for its two-stage framework, is outperformed by 15.0 PPL at 20% (21.0 vs. 5.97) and 3.56 at 50% (9.06 vs. 5.50). KIVI (KV quantization) is compared only on LongBench for LLaMA-3-8B, where PrefixKV wins at extreme 12.5% budget (40.2 vs. 21.8) but KIVI edges ahead at larger budgets (41.6 vs. 41.5 at 50%), confirming the two are complementary along different compression dimensions (length vs. precision).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV cache compression line of work initiated by H2O (attention-score-based eviction), StreamingLLM (position-based retention), and Elastic Cache (anchor-merging), all of which apply a uniform cache size across transformer layers. The key departure is the observation, formalized via Lorenz curves and Gini coefficients, that importance distributions are markedly heterogeneous across layers, and the reframing of per-layer cache allocation as a global prefix configuration problem solved by binary search. Unlike PyramidKV, which uses a manually designed shallow-to-deep gradient, PrefixKV derives the optimal per-layer ratios data-drivenly, and unlike KIVI (quantization), it operates on the length dimension of the cache.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method delivers a practical 1.8× inference speedup at 20% compression with minimal quality loss, and the offline estimation (a single random sample suffices) eliminates any runtime search overhead, making it drop-in deployable. The W-shaped cache-size pattern across layers offers architectural insights for future efficient LVLM design. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B/13B models, unexplored interaction with distillation or structural pruning, and the fact that at very aggressive budgets (≤12.5%) quantization-based methods like KIVI still outperform, suggesting the two compression dimensions are complementary rather than substitutable.
