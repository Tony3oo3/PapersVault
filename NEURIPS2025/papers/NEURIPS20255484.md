## Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models incur substantial per-timestep computational cost because they recompute all visual token representations from scratch at every control step, even though large portions of the scene (background, stationary objects) remain static across consecutive frames. Existing acceleration techniques (quantization, pruning, early-exit) require retraining or architectural changes and do not exploit the temporal redundancy that is intrinsic to closed-loop robotic manipulation, leaving a gap for a training-free method that reduces redundant computation without sacrificing action accuracy.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the method receives the current RGB frame (patchified into N×N patches of size p×p, e.g., 256 tokens for a 224×224 image) together with the previous frame's patch set and a natural-language instruction. It outputs the same discrete robotic action tokens as the base VLA model (e.g., 7 tokens for OpenVLA), but with a reduced effective token count in the language decoder. The key operational detail is that the method operates on cached key-value representations: static, task-irrelevant tokens inherit their K and V vectors from the prior frame, while dynamic or task-relevant tokens are freshly projected through the layer's W_K and W_V matrices.

## Method

VLA-Cache is a training-free, plug-and-play inference accelerator built on three mechanisms. First, static token selection computes cosine similarity between corresponding patches in consecutive frames and retains the top-k most stable tokens above a threshold τ. Second, a task-relevance filter extracts text-to-vision cross-attention scores from the decoder, averages them across heads and selected layers, and evicts tokens whose attention exceeds τ_task from the reuse set—preventing semantically critical but visually static regions (gripper, target object) from being cached. Third, a layer-adaptive strategy computes per-layer attention entropy, defines an entropy ratio R^l capturing how much more concentrated attention becomes relative to the previous layer, and accumulates these ratios to yield a per-layer reuse ratio α^l = min(k·ΣR^j, 1), so deeper layers with sharper attention reuse a larger fraction of tokens. For tokens in the final reuse set, their KV entries are directly copied from the previous frame's cache; all other tokens are recomputed. This reduces effective token count per decoder layer from L to L_r = α·|P_final|, yielding up to 1.7× CUDA latency reduction with negligible success-rate loss across OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and CogAct.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark with OpenVLA, VLA-Cache reduces CUDA latency from 51.91 ms to 31.83 ms (1.63× speedup) and FLOPs from 1.864T to 1.355T (27.3% reduction), while maintaining a 74.7% average success rate (only 0.3% below the 75.0% baseline). When applied to the faster OpenVLA-OFT architecture, VLA-Cache further boosts control frequency from 65.10 Hz to 78.98 Hz (+14 Hz) and reduces FLOPs by 22.8% (4.013T → 3.097T) with a slight success rate improvement (96.8% → 97.4%). On the SIMPLER environment with CogAct, VLA-Cache achieves a 1.37× latency reduction (54.29 → 39.63 ms) with comparable success rates (74.4% vs. 74.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLA-Cache decisively outperforms both SparseVLM and FastV, the two intra-frame token-pruning baselines. SparseVLM degrades average success rate by 10.3 points (75.0% → 64.7%) while paradoxically increasing latency from 51.91 ms to 83.39 ms, likely due to token merging overhead; VLA-Cache achieves 74.7% SR at 31.83 ms. FastV preserves accuracy (73.3% vs. 75.0%) but provides negligible speedup (53.28 ms vs. 51.91 ms) because it targets long output sequences, whereas VLA models generate only ~7 action tokens. Against the OpenVLA-OFT baseline (which already uses action chunking for high-frequency control), VLA-Cache delivers additive gains: +14 Hz control frequency and 22.8% FLOPs reduction with a 0.6-point SR improvement, confirming compatibility with optimized architectures. On SIMPLER with CogAct (a diffusion-policy head), VLA-Cache maintains near-identical success rates (74.4% vs. 74.8% in Visual Matching) while cutting latency by 27% (54.29 → 39.63 ms), demonstrating generality across different action heads.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLA-Cache extends the standard KV caching mechanism from the Transformer architecture [37] from within-frame autoregressive decoding to cross-frame visual token reuse, a setting unique to closed-loop robotic control. It departs from single-frame VLM acceleration techniques (FastV, SparseVLM, ToMe, PuMer) that prune or merge tokens within one image, and from VLA-specific efficiency methods (RoboMamba, TinyVLA, QAIL, DeeR-VLA) that require architectural changes or retraining. The key conceptual contribution is exploiting temporal redundancy across consecutive frames in VLA inference, combined with attention-based task-relevance filtering and layer-adaptive reuse ratios, to achieve a training-free, plug-and-play speedup without modifying the model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLA-Cache offers a practical, drop-in acceleration for the language decoder bottleneck in VLA systems, achieving ~1.7× speedup with negligible accuracy loss across three models and two simulators plus a real robot. However, its applicability is currently limited to LLaMA2-based decoders, and acceleration gains diminish in highly dynamic environments where fewer tokens remain static. Open questions include generalization to emerging VLA backbones (e.g., Gemma2 in π0), more complex multi-camera or 3D perception settings, and whether the attention-based task-relevance proxy remains reliable as VLA models scale or adopt fundamentally different action heads.
