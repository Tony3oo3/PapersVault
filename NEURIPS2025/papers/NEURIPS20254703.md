## Problem

Current large vision-language models perform 3D spatial reasoning implicitly through natural language descriptions (e.g., 'facing towards the viewer and slightly to its right'), lacking explicit 3D representations and reliable 3D computation. This leads to failures on questions trivial to humans, poor generalization to novel question types, and limited interpretability of the reasoning process. The paper asks whether integrating explicit 3D representations as a shared interface across perception, computation, and reasoning stages can yield both more accurate and more generalizable 3D spatial reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254703/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB image and a text spatial reasoning question, and produces a structured chain-of-thought trace that explicitly predicts 3D object locations and orientations as vectors in a calibrated camera 3D space (z-axis aligned with world vertical, origin near the ground plane), computes intermediate 3D metrics (distances, angles), and derives the final multiple-choice answer. The calibrated coordinate frame is a key design choice: it makes z-coordinates directly correspond to object heights and reduces many 3D spatial relationships (e.g., 'above', 'below') to simpler 2D comparisons on a ground-parallel plane.

## Method

SpatialReasoner is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and introduces explicit 3D representations (object locations and orientations as 3D vectors) as a unified interface shared across three reasoning stages: 3D perception (predicting object poses), 3D computation (deriving distances and angles from those poses), and 3D reasoning (aggregating computed values to answer spatial questions). Training follows a two-stage post-training pipeline: Stage I applies supervised fine-tuning on 24K synthetic SR-CoT data (generated via a 3D pseudo-annotation pipeline over OpenImages with aggressive filtering and optional human verification) to ground the model in explicit 3D representations; Stage II applies reinforcement learning via GRPO on 1.2K high-quality SR-QA examples with a composite reward (accuracy, format, and 3D-aware process rewards) to develop generalizable 3D thinking. The RL stage is shown to substantially improve zero-shot generalization to novel multi-object reasoning questions compared to SFT alone, while SFT provides the necessary structured reasoning traces that RL alone cannot produce. Ablations confirm that explicit 3D representations contribute a wide margin over implicit reasoning, and that removing KL divergence from GRPO improves training stability and final accuracy.

## Results

On 3DSRBench, SpatialReasoner achieves a new state-of-the-art mean accuracy of 60.3%, outperforming the best proprietary model Gemini 2.0 Flash (49.8%) by 10.5 points and the best open-source model Qwen2.5-VL-72B (54.9%) by 5.4 points. Gains are most pronounced on Orientation (+14.6% over Gemini 2.0 Flash) and Multi-Object reasoning (+8.2% over the second-best model). On CVBench-3D, SpatialReasoner reaches 87.3% on depth questions (vs. 82.5% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and 61.8% mean on GQA (vs. 58.8%), though it trades 9.9 points on CVBench-3D distance questions due to its refusal to exploit 2D bounding-box shortcuts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialReasoner (60.3% mean) outperforms all 11 baselines on 3DSRBench. Against proprietary models, it beats Gemini 2.0 Flash (49.8%) by 10.5 points, Gemini 2.0 Flash thinking (51.1%) by 9.2 points, Claude 3.5 Sonnet (48.2%) by 12.1 points, and GPT-4o (44.2%) by 16.1 points. Among open-source generalists, it surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-72B (54.9%) by 5.4 points, LLaVA-Next-8B (48.4%) by 11.9 points, and Cambrian-1-8B (42.2%) by 18.1 points. Among 3D-specialist models, it leads SpatialRGPT w/ depth (48.4%) by 11.9 points, SpatialLLM (44.8%) by 15.5 points, SpatialBot (41.0%) by 19.3 points, and SpaceLLaVA (42.0%) by 18.3 points. The largest single-category gains are on Orientation (55.2% vs. 43.1% for Qwen2.5-VL-72B) and Multi-Object reasoning (51.8% vs. 46.6%), indicating that explicit 3D representations and RL-based training most strongly benefit complex multi-step spatial computation. On CVBench-3D distance questions, SpatialReasoner (73.3%) underperforms Qwen2.5-VL-7B (83.2%) because the latter exploits 2D bounding-box shortcuts, but SpatialReasoner recovers a 21.5-point advantage on the analogous 3DSRBench multi-object-closer-to task (70.9% vs. 49.4%), confirming its robustness to spurious 2D correlations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D pseudo-annotation data generation pipelines from SpatialVLM, SpatialLLM, and SpatialRGPT by introducing explicit 3D representations (object locations and orientations in a calibrated camera 3D space) as a structured interface shared across perception, computation, and reasoning stages, rather than relying on natural-language descriptions of spatial relationships. The two-stage SFT-then-RL training strategy draws on the DeepSeek-R1 recipe and the SFT-memorizes/RL-generalizes finding from Chu et al., applied here to 3D spatial reasoning with GRPO and a composite reward scheme. The key conceptual departure from prior 3D-aware VLMs is the insistence on numeric 3D vectors as intermediate outputs, which enables compositional reasoning and exposes where in the pipeline errors originate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the primary bottleneck in 3D spatial VQA is 3D perception (predicting object locations/orientations from pixels), not 3D computation (deriving distances and angles from known 3D coordinates), redirecting future effort toward better visual front-ends. The discovery that a trivial 2D bounding-box L2-distance heuristic achieves 80.2% on CVBench-3D distance questions exposes a significant spurious-correlation problem in existing benchmarks, with implications for how the field evaluates spatial reasoning. Open questions include whether explicit 3D representations can be learned end-to-end without pseudo-annotation pipelines, whether the SFT+RL recipe transfers to video or multi-view 3D reasoning, and how to make the model selectively apply explicit 3D computation only when needed rather than always.
