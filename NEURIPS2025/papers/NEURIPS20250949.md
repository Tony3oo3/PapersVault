## Problem

Conventional adaptive density control (ADC) in 3D Gaussian Splatting relies solely on positional gradient magnitudes to decide when to split primitives and places sub-primitives randomly, leading to redundant splits in homogeneous regions and misaligned sub-primitives that fail to capture local structural boundaries. The directional information embedded in positional gradients—indicating whether a region is structurally coherent or heterogeneous—remains entirely unexploited by existing methods.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-in module within existing 3DGS pipelines (3DGS, AbsGS, Pixel-GS, Scaffold-GS, GES, LPM), consuming the same sparse SfM point cloud and multi-view RGB training images as the base method. It produces a 3D Gaussian model with up to 30% fewer primitives than the baseline while maintaining or improving reconstruction quality, and the reduced primitive count directly translates to faster per-frame rendering at inference time. The only additional computation (DC evaluation and split cost estimation) is confined to the training phase and is discarded after densification halts at 15K iterations.

## Method

DC4GS introduces Directional Consistency (DC), computed as the L2 norm of the circular mean of unit positional gradient vectors across all pixels influenced by a Gaussian, quantifying angular coherence (κ≈1 means aligned/homogeneous, κ≈0 means dispersed/heterogeneous). Two components leverage this cue: (1) the DC-weighted split Criterion (DCC) multiplies the accumulated gradient magnitude by (1−κ), suppressing splits in directionally coherent regions that a single Gaussian already represents well; (2) the DC-guided Split (DCS) replaces random sub-primitive placement by sampling N=5 candidate positions along the Gaussian's principal axis, computing a DC-based cost J(x)=(1−κ_l)·‖ĝ_l‖+(1−κ_r)·‖ĝ_r‖ for each candidate, and using polynomial regression over these discrete samples to locate the split that maximizes the DC of both resulting sub-primitives, thereby aligning the split with the true structural boundary.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, AbsGS+DC4GS achieves the best PSNR of 27.625 dB (vs. 27.504 for AbsGS alone) with 2,615K primitives (17% fewer than AbsGS's 3,149K) and 618 MB memory. On Tanks&Temples, AbsGS+DC4GS reaches 24.121 dB PSNR (vs. 23.636 for AbsGS) with only 1,093K primitives (18% reduction). On Deep Blending, AbsGS+DC4GS attains 29.654 dB PSNR with 1,499K primitives (24% fewer than AbsGS). Across all baselines, DC4GS consistently reduces primitive counts by 11–30% while maintaining or improving PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, and the gains over simply extending AbsGS training to 60K iterations confirm that improvements stem from structural-aware splitting rather than brute-force optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC4GS integrated with AbsGS yields the strongest gains: +0.121 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360, +0.485 dB on Tanks&Temples, and +0.154 dB on Deep Blending, while reducing primitives by 17–24% and memory by 17–24%. With 3DGS, DC4GS improves PSNR by +0.072 to +0.171 dB and cuts primitives by ~11% (e.g., 3,350K→2,968K on Mip-NeRF360). Pixel-GS+DC4GS gains +0.083 to +0.369 dB PSNR with ~11% fewer primitives. Scaffold-GS+DC4GS (DCC only, no DCS since Scaffold-GS has no explicit splitting) reduces primitives by 12–18% with near-identical quality. GES+DC4GS achieves the fewest primitives (1,323K on Mip-NeRF360, 816K on T&T) but shows a slight PSNR drop on T&T (−0.125 dB), indicating DC4GS is most beneficial when the base method already has a strong gradient criterion. LPM+DC4GS reduces primitives by 12–18% with marginal quality changes. Notably, extending AbsGS training to 60K iterations yields only +0.035 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 with no primitive reduction, confirming DC4GS's structural-aware splitting outperforms brute-force optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive density control (ADC) paradigm established by 3DGS [14] and refined by AbsGS [38] and Pixel-GS [44], all of which base split decisions solely on the L2 magnitude of view-space positional gradients. The key conceptual departure is treating the angular distribution of those gradients as an independent structural signal: the circular mean (directional consistency) of per-pixel gradient directions within a Gaussian reveals whether the primitive covers a homogeneous or heterogeneous region, information that magnitude alone cannot capture. The method is explicitly designed as a drop-in module (DCC for split selection, DCS for split placement) that composes with any existing gradient-magnitude criterion, rather than replacing the densification framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a consistent 11–30% reduction in primitive count with equal or improved reconstruction quality across six 3DGS-based pipelines, directly benefiting memory-constrained deployments (AR/VR, mobile). The plug-and-play design means existing 3DGS codebases can adopt DC4GS without architectural changes. Limitations include a ~48 ms per-iteration training overhead from DC evaluation and split-cost computation, and the approach remains tied to the gradient-based densification paradigm; it does not address aliasing or scale-adaptive rendering. Open questions include whether directional consistency can inform other primitive attributes (opacity, scale) or generalize to non-Gaussian representations, and whether the polynomial-regression split placement handles highly complex multi-structure junctions beyond the N=5 sampling budget.
