## Problem

Few-shot node classification on text-attributed graphs (TAGs) is hampered by two intertwined challenges: (1) existing knowledge distillation pipelines lack a principled way to select which nodes to annotate with LLM-generated labels so as to maximally improve teacher GNNs, and (2) nodes in real-world TAGs exhibit diverse local topologies that no single message-passing mechanism can handle, yet prior multi-teacher distillation methods apply a uniform teacher to all nodes. The paper asks how to synergize LLMs and heterogeneous GNNs to address both the label-scarcity and topology-heterogeneity problems simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text-attributed graph where each node carries a text description (encoded into a sentence embedding via a language model) and the graph adjacency matrix, together with a minimal set of ground-truth labels (1, 3, or 5 per class). It outputs (a) predicted class labels for all unlabeled nodes and (b) a per-node assignment of which of B teacher GNNs (e.g., GCN, GAT, APPNP, H2GCN for homophily; DirGNN, GPRGNN, HoloNets, H2GCN for heterophily) is most appropriate for that node. The LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct by default) is first fine-tuned with LoRA on graph-topology-aware (GTA) prompts before it is used for annotation and teacher selection.

## Method

PKD comprises two coupled modules. The GNN-preference-driven Node Selector (GNS) computes a K-uncertainty score for every node as the sum of pairwise KL divergences among the SoftMax outputs of B teacher GNNs, ranks nodes by this score, and selects the top-W most uncertain nodes for LLM annotation; a Distance-based Neighbor Selector (DNS) performs KNN in each teacher's embedding space to build diverse structural context for the annotation prompt. The Node-preference-driven GNN Selector (NGS) formulates per-node teacher assignment as a discrete-action reinforcement learning problem: the fine-tuned LLM acts as the policy agent, the state is a prompt encoding the node's semantic, structural, and prediction attributes, the action is a one-hot teacher choice, and the reward combines student GNN classification accuracy, cross-entropy loss, and distillation loss on the expanded training set. The agent is optimized with a simplified PPO (CLIP objective, entropy bonus, value-function critic), while the student GNN is trained with a three-term loss (distillation from the selected teacher, cross-entropy on true labels, and an entropy-regularization term) in alternating KD and NGS phases.

## Results

PKD achieves the best or near-best few-shot node classification accuracy across all 9 real-world text-attributed graphs. On CORA (#LN5), PKD_Llama reaches 90.27% accuracy, substantially outperforming GCNII (77.74%), IceBerg (76.23%), and MSKD (43.91%). On WIKI CS, PKD achieves 81.39% versus 56.29% for GCNII. The method's advantage is consistent across homophily (CORA, PUBMED, OGBN-ARXIV) and heterophily (CORNELL, WASHINGTON, TEXAS, WISCONSIN) graphs, and holds when swapping the LLM backbone (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mixtral-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PKD outperforms all 19 baselines on the majority of datasets. Against advanced GNNs, PKD surpasses GCNII by 12.5 points on CORA (90.27 vs. 77.74) and by 25.1 points on WIKI CS (81.39 vs. 56.29). Among LLM-enhanced methods, PKD dramatically defeats LLMGNN (which the authors attribute to its lack of graph-topology-aware fine-tuning) and outperforms GAugLLM on all datasets except PUBMED, where GAugLLM edges ahead. Among KD baselines, PKD beats MTAAM (the strongest KD competitor) by 5.9 points on CORA and 12.7 points on WIKI CS, and surpasses FairGKD by 4.1 points on CORA. The RL-based teacher selection in NGS is critical: it yields 90.27% on CORA versus 75.70% (entropy-based), 62.80% (random), and 60.29% (end-to-end), confirming that node-specific teacher assignment is essential. PKD also outperforms simple annotation strategies (RANDOM/5LN, VOTING/5LN) on all datasets, validating the K-uncertainty-based node selection in GNS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: LLM-assisted graph learning (extending LLMGNN [14] with GTA fine-tuning and uncertainty-based node selection), multi-teacher knowledge distillation for GNNs (departing from BGNN [21], MTAAM [22], and DMKD [18] by making teacher assignment node-specific rather than global), and reinforcement learning for model selection (applying PPO [41] to the discrete teacher-assignment problem). The key conceptual departure is that prior multi-teacher KD methods distill from all teachers simultaneously or sequentially without accounting for node-specific local topologies, while PKD uses an RL agent to tailor the message-passing mechanism per node. The K-uncertainty metric for node selection builds on active learning principles but applies them in the multi-GNN disagreement space rather than a single model's embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Few-shot node classification on text-attributed graphs is practically important in domains like citation networks and product recommendation where labels are expensive to obtain. The node-specific teacher selection via RL offers a principled way to handle heterogeneous local topologies that fixed-architecture GNNs struggle with, and the framework's generality across multiple LLMs (Llama, Qwen, Mixtral) suggests broad applicability. However, the method is limited to TAGs, incurs substantial training overhead (7.3s/epoch vs. sub-second for GNN baselines, plus ~10 hours for LLM fine-tuning), and the RL loop adds implementation complexity. Open questions include whether the approach scales efficiently to graphs with millions of nodes, whether the RL-based selection can be approximated by lighter-weight methods, and how the framework extends to non-textual graph attributes.
