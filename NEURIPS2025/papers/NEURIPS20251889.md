## Problem

Existing visuomotor policy methods—whether diffusion-based or autoregressive—treat robot action sequences as monolithic signals, overlooking the structured frequency-domain hierarchy where low-frequency components encode global motion and high-frequency components encode fine details. Discrete autoregressive approaches suffer information loss from quantization, while diffusion methods incur high inference latency, and neither exploits the fact that different task complexities require different levels of frequency detail.

## Input / Output

The method consumes visual observations (RGB images, colored point clouds, or depth maps) along with proprioceptive state, processed through the same observation encoders as Diffusion Policy or 3D Diffusion Policy. It outputs a full action trajectory of variable length N (typically 16 timesteps) and dimension d (ranging from 10 DoF for parallel grippers to 26–28 DoF for dexterous hands). Unusually, the action generation is structured hierarchically: the model first produces a low-frequency (global) motion estimate, then progressively refines it with higher-frequency details over N_iter autoregressive steps (default 4), with each step conditioned on the DCT-filtered output of the previous step.

## Method

FreqPolicy applies the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to decompose action sequences into frequency coefficients, then trains a masked transformer encoder-decoder to map a k-level DCT reconstruction (the first k coefficients inverse-transformed back to the time domain), observation features, and a frequency-level index into a continuous latent token z^k. A small MLP-based diffusion model is trained with a standard denoising loss to generate the full-frequency action sequence conditioned on z^k, enabling end-to-end optimization without any discretization of the action space. At inference, the model performs coarse-to-fine autoregressive generation: starting from a zero action (l_0=0), each iteration i produces a full-spectrum prediction via the diffusion sampler, which is then DCT-filtered to retain only the first l_i coefficients before feeding into the next iteration, progressively enriching the signal from global trends to fine-grained motion details. A frequency-aware masking strategy (adapted from FAR) applies higher mask ratios at lower-frequency levels to reduce training redundancy and improve generation diversity.

## Results

Across 48 simulation tasks spanning Adroit, DexArt, and MetaWorld, FreqPolicy achieves a 67.9% average success rate, outperforming DP3* (64.6%) and Mamba Policy (65.8%). On the 10-task subset in Table 1, FreqPolicy reaches 71.5% average, surpassing the no-DCT ablation (61.4%) and matching or exceeding DP3* (67.8%) and Mamba Policy* (68.4%) on 8 of 10 tasks. Inference is 10× faster than Diffusion Policy (0.21s vs. 2.11s on Robomimic), and real-world deployment achieves 70 FPS versus DP3's 25 FPS on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqPolicy outperforms all baselines on the 48-task average: it leads DP3* by 3.3 points (67.9% vs. 64.6%) and Mamba Policy by 2.1 points (67.9% vs. 65.8%), with the largest gap on Adroit (74.7% vs. 67.7% for DP3*). Against the original DP3 numbers (62.9%), the improvement is 5.0 points, though this comparison is less controlled due to different demonstration sets. On the 10-task Table 1, FreqPolicy wins on 8 of 10 tasks versus DP3* and Mamba Policy*, with notable gains on Adroit Pen (59% vs. 50%/55%) and DexArt Laptop (85% vs. 83%/79%). Against discrete AR baselines CARP and BeT on Robomimic/Push-T (Table 6), FreqPolicy matches or exceeds their success rates while running 10× faster (0.21s vs. 0.09s for CARP, but CARP fails entirely on Transport). In real-world handover, FreqPolicy achieves 70 FPS single-iteration inference versus DP3's 25 FPS, a 2.8× speedup critical for dynamic interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreqPolicy transplants the frequency-domain autoregressive generation paradigm from image synthesis (FAR [45]) into robotic manipulation, while adopting the continuous-token diffusion decoding strategy from MAR [19] to avoid the information loss inherent in discrete action tokenization (as used by BeT [32] and CARP [16]). The architecture and observation processing are inherited directly from Diffusion Policy [10] and 3D Diffusion Policy [48], positioning FreqPolicy as a drop-in replacement for the action generation head. The key conceptual departure from prior AR robotics methods is the shift from temporal-domain coarse-to-fine (CARP, DensePolicy) to frequency-domain hierarchical generation, and from discrete to continuous tokens, motivated by the observation that DCT energy distribution varies systematically with task complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical efficiency-quality tradeoff for real-time robotic control: 10× faster inference than Diffusion Policy with comparable or better success rates, and a principled mechanism (progressive frequency filtering) that confers measurable noise robustness. However, the average improvements over strong 3D baselines (DP3, Mamba Policy) are modest (~2-3 points across 48 tasks), and the authors acknowledge that 2D task performance still lags and degrades under fine-grained frequency partitioning. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain decomposition generalizes to VLA frameworks with language conditioning, whether the DCT choice is optimal versus other transforms, and how the method scales to longer horizons or higher-DoF systems beyond the 26-DoF dexterous hands tested here.
