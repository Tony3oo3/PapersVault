## Problem

Existing image geolocalization methods rely on predefined grid cells or image galleries, whose spatial generalizability degrades when test distributions mismatch training choices. Generative alternatives (diffusion/flow matching on raw coordinates) avoid this dependency but lack multi-scale spatial information and face Riemannian projection distortions. The paper identifies two fundamental obstacles to latent diffusion for spherical locations: sparsity of valid position encodings (making noise addition/removal ill-defined) and non-linearity of learned decoders (making small perturbations in embedding space cause large geographic drifts).

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input, encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder into a 768-dimensional embedding that conditions the diffusion process. The output is a geolocation (latitude, longitude) on Earth's spherical surface, produced by sampling an SHDD coefficient vector (dimension (L+1)², e.g., 2304 for L=47) from Gaussian noise via 200 DDPM steps, then applying a learning-free mode-seeking decoder that finds the spherical point maximizing local probability mass within a tunable radius ρ. At inference, 16 augmented copies of the input image are processed independently and their predictions are averaged at the geographical center.

## Method

LocDiff performs latent diffusion in a novel Spherical Harmonics Dirac Delta (SHDD) space, where each spherical point (θ₀,φ₀) is encoded as the vector of spherical harmonics coefficients of its Dirac delta function — a deterministic look-up that yields a dense Hilbert space in which every vector corresponds to a valid spherical probability distribution. The backward diffusion process is learned by a Conditional Siren-UNet (CS-UNet), a SirenNet-based UNet that takes the noisy SHDD vector, a CLIP image embedding, and a sinusoidal timestep embedding, and predicts the denoised SHDD vector; Sine activations are chosen because SH coefficients are sums of sinusoidal functions, preserving gradient flow. Training minimizes a reverse KL-divergence loss between the generated and ground-truth spherical distributions (approximated over 2048 randomly sampled anchor points per batch), which is more stable than spherical MSE and preserves multi-scale structure. Decoding is learning-free: the mode-seeking SHDD decoder finds the spherical point whose Dirac delta minimizes the KL-divergence to the generated distribution, equivalent to locating the center of probability mass concentration. A hybrid variant (LocDiff-H) further restricts GeoCLIP retrieval to 200 km neighborhoods around LocDiff's candidate locations to boost fine-scale accuracy.

## Results

On the three primary GeoCLIP-evaluation datasets, LocDiff (L=47) achieves 15.3% / 36.5% / 56.4% / 75.2% / 87.4% accuracy at 1 km / 25 km / 200 km / 750 km / 2500 km on Im2GPS3k, 13.2% / 26.0% / 41.9% / 64.5% / 80.3% on YFCC-26k, and 0.9% / 7.4% / 33.5% / 66.2% / 85.0% on GWS15k. Against the strongest generative baseline RFM S₂, LocDiff improves 25 km accuracy from 5.4% to 11.0% on OSV-5M and from 23.7% to 33.3% on YFCC-4k, while also surpassing the 10M-parameter RFM S₂ variant on 200 km (46.7% vs 45.3%) and 750 km (65.2% vs 61.1%) on YFCC-4k. The hybrid LocDiff-H variant further boosts 1 km accuracy to 15.3% on Im2GPS3k by restricting GeoCLIP retrieval to LocDiff's 200 km candidate region.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LocDiff (L=47) outperforms all 12 baselines on the majority of spatial scales across the three primary datasets. Against the strongest classification/retrieval baselines, LocDiff beats GeoCLIP [45] by +4.0 pp at 1 km and +2.1 pp at 200 km on Im2GPS3k, and by +2.7 pp at 1 km and +1.3 pp at 750 km on YFCC-26k; it also surpasses PIGEON [10] by +4.4 pp at 1 km on Im2GPS3k and +1.2 pp at 200 km on GWS15k. On the generative-model comparison (Table 2), LocDiff dominates RFM S₂ [8] by +5.6 pp at 25 km on OSV-5M (11.0% vs 5.4%) and by +9.6 pp at 25 km on YFCC-4k (33.3% vs 23.7%), while also exceeding the 10M-parameter RFM S₂ variant on 200 km and 750 km. LocDiff slightly trails GeoCLIP at 25 km on Im2GPS3k (36.5% vs 36.7%) and at 25 km on GWS15k (7.4% vs 9.2%), but the hybrid LocDiff-H recovers and surpasses both. In the generalizability experiment (Table 3), LocDiff's performance at 200 km, 750 km, and 2500 km degrades by <3.5% when switching from the MP16 gallery to a uniform grid, whereas GeoCLIP drops by 34–61% at 200 km, demonstrating LocDiff's substantially stronger spatial generalizability to unseen locations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM latent diffusion framework and SirenNet's sinusoidal activation functions, but introduces a fundamentally new encoding space for spherical location generation: the Spherical Harmonics Dirac Delta (SHDD) representation, which maps points on a sphere into a Hilbert space of spherical harmonics coefficients. It departs from grid-based classification (PlaNet, CPlaNet, PIGEON) and retrieval-based methods (GeoCLIP) that rely on predefined spatial partitions or image galleries, and from recent generative geolocalization models (Diff R³, FM R³, RFM S²) that perform diffusion directly on raw 3D or spherical coordinates without multi-scale structure. The key conceptual departure from prior location encoders (Sphere2Vec, RBF, SH/Siren) is that SHDD provides a dense, learning-free encoding-decoding pair where the difference measure is a continuous KL-divergence, resolving both the sparsity problem during diffusion and the non-linearity problem during decoding that plague neural-network-based encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LocDiff demonstrates that the choice of latent encoding space is as critical as the diffusion architecture for spherical generation tasks, and that a learning-free mode-seeking decoder in a well-chosen Hilbert space can outperform learned neural decoders by orders of magnitude in spatial drift robustness (5.3 km vs. 89–102 km under small perturbations). The generalizability experiments reveal a practical limitation of retrieval-based geolocalization: performance degrades sharply (up to 61% at 200 km scale) when the gallery's spatial distribution mismatches the test distribution, a problem that generative approaches inherently avoid. Open questions include how to push the SHDD degree L beyond 47 (currently limited by numerical overflow in coefficient magnitudes), how to integrate gallery information during the diffusion process rather than post-hoc, and whether the SHDD framework generalizes to other spherical or manifold-valued generation tasks beyond geolocalization.
