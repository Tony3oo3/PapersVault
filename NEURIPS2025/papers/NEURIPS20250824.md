## Problem

Vision foundation models are only released in a handful of fixed sizes, forcing users to pick the largest model that fits their compute budget—a sub-optimal choice. Existing elastic inference methods require specialized pretraining (e.g., Matryoshka nesting), and most structured pruning techniques demand retraining, labeled data, or a classification head, leaving no practical way to extract a continuum of sub-networks from an arbitrary pretrained ViT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250824/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained ViT checkpoint (DINO, DINOv3, SigLIPv2, DeIT, or AugReg) and 1,000 unlabeled ImageNet-1k training images (batch size 16) for gradient estimation, plus 1,000 fixed evaluation images for fitness scoring during the evolutionary search. It outputs a single global prunability ranking over all attention heads and FFN hidden neurons; thresholding this ranking at any sparsity S ∈ [0,1] yields a ready-to-use sub-network, so one run produces the entire elastic family without re-optimization. Pruning is constrained to remove at most 80% of heads and 95% of FFN neurons per block.

## Method

SnapViT decomposes the Hessian into a local diagonal term (parameter-wise sensitivity from self-supervised DINO gradients, making the method label-free and head-agnostic) and a global off-diagonal term (cross-block correlations) estimated implicitly via the Exponential Natural Evolution Strategy (xNES). xNES samples blockwise reweighting vectors c ~ N(μ, Σ), constructs trial pruning masks by rescaling local sensitivities, and evaluates each candidate by measuring PCA-projected cosine similarity between original and pruned model embeddings across multiple sparsity targets (10–60%). The covariance Σ evolves toward the inverse inter-block Hessian, so its inverse serves as a tractable proxy for global curvature. The final prunability score is the element-wise product of the diagonal local sensitivity and the block-expanded global factor, and a single global sort of these scores enables single-shot extraction of sub-networks at any desired sparsity in under five minutes on one A100 GPU.

## Results

SnapViT prunes DINO ViT-B/16 to 40% sparsity with less than 5% accuracy degradation while achieving a 1.58× inference speedup. In the full fine-tuning setting (50% sparsity, DeIT ViT-B/16), SnapViT recovers 82.6% ImageNet-1k top-1 accuracy and 75.4% average k-NN accuracy across 7 datasets, matching or exceeding the unpruned model's 81.8% ImageNet-1k score. The entire pruning pipeline (all sparsity levels from 10% to 60%) completes in under 5 minutes on a single A100 GPU, producing a full continuum of elastic sub-networks in one shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the retraining-free single-shot setting, SnapViT outperforms SNIP Magnitude by 7% and FPTP by 12.3% at 50% sparsity on supervised backbones, and surpasses LAMP by 53.2% in linear classification on DINO ViT-B/16. Against the LLM Surgeon (which requires 5 correction shots and labeled data), SnapViT is competitive or superior despite being a single-shot, label-free method. In the full fine-tuning comparison (Table 1), NViT achieves the highest ImageNet-1k accuracy (83.3%) but generalizes worse: its average k-NN and linear classification scores are 1.7% and 3.9% lower than SnapViT's across the 7-dataset benchmark. SnapViT matches SAViT on ImageNet-1k (82.6%) while substantially outperforming SN-Net (70.2% vs. 75.4% avg. k-NN) and LPViT (80.6% vs. 82.6% ImageNet-1k). For self-supervised foundation models (DINOv3, SigLIPv2), where most baselines cannot be applied due to their reliance on a classification head, SnapViT still improves over the applicable baselines (LAMP, SNIP Magnitude) by up to 21.7% (SigLIPv2) and 34.3% (DINOv2) in linear classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Hessian-based pruning lineage (OBD/OBS) by decomposing the Hessian into a local diagonal term estimated from self-supervised gradients and a global off-diagonal term approximated implicitly via the xNES evolutionary strategy, avoiding the quadratic cost of explicit curvature computation. It departs from prior elastic-inference methods (Matryoshka, OFA, Slimmable) that require specialized nested architectures during pretraining, instead extracting a continuum of sub-networks post-hoc from any pretrained ViT. The self-supervised DINO loss replaces the classification-head-dependent gradients used by most prior pruning methods (FPTP, SparseGPT, LLM Surgeon, NViT), enabling label-free operation on foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single <5-minute run on one A100 produces a full sparsity continuum for any pretrained ViT without labels or retraining, directly addressing the deployment bottleneck of foundation models being available only in fixed sizes. However, the paper reveals that large-scale pretrained models (DINOv3 with 1.7B samples, SigLIPv2 with 10B) resist pruning beyond ~30% sparsity, suggesting that the representational redundancy exploited by the method diminishes with pretraining scale. Open questions include whether the evolutionary Hessian approximation scales to billion-parameter models, whether per-neuron (rather than per-block) reweighting closes the remaining gap, and whether the approach transfers to non-ViT architectures like state-space models.
