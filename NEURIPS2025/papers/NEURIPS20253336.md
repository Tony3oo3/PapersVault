## Problem

Existing subgraph matching in EDA relies on structural isomorphism, which fails when logic synthesis and technology mapping substantially alter circuit topology while preserving the underlying function. Equivalent logic functions can be realized through widely differing structural implementations, so exact structural correspondence rarely persists across design stages, severely limiting the utility of traditional matching for cross-stage queries such as identifying AIG subgraphs within technology-mapped netlists.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query AIG subgraph (obtained by randomly dropping nodes from a larger AIG) and a candidate circuit graph that may be in a different modality—either a synthesized AIG or a post-mapping netlist with Skywater PDK cells whose gate types differ substantially from AIG gates. Stage 1 outputs a binary label indicating whether the query's logic function is implicitly contained in the candidate. Stage 2 outputs a per-node segmentation mask over the candidate graph, marking which nodes belong to the minimal fuzzy boundary that functionally encapsulates the query, rather than a clean structural subgraph boundary.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage multi-modal pipeline. Stage 1 encodes AIG graphs with DeepGate2 and post-mapping netlists with DeepCell, then trains jointly with a classification head (BCE loss) plus intra-modal InfoNCE alignment (AIG ↔ synthesized AIG) and inter-modal InfoNCE alignment (AIG ↔ post-mapping netlist), exploiting the Functional Equivalence Preservation property to produce structure-agnostic, function-invariant embeddings. Stage 2 fine-tunes the pretrained encoders for fuzzy boundary identification by formulating the task as graph segmentation: the query embedding is concatenated with each node embedding from the candidate and passed through a 3-layer MLP for per-node binary classification, which leverages information from interior boundary nodes that prior input-output classification approaches discard. Data is generated by extracting subgraphs from AIGs, applying random ABC synthesis flows (verified non-isomorphic via VF2), and mapping to Skywater PDK cells, with node annotations for Stage 2 derived through a library-based AIG-to-cell mapping function.

## Results

On the three EDA benchmarks (ITC99, OpenABCD, ForgeEDA), the proposed two-stage framework achieves an average accuracy of 93.8% for functional subgraph detection (Stage #1) and a DICE score of 91.3% for fuzzy boundary identification (Stage #2). Specifically, for the Gsub→Gsyn task, accuracy ranges from 92.3% (OpenABCD) to 96.0% (ForgeEDA), while for the cross-modal Gsub→Gpm task it ranges from 90.8% to 95.3%. In Stage #2, IoU scores range from 83.0% to 85.2% and DICE scores from 90.7% to 92.0% across the three datasets, substantially exceeding all structural and functional-equivalence baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines spanning structural isomorphism and functional equivalence approaches. Structural methods (NeuroMatch, HGCN) perform at or near random (≈50% accuracy) with extremely high variance in precision/recall (e.g., ±47.1 std on recall), confirming they cannot detect functionally present subgraphs after synthesis transformations. Functional-equivalence methods (Gamora, ABGNN) also fail, with accuracy between 34–56% and F1-scores as low as 1.0% (Gamora on ForgeEDA Gsub→Gsyn), because they require an explicit subgraph to be functionally equivalent to the query, which does not exist after global restructuring. The classical VF3 algorithm achieves 100% precision but only 0.32% recall on Gsub→Gsyn, as it can only find exact structural matches that have been destroyed by synthesis. The proposed method outperforms all baselines by 37–95 percentage points in accuracy across all datasets and tasks, with the largest gaps observed on ForgeEDA (96.0% vs. 52.3% for ABGNN) and OpenABCD (92.3% vs. 50.0% for NeuroMatch). In Stage #2 (fuzzy boundary), the method achieves 91.3% average DICE versus 59.2% (HGCN) and 48.2% (ABGNN), with the segmentation formulation contributing a 45-point DICE improvement over the input-output classification approach used by prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing 'functional subgraph matching' (Definition 3), which subsumes both structural isomorphism (Definition 1) and subgraph equivalence (Definition 2) as special cases. It repurposes existing functional-equivalence encoders (DeepGate2 for AIGs, DeepCell for post-mapping netlists) and contrastive alignment (InfoNCE) from the circuit representation learning literature, but applies them to a fundamentally different task: detecting whether a query's logic is *implicitly* present in a candidate even when no explicit subgraph exists. The fuzzy boundary identification stage departs from prior arithmetic block identification work (Gamora, ABGNN) by reframing boundary detection as graph segmentation rather than input-output node classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in EDA flows where synthesis and technology mapping destroy structural correspondence while preserving function, making traditional subgraph matching useless. The formal framework (Definition 3 with its reflexivity, transitivity, and anti-symmetry properties) provides a clean theoretical foundation that prior work lacked. Key open questions include scalability to million-node netlists (F1 drops from 95.3% to 81.2% on medium-sized circuits), handling multiple overlapping fuzzy boundaries, and extending beyond single-output circuits. The VF3 comparison (100% precision, 0.32% recall) starkly illustrates that this is a fundamentally different problem from isomorphism, not merely a harder instance of it.
