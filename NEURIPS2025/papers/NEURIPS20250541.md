## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for the REINFORCE algorithm in the stochastic setting either require oracle access to exact gradients, impose impractically small or decaying learning rates, or rely on a unique-reward assumption that is unverifiable in practice and does not extend to MDPs where multiple optimal policies commonly exist. No prior work established that REINFORCE with a softmax parameterization converges to a globally optimal policy in finite-horizon MDPs with an arbitrary constant learning rate.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a tabular finite-horizon MDP with bounded rewards in [-R, R], a finite state-action space per horizon, and a constant learning rate η ∈ Θ(1). The output is a sequence of softmax-parameterized policy parameters θ_t over state-action pairs that converge almost surely to assign all probability mass to the set of optimal actions at each reachable state (a 'generalized one-hot' policy), rather than necessarily to a single optimal action. The convergence is in the sense that V_0^{π_t}(s) → V_0^*(s) for all initial states, with the policy probabilities for suboptimal actions tending to zero.

## Method

The proof rests on two key innovations developed in the bandit setting and then lifted to MDPs. First, a novel exploration lemma (Lemma 4.1) shows that the stochastic gradient bandit algorithm samples every arm infinitely often with any constant learning rate, strengthening prior results that only guaranteed two arms are sampled i.o.; this is proven via a contradiction argument combining the Extended Borel-Cantelli lemma with parameter boundedness for finitely-sampled arms. Second, the unique-reward assumption is removed by proving that the sum of optimal-arm parameters diverges to +∞ while each suboptimal-arm parameter diverges to -∞, using a Freedman Divergence Trick that converts non-summable conditional expectations into almost-sure divergence of the parameter sums. For the MDP extension, the bandit results are ported to a nonstationary bandit setting (where reward means can drift but eventually settle within Δ/3 of their true values), and convergence is established by backward induction over horizons: at each state, once later-horizon policies are near-optimal, the Q-value gap at the current state is bounded away from zero, reducing the problem to the nonstationary bandit convergence result.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 5.2) is a theoretical guarantee: REINFORCE with softmax parameterization and any constant learning rate η ∈ Θ(1) converges almost surely to an optimal policy in finite-horizon MDPs, i.e., V₀^{π_t}(s) → V₀*(s) as t → ∞. In the bandit setting (Theorem 4.2), the algorithm converges to playing optimal arms (Σ_{a∈A*} π_t(a) → 1 a.s.) without requiring unique mean rewards. Empirically, on a chain MDP (H=4, R=7, T=10⁵ episodes), η=0.1 reduces the suboptimality gap substantially faster than η=0.00001, while excessively large rates (η ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}) introduce high variance and slow convergence, confirming a 'bowl-shaped' relationship between learning rate and performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so comparisons are against prior convergence guarantees rather than empirical baselines. The key positioning is against three lines of prior work: (1) Klein et al. (2024) prove convergence for constant learning rates but require η ≤ 1/(5H²R√T) ≈ 10⁻⁷ for the paper's test environment, making the rate impractically small; this paper removes that constraint entirely. (2) Mei et al. (2024a) prove convergence for arbitrary learning rates in bandits but require Assumption 3.1 (no ties in mean rewards), which this paper removes via a new exploration lemma (Lemma 4.1) showing every arm is sampled i.o. (3) Zhang et al. (2020a) and Ding et al. (2024) require decaying learning rate schedules (O(1/√t) or O(1/t)), which this paper eliminates. The empirical section validates that moderately large constant rates (η=0.1) outperform the tiny rates required by prior theory, while the backward-induction convergence pattern in Fig. 4 (last horizon converges first) matches the proof structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic policy gradient (SPG) convergence literature by removing two key restrictions simultaneously: the requirement for small or decaying learning rates (Zhang et al., 2020a; Klein et al., 2024) and the assumption of unique mean rewards (Mei et al., 2024a). The proof strategy builds on Mei et al. (2024a)'s bandit convergence framework but replaces their exploration lemma (at least two arms sampled i.o.) with a stronger result (all arms sampled i.o.), then extends the bandit analysis to a nonstationary setting that can be embedded into MDPs via backward induction. The REINFORCE algorithm itself is the classic Williams (1992) method with softmax parameterization; the contribution is entirely in the convergence analysis, not in algorithmic design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This closes a significant gap between theory and practice: REINFORCE with large constant learning rates is the standard in practice, yet prior convergence guarantees required rates so small (e.g., η ≈ 10⁻⁷ in the paper's test environment) as to be useless. The exploration result—that SPG inherently samples every action infinitely often without any explicit exploration mechanism—is a fundamental structural property of the algorithm that was previously unknown and may inform the design of other stochastic optimizers. Open questions include convergence rates under large learning rates (the paper notes high variance impedes convergence speed), extension to infinite-horizon or continuous MDPs, and whether the nonstationary bandit embedding technique generalizes to function-approximation settings. The tabular, finite-horizon scope limits immediate practical impact but provides a clean theoretical foundation.
