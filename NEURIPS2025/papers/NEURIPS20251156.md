## Problem

While gradient descent in deep networks exhibits distinct learning phases characterized by various order parameters (loss rate collapse, NTK alignment, gradient confusion), these transitions have been studied in isolation and primarily in shallow classification models. No unifying framework existed to analytically connect these phase transitions to the local structure of the NTK in deep, nonlinear regression settings such as SIREN-based image reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251156/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D pixel coordinates (x ∈ R²) paired with grayscale intensities from 64×64 downsampled images (sourced from scikit-image and ImageNet), fitting SIREN models via full-batch gradient descent. Evaluation is performed at 256×256 resolution as a super-resolution task. Beyond the reconstructed image, the framework produces a suite of NTK-derived order parameters—correlation lengthscale ξ, principal eigenvector v₀, minimum C_NTK, and background contribution μ_r K_∞—that jointly characterize the timing and nature of phase transitions during training.

## Method

The paper derives two complementary local approximations of the SIREN NTK: a Gaussian kernel (valid at large separations) that yields a diffusion equation for residual evolution, and a Cauchy distribution (valid at small separations) parameterized by local gradient structure (a_x, D_x, H_x). From these, the authors analytically obtain closed-form expressions for the correlation lengthscale, the principal eigenvector (scaling as O(‖∇_θ f‖⁴)), and the minimum of the Cosine NTK. The key insight is that all three phase transitions—onset of diffusion wavecrests in residuals, loss rate collapse, and NTK alignment with image edges—share a common mechanism: as long-range gradient correlations decay (c_∞ → 0), the NTK becomes locally structured, the principal eigenvalue diverges, and the principal eigenvector concentrates on high-gradient regions (edges). Empirically, critical points for all order parameters cluster in time across 30 images and multiple SIREN architectures (depth 3–5, ω₀ ∈ {15,30,60,90}).

## Results

The paper's central empirical finding is that three distinct phase transitions—onset of diffusion wavecrests in residuals, loss rate collapse, and NTK alignment—consistently cluster at the same critical time across all 12 hyperparameter configurations (depth × ω₀) and 30 images (Figure 3). Table 1 shows that decreasing ω₀ from 90 to 15 shifts the critical time earlier (log₁₀ t_crit drops from ~2.8 to ~1.9 for depth 3) but degrades final evaluation loss (log₁₀ L_eval worsens from −2.01 to −1.83) and increases residual correlation (0.35 → 0.48), indicating premature convergence and underfitting. Increasing depth from 3 to 5 at fixed ω₀=60 improves edge alignment AUC from 0.60 to 0.71 and reduces evaluation loss from −2.00 to −2.05, while also reducing residual correlation (0.40 → 0.39). The principal eigenvector v₀ becomes increasingly concentrated on image edges for deeper models and lower ω₀, with Pearson correlation ρ between log‖∇_θ f‖² and ‖∇_x I‖ rising from ~0.06 (depth 3, ω₀=90) to ~0.55 (depth 4, ω₀=15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is an analytical and empirical study of SIREN training dynamics rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark against competing architectures or training algorithms. The 'comparisons' are instead across hyperparameter configurations (depth ∈ {3,4,5} and ω₀ ∈ {15,30,60,90}) to isolate the effects of each on phase transition timing and final performance. The key comparative finding is that depth and ω₀ have opposing effects: deeper models converge to better solutions (lower L_eval, lower residual correlation) while lower ω₀ triggers earlier phase transitions but leads to premature convergence and underfitting (higher residual correlation, worse L_eval). The paper also implicitly contrasts SIREN dynamics with prior NTK alignment literature, which has been restricted to shallow or linear classification models, by demonstrating that the same alignment phenomenon occurs in deep nonlinear regression settings with qualitatively different signatures (edge alignment rather than class-boundary alignment). No direct numerical comparison against ReLU+PE baselines is provided in the main text (referenced only in Appendix D).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Tangent Kernel Alignment (NTKA) literature—which has been largely confined to classification problems with shallow or linear networks (Atanasov et al. 2021, Shan & Bordelon 2022, Baratin et al. 2021)—to deep nonlinear SIREN models performing 2D image regression. It also unifies three previously studied but analytically disconnected phenomena: the fast-to-slow learning transition tracked via gradient confusion (Fort et al. 2020, Sankararaman et al. 2020), the divergence of the principal NTK eigenvalue, and the emergence of diffusion-like wavecrests in residuals (a novel observation). The analytical backbone—Gaussian and Cauchy approximations of the local SIREN NTK—draws on the layer-wise NTK decomposition and the spectral-bias literature for INRs (Tancik et al. 2020, Belbute-Peres & Kolter 2022), but repurposes these tools to derive closed-form order parameters rather than to characterize initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unifying framework gives practitioners a single set of interpretable, locally-computable quantities (correlation lengthscale, asymptotic C_NTK value, gradient-magnitude Hessian) from which multiple phase-transition diagnostics can be derived, potentially aiding diagnosis of underfitting or premature convergence in INR training. The finding that NTK alignment in image regression is driven by the difficulty of modeling edges, and that lower ω₀ causes premature convergence by inflating the correlation lengthscale, offers concrete hyperparameter guidance. However, the analysis is restricted to SIRENs, 2D grayscale images, and full-batch gradient descent; whether the Gaussian/Cauchy approximations and the three-transition clustering hold for ReLU+PE INRs, 3D volumes, color images, or adaptive optimizers like Adam remains open and is acknowledged by the authors.
