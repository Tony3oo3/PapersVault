## Problem

When retrieval systems update their embedding models, the new model's representations are incompatible with the old gallery's, forcing costly backfilling. Existing adaptation strategies face a plasticity-stability trade-off: affine transformations adapt well to new distributions but distort the original geometric structure, while strictly orthogonal transformations preserve structure but offer no adaptability to downstream tasks. The paper addresses how to align updated model representations with previous ones while retaining the newly learned representation space, particularly when the adapter must generalize to distributions different from its training data.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely on pre-extracted feature vectors from frozen models (no raw images or model retraining), taking paired embeddings from the old model h^k ∈ R^d and the new model h^t ∈ R^n along with class labels. If dimensionalities differ, the higher-dimensional vector is truncated to match the smaller space. The outputs are learned transformation parameters (a backward orthogonal or λ-orthogonal matrix B and a forward affine map F), aligned representations in a unified space, and a distance-based ordering that determines which gallery entries to backfill first for partial gallery updates.

## Method

The core contribution is a λ-Orthogonality Regularization that replaces the hard orthogonality constraint with a sigmoid-modulated penalty L_λ = σ(α(‖WW^T − I‖_F − λ)) · ‖WW^T − I‖_F, where the threshold λ controls the maximum allowed deviation from orthogonality and α sets the steepness of the transition, enabling distribution-specific adaptation while preserving global geometric structure. The full pipeline learns a backward transformation B (parameterized as the matrix exponential of a skew-symmetric matrix for strict orthogonality, or as an affine map with λ-orthogonality regularization for downstream adaptation) that maps new-model embeddings into the old-model space, jointly with a forward affine transformation F that maps old-model embeddings into the backward-aligned new-model space. A supervised contrastive loss is applied to both F(h^k) and B(h^t) to promote intra-class clustering and inter-model alignment without requiring access to either model's classifier. The total loss is a weighted sum of forward MSE alignment, backward MSE alignment, the contrastive objective, and the λ-orthogonality term, optimized with Adam while all model parameters remain frozen. A novel backfilling ordering prioritizes gallery entries farthest from their class mean in the forward-adapted space, enabling the system to reach full new-model performance with less than 50% of the gallery backfilled.

## Results

On the Independently Pretrained Models setting (ResNet-18 → ViT-L-16, ImageNet1K), the proposed method achieves 73.53% CMC-Top1 and 52.06% mAP for cross-model retrieval (B_⊥(φ_new)/F(φ_old)), while uniquely maintaining backward compatibility with the old model at 65.54% CMC-Top1 (B_⊥(φ_new)/φ_old) — a setting where FCT and FastFill collapse to 0.04%. The backward-aligned new model (B_⊥(φ_new)/B_⊥(φ_new)) exactly matches the independently trained new model (76.62% CMC-Top1, 56.84% mAP), confirming zero performance loss. On the downstream CUB200 task with λ-orthogonality (λ=12), the method reaches 70.72% CMC-Top1 while preserving zero-shot ImageNet1K performance at 75.44% (+3.66 over the new model alone). For partial backfilling, the proposed distance-to-class-mean ordering achieves 76.59% CMC-Top1 (Independently Pretrained setting), outperforming FastFill (75.06%) and FCT (73.86%), reaching full new-model performance with <50% of the gallery backfilled.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against FCT (Ramanujan et al., CVPR 2022) and FastFill (Jaeckle et al., ICLR 2023), the two dominant mapping-based compatibility methods. The most critical distinction is backward compatibility: when querying with the new model against the *unmodified* old gallery (φ_new/φ_old or B_⊥(φ_new)/φ_old), FCT and FastFill both collapse to 0.04% CMC-Top1, while the proposed method achieves 65.54% CMC-Top1 and 38.55% mAP — a >65-point gap. For forward-compatible retrieval (new query vs. forward-adapted gallery), the proposed method edges out FastFill by 0.2% CMC-Top1 (73.53 vs. 73.33) and FCT by 1.0% (73.53 vs. 72.54) on ImageNet1K. On the downstream CUB200 task with λ-orthogonality, the method surpasses FastFill by 9.2% CMC-Top1 (70.72 vs. 61.49) and FCT by 8.6% (70.72 vs. 62.13), while also preserving zero-shot ImageNet1K accuracy (+3.66 over the new model alone). For partial backfilling, the proposed distance-to-class-mean ordering outperforms FastFill's Bayesian strategy by 1.53% CMC-Top1 (76.59 vs. 75.06) and FCT's random ordering by 2.73% (76.59 vs. 73.86) in the Independently Pretrained setting, reaching full new-model performance with less than 50% of the gallery updated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backward-compatible representation learning line initiated by BCT [15] and the forward-compatible mapping approaches of FCT [25] and FastFill [22]. Its core methodological departure is the introduction of a λ-orthogonality regularization that smoothly interpolates between strict orthogonal (stability) and unconstrained affine (plasticity) transformations via a sigmoid-gated Frobenius-norm penalty, building on the soft orthogonality constraint of Bansal et al. [36] and the matrix-exponential parameterization of Lezcano-Casado [45]. The supervised contrastive loss for intra-class clustering and inter-model alignment, along with the distance-to-class-mean backfilling ordering, are novel additions that make the pipeline architecture-agnostic and remove the dependency on the new model's classifier required by FastFill.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For large-scale retrieval systems, avoiding full gallery re-encoding (backfilling) upon model updates is a critical operational concern, and this paper provides a practical, tunable mechanism (the λ hyperparameter) to balance representation preservation against downstream adaptability. The method's architecture-agnosticism—validated across ResNet, ViT, DenseNet, EfficientNet, DINOv2, and CLIP—broadens its applicability to real-world deployment scenarios where models are updated incrementally. Open questions include whether λ can be learned automatically rather than hand-tuned, how the approach scales to multi-modal or very large gallery settings, and whether the single-seed evaluation (acknowledged as a limitation) holds under distributional shift. The backfilling ordering strategy, while effective, remains heuristic and its optimality is not formally characterized.
