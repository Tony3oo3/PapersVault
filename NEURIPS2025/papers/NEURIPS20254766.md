## Problem

Principled exploration in model-based RL (optimism in the face of uncertainty) has strong theoretical guarantees but requires solving intractable non-convex optimization over dynamics models, making it computationally prohibitive for high-dimensional and real-world settings. Naive exploration strategies like Boltzmann exploration are scalable but provably suboptimal, especially under sparse rewards. This paper bridges the gap between theory and practice by proposing a simple, scalable exploration method that retains the same regret guarantees.

## Input / Output

SOMBRL consumes transition data (state, action, next-state tuples) or RGB image observations for visual control tasks, and produces a policy mapping states to actions. The method is agnostic to the base MBRL algorithm: it plugs into MBPO (state-based, using SAC for policy training), DREAMER (visual control), or SIMFSVGD (hardware), requiring only that the base method provides an uncertainty-aware dynamics model (deep ensemble disagreement or GP posterior). The scalar hyperparameter λ_n controls the exploration-exploitation trade-off and can be auto-tuned.

## Method

SOMBRL alternates between learning an uncertainty-aware dynamics model (mean μ_n and epistemic uncertainty σ_n via GP or deep ensemble) and solving a policy optimization that maximizes the expected sum of extrinsic reward plus a weighted epistemic uncertainty term, λ_n‖σ_n(x,u)‖, under the mean dynamics. The key insight is that this simple 'uncertainty-as-intrinsic-reward' formulation is provably equivalent to optimistic exploration: the resulting objective J_n(π) is an upper bound on the true return J(π) for all policies, so maximizing it yields sublinear regret. Unlike prior optimistic methods (HUCRL, Kakade et al.) that require maximizing over a confidence set of dynamics—a non-convex, high-dimensional optimization—SOMBRL only plans under the mean model, making it compatible with any existing policy optimizer or planner. The paper proves sublinear regret bounds of O(Γ_N^{3/2}√N) for finite-horizon, γ-discounted infinite-horizon, and non-episodic average-reward settings with continuous state-action spaces, matching the asymptotic rates of prior work while being substantially simpler and more scalable.

## Results

SOMBRL consistently outperforms all greedy and naive-exploration baselines across state-based (DMC), visual control (DMC + Atari), and real-world hardware (RC car) tasks. On sparse-reward tasks (MountainCar, CartPole, Atari Venture), greedy baselines (MBPO-MEAN, DREAMER, SIMFSVGD) fail to obtain any meaningful reward, while SOMBRL variants solve the tasks. On the dynamic RC car hardware, SIMFSVGD-OPTIMISTIC learns a complex drifting parking maneuver in only 20 real-world episodes, whereas SIMFSVGD fails in 4 out of 5 runs (converging to a local optimum of not moving). In the GP-dynamics validation (Section 5.3), SOMBRL outperforms HUCRL, PETS, and greedy planning in both episodic and nonepisodic settings, while HUCRL requires roughly 3× more compute time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **PETS** (Chua et al., 2018, as MBPO-PETS), SOMBRL (MBPO-OPTIMISTIC) achieves higher returns across all DMC state-based tasks, with the gap most pronounced on sparse-reward environments (MountainCar, CartPole) where PETS fails entirely. Against **HUCRL** (Curi et al., 2020), SOMBRL matches or exceeds performance in both episodic and nonepisodic GP-dynamics experiments while being significantly cheaper (HUCRL requires ~3× more compute). Against **DREAMER** (Hafner et al., 2023), DREAMER-OPTIMISTIC performs on-par on most visual-control tasks but clearly outperforms on Finger-spin (DMC) and Venture (Atari, where DREAMER scores zero reward); with action costs added, DREAMER fails on both Finger-spin and CartPole while DREAMER-OPTIMISTIC still achieves high returns. Against **SIMFSVGD** (Rothfuss et al., 2024), SIMFSVGD-OPTIMISTIC matches performance under dense rewards but decisively wins as rewards become sparser; on hardware, SIMFSVGD fails to solve the parking task (1/5 success) while SIMFSVGD-OPTIMISTIC succeeds in 20 episodes. Against **NEORL** (Sukhija et al., 2024b), SOMBRL achieves better average reward and lower regret in the nonepisodic setting, attributed to the practical difficulty of solving NEORL's intractable dynamics-maximization (Equation 7).

## Relation to Prior Work

SOMBRL builds on the optimistic exploration principle established by Kakade et al. (2020) and Curi et al. (2020) for finite-horizon MBRL, and on NEORL (Sukhija et al., 2024b) for the nonepisodic setting, all of which require solving a joint policy-dynamics optimization that is computationally intractable in high dimensions. The key departure is replacing this constrained optimization with a simple reward augmentation: planning under the mean dynamics while adding epistemic uncertainty as an intrinsic reward. This connects to the intrinsic motivation literature (Pathak et al., 2017; Sekar et al., 2020) and the data-driven control 'separation principle' (Aström & Wittenmark, 1971), but SOMBRL is the first to show this augmentation provably yields optimistic exploration with sublinear regret across multiple RL settings simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOMBRL closes a significant gap between theoretically grounded exploration (with regret guarantees) and practical scalability, making principled exploration accessible to high-dimensional tasks like visual control and real-world robotics where prior optimistic methods were infeasible. The hardware experiment on an RC car—where the non-optimistic baseline (SIMFSVGD) fails completely while SOMBRL solves the task in 20 episodes—provides compelling evidence that naive exploration is insufficient in practice, not just in theory. A key limitation is the requirement for a probabilistic model (e.g., deep ensembles) to quantify epistemic uncertainty, and the regret bound is slightly looser (by √Γ_N) than the intractable prior methods. Open questions include whether the guarantees extend to non-GP, non-RKHS model classes and whether the auto-tuned λ_n can be made more robust across task distributions.
