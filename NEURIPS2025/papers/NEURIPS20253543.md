## Problem

Existing bioactivity prediction methods focus on local binding interactions within protein-ligand complexes while neglecting spatial emptiness—the unoccupied regions in the binding pocket that determine whether a ligand effectively blocks endogenous substrates (competitive inhibition). Additionally, prior approaches treat each pocket-ligand pair as an independent data point, discarding the global geometric context of the full binding region, and rely on MSE loss that ignores the relative ordering of bioactivity values critical for ranking drug candidates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D coordinates of docked protein-ligand complexes represented as heterogeneous graphs (nodes for pocket and ligand atoms, edges for covalent bonds, hydrogen bonds, van der Waals contacts, etc.), along with multiple protein pocket definitions per target. Multiple docking poses per ligand are processed through a shared pose-level network and aggregated via a permutation-invariant pooling function (mean, sum, or attention) to yield a single continuous bioactivity prediction (IC50, EC50, Ki, or Kd) per ligand.

## Method

LigoSpace integrates three components into existing GNN backbones (DTIGN, GIGN, GAT). First, GeoREC partitions the space around each node into S uniform cones, identifies the nearest atom in each non-empty cone, and adds geometric edges connecting the node to these nearest neighbors, thereby encoding local spatial emptiness as additional graph connectivity. Second, Union-Pocket replaces fragmented local pocket representations with the union of all atoms across every candidate pocket for a given protein, providing a consistent global structural context that lets the GNN integrate information across all binding sites simultaneously. Third, a pairwise L2 loss penalizing discrepancies in predicted versus true bioactivity differences between all sample pairs is combined with standard MSE via a weighted hybrid objective, encouraging the model to preserve relative ranking. The enhanced graph G′=(V, E, E_geo) with geometric edges is fed into the GNN, and the Union-Pocket ensures spatial consistency when comparing different ligands on the same protein.

## Results

On the DTIGN dataset (8 protein targets), LigoSpace-enhanced DTIGN reduces average RMSE by 9.93% (0.9751→0.8783), increases Pearson r by 18.39% (0.5757→0.6815), and improves Kendall's τ by 19.09% (0.4183→0.4982) over the DTIGN baseline. On the larger SIU dataset (201K+ pairs), DTIGN+LigoSpace achieves r=0.321 and Spearman=0.326 on SIU-0.9/Kd, where the DTIGN baseline yields near-zero correlations (r=−0.001, Spearman=−0.042), and outperforms Uni-Mol on 43 out of 48 dataset-metric combinations. GIGN+LigoSpace also shows consistent gains (7.72% RMSE reduction, 9.03% r increase on DTIGN averages).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LigoSpace is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement on three GNN baselines: DTIGN (multi-pocket docking + self-attention), GIGN (3D geometric interaction graph), and GAT (standard attention-based GNN). Against DTIGN, LigoSpace wins on all 8 DTIGN subsets for all three metrics, with the largest gains on E1 (r: 0.3363→0.4969) and I1 (r: 0.3547→0.5895). Against GIGN, LigoSpace improves 7 out of 8 subsets, with notable gains on I1 (r: 0.4357→0.6861) and E1 (r: 0.4435→0.4912), though E2 shows a slight r drop (0.7246→0.6637). Against GAT, improvements are more modest (6 out of 8 subsets), consistent with GAT's weaker baseline. On the SIU dataset, DTIGN+LigoSpace surpasses the Uni-Mol benchmark on SIU-0.9/Kd (r: 0.321 vs −0.033) and matches it on SIU-0.9/Ki (r: 0.485 vs 0.485), while GIGN+LigoSpace achieves the best Spearman on SIU-0.6/Ki (0.463 vs Uni-Mol's 0.452). The paper reports no case where LigoSpace degrades performance relative to its respective baseline on the DTIGN dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GNN-based bioactivity prediction line established by DTIGN (Yin et al., 2024) and GIGN (Yang et al., 2023), which model protein-ligand complexes as interaction graphs but focus exclusively on local binding-region features. The key departure is the introduction of GeoREC, which encodes spatial emptiness (unoccupied volume around each node) as geometric edges—a concept absent from all prior protein-ligand modeling work—and the Union-Pocket, which generalizes DTIGN's per-ligand multi-pocket aggregation into a single global pocket representation shared across all ligands on a protein. The pairwise loss is a straightforward but underutilized addition to the standard MSE objective in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spatial emptiness framing connects directly to the pharmacological principle of competitive inhibition, giving the model a biologically grounded signal for how effectively a ligand blocks endogenous substrate access. The mutual reinforcement between GeoREC and Union-Pocket (ablation on E1 shows r dropping from 0.50 to ~0.31 when either is removed) suggests that global geometric context and local emptiness are complementary rather than redundant. Limitations include the absence of released code (patent pending), no error bars across runs, and modest gains on weaker baselines (GAT), raising questions about whether the improvements are architecture-dependent. Open questions include whether GeoREC generalizes to other structure-based tasks (binding site prediction, pose scoring) and how computational cost scales with pocket size.
