## Problem

Standard next-token prediction with teacher forcing encourages shortcut learning and underperforms on tasks requiring long-range planning. While multi-token prediction has been shown to improve pretraining, existing implementations rely on additional transformer output heads that introduce large numbers of new parameters, making them poorly suited for fine-tuning or parameter-efficient fine-tuning of pretrained models. The paper asks whether multi-token prediction can be achieved with negligible parameter overhead while remaining fully compatible with off-the-shelf pretrained architectures.

## Input / Output

For language tasks, the method consumes (prefix, answer) text pairs—math word problems with chain-of-thought solutions or dialogue transcripts—and produces generated text evaluated by exact-match accuracy or ROUGE scores. For image generation, it takes a class label or caption as conditioning and produces 256×256 RGB images from VQ-VAE token sequences (codebook size 16384, 16×16 grid). Register tokens are interleaved only within the answer/image-token portion during training and are entirely discarded at inference, so the model's input/output interface and generation latency remain identical to the base model.

## Method

MuToR interleaves learnable register tokens between regular tokens in the training sequence, each tasked with predicting a future token at a uniformly sampled offset d (up to d_max). A modified causal attention mask ensures regular tokens never attend to registers and registers never attend to other registers, preserving the standard next-token objective for regular tokens while allowing registers to learn forward-looking representations. All registers share a single learnable embedding (~2K parameters), and the prediction offset is encoded via RoPE position IDs (register after x_t predicting x_{t+d} receives position t+d−1). The training loss is a weighted sum of the standard next-token prediction loss and the auxiliary register loss. For autoregressive image generation, the 1D offset is replaced by a 2D offset pair (d_h, d_w) sampled over a spatial neighborhood, enabling prediction of up to d_max²−1 future tokens without additional heads. The method integrates seamlessly with LoRA and requires no architectural modifications to the base transformer.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning, MuToR achieves 42.10% on GSM8K and 68.33% on 1M-GSM with Gemma 2B, outperforming Next-Token (38.87% / 66.09%) and Multi-Token (40.66% / 66.69%). With Llama 3 8B, MuToR reaches 67.85% on GSM8K and 87.11% on 1M-GSM. On ImageNet 256×256 image generation at 360K steps, MuToR-2D achieves FID 5.09 and IS 195.3, substantially beating the Next-Token baseline (FID 6.18, IS 171.5). MuToR requires only ~2K additional parameters versus 110M+ for the Multi-Token baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuToR consistently outperforms both multi-token prediction baselines across all evaluated settings. Against Gloeckle et al.'s parallel Multi-Token (110M+ extra parameters), MuToR (2K extra parameters) gains +1.44% on GSM8K and +1.64% on 1M-GSM with Gemma 2B, and +1.29% / +1.44% with Llama 3 8B. Against Liu et al.'s sequential DS-Multi-Token (118M extra parameters), MuToR leads by +1.49% on GSM8K and +1.36% on 1M-GSM. Notably, Multi-Token's advantage over Next-Token diminishes or vanishes at larger d_max values and with more training data, whereas MuToR's gains are stable across d_max ∈ {2,3,4,6} and training set sizes. In the PEFT (LoRA) setting, MuToR matches or exceeds full fine-tuning Next-Token performance, while Multi-Token is poorly suited to PEFT due to its many new parameters. On ImageNet generation, MuToR-2D at 100K steps (FID 6.57) surpasses Next-Token at 200K steps (FID 6.83), demonstrating faster convergence alongside better final quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

MuToR repurposes the register token concept from Darcet et al. (2024, originally for suppressing high-norm artifacts in ViTs) as a mechanism for multi-token prediction, departing fundamentally from the dominant approach of Gloeckle et al. (2024) and Liu et al. (2024), which attach parallel or sequential transformer output heads for each future token position. The key conceptual shift is encoding the prediction offset d entirely through RoPE positional bias on a single shared learnable embedding, rather than through separate prediction heads—reducing additional parameters from 110M–550M to roughly 2K. This design choice is what makes the method compatible with off-the-shelf pretrained models and PEFT (LoRA), a setting where prior multi-token prediction methods have shown limited or no benefit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that multi-token prediction becomes a drop-in modification for fine-tuning pipelines (full or LoRA-based) without architectural surgery, addressing a gap where prior multi-token methods were primarily validated in pretraining. The 2D extension for autoregressive image generation—predicting a spatial neighborhood with one shared embedding where prior methods would require 14+ additional heads—suggests the approach generalizes beyond 1D sequences. Open questions include whether adaptive or task-aware register placement (currently uniform or random) could yield larger gains, whether benefits persist in data-abundant pretraining at scale, and whether the modest 1–2 point improvements on math benchmarks translate to more complex reasoning tasks.
