## Problem

Biomedical image processing currently relies on task-specific specialist models (separate networks for restoration, super-resolution, registration, and fusion), forcing biologists to select, train, and integrate multiple models for workflows that often require sequential steps. These specialist models ignore the interrelated nature of imaging degradations and the computational burden of running multiple large models on inherently large biomedical volumes. Orochi addresses this by proposing the first versatile foundational model that unifies all four low-level tasks under a single architecture and pre-training strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250471/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts raw multi-channel biomedical images (2D or 3D, 2–5D) and, during pre-training, extracts patches/volumes at three scales (1×, 1/2×, 1/4×) via Random Multi-scale Sampling. For paired tasks (registration, fusion), two images are fed jointly; for single-image tasks (restoration, super-resolution), a degraded version is reconstructed. The output is task-dependent: a restored/super-resolved image, a dense deformation field (registration flow), or a fused multi-modal volume. A replaceable decoder head (standard conv or depth-wise separable conv) is swapped at fine-tuning time to match the target task's output format.

## Method

Orochi's backbone is a Multi-head Hierarchy Mamba (MHM) that combines Mamba's linear-complexity selective state-space blocks with Swin-Transformer-style hierarchical patch merging across four stages (embed_dim=128, depths 4×4). Pre-training uses Task-related Joint-embedding Pre-training (TJP), which applies four task-specific degradations simultaneously—dual-masking reconstructive fusion (two partially overlapping binary masks), spatially-varying Gaussian down-sampling with random scale and noise, multi-octave Perlin-noise deformation fields smoothed by spatially-varying Gaussian kernels, and multi-stage noise (Gaussian + Poisson + salt-and-pepper)—so the model learns interrelations among degradations rather than relying on standard MIM. A three-tier fine-tuning framework (Full: 100% params, Normal: 10–30% via a frozen encoder + trainable conv head, Light: <5% via depth-wise separable conv head) enables parameter-efficient adaptation, with the Light variant often matching or exceeding full fine-tuning on small biomedical datasets.

## Results

On the CARE restoration benchmark, Orochi(Light) achieves 29.77 PSNR / 0.71 SSIM (XY) and 29.98 PSNR / 0.72 SSIM (XZ), surpassing the strongest pre-trained baseline UniFMIR (27.12/27.67 PSNR) by over 2.6 PSNR with fewer than 5% trainable parameters. On the HBA super-resolution task, Orochi(Full) reaches 35.33 PSNR at 4 mm and 31.93 PSNR at 8 mm, exceeding VCM (27.54/27.52) by 7.79/4.41 PSNR. For inter-patient brain registration on OASIS, Orochi(Full) attains 83.62 Dice and 1.60 HD95, outperforming TransMorph-L (82.22 Dice) and MambaMorph (81.81 Dice). On CT-MRI fusion (VIFB), Orochi(Full) scores 0.41 Q_abf, 2351.57 Q_cv, and 1.39 SSIM, beating BSAFusion (0.39 / 4155.10 / 1.38) on all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Orochi outperforms all 46 specialist baselines across the four evaluated tasks. In restoration, the Light variant (depth-wise separable convolution head, <5% parameters) leads with 29.77 PSNR, beating the pre-trained foundation model UniFMIR by +2.65 PSNR and train-from-scratch SwinIR by +3.79 PSNR; the authors attribute this to the small dataset (<100 patches) making full fine-tuning prone to overfitting. In super-resolution, Orochi(Full) surpasses the strongest diffusion-based baseline VCM by +7.79 PSNR (4 mm) and +4.41 PSNR (8 mm), and exceeds LIIF (implicit neural representation) by +2.92 PSNR at 4 mm. For registration, Orochi(Full) achieves 83.62 Dice, improving over TransMorph-L by +1.40, MambaMorph by +1.81, and ConvexAdam by +2.42 points, while also reporting the lowest HD95 (1.60). In fusion, Orochi(Full) edges out BSAFusion by +0.02 Q_abf and −1803.53 Q_cv, and the Normal/Light variants still surpass all baselines on SSIM (1.45/1.43 vs. BSAFusion's 1.38). The ablation in Table 5 further shows that the proposed TJP pre-training strategy yields +12.40 Dice over MAE and +13.65 Dice over I-JEPA on registration, confirming that task-related degradation is critical for low-level tasks where MIM-based pre-training underperforms.

## Relation to Prior Work

Orochi unifies the Mamba state-space model's linear-complexity sequence processing with the Swin-Transformer's hierarchical patch-merging architecture, creating a Multi-head Hierarchy Mamba backbone tailored for large biomedical volumes. Its self-supervised pre-training departs from standard Masked Image Modelling by adopting a Task-related Joint-embedding Pre-training (TJP) strategy inspired by JEPA, where multiple task-specific degradations (dual-masking, spatially-varying Gaussian down-sampling, Perlin-noise deformation, multi-stage noise) serve as mutually informative contexts. The Random Multi-scale Sampling data pipeline extends the noisy down-sampling principles from DeepLP to a scalable, multi-scale patch extraction scheme over 100+ public studies, positioning Orochi as a generalist alternative to the prevailing specialist-model paradigm (CARE, TransMorph, BSAFusion, etc.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Orochi demonstrates that a single pre-trained model with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (<5% parameters) can match or exceed task-specific specialist models across restoration, super-resolution, registration, and fusion, offering biologists a practical all-in-one workflow that eliminates the burden of selecting and integrating multiple plugins. The finding that the Light PEFT variant outperforms full fine-tuning on small biomedical datasets challenges the default assumption that more trainable parameters are always beneficial. Key open questions include the scaling behavior of TJP pre-training (not investigated due to compute constraints), whether the unified approach extends to high-level semantic tasks (segmentation, detection), and how performance holds on truly out-of-distribution modalities beyond the 100+ studies used for pre-training.
