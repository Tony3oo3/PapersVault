## Problem

Existing flow-field reconstruction methods rely on unrealistic assumptions—2D domains, known governing PDEs, synthetic data from idealized solvers, and freely placed sensors—while conventional sensor placement strategies (SVD, QR pivoting) perform poorly when paired with deep learning reconstructors due to model performance disparity across placements. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating reconstruction under realistic 3D conditions with boundary-only sensors and no assumed physics, and by learning a sensor placement policy that accounts for both flow variability and the reconstructor's differential sensitivity to placement.

## Input / Output

The reconstruction model takes a mesh graph of the domain boundary where each node carries a 3D velocity vector, a scalar pressure, and a binary sensor mask; non-sensor nodes have randomly initialized values that must be imputed. Sensors are restricted to boundary nodes only (not advected with the flow), and the model outputs reconstructed velocity and pressure at all masked-out nodes. The sensor placement policy takes the complete mesh with full field information and outputs a Bernoulli probability per node, constrained to select exactly k|V| sensors via an equality constraint on the total count.

## Method

The reconstruction model is a directional transport-aware GNN (DTA-GNN) with an Encoder-Processor-Decoder architecture. Its processor computes a directional score as the inner product between a node's latent representation and edge features, then multiplies this score by the difference between neighboring latent states before passing through an MLP—explicitly mimicking the advection operator ν·∇φ in discrete, mesh-based form and acting as a learnable interpolation operator. For sensor placement, the paper introduces a Two-Step Constrained PPO procedure: the first stage trains with a penalized reward that softly enforces the sensor-count constraint via a quadratic penalty (theoretically justified by a Lipschitz-based equivalence result), and the second stage switches to hard-constrained sampling using Gumbel Top-k selection combined with a saddle point approximation for the constrained log-probability, achieving O(|V|) complexity instead of the O(k|V|²) required by exact dynamic programming.

## Results

On the four 3D DNS geometries (Sphere, Ellipsoid, Cylinder, NACA 4-digit airfoil), DTA-GNN achieves the lowest reconstruction MSE (×10⁻⁴) across all sensor densities (5%–30%) and both Uniform and Random placement strategies. On the most challenging NACA 4-digit airfoil at 10% Uniform sensors, DTA-GNN reaches 43.647 MSE, outperforming the next-best MeshGraphNets (74.549) by ~41%. On the NOAA OISST V2 sea surface temperature dataset at 10% sensor density, DTA-GNN achieves 6.853 MSE, a 14.01% improvement over the strongest baseline DiffusionPDE (7.969). The learned PPO sensor placement policy further reduces MSE by ~15% relative to uniform placement at 10% sensor density.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTA-GNN outperforms all five baselines (OFormer, GKO, FCN, MeshGraphNets, DiffusionPDE) across every geometry, sensor density, and placement strategy in the main comparison. The largest margins appear on the Ellipsoid and NACA 4-digit airfoil: at 10% Uniform on NACA, DTA-GNN (43.647) beats MeshGraphNets (74.549) by 41% and DiffusionPDE (66.277) by 34%. On the Sphere dataset, where baselines are more competitive, DTA-GNN still leads by 12–30% over the nearest competitors (e.g., 3.092 vs. MeshGraphNets 3.537 at 10% Uniform). On the real-world NOAA OISST V2 sea surface temperature dataset, DTA-GNN (6.853) surpasses DiffusionPDE (7.969) by 14.01% and MeshGraphNets (8.176) by 16.2%. Notably, traditional sensor placement methods (QR pivoting, D-optimality) perform worse than uniform or random placement when paired with DTA-GNN, motivating the learned PPO policy which then achieves a further ~15% MSE reduction over uniform placement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow-field reconstruction task from sparse sensors beyond the 2D, known-PDE, synthetic-data, unconstrained-placement assumptions that dominate prior work (Zhong et al., He et al., Hosseini & Shiri, etc.), moving to 3D DNS-generated data with unknown governing equations and boundary-restricted sensors. Architecturally, the DTA-GNN departs from standard message-passing GNNs (MeshGraphNets, FCN) by explicitly parameterizing directional alignment and transported quantities in the message function, drawing a direct analogy to the advection operator. For sensor placement, the paper builds on PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and Gumbel Top-k sampling (Kool et al., 2020), but introduces a novel Two-Step Constrained PPO procedure that combines a penalized-reward warmup (grounded in Liu et al., 2024) with a constrained stage using saddle-point approximation for the log-probability, addressing a computational bottleneck that prior constrained-sampling methods (Ahmed et al., 2023) cannot handle at mesh scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most practically relevant finding is that classical sensor-placement heuristics (QR pivoting, D-optimality) perform substantially worse than uniform placement when paired with deep-learning reconstruction models, suggesting that information-theoretic criteria designed for linear reconstruction do not transfer to nonlinear learned models. This motivates the learned-policy approach and opens the question of how to co-design sensor strategies with specific reconstruction architectures. A key limitation is that all flow data come from COMSOL DNS simulations rather than real experiments, so the gap to deployed sensor networks (with noise, drift, and calibration errors) remains untested. The sea-surface-temperature experiment partially addresses this but uses a regular grid rather than irregular 3D meshes, leaving open whether the boundary-constrained, geometry-agnostic formulation scales to industrial-scale CFD domains.
