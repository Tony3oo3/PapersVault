## Problem

Existing medical AI approaches inject domain knowledge primarily during pretraining (via medical corpora or knowledge graphs), providing only implicit guidance that fails to explicitly align models with task-specific, disease-level diagnostic criteria at inference time. This limits both diagnostic accuracy on diverse downstream tasks and the interpretability of decisions, since black-box models cannot trace their predictions back to clinical guidelines. Additionally, no quantitative framework exists to evaluate whether multimodal diagnostic models actually attend to guideline-relevant indicators and lesion regions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a radiology image (chest X-ray, fundus photo, dermatology image, or 3D MRI) paired with a text input combining the clinical report and EHR laboratory values, where numeric lab results are quantized to a 0–10 integer scale relative to their normal range limits. It outputs a multi-label disease prediction vector (m diseases, e.g., 53 ICD-10 codes) along with cross-attention maps that serve as interpretability evidence: textual attention weights over guideline-specified lab indicators and spatial attention maps over image regions for visual grounding evaluation.

## Method

RAD operates through three synergistic components spanning the full diagnostic pipeline. First, a retrieval-and-refinement stage uses MedCPT to retrieve top-k documents per disease from four medical sources (Wikipedia, PubMed, clinical guidelines, textbooks), then Qwen2.5-72B summarizes them into a ~2000-character structured guideline. Second, a Guideline-Enhanced Contrastive Loss (GECL) aligns pooled visual and textual features with disease-guideline prototypes in a shared latent space via a supervised contrastive objective, pulling sample features toward positive-disease guideline anchors while repelling from negatives. Third, a dual transformer decoder performs cross-modal fusion: one branch uses the encoded guideline as query and the other uses disease names as query, both attending over concatenated image+text embeddings as key/value, with binary cross-entropy applied to both branches. The total training loss combines both BCE branches with a weighted GECL term, and the framework is evaluated with a novel dual-axis interpretability system measuring Guideline Recall (textual indicator attention) and visual grounding mIoU against expert-annotated lesion boxes.

## Results

RAD achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four datasets spanning chest, eye, skin, and brain anatomies. On MIMIC-ICD53 (53 diseases, 51,830 samples), RAD attains an average score of 57.28 (F1=39.71, AUC=93.00, mAP=36.74, Acc-S=42.33), outperforming the second-best method (KAD, 54.19) by 3.09 points. On the single-label FairVLMed eye dataset, RAD reaches 86.63 average (F1=84.30, AUC=91.32), and on SkinCAP (50 skin diseases) it achieves 88.64 average (F1=85.48, AUC=97.97). On the brain MRI dataset NACC, RAD scores 58.12 average (F1=37.65, AUC=87.11), consistently leading all baselines by 2.2–3.1 points in average score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAD outperforms all five multimodal fusion/pretraining baselines (MedFuse, BiomedCLIP, KAD, DrFuse, HEALNet) on every dataset and every reported metric. The largest margin is on MIMIC-ICD53, where RAD improves F1 by 3.39 points and Precision by 5.27 points over KAD (the strongest baseline there), reflecting better handling of complex multi-label chest X-ray diagnosis. On FairVLMed and SkinCAP, HEALNet is the closest competitor, but RAD still leads by 2.24 and 2.49 points in average score respectively, with particularly strong gains in mAP (+1.43 and +3.15). On NACC, KAD is again the runner-up, and RAD leads by 2.45 points in average, with a notable 7.32-point Precision advantage. In the MLLM comparison (Figure 5), RAD's discriminative framework surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-7B, HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B, and Lingshu-7B on the FairVLMed VQA task under both few-shot and fine-tuning settings, while requiring significantly less computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAD carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: multimodal medical fusion (MedFuse, HEALNet, DrFuse, BiomedCLIP) and medical knowledge injection (KAD, DRAGON, PubMedBERT). Prior multimodal methods perform cross-modal alignment without explicit disease-level guidance, while prior knowledge-injection methods confine knowledge to the pretraining phase or use online retrieval for generative QA tasks. RAD departs from both by performing offline retrieval of disease-specific guidelines and embedding them structurally into the discriminative pipeline—as contrastive anchors in the latent space and as queries in a dual transformer decoder—rather than treating knowledge as a one-time pretraining signal or an input-side augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in clinical AI deployment: black-box multimodal models lack traceable, guideline-grounded decision pathways that clinicians can audit. The dual-axis interpretability evaluation system (textual guideline recall + visual grounding IoU) is a useful contribution that the field has lacked for multimodal diagnostic models. Key limitations include the static knowledge corpus requiring manual updates as guidelines evolve, and the dependence on LLM-based refinement whose quality is only partially validated. Open questions include whether the retrieval-and-refine pipeline scales to hundreds of ICD codes without manual verification, and whether the approach transfers to settings where no structured guideline exists for a given disease.
