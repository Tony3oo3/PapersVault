## Problem

Action-minimizing problems over probability densities—encompassing constrained optimal transport, stochastic optimal control, and mean-field games—require solving infinite-dimensional PDE-constrained optimizations that are computationally intractable in high dimensions. Existing neural approaches (GSBM, APAC-Net, NLOT) handle specific problem classes but lack a unified parametric framework that flexibly accommodates diverse potential terms (obstacles, interactions, Fisher information) while maintaining both accuracy and computational efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes initial and terminal densities ρ₀, ρ₁ (specified via samplers), a potential function F combining external, internal, and interaction terms, and a reference density λ (typically standard normal, but can be set to ρ₀ for exact boundary matching). The output is not a single trained network but a collection of K+2 Neural ODE models whose parameters lie on a cubic Hermite spline trajectory, defining a continuous density path ρ(t) = (T_{θ(t)})_#λ. Boundary parameters θ₀, θ₁ are pre-trained via Flow Matching and are reusable across problems sharing the same marginal distributions, amortizing their training cost.

## Method

PDPO reformulates the dynamic Benamou-Brenier action-minimization as a static coupling problem in the parameter space of a Neural ODE transport map T_θ, then approximates the optimal parameter curve θ(t) with a cubic Hermite spline using only 3–5 control points. The key theoretical contribution is proving equivalence between the dynamic and static formulations (Theorem 1) and establishing an O(h^{κ−1}) error bound on the action from spline interpolation (Theorem 2). Optimization alternates between updating interior control points to minimize the action (via Monte Carlo + trapezoidal integration with automatic differentiation) and updating boundary parameters to improve coupling quality (via a penalized Flow Matching loss). This bilevel scheme, combined with the spline's ability to exploit parameter-space geometry (e.g., curving around obstacles that linear interpolation would cross), yields action-minimizing paths with substantially fewer control points and faster runtimes than GSBM.

## Results

PDPO achieves the lowest action across all three obstacle-avoidance benchmarks: 39.90±0.16 on SCC, 148.91±0.72 on VNEFI, and 79.46±0.62 on GMM. Boundary accuracy (W₂ distance) is substantially improved, e.g., (0.028, 0.014) on SCC versus (0.21, 0.25) for GSBM, and (0, 0.359) on GMM versus (0.806, 0.258) for GSBM. Runtime is reduced by 40–93%: 11 min vs. 21 min (SCC), 70 min vs. 115 min (VNEFI), and 8 min vs. 123 min (GMM). The opinion depolarization task in 1000 dimensions completes in 23 minutes versus over 5 hours for GSBM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDPO outperforms GSBM [20] on all three benchmarks in both action and runtime: on GMM the action gap is ~10% (79.46 vs. 88.77) with a 93% speedup (8 min vs. 123 min); on VNEFI the action is ~5% lower (148.91 vs. 156.80) with a 39% speedup; on SCC the action is marginally lower (39.90 vs. 40.02) but boundary W₂ accuracy improves by an order of magnitude (0.028/0.014 vs. 0.21/0.25). Against APAC-Net [17] on SCC, PDPO achieves a slightly lower action (39.90 vs. 39.95) and substantially better terminal boundary accuracy (W₂=0.014 vs. 0.093), while both methods violate the obstacle constraint. Against NLOT [29] on GMM, PDPO achieves a lower action (79.46 vs. 82.06) and is 14× faster (8 min vs. 117 min), though NLOT reports a near-zero potential energy term (0.006) reflecting its restriction to linear potentials. In the 1000-dimensional opinion depolarization task, PDPO matches GSBM's solution quality (comparable PCA projections and cosine-similarity histograms) while completing in 23 minutes versus over 5 hours.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDPO extends the parametric Wasserstein dynamics line of work (Wu et al. 2025, Liu et al. 2022, Jin et al. 2025) from initial-value problems (gradient flows, Hamiltonian systems) to boundary-valued action-minimizing problems, and departs from GSBM (Liu et al. 2024) and NLOT (Pooladian et al. 2024) by placing the cubic spline in the parameter space of a Neural ODE rather than at the particle or Gaussian-moment level. The static coupling formulation (Theorem 1) is presented as novel relative to the dynamic Benamou-Brenier formulation, and the closest prior parametric action-minimization work (Bunne et al. 2023) is restricted to Gaussian families. Flow Matching (Lipman et al. 2023) is repurposed as both a boundary-initialization tool and the dissimilarity metric in the coupling optimization step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from infinite-dimensional density-path optimization to a finite-dimensional spline problem over 3–5 Neural ODE control points offers a practical and theoretically grounded route to constrained transport that is 40–80% faster than GSBM while matching or slightly improving action values. Key limitations include the restriction to MLP-based NODEs (precluding image-scale transport), the strong Lipschitz assumptions in the error bound (Theorem 4), and the demonstrated scalability ceiling of 50 dimensions for Fisher Information problems. Open questions include relaxing the global Lipschitz condition on T, developing unbiased estimators for ∇log(ρ), and extending the framework to CNN-based architectures for high-dimensional data.
