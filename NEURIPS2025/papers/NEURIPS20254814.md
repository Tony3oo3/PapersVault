## Problem

Existing continual model merging methods suffer from severe parameter interference between tasks (causing catastrophic forgetting) and cannot adapt to evolving test distributions, because they rely on static, input-agnostic merging rules. The paper formalizes Test-Time Continual Model Merging (TTCMM), a new paradigm that introduces a small set of unlabeled test samples at each merging stage to simultaneously mitigate task conflicts and handle distribution shift, without ever accessing original training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254814/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a shared pre-trained backbone (CLIP ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14, or Flan-T5-base), a sequence of independently fine-tuned models, and 5 unlabeled seed samples per class (vision) or 100 total (NLP) from the current task at each merging step. It produces a single merged model with per-task LoRA experts (rank 64) and learned gating functions that outputs class labels across the union of all task label spaces. No training data from any prior task is available during merging, and adaptation runs for only 50 gradient steps per task.

## Method

MINGLE constructs a mixture-of-experts architecture where each arriving task contributes a low-rank expert (built by orthogonally projecting the task vector onto the complement of prior expert directions, then applying truncated SVD) and a linear gating function. The key mechanism is Null-Space Constrained Gating: during test-time adaptation, gradient updates to the new gate are projected onto the null space of cached dominant feature subspaces from all prior tasks, suppressing activations on old-task inputs. An Adaptive Relaxation Strategy replaces the hard null-space projector with soft per-direction shrinkage coefficients (λ = exp(−γS)) learned online via an exponential moving average of instantaneous interference signals, smoothly interpolating between full protection and unconstrained updates. Test-time adaptation minimizes KL divergence between the merged model's predictions and the individual fine-tuned model's predictions on the seed samples, with only the current gate being updated while all prior experts and gates remain frozen.

## Results

On the primary vision benchmark (CLIP ViT-B/16, 8 tasks), MINGLE achieves 88.3% average accuracy with only −0.4% backward transfer, outperforming the best non-adaptive baseline OPCM (81.8%, −4.8% BWT) by 6.5 points in accuracy and 4.4 points in BWT. Scaling to 20 tasks, MINGLE retains 81.9% ACC with −1.9% BWT, while OPCM drops to 70.3% (−6.3% BWT) and TTA-based baselines (LoRA-WEMOE, AdaMerging) collapse to 55.0% and 61.5% respectively. On the NLP benchmark (Flan-T5-base, 8 GLUE tasks), MINGLE reaches 83.3% ACC with +0.1% BWT, surpassing LoRA-WEMOE (81.4%) and OPCM (80.6%). The paper reports a consistent 7–9% average improvement over prior SOTA across all backbones and task orders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MINGLE dominates all 14 baselines across every backbone and task count. Against the strongest non-adaptive method OPCM [70], MINGLE gains +6.5 to +11.5 points in ACC (e.g., 88.3 vs 81.8 on ViT-B/16, 8 tasks) while reducing BWT from −4.8 to −0.4. Against the best TTA-based merging methods, the gap is even larger: C. LoRA-WEMOE [68] achieves only 72.6% ACC with −18.0% BWT, and C. LW AdaMerging [87] drops to 59.9% with −27.8% BWT on the same setting, showing that naively applying multi-task TTA merging to a continual pipeline causes severe forgetting. Among static merging baselines, C. Task Arithmetic [24] (77.1%), MagMax-Ind [41] (76.7%), and Average/SWA [26] (72.3%) all trail MINGLE by 6–16 points. On the NLP benchmark (Table 2), MINGLE (83.3% ACC, +0.1% BWT) edges out LoRA-WEMOE (81.4%, +0.1%) and OPCM (80.6%, −2.5%), while Task Arithmetic [24] and Ties-Merging [86] fall further behind at 77.6% and 75.6% respectively. The lightweight MINGLE* variant (experts on attn.qkv and mlp.fc1 only) matches the full model within 0.5–1.5 points, confirming that the gains are not driven by parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

MINGLE sits at the intersection of three research threads: continual model merging (CMM), test-time adaptation (TTA), and mixture-of-experts architectures. It directly builds on OPCM's orthogonal projection for constructing low-rank experts from task vectors, and on LoRA for parameter-efficient expert representation. The key departure is from WEMOE and AdaMerging, which apply TTA-based MoE merging in a multi-task setting where all models and test data are simultaneously available; MINGLE reformulates this for the sequential, data-free continual setting where only a few unlabeled seed samples from the current task are accessible. The null-space constrained gating mechanism is novel, projecting gradient updates away from subspaces spanned by prior task features to prevent interference without requiring access to old-task data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing TTCMM and showing that near-zero forgetting (BWT ≈ 0) is achievable with only 5 unlabeled samples per class and ~10 seconds of adaptation per task, MINGLE offers a practical path to continual model merging in privacy-sensitive or distributed settings where training data is unavailable. The finding that existing TTA-based merging methods (WEMOE, AdaMerging) catastrophically fail when naively applied to the continual setting (dropping to 3–4% accuracy) highlights that the sequential, data-free constraint is fundamentally different from multi-task fusion and cannot be addressed by existing designs. Open questions include scalability to very long task sequences (beyond 20 tasks), applicability to generative or autoregressive models, and whether the null-space constraint generalizes to non-linear expert interactions.
