## Problem

Existing text-to-image models produce RGB images with baked-in lighting, making them unusable for PBR workflows in gaming, VR, and relighting. Intrinsic image decomposition methods that recover PBR maps from RGB inputs are constrained to match the input appearance and generalize poorly to out-of-distribution scenes. No prior method directly generates physically-based rendering maps from text in a single probabilistic process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252605/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and jointly produces four aligned PBR maps: a 3-channel albedo, a 3-channel normal map, and a 3-channel image encoding roughness (channel 1), metallic (channel 2), and a zero channel (channel 3) for VAE compatibility. All maps are generated in the latent space of Flux.1-dev and decoded to pixel space, where they can be rendered under arbitrary lighting via a Disney BRDF deferred shading pipeline. A conditional variant additionally accepts a rendered normal map (or RGB image) to guide generation for scene texturing.

## Method

IntrinsiX converts a pretrained Flux.1-dev diffusion transformer into a PBR generator through a two-stage LoRA fine-tuning process. Stage 1 trains three separate LoRA adapters (rank 64) on small curated datasets—20 samples for albedo/normal and 20K for roughness/metallic—using conditional flow matching, preserving the T2I prior's diversity. Stage 2 jointly fine-tunes all LoRAs by replacing self-attention with cross-intrinsic attention (concatenating key/value features across the three batch elements in every DiT block) and introducing a rendering loss that evaluates the Disney BRDF on decoded PBR maps with importance-sampled directional lights (pixel selection weighted by inverse roughness, light direction set to the reflected view direction). Dropout (p=0.25) on cross-intrinsic attention preserves individual modality priors, and the rendering loss is not backpropagated to the normal LoRA to avoid material-geometry ambiguity.

## Results

IntrinsiX achieves the best out-of-distribution albedo FID (A-OOD-FID) of 71.39, substantially outperforming IID (98.77), RGBX (90.12), ColorfulShading (86.48), and even IID retrained with FLUX-LoRA (79.29). In the user study, IntrinsiX is preferred for albedo quality 67.36% of the time (vs. 14.24% for IID and 15.63% for RGBX), and achieves the highest scores on rendered image quality (R-PQ 3.93/5), specularity quality (S-PQ 3.62/5), and prompt coherence (PC 4.62/5). On in-distribution FID (A-ID-FID), RGBX achieves a slightly lower score (61.36 vs. 72.09), reflecting its training on the same synthetic indoor distribution, but IntrinsiX's advantage is most pronounced on out-of-distribution prompts where it generalizes far better.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IntrinsiX outperforms all three baselines on the critical out-of-distribution metric (A-OOD-FID): 71.39 vs. 98.77 (IID), 90.12 (RGBX), and 86.48 (ColorfulShading), a margin of 15–27 FID points. In the user study, IntrinsiX is overwhelmingly preferred for albedo quality (67.36% vs. 14.24% for IID and 15.63% for RGBX), and leads on rendered image quality (3.93 vs. 2.95/2.96), specularity quality (3.62 vs. 2.82/2.57), and prompt coherence (4.62 vs. 4.47/4.33). The only metric where a baseline is competitive is in-distribution FID, where RGBX achieves 61.36 vs. IntrinsiX's 72.09, but this reflects RGBX's training on the same InteriorVerse distribution rather than a fundamental quality advantage. ColorfulShading is only comparable on albedo metrics since it decomposes into albedo and shading without full PBR properties, making it unsuitable for relighting or specular editing. The paper also shows that retraining IID with the same FLUX-LoRA architecture (IID w/ FLUX-LoRA) still yields worse OOD FID (79.29) than IntrinsiX, confirming that the two-stage PBR prior training and cross-intrinsic attention are the key differentiators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the intrinsic image decomposition line of work (IID, RGBX, ColorfulShading), which requires an RGB input image and is constrained to match its appearance, by instead directly generating PBR maps from text in a diffusion process. It repurposes the strong image prior of Flux.1-dev through a two-stage LoRA training scheme: first learning per-modality priors on small curated datasets, then aligning them via a novel cross-intrinsic attention mechanism inspired by multi-view diffusion. The scene texturing application builds on SceneTex's score-distillation framework, substituting the RGB diffusion model with the PBR-generating variant and adapting the distillation loss to flow matching (VFDS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing PBR maps directly from text rather than decomposing a shaded RGB image, the method removes the fundamental constraint that limits decomposition-based approaches on out-of-distribution inputs, enabling relighting, material editing, and for the first time room-scale PBR scene texturing from text alone. Key limitations include reliance on a screen-space Disney BRDF renderer (a neural renderer could improve quality), reduced compositional diversity compared to the base T2I model, and training data limited to indoor scenes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to outdoor or object-scale settings, whether a billion-scale PBR dataset would further improve generalization, and whether the cross-intrinsic attention formulation extends to additional material properties (e.g., clearcoat, transmission).
