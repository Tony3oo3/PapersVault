## Problem

Existing concept removal methods for text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., ESD, UCE, MACE) degrade text-image alignment progressively when concepts must be removed one after another in a continuous sequence. This 'entity forgetting' problem arises because removing one concept shifts the semantic space, and repeated removals compound the misalignment, making the model unable to faithfully follow prompts for non-target entities. No prior method can continuously excise multiple concepts while preserving generation quality.

## Input / Output

CCRT takes a frozen original diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.4 or SD-XL), a sequence of target concepts to remove (artist styles, improper content categories, IP characters, or objects), and an ImageNet-initialized entity set. It produces an iteratively edited diffusion model where each removal step (~3 GPU-hours of light fine-tuning) builds on the previously edited model rather than the original. The calibration prompt set is generated internally via a genetic algorithm operating on ImageNet class entities, with LLM (GPT-4) used for synonym mutation and prompt composition.

## Method

CCRT formulates continuous concept removal as a knowledge distillation problem with two jointly optimized losses: a removal loss (adapted from ESD's negative guidance direction) that drives the edited model to ignore the target concept, and a regularization loss (MSE between student and teacher noise predictions on calibration prompts) that anchors the edited model's semantic space to the original. The key innovation is the calibration prompt set generation mechanism: a genetic algorithm with LLM-enhanced fuzzing (crossover over ImageNet semantic hierarchy + mutation_fuzzing for synonym replacement and entity augmentation) iteratively mines 'hard' entities ranked by Misalignment Distance (MD), which serve as semantic anchors to stabilize alignment. The process is memoryless and iterative—each new concept removal initializes from the previously edited model and re-runs the full distillation with a freshly optimized calibration set, enabling dynamic, sequential removal without retraining from scratch.

## Results

On continuous artistic style removal (Van Gogh → Picasso → Monet → Cezanne), CCRT achieves an average RR-CLS of 0.753 and RR-LLM of 0.874, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline MACE (0.406 / 0.150). CCRT also improves the CLIP-Score from 21.698 (ESD) to 25.005 while maintaining an average removal rate of 0.87 across four consecutive removals. On continuous improper content removal (eroticism → violence → self-harm), CCRT reaches RR-CLS of 0.99, 0.95, and 0.97 respectively, and on IP removal (Spider Man → Super Mario → Iron Man) achieves RR-LLM of 0.87, 0.94, and 0.96.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCRT is compared against six baselines: unmodified Stable Diffusion (SD), UCE, MACE, SPM, ESD, and AdvUn. Against the three 'weak' baselines (UCE, MACE, SPM), CCRT achieves a 3.5× improvement in average RR-CLS (0.753 vs. 0.215/0.406/0.304) and a 5.8× improvement in RR-LLM (0.874 vs. 0.046/0.150/0.154), indicating these methods fail to remove deeply embedded concepts like 'Van Gogh' in a continuous setting. Against the strongest baseline ESD, CCRT achieves comparable concept removal (human evaluation ranks CCRT 1.41–1.70 vs. ESD 1.31–1.73 on Concept Removal) but decisively wins on text-image alignment (CLIP-Score 25.005 vs. 21.698), other concept preservation, and image quality in the human study. AdvUn, another strong baseline, similarly suffers from text-image misalignment during continuous removal, which CCRT avoids via its distillation-based regularization. The unmodified SD baseline shows near-zero removal (RR-CLS ≈ 0.119), confirming that no removal occurs without intervention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ESD (Gandikota et al., ICCV 2023) for its removal loss formulation, adapting the negative guidance direction to an iterative setting. It departs from the broader concept-erasure literature (UCE, MACE, SPM, AdvUn) by introducing a knowledge distillation paradigm that simultaneously erases target concepts and preserves text-image alignment via a frozen teacher model. The calibration prompt set generation via genetic algorithm with LLM-enhanced fuzzing is a novel component not present in prior erasure methods, motivated by the observation that random calibration prompts produce unstable alignment. The key conceptual departure is framing removal as a sequential, memoryless process where each step builds on the previously edited model rather than the original.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous concept removal problem is practically motivated—model operators face ongoing, sequential takedown requests from artists and safety teams—and the paper provides the first method that handles this without catastrophic degradation of text-image alignment. The identification of 'entity forgetting' as a distinct failure mode of iterative erasure is useful for the field. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4 for the fuzzing stage, evaluation primarily on SD v1.4 (with limited SD-XL results), and the open question of how the approach scales when hundreds of concepts must be removed sequentially. The ~3 GPU-hours per removal step is practical but the cumulative cost over many removals remains a concern.
