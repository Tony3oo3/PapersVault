## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for the Best Response (fictitious play) flow on the Wasserstein space of probability measures require the objective functional to be convex, which excludes important applications such as policy optimization with softmax-parametrized mean-field neural networks where the normalization constant renders the objective non-convex. This paper asks whether the convexity assumption can be relaxed by making a specific choice of the entropic regularizer, and characterizes precisely how the degree of non-convexity, the regularization parameter, and the tail behaviour of the reference measure must be balanced to ensure convergence.

## Input / Output

The method operates on probability measures over R^d (the parameter space of a mean-field neural network) and requires the reference measure ξ to have a density e^{-U^ξ} with at least linear growth, while the regularization parameter σ must exceed a threshold (condition 10) that scales with the Lipschitz constant of the flat derivative of F and the first moment of ξ. In the MDP application, the input is the full MDP tuple (state/action spaces, transition kernel, cost, discount factor) plus the activation function f, and the output is a probability measure ν* on the network parameters whose induced softmax policy π* is the unique optimal policy; in the two-player min-max setting the output is a pair (ν*, μ*) constituting the unique mixed Nash equilibrium.

## Method

The key technical contribution is proving that the Best Response operator Ψ_σ, defined via the exponential tilting of the reference measure by the flat derivative of F, is a W₁-contraction whenever σ exceeds 2C_F + e(e+1)L_F·E_ξ[|x|], where C_F and L_F bound the flat derivative and its Lipschitz constant. This contraction property, established through a careful estimate of the W₁ distance between Ψ_σ[ν] and Ψ_σ[ν'] that exploits the first moment of ξ, guarantees via Banach's fixed point theorem a unique fixed point that coincides with the unique global minimizer of the regularized functional. The Best Response flow dν_t = α(Ψ_σ[ν_t] − ν_t)dt then converges exponentially in W₁ at rate α(1 − L_{Ψ_σ,U^ξ}), with a stability estimate quantifying sensitivity to both the initial condition and σ. For the min-max extension, the joint operator (Ψ_{σ_ν}, Φ_{σ_μ}) is shown to be a contraction on the product space under analogous conditions that additionally involve the learning rates α_ν and α_μ, yielding exponential convergence to the unique MNE without requiring convex-concavity of F or uniform bounds on second-order flat derivatives.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The headline results are: (1) Theorem 2.3 shows the Best Response operator Ψ_σ is an L¹-Wasserstein contraction whenever σ > 2C_F + e(e+1)L_F ∫|x|e^{-U^ξ(x)}dx, linking the required regularization to the non-convexity of F and the tail behavior of the reference measure ξ. (2) Theorem 2.6 establishes exponential convergence of the Best Response flow to the unique global minimizer at rate O(e^{-α(1-L_{Ψ_σ,U^ξ})t}) in W₁ distance. (3) Theorem 3.3 extends this to the two-player min-max setting, proving exponential convergence to the unique mixed Nash equilibrium at rate O(e^{-t(min{α_ν,α_μ} − (α_ν L_{Ψ_{σ_ν},U^ξ} + α_μ L_{Φ_{σ_μ},U^ρ})})} in the product W₁ metric, without requiring equal learning rates or equal regularization parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper makes no empirical comparisons but provides detailed theoretical comparisons to four prior works. Compared to [8] (Chen et al., JMLR 2023), which handles convex F with arbitrary σ > 0 and arbitrary reference measure, this paper relaxes convexity to non-convex F at the cost of requiring σ to exceed a threshold (10) that depends on the non-convexity constant L_F and the first moment of ξ; additionally, this paper provides an explicit exponential convergence rate in W₁, whereas [8] establishes convergence without a rate. Compared to [24] (Leahy et al., ICML 2022), which uses the Wasserstein gradient flow and requires U^ξ to be strongly convex with bounded Hessian (essentially forcing U^ξ(x) = |x|²), this paper only requires U^ξ to be bounded below with at least linear growth, and achieves a comparable exponential rate under weaker regularity assumptions on F (no bounded Hessian needed). Compared to [23] (Lascu et al., AMO 2025), which handles convex-concave F with equal learning rates α_ν = α_μ and equal regularization σ_ν = σ_μ, this paper removes the convexity-concavity assumption, the uniform bound on second-order flat derivatives, and allows distinct α_ν, α_μ and σ_ν, σ_μ. Compared to [21] (Kim et al., ICLR 2024), which covers convex-concave two-player Markov games without policy parametrization, this paper extends to non-convex-non-concave objectives with softmax-parametrized policies in the mean-field regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Best Response (fictitious play) flow analysis from Chen et al. [8] (convex single-agent) and Lascu et al. [23] (convex-concave min-max) to the non-convex and non-convex-non-concave regimes, by showing that the convexity assumption on F can be replaced with a specific lower bound on the regularization parameter σ that depends on the reference measure's first moment. It also provides an alternative to the Wasserstein gradient flow approach of Leahy et al. [24] for entropy-regularized MDPs with softmax policies, achieving comparable convergence rates under weaker regularity assumptions on the potential U^ξ. In the min-max setting, it generalizes Kim et al. [21] from convex-concave Markov games without policy parametrization to non-convex-non-concave games with softmax parametrized policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first convergence guarantee for the Best Response flow in the non-convex mean-field regime, which is directly relevant to training two-layer neural networks and policy optimization in MDPs where the softmax parametrization inherently breaks convexity. The key open question is the discrete-time analysis: the continuous-time condition (10) does not directly translate to the Euler discretization used in practice, and the interaction between σ and step sizes remains uncharacterized. Additionally, the requirement that σ be sufficiently large (satisfying condition 10) may be restrictive in practice, as it couples the regularization strength to the tail behavior of the reference measure, limiting the range of applicable settings.
