## Problem

Global convergence of finite-width neural networks under gradient flow remains poorly understood outside the lazy (NTK) and infinite-width (mean-field) regimes. Prior analyses relying on orthogonality of inputs or finely tuned initialization scales do not capture the natural high-dimensional setting where inputs are merely weakly correlated. This paper asks: under what natural data conditions does a shallow ReLU network with only O(log n) neurons provably converge to a global interpolator, and at what rate?

## Input / Output

The method takes n regression pairs (x_i, y_i) ∈ R^d × R with inputs bounded away from zero, a one-hidden-layer ReLU network with p neurons, and an asymmetric initialization (|a_j(0)| ≥ ||w_j(0)||). The key structural constraint is that the Gram matrix satisfies ||X^T X − D_X|| < (C_x^-)²/(2√n) · C_y^-/C_y^+, which holds with high probability when d ≳ n² for whitened sub-Gaussian data. The output is a high-probability guarantee that the loss decays exponentially at rate ≥ C/n, plus refined upper bounds of order 1/√n and a phase-transition characterization in the orthogonal case.

## Method

The core technique is a trajectory-wise Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) analysis: defining the local-PL curvature μ(t) = p||∇L(θ_t)||²/L(θ_t) along the gradient-flow trajectory, the paper shows μ(t) ≥ (C/n)·min_i|1 − r_i(t)/y_i| under the weak-correlation assumption, yielding exponential convergence. The proof rests on three pillars: (i) the loss strictly decreases as long as each data point is activated by at least one neuron, (ii) a correctly initialized neuron for data point i remains active throughout the dynamics (an invariance argument using the asymmetric norm), and (iii) with p ≥ 4 log(n/ε) neurons, every data point is correctly initialized with probability ≥ 1−ε. For orthogonal inputs, the analysis is sharpened to bound μ(t) above by O(√(p/n)), and a group-initialization construction shows that any intermediate rate 1/n^α for α ∈ [1/2, 1] is achievable, with a phase transition from 1/n to 1/√n occurring in relative time O(1/log n).

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes global convergence of a one-hidden-layer ReLU network trained by gradient flow with square loss, requiring only p ≥ 4 log(4n/ε)(1 + (C_{a,w} C_x⁺/C_y⁺)²) neurons (i.e., O(log n)) in dimension d ≳ n², with an exponential convergence rate of at least C/n. In the orthogonal data case, the local-PL curvature is bounded between 1/n and 1/√n (Propositions 1–2), and a phase transition in the convergence rate is identified with a transition window of relative width O(1/log n) (Theorem 2). Empirically, for d = 100, the convergence probability drops sharply from near 1 to near 0 around n ≈ 3000, consistent with a threshold scaling as N(d,p) ≃ C(p)·d, and log-log plots of the asymptotic PL curvature confirm a slope of approximately −1/2, supporting the conjectured 1/√n rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct head-to-head method comparisons; the 'baselines' are prior theoretical regimes. The paper's O(log n) neuron requirement contrasts sharply with the polynomial-in-n neuron count of the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) regime [Jacot et al., 2018; Allen-Zhu et al., 2019] and the infinite-width requirement of the mean-field regime [Chizat and Bach, 2018; Mei et al., 2018]. Compared to the orthogonal-data analysis of Boursier et al. [2022], the present work extends convergence guarantees to the more realistic weakly-correlated (near-orthogonal) setting that arises naturally when d ≳ n², while retaining the same O(log n) neuron sufficiency. The asymmetric-norm initialization assumption (Assumption 1) is shared with Boursier and Flammarion [2024a,b], but here it is shown to be sufficient for global convergence under low-correlation data without requiring any specific initialization scale, unlike the NTK regime which demands a particular small-scale initialization. The paper does not claim to outperform any competing optimization method; rather, it provides the first rigorous global convergence guarantee for finite-width shallow ReLU networks in a regime that is neither lazy nor infinite-width.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of shallow ReLU networks from the perfectly orthogonal data setting of Boursier et al. (2022) to the more natural regime of weakly correlated inputs in high dimensions, and from the infinite-width mean-field regime of Chizat and Bach (2018) to a finite width of order log(n). It leverages the asymmetric norm initialization and early alignment results of Boursier and Flammarion (2024a,b) to show that high dimensionality (d ≳ n²) makes the early alignment phenomenon benign, enabling global convergence without the polynomial width of the NTK regime or the infinite width of the mean-field regime. The Polyak-Lojasiewicz trajectory-wise viewpoint provides a unified lens to quantify convergence speed across regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first quantitative convergence guarantee for finite-width shallow networks in a regime that arises naturally from high-dimensional data geometry rather than from special initialization scales or infinite width, bridging a gap between the NTK and mean-field analyses. The identification of a phase transition in the convergence rate (from 1/n to 1/√n) and the conjecture that the asymptotic rate is 1/√n with high probability open concrete questions about what governs the speed of feature learning. Key limitations include the restrictive d ≳ n² requirement (versus the d ≳ n that would be ideal), the one-hidden-layer scope, and the reliance on gradient flow rather than discrete optimization, leaving open whether these results extend to deeper architectures, SGD, or lower-dimensional settings.
