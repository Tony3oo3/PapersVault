## Problem

Existing multi-agent reinforcement learning methods for safety-critical autonomous systems either lack formal safety guarantees (flat policies with safety embedded in rewards) or provide only statistical, trajectory-level safety via CMDPs rather than pointwise-in-time constraints that must hold at every step. Hierarchical MARL reduces sample complexity but has not been combined with safety mechanisms. This paper addresses the gap by providing a framework that guarantees pointwise-in-time safety for every agent on every trajectory while still learning cooperative behavior at scale in partially observable settings.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a partial observation comprising its own state (position, velocity), the states of neighboring agents within sensor range, and relevant environment state (road network topology); in the lidar-based environments, observations are derived from lidar sensor readings. The output is two-tiered: the high-level policy emits a discrete skill selection per agent, and the low-level policy produces continuous control actions (acceleration and steering) by solving a quadratic program at every timestep. The QP parameters are themselves learned by RL, so the low-level 'policy' is a parametric optimization rather than a direct neural network output, enabling hard safety constraints to be enforced as QP inequalities rather than soft reward penalties.

## Method

HMARL-CBF decomposes the safe multi-agent problem into a bi-level RL hierarchy under a CTDE scheme. The high-level policy (trained with MAPPO or QMIX) learns a joint cooperative policy over a discrete set of skills per agent, using an extrinsic reward that includes task cost and safety-violation penalties. The low-level policy executes each selected skill by solving a per-timestep quadratic program whose objective minimizes tracking error and control effort, subject to CBF constraints (ensuring forward invariance of the safe set) and CLF constraints (driving the agent toward the skill's termination set). The QP parameters (cost matrix, linear term, CBF/CLF tuning functions) are parameterized by neural networks conditioned on the agent's observation and the active skill, and are trained via policy gradients with the reparameterization trick to allow stochastic exploration. This design yields pointwise-in-time safety guarantees through the CBF forward-invariance theorem at every step of both training and deployment, while the hierarchical skill decomposition reduces the effective action space and accelerates convergence compared to flat MARL baselines.

## Results

HMARL-CBF achieves a near-perfect success/safety rate of ≥97% across all five METADRIVE environments (Merging: 99%, Intersection: 97%, Roundabout: 99%, Bottleneck: 99%, Tollgate: 99%), dramatically outperforming all baselines which range from 0% to 85%. The method also achieves the best success-weighted time (e.g., 166.70 steps in Merging vs. 531–762 for baselines) and energy (e.g., 13.92 in Merging vs. 13.81–33.96 for baselines) across all environments. Ablation studies confirm that removing the hierarchical structure, CBF constraints, or cooperative high-level policy causes success rates to drop to 0–68%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HMARL-CBF decisively outperforms all seven baselines on success rate across every environment. Against the CMDP-based methods MAPPO-Lagrangian and MACPO [24], the gap is most dramatic: these methods achieve only 0–34% success (e.g., 0.02% in Roundabout, 0.00% in Tollgate) while HMARL-CBF reaches 97–99%, confirming that trajectory-level expected-cost constraints are insufficient for pointwise-in-time safety. The flat MARL baselines MFPO [80] and CoPo [49] achieve moderate success (43–85%) but still fall 14–99 percentage points short of HMARL-CBF, and they also require 2000+ hours of pre-trained driving data. IPPO [14] and Curriculum Learning [45] (evaluated in the Bottleneck environment) achieve 9–58% success with penalty factors of 1–10, showing that simply increasing safety penalties does not yield reliable safety. In the lidar-based environments (Target, Spread, Target-Bicycle), HMARL-CBF outperforms DGPPO [85] and InforMARL [46] on both success/safety rate and reward (Figure 7). Notably, HMARL-CBF also achieves the best time and energy efficiency in most environments, indicating that the safety mechanism does not come at a performance cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: hierarchical multi-agent RL (skill-based decomposition à la Sutton's options and feudal architectures), safe RL via Control Barrier Functions (Ames et al., Cheng et al., Emam et al.), and CTDE multi-agent training (MAPPO, QMIX). Prior safe MARL work operates within the CMDP framework, enforcing trajectory-average constraints that lack pointwise-in-time guarantees, while prior CBF-RL methods are largely single-agent or rely on a fixed coordination policy. The key departure here is the joint training of a high-level cooperative skill-selection policy and a low-level CBF-constrained QP-based skill executor within a single hierarchical framework, so that safety is structurally enforced at every timestep rather than statistically over trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: safety-critical multi-agent systems (autonomous driving, UAV swarms) require hard, pointwise-in-time safety guarantees that CMDP-based methods cannot provide, especially in partially observable settings where decentralized execution is mandatory. The near-perfect safety rates (≥95%) achieved from the start of training, combined with faster convergence than flat-policy baselines, suggest that the hierarchical decomposition is not merely a safety add-on but a structurally beneficial factorization. Open questions include jointly learning the skill set alongside the policies (currently skills are hand-designed), scalability to very large agent counts, and validation on real hardware where model uncertainty would require robust CBF variants.
