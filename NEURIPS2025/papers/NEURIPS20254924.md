## Problem

Existing neural network fingerprinting methods lack a theoretically guaranteed resistance to fine-tuning: prior work either selected empirically stable parameters, boosted trigger-set confidence, or proved safe parameter-change radii, but none identified an intrinsically invariant quantity that provably survives gradient descent. This paper asks whether a mathematically certified fingerprint metric exists that is invariant to fine-tuning and equivariant to weight scaling and permutation attacks simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source DNN and a suspicious DNN, each containing a fingerprint module of D=256 convolutional filters (kernel size K=3) connected in parallel to the backbone. From each filter, a revised discrete Fourier transform extracts complex-valued frequency component vectors F_W^(uv) ∈ C^C at specific frequencies in the set S' = {(u,v) | u = ⌊iM/K⌉ or v = ⌊jN/K⌉, i,j ∈ {1,…,K−1}}. The output is a scalar fingerprint score ρ ∈ [0,1] computed via optimal permutation matching and cosine similarity of these frequency vectors, compared against a threshold t to yield a binary ownership verdict.

## Method

The core insight is that when a convolutional layer's input contains only low-frequency components (enforced by a low-pass filter Λ(·) in the fingerprint module), the change in each frequency component F_W^(uv) under a single gradient-descent step is proportional to sin(K(u−u')π/M)/sin((u−u')π/M) · sin(K(v−v')π/N)/sin((v−v')π/N), which vanishes exactly at u = iM/K or v = jN/K for integer i,j ∈ {1,…,K−1}. This yields Corollary 3.3 (exact invariance for pure fundamental-frequency inputs) and Proposition 3.4 (approximate invariance for low-frequency inputs with integer DFT frequencies). The fingerprint module is a parallel branch of low-pass filter + D conv filters; the fingerprint is the collection of frequency components at S' across all D filters. Detection finds the optimal permutation π maximizing the fraction of filter pairs whose frequency components have cosine similarity ≥ τ=0.99. An auxiliary loss L_attack adds a pseudo-classification term that penalizes the network for maintaining accuracy when the fingerprint module weights are perturbed, thereby making overwriting attacks costly to the model's utility.

## Results

Under combined attacks (permutation + scaling + fine-tuning), the proposed method achieves 90.0% model source tracing accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 100.0% on Imagenette at the most aggressive learning rate (1.0), where all baselines drop to 50–63%. At lower learning rates (10⁻³ and below), all methods converge to 100% accuracy, but the proposed method maintains 100% across all learning rates on both datasets. Individual attack experiments confirm fingerprint scores remain at 1.00 after fine-tuning, scaling (a=10 or 100), and permutation attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines under combined permutation + scaling + fine-tuning attacks. At the most aggressive fine-tuning learning rate (1.0) on CIFAR-10, the proposed method achieves 90.0% tracing accuracy, substantially outperforming RS (51.1%), MW (50.0%), ICS (56.7%), and CKA (55.0%)—all of which drop to near-random performance. On Imagenette at LR=10⁻¹, the gap is equally stark: Ours achieves 100.0% while RS falls to 63.3%, MW to 51.1%, ICS to 61.1%, and CKA to 55.6%. At lower learning rates (≤10⁻²), all methods recover to near-100% accuracy, narrowing the practical advantage. The proposed method is the only one to maintain 100% accuracy across all learning rates on both datasets, and it is the only method with a theoretical guarantee of invariance to fine-tuning rather than an empirical or engineering-based defense.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tang et al. (ICML 2023), which reformulated convolution as vector multiplication in the frequency domain using a revised discrete Fourier transform on the filter. The key departure is proving that specific frequency components of the filter are invariant under gradient descent when inputs are low-frequency, and equivariant to weight scaling and permutations simultaneously — a property no prior fingerprinting method (Bansal et al. 2022, Kim et al. 2023, Zeng et al. 2024, Zhang et al. 2024) had achieved. Prior work either relied on engineering heuristics (selecting stable parameters, boosting trigger confidence) or proved robustness only under bounded l_p-norm parameter changes, rather than identifying an intrinsically invariant quantity in the training dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretically guaranteed fingerprint robust to fine-tuning, where the invariance is intrinsic to the gradient update structure rather than dependent on bounded parameter perturbations. However, the practical scope is constrained: it applies only to convolutional layers, requires a parallel low-pass filter module to enforce the low-frequency input assumption, and the invariance is approximate (not exact) when frequencies must be integers. Open questions include whether the approach extends to transformers or attention-based architectures, whether it holds for deeper/wider networks, and whether the overwriting defense (auxiliary pseudo-category loss) generalizes beyond the tested settings. The experiments are preliminary, using only AlexNet and ResNet-18 on small datasets.
