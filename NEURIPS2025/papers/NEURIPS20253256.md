## Problem

Discrete diffusion models for language modeling lose informative signals during stochastic jumps between discrete states, limiting their iterative refinement capability. Existing continuous diffusion approaches for discrete data either apply unconstrained relaxation or operate on the probability simplex with imperfect assumptions, and the theoretical connection between discrete and continuous diffusion for categorical data remained unclear, hindering the development of a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253256/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For language modeling, the process starts from a mask token (or the barycenter of the simplex for uniform diffusion) reparameterized onto a (d−1)-dimensional hypersphere via the square-root mapping p_i → √p_i, and produces a full token sequence by running the SDE to the terminal time and taking argmax. For large vocabularies (e.g., LM1B with d=30522), dimension splitting decomposes each token index into base-b digits, mapping to a product manifold (S^b)^m to reduce per-token dimensionality. For conditional DNA generation, the model is additionally conditioned on a transcription profile.

## Method

RDLM establishes that the transition distribution of any discrete diffusion process is a categorical distribution, hence a point on the statistical manifold, and that discrete masked/uniform diffusion correspond to specific continuous flows on the hypersphere S^{d-1}. Building on this, it constructs a generative SDE as a mixture of logarithm bridge processes from a fixed initial point to each one-hot vector, weighted by the predicted probability p_{T|t}(e_k|X_t) learned by a diffusion transformer. Training is simulation-free: the intractable bridge transition distribution is approximated by a Riemannian normal whose parameters (α_t, ρ_t) are precomputed from 1D projected SDEs exploiting radial symmetry, yielding a ×50 speedup. A cross-entropy loss on the predicted endpoint probabilities serves as a tractable surrogate for the Girsanov-based likelihood bound, and a mixture path interpolating between masked and uniform diffusion further improves performance.

## Results

On Text8 (character-level LM), RDLM achieves ≤1.32 BPC, outperforming all discrete diffusion baselines (MD4 ≤1.37, MDLM ≤1.40, SEDD ≤1.39) and continuous diffusion baselines (BFN ≤1.41, Plaid ≤1.48). On the larger LM1B benchmark (vocab 30,522), RDLM reaches ≤28.44 PPL, competitive with the best discrete diffusion model MDLM (≤27.04) while significantly outperforming the prior continuous diffusion model Diffusion-LM (≤118.62). On CIFAR-10 pixel-level modeling, RDLM achieves the best BPD of ≤2.73, surpassing MD4 (≤2.78) and all autoregressive baselines. On promoter DNA sequence design, RDLM attains the lowest MSE of 0.027, beating Fisher-Flow (0.029) and DirichletFM (0.034).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Text8, RDLM (≤1.32 BPC) outperforms all discrete diffusion models—MD4 (≤1.37), MDLM (≤1.40), SEDD (≤1.39), D3PM Absorb (≤1.45)—as well as continuous diffusion baselines BFN (≤1.41) and Plaid (≤1.48), and any-order autoregressive models MAC (≤1.40) and ARDM (≤1.43). On LM1B, RDLM (≤28.44 PPL) is slightly behind the best discrete diffusion model MDLM (≤27.04) but substantially outperforms SEDD (≤32.79) and the prior continuous diffusion model Diffusion-LM (≤118.62), demonstrating that incorporating the Fisher-Rao geometry is critical for competitive performance at scale. On CIFAR-10, RDLM (≤2.73 BPD) achieves the best result overall, beating MD4 (≤2.78), Sparse Transformer (2.80), and PixelSNAIL (2.85). On DNA sequence design, RDLM (0.027 MSE) surpasses Fisher-Flow (0.029), DirichletFM (0.034), DDSM (0.033), and D3PM Uniform (0.038). The only comparison where RDLM does not achieve the best result is LM1B, where it trails MDLM by ~1.4 PPL, though it remains within the top tier of diffusion-based language models.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDLM builds directly on the Riemannian diffusion mixture framework (Jo & Hwang, ICML 2024) to construct generative processes on the hypersphere, and establishes a formal equivalence between discrete diffusion transition distributions and continuous flows on the statistical manifold (Fisher-Rao metric). It departs from prior continuous approaches for discrete data (Diffusion-LM, Plaid, BFN) that use unconstrained relaxation or Dirichlet priors, by properly encoding the geometry of categorical distributions via the diffeomorphism to S^{d-1}. It also generalizes prior flow matching on the statistical manifold (Categorical Flow Matching, Fisher Flow) from single tokens to sequences and large vocabularies through dimension splitting, while subsuming both masked and uniform discrete diffusion as special cases of its mixture path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled theoretical bridge between discrete and continuous diffusion for categorical data, resolving a gap that has hindered the development of unified generative frameworks. The simulation-free training via the Riemannian normal approximation (50× speedup) and the dimension splitting technique make the approach practical for large-vocabulary language modeling, where prior manifold-based methods were infeasible. Open questions include whether the continuous formulation enables the controllability and few-step sampling advantages that motivate continuous diffusion in image generation, and whether the framework scales to very large language models or benefits from the optimized solvers (DPM-Solver, etc.) available for continuous diffusion. The modest performance gap remaining to autoregressive models on LM1B suggests further gains may come from architectural or scaling improvements rather than the diffusion formulation itself.
