## Problem

Vision models often achieve high accuracy by relying on spurious visual attributes (background, color, demographic cues) rather than the intended target, but existing interpretability tools require predefined concept sets, manual inspection, or single-pass analysis that cannot scale to complex model behaviors. No prior framework autonomously discovers which attributes drive a model's predictions, validates those findings against actual model behavior, and iteratively corrects itself when the explanation fails to generalize.

## Input / Output

The method takes any pretrained vision model that assigns a scalar score to input images (classifiers, object detectors, or composite models) together with a target concept specified as a short text prompt (e.g., 'vase', 'teacher', 'pedestrians'). It produces a natural-language description of the visual attribute the model selectively relies on, along with two sets of synthetic test images—predicted high-scoring and predicted low-scoring—each annotated with the model's actual confidence score, enabling direct behavioral verification of the explanation.

## Method

SAIA is a self-reflective agent built on a Claude-Sonnet-3.5 backbone that treats attribute-reliance discovery as an iterative scientific loop with two alternating stages. In the hypothesis-testing stage, the agent autonomously proposes candidate visual attributes, writes Python code to compose experiments (generating images via Flux/DALL-E 3, editing them with InstructDiffusion, retrieving high-scoring exemplars, and summarizing visual commonalities with GPT-4o), queries the subject model, and refines its beliefs until it converges on a candidate explanation. In the self-reflection stage, a separate LLM instance generates 10 prompts predicted to elicit high scores (target concept + detected attribute present) and 10 predicted to elicit low scores (attribute absent); the resulting images are scored by the subject model, and if the mean scores between the two groups are insufficiently separated, the agent reflects on the discrepancies, narrows or replaces its hypothesis, and re-enters the testing loop. This unsupervised behavioral-validation mechanism—requiring no ground-truth labels—is the key distinction from prior agents like MAIA, which perform hypothesis testing but never verify whether their conclusion actually predicts model behavior on unseen inputs. The paper also introduces a benchmark of 130 composite models (Grounding DINO object detector modulated by SigLIP or FairFace attribute detectors) spanning 18 reliance categories with tunable strength α, enabling controlled evaluation of detection accuracy.

## Results

On the 2AFC evaluation (LLM-as-judge), SAIA's reliance descriptions are preferred over the Milan baseline at 54–59%, over MAIA at 56–60%, and over a 10-agent Multi-Agent ensemble at 53–62%, with stronger margins at the higher reliance magnitude (α=0.9). Predictiveness scores improve monotonically across self-reflection rounds, approaching the ground-truth upper bound, and SAIA converges in 2–4 rounds on average. For compositional (AND-logic) attribute reliance, SAIA correctly identifies both dependencies in 80% of synthetic models, whereas the non-reflective MAIA baseline fails in all cases. SAIA also uncovers previously unreported attribute dependencies in CLIP-ViT (e.g., lab backgrounds for 'scientist') and YOLOv8 (e.g., crosswalks for 'pedestrian').

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAIA is compared against three non-reflective baselines: Milan (Hernandez et al., 2021), which performs one-shot reliance detection from precomputed exemplars; MAIA (Rott Shaham et al., 2024), which runs iterative hypothesis testing but without self-reflection; and a Multi-Agent ensemble of 10 independent MAIA-style agents selecting the best-scoring explanation. In the 2AFC LLM-judge evaluation, SAIA is preferred over all three baselines at both reliance strengths, with the largest margin over the Multi-Agent ensemble at α=0.9 (62% preference). In the compositional reliance setting (AND logic), SAIA recovers both injected attributes in 80% of models while MAIA fails to detect both in any case, demonstrating that self-reflection provides a distinct advantage over repeated sampling. For OR-logic compositional models, both methods struggle (SAIA 20%, MAIA 0%), indicating a shared limitation in multi-attribute exploration. On real-world pretrained models (CLIP-ViT, YOLOv8), SAIA surfaces attribute dependencies not previously documented, whereas the baselines are not evaluated in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MAIA (Rott Shaham et al., 2024), a multimodal automated interpretability agent, by introducing a self-reflection loop in which the agent validates its own conclusions against model behavior on generated test images and iteratively revises hypotheses when inconsistencies arise. It departs from earlier interpretability approaches (saliency maps, TCAV, Milan-style one-shot descriptions) that rely on predefined concept sets or manual inspection, positioning itself as a fully automated, hypothesis-driven discovery process. The paper also introduces a controlled benchmark of 130 synthetic models with injected attribute reliances, filling a gap left by prior evaluation settings (WaterBirds, CelebA) that use label co-occurrence as a proxy rather than explicit, tunable reliance injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a scalable, model-agnostic tool for auditing pretrained vision models for hidden attribute dependencies, with practical demonstrations uncovering previously unreported biases in CLIP and YOLOv8. Key limitations include dependence on text-to-image generation quality (limiting robustness for rare concepts), inheritance of backbone LLM biases that constrain the hypothesis space, and notably poor performance on compositional OR-logic reliances (only 20% recovery). Open questions include whether hierarchical or tree-structured experimental designs can address multi-attribute detection, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the 18 reliance categories tested.
