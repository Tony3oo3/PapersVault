## Problem

Existing 3D stylization methods require dense posed inputs and time-consuming per-scene or per-style optimization, making them impractical for casual users. While 2D style transfer is fast, naively extending it to 3D breaks multi-view consistency. The paper asks whether a single feed-forward network can produce multi-view-consistent stylized 3D scenes from just a few unposed images and an arbitrary style reference in under a second, without any test-time optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255701/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 2–8 sparse unposed RGB content images (256×256) and one arbitrary style image (256×256), requiring no camera poses or calibration. It outputs a set of pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians parameterized by position, opacity, orientation, scale, and color, using 0-degree spherical harmonics. By feeding a content image into the appearance branch instead of a style image, the same network also produces non-stylized Gaussians for standard novel view synthesis, enabling a single model to serve both reconstruction and stylization.

## Method

Styl3R uses a dual-branch ViT architecture that decouples structure from appearance: a structure branch (shared ViT-L encoder + per-view ViT-B decoder with cross-view attention, following DUSt3R/NoPoSplat) regresses Gaussian geometry via DPT heads, while an appearance branch (separate ViT-L style encoder + stylization decoder) blends style tokens with multi-view content tokens via global self-attention followed by cross-attention, then predicts stylized colors. This separation prevents stylistic transfer from distorting the underlying 3D geometry. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: first, end-to-end novel view synthesis pre-training with photometric loss; second, stylization fine-tuning that freezes the structure branch and optimizes the appearance branch with a weighted combination of VGG19 style loss (relu1_1–relu4_1), content loss (relu3_1 + relu4_1), and an identity loss that randomly substitutes a content image for the style image to preserve photorealistic rendering capability. The model is initialized with MASt3R pretrained weights and trained on 8 A100 GPUs for ~1.5 days, achieving inference in ~0.15 s.

## Results

On RE10K, Styl3R achieves the best multi-view consistency among all methods: short-range LPIPS of 0.044 and long-range LPIPS of 0.107, outperforming the closest 3D baseline StyleGaussian (0.048 / 0.137) and all 2D baselines (≥0.163 / ≥0.315). Stylization time is 0.147 s, roughly 80×–900× faster than 3D optimization-based methods (12–132 min). On ArtFID (lower is better), Styl3R scores 35.12 on RE10K and 38.05 on Tanks and Temples, beating all baselines including ARF (42.95 / 48.98) and StyTr2 (38.93 / 39.24). In a user study with ~2,000 votes, Styl3R receives 53.3% preference on RE10K and 38.8% on Tanks and Temples, far exceeding the next-best method (StyTr2 at 24.1% / 24.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Styl3R outperforms all 2D baselines (AdaIN, AdaAttN, StyTr2) on multi-view consistency by a wide margin—short-range LPIPS of 0.044 vs. 0.163–0.224—while matching their sub-second inference speed (0.147 s vs. 0.004–0.029 s). Against 3D optimization-based methods, Styl3R beats StyleRF (0.062/0.172) and ARF (0.093/0.217) on both short- and long-range LPIPS, and edges out StyleGaussian (0.048/0.137) on long-range consistency, all while being 80–900× faster (0.147 s vs. 12–132 min). On ArtFID, Styl3R (35.12 RE10K, 38.05 TnT) surpasses every baseline, with the largest gaps over StyleGaussian (55.75/55.03) and StyleRF (46.59/64.81). For NVS quality (Table 6), Styl3R's non-stylized output (PSNR 24.87, SSIM 0.837) is nearly on par with NoPoSplat (25.03/0.838), confirming that the dual-branch design preserves reconstruction fidelity; the stylized variant drops slightly to 24.06/0.820. In the user study, Styl3R dominates with 53.3% votes on RE10K, roughly 2.2× the next-best StyTr2 (24.1%) and 3.8× ARF (14.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

Styl3R sits at the intersection of two research threads: feed-forward 3D reconstruction from sparse unposed images (NoPoSplat, DUSt3R) and 3D style transfer (StyleRF, StyleGaussian, ARF). It directly adopts the DUSt3R/NoPoSplat encoder-decoder architecture for its structure branch and the multi-view cross-attention decoder design from NoPoSplat, while introducing a parallel appearance branch that performs cross-attention between content and style tokens. The key departure from prior 3D stylization work is the elimination of test-time optimization and dense posed inputs: where StyleRF, StyleGaussian, and ARF all require per-scene (and sometimes per-style) optimization over 100+ calibrated views, Styl3R achieves zero-shot stylization in a single forward pass on 2–8 unposed images. The identity loss concept is adapted from SANet's 2D style-attentional framework to preserve photometric reconstruction capability during stylization fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing 3D stylization from 12–132 minutes of per-scene optimization to 0.15 seconds of feed-forward inference opens the door to interactive and mobile applications that were previously infeasible. The dual-branch architecture (freezing structure during stylization) is a clean and generalizable design principle that could inform future work combining reconstruction with other appearance modifications. However, the method is currently limited to 256×256 resolution, 2–8 input views, and static scenes, and the comparison with baselines is somewhat asymmetric (sparse unposed inputs vs. dense posed inputs for baselines). Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and larger scenes without VRAM bottlenecks, whether it extends to dynamic content, and whether the quality gap versus optimization-based methods narrows or widens at higher resolutions.
