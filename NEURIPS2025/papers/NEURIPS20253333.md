## Problem

Strategyproof mechanisms for unrelated machine scheduling are known to require an n-approximation in the worst case (Nisan–Ronen lower bound, confirmed by Christodoulou et al.), leaving a large gap versus the factor-2 achievable in the non-strategic setting. Prior learning-augmented approaches either consumed the full n×m processing-time matrix (quadratic predictions) or, with only per-job output predictions, could not beat O(n²)-consistency. The paper asks whether a parsimonious set of O(n+m) predictions suffices to recover near-optimal robustness/consistency tradeoffs while remaining strategyproof.

## Input / Output

The mechanism consumes the reported processing-time matrix p (which strategic machines may misreport) together with three prediction objects: a scalar threshold T̂ for the optimal makespan, a vector of machine biases β̂_i constrained to [1/cn, 1], and a predicted assignment φ̂ mapping each job to one machine. If the predicted biases fall outside the valid range, the mechanism falls back to the n-approximation greedy baseline. The output is a single integral assignment x ∈ {0,1}^{n×m}; the mechanism is a weighted-greedy rule that, for each job, restricts attention to machines with reported size ≤ T̂ (the 'small' set) and picks the minimizer of β̂_i·p_ij, breaking ties toward φ̂(j).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new LP relaxation P_c(T,p) that augments the standard feasibility LP with slack variables Y_i and a global variable Z, whose dual D_c(T,p) imposes the bounds 1/cn ≤ β_i and Σβ_i ≤ 1, thereby controlling the range of machine weights. The mechanism uses the predicted dual variables as biases in a weighted-greedy assignment, and the bounded dual range is what yields the (1+c)n-robustness guarantee independent of prediction quality. Consistency is proved via complementary slackness: when predictions are correct, the predicted machine φ̂(j) is exactly the minimizer of β̂_i·p_ij over the small set, so the mechanism reproduces the rounded LP solution with makespan (2+1/(c−1))·T̂. Strategyproofness follows from item-wise monotonicity of the weighted-greedy rule, invoking the Nisan–Ronen/Saks–Yu characterization. An error-tolerant variant relaxes the small-set threshold to η²T̂ and rescales non-predicted biases by η², achieving η²-dependent robustness and consistency when predictions are only η-accurate.

## Results

The DUAL-PREDICTOR mechanism achieves (1+c)n-robustness and (2 + 1/(c−1))-consistency using only O(n+m) predictions (one predicted makespan value, one dual variable per machine, and one predicted machine per job). For example, with c=2 the mechanism is 3n-robust and 3-consistent; with c=3 it is 4n-robust and 2.5-consistent. The error-tolerant variant achieves (1+c)η²n-robustness and η̂²γ(2 + 1/(c−1))-consistency when predictions are η̂-good. These guarantees match the robustness of Balkanski et al. (2023) while improving consistency from (4+2/c) to (2+1/(c−1)), all with linearly many predictions instead of quadratically many.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Balkanski et al. (2023), who achieve (1+c)n-robustness and (4+2/c)-consistency using the full n×m processing-time matrix, this paper matches the robustness exactly while improving the consistency constant from (4+2/c) to (2+1/(c−1)) (e.g., at c=2: 5 vs. 3) and reducing predictions from n·m to O(n+m). Compared to Christodoulou et al. (2024), who use only m output predictions (predicted machine per job) and achieve (β+1)-robustness with (n²/β)-consistency, this paper adds n+1 extra predictions (dual variables and predicted OPT) to obtain constant consistency (2+1/(c−1)) at the cost of O(n) robustness rather than O(1). The Nisan-Ronen greedy baseline (n-approximation, no predictions) is strictly dominated: this paper's robustness is (1+c)n (a constant factor worse) but its consistency is a constant independent of n, a qualitative improvement. The paper does not include empirical comparisons; all results are theoretical guarantees proven via LP duality and monotonicity arguments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of strategyproof mechanism design (Nisan and Ronen 2001; Christodoulou et al. 2022, 2023) and learning-augmented algorithms (Balkanski et al. 2023; Christodoulou et al. 2024). It builds directly on the SCALED GREEDY mechanism of Balkanski et al. (2023), which uses the full n×m processing-time matrix as predictions, and on the output-predictions model of Christodoulou et al. (2024), which uses only a predicted machine per job. The key departure is a new LP relaxation whose bounded dual variables serve as machine biases, combined with the classical relax-and-round rounding of Lenstra et al. (1990) and Shmoys and Tardos (1993), to achieve the same robustness/consistency tradeoff with only O(n+m) predictions instead of O(nm).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result shows that the quadratic prediction overhead in prior learning-augmented scheduling mechanisms is unnecessary: a linear number of carefully chosen predictions (one scalar for OPT, one dual variable per machine, one predicted assignment per job) suffices to match the best-known robustness and consistency guarantees. The 'range-controlled dual' technique—designing an LP relaxation specifically so that its dual variables lie in a bounded range to control mechanism robustness—is a potentially reusable idea for other mechanism-design problems. Open questions include whether O(n) predictions alone (dropping the per-job predictions) suffice, whether the sample complexity of learning these predictions is provably smaller than learning full processing times, and whether the range-controlled dual idea transfers to other strategic allocation problems.
