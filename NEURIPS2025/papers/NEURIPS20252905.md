## Problem

High-performing brain decoding models map fMRI activity directly into entangled, non-interpretable latent spaces such as CLIP embeddings, making it impossible to determine which brain regions encode which visual concepts. Although simple linear regressions achieve strong decoding performance, their weight matrices remain opaque to neuroscientific interpretation. This paper asks whether a semantically structured bottleneck can be inserted into the decoding pipeline to yield voxel-level interpretability without substantially sacrificing reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252905/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 7T fMRI beta weights (~15,000 masked voxels per subject) from the Natural Scenes Dataset and produces three outputs: a 214-dimensional binary concept vector (each dimension answering a specific yes/no question about the stimulus image), a final reconstructed image generated by Versatile Diffusion conditioned on predicted CLIP embeddings, and per-dimension voxel importance maps projectable into MNI space for comparison with canonical brain atlases. The 214-dim interpretable space acts as a bottleneck at roughly 28% of CLIP's 768-dim embedding, a ratio chosen based on prior evidence that low-dimensional representations retain most decoding performance.

## Method

The core contribution is factorizing BrainDiffuser's single linear mapping from voxels to CLIP space (W, shape n_voxels × 768) into two matrices: A (n_voxels × 214) mapping brain activity to an interpretable space L, and B (214 × 768) mapping L to CLIP embeddings. The interpretable space is constructed by having GPT-4o generate 256 diverse binary questions from NSD image captions (reduced to 214 after manual deduplication), then using BLIP-2 to answer each question for every image, yielding a 214-dim binary vector per stimulus. Both A and B are trained as ridge regressions with 5-fold cross-validation for regularization; the structural stream (VDVAE latents) is preserved unchanged from the original BrainDiffuser. Because A is linear, each of its columns directly encodes the signed contribution of every voxel to one semantic dimension, producing interpretable activation maps that can be compared against canonical category-selective regions (faces, bodies, places, words) in the visual cortex.

## Results

On the Natural Scenes Dataset (4 subjects), the interpretable model (Int-BD) achieves a mean CLIP similarity of 0.877 and 50-way-top-1 accuracy of 0.445, compared to 0.915 and 0.525 for the original BrainDiffuser, representing a modest drop of approximately 4% and 8% respectively. InceptionV3 2-way accuracy decreases from 0.907 to 0.846 on average. The Int-to-CLIP upper bound (which bypasses fMRI data entirely) reaches 0.951 CLIP similarity and 0.685 50-way accuracy, confirming that the 214-dimensional interpretable space retains most of the semantic information needed for reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is the original BrainDiffuser pipeline [Ozcelik & VanRullen, 2023], which maps fMRI activity directly to CLIP embeddings via a single linear regression. The interpretable model (Int-BD) consistently trails BrainDiffuser on every metric across all four subjects: CLIP similarity drops by 3.5–4.1 percentage points (e.g., 0.885 vs. 0.923 for Subj01), 50-way-top-1 accuracy drops by 7–10 percentage points (e.g., 0.46 vs. 0.53 for Subj01), and low-level metrics such as PixCorr fall more noticeably (0.153 vs. 0.285 for Subj01). The Int-to-CLIP model, which maps ground-truth interpretable vectors directly to CLIP without any fMRI input, serves as an upper bound rather than a true baseline; it outperforms both Int-BD and BrainDiffuser on all high-level metrics (e.g., 0.955 CLIP vs. 0.885 and 0.923), confirming that the 214-dimensional semantic space is sufficiently informative and that the performance gap is attributable to the fMRI-to-interpretability mapping rather than to information loss in the bottleneck itself. Overall, the paper demonstrates that inserting a fully interpretable semantic bottleneck costs only ~4% CLIP similarity and ~8% 50-way accuracy relative to the non-interpretable BrainDiffuser, while enabling voxel-level neuroscientific interpretation that the baseline cannot provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BrainDiffuser (Ozcelik & VanRullen, 2023), a two-stage linear-regression-plus-diffusion pipeline for fMRI-to-image reconstruction, and inserts a 214-dimensional binary semantic bottleneck between the brain-to-CLIP linear map. Conceptually, it extends the idea from Benara et al. (2024) of replacing non-interpretable language embeddings with question-answer vectors to the visual domain, and is motivated by BrainBits (Mayo et al., 2024), which showed that low-dimensional bottlenecks in visual decoding pipelines retain most performance. The key departure is making the bottleneck interpretable by design: each dimension corresponds to a specific yes/no question about the image, generated by GPT-4o and answered by BLIP-2, so that the brain-to-concept weight matrix A can be directly visualized as voxel-level activation maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first complete brain decoding pipeline with a built-in, human-readable intermediate representation, enabling voxel-level neuroscientific analysis of semantic encoding without post-hoc explainability. The finding that a 214-dim binary question space costs only ~4% CLIP similarity and that the resulting voxel maps align with canonical category-selective regions (faces, bodies, places, words) provides a practical tool for probing how concepts are distributed in the visual cortex. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond NSD and visual decoding to other modalities (language, music), whether hierarchical or more granular concept spaces improve both reconstruction and interpretability, and how to incorporate structural/geometric information that the current question set captures only partially.
