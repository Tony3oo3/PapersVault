## Problem

Existing graph few-shot learning methods rely on fixed, globally applied graph filters (low-pass or high-pass) that cannot accommodate the coexistence of homophilic and heterophilic local structures in real-world graphs. Additionally, they implicitly assume support and query sets share the same distribution, an assumption violated under few-shot conditions where limited labeled data and random sampling introduce systematic distributional shifts that degrade generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255361/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on an attributed graph G=(V,E,X,A) under an episodic N-way K-shot protocol: each meta-task provides a support set of K labeled nodes per class and a query set of M unlabeled nodes per class. The model produces predicted class labels for query nodes by assigning each to the nearest calibrated prototype in Euclidean space. During meta-training, tasks are sampled from a base label space; during meta-testing, tasks are drawn from a disjoint new label space, requiring the model to generalize across unseen classes.

## Method

GRACE integrates two components. First, an adaptive spectrum expert module uses a mixture-of-experts architecture with a low-pass GCN expert (capturing homophilic smoothing) and a high-pass attention-based expert (amplifying feature differences for heterophilic regions); a gating network takes concatenated node features, one-hop neighbor differences, feature-wise standard deviation, and node degree to assign per-node expert weights. Second, a cross-set distribution calibration step computes class prototypes from the support set, then refines them via a KDE-inspired weighted correction using query-set samples, with a trainable magnitude parameter controlling the calibration strength. The model is trained via episodic meta-learning with a distance-based cross-entropy loss, and the paper provides a generalization bound decomposed into expert variance, gating error, and Wasserstein-distance-based distribution shift terms.

## Results

GRACE achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all seven benchmark datasets under multiple few-shot settings. On the standard 2-way 5-shot protocol, GRACE reaches 86.19% on Cora, 79.64% on CiteSeer, and 94.66% on Amazon-Computer, outperforming the strongest baselines (TEG, Meta-GPS, Meta-BP) by 1.7–4.3 percentage points. On harder multi-way settings, GRACE attains 86.03% on Coauthor-CS (5-way 3-shot), 81.72% on DBLP (5-way 5-shot), 78.22% on CoraFull (5-way 3-shot), and 62.31% on ogbn-arxiv (5-way 3-shot), consistently leading by 2–5 points over the next-best method. Ablation studies confirm both the adaptive spectrum experts and cross-set distribution calibration contribute meaningfully, with removing both components dropping accuracy by up to 25 points on CoraFull.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRACE outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and shot setting evaluated. Among graph meta-learning methods, TEG is the closest competitor, trailing GRACE by 1.69 points on Cora (84.50 vs 86.19), 2.85 on CiteSeer (76.79 vs 79.64), and 2.26 on Amazon-Computer (92.40 vs 94.66) under 2-way 5-shot; the gap widens to 5.25 points on Coauthor-CS (80.78 vs 86.03) and 4.96 on CoraFull (72.14 vs 78.22) under 5-way 3-shot. Meta-GPS and Meta-BP, the other top-performing graph meta-learners, are consistently 2–6 points behind GRACE. Graph embedding methods (GCN, SGC, DeepWalk, node2vec) and generic meta-learning methods (ProtoNet, MAML) lag substantially, with GCN reaching only 67.95% on Cora and ProtoNet 57.92%, confirming that task-specific graph meta-learning is essential. The largest absolute gains appear on CiteSeer (where GRACE leads by 2.85 points over TEG) and CoraFull (where GRACE leads by 5.09 points over COSMIC), suggesting the adaptive spectrum experts and distribution calibration are particularly effective on graphs with mixed homophily/heterophily and larger label spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRACE extends the prototypical network framework for few-shot classification by introducing two targeted modifications for graph-structured data: a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module that replaces the fixed low-pass or high-pass graph filter with node-adaptive spectral experts, and a KDE-inspired cross-set distribution calibration that shifts class prototypes toward query-set density. The MoE design draws on recent graph MoE work (GMoE, GraphMETRO) but is the first to apply it specifically to graph few-shot learning, while the calibration step adapts the distribution-calibration idea from Yang et al. (ICLR 2021) to the episodic support-query setting. The paper departs from prior graph FSL methods (GPN, G-Meta, TEG, COSMIC, Meta-BP) that implicitly assume a single global spectral regime (homophily or heterophily) and treat support and query sets as identically distributed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded (Rademacher + Wasserstein) generalization bound that explicitly decomposes error into expert variance, gating error, and distribution-shift terms, offering a principled justification for both design choices. Practically, the consistent SOTA improvements across seven datasets of varying scale (from 2.7K to 169K nodes) and the case study confirming that gating weights track local homophily make the framework a useful reference for node-adaptive graph filtering. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the restriction to only two spectral experts (low-pass and high-pass); extending to richer spectral bands or learned filter families remains open. The theoretical analysis also assumes normalized expert outputs and a bounded label-space diameter, conditions that may not hold in all practical settings.
