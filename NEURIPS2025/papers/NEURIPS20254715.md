## Problem

Most existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods update Batch Normalization layers to handle domain shift, but BN statistics become unreliable under small batch sizes and modifying them risks disrupting pretrained representations, leading to catastrophic forgetting. While a few recent works freeze the backbone and adapt lightweight auxiliary components, they still require a warm-up phase with source data, undermining the source-free premise. The paper identifies that the field has focused on *how* to update (entropy minimization, consistency regularization) while neglecting *what* to update, and proposes a structurally decoupled adaptation target that is truly source-free and forgetting-resilient.

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled target-domain RGB images in small batches (BS=2, 4, 16, 64, 256) and a frozen pretrained CNN backbone (WRN28, WRN40, ResNeXt, ResNet-50, or ResNetV2-50), producing standard class labels. The Buffer layer is randomly initialized with no pretraining or source-data warm-up, and is inserted only in the early stages of the network in parallel to the backbone. The output is computed as the sum of the frozen backbone's prediction and the Buffer layer's residual correction, meaning the backbone's original output is always available and unaltered.

## Method

The Buffer layer is a lightweight convolutional module consisting of parallel 1×1 and 3×3 convolutions whose outputs are scaled by a learnable coefficient α and residually added to the original activations, inserted in parallel to the pretrained backbone without modifying any of its parameters. During test-time adaptation, all backbone parameters (including BN affine parameters) are frozen, and only the Buffer layer's weights are updated using any standard TTA objective such as entropy minimization or consistency regularization. The key architectural insight is that by externalizing adaptation into this residual branch, the method avoids the instability of BN statistics under small batches, eliminates the need for source-data warm-up, and inherently prevents catastrophic forgetting since the pretrained representations remain intact. Ablations show that placing the Buffer layer after activation functions in early network stages is most effective for small batch sizes, while combining early and middle placements benefits larger batches, and the dual-path 1×1+3×3 configuration consistently outperforms single-convolution variants.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C (WRN28, BS=2), replacing BN updates with the Buffer layer reduces classification error from 82.56% (TENT@BN) to 37.05% (TENT@Buffer), a 45.51-point improvement. On CIFAR-10-W (BS=2), TENT@Buffer achieves 64.14% error versus 89.30% for TENT@BN, and DeYo@Buffer reaches 46.96% versus 84.37% for DeYo@BN. At larger batch sizes (BS=16), the gap narrows substantially, and joint @BN+Buffer updates consistently outperform either component alone, e.g., 63.48% vs. 63.61% (TENT) on ImageNet-C Res50. Across all 15 corruption types on CIFAR-10-C under continuously shifting domains, TENT@Buffer achieves 29.17% average error versus 86.90% for TENT@BN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Buffer layer (@Buffer) consistently outperforms its BN-based counterpart (@BN) at small batch sizes (BS=2, 4) across all six BN-updating baselines (TENT, EATA, SAR, DeYo, CMF, ROID) on both CIFAR-10-C and CIFAR-100-C. The largest gains appear with TENT and DeYo: on WRN28/CIFAR-10-C at BS=2, TENT improves by 45.51 points (82.56→37.05) and DeYo by 34.30 points (69.95→35.65). At BS=16, the advantage narrows or reverses for some methods (e.g., SAR@Buffer 23.06 vs. SAR@BN 22.94 on WRN28), and joint @BN+Buffer updates become competitive or superior, particularly on ImageNet-C. Against the full-model-update baselines CoTTA and AdaContrast (which have no @Buffer variant), TENT@Buffer at BS=16 on WRN28/CIFAR-10-C (29.62%) underperforms AdaContrast (18.38%) but substantially outperforms CoTTA (22.58%). On CIFAR-10-W, the Buffer layer's advantage is most dramatic at BS=2 (e.g., DeYo: 84.37→46.96) but diminishes at BS=16 where @BN+Buffer joint updates (e.g., 29.22 for CMF) match or slightly beat @Buffer alone (30.63). Overall, the Buffer layer wins decisively in the small-batch regime where BN statistics are unreliable, ties or slightly loses at large batch sizes when BN is stable, and provides complementary gains when co-trained with BN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant TTA paradigm of updating Batch Normalization layers (TENT, EATA, SAR, DeYo, CMF, ROID) or fine-tuning the full model (CoTTA, AdaContrast) by reformulating the problem around *what* to update rather than *how*. It introduces an external, residual Buffer layer of lightweight 1×1 and 3×3 convolutions inserted in parallel to the frozen backbone, conceptually repurposing the residual-connection idea as a dedicated adaptation unit. Unlike prior auxiliary-module approaches such as EcoTTA and L-TTA, which still require a source-data warm-up phase, the Buffer layer operates in a fully source-free and online manner with no initialization dependency. The paper positions itself as a method-agnostic drop-in replacement: each existing TTA algorithm's optimization objective is retained while the update target is swapped from BN parameters to Buffer parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Buffer layer offers a practical, plug-and-play mechanism for source-free TTA that simultaneously addresses two long-standing pain points: instability under small batch sizes (where BN statistics are unreliable) and catastrophic forgetting of source-domain knowledge. The finding that externalizing adaptation into a parallel residual branch preserves source accuracy while improving target accuracy challenges the commonly assumed adaptation–retention trade-off in TTA. Key open questions remain: the optimal Buffer architecture and placement are batch-size-dependent and not yet principled, the scaling coefficient α is sensitive to initialization, and the adaptation objective is borrowed wholesale from existing methods rather than designed for the Buffer's residual nature. Whether the approach scales to non-CNN backbones (e.g., vision transformers) or to tasks beyond image classification is unexplored.
