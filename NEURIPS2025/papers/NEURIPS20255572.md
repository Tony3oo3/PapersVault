## Problem

Existing autoregressive (AR) methods for multi-agent combinatorial optimization suffer from two critical limitations: poor inter-agent coordination leading to suboptimal solutions and poor generalization, and high inference latency because each action depends sequentially on all previous ones. Prior parallel approaches (e.g., MAPDP) use fixed per-agent decoders with naive random conflict resolution, limiting both generalizability and solution quality. PARCO addresses these gaps by enabling truly parallel, coordinated multi-agent solution construction with learned conflict handling.

## Input / Output

The model takes a problem instance x comprising node features (e.g., customer locations and demands for routing, job processing times for scheduling) and agent features (e.g., vehicle capacities and speeds, or machine characteristics), encoded into a shared d-dimensional embedding space. At each decoding step, all M agents simultaneously output a joint action vector a_t ∈ A_1 × ... × A_M over N candidate nodes, with infeasible actions masked to −∞. The final output is a feasible multi-agent solution (routes or schedules) constructed in max_m T_m steps rather than the sum_m T_m steps required by sequential AR, with conflicts resolved by assigning fallback 'do-nothing' actions to losing agents.

## Method

PARCO reformulates multi-agent CO as a cooperative multi-agent MDP where all M agents select actions simultaneously at each step, reducing total construction steps from ΣT_m to max T_m. The architecture comprises three key components: (1) Communication Layers — a single transformer block (self-attention + MLP) applied over dynamic agent queries at each decoding step, enabling agents to attend to each other's states and the problem structure for coordinated decision-making; (2) a Multiple Pointer Mechanism that extends the standard pointer network to produce an M×N joint logit matrix via masked cross-attention between agent queries and node embeddings, followed by a tanh-scaled scoring; and (3) Priority-based Conflict Handlers that resolve simultaneous same-node selections by ranking agents by their model output probabilities (learned priorities) and assigning fallback actions to losers. Training uses REINFORCE with SymNCO symmetric-augmentation shared baselines, and the model is trained on varying problem sizes and agent counts to support zero-shot generalization to unseen scales.

## Results

On HCVRP (N=100, M=7, sampling), PARCO achieves a 2.92% gap to best-known solutions in 0.09s, outperforming the best neural baseline 2D-Ptr (5.17% gap, 0.24s) and traditional solvers (SISRs: 0% gap but 625s). On OMDCPDP (N=100, M=100, sampling), PARCO reaches 0.00% gap in 0.03s, surpassing OR-Tools (1.53% gap, 60s) and MAPDP (1.13% gap, 0.02s). On FFSP (N=50, M=18, sampling), PARCO achieves 0.0% gap in 4.04s, outperforming MatNet (0.3% gap, 18s) and Gurobi (27.6% gap at N=20, 600s). PARCO consistently provides 2–25× speedups over neural baselines while matching or exceeding their solution quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On HCVRP, PARCO (sampling) matches or beats the strongest neural baseline 2D-Ptr across all tested (N,M) configurations, e.g., 2.92% vs 5.17% gap at N=100,M=7, while being 2.7× faster (0.09s vs 0.24s); it also outperforms sequential baselines DPN, DRL_Li, ET, and AM by 2–35 percentage points in gap. On OMDCPDP, PARCO achieves 0.00% gap (matching best-known) at all scales, surpassing MAPDP (0.02–1.13% gap) and the classical OR-Tools solver (1.53–4.99% gap) while being 2000× faster than OR-Tools. On FFSP, PARCO reaches 0.0% gap at N=50 and N=100, outperforming MatNet by 0.3–13.9 percentage points in gap and being 4–7× faster (e.g., 4.04s vs 18s at N=50,M=18); Gurobi cannot produce solutions beyond N=20 within 10 minutes. In large-scale generalization (Table 2, N=1000, M=200), PARCO achieves 0.00% gap in 1.26s while OR-Tools (600s) reaches only 36.81% gap and HAM fails to generalize (7.63% gap). PARCO's advantage grows with the number of agents M, as parallel decoding reduces construction steps from ΣT_m to max(T_m), yielding up to 24.7× speedups over sequential AR methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARCO extends the autoregressive pointer network lineage (Vinyals et al. 2015; Kool et al. 2019) from single-agent to multi-agent combinatorial optimization by introducing a Multiple Pointer Mechanism that decodes actions for all M agents simultaneously rather than sequentially. It departs from prior multi-agent AR methods (Son et al. 2024, Zheng et al. 2024, Zong et al. 2022) that either solve agents one-at-a-time or use fixed per-agent decoders, by adding transformer-based Communication Layers for inter-agent coordination during decoding and Priority-based Conflict Handlers that replace random tie-breaking with learned priorities. The training scheme builds on POMO/SymNCO shared-baseline REINFORCE, and the encoder design draws on MatNet's cross-attention between agent and node embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-agent CO problems (coordinated routing, flexible scheduling) are central to logistics and manufacturing, yet prior learning-based solvers suffered from high latency and poor generalization as agent counts grew. PARCO's parallel formulation reduces construction steps from ΣT_m to max(T_m), yielding 3–25× speedups that paradoxically improve with more agents, and demonstrates zero-shot generalization to 10× unseen problem scales with 0% gap. A key limitation is the requirement of a fixed, known number of agents M, leaving open the important question of handling variable or unknown agent counts in real deployment. The framework's generality across routing and scheduling domains suggests it could become a standard baseline for multi-agent CO research.
