## Problem

Current flow-based inverse problem solvers suffer from two key limitations: they operate directly in pixel space, restricting scalability to high-resolution images, and they employ guidance strategies with prior-agnostic posterior covariances that can weaken alignment with the learned generative trajectory, degrading posterior coverage and reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253606/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy linear measurement y = Ax₀ + n (with known operator A and noise level σ_y = 0.01), a pretrained LFM-VAE (DiT-based, 32×32 latent space), and produces a reconstructed 256×256×3 RGB image. No task-specific training or fine-tuning is required; the flow model and autoencoder are used entirely off-the-shelf. The reverse ODE is initialized at t_s < 1 from a partially corrupted encoding of y rather than from pure noise, making the starting point closer to the posterior mode.

## Method

LFlow performs ODE sampling in the latent space of a pretrained LFM-VAE (DiT architecture) along optimal transport paths, avoiding the computational burden of pixel-space operations. The key theoretical contribution is a time-dependent posterior covariance Cov[z₀|z_t] = r²(t)·I derived via Tweedie's covariance formula from the Jacobian of the optimal vector field under a Gaussian latent prior assumption, making the guidance explicitly informed by the pretrained flow rather than prior-agnostic. This covariance enters the likelihood gradient ∇_{z_t} log p(y|z_t) through a first-order Taylor linearization of the decoder, yielding a Jacobian-vector product computable via automatic differentiation. The conditional velocity field is approximated as v_θ(z_t, t) − (t/(1−t))∇_{z_t} log p(y|z_t), and the reverse ODE is integrated with an adaptive Heun solver. The approach also provides a sandwich bound on the vector field Jacobian under strong log-concavity of the latent prior, ensuring numerical stability of the covariance.

## Results

On FFHQ, LFlow achieves the best LPIPS across all four tasks: 0.166 (Gaussian deblurring), 0.168 (motion deblurring), 0.176 (SR×4), and 0.132 (box inpainting), surpassing the second-best method (SITCOM) by up to 8.8% in LPIPS on Gaussian deblurring. On ImageNet, LFlow similarly leads in LPIPS (0.328, 0.344, 0.338, 0.227) and SSIM across all tasks. While PSLD slightly edges out LFlow in PSNR for Gaussian deblurring (30.28 vs. 29.10 dB on FFHQ), LFlow closes the gap within 1.18 dB while delivering substantially sharper reconstructions with lower perceptual error. Inference time is also competitive: LFlow completes Gaussian deblurring in ~268 s and SR×4 in ~228 s per image on a single RTX 3090, roughly 2× faster than PSLD and Resample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFlow outperforms all six latent-diffusion baselines (SITCOM, DAPS, DMPlug, Resample, MPGD, PSLD) on LPIPS across all four tasks on both FFHQ and ImageNet, with the largest margin over SITCOM (0.166 vs. 0.237 LPIPS on FFHQ Gaussian deblurring, an 8.8% relative improvement). The closest competitor is PSLD, which achieves a slightly higher PSNR on Gaussian deblurring (30.28 vs. 29.10 dB on FFHQ) but trails LFlow by 0.115 in LPIPS, reflecting a perceptual-fidelity trade-off where LFlow preserves fine texture and high-frequency detail more faithfully. Against the pixel-space flow-based baseline OT-ODE, LFlow improves LPIPS by 0.032 on FFHQ Gaussian deblurring (0.166 vs. 0.198) and by 0.093 on inpainting (0.132 vs. 0.225), demonstrating the benefit of operating in latent space with a theoretically grounded posterior covariance. Compared to the pixel-space diffusion baseline ΠGDM, LFlow achieves lower LPIPS on every task (e.g., 0.166 vs. 0.182 on Gaussian deblurring) while also being faster in inference. The ablation further shows that replacing LFlow's time-dependent covariance with the prior-agnostic ΠGDM covariance degrades LPIPS by 0.013–0.017 and nearly doubles inference time (478 s vs. 268 s for Gaussian deblurring), confirming that the learned posterior covariance is critical for both quality and convergence speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

LFlow extends the flow-matching framework (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) from pixel space into the latent space of a pretrained LFM-VAE (Dao et al., 2023), combining two previously separate lines of work: latent-space inverse solvers (PSLD, Resample, DAPS) and flow-based ODE solvers (OT-ODE, C-ΠGFM). The key conceptual departure is the derivation of a time-dependent posterior covariance directly from the pretrained optimal vector field via Tweedie's covariance formula, rather than relying on prior-agnostic identity covariances borrowed from diffusion guidance (ΠGDM) or zero-covariance approximations (DPS). This makes the guidance term explicitly informed by the generative dynamics, a distinction the authors argue is critical for stable ODE integration and posterior coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in flow-based inverse solvers: operating in pixel space limits scalability, and prior-agnostic covariances degrade trajectory alignment. By moving to latent space and grounding the covariance in the vector field, LFlow achieves roughly 2× faster inference than competing latent diffusion solvers while improving perceptual quality (LPIPS) across deblurring, SR, and inpainting. However, inference still takes 3–10 minutes per image on a single GPU, limiting real-time applicability, and the method's reliance on a Gaussian latent prior assumption (σ_latr = 1) may not hold for all pretrained autoencoders. Open questions include robustness under distributional shift and whether the covariance formulation generalizes to non-Gaussian latent priors or nonlinear measurement operators.
