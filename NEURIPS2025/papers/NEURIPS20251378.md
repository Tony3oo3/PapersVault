## Problem

Explicit noise-level (timestep) conditioning is a foundational assumption in Graph Diffusion Models, yet whether it is truly necessary for discrete, structure-preserving graph corruption processes remains unaddressed. While recent work on continuous data suggests high-capacity denoisers can implicitly infer noise scale, the discrete and combinatorial nature of graphs makes this non-trivial. This paper provides the first unified theoretical framework rigorously characterizing when explicit conditioning can be safely omitted in GDMs.

## Input / Output

The denoiser f_θ receives only the noisy adjacency matrix Ã_t (and optionally noisy node features X̃_t) at a given diffusion step, with no scalar timestep or noise-level embedding as input. The corruption is discrete—Bernoulli edge flips over M = C(n,2) potential edges—so the noisy graph's global statistics (e.g., edge-difference count) implicitly encode the corruption scale. The output is the predicted clean adjacency matrix A_0 (and features X_0), trained via per-edge binary cross-entropy loss without any time-dependent conditioning signal.

## Method

The paper establishes three interconnected theorems: Edge-Flip Posterior Concentration (EFPC) proves the flip-rate posterior variance shrinks as O(M⁻¹) via Bernstein-von Mises, making the noise level statistically inferable from the graph; Edge-Target Deviation Bound (ETDB) shows the single-step denoising target deviation from omitting t is O(M⁻¹) under Lipschitz regularity; and Multi-Step Denoising Error Propagation (MDEP) bounds cumulative T-step reconstruction error at O(T/M), confirming errors grow only linearly. The framework extends to a coupled Gaussian structure-feature noise model with joint posterior concentration at O(D⁻¹) where D = M + n·d_f. Empirically, t-free variants of GDSS (SDE-based) and DiGress (discrete categorical) are trained and evaluated on QM9 and soc-Epinions1, matching or surpassing t-aware counterparts while reducing parameters by 4–6% and per-epoch runtime by 8–10%.

## Results

On QM9, GDSS t-free outperforms GDSS t-aware on both Validity (94.00% vs. 92.32%) and Uniqueness (89.60% vs. 81.08%) while using 5.3% fewer parameters (1.79M vs. 1.89M) and training 8.2% faster (8.78s vs. 9.56s per epoch). On soc-Epinions1, GDSS t-free(warm) achieves the best overall structural fidelity (MMD_Overall = 0.66 vs. 0.76 for t-aware), and DiGress t-free matches t-aware on MMD_Overall (0.49 vs. 0.53) with 3.9% fewer parameters. However, DiGress t-free(warm) suffers a severe validity collapse on soc-Epinions1 (23.67% vs. 100%), revealing architecture-specific sensitivity to removing timestep conditioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks two GDM architectures—DiGress (discrete categorical diffusion) and GDSS (SDE-based score matching)—each in t-aware (standard), t-free (no timestep input), and t-free(warm) (fine-tuned from t-aware weights) variants. On QM9, GDSS t-free clearly wins over GDSS t-aware, improving Validity by 1.7 pp (94.00% vs. 92.32%) and Uniqueness by 8.5 pp (89.60% vs. 81.08%) while reducing parameters and training time. DiGress t-free essentially ties DiGress t-aware on QM9 Validity (99.99% vs. 99.98%) with a small Uniqueness trade-off (4.65% vs. 4.76%). On soc-Epinions1, GDSS t-free(warm) achieves the best MMD_Overall (0.66 vs. 0.76 for t-aware), and DiGress t-free slightly improves MMD_Overall (0.49 vs. 0.53). A critical caveat: DiGress t-free(warm) collapses in validity on soc-Epinions1 (23.67% vs. 100%), indicating that transformer-based discrete diffusion architectures remain heavily dependent on explicit timestep embeddings, whereas SDE-based models like GDSS transition more gracefully to unconditional generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'blind denoising' line of work (Sun et al. 2025; Heurtel-Depeiges et al. 2024; Sahoo et al. 2024), which showed that high-capacity denoisers on continuous data can implicitly infer noise scale from corrupted inputs, to the discrete graph setting where corruption is structural (Bernoulli edge flips, categorical rewiring) rather than Gaussian. It builds directly on two GDM architectures—DiGress (Vignac et al. 2022, discrete categorical Markov processes) and GDSS (Jo et al. 2022, coupled SDEs)—by removing their explicit timestep conditioning and analyzing the consequences. The key conceptual departure from prior GDM work is the introduction of a unified theoretical framework (EFPC, ETDB, MDEP) that rigorously characterizes *when* the noise level is inferable from graph structure alone, rather than simply empirically ablating the timestep input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If explicit noise conditioning is dispensable for sufficiently large graphs, GDM architectures can be simplified with modest efficiency gains (4–6% fewer parameters, 8–10% faster per-epoch runtime), and the theoretical scaling laws (O(M⁻¹) single-step, O(T/M) cumulative error) give practitioners a principled criterion for when t-free models are safe to deploy. However, the results are architecture-dependent (DiGress t-free(warm) suffers a dramatic validity collapse from 100% to 23.67% on soc-Epinions1) and graph-size-dependent (small/sparse graphs still benefit from conditioning), so the finding is not a universal 'drop the timestep' prescription. Open questions include whether the theory extends to web-scale heterogeneous graphs, whether adaptive coupling strategies can close the remaining gap for transformer-based GDMs, and whether the Lipschitz and model-capacity assumptions (A2, A4) hold in practice for deeper or more expressive architectures.
