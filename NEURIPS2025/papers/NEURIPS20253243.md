## Problem

Diffusion Transformers for video generation suffer from quadratic attention complexity, with 3D spatio-temporal attention accounting for over 80% of end-to-end runtime. Existing sparse attention methods fail to approach oracle-level quality under the same computation budget because they cluster tokens by position rather than semantics (causing inaccurate critical token identification) and leave critical tokens scattered across the tensor (causing up to 80% computation waste on GPU tensor cores that require contiguous inputs).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253243/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free drop-in replacement for the attention module in existing DiT video generation pipelines. For text-to-video, it takes a text prompt and generates 720p videos (21 frames at 3600 tokens/frame for Wan 2.1, 33 frames at 3600 tokens/frame for HunyuanVideo). For image-to-video, it additionally takes a reference image cropped to 16:9 ratio. The output is a generated video with the same frame count and resolution as the dense-attention baseline, with sparse attention skipped during the first 30% of denoising steps to preserve quality.

## Method

SVG2's core mechanism is semantic-aware permutation: k-means clustering is independently applied to Query and Key tokens per attention head and layer, grouping tokens by activation similarity rather than spatial position. This simultaneously improves identification accuracy (centroids of semantically coherent clusters provide precise attention score approximations) and reduces computation waste (critical tokens within a cluster tend to be all-critical or all-non-critical, so permuting clusters into contiguous layout eliminates padding). A centroid-based top-p selection strategy dynamically allocates the computational budget by selecting clusters in descending order of estimated attention score until cumulative mass reaches a target p. A customized dynamic block-sparse attention kernel (supporting both FA2 on A100 and FA3 on H100) handles the naturally variable cluster sizes without padding, and a centroid cache reuses k-means centroids across consecutive denoising steps to reduce clustering overhead by 76×.

## Results

SVG2 achieves up to 2.30× end-to-end speedup on HunyuanVideo (13B, 720P T2V) with PSNR 30.45, SSIM 0.910, and VBench 0.852 at 25.45% density, and up to 1.89× speedup on Wan 2.1 (14B, 720P T2V) with PSNR 23.68 at 12.87% density (Turbo mode). In the standard configuration, SVG2 delivers 1.58× speedup on Wan 2.1 I2V (PSNR 26.56) and 1.60× on Wan 2.1 T2V (PSNR 25.81), consistently outperforming all baselines in quality at comparable or lower computational budgets. The method also supports FP8 quantization, pushing HunyuanVideo speedup to 2.55× with negligible quality loss (PSNR 30.39).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SpargeAttn (dynamic, position-based clustering), SVG2 achieves substantially higher PSNR across all settings: 26.56 vs. 21.18 on Wan 2.1 I2V, 25.81 vs. 20.52 on Wan 2.1 T2V, and 30.45 vs. 27.89 on HunyuanVideo, while using comparable or lower density (e.g., 25.45% vs. 42.62% on Hunyuan). Against SVG (static spatial-temporal sparsity), SVG2 improves PSNR by 2.5–4.5 dB at similar density (e.g., 30.45 vs. 29.16 on Hunyuan at 25.45% vs. 29.86% density) and achieves higher end-to-end speedup (2.30× vs. 1.91×). Against XAttention (block sparse with antidiagonal scoring, evaluated only on Hunyuan), SVG2 wins on both quality (PSNR 30.45 vs. 28.89) and efficiency (25.45% density vs. 39.32%, yielding 2.30× vs. 1.56× speedup). In the Turbo configuration, SVG2 reduces density to 12.87–14.13% while maintaining PSNR competitive with baselines at ~30% density, demonstrating a 2.3× density reduction at equal quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of dynamic sparse attention for video DiTs (SpargeAttention, XAttention, Sparse VideoGen) by identifying two specific failure modes of existing block-wise identification: position-based clustering produces imprecise aggregated representations, and scattered critical tokens cause up to 80% computation waste on tensor cores. The core departure is semantic-aware permutation via k-means clustering, which simultaneously improves identification accuracy (by grouping semantically similar tokens) and reduces computation waste (by densifying critical tokens into contiguous blocks). The top-p selection strategy is borrowed from Tactic and Twilight, and the permutation idea draws conceptual inspiration from PIT and MMInference's modality-aware permutation, but is applied here to semantic rather than positional or modality-based grouping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVG2 provides a training-free, drop-in acceleration for existing video DiTs (HunyuanVideo, Wan 2.1) achieving up to 2.3× end-to-end speedup while maintaining PSNR ≥ 26, making high-quality video generation substantially more practical on single GPUs. The semantic-aware permutation principle—cluster by activation semantics rather than position, then permute for hardware-friendly layout—is generalizable and could inform sparse attention design in other long-sequence transformer settings. The main limitation acknowledged is that the method is only evaluated on DiT-based video models, leaving open whether the approach transfers to other attention architectures (e.g., linear attention, SSM hybrids) or to image generation. The reliance on k-means per head per layer, even with centroid caching, introduces a non-trivial overhead that may limit applicability to very small or very large sequence lengths.
