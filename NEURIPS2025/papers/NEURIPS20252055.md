## Problem

Discrete (binary) reward functions in GRPO-based LLM training cause severe gradient vanishing and explosion, leading to unstable optimization and slow convergence. While such rule-based rewards prevent reward hacking, their minimal variance within mini-batches provides insufficient learning signal, particularly in early training when most outputs receive identical zero rewards. The paper asks whether a simple perturbation to the reward signal can restore the variance needed for efficient gradient-based optimization without sacrificing reward correctness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a batch of G discrete rewards (e.g., 0/1 correctness scores) computed by a rule-based reward function for sampled outputs, along with noise parameters (Gaussian standard deviation σ or Uniform radius a, typically a=0.05). It produces smoothed continuous rewards r̃ᵢ = rᵢ + εᵢ, which replace the raw discrete rewards in the GRPO advantage normalization. The output is an updated policy π_θ via the standard GRPO objective, with the only modification being the use of perturbed rewards in the advantage computation. The method is a drop-in modification to the reward function, requiring no changes to the policy architecture or optimizer.

## Method

ReDit injects independently sampled zero-mean perturbations (Gaussian N(0,σ²) or Uniform U[-a,a]) into each discrete reward before computing GRPO's batch-relative advantage, transforming the advantage from a ratio of discrete reward differences to a ratio of continuous smoothed reward differences. This single modification preserves the overall GRPO objective structure while converting sparse, identical-reward batches into ones with informative continuous variation, providing non-zero gradients even when all outputs receive the same binary score. Two theoretical propositions formalize the mechanism: Proposition 1 proves the perturbation yields an unbiased gradient estimate of the original objective (since E[ε]=0), and Proposition 2 shows the injected noise adds a controlled gradient variance term σ²·E[‖∇_θ log π_θ‖²] that simultaneously mitigates vanishing gradients (by providing stochastic updates in flat regions) and exploding gradients (by probabilistically bypassing high-curvature regions). The approach is algorithm-agnostic, demonstrated to work with GRPO, DAPO, Dr.GRPO, and REINFORCE++, and requires only a one-line modification to the reward function.

## Results

On GSM8K, ReDit (Gaussian) achieves 90.76% test accuracy vs. 89.07% for vanilla GRPO (+1.69), and on MATH it reaches 52.55% vs. 48.01% (+4.54). On Geometry3K, ReDit attains 44.67% vs. 43.10% for GRPO (+1.57). Notably, ReDit reaches performance comparable to full 9000-step GRPO training in only ~1000 steps (~10% of training), while still yielding a further ~4% improvement when trained for the same duration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReDit consistently outperforms vanilla GRPO across all three primary datasets, with the largest gain on MATH (+4.54 points) where discrete binary rewards cause the most severe gradient instability. When applied to alternative RL baselines on GSM8K, ReDit improves DAPO from 87.52% to 89.34% (+1.82), Dr.GRPO from 86.13% to 87.69% (+1.56), and REINFORCE++ from 86.25% to 87.96% (+1.71), confirming generality across policy-gradient variants. Against direct gradient-manipulation baselines (Gradient Clipping and Dynamic Sampling), ReDit substantially outperforms both, as these heuristics only address one failure mode (explosion or vanishing) while ReDit simultaneously mitigates both. The Gaussian perturbation variant consistently edges out the Uniform variant by a small margin across all settings, and the CosineReverse scheduled perturbation yields the strongest final performance among scheduling strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO framework introduced by DeepSeek-R1, which uses discrete rule-based rewards (e.g., binary correctness) for LLM policy optimization. It departs from prior gradient-level fixes for instability (gradient clipping, DAPO's dynamic sampling) by instead modifying the reward signal itself, drawing on theoretical results from Razin et al. [41] showing that reward variance is inversely related to convergence time. The work also connects to the 'accuracy paradox' literature [34,35,36] demonstrating that perfectly accurate reward models can paradoxically hinder optimization, and positions ReDit as a minimal intervention that preserves reward correctness while injecting the variance that theory predicts is necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReDit offers a trivially simple, drop-in modification (adding one line of noise to the reward function) that yields ~10× training speedup and ~4% absolute accuracy gains across multiple LLMs, datasets, and RL algorithms, making it immediately practical for anyone training LLMs with GRPO-style objectives. The clean ablation showing ReDit has no effect on already-continuous reward-model scores validates that the benefit is specifically tied to discrete-reward pathology. Key open questions include automatic variance scheduling (the paper acknowledges manual tuning is needed) and whether the approach extends to multi-step or dense reward settings beyond the sequence-level binary rewards tested here.
