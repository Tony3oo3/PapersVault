## Problem

Video diffusion models rely on 3D dense attention whose quadratic scaling with token count makes training and inference on long videos computationally prohibitive. Existing sparse attention approaches either use dynamic per-head profiling (e.g., SVG) that is unreliable on unseen distributions and incompatible with training, or fixed local windows (e.g., STA) that limit long-range temporal dependencies. The paper asks whether a single static sparse pattern can match the expressiveness of dynamic methods while enabling efficient training and length extension.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a generated video by modifying the attention computation inside pre-trained video diffusion transformers (HunyuanVideo at 720p/125 frames, Wan2.1-14B at 720p/81 frames, Mochi 1 at 480p/162 frames). For length extension, it generates videos up to 4× the default length (e.g., 509 frames for HunyuanVideo, 667 for Mochi 1) after lightweight LoRA fine-tuning on 2k sampled videos from OpenVid-1M. The sparse mask is applied in the latent token space where a 5-second 720p video corresponds to roughly 110K tokens, and attention is computed over 128×128 blocks for hardware efficiency.

## Method

Radial Attention exploits an observed Spatiotemporal Energy Decay phenomenon—post-softmax attention scores decay exponentially with both spatial and temporal distance between tokens—and translates this into a static O(n log n) sparse mask. The mask is organized into 2⌈log₂(f)⌉−1 diagonal bands centered on the main diagonal: the central band retains full compute density, while each successive outer band halves the density and doubles the diagonal width, so that distant frames receive progressively sparser attention. When the diagonal width drops below one token, the mask switches to retaining only same-position tokens in sub-sampled frames to maintain the same amortized density decay. For long-video extension, the pre-trained model is adapted via LoRA (rank 128) on Q, K, V, and output projections, which the paper shows matches or exceeds full fine-tuning quality while reducing training cost by up to 4.4×. A theoretical ℓ₁ error bound of O(C_rel · e^{−min(β/2, α)·s}) confirms that the approximation error decays exponentially with the spatial token count per frame.

## Results

At default video length, Radial Attention achieves 1.88× speedup on HunyuanVideo (PSNR 27.3, LPIPS 0.114) and 1.77× on Wan2.1-14B (PSNR 23.9, LPIPS 0.163) while matching dense-attention quality. For 4× longer video generation (509 frames on HunyuanVideo), it achieves 88.3% sparsity with 4.37× training speedup and 3.71× inference speedup, attaining a Vision Reward of 0.134 that surpasses the original model's 0.054 at that length. The method reduces attention computation by 9× compared to dense attention for 4× longer videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVG (the closest prior method), Radial Attention matches quality at default length (PSNR 27.3 vs. 27.2 on HunyuanVideo) while being a static mask that supports training, unlike SVG's dynamic profiling which fails on unseen distributions. Radial Attention clearly outperforms STA on all similarity metrics (PSNR 27.3 vs. 26.7, LPIPS 0.114 vs. 0.167 on HunyuanVideo) and PowerAttention by a large margin (PSNR 27.3 vs. 22.1), confirming that video-specific spatiotemporal structure matters. For 4× longer video generation, Radial Attention (Vision Reward 0.134) substantially outperforms RIFLEx (0.037), SANA/linear attention (−0.205), and LongLoRA (0.130), while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning with dense attention (0.133). On Wan2.1-14B at 2× length, Radial Attention achieves Vision Reward 0.145 versus 0.150 for full fine-tuning, at 1.93× lower training cost and 2.01× faster inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

Radial Attention directly builds on SVG (Sparse VideoGen), which dynamically classifies each attention head as spatial or temporal and applies a corresponding mask at inference time. The key departure is replacing this dynamic, inference-only profiling with a single static mask that unifies both spatial and temporal locality into an O(n log n) pattern, making it usable during training as well. The paper also positions itself against other O(n log n) attention mechanisms (PowerAttention, Reformer, H-Transformer) by exploiting the inherent spatio-temporal structure of video tokens rather than treating the sequence as flat, and against linear attention methods (SANA, LinFusion) by preserving the softmax mechanism and local detail. The LoRA-based length extension strategy draws on LongLoRA's shifted local attention idea but applies it in the video diffusion setting with the Radial mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 3.7× inference speedup and 4.4× training cost reduction for 4× longer videos makes long video generation far more accessible, while the 1.9× speedup at default length is immediately deployable. The static mask design is a notable architectural choice because it enables training (unlike SVG's dynamic profiling) and is hardware-friendly (block-sparse, FlashInfer-compatible), increasing the likelihood of broad adoption. Key limitations remain: the method is still O(n²) in spatial resolution, the exponential decay assumption (Eq. 3) may not capture all long-range dependencies in natural video, and the quality gains over dense attention at default length are marginal (matching rather than exceeding). Open questions include whether more principled decay models or adaptive masks could further close the gap to dense attention at very long sequences, and how the approach extends to higher resolutions where the spatial quadratic term dominates.
