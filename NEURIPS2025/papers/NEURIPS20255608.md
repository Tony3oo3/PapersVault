## Problem

Existing event-based recognition methods are constrained to a narrow distribution of event lengths (10^6–10^7) due to quadratic attention complexity, and they degrade significantly (up to 20.69% accuracy drop) when the inference sampling frequency differs from the training frequency. This limits the full exploitation of event cameras' microsecond temporal resolution for high-speed or long-duration scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255608/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams from DVS cameras (e.g., DVS346 at 346×240), which are first sampled at a fixed frequency f and grouped into P = T·f event groups, then converted to RGB event frames F ∈ R^{P×H×W×3}. The PEAS module adaptively selects K frames from P (where P varies with event length from 10^6 to 10^9 events), producing a fixed-dimension 1D sequence via bidirectional scan. The output is a single class label for object or action recognition, with the model handling arbitrary event durations from ~1 s to ~263 s.

## Method

PASS combines three components: (1) a Path-selective Event Aggregation and Scan (PEAS) module that uses a lightweight 3D-convolution score predictor with Gumbel-Softmax to learn a differentiable selection mask M ∈ R^{K×P}, selecting K informative frames from P aggregated event frames and converting them to a fixed-length 1D sequence via a bidirectional left-to-right, top-to-bottom scan; (2) a Multi-faceted Selection Guiding (MSG) loss comprising a Within-Frame Event Information Entropy (WEIE) term that maximizes the information content of each selected frame and an Inter-frame Event Mutual Information (IEMI) term that minimizes redundancy between consecutive selected frames, jointly reducing the randomness and overlap introduced by random initialization; (3) a stack of B-Mamba (bidirectional Mamba) blocks with linear complexity for spatiotemporal modeling, initialized from pre-trained VideoMamba checkpoints. The key insight is that SSMs' linear complexity and robust frequency generalization make them ideal for high-temporal-resolution events, but their sensitivity to sequence length and token order necessitates the PEAS module to normalize variable-length event streams into fixed-dimension sequences before SSM processing.

## Results

On the three standard action recognition datasets (~10⁷ events), PASS-M-K(8) achieves 98.28% Top-1 on PAF (+3.45% over ExACT), PASS-M-K(16) reaches 66.38% on SeAct (+0.38% over ExACT), and PASS-S-K(16) attains 98.41% on HARDVS (+8.31% over ExACT). For object recognition (~10⁶ events), PASS-M-K(2) scores 94.60% on N-Caltech101 and 61.32% on N-Imagenet, outperforming the prior best by +2.25% and +3.43% respectively. On the proposed arbitrary-duration datasets (~10⁹ events), PASS-M-K(32) achieves 97.35% on ArDVS100, PASS-S-K(32) reaches 100% on Real-ArDVS10, and PASS-T-K(32) scores 89.00% on TemArDVS100. Across varying inference frequencies, PASS exhibits a maximum accuracy drop of only −8.62% versus −20.69% for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASS outperforms the strongest prior method ExACT [77] (471M params) on all three action recognition datasets: +3.45% on PAF (98.28% vs 94.83%), +0.38% on SeAct (66.38% vs 66.07%), and +8.31% on HARDVS (98.41% vs 90.10%), while using 6–19× fewer parameters. Against the SSM-based S5-ViT [80] (17.5M params), PASS-T-K(16) (7M params) surpasses it by +1.91% on PAF (94.83% vs 92.93%) and by +2.46% on SeAct (62.07% vs 59.61% best). On object recognition, PASS-M-K(2) (7M params) beats EventDance [74] by +2.25% on N-Caltech101 (94.60% vs 92.35%) and MEM [31] by +3.43% on N-Imagenet (61.32% vs 57.89%). On the proposed ~10⁹-event datasets, PASS-T-K(32) exceeds S5-ViT-B-K(32) [80] by +9.38% on TemArDVS100 (89.00% vs 79.62%) and by +3.96% on ArDVS100 (97.35% vs 93.39%). The frequency generalization gap is stark: PASS suffers at most −8.62% accuracy drop across 20–100 Hz inference frequencies, while the Time Windows baseline drops up to −29.32%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the selective state space model lineage (Mamba, VideoMamba) and the first SSM-for-events work by Zubic et al. (S5-ViT, CVPR 2024), but departs in two key ways: it replaces the S5/S4D backbone with B-Mamba for its superior selectivity, and it introduces the PEAS module to adaptively select and scan a fixed number of event frames from arbitrarily long sequences, rather than processing all sampled frames. The MSG loss (combining within-frame entropy and inter-frame mutual information) is a novel training signal specifically designed to stabilize the Gumbel-Softmax-based selection, addressing a gap not addressed by prior SSM-for-events work. The paper also extends the evaluation scope from the ~10^6–10^7 event regime of existing datasets to 10^9 events via newly synthesized and recorded benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency generalization result (−8.62% vs. −20.69% drop) is practically significant for deploying event-based recognition in real-world scenarios where inference frequency may differ from training, and the new long-duration datasets (ArDVS100, TemArDVS100, Real-ArDVS10) fill a clear gap in the event vision benchmark landscape. However, the 10^9-event results rely heavily on synthetic concatenation of HARDVS clips, and the real-world validation set contains only 100 samples across 10 classes, leaving open whether the approach truly generalizes to genuinely long, non-repetitive event streams. The paper also notes that larger Mamba variants overfit, and no code is released, which limits reproducibility and broader adoption.
