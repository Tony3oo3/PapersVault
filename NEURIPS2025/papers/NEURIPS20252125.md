## Problem

Existing visual reasoning and manipulation benchmarks either use discrete action spaces, dense coordinate-based rewards that reduce tasks to smooth optimization, or lack a principled axis for controlling and measuring generalization. KNOTGYM addresses this by introducing a knot manipulation environment where task complexity is quantified by the number of crossings, goals are defined by topological equivalence (Gauss code) rather than exact configuration matching, and a natural train-test complexity split enables systematic study of generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252125/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent observes a single RGB image of shape [3, 128, 256], formed by horizontally concatenating the z-plane projections of the current manipulated knot and the goal exemplar knot. The larger-than-typical image resolution is necessary because over/under-crossings span only a few pixels and are critical for determining the Gauss code. The output is a 6-dimensional action vector (x, y, z, f_x, f_y, f_z) ∈ [-1,1]^6 specifying a 3D point on the rope (snapped to the nearest key point) and a force vector to apply, abstracting away specific robot end-effectors.

## Method

KNOTGYM is a MuJoCo-simulated rope environment implementing the Gymnasium API, where the reward is sparse and based on a symbolic oracle that computes the Gauss code of the current knot and checks topological equivalence with the goal. Three tasks (unknot, tie, convert) are defined by different initial/goal crossing configurations, with complexity tunable from 0 to 4 crossings. The paper benchmarks four method classes under a fixed 1M-step budget: model-free RL (PPO), model-based RL (DreamerV3), model-predictive control (TD-MPC2), and VLM chain-of-thought prompting (GPT-4.1-nano in open, stateless, and stateful modes). Key findings show DreamerV3 achieves ~93% on unknot but all methods collapse to near-random performance on tie and convert at #X=4, and VLMs fail to produce sufficiently precise grounded actions despite correctly identifying the goal Gauss code.

## Results

On the easiest task (unknot), DreamerV3 achieves the highest success rates: 93.3% at #X=2, 93.4% at #X=3, and 89.3% at #X=4, outperforming TD-MPC2 (71.3/55.4/50.3%) and PPO (65.7/63.3/37.0%). On the harder tie and convert tasks, all RL methods degrade sharply with increasing crossings: at #X=4, no method exceeds the random baseline (e.g., tie #X=4: DreamerV3 4.1% vs. random 1.4%; convert #X=4: DreamerV3 5.4% vs. random 2.9%). VLM prompting (GPT-4.1-nano) performs worst overall, with success rates at or below random on most setups, except unknot #X=2 (stateful: 20.9%) and #X=3 (stateful: 17.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamerV3 (model-based RL) is the strongest RL method across all nine setups, achieving 89–93% on unknot and 4–83% on tie/convert, consistently outperforming PPO and TD-MPC2. PPO and TD-MPC2 are competitive on unknot #X=2 (65.7% and 71.3% respectively) but fall well behind DreamerV3 as complexity increases, with PPO dropping to 37.0% on unknot #X=4. On tie and convert at #X≥3, all three RL methods struggle: only DreamerV3 clearly exceeds the random baseline at #X=3 (16.1% vs. 2.5% on tie), while at #X=4 all methods are merely marginally above random. GPT-4.1-nano prompting (all three modes: open, stateless, stateful) is the weakest approach overall, scoring 0% on most tie and convert setups and only exceeding random on unknot #X=2 and #X=3 in stateful mode (20.9% and 17.0%). The stateful prompting mode consistently outperforms stateless and open modes, suggesting that observation history helps VLMs track system dynamics, though the gap remains small.

## Relation to Prior Work

KNOTGYM carves new ground at the intersection of deformable object manipulation and formal knot theory. It departs from prior rope manipulation benchmarks like SoftGym (Lin et al., 2020), which use dense coordinate-wise distance rewards, by replacing the goal with a topological equivalence criterion (Gauss code) that requires abstract spatial reasoning rather than smooth optimization. The environment also draws on the narrow-focus design philosophy of lilGym (Wu et al., 2023) but transplants it from language-grounded rigid-object relations to continuous 3D rope deformation. Rather than building on a specific prior method, the paper repurposes standard RL algorithms (PPO, DreamerV3, TD-MPC2) and VLM prompting as test subjects against a newly structured problem space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factorial complexity ladder provided by knot crossings offers a rare, mathematically grounded generalization axis for visual spatial reasoning — a clean train/test split by number of crossings that most physical reasoning benchmarks lack. The key open question is whether any learned policy can generalize from #X=2 to #X=4 on goal-conditioned tasks (tie, convert), where all current methods fail to exceed random performance. The paper also highlights a striking dissociation in VLMs: they correctly identify the goal Gauss code and form reasonable plans, yet their predicted forces are too imprecise to execute, pointing to a fundamental gap between visual understanding and grounded continuous action. Limitations include CPU-only simulation throughput (~20 steps/s), restricted to reducible knots, and a single VLM (GPT-4.1-nano) for prompting evaluation.
