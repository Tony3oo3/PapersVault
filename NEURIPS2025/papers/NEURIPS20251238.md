## Problem

While Nesterov-style momentum methods achieve O(1/n²) acceleration in Euclidean optimization, extending such acceleration to Riemannian manifolds has required strong geometric assumptions—upper and lower curvature bounds, diameter constraints, or intractable nonlinear operators. The paper asks whether the simpler silver stepsize schedule, which accelerates vanilla gradient descent in Euclidean space to O(n^{-log₂ρ}) without any algorithmic modification, can be transferred to Riemannian optimization, and if so, under what minimal geometric conditions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a geodesically convex differentiable function f on a Riemannian manifold, an initial point x₀, a fixed base point b, a linear vector transport operator VT, and a smoothness constant L. It produces iterates converging to the minimizer x* at rate O(n^{-log₂ρ}) ≈ O(n^{-1.2716}), with the error measured as ‖log_b x_n − log_b x*‖²_b rather than the geodesic distance d(x_n, x*). A notable feature is that in 2-Wasserstein space with VT = Γ (parallel transport), the algorithm reduces exactly to standard Wasserstein gradient descent, so no algorithmic modification is needed there—only the stepsize schedule changes.

## Method

The core contribution is Vector Transported Riemannian Gradient Descent (VTRGD), which anchors all updates to a fixed base point b via vector transport: x_{n+1} = exp_b(log_b x_n − (η_n/L) VT_{x_n}^b Grad f(x_n)). This differs from standard RGD (which uses exp_{x_n}(−η_n Grad f(x_n))) because dynamic stepsize methods require tracking relationships between non-consecutive iterates, which the local exponential map in standard RGD cannot express. The paper introduces VT-geodesic convexity and VT-geodesic L-smoothness as the natural function classes under which the inductively constructed silver stepsize sequence (with ρ = 1+√2) provably achieves the accelerated rate, and a restarting scheme extends this to exponential convergence for strongly convex objectives. Crucially, no curvature or diameter bounds on the manifold are required, and when VT = Γ in 2-Wasserstein space, VTRGD coincides with classical Wasserstein gradient descent, yielding the first provable acceleration for potential functional optimization in that space.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) establishes that silver stepsize VTRGD achieves a convergence rate of O(n^{-log₂ρ}) ≈ O(n^{-1.2716}) for VT-geodesically convex and L-smooth objectives, improving upon the O(1/n) rate of constant stepsize RGD. For the strongly convex case (Theorem 4.2), the method achieves an exponential rate of exp(-O(n/κ^{log_ρ 2})), improving upon the O(κ log(1/ε)) iterations needed by constant stepsize RGD. Numerical experiments on the Bures-Wasserstein space (potential functional optimization in R¹⁰) and SPD(50) (linear minus log-determinant) confirm that silver stepsize consistently outperforms constant stepsize across condition numbers κ = 10¹ to 10¹³, with the gap widening as κ increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares silver stepsize VTRGD against constant stepsize RGD (the standard algorithm from [ZS16, KY22]) as the primary baseline. In the Bures-Wasserstein space experiments (Figure 2), silver stepsize WGD outperforms constant stepsize WGD across all tested condition numbers (κ = 10¹, 10³, 10⁷, 10¹³), with the performance gap growing substantially as κ increases. In the SPD(50) linear-minus-log-determinant experiments (Figure 3), VTRGD with silver stepsize similarly outperforms constant stepsize RGD for both κ = 10 and κ = 10⁵. The key practical advantage over existing accelerated Riemannian methods (e.g., [LSC⁺17, KY22, HMJG23]) is that silver stepsize VTRGD requires neither upper nor lower sectional curvature bounds nor diameter bounds on the domain, making it applicable to spaces like the 2-Wasserstein space where such bounds do not exist. The method also avoids the intractable nonlinear operators required by [LSC⁺17] and the submanifold structure assumptions of [HMJG23].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the silver stepsize schedule from Euclidean optimization (Altschuler & Parrilo, 2024) to Riemannian manifolds by introducing a vector-transported RGD (VTRGD) update that replaces the standard exponential-map step with a base-point-referenced transport. It departs from the dominant line of Riemannian acceleration work (Liu et al. 2017, Zhang & Sra 2018, Alimisis et al. 2021, Kim & Yang 2022, Han et al. 2023), which relies on momentum-based extrapolation and requires sectional curvature bounds or diameter constraints, by instead achieving acceleration purely through a dynamic stepsize schedule under a new VT-geodesic convexity/smoothness framework. The strongly convex variant borrows the adaptive restarting scheme of O'Donoghue & Candès (2015), adapted to the Riemannian setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that VTRGD with parallel transport exactly recovers standard Wasserstein gradient descent, yielding the first provable accelerated convergence guarantee for potential functional optimization in 2-Wasserstein space—a setting where all prior momentum-based accelerated methods fail due to the absence of an upper curvature bound. The VT-geodesic convexity/smoothness framework is a genuinely new conceptual tool, but the authors acknowledge it is unclear how restrictive these conditions are beyond the Wasserstein and SPD examples. Open questions include whether standard RGD (without the VT modification) can be accelerated, whether stochastic or proximal variants exist, and whether the silver stepsize can be extended to arbitrary iteration counts or matched to the O(n^{-2}) rate of Nesterov's method in the Riemannian setting.
