## Problem

Existing 3D head texturing methods either bake hair and its specific hairline directly into the texture—making the result incompatible with any other hairstyle—or restrict textures to the facial region, neglecting the full scalp. Achieving seamless 360° coverage typically demands expensive multi-view datasets with precise tracking, and no prior method produces a fully bald, compositionally neutral head texture that can serve as a base for arbitrary 3D hair, eyeglasses, or jewelry assets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D FLAME head mesh (parameterized by shape α, expression δ, and pose θ), a free-form text prompt, and precise skin/ear segmentation masks rendered from the mesh as conditioning. At inference the mesh is rendered from 14 viewpoints with a uniform background; the output is a complete 1024×1024 UV texture map in FLAME's atlas space, fully bald and photorealistic, with no hair, accessories, or synthetic data required during training. The texture generalizes beyond human skin to non-human and stylized appearances (e.g., animal fur, gemstone materials) by adjusting classifier-free guidance and ControlNet strength.

## Method

HairFree fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion 2.1 latent diffusion model with a ControlNet branch on ~100K mostly frontal face images (FFHQ + CELEB-A-HQ), conditioning on rendered FLAME mesh geometry, face-parsing masks (skin, hair, ears, accessories), and background, for ~1500 GPU hours on an H100. The key architectural insight is that the semantic conditioning—particularly the hair mask used only during training—teaches the model to learn hair locations so it can cleanly remove them at inference, yielding a bald scalp without any bald training data. At inference, the pipeline first generates a high-quality frontal view, splats its pixels onto the UV atlas, then iteratively rotates a virtual camera through 14 viewpoints: visible regions are rendered and accumulated via visibility-checked UV splatting, while unseen regions are filled by a diffusion inpainting model in image space before re-mapping. Progressive front-to-back inpainting with reduced ControlNet strength avoids the 'Janus effect' (hallucinating a face on back views), and morphological erosion plus bilinear interpolation at UV boundaries prevent blending seams, producing a seamless 360° texture in a single-stage, unsupervised pipeline.

## Results

In a user study with 87 and 94 participants for two identities, HairFree is preferred by ~40% of users (40.23% and 39.36%), roughly double the next-best method DreamCraft3D (18.39% and 14.89%). In the runtime/quality comparison (Table 1), HairFree achieves 'High' quality in minutes with a single stage, matching FaceLift's quality rating while requiring fewer stages than DreamCraft3D (4 stages, 3 hours) or Arc2Avatar (2–3 stages, 1–2 hours). At full ControlNet strength (CS=1.0), the 2D prior achieves FID 6.6, KID 0.0027, and PSNR 18.38 on 10K samples, with diversity increasing as CS is reduced (FID rises to 384 at CS=0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HairFree outperforms all 12 3D avatar baselines in the user study, winning ~40% of votes versus 15–18% for DreamCraft3D (the clear runner-up) and ~7–10% for FaceLift, Arc2Avatar, and DiffSplat. Among SDS-based methods (Zero123-XL, Magic123, TADA!), the paper reports oversaturation, low detail, and inconsistent geometry, all rated 'Low' quality. Gaussian-splatting methods (DreamCraft3D, DiffSplat) produce blurry, low-frequency textures; DreamCraft3D is the only other 'High'-quality method but requires 4 stages and 3 hours versus HairFree's 1 stage in minutes. FaceLift matches HairFree's 'High' quality rating but hallucinates hair despite bald constraints and suffers from view-specific illumination artifacts. Arc2Avatar produces a full 360° avatar but shows entanglement (visible clothing, non-bald hairline). FitMe, Relightify, and DreamFace are limited to the facial region or jointly reconstruct geometry, preventing clean bald-scalarp textures. For the 2D bald-proxy baselines (HairMapper, HairCLIPv2, LDM Inpaint), HairFree preserves head shape and pose more accurately while fully removing hair, whereas HairCLIPv2 still generates hair due to limited bald training data and HairMapper degrades overall image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

HairFree builds directly on the Stable Diffusion LDM architecture and ControlNet conditioning mechanism, fine-tuning them on 100K frontal face images with 3D geometry and semantic mask inputs. It departs from prior 3D head texturing methods (DreamFace, FitMe, Arc2Avatar, TADA) that either bake hair into textures, cover only the facial region, or rely on SDS-based multi-view synthesis, by introducing a compositional 2D prior that explicitly disentangles skin from hair and a progressive image-space inpainting pipeline that accumulates a full 360° UV texture on a FLAME mesh. The key conceptual departure is treating bald, hair-free texture generation as a first-class objective rather than a byproduct of face reconstruction, enabling truly compositional 3D layering of independent hair and accessory assets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D avatar pipelines: the inability to swap hairstyles, hats, or accessories without visible seams or hairline mismatches. By producing a clean, hair-free 360° base texture from a single text prompt without any multi-view or bald training data, it lowers the barrier to customizable digital humans for gaming and VR. Open questions include handling of lighting inconsistencies (the authors note sensitivity to illumination and suggest intrinsic decomposition as a fix), the extent to which biases in the underlying diffusion prior propagate into generated textures, and whether the progressive inpainting strategy scales to more complex geometry beyond the FLAME head model.
