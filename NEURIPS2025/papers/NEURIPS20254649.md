## Problem

Autoregressive image generation models produce high-fidelity images but suffer from slow sequential token-by-token decoding. While speculative decoding has accelerated text generation, its direct transfer to image generation is hindered by the vastly larger sampling space (20–400× more vocabulary candidates) and the two-dimensional spatial structure of images, which text-oriented speculative methods ignore. No prior work has effectively exploited the vertical spatial dependencies in images to improve draft-target alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt and produces a 768×768 RGB image, represented internally as a sequence of discrete VQ-VAE tokens generated in raster scan order (left-to-right, top-to-bottom). Inference uses top-k sampling with k=2000, temperature 1.0, and classifier-free guidance at scale 3.0 on the Lumina-mGPT base model. The acceleration is achieved entirely at inference time without modifying the base model's weights or changing the output distribution.

## Method

Hawk introduces dual-direction draft heads that speculate simultaneously along the horizontal axis (positions T+1 through T+n) and the vertical axis (positions T+IW×VSD, i.e., tokens in subsequent rows), motivated by attention-sinking experiments showing that image generation attends strongly to spatially adjacent tokens from the previous row. Vertical predictions are cached and, at each speculation point, combined with horizontal predictions to form a two-dimensional spatial sampling pool from which tree-structured candidates are generated via Cartesian product. These candidates are verified in a single forward pass of the target model using standard speculative sampling with min(1, p_target/p_draft) acceptance, and the paper proves that combining multiple draft distributions from distinct spatial directions preserves the original model's output distribution while reducing the residual rejection probability. Only the lightweight draft heads are trained (on 6,000 LAION images, ~8–12 hours on one RTX 3090), adding less than 1% parameters to the base model, yielding a 1.71× speedup with negligible FID/CLIP degradation.

## Results

On COCO 2017, Hawk with spatial draft heads achieves a 1.71× inference speedup over vanilla autoregressive decoding while maintaining a FID of 90.71 (vs. 90.68 for the baseline) and a CLIP score of 33.39, with an average accepted speculative length of 1.890 tokens per step. On Flickr30K, the method achieves a 1.69× speedup with FID 104.50 and CLIP score 34.45. The spatial draft heads consistently outperform both horizontal-only (Medusa) and relaxed-verification (LANTERN++) baselines in accepted length and image quality metrics across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Medusa (horizontal-only draft heads), Hawk with spatial heads achieves a higher speedup (1.71× vs. 1.58× on COCO 2017; 1.69× vs. 1.55× on Flickr30K) and a substantially better FID (90.71 vs. 92.51 on COCO 2017), while also improving the accepted speculative length from 1.728 to 1.890. Against LANTERN++, which relaxes the verification threshold to boost acceptance, Hawk achieves a comparable speedup (1.71× vs. 1.69×) but with markedly better image quality: FID of 90.71 vs. 93.22 on COCO 2017 and 104.50 vs. 106.10 on Flickr30K, as well as higher CLIP scores (33.39 vs. 33.08 and 34.45 vs. 34.25). The paper attributes LANTERN++'s quality degradation to its relaxed acceptance criterion, which admits inaccurate draft predictions. On Flickr30K, Medusa slightly edges out Hawk on FID (103.51 vs. 104.50) and CLIP (34.48 vs. 34.45), suggesting dataset-dependent spatial dependency patterns, but Hawk still leads in speedup and accepted length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends speculative decoding from the 1D text domain to the 2D image domain by introducing spatial draft heads that speculate along both horizontal and vertical axes. It builds directly on Medusa's draft-head architecture and the speculative sampling verification framework, but departs from prior image acceleration work (LANTERN++, SJD) by preserving the strict verification criterion rather than relaxing acceptance thresholds. The attention-sinking analysis, adapted from streaming LLM literature, provides the empirical motivation for why vertical spatial context is informative in AR image generation—a dependency absent in text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hawk demonstrates that the 2D spatial structure of images is a previously untapped resource for accelerating AR image generation, and the finding that vertical speculation outperforms horizontal speculation at equivalent Euclidean distance challenges the intuition that closer-in-sequence tokens are easier to predict. However, the evaluation is limited to a single model (Lumina-mGPT) and a single GPU, and the 1.71× speedup is modest given the added draft-head overhead. Open questions include whether the spatial speculative decoding strategy generalizes to other AR image architectures (e.g., VAR, next-scale prediction) and whether integration with stronger speculative backbones (Eagle, Hydra) would yield substantially larger gains.
