## Problem

Existing super-resolution methods, even recent generative approaches, are limited to modest upscaling factors (≤4×), handle only a narrow range of degradations, and generalize poorly to out-of-distribution domains such as scientific imaging, AI-generated content, and medical modalities. No prior system can universally upscale an arbitrary image—regardless of input type, degradation severity, or domain—to 4K resolution without domain-specific retraining, nor can it flexibly adapt to user-specific restoration preferences (e.g., fidelity vs. perceptual quality).

## Input / Output

The system accepts any RGB image with resolution ≥250×250 (down to 256×256 with severe combined degradations) and automatically computes the required scale factor from {2, 4, 8, 16} to reach a 4096×4096 output. Beyond the raw image, the Perception Agent consumes computed IQA scores (CLIPIQA, TOPIQ, MUSIQ, NIQE) and a user-configurable Profile with seven parameters (VLM choice, scale factor, restore option, face restore toggle, brightening, and perception/fidelity preference). The primary output is a 4K photorealistic image, but the system also produces an interpretable restoration plan (an ordered sequence of operators like denoise→deblur→SR) and a per-step degradation diagnosis.

## Method

4KAgent is a multi-agent orchestration framework that requires no training; it composes existing restoration models (OSEDiff, DiffBIR, HAT, etc.) under the control of a Perception Agent and a Restoration Agent. The Perception Agent uses a VLM (e.g., Llama-vision) conditioned on multi-metric IQA scores to diagnose degradations, caption content, and generate a sequential restoration plan. The Restoration Agent executes each planned step via a Quality-Driven Mixture-of-Experts (Q-MoE) policy: the image is passed through every tool in the toolbox, and a reflection module scores all candidates using a weighted combination of HPSv2 human-preference and no-reference IQA metrics (NIQE, MANIQA, MUSIQ, CLIPIQA) to select the best output. A rollback mechanism reverts to the previous image and substitutes an alternative task if the quality score falls below a threshold. A dedicated Face Restoration Pipeline, triggered after the SR step, applies multiple face-restoration models and selects the best via a composite score that includes ArcFace identity-preservation similarity and CLIB-FIQA face quality, ensuring identity-consistent facial detail in portraits.

## Results

On the RealSR benchmark, 4KAgent(GenSR-s4-P) achieves the best no-reference perceptual quality with NIQE 4.78 and MUSIQ 71.77, outperforming OSEDiff (NIQE 5.65, MUSIQ 69.09) and SeeSR (NIQE 5.38, MUSIQ 69.37). On the B100 classical SR benchmark, 4KAgent(ExpSR-s4-F) matches HAT-L on PSNR (28.09 vs. 28.08) while improving NIQE (6.02 vs. 6.20), and 4KAgent(ExpSR-s4-P) achieves the best perceptual scores (NIQE 3.86, MUSIQ 69.42). On the multiple-degradation Group C benchmark, 4KAgent(GenMIR-P) sets new state-of-the-art on PSNR (19.77), MANIQA (0.3545), CLIPIQA (0.5233), and MUSIQ (55.56), surpassing the previous best MAIR by 0.35 dB PSNR and 4.2 points MUSIQ. For AIGC 4K upscaling, 4KAgent consistently reduces NIQE by 1.2–3.2 points across all tested generators (e.g., FLUX.1-dev from 6.18 to 2.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the RealSR benchmark, 4KAgent dominates on perceptual metrics: GenSR-s4-P achieves NIQE 4.78 and MUSIQ 71.77, beating the strongest diffusion-based baselines OSEDiff (5.65/69.09) and SeeSR (5.38/69.37) by 0.87 and 2.36 points respectively, while also outperforming the agentic baseline AgenticIR (5.81/65.87) by a wide margin. On B100 classical SR, 4KAgent(ExpSR-s4-F) matches HAT-L on PSNR (28.09 vs. 28.08) and slightly improves NIQE (6.02 vs. 6.20), while the perception-oriented profile (ExpSR-s4-P) surpasses OSEDiff on both NIQE (3.86 vs. 4.08) and MUSIQ (69.42 vs. 68.54). On the multiple-degradation Group C benchmark, 4KAgent(GenMIR-P) outperforms the previous best MAIR on all metrics except LPIPS, with notable gains in MANIQA (0.3545 vs. 0.2798) and MUSIQ (55.56 vs. 51.36), and also beats AgenticIR by 0.95 dB PSNR and 6.88 points MUSIQ. For AIGC 4K upscaling, 4KAgent consistently improves NIQE across all five tested generators (SANA, GPT-4o, FLUX.1-dev, PixArt-Σ, SD3-Medium), with the largest gain on FLUX.1-dev (6.18→2.98), though it slightly reduces MUSIQ-P in some cases, suggesting a trade-off between global and local quality. On the 16× RealSRSet and DIV4K-50 benchmarks, comparisons are primarily visual, with 4KAgent producing finer-grained details than HAT-L, DiffBIR, OSEDiff, and PiSA-SR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads: (1) diffusion-based real-world SR methods (OSEDiff, DiffBIR, StableSR) that handle unknown degradations but are limited to 4× scale; (2) agentic LLM/VLM frameworks for image restoration (AgenticIR, MAIR, RestoreAgent) that introduced planning-and-execution loops but underperformed specialized models; and (3) quality-driven tool selection via IQA metrics. 4KAgent departs from prior agentic systems by introducing a Q-MoE policy that runs all candidate tools in parallel and selects via a composite quality score, a rollback mechanism for failed steps, and a Profile Module for user-configurable behavior. It also extends the scale factor to 16× (4K output), a regime no prior SR or agentic restoration method addressed, and generalizes to scientific imaging domains (remote sensing, microscopy, medical) without domain-specific retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that a single agentic pipeline, without retraining, can serve as a universal 4K upscaler across natural, portrait, AIGC, and scientific imaging domains — a practical capability that previously required domain-specific models. The DIV4K-50 benchmark (256×256 → 4096×4096 with compound degradations) fills a gap in extreme-scale SR evaluation. Key open questions include the high computational cost of running N tools per step plus VLM calls (limiting real-time deployment), the sensitivity of the Q-MoE selection to the quality of IQA metrics, and whether the agentic planning overhead is justified over a well-tuned single diffusion model for standard 4× SR. The authors also flag safety concerns (privacy leakage, harmful content generation) as unresolved.
