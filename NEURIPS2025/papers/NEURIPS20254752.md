## Problem

Existing GUI trajectory evaluators rely on proprietary MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4V, QwenVL-Max) via tailored prompting, suffering from a significant domain gap between general-purpose reasoning and GUI-specific evaluation, as well as substantial computational cost and latency from API calls. No prior work has explored fine-tuning lightweight open-source MLLMs as trajectory evaluators through self-improvement, leaving a gap in efficient, domain-adapted evaluation for noisy GUI navigation datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254752/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluator receives a task instruction and a sequence of state-action pairs, where states are screenshots (capped at 802,816 pixels, cropped/resized preserving aspect ratio) and actions include click coordinates and scroll directions. To balance context richness against token overhead, only the last two screenshots are fed to the model. The output is a chain-of-thought reasoning enclosed in think/endthink tags followed by a binary Yes/No judgment on whether the trajectory successfully completed the task, trained with a language modeling objective rather than binary classification.

## Method

URST iteratively fine-tunes a lightweight MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) using its own generated thoughts and judgments, starting from a cold-start SFT on 300 proprietary-MLLM-annotated samples. Each iteration applies uncertainty-aware sampling: K outputs are generated per input via temperature sampling, and the top-L inputs with highest judgment entropy (measuring prediction uncertainty) are selected as the most informative training examples. For optimization, the paper introduces Simplified Group Policy Optimization (SGPO), a modified GRPO that computes group-level advantages from format and judgment rewards but removes the standard-deviation normalization present in original GRPO, thereby eliminating input-level difficulty bias and treating each training input equally. This design leverages both positive and negative samples for contrastive learning rather than discarding incorrect outputs as in rejection-sampling fine-tuning, and the entire loop is repeated for M iterations to progressively improve the evaluator.

## Results

URST achieves an overall F1 of 84.13% and accuracy of 86.39% across three trajectory evaluation test sets (AITW-ID-traj, AITW-OOD-traj, AW-OOD-traj), outperforming the best prompting-based baseline AutoEval* (81.48% F1) by 2.65% and the best fine-tuning baseline SFT (79.01% F1) by 5.12%. On the in-domain set, URST reaches 90.45% F1; on the cross-environment OOD set (AW-OOD-traj), it achieves 79.60% F1, a 7.69% gain over SFT. When used to filter noisy navigation data for GUI agent training, URST improves step success rate by up to 19.53% (AITW high-noise: 20.88% → 40.41%) and boosts online AndroidWorld success rate from 39.7% to 46.6% via Reflexion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

URST outperforms all six baselines on overall F1. Against the strongest prompting-based method AutoEval* (81.48% F1), URST gains +2.65% F1, with the largest margin on the cross-environment OOD set AW-OOD-traj (+3.70% F1). Against SFT (distillation from Qwen-VL-Max, 79.01% F1), URST improves by +5.12% F1, demonstrating that self-generated data with RL-based optimization surpasses externally annotated data. Among self-training baselines, STaR (76.60% F1) and ReSTEM (78.11% F1) both underperform SFT, while URST exceeds them by +7.53% and +6.02% F1 respectively, attributed to uncertainty-aware sampling and SGPO. On the in-domain set, URST leads all methods by at least 1.75% F1; on AITW-OOD-traj, it achieves the highest F1 (82.05%) though STaR edges it slightly on accuracy (95.00% vs 94.16%). On AgentRewardBench (web trajectories), URST also achieves the best F1 (77.85%) over AutoEval* (74.67%) and SFT (74.36%), confirming generalization beyond mobile GUI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) GUI trajectory evaluation, where prior work (AutoEval, WebVoyager, WebJudge, AgentTrek) relies on prompting proprietary MLLMs; (2) self-training for LLMs (STaR, ReST, ReSTEM), which iteratively fine-tunes models on their own outputs; and (3) group-level policy optimization (GRPO from DeepSeek), which contrasts multiple candidate responses per input. The key departure is combining uncertainty-aware sampling with a simplified GRPO variant (SGPO that removes standard-deviation normalization) to fine-tune a lightweight open-source MLLM as a trajectory evaluator — the first fine-tuning-based approach for this task, as opposed to the dominant prompting-based paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a 3B open-source evaluator that outperforms expensive proprietary MLLM API calls (Qwen-VL-Max) for GUI trajectory quality assessment, enabling low-cost dataset filtering that consistently improves downstream GUI agent training. The finding that self-generated synthetic data (84.13% F1) substantially outperforms data distilled from a proprietary MLLM (79.01%) challenges the assumption that larger teacher models always produce better training signals for domain-specific tasks. Limitations include evaluation on only the Qwen2-VL model family, restriction to mobile GUI (Android) tasks, and reliance on only the last two screenshots, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to computer GUIs, other model architectures, or longer trajectory contexts.
