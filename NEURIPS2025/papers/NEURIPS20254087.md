## Problem

Human motion sequences remain absent from general-purpose multi-modal embedding frameworks such as ImageBind and LanguageBind, which align images, text, audio, and video but lack a motion modality. While prior work has explored motion-text alignment in isolation, no unified framework simultaneously grounds motion in text, vision, and audio to enable cross-modal retrieval, zero-shot action recognition, and any-to-motion generation from a shared semantic space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D human motion sequences standardized to a 22-joint (or 21-joint for KIT-ML) skeleton representation at 20 FPS, with sequences longer than 196 frames segmented and shorter ones zero-padded. Conditioning inputs for generation include text prompts, video clips, or audio clips, each encoded by frozen LanguageBind encoders into 768-dimensional embeddings. Outputs include motion embeddings in the shared 768-dim space (for retrieval and recognition), reconstructed or generated 3D motion sequences decoded from the 512-dim MuTMoT latent space, and action class predictions via cosine similarity against text-encoded labels.

## Method

MotionBind comprises two components. MuTMoT is a hierarchical encoder-decoder with B=3 transformer blocks, each interleaving multi-head self-attention with 1D convolutional temporal downsampling (kernel 4, stride 2) to capture motion at multiple temporal resolutions; dataset-specific FiLM layers handle heterogeneous joint formats, and motion embedding tokens from each scale are fused via learned softmax-weighted averaging into a single 512-dim representation. Multi-modal alignment is achieved by projecting both MuTMoT motion embeddings and frozen LanguageBind modality embeddings through lightweight residual adapters into a common 768-dim space, trained with an InfoNCE contrastive loss augmented by motion-length-aware weighting and dynamic semantic margins that modulate negative pair penalties. REALM performs denoising diffusion in the MuTMoT latent space (L=T/4 frames, D=512), retrieving top-2 semantically similar motions as reference signals and injecting frame-specific conditioning through learnable temporal tokens refined by Time-Conditioned Cross-Attention blocks with adaptive layer normalization, combined with classifier-free guidance (ω=2.75) for faithful multi-modal generation.

## Results

On motion reconstruction, MuTMoT achieves the best FID (0.077 on KIT-ML, 0.031 on HumanML3D) and MPJPE (23.5 and 25.6 mm respectively), outperforming all prior autoencoders. For cross-modal retrieval, MuTMoT attains R@1 of 69.56% (HumanML3D text→motion) and 97.16% (AMASS video→motion), surpassing TMR and TEMOS across all datasets. On text-to-motion generation, REALM achieves R@1 of 0.511 and FID of 0.204 on HumanML3D, and R@1 of 0.435 and MM Dist of 2.718 on KIT-ML, placing it competitively with MoMask and ReMoDiffuse. In zero-shot action recognition, MuTMoT (N=0) reaches 53.48% Top-5 on BABEL-60, while the linear probe (MuTMoT-BABEL) matches the supervised 2s-AGCN baseline at 42.30% Top-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On motion reconstruction (Table 1), MuTMoT clearly dominates all baselines, achieving FID of 0.077 on KIT-ML (vs. 0.112 for MoMask, 0.413 for M2DM) and 0.031 on HumanML3D (vs. 0.019 for MoMask, 0.063 for M2DM), with the lowest MPJPE of 23.5 and 25.6 mm respectively. For cross-modal retrieval (Table 2), MuTMoT outperforms TMR [5] and TEMOS [13] on every dataset and direction, e.g., R@1 of 69.56% vs. 67.16% (TMR) on HumanML3D text→motion and 97.16% on AMASS video→motion with no prior baseline reported. In text-to-motion generation (Table 4), REALM is competitive but not uniformly best: on HumanML3D it trails MoMask [12] on FID (0.204 vs. 0.045) and MModality (2.483 vs. 1.241) while matching R-Precision closely (R@1 0.511 vs. 0.521); on KIT-ML it edges out MoMask on R@1 (0.435 vs. 0.433) and MM Dist (2.718 vs. 2.779) but lags on FID (0.264 vs. 0.204). For action recognition (Table 3), the supervised MuTMoT linear probe (42.30% Top-1 on BABEL-60) slightly exceeds 2s-AGCN (41.14%), while the zero-shot MuTMoT (N=0) achieves 53.48% Top-5 without any task-specific training, substantially outperforming MotionCLIP [6] (57.71% Top-1 but only on BABEL-60 with supervised training). Overall, the paper establishes clear wins on reconstruction and retrieval, competitive (not dominant) results on generation, and strong zero-shot recognition performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-modal representation learning lineage (CLIP → ImageBind → LanguageBind) by adding 3D human motion as a first-class modality in a shared embedding space, using a novel Multi-Scale Temporal Motion Transformer (MuTMoT) with FiLM-based dataset adaptation and hierarchical temporal downsampling. For generation, it builds on retrieval-augmented diffusion (ReMoDiffuse) and latent diffusion (MLD) but operates in the learned MuTMoT latent space rather than raw motion space, and introduces a temporal conditioning module with learnable frame tokens that adaptively attend to conditioning context at each denoising step. It departs from prior motion-language work (MotionCLIP, TMR, MotionGPT) by generalizing beyond text to include vision and audio modalities simultaneously, enabling emergent cross-modal capabilities without explicit training pairs for every modality combination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MotionBind is the first framework to integrate human motion into a general-purpose multi-modal embedding space alongside text, vision, and audio, enabling unified retrieval, zero-shot recognition, and any-to-motion generation from a single model. The zero-shot action recognition results (competitive with supervised baselines on BABEL) and strong cross-modal retrieval (97% R@1 on AMASS video-to-motion) demonstrate that multi-modal alignment transfers effectively to motion understanding. Key limitations include quantitative evaluation restricted to text-conditioned generation (audio/video conditioning only qualitatively shown), dependence on LanguageBind encoder quality, and sensitivity to retrieval coverage for out-of-distribution motions. Open questions include whether the framework scales to more diverse movement types, whether the multi-scale temporal encoding is necessary versus a single-scale transformer, and how to robustly evaluate generation quality across all supported modalities.
