## Problem

Autoregressive (AR) models for biological sequence generation are fundamentally limited by their unidirectional attention, which prevents them from capturing the global bidirectional token dependencies that are critical for interpreting biological sequences. Non-autoregressive (NAR) models learn rich bidirectional representations but suffer from generative coherence issues, length prediction errors, and training instability. No existing framework successfully combines the sequential generation stability of AR models with the holistic bidirectional contextual awareness of NAR models.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input an MS/MS mass spectrum represented as a peak list of (m/z, intensity) pairs, along with the precursor mass and charge state. Peaks are encoded via a domain-adapted sinusoidal scheme into 400-dimensional embeddings before being processed by the shared encoder. The output is a peptide amino acid sequence generated autoregressively with beam search (beam size 5), where each generation step is additionally conditioned on prefix and suffix mass constraints to enforce biochemical validity.

## Method

CROSSNOVO couples a shared 9-layer Transformer spectrum encoder with two specialized decoders: an AR decoder using causal self-attention for sequential peptide generation, and a NAT decoder using bidirectional (non-causal) self-attention that receives only positional embeddings as input to learn holistic spectral-to-sequence context. The key architectural innovation is a cross-decoder attention module in which the AR decoder's cross-attention queries attend to an augmented context formed by concatenating the NAT decoder's final-layer latent representations with the original spectrum features, using distinct positional encodings to differentiate the two sources. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 jointly optimizes both decoders under a multi-task loss (cross-entropy for AR, CTC for NAT) with importance annealing that linearly increases the AR loss weight from 0 to 1 over training; Stage 2 fine-tunes only the AR decoder with the cross-decoder attention active while applying gradient blocking (detaching NAT features) to prevent representational drift and ensure stable knowledge transfer.

## Results

On the 9-species-v1 benchmark, CROSSNOVO achieves an average amino acid precision of 0.811 and peptide recall of 0.654, surpassing the best autoregressive baseline ContraNovo (0.785 / 0.621) and the non-autoregressive baseline PrimeNovo (0.788 / 0.638). On the more challenging 9-species-v2 benchmark, CROSSNOVO reaches 0.906 AA precision and 0.786 peptide recall, again leading all baselines. A beam size of 5 provides the optimal accuracy–cost trade-off, with performance plateauing beyond that size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CROSSNOVO outperforms all nine baselines on both amino acid precision and peptide recall across the 9-species-v1 benchmark. Against the closest autoregressive competitor ContraNovo (AA 0.785, Peptide 0.621), CROSSNOVO gains +2.6 pp in AA precision and +3.3 pp in peptide recall. The non-autoregressive PrimeNovo (AA 0.788, Peptide 0.638) is also surpassed by +2.3 pp and +1.6 pp respectively, demonstrating that the hybrid architecture captures both NAT's bidirectional context and AT's sequential fidelity. Earlier AT models (Casanovo, InstaNovo, HelixNovo) trail by 7–14 pp in AA precision, while the database-search baseline PEAKS is far behind (0.663 / 0.322). On the harder 9-species-v2 benchmark, the same ordering holds: CROSSNOVO (0.906 / 0.786) beats ContraNovo (0.882 / 0.752) and PrimeNovo (0.891 / 0.777). In the zero-shot antibody downstream task (IgG1-Human), CROSSNOVO achieves up to 5% improvement in both peptide recall and AA precision over Casanovo V2 and ContraNovo without any antibody-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

CROSSNOVO sits at the intersection of two established paradigms in de novo peptide sequencing: autoregressive Transformer decoders (Casanovo, ContraNovo, HelixNovo) that generate tokens sequentially with high fidelity but limited bidirectional context, and non-autoregressive Transformers (PrimeNovo) that capture holistic sequence context in parallel but suffer from length-prediction errors and training instability. The paper departs from treating these as competing alternatives by proposing a hybrid architecture where a shared spectral encoder feeds both an AR decoder (for generation) and an NAR decoder (for bidirectional feature learning), with a novel cross-decoder attention module distilling NAR representations into the AR decoder's cross-attention. The training strategy—importance annealing to schedule the relative weight of AR vs. NAR losses, and cross-decoder gradient blocking to prevent representational drift—further distinguishes this from standard multi-task or knowledge-distillation setups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

De novo peptide sequencing is critical for applications where database search fails (antibody characterization, neoantigen discovery, metaproteomics), and the consistent 2–4% improvements over the strongest AR and NAR baselines across nine species and two benchmark versions make CROSSNOVO a practically relevant advance for the proteomics community. The cross-decoder attention with gradient blocking is a clean, generalizable architectural pattern that could inform hybrid AR/NAR designs in other sequence-generation domains. However, the evaluation is confined to a 9-species benchmark and a single antibody downstream task; performance on highly divergent organisms or non-peptide biological sequences remains untested, and the unidirectional (NAR→AR) knowledge transfer leaves open whether reciprocal transfer with proper information isolation could yield further gains.
