## Problem

Forecasting where a person's visual perception will focus in their 3D surrounding environment remains largely unexplored, despite its fundamental role in guiding human actions and its implications for AR/VR and assistive technologies. Existing 2D gaze anticipation is ill-posed in dynamic egocentric scenarios because it must jointly model self-motion and attention, both of which are inherently directed toward specific 3D locations rather than arbitrary 2D image regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254404/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal sequence of T_p frames, each represented as a 5×R×R×R binary occupancy grid (4 multi-level visual span channels plus 1 full-scene channel) derived from SLAM semidense keypoints, gaze direction vectors, and SE(3) camera poses within a D=3.2m cube at R=16 resolution (20cm cell precision). It outputs a 4×R×R×R soft occupancy volume representing the union of predicted visual spans over the future T_f frames, with four concentric levels: orientation (55°), peripheral (30°), central (8°), and foveal (2°) eccentricity.

## Method

EgoSpanLift lifts 2D gaze into 3D by filtering SLAM keypoints (temporal window, spatial cube, neighbor-based outlier removal), classifying points within multi-level gaze cones via angular distance thresholds, and voxelizing them into binary occupancy grids. The forecasting network uses a 3D U-Net encoder to compress spatial dimensions by a factor of R, producing T_p×C temporal embeddings plus a global embedding that aggregates all spans as the prediction head. A unidirectional transformer with causal masking learns temporal dependencies across these embeddings, and a U-Net decoder upsamples the final embedding back to 4×R×R×R via residual connections. Training uses dice loss to handle extreme class imbalance (foveal span occupies <1% of the grid), and the multi-level joint prediction exploits the interconnection between gaze and periphery to capture cues about future foveal focus from previous peripheral spans while implicitly mitigating self-motion uncertainty end-to-end.

## Results

On FoVS-Aria, the full model achieves IoU of 0.584 (Orientation), 0.489 (Peripheral 30°), 0.351 (Central 8°), and 0.284 (Foveal 2°), with corresponding F1 scores of 0.725, 0.635, 0.476, and 0.371. On the larger FoVS-EgoExo benchmark (341.4K samples), performance improves to IoU 0.523, 0.511, 0.421, and 0.369 across the four levels. The method outperforms the strongest baseline (EgoChoir) by 17.8% relative IoU on Foveal span (0.284 vs. 0.199) and by 11.3% on Central span (0.351 vs. 0.261) on FoVS-Aria. When back-projected to 2D, the 3D predictions match 2D-specific gaze anticipation models (F1 0.515 vs. 0.515 for CSTS) without any 2D-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines across every visual span level on both FoVS-Aria and FoVS-EgoExo. EgoChoir [9], which predicts 3D interaction hotspots, is the strongest baseline, achieving Foveal IoU of 0.199 vs. 0.284 for the proposed method (a 43% relative gap). Among 2D gaze anticipation models lifted to 3D via EgoSpanLift, CSTS [20] and GLC [45] perform comparably (Foveal IoU ≈ 0.13–0.14) but remain substantially below the proposed end-to-end 3D approach. Pure 3D occupancy/scene-completion methods (OccFormer [81], VoxFormer [82]) perform worst, with Foveal IoU below 0.05, indicating that generic 3D semantic prediction does not transfer well to gaze-specific forecasting. The IAG [8] affordance model sits in the middle (Foveal IoU 0.075), confirming that interaction-region prediction is closer to but still distinct from visual span prediction. Notably, the 2D gaze models' advantage over 3D localization baselines disappears when the proposed method is back-projected to 2D, where it matches CSTS's F1 of 0.515 without any 2D-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of egocentric gaze anticipation (2D, e.g., CSTS, GLC) and 3D egocentric interaction prediction (EgoChoir, IAG), introducing the task of forecasting multi-level 3D volumetric visual span regions. The core methodological contribution, EgoSpanLift, repurposes SLAM-derived semidense keypoints and a vision-science taxonomy of visual eccentricity (foveal 2°, central 8°, peripheral 30°, orientation 55°) to construct a 3D representation that neither prior 2D gaze models nor 3D interaction predictors provide. The forecasting framework itself—3D U-Net for spatial encoding plus a unidirectional transformer for temporal dynamics—is a straightforward composition of existing architectures, but its application to this multi-level volumetric forecasting problem is novel. The paper explicitly departs from the post-processing paradigm (predict 2D gaze, then project to 3D via pose extrapolation) in favor of end-to-end 3D prediction, arguing that this avoids compounding self-motion and geometric-correspondence uncertainties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: accurate 3D visual span forecasting enables proactive foveated rendering in AR/VR, ambient computing, and assistive technologies by anticipating where a user will look before they look. The curated benchmarks (FoVS-Aria: 23.2K samples; FoVS-EgoExo: 341.4K samples) fill a genuine gap—no prior testbed existed for this task—and the finding that 3D predictions projected back to 2D match 2D-specific models without any 2D training validates 3D as a more natural representation of perceptual focus. Open questions include robustness to SLAM degradation in novel environments, extension to non-visual modalities (auditory, proprioceptive), and whether the semidense-keypoint representation suffices for dense-scene attention tracking. The baselines, while diverse, are all adapted from adjacent domains rather than purpose-built for visual span forecasting, so the large performance gaps partly reflect task mismatch rather than methodological superiority.
