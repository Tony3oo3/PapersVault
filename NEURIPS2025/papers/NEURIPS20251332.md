## Problem

Despite strong performance on high-level benchmarks, VLMs exhibit persistent failures on tasks requiring spatial reasoning, selective attention, and multi-object comparison, yet existing evaluations do not systematically decompose these failures along core cognitive axes. This paper asks: which specific cognitive mechanisms—perception, attention, or memory—break down, and is the bottleneck in visual encoding, linguistic reasoning, or the integration of the two?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251332/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task trial presents a short sequence of image frames (1–9 frames) containing 3D-rendered ShapeNet objects (8 categories, 8 objects per category) placed in one of four spatial quadrants, interleaved with blank or distractor frames, plus a natural-language instruction specifying the target object and the property to report or compare. The model outputs a single text answer: an object category name, a spatial location (e.g., 'top left'), or a true/false judgment for comparison tasks. The PAM suite comprises 22 task types across Perception, Attention, and Memory (each with Report and Compare variants for Category and Location), while the CVR suite adds three complexity levels (Low, Medium, High) of procedurally generated composite reasoning problems.

## Method

The paper introduces two procedurally generated datasets—PAM (isolating Perception, Attention, and Memory) and CVR (composite visual reasoning via iWISDM AutoTask)—and evaluates seven VLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-Mini, Qwen2.5-VL-7B/72B, LLaVA-OneVision-7B, InternVL2.5-8B, MiniCPM-V-2.6-8B) against human baselines. To localize the reasoning bottleneck, the authors implement a vision-text decoupling paradigm with three captioning conditions: Pre-captioning (PC, ground-truth text replaces images), Self-captioning (SC, model-generated captions replace images), and Self-captioning-Interleaved (SC-I, captions appended alongside images). The key finding is that SC yields large gains on location-based tasks (average +18.31%) but minimal gains on category tasks (+3.54%), indicating the bottleneck lies in the model's inability to translate visual tokens into spatial reasoning within its CoT, not in the LLM's logical capacity or low-level perception. As a complementary intervention, supervised LoRA fine-tuning on 1k–100k randomly generated CVR trials (with the ViT frozen and the vision projector unfrozen) produces significant improvements on core PAM cognitive tasks and modest gains on out-of-distribution benchmarks (MMBench, VQAv2, MMMU-Pro).

## Results

GPT-4o achieves the highest overall performance, reaching 90.33% on Perception (Category) and 89.00% on Perception (Location), approaching human levels (93.75% and 97.50% respectively). On composite visual reasoning, GPT-4o exceeds human accuracy on CVR-Cat-M (96.67% vs. 95.00%), CVR-Cat-H (83.67% vs. 72.50%), and CVR-Loc-M (82.67% vs. 70.00%), but still lags humans on CVR-Loc-L (66.00% vs. 92.50%) and CVR-Loc-H (64.67% vs. 75.00%). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-7B leads with 83.67% on Perception (Cat) but drops to 44.33% on Perception (Loc), revealing a pervasive spatial reasoning deficit across all compact VLMs. Self-captioning (SC) improves Qwen2.5-VL-7B's location task accuracy by an average of 18.31% versus only 3.54% for category tasks, confirming that the bottleneck lies in visual-to-text integration rather than language reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o dominates all 12 task categories, outperforming GPT-4o-Mini by 10–30 points on location-based tasks (e.g., 89.00% vs. 57.67% on Perception-Loc) and by 15–25 points on CVR location tasks (e.g., 64.67% vs. 39.67% on CVR-Loc-H). Among the four open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-7B consistently leads on category tasks (83.67% Perc-Cat, 78.59% Mem-Cat) but trails MiniCPM-V-8B on Perception-Loc (44.33% vs. 39.00%) and is closely matched by InternVL2.5-8B on Spatial Attention (57.48% vs. 60.89%). LLaVA-OneVision-7B is the weakest open-source model on most tasks, scoring 71.00% on Perc-Cat and 20.00% on CVR-Loc-H. The most striking cross-model finding is the spatial reasoning gap: while GPT-4o narrows the Cat–Loc difference to ~1 point (90.33% vs. 89.00%), all open-source models show 25–45 point deficits on location tasks relative to their category scores. GPT-4o exceeds human performance on four of six CVR tasks (Cat-L, Cat-M, Cat-H, Loc-M) but still falls 26.5 points below humans on CVR-Loc-L (66.00% vs. 92.50%), indicating that even SOTA models retain a fundamental spatial localization weakness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing VLM benchmarks (MMMU-Pro, MMBench, MME) that evaluate high-level reasoning in isolation, instead adopting a cognitive-science decomposition into Perception, Attention, and Memory axes to diagnose *where* visual reasoning fails. It builds on the iWISDM procedural task-generation framework (Lei et al., 2024) for scalable task creation and extends the vision-text decoupling idea from the Prism framework (Qiao et al., 2024) by applying self-captioning within the same model rather than offloading reasoning to a separate LLM. The spatial-reasoning deficit in VLMs has been documented in prior work (Kamath et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025), but this paper isolates the specific mechanism—insufficient CoT integration of visual tokens—rather than merely cataloguing the failure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple self-captioning prompt modification yields large, consistent gains (up to +55% on spatial perception) even for GPT-4o reframes the spatial-reasoning bottleneck as a CoT integration problem rather than a perceptual or LLM-reasoning one, pointing toward a concrete and cheap fix. The PAM/CVR evaluation framework is validated by strong correlations (r=0.94 for Memory) with established benchmarks, making it a potentially reusable diagnostic tool. Key open questions include whether more sophisticated CoT training (e.g., RL with reasoning rewards) can internalize the captioning step to eliminate the 2× inference overhead, and whether gains transfer to naturalistic, cluttered scenes beyond the controlled ShapeNet stimuli used here.
