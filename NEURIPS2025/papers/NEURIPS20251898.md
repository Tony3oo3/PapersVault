## Problem

While RL-based post-training (e.g., GRPO) has substantially improved reasoning in autoregressive LLMs, it remains unclear whether diffusion-based LLMs can leverage these advances. The core obstacle is that standard policy gradient methods require computing sequence log-probabilities via sequential factorization, which is inapplicable to dLLMs' iterative, non-sequential denoising process, leaving RL post-training for masked dLLMs largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (e.g., a math word problem, Sudoku grid, or coding task) and produces a structured reasoning trace with a final answer, generated via iterative denoising over a fixed-length sequence (256 tokens during RL training, evaluated at 128/256/512). Unlike autoregressive generation, dLLMs refine the entire sequence simultaneously through multiple unmasking steps, using a semi-autoregressive block-based decoding strategy (blocks of 32 tokens, unmasking 2 highest-confidence tokens per step) at inference time.

## Method

d1 is a two-stage post-training framework: masked SFT on s1k reasoning traces to instill step-by-step problem-solving and self-correction behavior, followed by diffu-GRPO, a critic-free policy gradient algorithm adapted for masked dLLMs. The key technical contribution is an efficient log-probability estimator combining a mean-field decomposition for sequence-level probabilities with a one-step unmasking prediction under random prompt masking (probability p_mask) for per-token probabilities, replacing the expensive multi-step Monte Carlo estimation used by LLaDA. The random prompt masking introduces stochastic perturbation that acts as implicit regularization, enabling the number of inner gradient updates (μ) to be scaled to 12–24 without in-batch overfitting, thereby reducing the number of costly online generations and substantially lowering RL training compute.

## Results

On the four primary reasoning benchmarks, d1-LLaDA (SFT + diffu-GRPO) achieves 82.1% on GSM8K, 40.2% on MATH500, 42.2% on Countdown, and 22.1% on Sudoku (best sequence length per task), outperforming the base LLaDA-8B-Instruct by +3.9%, +4.0%, +26.2%, and +10.0% respectively. The gains are most dramatic on planning tasks (Countdown and Sudoku), where performance nearly doubles over the base model. On coding benchmarks, diffu-GRPO consistently improves over initialization: +3.7% on HumanEval (256 tokens) and +4.3% on MBPP (256 tokens) from the base model, with larger gains (+9.8% on HumanEval at 128 tokens) when starting from an SFT checkpoint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base LLaDA-8B-Instruct model (Nie et al., 2025), d1-LLaDA wins on all four reasoning tasks at their best sequence lengths: GSM8K 82.1 vs 78.2, MATH500 40.2 vs 36.2, Countdown 42.2 vs 20.7, and Sudoku 22.1 vs 11.7. The improvement is modest on math tasks (~4%) but substantial on planning tasks (Countdown +26.2%, Sudoku +10.0%), suggesting the base model is near saturation on math while planning tasks have more headroom. diffu-GRPO alone also outperforms the base model in all 12 task/sequence-length combinations, while SFT alone wins in only 7 of 12, confirming RL as the stronger single intervention. In the broader comparison with state-of-the-art dLLMs and AR LLMs of similar size (Figure 3, using scores from Dream 7B, Ye et al. 2025), d1-LLaDA achieves the highest GSM8K score and the second-highest MATH500 score among all models shown, validating that the RL post-training recipe closes the gap with autoregressive reasoning models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO policy gradient framework (from DeepSeekMath [38]) to masked diffusion LLMs, addressing the core technical obstacle that dLLMs lack the sequential log-probability factorization that AR models enjoy. It builds on LLaDA [30] as the base model and draws on the broader RL post-training literature (DeepSeek-R1, Kimi K1.5) for the SFT-then-RL recipe, while departing from prior discrete diffusion RL work [51] that relied on concrete score matching rather than masked objectives. The key conceptual departure is the one-step log-probability estimator with random prompt masking, which has no direct analogue in AR-based RL and exploits the iterative denoising structure unique to diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that policy gradient RL post-training can be effectively applied to large masked diffusion LLMs, opening a previously unexplored direction as dLLMs gain traction as non-autoregressive alternatives. The random masking trick, which acts as implicit regularization enabling much higher inner update counts (μ=12–24 vs. typical μ=2), offers a practical compute efficiency gain specific to diffusion models. Key limitations include the fixed 256-token generation length during RL (constraining chain-of-thought exploration), evaluation on a single 8B base model, and absence of error bars. Open questions include whether these gains transfer to larger dLLMs, variable-length generation (e.g., Block Diffusion), and whether the SFT+RL synergy observed here generalizes beyond the s1k dataset.
