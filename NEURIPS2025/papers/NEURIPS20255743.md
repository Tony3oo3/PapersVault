## Problem

Recent work by Liu [34] claimed that rectified flows with gradient-constrained velocity fields (R_p) yield optimal transport solutions, with a fixed-point equivalence (Theorem 5.6) linking zero loss to optimality. This paper shows that claim is false without additional assumptions: it constructs explicit counterexamples where non-optimal couplings are fixed points of R_p with zero loss, and where the loss vanishes while the coupling remains arbitrarily far from optimal. The central question addressed is whether iterative rectification is a reliable computational tool for optimal transport, and the answer is negative under realistic conditions such as disconnected supports.

## Input / Output

The fundamental input is a coupling (X₀, X₁) between a source distribution μ₀ and a target distribution μ₁ on ℝᵈ, not merely the two marginals. The method produces a time-indexed velocity field v_t (expressed as a conditional expectation E[X₁−X₀ | X_t = x]) that defines transport paths via the ODE ẑ_t = v_t(z_t), along with the interpolated distribution path μ_t = law of (1−t)X₀ + tX₁. In the gradient-constrained variant R_p, the velocity field is additionally required to be a gradient ∇φ_t, and the paper proves this constrained solution always exists in a weak L² sense as the minimal-norm solution of the continuity equation. The paper also introduces a smoothed variant that injects Gaussian noise into X₀ at each iteration to preserve rectifiability.

## Method

The paper's core technical contributions are threefold. First, it derives invariance properties of the rectification operator R under affine transformations and provides explicit closed-form velocity fields for Gaussian and Gaussian mixture couplings, showing that one rectification step suffices for jointly diagonalizable Gaussians. Second, it constructs two families of counterexamples: (i) disconnected-support couplings where both an optimal and a non-optimal coupling are fixed points of R_p with zero loss (invalidating [34, Theorem 5.6]), and (ii) a non-rectifiable coupling X₁ = −X₀ ~ N(0,Id) where the gradient-constrained loss is zero but the coupling is maximally non-optimal, with a corollary showing that arbitrarily small loss does not imply proximity to optimal transport. Third, it proves a sufficient rectifiability criterion based on smoothness of the conditional P_{X₀|X₁} and proposes a smoothed rectification procedure that injects diminishing Gaussian noise into X₀ at each step, preserving marginal structure and maintaining the O(1/K) loss decay rate while guaranteeing rectifiability throughout the iteration.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: it constructs explicit counterexamples showing that gradient-constrained rectified flows (R_p) admit non-optimal fixed points when the interpolated distributions have disconnected support (Proposition 10: transport cost 16 vs. optimal 4), and that vanishing loss does not imply optimality (Corollary 17: loss < ε yet transport distance > 4−ε for arbitrarily small ε). The smoothed rectification procedure (Theorem 18) recovers the O(1/K) convergence rate of the loss when noise levels are summable. Numerically, on the 2D counterexample from Remark 12, the optimal transport distance is 2.66, and smoothed rectification with c₀=0.01 reaches 2.68 after 5 steps, while c₀=0 (standard R_p) stagnates at 2.79.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its counterexamples against Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge Matching (DSBM, Shi et al. 2023) in Appendix F. DSBM is the only baseline with a guaranteed convergence to the entropically regularized optimal transport plan, but the numerical experiments (Figures 13–14) show that on the same disconnected-support counterexample, DSBM's convergence becomes arbitrarily slow as the regularization parameter ε → 0, mirroring the failure modes identified for rectified flows. Specifically, DSBM initialized at the non-optimal fixed point (X̃₀, X̃₁) from equation (11) shows persistent transport distance error and marginal distortion (measured in energy distance) that do not vanish quickly for small ε. The paper's key finding is that the gradient constraint on rectified flows is not a reliable route to optimal transport, a claim that DSBM's entropic regularization partially addresses but only at the cost of slow convergence in the unregularized limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of rectified flows/flow matching (Liu et al. 2023, Lipman et al. 2023) and the Benamou-Brenier dynamic formulation of optimal transport, directly analyzing and correcting the gradient-constrained rectification procedure introduced by Liu (2022). While prior work established that rectified flows produce efficient generative models and that the Benamou-Brenier velocity field admits a potential, the specific claim that imposing a gradient constraint on rectified flows recovers optimal transport (Liu 2022, Theorem 5.6) is shown here to be false without a connected-support assumption. The paper also connects to the broader flow-matching literature on stochastic interpolants (Albergo et al. 2023) and Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge Matching (Shi et al. 2023), positioning its counterexamples within the entropically regularized OT framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper definitively closes the door on using gradient-constrained rectified flows as a general-purpose optimal transport solver, which is practically important given that real-world datasets often have disconnected supports. The smoothed rectification procedure (injecting noise per iteration) provides a partial remedy that preserves rectifiability and maintains the same loss-decay rate, but convergence to the true optimal transport remains unproven. Open questions include whether the iterative procedure converges to the optimal fixed point from natural initializations (independent coupling, minibatch OT), whether non-rectifiable couplings arise in practice during iteration, and how the convergence rate of DSBM behaves as the entropic regularization parameter vanishes.
