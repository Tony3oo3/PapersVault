## Problem

Predicting electrolyte conductivity in multi-molecular mixture systems is hindered by two gaps: the absence of standardized, geometrically enriched benchmarks, and the inability of existing models to capture fine-grained 3D intermolecular interactions while respecting the full symmetry structure of mixtures. Prior methods either ignore 3D geometry (SMILES/topological graphs) or collapse molecules into global embeddings before simulating interactions, losing atom-level cross-molecular structure. The core technical challenge is maintaining Set-SE(3) equivariance—simultaneous permutation equivariance within each molecule, independent rotation equivariance per molecule, and permutation equivariance across molecules—which no prior architecture achieves at the atom level.

## Input / Output

The model takes a set of M molecular geometric graphs (atoms as nodes with scalar features like atomic numbers and 3D coordinates, edges within a 6 Å cutoff), a proportion vector w summing to 1, and an environment descriptor c (temperature, pressure). Coordinates are pre-centered at the origin to enforce translation invariance. The primary output is a scalar conductivity value κ, but the architecture also produces node-level scalar and equivariant features for each atom, which can be read out at graph or system level. The model must satisfy Set-SE(3) equivariance: outputs transform consistently under independent atom permutations, independent SE(3) rotations/translations of each molecule, and arbitrary reordering of molecules in the mixture.

## Method

GeoMix combines equivariant intramolecular encoders (EGNN or TFN) with a novel Geometric Interaction Network (GIN) for intermolecular message passing. The key insight is constructing a PCA-based local coordinate frame for each molecule, then learning atom-pair-specific transformations (a 3×3 matrix I_ij = F_m R_ij F_n^T and a translation vector t_ij) that map equivariant messages from the source molecule's frame into the target molecule's frame, enabling equivariant cross-molecular communication. Theorem 3.1 proves this factorized form is the most general SO(3)-equivariant transformation between two frames. GIN aggregates messages from all atoms in all other molecules via averaging, updates both invariant and equivariant node features, and is followed by a self-attention readout over graph-level embeddings. Training uses MSE loss plus a Noisy Nodes denoising auxiliary loss (γ=128) that regularizes the equivariant encoder by predicting clean coordinates from perturbed inputs.

## Results

GeoMix achieves the best performance across all metrics on both datasets. On CALiSol, GeoMix-TFN reaches MSE 0.432 and Pearson r 0.987, while GeoMix-EGNN achieves MSE 0.552 and Pearson r 0.985. On DiffMix, GeoMix-TFN attains MSE 0.035 and Pearson r 0.997, and GeoMix-EGNN reaches MSE 0.088 and Pearson r 0.992. These results represent substantial improvements over the strongest non-GeoMix baselines (TFN-linear: MSE 1.107 / 0.285) by factors of ~2.6× and ~8× on CALiSol and DiffMix respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoMix consistently outperforms all four baseline families on both datasets. Against MM-MoLFormer [12] (SMILES-based), GeoMix-TFN reduces MSE from 5.488 to 0.432 on CALiSol and from 1.901 to 0.035 on DiffMix, a >12× improvement, confirming that geometric 3D information is critical. Against MolSets [13] (topology-based GNN), GeoMix-TFN improves MSE from 2.230 (Conv) / 2.751 (SAGE) to 0.432 on CALiSol and from 1.440 / 0.708 to 0.035 on DiffMix, showing that explicit intermolecular geometric message passing surpasses attention over topological graph embeddings. Against the strongest geometry-based baselines EGNN-linear [54] and TFN-linear [55], GeoMix-EGNN halves the MSE (0.552 vs 1.461 on CALiSol; 0.088 vs 0.195 on DiffMix) and GeoMix-TFN reduces it by ~2.6× (0.432 vs 1.107) and ~8× (0.035 vs 0.285), demonstrating that the GIN's cross-molecular equivariant message passing provides a substantial advantage over simple composition-weighted aggregation of per-molecule equivariant features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior electrolyte modeling work (MM-MoLFormer, MolSets, DiffMix, Uni-ELF) by introducing Set-SE(3) equivariance as a formal requirement for mixture systems, going beyond the single-reference-frame equivariance assumed by standard equivariant GNNs like EGNN and TFN. The Geometric Interaction Network (GIN) is a novel architectural component that enables equivariant message passing across molecules in independent local coordinate frames, using PCA-based frame construction and learnable intermolecular transformations. The paper also repositions the dataset landscape by curating and standardizing CALiSol and DiffMix with geometric graph representations, filling a gap where prior work lacked unified, reproducible benchmarks. Conceptually, the work bridges equivariant geometric deep learning (single-molecule) with mixture-system modeling (multi-molecule), a combination not previously addressed with full equivariance guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The standardized geometric benchmarks (CALiSol with 13K experimental samples, DiffMix with 25K simulation samples) provide the field's first unified evaluation platform for electrolyte conductivity prediction, enabling fair cross-method comparisons that were previously impossible. The Set-SE(3) equivariance framework and GIN module are generalizable beyond electrolytes to any multi-component molecular system (pharmaceuticals, materials), making this a potential building block for broader mixture modeling. Key limitations include the absence of MD-based microscopic structural information, limited chemistry coverage (Li-only, no Na/K electrolytes), and the relatively simple encoder architecture. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger mixture systems, whether the PCA-based frame construction can be replaced by learned frames, and how the approach performs when true intermolecular distances (from MD) replace the current single-conformation assumption.
