## Problem

Existing cooperative perception datasets (DAIR-V2X, V2X-Seq, V2V4Real, V2X-Real) focus exclusively on camera and LiDAR sensors, overlooking 4D Radar—a modality proven to deliver robust perception in adverse weather conditions. This absence prevents systematic study of how 4D Radar can complement LiDAR and cameras in vehicle-to-infrastructure cooperative perception, particularly under rain, fog, and snow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253789/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each synchronized frame provides 80-beam LiDAR point clouds (360° horizontal, ±25° vertical FOV), sparse 4D Radar point clouds with per-point Doppler velocity (79 GHz roadside / 77 GHz vehicle, ±56°/±50° horizontal FOV), and multi-view RGB images (3× 1536×864 roadside, 1× 1920×1080 vehicle), all aligned within 20 ms across the two platforms. Annotations are 7-DoF 3D bounding boxes (x, y, z, w, h, l, θ) across five categories (pedestrian, cyclist, car, bus, truck), with collaborative annotations deduplicated via IoU-based matching in a unified roadside LiDAR coordinate system. The dataset is partitioned into three sub-datasets—V2X-Radar-C (40 cooperative sequences), V2X-Radar-V (10 vehicle-only), and V2X-Radar-I (10 infrastructure-only)—each with distinct evaluation areas and task definitions.

## Method

The core contribution is a large-scale real-world dataset (20K LiDAR frames, 40K camera images, 20K 4D Radar samples, 350K 3D boxes) collected over nine months across diverse weather (sunny, rainy, foggy, snowy) and time-of-day conditions, with 50 representative intersection scenarios hand-selected from 15 hours of driving logs. Cross-platform synchronization is achieved via GPS-time-aligned PTP/PPS hardware triggers, and spatial registration combines RTK initialization with CBM refinement and manual adjustment. Annotation follows an auto-labeling-plus-manual-refinement pipeline with multi-round quality control. The paper establishes comprehensive benchmarks: single-agent 3D detection (LiDAR, camera, and 4D Radar methods) on V2X-Radar-I and V2X-Radar-V, and cooperative 3D detection (late, early, and intermediate fusion strategies including F-Cooper, V2X-ViT, CoAlign, and HEAL) on V2X-Radar-C under both synchronous and 100 ms asynchronous communication settings. Key empirical findings include that 4D Radar-only detection outperforms LiDAR-only by 1–2% mAP in adverse weather, and that all intermediate-fusion cooperative methods suffer substantial degradation under asynchronous conditions, exposing a critical robustness gap in current cooperative perception.

## Results

On the cooperative detection task (V2X-Radar-C), HEAL with LiDAR achieves the best overall AP of 67.57/83.00 (IoU=0.7/0.5) under synchronous settings, dropping to 57.76/79.66 under 100 ms asynchronous delay. For single-agent roadside detection (V2X-Radar-I), SQDNet with LiDAR leads at 89.12/95.10 AP (Vehicle, IoU=0.7/0.5, Easy), while RPFA-Net with 4D Radar reaches 64.79/82.58. Under adverse weather (rain, fog, snow), 4D Radar-only outperforms LiDAR-only by ~1–2% mAP, and LiDAR+4D Radar fusion (M2-Fusion) achieves the best vehicle AP of 53.61 (Easy, IoU=0.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among cooperative LiDAR methods, HEAL (67.57/83.00 Sync Overall) outperforms CoAlign (60.18/80.42), V2X-ViT (52.15/79.35), F-Cooper (50.04/73.44), and Late Fusion (39.37/65.75), with the gap widening at longer ranges (50–100 m: HEAL 34.79 vs. CoAlign 29.62). Under 100 ms async delay, all methods degrade substantially—HEAL drops ~10 AP points (67.57→57.76) and V2X-ViT drops ~13 points (52.15→39.06)—exposing a critical vulnerability to communication latency. For camera-based cooperative detection, HEAL (25.05/46.94) and CoAlign (24.26/46.89) lead, both far below their LiDAR counterparts, confirming LiDAR's dominance in this setting. 4D Radar cooperative methods remain the weakest (HEAL: 12.50/29.04), though they still surpass camera Late Fusion (3.77/17.24). In single-agent roadside detection (V2X-Radar-I), LiDAR methods (SQDNet 89.12, PV-RCNN 88.83 for Vehicle Easy) dominate over 4D Radar (RPFA-Net 64.79) and camera (BEVHeight++ 48.48). The adverse-weather ablation shows 4D Radar-only (48.35) slightly beating LiDAR-only (47.23) for Vehicle Easy, while LiDAR+Radar fusion (M2-Fusion, 53.61) achieves the best overall, validating the complementary nature of the two modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established research threads: cooperative perception datasets (DAIR-V2X, V2V4Real, V2X-Real, OPV2V) and single-vehicle 4D Radar perception (K-Radar, Dual-Radar). All prior cooperative perception datasets exclusively use camera and LiDAR, while 4D Radar has only been studied in single-vehicle settings. V2X-Radar is the first to integrate 4D Radar into a real-world V2I cooperative perception dataset, and it benchmarks existing intermediate-fusion methods (F-Cooper, V2X-ViT, CoAlign, HEAL) alongside single-modal LiDAR, camera, and radar detectors (PointPillars, CenterPoint, PV-RCNN, RPFA-Net, BEVHeight++) to establish reference performance across all three sensor types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a critical gap by providing the only real-world cooperative perception benchmark with 4D Radar, enabling research on radar-robust V2X perception under adverse weather. Two key findings have practical implications: (1) 4D Radar outperforms LiDAR by 1–2% mAP in rain/fog/snow, confirming its complementary role, and (2) a mere 100 ms communication delay causes severe degradation (e.g., HEAL drops ~10 AP points at IoU=0.7), exposing a critical robustness weakness in current cooperative methods. Open questions include whether delay-tolerant fusion architectures can close this gap, and whether the dataset's intersection-centric, China-specific scenarios generalize to other geographies and traffic patterns. The authors note the dataset currently lacks tracking, prediction, and occupancy tasks, limiting its scope for world-model research.
