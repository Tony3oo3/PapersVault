## Problem

Traditional deep image watermarking methods treat the entire image as a uniform entity, making them unable to extract watermarks from small surviving regions after tampering, localize which areas still contain watermark signals, or embed watermarks only in specific regions of interest. This lack of spatial awareness in both the encoder and decoder prevents fine-grained forensic analysis and targeted content protection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254895/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a 256×256 RGB image (512×512 at evaluation), a binary bit vector of configurable length (32, 64, or 128 bits), and optionally a binary spatial mask (for MaskWM-ED) to produce a watermarked image. The decoder takes a distorted image and outputs both a pixel-level localization mask (via a U²-Net branch) and the recovered bit sequence (via a U-Net + CNN branch). During inference, the localization mask is applied to zero out non-watermarked regions before bit extraction, reducing interference from irrelevant content.

## Method

MaskWM introduces a masking mechanism during training to instill spatial awareness into the encoder-decoder pipeline. In MaskWM-D, four types of masks (Full, Rectangle, Irregular, Segment from MS-COCO) are randomly applied to the watermarked image before extraction: unmasked regions are replaced with the original clean image, and the masked region is isolated for the decoder, forcing it to learn where watermark signals reside and extract from partial areas. In MaskWM-ED, the mask is additionally concatenated as a channel to the U-Net encoder input, explicitly guiding it to concentrate watermark energy within designated regions. The encoder uses a U-Net with JND-based modulation for perceptual quality, while the decoder combines a U²-Net for mask prediction with a U-Net + CNN for bit recovery, all trained end-to-end with MSE losses over 100k steps on a single A6000 GPU (~20 hours).

## Results

On the MS-COCO 2014 validation set (512×512, 32-bit watermark), MaskWM-D achieves PSNR 39.55, SSIM 0.9814, and 100% bit accuracy under no distortion, valuemetric distortions, and 99.98% under geometric distortions. MaskWM-ED reaches PSNR 39.52, SSIM 0.9828, and a perfect 100% bit accuracy across all three distortion categories. For local watermarking, MaskWM maintains near-100% extraction accuracy even when only 5% of the image carries watermark signals, substantially outperforming WAM (the prior state-of-the-art local method) especially under geometric distortions where WAM drops to ~90%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskWM decisively outperforms WAM [20], the closest local-watermarking competitor, on geometric robustness (1.0000 vs. 0.8979 for MaskWM-ED) and valuemetric robustness (1.0000 vs. 0.9986), while also achieving slightly better PSNR (39.52 vs. 39.32) and SSIM (0.9828 vs. 0.9791). Against global methods, MaskWM's geometric robustness is dramatically superior: TrustMark [2] drops to 0.7868, Robust-Wide [8] to 0.4951, VINE [14] to 0.5012, and EditGuard [30] to ~0.49, whereas MaskWM maintains ≥0.9998. In valuemetric robustness, MaskWM matches or exceeds all baselines (1.0000 vs. 0.9955 for TrustMark, 0.9944 for Robust-Wide, 0.9986 for VINE). For local watermarking (small watermarked regions) and watermark localization (IoU), MaskWM consistently surpasses both WAM and EditGuard across all area ratios and distortion conditions. In multi-watermark embedding (up to 5 watermarks at 5% area each), MaskWM-ED outperforms WAM in both extraction accuracy and localization IoU, despite being trained only with single-watermark supervision. The only trade-off is that MaskWM's PSNR (~39.5) is marginally lower than TrustMark (41.19) and Robust-Wide (41.58), but this comes with a 20+ point improvement in geometric robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskWM builds on the classical encoder–distortion–decoder training paradigm established by StegaStamp, TrustMark, and subsequent global watermarking methods, but departs from them by introducing a spatial masking mechanism during training to instill spatial awareness in both encoder and decoder. Its closest conceptual predecessor is WAM, which treats local watermark extraction as a pixel-level segmentation task; MaskWM instead uses a dedicated U²-Net localization module and a mask-guided extraction pipeline, avoiding WAM's reliance on per-pixel averaging. The masking strategy itself is borrowed from inpainting literature (LaMa), repurposed here to guide the model toward local rather than global watermark signals. The JND-based visual quality modulation and the U-Net backbone are standard components, so the core novelty lies in the training-time masking protocol rather than in any new architectural primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a 15× reduction in training compute over WAM (20 h on one A6000 vs. 90 h on eight V100s), scalability to 64- and 128-bit watermarks where WAM is capped at 32 bits, and the ability to fine-tune against new adaptive attacks in ~5 hours make MaskWM a deployable option for real-world content-protection pipelines. The finding that a single model trained with single-watermark supervision generalizes to multi-watermark embedding (up to 5 distinct messages) without retraining is a useful property for multi-source provenance tracking. Open questions include whether the visible artifacts in smooth regions (acknowledged by the authors) can be resolved without sacrificing local robustness, and whether the geometric-distortion degradation in multi-watermark settings (corner shrinkage under rotation) can be mitigated. The framework's simplicity also raises the question of whether further gains require fundamentally new mechanisms or can be achieved through better mask scheduling and loss design.
