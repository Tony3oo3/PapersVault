## Problem

RL fine-tuning of LLMs is bottlenecked by the high computational cost of multi-sampling per instance for policy gradient estimation, and existing data-selection strategies (e.g., DAPO's accuracy filtering) still require this expensive multi-sampling to identify which samples to discard. The paper asks whether one can efficiently pinpoint the most informative training instances—those within the model's Zone of Proximal Development—using a single forward pass rather than repeated rollouts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of math word problems paired with ground-truth answers and a pre-trained decoder-only LLM. For each problem, it performs one autoregressive forward pass to generate a full solution and records the conditional log-probability at every decoding step. The output is a 10% subset of the original dataset ranked by a continuous 'fuzziness score' (proximity to mean confidence), which is then fed into the GRPO RL loop to produce the fine-tuned model.

## Method

UFO-RL replaces costly multi-sampling accuracy with a single-pass confidence metric: the average log-probability of the generated tokens, exponentiated to yield a geometric-mean score s_i ∈ (0,1). A 'fuzziness score' of 1 − (s_i − μ)², where μ is the dataset mean confidence, assigns highest weight to samples at intermediate uncertainty—operationalizing the ZPD principle that optimal learning occurs on tasks that are challenging but achievable. The top 10% of samples by this score are selected for RL training under the GRPO framework. This single-pass evaluation achieves up to 185× speedup over 16-sample accuracy estimation and, by reducing the training set to 10%, cuts overall RL fine-tuning time by up to 16× while matching or exceeding full-data performance across Qwen2.5 (0.5B–7B), Llama3.1-8B, and Mistral-7B on GSM8K, Math500, and MMLU.

## Results

UFO-RL selects only 10% of training data via single-pass confidence scoring and achieves performance comparable to or exceeding full-data RL training across all six models (Qwen2.5 0.5B–7B, LLaMA3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) on GSM8K, Math500, and MMLU. For example, on GSM8K (trained on GSM8K), Qwen2.5-3B reaches 84.37% with UFO vs. 83.33% full-data, and Qwen2.5-7B reaches 92.03% vs. 91.88%. On the harder Math500 benchmark, UFO consistently outperforms full-data training (e.g., LLaMA3.1-8B: 55.00% vs. 51.00%; Qwen2.5-3B: 67.40% vs. 66.20%). Overall RL fine-tuning time is reduced by up to 16× (Mistral-7B: 1,454 s vs. 22,955 s on 8×A100), and the data-evaluation step itself is up to 185× faster than multi-sampling accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is DAPO's AccFilter strategy, which removes examples with 0% or 100% multi-sample accuracy (requiring 16 inference passes per instance). UFO-RL matches or exceeds AccFilter on most model–dataset combinations: on GSM8K-trained runs, UFO beats AccFilter on Qwen2.5-3B (84.37 vs. 83.61), Qwen2.5-7B (92.03 vs. 91.35), and Mistral-7B (56.67 vs. 55.46), while being competitive on the smaller models. On Math500, UFO outperforms AccFilter across nearly all models (e.g., LLaMA3.1-8B: 55.00 vs. 52.00; Qwen2.5-3B: 67.40 vs. 65.40). Compared to the Full Data baseline, UFO with only 10% of the data achieves equal or better accuracy on GSM8K and Math500 for most models, while requiring up to 16× less training time. The High Conf and Low Conf extremes both underperform UFO, confirming that intermediate-uncertainty (ZPD) data is the most informative subset. Random 10% sampling (averaged over 5 runs) is consistently below UFO, demonstrating that targeted uncertainty-based selection is meaningfully better than uniform subsampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

UFO-RL builds directly on the GRPO policy-gradient framework (DeepSeekMath) and the open-r1 training infrastructure, while explicitly departing from DAPO's multi-sampling-based data filtering by replacing it with a single-pass log-softmax confidence metric. Conceptually, it repurposes Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development theory from cognitive science as a principled justification for selecting intermediate-difficulty training data, connecting to the broader 'less is more' data selection literature (LIMO, LESS, LIMR) but addressing the unique computational bottleneck of RL where evaluating data informativeness traditionally requires expensive multi-step rollouts. The key methodological departure is showing that a continuous, domain-agnostic confidence score from one forward pass can serve as a fine-grained difficulty proxy, overcoming the discreteness and cost of rule-based reward signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16× reduction in RL training time while matching or exceeding full-data performance is a practically significant efficiency gain for anyone scaling RL fine-tuning of LLMs, and the single-pass confidence metric is simple enough to be readily integrated into existing training pipelines. However, the evaluation is confined to mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, DAPO-MATH-17K) and a fixed 10% selection ratio, leaving open whether the ZPD-based selection generalizes to other reasoning domains (code, science) or to dynamic settings where the model's ZPD shifts during training. The model-specific nature of the confidence score also raises questions about whether the optimal 'fuzzy' band needs recalibration as the model improves, and whether the approach remains effective at larger scales (70B+) where the distribution of difficulties may differ substantially.
