## Problem

Jointly learning invariant and equivariant representations with separate projection heads (as in EquiMod) implicitly assumes the two objectives are independent, yet they are inherently interdependent—semantic identity informs orientation and vice versa. This independence assumption causes both branches to redundantly encode shared information, wasting model capacity and degrading generalization. The paper asks how to explicitly coordinate shared and task-specific information across the two learning objectives.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (STL-10 at 96×96 or ImageNet-100 at 224×224), generates two augmented views per image via standard data augmentations, and routes encoder features through a multi-expert projection module to produce an invariant embedding and an equivariant embedding per view. During pretraining, the equivariant branch additionally predicts a shifted embedding via a residual predictor conditioned on the projected augmentation parameter. Only the encoder weights are retained for downstream transfer; the projection heads and predictor are discarded, so no routing is needed at inference time.

## Method

STAR models projection heads as experts in a Multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) module with N shared experts and two task-specific routers (one for invariant, one for equivariant learning). Each router computes soft routing weights via softmax over expert scores, and the invariant/equivariant embeddings are formed as weighted sums of all expert outputs, enabling input- and task-adaptive allocation. A simpler variant (STAR-SS) uses a single shared expert whose output is additively combined with task-specific experts. The equivariant loss uses a residual predictor φ_T(z⁰, ψ(a)) added to the original equivariant embedding, trained with InfoNCE. Because the MMoE is confined to the projection head during pretraining, expert specialization (verified via routing weight analysis and k-NN retrieval) reduces redundant feature learning—measured by lower canonical correlations between expert outputs—without imposing routing overhead at transfer time.

## Results

On out-of-domain linear evaluation with ImageNet-100-pretrained ResNet-50, STAR-MMoE achieves a mean accuracy of 71.23% across 11 downstream datasets, outperforming EquiMod (69.72%), AugSelf (69.24%), and SimCLR (68.42%). With the smaller STL-10-pretrained ResNet-18, STAR-MMoE reaches 53.07% mean accuracy versus 49.54% for EquiMod and 45.55% for SimCLR. On object detection (VOC07+12, frozen R50-C4 backbone), STAR-MMoE attains 48.85 AP / 76.81 AP50 / 53.01 AP75, the best among all methods. In few-shot classification (5-way 1-shot on FC100), STAR-MMoE scores 38.26% (ImageNet-100) and 39.35% (STL-10), again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR-MMoE consistently outperforms all five baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the strongest equivariant baseline EquiMod, STAR-MMoE gains +1.51% mean out-of-domain accuracy (71.23 vs. 69.72) with ImageNet-100/ResNet-50 and +3.53% (53.07 vs. 49.54) with STL-10/ResNet-18, winning on 10 of 11 datasets in the former and 7 of 11 in the latter. Compared to AugSelf, the margin is +1.99% (ImageNet-100) and +4.15% (STL-10). The invariance-only SimCLR baseline trails by +2.81% and +7.52% respectively, while CARE underperforms substantially (−13.72% on ImageNet-100). In object detection, STAR-MMoE leads EquiMod by +0.33 AP and +0.19 AP75; in few-shot 5-way 1-shot on FC100, it beats EquiMod by +3.05% (ImageNet-100) and +2.93% (STL-10). The supplementary comparison against STL (NeurIPS 2024) shows STAR-MMoE surpassing STL+AugMix by +5.32% mean accuracy on ImageNet-100 and +2.35% on STL-10, confirming gains over the most recent equivariant method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivariant representation learning line (EquiMod, AugSelf, CARE, SIE, STL) by identifying a structural flaw in the standard two-branch design: separate projection heads for invariant and equivariant objectives redundantly encode shared information. The solution repurposes the Multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) architecture from supervised multi-task learning as a projection module during SSL pretraining, with the key departure that experts are discarded at transfer time, sidestepping the transferability limitations of conventional MoE. The single-shared-expert variant (STAR-SS) can be viewed as a degenerate case of the full MMoE formulation, providing a minimal intervention baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of redundant feature learning between invariant and equivariant branches, validated through canonical correlation analysis and k-NN retrieval, provides a useful diagnostic lens for multi-objective SSL architectures more broadly. The MMoE-as-projection-head trick is elegant in that it induces expert specialization during pretraining without imposing routing overhead at inference, making it a drop-in replacement for any two-head SSL framework. However, the reliance on soft routing (incompatible with sparse top-k routing due to batch normalization instability) limits scalability, and the performance gains over EquiMod, while consistent, are modest (1–2 points). Open questions include whether alternative normalization or routing schemes could enable sparse expert activation, and whether the shared/task-specific decomposition generalizes to other multi-objective SSL settings beyond invariant-equivariant learning.
