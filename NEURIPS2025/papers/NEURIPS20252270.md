## Problem

Current image generation and editing systems map textual prompts directly to visual outputs without explicit reasoning about object relationships, spatial arrangements, or operational steps, making them struggle with complex compositional scenes. While MLLMs excel at structured reasoning, their capabilities remain disconnected from the generation process—prior work either uses LLMs as mere text encoders or treats layout planning as a separate pre-generation stage rather than integrating reasoning end-to-end with synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252270/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the system takes a concise text prompt and first produces an intermediate semantic-spatial reasoning chain (GoT) in natural language with explicit bounding-box coordinates in normalized [0,1000) format before synthesizing the image. For editing, it accepts a source image plus a text instruction and generates a step-by-step reasoning chain that localizes edit regions with coordinates, describes modifications, and predicts the result. The GoT chain itself is a user-editable artifact, enabling interactive generation where users can modify object descriptions or reposition bounding boxes to steer the output.

## Method

The GoT framework integrates Qwen2.5-VL-3B as a reasoning engine that generates an explicit semantic-spatial chain, from which two guidance signals are derived: 64 learnable embeddings processed through the MLLM's causal decoder form semantic guidance G_t, while parsed coordinates are rendered as color-coded masks encoded by a VAE to produce spatial guidance G_s. A novel Semantic-Spatial Guidance Module (SSGM) injects both signals—along with a reference image guidance G_r following InstructPix2Pix—into an SDXL-based diffusion model via cross-attention and latent concatenation, using a weighted classifier-free guidance formula that separately scales each guidance type. Training is end-to-end with LoRA (r=32) on the MLLM and full optimization on the diffusion module, using a two-phase schedule (60K pretraining + 10K finetuning steps) over 9M+ GoT-annotated samples constructed via a multi-stage Qwen2-VL/Qwen2.5 annotation pipeline.

## Results

On the GenEval text-to-image benchmark, GoT achieves the highest overall score of 0.64, surpassing the best frozen-text-encoder method (SD3 at 0.62) and the best LLM/MLLM-enhanced method (JanusFlow at 0.63). GoT excels in single-object generation (0.99), counting (0.67), and color accuracy (0.85). On image editing, GoT attains the best CLIP-I (0.864), CLIP-T (0.276), and ImagenHub GPT-4o score (0.533), while scoring 0.561 on Reason-Edit (second only to SmartEdit's 0.572).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval (T2I), GoT (0.64 overall) outperforms all frozen-text-encoder baselines (SD3 at 0.62, SD-XL at 0.55) and all LLM/MLLM-enhanced methods (JanusFlow at 0.63, Janus at 0.61, Emu3-Gen at 0.54), with particularly large margins in counting (0.67 vs. 0.45 for JanusFlow) and single-object generation (0.99). However, JanusFlow substantially outperforms GoT on position (0.53 vs. 0.34) and attribute binding (0.42 vs. 0.27), indicating that autoregressive models retain an edge in fine-grained spatial-attribute alignment. On image editing, GoT leads all baselines on CLIP-I (0.864 vs. CosXL-Edit's 0.860), CLIP-T (0.276 vs. 0.274), and ImagenHub (0.533 vs. MagicBrush's 0.513), while trailing only SmartEdit on Reason-Edit (0.561 vs. 0.572), a benchmark specifically designed for reasoning-based removal/replacement. GoT also uses fewer parameters (2.8B+3B) than SEED-X (2.8B+14B) while achieving higher scores across all editing metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from layout-guided generation methods (GLIGEN, LayoutGPT, RPG) that treat spatial planning as a separate pre-generation stage, and from LLM-as-text-encoder approaches that leverage language models without introducing explicit reasoning. It also distinguishes itself from unified MLLM models (Chameleon, Emu3, Janus) that combine understanding and generation but do not produce an intermediate structured reasoning chain that directly conditions synthesis. The core architectural lineage is SDXL for the diffusion backbone and InstructPix2Pix for reference-image conditioning, but the key conceptual departure is making a semantic-spatial reasoning chain with explicit coordinates a first-class intermediate representation that jointly drives both semantic and spatial guidance in a single end-to-end framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9M+ sample GoT dataset and the SSGM module provide a reusable foundation for reasoning-conditioned visual synthesis, and the interactive generation capability (editing reasoning steps to adjust outputs) offers a practical control interface absent in black-box systems. However, the GenEval improvement over the strongest baseline (JanusFlow, 0.63) is marginal (0.64), and the paper reports no error bars or multi-seed results, making it difficult to assess the robustness of the gains. Open questions include whether the two-stage MLLM-then-diffusion pipeline scales to video or 3D generation, whether the reasoning chain quality degrades with scene complexity beyond the tested range, and whether the approach's benefits persist when the MLLM backbone is swapped or scaled.
