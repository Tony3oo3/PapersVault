## Problem

Test-time adaptation (TTA) has been extensively studied for VLM-based image classification but remains entirely unexplored for dense prediction tasks such as Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation (OVSS). OVSS models, which leverage VLMs' zero-shot recognition to segment unseen object categories, are highly vulnerable to domain shifts (corruptions, weather, rendering style) at inference time, and no existing mechanism enables them to adapt without labels or source data.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 224×224, or split into overlapping 224×224 patches with 112-pixel overlap for high-resolution datasets like Cityscapes and ACDC) and a set of K natural-language concept descriptions encoded via 7 CLIP prompt templates. It produces a pixel-wise segmentation mask of shape H×W assigning one concept per pixel. Adaptation requires no labels, no source data, and operates on a batch of 2 images with only 10 optimization steps, updating just 0.02% of model parameters (LayerNorm weights in the vision encoder).

## Method

MLMP (Multi-Level Multi-Prompt) is a plug-and-play TTA framework with three complementary components. First, Uncertainty-Aware Multi-Level Fusion (UAML) aggregates visual features from intermediate ViT layers, weighting each layer by a softmax of its negative prediction entropy (β=0 for uniform contribution during adaptation, β=1 to sharpen toward confident layers at inference). Second, an Image-Level Entropy (ILE) term minimizes entropy on the CLS token to enforce global prediction consistency. Third, the combined UAML+ILE loss is minimized across 7 diverse text prompt templates at the loss level (rather than averaging adapted parameters as in WATT), which the authors prove yields a 1/T gradient variance reduction (Proposition 1). Only LayerNorm parameters of the vision encoder are updated via Adam (lr=1e-3, 10 steps) while the text encoder remains frozen, keeping the method lightweight at ~82 GFLOPs and 2.1 GB peak memory.

## Results

On the primary V20 benchmark, MLMP achieves 83.76 mIoU on clean data (+7.85 over the no-adaptation baseline of 75.91) and 77.58 mIoU averaged over 15 synthetic corruptions (+8.60 over the 69.01 baseline). Gains are consistent across all nine evaluated datasets: +5.66 on V21, +3.72 on P59, +3.04 on P60, +3.86 on Cityscapes, +5.04 on COCO-Object, and +2.91 on COCO-Stuff. On real-world domain shifts (ACDC: fog, night, rain, snow), MLMP yields an average +6.9 mIoU over the baseline, and on the rendered GTA-V dataset it improves by +3.8 mIoU, across a total of 87 distinct test scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLMP decisively outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and corruption type. TENT, the closest competitor, provides only marginal gains (e.g., 69.33 vs. 69.01 on V20-C) and is surpassed by MLMP by roughly 8 mIoU under corruption and 7 mIoU on clean data. TPT (prompt tuning) essentially matches the no-adaptation baseline on most datasets, indicating that learnable prompt tokens are ineffective for dense prediction. WATT (weight averaging across parallel branches) suffers severe degradation—dropping to 48.30 mIoU on V20-C versus 69.01 without adaptation—because its parameter-averaging strategy is ill-suited to the spatial token structure of segmentation. CLIPArTT (conformal pseudo-labeling) achieves moderate improvements over no-adaptation (63.39 on V20-C) but still trails MLMP by 4.2 mIoU, and it could not be run on Cityscapes due to GPU memory constraints. The paper also shows that on clean data (no distributional shift), MLMP is the only method that reliably improves performance, whereas TPT and WATT either stagnate or degrade, confirming that MLMP's multi-level, multi-prompt design avoids the overfitting pitfalls of simpler adaptation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) paradigm—well established for image classification (TENT, WATT, TPT, CLIPArTT)—to the open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) setting, which the authors identify as entirely unexplored. Methodologically, it repurposes entropy minimization from TENT but generalizes it from the CLS token to all spatial patch tokens, borrows the multi-scale feature fusion philosophy from U-Net-style architectures to aggregate intermediate ViT layers, and adapts WATT's multi-template idea by integrating prompts at the loss level rather than the parameter level. The OVSS backbone (NACLIP with neighborhood attention) is treated as a fixed, plug-and-play component, positioning MLMP as an orthogonal adaptation layer rather than a new segmentation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 87-scenario benchmark (9 datasets × 15 corruptions + real/rendered shifts) provides the first standardized testbed for TTA in open-vocabulary segmentation, which will likely become a reference evaluation suite for future work. The plug-and-play, label-free, single-sample-capable design makes the method immediately deployable on top of any existing OVSS pipeline without retraining. Key limitations include reliance on a shared projection head for layer-confidence estimation (acknowledged by the authors as a constraint) and evaluation primarily on CLIP-based VLMs; whether the multi-level, multi-prompt strategy transfers to newer architectures (e.g., SigLIP-2, Long-CLIP) or to video segmentation remains open.
