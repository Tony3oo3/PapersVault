## Problem

Standard Visual Instruction Tuning (VIT) for Multimodal LLMs overemphasizes instruction-following while neglecting proactive understanding of visual content, leading to overfitting and shortcut learning where models ignore images and generate responses from language priors alone. This gap degrades performance on visually grounded tasks like OCR, captioning, and hallucination-sensitive benchmarks, and existing mitigations rely on larger datasets or models rather than addressing the training objective itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251580/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed through a vision encoder such as CLIP-ViT-L/14 or SigLIP-400M into visual tokens) and a text instruction as input, and produces an autoregressive text response as output. During training, the model additionally generates the instruction tokens from the image alone (with system and task templates stripped out), so the effective training signal covers both the instruction and response sequences conditioned on the image. No additional data or architectural changes are required; the only modification is to the loss function applied during the fine-tuning stage.

## Method

L2T modifies the VIT training objective by adding a negative log-likelihood term over the instruction tokens (minus templates) conditioned on the image, alongside the standard response-generation loss: L = -log p(X_I | X_V) − log p(X_A | X_V, X_I). Two categories of template tokens are excluded from the instruction loss—system templates (USER/ASSISTANT role markers) and task templates (high-frequency, low-information phrases identified by corpus-wide frequency counting)—so the model learns only image-relevant instruction content. This dual-loss design serves as a regularizer: it expands the effective training signal without enlarging the dataset, and forces the model to encode visual information sufficient to reconstruct the instruction, thereby preventing shortcut reliance on language priors. The method is applied exclusively during the end-to-end fine-tuning stage (not pretraining), requires no architectural changes, and incurs less than 1% additional computational overhead, making it orthogonal to and composable with existing MLLM improvements.

## Results

L2T achieves an average relative improvement of up to 8.5% over VIT across 16 multimodal benchmarks, with the largest gains in image captioning (up to +17.6% on LLaVA-NeXT) and OCR/chart/document understanding (up to +8.8%). On multi-discipline benchmarks (MME, MMMU, MMStar), L2T yields up to +3.7% relative improvement, while general VQA tasks see up to +1.5%. In hallucination evaluation, L2T reduces the MMHAL-Bench hallucination rate by 22% (0.68→0.53) and improves the GPT score by 36% (1.73→2.36) on LLaVA-1.5 Vicuna-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard Visual Instruction Tuning (VIT) as introduced by LLaVA [Liu et al., 2023a], where the model is trained only to generate responses given an image and instruction. L2T consistently outperforms VIT across all 16 benchmarks and all five model architectures tested, with no benchmark showing a regression. The largest relative gains appear in image captioning (up to +17.6% on LLaVA-NeXT, e.g., COCO2017 CIDEr 99.96→139.21) and OCR/document understanding (up to +8.8%, e.g., DocVQA 68.49→72.52 on LLaVA-NeXT), confirming that learning to instruct forces the model to attend more to visual content. On hallucination benchmarks, L2T reduces the MMHAL-Bench hallucination rate from 0.68 to 0.53 (−22%) and improves the GPT score from 1.73 to 2.36 (+36%) on LLaVA-1.5 Vicuna-7B, while also lowering CHAIR_s from 48.60 to 46.20 on COCO2014. The method incurs less than 1% additional computational overhead (0.331 vs. 0.334 steps/s), making it a drop-in improvement orthogonal to existing MLLM design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Visual Instruction Tuning (VIT) from LLaVA by modifying the fine-tuning loss to include the instruction sequence alongside the response, effectively turning the model into a joint instruction-and-response generator. The closest prior work is IM (Shi et al., 2024), which also adds instruction loss during language-model instruction tuning, but L2T differs in scope (MLLMs vs. LLMs), motivation (preventing visual shortcut learning vs. mitigating overfitting alone), and template filtering strategy. The paper explicitly positions L2T as orthogonal to the dominant trend of scaling up datasets, backbones, and connectors, offering a loss-level regularizer that can be layered on top of any existing VIT pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

L2T provides a nearly free (under 1% compute overhead) regularization that consistently improves MLLMs across 16 benchmarks, with the largest gains in OCR (+8.8%) and captioning (+17.6%), and meaningfully reduces hallucination across four evaluation suites. Its orthogonality means it can be adopted by any VIT-based pipeline without architectural changes, making it a practical drop-in improvement. Open questions include whether the self-bootstrapping exploration (using model-generated instructions as additional training data) can be scaled into a genuine self-improvement loop, and whether the benefits persist at much larger model scales or with fundamentally different connector designs. The absence of error bars and the reliance on a single training recipe per architecture limit the statistical confidence in the reported margins.
