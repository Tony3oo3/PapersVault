## Problem

Existing single-cell foundation models (scGPT, Geneformer, scFoundation) impose artificial gene sequence order by converting expression profiles into token sequences for language-modeling objectives, ignoring the inherently tabular, unordered structure of cell-by-gene matrices. Additionally, these models rely on centralized training over sensitive genomic data, creating significant privacy risks through potential linkage attacks, and use unrealistic corruption strategies (e.g., masking with -1) that create a training-inference mismatch.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a cell-by-gene expression matrix where each cell is represented by 1,200 highly variable genes (HVGs) per study, with raw read counts binned into discrete intervals (including a dedicated zero bin to preserve biological signal from non-expressed genes). A [CLS] token is prepended to each cell's gene feature sequence to aggregate a row-level cell embedding, while individual gene columns yield column-level gene embeddings. Downstream outputs include imputed expression values, perturbation response vectors, cell type labels, and learned cell/gene embeddings, all produced by attaching task-specific heads to the pretrained tabular transformer.

## Method

TABULA formulates single-cell pretraining as a dual-axis tabular learning problem: column-wise gene reconstruction (MSE loss recovering original binned expression from a corrupted view across all cells in a batch) captures gene-level dependencies, while row-wise cell contrastive learning (SimCLR loss treating original and corrupted views of the same cell as positive pairs) captures cell-level heterogeneity. Corrupted views are generated by resampling 60% of gene values from the empirical marginal distribution within the batch (following XTab), avoiding the unrealistic sentinel values used by prior FMs. The architecture consists of a tissue-specific Tabular Embedder (gene token embedding + binned value embedding + [CLS] token) feeding into a shared Tabular Transformer, trained under a federated learning framework where each tissue client retains local data and only exchanges transformer weights with a central server for federated averaging. This decoupling of client-specific embedders from the global transformer enables tissue-aware representation learning while preserving data privacy across institutions.

## Results

On cell type annotation, TABULA achieves 98.10% accuracy on hPancreas (vs. 96.80% for scGPT and 94.92% for scFoundation) and 99.35% on Cell Lines, while remaining competitive on Myeloid (62.13% vs. 64.20% for scGPT). On genetic perturbation prediction, TABULA achieves the highest Pearson^delta on all genes for Adamson (0.695 vs. 0.615 for scGPT) and Replogle (0.311 vs. 0.242 for scGPT), and the best DE-gene score on Norman (0.778 vs. 0.742 for scGPT). For gene imputation, TABULA consistently achieves lower MSE than scGPT, scBERT, and Geneformer across four datasets with statistically significant improvements (Wilcoxon P < 0.05). Notably, TABULA achieves these results using only half the pretraining data (15M cells) compared to scGPT's 30M+ cells.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TABULA outperforms scGPT [4], the strongest foundation model baseline, on hPancreas cell type annotation (98.10% vs. 96.80% accuracy) and on perturbation prediction across all three datasets (e.g., +0.080 Pearson^delta on Adamson all genes, +0.069 on Replogle all genes). Against Geneformer [1], TABULA achieves substantially higher scores on perturbation prediction (0.695 vs. 0.199 on Adamson all genes) and cell type annotation (98.10% vs. 93.95% on hPancreas). scFoundation [2] is marked OOT (out-of-time) on perturbation prediction and is slightly ahead of TABULA on Myeloid accuracy (63.52% vs. 62.13%), but TABULA wins on hPancreas and Cell Lines. Task-specific baselines GEARS [18] and CPA [19] are clearly outperformed by TABULA on perturbation prediction (e.g., 0.695 vs. 0.531 for GEARS on Adamson all genes). scBERT [8] is only compared on gene imputation, where TABULA achieves lower MSE across all four datasets. The one clear loss is on Myeloid cell type annotation, where scGPT and scFoundation both exceed TABULA by 1–2% in accuracy, though TABULA still leads in precision and recall on that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

TABULA departs from the dominant NLP-inspired paradigm in single-cell foundation models (Geneformer, scGPT, scFoundation, CellPLM), which convert gene expression profiles into token sequences and train with autoregressive or masked language modeling objectives. Instead, it reframes pretraining as a tabular learning problem, borrowing the corrupted-view generation strategy from XTab (empirical marginal resampling) and adapting scGPT's value binning to include zero-expressed genes. The dual-axis objective—column-wise gene reconstruction paired with row-wise SimCLR contrastive learning—is a novel combination that explicitly models the cell-by-gene matrix without imposing artificial gene ordering. The federated training setup, with tissue-specific embedders and a shared global transformer, is a new architectural choice in the single-cell FM literature motivated by privacy concerns around linkage attacks on scRNA-seq data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TABULA addresses a genuine and underexplored gap: the tension between the scale of public single-cell atlases and the privacy risks of centralized training on individual-level genomic data. The demonstration that a federated, tabular-modeling approach can match or exceed centralized sequence-based models with half the pretraining data is practically significant for institutions that cannot share raw data. However, the privacy guarantees remain informal (no differential privacy or formal threat-model analysis), the federated evaluation is limited to four tissue clients with 250K cells each, and the framework's scalability to hundreds of institutions or to multi-omics modalities remains untested. Open questions include whether the tabular objective generalizes to spatial transcriptomics or proteomics, and whether the tissue-specific embedder design can be replaced by more principled domain-adaptation mechanisms.
