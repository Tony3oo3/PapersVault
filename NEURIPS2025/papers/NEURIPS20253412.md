## Problem

While grid cells in the medial entorhinal cortex are known to support path integration, the exact computational mechanism by which the neural system performs this computation remains unresolved. Prior RNN-based studies focused on whether grid-like representations emerge or are necessary for path integration, but left the internal mechanism of trained networks as a black box and did not examine the functional role of band cells. This paper addresses the gap by using neuroscience prior knowledge and methods to dissect how a trained RNN actually implements path integration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253412/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The RNN receives a sequence of 2D velocity vectors (v_t ∈ R²) at each timestep and an initial state set by a linear projection of the initial place cell bump vector (r_0 = M_0 p_0), mimicking the HPC→MEC anchoring signal. At each step, the network outputs a 512-dimensional place cell activity vector decoded via a linear readout matrix W, from which the agent's 2D position is recovered by averaging the centers of the three most active place cells. The environment is a 2.2m × 2.2m square arena with 20-step trajectories and an average agent speed of 0.1 m/s.

## Method

The paper trains a vanilla 4096-unit RNN with ReLU nonlinearity via BPTT on the path integration task, then applies three neuroscience-inspired analyses to interpret the trained model: (1) spatial rate mapping and spectral/autocorrelation scoring to classify neurons into grid cells, band cells, and undefined cells; (2) systematic connection pruning (velocity inputs, readout connections, cell types, and inter-type connections) to dissect information flow; and (3) Isomap dimensionality reduction and connectivity profiling to reveal 1D continuous attractor dynamics in band cell populations. These analyses uncover a hierarchical pathway where velocity is first processed by band cells, band and grid cells interact bidirectionally to integrate motion, and grid cells output 2D location. Inspired by this, the authors construct a mechanistic neural circuit model in which band cells form 1D CANNs (with pure band cells, direction-conjunctive v⁺ and v⁻ sub-populations) that feed into a 2D CANN of grid cells, reducing the total neuron count from 5N² (grid-only models) to N² + 6N while preserving path integration accuracy.

## Results

The trained RNN (N_R=4096) achieves a path integration error of 2.09 ± 0.06% (normalized by arena size), with stable performance (<2.19%) for N_R ≥ 4096. Pruning experiments reveal that removing velocity inputs to band cells increases error to 2.32% (vs. 0.11% for grid cells), while pruning read-out connections from grid cells increases error to 9.15% (vs. 2.67% for band cells), confirming the hierarchical pathway: velocity → band cells → (bidirectional) → grid cells → location output. The proposed hierarchical neural circuit model reduces total neuron count from 5N² (grid-only models) to 6N + N² while maintaining path integration performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing path-integration methods in a traditional sense; its primary contribution is interpretability of a trained RNN. However, it provides several meaningful comparisons. First, against grid-only CANN models [11,12,13], the proposed hierarchical band-grid circuit reduces neuron count from 5N² to 6N+N² (e.g., for N=100: 50,000 → 700 neurons) without sacrificing performance, offering a computational efficiency argument. Second, the hierarchical RNN with the band-grid pathway as an architectural prior achieves performance comparable to the vanilla RNN, validating that the discovered pathway is the functional core. Third, across three alternative training paradigms (LSTM variant, Xu et al. 2022, Petterson et al. 2024), band cells consistently show the greatest sensitivity to velocity input pruning (e.g., 8.36–15.27% error increase for band vs. <1% for grid in the Xu et al. paradigm), confirming the robustness of the hierarchical finding across architectures and loss functions. The location reconstruction control (no velocity input) produces only grid cells without band cells, demonstrating that band cell emergence is specifically tied to the path integration demand.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RNN path-integration paradigm established by Banino et al. (2018) and Cueva & Wei (2018), but shifts the focus from whether grid cells emerge to *how* the trained network computes path integration. It departs from prior RNN-based studies (which asked whether grid cells are necessary) by applying neuroscience interpretability tools—neuron-type classification via spatial tuning maps, connection pruning, and attractor-dynamics analysis—to reveal a hierarchical band→grid information pathway. The resulting neural circuit model draws on the 2D CANN framework of Burak & Fiete (2009) for grid cells, the 1D CANN with direction-conjunctive cells of Zhang et al. (2022) for band cells, and the earlier band-grid hierarchical proposals of Bush & Burgess (2014), but with distinct mathematical formulations and a principled derivation from the trained RNN rather than hand-design alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the long-standing assumption that grid cells are the principal velocity integrator in path integration, proposing instead that band cells (found in parasubiculum, upstream of MEC) serve as the primary velocity processors feeding into a grid-cell 2D CANN. This reframing has concrete computational advantages (reducing neuron count from ~5N² to ~N²+6N) and generates testable experimental predictions about band-cell clustering, attractor organization, and their role in Alzheimer's-related navigation deficits. Open questions include the functional role of 'undefined' cells, whether the band-grid interaction profile in the RNN directly maps onto the hand-designed circuit, and whether the hierarchical pathway generalizes to path integration in abstract (non-physical) spaces.
