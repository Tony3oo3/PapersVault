## Problem

Existing protein cleavage site predictors are trained independently for each enzyme, making them unable to generalize to proteases without labeled data. This limitation is severe because cleavage site databases (e.g., MEROPS) cover only ~866 enzymes, while thousands more proteolytic enzymes exist. The paper addresses this by building a unified predictor that generalizes across diverse proteolytic enzymes, including unseen ones, by leveraging the more widely available active-site annotations.

## Input / Output

The model takes an enzyme and a substrate, each represented by ESM-2 480-dimensional residue embeddings (padded to 1500 residues) plus 3D Cα coordinates from PDB, AlphaFoldDB, or OmegaFold. For the enzyme, a pairwise energy frustration matrix (computed via Frustratometer with AWSEM potentials) is additionally provided; for the substrate, only the pairwise distance matrix is used. The model outputs a binary cleavage-site label for each substrate residue (predicted over 31-residue windows centered on each position) and, as an auxiliary output, a per-residue active-site probability for the enzyme.

## Method

UniZyme's core is a biochemically-informed enzyme encoder: a graph transformer whose self-attention scores are biased by both 3D residue-pair distances and local energy frustration scores, each mapped through learnable Gaussian basis kernels (K=10) and an MLP. To incorporate active-site knowledge, the model (i) pretrains the enzyme encoder on 11,530 UniProt enzymes with annotated active sites, (ii) uses an active-site-aware pooling mechanism where predicted active-site probabilities (via a Gaussian-basis + MLP mapping) determine residue-level aggregation weights instead of uniform mean pooling, and (iii) jointly optimizes a weighted sum of cleavage-site BCE loss and active-site BCE loss (λ=10). The substrate encoder mirrors the enzyme transformer but omits the energy-frustration channel. Cleavage prediction concatenates a 31-residue slice of the substrate representation with the pooled enzyme vector and passes it through an MLP.

## Results

On the supervised benchmark (69 enzyme families, ~21K pairs), UniZyme achieves 79.3% average PR-AUC with a 75.0% Rate of Rank 1 and average rank of 1.26. On the zero-shot benchmark (23 enzyme families, ~5.3K pairs, all test enzymes <60% sequence identity to training), UniZyme reaches 71.1% average PR-AUC with a 78.3% Rate of Rank 1. In a case study on an unseen HIV-1 enzyme, UniZyme correctly predicts all four experimentally validated cleavage sites on substrate P62157 (100% accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniZyme outperforms the two unified enzyme-substrate baselines (ClipZyme and ReactZyme) by 4.6 and 9.3 PR-AUC points respectively in the supervised setting, and by 9.4 and 6.4 points in the zero-shot setting; all differences are statistically significant (p < 0.05, two-sample t-test). Against enzyme-specific deep models (ProsperousPlus, DeepCleave, DeepDigest), UniZyme's advantage is dramatic—79.3% vs. 4.2–7.2% PR-AUC—highlighting the benefit of a unified multi-enzyme framework over per-enzyme predictors. On the C14.003 family, UniZyme (45.9%) also surpasses the dedicated caspase-3 tools CAT3 (18.5%) and ScreenCap3 (29.2%). The gap over ClipZyme (which uses an EGNN enzyme encoder pretrained on reaction prediction) is attributed to UniZyme's biochemically-informed encoder with energy frustration and active-site-aware pooling, while the gap over ReactZyme (ESM-2 + MLP) reflects the added value of structural and active-site signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of enzyme-specific cleavage site predictors (CAT3, DeepCleave, ProsperousPlus, DeepDigest), which train independent models per enzyme and cannot generalize to unseen proteases. It also extends the enzyme-substrate reaction prediction line (ClipZyme, ReactZyme) by moving from substrate-level reaction classification to residue-level cleavage site prediction, while incorporating a biochemically-informed encoder that fuses energetic frustration scores (via Frustratometer/AWSEM) with 3D distance geometry in a transformer attention mechanism. The active-site-aware pooling and pretraining on UniProt's 11,530 annotated enzymes represent a novel strategy to overcome the severe enzyme-substrate imbalance (1:11.7) in MEROPS, leveraging the much cheaper active-site annotation as a proxy signal for catalytic function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to predict cleavage sites for entirely unseen enzymes (zero-shot, PR-AUC 71.1%) opens practical pathways for off-target assessment of therapeutic proteins and rapid characterization of novel proteases without requiring expensive experimental assays. The paper's HIV-1 case study demonstrates immediate translational relevance for inhibitor design. Key open questions include whether the approach extends beyond proteolytic enzymes to other catalytic classes, whether dynamic (rather than static) structural information would further improve accuracy, and whether enzyme-catalyzed reactions follow scaling laws with more data. The limited enzyme coverage in MEROPS (866 enzymes) remains a fundamental bottleneck that active-site pretraining mitigates but does not eliminate.
