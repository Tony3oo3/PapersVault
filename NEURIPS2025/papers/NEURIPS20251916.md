## Problem

Existing adaptive gradient algorithms for stochastic hierarchical optimization (minimax and bilevel) cannot achieve optimal convergence rates across varying levels of stochastic gradient noise without prior knowledge of the noise magnitude. While AdaGrad-type methods achieve such adaptivity in single-level optimization, extending them to hierarchical settings introduces intractable randomness dependencies between upper- and lower-level adaptive stepsizes that require strong assumptions (bounded gradients, bounded function values) to analyze, undermining the very adaptivity they aim to provide.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume stochastic gradient samples drawn from unknown distributions at each iteration, requiring two independent samples per iteration (ξ_t and ξ'_t) to estimate gradient variance online. The bilevel variant additionally requires stochastic Hessian/Jacobian samples for the Neumann series hypergradient approximation. The output is a sequence of upper- and lower-level parameter iterates whose average gradient norm satisfies a high-probability bound of Õ(1/√T + √σ̄/T^{1/4}), automatically interpolating between deterministic and stochastic regimes without any user-specified noise parameter.

## Method

The core mechanism combines normalized SGD with momentum (Cutkosky & Mehta, 2020) for the upper-level variable with AdaGrad-Norm for the lower-level variable, but with a novel adaptive momentum parameter β_t that is set online from the empirical variance of paired stochastic gradients (‖g_{x,k} − g̃_{x,k}‖²). This adaptive momentum simultaneously drives the upper-level stepsize η_{x,t} = η√(α'_t)/√t and controls the ratio between upper- and lower-level stepsizes, maintaining balanced progress without knowing the noise level. The key analytical contribution is a high-probability framework that handles the statistical dependencies between time-varying adaptive parameters and stochastic gradients using martingale concentration bounds (empirical Bernstein) and a linear programming argument to decouple the cross-term correlations in the momentum recursion. The lower-level analysis generalizes the AdaGrad-Norm convex analysis of Attia & Koren (2023) to accommodate iteration-dependent shifts from the upper-level variable, yielding tight bounds on the (hyper)gradient estimation bias that feed into the upper-level convergence proof.

## Results

The proposed Ada-Minimax and Ada-BiO algorithms achieve a sharp high-probability convergence rate of Õ(1/√T + √σ̄/T^{1/4}) for the gradient norm in T iterations, automatically interpolating between Õ(1/√T) in the deterministic regime and Õ(√σ̄/T^{1/4}) in the stochastic regime without prior knowledge of the noise level. In deep AUC maximization on the imbalanced Sentiment140 dataset (p=0.9), Ada-Minimax achieves 20% higher training AUC and 2% higher test AUC than the best baseline (PDSM), while also demonstrating the fastest convergence in wall-clock time. In synthetic minimax experiments, Ada-Minimax consistently outperforms TiAda across all noise levels (σ = 0, 20, 50, 100), and notably converges under high noise (σ=100) where TiAda fails entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TiAda [47], the closest adaptive minimax baseline, Ada-Minimax wins decisively: in synthetic experiments it outperforms TiAda at every noise level tested, and under high noise (σ=100) TiAda fails to converge after extensive tuning while Ada-Minimax converges successfully. In the deep AUC maximization task on Sentiment140, Ada-Minimax surpasses PDSM [30] by 20% in training AUC and 2% in test AUC, and also outperforms SGDA [50] and TiAda [47] in both AUC and wall-clock convergence speed. For bilevel optimization, Ada-BiO is compared against S-TFBO [73] in Table 2; unlike S-TFBO which is restricted to the deterministic setting, Ada-BiO provides a unified high-probability guarantee covering both deterministic and stochastic regimes with an explicit dependence on the noise parameters. The paper also demonstrates strong hyperparameter robustness: varying α ∈ [0.1, 2.0], η_x ∈ [0.001, 0.01], η_y ∈ [0.001, 0.05], and γ ∈ [0.01, 2.0] produces minimal changes in final performance, reducing practical tuning burden relative to the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the momentum normalization framework of Cutkosky & Mehta (ICML 2020) and the AdaGrad-Norm analysis of Attia & Koren (ICML 2023) from single-level to hierarchical (minimax and bilevel) optimization. The key departure from prior adaptive methods (TiAda, BiAdam, AdaGDA) is that standard AdaGrad stepsizes applied simultaneously to upper- and lower-level variables create intractable randomness dependencies; instead, the paper uses momentum normalization for the upper level with a noise-aware adaptive momentum parameter, paired with AdaGrad-Norm for the lower level. This design choice is what enables the first high-probability, parameter-free convergence guarantees that automatically adapt to unknown noise levels in nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax and nonconvex-strongly-convex bilevel settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The automatic rate interpolation from Õ(1/T^{1/4}) in high-noise to Õ(1/√T) in low-noise regimes, without requiring any prior knowledge of the noise magnitude, addresses a practical gap: practitioners running adversarial training, meta-learning, or hyperparameter optimization rarely know their effective gradient noise level a priori. A notable limitation is the lower-bounded noise assumption (Assumptions 3.2(ii) and 3.4(vi)), which the authors acknowledge as a key open problem; removing it while preserving sharp rates remains unresolved. The empirical validation, while positive, is limited to one synthetic function, one AUC maximization dataset (Sentiment140), and one hyperparameter optimization task, leaving open questions about scalability to larger architectures and more diverse problem structures.
