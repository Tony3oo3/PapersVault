## Problem

Existing 3D indoor layout generation methods either overfit to small, limited datasets or depend on manually defined spatial constraints (scene graphs, symbolic rules) that sacrifice flexibility and fail to capture fine-grained user instructions. This leaves a gap: no approach can produce open-vocabulary, instruction-aligned 3D layouts that are simultaneously physically plausible, semantically coherent, and responsive to detailed spatial descriptions without rigid constraint-based optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text description of a room (at coarse, medium, or fine granularity) plus user-specified room dimensions (length, width, height as integers ≤ 256 px) and produces a full 3D numerical layout specifying per-object center coordinates (x, y, z), dimensions (l, w, h), orientation, and a natural-language prompt for 3D asset generation. The layout is internally represented in a CSS-like bounding-box format, first as a 2D top-down BEV layout and then lifted into 3D, before being assembled into a rendered scene using an external 3D object generator (Trellis).

## Method

DirectLayout decomposes scene synthesis into three stages: (1) a BEV Layout Generator (fine-tuned Qwen2.5-32B) produces a 2D top-down layout via a four-step Chain-of-Thought process (entity extraction → order decision → spatial reasoning → answer organization), (2) a 3D Layout Generator lifts the BEV layout into 3D by inferring vertical positions and generating per-object asset prompts in zero-shot, and (3) Iterative Asset-Layout Alignment refines the 3D layout using evaluator feedback after 3D assets are rendered. Training uses two stages: SFT on ~6,500 GPT-4o-annotated CoT layouts from 3D-Front, followed by DPO with a CoT-Grounded Generative Layout Reward computed by a dual-evaluator (a VLM as Spatial Evaluator for high-level semantic violations and a reasoning LLM as Quantitative Evaluator for fine-grained numerical inconsistencies) across seven object-level criteria, aggregated via an entropy-weighting scheme. This reward-based preference learning is the key mechanism that improves generalizable spatial reasoning beyond what SFT alone achieves.

## Results

On 15 indoor scene categories, DirectLayout (full) achieves the best scores across all six evaluation metrics: 8.74% Out-of-Bound rate, 6.31% Collision rate, 72.04 Positional Coherency, 69.87 Rotational Coherency, 75.56 PSA, and 27.43 CLIP Score. The open-source-only variant (Qwen2.5-72B + Qwen2.5-VL-72B + QwQ-32B) also outperforms all baselines, reaching 9.20% Out-of-Bound, 8.45% Collision, 70.73 Pos., 67.51 Rot., 73.78 PSA, and 26.47 CLIP Score. In human evaluation, DirectLayout scores 4.56–4.84 across semantic compliance, physical plausibility, and layout rationality at all prompt granularities, versus 1.64–3.72 for the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DirectLayout (full) outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against LayoutGPT, it reduces Out-of-Bound from 33.91% to 8.74% and Collision from 15.89% to 6.31%, while improving PSA from 55.11 to 75.56 and CLIP Score from 21.65 to 27.43. Against Holodeck, the gains are even more pronounced on semantic metrics: PSA jumps from 49.78 to 75.56 (+25.78) and CLIP Score from 25.21 to 27.43, while Out-of-Bound drops from 36.33% to 8.74%. I-Design is the weakest baseline overall, scoring only 13.47 on Pos. and 29.33 on PSA, reflecting its tendency to cluster objects locally and lose global semantic coherence. The ablation variant replacing the fine-tuned BEV generator with a general reasoning LLM (QwQ-32B) shows that dedicated spatial inductive bias is critical: it drops PSA from 75.56 to 47.11 and Pos. from 72.04 to 44.33, confirming that general-purpose reasoning alone is insufficient for globally consistent layout synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

DirectLayout departs from the dominant paradigm in 3D indoor layout generation that relies on predefined spatial constraints or scene-graph intermediates (Holodeck, I-Design, InstructScene, AnyHome) by directly generating numerical layouts through LLM spatial reasoning. It builds on DPO [Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023] as the preference-optimization backbone, but introduces a novel CoT-Grounded Generative Layout Reward with a dual-evaluator (VLM + reasoning LLM) that provides fine-grained, criterion-level feedback rather than a single holistic score. The task decomposition into BEV generation, 3D lifting, and iterative refinement draws on the practical observation that 2D top-down planning is more tractable for LLMs than full 3D coordinate prediction, while the CoT Activation stage adapts structured reasoning techniques from symbolic/mathematical LLM work to the spatial domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that direct numerical layout generation with CoT-enhanced LLMs can simultaneously achieve strong physical plausibility and fine-grained semantic compliance without sacrificing flexibility to predefined rules, addressing a core tension in the field. However, the iterative alignment loop introduces significant inference latency that precludes real-time interaction, and the BEV representation inherently loses information for vertically stacked objects. Open questions include whether the approach scales to scenes with many small objects, whether the dual-evaluator reward can be distilled into a lighter model for efficiency, and whether the 3D-Front-derived CoT annotations generalize to architectural styles and room types beyond the training distribution.
