## Problem

Biological brains learn continually from mostly unlabeled data streams interspersed with sparse supervisory episodes, yet standard machine learning approaches suffer catastrophic forgetting when supervised fine-tuning is naively introduced into a continual setting. The key gap is how to integrate sparse class labels into a continually learning representation without degrading previously acquired knowledge, particularly in the class-incremental regime where task identity is unknown at evaluation time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255030/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 224×224 ImageNet-100) in a class-incremental stream partitioned into sessions of 20 disjoint classes, with only a small fraction (1–100%) of samples carrying class labels. It produces two outputs: (1) a modulation-invariant feature representation from a ConViT or ResNet-18 backbone, evaluated via linear probing or kNN on the last four layers, and (2) per-class affine modulation parameters (scaling and shifting vectors) that are learned once per new class, frozen thereafter, and applied during consolidation but not at inference. The modulations scale with the number of classes (~4.1M parameters for 100 classes) but are not used during evaluation.

## Method

TMCL operates in two alternating phases per session. First, when new class labels appear, per-class affine modulations (FiLM-style scaling/shifting) are learned on frozen feedforward weights using an orthogonal projection loss (OPL) that drives the new class's modulated representation center to be orthogonal to all other available class representations, approximating a one-vs-rest separation without requiring all-vs-all classification. Second, during consolidation, the feedforward weights are trained via a contrastive objective that jointly optimizes view invariance (standard augmented-view positives) and modulation invariance: modulated views of the same sample (using randomly sampled past modulations with stop-gradient) serve as additional positives, pulling the unmodulated representation toward its modulated counterparts. This co-opts the existing view-invariance machinery to consolidate task-specific knowledge encoded in the frozen modulations into the shared representation space, requiring no labels during consolidation and no exemplar replay, while the orthogonalized modulations provide a functional regularization that limits forgetting of previously learned classes.

## Results

On the class-incremental CIFAR-100 benchmark (5 sessions, 20 classes each), TMCL (VI+MI) achieves 60.7% linear readout accuracy with only 1% of labels, compared to 39.1% for SupCon and 41.6% for cross-entropy (CE). On ImageNet-100 at 1% labels, TMCL reaches 62.0% versus 33.6% (SupCon) and 35.2% (CE). In the fully labeled regime (100%), TMCL (60.7% CIFAR-100, 64.5% ImageNet-100) slightly underperforms SupCon (62.2%, 66.6%) but remains competitive with CE. TMCL is also robust to up to 99% label noise (59.4% accuracy), whereas fully supervised CE collapses to 6.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TMCL (VI+MI) decisively outperforms SupCon [93] in label-sparse settings: at 1% labels it gains +21.6 points on CIFAR-100 (60.7 vs 39.1) and +28.4 on ImageNet-100 (62.0 vs 33.6), but underperforms SupCon by ~1.5 points when 100% of labels are available. Against the CE baseline [94], TMCL leads by +19.1 points at 1% labels on CIFAR-100 and +26.8 on ImageNet-100, while roughly matching CE at 100% labels. On the ResNet-18 architecture (Table 6), TMCL (58.1% at 100% labels) outperforms all replay- and regularization-based baselines including ER [61] (54.6%), DER [48] (55.3%), LUMP [96] (57.8%), Less-Forget [51] (56.4%), POD [56] (55.9%), and CLS-ER [78] (56.7%), though it is slightly below the state-invariance methods CaSSLe (60.1%) and PNR (60.3%). The paper notes that MI and SI are complementary: combining them (VI+SI+MI) yields the strongest label-sparse performance, suggesting TMCL's modulation-based consolidation is orthogonal to model-state invariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the biological interpretation of top-down modulations from Iyer et al. [36] (active dendrites enabling multi-task learning) into a class-incremental continual learning algorithm. It builds on the session-based class-incremental protocol of Fini et al. [69] (CaSSLe) and repurposes the Orthogonal Projection Loss [81] for learning per-class affine modulations that orthogonalize new class representations. Conceptually, TMCL departs from state-invariance approaches (CaSSLe, PNR) that store a full network copy, instead using lightweight frozen modulations as a form of 'functional replay' that consolidates task-specific knowledge into feedforward weights via a modulation-invariant contrastive objective. The method also connects to the parameter-efficient fine-tuning literature (FiLM, BitFit, LoRA) but reframes modulations as a consolidation mechanism rather than a task-specific adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TMCL offers a biologically grounded mechanism for balancing stability and plasticity in continual learning, showing that sparse labels (as few as 1%) can be integrated without catastrophic forgetting while producing more transferable representations than supervised contrastive baselines. The robustness to 99% label noise—where TMCL never underperforms the pure self-supervised baseline—suggests a practical advantage in noisy natural learning environments. Open questions include whether static per-class modulations scale to open-vocabulary settings, whether the approach extends to domain-incremental learning, and whether a generative model could replace the stored modulation set. The finding that modulation invariance yields more generalizable features than class-based invariance (SupCon) even with full labels hints at a broader principle about what invariances to learn for transfer.
