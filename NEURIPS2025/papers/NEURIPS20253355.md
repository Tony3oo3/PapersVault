## Problem

Large-scale, formula-rich spreadsheet corpora for AI research remain scarce: existing datasets like EUSES (~4K files), Enron (~15K), and Fuse (~249K) are limited by small scale, narrow domains, or a lack of executable formula semantics. Moreover, spreadsheet-specific natural language tasks—mapping queries to cell ranges and generating formulas—lack dedicated benchmarks and annotated training data, leaving a gap between general table understanding (e.g., TURL, Spider) and the semi-structured, formula-laden reality of spreadsheets.

## Input / Output

For NL2SR, the model receives a natural language query alongside a serialized spreadsheet (cells as 'address,content' pairs separated by '|', rows by line breaks, merged cells listed as 'upper-left:lower-right' annotations) and outputs a single cell range address. For NL2Formula, the same spreadsheet context is paired with a query describing a computation, and the model outputs a syntactically valid Excel formula string. The underlying corpus comprises 290,509 deduplicated worksheets (from 324,988 workbooks) serialized to JSON with fields for filename, sheet name, used range, and per-cell content/formula, with 78%+ English content and rich formula coverage (IF, SUM, VLOOKUP, IFERROR dominating).

## Method

The corpus is built by integrating three sources (Fuse, Enron, and a custom ExcelForum crawl of 147K workbooks), then applying a preprocessing pipeline that standardizes .xls→.xlsx, filters by language (lingua, 0.8 confidence threshold), retains only syntactically valid single-sheet formulas, and deduplicates via MinHash (1000-length signatures) + LSH (r=100, b=10, Jaccard threshold 0.8), reducing 566K worksheets to 290,509. For task data generation, a rejection-sampling framework prompts Gemini-Flash-2.0 to produce k=5 candidate natural language queries per spreadsheet target (formula or range), which Claude-3.7-Sonnet then scores on clarity (30%), accuracy (30%), conciseness (20%), and completeness (20%); only candidates scoring ≥0.7 are retained. Fine-tuning uses LoRA (rank 16, all parameters targeted) or full-parameter training with DeepSpeed on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B (AdamW, lr=2e-5, cosine schedule, bf16, 5 epochs on 8×A800 GPUs), achieving 97.5% NL2SR and 71.7% NL2Formula accuracy—substantially outperforming zero/few-shot baselines including GPT-4o and 70B+ models.

## Results

After LoRA fine-tuning on Sheetpedia, LLaMA-3.1-8B achieves 97.50% accuracy on NL2SR and Qwen2.5-7B reaches ~95% on the same task, far exceeding the best few-shot baseline (Qwen2.5-72B at 75.83%). For NL2Formula, full-parameter fine-tuning of LLaMA-3.1-8B yields 71.67% exact-match accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o's best few-shot score of 64.17%. A mixed-data full-parameter strategy balances both tasks at 96.67% NL2SR and 70.00% NL2Formula, demonstrating that domain-specific fine-tuning on Sheetpedia substantially outperforms general-purpose LLMs in both zero-shot and few-shot settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The fine-tuned 8B/7B models decisively outperform all few-shot baselines on both tasks. On NL2SR, LoRA fine-tuning of LLaMA-3.1-8B reaches 97.50%, surpassing the strongest few-shot baseline Qwen2.5-72B (75.83%) by over 21 points and GPT-4o (69.17%) by nearly 29 points. On NL2Formula, full-parameter fine-tuning achieves 71.67%, beating GPT-4o's best few-shot score (64.17%) by 7.5 points and Qwen2.5-72B (61.67%) by 10 points. Notably, the fine-tuned 8B model exceeds the 70B/72B baselines on both tasks, indicating that domain-specific training data (Sheetpedia) compensates for the parameter-scale gap. The mixed-data full-parameter strategy (96.67% NL2SR, 70.00% NL2Formula) shows that multi-task fine-tuning preserves most of the single-task performance, whereas single-task LoRA fine-tuning causes severe cross-task degradation (e.g., 5.00% NL2Formula when fine-tuned only on NL2SR data).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of spreadsheet corpus construction and LLM-based spreadsheet understanding. It builds directly on the NL2Formula formulation from Zhao et al. (EACL 2024), which converted text-to-SQL pairs into spreadsheet formula tasks, and on NLP-SIR (Flood et al., 2009) for natural language retrieval over spreadsheet content, while introducing NL2SR as a new task that maps queries to specific cell ranges. The data generation pipeline adapts the rejection-sampling paradigm from code generation (Codex) and the LLM-as-a-Judge protocol (Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2023) to the spreadsheet domain, using Gemini-Flash-2.0 for candidate generation and Claude-3.7-Sonnet for quality filtering. Corpus construction draws on and extends prior spreadsheet datasets (EUSES, Enron, Fuse) by adding a large-scale ExcelForum crawl and applying MinHash/LSH deduplication to reach 290K unique worksheets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sheetpedia fills a genuine gap as the largest publicly available formula-rich spreadsheet corpus, providing a foundation that prior smaller datasets (EUSES ~4K, Enron ~15K) could not support for training LLMs on spreadsheet-specific tasks. The introduction of NL2SR as a formal benchmark task opens a new evaluation axis beyond formula generation. However, the experimental validation is limited: the test set contains only 240 samples per task, no error bars or significance tests are reported, and the catastrophic task interference observed (NL2SR fine-tuning dropping NL2Formula to 5%) suggests the multi-task learning story is underdeveloped. Open questions include whether the corpus generalizes beyond English, whether cross-sheet formula handling (excluded here) is critical for real-world utility, and whether the rejection-sampling data generation approach scales to more complex spreadsheet operations.
