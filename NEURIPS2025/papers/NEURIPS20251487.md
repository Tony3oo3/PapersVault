## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving models rely on label-supervised BEV feature extraction (e.g., 3D detection, segmentation labels), which constrains the generality of the learned visual representations. The human brain leverages embodied cognition to anticipate hazards and adapt to novel situations, yet no prior work has integrated human driving cognition—captured via EEG—into E2E-AD planning to go beyond what manual annotations can supervise.

## Input / Output

At inference, the system consumes the same inputs as standard E2E-AD baselines: multi-camera video clips (2 s at 2 fps), the ego vehicle's historical trajectory, and a high-level driving command, producing an autoregressively decoded planned trajectory. During Stage-1 training, the Driving-Thinking Model additionally requires paired 2 s EEG segments (64-channel, 200 Hz, normalized to [-1, 1]) from a self-collected cognitive dataset of 20 drivers, but this EEG input is entirely absent at Stage-2 training and inference, keeping the deployment footprint identical to the baseline.

## Method

The E³AD paradigm uses a two-stage pipeline. In Stage 1, a Video Swin Transformer is contrastively aligned with the frozen LaBraM large brain model via a symmetric InfoNCE loss (CLIP-style) on paired video-EEG clips, teaching the video encoder to extract driving-relevant cognitive features from raw visual input. In Stage 2, the frozen Driving-Thinking Model is plugged into mainstream E2E-AD frameworks (VAD, UniAD, GenAD, LAW) through three integration mechanisms: (i) an Attention Gate that modulates BEV spatio-temporal features with the cognitive embedding, (ii) cross-attention that enriches the ego-vehicle query with driving cognition before it interacts with visual features, and (iii) a multi-layer transformer decoder that performs chained reasoning over the initial planning feature using the cognitive embedding as key/value. Ablations confirm that the gains originate specifically from the contrastive learning with LaBraM (not from extra visual capacity), and that the third mechanism—directly interacting with planning features—yields the largest improvements, indicating that high-level decision-reasoning cognition is the most transferable component.

## Results

On nuScenes open-loop evaluation, E3AD(VAD-Base) achieves the best average collision rate of 0.18% (vs. 0.22% for VAD-Base, a 18.2% relative reduction) and 0.64 m average L2 error (vs. 0.72 m, a 11.1% relative reduction). E3AD(UniAD) reduces the average collision rate from 0.31% to 0.23% (25.8% relative improvement), matching the best UniAD-metric result in the table. In closed-loop Bench2Drive evaluation, E3AD(UniAD-Base) achieves a driving score of 50.07 and route completion rate of 20.12%, surpassing UniAD-Base (45.81 DS, 16.36% SR) by 10.1% and 23.0% respectively, while E3AD(VAD) reaches 47.63 DS and 19.54% SR versus VAD's 42.35 DS and 15.00% SR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E3AD consistently outperforms all seven baselines on collision rate, the paper's primary safety metric. Against VAD-Base, E3AD reduces average collision from 0.22% to 0.18% (18.2% relative) and L2 from 0.72 m to 0.64 m; against VAD-Tiny, collision drops from 0.38% to 0.35% and L2 from 0.78 m to 0.68 m. Against UniAD, E3AD(UniAD) cuts collision from 0.31% to 0.23% (25.8% relative) while maintaining the same L2 (1.03 m). Against GenAD, E3AD(GenAD) improves collision from 0.26% to 0.24% and L2 from 0.49 m to 0.47 m. Against LAW, E3AD(LAW) reduces collision from 0.30% to 0.22% (26.7% relative) with unchanged L2 (0.61 m). Against ST-P3 and BEV-Planner, E3AD methods achieve substantially lower collision rates (0.18–0.24% vs. 0.49–0.71%). In closed-loop Bench2Drive, E3AD(UniAD-Base) achieves 50.07 driving score and 20.12% route completion, beating UniAD-Base (45.81, 16.36%) and VAD (42.35, 15.00%) by 10.1% and 12.5% in driving score respectively, while incurring only ~10% additional inference latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-style contrastive learning paradigm from text-image alignment to video-EEG alignment, using the frozen LaBraM large brain model as a teacher to train a 'Driving-Thinking Model' (Video Swin Transformer) that infers driving cognition from visual inputs. It then plugs this frozen cognitive encoder into mainstream end-to-end driving frameworks (VAD, UniAD, GenAD, LAW) via three integration strategies, departing from prior cognitive-enhanced AI work (BrainBERT, CogBERT, HLTP) that applied brain signals to NLP or simpler trajectory prediction rather than full E2E-AD planning. The approach is conceptually distinct from prior E2E-AD methods that rely solely on label-supervised BEV features, introducing an implicit, brain-inspired supervision channel that requires no additional sensor input at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that general-purpose EEG representations can transfer useful driving cognition to vision-based E2E-AD models, opening a brain-inspired direction for autonomous driving. However, the cognitive dataset is very small (~2,400 clips from 20 drivers), the precise mechanism by which EEG alignment improves planning remains unexplained, and the performance gains, while consistent across multiple baselines, are modest in absolute terms (collision rate reductions of 0.04–0.08%). Key open questions include whether scaling the EEG-video dataset or using more diverse driving scenarios would yield larger gains, whether the benefits persist in fully closed-loop long-horizon deployment, and whether the observed improvements are truly due to 'cognition' or to the regularization effect of contrastive pretraining on the visual encoder.
