## Problem

Neural unsigned distance fields (UDFs) are inherently noisy, and at high meshing resolutions this noise causes single-pass triangulation methods (Marching Cubes, Dual Contouring variants) to miss surfaces entirely, producing holes and disconnected geometry. No existing UDF meshing approach incorporates information from previously extracted surface elements or reasons across neighboring voxels over multiple passes to correct these local failures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255692/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method queries a neural UDF (parameterized by a latent shape code) on a regular 3D grid at resolutions up to 512³, obtaining UDF values and gradients at the 8 vertices of each cubic cell. The network also consumes the current pseudo-sign configurations of the target cell and its 6 face-sharing neighbors (7×128 values), making the total input 928-dimensional. The output is a 128-way pseudo-sign configuration per cell (one-hot over all sign patterns up to global symmetry), which is triangulated via Marching Cubes or DualMesh-UDF into an explicit 3D mesh. The UDF is resolution-normalized by dividing by cell size before feeding the network.

## Method

The core contribution is an iterative refinement loop over a small fully connected network (2 hidden layers of 1024 nodes, 128 outputs) that predicts pseudo-sign configurations for each cell's 8 corners. At iteration 0 the neighbor sign inputs are zero vectors, so the network relies solely on local UDF values and gradients; in subsequent iterations (up to 5 additional passes), the sigmoid-activated outputs from the previous pass are concatenated as neighbor context, allowing spatial propagation of surface information from increasingly distant cells. Training uses cross-entropy loss summed over a uniformly random number of iterations (1–6) per epoch, with Gaussian noise augmentation (σ=1) on both UDF values and gradients to build robustness. A practical filtering strategy removes cells with UDF ≥ 0.1 (eliminating ~85% of cells at 256³) and skips cells whose predicted confidence exceeds 0.999 in later iterations, keeping inference time comparable to single-pass baselines. The final pseudo-signs are triangulated with standard Marching Cubes or DualMesh-UDF, requiring no post-processing.

## Results

At 512³ resolution on ShapeNet cars, the proposed method achieves a median L2 Chamfer Distance of 8.84×10⁻⁵, F1 of 65.6, and Image Consistency of 88.9, substantially outperforming the next-best MC-based baseline NSD-UDF (CD 56.9, F1 58.8, IC 83.8) and MeshUDF (CD 82.7, F1 57.0, IC 81.7). On chairs at 512³, the gap widens further: Ours+MC reaches CD 8.76 and F1 74.5 versus NSD-UDF's CD 295 and F1 64.7. At lower resolutions (128³) and on simpler shapes (MGN garments), the method is competitive but does not consistently dominate, with NSD-UDF achieving slightly lower CD on MGN (1.34 vs. 2.04).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all six baselines at high resolutions (256³ and 512³) on complex shapes. Against NSD-UDF [36], the closest competitor, the advantage is most dramatic: at 512³ on cars, Ours+MC achieves CD 8.84 vs. 56.9 (6.4× lower), and on chairs CD 8.76 vs. 295 (34× lower), while also improving F1 by 7–10 points. MeshUDF [13] and CAP-UDF [46] degrade severely at 512³ on cars (CD 82.7 and 31.8 respectively) due to their reliance on fixed thresholds that discard surface regions under noise. DCUDF [14] produces the smoothest meshes but loses entire surface portions, yielding CD values 25–250× higher than the proposed method on cars and chairs at 512³. UNDC [5] fails entirely at 512³ due to VRAM constraints, and DualMesh-UDF [41] (even with manual tuning) remains 4–25× worse in CD on cars at 512³. At 128³ on the simpler MGN garment dataset, the method is competitive but not always best (e.g., NSD-UDF achieves CD 1.34 vs. 2.04), confirming that the iterative refinement's benefit is concentrated in high-resolution, high-noise regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends NSD-UDF (Stella et al., ECCV 2024), which introduced a feedforward network to predict pseudo-sign configurations for UDF meshing, by making the process iterative: the network is run multiple times, each pass incorporating the previous iteration's sign predictions from face-adjacent cells to propagate spatial context. This draws on iterative refinement paradigms well-established in segmentation, pose estimation, and depth estimation, but is the first application to UDF surface extraction. It departs from all prior UDF meshing methods (CAP-UDF, MeshUDF, DCUDF, DualMesh-UDF, UNDC), which operate in a single pass over individual voxels without referencing previously extracted surface elements, and from heuristic propagation approaches that merely enlarge receptive fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the counterintuitive failure of UDF meshing at higher resolutions—where increased noise causes holes and missing surfaces—can be substantially mitigated by iterative spatial propagation, enabling practical high-resolution extraction where all prior single-pass methods break down. Limitations remain: the method still produces small holes and irregular boundaries, is inherently slower than single-pass approaches (though filtering brings it to comparable runtimes), and the iterative count is fixed rather than adaptive. Open directions include end-to-end joint learning of UDF and meshing, adaptive stopping criteria, and topology-preserving refinements that would make the pipeline fully production-ready for complex open surfaces.
