## Problem

The Forward-Forward (FF) learning algorithm, a bottom-up alternative to backpropagation, relies on a goodness function (sum of squared activations) that ignores correlated variability between neurons and requires task-specific negative samples for contrastive learning. This paper asks whether a goodness function grounded in the second-order statistical structure of neural responses can eliminate the need for negative samples while incorporating noise as a constructive computational resource.

## Input / Output

The method takes grayscale (MNIST, 28×28) or RGB (CIFAR-10/100, 32×32) images and produces class labels via a linear classifier. Unusually, during both training and inference, dropout (p=0.2) generates N=20 noisy variants of each input, and the final prediction score is computed as the mean of squared classifier outputs E[Y²] over these variants rather than the standard mean, effectively using output variability as an energy-based signal.

## Method

The core contribution is a dimensionality compression loss derived from the effective dimensionality (ED) of the uncentered second moment matrix E[X^T X] of each block's neural responses. The loss has two terms: ED_c (mean ED over noisy copies of individual inputs, minimized to enforce within-sample compactness) and ED_d (ED of the batch-averaged response distribution, maximized to prevent representational collapse), combined as L = α·ED_c − (1−α)·ED_d with α=0.5. Dropout-generated noisy variants replace negative samples, enabling fully unsupervised, layer-wise bottom-up training without labels. Each block's output is projected onto a lower-dimensional subspace via a fixed random orthonormal basis (FOP) before computing ED to stabilize estimation. After layer-wise pretraining (3 epochs per block), a linear classifier is trained for 60 epochs using cross-entropy on the E[Y²] score, linking the inference procedure to energy-based learning.

## Results

On MNIST, the proposed method achieves 99.31±0.07% validation accuracy, nearly matching BP (99.33±0.04%). On CIFAR-10, it reaches 76.96±0.73%, and on CIFAR-100, 53.29±1.02%. The method outperforms all FF-variant baselines (Original FF, CaFo FF, CwC FF) and DFA across all three datasets, while remaining competitive with Hebbian and GIM approaches. It trails EBL (89.6% on CIFAR-10, 65.8% on CIFAR-100) and BP, which the authors attribute to EBL's use of a deeper five-layer network and top-down information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method clearly outperforms all FF-variant baselines: it surpasses Original FF by ~7.9 points on CIFAR-10 (76.96 vs 59) and CaFo FF by ~7.5 points (76.96 vs 69.49), while also leading on CIFAR-100 (53.29 vs 42.13 for CaFo FF). Against DFA, it gains ~3.9 points on CIFAR-10 (76.96 vs 73.10) and ~12.3 points on CIFAR-100 (53.29 vs 41.00). The method is competitive with CwC FF, Soft Hebbian, and GIM: it trails CwC FF by ~1.2 points on CIFAR-10 (76.96 vs 78.11) and Soft Hebbian by ~3.4 points (76.96 vs 80.31), but edges out GIM on CIFAR-100 (53.29 vs 50.09) and Hard Hebbian on CIFAR-10 (76.96 vs 76). The largest gap is against EBL, which leads by ~12.6 points on CIFAR-10 (89.6 vs 76.96) and ~12.5 points on CIFAR-100 (65.8 vs 53.29), though EBL uses a deeper five-layer Hopfield network with top-down signals. Against BP under the same architecture, the gap is small on MNIST (0.02 points) but widens to ~5.5 points on CIFAR-10 and ~8 points on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Hinton's Forward-Forward (FF) algorithm by replacing the original sum-of-squared-activations goodness function with one derived from effective dimensionality (ED), which captures second-order correlation structure in neural responses. It departs from the FF framework's reliance on hand-crafted negative samples by using dropout-generated noisy variants of each input, reframing noise as a computational resource rather than a nuisance. Conceptually, the ED-based objective is positioned as a correlation-aware extension of energy-based learning (EBL), and the architecture mirrors Hebbian learning with winner-take-all mechanisms, while the layer-wise training pipeline is shared with Greedy InfoMax and Direct Feedback Alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a biologically plausible, label-free training objective that eliminates the practical bottleneck of negative sample generation in FF learning, making it a more natural fit for neuromorphic hardware where stochasticity is intrinsic. However, the method is limited to a shallow three-layer CNN and shows a clear performance gap versus backpropagation on CIFAR-10/100, with the authors acknowledging that scaling beyond three convolutional blocks leads to representational collapse. Open questions include how to combine bottom-up ED optimization with top-down signals for deeper networks, whether the approach transfers to larger-scale datasets, and how to implement the ED computation via local, biologically feasible plasticity rules (e.g., WTA plus inhibitory competition).
