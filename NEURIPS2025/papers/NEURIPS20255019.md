## Problem

Existing model-heterogeneous federated learning methods assume uniform client participation and overlook the fairness degradation caused by inconsistent participation probabilities in real deployments. Conversely, fair FL methods that address performance disparity typically operate under homogeneous model architectures and uniform participation, leaving the combined challenge of model heterogeneity and participation imbalance largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255019/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images (28×28 Fashion-MNIST or 32×32 CIFAR-10) and produces a class label via a weighted ensemble of its heterogeneous local CNN (one of GoogleNet, DenseNet-121, EfficientNet-B1, or ResNet-18) and a shared lightweight auxiliary GoogleNet model. The ensemble weight λ is a per-client trainable scalar optimized on a 10% adaptation subset each round, so the final prediction is argmax(λ·w(x) + (1−λ)·g(x)), blending local specialization with global knowledge.

## Method

PHP-FL introduces two complementary modules. The DEAL module performs dual-end alignment between each client's heterogeneous local model and a homogeneous auxiliary model: backbone representations are matched via an MMD loss with a Gaussian RBF kernel (using projection functions to handle dimension mismatches), while predictor outputs are aligned via KL divergence; a trainable ensemble weight λ is then optimized on a small adaptation set to combine both models for local training and inference. The ISPU module mitigates update conflicts from uneven participation by computing a per-client update ratio α via a sigmoid function of the client's cumulative participation fraction, selecting the top-α most important parameters by ℓ₁-norm to form a binary mask, and fusing only those masked parameters from the global auxiliary model into the local one—allowing infrequent clients to rapidly absorb global knowledge while frequent clients retain their learned representations.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under the heterogeneous setting, PHP-FL achieves 66.85% average accuracy (AM) and 8.07% fairness metric (FM, std of client accuracy) under the uniform participation pattern, improving over the strongest baseline FedAPEN by +6.55% AM and −1.38% FM. On Fashion-MNIST, PHP-FL reaches 97.64% AM and 4.15% FM (uniform), outperforming FedAPEN by +0.85% AM and −2.59% FM. Critically, PHP-FL maintains near-identical performance across all three participation patterns (uniform, normal, linear), with AM varying by less than 0.1% and FM by less than 0.1%, whereas baselines like FML degrade by up to 1.5% AM and 2% FM under non-uniform patterns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PHP-FL outperforms all six MH-FL baselines (FML, FedGen, FedKD, FedAPEN, FedTGP, FedMRL) on both AM and FM across all three participation patterns on both datasets. The strongest baseline, FedAPEN (KDD 2023), is surpassed by PHP-FL by +6.55% AM and −1.38% FM on CIFAR-10 (uniform), and by +0.85% AM and −2.59% FM on Fashion-MNIST (uniform). FML (arXiv 2020) and FedMRL (NeurIPS 2024) suffer the largest degradation under non-uniform participation, with FM increasing by 2–3% relative to the uniform setting, while PHP-FL's FM remains stable within 0.1%. In the homogeneous setting (Table 6), PHP-FL also beats the Fair-FL baselines FedFV (IJCAI 2021) and FedHEAL (CVPR 2024) on AM by +8.2% and +8.6% respectively, though FedHEAL achieves slightly lower FM (7.03% vs. 8.05%); PHP-FL also outperforms the client-unavailability methods FedAWE (NeurIPS 2024) and FedAU (ICLR 2024) on both AM (+5.2%) and FM (−1.3%). The ablation (Table 3) confirms that DEAL contributes the larger individual gain (e.g., +4.12% AM on CIFAR-10 over no-module), while ISPU adds a further +3.86% AM and −0.09% FM on top of DEAL.

## Relation to Prior Work

PHP-FL extends the auxiliary-model-plus-ensemble framework of FedAPEN (KDD 2023) by adding MMD-based backbone alignment alongside the existing logits-level KL alignment, creating a 'dual-end' knowledge transfer that the authors argue is missing in prior data-free MH-FL methods. It further departs from the client-unavailability literature (FedAWE, FedAU) by introducing the ISPU selective-update mechanism, which uses a sigmoid-scheduled, l1-norm-based parameter mask to let infrequent clients absorb more global knowledge while protecting frequent clients from straggler-induced conflicts. The paper positions itself at the intersection of three previously separate threads—model-heterogeneous FL, performance fairness, and inconsistent client participation—claiming to be the first to jointly address all three.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: real-world FL deployments (mobile, IoT, healthcare) inevitably exhibit non-uniform client availability, yet most MH-FL methods silently assume uniform participation, leading to biased models that favor frequently active clients. PHP-FL's near-constant accuracy across uniform, normal, and linear participation patterns (e.g., 97.64/97.59/97.58% on Fashion-MNIST) demonstrates that the fairness gap can be closed without sacrificing average performance. However, the evaluation is confined to two small-scale image datasets, four CNN architectures, and 20 clients, and the method incurs non-trivial communication overhead from uploading full auxiliary models plus binary masks each round. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to transformer-based backbones, larger client populations, and more complex participation dynamics (e.g., correlated or adversarial unavailability).
