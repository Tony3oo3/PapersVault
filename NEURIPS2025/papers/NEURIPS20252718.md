## Problem

Controllable image generation methods are built on image diffusion models that lack the continuous temporal and causal dynamics inherent in real-world scenes. Video generative models capture rich spatiotemporal priors (long-range context, non-rigid transformations, scene dynamics) that align closely with controllable generation goals, yet directly repurposing them for discrete image-to-image transitions is nontrivial because video models expect smooth inter-frame continuity while condition-target image pairs represent abrupt state changes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a condition image (canny edges, depth map, mask, blurred/grayscale image, or subject reference) at 512×512 resolution along with both a condition prompt and a target prompt, and produces a single 512×512 generated image. The condition image is encoded as the first latent frame of a 4-frame sequence (emulating ~37 pixel-space frames via Frame Skip Position Embedding), and the output is decoded from the last denoised latent frame after 50 sampling steps. For spatially-aligned tasks, the condition image is extracted directly from ground-truth images without a separate prompt; for subject-driven tasks, both condition and target prompts are provided to the MLLM text encoder.

## Method

DRA-Ctrl repurposes HunyuanVideo-I2V (a flow-matching DiT with 3D-VAE and full-attention) for controllable image generation via three key mechanisms. First, a mixup-based shot transition strategy constructs intermediate frames between condition and target images using temporal position-aware interpolation (F_α = ((1−β)F_0^γ + βF_1^γ)^(1/γ) with β = α²(3−2α), γ=2.2), bridging the gap between video models' smooth-transition prior and the discrete jump required by image generation. Second, Frame Skip Position Embedding (FSPE) scales temporal RoPE indices by a skip factor δ=12, allowing 4 latent frames to emulate a ~5-second video transition and drastically reducing compute. Third, a tailored attention masking matrix blocks cross-interactions between incompatible token sequences (e.g., condition image tokens × target prompt tokens) while an inference-time offset (ω=0.6) on the (T_I × T_P) region strengthens target-prompt guidance. Training uses LoRA (rank 16, scale 0 on generated-image tokens) with a loss reweighted by each frame's target-image content proportion, fine-tuned on ~160K spatially-aligned pairs or ~110K subject pairs.

## Results

On DreamBench (subject-driven generation), DRA-Ctrl achieves the highest VL Score (2.56), DINO (0.722), and CLIP-I (0.825) among all compared methods, with a competitive CLIP-T of 0.302. On COCO2017 (spatially-aligned generation), DRA-Ctrl attains the best controllability across nearly all sub-tasks: Canny F1=0.42, Depth MSE=76 (vs. 903 for OminiControl), Deblur MSE=11, and Mask MSE=16, while also achieving the best FID for deblurring (9.08), colorization (8.39), and mask (9.87). The method uses only 4 latent frames (vs. 37 for the I2V model) to achieve 90.4% faster generation than the full video model while maintaining the highest VL score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On subject-driven generation (DreamBench), DRA-Ctrl outperforms all 14 compared methods on VL Score (2.56 vs. 2.43 for UNO, 2.21 for OminiControl), DINO (0.722 vs. 0.702 for UniReal, 0.693 for OmniGen), and CLIP-I (0.825 vs. 0.821 for SSR-Encoder, 0.820 for FLUX.1 IP-Adapter). However, DRA-Ctrl trails UniReal (0.326) and UNO (0.315) on CLIP-T, attributed to the base model's use of LLaVA rather than CLIP for prompt understanding. On spatially-aligned generation (COCO2017), DRA-Ctrl dominates controllability metrics: it achieves the best Canny F1 (0.42 vs. 0.38 for OminiControl), Depth MSE (76 vs. 903 for OminiControl, 1092 for EasyControl), Deblur MSE (11 vs. 132 for OminiControl), and Mask MSE (16 vs. 6248 for OminiControl). For generation quality, DRA-Ctrl achieves the best FID on deblurring (9.08), colorization (8.39), and mask (9.87), but is slightly behind OminiControl on Canny FID (19.44 vs. 20.63) and Depth FID (20.83 vs. 20.39). The tuning-based baselines (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) are consistently outperformed on subject consistency, while the tuning-free full-attention methods (OminiControl, EasyControl, UNO, UniReal) represent the closest competitors, with DRA-Ctrl winning on holistic quality (VL Score) and subject fidelity but ceding prompt adherence to UniReal and UNO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of building controllable image generation on top of image-specific diffusion models (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, OminiControl, EasyControl) by instead repurposing a pretrained video generative model (HunyuanVideo-I2V) as the base architecture. It also distinguishes itself from prior video-model-for-image work (FramePainter, ObjectMover, Rotstein et al.), which targets pixel-aligned editing, by addressing holistic controllable generation including subject-driven synthesis, spatially-aligned translation, and style transfer. The key methodological contribution is a mixup-based shot-transition strategy that bridges the fundamental gap between video models' native continuous-frame dynamics and the discrete, abrupt condition-to-target transitions required by image tasks, complemented by Frame Skip Position Embedding and a tailored attention masking mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Dimension-Reduction Attack' framing raises a broader question about whether high-dimensional generative priors (temporal, causal, multi-view) systematically benefit lower-dimensional tasks, potentially reshaping how the community allocates training compute across modalities. However, the evidence is currently limited to a single base model (HunyuanVideo-I2V) and a single training recipe (LoRA rank 16), and the method incurs a ~2× inference cost over the I2V baseline while showing slightly worse FID/SSIM than image-specific approaches. Open questions include whether the transition strategy generalizes to other video architectures (Wan, CogVideoX), whether the efficiency gap can be closed with fewer transition frames or distillation, and whether the approach extends to 3D or multi-view generation where video priors might offer even greater advantage.
