## Problem

Self-supervised representations learned independently from 2D images and 3D point clouds capture complementary rather than redundant spatial information, yet no prior method jointly learns a unified representation space from both modalities. The paper asks whether a superior, modality-agnostic spatial representation can emerge from the interaction of 2D and 3D self-supervised learning, surpassing both individual models and their naive feature concatenation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252338/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes sparse 3D point clouds (processed by a PTv3-B encoder) paired with 2D RGB images at 518×518 resolution (processed by a frozen DINOv2 encoder), with camera intrinsics and extrinsics providing the geometric bridge between the two modalities. At inference, only point clouds are required; the learned features are modality-agnostic and support semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, and zero-shot language-based object localization via a linear projection into CLIP's text space. A video variant additionally ingests point clouds lifted from video frames via VGGT feed-forward reconstruction.

## Method

Concerto couples two self-supervised objectives on a shared PTv3 point cloud encoder: (a) intra-modal self-distillation using a momentum-updated teacher and online clustering loss (cross-entropy) at upcast level 2, inherited from Sonata, and (b) cross-modal joint embedding prediction in the JEPA sense, where the point encoder predicts image patch features from frozen DINOv2 using camera parameters as conditioning, with a cosine similarity loss at upcast level 3. For each image patch, the predicted feature is the mean of point features whose 3D-to-2D projection (verified by depth) falls within that patch. The image encoder remains frozen throughout training, so only the point cloud branch is optimized, and the two losses are balanced at a 2:2 weight ratio. Trained on 40k point clouds and 300k images, the resulting features exceed both Sonata and DINOv2 individually and their concatenation in linear probing, and achieve SOTA mIoU (80.7% on ScanNet) with full fine-tuning.

## Results

With full fine-tuning, Concerto achieves SOTA mIoU of 80.7% on ScanNet, 39.2% on ScanNet200, 50.7% on ScanNet++, and 77.4% on S3DIS Area 5. In the more challenging linear probing setting (frozen encoder, <0.2M learnable params), Concerto reaches 77.3% mIoU on ScanNet, outperforming Sonata (72.5%) by 4.8% and DINOv2 (63.1%) by 14.2%, and also surpassing the naive feature concatenation of Sonata×DINOv2 (75.9%) by 1.4%. With decoder probing (16.3M params, 13%), Concerto further improves to 79.5% mIoU on ScanNet and 37.8% on ScanNet200.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Concerto outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark and evaluation protocol. Against the strongest 3D self-supervised baseline Sonata [49], Concerto gains +1.3% mIoU on ScanNet (80.7 vs 79.4) and a substantial +2.4% on ScanNet200 (39.2 vs 36.8) with full fine-tuning; in linear probing the gap widens to +4.8% on ScanNet and +8.1% on ScanNet200. Against the 2D image SSL model DINOv2 [28], Concerto leads by +14.2% mIoU on ScanNet in linear probing (77.3 vs 63.1), confirming that 3D-aware joint learning far exceeds 2D-only features lifted into 3D. Critically, Concerto also surpasses the naive feature concatenation of Sonata×DINOv2 by +1.4% on ScanNet (77.3 vs 75.9), demonstrating that joint 2D-3D self-supervised learning produces emergent representations beyond what simple fusion can achieve. Among supervised baselines, Concerto beats PTv3 [47] by +3.1% and PPT [48] by +1.8% on ScanNet, and outperforms MSC [46] by +2.5% at the same 124.8M parameter scale. On the fine-grained ScanNet200 benchmark (200 classes), Concerto's advantage is most pronounced, exceeding the previous best (Sonata) by 2.4% in full fine-tuning and 8.1% in linear probing, indicating that cross-modal synergy is particularly beneficial for distinguishing many fine-grained object categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

Concerto builds directly on Sonata [49] for its intra-modal 3D self-distillation branch and incorporates DINOv2 [28] as a frozen 2D image encoder, following LeCun's JEPA [26] philosophy of cross-modal latent prediction. It departs from prior 2D-to-3D feature transfer methods (Lift3D, Clip2Scene, differentiable rendering approaches) by treating 2D self-supervised features not as supervision signals but as a joint prediction target within a unified self-supervised objective, so that the 3D encoder learns to internalize 2D semantic structure rather than merely imitate it. The architecture uses Point Transformer V3 [47] as the backbone, and the cross-modal correspondence mechanism relies on camera-parameter-based pixel-point projection with depth visibility checks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key finding that a single 3D model trained with joint 2D-3D self-supervised learning outperforms the naive concatenation of two independently trained SOTA models (DINOv2 + Sonata) suggests that multi-modal joint learning produces genuinely emergent representations beyond what either modality contributes in isolation. This opens a path toward unified spatial foundation models that do not require 2D features at inference time. Open questions highlighted by the authors include: unfreezing both encoders for true native multi-modal pretraining, moving from shallow linear language alignment to deep semantic grounding, and extending the paradigm to diverse point cloud domains (LiDAR, object-centric, dynamic egocentric). The video-lifted point cloud variant and LoRA fine-tuning results also point toward practical deployment in real-time spatial perception.
