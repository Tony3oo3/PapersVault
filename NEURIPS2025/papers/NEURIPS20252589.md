## Problem

Conventional multi-label image classification (MLIC) training pipelines rely on static hyperparameter configurations or heuristic staged scheduling, which cannot adapt to the non-stationary dynamics of label co-occurrence patterns, class difficulty, and feature-label mappings that evolve during training. This mismatch leads to training instability, premature convergence, and suboptimal performance, particularly for rare tail classes. The paper addresses the lack of fine-grained, real-time adaptive control over training components and their nonlinear inter-component interactions.

## Input / Output

At each decision step (per epoch or fixed interval), the system consumes a D-dimensional extended state vector aggregating current and historical observations: validation mAP, training/validation loss and their deltas, gradient L2-norms and relative update magnitudes, and dataset-specific descriptors such as average texture richness. The output is a joint discrete configuration tuple (augmentation policy, optimizer, LR scheduler, loss function) drawn from the Cartesian product of four predefined action spaces, which is immediately applied to the next training cycle of the underlying ResNet-101 MLIC model. The agents do not modify the low-level gradient update rule; they only select high-level training strategies.

## Method

MAT-Agent deploys four autonomous DQN-based agents (AUG, OPT, LRS, LOSS), each maintaining its own Q-network parameterized by θ_k, that jointly select a training configuration C_t from a large discrete space at every decision step. All agents share a unified global state representation I_t and a composite scalar reward R_{t+1} that balances mAP improvement (via a shaping function f(ΔmAP)), training stability (inverse loss fluctuation), convergence speed, and a penalty for unstable or expensive configurations. Each agent learns via ε-greedy exploration, experience replay, and target Q-networks, minimizing the TD error to approximate the Bellman optimality equation under the effective MDP induced by the other agents' policies. The closed-loop cycle (perceive → decide → execute → evaluate → learn) enables the agents to co-evolve their policies, dynamically adapting the training recipe to the evolving learning landscape rather than committing to a single static configuration. Theoretical analysis shows that when other agents' policies are temporarily fixed, each agent's learning reduces to standard single-agent Q-learning with local convergence guarantees, while the full multi-agent system exhibits non-stationary dynamics that the shared reward and experience replay help stabilize.

## Results

On Pascal VOC, MAT-Agent achieves mAP 97.4, OF1 92.3, and CF1 91.4, outperforming the closest competitor PAT-T (96.2 mAP, 91.1 OF1, 90.6 CF1) by 1.2 mAP points. On MS-COCO, it reaches mAP 92.8, OF1 88.2, and CF1 87.1, surpassing HSQ-CvN (92.0 mAP) and PAT-T (91.8 mAP). On the long-tailed VG-256 benchmark, MAT-Agent attains mAP 60.9, OF1 70.8, and CF1 61.1, leading PAT-T by 1.4 mAP and 1.0 OF1 points. MAT-Agent also converges 47% faster than standard training (47 vs. 80 epochs to reach 63.8% mAP on COCO) and shows a 2.5–3.0 point mAP lead over PBT, BOHB, and DARTS in cross-dataset transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAT-Agent outperforms all eight MLIC baselines (ML-GCN, C-Tran, BalanceMix, ASL, ML-Decoder, MLBOTE, HSQ-CvN, PAT-T) on every reported metric across all three primary datasets. The closest competitor is PAT-T, which MAT-Agent surpasses by 1.2 mAP on Pascal VOC (97.4 vs. 96.2), 1.0 mAP on COCO (92.8 vs. 91.8), and 1.4 mAP on VG-256 (60.9 vs. 59.5); HSQ-CvN is the second-closest on COCO (92.0 mAP) but is not evaluated on VG-256. In convergence analysis, MAT-Agent reaches 63.8% mAP in 47 epochs versus ~80 for standard training, and leads PBT by 2.2 points and BOHB by 1.5 points at the 47-epoch mark. In cross-dataset zero-shot transfer (MS-COCO → VOC/NUS-WIDE/OpenImages), MAT-Agent achieves 76.2/62.5/53.4 mAP, beating DARTS (73.8/59.7/50.8) by 2.4–2.8 points and PBT/BOHB by 2.5–3.0 points. In the GPU-hours vs. mAP comparison (Figure 4), MAT-Agent reaches ~62.5 mAP and ~40.3 Rare-F1 at 10² GPU hours, substantially ahead of grid search, random search, Bayesian optimization, BOHB, AutoLR, PBT, Auto-PyTorch, and DARTS, which have not yet converged in that budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes deep reinforcement learning (DQN with experience replay and target networks) from its traditional role in sequential decision-making to the novel application of dynamically selecting training configurations (augmentation, optimizer, learning rate schedule, loss function) during multi-label image classification. It departs from the dominant 'static configuration' paradigm in MLIC training and from offline AutoML approaches (AutoAugment, ENAS, DARTS) by integrating adaptation directly into the training loop as a closed-loop control problem. The multi-agent decomposition—assigning one DQN agent per training component with a shared composite reward—draws on multi-armed bandit and cooperative multi-agent RL literature, though the actual inter-agent interaction is limited to shared state and reward rather than explicit communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 47% reduction in training epochs to reach a target mAP is practically significant for resource-constrained settings, and the cross-dataset transfer of learned policies (2.5–3.0 point lead over PBT/BOHB/DARTS) suggests the meta-policies may generalize beyond a single dataset. However, the 'multi-agent' framing is somewhat overstated: agents share a global reward and state but lack explicit inter-agent communication, making credit assignment implicit and the coordination mechanism less principled than true MARL. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViT, Swin), whether the 10% per-epoch overhead remains acceptable at scale, and whether the learned policies transfer across fundamentally different architectures rather than just different datasets with the same ResNet-101 backbone.
