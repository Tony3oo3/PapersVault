## Problem

Behavior cloning for robot manipulation requires large-scale real-world demonstrations that are costly to collect, while simulation data is abundant but suffers from visual domain gaps that hinder real-world transfer. Existing sim-and-real co-training approaches lack explicit constraints to align learned feature spaces across domains, and marginal distribution matching (e.g., MMD) is insufficient for fine-grained manipulation because it does not preserve action-relevant geometric structure. This paper asks how to leverage abundant simulation data with only a few real-world demos to learn policies that generalize to real-world scenarios never seen during training.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes either RGB images (480×640, downsampled to 120×160, randomly center-cropped to 108×144) or 3D point clouds (2048 points extracted via FPS from a depth map projected into the robot base frame and cropped to a task-relevant bounding box), together with low-dimensional proprioceptive state (joint angles and gripper position). It outputs a 7-DOF target joint position and gripper action via a Diffusion Policy (ResNet-18 encoder for images) or 3D Diffusion Policy (PointNet encoder for point clouds). The observation encoder f_φ maps raw observations to a shared latent space Z, which is jointly optimized with the policy head under both behavior cloning and OT alignment objectives.

## Method

The core insight is that aligning the joint distributions of (visual latent feature, proprioceptive state) pairs across domains provides a richer alignment signal than matching marginal observation distributions alone, because it preserves the geometric relationships critical for action prediction. The paper formulates this as an Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) loss with entropic regularization (solved via Sinkhorn-Knopp) and KL-divergence penalties that relax strict marginal constraints, enabling selective alignment of the overlapping subset of states while down-weighting unmatched portions—critical given the severe data imbalance (N_src ≫ N_tgt). The ground cost combines distances in the learned latent space Z and the proprioceptive state space X (using proprioception as a cross-domain-consistent proxy for actions). A temporally-aware sampling strategy uses Dynamic Time Warping on proprioceptive sequences to match behaviorally similar source-target trajectory pairs before sampling transitions, ensuring mini-batches contain meaningfully corresponding state pairs. The encoder f_φ and policy π_θ are trained jointly, minimizing L_BC + λ·L_UOT, where the UOT term acts as a structure-preserving regularization that shapes the embedding space to be domain-invariant yet task-salient.

## Results

On sim-to-sim point cloud tasks (Table 3), the proposed method achieves an average in-distribution success rate of 0.68 and OOD success rate of 0.59, outperforming Co-training (0.62/0.47), MMD (0.46/0.40), and Target-only (0.38/0.00). In real-world in-distribution evaluation (Table 7), the method reaches 0.73 average full-task success for image-based policies and 0.77 for point cloud policies, compared to 0.53 and 0.50 for Co-training. Under real-world OOD conditions (Table 4), the method achieves 0.67 average success versus 0.50 for Co-training and 0.48 for Source-only, with the largest gains on BoxInBin (0.5 vs. 0.0 for Co-training under Reset OOD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: MMD (Tzeng et al., 2014), which aligns marginal feature distributions via Maximum Mean Discrepancy; Co-training (Wei et al., 2025), which jointly trains on mixed sim-and-real data without explicit alignment; and Co-training (Maddukuri et al., 2025), a similar mixed-training recipe. The proposed method consistently outperforms all baselines across every task and domain shift. Against MMD, the gap is largest in OOD settings (e.g., 0.59 vs. 0.40 average T-O on point cloud sim-to-sim; 0.36 vs. 0.30 on image sim-to-sim), confirming that marginal alignment alone is insufficient for task-relevant structure. Against Co-training, the method gains 6–12 percentage points in OOD success (e.g., 0.59 vs. 0.47 on point cloud sim-to-sim; 0.67 vs. 0.50 on real-world point cloud OOD), with the most dramatic improvement on real-world BoxInBin under Reset OOD (0.5 vs. 0.0). The method also surpasses Source-only by a wide margin in real-world settings (0.67 vs. 0.48 average), demonstrating that the OT-guided alignment enables effective exploitation of simulation data rather than simply ignoring the domain gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent sim-and-real co-training paradigm (Maddukuri et al. 2025; Wei et al. 2025), which showed that jointly training a single policy on mixed simulation and real data improves generalization, by adding an explicit feature-alignment objective. The alignment mechanism draws on the Optimal Transport literature for domain adaptation—particularly Joint Distribution OT (JDOT, Courty et al. 2017) and its deep variant DeepJDOT (Damodaran et al. 2018)—but repurposes it from unsupervised DA to a supervised co-training setting where action/proprioceptive labels from limited real demos guide the alignment. The Unbalanced OT formulation (Fatras et al. 2021; Chizat et al. 2018) is introduced to handle the inherent data imbalance between abundant simulation trajectories and scarce real-world demonstrations, and a temporally-aware DTW-based sampling strategy is added to improve mini-batch alignment quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a handful of real-world demonstrations (10–25) combined with abundant simulation data and OT-based feature alignment can yield up to 30% improvement in real-world success rates and enable generalization to scenarios seen only in simulation, offering a practical path toward scalable robot manipulation without expensive data collection. Key limitations include addressing only visual observation gaps (not dynamics), reliance on quasi-static prehensile tasks compatible with MimicGen, and the requirement for on-task real-world demonstrations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic manipulation, whether unstructured play data can replace on-task demos, and whether the OT alignment objective generalizes to multi-embodiment or long-horizon settings.
