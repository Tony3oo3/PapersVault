## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of self-attention dynamics (e.g., Geshkovski et al.) modeled tokens as interacting particles but relied on highly restrictive assumptions such as A = -I and W = I, limiting applicability to real pre-trained models. No prior work connected the convergence or divergence of token trajectories to downstream model performance, nor proposed architectural modifications grounded in these dynamical properties. This paper fills both gaps by deriving broader sufficient conditions on model parameters and empirically demonstrating that convergence to zero degrades performance while divergence is beneficial.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes the attention parameter matrices Q, K, V (with D_v = D so V is square) and positional encoding parameters from a pre-trained Transformer, and derives the matrix A = W(V^T)^{-1} where W = (1/√D_k)QK^T. The theoretical output is a characterization of token trajectories (convergence to zero, divergence to infinity, or clustering) based on the definiteness of A_sym and W_sym. The practical output is a modified query-key interaction matrix W_li = (1/√D_k)(QK^T + Q̄R_{θ,i-l}^D K̄^T + λI_D or λA) for rotary-encoding models, where λ is a learnable negative scalar that shifts the system away from the convergence regime.

## Method

The paper formulates the continuous-time limit of self-attention as an ODE system and proves that token distances are governed by the quadratic form q_A: if A_sym ≺ 0 tokens move closer, if A_sym ≻ 0 they move apart. It then establishes that when A ≺ 0 and W ≻ 0, all tokens converge to zero (generalizing the A = -I, W = I case from prior work), and when V has a positive eigenvalue with initial tokens on one side of the corresponding hyperplane, all tokens diverge to infinity. For positional encodings, it shows absolute encoding merely shifts the equilibrium (x_l → -p_l) without changing the dynamical regime, while rotary encoding introduces an additional rotation-dependent term ΔW_li = Q̄R_{θ,i-l}^D K̄^T that actively inhibits convergence. To exploit this, the authors add a learnable regularization term λI_D or λA (with λ < 0) to W_li in rotary-encoding Transformers, effectively subtracting a positive quantity from the diagonal to promote negative eigenvalues and discourage the convergence scenario, yielding consistent perplexity and BPC improvements on WikiText-103 and EnWik8.

## Results

On WikiText-103, constraining the attention parameters to the divergence regime (A_sym ≻ 0, W_sym ≻ 0) reduces validation perplexity to 31.12 and test perplexity to 32.07, outperforming the unconstrained baseline (31.63/32.37) by 0.51/0.30 points, while the convergence regime (A_sym ≺ 0, W_sym ≻ 0) degrades performance to 32.24/33.05. On EnWik8, the divergence setting achieves 1.324 BPC versus 1.331 (baseline) and 1.350 (convergence). On ImageNet-1K with DeiT, the divergence constraint improves top-1 accuracy to 71.96% (vs. 71.80% baseline and 71.34% convergence). The proposed RoPE modification (adding learnable λA to W_li) further reduces EnWik8 BPC from 1.295 to 1.281 and WikiText-103 test PPL from 32.35 to 32.04 over standard RoPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but instead contrasts four internal dynamical regimes (baseline, convergence, intermediate, divergence) by constraining the symmetric parts of A and W during training. The divergence regime consistently outperforms the baseline on all three benchmarks: −0.51 PPL on WikiText-103 validation, −0.007 BPC on EnWik8, and +0.16% top-1 on ImageNet-1K. Conversely, the convergence regime degrades performance by +0.61 PPL (WikiText-103), +0.019 BPC (EnWik8), and −0.46% top-1 (ImageNet-1K) relative to baseline, confirming the theoretical prediction that token collapse to zero is harmful. For the practical RoPE modification, adding a learnable λA (diagonal) to the query-key matrix W_li yields a 0.31-point PPL improvement over standard RoPE on WikiText-103 test and a 0.014 BPC reduction on EnWik8, while the simpler λI_D variant gives 0.26/0.007 respectively. The intermediate regime (mixed-sign A_sym) falls between convergence and divergence in all settings, supporting a monotonic relationship between token spread and model quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work modeling Transformers as interacting particle systems, building directly on Geshkovski et al. [17, 16] and Alcalde et al. [3], who showed tokens cluster in self-attention dynamics under restrictive parameter assumptions (e.g., A = -I_D, W = I_D). The key departure is relaxing these to general definiteness conditions on A_sym and W_sym, making the theory applicable to real pretrained models, and introducing the novel dimension of analyzing how absolute versus rotary positional encoding shifts the dynamical regime. The paper also bridges theory and practice by proposing concrete architectural modifications (adding λI_D or λA to the query-key matrix) motivated by the dynamical analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a dynamical-systems explanation for why rotary positional encoding (RoPE) tends to outperform sinusoidal encoding: RoPE's additional rotation term naturally promotes token divergence, which empirically correlates with better performance. This offers a principled design principle for attention mechanisms beyond ad-hoc architectural choices. However, the analysis is limited to the self-attention component in isolation (omitting LayerNorm and feed-forward networks in the theory), and the practical improvements from the proposed modifications are modest (≈0.3 PPL on WikiText-103, ≈0.01 BPC on EnWik8). Extending the framework to the full Transformer stack and validating at larger scale remain open questions.
