## Problem

Existing 3D scene synthesis methods rely on LLMs for spatial reasoning or image-based inpainting for visual priors, but LLMs produce incomplete or ambiguous spatial layouts due to limited 3D understanding, while image-based approaches suffer from viewpoint dependency and multi-view inconsistencies. Additionally, standard evaluation metrics based on single top-down views (CLIPScore, VQAScore) show near-zero correlation with human judgments, making it difficult to reliably assess scene quality.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a text prompt describing a room and its contents, an image prompt defining the initial field of view, or both as complementary modalities. The output is a metrically scaled 3D scene S = {o_1,...,o_N} where each object is parameterized by category, 3D size, 3D position, and orientation around the gravity axis, composed of high-quality 3D assets retrieved from Objaverse and refined to be collision-free. The video is sampled at 2 fps (~10 frames) for reconstruction, and the scene is rescaled to metric units using UniDepth monocular depth estimates.

## Method

VIPSCENE exploits the commonsense 3D spatial priors encoded in large-scale video diffusion models (Cosmos) by generating an egocentric video of the target scene, which naturally provides temporally coherent multi-view coverage that neither single images nor LLM text can. From the video, Fast3R performs feedforward multi-view 3D reconstruction to recover global geometry, and Grounded-SAM combined with MASt3R tracks 2D object detections across frames to assign consistent 3D object identities. An adaptive morphological erosion (scaled by object size) denoises the reconstructed point clouds before per-object extraction. For asset retrieval, PCA estimates object orientation, and ICP-based point cloud registration against candidate assets from Objaverse selects the best geometric match. A final gradient-based optimization with position, overlap, and boundary losses refines object placements to eliminate collisions while preserving the video-derived layout.

## Results

VIPSCENE outperforms both baselines across all First-Person View Score metrics (Gemini 2.0: 2.32 vs. 1.92/1.77; GPT-4o: 2.45 vs. 2.02/1.62; GPT 4.1: 2.43 vs. 1.94/1.76) and all user study criteria (PA: 2.52, LC: 2.51, OP: 2.39 on a 3-point scale). On top-down view metrics (CLIPScore, BLIPScore, VQAScore), differences between methods are minimal (e.g., CLIPScore: 29.98 vs. 29.17 vs. 29.95), which the authors argue reflects the inadequacy of single top-down views for evaluating 3D scene quality. The proposed FPVSCORE metric achieves the highest Kendall's τ correlation with human ratings (0.39) compared to CLIPScore (0.06), BLIPScore (0.07), VQAScore (0.13), and GPT-4o top-down (0.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIPSCENE is compared against two state-of-the-art 3D scene synthesis baselines: Holodeck (CVPR 2024), which uses LLM-based spatial reasoning with optimization, and Architect (NeurIPS 2025), which relies on diffusion-based 2D inpainting from single images. VIPSCENE clearly wins on all First-Person View Score metrics and user study criteria, with the largest margins over Architect (e.g., GPT-4o FPVSCORE: 2.45 vs. 1.62, a 0.83 gap) and consistent advantages over Holodeck (e.g., GPT-4o FPVSCORE: 2.45 vs. 2.02). On top-down view metrics, however, all three methods score nearly identically (CLIPScore within ~1 point), which the authors use to argue that top-down metrics fail to capture spatial incoherence and layout errors visible in first-person views. The user study (30 participants, 3-point scale) confirms VIPSCENE's superiority in prompt adherence (2.52 vs. 2.31/2.05), layout correctness (2.51 vs. 2.06/1.94), and overall preference (2.39 vs. 2.05/1.98). Notably, Architect underperforms Holodeck in this evaluation, contrasting with its reported results in the original paper, which the authors attribute to Architect's tendency to produce implausible object placements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LLM-based (Holodeck, LayoutGPT) and image-inpainting-based (Architect, Text2Room) paradigms for 3D scene synthesis by repurposing large-scale video diffusion models (Cosmos) as a source of commonsense 3D spatial priors. Rather than asking a language model to reason about object placements or iteratively inpainting multi-view images, VIPSCENE generates an egocentric video and then applies feedforward 3D reconstruction (Fast3R), open-vocabulary 2D perception (Grounded-SAM), and cross-frame tracking (MASt3R) to extract a structured scene. The evaluation contribution, FPVSCORE, also breaks from the field's reliance on single top-down-view metrics (CLIPScore, BLIPScore, VQAScore) by leveraging MLLM reasoning over 360° first-person view sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard top-down evaluation metrics show near-zero Kendall's τ correlation with human judgment (0.06–0.13) while first-person view MLLM scoring reaches 0.39 suggests the field has been optimizing against unreliable proxies, making FPVSCORE a practically important correction. The video-as-prior pipeline is a natural and extensible direction as video generation models improve, though current limitations include dependence on Objaverse asset quality (textures lack photorealism), indoor-only evaluation, and ~400s per-scene compute cost. Open questions include generalization to outdoor and large-scale scenes, integration with text-to-3D generation for novel assets, and whether the commonsense priors in video models can be further steered for architectural or robotic planning tasks.
