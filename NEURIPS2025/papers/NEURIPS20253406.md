## Problem

Existing decision-focused learning methods for marketing optimization (DHCL, DFCL) can only be trained on scarce RCT data, exacerbating the bias-variance dilemma by ignoring abundant but biased observational data. Additionally, they rely on dual decision losses that evaluate quality across all budgets rather than the specific real-world budget, and require manual hyperparameter tuning to balance prediction and decision objectives. The paper addresses both the prediction-decision misalignment and the bias-variance tradeoff simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two complementary data sources: RCT data (unbiased, ~2M samples) and OBS data (biased, ~20-34M samples), each containing individual features, assigned treatment index, and observed revenue-cost pairs. The Target Network outputs predicted revenue and cost for every individual-treatment pair, which are fed into a Lagrangian relaxation solver to produce the binary allocation policy z*. The Bridge Network outputs per-pair gating weights that blend the Target Network's predictions with a fixed Teacher Network's outputs to generate counterfactual pseudo-labels for the OBS data.

## Method

Bi-DFCL formulates a bi-level optimization where the upper level minimizes an unbiased decision loss on RCT data to train a Bridge Network, while the lower level minimizes a parameterized prediction loss on OBS data to train the Target Network. The Bridge Network produces gating coefficients that adaptively combine the Target Network with a frozen Teacher Network (pretrained on RCT) to create counterfactual pseudo-labels, dynamically correcting the learning direction from biased OBS data. Two surrogate decision losses handle the non-differentiability of the discrete MCKP: PPL uses a softmax relaxation of the Lagrangian relaxation solution, and PIFD uses black-box finite-difference perturbations with a PPL-aware acceleration. The Jacobian ∂θ*/∂φ is computed via implicit differentiation of the lower-level optimality condition, solved with conjugate gradient to avoid explicit Hessian inversion, enabling scalability to tens of millions of samples.

## Results

On CRITEO-UPLIFT v2 (Hybrid), Bi-DFCL-PIFD achieves 0.7812 AUCC (9.37% improvement over TSM-SL baseline), outperforming the best RCT+OBS baseline LTD-DR (0.7533) by 3.7%. On the two Meituan marketing datasets, Bi-DFCL-PPL/PIFD achieve 2.77%/2.97% EOM improvement on Marketing Data I and 2.52%/2.49% on Marketing Data II, surpassing the strongest baseline AutoDebias (1.75%/0.66%). In a four-week online A/B test with 790K shops, Bi-DFCL-PIFD delivers a 3.22% order lift over TSM-SL, compared to 1.80% for DFCL-PIFD and 0.91% for LTD-DR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bi-DFCL (both PPL and PIFD variants) wins on all three datasets against every baseline, with the largest margins over RCT-only end-to-end methods (DFCL-DIFD: +3.7% AUCC on CRITEO, +1.5% EOM on Mkt I) and RCT+OBS methods (LTD-DR: +3.7% AUCC, +1.3% EOM on Mkt I; AutoDebias: +3.2% AUCC, +1.2% EOM on Mkt I). Among RCT-only baselines, the proposed DFCL-PPL/PIFD (primal decision losses) outperform the dual-loss variants DFCL-DPL/DIFD by 0.2–0.5% EOM, confirming that primal formulation better targets specific budget constraints. Among OBS-only baselines, DragonNet is the strongest (0.7490 AUCC) but still trails Bi-DFCL by ~3% AUCC. In the online A/B test, Bi-DFCL-PIFD (3.22% order lift) beats DFCL-PIFD (1.80%) by 1.42 percentage points and LTD-DR (0.91%) by 2.31 points, demonstrating that the bi-level framework's ability to leverage abundant OBS data translates to meaningful real-world revenue gains beyond what RCT-only or reweighting-based approaches achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decision-Focused Learning (DFL) line of work for marketing, building directly on DHCL (AAAI 2023) and DFCL (KDD 2024), which introduced end-to-end training of uplift models with decision-aware losses. The key departure is a bi-level optimization framework that assigns the decision loss to RCT data (upper level) and the prediction loss to OBS data (lower level), connected through a Bridge Network that generates counterfactual pseudo-labels—something prior DFL methods could not do because they were restricted to RCT data alone. The paper also replaces the dual decision loss (which evaluates across all budgets) with primal decision losses (PPL, PIFD) that target a specific budget constraint, and solves the resulting bi-level problem via implicit differentiation with conjugate gradients rather than explicit unrolling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in industrial marketing: decision-focused methods that ignore abundant observational data suffer from high variance and poor generalization, while two-stage methods that use OBS data suffer from bias and prediction-decision misalignment. The bi-level framework with a Bridge Network offers a principled way to combine both data sources, and the deployment at Meituan with ~3% online revenue gains validates the approach at scale. Open questions include computational cost at even larger scales (the implicit differentiation step requires Hessian-vector products), sensitivity to the quality of the pretrained Teacher Network, and whether the strong ignorability assumption of RCT data holds in practice when treatment assignment is imperfect. Generalization beyond marketing resource allocation to other combinatorial decision problems remains unexplored.
