## Problem

Transforming static 3D models into animated assets requires expert-driven rigging (skeleton setup and skinning weight assignment) and animation, which remains a major bottleneck in content creation pipelines. Existing learning-based methods like RigNet struggle with complex mesh topologies and restrictive orientation assumptions, while MagicArticulate suffers from slow functional-diffusion inference and limited cross-dataset generalization. Critically, prior approaches address only the rigging stage, leaving animation as a separate manual task.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a raw 3D mesh as input, sampled as 8,192 surface points with normals and encoded into 257 shape tokens via a pre-trained shape encoder (Michelangelo). For skeleton generation, joint coordinates are discretized into a 128³ grid with parent indices appended, producing a compact 4j-token sequence. For animation, the rigged mesh is rendered as an initial frame and fed to a text-to-video model (Kling AI or JiMeng AI) with a text prompt to generate a ~50-frame reference video, which then guides the optimization. The final outputs are a skeleton (3D joint positions + bone connectivity), per-vertex skinning weights, and a temporally coherent animated mesh sequence.

## Method

Puppeteer integrates three components into a unified pipeline. For skeleton generation, an OPT-350M auto-regressive transformer uses a joint-based tokenization (4j tokens vs. 6b for bone-based, yielding shorter sequences) and hierarchical breadth-first ordering with annealed randomization to enhance bidirectional learning, trained with cross-entropy loss on the expanded 59.4k-model Articulation-XL2.0 dataset. For skinning weights, an attention-based network incorporates Topology-aware Joint Attention (TAJA), which augments self-attention with relative positional encodings derived from skeletal graph distances, combined with part-aware features from PartField and bone-based coordinates (parent + own position) to explicitly model inter-joint relationships. For animation, a parameter-free differentiable optimization jointly adjusts per-frame joint rotations and root motion, supervised by Pytorch3D rendering losses (RGB, mask, optical flow, depth) and Cotracker3-based 2D tracking losses with visibility-aware masking, producing stable animations in ~20 minutes on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On skeleton generation, Puppeteer (Ours*) achieves the best CD-J2J of 3.047×10⁻² on Articulation-XL2.0 and a substantial 2.483×10⁻² on the diverse-pose subset, outperforming the closest competitor MagicArticulate (3.172 and 4.525 respectively). For skinning weight prediction, Ours* reaches 87.9% precision and 0.333 L1-norm error on Articulation-XL2.0, compared to MagicArticulate's 74.6% and 0.451. On the diverse-pose benchmark, the gap widens further: Ours* achieves 86.4% precision vs. MagicArticulate's 74.9%, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen poses. Inference is 1.75×, 45×, and 59× faster than RigNet, MagicArticulate, and GVB respectively for skinning weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Puppeteer outperforms all seven baselines across every metric and dataset. Against the closest auto-regressive competitor MagicArticulate [67], the gains are consistent: ~4% lower CD-J2J on Articulation-XL2.0 (3.047 vs 3.172) and a dramatic 45% reduction on the diverse-pose subset (2.483 vs 4.525), confirming the value of pose augmentation. UniRig [100], the other concurrent auto-regressive method, is competitive on the diverse-pose set (3.252 vs 3.276 CD-J2J) but still trails Puppeteer on Articulation-XL2.0 and ModelsResource. RigNet [86] and Pinocchio [6] are substantially worse, with RigNet producing invalid skeletons on complex topologies and Pinocchio limited by its template-fitting approach. For skinning weights, Puppeteer surpasses MagicArticulate by 13.3 percentage points in precision on Articulation-XL2.0 (87.9% vs 74.6%) and by 11.5 points on diverse-pose (86.4% vs 74.9%), while also running 45× faster. In animation, Puppeteer produces artifact-free results whereas L4GM [59] exhibits geometric distortions and MotionDreamer [77] introduces unintended deformations in rigid parts.

## Relation to Prior Work

Puppeteer directly extends MagicArticulate [67], which first formulated skeleton generation as an autoregressive problem and introduced the Articulation-XL dataset. The key departures are: (1) replacing bone-based tokenization with a more compact joint-based scheme, (2) substituting functional diffusion for skinning weights with an attention-based architecture featuring topology-aware joint attention, and (3) expanding the dataset from 33k to 59.4k models with a diverse-pose subset. For animation, the paper departs from both learning-based 4D generation (L4GM, MotionDreamer) and prior video-guided SDS approaches (AKD) by proposing a parameter-free differentiable optimization that leverages off-the-shelf video generation models as motion guidance, building on the rigging output rather than requiring manual skeleton setup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified rigging-to-animation pipeline addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D content creation that prior work has treated as two separate manual tasks. The 59.4k-model Articulation-XL2.0 dataset is a significant community resource, and the 45–59× speedup in skinning prediction over prior methods makes large-scale rigging feasible. However, the animation quality is bounded by the fidelity of the external video generation model, and the optimization-based approach (20 min per clip) still requires per-object computation. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to very high-polygon meshes, whether the diverse-pose subset can be further expanded to cover more extreme articulations, and whether the video-guided optimization can be made more robust to identity drift in generated videos.
