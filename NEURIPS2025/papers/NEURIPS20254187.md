## Problem

Conventional diffusion-based guidance for 3D molecular generation suffers from fundamental limitations with hybrid modalities: gradient signals are ineffective or unstable for categorical atom types, and noisy intermediate states lack chemical validity for reliable property prediction. Additionally, existing SBDD evaluations rely on a single docking tool and a simplistic SA score, neglecting practical criteria like real retrosynthetic feasibility and target selectivity that are essential for viable drug candidates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target protein represented as a matrix of 3D atomic coordinates (R^{N_P×3}) and atom types (R^{N_P×K}), along with a desired property condition (e.g., binding affinity, SA score, or selectivity score), and produces a 3D ligand molecule as a set of atomic coordinates and categorical atom types. The generative backbone is a pretrained MolCRAFT BFN, and the property predictor is a separate SE(3)-invariant graph transformer (BNN) that outputs a Gaussian distribution over the target property, enabling uncertainty-aware guidance at sampling time without retraining the generative model.

## Method

CBYG reformulates the Bayesian Flow Network's iterative parameter update as a score-gradient operation via Tweedie's formula, revealing that the Bayesian update for both continuous (Gaussian) and discrete (categorical) variables naturally decomposes into an unconditional generation term plus a controllable guidance term (∇_x log p(1|x)). This allows gradient-based property guidance to be injected directly into the parameter-space update rather than the sample space, avoiding the instability of categorical gradient guidance in diffusion models. A Bayesian Neural Network property predictor provides uncertainty-quantified predictions (aleatoric + epistemic variance), which modulate the guidance strength to prevent overconfident steering toward unsupported regions. The framework inherits MolCRAFT's pretrained BFN backbone and uses a SE(3)-invariant graph transformer for property scoring, with guidance applied at sampling time via a guidance scale λ, enabling multi-property control (affinity, SA, selectivity) without retraining.

## Results

On CrossDocked2020 (100 test proteins, 100 molecules each), CBYG achieves the best binding affinity across all three docking tools: SMINA score −7.74 (vs. −6.03 for MolCRAFT), GNINA score −7.63 (vs. −6.85 for ALIDiff), and Vina score −8.60 (vs. −7.07 for ALIDiff). CBYG also leads in high-affinity rate (93.6% avg, 100% median), synthetic accessibility (SA 0.84 avg), and PoseBusters validity (94.9% avg), while trailing only in molecular diversity (0.61 avg vs. 0.73 for ALIDiff). On the AiZynthFinder retrosynthesis benchmark, CBYG achieves the highest Solved Routes (18.90) and Top Score (0.853), and on the kinase selectivity task it reaches a 78.3% success rate versus 68.6% for TargetOpt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CBYG outperforms all 10 baselines on every binding-affinity metric (SMINA, GNINA, Vina scores) and on high-affinity rate, SA, and PoseBusters validity. The largest margins are against generation-only models: CBYG's Vina score of −8.60 exceeds the best generation-only model (MolCRAFT, −6.61) by ~2.0 kcal/mol, and its high-affinity rate (93.6%) surpasses the best generation-only model (DecompDiff, 64.4%) by nearly 30 percentage points. Against optimization-based baselines, CBYG still leads: its Vina score (−8.60) beats ALIDiff (−7.07) and TargetOpt (−6.96), and its SA (0.84) exceeds TacoGFN (0.79) and RGA (0.71). The sole metric where CBYG trails is molecular diversity (0.61 avg), where ALIDiff (0.73) and MolCRAFT (0.70) are higher — a trade-off the authors attribute to conditional generation narrowing the output distribution. On the AiZynthFinder retrosynthesis benchmark, CBYG ties RGA on Solved rate (0.487) but leads on Solved Routes (18.90 vs. 11.43 for reference) and Top Score (0.853 vs. 0.826). On the kinase selectivity task, CBYG achieves a 78.3% success rate, outperforming TargetOpt (68.6%) and TargetDiff (58.2%), with the guidance injection contributing a ~16-point improvement over CBYG without guidance (62.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bayesian Flow Networks (BFN) from Graves et al. by proving that the Bayesian update rule for both continuous and categorical variables can be reformulated in score-gradient form via Tweedie's formula, thereby enabling gradient-based conditional guidance to be naturally embedded within BFN's parameter-space updates. It inherits the pretrained MolCRAFT BFN backbone for 3D molecular generation and adapts the TargetDiff graph-transformer architecture for the property predictor, while drawing on score-based guidance principles from Song et al. and Dhariwal & Nichol. The key departure from prior diffusion-based SBDD work (TargetDiff, DecompDiff, ALIDiff) is the argument that BFN's parameter-space formulation handles the hybrid continuous/categorical modality of 3D molecules more stably than diffusion's sample-space denoising, particularly for categorical atom-type guidance where argmax non-differentiability causes instability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in SBDD evaluation by demonstrating that the widely-used Synthetic Accessibility score poorly correlates with actual retrosynthetic feasibility (measured via AiZynthFinder MCTS), and by introducing a kinase selectivity benchmark that was previously absent from standard evaluations. The finding that roughly half of generated molecules from all tested models remain synthetically infeasible, even with high binding affinity, underscores that current generative models are not yet production-ready for drug discovery. Open questions include whether the BFN-guidance advantage persists at larger scale, whether the selectivity benchmark generalizes beyond kinases, and how to close the remaining synthetic-feasibility gap without sacrificing binding affinity.
