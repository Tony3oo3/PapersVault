## Problem

Secure aggregation of high-dimensional vectors (e.g., gradients in private federated learning) in the Prio two-server model requires communication linear in the dimensionality D, which is prohibitive for models with millions of parameters. Existing sparsity-based approaches using distributed point functions incur large overheads when the number of non-zero coordinates is in the tens of thousands, and naively reducing communication via random projections increases the sensitivity and thus the required privacy noise. The paper identifies block-sparsity as the missing abstraction that simultaneously enables efficient cryptographic aggregation and near-optimal privacy-utility trade-offs.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a D-dimensional vector (e.g., a gradient) and parameters block size B and sparsity k, dividing the vector into Δ = D/B blocks. The client randomly selects k blocks, rescales them by D/(kB) to produce an unbiased k-block-sparse vector, and communicates secret shares of only those k blocks (≈kB field elements plus O(kλ log(D/B)) overhead) to two servers. The servers collaboratively sum the recovered vectors and add Gaussian noise N(0, σ²I_D), producing a differentially private aggregate; the sparsity pattern remains hidden from each individual server.

## Method

PREAMBLE extends the tree-based DPF construction of Boyle et al. to handle k-block-sparse functions by modifying the tree so each leaf corresponds to an entire B-dimensional block, and by applying cuckoo hashing to reduce the number of correction words per layer to slightly more than k, cutting server computation to O(D) PRG evaluations in counter mode. Privacy is preserved through privacy amplification by sampling at the block level: because the sparsity pattern is hidden from each server, the sensitivity of the aggregate is governed by the per-block norm rather than the full vector norm, yielding asymptotically the same ε as the Gaussian mechanism when kB ≥ n/σ². Two subsampling schemes are provided—Partitioned Subsampling (structured, simpler to communicate) and Truncated Poisson Subsampling (more randomness, amenable to PRV accounting)—both achieving comparable utility and privacy bounds. Zero-knowledge proofs of validity (combining check-bit verification for k-block-sparsity with the PINE norm-bound proof) prevent malicious clients from corrupting the aggregate without changing asymptotic communication or runtime.

## Results

For an 8 million-dimensional vector (D=2^23) with 64-bit field size, PREAMBLE reduces per-client communication from 64 MB (full vector) to approximately 1 MB while increasing the noise standard deviation by only ~10% under (1, 10^{-6})-DP when aggregating n=10^5 vectors. In end-to-end private training experiments, PREAMBLE communicates roughly 2×10^4 parameters per client versus ~6×10^4 for the Gaussian mechanism on both MNIST (69,050 params) and CIFAR-10 (66,954 params), at a small accuracy cost. The privacy-utility trade-off (Fig. 4c) shows that for block sizes B ∈ [2^10, 2^13], the standard deviation of the aggregate error remains within a small constant factor of the Gaussian baseline across a wide range of communication budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard Gaussian mechanism applied to the full D-dimensional vector (i.e., sending all D coordinates with added Gaussian noise), which is the default in Prio-based deployments. PREAMBLE wins decisively on communication: it reduces per-client communication by roughly 64× (from 64 MB to ~1 MB for D=8M, 64-bit field) while incurring only a ~10% increase in noise standard deviation for (1, 10^{-6})-DP with 100K users. On the privacy-utility trade-off curve (Fig. 4c), PREAMBLE's error standard deviation stays nearly flat and close to the Gaussian baseline until the communication budget drops below a threshold, after which it rises sharply—indicating that the block-sparse approach essentially matches the Gaussian mechanism's accuracy for a large range of practical parameters. In the end-to-end DP-SGD experiments on MNIST and CIFAR-10 (Fig. 5b, 5c), PREAMBLE achieves comparable accuracy to the Gaussian mechanism while communicating only about one-third of the parameters (~2×10^4 vs. ~6×10^4), confirming that the communication savings come at a negligible utility cost. The paper does not compare against other sparse-vector DPF schemes (e.g., POPLAR, PBC-based constructions) in the main experiments, but notes in the related work that those approaches incur 2–3× overhead in large PRG evaluations or 2× communication overhead, which PREAMBLE avoids through its block-structured cuckoo-hashing design.

## Relation to Prior Work

PREAMBLE extends the Prio two-server secure aggregation framework by replacing full-vector communication with k-block-sparse DPFs, building directly on the tree-based DPF construction of Boyle et al. [BGI16] and the PINE norm-bound proofs of Rothblum et al. [ROCT24]. The key departure from prior work is identifying block-sparsity (rather than coordinate-level sparsity) as the structurally correct abstraction: prior DPF-based approaches for sparse vectors incur O(m·λ·log D) communication for m non-zeros, which is infeasible for the tens of thousands of non-zeros needed for good approximations. The paper further departs from random-projection-based communication reduction (which inflates sensitivity and thus privacy noise) by exploiting the hidden sparsity pattern to apply privacy amplification by sampling at the block level, recovering near-Gaussian-mechanism privacy bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For private federated learning with models in the millions-to-hundreds-of-millions of parameters, PREAMBLE reduces per-client communication from ~64 MB to ~1 MB (for 8M-dimensional, 64-bit-field vectors) while incurring only ~10% additional noise standard deviation at (1, 10⁻⁶)-DP — making large-model private FL practically feasible in the Prio deployment model. Open questions include tightening the numerical privacy gap for k-out-of-Δ sampling (conjectured to match Poisson sampling), eliminating the polynomially-small cuckoo-hashing failure probability without increasing server compute, and extending the analysis to more complex composition settings (e.g., multi-step DP-SGD). The block-level norm bound requirement (vs. per-coordinate) is a meaningful relaxation that may enable broader applicability.
