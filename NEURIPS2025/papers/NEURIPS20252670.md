## Problem

While discrete chain-of-thought (CoT) has well-established expressivity theory, the mechanism by which chain-of-continuous-thought (COCONUT) outperforms discrete CoT on reasoning tasks such as graph reachability remains theoretically unexplained. This paper asks: what structural property of continuous latent thoughts enables them to solve problems that require far more discrete decoding steps?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252670/cropped_page7_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a fixed-format token sequence: a list of (source, target, <e>) edge triples encoding a directed graph, followed by a question token, two candidate destination nodes, a reasoning token, and a root node. The model autoregressively generates C continuous d-dimensional thought vectors (appended directly to the sequence without token sampling) before an answer token triggers the final prediction of which candidate is reachable. Unlike discrete CoT where each intermediate step is a single sampled token, each continuous thought is a full embedding vector that can encode multiple nodes simultaneously.

## Method

The paper proves that a two-layer transformer with D steps of continuous CoT solves directed graph reachability for D-diameter graphs, versus O(n²) steps for the best known discrete CoT construction. The key mechanism is that each continuous thought is a normalized superposition of all vertices reachable within c steps, enabling implicit parallel breadth-first search: the second-layer attention head attends to all edges whose source appears in the current superposition, expanding the frontier by one hop per step. The first layer uses five 'attention chooser' heads (built on sinusoidal or RoPE positional encodings) to copy source and target node embeddings into buffer spaces on edge tokens, while MLP layers with thresholding nonlinearity filter noise and equalize superposition weights. Empirically, a 2-layer COCONUT model trained from scratch on ProsQA achieves near-perfect accuracy, outperforming a 12-layer discrete CoT model, and attention/representation analyses confirm that the superposition structure emerges automatically during training without explicit multi-path supervision.

## Results

On the ProsQA graph reachability benchmark (3–4 hop reasoning), a 2-layer transformer trained with COCONUT (continuous CoT) achieves near-perfect accuracy, while discrete CoT and No-CoT baselines (also 2-layer) solve only ~75% of tasks (random = 50%). Even scaling the discrete CoT baseline to 12 layers with 12 attention heads (CoT*) only reaches 83% accuracy. The theoretical construction further shows that D steps of continuous thought suffice for a D-diameter graph, versus O(n²) discrete CoT steps required by prior results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COCONUT (2-layer, 8 heads) decisively outperforms all discrete baselines on ProsQA. Against the same-architecture discrete CoT (2-layer, 8 heads), COCONUT improves accuracy from ~75% to near-perfect, a gap of roughly 24+ percentage points. Even when the discrete CoT baseline is scaled up 6× in depth (12 layers) and 1.5× in width (12 heads), it only reaches 83%, still well below COCONUT's performance. The No-CoT baseline performs at the same ~75% level as discrete CoT, suggesting that discrete intermediate tokens do not meaningfully help the 2-layer model on this task. The paper also introduces COCONUT-BFS, an ablation that replaces optimal-path supervision with uniform random frontier-node supervision; it matches the original COCONUT in accuracy, confirming that the superposition-based parallel search is not an artifact of curriculum design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical construction explaining why COCONUT (Hao et al., 2024) outperforms discrete chain-of-thought on graph reasoning, by showing that continuous thought vectors maintain superposition states encoding multiple search frontiers simultaneously. It departs from the transformer expressivity literature on discrete CoT (Merrill and Sabharwal, 2023a; Li et al., 2024), which established that O(n²) discrete steps are needed for graph reachability, by proving that only D continuous steps (where D is the graph diameter) suffice with a two-layer transformer. Unlike Gozeten et al. (2025), which studied continuous CoT for arithmetic with exponentially large embedding dimensions, this construction requires only linear embedding dimension and works with standard positional encodings (sinusoidal, RoPE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a key open question about the mechanism behind COCONUT's empirical advantage, showing that superposition states enabling implicit parallel BFS are the source of the efficiency gain over sequential discrete search. The empirical finding that such superpositional representations emerge automatically from standard training (even with only optimal-path supervision) without explicit multi-path guidance is notable and suggests the mechanism is robust. Open questions include deriving a matching lower bound on discrete CoT steps to establish a strict expressivity separation, understanding the training dynamics that produce exploration behavior, and extending the analysis to more general reasoning settings beyond graph reachability.
