## Problem

While parallelism has been shown to dramatically accelerate sampling for strongly log-concave distributions, the adaptive complexity (number of sequential rounds) of non-log-concave sampling remained completely unexplored. This paper initiates the study of parallel runtime complexity for minimizing relative Fisher information in the non-log-concave setting, establishing the first upper and lower bounds on the number of sequential rounds required.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252578/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives an initial point x⁰ drawn from a distribution μ₀ with KL(μ₀‖π_V) ≤ K₀, and queries an L-smooth gradient oracle ∇V in parallel batches of poly(d) points per round. It outputs a single sample x_t whose marginal distribution ρ achieves FI(ρ, π_V) ≤ ε². The complexity metric counts sequential rounds (adaptive complexity) rather than total oracle queries, with each round permitting up to O(L²dK₀/ε⁴) parallel gradient evaluations.

## Method

The algorithm combines averaged Langevin Monte Carlo (for initialization) with a diagonally parallelized Picard iteration (for refinement). First, averaged LMC runs for N = O(LK₀/ε²) time slices to produce initial grid values with bounded FI at all collocation points. Then, a diagonal-style Picard update propagates corrections across time slices in parallel: at each round j, all grids (n, m) with n + j = constant are updated simultaneously using the previous Picard iterate's gradients, rather than waiting for sequential time-slice completion. The key error analysis decomposes the Picard convergence error E_n^j into a geometric contraction along the diagonal (factor 0.5 per step) plus a bounded initialization term, yielding only O(log(Ld/ε²)) additional rounds beyond N. The lower bound of Ω(K₀/d) for ε = √(Ld) is proved by reducing non-log-concave sampling to finding stationary points in non-convex optimization, leveraging the known adaptive complexity lower bound for the latter.

## Results

The paper establishes the first upper and lower bounds on the adaptive (parallel) complexity of minimizing relative Fisher information for non-log-concave sampling. The proposed parallelized Picard algorithm (Theorem 3.1) achieves an adaptive complexity of O(LK₀/ε² + log(Ld/ε²)), improving over the sequential bound of O(L²dK₀/ε⁴) from Balasubramanian et al. When K₀ = O(d) (a common assumption), this reduces to Õ(Ld/ε²), a quadratic improvement in both L and ε. The paper further proves a matching lower bound of Ω(K₀/d) for the large-FI regime ε = √(Ld) (Theorem 4.1), establishing optimality of the parallel algorithm in that regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its parallelized algorithm against two key baselines. First, against the sequential averaged Langevin Monte Carlo of Balasubramanian et al. [BCE+22], the proposed method reduces the adaptive complexity from O(L²dK₀/ε⁴) to O(LK₀/ε² + log(Ld/ε²)), a quadratic improvement in both the smoothness parameter L and the accuracy ε (e.g., from O(L²d²/ε⁴) to Õ(Ld/ε²) when K₀ = O(d)). Second, against the query-complexity lower bound of Chewi et al. [CGLL23], which established Ω(K₀/d) for ε = √(Ld) with only 1 query per iteration, the paper proves a matching Ω(K₀/d) lower bound that holds even when poly(d) queries per iteration are allowed (Theorem 4.1). This means the parallel algorithm is provably optimal in the large-FI regime, and the sequential algorithm of Balasubramanian et al. is also adaptively optimal there. The paper also highlights a fundamental separation: while parallelism accelerates both log-concave and non-log-concave sampling, no analogous speedup exists for non-convex optimization, underscoring a key distinction between sampling and optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the relative Fisher information framework for non-log-concave sampling introduced by Balasubramanian et al. (2022) from sequential to parallel (adaptive) complexity, building on their averaged Langevin Monte Carlo analysis and the query complexity lower bounds of Chewi et al. (2023). The algorithmic core adapts the diagonal parallel Picard iteration developed by Zhou and Sugiyama (2024) for strongly log-concave sampling to the non-log-concave setting, where contraction properties of Langevin dynamics are unavailable. The lower bound is established via a reduction to the adaptive complexity of finding stationary points in non-convex optimization (Zhou et al., 2025), connecting two previously separate lines of analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first matching upper and lower bounds on adaptive complexity for non-log-concave sampling in a specific regime, and reveals a fundamental asymmetry: parallelism accelerates Langevin MCMC for both log-concave and non-log-concave targets, yet fails to accelerate gradient descent for non-convex optimization. This sampling-optimization separation is a conceptual contribution that reshapes how one thinks about the role of parallelism in probabilistic inference versus deterministic optimization. Key open questions include extending the lower bound beyond the ε = √(Ld) regime, characterizing whether functional inequalities (log-Sobolev, Poincaré) suffice for high-accuracy parallel sampling, and understanding the constant-dimensional case where gradient flow trapping might yield logarithmic-round samplers.
