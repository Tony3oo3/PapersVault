## Problem

Data attribution methods are highly sensitive to hyperparameters such as regularization and projection dimension, yet standard evaluation metrics (e.g., LDS, LOO correlation) require hundreds of model retrains on data subsets, making conventional hyperparameter tuning prohibitively expensive. This paper presents the first large-scale empirical study quantifying this sensitivity and identifies it as a critical, overlooked barrier to practical deployment of data attribution.

## Input / Output

The selection algorithm consumes the trained model parameters θ_S*, the empirical FIM F_S (or its projected version P^T F_S P), the gradient ∇_θ f(z', θ_S*) for each test example z' in a validation set T, and a discrete candidate set C of regularization values. It outputs a single scalar λ̂ chosen to minimize |ξ̄_{T,λ} − 0.5|, where ξ̄ is the mean surrogate indicator over T. No model retraining or subset sampling is required at any point; all quantities are computed from the single full-dataset model.

## Method

The paper derives a sufficient condition (Theorem 4.3) under which the Population Pearson LDS is increasing in λ, expressed as an inequality between two normalized 'angle' terms: one measuring alignment between the test gradient v_{z'} and the IFFIM update direction (F_S + λI)^{-1}v_{z'}, and another measuring alignment with the LOO-weighted ground-truth gradient g_{z'}. The right-hand side of this inequality defines a retraining-free surrogate indicator ξ_{z',λ} = t_{2,z',λ}/√(t_{3,z',λ}·t_{1,z',λ}), where t_k are bilinear forms of v_{z'} with powers of (F_S + λI)^{-k}F_S. The practical algorithm (Algorithm 1) simply selects the candidate λ whose mean ξ over a validation set is closest to 0.5, a threshold found to be uniformly effective across datasets, model scales, and projection dimensions. The approach is validated on IFFIM and TRAK attributors across six settings (logistic regression, MLP, ResNet-9, MusicTransformer) and generalizes to the projected variant without modification.

## Results

The hyperparameter sensitivity study confirms that most data attribution methods are highly sensitive to key hyperparameters: for example, increasing regularization from 1e-4 to 1e-2 for IF(CG) on MNIST+MLP yields a 3× increase in LDS. In the case study, the proposed surrogate-indicator-based algorithm for selecting λ (targeting ξ̄_{T,λ} = 0.5) generally achieves LDS values significantly better than λ=0 and close to the optimal λ across 6 experimental settings (LR/MLP on MNIST, ResNet-9 on CIFAR-2, MusicTransformer on MAESTRO). The method also generalizes from the IFFIM attributor to the TRAK attributor without modification. In downstream data-selection tasks, the selected λ produces visible model performance drops when the most influential training data are removed, confirming practical utility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's case study compares the proposed surrogate-indicator-based λ selection against two baselines: (1) no regularization (λ=0) and (2) a spectrum-based heuristic that sets λ to a fixed quantile (10%, 30%, 50%, 70%, or 90%) of the eigenvalues of the empirical FIM F_S. The proposed algorithm consistently outperforms λ=0 across all 6 settings for both IFFIM and TRAK, achieving LDS values close to the empirical optimum in most cases. In contrast, no single fixed quantile in the spectrum-based baseline performs well uniformly: the optimal quantile shifts across datasets, model architectures, and projection dimensions, making this naive heuristic unreliable. The proposed method's advantage is particularly pronounced at higher projection dimensions (e.g., 4096), where the FIM is closer to singular and the spectrum-based approach degrades more sharply. The paper also notes that the entanglement between regularization and projection-dimension (discovered in the sensitivity study) explains why TRAK's LDS counter-intuitively decreases with higher projection-dimension when regularization is not jointly tuned.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Influence Function framework of Koh and Liang (2017) and the FIM-based approximation of Grosse et al. (2023), extending them with a theoretical analysis of the regularization term λ and a retraining-free selection procedure. Prior work on hyperparameters in data attribution is fragmented and limited: Koh et al. (2019) examined λ in IF, Basu et al. (2021) studied architecture effects, and Klochkov and Liu (2024) used spectral properties for LiSSA, but none conducted a systematic large-scale study. The paper departs from existing benchmark studies (e.g., dattri, OpenDataVal) that largely adopt default hyperparameters from original papers without justification, by explicitly identifying hyperparameter tuning as a critical open challenge unique to data attribution due to the prohibitive cost of evaluation metrics requiring model retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental practical bottleneck: unlike standard ML where validation metrics are cheap, data attribution evaluation (LDS, LOO) requires hundreds of model retrains, making conventional hyperparameter search infeasible. The finding that regularization and projection dimension interact nontrivially—explaining why TRAK's LDS counter-intuitively decreases with higher projection dimension—resolves an open question in the TRAK paper and has direct practical implications for practitioners. However, the proposed surrogate indicator is limited to the regularization parameter in influence-function-based methods, and extending similar theoretical tools to other hyperparameters (projection dimension, training epoch) or to non-IF methods like TracIn remains open. The counter-intuitive result that larger training epochs can worsen LDS also raises unresolved questions about the relationship between model convergence and attribution quality.
