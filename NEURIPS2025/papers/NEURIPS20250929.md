## Problem

In unsupervised domain adaptation for person search, pseudo-labels generated by a source-biased model are inevitably contaminated with inaccurate bounding boxes, incorrect identities, and intra-scene identity conflicts, creating a negative feedback loop that degrades performance over training epochs. Prior methods also failed to exploit the scene prior—the inherent constraint that all persons within a single scene image must have distinct identities—leading to systematic labeling errors that compound across iterations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250929/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single query person crop and a set of gallery scene images (containing multiple persons per image) as RGB input, and produces per-person bounding boxes with associated identity embeddings in an end-to-end manner. The gallery size is variable: 50–4,000 images for CUHK-SYSU (default 100) and all test-set images for PRW. The architecture is based on SeqNet with a ConvNeXt-B backbone, a Faster-RCNN detection head, and a custom Attention Embedder that applies Batch Normalization and Dropout to multi-head attention outputs to produce discriminative person features.

## Method

STCSP introduces a three-pronged noise-elimination strategy. First, a Spatiotemporal Consensus pipeline maintains a memory bank of BBoxes and embeddings, applying temporal consensus (IoU-matched momentum updates) and spatial consensus (NMS for detection; dual-density DBSCAN with cluster consensus for re-ID) to filter unreliable pseudo-labels across epochs. Second, an Iterative Bilateral Extremum Matching (IBEM) algorithm performs GPU-accelerated approximate bipartite matching across all scene images to enforce intra-scene identity uniqueness, sparsifying the Re-Ranking distance matrix and preventing the same identity from being assigned to multiple persons in one scene. Third, a Scene Prior Contrastive Learning module combines a triplet loss (maximizing margin between a person's proxy and other same-scene persons) with a cross-entropy loss over a proxy table, directly teaching the model identity heterogeneity from the target domain. The full framework is trained with alternating source/target mini-batches, gradient reversal layers for domain alignment, and a Focal-Loss-based task cooperation module that supervises re-ID embeddings with detection foreground/background labels.

## Results

STCSP with ConvNeXt-B achieves 50.2% mAP and 87.3% top-1 accuracy on PRW, and 87.0% mAP and 87.9% top-1 accuracy on CUHK-SYSU, setting new state-of-the-art among all UDA person search methods. With ResNet-50, it reaches 42.4% mAP on PRW and 80.4% mAP on CUHK-SYSU. The method outperforms the previous best UDA method DDAM by 13.5% mAP on PRW and 7.5% mAP on CUHK-SYSU, and surpasses most fully supervised baselines on PRW despite using no target-domain labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among UDA methods, STCSP (ConvNeXt-B) decisively outperforms the previous best DDAM [1] by 13.5% mAP (50.2 vs 36.7) and 6.1% top-1 on PRW, and by 7.5% mAP (87.0 vs 79.5) and 6.6% top-1 on CUHK-SYSU. It also surpasses DAPS [11] by 15.5% mAP on PRW and 9.4% on CUHK-SYSU, and FOUS [4] by 14.8% and 8.3% respectively. Against fully supervised methods, STCSP (ConvNeXt-B) beats all ResNet-50-based supervised baselines on PRW (e.g., 50.2 vs 46.7 for SeqNet, 50.2 vs 44.0 for NAE+) but falls short of SEAS [9] (60.5 mAP with ConvNeXt-B, 52.0 with ResNet-50). On CUHK-SYSU, STCSP outperforms several supervised methods (OIM, IAN, NPSM, CTXG) but trails the strongest supervised baselines (QEEPS 88.9, NAE+ 92.1, SeqNet 93.8, SEAS 97.1). Among weakly supervised methods, STCSP dominates all baselines on both datasets, exceeding the best weakly supervised KCD [13] by 9.7% mAP on PRW and 0.2% on CUHK-SYSU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UDA person search line initiated by DAPS [11] and DDAM [1], which rely on pseudo-labeling with implicit domain alignment but suffer from noise accumulation in pseudo-labels. It builds on the SeqNet [12] end-to-end architecture and the BNR task-cooperation loss from SEAS [9], while introducing a Spatiotemporal Consensus pipeline (temporal memory-bank updates plus spatial NMS/DBSCAN consensus) to filter noisy pseudo-labels before training. A key conceptual departure is the exploitation of scene prior — the fact that persons within a single scene image must have distinct identities — enforced through a novel Iterative Bilateral Extremum Matching (IBEM) algorithm and a Scene Prior Contrastive Learning module, neither of which appears in prior UDA person search work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The noise-accumulation problem in pseudo-labels is a fundamental bottleneck for iterative UDA in person search, and the scene prior insight (intra-scene identity uniqueness) offers a principled, low-cost signal that prior methods overlooked. The IBEM algorithm, which replaces the O(n³) Hungarian solver with a GPU-accelerated iterative extremum search completing in seconds, is a practically reusable contribution for any task requiring large-scale bipartite matching. However, the framework is tested on only two benchmarks, the paper does not discuss limitations or report compute costs, and the many hyperparameters (θ, φ, ε, κ, α, M, δ, ϑ) raise questions about robustness across domains. Open questions include whether the scene prior generalizes to other multi-instance tasks and whether the STC pipeline's memory-bank approach scales to streaming or video settings.
