## Problem

Recent supervised semantic correspondence methods achieve strong accuracy on annotated keypoints but generalize poorly to unseen points, effectively reducing to sparse keypoint detectors. This limitation is masked by standard evaluation protocols that test on the same keypoints used for training, making it unclear how well these models learn true dense correspondences versus memorizing specific annotation locations.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a pair of RGB images depicting the same object category and produces dense per-pixel descriptors matched via cosine-distance nearest-neighbor search with a soft-argmax window. During training, it additionally consumes monocular depth maps (from MoGe), estimated camera intrinsics, and segmentation masks (from SAM) to construct posed 3D point clouds, but none of these are required at test time. The learned canonical keypoint set P and descriptors Z are category-specific, while the feature extractor Φ is shared across all 18 SPair-71k categories and generalizes to new ones.

## Method

The approach lifts 2D annotated keypoints into 3D via monocular depth backprojection, then aligns them across all training instances to a shared canonical keypoint set P using generalized Procrustes (Kabsch-Umeyama) with a scale constraint s≥1. Dense canonical coordinates for every pixel are computed as an attention-weighted interpolation over P, using the predicted descriptor as query and learnable keypoint descriptors Z as keys. A bidirectional local geometric alignment loss then enforces that small neighborhoods in the posed (depth-backprojected) and canonical point clouds are locally similar, using pseudo-geodesic surface sampling in the posed space to avoid selecting spurious neighbors across object deformations. This geometric loss is combined with a cross-entropy descriptor matching loss and the original Geo-SC objectives, all jointly optimized on top of frozen DINOv2 and Stable Diffusion backbones with additional bottleneck layers.

## Results

On the standard SPair-71k benchmark (seen keypoints), the proposed method achieves 82.9% per-image PCK@0.1_bbox, within 0.3% of its Geo-SC backbone (83.2%) and 2.7% below the AP-10K-pretrained Geo-SC† (85.6%). On the new SPair-U benchmark of unseen keypoints, the method achieves 62.4% PCK@0.1_bbox, outperforming Geo-SC (56.9%) by 5.5% and the best prior supervised model DINO+SD(S) (60.0%) by 2.4%. In cross-benchmark evaluation (Table 3), the method matches or exceeds Geo-SC on all four held-out datasets (SPair-U, AP-10K IS/CS/CF, PF-PASCAL) while Geo-SC degrades significantly outside its training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central finding is that supervised methods collapse on unseen keypoints: Geo-SC drops from 83.2% (SPair-71k) to 56.9% (SPair-U), a 26.3-point drop, while the proposed method drops only 20.5 points (82.9% → 62.4%). On SPair-U, the proposed method (62.4%) beats its Geo-SC backbone (56.9%) by 5.5%, the weakly-supervised SphericalMaps (61.0%) by 1.4%, and the supervised DINO+SD(S) (60.0%) by 2.4%. The older supervised baselines SCorrSan (32.7%), CATS++ (35.9%), and DHF (43.3%) are all outperformed by the unsupervised DINOv2+SD (59.4%), confirming the paper's thesis that these methods act as keypoint detectors rather than general correspondence learners. In cross-benchmark evaluation, the proposed method matches Geo-SC on SPair-71k (82.9 vs 83.2) but surpasses it on all four other benchmarks (e.g., 62.4 vs 56.9 on SPair-U; 71.3 vs 70.5 on AP-10K IS at PCK@0.10), while Geo-SC's advantage on SPair-71k vanishes entirely outside its training distribution. The AP-10K-pretrained variant (Ours†) further extends the lead to 66.1% on SPair-U, a 9.2-point margin over Geo-SC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Geo-SC [62] as its feature extraction backbone, adding a dense geometric alignment loss that lifts 2D keypoints into a canonical 3D space via monocular depth estimation (MoGe) and segmentation (SAM). It departs from prior supervised SC methods that rely solely on sparse keypoint contrastive supervision, and from geometry-aware unsupervised methods like Spherical Maps [32] and [46] that require spherical topology constraints or explicit camera viewpoint annotations. The key conceptual departure is using off-the-shelf depth and segmentation models to construct a continuous canonical manifold without any explicit 3D supervision, while also introducing SPair-U as a benchmark specifically designed to probe generalization beyond training keypoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a fundamental limitation in the supervised SC literature: models trained on sparse keypoint annotations effectively behave as keypoint detectors and fail to generalize to arbitrary points, a gap hidden by standard evaluation protocols that test on the same keypoints used for training. The SPair-U benchmark provides a practical tool for the community to assess this generalization gap, and the finding that unsupervised methods outperform supervised ones on unseen keypoints challenges the prevailing assumption that more supervision yields better correspondences. Open questions include whether the rigid transformation assumption and geometry-as-proxy-for-semantics hold for highly deformable or structurally diverse object categories, and whether the approach scales beyond the 18 SPair-71k categories to more complex real-world scenes.
