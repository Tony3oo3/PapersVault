## Problem

Crowd-sourced human preference data for LLM alignment contains substantial label noise (e.g., >25% in Anthropic HH-RLHF), yet no existing DPO variant satisfies the redescending property—meaning no method provably yields identical model parameters under heavy contamination. Prior robust DPO methods (R-DPO, Dr. DPO) only bound parameter estimation error in a way that degrades with noise fraction, and none can identify which specific data points are mislabelled without a clean validation set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254474/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a potentially contaminated preference dataset (prompt, preferred response, rejected response) along with a reference model π_ref and policy model π_θ, requiring no clean validation set. Beyond the aligned parameters, it produces a closed-form contamination ratio estimate ε̂ (Proposition 1) and a per-data-point clean-data likelihood score σ(g_θ*(s)) that ranks samples by their probability of being correctly labelled. The mislabel identification is gradient-free—simply sorting by the learned likelihood and taking the bottom |N·ε̂| samples—making it scalable without costly influence-function or Shapley-value computations.

## Method

Hölder-DPO replaces the KL divergence in the DPO objective with the Hölder divergence (specifically the density power divergence with γ>0), yielding a loss whose influence function contains a multiplicative weight σ(g_θ*(s_flip))^γ that drives the effect of extreme outliers to zero, satisfying the redescending property (Corollary 1). A model extension trick introduces a scaling parameter ξ into the target distribution, so that the optimised ξ* ≈ 1−ε provides a closed-form contamination ratio estimate without additional optimisation. The final training objective (Eq. 12) is a simple two-term empirical sum over σ(g_θ(s))^γ and σ(g_θ(s))^{1+γ}, serving as a direct plug-and-play replacement for the standard DPO loss. The aligned model σ(g_θ*(s)) then approximates the clean data distribution, enabling unsupervised mislabel detection by identifying low-likelihood samples as flipped labels.

## Results

On the OASST1 multilingual dialogue task (ε=0.4), Hölder-DPO achieves a GPT-4 win rate of 0.6314 on Mistral-8B and 0.6473 on NeMo-12B, outperforming the strongest baseline Dr. DPO (0.6058 and 0.6196) by 2.6 and 2.8 percentage points respectively. On the Golden HH helpful-assistant task, Hölder-DPO with Phi-2 is the only method to exceed a 50% GPT-4 win rate, slightly outperforming GPT-4-generated reference responses. On the controlled IMDb sentiment task, Hölder-DPO maintains near-constant average reward as contamination increases to ε=0.4, while all baselines degrade substantially. Applied to the raw Anthropic HH dataset, Hölder-DPO estimates ε≈0.25 contamination, and removing the identified mislabels improves GPT-4 win rates across all methods, including surpassing Hölder-DPO trained on the original noisy data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hölder-DPO outperforms all four baselines (DPO, R-DPO, Dr. DPO, and cDPO) across every experimental setting. On OASST1 at ε=0.4, it leads Dr. DPO (the strongest baseline) by 2.6 pp on Mistral-8B and 2.8 pp on NeMo-12B, and leads vanilla DPO by 5.1 pp and 4.5 pp respectively. On the Golden HH task, Hölder-DPO is the only method to surpass the 50% GPT-4 win rate threshold with the smaller Phi-2 model, while all baselines fall below it. On the controlled IMDb sentiment task, Hölder-DPO shows minimal reward degradation as ε increases from 0 to 0.4, whereas DPO, R-DPO, Dr. DPO, and cDPO all exhibit significant performance drops, with R-DPO and cDPO degrading most sharply. The paper also demonstrates that after using Hölder-DPO as a pre-filter to remove detected mislabels from Anthropic HH, even vanilla DPO and Dr. DPO trained on the cleaned data outperform Hölder-DPO trained on the original noisy data, confirming that the robustness advantage is most relevant under active contamination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust DPO line (R-DPO, Dr. DPO, C-DPO, IPO) by importing the redescending property and influence-function analysis from classical robust statistics (Hampel, Maronna) as the correct robustness criterion for preference alignment. It proves that all existing 'robust' DPO variants fail this criterion, then proposes Hölder-DPO—built on the density power divergence (Basu et al., 1998; Kanamori & Fujisawa, 2014)—as the first alignment loss satisfying it. The contamination-ratio estimation component adapts the model-extension technique of Kanamori & Fujisawa (2015) from density estimation to the Bradley-Terry preference setting, enabling unsupervised mislabel detection without a clean validation set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically grounded, gradient-free metric for valuing noisy human-feedback datasets, which is practically important given that widely used benchmarks like Anthropic HH contain ~25% label noise and manual verification is costly. Key limitations include the i.i.d. label-flip assumption (structured or topic-level noise is not handled), the sensitivity to the hyperparameter γ, and the coarse empirical approximation of the DP divergence. Open questions include extending the framework to non-i.i.d. and systematic annotator biases, dynamic γ scheduling, and whether the redescending criterion transfers to RLHF-based alignment methods beyond DPO.
