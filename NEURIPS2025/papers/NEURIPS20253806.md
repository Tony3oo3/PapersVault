## Problem

Mamba-based vision models (e.g., Vim) suffer drastic accuracy collapse—up to 38%—when token reduction techniques like pruning or merging are applied, far worse than Vision Transformers. The paper attributes this to Mamba's chain-like scanning mechanism, which creates rigid sequential dependencies that cascade into large-scale connection disruption when tokens are removed. The authors hypothesize that token connection survival rate is the critical factor and that diversifying scanning paths can mitigate this architectural fragility.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images resized to 224×224 for ImageNet-1K classification (1000 classes) or 512×512 for ADE20K semantic segmentation, producing a single class label or per-pixel mask respectively. A distinguishing aspect is that the architecture is explicitly designed to maintain accuracy under ToMe token merging applied at even-indexed blocks (12 layers total) with reduction ratios up to 0.46, where tokens are progressively pruned by [5, 8, 11, 14] per layer. The model is initialized from Vim pretrained weights and fine-tuned for 150 epochs with AdamW on 4× NVIDIA L40S GPUs.

## Method

AMVim extends Vim by replacing its symmetric dual-path scanning with a Window-based Multi-Scale State Space (WMS3) block that introduces asymmetric multi-scale scanning. One path retains Vim's global sequential scanning (D1), while the second path partitions tokens into non-overlapping 3×3 windows and scans vertically within each window while traversing horizontally across the full sequence (D2_D4), creating orthogonal dependencies that minimize redundant token connections. Each reordered sequence is independently encoded by a Conv1D + S6 selective state space module, and the two outputs are restored to spatial order and fused via a linear cross-merge. The key insight is that this asymmetry increases the token connection survival rate during pruning—diversifying which token pairs remain directly connected—while the window mechanism simultaneously captures local spatial dependencies that pure sequential scanning misses. The design achieves a 34% improvement in training-free pruned accuracy over Vim-T on ImageNet-1K with identical parameters and FLOPs, and only a 1.5% drop for AMVim-S, approaching ViT-level robustness.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224), AMVim-T achieves 76.3% top-1 accuracy and AMVim-S achieves 80.7%, both slightly exceeding their Vim counterparts (76.1% and 80.5%) at identical parameter counts and FLOPs. Under ToMe token reduction (ratio 0.17), AMVim-T retains 72.8% accuracy versus only 38.7% for ToMe-Vim-T — a 34.1-point improvement — while AMVim-S drops just 1.5 points to 79.2%. On ADE20K semantic segmentation with UperNet, AMVim-Ti reaches 40.2% mIoU (vs. 40.0% for Vim-Ti) and, under ToMe, achieves 27.0% mIoU versus 21.1% for ToMe-Vim-Ti, a 5.9-point gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Vim [6], AMVim wins in every setting: a marginal +0.2% in pruning-free accuracy and a dramatic +34.1% (Tiny) / +0.8% (Small) under ToMe token reduction, confirming that the architectural change, not the pruning strategy, drives robustness. Compared to Token Recognition [19] (a Mamba-specific pruning method), AMVim-ToMe (fine-tuned) outperforms by 3.7% on Tiny (75.3% vs. 71.3%) and 4.7% on Small (79.5% vs. 74.8%) at the same 0.17 reduction ratio. Against Hidden State Alignment [40], AMVim-T leads by 0.2% (75.3% vs. 75.1%) and AMVim-S by 0.7% (79.5% vs. 78.8%), while also reducing FLOPs slightly. Versus LocalVim [34], AMVim maintains roughly 30% higher accuracy across all reduction ratios and achieves approximately 2× the throughput, since LocalVim incurs heavier per-layer directional-change overhead. Overall, AMVim dominates all four baselines on both accuracy and efficiency under token reduction, with the largest margin over Vim and LocalVim.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Vim (Vision Mamba) by replacing its symmetric bidirectional scanning (D1-D2) with an asymmetric multi-scale design that integrates a window-aware scanning mechanism into one path while retaining global sequential scanning in the other. Conceptually, it departs from prior Mamba variants (LocalMamba, Multi-scale VMamba) that address local spatial awareness in isolation, instead unifying local window scanning with global scanning under an explicit 'token connection survival rate' framework. The paper also positions itself against specialized token-pruning methods for Mamba (Token Recognition, Hidden State Alignment), arguing that architectural robustness to token reduction is a more fundamental and generalizable solution than designing new pruning criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that asymmetric scanning paths—rather than the specific pruning algorithm—determine pruning robustness shifts the design philosophy for efficient SSM-based vision models from 'how to prune' to 'how to be prune-resistant,' which has broad implications for any token-reduction pipeline. The 34% accuracy improvement under token merging is well-evidenced across reduction ratios, model scales, pruning methods, and downstream tasks, making it a practically useful architectural modification. However, the base (pruning-free) performance gain is negligible (+0.2%), scalability beyond ImageNet-1K/ADE20K is unexplored, and the model is fine-tuned from Vim weights rather than trained from scratch, leaving open whether the advantage persists under full training or in video/multi-modal settings.
