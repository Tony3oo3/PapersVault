## Problem

Traditional probing classifiers for LLM hallucination detection operate on isolated layer-token pairs and are LLM-specific, making them unable to capture the full internal state or generalize across models. The paper demonstrates that the most predictive layer-token position varies substantially across tokens, layers, and even across different LLMs for the same dataset, rendering static single-position probes fundamentally limited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252865/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full activation tensor A ∈ R^(L_M × N × D_M) — hidden states across all layers and all generated output tokens — extracted in a single forward pass of the target LLM. A max-pooling step first reduces the two spatial dimensions (layers, tokens) to a fixed shape (L_p, N_p) = (8, 100) by default, handling variable sequence lengths and layer counts across models. The output is a single binary hallucination score per response, produced in approximately 10⁻⁵ seconds at inference time.

## Method

ACT-ViT exploits the structural analogy between activation tensors and images (layers × tokens as spatial dimensions, hidden dimension as channels) to apply Vision Transformer techniques to LLM internals. The pipeline consists of three components: (1) max-pooling over spatial dimensions to a fixed size, (2) a per-LLM Linear Adapter that projects the feature dimension to a shared size D′, aligning representation spaces across models, and (3) a shared ViT backbone that processes the resulting tensor as a sequence of non-overlapping patches with per-patch positional encodings. All components are jointly trained in a supervised fashion on activation tensors from multiple LLMs and datasets simultaneously. For adaptation to an unseen LLM, only the lightweight Linear Adapter is trained from scratch while the ViT backbone remains frozen, enabling fast transfer with minimal data.

## Results

ACT-ViT achieves AUC scores ranging from 70.23 to 97.03 across 15 LLM-dataset combinations (3 LLMs × 5 datasets), consistently outperforming the best prior baseline (Probe[*]) by +1.20 to +7.05 AUC points on 14 of 15 settings. Inference runs in ≈10⁻⁵ seconds per instance, and full training on all 15 combinations completes in under 3 hours on a single NVIDIA L-40 GPU. In the zero-shot leave-one-dataset-out setting, ACT-ViT outperforms the best training-free probability baseline in 13/15 cases, with a peak gain of +37 AUC points on IMDB/Mis-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against probing classifiers (Probe[*], the best static layer-token logistic regression probe), ACT-ViT wins on 14 of 15 LLM-dataset combinations, with the sole exception being IMDB on Mis-7B where it trails by 0.58 AUC points; the largest margin is +7.05 AUC on Qwen-7B HotpotQA. Against probability/logits-based methods (mean, max, min aggregation of token probabilities), ACT-ViT dominates across all 15 settings, typically by 15–30 AUC points, confirming that internal activation structure far exceeds surface-level confidence signals. Against LOS-Net (a learnable method on output probabilities), ACT-ViT outperforms on all 9 reported combinations (e.g., 84.33 vs. 72.92 on Mis-7B HotpotQA). The ablation comparing ACT-ViT to ACT-MLP (same pooling + linear adapter but MLP instead of ViT backbone) shows the Vision Transformer inductive bias is critical: ACT-MLP is often outperformed even by the simple Probe[*] baseline, while ACT-ViT consistently leads. Multi-LLM joint training (ACT-ViT vs. ACT-ViT(s)) yields gains in 12 of 15 cases, demonstrating that hallucination signals are partially shared across models and that the linear adapter effectively bridges representation spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant probing-classifier paradigm for hallucination detection (Orgad et al. 2024; Azaria & Mitchell 2023), which operates on isolated layer-token pairs, by treating the full activation tensor (layers × tokens × hidden dim) as a structured 2D object analogous to an image. It draws on Vision Transformer architectures (Dosovitskiy et al. 2020) for processing this tensor and on recent representation-alignment literature (Huh et al. 2024; Moschella et al. 2022) to motivate per-LLM linear adapters that project heterogeneous hidden spaces into a shared representation. The method also builds on the authors' own prior work on LLM output signatures (LOS-Net, Bar-Shalom et al. 2025), extending from probability-based detection to internal-activation-based detection with a learnable architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACT-ViT offers a practical, extremely fast (~10⁻⁵ s/inference) white-box hallucination detector that generalizes across LLMs and datasets, reducing the need to annotate new datasets for every new model or task. The zero-shot transfer to unseen datasets and the ability to adapt to a new LLM by training only a lightweight linear adapter are practically valuable for deployment pipelines. Key limitations include the lossy max-pooling step that may discard informative activation signals, and the evaluation is restricted to three 7–8B parameter LLMs and five datasets, leaving open whether the approach scales to much larger models or more diverse task distributions. Future directions include more sophisticated AT processing without aggressive pooling and shared adapters that exploit permutation symmetries across LLMs.
