## Problem

Existing two-stage dexterous grasping methods assume access to complete object geometry for optimization, which is unavailable in cluttered scenes due to occlusion and object stacking. One-stage end-to-end methods struggle with the non-linearity of mapping from partial point cloud observations to high-DoF grasp poses and the sensitivity of physical constraints to small pose errors. The paper addresses this by introducing a scene-decoupled, contact- and collision-aware intermediate representation that enables stable, collision-free grasp pose optimization without requiring full scene geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251003/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view point cloud of a cluttered scene (N×3 points) and produces a set of dexterous grasp poses, each defined as a wrist pose in SE(3) plus a joint configuration vector. The intermediate sparse IBS is a 40×40×40×3 voxel volume defined in a canonical space centered at the grasp seed point with the wrist rotation as direction, encoding three channels: IBS surface occupancy, thumb-object contact, and other-finger-object contact. The IBS volume spans 0.2m×0.2m×0.2m, compactly enclosing the hand-scene interaction region.

## Method

CADGrasp is a two-stage framework. In Stage 1, a wrist pose is estimated via ResUNet14 point features and a denoising diffusion model, then an occupancy-diffusion model (3D UNet backbone with 4 levels at 40³/20³/10³/5³ resolutions) generates multiple sparse IBS candidates conditioned on the canonicalized voxelized point cloud through voxel-level feature concatenation at each UNet level; candidates are ranked by force closure score. In Stage 2, a gradient-based optimizer minimizes a composite energy function with four terms—joint limits, self-penetration, IBS-surface contact (using IBS normals to enforce finger-object contact), and collision avoidance (enforcing hand points stay within the IBS safety boundary)—across multiple initialization trials, selecting the pose with minimal residual. The key insight is that the sparse IBS is scene-decoupled (it encodes the hand-scene relationship without requiring full scene geometry) and embodiment-agnostic, enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen hands like Allegro.

## Results

On the GraspNet-1Billion dense scenes, CADGrasp achieves 86.5% success rate, outperforming DexGraspNet2.0 (83.3%) by 3.2 points, and the best regression-based baseline ISAGrasp (63.4%) by 23.1 points. On ShapeNet dense scenes, CADGrasp scores 79.3%, slightly below DexGraspNet2.0 (81.5%) but well ahead of all other baselines. In real-world experiments across 5 cluttered scenes with 30 objects, CADGrasp achieves 93.8% success rate (30/32) versus 83.9% (26/31) for DexGraspNet2.0. The method also demonstrates cross-embodiment generalization, achieving 77.6% on GraspNet dense scenes with an unseen Allegro hand (zero-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADGrasp outperforms DexGraspNet2.0 (the strongest baseline) on GraspNet-1Billion across all three scene densities (86.5/85.5/80.1 vs. 83.3/79.5/73.9), with the largest gap on loose scenes (+6.2 points), but slightly trails on ShapeNet dense scenes (79.3 vs. 81.5). Against regression-based baselines, CADGrasp leads by a wide margin: HGC-Net (46.0% dense) and ISAGrasp (63.4% dense) are 23+ points behind on GraspNet dense scenes. GraspTTA, adapted for cluttered scenes without its optimization stage, scores 62.5% on GraspNet dense, well below CADGrasp. In real-world testing, CADGrasp achieves 93.8% success rate versus 83.9% for DexGraspNet2.0, with the advantage most pronounced on small and flat objects. The zero-shot cross-embodiment result (Allegro hand at 77.6% dense) has no direct baseline comparison since other methods do not support cross-hand transfer, but it retains ~90% of the Leap Hand performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage dexterous grasping paradigm (contact map prediction + optimization, as in GraspTTA and GenDexGrasp) to cluttered scenes by replacing the contact map with a scene-decoupled sparse IBS representation that requires only partial observations. The IBS concept originates from protein interaction geometry (Kim et al., 2006) and was previously used as an observation representation for dexterous manipulation (She et al., 2022); here it is repurposed as an optimization target. The generative backbone is an occupancy-diffusion model (Zheng et al., 2023) with voxel-level conditional guidance, and the wrist pose estimation module is directly inherited from DexGraspNet 2.0 (Zhang et al., 2024), which also provides the evaluation protocol and datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is enabling two-stage, collision-aware dexterous grasping under partial observations in cluttered scenes—a setting where prior two-stage methods fail because they assume complete object geometry. The zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer (Leap → Allegro) demonstrates that the IBS representation is genuinely hand-agnostic, which could reduce the cost of deploying grasping policies across different robot hands. Open questions include whether the 6.51 s inference time can be reduced to real-time via faster samplers (DDIM, consistency models), whether the approach scales to even denser or more geometrically complex scenes, and whether the representation generalizes beyond grasping to other manipulation tasks.
