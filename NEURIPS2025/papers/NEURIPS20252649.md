## Problem

VOneNets improve CNN robustness by prepending a fixed-weight V1-simulating front-end, but they omit the subcortical stages (retina and LGN) that precede V1 in the primate visual pathway. This paper asks whether explicitly modeling subcortical processing upstream of the VOneBlock yields additional gains in both biological alignment and perturbation robustness, without any task-specific optimization of the front-end.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252649/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images over a 7° field of view (reduced from the original VOneBlock's 8°) and produces ImageNet-1k class labels. The front-end operates in a spike-count formulation with a 50 ms integration window, and the Gabor filter bank is extended to higher spatial frequencies (0.5–8.0 cpd vs. 0.5–5.6 cpd) to better match primate V1 tuning. Beyond classification, the paper reports aggregate robustness scores (averaging adversarial, corruption, and domain-shift accuracies) and BrainScore V1 alignment metrics (neural predictivity and 22 response-property scores).

## Method

EVNets prepend a two-stage fixed-weight front-end to a standard CNN (ResNet50, EfficientNet-B0, or CORnet-Z): a novel SubcorticalBlock followed by a modified VOneBlock. The SubcorticalBlock implements parallel parvocellular (P) and magnocellular (M) pathways, each comprising light adaptation (global luminance normalization), a Difference-of-Gaussian convolution with color-opponent channels for P cells (red-green, green-red, blue-yellow) and an achromatic channel for M cells, divisive contrast normalization with a Gaussian pooling window, and a sub-Poisson noise generator. DoG filter parameters are set via Bayesian optimization to minimize the log-ratio loss between model F1 responses and empirical LGN population distributions across six response properties (center/surround radii, excitation/inhibition radii, suppression and saturation indices). The push-pull pattern is handled analytically by exploiting the antisymmetry of the contrast-normalization nonlinearity, avoiding explicit rectification. The entire front-end is fixed-weight and not learned; only the CNN back-end is trained end-to-end on ImageNet-1k, and the paper shows that this biologically constrained assembly improves V1 response-property alignment, shape bias, and robustness across adversarial attacks, corruptions, and domain shifts without any explicit optimization for those objectives.

## Results

EVResNet50 achieves a Robustness Score of 36.1%, improving 9.3% over the base ResNet50 (26.8%) and 1.6% over VOneResNet50 (34.5%), while incurring a 5.0-point drop in clean ImageNet accuracy (70.4% vs. 75.4%). On V1 alignment, EVResNet50 attains the highest mean V1 response property score (0.826) and shape bias (48.9%), surpassing VOneResNet50 (0.754; 31.6%) and ResNet50 (0.637; 18.8%). When combined with PRIME data augmentation, EVResNet50+PRIME reaches a Robustness Score of 42.7%, exceeding VOneResNet50+PRIME (42.1%) by 6.2% and ResNet50+PRIME (36.5%) by 17.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the plain ResNet50 baseline, EVResNet50 improves the aggregate Robustness Score by 9.3 points (36.1% vs. 26.8%) and adversarial accuracy by 20.0 points (28.3% vs. 8.3%), at the cost of a 5.0-point clean accuracy drop. Compared to VOneResNet50, EVResNet50 gains 1.6 points on Robustness Score, 1.5 points on corruption accuracy, and 1.0 point on domain shift, while VOneResNet50 retains a slight edge on V1 neural predictivity (0.375 vs. 0.364). Against ResNet50+PRIME (36.5%) and ResNet50+AT_L∞ (34.4%), EVResNet50+PRIME (42.7%) achieves the highest Robustness Score, demonstrating that architectural priors and data augmentation are complementary rather than redundant. On backend generalization, EVEfficientNet-B0 outperforms EfficientNet-B0 by 3.8 points on mean corruption accuracy (34.1% vs. 30.3%) and 4.6 points on domain shift (33.5% vs. 28.9%), while EVCORnet-Z improves over CORnet-Z by 3.3 points on corruption (21.3% vs. 18.0%) and 1.6 points on domain shift (21.1% vs. 19.5%), confirming that the SubcorticalBlock+VOneBlock front-end transfers across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VOneNet framework (Dapello et al., NeurIPS 2020), which prepended a fixed-weight V1-simulating Gabor filter bank (VOneBlock) to standard CNNs to improve robustness. The key conceptual departure is that VOneNets abstracted away subcortical processing (retina, LGN), whereas EVNets introduce a SubcorticalBlock upstream of the VOneBlock, instantiated from Difference-of-Gaussian models, divisive normalization, and light-adaptation mechanisms drawn from decades of neurophysiology (Rodieck, Carandini & Heeger, Mante et al.). The resulting cascading linear-nonlinear architecture is parameterized via Bayesian optimization to match empirical LGN response properties, and is evaluated using the BrainScore platform alongside standard robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-C, PGD adversarial attacks, domain-shift datasets). The paper also positions itself relative to PRIME data augmentation and adversarial training as complementary rather than competing robustness strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides evidence that explicitly modeling subcortical stages (not just V1) yields measurable gains in both biological alignment (V1 response properties, shape bias) and perturbation robustness, suggesting that the full early visual pathway matters for building brain-like and robust vision models. A notable practical finding is that neuro-inspired architectural priors and SOTA data augmentation (PRIME) provide additive, non-redundant robustness gains, offering a concrete recipe for practitioners. Open questions include whether V1-level divisive normalization or recurrent/feedback mechanisms would further close the alignment gap, whether task-driven fine-tuning of the fixed front-ends helps, and whether the modest clean-accuracy cost (~2–5%) is acceptable in deployment. The finding that removing the VOneBlock barely degrades corruption robustness raises questions about which specific V1 computations are actually driving the robustness gains.
