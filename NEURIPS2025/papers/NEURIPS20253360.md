## Problem

Pairwise dense matching methods like DUSt3R predict pointmaps from only two images at a time, leading to imprecise geometry (especially in short-baseline cases) and mutually inconsistent pointmaps for the same reference view when paired with different source views. These inconsistencies persist through global alignment and are further exacerbated by the model's limited generalization to unseen scenes, resulting in degraded 3D reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253360/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images of a scene, organized into triplets of one reference view and two source views. It produces 3D pointmaps (per-pixel 3D coordinates in the reference view's coordinate frame) and depth maps for each view. Unlike standard DUSt3R inference, Test3R requires at least three views per scene to form a triplet, and the optimization uses only the unlabeled test images without any ground-truth depth or camera parameters.

## Method

Test3R exploits cross-pair consistency as a self-supervised signal: for a triplet (I_ref, I_src1, I_src2), DUSt3R independently predicts two pointmaps of the reference view (X_1 from the first pair, X_2 from the second pair), and the test-time objective minimizes their L1 distance ‖X_1 − X_2‖_1. To optimize without overfitting on limited test data, the method freezes the entire DUSt3R backbone and inserts a small set of learnable visual prompt tokens into each ViT encoder layer (Visual Prompt Tuning), so only the prompts are updated via gradient descent at test time. This layer-specific prompt design accounts for varying feature distributions across encoder depths, and the small parameter footprint keeps the adaptation nearly cost-free while preserving the reconstruction knowledge from large-scale pretraining.

## Results

On 7Scenes, Test3R achieves a mean Accuracy of 0.105 and Completion of 0.136, outperforming vanilla DUSt3R (0.146 / 0.181) and CUT3R (0.126 / 0.154), while also attaining the best Normal Consistency (0.746). On NRGBD, Test3R reaches 0.083 Acc and 0.079 Comp, again surpassing DUSt3R (0.144 / 0.154) and CUT3R (0.099 / 0.076). For multi-view depth estimation, Test3R reduces the Absolute Relative Error to 2.0 on DTU and 3.2 on ETH3D (average 2.6), with an Inlier Ratio of 79.1%, outperforming DUSt3R (3.3 / 3.3, 71.5%) and surpassing camera-parameter-based methods like Robust MVD Baseline (5.9 rel, 62.3% τ) and COLMAP (8.6 rel, 75.8% τ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Test3R consistently outperforms vanilla DUSt3R on Accuracy and Completion across both 7Scenes and NRGBD (e.g., 0.105 vs 0.146 Acc on 7Scenes), while matching or slightly exceeding DUSt3R on Normal Consistency (0.746 vs 0.736 on 7Scenes; 0.870 vs 0.871 on NRGBD). Against CUT3R, the current state-of-the-art in 3D reconstruction, Test3R achieves lower Acc and Comp on both datasets (0.105 vs 0.126 on 7Scenes; 0.083 vs 0.099 on NRGBD) and higher NC on 7Scenes (0.746 vs 0.727). Test3R also surpasses MAST3R on Acc and Comp for 7Scenes (0.105 vs 0.189) and NRGBD (0.083 vs 0.085), though MAST3R retains an edge on NC for NRGBD (0.794 vs 0.870 — actually Test3R wins here too). For multi-view depth, Test3R (no camera parameters) beats Robust MVD Baseline (which uses GT pose and intrinsics) by a large margin on ETH3D (3.2 vs 9.0 rel error) and on DTU (2.0 vs 2.7), and outperforms COLMAP on ETH3D (3.2 vs 16.4 rel) while remaining competitive on DTU (2.0 vs 0.7). The generalization study confirms Test3R improves MAST3R (Acc 0.189→0.179) and MonST3R (Acc 0.240→0.218) on 7Scenes, validating its plug-and-play applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

Test3R builds directly on DUSt3R's pairwise pointmap prediction architecture and borrows the visual prompt tuning (VPT) mechanism from 2D domain adaptation, repurposing it as a lightweight test-time adaptation tool for 3D geometry. Conceptually, it departs from the DUSt3R lineage (MAST3R, MonST3R, Spann3R, CUT3R) which all attempt to fix the pairwise paradigm's inconsistencies through architectural changes or additional training objectives, by instead treating cross-pair consistency as a self-supervised signal that can be optimized at inference time without modifying the backbone. It also extends the test-time training (TTT) literature from classification and policy adaptation into the 3D reconstruction domain, where the self-supervised objective is geometric rather than perceptual.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The technique's main practical value is its universality: it can be plugged into any pairwise 3D reconstruction model (validated on DUSt3R, MAST3R, MonST3R, and mentioned for VGGT) with minimal parameter overhead, making it a low-cost drop-in improvement for the entire 3R family. An open question is scalability: experiments use only 3–5 views per scene, and it remains unclear how the triplet-based consistency objective behaves with the hundreds of views typical in real-world reconstruction pipelines, or how test-time optimization time grows with scene size. The paper also does not deeply analyze failure modes when views have very little overlap or when the scene contains significant dynamic content.
