## Problem

Traditional active learning for virtual screening relies on hand-crafted acquisition functions (greedy, uncertainty, mutual information, diversity) that are static and cannot dynamically balance exploration and exploitation as new data arrive. Existing adaptive frameworks like SelectAL and AutoAL only learn to select among predefined strategies rather than directly learning which molecules to query, limiting their effectiveness in the vast, complex chemical spaces encountered in drug discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252034/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled molecule library (up to ~10^8 compounds, represented as ECFP fingerprints, molecular graphs, or pretrained GNN embeddings) and a small initial labeled set of active/inactive molecules. It produces a trained policy network that outputs per-molecule select/exclude probabilities, and iteratively yields an enriched labeled dataset. The policy is conditioned on the current labeled set D, so its selection behavior adapts across active learning iterations as D grows.

## Method

GLARE reformulates virtual screening as a Markov Decision Process where candidate molecules are partitioned into groups and the policy sequentially decides select or exclude for each molecule. The policy model comprises a molecular encoder (MLP on ECFP, GIN, or pretrained GNN like GraphMVP/3DInfomax) feeding into a fully connected action scoring layer with softmax output. Training uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with group-wise advantage estimation, a KL-regularized objective for stability under limited data, and a custom reward that balances exploitation (correctly selecting actives) against exploration (selecting uncertain inactives) via a gradient-norm-based discount factor. This replaces all hand-crafted acquisition heuristics with a single learnable policy that adapts its selection strategy as the labeled set evolves across active learning iterations.

## Results

On the LIT-PCBA benchmarks (ALDH1, PKM2, VDR), GLARE with a pretrained GNN achieves Enrichment Factors of 7.205, 7.768, and 7.992 at the final iteration, representing 27.0%, 90.9%, and 76.5% improvements over the best TcsAL baseline. On large-scale Enamine benchmarks, GLARE (Pre.GNN) reaches RR_500 of 0.8869 on Enamine50k and RR_1,000 of 0.9732 on EnamineHTS-0.2, outperforming the best pretrained baseline (PtAL + MoLFormer + UCB) by 11.9% and 4.0% respectively. When applied to enhance the DrugCLIP foundation model, GLARE(15) achieves an 8-fold improvement in EF_0.5% (77.03 vs. 8.56) using only 15 active molecules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLARE consistently outperforms all TcsAL variants (MLP/GNN × Random/Similarity/Uncertainty/Greedy/MI) across all three LIT-PCBA subsets, with the largest gap on PKM2 where GLARE (Pre.GNN) reaches EF 7.768 vs. the best TcsAL (MLP+MI) at 4.070. On large-scale Enamine benchmarks, GLARE (Pre.GNN) surpasses PtAL with MoLFormer+UCB (the strongest pretrained baseline) by 11.9% on Enamine50k RR_500 (0.8869 vs. 0.7924) and by 4.0% on EnamineHTS-0.2 RR_1,000 (0.9732 vs. 0.9338), while also beating RF, LightGBM, and D-MPNN baselines. Against virtual screening foundation models on LIT-PCBA, GLARE(15) with GRPO outperforms DrugCLIP (EF_0.5%: 77.03 vs. 8.56), DrugTTA (EF_0.5%: 83.51 vs. 74.39; EF_1%: 47.36 vs. 42.74), and all docking-based methods (Surflex, Glide-SP, Gnina) as well as deep learning baselines (Planet, DeepDTA, BigBind). The only metric where DrugTTA is competitive is BEDROC (45.08 vs. 46.39 for GLARE(20) GRPO), a narrow margin. Ablation in Table 13 confirms GRPO outperforms DPO across all encoder types, with the gap most pronounced on VDR (7.992 vs. 7.590 for Pre.GNN).

## Relation to Prior Work

GLARE builds on the active learning virtual screening line (TcsAL, PtAL) that relies on handcrafted acquisition functions (greedy, MI, UCB, uncertainty, similarity), and on earlier RL-for-active-learning work that used Q-learning to learn acquisition policies. It departs from both by reformulating molecular selection as a Markov Decision Process and training a policy network with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO, borrowed from the LLM reasoning literature), shifting the paradigm from 'learning to select rules' (SelectAL, AutoAL) to 'learning to select molecules' directly. The molecular encoder is modular (ECFP-MLP, GIN, or pretrained GNNs like GraphMVP/3DInfomax), and the framework is demonstrated as a plug-in enhancer for existing VS foundation models such as DrugCLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8-fold EF_0.5% improvement over DrugCLIP with only 15 active molecules suggests that adaptive, RL-driven selection can dramatically reduce the experimental annotation budget needed to unlock foundation-model performance in drug discovery, which has direct cost implications for pharmaceutical screening campaigns. However, the authors note that performance diminishes as annotation budget grows and that scaling to ultra-large spaces (10^10 compounds) remains challenging due to time costs. Open questions include whether the GRPO-based policy generalizes across targets without retraining, how it behaves under noisy or ambiguous biological labels, and whether the approach can be extended to multi-objective screening (e.g., simultaneously optimizing for potency, selectivity, and synthesizability).
