## Problem

Large Multimodal Models frequently produce semantically plausible yet visually incorrect answers when spotting scene text that is non-semantic (e.g., random character strings, isolated numbers), a failure mode the authors term semantic hallucination. This occurs because LMMs rely on language priors rather than true visual grounding, and no prior work has systematically identified the intra-layer attention mechanisms responsible for this behavior or provided a training-free mitigation strategy.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free, test-time plug-in on existing LMMs (Mini-Monkey, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-NeXT) without any architectural modification or additional trainable parameters. It consumes a standard RGB scene text image and a text query, and produces the model's autoregressive text output (recognized scene text or a VQA answer). The only hyperparameters are the top-K token count (K=128) for region selection and a fusion weight (w=0.1) for hidden-state blending, both fixed at inference time.

## Method

The framework has two components. ZoomText performs coarse-to-fine text region localization without external detectors: a Glimpse step extracts query-to-image cross-attention from the final LLM layer to identify candidate text regions, and a Refocus step filters spurious 'register' tokens by computing normalized attention shift scores between the first and last transformer layers. Grounded Layer Correction (GLC) then selects the transformer layer with the highest text-region attention score (the layer most visually grounded on scene text) and fuses its hidden states into the final-layer hidden states via a weighted blend (w=0.1) before decoding. The key empirical insight driving the design is a strong negative Spearman correlation (r ≈ −0.68 to −0.80) between a layer's hallucination tendency and its attention allocation to ground-truth text regions, establishing that layers attending more to text are less prone to semantic hallucination.

## Results

On the proposed TextHalu-Bench, Qwen2.5-VL+Ours achieves 53.8 F1, surpassing the best proprietary model GPT-4o (45.3) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (43.2), while Mini-Monkey+Ours reaches 50.6 F1 (vs. 46.5 baseline, +4.1). On ST-VQA, Mini-Monkey+Ours gains +3.9 accuracy (66.7→70.6), and on SEED-Bench Text, Qwen2.5-VL+Ours improves by +3.5 (66.7→70.2). The method is training-free and introduces no additional parameters, with only ~0.55s extra prefilling overhead (1.08s vs. 0.53s baseline on Qwen2.5-VL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is integrated into three LMMs (LLaVA-NeXT, Mini-Monkey, Qwen2.5-VL) and compared against 10 other open-source and 2 proprietary models. On TextHalu-Bench, Qwen2.5-VL+Ours (53.8) outperforms all baselines including GPT-4o (45.3), Gemini 1.5 Pro (43.2), InternVL2.5 (42.0), and Mini-Monkey (46.5), establishing a new state of the art for hallucination resistance. On ST-VQA, Mini-Monkey+Ours (70.6) still trails InternVL2.5 (75.4) and Ovis1.6 (72.6), but surpasses all other open-source models; on TextVQA, Qwen2.5-VL+Ours (80.3) edges out InternVL2.5 (79.0) and LLaVA-OV (78.5). On GOT scene text, Mini-Monkey+Ours (89.2) is competitive with InternVL2.5 (90.0) but far ahead of LLaVA-OV (43.9) and Monkey (45.7). On SEED-Bench Text, Qwen2.5-VL+Ours (70.2) surpasses Molmo-D (77.4 is actually higher—correction: Molmo-D scores 77.4, so Qwen2.5-VL+Ours at 70.2 still trails it), but Mini-Monkey+Ours (84.5) leads all open-source models. The method consistently improves every base model it is applied to, with the largest gains (+4–5.5 F1) observed on TextHalu-Bench where non-semantic text dominates, confirming that the hallucination mitigation is most impactful precisely where semantic priors mislead the model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of LMM-based OCR and hallucination mitigation, departing from prior hallucination work that focuses on object, knowledge, or attribute hallucinations by identifying a distinct 'semantic hallucination' failure mode specific to scene text. Methodologically, it builds on the LLaVA-style architecture (visual encoder + projector + LLM) and repurposes intra-LLM cross-attention and self-attention maps for text region localization (ZoomText) and layer selection (GLC), avoiding external detectors or additional training. The layer-wise analysis draws on prior observations that different transformer layers encode different information types, but the specific finding that attention allocation to text regions inversely correlates with hallucination tendency is new to the OCR context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of semantic hallucination as a distinct, measurable failure mode in LMMs for scene text fills a gap in the hallucination literature, and TextHalu-Bench provides a much-needed evaluation tool that existing benchmarks (dominated by semantically coherent text) cannot capture. The training-free nature of the method makes it immediately deployable, though its effectiveness is bounded by the base model's OCR perception ability and it introduces prefilling-stage overhead. Open questions include whether the layer-selection strategy generalizes to video text or multilingual settings, and whether the modest 4–5% gains on TextHalu-Bench translate to meaningful improvements in downstream applications like autonomous driving or assistive technology.
