## Problem

Most latent variable models for neural population activity have been developed and validated on motor cortex data under controlled behavioral tasks, leaving a significant gap for visual cortex analysis under naturalistic stimuli. Furthermore, existing LVMs largely ignore the temporal structure inherent in visual neural dynamics, treating time points independently or using fixed convolutional kernels rather than explicitly modeling how latent representations evolve over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254377/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes sequential spike-count vectors (N neurons × T time windows, binned at 10 ms for natural scenes or 120 Hz for natural movies) as input and produces a time-resolved latent representation z_t ∈ R^M at each step, split into a deterministic external part and a stochastic internal part, along with inferred Poisson firing rates for reconstruction. For downstream decoding, the latent variables across a trial (or across the four sub-frame time points of a movie frame) are concatenated or averaged into a single vector used with KNN to classify 118 natural scenes or 900 movie frames.

## Method

TE-ViDS is a sequential VAE that introduces two GRU-based state factors to evolve the latent variables causally over time: the external state factor h_t^(e) is updated from the input features alone, while the internal state factor h_t^(i) additionally receives both latent parts, reflecting that internal states are influenced by stimuli. The external latent variables are deterministic and shaped by an NT-Xent contrastive loss that pulls time-shifted positive pairs (offset by a few time steps) together while pushing apart random negatives, explicitly linking them to stimulus content. The internal latent variables are stochastic, regularized by KL divergence to a time-dependent prior (conditioned only on h_{t-1}^{(i)}), capturing spontaneous internal-state variability. A swap operation exchanges external latents between positive pairs while holding internal latents fixed, adding an auxiliary reconstruction loss that further entangles the two parts. The full loss combines Poisson negative log-likelihood reconstruction, the contrastive term, and the KL regularization, all optimized end-to-end.

## Results

On the Allen Brain Observatory Visual Coding dataset, TE-ViDS achieves the highest KNN decoding accuracy for 118 natural scenes across all five mice, reaching 50.86% (Mouse 1), 38.05% (Mouse 4), and 29.90% (Mouse 3), compared to the next-best baseline Swap-VAE at 32.81%, 14.85%, and 14.36% respectively. For natural movie frame decoding (1 s constraint window), TE-ViDS attains 65.38% (Mouse 2), 59.88% (Mouse 3), and 54.33% (Mouse 4), outperforming CEBRA (52.76%, 61.01%, 42.11%) and Swap-VAE (51.31%, 45.96%, 41.53%). The external latent representations consistently outperform the internal ones in both scene and movie decoding, confirming the disentanglement design. On held-out (unseen) stimuli, TE-ViDS also leads, e.g., 65.91% on unseen scenes for Mouse 1 versus 55.48% for Swap-VAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TE-ViDS outperforms all four baselines (LFADS, pi-VAE, Swap-VAE, CEBRA) on natural scene decoding for every one of the five mice, with the largest margins over CEBRA (which scores only 1–5%, near chance) and pi-VAE. The closest competitor is Swap-VAE, which leads on Mouse 2 (24.34% vs. 27.24%) and is competitive on Mouse 3, but TE-ViDS still wins by 2.9–23.2 percentage points across mice. On natural movie frame decoding, TE-ViDS wins for four of five mice; the sole exception is Mouse 3, where CEBRA edges ahead (61.01% vs. 59.88%). CEBRA's fixed convolutional kernels capture temporal structure well for continuous movie stimuli but fail almost entirely on static scene discrimination, whereas TE-ViDS's GRU-based time-evolving latents handle both stimulus types. LFADS, the only other sequential model, is consistently outperformed by TE-ViDS on both tasks, suggesting that the split external/internal design and contrastive loss provide a meaningful advantage over a single-stream sequential VAE.

## Relation to Prior Work

TE-ViDS builds directly on Swap-VAE's split latent variable architecture and swap operation, extending it with GRU-based temporal evolution so that both external and internal latent representations evolve over time rather than being computed independently at each time point. It also draws on the sequential VAE framework of LFADS (bidirectional RNNs for motor cortex) but departs by using unidirectional GRUs with separate state factors for each latent branch, and by introducing a time-dependent prior for the internal variables. The paper explicitly positions itself as filling a gap: most prior LVM work (LFADS, pi-VAE, Swap-VAE) targeted motor cortex under controlled behavioral tasks, while visual cortex LVMs (fLDS, CEBRA) either used simple stimuli or lacked explicit temporal modeling of neural dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first LVM framework that simultaneously models temporal evolution and disentangles stimulus-relevant from internal-state components in mouse visual cortex under naturalistic stimuli, yielding the best decoding performance for both static scenes and continuous movies. The RSA analysis revealing that one mouse's internal state fluctuations dominate neural variability over visual stimulus effects, and the region-specific decoding hierarchy (VISrl lowest as a multi-sensory area), offer concrete neuroscientific insights. Key open questions include how to quantitatively interpret internal latent variables without recorded behavioral or physiological state data, and how to reduce the substantial cross-subject variability in decoding performance, which the authors attribute to unmeasured internal states.
