## Problem

Finding the optimal learning rate for foundation model pretraining typically requires resource-intensive hyperparameter searches on proxy models or is constrained to specific training scenarios with limited generalizability. No existing method can adaptively locate the optimal LR online within a single training run without additional independent experiments or scenario-specific tuning.

## Input / Output

AdaLRS consumes the running training loss sequence (sampled over sliding windows of k steps, e.g., 1000–2500 steps) and the current LR produced by a base scheduler (cosine, WSD, etc.) as its sole inputs. It outputs a multiplicatively adjusted learning rate at each decision point, where the adjustment magnitude decays geometrically over training via a factor λ ∈ (0,1). The method requires no gradient information, no proxy models, and no validation set—only the loss trajectory already logged during training.

## Method

AdaLRS exploits the observation that both pretraining loss and its descent velocity are convex functions of LR sharing the same optimum, enabling online LR search by optimizing loss descent velocity within a single run. At each decision point, the algorithm estimates the loss curve slope via least-squares fitting over a k-step window; when the slope decays below a threshold, it triggers a trial LR upscaling followed by validation steps at comparable loss levels. The upscaling is retained only if the post-adjustment velocity exceeds the pre-adjustment velocity by more than 2e (the estimation error bound); otherwise, model states are restored and a downscaling is applied instead. The scaling factors α′ = max(λ^t·α, 1) and β′ = 1/max(λ^t·β, 1) decay over time, guaranteeing geometric error decay (Theorem 2.4) and convergence to the e-neighborhood of the optimum in O(log R) adjustments. The algorithm is scheduler-agnostic, operates only during early training stages, and includes stability mechanisms such as early stopping on loss spikes and a backtracking strategy that restores model parameters after failed upscaling attempts.

## Results

On 2B LLM pretraining with an excessively small LR (2e-5), AdaLRS reduces validation PPL from 32.69 to 12.66 and improves Alpaca-Gen from 15.40 to 17.10. On 7B LLM with the same small LR, validation PPL drops from 15.30 to 10.61, and KNIGHT-Gen improves from 12.29 to 13.53. For the 2B VLM, AdaLRS starting from a small LR achieves an average benchmark score of 57.34 versus 53.77 for the baseline, surpassing even the Fit LR baseline (56.16). Under large LR settings, AdaLRS still improves training loss (e.g., 5.21 vs 5.32 for 2B LLM) but cannot fully recover from initialization disruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external LR search methods; instead, baselines are the same training pipeline with a fixed LR and cosine/WSD scheduler but without AdaLRS. Under small LR settings, AdaLRS dramatically outperforms baselines: 2B LLM validation PPL improves from 32.69 to 12.66 (61% reduction), and 7B LLM from 15.30 to 10.61 (31% reduction). For the 2B VLM, AdaLRS from a small LR achieves 57.34 average benchmark score, beating both the small-LR baseline (53.77) and the Fit-LR baseline (56.16). Under large LR settings, AdaLRS provides moderate gains (e.g., 2B LLM validation PPL 183.94 vs 204.97) but cannot fully recover from the initialization damage caused by the excessively large LR. Under Fit LR settings, AdaLRS is roughly on par with or slightly below the baseline (e.g., 2B LLM training loss 2.62 vs 2.54), confirming it does not degrade already-optimal configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaLRS extends the convexity analysis of training loss w.r.t. learning rate established by Schaipp et al. [38] by hypothesizing and empirically validating that the loss descent velocity (slope) is also convex and shares the same optimal LR, enabling online search within a single training run. This departs from three prior paradigms: scaling-law-based LR prediction (Kaplan et al., DeepSeek, MM1) which lacks generalizability across architectures; proxy-model transfer (μP, μTransfer) which still requires expensive multi-run searches on smaller models; and auto hyperparameter search (Bayesian optimization, Hyperband, PBT) which demands many independent runs. The paper also distinguishes itself from Hypergradient Descent [3], which performs online LR adjustment but functions as a scheduler only compatible with constant LRS on small networks, whereas AdaLRS is a search algorithm compatible with modern schedulers (cosine, WSD) and validated on multi-billion-parameter foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaLRS offers a practically significant reduction in pretraining cost by eliminating the need for grid searches, proxy-model experiments, or multi-run hyperparameter optimization to find the optimal learning rate—a critical hyperparameter for foundation model pretraining. The geometric convergence guarantee (O(log R) adjustments) and the finding that starting from a deliberately small LR can even surpass a carefully tuned 'fit' LR baseline (e.g., 7B LLM validation PPL 10.61 vs. 10.72) suggest a robust default strategy for unexplored pretraining tasks. Key limitations remain: excessively large initial LRs cause irreversible parameter disruption that AdaLRS cannot recover from, and the fine-grained precision of the final LR approximation is not characterized. Open questions include whether the convexity-of-slope property holds under more complex optimizers (e.g., AdamW with decoupled weight decay), mixed-precision training, or non-standard model architectures beyond transformers.
