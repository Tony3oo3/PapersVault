## Problem

CLIP's image-level contrastive pre-training produces noisy, spatially inconsistent masks when naively adapted to pixel-level open-vocabulary segmentation. Existing training-free attention-refinement methods (e.g., SCLIP, MaskCLIP, ProxyCLIP) impose local compactness priors on query-key weights but systematically underweight global connectivity—the long-range dependencies among distant tokens of the same semantic category—leading to fragmented or contextually incomplete segmentations. The paper asks whether a lightweight, parameter-frozen module can inject both local and global structure into CLIP's attention without redesigning the full pipeline or updating CLIP weights.

## Input / Output

At inference, OPMapper consumes the frozen query and key tensors from the final layer of a pre-trained CLIP ViT encoder (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14) along with the corresponding value tensor and CLIP text embeddings for L class prompts formatted as 'a photo of a {class}'. It outputs a pixel-level segmentation mask of shape H×W×L by computing enhanced Q′K′ᵀ attention weights that re-aggregate the unchanged value embeddings, followed by a dot-product with text embeddings and argmax. The mapper itself contains only 0.8M parameters and requires ≤2 GB GPU memory per batch, making it a drop-in replacement for the standard Q-K dot product in any CLIP-based segmentation pipeline.

## Method

OPMapper comprises two modules: Context-aware Attention Injection (CAI) and a training-only Semantic Attention Alignment (SAA) auxiliary. CAI transforms the frozen last-layer Q and K through two cross-attention blocks each, producing Q′ and K′ whose dot product yields pixel-aware attention weights. It is supervised by KL divergence against a manually constructed prior matrix that fuses a local compactness term (exponential decay of Euclidean distance, restricted to same-class pairs) with a global connectivity term (binary same-class mask, row-mean normalized), blended at λ=0.3. SAA performs three iterative cross-attention stages—text-guided visual enhancement with top-k category masking, visual-guided text refinement, and a final cross-modal fusion—to align CAI's attention with textual semantics during training. The total loss combines KL, DICE, and BCE terms (weights 10/5/10). After training, SAA is discarded entirely, leaving CAI as a lightweight plug-and-play module that can be inserted into both training-based and training-free CLIP segmentation methods by simply replacing their Q-K attention computation.

## Results

OPMapper is a 0.8M-parameter plug-and-play module that improves open-vocabulary segmentation across 8 benchmarks. In the training-free setting with ViT-B/16, adding OPMapper to MaskCLIP yields the largest gain, boosting average mIoU from 27.89 to 37.68 (+9.79), while CASS+OPMapper reaches 45.37 average mIoU (vs. 44.16 for CASS alone). In the training-based setting, SCAN+OPMapper improves VOC-20 mIoU from 94.9 to 96.0 and Context-59 from 56.8 to 58.3. The module is trained on COCO-Stuff in 3–4 hours on 4×A100 GPUs with ≤2 GB GPU memory per batch, and all CLIP parameters remain frozen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPMapper is evaluated as an add-on to 11 existing CLIP-based methods across 8 benchmarks, and it consistently improves nearly every baseline. The largest absolute gains appear when paired with weaker training-free baselines: MaskCLIP+OPMapper jumps +9.79 mIoU on average (e.g., +18.9 on CityScapes, +11.9 on VOC-21), and SCLIP+OPMapper gains +3.46 on average. Against stronger training-free baselines that already incorporate external features (ProxyCLIP, CASS), the gains shrink to +1.04 and +1.21 respectively, with one small regression (ProxyCLIP CityScapes: −0.9). In the training-based setting, improvements are more modest (+0.2 to +1.5 mIoU per dataset), which the authors attribute to fine-tuning already partially optimizing attention for dense prediction. The only notable loss is CAT-Seg on VOC-21 (−0.4 mIoU), while all other training-based comparisons show positive or neutral results. Overall, OPMapper wins or ties on 47 of 48 reported dataset-level comparisons in the training-free ViT-B/16 setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPMapper extends the training-free OVSS line (SCLIP, ProxyCLIP, MaskCLIP, ClearCLIP) that re-engineers CLIP's query-key attention without updating model parameters. Its key conceptual departure is that prior methods predominantly encode local compactness (via q-q, k-k, or identity-matrix attention) while under-representing global connectivity among same-category but spatially distant tokens; OPMapper explicitly constructs a prior attention matrix combining both and trains a lightweight mapper (CAI) to reproduce it, supervised by KL divergence. A training-only auxiliary module (SAA) provides cross-modal alignment signal during training but is discarded at inference, making the deployed system a 0.8M-parameter plug-in that can be grafted onto either training-based or training-free pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (0.8M params, 3–4 h training on 4×A100) makes OPMapper a low-friction enhancement for the many existing CLIP-based segmentation methods, and the consistent gains across 11 models and 8 benchmarks demonstrate broad applicability. The long-range attention quantification (Table 9: 0.373 vs. ≤0.289 for prior methods) provides concrete evidence that global connectivity was a genuine blind spot in the field. The stated limitation—that benefits diminish when CLIP's vision encoder is heavily fine-tuned—leaves open the question of how to adapt the mapper to shifted parameter distributions, and the modest gains over already-strong baselines (e.g., +1.21 over CASS) suggest diminishing returns as base methods improve.
