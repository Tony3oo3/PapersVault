## Problem

Conventional endoscopes are bulky and expensive due to large multi-element optics, while existing fiber-based alternatives require mechanical raster scanning or multicore bundles that limit resolution and speed. Classical ghost imaging, though compatible with single-pixel detectors, is inherently slow because illumination patterns must be projected sequentially, making real-time imaging impractical. This paper addresses the gap by demonstrating a hardware-software co-design that achieves snapshot (fully parallel) speckle projection through a single-core fiber and reconstructs high-fidelity images from ultra-low sampling ratios (~0.29%) in real time.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of ~200 uncorrelated 256×256 speckle patterns (physically generated at the tip of a 200 μm multimode fiber by dual-comb wavelength-division multiplexing across 1530–1565 nm) and their corresponding scalar bucket-sum measurements obtained from a single-pixel InGaAs photodetector via FFT of the dual-comb interferogram. The output is a reconstructed 256×256 grayscale intensity image of the target. Notably, the 2D camera used to record speckle patterns is only needed during a one-time calibration phase and is absent during actual imaging, reducing the physical footprint to essentially zero-dimensional (single spot in, single pixel out).

## Method

The hardware side uses two electro-optic frequency combs with a 200 Hz repetition-rate offset to generate ~200 mutually uncorrelated speckle patterns simultaneously through a multimode fiber, with a single-pixel detector capturing all bucket sums in one snapshot via dual-comb beat-note detection. The software side introduces Optical Ghost-GPT, a ~270M-parameter transformer where each token concatenates a flattened speckle pattern (linearly projected to a 31-dim latent vector) with its scalar bucket-sum value, augmented by learned positional embeddings. The model stacks 12 transformer blocks (8 attention heads, embedding_dim=32, GELU feedforward, batch normalization, 0.1 dropout) and projects the final sequence through a linear layer with sigmoid activation to recover the 256×256 image. Trained end-to-end with MSE loss (AdamW, lr=3e-4, weight decay=1e-3) on 38,560 synthetic speckle-bucket pairs generated by convolving recorded speckle patterns with MNIST and Omniglot digits, the model exploits self-attention to capture global correlations across the token sequence, achieving SSIM 0.788 and MSE 0.008 at 0.29% sampling ratio while reconstructing in ~14 ms—approximately 263× faster than FISTA.

## Results

Ghost-GPT achieves an average MSE of 0.008 and SSIM of 0.788 on 256 validation images at an ultra-low sampling ratio of 0.29%, substantially outperforming all classical reconstruction algorithms. Reconstruction takes only 14 ms on GPU, approximately 263× faster than FISTA (3680 ms on CPU). On experimental USAF resolution targets, Ghost-GPT also produces higher-fidelity reconstructions than classical methods, resolving features down to ~0.077 mm. The model maintains recognizable reconstructions at SNR levels above 20 dB, matching the noise floor of the physical measurements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ghost-GPT decisively outperforms both classical baselines on every metric. Against Differential Ghost Imaging [Liu, Sci. Rep. 2020], Ghost-GPT reduces MSE from 0.184 to 0.008 (23× lower) and raises SSIM from 0.042 to 0.788 (~19× higher), while running at comparable speed (14 ms vs 15.7 ms on GPU). Against FISTA [Beck & Teboulle, SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 2009], Ghost-GPT achieves 5.6× lower MSE (0.008 vs 0.045) and 8.6× higher SSIM (0.788 vs 0.092), while being ~263× faster (14 ms GPU vs 3680 ms CPU). The Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, though faster than FISTA, still lags Ghost-GPT by 11.6× in MSE and 14.3× in SSIM. In a separate comparison with matched-parameter deep learning baselines (~270M params), Ghost-GPT achieves 47.7% lower MSE than a decoder-only CNN and 38.2% lower MSE than a U-Net, though the CNN and U-Net report slightly higher SSIM (0.808 and 0.832 respectively), suggesting the transformer's self-attention captures global correlations more effectively for pixel-level accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of dual-comb interferometry (Coddington et al., 2016) and single-pixel ghost imaging (Bromberg et al., 2009), combining them into a hardware-software co-design where wavelength-division multiplexing of comb lines replaces the slow sequential pattern projection that has been the bottleneck of classical ghost imaging. On the reconstruction side, it departs from prior deep-learning ghost imaging work (which was largely confined to 28×28 images with binary masks or used CNN/U-Net post-processing of classical reconstructions) by introducing a transformer whose tokens jointly encode each speckle pattern and its bucket sum, exploiting the global correlation structure inherent in the measurement. The key conceptual departure from prior ghost imaging is the elimination of both spatial and spectral scanning via dual-comb parallelism, making the physical acquisition truly snapshot rather than the serial process assumed by most reconstruction algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling real-time (14 ms reconstruction) endomicroscopy through a single-core fiber with a single-pixel detector, a form factor that is orders of magnitude smaller than conventional endoscopes. However, the current demonstration is constrained to ~200 comb lines and binary (MNIST/Omniglot) targets, so grayscale video-rate imaging of real biological tissue remains an open challenge. The domain gap between synthetic training data and clinical targets, the need for reflective-mode beam collection, and the trade-off between sampling ratio and frame rate under the Nyquist condition are all acknowledged but unresolved. For the DL community, the paper is a relatively straightforward application of a standard transformer to a structured inverse problem, and the comparison against classical baselines (DGI, PI, FISTA) is somewhat one-sided given their very low SSIM values (0.04–0.09).
