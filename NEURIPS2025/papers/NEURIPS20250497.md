## Problem

Despite rapid development of concept erasure methods for diffusion models, it remains unclear whether these approaches truly remove target concepts or merely redirect generation away from them while leaving underlying knowledge intact. The paper formalizes two conceptual mechanisms—guidance-based avoidance (modifying conditional guidance while preserving features) and destruction-based removal (suppressing unconditional likelihood)—and asks how to empirically distinguish between them using independent probing techniques.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250497/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation suite probes erased Stable Diffusion 1.4 models (512×512 images) across 13 concepts (10 objects, 3 art styles) using five independent techniques that consume different input signals: standard text prompts, masked images for inpainting, intermediate noisy latents from the original model at t=5 or t=10 out of 50 steps, text prompts with injected Gaussian noise at each denoising step, and text prompts augmented with gradients from a timestep-aware latent classifier. Outputs are generated images scored by CLIP ViT-B/32 similarity and ResNet-50 (Imagenette) top-1 classification accuracy, plus concept centroid trajectories in CLIP embedding space across progressive erasure strengths.

## Method

The paper introduces a five-probe evaluation framework designed to uncover residual concept knowledge through independent pathways: (1) optimization-based probing via Textual Inversion and UnlearnDiffAtk, (2) in-context probing via inpainting and diffusion completion from intermediate latents, (3) a training-free noise-based probe that scales the stochastic term in the DDIM formulation (η ∈ [1.0, 1.85]) to expand the diffusion trajectory bandwidth and surface suppressed concepts, (4) steered latent probing that trains a lightweight timestep-aware MLP classifier in latent space and injects its BCE gradient at each denoising step to steer trajectories toward residual concept regions, and (5) dynamic concept tracing that tracks CLIP embedding centroids across progressive erasure strengths. The key finding is that no single probe is sufficient—methods robust to adversarial text optimization (e.g., STEREO, Task Vector) remain vulnerable to context-based or noise-based probes, revealing that most current erasure methods operate through guidance-based avoidance rather than true knowledge destruction.

## Results

Under standard prompts, STEREO achieves 0.0% classification accuracy on erased concepts (CLIP 19.6), followed by GA (0.6%, CLIP 24.3) and Task Vector (2.2%, CLIP 23.1), indicating near-complete erasure. However, UCE and ESD-x remain highly vulnerable to Textual Inversion, with classification accuracies of 71.2% and 65.9% respectively, revealing that residual knowledge persists. The paper's central finding is that no erasure method is robust to all probing techniques: even STEREO, which achieves 0.0% under standard prompts and 6.3% under Textual Inversion, can be recovered to 20.3% classification accuracy via classifier-guided noise-based probing. On unrelated concepts, UCE best preserves general utility (75.0% Class Acc, CLIP 31.2), while STEREO and GA show the greatest collateral degradation (52.8% and 52.2% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates seven erasure methods (plus an unedited baseline) across five independent probing techniques, revealing that each method has distinct vulnerabilities. UCE and ESD-x, while achieving low scores under standard prompts (4.4% and 3.6% Class Acc), are easily broken by Textual Inversion (71.2% and 65.9%) and noise-based probing (21.9% and 30.7%), confirming they operate as guidance-based avoidance rather than true destruction. Task Vector is robust to Textual Inversion (6.2%) and UnlearnDiffAtk (10.3%) but remains vulnerable to inpainting (66.8% Class Acc) and diffusion completion at t=5 (2.4%), suggesting the concept knowledge persists in the unconditional distribution. STEREO is the most robust to optimization-based attacks (0.0% standard, 6.3% TI, 3.7% UnlearnDiffAtk) but is recoverable to 20.3% via classifier-guided noise probing and 21.2% via diffusion completion at t=10. RECE shows intermediate robustness to adversarial probes (58.2% under TI) but is notably vulnerable to diffusion completion (45.4% at t=10). Gradient Ascent achieves the strongest overall erasure across most probes but causes the greatest degradation on unrelated concepts (52.2% vs. 75.0% for UCE), illustrating a robustness–utility trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established concept erasure literature (UCE, ESD, Task Vector, STEREO, RECE) and prior adversarial probing work (Textual Inversion, UnlearnDiffAtk), but departs from the single-axis evaluation paradigm by introducing a multi-perspective probing suite. It draws on the DDIM formulation for its noise-based trajectory probe and on classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol) for its steered latent probe, repurposing both as diagnostic tools rather than generation techniques. The key conceptual departure is the formalization of two erasure regimes—guidance-based avoidance versus destruction-based removal—which prior work treated implicitly but never operationalized as a testable distinction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that most current erasure methods operate through guidance redirection rather than true knowledge destruction reframes the field's understanding of what 'erasure' actually achieves, and the noise-based probe offers a simple, training-free diagnostic that outperforms complex adversarial attacks. However, the study is limited to Stable Diffusion 1.4 and 13 concepts, and the paper itself acknowledges that optimization-based probes may create rather than reveal knowledge. Open questions include whether destruction-based removal can be achieved without collateral damage to related concepts, and whether the guidance-vs-destruction distinction holds for more abstract concepts (verbs, relationships, abstract ideas).
