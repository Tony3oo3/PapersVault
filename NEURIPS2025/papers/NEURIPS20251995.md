## Problem

Standard matroid secretary and prophet inequality problems maximize total weight (social welfare) of accepted elements, which can produce highly unequal outcomes where some elements are never selected. This paper introduces and studies fair matroid selection, where the objective is instead to maximize the minimum acceptance probability across all elements (f-fairness), ensuring every element has a guaranteed chance of being selected. The paper characterizes the optimal fairness in terms of matroid arboricity and designs near-optimal online algorithms under both adversarial and random arrival orders.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives elements of an unknown matroid as an online stream (adversarial or random order), with access to an independence oracle restricted to previously seen elements; in the graphic case, each element reveals its two endpoints, and in the laminar case, each element reveals which sets contain it. The algorithm may optionally know the arboricity α and rank k upfront, or be fully oblivious. The output is an independent set of accepted elements, with the guarantee that every element in the matroid is accepted with probability at least 1/f, where f is the fairness parameter the algorithm aims to minimize.

## Method

The central technique reduces fair matroid selection to an online coloring problem: the algorithm colors arriving elements so that each color class forms an independent set, then selects all elements of a uniformly random color chosen in advance. For general matroids, a greedy coloring assigns each element to the first color class where it remains independent; since the first class is a maximal independent set of rank k and the matroid has at most αk elements, each class removes at least a 1/α fraction of remaining elements, requiring only O(α log k) colors. For laminar matroids, the algorithm maintains an online ordering simulating an Euler tour of the laminar tree and colors so that any α consecutive elements have distinct colors, using 2α−1 colors to guarantee independence. For graphic matroids under random arrival, the algorithm learns an approximate degeneracy ordering from two edge samples by computing d-levels on a directed subgraph, then accepts at most one edge per vertex to its earlier neighbors. A general oblivious-adaptation framework (Theorem 8) removes all upfront knowledge with only a logarithmic fairness loss via a feasible-function-based randomization over parameter values.

## Results

For general matroids under adversarial arrival, the paper achieves an α(ln k + 1)-fair algorithm (Theorem 10), where α is the arboricity and k is the rank, which is nearly optimal since the offline optimum is α-fair (Theorem 7). For laminar matroids under adversarial arrival, a (2α−1)-fair algorithm is achieved (Theorem 11), optimal up to a constant factor of 2. For graphic matroids under random arrival, a (4+o(1))α-fair algorithm is obtained (Theorem 15), matching the offline optimum up to constant factors. All results extend to a fully oblivious setting with only a logarithmic loss in the fairness parameter (e.g., Õ(α log k) for general matroids).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines; comparisons are made against the offline optimum and prior theoretical bounds. The offline optimum for fair matroid selection is exactly α-fair (Theorem 7), and the paper's adversarial-order general matroid algorithm achieves α(ln k + 1)-fairness, incurring only a logarithmic gap. For laminar matroids, the (2α−1) guarantee is within a factor of 2 of the offline α-optimum, which the paper shows is tight (no (α−1)-fair algorithm exists even offline). In the random-order graphic matroid setting, the (4+o(1))α result matches the offline optimum up to a constant factor of 4, leveraging the degeneracy-arboricity relationship (α ≤ d < 2α). The paper also notes that prior work on probability competitive ratio for matroid secretary achieves O(log k) in the random-order model, whereas this paper achieves a similar O(log k) gap under the significantly harder adversarial arrival model. No direct comparison to the matroid secretary problem's competitive ratios is made, as the objectives (maximizing sum vs. maximizing minimum probability) are fundamentally different.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the fair matroid selection problem, which departs from the classical matroid secretary problem (Babaioff et al., 2007) by replacing the total-weight objective with a max-min fairness criterion on acceptance probabilities. It builds on Edmonds' (1965) formula for matroid arboricity as the fundamental structural parameter characterizing optimal fairness, and adapts the vertex-ordering-and-grouping strategy from Korula and Pál (2009) for graphic matroids to the fair selection setting. The key technical novelty is a reduction from fair selection to online coloring, combined with a novel d-level-based sampling technique for learning degeneracy orderings in the random-order graphic matroid case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of fairness in online matroid selection, providing tight characterizations (within O(log k) for general matroids, within a factor of 2 for laminar matroids) that were previously unaddressed. The result that arboricity cleanly characterizes optimal offline fairness up to an additive 1, and that a simple greedy coloring algorithm achieves near-optimal fairness even under adversarial ordering with no prior knowledge, is a clean and broadly applicable structural insight. Open questions include whether the O(log k) gap for general matroids can be closed, whether the constant factors for laminar and graphic matroids are tight, and how these results extend to other matroid classes or to settings with weights and predictions.
