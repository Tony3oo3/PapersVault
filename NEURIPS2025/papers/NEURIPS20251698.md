## Problem

Existing time series foundation models trained on general-purpose data struggle to generalize to observability metrics, which are characterized by extreme nonstationarity, heavy-tailed distributions, high-cardinality multivariate structure, and sparse spiky behavior. Additionally, no large-scale benchmark existed to rigorously evaluate forecasting models on real-world observability data at production scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251698/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TOTO takes as input a multivariate time series context of length L=4096 timesteps across M variates (median 60 in BOOM, ranging 1–100), sampled at intervals from 10 seconds to 1 day. The context is divided into non-overlapping patches of size P=64, yielding 64 patch tokens per variate. The model outputs a probabilistic next-patch forecast parameterized as a Student-T mixture with K components (each with learned mean, scale, and degrees of freedom), from which point and interval forecasts are obtained via Monte Carlo sampling.

## Method

TOTO is a 151M-parameter decoder-only transformer trained on a next-patch prediction task over ~2.36 trillion time series points (43% Datadog observability telemetry, plus GIFT-Eval Pretrain, Chronos, and synthetic data). Four architectural components target observability-specific challenges: (1) patch-based causal instance normalization computed via a vectorized Welford's algorithm, with variate-level clipping to handle extreme nonstationarity; (2) proportional factorized attention with an 11:1 ratio of time-wise to variate-wise transformer blocks, reducing complexity from O(M²·(L/P)²) to O(M·(L/P)² + (L/P)·M²); (3) a Student-T mixture prediction head (K components) that robustly models heavy-tailed and skewed output distributions; and (4) a composite training loss combining negative log-likelihood with a Cauchy robust loss (λ_NLL=0.57, δ=0.1) to prevent mixture collapse and mitigate outlier sensitivity.

## Results

On the newly introduced BOOM benchmark (350M observations, 2,807 multivariate series), TOTO achieves MASE 0.617 and CRPS 0.375, outperforming the next-best zero-shot model (Moirai_Base) by 13.1% and 12.4% respectively, with a mean rank of 2.369 vs. 4.328. On GIFT-Eval, TOTO attains the top leaderboard position with MASE 0.673, CRPS 0.437, and rank 5.495, surpassing all zero-shot and full-shot competitors. On LSF, TOTO achieves the best result on 8 of 12 dataset-metric pairs, with the lowest average MAE (0.324) and MSE (0.304) among all zero-shot methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOTO outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every benchmark and metric. On BOOM, TOTO leads Moirai_Base (Woo et al., ICML 2024) by 13.1% MASE and 12.4% CRPS, and beats TimesFM 2.0 (Das et al., ICML 2024) and Chronos Bolt-Base (Ansari et al., 2024) by roughly 15–17% in MASE. Among zero-shot foundation models, Timer (Liu et al., ICML 2024) and Time-MoE_Base (Shi et al., ICLR 2025) trail TOTO by 29% and 42% in MASE respectively, while VisionTS (Chen et al., 2024) lags by 60%. On GIFT-Eval, TOTO (MASE 0.673, CRPS 0.437, Rank 5.495) surpasses Moirai_Large (0.785/0.506/10.330), TimesFM 2.0 (0.680/0.465/8.412), Chronos Bolt-Base (0.725/0.485/8.309), TabPFN-TS (Hoo et al., 2025) (0.748/0.480/8.402), and full-shot methods PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023) (0.762/0.496/10.268) and TFT (0.822/0.511/11.629). Classical baselines Auto-ARIMA (Box & Jenkins, 1970) and Auto-ETS (Li et al., NSDI 2020) are consistently outperformed by 15–60% in MASE on both BOOM and GIFT-Eval. On LSF, TOTO achieves the best MAE on 5 of 6 datasets and best MSE on 3 of 6, edging out Time-MoE_Ultra and VisionTS which are the closest competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOTO extends the decoder-only time series foundation model paradigm established by TimesFM, Chronos, Moirai, and Time-MoE, but introduces four domain-specific architectural components motivated by observability data: causal patch-based instance normalization (replacing the first-patch or global normalization in prior TSFMs), proportional factorized attention with an 11:1 time-to-variate block ratio (a relaxation of the equal-allocation factorized attention in Crossformer and Moirai), a Student-T mixture prediction head (replacing Gaussian mixtures or quantile heads), and a composite NLL+Cauchy robust loss for training stability. The training corpus is 4–10× larger than prior TSFMs, mixing Datadog's internal observability telemetry with GIFT-Eval Pretrain, Chronos, and synthetic data. The paper also introduces BOOM, a 350M-point observability benchmark that follows GIFT-Eval's evaluation protocol but targets a fundamentally different data distribution (high-cardinality multivariate series with heavy tails, sparsity, and nonstationarity).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TOTO is the first observability-specialized TSFM that simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art on both domain-specific (BOOM) and general-purpose (GIFT-Eval, LSF) benchmarks, suggesting that architectural choices driven by observability's extreme nonstationarity and high cardinality generalize broadly. The open-sourcing of both the 151M-parameter model and the BOOM benchmark under Apache 2.0 provides the community with a realistic, large-scale evaluation environment for production telemetry that was previously absent. Key limitations include the fixed-interval assumption (missing points handled by heuristic imputation rather than native modeling), the absence of calendar-based features, and the lack of error bars or multi-seed evaluation, which limits confidence in the exact magnitude of reported gains. Open questions include whether the proportional factorized attention ratio and SMM head transfer to other high-cardinality domains, and how performance degrades at extreme prediction lengths.
