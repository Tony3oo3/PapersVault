## Problem

Deep neural networks trained on long-tailed data produce majority-biased decision boundaries that severely degrade tail-class performance. Existing two-stage methods fail at both stages: representation learning yields poorly separated feature spaces for tail classes, and sampling strategies either suffer from mode collapse (oversampling) or cause significant information loss (undersampling), preventing simultaneous improvement across head and tail classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251567/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist 2018) and outputs a single class label per image. During training, each image is augmented into two views for the contrastive representation stage, and the full dataset is partitioned into per-class subsets for the DPP-based balanced sampling stage, where a fixed cardinality k = 10·N_C is drawn from each majority class to match the smallest class size.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (BNS) formulates representation learning as maximizing mutual information between two augmented views via a Noise Contrastive Estimation lower bound, with the key innovation of sampling m additional same-class images as extra positive pairs to balance the loss across head and tail classes; this is proven equivalent to minimizing intra-class distance and captures both instance-level and class-level semantics. Stage 2 (IP-DPP) constructs a fixed-size Determinantal Point Process over a symmetric stochastic matrix derived from per-sample classification probabilities, so that the sampling probability is proportional to each instance's Shannon information content (−log p(y|x)); this selects mathematically informative, hard-to-classify instances from majority classes to form balanced subsets that rectify biased decision boundaries while preserving overall dataset information.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT, the proposed method achieves 76.4% overall accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (OTmix, 73.8%) by 2.6%, with particularly strong few-shot accuracy of 67.2% (vs. 57.6% for SBCL). On CIFAR-100-LT, it reaches 52.4% overall accuracy, surpassing DisA (49.2%) by 3.2%. On large-scale datasets, the method achieves 51.7% on ImageNet-LT (beating DisA by 2.3%) and 74.0% on iNaturalist 2018 (beating SBCL by 3.6%), consistently achieving the highest medium-shot and few-shot accuracies across all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against nine baselines spanning reweighting (Focal Loss, LDAM Loss), logit adjustment (τ-norm), routing (RIDE), contrastive learning (KCL, TSC, SBCL), optimal transport (OTmix), and neural collapse (DisA) approaches. The proposed method achieves the highest overall accuracy on all four datasets: 76.4% on CIFAR-10-LT (+2.6% over OTmix), 52.4% on CIFAR-100-LT (+3.2% over DisA), 51.7% on ImageNet-LT (+2.3% over DisA), and 74.0% on iNaturalist 2018 (+3.6% over SBCL). The method consistently sacrifices some many-shot accuracy (e.g., 82.0% vs. OTmix's 87.9% on CIFAR-10-LT) in exchange for substantially better medium-shot and few-shot performance (76.3% and 67.2% vs. 67.8% and 47.3% for OTmix), demonstrating its ability to rectify majority-biased decision boundaries. Among contrastive-learning baselines, SBCL is the strongest competitor, but the proposed method still outperforms it by 3.8% overall on CIFAR-10-LT and 3.9% on CIFAR-100-LT. The gap is most pronounced on tail classes, where the method achieves 67.2% few-shot on CIFAR-10-LT versus 57.6% for SBCL and 50.3% for DisA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage learning paradigm for long-tailed recognition by combining two distinct ideas: a Noise Contrastive Estimation (NCE)-based contrastive learning objective augmented with same-class negative sampling (BNS), and a Determinantal Point Process (DPP) adapted for information-preservable undersampling (IP-DPP). It departs from prior long-tail-specific contrastive methods (KCL, TSC, SBCL) by framing representation learning through mutual information maximization, which the authors prove equivalent to intra-class distance minimization. The IP-DPP component repurposes the DPP framework from submodular set selection into a class-balanced sampling strategy grounded in Shannon information content, contrasting with both oversampling (SMOTE, GAN-based) and naive undersampling approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the persistent tension in long-tailed learning between head-class and tail-class performance, offering a principled mechanism (information-preservable sampling) that avoids the information loss of undersampling and the mode collapse of oversampling. The theoretical grounding—linking mutual information maximization to intra-class distance minimization—provides a clean justification for the BNS objective beyond empirical tuning. Open questions include how the DPP construction scales to extremely large ground sets (the N×N matrix becomes prohibitive), whether the independence assumption in the joint probability p(i,j)=p(i)p(j) holds in practice, and whether the uniform eigenvalue distribution assumption in the expected sample size theorem significantly distorts behavior on real data.
