## Problem

Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) rules are central to Hebbian learning in biological neural networks, but rigorous mathematical analysis of their long-run behaviour has been limited to ensemble averages or intractable master equations. The intrinsic randomness of spike-timing dynamics, combined with the non-convex geometry of the probability simplex, has prevented anyone from proving convergence rates for the full stochastic learning process. This paper closes that gap by connecting a biologically plausible STDP rule to noisy gradient descent and establishing exponential convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spike trains generated by d independent Poisson processes with fixed intensities λ_i > 0, initial non-negative synaptic weights w_i(0), and a scalar learning rate α. The output is a sequence of spike-triggering probability vectors p(k) on the probability simplex that converge to the standard basis vector e_j corresponding to the highest-intensity input neuron, implementing a winner-take-all mechanism. Weights evolve multiplicatively (w(k+1) = w(k) ⊙ (1 + αY(k))), so they grow without bound over time—a known instability of Hebbian learning that the authors address by interpreting the dynamic as a finite-length learning phase followed by homeostatic stabilisation.

## Method

The core contribution is a Taylor expansion of the multiplicative weight update that reveals the probability dynamics p(k+1) = p(k) − α∇L(p(k)) + αξ(k) as noisy gradient descent on the simplex, where L(p) = −(1/3)Σp_i³ + (1/4)(Σp_i²)² is a non-convex loss whose only local minima are the standard basis vectors. The centred noise ξ(k) arises from the multinomial sampling of which presynaptic spike triggers the postsynaptic response plus bounded contributions from non-triggering spikes. To prove exponential convergence despite constant noise, the authors restrict to an event Ω where the gap between the top probability and all others remains bounded below, then apply Doob's submartingale inequality to the cumulative noise martingale M(k) = αΣξ(i) to show Ω occurs with probability ≥ 1−ε/2 for sufficiently small α. The associated gradient flow is identified as a replicator equation, and the discrete rule is further connected to entropic mirror descent via the Fisher information metric on the simplex, linking the analysis to natural gradient and evolutionary game theory.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2.2) establishes that the spike-triggering probability vector p(k) converges to the standard basis vector e_1 (corresponding to the highest-intensity input neuron) at an exponential rate: E[‖p(k) − e_1‖₁ · 1_Θ] ≤ 2(1 − p₁(0)) exp(−(α/16)(4Δ/d + Δ²)k) on an event Θ of probability ≥ 1 − ε/2, with a constant learning rate α. The associated gradient flow (Theorem 2.4) yields the analogous continuous-time bound ‖e₁ − p(t)‖₁ ≤ 2(1 − p₁(0)) exp(−(Δ/d)(1 + (d−1)Δ)t). The loss landscape (Lemma 2.1) shows that all d standard basis vectors are the unique global minima, while all other critical points (uniform distributions over subsets of size ≥ 2) are saddle points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or comparison tables. The paper positions its contribution relative to prior work in two ways: (1) Compared to Mertikopoulos et al. [27] on noisy SGD for non-convex problems, the present work achieves linear (exponential) convergence with a constant learning rate, whereas prior results typically require diminishing step sizes or only establish convergence to a stationary regime. (2) The multi-vector extension (Algorithm 1) is inspired by Sanger's rule [35] for streaming PCA, but the paper notes that a rigorous analysis of the joint multi-neuron dynamics remains open, providing only a proof-of-concept simulation showing that slower learning rates (10⁻⁴ vs. 10⁻³) reduce the plateau in Frobenius error ‖P(k) − I‖²/2 from ~1–2 to near zero over 4×10⁴ iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard STDP analysis paradigm (ensemble averages, master equations, or slow-learning-rate ODE approximations) by recasting a biologically motivated pair-based STDP rule as noisy gradient descent on the probability simplex. The convergence proof directly builds on Mertikopoulos et al.'s [27] framework for almost-sure convergence of noisy SGD in non-convex settings, refining their martingale concentration arguments to handle the specific structure of the STDP noise. The multi-output extension draws on Sanger's rule [35] for orthogonalization, and the continuous-time limit is identified as a replicator equation from evolutionary game theory, linking the analysis to the Fisher-information-metric / natural-gradient literature in information geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first rigorous exponential convergence guarantee for a discrete-time, biologically plausible STDP rule, going beyond ensemble averages to track the realized noise at every step. The key open questions include extending the analysis to multi-layer networks with inhibitory neurons, time-varying firing rates, and voltage-based STDP rules, all of which the authors identify as natural but mathematically challenging extensions. The weight-explosion issue (intrinsic to Hebbian learning) limits biological plausibility at long timescales, and the authors argue for a homeostatic phase following the Hebbian phase—a view supported by experimental evidence but not yet formalized in the theory. For the broader optimization community, the finding that constant noise injection does not prevent exponential convergence in this structured non-convex setting is a useful counterexample to the standard 'noise causes fluctuation around a minimizer' heuristic.
