## Problem

Standard set-to-vector neural models (DeepSets, Set Transformers) lack structural guarantees for set-containment tasks: they are neither monotone nor separable by construction, leading to false positives and false negatives when testing S ⊆ T via element-wise dominance. This paper formalizes the Monotone and Separating (MAS) property—requiring S ⊆ T iff F(S) ≤ F(T)—proves that finite-dimensional MAS functions do not exist for infinite ground sets (the practical case), and introduces a relaxed 'weakly MAS' framework with a corresponding neural architecture.

## Input / Output

The model consumes pairs of multisets S, T, where each multiset is an unordered collection of d-dimensional vectors (e.g., 768-d BERT word embeddings for text, 3D coordinates for point clouds, or 4-d Gaussian samples for synthetic data). It produces a fixed m-dimensional embedding per set (m=256 for synthetic, m=50 for text/point-cloud experiments), and containment is tested by checking element-wise dominance F(S) ≤ F(T). A scalar variant also outputs a single real value for monotone function approximation. The embedding dimension m is theoretically constrained: m ≥ |V| for unbounded cardinality on finite ground sets, and m ≥ 2k for bounded cardinality k.

## Method

MASNET is a DeepSets-style architecture F(S) = M₂(Σ_{x∈S} σ(M₁(x))) with two key architectural constraints: (1) M₂ is monotone (often identity) and σ is non-negative to guarantee pointwise monotonicity for all parameter choices; (2) σ is a 'hat activation'—non-negative, compactly supported, continuous (e.g., a parametric piecewise-linear triangle σ_{α,β,γ})—or a two-layer ReLU network that simulates a hat function, ensuring weak separability (for any S ⊄ T, some parameter w separates them). The paper proves these constructions are lower Hölder separable with respect to an asymmetric Earth Mover Distance Δ(S,T), providing stability: approximate containment implies approximate dominance. Training uses a fixed-margin hinge loss that directly enforces vector dominance for positive pairs and anti-dominance for negative pairs, rather than standard classification losses.

## Results

On text-based set containment (MSWEB, MSNBC, Amazon-Feeding, Amazon-Bedding), MASNET-ReLU achieves 97–99% accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline FlexSubNet (85–92%) by 5–13 points. On ModelNet40 point-cloud containment, MASNET-ReLU reaches 98% accuracy at |S|=128, compared to 84% for FlexSubNet and 52% for DeepSets. For monotone set function approximation, MASNET-Hat achieves the lowest MAE (0.00959 ± 0.00016), beating DeepSets (0.01024) and SetTransformer (0.01026).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASNET-ReLU dominates all four baselines across every dataset. Against DeepSets, the gap is largest on Amazon-Bedding (98% vs. 51%) and Amazon-Feeding (98% vs. 50%), where DeepSets is near chance level; on MSWEB the gap narrows to 11 points (99% vs. 88%). SetTransformer is the strongest standard set model, reaching 94% on MSNBC, but MASNET-ReLU still leads by 3 points there and by 10–21 points on the Amazon datasets. FlexSubNet, the best dedicated set-containment baseline, achieves 88–92% on text and 60–84% on point clouds, consistently trailing MASNET-ReLU by 5–13 points. Neural SFE performs comparably to DeepSets (near chance on Amazon, ~88% on MSWEB), confirming that neither standard permutation-invariant architectures nor prior set-containment methods match the inductive bias provided by the MAS constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

MASNET is architecturally derived from DeepSets (Zaheer et al., 2017), adopting the same per-element transform + sum + outer MLP structure, but constraining the activation and outer function to enforce monotonicity by construction. Theoretically, the paper extends Davidson and Dym's (2025) Hölder stability framework for multiset networks by replacing symmetric EMD with an asymmetric set distance and proving lower Hölder separability guarantees. The MAS property (joint monotonicity and separability) is positioned as strictly stronger than the injectivity studied in prior multiset representation work (Amir et al., 2023; Wagstaff et al., 2019), and the Erdős-Szekeres-based lower bounds connect to classical order-dimension results (Dushnik & Miller, 1941) that had not previously been applied to learnable set functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first complete existential characterization of MAS functions (dimension bounds, non-existence for infinite ground sets) and a practical neural architecture (MASNET) with provable weak-MAS and Hölder-separability guarantees, giving set-containment systems a principled inductive bias absent from standard DeepSets or Set Transformer baselines. Key limitations include: probabilistic separation guarantees hold only at random initialization and do not transfer to trained models; the universality result is restricted to finite ground sets; and the framework does not yet extend to subgraph isomorphism. Open directions include extending MAS-style constraints to graph neural networks, exploring alternative relaxations of separability, and closing the large gap between the logarithmic lower bound and the exponential-in-k upper bound on embedding dimension.
