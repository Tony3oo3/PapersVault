## Problem

Reduced-order PDE solvers that compress the spatial domain into a latent space face a trade-off: CNN-based encoders/decoders are memory-efficient but require regular grid discretization, while Transformer-based attention mechanisms handle irregularly sampled domains but incur high memory costs. CALM-PDE addresses this gap by introducing a convolution-based encoder-decoder that operates on arbitrarily discretized spatial domains without the quadratic memory overhead of attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255360/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an initial condition u⁰ evaluated at N arbitrary spatial points (regularly or irregularly sampled, 1D–3D) along with their coordinates, and outputs the PDE solution at a separate set of queried spatial points that need not coincide with the input points. The spatial domain is normalized to [0,1) and the number of input points can vary across samples (e.g., 1885 points on average for the cylinder dataset with changing geometry). Internally, the solution is compressed into a fixed-size latent representation Z ∈ ℝ^{l×d} whose dimension is independent of N, enabling efficient latent time-stepping.

## Method

The core building block is the CALM layer, a continuous convolution where the kernel is a 2-layer MLP over Random Fourier Features of the translation vector (a−α), constrained to a percentile-based epsilon neighborhood with softmax distance weighting (locality inductive bias), and modulated per-query-point via FiLM-style scaling and shifting so co-located query points can extract different features. Query points are learnable parameters optimized via gradient descent, allowing the model to adaptively concentrate sampling in regions of high spatial variation (e.g., near obstacles). The full architecture stacks CALM layers as an encoder (reducing spatial points, increasing channels), a Transformer processor that evolves latent tokens autoregressively (interpretable as a NODE with explicit Euler integration), and a decoder (increasing spatial points, reducing channels) that queries the solution at arbitrary locations. Training is end-to-end with a curriculum strategy that gradually increases trajectory length and randomized starting points along trajectories.

## Results

On regularly sampled spatial domains, CALM-PDE achieves the lowest relative L2 error on 2 out of 4 benchmarks: 0.0246 on 1D Burgers' (ν=1e-3) and 0.0301 on 2D Navier-Stokes (ν=1e-4), outperforming all baselines. On 2D Navier-Stokes (ν=1e-5), CALM-PDE (0.1033) is third behind LNO (0.0789) and F-FNO (0.0844), and on 3D Navier-Stokes it ranks third (0.6761) behind F-FNO (0.6466) and OFormer (0.6719). On irregularly sampled domains, CALM-PDE achieves 0.0515 on 2D Airfoil (third, behind AROMA 0.0372 and Geo-FNO 0.0388) and 0.1186 on 2D Cylinder (second, behind AROMA 0.1139). In terms of efficiency, CALM-PDE requires 14,147 MB memory and 138.25 ms/batch during training, compared to 61,191 MB and 1,196.57 ms for LNO and 43,807 MB and 786.36 ms for OFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CALM-PDE outperforms all six baselines on 1D Burgers' (0.0246 vs. best baseline LNO at 0.0309) and 2D Navier-Stokes ν=1e-4 (0.0301 vs. OFormer at 0.0380), with margins of 26% and 21% respectively. On 2D Navier-Stokes ν=1e-5, LNO (0.0789) and F-FNO (0.0844) both beat CALM-PDE (0.1033) by 24% and 18%, while on 3D Navier-Stokes, F-FNO (0.6466) and OFormer (0.6719) edge out CALM-PDE (0.6761) by 4% and 1%. On irregular meshes, AROMA dominates both benchmarks (0.0372 on Airfoil, 0.1139 on Cylinder), with CALM-PDE placing third on Airfoil (0.0515) and second on Cylinder (0.1186, only 4% behind AROMA). Transformer-based baselines OFormer and LNO are consistently outperformed by CALM-PDE on irregular domains, while FNO and F-FNO (which rely on FFT and require regular grids) degrade significantly on irregular meshes. In terms of efficiency, CALM-PDE is ~8.7× faster and ~4.3× less memory-intensive than LNO during training, and ~5.7× faster and ~3.1× less memory-intensive than OFormer.

## Relation to Prior Work

CALM-PDE builds on deep parametric continuous convolutional neural networks (Wang et al., 2018), which approximate convolution kernels with MLPs and integrals with Monte Carlo sampling, originally developed for point cloud segmentation. It departs from the dominant Transformer/attention-based approaches for irregularly sampled PDEs (AROMA, LNO, UPT) by replacing cross-attention in the encoder and decoder with continuous convolutions that have a locality inductive bias (epsilon neighborhood + distance weighting), while retaining a Transformer only for the latent time-stepping processor. The learnable query points with kernel modulation extend the continuous convolution framework by allowing the model to adaptively choose where to sample the spatial domain, a capability absent in both fixed-grid CNNs (LE-PDE) and position-attention methods (PIT) that use predefined latent grids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CALM-PDE offers a practical middle ground between memory-efficient CNNs (limited to regular grids) and flexible but memory-hungry Transformer-based methods for irregularly sampled PDEs, achieving ~5–8× lower inference time and ~3–4× less training memory than OFormer and LNO while remaining competitive in accuracy. However, the model does not consistently outperform all baselines (AROMA wins on airfoil; F-FNO and LNO win on 3D Navier-Stokes), and the authors acknowledge that even at 64³ resolution the method is not yet efficient enough for real-world domains with millions of points. Open questions include whether dynamic query points that adapt to the initial condition or geometry, and improvements for high-frequency content, can close the remaining accuracy gap and extend practical applicability.
