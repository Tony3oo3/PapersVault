## Problem

Existing differentiable vector graphics rasterizers, particularly DiffVG, rely on computationally intensive boundary sampling and gradient derivation via the Reynolds transport theorem, making them slow (hours for 2K images) and limiting their scalability to high-resolution or complex scenes. This paper addresses the need for a differentiable VG representation that achieves order-of-magnitude speedup in both forward and backward rasterization while maintaining or improving rendering fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251717/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB raster image (tested at 2K, 4K, and 8K resolutions) and produces a set of parametric Bezier curves—open curves composed of three connected cubic segments (10 control points each) or closed curves using a paired Bezier structure with interpolated interior curves. The optimized curves can be directly exported to standard SVG XML format, enabling seamless integration with existing vector editing tools like Adobe Illustrator. The representation supports arbitrary-resolution re-rendering by proportionally increasing the Gaussian sampling density.

## Method

Bezier Splatting samples 2D Gaussians uniformly along Bezier curves (and along interpolated interior curves for closed regions via a paired Bezier structure), then rasterizes them using the efficient tile-based Gaussian splatting pipeline with alpha-blending for proper occlusion. The key insight is that 2D Gaussians inherently provide direct positional gradients at object boundaries through their differentiable formulation, eliminating DiffVG's expensive boundary sampling and gradient computation steps. For closed curves, a paired Bezier curve structure linearly interpolates control points between two boundary curves to create a dense strip of intermediate curves, with Gaussian scales derived from local point spacing and rotations aligned to curve tangents. An adaptive pruning and densification strategy dynamically removes low-opacity, small-area, or redundant-overlapping curves while inserting new curves into high-error regions, creating a 'global receptive field' that helps optimization escape local minima. Training minimizes an L2 reconstruction loss plus a Xing convexity loss using Adam over 10K–15K iterations, achieving 31× faster forward and 149× faster backward passes compared to DiffVG for open curves.

## Results

On DIV2K with 1024 open curves, Bezier Splatting achieves SSIM 0.699, PSNR 25.45, and LPIPS 0.448 in 3.2 min, outperforming DiffVG (SSIM 0.616, PSNR 22.62, LPIPS 0.517, 30.6 min). For 1024 closed curves, it reaches SSIM 0.639, PSNR 23.45, LPIPS 0.507 in 8.6 min, beating DiffVG (25.1 min) and LIVE (5.1 h) in optimization time while matching or exceeding their fidelity. In per-step rasterization timing on a 2040×1344 image with 2048 curves, Bezier Splatting is 31.4× faster in the forward pass and 149.2× faster in the backward pass for open curves compared to DiffVG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffVG, Bezier Splatting wins on every metric for both open and closed curves at all curve counts, with the largest gap at 1024 open curves (PSNR 25.45 vs 22.62, a 2.8 dB improvement) while being roughly 10× faster to optimize (3.2 min vs 30.6 min). Against LIVE, Bezier Splatting achieves higher PSNR (23.45 vs 23.11) and comparable LPIPS (0.507 vs 0.495) for 1024 closed curves, though LIVE edges ahead on SSIM (0.648 vs 0.639); critically, Bezier Splatting completes optimization in 8.6 min versus LIVE's 5.1 hours. Against LIVSS, Bezier Splatting substantially outperforms on PSNR (23.45 vs 19.83) and LPIPS (0.507 vs 0.517) but trails on SSIM (0.639 vs 0.678), and is ~10× faster (8.6 min vs 1.4 h). Against Adobe Image Trace (conventional, non-differentiable), Bezier Splatting achieves a higher average PSNR of 25.73 dB versus 24.90 dB across five DIV2K images at comparable parameter budgets (~20K).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the 2D Gaussian Splatting framework (originally developed for 3D novel view synthesis and 2D image representation) as a differentiable rasterizer for Bezier curves, replacing DiffVG's per-pixel boundary sampling and Reynolds transport theorem gradient computation with an efficient tile-based splatting pipeline. It builds directly on DiffVG's parametric Bezier curve formulation and LIVE's layer-wise vectorization and Xing loss, but departs from the DiffVG lineage by eliminating the BVH tree and per-pixel equation solving that made prior methods slow. The paired Bezier curve structure for closed-curve interior sampling and the modified pruning/densification strategy (which rejects 3DGS-style size-based pruning as incompatible with VG's large uniform regions) are novel contributions specific to the VG setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30×–150× rasterization speedup reduces high-resolution VG optimization from hours to minutes, making differentiable vectorization practical for interactive and large-scale applications. SVG XML compatibility ensures the output integrates seamlessly with existing professional tools (Adobe Illustrator, etc.), bridging the gap between research methods and production pipelines. Key limitations include the convexity constraint on closed curves (via Xing loss) that restricts representational capacity, and the higher cost of closed-curve color filling relative to open strokes. Open directions include leveraging the efficiency for text-to-VG generation and text-guided editing, and removing the convexity constraint to handle non-convex shapes natively.
