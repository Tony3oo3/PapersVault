## Problem

Verifying estimation-collapsibility in high-dimensional Bayesian networks is computationally prohibitive under the existing DAG-based criterion (sequential c-removability), which requires iterative vertex searches with O(|V|^4) worst-case complexity. Moreover, practical causal discovery from observational data yields only a CPDAG (Markov equivalence class) rather than a specific DAG, leaving no established criterion for checking collapsibility directly on the CPDAG.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254726/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a CPDAG G* = (V, E*)—typically obtained from causal discovery on observational data—and a target vertex subset M ⊆ V representing variables to marginalize out. It returns a boolean verdict on whether M is a sequential c-simplicial set in G*, which under the non-trivial model assumption is equivalent to estimation-collapsibility over M. The algorithm operates on the chain components (undirected chordal subgraphs) of the CPDAG, and the checks across different chain components are independent and can be executed in parallel.

## Method

The paper introduces sequential c-simplicial sets—a vertex is c-simplicial if it has no directed children and its undirected neighbors form a complete subgraph—and proves (Theorem 1) that M is sequential c-simplicial in the CPDAG if and only if the model is estimation-collapsible over M, unifying prior DAG and undirected-graph criteria. The key algorithmic insight (Theorem 2) is an order-free characterization: M is sequential c-simplicial iff ch_{G*}(M) = ∅ and each connected component of M ∩ τ_i is a simplicial set within the i-th chain component τ_i, eliminating the need for iterative vertex removal. The DSCS algorithm exploits this by first checking the empty-children condition, then decomposing the CPDAG into chain components, and finally verifying simpliciality of each M-component within its chordal subgraph using maximum cardinality search for linear-time clique verification, achieving overall O(|V| + |E*|) complexity.

## Results

The DSCS algorithm achieves a worst-case complexity of O(|V| + |E*|) compared to O(|V|^4) for the prior DCR (Detecting Sequential C-removability) approach. In experiments on Erdős-Rényi graphs, DSCS is more than 530× faster than DCR at n=500, p=0.1, |M|=450, and more than 4000× faster at n=1500, p=0.1, |M|=1350. For larger graphs (n up to 10,000), DCR becomes intractable while DSCS remains feasible, and the speedup advantage grows with n, p, and |M|.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is DCR (Detecting Sequential C-removability), the iterative vertex-removal algorithm from Xie & Geng [28] that checks collapsibility directly on a DAG. DSCS consistently outperforms DCR across all tested configurations of graph size n, edge probability p, and subset size |M|. The advantage is modest at small scales but becomes dramatic as the graph grows: at n=500 with p=0.1 and |M|=450, DSCS is over 530× faster, and at n=1500 with the same p and |M|=1350, the speedup exceeds 4000×. DCR's runtime grows rapidly with both p and |M| due to its O(|V|^4) worst-case complexity, while DSCS exhibits much slower growth consistent with its O(|V|+|E*|) bound. For n ≥ 2000, DCR simply cannot finish within practical time limits, whereas DSCS completes all runs, making it the only viable option for high-dimensional Bayesian networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the estimation-collapsibility verification problem from the DAG setting (Xie & Geng [28], sequential c-removability) and the undirected graph setting (Asmussen & Edwards [23], Frydenberg [25], simplicial sets) to the CPDAG framework, which is what causal discovery algorithms actually produce. The key conceptual move is introducing 'sequential c-simplicial sets' as a unified criterion that reduces to c-removability when the CPDAG is a DAG and to simplicial sets when it is a chordal graph. The DSCS algorithm then exploits the chain-component structure of CPDAGs to replace the O(|V|^4) iterative vertex-removal procedure with an order-free, O(|V|+|E*|) parallel check.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In practice, causal discovery yields a Markov equivalence class (CPDAG), not a single DAG, so the prior DAG-based criterion was inapplicable to the most common real-world scenario. The O(|V|+|E*|) complexity makes collapsibility verification feasible for networks with tens of thousands of variables (e.g., the 20,000+ gene networks in TCNG), enabling researchers to safely reduce model dimensionality without risking Simpson-paradox-type errors. Open questions include extending DSCS to interventional data, dynamic Bayesian networks, and settings where the CPDAG is only partially or approximately learned.
