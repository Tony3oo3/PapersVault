## Problem

No unified real-world benchmark exists for simultaneously evaluating domain generalization and open-ended 3D object detection in autonomous driving, and existing 3D datasets contain only a handful of common categories with fixed-label evaluation metrics. Additionally, current open-world perception models suffer from low precision on common objects, making them impractical as replacements for specialized detectors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254084/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The proposed 3D open-world detector consumes multi-camera images (224×224 crops per 2D proposal), optional LiDAR point clouds, and 2D bounding boxes with natural language labels from a frozen open-world 2D detector (e.g., GenerateU or YOLO-World). It outputs 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation) paired with free-form semantic category labels. The OpenAD benchmark itself spans 2,000 temporal scenes from five datasets (Argoverse 2, KITTI, nuScenes, ONCE, Waymo) with 19,761 annotated objects across 206 corner-case categories, each tagged as seen/unseen per source dataset.

## Method

The core framework is a vision-centric 2D-to-3D BBox Converter that lifts frozen 2D open-world proposals into 3D space using only a few trainable parameters. A dual-branch architecture extracts (1) pseudo-point features via a Swin-tiny Partial Encoder and a trainable MLP Depth Net that generates a depth map, projects it into pseudo point clouds refined by LiDAR, and encodes them with PointNet; and (2) convolutional features from the depth and feature maps within the 2D box via global pooling. The concatenated features feed an MLP that regresses the 3D box. To address the precision gap of open-world models, the paper further introduces a General-Specialized Fusion strategy that aligns confidence scores between an open-world detector and a specialized detector, then applies dual-threshold NMS (IoU + CLIP text-embedding cosine similarity) to merge predictions, yielding consistent AP and AR gains over either model alone.

## Results

On the OpenAD 3D benchmark, the proposed vision-centric baseline (OpenAD-Y with LiDAR+Camera) achieves 15.54 AP and 36.07 AR, outperforming the prior 3D open-world method FnP (8.85 AP, 18.97 AR) by 6.7 AP and 17.1 AR. The best ensemble method (OpenAD-Ens: OpenAD-G + BEVFusion) reaches 16.30 AP and 48.25 AR, substantially surpassing both the strongest specialized model (BEVFormer v2: 14.64 AP, 33.13 AR) and the strongest open-world model. For 2D detection, the ensemble (YOLO-World + Co-DETR Swin-L) achieves 16.94 AP and 34.38 AR, the best overall 2D result on OpenAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed vision-centric 3D baseline (OpenAD-Y, LC) outperforms the prior 3D open-world method FnP [18] by 6.7 AP (15.54 vs. 8.85) and 17.1 AR (36.07 vs. 18.97), demonstrating the advantage of leveraging a strong 2D open-world detector (YOLO-World) over LiDAR-only pseudo-labeling. Among specialized 3D models, BEVFormer v2 [62] and BEVFusion [37] achieve the highest in-domain AR_nusc_seen (56.63 and 59.93 respectively) but have near-zero AR_nusc_unseen (0.00), confirming they cannot detect unseen categories. The proposed ensemble (OpenAD-Ens: OpenAD-G + BEVFusion) achieves the best overall 3D performance at 16.30 AP and 48.25 AR, improving over BEVFusion alone by 0.73 AP and 14.75 AR while also recovering unseen-category recall (16.85 AR_others_unseen vs. 0.00). In 2D, the ensemble of YOLO-World + Co-DETR (Swin-L) [72] reaches 16.94 AP and 34.38 AR, surpassing both the best open-world 2D model (GroundingDINO [41]: 8.52 AP, 26.67 AR) and the best specialized 2D model (Co-DETR alone: 16.21 AP, 27.76 AR). The ablation in Table 5 confirms that General-Specialized Fusion consistently improves both AP and AR over naive NMS ensembles of two general or two specialized models, validating the complementary nature of the two model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by constructing the first real-world 3D open-world benchmark for autonomous driving, departing from prior 3D open-world efforts that are either synthetic (AnoVox, CARLA-WildLife) or indoor (SUN-RGBD, ScanNet). Methodologically, it repurposes frozen 2D open-world detectors (YOLO-World, GenerateU, VL-SAM) as the semantic front-end for a lightweight 2D-to-3D bounding box converter, sidestepping the data scarcity that plagues direct 3D open-world training. The fusion strategy of combining general (open-world) and specialized (closed-set) detectors via dual-threshold NMS extends the ensemble literature by explicitly exploiting the complementary strengths of the two model families rather than merely averaging predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenAD fills a critical gap: prior 3D perception benchmarks presuppose fixed category sets, making them unable to evaluate a model's ability to recognize and localize genuinely novel objects in driving scenes. The empirical finding that in-domain SOTA improvements (e.g., SparseBEV) can coincide with poor domain generalization challenges the assumption that standard benchmark progress translates to real-world robustness. Key limitations include the benchmark's restriction to 2D/3D detection (no occupancy or segmentation), the relatively modest 2,000-scene scale, and the fact that open-world models still trail specialized models on common categories, leaving the practical deployment question open.
