## Problem

The Prompting Decision Transformer (PDT) samples trajectory prompts uniformly from expert demonstrations at inference time, ignoring that not all prompts are equally informative for task identification. Prior prompt-tuning methods (hill-climbing, generative perturbation) treat prompts as flat, unstructured inputs, leading to poor scaling with prompt size and state/action-space dimensionality, and failing in discrete settings. This paper asks whether prompt selection can be formulated as a structured, sample-efficient optimization problem that scales linearly rather than combinatorially with prompt length.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained PDT backbone, a small pool of expert demonstration trajectories (limited to ~10 high-return trajectories per task to keep the segment pool tractable), and an MDP simulator for online rollouts. At each bandit round, it outputs a trajectory prompt composed of J segments of length H (each segment being a sequence of (return-to-go, state, action) triplets), which is prepended to the most recent L transitions to form the PDT input. The PDT itself can optionally serve as a feature extractor, mapping each raw segment to a fixed-dimensional latent embedding so that reward model input size remains constant regardless of state/action dimensionality.

## Method

The core contribution is a structured contextual multi-armed bandit that factorizes prompt construction across J segment positions, maintaining J independent reward models (small MLPs) instead of one model per full prompt, thereby reducing the search space from combinatorial in J to linear. At each round, every reward model scores all candidate segments for its position, producing a J×|P| prediction matrix from which the top-scoring segments are selected (subject to an exploration strategy: ε-greedy, UCB, or Thompson Sampling). The pre-trained PDT is leveraged as a frozen feature extractor, encoding raw trajectory segments into fixed-size token embeddings that serve as arm features for the reward models, enabling deployment in high-dimensional (e.g., pixel-based) settings. The paper proves a cumulative regret bound of O(√(K log|P|) + Kε), where ε captures bounded inter-segment interaction, and empirically validates the near-independence assumption via attention-weight analysis showing PDT attends predominantly to the single most informative segment regardless of its position.

## Results

On in-distribution MuJoCo and Meta-World tasks, the bandit-based prompt-tuning method (Thompson Sampling) consistently improves frozen PDT performance: on MuJoCo Ant (J=1, H=5), TS achieves 835.4±171.3 mean return versus 694.4±227.0 for un-tuned PDT, surpassing the single-task CQL oracle (760.5±288.2). On MuJoCo Half Cheetah (J=2, H=20), TS reaches −26.3±10.1, approaching the CQL oracle (−25.8±12.5) and substantially outperforming un-tuned PDT (−42.7±1.3). On Meta-World Pick-Place, gains are more modest (TS: 556.1±24.6 vs. PDT: 551.6±26.1) but still consistent. OOD generalization (Table 2) shows similar improvements, with TS achieving −29.3±21.3 on Half Cheetah OOD versus −64.8±36.9 for un-tuned PDT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the un-tuned PDT baseline (uniform prompt sampling), the bandit method wins across all six environment/configuration settings, with the largest gains on MuJoCo Ant (TS: 835.4 vs. 694.4, a ~20% improvement) and MuJoCo Half Cheetah (TS: −26.3 vs. −42.7, a ~38% reduction in negative return). Against the single-task CQL oracle, the bandit method surpasses it on MuJoCo Ant (TS: 835.4 vs. 760.5) and nearly matches it on Half Cheetah (−26.3 vs. −25.8), while on Meta-World Pick-Place both the oracle and bandit methods perform similarly (~555). Against ZORankSGD (Hu et al., 2023), the bandit method is consistently superior, particularly on Half Cheetah where ZORankSGD achieves only −34.8 (J=2) versus TS's −26.3, and on Ant where ZORankSGD (735.5) trails TS (835.4) by over 100 return. Against hill-climbing, the bandit method also wins across all settings with notably lower variance (e.g., TS: ±10.1 vs. hill-climbing: ±22.8 on Half Cheetah J=2). The Transformer-encoded variants (marked Ψ) achieve comparable returns to raw-segment variants, confirming the PDT as a viable fixed-dimension feature extractor for scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Prompting Decision Transformer (PDT) framework by replacing its uniform random prompt sampling with a structured contextual bandit that learns to select informative trajectory segments at inference time. It departs from prior PDT prompt-tuning methods (Hu et al. 2023/2024, Yuan et al. 2024) that treat prompts as flat, unstructured inputs or rely on generative/noise-based perturbation, instead exploiting the modular J-segment structure of prompts to reduce the search from combinatorial to linear complexity. The use of the frozen PDT backbone as a feature extractor for the bandit's reward models is a practical departure from operating directly on raw MDP modalities, and the regret analysis provides theoretical grounding absent in prior prompt-tuning work for decision transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured bandit architecture offers a principled, theoretically-grounded alternative to ad-hoc prompt optimization in offline multi-task RL, with a clean regret bound that scales sublinearly in rounds. The demonstrated robustness to non-expert demonstration data (achieving near-optimal performance with as little as 10% expert trajectories) broadens the practical applicability of PDT beyond settings with abundant expert data. Key open questions include how the method scales when the demonstration pool grows large (the combinatorial search space over segments becomes impractical), whether the segment-independence assumption holds in more complex or partially observable MDPs, and how meta-learning techniques (e.g., In-Context RL) could be integrated into PDT pretraining to further reduce the gap between pre-training and adaptation.
