## Problem

Existing test-time scaling (TTS) research focuses on single-stage tasks, yet many real-world applications (retrieval-then-generation QA, software development, task automation) are multi-stage workflows where each subtask requires a model with specific capabilities. The compute-optimal allocation problem in this setting is intractable by brute force due to the combinatorial model-and-budget search space (up to ~10^6 configurations) and the interdependence between subtasks, where the quality of one subtask's output shifts the optimal budget and model choice of downstream subtasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-stage task definition (a sequence of n subtasks, each with a candidate model set ranging from 2B to 72B parameters), a total compute budget B expressed in normalized FLOPs units (where 1 unit equals one inference pass of a 3B model on the lowest-cost task), and a small training set (50 samples) for in-loop evaluation. It outputs a per-subtask configuration (M_i, B_i) specifying which model to use and how many repeated-sampling passes to allocate, such that sum(B_i) = B and overall task performance is maximized. The budget normalization accounts for differences in model size, prompt length, and generation length across subtasks via a closed-form FLOPs conversion (Eq. 4).

## Method

AgentTTS is an LLM-agent-based iterative search framework with three components: an Agent (GPT-o3-mini) that generates candidate trials and exploration guidelines, an Environment that executes trials on actual task platforms and returns performance feedback, and an Archive that stores the evolving history of trials, guidelines, and scores. The search is guided by three empirical insights derived from pilot experiments: (1) subtask-specific model preferences (large models for retrieval, small models for QA) used to initialize the search; (2) per-subtask optimal budgets beyond which additional sampling yields diminishing or negative returns, used to constrain the search direction; and (3) inter-subtask dependencies where upstream quality shifts downstream scaling dynamics, used to prompt the LLM to explore allocation trade-offs. The test-time scaling mode is repeated sampling with fusion (temperature 0.9, same LLM generates and fuses candidates), and the agent iteratively refines trials until a stopping criterion is met, typically converging in fewer than 10 trials versus 30–50 for baselines.

## Results

AgentTTS achieves the best or tied-best test-set performance across all six datasets while requiring substantially fewer search iterations. On 2WikiMultiHopQA, it reaches 0.72 EM in just 2.5 hours of search time, outperforming all baselines (best baseline: 0.70 EM at 8.3–12.5 h). On WebQSP, it attains 0.89 EM in 7.8 h versus 37.7–48.1 h for LLM-based baselines. On TaskBench, it achieves 0.53 p-F1, exceeding Random search (0.40) and BO (0.52). The method also demonstrates superior generalization: despite similar training-set scores, AgentTTS often leads by 1–2% on the 500-sample test set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bayesian Optimization (BO), AgentTTS wins on test performance for 2Wiki (0.72 vs. 0.60 EM), Hotpot (0.74 vs. 0.71), WebQSP (0.89 vs. 0.85), and TaskBench (0.53 vs. 0.52 p-F1), while tying on CWQ (0.78) and ChatDev (0.75); BO also fails to report search times, indicating it frequently fails to converge within 50 trials. Against MLCopilot, AgentTTS matches test performance on CWQ (0.78), TaskBench (0.53), and ChatDev (0.75), edges ahead on 2Wiki (0.72 vs. 0.70) and Hotpot (0.74 vs. 0.72), and ties on WebQSP (0.89 vs. 0.88), while reducing search time by 2–5× (e.g., 2.5 h vs. 12.5 h on 2Wiki, 7.8 h vs. 20.8 h on WebQSP). Against AgentHPO, AgentTTS ties on Hotpot (0.74), CWQ (0.78), and WebQSP (0.89), wins on 2Wiki (0.72 vs. 0.70) and TaskBench (0.53 vs. 0.49), and ties on ChatDev (0.75 vs. 0.74), again with 2–6× lower search time (e.g., 11.1 h vs. 37.4 h on CWQ, 7.8 h vs. 48.1 h on WebQSP). Overall, AgentTTS is the only method that completes the search within 50 trials on all six datasets, and its insight-guided initialization and guideline generation yield both faster convergence and better generalization to the held-out test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends test-time scaling (TTS) from single-stage tasks (math, code generation) to multi-stage complex tasks with heterogeneous subtasks, where model selection and budget allocation are interdependent. Methodologically, it builds on the LLM-agent-based hyperparameter optimization paradigm (AgentHPO, MLCopilot) but departs by embedding domain-specific empirical insights about TTS behavior (subtask model preferences, diminishing returns, inter-subtask dependencies) directly into the agent's search process, rather than relying on generic HPO heuristics. The repeated-sampling-with-fusion scaling mode follows prior work on parallel TTS, while the FLOPs-based budget normalization framework is novel in unifying cost comparisons across models and tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-stage LLM pipelines (RAG, software development, task automation) are increasingly common in production, yet compute allocation across their subtasks has been largely unaddressed. The three empirical insights—particularly the inter-subtask dependency (Insight 3), where upstream retrieval quality shifts the optimal downstream budget—are actionable findings that could inform pipeline design beyond this specific framework. Open questions include scalability to more than 3–4 subtasks, dynamic budget reallocation during execution, and whether the insights hold for non-LLM components (e.g., embedding models, retrievers). The evaluation is limited to 50-sample training sets for search, and the method's advantage over baselines narrows on some datasets (e.g., TaskBench at 64.3h search time).
