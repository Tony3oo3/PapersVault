## Problem

AI systems for trauma-focused mental health support are hindered by the scarcity of real-world therapeutic conversation data, which is restricted by privacy regulations, small sample sizes, and insufficient demographic diversity. Existing datasets fail to capture the structured six-phase progression of Prolonged Exposure (PE) therapy or the full range of trauma types and associated behaviors needed to train clinically faithful models.

## Input / Output

The generation pipeline consumes structured metadata profiles (client demographics, co-occurring conditions, 1–3 trauma-related behaviors, therapist attributes, a trauma type from 12 categories, and a session topic from 20 options) and produces 3,000 therapist–client dialogue transcripts organized into six PE therapy phases per case. For the benchmark, each model receives the six conversation segments of a session and must output a single emotional intensity score (1–10) per phase, yielding a six-point trajectory that is compared against a Claude Sonnet 3.5 zero-shot baseline using Pearson correlation, DTW distance, and RMSE.

## Method

The dataset is generated with Claude Sonnet 3.5 using six phase-specific prompt templates (P5–P11) that encode PE therapy protocol instructions—e.g., concise therapist utterances, elaborate client narration, SUDS monitoring cadence, and reinforcing-comment timing—while deterministic and probabilistic sampling methods construct diverse client/therapist profiles across age, gender, ethnicity, and trauma type. A companion emotional-trajectory benchmark establishes a zero-shot baseline by having Claude Sonnet 3.5 rate each of the six phases on a 1–10 distress scale, then evaluates eight comparison models (Mistral Large, Amazon Nova Pro, Llama 3/3.1 70B/8B, Mistral 7B, Mistral Small) via Bedrock API using default settings. Performance is summarized with an Absolute Composite Score (S_abs) that normalizes Pearson, DTW, and RMSE to [0,1] with direction alignment and averages them, enabling a single comparable metric. Clinical fidelity was validated by seven practicing therapists (6–30 years experience) who rated content depth, perceived value, appropriateness, and patient engagement on two full synthetic transcripts.

## Results

On the emotional trajectory benchmark (500 PE therapy sessions), Mistral Large achieves the highest Absolute Composite Score (S_abs = 0.74) with Pearson correlation of 0.80, DTW of 2.38, and RMSE of 1.07 against the Claude Sonnet 3.5 zero-shot baseline. Amazon Nova Pro and Llama 3 70B Instruct tie for second place at S_abs = 0.69. Performance degrades with model size within the same family: Mistral Small (S_abs = 0.59) underperforms Mistral 7B Instruct (S_abs = 0.63), and Llama 3 8B (0.61) trails Llama 3 70B (0.69), indicating that instruction-following capability matters more than raw parameter count for this task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All models are evaluated against a Claude Sonnet 3.5 zero-shot baseline that rates emotional intensity (1–10) across six PE therapy phases. Mistral Large leads on every individual metric (Pearson 0.80, DTW 2.38, RMSE 1.07) and the composite S_abs (0.74), outperforming the next-best models (Amazon Nova Pro, Llama 3 70B) by 0.05 in S_abs. A notable finding is that Mistral Small (S_abs = 0.59) performs worse than the smaller Mistral 7B Instruct (0.63), which the authors attribute to weaker instruction-following rather than model capacity. Within the Llama family, the 70B variants consistently outperform the 8B variants by 0.06–0.08 in S_abs, with Llama 3.1 70B (0.67) slightly ahead of Llama 3 70B (0.69) on DTW but behind on Pearson. The benchmark successfully differentiates models across architectures and sizes, with standard deviations (e.g., ±0.14 for Mistral Large vs. ±0.23 for Llama 3 8B) reflecting greater consistency in stronger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of synthetic clinical data generation and structured psychotherapy modeling. Prior work on LLM-generated clinical data (Wu et al. for PTSD diagnosis, TIDE for empathetic validation) produced unstructured or single-purpose transcripts, while demographic-diversity studies (Mori et al., Lozoya et al.) addressed representation without therapeutic protocol alignment. The Thousand Voices dataset departs from these by embedding generation within the six-phase Prolonged Exposure protocol (orientation → imaginal exposure → SUDS monitoring → reinforcement → eliciting → processing), creating a structured therapeutic arc rather than a flat clinical interview. The accompanying emotional trajectory benchmark, using Pearson/DTW/RMSE against a Claude Sonnet 3.5 zero-shot baseline, has no direct predecessor in the PE-therapy literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a genuine scarcity: no prior large-scale resource captures the full PE session progression with diverse demographics, 20 trauma types, and 10 trauma-related behaviors, and its privacy-preserving synthetic nature circumvents the ethical barriers that limit real-world therapy transcript collection. However, the benchmark's reliance on an AI-generated (not human-rated) baseline, the predefined categorical structure that may not capture intersectional cultural dynamics in trauma expression, and the single-session (non-longitudinal) design leave important open questions about ecological validity. Future work on fine-grained emotion detection, multimodal signals, and culturally grounded generation methods would strengthen the resource's applicability to real clinical deployment.
