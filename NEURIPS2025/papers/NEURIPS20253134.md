## Problem

The folklore 2-approximation algorithm for dynamic diameter (pick a random anchor, track its furthest neighbor) is fragile against an adaptive adversary: if the adversary deletes the anchor, the guarantee collapses and the algorithm must restart in Ω(n) time. No prior efficient fully-dynamic algorithm achieved a non-trivial constant approximation with worst-case poly(d, log n) update time against a randomness-adaptive adversary in high dimensions, where the adversary observes all random bits used by the algorithm up to the current time.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of n insertions and deletions on a point set P ⊂ R^d (where d is not constant and may be large), and must answer diameter queries at any point in time. It outputs a 2-approximate diameter estimate (equivalently a minimum enclosing ball) with worst-case update time O(d^5 log^1.5(d) log^1.5(n/δ)), and a (4+ε)-approximate k-center clustering with amortized update time k^2.5 d · poly(ε^{-1}, log n). The guarantees hold with probability at least 1−δ against a randomness-adaptive adversary who sees all prior random choices, and the algorithm does not require knowing n in advance.

## Method

The key insight is to replace the fragile random anchor with an ε-robust representative point constructed via centerpoints (points of high Tukey depth), which remain representative of the surviving point set even after O(ε|P|) adversarial deletions. This is the first application of centerpoints in dynamic algorithms: a 1/(3d²)-centerpoint computed via iterated Radon points (Har-Peled and Jones, 2020) guarantees that the point stays in the convex hull of the remaining set, yielding a 2-approximation via the triangle inequality. A de-amortization technique spreads the expensive centerpoint recomputation across multiple update steps (starting at 3/4 of the validity window), converting the amortized bound into a worst-case guarantee. For k-center, the algorithm samples Õ(k) points and selects the one with the highest local density as a robust center, with a separate sampling strategy (reducing to Õ(√k) samples and testing against the full dataset) for the regime k > n^{2/3}.

## Results

For the diameter problem in d-dimensional Euclidean space, the paper achieves a 2-approximation with worst-case update time O(d⁵ log^{1.5}(d) log^{1.5}(n/δ)) against a randomness-adaptive adversary — the first such guarantee in high dimensions. For the k-center problem, the paper achieves a (4+ε)-approximation with amortized update time k^{2.5}·d·poly(log n, ε^{-1}, δ) against the same adversary model, improving the prior best adaptive-adversary bound of k⁶·d·poly(ε^{-1}, log n) by Biabani et al. (NeurIPS'24). The diameter result also extends to maintaining a minimum enclosing ball (1-center) with the same guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the folklore 2-approximation algorithm (O(d log n) amortized, oblivious adversary), this paper matches the approximation ratio but upgrades both the guarantee from amortized to worst-case and the adversary model from oblivious to randomness-adaptive, at the cost of a higher polynomial in d. Compared to Indyk (2003) and Zarrabi-Zadeh (2011), which achieve (1+ε)-approximations, this paper sacrifices approximation quality (2 vs. 1+ε) but obtains dramatically better update times (poly(d, log n) vs. n^{1/(1+ε)²} or ε^{-Θ(d)}) and handles a strictly stronger adversary. Against Chan and Pathak (2014), which achieves a (√2+ε)-approximation with O(d log n) amortized time under an oblivious adversary, this paper provides a worse approximation (2 vs. √2+ε) but with worst-case guarantees and adaptive-adversary robustness. For k-center clustering, the paper improves upon Biabani et al. (NeurIPS'24) by reducing the amortized update time from Õ(k⁶) to Õ(k^{2.5}) while maintaining the same (4+ε)-approximation and adaptive-adversary guarantee, representing a polynomial improvement in the dependence on k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard dynamic diameter approach (folklore 2-approximation via a random anchor point, as in Chan and Pathak 2014) by showing that randomness used for decision-making is exploitable by an adaptive adversary. Instead, it introduces centerpoints (Tukey depth theory from Har-Peled and Jones 2020, Clarkson et al. 1993) as the first application of this classical geometric concept in dynamic algorithms, providing provably robust representatives that survive adversarial deletions. For k-center, it builds directly on Biabani et al. (NeurIPS 2024), improving the amortized update time from Õ(k⁶) to Õ(k^{2.5}) through more careful random sampling and a two-regime analysis (k ≤ n^{2/3} vs. k > n^{2/3}). A de-amortization technique, inspired by Bernstein et al. (2021) for spanners, converts the Monte Carlo amortized guarantee into a worst-case bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first efficient fully-dynamic 2-approximation diameter algorithm in high dimensions that is robust against a randomness-adaptive (white-box) adversary, closing a gap where the fragile folklore algorithm remained the best known. The centerpoint-based robustness framework is a general technique the authors believe has broader potential for dynamic and streaming algorithms under adaptive adversaries. Open questions include whether the 2-approximation can be improved, whether the k-center update time can be further reduced, and how the gap between output-adaptive and randomness-adaptive adversaries manifests for other geometric problems. The results are purely theoretical with no experimental validation, and the worst-case update time of O(d⁵ log^{1.5}d log^{1.5}(n/δ)) may be impractical for very large d.
