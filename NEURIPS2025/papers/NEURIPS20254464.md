## Problem

Standard two-stage CATE meta-learners (e.g., DR-learner) minimize estimation error uniformly across the covariate space, but this can yield suboptimal thresholded treatment decisions when the second-stage model class is restricted (e.g., by regularization, fairness, or interpretability constraints). The paper proves that for any restricted function class, there exist ground-truth CATEs for which the best L² approximation produces the wrong sign near the decision boundary, leading to strictly lower policy value than an alternative function in the same class.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard causal inference data (X, A, Y) and first-stage nuisance estimates (μ₁, μ₀, π_b) to produce a re-targeted CATE function g(x) parameterized as a feed-forward neural network with tanh activations, alongside a separate adaptive indicator network α(x) with ReLU activations and a softplus+constant final layer ensuring α(x) > a > 0. The binary treatment decision is obtained by thresholding g(x) at zero, and the hyperparameter γ ∈ [0,1] controls the trade-off between CATE fidelity (γ=0) and pure policy-value maximization (γ=1).

## Method

The core contribution is a new second-stage loss (the γ-PT-CATE loss) that takes a convex combination of CATE MSE and the negative expected policy value of the thresholded policy, with the non-differentiable indicator 1(g>0) replaced by a covariate-adaptive sigmoid σ(α(X)·g(X)). The adaptive α(X) is learned as a separate neural network via a weighted binary cross-entropy objective that identifies regions where g has the wrong sign, allowing large α (sharp indicator) where the sign is correct and small α (smooth gradient) where correction is needed. Training follows a three-step iterative procedure: (1) initialize g by standard CATE regression (γ=0), (2) train α to detect sign-error regions, and (3) retrain g under the full PT-CATE loss with α frozen, repeating steps 2–3 until convergence. Theoretical results establish consistency of the pseudo-outcome-based loss and provide error-rate bounds showing doubly robust convergence for the DR pseudo-outcome, requiring only one of the propensity score or response functions to be well-estimated.

## Results

On the Hillstrom Email Marketing dataset (n=64,000), the PT-CATE policy with γ=0.98 achieves a policy value of 1.805 ± 0.006, a 24.45% improvement over the observational policy (1.450), compared to 19.83% for the standard DR-learner (γ=0, policy value 1.738 ± 0.045). On synthetic settings with restricted model classes (linear g in Setting A, regularized g in Setting B), increasing γ from 0 to 1 consistently decreases policy loss across all four pseudo-outcomes (PI, RA, IPW, DR) while only minimally increasing PEHE. The IPW and DR variants show higher variance due to propensity-score division, but the directional improvement in policy loss holds across all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its PT-CATE method against four standard two-stage CATE meta-learners (PI, RA, IPW, and DR-learner), all of which correspond to the proposed framework at γ=0. On the real-world Hillstrom dataset, the DR-learner baseline (γ=0) achieves a policy value of 1.738, while PT-CATE at γ=0.98 reaches 1.805, a relative gain of approximately 3.9% in policy value with only a marginal increase in PEHE. On synthetic Settings A and B, the same pattern holds across all four pseudo-outcomes: increasing γ monotonically reduces policy loss while PEHE increases only slightly, confirming that the re-targeting trades a small amount of CATE accuracy for meaningful decision-performance gains. The IPW and DR baselines exhibit noisier curves than PI and RA due to the higher variance of propensity-score-weighted pseudo-outcomes, but the proposed method still outperforms them on average policy loss at every γ>0. The paper does not benchmark against direct OPL methods (which correspond to γ=1 in their framework) as a separate baseline, noting that OPL is a special case of their approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed literatures: two-stage meta-learners for CATE estimation (DR-learner, RA-learner, IPW-learner) and direct off-policy learning (OPL) for policy optimization. It departs from the CATE estimation line by showing that the standard second-stage regression objective, while optimal for CATE accuracy, can yield suboptimal thresholded policies when the model class is restricted (e.g., via regularization or fairness constraints). The proposed PT-CATE objective and adaptive sigmoid approximation draw on classification-based policy learning ideas (Zhang et al., Bennett & Kallus) but reframe them within the two-stage meta-learner architecture, preserving the interpretability of CATE-based decisions that direct OPL methods sacrifice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The suboptimality theorem (Theorem 4.1) challenges the common practitioner assumption that minimizing CATE estimation error automatically yields optimal decisions, which is a meaningful conceptual correction for fields like medicine and public policy where CATE thresholding is standard practice. The PT-CATE framework provides a principled, tunable trade-off (via γ) between estimation fidelity and decision performance, with doubly robust convergence guarantees. Key limitations include applicability only under restricted model classes, restriction to binary treatments, and the need for domain-informed γ selection; open directions include extensions to continuous treatments, dynamic settings, and uncertainty quantification.
