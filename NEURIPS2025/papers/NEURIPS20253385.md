## Problem

RLHF with multiple reference models has shown practical utility but lacks a closed-form solution for the reverse KL-regularized objective; prior work (Le et al., 2024) only derived a lower-bound approximation. Consequently, no sample complexity guarantees exist for the multi-reference setting under either reverse or forward KL regularization, leaving the theoretical foundations of this increasingly important alignment paradigm unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x, K pre-trained reference policies {π_ref,i}, and a preference dataset of (prompt, chosen, rejected) triples. For reverse KL regularization, the effective reference is a generalized escort distribution (normalized geometric mean of the K references raised to their respective weights α_i); for forward KL, it is a weighted arithmetic mean. The output is a closed-form (RKL) or implicit (FKL) optimal policy distribution over response tokens, from which aligned text responses are sampled.

## Method

The key insight is that a weighted sum of reverse KL divergences against K reference policies decomposes, via the escort distribution identity, into a single KL divergence against the generalized escort reference plus a constant, yielding the exact closed-form solution: the optimal policy is proportional to the escort reference times exp(γr(x,y)). For forward KL regularization, the solution is implicit, with the policy inversely proportional to (Z̃ − r(x,y)) scaled by the arithmetic-mean reference. Sample complexity bounds are then derived using functional derivatives of the concave objective and the mean-value theorem on the softmax policy's sensitivity to reward, combined with concentration inequalities over a finite reward class. This yields O(1/n) sub-optimality gap for RKL and O(1/√n) for both RKL and FKL optimality gaps, with the RKL bound strictly tighter than prior single-reference results.

## Results

On the 7B UltraFeedback experiment (Table 2), the proposed multi-reference DPO achieves a 66.1% win rate against the preferred answer, substantially outperforming single-reference DPO with the SFT model (56.4%) and single-reference DPO with the 14B model (59.8%). In the online RL experiment on GSM8K (Figure 1a), the analytical multi-reference RKL objective outperforms both the MRPO approximation and the single-reference objective across all tested α values. In the offline RL experiment on UltraFeedback (Figure 1b), the exact analytical solution similarly surpasses the MRPO approximation and single-reference baseline in mean normalized reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines. First, against MRPO (Le et al., 2024), which provides only a lower-bound approximation of the multi-reference RLHF objective, the proposed exact analytical solution consistently outperforms MRPO in both the online GRPO experiment on GSM8K and the offline DPO experiment on UltraFeedback (Figure 1). Second, against single-reference DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), the multi-reference DPO achieves a 66.1% win rate on the 7B UltraFeedback benchmark, beating single-reference DPO with the SFT model by 9.7 percentage points (66.1% vs. 56.4%) and single-reference DPO with the 14B model by 6.3 percentage points (66.1% vs. 59.8%). The theoretical comparison (Table 1) further shows that the paper is the first to provide sample complexity bounds for both RKL and FKL sub-optimality and optimality gaps, matching the best-known O(1/n) rate for RKL sub-optimality gap while extending coverage to FKL settings previously unstudied.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-reference RLHF framework introduced by Le et al. (2024), who proposed a lower-bound approximation (MRPO) for the multi-reference objective, by deriving the first exact closed-form solution under reverse KL-regularization. The solution reveals that the optimal policy is a Gibbs tilt of the geometric mean (escort distribution) of reference models, reducing the multi-reference problem to a single-reference problem with a composite reference. The paper also extends the single-reference sample complexity analysis of Zhao et al. (2024) and Song et al. (2024) to the multi-reference setting, and provides the first theoretical treatment of forward KL-regularized RLHF, building on Wang et al.'s (2024a) FKL-DPO formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the open-source LLM ecosystem grows with diverse pre-trained models, multi-reference alignment becomes practically essential for leveraging collective model intelligence while avoiding single-model bias. The exact solution and O(1/n) sub-optimality gap bound under RKL provide the theoretical foundation needed to reason about when and how many reference models to use. Key limitations include the bounded-reward and finite-reward-class assumptions, and the coverage conditions that may be restrictive in practice. Open directions include extending to unbounded or sub-Gaussian rewards, general f-divergences, and preference models beyond Bradley-Terry.
