## Problem

Diffusion models incur high inference cost from repeated full-network denoising, and existing compression strategies fail to exploit temporal asymmetry: static pruning removes layers uniformly across all timesteps, while sample-wise dynamic methods (e.g., APTP) commit to a single sub-model for the entire trajectory, under-utilizing model capacity. Additionally, the common sequential pruning-then-fine-tuning pipeline creates a mismatch between fixed pruning decisions and evolving model weights, leading to sub-optimal recovery.

## Input / Output

ALTER takes a text prompt and a noisy latent as input to a pretrained diffusion UNet (SDv2.1 at 768×768 or DiT-XL/2 at 256×256) and produces a denoised latent at each timestep, ultimately yielding a generated RGB image. A key property is that the active sub-network changes per denoising step: at each timestep t, the temporal router selects one of N_e pruned expert configurations, so the forward pass skips different layers at different steps. The resulting model is inference-schedule-agnostic—once trained, it can be deployed with any number of denoising steps without retraining or parameter adjustment.

## Method

ALTER introduces a trainable hypernetwork H_Φ with two modules: an Expert Generator that produces N_e binary layer-pruning masks (defining specialized sub-networks via Gumbel-Sigmoid with Straight-Through Estimator) and a Temporal Router that maps timestep embeddings to expert-selection logits (via Gumbel-Softmax + STE). During training, layer skipping is simulated differentiably via x_out = (1−m_l)·x_in + m_l·f_l(x_in), enabling end-to-end gradient flow through discrete architectural decisions. The UNet and hypernetwork are co-optimized in a single-stage alternating scheme: the UNet is updated with denoising loss plus output/feature-level knowledge distillation from a frozen teacher, while the hypernetwork is updated with a performance loss, a log-ratio sparsity regularizer targeting a user-defined FLOPs budget, and a router balance loss preventing expert collapse. At inference, hard layer skipping is applied per the finalized masks, yielding a 3.64× speedup at 35% sparsity on SDv2.1 with 20 steps while matching or exceeding the unpruned model's FID and CLIP scores.

## Results

On MS-COCO with 25-step PNDM inference, ALTER(0.65) achieves a FID of 13.70 and CLIP score of 32.17, matching or exceeding the unpruned SDv2.1 (FID 14.29, CLIP 32.17) while using only 899.7G MACs (vs. 1384.2G for the full model). With just 20 inference steps, ALTER still delivers FID 13.89 and CLIP 32.18 on MS-COCO, surpassing the 25-step performance of both BK-SDM-v2 and APTP. On the 50-step MS-COCO 2017 benchmark, ALTER(0.65) at 20 steps achieves a 3.64× speedup with FID-5K of 25.25, outperforming both the 50-step SDv2.1 (FID-5K 27.29) and DiP-GO(0.7) (FID-5K 25.98). On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet 256×256), ALTER achieves a 1.92× speedup with FID 2.36, closely matching the full model's 2.27.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALTER decisively outperforms both static and sample-wise dynamic pruning baselines. Against BK-SDM-v2 (static pruning), ALTER(0.65) at 25 steps improves FID from 19.06 to 13.70 on MS-COCO (a 28% reduction) and from 17.53 to 16.74 on CC3M, while using comparable MACs (~899.7G vs. 876.5G). Against APTP (sample-wise MoE pruning), the advantage is even more dramatic: APTP(0.66) degrades severely (FID 39.12 on MS-COCO, 60.04 on CC3M), while ALTER maintains near-original quality at similar computational cost. Against the cache-based DiP-GO on MS-COCO 2017, ALTER(0.65) at 20 steps achieves lower MACs (9.89T vs. 11.42T), higher speedup (3.64× vs. 3.02×), and better FID-5K (25.25 vs. 25.98). On DiT-XL/2, ALTER (1.92× speedup, FID 2.36) outperforms static TinyFusion (1.96×, FID 2.86) on quality metrics and achieves higher speedup than dynamic DyDiT (1.72×, FID 2.07), though DyDiT retains a slight FID edge. Overall, ALTER matches or exceeds the unpruned SDv2.1 on FID and CLIP at 20–25 steps while reducing per-step computation by ~35%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALTER extends the structured layer-pruning line for diffusion models (BK-SDM, LAPTOP-Diff, LD-Pruner) by replacing a single static mask with timestep-conditioned expert routing, drawing on MoE routing mechanisms from LLMs (GShard, Switch Transformers) and the prompt-based dynamic pruning of APTP. Its key departure is twofold: (1) routing granularity shifts from the sample/prompt level to the timestep level, exploiting the temporal asymmetry of denoising, and (2) a trainable hypernetwork unifies pruning-mask generation, expert routing, and UNet fine-tuning into a single-stage alternating optimization, avoiding the misalignment inherent in sequential prune-then-finetune pipelines. The differentiable simulation of layer skipping via Gumbel-Sigmoid/Softmax with STE is a standard technique repurposed here for architectural search over pruned sub-networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALTER offers a practically deployable acceleration path (3.64× speedup at 35% sparsity matching 50-step quality) that is inference-schedule-agnostic and orthogonal to other accelerators like token merging (ToMeSD) and feature caching, making it composable in real systems. The single-stage co-optimization addresses a genuine limitation of prior sequential approaches, and the temporal-expert framing provides a principled way to allocate compute across the denoising trajectory. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video diffusion or much larger DiT backbones, whether the required post-training fine-tuning stage can be eliminated, and how the method interacts with step-distillation or consistency-model paradigms that fundamentally change the temporal structure.
