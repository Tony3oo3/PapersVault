## Problem

Estimating causal treatment effects in the presence of unobserved confounders via proxy variables (negative controls) relies on the causal bridge function, but the conditions under which the bridge is well-defined were not fully characterized, and prior methods (e.g., DFPV) used iterative conditional-expectation estimation that is both theoretically opaque and statistically inefficient. This paper provides a new error bound for the bridge when its independence from the treatment proxy is violated, and introduces a generative-plus-autoencoder learning framework that shares statistical strength across all observed quantities to improve bridge estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two potentially disjoint datasets: D1 = {(x_i, z_i, w_i)} for training a conditional generative sampler of p(W|x,z), and D2 = {(x_i, z_i, y_i)} for fitting the bridge and autoencoder, so the outcome dataset need not contain W. For survival outcomes, Y is replaced by a (time, censoring-indicator) pair and the target shifts from E[Y|do(X=x)] to a hazard ratio. At inference, 100 samples of W are drawn per (x,z) pair and 5 noise vectors ε per W draw to Monte-Carlo approximate the bridge expectation, yielding a point estimate and empirical 95% CI for the causal effect.

## Method

The core architecture couples a conditional GAN that samples from p(W|x,z) with a shared latent encoder h_θ_U(W,x,ε) that generates samples of the unobserved confounder U. The bridge is generalized to b(W,x) = E_ε[g_θ_Y(x, W, h_θ_U(W,x,ε))], where both g and the encoder are jointly learned by minimizing a squared-error loss on Y. Crucially, an autoencoder loss L_θX + L_θZ is added, reconstructing X and Z from the same h_θ_U samples, so the encoder is regularized by all three observed variables rather than Y alone, improving statistical strength especially at small sample sizes. For survival outcomes the Y-loss is replaced by a Cox partial-likelihood objective with the bridge placed inside the exponential hazard term. Theoretically, Theorem 3 bounds the average bridge error by CR·√(2 I(U;Z|W,x)), and Corollary 1 shows this mutual information vanishes quadratically in the noise variance when W is a low-noise invertible transformation of U, justifying the proxy-based identification strategy.

## Results

On synthetic Demand and dSprite datasets, CB+AE achieves the lowest out-of-sample MSE for average causal effect estimation across both sample sizes (1,000 and 5,000), outperforming DFPV, CEVAE, NMMR-U, NMMR-V, and KAP. On the Framingham survival dataset, CB+AE yields a hazard ratio (HR) below 1 with a 95% CI entirely away from the null (HR=1), consistent with the RCT reference estimate, while CoxPH-Uniform and CoxPH-IPW produce HR > 1 (incorrect direction due to confounding). The autoencoder component provides the largest relative gains at smaller sample sizes (N=1,000), and the generative sampling of p(W|x,z) alone (without the autoencoder) already substantially improves over the original DFPV iterative procedure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CB+AE outperforms all proxy-based causal inference baselines (DFPV, NMMR-U, NMMR-V, KAP) on both synthetic datasets in terms of MSE, with the largest margins on dSprite where KAP and NMMR variants struggle most. CEVAE performs so poorly on both synthetic datasets that its results are excluded from the main figure (described as 'out of range'), and KAP on Demand is similarly excluded. On the Framingham survival task, CB+AE is the only method (besides CoxPH-OW) that recovers the correct treatment direction (HR < 1, i.e., statins reduce CVD risk), and it produces the tightest 95% CI that excludes the null, aligning with the RCT reference. CoxPH-Uniform and CoxPH-IPW both fail by yielding HR > 1, reflecting residual confounding from high-risk patients preferentially receiving statins. The ablation from DFPV → DFPV+sampling → CB → CB+AE shows progressive gains, with the autoencoder contributing most at N=1,000, confirming that sharing statistical strength across (X, Z, Y) is the key practical advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal bridge function framework (Miao et al. 2018; Cui et al. 2024) by replacing the iterative two-step estimation of DFPV (Xu et al. 2021) with a conditional generative model (e.g., cGAN) for sampling p(W|x,z), and by coupling the bridge with an autoencoder over (X, Z) to share statistical strength in the latent confounder representation. It also departs from CEVAE (Louizos et al. 2017) by avoiding a KL-regularized variational posterior in favor of a plain autoencoder regularized by the bridge loss and the generative sampler, and by using W as a strong proxy for U rather than modeling p(U|x,z) directly. The new information-theoretic bound (Theorem 3) connecting bridge approximation error to the conditional mutual information I(U;Z|W,x) is a novel theoretical contribution not present in prior bridge-function work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The information-theoretic bound provides practitioners with a concrete diagnostic: the quality of causal-bridge estimates is governed by how well W captures U relative to Z, which guides proxy selection in practice. The extension to survival (time-to-event) outcomes broadens the bridge framework to a common biomedical setting, and the RCT-validated Framingham experiment demonstrates that the approach can recover the correct direction and magnitude of treatment effects where standard CoxPH variants fail due to confounding. Key open questions include verifying the bridge assumptions in practice, the challenge of splitting covariates into valid W and Z proxies, and whether the proportional-hazard assumption for survival outcomes limits applicability to more complex hazard structures.
