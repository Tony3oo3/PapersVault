## Problem

Existing Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) methods assume labeled and unlabeled data share the same domain, yet real-world unlabeled data often originates from both unseen categories and entirely new visual domains. This Domain-Shifted GCD (DS_GCD) setting causes substantial performance degradation in current GCD methods, and standard unsupervised domain adaptation techniques cannot be directly applied because unknown categories lack label supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253731/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled dataset of RGB images from a known domain (e.g., Real in DomainNet) with class labels, plus an unlabeled dataset whose images may originate from the same known domain or from one or more unknown domains (e.g., Painting, Sketch, or corrupted variants). It produces cluster assignments for all unlabeled images, recovering both known and novel categories. The backbone is a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 with only the final layer fine-tuned, and the total number of categories is either given as a prior or estimated offline.

## Method

FREE decomposes the DS_GCD problem into four frequency-domain components. First, a Frequency-Based Domain Separation (FDS) module computes KNN density scores between each unlabeled image's FFT amplitude spectrum and an anchor set of labeled amplitudes, then fits a two-component GMM via EM to soft-partition samples into known and unknown domains. Second, a Cross-Domain Frequency Perturbation (CDFP) strategy performs class-aware low-frequency amplitude swapping between same-predicted-class samples from different domains to bridge the distribution gap, using a FIFO memory bank to handle class coverage. Third, an Intra-Domain Frequency Perturbation (IDFP) strategy exchanges low-frequency amplitudes between two augmented views of the same unknown-domain sample to build invariance to domain-specific style variations. Fourth, a Clustering Difficulty-Aware Sampling (CDAS) mechanism computes per-class difficulty from intra-class feature variance and inter-class prototype cosine similarity, converting it into a softmax sampling distribution that biases training toward harder-to-cluster categories. All components are trained jointly with SimGCD's parametric classifier using extended contrastive and clustering losses.

## Results

On the SSB-C benchmark, FREE achieves 60.4% / 55.7% clustering accuracy (All) on CUB-C (original/corrupted), 43.6% / 38.9% on Scars-C, and 48.5% / 35.0% on FGVC-C, outperforming the strongest baseline HiLo by 3.7%, 3.3%, and 3.8% on the corrupted domains respectively. On DomainNet (Real as known domain), FREE reaches 67.7% / 45.6% (All) on Real+Painting, surpassing HiLo (64.4% / 42.1%) by 3.3% and 3.5%. FREE also improves known-domain accuracy, e.g., 67.7% vs. 64.4% on Real in the Painting pairing, demonstrating that cross-domain frequency perturbation benefits both domains simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FREE outperforms all nine baselines on the SSB-C benchmark across every dataset and domain split. Against the strongest DS_GCD-specific baseline HiLo, FREE gains 3.7% (CUB-C), 3.3% (Scars-C), and 3.8% (FGVC-C) on corrupted-domain All accuracy, while also improving known-domain accuracy by 3.6%, 4.1%, and 4.3% respectively. On DomainNet, FREE beats HiLo by up to 3.5% on the unknown domain (Painting: 45.6% vs. 42.1%) and 3.3% on the known domain (Real: 67.7% vs. 64.4%), though HiLo retains a slight edge in the Real+Quickdraw pairing (58.6% vs. 51.4% on Real All). Compared to general GCD methods (SimGCD, SPTNet, RLCD), FREE achieves substantially larger margins—e.g., 32.2% over SimGCD on CUB-C corrupted All (55.7% vs. 28.8%)—highlighting that standard GCD methods collapse under domain shift. The NCD-adapted baselines (RankStats+, UNO+) and ORCA are far behind, confirming that domain-aware frequency manipulation is critical rather than merely improving clustering. CDAD-Net, the other DS_GCD method, is consistently outperformed by FREE by 10–18% on corrupted domains, suggesting that adversarial/entropy-driven alignment is less effective than frequency-domain perturbation for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

FREE builds on the GCD framework established by SimGCD (parametric classifier with contrastive + clustering losses) and the frequency-domain adaptation insight from FDA, which showed that amplitude encodes style while phase encodes semantics. It departs from prior DS_GCD methods—HiLo (mutual-information disentanglement with PatchMix) and CDAD-Net (adversarial prototype alignment)—by replacing adversarial or information-theoretic objectives with explicit, class-aware frequency-domain perturbations. The key conceptual departure is a divide-and-conquer strategy: first separating known and unknown domains via amplitude-similarity GMM, then applying domain-specific (cross-domain vs. intra-domain) amplitude swaps, rather than treating all unlabeled samples uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically important gap: real-world category discovery rarely operates under a single domain, yet most GCD methods assume domain homogeneity. The frequency-domain formulation offers a lightweight, non-adversarial alternative to prior DS_GCD approaches, and the negative-transfer finding (random FDA hurts unknown-domain clustering) cautions against blindly applying frequency augmentation without domain awareness. Key limitations include the requirement that unknown-domain data be available during training (ruling out domain generalization) and the restriction to image classification; extending to segmentation, detection, or truly unseen-domain settings remains open.
