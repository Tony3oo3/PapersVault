## Problem

Dynamic 4D Gaussian Splatting models require large storage due to numerous Gaussians, per-timestamp parameters, and temporal redundancies, yet no entropy-aware, bit-level rate-distortion compression framework exists for 4DGS. Existing 3DGS compression methods do not exploit the temporal smoothness prior inherent in dynamic point trajectories, and prior 4DGS compression work (e.g., QUEEN) targets per-frame streaming rather than whole-model storage with flexible RD control.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully pre-trained Ex4DGS model (after densification terminates) as input, which contains static Gaussians with linear position trajectories and dynamic Gaussians with keyframe-based positions, rotations, and temporal opacity parameters. It produces a compressed 4DGS model at one of six user-selectable compression levels (L1–L6), structured as entropy-coded VQ indices, masked Haar wavelet coefficients for dynamic positions, codebooks, sorting indices, and opacity variance parameters. The compressed model renders novel-view images at up to 214 FPS (L1) or ~101 FPS (L6) on an RTX 3090, with model sizes ranging from ~1.3 MB to ~11 MB on N3V scenes.

## Method

The framework extends the RD-optimized 3DGS compression of Wang et al. to the 4D domain by combining three mechanisms under a single end-to-end loss: (1) learnable Gaussian and SH-coefficient pruning via sigmoid masks with the straight-through estimator, (2) entropy-constrained vector quantization (ECVQ) for scale, rotation, and SH coefficients with selective scalar quantization for opacity (excluding variance parameters found to be quantization-sensitive), and (3) a novel single-level Haar wavelet transform applied along the temporal axis of each dynamic point's position trajectory, explicitly discarding high-frequency detail coefficients to exploit the smoothness prior of real-world motion. The total loss jointly optimizes reconstruction fidelity (L1 + D-SSIM), an aggregated rate term (pruning losses + entropy + VQ distortion), and Ex4DGS regularization, with the wavelet transform's effect on fidelity and storage captured through the distortion and rate terms respectively. Ablations confirm that the wavelet step shifts the entire RD curve favorably (higher PSNR at every size) and that single-level decomposition outperforms deeper multi-level decompositions on the RD frontier.

## Results

On the N3V dataset, the proposed method achieves up to 91× compression (1.26 MB vs. 115 MB for Ex4DGS) at Level 1 with 27.04 dB average PSNR, and 10× compression (11.06 MB) at Level 6 with 29.66 dB PSNR, while rendering at 163 FPS and 101 FPS respectively. On the Technicolor dataset, Level 6 achieves 32.20 dB PSNR at 19.6 MB (86% reduction) and Level 1 reaches 28.60 dB PSNR at just 2.1 MB (98.5% reduction) with 214 FPS. The wavelet transform for dynamic positions provides an additional ~19% storage reduction and 0.19 dB PSNR gain at Level 1 over the same model without it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Ex4DGS [18], the method trades 2.45 dB PSNR (29.66 vs. 32.11) for a 10× size reduction at Level 6, and 5.07 dB for a 91× reduction at Level 1, while simultaneously increasing rendering speed from 72.3 to 100.9 and 163.0 FPS. Notably, at Level 6 the method outperforms 4DGaussians [9] on both PSNR (29.66 vs. 28.63 dB) and size (11.06 vs. 34 MB), making it the best quality-to-size trade-off among Gaussian Splatting-based methods. Against the concurrent Light4GS [14] (Appendix E), the method is 2.5× smaller (1.48 vs. 3.77 MB) and ~4× faster (~160 vs. ~40 FPS) at high compression, though Light4GS achieves higher peak PSNR (31.48 vs. 27.90 dB), a gap the authors attribute to different baseline models and design priorities rather than the compression methodology itself. Compared to NeRF-based methods (NeRFPlayer, HyperReel, K-Plane, MixVoxels), the method is orders of magnitude smaller and faster (163 FPS vs. 0.05–37.7 FPS) at the cost of 1–5 dB PSNR. The method does not match the absolute PSNR of the uncompressed Ex4DGS baseline, but provides a user-controllable RD curve spanning 91× compression with no single fixed operating point.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the end-to-end rate-distortion optimized compression framework for 3D Gaussian Splatting (RD3DGS, Wang et al., ECCV 2024) to the 4D dynamic setting, using Ex4DGS (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2024) as the baseline representation. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a pointwise Haar wavelet transform on dynamic position trajectories, which exploits the temporal smoothness prior inherent in real-world motion — a mechanism absent from prior 4DGS compression methods (Light4GS, QUEEN, 4DGS-1K) that either prune or quantize without modeling inter-frame trajectory structure. The overall RD-optimization paradigm (differentiable rate + distortion + regularization) follows Ballé et al. (ICLR 2017), adapted here to jointly optimize pruning masks, ECVQ codebooks, and wavelet-compressed positions in a single training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing 4DGS models to ~1% of their original size (up to 91×) opens the door to volumetric video on resource-constrained edge devices and efficient transmission, a deployment bottleneck that has limited 4DGS adoption. However, the wavelet-based high-frequency filtering introduces motion blur on fast-moving objects, and dynamic points still dominate storage (as shown in Appendix H), leaving substantial room for improvement in compressing the most visually salient components. Open questions include whether separate static/dynamic pruning weights, learned multi-level wavelet selection, or context models over trajectories could push the RD frontier further without sacrificing fidelity on complex motions.
