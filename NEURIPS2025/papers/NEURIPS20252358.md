## Problem

Discrete diffusion models require 1024+ function evaluations for high-quality sampling, and naively reducing steps amplifies both compounding decoding error (from factorized per-token predictions) and discretization error (from Euler/τ-leaping approximations), causing severe quality degradation. Existing acceleration methods like JYS optimize *when* to sample (time schedules) but still rely on intrinsically biased per-step approximations, while τ-leaping variants remain first-order accurate. The paper addresses the gap of learning *how* to sample—adapting the sampling dynamics themselves—rather than merely reordering or skipping steps.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a pre-trained score network s_θ, an initial discrete state x_{t_0} (e.g., an all-masked token sequence for absorbing DDMs, or a uniform random state for uniform DDMs), and a set of M learned per-step scalar coefficients Φ(t_k) (and optionally learned step sizes κ_k in LSD+), then produces a generated discrete sample in M steps. During training, the student sampler additionally receives a perturbed initial state ẋ_{t_0} within a small Hamming distance (~5% of sequence length) of the true x_{t_0}, and the teacher sampler's intermediate scores are cached at matching time points. The learned coefficients introduce zero additional computational cost at inference beyond a scalar multiplication per step.

## Method

LSD trains a few-step student sampler to match a many-step teacher sampler by aligning their intermediate concrete score trajectories rather than final outputs, which is infeasible due to non-differentiable categorical sampling at each step. Specifically, at each student step t_k, the loss minimizes KL divergence between the teacher's cached score s*_k = s_θ(x^*_{t_k}, t_k) and the coefficient-scaled student score Φ(t_k)·s_k = Φ(t_k)·s_θ(x_{t_k}, t_k), providing a differentiable gradient path for the learnable coefficients. LSD+ extends this by also learning non-uniform step sizes κ_k through matching effective transition terms (κ_k·s_θ vs. (T−ε)/N·s*_θ), allowing adaptive allocation of steps where dynamics change rapidly. A relaxed training objective further improves feasibility by permitting the student to start from a Hamming-perturbed initial state ẋ_{t_0} (within ~5% of sequence length) rather than requiring exact trajectory matching from the same x_{t_0}, which is only used during training and not at inference.

## Results

On the SEDD-small text generation backbone (OpenWebText), LSD+-Euler reduces generative perplexity from 423.1 (Euler) to 128.4 at 8 NFEs, and from 56.2 to 20.7 at 64 NFEs. On the RADD backbone, LSD+-Euler achieves 89.8 perplexity at 8 NFEs versus 671.0 for vanilla Euler. On ImageNet 256×256, LSD+-Halton improves FID from 14.16 to 12.78 at 4 NFEs. The learned non-uniform schedules in LSD+ consistently outperform the fixed-schedule LSD variant across all backbones and NFE budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSD+ dominates all baselines across every backbone and NFE budget tested. Against the standard Euler and Tweedie τ-leaping samplers (SEDD [6]), LSD+-Euler on SEDD-small achieves 128.4 vs. 423.1 perplexity at 8 NFEs (a 3.3× reduction). Against JYS [17], which optimizes time-step allocation but still uses fixed first-order updates, LSD+ wins at every NFE (e.g., 128.4 vs. 308.1 at 8 NFEs on SEDD-small). Against the higher-order θ-RK2 and θ-Trapezoidal solvers [45] on RADD, LSD+-Euler achieves 89.8 vs. 123.6 (θ-Trapezoidal) at 16 NFEs. On ImageNet 256×256, LSD+-Halton [58] improves FID from 14.16 to 12.78 at 4 NFEs. Against τLDR-10 [8] on SEDD-small, LSD+ reduces perplexity from 443.2 to 205.4 at 16 NFEs. Against SDTT-KLD Ancestral [59], LSD+ improves MAUVE from 0.884 to 0.905 and perplexity from 110.4 to 68.1 at 8 NFEs. Against ReMDM [60] on MDLM, LSD+ cuts perplexity from 434.1 to 201.5 at 16 NFEs. Against Fast-dLLM [63] on LLaDA, LSD+ improves throughput from 54.4 to 62.5 token/s on GSM8K while matching accuracy (79.0 vs. 78.5). Against DiffuGPT-S and DiffuLLaMA [64], LSD+ nearly halves perplexity at 16 NFEs (53.9 vs. 117.3 and 49.8 vs. 100.0). Against DNDM [12] on FairSeq, LSD+ reduces perplexity from 919.2 to 601.2 at 8 NFEs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the continuous-domain solver distillation paradigm of S4S (Frankel et al., ICML 2025) and LD3 (Tong et al., ICLR 2025) to discrete diffusion models, where the non-differentiability of categorical sampling at each step blocks the gradient-based final-output matching that S4S relies on. Instead, LSD aligns intermediate score trajectories between student and teacher samplers, and replaces S4S's L2-ball initial-noise perturbation with a Hamming-distance-based relaxed objective suited to discrete token sequences. The method operates within the CTMC reverse-process framework established by Campbell et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and SEDD (Lou et al., ICML 2024), modifying their Euler and Tweedie τ-leaping update rules by inserting learnable time-dependent coefficients Φ(t_k) that compensate for accumulated discretization and compounding decoding errors at large step sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDMs currently require 1024+ NFEs for acceptable quality, making them impractical for deployment; LSD/LSD+ reduces this to 8–64 steps with no additional inference cost and only ~5 minutes of training on a single GPU, and the technique is model-agnostic, demonstrated across SEDD, RADD, CTMC, MaskGIT, DiffuGPT, DiffuLLaMA, DNDM, and MDLM backbones with multiple sampler families. The key limitation is that the student sampler's quality ceiling is bounded by the teacher, and no theoretical guarantees are provided for the distributional discrepancy between student and teacher outputs in the discrete setting. Open questions include whether the learned coefficients transfer across model scales or require retraining, and whether the relaxed Hamming-distance objective can be given rigorous convergence analysis analogous to continuous perturbation bounds.
