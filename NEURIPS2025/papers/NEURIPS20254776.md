## Problem

Existing multi-modal semantic segmentation methods process supplementary modalities (depth, thermal, LiDAR, event) with RGB-pretrained or randomly initialized weights, causing mismatched encoding that limits performance. While DFormer addressed this for RGB-D specifically, no flexible pretrain-and-finetune framework exists that can handle arbitrary combinations of multiple visual modalities in a unified manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254776/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image plus any arbitrary subset of supplementary modalities (depth map, thermal image, LiDAR range-view image, event data), with input resolutions ranging from 480×480 (SUNRGBD) to 1408×376 (KITTI-360) depending on the benchmark. The number of supplementary modalities is not fixed at training time; the same pretrained weights initialize separate encoding branches for whichever modalities are present at inference. Output is a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask with class counts varying from 8 (MFNet) to 40 (NYU DepthV2).

## Method

OmniSegmentor introduces two key components: (1) ImageNeXt, a 1.2M-sample dataset built on ImageNet where each RGB image is augmented with synthetic depth (Omnidata), thermal (a trained estimation model), pseudo-LiDAR (from depth), and event (N-ImageNet) data; and (2) an efficient pretraining strategy that, at each iteration, feeds RGB plus one randomly selected supplementary modality into a DFormer-style building block for feature alignment, rather than simultaneously processing all five modalities. This avoids the optimization instability and 2.6× compute overhead of joint multi-modal pretraining while still achieving 83.0% Top-1 vs. 79.9% for simultaneous pretraining. For finetuning, the shared pretrained modality branch is replicated into modality-specific stem layers and MLPs (initialized from the pretrained weights), whose outputs are aggregated via addition and layer normalization before being fused with RGB features in each block, followed by a Ham head decoder for segmentation.

## Results

OmniSegmentor achieves new state-of-the-art mIoU across all six multi-modal segmentation benchmarks. With the DFormer-L backbone, it reaches 57.6% on NYU Depthv2 (RGB-D), 52.8% on SUNRGBD (RGB-D), 60.6% on MFNet (RGB-T), 69.2% on KITTI-360 (RGB-L), 67.6% on EventScape (RGB-D-E), and 68.0% on DeLiVER (RGB-D-E-L). The method also demonstrates efficiency: with only 39.0M parameters and 65.7G FLOPs, it outperforms heavier baselines like CMNeXt-B4 (119.6M params, 131.9G FLOPs) and CMX-B5 (181.1M params, 167.8G FLOPs). On the DeLiVER benchmark, OmniSegmentor (MiT-B2) surpasses CMNeXt (MiT-B2) by 1.2% mIoU in the full RGB-D-E-L setting, with the gap widening to +2.6% on EventScape RGB-D-E.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniSegmentor consistently outperforms all 30 compared baselines across every benchmark and modality setting. Against the direct predecessor DFormer [66], it gains +0.4% on NYU Depthv2 (57.6 vs 57.2), +0.3% on SUNRGBD (52.8 vs 52.5), +1.0% on MFNet (60.6 vs 59.6), and a substantial +2.9% on KITTI-360 (69.2 vs 66.3), confirming that multi-modal pretraining generalizes beyond RGB-D. Versus the strongest multi-modal baselines CMNeXt [72] and CMX [71], OmniSegmentor (MiT-B2) exceeds CMNeXt (MiT-B2) by +1.2% on DeLiVER RGB-D-E-L and by +2.6% on EventScape RGB-D-E, with the advantage growing as more modalities are added. On NYU Depthv2, OmniSegmentor (DFormer-L) surpasses MultiMAE (ViT-B, 56.0%) by 1.6% and CMX (MiT-B5, 56.9%) by 0.7%. On KITTI-360, the +2.9% margin over DFormer-L and +4.6% over CMNeXt (MiT-B2, 64.6%) highlight the benefit of LiDAR-aware pretraining. Notably, OmniSegmentor achieves these gains with fewer parameters (39.0M) and lower FLOPs (65.7G) than CMNeXt-B4 (119.6M, 131.9G) and CMX-B5 (181.1M, 167.8G), yielding 28.0 FPS versus 13.7 and 10.1 FPS respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DFormer, which was designed for RGB-D pretraining, into a general multi-modal framework covering five visual modalities (RGB, depth, thermal, LiDAR, event). It departs from prior multi-modal segmentation methods (CMX, CMNeXt, TokenFusion) that apply RGB-pretrained or randomly initialized weights to supplementary modalities, causing a mismatch between pretraining and finetuning. The efficient pretraining strategy—feeding RGB plus a randomly selected supplementary modality at each iteration—draws inspiration from single-encoder multi-modal models like Omnivore and Meta-transformer, but is applied here in a supervised classification setting on the newly assembled ImageNeXt dataset built atop ImageNet with synthetic modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniSegmentor provides the first large-scale (1.2M samples) multi-modal pretraining dataset covering five popular visual modalities, along with a practical pretrain-and-finetune pipeline that handles arbitrary modality combinations at inference time. The finding that simultaneous multi-modal pretraining is both slower and less effective than random modality selection challenges the intuitive assumption that more modalities per iteration is always better. Key limitations include heavy reliance on synthetic data for depth, LiDAR, and thermal modalities, and the evaluation benchmarks themselves are limited in scope and partly simulation-based; the authors note that real-world multi-modal data collection and unsupervised pretraining remain open directions.
