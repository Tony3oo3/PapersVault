## Problem

Existing temporal visual datasets model coarse-grained category drift or specialized domains, but none captures the long-term appearance evolution of fine-grained technological artifacts. This paper asks how the depictions of fine-grained objects (car models) over nearly two decades should be modeled in visual classification and generation systems, and whether static pretraining suffices or explicit temporal adaptation is needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252894/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For classification, the method consumes RGB car crops (segmented from Flickr photos) and produces one of 190 car model labels, evaluated across all train-test year combinations (2007–2023) to measure backward, forward, and same-year accuracy. For generation, the input is a text prompt of the form 'A photo of CAR_MODEL in YEAR' and the output is a 512×512 synthetic car image, scored by KID and by a real-image-trained classifier's accuracy on the generated set. The labeled subset contains 787K samples across 190 models (48 brands) with year-level timestamps, while the unlabeled pretraining subset spans 5.1M images from 2005–2023.

## Method

CaMiT is built via a semi-automatic annotation pipeline that ensembles Qwen2.5-7B, GPT-4o, and two DeiT classifiers (trained on VLM weak labels) with manual verification, achieving 99.6% label accuracy. The paper evaluates three classification paradigms: static pretraining (SPT) with DINOv2, CLIP, and car-specific MoCo v3 backbones; time-incremental pretraining (TIP) using yearly LoRA or reservoir-based backbone updates; and time-incremental classifier learning (TICL) that freezes the backbone and incrementally updates class prototypes via NCM-TI, FeCAM, RanPAC, or RanDumb. TICL yields the largest gains (up to 20+ points over SPT) and exhibits positive backward transfer, a phenomenon not previously reported in continual learning. For generation, the paper fine-tunes Stable Diffusion 1.5 with LoRA using captions that include the publication year, showing that this simple temporal conditioning reduces KID and improves classifier-verified realism over both standard and year-agnostic fine-tuning.

## Results

In the time-incremental classifier learning (TICL) setting, RanPAC with LoRA-adapted MoCo v3 (ViT-B) achieves the best overall A_avg of 87.8%, surpassing the best static pretraining result (66.0%) by over 20 points and the best time-incremental pretraining result (78.5%) by 9.3 points. For ViT-S, RanPAC with MoCo v3+Li reaches 86.6% A_avg. In time-aware image generation, SD1.5_TAIG reduces KID from 6.83×10⁻⁴ (standard SD1.5) to 4.19×10⁻⁴ and improves A_avg^gen from 46.1% to 54.1%, confirming that temporal metadata in captions yields more realistic generated car images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Specialized pretraining (MoCo v3) substantially outperforms generic pretraining (DINOv2) in the SPT setting: 55.9% vs. 20.9% A_avg for ViT-S, and 62.4% vs. 26.1% for ViT-B, contradicting prior findings for commonsense-level classes. After LoRA domain adaptation, MoCo v3+Li (66.0%) and CLIP+Li (65.6%) reach comparable accuracy, showing that parameter-efficient adaptation closes the gap between generic and specialized models. Among TICL algorithms, RanPAC dominates for LoRA-adapted backbones (87.8% with MoCo v3+Li ViT-B), while RanDumb is slightly better for non-adapted MoCo v3 (82.2% vs. 72.7% for RanPAC). FeCAM, despite storing full covariance matrices, trails RanPAC by 1–5 points across most configurations. For generation, SD1.5_TAIG (KID 4.19×10⁻⁴) outperforms both standard SD1.5 (6.83×10⁻⁴) and year-agnostic fine-tuning SD1.5_FT (4.48×10⁻⁴), with all differences statistically significant (p<0.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual learning literature by introducing a time-incremental classification setting where classes naturally emerge, evolve, and disappear over ~17 years, departing from the random-split or task-identity assumptions of standard class-IL and domain-IL benchmarks. It adapts existing exemplar-free continual classifiers (FeCAM, RanPAC, RanDumb) and the NCM prototype classifier to this temporal setting, where class samples arrive gradually rather than in fixed batches. On the generative side, it builds on Stable Diffusion fine-tuning with LoRA but introduces temporal metadata in captions as a simple mechanism for time-conditioned generation, drawing on prior work on time-conditioned image synthesis and continual generation benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CaMiT provides the first fine-grained, year-annotated dataset for a single object category spanning nearly two decades, enabling controlled studies of temporal appearance drift that coarse-grained or domain-specific temporal datasets (VCT-107, TIC-DataComp, AmsterTime) cannot support. The finding that specialized in-domain pretraining matches or exceeds generic large-scale models for fine-grained classification contradicts prior results for commonsense-level categories and suggests practitioners should invest in domain-specific encoders rather than relying solely on foundation models. Open questions include disentangling genuine design evolution from physical aging in temporal annotations, extending the framework to other rapidly evolving artifact categories, and developing more refined temporal conditioning for generative models beyond simple year tokens in captions.
