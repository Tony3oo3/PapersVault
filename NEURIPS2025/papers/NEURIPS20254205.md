## Problem

When training data contains label bias—systematic differences in label error rates across demographic groups—models trained on noisy labels are provably miscalibrated with respect to the true labels, violating group sufficiency (equal calibration across groups). Prior work on fairness under label bias has only addressed demographic parity and equal odds, leaving group sufficiency unexplored, and standard noise-robust losses are agnostic to group structure and thus cannot correct this disparity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard classification inputs (tabular features, RGB images, or text) together with a binary group attribute and noisy labels, and additionally requires per-group noise transition matrices T^g (either known or estimated via Confident Learning). The output is a K-dimensional class probability vector. Unlike multicalibration post-processing, the group attribute is used only during training and is not needed at inference time, making the resulting model deployable without group information.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form decomposition of the per-group sufficiency gap (Theorem 4.1) into a posterior-shift term and a noise-matrix-mismatch term, then proves that the sufficiency gap is upper-bounded by the conditional mutual information I(Y; G | f(X)) (Proposition 5.1). CMI-REG operationalizes this bound by estimating the two entropy terms H(Y|f(X)) and H(Y|f(X),G) using backward correction with the group-specific inverse transition matrix T_g^{-1}, since true labels are unobserved. Two auxiliary linear heads (h_φ for the marginal posterior and h_ψ for the group-conditional posterior) are trained with forward-corrected cross-entropy losses, and the regularizer is the difference of the backward-corrected log-likelihoods. Training alternates C head-update steps (with the classifier frozen) and one classifier update on the combined prediction loss plus λ·R(f_θ), where ℓ_pred can be any off-the-shelf noise-robust loss (GCE, Peer, Backward, etc.). A second variant, SUF-REG, directly minimizes the Theorem 4.1 expression by plugging head estimates into the p-norm of the posterior difference, avoiding mutual-information estimation at the cost of higher gradient variance.

## Results

On four tabular datasets with synthetic group-asymmetric noise (η=0.75, known transition matrices, clean Brier model selection), CMI-REG consistently achieves the lowest sufficiency gap across all seven base losses. For example, on the adult dataset, CE+CMI-REG reduces the sufficiency gap from 0.029 (CE alone) to 0.016 while improving AUROC from 0.899 to 0.903; on lsac, GCE+CMI-REG cuts the gap from 0.099 to 0.050. The paper reports up to a 7.2% reduction in sufficiency gap with no significant decrease in overall accuracy, and in several cases (e.g., ELR+SUF-REG on income: AUROC 0.754 vs. 0.731 for ELR alone) the regularizer actually improves AUROC by up to 25.4% in one instance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMI-REG and SUF-REG Pareto-dominate all seven base losses (CE, Forward, Backward, DMI, ELR, GCE, Peer) on the AUROC-vs-sufficiency-gap trade-off in almost all cases, with CMI-REG achieving the lowest sufficiency gap in 7 of 8 dataset-noise combinations. GCE and Peer loss are identified as the best base losses to pair with the regularizers; GCE+CMI-REG on lsac reduces Suf from 0.099 to 0.050 (a 49% reduction) while maintaining competitive AUROC. The multicalibration (MC) post-processing baseline performs poorly, with substantially lower AUROC (e.g., 0.561 on income vs. 0.731 for CE) and only limited fairness gains, attributed to its sample-inefficient nature and the limited effectiveness of the noise-aware label-flipping procedure. GroupPeer (Wang et al., FAccT 2021), applicable only to binary tasks, is competitive on adult (Suf 0.035 vs. 0.016 for CE+CMI-REG) and lsac (Suf 0.095 vs. 0.072 for CE+CMI-REG) but is outperformed by CMI-REG on both. The oracle (trained on clean labels) remains the upper bound, achieving Suf values of 0.003–0.046, confirming that the regularized methods close a substantial fraction of the gap between noisy-label training and clean-label training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads: noise-robust classification (forward/backward correction, GCE, peer loss, ELR, DMI) and fairness under label bias (Wang et al. for equal odds, Wu et al. for counterfactual fairness, Mhasawade et al. for a causal perspective). It departs from prior fairness-under-label-bias work, which has exclusively targeted demographic parity and equal odds, by introducing group sufficiency (equal calibration) as the fairness criterion under label bias. Methodologically, it builds on the backward-correction framework for noisy labels and the mutual-information-based sufficiency literature, combining them into a drop-in CMI regularizer that can be added to any existing noise-robust loss. The theoretical decomposition (Theorem 4.1) extends Mhasawade et al.'s causal framework by providing a closed-form characterization of the sufficiency gap under class-attribute conditioned noise, a setting where they offered no guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally study and algorithmically address group sufficiency under label bias, filling a gap between the noise-robust learning and fairness literatures. The clean theoretical result—that label bias is both necessary and sufficient to induce a sufficiency gap when the model is group-sufficient for noisy labels—gives practitioners a precise diagnostic for when naive training will be unfair. Key limitations constrain broader impact: the method requires (or estimates) per-group transition matrices, assumes class-attribute conditioned rather than instance-dependent noise, and handles a single protected attribute at a time. Open questions include extending the analysis to instance-dependent label bias, intersectional fairness, and settings where transition matrices are not identifiable.
