## Problem

Data-dependent (small-loss) regret bounds, which scale with the cumulative loss of an optimal-in-hindsight strategy rather than the horizon T, have been well-studied in unconstrained adversarial MABs but are completely absent from the constrained MAB literature. This paper asks whether such bounds can be derived when the learner must additionally satisfy constraints at every round with high probability (hard constraints), a setting where the classical impossibility result of Mannor et al. (2009) rules out sub-linear regret under fully adversarial constraints.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives, at each round, an adversarially chosen loss vector ℓ_t ∈ [0,1]^K and a stochastically sampled constraint cost vector g_{t,i} ∈ [0,1]^K for each of m constraints, along with a pre-specified strictly feasible strategy x^◇ (satisfying Slater's condition with margin ρ) and constraint thresholds α_i. It outputs a probability distribution x_t over K actions per round that is guaranteed to satisfy g_i^⊤ x_t ≤ α_i for all i and t with high probability, and achieves a regret bound decomposed into a Safety Complexity term (scaling with the squared loss gap between x^◇ and the optimal feasible strategy x^*) and a Bandit Complexity term (scaling with the optimal cumulative loss).

## Method

The SOLB algorithm (Safe OMD with Log-Barrier) combines the OMD-with-log-barrier subroutine of Lee et al. (2020a)—which achieves small-loss bounds via an increasing learning rate over a truncated simplex—with a safe decision space constructed from optimistic cost estimates. At each round, SOLB plays a convex combination x_t = γ_t x^◇ + (1−γ_t) x̃_t, where x̃_t is the OMD update projected onto the truncated safe decision space (intersection of the truncated simplex and a relaxed safe set), and γ_t is an adaptive combination factor that is zero when x̃_t satisfies constraints with high probability and otherwise proportional to the pessimistic violation. This mechanism ensures hard constraint satisfaction while preserving the data-dependent regret structure of the underlying OMD. The resulting regret bound decomposes into two data-dependent terms: a Safety Complexity term Õ(√Σ(ℓ_t^⊤(x^◇−x^*))²) capturing the cost of remaining near the strictly feasible strategy, and a Bandit Complexity term Õ(√Σ ℓ_t^⊤ x^*) capturing the intrinsic learning difficulty. A matching lower bound via constrained small-loss balls confirms that both terms are fundamental to the problem, not artifacts of the analysis.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 4) shows that SOLB achieves a high-probability regret bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{K\ln(1/\delta)}{\rho}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\ell_t^\top(x^{\diamond}-x^*))^2} + \frac{1}{\rho}\sqrt{K\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_t^\top x^*\ln(1/\delta)}\right)$ in the hard-constraints setting, decomposing into a Safety Complexity term and a Bandit Complexity term. The warm-up result (Theorem 2) shows COLB achieves $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sqrt{K\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_t^\top x^*\ln(1/\delta)}\right)$ regret with soft constraints. A matching lower bound (Theorem 5) establishes $\Omega\left(\frac{\Delta}{\rho}\sqrt{T}+\sqrt{\omega T}\right)$ over constrained small-loss balls, confirming the upper bound is tight up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The key comparison is against the state-of-the-art $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\rho\sqrt{T})$ regret bounds for hard-constrained MABs (e.g., Pacchiano et al., 2021). SOLB's bound improves upon these in the best case (when the optimal strategy $x^*$ has small cumulative loss or is close to the strictly feasible strategy $x^{\diamond}$) while remaining equivalent in the worst case. The lower bound (Theorem 5) shows that no algorithm can achieve better dependence on the instance parameters $\omega$ (bandit difficulty) and $\Delta$ (constraint difficulty), establishing that SOLB's two-term decomposition is fundamental rather than an artifact of the analysis. The paper also notes an open question about whether the $1/\rho$ factor in the Bandit Complexity term can be removed, suggesting the upper bound may not be fully tight in that component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OMD-with-log-barrier framework of Lee et al. (2020a), which achieves small-loss regret bounds in unconstrained adversarial MABs, to the constrained setting by intersecting the truncated simplex with a safe decision space defined via optimistic cost estimates. It departs from the constrained MAB literature (Pacchiano et al. 2021, Bernasconi et al. 2022, Liu et al. 2021), which has exclusively provided worst-case Õ(√T) regret bounds, by showing that data-dependent bounds are achievable even under hard constraints. The key algorithmic novelty is the adaptive convex combination (SOLB) between the OMD-proposed strategy and a known strictly feasible strategy, with a combination factor that shrinks as confidence intervals tighten.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish that data-dependent regret bounds are possible in constrained MABs, opening a new research direction that had been entirely overlooked. The tight decomposition of regret into 'Safety Complexity' (distance between the optimal and strictly feasible strategies) and 'Bandit Complexity' (standard small-loss term), confirmed by a matching lower bound, provides a clean theoretical characterization of how constraints fundamentally alter learning difficulty. An open question the authors identify is whether the 1/ρ dependence in the Bandit Complexity term can be removed, suggesting the upper bound may not be fully tight. The results are purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the setting is restricted to adversarial losses with stochastic constraints and a known strictly feasible strategy.
