## Problem

Self-calibrating BCIs (SC-BCIs) that infer decoding relationships without experimenter-provided labels have been demonstrated only for discrete stimulus sets with categorical feedback (e.g., P300 spellers with match/non-match). Recovering a mental target in a continuous high-dimensional latent space—where the system must search over a theoretically infinite stimulus space using continuously-valued feedback decoded from brain signals—remains unsolved. This paper introduces the first SC-BCI framework and algorithm for continuous domains, eliminating the need for labeled calibration data, pre-trained decoders, or heuristics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired EEG response vectors (203-dim, derived from 29 channels × 7 equidistant time windows between 50–800 ms post-stimulus) and 512-dim face latent embeddings from a CelebA-HQ GAN, with no labels or target identity provided. For each hypothesis target embedding h, CURSOR produces a scalar similarity score S(h); downstream, these scores yield a ranked list of candidate faces, a generated face image (via CMA-ES optimization in PCA-reduced 10-dim latent space) that is perceptually indistinguishable from the unknown mental target, and reconstructed ground-truth distance labels (RMSE ≈ 0.18, matching supervised baselines).

## Method

CURSOR breaks the reciprocal dependency between the EEG-to-distance decoder θ_h and the similarity function ρ_h by fixing ρ_h(z_i) = ‖h − z_i‖₂ for any hypothesis h, then constructing a hypothesis-specific regression dataset Γ_N^h = {(e_i, d_i^h)} and training a standard estimator (e.g., linear regression) on it. The CURSOR score S(h) is defined as the ratio of RMSE from a deliberately shuffled (misaligned) dataset to the aligned dataset, which neutralizes distributional differences in distance values across hypotheses and ensures that only genuine EEG-to-distance predictability inflates the score. The hypothesis maximizing S(h) is taken as the estimated mental target. For optimization, CMA-ES searches a PCA-reduced 10-dim face latent space (with EEG reduced to 20 dims) guided by S(h), converging to faces at mean distance 0.93 from the true target—within the perceptual indistinguishability threshold (d ≤ 1.6) established by a 53-participant user study.

## Results

On the ranking task (N=9234 pairs, L=60 hypotheses), CURSOR with a Linear Regressor achieves a Pearson correlation of R = −0.77 ± 0.04 between predicted scores and ground-truth distances, a mean target rank of 6.63/60, and a top-ranked face at mean Euclidean distance 2.90 ± 3.08 from the unknown target. In the optimization task (CMA-ES in PCA-reduced spaces), CURSOR converges to a face at mean distance 0.93 from the target, which is perceptually indistinguishable to human subjects (η(1.6) = 0.503, chance level). For label recovery, CURSOR reconstructs ground-truth distances at RMSE = 0.18 ± 0.08, matching the supervised decoder in [71] (RMSE = 0.17 ± 0.09) without any labeled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CURSOR (LR) significantly outperforms both control conditions—S-LR (shuffled pairs) and Dummy (mean-distance predictor)—on all three ranking metrics (Mann-Whitney U, p < 0.001, Bonferroni corrected), with the top-ranked face at d = 2.90 versus ~21–22 for controls, a difference confirmed perceptually significant (Fisher exact test, odds ratio = 18.49, p < 0.001). In the optimization task, CURSOR converges to d = 0.93 (perceptually indistinguishable, η = 0.503) while S-LR and Dummy stall at d ≈ 24 and 22.5 (clearly distinguishable, η = 0.014; odds ratio = 31.00, p < 0.001). The most direct external comparison is with the supervised decoder in [71] (de la Torre-Ortiz & Ruotsalo, ACM MM 2024), which reports RMSE = 0.17 ± 0.09 on the same EEG-to-distance regression task; CURSOR's fully unsupervised label recovery achieves RMSE = 0.18 ± 0.08, essentially matching the supervised baseline without any labeled calibration data. Among the tested estimators, LR outperforms SVR (R = −0.69) and MLP (R = −0.32), confirming that a simple linear model is sufficient and that CURSOR's information gain comes from the EEG signal rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-calibrating BCI (SC-BCI) paradigm, previously confined to discrete stimulus sets with categorical feedback (e.g., P300 spellers with match/non-match), to continuous latent spaces with continuously-valued distance feedback. It builds directly on de la Torre-Ortiz & Ruotsalo (2024), which decoded EEG-to-distance mappings for mental target recovery but required an explicit labeled calibration phase, and on Kangassalo et al. (2020), which performed iterative mental target search using labeled target trials. CURSOR's key conceptual departure is breaking the circular dependency between the unknown similarity function and the unknown decoder by scoring each hypothesis against a shuffled baseline, eliminating the need for any ground-truth labels at any stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that an unsupervised pipeline can recover ground-truth distance labels at near-supervised accuracy (RMSE 0.18 vs. 0.17) suggests that the calibration bottleneck in continuous-domain BCIs may be fundamentally unnecessary, potentially enabling truly zero-setup brain-computer interfaces. However, the current evidence is constrained to synthetic GAN-generated faces from a single model, offline (non-closed-loop) evaluation, and a single acquisition protocol with logarithmic sampling bias; generalization to real faces, real-time operation, cross-subject transfer, and broader stimulus spaces remain open. The released dataset (N=9234) and codebase provide the first infrastructure for this task, but the narrow stimulus domain and lack of closed-loop validation limit immediate practical impact.
