## Problem

Existing training paradigms for foundation model web agents rely on static, human-authored task datasets and curated expert demonstrations, which are expensive to collect and fail to capture the long-tail complexity of real web interactions. This limits scalability, adaptability, and the agent's capacity for continuous self-improvement beyond its training data.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language task instruction alongside a rendered webpage screenshot annotated with set-of-marks (numeric labels on interactive elements) and a structured accessibility tree capturing the full hierarchical layout of all interactive elements. These two modalities are aligned via one-to-one numeric identifiers so the agent can reference elements consistently across visual and structural representations. The agent outputs a sequence of discrete web actions (click, type, scroll, return) and a final answer, with a multi-action policy that produces up to k candidate actions per step, each with a confidence score used differently during MCTS exploration versus standard execution.

## Method

SAGE introduces a three-tier hierarchical exploration framework for self-guided web agent training without human demonstrations. A pre-exploration phase uses BFS to autonomously map website structure, building a knowledge base of page layouts, transitions, and interactive elements. A top-level exploration strategy employs a self-adaptive task generator that dynamically proposes, decomposes, and composes tasks from easy to hard based on the agent's rolling performance, managed through a double-buffer replay system. A low-level exploration strategy uses MCTS with UCB-based selection to efficiently search action sequences, combined with a multi-action policy and a learned outcome evaluator, with policy updates performed via Step-DPO (step-wise preference optimization) that identifies divergence points between successful and failed trajectories for localized credit assignment.

## Results

On WebArena, Qwen2.5VL-32B trained with SAGE achieves 39.7% average task success, surpassing the proprietary Claude 3.5 Sonnet baseline (35.6%) by 4.1 points. On WebVoyager (13 real-world websites), the same model reaches 47.5% average success, approaching Claude 3.5 Sonnet's 50.5%. SAGE improves over the PAE baseline by 4.4 points (7B) and 8.2 points (32B) on WebArena, and by 5.1 and 6.7 points respectively on WebVoyager. The largest gains appear on harder, long-horizon tasks (>15 steps), where Qwen2.5VL-32B SAGE matches Claude 3.5 Sonnet at 15.0% success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the proprietary Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Qwen2.5VL-32B SAGE wins on WebArena (39.7% vs. 35.6%) but trails slightly on WebVoyager (47.5% vs. 50.5%), indicating the open-source model with structured self-supervised training can match or exceed a leading proprietary VLM in closed-domain settings. Against the PAE baseline (the closest prior method), SAGE consistently wins across all model sizes and both benchmarks: +4.4 pts (7B) and +8.2 pts (32B) on WebArena, +5.1 and +6.7 pts on WebVoyager. Against untrained open-source VLMs, SAGE yields dramatic improvements—Qwen2.5VL-32B jumps from 15.6% to 39.7% on WebArena and from 11.3% to 47.5% on WebVoyager—while LLaVA-34B goes from 0.9% to 37.6% on WebVoyager. The gap between SAGE and PAE widens on harder tasks (>15 steps), where SAGE reaches 15.0% versus PAE's 9.0% for the 32B model, suggesting the hierarchical curriculum and MCTS planning are most beneficial for long-horizon reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGE extends the PAE framework (Zhou et al., ICML 2025), which established an iterative Proposer-Agent-Evaluator loop for autonomous web agent training, by introducing a three-tier hierarchical exploration structure: a BFS-based pre-exploration phase for environment mapping, a self-adaptive top-level curriculum that evolves task difficulty based on agent performance, and a low-level MCTS-guided rollout with Step-DPO policy updates. While PAE relies on a static instruction distribution and standard rollouts, SAGE replaces these with structured planning (MCTS with UCB selection) and preference-based stepwise learning, drawing on Coulom's MCTS formulation and Lai et al.'s Step-DPO objective. The paper also departs from the broader web-agent training literature, which predominantly depends on human-curated task datasets and expert demonstrations, by making the entire training signal self-generated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that foundation model web agents can acquire skills without any human-annotated demonstrations or predefined task sets, which is a meaningful step toward scalable, self-improving agents. The curriculum-driven design (decomposing hard tasks into subtasks, then recombining) provides a concrete mechanism for how agents can progress from primitive operations to compound behaviors. However, the approach assumes a stable, scriptable browser environment with reproducible state resets, limiting applicability to fully open-web scenarios with CAPTCHAs, login walls, or irreversible actions. Open questions include whether the hierarchical exploration strategy generalizes beyond the tested websites, how it scales to multi-site or cross-domain tasks, and whether the MCTS planning overhead is justified at inference time.
