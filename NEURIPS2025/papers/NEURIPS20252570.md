## Problem

Neural combinatorial optimization models trained on small-scale VRP instances (e.g., 100 nodes) suffer severe performance degradation on large-scale problems (1K–100K nodes) due to distributional shift between training and test data. Existing projection techniques that align input subgraphs with the training distribution require joint training with the neural network, imposing additional computational overhead and limiting adaptability to new problem settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252570/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive construction step, the method receives the 2D coordinates of the current node plus its k=100 nearest neighbors (and for CVRP, the depot coordinate, per-node demands, and vehicle capacity). The LLM-designed projection function transforms these KNN subgraph coordinates into a normalized form matching the 100-node training distribution before the NCO model selects the next node. The final output is a complete tour or set of vehicle routes with total Euclidean length, constructed greedily or via Random Re-Construction over up to 1000 iterations.

## Method

TTPL is an LLM-driven evolutionary framework (built on EoH) that searches for an optimal coordinate projection strategy entirely at test time, requiring no model retraining. GPT-4o-mini generates and evolves PyTorch normalization functions over 105 generations (population size 20) using four prompt strategies—two exploratory (E1, E2) and two modification-based (M1, M2)—with fitness evaluated by running the projected subgraph through a frozen LEHD model on a small evaluation set. A complementary Multi-View Decision Fusion (MVDF) module then generates M geometrically augmented views of each projected subgraph, passes them through the NCO model independently, and aggregates the resulting logits via a shared softmax to produce a more robust next-node selection probability. The key distinction from prior work is that the projection is discovered by an LLM reasoning about distributional relationships rather than being hand-designed or co-trained with the solver, enabling plug-and-play deployment across different base models (validated on both LEHD and POMO).

## Results

On synthetic TSP instances, TTPL-MVDF with RRC (1000 iterations) achieves optimality gaps of 1.04%, 1.13%, 1.55%, 1.63%, and 2.56% for TSP1K through TSP100K, respectively, using a base model (LEHD) trained only on 100-node instances. On CVRP, the same configuration yields gaps of 2.37%, 1.02%, 2.25%, and 3.32% for CVRP1K through CVRP50K. Inference time remains under 6 minutes even at 100K nodes (5.9s for greedy, 5.9m for RRC1000 on TSP100K), while many competing NCO methods exceed GPU memory limits (OOM) beyond 10K nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTPL-MVDF RRC1000 outperforms the strongest scalable NCO baseline, INViT-3V greedy, by a wide margin: 1.04% vs. 6.90% on TSP1K, 1.13% vs. 7.07% on TSP5K, and 1.63% vs. 7.20% on TSP50K (roughly 6× lower gap). On CVRP, the advantage is even more pronounced, with TTPL-MVDF RRC1000 achieving 1.02% on CVRP5K versus INViT's 15.31%, and 3.32% on CVRP50K versus INViT's 28.55%. Most competing NCO methods (POMO, ELG, BQ, SIGD, LEHD) fail entirely (OOM) on TSP50K and TSP100K, while TTPL handles all scales within seconds to minutes. Among decomposition-based methods, GLOP achieves 4.39–5.14% on TSP5K–50K but OOMs on CVRP10K+, whereas TTPL maintains 1–3% gaps across all tested scales. On real-world TSPLIB/CVRPLIB benchmarks (Table 3), TTPL-MVDF RRC1000 achieves 1.37% average gap on TSP (33/33 instances solved) and 7.50% on CVRP (14/14 solved), outperforming LEHD RRC1000 (7.37% TSP, 11.04% CVRP) and INViT greedy (11.04% TSP, 20.49% CVRP). The versatility study (Table 6) confirms that the TTPL projection strategy transfers to POMO, reducing its TSP100K gap from 597.8 (KNN-only) to 296.6 (TTPL), demonstrating model-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) local-policy-based NCO methods (INViT, ELG, GLOP) that reduce the search space to KNN subgraphs and apply hand-designed projection strategies to align inference inputs with training distributions, and (2) LLM-based automatic heuristic design (EoH, FunSearch, ReEvo) that uses evolutionary search to discover algorithmic components. The key departure is that prior projection strategies (e.g., INViT's normalization) must be co-designed with or jointly trained alongside the NCO model, whereas TTPL treats projection as a standalone, test-time-optimizable component discovered by an LLM-driven evolutionary loop, decoupling it entirely from model training. The MVDF module repurposes the geometric data augmentation from Kool et al. (2018) in a new ensemble-aggregation role at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that any pre-trained NCO model (LEHD, POMO, etc.) can be made scalable to 100K-node instances simply by swapping in a better projection function at inference time, with no retraining or architectural modification. This lowers the barrier to deploying NCO solvers on real-world logistics problems where instance sizes vary widely. The main limitation acknowledged is the slow convergence of the LLM evolutionary search (105 generations), and an open question is whether the discovered projections transfer across problem types (TSP→CVRP) or require per-problem re-optimization. The versatility study with POMO and cross-distribution tests (cluster, explosion, implosion) partially addresses generalizability but remains limited to two base models and two problem families.
