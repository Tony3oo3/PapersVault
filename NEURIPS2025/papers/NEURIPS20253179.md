## Problem

In vertically federated learning, observations of two variables are split across separate servers (X on one, Y on the other), each subject to its own differential privacy budget. While horizontal federation has been extensively studied, the minimax optimal rates for correlation estimation under server-level (central) DP in this vertically separated setting were previously unknown, and no rate-optimal estimators or inference procedures had been established.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253179/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n coordinate-wise separated observations—server 1 holds {X_i} and server 2 holds {Y_i}—along with per-server privacy budgets (ε₁, δ₁) and (ε₂, δ₂), assuming pre-normalized sub-Gaussian data (mean 0, variance 1). It produces a point estimate ρ̂ of the Pearson correlation and asymptotically valid confidence intervals. The output structure differs between the non-interactive protocol (both servers release privatized batch statistics independently) and the interactive protocol (one server observes the other's transcript before computing its own release), with the interactive variant yielding strictly tighter rates whenever ε₁ ≠ ε₂.

## Method

For the non-interactive protocol, each server computes batch means of sign indicators (Gaussian case) or clipped values (sub-Gaussian case), adds Laplace noise calibrated to its privacy budget, and the correlation is recovered via a method-of-moments transformation (e.g., ρ = sin(πη/2) for the Gaussian sign-based statistic) or direct product averaging. For the interactive protocol, the server with the less stringent budget (larger ε) first releases its privatized per-observation statistics—using a sign-flip mechanism for Gaussian data or clipped-plus-Laplace for sub-Gaussian data—and the other server computes the correlation from the received transcript and its own data, then adds a single Laplace noise term. The key theoretical contribution is proving minimax lower bounds via the Van Trees inequality with direct Fisher information curvature control, showing the NI rate scales as 1/(nε₁²) + 1/(nε₂²) while the INT rate improves to 1/(n(ε₁∨ε₂)²) + 1/(n²ε₁²ε₂²), making interaction strictly beneficial whenever the two privacy budgets differ.

## Results

The paper establishes minimax optimal rates for correlation estimation under server-level differential privacy in a vertically distributed setting. The non-interactive (NI) rate is L_n(1/(nε₁²) + 1/(nε₂²)), while the interactive (INT) rate improves to L_n(1/(n(ε₁∨ε₂)²) + 1/(n²ε₁²ε₂²)), where L_n = O(log n). The key finding is that INT is strictly better than NI whenever ε₁ ≠ ε₂, since the dominant term depends only on the less stringent budget. Empirically, on the HRS dataset (~20k individuals, age vs. BMI), at ε_corr = 1 the INT confidence interval excludes zero while the NI interval still includes it, demonstrating the practical advantage of interaction.

**Table: Minimax rates for correlation estimation under different privacy models.**

| Setting | Minimax Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Central DP (jointly observed) | 1/(n²ε²) | Best case; no vertical split |
| **NI (this paper)** | **L_n(1/(nε₁²) + 1/(nε₂²))** | No interaction between servers |
| **INT (this paper)** | **L_n(1/(n(ε₁∨ε₂)²) + 1/(n²ε₁²ε₂²))** | One-way interaction allowed |
| Local DP (Amorino & Gloter 2023) | 1/(nε₁²ε₂²) | Per-observation privacy; worst case |

*All rates are for MSE of ρ̂ over ρ ∈ [−1,1], with L_n = O(log n) and δ_k = o(n⁻¹).*

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions its rates against three existing benchmarks. Compared to central DP on jointly observed data (rate 1/(n²ε²)), the vertical federation setting incurs a strictly slower rate, highlighting the fundamental cost of feature separation. Compared to component-wise local DP (Amorino & Gloter 2023, rate 1/(nε₁²ε₂²)), the server-level DP rates in this paper are strictly faster, since server-level privacy is a weaker constraint than per-observation local privacy. The most important internal comparison is NI vs. INT: whenever ε₁ ≠ ε₂, the INT rate is strictly smaller because its dominant term 1/(n(ε₁∨ε₂)²) depends only on the looser budget, whereas the NI rate 1/(nε₁²) + 1/(nε₂²) is penalized by both. In the special case ε₁ = ε₂ = ε, both rates coincide at 2L_n/(nε²), so interaction provides no benefit. Numerically, INT achieves uniformly lower MSE and shorter confidence intervals than NI across all tested (n, ρ, ε₁, ε₂) configurations, with the gap widening as the privacy budgets become more asymmetric (e.g., (1.5, 0.5) vs. (1, 1)).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differential privacy estimation literature from the well-studied horizontal federation setting (Kairouz et al. 2021; Li et al. 2020) and central DP correlation estimation (Biswas et al. 2020; Cai et al. 2021) to the vertically distributed, server-level DP regime, where X and Y reside on separate servers. While vertical federated learning has been explored algorithmically (Chen et al. 2020; Wu et al. 2020; Wei et al. 2022), the authors note that minimax-optimal estimation and inference theory for this setting was largely absent. The lower-bound technique departs from the mutual-information-based bounds of Hadar et al. (2019) for communication-constrained correlation estimation, instead using a direct Fisher-information curvature argument via the Van Trees inequality tailored to central DP with vertical splitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first minimax-optimal rates for correlation estimation under server-level DP in a vertically distributed setting, cleanly separating the non-interactive and interactive regimes and showing that one-way interaction is strictly beneficial whenever privacy budgets are asymmetric. The practical finding that private normalization incurs no measurable cost in MSE or CI width is reassuring for deployment. Open questions include extending to multi-feature servers (where inter-feature correlations complicate sensitivity analysis) and to heavy-tailed distributions, both of which the authors flag as future work. The scope remains bivariate and two-server, so the theory does not yet address the higher-dimensional vertical federation scenarios that arise in real healthcare or finance applications.
