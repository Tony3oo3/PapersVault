## Problem

Existing diffusion-based methods for single-image 3D reconstruction produce novel views with geometric distortions and cross-view inconsistencies, especially at large angular deviations from the input perspective. This prevents accurate omnidirectional 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction, as prior approaches like LiftImage3D only cover limited forward-facing angles rather than the full 360° scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254486/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a full omnidirectional 3D Gaussian Splatting scene that can be rendered from any viewing angle. Internally, it synthesizes 48 novel views per orbit across multiple orbital directions (left, right, up, down, then lateral and back-hemisphere extensions), each accompanied by a refined camera-to-world pose and camera intrinsics (focal lengths and principal point) estimated via MASt3R and PnP.

## Method

Omni3D's core contribution is a synergistic Pose-View Optimization (PVO) module that iteratively refines both generated view content and camera poses by minimizing 3D reprojection errors between view pairs. For each pair, a lightweight CNN (zero-initialized so the initial output equals the input) is overfit online to predict a homography, 2D flow, and residual that warp the generated view; MASt3R provides differentiable pointmaps for reprojection, and PnP+RANSAC yields poses and intrinsics. A progressive sliding-window pairing scheme (window size N = I/4) balances angular disparity against error accumulation along each orbit. The process runs in three stages—frontal hemisphere, lateral expansion, and back hemisphere—each feeding key peripheral views back into the LoRA-tuned CogVideoX MVD model for new view synthesis before PVO refinement. Pairs sharing the same reference view are computed in parallel (up to 24 concurrent on 8×A100), keeping total runtime around 34 minutes, after which a standard 3DGS is trained on all optimized views and poses.

## Results

On Tanks and Temples, Omni3D achieves 16.30 dB PSNR, 0.5308 SSIM, and 0.5166 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline LiftImage3D by 1.45 dB in PSNR. On Mip-NeRF 360, Omni3D reaches 15.89 dB PSNR (+1.62 dB over LiftImage3D), and on DL3DV it attains 17.08 dB PSNR (+0.87 dB over LiftImage3D). A 10-user study confirms the quantitative gains, with Omni3D receiving average ratings of 7.6–8.2/10 versus 4.5–5.8 for LiftImage3D across the three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni3D consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor LiftImage3D, Omni3D gains 1.45 dB PSNR on Tanks and Temples, 1.62 dB on Mip-NeRF 360, and 0.87 dB on DL3DV, while also improving SSIM and LPIPS in all cases. ViewCrafter is the second-strongest baseline on DL3DV (16.61 dB PSNR) but trails Omni3D by 0.47 dB there and by roughly 2.4 dB on Tanks and Temples. ZeroNVS, which relies on Score Distillation Sampling, is the weakest baseline across all datasets (11.28–13.40 dB PSNR) and also the slowest at >2 hours per scene. The ablation further shows that adding PVO to LiftImage3D's MVD backbone yields only a 0.37 dB gain, confirming that the full Omni3D pipeline (multi-stage view expansion + PVO) is necessary for the larger improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

Omni3D sits at the intersection of three active research threads: multi-view diffusion models (CogVideoX, MotionCtrl, ViewCrafter) for novel view generation, geometric pose estimation via DUSt3R/MASt3R, and explicit 3D representation through 3D Gaussian Splatting. It departs from prior single-image 3D reconstruction methods (ZeroNVS, LiftImage3D, ViewCrafter) by targeting full omnidirectional coverage rather than limited forward-facing angles, and introduces a synergistic Pose-View Optimization (PVO) loop that iteratively co-refines generated view content and camera poses using 3D reprojection error as the geometric signal. The progressive sliding-window pairing scheme is a practical design choice to balance angular disparity against error accumulation, and the multi-stage (frontal → lateral → back) generation strategy is specific to the omnidirectional setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to deliver full 360° 3DGS reconstruction from a single image with consistent geometric quality, which is a prerequisite for immersive AI-generated environments. The PVO module's demonstrated generalizability (improving LiftImage3D by 0.37 dB without architectural changes) suggests it could be a reusable component for other diffusion-based 3D pipelines. However, the multi-stage 2D-synthesis-then-3D-reconstruction pipeline remains a fundamental bottleneck in both speed and fidelity, as the authors themselves acknowledge; the open question is whether foundation models can eventually generate 3DGS representations directly from a single image, bypassing the 2D intermediate entirely. On a single GPU, the method is still substantially slower than LiftImage3D, limiting near-term practical deployment.
