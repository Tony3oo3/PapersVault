## Problem

Existing training paradigms for visual chain-of-thought reasoning in LVLMs (SFT, PPO, GRPO) optimize next-token distributions or scalar rewards, which limits generalization across unseen reasoning tasks, constrains exploration via KL penalties, and invites reward hacking. The paper addresses the gap of treating visual CoT as a probabilistic inference problem over latent rationales, enabling diverse, high-likelihood reasoning trajectories without dependence on a biased external reward model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253465/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the model consumes an image paired with a text instruction and a ground-truth answer (X, Y), using the answer to define the posterior over latent rationales; at inference, only the image and instruction are provided. The system produces a latent rationale Z (up to 700 tokens, minimum 64) sampled from a learned policy q_θ(Z|X), followed by a final answer selected via Bayesian inference scaling over N rationale-answer pairs. A new 'Analyzer' role token allows the model to selectively emit reasoning steps rather than forcing CoT on every instruction, and the rationale is never directly supervised—only its contribution to the joint likelihood P(XZY) is optimized.

## Method

LaCoT reformulates visual CoT as amortized variational inference: a rationale sampler q_θ(Z|X) is trained via a GFlowNet Sub-Trajectory Balance objective so that trajectory probability is proportional to the unnormalized posterior P(XZY). Two key mechanisms make this tractable for long multimodal sequences: (1) a token-level marginal reward approximation that computes exact log-likelihood rewards every λ=8 tokens and linearly interpolates intermediate values, reducing per-step computation; and (2) Reference-Guided GFlowNet Fine-tuning (RGFN), which explores m candidate rationales per step and back-propagates only through those exceeding an annealed threshold relative to a teacher-generated reference rationale, preventing catastrophic forgetting without KL penalties. At inference, the BiN strategy samples N rationales, generates corresponding answers, and ranks them by a length-normalized marginal likelihood—eliminating the need for an external critic model and providing a probabilistically grounded selection criterion.

## Results

LaCoT-Qwen-7B achieves 68.4% on MathVista, 54.9% on MMMU, 43.3% on MathVerse (vision-only), and 74.2% on MMVet, improving over its Qwen2.5-VL-7B base by 6.6% on average and outperforming the GRPO-based R1-Onevision by 10.6%. The 3B variant reaches 63.2% on MathVista and 40.0% on MathVerse, surpassing larger models such as LLaVA-CoT-11B (52.5% / 22.6%) and LLaVA-OV-7B (63.2% / 26.2%). On MMMU, LaCoT-Qwen-7B (54.9%) narrows the gap to GPT-4o (70.7%) to under 3 points among open-source 7B models, while on MME it scores 2372, exceeding all open-source baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LaCoT-Qwen-7B gains +4.7 on MathVista (68.4 vs 63.7), +5.1 on MathVerse (43.3 vs 38.2), and +4.9 on MMMU (54.9 vs 50.0). Compared to the GRPO-trained R1-Onevision, LaCoT-Qwen-7B leads by +4.3 on MathVista, +5.5 on MathVerse, and +7.0 on MMMU, while R1-Onevision retains a slight edge on MMVet (71.1 vs 74.2) and a large gap on MME (1111 vs 2372). The 3B LaCoT model outperforms the 11B LLaVA-CoT by +10.7 on MathVista and +17.4 on MathVerse, and beats the 7B LLaVA-OV by +13.8 on MathVerse. Against GPT-4o, LaCoT-Qwen-7B wins on MathVista (68.4 vs 60.0) and MathVerse (43.3 vs 40.6) but trails on MMMU (54.9 vs 70.7) and MMMU-Pro (35.3 vs 51.9). Among open-source 7B models, LaCoT-Qwen-7B is the strongest on MathVista, MathVerse, MMMU, MMMU-Pro, MMVet, and MME, with the only exception being MiniCPM-V2.6's MME score (2348 vs 2372, a narrow margin). The ablation (Table 3) confirms that RGFN training outperforms SFT (+5.7 on MathVista) and GRPO (+5.8 on MathVista) on the same 7B base.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNets-based amortized variational inference framework (Bengio et al., 2023; Malkin et al., 2023) and its application to language model reasoning (Hu et al., 2024) to the multimodal setting, where CoT sequences are substantially longer (~1k tokens) and the state space is richer. It departs from standard RL training paradigms (PPO, GRPO) by replacing scalar-reward policy optimization with a sub-trajectory-balance objective that provides token-level learning signals, and introduces a reference-guided exploration mechanism (RGFN) to avoid the KL-penalty constraint that limits diversity in PPO/GRPO. The inference-time scaling strategy (BiN) is positioned as a principled alternative to Best-of-N and beam search, grounded in marginal likelihood estimation rather than external critic models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a probabilistically grounded alternative to reward-model-dependent RL for training visual reasoning models, with the practical advantage that BiN can be applied as a plug-and-play inference-time improvement to any existing reasoning LVLM (demonstrated on SFT-only Qwen2.5-VL). The finding that GRPO fine-tuning can underperform zero-shot prompting on visual reasoning benchmarks highlights a real and underappreciated failure mode of reward-model-based RL in the multimodal setting. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B parameters, how it handles hallucination (explicitly acknowledged as unaddressed), and whether the linear interpolation reward approximation remains valid for substantially longer or more complex reasoning chains.
