## Problem

The audio domain lacks a rigorous deep reasoning benchmark analogous to MMLU-Pro in the textual domain. Existing audio benchmarks (AudioBench, MMAU, AIR-Bench) primarily assess surface-level understanding on unimodal or artificially synthesized audio, with limited evaluation of multi-step reasoning that demands graduate-level perceptual and domain-specific knowledge. This gap hinders progress in evaluating and advancing audio deep reasoning capabilities in Audio-Language Models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252252/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item pairs a real-world audio clip (≤30 seconds, averaging ~20 seconds) crawled from internet videos with a question and 2–4 multiple-choice options. The audio spans seven domain categories covering single modalities (sound, music, speech) and all pairwise and triple mixed modalities, with questions hierarchically categorized across four reasoning layers (Signal, Perception, Semantic, Cultural) and 16 sub-categories. Models output a selected answer letter scored by classification accuracy via regex and string matching; for reasoning models, only the final answer (not the chain-of-thought) is evaluated to ensure fairness across model types.

## Method

MMAR is constructed through a five-stage pipeline: expert brainstorming, Human-LLM collaborative taxonomy construction, heuristic-based annotation from real-world videos, LLM-assisted content enrichment (distractor generation and CoT refinement), and iterative multi-person quality inspection enforcing role separation (independent authoring, correction, and review by different individuals) with a two-round discard threshold. Every question is annotated with a manually labeled Chain-of-Thought rationale that explicitly traces the multi-step reasoning process. The benchmark evaluates 30 models across five categories (LALMs, LARMs, OLMs, LLMs, and LRMs with audio-caption inputs), using Poisson Binomial distribution with Bonferroni correction (α=0.001) to test statistical significance over random guessing. Key findings show no open-source LALM significantly outperforms random guessing, a substantial open-vs-closed gap (Qwen-2.5-Omni 7B at 56.7% vs. Gemini 2.0 Flash at 65.6%), and consistent advantages for reasoning-enhanced models over non-reasoning counterparts in both end-to-end and cascaded settings.

## Results

On MMAR (1,000 multiple-choice audio deep-reasoning questions), Gemini 2.0 Flash achieves the highest average accuracy at 65.6%, followed by GPT-4o Audio at 63.5% and Qwen-2.5-Omni 7B (best open-source) at 56.7%. No open-source LALM achieves statistically significant improvement over random guessing (29.3% baseline) under Bonferroni-corrected testing. Music-related tasks are the most challenging across all models, with even Gemini 2.0 Flash scoring only 51.0% on the Music domain versus 72.1% on Speech.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini 2.0 Flash (65.6%) outperforms all 29 other evaluated models, including the best cascaded pipeline (Caption + DeepSeek-R1 at 55.5%) by over 10 points, demonstrating the advantage of tightly integrated multimodal architectures. Among open-source models, Qwen-2.5-Omni 7B (56.7%) leads, surpassing the best LARM (Audio-Reasoner, 36.8%) by nearly 20 points and the best open-source LALM (SALMONN 13B, 33.2%) by over 23 points. Reasoning-enhanced models consistently outperform their non-reasoning counterparts: Audio-Reasoner (36.8%) beats Qwen2-Audio (30.4%) and Qwen2-Audio-Instruct (30.0%), and Caption + DeepSeek-R1 (55.5%) beats Caption + DeepSeek-V3 (47.6%). GPT-4o Audio (63.5%) is the strongest closed-source LALM but still trails Gemini 2.0 Flash by 2.1 points. All open-source LALMs (ranging from 6.6% for MusiLingo to 33.2% for SALMONN 13B) fail to achieve statistically significant improvement over the 29.3% random-guess baseline under Bonferroni correction, underscoring the benchmark's difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first benchmark explicitly targeting *deep* reasoning in audio, drawing an analogy to MMLU-Pro in the text domain. Prior audio benchmarks (MMAU, AIR-Bench, AudioBench) assess surface-level understanding or shallow reasoning over unimodal or artificially synthesized audio; MMAR departs by requiring multi-step inference over real-world mixed-modality clips (sound + music + speech) and organizing tasks into a four-level hierarchical taxonomy (Signal → Perception → Semantic → Cultural). It also broadens the evaluation scope beyond LALMs to include LARMs, OLMs, and cascaded LLM/LRM pipelines, a model coverage that prior audio benchmarks did not attempt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-random performance of all open-source LALMs (none significantly above the 29.3% random baseline) exposes a substantial capability gap in audio deep reasoning that the community has not yet addressed, motivating both data and algorithm innovation. The residual language-prior bias revealed by the noise-input ablation (Qwen-2.5-Omni still above random without audio) raises methodological concerns for future multimodal benchmarks. Key open questions include whether scaling model size or training data can close the open-source-to-closed-source gap, whether the 1,000-example limit constrains statistical power, and how to eliminate text-only answerability at scale.
