## Problem

Standard recalibration methods for probabilistic forecasts are largely restricted to univariate outputs, and while multivariate conformal prediction methods yield prediction regions with coverage guarantees, they do not provide an explicit probability density function. This leaves a gap for multi-output regression tasks that require a fully specified, calibrated multivariate distribution for downstream decision-making, density evaluation, or anomaly detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250175/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained conditional normalizing flow (or flow matching model) with a known latent distribution (typically standard Gaussian), a held-out calibration set of (X, Y) pairs, and a new covariate vector x. It produces a recalibrated conditional PDF f'_{Y|X=x}(y) with a closed-form expression via the change-of-variables formula, enabling direct density evaluation, efficient sampling, and computation of probabilities over arbitrary regions. The calibration map is estimated from latent norms computed on the calibration set using kernel density estimation with Gamma kernels or rational quadratic splines, all operations performed in log-space for numerical stability.

## Method

LR introduces latent calibration, a new notion that assesses whether the probability integral transform of the latent norm ρ_Z(T̂^{-1}(Y;X)) is uniformly distributed, reducing the multivariate calibration problem to a univariate one on norms. The recalibration procedure learns a radial transformation R of the latent space that rescales each vector along its direction by replacing its norm ‖z‖ with r(‖z‖) = F̂_L^{-1}(F_{ρ_Z(Z)}(‖z‖)), where F̂_L is the estimated CDF of calibration-set latent norms. The recalibrated model is the composite T̂ ∘ R applied to the original latent variable Z, and its PDF is computed in closed form using the Jacobian determinant of the radial transform, which has an efficient analytical expression involving (r(l)/l)^{d-1} · ∂r/∂l. Finite-sample coverage guarantees are inherited from Marx et al. (2022), and the method is shown to be equivalent to conformal prediction sets from CONTRA and L-CP when using the empirical CDF as the calibration map, while also generalizing quantile recalibration in the univariate case.

## Results

On the AFHQ image dataset (d=196,608), LR reduces L-ECE from 0.474 (BASE) to 0.009 while slightly improving BPD from 5.477 to 5.465. Across 29 tabular datasets, LR consistently reduces L-ECE (from up to 0.25 to near 0) and improves NLL on the majority of datasets, while also improving HDR-ECE to levels comparable to the dedicated HDR-R method. In a decision-making experiment on the SLUMP dataset, LR with PDF-based numerical integration achieves an average utility of 113.31, substantially outperforming BASE with the same strategy (76.23) and BASE with sampling (62.53).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LR against the unrecalibrated base model (BASE) and against HDR-R (Chung et al., 2024), the only existing multivariate recalibration method. Against BASE, LR wins decisively on all calibration metrics: L-ECE drops from up to 0.25 (tabular) or 0.474 (AFHQ) to near zero, and HDR-ECE improves substantially on most datasets. Against HDR-R, LR matches or exceeds HDR-ECE performance while additionally achieving latent calibration (which HDR-R does not target), producing an explicit multivariate PDF (HDR-R only yields resampled points), and being computationally more efficient (no K-sample generation at test time). On the decision-making task, LR with PDF integration (utility 113.31) far outperforms HDR-R with sampling (32.38), demonstrating that the explicit calibrated density enables better downstream decisions than resampling from an uncalibrated model. The one metric where LR does not clearly win is the Energy Score, which remains largely unchanged relative to BASE, attributed to ES's insensitivity to variance and correlation misspecifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends univariate quantile recalibration (Kuleshov et al., 2018) to the multivariate setting by operating in the latent space of conditional normalizing flows, where the multivariate calibration problem reduces to calibrating a univariate norm distribution. It departs from sampling-based HDR recalibration (Chung et al., 2024) and conformal prediction methods (CONTRA, L-CP) by producing an explicit recalibrated PDF rather than prediction sets or resampled points, while inheriting finite-sample coverage guarantees from the modular conformal calibration framework (Marx et al., 2022). The method is also shown to be compatible with flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022), broadening its applicability beyond classical NF architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LR fills a practical gap: practitioners using NFs for multi-output regression now have a post-hoc recalibration step that yields a fully specified, calibrated density amenable to numerical integration, anomaly detection, and decision-making — capabilities unavailable from set-based or sampling-based alternatives. Key limitations include the restriction to magnitude-only (radial) adjustments in latent space, which cannot correct directional miscalibration, and the requirement for an invertible transformation with a known latent distribution, excluding VAEs and diffusion models. Open questions include whether richer latent-space transformations (e.g., anisotropic or learned) could address directional errors, and whether the approach extends to non-invertible generative architectures.
