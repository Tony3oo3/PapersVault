## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation for VLMs follows two disconnected paradigms: generation adjustment (modifying decoding to reduce hallucination but with no mechanism to correct tokens once produced) and post-hoc verification (using external models to detect and reject outputs, but without enabling the model to refine them). No prior method allows a single VLM to both detect its own hallucinations mid-generation and iteratively self-correct them on the fly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252206/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a text prompt (captioning instruction or open-ended question) and produces a natural-language caption or answer. During generation, the model internally emits special confidence tokens (<SPAN>, </CN>, </UN>) around key noun phrases to signal grounding; these tokens are stripped before the final output is returned. If self-correction exhausts its maximum attempts (N=50), the output is finalized with an explicit indication that a hallucination was detected but could not be corrected.

## Method

REVERSE introduces two coupled components. First, hallucination-aware training fine-tunes a VLM on a 1.3M semi-synthetic dataset where positive phrases are wrapped in <SPAN>...</CN> and negative (hallucinated) phrases in <SPAN>...</UN>, using a modified cross-entropy loss that assigns zero weight to tokens inside <SPAN>...</UN> spans so the model learns to emit confidence markers without reinforcing ungrounded language priors. Second, at inference, retrospective resampling continuously monitors the probability of the </UN> token; when it exceeds a threshold τ, the model backtracks to the most recent </CN> checkpoint and regenerates the flagged phrase via rejection sampling at progressively increased temperature, optionally augmented by query rewriting that injects the negative keywords as a clarification hint in the prompt. A hierarchical fallback escalates from local phrase correction to sentence-boundary backtracking before finalizing the output, enabling interpretable, controllable self-correction within a single model without external verifiers.

## Results

On CHAIR-MSCOCO (LLaVA-v1.5-7B), REVERSE (τ=0.0003) reduces CHAIR_i from 15.4 (base) to 6.1, outperforming the best training-based baseline HA-DPO (11.0) by 44.5% and the best training-free baseline MEMVR (13.0) by 53%. On AMBER-G, REVERSE (τ=0.0003) achieves CHAIR=4.0, Hall=10.2, and Cog=0.9, substantially below all baselines. On HaloQuest, REVERSE (τ=0.0003) improves average accuracy from 22.6 (base LLaVA-v1.5) to 32.3, a 34% gain over the best baseline HALVA (23.9), with the largest gains on insufficient-context questions (10.7→58.8). On MMHal-Bench, REVERSE (τ=0.0003) achieves a score of 3.28 with hallucination rate 0.30, versus 2.33/0.56 for DoLA (the best prior method).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REVERSE outperforms all 11 baselines across every main benchmark. Among training-free generation-adjustment methods, MEMVR (CHAIR_i=13.0) and DoLA (14.1) are the strongest on LLaVA-v1.5, but both are surpassed by REVERSE (τ=0.003) at 10.3 and decisively by REVERSE (τ=0.0003) at 6.1. Among training-based methods, HA-DPO (11.0) and HALVA (11.7) are the closest competitors on CHAIR-MSCOCO, yet REVERSE (τ=0.003) still leads at 10.3, and the lower-threshold setting extends the gap to 6.1. The post-hoc verifier Woodpecker (14.8) underperforms even the base model on CHAIR_i, likely due to error propagation in its single-round rewriting, while REVERSE's iterative self-correction avoids this. On open-ended VQA, REVERSE (τ=0.003) beats HALVA (23.9→30.7) and DoLA (22.9→30.7) on HaloQuest average accuracy, and on MMHal-Bench it surpasses DoLA (2.33→2.56) and HALVA (2.25→2.56) in score while reducing hallucination rate from 0.56 to 0.47. Notably, REVERSE is the only method that improves simultaneously on both hallucination metrics (CHAIR, Hall) and coverage (Cover) without sacrificing the other, whereas EOS reduces hallucination by producing shorter, less informative captions (Cover drops to 49.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

REVERSE unifies two previously separate paradigms for VLM hallucination mitigation—generation adjustment (VCD, OPERA, DoLA, HALVA, HA-DPO) and post-hoc verification (Woodpecker, LURE)—into a single model that both generates and verifies its own output. The confidence-token mechanism (<SPAN>, </CN>, </UN>) draws on the broader LLM literature on explicit uncertainty modeling (e.g., [IDK] tokens, confidence routing), but repurposes it for phrase-level visual grounding in VLMs. The retrospective resampling step (backtracking + rejection sampling + query rewriting) is a novel inference-time procedure that departs from prior post-hoc methods by enabling iterative, in-place correction rather than a single external rewrite pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first user-controllable knob (threshold τ) for trading off hallucination rate against content coverage in a single VLM, which is practically valuable for deployment in safety-critical settings. The released 1.3M semi-synthetic dataset and training pipeline lower the barrier for future hallucination-aware VLM research. Key limitations include no benefit on discriminative (Yes/No) tasks, up to 3× token overhead from correction loops, and inherited biases from MS-COCO-derived data. Open questions include adaptive threshold selection, more efficient correction strategies (e.g., KV-cache reuse), and whether the contrastive training signal from </CN> vs </UN> can be formalized as a preference-optimization objective.
