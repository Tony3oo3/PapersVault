## Problem

Source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) requires adapting a source-trained classifier to an unlabeled target domain without access to raw source data, yet existing methods lack a mechanism for explicit discriminative knowledge transfer under this constraint. Meanwhile, latent diffusion models (LDMs) have been explored almost exclusively for generative tasks, leaving their potential for structured, label-preserving feature transport in discriminative settings largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254889/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target RGB images along with two frozen artifacts released by the source provider: a pre-trained classifier (ResNet-50/101 encoder + linear head) and a compact conditional UNet drift module trained once on source data. At inference, the diffusion module is not invoked; predictions are produced by a single forward pass through the adapted encoder and frozen classifier, yielding standard class labels with no additional runtime overhead beyond a conventional CNN forward pass.

## Method

DVD encodes each source feature's label information into its latent vicinity by fitting a Gaussian prior over its k-nearest neighbors in latent space, then trains a drift-only (deterministic) diffusion network to transport noisy samples sampled from that prior back to the label-consistent source feature. The drift network predicts the global transport vector (z₁ − z₀) rather than per-step increments, guaranteeing straight-line transport without error accumulation. During target adaptation, the frozen drift module is applied to samples drawn from each target feature's own k-NN Gaussian prior, generating source-aligned cues; a Source-Informed Latent Geometry Aggregation (SiLGA) step blends these cues with the target's local k-NN centroid to mitigate label mismatches under large domain gaps. An InfoNCE contrastive loss then aligns the target encoder's outputs with these blended positives, explicitly transferring source decision boundaries without any source data access.

## Results

On the VisDA-C 2017 SFDA benchmark (ResNet-101), DVD achieves 88.9% average target accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline NRC++ (87.8%) by 1.1% and DaC (87.3%) by 1.6%. On Office-Home (ResNet-50), DVD reaches 73.7% average across all domain pairs, surpassing CPD (73.0%) and DaC (72.8%). On Office-31, DVD attains 91.2% average, exceeding CPD (90.8%) and NRC++ (89.5%). In domain generalization, DVD improves over the ResNet-50 baseline by at least 9% on every dataset (e.g., 50.8% on DomainNet vs. 40.2%), and outperforms PromptStyler on PACS (93.8% vs. 93.2%) and DomainNet (50.8% vs. 49.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVD outperforms all 12 compared baselines across the three SFDA benchmarks. On VisDA-C 2017, DVD (88.9%) beats the strongest no-extra-module method NRC++ (87.8%) by 1.1% and DaC (87.3%) by 1.6%, while also surpassing the best method with an additional module (SFADA, 83.4%) by 5.5%. On Office-Home, DVD (73.7%) edges out CPD (73.0%) and DaC (72.8%), and on Office-31 it leads CPD (90.8%) by 0.4% and NRC++ (89.5%) by 1.7%. In domain generalization, DVD surpasses PromptStyler on PACS (93.8% vs. 93.2%), Office-Home (74.5% vs. 73.6%), and DomainNet (50.8% vs. 49.5%), though it trails slightly on VLCS (81.9% vs. 82.3%). The runtime analysis shows DVD's inference cost (26.1 ms, 38.2 FPS) is essentially identical to SHOT and NRC++, confirming that the diffusion module adds no deployment overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

DVD repurposes the deterministic drift-only diffusion mechanism from Heitz et al. (SIGGRAPH 2023) — originally designed for image blending — as a discriminative transport operator in feature space, and combines it with the latent k-NN neighborhood discovery of Huang et al. (ICML 2019) to parameterize local Gaussian priors. Unlike prior diffusion-for-domain-adaptation work (e.g., Benigmim et al., Kim & Chun) that synthesizes pixel-level samples for data augmentation, DVD operates entirely in the encoder's latent space to transfer class-consistent decision boundaries. The paper also extends the standard SFDA protocol by permitting the source provider to release a lightweight auxiliary diffusion module alongside the classifier, framing the problem as 'privacy-preserving domain adaptation' rather than strict source-free adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DVD addresses a genuine gap in SFDA: prior methods (SHOT, DaC, NRC++, CPD) rely on implicit alignment via contrastive losses or prototype matching, but lack an explicit mechanism for transferring source decision boundaries without raw data. The dual benefit — improved source-domain accuracy for the provider and reliable adaptation for the target user — makes the approach practically attractive in privacy-sensitive settings like healthcare. However, the 'privacy-preserving' label is somewhat loose: the frozen diffusion module encodes source discriminative structure, and the relaxed protocol (sharing an extra module) weakens the source-free guarantee. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-image modalities, very large class spaces, or settings where even the auxiliary module cannot be shared.
