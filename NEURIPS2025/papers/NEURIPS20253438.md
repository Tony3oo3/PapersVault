## Problem

Current video diffusion models learn object motion purely as 2D pixel changes, lacking explicit understanding of 3D shape and spatial dynamics. This causes prevalent artifacts such as object morphing, non-physical deformation, and failure in contact-rich scenarios (e.g., hand-object interactions) where out-of-plane geometry is essential for perceiving true shape and motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253438/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single reference image and a text prompt (same conditioning as the base video model) and jointly generates an RGB video and pixel-aligned 3D point trajectories. During training, the PointVid dataset augments standard RGB videos (640×360, ~50 frames) with 3D tracking data stored as three extra channels (u, v, depth) in the same [T,H,W,C] tensor format, with background pixels zeroed out. The UNet variant operates at 448×256/16 frames while the DiT variant uses 1360×768/48 frames.

## Method

The approach has three components: (1) PointVid dataset construction, where foreground objects are segmented via Grounded-SAM-2, sparse 3D points are tracked with SpaTracker, and trajectories are stored as pixel-aligned 3-channel tensors after Kalman filtering and KDTree interpolation; (2) point augmentation in the diffusion denoiser, where the video and point tensors are concatenated along the channel dimension (2C channels) and a bidirectional cross-attention layer is inserted at the input to enforce cross-modality alignment, then the pretrained model (I2VGen-XL or CogVideoX 1.5) is fine-tuned; (3) point regularization, which decodes the denoised latent back to explicit 3D points and applies a reconstruction loss (penalizing position error, velocity discontinuity, and acceleration jumps) plus a rigidity loss (preserving kNN neighbor distances from the reference frame), jointly optimized with the standard diffusion loss to suppress non-physical deformation in the RGB output.

## Results

On VBench, Ours(UNet) achieves the highest subject consistency (SC: 0.960) and background consistency (BC: 0.951) among all tested models, while Ours(DiT) attains the best motion smoothness (MS: 0.992) and aesthetic quality (AQ: 0.453). On the VideoPhy physical commonsense benchmark (PC), Ours(DiT) scores 0.414, substantially outperforming CogVideoX 1.5 (0.365), SVD (0.347), LTX Video (0.334), and I2VGen-XL (0.329). The 3D point MSE drops from 0.2125 (untrained) to 0.0020 (with regularization), confirming effective 3D trajectory prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct UNet baseline I2VGen-XL, Ours(UNet) improves SC by +0.123 (0.960 vs 0.837), BC by +0.058, MS by +0.023, IQ by +0.007, and PC by +0.049, while slightly losing on AQ (−0.009). Against its DiT baseline CogVideoX 1.5, Ours(DiT) improves PC by +0.048 (0.414 vs 0.365), MS by +0.004, AQ by +0.008, and IQ by +0.005, with SC essentially tied (0.918 vs 0.918). Compared to SVD, Ours(UNet) leads on SC (+0.042), BC (+0.016), MS (+0.007), IQ (+0.058), and PC (+0.031), but trails on AQ (−0.010). Against LTX Video, Ours(UNet) wins on SC (+0.070), BC (+0.047), IQ (+0.023), and PC (+0.044), but loses on MS (−0.007) and AQ (−0.024). DynamiCrafter is compared only qualitatively (Figures 1 and 8), where the paper reports that DynamiCrafter exhibits severe hand and object distortions during task-oriented interactions, while the proposed method preserves shape consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends latent video diffusion models (I2VGen-XL, CogVideoX 1.5) by jointly generating RGB video and 3D point trajectories, departing from prior dynamics-aware approaches (PhysGen, MotionCraft, PhysDreamer) that inject 2D controls like optical flow or force/torque without explicit 3D geometry. Rather than pursuing full 3D reconstruction (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting) or 4D generation, it uses sparse 3D point tracking (SpaTracker) as a lightweight pseudo-3D representation aligned to pixel space, making the approach architecture-agnostic and applicable to both UNet and DiT backbones. The rigidity loss, which penalizes changes in local pairwise distances, is a novel regularization that enforces solid-object shape preservation without requiring mesh or volumetric representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized failure mode of video diffusion models—object morphing and non-physical deformation—by injecting 3D shape awareness through a practical, plug-and-play augmentation. The ~86% user preference margin and consistent VideoPhy score improvements across two distinct architectures suggest the technique is broadly applicable to existing video generation pipelines. Key limitations include dependence on point tracking resolution (objects not well-represented by sparse points may still deform) and the base model's inherent quality ceiling. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos, more complex multi-object interactions, and whether denser or learned 3D representations could further close the gap to true physical simulation.
