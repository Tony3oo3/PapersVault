## Problem

Existing approaches for integrating non-text modality representations (e.g., molecular, visual, audio) into text-based LLMs require costly supervised training of projection layers or the LLM itself, restricting on-the-fly adaptation to new domains and modalities. This paper asks whether a text-based LLM can leverage representations from modality-specific foundation models during inference in a completely training-free manner, without paired text-modality data or fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250209/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes high-dimensional FM representation vectors (e.g., 1024-d from Uni-Mol for molecules, ViT-B/16 for images, wav2vec2 for audio) along with few-shot labeled examples, and produces regression predictions (e.g., solubility, permeability, toxicity values). FM representations are injected either as PCA-reduced text strings (occupying ~20 tokens) or as a single embedding-level token per sample, dramatically reducing context window usage compared to raw SMILES (6–21 tokens) or amino acid sequences (103–214 tokens). The OT alignment step requires only ~2 seconds of CPU computation per domain and is computed once, then reused via simple affine transforms at inference.

## Method

ICRL proposes two injection levels for integrating FM representations into a text-based LLM without training: (1) text-level injection via PCA dimensionality reduction, converting reduced vectors into prompt strings, and (2) embedding-level injection that maps FM features directly into the LLM's embedding space using an untrained linear projector followed by Optimal Transport (OT) alignment. The OT alignment matches the per-dimension mean and variance of projected FM representations to a target distribution (either the LLM's own text embeddings or the embeddings of PCA strings), formalized as a distribution-matching objective. The projector is deliberately kept linear (no activation functions), supported by theoretical proofs showing that random Gaussian linear maps concentrate norms and preserve cosine similarities, while nonlinear activations like ReLU systematically inflate pairwise similarities and distort geometric structure. The framework is evaluated across molecular, protein, vision, and audio domains, with mechanistic analyses revealing that ICRL performance depends on representation diversity and alignment with the LLM's embedding distribution, and that the model shifts between task-learning and task-retrieval modes depending on whether raw text is co-present.

## Results

On the ESOL and Lipophilicity regression benchmarks, the training-free OT-PCA+ICL method achieves 1.094 and 1.277 RMSE respectively, using only ~2 seconds of CPU computation and a single injected token per sample. This matches or surpasses lightweight instruction-tuning (MolecularGPT: 1.471/1.157) and supervised pretraining+finetuning (GIMLET: 1.132/1.345) baselines, while remaining behind heavy pretraining+finetuning pipelines (GPT-MolBERTa: 0.477/0.758; SELFormer PT+FT: 0.682/1.005). Across five molecular datasets (ESOL, Caco2_Wang, AqSolDB, LD50_Zhu, AstraZeneca), OT-PCA consistently ranks among the top ICRL variants, with the PCA text-injection method performing best on most datasets when context window is not a constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to MolecularGPT (instruction-tuning, 4×A800, <1 day), ICRL wins on ESOL (1.094 vs 1.471) and average (1.186 vs 1.314) but loses on Lipophilicity (1.277 vs 1.157). Against GIMLET (supervised pretraining + finetuning, ~1 day), ICRL wins on both ESOL (1.094 vs 1.132) and Lipophilicity (1.277 vs 1.345), achieving a lower average RMSE (1.186 vs 1.239) with zero GPU training. However, ICRL is clearly outperformed by the heavy pretraining+finetuning baselines: GPT-MolBERTa achieves 0.477/0.758 RMSE and SELFormer PT+FT achieves 0.682/1.005, both requiring ~2 weeks of multi-GPU training. The paper's key claim is not state-of-the-art accuracy but that a training-free, CPU-only, ~2-second pipeline can match or beat lightweight tuning methods, making it a practical alternative when retraining is infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends in-context learning (ICL) from text-label pairs to representations extracted from modality-specific foundation models (FMs), departing from both standard ICL and multi-modal LLMs that require supervised training for modality alignment. The closest prior work, Vector-ICL, trains a projector to align external model representations into the LLM embedding space; ICRL instead derives the projector training-free via optimal transport (OT) distribution matching or simple PCA stringification. The theoretical analysis builds on the modality gap literature and random matrix concentration results to justify why linear (non-activated) projectors preserve embedding geometry better than nonlinear ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICRL offers a practical 2-second CPU alternative to days/weeks of GPU training for integrating domain-specific FM knowledge into LLMs, which is particularly valuable in resource-constrained or rapidly evolving domains (e.g., new molecular targets, sensor modalities) where paired training data is unavailable. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the molecular domain to vision/audio at scale, whether the performance gap with supervised methods can be closed via lightweight training without destabilizing ICL, and how the dual operational modes (task learning vs. task retrieval) interact with model scale and context length. The finding that representation diversity and text-alignment are both necessary but insufficient conditions for effective ICRL suggests a richer design space for future work.
