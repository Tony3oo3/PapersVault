## Problem

Current quantitative research pipelines suffer from three critical limitations: limited automation requiring extensive human intervention in hypothesis generation, coding, and tuning; poor interpretability of LLM-based agents that produce trading signals without grounded factor construction or transparent model logic; and fragmented optimization where factor mining and model innovation operate in silos without systematic cross-stage feedback. No existing framework unifies these components into a single data-centric, goal-directed research loop that automates the full-stack R&D process.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a market data tensor X ∈ R^{N×T×P} (stocks × time × factor dimensions) and an optimization target, but the LLM backend is never exposed to raw market data—only schema-level information from the Specification Unit, which mitigates temporal data leakage. Outputs are executable Python factor and model code validated through Qlib backtesting, producing a maintained SOTA factor library (with IC ≥ 0.99 cross-correlation deduplication) and standardized performance metrics (IC, ICIR, ARR, IR, MDD, Calmar ratio) under a daily long-short trading strategy with realistic transaction costs.

## Method

R&D-Agent(Q) decomposes quant R&D into five LLM-powered units in a closed loop: Specification (task context and constraints), Synthesis (hypothesis generation via a structured knowledge forest with action-conditioned historical subsets), Implementation (Co-STEER code generation), Validation (backtesting with factor deduplication), and Analysis (evaluation plus a contextual two-armed bandit scheduler using linear Thompson sampling over an 8-dimensional performance state vector to adaptively select between factor and model optimization). The key novel component is Co-STEER, a code-generation agent that integrates DAG-based task scheduling with adaptive complexity scoring, chain-of-thought reasoning, iterative feedback-driven refinement, and a growing knowledge base of task-code-feedback triples enabling cross-task retrieval. The Synthesis Unit adapts its generation strategy based on empirical feedback—increasing complexity on success, introducing novel variables on failure—forming an idea forest that balances exploration and exploitation. Joint factor-model co-optimization is achieved by alternating between the two optimization paths under the bandit scheduler, with each round's hypotheses, implementations, and results persistently stored for cumulative knowledge growth across iterations.

## Results

On CSI 300, R&D-Agent(Q) with o3-mini achieves the best overall performance: IC of 0.0532, ARR of 14.21%, IR of 1.74, and MDD of −7.42%, surpassing all 26 baselines. R&D-Factor (GPT-4o) reaches an ARR of 14.61% using only ~22% of the factors in Alpha 158/360, while R&D-Model (o3-mini) attains the best Rank IC (0.0546) and lowest MDD (−6.94%). On the out-of-sample CSI 500 and NASDAQ 100 (tested 2024–mid-2025), R&D-Agent(Q) (o4-mini) again leads with IR of 2.17 (CSI 500) and 1.77 (NASDAQ 100), confirming cross-market generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against factor libraries, R&D-Factor (GPT-4o) achieves ARR of 14.61% versus Alpha 360's 4.38% and Alpha 158's 5.70%, roughly 2.5× higher returns while using only ~22% of the factors (≈35 vs. 158/360). Against ML baselines, the gap is even wider: DoubleEnsemble (the strongest ML model) reaches ARR of 5.51% and IR of 0.80, while R&D-Agent(Q) (o3-mini) delivers 14.21% ARR and 1.74 IR. Among deep learning models, specialized stock predictors TRA (ARR 6.49%, IR 1.01) and MASTER (ARR 8.96%, IR 1.34) are the closest competitors, yet R&D-Agent(Q) still exceeds them by 7–8 percentage points in ARR and 0.4–0.7 in IR. General-purpose time-series models (PatchTST, Mamba, iTransformer) underperform on predictive metrics (IC < 0.03) despite iTransformer's relatively high ARR (9.79%), confirming a mismatch between generic sequence forecasting and cross-sectional stock prediction. On the out-of-sample CSI 500 and NASDAQ 100 (2024–2025), R&D-Agent(Q) (o4-mini) maintains its lead with IR of 2.17 and 1.77 respectively, outperforming the best baselines (LSTM at IR 0.69 on CSI 500; GATs at IR 0.58 on NASDAQ 100) by a wide margin, validating cross-market and temporal generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qlib's data-processing and backtesting infrastructure and on chain-of-thought prompting for code generation, but departs sharply from prior LLM-in-finance work (FinAgent, TradingAgents, AutoAlpha) that targets narrow subtasks like text-signal extraction or single-factor generation. Where symbolic-regression and RL-based factor miners optimize one component in isolation, R&D-Agent(Q) introduces a closed-loop, multi-agent pipeline that co-optimizes factors and models simultaneously, with a contextual Thompson-sampling scheduler deciding which axis to refine next. The Co-STEER code-generation agent unifies scheduling (DAG-based task ordering), reasoning (CoT), iterative self-correction, and a persistent knowledge base—capabilities that prior code-gen methods (Reflexion, Self-Planning, Self-Debugging) each address only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a fully automated, LLM-driven R&D loop can match or exceed handcrafted factor libraries and specialized deep architectures at a fraction of the factor count and under $10 in API cost, which is practically significant for quant teams seeking to scale research throughput. However, the framework relies entirely on the LLM's internal financial knowledge rather than external domain priors, and its evaluation, while spanning three markets, is concentrated on a single time window per market, leaving open questions about robustness across extended regime shifts and whether the bandit scheduler's advantage persists over hundreds of iterations. The data-centric design (LLM never sees raw prices) is a clean anti-leakage guarantee, but it also caps the information the agent can exploit, suggesting future work on integrating structured external signals (earnings calls, macro releases) without breaking the interpretability guarantee.
