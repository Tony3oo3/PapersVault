## Problem

Existing model-free RL algorithms face a fundamental trade-off: near-optimal regret methods like UCB-Advantage and FedQ-Advantage require superlinear burn-in costs in the number of states S and actions A, while Q-EarlySettled-Advantage achieves linear burn-in but incurs a linearly scaling switching cost. No prior model-free algorithm simultaneously achieves near-optimal regret Õ(√(H²SAT)), a burn-in cost linear in S and A, and logarithmic switching or communication cost in either the single-agent or federated setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on tabular episodic MDPs with S states, A actions, and H steps per episode, where rewards are deterministic and bounded in [0,1] and initial states are chosen adversarially. In the federated variant, M agents each interact with an independent MDP; the central server broadcasts the current policy and value estimates, while agents return six local aggregation sums per visited state-action-step (covering V, LCB-V, reference-V, squared reference-V, advantage-V, and squared advantage-V). The output is a deterministic policy mapping each state-step to an action, maintained alongside Q-value, V-value, lower-bound, and reference function estimates that are updated only at round boundaries rather than per episode.

## Method

The algorithm employs a round-based design with event-triggered termination: policies are updated only when a visit-count threshold is met across all agents, ensuring logarithmic switching/communication cost while allowing heterogeneous exploration speeds. Within each round, the server maintains three Q-estimates per state-action-step—a UCB-type for optimism, an LCB-type for early reference settlement, and a reference-advantage-type that decomposes value into a settled reference plus a learned advantage—combined via a min operation to preserve optimism. The reference function is settled early when the UCB–LCB V-gap drops below a threshold β, reducing burn-in cost to Õ(SAH¹⁰) instead of the superlinear thresholds used by prior methods. A key technical innovation is the surrogate reference function, which resolves the simultaneous non-adaptiveness of round-based weights and the settled reference in the regret proof, enabling round-wise concentration inequalities and yielding a log(SAT) regret improvement over Q-EarlySettled-Advantage. The cumulative bonus term is further refined by removing its (N_h^k)^(3/4) dependence, tightening the overall regret bound.

## Results

Q-EarlySettled-LowCost achieves a worst-case regret of O((1+β)√(H²SAT·log²(SAT/p)) + H⁶SA·log²(SAT/p)/β), matching the information-theoretic lower bound Õ(√(H²SAT)) up to logarithmic factors when T > Õ(SAH¹⁰). The switching cost is O(H³SA·log(T/(HSA))), growing only logarithmically in T. In the federated setting, FedQ-EarlySettled-LowCost achieves regret Õ(√(MH²SAT)) with burn-in cost Õ(MSAH¹⁰) (linear in S and A) and total communication cost O(M²H⁴S²A·log(T)). Numerical experiments on synthetic tabular MDPs with (H,S,A)=(5,3,2) and (7,10,5) confirm that both algorithms achieve the lowest regret among all compared model-free baselines, with regret curves approaching horizontal lines when normalized by log(T/H+1), consistent with the gap-dependent logarithmic regret bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-EarlySettled-LowCost is the first model-free single-agent RL algorithm to simultaneously achieve all three desirable properties: near-optimal regret Õ(√(H²SAT)), logarithmic switching cost, and linear-in-S,A burn-in cost Õ(SAH¹⁰). Compared to UCB-Advantage [111], which also achieves near-optimal regret and logarithmic switching cost, the key improvement is reducing the burn-in cost from Õ(S⁶A³H²⁸) to Õ(SAH¹⁰), eliminating the superlinear dependence on S and A. Compared to Q-EarlySettled-Advantage [50], which achieves the same low burn-in cost, the key improvement is reducing the switching cost from linear in T to logarithmic in T, while also improving the regret bound by a factor of log(SAT/p) through a refined bonus term. In the federated setting, FedQ-EarlySettled-LowCost eliminates the superlinear S³A² dependence in the burn-in cost of FedQ-Advantage [114] (reducing it from Õ(MS³A²H¹²) to Õ(MSAH¹⁰)) while maintaining the same near-optimal regret Õ(√(MH²SAT)) and logarithmic communication cost. Numerical experiments confirm that both proposed algorithms consistently achieve the lowest regret among all compared model-free baselines across both MDP configurations tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously incompatible design choices in model-free RL: the LCB-based early reference function settlement from Q-EarlySettled-Advantage [50] (which achieves low burn-in cost but per-episode policy updates) and the round-based infrequent-update design from UCB-Advantage [111] and FedQ-Advantage [114] (which achieves logarithmic switching/communication cost but superlinear burn-in in S and A). The key technical bridge is adapting the surrogate reference function from the gap-dependent analysis of [115] into the worst-case regret proof, resolving a 'double non-adaptiveness' challenge where both the aggregation weights and the settled reference function are non-adaptive to the data. In the federated setting, the paper further introduces LCB into the FedQ-Advantage framework [114] for the first time, combining it with the round-wise approximation techniques from [113].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a gap in the model-free RL literature by showing that near-optimal regret, linear-in-S-and-A burn-in cost, and logarithmic switching/communication cost are simultaneously achievable—answering two open questions that had resisted resolution. The burn-in reduction from S^6 A^3 (UCB-Advantage) to SA is practically significant for large-scale tabular problems, and the log(SAT) regret improvement over Q-EarlySettled-Advantage addresses a known sensitivity to logarithmic factors in T. Limitations include the restriction to tabular episodic MDPs with deterministic rewards; extending these techniques to function approximation, stochastic rewards, or non-episodic (continuing) settings remains open. The surrogate reference function technique, originally developed for gap-dependent analysis, may prove broadly useful for other RL algorithms facing similar non-adaptiveness challenges in their proofs.
