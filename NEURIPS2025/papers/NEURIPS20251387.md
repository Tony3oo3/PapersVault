## Problem

The fundamental training dynamics of transformers remain poorly characterized beyond task-specific settings such as in-context learning or linear regression, leaving no general, task-agnostic framework for how attention and feed-forward components interact during optimization. Although small initialization has been empirically linked to improved reasoning in large language models, the theoretical mechanisms governing parameter evolution—particularly the sequential activation of outer versus attention parameters—lack a principled explanation.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a binary classification dataset of input sequences (s × d_m) with ±1 labels and initializes all transformer parameters from N(0, ε²) with ε ≪ 1, using exponential loss ℓ(q) = e^{-q} under gradient flow. The theoretical output is a two-stage dynamical characterization: outer parameters (W_V, W^[1], W^[2]) first condense row-wise toward a data-dependent direction v while W_Q and W_K remain quasi-static, followed by asymptotic rank-1 collapse of the normalized key-query matrices. Experiments validate these predictions on synthetic anchor-function data and WikiText using one- and two-layer transformers with tanh or GeLU activations.

## Method

The paper performs a perturbative expansion of the empirical risk in powers of ε, yielding an effective gradient flow (Eq. 7) that decouples outer parameters from attention parameters during early training. In Stage 1, conservation laws and a Riccati-type differential inequality prove finite-time blow-up of the energy E = W_v W^[1] W^[2], while a condensation condition (Assumption 1) guarantees W_V rows align toward the target direction v via geometric propagation and two-sided energy control. In Stage 2, once outer parameters reach a quasi-steady state (Assumption 2), the key-query dynamics reduce to the linear ODE system dW_Q/dt = FW_K, dW_K/dt = F^T W_Q, where F is a data-dependent matrix built from the frozen outer parameters. The unique largest singular value of F then drives both normalized matrices to asymptotic rank-1 collapse (Theorem 3), generalizing classical directional convergence results. The framework is validated through cosine similarity matrices, effective rank tracking, and norm evolution on both synthetic and real datasets.

## Results

The paper establishes a two-stage training dynamics framework for transformers under small initialization. In Stage 1 (Condensation), outer parameters (W_V, W^[1], W^[2]) rapidly align toward a task-relevant direction, with the proportion of Assumption 1 conditions satisfied approaching 1 within ~200 training steps, and effective rank dropping monotonically. In Stage 2 (Key-Query Rank Collapse), after outer parameters reach a quasi-steady state, the previously static W_Q and W_K matrices undergo active training, driving their normalized effective rank to collapse (asymptotically to rank 1 when F has a unique largest singular value). These dynamics are confirmed on both a synthetic anchor-function dataset (one-layer transformer) and WikiText (two-layer transformer with GeLU and residual connections), where the same three-phase trajectory—condensation, key-query rank collapse, and further training—is observed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis and phenomenological study rather than a method-comparison paper, so no direct baselines are benchmarked. The paper's primary validation is internal: it verifies that its theoretical predictions (blow-up in finite time, condensation under Assumption 1, asymptotic rank collapse under Assumption 2) are consistent with empirical training trajectories on both synthetic and real data. The synthetic anchor-function experiments confirm that the condensation condition (Assumption 1) is satisfied with near-100% proportion early in training, and that the dynamics separation (Assumption 2/2*) holds during Stage 2, with outer parameter directions remaining nearly frozen (cosine similarity between adjacent-step singular vectors ≈ 1) while attention parameter norms remain O(δ). The WikiText experiments with a two-layer transformer confirm that the two-stage pattern generalizes beyond the one-layer binary classification setting analyzed theoretically, though the paper acknowledges in its limitations that the theoretical proofs are restricted to binary classification and single-layer architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-flow condensation framework of Zhou et al. (2022) and the conservation-law techniques of Ji and Telgarsky (2019) from deep linear networks to the attention module of transformers. Prior transformer dynamics analyses have been tightly coupled to specific tasks (in-context linear regression, Markov chain prediction, topic structure), whereas this work explicitly targets a task-independent characterization by isolating the effective dynamics of outer parameters versus key-query matrices under small initialization. The two-stage decomposition—outer-parameter condensation followed by key-query rank collapse—generalizes classical directional convergence results and provides a unified lens that subsumes several task-specific findings in the ICL and induction-head literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, task-agnostic explanation for why small initialization improves reasoning in large language models: it first drives outer parameters into a low-rank condensed state, then activates the attention mechanism to refine representations via rank collapse. This two-stage picture gives a concrete mechanistic account of the implicit regularization that practitioners have observed empirically but lacked a unified theoretical description for. Key limitations include the restriction to binary classification (acknowledged by the authors), the omission of layer normalization and multi-head attention in the theory, and the reliance on Assumptions 1 and 2 whose necessity is only empirically supported. Extending the framework to multi-class settings, deeper architectures, and realistic optimizers remains an important open direction.
