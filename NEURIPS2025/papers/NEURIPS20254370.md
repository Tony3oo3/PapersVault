## Problem

Existing approximate machine unlearning methods based on label manipulation or weight perturbation achieve limited unlearning effectiveness, often degrading utility on retain data or requiring access to forget data. The paper identifies that the gold-standard Retrain model classifies forget samples into clusters of retain samples with the highest semantic similarity, but no prior approximate method replicates this behavior, leaving a persistent performance gap with exact unlearning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254370/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CoUn takes a pre-trained Original model (ResNet-18, VGG-16, or ViT) and a batch of retain images with their class labels as the sole training signal—no forget data is required. Images are standard RGB at 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) or 64×64 (TinyImageNet), augmented via random cropping, horizontal flipping, and color normalization. The output is an unlearned model θ_u initialized from θ_o, trained for 50 epochs with a combined cross-entropy and contrastive loss, evaluated on retain accuracy, unlearn accuracy, test accuracy, and membership inference attack success rate.

## Method

CoUn applies a SimCLR-style InfoNCE contrastive loss exclusively on retain data, pulling two augmented views of the same sample together while pushing all other batch samples apart. Because the batch contains false negatives (same-class samples treated as negatives), this induces cluster collision that indirectly shifts forget representations toward semantically similar retain clusters—mimicking how the Retrain model classifies forget data. A cross-entropy supervised loss on retain data counteracts cluster collision for retain samples, preserving utility. The final objective is L = L_CE + λ·L_CL, where λ balances the two terms. Theoretically, the paper proves via a generalization bound from Huang et al. that the misclassification rate on forget data exceeds that on retain data, since the alignment probability R[ε] is larger for forget data (higher local Lipschitz constant) while supervised learning keeps retain misclassification low.

## Results

The primary metric is the average gap (Avg. Gap) between each method's four evaluation metrics (Retain Accuracy, Unlearn Accuracy, Test Accuracy, MIA Efficacy) and the gold-standard Retrain model; lower is better. On CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 with 10% random forgetting, CoUn achieves an Avg. Gap of 0.25, outperforming the best baseline NoT (0.30) by 16.7%. On CIFAR-100/ResNet-18, CoUn reaches 1.39 vs. NegGrad+ℓ₁-sparse's 1.67 (15.8% improvement), and on CIFAR-100/ViT, CoUn achieves 3.80 vs. NegGrad+ℓ₁-sparse's 3.98 (4.5% improvement). Under 50% random forgetting (Table 3), CoUn maintains its lead, e.g., 0.40 on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 vs. NegGrad+ℓ₁-sparse's 0.90.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoUn outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset–architecture combination. Against the strongest competitors, CoUn reduces the Avg. Gap by 16.7% over NoT (0.25 vs. 0.30) on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, by 15.8% over NegGrad+ℓ₁-sparse (1.39 vs. 1.67) on CIFAR-100/ResNet-18, and by 4.5% over NegGrad+ℓ₁-sparse (3.80 vs. 3.98) on CIFAR-100/ViT. FT and SalUn show the largest gaps (up to 14.78 on CIFAR-100/ViT for FT), confirming that simple fine-tuning or random relabeling fails to match the Retrain model's behavior on forget data. The additional baselines BadT, SSD, SCRUB, and CU (Table 7) all underperform CoUn; for instance, on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, CU achieves 0.61 and SSD 3.83 Avg. Gap versus CoUn's 0.25. Notably, integrating CoUn's CL module into NegGrad+, ℓ₁-sparse, and NoT yields 42–45% Avg. Gap reductions at 10% forgetting and up to 87.7% at 50% forgetting, demonstrating the CL module's transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoUn sits at the intersection of machine unlearning (MU) and contrastive learning (CL). It departs from label-manipulation MU methods (NegGrad+, Amnesiac, SalUn, BadT, SCRUB) that require access to forget data and from weight-perturbation methods (ℓ₁-sparse, NoT, SSD) that risk erasing useful knowledge, by applying CL exclusively on retain data and exploiting the cluster-collision phenomenon to indirectly shift forget representations. The closest prior work is Zhang et al.'s Contrastive Unlearning [33], which uses supervised CL to push forget samples away from same-class retain samples; CoUn instead uses self-supervised CL (SimCLR-style InfoNCE) on retain data only, requiring no forget data access and not forcing misclassification. The theoretical grounding draws on Huang et al.'s [35] generalization bounds for CL, adapted to the unlearning setting via a Lipschitz-constant argument distinguishing retain from forget data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoUn offers a practical, forget-data-free unlearning mechanism whose CL module can be plugged into existing baselines (NegGrad+, ℓ₁-sparse, NoT) to yield 40–88% average-gap reductions, making it a potentially reusable component for the MU community. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 1 + Lemma 1) provides the first CL-based misclassification-rate bound specifically for the unlearning setting, connecting representation alignment to forget quality. Limitations include sensitivity to the scaling factor λ and temperature τ, a modest increase in computational cost from the second forward pass, and evaluation restricted to image classification on CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet. Open questions include whether the approach extends to federated unlearning (where forget data is unavailable by design), to generative models, and to larger-scale benchmarks like ImageNet.
