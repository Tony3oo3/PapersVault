## Problem

Existing GNN explanation methods are fragile to small structural perturbations, yet prior robustness-enhancing approaches either retrain the model (producing explanations that no longer reflect the original model) or ensemble multiple explanations (yielding contradictory importance assignments). No prior work has explored whether intrinsic geometric properties of graph edges can directly bound and improve explanation robustness without modifying the model or aggregating multiple explanations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251031/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained GNN, an input graph with node features and edges, and edge importance scores F(u,v) produced by any base explanation method (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, GNN-LRP, DeepLIFT, FlowX, or Convex). It additionally computes Ollivier-Ricci curvature κ(u,v) and effective resistance R(u,v) for every edge in the graph. The output is a binary edge-selection mask x(u,v) defining a robust explanation subgraph G_s of fixed size |E_s|, where edges are chosen to jointly maximize importance and geometric robustness scores.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorems 4.3–4.8) that the minimum Ollivier-Ricci curvature and average effective resistance over edges in the T-hop neighborhood or explanation subgraph provide upper bounds on the change in model prediction probability under edge perturbations, establishing a formal link between graph geometry and explanation robustness. Building on this, the method defines a combinatorial selection objective (Equation 7) that maximizes the sum of x(u,v)·(F(u,v) + λ·C(u,v)) over edges, where C(u,v) is either κ(u,v) (curvature-based) or −R(u,v) (resistance-based), and λ is tuned via Optuna on a training split. Because curvature and resistance are negatively correlated, the two variants are applied separately rather than combined. The approach is entirely post-hoc and model-agnostic: it requires no retraining, no ensemble of multiple explanations, and can be plugged into any existing GNN explanation method by simply re-weighting its edge importance scores with the geometric robustness scores before subgraph selection.

## Results

Across nine datasets and six explanation methods, the curvature- and resistance-based robust explanations consistently improve relative error (robustness) over their base counterparts. The largest gains appear for Convex (e.g., Citeseer: 41.8% → 60.6% with curvature; 32.6% → 40.5% with resistance) and GNNExplainer (Cora: 4.5% → 16.0% with curvature). Fidelity_KL is maintained or improved in 80–85% of cases depending on the geometric metric and aggregation function. The paper reports up to a 10% relative improvement in robustness, with the method being agnostic to the underlying explanation algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement on top of six existing GNN explanation baselines: GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, Convex, DeepLIFT, GNN-LRP, and FlowX. Curvature-based enhancement wins on the majority of dataset-method pairs for relative error: GNNExplainer improves from −6.0% to 2.4% on Citeseer and from 4.5% to 16.0% on Cora; Convex shows the largest absolute gains (e.g., +18.8 pp on Citeseer, +11.1 pp on BC-OTC); GNN-LRP flips from negative to positive on Citeseer (−2.0% → 6.2%). Resistance-based enhancement (Table 3) similarly dominates, with Convex reaching 72.7% on BC-OTC (vs. 63.7% base) and GNNExplainer jumping from 19.8% to 25.6% on BC-OTC. Fidelity_KL is preserved or slightly improved in 80–85% of cases, though GNN-LRP and FlowX occasionally show small fidelity drops when importance scores are near-uniform. No baseline is consistently beaten by the base method over the enhanced version across all nine datasets, confirming the geometric scores provide a uniform robustness lift regardless of the underlying explanation algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the robustness metric δ* from Fang et al. (NeurIPS 2024) and the geometric analyses of Ollivier-Ricci curvature (Nguyen et al., ICML 2023) and effective resistance (Black et al., ICML 2023), which were previously studied in the context of over-squashing and over-smoothing in GNNs. It departs from prior robust-explanation approaches that either retrain the model with regularization (altering the target model) or aggregate explanations from perturbed graphs (risking contradictory results), instead introducing a model-agnostic geometric scoring mechanism. The key conceptual leap is repurposing curvature and resistance—previously linked to information-flow bottlenecks—as robustness scores that can be additively combined with any base explanation method's importance scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical proof that graph geometric properties (minimum Ricci curvature, average effective resistance) can bound both model-prediction robustness and explanation robustness, offering a principled, model-agnostic way to enhance explanation reliability without retraining or ensembling. The practical impact is moderate: consistent but modest improvements (up to 10% relative robustness gain, 80% fidelity improvement) across six explanation methods and nine datasets. A key limitation is that the analysis is restricted to structural (edge) perturbations; extending the geometric robustness bounds to feature-space perturbations remains an open and important direction for high-stakes applications where node features may also be adversarially modified.
