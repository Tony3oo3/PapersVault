## Problem

Multi-target instance-agnostic adversarial attacks suffer from low transferability due to limited generator capacity when conditioning a single model on multiple target classes. Existing diffusion-based attacks are all instance-specific, requiring gradient access to the victim model at inference time, and the choice between stochastic and deterministic sampling for adversarial perturbation generation remains unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252351/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a target class label as input, where the label is encoded via CLIP text embeddings (formatted as 'a photo of a {class}') and injected through cross-attention into a LoRA-augmented U-Net. It produces an adversarial perturbation δ constrained to ‖δ‖∞ ≤ ε (default 16/255) and the corresponding perturbed image x^ε = x + δ. Once trained, inference requires no gradient access to any classifier, making it fully instance-agnostic and black-box compatible.

## Method

Dual-Flow decomposes adversarial perturbation generation into two cascaded ODE flows: a forward flow using a pretrained Stable Diffusion velocity function v_φ maps the input image from X to an intermediate distribution X_τ (at τ=0.25, N=6 steps), and a reverse flow using a fine-tuned LoRA-based velocity function v_θ (rank 16) maps X_τ back to the constrained output space X^ε. The key training innovation is Cascading Distribution Shift Training, which circumvents the inaccessibility of exact intermediate ODE samples by iteratively predicting x_0 at each reverse step, applying dynamic gradient clipping to enforce the ℓ∞ bound, and updating v_θ with cross-entropy loss against the source model. A theoretical result (Cascading Improvement at Adjoint Timesteps) proves that updating θ at each timestep yields equal or lower cross-entropy at subsequent timesteps, formalizing the coarse-to-fine refinement. The authors demonstrate that the deterministic Cascading ODE formulation substantially outperforms both Cascading SDE and Random SDE variants, with the latter suffering from distribution mismatch during training.

## Results

On the ImageNet-NeurIPS validation set with ε=16/255, Dual-Flow achieves 73.96% average black-box attack success rate (ASR) using Inception-v3 as source model, outperforming the best prior multi-target method CGNC (52.80%) by 21.16 percentage points. With ResNet-152 as source, Dual-Flow reaches 70.76% average ASR versus CGNC's 58.40% (+12.36 pp). Against robustly trained models, Dual-Flow improves average ASR from 28.60% (CGNC) to 62.28% (Inc-v3 source) and from 51.06% to 66.94% (Res-152 source), demonstrating substantially stronger robustness against adversarial defenses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual-Flow decisively outperforms all 12 baselines across both source models. Against the closest multi-target generative competitors, it beats CGNC (Fang et al., ECCV 2025) by 21.16 pp (Inc-v3 source) and 12.36 pp (Res-152 source) in average black-box ASR, and C-GSP (Yang et al., ECCV 2022) by 22.88 pp and 23.73 pp respectively. Instance-specific methods (MIM, TI-MIM, SI-MIM, DIM, Logit, SU) achieve only 0.38–12.80% average transfer ASR, confirming their poor generalization to unseen models. In single-target comparisons (Table 2), the fine-tuned Dual-Flow† reaches 76.79% (Inc-v3 source) and 76.12% (Res-152 source), surpassing the best single-target generative baselines DGTA-PI (52.77%/65.42%) and CGNC† (70.00%/68.84%). Against robustly trained models (Table 3), Dual-Flow's advantage is even more pronounced: 62.28% vs. CGNC's 28.60% (Inc-v3 source) and 66.94% vs. 51.06% (Res-152 source), with the gap widening on adversarially trained Inception-v3 (51.54% vs. 24.36%) and ensemble-adversarial Inception-ResNet-v2 (55.62% vs. 22.54%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of multi-target, instance-agnostic generative attacks (C-GSP, CGNC) by replacing GAN-based or CLIP-guided generators with a flow-based architecture built on Stable Diffusion. The key conceptual departure is the Dual-Flow design: a frozen pretrained diffusion ODE serves as the forward flow to an intermediate distribution, while a LoRA-fine-tuned velocity function acts as the reverse flow for adversarial refinement. The Cascading Distribution Shift Training strategy is novel, addressing the previously unaddressed challenge of training a reverse flow when exact intermediate samples along the ODE trajectory are inaccessible. Unlike prior diffusion-based attacks (AdvDiffuser, DiffPGD, DiffAttack), which are all instance-specific and require classifier gradients at inference, Dual-Flow is fully instance-agnostic once trained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for adversarial robustness evaluation: a single trained model can generate transferable multi-target attacks without any gradient access to the victim, and the method maintains high success rates even against adversarially trained defenses where prior methods drop to near-zero. The finding that deterministic ODE-based cascading substantially outperforms SDE-based variants (which more closely mirror standard diffusion sampling) challenges the intuition that stochasticity aids distribution matching in this adversarial context. Open questions include whether Schrödinger bridge formulations could close the gap for SDE-based approaches, and whether the method extends to video or 3D modalities where the perturbation budget and distributional structure differ fundamentally.
