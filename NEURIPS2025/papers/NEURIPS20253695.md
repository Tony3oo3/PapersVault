## Problem

Training-free methods for subject-consistent visual story generation face a fundamental trade-off: directly sharing visual features across images (e.g., StoryDiffusion, ConsisStory) weakens prompt fidelity and generative diversity, while relying solely on unified prompts (e.g., 1Prompt1Story) fails to maintain subject identity when prompts lack detail. No existing approach simultaneously achieves strong subject consistency and faithful text alignment without per-subject fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253695/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two types of text input: an identity prompt describing only the subject's visual characteristics (used once to cache subject-relevant visual tokens), and a sequence of scene-level prompts (one per story image, concatenated into a single fused prompt with per-scene reweighting). It outputs a sequence of RGB images (one per scene prompt) in which the subject's identity is preserved across all frames while each image reflects its corresponding scene description. The method is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on both U-Net-based SDXL (50 steps) and transformer-based FLUX (28 steps).

## Method

The core contribution is Asymmetric Zigzag Sampling (AZS), which decomposes each diffusion denoising step into three sub-steps—zig (forward denoise), zag (inverse denoise), and generation (forward denoise)—and applies asymmetric conditioning across them. During the zig step, subject-specific visual tokens (top-k key/value projections selected via text-image attention scores from an identity-only prompt) are concatenated into the self-attention layers, injecting identity semantics directly into the latent representation. The zag step performs inverse denoising with a null prompt, propagating the injected identity into the noisy latent without conflicting text supervision. The generation step uses the full reweighted one-prompt embedding with classifier-free guidance to align the output with the scene description. This asymmetric design—visual injection only in zig, null prompt only in zag, full text guidance in zig and generation—prevents the over-conditioning and prompt-identity conflicts that degrade prior methods.

## Results

On the SDXL backbone, the proposed Asymmetry Zigzag Sampling (AZS) achieves the best performance among all training-free methods: CLIP-T 0.8946, CLIP-I 0.923, and DreamSim 0.1798. On the FLUX backbone, AZS similarly leads with CLIP-T 0.8949, CLIP-I 0.9216, and DreamSim 0.1843. In the user study, images generated by AZS received the highest human preference at 35.02%, compared to 24.50% for the next-best method (1Prompt1Story). The method narrows the DreamSim gap to the strongest training-based baseline (IP-Adapter, 0.1462) by 63.27% relative to the previous best training-free result.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free methods on SDXL, AZS outperforms 1Prompt1Story (the closest competitor) on all three metrics: CLIP-T 0.8946 vs 0.8942, CLIP-I 0.923 vs 0.9117, and DreamSim 0.1798 vs 0.1993. It also clearly surpasses StoryDiffusion (DreamSim 0.1798 vs 0.3212) and ConsiStory (DreamSim 0.1798 vs 0.3188), indicating substantially better subject consistency. Against the training-based IP-Adapter, AZS trades a small amount of subject consistency (CLIP-I 0.923 vs 0.9429, DreamSim 0.1798 vs 0.1462) for a large gain in prompt alignment (CLIP-T 0.8946 vs 0.8458), which the authors attribute to IP-Adapter's tendency to overemphasize identity at the expense of text fidelity. On the FLUX backbone, AZS beats the re-implemented 1Prompt1Story baseline on every metric (CLIP-T 0.8949 vs 0.8716, CLIP-I 0.9216 vs 0.9118, DreamSim 0.1843 vs 0.1957). In the human preference study, AZS is preferred 35.02% of the time versus 24.50% for 1Prompt1Story, 19.18% for ConsiStory, 16.25% for StoryDiffusion, and 5.15% for IP-Adapter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the zigzag sampling framework (which decomposes each denoising step into zig, zag, and generation sub-steps) and the attention-based subject token extraction from ConsisStory, while adapting the one-prompt fusion and IPCA techniques from 1Prompt1Story. It departs from prior training-free methods (StoryDiffusion, ConsisStory) that inject subject information uniformly across all generation steps by introducing an asymmetric design: visual tokens are injected only during the zig (exploration) step, and a null prompt is used during the zag (refinement) step to prevent text-driven interference with subject identity. This positions the work as a targeted refinement of the zigzag sampling paradigm, applying the distinct functional roles of each sub-step to the specific problem of multi-image subject consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent tension in training-free story generation: methods that enforce subject consistency tend to degrade prompt fidelity, and vice versa. The asymmetric zigzag design offers a principled resolution by separating the roles of identity grounding (zig), latent refinement (zag), and text alignment (generation), validated across both UNet (SDXL) and transformer (FLUX) architectures. However, the ~3× inference time overhead limits practical deployment, and the authors note occasional failures on SDXL, suggesting architecture-specific tuning remains an open problem. A key open question is whether the three-sub-step decomposition can be made more efficient without sacrificing the consistency gains.
