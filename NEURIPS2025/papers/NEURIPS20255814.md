## Problem

Existing causal effect estimation methods require interventional data, knowledge of the ground-truth causal graph, or strong assumptions like unconfoundedness, limiting their applicability in real-world settings. This paper asks whether the prior-data fitted network (PFN) paradigm—pre-trained on synthetic data to solve tabular prediction via in-context learning—can be extended to estimate causal effects from observational data alone, without any graph knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255814/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Do-PFN takes as input an entire observational dataset of (t, x, y) tuples (fed in-context, zero-padded to handle varying sizes and dimensionalities) plus a query consisting of an intervention value t and covariate vector x. It outputs the full conditional interventional distribution p(y|do(t), x) parameterized as a bar distribution, from which CATE and ATE are derived as differences of conditional means. Unlike point-estimate methods, the output is a complete predictive distribution that captures aleatoric, epistemic, and unidentifiability uncertainty.

## Method

Do-PFN is a 7.3M-parameter transformer (architecturally similar to TabPFN) pre-trained via prior-fitting on millions of synthetic structural causal models sampled from a diverse prior over DAGs, nonlinearities, and noise. During pre-training, each iteration samples an SCM, generates both an observational dataset and an interventional dataset (by removing incoming edges to the treatment node and fixing its value), then performs one SGD step to minimize the negative log-likelihood of predicting interventional outcomes given the observational context. The key insight is that this objective meta-learns the appropriate causal adjustment (back-door, front-door, etc.) implicitly across millions of graph structures, eliminating the need for explicit graph knowledge at test time. Theoretically, the paper proves this training minimizes the expected forward KL divergence to the true CID, and establishes a consistency result showing the posterior concentrates on the Markov equivalence class of the true SCM as observational data grows infinite.

## Results

Do-PFN is evaluated using normalized mean squared error (MSE) for CID prediction, CATE estimation, and ATE estimation across six synthetic case studies (100 datasets each), the RealCause benchmark (4 dataset groups), and two known-graph real-world datasets. On synthetic data, Do-PFN significantly outperforms all tabular regression baselines (Random Forest, TabPFN v2, Dont-PFN) in CID prediction, and significantly outperforms meta-learners (S/T/X-Learner), Causal Forest DML, TARNet, and DragonNet in CATE estimation. On RealCause, Do-PFN (v1.1) achieves strong performance on 3 of 4 dataset groups and Pareto-optimal speed–accuracy tradeoff; on Law School Admissions it achieves the best CATE performance, and on Amazon Sales it is not significantly worse than the best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against tabular regression baselines (Random Forest, TabPFN v2, Dont-PFN), Do-PFN significantly outperforms all three in CID prediction, with the gap to Dont-PFN (a regression model trained on the same prior but without interventional pre-training) providing direct evidence that the interventional pre-training objective captures something fundamentally different from standard posterior predictive estimation. Against gold-standard DoWhy baselines, Do-PFN is competitive with DoWhy (Int.) in CID prediction and with DoWhy (Cntf.) in CATE estimation, despite having no access to the ground-truth causal graph. Against meta-learners (S/T/X-Learner with TabPFN or HPO), Causal Forest DML, and deep-learning methods (TARNet, DragonNet), Do-PFN significantly outperforms all of them in CATE and ATE estimation on synthetic data, and remains more robust when unconfoundedness is violated (e.g., in Front-Door and Unobserved Confounder settings) while baselines degrade substantially. On the RealCause benchmark, where unconfoundedness is guaranteed by construction (inherently favoring the baselines), Do-PFN still remains competitive and achieves Pareto-optimal inference speed. On the known-graph real-world datasets, Do-PFN achieves the strongest CATE performance on Law School Admissions and is not significantly worse than the best method on Amazon Sales, a dataset with complex mediation structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

Do-PFN directly extends the Prior-data Fitted Network (PFN) framework (Müller et al., 2022; Hollmann et al., 2023, 2025) from predictive tabular tasks to causal effect estimation, repurposing the in-context learning paradigm to predict conditional interventional distributions rather than posterior predictive distributions. It departs from classical causal inference (do-calculus, back-door/front-door adjustment, unconfoundedness-based meta-learners) by replacing explicit graph knowledge or identifiability assumptions with amortized inference over a prior on structural causal models. Among prior amortized causal inference efforts (Ke et al., 2022; Lorch et al., 2022; Sauter et al., 2025; Bynum et al., 2025), Do-PFN is the first to target the full CID with a transformer architecture and to provide a KL-optimality guarantee (Proposition 1) plus a consistency theorem (Proposition 3) characterizing convergence to the Markov equivalence class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Causal effect estimation underpins medicine, economics, and the social sciences, yet current methods require RCTs, known causal graphs, or unverifiable unconfoundedness assumptions; Do-PFN offers a path to estimating CIDs and CATEs from observational data alone without any of these, which could substantially lower the barrier to causal analysis. Key open questions include how well the synthetic prior captures real-world causal complexity (the paper's validation is predominantly synthetic), whether the approach extends beyond binary interventions and i.i.d. data to counterfactuals and longitudinal settings, and what principled methods exist for prior design and validation. The consistency result clarifies that unidentifiability-induced uncertainty is fundamental and cannot be eliminated by more data, which is an important theoretical boundary for any causal estimator.
