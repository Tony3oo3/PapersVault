## Problem

Latent diffusion models for inpainting and local editing introduce persistent pixel-level artifacts—chromatic shifts, texture mismatches, and visible seams at editing boundaries—due to inherent latent compression and spatial entanglement in the VAE. Existing remedies such as background-conditioned latent decoding and post-hoc harmonization fail to fully eliminate these artifacts and do not generalize across different latent representations or editing tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254271/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The refiner takes a partially composited RGB image (the LDM output with the original background pasted back outside the mask) and a binary mask delineating the edited region, and produces a pixel-level refined image where the edited region is harmonized with its surroundings. Training is performed at 1024×1024 resolution on ~300M images, and at inference the refiner operates as a single feed-forward pass in pixel space, adding only ~2.7 s on a 512×512 image versus ~9.7 s for the underlying FLUX-Fill diffusion sampling.

## Method

PixPerfect is a GAN-based pixel-level refinement network (built on CMGAN, made fully convolutional with 41M parameters) that corrects local editing artifacts without operating in latent space. Its key innovation is a discriminative pixel space: a differentiable per-channel polynomial tone-mapping function (degree 5) fitted via Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse to amplify subtle hue and texture discrepancies between the prediction and ground truth, which is then used as an additional L1 + perceptual + adversarial loss on top of the standard pixel-space loss. Training supervision comes from a synthetic artifact simulation pipeline that injects non-uniform color shifts (gradient alpha-blending of jittered copies), texture-pattern mismatches (selective VAE reconstruction, JPEG compression, Gaussian smoothing inside vs. outside the mask), and content discontinuities (narrow-band inpainting straddling the mask edge) into clean images, with soft/hard boundary mixing via morphological dilation/erosion and Gaussian blur. At inference, a color-jittering pooling strategy applies the refiner to N jittered variants of the input and selects the output with the smallest input–output L1 difference, further stabilizing predictions.

## Results

On the MISATO inpainting benchmark, FLUX-Fill+PixPerfect achieves a FID of 10.87, LPIPS of 0.141, and PSNR of 22.18, substantially outperforming the FLUX-Fill baseline (FID 14.66) and both the decoder-based Asymmetric VQGAN (FID 15.99) and harmonization-based DiffHarmony++ (FID 14.02). On Places2, FLUX-Fill+PixPerfect reaches FID 15.61 and P-IDS 11.69, again leading all baselines. For object removal on RORDS, PixPerfect reduces OmniPaint's FID from 23.05 to 18.87 and improves PSNR from 24.67 to 27.96, while for object insertion it consistently improves LPIPS, L1, MUSIQ, and MANIQA across all four tested models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixPerfect consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across the three tasks. On inpainting (MISATO/Places2), it beats SDv1.5 (FID 13.25 vs 18.15), SDv2 (16.28 vs 18.68), and FLUX-Fill (10.87 vs 14.66) by large margins, and decisively surpasses the two dedicated artifact-correction methods: Asymmetric VQGAN (FID 10.87 vs 15.99) and DiffHarmony++ (10.87 vs 14.02). For object removal on RORDS, PixPerfect improves every baseline—most dramatically on OmniPaint (FID 18.87 vs 23.05, PSNR 27.96 vs 24.67) and PowerPaint (FID 43.40 vs 53.33). For object insertion, PixPerfect improves LPIPS, L1, MUSIQ, and MANIQA on Pbe, ObjectStitch, and AnyDoor, though on OmniPaint it shows a marginal FID regression (57.42 vs 56.80) while still improving all other metrics, suggesting the base model's FID is already near-optimal for that task. Overall, the gains are largest for weaker baselines (e.g., BrushNet FID drops from 148.99 to 144.54) and remain meaningful even for the strongest models, confirming PixPerfect's value as a universal refinement stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixPerfect builds on the CMGAN architecture for its refinement backbone but departs fundamentally from prior artifact-mitigation strategies that operate in latent space (Asymmetric-VQGAN, ASUKA) or rely on post-hoc gradient-domain blending (Poisson) and learned harmonization (DiffHarmony++). Its key conceptual departure is formulating the problem as a pixel-space GAN refinement with a novel discriminative pixel-space loss—a per-sample polynomial tone mapping that amplifies subtle chromatic and textural discrepancies—rather than modifying the VAE decoder or the diffusion process itself. The artifact simulation pipeline also extends prior color-augmentation approaches (e.g., ASUKA's uniform shifts) into a multi-degradation framework covering non-uniform color shifts, texture-pattern mismatches, content discontinuities, and soft/hard boundary effects simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a lightweight, plug-and-play pixel-space refiner (adding only ~2.7 s to a 9.7 s diffusion pass), PixPerfect offers a practical drop-in module for any LDM-based inpainting or editing pipeline, and its consistent gains across SD, FLUX, BrushNet, OmniPaint, and others suggest broad adoptability. The finding that more expressive latent representations (FLUX's 16-channel VAE) paradoxically worsen boundary artifacts challenges the assumption that higher-capacity VAEs uniformly improve local editing fidelity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video inpainting, whether the discriminative pixel-space loss generalizes to other generative modalities (3D, audio), and whether the reliance on a 300M-image training set can be reduced via synthetic-only or few-shot training.
