## Problem

Existing medical RAG systems retrieve exclusively from static knowledge bases (guidelines, literature), neglecting the experiential knowledge clinicians gain from encountering similar patient cases over time. This omission limits their ability to emulate doctor-like reasoning in complex, context-dependent tasks such as differential diagnosis and personalized treatment planning, while standard dense retrieval also struggles to capture fine-grained medical semantic distinctions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255027/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a patient query (symptoms or clinical conversation) and retrieves from two pre-constructed sources: a Knowledge Base of MedQA-derived declarative statements annotated with first-level ICD-10 concept tags, and a Patient Base of de-identified records (chief complaint, structured diagnoses/treatments, demographics) built from 80% of each evaluation dataset with near-duplicates (similarity > 0.99) excluded to prevent leakage. Outputs are task-specific—disease diagnosis labels, treatment recommendations, multiple-choice QA answers, or free-form medical text—each refined through three iterations of Med-TextGrad before final delivery.

## Method

DoctorRAG operates in two stages. First, Expertise-Experience retrieval transforms knowledge chunks into declarative statements tagged with ICD-10 categories, then performs concept-constrained semantic retrieval (cosine similarity gated by tag overlap) over the Knowledge Base alongside unconstrained cosine retrieval over the Patient Base, concatenating top-k results from both into a unified context. Second, Med-TextGrad iteratively refines the generated answer via a multi-agent textual gradient computation graph: a Generator produces an answer, a Context Criterion and Patient Criterion independently critique it, answer-level and prompt-level textual gradients are computed by dedicated LLM agents, and a Textual Gradient Descent step synthesizes both gradients to update the prompt for the next iteration (T=3). The key architectural distinction is the dual-source retrieval that mirrors how clinicians fuse textbook expertise with case-based experience, combined with the dual-criteria gradient loop that simultaneously enforces factual alignment with medical knowledge and patient-specific relevance.

## Results

DoctorRAG achieves the highest accuracy on most tasks across all LLM backbones. On DDXPlus (EN) disease diagnosis, DoctorRAG (GLM-4-Plus) reaches 98.27% accuracy, substantially outperforming Graph RAG (92.37%) and Proposition RAG (90.20%). On the French DDXPlus, DoctorRAG (GPT-4.1-mini) attains 98.73% DD accuracy, and on NEJM-QA it scores 72.64% QA accuracy. For text generation, DoctorRAG (GLM-4-Plus) achieves a Rouge-L of 31.98 on RJUA, the highest among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoctorRAG consistently outperforms all three RAG baselines (Vanilla RAG, Proposition RAG, Graph RAG) and direct generation on the majority of tasks and metrics. The most striking gap appears on DDXPlus disease diagnosis: DoctorRAG (GLM-4-Plus) reaches 98.27% (EN) and 98.53% (FR), exceeding Graph RAG by ~6 percentage points and Proposition RAG by ~8 points. On NEJM-QA, DoctorRAG (GPT-4.1-mini) achieves 72.64% accuracy versus 70.95% for Graph RAG and 70.82% for Proposition RAG. For RJUA text generation (Rouge-L), DoctorRAG (GLM-4-Plus) scores 31.98, well ahead of Graph RAG (27.52) and Proposition RAG (24.01). The one area where DoctorRAG trails is BERTScore on RJUA and COD text generation, where Vanilla RAG with GPT-4.1-mini edges ahead (95.52 vs. 93.29 and 97.04 vs. 96.81), which the authors attribute to DoctorRAG producing longer, more comprehensive outputs that favor recall-oriented metrics like Rouge-L over brevity-oriented BERTScore.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoctorRAG extends the medical RAG lineage (Almanac, MedRAG, ClinicalRAG, i-MedRAG) by introducing a dual-source retrieval mechanism that combines explicit clinical knowledge with implicit case-based experience from analogous patient records, mirroring how human clinicians integrate expertise with experience. The iterative refinement component, Med-TextGrad, is a direct domain adaptation of TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al., Nature 2025), repurposing its multi-agent textual gradient backpropagation for medical answer optimization with dual criteria (context faithfulness and patient relevance). The concept-constrained retrieval using ICD-10 first-level tags and declarative statement transformation departs from standard dense vector search by adding a structured semantic filter, positioning the work between Vanilla RAG and Proposition/Graph RAG in retrieval granularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in medical RAG: the systematic exclusion of experiential, case-based knowledge that is central to human clinical reasoning. The Med-TextGrad iterative refinement offers a practical, LLM-native alternative to fine-tuning for grounding outputs in retrieved context, and the convergence after ~2 iterations suggests a favorable cost-quality trade-off. However, the framework's performance is tightly coupled to LLM backbone quality and knowledge base maintenance, the 'textual gradients' are conceptual rather than mathematical, and the evaluation of iterative refinement relies on LLM-based pairwise voting rather than automated metrics. Open questions include whether the patient-case retrieval generalizes to rare diseases with few analogous records, whether the approach scales to real-time clinical deployment, and whether the dual-criterion optimization can be replaced by more principled automated faithfulness metrics.
