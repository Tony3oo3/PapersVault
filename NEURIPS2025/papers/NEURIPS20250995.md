## Problem

Diffusion language models (DLMs) underperform autoregressive LLMs on reasoning tasks, particularly as denoising steps decrease. The paper identifies the root cause as the independent, parallel generation of masked tokens at each denoising step, which fails to capture dependencies among tokens and leads to inconsistent reasoning paths. Prior work (DoT, d1) addresses reasoning in DLMs but overlooks this fundamental token-correlation gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a math word problem or logic puzzle) and produces a structured text response containing chain-of-thought reasoning enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in answer tags. During decoding, the DLM starts from a fully masked response and iteratively predicts multiple masked tokens in parallel over T denoising steps, with a fraction of predicted tokens re-masked at each step. The response length is configurable (64–512 tokens), and the number of denoising steps is typically set to half the response length.

## Method

MRO models the iterative denoising process as a Markov Decision Process and optimizes three rewards simultaneously: a token verification reward (measuring leave-one-out mutual information among tokens predicted in parallel at one step), a perplexity reward (measuring fluency of the intermediate response via a pre-trained AR LM), and a format-and-accuracy reward (evaluating the final CoT structure and answer correctness). These rewards are combined via potential-based reward shaping and optimized through three mechanisms: test-time scaling (beam search over k candidate responses per step), rejection sampling (SFT on high-reward denoising trajectories), and policy-gradient reinforcement learning. To address the increased reward variance introduced by potential-based shaping over long denoising trajectories, the paper proposes Step-wise Group Reward Optimization (SGRO), which groups w consecutive denoising steps and assigns a single reshaped reward per group, provably reducing variance while also cutting reward-computation overhead.

## Results

On GSM8K (512 tokens), LLaDA-MRO-4 achieves 82.6% accuracy, improving over the LLaDA baseline (79.4%) by +3.2 points and approaching Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (85.4%). On MATH500, MRO-4 reaches 36.2% (vs. 34.4% baseline), and on GPQA it reaches 34.3% (vs. 30.3%). The largest gains appear in logical reasoning: Countdown improves by +7.9 points (14.1%→22.0%) and Sudoku by +6.0 points (11.2%→17.2%). MRO also enables comparable performance with fewer denoising steps (e.g., 128 steps matching 256-step baseline), accelerating decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRO-4 consistently outperforms the LLaDA base DLM across all five benchmarks, with the largest margins on Countdown (+7.9 pts) and Sudoku (+6.0 pts), suggesting these logical tasks rely most heavily on token correlation. Against the LLaDA-s1 reasoning variant, MRO-4 also wins on every task (e.g., +1.4 on GSM8K, +4.5 on GPQA, +5.4 on Countdown). Compared to AR LLMs of similar scale, LLaDA-MRO-4 dominates: it surpasses LLaMA-3-8B by +29.5 on GSM8K, Mistral-7B by +46.4, and Deepseek-LLM-7b by +65.2. The closest comparison is Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, which still leads on GSM8K (85.4 vs. 82.6), MATH500 (41.1 vs. 36.2), GPQA (36.4 vs. 34.3), and Sudoku (21.0 vs. 17.2), but MRO-4 substantially outperforms it on Countdown (22.0 vs. 6.2), indicating DLMs with MRO can match or exceed AR LLMs on tasks requiring strong intra-step token consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work applying reinforcement learning to diffusion language models (DLMs) for reasoning, building directly on d1 [26] which first demonstrated RL-based fine-tuning of DLMs, and on DeepSeek-R1 [11] which established the reward-optimization paradigm for AR LLMs. The key departure is identifying token correlation (both intra-sequence and inter-sequence) as the fundamental bottleneck in DLM reasoning — a limitation that prior DLM reasoning work (DoT, d1) overlooked — and designing targeted multi-reward optimization to address it. The Step-wise Group Reward Optimization (SGRO) mechanism, with its potential-based reward shaping analysis, is a novel contribution to the reward optimization literature for sequential decision-making in generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DLMs are increasingly viewed as a viable alternative to autoregressive LLMs, but their reasoning gap has been a major obstacle to adoption. By showing that targeted token-correlation rewards can close part of this gap while simultaneously enabling faster denoising (128 steps matching 256-step baseline performance), the paper provides a practical pathway for DLMs to compete on reasoning tasks without the compute overhead of scaling. Limitations include evaluation only on 8B-parameter models, sensitivity to SGRO hyperparameters (group size w), and reliance on an external AR model (1mppl) for the perplexity reward. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger DLMs, generalizes to non-masked diffusion architectures, and whether the correlation rewards transfer to non-reasoning generation tasks.
