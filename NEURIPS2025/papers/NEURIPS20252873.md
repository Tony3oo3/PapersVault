## Problem

Directly applying DP-SGD to relational learning fails for two reasons: entities participate in multiple relations (inflating gradient sensitivity far beyond the single-sample assumption), and mini-batch construction involves coupled, interdependent sampling of positive and negative edges that breaks standard privacy amplification analyses. This paper provides the first principled framework for entity-level DP in relational learning that addresses both challenges simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252873/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text-attributed graph (nodes with text attributes, edges representing binary relations) and a pre-trained text encoder (e.g., BERT, Llama2-7B) as input. It produces privately fine-tuned encoder weights that can score potential edges between any pair of entities, enabling zero-shot relation prediction on unseen nodes. Training uses contrastive edge tuples (one positive edge vs. k_neg negative edges) with InfoNCE or Hinge loss, and the entire pipeline is governed by a node-level (ε,δ)-DP guarantee where δ = 1/|E_train|.

## Method

The core contribution is a tailored DP-SGD variant (Algorithm 3) built on three key mechanisms: (1) a frequency-based adaptive gradient clipping (FREQ-CLIP) that scales each edge tuple's clipping threshold inversely with the maximum node-occurrence frequency in the batch, reducing local sensitivity from the worst-case 2|B|·C to (1+|B_-(u*)|)·C; (2) a negative sampling without replacement strategy (NEG-SAMPLE-WOR) that draws k_neg·|B| nodes independently of positive edge contents, simultaneously decoupling the two-stage sampling to a cardinality-dependent form and guaranteeing |B_-(u)|≤1 for every node; and (3) a new RDP privacy amplification bound (Theorem 4.1) for this cardinality-dependent coupled sampling, expressed as a single univariate integral over a Binomial mixture of shifted Gaussians. Node degree is capped at K via random neighbor subsampling to bound the number of edges affected by a single node removal, and the per-iteration RDP bound is composed across T iterations to yield the final (ε,δ)-DP guarantee.

## Results

On relation prediction (zero-shot link prediction) across four text-attributed graph domains, the proposed adaptive-clipping DP-SGD consistently outperforms standard per-sample clipping at the same privacy budget. For BERT.base on MAG(CHN→USA), PREC@1 improves from 12.44 (standard, ε=10) to 17.39 (Ours, ε=10), and from 11.03 to 15.18 at ε=4. Even under the stricter ε=4 constraint, the proposed method (e.g., 15.18 PREC@1 for BERT.base on MAG(CHN→USA)) substantially exceeds the non-fine-tuned base model (4.41), demonstrating that meaningful relational knowledge is retained under strong entity-level privacy. Llama2-7B achieves 34.82 PREC@1 on AMAZ(Sports→Cloth) at ε=10 versus 29.99 for standard clipping, approaching the non-private upper bound of 41.01.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: (a) base models without fine-tuning (no privacy cost, no relational learning), (b) non-private fine-tuning at ε=∞ (upper bound on utility), and (c) standard per-sample gradient clipping DP-SGD at the same ε. The proposed adaptive-clipping method wins against standard clipping on every model–domain–ε combination: at ε=10, BERT.base gains +4.95 PREC@1 on MAG(CHN→USA) (17.39 vs 12.44) and +4.10 on AMAZ(S→C) (27.76 vs 23.66); Llama2-7B gains +6.34 on AMAZ(S→C) (34.82 vs 29.99). At the stricter ε=4, the advantage persists (e.g., BERT.base: 15.18 vs 11.03 on MAG(CHN→USA)). Against the non-fine-tuned base model, the proposed method at ε=4 still achieves 3–4× higher PREC@1 (e.g., 15.18 vs 4.41 for BERT.base on MAG(CHN→USA)), confirming that entity-level DP at practical budgets preserves substantial relational signal. The gap to the non-private upper bound (ε=∞) narrows from ~10–15 points at ε=4 to ~5–7 points at ε=10, indicating graceful degradation with privacy budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) and the privacy amplification by subsampling framework (Balle et al., 2018; Mironov et al., 2019) to the relational learning setting, where mini-batches are constructed through multi-stage, interdependent sampling of positive and negative edges. It departs from existing node-level DP for GNNs (Daigavane et al., 2021; Sajadmanesh et al., 2023), which protect node features during neighborhood aggregation, by addressing the distinct challenge where relations themselves appear in the loss function and a single entity can participate in many loss terms. The key technical departures are an adaptive gradient clipping scheme that normalizes by node occurrence frequency (rather than a fixed per-sample threshold) and a new privacy amplification bound for cardinality-dependent coupled sampling, a setting not covered by standard single-step subsampling analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first principled framework providing entity-level DP guarantees for relational learning, enabling private fine-tuning of foundation models on sensitive relational data (e.g., healthcare, finance) where the relations themselves are confidential. The coupled sampling amplification result opens a path toward analyzing more complex multi-stage sampling procedures in private ML beyond the standard independent-subset assumption. Limitations include the restriction to cardinality-dependent coupling (general coupled sampling remains analytically open), the need for degree capping that may affect utility on highly heterogeneous graphs, and empirical validation on a limited set of medium-scale text-attributed graphs without error bars. Open questions include extending the analysis to multi-relational settings, general coupled sampling, and scaling to very large graphs.
