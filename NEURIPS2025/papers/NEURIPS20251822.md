## Problem

Heterogeneous multi-agent collaborative perception faces a fundamental tension: existing adaptation-based and reconstruction-based methods either require intrusive retraining of encoders or fusion networks (disrupting established semantic consistency among agents) or incur high computational costs when accommodating new agents. The paper asks how to integrate emerging heterogeneous agents into collaboration with minimal cost while preserving the semantic consistency already established among existing agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251822/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent encodes its raw observation (LiDAR point clouds via PointPillars/SECOND or RGB images via EfficientNet/ResNet-101) into a BEV feature map of shape [128, 64, 128] (or [128, 64, 64] for camera agents with smaller perception range). Rather than transmitting these full features, agents exchange only a 2-channel spatial message resized to the receiver's resolution, reducing communication volume by up to 64×. The ego agent consumes these spatial messages as conditional inputs to a diffusion-based generator, producing collaborator features in its own semantic space, which are then fused with its own BEV features and decoded into 3D object detection predictions.

## Method

GenComm replaces feature transmission with local feature generation via a conditional diffusion model. A Deformable Message Extractor uses deformable convolution to capture spatial confidence information from each agent's BEV features, producing a compact 2-channel message. Upon receiving collaborators' messages, the ego agent runs a 3-step conditional U-Net diffusion process (initialized from its own BEV features) to generate collaborator features aligned to its semantic space while preserving their spatial layout. A Channel Enhancer (PConv + depthwise separable convolution + channel attention) then refines the generated features along the channel dimension before fusion. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 trains all three components end-to-end in a homogeneous setting; Stage 2 fine-tunes only a lightweight per-pair message extractor to align numerical distributions across heterogeneous agents, leaving all pretrained encoders and fusion networks frozen. This design reduces the cost of adding a new agent by over 81% compared to the best adaptation-based baseline (STAMP) while maintaining or improving detection accuracy.

## Results

On OPV2V-H with the V2X-ViT fusion network, GenComm achieves AP50=0.8673 and AP70=0.6991 in the L64_P–L32_S setting, outperforming the best baseline (BackAlign at AP50=0.8553, AP70=0.6933). On DAIR-V2X (L64_P–L40_S), GenComm reaches AP30=0.5651 and AP50=0.4665, surpassing STAMP (AP30=0.5421, AP50=0.4935) and all other baselines. In the dynamic scalability setting (Table 2), GenComm uses only 0.31M parameters and 0.615G FLOPs—reducing parameter count by 81% and computation by 80% compared to STAMP—while maintaining the highest AP scores as agents are progressively added. Communication volume is reduced to 16.0 (log₂) versus 22.0 for most baselines, a 64× reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenComm outperforms all four baselines across nearly all evaluation settings. Against MPDA (one-stage adaptation), GenComm gains +1.8 AP50 points on OPV2V-H (0.8673 vs 0.8495 with V2X-ViT) and +9.3 AP30 points on DAIR-V2X (0.5651 vs 0.4717), while also reducing communication volume from 22.0 to 16.0 (log₂). Against BackAlign/HEAL (encoder retraining), GenComm leads by +1.2 AP50 on OPV2V-H (0.8673 vs 0.8553) and +7.5 AP30 on DAIR-V2X (0.5651 vs 0.4902), without requiring any encoder modification. Against CodeFilling (reconstruction via shared codebook), GenComm achieves +0.8 AP50 on OPV2V-H (0.8673 vs 0.8597) and +12.1 AP30 on DAIR-V2X (0.5651 vs 0.4446), with comparable communication volume (16.0 vs 15.0). Against STAMP (two-stage protocol-space adaptation), the closest competitor, GenComm wins by +2.3 AP50 on OPV2V-H (0.8673 vs 0.8442) and +2.3 AP30 on DAIR-V2X (0.5651 vs 0.5421), while using 81% fewer parameters (0.31M vs 1.64M) and 80% fewer FLOPs (0.615G vs 3.084G) in the dynamic multi-agent setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for heterogeneous collaborative perception—adaptation-based methods (MPDA, STAMP, BackAlign/HEAL) that transform features into a shared semantic space, and reconstruction-based methods (CodeFilling) that rebuild features from a shared codebook—by introducing a generation-based communication mechanism. It repurposes conditional diffusion models, previously applied to BEV denoising (DiffBEV, V2X-R, CoDiff), as an inter-agent feature synthesis tool: instead of transmitting or transforming full feature maps, agents exchange lightweight spatial messages extracted via deformable convolution, and the receiver locally generates collaborator features aligned to its own semantic space. The approach also draws on partial convolution and channel attention (from the PConv and gating literature) for a Channel Enhancer that refines generated features before fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GenComm addresses a critical deployment bottleneck: accommodating new heterogeneous agents without retraining core networks or incurring prohibitive computational cost, which prior adaptation and reconstruction methods cannot do simultaneously. The 81% reduction in incremental cost and 64× communication volume reduction make it a practical candidate for large-scale V2X systems where agents from different vendors continuously join. However, the approach still requires bilateral vendor consensus to train pairwise message extractors, and the paper acknowledges that commercial competition and potential malicious attacks remain unresolved barriers. Open questions include whether the diffusion-based generation generalizes to more than four agents, whether the 3-timestep U-Net suffices for higher-resolution or multi-task settings, and how the method behaves under adversarial message injection.
