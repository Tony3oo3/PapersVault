## Problem

Invariance-based SSL methods compress images into single global vectors, discarding spatial structure needed for dense prediction, while generative methods capture local detail but lack semantic discriminability. Existing patch-level approaches still treat each patch independently without explicitly grouping them into coherent visual entities, and they assume augmentations preserve semantic content—an assumption that breaks in cluttered scenes. CG-SSL addresses this by explicitly discovering, tracking, and aligning coherent visual regions (concepts) across augmented views without any supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254823/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images as two strongly augmented 224×224 views (one masked for the student) plus a lightly augmented 448×448 reference view (no cropping) for the teacher in Phase 3. It produces dense patch features (S×D), N=4 concept tokens (N×D) with associated spatial attention masks (S×N), and downstream outputs via frozen kNN or linear probes yielding segmentation maps (mIoU on ADE-20K, Pascal-VOC, Cityscapes) or class labels. The higher-resolution reference view provides denser patch tokens for mask generation without backpropagation overhead.

## Method

CG-SSL follows a three-phase curriculum: (1) global representation learning via iBOT's student-teacher ViT with cls and patch cross-entropy, (2) concept discovery where N learnable tokens interact with student patch features through L=4 transformer decoder blocks with cross-attention, trained by masked concept distillation (student-teacher cross-entropy on concept tokens) and a spatial broadcast decoder that reconstructs teacher patch features in feature space, and (3) view-invariant alignment where the teacher's attention masks on the reference image are geometrically projected onto each augmented view using known crop parameters, then masked average pooling aggregates per-concept features which are aligned via cross-entropy. The key differentiator is the explicit geometric mask transfer in Phase 3, which establishes reliable cross-view correspondences under heavy augmentation rather than relying on implicit crop overlap, enabling compact, disentangled, and spatially coherent representations.

## Results

On semantic segmentation with frozen features, CG-SSL (ViT-L/16) achieves 30.5 mIoU (k-NN) and 35.6 mIoU (linear) on ADE-20K, 64.0/70.6 on Pascal-VOC, and 36.3/45.5 on Cityscapes. For the ViT-S/16 backbone, CG-SSL reaches 25.8/28.9 mIoU on ADE-20K and 60.8/66.7 on Pascal-VOC, outperforming iBOT (19.3/23.6 and 48.1/56.4) by over 6 mIoU in the k-NN setting. Notably, CG-SSL ViT-S surpasses CAPI (trained on LVD-142M, ~100× more data) by 10.3 mIoU on Pascal-VOC k-NN (60.8 vs 50.5), while CG-SSL ViT-L approaches DINOv2 (LVD-142M) within 3.5 mIoU on ADE-20K k-NN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CG-SSL consistently outperforms iBOT across all three segmentation benchmarks and all backbone sizes, with the largest gap on Pascal-VOC k-NN (e.g., 60.8 vs 48.1 for ViT-S, a 12.7 mIoU improvement). Against DINO and MAE, the margins are even wider (e.g., 25.8 vs 15.5 on ADE-20K k-NN for ViT-S). Compared to CAPI trained on LVD-142M (~100× more data), CG-SSL ViT-S wins on Pascal-VOC k-NN by 10.3 mIoU (60.8 vs 50.5) and on Cityscapes k-NN by 2.9 mIoU, though CAPI retains a small edge on ADE-20K linear for ViT-B (33.6 vs 31.5) and ViT-L (37.2 vs 35.6). Against DINOv2 (LVD-142M) at ViT-L, CG-SSL trails by 3.5 mIoU on ADE-20K k-NN (30.5 vs 34.0) and 5.7 on Cityscapes k-NN (36.3 vs 42.0), but narrows the gap to 1.2 mIoU on Pascal-VOC linear (70.6 vs 72.8). In the ablation comparison, CG-SSL's learnable cross-attention clustering outperforms Slot Attention by 4.5 mIoU on ADE-20K (24.3 vs 19.8) and 3.9 on Pascal (56.1 vs 52.2), while a frozen k-means baseline achieves 23.1/54.8, confirming the value of the end-to-end learnable design.

## Relation to Prior Work

CG-SSL builds directly on iBOT's student-teacher ViT framework (Phase 1) and extends it with a concept-discovery stage inspired by object-centric learning (Slot Attention, SlotCon) and a spatial broadcast decoder from disentangled VAEs. It departs from standard invariance-based SSL, which compresses an image into a single global vector, by introducing learnable concept tokens that attend to patches via cross-attention and are aligned across views through geometric mask projection rather than implicit crop overlap. Unlike prior region-level SSL methods (CrOC, CrIBo, SegDiscover) that rely on pre-trained models, offline clustering, or hand-tuned spatial priors, CG-SSL discovers and tracks concepts end-to-end within a single self-supervised curriculum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CG-SSL demonstrates that structured, region-level alignment can close much of the gap between ImageNet-1K-pretrained SSL models and those trained on 100× more data (e.g., DINOv2, CAPI on LVD-142M), particularly under non-parametric kNN evaluation where representation quality is most transparently measured. The fixed number of concept tokens (N=4) is a clear limitation for scenes with varying object counts, and the authors acknowledge that adaptive token allocation and video extension are open problems. The ablation showing that frozen k-means plus geometric alignment nearly matches the full learnable pipeline (23.1 vs 24.3 mIoU on ADE20K) raises the question of whether the cross-attention clustering module is truly necessary or whether the geometric alignment mechanism is the primary driver of performance. With only single-run results and ablations conducted on a 170K-image subset rather than full ImageNet, the robustness of the reported margins remains to be confirmed at scale.
