## Problem

Existing structural pruning methods for LLMs face a fundamental trade-off: local pruning is computationally efficient but enforces uniform sparsity and ignores global topology, while global pruning methods typically adopt a two-stage paradigm (estimate substructure saliency, then apply global ranking) that ignores inter-structure dependencies and cannot achieve end-to-end optimization. The paper asks how to perform efficient global structural pruning that jointly optimizes the sparsity distribution across all layers without backpropagation or additional trainable parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (Llama-2/3.x, Mistral, 3B–70B scale), approximately 4M tokens of calibration text from FineWeb-Edu (~1k samples at 4k max length), and a target overall sparsity ratio (12.5%–50%). It produces a structurally pruned model with a non-uniform per-layer sparsity distribution over attention heads and FFN intermediate neurons, while leaving embed_tokens and lm_head layers untouched. Notably, only one full LLM resides in HBM at any time (14 GB for 7B, 140 GB for 70B), with pruned substructures stored on disk, and the entire pipeline requires no gradient computation or fine-tuning.

## Method

Tyr-the-Pruner constructs a supernet by repeatedly applying a Taylor-expansion-based local pruning (using both first-order gradient and second-order Hessian information to identify redundant channels and adjust remaining weights progressively) across a range of sparsity ratios for each layer. An expectation error accumulation scheme computes a sparsity-weighted average of output activations across all sparse structures in a layer, enabling balanced mutual awareness during supernet construction without requiring a single fixed pruning path. The optimal sparsity distribution is then found via an iterative prune-and-search strategy: starting with a coarse 12.5% sparsity interval, each iteration halves the interval and re-centers it on the current optimum, while a sparsity-shift-driven evolutionary search (shifting sparsity between layers to maintain the global constraint) selects the best subnet using a distillation-inspired metric that measures activation and logit similarity to the dense model. This coarse-to-fine refinement reduces the search space from ~10^145 to ~10^76 per iteration, converging to 1.5625% granularity in four iterations with only 50 generations of 128 candidates each.

## Results

At 50% sparsity on Llama-3.1-70B, Tyr-the-Pruner retains 97% of the dense model's average downstream accuracy (68.56% vs. 70.63%), substantially outperforming FLAP (76%), OSSCAR (89%), and ZipLM (89%). At 37.5% sparsity on Llama-3.1-8B, it achieves 21.64 WikiText2 perplexity and 53.66% average accuracy, beating the previous SOTA FLAP by 3.45 perplexity points and 10.26% accuracy. At 50% sparsity on Llama-3.1-8B, it reaches 30.89 perplexity and 47.79% average accuracy, compared to FLAP's 134.28 perplexity and 36.59% accuracy. Inference benchmarks show 1.75× faster time-to-first-token and 1.38× higher decode throughput at 50% sparsity on Llama-3.1-8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tyr-the-Pruner consistently outperforms all eight baselines across sparsity levels from 12.5% to 50%. Against the closest competitor FLAP [1], the gap widens dramatically at higher sparsity: at 37.5% on Llama-3.1-8B, Tyr-the-Pruner achieves 18.09 perplexity vs. FLAP's 21.54, and at 50% the gap explodes to 30.89 vs. 134.28 perplexity (a 4.3× difference). On the 70B scale (Table 2), FLAP retains only 76% accuracy at 50% sparsity on Llama-3.1-70B, while Tyr-the-Pruner retains 97%, a 21-point gap. OSSCAR [26] and ZipLM [17] are competitive at ≤25% sparsity but degrade sharply beyond 37.5%, with OSSCAR reaching 198.87 perplexity at 50% on Llama-3.1-8B. LLM-Pruner [23] shows the most severe scaling failure, dropping to only 45% accuracy maintenance on Llama-3.1-70B at 50% sparsity. Layer-pruning methods (ShortGPT [24], LaCO+ [50]) and embedding-dimension pruning (SliceGPT [2]) are consistently the weakest, with ShortGPT producing perplexities exceeding 1000 at 25%+ sparsity on most models. Wanda-sp [37] is highly inconsistent, ranging from competitive (6.86 PPL on Mistral-7B at 12.5%) to catastrophic (962.72 PPL on Llama-3.1-8B at 25%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structural pruning lineage from local methods (ZipLM, OSSCAR) and two-stage global methods (LLM-Pruner, FLAP) by unifying them into an end-to-end search framework. It builds on OBS/OBC second-order pruning theory for the local pruning step and on EvoPress's evolutionary search paradigm for the global sparsity allocation step, but departs from prior global methods by eliminating the saliency-estimation-then-prune pipeline in favor of directly searching over a supernet of locally-pruned candidates. The expectation error accumulation mechanism is a novel contribution that addresses a problem (error propagation across multiple sparse structures) not explicitly handled by prior supernet or NAS-based compression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 97% accuracy retention at 50% sparsity on Llama-3.1-70B is a practically significant result, as 50% structural pruning was previously considered aggressive for models of this scale. The iterative coarse-to-fine prune-and-search strategy offers a reusable recipe for navigating large combinatorial search spaces in model compression, and the finding that it outperforms a fine-grained search-only baseline (47.79 vs. 43.58 average accuracy) suggests that progressive refinement is more effective than brute-force exploration. Open questions include whether the approach extends to embedding-dimension and depth pruning, whether search time can be further reduced for even larger models, and how the method interacts with training-based compression pipelines.
