## Problem

Existing music-to-dance generation methods treat dance genre as a weak auxiliary bias (e.g., shallow feature addition or cross-attention) rather than a core semantic driver, causing misaligned music-motion synchronization and disrupted genre continuity—such as Uyghur movements bleeding into a Popping routine during complex rhythmic transitions. No prior architecture provides robust genre controllability, especially under cross-modal conflicts like generating Breaking for soft-paced Chinese music.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250366/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 35-dimensional per-frame music features (20-dim MFCC, 12-dim Chroma, 1-dim Peak, 1-dim Beat, 1-dim Envelope) extracted via Librosa at 30 fps, together with a one-hot genre label (16 classes on FineDance, 10 on AIST++). It produces a 147-dim SMPL parameter sequence (root translation plus 6D rotation representation) per frame, generated autoregressively over discrete latent codes from separate upper-body and lower-body codebooks (size 4375 each). Sequences up to 5.5 s are generated directly; longer sequences use sliding-window prediction with 5.5 s overlap.

## Method

MEGADance is a two-stage architecture. Stage 1 (HFDQ) encodes dance into latent codes via Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) with separate upper/lower-body codebooks, replacing VQ-VAE to achieve 100% codebook utilization; reconstruction is supervised by both SMPL-parameter loss and forward-kinematics joint loss, each augmented with velocity and acceleration terms for spatio-temporal coherence. Stage 2 (GADG) maps music to latent codes through an L-layer Mixture-of-Experts design: genre-specific Specialized Experts are hard-routed by the genre label to capture fine-grained stylistic motifs, while a shared Universal Expert models genre-invariant rhythmic and temporal structures. Each expert employs a Mamba-Transformer hybrid backbone—Mamba's selective state-space scan captures intra-modal local dependencies, and a sliding-window cross-modal Transformer attention (structured as a 3×3 block matrix over music, upper-body, and lower-body streams) models global context while aligning training with the autoregressive-plus-sliding-window inference procedure.

## Results

On the FineDance dataset, MEGADance achieves the best FID across all three feature spaces (FIDk=50.00, FIDg=13.02, FIDs=2.52), the highest motion diversity (DIVk=6.23, DIVg=6.27, DIVs=5.78), and the best Beat Align Score (BAS=0.226), outperforming all baselines. On AIST++, MEGADance similarly leads in FIDk (25.89) and FIDg (12.62), with strong diversity (DIVg=6.23) and BAS (0.238). In the user study, MEGADance scores highest on Dance Synchronization (4.30), Dance Quality (4.25), and Dance Creativity (4.23) on a 5-point scale. For genre controllability, MEGADance achieves 75.64% accuracy and 70.81 F1, closely approaching ground-truth performance (78.31% / 76.35%), while Lodge reaches only 51.86% / 45.23%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEGADance outperforms all four baselines on FineDance across every reported metric. Against Lodge (the closest competitor), MEGADance reduces FIDk by 5.03 (50.00 vs. 55.03), FIDg by 1.85 (13.02 vs. 14.87), and FIDs by 2.70 (2.52 vs. 5.22), while improving BAS from 0.218 to 0.226. Against FineNet, the margins are larger: FIDk drops from 65.15 to 50.00 and FIDs from 13.22 to 2.52. On AIST++, MEGADance achieves the best FIDk (25.89 vs. 30.21 for Bailando++ and 35.35 for FACT) and FIDg (12.62 vs. 15.48 for Bailando++), though Lodge edges it slightly on BAS (0.247 vs. 0.238). In the genre controllability evaluation, MEGADance's 75.64% accuracy and 70.81 F1 substantially exceed Lodge (51.86% / 45.23%) and FineNet (42.06% / 37.44%), confirming the MoE routing's effectiveness in preserving genre identity under cross-modal conflicts.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEGADance extends the two-stage music-to-dance generation paradigm (quantization followed by autoregressive code prediction) by replacing VQ-VAE with Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) for the quantization stage and introducing a Mixture-of-Experts architecture with a Mamba-Transformer hybrid backbone for the generation stage. It departs from prior genre-conditioned approaches (e.g., Lodge, FineNet, GTN-Bailando) that treat genre as a shallow auxiliary bias via cross-attention or feature addition, instead elevating genre to a structural routing signal that selects among Specialized Experts while a Universal Expert captures genre-invariant dynamics. The Mamba-Transformer hybrid draws on recent state-space model work for efficient local dependency modeling, complementing the Transformer's global cross-modal attention in a way not previously applied to dance generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that genre-aware MoE routing can substantially improve both motion quality and stylistic coherence in music-to-dance generation, addressing a practical gap where prior methods produced genre-inconsistent or cross-contaminated motions. The FSQ replacement achieving 100% codebook utilization (vs. 75% for VQ-VAE) is a practical improvement that reduces wasted capacity. Open questions include whether the modular MoE design scales gracefully beyond 16 genres, how well it handles genuinely novel or hybrid genres not seen in training, and whether the autoregressive formulation can be accelerated for real-time applications. The authors also identify text-conditioned generation and noise-robustness as important future directions.
