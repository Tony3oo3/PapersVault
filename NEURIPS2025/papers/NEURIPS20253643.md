## Problem

Existing LLM-based molecular relational learning (MRL) models adopt rigid architectures that support only a single molecular representation modality (typically 1D SMILES or 2D graphs), overlook explicit modeling of inter-molecular interactions, and lack systematic benchmarking across diverse backbones and encoders. No unified framework exists that simultaneously supports flexible 1D/2D/3D molecular inputs, dynamic architectural switching, and fair cross-model comparison.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253643/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts molecular pairs in up to three concurrent modalities—1D SMILES/SelfIES strings, 2D molecular graphs, and 3D conformations (generated via RDKit)—along with task-specific text prompts (direct or chain-of-thought). Outputs are task-dependent: binary interaction labels for DDI, continuous solvation free energy values for SSI, or chromophore optical properties for CSI. The modular design allows any subset of modalities to be active per configuration, with alignment via MLP or Q-former before feeding into the LLM.

## Method

ModuLM is a modular assembly framework providing 8 2D graph encoders (GCN, GIN, GAT, etc.), 11 3D conformation encoders (Uni-Mol, GotenNet, EGNN, SchNet, etc.), 7 interaction layers (bilinear attention, cross-attention, gated fusion, etc.), and 7 LLM backbones (DeepSeek, LLaMA, Galactica), enabling over 50,000 distinct model configurations. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: incremental pretraining on curated molecular property texts using one of three strategies (molecular interaction-based, substructure-based, or structure similarity-guided grouping), followed by LoRA fine-tuning (r=16) on downstream MRL tasks. A key architectural contribution is the explicit interaction layer that models pairwise relationships between molecular embeddings before LLM inference, addressing a gap in prior LLM-based MRL designs. The framework supports user-defined custom encoders and ablation via JSON-based configuration, demonstrated by a custom model that re-aggregates Uni-Mol atom features via learned attention weights.

## Results

On DDI tasks, the best ModuLM configuration (1.8: DeepSeek-1.5B + Uni-Mol 3D encoder) achieves 96.4% accuracy on ChChMiner, 90.7% on ZhangDDI, and 95.9% on DeepDDI, surpassing the strongest baseline MolTC (95.7%, 89.6%, 95.6% respectively). On SSI tasks, the same configuration reduces RMSE to 0.698 (FreeSolv), 0.298 (CompSol), and 0.388 (CombiSolv), compared to MolTC's 0.726, 0.356, and 0.465. The custom model with a redesigned encoder, interaction layer, and CoT prompting further improves to 96.8% accuracy (ChChMiner) and 0.359 RMSE (CombiSolv). Across all 15 tested configurations, 3D conformation-based encoders consistently outperform 2D graph encoders, which in turn outperform text-only inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ModuLM's best configuration (1.8: DeepSeek-1.5B + Uni-Mol 3D encoder) outperforms MolTC [17] on accuracy across all three DDI benchmarks (96.4% vs 95.7% ChChMiner, 90.7% vs 89.6% ZhangDDI, 95.9% vs 95.6% DeepDDI) and on all three SSI benchmarks (RMSE 0.698 vs 0.726 FreeSolv, 0.298 vs 0.356 CompSol, 0.388 vs 0.465 CombiSolv). MolTC retains a slight edge on AUC-ROC for DeepDDI (0.977 vs 0.972), but the gap is within one standard deviation. The custom model with a redesigned attention-weighted encoder, interaction layer, and CoT prompting further extends the lead over MolTC, reaching 96.8% accuracy (ChChMiner) and 0.359 RMSE (CombiSolv). All other baselines—ChemT5, MolCA, and MolT5—are substantially outperformed, with MolT5 being the closest among them (90.7% AUC-ROC on DeepDDI vs ModuLM's 97.2%). The paper also shows that 3D conformation encoders (Uni-Mol, GotenNet, EGNN) consistently yield 2–5% accuracy gains over 2D graph encoders (GIN, GAT, CoATGIN), which in turn outperform text-only SMILES inputs by 3–8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ModuLM builds directly on MolTC's pretraining and alignment strategies for LLM-based molecular relational learning, extending the line of work that includes ReactionT5, MolT5, and MolCA. However, it departs from these prior LLM-based MRL models by replacing their rigid, single-encoder, single-backbone architectures with a modular framework supporting 8 graph encoders, 11 3D conformation encoders, 7 interaction layers, and 7 LLM backbones. The key conceptual departure is the systematic inclusion of 3D molecular conformations as a first-class input modality in LLM-based MRL, a capability that prior LLM-based approaches have largely overlooked despite its importance for modeling spatial interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ModuLM addresses a real gap in the LLM-based MRL literature: the absence of a unified framework enabling fair, apples-to-apples comparison across encoders, backbones, and input modalities. The finding that 3D conformations consistently outperform 2D graphs, and that smaller LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-1.5B) can match or exceed larger ones (e.g., DeepSeek-14B) after fine-tuning, provides actionable guidance for practitioners. However, the benchmark only samples 15 of 50,000+ possible configurations, leaving the full model space unexplored, and the framework's practical impact depends on community adoption. Open questions include how to handle very large molecules where performance degrades sharply, and whether the modular design extends naturally to protein-protein or drug-target interaction tasks.
