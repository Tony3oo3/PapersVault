## Problem

Standard cross-entropy fine-tuning is misaligned with pass@N test-time strategies: while pass@1 improves monotonically with training, pass@N for large N can actually *decrease* over epochs, a novel form of overfitting caused by model overconfidence. The paper asks how the training objective should be co-designed with a given test-time compute budget and sampling strategy to maximize coverage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255304/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a math problem statement or a Lean4 proof state and produces a direct answer, a chain-of-thought trace followed by an answer, or a single proof tactic (for interactive theorem proving). At test time, N independent samples are drawn and an oracle verifier—answer checker for MATH/AIME24 or the Lean4 proof assistant for MiniF2F/Mathlib—determines correctness. In the theorem-proving setting the model interacts step-by-step with the assistant, emitting one tactic per step until the proof closes or an invalid tactic is produced.

## Method

The core contribution is the Direct Coverage Optimization (DCO) loss, ℓ_DCO^N(x,y) = −log(1−(1−p̂(y|x))^N), which directly optimizes the probability of finding the correct answer within N samples. Its gradient equals the standard CE gradient multiplied by an overconfidence regularization factor F(N, p̂(y|x)) that monotonically decreases with model confidence, automatically attenuating updates on examples where the model is already confident (vanishing once confidence exceeds ~1/N). Theoretical lemmas establish that optimal pass@N policies must cap max confidence at large N (favoring exploration) while requiring high confidence at small N (favoring exploitation), explaining the CE/pass@N misalignment. For theorem proving, a step-wise variant (DCO^step) introduces a hyperparameter N_eff that controls the effective branching factor of the proof search tree, and ensembling models trained with different N_eff values yields substantial gains over CE at matched test-time compute. For chain-of-thought settings where p(y|x) requires intractable marginalization over reasoning traces, an approximate variant (DCO^a) substitutes a Monte Carlo estimate.

## Results

On the MATH dataset with Llama-3-8B-base, CE loss training causes pass@256 to drop from 65.2% (epoch 1) to 44.5% (epoch 4) while pass@1 rises from 4.4% to 7.4%, demonstrating the core misalignment. DCO with N'=256 rescues this, tracing a Pareto-optimal frontier where pass@N is maximized when the training N' matches the test-time N. On theorem proving (LeanDojo), the DCO^step ensemble achieves 62.2% on Mathlib and 43.6% on MiniF2F with pass@4k, outperforming CE loss (55.6% and 37.4%) by 6.6 and 6.2 percentage points respectively. In the CoT setting, DCO^a (N'=64) reaches 64.3% pass@64 at epoch 5 on MATH, surpassing both CE (60.9%) and GRPO (35.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (DeepSeekMath [29]), DCO^a clearly wins at larger N: at epoch 5 on MATH, DCO^a achieves 64.3% pass@64 versus GRPO's 35.4%, a 28.9-point gap, while GRPO leads on pass@1 (14.6% vs 7.8%). The paper attributes GRPO's degradation to the same overconfidence mechanism identified for CE loss, with Figure 3 showing GRPO-trained models become progressively overconfident and wrong. Against Focal Loss [50], DCO outperforms across a broad range of N on both in-distribution (MATH) and out-of-distribution (AIME24) test sets (Figure 11), with the DCO Pareto frontier consistently above all Focal Loss curves for γ values tested. The CE loss baseline (equivalent to N_eff=1 in DCO^step) is the primary comparison throughout: DCO^step with N_eff=4–8 improves MiniF2F by 1.6–2.1 points, and the ensemble of all N_eff values improves Mathlib by 6.6 points over CE at the same test-time compute budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of test-time compute scaling (pass@N, self-consistency, tree search) and supervised fine-tuning with cross-entropy loss, identifying a specific misalignment between the two that prior work had not isolated. It is closest in spirit to Chow et al. [14], who derive an analogous training objective for RL to optimize best-of-N sampling, and to Li et al. [21], who note that CE limits output diversity; however, this paper provides a mechanistic explanation (overconfidence) with theoretical bounds on optimal confidence (Lemmas 4.2–4.3) and a simple multiplicative gradient modification (DCO) rather than a full RL reformulation. The step-wise DCO extension to theorem proving connects to the LeanDojo/Lean4 line of work on LLM-guided proof search, but reframes the search-tree branching factor as a training-time hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As test-time compute becomes a primary axis for improving LLM reasoning, the finding that standard CE fine-tuning actively degrades pass@N performance with more training epochs is a practical warning for anyone scaling inference budgets. The DCO loss is trivially implementable (a single multiplicative factor on the CE gradient) and the Pareto-frontier relationship between training N′ and test N gives practitioners a clear knob to tune. Key limitations include the requirement for an efficient verifier (restricting applicability to verifiable tasks), the focus on SFT rather than RL, and evaluation only on mathematical reasoning; extending to coding, best-of-N, and MCTS-based strategies remains open.
