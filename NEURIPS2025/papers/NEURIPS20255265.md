## Problem

Learning a product distribution on the d-dimensional Boolean hypercube requires Θ(d/ε²) samples in the absence of prior information. This paper asks whether an advice mean vector q (with no a priori guarantee on its accuracy) can reduce sample complexity, and how to do so robustly: the algorithm must self-detect the quality of q and adapt, achieving sublinear-in-d samples when q is close to the true mean p while never degrading beyond the standard d/ε² bound.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives i.i.d. samples from an unknown τ-balanced product distribution Ber(p) on {0,1}^d, an advice vector q ∈ [0,1]^d whose distance from p is unknown to the algorithm, and parameters ε (target TV error), δ (failure probability), η ∈ [0,1/4] (controlling the sublinearity exponent), and τ (balancedness margin). It outputs a mean vector p̂ ∈ [0,1]^d defining a product distribution Ber(p̂) with d_TV(Ber(p), Ber(p̂)) ≤ ε with probability ≥ 1−δ, running in poly(n,d) time. Crucially, the algorithm never receives ‖p−q‖₁ as input and must estimate it from samples alone.

## Method

The TESTANDOPTIMIZEMEAN algorithm operates in two phases. First, a novel Tolerant Mean Tester (TMT) uses Poisson-sampled per-coordinate counts and a chi-squared-like statistic Z = Σ((Xᵢ − mqᵢ)² − Xᵢ) to test whether ‖p−q‖₂ ≤ ε within each of d/k coordinate blocks, with a binary-search over thresholds to produce an APPROXL1 estimate λ of ‖p−q‖₁. Second, if λ < ε√d, the algorithm solves a constrained LASSO problem—minimizing empirical squared loss subject to ‖b−q‖₁ ≤ λ—yielding p̂ with ℓ₂-error O(ε); otherwise it falls back to the empirical mean. The key technical insight is that partitioning coordinates into blocks of size k improves the ℓ₁/ℓ₂ norm ratio from √d to √k, so choosing k = d^{4η} makes the sample complexity Õ(d/ε² · (d^{−η} + ‖p−q‖₁²/(d^{1−4η}ε²))), which is sublinear in d when the advice is sufficiently accurate. A balancedness assumption (τ-bounded marginals) is shown to be necessary: without it, even O(1)-close advice cannot yield sublinear sample complexity.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.1) establishes a sample complexity of $\tilde{O}\!\left(\frac{d}{\varepsilon^2}\left(d^{-\eta}+\min\!\left\{1,\,\frac{\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}\|_1^2}{d^{1-4\eta}\,\tau^6\,\varepsilon^2}\right\}\right)\right)$ for learning a $\tau$-balanced product distribution to TV distance $\varepsilon$, where $\eta$ is a small constant and $\tau$ is the balancedness parameter. When the advice is sufficiently accurate ($\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}\|_1 < \varepsilon\, d^{0.5-\Omega(\eta)}$), this reduces to $\tilde{O}(d^{1-\eta}/\varepsilon^2)$, which is sublinear in $d$. Matching lower bounds are proved: $\tilde{\Omega}(d/\varepsilon)$ samples are necessary for unbalanced distributions even with $O(\varepsilon)$-close advice (Lemma 4.2), and $\tilde{\Omega}(\min\{\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}\|_1^2/\varepsilon^4,\; d/\varepsilon^2\})$ for balanced distributions (Lemma 4.3), confirming the upper bound is tight up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The natural baseline is the standard empirical-mean algorithm, which uses $\Theta(d/\varepsilon^2)$ samples and is optimal without advice. The proposed TESTANDOPTIMIZEMEAN algorithm matches this baseline when the advice is poor ($\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}\|_1 \gg \varepsilon\sqrt{d}$), falling back to the empirical mean, but achieves a strict improvement—sublinear in $d$—when the advice is accurate. The lower bound in Lemma 4.3 shows that the $\tilde{\Omega}(\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}\|_1^2/\varepsilon^4)$ dependence on advice quality is information-theoretically necessary for balanced distributions, so the algorithm is essentially optimal. A key distinction from the Gaussian setting of [BCGG25] is that the balancedness assumption ($\tau$-balanced marginals) is essential here: Lemma 4.2 proves that without it, even $O(\varepsilon)$-close advice in $\ell_1$ cannot reduce the sample complexity below $\tilde{\Omega}(d/\varepsilon)$, in contrast to identity testing where $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d}/\varepsilon^2)$ samples suffice without any balancedness requirement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-augmented algorithms framework (Purohit et al. 2018; Gollapudi & Panigrahi 2019) to the problem of learning product distributions on the Boolean hypercube, directly building on the authors' prior work on learning multivariate Gaussians with imperfect advice (BCGG25, ICML 2025). The core technical machinery draws on the tolerant testing framework of Diakonikolas and Kane (FOCS 2016) for the ℓ₂-approximation step, and on identity testing results for product distributions (Daskalakis & Pan 2017; Canonne et al. 2017). A key departure from the Gaussian setting is the critical reliance on a balancedness assumption to relate TV distance to ℓ₂ error of the mean vector, and the development of a new ℓ₂-approximation algorithm specific to product distributions, since no direct analogue of the Gaussian mean-estimation technique applies here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that advice can reduce sample complexity from the classical Θ(d/ε²) to sublinear in d for balanced product distributions, while proving that this improvement is impossible without balancedness—even O(1) advice yields no sublinear gain when coordinates are near-deterministic. This balancedness necessity is a sharp negative result that delineates the boundary of when learning-with-predictions can help in discrete distribution learning. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other structured families (Bayesian networks, Ising models) and whether advice can improve sample complexity for unstructured distributions over [n] beyond the classical O(n/ε²) bound.
