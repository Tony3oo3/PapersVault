## Problem

While first-order preconditioned optimizers like Adam are well-understood, it was unclear whether preconditioning is theoretically necessary in zeroth-order optimization for LLM fine-tuning, what the optimal preconditioner order should be, and how to practically estimate Hessian information using only forward passes. The paper shows that unlike first-order SGD (which already achieves the optimal rate without preconditioning), ZO-SGD provably cannot match the ideal Õ(d²/T) convergence rate, establishing that preconditioning is essential in the zeroth-order setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM model parameters, a mini-batch of training examples, and a loss function, requiring only three forward passes per step (no backpropagation or activation caching). It produces updated model parameters with O(d) additional memory for the diagonal Hessian estimate (versus O(d²) for a full Hessian). The perturbation direction is preconditioned by the inverse square root of the estimated diagonal Hessian, and the algorithm supports both full-parameter and PEFT (LoRA, prefix-tuning) configurations.

## Method

PaZO introduces Preconditioned Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (PSPSA), which perturbs parameters along H^{-α}u and multiplies the resulting gradient estimate by H^{-α}u, yielding an effective H^{-2α} preconditioned gradient. The key theoretical result proves that α=1/2 (i.e., using H^{-1/2} in the perturbation, equivalent to H^{-1} as the effective preconditioner) is necessary and sufficient to achieve the fastest convergence rate Õ(d²/T), while ZO-SGD (α=0) provably falls short. In practice, the algorithm estimates the diagonal Hessian from three forward-pass losses (ℓ+, ℓ−, ℓ) via a Taylor-expansion-based identity, stabilizes it with exponential moving average and periodic resets, and incorporates a small gradient-magnitude correction term (β₁ ≈ 10⁻⁸) to prevent numerical instability from outlier zeroth-order estimates.

## Results

On OPT-1.3B across 9 downstream tasks, PaZO achieves 68.6% average accuracy (full-parameter), outperforming MeZO (67.6%) and HiZOO (67.9%). With LoRA, PaZO reaches 67.7% versus MeZO(LoRA) at 65.7% and HiZOO(LoRA) at 67.6%. On RoBERTa-large (350M), PaZO achieves 65.6% average accuracy versus MeZO's 64.2%, approaching full fine-tuning with Adam (68.8%) while using only forward passes. PaZO requires up to 6× less GPU memory than standard fine-tuning and 3× less than prefix-tuning, at a 1.5× per-step wall-clock cost over MeZO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaZO consistently outperforms MeZO across all three tuning settings on OPT-1.3B: full-parameter (68.6% vs 67.6%), LoRA (67.7% vs 65.7%, a 2.0-point gap), and prefix-tuning (66.6% vs 66.0%). Against HiZOO, PaZO wins on full-parameter (68.6% vs 67.9%) and LoRA (67.7% vs 67.6%), with the largest per-task gains on CB (+1.7 over HiZOO full) and WIC (+1.0). On RoBERTa-large, PaZO improves over MeZO by 1.4 points on average (65.6% vs 64.2%), with notable gains on MNLI (+9.3%) and TREC (+5.4%). PaZO does not uniformly dominate on every single task—HiZOO edges out PaZO on WSC (62.5% vs 61.5%) and MeZO leads on COPA (76.0% vs 75.0%)—but the all-round average advantage is consistent. The LoRA variant of PaZO shows the most dramatic improvement over MeZO(LoRA) on CB (73.2% vs 60.7%), suggesting the Hessian preconditioner is particularly beneficial when the parameter budget is constrained.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zeroth-order optimization line initiated by MeZO (ZO-SGD for LLM fine-tuning) and HiZOO (Hessian-informed ZO) by providing the first theoretical framework—PSPSA—that unifies preconditioned zeroth-order gradient estimation with a tunable Hessian order α. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it proves that unlike first-order SGD for least squares (which already matches the information-theoretic lower bound without preconditioning), ZO-SGD alone cannot achieve the ideal Õ(d²/T) rate, establishing the *necessity* of preconditioning in the zeroth-order setting; and (2) it identifies α=1/2 (i.e., H^{-1} as the effective preconditioner) as the unique optimal choice via Cauchy-Schwarz on the trace product. The practical diagonal Hessian estimator borrows the unbiased estimation identity from Mirror Natural Evolution Strategies (Ye, 2023) and adapts it to the ZO perturbation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that preconditioning is provably necessary in zeroth-order optimization (but not in first-order for least squares) resolves a conceptual gap that had left practitioners to rely on heuristics when adapting Adam-like preconditioners to ZO settings. The practical PaZO algorithm achieves consistent but modest improvements (1–3%) over MeZO and HiZOO across tasks and PEFT variants, with only 1.5× wall-clock overhead and O(d) extra memory for the diagonal Hessian. Key limitations include evaluation only on 350M–1.3B parameter models, reliance on a diagonal (not full) Hessian approximation, and the open question of whether the theoretically optimal α=1/2 transfers cleanly to the highly non-convex, ill-conditioned landscapes of modern LLMs. Scaling to 7B+ models and understanding when the diagonal approximation suffices versus full curvature remain important next steps.
