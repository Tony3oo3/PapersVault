## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods identify critical tokens through static or heuristic modeling of attention scores, neglecting the temporal patterns (re-access, sequential, seasonal) that make attention scores predictable across decoding steps. This leads to inaccurate critical token identification and noticeable LLM performance degradation, especially under aggressive compression budgets. The paper asks whether directly predicting next-step attention scores as a 2D spatiotemporal time series problem can yield more accurate critical token selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254624/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The predictor consumes a 2D spatiotemporal attention history of H=64 recent steps, where each step's attention vector is block-compressed via max-pooling with block size b=16, reducing the spatial dimension to t/b. It outputs a predicted attention score vector for the next decoding step in the same compressed form, from which top-K positions are selected and expanded by factor b to yield the final critical token index set S. A distribution error calibration step periodically (every M=5 steps) replaces the compressed history with full dense attention to correct accumulated distribution drift.

## Method

AttentionPredictor formulates next-step attention prediction as a 2D time series problem and learns a lightweight CNN (two 3×3 2D convolutions followed by a 1D convolution, totaling ~4.9K parameters) to capture spatiotemporal patterns including re-access, sequential, and seasonal invariances. The model is trained with MSE loss on only ~3% of attention data extracted from LongBench and is shared uniformly across all transformer layers and heads, consuming just 21KB versus SeerAttention's 101MB per-layer models. Block-wise max-pooling compression reduces prediction computation to roughly 1/b of the full attention, while a cross-token prefetching framework asynchronously overlaps prediction and KV cache transfer with the main LLM inference, hiding both latencies within the per-token decode time. The theoretical grounding shows that high query self-similarity (cosine autocorrelation 0.87) and RoPE position encoding produce the stable spatiotemporal structures the CNN exploits.

## Results

On LongBench with LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, AttentionPredictor achieves 48.58 average score at 1K KV budget (vs. 48.17 full cache), outperforming all baselines by 1.66–6.23 points. Under extreme 8% compression (1K/13K), it matches full-cache performance while all other methods lose at least 1.25%. In the cache offloading scenario, it delivers a 5.6× decode speedup over FlashAttention2 at 32K tokens and 13× KV cache compression with <0.5% accuracy loss. On the AIME long-reasoning task with QwQ-32B, it maintains 76.7% accuracy at 0.13 KV budget ratio, whereas Quest collapses to 43.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttentionPredictor outperforms all six LongBench baselines (StreamingLLM, Cascade, H2O+, SnapKV, Quest) across every KV budget (1K–4K) on both LongChat-7B and LLaMA-3.1-8B, with the largest margin over StreamingLLM (up to 6.23 points at 1K on LLaMA). Against the closest competitor Quest, it gains 1.66 points at 1K and 0.58 points at 4K on LLaMA, with the advantage widening under extreme compression (7% higher attention recovery rate at 512 budget). Versus the learning-based SeerAttention, AttentionPredictor achieves 7.27% higher average accuracy at 512 budget on LLaMA while using a model that is only 0.02% the size (21KB vs. 101MB). On InfiniteBench (214K context), it leads Quest by 1.5 points and SnapKV by 2.4 points in average score. In the cache offloading efficiency experiment, it achieves 5.6× speedup over FlashAttention2 (full-cache offloading) and 1.6× over H2O at 32K tokens, with per-layer overhead remaining stable (47.3→50.0 ms) as context grows from 4K to 32K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV cache compression literature by reframing critical token identification as a 2D spatiotemporal attention score prediction problem, departing from both heuristic eviction methods (H2O, StreamingLLM, SnapKV) that accumulate static scores and retrieval methods (Quest, InfLLM) that estimate attention over compressed key representations. It also differentiates from prior learning-based approaches (SeerAttention, NSA) that train separate per-layer models to encode keys or hidden states, instead using a single unified CNN shared across all layers and heads to directly predict the attention score distribution. The block-wise max-pooling compression is inspired by Quest's paged key structure, and the observed re-access and sequential patterns build on MInference's inductive attention templates, though the paper adds a novel seasonal pattern and a dynamic (rather than fixed-template) predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13× KV cache compression with <0.5% performance degradation and 5.6× decode speedup in cache-offloading scenarios addresses a critical bottleneck for long-context LLM deployment, making very long contexts practically feasible on consumer hardware. The unified predictor's negligible footprint (21KB vs. 101MB for SeerAttention) and cross-dataset generalization (trained on ~3% of LongBench, transfers to GSM8K) suggest broad applicability without per-model retraining. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models (70B+) with more diverse head behaviors, how it interacts with KV quantization and multi-turn dialogue settings, and whether the seasonal pattern persists across all architectures or is specific to RoPE-based models.
