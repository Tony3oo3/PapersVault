## Problem

Existing semantic retrieval datasets are confined to single languages, single images, or singular retrieval conditions, and fail to exploit the expressive capacity of visual information (performance is maintained when images are replaced with captions). In practical product search, users issue interleaved multi-condition queries combining multiple images and text across multiple languages, a scenario entirely unaddressed by prior benchmarks. Furthermore, existing MLLM-based retrieval models, fine-tuned with contrastive learning supervised only at the [EOS] token, prioritize global semantics while neglecting specific conditional elements such as material attributes or visual textures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252512/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The query is an interleaved sequence of multiple product images and natural-language text in one of five languages (English, Malay, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Thai), specifying multiple simultaneous conditions (e.g., pattern, color, style). The retrieval target is a product represented by a single image paired with a GPT-4o-generated title. The model produces a single multimodal embedding vector per query and per product, from which a ranked list over the 135,000-product pool is obtained via cosine similarity. A key finding is that sequential (interleaved) multi-image input outperforms concatenating all images into one, reversing the behavior observed in prior single-image retrieval settings.

## Method

CORAL fine-tunes a pre-trained MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL) into a retrieval model by jointly optimizing three losses: (1) an InfoNCE contrastive loss that pulls query and positive-product [EOS] embeddings together while pushing negatives apart, capturing global semantics; (2) a vision reconstruction loss that masks 50% of visual self- and cross-attention, then reconstructs the original visual embedding using the [EOS] hidden state as a query through a randomly initialized BERT decoder, preserving fine-grained visual conditional elements; and (3) a masked language modeling loss that similarly masks 50% of linguistic attention and reconstructs the original text tokens via the MLLM's own language head, preserving textual conditional details. The overall objective is L_cl + λ₁·L_reg + λ₂·L_rec, where the reconstruction terms use both the query's and the target's [EOS] tokens as attention queries against the target's full embedding. This dual objective forces the model to retain attribute-level and texture-level information that pure contrastive training at [EOS] discards, yielding a 45.9% R@1 improvement over contrastive-only baselines on MERIT and consistent gains across eight standard retrieval benchmarks.

## Results

On MERIT, existing retrieval models struggle severely: the best zero-shot MLLM (InternVL2.5-MPO, sequential input) achieves only R@1=0.41, while the best embedding MLLM (LamRA-Qwen2.5VL 7B, concatenated input) reaches R@1=12.05. CORAL with full-parameter fine-tuning and sequential input achieves R@1=69.68, R@5=89.26, R@10=93.08, and MRR=78.33, representing a 45.9% R@1 improvement over contrastive-learning-only fine-tuning (R@1=47.76). Across 8 established retrieval benchmarks, CORAL delivers consistent gains, with a notable 181% improvement over the Qwen2-VL zero-shot baseline on VisDial.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CORAL (full-param, sequential input) achieves R@1=69.68 on MERIT, dramatically outperforming all 9 evaluated baselines. Among zero-shot MLLMs, InternVL2.5-MPO (R@1=0.41) and InternVL2.5 (R@1=0.27) are the strongest but remain negligible; Qwen2.5-VL zero-shot is weakest (R@1=0.09). Among embedding MLLMs, LamRA-Qwen2.5VL 7B (Cat) leads with R@1=12.05, followed by BGE-VL 7B (11.55) and GME-Qwen2VL 2B (8.47); LLaVE variants range from 4.89–8.03, while VLM2Vec (0.43) and E5-V (3.10) trail significantly. A striking finding is that sequential multi-image input collapses for most embedding MLLMs (e.g., LLaVE 2B drops from R@1=5.80 Cat to 0.12 Seq; GME from 8.47 to 5.29), whereas CORAL with sequential input surpasses all concatenated-input baselines by 5–16×. On the 8 external retrieval benchmarks (Fig. 9), CORAL consistently improves over the Qwen2-VL zero-shot baseline and CLIP, with the largest gain (181%) on VisDial, confirming generalization beyond MERIT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-to-retrieval-model adaptation paradigm (E5-V, GME, LLaVE, VLM2Vec, LamRA, BGE-VL), which uniformly relies on contrastive learning with [EOS]-token supervision, by integrating masked embedding reconstruction (borrowed from the RetroMAE lineage) to preserve fine-grained conditional elements that contrastive learning alone discards. On the data side, MERIT generalizes prior composed image retrieval benchmarks (CIRR, FashionIQ, OVEN, InfoSeek) from single-language, single-image, single-condition queries to interleaved multi-condition, multi-image, multilingual queries, filling a gap the authors argue reflects real-world product search. The key conceptual departure is the hypothesis that contrastive-only fine-tuning causes MLLMs to lose their native interleaved multi-image processing capability, a failure mode not previously identified in the retrieval literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification that existing retrieval models systematically neglect specific conditional elements (attribute errors and visual understanding errors dominate the error distribution) provides a concrete diagnostic for why recall plateaus on compositional queries, and CORAL offers a practical remedy with a 45.9% R@1 gain. The finding that fine-tuned retrieval models prefer concatenated over sequential multi-image inputs—contradicting MLLM behavior on comprehension tasks—raises an open question about whether contrastive fine-tuning on single-image datasets fundamentally degrades multi-image reasoning, with implications beyond retrieval. Limitations include the dataset's origin in ByteDance's internal e-commerce data across only six Southeast Asian countries and five languages, and the evaluation is confined to product retrieval; whether the interleaved-input degradation and the reconstruction benefit transfer to open-domain or non-product retrieval settings remains untested.
