## Problem

Planning under opponent uncertainty in partially observable multi-agent environments remains intractable: type-based methods depend on handcrafted behavior classes that do not scale, model-free adaptation is sample-inefficient, and Monte-Carlo Tree Search branching explodes when every node must marginalize over many hypothetical opponent policies. The paper tackles the open challenge of bridging expressive, learned opponent modeling with tractable belief-aware planning that supports real-time updates.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes its own observation-action history and the sequence of observed opponent actions (not the opponent's private observations, which are reconstructed via the environment simulator during rollouts). Offline, the method requires a PSRO-generated policy library (10 agent policies, 50 opponent policies) and a trained VQ-VAE; online, it outputs a single agent action selected by PUCT and a categorical belief vector of length K=16 over latent opponent types, updated at every real and simulated step. In the One-on-One environment, inputs include visual observations and continuous actions, while grid-world environments use discrete local observations.

## Method

QOM compresses the continuous opponent policy space into K discrete latent types via a VQ-VAE whose encoder (a 2-layer GRU) maps opponent trajectories to embeddings and a codebook of K=16 vectors, with a GRU-based autoregressive decoder producing step-wise action likelihoods per type. Online, the agent maintains a Bayesian belief over these K types, updated by Bayes' rule using decoder likelihoods with a temperature parameter β and a smoothing term to prevent premature collapse. A belief-weighted meta-policy is constructed as a mixture of soft best responses over the agent policy library, weighted by the current belief and an offline payoff matrix. During MCTS rollouts, opponent actions are sampled from the belief-weighted decoder, the meta-policy serves as the PUCT prior for agent action selection, and the belief is re-updated at every simulated step, coupling inference and search. The paper proves posterior concentration at rate O(log T log log T / T) under β-mixing, with an O(ξ) irreducible error floor from quantization.

## Results

QOM is evaluated across six multi-agent environments (Pursuit-Evasion evader/pursuer, Predator-Prey 2-agent/4-agent, Running-with-Scissors, and One-on-One football) under search time budgets of 0.1, 1, 5, 10, and 20 units, reporting mean return over 300 episodes with 95% confidence intervals. QOM consistently achieves the highest return across all environments and all time budgets, with the largest margins under tight computational constraints (0.1–1 unit). The advantage is most pronounced in Running-with-Scissors and the four-agent Predator-Prey task, where belief-driven planning and the meta-policy prior provide the greatest planning efficiency gains over particle-filter-based and model-free baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QOM outperforms all three baselines—POTMMCP, I-POMCP-PF, and MBOM—across every environment and search-time setting. Against POTMMCP [41], which uses a particle filter over opponent policies, QOM's compact discrete-type belief model enables faster and more structured inference, yielding a clear advantage especially at low search budgets where particle filtering is sample-inefficient. Against I-POMCP-PF, which relies on uniform random rollouts and shallow belief updates, QOM's belief-weighted meta-policy and step-level belief refinement produce substantially higher returns, particularly in the complex four-agent Predator-Prey and Running-with-Scissors environments. Against MBOM [57], which models opponents implicitly through predictive learning without an explicit policy library, QOM maintains an edge in strategic domains (Running-with-Scissors, One-on-One) where MBOM struggles to generalize online, though MBOM remains competitive in simpler tasks at high time budgets. In the One-on-One setting, all methods show higher variance due to fine-grained strategic adaptation, yet QOM still retains a slight but consistent advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

QOM unifies three previously separate research threads: type-based opponent modeling (which traditionally relied on handcrafted behavior classes), Bayesian posterior tracking over opponent policies, and Monte-Carlo tree search planning. The key architectural departure from POTMMCP [41] is replacing particle-filtered continuous belief representations with a compact discrete codebook learned by a VQ-VAE [52], enabling O(1) belief updates per step rather than O(N) particle operations. The planning backbone extends PUCT [45] by injecting the belief-weighted meta-policy as a dynamic prior, and the meta-policy concept itself is inherited from POTMMCP but now grounded in learned latent types rather than a fixed policy library. The theoretical analysis draws on standard β-mixing concentration inequalities to bound posterior concentration, with the quantization error ξ entering only as an additive O(ξ) floor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for belief-aware planning in multi-agent POMDPs that avoids the sample inefficiency of particle filtering while retaining principled uncertainty handling, which is valuable for real-time applications where per-step compute is limited. The theoretical guarantee that the posterior concentrates at rate Õ(1/T) up to an O(ξ) quantization floor gives practitioners confidence that the discrete approximation does not fundamentally degrade inference. However, the method assumes a fixed offline policy library and short-term opponent stationarity, so it may struggle with truly non-stationary or out-of-distribution adversaries; the authors note continual type discovery and hierarchical dynamics as open directions. Scaling to many simultaneous opponents or continuous action spaces remains unaddressed.
