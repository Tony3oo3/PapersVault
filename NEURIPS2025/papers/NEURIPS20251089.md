## Problem

Existing generative data augmentation methods optimize intrinsic data attributes such as visual fidelity and diversity, but neglect the fact that different downstream tasks and network architectures require distinct training data distributions for optimal performance. This paper asks how to explicitly optimize synthetic data for task-specific utility rather than merely meeting visual quality standards, and how to do so efficiently without prohibitively expensive retraining cycles to evaluate each sample's contribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes 16 real images per class (used for textual inversion to learn class-specific identifiers), class labels, a downstream classifier (e.g., ResNet-50), and a validation set as input. It produces 512×512 synthetic RGB images at 1×, 3×, or 5× the scale of the original real dataset, where each image is optimized to maximize its utility score for the specific downstream classifier. The generated data is task-specific: the same base diffusion model produces different high-utility samples depending on which classifier's feedback guides the optimization.

## Method

UTILGEN comprises three components forming a feedback loop between data generation and model training. First, a Task-Oriented Data Valuation module trains a lightweight MLP weight network via bi-level meta-learning to predict per-sample utility scores from classification loss, avoiding costly retraining-based valuation. Second, Model-Level Generation Capability Optimization (MLCO) iteratively fine-tunes the Stable Diffusion v2.1 U-Net using Diffusion DPO, constructing preference pairs by ranking generated samples with the weight network and pushing the model toward high-utility outputs. Third, Instance-Level Generation Policy Optimization (ILPO) performs per-sample refinement by jointly optimizing prompt embeddings (gradient ascent on utility with CLIP-based semantic regularization) and initial noise (DDIM inversion with asymmetric CFG scales to inject high-utility semantic information), maximizing the utility score of each individual generated image.

## Results

On eight benchmark datasets using ResNet-50, UTILGEN achieves an average top-1 accuracy of 37.17% when trained solely on synthetic data, outperforming the previous SOTA (DataDream, 33.30%) by +3.87%. In the joint training setting (synthetic + real data), UTILGEN reaches 62.04% average accuracy, surpassing DataDream (58.67%) by +3.37%. Notably, at 3× synthetic data scale, UTILGEN-trained ResNet-50 surpasses its real-data-trained counterpart on three datasets, and at 5× scaling this extends to four datasets. The method also generalizes across architectures, achieving 37.62% (ResNeXt-50), 37.82% (WideResNet-50), and 40.59% (MobileNetV2) on ImageNet-100 with synthetic data only.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UTILGEN consistently outperforms all four baselines across all eight datasets in both synthetic-only and joint-training settings. Against the strongest baseline DataDream (which uses LoRA fine-tuning for fidelity alignment), UTILGEN gains +3.87% average accuracy in the synthetic-only setting and +3.37% in joint training, with the largest per-dataset margins on CUB (+7.94%) and Caltech 101 (+5.70%). GIF (diversity-focused via feature perturbations) and GAP (loss-based adversarial prompt feedback) trail UTILGEN by 7.94% and 9.37% respectively in the synthetic-only average, confirming that optimizing intrinsic visual attributes or raw loss is less effective than explicit task-utility optimization. Vanilla Stable Diffusion v2.1 without any augmentation strategy lags far behind, achieving only 22.49% average on synthetic data alone. UTILGEN also maintains its lead across alternative backbones (ResNeXt-50, WideResNet-50, MobileNetV2) and across synthesis budgets from 1× to 5×, with the performance gap over DataDream widening from +0.92% at 1× to +5.46% at 5× on ImageNet-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

UTILGEN sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) diffusion-model-based data augmentation (GIF, DataDream, GAP), which optimizes intrinsic attributes like diversity or fidelity; (2) data valuation via meta-learned weight networks (Meta-Weight-Net), repurposed here as an efficient utility predictor rather than a training-time re-weighting tool; and (3) preference-based alignment of diffusion models (Diffusion-DPO), adapted to steer generation toward high-utility outputs. The key departure is reframing the augmentation objective from visual quality to downstream task utility, with a dual-level optimization (model-level DPO + instance-level prompt/noise tuning) that no prior generative augmentation method has combined. The noise optimization leverages the CFG-scale discrepancy trick from recent 'golden noise' work, but applies it to maximize a learned utility score rather than a fixed perceptual metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The utility-centric framing offers a principled alternative to the prevailing fidelity/diversity trade-off in generative augmentation, and the demonstration that 3× synthetic-only training can match or exceed real-data baselines on several benchmarks is practically significant for data-scarce settings. However, the approach inherits the base diffusion model's quality ceiling, incurs non-trivial computational overhead (~74h total on 8×A100 for ImageNet-1K), and the utility signal is tied to a specific classifier architecture during training (though reusability experiments suggest moderate transfer). Open questions include whether the utility-centric paradigm extends to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), whether it scales to open-vocabulary or multi-modal settings, and whether the DPO-based model adaptation can be replaced by lighter-weight fine-tuning without sacrificing the utility gain.
