## Problem

Achieving sublinear regret in online decision making with resource constraints is provably impossible when reward and cost distributions change adversarially over time. In practice, advertising platforms like Meta and Google provide a spending plan—a sequence of per-round budget recommendations—but no theoretical framework existed to exploit this structure for regret guarantees. This paper shows that a spending plan is sufficient to recover sublinear regret even under adversarially changing distributions, bridging the gap between the impossibility of the fully adversarial setting and the practical need for robust guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a spending plan (a sequence of per-round budgets B_t^(i) for each resource i, summing to the total budget B), the horizon T, and black-box regret minimizers (primal and/or dual) as input. The decision set X is general—non-convex, integral, or non-compact—and the spending plan acts as a soft constraint in expectation, while the aggregate budget B remains a hard constraint that triggers early stopping. The output is a sequence of strategy mixtures achieving Õ(1/ρ_min · √T) regret (or Õ(T^(3/4)) in the worst case) against baselines that follow the spending plan.

## Method

The core contribution is a family of (primal-)dual algorithms where the Lagrangian multiplier space is bounded by 1/ρ_min—the minimum per-round budget in the spending plan—rather than the standard 1/ρ, yielding regret that improves as the spending plan distributes budget more evenly across rounds. For ORA, a dual algorithm solves the per-round Lagrangian maximization and updates multipliers via a dual regret minimizer; for OLRC, a primal regret minimizer over strategy mixtures is added to handle the case where reward and cost functions are not observed in advance (under full or bandit feedback). A meta-procedure handles the degenerate case where ρ_min ≤ O(T^{-1/4}) by capping the Lagrangian space at ρ/T^{1/4} and rescaling the spending plan by a (1−T^{-1/4}) factor, recovering Õ(T^{3/4}) regret. The framework is robust to baselines that deviate from the spending plan by sublinear errors, with regret degrading smoothly in the magnitude of the deviation.

## Results

The paper establishes sublinear regret guarantees for online decision making under adversarially changing reward/cost distributions, enabled by a spending plan input. For Online Resource Allocation (ORA), the dual algorithm (Algorithm 1) achieves dynamic regret of order $\widetilde{O}(\frac{1}{\rho_{\min}}\sqrt{T})$ (Theorem 3.2). For Online Learning with Resource Constraints (OLRC), the primal-dual algorithm (Algorithm 2) achieves static regret of order $\widetilde{O}(\frac{1}{\rho_{\min}}\sqrt{T})$ under both full and bandit feedback (Theorem 4.2). A meta-algorithm (Algorithm 3) handles the worst case where $\rho_{\min} \leq O(T^{-1/4})$, guaranteeing $\widetilde{O}(T^{3/4})$ regret in both ORA and OLRC settings (Theorem 5.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are made against prior theoretical results. Compared to Balseiro et al. [14] (best-of-many-worlds ORA), this paper handles adversarially *changing* distributions (a strictly harder setting) and achieves sublinear regret where [14] only provides competitive ratios in the adversarial case. Compared to Balseiro et al. [13] and Jiang et al. [34], which study spending-plan-based ORA under adversarially changing distributions, this paper removes the assumption $f_t(x) \leq \kappa \cdot c_t(x)[i]$ (which yields $\widetilde{O}(\kappa\sqrt{T})$ regret in those works) and instead obtains a $\widetilde{O}(\frac{1}{\rho_{\min}}\sqrt{T})$ bound that depends on the spending plan's balance rather than a reward-to-cost ratio. Compared to Castiglioni et al. [19] (OLRC best-of-both-worlds), this paper extends the primal-dual framework to the adversarially changing distribution setting with spending plan constraints. The key trade-off is that the $1/\rho_{\min}$ factor can be large for imbalanced spending plans, which the meta-algorithm (Algorithm 3) addresses at the cost of degrading from $\sqrt{T}$ to $T^{3/4}$ scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual and Lagrangian-relaxation frameworks for online resource allocation (ORA) and online learning with resource constraints (OLRC) by introducing a spending plan as a given input that guides per-round budget allocation. It builds directly on Balseiro et al. [14] (dual mirror descent for ORA) and Castiglioni et al. [19] (primal-dual for bandits with knapsacks), adapting their Lagrangian space bounding to the spending-plan setting via the Slater parameter ρ_min. A key departure from concurrent work by Jiang et al. [34] and Balseiro et al. [13] is the avoidance of a κ-boundedness assumption (f_t(x) ≤ κ·c_t(x)[i]), which those works require to achieve Õ(κ√T) regret; here the dependence is instead on 1/ρ_min, capturing how budget imbalance across rounds affects performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental impossibility: Balseiro and Gur [12] proved that sublinear regret is unattainable in adversarial ORA/OLRC, yet this work shows that a spending plan—a simple per-round budget sequence already provided by platforms like Meta and Google—is sufficient to recover Õ(√T/ρ_min) regret. The ρ_min dependence and the meta-algorithm for arbitrarily small ρ_min (yielding Õ(T^{3/4})) provide a clear performance-robustness tradeoff that practitioners can exploit. Open questions include how the guarantees degrade when the spending plan is adversarially wrong (beyond the sublinear-error analysis in Section 5.2), whether the ρ_min dependence is tight, and whether the framework extends to multi-state settings like constrained MDPs with non-stationary constraints.
