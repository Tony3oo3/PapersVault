## Problem

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are motivated by the Linear Representation Hypothesis, which assumes neural representations decompose into globally quasi-orthogonal, linearly accessible directions. However, recent evidence shows that neural network representations exhibit hierarchical structure with conditional orthogonality (orthogonal across levels but not within), nonlinearly accessible features, and multimodal entanglement that fall outside LRH's scope. This paper asks whether existing SAEs can faithfully capture such structures and whether an architecture explicitly designed for them yields more meaningful interpretability.

## Input / Output

MP-SAE consumes frozen activations from pretrained vision or vision-language models (e.g., ~261 spatial tokens per image from DINOv2, or joint image+text embeddings from CLIP on COCO) as well as synthetic hierarchical inputs generated from a two-level concept tree. Unlike standard SAEs that use separate encoder and decoder matrices, MP-SAE uses a single shared (tied) dictionary D for both encoding and decoding. The output is a sparse code with at most T active features (where T is the number of unrolled MP steps), a reconstructed representation, and a learned dictionary whose columns are unit-norm concept vectors; the sparsity level is not fixed at training time but determined adaptively at inference by a residual-norm stopping criterion.

## Method

MP-SAE embeds the classical Matching Pursuit algorithm into a sparse autoencoder by unrolling its greedy inference into T sequential encoder steps. At each step, the current residual is projected onto the shared dictionary, the most correlated feature is selected, its contribution is added to the reconstruction, and the residual is updated—guaranteeing stepwise orthogonality (Proposition 3.1) between the newly selected feature and the updated residual. This residual-guided mechanism promotes conditional orthogonality across selected features and, because the residual is a nonlinear function of the input, enables recovery of features that are not accessible via any single linear projection from the raw representation. Training minimizes standard reconstruction loss with tied weights and no fixed sparsity target; inference halts when the residual norm drops below a threshold or the support stabilizes, yielding adaptive sparsity at test time with monotonically decreasing reconstruction error as more steps are added.

## Results

On a synthetic hierarchical tree with 20 concepts (11 parents, 9 children), MP-SAE is the only SAE that faithfully recovers both intra-level correlations and inter-level orthogonality across all tested interference levels (0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9), while Vanilla and BatchTopK suffer feature absorption and Matryoshka introduces negative sibling interference. On pretrained vision models (SigLIP, DINOv2, CLIP, ViT) trained on ImageNet-1K with expansion factor 25, MP-SAE consistently achieves higher R² at comparable sparsity levels than all baselines, indicating more informative features per unit of sparsity. MP-SAE is the only architecture exhibiting monotonic improvement in reconstruction error as inference-time sparsity k increases, and uniquely recovers multimodal features in CLIP (flatter modality score distribution vs. sharply bimodal for standard SAEs) on COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Vanilla (ReLU) SAE [23] and BatchTopK [17], MP-SAE wins decisively on the synthetic hierarchical benchmark: both baselines exhibit feature absorption (child concepts collapse into parent directions), while MP-SAE achieves exact recovery of the ground-truth dictionary at low-to-moderate intra-level correlation. Against Matryoshka SAE [20], which was specifically designed for hierarchical structure, MP-SAE still wins: Matryoshka avoids absorption but introduces negative interference between sibling features, distorting flat structure, whereas MP-SAE preserves both intra- and inter-level organization simultaneously. On the large-scale expressivity comparison (Figure 5), MP-SAE outperforms all three baselines in R² at every sparsity level across SigLIP, DINOv2, CLIP, and ViT. On adaptive inference-time sparsity (Figure 8), TopK-based SAEs [15] degrade when k is changed at test time due to sparsity mismatch, while MP-SAE improves monotonically; ReLU SAEs plateau. On multimodal feature recovery in CLIP (Figure 9), standard SAEs produce sharply bimodal modality scores (features fire exclusively on image or text), while MP-SAE yields a significantly flatter distribution with substantial midrange mass, uniquely recovering cross-modal shared concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-contextualizes the classical Matching Pursuit algorithm from sparse coding (Mallat & Zhang, 1993) within the sparse autoencoder framework, motivated by recent evidence that neural representations exhibit hierarchical, nonlinearly accessible, and multi-dimensional structure beyond the Linear Representation Hypothesis. It departs from standard SAEs (Bricken et al., Cunningham et al.) and hierarchy-aware variants like Matryoshka SAEs (Bussmann et al., 2025) by embedding a residual-guided sequential encoder that promotes conditional orthogonality rather than global quasi-orthogonality. The conceptual lineage draws on the sparse coding literature's unrolling paradigm (LISTA, unrolled ISTA) but applies it specifically to the interpretability setting, where the inductive bias is chosen to match observed representational phenomenology rather than a fixed linear mixing model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides concrete evidence that the assumption of linearly accessible, globally quasi-orthogonal features is insufficient for capturing the full structure of neural representations, and that this insufficiency has practical consequences: existing SAEs (including hierarchy-aware ones) fail to recover conditionally orthogonal features and produce modality-split dictionaries in vision-language models. The multimodal feature recovery in CLIP and the adaptive inference-time sparsity property are practically useful, but the method's greedy nature and its reliance on incremental explainability limit its applicability to flat or highly entangled regimes. Open questions include whether the conditional orthogonality inductive bias generalizes to language model representations, how MP-SAE scales to very large dictionaries, and whether the 'dark matter' phenomenon is fully explained by nonlinearly accessible features or involves additional structure.
