## Problem

Existing multi-task multi-agent RL methods apply modularity to the policy network, but this overlooks a key structural property: task dynamics exhibit 'bounded similarity'—strong local similarity within task groups (e.g., door-open vs. door-close) but sharp divergence across unrelated groups (e.g., door vs. catch tasks). Because policy distributions are mutually distinct even among dynamically similar tasks, policy-centric modularity cannot exploit the shared dynamics that would improve learning efficiency and sample efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-agent observation vectors (up to 229-dim in Bi-DexHands) and a one-hot task label, which is converted to a learnable task embedding that conditions every component of the world model. It outputs a joint multi-agent action vector (up to 26-dim) produced by an MPPI planner that evaluates candidate action sequences against H-step latent rollouts from the world model. The world model itself operates entirely in a SimNorm-projected latent space (512-dim) without a decoder, and the actor module generates only candidate actions for the planner rather than serving as the explicit decision-making policy.

## Method

M3W integrates Mixture-of-Experts into the world model rather than the policy. The dynamics model uses SoftMoE: a soft router produces an n×N weight matrix that dispatches n multi-agent (z,a) tokens to N independent MLP experts (weighted combination along rows) and aggregates their outputs back to n predicted latent tokens (weighted combination along columns), enabling shared experts for similar tasks while isolating gradients for dissimilar ones. The reward predictor uses SparseMoE with self-attention experts: a sparse router selects K experts via roulette sampling during training or top-K during execution, and each expert applies multi-head attention followed by average pooling to produce a shared scalar reward. At execution time, an MPPI planner samples N_p action sequences (augmented with N_π actor-prior samples), rolls them through the world model for H steps, evaluates each trajectory's return via predicted rewards plus a critic Q-value at the terminal state, and iteratively refines the action distribution over K_p iterations—bypassing any explicit policy at decision time. The actor (trained with HASAC) and critic are optimized jointly with the world model under a self-supervised loss that includes dynamics L2, soft cross-entropy reward, and Q-value terms, plus a load-balance loss for the SparseMoE router.

## Results

On Bi-DexHands (14 tasks), M3W achieves an average normalized return of 84.1±0.8, outperforming the best baselines HMASD (72.3±1.4) and MACD (72.0±1.2) by over 12 points. On MA-Mujoco (24 tasks), M3W reaches 40.2±1.1 average, surpassing HMASD (33.1±1.1) and MACD (29.5±1.1). Across all 38 tasks, M3W achieves the best performance on 71% (27/38) of tasks, with statistically significant improvements (p<0.05) on 27 tasks and explicit advantage (PoS>64%) on 26 tasks, while showing explicit disadvantage on only 6 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M3W consistently outperforms all five baselines on average across both benchmarks. Against MACD (model-based, transformer world model), M3W wins on 11/14 Bi-DexHands tasks and 17/24 MA-Mujoco tasks, with the largest gaps on catch-abreast (+19.6) and door-close-inward (+37.3); MACD edges out M3W on door-open-inward (92.7 vs 91.5) and swimmer-swimbwd (57.9 vs 46.6). Against HMASD (hierarchical skill discovery), M3W wins on 12/14 Bi-DexHands tasks and 16/24 MA-Mujoco tasks; HMASD retains an advantage on scissors (86.0 vs 76.4) and several 6-agent cheetah tasks (e.g., 6-cheetah-runbwd: 18.1 vs 12.3). Against MAT (sequence-modeling MARL), M3W dominates on nearly all tasks, with the most dramatic wins on catch-abreast (+45.0) and humanoid-walk (+36.0). Against MAPPO and HATRPO (policy-gradient baselines), M3W achieves substantially higher returns on complex manipulation tasks (e.g., catch-abreast: 64.1 vs 10.0/13.0) while remaining competitive on simpler tasks like bottle-cap where all methods converge near 90. Overall, M3W's MoE-based world model provides the most robust multi-task performance, particularly excelling on tasks with bounded similarity (catch group, door group) where knowledge reuse is most beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

M3W departs from the dominant policy-centric paradigm in multi-task MARL (subtask reorganization, skill discovery, network decomposition) by relocating modularity into the world model. It builds on SoftMoE (Puigcerver et al., ICLR 2024) for the multi-agent dynamics model and SparseMoE (Shazeer et al., ICLR 2017) for the reward predictor, while leveraging the MPPI planning framework (Williams et al., 2015) and HASAC (Liu et al., ICLR 2024) for candidate action generation. The key conceptual departure from prior MoE-in-MTRL work (CARE, Paco, AMESAC, MOORE, MCP, D2R, M3) is that all of those apply mixture-of-experts to the policy network, whereas M3W is the first to apply it to the world model, motivated by the observation that dynamics exhibit bounded similarity across task groups while policies do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bounded similarity finding provides a principled rationale for where modularity should live in multi-task learning, potentially redirecting future work away from policy decomposition toward dynamics-aware architectures. The planning-based execution (bypassing an explicit policy) offers a practical advantage for tasks with opposing or conflicting objectives, as demonstrated on door open/close tasks. However, the method is currently limited to continuous action spaces due to the MPPI planner, and extending it to discrete settings (via MCTS or CEM) remains open. The approach also assumes agents can access each other's observations and actions (single-round communication), which may not hold in all multi-agent deployments.
