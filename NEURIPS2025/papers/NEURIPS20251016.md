## Problem

Universal cross-domain retrieval (UCDR) requires extensive multi-domain annotated data to generalize to unseen domains and classes, which is prohibitively expensive in practice. While parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) of CLIP shows promise for few-shot settings, modality-coupled cross-modal interaction has never been explored in the most inference-efficient PEFT category (partially fine-tuned methods like LoRA and BitFit), leaving a gap between the proven benefits of cross-modal alignment and zero additional inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For retrieval, the method takes a query image from an entirely unseen domain (e.g., sketch, clipart, painting) and retrieves semantically matching images from the real domain, evaluated via mAP200 and Prec200 under both UnseenGallery and MixedGallery protocols. For few-shot classification, it takes RGB images and produces class labels using CLIP's text-encoder-based zero-shot inference with prompts like 'a photo of a <category>'. All retrieval experiments use a 2-shot setting (2 samples per class per source domain), while classification uses 16-shot, and the backbone is ViT-B/32 with d_t=512 and d_v=768.

## Method

MAIL freezes selected target layer pairs in CLIP's transformer blocks and inserts lightweight agent layers (a scaling vector a and shifting vector b) after each target layer to approximate localized parameter updates via the operation AL∘OP(x) = OP(x)·Λ(a) + b. The key innovation is a bottleneck-based bridge function that projects the text agent layer's scaling vector into the vision agent layer (a̅_v = a_v + W_up·W_down·a_t), enabling gradient communication across modalities during training and enforcing aligned parameter updates. The coupling direction is language-to-vision (L→V), as text features are semantically compact and better suited to guide alignment without dilution from visual background noise. At inference, the agent layers are seamlessly reparameterized into the frozen target layers, preserving CLIP's original inference complexity with zero additional latency. The method fine-tunes (8+4r)·(d_t+d_v) parameters per transformer block with r=8, comparable to MMA but significantly fewer than MaPLe.

## Results

On the primary FS-UCDR task (2-shot, DomainNet, 5 query domains), MAIL achieves an average UnseenGallery mAP@200 of 62.05% and Prec@200 of 58.31%, surpassing the full-data SOTA ProS (60.52% mAP@200) by 1.53 points while using only ~1/140 of ProS's training data. Among PEFT baselines, MAIL leads the closest competitor MMA by 1.74 mAP@200 points (62.05 vs. 60.31) and the modality-coupled prompt method MaPLe by 1.76 points. On few-shot classification (16-shot, base-to-novel, 11 datasets), MAIL attains a harmonic mean of 81.10%, outperforming the previous best DeKg (80.44%) by 0.66 points without any regularization loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAIL outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary FS-UCDR task. Against the full-data SOTA ProS [13], MAIL gains +1.53 mAP@200 (62.05 vs. 60.52) using only 2-shot data (~1/140 of ProS's training samples). Among modality-coupled PEFT methods, MAIL beats MMA [49] by +1.74 mAP@200 and MaPLe [24] by +1.76 mAP@200, confirming that aligning internal parameter updates is more effective than coupling prompts or shared adapter projections. Modality-independent variants (IVLP, IVLA) trail their coupled counterparts by 3–6 mAP@200 points, validating the importance of cross-modal interaction in low-data settings. Partially fine-tuned methods without coupling (BitFit, LoRA) lag further behind, with LoRA [22] achieving only 55.40 mAP@200. On few-shot classification (16-shot base-to-novel), MAIL surpasses DeKg [30] (previous best) by +0.66 HM (81.10 vs. 80.44) and MMA [49] by +1.23 HM, while also outperforming prompt-based methods CoOp [57], CoCoOp [56], KgCoOp [50], and PromptSRC [25] by 1–3 HM points across 11 datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the modality-coupled PEFT paradigm (established by MaPLe for prompts and MMA for adapters) to the previously unexplored 'partially fine-tuned' category, where methods like BitFit and LoRA introduce no inference overhead. The agent layer mechanism directly repurposes the scaling-and-shifting reparameterization from Lian et al. (NeurIPS 2022), while the bridge function borrows LoRA's low-rank bottleneck structure and initialization scheme to couple cross-modal updates. Conceptually, the paper reframes cross-modal interaction as a form of regularization that constrains parameter updates to remain coherent across encoders, a perspective not explicitly articulated in prior MCMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of FS-UCDR addresses a practical data-scarcity bottleneck in cross-domain retrieval, and the finding that 2-shot training outperforms full-data training (62.05% vs. 61.95% mAP) challenges the assumption that more source-domain data always helps under large domain gaps. The plug-and-play design that preserves CLIP's inference complexity makes the approach immediately deployable. However, the sequential AL∘OP(x) structure incurs slightly higher training memory and time compared to parallel MCMs like MaPLe and MMA, and the gains over existing modality-coupled methods (1–2% mAP) are modest, leaving open whether the alignment mechanism can be made parallel without sacrificing the cross-modal gradient coupling.
