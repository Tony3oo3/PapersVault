## Problem

Existing diverse decoding methods for LLMs (temperature sampling, diverse beam search, determinantal beam search) produce lexical variations but fail to ensure meaningful semantic differentiation among generated responses. This limitation constrains Best-of-N strategies, group-based reinforcement learning (GRPO, RLOO, Iterative-RLHF), and data synthesis, where semantically distinct candidates are essential. The ideal approach—generating many candidates, embedding and clustering them, then selecting representatives—is computationally prohibitive, motivating a method that achieves semantic diversity directly during decoding.

## Input / Output

Given a text query q, a pretrained language model M, and a lightweight sentence embedding model E (e.g., 0.5B–1.5B parameters), SemDiD produces k semantically diverse and high-quality text responses. The method operates as a drop-in replacement for standard beam search decoding without requiring any training or fine-tuning. A key implementation detail is that semantic diversity is assessed via dynamic multi-step lookahead (exploring L_t ≈ 10 tokens ahead per beam) rather than token-by-token, since single-token semantic shifts are too subtle for reliable embedding-based measurement. The first group always uses greedy decoding to establish a quality baseline and reference direction.

## Method

SemDiD runs k parallel beam-search groups, each steered toward a distinct semantic region via three complementary mechanisms: (1) orthogonal directional guidance using Gram-Schmidt-constructed vectors in embedding space to establish initial trajectory divergence, (2) dynamic inter-group repulsion that penalizes candidates approaching other groups' semantic regions, and (3) position-debiased probability scoring that corrects for sequence-length and sentence-position biases to serve as a quality lower bound. A stage-aware transition (controlled by α_t) shifts weight from directional guidance to inter-group repulsion as decoding progresses, recognizing that fixed directions become suboptimal for longer sequences. Quality and diversity are balanced through a harmonic gain function that adaptively prioritizes the weaker metric, combined with an ε-constraint that guarantees each group's quality stays within a bounded factor of the greedy baseline. The approach is training-free and adds only 25–35% latency over standard group beam search.

## Results

On Best-of-N evaluation with Qwen-2.5-3B, SemDiD achieves 82.4% coverage on ARC-Challenge, 85.6% on BBH, 98.1% on GSM8K, and 82.63% on MMLU-Pro+ with 25 samples, outperforming all baselines by 1.4–5.2 percentage points. In RLHF training with Qwen-2.5-7B, SemDiD reaches 88.2% accuracy on GSM8K (GRPO) and 73.4% win rate on TLDR (GRPO) at 60 rollouts, improving accuracy by 1.8–2.1% over the best baseline while accelerating convergence by 15%. The method adds only 25–35% computational overhead over standard group beam search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemDiD consistently outperforms all four baselines—Temperature Sampling, Arithmetic Sampling, Diverse Beam Search, and Determinantal Beam Search—across all nine benchmarks. Group Beam Search (the structured variant of Diverse Beam Search) is the strongest baseline, trailing SemDiD by roughly 1–2 percentage points on most tasks, while Temperature Sampling shows the most inconsistent performance (e.g., 77.5% on BBH but only 71.2% on Minerva Math at N=25). The largest absolute gains appear on MMLU-Pro+ (5.2% over the best baseline) and Minerva Math, where semantic diversity in reasoning paths is most impactful. In RLHF experiments, SemDiD combined with GRPO achieves 88.2% on GSM8K and 73.4% win rate on TLDR, exceeding the best baseline (Determinantal Beam Search + GRPO) by 1.8–2.1% and reaching target performance 15% faster in terms of rollout steps. The paper notes that group-based diversity strategies (Diverse/Determinantal Beam Search) consistently outperform independent sampling methods (Temperature/Arithmetic Sampling), confirming that explicit inter-sequence interaction is essential for effective exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemDiD extends the diverse beam search lineage (Vijayakumar et al. 2018; Li et al. 2016) and determinantal beam search (Meister et al. 2021) by shifting the diversity mechanism from token/n-gram-level penalties to explicit semantic-space operations: Gram-Schmidt orthogonal directional guidance and inter-group repulsion in embedding space. It also draws on the group-based RLHF framework (GRPO, RLOO, Iterative-RLHF) and Best-of-N self-consistency strategies, positioning semantic diversity as a prerequisite for effective reward signal diversity. The position-debiased probability assessment and harmonic-mean quality-diversity balancing are novel contributions that depart from prior work treating raw token likelihood as a sufficient quality proxy, and the stage-aware transition between directional guidance and repulsion distinguishes SemDiD from fixed-penalty diverse beam search variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: existing diverse decoding methods produce lexically varied but semantically redundant outputs, limiting Best-of-N coverage and RLHF exploration efficiency. The 25–35% computational overhead for 1.4–5.2% coverage gains and 15% faster RLHF convergence is a reasonable trade-off for applications where semantic diversity is critical. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-autoregressive or diffusion-based LLMs, whether the lightweight embedding model assumption holds for highly domain-specific tasks, and whether the harmonic balancing objective is truly optimal versus learned or task-adaptive weighting. The 25–35% latency penalty also raises questions about applicability in latency-sensitive deployment settings.
