## Problem

Existing EEG foundation models inherit a scale-agnostic dense modeling paradigm from NLP and vision, applying fixed-granularity tokenization and uniform dense attention across all tokens. This design overlooks the intrinsic cross-scale spatiotemporal structure of neural activity, where different decoding tasks span vastly different temporal scales (transient motor bursts vs. slow sleep oscillations) and spatial scales (focal cortical activations vs. distributed emotion-related networks), leading to scale mismatch, spurious dependencies, and weakened cross-task generalization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw multichannel EEG signals (E ∈ R^{|C_x|×T}) that are band-pass filtered (0.3–75 Hz), notch-filtered at 60 Hz, resampled to 200 Hz, amplitude-normalized to 100 μV, and segmented into 30-second windows. During pretraining on TUEG (1.1M segments, >9,000 hours), the output is a reconstructed EEG segment via MSE loss; during downstream fine-tuning, a lightweight task head produces class labels (2–9 classes) or continuous regression values (vigilance score) depending on the target task.

## Method

CSBrain alternately stacks two modules for L layers: (1) Cross-scale Spatiotemporal Tokenization (CST), which applies K multi-scale temporal convolutions within local windows and K multi-scale spatial convolutions within anatomical brain regions (10-20 system), concatenating outputs with exponentially decaying embedding dimensions (d_k ∝ 1/2^k) to form compact scale-aware tokens; and (2) Structured Sparse Attention (SSA), which captures long-range dependencies via inter-window attention (self-attention over tokens sharing the same relative position across windows) and inter-region attention (self-attention over regional descriptors combining a sampled token with mean-pooled region features), achieving O(N·k) complexity instead of O(N²). Pretraining uses masked autoencoding with 50% temporal masking and MSE reconstruction, and the model is fine-tuned with a task-specific head across 11 BCI tasks on 16 datasets.

## Results

CSBrain achieves a macro-average score of 0.7095 across 11 BCI tasks and 16 datasets, outperforming the strongest foundation model baselines CbraMod (0.6760), LaBraM (0.6697), and BIOT (0.6322) by 3.35%, 3.98%, and 7.73% respectively. The largest gains appear on BCIC2020-3 (B-Acc 0.6004 vs. 0.5373 for CbraMod), TUSL (B-Acc 0.8571 vs. 0.7625 for LaBraM), and BCIC-IV-2a (B-Acc 0.5657 vs. 0.5138 for CbraMod). On the regression task SEED-VIG, CSBrain achieves the best R² of 0.2363 and Pearson correlation of 0.6314. CSBrain is the only model to rank first or second on every single dataset-metric pair in the evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSBrain outperforms all three EEG foundation model baselines (BIOT, LaBraM, CbraMod) on the macro-average by 7.73%, 3.98%, and 3.35% respectively, and wins or ties on 30 of 32 dataset-metric pairs. The most decisive margins are on BCIC2020-3 (B-Acc 0.6004 vs. CbraMod's 0.5373, a 6.3-point gap), TUSL (B-Acc 0.8571 vs. LaBraM's 0.7625), and MentalArithmetic AUROC (0.8478 vs. CbraMod's 0.7905). CSBrain narrowly trails CbraMod on CHB-MIT B-Acc (0.7262 vs. 0.7398) and ISRUC/HMC F1-W (0.7990 vs. 0.8011), but leads on the corresponding AUROC and B-Acc metrics for those datasets. All foundation models substantially outperform the seven task-specific baselines (EEGNet, Conformer, SPaRCNet, ContraWR, CNN-Trans, FFCL, ST-Trans), with the macro-average gap exceeding 10 points, confirming the advantage of large-scale self-supervised pretraining over task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSBrain extends the EEG foundation model lineage (BIOT, LaBraM, CBraMod) by departing from the scale-agnostic, dense-attention paradigm inherited from NLP and vision. Where prior models segment EEG into fixed-granularity tokens and apply uniform self-attention across all of them, CSBrain introduces multi-scale convolutions within anatomically defined brain regions (Cross-scale Spatiotemporal Tokenization) and replaces dense attention with grouped inter-window and inter-region attention (Structured Sparse Attention). The masked autoencoding pretraining objective follows the MAE paradigm, and the 10-20 electrode system provides the anatomical structure that grounds the spatial grouping, a design choice absent from prior EEG foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing cross-scale spatiotemporal modeling as a key inductive bias, the paper provides a principled architectural rationale for why fixed-scale tokens and dense attention underperform on noisy, multi-scale EEG signals. The comprehensive evaluation across 11 tasks and 16 datasets is among the most thorough to date for EEG foundation models, giving the community a strong reference point. Open questions include whether the anatomical region grouping generalizes to non-standard or high-density electrode configurations, whether the cross-scale structure remains beneficial at much larger pretraining scales, and whether the structured sparsity can be further relaxed to capture task-specific long-range dependencies without reintroducing spurious correlations.
