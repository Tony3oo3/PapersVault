## Problem

Existing generative copy-paste augmentation methods for long-tail instance segmentation pre-generate a large pool of synthetic objects (e.g., 1200k) before training, then selectively paste them, resulting in substantial wasted generation and a mismatch between the generated data and the model's evolving learning needs. This paper asks whether the generation process itself can be guided by the instance segmentation model's feedback during training to produce only the most informative objects, achieving higher performance with far fewer synthetic samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252454/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes real LVIS training images, text prompts derived from class labels for diffusion conditioning, and three types of model feedback (classifier gradients, class-wise validation accuracy, and classifier weight vectors) extracted from the instance segmentation model at each round. It produces 512×512 synthetic object images with dichotomous segmentation masks (via BiRefNet), filtered by CLIP score > 0.25, which are then pasted onto real images with accuracy-based scaling to yield augmented training images with updated bounding box, mask, and class annotations. The total generation budget is only 72k objects across 10 rounds, compared to 1200k in baselines.

## Method

MRCA alternates training an instance segmentation model (CenterNet2) with generating objects via Stable Diffusion 3 Medium over N rounds, where each round's model feedback guides the next round's generation and pasting. For generation, the standard classifier-free guidance is extended with a third feedback term ω∇x C(x,y,M_φ) that steers the diffusion toward high-entropy (uncertain) objects using the model's classifier gradients, with the whole image treated as a single box proposal to extract features. For pasting, a class-wise budget formula B_r(c) = α_r(1−A_{r−2,c})·‖C_{r−2,c}−C_{r−3,c}‖·1/S_{r−2,c} allocates more objects to low-accuracy, low-coverage, and data-scarce classes, while accuracy-based resizing scales low-accuracy class objects larger and high-accuracy class objects smaller. A quota-balanced unique mapping ensures each synthetic object is used exactly once per class quota. The paper provides theoretical guarantees (Assumption 3.1, Lemma 3.2, Theorem 3.3) showing that multi-round feedback-guided augmentation monotonically improves data quality over single-round or unguided augmentation under conditions of sufficient model capacity, controlled distribution shift, and proper feedback direction.

## Results

On LVIS v1.0 with a ResNet-50 backbone, MRCA achieves 35.56 AP^box and 31.81 AP^mask using only 72k synthetic objects (6% of the 1200k budget used by baselines), outperforming BSGAL (35.40 / 31.56) and X-Paste (34.20 / 30.39). With a Swin-L backbone, MRCA reaches 51.80 AP^box and 45.91 AP^mask, surpassing DiverGen (51.24 / 45.48) and BSGAL (50.47 / 44.85). The gains are most pronounced on rare classes, where MRCA achieves 28.14 AP^box_r and 25.93 AP^mask_r (ResNet-50) and 51.58 / 46.84 (Swin-L), the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRCA outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric while generating only 72k objects versus their 1200k (a 6% budget). Against X-Paste, MRCA gains +1.36 AP^box and +1.42 AP^mask on LVIS v1.0 (ResNet-50), with the largest margin on rare-class mask AP (+3.72). Against BSGAL, the closest competitor, MRCA improves AP^box by +0.16 and AP^mask by +0.25, and extends the rare-class advantage to +0.19 AP^box_r and +0.50 AP^mask_r. On the Swin-L backbone (Table 2), MRCA beats DiverGen by +0.56 AP^box and +0.43 AP^mask, and BSGAL by +1.33 AP^box and +1.06 AP^mask. The consistent pattern across both backbones and all three frequency splits (rare, common, frequent) confirms that the multi-round feedback mechanism delivers more informative synthetic data per generated object rather than relying on volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRCA extends the copy-paste generative augmentation lineage (X-Paste, BSGAL, DiverGen) by replacing the static pre-generated object pool with an iterative, feedback-driven loop between the instance segmentation model and the diffusion generator. It draws on classifier-guided diffusion (classifier-free guidance, Universal Guidance) and prior feedback-guided synthesis for classification (Askari-Hemmat et al., Hayden et al.), but is the first to apply model gradients, class-wise validation accuracy, and classifier weight dynamics jointly to steer both object generation and pasting in a multi-round curriculum for instance segmentation. The multi-round structure connects to curriculum learning principles, but the key departure is that the 'curriculum' is not pre-defined over a fixed pool; instead, the most informative objects are generated on-the-fly at each round based on the model's current deficiencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6% generation budget result (72k vs. 1200k objects) while matching or exceeding SOTA is a compelling efficiency claim for practitioners constrained by compute or annotation budgets, and the easy-to-hard curriculum emerges naturally from the feedback loop rather than requiring manual scheduling. However, the scalability limitation (Table 8: 144k objects yield negligible gain over 72k) and the single-generator constraint suggest the method's gains are concentrated in the feedback mechanism rather than raw data volume, leaving open whether integrating multiple generators or diversity-focused objectives (à la DiverGen) can unlock further improvements. The accuracy-based resizing strategy, while effective on LVIS, may be inappropriate in domains where object scale carries semantic meaning (e.g., medical imaging), limiting direct transferability.
