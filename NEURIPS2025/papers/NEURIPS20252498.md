## Problem

Existing medical LLM benchmarks (MedQA, PubMedQA, MedMCQA) evaluate static, isolated question-answering that lacks three hallmarks of real clinical practice: patient-specific personalization, dynamic information gathering through doctor-patient interaction, and sequential dependency where errors at one stage cascade into subsequent decisions. No prior benchmark simulates the complete end-to-end clinical workflow from triage through treatment in an interactive environment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252498/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent initially receives only the patient's chief complaint and basic demographics; all other information (medical history, examination results) must be actively elicited through multi-turn dialogue with a Gemma2-9b patient agent that withholds the diagnosis. Medical images (7,338 across the dataset, spanning X-ray, MRI, CT, ultrasound, etc.) are provided at the examination stage. Outputs are structured across five sequential stages: a primary/secondary department assignment (19 × 156 taxonomy), a set of physical and auxiliary examination items, a free-text imaging report, a free-text diagnosis, and a set of treatment items (surgery, medication, chemotherapy, etc.), where each stage's output becomes the input context for the next.

## Method

MedChain-Agent is a multi-agent framework with three specialized roles—General Agents (domain-specific specialists recruited per task), a Summarizing Agent (synthesizes collective expertise into a decision), and a Feedback Agent (evaluates outputs and triggers iterative refinement)—operating in a cyclical loop where subsequent-stage physicians can reject and send back current-stage decisions for re-discussion. The framework integrates MedCase-RAG, which maps each case into a 12-dimensional clinical feature vector (age, sex, chief complaint, symptoms, history, physical exam, lab tests, imaging, diagnosis, treatment, primary/secondary department) and retrieves the top-3 most similar cases via cosine similarity on text-embedding-ada-002 embeddings of the symptom description, providing structured case-based references rather than chunked document retrieval. The RAG database dynamically expands with pseudo-data from resolved cases at lower retrieval priority. The benchmark itself comprises 12,163 de-identified EHRs from the iiYi platform across 19 specialties, with quality validated by five senior physicians (94.7% pass rate, Cohen's κ = 0.82), and evaluation uses task-specific metrics including IoU for department/examination/treatment matching, DocLens claim recall for imaging reports, and a five-level grading rubric for diagnosis.

## Results

On the MedChain benchmark (12,163 clinical cases across 5 sequential stages), MedChain-Agent achieves the highest average score of 0.5200, outperforming the best base LLM (Claude-3.5-sonnet, 0.4262), the best single-agent method (RAG, 0.4704), and the best multi-agent baseline (MDAgent+RAG, 0.4190). The largest per-task gains are in History-taking (0.5836 vs. 0.4870 for Few-shot) and Examination (0.6566 vs. 0.6408 for RAG). On established medical QA benchmarks (MedQA, PubMedQA, PathVQA, MedBullets), MedChain-Agent also achieves the best average of 0.576, surpassing CoT (0.537) and MDAgents (0.546).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedChain-Agent (avg. 0.5200) outperforms all 17 baselines on the MedChain benchmark. Against the strongest base LLM, Claude-3.5-sonnet (0.4262), it gains +9.38% on average, with the largest per-task advantage in History-taking (+22.74%) and Specialty Referral Level 2 (+14.55%). Among single-agent methods, RAG is the closest competitor (0.4704), but MedChain-Agent still leads by +4.96% overall and by a wide margin in History-taking (+11.04%) and Specialty Referral Level 2 (+10.38%). Among multi-agent baselines, MDAgent+RAG (0.4190) is the strongest, yet MedChain-Agent surpasses it by +10.10% on average, with particularly large gains in History-taking (+14.24%) and Examination (+12.20%). On the four external medical QA benchmarks (MedQA, PubMedQA, PathVQA, MedBullets), MedChain-Agent also achieves the best average (0.576), beating the top single-agent CoT (0.537) by +3.9% and the top multi-agent MDAgents (0.546) by +3.0%, confirming generalizability beyond the sequential clinical workflow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of evaluating medical LLMs via static, isolated QA benchmarks (MedQA, PubMedQA, MedMCQA) by introducing a sequential, interactive, and personalized clinical workflow evaluation. It extends the line of interactive clinical simulation work (AI Hospital, AgentClinic, CoD) by combining all three characteristics—personalization, interactivity, and sequentiality—into a single large-scale (12,163 cases) multimodal benchmark, whereas prior efforts addressed at most one or two of these dimensions. On the method side, MedChain-Agent builds on multi-agent collaboration frameworks (AutoGen, DyLAN, MedAgents, MDAgent) but introduces a cross-stage feedback mechanism and a structured case-based RAG module (MedCase-RAG) that replaces the chunk-based retrieval used in prior medical RAG systems (MIRAGE, Medical Graph RAG) with 12-dimensional feature vector matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MedChain fills a critical gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously captures the sequential dependency, dynamic information gathering, and patient-specific context that define real clinical decision-making, making it a likely reference standard for evaluating medical AI agents. The finding that even GPT-4o-mini achieves only ~0.42 average and that removing sequentiality or interactivity improves most models' scores underscores how far current LLMs are from reliable autonomous clinical reasoning. Key limitations include reliance on a single Chinese data source with specialty imbalance, and a simulated patient (Gemma2) that may not capture the full complexity of real doctor-patient communication. Open questions include whether the framework's gains transfer to Western medical systems, whether the pseudo-data expansion in MedCase-RAG degrades over time, and whether the feedback mechanism scales to longer clinical workflows.
