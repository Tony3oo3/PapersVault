## Problem

Adapting large foundation models to downstream tasks while generating multiple diverse solutions for improved robustness and ensemble performance remains computationally infeasible with full fine-tuning. Existing multi-solution optimizers like SVGD promote diversity solely in parameter space via kernel-based repulsion, ignoring the geometric structure of the loss landscape and failing to enforce diversity in output-space predictions. This paper addresses both gaps by integrating geometric pre-conditioning and output-space divergence into a unified multi-solution optimization framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and produces class labels with predicted probability distributions from an ensemble of M=4 particle models. Each particle is a lightweight LoRA adapter module attached to a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-21K, so only the small adapter parameters are optimized while the shared backbone remains fixed across all particles. The final prediction aggregates outputs from all particles, and calibration is evaluated via Expected Calibration Error on the ensemble's probability outputs.

## Method

GAC-MSO extends Stein Variational Gradient Descent with two key mechanisms grounded in gradient flow theory over the Wasserstein space of probability measures. First, a geometry-aware pre-conditioner uses the Fisher information matrix H(θ) (estimated via its diagonal with a moving average over mini-batches) to scale gradient updates, effectively performing a natural-gradient step that accounts for the local curvature of the loss landscape. Second, a DPP-inspired divergence loss encourages the non-maximal (non-ground-truth) predictions of all particles to span a large volume in output space, promoting genuine predictive diversity rather than mere parameter-space separation. The practical update rule (Eq. 14) combines the standard SVGD kernel repulsion, the energy gradient weighted by the kernel, and the divergence loss gradient, all divided by the diagonal Fisher estimate, yielding a tractable per-particle update that balances collaborative accuracy with diverse output behavior.

## Results

On the VTAB-1K benchmark (19 datasets, ViT-B/16 + LoRA), GAC-MSO achieves 74.5% average top-1 accuracy with 4 particles, outperforming the best multi-solution baseline BayesTune (72.2%) by 2.3% and the best single-solution baseline AdamW (72.0%) by 2.5%. On calibration, GAC-MSO attains an average ECE of 0.16, matching SAM (0.16) and beating SVGD (0.18) and SADA-JEM (0.19). In the domain generalization setting (16-shot ImageNet-1K fine-tuning), GAC-MSO reaches 37.3% on ImageNet-Sketch, 69.1% on ImageNet-V2, 19.6% on ImageNet-A, and 35.8% on ImageNet-R, consistently exceeding all baselines by 1–2.5 points on the harder OOD splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAC-MSO outperforms all nine baselines on average VTAB-1K accuracy. Against the strongest multi-solution competitors, it beats BayesTune (72.2%) by 2.3%, SADA-JEM (72.1%) by 2.4%, and SVGD (70.9%) by 3.6%; the gap widens to 4.1% over SGLD, 6.4% over SA-BNN, and 7.5% over Deep Ensemble. Among single-solution methods, GAC-MSO exceeds AdamW (72.0%) by 2.5% and SAM (70.5%) by 4.0%, despite using 4× more trainable LoRA parameters. On ECE, GAC-MSO (0.16) ties SAM and beats SVGD (0.18), SADA-JEM (0.19), and BayesTune (0.23), indicating better-calibrated ensemble predictions. In domain generalization, GAC-MSO leads on all five test sets (e.g., 19.6% vs. 17.1% on ImageNet-A, 35.8% vs. 33.9% on ImageNet-R) and maintains a lower average ECE (0.049) than SVGD (0.232), though it is slightly higher than Deep Ensemble (0.034) on the in-distribution ImageNet split.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) by embedding it within a gradient-flow formulation over the Wasserstein space of probability measures, then augmenting the update with two new ingredients: a geometry-aware proximal term derived from the Fisher information matrix (analogous to a natural gradient in particle space) and a DPP-inspired divergence loss that enforces output-space diversity. The practical instantiation operates on LoRA modules atop a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone, positioning the work at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, Adapters, Prompt Tuning) and variational posterior sampling (SGLD, SVGD, BayesTune, deep ensembles). Unlike prior multi-solution PEFT methods that promote diversity solely through kernel repulsion in parameter space, GAC-MSO explicitly couples parameter-space geometry with output-space calibration, a combination not present in the SVGD or deep-ensemble literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, theoretically grounded recipe for generating diverse yet collaborative PEFT solutions at modest computational cost (only LoRA parameters are duplicated across particles), which is practically relevant for resource-constrained fine-tuning of large vision transformers. The consistent 2–3% accuracy gains and notably lower ECE across transfer learning, few-shot, and domain generalization settings suggest the geometry-aware update and output-diversity term are not merely cosmetic. However, the paper does not discuss limitations (no dedicated section), does not report compute resources, and is evaluated only on ViT-B/16 with four particles and LoRA, leaving open whether the approach scales to more particles, other PEFT variants (adapters, prompt tuning), or non-vision modalities. The diagonal Fisher approximation and moving-average estimation also raise questions about stability in high-dimensional or ill-conditioned regimes.
