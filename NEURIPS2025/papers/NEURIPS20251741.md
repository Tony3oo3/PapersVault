## Problem

Data deviations in EHR records—discrepancies between logged entries and a patient's true physiological state—undermine clinical decision-making, yet detecting them remains largely unexplored. Existing data valuation methods like the data Shapley value are inherently task-specific and computationally prohibitive, offering no efficient, task-agnostic measure of EHR data fidelity that could support real-time, pre-hoc deviation detection at the point of data entry.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular EHR features (e.g., 43 laboratory test values over a 90-day observation window) shared across multiple clinical prediction tasks (CKD progression, Stage 5 CKD onset, RRT dependence, mortality), each with its own label. It produces a single scalar fidelity score Ψ(x) per patient that is task-agnostic; a deviation is flagged when the fidelity drop ΔΨ = Ψ(x) − Ψ(x + Δx) between an original and a perturbed record exceeds a threshold δ, enabling a binary anomaly signal without requiring task-specific re-evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is a bi-level knowledge distillation pipeline. In the first level, task-specific data Shapley values are computed via Monte Carlo permutation sampling and distilled into per-task neural oracles O_nn trained jointly under multi-task learning with dynamic loss re-weighting, amortizing the exponential Shapley computation into a single forward pass. In the second level, all O_nn outputs are aggregated into a unified fidelity predictor Ψ through a learned attention mechanism that weights each task's oracle output based on both the task-specific hidden representation and Ψ's own hidden state. Training Ψ combines three losses: a knowledge-distillation MSE against the weighted oracle ensemble, a KL-divergence entropy regularization that encourages balanced utilization of all tasks, and a pairwise similarity penalty that suppresses redundant attention on highly correlated oracle outputs. The resulting Ι provides a holistic, task-agnostic fidelity signal whose temporal tracking reveals subtle deviations (as small as 0.1σ) that conventional anomaly detectors miss.

## Results

On the NUS EHR dataset (2,237 AKI patients, 43 lab tests), the proposed fidelity predictor Ψ achieves an AUC of 0.93 at a perturbation magnitude of just 0.1σ, and 0.95 at 5σ, in the controlled deviation injection experiment. In contrast, all five unsupervised anomaly detection baselines (One-Class SVM, LOF, GMM, k-Means Distance, PCA Reconstruction Error) remain near random (AUC ≈ 0.5) at 0.1σ and only begin to show meaningful detection above 1σ. The fidelity decline ΔΨ increases monotonically with deviation magnitude, and its sign remains a reliable detection signal even when |ΔΨ| is close to zero near the decision boundary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Ψ against five widely adopted unsupervised anomaly detection baselines: One-Class SVM (RBF kernel, 5% outlier allowance), Local Outlier Factor (20 nearest neighbors, 5th percentile threshold), Gaussian Mixture Model (log-likelihood, 5th percentile threshold), k-Means Distance (90th percentile threshold), and PCA Reconstruction Error (60% variance retained, 90th percentile threshold). Ψ decisively outperforms all baselines at small perturbation magnitudes: at 0.1σ, Ψ reaches AUC 0.93 while every baseline hovers near 0.5, indicating no effective discrimination. Most baselines only begin to exhibit meaningful detection performance when the deviation exceeds 1σ, and none approach Ψ's accuracy until the deviation reaches 5σ (where Ψ achieves 0.95). The paper notes that in practical clinical settings, deviations of several standard deviations are often already identifiable through conventional rule-based validation, so Ψ's key advantage lies in detecting subtle, small-magnitude deviations that are clinically significant yet easily overlooked by conventional approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Data Shapley value framework (Ghorbani & Zou, ICML 2019) for data valuation, but departs from its inherently task-specific nature by introducing a multi-task perspective that synthesizes task-specific valuations into a unified, task-agnostic EHR data fidelity measure. The bi-level knowledge distillation architecture (Shapley oracle → task-specific neural oracle → unified fidelity predictor) draws on amortized computation (Amos 2022; Covert et al., NeurIPS 2024) to replace prohibitively expensive per-sample Shapley computation with learned neural approximations, and on knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) for the hierarchical transfer. The attention-based aggregation with entropy and similarity constraints in the second distillation level is inspired by multi-task learning with attention (Liu et al., CVPR 2019), but applied here to fuse data valuation signals rather than task predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal treatment of EHR data deviation detection as a distinct problem, with direct clinical implications for pre-hoc quality control at the point of data entry. The task-agnostic fidelity formulation is a useful conceptual contribution that could extend beyond EHR to other domains where data provenance reliability matters. However, the evaluation is limited to a single clinical condition (AKI) at one hospital plus MIMIC-III, with only four prediction tasks and controlled (synthetic) perturbations; real-world deviation detection under natural, uncontrolled error conditions remains untested. The authors themselves note that low-fidelity cases may require additional uncollected data modalities to fully explain, leaving the completeness of the fidelity signal as an open question.
