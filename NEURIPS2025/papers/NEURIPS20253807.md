## Problem

Tensor-parallelism in LLM training and inference requires all-reduce operations to fully synchronize activations across devices, creating a communication bottleneck that grows as the tensor-parallel dimension increases. As compute power outpaces communication bandwidth, this overhead can dominate total training time, limiting practical tensor-parallelism to 8 devices within a node. The paper asks whether activations can be trained without full synchronization, reducing communication while preserving accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253807/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard tokenized text sequences for pretraining (RedPajama dataset) and produces generated token sequences during inference, identical to vanilla transformer LLMs. The unusual aspect is internal to the tensor-parallel execution: after each layer, devices hold different activation values in 'private' channels (the last (1-p)·h channels of the hidden dimension), while only the first p·h 'shared' channels are identical across devices. This means the model's computation graph is device-dependent, and inference with a different tensor-parallel dimension than training requires simulating multiple 'logical devices' on a single physical device.

## Method

CAAT-Net replaces the standard all-reduce with a 'partial channel-reduce' that synchronizes only a fraction p of the hidden-dimension channels between tensor-parallel devices. This requires redefining the MLP and attention output projections: the second weight matrix B is partitioned into four blocks (B₁₁, B₂₁, B₁₂, B₂₂) rather than two, so that shared channels receive contributions from all devices while private channels are computed locally. Two critical implementation adjustments are needed: (1) the backward-pass all-reduce must be moved to occur after backpropagating through the normalization function (rather than before, as in Megatron-LM), because the forward-backward equivalence that holds under full synchronization breaks under partial synchronization; and (2) gradient accumulation in the backward all-reduce must use 32-bit precision to avoid numerical divergence. An optional √r scaling factor on private channels equalizes activation variance across shared and private channels. With p=0.5 and tensor-parallel 8, a 7B-parameter Llama2 model trained on 160B tokens achieves near-identical benchmark accuracy while halving tensor-parallel communication, yielding up to 14% training throughput and 26% inference TTFT speedup.

## Results

On a 7B-parameter Llama2 model trained over 160B tokens with p=0.5 (50% communication reduction) and tensor-parallel 8, CAAT-Net achieves a validation loss of 1.00 vs. 1.01 for the full all-reduce baseline, and WikiText-103 perplexity of 12.46 vs. 12.51. Zero-shot accuracy is statistically indistinguishable across HellaSwag (46.10 vs. 45.85), WinoGrande (62.19 vs. 61.48), PIQA (72.86 vs. 72.91), and LAMBADA (61.05 vs. 61.34), with only BoolQ showing a marginally significant drop (62.51 vs. 64.89, p=0.046). Inference TTFT speedup reaches up to 26% (TP16, p=0.25) and 14% (TP8, p=0.5), while training throughput improves by up to 14% (TP16, p=0.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard full all-reduce tensor-parallelism as implemented in Megatron-LM (Intel's fork), where all hidden-dimension channels are synchronized between devices after every MLP and attention layer. CAAT-Net with p=0.5 reduces the tensor-parallel communication payload by 50% while matching or slightly exceeding the baseline on 6 of 8 evaluation metrics: validation loss (1.00 vs. 1.01), WikiText perplexity (12.46 vs. 12.51), HellaSwag (46.10 vs. 45.85), WinoGrande (62.19 vs. 61.48), PIQA (72.86 vs. 72.91), and LAMBADA (61.05 vs. 61.34). The only marginally significant degradation is on BoolQ (62.51 vs. 64.89, p=0.046) and OpenBookQA (24.00 vs. 26.60), both within the range of run-to-run variance for the other benchmarks. Ablations on 130M and 1.1B models confirm that p=0.5 consistently yields equal or better validation loss across tensor-parallel dimensions 2–16, while p values below 0.25 cause clear degradation. The paper also shows that compression-based alternatives (Top-K masking, random masking) suffer severe accuracy drops even at minimal compression rates, whereas CAAT-Net preserves accuracy because non-shared channels remain fully utilized rather than being discarded.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Megatron-LM's tensor-parallelism framework, which partitions weight matrices across devices and uses all-reduce to synchronize activations. It departs from the two dominant strategies for reducing tensor-parallel communication—pipelining (Domino, Ladder Residual) and compression (Top-K, auto-encoders)—by instead reducing the total communication volume through a new architectural primitive, partial channel-reduce, that synchronizes only a subset of hidden-dimension channels. The authors explicitly position their method as orthogonal to and complementary with pipelining, and show empirically that compression-based alternatives (random masking, Top-K) cause severe accuracy degradation even at minimal compression ratios, whereas partial channel-reduce preserves accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As compute-to-bandwidth ratios continue to worsen, tensor-parallel communication is becoming the dominant bottleneck, capping TP dimensions at 8 in most production deployments. CAAT-Net demonstrates that this cap can be relaxed by reducing communication by 50% with negligible accuracy loss, potentially enabling larger TP dimensions and better hardware utilization. However, the practical speedup is modest (14–26%), the method requires non-trivial architectural and backward-pass modifications rather than being a drop-in optimization, and the accuracy gains from reduced communication (slight validation loss improvement) are inconsistent across model sizes. Open questions include how partial channel-reduce interacts with other parallelism strategies (context-parallelism, pipeline-parallelism), whether the optimal synchronization factor p shifts with model scale, and whether the approach generalizes to architectures beyond the Llama/GPT family.
