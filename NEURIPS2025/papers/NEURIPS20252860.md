## Problem

Existing graph domain adaptation (GDA) methods are predominantly model-centric, relying on specific GNN architectures and training procedures that are brittle under severe distribution shifts and computationally expensive to tune. This model-dependency also ignores source data quality, where harmful training samples under covariate shift can degrade generalization. The paper asks: how can one select the most relevant source-domain training data for target-domain graph classification without depending on any GNN model's predictions or training recipe?

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source-domain training graphs and labeled source-domain validation graphs (which proxy the target domain distribution), each represented as an attributed graph with an adjacency matrix and node feature matrix. It produces a sparse weight vector over the training set where non-zero entries (constrained to at most ⌊n·τ⌋ by a sparsity ratio τ) define the selected training subset. The validation set must be labeled for the supervised GDA setting, though a label-free variant (c=0) is also supported for unsupervised GDA.

## Method

GRADATE uses Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) distance as a model-free graph comparison metric, justified by Theorem 3.1 which proves FGW upper-bounds the Wasserstein distance between any k-layer ReLU GNN's representations of two graphs. It defines Graph Dataset Distance (GDD) as an optimal-transport distance between graph datasets that combines LinearFGW pairwise graph distances with label-specific distribution distances (a graph-domain extension of OTDD), and Theorem 3.3 shows the domain generalization gap is upper-bounded by GDD. The core optimization, GREAT, iteratively alternates between (i) gradient-based optimal transport alignment that updates training sample weights to minimize GDD between the weighted training distribution and the validation distribution, and (ii) sparse projection onto the probability simplex that retains only the top-k largest weights and re-normalizes. After T iterations, the non-zero weight indices define the selected training subset, which can then be used to train any vanilla GNN or augment existing GDA methods without requiring model-specific architecture or training procedures.

## Results

On six graph-level datasets under covariate shift (graph density), GRADATE achieves the best or near-best classification accuracy across all selection ratios (10%, 20%, 50%) with both GCN and GIN backbones. Notably, with only 10% of training data, GRADATE already matches or exceeds full-data GNN performance on most datasets (e.g., 0.805 vs. 0.822 full on IMDB-BINARY/GCN; 0.657 vs. 0.671 full on ogbg-molbbbp/GIN). At 50% selection, GRADATE reaches 0.900 on IMDB-BINARY/GIN and 0.942 on MSRC_21/GIN, surpassing full-data baselines. When combined with vanilla GNNs at 20% data, GRADATE matches or exceeds state-of-the-art GDA methods (AdaGCN, GRADE, ASN, UDAGCN) on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRADATE consistently outperforms LAVA [26] across nearly all dataset–architecture–ratio combinations; LAVA frequently produces identical scores regardless of selection ratio (e.g., 0.620 at all τ on IMDB-BINARY/GCN), indicating it fails to exploit the sparsity constraint effectively. Against K1DD-LR [66], a model-centric graph dataset distillation method, GRADATE wins on 5 of 6 datasets for GCN at 20% selection (e.g., 0.855 vs. 0.787 on IMDB-BINARY; 0.601 vs. 0.477 on ogbg-molbbbp) and on 5 of 6 for GIN. Compared to random selection, GRADATE gains are substantial under severe shift: on IMDB-MULTI/GIN at 20%, GRADATE reaches 0.266 versus 0.180 for random. When paired with vanilla GNNs at 20% data, GRADATE matches or exceeds all four GDA baselines (AdaGCN, GRADE, ASN, UDAGCN) on most datasets—for instance, GRADATE+GCN achieves 0.830 on IMDB-BINARY versus 0.808 (AdaGCN) and 0.822 (GRADE) on full data. The one notable exception is ogbg-molhiv, where LAVA slightly edges GRADATE with GIN at 50% (0.641 vs. 0.658), and GraphSAGE with random selection occasionally matches GRADATE due to neighbor-sampling noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Optimal Transport Dataset Distance (OTDD) framework of Alvarez-Melis and Fusi from image/text data to attributed graphs, using Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) distance as the underlying pairwise graph metric. It departs from the dominant model-centric GDA paradigm (AdaGCN, GRADE, ASN, UDAGCN) by proposing a model-free data selection approach that is agnostic to the downstream GNN architecture. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) establishes a novel upper-bound relationship between FGW distance and multi-layer GNN representation distances, providing the first principled justification for using FGW as a model-free graph comparison tool. The optimization procedure GREAT draws on sparse projection over the probability simplex, connecting to data valuation methods like LAVA but with a fundamentally different objective (direct GDD minimization vs. gradient-based valuation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication that 10–20% of carefully selected training data with a vanilla GNN can match or exceed full-data GDA methods offers a compelling data-efficiency story for practitioners facing severe distribution shift with limited compute. The model-free nature means GRADATE can be plugged in front of any existing GDA pipeline without architectural changes, broadening its applicability. However, the method requires labeled validation data from the target domain, which limits its use in truly unsupervised settings, and the empirical gains are inconsistent across datasets (e.g., modest improvements on ogbg-molhiv and ogbg-molbbbp, high variance on IMDB-MULTI). Open questions include scalability to very large graph collections, extension to multi-source and continual domain adaptation, and whether the FGW-based distance can be further approximated for graphs with heterogeneous node counts.
