## Problem

Existing indoor layout generation methods are either prompt-driven (relying on closed-source LLM APIs with poor spatial consistency and no fine-tuning capability) or learning-based (diffusion models trained on small datasets like 3D-Front with fewer than 7,000 layouts, limited to bedrooms and living rooms, and lacking any human-preference alignment objective). The paper tackles three intertwined gaps: insufficient scene diversity across room types, the absence of explicit spatial reasoning in generation, and the lack of a preference-aware training signal that aligns outputs with human design intuition and physical plausibility.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language instruction that is parsed by a Qwen2.5-7B wrapper into structured JSON specifying room type, floor dimensions, object descriptions with quantities, and retrieved 3D bounding boxes from the Objaverse database. The LLM produces a two-part output: first a free-form high-level semantic summary describing spatial organization and functional zoning (e.g., 'place the sofa against the long wall with the coffee table in front'), then a JSON block with per-object 3D coordinates (meters, origin at room center, z-up) and rotation angles in radians. The final layout is rendered into a 3D scene via NVIDIA Isaac Sim for visualization and downstream evaluation.

## Method

OptiScene fine-tunes Qwen3-8B via LoRA in two stages. Stage I (SFT) introduces a reasoning-guided generation format where the model must first produce a natural-language spatial summary before predicting concrete object placements, encouraging it to internalize functional zoning and object relationships rather than regurgitating coordinates. Stage II applies a two-stage Direct Preference Optimization: DPO-Stage 1 contrasts ~2,200 expert-curated human-consensus layouts (positives) against SFT model generations (negatives) to capture stylistic and structural preferences; DPO-Stage 2 synthesizes hard negatives by injecting targeted geometric violations (object collisions, boundary overflows) into positive scenes, sharpening the model's sensitivity to physical plausibility. The training data is 3D-SynthPlace, a 16,666-scene dataset combining 7,306 filtered 3D-Front layouts with 9,360 manually corrected Holodeck-generated scenes, expanding coverage to kitchens and bathrooms and adding GPT-generated semantic descriptions to every sample.

## Results

On the 3D-SynthPlace test set, OptiScene achieves the lowest FID across all four room types (26.45 bedroom, 31.25 living room, 41.73 kitchen, 35.29 bathroom) and the lowest Object Overlap Rate (0.01–0.05), outperforming the strongest retrained baseline LLplace (w/ 3D-Synth) by 11.21 FID in bedrooms and halving OOR in several categories. In terms of generation success rate, OptiScene reaches 75% for bedrooms and 50% for living rooms, compared to 40%/30% for LLplace and 30%/20% for DiffuScene. GPT-4o ratings confirm the advantage, with OptiScene scoring up to 9.4 in layout rationality for bathrooms versus 7.6 for DiffuScene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OptiScene outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the prompt-driven I-Design [2], OptiScene reduces FID by roughly 50 points (e.g., 26.45 vs. 76.42 in 3D-SynthPlace bedrooms) and cuts OOR from 0.18 to 0.01, while I-Design also fails to generate scenes with more than 5–6 objects. Versus the diffusion-based DiffuScene [33] (retrained on 3D-SynthPlace), OptiScene lowers FID by 12.51 in bedrooms and 13.00 in living rooms, and reduces OOR from 0.06 to 0.01; DiffuScene also fails entirely on kitchen and bathroom categories in its original form. Compared to the LLM-based LLplace [41] (retrained on 3D-SynthPlace), OptiScene achieves 11.21 lower FID in bedrooms and 9.47 lower in living rooms, with OOR halved in most room types. On generation success rate (Table 3), OptiScene leads by 35 percentage points over LLplace in bedrooms (75% vs. 40%) and by 20 points over Holodeck [42] in living rooms (50% vs. 30%), while Holodeck remains the strongest prompt-driven baseline in bedrooms at 55%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) to 3D indoor layout generation, a domain where DPO had not previously been used. It departs from the dominant prompt-driven paradigm (Holodeck, LayoutGPT, I-Design) by fine-tuning an open-source LLM (Qwen3-8B) rather than relying on proprietary APIs, and from diffusion-based learning methods (DiffuScene, LLplace) by using autoregressive generation with a high-level semantic reasoning step before coordinate prediction. The two-stage DPO design—first aligning with human-curated consensus layouts, then injecting synthetic geometric violations as hard negatives—repurposes the DPO framework for spatial plausibility in a way not seen in prior layout generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3D-SynthPlace dataset (16,000+ scenes across four room types) addresses a real bottleneck in the field, where prior work was constrained to ~7,000 3D-Front layouts dominated by bedrooms and living rooms. The demonstration that preference-aligned LLMs can outperform both prompt-driven and diffusion-based baselines on success rate and physical validity suggests a viable path toward deployable, open-source layout generators. However, the work is limited to single-room, furniture-only generation, and the evaluation relies on a small test set (423+53+200 scenes) without error bars, leaving open whether the gains generalize to multi-room or full-building layout tasks and to more diverse object categories.
