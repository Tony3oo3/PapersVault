## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction methods for LLM inference rely on simple heuristics—primarily attention weights—to score token importance, overlooking the spatial geometry of token value vectors in the embedding space. This leads to suboptimal token retention decisions and measurable performance degradation, particularly under tight cache budgets where the choice of which tokens to keep is most critical.

## Input / Output

The method operates during the prefilling phase of a Transformer LLM, consuming the key-value pairs from all attention heads of a single layer along with queries generated from an observation window of the last 32 prompt tokens. Given a per-head budget size B, it outputs a subset of retained KV pairs per head, with the total budget allocated adaptively across heads via a global top-k selection over concatenated scores. Tokens are first merged into chunks of size 4 (weighted-sum of values, summed attention weights) before scoring, and the first token is always preserved.

## Method

AnDPro formulates KV cache eviction as a combinatorial optimization problem minimizing the squared error between pre- and post-eviction attention outputs under a sparsity constraint, then applies mild relaxations to obtain a convex sparse optimization problem (P1). By deriving the KKT conditions of the dual problem, the paper shows that a scoring function of the form s_i = a_i(θ^T v_i + b) can identify which tokens are active (retained) versus inactive (evicted) in the optimal solution. The key practical insight is setting the anchor direction θ to the pre-eviction output y itself and b to 0, yielding the simple score s_i = a_i · y^T v_i, which measures each token's attention-weighted projection onto the direction of the full attention output. This projection-based score is computed per observation-window query and summed, then used for cross-head top-k selection with chunk merging to maintain semantic coherence among adjacent retained tokens.

## Results

On LongBench (16 datasets, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2), AnDPro achieves an average score of 40.84 at budget=256, maintaining 96.07% of the full-cache accuracy (42.51) while using only 3.44% of the total KV cache (256/7425 tokens). This represents a 46.0% reduction in KV cache budget compared to the previous SOTA (CriticalKV, which requires budget 474 for comparable performance). On the Needle-in-a-Haystack retrieval test at budget=128, AnDPro scores 97.37, nearly matching full-cache performance. Results are consistent on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, where AnDPro also leads all baselines across all budget sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnDPro consistently outperforms all six baselines across every budget size (128–1024) on both Mistral-7B and Llama-3.1-8B. Against the closest competitor CriticalKV, AnDPro gains +0.53 (budget=128), +0.81 (budget=256), +0.64 (budget=512), and +0.20 (budget=1024) in average LongBench score on Mistral. The gap over Ada-KV is larger at smaller budgets (+1.55 at budget=128) and narrows at larger budgets (+0.21 at budget=1024), suggesting the projection-based scoring function is most beneficial under tight memory constraints. Against SnapKV and PyramidInfer, AnDPro leads by 3.87 and 3.38 points respectively at budget=128. On Needle-in-a-Haystack (budget=128), AnDPro achieves 97.37 versus 93.50 for CriticalKV and 92.80 for Ada-KV, confirming superior long-context retrieval. The key efficiency claim is that AnDPro at budget=256 matches CriticalKV's performance at budget=474, a 46% reduction in required KV cache.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV cache eviction line initiated by H2O, SnapKV, and Ada-KV, which all rely on attention weights (or simple combinations thereof) as the token importance criterion. The key departure is formulating eviction as a sparse optimization problem and deriving, via KKT conditions, a projection-based scoring function s_i = a_i · y^T v_i that accounts for the geometric relationship between value vectors and the pre-eviction output direction. The implementation borrows the observation-window mechanism from SnapKV and the cross-head budget allocation from Ada-KV, positioning the projection score as a drop-in replacement for the attention-only score within those frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 46% KV cache budget reduction at matched accuracy (96.07% of full-cache with only 3.44% budget) is practically significant for deploying long-context LLMs under tight GPU memory constraints, and the method composes orthogonally with existing layer-wise or head-wise budget allocation strategies. The theoretical grounding via sparse optimization provides a principled justification for why projection onto the output direction should outperform raw attention weights, though the practical formula is simple enough to adopt without deep optimization background. Open questions include whether the anchor-direction choice generalizes to multi-query or grouped-query attention variants, whether the approach degrades gracefully at extreme compression ratios below 3% budget, and whether the chunk-merging heuristic can be made adaptive rather than fixed.
