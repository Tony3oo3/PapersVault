## Problem

Classical correlation clustering only models binary similarity/dissimilarity, but real-world applications involve multi-class relationship types (e.g., 'colleague' vs. 'family') or varying edge reliability. Two natural generalizations—Chromatic Correlation Clustering (CCC) and pseudometric-weighted CC—had suboptimal approximation guarantees: 2.5 for CCC and 17 for the pseudometric-weighted variant. This paper closes much of that gap by designing problem-specific rounding functions within a unified LP-pivoting framework.

## Input / Output

For pseudometric-weighted CC, the input is a complete graph with '+'/'−' edge labels and nonnegative weights satisfying the triangle inequality; the output is a vertex partition minimizing weighted disagreements. For CCC, the input is a complete graph whose edges are colored from a set L plus a special color γ (indicating separation), and the output is both a vertex partition and a single color assigned to each cluster. In both cases, the algorithm first solves an LP relaxation (polynomial time) and then applies a randomized pivoting rounding step in O(|V|²) time, producing the clustering in expectation.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified LP-PIVOT algorithm that partitions edges into three subsets (E⁺, E⁻, E°) and applies edge-type-specific rounding functions f⁺, f⁻, f° to convert LP values into non-selection probabilities during a random-pivot sweep. For pseudometric-weighted CC, a piecewise-linear rounding function with breakpoints at 0.4 and 0.6 yields a tight 10/3 approximation, and a lower-bound argument via adversarial triangle configurations proves no rounding function can do better within this framework. For CCC, the LP-CCC algorithm first assigns each vertex to its LP-majority color, then runs LP-PIVOT independently per color class, treating same-color edges as positive, γ-edges as negative, and cross-color edges as neutral; a newly designed f° for neutral edges (piecewise-linear with a slope change at 0.5) avoids overcounting and achieves a 2.15 approximation, with a matching 2.11 lower bound established through triple-based analysis that exploits LP constraint structure to bound the LP contribution of neutral edges below.

## Results

For pseudometric-weighted correlation clustering, the paper achieves a tight 10/3 (≈3.33) approximation via a refined LP-based pivoting algorithm with a piecewise-linear rounding function, and proves this is optimal within the standard LP relaxation framework. For Chromatic Correlation Clustering (CCC), the approximation ratio is improved from the previous best of 2.5 to 2.15, with a matching lower bound of 2.11 established within the same analytical framework, demonstrating near-optimality. Both results are obtained through a unified triple-based analysis of LP-rounding schemes with problem-specific rounding functions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For pseudometric-weighted CC, the paper's 10/3 approximation substantially improves upon the 17-approximation obtained by Charikar and Gao [14] (LP-UMVD-PIVOT with L=2), which the authors show is a special case of their LP-PIVOT framework but with suboptimal rounding parameters (α=1/3, β=0 yielding factor 6 under triple-based analysis). For CCC, the 2.15 approximation improves upon Xiu et al. [32]'s 2.5-approximation, which used the identity rounding function f(x)=x for all edge types; the improvement comes from a tailored f° function for neutral edges and the Chawla et al. [16] rounding for positive/negative edges. The lower bound of 2.11 (Theorem 3) shows the gap between the best achievable and the best proven is only 0.04, analogous to the classical CC setting where the gap is under 0.04 [16]. The paper also notes that the integrality gap of the underlying LP is at least 3 for pseudometric-weighted CC (via bipartite CC) and at least 2 for CCC (via classical CC), providing context for the approximation landscape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LP-based pivoting framework of Ailon, Charikar, and Newman [2] and the triple-based analysis of Chawla et al. [16] to two structured variants of correlation clustering. For pseudometric-weighted CC, it generalizes the pivoting approach of Charikar and Gao [14] (which yields a 17-approximation when L=2) by designing a tailored piecewise-linear rounding function that achieves a tight 10/3 bound. For CCC, it builds on the LP formulation of Xiu et al. [32] (2.5-approximation) by introducing a new neutral-edge rounding function f° and a decoupled color-assignment step, reducing the ratio to 2.15 while establishing a 2.11 lower bound within the same analytical framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tightness of the 10/3 bound for pseudometric-weighted CC is a significant negative result: it certifies that no rounding function, however clever, can beat 10/3 within the standard LP relaxation, redirecting future progress toward stronger relaxations (e.g., Sherali-Adams or cluster LP). For CCC, the narrow 2.15-vs-2.11 gap similarly suggests the LP framework is nearly exhausted, making it a natural stopping point for this line of analysis. Open questions include whether the cluster LP or preclustering techniques that push classical CC to 1.437 can be adapted to these weighted and chromatic settings, and whether the pseudometric constraint can be relaxed while preserving constant-factor guarantees.
