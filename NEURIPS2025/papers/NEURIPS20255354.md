## Problem

Enforcing hard physical constraints (conservation laws, boundary conditions) in pretrained flow-based generative models for PDEs remains unsolved: soft-penalty methods (PINNs) lack exact guarantees, gradient-based approaches (DiffusionPDE, D-Flow) are computationally expensive and only approximate constraints, and the ECI framework handles only simple linear, non-overlapping constraints. No existing method can enforce arbitrary nonlinear equality constraints exactly at inference time without retraining the underlying generative model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained unconditional FFM model (FNO backbone) trained on PDE solution datasets, an initial noise sample drawn from a Matérn GP or Gaussian prior, and a user-specified constraint residual function h(u) that encodes linear or nonlinear equality constraints (e.g., fixed IC, global mass conservation, nonlinear flux collocation). It outputs a spatiotemporal PDE solution field on the same grid as training (100×100 for 1D problems, 64×64×50 for 2D Navier-Stokes) that satisfies all imposed constraints to machine precision (residual < 10⁻⁶) while remaining aligned with the learned generative distribution.

## Method

PCFM is a zero-shot inference-time algorithm that interleaves constraint corrections with flow integration at each substep. At every step τ→τ+δτ, it (i) forward-shoots to τ=1 via a cheap ODE solve, (ii) applies a single Gauss-Newton projection onto the linearized constraint manifold, (iii) propagates the correction back to τ+δτ using the OT displacement interpolant (u₁−u₀) rather than integrating the learned vector field backward—avoiding the O(1) instability of reverse ODE integration—and (iv) applies a relaxed penalty correction to handle residual discretization error. A final full Newton-Schur projection guarantees exact constraint satisfaction at the terminal state. The projection solver assembles and inverts only an m×m Schur complement (m = dim h ≪ n), yielding O(n) per-sample cost, and is implemented as a batched, differentiable routine compatible with standard deep learning pipelines.

## Results

PCFM achieves zero constraint error (CE) to machine precision across all four PDE benchmarks (Heat, Navier-Stokes, Reaction-Diffusion, Burgers), while also delivering the best or near-best generation fidelity. On the most challenging Burgers IC setting, PCFM reduces MMSE to 0.052×10⁻² versus 10.0×10⁻² for ECI and 14.3×10⁻² for DiffusionPDE (a >99.5% improvement), and FPD to 0.101 versus 1.31 (ECI) and 35.8 (DiffusionPDE). On linear-constraint problems (Heat, Navier-Stokes), PCFM matches ECI's zero constraint error while achieving lower MMSE (0.241 vs 0.697 on Heat; 4.59 vs 5.23 on Navier-Stokes) and FPD (1.22 vs 1.34; 1.00 vs 1.04). The method incurs modest runtime overhead (e.g., 292 ms/sample on Heat vs 65 ms for vanilla FFM) but remains 4–6× faster than D-Flow.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCFM dominates on the hardest setting (Burgers IC with nonlinear mass conservation + local flux constraints), reducing MMSE by 193× over ECI (0.052 vs 10.0) and 275× over DiffusionPDE (0.052 vs 14.3), while achieving zero constraint residual where ECI shows CE(CL)=205×10⁻². On linear-constraint problems (Heat, Navier-Stokes), PCFM ties ECI and PDM on constraint error (all achieve zero) but wins on MMSE and FPD, e.g., 0.241 vs 0.697 MMSE on Heat. ECI is the closest competitor overall but cannot enforce nonlinear conservation laws (CE(CL)=6.00 on Reaction-Diffusion, 15.7–205 on Burgers) and loses on FPD by 8–13× on nonlinear problems. D-Flow and DiffusionPDE, which rely on gradient-based PINN losses during sampling, fail to satisfy hard constraints (CE values of 10²–10³) and are 4–6× slower than PCFM. PDM achieves zero constraint error on linear problems but over-constrains the trajectory, yielding the worst FPD on Burgers IC (99.7) and Reaction-Diffusion (109). PCFM's only losses are on SMSE for Reaction-Diffusion (0.583 vs ECI's 0.060) and Burgers BC (0.123 vs ECI's 0.089), and on FPD for Heat (1.22 vs PDM's 0.17), suggesting PDM's per-step projection is slightly better for simple linear cases but degrades sharply on nonlinear dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCFM builds directly on Functional Flow Matching (FFM) as its base generative model and on the Optimal Transport displacement interpolant from Flow Matching for its reverse-update mechanism. It generalizes the ECI framework (Cheng et al., ICLR 2025), which handles only linear, non-overlapping constraints with closed-form projections, to arbitrary nonlinear equality constraints via Gauss-Newton/Schur-complement projection. The method departs from gradient-based constraint guidance (DiffusionPDE, D-Flow) by avoiding backpropagation through the ODE entirely, and from soft-penalty approaches (PINNs) by enforcing constraints as hard requirements at inference time without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCFM provides a practical, zero-shot mechanism to retrofit exact physical consistency onto any pretrained flow-based PDE solver, which is critical for applications where constraint violation leads to unphysical or unstable results. The finding that stacking complementary hard constraints (local flux, IC, global mass) improves generation fidelity—contrary to the well-known degradation from competing soft objectives in PINNs—suggests a fundamentally different regime for constraint-aware generation. Open questions include extending to inequality constraints (e.g., positivity, TVD), exploiting sparsity in constraint Jacobians for scalability to very high-dimensional systems, and whether the approach transfers to other generative paradigms beyond flow matching.
