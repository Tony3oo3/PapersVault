## Problem

Fully resolving three-dimensional turbulence via direct numerical simulation requires Re^(9/4) resolution, making it computationally prohibitive at high Reynolds numbers. Existing ML surrogates face a scalability-accuracy tradeoff: Fourier neural operators struggle with large 3D kernels, and standard Transformers incur quadratic attention cost over the full mesh. The paper addresses this gap by designing an architecture that retains spectral-method accuracy while achieving near-linear scaling in the number of resolved modes.

## Input / Output

The model takes the initial velocity field expressed as spectral-element (SEM) expansion coefficients over a structured mesh of N³ elements with M³ modes per element (e.g., 8³ elements × 13³ modes for Re94), optionally augmented with a coarse DNS solution for learned-correction mode. The output is the velocity field at the next time step in the same SEM coefficient representation, decomposed into a spectrally truncated LES component (k_max modes per axis) and an unfiltered SGS residual. For the Re94 benchmark, the model predicts one step of Δt = 0.5 s on a 96³ grid, achieving accuracy comparable to a 256³ DNS while running 30× faster on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

EddyFormer tokenizes the flow field using the spectral-element method, where each coarse element becomes one token, reducing the attention sequence length from N³M³ (all resolved modes) to N³ (number of elements). The architecture has two complementary streams: an SGS stream that applies SEM-based spectral convolutions with small, factorized Fourier kernels (computed via quadrature over neighboring elements) to capture local eddy interactions, and an LES stream that applies multi-head self-attention at each collocation point independently across all elements to model global coherent structures. The LES stream operates on spectrally truncated tokens (k_max < M modes), and the SGS stream receives LES features through a learnable scalar coupling, reflecting the turbulence cascade hierarchy. Rotary position encoding is adapted to the SEM local coordinates to provide translational invariance. The model is trained with supervised one-step relative L2 loss (and optionally a 5-step rollout loss for 2D flows), achieving DNS-level accuracy at 256³ resolution with a 30× speedup and generalizing to domains up to 4× larger via attention masking.

## Results

On the 3D Re94 isotropic turbulence benchmark (direct prediction), EddyFormer achieves 8.61% one-step relative L2 error (Chebyshev basis) and 8.52% (Legendre basis), outperforming the best baseline F-FNO (22.5%) by a factor of ~2.6×. In the learned-correction setting, EddyFormer matches DNS at 256³ resolution (18.2% vs 16.3% L2 error at t=5) while running 30× faster (4.86s vs 152s per step on a single A100 GPU). On The Well benchmark, EddyFormer is the only model that converges on the Rayleigh-Taylor instability case (VRMSE 0.1115), where all baselines diverge (VRMSE >10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EddyFormer outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On Re94 direct prediction, it reduces one-step error from 22.5% (F-FNO, the strongest baseline) to 8.61%, a ~2.6× improvement, while also beating FNO (23.1%), TF-Net (27.7%), AViT (26.6%), GNOT (69.7%), and ResNet (87.2%). On KF4 learned correction at t=10, EddyFormer achieves 2.61% relative L2 error versus 5.28% for F-FNO, 7.58% for ResNet, and 74.5% for the prior Kochkov et al. approach. On The Well, EddyFormer (Cheb) achieves the lowest VRMSE on all four flow cases (MHD_64: 0.1160, shear_flow: 0.1123, rayleigh_benard: 0.2527, rayleigh_taylor: 0.1115), and is the only model that converges on Rayleigh-Taylor instability where FNO, T-FNO, U-net, and CNextU-net all diverge (VRMSE >10). The Legendre-basis variant is slightly better on shear_flow (0.0837) and rayleigh_benard (0.1854), while the Chebyshev variant edges ahead on MHD_64 and rayleigh_taylor.

## Relation to Prior Work

EddyFormer synthesizes three previously separate threads: (1) the spectral-element method (SEM) from computational fluid dynamics, repurposed as a tokenization scheme that decomposes the flow field into compact per-element tokens; (2) the LES conceptual decomposition into resolved large-scale and subgrid-scale dynamics, implemented as a dual-stream architecture with attention (LES stream) and localized spectral convolution (SGS stream); and (3) the learned-correction paradigm of Kochkov et al., where a neural network corrects a coarse DNS rather than predicting from scratch. The key departure from prior neural-operator work (FNO, F-FNO) is replacing global Fourier kernels with SEM-based convolutions and from prior Transformer-for-PDE work (GNOT, AViT, Transolver) is using SEM tokens instead of mesh-point tokens, reducing the attention sequence length from N³M³ to N³ and avoiding quadratic scaling. The rotary position encoding adapted for SEM coordinates is a minor but necessary addition, since turbulence simulation is highly position-sensitive unlike vision Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30× speedup over 256³ DNS while matching its accuracy on 3D isotropic turbulence is practically significant for enabling long-time ensemble rollouts and real-time simulation on a single GPU. The domain-generalization result—extending to 4× larger domains via attention masking without retraining—suggests the model learns domain-independent flow dynamics, a capability absent in prior neural operators. However, the work is limited to periodic domains at moderate Reynolds numbers (Re≈94 in 3D, Re≈800 in 2D), with no wall-bounded flows, complex geometries, or extreme-scale turbulence. Open questions include whether the SEM tokenization and dual-stream design scale to higher Reynolds numbers where the inertial range spans more decades, and whether the approach extends to non-periodic or irregular domains without the structured element tiling assumption.
