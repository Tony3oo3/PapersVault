## Problem

Existing feature attribution methods for sequential models assume a fixed feature ordering, conflating the effects of feature values with their positions within the input sequence. Permuting feature order while keeping values unchanged can significantly alter model predictions (e.g., in EHR-based length-of-stay prediction), yet no prior method explicitly disentangles these two effects for local, sample-level attribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single input sample x as a sequence of d features (tokens) and a trained black-box model f, then produces a d×d OrdShap matrix γ where element γ_{i,ℓ} quantifies the importance of feature i conditioned on being permuted to position ℓ. Two summary vectors are derived: OrdShap-VI (row averages, capturing value importance independent of position) and OrdShap-PI (linear regression slopes of γ_{i,ℓ} over ℓ, capturing the direction and magnitude of positional sensitivity). The full matrix is only available via the sampling algorithm; the faster least-squares algorithm outputs only the two summary vectors.

## Method

OrdShap introduces a novel characteristic function ω̃(S, σ) that first applies a full-sequence permutation σ to the input, then ablates features in N\S, resolving the non-uniformity problem that arises when permuting only within subsets. The OrdShap value γ_{i,ℓ} is defined as a Shapley-style marginal contribution averaged over all subsets S containing i and all permutations σ placing i at position ℓ, yielding a position-conditioned attribution. Theoretically, averaging γ_{i,ℓ} over positions recovers the Sanchez-Bergantiños value (Theorem 1), and the least-squares formulation's α coefficients exactly recover SB values under a specific subset-size weighting (Theorem 2). Two approximation algorithms are provided: a permutation-sampling method for the full d×d matrix, and a more efficient least-squares method that decomposes into solving OrdShap-VI via KernelSHAP and OrdShap-PI via weighted linear regression, reducing complexity from O(d!·2^d·δ_f) to O(KL·δ_f + d²KL + d³).

## Results

On Inclusion AUC (higher is better), OrdShap-VI achieves the best score across all five evaluation settings: 0.913 on EICU-LOS, 0.809 on EICU-Mort, 0.899 on MIMICIII-LOS, 0.862 on MIMICIII-Mort, and 0.866 on IMDB, outperforming the next-best method by 0.003–0.007 in each case. On Exclusion AUC (lower is better), OrdShap-VI is best on four of five settings (e.g., 0.573 on EICU-LOS vs. 0.614 for DeepLIFT) and second-best on IMDB (0.779 vs. 0.766 for KernelSHAP). For Position Importance, permuting features according to OrdShap-PI attributions consistently increases or maintains the model's predicted-class probability, whereas permuting according to any competing method either has no effect or degrades the prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrdShap-VI outperforms all five baselines (DeepLIFT, Integrated Gradients, KernelSHAP, LIME, LOCO) on Inclusion AUC across every dataset, with margins of 0.003–0.007 over the closest competitor. On Exclusion AUC, OrdShap-VI is best on four of five settings, with the largest gap on EICU-LOS (0.573 vs. 0.614 for DeepLIFT, a 6.6% relative improvement); it trails KernelSHAP only on IMDB (0.779 vs. 0.766), which the authors attribute to the DistilBERT model being largely insensitive to sentence order on that task. For the Position Importance evaluation (Fig. 4), OrdShap-PI is the only method whose permutation strategy consistently increases the predicted-class probability; all competing methods either leave the prediction unchanged (EICU-LOS) or degrade it (all other settings). On the synthetic disentanglement experiment, OrdShap-VI and OrdShap-PI fully separate seven tokens by their true value and position importance, whereas all five baselines produce entangled attributions that cannot distinguish tokens sharing the same value but differing in position. Execution time is substantially higher for OrdShap (5.5–54.6 s per sample with the LS algorithm) compared to gradient-based methods (0.01–0.22 s), reflecting the cost of evaluating the model over multiple permutations.

## Relation to Prior Work

OrdShap extends the Shapley value framework [65] to sequential data by incorporating feature permutations, establishing a novel theoretical link to the Sanchez-Bergantiños value [63] for generalized characteristic functions — a game-theoretic object previously unexplored in the XAI literature. Practically, it builds on KernelSHAP's least-squares machinery [43] and permutation sampling [74] to make the computation tractable. The paper positions itself against existing sequential-model explainers (TransSHAP, TimeSHAP, MSP) that handle temporal structure but do not explicitly attribute importance to feature position, and against PoSHAP [20], which is a global method averaging over positions rather than a local, per-sample disentanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In high-stakes sequential domains like EHR prediction, the timing of a clinical event can be as diagnostically relevant as its value, yet standard attributions silently conflate the two — potentially misleading clinicians about why a model flagged a patient. OrdShap's disentanglement (VI vs. PI) offers a more faithful explanation, and the case study showing that a routine test like Bedside Glucose receives high SHAP scores purely due to its late position is a concrete illustration of this failure mode. Key open questions include the substantial computational overhead (orders of magnitude slower than baselines), the lack of an axiomatic characterization for OrdShap-PI, and whether the efficiency gains from recent Shapley approximation literature can be extended to the permutation-augmented setting.
