## Problem

Most multi-view clustering methods assume strict one-to-one sample correspondence across views, an idealization that breaks down in real-world data where samples are often unsynchronized or misaligned. Existing alignment approaches rely on rigid index matching (e.g., Hungarian algorithm) that cannot model many-to-many semantic relationships, are not scalable to other clustering methods, and lack a principled way to select a reliable baseline view for alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes unaligned multi-view data partitioned into aligned (X_1^v) and unaligned (X_2^v) sample subsets per view, along with the number of clusters k and a trade-off hyperparameter λ. It outputs a unified cluster assignment via k-means on the consensus partition F*, and additionally produces a sample realignment matrix M^v for each view that maps unaligned samples to the baseline view — a byproduct that can be plugged into other MVC methods to improve their performance under misalignment without modifying their internal architecture.

## Method

SSA-MVC reformulates cross-view alignment as a soft, structure-preserving reconstruction problem rather than rigid index matching. First, the CLM algorithm selects the baseline view by scoring each view's structural consistency with ground-truth cluster labels. Second, for each view, structural representations W^v of unaligned samples are constructed via adaptive neighbor graph learning (similarity to aligned samples within the same view). Third, cross-view similarity graphs S^v are built by comparing these structural representations against the baseline view, and the resulting alignment is integrated into a late-fusion objective through an orthogonal realignment matrix M^v, a feature rotation R^v, and view-specific weights α_v. The full objective is solved by alternating SVD-based updates for F*, R^v, M^v and a closed-form Cauchy-inequality solution for α_v, with convergence guaranteed within a few iterations.

## Results

Under a 50% sample alignment ratio (ρ=50%), SSA-MVC achieves the best ACC across all eight datasets, with headline values of 83.52% on MSRCV, 96.55% on HW, 77.38% on EMNIST, and 70.63% on 100leaves. The method also leads on NMI (e.g., 92.09 on HW, 85.34 on 100leaves), ARI (92.49 on HW, 66.34 on MSRCV), and F1-score (93.24 on HW, 71.03 on MSRCV). The largest absolute ACC gains over the second-best baseline are +17.83% on EMNIST (vs. DCMVC at 59.55%) and +10.66% on MSRCV (vs. EEOMVC at 72.86%). TMSL encounters out-of-memory errors on EMNIST and Hdigit, while all other baselines are consistently outperformed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSA-MVC outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and every metric (ACC, NMI, ARI, F1) at ρ=50%. The most striking margins are on EMNIST, where SSA-MVC reaches 77.38% ACC versus DCMVC's 59.55% (+17.83 pp) and EEOMVC's 46.11%, and on MSRCV, where it scores 83.52% versus EEOMVC's 72.86% (+10.66 pp). Among methods specifically designed for non-aligned data, Vsc_mH (62.13% on 3sources) and OpVuC (57.40% on 3sources) are the closest competitors, yet SSA-MVC still leads by 2.31 and 7.04 pp respectively on that dataset. Deep-learning baselines (DealMVC, MVCAN, DCMVC) suffer the largest drops under misalignment—DealMVC falls to 7.69% ACC on 100leaves—while SSA-MVC maintains 70.63%. Notably, appending the Hungarian algorithm to baselines does not consistently help (e.g., EEOMVC+Hungarian drops from 58.18 to 52.73 on Yale), whereas SSA-MVC's learned soft alignment matrix can be reused to improve other methods (Table 5 shows DealMVC ACC rising from 7.69 to 12.42 on 100leaves after realignment). TMSL fails entirely (OOM) on the two largest datasets (EMNIST, Hdigit), highlighting a scalability limitation absent in SSA-MVC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three key threads: adaptive neighbor graph learning (Nie et al., KDD 2014) for constructing view-specific structural representations, the late-fusion multi-view clustering framework (Wang et al., IJCAI 2019) for partition integration, and the CLM criterion (Jeon et al., TPAMI 2025) for selecting a reliable baseline view. It departs from prior alignment-based MVC methods (Huang et al., Yang et al., Ren et al.) by replacing rigid one-to-one Hungarian matching with a soft, structure-preserving similarity reconstruction, and by decoupling the alignment module from the clustering objective so the learned alignment matrix can be plugged into other MVC methods. Unlike Vsc_mH and OpVuC, which mine alignment directly from raw features via hard pairwise matching, SSA-MVC operates on learned structural representations of unaligned samples relative to aligned ones within each view.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant: real-world multi-view data (video, sensor arrays, multi-source text) is frequently unsynchronized, and the paper demonstrates that naively applying Hungarian alignment to existing MVC methods can actually degrade performance, highlighting a non-trivial pitfall. The decoupled alignment matrix offers a drop-in enhancement for any late-fusion MVC method, broadening the method's utility beyond its own pipeline. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the method requires at least some aligned samples to bootstrap the structural representations, leaving the fully unaligned scenario open for future work. The scalability to larger datasets remains to be tested, as the largest evaluated dataset (EMNIST, 10K samples) already causes OOM for several tensor-based baselines.
