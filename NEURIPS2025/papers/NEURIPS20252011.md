## Problem

Existing robust matrix completion (RMC) methods suffer from three key limitations: they require a large oversampling ratio of observed entries, they fail when the input rank is overparameterized by even one dimension, and they cannot recover matrices with condition numbers as modest as 5. Additionally, the theoretical recovery guarantees for factorization-based RMC methods are weaker than those for vanilla matrix completion, with corruption-rate bounds that degrade sharply with rank and condition number.

## Input / Output

RGNMR operates on a factorized representation (U ∈ R^{n1×r}, V ∈ R^{n2×r}) rather than the full n1×n2 matrix, reducing the number of optimization variables to (n1+n2)r. Beyond the observed entries and rank, the method requires an upper bound k on the number of corrupted entries, which the paper shows can be estimated via a binary search scheme that exploits the convergence behavior of the outlier set Λ_t. The modified theoretical variant additionally requires the incoherence parameter μ and a per-row/column outlier fraction bound α, and constrains updates to a shrinking neighborhood of the current iterate with bounded row norms.

## Method

RGNMR alternates two steps per iteration: (i) a Gauss-Newton linearization that updates the factor matrices (U,V) by solving a rank-deficient least-squares problem on the entries not flagged as outliers, selecting the minimum-norm solution; and (ii) a re-estimation of the corrupted-entry set Λ by selecting the k entries with the largest residual magnitude in P_Ω(X − UVᵀ). The key insight is that GNMR's second-order convergence is preserved even when only a single Gauss-Newton step is taken per outlier-re-estimation cycle, avoiding redundant iterations while maintaining fast convergence. For theoretical guarantees, a modified variant replaces global k-largest-residual removal with a row-wise and column-wise thresholding operator T_{γα} that removes at most a γα-fraction of entries per row/column, and constrains each update to a δ/4^{t+1} neighborhood with bounded row norms. The initialization uses a spectral b-SVD decomposition of the outlier-removed, rescaled observation matrix followed by row-norm clipping, and the paper proves linear convergence (‖L_t − L*‖_F ≤ σ_r*/(2^t · c_e·√κ)) under the weakest known assumptions for factorization-based RMC methods.

## Results

For a 3200×400 matrix of rank r=5 with 5% corruption, RGNMR achieves near-perfect recovery (rel-RMSE < 10⁻³) at oversampling ratios where all six compared RMC methods fail. RGNMR is the only method that succeeds under rank overparameterization (input rank r+1 through r+5) and at condition numbers up to κ=10, whereas all methods except HUB fail at κ>5. The binary-search variant (RGNMR-BS), which estimates the number of corrupted entries without knowing k*, performs comparably to RGNMR given the true count. Theoretically, RGNMR matches the best-known sample complexity for factorization-based methods (μr log n₁) while allowing a corruption rate of 1/(rμκ), improving upon prior factorization-based guarantees by a factor of O(√r).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGNMR outperforms all six compared RMC methods—AOP, RMC, RPCA-GD, RRMC, HUB, and HOAT—on three critical dimensions: it requires significantly fewer observed entries to succeed, it is unaffected by rank overparameterization (input rank r+1 through r+5), and it recovers matrices with condition numbers up to κ=10 where all others (except HUB) fail. Among the baselines, HUB is the closest competitor on condition number robustness but still fails under overparameterization and requires more samples. GNMR (Zilber & Nadler, 2022), the non-robust predecessor that RGNMR builds upon, fails completely in the presence of even a small fraction of corrupted entries, highlighting the necessity of the outlier-removal step. The binary-search variant RGNMR-BS, which estimates k* rather than being given it, matches the performance of RGNMR with the true count, validating the proposed estimation scheme. In the real-data Hall video background extraction task (5% observed entries, rank r=1), RGNMR successfully recovers the background while competing methods produce visibly inferior results.

## Relation to Prior Work

RGNMR is a direct robust extension of GNMR (Zilber and Nadler, 2022), a Gauss-Newton iterative method for vanilla low-rank matrix recovery, by adding an iterative outlier-removal step based on residual thresholding. For initialization, it adapts the spectral b-SVD scheme from Yi et al. (2016) with a row-norm clipping procedure. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of factorization-based RMC methods (which enforce rank-r structure via UV^T parameterization) and robust loss approaches, but departs from both by using a second-order Gauss-Newton linearization rather than gradient descent or robust norms, and by explicitly removing suspected outliers rather than attenuating them. The binary search scheme for estimating the number of corrupted entries k* is a novel practical contribution not present in prior RMC methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RGNMR addresses three concrete failure modes of existing RMC methods—insufficient observed entries, rank overparameterization, and moderate ill-conditioning (κ > 5)—that collectively limit the practical applicability of current algorithms. The theoretical guarantees are the weakest known for factorization-based RMC, matching the sample complexity of the best non-robust method (Zheng and Lafferty, 2016) even in the presence of corruption and improving the allowed corruption rate by O(√r) over prior factorization-based results. Open questions include whether the theoretical dependence on κ can be removed (empirically performance is κ-independent), whether overparameterization can be covered theoretically, and how to reduce the quadratic runtime scaling that limits applicability to very large matrices.
