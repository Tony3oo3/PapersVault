## Problem

Multi-agent world modeling faces a fundamental tradeoff: centralized schemes that capture full joint dynamics incur computational costs scaling exponentially with the number of agents, while decentralized approaches suffer from structural misalignment with the global MDP and lack explicit supervision for their communication/aggregation modules. The paper asks whether a centralized modeling scheme can maintain global consistency with the true state transition P(s_{t+1}|s_t, a_t^{1:n}) while keeping complexity linear in the number of agents.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step, the model receives the current global state s_t, a single agent's action a_t^k (not the full joint action), a noise level σ(τ) sampled from a continuous-time log-normal scheduler, and a fixed window of past k global states and joint actions for temporal context. The output is the predicted clean next global state s_{t+1}, from which a separate Transformer-based head predicts reward and termination, and a VQ-VAE decodes joint observations for decentralized actor execution. The key structural constraint is permutation invariance: the optimization objective (Eq. 9) averages over all n! agent orderings, so the predicted next state is independent of which agent's action is revealed at which denoising step.

## Method

DIMA reformulates multi-agent dynamics prediction as a conditional denoising process where each of n denoising steps incorporates exactly one agent's action, progressively reducing uncertainty about the next state—mirroring the reverse diffusion process. Under a formal assumption (Assumption 1) that the global transition factorizes along this sequential agent-revelation path, the paper derives an ELBO (Theorem 2) whose denoising-matching term reduces to a per-agent L2 loss (Eq. 9) with noise levels sampled independently per step from the EDM scheduler. The denoiser D_θ is a neural network reparameterized with EDM preconditioners (c_skip, c_out, c_in, c_noise), and the forward noising process is proven condition-independent, allowing arbitrary noise-level sampling. This design compresses the modeling problem from a |S|×|A|^n×|S| joint space to a |S|×|A|×|S| per-step space, yielding linear complexity in the number of agents. For policy learning, DIMA is integrated into a learning-in-imagination loop using MAPPO with λ-returns, where the centralized critic exploits oracle global states (CTDE) while decentralized actors operate on VQ-VAE-decoded local observations.

## Results

DIMA achieves state-of-the-art final episode returns across all 11 evaluated tasks on MAMuJoCo (1M steps) and Bi-DexHands (300K steps). On MAMuJoCo, DIMA leads by large margins, e.g., 4881±756 on Ant-2x4 vs. 4471±553 (MARIE) and 1314±756 (MAMBA), and 6370±121 on HalfCheetah-2x3 vs. 4045±275 (MARIE). On Bi-DexHands, DIMA achieves 259.9±4.1 on BottleCap, 367.1±19.4 on DoorOpenOutward, and 290.4±29.0 on DoorOpenInward, outperforming the best model-free baseline (HASAC) by 23–210 points. In model analysis, DIMA also exhibits the lowest accumulated observation error (2.42±0.93 at H=15, 4.32±1.44 at H=25) and reward error (15.01±9.88 at H=15) on Ant-2x4, demonstrating superior long-horizon prediction stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIMA outperforms all five baselines on every evaluated task. Against the model-based baselines, the gap is most dramatic: on Ant-4x2, DIMA (4766) surpasses MARIE (1173) by over 4× and MAMBA (1618) by ~3×; on HalfCheetah-6x1, DIMA (5643) exceeds MARIE (1738) by 225%. On Bi-DexHands, DIMA leads MAMBA by 56–210 points across tasks, while MARIE is not reported due to OOM issues. Against model-free baselines, DIMA consistently dominates: on MAMuJoCo it beats the best model-free method (HAPPO or HASAC) by 1,000–4,000+ return points, and on Bi-DexHands it outperforms HASAC (the strongest model-free baseline) by 23–210 points. Notably, MARIE and MAMBA sometimes perform comparably to or worse than sample-inefficient on-policy methods like HAPPO and MAPPO (e.g., HalfCheetah-3x2: MARIE 2380 vs. HAPPO 3402), whereas DIMA maintains a clear lead over all methods in every setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIMA builds on the EDM diffusion framework (Karras et al., 2022) and score-based generative modeling (Song et al., 2020), repurposing the conditional denoising machinery from image generation to multi-agent state transition prediction. It departs from prior multi-agent world models (MAMBA, MARIE) that model local observation dynamics and rely on aggregation modules, and from diffusion-based MARL methods (MADiff, MADiTS, DoF) that use diffusion for trajectory-level generation rather than step-wise dynamics. The key conceptual departure is the observation that sequentially revealing each agent's action mirrors the reverse diffusion process, enabling a centralized model with linear (rather than exponential) complexity in the number of agents, inspired by DIAMOND's single-agent diffusion world model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIMA establishes that a centralized multi-agent world model can achieve linear modeling complexity while maintaining global MDP consistency, resolving a fundamental tradeoff that has constrained prior approaches. The finding that sequential agent modeling matches joint modeling in raw prediction accuracy while providing substantial sample-efficiency gains in low-data regimes (and persistent advantages on harder tasks) validates the formulation's practical value. The paper acknowledges scalability challenges for systems with hundreds of agents, pointing to grouping techniques as a future direction. An open question is whether the sequential denoising perspective generalizes to partially observable or adversarial multi-agent settings beyond the fully cooperative Dec-POMDP formulation.
