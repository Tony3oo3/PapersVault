## Problem

Interpretable attention mechanisms (e.g., MSSA from MCR²) retain quadratic complexity in sequence length, while efficient alternatives (linear attention, channel attention) lack principled interpretability. Prior work addresses these two goals in isolation, leaving the design of efficient attention largely heuristic and the mathematical connections between different attention mechanisms unexplored. This paper asks whether a single optimization objective can simultaneously yield interpretability and linear-time efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes patchified RGB images (e.g., 224×224 with 16×16 patches yielding N=196 tokens, or 512×512 for segmentation) and produces class labels or per-pixel segmentation masks. Internally, the N input tokens are projected onto K orthogonal p-dimensional subspaces, from which m representatives (m=p=d/K, typically 64) are extracted via cross-attention; the attention output is a gradient step that contracts these representatives and broadcasts the result back to all N tokens, yielding a token-mixing operation with O(Nmd) cost instead of O(N²d).

## Method

CBSA (Contract-and-Broadcast Self-Attention) is derived by unrolling one gradient descent step of a relaxed MCR² objective in which the compression term is evaluated on a small set of m representatives rather than all N tokens, with inequality constraints bounding the coding-rate gap between representatives and full tokens. The forward pass has three stages: (1) representative initialization via average pooling and extraction via cross-attention (query=initial reps, key=value=projected tokens), (2) representative contraction via self-attention among the m representatives, and (3) contraction broadcast back to all N tokens by transposing the extraction attention matrix. By varying the representative set—input tokens themselves (m=N, recovering softmax/MSSA), input-dependent orthogonal directions from SVD (recovering linear attention), or fixed basis vectors (recovering channel attention/TSSA)—CBSA provides a unified formula that attributes differences among attention mechanisms to their distinct compression patterns. The full CBT model stacks CBSA with an ISTA feed-forward module, both derived via algorithm unrolling, and is trained end-to-end with a learnable step size κ.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, CBT-Small achieves 71.4% top-1 accuracy using only 6.7M parameters and 4.0G FLOPs, matching ViT-Small (72.4%) with 30% fewer parameters and 40% fewer FLOPs. CBT-Base (73.4%) and CBT-Large (74.4%) outperform both CRATE-Base (70.8%) and CRATE-Large (71.3%) by 2.6% and 3.1% respectively. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, the CBT decoder improves upon Segmenter by 1.5% mIoU while using only 20% of the FLOPs and 0.06% of the pairwise similarities in the decoder. Throughput at 512×512 resolution shows CBT-Small training at 205 images/sec versus 94 for ViT-Small (2.2× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViT-Small (softmax attention), CBT-Small achieves 71.4% vs 72.4% top-1 on ImageNet-1K (−1.0%) but with 30% fewer parameters and 40% fewer FLOPs; CBT-Base (73.4%) surpasses ViT-Small by 1.0%. Against CRATE (MSSA/softmax attention), CBT-Base and CBT-Large outperform CRATE-Base and CRATE-Large by 2.6% and 3.1% respectively while using fewer FLOPs. In the fair comparison (Table 4) where all models share identical architecture, CBSA-Small (71.4%) beats TSSA-Small (68.5%) by 2.9% and Agent-Small (71.8%) by 0.4%, while computing fewer pairwise similarities than MSSA-Small (1.1M vs 2.8M). On ADE20K segmentation, the CBT decoder outperforms both DEPICT (white-box, MSSA-based) and Segmenter (black-box, MHSA-based), improving over Segmenter by 1.5% mIoU with only 20% of decoder FLOPs. For zero-shot segmentation on PASCAL VOC12, the hybrid CRATE+CBT model achieves the best foreground IoU (0.68) and boundary IoU (0.18), outperforming CRATE-S (0.65/0.17), CBT-S (0.51/0.15), and ToST-S (0.42/0.12).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MCR² (Maximal Coding Rate Reduction) line of work, which previously yielded interpretable but quadratic-complexity softmax attention (MSSA/CRATE) and linear-complexity but degenerate channel attention (TSSA/ToST), by introducing a 'representatives' mechanism that compresses all tokens by contracting a few. The derivation technique is algorithm unrolling, inherited from Gregor & LeCun's sparse coding unrolling and applied to attention by Yu et al. (2023). The key conceptual departure is treating interpretability and efficiency as jointly derivable from a single optimization objective rather than as separate design axes, and showing that softmax, linear, and channel attention are all special cases of the resulting CBSA operator distinguished only by the number and structure of representatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified formula connecting softmax, linear, and channel attention through a single compression objective provides a principled lens for understanding the trade-off between expressive capacity and efficiency, potentially guiding the design of future attention variants. Practically, CBT-S matches ViT-S accuracy with 30% fewer parameters and 40% fewer FLOPs, and the robustness to parameter perturbation offers a tangible advantage over black-box attention. However, the framework is validated only on visual tasks, the over-parameterization of the projection-back step compromises theoretical rigor, and the authors acknowledge that deeper mathematical connections to the broader family of efficient attention mechanisms (e.g., Performer, BigBird) remain unexplored.
