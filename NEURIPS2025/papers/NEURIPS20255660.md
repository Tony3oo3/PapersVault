## Problem

Existing video generation paradigms face a fundamental trade-off: diffusion models produce high-fidelity clips but require dozens of sequential denoising steps and cannot extend beyond fixed durations, while autoregressive next-token models support streaming generation but suffer from poor visual fidelity and prohibitive inference latency (tens of thousands of steps). No prior discrete autoregressive model has achieved industrial-grade 720p video quality at practical speeds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255660/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt and optionally a reference image (for zero-shot I2V) or a prior video clip (for extrapolation), producing discrete token sequences that are decoded into 720p images or 5-second 720p videos at 16 fps (80 frames per clip). The video is structured as a first-frame image pyramid followed by sequential clip pyramids, each with K spatial scales growing from (1,1) to full resolution while keeping temporal extent fixed at T frames. The tokenizer uses a 4×16×16 compression rate with 64-dim latents, employing vocabulary sizes of 2^16 for early small scales and 2^64 for later large scales.

## Method

InfinityStar introduces a spacetime pyramid architecture where a video is decomposed into a single image pyramid (first frame, encoding static appearance) and a sequence of clip pyramids (each a 3D volume with K scales growing only spatially), decoupling appearance from motion and enabling unified T2I/T2V/I2V/extrapolation in one model. The discrete tokenizer inherits architecture and pretrained weights from the continuous Wan 2.1 VAE, inserting a parameter-free binary spherical quantizer (BSQ) between encoder and decoder, and applies Stochastic Quantizer Depth during training to force balanced information distribution across scales. The 8B-parameter VAR Transformer employs three key modifications: Semantic Scale Repetition (repeating the first 12 semantic scales 3× to refine coarse predictions), Spacetime RoPE for 3D positional encoding, and Spacetime Sparse Attention that attends only to the largest scale of the preceding clip for temporal consistency, reducing attention cost by 1.5× while improving quality over full attention. Training proceeds in four progressive stages (T2I pre-training, then T2V fine-tuning at 192p→480p→720p) on 130M images and 16M videos, with the scale schedule extended at each resolution to preserve learned representations.

## Results

On VBench (the primary video generation benchmark), InfinityStar achieves an overall score of 83.74, surpassing HunyuanVideo (83.24) and all autoregressive baselines, though trailing Wan 2.1 (84.70). On the text-to-image side, InfinityStar-T2I scores 0.79 on GenEval and 86.55 on DPG, exceeding Infinity (0.73 / 83.46) and FLUX-schnell (0.71 / 84.8). In terms of inference speed, InfinityStar generates a 5-second 720p video in 58 seconds on a single GPU, achieving a 32× speedup over Wan 2.1 (1864 s) and a 6× speedup over Nova (354 s at 480p).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VBench, InfinityStar (83.74) outperforms all autoregressive baselines by a wide margin—Nova (80.12) by 3.62 points and Emu3 (80.96) by 2.78 points—and also surpasses diffusion models CogVideoX-5B (81.61), OpenSora V1.2 (79.23), Show-1 (78.93), and HunyuanVideo (83.24). The only model that beats InfinityStar on VBench overall is Wan 2.1 (84.70), which has a larger parameter count (14B vs. 8B) and uses a continuous diffusion paradigm. On the text-to-image benchmarks, InfinityStar-T2I leads all autoregressive models on GenEval (0.79 vs. Infinity's 0.73 and Emu3's 0.66) and DPG (86.55 vs. Infinity's 83.46), and also exceeds FLUX-schnell (84.8) and FLUX-dev (84.0) on DPG. In inference latency, InfinityStar is 32× faster than Wan 2.1 (58 s vs. 1864 s for 5 s 720p video) and 6× faster than Nova (58 s vs. 354 s), while producing higher-resolution output. The paper also demonstrates zero-shot image-to-video and video extrapolation capabilities without any task-specific fine-tuning, a flexibility not shared by the diffusion baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the next-scale prediction paradigm of VAR [23] and Infinity [12] from spatial-only image generation to the temporal dimension, introducing a 'spacetime pyramid' that decomposes video into an image pyramid (appearance) followed by sequential clip pyramids (motion). The discrete tokenizer inherits architecture and weights from the continuous Wan 2.1 video VAE [26] via binary spherical quantization [38], departing from prior discrete video tokenizers that were trained from scratch or from image-only pretrained weights. Key architectural departures from Infinity include stochastic quantizer depth to balance information across scales, semantic scale repetition to refine early semantic tokens, and spacetime sparse attention to handle long video contexts efficiently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InfinityStar demonstrates that a purely discrete autoregressive model can produce industrial-grade 720p video at 32× the speed of leading diffusion models while matching or slightly exceeding their VBench scores, challenging the assumption that diffusion is necessary for high-fidelity video synthesis. The unified spacetime pyramid design naturally supports T2I, T2V, I2V, and video extrapolation in a single model, simplifying deployment. Open questions include whether the quality gap in high-motion scenes (acknowledged trade-off between detail and motion fidelity) can be closed at larger scale, and whether the paradigm can be pushed to longer durations and higher resolutions as compute budgets grow.
