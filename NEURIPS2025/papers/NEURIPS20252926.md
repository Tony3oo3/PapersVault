## Problem

While in-context learning and causal reasoning have each been studied for language models, whether Transformers can perform in-context counterfactual reasoning—inferring unobserved latent noise from factual observations and predicting outcomes under hypothetical interventions—remains unexplored. The paper also lacks theoretical identifiability guarantees for counterfactuals in the exchangeable (mixture-of-distributions) setting that naturally arises from pre-training on diverse corpora.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252926/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a prompt of n in-context (x, y) pairs drawn from a linear regression SCM parameterized by a latent θ, followed by an index token z (selecting which factual pair to base the counterfactual on) and a counterfactual query x^CF. The model must output y^CF = βx^CF + u_y, where u_y is the noise abduced from the z-th factual pair. Sequences use E=5-dimensional embeddings, variable n ∈ {2,…,50} to prevent positional shortcuts, and the index token z is randomized per sequence to avoid positional-encoding-only solutions.

## Method

Theoretically, Lemma 1 reduces counterfactual reasoning to a transformation h(f(x^CF), f(x), y) operating only on observed values, covering additive, multiplicative, and exponential noise models; Theorem 1 then establishes counterfactual identifiability under exchangeability by extending bijective generation mechanism results to the conditional-independence setting. Empirically, the authors train GPT-2, LSTM, GRU, and Elman RNN architectures on synthetic data and show that self-attention (not MLP) and model depth are necessary for counterfactual reasoning, while pre-training data diversity (measured by effective support size) drives out-of-distribution generalization. Mechanistic analysis reveals a designated noise abduction head at layer 7 and linear encoding of the latent θ in the residual stream from layer 2 onward. The framework is extended to Itô SDE dynamics (Lotka-Volterra predator-prey), where the model generates full counterfactual trajectories by copying Brownian noise across the sequence, providing preliminary evidence for counterfactual story generation.

## Results

On the linear regression counterfactual task, GPT-2, LSTM, and GRU achieve comparable in-context MSE, while the Elman RNN shows significantly higher error for prompts with more than 14 in-context examples. On the Lotka-Volterra SDE task (20 time points), the Full GPT-2 Transformer achieves MSE ≈ 0.0025, substantially outperforming the Elman RNN, LSTM, and GRU which all remain above 0.1. The trained GPT-2 model eventually surpasses the estimated optimal OLS MSE (0.4739–0.6132 for 30–50 in-context examples), indicating emergent counterfactual reasoning beyond simple regression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the GPT-2 Transformer against three RNN baselines (LSTM, GRU, Elman RNN) on two tasks. On the linear regression counterfactual task (Figure 2), GPT-2 achieves the lowest error and fastest convergence for small numbers of in-context examples, though the difference versus LSTM and GRU is not statistically significant; the Elman RNN degrades significantly beyond 14 in-context examples. On the more complex Lotka-Volterra SDE task (Figure 6a), GPT-2 dominates with MSE ≈ 0.0025 versus >0.1 for all three RNN architectures, a gap of over 40×. The paper also ablates within the Transformer family, showing that self-attention (vs. MLP-only) and model depth (8 layers, 1 head outperforms 1 layer, 8 heads) are both critical for counterfactual reasoning performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning literature (Garg et al., 2022; Akyürek et al., 2023) to the specific subtask of counterfactual prediction, which requires noise abduction in addition to function estimation. Theoretically, it builds on the causal de Finetti framework of Guo et al. (2024a,b) for exchangeable data and extends the counterfactual identifiability results for bijective generation mechanisms (Nasr-Esfahany et al., 2023) from the Markovian to the exchangeable setting. Mechanistically, it connects to the induction head literature (Olsson et al., 2022) by showing that self-attention's copying capability is repurposed for noise abduction, and to the Linear Representation Hypothesis (Park et al., 2024) via residual stream probing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical reduction (Lemma 1) showing that counterfactual reasoning over a broad class of invertible noise models is equivalent to a transformation on observed values, giving a principled explanation for why Transformers can perform this task. The empirical finding that data diversity (measured via effective support size) is necessary for OOD generalization, combined with the mechanistic identification of a dedicated noise abduction head, offers concrete design principles for building models with causal reasoning capabilities. Key limitations include the restriction to small GPT-2-scale models, synthetic bivariate data, and the absence of confounders; extending to natural language, larger models, and realistic multi-variable causal graphs remains open.
