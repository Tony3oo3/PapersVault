## Problem

Next-frame-prediction video diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off between forgetting (loss of temporal memory over long sequences) and drifting (error accumulation that degrades visual quality). Any mechanism that strengthens memory to reduce forgetting simultaneously accelerates error propagation, while any mechanism that interrupts error propagation weakens temporal dependencies and worsens forgetting. Additionally, naively encoding more history frames to combat forgetting causes intractable quadratic attention growth, making long-context video generation computationally prohibitive.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text prompt, an optional first-frame image, and a variable-length history of VAE-encoded latent frames (potentially thousands) at 480p resolution, producing next-frame sections of 1–9 latent frames per iteration. The key design constraint is that the total transformer context length remains bounded (converging to a fixed upper bound) regardless of how many history frames are provided, achieved through progressive per-frame compression. All operations occur in latent space, with per-frame context of ~1560 tokens for 480p frames in the base HunyuanVideo/Wan architecture.

## Method

FramePack assigns each history frame a context length that decays geometrically (by a factor λ, typically 2) according to its importance—measured by time proximity, feature similarity, or a hybrid—implemented via different 3D patchify kernel sizes in the DiT input layer, so the total context length converges to a fixed bound independent of input length. For drift prevention, the paper introduces three complementary strategies: (1) planned endpoints with adjusted sampling orders that establish bi-directional context by generating anchor frames first and filling gaps between them; (2) inverted sampling for image-to-video that iteratively refines toward the high-quality user input; and (3) history discretization via K-Means quantization of latent frames into a finite codebook, reducing the train-inference distribution gap that drives error accumulation. The entire framework is applied as LoRA finetuning on pretrained video diffusion models (HunyuanVideo, Wan), enabling batch sizes of ~64 on a single 8×A100 node and inference on 6–8 GB consumer GPUs.

## Results

On the main comparison (Table 2, HunyuanVideo base, 30-second videos), the two proposed FramePack variants achieve the top ELO scores: inverted anti-drifting sampling (ELO 1220) and vanilla sampling with discrete history K=256 (ELO 1224), both ranked 1st. The inverted anti-drifting method attains the best values on all four drifting metrics (ΔClarity 2.25%, ΔMotion 1.85%, ΔSemantic 2.68%, ΔAnatomy 8.58%), while the discrete-history variant achieves the highest Dynamic score (91.74%) and Semantic score (28.37%) among all methods. Both proposed methods outperform the strongest baseline (DiffusionForcing, ELO 1174) by 46–50 ELO points, well beyond the ±16 tie threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FramePack's inverted anti-drifting sampling (ELO 1220) and vanilla + discrete history (ELO 1224) both rank 1st, outperforming all four external baselines by 46–209 ELO points. Against DiffusionForcing (best variant ELO 1174), FramePack wins on every drifting metric (e.g., ΔClarity 2.25% vs. 3.55%) and on Clarity (71.15% vs. 67.41%), though DiffusionForcing retains a slight edge on Dynamic (91.08% vs. 89.29%). Against Anchor frames/StreamingT2V (ELO 1173), FramePack achieves substantially lower drift (ΔAnatomy 8.58% vs. 9.25%) and higher Clarity (71.15% vs. 69.58%), while StreamingT2V has a higher Motion score (99.96% vs. 99.45%). Against Causal attention/CausVid (ELO 1087), FramePack dominates across nearly all metrics, with Clarity 71.15% vs. 62.88% and ΔClarity drift 2.25% vs. 7.45%. Against HistoryGuidance (ELO 1152), FramePack wins on Clarity, Dynamic, and all drifting metrics, though HistoryGuidance scores slightly higher on Identity (83.42% vs. 82.11%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on pretrained video diffusion transformers (HunyuanVideo, Wan) and addresses the well-identified forgetting-drifting trade-off in next-frame prediction, a dilemma discussed by CausVid, DiffusionForcing, and Wang et al. Its key departure is FramePack: a structured progressive-compression scheme that assigns frame-wise context lengths via geometric decay based on time proximity or feature similarity, ensuring bounded transformer context regardless of history length. The anti-drifting components—endpoint planning, inverted sampling order, and K-Means history discretization—are positioned as complementary to existing noise-scheduling approaches (DiffusionForcing, RollingDiffusion) and guidance-based methods (HistoryGuidance), offering an alternative axis for interrupting error propagation without weakening temporal dependencies as aggressively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FramePack's bounded-context design makes long-horizon next-frame prediction practically feasible on consumer hardware (13B models on 6–8 GB GPUs), lowering the barrier for iterative video generation research. The anti-drifting sampling strategies (especially inverted generation and discrete history) are general and could be grafted onto other autoregressive video pipelines beyond the tested DiT backbones. Open questions include whether the forgetting-drifting trade-off can be further resolved at scale, how feature-similarity packing behaves in open-world (non-game) settings, and whether the discrete-history approach interacts favorably with longer training horizons or larger codebooks.
