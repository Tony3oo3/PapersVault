## Problem

Generative modeling of inherently discrete, non-negative data (e.g., symbolic music, image pixel counts) suffers from two persistent gaps: existing diffusion models either embed discrete data into continuous spaces (introducing discretization artifacts and modeling PDFs instead of PMFs) or rely on variational ELBO objectives that lack an exact relationship to true data likelihood. ItDPDM addresses both limitations jointly by operating natively in the discrete domain with a principled, non-variational likelihood objective.

## Input / Output

The model consumes non-negative discrete data directly—CIFAR-10 as 32×32 RGB images (pixel values shifted by ε=10⁻⁶ to satisfy Poisson channel constraints) and Lakh MIDI as 1024-length integer sequences with values 0–89 (rests, continuations, note pitches). It produces unconditional generated samples (images or music sequences) and, uniquely, a closed-form negative log-likelihood estimate via the I-MPRL identity, requiring no dequantization or post-processing discretization step. The Poisson noise channel is non-additive and non-source-separable, so the forward process starts from a zero-photon (black) state rather than white noise.

## Method

ItDPDM replaces the Gaussian noise channel with a Poisson channel where z_γ ~ P(γx), parameterized by SNR γ, and introduces the Poisson Reconstruction Loss (PRL) l(x, x̂) = x log(x/x̂) − x + x̂, a Bregman divergence whose optimal denoiser is the conditional expectation E[X|Z_γ]. The key theoretical result is the I-MPRL identity (d/dγ I(x; z_γ) = mprl(γ)), which yields an exact expression for NLL as the integral of the minimum PRL over all SNR levels, eliminating the ELBO approximation entirely. Training minimizes an importance-sampling quadrature of the PRL over log-SNR (sampled from a logistic distribution) using a neural denoiser (U-Net for images, DenseDDPM/ConvTransformer for music), while sampling reverses the Poisson process from zero in T discrete steps. The framework extends component-wise to multivariate settings and provides analytic tail bounds that restrict the integration range for computational efficiency.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ItDPDM (Poisson+PRL) achieves NLL of 0.18 (DDPM backbone) and 0.17 (IDDPM backbone), substantially outperforming Gaussian+MSE (0.44/0.48) and ITDiff (2.97/0.86). On Lakh MIDI, ItDPDM reduces NLL to 4.61×10⁻⁵ (total data) and 0.23 (without rests) versus 0.51 and 1.41 for Gaussian+MSE. On six synthetic discrete distributions, ItDPDM achieves the lowest Wasserstein-1 distance in all cases (e.g., 0.99 vs 3.76 for DDPM on PoissMix) and outperforms LTJ on 4 of 6 distributions. For generation quality, ItDPDM attains FID of 0.18 dB (second-best after DDPM's 0 dB) and SSIM of 0.91 on CIFAR-10, while on Lakh MIDI it achieves FAD 0.64, Consistency 0.94, and WD 2.14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPM [13], ItDPDM achieves substantially lower NLL on both CIFAR-10 (0.18 vs 0.44) and Lakh MIDI (4.61×10⁻⁵ vs 0.51), but DDPM retains a slight edge in FID (0 vs 0.18 dB) and SSIM (0.93 vs 0.91) on images. Against LTJ [20], ItDPDM wins on NLL, on 4 of 6 synthetic Wasserstein distances, and on most audio metrics (FAD 0.64 vs 0.66, C 0.94 vs 0.92, MSID 0.67 vs 0.71, WD 2.14 vs 2.23), while LTJ edges ahead on FID (0.30 vs 0.18 dB is actually ItDPDM winning here—correction: ItDPDM's 0.18 dB is better than LTJ's 0.30 dB). Against D3PM [17], ItDPDM clearly wins on image FID (0.18 vs 2.93 dB) and SSIM (0.91 vs 0.86), but D3PM achieves better audio metrics (FAD 0.61, C 0.98, MSID 0.59, WD 1.99). Against ITDiff [12], ItDPDM achieves dramatically lower NLL (0.17–0.18 vs 0.86–2.97), confirming that the Poisson+PRL formulation is more effective than fine-tuned Gaussian diffusion for discrete data. Overall, ItDPDM's key advantage is exact likelihood estimation (no ELBO approximation), which translates to consistently lower NLL, while its generation quality is competitive but not uniformly best across all domain-specific metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information-theoretic diffusion framework of ITDiff (Kong et al., 2023) from Gaussian channels to Poisson channels, replacing the I-MMSE identity with a novel I-MPRL identity that links mutual information to a minimum Poisson reconstruction loss (a Bregman divergence). It departs from variational discrete-state models like LTJ (Chen & Zhou, 2023) and D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) by eliminating the ELBO surrogate entirely, yielding an exact likelihood expression via thermodynamic integration. The Poisson noise channel itself draws on classical estimation theory (Atar & Weissman, 2010) and the Turing-Good-Robbins formula, repurposing them as the forward process of a generative model rather than a detection problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact likelihood relation (Eq. 17) removes the two-layer approximation (ELBO + denoiser suboptimality) that plagues all prior diffusion-based discrete models, offering a principled path to distribution-robust generative modeling of count data, symbolic music, and low-light imaging. However, the paper explicitly positions itself as a proof of concept: training is limited to 600 epochs (vs. 3600 for LTJ), architectures are not fully tuned, and generation quality (FID, FAD) trails stronger baselines. Open questions include whether the framework scales to conditional generation, out-of-distribution sampling, and high-dimensional settings like ImageNet, and whether the Poisson assumption is truly necessary or whether other exponential-family channels (e.g., negative binomial) could yield analogous exact identities.
