## Problem

Prior 4D reconstruction methods—optimization-based, geometry-based, and generative—each suffer from at least one of efficiency, generalization, or faithfulness limitations, and none support arbitrary unconstrained input views and timestamps. Existing Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs) are designed for static 3D objects, and extending them to general 4D reconstruction with sparse multi-views and missing timestamps remains an open problem. The paper asks whether scaling spatiotemporal pretraining can learn a generic space-time representation that reconstructs an object from a few views at some time points to any view at any time.

## Input / Output

The model takes V posed RGB images at arbitrary timestamps (e.g., 24 views over 24 frames, one per frame) along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics; each image is augmented with Plücker ray coordinates and a per-pixel timestamp map to form a 10-channel feature map before patchification. The output is a cloud of per-pixel 4D Gaussian primitives (20 parameters each: 3D RGB, 4D scale, two 4D rotation quaternions, opacity, and 4D space-time center) that can be rendered at any novel view-time combination via conditional 3DGS sampling. A 24-frame dynamic object is reconstructed in a single forward pass in under 1.5 seconds on one A100 GPU, with training resolutions of 128×128 and 256×256.

## Method

4D-LRM is a ~300M-parameter Transformer that patchifies temporally posed images (RGB + Plücker coordinates + timestamp) into tokens, processes them through standard Transformer blocks (PreLayerNorm, multi-head self-attention, MLP with residuals), and directly regresses per-pixel 4D Gaussian primitives via a single linear layer. The key representation is the unified 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) formulation, where each Gaussian has a 4D mean and 4D covariance parameterized by two isoclinic quaternions, enabling anisotropic deformation across both space and time; rendering at a query time t is done by sampling conditional 3DGS from the 4DGS via multivariate Gaussian conditioning (Eq. 2). Pixel-aligned Gaussians tie each primitive to an input ray, with depth predicted by a sigmoid-interpolated scalar between near/far bounds, while optional learnable free Gaussian tokens provide generative flexibility for sparse-view 4D generation. Training uses a curriculum (128×128 for 100K steps, then 256×256 for 20K steps) with MSE + perceptual loss on randomly sampled supervision views, and the Transformer can be initialized from GS-LRM weights for faster convergence.

## Results

On Consistent4D (256×256), 4D-LRM-Large with GS-LRM initialization achieves PSNR 32.18 / LPIPS 0.028 / SSIM 0.980 under Alternating Canonical Views and PSNR 32.15 / LPIPS 0.028 / SSIM 0.980 under Frame Interpolation, substantially outperforming GS-LRM (All in) at PSNR 21.61 and SoM at PSNR 25.59. Under the more challenging Random Input Views setup, 4D-LRM reaches PSNR 27.99 vs. 19.67 for GS-LRM (All in) and 17.64 for SoM. For 4D generation (Table 4), 4D-LRM-Free achieves FVD 677.58 and CLIP 0.94, matching or surpassing L4GM (FVD 691.87) and SV4D (FVD 677.68), while 4D-LRM-Free with ground-truth multi-view first frame reaches PSNR 26.12 / LPIPS 0.055 / FVD 674.59 / CLIP 0.96.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4D-LRM consistently outperforms all baselines across every camera setup. Against GS-LRM (the closest feed-forward 3D reconstruction baseline), 4D-LRM gains approximately 10.6 dB PSNR under Alternating Canonical Views (32.18 vs. 21.61 for GS-LRM All-in) and 8.3 dB under Random Input Views (27.99 vs. 19.67), demonstrating that joint spatiotemporal modeling far exceeds per-frame or naive multi-view 3D reconstruction. Against SoM (an optimization-based 4D method), 4D-LRM leads by 6.6 dB PSNR on Alternating Canonical Views and 10.4 dB on Random Input Views, where SoM's causal motion assumption breaks down. In the 4D generation setting (Table 4), 4D-LRM-Free matches SV4D on FVD (677.58 vs. 677.68) and surpasses L4GM (691.87) while achieving a lower LPIPS (0.117 vs. 0.120 for L4GM, 0.129 for SV4D); with ground-truth multi-view first-frame input, 4D-LRM-Free‡ achieves the best PSNR (26.12) and lowest LPIPS (0.055) among all generation baselines. The paper also shows that GS-LRM only becomes competitive with 4D-LRM when given 4 canonical views per frame (Table 3), whereas 4D-LRM maintains its advantage even with just 1 random view per frame (28.34 vs. 18.74 PSNR on Objaverse4D Test).

## Relation to Prior Work

4D-LRM sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: the unified space-time 4D Gaussian Splatting representation (Yang et al., ICLR 2024), which models dynamic objects as anisotropic 4D Gaussians with joint spatial-temporal covariance, and the feed-forward Large Reconstruction Model paradigm (GS-LRM, ECCV 2024), which uses a Transformer to regress pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians from sparse posed images. The key departure is combining these: a Transformer directly predicts per-pixel 4D Gaussian primitives from temporally posed image tokens, extending the LRM framework from static 3D to general 4D reconstruction. Prior 4D approaches were either per-scene optimization (requiring dense sampling), per-frame geometry estimation, or single-view generative models that sacrifice faithfulness for perceptual quality; 4D-LRM is the first to handle unconstrained view-time combinations in a single feed-forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that scaling spatiotemporal pretraining with a unified 4D representation yields a generalizable model that reconstructs dynamic objects from as few as one view per frame in under 1.5 seconds, opening a practical path to 4D asset generation and world modeling for robotics. The natural frame interpolation behavior (Gaussians reallocate to unseen timestamps) suggests the learned representation captures genuine temporal structure rather than memorizing discrete frames. Key limitations include object-level scope (not scenes), dependence on posed inputs, and performance degradation with very long sequences, pointing toward future work on long-context variants and test-time training.
