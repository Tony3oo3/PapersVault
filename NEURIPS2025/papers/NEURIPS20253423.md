## Problem

Existing Embodied Question Answering (EQA) benchmarks predominantly target household environments (kitchens, rooms) and evaluate only general perception with short phrasal answers, overlooking safety-critical reasoning, human-agent interactions, and industrial-specific hazards. This limits their utility for assessing whether embodied agents are ready for real-world industrial applications where safety awareness and causal reasoning are essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253423/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 76 egocentric episodic memory videos (1080P, 30 fps, MP4) captured by a virtual camera mounted on a ROS-based Carter robot navigating Isaac Sim warehouse scenes, paired with 1,344 text questions spanning six categories. Each question requires the agent to produce two structured outputs: a concise direct answer (a unique factual response) and a reasoning answer (a single sentence justifying the direct answer from visual evidence). Both outputs are scored by an LLM judge on a 1–5 correctness scale, normalized to a percentage via the LLM-Match formula.

## Method

IndustryEQA is built on the NVIDIA Isaac Sim platform, where four experts designed 60 small and 16 large warehouse layouts populated with industrial assets (forklifts, pallet racking, conveyor systems, robotic manipulators) and animated human workers positioned in OSHA-inspired hazardous or non-hazardous configurations. QA pairs are generated by Gemini 2.5 Pro under a safety-focused prompt requiring ≥50% safety-related questions, then refined by a multi-model VLLM pipeline (Gemini 2.5 Pro, o4-mini, o3) that transforms binary Yes/No questions into open-ended or multiple-choice formats, and finally filtered and reannotated by human safety experts. The evaluation framework compares three model families—blind LLMs, multi-frame VLLMs (30–40 sampled frames), and video VLLMs (full clips)—in a zero-shot setting, with robustness validated through ICC analysis across two LLM judges (GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-2.0-flash) and ablations on sampled frame density.

## Results

On IndustryEQA, the best-performing model Gemini-2.5-Pro (Video VLLM) achieves 77.31% Direct Score and 71.33% Reasoning Score on small warehouses, and 75.71%/66.30% on large warehouses. Among non-annotation models, o4-mini (Multi-Frame VLLM) leads with 69.67% Direct and 67.71% Reasoning on small warehouses, while Gemini-2.5-Flash (Video VLLM) reaches 66.76%/60.14%. Blind LLMs (text-only) score only 36–44% Direct and 27–33% Reasoning, confirming that visual input is indispensable. Human annotators achieve 72.67% Direct on small warehouses but only 51.39% Reasoning, falling below top VLLMs on the reasoning metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 11 baseline models across three categories. Blind LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash, DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-V3) all cluster around 36–44% Direct and 27–33% Reasoning, confirming that text-only models cannot adequately answer warehouse-specific questions without visual input. Among Multi-Frame VLLMs, o4-mini leads with 69.67% Direct and 67.71% Reasoning on small warehouses, outperforming GPT-4.1 (63.72%/56.16%), InternVL2.5-78B (60.17%/52.57%), and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (54.09%/45.75%) by 6–15 points. In the Video VLLM category, Gemini-2.5-Pro (the annotation model) achieves the highest scores overall (77.31% Direct, 71.33% Reasoning on small warehouses), followed by Gemini-2.5-Flash (66.76%/60.14%), while Gemini-2.0-Flash lags significantly on reasoning (38.34%). Notably, human annotators score 72.67% Direct on small warehouses—competitive with top VLLMs—but only 51.39% Reasoning, well below o4-mini (67.71%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (71.33%), suggesting that generating coherent multi-step justifications remains harder for humans under the paper's strict evaluation criteria than for the strongest video-capable models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Embodied Question Answering (EQA) task, originally defined by Das et al. (CVPR 2018) and later adapted to the episodic-memory setting by OpenEQA (CVPR 2024), from household environments to safety-critical industrial warehouses. It departs from all prior EQA benchmarks (EQA-v1, IQA, MP3D-EQA, ScanQA, RoboVQA, OpenEQA, CityEQA) by introducing OSHA-guided hazardous scenarios, human worker agents, and a dedicated safety evaluation axis (equipment safety and human safety) that no previous benchmark addresses. The LLM-based scoring methodology and open-vocabulary answer format are directly inherited from OpenEQA, while the simulation infrastructure (NVIDIA Isaac Sim with ROS-controlled robots) represents a shift away from the House3D, AI2-THOR, and Matterport3D platforms used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IndustryEQA fills a clear gap: no prior EQA benchmark evaluates safety-aware reasoning in industrial settings, yet this is where embodied agents face the highest-stakes deployment. The consistent finding that reasoning scores lag direct scores by 10–20 points across all models (including top-tier VLLMs like o4-mini and Gemini-2.5-Pro) highlights that current systems still struggle with the causal and spatial reasoning that safety-critical applications demand. Key limitations include reliance on simulation (limiting ecological validity), a single warehouse domain, absence of audio or dynamic event simulation, and the use of the annotation model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) as both data generator and top-performing evaluator, which raises self-bias concerns. Open questions include whether findings transfer to real industrial data, whether active (non-episodic) navigation changes the difficulty landscape, and whether safety-focused process reward models can close the reasoning gap.
