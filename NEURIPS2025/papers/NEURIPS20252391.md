## Problem

Existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods from blurry monocular videos are tailored to either defocus blur or motion blur, as the two blur types have fundamentally different formation processes. No prior method can simultaneously handle both blur types while producing high-quality sharp novel views, leaving a gap in unified blurry video reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252391/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a blurred monocular video (defocus or motion blur) along with camera poses, monocular depth estimates (Depth-Anything), 2D tracking points (TAPIR), and motion masks (SAM). It produces a sharp dynamic 3D Gaussian representation that renders novel views at 512×288 resolution at 65 FPS on an RTX 3090, alongside per-pixel blur kernels (9×9) and blur intensity maps. Training uses left-view blurred sequences and evaluates on right-view sharp sequences, with each scene requiring approximately 1 hour of optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified blur synthesis framework that models both defocus and motion blur as per-pixel kernel convolution, with a Blur Prediction Network (BP-Net) — a 4-layer CNN taking camera embeddings, scene features (rendered image, depth, motion mask), and pixel positional encodings — that jointly predicts per-pixel blur kernels and blur intensity. A blur-aware sparsity constraint links blur intensity to kernel center weight via a stop-gradient sigmoid, preventing unrealistic over-blurring of mildly blurred regions. Dynamic Gaussians are initialized from visible 2D tracking points using Shape-of-Motion's SE(3) motion bases, then densified via foreground remapping of dynamic regions reprojected from all observation frames back to the canonical space. The training schedule progressively introduces the blur model (iteration 3500), sparsity constraint (iteration 5500), and unseen-view supervision (every 5 iterations from iteration 3000), where unseen views are generated by interpolating along and perturbing perpendicular to the camera trajectory to mitigate monocular overfitting.

## Results

On the D2RF defocus blur dataset, the method achieves PSNR 29.39, SSIM 0.859, and LPIPS 0.078, outperforming all baselines. On the DyBluRF motion blur dataset, it reaches PSNR 27.01, SSIM 0.876, and LPIPS 0.056. The method uses 192.2M parameters, trains in approximately 1 hour on an NVIDIA RTX 3090, and renders at 65.143 FPS at 512×288 resolution. On the combined-blur datasets (D2RF-v2, DyBluRF-v2), it further achieves PSNR 30.26 (defocus) and 28.55 (motion), confirming its advantage when both blur types co-occur.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five primary baselines on both blur types across all three metrics. Against the closest competitor De4DGS [58] (motion-blur-specific), it gains +0.90 dB PSNR on defocus blur and +0.39 dB on motion blur while using far fewer parameters (192.2M vs. 754.6M) and training 20× faster (1 hr vs. 20 hrs). Against SoM [52] (the dynamic 3DGS backbone it builds on), improvements are +1.07 dB (defocus) and +0.80 dB (motion) in PSNR. Against blur-specific methods D2RF [32] and DyBluRF [47], gains are larger (+2.35/+3.34 dB and +3.15/+2.48 dB respectively), confirming that a unified blur-kernel approach surpasses methods tailored to a single blur type. Even when SoM is fed with deblurred images from the state-of-the-art video deblurring method BSSTNet [66], the proposed method still wins by +0.83 dB PSNR on defocus blur, demonstrating that joint 3D-consistent blur modeling is superior to a deblur-then-reconstruct pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Shape-of-Motion (SoM) for its dynamic Gaussian representation with SE(3) motion bases and on 3DGS for differentiable rasterization, but departs from the prior blurry-video reconstruction line by unifying defocus and motion blur under a single per-pixel blur kernel convolution. Prior methods (D2RF for defocus, DyBluRF/De4DGS for motion blur) each model only one blur type with task-specific rendering pipelines; here, a Blur Prediction Network with a blur-aware sparsity constraint replaces those specialized mechanisms, while dynamic Gaussian densification via foreground remapping and unseen-view supervision address the monocular ill-posedness that plagues all dynamic 3DGS variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first unified framework that handles both defocus and motion blur in dynamic 3DGS, this work removes a practical barrier for applications (AR/VR, 3D content creation) where real monocular footage inevitably contains mixed blur. The 65 FPS rendering and ~1-hour training make it deployable, and the fact that it outperforms pipelines that first deblur with a SOTA video deblurring model highlights the value of joint 3D-consistent optimization over sequential 2D-then-3D approaches. Open questions include whether the unified kernel model generalizes to more complex or spatially-varying blur (e.g., rolling shutter, lens aberrations), whether the method can be extended to real-world (non-synthetic) blurry videos, and how it scales to longer sequences or larger scenes given its per-scene optimization requirement.
