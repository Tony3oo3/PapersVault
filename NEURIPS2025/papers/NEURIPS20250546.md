## Problem

Existing fair federated learning methods either address only global or only local fairness, and predominantly handle binary classification, leaving the multi-class multi-group setting unexplored. Moreover, the non-decomposable, non-differentiable nature of group-fairness criteria makes it difficult to achieve a controllable, theoretically optimal accuracy-fairness trade-off with convergence guarantees. FedFACT tackles both gaps by characterizing the federated Bayes-optimal fair classifier under simultaneous global and local fairness constraints.

## Input / Output

Each client k holds a local dataset of feature vectors x, labels y, and sensitive attributes a; in the post-processing variant the classifier is attribute-aware (takes both x and a as input), while in-processing classifiers are attribute-blind. The method outputs a personalized classifier per client (an ensemble of a unified and a local model in in-processing, or a plug-in reweighting of Bayes scores in post-processing) along with tunable fairness bounds ξ^g and ξ^k that control the global and local disparity levels. Experiments span tabular datasets (Compas, Adult, ENEM) and RGB face images (CelebA) with 2–5 clients.

## Method

FedFACT characterizes the federated Bayes-optimal fair classifier as a linear combination of deterministic classifiers (Proposition 1), then derives two complementary algorithms. For in-processing, it reformulates the constrained problem as a saddle-point optimization and shows the inner minimization is equivalent to a personalized cost-sensitive classification with a calibration matrix M^{λ,μ} (Proposition 3); each client ensembles a unified model θ with a local model φ_k using an adaptive weight, while dual parameters λ (global) and μ_k (local) are updated via projected subgradient steps, yielding an O(1/√T) regret bound. For post-processing, it exploits the closed-form Bayes-optimal fair classifier under an η-continuity assumption (Theorem 5) and formulates a bi-level optimization: the outer level aggregates global dual updates λ across clients, while the inner level lets each client independently optimize its local dual μ_k, with a softmax relaxation of the max operator ensuring L-smoothness and enabling standard federated convergence. The two approaches are complementary rather than combinable, since the in-processing classifier is not designed to approximate the Bayes score function needed by post-processing.

## Results

On the four real-world datasets (Compas, Adult, CelebA, ENEM) under Dirichlet (γ=0.5) and heterogeneous partitions, FedFACT (post-processing) consistently achieves the best or near-best accuracy while simultaneously attaining the lowest global and local fairness disparities. For example, on Compas (γ=0.5), FedFACTl(Post) reaches 67.49% accuracy with global fairness 0.0315 and local fairness 0.0552, compared to FedAvg's 69.73% accuracy but 0.2766/0.3590 fairness. On Adult, FedFACTg(Post) achieves 82.83% accuracy with global fairness 0.0173 and local fairness 0.0276, outperforming all baselines on both fairness metrics while retaining competitive accuracy. The in-processing variants (FedFACTg&l(In)) also substantially reduce fairness disparities (e.g., 0.0014 global on Adult) at a modest accuracy cost (~0.5–1% vs. FedAvg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedFACT (post-processing) dominates all six baselines on both fairness metrics across all four datasets while maintaining accuracy within 1–2% of the unconstrained FedAvg. The strongest baseline, Cost (Duan et al. [25]), achieves the best fairness among prior methods (e.g., 0.0262 global on Adult) but still lags FedFACT by 3–5× on global fairness (0.0173 for FedFACTg(Post)) and by ~2× on local fairness. FairFed [31], FedFB [93], FCFL [18], and praFFed [85] all sacrifice 3–10% accuracy relative to FedAvg to achieve moderate fairness reductions, yet none simultaneously optimize both global and local fairness as effectively as FedFACT. On the heterogeneous partition, FedFACTg&l(Post) achieves the best overall balance (e.g., 64.41% Acc, 0.0398 global, 0.0680 local on Compas), confirming robustness to non-IID sensitive-attribute distributions. The in-processing variant FedFACTg&l(In) achieves the lowest global fairness on Adult (0.0014) but at a larger accuracy cost (~2.5% below FedAvg), illustrating the complementary trade-off between the two stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedFACT extends the Bayes-optimal fair classifier framework from centralized settings (Zeng et al. [91]) to the federated setting, and generalizes Logofair [95] from binary to multi-class classification. It builds on the theoretical insight from Hamman & Dutta [33] that global and local fairness diverge under statistical heterogeneity, and improves upon Duan et al. [25], who formulate the problem as a linear program but do not achieve the Bayes-optimal accuracy-fairness balance. The key methodological departure is reducing in-processing fair FL to personalized cost-sensitive learning and post-processing to bi-level optimization, both with convergence guarantees, rather than relying on surrogate fairness losses used by FairFed, FedFB, FCFL, and praFFed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to simultaneously enforce both global and local group fairness in multi-class federated classification with provable convergence to near-Bayes-optimal performance, filling a gap where prior methods addressed only one fairness level or only binary tasks. A notable limitation is that post-processing underperforms in multi-class settings due to limited local samples for fairness estimation, and the two approaches (in- vs. post-processing) cannot be combined. Open questions include scalability to hundreds of clients, extension to intersectional fairness criteria, and whether the cost-sensitive reformulation transfers to other non-decomposable constraint settings beyond group fairness.
