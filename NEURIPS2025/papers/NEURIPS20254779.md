## Problem

Cache-based test-time adaptation methods for vision-language models (e.g., TDA) store a fixed-size set of representative test samples to guide classification, but this instance-level memorization inevitably causes catastrophic forgetting when older samples are evicted. The finite cache capacity limits how much of the evolving test distribution can be captured, constraining adaptation quality and causing performance to degrade over long test streams.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254779/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of test image embeddings (produced by a frozen CLIP image encoder, batch size 1) and pre-computed zero-shot text prompt weights (one per class, e.g., 'a photo of {class}'). It produces posterior class probabilities by fusing the zero-shot softmax logits with a GDA-based test-time classifier whose weight λ = min(ρc, η) grows with the number of samples seen, so early predictions lean heavily on the zero-shot prior while later predictions rely more on the estimated distribution. No ground-truth labels are required at test time; zero-shot prediction probabilities serve as soft assignments for distribution estimation.

## Method

Dota replaces discrete sample caching with continuous per-class Gaussian distribution estimation (mean vector and covariance matrix) in the CLIP embedding space. Using an EM framework where zero-shot probabilities act as soft class assignments, it updates each class's parameters online via a regret-minimizing streaming rule (Dasgupta & Hsu), requiring only matrix additions and a single shared-covariance inversion per step—no gradient backpropagation. Classification then proceeds through Bayes' theorem with the GDA discriminant function f_k(x) = -½(x-μ_k)ᵀΣ_k⁻¹(x-μ_k) - ½log|Σ_k|, and the final prediction is an additive logit-level fusion of the zero-shot score and the test-time discriminant score, with the test-time weight ramping up as more samples accumulate. Shrinkage regularization (ε=10⁻⁴) on the precision matrix and a shared-covariance approximation across classes keep the per-sample computation to O(d²) rather than O(Kd²), yielding inference speed comparable to TDA but with 20–60× speedup over gradient-based prompt-tuning methods.

## Results

On the cross-domain generalization benchmark (10 datasets), Dota achieves 69.71% average top-1 accuracy with ViT-B/16, outperforming the strongest cache-based baseline TDA (67.53%) by 2.18% and the prompt-based method HisTPT (67.60%) by 2.11%. Under the natural distribution shift scenario, Dota reaches 66.31% average accuracy across ImageNet, ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, and ImageNet-S, surpassing the second-best method TDA (65.10%) by 1.21%. With prompt enhancement, Dota w/ PE further improves to 71.04% average on cross-domain generalization, exceeding DMN w/PE (70.30%). On medical pathology classification with PLIP, Dota achieves 70.56% average, a 6.89% improvement over TDA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dota outperforms all gradient-free cache-based baselines (TDA, BoostAdapter, ZERO) on average across both cross-domain and NDS scenarios, with the largest margin over TDA (+2.18% on cross-domain, +1.21% on NDS). Against gradient-based prompt tuning methods (TPT, DiffTPT, HisTPT), Dota achieves substantially higher accuracy while being 24–61× faster in inference time (22 min vs. 447–1346 min on ImageNet). Dota wins on 8 out of 10 cross-domain datasets against TDA, with the largest gains on Flower102 (+3.81%) and Pets (+3.38%), but loses on Aircraft (26.25 vs. 27.45 for BoostAdapter) and DTD (47.64 vs. 55.85 for DMN w/PE). On the NDS benchmark, Dota achieves the best result on every dataset (ImageNet 70.69, ImageNet-A 61.50, ImageNet-R 81.21, ImageNet-S 51.84), beating TDA by 1.18–1.30% per dataset. A notable failure case is ImageNet-V2 with only 10 samples per class, where Dota (64.50%) slightly underperforms TDA (64.67%), attributed to insufficient samples for reliable distribution estimation. With prompt enhancement, Dota w/ PE surpasses DMN w/PE (71.04% vs. 70.30%) on average, closing the gap on Aircraft and DTD while maintaining its lead on most other datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOTA departs from two dominant lines of test-time adaptation for VLMs: (1) gradient-based prompt tuning (TPT, DiffTPT) which is computationally expensive, and (2) cache-based methods (TDA, BoostAdapter) that store a limited set of representative samples and suffer from catastrophic forgetting when entries are evicted. Instead, DOTA repurposes classical Gaussian Discriminant Analysis and a single EM iteration—using zero-shot CLIP probabilities as soft responsibilities—to continuously estimate per-class distributions in an online fashion, then applies Bayes' theorem for classification. The online Gaussian parameter update follows Dasgupta & Hsu [8], and the logit-level fusion with zero-shot predictions is borrowed from prior TTA practice [25, 52], but the distributional framing as a replacement for instance-level caching is the paper's core conceptual contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a well-evidenced demonstration that cache-based TTA methods actively degrade with more data (forgetting), while distributional estimation monotonically improves—this reframes the design space for efficient TTA beyond 'bigger cache' thinking. Practical impact is significant for deployment: 20×+ speedup over gradient-based methods with comparable or better accuracy makes test-time adaptation viable on resource-constrained inference devices. Open questions remain around the single-Gaussian assumption (multimodal class distributions), uniform class priors (real-world imbalance), and the heuristic fusion weight λ, all of which the authors acknowledge as limitations. The method's reliance on well-calibrated zero-shot probabilities (validated via ECE in Appendix A.7) also raises the question of how it behaves with less well-calibrated foundation models.
