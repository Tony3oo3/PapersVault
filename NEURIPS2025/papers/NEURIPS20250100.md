## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) doubles the number of function evaluations (NFE) at every denoising timestep by requiring both conditional and unconditional network calls, making real-time diffusion sampling prohibitively expensive. Existing training-free accelerators (caching, higher-order solvers, guidance skipping) still perform two forward passes per timestep, implicitly assuming both branches are equally indispensable. The paper identifies that the noise estimate and the additional guidance term exhibit markedly different sensitivity to numerical error—a redundancy that conventional single-rate solvers fail to exploit.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and an initial Gaussian noise sample in the latent space of a pretrained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion 1.5, SD 3.5 Large, AudioLDM 2) and produces a generated image or audio clip at the same resolution as the underlying model. It is entirely training-free and solver-agnostic: the same coarse timestep grid C can be precomputed once and reused across all subsequent inferences without additional NFEs. The output quality matches standard CFG sampling with Δ ImageReward ≤ 0.032 while reducing NFE from 2N to N+|C|−1 (e.g., 100→70 for SD 1.5).

## Method

THG reformulates the CFG diffusion ODE as a two-state multirate system: a 'tortoise' equation carrying the conditional noise estimate ε̂_c(x_t) integrated on the original fine-grained timestep grid, and a 'hare' equation carrying the additional guidance term (ω−1)·Δε̂_c(x_t) integrated only on a coarser grid C. An error-bound analysis (Eq. 12) shows the hare's local integration error scales as m^{p+1} versus the tortoise's m·(Δt)^{p+1} over m fine steps, so the maximum allowable coarse step m_max is determined via Richardson extrapolation estimates of the error constants ‖c^T‖ and ‖c^H‖. A 'look before you leap' algorithm (Algorithm 2) constructs C adaptively so the hare-to-tortoise error ratio stays below a user threshold ρ. Two practical refinements complete the scheme: a constant boost factor b multiplies Δε̂_c to compensate for the coarser integration, and a threshold index i_hi zeros out the guidance term in the late denoising phase where it contributes little, further reducing NFE.

## Results

THG reduces NFE by up to 30% (e.g., 100→70 on SD 1.5, 56→38 on SD 3.5 Large) while maintaining generation fidelity with ΔImageReward ≤ 0.032. On SD 1.5 with DPM-Solver-2 at 70 NFE, THG achieves FID 12.909 and IR 0.14926, actually beating the 100-NFE CFG baseline (FID 13.255, IR 0.16148) on FID. On SD 3.5 Large at 38 NFE, THG attains IR 1.02365 (vs. 1.03569 for 56-NFE CFG) and the highest CS (26.672) among all 38-NFE methods. On AudioLDM 2 at 70 NFE, THG achieves FAD 2.764 and CLAP Score 0.2342, outperforming CFG-Cache w/o FFT (FAD 2.901, CLAP 0.2251).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla CFG, THG cuts NFE by 30% (100→70 on SD 1.5, 56→38 on SD 3.5 Large) with a maximum ImageReward drop of 0.032, and on SD 1.5 with DPM-Solver-2 it even improves FID over the 100-NFE baseline (12.909 vs. 13.255). Against CFG-Cache w/o FFT at the same NFE, THG wins on every metric across all three models: lower FID (14.165 vs. 14.240 on SD 1.5/DDIM), lower CMMD, higher CS, and substantially higher IR (0.11499 vs. 0.08757). Against full CFG-Cache (with FFT enhancement), THG wins on CS and IR for SD 1.5 (26.189/0.11499 vs. 26.180/0.09735), wins on IR for SD 3.5 Large (1.02365 vs. 1.00745), and wins on both FAD and CLAP Score for AudioLDM 2 (2.764/0.2342 vs. 2.901/0.2251). In the extended comparison (Table 7, Appendix E), THG also outperforms Selective Guidance and Guidance Interval on prompt fidelity (CS and IR) at matched NFE, though Selective Guidance achieves lower FID at the cost of severely degraded prompt alignment (IR as low as −0.067). Overall, THG consistently delivers the best prompt-fidelity metrics among all training-free accelerators at identical compute budgets, while remaining competitive on distributional similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

THG draws on classical multirate ODE integration (Rice 1960; Gear & Wells 1984) and applies it, for the first time, to the classifier-free guidance diffusion ODE, decomposing it into a numerically sensitive 'tortoise' branch (noise estimate) and a less sensitive 'hare' branch (additional guidance term). This departs from prior CFG acceleration methods—CFG-Cache/FasterCache, Adaptive Guidance, DeepCache, Learning-to-Cache—which rely on feature reuse, cosine-similarity heuristics, or architecture-specific caching without a formal error analysis. The error-bound framework borrows Richardson extrapolation (Hairer et al. 1993) to estimate solver error constants and determine the coarse grid adaptively, and the semi-linear ODE structure follows the DPM-Solver formulation (Lu et al. 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a rigorous error-bound justification for why the unconditional CFG pass can be evaluated at a coarser temporal resolution, THG offers a theoretically grounded, training-free, architecture-agnostic 30% NFE reduction that slots into existing diffusion pipelines without modification. This is practically important for real-time and large-scale serving where every forward pass of a large backbone is costly. Open questions include whether the multirate decomposition extends to other guidance schemes (e.g., IP-Adapter, T2I-Adapter), video diffusion with temporal coherence constraints, and whether the precomputed coarse grid C remains valid under distribution shift (e.g., novel prompts or fine-tuned models). The paper's evaluation is limited to latent diffusion models on COCO 2014 and AudioCaps, so generality to pixel-space models, 3D generation, or autoregressive diffusion remains untested.
