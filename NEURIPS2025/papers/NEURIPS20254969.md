## Problem

World models in reinforcement learning typically require extensive training to learn environment transition dynamics, with complex architectures that are prone to hallucinations and error accumulation over long horizons. This paper asks whether the transition dynamics P(x_{t+1}|x_t, a_t) can be approximated without any training of the dynamics model itself, by leveraging similarity search over a pre-encoded memory of observed trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254969/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images of environment states and optional actions as input; images are first encoded by a separately trained β-VAE into stochastic latent vectors (μ, σ), and all subsequent search and prediction operate entirely in this latent space rather than pixel space. Outputs include a predicted next-state latent distribution, a decoded next-state image, long-horizon latent sequences (up to 20 steps), and optionally a planned action sequence selected as the mode of a retrieved action distribution. The only trained component is the VAE encoder/decoder; the dynamics model is purely retrieval-based and requires no gradient updates.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces a learned transition model with a retrieve-and-reconstruct procedure: given a query latent z_t, the system searches a memory of stored transitions (z_τ, a_τ, z_{τ+1}, μ_τ, σ_τ) for K similar entries and estimates the next-state distribution from the retrieved batch. Three search structures are proposed: a Rollout buffer that preserves temporal ordering within each trajectory and retrieves one nearest transition per trajectory, a Replay buffer using L2 distance in latent space to find k-nearest transitions, and a Replay buffer using KL divergence to directly match the full Gaussian prior N(μ, σ) rather than a point estimate. For long-horizon prediction, the procedure is applied iteratively, feeding each predicted latent back as the next query, with future actions assumed known to isolate dynamics quality from action-selection effects. Action conditioning is implemented by restricting the searchable set to transitions whose stored action matches the desired one, effectively approximating P(z_{t+1}|z_t, a_t) rather than the marginal P(z_{t+1}|z_t).

## Results

In long-horizon dynamics prediction (20-step), Rollout and Replay-KL achieve significantly lower KL divergence than the PlaNet baseline across SuperTuxKart, Minecraft, and Atari, while Replay-L2 produces visually incoherent reconstructions despite competitive latent-space error. In the ablation study on SuperTuxKart, Rollout reduces average KL divergence from 311.89 (5 trajectories) to 86.27 (30 trajectories, −72.34%), and even with only 5 encoded trajectories, Rollout and Replay-KL report lower KL than the best PlaNet configuration (30 trajectories, KL ≈ 169.46). For planning in SuperTuxKart, search-based methods outperform PlaNet in 4 out of 5 tracks, with Replay-KL achieving the highest mean reward on snowmountain (15.66 vs. 5.32 for PlaNet) and Replay-L2 leading on snes_rainbowroad (6.34 vs. 3.16). Inference time for the search-based methods (0.8–2.5 ms) is roughly 20× faster than PlaNet (44.5 ms) in typical small-scoped tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is PlaNet (Hafner et al., 2019), a learned state-space model from the Dreamer family. In one-step dynamics prediction, all three search-based variants (Rollout, Replay-L2, Replay-KL) are visually indistinguishable from PlaNet in KL divergence, L1, and SSIM. In long-horizon (20-step) prediction, Rollout and Replay-KL clearly outperform PlaNet: PlaNet hallucinates after ~10 timesteps while the search-based models maintain consistency, with Replay-KL showing the closest resemblance to ground-truth sequences. Replay-L2 is the weakest variant, producing visually incoherent reconstructions despite competitive latent-space KL, due to high variance in its retrieved batches. For planning (Table 1), the search-based methods collectively win 4 out of 5 SuperTuxKart tracks, with no single method dominating all environments. The ablation study further shows that even with only 5 encoded trajectories, Rollout and Replay-KL achieve lower average KL divergence than PlaNet trained on 30 trajectories, suggesting the search-based approach is more data-efficient in small-scoped tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes similarity search over VAE-encoded trajectories as a substitute for the trained recurrent state-space model (SSM) that underpins PlaNet and the Dreamer family of world models. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: the PlaNet/Dreamer paradigm (learn a latent dynamics model) and the locally-weighted-learning / zero-shot-imitation-policy literature (retrieve and reuse stored experience). The key departure is that the transition dynamics P(z_{t+1}|z_t, a_t) are never learned; instead, they are approximated by searching for temporally adjacent transitions in a memory of encoded trajectories, with three retrieval strategies (rollout buffer, replay buffer with L2, replay buffer with KL divergence) offering different trade-offs between structure and flexibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a trained dynamics model is not strictly necessary for competitive world-model performance in small-scoped, visually bounded environments, offering a lightweight and immediately deployable alternative that avoids the data hunger and error-accumulation problems of learned SSMs. However, the approach is fundamentally limited by latent-space coverage: in open-ended tasks like Minecraft Navigate-v0, where the state space is effectively unbounded, the search-based models degrade and the trained baseline regains the advantage. Open questions include whether richer retrieval structures (hierarchical, multi-scale) or larger memory pools could close this gap, and whether the 'zero-shot' framing (which still requires VAE training) can be pushed further toward truly training-free operation.
