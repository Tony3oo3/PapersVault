## Problem

Invisible generative watermarking for video remains largely underexplored, and existing video watermarking methods (e.g., VideoShield, LVMark) only embed low-capacity binary strings, failing to exploit the high information bandwidth of video for richer graphical watermarks such as logos or icons that serve as intuitive ownership evidence. Safe-Sora is the first framework to embed graphical watermarks directly into the video generation process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255236/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (8 frames at 320×512, generated by a latent video diffusion backbone such as VideoCrafter2 or Open-Sora) and a 256×256 graphical watermark image (e.g., a logo from Logo-2K+), which is partitioned into 16×16 patches. It produces a watermarked video at the same resolution and a recovered watermark image. Patch positions are tracked via an 8-bit binary position channel (supporting up to 256 positions) that is concatenated with patch content, enabling the extraction network to reconstruct the full watermark layout without explicit positional supervision at test time.

## Method

The core insight is that watermarking performance strongly correlates with visual similarity between the watermark and cover content. A Coarse-to-Fine Adaptive Patch Matching (CFAPM) mechanism first assigns each watermark patch to the most visually similar video frame (coarse stage) and then to the most similar spatial region within that frame (fine stage), computing similarity via dot products of features extracted from the video's latent representations. For spatiotemporal fusion and extraction, the paper introduces a 3D Wavelet Transform-enhanced Mamba (SFMamba) architecture with a dual-stream spatial/frequency design; the 3D frequency branch applies a novel spatiotemporal local scanning strategy that traverses the eight 3D-DWT subbands hierarchically from low to high frequency (and in reverse), using a spatial-first, temporal-second pattern within each subband to capture long-range dependencies efficiently. Training uses a combined MSE loss for video reconstruction and watermark reconstruction (weighted by λ=0.75), and a confidence-guided greedy assignment algorithm resolves position conflicts during extraction.

## Results

Safe-Sora achieves a watermark PSNR of 37.71 dB and video PSNR of 42.50 dB, substantially outperforming all five baselines. The most striking result is the Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of 3.77, compared to 154.35 for the second-best baseline (PUSNet), demonstrating near-perfect temporal consistency. Watermark LPIPS reaches 0.04 and video LPIPS reaches 0.01, indicating minimal perceptual distortion in both the recovered watermark and the watermarked video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Safe-Sora outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor Wengnet (video steganography), Safe-Sora improves watermark PSNR by 4.53 dB (37.71 vs. 33.18) and reduces FVD by 70× (3.77 vs. 265.82). PUSNet, the best image steganography baseline, achieves the second-lowest FVD (154.35) but still trails Safe-Sora by over 40×, and its watermark PSNR (28.86) is 8.85 dB lower. The generative image watermarking method Safe-SD performs worst on video quality (FVD 849.83, video PSNR 22.32), while UDH and Balujanet (classic image hiding networks) show the poorest watermark fidelity (PSNR 22.90 and 25.28 respectively) and severe temporal inconsistency. The large FVD gaps across all baselines are attributed to their frame-independent embedding strategies, whereas Safe-Sora's 3D SFMamba with spatiotemporal local scanning explicitly models cross-frame dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends video watermarking from the binary-string paradigm (VideoShield, LVMark) to graphical watermarking, and is the first to integrate watermark embedding directly into a latent video diffusion generation pipeline rather than treating it as a post-processing step. Architecturally, it pioneers the use of state space models (Mamba) for watermarking by proposing SFMamba blocks that combine spatial and 3D wavelet frequency branches with a novel spatiotemporal local scanning strategy. The coarse-to-fine adaptive patch matching mechanism draws on the observation that watermark-cover visual similarity drives embedding quality, a principle previously demonstrated in image steganography but not exploited in the video setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that graphical watermarks (logos, icons) can be embedded and recovered from generated video with high fidelity and strong robustness to H.264 compression, rotation, and noise, this work provides a practical copyright-protection tool for the rapidly growing AI video generation ecosystem. The first application of SSMs to watermarking opens a promising direction for efficient long-range spatiotemporal modeling in related tasks. Key limitations include support only for static image watermarks (not animated or temporally dynamic patterns), evaluation on short 8-frame clips at 320×512, and the absence of statistical significance reporting, leaving open questions about scalability to longer, higher-resolution videos and real-world deployment robustness.
