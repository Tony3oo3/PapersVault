## Problem

CroCo's cross-view completion pre-training is fundamentally ill-posed in non-covisible regions: when a pixel in one view is occluded or outside the field of view in the other, there is no meaningful reconstruction target. This forces CroCo to restrict training to image pairs with at least 50% overlap, leaving the model unable to learn from the geometrically challenging configurations that are most common in real-world binocular vision tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250463/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of unmasked RGB images (resized to a maximum dimension of 512 px while preserving aspect ratio) from the same scene at different viewpoints. During pre-training, it outputs a per-pixel three-class covisibility segmentation map (covisible, occluded, outside FOV) for each image with respect to the other. During fine-tuning, it additionally outputs a metric relative translation vector in ℝ³ and a relative rotation as a unit quaternion in ℝ⁴, regressed from globally pooled decoder features.

## Method

Alligat0R retains CroCo's ViT encoder + cross-attention transformer decoder architecture but replaces the asymmetric masked-reconstruction objective with a symmetric, unmasked covisibility segmentation task: each pixel in both views is classified into one of three geometric categories via a softmax head, trained with cross-entropy loss. This makes the pre-training signal well-defined in both covisible and non-covisible regions, and the outputs are directly interpretable as geometric reasoning. For downstream relative pose regression, a two-phase fine-tuning strategy is used: first the backbone is frozen while a pose head (global average pooling → shared MLP → separate translation and quaternion heads) is trained with a homoscedastic loss; then the full network is unfrozen and jointly optimized with both the pose loss and the segmentation loss to preserve interpretability. The approach is supported by Cub3, a 5M-pair dataset with dense covisibility annotations derived from nuScenes (via automated depth/pose estimation) and ScanNet (via ground-truth depth and poses), covering both high-overlap and extremely low-overlap configurations.

## Results

On the RUBIK benchmark, Alligat0R pre-trained on Cub3-all with an unfrozen backbone achieves 60.3% success rate at 5°/2m and 81.9% at 10°/5m, substantially outperforming CroCo pre-trained on the same data (38.6% and 66.2% respectively). On ScanNet1500, Alligat0R reaches 95.1% at 10°/1m versus 85.0% for CroCo. Against state-of-the-art methods on RUBIK (Table 3), Alligat0R achieves the best overall success rate of 60.3%, surpassing DUSt3R (54.8%), MASt3R (53.6%), and RoMa (47.3%), while running at 57 ms per pair—among the fastest methods evaluated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Alligat0R consistently outperforms CroCo across every configuration tested. With a frozen backbone on Cub3-all, Alligat0R achieves 55.3% at 5°/2m on RUBIK versus only 8.9% for CroCo—a 6.2× improvement—demonstrating that covisibility segmentation extracts far more useful geometric features from challenging low-overlap pairs. Even when CroCo's backbone is unfrozen (12.5%, 38.6%, 66.2%), Alligat0R with a frozen backbone (24.0%, 55.3%, 82.3%) still wins by large margins. Against the official CroCo v2 model (pre-trained on 5 datasets), Alligat0R trained on a single dataset (nuScenes) achieves 60.3% vs. 44.5% at 5°/2m on RUBIK with unfrozen backbones. On the RUBIK SOTA comparison (Table 3), Alligat0R's 60.3% overall success rate beats DUSt3R (54.8%), MASt3R (53.6%), RoMa (47.3%), and all detector-free matchers (LoFTR 24.9%, ELOFTR 26.6%, ASpanFormer 24.8%). On zero-shot ETH3D correspondence estimation, Alligat0R (43.82 AEPE) outperforms CroCo (77.61) and even CroCo v2 (51.55), confirming superior generalization of the learned features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on CroCo's ViT encoder–transformer decoder architecture with cross-attention, but fundamentally departs from the cross-view completion objective that underpins CroCo, CroCo v2, DUSt3R, and MASt3R. Instead of masked pixel reconstruction, Alligat0R formulates pre-training as a three-class covisibility segmentation task (covisible, occluded, outside FOV), a formulation the authors note is the first to use covisibility as a pre-training signal rather than merely a training-pair filter or matchability score. For fine-tuning, it follows Reloc3R's relative pose regression setup but predicts metric translation rather than direction-only, and introduces a two-phase training strategy with homoscedastic loss balancing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-identified weakness of cross-view completion (ill-posedness in non-covisible regions) with a clean, interpretable alternative, and the 5M-pair Cub3 dataset provides a valuable resource for binocular vision research. However, the approach is currently limited to autonomous driving (nuScenes) and indoor (ScanNet) scenes, requires 3D geometry (depth + poses) to generate annotations, and has only been validated on pose regression and zero-shot correspondence—its transfer to stereo matching, optical flow, or outdoor natural scenes remains open. The finding that the method benefits from harder training pairs while CroCo does not suggests a broader principle about when reconstruction-based pre-training breaks down.
