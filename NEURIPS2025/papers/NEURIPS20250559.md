## Problem

Existing linear GCN models for recommendation (e.g., LightGCN) rely on the graph Laplacian norm to suppress information propagation from first-order neighbors, which inadvertently dilutes significant interaction signals and slows the model's ability to learn sparse interaction patterns. The paper argues that this suppression paradigm overlooks the fact that items with more interactions carry disproportionately important signals (conformity, trust), and proposes shifting from suppression to strategic amplification of these significant interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250559/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a bipartite user-item interaction graph where the only trainable parameters are the initial ID embeddings of users and items (dimension d=64); all aggregation weights are determined non-parametrically by node degrees via a fixed logarithmic function (β=e, Δ^log=1). Output is a scalar ranking score r̃_(u,i) = ẽ_u^T ẽ_i computed from weighted layer-combined embeddings, used for Top-N (N∈{5,15}) recommendation. The model requires no feature transformations, no attention parameters, and no per-dataset hyperparameter tuning beyond a simple λ guideline based on interaction count.

## Method

BoostGCN replaces the standard Laplacian-normalized aggregation weight 1/(√|N_u|·√|N_i|) with an amplification weight (log_β(|N_i|)+1)/|N_u|, which increases with the neighbor's degree rather than decreasing with it. The key insight is that interaction significance (driven by conformity, trust, and quality signals) is approximately proportional to an item's interaction volume |N_i|, so amplifying high-degree neighbors compensates for the information loss caused by Laplacian suppression. The logarithmic function is selected over linear or exponential alternatives because it satisfies three criteria: monotonic increase with degree, sub-linear growth to prevent over-amplification of extremely popular nodes, and closed-form differentiability for efficient optimization. Training uses BPR loss with only the 0-th layer embeddings as parameters, and final representations are a uniform weighted sum across K layers. The paper provides theoretical guarantees including a spectral-radius bound showing near-optimal linear convergence and a high-probability error bound under stochastic training.

## Results

BoostGCN achieves the best performance across all four datasets on every metric (R@5, N@5, R@15, N@15). Headline numbers include R@5 of 0.2982 on MovieLens-100k, 0.2641 on MovieLens-1M, 0.2965 on Gowalla, and 0.2001 on Yelp2018. In terms of efficiency, BoostGCN trains 49%–90% faster than LightGCN while simultaneously improving accuracy (e.g., 438s vs. 4525s on Yelp2018 with +45.13% R@5). The model uses fixed hyperparameters (β=e, Δ^log=1, batch size 512) across all datasets without per-dataset tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BoostGCN outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all four datasets, with no losses. The closest competitors are NSE-GCN (best on 100k and Gowalla) and LayerGCN (best on 1M and Yelp); for example, on Yelp R@5 BoostGCN scores 0.2001 vs. LayerGCN's 0.1845 (+8.5%), and on 100k R@5 it scores 0.2982 vs. NSE-GCN's 0.2963 (+0.6%). Against the primary baseline LightGCN, improvements range from +3.73% (100k, layer 1) to +45.13% (Yelp, layer 4) in R@5, with the largest gains on the sparser, larger datasets. In efficiency, BoostGCN trains 49% faster on 100k (23s vs. 45s) and 90% faster on Yelp (438s vs. 4525s) while simultaneously improving accuracy. Traditional MF-BPR and NGCF are consistently the weakest baselines, confirming the advantage of linear GCN aggregation over matrix factorization and nonlinear feature transformations.

## Relation to Prior Work

BoostGCN directly extends the LightGCN linear GCN framework by replacing the graph Laplacian norm aggregation—which suppresses information from high-degree neighbors—with a logarithmic amplification function that emphasizes significant first-order interactions. This departs from the entire family of Laplacian-based GCNs (LightGCN, UltraGCN, LayerGCN, NSE-GCN) that share the same suppression mechanism, and also contrasts with attention-based approaches (GAT) by showing that a parameter-free, degree-based reweighting can be more sample-efficient and noise-robust. The theoretical grounding draws on spectral graph theory, VC-dimension analysis, and stochastic optimization to justify the logarithmic choice among candidate amplification functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a long-standing design assumption in GCN-based recommendation—that Laplacian normalization's suppression of high-degree neighbors is beneficial—by showing that amplification yields both better accuracy and substantially faster training (up to 90% speedup on Yelp2018). The model's near-fixed hyperparameters (β=e, Δ^log=1) make it immediately practical for practitioners. Key limitations include its restriction to static graphs, the heuristic nature of the amplification function, and the fact that the largest gains appear on very sparse datasets where the baseline is weakest. Open questions include whether the amplification paradigm transfers to dynamic/real-time recommendation, whether other non-logarithmic amplification functions could further improve performance, and how BoostGCN interacts with emerging multimodal or sequential recommendation architectures.
