## Problem

Modern vision-language-action (VLA) models have inference latencies of 100–300+ ms, far exceeding the 20 ms control period required for real-time robot operation. Action chunking provides temporal consistency but does not resolve the latency gap: synchronous execution introduces visible pauses, while naive asynchronous execution produces out-of-distribution discontinuities at chunk boundaries that degrade performance in dynamic and precision-sensitive tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251122/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Beyond standard VLA inputs (RGB images and language instructions), the method consumes the remaining unexecuted actions from the previous chunk as a conditioning signal. The output is a full action chunk of length H in which the first d actions (d = inference delay in controller timesteps) are guaranteed to match the already-executing values, while the remaining H − d actions are freshly generated to be consistent with that frozen prefix. The execution horizon s adapts per chunk as max(d, s_min), constrained by d ≤ s ≤ H − d.

## Method

RTC poses asynchronous action chunking as an inpainting problem: while executing the current chunk, a background thread generates the next chunk by freezing the first d actions to their known values and inpainting the remainder via pseudoinverse-guided flow matching (Π-GDM). A key innovation is soft masking, where the guidance weight decays exponentially from 1 (frozen region) to 0 (fresh region) across the intermediate overlapping timesteps, yielding stronger cross-chunk continuity than hard 0/1 masking. The guidance weight is additionally clipped at β (set to 5) to prevent divergence with the small number of denoising steps (n = 5) typical in control. The entire procedure is purely inference-time, requires no retraining, and is applicable to any diffusion- or flow-based VLA out of the box.

## Results

In the Kinetix simulation benchmark (12 dynamic tasks, 2048 rollouts per data point), RTC achieves the highest solve rate across all inference delays (d=0 to 4), with the performance gap over baselines widening as delay increases. In real-world bimanual manipulation (6 tasks, 480 episodes, 28 hours of robot time), RTC achieves the best average throughput at all tested inference delays (d≈6, 11, 16) and is 20% faster than synchronous inference. RTC remains robust to +200ms of injected latency (d≈16, >30% of the 50-step prediction horizon), while both temporal ensembling variants trigger the robot's protective stop and synchronous inference degrades linearly. Model latency increases modestly from 76ms (vanilla π_0.5) to 97ms with RTC on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bidirectional Decoding (BID; Liu et al. [39]), RTC achieves higher solve rates across all inference delays in the Kinetix simulation while using substantially less compute: BID requires sampling batches of 64 action chunks (32 from a strong model, 32 from a weak model), yielding 2.3× the latency of RTC on π_0.5 (223ms vs. 97ms for full BID). Against Temporal Ensembling (TE; Zhao et al. [68]), RTC wins decisively in both settings: in simulation, TE performs poorly even at zero inference delay (d=0) because averaging multi-modal action predictions produces invalid actions; in the real world, both TE variants (sparse and dense) cause such high oscillations at +100ms and +200ms injected latency that the robot's protective stop is triggered, making them inoperable, whereas RTC shows no degradation. RTC also outperforms the naive asynchronous baseline (which ignores the previous chunk entirely) by maintaining cross-chunk continuity through soft-masked inpainting, and it uniquely benefits from shorter execution horizons (more closed-loop corrections) where other methods degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes training-free image inpainting (Pokle et al. [48], built on pseudoinverse-guided diffusion [55]) for the novel domain of real-time robot action generation, adapting it to conditional flow matching [36] policies. It departs from the standard temporal ensembling approach (Zhao et al. [68]) and the rejection-sampling-based Bidirectional Decoding (Liu et al. [39]) by framing cross-chunk continuity as a guided generation problem rather than an averaging or resampling one. The soft masking extension (exponentially decaying guidance weights over the overlap region) is the paper's key algorithmic contribution beyond the direct inpainting transfer, addressing the insufficiency of hard masking when inference delay is small relative to the prediction horizon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RTC addresses a fundamental deployment bottleneck for VLAs: inference latency that exceeds the control period, which currently forces either pauses (synchronous) or jerky discontinuities (naive async). Because it is purely inference-time and requires no retraining, it is immediately applicable to any existing diffusion- or flow-based VLA, making it a practical drop-in for the growing ecosystem of billion-parameter robot policies. Limitations include applicability only to iterative denoising frameworks (not autoregressive or one-shot policies), a 2.5× per-step compute overhead from backpropagation, and the absence of real-world legged locomotion experiments. Open questions include whether the inpainting formulation extends to non-iterative generative policies and how it interacts with hierarchical System 1/System 2 VLA architectures.
