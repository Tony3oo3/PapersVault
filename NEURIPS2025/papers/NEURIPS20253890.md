## Problem

Top-k decoding is the default sampling method in major LLMs, yet no precise theoretical justification exists for why truncating to the top-k probabilities and renormalizing is optimal. The paper addresses this gap by developing a principled optimization framework that both recovers standard top-k decoding as a special case and yields a rich family of adaptive generalizations with provable properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253890/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw LLM next-token probability vector p ∈ Δ_V (V = vocabulary size) at a single decoding step and outputs a sparse probability distribution with exactly k* nonzero entries, from which the next token is sampled. Unlike fixed-k top-k decoding, the sparsity level k* is chosen adaptively per token by minimizing an ℓ₀-regularized Bregman objective, and the renormalization of the selected entries is a Bregman projection rather than simple division by their sum, allowing non-linear reweighting of the retained probabilities.

## Method

The paper formulates per-token decoding as minimizing a separable Bregman divergence to the raw LLM distribution with an ℓ₀ sparsity penalty, in both primal (divergence in first argument) and dual (divergence in second argument) forms. Two structural theorems make the resulting combinatorial problem tractable: (i) a greedy property proving that selecting the top-k largest probabilities is always optimal for any fixed k, and (ii) discrete convexity of the objective in k, enabling the optimal k* to be found via binary search in O(log V) steps. Instantiating the Bregman generator with Tsallis α-entropies yields a one-parameter family of decoders where α=1 recovers standard top-k, α→∞ converges to water-filling, and intermediate values produce tunable up-weighting or down-weighting of larger probabilities after renormalization. The framework is validated empirically on GPT-2 Large and LLaMA 3.1 8B for open-ended text generation (WebText) and math reasoning (GSM8K), showing competitive performance with standard top-k.

## Results

On GSM8K with LLaMA 3.1 8B, Bregman primal decoding (α=1.5, λ=0.01) achieves 85.14% accuracy at temperature 0.3, outperforming matched top-k decoding (83.62%) by ~1.5 points. At higher temperature (1.5) with weaker regularization (λ=0.0001), the gap widens substantially: Bregman α=2.0 reaches 64.97% versus 58.53% for top-k (a 6.4-point advantage), and Bregman α=1.5 reaches 57.24% versus 43.21% for top-k (a 14-point advantage). On open-ended WebText generation, α=2.0 Bregman decoding shows the smallest perplexity and repetition gaps relative to human text among all tested methods, indicating improved diversity over standard top-k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard top-k decoding (equivalent to α=1 in the Bregman framework), with k set to the adaptive k* from the corresponding Bregman run for a matched comparison. At low temperatures (0.3–0.7) and stronger regularization (λ=0.01), Bregman decoding and top-k are essentially tied or Bregman leads by 1–2 points (e.g., 85.14% vs. 83.62% at temp 0.3). The most striking advantage appears at high temperature (1.5) with weak regularization (λ=0.0001), where top-k degrades severely (43.21% for α=1.5, 58.53% for α=2.0) while Bregman decoding retains substantially higher accuracy (57.24% and 64.97% respectively), yielding gaps of 14 and 6.4 points. On open-ended WebText generation, α=2.0 Bregman decoding achieves the lowest perplexity difference and repetition difference relative to human text, suggesting that the non-linear up-weighting of smaller probabilities within the top-k set improves sampling diversity compared to the uniform renormalization of standard top-k. Overall, the paper positions Bregman decoding as competitive with top-k at standard settings while offering a principled mechanism for improved robustness at higher temperatures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first rigorous theoretical foundation for top-k decoding (Fan et al., 2018), which has been widely deployed in LLMs but lacked a principled optimization-theoretic justification. It embeds top-k as a special case (α=1, KL divergence) within a broader framework of ℓ0-regularized separable Bregman divergence minimization, drawing on the Bregman projection literature (Krichene et al., 2015; Lim & Wright, 2016) for the inner renormalization step and on Tsallis/Havrda-Charvát α-entropies for a one-parameter family of new decoders. The paper departs from prior Bregman projection work by adding the ℓ0 sparsity layer and by analyzing the dual (second-argument) Bregman projection, which had not been directly studied before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The greedy-selection and k-convexity theorems show that a problem that appears combinatorial (choosing which k tokens to keep under ℓ0 regularization) admits an O(log V) exact solution, which is a clean structural result with potential applicability beyond LLM decoding. The framework generates a tunable family of decoders (α-decoding) whose behavior ranges from concentrating mass on the top token (α→−∞) to water-filling (α→∞), offering practitioners a principled dial for controlling sampling diversity. Open questions include extending the theory to top-p and min-p sampling, characterizing how the adaptive k* scales with λ and distribution shape, and quantifying the primal-dual decoding gap. Empirically, the new decoders are competitive with top-k but do not dramatically outperform it, so the primary contribution is theoretical rather than practical.
