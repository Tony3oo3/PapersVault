## Problem

Existing generalization analyses of Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) focus almost exclusively on approximation error while neglecting estimation error, leaving the bias-variance trade-off unaddressed and providing no structural complexity measure beyond parameter count. This gap means there is no principled way to understand why KANs' larger hypothesis space can hurt generalization, nor a theoretical basis for designing KAN variants with better generalization guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous inputs—RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 96×96 for STL-10, 28×28 for MNIST), text (AG News), image+audio pairs (AVMNIST), clinical time series (MIMIC-III), and numerical vectors (Feynman equations)—and outputs class labels or continuous function values. Internally, each input is processed through KAN layers where B-spline basis functions (grid size 5, range [−10,10], degree 3) are applied element-wise per neuron, with a hidden width of 64; the LipKAN variant additionally applies a fixed 1-Lipschitz function (tanh, sigmoid, or ReLU) between each activation family and its corresponding linear weight tensor.

## Method

The paper first reformulates KANs as alternating compositions of a shared activation family Σ and 3D weight tensors W_i, then defines Lipschitz complexity R_{W_1^L} = (‖Σ‖_Lip^L · ∏‖W_i‖_σ) · (∑‖W_i^T‖_{2,2,1}^{2/3})^{3/2} as the first structural measure capturing both basis smoothness and weight magnitude. Using this measure, it derives uniform high-probability generalization bounds (Theorem 4.5) that scale with R_{W_1^L} and reduce to classical MLP margin bounds when G=1. To tighten these bounds, LipKANs insert a fixed 1-Lipschitz layer ℓ (e.g., tanh) between each Σ and W_i to reduce ‖Σ‖_Lip, and replace the standard L1 penalty with an L1.5 regularization λ∑‖W_i^T‖_{2,2,1} that directly penalizes the structured (2,2,1) norm appearing in the complexity term. Training uses AdamW with decoupled weight decay, and the two components—Lip layers and L1.5 regularization—act on orthogonal axes of Lipschitz complexity (basis smoothness vs. weight magnitude), yielding complementary variance reduction.

## Results

LipKANs consistently outperform their non-Lip counterparts across all seven benchmark datasets, with the largest gains on complex distributions: +12.83% accuracy on STL-10 (38.77% vs. 25.94%), +5.74% on CIFAR-100 (20.51% vs. 14.77%), and +3.79% F1 on MIMIC-III (70.18% vs. 66.39%). On simpler datasets like MNIST and CIFAR-10, improvements are more modest (+1.09% and +4.39% respectively). The same pattern holds for Rational-KAN and RBF-KAN variants, where adding Lip layers and L1.5-regularization yields consistent but smaller gains (e.g., +4.75% on STL-10 for LipRational-KAN, +6.73% F1 on MIMIC-III for LipRBF-KAN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LipKANs are evaluated against three KAN baselines: Original KAN (B-spline), Rational-KAN, and RBF-KAN. LipKAN achieves the largest absolute improvement over Original KAN, gaining +12.83% on STL-10 and +5.74% on CIFAR-100, while the gain on MNIST is only +1.09%, confirming that the benefit is most pronounced on complex distributions. LipRational-KAN improves over Rational-KAN by +4.75% on STL-10 and +3.42% on AG News, though Rational-KAN already starts from a higher baseline (e.g., 71.56% on AG News vs. 65.58% for KAN). LipRBF-KAN shows the largest relative gain on MIMIC-III (+6.73% F1, reaching 74.25%), surpassing both LipKAN (70.18%) and LipRational-KAN (69.12%) on that clinical dataset. Across all three baseline families, the Lip variant consistently wins on every dataset, with the magnitude of improvement scaling with dataset complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the original KAN architecture (Liu et al., ICLR 2025) by inserting fixed Lipschitz-constrained layers between activation and linear layers, and replacing the standard L1 regularization with an L1.5 penalty derived from a new structural complexity measure. Theoretically, it builds on the covering-number induction technique from Bartlett et al. (NeurIPS 2017) for spectrally-normalized margin bounds, adapting it to the tensor-weight structure of KANs. It departs from prior KAN generalization work (Zhang and Zhou, 2024), which provided bounds but overlooked architectural structural complexity, and from approximation-error-focused analyses (Ataei et al. 2025, Qiu et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2025a) by explicitly addressing the estimation-error side of the bias-variance trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Lipschitz complexity as the first structural measure for KANs provides a principled, architecture-specific lens for understanding why KANs may undergeneralize relative to their approximation capacity, and gives concrete design guidance (lower-Lipschitz activations, structured norm regularization). The L1.5 regularization is a lightweight, drop-in replacement for L1 that practitioners can adopt immediately. Open questions include whether the derived bounds are tight (the authors acknowledge they may not be optimal), whether the approach extends to very deep or wide KANs with inter-layer dependencies made explicit, and whether L1.5 is truly the optimal norm or merely a convenient choice within the (2,2,1) family. The concentration of empirical gains on complex distributions (STL-10, CIFAR-100, MIMIC-III) with minimal change on simpler benchmarks suggests the method's value is conditional on hypothesis-space complexity being the binding constraint.
