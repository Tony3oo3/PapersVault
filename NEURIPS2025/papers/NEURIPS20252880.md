## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models trained on web-crawled data can generate harmful content (nudity, violence, copyrighted artist styles) when given malicious prompts. Existing safe generation methods either require costly retraining with curated safety data, or are training-free but operate without directly incorporating the diffusion model's intermediate samples into their safety mechanism, leaving their impact on generated outputs theoretically ungrounded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252880/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (encoded by a pre-trained text encoder such as CLIP ViT-L/14) and a pluggable safety function g that scores clean images (e.g., NudeNet confidence for nudity, negative CLIP score against violence keywords, or CLIP score difference for artist-style removal). During DDIM sampling at 512×512 resolution, the text embedding is updated at selected intermediate timesteps using the safety function evaluated on the Tweedie-estimated denoised image, and the final output is a 512×512 RGB image that suppresses the targeted unsafe concept while preserving the prompt's core semantics.

## Method

STG applies gradient ascent on a time-dependent safety function g_t(x_t, c) = g(x̄_0(x_t, c)) to the text embedding c at each diffusion timestep, where x̄_0 is the Tweedie-estimated clean image from the current noisy latent. The key distinction from prior data-space guidance (SDG/Universal Guidance) is that STG modifies the conditioning signal (text embedding) rather than the perturbed data, so the score network s_θ(x_t, c_updated, t) naturally preserves the original model's likelihood structure. Theorem 1 proves that this embedding update is equivalent to adding a safe guidance term ∇_{x_t}{ρ ∇_c g_t^T ∇_c log q_t(x_t|c)} to the score, where the implicit safe probability aligns the safety gradient with the text-conditional likelihood. Three hyperparameters—update scale ρ, safety threshold τ, and update step ratio γ—provide a controllable safety–quality trade-off at inference time without any retraining.

## Results

On the COCO generation quality benchmark (3,000 images), STG achieves the lowest FID of 22.00, outperforming the base Stable Diffusion v1.4 model (23.22) and all baselines, while maintaining a competitive CLIP score of 31.14. On the primary safety metrics (Defense Success Rate vs. Prior Preservation), STG consistently occupies the upper-right corner of the trade-off curve across nudity (Ring-A-Bell, SneakyPrompt) and violence (Ring-A-Bell) scenarios, achieving the best simultaneous DSR and PP among all methods. For artist-style removal, STG achieves the highest LPIPS_e (0.46 for Van Gogh, 0.58 for McKernan) and lowest ACC_e (0.30, 0.10), indicating the most effective target-style suppression while preserving non-target styles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STG outperforms all seven baselines on the COCO FID metric, achieving 22.00 versus the best training-based method ESD (22.85), SPM (23.53), and DUO (23.27), and the best training-free methods RECE (23.15) and UCE (23.20). On the primary safety trade-off (DSR vs. PP), STG dominates the Pareto frontier for both nudity and violence, surpassing DUO (the strongest training-based baseline) on violence where DUO's effectiveness degrades due to the diversity of violence categories. Among training-free baselines, SAFREE shows the weakest generation quality (FID 28.39) and moderate DSR, while SLD achieves competitive DSR (0.76) but at the cost of lower PP (0.65) and higher FID (24.32). For artist-style removal, STG achieves the best target-style erasure (ACC_e = 0.30 for Van Gogh, 0.10 for McKernan) while maintaining the highest non-target style preservation (ACC_u = 0.85, 0.65), outperforming SDG, SAFREE, and all other baselines on both LPIPS_e and ACC_e simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

STG sits at the intersection of two guidance frameworks: Universal Guidance (Bansal et al., CVPR 2023), which approximates the expected denoised image via Tweedie's formula to apply a time-independent classifier in the data space, and Diffusion Adaptive Text Embedding (DATE, Na et al., NeurIPS 2025), which shifts the guidance target from perturbed data to the text embedding. The paper's key departure from prior training-free safe generation methods (SLD, UCE, RECE, SAFREE) is that it directly incorporates the diffusion model's own intermediate latent states into the safety signal, rather than manipulating attention weights or projecting embeddings onto unsafe subspaces without reference to the generated content. Theoretically, Theorem 1 unifies STG with the Classifier Guidance framework by showing that text-embedding updates induce an implicit safe guidance term in the data space, where the safe probability is defined by the alignment between the safety gradient and the text-conditional likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STG offers a practical, training-free safety mechanism that generalizes across diverse backbones (FLUX, SDXL, SD3, PixArt-α, LCM) without requiring curated safety-annotated datasets or model fine-tuning, addressing a key limitation of training-based unlearning methods. The toy experiment demonstrating that STG is more robust than data-space guidance (SDG) when the safety function only preserves relative ordering rather than matching the true safe distribution is a useful practical insight for practitioners who must rely on imperfect proxy classifiers. Open questions include how STG scales to video generation, whether the single-step gradient update on text embeddings suffices for more complex multi-constraint safety objectives, and how the method interacts with increasingly adversarial prompt engineering that evolves faster than static safety functions can adapt.
