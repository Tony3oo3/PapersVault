## Problem

Existing grouping methods for the Bradley-Terry model (fused lasso, Group Rank Centrality) are designed exclusively for static settings, treating all comparisons as if collected simultaneously. In practice, item abilities evolve over time, and the underlying group structure can shift abruptly (e.g., due to player trades or market shocks). No prior work has jointly addressed the identification of dynamic ranking groups and the detection of structural change points in the BT model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary pairwise comparison outcomes y_ij(t_k) with timestamps t_k uniformly distributed over [0,T] for n items, along with a kernel bandwidth h and a candidate set of change points {ξ_i}. It produces a time-varying partition of items into B groups, per-item ability scores π̂_i(t) (with an optional refit step that aggregates within-group comparisons), group-level stationary distributions, and a set of detected structure change points. The comparison count M per pair and the number of discretized time points m are user-specified, with m allowed to grow for integral approximation.

## Method

The framework nests two stages: (1) for any sub-interval, a kernel-smoothed spectral estimator (Markov chain stationary distribution) is combined with an adaptive group lasso penalty via a technical transformation using matrix Q, converting the constrained BT estimation into an unconstrained group lasso problem whose solution identifies group boundaries; (2) over a longer horizon, a separable objective combining negative log-likelihood, a group-count penalty γ₁, and a change-point penalty γ₂ is minimized via dynamic programming over the candidate change-point set. The key theoretical challenge is that the design matrix X is random (induced by a reversible Markov chain) rather than deterministic, requiring novel concentration arguments. A group inverse of I − P*_G(t) is used to derive the asymptotic normal distribution of group-level ability estimates, and a refit step re-estimates scores within identified groups to reduce shrinkage bias.

## Results

On the harder Setting 2 (with score intersections among groups), the proposed method achieves Kendall τ of 0.9416–0.9683 and MSE of 0.0250–0.0494, substantially outperforming GRC (Kendall τ ≈ 0.797, MSE ≈ 0.217) and KRC (Kendall τ ≈ 0.76–0.77, MSE ≈ 0.06–0.12). Group specificity reaches 100% for the proposed method in all settings, while GRC drops to 63.6–63.7% in Setting 2, confirming the necessity of the dynamic formulation. For structural change detection (Table 2), the proposed method achieves near-zero Hausdorff distance (0.0000–0.0004) with 97–100% exact change-point recovery at moderate sample sizes, whereas the naive baseline consistently over-detects change points (96–100% over-detection rate) with Hausdorff distances 20–50× larger.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRC (Tian & Shi, Stat 2023), the proposed method is comparable on the simple Setting 1 but decisively superior on the harder Setting 2 where group scores intersect: Kendall τ improves from 0.797 to 0.94–0.97, MSE drops from 0.217 to 0.025–0.05, and specificity jumps from 63.6% to 100%, demonstrating that the static GRC cannot distinguish groups whose scores cross over time. Against KRC (Tian et al., Stat 2024), the proposed method achieves higher Kendall τ (e.g., 0.964 vs. 0.774 in Setting 2, n=50) and lower MSE (0.029 vs. 0.061), while additionally providing group structure (sensitivity/specificity) that KRC does not offer. The refit strategy yields modest but consistent improvements over the non-refit estimator (e.g., MSE 0.0250 vs. 0.0291 in Setting 2, n=50). For structural change detection, the proposed dynamic-programming approach with the integrated penalty objective achieves near-perfect recovery (H-dist ≤ 0.0004, ≥97% exact) with far fewer samples than the naive interval-by-interval baseline, which over-detects change points in 96–100% of runs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral estimation framework for the dynamic Bradley-Terry model (Tian et al., 2024; Negahban et al., 2017) by incorporating an adaptive group lasso penalty to simultaneously identify homogeneous ranking groups, building on the static Group Rank Centrality method (Tian & Shi, 2023) and earlier fused-lasso ranking approaches (Masarotto & Varin, 2012). The key departure is the introduction of a dynamic programming-based change point detection objective that identifies when group membership structures shift over time—a problem not addressed by prior static grouping methods or by Li et al. (2022), who detect score-level change points but not structural group changes. The theoretical analysis leverages the reversible Markov chain structure of the BT model to establish consistency, and a group inverse technique is tailored for uncertainty quantification of group-level ability estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled framework for detecting when underlying group structures in preference-based data undergo significant changes, with full theoretical guarantees (group consistency, change point consistency, asymptotic normality). The NBA application demonstrates practical interpretability, showing how team groupings shift around trade deadlines and season transitions. Limitations include the restriction to pairwise comparisons (no groupwise or contextual extensions), the assumption of a constant number of comparisons per pair for theoretical results, and the reliance on a preset candidate set for change points. Open questions include extending to k-wise comparisons, incorporating covariates into the BT model, and developing computationally efficient hyperparameter selection beyond cross-validation.
