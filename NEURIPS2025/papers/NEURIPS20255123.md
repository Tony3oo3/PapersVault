## Problem

Existing protein generative models cannot produce enzyme backbones that are simultaneously substrate-specific and functionally accurate: they either ignore substrate molecules during generation, randomly assign catalytic sites, or rely on post-hoc filtering that cannot tailor structures to a target substrate. Additionally, no high-quality, experimentally validated benchmark exists for evaluating enzyme backbone generation at the enzyme-family level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255123/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two inputs: (1) a set of MSA-annotated catalytic residues (the functional motif) identified via MAFFT alignment across enzymes sharing the same second-level EC number, and (2) the substrate as a 2D chemical graph (not a 3D conformer, since the binding pose is unknown a priori). The output is a full enzyme backbone as a chain of SE(3) residue frames; a protein sequence is then obtained via ProteinMPNN inverse folding and a predicted structure via ESMFold for evaluation.

## Method

EnzyControl extends the FrameFlow SE(3) flow-matching motif-scaffolding model with a lightweight EnzyAdapter that injects substrate awareness via cross-attention layers (Q from enzyme node features, K/V from substrate features) inserted at every transformer layer, without modifying the base IPA architecture. Substrate features are extracted by a frozen Uni-Mol graph encoder and mapped into the enzyme embedding space through a small trainable projector (two linear layers + LayerNorm). A two-stage training paradigm first trains only the projector and EnzyAdapter against the frozen base network to align substrate representations with enzyme structures, then fine-tunes the full model with LoRA while continuing adapter updates guided by the flow-matching generation loss. The training objective is a squared-error loss on SE(3) vector fields (translation and rotation components) conditioned on both the motif and substrate, enabling substrate-aware scaffold generation while preserving catalytic site geometry.

## Results

On EnzyBind, EnzyControl achieves a designability rate of 0.7160 (13% improvement over the second-best model Proteina at 0.6328), an EC match rate of 0.5041 (+10%), and a catalytic rate constant (kcat) of 2.9168 (+13% over Proteina's 2.4592). It also attains the best binding affinity (−6.93 kcal/mol, +3% over Proteina) and the highest success rate (0.1195, +23%), where success requires simultaneously matching the native EC number, achieving scTM > 0.5, scRMSD < 2 Å, and better binding affinity than the native enzyme. On EnzyBench, EnzyControl outperforms EnzyGen in binding affinity (−9.76 vs. −9.44 kcal/mol, +3%) and pLDDT (88.37 vs. 87.45), though its ESP score is marginally lower (0.63 vs. 0.65).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EnzyControl substantially outperforms EnzyGen (the closest enzyme-specific competitor) across nearly all metrics: designability improves from 0.0263 to 0.7160, kcat from 1.4142 to 2.9168, and success rate from 0.0125 to 0.1195, reflecting EnzyGen's post-hoc substrate filtering approach versus EnzyControl's substrate-aware generation. Against the strongest general-purpose baselines (Proteina, RFDiffusion, Chroma), EnzyControl leads on all functional metrics—EC match rate (0.5041 vs. 0.4583/0.0812/0.4579), kcat (2.9168 vs. 2.4592/2.3412/2.5325), and binding affinity (−6.93 vs. −6.35/−6.74/−6.73 kcal/mol)—while trailing on diversity and novelty, which the authors attribute to those models' larger, more heterogeneous training sets. On the EnzyBench benchmark, EnzyControl beats EnzyGen in binding affinity (−9.76 vs. −9.44 kcal/mol) and pLDDT (88.37 vs. 87.45) but is slightly behind on ESP score (0.63 vs. 0.65), a gap the authors note is within the 'strong interaction' threshold. The ablation confirms that both the MSA-based functional site annotation and the EnzyAdapter are critical: removing either degrades kcat by 11–16% and EC match rate by 5–6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends FrameFlow (SE(3) flow matching for protein backbone generation) by adding substrate-specific conditioning through a lightweight cross-attention adapter (EnzyAdapter) that injects Uni-Mol-encoded substrate features into each layer of the base network. It departs from EnzyGen, which uses substrate information only for post-hoc filtering of generated enzymes, by incorporating substrate awareness directly into the generative process. The two-stage training paradigm (freeze base → LoRA fine-tune with continued adapter updates) follows the parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm common in NLP and vision, applied here to the protein structure generation setting. The EnzyBind dataset, curated from PDBbind with MSA-annotated catalytic sites, addresses the absence of experimentally validated enzyme-substrate benchmarks with precise pocket structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in computational enzyme engineering: generating backbones that are simultaneously structurally designable, functionally aligned (correct EC number), and substrate-specific. The EnzyBind dataset (11,100 experimentally validated pairs) and the EC-family-aware evaluation protocol provide the community with a more rigorous benchmark than prior synthetic or sequence-only datasets. Key limitations include the single-chain, single-substrate scope, the absence of conformational modeling upon binding, and a noted tradeoff between diversity and designability. Open directions identified include multi-substrate conditioning, multimeric assembly, and docking-aware optimization integrated into the generative loop.
