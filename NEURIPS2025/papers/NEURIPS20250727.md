## Problem

State-of-the-art novel view synthesis relies heavily on 3D Gaussian Splatting, whose unbounded, non-homogeneous Gaussian kernels, while effective, limit interpretability and downstream manipulability. The paper asks whether simple, bounded polyhedral primitives with homogeneous density—specifically octahedra and tetrahedra—can serve as a viable alternative scene representation that achieves comparable reconstruction fidelity while offering a more intuitive, mesh-compatible geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250727/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and an SfM point cloud for initialization, operating at quarter resolution for Mip-NeRF 360 outdoor scenes, half resolution for indoor scenes, and full resolution for ScanNet++. Each octahedron is parameterized by 11 geometric floats (center, 3 symmetric axis distances, rotation quaternion, opacity) plus 48 spherical harmonic coefficients; tetrahedra require 12 geometric floats. The output is an explicit collection of transparent polyhedra that can render novel views at interactive rates (68 fps on ScanNet++, 29 fps on Mip-NeRF 360 for octahedra), extract depth maps via first-opacity-threshold, and be converted to surface meshes via TSDF fusion.

## Method

LinPrim replaces Gaussian kernels with transparent convex polyhedra (octahedra or tetrahedra) whose triangular faces bound a homogeneous-density volume. Rendering uses a two-stage GPU pipeline: per-primitive preprocessing constructs vertices from compact features and projects them into an affine ray-space approximation, followed by per-pixel rasterization that applies the fully differentiable Möller-Trumbore intersection test against each primitive's faces—since the primitives are convex, each ray intersects at most twice, and opacity is derived from the normalized distance between the two intersection points. Gradients from the L1+SSIM loss backpropagate through the blending, intersection, and projection stages to update center, corner distances, rotation quaternion, and opacity. Population control adapts 3DGS-style cloning and splitting (sampling new positions from a PDF aligned with the primitive's axis distances) and is also compatible with GS-MCMC densification. Anti-aliasing combines Mip-Splatting's 3D visibility filter with a custom 2D strategy that expands screen-space bounding boxes of primitives whose vertices lie on tile boundaries, acting as a coarse band-limit on high frequencies.

## Results

On ScanNet++ v2, LinPrim achieves a mean PSNR of 24.04 using only 255k octahedra, closely matching 3DGS (24.09 at 738k) and Mip-Splatting (24.12 at 977k) while using roughly 3× fewer primitives. On Mip-NeRF 360, LinPrim reaches 26.63 PSNR / 0.803 SSIM / 0.221 LPIPS with 1.79M primitives, compared to 3DGS's 27.43 / 0.813 / 0.217 at 3.32M. When combined with MCMC-based densification, LinPrim+MCMC improves to 24.41 PSNR at 255k on ScanNet++ (exceeding 3DGS at 738k) and 27.04 PSNR at 3.32M on Mip-NeRF 360. Rendering speed is real-time: 68 fps on ScanNet++ and 29 fps on Mip-NeRF 360 for octahedra, and 175 fps / 66 fps for tetrahedra.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS, LinPrim matches within 0.05 dB PSNR on ScanNet++ (24.04 vs 24.09) while using only 255k primitives versus 738k, and trails by 0.80 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 (26.63 vs 27.43) with 1.79M vs 3.32M primitives. Against Mip-Splatting, LinPrim is slightly lower on both datasets (−0.08 dB on ScanNet++, −1.16 dB on Mip-NeRF 360) but uses fewer primitives on ScanNet++ (255k vs 977k). Against 3D Convex Splatting (3DCS), LinPrim is 0.22 dB lower on ScanNet++ and 0.59 dB lower on Mip-NeRF 360, though 3DCS uses fewer primitives on Mip-NeRF 360 (1.02M vs 1.79M) while using more on ScanNet++ (440k vs 255k). Against GS-MCMC, LinPrim+MCMC is 0.09 dB lower on ScanNet++ at equal 255k population and 1.05 dB lower on Mip-NeRF 360 at equal 3.32M population, indicating that the MCMC population control strategy is more effective for Gaussian primitives than for polyhedral ones. Overall, LinPrim's key advantage is its compact representation: it achieves near-parity quality with 2–4× fewer primitives under default population control, and with MCMC densification it surpasses 3DGS on ScanNet++ at a fraction of the primitive budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

LinPrim departs from the dominant 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm by replacing elliptical, non-homogeneous Gaussian kernels with bounded polyhedral primitives (octahedra and tetrahedra) that have homogeneous density and triangular faces. It inherits the 3DGS optimization pipeline, tiling/sorting infrastructure, and loss formulation, adopts Mip-Splatting's 3D anti-aliasing filter, and integrates GS-MCMC population control, but the core rendering step is fundamentally different: instead of evaluating a Gaussian PDF per pixel, it performs differentiable Möller-Trumbore ray-triangle intersections against convex polyhedra. This positions it alongside Beyond Gaussians (linear but still elliptical kernels) and 3D Convex Splatting (smooth convexes), but with the distinction of using simple, symmetric, homogeneous volumes that are directly compatible with conventional triangle-mesh tooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that extremely simple bounded primitives (11 floats for geometry, matching a single Gaussian's parameter budget) can match 3DGS reconstruction quality while using roughly 3× fewer primitives, and that the hard geometric boundaries yield measurably better depth estimates and crisper reconstructions in view-dependent regions. However, the rendering pipeline is currently ~2–4× slower than the optimized Gaussian rasterizer, population control heuristics are adapted rather than designed for polyhedra, and the bounded nature introduces visible hard edges in sparsely observed regions. Open questions include whether specialized splitting/sampling strategies for polyhedra can close the remaining quality gap, whether the triangular faces enable direct mesh extraction without post-processing, and whether hardware-accelerated triangle rasterization can eliminate the current performance penalty.
