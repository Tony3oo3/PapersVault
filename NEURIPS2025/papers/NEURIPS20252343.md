## Problem

Structure-based generative models for drug design are typically specialized to a single molecular modality (small molecules, peptides, or antibodies), limiting cross-modality generalization and preventing a single model from leveraging the full diversity of atomic systems. This modality-specific design also restricts data sharing and makes it difficult to handle variable atom/residue counts and non-canonical amino acids within a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252343/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a target protein structure (voxelized at 1.0 Å resolution into a 4×32³ grid over C, O, N, S elements) and a one-hot modality class label (small molecule, macrocyclic peptide, or CDR loop) as conditioning signals. It outputs an all-atom 3D molecular structure represented as a continuous atomic density field over 8 element types (C, O, N, S, F, Cl, P, Br) in a 32 Å³ volume at 0.25 Å resolution, from which atom coordinates, bonds, and residue identities are recovered via peak detection, gradient ascent, and OpenBabel post-processing.

## Method

FuncBind is a two-stage latent score-based generative model. First, a 3D CNN encoder maps a low-resolution (16³) voxelized molecule into a spatially-arranged feature map latent (C×16³), which modulates a shared Multiplicative Filter Network decoder with Gabor filters to reconstruct the continuous atomic density field; this replaces the global embedding used in prior neural-field work, enabling compatibility with expressive U-Net denoisers and better local signal capture. Second, a 5B-parameter 3D U-Net denoiser (adapted from EDM2) is trained to denoise the latent conditioned on the target encoding, modality class, and noise level, using the EDM preconditioning and reweighting scheme. The model is trained jointly across all three modalities with rotation/translation data augmentation in place of SE(3) equivariance constraints, and samples are generated via either variance-exploding diffusion (EDM2 SDE sampler) or multimeasurement walk-jump sampling (WJS-16), both relying on the conditional Tweedie-Miyasawa formula to convert the denoiser into a score function.

## Results

On CrossDocked2020 small molecule generation, FuncBind achieves a VinaDock score of −7.26, QED of 0.50, and diversity of 0.70, competitive with state-of-the-art methods like VoxBind (VinaDock −8.30) and MolCraft (VinaDock −7.92). On SAbDab CDR loop inpainting, FuncBind achieves AAR of 86.9% (H1), 78.2% (H2), 47.5% (H3), and 80.8% (L3) with Cα RMSD of 0.41, 0.52, 2.04, and 0.68 Å respectively, outperforming all baselines by 1.5–3×. On the new MCP benchmark, FuncBind achieves L-RMSD of 2.6 Å, I-RMSD of 1.8 Å, and 41% of samples with better Vina docking scores than the seed, outperforming RFPeptide and AfCycDesign on structural fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On small molecule generation (CrossDocked2020), FuncBind is competitive but slightly trails VoxBind (VinaDock −8.30 vs −7.26) and MolCraft (−7.92) on docking scores, and has higher strain energy (217 vs 162/195) and more clashes (7.4 vs 5.1/7.1) than VoxBind. On CDR loop inpainting (SAbDab), FuncBind substantially outperforms all baselines: AAR of 86.9% on H1 vs 76.3% (AbDiffuser) and 65.8% (DiffAb), and Cα RMSD of 0.41 Å vs 1.58 Å (AbDiffuser) and 1.19 Å (DiffAb), a 1.5–3× improvement. For H3, FuncBind achieves 47.5% AAR and 2.04 Å RMSD, beating AbDiffuser (34.1%, 3.35 Å), AbX (30.3%, 3.41 Å), and dyMEAN (29.3%, 4.80 Å). On the MCP benchmark, FuncBind achieves the lowest L-RMSD (2.6 Å vs 12/7.6 Å) and I-RMSD (1.8 Å vs 3.3/3.7 Å) and highest Vina dock percentage (41% vs 8.8%/29%), but lower Tanimoto similarity (0.33 vs 0.31/0.34) and fewer residues with TS≥0.5 (25% vs 29%), expected given its access to non-canonical amino acids. In vitro, FuncBind achieves a 45% binding rate on a rigid epitope and 2–4% on a flexible epitope from 190 tested designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

FuncBind directly extends Kirchmeyer et al. (NeurIPS 2024), which introduced neural field representations for score-based 3D molecule generation, by replacing the global latent embedding with a spatially arranged feature map (borrowed from 3D occupancy networks and spatial functa in computer vision) and scaling the denoiser to a 5B-parameter 3D U-Net adapted from EDM2 (Karras et al., CVPR 2024). It also generalizes VoxBind (Pinheiro et al., ICML 2024) from a single-modality voxel-based small molecule generator to a unified framework spanning small molecules, macrocyclic peptides, and antibody CDR loops, while replacing SE(3) equivariance constraints with data augmentation. The walk-jump sampling component draws on Saremi & Hyvarinen's (JMLR 2019) probabilistic formulation of least-squares denoising, extended here to a conditional multimeasurement setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that a single 5B-parameter model can match or outperform modality-specific baselines across three chemically distinct drug classes, with in vitro validation showing 45% binding rate for de novo CDR H3 redesigns against a rigid epitope. The newly introduced ~190K-complex macrocyclic peptide benchmark fills a critical gap, as no prior target-conditioned MCP generative model handled non-canonical amino acids. Open questions include whether the unified approach transfers to additional modalities (e.g., nucleic acids, larger proteins), how performance scales further beyond 5B parameters, and whether the reliance on accurate target structures limits real-world applicability where structures are unavailable or uncertain.
