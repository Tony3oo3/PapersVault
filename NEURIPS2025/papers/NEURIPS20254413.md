## Problem

Vision Transformers suffer significant performance degradation on small-scale datasets due to their lack of architectural inductive biases, unlike CNNs which generalize well with limited data. Existing remedies either modify the ViT architecture with convolutional components, require access to large pretrained models, or match empirical weight distributions without imposing structural constraints on attention maps. The paper asks whether CNN-like inductive bias can be injected into ViTs purely through weight initialization, preserving architectural flexibility while improving small-data generalization.

## Input / Output

The method operates at the initialization stage before any training begins: it takes the randomly initialized positional encoding matrix P (of shape N×D, where N is the number of patches) as a data-independent pseudo-input and produces initialized Q_init and K_init matrices (each D×d, where d = D/h is the per-head dimension) for every attention head in every transformer block. During actual inference and training, the model consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs class labels, with no architectural or forward-pass modifications relative to a vanilla ViT.

## Method

The paper establishes a theoretical link (Proposition 1) showing that when input embeddings are rank-deficient (rank k ≪ D), any set of spatial convolution filters that is f²-k spanned can be matched by learning only channel-mixing weights, justifying the use of fixed random impulse filters as attention map priors. Concretely, for each attention head, the method constructs a target attention map approximating a random 3×3 impulse convolution matrix H_impulse, then solves P Q_init K_init^T P^T ≈ α H_impulse + β Z (where Z is Gaussian noise and P is the positional encoding) via SVD to obtain low-rank Q_init and K_init, followed by Frobenius-norm scaling with γ=2. The α:β ratio is set to 40:1 to ensure a clear impulse structure dominates the attention map. This initialization is applied identically across all transformer blocks and heads (with different random impulse patterns per head), requires no pretrained models, no architecture changes, and no data access, making it a drop-in replacement for standard truncated-normal initialization of Q and K.

## Results

On small and medium-scale datasets, the proposed impulse initialization consistently outperforms default ViT-Tiny initialization by 2–5% on medium-scale benchmarks (e.g., 77.02% vs. 71.67% on CIFAR-100, 81.85% vs. 77.95% on Food-101) and by up to 8–24% on small-scale benchmarks (e.g., 70.21% vs. 61.86% on STL-10, 50.84% vs. 26.58% on Pets). On the large-scale ImageNet-1K, the method maintains competitive performance, achieving 81.83% top-1 accuracy with ViT-Base (vs. 81.24% default) and 80.40% with ViT-Small (vs. 79.68% default), confirming that the convolutional inductive bias does not hinder scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the default timm initialization [31], the impulse method wins on every dataset, with margins growing as data shrinks: +3.90% on Food-101, +5.35% on CIFAR-100, +8.35% on STL-10, and +24.26% on Pets. Against mimetic initialization [28], the impulse method also wins on all six small/medium-scale datasets (e.g., +1.66% on CIFAR-10, +1.85% on STL-10), and critically, on ImageNet-1K with ViT-Base, mimetic degrades performance to 80.56% while impulse improves it to 81.83%, demonstrating that distribution-matching from pretrained models does not generalize to larger architectures. Against the weight-selection method from Xu et al. [33], the paper shows in Table 4 that pretraining ViT-Small on ImageNet-1K with impulse initialization and then transferring to ViT-Tiny achieves 87.43% on Food-101 and 97.19% on CIFAR-10, matching or exceeding the 21K-pretrained default baseline (87.14% and 97.07%) while using an order of magnitude less pretraining data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the empirical observation by Cazenavette et al. [3] that randomly initialized convolution filters match trained ones in ConvMixer and ResNet, and on the mimetic initialization framework of Trockman and Kolter [28] that replicates pretrained attention weight distributions. It departs from both the architectural-modification line (CoaTNet, CvT, UniFormer) that injects convolutions into ViT layers, and the pretrained-weight-sampling line (Xu et al. [33], Samragh et al. [23,24]) that requires access to large-scale pretrained models. The key conceptual departure is providing a theoretical justification (Proposition 1, M-k spanned sets) for why random impulse filters suffice, and translating this into a purely initialization-based strategy that imposes convolutional structure on attention maps without any architectural change or pretrained model dependency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a drop-in initialization that requires no pretrained model, no architecture modification, and no additional training compute, making it particularly relevant for domain-specific or resource-constrained settings where large-scale pretraining is impractical. Its consistent gains across ViT variants, Swin Transformers, and MLP-Mixer suggest broad applicability, and the finding that it preserves large-scale ViT performance (ImageNet-1K) while boosting small-scale results by up to 20% addresses a core practical pain point. Open questions include whether the limited number of heads in small ViTs (3 for ViT-Tiny) is sufficient to span the filter space, whether the positional-encoding pseudo-input loses influence in deeper layers, and whether extending the structured initialization to value projections and patch embeddings could yield further gains.
