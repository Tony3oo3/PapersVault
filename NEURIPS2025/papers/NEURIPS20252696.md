## Problem

Neural Probabilistic Circuits (NPCs) combine a neural attribute recognition model with a probabilistic circuit to produce interpretable image classifications, but the neural recognition module remains vulnerable to adversarial perturbations that can flip attribute predictions and compromise final class outputs. It was unclear how robustness decomposes across the two modules, and no principled inference procedure existed to make the overall model robust even when attribute predictions are perturbed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252696/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard input image (e.g., concatenated digit images for MNIST-Add3/5, synthesized face images for CelebA-Syn, or traffic sign images for GTSRB-Sub) and produces a normalized class probability distribution over the downstream task's label set. The prediction is interpretable: it is decomposed into per-attribute probability vectors from independent MLPs and a conditional class probability from the probabilistic circuit, so the final score for each class can be traced back to specific attribute instantiations and their learned dependencies.

## Method

RNPC retains NPC's architecture (independent per-attribute MLPs trained with cross-entropy, plus a probabilistic circuit learned via LearnSPN and CCCP) but replaces NPC's node-wise inference with a class-wise integration. The attribute space is partitioned into per-class high-probability regions V_y, and each class is assigned a neighborhood N(y,r) of radius r = ⌊(d_min−1)/2⌋ in Hamming distance. At inference, RNPC computes the probability mass falling within each class's neighborhood (from the recognition model) and multiplies it by the average conditional class probability over that class's high-probability nodes (from the circuit), then normalizes across classes. Theoretically, under an ε-DP assumption on the recognition model, RNPC's prediction perturbation bound is O(α_ε) while NPC's is O(|A_1|…|A_K|·α_ε/2), yielding an exponential improvement that does not scale with the number of attributes. RNPC also achieves lower inference complexity O(|S|·|V|) versus NPC's O(|S|·∏|A_k|) since it only queries the circuit over the high-probability set V rather than the full attribute space.

## Results

On benign inputs, all four models achieve near-perfect accuracy (98.5–100%) across the four datasets, with RNPC matching or slightly exceeding NPC (e.g., 99.51% vs 99.40% on MNIST-Add5). Under ∞-norm PGD attacks, the headline adversarial result is on MNIST-Add5 at a norm bound of 0.11: NPC's adversarial accuracy drops below 40% while RNPC maintains above 80%. On MNIST-Add3 (radius r=1), RNPC achieves up to 45% higher adversarial accuracy than the best baseline (NPC) when a single attribute is attacked, and 37% higher on MNIST-Add5 (r=2) when two attributes are attacked.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla CBM [Koh et al., ICML 2020] and DCR [Barbiero et al., ICML 2023], RNPC consistently achieves higher adversarial accuracy across all four datasets and all three attack types (∞-norm PGD, 2-norm PGD, 2-norm CW), while also matching or exceeding their benign accuracy. The gap is most pronounced on MNIST-Add3 and MNIST-Add5, where CBM and DCR suffer large accuracy drops under moderate norm bounds while RNPC remains above 80%. Against NPC [Chen et al., 2024], RNPC's advantage is dataset-dependent: on MNIST-Add5 at ∞-norm bound 0.11, RNPC maintains >80% adversarial accuracy versus NPC's <40%, a gap of over 40 percentage points. On CelebA-Syn, RNPC and NPC perform nearly identically due to the larger intrinsic radius (r=2) providing more robustness margin. On GTSRB-Sub, RNPC's advantage over NPC diminishes at larger norm bounds due to spurious correlations causing attack propagation (attacking one attribute inadvertently degrades others), though both still outperform CBM and DCR. RNPC also matches NPC's benign accuracy on three of four datasets, with a negligible 0.08% drop on GTSRB-Sub (99.49% vs 99.57%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Neural Probabilistic Circuits (NPCs) by Chen et al., which combine a neural attribute recognition model with a probabilistic circuit for interpretable image classification. It departs from the adversarial robustness line of work on concept bottleneck models (Sinha et al., Rasheed et al.) by not attempting to prevent concept probability perturbations via adversarial training, but instead designing an inference procedure (class-wise integration) that is robust even when attribute predictions are corrupted. The probabilistic circuit component draws on sum-product networks (Poon & Domingos) and is trained with LearnSPN/CCCP, while the theoretical analysis leverages differential privacy and total variation distance bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result that the probabilistic circuit is 'free' for robustness (NPC's robustness depends solely on the recognition module) provides a useful design principle for building interpretable models: one can swap in expressive reasoning modules without sacrificing adversarial guarantees. However, the approach is constrained by the complete information assumption, requires concept annotations, and the radius-based neighborhood structure becomes limiting on real-world datasets with small inter-class distances (e.g., original GTSRB has radius 0). Open questions include scaling to annotation-free datasets via multimodal concept transfer, relaxing conditional independence, and mitigating attack propagation from spurious correlations in the recognition module.
