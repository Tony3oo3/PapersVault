## Problem

Existing retrieval-based in-context learning methods train the retriever with a classification objective that labels the single best example as positive and all others as negative, yet at inference time examples are selected by score ranking. This train-test mismatch means classification-based retrievers fail to capture the broader relative utility across examples, particularly on challenging tasks where no single 'representative' example dominates. The paper asks whether formulating ICL example retrieval as a learning-to-rank problem can better align the retriever with the ranking-based selection process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a test query x and a corpus C of input-output pairs as in-context examples, using a DPR-style bi-encoder (BERT-base-uncased, ~110M params per encoder) to compute similarity scores. At inference, beam search (beam width w=3) sequentially retrieves K=3 examples, where each newly retrieved example is concatenated to the running context so later selections observe earlier ones. The output is an ordered K-shot sequence e_{[1:K]} prepended to x for the LLM. Preference training data is constructed as pairwise comparisons (e_k^w ≻ e_k^l | x̃_{k-1}) derived from LLM likelihood-ratio scoring of candidate examples at each sequential step.

## Method

SeDPO adapts Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to the dense retrieval setting by reparameterizing retrieval scores into a sequential policy via softmax over the corpus, then applying a Bradley-Terry pairwise ranking loss on the log-ratio of policy-to-reference similarity scores. The key technical contribution is a sequential relaxation: instead of modeling the full K-shot joint policy (whose partition function requires re-embedding the entire corpus at each step), the policy is factorized as ∏_k π(e_k | x̃_{k-1}), which cancels the partition function term (γ_w − γ_l = 0) and yields a tractable pairwise loss on individual example scores. Training data is built by greedily scoring L=50 candidate examples per step with a frozen LLM (GPT-Neo-2.7B) using an MCQ likelihood-ratio scoring function, then pairing top-T and bottom-T examples to form preference pairs with large score margins. The resulting retriever learns transitive partial orders over examples conditioned on the running context, capturing diverse query-dependent rationales for example utility rather than a single representative pattern.

## Results

SeDPO achieves the best average performance across all four task categories on 9 NLP tasks, with category averages of 77.9% (Paraphrase), 62.5% (Coreference/WSC), 72.0% (Reading), and 71.3% (NLI). The largest gains are on challenging tasks: PAWS accuracy improves from 54.7% (Se²) to 73.0% (+18.3%), and WSC accuracy from 55.1% to 62.5% (+7.4%). On the strongest baseline Se², SeDPO improves by 1.4%–7.4% depending on category, with the most consistent relative gains on tasks where classification-based retrieval underperforms random selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeDPO outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated setting. Against off-the-shelf retrievers, BM25 and SBERT lag by 15–20+ points on Paraphrase and NLI, confirming that learned retrieval is essential. Among finetuning-based methods, UPRISE (which scores examples independently) is surpassed by 4–8 points per category, while UDR (which adds local LambdaRank regularization) remains below Se² on average, showing that local rank signals are insufficient without global preference alignment. The strongest baseline Se² (sequential classification-based retrieval) is beaten by 1.4% on NLI up to 7.4% on Coreference and 18.3% on PAWS, with the gap largest on tasks where Se² underperforms random selection (MultiRC, WSC). On the math-inference AUQA task, SeDPO (27.56 at 8-shot) surpasses reasoning-specific retrievers CEIL (22.83), DQ-LoRe (25.20), and RGER (25.59) without any reasoning-oriented design. On the BEIR zero-shot retrieval benchmark, SeDPO achieves 0.0149 average nDCG@10 versus 0.0128 for DPR-BERT and 0.0066 for Se², though all fine-tuned retrievers show limited generalization to human-labeled documents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Se² (Liu et al., 2024), which introduced sequential example selection for ICL using a classification objective, and on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024), which reparameterizes reward functions for preference learning. The key departure is reframing in-context example retrieval from a classification problem (labeling the best example as positive, the rest as negative) to a learning-to-rank problem with pairwise preference constraints, motivated by the observation that inference retrieves by score ranking rather than by classification. The sequential relaxation from Se² is repurposed to make DPO tractable for retrieval, eliminating the need to compute the full corpus partition function at each training step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete train-test mismatch in ICL retrieval (classification training vs. ranking inference) and shows that a simple pairwise DPO loss resolves it, with consistent gains across 9 tasks and 4 LLMs of varying scale. The finding that Se² underperforms random selection on challenging tasks (MultiRC, WSC) while SeDPO consistently beats random is practically important, suggesting classification-based retrievers can be actively harmful when query-dependent diversity matters. Open questions include extending the approach to open-ended QA (where the label space makes the scoring function intractable), reducing reliance on a frozen scoring LLM, and whether the ranking formulation generalizes to non-MCQ settings without the clean likelihood-based supervision.
