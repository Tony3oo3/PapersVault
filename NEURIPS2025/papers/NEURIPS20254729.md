## Problem

Transformer skip-connection methods face a fundamental trade-off: feature-augmentation approaches (e.g., DenseFormer, ResFormer) improve representation but leave KV cache unchanged, while layer-replacement approaches (e.g., YOCO, CLA) reduce KV cache at the cost of weaker model quality. This paper asks whether a single skip-connection design can simultaneously enrich representations and reduce KV-cache/storage requirements, and if so, which parameters are redundant enough to replace and which intermediate features are critical to reuse.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text token sequence as input to a decoder-only Transformer and produces next-token probability distributions as output, identical in form to any autoregressive language model. The unusual aspect lies in the internal KV cache structure during decoding: instead of caching full Value tensors per layer, SkipV1Former caches only the first-half Value heads for every layer plus the second-half Value heads exclusively from layer 1, then concatenates them along the head dimension at each decoding step. This reduces the per-token KV cache by approximately 25% without altering the input format or output interface.

## Method

SkipV1Former modifies a standard H-head MHA Transformer by replacing half of each layer's Value heads (layers 2 through L) with the corresponding heads from layer 1, while computing the remaining half normally. This deterministic head-level substitution cuts Value projection parameters and V cache by ~50% (yielding ~25% total KV cache reduction) without changing head count or depth. The theoretical analysis frames Transformers as mesa-optimizers performing implicit gradient descent on a latent objective, showing that first-layer Values retain uncompressed token information that restores fidelity lost to the copying/compression mechanism in deeper layers, provably lowering prediction error by a constant c. The paper further provides an uptraining recipe that converts existing MHA checkpoints via mean-pooling over paired W_V heads with only 10–15% additional compute, and demonstrates seamless composition with GQA and MLA for further KV savings.

## Results

On LLaMA models scaled from 60M to 3B, SkipV1Former consistently outperforms the MHA Transformer baseline: at 3B it achieves a validation loss of 2.652 vs. 2.664 and perplexity of 14.18 vs. 14.35, while at 60M it reaches 3.504 vs. 3.549 (PPL 33.25 vs. 34.78). On GPT-2 (125M–550M), SkipV1Former matches ResFormer for the lowest validation loss and significantly outperforms MHA Transformer, DenseFormer, YOCO, and CLA in the same KV-cache-saving regime. All gains come with a ~25% reduction in KV cache and ~4% fewer parameters. On 3B downstream tasks, SkipV1Former achieves an average accuracy of 48.3%, surpassing the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MHA Transformer baseline, SkipV1Former wins at every scale tested (60M–3B LLaMA, 125M–550M GPT-2), with validation loss improvements of 0.03–0.05 on smaller models and consistent gains at 1B–3B. Compared to ResFormer (which interpolates first-layer Values into every head), SkipV1Former matches its final validation loss on GPT-2 but saves ~25% KV cache, whereas ResFormer offers no cache reduction. In the same KV-saving regime, SkipV1Former significantly outperforms both YOCO (V-only) and CLA (V-only) on GPT-2 validation loss, confirming that direct first-layer Value head injection is more effective than generic cross-layer KV sharing. When combined with GQA, SkipV1Former-GQA achieves a validation loss of 2.893 vs. 2.912 for Transformer-GQA while reducing KV bytes/token by 24.0%; with MLA, SkipV1Former-MLA reaches 2.888 vs. 2.896 for Transformer-MLA with a 44.4% KV reduction. On 3B downstream tasks, SkipV1Former edges out the baseline on BoolQ (60.3 vs. 55.5), PIQA (69.3 vs. 68.4), RTE (53.8 vs. 53.1), and SciQ (79.1 vs. 77.5), yielding a higher average accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkipV1Former sits at the intersection of two previously separate threads in Transformer efficiency research: feature-augmentation skip connections (DenseFormer, ResFormer, RealFormer) that improve representation without reducing KV cache, and layer-replacement methods (YOCO, CLA, LCKV) that cut KV cache at the cost of weaker representations. Conceptually, it is a discrete, head-level extension of ResFormer's scalar interpolation of first-layer Values, but the key departure is that by replacing entire heads rather than blending, it achieves actual KV cache savings (~25%) while preserving the representational benefit. The theoretical grounding draws on the mesa-optimization view of Transformers (Oswald et al., Dai et al., Mahankali et al.), formalizing why uncompressed first-layer Value signals specifically accelerate implicit gradient-descent steps in deeper layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a 25% KV cache reduction with simultaneous perplexity improvement, plus a 10–15% uptraining recipe that makes retrofitting existing MHA checkpoints feasible, and seamless composition with GQA/MLA for further savings. However, the theoretical analysis is confined to a simplified two-layer, no-residual linear-attention model, and the paper itself notes the method fails to help on non-autoregressive tasks, limiting generality. Open questions include whether kernel-level optimizations can close the small throughput gap, whether training-free transformations can replace the uptraining step, and whether the mesa-optimization theory extends rigorously to deep, residual-connected Transformers.
