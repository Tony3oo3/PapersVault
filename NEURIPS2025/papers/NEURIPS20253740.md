## Problem

Automated petrographic mineral classification has been hindered by the absence of large-scale, publicly available datasets with dual-polarization imaging and expert annotations. Existing resources contain only hundreds to a few thousand images, cover at most 16 mineral classes, are often restricted or unavailable, and lack reproducible baseline models. This paper addresses these gaps by introducing LITHOS, the largest publicly available petrographic dataset (211,604 patches, 105,802 annotated grains, 25 classes), along with a benchmark and a dual-polarization baseline model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253740/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired 256×256 RGB patches extracted from thin sections imaged at 40× magnification (≈3.5 μm per pixel) under plane-polarized light (PPL, 0°) and cross-polarized light (XPL, 0°). Each patch is centered at the intersection of the expert-annotated major and minor axes of a single mineral grain. The model outputs a mineral class label in either a binary setting (Quartz vs. Non-Quartz, aggregating three quartz subtypes) or a 25-class multi-class setting, evaluated with macro-averaged metrics to account for the dataset's strong long-tail imbalance (≈50% quartz).

## Method

The LITHOS Baseline is a dual-encoder transformer that fuses polarization-specific features. Two ViT encoders are first pretrained independently on PPL-only and XPL-only subsets of LITHOS, then frozen to preserve complementary optical representations. A dual-decoder module, inspired by cross-modality transformers, applies two consecutive layers of self-attention within each stream and cross-attention between streams, followed by a feed-forward network. The two decoder outputs are combined via a single learnable scalar α in a weighted sum (F_combined = αF₁ + (1−α)F₂), and the [CLS] token of the fused representation is passed through a linear classification head trained with cross-entropy loss. Only ~10% of the model's ~674M parameters are unfrozen during training, keeping the computational cost moderate while leveraging the rich frozen polarization-specific features.

## Results

On the multi-class 25-mineral classification task, the LITHOS Baseline achieves 62.3% accuracy, 51.9% macro-precision, and 43.2% macro-F1, outperforming the best single-polarization model (Swin Transformer with XPL: 60.4% accuracy, 47.2% precision, 42.4% F1). On the binary Quartz vs. Non-Quartz task, the LITHOS Baseline reaches 85.1% accuracy and 86.1% F1, surpassing the best single-modality baseline (Swin Transformer XPL: 84.3% accuracy, 85.5% F1). The dual-polarization advantage is most pronounced in precision, where the LITHOS Baseline gains approximately 4.7 points over the strongest single-modality competitor in the multi-class setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The LITHOS Baseline is compared against four single-polarization architectures (ResNet, GoogLeNet, ViT, Swin Transformer), each evaluated independently under PPL and XPL. In the multi-class task, the LITHOS Baseline wins on accuracy (+1.9 pts over Swin-XPL), precision (+4.7 pts), and F1 (+0.8 pts), but trails Swin-XPL slightly on macro-recall (0.415 vs. 0.421). In the binary task, it leads on F1 (+0.6 pts), precision (+1.3 pts), and accuracy (+0.8 pts) over Swin-XPL, while Swin-XPL retains a marginal recall edge (0.892 vs. 0.888). XPL consistently outperforms PPL across all architectures, confirming that cross-polarized light carries more discriminative mineral information. The gap between the best single-modality model and the dual-encoder LITHOS Baseline is largest in precision, suggesting that the cross-attention fusion mechanism primarily reduces false positives by cross-validating features across polarization modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground primarily as a dataset and benchmark contribution rather than a methodological departure. Prior automated petrography work has employed CNNs, U-Nets, Mask2Former, and dual-modal transformers (e.g., the Multi-channel Attention Transformer of Ren et al. and Trans-SedNet of Zheng et al.), but these efforts were constrained by small, non-public, or partially reproducible datasets. The LITHOS Baseline architecture repurposes the cross-modality transformer design (self-attention + cross-attention decoders, as in LXMERT) applied to frozen, polarization-specialized ViT encoders, a combination not previously explored in the petrographic imaging literature. The paper explicitly positions itself against the reproducibility gap: prior dual-polarization models lacked public code or pretrained weights, and existing datasets were 1–2 orders of magnitude smaller.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LITHOS is the largest publicly available petrographic dataset to date (211K paired patches, 105K expert annotations, 25 classes), and its open release with code and pretrained models addresses a critical reproducibility bottleneck that has limited the field. The consistent but modest gains from dual-polarization fusion over single-modality baselines validate the domain intuition that PPL and XPL carry complementary information, yet the long-tail class imbalance (quartz dominates ~50% of annotations) remains a significant open challenge, with rare classes achieving F1 below 0.2. Future work could leverage the full major/minor axis paths for richer geometric supervision, apply class-balancing strategies, or extend the benchmark beyond Colombian soil samples to broader geological contexts.
