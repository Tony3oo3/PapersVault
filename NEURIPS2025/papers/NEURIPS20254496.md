## Problem

Online free-viewpoint video reconstruction via streaming 3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from three coupled bottlenecks: redundant deformation-field storage caused by the mismatch between uniform per-Gaussian motion encoding and the inherently hierarchical structure of physical motion, progressive error accumulation from incremental per-frame updates, and the absence of a mechanism to dynamically trade reconstruction fidelity against storage under fluctuating resource budgets. Existing streaming methods either rely on auxiliary optical-flow networks that inflate training cost or employ static anchor strategies that cannot adapt to evolving scene geometry.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-view video sequence (18–31 synchronized cameras, 150–300 frames at 30 FPS, resolutions ranging from 640×360 on PanopticSports to 2704×2028 on N3DV) together with calibrated camera poses, processing frames strictly in temporal order in a streaming fashion. It produces a compact streamable 3DGS representation consisting of a frozen base Gaussian set from frame 0 plus per-frame hierarchical deformation parameters (three levels of anchor-level rigid transforms), yielding novel-view rendered frames at approximately 250 FPS with a per-frame storage footprint of 0.40–0.44 MB—roughly 50% less than the closest streaming baselines at comparable PSNR.

## Method

ReCon-GS decomposes scene motion into a three-level hierarchy of rigid-body transforms governed by Anchor Gaussians placed via grid-based farthest-point sampling, so that coarse levels capture global shared motion while the finest level resolves per-Gaussian residual deformation; General Gaussians inherit blended motion from their nearest anchor at each level, and appearance attributes (SH coefficients, opacity) are frozen to keep storage minimal. A Dynamic Hierarchy Reconfiguration step periodically re-samples anchors to track evolving geometry, and Intra-hierarchical Deformation Inheritance propagates translational and rotational priors from the three nearest legacy anchors at the same hierarchy level to prevent temporal discontinuities. Training follows a decoupled two-phase schedule—Phase 1 optimizes only the deformation fields while freezing base Gaussian geometry, and Phase 2 activates view-based densification to recover high-frequency details—enabling a storage-aware rate-distortion trade-off by simply adjusting the anchor density at each hierarchy level.

## Results

On the N3DV dataset, ReCon-GS achieves 32.66 dB PSNR, 0.957 SSIM, and 0.123 LPIPS among online methods, outperforming the closest competitor HiCoM (32.08 dB) by 0.58 dB while using less storage (0.44 MB vs. 0.69 MB) and training faster (6.4 s vs. 6.6 s). On PanopticSports, ReCon-GS reaches 29.33 dB PSNR with only 0.8 MB storage, reducing memory by ~60% compared to HiCoM (2.11 MB) and ~90% compared to 3DGStream (8.1 MB). On Technicolor, ReCon-GS attains the best PSNR (33.83 dB) and lowest storage (0.82 MB) across all compared methods, including offline approaches. Rendering speed is competitive at 250 FPS on N3DV and 410 FPS on PanopticSports.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest streaming baseline HiCoM [3], ReCon-GS consistently wins on PSNR across all four datasets (32.66 vs. 32.08 on N3DV, 30.84 vs. 29.57 on Meet Room, 29.33 vs. 29.17 on PanopticSports, 33.83 vs. not reported on Technicolor) while also reducing storage by 36% on N3DV (0.44 vs. 0.69 MB) and 62% on PanopticSports (0.8 vs. 2.11 MB). Compared to 3DGStream [6], ReCon-GS improves PSNR by 1.31 dB on N3DV and 6.31 dB on PanopticSports while cutting storage by ~94% (0.44 vs. 7.8 MB). Against QUEEN-1 [4], ReCon-GS gains 0.47 dB PSNR on N3DV with 41% less storage (0.44 vs. 0.75 MB) and 19% faster training. Notably, ReCon-GS matches or exceeds the best offline method SplineGS [47] in PSNR on N3DV (32.66 vs. 32.60) while being an online streaming method, and on Technicolor it surpasses the offline Ex4DGS [34] by 0.21 dB PSNR with 71% less storage (0.82 vs. 2.81 MB). The only metric where ReCon-GS is slightly behind is rendering FPS on N3DV (250 vs. 255 for HiCoM), a negligible 2% difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReCon-GS extends the streaming 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction paradigm, building directly on 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) as the base representation and on HiCoM (Gao et al., NeurIPS 2024) for its hierarchical motion decomposition. The key departures are: (1) replacing HiCoM's uniform anchor sampling with grid-based farthest point sampling to align anchors with scene geometry, (2) introducing Dynamic Hierarchy Reconfiguration with intra-hierarchical deformation inheritance to combat anchor drift over long sequences, and (3) reframing the optimization as a storage-aware fidelity problem analogous to rate-distortion theory, enabling controllable quality-memory trade-offs. The method also decouples geometric deformation from appearance parameters (freezing SH and opacity), a design choice that prior streaming methods like 3DGStream and 4DGC do not exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >50% storage reduction at equivalent rendering quality addresses a critical bottleneck for practical deployment of streaming FVV in XR/AR/VR and live broadcasting, where memory budgets are tightly constrained. The rate-distortion optimization framing provides a principled mechanism for adapting reconstruction fidelity to available resources, which prior streaming methods lack. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: it is not optimized for monocular inputs, and like other streaming architectures, it struggles with abrupt object emergence or dissolution requiring instant structural adaptation. Open questions include whether the 3-level hierarchy generalizes to scenes with extreme motion complexity and whether the periodic re-hierarchization interval can be made fully adaptive rather than fixed.
