## Problem

The interpretability community has increasingly relied on proxy modules such as sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to discover features in LLMs, yet it remains unclear whether these learned features offer meaningful advantages over the feature vectors already encoded in the original feed-forward (FF) layers. This paper systematically asks whether proxy-based decomposition empirically benefits model interpretation compared to directly analyzing FF key-value memories using modern interpretability benchmarks like SAEBENCH.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255724/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes text tokens from OpenWebText and The Pile, processes them through five LLMs (Gemma-2-2B/9B, Llama-3.1-8B, GPT-2, Pythia-70M), and extracts feature activations either from FF layers (treating SwiGLU-gated key activations as features and W_V rows as feature vectors) or from pretrained SAEs/Transcoders. Outputs include interpretability scores across eight SAEBENCH metrics, human-annotated feature quality labels (superficial/conceptual/uninterpretable), and cosine-similarity-based alignment statistics between FF-KV and Transcoder feature sets. The FF-KV implementation uses SAEBENCH's custom_sae class with hook-based injection to faithfully reproduce FF forward passes.

## Method

The core contribution is implementing FF layers as 'pseudo-SAEs' within the SAEBENCH evaluation framework, where the FF's internal neuron activations serve as feature activations and the W_V rows serve as feature vectors, enabling direct comparison with SAEs under identical metrics. Two variants are introduced: TopK FF-KV, which applies a top-k sparsity constraint to FF activations to match SAE sparsity conventions, and Normalized FF-KV, which L2-normalizes W_V rows and compensates by scaling activations with the original vector norms. The paper evaluates across five models and multiple layers using SAEBENCH's eight metrics (Feature Alive Rate, Explained Variance, Absorption, Sparse Probing, Auto-Interpretation, SCR, RAVEL-ISO, RAVEL-CAU), supplemented by human evaluation of 200 randomly sampled features and a feature alignment analysis measuring max-cosine similarity between FF-KV and Transcoder decoder vectors. The key finding is that FF-KV and SAE scores fall in similar ranges across all metrics, with FF-KV showing better absorption (less feature redundancy), while 41–66% of features between FF-KV and Transcoder remain unaligned, suggesting proxy modules may hallucinate features rather than faithfully translating original FF workings.

## Results

Across SAEBENCH's eight metrics on Gemma-2-2B (layer 13) and Llama-3.1-8B (layer 16), FF-KV and SAE scores fall in a similar range with absolute differences typically smaller than seed/layer variance. FF-KV achieves perfect explained variance (1.000) by construction and near-zero absorption (0.000), indicating less feature redundancy than SAEs (0.087–0.097). On sparse probing, FF-KV scores 0.827 (Gemma-2-2B) and 0.876 (Llama-3.1-8B), closely matching SAEs (0.846 and 0.879). SAEs show a small but consistent edge on auto-interpretation (0.782 vs. 0.710 on Gemma-2-2B) and SCR (0.170 vs. 0.041), while RAVEL-Isolation is comparable (0.985 vs. 0.952).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gemma-Scope SAEs [30], FF-KV matches or slightly underperforms on auto-interpretation (0.710 vs. 0.782) and SCR (0.041 vs. 0.170), but achieves comparable sparse probing (0.827 vs. 0.846) and RAVEL-Isolation (0.952 vs. 0.985), while decisively winning on absorption (0.000 vs. 0.087), indicating less feature redundancy. Against Llama-Scope SAEs [21], the pattern is similar: FF-KV ties on sparse probing (0.876 vs. 0.879) and RAVEL-Isolation (0.955 vs. 0.993), trails slightly on auto-interpretation (0.751 vs. 0.817) and SCR (0.048 vs. 0.219), but again dominates on absorption (0.000 vs. 0.097). Against Transcoders [12], FF-KV and TC show nearly identical scores across all metrics (e.g., sparse probing 0.827 vs. 0.854; RAVEL-ISO 0.952 vs. 0.940), and human evaluation confirms annotators cannot distinguish their features (origin-judgment accuracy 0.13 for SAE, 0.18 for TC, vs. 0.86 for FF-KV which is identifiable by activation magnitude patterns). Critically, the feature-alignment analysis reveals that 41% of FF-KV features and 66% of Transcoder features have no aligned counterpart in the other module, suggesting proxy modules may hallucinate features rather than faithfully translating the original FF's key-value structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Geva et al.'s (2021) interpretation of feed-forward layers as key-value memories, which established that FF value vectors encode interpretable concepts. It positions this organic decomposition against the now-dominant proxy-based paradigm of sparse autoencoders (SAEs) and Transcoders, which were developed to address polysemanticity and superposition. The key departure is methodological: rather than proposing a new feature-discovery method, the paper applies the recently developed SAEBENCH evaluation suite to FF-KV features for the first time, treating them as a strong baseline that the field has overlooked. The Transcoder [12] is identified as the closest existing counterpart to FF-KV analysis, and the paper's alignment analysis in Section 6 extends that comparison to a feature-level granularity not previously examined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that FF-KV features achieve comparable interpretability to SAEs across all eight SAEBENCH metrics challenges the implicit assumption that proxy-based decomposition is necessary for meaningful interpretation, with practical implications for compute costs and methodological simplicity. More critically, the discovery that 41–66% of Transcoder features are unaligned with FF-KV features raises a faithfulness question: are proxy modules translating the original model's representations or hallucinating new ones? This remains an open question the paper explicitly flags for future work, alongside the need for more diverse SAE/Transcoder configurations and broader model coverage. The paper's limitations—single annotator for human evaluation, fixed SAE widths, and limited Transcoder availability—mean the faithfulness concern is suggestive rather than conclusive.
