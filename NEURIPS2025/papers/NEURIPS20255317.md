## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models cannot effectively process ultra-high-resolution (UHR) remote sensing imagery due to two bottlenecks: (1) the absence of UHR image-text training data (current RS datasets average below 1K×1K resolution), and (2) token explosion from 8K×8K inputs that causes out-of-memory errors and dilutes semantically critical object tokens amid vast homogeneous background regions. Even leading models like GPT-4o and Qwen-VL are capped at 4K resolution and score below 0.45 accuracy on the XLRS-Bench.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255317/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single UHR remote sensing image at up to 8,064×8,064 pixels (processed as a 24×24 grid of ~336×336 patches) paired with a natural-language question, and produces a text answer in multiple-choice or free-form format. After the two-stage token compression, each 24×24 grid yields only 24 retained visual tokens (a 24× compression over the original 576 tokens per grid), keeping total visual tokens around 14K for a full 8K image. The 7B-parameter model is evaluated zero-shot on XLRS-Bench (3,040 VQA samples across 13 sub-tasks) and generalizes to the external LRS-VQA benchmark.

## Method

GeoLLaVA-8K is built by expanding LLaVA-Next-2K from a 7×7 to a 24×24 visual grid and applying a two-step token compression between the CLIP-ViT encoder and the multimodal projector. First, Background Token Pruning performs iterative local clustering: each token is assigned to a neighboring token with a learned probability, and overlapping clusters are merged, effectively collapsing redundant background regions (ocean, forest) into single representative tokens. Second, Anchored Token Selection reads the [CLS]-to-patch attention map from the second-to-last ViT layer and retains only the top-R tokens by attention score, preserving semantically rich object tokens that would otherwise be lost. The model is then full-parameter SFT-trained for one epoch on 81K image-text pairs from the newly curated SuperRS-VQA (12K samples, avg 8,376×8,376, manually annotated) and HighRS-VQA (69K samples, avg 2,000×1,912, semi-automatically generated via GPT-4o with LESS-based influence-function data selection), using ZeRO-2 parallelism with differential learning rates (1e-6 for vision, 5e-6 for projector/LLM).

## Results

On XLRS-Bench (L-3 VQA, 3,040 samples at ~8K×8K resolution), GeoLLaVA-8K (7B) achieves 51.5% average accuracy, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses all open- and closed-source MLLMs. Notably, this 7B model outperforms the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (50.2%) and InternVL3-78B (49.2%) by 1.3 and 2.3 points respectively. On the external LRS-VQA benchmark, GeoLLaVA-8K also achieves 56.28% accuracy, outperforming LLaVA-Next (55.07%) by 1.21 points, confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoLLaVA-8K (7B) achieves the highest average accuracy (51.5%) on XLRS-Bench, outperforming all 12 baselines. It beats the strongest open-source models—Qwen2.5-VL-72B (50.2%) and InternVL3-78B (49.2%)—by 1.3 and 2.3 points respectively, despite having roughly 10× fewer parameters. Among closed-source models, it surpasses Gemini 2.0 Flash (48.7%) by 2.8 points, Claude 3.7 Sonnet (40.5%) by 11.0 points, and GPT-4o (32.4%) by 19.1 points. The domain-specific GeoChat baseline (22.9%) is outperformed by a margin of 28.6 points, highlighting the gap between prior RS MLLMs and the proposed UHR-capable model. The largest per-subtask gains appear in Route Planning (66.0 vs. 43.0 for Gemini 2.0 Flash) and Overall Land Use Classification (49.0 vs. 39.0 for Qwen2.5-VL-72B), suggesting the token compression strategies are particularly effective for tasks requiring holistic scene understanding and long-range spatial reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoLLaVA-8K extends the LLaVA-Next-2K architecture (itself a variant of LLaVA-Next) by scaling its visual grid from 7×7 to 24×24 to accommodate 8K×8K remote sensing inputs, and introduces two RS-specific token compression strategies—Background Token Pruning via adaptive clustering and Anchored Token Selection via [CLS] attention—that are motivated by the paper's empirical finding that RS imagery exhibits extreme background redundancy. The influence-based data selection pipeline for the MHR dataset directly adopts the LESS framework's gradient-projection and cosine-similarity formulation. Conceptually, the paper departs from general high-resolution MLLM approaches (LLaVA-Next patches, Monkey, LLaVA-UHD, Cambrian, SliME) by exploiting domain-specific structure: rather than generic patch compression, it identifies and removes semantically redundant background tokens (ocean, forest) and anchors retention on object-relevant [CLS] attention, a strategy grounded in logit-lens and ablation analyses unique to the RS setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first RS MLLM to natively process 8K×8K inputs, and the accompanying SuperRS-VQA and HighRS-VQA datasets fill a critical gap—no prior RS training data exceeded ~1K resolution. The counterintuitive finding that pruning 50% of background tokens *improves* accuracy challenges the default assumption that more visual tokens are always beneficial, and could inform token-efficient design in other domains with high spatial redundancy. Key limitations include evaluation primarily on a single benchmark (XLRS-Bench, 3,040 samples), restriction to optical imagery (no SAR or multispectral), and the CLS-attention mechanism's tendency to favor a single dominant region over multi-object scenes. Open questions include whether the background-pruning insight generalizes beyond RS, how the model performs on other sensor modalities, and whether the 7B model's advantage over 72B competitors reflects genuine domain adaptation or benchmark-specific tuning.
