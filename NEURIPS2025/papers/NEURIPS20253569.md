## Problem

Existing robust statistics methods assume a uniform corruption rate across all samples, but real-world data from distributed or federated sources naturally exhibits heterogeneous corruption levels. This paper asks: what are the tight minimax rates for robust mean estimation and linear regression when each sample is independently corrupted with a known but non-identical probability, and how should an optimal estimator allocate information across samples of differing reliability?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253569/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n samples Z = (Z_1, ..., Z_n) from a distribution P, along with a known corruption rate vector λ ∈ [0,1]^n specifying the per-sample corruption probability (exact knowledge assumed, though constant-factor approximations suffice). For mean estimation it outputs an estimated mean μ̂ ∈ R^d with error measured in squared ℓ_2 norm; for linear regression it outputs coefficients β̂ ∈ R^d with error in the affine-invariant ‖·‖_Σ norm. The key structural constraint is that the estimator must decide, based solely on the known λ vector, how much weight to assign to each sample or whether to discard it entirely.

## Method

The central insight is that the minimax rate is governed by f(λ, k) = min_{t∈[0,1]} (k/N(t) + t²), where N(t) = |{i : λ_i ≤ t}|, implying the optimal estimator thresholds at some corruption level t and discards samples above it. For bounded distributions, the estimator is a weighted sample mean with weights w_i = (β − c(w^Tλ)λ_i)_+ solved via KKT conditions in near-linear time (Algorithm 1). For multivariate Gaussians and linear regression, the paper introduces weighted Tukey depth and weighted regression depth, where per-sample weights downweight more corrupted observations; the thresholding special case sets uniform weights on samples with λ_i ≤ t and zero otherwise. Lower bounds combine Le Cam's two-point method (bounded, univariate Gaussian) with Assouad's method over {−1,1}^{√d} hypercube hypotheses (multivariate Gaussian, regression), with an adversarial strategy that renders samples with λ_i ≥ 1 − e^{−δ√d} information-free by simulating the pointwise maximum of the hypothesis densities.

## Results

The paper establishes tight minimax rates for robust estimation under heterogeneous corruption rates. For bounded multivariate distributions, the minimax rate is r² · min_t(1/N(t) + t²), where N(t) = |{i : λ_i ≤ t}|, and this is achieved by a thresholding estimator in near-linear time. For univariate Gaussian mean estimation, the rate min_t(1/N(t) + t²) is tight (up to constants). For multivariate Gaussian mean estimation and linear regression, the paper achieves rates within a factor of √d: d^{-1/2}·min_t(d/N(t) + t²) ≲ rate ≲ min_t(d/N(t) + t²). The key structural finding is that the optimal estimator discards samples beyond a corruption threshold t* determined by the empirical distribution of λ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its heterogeneous robust estimators against the standard homogeneous robust statistics baselines: the sample mean (minimax optimal under uniform Huber contamination for bounded distributions) and the sample median / 1D Tukey median (minimax optimal for univariate Gaussian). In the preliminary synthetic experiments (Appendix A, Figure 2), both the thresholding method and the per-sample reweighting method outperform these homogeneous baselines across all corruption levels, with the gap widening as heterogeneity increases. The reweighting method (Algorithm 1) provides a marginal improvement over the simpler thresholding method in the bounded and univariate Gaussian settings, though the paper notes this advantage is small and unclear whether it persists in high dimensions. The theoretical contribution is that the homogeneous baselines are provably suboptimal in the heterogeneous setting: the minimax rate depends on the full distribution of corruption rates λ, not just their average, and the optimal threshold t* adapts to the empirical CDF of λ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Huber contamination model by allowing each sample to have its own corruption probability, departing from the homogeneous corruption assumption that underlies virtually all prior robust statistics work. The estimators are built on weighted versions of Tukey's depth (for mean estimation) and Gao's regression depth (for linear regression), where the weights encode the heterogeneity in corruption rates. The paper also positions itself relative to semi-verified learning and list-decodable estimation, showing these as special cases of the heterogeneous corruption framework, and uses Le Cam's and Assouad's methods to construct lower bounds that jointly capture both corruption heterogeneity and dimensionality—something standard two-stage lower bound techniques cannot do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical insight—that the optimal estimator thresholds samples at a corruption level determined by the empirical distribution of rates—gives a clear, implementable recipe for handling data from heterogeneous sources (federated learning, crowdsourcing, sensor networks). The √d gap between upper and lower bounds for multivariate Gaussians and regression is a significant open question: it suggests that in high dimensions, the noisier samples discarded by thresholding may carry exploitable information, and the authors' per-sample reweighting approach (weighted Tukey/regression depth) is proposed as a potential path to closing this gap. The paper also acknowledges that its upper bound is not tight in the semi-verified learning regime, where dimension-independent rates are already known, indicating that fundamentally new robustness measures may be needed for the high-dimensional heterogeneous setting.
