## Problem

In model cascade setups, smaller local models often defer too many or too few requests to larger remote models, leading to either wasted compute or degraded joint accuracy. Existing deferral strategies rely on post-hoc confidence thresholds or separate routing networks without explicitly training the small model to recognize its own errors. The paper asks: can we fine-tune the small model so that its confidence reliably separates correct from incorrect predictions, thereby maximizing deferral performance without architectural modifications?

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for TinyImageNet/Food-101), text prompts for closed-form LLM tasks (ARC, MMLU, GSM8K), or image+text pairs for vision-language tasks (VQAv2, AI2D, COCO Captions, Screen2Words). Beyond the standard prediction (class label, token sequence, or caption), the system produces a scalar deferral signal: max softmax confidence for classification models or mean negative predictive entropy across token positions for sequence models. A threshold τ on this signal determines whether the small model's output is accepted or the input is routed to the large model, making the deferral decision an intrinsic part of the inference pipeline rather than a separate routing module.

## Method

GATEKEEPER is a correctness-aware fine-tuning loss applied to an already-trained small model M_S in a single forward pass per mini-batch. It computes two binary masks on-the-fly (correct vs. incorrect predictions) and assembles a hybrid loss L = α·L_corr + (1−α)·L_incorr, where L_corr is standard cross-entropy on correctly predicted samples (sharpening their logits) and L_incorr is KL divergence to the uniform distribution on incorrectly predicted samples (flattening their confidence toward maximum entropy). For token-based models (LLMs, VLMs), the loss is applied per-token position, ensuring each mispredicted token is pushed toward uniform while correct tokens are reinforced. At inference, a gating function (max softmax for classifiers, mean negative predictive entropy for sequences) with a tunable threshold τ implements selective prediction: inputs above τ are answered by M_S, others are deferred to M_L. The hyperparameter α directly controls the trade-off between preserving M_S's raw accuracy and improving its deferral reliability, with lower α values yielding stronger correct/incorrect separation at the cost of some overall accuracy.

## Results

GATEKEEPER improves deferral performance ($s_d$) over an untuned baseline by 0.72× on CIFAR-100 and 2× on TinyImageNet (image classification), and by 7× on ARC-e and 10× on ARC-c (language modeling). The method is controlled by a hyperparameter α: lower α values push incorrect predictions toward uniform confidence, yielding stronger correct/incorrect separation (lower $s_o$) and higher deferral performance, but at the cost of reduced small-model accuracy over the full data distribution. Across all three model classes (encoder-only CNNs, decoder-only Gemma LMs, and encoder-decoder PaliGemma VLMs), the same trend holds—GATEKEEPER consistently moves the realized deferral curve closer to the ideal oracle curve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Narasimhan et al. (2022), GATEKEEPER achieves superior deferral performance and correct/incorrect confidence separation, but Narasimhan's method preserves the small model's raw accuracy; however, it requires an explicit architectural change (an added prediction head or a separate routing network) to estimate the expert's correctness, whereas GATEKEEPER needs only a loss-function swap with no architectural modification. Against Gupta et al. (2024) (token-level log-probability quantiles for LLM cascades), GATEKEEPER outperforms on both distributional overlap and deferral performance on ARC-e/c, MMLU, and GSM8K, again trading some overall utility for better deferral. Two black-box prompting baselines—'Reduce Confidence' (appending an instruction to lower confidence) and 'Answer N' (instructing the model to output 'N' when uncertain)—fail to reliably improve deferral, consistent with prior findings that LLMs remain overconfident under such prompts. Overall, GATEKEEPER wins on deferral quality across all tested settings but concedes a measurable drop in small-model accuracy, a trade-off directly governed by α.

## Relation to Prior Work

GATEKEEPER repurposes the KL-to-uniform confidence-flattening term from Outlier Exposure (Hendrycks et al., 2018)—originally designed for OOD detection—toward in-distribution misclassifications in a cascade deferral setting, combining it with standard cross-entropy on correct predictions under a tunable α trade-off. It departs from prior cascade work (Narasimhan et al., 2022; Gupta et al., 2024) that requires explicit estimation of expert correctness via added prediction heads or separate routing networks, instead achieving deferral purely through confidence calibration of the small model with no architectural modifications. The method also extends the model cascading literature from classification and NLP to vision-language encoder-decoder models, where prior deferral research has been largely absent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GATEKEEPER offers a drop-in loss function that practitioners can add to any existing fine-tuning pipeline to improve cascade deferral without architectural changes, making it broadly applicable across encoder-only, decoder-only, and encoder-decoder architectures. The α hyperparameter provides an explicit, interpretable knob for trading off small-model accuracy against deferral quality, which is critical for deployment decisions. Key open questions include whether token-level matching in language tasks should be replaced by semantic-level evaluation, whether multi-stage cascades (K > 2) benefit similarly, and whether the large model could also be jointly tuned to further improve the joint system. The reliance on a generative model (Gemini) for captioning evaluation introduces a potential circularity that limits the strength of the VLM captioning results.
