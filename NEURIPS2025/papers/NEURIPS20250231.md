## Problem

Long video understanding requires simultaneously perceiving dense local details and digesting global contexts, but existing VLMs are constrained by context length (forcing sparse frame sampling or aggressive token compression) and existing video agents rely on sequential key-frame retrieval that breaks parallelism and assumes a small number of key events. This paper addresses the gap by formally introducing the MapReduce principle from distributed systems as a framework for long video understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250231/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes hour-long videos (30 min to 2 hrs) and a text question as input, with the VLM explicitly constrained to perceive fewer than 40 frames per query (far below the 256+ typical of long-video VLMs). It produces a final text answer, but also generates intermediate artifacts: 500–2k dense scene captions with consistent character/object tags (e.g., 'person-b') that serve as a reusable textual medium for multiple questions on the same video. The entire pipeline is zero-shot, using Gemini-2.0-Flash as the VLM and GPT-4o for text-level reasoning, with no training.

## Method

MR. Video instantiates MapReduce as a two-stage video agent. The Captioning stage uses parallel Map steps to generate dense scene-level captions on short segments (with scene splitting and salient character identification), followed by a Reduce step that associates repeated characters across segments and assigns consistent names. The Analysis stage has two sub-stages: (1) Question Intention Analysis, where Map steps reason about each segment's relevance to the question using global video context rather than key-frame retrieval, and a Reduce step unifies these into a video-level localization of candidate scenes; (2) Goal-Aware Analysis, where the LLM proposes customized VLM queries for dense local or sparse global frame perception, and a final Reduce step aggregates results into the answer. The key differentiators are the sequence-parallel Map steps (enabling linear scaling with GPUs) and the replacement of key-segment retrieval with question intention analysis that reasons over the entire video context.

## Results

On LVBench (1,492 questions over 30-min to 2-hr videos), MR. Video achieves 60.8% overall accuracy, a >7% improvement over the best VLM baseline (AdaReTaKe, 53.3%) and a ~19.5% improvement over the best video agent (VCA, 41.3%). On LongVideoBench (long subset), MR. Video reaches 61.6%, surpassing GPT4o (58.6%) and VideoAgent (47.6%). On EgoSchema, MR. Video scores 73.8% (nearly matching VCA's 73.6%), and on Video-MME (long, no subtitles) it achieves 63.4%, beating all video agents but trailing GPT4o (65.3%) by ~2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MR. Video (60.8%) outperforms the strongest VLM baseline AdaReTaKe (53.3%) by 7.5 points and the strongest video agent VCA (41.3%) by 19.5 points on LVBench, with consistent gains across all six question categories (e.g., KIR: 71.4% vs. 62.2% for AdaReTaKe). On LongVideoBench (long subset), MR. Video (61.6%) beats GPT4o (58.6%) by 3 points and VideoAgent (47.6%) by 14 points, while also surpassing its own base VLM Gemini-2.0-Flash (45.7%) by 15.9 points. On EgoSchema, MR. Video (73.8%) essentially ties VCA (73.6%) and exceeds GPT4o (70.4%) by 3.4 points. On Video-MME (long, no subtitles), MR. Video (63.4%) trails GPT4o (65.3%) by ~2 points but beats all video agents (VCA 56.3%, VideoTree 53.1%, VideoAgent 46.4%) and its base VLM Gemini-2.0-Flash (63.0%). Notably, MR. Video uses a cheaper VLM (Gemini-2.0-Flash) with a restricted context of <40 frames per query, yet still outperforms agents built on GPT4o, highlighting the effectiveness of the MapReduce decomposition rather than raw model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the MapReduce principle from distributed systems (Dean & Ghemawat, 2008) as a conceptual framework for long video understanding, positioning itself against two existing paradigms: sequence-to-sequence VLMs (LLaVA, VideoChat-Flash) constrained by context length, and video agents (VideoAgent, VideoTree, VCA) that rely on sequential multi-round key-frame retrieval. The key departure is replacing iterative, sequential segment selection with parallel Map steps (dense short-segment perception) followed by global Reduce steps (aggregation), and introducing two under-explored designs—consistent character naming across captions and question intention analysis—that address long-horizon reasoning without assuming a small number of key events.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MapReduce framing offers a principled, deployment-friendly architecture for long video understanding with a short, controllable critical path that scales linearly with available compute, addressing a real bottleneck for hour-long videos. The >7% improvement on LVBench using a cheaper VLM (Gemini-2.0-Flash) with a smaller context window than the base VLMs it outperforms suggests the decomposition strategy itself, not raw model capacity, is the key lever. Open questions include whether the principle can be natively embedded in VLM architectures (local attention for Map, global attention for Reduce) rather than relying on expensive multi-model API pipelines, and whether post-training can align LLMs better with the visual reasoning demands of the Reduce steps.
