## Problem

While transfer learning and benign overfitting of minimum-ℓ2-norm interpolators (MNI) in high-dimensional linear regression have each been extensively studied, their intersection remains largely unexplored. The paper asks whether knowledge transfer from related source tasks can further enhance the already-remarkable out-of-sample generalization of overparameterized interpolators, and under what distribution shift conditions (model shift, covariate shift) this transfer is beneficial versus harmful.

## Input / Output

The method operates on tabular data where each task (one target, Q sources) provides a design matrix X^(q) ∈ R^{n_q × p} and response vector y^(q) ∈ R^{n_q} in the overparameterized regime n_q < p. The output is a coefficient vector β̂_TM^(q) ∈ R^p that exactly interpolates the target data while minimizing ℓ2 distance from the pre-trained source MNI, plus an ensemble WTM vector that is a weighted combination of multiple informative Transfer MNIs. A key structural property is that the TM estimate decomposes into the target-only MNI plus a null-space projection of the source MNI, so source knowledge enters only in directions where target samples provide no information.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-step Transfer MNI (TM): first pre-train a source-only minimum-ℓ2-norm interpolator, then fine-tune by solving a constrained optimization that interpolates target data while minimizing Euclidean distance to the pre-trained model. This yields a closed-form 'retain-plus-transfer' decomposition where target-learned signal is preserved in the row space of the target design matrix and source knowledge is injected only into the orthogonal null space. For multi-source settings, the paper introduces an Informative-Weighted Transfer MNI (WTM) that uses K-fold cross-validation to detect which sources induce positive transfer and aggregates them with data-adaptive inverse-CV-loss weights. The theoretical analysis provides non-asymptotic excess risk bounds under both model and covariate shifts, identifies necessary and sufficient conditions for positive transfer in terms of shift-to-signal and signal-to-noise ratios, specifies the optimal transfer size, and uncovers 'free-lunch' covariate shift regimes where uniform spectral upscaling of source covariances reduces variance inflation without affecting bias convergence.

## Results

In benign overfitting experiments (n₀=25, n_q=75, S=500, p∈{300,…,1000}), each Transfer MNI outperforms the target-only MNI even under severe model shift (SSR=0.6), and the WTM ensemble further reduces excess risk beyond any single TM. Under covariate shift with misaligned eigenvectors, TM^(3) suffers negative transfer, but WTM filters it out via CV-based source selection and consistently outperforms all competitors. In harmless interpolation experiments (n₀=n₁=50, S=10), TM^(2) with the optimal transfer size n₂* outperforms the target-only MNI (which converges to ‖β⁽⁰⁾‖²=10) despite SSR=0.4, and the free-lunch covariate shift (α=8) further accelerates convergence by reducing variance inflation by a factor of α⁻¹.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the target-only MNI (Bartlett et al. [5]), Transfer MNI achieves lower excess risk whenever SSR_q < 1 and SNR_q(1−SSR_q) exceeds a dimension-dependent threshold (Corollary 1); the WTM ensemble further reduces risk by adaptively weighting only informative sources. Against the pooled-MNI (Song et al. [56]), TM and WTM are dramatically more robust: under distribution shifts in Figure 1, PM's excess risk is 'catastrophic' while TM estimates remain stable; in Figure 2, PM eventually surpasses the baseline as p grows but still lags behind TM^(2) by a significant margin. Against SGD-based pretrain-finetune (Wu et al. [69]), SGD₂ also eventually surpasses the target-only MNI in the harmless interpolation setting but is consistently outperformed by TM^(2), which benefits from the closed-form 'retain-plus-transfer' decomposition rather than iterative optimization. Overall, TM and WTM win on all three comparisons under overparameterized, distribution-shifted settings, with the advantage most pronounced under covariate shift where PM degrades severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the benign overfitting theory of the minimum-ℓ2-norm interpolator (MNI) established by Bartlett et al. [5] into a transfer learning setting, proposing a two-step Transfer MNI that pre-trains a source-only MNI and fine-tunes it on target data while minimizing Euclidean distance to the pre-trained model. It departs from prior transfer-for-interpolators work—Song et al. [56] (pooled-MNI) and Wu et al. [69] (SGD pretrain-finetune)—by using a geometrically motivated 'stay close' constraint that yields a clean retain-plus-transfer decomposition, and by analyzing both model and covariate shifts simultaneously rather than one in isolation. The free-lunch covariate shift characterization (Corollary 2) has no direct analogue in the existing transfer learning literature, where covariate shift is typically treated purely as a challenge to be mitigated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first non-asymptotic excess risk bounds for a transfer MNI under joint model and covariate shifts, and identifies a concrete regime (uniform eigenvalue upscaling with aligned leading eigenvectors) where covariate shift *reduces* the cost of knowledge transfer—a counterintuitive 'free lunch' that could guide data collection strategies in multi-source settings. Key open questions include: (1) proving consistency of the cross-validation-based informative source detection (Algorithm 1), which currently lacks theoretical guarantees; (2) extending the analysis to minimum-RKHS-norm interpolators and NTK-type kernels, where benign overfitting mechanisms differ substantially; and (3) the variance inflation bound in Theorem 2 contains a reciprocal eigenvalue term (λ_p^(q))^{-1} that is loose without simultaneous diagonalizability, leaving a gap for general covariance structures. All experiments are synthetic, so practical validation on real multi-source regression tasks remains to be done.
