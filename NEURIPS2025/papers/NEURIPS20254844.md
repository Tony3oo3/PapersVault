## Problem

Gaussian mixture variational inference can capture multimodal Bayesian posteriors, but full-covariance mixtures require O(d²) memory per component, making them impractical in high dimensions. Simpler families (single Gaussians, mean-field) are efficient but too crude for multimodal targets. This paper identifies a middle ground: restricting to isotropic (scalar-variance) covariance matrices with uniform weights, which reduces per-component cost to O(d) while retaining multimodal expressiveness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the unnormalized potential V (so that π ∝ exp(−V)) and requires no access to the normalization constant. Each of the N mixture components is parameterized by a mean m^j ∈ R^d and a scalar variance ε^j > 0, giving a total of N(d+1) parameters versus N(d²+d) for full covariance. Gradients of the KL objective are estimated via Monte Carlo with B samples drawn from each Gaussian component, and the algorithm takes a step-size γ and iteration count T as hyperparameters. The output is the optimized set of (m^j, ε^j) pairs defining the approximate posterior mixture.

## Method

The paper develops a variational framework over mixtures of N isotropic Gaussians with uniform weights, where the Bures-Wasserstein geodesic between any two isotropic Gaussians remains in the isotropic family, yielding a tractable geometry. Optimization proceeds via joint updates: standard gradient descent on the means, and either a Bures-Wasserstein (IBW) or entropic mirror descent (MD) update on the variances. The IBW update derives from a JKO scheme on the discrete mixing measure with Bures-Wasserstein cost, producing ε_{k+1} = (1 − 2Nγ/d · ∇_{ε^j} F)² ε_k; the MD update uses the Von Neumann relative entropy as a Bregman divergence, giving ε_{k+1} = ε_k · exp(−2Nγ/d · ∇_{ε^j} F). Both variance updates structurally guarantee positivity, unlike natural gradient descent which can produce negative variances. The mixing-measure formulation with N equal-weight Dirac particles avoids the identifiability issues of overparameterized continuous mixing measures, and the resulting per-iteration cost is linear in d rather than quadratic.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative finding is that IBW (Bures-Wasserstein update on isotropic variances) achieves KL objectives comparable to the full-covariance BW method while reducing memory from O(N(d²+d)) to O(N(d+1)) and delivering faster per-iteration time (Figure 3). On a 2D 5-component Gaussian mixture target, increasing N from 1 to 20 monotonically decreases the final KL, with N=20 yielding the best approximation. On the high-dimensional target (d=20, 10 target components, N=20 variational components), IBW and MD both closely track BW in KL convergence, while NGD shows greater sensitivity to initialization. On Bayesian inference tasks (BLR on breast_cancer d=30, wine d=39; BNN on boston d=601, MNIST d=203530), IBW and MD achieve competitive test accuracy/RMSE versus HMC and ADVI, with a mean-field variant extending the approach to the 203K-parameter MNIST setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BW (Lambert et al., 2022, full-covariance Bures-Wasserstein), IBW matches the KL objective in both 2D and d=20 experiments while running faster per iteration and using linear rather than quadratic memory in d; the paper attributes the small KL gap to the isotropic restriction. Against GD (Huix et al., 2024, fixed shared variance, means-only update), IBW and MD clearly outperform because per-component variance optimization better captures heterogeneous mode shapes, as confirmed by the underperformance of the shared-variance ablations IBW-s and MD-s. Against NGD (Lin et al., 2019), the paper reports that NGD's mean update is rescaled by ε, making it more sensitive to initialization, and that NGD can produce negative variance estimates in practice (particularly on MNIST), whereas IBW and MD guarantee ε > 0 by construction. Against Normalizing Flows (Real NVP, Dinh et al., 2017), the NF baseline converges more slowly without achieving a lower final KL on the 2D target. Against ADVI (Kucukelbir et al., 2017) on Bayesian logistic regression and BNN tasks, IBW and MD are competitive in final accuracy/RMSE, with the advantage that the mixture family can represent multimodal posteriors that a single Gaussian (as in standard ADVI) cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wasserstein gradient flow framework for Gaussian VI (Lambert et al., 2022) to mixtures, but with a key structural restriction: isotropic (scalar) covariance matrices and uniform weights, reducing per-component parameters from d²+d to d+1. It departs from natural gradient descent methods for mixture VI (Lin et al., 2019; Arenz et al., 2023) by using Bures-Wasserstein or entropic mirror descent geometries for variance updates, which guarantee positivity and decouple mean/variance optimization. The fixed-variance mixture setting of Huix et al. (2024) is generalized here to allow per-component variances, and the JKO scheme is made fully explicit via a mixing-measure formulation that avoids the identifiability issues of infinite-component overparameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a memory and compute reduction from quadratic to linear in dimension for mixture-based VI, while empirically maintaining approximation quality close to full-covariance mixtures — a compelling trade-off for high-dimensional Bayesian inference (e.g., BNN posteriors with d > 200,000). Key open questions include: (i) a formal approximation-error comparison between isotropic and full-covariance mixtures to explain the small empirical KL gap, (ii) convergence guarantees for the N > 1 mixture case (current guarantees require N=1 and strong log-concavity), and (iii) whether the uniform-weight restriction loses significant expressivity for highly unbalanced targets. The paper acknowledges these as future work.
