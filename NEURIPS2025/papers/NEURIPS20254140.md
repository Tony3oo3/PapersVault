## Problem

Deep learning-based molecular docking methods produce conformations that are geometrically accurate but physically implausible—exhibiting steric clashes and high-energy configurations—because they under-utilize physical priors in model design. Traditional physics-based docking programs enforce these constraints but are too slow for high-throughput screening. The paper addresses this gap by integrating energy-based force guidance directly into the generative sampling process of a flow matching model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein structure (residue-level nodes with ESM2 embeddings at Cα positions), a ligand molecule (atom-level nodes with 12 feature types), and a predicted binding pocket from P2Rank as input. The ligand pose is parameterized as a triple (C, O, τ) on the product manifold R³ × SO(3) × (SO(2))^m, where C is the center-of-mass translation, O is the rotation matrix, and τ are m torsion angles. At inference, 40 candidate poses are sampled via ODE integration and ranked by a learned scoring model; initial ligand conformations are generated by ETKDG (RDKit) around the predicted pocket center.

## Method

ForceFM builds a Riemannian flow matching baseline that learns a vector field v_t(x) over the product manifold of translation, rotation, and torsion using conditional flow matching with E3-equivariant graph convolution layers on heterogeneous geometric graphs. The key contribution is a force-guided correction: Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2 show that the target Boltzmann-weighted distribution p₁(x) ∝ q₁(x)exp(−kE₁(x)) is generated by the modified flow ũ_t(x) = v_t(x) + r_t(x), where r_t(x) is an energy-weighted expectation over perturbed conformations. A separate equivariant force network h_t(x) approximates r_t(x) and is trained by matching its output to weighted residuals ζ_i computed from K=40 randomly perturbed ground-truth poses, with weights proportional to exp(−kE₁(x_a^i)). At inference, the ODE d/dt x = v_t(x) + η·h_t(x) is integrated with a user-defined guidance strength η, halving the number of sampling steps needed compared to the unguided baseline while steering poses toward low-energy, physically valid conformations.

## Results

On PDBBind blind self-docking (363 test structures), ForceFM with Glide guidance (Ours + Glide) achieves a mean ligand RMSD of 3.2 Å, 49.5% of poses below 2 Å, and 78.0% below 5 Å, outperforming all baselines including FABind+ (5.1 Å mean RMSD) and DiffDock+Glide (4.0 Å). Force guidance also halves inference time from 46.9 s (unguided) to ~25 s per pose. On the PoseBuster physical-validity benchmark, Ours + Glide raises the pass rate from 17.5% (unguided) to 31.4%, a 79% relative improvement, establishing a new state-of-the-art for physically plausible docking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForceFM with Glide guidance (mean RMSD 3.2 Å, 49.5% <2 Å) outperforms all 11 baselines on PDBBind blind self-docking. Among traditional docking tools, it surpasses GLIDE (16.2 Å), VINA (14.7 Å), GNINA (13.3 Å), SMINA (12.1 Å), and QVINA-W (13.6 Å) by 11–13 Å in mean RMSD. Among deep-learning methods, it beats the strongest regression-based baseline FABind+ (5.1 Å) by 1.9 Å and the sampling-based DiffDock (7.5 Å) by 4.3 Å. Notably, applying the same force-guidance procedure to DiffDock improves it from 7.5 Å to 4.0 Å (DiffDock+Glide), yet ForceFM still leads by 0.8 Å, confirming the advantage of the flow-matching backbone. On unseen proteins (Table 2), Ours + Glide achieves 4.8 Å mean RMSD and 70.3% <5 Å, again leading all baselines. On PoseBuster physical validity, Ours + Glide (31.4% pass) exceeds DiffDock+Glide (25.6%) by 5.8 percentage points, and the unguided ForceFM baseline (17.5%) already surpasses unguided DiffDock (14.3%), indicating the flow-matching architecture itself contributes to better physical plausibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

ForceFM extends Riemannian Flow Matching (Lipman 2022; Chen 2023) to the product manifold R³ × SO(3) × (SO(2))^m for ligand pose generation, building on SO(3)-CFM (Yim 2023) for rotation and torsional diffusion (Jing 2022) for internal angles. Its key departure from prior generative docking work (DiffDock, FABind) is the integration of exact energy-based force guidance, adapted from the contrastive energy prediction framework of Lu et al. (2023), which adds a corrective vector field to the learned flow to steer samples toward low-energy conformations. Unlike classifier-free or approximate guidance used in image/video generation, ForceFM derives a closed-form guided flow from a Boltzmann-weighted posterior, and demonstrates that the guiding potential can be swapped across multiple scoring functions (Vina, Glide, Gnina, Confscore) without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap in DL-based docking: generated poses often violate physical constraints (steric clashes, unrealistic bond geometries), as quantified by the PoseBuster benchmark. ForceFM's ~80% relative improvement in PoseBuster pass rate and halving of required sampling steps make it practically relevant for high-throughput screening where both speed and physical validity matter. Key limitations remain: the model assumes a rigid protein, is trained only on PDBBind (limiting exposure to diverse binding environments), and its performance is coupled to the quality of the chosen energy function. Open questions include whether force guidance can be extended to flexible docking with MD-informed energy landscapes and whether the approach generalizes beyond the PDBBind distribution to truly novel targets.
