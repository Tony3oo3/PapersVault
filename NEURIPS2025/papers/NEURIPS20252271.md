## Problem

While node-level over-smoothing in GCNs has been extensively studied, the paper identifies and formalizes a distinct graph-level over-smoothing phenomenon: deep GCNs produce increasingly indistinguishable representations for different graphs, degrading graph classification. This arises because (1) node features converge toward the graph mean under repeated message passing, and (2) simple permutation-invariant readout functions (sum, mean, max) discard the local structural distinctions that remain among nodes, causing structurally different graphs to map to nearly identical graph embeddings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph G(A, X) with adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}, where features are one-hot encodings of node labels (attributed graphs) or node degrees (unattributed graphs). Internally, the model maintains a three-dimensional view-specific tensor H^(l) ∈ R^{n×m×d^(l)} across m subgraph views, and the alignment-based readout produces an order-consistent cluster matrix Z̃ ∈ R^{s×d'} that is flattened and passed through an MLP to yield the final graph-level representation y used for classification.

## Method

MultiNet comprises three stages: a standard GCN layer for initial feature extraction; a local subgraph convolution module that (i) learns a row-wise softmax attention matrix P ∈ R^{n×m} to softly assign nodes to m subgraph views, (ii) applies gated message passing per view where each node's propagation is scaled by its view-specific attention score via diag(P_j), yielding a propagation kernel Ã_j = Ã·diag(P_j) with spectral radius strictly less than 1, and (iii) fuses view-specific features through concatenation and an MLP; and an alignment-based readout that learns a shared cluster assignment S ∈ R^{n×s} via graph convolution to establish order-consistent correspondences across graphs, enabling an expressive MLP readout on the aligned s×d' cluster matrix while preserving permutation invariance. The theoretical analysis shows that the sub-unity spectral radius of Ã_j prevents convergence to the dominant eigenvector, retaining multi-component spectral information and preserving local structural distinctions that simple readouts would otherwise erase.

## Results

MultiNet achieves the best average rank (2.28) across seven benchmark graph classification datasets, with the highest accuracy on MUTAG (89.81%), PTC_MR (62.65%), PROTEINS (76.40%), IMDB-B (76.49%), and IMDB-M (51.93%). On ENZYMES, MultiNet (54.83%) trails GIN (59.6%) and DiffPool (59.5%), while on DD it is slightly below WLSK (79.78% vs. 78.90%). The over-smoothing analysis (Figure 4) shows that MultiNet maintains a higher Average Cosine Distance (AD) between graph representations as network depth increases (up to 64 layers), whereas Vanilla GCN, GIN, and GraphSAGE exhibit significant AD degradation, confirming effective mitigation of graph-level over-smoothing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiNet outperforms all GNN-based baselines on MUTAG (89.81% vs. 1-RWNN's 89.2% and GIN's 84.7%), PTC_MR (62.65% vs. GKNN-GL's 60.13% and DiffPool's 60.8%), PROTEINS (76.40% vs. GKNN-GL's 75.36% and SPGK's 75.10%), IMDB-B (76.49% vs. JTQK's 72.45% and WLSK's 71.88%), and IMDB-M (51.93% vs. SPGK's 51.33% and JTQK's 50.33%). On ENZYMES, MultiNet (54.83%) is outperformed by GIN (59.6%), DiffPool (59.5%), and 3-RWNN (57.6%), and on DD it trails WLSK (79.78%) by roughly 0.9%. Among graph kernel methods, MultiNet surpasses SPGK, WLSK, JTQK, and QBMK on most datasets where they overlap, while maintaining an end-to-end trainable framework that avoids handcrafted kernel design. The over-smoothing experiments (Figure 4) further show that at 32–64 layers, MultiNet retains substantially higher AD values than Vanilla GCN, GIN, and GraphSAGE, whose representation diversity collapses, confirming that the subgraph convolution and alignment-based readout jointly mitigate graph-level over-smoothing more effectively than standard GCN architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the over-smoothing literature for GCNs from the well-studied node-level perspective to a newly formalized graph-level perspective, arguing that existing mitigation strategies (DropEdge, PairNorm, GCNII, residual connections) address node representation convergence within a single graph but leave the cross-graph representation collapse unaddressed. Methodologically, it repurposes attention-based message passing (reminiscent of GAT) in a constrained multi-view subgraph setting and adapts alignment concepts from the graph kernel literature (Bai et al.'s aligned subtree and transitive-aligned kernels) into a differentiable neural readout. The multi-view subgraph division and the combination of constrained propagation with alignment-based readout constitute the novel architectural contribution, with no single prior work providing both components together.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap by formalizing and empirically demonstrating that graph-level over-smoothing degrades classification accuracy independently of node-level over-smoothing, providing a theoretical proof (Theorem 1) under a feature-distribution-consistency assumption. The alignment-based readout is a creative bridge between classical graph kernel alignment and end-to-end neural training, offering a more expressive alternative to sum/mean/max pooling. However, the evaluation is limited to small molecular and social-network graphs (up to ~1,000 nodes), and the assumption that node features follow similar distributions across graphs (Assumption 1) may not hold in heterogeneous or out-of-domain settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large graphs, whether the alignment mechanism generalizes to node-level tasks, and how the method performs when the feature-distribution-consistency assumption is violated.
