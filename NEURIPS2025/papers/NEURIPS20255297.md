## Problem

Quantum Implicit Neural Representations (QINRs) remain heavily underexplored, with the closest prior work (QIREN) relying on heavy classical post-processing that reduces the quantum circuit to a mere feature generator, hand-crafted data encoding that risks misalignment with quantum circuit inductive biases, and unconstrained Hilbert-space exploration that triggers barren plateaus. This paper addresses these gaps by introducing a QINR architecture that eliminates classical post-processing, replaces hand-crafted encoding with a learnable energy-based scheme, and constrains the quantum ansatz to real-valued unitaries for stable gradient flow.

## Input / Output

QVF takes spatial query coordinates (2D pixel positions or 3D points, up to 100k samples per shape) and an optional latent code z as input, producing 2D image pixel values or 3D signed distance field values as output. Coordinates are first mapped through sinusoidal positional encoding before entering the classical energy-inference MLP, and the latent code conditions the model to jointly represent collections of visual fields (e.g., 50 images or 3 shapes simultaneously). Field values are extracted directly from projective Pauli-Z measurements on the first m qubits of the quantum circuit, with m ≤ n where n is the total qubit count, enabling multi-dimensional signal output from a single circuit evaluation.

## Method

QVF encodes classical data into quantum states via a neural amplitude encoding grounded in a learnable energy manifold: a small MLP infers an energy spectrum E from positional-encoded coordinates and latent code, which is converted into a Boltzmann-regulated probability distribution P that sets the quantum state amplitudes, replacing fixed hand-crafted embeddings with a data-driven, non-linear preparation step. The quantum ansatz is a fully entangled PQC built exclusively from Pauli-Y rotations and entangling gates, confining state evolution to the real Hilbert subspace; this design bounds Haar randomness to prevent barren plateaus while preserving full expressiveness for measurement-relevant quantities. Unlike QIREN's sandwich architecture with classical post-processing, QVF extracts field values directly from projective measurements, with the ansatz itself approximating the optimal measurement basis through unitary evolution. Training is end-to-end under a Bayesian framework: classical parameters are updated via gradient descent through the energy-inference MLP, quantum parameters via the parameter-shift rule, with a Gaussian likelihood (justified by the CLT over finite-shot measurements) and a smooth isotropic Gaussian prior on latent codes for coherent interpolation.

## Results

On 2D image representation (50 CIFAR-10 images, n=5 qubits), QVF with Sine activation and Gaussian initialisation achieves MSE 0.62×10⁻³ and PSNR 32.2 dB, outperforming the SIREN baseline (MSE 1.19×10⁻³, PSNR 29.2 dB) by ~1.9× on MSE and ~3 dB on PSNR. On 3D shape representation (ShapeNet SDFs, 100k samples per shape), QVF (Gaussian+Sin) achieves MAE 0.27×10⁻³, compared to 0.48×10⁻³ for SIREN and 1.43×10⁻³ for DeepSDF. Against the prior QINR method QIREN, QVF (Identity+Sin) reduces MSE by 30% (0.54 vs 0.78 ×10⁻³) and improves PSNR by ~1.6 dB (32.67 vs 31.03).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QIREN [57], the closest prior QINR method, QVF (Identity+Sin) achieves MSE 0.54×10⁻³ vs. QIREN's 0.78×10⁻³ (a 30% reduction) and PSNR 32.67 vs. 31.03 dB on 50-image 2D representation; QIREN also requires heavy classical post-processing that QVF avoids. Against DeepSDF (MLP+ReLU) [34], QVF (Gaussian+Sin) improves image MSE by 3.5× (0.62 vs. 2.17 ×10⁻³) and 3D shape MAE by 5.3× (0.27 vs. 1.43 ×10⁻³), with a ~5.6 dB PSNR gain on images. Against SIREN (MLP+Sin) [45], QVF (Gaussian+Sin) halves the image MSE (0.62 vs. 1.19 ×10⁻³) and reduces 3D shape MAE by 1.8× (0.27 vs. 0.48 ×10⁻³), while using only ~170 additional parameters from the quantum ansatz over the equivalent classical architecture. The paper notes that QVF's advantage is most pronounced in high-frequency signal reconstruction, consistent with the Fourier-structure properties of the quantum circuit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the QINR paradigm initiated by QIREN (Zhao et al., ICML 2024) by replacing its sandwich architecture (classical pre-processing → quantum circuit → classical post-processing) with a design where the quantum ansatz is the primary signal extractor, with classical components limited to data encoding. The encoding mechanism generalises hand-crafted amplitude encoding from 3D-QAE (Rathi et al., BMVC 2023) into a learnable, energy-based neural scheme grounded in a Gibbs-Boltzmann framework, while the Fourier-structure analysis of variational circuits from Schuld et al. (PRA 2021) is extended to justify the energy inference as an implicit frequency-spectrum selection. A key architectural departure is constraining the ansatz to real-valued unitaries (Pauli-Y rotations only), bounding Haar randomness to mitigate barren plateaus without sacrificing expressiveness for Z-basis measurements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QVF is the first QINR to support 3D signed distance field representation, latent-space interpolation, image inpainting, and shape completion, broadening the scope of quantum implicit representations beyond the 2D image setting of prior work. The practical insight that restricting to real-valued unitaries preserves gradient flow while maintaining universality for relevant observables offers a reusable design principle for future QINR architectures. However, all experiments remain on a noiseless PennyLane simulator with modest data scales (50 CIFAR-10 images, 3 ShapeNet shapes), and the field awaits validation on near-term noisy hardware and at scales where quantum advantage could plausibly emerge. Open questions include whether the Boltzmann-regulated encoding generalises to higher-dimensional or temporal fields, and how the approach scales as fault-tolerant hardware becomes available.
