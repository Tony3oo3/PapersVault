## Problem

Outcome-reward RL for multimodal multiple-choice tasks rewards any trajectory that selects the correct option, regardless of whether the intermediate reasoning is faithful. This allows models to reinforce spurious or hallucinatory chains of thought that happen to land on the right answer, a flaw that is particularly acute in the multiple-choice format where the model can guess after a faulty derivation. The paper identifies this unfaithful-reasoning gap and proposes a training-time remedy that does not require a separate process reward model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254395/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a multiple-choice question and produces a step-by-step reasoning trajectory followed by a single answer letter, formatted as <think> THINKING <answer> ANSWER </answer>. During training, SCS additionally consumes truncated prefixes of the initial trajectory and Gaussian-noise-perturbed versions of the input image (σ drawn uniformly from [σ_min, σ_max]) to generate m continuation samples per question. The consistency reward is computed from the set of distinct answer letters across these continuations, so the effective training signal depends on both the original trajectory and its resampled neighbors.

## Method

Self-Consistency Sampling (SCS) augments the standard accuracy+format outcome reward with a consistency reward r_con = (1/N)(N − |A|)·c, where A is the set of distinct answer options obtained by resampling m continuations from a truncated prefix of the initial trajectory (truncation ratio k) under independently sampled visual perturbations. The key insight is that a faithful reasoning prefix deterministically converges to the correct option across resamples (|A| ≈ 1, high reward), whereas an unfaithful prefix yields divergent answers (|A| large, low reward), so the consistency term differentially up-weights genuine reasoning without any external process reward model. SCS is algorithm-agnostic: it is plugged into RLOO, GRPO, and REINFORCE++ by simply adding r_con to the per-sample reward before computing the policy-gradient baseline. Ablations show the two components—truncation-resampling (+5.2 pp) and visual perturbation (+5.0 pp)—are individually beneficial and jointly recover the full +7.7 pp gain over vanilla RLOO on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

## Results

On six multimodal reasoning benchmarks (M3CoT, MMMU, ScienceQA, We-Math, MathVerse, MathVision), SCS integrated with RLOO on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct achieves an overall accuracy of 65.5%, a +7.7 percentage-point improvement over the RLOO baseline (57.8%). Gains are consistent across all RL algorithms: GRPO improves from 63.6% to 64.5% (+0.9 pp), REINFORCE++ from 60.9% to 62.9% (+2.0 pp), and REINFORCE++-baseline from 61.3% to 63.0% (+1.7 pp). SCS also generalizes to smaller and different-architecture models, yielding +3.2 pp on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and +1.6 pp on InternVL3-8B when paired with RLOO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCS is evaluated as a plug-in module on top of four outcome-reward RL algorithms, and it improves every one of them. The largest gain is with RLOO, where SCS lifts overall accuracy from 57.8% to 65.5% (+7.7 pp), surpassing even the SFT baseline (58.6%) by a wide margin. REINFORCE++ benefits by +2.0 pp (60.9% → 62.9%) and REINFORCE++-baseline by +1.7 pp (61.3% → 63.0%), while GRPO sees a smaller but still positive +0.9 pp (63.6% → 64.5%). Notably, vanilla RLOO without SCS (57.8%) actually underperforms SFT (58.6%), but adding SCS closes and reverses this gap, suggesting that the consistency reward is particularly important for algorithms with weaker built-in regularization. Across all six individual benchmarks, SCS+RLOO achieves the highest score in 5 out of 6 categories, with the only exception being We-Math where SFT (90.7%) slightly edges out SCS+RLOO (88.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the self-consistency idea from inference-time chain-of-thought reasoning (Wang et al., 2022) as a training-time reward signal within outcome-reward RL for MLLMs. It plugs this consistency reward into three existing policy-gradient algorithms—RLOO, GRPO, and REINFORCE++—which all rely solely on binary answer-correctness rewards. The key conceptual departure is identifying that in multiple-choice settings, outcome-reward RL actively reinforces unfaithful trajectories (correct answer via faulty reasoning), and proposing truncation-resampling plus stochastic visual perturbation as two lightweight mechanisms to compute a differentiable consistency score that penalizes such trajectories during policy updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that vanilla RLOO underperforms SFT on multiple-choice MLLM tasks (57.8 vs. 58.6) challenges the assumption that outcome-reward RL is uniformly beneficial, and SCS offers a simple, low-overhead fix (~38% extra training time) that recovers and exceeds SFT performance across three model families. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to open-ended generation, whether it scales to larger models or longer reasoning chains, and whether the visual-perturbation component generalizes beyond 2D images (e.g., video). The paper acknowledges it has not been tested on pure LLMs or a broader set of MLLM architectures, leaving generality partially unverified.
