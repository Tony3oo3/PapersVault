## Problem

GRPO-based video temporal grounding requires ground-truth temporal boundaries to compute reward signals, making it inapplicable to unlabelled target domains, and full-scale adaptation on thousands of target videos incurs prohibitive storage and latency costs for real-time deployment. This paper tackles both issues by introducing a data-efficient unlabelled cross-domain setting where a source-trained model adapts at test-time using only K=100–200 unlabelled target videos, and by quantifying the reliability of the resulting pseudo-labels to prevent the model from overfitting to noisy self-supervision.

## Input / Output

At inference the model receives an untrimmed video (up to 4096-token prompt) paired with a natural-language query and outputs normalized start and end timestamps in [0,1] wrapped in <answer> tags, preceded by a <think> reasoning chain. During target adaptation the only additional input is a small set of K unlabelled target videos (no temporal annotations); the model generates G=8 stochastic rollouts per video to construct pseudo-labels internally. The relaxed ground-truth boundary (α=0.1 extension) is applied to both source labels and target pseudo-labels to absorb annotation ambiguity.

## Method

URPA first trains a Qwen2.5-7B backbone on a labelled source domain using GRPO with a composite reward: a binary format reward enforcing think/answer structure and a relaxed tIoU accuracy reward that tolerates boundary ambiguity. For target adaptation, the policy samples G=8 rollouts per unlabelled target video; the pseudo-label is the mean of predicted timestamps across rollouts (with margin relaxation), and the standard deviation across rollouts serves as an epistemic uncertainty estimate analogous to MC Dropout. This uncertainty is converted to a confidence score c = exp(−γu) that multiplicatively weights the tIoU reward (while leaving the format reward unweighted), so high-variance, low-confidence pseudo-labels contribute less to the gradient update. A theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that as G→∞ and the KL divergence between the GRPO policy and the Bayesian predictive distribution vanishes, the rollout standard deviation converges to the Bayesian predictive standard deviation, establishing it as a consistent estimator of epistemic uncertainty.

## Results

On six cross-domain temporal grounding benchmarks (TACoS, ActivityNet Captions, Charades-STA), URPA with only 200 unlabelled target videos achieves R@0.5 of 55.54 on TACoS→Charades, 41.83 on TACoS→ActivityNet, and 65.12 on ActivityNet→Charades, outperforming the best full-dataset UDA baseline (UDA-TSL) by 14.15, 8.29, and 4.86 points respectively. With just 100 unlabelled target videos, URPA still reaches 54.78 R@0.5 on TACoS→Charades and 64.35 on ActivityNet→Charades. The method also improves over source-only GRPO training (e.g., 42.57 vs. 35.61 R@0.5 on Charades→ActivityNet), demonstrating that uncertainty-weighted pseudo-label adaptation adds meaningful gains even with minimal target data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

URPA (200-shot) outperforms the strongest full-dataset UDA baseline UDA-TSL [30] on three of six cross-domain settings: TACoS→Charades (55.54 vs. 41.39 R@0.5), TACoS→ActivityNet (41.83 vs. 33.54 R@0.5), and ActivityNet→Charades (65.12 vs. 60.26 R@0.5), despite using only 200 unlabelled target videos versus the entire target set. On the remaining three settings (Charades→ActivityNet, ActivityNet→TACoS, Charades→TACoS), URPA trails UDA-TSL, with the largest gap on ActivityNet→TACoS (10.38 vs. 29.83 R@0.5), where the target domain has very few videos (127) making full-set adaptation more feasible. Against the zero-shot Qwen2.5-7B baseline, URPA improves R@0.5 by 27–32 points across all settings. Compared to source-only GRPO training, URPA's target adaptation adds 4–7 R@0.5 points (e.g., 42.57 vs. 35.61 on Charades→ActivityNet), confirming that uncertainty-weighted pseudo-labels provide meaningful additional signal. The paper also shows in Appendix A.5 that URPA (200-shot) approaches the performance of fully supervised GRPO with 200 labelled target samples (e.g., 42.57 vs. 42.75 R@0.5 on Charades→ActivityNet), validating its data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: GRPO-based reinforcement learning for temporal grounding (TimeZero, Temporal-R1), test-time domain adaptation (TTDA) for video, and Bayesian uncertainty estimation via MC Dropout. The key conceptual move is recognizing that GRPO's stochastic rollouts play an analogous role to MC Dropout's stochastic subnetworks, enabling rollout variance to serve as an epistemic uncertainty signal for pseudo-labels. It departs from prior cross-domain TG work (UDA-TSL, TimeZero) by introducing a data-efficient setting requiring only K=100–200 unlabelled target videos rather than the full target set, and from standard GRPO by replacing ground-truth reward signals with uncertainty-weighted pseudo-label rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: eliminating both target annotations and full-dataset adaptation makes cross-domain temporal grounding feasible for real-time and on-device deployment, a setting prior methods could not address. The theoretical guarantee that rollout variance consistently estimates Bayesian predictive variance provides a principled foundation for the uncertainty-weighting mechanism. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than three domains, how it behaves when the source-target distribution shift is extreme, and whether the overfitting-to-noise phenomenon observed at higher shot counts can be further mitigated. The method's reliance on a strong source-trained GRPO backbone also limits its applicability when no suitable labelled source domain exists.
