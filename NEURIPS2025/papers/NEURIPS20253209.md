## Problem

Tri-plane-like representations in 3D-aware GANs suffer from three interrelated issues: Cartesian projection causes feature entanglement and mirroring artifacts, spherical projection introduces non-equal-area warping that wastes feature map capacity and creates seam/polar artifacts, and a previously unobserved cross-channel feature penetration causes persistent inter-plane interference. This paper is the first to systematically analyze all three problems and propose unified solutions.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a latent code z and a conditioning camera parameter c_con to produce a single-channel 512×512 unified feature map, which is spatially split into individual feature planes (e.g., three 256×256 planar planes and one 256×256 spherical plane in the 3+1 variant). A separate rendering camera c_ren queries these planes via volumetric rendering to produce a head image and mask, which are then upscaled by a StyleGAN2 super-resolution module to high resolution. For single-view inversion, a 2D head image is optimized via PTI to recover the latent code and camera parameters.

## Method

The core contribution is the hy-plane representation, which combines planar planes (for symmetric features) with a spherical plane (for anisotropic features) to eliminate mirroring while preserving uniform feature density. Three key innovations enable this: (1) a unify-split strategy that generates all feature planes from a single-channel feature map and then spatially partitions it, fundamentally eliminating cross-channel feature penetration; (2) a near-equal-area warping pipeline that replaces conventional θ-φ spherical mapping with Lambert azimuthal equal-area projection followed by elliptical grid mapping, maximizing square feature map utilization and removing seam artifacts; and (3) area-biased splitting that allocates a larger region of the feature map to the spherical plane to boost its expressive power for fine back-head details. A (2+2) variant uses two planar planes plus two spherical planes with opposing poles, blended via radius-based weights to fully resolve polar artifacts without relying on a hidden direction.

## Results

On the full-head image synthesis task (evaluated on 50K samples using FID and the newly proposed FID-random metric), HyPlaneHead with the hy-plane (3+1) representation and area-biased splitting achieves the best FID of 8.14 and FID-random of 9.88, while the hy-plane (2+2) variant achieves FID 8.17 and FID-random 9.84. These results outperform the strongest baselines: SphereHead (FID 8.64, FID-random 10.71), PanoHead (FID 8.77, FID-random 10.66), and EG3D tri-plane (FID 9.22, FID-random 11.23). The FID-random metric, which decouples conditioning pose from rendering pose, shows the largest relative improvement for HyPlaneHead, confirming superior view-consistency across arbitrary angles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyPlaneHead outperforms all three primary baselines on both FID and FID-random. Against EG3D's tri-plane (FID 9.22, FID-random 11.23), HyPlaneHead (3+1) reduces FID by 0.08 and FID-random by 1.35, with the FID-random gap reflecting the elimination of mirroring artifacts on the back of the head. Against PanoHead's tri-grid (FID 8.77, FID-random 10.66), HyPlaneHead improves FID by 0.63 and FID-random by 0.78, showing that adding parallel planes does not resolve the fundamental Cartesian projection limitations. Against SphereHead's single spherical tri-plane (FID 8.64, FID-random 10.71), HyPlaneHead achieves a 0.50 FID improvement and a 0.83 FID-random improvement, while also eliminating the seam and polar artifacts inherent to θ-φ warping. Notably, the dual spherical tri-plane variant of SphereHead (FID 8.68, FID-random 10.28) narrows the FID-random gap but still trails HyPlaneHead by 0.44 in FID-random, and its over-smoothed textures are visible in qualitative comparisons. In single-view 3D inversion (PTI), HyPlaneHead also produces sharper hair details and fewer symmetry artifacts compared to both PanoHead and SphereHead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tri-plane lineage (EG3D → PanoHead → SphereHead) for 3D-aware full-head synthesis by proposing a hybrid-plane (hy-plane) representation that combines Cartesian planar planes for symmetric features with a spherical plane for anisotropic features. It departs from prior work on three fronts: (1) identifying and resolving inter-channel feature penetration via a unify-split strategy that generates a single-channel unified feature map before spatially partitioning it into planes, (2) replacing the standard θ-φ warping with a Lambert azimuthal equal-area projection plus elliptical grid mapping to achieve near-equal-area square-to-sphere mapping, and (3) introducing area-biased splitting to allocate more feature map area to the spherical plane for enhanced anisotropic detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The feature penetration observation is broadly applicable to any tri-plane-like representation in 3D-aware GANs, and the unify-split strategy offers a simple architectural fix that could be adopted by EG3D, PanoHead, SphereHead, and downstream methods (e.g., LRM, InstantMesh, 3DGS-based avatars). The hy-plane representation also generalizes beyond heads to other 3D objects via the (2+2) dual-sphere variant. Limitations include modest FID improvements over SphereHead (~0.5 FID), GAN-based training that may be superseded by diffusion or 3DGS pipelines, and the near-equal-area warping still leaves a small residual distortion at the circle-to-square boundary. Open questions include whether the unify-split strategy transfers to tri-plane diffusion models and whether hy-plane can be integrated with 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-time rendering.
