## Problem

Post-training quantization of Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) to ultra-low bit-widths (W4A4) suffers severe quality degradation due to two structural challenges: extreme activation outliers create large inter-channel variance that standard Hadamard rotations fail to equalize, and the iterative denoising process induces strong temporal shifts in activation distributions that a single static transformation cannot accommodate. Existing PTQ methods for DiTs (PTQ4DiT, Q-DiT, ViDiT-Q) collapse under W4A4, producing near-random outputs.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained DiT backbones (DiT-XL/2, PixArt-Σ, STDiT3) and converts their linear-layer weights and activations from FP16 to 4-bit or 8-bit integer representations. Generation inputs are class labels (ImageNet 256×256) or free-form text prompts (COCO text-to-image, Open-Sora text-to-video), and outputs are the corresponding generated images or video sequences. Calibration requires only a small set of 16 ImageNet classes or a handful of sample prompts, and the KLT-H transformation matrix is precomputed offline and fused into weights, adding no per-step overhead for most layers.

## Method

VETA-DiT introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, a KLT-enhanced Hadamard transform (KLT-H) addresses inter-channel variance imbalance: the paper proves that a plain Hadamard rotation leaves residual variance disparity because cross-covariance terms interact differently with each Hadamard column, whereas pre-multiplying by the KLT matrix (eigenvectors of the activation covariance) followed by Hadamard yields exactly equal per-channel variance (1/m · Σλₖ), since h²ₖⱼ = 1/m for all entries. The composite matrix remains orthogonal, so the inverse is its transpose, preserving the identity (XT)(TᵀW) = XW. Second, an incoherence-aware temporal adaptation strategy computes a per-timestep incoherence score (max |X| / ‖X‖_F/√(mn)), applies a softmax-weighted importance weighting across all T timesteps, and constructs a single synthetic calibration set that over-represents high-difficulty timesteps. This one calibration set drives the KLT computation, avoiding per-timestep transformation matrices while still adapting to temporal distribution shifts. Weight quantization uses static group-wise (size 128) quantization with GPTQ-style Hessian correction; activation quantization uses dynamic group-wise quantization.

## Results

On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet 256×256, 100 steps, CFG=1.5), VETA-DiT achieves FID 5.29 and IS 152.53 under W4A8, closely matching the full-precision FID of 4.56. Under the aggressive W4A4 setting, VETA-DiT reaches FID 7.22 and IS 137.98, while all baselines collapse (FID > 40). On PixArt-Σ (COCO), VETA-DiT reduces FID by 45.76 compared to the best baseline under W4A4 (65.22 vs. 107.98 for Q-DiT). Inference speedup reaches 2.08× with 3.98× memory reduction under W4A4 on DiT-XL/2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VETA-DiT outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated setting. Under W4A8 (DiT-XL/2, 100 steps, CFG=1.5), it achieves FID 5.29, narrowly beating Q-DiT (5.34) and substantially outperforming PTQ4DiT (7.33) and ViDiT-Q (20.87). Under W4A4, the advantage becomes dramatic: VETA-DiT reaches FID 7.22 while PTQ4DiT and Q-DiT produce essentially unusable outputs (FID 274.59 and 243.05, respectively), and ViDiT-Q—without its mixed-precision strategy—still lags at FID 40.87. On PixArt-Σ (COCO, W4A4), VETA-DiT achieves FID 65.22 versus 107.98 for Q-DiT and 227.80 for ViDiT-Q, confirming the gap generalizes across architectures. On video generation (Open-Sora, VBench, W4A4), VETA-DiT leads on Scene Consistency (0.431 vs. 0.311/0.333) and Overall Consistency (0.269 vs. 0.252/0.254), while maintaining competitive scores on Subject and Background Consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hadamard-rotation-based quantization lineage from LLMs (QuaRot, QuIP-#) to Diffusion Transformers, identifying that the standard Hadamard transform leaves residual inter-channel variance imbalance in DiT activations. It introduces a KLT-enhanced Hadamard composite transform to achieve true variance equalization, and adapts the incoherence concept from QuIP/QuIP-# into a temporal calibration strategy that accounts for the iterative denoising dynamics unique to diffusion models. The method is positioned as a direct successor to PTQ4DiT, Q-DiT, and ViDiT-Q, which the authors show fail under W4A4 due to insufficient variance alignment and lack of temporal adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VETA-DiT demonstrates that W4A4 quantization of DiTs is practically feasible with acceptable visual quality (FID ~7–22 vs. FP ~4–12), a regime where all prior PTQ methods produce unusable outputs. This is significant for deploying the now-dominant DiT architecture on resource-constrained hardware, with measured 2× speedup and ~4× memory reduction. Open questions include whether the data-dependent KLT matrix generalizes across prompts and domains, whether the approach extends to audio or 3D generation, and whether hardware-native support for the composite KLT-H transform can eliminate the small online-transformation overhead.
