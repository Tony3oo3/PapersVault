## Problem

Multiview transformers must condition on camera geometry to ground visual tokens in 3D space, but existing approaches are either absolute (raymaps sensitive to arbitrary world-frame choices) or relative SE(3) encodings (CAPE, GTA) that capture only extrinsics while ignoring intrinsics. The paper asks whether a single relative encoding can capture the complete camera frustum—both intrinsics and extrinsics—while retaining the global-frame invariance and generalization benefits of relative formulations.

## Input / Output

The method takes N calibrated images (256×256 RGB) along with per-camera intrinsics K_i ∈ R^{3×3} and extrinsics T_i^{cw} ∈ SE(3), and produces task-specific outputs: a rendered novel-view image (NVS), a per-pixel depth map (stereo depth estimation via UniMatch), or a classification label identifying the inconsistent image-camera pair (spatial cognition). Camera parameters are injected at the attention level via modified self-attention blocks rather than concatenated to pixel features, so no additional input channels are required for the projective term; an optional token-level CamRay (3D ray direction in camera frame) can be concatenated to the image for hybrid conditioning.

## Method

PRoPE (Projective Positional Encoding) replaces the relative SE(3) pose relationship used by CAPE and GTA with the relative projective frustum relationship P̃_{i₁} P̃_{i₂}^{-1} ∈ R^{4×4}, which encodes both intrinsics and extrinsics between every pair of cameras. This is implemented as per-token block-diagonal matrices D_t^{PRoPE} ∈ R^{d×d} split into two d/2 × d/2 submatrices: D_t^{Proj} = I_{d/8} ⊗ P̃_{i(t)} capturing the projective frustum relationship, and D_t^{RoPE} encoding relative 2D patch positions via rotary embeddings. These matrices are injected through GTA-style attention, which transforms Q, K, and V before and after the dot-product operation. The formulation is globally frame-invariant (world-frame redefinitions cancel algebraically), reduces to relative SE(3) attention when intrinsics are identity, and reduces to standard RoPE for token pairs within the same image, making it a strict generalization of prior relative encodings.

## Results

On feedforward novel view synthesis with varying intrinsics (the most challenging setting), PRoPE achieves 21.42 PSNR / 0.247 LPIPS / 0.678 SSIM on RealEstate10K and 22.98 PSNR / 0.138 LPIPS / 0.871 SSIM on Objaverse, substantially outperforming all baselines. With constant intrinsics, PRoPE reaches 22.80 PSNR on RealEstate10K and 23.70 PSNR on Objaverse, matching GTA and exceeding Plücker raymaps by ~2.3 dB. For stereo depth estimation, integrating PRoPE into UniMatch reduces RMSE from 0.678 to 0.573 on RGBD and from 0.575 to 0.474 on Scenes11. On the spatial cognition task, PRoPE+CamRay reaches 94.3% accuracy at 17 views versus 74.6% for Plücker raymaps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRoPE decisively outperforms both absolute raymap encodings: on RealEstate10K with varying intrinsics, it gains +1.53 dB PSNR over Plücker raymaps (21.42 vs. 19.89) and +0.86 dB over Naive raymaps (21.42 vs. 20.56). Against the relative SE(3) baselines, the advantage is most dramatic with varying intrinsics: PRoPE surpasses CAPE by +5.48 dB (21.42 vs. 15.94) and GTA by +5.65 dB (21.42 vs. 15.77) on RealEstate10K, and beats GTA by +4.98 dB (22.98 vs. 18.00) on Objaverse. With constant intrinsics (Table 1), PRoPE ties GTA exactly on Objaverse (23.70 PSNR) and edges it out on RealEstate10K (22.80 vs. 22.51), confirming that PRoPE reduces to GTA when intrinsics are uniform. For stereo depth, UniMatch+PRoPE improves RMSE by 15.5% on RGBD (0.573 vs. 0.678), 13.6% on SUN3D (0.343 vs. 0.397), and 17.6% on Scenes11 (0.474 vs. 0.575). In the CAT3D comparison (Table A.2), adding PRoPE yields modest but consistent gains (e.g., 18.98 vs. 18.67 PSNR at 9 views) with zero additional parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct analogy between the NLP transition from absolute to relative positional encoding (APE → RoPE) and the analogous need in multiview vision to move from absolute camera encodings (Plücker/naive raymaps) to relative ones. It builds on GTA (Miyato et al., ICLR 2024) and CAPE (Kong et al., CVPR 2024), which inject relative SE(3) pose relationships into self-attention, but extends them by encoding the full projective frustum relationship (intrinsics × extrinsics) rather than extrinsics alone. The paper also introduces CamRay, a frame-invariant token-level ray encoding that complements attention-level relative encodings without reintroducing absolute coordinate dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic demonstration that relative camera encodings outperform absolute raymaps across tasks, scales, and out-of-distribution settings provides the multiview vision community with clear design guidance for transformer-based 3D perception. The finding that attention-level intrinsic conditioning (PRoPE) is more effective than token-level (CamRay) for intrinsics generalization—and that combining them can sometimes hurt—challenges the assumption that more camera information is always better. Open questions include numerical stability for telephoto focal lengths, extension to distorted camera models, and multi-frequency encoding for non-commutative projective transforms, all of which the authors identify as future work.
