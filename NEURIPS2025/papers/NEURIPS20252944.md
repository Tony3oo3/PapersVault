## Problem

All widely used zeroth-order gradient estimators exhibit inherent bias that persists unless the perturbation stepsize asymptotically vanishes, creating a fundamental trade-off between bias and variance in gradient-free optimization. This paper asks whether it is possible to construct gradient estimators that are exactly unbiased while relying solely on function evaluations, a question previously assumed to have a negative answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252944/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes function evaluations f(x) at a small number of perturbed points (1 to 4 depending on the estimator variant), a random direction vector v drawn from a spherical distribution, and a pre-specified decreasing perturbation stepsize sequence {μ_n} converging to zero. It produces an unbiased scalar directional derivative estimate P(n,v) that, when multiplied by v, yields an unbiased full gradient estimate. The number of function evaluations per gradient estimate is fixed at k ∈ {1,2,3,4}, with a randomly sampled index n determining which pair of stepsizes (μ_n, μ_{n+1}) is used in the finite-difference computation.

## Method

The key insight is expressing the directional derivative ∇_v f(x) as a telescoping series over a decreasing stepsize sequence {μ_n}, then reinterpreting this series as an expectation over a discrete random variable n sampled from a probability mass function {p_n}. This yields a family of unbiased estimators P_k (k=1,2,3,4) that differ in the number of function evaluations required, with P_4 generalizing Chen's [2020] four-point estimator and P_1 achieving unbiasedness with a single evaluation (though at the cost of infinite variance). The paper derives variance bounds for all four constructions and solves the non-convex optimization problem of choosing {μ_n} and {p_n} to minimize variance, showing the optimal sampling distribution is p_n = (μ_n − μ_{n+1})/μ_1. Under this optimal choice, the P_3 and P_4 estimators achieve variance O(d‖∇f‖² + d³μ²), matching the classical two-point estimator's variance while eliminating bias, and SGD with these estimators achieves the optimal O(d/ε⁴) query complexity for smooth non-convex objectives.

## Results

On synthetic gradient estimation tasks (quadratic and logistic losses), the Zipf's P₃-estimator consistently achieves lower MSE than two-point estimators across all tested dimensions (d = 16 to 4096), with the gap widening in higher dimensions. On fine-tuning OPT-1.3B on SST-2, the proposed unbiased estimators (Zipf's P₃, Zipf's P₄, Geometric P₃, Geometric P₄) achieve faster convergence and lower final training loss under the same number of function evaluations compared to standard two-point estimators. The theoretical variance bound under optimal settings matches the classical two-point estimator order O(d‖∇f‖² + d³μ²) while eliminating bias, yielding the optimal O(d/ε⁴) function query complexity for smooth non-convex objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two-point zeroth-order estimators (the standard baseline from Duchi et al. [2015]) with both Gaussian and uniform spherical perturbations, as well as a centralized two-point variant with batch size adjusted to match function evaluation counts. In the synthetic experiments (Figure 1), the Zipf's P₃-estimator achieves consistently lower MSE than all two-point variants across dimensions 16–4096 for both quadratic and logistic losses, with the advantage growing in higher dimensions. In the LLM fine-tuning experiments (Figures 2–3), the unbiased estimators outperform the two-point baselines from Malladi et al. [2023] (MeZO) on training loss when measured by number of function evaluations, confirming the theoretical bias elimination translates to practical convergence gains. When compared directly against the two-point estimator with batch size b=1 (two function evaluations per step), the unbiased methods still show advantages in per-function-evaluation efficiency, though the two-point method with larger batches can achieve lower loss per update due to reduced variance at higher per-step cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the four-point unbiased estimator of Chen (2020) into a unified family of P_k-estimators (k=1,2,3,4) parameterized by a perturbation stepsize sequence and a sampling distribution, reducing the minimum function evaluations from 4 to 1. It departs from the standard ZOO literature (one-point and two-point finite-difference estimators of Flaxman et al., Ghadimi and Lan, Duchi et al., Nesterov and Spokoiny) by showing that the inherent bias of all these estimators at finite μ can be eliminated entirely through a telescoping-series decomposition of the directional derivative. The convergence analysis directly leverages the general unbiased-SGD framework of Khaled and Richtárik (2022), while the variance analysis connects to the noisy-oracle and ℓ₁-randomization lines of work by Akhavan et al. and Gasnikov et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that unbiased ZOO estimators exist with finite function evaluations challenges a widely held assumption in the field and provides a principled alternative to biased finite-difference methods that require μ→0 for consistency. The optimal O(d/ε⁴) complexity matches the known lower bound, and the practical demonstration on OPT-1.3B fine-tuning shows tangible gains in convergence speed under matched function-evaluation budgets. Key limitations include the d³μ² variance scaling (worse than d²μ² for some biased estimators in noisy settings), the infinite variance of the P₁-estimator, and empirical validation on a single model/dataset pair. Open questions include extending the framework to noisy function evaluations, exploring variance-reduced variants, and whether the optimal sampling distributions generalize beyond geometric and Zipf families.
