## Problem

Reconstruction-based anomaly detection methods trained exclusively on normal data can generalize to certain abnormal inputs, producing well-reconstructed anomalies with low reconstruction error and degraded detection performance. The key open question is how to make reconstruction discrepancies sufficiently discriminative for every abnormal input, rather than relying on ad-hoc masking strategies that cannot cover all abnormal patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254511/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts tabular feature vectors, natural images (CIFAR-10, 32×32), or industrial images (MVTec AD, resized to 224×224), each first encoded into a fixed-dimensional representation by a task-specific feature extractor (3-layer MLP, 3-layer CNN, or frozen WideResNet-50 with multi-layer feature fusion). It produces a scalar anomaly score combining ℓ₂ reconstruction error and negative log-likelihood, and for industrial images additionally yields a pixel-level anomaly map via patch-embedding-level scoring.

## Method

The core contribution is an invertible contraction mapping (CM) flow φ(·) = F_R ∘ … ∘ F_1(·), where each unit is f(x) = ((Id+G)/2)⁻¹(x) − x with G parameterized by i-DenseNet using LipSwish activations to enforce Lipschitz constant < 1. Training with a reconstruction loss on normal data provably places the CM's unique fixed point within the normal manifold (Proposition 1: ‖x*−x‖ ≤ L_rec/(1−η)), so abnormal inputs are iteratively pulled toward the normal manifold, inflating their reconstruction error. The same flow is invertible, enabling a backward density-estimation branch that applies the change-of-variables formula through φ⁻¹(·) with a learnable Gaussian prior fitted to the reconstructed output, yielding the input log-likelihood. The final anomaly score is S(x) = ‖φ(x)−x‖₂ − α·log p(x), and the entire bidirectional framework is trained end-to-end with L_total = L_rec + λ·L_MLE.

## Results

On 20 tabular anomaly detection datasets, the proposed method achieves an average AP of 78.64 and AUROC of 91.19, outperforming the strongest baseline MCM (74.86/90.44) by +3.78 AP and +0.75 AUROC. On CIFAR-10 (one-class setting), the method achieves the best per-class AUROC on 5 out of 10 classes (airplane 83.1, cat 73.4, deer 75.1, frog 85.2, horse 76.2). On MVTec AD industrial defect detection, the method reaches 99.5 image-level AUROC and 98.3 pixel-level AUROC, surpassing RD4AD (98.5/97.8) and approaching PatchCore (99.1/98.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On tabular data, the method outperforms the strongest reconstruction-based baseline MCM by +3.78 AP and +0.75 AUROC on average, with particularly large gains on Census (+13.48 AP), Glass (+31.62 AP), and Fraud (+14.07 AP), while losing slightly on Arrhythmia, Campaign, NSL-KDD, Optdigits, Pima, and Satellite. Among density-estimation baselines, it surpasses DSVDD by +23.13 AP and ICL by +18.37 AP on average. On CIFAR-10, the method beats DO2HSC (the strongest visual baseline) on airplane (+1.8), cat (+2.2), deer (+2.2), frog (+2.2), and horse (+0.7) AUROC, but trails on automobile (78.4 vs. 82.7), bird (69.8 vs. 71.3), dog (70.9 vs. 72.8), ship (82.3 vs. 84.4), and truck (80.2 vs. 82.0). On MVTec AD, the method achieves 99.5/98.3 image/pixel-level AUROC, surpassing RD4AD (+1.0/+0.5) and CFlow (+1.2/−0.3), but slightly trailing PatchCore (99.1/98.1) on image-level AUROC while matching it on pixel-level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the invertible monotone operator framework (Ahn et al., NeurIPS 2022) and i-DenseNet (Perugachi-Diaz et al., NeurIPS 2021) to construct a Lipschitz-constrained invertible network, but repurposes it through the lens of Banach fixed-point theory: the network is explicitly designed as a contraction mapping so that iterative application drives any input toward a fixed point guaranteed to lie on the normal manifold. This departs from prior reconstruction-based anomaly detection methods (MCM, UniAD) that rely on masking strategies to prevent well-reconstructed anomalies, and from prior normalizing-flow-based density estimators (CFlow-AD, SANFlow) that model the input distribution directly. The key conceptual move is unifying both paradigms—forward reconstruction via the contraction flow and backward density estimation via its inverse—within a single invertible architecture, rather than treating them as separate modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded solution to the 'well-reconstructed anomaly' problem that plagues reconstruction-based one-class classifiers, with Propositions 1 and 2 giving explicit bounds on how close the fixed point is to normal data. The unification of reconstruction and density estimation in one invertible flow is architecturally elegant and could inspire similar bidirectional designs in other generative or discriminative settings. However, the method is currently limited to tabular and image data (no graph or time-series), is sensitive to the K×R depth hyperparameter (too many iterations cause over-convergence to the fixed point, degrading detection), and the practical gains over strong baselines like MCM and RD4AD are modest (+3.78 AP on tabular, +1.0 image-level AUROC on MVTec). Open questions include whether the contraction property can be relaxed or adapted for structured data, and whether the fixed-point guarantee holds robustly under complex or multimodal normal distributions.
