## Problem

Large feed-forward reconstruction models have not been systematically applied to autonomous driving, where existing per-pixel Gaussian prediction causes artifacts in multi-view overlapping regions and a fixed Gaussian budget limits quality on complex objects. Additionally, prior methods supervise dynamic objects only with same-moment images, restricting geometric and appearance fidelity across time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255599/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4 temporal multi-view surround images (at 0s, 0.5s, 1.0s, 1.5s intervals) at 160×240 (Waymo) or 224×400 (nuScenes) resolution, along per-camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters that project directly into a world coordinate system anchored at the video start frame. It outputs a set of 4D Gaussians per pixel with position, RGB, opacity, scale, rotation, semantic logits, and per-point optical flow (Δxyz, Δr), enabling single-pass rendering of novel views at arbitrary camera poses and timestamps, as well as semantic segmentation and motion prediction of scene elements.

## Method

DrivingRecon uses a shared 2D image encoder feeding a range-view decoder whose core unit is the Prune and Dilate Block (PD-Block): it defines K spatial centers, computes pairwise cosine similarity to threshold and remove redundant Gaussians from overlapping views, then aggregates long-range (large-kernel) features for complex regions while retaining local features for simple backgrounds, effectively reallocating Gaussian budget by scene complexity. An unaligned Gaussian Adapter predicts per-point UV-coordinate shifts so pruned/dilated Gaussians need not be pixel-aligned, and world coordinates are recovered via the standard pinhole projection with the refined depth. Static-dynamic decoupling supervises dynamic Gaussians with next-frame images (cross-temporal L1 loss) and static Gaussians with multi-timestamp rendering (alpha-thresholded), while optional segmentation labels provide supervised semantic constraints. The full model has 53.37M parameters and is trained for 50k iterations on 24 A100 GPUs with AdamW at lr 4e-4 under bfloat16, achieving 0.08s inference per scene.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, DrivingRecon achieves 27.52 PSNR / 0.833 SSIM / 5.14 D-RMSE on full-image reconstruction and 24.01 PSNR / 0.662 SSIM / 7.03 D-RMSE on dynamic-only regions, outperforming the strongest feed-forward baseline STORM (26.38 PSNR / 0.794 SSIM / 5.48 D-RMSE) by over 1 dB in PSNR while running 2.25× faster (0.08 s vs. 0.18 s per frame). On nuScenes, DrivingRecon reaches 25.42 PSNR, 0.782 SSIM, 0.201 LPIPS, and 0.825 PCC, surpassing Omni-Scene (24.27 PSNR, 0.736 SSIM) and DrivingForward (24.32 PSNR, 0.732 SSIM). The model also demonstrates strong pre-training transfer: fine-tuning UniAD with the DrivingRecon encoder yields 53.21 NDS and 43.21 mAP on nuScenes, exceeding ViDAR (52.57 NDS, 42.33 mAP) across detection, tracking, occupancy prediction, mapping, motion forecasting, and planning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against per-scene optimization methods (EmerNeRF, 3DGS, PVG, DeformableGS), DrivingRecon achieves substantially higher PSNR (27.52 vs. 22.38–25.29) and lower D-RMSE (5.14 vs. 13.01–33.99) while being orders of magnitude faster (0.08 s vs. 14–29 min). Among feed-forward baselines, DrivingRecon outperforms STORM (the strongest competitor) by +1.14 PSNR on full image and +1.91 PSNR on dynamic regions, with 2.25× lower inference latency. It also surpasses SCube and DrivingForward by +1.80 and +1.20 PSNR respectively on full-image metrics. On nuScenes, DrivingRecon leads Omni-Scene by +1.15 PSNR and +0.021 PCC, and DrivingForward by +1.10 PSNR and +0.059 PCC. In the pre-training transfer experiment (Table 5), the DrivingRecon encoder fine-tuned on UniAD beats ViDAR by +0.64 NDS and +0.88 mAP on detection, and improves planning L2 error from 0.91 to 0.84. For domain generalization (Waymo→nuScenes), DrivingRecon with extrinsic augmentation achieves 0.490 NDS, exceeding PD-BEV (0.478) and DG-BEV (0.472).

## Relation to Prior Work

DrivingRecon extends the feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction paradigm (LGM, GS-LRM) to the 4D autonomous driving domain, but departs from the universal 'one Gaussian per pixel' assumption that all prior feed-forward methods share. Its core architectural innovation, the PD-Block, borrows the set-of-points aggregation idea from Image as Set of Points [36] to adaptively prune redundant Gaussians in overlapping multi-view regions and dilate them around complex objects. The range-view decoder is adapted from LiDAR-based detection work (RangeDet [13]), repurposing the range-view representation for efficient multi-view feature fusion in a reconstruction context. The shared 2D encoder design positions the model within the broader autonomous driving perception stack (BEVFormer, UniAD), enabling the reconstruction model to double as a pre-training backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.08s inference time with only 53M parameters (vs. STORM's 100M) makes large-scale 4D driving reconstruction practically deployable for simulation, data augmentation, and closed-loop evaluation—tasks that previously required per-scene optimization taking 14–29 minutes. The demonstrated pre-training transfer to UniAD (outperforming ViDAR on detection, tracking, occupancy, and planning) suggests that 4D reconstruction encoders can serve as a general-purpose geometric pre-training signal for end-to-end driving, a direction not previously explored. Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions and longer temporal sequences, whether the fixed PD-Block threshold generalizes across diverse driving conditions (night, rain, dense urban), and how the method performs when integrated into a full closed-loop driving stack rather than evaluated in isolation.
