## Problem

In-context learning (ICL) performance of transformers is fundamentally bottlenecked by the small number of labeled demonstrations that fit in a prompt, while vast amounts of unlabeled data remain underutilized. No prior theoretical work has established whether and how unlabeled data can provably improve ICL accuracy. This paper is the first to provide a rigorous answer, showing that unlabeled data in the prompt can strictly reduce prediction error beyond what labeled data alone permits.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prompt embedding matrix composed of three column blocks: N labeled (x, y) pairs, M unlabeled inputs x, and C initial reasoning tokens encoding class identities and zero mean estimates. Through T chain-of-thought steps, the transformer appends C new tokens per step, each carrying an updated class mean estimate. The final output is the T-th step's class mean vectors, from which labels for all M unlabeled samples are assigned via nearest-mean (maximum likelihood) decoding. The data model assumes a C-component isotropic Gaussian mixture with shared covariance Σ.

## Method

A 4-layer transformer with CoT prompting implements an EM algorithm for Gaussian mixture clustering: Layer 1 (softmax attention) computes soft class-membership weights over all prior CoT steps (E-step), Layers 2–3 (linear attention) perform one gradient-descent step on the weighted log-likelihood to update class means (M-step), and Layer 4 (ReLU attention) injects the labeled-data mean estimate only at step 0. The key theoretical result is that after t CoT steps the class-mean estimation error scales as O(log(1/ε)/(N + M^{1/4})), strictly improving the Ω(1/√N) lower bound for any labeled-only classifier. For training, the paper proves that gradient descent on a teacher-forcing population loss (cross-entropy between the transformer's CoT trajectory and a reference EM trajectory) drives the first layer's attention matrix W^{(k)} to Σ^{-1} at a linear rate, using a novel Stein's-lemma-based gradient decomposition and isotropy preservation argument.

## Results

Theoretically, the augmented ICL transformer achieves a class mean estimation error of O(log(1/ε)/(N + M^{1/4})) and an excess risk (label prediction error) of O(1/√(N + poly(M))), strictly improving over the O(1/√N) lower bound for any classifier using only N labeled samples. Empirically, with C=3 classes, d=3, N=5 labeled examples, and T=5 CoT steps, the trained transformer's class mean estimation error decreases and classification accuracy increases as M grows from 1 to 20 unlabeled samples. The advantage of augmented ICL over conventional ICL (M=1) is more pronounced when the noise level ε is smaller (ε=0.7 vs. ε=1.5), and the augmented ICL approach surpasses the Bayes-optimal classifier that relies solely on labeled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a traditional numerical comparison table; results are presented in Figure 1 as learning curves over 15,000 gradient descent iterations. The primary comparison is between augmented ICL (M=10 or M=20 unlabeled samples) and conventional ICL (M=1, which recovers the standard few-shot setting). Augmented ICL consistently outperforms conventional ICL in both class mean estimation error and label prediction accuracy, with the gap widening as M increases. A second implicit baseline is the Bayes-optimal classifier with known class means but only N labeled samples, which augmented ICL also surpasses. Theoretically, the paper's O(1/√(N+poly(M))) excess risk bound strictly improves upon the Ω(1/√N) minimax lower bound for any classifier trained on N labeled data alone (Li et al., 2017), establishing a provable advantage of incorporating unlabeled data. The concurrent work of Li et al. (2025) studies a similar semi-supervised ICL setting but only for a linear transformer in binary classification with asymptotic guarantees, whereas this paper handles a softmax-attention multi-layer transformer in the general multi-class setting with non-asymptotic bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work showing that transformers can emulate optimization algorithms in-context (gradient descent, preconditioned GD) by demonstrating that a 4-layer transformer with CoT prompting can implement an EM algorithm for Gaussian mixture clustering, combining labeled and unlabeled data. It departs from prior ICL studies that rely solely on labeled demonstrations or from pseudo-label approaches that inherit model biases, instead directly processing unlabeled inputs within the prompt. The training analysis builds on the teacher forcing framework for CoT transformers (Kim & Suzuki, 2025; Huang et al., 2025b), but introduces a novel gradient decomposition via Stein's lemma to handle the multi-step CoT loss. A concurrent work (Li et al., 2025) also studies semi-supervised ICL but in a simpler linear-attention, binary-classification, asymptotic setting, whereas this paper uses softmax attention, multi-class classification, and provides non-asymptotic guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical study establishing that unlabeled data can provably enhance transformer ICL performance, with a clean rate improvement from Ω(1/√N) to O(1/√(N+poly(M))) that strictly beats any labeled-only classifier. However, the analysis is restricted to multi-class linear classification with isotropic covariance, only the first transformer layer is trained (others are frozen with hand-constructed parameters), and experiments are on small synthetic data (d=3, C=3, N=5). Key open questions include whether these benefits transfer to real-world tasks and large pretrained transformers, whether the isotropic covariance assumption is essential, and how multi-layer end-to-end training dynamics behave under the CoT objective.
