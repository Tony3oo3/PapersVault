## Problem

Existing statistical theory for OT map estimation hinges on Brenier's theorem (quadratic cost, absolutely continuous source) to guarantee a unique deterministic map, then imposes further regularity (Lipschitzness, Hölder smoothness, density bounds) to derive error rates. In many real-world settings—domain adaptation with lower-dimensional sources, single-cell trajectories that branch—these conditions fail and optimal transport is intrinsically stochastic, leaving no applicable estimation theory. This paper closes that gap by introducing a general-purpose error metric for stochastic transport kernels and developing near-optimal finite-sample guarantees under minimal assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. samples from an unknown source distribution μ and n i.i.d. samples from an unknown target distribution ν (both on subsets of ℝ^d), and outputs a Markov kernel κ: X → Y (a regular conditional probability distribution) that transports μ to ν. Unlike prior work that recovers a single deterministic map T*, the output is a full stochastic map that can split mass at each source point. The quality of the kernel is measured by the proposed E_p metric, which decomposes into an optimality gap (excess transport cost over W_p(μ,ν)) and a feasibility gap (W_p distance between the kernel's pushforward and ν), and is well-defined even when no deterministic optimal map exists.

## Method

The paper introduces the error functional E_p(κ; μ, ν) = [transport cost − W_p(μ,ν)]_+ + W_p(κ_#μ, ν), which vanishes exactly on optimal kernels and generalizes the L^p(μ) benchmark to the stochastic setting. The technical foundation is a suite of stability lemmas bounding how E_p responds to TV and W_p perturbations of the input measures and to kernel composition. Building on these, a rounding-based estimator (round empirical source onto a grid, solve a near-optimal discrete OT problem, compose back) achieves E_p error Õ(n^{-1/(d+2p)}) under sub-Gaussian tails, matching a minimax lower bound up to the d∨2p vs. d+2p exponent. When an optimal kernel is Hölder continuous, a Wasserstein-DRO estimator reduces kernel estimation to distribution estimation, recovering the optimal n^{-1/(d∨2p)} rate. The framework further extends to adversarial robustness: under combined TV (global outliers) and W_p (local perturbations) corruptions, a Gaussian-convolution estimator achieves error √d·ε^{1/p} + √d·ρ^{1/(p+1)} + sampling term, with a matching lower bound showing a separation from robust distribution estimation.

## Results

The rounding estimator achieves E[E_p(κ̂_n; μ, ν)] = Õ_{p,d}(n^{-1/(d+2p)}) under sub-Gaussian assumptions (Theorem 2), which is near-optimal given the minimax lower bound of Ω(n^{-1/(d∨2p)}). Under a Lipschitz optimal kernel assumption, the WDRO-based estimator recovers the minimax rate Õ_{p,d}(n^{-1/(d∨2p)}) (Corollary 1). For robust estimation under combined TV (budget ε) and Wasserstein (budget ρ) corruptions, the convolutional estimator achieves error √d·ε^{1/p} + √d·ρ^{1/(p+1)} + O_{p,d}(n^{-1/(d+2p)}) (Theorem 4). In experiments (Setting A, d=3, n=100), the NN estimator's L¹ error remains above 1 while its E₁ error drops to ~0.3, illustrating the practical advantage of the proposed metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its rounding kernel estimator against the nearest-neighbor (NN) map estimator of Manole et al. [2024] in two synthetic settings with irregular OT maps. Under the E₁ metric, the NN estimator empirically outperforms the rounding estimator in low dimensions (d=3), but the gap diminishes in higher dimensions (d=10), where the rounding estimator's formal guarantees become more relevant. Critically, the L¹ error of the NN estimator is always greater than 1 in Setting A, rendering it useless as a quality measure, while E₁ correctly captures the estimator's strong transport performance. Theoretically, the paper's entropic kernel rate of n^{-1/(4d∨8)} for p=2 strictly improves upon the formal limit rate of n^{-1/(4d+4)} from Pooladian and Niles-Weed [2021], and the WDRO-based rate of n^{-1/(d∨2p)} matches Balakrishnan and Manole [2025] under a significantly weaker assumption (Lipschitz kernel vs. gradient of a smooth convex potential with bounded eigenvalues).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the Brenier map estimation literature (Hutter & Rigollet 2021, Pooladian & Niles-Weed 2021, Manole et al. 2024), which requires a unique deterministic map guaranteed by Brenier's theorem and imposes density/smoothness assumptions to obtain L^p error bounds. It builds on the Kantorovich relaxation (stochastic maps via Markov kernels), entropic OT solvers (Cuturi 2013) for computation, and Wasserstein DRO (Gao et al. 2022) for the Hölder-continuous kernel analysis. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the E_p error metric, which measures transportation quality without requiring map existence or uniqueness, and the connection to the Monge gap regularizer of Uscidda & Cuturi (2023), which the paper shows coincides with E_p up to constants when p=1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general-purpose finite-sample theory for stochastic OT map estimation, covering practical settings (domain adaptation across manifolds of different dimension, single-cell developmental trajectories with branching) where deterministic Brenier maps provably do not exist. The near-optimal n^{-1/(d+2p)} rate under only sub-Gaussian tail assumptions, combined with robust guarantees under combined TV + Wasserstein adversarial corruptions, provides a practical toolkit for evaluating and training transport maps in these previously unaddressed regimes. A notable open question is the gap between the upper bound n^{-1/(d+2p)} and the minimax lower bound n^{-1/(d∨2p)}, and whether a computationally efficient estimator can close it under Lipschitz kernel assumptions. The robust estimation separation (√ρ vs. ρ dependence) reveals a structural limitation: one cannot reduce robust map estimation to robust distribution estimation without loss.
