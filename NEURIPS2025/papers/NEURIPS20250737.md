## Problem

Text-driven talking head generation remains underexplored relative to audio-driven methods, and existing cascaded TTS-plus-THG pipelines suffer from error propagation, computational redundancy, and audio-visual style mismatches. No prior one-shot framework can jointly generate synchronized speech and facial animation from text while simultaneously replicating a target person's full speaking style—encompassing vocal timbre, head motion patterns, and facial expression dynamics—in real time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250737/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a driving text string and a 1–10 second reference video as input. The reference video is decomposed into a 100-dim log-mel spectrogram (94 fps) for audio style and per-frame visual codes (51 blendshapes + 6-DOF head pose + 4 eye-gaze coefficients, upsampled from 30 to 94 fps) for visual style; the reference audio is also transcribed via ASR to form a reference text sequence. Output is a synchronized pair: speech audio decoded by the Vocos vocoder and a 512×512 talking-head video rendered at 50 fps by a warp-based GAN, achieving 25 FPS end-to-end inference on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

OmniTalker employs a dual-branch diffusion transformer with 22 shared audio-visual fusion blocks that use multi-modal joint attention (MM-Attention, where Q/K/V are derived jointly from both modalities with RoPE) to enforce cross-modal alignment, followed by 4 single-modality DiT blocks per branch for modality-specific refinement. Training uses flow matching with a masked-infilling strategy: random temporal segments of the audio-visual sequence are occluded and the model reconstructs them conditioned on the unmasked context and concatenated reference-plus-driving text (embedded via ConvNeXt-V2), enabling in-context style transfer without dedicated style-extraction modules. Multi-condition classifier-free guidance (separately dropping reference audio, reference visual codes, and text) further sharpens style replication. The 0.8B-parameter model is trained on 690 hours of curated multimodal data and achieves real-time 25 FPS inference by combining the efficient DiT backbone with a lightweight Vocos vocoder and a warp-based GAN renderer.

## Results

On VoxCeleb2, OmniTalker achieves the best FVD (102.54), highest CSIM (0.890), and near-best Sync-C (6.37) among all compared talking head methods, while achieving orders-of-magnitude lower E-FID (0.02) and P-FID (0.04) than all baselines, demonstrating superior style preservation. On the Custom dataset, it similarly leads in FVD (176.32), CSIM (0.905), E-FID (0.08), and P-FID (0.12). For the TTS component, OmniTalker achieves WER of 1.55% (SEED-ZH) and 1.61% (Custom-ZH) with SIM-A of 0.766–0.771, matching or exceeding F5-TTS. The method runs at 25 FPS on a single RTX 4090, far outpacing diffusion-based baselines (EchoMimic at 0.78 FPS, Hallo at 0.65 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two GAN-based methods (SadTalker, AniTalker), OmniTalker substantially outperforms on all five video metrics: FVD drops from 243.92/181.58 to 102.54, E-FID from 0.67/0.94 to 0.02, and P-FID from 3.37/1.08 to 0.04, while matching or exceeding their inference speed. Against the style-aware StyleTalk, OmniTalker achieves dramatically lower E-FID (0.02 vs. 0.71) and P-FID (0.04 vs. 0.31), showing it captures both expression and head-pose styles rather than simply copying the reference pose sequence. Against the diffusion-based SOTA methods EchoMimic and Hallo, OmniTalker wins on FVD (102.54 vs. 191.77/156.33), CSIM (0.890 vs. 0.883/0.879), E-FID (0.02 vs. 0.60/0.59), and P-FID (0.04 vs. 2.74/1.03), while trailing slightly on Sync-C (6.37 vs. 5.88/6.53) — a gap the authors attribute to Sync-C favoring front-facing outputs. In the TTS comparison (Table 1), OmniTalker matches F5-TTS on WER (1.55% vs. 1.56% on SEED-ZH) and beats it on SIM-A (0.766 vs. 0.741), while clearly outperforming CosyVoice and MaskGCT on both metrics. The 25 FPS inference speed is 32–38× faster than EchoMimic and Hallo, making it the only method achieving true real-time performance end-to-end.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the dual-single-stream hybrid DiT architecture from text-to-image synthesis (Esser et al. 2024; Flux) for joint audio-video generation, replacing the standard cascaded TTS + audio-driven talking-head pipeline with a unified flow-matching framework. It inherits the two-stage motion-prediction-then-rendering paradigm from audio-driven THG work (VASA-1, LivePortrait) but conditions on text rather than audio, and introduces a masked-infilling strategy borrowed from LLM in-context learning to achieve style transfer without dedicated style extraction modules. The GAN-based visual renderer is adapted from the LivePortrait/VASA-1 lineage, while the audio decoder uses Vocos, keeping the system within the established real-time rendering ecosystem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling real-time (25 FPS) text-to-talking-head generation with full multimodal style replication on a single consumer GPU, which is directly applicable to conversational AI, virtual avatars, and interactive media. Key limitations include head-only generation (no hands or full body), GAN renderer artifacts under large motion amplitudes, and the reliance on a 3DMM/FaceVerse motion representation that constrains expressiveness. Open questions include whether the in-context style transfer approach generalizes to longer sequences or multi-speaker scenarios, and whether replacing the GAN renderer with a video diffusion model (as the authors suggest) would resolve quality degradation at scale without sacrificing real-time performance.
