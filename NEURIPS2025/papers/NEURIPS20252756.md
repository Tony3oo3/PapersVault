## Problem

Existing activation steering methods (e.g., Lin-ACT, ITI-c) optimize each layer's intervention map independently, ignoring how perturbations at one layer propagate through downstream layers. This local, layer-wise tuning produces causal inconsistencies that cause unintended distributional shifts out-of-sample and precludes global trade-offs such as sparse neuron selection across layers. The paper asks whether a single global distributional loss, optimized end-to-end across all intervened layers, can yield more robust and precise steering with the same low-data, weakly-supervised setup.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252756/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two unpaired sets of N prompts (e.g., 32 toxic and 32 non-toxic sentences, or 32 prompts with and without a target concept) and a frozen pretrained model (LLM or single-step T2I). It outputs a set of per-layer diagonal affine maps (one scale vector ω_ℓ and one bias vector b_ℓ per intervened layer) that are applied at inference time with a continuous strength parameter λ ∈ [0,1], producing steered text continuations or steered images. No paired counterfactuals, reward model, or human preference labels are required; the only tunable hyperparameter beyond the base model is the sparsity coefficient γ.

## Method

LinEAS interlaces L lightweight affine maps T_ℓ(z) = ω_ℓ ⊙ z + b_ℓ between the frozen layers of a pretrained model and trains all maps jointly by minimizing a global loss: the sum over layers of sliced Wasserstein-2 distances between the transported source activations and the untouched target activations, where each layer's distance is computed as the L2 norm between coordinate-wise sorted activation vectors. The key distinction from prior work (Lin-ACT, ITI-c) is that this global, end-to-end objective accounts for causal interdependencies across layers, so each map is optimized knowing how upstream maps alter the input distribution it receives. A sparse group lasso regularizer (combining ℓ1 and ℓ2 norms on ω_ℓ − 1 and b_ℓ) is added to the loss, enabling automatic selection of which neurons and layers to intervene on; the full objective is optimized with proximal SGD using soft-thresholding and group-shrinkage proximal operators. The approach requires only ~32 unpaired samples per set and adds fewer than 0.25M parameters to models up to 7B.

## Results

On toxicity mitigation with only 32 unpaired sentences per set, LinEAS achieves the best toxicity reduction among all steering methods across three LLMs. On Gemma2-2B, LinEAS reduces Tox_RTP from 4.00 to 0.73 (5.5× reduction) and Tox_TET from 13.39 to 4.02, while maintaining MMLU at 52.22 (vs. 53.03 baseline) and PPL_WIK at 15.46 (vs. 14.79). On Qwen2.5-7B, LinEAS reduces Tox_TET from 25.16 to 14.95 with minimal utility loss (MMLU 73.67 vs. 74.26). For text-to-image generation on DMD2, LinEAS achieves 63.3% user preference and 0.66 IMGScore (perceptual fidelity), substantially outperforming Lin-ACT (24.4%, 0.45) and ITI-c (12.4%, 0.24).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LinEAS consistently outperforms all weakly-supervised steering baselines on toxicity mitigation. Against Lin-ACT (the closest prior method), LinEAS reduces Tox_RTP by 28–54% (e.g., 0.73 vs. 1.60 on Gemma2-2B) and Tox_TET by 20–31% (e.g., 4.02 vs. 7.76 on Gemma2-2B), while maintaining comparable utility. Against ITI-c, LinEAS achieves 25–42% lower Tox_RTP (e.g., 1.95 vs. 2.63 on Qwen2.5-7B) with similar PPL and MMLU. CAA and ReFT, which are designed for paired data, suffer severe utility degradation when forced into the unpaired toxicity setting (e.g., CAA drops MMLU from 60.95 to 37.67 on Qwen2.5-1.5B), making their toxicity numbers unreliable. Against the oracle LoFIT-RL (which uses RTC toxicity labels as supervision), LinEAS achieves comparable utility (MMLU within 1 point, PPL within 0.6) but higher residual toxicity (e.g., 1.07 vs. 0.37 Tox_RTP on Qwen2.5-1.5B), reflecting the advantage of strong supervision. For T2I generation, LinEAS is preferred by users 63.3% of the time and achieves 0.66 IMGScore versus 0.45 (Lin-ACT) and 0.24 (ITI-c), while all methods reach similar CLIPScore (~0.18–0.19) for concept removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

LinEAS extends the activation steering lineage initiated by CAA, ITI-c, and Lin-ACT, which all intervene on intermediate activations to shift model behavior. Its closest predecessor, Lin-ACT (Rodríguez et al., 2025), also uses coordinate-wise affine maps but optimizes each layer independently in a sequential, closed-form manner; LinEAS departs by jointly optimizing all layer maps end-to-end with a global sliced Wasserstein loss that accounts for inter-layer causal dependencies. The addition of a sparse group lasso regularizer (Simon et al., 2013) for automatic neuron and layer selection is a novel coupling of optimal-transport-based steering with structured sparsity, and the method's reliance on unpaired source/target distributions distinguishes it from paired-data methods like ReFT and CAA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LinEAS demonstrates that a handful of unpaired examples (32 per set) suffice for effective, modality-agnostic steering that is competitive with oracle-supervised baselines, making low-budget alignment practical for both LLMs and single-step T2I models. The finding that sparsity regularization can reduce intervened support by ~100× while *improving* utility metrics (lower PPL, higher MMLU) offers a principled way to preserve model capability. Open problems remain: compositionality of multiple simultaneous interventions is still weak (only 19% dual-concept success), and the intervention is applied uniformly to all tokens without selective gating, limiting fine-grained control. The surprising observation that inverting the learned affine maps switches from concept removal to concept induction hints at exploitable geometric structure in activation space that warrants further investigation.
