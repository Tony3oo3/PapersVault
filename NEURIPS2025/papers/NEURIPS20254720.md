## Problem

Despite being highly overparameterized, practical diffusion models rarely memorize their training data, yet the mechanism behind the memorization-to-generalization transition remains poorly understood. Existing explanations—architectural inductive biases, finite learning rates, and early stopping—do not fully account for the observed regime shift, particularly the fact that the transition time depends on training set size in a way not captured by static capacity arguments.

## Input / Output

The numerical experiments use 32×32 grayscale CelebA face images as training data, with a U-Net score model conditioned on diffusion time t via sinusoidal embeddings. The model is trained to predict the injected noise (equivalently, the score) at each noise level, and generation proceeds by reversing the forward Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process from Gaussian noise. The key outputs tracked over training time τ are the FID (sample quality) and the memorization fraction f_mem (fraction of generated samples matching training data), which together delineate the generalization window [τ_gen, τ_mem].

## Method

The paper combines numerical experiments with a tractable theoretical analysis. Numerically, U-Net DDPMs of varying capacity (p from 0.26M to 16M parameters) are trained on CelebA with varying n (128 to 32768), revealing two well-separated timescales: τ_gen ≈ 100K steps (independent of n) for high-quality generation, and τ_mem ∝ n for the onset of memorization. Theoretically, a Random Features Neural Network score model is analyzed in the high-dimensional limit (d, p, n → ∞) using the replica method and random matrix theory to compute the eigenvalue spectrum of the feature correlation matrix U. In the overparameterized regime (ψ_p ≫ ψ_n ≫ 1), the spectrum splits into two well-separated bulks: a high-eigenvalue bulk ρ_2 governing the fast generalization timescale (order 1/Δ_t) and a low-eigenvalue bulk ρ_1 governing the slow memorization timescale (order ψ_n/Δ_t). The underlying mechanism is spectral bias: the empirical score decomposes into a low-frequency component close to the population score (learned quickly) and a high-frequency, dataset-specific component (learned slowly), and the gap between these learning rates widens linearly with n.

## Results

The paper identifies two well-separated training timescales in diffusion models: τ_gen ≈ 100K SGD steps (independent of training set size n) at which FID reaches its minimum and high-quality generation begins, and τ_mem which scales linearly with n (curves collapse when time is rescaled by τ/n, with memorization onset at τ/n ≈ 300). This creates a generalization window [τ_gen, τ_mem] that widens linearly with n. For the largest training set (n = 32,768), the test loss remains close to the training loss even after 11 million SGD steps, indicating that overfitting effectively disappears. Model capacity also modulates both timescales: τ_gen ∝ W⁻¹ and τ_mem ∝ nW⁻¹, where W is the U-Net base width (p ranges from 0.26M to 16M parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a characterization study rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark a proposed method against competing baselines. The theoretical analysis builds directly on George et al. [13], who computed asymptotic training and test losses for a Random Features Neural Network at τ→∞; the present work extends that framework to finite training times and identifies the dynamical origin of the two timescales. Numerically, the paper compares different training set sizes (n = 128 to 32,768) and model capacities (W = 8 to 64, i.e., p = 0.26M to 16M) on CelebA 32×32 grayscale images, showing that larger n consistently postpones memorization while leaving generation quality unchanged. The key finding is that the memorization-generalization transition is not due to data repetition (full-batch training still yields τ_mem ∝ n) but to a fundamental n-dependent change in the loss landscape, with the empirical score's high-frequency component requiring longer to learn as n grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the random-features score-matching analysis of George et al. [13], which computed asymptotic (τ→∞) training and test losses, by studying the full training dynamics and identifying two distinct timescales (τ_gen and τ_mem) that govern the generalization-to-memorization transition. It also builds on empirical memorization studies in diffusion models [16, 57, 8, 45] and on the spectral-bias literature for neural networks [38, 59], connecting the frequency-dependent learning speed to the delayed memorization observed in score-based generative models. The key departure from prior theoretical work is the explicit treatment of finite training time and early stopping, rather than only the infinite-time limit, revealing a dynamical regularization mechanism that was previously uncharacterized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that τ_mem scales linearly with n while τ_gen remains constant provides a concrete, actionable guideline: practitioners can safely train longer with larger datasets before memorization sets in, and early stopping within the [τ_gen, τ_mem] window avoids memorization even in highly overparameterized settings. The spectral-bias mechanism (low-frequency population score learned first, high-frequency empirical score learned later) offers a unifying explanation that connects DM memorization to a well-studied phenomenon in supervised learning. Open questions include how these timescales shift under Adam optimization, in conditional/guided settings, and for richer data distributions beyond Gaussian; the current theoretical analysis is restricted to random features and isotropic data, and the empirical range of model capacity (0.26M–16M parameters) is still limited for mapping the full (n,p) phase diagram.
