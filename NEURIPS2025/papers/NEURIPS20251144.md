## Problem

Existing token reduction methods for Vision Transformers (pruning and merging) implicitly discard high-frequency token information, accelerating the rank-collapsing and over-smoothing phenomena that limit ViT expressiveness. No prior method explicitly accounts for the frequency-domain structure of self-attention when deciding which tokens to retain or aggregate, leading to unnecessary loss of the high-frequency signals that ViTs still rely on despite the collapsing trend.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251144/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard 224×224 RGB images processed through pretrained ViT/DeiT backbones (patch size 16, yielding 196 tokens plus CLS). It reduces the token sequence at selected intermediate layers (e.g., 4th, 7th, 10th) by retaining a fraction τ of high-frequency tokens and replacing the rest with a small number of spatially-local DC tokens, while preserving the original input/output interface: a class label for ImageNet-1K classification or a per-pixel segmentation mask for ADE20K dense prediction via the Segmenter decoder.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes each attention matrix A into a low-pass component A^LP = (1/n)·11ᵀ and a high-pass residual A^HP = A − A^LP. Tokens are then partitioned by their column-wise mean weight in A^HP: the top-r tokens form the high-frequency (HF) set and are retained, while the remaining low-frequency (LF) tokens are spatially grouped and each group is collapsed into a single local DC token via averaging. To prevent the surviving HF tokens from collapsing in subsequent layers, the attention weights are reparameterized as Â = A^LP + (ω₁+1)·A^HP + (ω₂+1)·A^N_DC with learnable scalars ω₁, ω₂ that boost high-frequency attention and compensate for the Jensen's-inequality-induced under-attention on DC tokens. The method is applied plug-and-play to pretrained models via 30-epoch fine-tuning with self-distillation from the unpruned teacher, and is compatible with FlashAttention through a lightweight post-processing step that only requires block-mean attention scores.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the proposed frequency-aware token reduction matches or exceeds the unpruned baseline accuracy while reducing MACs by approximately 35–40%. Specifically, on DeiT-S it achieves 79.8% top-1 accuracy at 3.0 MACs (vs. 4.6 MACs baseline), on DeiT-B it matches 81.8% at 11.6 MACs (vs. 17.6), and on ViT-S-21K it slightly exceeds the baseline (81.2% vs. 81.1%) at 3.0 MACs. On self-supervised models (ViT-B-MAE, ViT-L-MAE), the method narrows the accuracy gap to baseline to within 0.5 points while halving computational cost. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, 30% pruning at layers 4/7/10 retains 50.8% mIoU (vs. 51.3% baseline) with 4× throughput improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven compared baselines across every model and training paradigm tested. Against ToMe (merging-based), the gap is most pronounced on ViT-S (79.0% vs. 77.7%, +1.3%) and ViT-S-21K (81.2% vs. 80.1%, +1.1%), consistent with the paper's analysis that merging suppresses high-frequency signals and accelerates rank collapse. Against EViT (CLS-attention-based pruning), the method wins by 0.3–1.1% across models, with the largest margin on ViT-S (+1.1%) where EViT's CLS-based selection poorly captures high-frequency tokens in early layers. Against DynamicViT, the advantage is 0.4–0.9% (e.g., DeiT-S: 79.8% vs. 79.3%). Against DiffRate, the method leads by 0.2–0.3% on DeiT-S and DeiT-B, and by 0.3% on ViT-B-MAE. Against Evo-ViT and ATS, the gains are smaller (0.4–0.8%) but consistent. Notably, the method matches or exceeds the unpruned baseline on DeiT-T (+0.1%), DeiT-S (tie), DeiT-B (tie), ViT-S (+0.2%), and ViT-S-21K (+0.1%), a result the authors attribute to the method's ability to mitigate rank collapse rather than merely preserving information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: token reduction for ViTs (ToMe, EViT, DynamicViT, Evo-ViT, DiffRate) and the frequency-domain analysis of self-attention as a low-pass filter causing rank collapse (Dong et al., Wang et al., Park & Kim). Prior token reduction methods were designed without considering how they interact with the spectral properties of attention; this paper provides a formal proof (Proposition 3.1) that both pruning and merging operations accelerate rank collapse by preferentially removing high-frequency content. The proposed method departs from prior work by decomposing the attention map into low-pass and high-pass components to guide token selection, introducing DC tokens to preserve low-frequency information, and adding learnable attention reweighting to counteract residual collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, frequency-aware alternative to ad-hoc token reduction that can match or slightly exceed baseline accuracy while cutting FLOPs by ~30–40%, and it extends naturally to dense prediction (ADE20K segmentation) and integrates with FlashAttention. However, the practical accuracy gains over strong baselines like EViT and ToMe are marginal (≤0.4 pp on most configurations), and the method requires 30-epoch fine-tuning with self-distillation, limiting its plug-and-play appeal. The authors acknowledge the work is limited to vision models; whether the frequency-aware selection transfers to language, multimodal, or other transformer settings remains open. A key open question is whether the DC-token mechanism and attention reweighting can be made training-free or require fewer fine-tuning steps.
