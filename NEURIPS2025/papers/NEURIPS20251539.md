## Problem

Existing LoRA initialization strategies rely on SVD of weight matrices or gradients, but none provably maximize the expected gradient signal at the start of fine-tuning, resulting in suboptimal convergence. Additionally, fixed uniform rank allocation across layers ignores the fact that different weight matrices have different variance structures, wasting parameter budget on low-variance directions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251539/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EVA consumes the frozen pre-trained model weights and a downstream dataset from which it extracts activation vectors via forward passes over minibatches. It produces LoRA adapters (A and B matrices) where A is initialized with the top right-singular vectors of the activation covariance, B is zero, and each layer receives a data-driven rank determined by how many of its singular vectors fall within the global top-l by explained variance ratio. The total rank budget l = N·r is preserved, but individual layer ranks vary, typically shifting rank from high-dimensional feedforward projections to lower-dimensional attention projections.

## Method

EVA performs incremental truncated SVD (following the sequential Karhunen-Loève algorithm) on minibatches of activation vectors, updating right-singular vectors and singular values until convergence (cosine similarity threshold τ). The converged right-singular vectors, sorted by explained variance, initialize the LoRA A matrix, which Theorem 3.1 proves is variance-optimal and Theorem 3.2 proves maximizes the expected squared gradient norm E[‖∂L/∂B‖²_F] ∝ Tr(AΣAᵀ). For adaptive rank allocation, all right-singular vectors across all N weight matrices are globally sorted by their normalized explained variance ratio ξ, and the top-l entries determine each layer's rank, with a hyperparameter ρ controlling how many candidate components to compute per layer. The paper further connects this initialization to the Neural Tangent Kernel, showing EVA approximates the dominant NTK subspace and thereby minimizes the spectral tail of the kernel-regression generalization bound.

## Results

On eight common-sense reasoning tasks, EVA achieves the highest average score across all five LLMs tested (e.g., 94.5 on Llama-3.1-70B, 83.4 on Llama-2-7B) while reducing trainable parameters by >15M compared to uniform-rank LoRA. On GLUE, EVA attains the best average of 89.6 (RoBERTa_Large) and 89.9 (DeBERTav3_Base), outperforming LoRA (89.3 / 89.6) and all other initialization baselines. On math reasoning (GSM8K/MATH after MetaMathQA fine-tuning), EVA reaches 61.9/13.1 on Llama-2-7B and 78.8/31.2 on Llama-3.1-8B, the best among all methods. EVA also achieves the best average accuracy on VTAB-1K image classification and significantly narrows the LoRA-to-FFT gap on Meta-World decision-making tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVA consistently outperforms standard LoRA (random init) on average across all four domains: +1.2 avg on Llama-2-7B common-sense tasks, +0.3 on RoBERTa GLUE, and +2.2 GSM8K on Llama-2-7B math. Among weight-driven initializations, EVA beats PiSSA by +1.4 (Llama-2-7B) and OLoRA by +0.5 on the same benchmark, while MiLoRA trails EVA by 0.9. Among data-driven baselines, EVA surpasses LoRA-GA by a narrow margin (83.4 vs 83.4 on Llama-2-7B, but EVA uses fewer parameters) and decisively outperforms CorDA, which suffers from high seed sensitivity (e.g., 78.4 vs 83.4 on Llama-2-7B, 80.9 vs 89.5 on Gemma-2-9B). Against AdaLoRA (adaptive ranks, random init), EVA wins by +2.4 on Llama-2-7B and +1.9 on Gemma-2-9B, demonstrating that variance-optimal initialization with rank redistribution is more effective than gradient-driven rank allocation alone. Combining EVA with DoRA (EVA+DoRA) yields the single best scores in most settings (e.g., 84.2 on Llama-2-7B, 89.7 on Gemma-2-9B), confirming that the initialization and weight-decomposition improvements are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVA extends the LoRA framework (Hu et al., 2022) by replacing its random initialization with a data-driven, variance-optimal scheme computed via incremental SVD (Ross et al., 2008) on downstream activation vectors. It departs from weight-driven initializations (PiSSA, MiLoRA, OLoRA) that decompose pretrained weights, and from data-driven alternatives (LoRA-GA, CorDA) that estimate gradients or input-output correlations, by instead aligning the adapter with principal activation directions that provably maximize expected gradient signal. The adaptive rank redistribution mechanism distinguishes EVA from AdaLoRA, which learns rank gates during training, by performing a one-shot, data-driven allocation before fine-tuning begins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EVA offers a practically attractive drop-in replacement for LoRA initialization: negligible overhead (0.2–0.7% of training time), no architectural changes, and a Pareto improvement in both accuracy and parameter count across language, vision, and RL domains. The NTK-based generalization analysis (Section 3.4) provides a theoretical justification for why variance-optimal directions should accelerate convergence and reduce generalization error, connecting PEFT initialization to kernel learning theory. Key limitations include the requirement for a fixed downstream dataset at initialization time, degraded performance at higher ranks (r ≥ 128) due to SVD cost, and the observation that rank redistribution does not always help (e.g., decision-making tasks). Open directions include incorporating gradient information, relating singular vectors to interpretable pretrained knowledge, and combining EVA with mixture-of-experts architectures.
