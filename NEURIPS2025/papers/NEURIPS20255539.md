## Problem

Existing isolation-based anomaly detectors like IForest rely on global axis-aligned splits that miss locally defined outliers, while hypersphere-based methods like INNE assign equal weights to all spheres and are sensitive to sample size. A critical unresolved issue is that anomalies can contaminate normal-centered hyperspheres (lying near boundaries) or themselves serve as sparse hypersphere centers, blurring the normal/anomalous distinction. ADERH addresses both failure modes simultaneously through density-aware, boundary-sensitive scoring within an ensemble of compact hyperspheres.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular dataset of m numerical points in R^d (normalized to [0,1] via MinMaxScaler) with no labels required. It outputs a single continuous anomaly score in [0,1] per data point, where scores near 1 indicate anomalies and scores near 0 indicate normal samples. The scoring is computed over n=256 random subsets of size ω=18, with the final score being the arithmetic mean of per-subset base scores, yielding linear-time scalability in dataset size.

## Method

ADERH constructs an ensemble of compact hyperspheres by sampling n random subsets and, within each, randomly pairing points to form two half-radius spheres (radius = dist(x,y)/2) per pair, yielding multi-scale coverage without a fixed global radius. Two complementary signals refine each hypersphere's isolation score: Pitch (the ratio of a point's distance to the sphere center over the radius, capped at 1) accentuates points near boundaries, while NDensity (the sphere's point count divided by radius, normalized by the maximum in the subset) down-weights sparse, anomaly-centered spheres. These are combined multiplicatively as WPitch = (1−NDensity)·Pitch, so high anomaly scores require both boundary proximity and local sparsity simultaneously. For each data point, the smallest cover (the hypersphere minimizing WPitch) determines the per-subset base score, and the final anomaly score is the average across all n subsets. Theoretical analysis under a δ-separation assumption proves that normal points receive scores near 0 while anomalies receive scores near 1, with variance bounded by 1/(4n) via Bernstein's inequality.

## Results

ADERH achieves the best average AUC-ROC rank of 1.68 across 19 real-world datasets, placing first in 11 datasets and second in 6. On AUC-PR, ADERH attains an average rank of 2.29, again the best among all 15 compared methods. Standout results include AUC-ROC of 0.998 on Satimage-2, 0.991 on AD-Leather, 0.962 on Pendigits, and 1.000 on Wbc, Lymphography, and Musk. ADERH also shows the lowest cross-dataset standard deviation (0.0133 AUC-ROC, 0.0317 AUC-PR), indicating superior stability compared to INNE (0.0241/0.0515) and IForest (0.0235/0.0417).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADERH (avg rank 1.68) decisively outperforms the closest isolation-based competitor INNE (6.32) and IForest (5.26), winning on the majority of datasets by 0.01–0.27 AUC-ROC margin (e.g., 0.775 vs. 0.766 on Optdigits, 0.962 vs. 0.953 on Pendigits, 0.768 vs. 0.740 on Waveform). Against deep learning baselines, ADERH dominates: DeepSVDD (12.21) and DTE (14.11) are consistently the weakest methods, with ADERH exceeding them by 0.2–0.5 AUC-ROC on most datasets. The strongest single-dataset competitors are UniCAD (5.84), which beats ADERH on Celeba (0.810 vs. 0.732) and Wine (0.930 vs. 0.839), and SLAD (6.84), which wins on AD-Toothbrush (0.937 vs. 0.901). ADERH also outperforms the classical covering methods GEM and DTM on 10 of 12 datasets in Table 2 (e.g., 0.998 vs. 0.895 on Satimage-2, 0.938 vs. 0.663 on Cardio). Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction confirm that ADERH's improvements over all competitors are statistically significant at α=0.05 on both AUC-ROC and AUC-PR.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADERH extends the hypersphere-based isolation lineage initiated by INNE (Bandaragoda et al., 2014), which scored anomalies via the ratio of two enclosing hypersphere radii. The key departures are: (i) replacing single hyperspheres with pairs of compact half-radius hyperspheres from random point pairings, yielding multi-scale coverage without a fixed neighborhood size; (ii) introducing Pitch (a ratio-based boundary measure) and NDensity (a normalized density term) to handle the two failure modes of strict δ-separation—boundary contamination and anomaly-centered sparse spheres; and (iii) ensemble-averaging across random subsets with formal variance and concentration bounds. Conceptually, the method also draws on the δ-separation assumption shared with density-based detectors (LOF, DBSCAN) and the random-subset ensemble philosophy of Isolation Forest, but replaces axis-aligned splits with geometric hypersphere isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADERH offers a theoretically grounded, linear-time isolation method that consistently outperforms both classical (IForest, LOF, OCSVM) and deep learning (DeepSVDD, RCA, DIF, SLAD, DTE, UniCAD) baselines across 19 diverse tabular datasets, with the lowest cross-dataset variance among all compared methods. This is practically significant for large-scale tabular anomaly detection where density-based and deep methods face scalability or overfitting issues. The acknowledged limitation is the curse of dimensionality—distance concentration weakens inlier-outlier contrast in very high-dimensional spaces—and the δ-separation assumption, while relaxed via Pitch and NDensity, still presumes a degree of local compactness in normal data that may not hold in highly heterogeneous or adversarially structured settings. Open directions include dimensionality-aware scoring and extension to non-Euclidean or structured data.
