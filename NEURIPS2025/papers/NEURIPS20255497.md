## Problem

In cascade frameworks that apply image restoration before object detection, the functional mismatch between the two networks—restoration being smooth and low-Lipschitz while detection is discontinuous and high-Lipschitz—causes imperceptible restoration noise to be amplified during detection, leading to unstable predictions under varying degradation intensities. This Lipschitz continuity disparity in both input and parameter spaces had remained unexplored as a root cause of cascade instability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255497/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (resized to 640×640) affected by haze or low-light conditions and simultaneously produces detection outputs (bounding boxes with class labels) and a restored clean image. Unlike cascade pipelines where restoration is a separate pre-processing step, here the restored image is generated as an auxiliary output from shared backbone features during a single forward pass, with the restoration head operating on low-level features from the first three YOLO backbone stages.

## Method

LROD integrates image restoration directly into the detector's feature learning by sharing the first three stages of the YOLO backbone between a detection head and a lightweight restoration head (composed of CSPLayers in a densely connected architecture). The total loss combines detection loss, a Charbonnier restoration loss weighted by λ, and a parameter-space regularization term λ_p·‖∇_θ f_θ(x)‖ that penalizes output sensitivity to parameter changes. The key insight, formalized in Remark 3, is that the low-Lipschitz restoration task implicitly constrains the backbone's Jacobian norm during training, acting as an input-space Lipschitz regularizer that smooths the high-Lipschitz detection network. This dual regularization—input-space smoothing via shared restoration learning and parameter-space smoothing via gradient-norm penalty—harmonizes the two tasks' Lipschitz constants, reducing noise amplification and stabilizing gradient flow during joint optimization.

## Results

On the haze benchmark, LR-YOLOv8 achieves 83.3 mAP on VOC_Haze_Val and 53.2 mAP on RTTS, outperforming the best cascade baseline (ConvIR→YOLOv8‡ at 81.4/50.1) by +1.9/+3.1 mAP. On the low-light benchmark, LR-YOLOv8 reaches 71.7 mAP on VOC_Dark_Val and 54.5 mAP on ExDark, surpassing the best end-to-end method (FeatEnHancer→YOLOv8 at 68.7/51.8) by +3.0/+2.7 mAP. On COCO, LR-YOLOv8 achieves 37.7 mAP (haze) and 35.3 mAP (dark), improving over the strongest baseline (FeatEnHancer→YOLOv8 at 36.7/34.1) by +1.0/+1.2 mAP. The method also generalizes to RT-DETR and Faster R-CNN, gaining +1.4 mAP on RTTS over the best competing method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LR-YOLO consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across every dataset and both detector backbones. Against cascade methods (SFNet, ConvIR, LLFormer, RetinexFormer), LR-YOLOv8 gains +1.9 to +3.1 mAP on RTTS and +2.9 to +7.9 mAP on ExDark, confirming that the Lipschitz mismatch in cascades is a real bottleneck. Against joint-training variants (adversarial training [2] and alternating training [16]), LR-YOLOv8 still leads by +1.1 to +3.1 mAP on RTTS, showing that merely aligning training objectives is insufficient without explicit Lipschitz regularization. Against end-to-end methods (IA [1], GDIP [3], FeatEnHancer [17]), LR-YOLOv8 achieves the largest margins on ExDark (+2.7 to +3.3 mAP), where degradation is more severe. On COCO (Table 2), LR-YOLOv8 leads FeatEnHancer→YOLOv8 by +1.0 mAP (haze) and +1.2 mAP (dark). Generalization experiments confirm the framework is plug-and-play: LROD applied to RT-DETR and Faster R-CNN beats all competing methods on RTTS by +1.4 mAP, and under additional degradations (motion blur, rain, snow, haze+rain) LR-YOLOv8 gains +2.1 to +2.6 mAP over the best baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant cascade paradigm (restoration → detection) and the emerging joint-training strategies (adversarial training in ReForDe, alternating training in SR4IR) by reframing the integration problem through Lipschitz continuity. It builds directly on YOLOv8/YOLOv10 as the detection backbone and on ConvIR-style restoration as the auxiliary task, but the key conceptual departure is using the Jacobian norm to quantify the smoothness mismatch between the two tasks in both input and parameter spaces, then designing regularization (shared-backbone restoration loss + parameter-space gradient norm penalty) to close that gap. Unlike spectral norm regularization (SN-GAN) which constrains weights globally, or PGD-based adversarial training which requires perturbed inputs, the proposed LROD achieves input-aware smoothness implicitly through multi-task feature sharing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic Lipschitz-continuity analysis explaining *why* cascade frameworks for adverse-condition detection are unstable, moving the field beyond empirical trial-and-error toward a principled design criterion. The plug-and-play nature (validated on YOLOv8, YOLOv10, RT-DETR, and Faster R-CNN across haze, low-light, rain, snow, and motion blur) makes it broadly applicable to safety-critical perception pipelines. A key limitation is the single-degradation-per-input assumption; extending to concurrent multi-degradation scenarios and to tasks with inherently ambiguous boundaries (e.g., camouflaged object detection) are natural next steps that the authors identify.
