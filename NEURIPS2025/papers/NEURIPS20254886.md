## Problem

Most generative models encode images into dense grid-based latents with uniform information density, ignoring the inherent spatial sparsity of natural images where backgrounds require far fewer latent units than intricate foregrounds. This paper asks whether a small set of non-grid latents paired with explicit positional properties (regions of interest) can both faithfully reconstruct and effectively generate images, challenging the dense grid convention in latent diffusion pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254886/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a class label and produces a 256×256 (or 512×512) RGB image via 64 sparse latents, each a 32-dim vector paired with a 4-dim RoI bounding box (x, y, h, w). For reconstruction, the autoencoder takes an RGB image and outputs the same sparse latent-RoI set. The number of latents N is fully decoupled from image resolution, allowing aggressive compression (e.g., 32 latents for a 256×256 image) without architectural changes, and the RoIs provide translation-equivariant spatial grounding absent in 1D tokenization schemes.

## Method

The pipeline has two components. SF-VAE uses a SparseFormer encoder to produce N latents and RoIs end-to-end without bounding-box supervision, and a neural-field decoder where each latent-RoI pair defines a continuous cosine-transform-based function mapping pixel coordinates to downsampled features, which are softmax-blended and upsampled to raw pixels. SF-SiT is a flow-matching transformer that jointly predicts the velocity of both latents and RoIs from pure noise to target samples, using RoI-based sinusoidal positional encodings (which change across timesteps as RoIs move) in place of fixed grid positions. A key design choice is an asynchronous interpolation schedule (β=2 polynomial) that denoises RoIs faster than latents, giving the model cleaner positional context for latent velocity prediction, combined with an auxiliary L1 loss on RoI velocity to sharpen bounding-box accuracy.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 generation, SF-SiT-XL with 64 sparse latents achieves a competitive 2.76 FID (CFG=1.375) and 247.7 IS, using only 64 tokens versus 256 for grid-based baselines. Without CFG, SF-SiT-XL w/ REPA reaches 7.35 FID and 131.9 IS after 1.4M training steps. For reconstruction, SF-VAE achieves 0.70 rFID with just 64×32=2048 latent units (vs. 4096 for SD-VAE), and can compress to 32 latents with 1.70 rFID. At the 400K-iteration budget without CFG, SF-SiT-XL matches SiT-XL/2 (18.6 vs. 18.8 FID) while using only 29.3 GFLOPs per NFE versus 114.4 GFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD-VAE (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), SF-VAE achieves a slightly higher rFID (0.70 vs. 0.63) but with half the latent units (2048 vs. 4096) and lower decoder FLOPs (153G vs. 311G). Against DC-AE (Chen et al., 2024), SF-VAE wins on rFID (0.70 vs. 0.77 for DC-AE-in, 0.96 for DC-AE-mix) while using fewer parameters (133M vs. 323M). Against DiT-XL/2 (Peebles et al., ICCV 2023), SF-SiT-XL with 64 tokens reaches 2.76 FID (CFG=1.375) versus DiT's 2.27 (CFG=1.5) with 256 tokens, a gap attributable to the shorter 1.4M vs. 7M training budget. Against SiT-XL/2 (Ma et al., ECCV 2024), SF-SiT-XL is competitive at the 400K-iteration mark (18.6 vs. 18.8 FID without CFG) and reaches 2.76 FID with CFG versus SiT's 2.06, again with 4× fewer tokens. Against SiT-XL/2 w/ REPA (Yu et al., 2024), SF-SiT-XL w/ REPA achieves 7.35 FID (no CFG) versus 6.4, a modest gap that the authors attribute to the limited 1.4M-step budget on 8 A100s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dense grid-based latent convention that underpins latent diffusion (LDM, SD-VAE) and flow-matching transformers (SiT, DiT) by replacing structured 2D latent grids with a small set of non-grid latents paired with learned region-of-interest (RoI) bounding boxes. The encoder borrows the SparseFormer architecture for end-to-end latent+RoI extraction without box supervision, while the decoder introduces a novel neural-field design with continuous cosine-transform bases inspired by neural rendering. The generative model (SF-SiT) retains the SiT transformer backbone and flow-matching training objective but extends the prediction target to jointly model latent and RoI velocities, with an asynchronous interpolation schedule that lets RoIs converge faster than latents during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational assumption in visual generation—that latents must occupy a uniform spatial grid—by showing that 64 non-grid latents with RoIs can match 256 grid tokens in class-conditional ImageNet generation quality, achieving a 96× compression rate versus the standard 48×. This opens a path toward adaptive computation allocation (more latents for complex foregrounds, fewer for backgrounds) and potentially more efficient generation pipelines. However, the practical gap remains: at full training budget with CFG, SF-SiT-XL (2.76 FID) still trails SiT-XL/2 (2.06 FID), and the approach has not been validated on text-to-image, video, or higher resolutions. Open questions include whether the RoI-based positional encoding generalizes to open-vocabulary settings and whether the neural-field decoder scales efficiently to larger resolutions.
