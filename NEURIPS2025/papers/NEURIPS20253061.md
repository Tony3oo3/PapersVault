## Problem

Standard STaR (Self-Taught Reasoner) relies on random observation sampling, which causes persistent training imbalance: easy examples are over-trained while challenging ones are under-trained, wasting compute and slowing convergence. Prior STaR variants improve accuracy at the cost of even more compute (larger K, process reward models), but none target training efficiency. Additionally, naively biasing toward harder examples to fix the imbalance increases false positives (correct answers with flawed CoTs), creating a tension between diversity and CoT quality that remains unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a supervised dataset of query-answer pairs (no ground-truth CoTs), a pre-trained-only LM (Llama 3.2 3B, Qwen 2.5 3B, or Gemma 7B), and a fixed set of few-shot CoT exemplars. It outputs a fine-tuned reasoning LM evaluated via zero-shot greedy decoding. A key operational detail is that AdaSTaR stops sampling within each iteration once the correct-sample buffer reaches the predetermined batch size β^t, avoiding the wasteful full-dataset inference-then-filter pattern of vanilla STaR; all three adaptive statistics (last-sampled iteration, win rate, training accuracy) are extracted as byproducts of the existing inference and training steps, adding near-zero runtime overhead.

## Method

AdaSTaR integrates two adaptive sampling mechanisms into the STaR-Acc (model-accumulating) training loop. First, Adaptive Sampling for Diversity (AdaD) maintains a Hierarchical Min Heap over all observations, ordered by (t̃_i, w_i): the last iteration each observation was sampled and its win statistic (fraction of K CoT samples yielding the correct answer at t̃_i). This prioritizes under-trained and harder observations without any extra forward passes, since the K samples are already part of STaR's rejection-sampling step. Second, Adaptive Sampling for Curriculum (AdaC) regularizes AdaD using the current iteration's training accuracy α as a proxy for model strength: of the m observations sampled per iteration, only ⌊mα²⌋ highest-priority ones have their heap statistics updated before reinsertion. When α is low (weak model), most observations retain their stale (easier) statistics and are re-selected, implicitly mixing in easier examples; as α grows, the regularization fades and the system increasingly targets harder observations. The ablation confirms that AdaD alone reduces training-frequency variance but sharply degrades accuracy due to increased false positives, while AdaC restores accuracy while preserving the diversity gains.

## Results

AdaSTaR achieves the best test accuracy across all 6/6 benchmarks (ARC-C: 73.8%, CQA: 78.0%, CLadder 1.5: 95.6%, ANLI: 66.8%, GSM8K: 77.0%, SVAMP: 75.5%) while simultaneously reducing training FLOPs by an average of 58.6% (range: 19.2%–93.7%) relative to the strongest accuracy-achieving baseline. The most dramatic FLOPs reduction is on GSM8K (93.7% reduction, 19.3 PFLOPs vs. 305.2 PFLOPs for STaR-Acc), while the smallest is on CLadder 1.5 (19.2% reduction). Results generalize across three base model families: Llama 3.2 3B, Qwen 2.5 3B, and Gemma 7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaSTaR outperforms all five primary baselines on every benchmark. Against the original STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022), AdaSTaR improves accuracy by 2.2–20.4 points (e.g., 73.8% vs. 71.6% on ARC-C, 95.6% vs. 53.4% on CLadder 1.5) while using 50–97% fewer FLOPs. Against ReST^EM (Singh et al., 2024), which also targets training imbalance via per-observation cut-off thresholds, AdaSTaR achieves higher accuracy on 5/6 datasets (tying on GSM8K at 77.0%) with dramatically lower compute (e.g., 19.3 vs. 2229.1 PFLOPs on GSM8K). Against B-STaR (Zeng et al., 2025), AdaSTaR wins on all six benchmarks in accuracy (e.g., 73.8% vs. 67.8% on ARC-C, 75.5% vs. 72.0% on SVAMP) with 19–94% fewer FLOPs. Against the SFT-based baselines (SFT+8-CoT from Wei et al., 2022 and SFT+5-SC from Wang et al., 2023), AdaSTaR achieves substantially higher accuracy (e.g., 73.8% vs. 59.0%/63.8% on ARC-C) while using comparable or lower total FLOPs than the more expensive SFT+5-SC variant. The paper also shows that model accumulation (STaR-Acc) consistently outperforms resetting (STaR), and that scaling K (number of CoT samples) does not reliably improve performance, supporting AdaSTaR's choice of K=2.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaSTaR extends the STaR/RFT self-improvement framework (Zelikman et al., 2022) by replacing its random observation sampling with an adaptive mechanism built on a hierarchical min-heap that tracks per-observation last-sampled iteration and a win-statistic difficulty proxy. While prior STaR variants (V-STaR, B-STaR, ReST^EM, Lean-STaR) focus on improving reasoning quality through better verification, more CoT samples, or process reward models—at substantial additional compute cost—AdaSTaR targets the orthogonal axis of training efficiency by ensuring balanced exposure across observations. The curriculum regularization component draws on adaptive curriculum learning (Hacohen & Weinshall, 2019; Kong et al., 2021) and data-selection literature showing that mixing easy and hard samples outperforms hard-only selection, applied here to the STaR loop for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 58.6% average FLOPs reduction while achieving best accuracy on all six benchmarks makes STaR-based self-improvement substantially more accessible, particularly for resource-constrained settings. A key open question the paper highlights is the optimal placement of the STaR stage within the broader training pipeline that now combines SFT, STaR, and RL-based long-CoT generation (as in DeepSeek-R1 and Kimi k1.5), and whether STaR-phase gains propagate to downstream RL performance. The paper also notes that the curriculum hyperparameter f(α)=α² is fixed and could benefit from more granular tuning, and that evaluation on models larger than 7B remains future work.
