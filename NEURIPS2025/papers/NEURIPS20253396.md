## Problem

Existing RLHF algorithms implicitly assume that human labelers report preferences truthfully, yet in pluralistic settings where multiple labelers' feedback shapes the final policy, each labeler has a direct incentive to strategically misreport to steer the policy toward their own preferences. The paper shows that standard RLHF methods (including recent pluralistic approaches) are not strategyproof and that even a single strategic labeler can cause arbitrarily large misalignment with social welfare. Furthermore, it proves a fundamental impossibility: any exactly strategyproof RLHF algorithm must suffer at least constant suboptimality and achieve at most a 1/k approximation ratio to the optimal policy.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes offline preference data from k labelers, where each labeler i provides n examples of (initial state s, trajectory pair (τ₀, τ₁), preference label o) generated under a Bradley-Terry model with a linear reward function r_θ(s,a) = ⟨θ, φ(s,a)⟩ over a known feature embedding φ. The output is a history-independent policy π mapping states to action distributions that maximizes average social welfare across all labelers. The setting covers both contextual bandits (H=1) and episodic MDPs (H>1), with the key structural assumption that the set of achievable feature occupancies spans a hyperrectangle in R^d.

## Method

The Pessimistic Median of MLEs algorithm first computes the Maximum Likelihood Estimate of each labeler's reward parameter from their preference data, then constructs a confidence ellipsoid around each MLE using the covariance of the feature differences. It then defines a pessimistic median return: for each policy, it takes the worst-case (minimum) return over the coordinate-wise median of all k confidence sets, and selects the policy maximizing this pessimistic median return. The key insight is that the combination of the median aggregation rule (which is strategyproof in one-dimensional resource allocation) with pessimistic uncertainty quantification yields approximate strategyproofness in the high-dimensional, policy-optimization setting. The degree of strategyproofness for labeler i scales as Õ(κ_i √(d/n)), where κ_i is the uniform policy coverage of labeler i's data, while the suboptimality of the resulting policy is bounded by Õ(√(d/k) + max_i κ_i* · k√(d/n)), converging to the optimal policy as both k and n grow.

## Results

The paper establishes that any strategyproof RLHF algorithm suffers worst-case suboptimality of at least (k−1)/k and approximation ratio at most 1/k (Theorem 3.5, Corollary 3.6), revealing a fundamental trade-off between incentive alignment and policy alignment. The proposed Pessimistic Median of MLEs algorithm is Õ(κ_i√(d/n))-strategyproof for each labeler i (Theorem 4.1), where κ_i is the uniform policy coverage coefficient. Under truthful or weakly dominant reporting, the suboptimality is bounded by Õ(√(d/k) + max_i κ_i* · k√(d/n)) (Theorem 4.2), which converges to zero as both k and n grow. In the single-labeler special case (k=1), the bound reduces to Õ(κ_1*√(d/n)), matching existing offline RLHF guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include empirical comparisons but provides theoretical comparisons against existing pluralistic RLHF methods. Proposition 3.3 shows that both Pessimistic Social Welfare (Zhong et al., 2024) and MaxMin-RLHF (Chakraborty et al., 2024) are not strategyproof, with explicit counterexamples where a single labeler's misreporting strictly increases their utility. Proposition 3.4 further demonstrates that a single strategic labeler can degrade Pessimistic Social Welfare's social welfare to at most ε while the optimal welfare is at least BL−2ε, yielding suboptimality of at least BL−3ε — i.e., arbitrarily bad performance. In contrast, the proposed Pessimistic Median of MLEs provides approximate strategyproofness guarantees (Theorem 4.1) while maintaining suboptimality bounds that converge to zero (Theorem 4.2), though at the cost of an additional factor of k in the estimation term compared to the single-labeler case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of offline RLHF, mechanism design, and social choice theory. It builds on the pessimistic offline RL framework (Rashidinejad et al. 2021) and MLE-based reward estimation with confidence ellipsoids (Zhu et al. 2023), but departs from existing pluralistic RLHF methods (Pessimistic Social Welfare, MaxMin-RLHF) by showing they are not strategyproof. Unlike prior mechanism-design-for-RLHF work that relies on payment rules (VCG-style), this paper achieves approximate strategyproofness without financial incentives by combining pessimistic estimation with a coordinate-wise median aggregation rule drawn from social choice theory (Moulin 1980). The impossibility results connect RLHF directly to the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem, a link not previously drawn in the RLHF literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a fundamental impossibility: any strategyproof RLHF algorithm is k-times worse than optimal in the worst case, revealing an irreconcilable tension between incentive alignment and social welfare maximization. This has direct implications for pluralistic AI alignment systems where diverse human preferences are aggregated, suggesting that current approaches implicitly assume truthful feedback in a way that is exploitable. Key limitations include the restriction to linear reward functions, the Bradley-Terry preference model, and the need for uniform policy coverage (stronger than standard offline RL assumptions). Open questions include extending to non-linear rewards, empirical validation of strategic behavior in real annotation pipelines, and whether the k-factor degradation is tight in practice or merely a worst-case artifact.
