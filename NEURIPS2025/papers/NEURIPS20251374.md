## Problem

Existing transferability estimation metrics (LogME, ETran, NCTI, etc.) are designed for computer vision and fail on time series due to three compounding challenges: cross-task heterogeneity (classification, forecasting, imputation, anomaly detection impose different representation requirements), high-dimensional computational infeasibility on large-scale time series datasets, and feature space mismatch caused by heterogeneous model architectures that handle inter-channel dependencies differently, rendering magnitude-based statistics unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251374/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of M pre-trained time series models (e.g., MOMENT, Timer, UniTS families with encoder/decoder architectures and 3M–380M parameters) and a target dataset, extracting features from the first and last encoder layers (H¹, H) and from a moving-average trend component (T) of the input series. It outputs a single scalar transferability score per model, computed without any fine-tuning, which is then used to rank models by predicted downstream performance. The evaluation is validated against brute-force fine-tuning rankings using weighted Kendall's τ_w across 32 datasets spanning 5 task types.

## Method

TEMPLATE decomposes transferability into three complementary scores computed from the feature matrices. The Dependency Learning Score (S_dl) measures the Pearson correlation between the top singular vectors of the full feature matrix H and the trend-component feature matrix T, quantifying long-term dependency capture. The Pattern Learning Score (S_pl) computes the ratio of the largest singular value to the nuclear norm of T, providing a scale-invariant measure of primary temporal pattern focus that avoids the feature-space mismatch problem. The Task Adaptation Score (S_ta) uses normalized HSIC with an RBF kernel (bandwidth set to median pairwise distance) to measure similarity between first-layer and last-layer encoder features, with a task-dependent sign (λ = −1 for classification/imputation favoring hierarchical representations; λ = +1 for forecasting/anomaly detection favoring low-level feature preservation). For computational efficiency, the largest singular value and vector are approximated via 10-step power iteration rather than full SVD, and the nuclear norm is computed through the trace of the square root of TᵀT. All three scores are min-max normalized across the model pool before being summed with equal weights, requiring no per-dataset hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

TEMPLATE achieves a state-of-the-art average weighted Kendall's τ_w of 0.608 on the 9-dataset UEA classification benchmark, outperforming the second-best method PACTran (0.490) by 24%. Across all 5 downstream tasks (classification, long-term forecasting, short-term forecasting, imputation, and anomaly detection), TEMPLATE improves the average τ_w by 35% over the previous SOTA method ETran. On long-term forecasting, TEMPLATE reaches 0.362 average τ_w versus ETran's 0.265 and LogME's 0.190; on imputation, TEMPLATE achieves 0.518 versus ETran's 0.372. Notably, TEMPLATE handles high-dimensional datasets (Traffic with 862 channels, Electricity with 321 channels) where LogME fails entirely due to out-of-memory errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEMPLATE outperforms all 8 baselines on the classification benchmark in average τ_w (0.608 vs. PACTran 0.490, RetMMD 0.419, ETran 0.345, TMI 0.191, NCTI 0.186, NLEEP 0.138, LogME 0.136, SFDA 0.119), though it does not win on every individual dataset (e.g., PACTran leads on SpokenArabicDigits at 0.744 vs. 0.637, and RetMMD leads on JapaneseVowels at 0.654 vs. 0.447). On regression-based tasks (forecasting, imputation), TEMPLATE consistently beats both LogME and ETran: on long-term forecasting it achieves 0.362 vs. ETran's 0.265 and LogME's 0.190, and on imputation 0.518 vs. 0.372 and 0.258 respectively. A key differentiator is robustness to high-dimensional inputs: LogME and TMI produce negative or near-zero τ_w on multi-channel datasets (FaceDetection with 144 channels, PEMS-SF with 963 channels, Electricity with 321 channels) and LogME fails outright on Traffic (862 channels) due to out-of-memory, whereas TEMPLATE maintains positive scores (0.597, 0.470, 0.412, 0.529) across all of them. On anomaly detection, TEMPLATE's advantage is more modest (0.236 average vs. LogME 0.207 and ETran 0.187), and it actually underperforms ETran on the PSM dataset (0.142 vs. 0.433). The paper reports a statistically significant overall improvement over ETran (mean difference 0.124, t-statistic 2.679, p-value 0.012) across all five tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model transferability estimation (MTE) literature—dominated by computer vision methods like LogME, NLEEP, SFDA, TMI, NCTI, and ETran—to the time series domain, where three structural challenges (cross-task heterogeneity, high-dimensional infeasibility, and feature-space mismatch from heterogeneous inter-channel processing) render direct application of CV metrics ineffective. Rather than building on a single prior method, TEMPLATE repurposes SVD/power iteration (matrix decomposition) and HSIC (kernel alignment) in a novel three-component combination: a Pearson-correlation-based dependency score on trend-decomposed features, a nuclear-norm-ratio pattern score, and a task-adaptive hierarchical similarity score. The empirical observation that dominant singular values are the most transferable components (Figure 2) motivates the SVD-centric design, while the use of series decomposition to isolate long-term dependencies is a time-series-specific adaptation absent from prior MTE work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As time series foundation models scale (Time-MOE at 2.4B parameters) and proliferate across model families (MOMENT, Timer, UniTS), the cost of brute-force fine-tuning all candidates becomes prohibitive, making fast transferability estimation a practical necessity. TEMPLATE's 35% improvement over ETran and its ability to handle multi-channel datasets where LogME and TMI fail (negative τ_w) address a concrete gap. However, the authors acknowledge limitations on datasets with severe distribution shifts or structural breaks, and the method's reliance on a fixed model pool of 10 models from 4 families leaves open whether the three-component decomposition generalizes to much larger or architecturally more diverse model pools. Future work could explore whether the trend-decomposition step and the λ sign convention for task type generalize to emerging time series tasks beyond the five evaluated.
