## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has driven major reasoning gains in LLMs, but open-source replication efforts have concentrated almost exclusively on mathematics and coding, where binary reward rules are straightforward to design. Logical reasoning is hypothesized to be a fundamental building block of general reasoning, yet existing logic benchmarks (BBH, BBEH, KOR-Bench) either lack training data or cover only a handful of tasks, leaving a critical gap in diverse, verifiable reasoning data suitable for RL.

## Input / Output

The synthesis framework takes task-specific difficulty hyperparameters (e.g., Sudoku grid size, missing-number ratio in Math Path, number of participants in Goods Exchange) and produces natural language problem instances in both English and Chinese, each paired with a task-specific rule-based verifier. For RL training, the model receives a formatted prompt containing the logic problem and must output a chain-of-thought reasoning block enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags; the binary reward is 1 only if both the format is correct and the answer passes the task's verification rule.

## Method

SYNLOGIC is a data synthesis framework covering 35 diverse logic tasks (Sudoku, Game of 24, Cryptarithm, Minesweeper, Zebra Puzzle, etc.), each implemented with three components: a rule-based instance generator, a task-specific correctness verifier, and configurable difficulty parameters. Difficulty is calibrated via a dual-bound strategy: the upper bound is set by the hardest parameter level solvable by DeepSeek-R1 or o3-mini at pass@10 > 0, and the lower bound by the easiest level where Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct achieves a pass rate between 0 and 0.5. Training uses GRPO (following DAPO) with binary outcome rewards on Qwen2.5-7B/32B-Base and OctoThinker-8B-Base in a zero-RL setting. A key empirical finding is that mixing SYNLOGIC data with math or coding data improves training efficiency in those domains (higher accuracy at the same number of consumed samples) while simultaneously boosting logic scores, and at 32B scale the mixed Zero-Mix-3 configuration outperforms DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B on BBEH, KOR-Bench, LiveCodeBench, and GPQA-Diamond.

## Results

On the 32B scale, SYNLOGIC-32B achieves 62.2% on KOR-Bench, 85.8% on BBH, and 25.5% on BBEH, surpassing Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct by 7.5 and 8.0 points on KOR-Bench and BBEH respectively. On the 7B scale, SYNLOGIC-7B reaches 48.1% on KOR-Bench (+9.5 over Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) and 10.0% on AIME 2024 (vs. 0.3% for the base model). In the 32B Zero-RL mixed-training setting (Zero-Mix-3), the model achieves 28.6% on BBEH, 65.0% on KOR-Bench, 40.7% on LiveCodeBench, and 57.5% on GPQA-Diamond, outperforming DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B on GPQA-Diamond and matching it on LiveCodeBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, SYNLOGIC-32B wins decisively on BBEH (25.5 vs. 19.2, +6.3 points) but trails on KOR-Bench (62.2 vs. 66.6) and is far behind on AIME 2024 (19.6 vs. 72.6), reflecting that R1-Distill was trained with math data while SYNLOGIC focuses on logic. In the 32B Zero-RL mixed-training comparison (Table 3), Zero-Mix-3 (SYNLOGIC+Math+Coding) outperforms DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B on GPQA-Diamond (57.5 vs. 55.0) and matches it on LiveCodeBench (40.7 vs. 40.2), while still lagging on AIME 2024 (35.8 vs. 47.0). Compared to the Qwen2.5 Instruct baselines, SYNLOGIC-7B gains +9.5 points on KOR-Bench and +3.8 on BBH, while SYNLOGIC-32B gains +7.5 on KOR-Bench and +8.0 on BBEH, with math scores roughly matching the instruct models without any math training data. On the 8B scale, SYNLOGIC-8B (OctoThinker-8B-Base) improves over its base by +6.8 on KOR-Bench, +11.7 on BBH, +7.9 on BBEH, and +11.2 on AIME 2024, confirming cross-architecture generalizability. The ablation Zero-Mix-2 (Math+Coding only) is consistently outperformed by Zero-Mix-3 by over 10 points on BBEH, 6 points on KOR-Bench, and 2.3 points on GPQA-Diamond, isolating the contribution of SYNLOGIC data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI-o1 from mathematics and coding into the logical reasoning domain, building on GRPO and DAPO training techniques. Prior logic-RL efforts (Logic-RL, K&K, Code2Logic) each targeted a single task, while existing logic benchmarks (BBH, BBEH, KOR-Bench) provide evaluation data but no training data or difficulty control. SynLogic departs from this by constructing a unified synthesis framework covering 35 diverse logic tasks with task-specific generators, verifiers, and adjustable difficulty parameters, positioning logical reasoning as a foundational building block for general reasoning rather than a narrow evaluation target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap: high-quality, verifiable, difficulty-adjustable logic training data was simply unavailable, and the open-sourced 35-task synthesis pipeline makes it reproducible. The cross-domain transfer results—pure logic RL training yielding ~10-point AIME gains and mixed logic+math training achieving comparable math performance with fewer math samples—suggest that logical reasoning may serve as a shared inductive bias across reasoning domains, with practical implications for how RLVR curricula are composed. Open questions include whether dynamic difficulty scheduling during training would further improve performance, whether the findings generalize beyond Qwen and LLaMA architectures, and whether the 35-task coverage is sufficient or whether additional task families (e.g., probabilistic reasoning, causal inference) would yield further gains.
