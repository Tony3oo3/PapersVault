## Problem

Existing protein inverse folding methods (autoregressive, one-shot, iterative refinement) primarily recover the native sequence, overlooking the one-to-many nature of the problem where multiple diverse sequences can fold into the same backbone. The inference/test-time phase remains underexplored: there is no framework that deliberately explores multiple design trajectories with intermediate feedback, lookahead, and backtracking to generate diverse yet structurally consistent sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250955/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed protein backbone structure (Cartesian coordinates of N, Cα, C, and optionally O atoms per residue) and produces a *set* of diverse amino acid sequences that fold into that structure, rather than a single recovered sequence. Generation proceeds iteratively from a fully masked sequence, progressively filling in residues over T denoising steps. The framework is entirely training-free: it operates on a frozen ESM-3 (1.4B) policy model and ESMFold for structure prediction, with no additional trainable parameters.

## Method

ProtInvTree reformulates iterative sequence denoising as a tree-based MDP and applies Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with UCT-based node selection to perform deliberate, step-wise design. At each step, a two-stage focus-and-grounding action first selects which positions to modify (Focus) and then generates residues at those positions (Grounding) via the structurally modulated PLM. To evaluate intermediate nodes without costly full rollouts, a jumpy denoising strategy performs a single-step DDIM-based completion of the partial sequence, whose predicted structure is scored against the target via TM-score as the reward. Node values are backpropagated up the tree, enabling the search to exploit promising branches and backtrack from poor ones. Test-time scaling is achieved by increasing the number of MCTS iterations, expansion width, or planning depth without any retraining.

## Results

On CATH v4.2 (All proteins), ProtInvTree achieves a sc-TMscore of 0.881 and RMSD of 1.513, outperforming the strongest baseline KW-Design (ESM-2) by 2.3% in sc-TMscore and 3.3% in RMSD. On CATH v4.3, ProtInvTree reaches 0.835 sc-TMscore and 1.702 RMSD, again leading all baselines. On the de novo TS45 benchmark, ProtInvTree achieves 0.724 sc-TMscore and 2.513 RMSD, surpassing KW-Design (0.711 / 2.643). Notably, ProtInvTree requires zero trainable parameters (0M/1.4B), using only the frozen ESM-3 model with test-time tree search, yet improves sc-TMscore by 7.8–18.3% over plain ESM-3 at the same number of denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtInvTree outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest iterative-refinement baseline KW-Design (ESM-2) [11], ProtInvTree gains +0.023 sc-TMscore (0.881 vs 0.858) and reduces RMSD by 0.053 (1.513 vs 1.566) on CATH v4.2 All, and +0.017 sc-TMscore on CATH v4.3. Against the one-shot model PiFold [13], the gap is larger: +0.039 sc-TMscore and −0.210 RMSD on CATH v4.2. Against autoregressive baselines (ProteinMPNN [10], GVP [24], GraphTrans [22]), ProtInvTree leads by 0.086–0.137 in sc-TMscore. Most strikingly, ProtInvTree uses the same frozen ESM-3 backbone as the ESM-3 baseline [18] but with zero additional training, yet improves sc-TMscore from 0.816 to 0.881 (+7.8%) on CATH v4.2 All and from 0.775 to 0.835 (+7.7%) on CATH v4.3, demonstrating that reward-guided tree search at test time unlocks substantial latent capacity of the pretrained PLM. On the de novo TS45 benchmark, ProtInvTree (0.724 sc-TMscore) beats KW-Design (0.711) and ESM-3 (0.690), confirming generalizability beyond fixed-backbone settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three established paradigms of protein inverse folding (autoregressive, one-shot, and iterative refinement) by introducing a tree-structured generation process. It transfers the test-time scaling and MCTS paradigm from LLM reasoning (AlphaGo, Tree of Thoughts) to protein design, using ESM-3 as a frozen policy model and ESMFold for structural reward evaluation. The focus-and-grounding action mechanism and jumpy denoising for fast intermediate-state evaluation are novel components specific to the protein domain, distinguishing this work from both prior IF methods and LLM tree-search approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a training-free tree-search framework (0 trainable parameters) can outperform all prior methods with millions of parameters, the paper validates test-time scaling as a viable paradigm for unlocking the full potential of pretrained protein language models. The Pareto-optimal diversity–consistency trade-off addresses a long-standing limitation of recovery-optimized methods that converge near the native sequence. Key open questions include the computational cost (up to ~44s per design vs. ~7s for ESM-3), the absence of wet-lab validation, and whether the framework generalizes beyond fixed-backbone IF to broader protein design tasks.
