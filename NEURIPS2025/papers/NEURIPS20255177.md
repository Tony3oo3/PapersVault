## Problem

LLM-assisted Evolutionary Search (LES) for automated algorithm discovery operates in abstract language spaces that lack explicit dimensionality and well-defined structure, making it difficult to apply established evolutionary computation techniques like niching that rely on distance-based partitioning. Prior LES methods (Funsearch, EoH, ReEvo) resort to oversimplified search mechanisms such as greedy fitness-based selection or random island grouping, which limit exploration-exploitation balance and overall search efficiency.

## Input / Output

PartEvo takes a natural language task description and a set of training instances as input, operating in a strict black-box setting where the LLM receives only performance feedback (objective values) without access to the underlying mathematical model. Each individual is a dual-format representation consisting of executable Python code paired with a natural language 'thoughts' rationale, following EoH's design. The output is a meta-heuristic algorithm constrained to 30,000 candidate solution evaluations and 180 seconds of runtime, discovered within a fixed budget of 500 LLM queries (GPT-4o-mini) over a population of 16.

## Method

PartEvo introduces feature-assisted niche construction to indirectly partition the abstract language search space: individuals are projected into a computable feature space (via CodeBLEU-based similarity vectors or BERT thought embeddings), then clustered with K-means into K=4 niches that serve as structured subregions. Within each niche, four evolutionary operators operate in parallel: two prompt-centric operators (Reflection-based Evolution and Summary-based Evolution) that use auxiliary LLMs to generate verbal gradients steering the generator toward semantically meaningful improvements, and two EC-inspired operators (Crossover between Niches and Local-Global guided Evolution) that exploit the niche structure for cross-subregion knowledge transfer and convergence acceleration. Sampling resources are distributed evenly across niches with elite-preserving probabilistic parent selection within each, enabling dynamic control over exploration-exploitation trade-off. The framework is general and modular—any feature representation, clustering method, or EC operator can be substituted—making it a blueprint for integrating diverse advanced EC techniques into future LES pipelines.

## Results

On the four optimization benchmarks (P1–P4), PartEvo consistently achieves the best or near-best objective values across all settings. On the most challenging task (P4, machine-level scheduling), PartEvo achieves a 90.1% reduction in manufacturing cost compared to the best human-designed DE baseline (14,396.1 vs. 237,000 on testing). On P3 (task offloading), PartEvo is the only LES method that surpasses the DE baseline (56,876.40 vs. 58,704.47 on testing). PartEvo also exhibits the lowest standard error of the mean across runs (e.g., ±79.74 on P3 vs. ±226.40 for Funsearch), indicating superior stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartEvo outperforms all three peer LES methods (Funsearch, EoH, ReEvo) on every benchmark. On P4, PartEvo (14,396.1 testing) is roughly 10× better than Funsearch (149,000) and 2.4× better than EoH (34,891.9), while ReEvo (134,000) is an order of magnitude worse. Against human-designed baselines, PartEvo surpasses GA and PSO on all four benchmarks and is the only LES method to beat the DE variant on P3 (56,876 vs. 58,704) and P4 (14,396 vs. 237,000, a 90.1% improvement). On P1 and P2, DE also reaches near-optimal values, so the gap is smaller, but PartEvo still matches or exceeds it. The average-performance table (Table 2) confirms PartEvo's dominance with the lowest mean and smallest SEM on P3 (6583.8 ± 79.74) and P4 (5171.6 ± 2189.14), indicating both superior quality and greater run-to-run stability than any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-assisted Evolutionary Search (LES) paradigm established by Funsearch, EoH, and ReEvo, which have primarily relied on simplified search mechanisms such as greedy fitness-based selection, random island grouping, or verbal gradients. The key departure is the introduction of feature-assisted niche construction to partition the abstract language search space, enabling structured allocation of LLM sampling resources across subregions. This bridges a gap between classical Evolutionary Computation niching techniques (which require explicit distance metrics in numerical spaces) and the implicit, unstructured nature of language spaces, providing a general framework for integrating mature EC operators into LES pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that EC-inspired operators (cross-niche crossover, local-global guided evolution) contribute comparably to prompt-centric operators (reflection, summarization) in LES, suggesting the field should give equal emphasis to search structure design as to prompt engineering. The 90.1% improvement over the best human-designed baseline on machine scheduling (P4) highlights practical value for industrial optimization. However, the approach requires continuous algorithm evaluation (15M+ evaluations in their setup), limiting applicability to expensive black-box problems like CFD or drug design, and the language search space remains only indirectly characterized through code similarity and thought embeddings—deeper structural understanding of this space remains an open question.
