## Problem

Standard PAC learning theory characterizes sample complexity for end-to-end supervision as O(d/ε), but chain-of-thought supervision provides a richer learning signal that should yield faster rates. Prior theoretical work (e.g., Joshi et al.) sidestepped the mismatch between the CoT training objective and the end-to-end evaluation metric by bounding CoT risk, which recovers the same 1/ε rate and fails to capture the information-theoretic advantage of observing intermediate reasoning steps. This paper asks: what is the fundamental quantity that governs how much faster CoT-supervised learning can be, and can it be characterized with matching upper and lower bounds?

## Input / Output

The learner observes i.i.d. triples (x_i, y_i, z_i) where x is an input, y is the target output, and z is the chain-of-thought (a token sequence representing the execution trace of the target function on x). The training objective minimizes CoT loss (disagreement in both y and z), while the evaluation metric is end-to-end risk (disagreement in y only). This asymmetry—training on a richer signal but testing on a coarser one—is the central structural feature that makes the analysis non-trivial and motivates the CoT information measure.

## Method

The paper introduces the CoT information I^CoT_{D,h*}(ε; H), defined as the infimum over all hypotheses h with end-to-end error exceeding ε of −log P[h^CoT(x)=h*^CoT(x), h^e2e(x)=h*^e2e(x)], measuring the minimal information a single CoT sample provides for distinguishing h* from any 'bad' alternative. For finite hypothesis classes in the realizable setting, the CoT-consistency rule achieves sample complexity O(log|H| / I^CoT), replacing the standard 1/ε rate; for infinite classes, the VC dimension of the CoT loss class enters alongside the CoT information. In the agnostic setting, an excess-risk variant of the CoT information governs CoT-ERM, though CoT supervision can be harmful when the hypothesis class is misaligned with the data. Information-theoretic lower bounds via Le Cam's two-point method and Fano's inequality (with a noisy channel model) confirm that the CoT information is the fundamental quantity governing the ε-dependence of sample complexity, and simulations on DFA-based and iterated linear-threshold CoT classes show 6× to 600× empirical sample-efficiency gains matching theoretical predictions.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Result 1) shows that for finite CoT hypothesis classes in the realizable setting, the sample complexity is m(ε,δ) = (log|H| + log(1/δ)) / I^CoT_{D,h*}(ε;H), improving the standard E2E rate of O(log|H|/ε) whenever I^CoT > ε. In simulations with deterministic finite automata (state space |S|=4, alphabet |Σ|=2, input length n=10), the ratio I^CoT/ε⁺ ≈ 600, yielding a ~600× empirical gain in sample efficiency for CoT-Cons over E2E-Cons. For iterated linear thresholds (window d=8, T=16 iterations), the ratio is ≈6, matching the observed ~5× improvement in empirical learning curves. Information-theoretic lower bounds (Results 4–5) confirm the CoT information is the fundamental quantity governing the ε-dependence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between three analytical frameworks for CoT-supervised learning: (1) standard E2E PAC learning, which gives rate O(d/ε); (2) the approach of Joshi et al. [10], which bounds the CoT risk and inherits the same 1/ε dependence (O(VC(L^CoT(H))/ε)); and (3) the proposed CoT information measure, which replaces 1/ε with 1/I^CoT_{D,h*}(ε;H). Since I^CoT ≥ ε always holds (Lemma 1), the CoT information approach is never worse than E2E supervision, and strictly better whenever the CoT provides additional discriminative signal. The key advantage over Joshi et al. is that the CoT information captures the per-sample information content of the CoT annotation, yielding a tighter ε-dependence that is validated by matching lower bounds (Results 4–5 via Le Cam's method and Fano's inequality). In the agnostic setting, the paper shows CoT supervision can be harmful (CoT-ERM may fail entirely if the CoT is unrealizable), motivating the excess-risk variant Ĩ^CoT that accounts for distribution–class misalignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the statistical learning theory of CoT-supervised learning initiated by Malach [17] and Joshi et al. [10], who formulated CoT learning as composition of sequence-domain functions and bounded sample complexity via the VC dimension of the CoT loss class. The key departure is the introduction of the CoT information measure, which captures the per-sample information content of CoT annotations for distinguishing hypotheses with different end-to-end behavior, rather than relying solely on loss-class complexity. This yields a fundamentally different ε-dependence in sample complexity (governed by CoT information rather than ε itself) and enables matching information-theoretic lower bounds via Le Cam's method and Fano's inequality, which the prior VC-dimension-based approach cannot achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CoT information measure provides a principled, quantitative answer to the practical question of how much value CoT-annotated training data adds over standard input-output examples, which is critical given the high cost of CoT annotation in LLM training pipelines. The paper also reveals an important caveat: in the agnostic setting, CoT supervision can be actively harmful when the hypothesis class is misaligned with the data distribution, motivating adaptive learning rules that balance CoT and E2E objectives. Open questions include establishing agnostic lower bounds, exploring optimal (rather than consistency/ERM-based) learning algorithms, extending to probabilistic CoT, and connecting the theory to the complexity measures (Rademacher complexity, covering numbers) more relevant to neural network hypothesis classes.
