## Problem

Bayesian Personalized Ranking (BPR) loss is the dominant loss function in graph self-supervised learning for recommendation, yet its theoretical underpinnings for node relation evaluation remain unexplored. The paper reveals that BPR's dot-product score merely counts 2-hop paths in the embedding network, capturing only local connectivity while ignoring global topological structure, resulting in coarse-grained similarity scores where a single value maps to a wide range of actual topological similarities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255267/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a bipartite user-item interaction graph and learnable ID embeddings of dimension N_e, constructing an embedding network of users, items, and N_e abstract nodes. For the drug repositioning application (TopoDR), it additionally ingests five drug similarity matrices (chemical structure, ATC code, side effect, drug-drug interaction, target profile) and two disease similarity matrices (phenotype, ontology), concatenated with network embeddings as a descriptor vector fed to a random forest classifier. The output is refined node embeddings for ranking, or for drug repositioning, a ranked list of candidate drugs per disease with association probability scores.

## Method

The key insight is that the BPR score y_ui = (EE^T)_ui equals the count of 2-hop paths between user u and item i through abstract nodes in the embedding network. Using the maximum entropy principle from latent hyperbolic geometry, the authors prove that 2-hop path counts are an imprecise proxy for energy distance, motivating the TopoLa distance d_topo(u,i) = (1/λ)|2-hop| − (1/λ²)|4-hop| + (1/λ³)|6-hop| − …, which incorporates all even-hop paths with alternating signs and geometric decay. The BPR+ loss substitutes this TopoLa distance for the raw dot product inside the sigmoid ranking objective. To avoid O(N_b³) matrix-power computation, they exploit SVD of the embedding matrix to reduce the cost to O(N_b·N_e² + N_b²·N_e + N_e³), making the loss tractable at scale. The approach is validated by plugging BPR+ into five existing GSL models (LightGCN, SGL, NCL, LightGCL, AdaGCL) across five datasets, and by building TopoDR, a drug repositioning framework that integrates lightGCN+ network embeddings with multimodal drug-disease features.

## Results

Applying BPR+ loss to five state-of-the-art GSL models yields consistent improvements across all five recommendation datasets. The most dramatic gain is on Amazon, where lightGCN+ achieves Recall@10 of 0.0722 versus 0.0535 for lightGCN (a 35% relative improvement) and NDCG@10 of 0.0607 versus 0.0439 (38% improvement). On Yelp, SGL+ improves NDCG@10 from 0.0554 to 0.0742 (34% gain). In the drug repositioning case study, TopoDR (built on lightGCN+) achieves AUC 0.9594 and MCC 0.7440 on Fdataset, surpassing the next-best method AdaDR by 2.89% and 7.34% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is not against 12 independent methods but rather a plug-and-play evaluation: BPR+ loss is swapped into five existing GSL frameworks (adaGCL, lightGCL, NCL, SGL, lightGCN) and consistently improves every one. The largest relative gains appear on sparser datasets: lightGCN+ on Amazon improves Recall@10 by 35% (0.0535→0.0722) and SGL+ on Yelp improves NDCG@10 by 34% (0.0554→0.0742), while gains on denser datasets like Gowalla are modest (1–2%). In the drug repositioning task, TopoDR (lightGCN+ with multimodal features) outperforms all four baselines—deepDR, DRHGCN, DDAGDL, and AdaDR—on both Fdataset and Cdataset, with the most notable margin over AdaDR being +7.34% MCC on Fdataset (0.7440 vs 0.6935). Statistical significance is confirmed via p-values (all < 1e-7) across every dataset-model pair. The SVD-based computation of BPR+ (BPR+ MF) adds only ~20% overhead over vanilla BPR (36.5s vs 30.5s per epoch on Amazon), making the improvement practically deployable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the widely-used BPR loss in graph self-supervised learning through a graph-theoretic lens, showing that the dot-product score y_ui is equivalent to counting 2-hop paths in an embedding network. It builds directly on the TopoLa distance framework (Zheng et al., 2024) and the latent hyperbolic geometry / maximum-entropy analysis of Krioukov to argue that 2-hop paths are an imprecise proxy for energy distance, then extends the path counting to all even-hop paths to form the BPR+ loss. The paper also applies this improved loss to LightGCN and several contrastive-learning variants (SGL, NCL, LightGCL, AdaGCL), and extends the framework to drug repositioning via TopoDR, building on prior drug repositioning methods (DeepDR, DRHGCN, DDAGDL, AdaDR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic graph-theoretic and network-geometric analysis of why BPR works in GSL, which had been largely unexamined despite BPR's ubiquity. The TopoLa distance and BPR+ loss offer a principled, theoretically grounded alternative that captures global connectivity and topological structure beyond local 2-hop neighborhoods, with consistent (if modest) empirical gains across five recommendation datasets and four drug repositioning benchmarks. Key open questions include whether the theoretical framework can guide the design of new GNN modules beyond loss-function replacement, how the method scales to very large graphs where even the SVD-optimized computation may become prohibitive, and whether the even-hop path formulation generalizes to other graph learning tasks beyond collaborative filtering.
