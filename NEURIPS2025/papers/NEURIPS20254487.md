## Problem

Existing stylized image generation methods transfer entangled style representations, forcing creators to accept all visual attributes (color and texture) from a reference image simultaneously. This paper introduces the Disentangled Stylized Image Generation (DisIG) problem, which requires independently controlling color and texture from separate reference images without any model fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254487/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a color reference image (either a full RGB image or a discrete color palette), and a texture reference image, all resized to 512×512. Optionally, a content image can be supplied via ControlNet (e.g., Canny edges) for image-based stylization. The output is a single stylized image where the color distribution matches the color reference and the textural characteristics match the texture reference, generated in approximately 10.3 seconds on a single L40s GPU using 50-step DDIM sampling.

## Method

SADis exploits the Image-Prompt Additivity property in the CLIP image embedding space to disentangle color and texture without training. Color is extracted by subtracting the grayscale image embedding from the color image embedding, while texture is extracted from the grayscale texture reference and refined via SVD rescaling (with an exponential augmentation on singular values) to suppress residual gray-tone influence. The disentangled embeddings are injected only into the first decoder layer's cross-attention via IP-Adapter projectors to avoid semantic content leakage. To ensure precise color palette alignment while preserving texture details, a Regularized Whitening-Coloring Transform (RegWCT) is applied to the noisy latent at intermediate timesteps, combining a standard WCT with a small Gaussian noise term that counteracts the signal-leak bias inherent in diffusion training.

## Results

On the DisIG benchmark (4,000 images from WikiArt and StyleDrop), SADis achieves the best CLIP score (0.281) for text-image alignment and dramatically outperforms all baselines on color metrics: MS-SWD of 5.57 (vs. 9.51–14.25 for baselines), C-Hist of 0.96, and GPT-4o color score of 3.34. For texture, SADis matches InstantStyle on CLIP-I (0.74) and is second only to IP-Adapter on KID (0.049 vs. 0.043). In the user study, SADis is preferred 41.83% of the time for color alignment and 37.84% for overall quality, far exceeding the next-best method (SDXL at 16.99% and 11.65% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SADis decisively outperforms all eight baselines on every color-alignment metric, with MS-SWD reduced from 9.51 (SDXL) to 5.57 and GPT-4o color score raised from 3.01 to 3.34. On texture, SADis ties InstantStyle on CLIP-I (0.74) and trails IP-Adapter slightly on KID (0.049 vs. 0.043), but IP-Adapter suffers severe semantic leakage (CLIP drops to 0.233) and poor color fidelity (MS-SWD 11.54). Among tuning-free methods, InstantStyle and IP-Adapter retain layout/structure from the texture reference (visible in Fig. 6), while SADis cleanly separates texture from content. In the user study, SADis is preferred 41.83% of the time for color (vs. 16.99% for SDXL, the next best) and 37.84% for overall quality, a margin of at least 20 percentage points over all competitors. On the supplementary DREAMBENCH++ complex-prompt evaluation (Table 3), SADis again leads on CLIP (0.301), MS-SWD (6.08), and C-Hist (0.95), confirming robustness to more challenging prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the IP-Adapter framework for image-conditioned T2I generation and the WCT (whitening-coloring transform) lineage from classical style transfer, but departs from both by introducing a training-free disentanglement mechanism. The key conceptual departure is the identification of an 'image-prompt additivity' property in the CLIP image embedding space—previously only studied in text embedding spaces—which enables color isolation via simple grayscale subtraction. The RegWCT component extends the signal-leak bias analysis from prior diffusion inversion work into a practical noise-regularization step applied during generation, and the SVD rescaling of texture embeddings draws on weighted nuclear norm minimization ideas to suppress residual gray-tone information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SADis provides the first practical, training-free mechanism for independently controlling color and texture from separate reference images, addressing a real workflow need for content creators who require specific color palettes paired with different textural styles. The image-prompt additivity finding is potentially reusable beyond color-texture disentanglement (the paper demonstrates object add/remove as a corollary), and compatibility with MMDiT-based models (FLUX, SD3.5) suggests the approach is not architecture-specific. However, the evaluation is limited to 40 reference images across two datasets, and the authors acknowledge that content-consistency stylization remains an open challenge. Whether the additivity property generalizes to other style attributes (e.g., brushwork, composition) or to non-CLIP encoders beyond SigLIP remains unexplored.
