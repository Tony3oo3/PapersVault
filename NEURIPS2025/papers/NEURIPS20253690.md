## Problem

Bandeira et al. (2022) established that the Franz-Parisi (FP) criterion from statistical physics is equivalent to low-degree polynomial lower bounds for Gaussian additive models, but this equivalence breaks down for other model classes. The core issue is that the FP criterion fixes the overlap to the Euclidean dot product ⟨u,v⟩, which may not capture the true geometric structure of the parameter space in many statistical models. This paper asks whether optimizing the overlap event in the FP definition yields a criterion that connects to a broader, more general rigorous hardness framework—specifically Statistical Query (SQ) lower bounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a P-versus-Q detection task specified by a signal prior π on Θ ⊆ S^{N-1}, a sample size m, a runtime proxy q, and a finite group G acting on the parameter space. It produces a GFP-hardness certificate (an upper bound on the annealed FP potential integrated over an optimized G-invariant overlap event) and, under a mild correlation assumption (Assumption 1), a corresponding SQ-hardness certificate with explicit parameter relationships. The key structural detail is that the optimizing event A is constrained to be G²-invariant and of the form {ρ_G(u,v) ≤ r(q)}, where ρ_G is defined via the maximum likelihood-ratio inner product over group orbits, rather than the fixed Euclidean overlap.

## Method

The paper proposes a Generalized Franz-Parisi (GFP) criterion that replaces the fixed Euclidean overlap event in the original FP definition with an infimum over all G-invariant events A with π²(A) ≥ 1−q⁻². The central technical result (Theorem 2) shows that under Assumption 1—a pointwise non-negativity condition on group-averaged likelihood-ratio inner products—the approximately optimal overlap event takes the explicit form {ρ_G(u,v) < r(q)}, where ρ_G(u,v) = max_{g,g'∈G} |⟨L_{g(u)}, L_{g'(v)}⟩_Q − 1|. The main equivalence theorem (Theorem 3) then connects this simplified ρ_G-FP hardness to SQ-hardness via a two-step argument: SQ-hardness implies ρ_G-FP-hardness through a Hölder/averaging argument, and ρ_G-FP-hardness implies SQ-hardness by iterating the chi-squared divergence bound over t-fold tensor products. The assumption is verified for Gaussian additive models (G=ℤ₂), planted sparse models (trivial group), non-Gaussian component analysis, single-index models, and convex truncation (where it reduces to the Gaussian correlation inequality).

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative metrics. The main result (Theorem 1) establishes that the proposed Generalized Franz-Parisi (GFP) hardness criterion is equivalent to Statistical Query (SQ) hardness under a mild symmetry assumption (Assumption 1), with matching polynomial-time hardness thresholds: a task is ((mn)^ω(1), m^{1-o(1)}, O(1))-GFP hard if and only if it is ((mn)^ω(1), m^{1-o(1)})-SQ hard. The equivalence extends the prior FP-to-LD equivalence of Bandeira et al. (2022) from Gaussian additive models to a much broader class including planted sparse models, non-Gaussian component analysis, single-index models, and convex truncation. As concrete applications, the paper yields new SQ lower bounds for mixed sparse linear regression and convex truncation detection, and shows that for convex truncation, Assumption 1 is exactly equivalent to the Gaussian correlation inequality (Royen, 2014).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare empirical methods but rather compares theoretical hardness frameworks. The original Franz-Parisi (FP) criterion of Bandeira et al. (2022) is shown to be a special case of the proposed GFP criterion (FP-hardness implies GFP-hardness by definition), but the converse does not hold: Section 5 constructs an explicit detection task satisfying Assumption 1 that is GFP-hard (and hence SQ-hard) yet not FP-hard, demonstrating that the Euclidean overlap ⟨u,v⟩ is not always the correct geometric quantity. For Gaussian additive models with symmetric priors, GFP reduces exactly to the original FP criterion (since ρ_G(u,v) = exp(λ²|⟨u,v⟩|) is monotone in |⟨u,v⟩|), recovering and extending the prior FP-to-LD equivalence to also yield FP-to-SQ equivalence. For planted sparse models, the paper provides the missing reverse direction (GFP-hardness implies SQ-hardness) that was absent in Bandeira et al. (2022), which only proved FP-hardness implies LD-hardness. The GFP criterion also subsumes the SQ framework of Feldman et al. (2017) and Brennan et al. (2021), providing a unified physics-motivated lens through which known SQ lower bounds for NGCA (Diakonikolas et al., 2017) and single-index models (Damian et al., 2024) can be re-derived.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Franz-Parisi (FP) criterion introduced by Bandeira et al. (2022), which established an equivalence between the annealed FP potential and low-degree polynomial (LD) lower bounds for Gaussian additive models. The key departure is optimizing the overlap event in the FP definition (replacing the fixed Euclidean dot product with a group-symmetrized likelihood-ratio overlap ρ_G), yielding a Generalized Franz-Parisi (GFP) criterion that is proven equivalent to Statistical Query (SQ) lower bounds under a mild correlation assumption. This builds on the SQ framework of Feldman et al. (2017) and the SQ-LD equivalence of Brennan et al. (2021), effectively creating a three-way bridge (GFP ↔ SQ ↔ LD) that was previously only partially established for a narrow class of models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GFP-SQ equivalence unifies two previously disconnected approaches to proving computational hardness in statistical inference, and applies to a much broader class of models (planted sparse, NGCA, single-index, convex truncation) than the original FP-LD equivalence. The paper yields new SQ lower bounds for mixed sparse linear regression and convex truncation, and shows that the convex truncation assumption is precisely the Gaussian correlation inequality—a 42-year open problem resolved by Royen (2014). Open questions include whether the equivalence extends to estimation tasks (the original domain of the FP potential), whether the annealed approximation is fundamental, and whether the optimal overlap ρ_G yields algorithmic insights for local search methods.
