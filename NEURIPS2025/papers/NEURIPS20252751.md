## Problem

Existing MGT detection methods face a fundamental trade-off in adversarial environments: statistical-based methods extract intrinsic features but rely on rigid thresholds that fail under distribution shifts from attacks, while fine-tuned classifiers achieve high accuracy on clean data but overfit to superficial patterns and degrade sharply when tested on attacked texts. The paper addresses how to simultaneously capture intrinsic invariant features between human and machine texts and ensure the classifier dynamically adapts to adversarial perturbations and paraphrases.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw text passage as input and produces a binary classification label (human-authored vs. machine-generated). Internally, the text is segmented into sentences for per-sentence statistical feature extraction (dependency parse, Flesch-Kincaid grade, MiniLM sentence embeddings), then aggregated via a sliding window of length 3 with step 1 to yield a 12-dimensional raw feature vector. This vector is projected to 64 dimensions and concatenated with the 768-dimensional RoBERTa CLS embedding to form an 832-dimensional representation for the final classification head.

## Method

OSTAR combines a Multi-Dimensional Statistical Profiling (MDSP) module with a Multi-Faceted Contrastive Learning (MFCL) strategy. MDSP extracts three intrinsic dimensions per sentence—maximum dependency distance (syntax), Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level (lexical), and inter-sentence cosine similarity variance (semantic)—then applies a 4-D sliding-window analysis (mean, standard deviation, autocorrelation, range) per dimension to produce a 12-dim vector that is projected and fused with RoBERTa's CLS embedding. MFCL categorizes adversarial attacks into Perturbation (surface-level changes without ownership alteration) and Paraphrase (machine rewriting that may change text ownership), and designs distinct contrastive objectives for each: an InfoNCE-style loss for paraphrase pairs (pulling same-author paraphrases together, pushing cross-author apart) and a weighted similarity regularization for perturbation pairs (preserving anchor-perturbed similarity scaled by attack impact ratio). The total training objective jointly optimizes cross-entropy classification and the combined contrastive losses, updating the statistical analyzer, classifier head, and RoBERTa backbone simultaneously.

## Results

On the attacked datasets (Table 2), OSTAR achieves state-of-the-art F1 across all 9 scenarios (3 datasets × 3 conditions). Under perturbation attacks, OSTAR's F1 degrades by at most 11.09% (DeepFake: 92.36→81.27), compared to 30.97% for Binoculars and 13.02% for RoBERTa. Under paraphrase attacks, OSTAR maintains F1 of 81.46 (DeepFake), 86.04 (CheckGPT), and 97.52 (HC3), with an average degradation of only 6.30% across all datasets versus 8.49% for the most robust baseline (RoBERTa). On clean data (Table 1), OSTAR achieves average ACC/Recall/F1 of 93.95%/94.09%/94.04%, outperforming fine-tuned RoBERTa by 2.81%/2.85%/2.97% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSTAR outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and attack condition. Against the strongest baseline RoBERTa, OSTAR gains +4.17 F1 on DeepFake-Pert (81.27 vs 77.10), +2.46 on DeepFake-Para (81.46 vs 79.00), and +5.45 on HC3-Pert (95.72 vs 90.27). The statistical method Binoculars suffers catastrophic degradation under attacks (F1 drops to 45.42 on DeepFake-Pert, 52.32 on CheckGPT-Pert), while OSTAR retains 81.27 and 84.48 respectively—a gap of over 35 points. Watermark-based detection collapses under paraphrase attacks (47.01 F1 on DeepFake-Para, 50.07 on CheckGPT-Para), whereas OSTAR maintains 81.46 and 86.04. CoCo and PECOLA, both contrastive-learning-based methods, show moderate robustness but still trail OSTAR by 11.73 and 3.14 F1 on DeepFake-Pert, and by 21.38 F1 on DeepFake-Para. RADAR performs poorly overall (F1 below 60 on most attacked settings), confirming that adversarial paraphrase training alone is insufficient without intrinsic statistical feature anchoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSTAR carves new ground by bridging two previously separate paradigms in MGT detection: statistical-based methods (Binoculars, DetectGPT, Eduard) that extract intrinsic textual features but rely on rigid decision thresholds, and classifier-based methods (fine-tuned RoBERTa, CoCo, RADAR, PECOLA) that achieve high accuracy but overfit to superficial patterns. The paper's core architectural idea—concatenating hand-designed multi-dimensional statistical profiles (MDSP) with transformer CLS embeddings to form an enhanced classifier—is explicitly framed as the first use of statistical features to guide a classifier-based detector for adversarial robustness. The contrastive learning component (MFCL) builds on the broader NLP contrastive learning literature (CoCo, DeTeCtive, PairCFR) but introduces a novel attack-type-aware formulation that separates perturbation and paraphrase losses based on their distinct impact mechanisms on text ownership and surface features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: current MGT detectors degrade sharply under real-world adversarial attacks (paraphrasing, perturbation), limiting their deployment in content moderation and academic integrity settings. OSTAR's moderate but consistent improvements across all nine experimental scenarios (3 datasets × 3 attack conditions) suggest that the statistical-feature-augmented classifier is a viable recipe for practitioners seeking robustness without architectural overhaul. Key limitations remain: the method struggles with unforeseen attack compositions (multiple paraphrases, combined attacks), the sliding-window statistical analysis requires sufficient text length, and the contrastive training on attacked data introduces a small fidelity loss on clean data. Open questions include whether the MDSP feature set generalizes to non-English or code-mixed texts, and whether the perturbation/paraphrase dichotomy adequately captures the full spectrum of real-world evasion strategies.
