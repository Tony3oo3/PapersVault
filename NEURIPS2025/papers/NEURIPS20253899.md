## Problem

While it is known that initial noise seeds influence text-image alignment in diffusion models, the precise mechanism by which specific noise patterns encode compositional information remains unexplored. This paper asks whether the noise-to-image map contains universal structural cues that persist across datasets, architectures, and pixel/latent spaces, and whether these cues can be identified and manipulated without model-specific training.

## Input / Output

The Patch PCA denoiser takes a noisy image x_σ at diffusion time step σ and produces a denoised estimate by applying a linear PCA filter to each overlapping p×p×c patch (4×4×3 for pixel-space models, 5×5×4 for Stable Diffusion's latent space), then reconstructing from center pixels. The zero-shot editing method takes an initial Gaussian noise tensor z and a reference image x_r, and outputs an edited noise z' by interpolating in selected PCA frequency bands. Experiments span 64×64 RGB images (ImageNet, FFHQ, AFHQ), 32×32 (CIFAR-10), and 4×64×64 latent representations.

## Method

The core contribution is a Patch PCA denoiser that applies a linear PCA-based denoiser independently to each local patch using a covariance matrix computed from generic natural image patches, and the paper proves (Theorem 5.1) that under a Gaussian patch prior and the local-patch constraint inherent to convolutional architectures, this denoiser is exactly optimal for the standard diffusion denoising loss. Empirically, the paper demonstrates that low-frequency patch PCA components of the initial noise predominantly determine compositional structure, and that identical noise seeds produce structurally similar images across different datasets (ImageNet, FFHQ, AFHQ, CIFAR-10) and architectures (U-Net, U-ViT, flow-matching, latent diffusion). The zero-shot editing method performs spherical linear interpolation (SLERP) between noise and reference image patches within selected low-frequency PCA bands (e.g., eigenvectors 1–9 excluding color-dominant directions), then rescales the edited component to preserve the original noise energy, enabling compositional control without any model fine-tuning or additional training.

## Results

The Patch PCA denoiser achieves SSIM scores of 0.474–0.548 with diffusion-model-generated images when sharing the same noise seed, exceeding even cross-dataset diffusion model comparisons (0.423–0.469), while random-pair SSIM drops to 0.029–0.031. In the zero-shot editing experiment on CIFAR-10 (256 seeds), low-frequency editing in band [1,9]\{2,5} raises SSIM vs. reference from 0.084 (no edit) to 0.289 while preserving color (histogram correlation 0.576 vs. 0.381 for the full band). For Stable Diffusion in latent space, seed-paired SSIM reaches 0.42 (vs. 0.18 random), climbing to 0.816 under top-1 PCA projection. Frequency perturbation analysis confirms that perturbing only high-frequency bands (n > 20) yields MSE below 0.01, while including low-frequency bands causes substantially larger image changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pre-trained EDM models (Karras et al.), the Patch PCA denoiser achieves higher same-seed SSIM (0.474–0.548) than any cross-dataset EDM pair (0.423–0.469), demonstrating that a simple linear patch-level method captures the same universal compositional structure as sophisticated neural networks. Compared to the U-ViT transformer architecture (Bao et al.), the U-ViT vs. EDM paired SSIM is 0.820 while U-ViT vs. Patch PCA paired SSIM is 0.481, confirming the compositional cue is architecture-agnostic. For Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al.), seed-paired SSIM with Patch PCA reaches 0.42 (vs. 0.18 random), and the top-1 projection SSIM of 0.816 shows the structural alignment is even stronger in the lowest-frequency subspace. The flow-matching model (Tong et al.) on AFHQ achieves paired SSIM of 0.784 vs. EDM and 0.512 vs. Patch PCA, further confirming cross-architecture consistency. In the zero-shot editing task, excluding color-encoding eigenvectors 2 and 5 from the editing band improves color preservation (histogram correlation 0.576 vs. 0.381) while also improving structural alignment (SSIM 0.289 vs. 0.252), showing the method's frequency-band selectivity outperforms naive full-band interpolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear PCA denoiser framework of Li et al. [20] from the full-image level to a patch-level setting, building on Kamb & Ganguli's [15] observation that convolutional architectures constrain each pixel's output to depend only on its local patch. It departs from prior noise-seed optimization work [23, 36, 39, 19] that established seeds influence composition but left the mechanism unexplained, by providing a mechanistic account through patch PCA frequency-band analysis. The approach also connects to the inductive-bias literature [20, 34, 24] but redirects it: rather than seeking dataset-specific optimal surrogates, the paper identifies universal compositional structures that generalize across diverse data distributions and architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that low-frequency noise components encode universal compositional cues—consistent across ImageNet, FFHQ, AFHQ, CIFAR-10, and both pixel-space and latent diffusion models—provides a mechanistic explanation for the long-observed seed-dependent composition phenomenon and opens a practical path to training-free compositional control. The zero-shot SLERP-based noise editing is immediately usable but currently demonstrated only on small (32×32, 64×64) images with simple single-object compositions; scaling to high-resolution multi-object scenes and complex layouts remains open. The pixel-to-latent subspace alignment (cosine ≈ 0.93) suggests a deeper structural correspondence between VAE encoders and natural image statistics that warrants further investigation.
