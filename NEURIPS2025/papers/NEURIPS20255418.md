## Problem

DeepSeek-R1-Zero demonstrated that large-scale RL training directly on base models (Reasoner-Zero) yields steady scaling of both reasoning performance and response length, but the pipeline was not open-sourced and its critical design choices remained underexplored. This paper addresses the gap by providing the first fully open-source, large-scale implementation and systematically identifying which algorithmic components are actually necessary for stable, scalable training—revealing that a surprisingly minimal recipe suffices.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single reasoning question as a text prompt (math, science, logic puzzles, code, or instruction-following tasks) and generates a free-form chain-of-thought reasoning process enclosed in <think>... tags followed by a final answer in <answer>...</answer> tags. During training, each prompt yields 64 sampled responses at temperature 1.0 and top-p 1.0, and a binary reward (1 for exact answer match, 0 otherwise) is assigned at the terminal token. The prompt template explicitly instructs the model to reason before answering and to place the answer inside the designated tags, which even unaligned base models adopt within a few training steps.

## Method

The core method is vanilla PPO with GAE(γ=1, λ=1) and no KL regularization, applied directly to Qwen2.5 base models (0.5B–32B) without any SFT or distillation warm-up. Setting γ=1 and λ=1 collapses the GAE advantage to the closed form Â_t = R − V_φ(s_t) and the value target to the scalar terminal reward R, eliminating the need for discounted-return bookkeeping. The learned critic is the key differentiator from GRPO: it performs fine-grained credit assignment by assigning lower values to states containing repetitive or degenerate patterns, which translates into consistently more negative advantages for those tokens and prevents the training collapse observed with GRPO. The reward is a minimal binary rule-based check on the extracted answer, and training uses a curated 57k diverse dataset (math + general reasoning) with strict on-policy updates (one policy step per rollout, 12 critic mini-batch steps per iteration). For the 32B model, an additional annealing stage on 13k hard prompts with linear LR decay further boosts performance on complex problems.

## Results

Open-Reasoner-Zero-32B (ORZ-32B), trained from the Qwen2.5-32B base model using vanilla PPO with GAE (γ=1, λ=1) and a binary rule-based reward, achieves 48.1 on AIME2024, 92.2 on MATH500, and 55.5 on GPQA Diamond—surpassing DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B on all three benchmarks while requiring only 1/10 of the training steps. On generalization benchmarks, ORZ-32B reaches 84.9 on MMLU and 74.4 on MMLU_PRO, outperforming Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (83.2 / 69.2) without any instruction tuning. The recipe scales consistently from 0.5B to 32B parameters and generalizes across model families (Qwen, Llama) and task domains (math, code, puzzles, instruction following) without algorithmic modifications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B (the same Qwen2.5-32B base model), ORZ-32B wins on all three reported benchmarks—AIME2024 (48.1 vs. 47.0), MATH500 (92.2 vs. 91.6), and GPQA Diamond (55.5 vs. 55.0)—while completing training in roughly one-tenth the number of steps, a substantial efficiency gain. Against DAPO-Qwen-32B, ORZ-32B is essentially tied on AIME2024 (48.1 vs. 48.3) but dramatically outperforms it on MATH500 (92.2 vs. 71.8, a 20.4-point gap) and GPQA Diamond (55.5 vs. 16.0, a 39.5-point gap); the authors attribute DAPO's weakness to its data-curation strategy that forces all answers into integer form, which degrades multiple-choice performance. On generalization, ORZ-32B also surpasses DAPO on MMLU (84.9 vs. 79.7) and MMLU_PRO (74.4 vs. 64.5). Overall, ORZ establishes a new open-source baseline that matches or exceeds both prior Reasoner-Zero implementations in both accuracy and training efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the Reasoner-Zero paradigm introduced by DeepSeek-R1 (RL directly on a base model with verifiable rewards) as the first fully open-source, large-scale implementation. It departs from DeepSeek's GRPO-based pipeline by adopting vanilla PPO with a learned critic, simplifying GAE to the closed-form γ=1, λ=1 case, and removing all KL regularization. The algorithmic backbone is standard PPO/GAE (Schulman et al.), but the key conceptual contribution is showing that the learned value function provides superior credit assignment—specifically devaluing repetitive degenerate patterns—that GRPO's group-relative baseline cannot replicate. Concurrent efforts like DAPO and VAPO explore similar scaling but with more complex algorithmic modifications or different data strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a deliberately minimalist recipe (no KL, no format reward, no algorithmic tricks) achieves competitive or superior performance with 1/10 the training steps of DeepSeek-R1-Zero, this paper lowers the barrier to entry for large-scale reasoning RL and provides a reproducible baseline the community can build upon. The mechanistic analysis of the critic's role in penalizing repetitive patterns offers a concrete explanation for GRPO's well-documented training collapse, guiding future algorithm design. Open questions remain around test-time scaling (multi-turn, multi-agent), code-generation reasoning, and whether the observed scaling continues indefinitely or eventually saturates at larger compute budgets.
