## Problem

Large reasoning models (LRMs) like OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1 excel at long-horizon reasoning but are constrained by static internal knowledge, limiting their performance on knowledge-intensive tasks and their ability to synthesize comprehensive research reports from diverse web sources. Existing open-source deep search agents rely on predefined RAG workflows that prevent LRMs from autonomously exploring deeper web information or tightly interleaving reasoning with search and writing. The paper addresses the need for a universal, flexible, open-source deep research framework where the LRM itself decides when to search, navigate, and draft.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text query (a complex reasoning question or an open-ended research topic) along with a task instruction; in Report Generation Mode it also receives a pre-generated research plan. During inference, the model dynamically consumes web search results (top-10 from Bing API) and full web page content (fetched via Crawl4AI), with the Deep Web Explorer sub-process recursively fetching additional pages by clicking links. In Problem-Solving Mode, output is a reasoning chain interleaved with tool calls, ending in a boxed final answer. In Report Generation Mode, output is a full markdown research report built incrementally through assistant-LLM-executed draft, check, and edit operations, rather than generated in a single pass.

## Method

WebThinker introduces a hierarchical agentic architecture with two key components: (1) a Deep Web Explorer module, which is itself an LRM-driven sub-process that recursively searches the web and navigates pages by clicking links/buttons to gather information beyond shallow search results, and (2) an Autonomous Think-Search-and-Draft strategy where the main LRM interleaves reasoning, web exploration, and report writing in real time using three specialized tools (section drafting, report checking, report editing) executed by a separate assistant LLM with access to a document memory of all explored pages. To optimize tool utilization, the paper employs iterative online DPO: the model self-samples multiple reasoning trajectories per task, preference pairs are constructed using a priority hierarchy (correctness > fewer tool calls > shorter reasoning), and the policy is updated on-policy across iterations with the reference model refreshed each round. This design differs from standard RAG pipelines by making all search, navigation, and writing decisions autonomous within the LRM's continuous reasoning process, with no predefined workflow or fixed retrieval schedule.

## Results

WebThinker-32B-RL achieves 70.7% average on GPQA, 48.5% on GAIA, 46.5% on WebWalkerQA, and 15.8% on HLE (Pass@1), setting new state-of-the-art among 32B open-source models across all four complex reasoning benchmarks. On the Glaive scientific report generation task, WebThinker-32B-RL scores 8.1/10 average (evaluated by DeepSeek-R1-671B and GPT-4o), surpassing Gemini2.0 Deep Research (7.9) and Grok3 DeeperSearch (6.5). The RL-trained variant improves over the training-free Base version by +8.5% on GAIA and +21.5% on HLE, and surpasses o3-mini (High) on HLE (15.8 vs. 14.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WebThinker-32B-RL outperforms Search-o1-32B by +21.9% on GAIA (48.5 vs. 39.8), +36.2% on HLE (15.8 vs. 10.8), and +22.9% on WebWalkerQA (46.5 vs. 34.1), demonstrating the advantage of deep web exploration over shallow search. Against direct-reasoning baselines, WebThinker-32B-RL surpasses QwQ-32B by +26.4% on GAIA and +42.2% on WebWalkerQA, and beats the much larger DeepSeek-R1-671B on GAIA (+17.4%) and WebWalkerQA (+36.5%), though it trails on GPQA (70.7 vs. 74.2). On HLE, WebThinker-32B-RL (15.8) exceeds o3-mini High (14.0) and all other open-source 32B models, but remains below the proprietary OpenAI Deep Research (26.6) and Gemini-2.0-Flash-Thinking (7.1 on HLE direct, but 26.6 with deep research). On Glaive report generation, WebThinker-32B-RL (8.1) edges out Gemini2.0 Deep Research (7.9) and substantially outperforms Grok3 DeeperSearch (6.5) and RAG-DeepSeek-R1 (6.8), with the largest gap in Completeness (8.3 vs. 8.1 for Gemini) and Thoroughness (8.4 vs. 8.0). The RL training provides consistent gains over the Base version across all benchmarks, with the largest improvement on HLE (+2.8 absolute, +21.5% relative), confirming the effectiveness of iterative online DPO for tool-use optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

WebThinker extends the line of search-augmented reasoning initiated by Search-o1 and RL-based search training (Search-R1, R1-Searcher) by introducing a hierarchical Deep Web Explorer that can navigate and click links beyond shallow search, and by interleaving autonomous report drafting with the reasoning loop. It departs from predefined RAG workflows (standard, iterative, query-planning) by embedding all tool invocations within a single continuous reasoning generation, and from prior RL-for-tool-use work by applying iterative online DPO specifically to multi-step research trajectories rather than single-turn search. The Autonomous Think-Search-and-Draft strategy, which uses an assistant LLM to execute writing operations while the main LRM orchestrates, is a novel architectural separation not present in prior agentic RAG systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WebThinker provides the first open-source, general-purpose deep research framework that matches or exceeds proprietary systems (OpenAI Deep Research, Grok3, Gemini2.0) on both complex reasoning and report generation, making advanced research automation accessible. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal web content, whether the limited tool set can scale to arbitrary APIs, and whether GUI-based navigation (rather than link-clicking) would further improve real-world robustness. The iterative online DPO recipe for tool-use improvement is a reusable technique likely to inform future agentic LRM training.
