## Problem

Efficient fine-tuning of pre-trained text-to-image models faces a fundamental trade-off: learning novel concepts from a few reference images for personalization while retaining instruction-following ability for multi-task generation and maintaining editability across diverse prompts. Existing low-rank methods like LoRA and PaRa suffer from overfitting on small datasets, limited fine-grained control over pose and spatial positioning, and degraded instruction adherence during extended training, particularly in complex multi-subject or multi-scene compositions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253413/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DEFT operates on both latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, SDXL) and unified transformer models (OmniGen), accepting text prompts, reference images for subject personalization, Canny edge maps, depth maps, and style reference images as conditioning signals. For personalization tasks, rank-4 adapters are injected into UNet and condition encoder layers; for universal image generation via in-context learning, rank-32 adapters are applied to transformer linear layers. The model produces generated images at standard diffusion resolutions, supporting single-subject personalization, multi-concept composition, scene adaptation, and camera-aware generation from SFM parameters.

## Method

DEFT decomposes the weight update ΔW into two components: a projection onto the orthogonal complement of a learned low-rank subspace, (I − PPᵀ)W₀, and a trainable low-rank adjustment PR, yielding W_total = (I − PPᵀ)W₀ + PR. The matrix P (factorized via QR, SVD, NMF, or LRMF) defines the subspace to project out, while R is a trainable low-rank matrix that injects task-specific directions within that subspace. The key insight is that when col(Q) from the factorization of P is not contained in col(W₀), the total column space strictly extends beyond the original weight's column space, enabling adaptation to new concepts while the projection term preserves the model's prior knowledge and prevents catastrophic forgetting. A higher learning rate is applied to R relative to P (mirroring LoRA's A/B asymmetry), and the framework supports plug-and-play structural biases such as ReLU-based non-negative projection for sparser updates.

## Results

On the DreamBench Plus benchmark (150 subjects, 8 diverse prompts), DEFT achieves the highest CLIP-T score of 0.361 on SDXL v1.0, surpassing PaRa (0.354), DreamBooth LoRA (0.341), and DreamBooth (0.323). On VisualCloze conditional generation, DEFT fine-tuned on OmniGen achieves CLIP-Score 95.78 and DINOv2 90.65 under Canny Edge (vs. OmniGen's 95.45/87.60), and 93.18/85.75 under Depth Map (vs. OmniGen's 92.02/77.39). For style transfer, DEFT attains the best Image Score of 0.69 on both InstantStyle and ReduxStyle tasks, with F1 of 0.59 on InstantStyle. In the ablation on training steps, DEFT maintains CLIP-T of 0.319 at 8000 steps while LoRA degrades to 0.219, demonstrating superior long-training stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEFT outperforms all 8 baselines on the primary DreamBench Plus CLIP-T metric, with the closest competitor being PaRa (0.354 vs. 0.361, +0.007) and DreamBooth LoRA (0.341, +0.020). On VisualCloze conditional generation, DEFT consistently beats OmniGen and both VisualCloze variants (dev and fill) on CLIP-Score, DINOv1, and DINOv2 under both Canny Edge and Depth Map conditions, with the largest gap being DINOv2 under Depth Map (85.75 vs. OmniGen's 77.39). For style transfer, DEFT achieves the highest Image Score (0.69) on both InstantStyle and ReduxStyle, outperforming OmniGen (0.52/0.58) and VisualCloze variants (0.53–0.55), though its Text Score is marginally lower than VisualCloze-dev (0.28 vs. 0.30 on InstantStyle). In Table 3 controllability/quality, DEFT achieves the best F1 (0.48) and SSIM (0.66) under Canny Edge, beating OmniControl (F1 0.47, SSIM 0.61) and OmniGen (F1 0.43, SSIM 0.47), and the best SSIM (0.72) under Depth Map. In the supplementary DreamBooth CLIP-I comparison (Table 7), DEFT (rank 4) scores 0.8339/0.9280/0.8721 on BEAR_PLUSHIE/CAT/DOG8, outperforming LoRA (rank 4: 0.7741/0.8057/0.7773) but slightly trailing PaRa (rank 8: 0.8051/0.9467/0.8955) on DOG8. Notably, DEFT degrades far less than LoRA at extended training (8000 steps): CLIP-T drops from 0.302 to 0.319 for DEFT but from 0.286 to 0.219 for LoRA, confirming DEFT's resistance to catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEFT extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning lineage for diffusion models by combining two previously separate ideas: PaRa's rank-reduction projection (W₀ − QQᵀW₀) and LoRA's trainable low-rank update (BA). Rather than treating these as alternatives, DEFT unifies them into a single two-component decomposition where a trainable projection matrix P removes a learned subspace from the frozen weights while a second trainable matrix R injects task-specific directions within that subspace. The paper further applies this adapter to unified transformer-based models (OmniGen) for visual in-context learning, a setting where prior LoRA/PaRa work has been less explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition offers a practical middle ground between LoRA's flexibility and PaRa's stability, with the training-steps ablation showing DEFT maintains instruction-following quality at 8000 steps where LoRA degrades substantially. The emergent multi-concept composition results (no separate adapter per concept) are promising but remain qualitative. The paper does not discuss limitations, and the decomposition-method ablation reveals that the specific factorization (QR, NMF, TSVD, LRMF) barely affects performance, suggesting the core benefit comes from the two-component structure rather than any particular decomposition. Open questions include whether the approach scales to many simultaneous concepts, whether it transfers to video generation, and whether the modest CLIP-T gains (0.361 vs. 0.354 for PaRa) persist at larger ranks or on more diverse benchmarks.
