## Problem

AI-generated videos from models like Sora have reached near-perfect visual realism, making detection via local artifacts (optical flow inconsistencies, appearance mismatches) increasingly unreliable. Existing methods ignore the physics-driven constraints (probability flow conservation) that govern natural spatiotemporal evolution, leaving them vulnerable to synthetic content that is visually plausible but physically implausible. The paper asks how to model intrinsic spatiotemporal dynamics of natural videos to expose such subtle physical violations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a test video (any length, any resolution) and a reference set of real videos (e.g., 10,000 clips from Kinetics-400). A pre-trained diffusion model is used as a frozen feature extractor to compute per-frame score functions; no explicit optical flow or motion decomposition is required. The output is a binary real/fake label determined by whether the MMD between the test video's NSG features and the reference set's NSG features exceeds a threshold τ (default 1.0), which the paper shows is stable across τ ∈ [0.7, 1.1].

## Method

The paper formulates video evolution as a probability flow velocity field governed by a continuity equation, then defines the Normalized Spatiotemporal Gradient (NSG) g(x,t) = ∇_x log p(x,t) / (−∂_t log p(x,t) + λ) as a dual field that captures the ratio of spatial probability gradients to temporal density changes without solving the ill-posed velocity inversion. For practical estimation, spatial gradients are obtained via a pre-trained diffusion model's score function s_θ in a single forward pass, while temporal derivatives are approximated through a brightness constancy constraint (∂_t log p ≈ −∇_x log p · Δx/Δt), eliminating explicit flow computation. Detection is performed by computing the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) between NSG feature distributions of the test video and a reference set of real videos, using a deep kernel optimized via multi-population aware training. A theoretical upper bound on the squared NSG feature distance is derived under Gaussian assumptions, showing the bound scales with the noncentrality parameter φ = ‖μ‖²/σ(t)², thereby proving that larger distribution shifts in generated videos produce larger MMD separations.

## Results

On the GenVideo benchmark (trained with 10,000 Kinetics-400 real videos and 10,000 Pika-generated videos), NSG-VD achieves 88.02% average Recall, 91.46% Accuracy, 90.87% F1-score, and 96.14% AUROC across 10 diverse AI-generated video sources. This represents a 16.00% improvement in Recall and 10.75% in F1-score over the strongest baseline DeMamba (72.02% Recall, 80.12% F1). Under a data-imbalanced setting (only 1,000 generated training videos), NSG-VD still achieves 93.21% average Recall, outperforming DeMamba by 29.12% in Recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NSG-VD outperforms all four baselines across every metric and every generator in both training settings. Against DeMamba [25] (the strongest baseline), NSG-VD gains 16.00% in Recall (88.02% vs. 72.02%) and 10.75% in F1 (90.87% vs. 80.12%), with the largest gap on Sora (78.57% vs. 48.21% Recall) and HotShot (100% vs. 98.80%). Against NPR [27], the gap widens to 30.67% in Recall and 22.48% in F1, as NPR struggles with unstable performance (Recall ranging from 16% to 98% across generators). TALL [24] is beaten by 27.24% in Recall and 18.24% in F1, with TALL collapsing to 25.00% Recall on Sora. STIL [36] is the weakest baseline, with NSG-VD exceeding it by 61.00% in Recall and 55.05% in F1; STIL achieves near-zero Recall on Sora (1.79%) and Gen2 (2.00%). Notably, NSG-VD's advantage is most pronounced on closed-source or emerging generators (Sora, HotShot, WildScrape), where artifact-based baselines fail most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in AI-generated video detection, which relies on artifact-specific cues (optical flow inconsistencies, appearance mismatches, spatial-temporal layout features) as exemplified by Demamba, TALL, NPR, and STIL. Instead, it reframes video dynamics through probability flow conservation (a continuity equation from fluid mechanics) and introduces the Normalized Spatiotemporal Gradient (NSG) as a generator-agnostic statistic. The NSG estimator repurposes pre-trained diffusion model score functions (Song & Ermon 2019; Song et al. 2021) not for generation but as spatial gradient approximations, combined with a brightness-constancy-based temporal derivative (Horn & Schunck 1981). For detection, it adapts the MMD framework with deep kernels (Gretton et al. 2012; Liu et al. 2020) previously applied to text and adversarial detection, now operating on NSG features rather than raw or learned representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As video generators like Sora approach photorealism, artifact-based detectors become increasingly brittle; a physics-driven, generator-agnostic signal offers a more robust detection principle. The theoretical upper bound (Theorem 1) provides a principled justification that distributional shifts amplify NSG discrepancies, though it relies on a strong Gaussian assumption that limits its direct applicability to real video distributions. Open questions include whether the brightness constancy assumption holds for fast or non-rigid motions, how the method scales to longer or 3D video content, and whether the pre-trained diffusion model's score approximation introduces systematic biases that could be exploited by adversarial generators. The data-imbalanced results (1,000 generated training samples) suggest practical deployability, but the single-benchmark evaluation (GenVideo) leaves cross-dataset generalization partially untested.
