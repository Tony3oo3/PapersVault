## Problem

2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) produces severe aliasing artifacts when rendering at sampling rates different from training (e.g., zooming in or out), limiting its practical use in applications requiring variable fields of view. The paper identifies two root causes: the absence of frequency constraints on the 2D Gaussian primitives, and an ineffective screen-space clamping heuristic that actually exacerbates rather than mitigates aliasing. Unlike volumetric 3DGS, 2DGS evaluates Gaussians at ray-splat intersection points in local primitive space, making standard screen-space anti-aliasing techniques inapplicable.

## Input / Output

The method is trained on multi-view RGB images with known camera poses (100–400 training views depending on dataset) and renders novel views via volumetric alpha blending of oriented 2D Gaussian disks. A key operational detail is that the model is trained at a single resolution (or multi-scale with biased sampling) and can render at arbitrary higher or lower resolutions to simulate zoom-in/zoom-out, with the anti-aliasing filters adapting per-pixel via the Jacobian of the ray-splat intersection mapping. Beyond RGB, the method also produces depth maps, surface normal maps, and 3D surface reconstructions (Chamfer distance on DTU), with anti-aliasing benefits extending to all rendered attributes.

## Method

AA-2DGS introduces two complementary anti-aliasing mechanisms adapted to the planar 2D Gaussian representation. First, a world-space flat smoothing kernel projects an isotropic 3D low-pass filter onto each primitive's tangent plane, adding σ_smooth²I₂ to the 2D covariance (where σ_smooth² is derived from the Nyquist limit of training views), which band-limits the representation's frequency content and prevents high-frequency magnification artifacts. Second, an object-space Mip filter leverages a first-order Taylor (affine) approximation of the ray-splat intersection mapping, whose Jacobian J maps a screen-space Gaussian Mip filter into local splat space, yielding a per-pixel modified covariance Σ_local' = I + σJJᵀ that is evaluated directly where the Gaussian lives. This avoids the numerical instabilities and computational overhead of screen-space evaluation while centering the affine approximation per pixel for accuracy with large primitives. The two components are complementary: the flat smoothing kernel addresses pre-aliasing from the representation (zoom-in), while the object-space Mip filter handles rendering-time aliasing (zoom-out), together replacing the original 2DGS clamping heuristic.

## Results

On the Blender dataset with multi-scale training and testing, AA-2DGS achieves 34.60 avg PSNR, 0.978 avg SSIM, and 0.020 avg LPIPS, outperforming 2DGS (29.40 PSNR) by over 5 dB and matching Mip-Splatting (34.56 PSNR). In the more challenging single-scale training / multi-scale testing setting (zoom-out), AA-2DGS reaches 32.11 avg PSNR versus 24.47 for 2DGS and 31.97 for Mip-Splatting, with the gap widening dramatically at 1/8 resolution (29.00 vs. 16.59 PSNR for 2DGS). On DTU surface reconstruction, AA-2DGS achieves 0.74 mean Chamfer distance, slightly improving over 2DGS (0.80) and matching GDF (0.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AA-2DGS substantially outperforms the original 2DGS across all multi-scale settings: on Blender multi-scale training/testing it gains +5.2 dB avg PSNR (34.60 vs. 29.40), and on the single-scale training / zoom-out test it gains +7.6 dB (32.11 vs. 24.47), with the gap reaching 12.4 dB at 1/8 resolution. Removing the clamping heuristic from 2DGS recovers much of the performance (31.05 avg PSNR on Table 1, 30.54 on Table 2), confirming that the original clamping actively harms rendering, but AA-2DGS still leads by 3.55 and 1.57 dB respectively. Against the strongest 3DGS-based competitor Mip-Splatting, AA-2DGS achieves a marginal edge on multi-scale Blender (34.60 vs. 34.56 PSNR) and single-scale-to-multi-scale Blender (32.11 vs. 31.97), but trails slightly on same-scale Mip-NeRF 360 (27.38 vs. 27.79 PSNR), which the authors attribute to the inherent sharpness trade-off of band-limiting. On DTU surface reconstruction, AA-2DGS (0.74 Chamfer) matches GDF and GaussianSurfels while beating 2DGS (0.80) and 3DGS (1.96), confirming that the anti-aliasing modifications preserve 2DGS's geometric accuracy. Against NeRF-based methods (MipNeRF, Tri-MipRF, Zip-NeRF), AA-2DGS is competitive or superior on multi-scale settings but slightly behind on same-scale Mip-NeRF 360, consistent with the known MLP overfitting advantage of NeRF at in-distribution resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the two-pronged anti-aliasing strategy of Mip-Splatting (3D frequency constraint + screen-space Mip filter) to the fundamentally different rendering pipeline of 2DGS, where Gaussians are evaluated at ray-splat intersections in local splat space rather than projected to screen space. The adaptation is non-trivial: the 3D smoothing kernel must be projected onto each planar primitive's tangent plane (yielding a 'flat' 2D smoothing), and the Mip filter must be mapped from screen space into object space via the Jacobian of the ray-splat intersection mapping. The work also builds on the EWA splatting framework for the Gaussian-under-affine-transform properties that enable the object-space filter derivation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

2DGS is the go-to representation for geometry-aware rendering (depth, normals, mesh recovery) where volumetric 3DGS fails, but its aliasing at non-training resolutions has limited practical deployment in zoomable or variable-FOV applications. The paper's finding that 2DGS's built-in clamping heuristic actively degrades performance (rather than helping) is a useful negative result that corrects a common assumption. Open questions include whether the fundamental zero-thickness problem of planar primitives (causing 'needle-like' artifacts at grazing angles during magnification) can be resolved without abandoning the 2D formulation, and whether adaptive per-scene filter parameters could close the remaining gap to Mip-Splatting in same-scale settings.
