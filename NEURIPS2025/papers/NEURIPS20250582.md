## Problem

Estimating causal effects from spatiotemporal observational data is hindered by the simultaneous presence of spatial interference, temporal carryover, and time-varying confounding—where covariates are shaped by past treatments and in turn drive future ones. Existing methods either impose rigid structural assumptions, require user-specified exposure mappings, or assume independent time series across units, leaving a gap for principled causal inference from a single observed spatiotemporal trajectory in data-scarce settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single spatiotemporal trajectory on an N_X × N_Y lattice over T time steps, comprising time-varying covariates (e.g., PM2.5, weather), binary treatments, continuous outcomes, and static spatial features, reorganized into overlapping 'prefixes' of length τ to create training samples. It also takes a predetermined interventional sequence a_{t:t+τ-1} specifying counterfactual treatments. The output is a full N_X × N_Y spatial grid of location-specific Conditional Average Potential Outcomes (CAPOs) at horizon t+τ, from which CATEs and other effect measures can be derived by differencing under two intervention sequences.

## Method

GST-UNet couples a U-Net encoder augmented with a ConvLSTM layer and attention gates (producing a shared spatiotemporal embedding φ) with τ G-computation heads that implement iterative G-computation in reverse temporal order: Q_τ regresses on real observed outcomes, while each earlier Q_k regresses on pseudo-outcomes generated by Q_{k+1} under the counterfactual treatment path. A key theoretical contribution is a representation-based time-invariance assumption that renders overlapping prefix segments conditionally exchangeable, enabling consistent estimation from a single trajectory (Theorems 1–3). Training uses a curriculum schedule that activates G-heads progressively—starting with Q_τ on real data and gradually incorporating earlier heads as pseudo-outcomes stabilize—combined with joint multi-task loss aggregation to prevent the shared embedding from being pulled in conflicting directions by different heads.

## Results

On synthetic spatiotemporal data (64×64 grid, T=200), GST-UNet achieves the lowest RMSE across all confounding levels (β₁ ≥ 0.5) and both horizons (τ=5, 10). At the strongest confounding (β₁=2.0), GST-UNet reduces RMSE by 32.2% (τ=5) and 21.3% (τ=10) relative to the best baseline (UNet+). In the real-world Camp Fire analysis, GST-UNet estimates ~4,650 excess respiratory hospitalizations (95% CI [1888, 6535]) over November 8–17, 2018, with substantially tighter confidence intervals than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GST-UNet is compared against three baselines: UNet+ (same U-Net+ConvLSTM+Attention backbone but no G-computation), STCINet [1] (U-Net-based direct/indirect effect estimation without time-varying confounding adjustment), and IPWUNet (inverse-propensity-weighting adapted from [49]). At β₁=0 (no confounding), UNet+ outperforms GST-UNet by 17.9% (τ=5) and 35.7% (τ=10), confirming that G-computation adds unnecessary noise when confounding is absent. As confounding strengthens, the gap reverses: at β₁=2.0, GST-UNet beats UNet+ by 32.2% (τ=5) and 21.3% (τ=10), and beats STCINet by 55.6% and 46.9% respectively. IPWUNet remains biased even at β₁=0 due to uncorrected spatial interference, and its estimates become implausibly high in the wildfire application (~20,500 excess cases vs. GST-UNet's 4,650). In the real-world Camp Fire analysis, GST-UNet produces the most stable and plausible counterfactual estimates, with a 95% bootstrap CI of [1888, 6535] compared to UNet+'s wider [-899, 5202] and STCINet's near-zero, highly variable [−3077, 3281].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends iterative G-computation (Robins & Hernán) from independent longitudinal trajectories to a single spatiotemporal chain with spatial interference, introducing a representation-based time-invariance assumption to enable valid pooling across time. Architecturally, it combines a U-Net encoder with ConvLSTM and attention gates—components drawn from medical imaging and precipitation nowcasting literature—with multiple G-computation heads trained via a curriculum-based multi-task strategy. It departs from prior neural G-computation methods (G-Net, G-Transformer, Causal Transformer) that assume access to many independent patient-level time series, and from spatiotemporal causal methods (IPW-based marginal structural models, STCINet) that either cannot handle interference or do not perform iterative confounding adjustment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GST-UNet provides the first end-to-end, theoretically grounded framework for counterfactual estimation from a single spatiotemporal trajectory under time-varying confounding and spatial interference—settings common in environmental health and policy but previously lacking principled tools. The wildfire application demonstrates practical utility, though the method's reliance on a regular lattice, a time-invariant representation, and unobserved-confounding assumptions limits direct transfer to irregular spatial structures or highly non-stationary regimes. Open questions include extending the framework to graph-structured spatial domains, handling regime shifts that violate Assumption 2, and scaling to higher-dimensional treatment spaces beyond binary interventions.
