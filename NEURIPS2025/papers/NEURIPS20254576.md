## Problem

Existing dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods model the motion of each Gaussian independently, ignoring the hierarchical and spatially correlated structure of real-world deformations. This independence assumption prevents the capture of coherent, structured motion patterns and creates a significant modeling gap, while MLP-based deformation approaches introduce prohibitive per-Gaussian computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254576/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video frames with known camera poses and a point cloud for initialization (100K points for D-NeRF monocular scenes, 300K points from COLMAP for Neural 3D Video at half resolution of 2704×2028). It produces a compact dynamic 3D scene representation where each Gaussian carries a pre-merged motion coefficient vector, enabling novel view synthesis at arbitrary viewpoints and timesteps at over 200 FPS without runtime tree traversal.

## Method

TreeSplat organizes Gaussian motions in an adaptive hierarchical tree where each node stores coefficients for shared, time-varying basis functions generated by a lightweight MLP (queried once per timestep rather than per Gaussian). The tree grows in conjunction with Gaussian densification via two alternating modes: Leaf Expansion adds sibling leaf nodes under the same parent, and Depth Promotion converts an existing leaf into a parent with two new children. Each Gaussian's full motion is computed by aggregating ancestor coefficients along its leaf-to-root path, weighted by a learnable per-Gaussian decay factor that suppresses distant ancestors and stabilizes gradient flow. The key architectural property is mergeability: after training, the linear aggregation is reordered so that per-Gaussian coefficients are pre-computed offline, collapsing the tree into a flat representation that eliminates all traversal overhead at inference while preserving reconstruction quality to within floating-point precision.

## Results

On the D-NeRF dataset, TreeSplat achieves an average PSNR of 37.11 dB, SSIM of 0.98, and LPIPS of 0.02, outperforming the best baseline DynMF (35.72 dB) by 1.39 dB while using fewer Gaussians (85K vs. 101K) and achieving 230 FPS after tree merging. On the Neural 3D Video dataset, TreeSplat reaches an average PSNR of 32.21 dB with 206 FPS rendering speed, 170 MB storage, and 0.57 hours training time per scene, surpassing the closest competitor SpaceTimeGS (32.05 dB) and RealTime4DGS (32.01 dB). The method also trains in just 4 minutes per D-NeRF scene, matching the fastest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On D-NeRF, TreeSplat (37.11 dB) decisively outperforms all 9 baselines, with the largest margins over 4DGaussian (+3.06 dB), 4DRotorGS (+2.85 dB), and DynMF (+1.39 dB), while also achieving the highest FPS (230) and lowest Gaussian count (85K). On Neural 3D Video, TreeSplat (32.21 dB) edges out SpaceTimeGS (32.05 dB) and RealTime4DGS (32.01 dB) by 0.16 and 0.20 dB respectively, while also delivering the fastest rendering (206 FPS vs. 140 and 101 FPS) and the smallest storage (170 MB vs. 200 MB and 2085 MB). Among NeRF-based methods, TreeSplat surpasses the best (MixVoxels-X at 31.73 dB) by 0.48 dB on N3V. Notably, TreeSplat matches or beats the fastest training times (4 min on D-NeRF, 0.57 h on N3V) while using significantly fewer Gaussians than 4DRotorGS (392K) and RealTime4DGS (445K), indicating that the hierarchical motion tree enables more efficient scene representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

TreeSplat extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with explicit temporal components, building directly on DynMF's per-Gaussian coefficient formulation with shared basis functions, but replaces the independent per-Gaussian motion parameters with a hierarchical tree that couples motion across spatially adjacent Gaussians. It departs from prior dynamic GS methods (4DGaussian, SpaceTimeGS, RealTime4DGS) that model each Gaussian's deformation in isolation, and from concurrent hierarchical-motion work (HiCoM, HiMoR) that uses fixed-depth or heuristic tree structures, by adaptively growing the tree alongside Gaussian densification. The mergeability property—pre-aggregating tree-path coefficients offline so that inference reduces to a simple linear combination—has no direct analogue in prior dynamic GS or hierarchical GS literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mergeable tree design offers a practical recipe for dynamic GS practitioners: use structured, collaborative motion modeling during training for quality, then collapse to per-Gaussian coefficients for fast inference, achieving 200+ FPS with compact storage. The ablation showing that temporal opacity windows actively degrade reconstruction (Table 5) challenges a common design choice in prior 4DGS methods and suggests that spatial motion alone suffices for opacity modulation. Open questions include scalability to very large or streaming scenes where the tree must grow online, whether the fixed depth-29 sweet spot generalizes across scene complexity, and whether the tree structure can be leveraged for editing or partial re-optimization without full retraining.
