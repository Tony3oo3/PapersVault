## Problem

Standard iterative imputation imposes a single parametric model form (e.g., linear or tree) for all features, risking model misspecification when features exhibit heterogeneous dependencies. Additionally, it assumes every feature contains missing values, overlooking oracle (non-missing) features that could serve as high-quality supervisory signals, especially under high missing ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250706/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an incomplete data matrix X^(obs) ∈ R^{N×D} and a binary missingness mask M ∈ R^{N×D}, where missing entries are marked as NaN. It produces a fully imputed matrix X^(imp) by treating missing entries as learnable parameters updated via gradient descent, while observed entries remain fixed throughout training. Two mini-batches (source and target) are sampled from the imputation matrix each iteration, and the kernel ensemble weights Δ ∈ R^E are jointly optimized alongside the imputed values.

## Method

KPI reformulates iterative imputation as a bi-level optimization: the inner level learns a feature-specific imputation model in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS), where the representer theorem yields a closed-form kernel ridge regression solution f*(X^t) = K_{X^t X^s}(K_{X^s X^s} + λI)^{-1}Y^s, allowing each feature to adapt its functional form. The outer level exploits oracle features as supervisory signals by minimizing the prediction error of the optimal estimator on observed oracle values, thereby refining the imputed entries of other features. To handle the difficulty of kernel hyperparameter selection under incomplete data, KPI employs an adaptive kernel ensemble that learns simplex weights Δ over E Gaussian kernels with different bandwidths. Training proceeds via stochastic gradient descent with automatic differentiation, updating only missing entries (masked by M) and the ensemble weights, with gradients of the matrix inverse stopped for numerical stability.

## Results

KPI achieves the best MSE and MAE across all 7 UCI datasets (averaged over missing ratios 0.1–0.4). Headline MSE values: BT 0.397, CC 0.347, CBV 0.402, IS 0.400, PK 0.319, QB 0.264, WQW 0.491. The largest margins over the next-best method (NewImp) appear on CC (0.347 vs 0.412, ~16% lower MSE) and QB (0.264 vs 0.332, ~20% lower MSE). On the WASS distribution-discrepancy metric at 10% missingness, KPI achieves 0.010 on CC and 0.022 on BT, substantially below NewImp (0.110 and 0.091 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KPI outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset for both MSE and MAE. The closest competitor is NewImp (a diffusion-based method), which is second-best on most datasets but trails KPI by 16–20% MSE on CC and QB. Remasker (masked autoencoding) is competitive on BT (MSE 0.439 vs 0.397) and WQW (0.546 vs 0.491) but degrades sharply on CC (0.767) and IS (0.599). Among iterative imputers, MICE and MissForest are the strongest, with MICE achieving MSE 0.580 on BT and 0.417 on PK, yet KPI still leads by 32% and 23% respectively. Generative time-series methods (CSDI-T, MissDiff) suffer severe MAE degradation (e.g., 15.5 and 13.4 on IS), confirming their poor fit for tabular data. Distribution-matching methods (Sinkhorn, TDM) and GAIN are consistently outperformed, with Sinkhorn trailing KPI by over 2× MSE on most datasets. MIRACLE is competitive on CC (0.487) and WQW (0.555) but catastrophically fails on IS (MSE 3.208) and PK (3.518), highlighting its sensitivity to dataset characteristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative imputation paradigm (MICE, MissForest) by reformulating it as a bi-level optimization problem. The inner level replaces the fixed parametric model form with a nonparametric function in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, leveraging the representer theorem to obtain a finite-dimensional kernel-based solution. The outer level introduces a novel use of oracle features (fully observed columns) as supervisory signals to refine imputations of other features—a mechanism absent from prior iterative methods that only use oracle features as inputs. The adaptive kernel ensemble strategy further departs from prior kernel methods by learning a convex combination of multiple kernels to avoid manual bandwidth selection under incomplete data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle-feature supervision mechanism is a practical and underexploited insight: in real-world tabular data, some columns (demographics, reliable sensors) are always observed, and using them as direct supervisory signals rather than mere inputs can substantially improve imputation, especially at high missing ratios. The RKHS-based model form adaptation addresses a long-standing misspecification risk in iterative imputation without requiring per-feature model selection heuristics. However, the evaluation is confined to seven small UCI tabular datasets (≤780 samples), the kernel ensemble is acknowledged as heuristic without theoretical guarantees, and noise robustness is explicitly left for future work—limiting confidence in scalability to large, noisy industrial datasets.
