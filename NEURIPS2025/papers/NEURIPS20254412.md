## Problem

Corner-based floorplan representations are highly sensitive to noise and occlusion: a single missed corner can fragment or distort an entire room polygon, while line-grouping methods that improve robustness tend to over-smooth fine geometric details. No existing end-to-end method simultaneously captures global structural coherence and local geometric precision under incomplete observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254412/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D point clouds (from multi-view RGB-D scans) projected vertically into 256×256 2D density maps, where each pixel value is normalized to [0,1] by dividing the projected point count by the per-image maximum. The output is a set of directed edge sequences (each edge defined by two endpoints in [0,1]² plus a binary validity label) that are converted into closed, watertight room polygons via a deterministic edge-intersection procedure. Notably, predicted edge endpoints need not coincide with true polygon vertices—they may lie anywhere along wall segments, and actual vertices are recovered by intersecting adjacent edges.

## Method

CAGE replaces discrete corner regression with a continuity-aware edge-centric formulation where each wall is a directed edge whose endpoints encode directionality and structural continuity, making the representation inherently more stable under sparse or noisy observations. The decoder is a dual-query transformer (6 layers, 256-dim) that jointly processes latent queries (randomly initialized, for final prediction) and perturbed queries (ground-truth edges displaced by bounded noise with optional label flipping, for denoising supervision), following the DN-DETR paradigm to stabilize training and accelerate convergence. A Swin Transformer V2-L backbone with multi-scale deformable attention provides spatially grounded encoder features, and edge coordinates are refined iteratively via per-layer offset prediction. Training uses a two-level Hungarian matching (polygon-level with cyclic rotation handling) and a composite loss combining binary cross-entropy classification, L1 edge regression, differentiable rasterization Dice loss, and separate denoising losses for perturbed queries.

## Results

On the Structured3D test set, CAGE achieves state-of-the-art F1 scores of 99.1% (Room), 91.7% (Corner), and 89.3% (Angle) without any post-processing, using a SwinV2-L backbone. It also attains the highest Corner Precision (95.0%) and ties for the fastest inference speed (0.01 s/image) among all compared methods. On SceneCAD, CAGE achieves the best Room IoU (93.7%) and Angle F1 (79.2%), while in cross-dataset evaluation (trained on Structured3D, tested on SceneCAD) it reaches 85.6% Room IoU and 87.5% Corner Recall, outperforming all baselines on recall-oriented metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAGE outperforms the strongest corner-based baseline FRI-Net by +3.9 points in Corner F1 (91.7 vs. 87.8) and +2.4 points in Angle F1 (89.3 vs. 86.9), while matching its Room F1 (99.1). Against PolyRoom, CAGE leads by +1.5 in Corner F1 and +4.1 in Angle F1, though PolyRoom edges ahead in Corner Precision (94.6 vs. 95.0 is actually CAGE winning; PolyRoom's Corner F1 of 90.2 is still below CAGE's 91.7). On SceneCAD, CAGE achieves the highest Room IoU (93.7%) and Angle F1 (79.2%), while PolyRoom narrowly wins Corner F1 (91.2 vs. 90.6). In cross-dataset generalization, CAGE surpasses PolyRoom in Room IoU (85.6 vs. 85.2) and Corner Recall (87.5 vs. 79.9), and exceeds FRI-Net by +5.0 in Room IoU and +3.4 in Angle F1. Notably, applying PolyDiffuse post-optimization to CAGE yields only marginal gains (Corner F1 stays at 91.7), indicating the edge-based formulation already captures structure effectively without heavy post-processing, whereas FRI-Net benefits more from the same post-optimization (87.8 → 91.1 Corner F1).

## Relation to Prior Work

CAGE directly extends RoomFormer's two-level query transformer architecture by replacing its corner-based polygon representation with a directed, geometrically continuous edge formulation, while retaining the hierarchical room/edge token structure. The iterative coordinate refinement mechanism is inherited from Deformable DETR's offset prediction, and the dual-query decoder adapts DN-DETR's perturbed-query denoising strategy to the floorplan setting. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant corner-detection-and-connection paradigm (HEAT, RoomFormer) and the line-grouping approach (FRI-Net) by treating each wall segment as a first-class prediction target, which removes the dependency on precise vertex localization and enables global structural reasoning from partial observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The edge-centric reformulation offers a principled alternative to corner-based methods that are brittle under occlusion and noise, and the demonstrated cross-dataset generalization (85.6% Room IoU on SceneCAD from Structured3D training) suggests the representation captures transferable geometric priors. The method achieves SOTA with the fastest inference (0.01 s/image) and requires no post-processing, making it practical for real-time or resource-constrained deployment. Open questions remain around handling dense occlusions, fine architectural elements (columns, small interior rooms), and checkpoint instability during training; the authors also note future directions toward semantic priors, full architectural element recovery, and outdoor scaling.
