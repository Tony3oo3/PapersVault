## Problem

Kernel-based conditional independence (CI) tests such as KCI are highly sensitive to kernel bandwidth choices, yet existing data-driven kernel selection methods (statistic-to-variance ratio criteria) developed for two-sample and unconditional independence testing cannot be directly transferred to CI testing. The core obstacle is that CI testing relies on regression residuals to remove the conditioning effect, introducing an estimation bias that is absent in direct discrepancy-based tests, making gradient-based kernel optimization unreliable and leaving kernel selection for CI largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of i.i.d. observations (x_i, y_i, z_i) split into training and testing halves, along with kernel type specifications (Gaussian or Laplace) and a predefined list of 10 weight multipliers applied to median-heuristic bandwidths for φ_x and φ_y (yielding 100 candidate combinations; φ_z is fixed at the median heuristic). It outputs the selected kernel parameters, a p-value computed via weighted chi-squared or Gamma approximation of the null distribution, and a binary CI test decision at a user-specified significance level (default 0.05). The training set drives kernel selection while the test set is used exclusively for the final hypothesis test.

## Method

The proposed Power method first decomposes the KCI statistic to isolate φ_z(Z) from the concatenated regression residual φ_xz(X,Z), which avoids estimating the ill-specified identity operator μ_{Z|Z} and reduces CME estimation bias. It then defines a selection criterion as the ratio of the unbiased U-statistic estimator of the KCI statistic to its estimated asymptotic variance, which asymptotically approximates test power under H_1. Kernel parameters are selected via grid search over the 100 candidate combinations (parallelizable since each regression is independent), choosing the parameters that maximize the criterion on the training set. Theoretically, the paper establishes the first convergence rate for this power-based criterion under CI testing, decomposing the total error into a regression-bias term O(n^{-(β-1)/(2(β+p))}) and a random-sampling term O(n^{-1/2}), where β governs CME smoothness and p governs eigenvalue decay. Grid search is preferred over continuous optimization because the regression-induced bias renders gradient signals unreliable for improving test power, while the criterion remains informative for ranking discrete candidates.

## Results

On synthetic CI testing (n=500, d_Z=5, γ=1), Power achieves consistently lower Type II error than Median across all conditioning dimensions while both maintain Type I error below the 0.05 significance level; RBPT2 and CIRCE fail to control Type I error, and GCM slightly exceeds it. On causal discovery (Table 2), Power outperforms Median on F1 score for most synthetic graph densities (e.g., 0.603 vs 0.586 at density 0.4; 0.515 vs 0.490 at density 0.7) and on the SACHs benchmark (0.674 vs 0.576), though Median edges out Power on CHILD (0.790 vs 0.762). Testing time for Power is approximately 2× that of Median (e.g., 9.64s vs 4.42s at n=800), remaining manageable despite evaluating 100 parameter combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Median (KCI with median-heuristic bandwidth), Power achieves consistently lower Type II error across all synthetic settings (varying d_Z, n, and γ) while maintaining comparable Type I error control; the gap does not diminish with increasing sample size. On causal discovery, Power wins on 6 of 7 synthetic densities and on SACHs (0.674 vs 0.576 F1), but loses slightly on CHILD (0.762 vs 0.790). Against CIRCE (Pogodin et al., 2022), Power is clearly superior: CIRCE fails to control Type I error and accepts all H₁ instances, rendering it unreliable. Against GCM (Shah and Peters, 2020), Power maintains Type I error below 0.05 while GCM slightly exceeds the significance level, and Power achieves lower Type II error. Against RBPT2 (Polo et al., 2023), Power dominates on both error types: RBPT2 fails Type I error control and its Type II error increases with d_Z, performing near random guessing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the statistic-to-variance ratio criterion for kernel selection (Gretton et al., 2012b; Liu et al., 2021; Albert et al., 2022) from two-sample and unconditional independence testing to the conditional independence setting, where the test statistic depends on regression residuals and thus carries intrinsic estimation bias. It adopts the decomposition of the KCI statistic from Pogodin et al. (2024) to isolate the conditioning kernel from the regression residual, reducing the ill-posedness of estimating the identity operator. A key departure from prior kernel selection work is the use of grid search rather than continuous optimization, motivated by the finding that gradient-based methods are unreliable when the criterion is contaminated by regression-induced bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Kernel selection for CI testing was previously handled by the median heuristic, and this paper provides the first principled, power-based alternative with a convergence guarantee that explicitly accounts for estimation bias—a component absent from prior kernel selection theory. The practical impact is demonstrated through improved Type II error in CI testing and downstream causal discovery (PC algorithm on SACHs and CHILD). However, the paper acknowledges that formal Type I error control for KCI under general (non-additive) conditions remains unresolved, and the grid-based search does not guarantee optimality; establishing uniform convergence over kernel parameters and valid null calibration under general settings is identified as a key open direction.
