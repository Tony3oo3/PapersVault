## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on MLLMs optimize perturbations for specific image-prompt pairs, causing severe overfitting that renders them ineffective on unseen combinations. While a few recent works attempt cross-prompt or cross-image transfer, they require enumerating training prompts/images and fail entirely on unseen test samples. The paper addresses how to generate a single, input-agnostic perturbation that generalizes across diverse, previously unseen image-prompt pairs without exhaustive enumeration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252526/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of RGB images (from MS-COCO or DALLE-3) and task-specific text prompts (VQA questions, captioning/classification instructions) to estimate input distributions, then produces a single universal adversarial perturbation δ bounded by ‖δ‖∞ ≤ η (default 16/255). The perturbation is applied additively to the image input at inference time, and the goal is to steer the MLLM's text output toward a specified target answer (e.g., 'I am sorry.') regardless of which unseen image-prompt pair the user supplies.

## Method

The core idea is to model the joint image-prompt input space as a product of two Gaussian distributions via Laplace approximation, then optimize the perturbation to fit that distribution rather than individual samples. For images, the mean is the dataset average and the covariance is estimated from the Hessian of the log-posterior at the mean; for prompts, the mean is found via MAP estimation using momentum gradient descent on the MLLM loss, and the covariance is derived from a simplified Hessian estimate (Eq. 10). At each optimization iteration, Monte Carlo sampling draws image-prompt pairs from these Gaussians, and the perturbation is updated via sign-gradient (PGD-style) steps on the cross-entropy loss between the MLLM output and the target answer. This distribution-fitting objective implicitly disrupts shared semantic patterns across all possible inputs, yielding strong cross-image/prompt transferability with a single optimization run.

## Results

On MS-COCO, the proposed method achieves 49.8% EM-ASR and 51.4% CM-ASR in the Cross Images/Prompts setting on LLaVA-1.5, compared to 0.0% for PGD and CroPA and 19.2% for UniAtt. In the standard (No Cross) setting, the method remains competitive, reaching 96.7% EM-ASR on LLaVA-1.5 (vs. 96.1% for PGD). Across all three MLLMs and both datasets, the method is the only one achieving meaningful cross-image/prompt transfer, with EM-ASR ranging from 30.8% to 60.9% depending on model and dataset, while baselines drop to 0–20.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGD (Cui et al., CVPR 2024) is a sample-specific attack that achieves strong No Cross performance (e.g., 96.1% EM-ASR on LLaVA-1.5) but completely fails in all cross-image/prompt settings, dropping to 0.0% EM-ASR across every model and dataset. CroPA (Luo et al., ICLR 2024) supports cross-prompt transfer (e.g., 57.3% EM-ASR on LLaVA-1.5) but is entirely ineffective for cross-image or joint cross-image/prompt attacks (0.0% EM-ASR). UniAtt (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2024) is the closest competitor, achieving 19–21% EM-ASR in cross-image/prompt settings, but the proposed method outperforms it by 2.4–3.4× (e.g., 49.8% vs. 19.2% on LLaVA-1.5 MS-COCO, 60.9% vs. 20.9% on LLaVA-1.5 DALLE-3). In the standard No Cross setting, the proposed method is competitive but not always best (e.g., 49.3% vs. 75.6% for UniAtt on BLIP-2 MS-COCO), reflecting the trade-off of optimizing for distributional generality rather than a single sample. The paper also compares against DUAP (Kim et al., 2024) under an untargeted setting on ImageNet, where the proposed method achieves 98.6% ASR vs. 92.5% for DUAP in classification and 89.7% vs. 83.3% in captioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack literature on MLLMs by shifting the optimization target from specific image-prompt pairs (PGD) or enumerated prompt/image sets (CroPA, UniAtt) to the underlying input distribution itself. It repurposes Laplace approximation—a tool previously applied at the model level for Bayesian uncertainty estimation—into a data-centric role: approximating the joint distribution of images and prompts as Gaussians via Hessian-based covariance estimation. The Monte Carlo sampling mechanism then optimizes a single input-agnostic perturbation over this approximated distribution, departing from the sample-specific or enumeration-based paradigms of all prior MLLM attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in MLLM security: existing attacks require generating distinct perturbations per image-prompt pair, which is infeasible at scale. By fitting a perturbation to the input distribution rather than individual samples, the method achieves strong cross-image/prompt transfer (49–61% EM-ASR) where prior methods collapse to near-zero, while remaining computationally comparable to single-perturbation baselines. Key limitations include the white-box assumption, the Gaussian approximation's validity for highly non-Gaussian real-world inputs, and the simplified Hessian inverse estimation (Eq. 10). Open questions include whether the distribution-fitting paradigm extends to black-box settings, multi-turn conversations, or more complex multimodal inputs beyond image-text pairs.
