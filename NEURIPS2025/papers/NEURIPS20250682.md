## Problem

Existing channel permutation methods for N:M sparse LLMs rely on handcrafted quality metrics (e.g., sum of retained weight importance) to determine optimal channel ordering, but these proxies fail to capture the true impact of pruning on output loss and can even lead to performance degradation. No prior work had demonstrated a way to learn channel permutations end-to-end that directly minimizes the discrepancy between dense and sparse model outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250682/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices (linear layers only, ~99% of parameters) and 128 calibration samples of 1024 tokens each, under a fixed N:M sparsity constraint (e.g., 2:4 or 4:8). It outputs a sparse model where each layer's weights are both channel-permuted and pruned, along with the learned block-diagonal permutation matrices. A key structural detail is that input activations must be permuted by reordering the output channels of the preceding layer, which preserves the N:M sparsity pattern since it is a row-wise operation. Weight values themselves are never modified—only their ordering and which entries are zeroed out change.

## Method

PermLLM introduces learnable channel permutation (LCP) by relaxing discrete permutation matrices into differentiable soft permutation matrices via Sinkhorn normalization (5 iterations), parameterized by a learnable matrix W_P of the same shape. To make this tractable for LLMs with thousands of channels, a block-wise strategy restricts permutation to within blocks of size B=64, reducing parameters from C_in² to C_in×B and Hungarian-algorithm hardening complexity from O(C_in³) to O(C_in·B²). During the forward pass, soft permutations are hardened into strict permutation matrices via the Hungarian algorithm, and the pruning mask is determined by argmax over the permuted importance scores; during the backward pass, Straight-Through Estimator (STE) approximates gradients through both the hardening and argmax operations. The optimization objective is a cosine similarity loss between the dense model's output and the sparse model's output, trained with AdamW (lr 1e-3 to 5e-3) for 50 iterations while all weight matrices remain frozen. The framework is a drop-in plugin for existing one-shot pruning methods (Wanda, RIA), which supply the importance metric used for mask selection, and a custom CUDA kernel accelerates the channel permutation operation by 84× over PyTorch.

## Results

On Wikitext2 perplexity (lower is better), PermLLM_wanda achieves 9.39 on LLaMA-2 7B, 14.03 on LLaMA-3.1 8B, and 13.58 on Qwen-2.5 7B, consistently outperforming Wanda (12.16, 23.42, 24.44) and SparseGPT (11.12, 16.62, 14.34) respectively. On zero-shot average accuracy, PermLLM_wanda reaches 46.59% on LLaMA-2 7B (vs. 42.12% for Wanda and 44.52% for SparseGPT) and 52.17% on Qwen-2.5 7B (vs. 47.91% and 51.25%). The method also achieves ~1.67× inference speedup over dense models with 2:4 sparsity, with the channel permutation overhead reduced to 0.039ms via a custom CUDA kernel (84× speedup over PyTorch).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PermLLM_wanda consistently outperforms Wanda and Wanda+CP across all seven models on Wikitext2 perplexity, with the largest gains on LLaMA-3.1 8B (14.03 vs. 23.42/21.09) and Qwen-2.5 7B (13.58 vs. 24.44/18.76), where standard one-shot pruning degrades severely. Against SparseGPT (which updates remaining weights), PermLLM_wanda wins on LLaMA 7B (9.41 vs. 11.19), LLaMA-2 7B (9.39 vs. 11.12), LLaMA-3.1 8B (14.03 vs. 16.62), and Qwen-2.5 7B (13.58 vs. 14.34), but is slightly behind on OPT 6.7B (14.27 vs. 14.33). Similarly, PermLLM_RIA beats RIA+CP on all models, with the most notable improvement on LLaMA-3.1 8B (15.79 vs. 19.80) and Qwen-2.5 7B (15.93 vs. 17.58). On zero-shot tasks, PermLLM_wanda achieves the highest average accuracy among all non-weight-update methods on every model tested, e.g., 46.59% on LLaMA-2 7B vs. 44.52% (SparseGPT) and 42.12% (Wanda), and 52.17% on Qwen-2.5 7B vs. 51.25% (SparseGPT) and 47.91% (Wanda).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the N:M semi-structured pruning line (SparseGPT, Wanda, RIA) and the channel permutation technique introduced by Pool & Yu (2021) and refined in RIA (2024), which reorders input channels to maximize the sum of retained weight importance under N:M constraints. The key departure is replacing the handcrafted importance metric with an end-to-end learnable objective: the authors relax discrete permutation matrices into soft doubly stochastic matrices via Sinkhorn normalization, apply STE for gradient flow through both the Hungarian hardening step and the argmax mask selection, and parameterize permutations at block granularity to keep the problem tractable. The block-wise design draws on the block-wise operations already standard in post-training compression (GPTQ, Wanda), and the overall framework is explicitly positioned as a drop-in plugin for existing one-shot pruners rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

N:M sparsity is the only structured sparsity pattern with native hardware acceleration on NVIDIA GPUs, making it the most practically relevant pruning granularity for LLM deployment; improving its accuracy without weight updates is therefore directly impactful. The paper's demonstration that the standard importance-score proxy can actively worsen pruning error (Figure 1) challenges a widely used assumption and motivates learning-based alternatives. Open questions include whether the approach extends to quantization (where channel reordering has also shown benefits), whether it scales to 70B+ models within reasonable compute budgets, and whether the 2.5–5.5 hour training cost can be further reduced beyond the partial-layer variant already explored.
