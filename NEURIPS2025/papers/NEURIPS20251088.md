## Problem

Virtual footwear try-on (VFTON) is a critical but underexplored subtask of virtual try-on that cannot reuse garment VTON pipelines due to three unique challenges: data scarcity (no open-source dataset exists because subtle shoe variations make pair-matching difficult and companies keep try-on data proprietary), viewpoint misalignment (product shoe views are inherently misaligned with target foot poses, causing incomplete texture information), and background-induced color distortion (reflective shoe materials interact with environmental lighting, contaminating the perceived color of the footwear).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251088/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a human foot image, one or two product shoe images from different views, a shoe-agnostic binary mask, and foot pose landmarks as input, and produces a 768×768 virtual try-on image. The denoising stream receives four concatenated channel components (noisy human latents, VAE-encoded shoe-agnostic mask, resized agnostic mask, and foot pose landmarks) totaling 49 channels, while the conditioning stream processes the multi-view shoe images separately. At inference, the model runs for 25 denoising steps on a single A10 GPU.

## Method

ShoeFit builds on FLUX.1-Lite and replaces the text stream with a replicated image conditioning stream, creating a dual-image-stream DiT that decouples shoe feature extraction from the denoising process (saving ~40% of parameters by removing T5 and CLIP encoders). Multi-View Conditioning injects multiple product views via spatial Reference Concatenation and a novel 3D RoPE that extends the standard 2D rotary embedding with a view-index dimension (D_n=16, D_h=56, D_w=56) to prevent semantic discontinuities at concatenation boundaries. LayeredRefAttention uses a Squeeze-and-Excitation block with global average pooling on each conditioning view to derive background scale/shift parameters, which modulate the foreground shoe features before they enter the denoising attention; the denoising stream then attends only to foreground shoe KVs while the conditioning stream processes unmasked latents, effectively decoupling intrinsic shoe appearance from environmental lighting. The model is trained end-to-end with AdamW (lr=3e-5) for 7 days on 8 A100 GPUs using DeepSpeed ZeRO-2.

## Results

On the MVShoes benchmark, ShoeFit-1V achieves SSIM 0.772, LPIPS 0.159, FID 21.07, KID 0.944, and DISTS 0.1059 in the single-view setting, outperforming all four baselines on every metric. In the two-view setting, ShoeFit-2V further improves to SSIM 0.780, LPIPS 0.149, FID 20.18, KID 0.485, and DISTS 0.0941. The most dramatic gain is in KID, where ShoeFit-1V (0.944) reduces the score by 55–72% relative to the strongest baselines (OOTDiffusion 2.345, CatVTON 3.327), indicating substantially better distributional fidelity of synthesized try-on images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShoeFit-1V wins against all four single-view baselines on all five metrics. Against FLUX.1-Fill and FLUX.1-Fill-Redux (which use only LoRA with rank 64), ShoeFit improves FID by 3.4–3.6 points and KID by roughly 2×, reflecting the advantage of full-parameter training over LoRA-only fine-tuning. OOTDiffusion-SDXL is the strongest baseline on paired reconstruction (DISTS 0.1114 vs. 0.1059 for ShoeFit) but lags significantly on unpaired fidelity (KID 2.345 vs. 0.944), suggesting it produces images that look plausible in isolation but deviate from the true distribution. CatVTON-Flux-Lite achieves the best SSIM among baselines (0.745) yet has the worst KID (3.327), indicating structural similarity without distributional realism. In the two-view comparison, ShoeFit-2V outperforms CatVTON-Flux-Lite-2V by 0.024 SSIM, 0.022 LPIPS, 2.46 FID, 2.83 KID, and 0.0099 DISTS, confirming that the dual-stream architecture and LayeredRefAttention provide gains beyond simply adding a second conditioning view.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the virtual try-on (VTON) literature from flat garments to 3D multi-view footwear, building on the diffusion-based VTON lineage (OOTDiffusion, CatVTON, StableVTON) and the FLUX.1 DiT backbone. It departs from prior VTON methods in three key ways: (1) removing the text stream entirely in favor of a dual-image-stream architecture that replicates the denoising stream as a dedicated conditioning branch, (2) extending 2D RoPE to 3D RoPE to handle multi-view reference concatenation without positional discontinuities, and (3) introducing LayeredRefAttention, which uses SE-block-derived background statistics to modulate foreground shoe features rather than simply masking backgrounds. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf tools (Qwen-VL, CLIP, SAM, DINO, DWPose, Grounding-DINO) in a novel multi-stage filtering pipeline specific to footwear matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MVShoes (7,305 annotated triplets) fills a critical gap as the first open-source footwear try-on dataset, enabling reproducible research in a commercially important but previously unbenchmarked subdomain. The finding that naive background masking *worsens* color fidelity (Table 2) challenges the intuitive approach and motivates the more nuanced LayeredRefAttention design. Open questions remain around rendering small logos/text, robust mask generation for heavily occluded footwear, and whether explicit 3D geometric or material priors can further improve multi-view consistency. The dual-stream architecture's efficiency advantage over CatVTON (38% less GPU memory, 22% faster inference) makes it a practical baseline for future footwear try-on work.
