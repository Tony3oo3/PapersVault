## Problem

Pre-trained T2I diffusion models used for real-world image super-resolution produce outputs with highly variable perceptual quality due to inherent sampling stochasticity, yet existing preference-alignment methods (e.g., Diff-DPO) treat this randomness as a limitation and rely on simplistic best-vs-worst pairs from different models. The paper identifies that no prior work has designed a perceptual reward tailored to Real-ISR or systematically explored how preference pair construction should vary with model capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255723/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image as input, which is encoded by an image encoder and injected as a ControlNet condition into a frozen pre-trained T2I backbone (Stable Diffusion 2.0 UNet or FLUX MMDiT). During inference, a single high-resolution RGB image is produced via iterative denoising (25 steps for FLUX, 50 for SD2) with classifier-free guidance. During training, M diverse HR candidates (up to 64) are generated per LR input by varying the noise seed, and these are scored and paired to form preference supervision.

## Method

DP²O-SR constructs a hybrid perceptual reward by averaging normalized scores from three full-reference metrics (LPIPS, TOPIQ-FR, AFINE-FR) and six no-reference metrics (MANIQA, MUSIQ, CLIPIQA+, TOPIQ-NR, AFINE-NR, Q-Align), balancing structural fidelity against natural appearance. Rather than selecting a single best-vs-worst pair across models, it samples M outputs from the same stochastic model, ranks them by reward, and forms N² preference pairs from the top-N and bottom-N candidates, with the optimal N/M ratio depending on model capacity (1/4 for 0.8B C-SD2, 1/16 for 12B C-FLUX). On top of this, Hierarchical Preference Optimization (HPO) adaptively weights each training pair by its intra-group reward gap and its inter-group reward dispersion, so that high-contrast, high-diversity comparisons receive greater gradient emphasis. The final loss is a weighted sum of per-pair Diff-DPO objectives, trained for 1,000 iterations with β=5000 on 8×A800 GPUs.

## Results

On the out-of-domain RealSR benchmark, DP²O-SR (SD2) achieves the best perceptual scores across nearly all NR metrics: MANIQA 0.7031, CLIPIQA+ 0.7852, MUSIQ 73.16, Q-Align 4.0206, and VQ-R1 4.21, surpassing all six SOTA baselines. DP²O-SR (FLUX) further pushes Q-Align to 4.1492 and VQ-R1 to 4.32, the highest among all methods. On the in-distribution Syn-Test set, DP²O-SR (SD2) leads on MANIQA (0.7165), CLIPIQA+ (0.8124), and Q-Align (4.5526), while DP²O-SR (FLUX) achieves the top Q-Align (4.7060) and VQ-R1 (4.65). PSNR/SSIM decrease slightly (e.g., RealSR PSNR drops from 23.61 to 22.49 for SD2), consistent with the known perception–distortion tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP²O-SR (SD2) outperforms all six SOTA baselines on every trained NR perceptual metric on RealSR: MANIQA (0.7031 vs. AddSR's 0.6810), CLIPIQA+ (0.7852 vs. AddSR's 0.7438), MUSIQ (73.16 vs. AddSR's 71.39), and Q-Align (4.0206 vs. AddSR's 3.7625). On untrained NR metrics that test generalization, DP²O-SR (FLUX) leads with VQ-R1 4.32 (vs. OSEDiff's 4.09) and Q-Align 4.1492 (vs. AddSR's 3.7625), confirming that preference alignment transfers beyond the supervised reward. The method also wins on NIMA (5.2324 for SD2) and TOPIQ-IAA (5.1144 for SD2), both of which were not part of the training reward. The only metrics where baselines retain an advantage are PSNR and SSIM (e.g., CCSR achieves PSNR 26.21 vs. DP²O-SR's 22.49–23.49), reflecting the well-known perception–distortion tradeoff: richer synthesized details improve perceptual quality at the cost of pixel-level fidelity. On the in-distribution Syn-Test set, the same pattern holds—DP²O-SR dominates perceptual metrics while CCSR retains the PSNR lead (23.54 vs. 21.48). Overall, DP²O-SR converts moderate ControlNet backbones into top-performing Real-ISR models on perceptual quality without requiring any human annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diff-DPO (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024) from the text-to-image alignment setting to real-world image super-resolution, replacing the single best-vs-worst pair across different models with multiple preference pairs sampled from a single stochastic model's diverse outputs. It builds on the ControlNet paradigm (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) for conditioning diffusion/flow backbones on LR inputs, and on the DPO loss formulation (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) as the optimization backbone. The key conceptual departure is treating the inherent stochasticity of T2I models not as a limitation to suppress but as a source of free supervision, combined with a hybrid full-reference and no-reference IQA reward specifically designed for the fidelity-perception tradeoff in Real-ISR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, annotation-free recipe for boosting perceptual quality of diffusion-based SR models, with the capacity-dependent N/M finding providing actionable guidance for practitioners choosing between small and large backbones. The observation that a purely global IQA reward induces localized structural refinement (Sec. 5.5) hints at an implicit curriculum in preference-based training that remains unexplained. Open questions include whether online or iterative preference optimization could further close the gap to human judgment, and whether more interpretable or explainable reward models could replace the current black-box IQA ensemble. The fully offline pipeline and reliance on IQA metrics that only approximate subjective quality are acknowledged limitations that bound the method's ceiling.
