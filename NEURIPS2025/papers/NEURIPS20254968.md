## Problem

Existing single-image 3D generative models produce objects with inconsistent, non-canonical orientations because their large-scale training datasets (Objaverse, TRELLIS-500K) contain misaligned poses. This orientation misalignment breaks downstream tasks such as analysis-by-synthesis orientation estimation and intuitive object manipulation in AR/3D software, where users expect objects to appear in semantically meaningful upright and front-facing poses.

## Input / Output

For 3D generation, the method takes a single RGB image (including real-world internet photos and multi-object images) and outputs an orientation-aligned 3D mesh or 3D Gaussian Splatting model. For the multi-view diffusion path, the model internally generates six canonical views (front, front-left, front-right, left, right, back) from fixed camera poses before lifting to 3D via LGM. For the arrow-based manipulation application, the input is a 2D image with a user-drawn arrow (in AR) or a 3D arrow (in software), and the output is the generated object rotated so its forward axis aligns with the arrow direction while remaining grounded on the estimated plane.

## Method

The key insight is that fine-tuning pre-trained 3D generative models on a sufficiently diverse orientation-aligned dataset directly produces canonical outputs, eliminating the need for costly two-stage generate-then-align pipelines. The authors construct Objaverse-OA (14,832 models, 1,008 categories) via a hybrid pipeline: Gemini-2.0 VLM identifies front views from four rendered orthogonal views, followed by manual correction in Blender for stick-like objects, ambiguous-front objects, and geometrically thin items. On the 3D-VAE backbone (Trellis), only the sparse structure generator G_S is fine-tuned (30K steps, batch 64), since the pre-trained latent generator and decoder remain compatible with the aligned pose distribution. On the multi-view diffusion backbone (Wonder3D), LoRA serves as a lightweight domain adapter, the supervision is changed from input-view-dependent ground truth to six fixed canonical views, a pixel injector replaces the original input-view alignment mechanism, and the NeuS lifting module is swapped for LGM to improve efficiency. For zero-shot orientation estimation, the generated canonical 3D shape serves as a per-object template in FoundationPose's refinement module, with DINOv2 patch-feature L2 distance replacing the original pose selection to handle geometry/appearance mismatches.

## Results

On the 3D-VAE backbone (Trellis), Trellis-OA reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.0770 to 0.0407 on GSO and from 0.0951 to 0.0393 on Toys4k, while improving CLIP score from 82.88 to 88.41 (GSO) and 92.31 to 95.71 (Toys4k). On the multi-view diffusion backbone (Wonder3D), Wonder3D-OA reduces CD from 0.0894 to 0.0564 on GSO and from 0.0932 to 0.0548 on Toys4k, with CLIP improving from 76.37 to 80.30 (GSO) and 87.10 to 92.09 (Toys4k). For zero-shot orientation estimation on stick-like objects from ImageNet3D, the method achieves Acc@30 of 62.25° and absolute error of 34.20°, dramatically outperforming Orient Anything (ViT-L) at 9.8° and 78.19° respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Trellis-OA outperforms the unmodified Trellis baseline on all three metrics (CD, LPIPS, CLIP) across both GSO and Toys4k, with the largest gain in CD (nearly halved on Toys4k: 0.0393 vs. 0.0951). The PCA post-hoc alignment baseline actually degrades performance relative to unmodified Trellis on GSO (CD worsens from 0.0770 to 0.0798), confirming that geometric heuristics fail for diverse object shapes. The VLM-based post-hoc alignment (Gemini-2.0) achieves competitive CD on GSO (0.0421) but still trails Trellis-OA (0.0407) and lags significantly on Toys4k (0.0564 vs. 0.0393 CD; 95.19 vs. 95.71 CLIP). Orient Anything (ViT-L) as a post-hoc aligner is consistently worse than Trellis-OA on both datasets. For zero-shot orientation estimation, the method loses to Orient Anything (ViT-L) on Toys4k (Acc@30: 52.87 vs. 63.18) but wins decisively on stick-like objects from ImageNet3D (Acc@30: 62.25 vs. 9.8; Abs error: 34.20° vs. 78.19°), where all baselines fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-to-3D generation pipeline (Trellis's 3D-VAE framework and Wonder3D's multi-view diffusion framework) by introducing an orientation-alignment objective directly into the generative process, rather than treating it as a post-hoc correction step. It departs from the two-stage paradigm (generate-then-align via PCA, VLM, or Orient Anything) by fine-tuning only the sparse structure generator in Trellis or applying LoRA with a pixel injector in Wonder3D, leveraging a newly curated orientation-aligned dataset (Objaverse-OA). For the downstream orientation estimation application, it repurposes FoundationPose's template-based refinement module but replaces its pose selection with a DINOv2 feature similarity metric, and uses LGM instead of NeuS for 3D lifting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Orientation misalignment in generated 3D objects is a practical bottleneck for AR/VR content creation, robotic simulation, and analysis-by-synthesis pose estimation, yet has been largely overlooked because large-scale 3D training datasets (Objaverse, TRELLIS-500K) lack canonical orientations. The Objaverse-OA dataset (14,832 objects, 1,008 categories) is the largest orientation-aligned 3D collection by category coverage and is made publicly available, providing a resource the field has lacked. Open questions include whether a dedicated orientation-estimation model trained on generated templates could further improve zero-shot estimation, how well the approach handles severe occlusion (the authors defer to Amodal3R), and whether the yaw-only alignment assumption generalizes to objects with significant roll/pitch ambiguity.
