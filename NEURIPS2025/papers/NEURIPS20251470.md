## Problem

Existing prompt-based federated continual learning (FCL) methods suffer from insufficient class-wise knowledge coherence during prompt communication: intra-class distribution gaps across non-IID clients degrade learned semantics, and inter-prompt class-wise relevance is ignored during server-side aggregation, causing cross-class knowledge confusion. These issues exacerbate both temporal forgetting (across sequential tasks) and spatial forgetting (across heterogeneous clients), leading to significant performance degradation in long-term federated learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images as input and produces class labels via a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone. It operates in an exemplar-free federated setting with 5 clients learning sequential tasks asynchronously, where each client holds a non-IID subset of classes. Only lightweight prompts (discriminativity prompts of length 10 and compensation prompts of length 3, both 768-dim) are learned and communicated; no raw data or stored samples are exchanged, and no additional parameters are introduced at inference time.

## Method

C² Prompt introduces two complementary mechanisms on top of a CODAPrompt-based architecture with a frozen ViT-B/16. First, Local Class Distribution Compensation (LCDC) estimates a global Gaussian distribution per class by aggregating local client statistics (mean, variance, sample proportions) on the server, then trains class-specific compensation prompts to align local features with the global distribution via a Mahalanobis-distance loss, reducing intra-class semantic gaps across clients. Second, Class-aware Prompt Aggregation (CPA) records per-prompt class affinity histograms during local discriminativity training, computes an inter-prompt correlation matrix on the server from these histograms, and uses softmax-weighted matrix multiplication to selectively fuse class-relevant knowledge across client prompts while suppressing cross-class conflicts. The two types of prompts (compensation and discriminativity) are concatenated as input to the ViT during training, with the compensation prompts frozen after initial calibration and used with 50% dropout to balance local and global information.

## Results

On the primary FCL benchmarks, C²Prompt achieves 87.20% Avg accuracy on ImageNet-R and 78.88% on DomainNet, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art Powder (ICML 2024) by 2.51% and 2.90% respectively. It also attains the best Forward Transfer (7.63 on ImageNet-R, 3.87 on DomainNet) and Combined Transfer (8.52, 4.05) among all compared methods, while maintaining a negative Forgetting Measure (−0.36, −0.02), indicating no net forgetting. Under the FCIL setting with non-IID Dirichlet splits, C²Prompt reaches 75.23% FAA on ImageNet-R (β=0.5) and 89.93% on CIFAR-100 (β=0.5), surpassing LoRM (ICLR 2025) by 2.75% and 2.98% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²Prompt decisively outperforms the closest prompt-based FCL baseline Powder (ICML 2024) across all six metrics on both ImageNet-R and DomainNet, with the largest gains in Forward Transfer (+3.15% on ImageNet-R, +2.59% on DomainNet) and Combined Transfer (+2.48%, +2.65%), while also improving Avg by 2.51% and 2.90%. Against LoRA-based FCL methods (PILoRA, LoRM) and adapter-based Fed-MOS, C²Prompt leads by wide margins (e.g., 29.2% Avg on ImageNet-R over PILoRA), confirming that prompt-based approaches with class-aware aggregation substantially outperform low-rank adaptation in the FCL setting. Among fully-tuning baselines (FedWEIT, CFeD, GLFC, FedSpace), C²Prompt surpasses the best (GLFC) by 14.24% Avg on ImageNet-R and 9.13% on DomainNet. On the FCIL benchmark (disjoint class splits, Dirichlet non-IID), C²Prompt beats LoRM by 2.75%/5.91%/2.29% FAA at β=0.5/0.1/0.05 on ImageNet-R and by 2.98%/5.91%/0.49% on CIFAR-100, with the advantage growing as data heterogeneity increases. The only metric where C²Prompt trails Powder is Backward Transfer (1.12 vs. 1.95 on ImageNet-R), which the authors attribute to LCDC and CPA already extracting most available knowledge at each stage, leaving less room for later tasks to improve earlier ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CODAPrompt (CVPR 2023) as its architectural backbone for prompt-based continual learning and on Powder (ICML 2024) as the most recent prompt-based FCL method, adopting its experimental protocol and knowledge distillation loss. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'class-wise knowledge coherence'—encompassing both intra-class distribution gaps across clients and inter-prompt class relevance—as the specific bottleneck in existing prompt-based FCL aggregation. Rather than treating prompt communication as a uniform averaging problem, the paper introduces distribution-level compensation (LCDC) and class-affinity-weighted aggregation (CPA), positioning itself as a refinement of the prompt-communication paradigm rather than a new FCL formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The class-aware perspective on prompt aggregation offers a principled mechanism for reducing knowledge conflicts in federated continual learning, and the exemplar-free design preserves data privacy—a practical concern for real-world deployment. However, the method requires an initial round of class distribution communication and assumes Gaussian class distributions, which may not hold for highly multimodal or complex feature spaces. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-ViT backbones, scales to hundreds of clients, and whether the Gaussian approximation remains valid under extreme non-IID conditions. The moderate but consistent gains (2–3% over Powder) suggest the identified coherence issue is real but not the sole source of forgetting in FCL.
