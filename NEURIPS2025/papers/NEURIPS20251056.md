## Problem

Autoregressive image generation models rely on uniform sampling strategies (fixed top-K or top-p) that ignore the highly non-uniform spatial information distribution inherent to images, unlike text. This mismatch creates a persistent tradeoff: high randomness enriches visual content but destabilizes structure, while low randomness stabilizes structure but produces flat, oversmoothed details. The paper identifies that the entropy of predicted token logits naturally reflects local information density and can be exploited to resolve this tradeoff without any model modification.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free, inference-time intervention applied to pretrained autoregressive image generation models (LlamaGen, Lumina-mGPT, Meissonic, STAR). It consumes a text prompt and the per-token logit distributions produced by the model's transformer at each generation step, and outputs a generated RGB image (typically 256×256 or 512×512, quantized via VQ-VAE into discrete token sequences). No architectural changes or retraining are required; the method simply rescales logits before the final softmax at each decoding step, and is compatible with existing sampling parameters such as CFG and top-K.

## Method

The core mechanism computes the Shannon entropy ε of each predicted token distribution and maps it to a dynamic temperature via T = T₀·exp(−ε/α) + θ, which rescales logits before softmax: low-entropy (simple, homogeneous) regions receive higher temperatures to inject randomness and avoid repetitive tokens, while high-entropy (complex, foreground) regions receive lower temperatures to preserve structural coherence and text-image alignment. For mask-prediction models, the entropy-aware temperature is further integrated into the confidence-based token acceptance rule (Eq. 4–5), and for scale-wise models an additional scale-dependent decay (Eq. 6) reduces randomness at finer scales. The entropy-aware principle is also extended to speculative decoding by scaling the acceptance threshold with ε/e and modulating the stochastic noise term with (1 − λ·ε), yielding a ~15% reduction in inference steps with near-lossless quality. The entire approach is parameterized by a small set of hyperparameters (T₀, α, θ, λ, e) tuned per model, requiring no gradient updates or architectural changes.

## Results

On COCO 2017, the entropy-aware dynamic temperature sampling improves FID by 1.58 points on LlamaGen (21.94→20.36), 1.71 on Lumina-mGPT (29.15→27.44), 5.18 on Meissonic (53.61→48.43), and 2.68 on STAR (35.05→32.37). DPG-bench gains are most pronounced: +5.12 on LlamaGen (43.51→48.63) and +3.25 on Meissonic (63.83→67.08). For inference acceleration, the entropy-aware speculative decoding reduces latency to 85% of SJD (22.04s vs. 26.52s on LlamaGen; 72.35s vs. 84.97s on Lumina-mGPT) while maintaining or slightly improving FID and CLIP-Score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the same AR models without the proposed sampling (i.e., their original top-K/CFG settings), the entropy-aware method consistently wins: LlamaGen improves FID by 1.58 and DPG by 5.12; Meissonic improves FID by 5.18 and DPG by 3.25; STAR improves FID by 2.68; Lumina-mGPT (already well-trained) shows a smaller FID gain of 1.71 but a CLIP-Score gain of 0.21. Compared to diffusion reference baselines, LlamaGen+Ours achieves the best FID (20.36) among all listed models, beating SDv2.1 (22.87) and SDXL (23.20), though it trails SDv3-medium (29.82) on DPG. For acceleration, the entropy-aware speculative decoding outperforms SJD [26] on both latency (22.04s vs. 26.52s on LlamaGen; 72.35s vs. 84.97s on Lumina-mGPT) and average steps (535.5 vs. 626.9; 1594.5 vs. 1854.5) while matching or slightly exceeding SJD's FID and CLIP-Score, confirming near-lossless quality at ~85% inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the autoregressive image generation paradigm (LlamaGen, Lumina-mGPT, Meissonic, STAR) and the broader literature on sampling strategies for discrete token prediction (top-k, top-p, EDT for text). It departs from prior work by identifying that the non-uniform spatial information density in images—unlike the more uniform token distributions in text—makes fixed sampling parameters suboptimal, and by repurposing entropy of predicted logits as a spatially-varying signal to modulate sampling randomness. The acceleration component extends speculative Jacobi decoding (SJD) with an entropy-aware acceptance rule, whereas prior acceleration methods use uniform thresholds. Unlike EDT [20] (text) or PURE [27] (super-resolution), this is the first to apply entropy-guided temperature specifically to AR image generation across next-token, mask-based, and scale-wise paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is training-free, adds no inference overhead for the sampling component, and generalizes across multiple AR generation paradigms, making it a practical drop-in improvement for existing pipelines. However, gains are model-dependent—well-trained models like Lumina-mGPT see only marginal improvements, and the authors acknowledge that entropy is an imperfect proxy for information density (e.g., faces can exhibit low entropy despite rich semantics). Open questions include whether entropy-aware scheduling can be integrated into training to reduce hyperparameter sensitivity, whether combining entropy with token-similarity or spatial-adjacency signals yields better calibration, and whether the approach transfers to video or unified multimodal generation.
