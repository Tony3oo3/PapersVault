## Problem

Gradient boosted decision trees (GBDTs) are the dominant model for tabular data in high-stakes domains like finance and criminal justice, yet existing methods for learning models with guaranteed recourse actions cannot be applied to them: neural-network-based approaches require differentiable losses, and prior tree-based methods are limited to classification trees rather than the regression trees used as GBDT base learners. This paper closes that gap by designing a gradient boosting algorithm that natively incorporates a recourse loss while preserving the standard O(N·D) per-round complexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular instances with per-feature action bounds (encoding immutable or one-directional features), a cost budget β defining the maximum allowable action cost, and a trade-off hyperparameter γ balancing accuracy against recourse guarantee. It outputs a tree ensemble model whose prediction score is binary (via sign of the score) and which, by construction, admits a valid recourse action of cost ≤ β for a high fraction of misclassified instances. The post-processing stage additionally takes a pre-trained ensemble and a calibration set, producing refined leaf weights that satisfy an explicit recourse-loss constraint with a PAC-style generalization bound.

## Method

RABIT augments standard second-order gradient boosting with a recourse loss l_β that penalizes the absence of a low-cost action flipping the prediction to the desired class. The key technical obstacle is that l_β involves a min over actions, making it non-differentiable; the authors resolve this by deriving a differentiable upper bound via a LogSumExp relaxation, which admits a second-order Taylor expansion and yields closed-form leaf-weight solutions (equations 7–8) with an additional cross-term H̃_B coupling left and right weights. The resulting per-split optimization retains O(N·D) complexity by amortizing gradient/Hessian updates over sorted features, exactly as in XGBoost. A complementary post-processing step (leaf refinement) re-optimizes all leaf weights jointly under a recourse-loss constraint, formulated as a linear-model problem on the concatenated original and action-perturbed samples, and is backed by a PAC-style bound (Proposition 3) guaranteeing recourse existence for unseen instances with high probability.

## Results

On four real-world tabular datasets (FICO, COMPAS, Adult, Bail), RABIT achieves comparable predictive accuracy to standard GBDTs while significantly improving the recourse ratio (fraction of test instances with guaranteed valid actions at cost budget β=0.2). In terms of action quality, RABIT reduces average action cost (MPS) by 40–55% compared to Vanilla (e.g., 0.165 vs. 0.358 on FICO, 0.225 vs. 0.449 on Bail) and reduces sparsity (ℓ₀-norm) by 35–50% (e.g., 1.078 vs. 2.155 on FICO). Training time is statistically indistinguishable from standard gradient boosting across all datasets. With post-processing (leaf refinement), RABIT further improves recourse ratio from ~40% to ~80% on Adult while maintaining ~85% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RABIT against two baselines: Vanilla (standard unconstrained GBDT) and OAF (learning with only actionable features, following Kanamori et al. 2024). RABIT dominates both baselines on recourse ratio across all four datasets while maintaining comparable accuracy; OAF achieves a slightly higher recourse ratio than Vanilla but at the cost of significant accuracy degradation due to discarding non-actionable features. On action quality, RABIT consistently achieves the lowest cost and sparsity: on FICO, RABIT's average cost (0.165) is 54% lower than Vanilla (0.358) and 48% lower than OAF (0.315), while its sparsity (1.078) is roughly half that of both baselines. Plausibility scores (isolation forest outlier score) remain comparable across all methods, confirming that RABIT's actions are not unrealistic. Training time shows no significant difference among the three methods, confirming that the recourse-aware objective does not add computational overhead. With the leaf refinement post-processing applied, RABIT further extends its advantage, achieving up to ~80% recourse ratio on Adult (vs. ~40% for Vanilla) at ~85% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning models with guaranteed recourse actions—initiated by Ross et al. (NeurIPS 2021) for deep neural networks and Kanamori et al. (ICML 2024) for classification trees—to gradient boosted decision trees, the dominant model family for tabular data. The key technical contribution is adapting the recourse loss to the XGBoost-style second-order Taylor expansion framework by deriving a differentiable upper bound via a LogSumExp relaxation, enabling closed-form leaf weight solutions. The post-processing step (leaf refinement) adapts the global refinement concept from Ren et al. (CVPR 2015) to the recourse setting, adding a PAC-style generalization guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GBDTs are the de facto standard for tabular prediction in high-stakes domains (finance, criminal justice) where algorithmic recourse is most needed, yet no prior method could train them with recourse guarantees—this paper closes that gap with no computational overhead. The finding that a very small recourse-loss weight (γ=0.002) suffices for substantial recourse improvements suggests the bound in Proposition 1 is conservative, pointing to room for tighter formulations. Open questions include extending to general (non-ℓ∞) cost functions, characterizing when post-processing alone suffices versus when the learning-time recourse loss is necessary, and integrating modern GBDT techniques (histogram-based learning, stochastic sampling) into the RABIT framework.
