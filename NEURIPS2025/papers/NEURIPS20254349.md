## Problem

Existing ex-post DP mechanisms (Wu et al. 2019, Whitehouse et al. 2022) that achieve a desired utility with minimal privacy cost are restricted to simple Laplace or Gaussian noise mechanisms, and cannot handle complex mechanisms like DP-SGD. Furthermore, hyperparameter tuning for these mechanisms—including selecting the optimal privacy budget—has not been shown to be possible without additional privacy cost in the ex-post setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a dataset D, a collection of d ex-ante DP mechanisms M_1,...,M_d each with its own privacy budget ε_i, and a tunable distribution parameter ε' (controlling the drop probability). It outputs the lexicographically maximum (o, i) pair from the surviving candidates, or ⊥ if no mechanism survives, with the ex-post guarantee that the privacy cost is only 2ε_i + ε' (pure-DP) or (2+ℓ_i)ε_i + (1+ℓ_i)ε' + O(log d/α) (RDP) when the output originates from M_i. Mechanisms can be repeated T_i times in the input to control the expected number of invocations while preserving the same per-output privacy bound.

## Method

The core technique is random dropping with correlated randomness: a single random variable k is sampled once (from Geom_{e^{-ε'}} for pure-DP or Exp_{ε'} for RDP), and each mechanism M_i is included in the candidate set with probability e^{-ε_i·k}. The best output is then selected from the surviving candidates. The privacy proof couples k with k+1 (or k+1+ℓ_i for RDP) across neighboring datasets, showing that the probability ratio for any output (o,i) is bounded by e^{2ε_i+ε'}, regardless of which other mechanisms were evaluated—inspired by the Sparse Vector Technique's threshold coupling. For the RDP extension, the weaker RDP assumption breaks the direct inequality used in the pure-DP proof, so the coupling is shifted by a mechanism-specific offset ℓ_i and a reverse Hölder inequality plus a weighted AM-GM bound (Lemma 16) control the residual ratio. A privacy filter (Algorithm 2) then composes a sequence of ex-post RDP mechanisms under a total ex-ante RDP budget by subtracting realized ex-post costs after each query.

## Results

On the ML experiments (Table 3), Algorithm 1 achieves the lowest ex-post (ε, 10⁻⁶)-DP guarantee across all three datasets: 0.28 on Twitter (vs. 0.77 Brownian Motion, 0.55 Doubling), 0.32 on MNIST (vs. 0.62, 0.38), and 0.23 on Gisette (vs. 0.33, 0.54). On the pure-DP analytical task (Table 1), Algorithm 1 with Laplace noise produces the most estimates before budget exhaustion (e.g., 102.1 on S128000 vs. 76.09 for Doubling and 27.68 for Noise Reduction) while maintaining comparable precision (~91%). On the approximate-DP analytical task (Table 2), Algorithm 1 with Gaussian noise underperforms the Brownian Motion mechanism (e.g., 52.33 vs. 269.7 produced answers on S128000), which is expected since Brownian Motion is specifically tailored for that setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Doubling Mechanism and Noise Reduction Mechanism from Wu et al. [2019], Algorithm 1 consistently wins: on the pure-DP analytical task it produces 34% more estimates on S128000 (102.1 vs. 76.09) and 17% more on Reddit (327.5 vs. 279.7) at similar precision, and on all three ML datasets it achieves a substantially lower privacy budget (e.g., 0.28 vs. 0.55 on Twitter, 0.23 vs. 0.54 on Gisette). Against the Brownian Motion mechanism from Whitehouse et al. [2022], Algorithm 1 wins decisively on ML tasks (0.28 vs. 0.77 on Twitter, 0.32 vs. 0.62 on MNIST, 0.23 vs. 0.33 on Gisette) but loses on the approximate-DP analytical task (52.33 vs. 269.7 produced answers on S128000), which the authors attribute to Brownian Motion being specifically designed for Gaussian-noise analytics workloads. The paper's key advantage is generality: Algorithm 1 works with arbitrary mechanisms (including DP-SGD) while both baselines are restricted to Laplace or Gaussian noise, and the ML experiments demonstrate this by using DP-SGD with full hyperparameter search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ex-post DP framework introduced by Wu et al. [2019] and the ex-ante DP hyperparameter tuning results of Liu and Talwar [2019] and Papernot and Steinke [2022] to a significantly more general setting: arbitrary mechanisms (including DP-SGD) rather than just Laplace or Gaussian noise mechanisms. The key technical innovation is a 'random dropping' technique with correlated randomness sampled from a Geometric (or Exponential, for RDP) distribution, whose proof structure is directly inspired by the Sparse Vector Technique of Dwork et al. [2009]. Unlike prior ex-post DP work limited to simple noise-addition mechanisms, this approach supports any sequence of private estimators with potentially different privacy budgets, and the authors show that the privacy cost is attributed only to the mechanism whose output is actually released, not to higher-budget mechanisms that were evaluated en route.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first hyperparameter tuning algorithm with ex-post DP guarantees, enabling a 'utility-first' privacy paradigm where practitioners specify a desired accuracy threshold and the algorithm finds the minimal privacy cost to achieve it, without the prohibitive composition blow-up of naive approaches. The counterintuitive result that higher-budget mechanisms evaluated during selection do not incur additional privacy cost is, to the authors' knowledge, unprecedented in DP. Open questions include whether the ℓ_i parameters in the RDP guarantee (Theorem 10) can be eliminated, and whether a zCDP version can be proven to improve analytics workloads where the Gaussian mechanism is standard. The empirical results are mixed: the algorithm outperforms prior methods in pure-DP and ML settings but underperforms the purpose-built Brownian Motion mechanism in approximate-DP analytics tasks.
