## Problem

Vision-Language Models rely on expensive, human-annotated image-question-answer triplets for visual instruction tuning, and existing synthetic-data approaches depend on external supervision from stronger VLMs (e.g., GPT-4V) or environmental feedback. This paper asks whether a VLM can refine itself autonomously using only unlabeled images and its own outputs, without any external annotations or stronger teacher models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250862/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a set of unlabeled RGB images (2.8M from LAION in experiments) with no accompanying text, and produces a self-refined VLM that answers visual questions. Internally, the model generates image-question-answer (IQA) triplets for each image, then filters them via a consistency score before using them for instruction tuning. The process is iterative: the refined model and a new batch of unlabeled images serve as inputs to the next refinement round, so the output of one cycle becomes the starting point of the next.

## Method

The core mechanism is a three-stage self-refinement loop grounded in the Triangular Consistency principle: within an image-question-answer triangle, any masked element should be consistently reconstructable by the same model. Stage 1 fine-tunes the VLM with a multi-task objective (I→QA, IQ→A, IA→Q) to unlock its instruction-generation ability. Stage 2 generates IQA triplets from unlabeled images, then filters them by masking the question or answer, re-predicting the masked element, and computing a geometric-mean similarity score between original and re-predicted components (using Sentence Transformer, BERTScore, IoU, or exact match depending on data type); only the top 20% by consistency score are retained. Stage 3 merges the filtered synthetic data with the original seed dataset and performs standard visual instruction tuning. A causal-theoretic analysis (using ANM and independence-of-mechanism assumptions) provides a principled justification for why unlabeled images carry exploitable signal for refining the conditional P(X|Y) that the VLM models.

## Results

On the 7B scale, SRF-LLaVA-1.5 achieves consistent improvements over LLaVA-1.5 across all 8 benchmarks, with the largest gains on LLaVA-Bench In-the-Wild (+3.5, 66.9 vs 63.4), MM-Vet (+2.6, 33.1 vs 30.5), and MME-Cognition (+15.7, 331.8 vs 316.1). On the 13B scale, SRF-LLaVA-1.5 13B surpasses LLaVA-1.5 13B on all 8 benchmarks, with particularly large improvements on MME-Cognition (+38.8, 334.2 vs 295.4) and LLaVA-Wild (+2.8, 73.5 vs 70.7). The framework also generalizes to smaller models: SRF-MobileVLM (1.4B) improves over MobileVLM on all 6 benchmarks (e.g., GQA 58.1 vs 56.1, MMB 56.1 vs 53.2), and SRF-Qwen2.5-VL (3B) gains on MMMU (55.0 vs 53.1), MathVista (64.7 vs 62.3), and MathVision (23.3 vs 21.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct baseline LLaVA-1.5 7B [2], SRF-LLaVA-1.5 7B wins on all 8 benchmarks, with the most substantial gains on LLaVA-Wild (+3.5), MM-Vet (+2.6), and MME-Cognition (+15.7). Compared to the strong Recap-LLaVA-1.5 baseline (which uses 1M unfiltered self-generated captions), SRF-LLaVA-1.5 7B wins on 7 of 8 benchmarks, losing only on SQA-I (67.72 vs 69.01), demonstrating that the Triangular Consistency filtering with only 200K selected samples outperforms 1M unfiltered captions. Against LLaVA 1.0 [1], SRF-LLaVA-1.5 7B substantially outperforms on every reported benchmark (e.g., MMB 66.3 vs 34.1, MMB-CN 59.2 vs 14.1). Against InstructBLIP [5] and Qwen-VL-Chat [7], SRF-LLaVA-1.5 7B achieves higher scores on all overlapping benchmarks despite using far less training data (665K+200K vs 1.2M or 50M). On the MobileVLM 1.4B baseline [38], SRF-MobileVLM improves on all 6 benchmarks (e.g., GQA +2.0, MMB +2.9). On Qwen2.5-VL 3B [39], SRF-Qwen2.5-VL gains on 5 of 6 benchmarks (MMMU +1.9, MathVista +2.4, MathVision +2.1) but slightly underperforms on MMB (80.8 vs 81.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual instruction tuning paradigm established by LLaVA/LLaVA-1.5 by replacing external supervision (human annotations or teacher VLMs like GPT-4V) with a self-contained data generation and filtering loop. It departs from prior synthetic-data approaches (ShareGPT4V, LLaVA-Next, MLLM-DataEngine) that rely on a stronger external model for annotation, and from LLM self-refinement methods (Self-Refine, Self-Instruct) that use in-context learning or text-only rules, by introducing the Triangular Consistency principle as a modality-aware, self-referential quality filter. The causal-theoretical analysis (ANM + deconvolution) provides a novel formal justification for why unlabeled images carry exploitable signal for refining P(X|Y), a framing not previously applied to VLM self-improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that VLMs can improve themselves from unlabeled images alone, reducing dependence on costly human annotation or proprietary teacher models—a practical concern as the gap between open and closed VLMs narrows. However, the gains are deliberately modest (1–3% on most benchmarks), and the authors acknowledge diminishing returns across iterations and the risk of amplifying existing biases and hallucinations. Open questions include whether the Triangular Consistency filter can be made more discriminative, whether the approach transfers to video or multi-turn dialogue settings, and how far self-refinement can push a model before it saturates the signal available in unpaired images.
