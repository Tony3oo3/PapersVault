## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) and its variants dominate text-to-3D generation but rely on noisy score estimates from 2D diffusion models that are not guaranteed to be optimal for 3D optimization, and they require high Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) values that introduce over-saturation and over-smoothing artifacts. Variants designed for lower CFG (e.g., VSD, SDI) show limited success with 3D Gaussian Splatting representations. The paper asks whether a more principled, direct transport trajectory between the current rendering distribution and the text-aligned target distribution can be explicitly constructed and learned, rather than implicitly relying on the SGM's score function.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt and a randomly sampled camera parameter, the method renders the current 3DGS model to produce a 2D image, then dynamically estimates a target view via one-step denoising with a frozen pre-trained diffusion model conditioned on the prompt. The optimization iteratively updates the 3DGS parameters θ over approximately 14 minutes (peak VRAM ~18.7 GB), with the bridge time t annealed from 0.5 down to 0.02 across training. Unlike SDS which starts from pure Gaussian noise, the 'noisy' endpoint of the bridge is the actual current rendering, making the trajectory a direct interpolation between two data-space distributions rather than data-to-noise.

## Method

The paper first proves that SDS is a special case of the Schrödinger Bridge framework: when one endpoint is Gaussian noise, the Schrödinger factor Ψ≈1 and Nelson's duality reduces the bridge score to the standard SGM score, which is exactly what SDS uses. Building on this, TraCe constructs an explicit diffusion bridge between the current rendering x_rndr (bridge time t=1) and a one-step-denoised text-conditioned target x_0^pred (bridge time t=0), sampling intermediate states x_t from the analytically known Gaussian posterior q(x_t | x_0^pred, x_rndr) with mean γ_t·x_0^pred + (1−γ_t)·x_rndr. A LoRA-adapted copy of the diffusion model is then trained to predict the noise along this specific bridge trajectory, and the 3DGS gradient is computed as the difference between the LoRA model's prediction and the bridge noise direction (x_t − x_rndr)/σ_t, backpropagated through the rendering. A scheduled t-sampling strategy progressively anneals t from 0.5 to 0.02, shifting the bridge interpolation from broad states toward the target, which the ablations confirm is essential for avoiding color artifacts and achieving high-fidelity details.

## Results

On 83 prompts from the DreamFusion Gallery (120 views each), TraCe achieves the highest CLIP Score across all ViT backbones (69.26% ViT-L/14, 27.93% ViT-B/16, 27.70% ViT-B/32), the best GPTEval3D overall score of 1028.03, and the most favorable ImageReward of −0.2855. Generation takes 14 minutes on average with 18,741 MiB peak VRAM, offering a competitive balance of quality and efficiency relative to baselines that range from 10–20 minutes and 10,151–26,473 MiB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TraCe outperforms all five baselines on every quality metric. Against the closest competitor ISM [23], TraCe gains +0.25% on CLIP ViT-L/14, +0.38 on GPTEval3D, and improves ImageReward by 0.10, while also being 6 minutes faster (14 vs. 20 min). Compared to VSD [45], TraCe leads by +1.99% on CLIP ViT-L/14 and +20.5 on GPTEval3D, with substantially lower VRAM (18,741 vs. 26,473 MiB) and shorter runtime (14 vs. 17 min). Against SDS [47], the gains are more modest (+0.65% CLIP ViT-L/14, +9.94 GPTEval3D, +0.15 ImageReward) but consistent across all metrics, at the cost of 4 additional minutes. CSD [48] and SDI [29] trail significantly: CSD lags by +1.23% CLIP and +45 GPTEval3D, while SDI is the weakest baseline overall (63.04% CLIP ViT-L/14, 971.98 GPTEval3D). The paper further notes that TraCe achieves these results at a moderate CFG of 20, whereas standard SDS requires CFG=100 to reach comparable text alignment, and VSD/SDI degrade noticeably at low CFG values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the SDS lineage for text-to-3D generation (DreamFusion, VSD/ProlificDreamer, CSD, SDI, ISM) but departs conceptually by establishing that SDS is a degenerate special case of the Schrödinger Bridge framework, where one endpoint is Gaussian noise. Building on the tractable I2SB formulation [26], TraCe replaces the implicit noise-to-data trajectory with an explicit bridge between the current 3D rendering and a one-step-denoised text-conditioned target, then trains a LoRA adapter to learn the bridge's score dynamics. This contrasts with prior SDS variants that modify the gradient direction or weighting while still relying on the pre-trained model's score as the sole guidance signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical reframing gives SDS a principled transport interpretation and motivates a more direct optimization path that achieves competitive quality at CFG≈20 rather than the CFG≈100 typically required by standard SDS, reducing over-saturation and over-smoothing artifacts. However, the method still depends on a one-step denoising estimate for the target endpoint (a simplification that may limit fidelity for complex prompts), requires iterative LoRA training alongside 3D optimization, and is evaluated only on 3DGS representations. Open questions include whether the explicit bridge formulation generalizes to other 3D representations (NeRF, mesh), whether the LoRA training converges reliably across diverse prompts, and whether the bridge can be made fully one-shot to eliminate the iterative optimization loop.
