## Problem

Prior sharp analysis of Behavior Cloning (Foster et al., 2024) showed that when annotation cost is measured per trajectory, BC is minimax optimal and interactive methods like DAgger offer no general improvement. This paper challenges that conclusion by arguing the per-trajectory cost model is unrealistic for interactive annotation, and asks whether measuring cost per state reveals a provable advantage for interactive and hybrid imitation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251590/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The learner receives (i) N_off offline expert trajectories of length H (each (state, action) pair at unit cost) and (ii) access to a state-wise annotation oracle that, given a single state s and step h, returns the expert's action at cost C ≥ 1 per query. The MDP environment is accessible for rollouts at no cost. The output is a stationary policy (a first-step uniform mixture of historical policies from a finite class B) that maps states to actions, with suboptimality J(π^E) − J(π̂) as the performance metric.

## Method

STAGGER is a state-wise variant of DAgger that, at each iteration, rolls out the current policy, samples a single state from the rollout distribution d^{π^n}, queries the state-wise oracle for the expert action, and updates an exponential-weights online learner with log loss; the final policy is a first-step uniform mixture of all iterates. The key analytical insight is that the state-wise estimation error—evaluated on a single sampled state rather than all H states per trajectory—allows each annotation to serve as immediate online feedback, yielding a suboptimality bound of O(μH log B / N_int) that beats BC's O(RH² log B / (C·N_int)) whenever C ≪ R/μ. WARM-STAGGER extends this by restricting the policy class to those consistent with all offline demonstrations before running the same online procedure, achieving a guarantee no worse than min(BC, STAGGER) and, on a constructed MDP, strictly outperforming both pure baselines by leveraging offline coverage of hard-to-reach states while using a few interactive queries to resolve cold-start gaps.

## Results

Theoretically, STAGGER achieves suboptimality O(μH log B / N_int) using N_int state-wise expert annotations, which is strictly better than BC's O(R log B / N_off) trajectory-wise guarantee whenever the cost ratio C ≪ R/μ. WARM-STAGGER achieves O(min(R log(B̃/δ)/N_off, μH log(B_bc/δ)/N_int)), matching the best of either data source alone. On MuJoCo (Hopper, Ant, HalfCheetah, Walker2D with H=1000), STAGGER matches or exceeds BC with 50% fewer state-wise annotations (C=1), and WARM-STAGGER with C=2 outperforms both pure-BC and pure-STAGGER baselines across all four tasks when initialized with 1/4 to 1/2 of the budget as offline data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is log-loss Behavior Cloning (BC) from Foster et al. [17], which the paper shows is minimax optimal under trajectory-wise cost measurement but not under state-wise measurement. STAGGER provably beats BC in cost-efficiency when C ≪ R/μ, and empirically on MuJoCo it reaches near-expert return with significantly fewer state-wise annotations—gains are most pronounced on harder tasks (HalfCheetah, Walker2D) and less so on easier ones (Ant, Hopper). WARM-STAGGER, the hybrid method, matches STAGGER in sample efficiency at C=1 and clearly outperforms both BC and STAGGER at C=2 on all four MuJoCo tasks, confirming that combining offline trajectories with a small number of interactive state-wise queries reduces total annotation cost. On the synthetic MDP (Theorem 8), WARM-STAGGER achieves exact expert performance with O(S+C) total cost, versus Ω(HS) for BC and Ω(HSC) for STAGGER, demonstrating a strict sample-complexity separation that neither pure source can match.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DAgger's reduction of interactive imitation learning to no-regret online learning (Ross et al., 2011) and on Foster et al.'s (2024) sharp analysis showing Behavior Cloning is minimax optimal when annotation cost is measured per trajectory. The key departure is a change in the cost metric: by measuring annotation cost per state rather than per trajectory, the authors show that interactive methods like their STAGGER algorithm provably outperform BC under low-recovery-cost settings, reversing the conclusion of [17]. The paper also initiates the hybrid imitation learning formulation, combining offline trajectory-wise demonstrations with interactive state-wise annotations, which had not been studied with provable guarantees in this cost-asymmetric setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual reframing—showing that the answer to 'can interaction help?' depends on the cost metric—provides a clearer theoretical foundation for when to invest in interactive expert feedback versus relying on offline data. The WARM-STAGGER algorithm and its MDP example demonstrating strict sample complexity savings over both pure offline and pure interactive baselines offer a principled recipe for practical hybrid IL systems (e.g., autonomous driving with pre-collected demonstrations plus on-the-fly corrections). Key limitations include the deterministic-realizable-expert assumption, discrete action spaces, and the absence of environment interaction cost; open questions include state-wise lower bounds for general MDPs, continuous-action settings, and whether the log B̃ dependence in the hybrid guarantee can be sharpened to log B.
