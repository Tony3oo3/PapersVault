## Problem

Large reasoning models (LRMs) such as DeepSeek-R1 and QwQ demonstrate exceptional complex problem-solving, yet the internal dynamics of their reasoning processes remain largely unexplored. A natural but unanswered question is whether specific intermediate reasoning steps disproportionately influence the final answer, analogous to 'critical tokens' identified in LLM safety research. This paper investigates this question from an information-theoretic perspective, asking whether and how mutual information between intermediate representations and the correct answer evolves during LRM reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and a golden answer (ground-truth chain-of-thought plus solution) as inputs, extracting last-layer hidden representations at each autoregressive generation step. MI between each step's representation and the golden answer's representation is estimated via HSIC with Gaussian kernels (bandwidth selected by grid search over [50, 400]), and peaks are identified using an IQR-based threshold (Q3 + 1.5·IQR). The corresponding tokens are decoded via greedy decoding through the model's output projection head, yielding a set of 'thinking tokens' that drive the two proposed inference-time interventions.

## Method

The paper tracks mutual information between each step's hidden representation and the golden answer's representation throughout LRM reasoning, discovering that MI exhibits sparse, non-uniform spikes ('MI peaks') at specific steps that predominantly correspond to reflective or transitional tokens ('Hmm,' 'Wait,' 'Therefore'), termed 'thinking tokens.' Two theorems—a modified Fano's inequality and a Bayesian decision-theoretic bound—show that higher cumulative MI yields tighter upper and lower bounds on prediction error, theoretically linking MI peaks to reasoning accuracy. Two training-free methods exploit these findings: Representation Recycling (RR) re-feeds MI-peak representations through the same transformer layer for deeper processing, and Thinking Token based Test-time Scaling (TTTS) appends thinking tokens to prompt continued reasoning when additional token budget is available. Both methods consistently improve reasoning performance across GSM8K, MATH500, and AIME24 without any parameter updates, with RR yielding up to 20% relative improvement on AIME24.

## Results

The paper identifies MI peaks as sparse events (≤5% of all reasoning steps) where mutual information between intermediate representations and the golden answer spikes sharply. The proposed Representation Recycling (RR) method yields a 20% relative accuracy improvement for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLaMA-8B on AIME24 and consistent gains across GSM8K, MATH500, and AIME24. Thinking Token based Test-time Scaling (TTTS) continues to improve performance as token budget grows beyond 1024 tokens on GSM8K (where the original LRM plateaus) and surpasses the original model on AIME24 once the budget exceeds 6144 tokens. Suppressing thinking tokens causes significant reasoning degradation, while suppressing the same number of random tokens has minimal effect.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares each LRM (DeepSeek-R1-Distill series, QwQ-32B) against its corresponding non-reasoning base model (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Qwen2.5-14B/32B, Llama-3.3-70B-Inst). Across all pairs, LRMs consistently exhibit higher Mean MI (e.g., 3.30 vs. 2.20 for Qwen-7B; 4.04 vs. 1.77 for Qwen-32B), higher Std, and higher AOM, confirming that reasoning-intensive training produces sharper and more pronounced MI peaks. For the RR method, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLaMA-8B with RR outperforms the original model on all three benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, AIME24), with the largest gain being a 20% relative improvement on AIME24. For TTTS, the original LRM's performance plateaus at ~1024 tokens on GSM8K and ~3000 tokens on AIME24, while TTTS continues to improve and eventually surpasses the original model at 6144+ tokens on AIME24. The thinking-token suppression experiment shows that removing thinking tokens degrades accuracy substantially, whereas removing an equal number of random non-thinking tokens has negligible impact, confirming the unique functional role of these tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of information-theoretic analysis of LLMs (building on HSIC-based MI estimation from [32, 38, 13] and information-theoretic diagnostics of chain-of-thought from [47, 9]) and the emerging literature on critical tokens in LLMs [65, 29, 36, 31]. While prior work has applied MI to understand CoT reasoning or model compression, this paper is the first to track MI evolution step-by-step during LRM generation and identify sparse 'MI peaks' that correspond to specific linguistic tokens. The theoretical grounding via Fano's inequality variants [11] and the practical methods (Representation Recycling, Thinking Token Test-time Scaling) draw on test-time scaling paradigms [42, 54, 33] but repurpose them through the MI-peak lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, information-theoretic mechanism for why 'thinking' tokens like 'Hmm' and 'Wait' matter in LRM reasoning, moving beyond the heuristic observation in [19] that such tokens help. The two proposed methods (RR and TTTS) are training-free and simple, offering practical levers for improving LRM performance without retraining. However, the analysis is primarily validated on mathematical reasoning with a handful of models, the underlying causal mechanism for why reasoning-intensive training produces MI peaks remains unexplained, and the token-level granularity may miss coarser semantic units. Open questions include whether MI peaks generalize to non-mathematical reasoning at scale, whether the peaks can be predicted a priori, and whether the theoretical bounds become tight in practice.
