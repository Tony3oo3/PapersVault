## Problem

Existing multi-label complementary label learning (MLCLL) methods rely on unbiased risk estimators (UREs) that require complementary labels to follow a uniform distribution over irrelevant labels. This assumption breaks down in practice because annotators introduce instance-specific biases (e.g., preferring low co-occurrence labels), rendering URE-based methods unable to accurately estimate true classification risk. Additionally, URE-based approaches do not exploit label correlations that are critical in multi-label settings.

## Input / Output

Each training instance consists of a d-dimensional feature vector x and a single complementary label ȳ (a K-dimensional binary vector with exactly one 1 indicating a label known to be irrelevant to x). The model outputs a K-dimensional probability vector g(x) ∈ [0,1]^K, where the relative ordering of entries encodes the predicted relevance ranking of labels. The method is distribution-agnostic at training time: it requires no knowledge of whether complementary labels were sampled uniformly or with bias, and no transition matrix estimation.

## Method

ComRank introduces a complementary ranking loss that penalizes the predicted probability of the complementary label being higher than that of any non-complementary label, formalized as L_CR(g(x), ȳ) = Σ_{k≠ȳ} exp(g_ȳ(x) − g_k(x)). The exponential surrogate makes the originally non-convex 0-1 ranking loss tractable via standard gradient descent. The key theoretical contribution is proving Bayes consistency (Theorem 5.2): under both uniform and biased complementary label distributions, minimizing this loss yields predicted probabilities whose ordering matches the true posterior p(k ∈ Y | x), without requiring a transition matrix or distribution-specific correction. The framework adopts an instance-dependent assumption (complementary label independent of the true label set Y given x), which is more realistic than the label-dependent assumption used by prior URE work, and directly captures pairwise label correlations through the ranking structure.

## Results

On Average Precision (AP) with uniform complementary labels, ComRank achieves the best result on 6 of 7 datasets, with the most dramatic gain on bibtex (0.658 vs. 0.237 for L-UW, a 177% relative improvement). Under biased complementary labels, ComRank similarly leads on 6/7 datasets (e.g., 0.751 on scene, 0.665 on bibtex). Pairwise t-tests confirm statistical dominance: ComRank wins 156/40/0 (win/tie/loss) across all metrics under uniform labels and 162/34/0 under biased labels, with zero losses against most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ComRank outperforms all seven baselines on Average Precision for 6 of 7 datasets under both uniform and biased complementary labels, losing only on nuswideBoW to Ru(g) (0.585 vs. 0.595 uniform; 0.589 vs. 0.594 biased). Against the closest URE-based competitor GDF, ComRank gains 0.021 AP on scene (0.780 vs. 0.759) and 0.044 on bibtex (0.658 vs. 0.614) under uniform labels, and 0.022 on scene (0.751 vs. 0.729) under biased labels. The gap over the CLL baseline L-UW is largest on bibtex (0.658 vs. 0.237, +0.421) and enron (0.634 vs. 0.375, +0.259), demonstrating that ranking-based learning captures label correlations that URE-based methods miss. Against PML methods PMLMD and PARD, ComRank consistently leads, e.g., 0.634 vs. 0.537 (PMLMD) and 0.634 vs. 0.444 (PARD) on enron. Statistical t-tests confirm these gains are significant at the 0.05 level, with ComRank recording zero losses against L-UW, CCMN, PMLMD, PARD, and MAE across all four evaluation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Gao et al. (IJCAI 2023), which first formulated the multi-label complementary label learning (MLCLL) problem and derived an unbiased risk estimator (URE) under a uniform complementary label distribution. The key departure is showing that URE fundamentally breaks down when complementary labels follow a biased (instance-dependent) distribution, and replacing the URE paradigm with a ranking-based loss (ComRank) that enforces complementary labels to score below non-complementary ones. Conceptually, it draws on the ranking loss literature in supervised multi-label learning (Gao & Zhou 2011, Li et al. 2017) and adapts it to the complementary label setting, while also shifting from the label-dependent to the more realistic instance-dependent assumption used in other weakly supervised learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that URE-based methods—the dominant approach in MLCLL—cannot provide unbiased risk estimation under biased complementary label distributions is an important negative result that clarifies the boundary of when existing methods are valid. The Bayes consistency guarantee for ComRank under both uniform and biased settings is a stronger theoretical property than URE alone, and the ranking formulation naturally captures pairwise label correlations that URE methods ignore. However, the approach still relies on multiple assumptions (instance-dependence, specific parametric form of the bias), is validated only with linear classifiers, and the main experiments use a single complementary label per instance, leaving open whether the framework scales gracefully to multiple complementary labels or non-linear models.
