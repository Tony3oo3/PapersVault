## Problem

Conventional deepfake detectors output only a real/fake probability without human-comprehensible explanations, while existing MLLM-based approaches ignore a critical 'capability gap': MLLMs exhibit highly uneven discrimination across forgery features, so explanations grounded in features the model cannot reliably perceive become hallucinated and untrustworthy. No prior work has systematically identified which forgery cues an MLLM can actually leverage versus which it ignores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252809/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single facial image (or a set of video frames, 8 per video for training, 32 for testing) along with a scalar blending score produced by an external SFD (e.g., CDFA), which is appended as text to the MLLM prompt. The output is a structured natural-language response: a definitive 'This image is real/fake' statement, followed by an explanation citing specific visual forgery cues, and a closing clause reporting the SFD's blending-artifact assessment. AUC is computed directly from token probabilities rather than by averaging over multiple samples.

## Method

X²-DFD is a three-stage plug-and-play framework built on LLaVA (7B/13B) fine-tuned with LoRA. Stage 1 (MFA) uses GPT-4o to generate a pool of forgery-related yes/no questions, queries the base MLLM on an assessment set, and ranks features by Balanced Accuracy, with human verification to filter irrelevant items. Stage 2 constructs a VQA fine-tuning dataset via two complementary modules: SFS generates prompts emphasizing only the top-K strong features (avoiding noise from weak cues), while WFS injects SFD scores into training examples so the model learns to interpret weak-feature signals. Stage 3 fine-tunes the MLLM on this dataset; at inference the SFD score is appended to the prompt and the model produces the structured detection-plus-explanation output. Ablations confirm that selective strong-feature training outperforms using all features, and that WFS during training is necessary for SFD signals to yield synergistic gains at inference rather than causing degradation.

## Results

On the standard cross-dataset deepfake detection benchmark (trained on FF++ c23, tested on CDF-v2, DFDCP, DFDC, DFD), X²-DFD (7B) achieves an average frame-level AUC of 88.4% and video-level AUC of 91.6%, while the 13B variant reaches 89.4% and 92.1% respectively, outperforming all 33 compared detectors. For explainability on the DDVQA benchmark, the fine-tuned model scores 0.138 average text-similarity (BLEU/CIDEr/ROUGE-L/METEOR/SPICE) and 3.120/5 on human+GPT-4o evaluation, surpassing LLaVA-7B (0.108 / 1.955), Llama3.2-Vision (0.093 / 1.966), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (0.105 / 1.709), and GPT-4o (0.068 / 2.307). Inference time is approximately 1.3 s per image, comparable to other 7B MLLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X²-DFD (7B) achieves the highest average frame-level AUC (88.4%) and video-level AUC (91.6%) among all 33 compared detectors, beating the strongest conventional baselines LSDA (81.5% frame avg) and ViT-B/CLIP (80.5% frame avg) by 6.9 and 7.9 points respectively. Against the closest MLLM-based competitors, it outperforms KFD-VLM (which reports 89.9 on CDF but no full-set average) and VLFFD (83.2 on CDF) on the complete four-dataset protocol. On video-level AUC, X²-DFD (7B) surpasses SBI (85.4% avg) by 6.2 points and ViT-B/CLIP (84.3% avg) by 7.3 points, while matching LAA-Net's CDF score (95.4) and exceeding it on DFDCP (89.3 vs 86.9). For explainability, the fine-tuned model leads all compared MLLMs (LLaVA-7B, Llama3.2-Vision, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and GPT-4o on both text-similarity metrics (0.138 avg vs. 0.108 for LLaVA) and human/GPT-4o quality scores (3.120 vs. 1.955 for LLaVA, 2.307 for GPT-4o). The 13B variant further improves frame-level average to 89.4% and video-level to 92.1%, confirming consistent scaling gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLaVA's visual instruction tuning architecture as its base MLLM, extending it with LoRA fine-tuning for deepfake-specific features. It departs from prior MLLM-based deepfake detectors (FFAA, VLFFD, RepDFD, KFD-VLM) by introducing a systematic Model Feature Assessment (MFA) pipeline that empirically identifies which forgery cues the MLLM can actually discriminate, rather than assuming all annotated features are equally useful. The key conceptual departure is the 'capability gap' framing: prior work used GPT-4o to generate annotations for smaller consumer MLLMs without verifying whether the consumer model could actually perceive those cues. The plug-and-play integration of Specific Feature Detectors (e.g., CDFA's blending score) as a training-time signal is also novel relative to prior MLLM-based approaches that rely solely on the MLLM's intrinsic visual understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic MFA analysis is the first to quantify how MLLMs differentially handle semantic vs. signal-level forgery features, providing a principled basis for future MLLM-based forensics. The plug-and-play modular design means the framework can absorb future MLLMs and SFDs without architectural redesign, which is practically valuable given the rapid evolution of both. Key limitations include: static-image-only scope (no video/audio), inference cost (~1.3s vs. ~0.03s for conventional detectors), and a potential degradation path as deepfakes become artifact-free and semantic explanations lose discriminative power. The finding that SFD signals *hurt* performance at inference unless the model was explicitly trained to interpret them (WFS) raises an open question about how to safely integrate external detectors into MLLM pipelines in general.
