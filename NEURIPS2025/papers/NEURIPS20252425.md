## Problem

Low-rank structure is a pervasive implicit assumption in RL algorithms (successor representations, Q-functions, transition kernels), yet the standard successor measure M_π = (I − γP_π)⁻¹ is provably not approximately low-rank. No prior work explains under what conditions low-rank structure actually emerges or provides entry-wise estimation guarantees for successor measures, leaving the statistical efficiency of low-rank RL methods theoretically ungrounded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252425/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of independent transitions (s, a, s′) with per-state-action counts Z_{s,a}, a fixed policy π, a shift parameter k, a truncation rank r, and a discount factor γ. It produces the rank-r truncated ν-SVD of the k-shifted successor measure [M_{π,k}]_r, from which entry-wise Q-function estimates Q̂(s,a) = [M_{π,k}]_r R(s,a) and goal-conditioned greedy policies are derived. All guarantees are stated in the ‖·‖_{2,∞} (entry-wise) norm rather than the more standard Frobenius or spectral norm, which is the norm relevant for reliable greedy policy improvement.

## Method

The key insight is that while the unshifted successor measure is inherently full-rank, the k-shifted successor measure M_{π,k} = P_π^k(I − γP_π)⁻¹ becomes approximately low-rank once k exceeds a threshold tied to local mixing of the Markov chain. The paper introduces spectral recoverability ξ(M) = max_x Σ_i σ_i ψ_i(x)² as the unified quantity governing both the SVD truncation error (‖M − [M]_r‖_{2,∞} ≤ √(σ_{r+1} ξ(M))) and the estimation error from sampled entries. The main estimator builds an empirical transition matrix P̂_π, forms M̂_{π,k} = P̂_π^k(I − γP̂_π)⁻¹, and returns its rank-r truncated ν-SVD; Theorem 1 gives a finite-sample bound decomposing into an estimation term (via leave-one-out analysis) and an approximation term, both controlled by ξ(M_{π,k}). To characterize when ξ(M_{π,k}) is bounded, the paper introduces Type II Poincaré inequalities for Markov chains, showing the required shift k scales as log(Crν_min)/log((1+σ_{r+1}²)/2) — typically far smaller than the global mixing time — and connects this to local mixing via decomposable Markov chain analysis (e.g., the 4-room environment).

## Results

Theoretically, the paper establishes that under bounded spectral recoverability, the sample complexity for entry-wise estimation of the shifted successor measure scales as n/ε² (up to log factors), compared to the n²/ε² scaling without low-rank structure (Corollary 1). In the 4-room environment, the required shift for effective low-rank approximation is O(log n), far smaller than the full mixing time of O(n log n). Empirically, on the discrete Medium PointMaze (104 states, 4 actions), a moderate shift of k=3 consistently yields the best goal-reaching accuracy when combined with rank-r SVD truncation, while the unshifted (k=0) successor measure performs worst under low-rank constraints. Shifting sharpens the singular value spectrum, accelerating decay and improving the quality of low-rank approximations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external RL baselines; instead, the central comparison is between the unshifted successor measure (k=0) and its k-shifted variants at various ranks r. With oracle (true) successor measures, introducing a temporal shift improves both exact-goal accuracy and relaxed accuracy (reaching the 2-neighborhood of the goal), with the benefit most pronounced at low ranks where the unshifted measure's high-rank tail dominates the approximation error. When successor measures are learned via TD from finite data, the picture changes: larger shifts (e.g., k=17) degrade performance because estimating long-horizon dynamics amplifies estimation error, while a moderate shift (k=3) still outperforms k=0. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) shows the estimation error is governed by the spectral recoverability ξ(M_{π,k}), which decreases with k, but the concentration term Γ_δ grows with k, creating the observed trade-off. The 4-room example (Theorem 4) provides a concrete illustration: for bounded-degree expander subgraphs, a shift of O(log n) suffices for O(1) entry-wise error, versus the O(n log n) mixing time needed for the unshifted chain to become approximately rank-1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the successor representation framework (Dayan 1993) and the Forward-Backward / universal successor measure line of work (Touati & Ollivier 2021, 2023) by challenging the implicit low-rank assumption on the successor measure itself. It builds on the spectral entry-wise matrix estimation techniques of Stojanovic et al. (2023) and the leave-one-out analysis of Abbe et al. (2020), but departs from prior low-rank MDP work by showing that low-rank structure is not an assumption to be imposed but rather emerges naturally from a temporal shift of the successor measure, governed by local mixing properties of the underlying Markov chain. The goal-conditioned RL experimental setup follows Eysenbach et al. (2022), and the paper provides the first sample complexity analysis for successor measure estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical explanation for why low-rank approximations of successor measures work empirically in RL, replacing an unexplained heuristic with a principled connection to Markov chain mixing. The key practical insight is that the required shift is typically O(log n) rather than the full mixing time O(n log n), making it a cheap modification. However, the analysis assumes a generative model (i.i.d. transitions), is restricted to tabular MDPs, and the empirical validation is limited to a single 104-state PointMaze environment, leaving open whether the benefits persist in high-dimensional or continuous settings. The connection between the shift parameter and local mixing also raises the question of how to select it adaptively in practice without knowing the spectral structure a priori.
