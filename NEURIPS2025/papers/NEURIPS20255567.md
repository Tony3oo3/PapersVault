## Problem

Online RLHF iteratively collects new preference data and re-optimizes the reward model from scratch over the entire growing dataset at each iteration, causing computational and storage costs to scale linearly with the number of iterations. While prior work has established statistical efficiency guarantees for online RLHF, none address this growing computational burden. The paper asks whether one can design online RLHF algorithms that are simultaneously statistically and computationally efficient, achieving constant-time updates per iteration without sacrificing estimation quality.

## Input / Output

At each iteration, the method consumes a single preference tuple (context x, two responses a and a', binary label y) and produces an updated reward parameter vector θ̃_{t+1} ∈ R^d, where features φ(x,a) are extracted from the last hidden layer of a frozen pre-trained LLM (d=4096 for Llama-3-8B-Instruct). The key constraint is one-pass processing: only the current sample and a running local norm matrix H_t are retained, eliminating storage of all historical data. In the active and deployment settings, the method additionally outputs a policy π(x) = argmax_a φ(x,a)ᵀθ̃ and an uncertainty-driven query selection for the next data point.

## Method

The core contribution is a one-pass reward modeling algorithm built on online mirror descent with a tailored local norm that captures second-order (Hessian) information. Instead of solving the full MLE over the historical dataset, the method approximates the current log-loss ℓ_t(θ) via a second-order Taylor expansion around the current estimate θ̃_t, then solves the resulting quadratic subproblem in closed form: θ̃_{t+1} = argmin_θ {⟨g_t(θ̃_t), θ⟩ + (1/2η)‖θ − θ̃_t‖²_{H̃_t}}, where H̃_t = H_t + ηH_t(θ̃_t) accumulates rank-one Hessian updates at lookahead points. This yields O(1) per-iteration time and storage, improving the estimation error bound by a factor of √κ over prior MLE-based methods. For practical LLM-scale deployment, the matrix inversion is replaced by Hessian-Vector Products combined with conjugate gradient descent (reducing cost to O(d) per step), and model uncertainty for active query selection is approximated via rejection sampling over n sampled responses ranked by the current reward model.

## Results

In the active data collection setting (the only setting with a concrete numerical table), the proposed OMD-based method achieves 70.43% ± 0.3 evaluation accuracy, outperforming Active-MLE (69.82% ± 0.4) and Rand-MLE (69.51% ± 0.5), while reducing training time from ~4,982 s to ~1,489 s (approximately 3× speedup). Theoretically, across all three online RLHF settings, the method improves the statistical bound by a factor of √κ over prior work (e.g., sub-optimality gap of Õ(√d·‖Φ‖_{H_T^{-1}}) vs. Õ(√d·κ·‖Φ‖_{V_T^{-1}}) for passive collection) and reduces per-iteration time and storage from O(t log t) and O(t) to a strict O(1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Zhu et al. [2023] (passive data collection), the proposed method improves the sub-optimality gap by a factor of √κ (from Õ(√d·κ·‖Φ‖_{V_T^{-1}}) to Õ(√d·‖Φ‖_{H_T^{-1}})) and reduces amortized per-iteration time from O(log T) to O(1) with storage dropping from O(t) to O(1). Against Das et al. [2025] (active data collection), the method matches the statistical guarantee Õ(d√(κ/T)) but cuts per-iteration time from O(t log t) to O(1) and storage from O(t) to O(1); experimentally it achieves 70.43% accuracy vs. 69.82% for Active-MLE while training ~3× faster (1,489 s vs. 4,982 s). Against Saha et al. [2023] (deployment-time adaptation), the method improves the regret bound by a factor of √κ (from Õ(dκ√T) to Õ(d√(κT))) and again reduces time and storage to O(1) per iteration. In the passive setting, the OMD-based method also converges faster to lower training loss and higher evaluation accuracy than MLE with SGD, particularly in the small-sample regime (T < 10,000). In deployment-time adaptation, the proposed action selection strategy (best + diverse second arm) outperforms random, best+second-best, and best+worst baselines in both cumulative reward and win rate for both MLE and OMD variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of online RLHF (formulated as contextual dueling bandits following Zhu et al. 2023, Das et al. 2025, Saha et al. 2023) and the online mirror descent framework for logistic bandits (Faury et al. 2022, Zhang and Sugiyama 2023). All prior online RLHF methods rely on maximum likelihood estimation over the full historical dataset, incurring O(t log t) time and O(t) storage per iteration. The key departure here is replacing MLE with a one-pass OMD update that uses a second-order Taylor approximation of the current loss and a tailored local norm capturing second-order curvature, yielding O(1) per-iteration cost while simultaneously tightening the estimation error bound by a factor of √κ over prior results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear growth of computational and storage costs with iteration count is a genuine bottleneck for long-horizon online RLHF, particularly on edge devices or in continuous deployment settings where the model must adapt indefinitely. By achieving constant-time updates without sacrificing (and in fact improving) statistical efficiency, this work makes sustained online alignment practically feasible. Key limitations include the linear reward model assumption, fixed feature mapping, and restriction to the Bradley-Terry model; extending to dynamic features, non-linear reward functions, or other preference models (e.g., Plackett-Luce) remains open. The practical implementation via Hessian-vector products and conjugate gradient descent is a reasonable approximation, but the gap between the theoretical O(1) guarantee and actual wall-clock performance at LLM scale (d=4096) warrants further scrutiny.
