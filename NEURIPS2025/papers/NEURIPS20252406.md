## Problem

Prior target speaker extraction methods require clean audio enrollments of the target speaker, which are impractical in real-world noisy environments (e.g., identifying a stranger at a cocktail party). Limited prior work on noisy enrollments either exploits timesteps where only the target speaks or assumes the user participated in the recording, leaving the challenging case of significant overlap between target and interfering speakers in the enrollment largely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252406/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes three audio inputs: a noisy Positive Enrollment (target speaker active plus interfering speakers and background noise), a noisy Negative Enrollment (target silent, interferers and noise present), and the Audio Mixture to extract from. Enrollments are user-labeled (e.g., via a button press) and need not be precisely timed; the model tolerates ~0.3s labeling inaccuracy. The output is the extracted target speaker's speech waveform, produced causally via ISTFT from a transposed convolution. Both monaural (2-channel STFT) and binaural (4-channel stacked real/imaginary) variants are supported.

## Method

The core mechanism is a Siamese TF-GridNet encoder pair that processes the Positive and Negative Enrollments with shared parameters, followed by an Encoder Fusion Module that adds learnable segment embeddings, concatenates the two enrollment sequences along time, and applies two full-band self-attention layers. This cross-enrollment self-attention lets the model identify Positive Interferers (absent in some positive frames) and Negative Interferers (present in negative frames) and suppress them, retaining only the target speaker's embedding. A causal TF-GridNet extraction branch then uses cross-attention fusion blocks (enrollment embedding as Key/Value, mixture features as Query) to extract the target voice. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 distills the Siamese encoder toward a frozen TF-GridNet encoder's clean-speech embedding (MSE loss), and Stage 2 trains the extraction branch with negative SNR loss. This two-stage strategy reduces optimization steps to reach 3 dB SNR by 60% compared to end-to-end training.

## Results

On monaural target speaker extraction from 2-speaker mixtures conditioned on 2-speaker noisy enrollments, the proposed method achieves 10.14 dB SNRi and 8.85 dB SI-SNRi, outperforming the closest prior work TCE (8.48 dB SNRi, 6.67 dB SI-SNRi) by over 2.1 dB in SI-SNRi. Performance degrades gracefully as enrollment difficulty increases: with 4 interfering speakers in the enrollment, SI-SNRi drops to 8.30 dB (2-speaker mixture) and 7.82 dB (3-speaker mixture). The two-stage training strategy reduces optimization steps to reach 3 dB SNR by 60% (240k vs. 600k steps). On real-world audio, the model achieves a MOS of 3.35/4, substantially outperforming unprocessed audio (2.10) and all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TCE [15], the closest prior method using noisy enrollments, the proposed model wins on SI-SNRi across all six conditions by 1.5–3.7 dB (e.g., 8.85 vs. 6.67 dB for 2spk/2spk), and also leads on SNRi, PESQ, STOI, DNSMOS, and WER in nearly every setting. Against USEF-TFGridnet [17], which assumes clean enrollment, the gap is dramatic: the proposed method achieves ~8.9 dB higher SI-SNRi, confirming that clean-enrollment models fail catastrophically when interferers are present in the enrollment. NMF [36] performs unconditional separation and yields negative SI-SNRi, making it an ineffective baseline for target speaker extraction. In the binaural setting (Table 2), the proposed model outperforms LookOnceToHear [16] by 0.2–0.4 dB SI-SNRi across all conditions, with statistically significant gains confirmed by two-sample t-tests at 90% confidence, though the margin is smaller than in the monaural case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends target speaker extraction (TSE) from the well-studied clean-enrollment setting to a more realistic noisy-enrollment setting by introducing a positive/negative enrollment pair as the conditioning signal. It builds on TF-GridNet as the backbone and adopts the causal modification and frozen-encoder knowledge distillation from LookOnceToHear, but departs conceptually from prior noisy-enrollment methods (TCE, ADEnet, LookOnceToHear) that either require the user to be in the mixture, exploit clean segments within the enrollment, or rely on spatial/binaural cues. The key positioning is that the positive/negative comparison strategy exploits the stochastic temporal misalignment between independent speakers to disambiguate the target without any clean audio or spatial information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: it removes the infeasible requirement of obtaining a clean enrollment in real-world scenarios (cocktail parties, meetings), replacing it with a simple binary tap-to-label interaction. The two-stage training with knowledge distillation (60% fewer optimization steps) is a reusable training recipe for conditioning on noisy inputs. However, the paper acknowledges that the frozen encoder caps the encoding branch's quality, extracted speech still contains audible artifacts (low DNSMOS), and the method does not surpass SOTA clean-enrollment models, leaving open whether a jointly-trained encoder could close this gap while maintaining fast convergence.
