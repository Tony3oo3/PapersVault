## Problem

Unsupervised vertex hunting (VH) requires strong identification conditions (e.g., pure points at each vertex), does not improve with more data, and degrades for large K due to thin simplex geometry. This paper introduces semi-supervised vertex hunting (SSVH), where partial barycentric coordinate information is available for a subset of points but only up to an unknown positive rescaling vector b, and shows how to exploit this side information to overcome all three limitations.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes the vertex count K, the noisy projected data matrix X_S ∈ ℝ^{N×d} for N labeled points, and the partial soft-label matrix Π_S ∈ ℝ^{N×K} whose rows π_i are related to the true barycentric coordinates w_i via w_i = (b ∘ π_i)/‖b ∘ π_i‖₁ for an unknown positive vector b. The output is the estimated vertex matrix V̂ ∈ ℝ^{K×d} (rows are estimated vertices), obtained through a two-step procedure: first estimating b via an eigenvector computation, then performing a linear regression to recover the vertices. The key unusual aspect is that the provided labels are not the barycentric coordinates themselves but require solving for the unknown transformation b before they can be used.

## Method

The core technical contribution is an optimization-free estimator for the unknown rescaling vector b, built on a novel linear-algebraic insight involving orthogonal projection matrices. The method constructs a K×K matrix M(α) = Π_S′ diag(Hα) R_S R_S′ diag(Hα) Π_S, where H is the projection onto the orthogonal complement of Π_S's column space, and proves that b is an eigenvector of M(α) associated with the zero eigenvalue (Theorem 2.1). In the noisy case, b̂ is taken as the eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue of the plug-in matrix M̂(α), and the vector α is chosen via a closed-form eigenvector computation (eigenvector of Hf(Γ_S)H for the largest eigenvalue) that depends only on Π_S, not on noisy data. Once b̂ is obtained, the barycentric coordinates ŵ_i are recovered directly, and the vertices are estimated by the simple regression V̂ = (Ŵ_S′Ŵ_S)⁻¹Ŵ_S′X_S. Theoretical analysis under sub-Gaussian noise yields an error bound of order N^{-1/2}K^{1.25}σ_n√log(n), which decays with the number of labeled points N—unlike unsupervised VH whose error is independent of n.

## Results

On the co-authorship network (2831 nodes, K communities), SSVH achieves a median vertex estimation error of 0.16 (MAD 0.04), compared to 2.37 for SP and 1.05 for SVS — a 15× and 6.6× improvement respectively. On the MADStat text corpus (4129 documents, K=11), SSVH achieves 1.22 (MAD 0.46) versus 1.77 (SP) and 1.64 (SVS). In controlled simulations (K=3, σ=0.2, 3% labels), SSVH reduces the median error to 0.053, outperforming SP (0.319), MVT (0.191), and AA (1.863). The theoretical error bound scales as N^{-1/2}K^{1.25}σ_n√log(n), confirming a faster convergence rate than unsupervised VH which does not decay with n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSVH decisively outperforms Successive Projection (SP, Araújo et al. 2001) across all settings: on the network, the vertex error drops from 2.37 to 0.16 (15× reduction), and on the text corpus from 1.77 to 1.22. Against the de-noised variant SVS (Jin et al. 2023), SSVH achieves a 6.6× improvement on the network (0.16 vs. 1.05) and a modest 25% gain on text (1.22 vs. 1.64); the downstream excess error on text is nearly tied (0.031 vs. 0.033). In the anchor-free simulation (Table 1, no pure nodes), SSVH beats Minimum Volume Transformation (MVT, Craig 1994) by 3.6× at low noise (0.053 vs. 0.191) and by 1.26× at high noise (0.416 vs. 0.522), while also being ~2600× faster at K=3 (3.2 ms vs. 8489 ms). SSVH also substantially outperforms Archetypal Analysis (AA, Javadi & Montanari 2020), which suffers from the largest errors (1.863 at σ=0.2, 18.925 at σ=1) due to its reliance on convex-hull geometry. Notably, SSVH's advantage over SP grows with noise level and dimension K, consistent with the theoretical prediction that unsupervised VH error does not decay with the number of labeled points N.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established vertex hunting (VH) problem—also known as linear unmixing or archetypal analysis—to a semi-supervised setting where partial barycentric coordinate information is available up to an unknown positive scaling vector b. The spectral projection and normalization frameworks from Jin et al. [21] (mixed membership estimation) and Ke & Wang [25] (topic modeling) serve as the front-end that produces the simplicial data structure to which SSVH is applied. The key methodological departure from prior unsupervised VH algorithms (SP, MVT, archetypal analysis) is an optimization-free estimation of b via eigenvectors of a matrix constructed from orthogonal projection matrix properties, a linear algebra insight the authors note was previously 'hidden in the proofs' of related works. The paper also positions itself against semi-supervised community detection methods, arguing that existing approaches for discrete labels do not naturally generalize to continuous mixed-membership vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSVH addresses three concrete limitations of unsupervised VH: it requires weaker identification conditions (no pure nodes needed), achieves a faster N^{-1/2} error decay rate, and handles large K where the simplex becomes 'thin' and vulnerable to noise. The practical impact is demonstrated in network mixed-membership estimation and topic modeling, where even a 5% label ratio yields substantial error reductions over SP and SVS. Open questions include the behavior under noisy or incorrect labels (sketched in Remark 3 but not fully analyzed), the sensitivity to the choice of projection matrix U and normalization vector η, and whether the theoretical conditions (particularly Assumption 3.3 on the rank of Σ(α)) hold in realistic network/text settings with heterogeneous degree structures.
