## Problem

Existing scientific benchmarks for MLLMs predominantly evaluate knowledge understanding from secondary sources (textbooks, academic materials) while neglecting the full cognitive spectrum from perception to reasoning. This leaves a fundamental gap: there is no benchmark that granularly measures how MLLMs perceive raw scientific signals, interpret domain-specific attributes, and perform structured comparative reasoning across multiple disciplines using native scientific data formats.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250562/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each VQA instance pairs 1–18 scientific visualization images (average 2.3) rendered from 17 native data formats (e.g., .mgf mass spectra, .pdb protein structures, .stockholm RNA sequences, .sto crystal files) with a bilingual (English/Chinese) text prompt averaging ~88 tokens. Outputs span three question types—multiple-choice (284), exact-match (420), and open-ended (126)—and for the remote sensing perception subtask, models must additionally emit bounding-box coordinates scored by IoU. All 830 pairs are provided in both English and Chinese to test multilingual robustness.

## Method

SFE introduces a three-level cognitive taxonomy—L1 Scientific Signal Perception (discerning components in raw visualizations), L2 Scientific Attribute Understanding (interpreting domain-expert knowledge), and L3 Scientific Comparative Reasoning (deriving insights through structured comparison of multiple visual sources)—and instantiates it as 66 expert-curated tasks across five disciplines (Astronomy, Chemistry, Earth, Life, Materials). The data pipeline proceeds in three stages: experts define 18 scientific directions, instantiate them into tasks mapped to specific cognitive levels and question types, and then author VQA pairs from rendered native data, followed by two-stage validation (expert cross-review plus rule-based format checking). Evaluation benchmarks 16 state-of-the-art MLLMs in zero-shot mode (temperature 0, max 1024 tokens) using LLM-as-a-Judge (GPT-4o) for semantic correctness, BERTScore F1 for open-ended answers, and IoU for grounding tasks. Key findings include a 26-point gap between the best (GPT-o3, 34.08%) and worst (Gemini-2.5-Pro, 8.04%) models, a clear closed-vs-open-weight divide, and evidence that newer models improve primarily on L3 reasoning rather than L2 knowledge, suggesting gains come from reasoning training rather than broader knowledge acquisition.

## Results

On the SFE benchmark (830 VQA pairs, 66 tasks, 5 disciplines), GPT-o3 achieves the best overall LLM-as-a-Judge score of 34.08% (EN) / 31.60% (ZH), while the best open-weight model InternVL-3-78B reaches only 26.52% / 24.30%. The weakest model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, scores just 8.04% / 8.96% due to token-budget exhaustion from excessive reasoning. Across cognitive levels, L1 (perception) averages 32.00%, L2 (understanding) 24.76%, and L3 (reasoning) 22.89% in English, with newer reasoning models (GPT-o3, Claude-3.7-Sonnet) showing the largest gains specifically on L3 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-o3 leads all 16 evaluated MLLMs with a 34.08% average (EN), outperforming the next-best closed model GPT-o1 by ~2% and the strongest open-weight model InternVL-3-78B by ~7.6%. Within the GPT family, GPT-o3's key advantage over GPT-4.1 is on L3 comparative reasoning (36.48% vs. 26.64%), while L2 understanding scores remain nearly identical (30.30% vs. 30.02%), indicating that reasoning-focused training drives the gain rather than broader knowledge. Claude-3.7-Sonnet improves over Claude-3-Opus by over 7% in both languages, and InternVL-3 surpasses InternVL-2.5 by 8% on L3 tasks, confirming that successive model versions primarily enhance high-order reasoning. Material Science is the most tractable discipline (GPT-o3: 63.44%), while Astronomy is the hardest (GPT-o3: 24.24%), and the overall closed-vs-open-weight gap is 6–8%. Gemini-2.5-Pro anomalously scores only 8.04% due to token-budget exhaustion from over-reasoning, creating a >26% gap versus GPT-o3 and highlighting that reasoning capability without efficient token management can be counterproductive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in scientific MLLM evaluation by introducing a three-level cognitive taxonomy (perception → understanding → comparative reasoning) that prior benchmarks like ScienceQA, MMMU, SuperGPQA, and CURIE lack. While those benchmarks extract tasks from textbooks, curricula, or secondary sources, SFE constructs VQA pairs from native scientific raw data formats (spectra, .pdb files, XRD patterns, .mgf mass spectra) and organizes them explicitly by cognitive depth. The paper also departs from the monolingual norm of nearly all prior scientific benchmarks by providing bilingual (English/Chinese) prompts and answers, positioning SFE for global deployment evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even GPT-o3 achieves only 34.08% and the cross-model average is ~26%, establishing SFE as a genuinely challenging frontier for scientific MLLMs. The cognitive-level breakdown exposes a specific gap: newer models improve on L3 comparative reasoning (e.g., GPT-o3 jumps from 26.64% to 36.48% over GPT-4.1) without corresponding L2 knowledge gains, suggesting RL-based reasoning training helps structured comparison but does not expand domain knowledge. Open questions include whether the 5-discipline scope generalizes, whether the single-run evaluation (no error bars) obscures smaller differences, and whether the low scores reflect true capability gaps or evaluation artifacts from LLM-as-a-Judge scoring. The authors also note the broader concern that increasing AI reliance in science may undermine human intuition and creativity.
