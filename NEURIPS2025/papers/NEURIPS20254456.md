## Problem

Model merging has been extensively studied in the post-training stage (combining task-specific fine-tuned models), but its application during large-scale pre-training—where one merges sequential checkpoints from a single training trajectory—remains largely unexplored, particularly at the 100B+ parameter scale. The paper addresses how merging affects performance across different phases of the WSD learning schedule, what hyperparameters are optimal, and whether merging can serve as a low-cost substitute for annealing or a tool for training stabilization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N sequential checkpoints saved at fixed token intervals V along a single pre-training trajectory (e.g., 10 checkpoints spaced 8B tokens apart for a 1.3B/13B MoE model), all from the same training run rather than independent models. The output is a single merged weight tensor computed as a weighted sum, which can be deployed directly for evaluation, used as initialization for continued training or SFT (PMA-init), or applied to recover from irrecoverable loss spikes by merging pre-spike checkpoints and resuming training.

## Method

The paper introduces Pretrained Model Average (PMA), which applies weighted averaging (SMA, EMA, or WMA) to N checkpoints collected during the constant-learning-rate stable phase of a WSD schedule. The key empirical finding is that PMA applied early in the cosine-decay phase achieves performance comparable to or exceeding the fully annealed model, effectively simulating annealing without the additional compute. Ablations reveal that the optimal merging interval V scales with model size (4B tokens for 0.7B/7B up to 80B for 10B/100B), and that more checkpoints consistently improve final performance once training stabilizes. A second contribution, PMA-init, uses merged weights as initialization for downstream CT/SFT stages, yielding smoother GradNorm curves and enabling recovery from irrecoverable loss spikes. A second-order Taylor expansion analysis shows merging is effective when deviation vectors of individual checkpoints exhibit negative cross-correlation in the Hessian metric, explaining why checkpoints from the stable phase (where weights explore complementary directions) merge well while tightly-converged annealed checkpoints do not.

## Results

Merging checkpoints from the stable (constant-LR) phase yields consistent gains across model scales: Seed-MoE-1.3B/13B improves HumanEval from 31.1 to 36.6, Seed-MoE-10B/100B from 54.3 to 61.6, and Dense-70B from 50.6 to 57.9 (GSM8K: 85.9→91.3). Remarkably, PMA applied at the early cosine-decay stage matches or exceeds the fully annealed model, enabling constant-LR training + merging as a low-cost substitute for the decay phase. For SFT initialization (PMA-init), the merged model achieves 87.1 MMLU and 52.0 LiveBench versus 86.8/50.5 for the baseline, with in-house OOD and Instruction-Following gains of +2.1 and +2.5 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is PMA (merged) vs. the unmerged checkpoint at the same training step. Across all tested MoE sizes (0.7B/7B through 20B/200B) and Dense sizes (411M through 70B), PMA consistently outperforms the unmerged baseline on the weighted-average benchmark score, with the largest absolute gains on code and math tasks (e.g., +5.5 on HumanEval for 1.3B/13B, +7.3 for 10B/100B). Among merging strategies, WMA slightly edges out EMA and SMA in early training (204B tokens), but the gap narrows to near-zero as weights stabilize; the authors default to SMA for simplicity. For the annealing-prediction experiment, constant-LR + PMA matches or exceeds the fully cosine-annealed model after only 250B additional tokens, effectively eliminating the need for a decay phase. PMA-init for CT/SFT produces smoother GradNorm curves and marginally lower early loss, but final downstream performance is roughly on par with standard initialization—its main value is training stabilization (recovering from irrecoverable loss spikes) rather than a direct accuracy gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied post-training model merging literature (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, DARE, Fisher Merging) into the pre-training phase, where prior work is limited to small-scale demonstrations like LAWA and Checkpoint Merging on Baichuan2. While DeepSeek-V3 and LLaMA-3.1 have acknowledged using model merging in pre-training, no prior work has provided systematic technical details at the 100B+ parameter scale. The paper repurposes standard weight-averaging schemes (SMA, EMA, WMA) as a pre-training tool (PMA) and introduces a novel application—PMA-init—for stabilizing downstream training and recovering from loss spikes, which departs from the purely performance-oriented framing of prior merging work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: if PMA at early annealing reliably predicts final annealed performance, teams can skip the expensive decay phase entirely, saving significant compute and accelerating validation cycles. The PMA-init recovery mechanism offers a concrete alternative to retraining from scratch after irrecoverable loss spikes, a costly problem in large-scale training. However, the absence of released code, undisclosed architectures/datasets, and lack of error bars limit independent verification and generalizability. Open questions include the interaction between learning rate magnitude and merging effectiveness, and whether these findings transfer to RL-based post-training where adjacent checkpoints are also available.
