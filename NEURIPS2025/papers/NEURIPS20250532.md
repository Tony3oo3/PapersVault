## Problem

Existing LLM routing methods rely on static parametric knowledge representations, but in RAG settings, retrieved documents dynamically reshape each model's ability to answer a query—sometimes reversing answerability entirely. This 'Static Knowledge Assumption' makes non-RAG-aware routers suboptimal, as they cannot capture the interplay between a model's internal knowledge and external retrieved content. The paper formally defines the RAG-aware routing problem and proposes the first routing method that explicitly models retrieval-induced knowledge shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250532/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The router consumes a text query, one or more retrieved documents, and a set of candidate LLM IDs (15 open-source models from 0.5B–72B parameters, optionally augmented with closed-source models like GPT-4o). It outputs a routing decision selecting one LLM per query, along with a relevance score for each candidate that enables a score-threshold mechanism for latency-aware routing. The query and document encoders share parameters (all-mpnet-base-v2), while a separate cross-encoder (ms-marco-MiniLM-L12-v2) processes query-document pairs, yielding a total router size of ~136M parameters with ~0.011s per-instance inference time.

## Method

RAGRouter updates each LLM's static knowledge embedding v_k with a fused knowledge vector v_f computed via multi-head attention over three signals: a document embedding (non-parametric knowledge), a per-LLM RAG capability embedding (intrinsic capacity to exploit retrieved evidence), and a cross-encoder interaction embedding (query-document relevance). The routing decision is argmax over cosine similarity between the query embedding and the updated representations v_k' = v_k + v_f. Training employs a contrastive objective combining Cross-Setting Contrast (CSC, comparing non-RAG vs. RAG representations of the same model) and Intra-Setting Contrast (ISC, comparing different models within the same setting), plus a binary classification loss on answerability labels. This dual-contrast design captures both retrieval-induced knowledge shifts and inter-model heterogeneity, moving beyond the static knowledge assumption that limits prior routers.

## Results

RAGRouter achieves an average accuracy of 64.46% across five knowledge-intensive tasks (PopQA, MedMCQA, NQ, WebQ, TriviaQA) under both local and online retrieval settings, surpassing the best single LLM (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct at 60.85%) by +3.61% and the Oracle Single Best baseline (61.22%) by +3.24%. With closed-source LLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-Max, DeepSeek-R1) added to the candidate pool, RAGRouter reaches 59.38% average on NQ/WebQ, outperforming GPT-4o (57.71%) by +1.67% while invoking it in only 44.79% of samples. The router itself is lightweight (~136M parameters), processing 270 instances in 3 seconds (0.011s per instance) on a single RTX 4090D GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAGRouter outperforms all five non-RAG-aware routing baselines across all tasks. Against the strongest baseline, GraphRouter (61.17%), RAGRouter gains +3.29% in average accuracy; against KNN Router (60.44%) and MF (60.25%), the margins are +4.02% and +4.21% respectively; and against RouterDC (56.71%), the gap widens to +7.75%. The largest improvement is over Prompt LLM (55.13%) at +9.33%. Notably, RAGRouter also exceeds the Oracle Single Best baseline (61.22%) by +3.24%, demonstrating that routing across multiple RAG-augmented LLMs can surpass even the best single model selected per dataset. The gains are consistent across both local and online retrieval settings, with the largest per-task improvements on MedMCQA (Online: 74.44% vs. 71.48% for MF) and NQ (56.67% vs. 52.08% for KNN Router).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM routing and retrieval-augmented generation. Prior routing methods (RouterDC, GraphRouter, RouteLLM, EmbedLLM, KNN Router) assume static parametric knowledge representations and operate solely on query-model compatibility, ignoring the dynamic impact of retrieved documents. RAGRouter repurposes contrastive learning (as in RouterDC) and multi-head attention to model a fused knowledge representation that shifts with retrieved documents, introducing a RAG capability embedding per LLM and a cross-encoder for query-document interaction. The paper formally subsumes conventional LLM routing as the boundary case where no documents are present (d=∅), positioning its contribution as a principled generalization rather than a standalone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical and timely gap: as organizations deploy heterogeneous pools of LLMs with RAG, the assumption that a model's capability is fixed breaks down, and naive routing strategies can actively misroute queries. The finding that RAG-induced positive gains (~31%) and negative interference (~31%) are nearly balanced underscores that retrieval is not uniformly beneficial, making document-aware routing essential. Open questions include how the approach scales to larger model pools, multi-document or iterative retrieval settings, and whether the contrastive formulation generalizes to non-factoid or multi-turn tasks. The substantial gap to the oracle upper bound (64.46% vs. 76.77%) also signals considerable room for improvement in routing accuracy.
