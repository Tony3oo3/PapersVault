## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods decouple task and distillation gradients at only a single level (e.g., DOT), overlooking the inherent optimization conflict between target-class and non-target-class knowledge flows. Additionally, low-confidence dark knowledge in non-target classes introduces noisy gradient signals that degrade transfer quality, and the theoretical justification for momentum allocation across different loss components remains absent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250915/cropped_page1_idx8.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on frozen teacher–student pairs consuming RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K, variable resolution for MS-COCO detection). Both networks process the same input simultaneously; the teacher's softened logits serve as supervision signals while the student is trained end-to-end. Outputs are class probabilities for classification tasks or bounding-box predictions for detection (Faster-RCNN-FPN backbone), with the student model being the sole deployed artifact at inference time.

## Method

DeepKD introduces a dual-level gradient decoupling strategy that splits the student's training gradient into three independent streams—task-oriented (TOG), target-class (TCG), and non-target-class (NCG)—each maintained in its own momentum buffer. The key insight is that optimal momentum coefficients should be positively correlated with each component's gradient signal-to-noise ratio (GSNR): components with higher GSNR (TOG, NCG) receive μ+Δ momentum while the lower-GSNR TCG receives μ−Δ, preventing mutual interference during optimization. A second mechanism, Dynamic Top-K Masking (DTM), progressively filters low-confidence non-target logits from both teacher and student outputs, starting at 5% of classes and expanding to the full class count over three curriculum-learning phases (easy → transition → hard), thereby purifying dark knowledge early in training. The framework is plug-and-play, requiring only modification of the optimizer's first-order momentum and a single hyperparameter Δ, and integrates seamlessly with existing logit-based distillation losses (KD, DKD, MLKD, CRLD) without architectural changes.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (ResNet32×4→ResNet8×4), DeepKD achieves 77.03% top-1 accuracy with vanilla KD (+3.70% over baseline) and 79.25% with CRLD (+1.65%), the best result among all logit-based methods. On ImageNet-1K, KD+DeepKD (w. top-k) boosts ResNet50→MobileNet-V1 top-1 accuracy by +4.15% (74.65% vs. 70.50%), and CRLD+DeepKD reaches 75.75% on RegNetY-16GF→DeiT-Tiny (+1.89%). On MS-COCO object detection, DKD+DeepKD with dynamic top-k masking achieves 34.20% AP (+1.86% over vanilla DKD), surpassing ReviewKD's 33.71% using only logit distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepKD consistently outperforms all compared baselines across every teacher-student pair and dataset. Against vanilla KD [1], gains range from +0.92% to +3.70% on CIFAR-100 and up to +4.15% on ImageNet (ResNet50→MN-V1). Compared to DOT [19], which only decouples task vs. distillation gradients at one level, DeepKD's dual-level decoupling (TOG/TCG/NCG) yields an additional +1.7% to +2.1% on CIFAR-100. Against DKD [18], which splits the loss into TCKD/NCKD but uses uniform momentum, DeepKD adds +0.91% to +1.22% on CIFAR-100 and +1.45% to +1.60% on ImageNet. Versus LSKD [49] (logit standardization), DeepKD achieves +0.41% to +1.27% on CIFAR-100 and +0.90% to +2.45% on ImageNet. Against MLKD [55] and CRLD [13], the strongest existing logit-based methods, DeepKD still gains +1.65% to +2.07% on CIFAR-100 and +0.97% to +1.89% on ImageNet. On MS-COCO, DKD+DeepKD† (34.20% AP) surpasses ReviewKD [56] (33.71% AP) and LSKD [49] (33.98% AP) using only logit distillation, while also outperforming feature-based LSKD on AP75 (36.59% vs. 36.34%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-decoupling line initiated by DOT, which separated task and distillation gradients with different momentum coefficients, by further decomposing the distillation gradient into target-class (TCG) and non-target-class (NCG) components, yielding a three-way decoupling (TOG/TCG/NCG). The key conceptual departure is providing a GSNR-based theoretical justification for momentum allocation, replacing DOT's empirically chosen Δ with a principled signal-to-noise argument. On the denoising side, the dynamic top-k masking (DTM) generalizes ReKD's static top-k filtering and CTKD's temperature curriculum into a phase-wise k-scheduling that progressively admits more non-target classes, positioning itself as a curriculum-learning-based purification of dark knowledge rather than a fixed filter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GSNR-driven momentum allocation gives practitioners a theoretically grounded rule for setting per-component momentum in KD, reducing the reliance on hand-tuned hyperparameters that plagued DOT. The dynamic top-k masking offers a simple, parameter-light denoising mechanism that consistently adds 0.3–1.9% over the decoupling-only variant across CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and MS-COCO. However, the 47–62% per-epoch training time overhead (from maintaining three separate momentum buffers and gradient sampling) is a practical concern, and the method is currently limited to logit-based distillation with a single Δ hyperparameter that still requires per-dataset tuning. Open questions include whether the GSNR framework extends cleanly to feature-based or multi-teacher distillation, and whether the dynamic top-k schedule can be made fully adaptive rather than relying on ablation-determined phase boundaries.
