## Problem

Prior studies linking pulse oximetry racial bias to ICU outcomes relied on statistical associations or single-mediator regression, failing to causally isolate the effect of race mediated specifically through oxygen saturation discrepancies in the presence of other post-admission mediators. No finite-sample guarantees existed for path-specific effect estimators, which are critical for generating robust evidence in the constrained sample sizes typical of critical care.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251271/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular ICU records where pre-admission confounders (age, sex, Charlson Comorbidity Index, OASIS score) are separated from post-admission mediators W (ABG excluding SpO2/SaO2, creatinine, lactate, vitals) and V (SpO2, SaO2, and their matched discrepancy Δ within 5 minutes). Time-series measurements are aggregated via exponentially weighted averages up to first ventilation or discharge, and the analysis is restricted to stays ≥24 hours with at least one matched SpO2–SaO2 pair. The output is the VDE point estimate with 95% confidence intervals from 500 bootstraps, alongside TE, NDE, NIE, and a simplified NIE* that ignores W for comparison with prior work.

## Method

The paper defines the V-specific Direct Effect (VDE) as E[Y_{x1}] − E[Y_{x1,V_{x0},W_{x1}}], isolating the causal pathway from race through oximetry discrepancies while holding other post-admission mediators at their x1 values. It constructs a doubly robust estimator using a nested hierarchy of three regression functions (μ₀¹, μ₀², μ₀³) and three importance-sampling weights (π₀¹, π₀², π₀³), where the functional φ is unbiased if either the regression or the propensity is correctly specified. A self-normalized variant rescales each propensity by its empirical mean to reduce variance from small denominator propensities, which is critical in the small-sample ICU setting. Nuisance parameters are estimated with XGBoost under 5-fold sample splitting, and the paper provides novel finite-sample bounds (Theorem 2) decomposing the estimation error into a near-Gaussian term and a doubly-robust product term. Identifiability holds under the specified DAG with no latent W–V confounding, and the non-identifiability case is formally characterized in the appendix.

## Results

On eICU (n=37,222) and MIMIC-IV (n=4,897), the V-specific Direct Effect (VDE) for invasive ventilation rate is small: −0.21 pp (95% CI [−0.23, −0.19]) on eICU and −0.46 pp (95% CI [−0.56, −0.36]) on MIMIC-IV, indicating minimal racial disparity in ventilation initiation mediated by oximetry discrepancy. For ventilation duration, the VDE is more pronounced: 2.3 hrs (95% CI [2.2, 2.5]) on eICU and 7.4 hrs (95% CI [6.9, 7.9]) on MIMIC-IV, with Black patients experiencing longer ventilation. The self-normalized doubly robust estimator exhibits significantly lower variance than the canonical variant across all synthetic and semi-synthetic convergence experiments (Figures 3a–3d).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not present a head-to-head numerical benchmark against competing estimators in the main text (detailed baseline comparisons are deferred to Appendix I). However, it positions its contribution relative to prior work: Miles et al. [30] provide a path-specific estimator with multiple mediators but under parametric assumptions and without self-normalization, which the authors show exhibits substantially higher variance in small-sample ICU settings. Gottlieb et al. [14] quantify the effect of oximetry discrepancy using a simplified NIE* that ignores post-admission mediators W; the authors demonstrate that this approach can exaggerate the magnitude of disparity and even flip its direction compared to the full VDE. Sjoding et al. [45] and Wong et al. [50] establish only statistical associations between race, hidden hypoxemia, and outcomes without causal formalization. The self-normalized variant is adopted for all real-world analyses based on its superior variance properties demonstrated on semi-synthetic eICU data (Figures 3c–3d), where the canonical estimator's confidence intervals are visibly wider at matched sample sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the path-specific causal analysis framework of Miles et al. (Biometrika 2017), which handled multiple mediators under parametric assumptions, into a non-parametric doubly robust setting with a novel self-normalized variant for improved sample efficiency. It also draws on the unified covariate adjustment framework of Jung et al. (NeurIPS 2024) to derive finite-sample guarantees for the estimator. The paper departs from prior ICU disparity studies (Sjoding et al., Wong et al., Gottlieb et al.) that relied on statistical associations or single-mediator regression, by formally isolating the causal pathway through oximetry discrepancy (V) while conditioning on other post-admission mediators (W), and by providing the first finite-sample guarantees for path-specific effect estimators in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the VDE on ventilation rates is minimal (contrary to prior association-based studies suggesting larger disparities) while the effect on ventilation duration is more pronounced (2.3 hrs in eICU, 7.4 hrs in MIMIC-IV) reframes where pulse-oximetry bias actually manifests in clinical decision-making. The observation that ignoring the W mediators (NIE*) can flip the direction of disparity underscores that naive mediation analysis can be misleading, validating the need for proper path-specific decomposition. Open questions include whether the duration effect reflects genuine bias or unmeasured confounding, how the framework extends to repeated ventilation events, and whether the causal graph assumptions hold in other ICU contexts. The self-normalized estimator with finite-sample guarantees is broadly applicable to other multi-mediator fairness problems in low-sample clinical settings.
