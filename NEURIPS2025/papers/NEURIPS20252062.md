## Problem

Standard Transformer attention is permutation-equivariant and agnostic to the underlying domain geometry, making it ill-suited for spherical data where rotational symmetry and topology are physically meaningful. Existing spherical CNNs capture local equivariant features but cannot model long-range dependencies, while prior spherical Transformers on icosahedral grids only exploit discrete graph symmetries rather than the full continuous SO(3) rotational equivariance of the sphere.

## Input / Output

The method consumes signals defined on S² sampled on equiangular or Gaussian grids (e.g., 128×256 equirectangular RGB/depth images or 128×256 Gaussian-grid geopotential and velocity fields), with quadrature weights encoding the non-uniform sampling density. Outputs are per-grid-point predictions: 14-class segmentation masks, metric depth maps (with 15% polar masking), or fluid state fields (geopotential height, vorticity, divergence) after single or multi-step autoregressive rollouts. Because the formulation is a neural operator, the same architecture operates at arbitrary resolutions that admit a valid quadrature rule.

## Method

The paper derives a continuous attention operator on S² by replacing the Euclidean integration domain with the sphere and the Lebesgue measure with the invariant Haar measure, yielding an approximately SO(3)-equivariant attention (Eq. 4). A neighborhood variant (Eq. 5–6) restricts interactions to a geodesic disk of radius θ_cutoff, reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(kN) while preserving equivariance. Discretization via quadrature rules incorporates weights ω_i into both the softmax normalization and the value aggregation, correcting for the non-uniform point density that would otherwise break rotational symmetry. The resulting S² Transformer and S² SegFormer architectures replace patch embeddings with DISCO spherical convolutions, use spectral (spherical-harmonic) positional embeddings, and employ custom CUDA kernels for the sparse neighborhood attention. The approach is validated on three tasks—spherical segmentation, depth estimation, and shallow-water PDE simulation—where spherical variants consistently outperform their Euclidean counterparts, especially in autoregressive rollouts where geometric artifacts accumulate.

## Results

On the Stanford 2D3DS segmentation task (128×256), the S² SegFormer(local) achieves the best IoU of 0.667 and accuracy of 0.971, outperforming the Euclidean SegFormer(local) (IoU 0.637, Acc 0.968) and all external baselines. On the shallow water equations task, the advantage is most dramatic: after 5 autoregressive steps, the S² Transformer(local) achieves L1 error of 0.137 versus 0.933 for the R² Transformer(local) and 3.282 for the full R² Transformer, demonstrating that geometric priors are critical for stable long-horizon PDE rollouts. On spherical depth estimation, the S² SegFormer attains the lowest validation loss (0.935) and L1 error (0.165) among all tested architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EGFormer [40], the S² SegFormer(local) improves IoU from 0.586 to 0.667 (+8.1 points) and accuracy from 0.962 to 0.971 on segmentation, and reduces depth L1 error from 0.182 to 0.165. Against SphericalCNN [14], the S² SegFormer(local) gains 4.5 IoU points (0.667 vs 0.622) and 0.5 accuracy points, while on depth estimation it reduces L1 error from 0.315 to 0.165. Against SphereUFormer [3] at higher resolution (256×512), the S² SegFormer(local,large) achieves IoU 0.748 vs 0.722 and accuracy 0.979 vs 0.886, a substantial 9.3-point accuracy gap. Against LSNO [30] on depth estimation, the S² SegFormer reduces L1 error from 0.248 to 0.165 while matching the W^{1,1} error (7.70 vs 7.72). Across all three tasks, the consistent pattern is that spherical variants outperform their Euclidean counterparts, with the largest margins appearing in the autoregressive PDE simulation where geometric fidelity prevents error accumulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the standard attention mechanism (Vaswani et al.) and its neighborhood variant (Hassani et al.) to the sphere by deriving a continuous formulation and discretizing it with quadrature rules, yielding approximately SO(3)-equivariant attention. It departs from prior spherical transformers (Cho et al., Benny & Wolf) that operated on icosahedral graphs and achieved only permutation equivariance, by instead using geodesic distances and quadrature weights to preserve continuous rotational symmetry. The architectural template follows ViT and SegFormer, with DISCO spherical convolutions replacing patch embeddings and spectral spherical-harmonic embeddings replacing learned positional encodings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first attention mechanism on the sphere with a principled equivariance guarantee, making it directly applicable to weather/climate modeling, 360° perception, and geophysical PDE simulation where pole distortion in equirectangular projections is a persistent failure mode. The dramatic SWE autoregressive results (validation loss ~37 for R² Transformer vs. ~0.06 for S² Transformer after 5 steps) underscore how critical geometric fidelity is for long-horizon rollouts. Open questions include whether the custom CUDA kernels can support mixed precision and distributed memory, and whether the equivariance advantage persists at the resolutions needed for operational weather forecasting.
