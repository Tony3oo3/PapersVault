## Problem

Existing AI-generated text detectors suffer from two critical weaknesses: they degrade sharply on out-of-distribution and adversarially attacked data, and they provide only opaque scores (e.g., a percentage) without verifiable evidence for their decisions. This undermines both real-world reliability and user trust, particularly when false accusations carry significant consequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text passage T as input and produces a binary HWT/LGT label along with a structured evidence bundle: the predicted prompt P, the regenerated text T′ (produced by gpt-3.5-turbo from P), and two calibrated probability scores (p_PTCV and p_RC) fused into a final decision score. All three fine-tuned modules run on the lightweight Phi-3-medium-128k-instruct model (32-layer decoder-only Transformer), deployable on a single Nvidia V100 GPU, with one additional API call to gpt-3.5-turbo for regeneration.

## Method

IPAD reformulates AI-text detection as a multi-step logical reasoning pipeline rather than a direct classification task. A Prompt Inverter, fine-tuned via LoRA on 45,400 prompt-text pairs, reconstructs the most plausible prompt P from the input text T. Two Distinguishers then independently assess consistency: the Prompt-Text Consistency Verifier (PTCV) judges whether T could plausibly be LLM-generated from P, while the Regeneration Comparator (RC) compares T against a regenerated text T′ (produced by gpt-3.5-turbo from P) to check whether both could stem from a similar prompt. Both distinguishers are fine-tuned on 28,800 paired samples using softmax-based binary probability estimation over 'yes'/'no' logits. The final decision fuses the two scores via a weighted ensemble (w=0.45 for PTCV, 0.55 for RC) against a grid-searched threshold τ=0.54. The key insight is that PTCV captures global distributional properties of LLM generation while RC provides local content-level consistency, and their combination yields superior robustness across OOD, attacked, and structured-prompt settings while exposing the full reasoning chain as interpretable evidence.

## Results

On in-distribution data (OUTFOX benchmark), IPAD achieves 99.25% AvgRec and 100% AUROC with gpt-3.5-turbo as both generator and re-generator, surpassing the strongest baseline by 9.05% in AvgRec. On four diverse OOD datasets (arXiv, XSum, Writing Prompts, Yelp Reviews), IPAD achieves 99.38% average AUROC, outperforming the strongest baseline (Binoculars at 86.45%) by 12.93%. Under three adversarial attacks (prompt, paraphrase, perturbation), IPAD maintains 97.13% average AUROC, a 5.48% improvement over the best baseline (RoBERTa-Base at 91.65%). On structured prompts (LongWriter, Code-Feedback, MATH), IPAD reaches 95.27% average MachineRec, exceeding the strongest baseline by 9.87%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPAD decisively outperforms all statistical and zero-shot baselines: DetectLLM (LRR) and DetectLLM (NPR) achieve only 53.37% and 48.37% average AUROC on OOD data respectively, while Fast-DetectGPT scores just 48.35%, all far below IPAD's 99.38%. Among the stronger baselines, Binoculars (86.45% AUROC) and RoBERTa-Base (83.00%) are the closest competitors on OOD data, but IPAD still leads by 12.93% and 16.38% respectively. Under adversarial attacks, RoBERTa-Base is the strongest baseline at 91.65% average AUROC, yet IPAD still achieves 97.13%, with the gap widening under perturbation attacks (98.10% vs. 92.12%). On in-distribution data, IPAD surpasses HC3 and OUTFOX by 9.05% in AvgRec, with the gap widening under DIPPER and OUTFOX attacks where baselines degrade significantly. For prompt extraction, IPAD's Prompt Inverter outperforms both DPIC and PE across all four similarity metrics (e.g., BLEU 0.24 vs. 5.61E-05 for DPIC on LGT), and as a full detection system, IPAD (Version 2) achieves 0.9883–1.0000 average AUROC versus DPIC's 0.9634–0.9878 across ChatGPT, GPT-4, and Claude 3 generators. As a distinguisher, IPAD's PTCV+RC ensemble (99.25% AvgRec) substantially outperforms Sentence-BERT (78.20%), Bart-large-cnn (69.90%), and direct ChatGPT prompting (48.85%).

## Relation to Prior Work

IPAD sits at the intersection of two research threads: prompt extraction (previously studied as a security/privacy problem in works like DPIC, PE, and output2prompt) and regeneration-based detection (RAIDAR, MAGRET, DNA-GPT, TOCSIN). Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes prompt inversion as a detection signal and wraps it in a dual-consistency verification pipeline (PTCV + RC) that has no direct predecessor. The closest conceptual ancestor is the regeneration-comparison family, but IPAD's explicit prompt reconstruction step and the fine-tuned (rather than zero-shot) prompt inverter represent a meaningful architectural departure from both the statistical-curvature methods (DetectGPT, Binoculars) and the encoder-classifier line (RoBERTa, HC3, OUTFOX).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent pain points in AI text detection: poor OOD/adversarial robustness and the absence of human-verifiable evidence. By surfacing the predicted prompt and regenerated text as decision evidence, IPAD offers a practical path toward defensible detection in high-stakes settings (academic integrity, content moderation). Key limitations include the computational overhead of three Phi-3 calls plus an API call per sample, the Prompt Inverter's difficulty with in-context-learning prompts, and the fact that the interpretability study involved only ten participants. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multilingual settings, whether the regeneration step can be replaced by a local model to eliminate the API dependency, and how performance holds as generator LLMs continue to improve.
