## Problem

Classical optimisation theory guarantees monotonic loss decrease for gradient descent only in a small-step-size regime, yet practical deep learning training operates at the 'edge of stability' where the learning rate exceeds the classical threshold and the loss decreases non-monotonically. While the implicit bias of large-step GD toward flatter minima has been observed empirically, convergence rate guarantees have so far been limited to settings where monotonic decrease is eventually restored. This paper provides the first convergence rates for large-step GD in regimes where sustained non-monotonicity persists.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a codimension-1 overparametrised least squares problem (a smooth map f: R^p → R and target y ∈ R), a constant learning rate η, and initial parameters θ₀ in a tubular neighbourhood of the solution manifold M = f⁻¹{y}. It produces explicit convergence rates (linear or power-law) in three distinct regimes determined by η relative to 2/λ(θ*∥), along with a characterisation of which global minimum is reached and its sharpness. Notably, in the supercritical regime the output is not a single converged point but a stable period-2 orbit orthogonal to M centred on the optimally flat minimum.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a normal form for GD in tubular neighbourhood coordinates (θ^∥, θ^⊥) around the solution manifold M, where θ^∥ ∈ M and θ^⊥ ∈ R parametrises the normal direction. The parallel component reduces to Riemannian gradient descent on the sharpness λ along M with a time-varying step size proportional to (θ^⊥)², while the orthogonal component takes the normal form of a period-doubling (flip) bifurcation: θ^⊥ ↦ (1 − ηλ(θ^∥))θ^⊥ + (θ^⊥)³. This decomposition, derived via Taylor expansion of the gradient in tubular coordinates and a standard bifurcation-theory coordinate change, allows the authors to transfer known dynamics of the unperturbed flip map to the coupled system. Under an additional assumption of geodesic strong convexity of λ on a geodesic ball in M (verified for deep scalar factorisation), three convergence theorems are proved: subcritical (transient instability then linear convergence to a suboptimally flat minimum), critical (power-law t^{-1/2} convergence to the optimally flat minimum), and supercritical (linear convergence to a period-2 orbit at the optimally flat point).

## Results

The paper establishes convergence rates for large-step-size GD in three regimes on overparametrised least squares. In the subcritical regime (2/λ(θ*) > η > 2/λ(θ₀)), GD converges linearly at rate O(β^t) to a suboptimally flat global minimum after a finite transient instability of duration τ = O((θ₀^⊥)^{-2} · ((λ(θ₀^∥)−λ*)/(2/η−λ*))^γ). In the critical regime (η = 2/λ(θ*)), GD converges non-monotonically at the power-law rate Θ(t^{−1/2}) to the optimally flat minimum. In the supercritical regime (η > 2/λ(θ*)), GD converges linearly at rate O((1−C₃(ηλ(θ*)−2))^t) to a stable period-2 orbit of amplitude ≈(ηλ(θ*)−2)^{1/2} centred on the optimally flat minimum. Numerical verification on depth-5 scalar factorisation confirms all three regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no head-to-head method comparisons or benchmark tables. The paper positions itself relative to two prior lines of work: (1) general self-stabilisation analyses (Damian et al., 2023; Cohen et al., 2021) which identify the mechanism but do not provide quantitative convergence rates, and (2) architecture-specific analyses (Wu et al., 2023; Cai et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025) which derive rates but only in regimes where monotonic loss decrease is eventually guaranteed. The key novelty is that the critical regime (η = 2/λ(θ*)) with power-law convergence Θ(t^{−1/2}) has not been previously identified or characterised, and the supercritical regime's period-2 orbit convergence rate O((1−C₃(ηλ(θ*)−2))^t) is the first general quantitative theorem of its kind. The paper also provides the first rigorous demonstration that sharpness λ is geodesically strongly convex along the solution manifold for non-convex scalar factorisation problems, a structural observation not made in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesises the 'self-stabilization' intuition of Damian, Nichani, and Lee (2023) with bifurcation theory and Riemannian geometry to derive the first convergence rates for gradient descent at the edge of stability in regimes where monotonic loss decrease never occurs. Prior large-step-size convergence analyses (Wu et al. 2023, Cai et al. 2024, Liu et al. 2025) were restricted to settings where the iterates eventually enter a monotonically decreasing phase; this work extends the analysis to the full range of learning rates, including a previously unidentifed critical regime. The authors position their framework as a 'middle ground' between the architectural generality of self-stabilization analyses and the quantitative precision of architecture-specific convergence proofs, using overparametrisation to guarantee the solution set forms a Riemannian manifold that enables a clean parallel/orthogonal decomposition of the GD dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of three distinct convergence regimes (subcritical, critical, supercritical) with explicit rates provides the first quantitative picture of how GD at the edge of stability selects among global minima of different sharpness, and the critical regime (η = 2/λ*) appears to be entirely new to the literature. The surprising structural result that sharpness is geodesically strongly convex along the solution manifold for non-convex problems like scalar factorisation, if it generalises, could explain the apparent ease of optimisation in overparametrised networks. Key limitations include restriction to codimension-1 problems, the requirement that initialisation lies in a tubular neighbourhood of the solution manifold (unverified for standard initialisation), and treatment of only 2-periodic oscillations rather than the higher-order periodicity and chaos observed empirically at larger learning rates.
