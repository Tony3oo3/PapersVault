## Problem

Task imbalance in multi-task learning causes certain tasks to dominate training while others underperform, yet existing methods rely on crude approximations of convergence speed (e.g., loss differences between consecutive steps) that fail to capture a task's true convergence potential when loss scales and minima are heterogeneous. The paper identifies that no principled theoretical tool existed to characterize the training dynamics of multiple tasks within a shared-parameter network, leaving the root cause of imbalance unaddressed at the level of optimization dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method is modality-agnostic: it consumes standard task inputs (RGB images for segmentation/depth/normals on NYUv2 and CityScapes, molecular graphs for 11-property regression on QM9, 40 binary attribute labels for CelebA, and robot state observations for MetaWorld MT10) and produces the corresponding task outputs. Internally, the method's key intermediate computation is the per-task NTK matrix built from n mini-batch gradients (n=1 for NTKMTL, n=4 for NTKMTL-SR), from which only the maximum eigenvalue of each task's diagonal block K_ii is extracted to compute scalar task weights. The final output is a single weighted aggregated gradient (or loss) used to update shared parameters once per iteration.

## Method

The paper extends Neural Tangent Kernel theory to MTL by defining an extended NTK matrix K̃ as a k×k block matrix whose (i,j) block is the inner product of task-i and task-j Jacobians with respect to shared parameters. Spectral decomposition of K̃ reveals that tasks with larger NTK eigenvalues converge faster, directly explaining task imbalance as a spectral-bias phenomenon across tasks. To counteract this, NTKMTL sets each task's weight as ω_i = √(λ̃/λ_i), where λ_i is the maximum eigenvalue of task i's own NTK block K_ii and λ̃ is the average across tasks, thereby equalizing the effective convergence rates. The efficient variant NTKMTL-SR replaces the full Jacobian with the gradient of each task head with respect to the shared representation z (the output of the shared trunk), which requires only backpropagation through the small task-specific heads; the resulting weights are then applied to the aggregated loss for a single backpropagation through shared parameters per iteration, matching the computational cost of standard loss-oriented methods.

## Results

On NYUv2 (3-task dense prediction), NTKMTL achieves the best Mean Rank of 4.33 and the best performance drop of −6.99% relative to STL, outperforming the previous best (DB-MTL: MR 4.56, Δm% −5.36%). On CelebA (40-task classification), NTKMTL is the only method to achieve a negative Δm% (−0.77%), meaning it outperforms single-task learning on average, with an MR of 4.35. On QM9 (11-task regression), NTKMTL-SR achieves SOTA with MR 4.00 and Δm% 30.7%, surpassing the best baseline (FAMO: MR 5.91, Δm% 38.9%). On the MT10 multi-task RL benchmark, NTKMTL achieves a 0.96±0.03 success rate, competitive with the best baseline Aligned-MTL (0.97±0.05) and well above CAGrad (0.83) and FairGrad (0.84).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTKMTL outperforms all 18 baselines on NYUv2 in both MR (4.33 vs. best baseline DB-MTL at 4.56) and Δm% (−6.99% vs. −5.36%), with particularly strong gains on the surface normal task where most baselines underperform STL. On CelebA (40 tasks), NTKMTL achieves the best Δm% of −0.77% (the only method to beat STL on average) and MR of 4.35, substantially outperforming the strongest baselines FairGrad (Δm% 0.37%, MR 6.92) and IMTL-G (Δm% 0.84%, MR 6.25). On QM9, NTKMTL-SR achieves SOTA (MR 4.00, Δm% 30.7%), beating the best baseline FAMO (MR 5.91, Δm% 38.9%) by a wide margin, while NTKMTL (MR 5.91, Δm% 56.7%) is competitive but trails NTKMTL-SR. On CityScapes, NTKMTL achieves the best Δm% (1.92%) but FairGrad attains a lower MR (2.25 vs. 7.00), indicating a trade-off between balance and per-task ranking. On MT10 (multi-task RL), NTKMTL (0.96±0.03) is competitive with Aligned-MTL (0.97±0.05) and clearly surpasses CAGrad (0.83), FAMO (0.83), FairGrad (0.84), PCGrad (0.72), and MoCo (0.75). Loss-oriented baselines (LS, SI, DWA, UW, RLW) consistently show the worst task imbalance (positive Δm% on NYUv2 and CelebA), while gradient-oriented methods (CAGrad, PCGrad, MGDA) improve balance but still trail NTKMTL on the headline metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework—originally developed for single-task learning dynamics (Jacot et al. 2018, Du et al. 2018, Arora et al. 2019, Bietti & Mairal 2019)—to the multi-task setting by introducing an extended NTK matrix that captures cross-task spectral interactions. It departs from the two dominant MTL families (loss-oriented weighting and gradient projection) by grounding task weighting in the eigenvalue structure of task-specific NTK matrices rather than heuristic loss-ratio or gradient-conflict signals. The shared-representation approximation (NTKMTL-SR) draws on the efficiency principle from prior work like Rotograd and Aligned-MTL that exploits the shared-parameter bottleneck to reduce per-iteration cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded lens for understanding task imbalance as an NTK spectral-bias phenomenon, which could inform future work on task grouping, curriculum design, and off-diagonal interaction analysis that the authors explicitly leave open. Practically, NTKMTL-SR achieves competitive performance at loss-oriented computational cost (single backpropagation), making it accessible for large-scale MTL. Key limitations include reliance on the near-constant-kernel NTK approximation (which may degrade in finite-width or non-infinitesimal-learning-rate regimes), the restriction to diagonal NTK blocks for weight computation, and the inability to apply the SR variant to MTRL where shared/task-specific parameter partitioning is unclear.
