## Problem

Transformer-based MIL pooling architectures have become the default for WSI classification, yet they yield only marginal gains in the low-data regimes that characterize most publicly available pathology datasets. The paper argues that the field has over-invested in architectural complexity while neglecting the fundamental max-vs-mean trade-off in classical pooling, and that this trade-off admits a precise theoretical characterization via a temperature-controlled log-sum-exp (LSE) function with a critical threshold at β_crit = O(log N).

## Input / Output

WSIs are tiled into 256×256 patches at 20× magnification with background discarded; each patch is encoded by a fixed pre-trained ViT (e.g., DINO, UNI, Prov-GigaPath) followed by a trainable MLP and sigmoid to produce per-patch scores q_i ∈ [0,1]. Because the method operates at the instance level (Φ is identity in the MIL decomposition), it directly outputs both a WSI-level class probability and interpretable patch-level cancer probabilities, enabling pathologists to validate the model's reasoning at the patch level.

## Method

The core contribution is Maxsoft pooling, which uses LSE_∞ (i.e., exact max) in the forward pass for inference-time sensitivity while backpropagating the gradient of LSE_β with a moderate β for training stability and generalization—a hard-forward/soft-backward design inspired by BPDA. Theoretically, the paper proves that LSE pooling undergoes a smooth phase transition at β_crit = O(log N): below this threshold, generalization error is bounded by O((nN)^{-1/2} + n^{-1}), while above it, the bound degrades to Ω(n^{-1/2}); conversely, sensitivity (true positive rate) requires β ≥ O(log(N/α)). A complementary PerPatch augmentation strategy independently samples each patch from its original or one of m precomputed augmented variants (rather than applying a single transformation to all patches as in PerSlide), combinatorially increasing training diversity. The β value is selected via validation per dataset, reflecting the Pareto-optimal trade-off between generalization and sensitivity that the theory predicts.

## Results

With the DINO Domain encoder, Maxsoft achieves 0.934 slide-level AUC on CAMELYON16 and 0.983 on CAMELYON17, outperforming all Transformer-based baselines (ABMIL, DSMIL, Snuffy) and non-MIL methods (GTP, PANTHER). With the Prov-GigaPath foundation model encoder, Maxsoft reaches a WSI-level AUC of 1.0 on CAMELYON17 and 0.966 on TCGA-Lung. Adding PerPatch augmentation further improves CAMELYON16 slide AUC to 0.938 and CAMELYON17 to 0.950 while reducing variance. Notably, Maxsoft with DINO Domain surpasses Prov-GigaPath in accuracy while requiring 0.001× less computation and 0.0026× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Maxsoft consistently outperforms all Transformer-based MIL baselines: it beats ABMIL by 11.5 points on CAMELYON16 slide AUC (0.934 vs. 0.819) and by 23.3 points on CAMELYON17 (0.983 vs. 0.750); it surpasses DSMIL by 2.4 points on CAMELYON16 (0.934 vs. 0.910) and by 15.0 points on CAMELYON17 (0.983 vs. 0.833); and it exceeds Snuffy by 18.0 points on CAMELYON16 (0.934 vs. 0.754). Against non-MIL baselines, Maxsoft leads GTP by 33.5 points on CAMELYON16 (0.934 vs. 0.599) and PANTHER by 2.1 points on TCGA-Lung with Prov-GigaPath (0.966 vs. 0.945). Among augmentation methods, PerPatch (0.938 CAMELYON16 slide AUC) outperforms ReMix (0.883), RankMix (0.913), AugDiff (0.801), and SSRDL (0.810), while also reducing run-to-run variance. On TCGA-Lung with DINO Domain, Maxsoft (0.940) edges out DSMIL (0.937) and Snuffy (0.921), and with UNI it achieves 0.942 vs. DSMIL's 0.941, confirming that encoder quality dominates architectural complexity at current dataset scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant trend of Transformer-based MIL pooling (ABMIL, DSMIL, Snuffy, GTP) by arguing that architectural complexity offers marginal gains in the low-data regime typical of public WSI datasets. It builds on classical non-attention pooling (max, mean) and the log-sum-exp function, drawing a conceptual bridge from the Backward Pass Differentiable Approximation (BPDA) technique in adversarial robustness [32] and straight-through estimators [33,34] to the MIL setting. The PerPatch augmentation extends patch-level data augmentation ideas from prior work (AugDiff, SSRDL) but replaces latent generative models with simple precomputed variants and independent per-patch sampling, a previously unexplored strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the field's assumption that attention-based architectures are necessary for competitive WSI classification, showing that encoder quality dominates pooling complexity at current dataset scales. This has practical implications for the majority of research groups without access to the massive datasets (200K+ WSIs) required to justify Transformer training. Open questions remain: β selection still requires dataset-specific validation search, patch-level performance lags behind WSI-level accuracy, and the authors note that standard spatial augmentations fail to capture real clinical distribution shifts. The authors also suggest anomaly detection as a promising direction for data-scarce cancer detection.
