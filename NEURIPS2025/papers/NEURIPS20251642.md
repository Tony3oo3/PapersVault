## Problem

Test-time adaptation for 3D perception in autonomous driving suffers from unstable optimization and sharp minima under sudden distribution shifts (adverse weather, sensor failures). While model merging via linear mode connectivity improves stability, existing approaches like MOS require loading K full checkpoints and performing K forward passes per adaptation step, making them prohibitively expensive for real-time driving. The paper asks whether merging decisions can be made in a compact latent space without accessing full model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251642/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled target-domain inputs (multi-view images resized to 256×704 or LiDAR point clouds) and, at each step, extracts penultimate features from a *frozen* source-model feature extractor, randomly projecting them to a 1024-dimensional fingerprint. These fingerprints serve as keys in a growing codebook whose values are the corresponding fine-tuned checkpoint weights. The output is a sign-consistently merged model whose weights are used to generate pseudo-labeled 3D boxes for self-training, which in turn propagate to downstream tracking, mapping, motion prediction, and planning modules without any additional fine-tuning.

## Method

CodeMerge represents each fine-tuned checkpoint by a low-dimensional fingerprint computed from the frozen source model's penultimate activations via a fixed Gaussian random projection (d′=1024), forming a key-value model codebook. At each adaptation step, ridge leverage scores are computed over the stored fingerprints to rank checkpoint informativeness; the paper proves that under a linear-head approximation, these scores are proportional to the inverse Hessian quadratic form δᵢᵀH⁻¹δᵢ, so high-leverage checkpoints explore novel regions of the loss landscape. The top-K checkpoints (K=5) are then merged using a sign-consistent weighted scheme (inspired by TIES-merging) that masks out parameters whose signs disagree with the majority vote, preventing destructive interference. Because merging decisions rely only on the compact fingerprint matrix rather than full model parameters, CodeMerge requires just one extra forward pass through the frozen encoder per step—reducing GPU memory by 8–21% and runtime by 37–42% compared to MOS—while achieving higher detection, tracking, and planning accuracy across nuScenes-C, KITTI-C, and cross-dataset transfer benchmarks.

## Results

On the nuScenes-C end-to-end AD benchmark (SparseDrive), CodeMerge achieves 0.2334 mAP and 0.4016 NDS for 3D detection, and 0.1907 AMOTA for tracking, outperforming the strongest baseline MOS (0.2028 mAP, 0.3551 NDS, 0.1599 AMOTA) by 13.3%, 13.1%, and 19.3% respectively. On cross-dataset LiDAR detection (nuScenes→KITTI), CodeMerge reaches 77.41 AP_BEV / 58.54 AP_3D, closing 81.3%/73.2% of the gap to the fully supervised oracle and surpassing ST3D by +1.9% AP_BEV and +8.1% AP_3D. Under KITTI-C corruptions, CodeMerge raises mean AP_3D to 58.97, a +7.7% gain over no adaptation and +2.6% over MOS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CodeMerge outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the strongest TTA baseline MOS [8], CodeMerge gains +13.3% mAP and +13.1% NDS on nuScenes-C detection, +19.3% AMOTA on tracking, and +2.6% mean AP_3D on KITTI-C corruptions, while also reducing GPU memory by 21% and runtime by 37.5% on SparseDrive. Tent [44] and CoTTA [46] both underperform or even degrade relative to no adaptation on several corruptions (e.g., Tent drops mAP to 0.1648 vs. 0.1747 for no adaptation on nuScenes-C average), whereas CodeMerge consistently improves. SAR [34] and MemCLR [43] show modest gains over no adaptation but remain 15–20% below CodeMerge in AP_3D on cross-dataset transfer (Table 3). DPO [9] and Reg-TTA3D [58] are competitive on Waymo→KITTI (75.81/55.74 and 81.60/56.03 AP_BEV/AP_3D respectively) but are surpassed by CodeMerge (84.62/66.31). Among domain adaptation baselines, SN [48] and ST3D [56] require multi-epoch training and still trail CodeMerge by 3.3% and 2.8% AP_BEV on nuScenes→KITTI, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

CodeMerge extends the model-merging-for-TTA line initiated by MOS (Model Synergy), which dynamically fuses top-K checkpoints using kernel similarities computed via K forward passes over the current test sample. The key departure is replacing full-model access with compact fingerprints derived from a frozen source model's penultimate features, projected into a low-dimensional space via a fixed Gaussian matrix, and ranking them with ridge leverage scores theoretically linked to the inverse Hessian. This draws on LMC (linear mode connectivity) for the interpolation guarantee, TIES-merging for sign-consistent composition, and leverage-score theory from statistical learning, but repurposes them into a single-pass, memory-efficient merging pipeline that avoids repeated checkpoint loading. The paper also extends TTA from standalone 3D detectors to full end-to-end AD pipelines (SparseDrive, DiffusionDrive, MomAD), a setting the authors note remains unexplored in prior TTA literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making model-merging-based TTA viable for real-time autonomous driving: CodeMerge cuts per-frame latency by ~43% and GPU memory by 8–21% relative to MOS while improving detection, tracking, mapping, and planning simultaneously without touching downstream modules. A notable limitation is that the approach depends on the base model retaining sufficient competence under corruption; architectures like VAD collapse to near-zero mAP before TTA can extract useful self-supervision, capping the method's ceiling. Open questions include whether the fingerprint-based merging generalizes to architectures with non-linear heads, whether the fixed random projection can be replaced by learned (but still lightweight) embeddings, and whether the approach scales to longer adaptation horizons where the codebook grows beyond the current K=5 buffer.
