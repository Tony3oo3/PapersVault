## Problem

No existing algorithm for multi-armed bandits with adversarially chosen delays simultaneously achieves near-optimal regret in both stochastic and adversarial loss regimes. Prior Best-of-Both-Worlds results (Masoudian et al.) leave significant gaps to known lower bounds, particularly in the stochastic setting where the delay-dependent term scales as σ_max/Δ_i rather than the optimal σ_max·Δ_i/K, and in the adversarial setting where an extra Φ* = min{d_max·K^{2/3}, √(D·K^{2/3})} term appears.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives K arms, a known horizon T, and a pluggable adversarial algorithm ALG (e.g., Zimmert-Seldin). At each round it selects one arm and receives the loss only after an adversarially chosen delay d_t, so the agent must act on a sliding window of previously observed feedback B(t). The output is a per-round arm selection together with regret guarantees: Õ(√(KT) + √D) in the adversarial regime and O(Σ log(T)/Δ_i + (σ_max/K)ΣΔ_i) in the stochastic regime, with no a-priori knowledge of which regime applies.

## Method

The algorithm (Delayed SAPO) builds on the SAPO framework of Auer and Chiang, using a successive-elimination core with confidence bounds. Two key subroutines drive the design: BSC (Basic Stochastic Checks) monitors whether importance-sampling estimates remain within widened confidence intervals and whether cumulative regret stays below a stochastic threshold, triggering a switch to ALG upon violation; EAP (Eliminated Arms Processing) maintains a decaying sampling probability for each eliminated arm in discrete phases, doubling the probability on 'phase errors' (observed losses significantly below the elimination-time mean) and halving it on successful phase completion. A critical technical ingredient is a stricter elimination criterion requiring 9 confidence widths of separation, which forces arms to be eliminated in roughly decreasing order of sub-optimality gap (Lemma 4.2: Δ_{i2} ≤ 20Δ_{i1} if i1 is eliminated before i2). This ordering enables the additive delay term to depend on Δ_avg = (1/K)ΣΔ_i rather than Δ_max, matching the stochastic lower bound. Probability banks store samples observed at one sampling probability for reuse in later phases, removing a log(T) factor from the delay-dependent regret term.

## Results

In the adversarial regime, the proposed algorithm achieves regret of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{TK\log T} + \sqrt{D})$, matching the known lower bound $\sqrt{TK} + \sqrt{D}$ up to a logarithmic factor in the first term. In the stochastic regime, the regret is $O\big(\sum_{i:\Delta_i>0}(\log T/\Delta_i + \sigma_{\max}\Delta_i/K)\big)$, which matches the lower bound exactly (up to a $\log K$ factor removable with a more involved analysis). This is the first BoBW algorithm to simultaneously match lower bounds in both regimes under adversarial delays, eliminating the $\Phi^* = \min\{d_{\max}K^{2/3}, \sqrt{DK^{2/3}}\}$ term present in prior BoBW results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior BoBW algorithm of Masoudian et al. [22], this paper eliminates the additive $\Phi^*$ term in the adversarial regime (which can be as large as $\sqrt{DK^{2/3}}$ when a single delay is large) and improves the stochastic delay term from $\sigma_{\max}/(\Delta_i\log K)$ to $\sigma_{\max}\Delta_i/K$, a factor of $K/(\Delta_i^2\log K)$ improvement per arm. Compared to the best stochastic-only algorithm of Joulani et al. [18], the delay term improves from $\sigma_{\max}\Delta_i$ to $\sigma_{\max}\Delta_i/K$, a strict $K$-factor gain. Against the adversarial-only algorithms of Thune et al. [30], Zimmert & Seldin [40], and Gyorgy & Joulani [13], the paper matches the $\sqrt{TK}+\sqrt{D}$ bound up to a $\log T$ factor in the first term, while also providing the stochastic guarantee simultaneously. The stochastic bound matches the lower bound of Lancewicki et al. [19] (constant-delay case) and the adversarial bound matches the lower bound of Masoudian et al. [21], making this the first algorithm to close both gaps simultaneously. The only remaining gap is the $\log T$ factor in the adversarial $\sqrt{TK}$ term, which is independent of the delay structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAPO algorithm of Auer and Chiang [1], which uses an adaptive 'start stochastic, switch to adversarial' strategy, and integrates it with a successive elimination framework [11] adapted for delayed feedback. The key prior BoBW result for delayed MAB is Masoudian et al. [22], whose algorithm carries an additive Φ* = min{d_max·K^{2/3}, √(D·K^{2/3})} term in both regimes and a weaker stochastic delay dependence of σ_max/(Δ_i·log K). This paper departs from that line by eliminating Φ* entirely and replacing the stochastic delay term with (1/K)·σ_max·Δ_i, while also improving upon the best stochastic-only algorithm of Joulani et al. [18] by a factor of K in the delay-dependent term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first BoBW algorithm for delayed MAB that simultaneously matches the known lower bounds in both stochastic and adversarial regimes, closing a gap that Masoudian et al. left open. The stochastic improvement (factor of K over Joulani et al.) is significant even outside the BoBW setting, as it tightens the best known bound for stochastic losses under adversarial delays. The paper notes that the simpler 'start adversarial' paradigm used by Masoudian et al. remains an open question for achieving optimality in the delayed case, and that structured delays (i.i.d., payoff-dependent) could yield further improvements. The algorithm requires knowing T in advance or incurs an extra log factor, a practical limitation compared to any-time guarantees.
