## Problem

Constrained alignment of LLMs requires maximizing a primary reward while satisfying secondary utility constraints (e.g., safety), but existing iterative primal-dual methods suffer from policy nonconvergence, and non-iterative one-shot dual-based methods lack optimality guarantees in the LLM parameter space. The paper asks whether constrained alignment methods operating in the non-convex LLM parameter space can provably find an optimal constrained LLM policy, and provides an affirmative answer within a Lagrangian dual framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained reference LLM (e.g., Alpaca-7b or LLaMA2-7B), a reward model and m utility models (e.g., Beaver-7b for helpfulness and safety scoring), a prompt dataset, and constraint thresholds b_i specifying minimum relative utility improvements over the reference model. It outputs a sequence of aligned LLM policies {θ*(t)}_{t=1}^T, where each policy is obtained by maximizing the Lagrangian at a progressively refined dual variable. The LLM policy optimization step is implemented via DPO with pseudo-preferences generated from a synthetic Bradley-Terry model over the composite reward r + λ^T g, making the method compatible with standard preference-learning pipelines.

## Method

The paper proposes CAID (Constrained Alignment via Iterative Dualization), which alternates between a dual subgradient step (projected gradient descent on the dual variable λ) and an LLM policy optimization step (maximizing the Lagrangian L(π_θ, λ) over the parameter space Θ using DPO or PPO). This generalizes one-shot safety alignment to multi-shot: the LLM is re-aligned at each iteration with an updated dual variable, with the one-shot solution providing a zero-cost warm start. Theoretically, the paper bounds the primal-dual gap between the parametrized dual value D_p* and the distribution-space primal value P* as O(ν), where ν is the LLM parametrization gap, and further quantifies the reward and utility optimality gaps of the learned policy π_p*(λ_p*) as O(√ν) under local strong convexity of the dual function. The key insight is that the dual function D(λ) = β E_x[log Z(x;λ)] is strictly convex and smooth under a mild constraint-span assumption, enabling efficient dual optimization while the non-convexity of the LLM parameter space is handled by the parametrization gap.

## Results

On the PKU-SafeRLHF-30K single-constraint safety alignment task, the multi-shot CAID method aligns more closely with all tested safety thresholds (b = 3–9) compared to the one-shot baseline, while achieving a higher empirical Pareto trade-off curve between helpfulness and safety. On the Anthropic HH-RLHF multi-constraint task (harmlessness + humor), CAID satisfies both constraints across 14 threshold combinations, with actual improvements lying close to the expected reference lines. Helpfulness improvements range from +1.11 (loosest constraints: b_harmless=0.20, b_humor=0.20) down to −0.02 (tightest: b_harmless=1.00, b_humor=1.00), confirming the expected trade-off between constraint tightness and primary reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the one-shot safety alignment method of Huang et al. [21], which computes a single optimal dual variable in the distribution space and then trains the LLM once. CAID (multi-shot) outperforms one-shot on both constraint satisfaction and the helpfulness–safety trade-off: in Figure 1a, one-shot tends to undershoot for small thresholds (b=3,4) and overshoot for large ones (b=8,9), while multi-shot tracks the target thresholds more closely across the full range. In Figure 1b, multi-shot achieves a strictly higher Pareto frontier, meaning it delivers more helpfulness at the same safety level (and vice versa) compared to one-shot. The paper also includes two single-objective DPO baselines (DPO_s optimizing only safety, DPO_h optimizing only helpfulness) as reference points; these occupy the extremes of the trade-off curve and confirm that multi-shot achieves a superior balance. In the multi-constraint setting, no direct one-shot comparison is provided, but CAID satisfies both harmlessness and humor constraints simultaneously, which the one-shot approach is not designed to handle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-shot safety alignment framework of Huang et al. [21], which computes an optimal dual variable in the distribution space via a closed-form Lagrangian maximizer, by generalizing it to an iterative multi-shot scheme (CAID) that alternates between dual descent and LLM policy optimization. It departs from iterative primal-dual methods used in safe RLHF [11] and constrained RLHF [31], which the authors note suffer from policy nonconvergence, and from stepwise alignment [44], which uses approximate dual variables. The key conceptual departure is providing the first rigorous analysis of the gap between distribution-space optimality and LLM parameter-space optimality, framed through a 'parametrization gap' assumption, drawing on constrained learning theory [7,8,15] rather than the constrained MDP literature that prior RLHF work relies on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee that dual-based alignment methods can recover an optimal constrained LLM policy in the parameter space, up to a parametrization gap, which addresses a fundamental question left open by prior one-shot and stepwise methods. The multi-shot approach empirically improves constraint satisfaction and the helpfulness-safety Pareto frontier over one-shot, but validation is limited to 7B models on two datasets. Open questions include whether the parametrization gap assumption holds for larger, more overparameterized models, how the method behaves under complex or non-linear constraints, and whether the theoretical bounds are tight in practice. The framework also leaves robustness analysis, sample complexity, and integration with supervised fine-tuning for future work.
