## Problem

Determining the minimum width for deep, narrow MLPs to achieve universal approximation under the uniform norm has a significant gap: existing bounds range from max(d_x+1, d_y) to d_x+d_y, with no tight characterization in general. This paper addresses the gap by reframing the width question as a purely topological problem about the dimension of diffeomorphisms needed to approximate arbitrary continuous functions.

## Input / Output

The framework takes input dimension d_x, output dimension d_y, and an activation function σ (Leaky-ReLU, ReLU, or general C¹ activations satisfying a non-degeneracy condition) as parameters, and produces the minimum width w_min(d_x, d_y, σ) along with explicit upper and lower bounds. The approximation target is any continuous function f ∈ C(K, R^{d_y}) on a compact domain K ⊂ R^{d_x}, measured in the uniform norm. The central output is a network-independent topological quantity w(d_x, d_y) defined via the closure of smooth embeddings, which equals the Leaky-ReLU minimum width exactly.

## Method

The paper reduces the MLP width problem to a geometrical question in two steps. First, it proves that deep narrow MLPs of width d (Leaky-ReLU), d+1 (ReLU), or d+2 (general activations) can uniformly approximate any C²-diffeomorphism on R^d, by showing they approximate single-coordinate transformations via an inductive construction using affine coupling flows. Second, it defines w(n,m) as the minimum ambient dimension l such that projections of the closure of smooth embeddings Emb([0,1]^n, R^l) onto the first m coordinates cover all of C([0,1]^n, R^m), then uses Palais' extension theorem to show any such embedding extends to a global diffeomorphism approximable by the MLP. The Whitney embedding theorem yields the upper bound w(n,m) ≤ max(2n+1, m), while algebraic topology tools—specifically the Borsuk-Ulam theorem, Hurewicz homomorphism, and winding number arguments on a double-cover map—establish the nontrivial lower bound w(2,2) = 4, disproving the previously claimed max(d_x+1, d_y) formula.

## Results

The paper establishes that deep, narrow MLPs with width max(2d_x+1, d_y) + α(σ) (where α = 0 for Leaky-ReLU, 1 for ReLU, 2 for general activations) achieve universal approximation of continuous functions under the uniform norm. It proves the exact optimal minimum width w(2,2) = 4 for both ReLU and Leaky-ReLU, and w(2,3) = 4 for Leaky-ReLU, using algebraic topology (winding numbers, Borsuk-Ulam, Hurewicz theorem). For even k, it shows w(k, 2k−1) = 2k is optimal, strictly improving upon both the d_x + d_y and 2d_x + 1 bounds in those regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Hanin (2019), which gave the upper bound d_x + d_y for ReLU, this paper's bound max(2d_x+1, d_y)+1 is strictly tighter whenever d_y > d_x+1 (e.g., for d_x=2, d_y=10: 11 vs. 12). Against Kidger & Lyons (2020), whose bound was d_x+d_y+1 for general activations, the new bound max(2d_x+1, d_y)+2 again improves in the large-d_y regime. The paper confirms and sharpens Kim et al. (2024)'s lower bound w_min ≥ d_y + 1_{d_x<d_y≤2d_x} by matching it exactly for Leaky-ReLU when d_y = 2d_x (giving w_min = 2d_x+1). It refutes Li et al. (2023)'s claimed formula max(d_x+1, d_y) + 1_{d_x+1=d_y} by proving w(2,2) = 4 > max(2+1, 2) = 3, showing the L_p-norm result does not transfer to the uniform norm. The exact value w(2,2) = 4 also confirms Park et al. (2021)'s observation that the uniform norm requires more width than L_p norms, and extends it from d_x=1 to d_x=2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimum-width-for-universal-approximation line initiated by Hanin (2019), Kidger & Lyons (2020), and refined by Park et al. (2021), Cai (2023), and Kim et al. (2024), but introduces a fundamentally different analytical strategy: reducing the width question to a purely topological quantity w(n,m) via diffeomorphism approximation. The framework draws on Teshima et al. (2020) for decomposing diffeomorphisms into single-coordinate transformations, Palais (1960) for extending embeddings to global diffeomorphisms, and classical algebraic topology (Borsuk-Ulam, Hurewicz, winding numbers) for lower bounds. It explicitly corrects Li et al. (2023), who claimed max(d_x+1, d_y) + indicator was optimal, by showing the uniform norm genuinely requires more width in certain regimes (e.g., w(2,2)=4 rather than 3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes the gap between lower and upper bounds in several concrete settings (w(2,2)=4, w(2,3)=4, w(k,2k-1)=2k for even k) and provides a general upper bound of max(2d_x+1, d_y)+α(σ) that improves on prior d_x+d_y-type bounds when the output dimension is large. The topological framework is promising for future exact characterizations, but the odd-k case and higher-dimensional intersection settings remain open due to the lack of suitable tools for analyzing self-intersection structures of uniformly perturbed functions. For practitioners, the main takeaway is that the uniform norm is strictly harder than L_p norms for narrow networks, and the previously claimed optimal formula from Li et al. (2023) is incorrect.
