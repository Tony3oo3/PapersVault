## Problem

Existing LLM safety alignment methods (SafeRLHF, SACPO) impose expected safety constraints, requiring only the average harmlessness score to stay below a threshold. This permits 'safety compensation,' where safe prompt-response pairs offset unsafe ones, leaving individual responses potentially harmful. The paper asks whether safety can be guaranteed for nearly all prompt-response pairs rather than merely in expectation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252120/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts from the PKU-SafeRLHF training set and generates text responses via the policy model (Alpaca-7B or Llama3.2-3B). Trajectory-level reward R(x,y) and cost C(x,y) signals from frozen beaver-7B reward/cost models are decomposed into token-level rewards and costs by assigning the full signal at the final token and incorporating per-token KL divergence terms. The batch is partitioned into safe (C(x,y)≤0) and unsafe sub-batches, which receive different optimization objectives.

## Method

RePO replaces the expected safety constraint E[C(x,y)]≤0 with a critical safety constraint C(x,y)≤0 for every prompt-response pair, then proves (Theorem 1) an equivalent min-max rectified formulation using the operator {·}⁺=max{·,0}. The policy is updated via a rectified policy gradient: safe samples in the batch optimize only the clipped PPO helpfulness objective, while unsafe samples receive a penalty-weighted combination of helpfulness and harmlessness objectives scaled by a non-decreasing rectified penalty λ. Unlike Lagrangian dual updates in SafeRLHF, λ only increases (capped at λ_max) and does not rely on strong duality, which can fail under strict constraints. The algorithm follows an actor-critic structure with separate reward and cost critic models updated via mean-squared temporal-difference loss, and GAE-based advantage estimation for the clipped surrogate objectives. Theorem 2 provides O(√T) convergence guarantees for both reward optimality and safety violation under standard boundedness and feasibility assumptions.

## Results

On Alpaca-7B, RePO achieves a safety rate (S.R.) of 96.08% with a harmlessness score of −13.85 and a positive helpfulness gain of +1.01, outperforming SafeRLHF (S.R. 90.58%, ΔHelp −0.71) and SACPO (S.R. 80.72%, ΔHelp −0.19). On Llama3.2-3B, RePO reaches 91.46% S.R. with harmlessness −12.43 and ΔHelp +0.16, while SafeRLHF achieves only 76.74% S.R. (ΔHelp −1.20) and SACPO drops to 53.73% S.R. (ΔHelp +2.90 but harmlessness still positive at 4.12). In GPT-4 evaluation, RePO is preferred 78.03% of the time on Alpaca-7B (vs. 65.62% for SafeRLHF) and 71.12% on Llama3.2-3B (vs. 60.11% for SafeRLHF), with GPT-4 safety rates of 90.04% and 89.59% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RePO consistently outperforms both baselines on safety metrics across both model scales. Against SafeRLHF (PPO-Lagrangian with expected safety constraints), RePO improves the safety rate by 5.5 pp on Alpaca-7B (96.08% vs. 90.58%) and by 14.7 pp on Llama3.2-3B (91.46% vs. 76.74%), while also reversing the helpfulness trade-off from negative to positive (ΔHelp +1.01 vs. −0.71 on Alpaca-7B; +0.16 vs. −1.20 on Llama3.2-3B). Against SACPO (sequential DPO alignment), RePO achieves substantially higher safety rates (96.08% vs. 80.72% on Alpaca-7B; 91.46% vs. 53.73% on Llama3.2-3B), and on Llama3.2-3B SACPO's harmlessness score remains positive (4.12), meaning the model is still unsafe on average despite its higher helpfulness gain (+2.90). In GPT-4 pairwise evaluation, RePO is preferred over SafeRLHF 78.03% of the time on Alpaca-7B and 71.12% on Llama3.2-3B, confirming that the rectified critical-safety design yields responses that are both safer and more helpful in a human-judged sense.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SafeRLHF (Dai et al., ICLR 2023), which first framed LLM safety alignment as a constrained RLHF problem using PPO-Lagrangian with an expected safety constraint (average cost ≤ 0). The key conceptual departure is replacing this expected constraint with a 'critical safety constraint' requiring every individual prompt-response pair to be safe, motivated by the identified 'safety compensation' phenomenon where safe pairs mask unsafe ones in the average. The rectified reformulation (Theorem 1) draws from online convex optimization with hard constraints (Guo et al., 2022), and the practical algorithm adapts PPO's clipped surrogate with a batch-splitting strategy to handle the non-smooth rectification operator. The paper also positions against DPO-based safety methods (SACPO, Huang et al.) that share the same expected-constraint limitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'safety compensation' issue has direct deployment implications: a model that is 'safe on average' can still generate harmful responses for specific adversarial prompts, undermining user trust and safety guarantees. The practical limitation that RePO requires additional critic models of comparable size to the policy (doubled memory/compute) and has only been validated on 3B–7B models constrains near-term adoption. The acknowledged over-refusal trade-off (6.8% → 20.4% on XSTest) raises open questions about whether the critical constraint is too strict for production settings. Future work on critic-free variants (e.g., combining RePO's rectified penalty with GRPO) and scaling to larger models would strengthen the practical case.
