## Problem

Multi-part point cloud pose estimation—spanning both pairwise registration and complex shape assembly—remains fragmented across task-specific architectures that assume known categories, canonical targets, or manual correspondences. The problem is inherently under-constrained by part symmetry and interchangeability, causing conventional per-part pose regression to produce locally valid but globally inconsistent assemblies. No prior method unifies these tasks under a single parameterization that intrinsically handles symmetry without hand-engineered augmentation or labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered set of unposed part-level point clouds {X_i ∈ R^{3×N_i}} with no known correspondences, categories, or semantic labels; the first part (or a randomly rotated anchor in the anchor-free variant) fixes the global frame. It outputs per-part rigid transformations T_i ∈ SE(3) recovered via a Procrustes/SVD step between the input part and the predicted assembled point cloud, along with the full assembled point cloud in the global frame. The flow model operates on dense per-point coordinates (M_i sampled points per part) rather than compact pose vectors, and inference requires K=20 Euler integration steps from t=1 to t=0.

## Method

Rectified Point Flow formulates pose estimation as conditional generative modeling: a DiT-based flow network predicts a per-point velocity field in 3D Euclidean space that transports independently sampled Gaussian noise toward the assembled point cloud, conditioned on overlap-aware features from a frozen PointTransformerV3 encoder pretrained on point-wise overlap detection. The straight-line interpolation between noise and target (Rectified Flow) guarantees invariance under the assembly symmetry group G (rotational stabilizers × identical-part permutations), so the model learns all symmetries and interchangeabilities without explicit labels or augmentation. The DiT architecture alternates part-wise self-attention (intra-part geometry) and global self-attention (inter-part context) with AdaLayerNorm modulated by timestep, and the encoder features are concatenated with multi-frequency Fourier positional encodings of coordinates, normals, and part indices. This unified formulation enables joint training across six heterogeneous datasets (from 84 to 35K samples), where shared geometric priors boost performance on each individual task, and the SVD-based Procrustes recovery step contracts the high-dimensional prediction space to yield generalization bounds matching those of 6-DoF regression.

## Results

On multi-part shape assembly, Rectified Point Flow (Joint) achieves 7.4° RE and 91.1% Part Accuracy on BreakingBad-Everyday, 13.2° RE and 3.0 cm TE on TwoByTwo, and 21.8° RE and 53.9% Part Accuracy on PartNet-Assembly, substantially outperforming the closest competitor GARF (66.9° RE, 25.7% Part Acc on PartNet-Assembly). On pairwise registration, the method reaches 97.7% Recall@5° and 99.0% Recall@1cm on TUD-L, and 0.93° RE / 0.002 TE on ModelNet-40, surpassing all registration-specific baselines. Joint training across all six datasets consistently improves over dataset-specific training, e.g., reducing TwoByTwo rotation error by ~30% (18.7°→13.2°) and ModelNet-40 RE by ~32% (1.37°→0.93°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GARF [11], the closest competitor using 6-DoF pose regression with fracture-aware pretraining, Rectified Point Flow wins decisively on PartNet-Assembly (21.8° vs 66.9° RE; 53.9% vs 25.7% Part Acc) and TwoByTwo (13.2° vs 22.1° RE; 3.0 vs 7.1 cm TE), while matching or slightly trailing on BreakingBad-Everyday Part Accuracy (91.1% vs 93.0%) but achieving lower RE (7.4° vs 9.9°). On pairwise registration, the method outperforms all dedicated registration baselines: GeoTransformer [32] (88.0% vs 97.7% Recall@5° on TUD-L; 1.58° vs 0.93° RE on ModelNet-40), Diff-RPMNet [22] (90.0% vs 97.7% Recall@5°), RPMNet [30], and DCPNet [31]. Among assembly baselines, PuzzleFusion++ [25] (38.1° RE on BreakingBad) and Jigsaw [60] (42.3° RE) are far behind, while SE(3)-Assembly [49] and MSN [48] are competitive only on the easier TwoByTwo task. The paper also shows that GARF, despite being retrained on registration datasets, fails to generalize (42.5° RE on ModelNet-40), whereas Rectified Point Flow's unified formulation transfers seamlessly across tasks. On IKEA-Manual, the method achieves 33.2% Part Accuracy overall and 29.9% on Chair, where all category-specific baselines (B-LSTM, RGL-Net, Huang et al.) collapse to ≤6.9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Rectified Flow and Conditional Flow Matching from image generation to 3D point cloud pose estimation, departing fundamentally from the dominant paradigm of regressing 6-DoF pose vectors (Euler angles, quaternions, or 6D/7D representations) in SE(3). The closest prior work, GARF, also uses flow matching but still performs per-part 6-DoF pose regression; Rectified Point Flow instead predicts the full assembled point cloud in Euclidean space and recovers poses via Procrustes/SVD, which the authors show is intrinsically invariant to assembly symmetries (Theorem 1). The overlap-aware encoder pretraining is positioned as a lightweight, simulation-free alternative to GARF's fracture-based physical simulation, and the unified formulation deliberately collapses pairwise registration and multi-part assembly into a single conditional generative problem rather than treating them as separate tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified flow-based parameterization offers a practical path toward a single model that handles diverse registration and assembly tasks with shared geometric priors, which is directly relevant to robotic manipulation and digital manufacturing. The intrinsic symmetry handling (no symmetry labels or hand-crafted augmentations needed) addresses a long-standing pain point in multi-part assembly. However, the method remains object-centric and struggles with cluttered scenes, occlusion, and objects exceeding a certain geometric complexity; it can also produce geometrically plausible but mechanically nonfunctional assemblies. Open questions include whether the approach scales to scene-level multi-body registration, whether mechanical functionality constraints can be incorporated, and whether the dense point-cloud prediction is necessary or whether sparser representations could achieve similar symmetry robustness at lower cost.
