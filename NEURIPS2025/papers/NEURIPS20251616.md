## Problem

Multimodal contrastive learning (MCL) is typically trained on all modality pairs simultaneously, but in practice multimodal data is collected incrementally and training from scratch is prohibitively expensive. Existing continual learning methods cannot handle the intermodal complexity and task-agnostic constraints inherent to MCL, where the contrastive alignment objective remains consistent across steps but different modality pairs are introduced sequentially. This paper formalizes the resulting problem as Continual Multimodal Contrastive Learning (CMCL) and provides the first dedicated solution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of bi-modal datasets (e.g., video-text at step 1, video-audio at step 2, text-audio at step 3) along with frozen pre-trained modality-binding encoders (ImageBind, LanguageBind, or UniBind) that produce fixed-dimension embeddings (768 or 1024). Trainable linear projection matrices W^m ∈ R^{d×d} are initialized as identity matrices on top of these encoders, so the model starts from the prior's alignment quality. At inference, a single-modality sample is encoded and projected through the learned W^m to yield an aligned representation in the joint space, from which cross-modal retrieval rankings (Recall@k) or class labels (via nearest text-category vector) are produced.

## Method

The core contribution is a Dual-sided Null Space (DNS) gradient projection that ensures new updates do not disturb previously learned modality alignments. Theoretically, the paper shows that stability requires the global parameter update W̃ to satisfy (Z_{t-1}^{m₁})ᵀ W̄ Z_{t-1}^{m₂} = 0, whose closest solution is W̄ = W̃ − P′W̃P, where P and P′ are column-space projectors built from prior-step features of the two interacting modalities. For practical per-encoder updates, this decomposes into local projections ΔW_t^{m₁} = ∇W_t^{m₁} − P̃∇W_t^{m₁}P′ and ΔW_t^{m₂} = ∇W_t^{m₂} − P̃′∇W_t^{m₂}P, where the 'dual-sided' aspect means each gradient is constrained by both its own modality's input feature space and the interacted modality's output feature space. Projectors are constructed via truncated SVD of accumulated (running-average) feature covariance matrices, updated only at step boundaries. Two theoretical bounds are provided: the stability loss is O(η²)·‖Z‖²·F(∇W), and the plasticity condition reduces to a high-order term o(η)/η ≤ 0, both empirically verified.

## Results

Across three modality-binding backbones (ImageBind, LanguageBind, UniBind) and 7 datasets, DNS consistently achieves the highest classification accuracy (52.52%, 64.07%, 52.86% respectively) and best retrieval Recall@10 (40.89, 42.44, 41.44). Critically, DNS achieves near-zero or even positive backward transfer (BWT_A = -0.02, -0.09, +0.31), indicating virtually no catastrophic forgetting, while the strongest baselines (CILA, C-FLAT) still suffer 2.6–4.4% accuracy drops. On retrieval, DNS leads all Recall@k metrics by 0.5–1.5 points over the best baseline across all backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNS outperforms all six baselines on classification accuracy across all three backbones, with the largest margins over GEM (e.g., +11.5% on ImageBind) and Vanilla (e.g., +10.7% on LanguageBind). Among the strongest continual-learning baselines, CILA achieves the best BWT_A (-3.20 to -2.63) but still lags DNS by 1.8–4.8% in accuracy; C-FLAT is competitive on retrieval (R@10 within 0.5–1.0 points) yet DNS leads on both accuracy and BWT. EWC and Co²L show moderate improvements over Vanilla but are consistently outperformed by DNS on every metric. Notably, DNS is the only method achieving near-zero or positive BWT_A (−0.02, −0.09, +0.31), confirming that the dual-sided null-space projection effectively eliminates catastrophic forgetting while maintaining full plasticity on new modality pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multimodal contrastive learning (CLIP, ImageBind, LanguageBind, UniBind) and continual learning (GEM, EWC, Co²L, CILA, C-FLAT), but explicitly positions itself as carving out a previously unaddressed problem: continual training across a sequence of modality pairs. The gradient-projection mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from null-space continual learning (Adam-NSCL), but the authors note that prior null-space methods operate on layer-wise features and cannot handle the inter-modality coupling inherent in contrastive alignment. The key methodological departure is the 'dual-sided' projection: gradients are constrained simultaneously by the own-modality input space and the interacted-modality output space, a structure that naturally emerges from the bilinear form of the alignment objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formally defining CMCL with explicit stability and plasticity criteria and providing two theoretical upper bounds, this work establishes a foundational framework that the multimodal and continual learning communities can build upon. The replay-free nature of DNS (only requiring SVD-based projectors updated per step) makes it practically attractive compared to memory-buffer baselines. However, the experimental validation is limited to linear probes on frozen pre-trained encoders across seven relatively small datasets, leaving open whether the approach extends to full fine-tuning of deep encoders, longer sequences of modality pairs, or larger-scale settings. A key open question is whether the linear parameterization assumption (W ∈ R^{d×d}) is sufficient for the theoretical guarantees to hold in deeper, non-linear architectures.
