## Problem

Deploying LLMs on memory-constrained hardware is bottlenecked by the quadratic KV cache of self-attention, yet retraining new architectures from scratch requires trillions of tokens and is prohibitively expensive. Existing post-training hybrid approaches (e.g., MambaInLlama, Llamba) either retain too much KV cache or suffer significant accuracy drops when attention is replaced too aggressively. The paper asks: can one compose extremely efficient hybrid models directly from existing pre-trained Transformers using only 7–11B training tokens and a same-size teacher, while preserving Transformer-level accuracy?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252617/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The models consume text sequences (up to 2048 tokens during training) and autoregressively generate text tokens, identical in interface to standard Llama models. The critical difference is in memory: Mamba2 layers require zero KV cache, while MLA layers store only a compressed latent vector of rank r_kv (128 for 1B/3B, 160 for 8B) plus a small RoPE component, reducing total KV cache to 2–7% of the base Transformer. At inference, the 8B model processes 131k-token contexts within ~63–105 GB of peak memory on a single MI300X GPU, where the base Llama and MambaInLlama run out of memory beyond 16–32k tokens.

## Method

Zebra-Llama composes hybrid architectures by interleaving Mamba2 (zero-KV SSM) and MLA (low-rank latent attention) layers, initialized from a pre-trained Llama via structured SVD-based weight mapping for MLA and attention-to-SSM parameter transfer for Mamba2. A lightweight Intermediate Layer Distillation (ILD) stage then aligns each layer's hidden-state output with the corresponding Transformer layer using MSE loss on ~1.4B tokens, correcting the representational gap introduced by the architectural swap. The final layer placement is determined by SMART (Sensitivity Measure-Aware Replacement of Transformer Layers), which computes per-layer KL-divergence sensitivity scores and selects MLA positions via a three-step procedure: terminal preservation (highest-sensitivity layers at the edges), near-uniform intermediate distribution (constrained gap enumeration), and maximal cumulative sensitivity selection. End-to-end knowledge distillation (KL loss against the teacher) followed by DPO completes training, requiring only 7–11B total tokens and a same-size 8B teacher to achieve 25×–50× KV cache compression with >97% of base-model zero-shot accuracy.

## Results

On the LM Harness zero-shot benchmark, Zebra-Llama achieves 52.03% average (1B, 4MLA-12M2), 59.75% (3B, 6MLA-22M2), and 64.12% (8B, 8MLA-24M2), matching or exceeding the base Llama models (51.87%, 58.13%, 65.53%) while reducing KV cache to 3.91%, 2.01%, and 2.73% of the original (25.6×, 49.8×, and 36.6× compression). In few-shot evaluation, Zebra-Llama-8B (16MLA-16M2) achieves 66.78% average with only 5.47% KV cache, surpassing Minitron-8B (62.44%) by 7% while using 8× fewer training tokens and a smaller teacher (8B vs. 15B). Inference throughput reaches 3.9× that of base Llama-3.1-8B and 3.28× that of MambaInLlama-8B, with peak memory as low as 17% of the base model at 16K context length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MambaInLlama [5], Zebra-Llama achieves comparable or better zero-shot accuracy (52.03 vs. 52.31 at 1B; 64.12 vs. 66.03 at 8B) while using 12.8× and 9.1× smaller KV cache, a much smaller teacher (8B vs. 70B at 8B scale), and fewer training tokens (11B vs. 20B), plus 1.4–3.3× higher inference throughput. Against X-EcoMLA [12], Zebra-Llama matches accuracy (52.03 vs. 52.01 at 1B; 64.12 vs. 64.16 at 8B) with up to 3.4× less KV cache (3.91% vs. 9.37%). Against Llamba [11], Zebra-Llama substantially outperforms on zero-shot (52.03 vs. 51.34 at 1B; 64.12 vs. 63.30 at 8B) and few-shot (66.78 vs. 63.44 at 8B) while using a smaller teacher and fewer tokens. Against Minitron [13], Zebra-Llama-8B surpasses by 7% in few-shot average (66.78 vs. 62.44) with 8× fewer training tokens (11B vs. 94B), >12× smaller KV cache (2.73% vs. 66.67%), and a smaller teacher (8B vs. 15B). Against pre-trained-from-scratch hybrids (Samba [3], Hymba [8], Mamba-2-Hybrid [9]), Zebra-Llama achieves competitive or superior accuracy using only 7–11B tokens versus 1.5T–3.5T tokens—a 214×–500× reduction in training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two post-training lines: the MambaInLlama/MOHAWK/Llamba lineage that distills Transformer knowledge into pure SSM architectures, and the X-EcoMLA line that upcycles attention into low-rank MLA. Rather than committing to one extreme (pure Mamba2 or pure MLA), Zebra-Llama strategically interleaves both, using a novel SMART sensitivity-based layer selection to decide placement. The SVD-based MLA initialization follows X-EcoMLA, the Mamba2 weight mapping follows MambaInLlama, and the ILD stage adapts MOHAWK's intermediate-layer alignment — but the key departure is the end-to-end composition pipeline (structured init → ILD → SMART → SFT → DPO) that avoids the trillions-of-tokens cost of pre-training hybrid models from scratch (Jamba, Hymba, Samba).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a concrete, reproducible recipe for building 25–50× KV-cache-compressed hybrid models using only 7–11B tokens and a same-size teacher, making efficient LLM deployment accessible without multi-node pre-training budgets. The finding that an 8B teacher suffices for an 8B student (vs. 70B in MambaInLlama) challenges the assumption that larger teachers are necessary, though the authors note diminishing returns and a 'capacity gap' at extreme size ratios. Key limitations include the 2048-token training sequence length (limiting long-context performance), reliance on a strong teacher, and the observed MMLU formatting gap inherited from SSM layers. Open questions include scaling to MoE architectures, teacher-free distillation, and whether the SMART selection generalizes beyond the Llama/Qwen families tested.
