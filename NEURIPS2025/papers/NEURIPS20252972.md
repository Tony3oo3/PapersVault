## Problem

Existing spatial intelligence benchmarks for MLLMs predominantly employ VQA-style tasks that evaluate abstract metric-level spatial understanding (distances, coordinates, object relations) but fail to assess whether agents can translate that understanding into concrete, executable 3D construction plans. This gap between spatial cognition and task execution remains unexplored, particularly in open-ended, creative architectural design settings where no single correct answer exists.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252972/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Agents receive multi-modal instructions ranging from free-text building prompts with material specifications, step-by-step relative-coordinate layer descriptions (e.g., 'Layer 2: red_wool: [(0,0),(1,0)]'), visual reference screenshots of target structures, and geometric stimuli images for mental-rotation VQA. The primary output is an executable blueprint matrix—a 3D coordinate grid specifying block type at each (x,y,z) position—which is parsed by the mineflayer simulator to auto-construct the architecture in a blank Minecraft world. For spatial reasoning and commonsense sub-tasks, the output is a standard VQA answer (multiple-choice or yes/no). Plans that fail to parse as valid matrices are scored as zero.

## Method

MineAnyBuild is a 4,000-task benchmark built on Minecraft that evaluates spatial planning across four dimensions: executable spatial plan generation, spatial understanding, creativity, and spatial reasoning/commonsense. The data curation pipeline collects ~7,000 player-generated architectures from GrabCraft and Minecraft Wiki plus ~500 indoor decoration assets, then applies quality filtering and human/MLLM annotation to produce instructions and ground-truth blueprint matrices. Spatial reasoning tasks are constructed from 48 block-based geometric stimuli with chiral distractors generated via X/Y/Z-axis symmetries, yielding 1,900 mental-rotation VQA pairs inspired by Shepard-Metzler experiments. Evaluation uses GPT-4.1 as a critic model scoring plans on sub-dimensions (structural correctness, material appropriateness, aesthetic quality) with weighted aggregation, while spatial reasoning uses direct accuracy. An infinitely expandable paradigm allows new player-generated content to be automatically converted into tasks by extracting 3D bounding boxes, querying block data via mineflayer, and generating blueprints and screenshots in a blank environment.

## Results

On MineAnyBuild, GPT-4o achieves the highest overall score of 41.02/100, followed by Claude-3.7-Sonnet (40.70) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (40.54). Spatial reasoning is the weakest dimension across all agents, with the best model (GPT-4o-mini) reaching only 26.7% accuracy on mental rotation tasks. Open-source models (InternVL2.5, Qwen2.5VL, LLaVA-OneVision) score between 15.56 and 23.30 overall, less than half the performance of proprietary models, with executable spatial plan generation scores below 1.3 for all open-source agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 13 MLLM-based agents (7 proprietary, 6 open-source) on MineAnyBuild. GPT-4o leads overall (41.02), narrowly ahead of Claude-3.7-Sonnet (40.70) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (40.54), with all proprietary models clustering in the 33–41 range. Proprietary models dominate open-source models by a wide margin: the best open-source agent (Qwen2.5VL-7B, 23.30) scores less than 60% of GPT-4o's overall score. On spatial reasoning (mental rotation), even the top model GPT-4o-mini reaches only 26.7% accuracy, and notably GPT-4o (24.4%) underperforms its smaller variant, suggesting this task remains unsolved. On spatial commonsense, proprietary models perform relatively well (6.82–7.52/10), while open-source models trail at 4.94–6.30. The most striking gap appears in executable spatial plan generation, where open-source models score below 1.3/10 versus 2.6–3.5 for proprietary models, indicating a fundamental inability to produce valid 3D blueprint matrices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VQA-based paradigm in spatial intelligence benchmarks (VSI-Bench, SpatialVLM, Lego-Puzzles) by requiring agents to generate executable 3D blueprint matrices rather than answer geometric questions, thereby bridging the gap between abstract spatial understanding and concrete task execution. It also distinguishes itself from prior Minecraft-based agent work (MineDojo, Voyager, VPT, Optimus), which focuses on skill learning and tech-tree objectives, by targeting open-ended architectural construction as the evaluation task. The infinitely expandable data curation pipeline, leveraging millions of player-generated builds via mineflayer, repurposes the Minecraft ecosystem as a scalable spatial planning data source rather than a game environment for embodied control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a persistent 'implementation gap' where even top proprietary MLLMs (GPT-4o at 41/100) can articulate spatial plans in text but fail to convert them into correct executable 3D matrices, a failure mode that VQA-style benchmarks cannot expose. The infinitely expandable paradigm is practically significant for the spatial intelligence community, offering a path to scale evaluation data without manual annotation. Open questions include whether the Minecraft-specific block-based representation generalizes to continuous 3D environments, whether the GPT-4.1-as-critic evaluation introduces circularity bias, and whether targeted spatial data training can close the implementation gap identified here.
