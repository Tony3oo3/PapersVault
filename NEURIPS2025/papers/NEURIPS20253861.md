## Problem

Existing functional alignment methods for multi-subject fMRI (hyperalignment, SRM, CCA-based approaches) require temporal synchronization and uniform time-series lengths across subjects, and multi-site extensions must contend with scanner hardware and protocol batch effects. There is no unified framework that aligns neural responses across subjects and sites without explicit temporal preprocessing while simultaneously handling variable sequence lengths and inter-site variability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253861/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-subject fMRI BOLD time series as T_s × V matrices (V = 19,742 voxels at 4 mm isotropic resolution on the MNI152 template) with stimulus class labels, where T_s can differ across subjects and no temporal realignment is required. Shorter sequences are zero-padded to a common window T with a binary mask, and voxels outside the ROI are zeroed so QR decomposition automatically ignores them. The output is a shared d = 256-dimensional embedding per time point (T_s × d) in a common representational space, from which a ν-SVM classifier predicts the cognitive state label.

## Method

OCL uses a dual-encoder architecture (online network trained with a contrastive loss; target network updated via exponential moving average) where each layer applies four tightly coupled operations: thin QR decomposition to extract orthonormal feature bases that decorrelate signals, locality-sensitive hashing on the upper-triangular R-factor to produce a compact scalar subject-specific signature (expanded to a d-dimensional vector via an MLP), sinusoidal positional encoding to inject temporal context, and a transformer encoder with multi-head self-attention to produce discriminative stimulus-aligned embeddings. The contrastive loss (Eq. 2) combines a softmax attraction term for same-stimulus pairs with a margin-based repulsion term for different-stimulus pairs, weighted by a between-class coefficient λ. The model is pretrained unsupervised on synthetic matrices generated by applying random orthonormal rotations to class-structured Gaussian data, learning to factor out arbitrary rotations before fine-tuning on real fMRI. For multi-site transfer, independent OCL instances are trained per site and their target-encoder parameters are averaged to form a frozen shared prior that projects all sites into a common feature space without additional fine-tuning.

## Results

Across 10 fMRI datasets (6 task-based, 2 movie-watching, 2 multi-site), OCL achieves the highest ν-SVM classification accuracy in every comparison, with all differences statistically significant (p < 0.05, two-sided t-test). The evaluation spans 440 total pairwise comparisons: 28 on temporally aligned vs. unaligned cognitive tasks (Fig. 2), 392 on movie stimuli across 7 voxel counts × 4 TR lengths × 2 datasets (Fig. 3), and 20 on multi-site cross-site transfer (Fig. 4). A key finding is that OCL maintains stable accuracy on temporally unaligned data, whereas traditional alignment methods (FastSRM, ShIndICA, HyperHMM) suffer significant degradation when temporal synchronization is removed. OCL uses d=256-dimensional embeddings, 32 transformer layers with 16 attention heads, and is pretrained on 2 million synthetic matrices before fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCL outperforms all 9 baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting, with p < 0.05 in all 440 comparisons. Against traditional alignment methods (FastSRM, ShIndICA, HyperHMM), OCL's advantage is most pronounced on temporally unaligned data, where these methods degrade sharply because their shared-space templates misalign stimuli across rows; OCL's contrastive objective learns flexible mappings rather than fixed templates. Against deep multi-view methods (DHA, DeepGeoCCA), OCL's orthogonal QR decomposition and LSH-based subject signatures provide stronger decorrelation and compactness. Against self-supervised constructive methods (MindEye2, MindAligner), OCL's pretrained 'OCL_zero' encoder and dual-encoder EMA architecture yield more robust cross-subject alignment. In the multi-site setting, OCL surpasses SSTL (linear), DeepSSTL (MLP deep kernel), and XG-GNN (graph neural networks with domain-generalizable explainability), with the improvement attributed to the pretrained multi-representational alignment module and the contrastive loss enforcing cross-site shared information. No baseline matches or exceeds OCL on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

OCL sits at the intersection of two established lines of fMRI research: functional alignment (hyperalignment, SRM, ShIndICA, DeepGeoCCA) and self-supervised contrastive learning (BYOL, SimCLR, DINO). The dual-encoder with EMA target network is directly inherited from BYOL, while the multi-view framing (each subject as a 'view') follows the CCA-based alignment tradition. The key architectural departure is the per-layer composition of QR decomposition, locality-sensitive hashing, sinusoidal positional encoding, and a transformer encoder—a combination not seen in prior alignment or contrastive-learning work—and the use of synthetic-data pretraining to initialize the encoder before fine-tuning on real fMRI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for temporal preprocessing and uniform time-series lengths, OCL lowers a practical barrier to pooling heterogeneous multi-site fMRI datasets, which is increasingly important as open repositories (OpenNEURO, HCP, NIMH) grow. The synthetic pretraining strategy offers a reusable recipe for bootstrapping contrastive encoders in low-data neuroimaging settings. Open questions include whether the individual components (QR, LSH, positional encoding) each contribute meaningfully (no ablation is provided), how performance degrades under extreme domain shift beyond the tested sites, and whether the approach scales to whole-brain, multi-modality (MEG/EEG) foundation models without prohibitive compute.
