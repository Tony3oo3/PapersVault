## Problem

Sequential (time-uniform) inference for quantiles under local differential privacy has been entirely unexplored. Constructing confidence sequences with valid coverage at every sample size requires an almost-surely consistent variance estimator, which is non-trivial because the quantile loss is non-smooth (only 1/2-Hölder) and the LDP noise inflates the asymptotic variance relative to the non-private case. Existing parallel SGD approaches for high-confidence inference use a fixed number of chains, which cannot guarantee variance consistency as the sample size grows.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential observations that are privatized on-device via an interactive randomized-response mechanism (the truthful response rate r determines the LDP budget ε = log(1+r) − log(1−r)), along with the target quantile level τ and significance level α. It outputs time-uniform asymptotic confidence sequences for the τ-quantile valid simultaneously at all sample sizes, pointwise confidence intervals, and—as a byproduct—consistent estimates of the asymptotic variance and the density at the true quantile. The entire procedure is fully online, requiring only O(κ) memory where κ ≪ T is the number of parallel chains, and the curator never observes raw data.

## Method

The algorithm employs a dynamically chained parallel SGD in which the number of chains κ = h(T) grows with the sample size (e.g., at rate log T), with data allocated via a round-robin scheme that prevents new chains from being added before existing ones are length-aligned. Each chain runs SGD with the privatized gradient from the binary-response mechanism, and the quantile estimator is a weighted Polyak-Ruppert average across chains. The key theoretical contribution is an almost-sure Gaussian approximation at rate O_a.s.((T/log log T)^(−1/2)), which is faster than the law of the iterated logarithm and handles the non-smooth quantile loss that defeats prior smooth-loss results. The variance is estimated by the weighted sample variance across chains, and the growing chain count ensures almost-sure consistency without additional privacy cost. Combining this approximation with the consistent variance estimator and standard Gaussian boundaries (stitched or Robbins mixture) yields the first sequential quantile-inference procedure in the LDP setting.

## Results

Across all simulation settings (Normal and Cauchy data, τ ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.8}, r ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 1}), the proposed AsympCSs maintain the nominal 5% time-uniform type I error rate. The Gaussian mixture boundary (equation 6) produces tighter finite-sample confidence sequences than the stitched boundary (equation 4), despite having a slower asymptotic rate of O(√(log T/T)) vs. O(√(log log T/T)). The pointwise confidence interval from Corollary 1 is consistently narrower than the self-normalized interval from [38] across all r and τ settings. On real data (Law School GPA, Government Salary), the CSs cover similar central values and their length decreases monotonically as the truthful response rate r increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against [38] (Liu et al., ICML 2023, self-normalized LDP quantile inference), the proposed method achieves narrower pointwise confidence intervals across all combinations of r and τ, attributed to the lighter tails of the Gaussian approximation versus the self-normalized distribution. However, for point estimation MSE, the proposed method is comparable to [38] near τ = 0.5 but slightly worse as τ moves toward the extremes (0.3 or 0.8), a trade-off introduced by the dynamically chained parallel procedure. Against [27] (Howard & Ramdas, Bernoulli 2022, order-statistics-based non-private sequential quantile estimation), the P-SGD-based AsympCS is tighter than [27]'s nonasymptotic confidence sequences when r = 1 (no privacy constraint), while still maintaining valid type I error control. This suggests the SGD-based approach can improve efficiency even without privacy constraints. Under privacy (r < 1), the comparison with [27] is not directly applicable since [27] requires access to raw data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) LDP quantile estimation via SGD with a randomized-response mechanism [38], (2) parallel SGD (P-SGD) for variance estimation in high-confidence inference [58], and (3) asymptotic confidence sequences (AsympCS) built on time-uniform central limit theory [52]. The key departure from [58] is replacing its fixed number of chains with a dynamically growing chain count κ = h(T) to achieve almost-sure variance consistency, and the departure from [54] and [58] is extending their smooth-loss Gaussian approximations to the non-smooth quantile loss (which only enjoys 1/2-Hölder gradient regularity). The AsympCS construction itself follows the framework of [52], requiring an almost-surely consistent variance estimator and a Gaussian approximation rate faster than the LIL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sequential (time-uniform) inference procedure for quantiles under LDP, filling a gap where prior LDP quantile methods [38, 39] only provided pointwise confidence intervals. The practical implications are significant for streaming privacy-preserving analytics (e.g., continuous monitoring of financial risk quantiles or health thresholds) where one needs valid coverage at every time point without pre-specifying a stopping rule. Open questions include: whether non-asymptotic (finite-sample) confidence sequences can be derived for SGD iterates under LDP, whether a fixed-κ variant with t-distribution-based bounds is feasible, and how sensitive the method is to the learning-rate and initialization choices in adversarial or heavy-tailed regimes beyond the tested distributions.
