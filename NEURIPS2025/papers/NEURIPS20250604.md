## Problem

Dempster's Rule of Combination (DRC) in Dempster-Shafer evidence theory suffers from exponential computational complexity O(kN·2^{2n}) as the frame of discernment grows, limiting its use in real-time uncertain pattern classification. While a prior quantum circuit (QC-DRC) reduced this to O(kn+kN), it can only fuse two pieces of evidence per circuit invocation, requiring k−1 sequential combinations. This paper asks whether a single adaptive quantum circuit can fuse all k evidence sources in one pass while preserving exact agreement with the classical DRC result.

## Input / Output

The method takes k basic probability assignments (BPAs) defined over a frame of discernment of n mutually exclusive events, where each BPA is generated from tabular data attributes via the approach of Jiang et al. [66]. Each BPA is encoded as an n-qubit superposition state, and the full circuit requires (2k−1)n qubits (kn for encoding, (k−1)n as auxiliary). The output is either a combined BPA (via full-state QBP measurement) or per-element plausibility values (via single-qubit QPl measurement), from which a class label is selected by argmax; the quantum results are provably identical to the classical DRC output.

## Method

The AQC-DRC comprises three stages: (1) QBPA-based quantum amplitude encoding, where each BPA m_h is mapped to an n-qubit state |Q_Mh⟩ = Σ √m_h(ψ_j)|ψ_j⟩ via a state-preparation oracle; (2) an adaptive quantum circuit that applies (k−1) Toffoli (CCNOT) gates recursively to fuse all k encoded states in a single circuit pass, followed by partial trace over the input subsystems to yield a reduced density matrix ρ_{Q_M_{k…1}}; and (3) one of two measurement operators—M_QBP (projecting onto full n-qubit basis states to recover the combined BPA) or M_QPl (tracing out all but one qubit and measuring |1⟩⟨1| to recover plausibility). The key insight is that the quantum basic probability amplitude, quantum basic probability, and quantum plausibility functions in QET naturally correspond to quantum amplitude encoding, measurement outcomes at the probability level, and measurement outcomes at the plausibility level, respectively, yielding deterministic computation that exactly matches classical DRC while reducing time complexity to O(kn+N) for the QBP variant.

## Results

At 16,384 measurement shots, the proposed AQC-DRC methods closely match classical DRC accuracy across all three datasets: on Iris, AQC-DRC_QBP achieves 0.9533 vs. DRC's 0.9467; on Abalone, all methods converge to 0.9823; on Knowledge, AQC-DRC_QBP reaches 0.8538 vs. DRC's 0.8539. The Dif metric (fraction of differently classified instances relative to classical DRC) converges to 0 for all quantum methods at high shot counts, confirming deterministic equivalence. In terms of computational complexity, AQC-DRC_QBP reduces time complexity from O(kN·2^{2n}) (classical DRC) to O(kn+N), and AQC-DRC_QPl to O(kn²), representing exponential speedup when N approaches 2^n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classical DRC [11], the proposed AQC-DRC methods achieve near-identical accuracy at high shot counts (16,384): on Iris, AQC-DRC_QBP slightly exceeds DRC (0.9533 vs. 0.9467); on Abalone, all methods tie at 0.9823; on Knowledge, AQC-DRC_QBP matches DRC within 0.0001 (0.8538 vs. 0.8539). The Dif metric confirms this: at 16,384 shots, AQC-DRC_QBP on Abalone achieves Dif = 0.0000, and on Iris the Dif drops to 0.0200, demonstrating convergence to the classical result. Against the prior quantum method QC-DRC [62], AQC-DRC provides a clear complexity advantage: AQC-DRC_QBP reduces time complexity from O(kn+kN) to O(kn+N) by fusing all k evidences in a single circuit pass rather than k−1 sequential passes. On the Knowledge dataset at 16,384 shots, AQC-DRC_QBP (0.8538) also marginally outperforms QC-DRC_QBP (0.8509), and AQC-DRC_QBP+QPl (0.8620) outperforms QC-DRC_QBP+QPl (0.8591). The trade-off is that AQC-DRC requires (2k−1)n qubits versus 3n for QC-DRC, a linear increase in k that is acceptable for the small k values (3–5) typical in multi-source fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantum Dempster's rule of combination (QC-DRC) of He and Xiao (2024), which could only fuse two pieces of evidence per circuit invocation, into an adaptive circuit (AQC-DRC) that combines k evidences simultaneously via (k−1) Toffoli gates in a single pass. The theoretical grounding draws on Xiao's quantum evidence theory (QET), which generalizes Dempster-Shafer evidence theory to complex-valued amplitudes, and the paper demonstrates that QET's quantum basic probability amplitude (QBPA) function naturally instantiates quantum amplitude encoding, while the quantum basic probability and quantum plausibility functions naturally correspond to measurement outcomes at two decision-making levels. The classical DRC and DRC+QPl serve as the deterministic baselines against which the quantum circuit's correctness is verified.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AQC-DRC provides a rigorous proof that quantum evidence fusion can be made deterministic (exactly matching classical DRC) while reducing time complexity from O(kN·2^{2n}) to O(kn+N) or O(kn²), which is significant for scaling multi-source fusion to large frames of discernment. However, the practical impact is currently constrained by the need for (2k−1)n qubits and the use of a quantum simulator (IonQ) rather than fault-tolerant hardware, and the method cannot yet handle complex-valued input data. Open questions include whether the adaptive circuit design generalizes to non-independent evidence sources, how it performs under realistic quantum noise, and whether the QET-based physical interpretation of amplitude encoding and measurement outcomes extends beyond the DRC setting to other evidence-theoretic operations.
