## Problem

Existing iEEG neurofoundation models either omit spatial encoding, collapse it across preselected channels, or encode it only at the single-channel scale, leaving it unclear how spatial scale choices in both token encoding and masked reconstruction pretraining affect learned representations and downstream decoding. No prior work has explored encoding spatial information at larger-than-channel scales (e.g., brain regions or lobes) while preserving individual channel temporal statistics, nor has it systematically studied whether channel-level or region-level masking is preferable for self-supervised pretraining of multiregional neural activity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3-second multivariate iEEG segments (C channels × 6144 samples at 2048 Hz) along with per-channel spatial meta-information (3D LPI coordinates, Destrieux atlas parcel IDs, or lobe assignments). Each segment is split into 12 temporal patches of 250 ms (512 samples) that are tokenized independently. The pretrained model outputs d=64-dimensional neural embeddings per channel-patch for downstream use; finetuned variants additionally produce binary classification scores (speech/non-speech, sentence onset) or reconstructed raw channel time-series patches via a linear head on top of the predictor network.

## Method

BaRISTA combines a dilated CNN tokenizer (5 conv blocks with exponential dilation, projecting 250 ms patches to d=64 tokens) with a 12-layer encoder transformer that attends to space and time concurrently via interleaved channel-temporal token ordering and RoPE for temporal position. The key architectural innovation is a learnable spatial embedding dictionary whose size and granularity are determined by a user-selected spatial scale (channel coordinates, atlas parcels, or lobes), added to each token so that channels sharing a spatial category receive the same spatial encoding. Pretraining uses a masked latent reconstruction objective where target tokens are selected by randomly sampling spatial categories (not individual channels), and a separate EMA-updated target tokenizer provides the reconstruction targets, with a shared learnable mask token replacing masked inputs. This design explicitly dissociates the spatial scale used for encoding from the spatial scale used for masking, enabling independent ablation of each factor's contribution to representation quality.

## Results

On the Brain Treebank dataset, BaRISTA with parcel-level spatial encoding and channel-level masking (parcels/channels) achieves 0.862 ± 0.016 AUC on Sentence Onset classification and 0.869 ± 0.016 AUC on Speech/Non-Speech classification, outperforming both SOTA iEEG baselines. The channel-level encoding variant (channels/channels) achieves 0.778 and 0.764 AUC respectively, performing on par with PopT. A two-way ANOVA confirms that spatial encoding scale has a significantly larger effect on downstream performance than spatial masking scale (encoding p < 1e-3, masking p = 0.010–0.037, no significant interaction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BaRISTA (parcels/channels) outperforms both SOTA iEEG baselines on both downstream tasks. Against PopT + BrainBERT [19], BaRISTA improves Sentence Onset AUC from 0.795 to 0.862 (+6.7 points) and Speech/Non-Speech AUC from 0.775 to 0.869 (+9.4 points), with both differences highly significant (Wilcoxon p = 5.0e-6 and 2.3e-10, respectively). Against Brant [15], the gains are even larger: +9.5 points on Sentence Onset (0.767 → 0.862) and +17.8 points on Speech/Non-Speech (0.691 → 0.869). Notably, BaRISTA's channel-level variant (channels/channels) performs roughly on par with PopT (no significant differences except BaRISTA being significantly better than Brant on speech, p = 3.9e-5), indicating that the key advantage comes from using larger-than-channel spatial encoding rather than the architecture itself. Randomly initialized BaRISTA models (0.683–0.688 AUC) confirm that the self-supervised pretraining contributes substantially to downstream performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iEEG neurofoundation model line (BrainBERT, Brant, PopT, Du-IN) by introducing a flexible spatial encoding scheme that decouples the spatial scale of token encoding from the spatial scale of masked reconstruction. Prior works either omitted spatial encoding entirely (Brant), collapsed channels into preselected regions (Du-IN), or encoded space only at the single-channel level using 3D volumetric coordinates (PopT, Mentzelopoulos). BaRISTA repurposes the masked latent reconstruction paradigm (analogous to MAE/BERT) with an EMA-updated target tokenizer, but adds the novel ability to guide masking via neuroanatomical meta-information (atlas parcels, lobes) rather than random channel selection, and to encode spatial identity at coarser-than-channel scales while preserving individual channel tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that parcel-level spatial encoding substantially outperforms channel-level encoding (e.g., 0.862 vs 0.778 AUC on sentence onset) challenges the default assumption in the iEEG transformer literature that finer spatial granularity is always better, and suggests that neuroanatomically meaningful groupings may capture functionally relevant network structure. The demonstration that larger-scale encoding does not sacrifice channel-level reconstruction capability is practically important for building models that operate at multiple spatial resolutions. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (Brain Treebank, 10 subjects), restriction to language-related downstream tasks, and use of spatial-only masking without spatiotemporal masking; whether the spatial-scale finding generalizes to other behavioral paradigms, larger datasets, or non-language tasks remains open.
