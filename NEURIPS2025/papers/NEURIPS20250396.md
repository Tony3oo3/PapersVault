## Problem

Data-driven neural operators for differential equations suffer significant performance degradation when the testing distribution differs from the training distribution (domain shift). Existing transfer learning methods for DEs either lack generalizability across equation types (analytic methods), are insufficient under limited target data (finetuning), or may destroy the underlying physical relations during feature alignment (domain adaptation). No general method exists that simultaneously corrects the operator bias while preserving the physics of the specific problem.

## Input / Output

The method consumes abundant source-domain (k, u) pairs and a small set of target-domain (k, u) pairs (50–100 samples), where k is the input function (initial condition, diffusion coefficient, or gridded climate field) and u is the corresponding solution. No equation-specific information (symbolic form, coefficients) is provided during training, mimicking real-world settings where governing equations are unknown. Outputs are solution functions at the same spatial/temporal resolution as inputs: 1024-point 1D curves for Burgers' and Advection equations, 64×64 2D fields for Darcy flow, and 5.625° gridded fields for ERA5 climate forecasting.

## Method

POTT learns a neural transport map T_θ = (T_k, T_u) between source and target product distributions via a physics-regularized Optimal Tensor Transport (OTT) problem, solved in dual (min-max) form with a Lagrange multiplier network f_φ. The pushforward distribution P^r = T_#P^s provides a physics-preserved characterization of the target domain, and the transferred operator G_η is trained jointly on both the limited target data and the pushforward-generated samples. A physical regularization term R(T) enforces preservation of the operator relation: in general cases it penalizes the discrepancy m(G(k^r), u^r) between the operator-predicted and transported outputs, while problem-specific priors (e.g., value preservation for advection) can substitute this term. A consistency theorem guarantees that the saddle-point solution equals the physics-regularized Kantorovich cost, and degenerates to the Monge map when the pushforward exactly matches the target distribution.

## Results

On the Darcy flow benchmark (the hardest transfer task D₂→D₃), POTT reduces relative MSE by 25% (0.4693→0.3527) with 50 target samples and by 36% (0.3553→0.2271) with 100 samples compared to finetuning. Across all three simulation equations (Burgers', Advection, Darcy flow), POTT achieves the lowest average rMSE in every transfer task, e.g. 0.0809 vs. 0.1285 (COD) on Burgers' and 0.0504 vs. 0.0516 (COD) on Advection with 100 target samples. On the real-world WeatherBench climate forecasting task (North America → global), POTT consistently reduces latitude-weighted RMSE over both source-only and finetuning baselines across all five forecast variables and lead times, approaching the performance of a model trained with sufficient target data (Oracle).

## Baselines & Comparisons

POTT outperforms all three DA baselines (TL-DeepONet, DARE-GRAM, COD) and finetuning across every transfer task on all three simulation equations. The gap is most pronounced on the hardest Darcy flow task D₂→D₃, where POTT achieves 0.3527/0.2271 rMSE (50/100 samples) versus 0.4437/0.2836 for COD and 0.4693/0.3553 for finetuning — a 25–36% relative error reduction. On the simpler Burgers' and Advection tasks, POTT still leads by 10–20% over the best DA baseline (COD), while TL-DeepONet and DARE-GRAM sometimes exhibit negative transfer (worse than source-only). On the WeatherBench climate forecasting task, POTT consistently beats both source-only and finetuning across all five variables (z, t, t2m, u10, v10) and lead times (6–36 h), narrowing the gap to the Oracle (sufficient target data) model and approaching the SOTA ClimaX model trained with full target data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Optimal Tensor Transport (OTT) framework of Kerdoncuff et al. (AAAI 2022) to the PDE transfer learning setting, introducing a physics-preserved regularization term that encodes problem-specific invariants (e.g., value preservation in advection) or marginal-distribution consistency. The dual optimization formulation and consistency theorem directly build on the Neural Monge Map estimation framework of Fan et al. (TMLR 2023), while the neural parameterization of the transport map follows the large-scale neural OT lineage of Seguy et al. (ICLR 2018) and Korotin et al. (ICLR 2023). Conceptually, the paper departs from standard domain adaptation (which aligns feature distributions and may destroy physical structure) and from finetuning (which is insufficient under limited target data) by instead characterizing the target domain through a physics-constrained pushforward distribution, positioning POTT as a bridge between data-driven and physics-driven paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: no prior method simultaneously provides generalizability across arbitrary PDE families and explicit physics preservation during transfer, which is critical when governing equations are unknown or only partially specified. The climate forecasting experiment (North America → global, 1 year target data approaching ClimaX-level performance) demonstrates practical relevance beyond toy PDEs. Key limitations remain: the min-max optimization is computationally expensive and may not scale to high-resolution or large-parameter models, and the method degrades sharply below ~10 target samples, limiting its applicability in truly one-shot scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach can leverage pretrained foundation models to reduce the training burden of the transport map, and whether the physics regularization can be made fully automatic without problem-specific design.
