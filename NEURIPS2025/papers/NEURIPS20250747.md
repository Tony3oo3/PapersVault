## Problem

Existing high-probability convergence analyses for clipped SGD under heavy-tailed noise rely on standard smoothness and/or bounded gradient assumptions, while practical models (e.g., LLMs, vision transformers) often satisfy only (L0,L1)-smoothness and exhibit heavy-tailed gradient noise simultaneously. In federated learning, no prior work provides high-probability convergence or generalization guarantees under these weaker conditions, and the gap between expected and high-probability rates remains unbridged by naive clipped SGD.

## Input / Output

The method consumes stochastic data samples whose gradient noise has a bounded p-th moment (p∈(1,2]) rather than finite variance, along with initial parameters and hyperparameters (clipping threshold λ, local step size η, global learning rate γ, batch size b, and local iterations K). It outputs trained model parameters accompanied by explicit high-probability convergence bounds of the form O(T^((2-2p)/(3p-2)) log^((2p-2)/(2p-1))(T/δ)) and generalization bounds, all holding with probability at least 1−δ without requiring any bounded-gradient assumption. In the federated setting, the output additionally quantifies the trade-off between optimization speed (governed by γ) and generalization to unseen client distributions.

## Method

For centralized learning, the paper proves a tighter high-probability convergence rate for clipped SGD via an inductive argument that solves a quadratic inequality in ‖∇F_S(x)‖ to handle the implicit gradient dependence introduced by (L0,L1)-smoothness, and constructs a novel martingale difference sequence (L1η²‖∇F_S(x_t)‖−η)⟨∇F_S(x_t),θ_t^a⟩ that does not appear under standard smoothness. For federated learning, the proposed FedCBG algorithm uses clipped batch gradients on each client (batch size b) and a sum-aggregation paradigm at the server (x_{t+1}=x_t−γ∑Δ̃_t) instead of the standard average-aggregation. The key technical enabler is a new batch gradient variance bound E_t‖∇f_i(x)−∇f_i(x;ξ)‖²≤3σ^pλ^{2−p}/b under heavy-tailed noise, which introduces b as an extra degree of freedom to balance the inductive constraints and achieve a convergence rate O((mKT)^((2−2p)/(3p−2)) log^((p−1)/p)(T/δ)) matching the known lower bound up to a logarithmic factor. Generalization bounds are derived for both settings via a generalized uniform convergence lemma for (L0,L1)-smooth objectives, yielding the first high-probability generalization guarantees without bounded gradients.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, FedCBG achieves 85.6% test accuracy in 89 communication rounds, outperforming FAT-clipping-PR (83.1%, 282 rounds), FAT-clipping-PI (84.0%, 189 rounds), and FedAvg (83.8%, 201 rounds). On CIFAR-100, FedCBG reaches 44.2% accuracy in 221 rounds versus 42.2% (PR, 412 rounds), 42.8% (PI, 346 rounds), and 42.0% (FedAvg, 409 rounds). On the Shakespeare text dataset, FedCBG attains 36.5% accuracy in 98 rounds compared to 34.8% (PR, 219 rounds), 35.2% (PI, 178 rounds), and 32.0% (FedAvg, 268 rounds). Overall, FedCBG achieves 1.6–3.1× fewer communication rounds to reach target accuracy across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCBG consistently outperforms all three baselines on every dataset in both test accuracy and communication efficiency. Against FAT-clipping-PR [47], FedCBG gains +2.5 pp accuracy on CIFAR-10 (85.6% vs 83.1%), +2.0 pp on CIFAR-100 (44.2% vs 42.2%), and +1.7 pp on Shakespeare (36.5% vs 34.8%), while requiring 3.1×, 1.9×, and 2.2× fewer rounds respectively. Against FAT-clipping-PI [47], the accuracy gains are +1.6 pp, +1.4 pp, and +1.3 pp with 2.1×, 1.6×, and 1.8× fewer rounds. Against FedAvg [31], FedCBG achieves +1.8 pp, +2.2 pp, and +4.5 pp accuracy improvements with 2.3×, 1.9×, and 2.7× fewer rounds. The largest relative gain is on Shakespeare, where FedAvg's lack of clipping makes it particularly vulnerable to heavy-tailed noise, and FedCBG's batched clipped gradient with sum-aggregation provides the most substantial advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work on clipped SGD: high-probability convergence under heavy-tailed noise (Nguyen et al. [35], Cutkosky & Mehta [7]) and convergence under (L0,L1)-smoothness (Zhang et al. [53]), which prior analyses treated in isolation. It departs from Yang et al. [47] on federated heavy-tailed optimization by replacing the bounded-gradient assumption with a weaker p-th-moment condition, upgrading from expected to high-probability guarantees, and introducing a new FedCBG algorithm whose batch-gradient and sum-aggregation design is specifically engineered to match the lower bound in high probability. The generalization analysis extends Li & Liu [24,25] by removing their bounded-gradient requirement and providing the first high-probability generalization bound for FL under heavy-tailed noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: large language and vision models empirically satisfy (L0,L1)-smoothness and exhibit heavy-tailed gradient noise (verified in Fig. 1–2), yet prior theory required standard smoothness and/or bounded gradients that fail even for quadratic losses. The FedCBG algorithm's 1.6–3.1× reduction in communication rounds over state-of-the-art baselines on CIFAR-10/100 and Shakespeare suggests tangible efficiency gains for cross-device FL deployments. Open questions include whether the induction-based technique extends to recursive momentum or minimax settings (flagged as future work), and whether the generalization-optimization trade-off identified in Theorem 4 can be mitigated by adaptive learning-rate schedules.
