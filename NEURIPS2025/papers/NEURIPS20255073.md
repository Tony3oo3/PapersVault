## Problem

While 2D spatial grounding (bounding boxes, segmentation masks) has been well-studied in text-to-image generation, 3D orientation grounding—controlling the azimuth, polar, and rotation angles of objects in generated images—remains largely unexplored. Existing orientation-conditioned methods (Zero-1-to-3, C3DW, OrientDream) are restricted to single-object images, rely on synthetic training data, or support only limited orientation ranges, and none can handle multiple objects across open-vocabulary categories in a zero-shot manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing a scene, a set of object phrases (e.g., {"dog", "person"}), and per-object 3D orientation conditions specified as three Euler angles (azimuth ∈ [0,360°), polar ∈ [0,180°), rotation ∈ [0,360°)), and produces a single RGB image in which all N objects appear with their specified orientations. The generation is driven by a one-step flow model (FLUX-Schnell) that maps a latent vector drawn from N(0,I) directly to a 512×512 image, with the latent iteratively refined over 50 function evaluations at test time. The reward signal is computed by cropping detected objects via GroundingDINO and scoring their orientations with OrientAnything, which outputs categorical probability distributions over one-degree bins for each Euler angle.

## Method

ORIGEN reformulates the reward-maximization problem as a sampling problem: instead of gradient ascent on the latent (which suffers from local optima and distribution drift), it simulates Langevin dynamics whose drift term is the orientation-grounding reward and whose noise term preserves proximity to the Gaussian prior. The key theoretical result (Proposition 1) shows that the Euler-Maruyama discretization of this SDE reduces to a remarkably simple update—standard gradient ascent plus a single line of stochastic noise injection and a scaling factor—while provably converging to the target distribution q* = argmax_p E[R̂(x)] − α·D_KL(p‖q). To accelerate convergence, ORIGEN introduces reward-adaptive time rescaling via a monitor function G(R̂(x)) = s_min − tanh(k·R̂(x))·(s_max − s_min), which assigns larger steps in low-reward regions and smaller steps in high-reward regions, with a correction drift term (½∇log G) that provably preserves the stationary distribution. The entire pipeline is zero-shot: no fine-tuning of the generative model is required, and the reward is computed at test time using pretrained GroundingDINO and OrientAnything.

## Results

On ORIBENCH-Single, ORIGEN achieves 87.1% angular accuracy (Acc.@22.5°) and 17.41° absolute azimuth error, outperforming the best one-step baseline ReNO (79.6%, 20.56°) and the best multi-step baseline FreeDoM (74.1%, 20.90°). On ORIBENCH-Multi, ORIGEN reaches 69.2% accuracy and 28.4° error, substantially ahead of ReNO (47.8%, 45.95°) and FreeDoM (59.1%, 31.77°). In a user study with 100 participants, ORIGEN was preferred by 58.18% of raters versus 20.58% for Zero-1-to-3 and 21.24% for C3DW. The reward-adaptive time rescaling reduces average iterations to reach the ±22.5° threshold from 14.2 to 12.8 NFEs (13.5 s vs. 14.9 s per image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORIGEN outperforms all six baselines on both orientation alignment metrics across both benchmarks. Against the training-based methods, ORIGEN surpasses Zero-1-to-3 (49.9% → 87.1% Acc.@22.5°) and C3DW (42.6% → 87.1%) by a wide margin, with absolute error reduced from 59–65° to 17.4°, while also matching or exceeding their text-to-image alignment scores. Among multi-step guided-generation methods, ORIGEN beats FreeDoM (74.1%, 20.90°), MPGD (68.9%, 27.62°), and DPS (66.4%, 28.16°) on ORIBENCH-Single, with the gap widening on ORIBENCH-Multi where FreeDoM drops to 59.1% and DPS to 48.7%. The closest competitor is ReNO, the one-step gradient-ascent baseline, which achieves 79.6% on Single and 47.8% on Multi; ORIGEN improves upon it by 7.5 and 21.4 percentage points respectively, with the multi-object gap attributed to ReNO's susceptibility to local optima in the latent space. In the user study, ORIGEN is preferred by 58.18% of participants, roughly 2.7× the preference rate of the best training-based baseline (C3DW at 21.24%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of orientation-conditioned image generation (Zero-1-to-3, C3DW, OrientDream) from single-object, limited-azimuth, training-based settings to a zero-shot, multi-object, full-3D-orientation formulation. It departs from the dominant optimization-based test-time guidance paradigm (DPS, MPGD, FreeDoM, ReNO) by reframing reward maximization as a sampling problem via Langevin dynamics, leveraging a one-step flow model (FLUX-Schnell) rather than multi-step diffusion. The reward function composes two pretrained discriminative models (GroundingDINO for detection, OrientAnything for orientation estimation), avoiding the need for task-specific training data that prior supervised methods required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ORIGEN is the first method to handle multi-object 3D orientation grounding in a zero-shot setting, and the ORIBENCH benchmark it introduces fills a gap in quantitative evaluation for this task. The Langevin-dynamics formulation provides a convergence guarantee to a well-defined target distribution, and the resulting update rule (gradient ascent + noise, one line of code) is practically attractive. However, performance is inherently bounded by the OrientAnything discriminator used for both reward and evaluation, creating a circularity that limits confidence in absolute numbers; whether the approach transfers to other 3D grounding modalities (e.g., 6D pose, depth) or benefits from stronger discriminators remains open.
