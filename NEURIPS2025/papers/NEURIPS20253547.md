## Problem

Flow matching policies trained via imitation learning lack a built-in exploration mechanism and are limited by the quality of expert demonstrations, yet fine-tuning them with online RL is technically challenging: the deterministic ODE path makes log-probability computation intractable (requiring Monte-Carlo trace estimation with large discretization error at few denoising steps), and no principled exploration mechanism exists for conditional flows. This paper presents the first online RL algorithm that stably fine-tunes a family of flow matching policies, including Rectified Flow and Shortcut Models, even at a single denoising step.

## Input / Output

The policy receives either a proprioceptive state vector (e.g., 11-dim for Hopper, 376-dim for Humanoid) or RGB images (96×96 single or dual views for Robomimic tasks) as observation, and outputs a continuous action chunk of size C (e.g., 3×4 for Hopper, 17×4 for Humanoid) executed sequentially before the next observation. During fine-tuning, a lightweight noise injection network σ_θ′ conditions on the observation, denoising time t_k, and current denoised action a^k to produce per-step Gaussian noise; after fine-tuning, this network is discarded and the policy reverts to a deterministic flow map, so deployment requires only the velocity field v_θ.

## Method

ReinFlow injects learnable Gaussian noise at each denoising step of the flow ODE integration, converting the deterministic path into a discrete-time Markov process with closed-form Gaussian transition probabilities. This yields an exact joint log-probability expression (Eq. 7) that is valid at any step size—eliminating the Monte-Carlo trace estimation and discretization error that plague continuous-time flow likelihoods—making policy gradient optimization stable even at one denoising step. The paper derives a policy gradient theorem (Theorem 4.1) for policies parameterized by such Markov processes, then implements it with PPO's clipped surrogate loss, jointly optimizing the velocity network θ and noise network θ′. The noise network automatically balances exploration and exploitation during training and is discarded afterward, recovering a standard deterministic flow policy. The method supports W2 and entropy regularization, and is applicable to both Rectified Flow and Shortcut Model variants with shared hyperparameters.

## Results

In OpenAI Gym locomotion tasks (Hopper, Walker2d, Ant, Humanoid), ReinFlow-R (Rectified Flow) achieves an average net reward growth of 135.36% over the pre-trained policy while using fewer denoising steps and saving 82.63% wall time compared to DPPO. In Robomimic visual manipulation tasks (Can, Square, Transport), ReinFlow-S (Shortcut Model) achieves an average net success rate increase of 40.34% at four or even one denoising step, with performance comparable to fine-tuned DDIM policies while saving 23.20% computation time. Across all tasks, ReinFlow reduces wall time by 62.82% on average compared to DPPO. In Franka Kitchen state-input manipulation, ReinFlow achieves the best overall task completion rates across complete, mixed, and partial data settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPPO (the state-of-the-art online RL method for diffusion policies), ReinFlow achieves comparable or slightly better success rates in Robomimic visual manipulation while using as few as one denoising step (vs. five-step DDIM in DPPO) and four steps in Transport, saving 23.20% computation time. In OpenAI Gym locomotion, ReinFlow-R outperforms DPPO in asymptotic reward across all four tasks (Hopper, Walker2d, Ant, Humanoid) while reducing wall time by 82.63% due to fewer denoising steps. Against FQL (state-of-the-art offline RL for flow policies), ReinFlow outperforms it asymptotically in both reward and convergence speed, as FQL's distillation-based one-step policy limits representation capacity and its W2 constraint during training hinders exploration. Overall, ReinFlow achieves the best wall-time efficiency across all tasks, reducing total fine-tuning time by 62.82% on average compared to DPPO, while matching or exceeding its final performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of online RL fine-tuning for generative robot policies from diffusion models (DPPO, QSM) to flow matching policies (Rectified Flow, Shortcut Models), a transition that is non-trivial due to fundamental mathematical differences between the two families. The key conceptual departure is the injection of a learnable Gaussian noise network into the flow's deterministic ODE path, converting it into a discrete-time Markov process with closed-form Gaussian transitions—thereby enabling exact log-probability computation without the trace estimation and discretization errors that plague few-step flow inference. This contrasts with FQL, which handles flow policies only offline via distillation to a one-step model, and with DPPO, which relies on a bilevel MDP formulation specific to DDIM's stochastic sampling structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As flow matching policies (e.g., π0) become the dominant architecture for general robot control, a stable and efficient online RL fine-tuning method is practically critical for closing the gap between imitation-learned policies and task-optimal behavior, especially under sparse rewards and imperfect demonstrations. The ability to fine-tune at one denoising step while maintaining stability is a significant practical win for real-time deployment, and the 62% wall-time reduction over DPPO makes online RL fine-tuning more accessible. Open questions include scaling to large vision-language-action models, developing sample-efficient (off-policy) variants for expensive real-world data collection, and reducing sensitivity to the noise-magnitude hyperparameter, which currently requires manual tuning per task.
