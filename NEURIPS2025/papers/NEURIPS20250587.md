## Problem

Graph-based LLM-agent workflows assume each subtask's output is conditionally independent of non-parent nodes given its parents' responses. In practice, agents produce only approximate responses, so hidden dependencies persist through the latent ground-truth variables, causing inter-agent misalignment—style conflicts, factual inconsistencies, and coordination breakdowns that degrade output quality. The paper identifies this infeasible conditional-independence assumption as the root cause and asks how to execute such workflows reliably without it.

## Input / Output

The system takes a high-level task prompt, a DAG of subtasks with parent-child edges, and a pool of diverse LLM models (e.g., o4-mini and o3-mini in experiments). Execution proceeds sequentially over a topological ordering, with each agent generating bounded-length token segments (controlled by hyperparameter l_0) conditioned on the full verified response history rather than only parent-node outputs. The final output is the concatenated sequence of verified segments, yielding a complete artifact (Python/Pygame code, LaTeX slides, HTML/CSS sites, or travel itineraries) that has passed cross-model consensus at every segment boundary.

## Method

SEQCV replaces parallel DAG execution with a sequential generate-verify-split loop. At each subtask, every agent in the pool generates a short candidate segment conditioned on the entire verified history; peer agents then cast binary coherence votes, and a segment is accepted only if it reaches a majority threshold τ. If no candidate passes, a learned decomposition model M_split recursively splits the subtask into simpler children inserted into the DAG, and execution resumes at the new nodes up to a maximum depth d_max. This full-history conditioning captures hidden dependencies that parent-only conditioning misses, while segment-level verification catches errors early before they cascade downstream. The design avoids the summary-and-rerun modules used by prior systems (Flow, AFlow), allocating tokens to new content generation rather than conflict resolution, which yields both higher accuracy (up to +30% over baselines) and lower wall-clock time than parallel pipelines.

## Results

Across seven creative agentic tasks (website development, LaTeX lecture slides, game development, travel planning, etc.), SEQCV achieves an average score of 88% (HR 83%, ES 100%, CA 100%), outperforming the strongest single-model baseline o4-mini-high (82%) by 6 points and the best multi-agent baseline Flow (58%) by 30 points. SEQCV attains perfect Execution Success and Constraint Adherence scores, indicating that its sequential conditioning and cross-model validation eliminate the inter-agent misalignment that causes downstream failures. In terms of efficiency, SEQCV's average runtime of 217 s is 2.5× faster than Flow (665 s) and 2.1× faster than Atom (523 s), despite using a weaker model ensemble (o4-mini + o3-mini) than o4-mini-high.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEQCV (using an ensemble of o4-mini and o3-mini) outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against the strongest single-model baseline o4-mini-high, SEQCV gains +6 points on average (88% vs 82%), driven primarily by a 30-point advantage in Constraint Adherence (100% vs 70%) while matching its perfect Execution Success score. Against the multi-agent baselines, the margins are much larger: +30 points over Flow (58%), +52 points over AFlow (36%), and +59 points over Atom (29%), reflecting the severe inter-agent misalignment that parallel DAG execution introduces. Notably, SEQCV's ensemble of two weaker models (o4-mini + o3-mini) surpasses the single stronger o4-mini-high model, demonstrating that the sequential verification and recursive splitting mechanisms add value beyond raw model capability. On runtime, SEQCV (217 s avg) is slower than o4-mini-high (54 s) and AFlow (161 s) but substantially faster than Flow (665 s) and Atom (523 s), confirming that its sequential strategy avoids the costly misalignment corrections that erode parallel pipelines' effective throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the graph-as-workflow paradigm for LLM-agent orchestration (Flow, AFlow, GPTSwarm, AgentPrune, AoT), which assumes each subtask's output is conditionally independent of non-parent nodes given its parents. It departs from this lineage by identifying—via the MASFT 2.5 failure taxonomy [1]—that this conditional independence assumption is fundamentally infeasible because LLMs produce only approximate responses, creating hidden statistical dependencies that propagate through the DAG. SEQCV also draws on cross-model ensemble validation (self-consistency voting, LLM-as-a-judge) and recursive decomposition (Reflexion, Tree-of-Thought), but unifies them into a single sequential generate-verify-split loop with segment-level granularity, rather than applying them as post-hoc correction modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of conditional independence violation as the root cause of inter-agent misalignment provides a clear diagnostic for practitioners building DAG-based LLM-agent pipelines, and the sequential-with-early-verification design offers a practical mitigation that avoids the costly post-hoc reconciliation loops common in existing systems. However, the framework is limited to acyclic task graphs (cyclic or recurrent dependencies are explicitly out of scope), scales poorly with context window constraints on long task sequences, and provides no benefit for strictly isolated wide graphs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger model ensembles, whether the recursive splitting depth can be learned adaptively, and how it interacts with emerging agentic frameworks that support dynamic, non-DAG topologies.
