## Problem

Existing video-language benchmarks either focus on coarse activity recognition or predict scalar performance scores, leaving no rigorous evaluation for expert-level, fine-grained understanding of skilled physical human actions. While recent datasets (e.g., Ego-Exo4D) provide expert commentaries, they lack structured evaluation tasks, making it impossible to measure how well VLMs truly understand nuanced physical skills versus merely recognizing scene-level content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250246/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a video clip (32 uniformly sampled frames at 796×448, resized per model requirements) with a 5-way multiple-choice question where all five options are expert-level feedback commentaries of similar length (80–120% of the correct answer, ≤8-word difference). The model outputs a single option number (1–5) identifying which commentary correctly describes the observed execution. The distractors are not random alternatives but are generated via targeted strategies (action replacement, misinterpretation, false causality, ineffective suggestion) that require domain-specific reasoning to distinguish from the ground truth.

## Method

EXACT is constructed via a four-stage pipeline built on Ego-Exo4D's raw expert commentaries: (1) GPT-4o corrects ASR errors and segments unstructured transcripts into concise, self-contained feedback categorized as Good Execution or Tips for Improvement; (2) an LLM generates four distractors per sample using activity-specific strategies (e.g., action replacement for GE, action misinterpretation / incorrect technical reasoning / false cause-effect / ineffective suggestion for TIPS); (3) two filtering steps remove biased samples—length-similarity constraints and a blind-LLM test where five state-of-the-art LLMs must not identify the correct answer above 20% (random chance) without video access; (4) 16 domain experts validate visual grounding and eliminate any sample where a distractor is also visually observable. Evaluation is zero-shot: ten SOTA VLMs (proprietary and open-source) are prompted with the 32-frame video and five options, and accuracy is computed per activity, per domain, and overall across 3,521 samples spanning 11 activities in 6 physical domains.

## Results

On the EXACT benchmark (3,521 QA pairs across 11 activities in 6 domains), the best-performing VLM, Gemini 2.5 Pro, achieves only 55.35% overall accuracy, well below the 82.02% achieved by trained human experts and even the 61.86% of non-expert humans. Among open-source models, LLaVA-Video-72B leads at 41.58%, while smaller models (PerceptionLM-8B at 24.65%, VideoLLaMA3-7B at 26.38%) perform barely above the 20% random-chance baseline. Gemini 2.5 Pro shows the strongest domain-specific performance in Bike Repair (65.05%) and Health (60.71%), while struggling most in Music (53.05%) and Dance (53.98%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All nine evaluated VLMs fall substantially short of human expert performance (82.02%), with the best model (Gemini 2.5 Pro at 55.35%) trailing by over 26 percentage points. Proprietary models consistently outperform open-source alternatives: GPT-4.1 (50.89%) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (55.35%) surpass the best open-source model LLaVA-Video-72B (41.58%) by 9–14 points. Within open-source models, scale matters significantly—7B-parameter models (PerceptionLM-8B at 24.65%, VideoLLaMA3-7B at 26.38%) perform near random chance, while 72B+ models reach 33–42%. Gemini 2.5 Pro dominates across all six domains, with its largest margins in Bike Repair (65.05% vs. 58.90% for GPT-4.1) and Health (60.71% vs. 51.10%), suggesting procedural knowledge domains are more tractable than fine-grained physical movement domains like Music (53.05%) and Dance (53.98%). Notably, even non-expert humans (61.86%) outperform every evaluated VLM, underscoring that current models lack the nuanced, expert-level understanding that EXACT is designed to probe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Ego-Exo4D dataset's expert commentaries, transforming unstructured, ASR-noisy spoken feedback into a rigorous multiple-choice video QA benchmark. It departs from prior skill assessment work (FineDiving, BASKET, JIGSAWS) that relies on scalar or categorical quality scores, and from skilled-activity video-language datasets (VidDiffBench, EgoExo-Fitness, EgoExoLearn) that provide free-form expert descriptions but lack a formal evaluation protocol. The key methodological departure is the four-stage construction pipeline—LLM-based preprocessing, strategy-driven distractor generation, blind-LLM filtering to eliminate language shortcuts, and domain-expert validation—that produces a benchmark uniquely combining expert-level knowledge, free-form language annotations, and a well-defined MCQ accuracy metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and consistent gap: the best VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) achieves only 55.35% accuracy versus 82.02% for trained human experts, and even non-expert humans (61.86%) outperform all evaluated models. This quantifies how far current VLMs are from serving as reliable AI coaches for physical skill learning, a vision the paper motivates. Limitations include coverage of only 11 activities across 6 domains, potential time-sensitivity of certain tasks (e.g., COVID-19 safety), and privacy concerns with identifiable faces. Open questions include whether architectural changes (longer temporal context, domain-specific pretraining) can close the gap, and whether the MCQ format adequately captures the full richness of expert feedback.
