## Problem

Prior algorithms for constrained online convex optimization (COCO) achieve a universal CCV of O(√T log T), yet the best known lower bound is only Ω(√d), leaving a fundamental gap. The paper shows that even for trivial instances (d=1, time-invariant f and g), the state-of-the-art algorithm suffers Ω(√T log T) CCV because it treats constraint violation minimization as regret minimization for g_t, failing to exploit the geometry of the nested feasible sets S_t. The central question is whether O(1) or o(√T) CCV can be simultaneously achieved with O(√T) regret.

## Input / Output

Each round, the algorithm selects an action x_t from a closed convex admissible set X ⊂ ℝ^d of diameter D, after which the adversary reveals a G-Lipschitz convex cost f_t and a convex constraint g_t (equivalently, the feasible set G_t = {x : g_t(x) ≤ 0}). The algorithm must maintain the feasibility assumption that the intersection of all G_t is non-empty. The CCV bound is instance-dependent: it is a function of the angular width c* of cones containing the convex hulls of midpoints and S_t, the dimension d, and the diameter D, rather than a universal function of T alone. The Switch variant additionally tracks cumulative CCV and switches to a fallback algorithm once CCV exceeds √T log T.

## Method

Algorithm 2 is a projected OGD method that, at each round, takes a gradient step from x_t with step size D/(G√t) with respect to f_t, projects the result onto S_{t-1} to obtain y_t, and then projects y_t onto S_t to produce x_{t+1}. The key insight is that the CCV is bounded by the total movement cost M_T = Σ‖x_t − P_{S_t}(x_t)‖, which can be controlled via the geometry of the nested sets: for spheres or axis-parallel cuboids, M_T = O(d^{3/2}D); for d=2 with monotonic projection hyperplane angles, M_T = O(D); and in general, M_T ≤ (2V_d(d−1)/V_{d−1})(1/c*)^d D, where c* is the minimum angular width over all time steps. The proof for the general case relates the per-step projection distance to the decrease in mean width of the convex body (via a cone-containment argument), while the d=2 and OCS results use the Manselli–Pucci theorem on self-expanded curves. The Switch algorithm combines Algorithm 2 with the prior OGD-based Algorithm 1, yielding a best-of-two CCV guarantee of min{O(√d(1/c*)^d D), O(√T log T)} while preserving O(√T) regret.

## Results

The proposed Algorithm 2 achieves O(√T) static regret while reducing the cumulative constraint violation (CCV) from the prior universal bound of O(√T log T) to an instance-dependent bound O(√d · (1/c*)^d · D), where c* captures the geometric shape of the nested constraint sets. For structured instances (spheres, axis-parallel cuboids, or d=2 monotonic instances), the CCV drops to O(1), independent of the time horizon T. The Switch algorithm (Algorithm 3) provides a best-of-two-worlds guarantee: CCV = min{O(√d · (1/c*)^d · D), O(√T log T)}. For the OCS special case (f_t ≡ 0), Algorithm 2 achieves CCV = O(d^{d/2} · D) = O(1), a fundamental improvement over the O(√T log T) of prior work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is Algorithm 1 from Sinha and Vaze [2024], which achieves O(√T) regret and O(√T log T) CCV using a Lagrangian-style combined loss with adaptive OGD. The proposed Algorithm 2 matches the O(√T) regret but strictly improves CCV: for the simple d=1 instance with time-invariant f and g, Algorithm 1 suffers CCV = Ω(√T log T) (Lemma 6, confirmed experimentally in Fig. 3), while Algorithm 2 maintains O(1) CCV. On synthetic d=10 data (Fig. 4), Algorithm 2 outperforms Algorithm 1 on both regret and CCV. Compared to Neely and Yu [2017] and Liakopoulos et al. [2019] (both O(√T) CCV but requiring Slater's condition and per-round convex optimization), the proposed method achieves the same or better CCV with only a projection step and no Slater's condition. Against Guo et al. [2022] (O(T^{3/4}) CCV) and Yi et al. [2023] (O(T^{1−β/2}) CCV), the improvement is substantial. The Switch algorithm further guarantees that in the worst case the CCV never exceeds O(√T log T), matching the best universal bound while exploiting geometry when available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Sinha and Vaze (NeurIPS 2024), whose algorithm achieves the state-of-the-art O(√T) regret and O(√T log T) CCV for constrained online convex optimization (COCO), and on the classical Manselli-Pucci (1991) result bounding the length of self-expanded curves in convex sets. The key conceptual departure is that prior algorithms treat cumulative constraint violation as a regret minimization problem for the constraint functions g_t, whereas this paper exploits the geometric structure of the nested feasible sets S_t = ∩_{τ≤t} G_τ, using projection curves and mean-width arguments to derive instance-dependent CCV bounds. The Switch algorithm further combines the geometric approach with the prior universal bound to yield a best-of-two-worlds guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the O(√T log T) CCV bound of the state-of-the-art algorithm is not tight even for the simplest instances (d=1, time-invariant functions), where O(1) CCV is achievable by exploiting set geometry. This shifts the conceptual framework for COCO from treating constraints as loss functions to leveraging the nested convex geometry, opening a path toward o(√T) CCV in structured settings. However, the fundamental open question—whether O(√T) regret and o(√T) (or O(1)) CCV can be simultaneously achieved universally—remains unresolved, as the general-case bound still depends on an instance-dependent geometric quantity c* with no universal upper bound. The O(1) CCV result for the online constraint satisfaction (OCS) subproblem is a clean and practically relevant improvement.
