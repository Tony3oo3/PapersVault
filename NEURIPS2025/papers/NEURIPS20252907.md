## Problem

Integrating logical constraints into autonomous driving object detection via t-norm-based constrained loss is promising, but prior works statically selected only 3 t-norms (Product, Gödel, Lukasiewicz) without systematic comparison. The choice of t-norm and regularization weight λ critically affects both constraint satisfaction and detection accuracy, and suboptimal selections can severely degrade model behavior. This paper addresses the gap by conducting an extensive empirical study across 12 t-norms and proposing adaptive mechanisms for t-norm selection and λ scheduling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB driving scene images from the ROAD-R (Oxford RobotCar-based, 41 classes, 122K frames) and ROAD-Waymo-R (Waymo-based, 58 classes, 198K frames) datasets and outputs bounding boxes with multilabel confidence scores over object, location, and action classes in tuple format (o, l, a). Before t-norm computation, detections are pruned to retain only those with at least one label confidence above 0.5, treating lower-confidence outputs as background. The model is instantiated on YOLOv8n and YOLO11n backbones with NMS applied per agent label.

## Method

MOD-ECL extends the prior MOD-CL framework with three key innovations: (1) a library of 12 t-norms (6 non-parameterized including Drastic, Hamacher Product, Nilpotent Minimum, and 6 parameterized including Schweizer-Sklar, Frank, Yager, Sugeno-Weber, Hamacher, Aczél-Alsina), (2) an adaptive t-norm selection algorithm framed as a multi-armed bandit that balances exploration (random selection with probability β) and exploitation (highest-scoring t-norm), updating scores via exponential discounting (δ) on normalized constraint-loss improvements, with a proven regret bound, and (3) a λ scheduler that holds λ constant during a warmup phase then multiplies it by a growth rate γ each epoch, letting the model learn detection first before progressively enforcing constraints. The constrained loss averages the t-norm evaluation of strongly-negated predictions across all constraints and detections, added to the standard neural loss with weight λ.

## Results

On ROAD-R (λ=50), MOD-ECL with YOLOv8n and T_P achieves 61.00 f-mAP@0.5 (vs. 57.55 baseline) while reducing MPBV from 40.45% to 35.66%; YOLO11n with T_L reaches 61.74 f-mAP@0.5 (vs. 61.43 baseline). On ROAD-Waymo-R (λ=100), YOLOv8n with T_SW¹ achieves 37.08 f-mAP@0.5 (vs. 34.88 baseline) and YOLO11n with T_L reaches 35.69 f-mAP@0.5 (vs. 34.33 baseline). The λ-scheduler with γ=1.25 on ROAD-R reduces MPBV to 10.36% (YOLOv8n) and 19.48% (YOLO11n) while maintaining f-mAP@0.5 above 57. The adaptive t-norm algorithm consistently reduces violations below baseline across all (β,δ) configurations, with T_Y² selected most frequently (>40% of iterations under conservative settings).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOD-ECL with YOLOv8n/YOLO11n substantially outperforms the 3D-RetinaNet baseline [26] on ROAD-R, achieving 57.55/61.43 f-mAP@0.5 versus 33.39 (a gap of ~24–28 points), and similarly dominates the I3D baseline [21] on ROAD-Waymo-R with 34.88/34.33 versus 16.38 f-mAP@0.5 (~18–19 points higher). Against its own no-constraint baseline (λ=0), several t-norms improve detection accuracy: T_P raises YOLOv8n f-mAP from 57.55 to 61.00 on ROAD-R, and T_SW¹ lifts YOLOv8n from 34.88 to 37.08 on ROAD-Waymo-R, while simultaneously reducing MPBV (e.g., 40.45%→35.66% on ROAD-R). However, the tradeoff is not uniform: T_L increases accuracy on both datasets but worsens violations (e.g., 39.52%→67.61% MPBV for YOLO11n on ROAD-R), and T_Y² sharply reduces violations (to ~29–30%) at the cost of 4–7 f-mAP points. The external baselines (3D-RetinaNet, I3D) do not report MPBV, so constraint-violation comparisons are limited to the paper's own baseline; the key takeaway is that MOD-ECL's YOLO backbones already far exceed prior SOTA in raw detection accuracy, and the constrained loss can further improve or trade off both metrics depending on t-norm and λ choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constrained-loss paradigm for neurosymbolic object detection introduced by Diligenti et al. [7] and operationalized for autonomous driving by Stoian et al. [26] and the MOD-CL framework [22]. While prior work considered only three t-norms (Product, Gödel, Lukasiewicz) with a fixed regularization weight, MOD-ECL expands the t-norm library to 12 (including parameterized families), introduces a multi-armed-bandit-based adaptive selection algorithm with a regret bound, and adds an exponential λ-scheduler that progressively increases constraint enforcement during training. The I3D baseline from the Waymo Open Dataset [21] and 3D-RetinaNet results from [26] serve as state-of-the-art comparisons on ROAD-Waymo-R and ROAD-R respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic empirical comparison of a broad t-norm family for constrained object detection, revealing that t-norm choice has a non-trivial and dataset-dependent effect on the accuracy–compliance tradeoff (e.g., T_Y² dominates adaptively on ROAD-R but degrades both metrics statically on ROAD-Waymo-R). The λ-scheduler finding that moderate growth (γ=1.25) can reduce violations from ~40% to ~10–19% while preserving accuracy offers a practical knob for deployment. Limitations include evaluation on only two YOLO variants and two datasets, single-run results in the main tables (multi-seed results appear only in the appendix), and theoretical guarantees that rely on stationarity assumptions unlikely to hold throughout training. Open questions include whether the adaptive mechanism generalizes to other neurosymbolic settings (healthcare, robotics) and whether stronger backbones or longer training would shift the optimal t-norm landscape.
