## Problem

Variational mean field approximations and other standard Bayesian inference methods fail on overparametrized deep neural networks because they cannot capture the many-to-one geometry between parameters and functions induced by reparametrizations. This leads to unstable training, poor predictive power, and miscalibrated uncertainties, despite the theoretical promise of Bayesian treatment. The paper asks whether a variational family that explicitly reflects the kernel/image decomposition of the Fisher-Rao metric can restore the benefits of Bayesian inference for deep networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard supervised inputs (32×32 or 28×28 grayscale/color images for classification, 1D data points for regression, and 64-dimensional latent vectors for generative modeling) and produces a fully correlated Gaussian approximate posterior over all network parameters, parameterized by only two scalars: σ_ker (uncertainty in kernel directions that preserve training loss) and σ_im (uncertainty in image directions that change it). At inference, 20 posterior samples are drawn to produce calibrated class probabilities, OOD scores via maximum softmax variance, or per-pixel reconstruction variances for generative models.

## Method

VIKING defines a variational family q(θ) = N(θ | θ̂, Σ) where the covariance Σ = σ²_ker · UUᵀ + σ²_im · (I − UUᵀ) decomposes the parameter space into the kernel and image of the empirical Fisher-Rao metric, making the posterior fully correlated yet governed by only two interpretable hyperparameters. The ELBO is optimized with a single Monte Carlo sample per step, where drawing a sample requires projecting a standard Gaussian vector onto the kernel of the loss-Jacobian matrix via reorthogonalized conjugate gradients (10 iterations, matrix-free). Because the full-data projection is infeasible during mini-batched ELBO optimization, the paper introduces a stochastic extension of von Neumann's alternating projections: at each mini-batch, the kernel sample is updated as ε^(t) = U^(t)U^(t)ᵀ(√γ · ε^(t−1) + √(1−γ) · η^(t)), where γ ∈ [0,1] controls how much previous projection history is retained versus replaced by fresh noise. The KL term is evaluated in closed form using Hutchinson's trace estimator for the kernel dimension R, and the prior precision α is tied to σ_ker (α = 1/σ_ker²) so the KL reduces to an ℓ₂ regularizer on the mean.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, VIKING achieves 87.7% accuracy, 0.407 NLL, 0.041 ECE, and 0.331 MCE, substantially outperforming IVON (83.5% acc, 0.817 NLL, 0.086 ECE) and SWAG (86.5% acc, 0.445 NLL, 0.694 ECE). On SVHN, VIKING reaches 96.0% accuracy with 0.028 ECE, compared to IVON's 94.3% and 0.082 ECE. On the larger Imagenette dataset (ResNet-34, 21.7M params), VIKING achieves 88.7% accuracy and 0.403 NLL versus IVON's 87.6% and 0.656 NLL, though IVON edges ahead on ECE (0.069 vs 0.077) and MCE (0.464 vs 0.612). For OOD detection, VIKING achieves AUC-ROC of 0.972 on MNIST→FMNIST and 0.926 on FMNIST→KMNIST, matching or exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIKING's most direct competitor is IVON (Shen et al., 2024), a mean-field variational method with Newton-like optimization. On the harder, more overparametrized datasets (SVHN, CIFAR-10), VIKING clearly dominates: on CIFAR-10 it achieves 0.407 NLL vs IVON's 0.817 and 0.041 ECE vs 0.086, while on SVHN it reaches 0.177 NLL vs 0.302. On MNIST and Fashion MNIST the two are close, with IVON holding a slight edge on ECE (0.077 vs 0.096 on MNIST). The post-hoc kernel-projection method of Miani et al. (2025) is consistently the weakest baseline, with NLL values 3–6× worse than VIKING (e.g., 2.643 vs 0.407 on CIFAR-10) and ECE near 0.56–0.73, confirming that restricting the posterior to the kernel alone is insufficient. SWAG and Last Layer Laplace are both dominated by VIKING on every calibration metric (ECE, MCE) across all five datasets, with ECE gaps of 0.6–0.7 on SVHN/CIFAR-10. Against the MAP point estimate, VIKING improves accuracy by 1–5 points and reduces ECE by 50–90% on all datasets, demonstrating that the fully correlated kernel/image decomposition captures meaningful uncertainty that a single point estimate cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Roy et al. (2024), who characterized the reparametrization manifold of overparametrized networks via the kernel of the Fisher-Rao metric, and on Miani et al. (2025), who proposed von Neumann alternating projections for post-hoc fully-correlated posterior approximation. VIKING departs from both by (i) embedding the kernel/image decomposition into a proper variational family with an ELBO objective rather than a post-hoc projection, and (ii) introducing a stochastic extension of alternating projections that makes the kernel projection compatible with mini-batched ELBO optimization. It also explicitly contrasts with mean-field variational methods (Blundell et al. 2015, IVON/Shen et al. 2024) and Laplace/SWAG approximations, arguing that their independence or local-curvature assumptions fail to capture the global reparametrization geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, architecture-agnostic recipe for Bayesian training of overparametrized networks that respects reparametrization invariance, addressing a long-standing failure mode where approximate posteriors assign different densities to the same function under different parametrizations. The surprising competitiveness of a two-scalar variational family (σ_ker, σ_im) suggests that the geometry of overparametrization, not the richness of the covariance, is the critical ingredient. Key limitations include the per-sample conjugate-gradient overhead (multiple backward passes per ELBO step) and the observation that full MLE convergence before ELBO training can trap optimization in sharp minima. Open questions include scaling to billion-parameter models, whether richer kernel/image covariance structures (beyond isotropic per-subspace) yield further gains, and whether the stochastic projection hyperparameter γ can be adapted automatically.
