## Problem

Pretrained video diffusion models are trained almost exclusively on perspective footage, creating a fundamental modality gap when applied to 360-degree panoramic video generation. Existing panoramic representations force a trade-off: Equirectangular Projection (ERP) offers end-to-end continuity but introduces severe polar distortion, while Cubemap Projection (CP) preserves local fidelity but fragments the scene into six disjoint faces with hard seams. This makes it difficult to leverage the rich generative priors of perspective video models for high-quality, spatially consistent panoramic video synthesis.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a narrow field-of-view perspective video (e.g., a smartphone clip) and an optional text prompt as conditioning, and outputs a full 360°×180° panoramic video at 512×1024 resolution with up to 49 frames. The input perspective video is projected into the ViewPoint map format and concatenated with the noisy latent and a mask along the channel dimension (expanding the patch embedding from 16 to 33 channels). The method also supports text-only generation without a video input, though the primary use case is perspective-to-panorama video outpainting.

## Method

ViewPoint introduces a novel panorama representation that reprojects six cube faces into four overlapping pseudo-perspective panels by diagonally splitting adjacent faces and concatenating them around a central face, yielding a single unified 2D map with global spatial continuity and minimal distortion. A Pano-Perspective Attention mechanism then operates on this map: Pano-Attention blocks span the entire stitched map (and time) to enforce global coherence across opposite directions, while Perspective-Attention blocks are applied per-panel (via reshaping to 4×batch) to preserve fine-grained texture and motion details. An overlapping gradient fusion mechanism interpolates the rhombus-shaped overlap regions between adjacent subregions using a linearly decaying weight matrix, and a similar petal-shaped fusion handles the U-face semicircles. The entire pipeline is built on Wan2.1 with flow-matching training, fine-tuned end-to-end on 8×A100 GPUs using a joint image-video strategy.

## Results

On the ODV360 dataset evaluated via VBench, ViewPoint (Wan2.1) achieves the best scores across all four metrics: subject consistency 0.8793, imaging quality 0.5927, motion smoothness 0.9800, and dynamic degree 0.9083. The dynamic degree score of 0.9083 substantially exceeds 360DVD (0.5083) and Imagine360 (0.8148), indicating significantly more motion in the generated panoramic videos. In a 50-participant user study, ViewPoint receives the majority of votes across all four subjective dimensions (62% spatial continuity, 64% temporal quality, 54% aesthetic preference, 50% condition alignment).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViewPoint (Wan2.1) outperforms all three baselines on every VBench metric. Against 360DVD, the most striking gap is in dynamic degree (0.9083 vs. 0.5083), reflecting that 360DVD produces nearly static videos; 360DVD also trails in imaging quality (0.5394 vs. 0.5927). Against Imagine360, ViewPoint leads on subject consistency (0.8793 vs. 0.8547), imaging quality (0.5927 vs. 0.5859), and dynamic degree (0.9083 vs. 0.8148), while Imagine360's dual-branch ERP pipeline suffers from spatial-temporal discontinuity and polar distortion. Against Follow-Your-Canvas, a perspective outpainting method, ViewPoint wins on all four metrics, with the largest margin in imaging quality (0.5927 vs. 0.4464), since F-Y-C's projected panoramic views exhibit severe spatial distortion. In the user study, ViewPoint receives 62–64% of votes on spatial continuity and temporal quality, far ahead of the runner-up (CP-ICLoRA at 22% and 8% respectively in the ablation setting, and Imagine360 as the second choice among external baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on pretrained perspective video diffusion models (Wan2.1, AnimateDiff) and adapts them to panoramic video generation through a novel representation (ViewPoint Map) and attention mechanism (Pano-Perspective Attention). It departs from prior panoramic video generation methods (360DVD, Imagine360) that rely on equirectangular projection and suffer from severe polar distortion, and from cubemap-based approaches that introduce spatial discontinuities at face boundaries. The key conceptual departure is the pseudo-stitching scheme that merges cube faces into overlapping perspective panels, enabling a single unified 2D representation that is both spatially continuous and locally distortion-free, thereby remaining compatible with the native 3D attention of pretrained DiT-based video models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a fundamental modality gap between the perspective data that dominates diffusion model training and the panoramic data needed for VR, world models, and spatial intelligence applications. The ViewPoint Map representation and Pano-Perspective Attention offer a practical pathway to leverage existing pretrained video models without requiring large-scale panoramic video datasets, which remain scarce. Key limitations include reliance on a two-stage upscale pipeline for high resolution, watermark and mosaic artifacts inherited from training data, and the absence of end-to-end ultra-high-resolution generation. Open questions include whether the representation generalizes to longer video sequences (autoregressive extension) and whether the approach can be extended to interactive or controllable panoramic generation.
