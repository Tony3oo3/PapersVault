## Problem

Cross-Domain Few-Shot Object Detection requires both strong generalization to unseen domains and accurate localization, but existing well-trained detectors (e.g., DETR) excel at localization while lacking cross-domain generalization, and vision foundation models (VFMs) generalize well but lack precise localization. Prior CD-FSOD methods address this by redesigning detector architectures and retraining on base classes, incurring substantial computational cost and failing to leverage existing well-trained detectors. This paper asks whether a well-trained detector can be extended to detect novel classes in unseen domains without any retraining on base data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254896/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images from an unseen target domain along with 1, 5, or 10 annotated support samples per novel class, and produces bounding boxes with class labels. It operates as a plug-in module on a frozen, pre-trained DINO detector (ResNet-50 backbone, trained on COCO) combined with a frozen DINOv2 VFM, requiring fine-tuning only of the classification head, regression head, and the proposed MoE parameters. The method is evaluated on six cross-domain datasets (ArTaxOr, Clipart1k, DIOR, DeepFish, NEU-DET, UODD) spanning insects, clipart, remote sensing, underwater fish, industrial defects, and marine organisms.

## Method

The paper proposes a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module that injects VFM features into a well-trained DETR backbone without modifying its architecture. Two routing mechanisms guide the fusion: an Expert-wise Router (ER) applies global average pooling followed by a learned FC layer and softmax on detector features to produce per-layer gating weights that select which VFM layer's features best match the semantic level of each detector backbone layer; a Region-wise Router (RR) applies a channel-wise FC and spatial softmax to generate region-wise gating weights that emphasize foreground and suppress background in the VFM feature map. To bridge dimensional mismatches with minimal parameters, a Shared Expert Projection (SEP) projects common features (e.g., object shape) across all experts via one shared linear layer, while a Private Expert Projection (PEP) projects expert-specific features via per-expert linear layers, with the channel split controlled by hyperparameter m. The final fused feature at each backbone layer is computed as F_f^l = F^l + Σ_n(α·G_e^{n,l} * F_{d'}^{n,l} + β·G_r^{n,l} * F_{d'}^{n,l}), with α=β=0.5, and this process is repeated across all backbone layers before feeding into the standard DETR decoder.

## Results

On the CD-FSOD benchmark (trained on COCO, evaluated on six cross-domain datasets), the proposed method achieves 18.2/34.7/39.8 mAP under 1/5/10-shot settings, outperforming the previous SOTA CD-ViTO by 4.3/8.6/10.2 mAP and the DINO DETR baseline by 11.1/21.9/24.6 mAP. The method also surpasses open-vocabulary detectors (Grounding DINO: 17.7 mAP, YOLO-World: 13.5 mAP) and MLLMs (Qwen: 12.3 mAP, Ferret: 3.6 mAP) by 22.1–36.2 mAP under the 10-shot setting. Extensibility is confirmed across DAB-DETR (9.6→35.9), DETA (16.1→36.5), and AlignDETR (16.0→37.5) mAP, and across backbones (ResNet50: 39.8, Swin-B: 42.2, ViT-L/14: 44.4 mAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across the six cross-domain datasets. Against the DINO DETR baseline (ResNet50), it gains 11.1/21.9/24.6 mAP at 1/5/10-shot, demonstrating that the MoE module effectively transfers DINOv2's generalization without retraining on base classes. Compared to the previous SOTA CD-ViTO (ViT-L/14), the method achieves +4.3/+8.6/+10.2 mAP, with particularly large margins on NEU-DET (+5.5/+7.6/+10.9) and UODD (+5.9/+12.8/+15.1) where domain shift is most severe. Against open-vocabulary and MLLM baselines (10-shot), the method surpasses Grounding DINO by 22.1 mAP, YOLO-World by 26.3 mAP, Qwen by 27.5 mAP, and Ferret by 36.2 mAP, attributed to its cross-domain fine-tuning strategy versus zero-shot inference. The method also demonstrates strong extensibility: applying it to DAB-DETR, DETA, and AlignDETR yields 26.3/20.4/21.5 mAP improvements respectively, and upgrading the backbone from ResNet50 to ViT-L/14 adds a further 4.6 mAP. Notably, the method outperforms Frozen DETR (which uses a two-stage base-training + fine-tuning paradigm) by 25.5/16.0/6.3/16.6/14.6/18.9 mAP on the six cross-domain datasets while also improving COCO in-domain performance from 51.9 to 55.2 mAP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DINO DETR detector (Zhang et al., ICLR 2023) as a frozen baseline and the DINOv2 self-supervised vision foundation model (Oquab et al., 2023) as a feature provider, but departs fundamentally from prior CD-FSOD methods (AcroFOD, OA-FSUI2IT, CD-ViTO) that require two-stage training and detector redesign. Rather than using the VFM as a replacement backbone (as in DE-ViT or Frozen DETR) or modifying the detector architecture, the paper introduces a Mixture-of-Experts module with Expert-wise and Region-wise Routers plus Shared/Private Expert Projections that grafts VFM features onto the existing detector's backbone layers with minimal trainable parameters. This positions the work at the intersection of MoE routing strategies and foundation-model augmentation, treating the VFM as an independent expert rather than a structural component of the detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: practitioners can take any well-trained DETR-family detector and extend it to cross-domain few-shot detection without the costly base-class retraining that prior CD-FSOD methods demand, reducing both computational and time overhead. The demonstrated extensibility across DAB-DETR, DETA, AlignDETR, and Grounding DINO suggests a broadly applicable plug-in paradigm. However, the approach is currently validated only on DETR-family detectors and a single VFM (DINOv2-large), and the added MoE module roughly doubles training time; whether the routing mechanism generalizes to non-DETR architectures (e.g., anchor-based detectors) or to more extreme domain shifts remains open. The finding that DINOv2-giant underperforms DINOv2-large also raises questions about the optimal VFM scale for this augmentation strategy.
