## Problem

While sparse autoencoders have revealed interpretable features in language models and single-step diffusion models, the temporal evolution of visual concepts through the multi-step reverse diffusion process remains unexplored. It is unclear what level of scene composition is encoded in early diffusion steps (where the model output is still pure noise), how representations refine over time, and whether discovered concepts can be causally manipulated to steer generation in an interpretable, time-dependent manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255566/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (from LAION-COCO) fed into Stable Diffusion v1.4 and extracts residual updates (Δ) from three cross-attention blocks (down_block, mid_block, up_block) at timesteps t∈{0.0, 0.5, 1.0}, with spatial dimensions of 16×16×1280 or 8×8×1280 depending on the block. Outputs include a concept dictionary mapping each SAE feature (CID) to a list of object labels, predicted segmentation masks for prompt-specified objects at any intermediate timestep, and modified generated images produced by spatially-targeted or global interventions applied in the SAE latent space.

## Method

The paper trains separate k-sparse autoencoders (TopK activation, n_f=4d=5120 features) on residual updates of individual cross-attention blocks at each timestep, using an AuxK auxiliary loss to prevent dead features. A scalable vision-only pipeline (RAM + Grounding DINO + SAM) assigns object labels to SAE features by matching activation maps against ground-truth segmentation masks (IoU > 0.5), avoiding LLM-based labeling. Image composition is predicted by building a spatial conceptual map from top-activating concepts and comparing Word2Vec embeddings to target objects. Two intervention types are designed: spatially-targeted edits that amplify a concept in a designated region and zero it elsewhere (Eq. 2), and global edits that add a concept vector uniformly across all spatial locations (Eq. 3), both with norm-based normalization. The key empirical finding is a three-stage temporal hierarchy: composition is controllable at t∈[0.6,1.0], style is controllable at t∈[0.2,0.6] while composition is locked, and only minor textural changes are possible at t∈[0,0.2].

## Results

The paper demonstrates that image composition emerges as early as the first reverse diffusion step, with predicted segmentation masks achieving IoU ≈ 0.26 from mid_block SAE activations at t=1.0, before any visual information is present in the model output. Spatially targeted interventions succeed 80% of the time at t=1.0 (vs. 25% random baseline) but drop to 35% at t=0.5 and 23% at t=0.0, confirming that composition is locked in by the middle of diffusion. Global style interventions achieve 93% edit success with ΔCLIP +0.021 at t=0.5, compared to 78% (+0.006) at t=0.0 and 69% (+0.007) at t=1.0, establishing the middle stage as the optimal window for style manipulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability study rather than a method comparison, so no external baselines are benchmarked. The paper's internal comparisons are temporal: the same SAE intervention technique is applied at three diffusion stages (early t∈[0.6,1.0], middle t∈[0.2,0.6], final t∈[0,0.2]) to isolate when each type of control is effective. Against the random baseline (0.25, reflecting uniform quadrant probability), spatially targeted interventions achieve 0.80 success at t=1.0 but fall to chance level (0.23–0.35) at later stages. For global style interventions, the middle stage (t=0.5) dominates with 93% edit success and the largest ΔCLIP (+0.021), while early-stage interventions (69% success, ΔCLIP +0.007) alter composition rather than style, and final-stage interventions (78% success, ΔCLIP +0.006) produce only minor textural changes. The paper also compares three U-Net blocks (down_block, mid_block, up_block) and finds up_block yields the most accurate segmentation predictions while mid_block (lowest spatial resolution) gives the least, with no strong specialization for composition-related information across blocks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAE-based mechanistic interpretability framework from Gao et al. (TopK sparse autoencoders) and Surkov et al. (SAEs on single-step SDXL Turbo activations) to multi-step diffusion, training time-specific SAEs to capture how interpretable concepts evolve across the reverse diffusion trajectory. It departs from prior SAE-on-diffusion work in two key ways: (1) analyzing temporal dynamics rather than a single denoising step, and (2) replacing LLM-based concept labeling with a scalable vision-only pipeline (RAM + Grounding DINO + SAM) that yields pixel-level annotations. The paper also positions itself against cross-attention saliency maps and Jacobian-based editing directions, arguing that SAE-discovered concepts provide a more holistic and granular characterization of internal representations while simultaneously enabling interpretable causal interventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that coarse scene composition is already encoded in SAE activations before the first complete diffusion step completes challenges the common intuition that early diffusion is a 'chaotic' stage devoid of structural information, and provides a principled basis for time-adaptive editing (composition control early, style control mid, texture refinement late). A key limitation is the U-Net skip-connection leakage that dilutes interventions; the authors hypothesize that diffusion transformers (e.g., FLUX) would mitigate this, but the work is validated only on SDv1.4's U-Net architecture. Open questions include whether the temporal hierarchy of concept emergence generalizes across architectures, whether the vision-only labeling pipeline scales to more diverse concept types (e.g., abstract styles, spatial relationships), and whether the discovered concepts can be leveraged for more fine-grained compositional control beyond quadrant-level layout.
