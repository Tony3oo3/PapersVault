## Problem

Existing layer-wise post-training quantization (PTQ) methods treat each layer's quantization as an independent optimization problem, ignoring how quantization errors propagate and accumulate across layers. This leads to exponential error growth that severely degrades model performance, especially in low-bit regimes (INT2–INT3), where standard methods become practically infeasible. The paper identifies this error accumulation as the fundamental bottleneck limiting further progress in layer-wise PTQ.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (Llama-2 7B/13B/70B, Llama-3 8B, Mistral-7B) and a small calibration set (128 segments of 2048 tokens from C4 or Pile) to produce per-channel or group-wise quantized weight matrices at INT4, INT3, or INT2 precision. A key detail is that the correction term requires both full-precision activations X_l and quantized activations X̂_l at each layer, computed via forward passes through the original and partially-quantized networks respectively, making the method inherently sequential from input to output layers.

## Method

QEP reformulates the standard layer-wise PTQ objective from minimizing ‖W_l X_l − Ŵ_l X_l‖² to minimizing ‖W_l X_l − Ŵ_l X̂_l‖², where X̂_l are quantized activations carrying accumulated upstream errors. This yields a closed-form weight correction W_l* = W_l + α_l · W_l δ_l X̂_l^T Ĥ_l^{−1}, where δ_l = X_l − X̂_l captures the accumulated quantization error and Ĥ_l is the Hessian from quantized activations. The tunable propagation strength α_l ∈ [0,1] acts as a regularization parameter that prevents overfitting to the calibration set, particularly in parameter-heavy MLP blocks. The corrected weights W_l* are then quantized using the same Hessian-based machinery as existing methods (GPTQ, AWQ, QuIP), making QEP a plug-and-play, orthogonal enhancement that preserves the computational complexity of the base PTQ pipeline while provably reducing output quantization error.

## Results

On WikiText-2 perplexity, QEP consistently reduces error across all tested PTQ methods and bit-widths, with the most dramatic gains at extreme low-bit settings. For Llama-2-7B at INT2, QEP-enhanced QuIP achieves 11.97 PPL versus 65.59 without QEP (a 5.5× reduction), and QEP-enhanced RTN drops from 539.87 to 17.31 at INT3 (a 31× reduction). At INT4, QEP-enhanced QuIP on Llama-2-7B reaches 5.75 PPL, approaching the FP16 baseline of 5.47, while QEP-enhanced GPTQ on Llama-2-70B achieves 3.576 PPL versus 3.594 without QEP. Zero-shot accuracy gains are similarly pronounced: QEP-enhanced QuIP at INT2 on Llama-2-70B reaches 0.6998 average accuracy, comparable to RTN/GPTQ at INT3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QEP is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement on top of four representative layer-wise PTQ baselines: RTN, GPTQ, AWQ, and QuIP. Against QuIP, QEP delivers the largest absolute gains, especially at INT2 where Llama-2-7B perplexity drops from 65.59 to 11.97 and Llama-2-70B from 6.54 to 5.87, making 2-bit quantization practically viable. Against GPTQ, QEP consistently improves perplexity at INT4 (e.g., 6.08→5.93 on 7B) and INT3 (10.88→7.90 on 7B), and also reduces INT2 perplexity (13051→7214 on 7B). Against RTN, QEP produces the most dramatic relative improvements at INT3 (539.87→17.31 on 7B, a 31× reduction) and INT4 (6.12→6.02 on 7B). Against AWQ, QEP improves INT4 (5.83→5.76) and INT3 (15.30→11.13) perplexity on Llama-2-7B, though at INT2 the already-severe degradation of AWQ is not fully rescued (199449→229888 on 7B), suggesting AWQ's salience-based scaling interacts poorly with the error-correction term at extreme low bit. Overall, QEP wins or ties on perplexity in nearly all settings, with the exception of a few INT2 AWQ cases where both variants remain impractical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GPTQ/Optimal Brain Compression lineage of layer-wise PTQ, which has dominated the field since 2022. The key departure is a reformulation of the per-layer objective: instead of minimizing reconstruction error against a shared activation input (Eq. 1), QEP minimizes the discrepancy between full-precision and quantized outputs computed with their respective upstream inputs (Eq. 3), yielding a closed-form weight correction term that compensates for accumulated upstream errors. The authors explicitly position QEP as orthogonal to and composable with recent advances in rotation-based (QuIP), salience-based (AWQ), and nonlinear quantization, making it a plug-and-play enhancement rather than a competing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying exponential error accumulation as the core bottleneck of layer-wise PTQ, the paper reframes a problem the community had been approaching through increasingly sophisticated per-layer quantizers. The practical impact is most visible at INT2, where QEP-enhanced QuIP reduces Llama-2-7B perplexity from 65.6 to 12.0, making 2-bit deployment feasible where it was previously infeasible. Open questions include adaptive per-layer tuning of the propagation strength α_l (currently fixed at 0.5 or 0), integration with nonlinear and block-wise quantization schemes, and whether the framework can push performance beyond QAT at extreme bit widths. The method's reliance on a small calibration set and its sensitivity to calibration data quality (though less than GPTQ) remain practical constraints.
