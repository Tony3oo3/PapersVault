## Problem

Training neural PDE surrogates requires expensive high-fidelity simulations, and existing active learning methods for PDE solvers are either pool-based (restricted to discrete candidate sets that cannot adapt to model progress) or coordinate-based (tied to PINN architectures and inapplicable to grid-based surrogates like U-Net). There is no general mechanism that enables continuous, model-aware query synthesis while remaining compatible with diverse acquisition strategies.

## Input / Output

The method takes PDE initial conditions parameterized by sinusoidal wave coefficients (amplitudes, wavenumbers, phases) and scalar PDE parameters (e.g., viscosity) as the continuous design space, along with a trained neural PDE surrogate and its SWAG posterior over output-layer parameters. It produces a batch of refined initial-condition settings that are both high-EIG and spatially diverse; these are then fed to a numerical PDE solver to generate ground-truth trajectories for surrogate retraining. The optimization operates on the generative parameters of the ICs rather than on the full spatial field, keeping the search space low-dimensional.

## Method

PaPQS performs gradient ascent in the continuous IC parameter space using Adam, where the policy function combines an EIG acquisition term with a Kozachenko-Leonenko entropy-based diversity regularizer to prevent mode collapse. EIG is estimated via the MINE-f mutual information lower bound using a neural critic T_φ(θ, u), with posterior parameter samples drawn from SWAG applied only to the output layer (justified by Proposition 1) to avoid costly ensemble training. Gradients with respect to the IC parameters are computed by backpropagating through the autoregressive neural PDE solution trajectories, evaluated at three time steps (first, middle, last) for efficiency, and an amortized update jointly refines both the ICs and the critic. A threshold filter (η) on the policy score change rejects non-contributive updates, and the entire module is designed as a plug-in that can refine candidate ICs from any existing pool-based or feature-based AL strategy.

## Results

PaPQS consistently reduces RMSE across all four PDE systems (Burgers', KS, Combined Equation, 2D-CNS) compared to pool-based and static baselines. On the Burgers' equation, PaPQS reduces average RMSE by 48.7% over random sampling, 15.3% over SBAL, and 7.4% over LCMD across active learning iterations. When combined with existing AL strategies (SBAL+PaPQS, LCMD+PaPQS), the plug-and-play refinement yields further gains over the stand-alone baselines. Gains are most pronounced on simpler dynamics (Burgers') and more moderate on tightly coupled multi-field systems (2D-CNS), while PaPQS also achieves lower wall-clock time per unit accuracy than random sampling due to faster surrogate convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaPQS outperforms all four baseline families across the four PDEs. Against static DoE baselines (random, LHS), PaPQS achieves the largest absolute RMSE reductions (e.g., 48.7% on Burgers'), confirming that adaptive query synthesis substantially exceeds uniform or stratified sampling. Against pool-based predictive-variance methods (SBAL, Top-K), PaPQS wins by 15.3% on Burgers' and shows narrower confidence intervals, indicating more stable acquisition signals; the paper attributes this to EIG filtering out chaotic, aleatoric-dominated regions that inflate variance-based scores. Against feature-based pool methods (LCMD, CoreSet), PaPQS still improves RMSE by 7.4% (LCMD) on Burgers', and the hybrid LCMD+PaPQS combination further outperforms LCMD alone, validating the plug-and-play design. Against the hybrid BAIT strategy (LCMD/CoreSet + posterior variance minimization), PaPQS maintains consistent advantages, particularly in early iterations where labeled data is scarce. The one setting where gains are most modest is 2D-CNS, where complex multi-variable coupling makes informative IC optimization harder, though PaPQS still provides stable improvement over all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the active learning literature for neural PDE solvers (established by the AL4PDE benchmark and pool-based methods like SBAL, CoreSet, and LCMD) by replacing discrete candidate-pool selection with continuous query synthesis via gradient ascent. It builds on the MINE-f mutual information estimator (Kleinegesse & Gutmann, ICML 2020) and SWAG (Maddox et al., NeurIPS 2019) for efficient EIG estimation and posterior sampling, and incorporates Kozachenko-Leonenko entropy for batch diversity. The key conceptual departure is from predictive-variance-based acquisition, which the authors argue is misleading under chaotic PDE dynamics and recurrent prediction, toward an EIG-based criterion that explicitly targets epistemic uncertainty reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design of PaPQS as a refinement module over existing AL strategies (random, SBAL, LCMD) makes it broadly applicable across PDE systems and surrogate architectures without requiring architectural changes, which is practically valuable for the growing SciML community. However, gains are modest on tightly coupled multi-field systems (2D compressible Navier-Stokes), and the framework currently operates only in the spatial/parameter design space, leaving temporal-domain active learning as an open direction. Future work on adaptive step sizes, physics-informed regularization, and extension to non-periodic boundary conditions could broaden applicability.
