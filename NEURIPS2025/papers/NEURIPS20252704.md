## Problem

General policy methods for multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) suffer from two fundamental inefficiencies: (1) policies under different preferences redundantly re-learn common environment dynamics knowledge, and (2) optimizing the shared value network for one preference can degrade value estimates for conflicting preferences, creating a tug-of-war that impedes accurate value approximation. These issues result in high sample complexity and suboptimal Pareto frontier coverage.

## Input / Output

The policy network takes the raw environment state s_t (17-dim for HalfCheetah, 27-dim for Ant, or RGB images for DMC), the 50-dimensional latent state representation z_t from OADM, and a continuous preference vector ω on the simplex, producing a Gaussian action distribution. The value function additionally consumes the latent state-action representation c_{t+1} and the action a_t. The preference space is the (d−1)-simplex for d objectives, and the method outputs both actions and per-preference Q-value estimates that collectively approximate the Pareto frontier.

## Method

COLA builds on Envelope SAC and introduces two components. First, the Objective-agnostic Latent Dynamics Model (OADM) consists of a state encoder E_ψ and a transition model D_ψ trained with a temporal consistency loss that forces D_ψ(z_t, a_t) to predict E_ψ(s_{t+1}), extracting a 50-dim latent space of shared dynamics knowledge without any reward or preference signal. All subsequent multi-objective value and policy optimization operates in this compact latent space rather than the raw state space. Second, Conflict Objective Regularization (COR) computes the cosine similarity (stiffness) ρ(ω_i, ω_j) between the Q-loss gradients of the current preference ω_i and a randomly sampled preference ω_j; when ρ falls below a conflict boundary c=0.25, a weighted penalty term λ·(Q(ω_j) − Q_old(ω_j))² is added to the critic loss, where λ = max(c − ρ, 0) scales with conflict severity. This surrogate-based regularization (avoiding MAML-style second-order computation) constrains the value network from drifting away on conflicting preferences during each update, enabling simultaneous maintenance of accurate value estimates across the preference space.

## Results

Across ten multi-objective continuous control tasks (2d–5d objectives), COLA achieves an average 26.8% improvement in Hypervolume (HV) and 12% improvement in Expected Utility (UT) over the second-best baseline. The advantage grows with task complexity: on Ant-3d, COLA reaches HV=18.58×10⁹ versus 9.88×10⁹ for CAPQL (88% gain), and on Hopper-5d it attains HV=10.41×10¹⁶ versus 9.80×10¹⁶ for PGMORL. In pairwise Episodic Dominance (ED) comparisons, COLA consistently outperforms all baselines, with ED values of 0.85–0.97 across tasks. On the DMC image-based benchmark, COLA improves HV by 48% over Envelope-RAD on Cheetah_Run-2d (12.71 vs 8.61 ×10⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COLA outperforms all four main MORL baselines (Envelope SAC, PGMORL, Q-Pensieve, CAPQL) on every metric across all ten MuJoCo tasks. The margin is most pronounced on multi-objective tasks with 3+ objectives: on Ant-3d, COLA's HV of 18.58×10⁹ nearly doubles CAPQL's 9.88×10⁹, and on Hopper-5d it surpasses PGMORL (the best baseline there) by 6% in HV. On the DMC image-based benchmark, COLA-RAD improves HV by 48% over Envelope-RAD on Cheetah_Run-2d and by 811% on Hopper_Hop-2d (31.59 vs 3.47 ×10²). On MO-Gym, COLA dominates GPI/LS, GPI/PD, and MORL/D on Mo-lunar-lander (HV 6.42 vs 4.66/4.37/3.84 ×10⁸; UT 30.18 vs 15.12/13.52/3.71), but shows a slight UT disadvantage on Mo-mountain-car (−50.42 vs GPI/PD's −42.47), while still matching GPI/PD on HV (3.31 vs 3.46 ×10³). Overall, COLA wins or ties on 29 of 30 task-metric comparisons in the main table, with the sole exception being a marginal UT gap on Ant-5d where all methods are within 0.01×10³.

## Relation to Prior Work

COLA builds directly on the Envelope SAC framework (Envelope Q-Learning + Soft Actor-Critic) as its base algorithm, extending the general policy methods lineage that includes CN, Envelope Q-Learning, PD-MORL, Q-Pensieve, and CAPQL. Its two core components repurpose ideas from adjacent areas: the Objective-agnostic Latent Dynamics Model adapts temporal consistency losses from single-objective world models (TD-MPC, self-predictive representations) but deliberately strips out task-specific reward and value signals to remain preference-agnostic, while the Conflict Objective Regularization imports the stiffness metric from neural network generalization theory to quantify and penalize optimization-direction conflicts between preferences in the shared value network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COLA addresses two concrete bottlenecks in general-policy MORL—redundant learning of shared dynamics across preferences and destructive interference in the shared value network—yielding consistent 20–30% HV gains over strong baselines across 2-to-5-objective MuJoCo tasks and DMC image-based tasks. However, the paper provides no theoretical guarantees (convergence, optimality), is restricted to linear scalarization, and acknowledges policy degradation/forgetting that population-based methods avoid. Open directions include extending to nonlinear utility functions, improving representation expressivity, and integrating evolutionary algorithms for collaborative multi-objective optimization, which the authors identify as a key next step.
