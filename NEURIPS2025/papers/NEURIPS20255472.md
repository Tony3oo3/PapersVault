## Problem

Diffusion models for time-series forecasting and imputation require many sequential denoising steps (typically NFE=20+) at inference, making them slow and resource-intensive, especially for long horizons. While the forward diffusion process induces a structured, frequency-dependent smoothing of the data distribution, existing methods do not exploit this structure to enable single-step generation. The paper asks whether the specific frequency-domain characteristics of time-series data can be leveraged to learn a single-step inverse of the diffusion operator that matches or exceeds multi-step baselines.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series x ∈ R^{C×d} with a binary mask M ∈ {0,1}^{C×d} indicating observed versus unobserved entries, along with a noise level σ. For forecasting, M masks future timesteps; for imputation, M zeroes out consecutive segments sampled from a geometric distribution. At inference, the model draws a single noisy sample at σ_max, injects the observed portion via masking, and produces the full completed/forecasted sequence in one forward pass. Probabilistic outputs are obtained by drawing 100 independent trajectories and reporting the median as the point estimator.

## Method

The core contribution is a learnable single-step inverse operator φ(γ,τ,·) that maps a noisy sample at noise level σ(τ) directly to a cleaner sample at σ(γ) in one step, replacing the multi-step reverse diffusion. The architecture embeds a Frequency-Aware Block (FAB) that projects the input onto a wavelet basis, applies a learnable noise-level-dependent modulation α_θ(σ(τ)) to each spectral coefficient, and reconstructs the signal—selectively restoring the high-frequency components that forward diffusion exponentially damps (Theorem 3.2). The operator is parameterized as a convex combination φ(γ,τ,x) = ((τ−γ)/τ)H_θ(x) + (γ/τ)x, where H_θ incorporates the FAB within an EDM-style skip-residual network. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a standard EDM denoising loss enforces the boundary condition φ(0,τ,x_σ)=x_0; second, a semigroup composition loss enforces φ(ρ,γ,φ(γ,τ,x))=φ(ρ,τ,x) with an adaptive linear schedule that progressively refines the noise-level discretization grid, ensuring the learned operator is consistent across all intermediate noise scales.

## Results

On 192-step multivariate time-series forecasting, SSOL achieves MSE of 0.369 (ETTm1), 0.238 (ETTm2), 0.209 (Weather), 0.189 (Solar-Energy), 0.155 (Electricity), and 0.550 (Traffic), using only a single denoising step (NFE=1) versus 20 steps for multi-step baselines. On probabilistic evaluation (CRPS_sum), SSOL attains the best scores on ETTm2 (0.131), Solar-Energy (0.116), and Traffic (0.176). For imputation, SSOL consistently achieves the lowest MSE across all missing ratios (10%–90%) on ETTh1 and Energy, outperforming Diffusion-TS, CSDI, and DiffWave. Inference is 3.5× faster than D3U and 25× faster than NsDiff, with 35–85% less GPU memory usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSOL (1 NFE) outperforms the strongest multi-step probabilistic baseline D3U (20 NFE) on 5 of 6 forecasting datasets, with the largest margins on Solar-Energy (0.189 vs 0.237 MSE, −20%) and Electricity (0.155 vs 0.179, −13%); D3U retains a narrow edge on ETTm1 (0.368 vs 0.369) and Traffic (0.468 vs 0.550). Against NsDiff, SSOL wins on all six datasets, with gaps of 0.05–0.11 in MSE. Compared to deterministic forecasters, SSOL matches or beats iTransformer and PatchTST on five datasets while remaining a fully probabilistic model that produces 100-sample distributions. On imputation (ETTh1, Energy, Weather, ETTm1), SSOL achieves the lowest MSE at every missing ratio from 10% to 90%, surpassing Diffusion-TS by 15–50% and CSDI/DiffWave by wider margins at high missingness. The CRPS_sum results confirm that SSOL's single-step operator captures aggregate channel distributions well (best on 4/6 datasets), though D3U edges it on per-channel CRPS for ETTm1 and Weather.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) continuous-time score-based diffusion (Song et al., 2021b) and the EDM training framework (Karras et al., 2022), which provide the forward SDE and denoising loss; (2) neural operator learning (Kovachki et al., 2023; Li et al., 2021), which motivates viewing the diffusion semigroup as a learnable map between function spaces; and (3) frequency-domain time-series modeling (Crabbe et al., 2024; Galib et al., 2024), which operate in Fourier/wavelet space but still rely on multi-step sampling. The key departure is replacing iterative reverse diffusion with a single-step inverse operator constrained by the semigroup composition law, augmented by a frequency-aware block whose design is directly motivated by the exponential Fourier-mode damping proven in the Fokker-Planck analysis (Theorem 3.2). Unlike consistency distillation (Song et al., 2023) or progressive distillation (Salimans and Ho, 2022), which reduce steps via teacher-student paradigms, this approach learns the inverse natively through a two-stage objective (boundary denoising + composition consistency) without requiring a pre-trained multi-step teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing inference from 20 NFEs to 1 while matching or exceeding multi-step diffusion baselines on six forecasting and four imputation benchmarks makes probabilistic time-series generation substantially more deployable in latency-sensitive settings. The semigroup composition training is a principled alternative to ad-hoc step-schedule tuning, and the frequency-aware block provides a theoretically grounded mechanism for restoring the high-frequency content that forward diffusion provably destroys. Open questions include whether the single-step operator generalizes to very long horizons (beyond 720 steps) or ultra-high-dimensional series (thousands of channels), whether the wavelet-based FAB can be replaced by other localized transforms for non-stationary data, and whether the semigroup constraint remains well-conditioned when the noise schedule is highly non-uniform. The paper does not explore adversarial robustness, out-of-distribution generalization, or the interaction between the single-step design and conditional guidance strategies beyond simple masking.
