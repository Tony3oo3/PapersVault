## Problem

AI-based talking-head videoconferencing systems transmit only compact pose-expression latents and re-synthesize video at the receiver, making them vulnerable to puppeteering attacks where an adversary substitutes a victim's reference portrait and drives the reconstruction with their own motion. Because every frame is synthetically generated, standard deepfake and synthetic-video detectors are fundamentally unable to distinguish authorized from unauthorized identity. Existing puppeteering defenses either require per-user enrollment of motion signatures or rely on compute-heavy decode-and-re-encode pipelines that degrade under large head rotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two low-dimensional vectors available at the receiver: the per-frame pose-expression latent z_t transmitted by the sender and the static reference portrait embedding f(R) sent at call setup. No RGB frames, facial landmarks, or additional sensors are required. It produces a per-frame cosine similarity score in the learned EBL space, which is then aggregated over a 40-frame window by an LSTM to yield a single binary authentication decision (authorized vs. puppeteered) with sub-millisecond latency.

## Method

The core insight is that pose-expression latents inherently leak persistent biometric identity cues (e.g., inter-ocular distance, jaw curvature) that can be amplified while suppressing pose and expression variance. Two lightweight projection heads (h1 for live latents, h2 for the reference) remap inputs into a shared compact Enhanced Biometric Leakage (EBL) space, with separate heads accommodating the different distributions of drifting per-frame embeddings versus the fixed reference. Training uses a novel Pose-Conditioned Large-Margin Cosine Loss (PC-LMCL) that constructs hard negatives by rendering impostor identities at the identical pose, forcing the gradient to discriminate on biometric cues alone while being orthogonal to pose variation; extreme-pose frames are excluded to keep gradients focused on informative biometric variation. An LSTM then fuses similarity scores over a 40-frame temporal window to produce stable, low-latency authentication decisions, achieving 75 FPS on an RTX 3090 with under 1M parameters.

## Results

Across 15 dataset–generator combinations (3 datasets × 5 talking-head generators), the proposed EBL method achieves a mean detection AUC of 0.971, with a minimum of 0.952 on any single combination. In the cross-domain setting (trained only on NVIDIA VC, 46 identities), it retains 0.925 average AUC on unseen CREMA-D and RAVDESS data—a mere 5% drop. Inference runs at 75 FPS on an RTX 3090 with under 1 M parameters, well above the 60 FPS real-time threshold, compared to 32 FPS for the prior best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms the closest prior puppeteering defense, Vahdati et al. [10], by 2.6 AUC points on average (0.971 vs. 0.945), with the gap widening to 5.4 points on SDFR-generated videos (0.967 vs. 0.913) where landmark-based re-encoding degrades most. All seven deepfake detectors (Efficient ViT, CCE ViT, CNN Ensemble, TALL, SupCon, CLR Net, LAANet) cluster between 0.52 and 0.67 AUC, confirming that real-vs-synthetic pixel-level cues are uninformative when every frame is AI-generated; the strongest, SupCon [15], reaches only 0.672. In the cross-domain protocol (train on NVIDIA VC only, test on CREMA-D and RAVDESS), the proposed method drops just 2 AUC points (0.945→0.925) while Vahdati et al. drops 2.1 points (0.924→0.903), and all deepfake baselines remain near chance. The paper also reports a 46% relative error reduction over Vahdati et al., and the minimum per-combination AUC (0.952) exceeds Vahdati's worst case (0.908), indicating more consistent detection across generators and datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of talking-head videoconferencing security and biometric representation learning. It departs from prior puppeteering defenses—Avatar Fingerprinting (Prashnani et al., which requires per-user enrollment) and Vahdati et al. (which decodes and re-encodes RGB frames)—by operating entirely in the transmitted pose-expression latent space with no enrollment or landmark tracking. Conceptually, it repurposes a known vulnerability (identity traces persisting in pose-conditioned representations, as noted in prior disentanglement work) into a defensive signal, and applies large-margin cosine embedding theory to learn a compact biometric-leakage space that cancels pose while amplifying identity. The temporal LSTM fusion and extreme-pose exclusion protocol are practical additions not present in prior puppeteering defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AI-based videoconferencing systems from NVIDIA and Google approach commercial deployment, puppeteering represents a real-time impersonation threat that all existing deepfake and synthetic-media detectors are structurally unable to catch—every frame is synthetic by design. This paper provides the first enrollment-free, real-time (75 FPS, <1M params) defense that plugs directly into the latent stream, making it immediately deployable without modifying the conferencing pipeline. Key limitations remain: extreme head rotations, occlusion, poor lighting, and motion blur degrade detection, and the method has not been tested against adaptive adversaries who could learn to suppress biometric leakage in the latent. Open questions include whether the defense generalizes to future codec revisions that might explicitly strip identity from the latent, and whether a formal security guarantee (beyond the angular margin proposition) can be established under adversarial perturbation of the transmitted embedding.
