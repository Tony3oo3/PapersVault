## Problem

In MLLMs, the projector connecting a pretrained vision encoder to a pretrained LLM is typically trained only with caption loss, which implicitly and coarsely aligns vision tokens with word embeddings. This results in weak patch-level alignment where many visual tokens correspond to meaningless or garbled words, limiting region-specific understanding and causing hallucinations. The paper asks how the projector actually aligns multimodal information and how to quantify and improve this alignment at the patch level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250032/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed by a frozen CLIP-ViT-L@336px encoder (producing 256 vision tokens) along with text captions and, during patch-aligned pretraining, per-object segmentation masks with associated tags from the PAD dataset. The projector outputs aligned vision embeddings in the LLM's embedding space (matching Vicuna-7B's dimensionality), which are then consumed by the frozen LLM to produce captions, bounding boxes for referring expression comprehension, or text answers for VQA and instruction-following tasks.

## Method

The paper introduces Patch-Aligned Training, which augments the standard caption loss during stage-1 pretraining with a lightweight patch-alignment loss that maximizes cosine similarity between mask-selected vision embeddings (averaged over patches covered by each object's segmentation mask) and the corresponding text embedding (averaged over subtoken embeddings from the LLM's word matrix). This patch loss requires no LLM forward pass—only the embedding matrix—making it computationally negligible. To enable this training, the authors build the Patch-Aligned Dataset (PAD) by running an automated annotation pipeline (RAM → Grounding DINO → SAM) over the 558K LLaVA pretraining images, yielding 2.3M region annotations with 33.5K unique tags. The balance parameter β is linearly scheduled from 0 to 5 across pretraining to avoid premature over-compression, and the analysis uses Von Neumann entropy to quantify information compression and matching pursuit to decompose vision embeddings into sparse combinations of LLM word embeddings, revealing the multi-semantic structure of each patch.

## Results

Under the same SFT setting as LLaVA-1.5 (Vicuna-7B, 336px), Patch-Aligned Training achieves a ~16% average improvement on referring expression comprehension (e.g., RefCOCO val: 65.97 vs. 56.22), a ~4% gain on VQA benchmarks (GQA: 63.0 vs. 62.0; OKVQA: 58.3 vs. 53.4), and a ~3% improvement on modern instruction-following benchmarks (MMMU: 36.56 vs. 35.30; MMVet: 31.61 vs. 30.70). The patch-aligned projector also achieves stronger compression (ΔH = 3.84 vs. 2.80 for LLaVA) and better patch-level alignment (Align(V,W) = 0.279 vs. 0.142), enabling higher-quality captions (SPICE: 0.1710 vs. 0.1571) with the LLM and vision encoder frozen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary and fairest comparison is against LLaVA-1.5, which uses the identical architecture (CLIP-ViT-L@336px + Vicuna-7B + 2-layer MLP), training data, and SFT protocol; Patch Aligned wins on every reported metric, with the largest gap on OKVQA (+4.9) and RefCOCO val (+9.75). Against LLaVA (original, 224px), direct numerical comparison is not possible as the paper reports no scores for it in Table 7. Compared to BLIP-2 (Vicuna-13B, 224px) and InstructBLIP (Vicuna-7B/13B, 224px), Patch Aligned achieves substantially higher GQA scores (63.0 vs. 41.0 and 49.2–49.5), though these comparisons involve different image resolutions and LLM sizes. Against Qwen-VL (Qwen-7B, 448px), Patch Aligned edges out on GQA (63.0 vs. 59.3) and SciQA (68.7 vs. 67.1) despite using a smaller image size (336 vs. 448). IDEFICS-9B/80B and Shikra are listed in Table 7 but with incomplete or missing scores, limiting direct comparison. The compatibility study (Table 9) further confirms that the patch-aligned projector transfers consistently to Llama 3.1 8B and C-Abstractor, maintaining improvements over their respective LLaVA counterparts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA/LLaVA-1.5 two-stage training paradigm (frozen vision encoder + trainable projector + LLM) and departs from prior work by treating the projector not as a black-box connector but as an object of systematic analysis. It extends the platonic representation hypothesis into a concrete measurement framework using Von Neumann entropy for compression and a novel patch-level alignment metric (Align(V,W)) that goes beyond coarse image-caption similarity scores (AC Score, MIR) and logit-lens localization analyses. The proposed patch-aligned training loss is conceptually simpler than concurrent approaches like SEA (which relies on CLIP and predefined word lists) and complementary to inference-time grounding methods (GPT4RoI, Kosmos-2, GLaMM) that add tokens or external modules rather than improving the representation itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a computationally negligible cosine-similarity loss added during projector pretraining can yield a 16% improvement on referring expression grounding, suggesting that much of the fine-grained understanding gap in current MLLMs stems from weak patch-level alignment rather than insufficient model capacity. The released Patch-Aligned Dataset (2.3M annotated regions from 558K images) and the annotation pipeline (RAM + Grounding DINO + SAM) provide a reusable resource for the community. Key open questions remain: the multi-semantic alignment hypothesis is only qualitatively verified via matching pursuit, the approach aligns each patch to a single averaged word embedding which may limit expressiveness, and the optimal compression level (the tradeoff shown in Figure 4) is dataset- and task-dependent, leaving room for adaptive alignment strategies.
