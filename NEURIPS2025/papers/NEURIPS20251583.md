## Problem

Existing visual tokenizers for autoregressive video generation produce a fixed number of tokens regardless of content complexity, wasting capacity on simple scenes and under-allocating for complex ones. Moreover, tokenizer training and downstream AR model training are decoupled into separate stages, leaving the tokenizer unaware of generation quality. This paper addresses both gaps by learning an adaptive, task-aware token budget directly from visual complexity priors and reinforcement learning signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (16 frames at 128×128 in Stage 1; 80 frames split into 5 clips in Stage 2) and patchifies it into 1024 tokens with f_T=4, f_H=8, f_W=8. It outputs a variable-length discrete token sequence whose length is sampled from a learned per-video distribution (ranging from a minimum threshold up to K=1024), along with a reconstructed video from the decoder. Unlike fixed-rate tokenizers, the same input can yield different token counts across training samples, and at inference the truncation index is set to the argmax of the learned taildrop probability.

## Method

VaporTok uses a two-stage training pipeline. Stage 1 introduces Probabilistic Taildrop: a Taildrop Probability Query Module (2 transformer blocks + softmax over K=1024 positions) predicts a categorical distribution over truncation indices, supervised by a Gaussian prior whose mean is derived from the video's spatial entropy × temporal frame-difference complexity mapped through an empirical CDF. During training, a truncation index is sampled from this distribution and only the first t latent tokens are retained for reconstruction. Stage 2 formulates the multi-clip tokenization as a non-Markovian sequential decision process and optimizes the taildrop module via Parallel Sample GRPO, which draws G=8 full truncation paths per video from the entire categorical distribution (rather than top-k) to avoid mode collapse. The composite Vapor Reward combines four signals—token efficiency, inter-path diversity, reconstruction MSE, and AR prior top-5 accuracy—allowing non-differentiable generation-quality feedback to shape the tokenizer's adaptivity without requiring gradient flow through the discrete sampling operation.

## Results

On UCF-101 class-conditional video generation, VaporTok (Stage 1) uses an average of 498 tokens per video (vs. 1024 for LARP) and achieves rFVD 53.9 and gFVD 80, matching LARP-512 (rFVD 53.3, gFVD 86) with fewer tokens. After Parallel Sample GRPO (Stage 2), the average token count drops to 361 (~35% of the original 1024) while gFVD remains at 98. A key advantage is the gFVD/rFVD ratio: VaporTok achieves 1.48 (Stage 1) and 1.47 (Stage 2) versus 2.85 for LARP-1024, indicating substantially less autoregressive error accumulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest baseline LARP-1024 (1024 tokens, rFVD 20, gFVD 57 on UCF), VaporTok uses roughly half the tokens (498) and trades some reconstruction quality (rFVD 53.9 vs. 20) for a dramatically smaller gFVD/rFVD gap (1.48 vs. 2.85), indicating far less AR error accumulation. Compared to LARP-512 (512 tokens, rFVD 53.3, gFVD 86), VaporTok matches reconstruction quality with fewer tokens and improves gFVD by 6 points (80 vs. 86). Against OmniTokenizer (1280 tokens, gFVD 191 on UCF), VaporTok uses 61% fewer tokens and achieves a 2.4× lower gFVD (80 vs. 191). After GRPO fine-tuning, VaporTok-GRPO further compresses to 361 tokens (~35% of LARP-1024) while maintaining gFVD 98, still competitive with LARP-512's 86 at a similar token budget. The paper does not outperform LARP-1024 on absolute rFVD or gFVD at the same token count, but its core claim is that adaptive tokenization with a complexity-informed prior achieves comparable generation quality at substantially lower token cost while reducing the AR error-accumulation gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

VaporTok builds directly on LARP's holistic-token VQ-VAE architecture and its learned autoregressive prior, extending it with a probabilistic taildrop mechanism that replaces the fixed token budget with a complexity-conditioned truncation distribution. It departs from prior adaptive tokenizers (ElasticTok, FlexTok, ALIT) that use uniform sampling or threshold-based selection by introducing a learned Gaussian prior over token counts and, more substantially, by formulating tokenization as a non-Markovian sequential decision process optimized via GRPO—a technique previously applied only to LLM reasoning and visual generation, never to tokenizer training itself. The Vapor Reward design composes efficiency, diversity, reconstruction, and generation signals into a single RL objective, drawing on the multi-reward composition patterns from recent GRPO applications in vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to use reinforcement learning to train a visual tokenizer, opening a path toward task-aware tokenization where the tokenizer's adaptivity is directly shaped by downstream generation quality rather than reconstruction alone. However, the current scope is narrow: GRPO optimizes only the truncation probability (not the full VAE), considers only class-conditional generation as the downstream task, and operates on small datasets (UCF-101, Kinetics-600) at modest resolution. The persistent mode collapse after GRPO (entropy drops from 5.11 to 4.11 even with parallel sampling and diversity reward) and the modest absolute gains (VaporTok-GRPO's rFVD of 66.6 is worse than LARP-512's 53.3) suggest the RL signal is still underexploited. Open questions include whether GRPO can be extended to the full encoder-decoder, whether a true MDP formulation (with inter-clip state) would alleviate the non-Markovian collapse, and whether the framework transfers to understanding or unified generation-and-understanding settings.
