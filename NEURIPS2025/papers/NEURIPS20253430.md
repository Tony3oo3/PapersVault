## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) frequently exhibit overthinking, generating excessive redundant reasoning steps that inflate inference cost and can degrade accuracy. Existing mitigation approaches rely on external monitoring or auxiliary models, adding computational overhead, while prior mechanistic-interpretability steering methods using high-dimensional difference-in-means directions quickly plateau or even deteriorate as intervention strength increases due to interference noise in the orthogonal complement of the activation manifold.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253430/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes a standard text prompt (instruction) and a pre-computed unit steering direction in R^d, derived offline via PCA on residual-stream activations from 500 redundant and 500 concise reasoning samples on OpenMathInstruct-2. The direction is applied as a rank-one projection to every residual-stream activation at every layer and token position during autoregressive generation, with a scalar intervention strength α (typically 0.3–0.7) controlling the trade-off between token reduction and accuracy. The output is the model's reasoning trace and final answer, now with substantially fewer tokens (up to 71% reduction) while preserving or improving Pass@1.

## Method

The paper first extracts an overthinking direction via difference-in-means between redundant and concise residual-stream activations, then shows empirically and theoretically (Theorems 4.1–4.2) that this high-dimensional direction decomposes into a true overthinking component lying on a low-dimensional activation manifold M plus an orthogonal interference component r_other whose expected norm is tr((I−P_M)Σ_noise) and which amplifies layer-by-layer through attention and non-linearities. The key intervention is Manifold Steering: projecting the raw direction onto M via P_M = U_eff U_eff^T (top-k PCA components, k≈10 capturing >70% variance), which nullifies the interference term and yields a purified direction r_overthinking. The purified direction is then applied as a rank-one ablation h' = h − α·r_overthinking(r_overthinking^T h) across all layers, enabling sustained token reduction at higher α without the rebound observed with the unprojected direction. The approach is training-free, incurs negligible latency, and transfers across domains (math, code, knowledge QA) and tasks (including refusal ablation) without re-computation.

## Results

Manifold Steering achieves up to 71% token reduction on GSM8K (R1-1.5B: 2035→593 tokens) while maintaining or improving Pass@1 accuracy across all four mathematical benchmarks. On R1-7B, the method reduces tokens by 62% on GSM8K (1143→440) and 42% on MATH500 (3824→2239) with accuracy preserved or slightly improved (88.4% vs. 88.2% vanilla). Cross-domain transferability is confirmed with 12–27% token reduction on LiveCodeBench and GPQA-Diamond without accuracy loss. On the refusal-ablation task (AdvBench), manifold steering achieves 100% jailbreak success rate versus 74% for the single-direction baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dynasor [16], Manifold Steering consistently achieves substantially larger token reductions (e.g., 62% vs. 36% on R1-7B GSM8K; 42% vs. 29% on MATH500) while Dynasor degrades accuracy on harder benchmarks (−2.5% on AMC2023, −3.3% on AIME2024 for R1-7B). Against SEAL [8], Manifold Steering again wins on token reduction (62% vs. 32% on GSM8K, 42% vs. 34% on MATH500 for R1-7B) and avoids the accuracy drops SEAL exhibits (−0.4% on MATH500, −3.3% on AIME2024). The advantage is most pronounced on simpler problems (GSM8K, MATH500) where overthinking is most severe, and narrows on harder benchmarks (AMC2023, AIME2024) where models genuinely need more reasoning tokens. In the cross-task refusal-ablation experiment, Manifold Steering reaches 100% JSR on AdvBench compared to 74% for the original single-direction method [2] at α=2.0, demonstrating that projecting onto the low-dimensional manifold generalizes beyond overthinking mitigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-steering paradigm established by Arditi et al. (2024) for refusal ablation and the difference-in-means direction extraction of Belrose (2023) to the overthinking problem in Large Reasoning Models. It departs from prior overthinking-mitigation work (Dynasor, SEAL, Thought Manipulation) by operating entirely within the model's internal activation space rather than relying on external monitoring or auxiliary models. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis that naive high-dimensional steering directions contain an orthogonal interference component that plateaus and then degrades performance, and the proposed fix—projecting the direction onto a PCA-estimated low-dimensional manifold—is a targeted refinement of the steering framework rather than a new intervention mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: up to 71% token reduction on GSM8K with maintained or improved accuracy, and cross-domain transfer to code generation and knowledge QA without domain-specific tuning, all at near-zero additional latency. The theoretical characterization of interference noise (Theorems 4.1–4.2) provides a general diagnostic for why activation steering in high-dimensional spaces fails, which could inform future steering work beyond overthinking. Open questions include applicability to multimodal LLMs, dynamic adaptation of intervention strength to task difficulty, and whether the low-dimensional manifold structure holds for non-distilled or differently trained reasoning models.
