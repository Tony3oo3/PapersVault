## Problem

Prior nonasymptotic value-iteration guarantees for general (multichain) average-reward MDPs fail to adequately capture the navigation subproblem of steering towards the best recurrent class: existing bounds either depend on excessively large parameters (B or 1/Δ) that remain large even when navigation is trivial, or only yield non-vacuous guarantees after the navigation problem is solved exactly. This paper develops faster VI algorithms with sharper complexity measures that provide meaningful performance at all iteration budgets.

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a finite MDP (state space S, action space A, transition kernel P, reward r ∈ [0,1]^S×A), an initial value vector h_0 ∈ R^S, and an iteration budget n. They output a deterministic policy π̂ (greedy with respect to the final iterate) along with nonasymptotic suboptimality and fixed-point error bounds that hold for all n ≥ 1. A key distinction from prior work is that the bounds depend on the gain-dropping time T_drop (expected time spent taking actions that reduce the optimal gain) rather than the transient time B or the inverse gain-optimality gap 1/Δ, and T_drop = 0 whenever ρ* is constant (e.g., weakly communicating MDPs).

## Method

The paper introduces a refined complexity parameter T_drop (gain-dropping time) and builds two main algorithms around it. Algorithm 1 (Approximately Shifted Halpern Iteration) runs n Picard steps to estimate the gain ρ* and produce a warm-start x_n aligned with nρ*, then switches to Halpern iteration on the approximately shifted operator T̂ = T − ρ̂; the alignment of x_n with nρ* is essential because it ensures the final greedy policy approximately maximizes P_π ρ*, thereby solving the navigation subproblem. Algorithm 3 (Warm-Start Halpern-Then-Picard) solves the average-reward problem via a discounted reduction: it first runs E' undiscounted Picard steps to reduce the initial value error at a sublinear O(1/t) rate (Lemma 4.3), then applies Algorithm 2 (Halpern-Then-Picard, which achieves the optimal γ^t/Σγ^i fixed-point error rate for contractive operators in Banach spaces) for the remaining iterations. The theoretical foundation includes new suboptimality decompositions (Lemma 2.1, Corollary 2.2) that separate the navigation and recurrent subproblems, a coupling-based analysis for nonexpansive operators without fixed points (Lemma 3.1), and a restricted commutativity property of the Bellman operator that enables the shifted Halpern approach for general MDPs.

## Results

The paper establishes that Algorithm 1 (Approximately Shifted Halpern Iteration) achieves policy suboptimality O((T_drop + 1)/n · ‖h₀ − h‖∞) for all n ≥ 1 (Theorem 3.4), and improves to O(1/n · ‖h₀ − h‖∞) in the large-n regime n ≥ 4‖h₀ − h‖∞/Δ (Theorem 3.5). For the discounted VI approach, Algorithm 3 achieves a final suboptimality bound of O((T_drop + 1) · min{‖h‖_sp, ‖h^{π*}‖_sp + T_drop + ‖h^{π*}‖_sp · T_drop} / n) (Theorem 4.5). Algorithm 2 (Halpern-Then-Picard) achieves the optimal fixed-point error rate O(γ^t / Σᵢ₌₀ᵗ γⁱ · ‖x₀ − x*‖) for γ-contractive operators in general Banach spaces (Theorem 4.2), matching the Park–Ryu lower bound up to a constant of 8e/(1+γ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against two prior nonasymptotic VI results for general average-reward MDPs. Against Zurek & Chen [2025b], the new T_drop parameter strictly dominates B (T_drop ≤ B) and equals zero when ρ* is constant (e.g., weakly communicating MDPs), whereas B can be arbitrarily large in that case; additionally the rate removes the log n factor. Against Lee & Ryu [2024], the fixed-point error rate O(1/n · ‖h₀−h‖∞) improves upon their O(‖h‖∞ · (1+1/Δ)/n) by a factor of O(1+1/Δ), and crucially holds for all n ≥ 1 rather than only n > K = O(‖V₀−h‖∞/Δ). Theorem 3.5 further shows that in the large-n regime, general MDPs achieve the same O(1/n) policy suboptimality rate as weakly communicating MDPs, disproving a conjecture in Lee & Ryu [2024] that general MDPs are inherently harder. The discounted VI approach (Theorem 4.5) additionally avoids the ‖h‖_sp dependence that can hide a 1/Δ factor (as shown in Theorem D.4), providing a guarantee that depends only on T_drop and ‖h^{π*}‖_sp.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of nonasymptotic value-iteration analysis for average-reward MDPs initiated by Lee and Ryu (2024), who first applied Halpern iteration to the nonexpansive average-reward Bellman operator. The key departure is the introduction of the gain-dropping time T_drop as a sharper complexity parameter that captures the navigation subproblem's difficulty, replacing the coarser B (transient time) and 1/Δ (minimum gain gap) parameters that degrade in easier instances. The paper also builds on the discounted-to-average-reward reduction framework of Zurek and Chen (2025b) and the optimal fixed-point iteration theory of Contreras and Cominetti (2022) and Park and Ryu (2022), combining them into a unified algorithmic pipeline (warm-start Picard → Halpern → Picard) that achieves improved rates for both discounted and average-reward settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central theoretical finding—that general multichain MDPs are no harder than weakly communicating MDPs in terms of fixed-point error convergence rate—disproves a conjecture in Lee and Ryu (2024) and simplifies the theoretical landscape for average-reward RL. The Halpern-Then-Picard algorithm (Algorithm 2) is notable for its simplicity and optimality in general Banach spaces, potentially useful beyond MDPs. Open questions include whether matching lower bounds exist for policy suboptimality (as opposed to fixed-point error) in the general MDP setting, and whether the results extend to stochastic (sample-based) Bellman evaluations, which the authors identify as a key limitation. For practitioners, the sharper T_drop parameter provides a more meaningful measure of instance difficulty than prior parameters.
