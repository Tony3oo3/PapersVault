## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can be implicitly induced to generate harmful content through images, and fine-tuning them for capability often erodes previously learned safety alignment. Existing multimodal safety datasets fail to disentangle helpfulness and safety into independent preference signals, and it remains unverified whether the constrained optimization framework (Safe RLHF) that works for text-only LLMs can be stably applied in the multimodal setting.

## Input / Output

The MLLM receives an RGB image paired with a text prompt and generates a text response; the guard model (Beaver-Guard-V) additionally outputs a fine-grained safety classification (Safe, Minor, Moderate, Severe) for both the prompt and the generated response. The training dataset BeaverTails-V provides dual preference annotations where helpfulness and safety are independently labeled per response pair, with unsafe pairs further graded by harm severity, enabling the cost model to learn a continuous safety signal rather than a binary safe/unsafe distinction.

## Method

Safe RLHF-V formulates multimodal safety alignment as a constrained optimization problem solved via Lagrangian min-max: a Reward Model-V (RM-V) trained on helpfulness preferences provides the reward signal, while a Cost Model-V (CM-V) trained on safety preferences with an added classification loss term provides the constraint. The key technical contribution is a budget bound on the Lagrange multiplier λ (projected into [0, ν_max]) that prevents the model collapse observed when naive Lagrange updates are applied in the multimodal setting, where optimization is far more unstable than in text-only RLHF. Training uses PPO with a clipped surrogate loss, GAE for advantage estimation, a per-token KL penalty against a reference model, and a PTX loss on an SFT dataset to preserve generation quality. The framework is validated on LLaVA-1.5/1.6-7B and Qwen2-VL-7B, achieving +34.2% safety and +34.3% helpfulness over the base models.

## Results

Safe RLHF-V achieves the best dual-objective performance across all three MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2-VL-7B, LLaVA-1.6-7B) and five safety benchmarks. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, Safe RLHF-V reaches 0.579 safety and 0.770 helpfulness on BeaverTails-V, 0.720/0.774 on MM-SafetyBench, and 0.852/0.793 on VLGuard, consistently improving both dimensions over the base model (0.5/0.5). The paper reports an average safety improvement of 34.2% and helpfulness improvement of 34.3% over the unaligned baseline. For the guard model, Beaver-Guard-V achieves 85% accuracy and 88% F1 in multi-level classification, substantially outperforming Llama-Guard-3-11B-Vision (64% accuracy, 74% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Safe RLHF-V (dual-objective) consistently outperforms all single-dimension baselines in simultaneously improving both safety and helpfulness. Single-dimension safety methods (DPO-Safety, PPO-Safety, MM-RLHF-Safety) achieve high safety scores (e.g., PPO-Safety reaches 0.957 on BeaverTails-V) but severely degrade helpfulness (0.253), confirming the helpfulness-safety trade-off. Conversely, helpfulness-focused methods (DPO-Helpful, PPO-Helpful) boost helpfulness (0.851, 0.672) while sacrificing safety (0.235, 0.528). Safe RLHF-V avoids this trade-off via Lagrangian constrained optimization, achieving 0.579 safety and 0.770 helpfulness on BeaverTails-V — surpassing PPO-Helpful on helpfulness (+0.098) while maintaining far higher safety (+0.051). For the guard model, Beaver-Guard-V (multi-level) achieves 85% accuracy and 88% F1, outperforming Llama-Guard-3-11B-Vision (64% accuracy, 74% F1) by 21 and 14 percentage points respectively, with a substantially lower false positive rate (20% vs. 71%). The dynamic Lagrangian update with budget bound proves critical: static reward shaping with fixed λ₀ produces parabolic performance sensitivity, while the proposed mechanism maintains stable ~70% win rates regardless of initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Safe RLHF framework (Dai et al., 2024), which decouples helpfulness and safety into separate reward and cost models optimized via Lagrangian min-max, from the text-only LLM setting to multimodal large language models. It departs from prior MLLM alignment work (RLHF-V, Llava-RLHF, MM-RLHF) that addresses single-dimensional objectives like hallucination reduction or factual accuracy without explicit safety constraints. The key methodological departure is the introduction of a budget bound on the Lagrange multiplier to stabilize the otherwise collapsing multimodal RL training, and the construction of BeaverTails-V as the first dataset with independently annotated dual preferences (helpfulness and safety) plus multi-level severity labels for MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end framework that simultaneously improves both safety and helpfulness in MLLMs through constrained optimization, addressing a practical gap where prior MLLM alignment methods risked degrading safety while boosting capability. The BeaverTails-V dataset and Beaver-Guard-V guardrail provide reusable infrastructure for the multimodal safety community. However, the work is limited to image-text pairs, and the constrained optimization approach, while validated here, remains computationally expensive and sensitive to hyperparameters; generalization to video, audio, or other modalities is left open. A key open question is whether the budget bound is sufficient for larger-scale or multi-turn multimodal interactions where safety constraints compound over time.
