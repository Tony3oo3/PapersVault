## Problem

Current AI systems fail in safety-critical environments with dense smoke, thermal distortion, and structural deformation, where perception breakdowns carry life-threatening consequences. Existing benchmarks rely on clean imagery, synthetic simulations, or isolated objects without temporal reasoning, and none test whether models can maintain object identity across irreversible physical transformations in unpaired scenes. Fire360 addresses this by introducing a benchmark for robust perception and a novel Transformed Object Retrieval (TOR) task that evaluates episodic memory under degradation.

## Input / Output

Inputs are 360° equirectangular frames (3840×1920, 60 fps from a Ricoh Theta V) or rectilinear projections with 90° FOV, alongside text prompts, 10–20 second video clips, and pristine object exemplar images for TOR. In TOR specifically, Grounding DINO proposes ~36.2 candidate regions per frame at a 0.4 confidence threshold, and the model must select the single degraded counterpart matching the reference exemplar via cosine similarity of VLM embeddings, with success defined as IoU ≥ 0.5. Outputs span text answers, BLEU-4-scored captions, bounding boxes, binary safety labels with justifications, and a single retrieved region.

## Method

Fire360 is a benchmark built from 228 professionally recorded 360° firefighter training videos (50 hours total) annotated with temporal action segments, spatial bounding boxes, and degradation metadata, verified by certified instructors (κ = 0.87–0.91). Five zero-shot tasks probe distinct competencies: VQA (spatial reasoning), Temporal Action Captioning, Object Localization, Safety-Critical Reasoning, and TOR. TOR is the core methodological novelty: given a pristine exemplar, the model must retrieve its fire-damaged counterpart from a completely unpaired 360° scene with no temporal or spatial continuity, using cosine similarity between VLM embeddings (CLIP, BLIP-2, or GPT-4o) of Grounding DINO-proposed regions. All 17 evaluated models operate in a zero-shot or prompted setting with no fine-tuning on Fire360, reflecting real-world deployment where generalization to unseen degraded inputs is essential. The benchmark reveals a 43.7% human-model gap on TOR (83.5% vs. 39.8% for GPT-4o), exposing fundamental failures in transformation-invariant recognition.

## Results

Across all five Fire360 tasks, state-of-the-art models exhibit large gaps versus human experts. GPT-4o achieves 53.8% top-1 accuracy on VQA (vs. 91.4% human), 39.8% retrieval accuracy on TOR (vs. 83.5% human), and 28.9% checklist accuracy on Safety-Critical Reasoning (vs. 94.6% human). Grounding DINO scores 38.4% mean IoU on Object Localization (vs. 85.2% human), and GLaMM-7B reaches 0.341 BLEU-4 on Temporal Action Captioning (vs. 0.85 human). The average human–model gap is 57.2% on VQA and 43.7% on TOR, with performance dropping below 10% in the most degraded VQA regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 17 models across five tasks, all evaluated zero-shot on 360° equirectangular inputs. On VQA, GPT-4o leads among models at 53.8%, ahead of LLaVA-v1.5-13B (50.3%), Qwen-VL (47.2%), and BLIP-2 (42.7%), yet all trail the 91.4% human ceiling by 38–49 points. On TOR, GPT-4o (39.8%) outperforms BLIP-2 (35.1%) and CLIP ViT-B/32 (32.5%), but the 43.7-point gap to human experts (83.5%) is the largest single-task disparity in the benchmark. On Safety-Critical Reasoning, Qwen-VL (32.5%) narrowly beats GPT-4o (28.9%), with both far below the 94.6% human score; the extended evaluation adds Claude-3 Sonnet (33.0%) and Llama-Guard-3-8B (27.4%), confirming no safety-specialized model closes the gap. For Object Localization, Grounding DINO (38.4% mean IoU) is the sole detector evaluated, with OWLv2 (39.8%) and YOLO-World (36.5%) in the extended set showing comparable degradation under occlusion. Across all architectural families—instruction-tuned VLMs, temporal captioners, open-vocabulary detectors, safety classifiers, and retrieval models—the mean human–model gap remains 54.3% (std = 8.7), indicating systemic brittleness rather than model-specific failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of panoramic video understanding, safety-critical AI, and episodic memory. Prior work on 360° video (360+x, KITTI-360, Ego4D) targets clean or low-risk scenes, while firefighting-specific datasets (FASDD, D-Fire, ACT360) focus on fire classification or action detection without modeling degradation or human-agent interaction. The Transformed Object Retrieval (TOR) task departs from existing retrieval benchmarks (EgoTracks, FOR, Obelics) that assume spatial or temporal continuity between reference and query, instead requiring identity matching across unpaired scenes with irreversible physical transformation. The paper also builds on memory-augmented vision and world-model literature conceptually, but operationalizes these ideas in a safety-critical, degradation-heavy setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fire360 is the first benchmark to systematically evaluate VLMs under compound visual degradation (smoke, thermal distortion, low light) in 360° safety-critical video, exposing a 43.7% human-model gap on TOR that no prior benchmark has surfaced. The consistent failure across 17 architecturally diverse models suggests a fundamental limitation in current vision-language representations for transformation-invariant recognition, rather than a model-specific weakness. Key open questions include whether fine-tuning or material-aware pretraining can close the gap, whether temporal modeling in TOR would improve robustness, and whether the single-institute collection limits generalization to diverse real-world deployments. Broader applications span medical imaging (tracking deformed tissues), disaster response, and post-fire insurance workflows.
