## Problem

Existing symbolic logical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., FOLIO, ProofWriter, Multi-LogiEval) operate exclusively on text, while multimodal benchmarks (e.g., MMMU, MathVista, LogicVista) test contextual or mathematical reasoning without requiring application of formal logical rules. No standard definition or benchmark exists for reasoning that must jointly integrate visual and textual evidence through explicit symbolic logic (e.g., Modus Ponens, first-order quantifier tracking), a capability critical for high-stakes domains like autonomous driving and medical diagnosis.

## Input / Output

Each instance pairs an RGB image (sourced from COCO, Flickr30k, ScienceQA, MIMIC, RVL-CDIP, or manually collected traffic reports) with a text passage of 35–1,484 tokens containing contextual rules and a question. The model must produce either a truth label (True/False/Unknown) or a 4-way multiple-choice selection, accompanied by an explicit step-by-step reasoning chain of 2–9 formal inference steps. Visual details are pre-extracted via GPT-4o to ensure fine-grained descriptions, and the benchmark spans 7 domains (healthcare, traffic, sports, entertainment, social issues, science, finance) across propositional, first-order, and non-monotonic logic.

## Method

LogiCAM is a modular, iterative CoT framework built on GPT-4.1 that decomposes multimodal symbolic reasoning into three coupled modules. The Premise Selector identifies which symbolic rules in the text are relevant to the image and extracts the corresponding visual details, producing a compact critical-premise set that avoids noise from irrelevant multimodal content. The Reasoning Type Identifier then decides, at each iteration, whether a formal inference rule (e.g., Modus Ponens, Disjunctive Syllogism) can be applied to the selected premises or whether commonsense heuristics must supplement the symbolic step. The Reasoner executes the chosen inference—applying syllogistic deduction for symbolic steps or generating a contextually plausible implication for heuristic steps—and the loop repeats, appending each derived conclusion to the context, until the accumulated knowledge entails the answer or a maximum iteration bound is reached. This design avoids the information loss inherent in translating images to text before feeding a separate theorem prover, instead letting the VLM directly access visual content while enforcing formal logical structure at each deduction step.

## Results

On MuSLR-Bench (1,093 instances across 7 domains), the best baseline GPT-4.1 achieves only 46.84% overall accuracy with three-shot CoT prompting, while the best open-weight model InternVL3 reaches 45.20%. LogiCAM, built on GPT-4.1, boosts overall accuracy by 14.13 percentage points to approximately 60.97%, with the largest relative gains in first-order logic (+48.93%), followed by propositional logic (+31.93%) and non-monotonic logic (+26.17%). Performance degrades sharply with reasoning depth: GPT-4.1 drops ~16% from 2–3 steps to 8–9 steps, while LogiCAM maintains 54.61% at the deepest level (surpassing GPT-4.1 by 13 points at depths 8–9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LogiCAM (on GPT-4.1) outperforms all seven baselines across every logic type and domain. Against the strongest baseline GPT-4.1 (46.84%), LogiCAM gains +14.13 points overall, with the most dramatic improvement in FOL where GPT-4.1 scores 0% on several domains (Healthcare, Entertainment, General) and LogiCAM reaches 50–75%. Among open-weight models, InternVL3 (45.20%) is the closest competitor but still trails LogiCAM by ~16 points; Qwen2.5-VL (41.63%) and GPT-4o (38.93%) form a second tier. Claude-3.7-Sonnet (33.49%) and LLaVA (35.13%) perform weakest overall, with Claude suffering a sharp 20% accuracy drop at deeper reasoning chains. In the supplementary comparison (Table 2), LogiCAM also substantially outperforms the Logic-LM+VLM neuro-symbolic baseline (60.44% vs. 35.14% on PL; 42.55% vs. 32.65% on FOL), confirming that native multimodal access without lossy image-to-text translation is critical for symbolic reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established but previously disjoint research threads: textual symbolic logic reasoning benchmarks (FOLIO, ProofWriter, Multi-LogiEval) and multimodal reasoning benchmarks (LogicVista, VisuLogic, MMMU). Prior symbolic reasoning work assumes fully specified text-only inputs, while prior multimodal benchmarks test contextual or mathematical reasoning without explicitly requiring application of formal logical rules (Modus Ponens, De Morgan's Law, etc.) grounded in both visual and textual evidence. The proposed LogiCAM framework also departs from the dominant neuro-symbolic paradigm (LLM + theorem prover, e.g., Logic-LM, LINC) by using a VLM to directly approximate symbolic inference over multimodal inputs, avoiding the information loss inherent in translating visual content into text before passing it to a text-only prover.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MuSLR-Bench is the first benchmark to explicitly test formal symbolic logical deduction over joint image-text inputs, filling a gap that is critical for high-stakes applications like autonomous driving and medical diagnosis where reasoning errors have real consequences. The finding that ~70% of failures across all evaluated models stem from logical misalignment between modalities (rather than from incorrect rule application) reframes the bottleneck as a cross-modal grounding problem rather than a pure logic problem, offering a concrete target for future architecture design. Open questions include whether dedicated cross-modal alignment modules trained with logic-grounded objectives can close this gap, and whether the performance ceiling improves with deeper multimodal pretraining or remains fundamentally limited by the current VLM paradigm.
