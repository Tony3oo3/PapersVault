## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves high appearance fidelity but suffers from multi-view inconsistency that limits geometric accuracy, while 2D Gaussian Splatting enforces geometric consistency at the cost of over-smoothed surfaces and lost high-frequency detail. No prior method unifies both representations in a single exchangeable framework that dynamically exploits their complementary strengths for general-purpose novel view synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254938/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with camera poses (from COLMAP/SfM) and initializes a hybrid set of 2D and 3D Gaussians from the sparse point cloud, with types randomly assigned at start. Images are downsampled by dataset-specific factors (1×–8×) following prior work. The output is a hybrid 2D/3D Gaussian scene representation that renders novel views via a single CUDA-accelerated alpha-blending pass, achieving ~100–270 FPS at inference depending on scene complexity.

## Method

EGGS introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, Hybrid Gaussian Rasterization renders 3D Gaussians via affine projection and 2D Gaussians via ray-splat-intersection within a unified alpha-blending pass, avoiding the geometric distortion that arises from forcing 2D surfels through the 3DGS projector. Second, Adaptive Type Exchange uses the effective rank of each Gaussian's scale vector (threshold 2.05) to dynamically convert 3D→2D (with permutation-based reparameterization preserving covariance) or 2D→3D (with opacity-conditioned soft modulation of the z-scale), every 500 iterations. Third, Frequency-Decoupled Optimization applies a level-1 Haar DWT to decompose photometric loss into low- and high-frequency components, then performs asymmetric gradient updates: low-frequency loss supervises 2D Gaussians for geometric consistency while high-frequency loss supervises 3D Gaussians for appearance detail, with gradient projection (inspired by gradient surgery) to resolve conflicts when the two frequency gradients point in opposing directions.

## Results

On appearance benchmarks, EGGS achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all three datasets: 27.96/0.851/0.192 on Mip-NeRF360, 27.34/0.895/0.083 on LLFF, and 24.41/0.923/0.153 on Tanks&Temples. On geometry (DTU), EGGS reports a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.91 and PSNR of 33.65, slightly behind 2DGS (mCD 0.80) and GOF (mCD 0.74) in surface accuracy but substantially ahead of 3DGS (mCD 1.96) and SUGAR (mCD 1.33). In efficiency, EGGS uses 581K Gaussians and trains in 9 min on LLFF, fewer primitives and faster than 3DGS (919K, 10 min) and GaussianPro (933K, 20 min). In out-of-distribution view synthesis, EGGS reaches 24.17 PSNR, surpassing both 3DGS (20.21) and 2DGS (23.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGGS outperforms all six baselines on every appearance metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across Mip-NeRF360, LLFF, and Tanks&Temples. Against 3DGS, EGGS gains +0.53 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 and +1.22 dB on LLFF while also improving SSIM and LPIPS, confirming that the hybrid representation recovers high-frequency detail that pure 3D Gaussians miss. Against 2DGS, EGGS improves PSNR by +1.15 dB (Mip-NeRF360) and +2.41 dB (LLFF), addressing 2DGS's over-smoothing, though 2DGS retains a slight edge in Chamfer Distance on DTU (0.80 vs. 0.91). Against GaussianPro, the strongest 3D-regularized baseline, EGGS leads by +0.04 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 and +0.81 dB on LLFF, while also training 2× faster (9 min vs. 20 min on LLFF). Against GOF, EGGS achieves comparable geometry (mCD 0.91 vs. 0.74) but substantially better appearance (PSNR 33.65 vs. 32.58 on DTU). In the OOD generalization setting, EGGS surpasses 2DGS by +0.65 dB and 3DGS by +3.96 dB, indicating that the balanced multi-view consistency of the hybrid representation transfers better to unseen viewpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

EGGS sits at the intersection of 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) and 2DGS (Huang et al., 2024), which have been treated as competing single-representation approaches: 3DGS excels in appearance fidelity while 2DGS enforces multi-view geometric consistency. Prior hybrid attempts (HybridGS for transient objects, HorizonGS for aerial-ground scenes) combine 2D and 3D Gaussians in task-specific, non-exchangeable ways, and none provide a unified rasterizer or dynamic type switching. EGGS departs from this lineage by making individual Gaussians exchangeable during training via effective-rank-based type switching, a unified CUDA hybrid rasterizer, and a frequency-domain supervision strategy inspired by gradient surgery (Yu et al., 2020) to resolve conflicting low- and high-frequency gradient signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EGGS offers a practical, general-purpose representation that simultaneously improves the geometry-appearance trade-off that has constrained Gaussian splatting since 3DGS, with the added benefit of fewer Gaussians and shorter training time than 3DGS. Its exchangeable design means it can serve as a drop-in replacement for the 3DGS pipeline and is compatible with orthogonal optimizations (MCMC densification, ScaffoldGS-style MLPs). Open questions include whether semantic or structural priors could replace the random type initialization, whether the framework extends to extreme conditions (low-light, highly reflective, transient-heavy scenes), and whether the effective-rank heuristic for type switching generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks.
