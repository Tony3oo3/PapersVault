## Problem

Prior optimization analyses of transformers with chain-of-thought were confined to fully parallelizable TC0 tasks, leaving open whether gradient descent can provably learn CoT reasoning for inherently sequential (NC1-complete) problems, and whether the learned reasoning generalizes to longer chains than those seen in training. This paper provides the first optimization guarantees bridging the gap between what constant-depth transformers can express with CoT (NC1) and what they can actually learn via training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a LEGO sentence encoded as a sequence of 5-token clauses (predicate clauses x=g(x') and answer clauses x=y), each embedded into R^{5d} via an orthonormal token embedding table. Operating autoregressively, given a partial transcript Z^{L,L'} containing all L predicate clauses and the first L'+1 answer clauses, the model predicts the next answer clause Z_{ans,L'+1} (i.e., the value y_{L'+1}) by sampling from a product of five per-token softmax distributions. The training objective is a teacher-forced next-clause cross-entropy loss summed over all intermediate steps, enforcing step-by-step CoT generation.

## Method

The paper analyzes a one-layer NoPE decoder transformer (softmax attention with a single query matrix Q, followed by a position-wise FFN with smooth ReLU) trained by gradient descent on LEGO state-tracking tasks with two distinct algebraic structures. For simply transitive (cyclic) group actions, a two-stage curriculum (Algorithm 1) first trains the FFN to implement the group operation on T^1, then trains attention to route the correct predicate and answer clauses on T^2; the resulting attention concentration on the shared variable x_ell (appearing as the third token of Z_{pred,ell+1} and the fourth token of Z_{ans,ell}) yields direct generalization to O(d^{c*})-length chains. For symmetry group actions (S_n, n≥5), where distractor clauses prevent strong concentration, a recursive self-training scheme (Algorithm 2) bootstraps the solvable length by doubling at each round—training on the model's own greedy CoT traces—provably reaching the maximal |X|-length chain. The key mechanistic insight is that the algebraic structure of the group action governs attention concentration, which in turn dictates the degree of length generalization: simply transitive actions yield unique retrieval targets enabling robust long-context generalization, while symmetry actions admit many distractors that dilute attention and limit extrapolation.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical guarantees on synthetic LEGO state-tracking tasks. For simply transitive (cyclic) group actions, Theorem 4.1 proves that a one-layer NoPE transformer trained via gradient descent on short chains (T¹ and T²) achieves Acc_L ≥ 1 − 1/poly(d) for all L ≤ O(d^{c*}), i.e., strong short-to-long length generalization. For symmetry group actions (NC¹-complete), Theorem 5.1 proves that a recursive self-training scheme bootstraps solvable length by a constant factor per round (L_k = 2^k → L_{k+1} = 2^{k+1}), and Corollary 5.1 shows that after K = Θ(log d) rounds the model achieves Acc_L ≥ 1 − 1/poly(d) for all L ≤ |X| = Θ(d). Empirically, a C₆ model trained at L=5 generalizes to near-perfect accuracy at L=160, while an S₅ model with three rounds of self-training (L=5→10→20→40) also reaches near-perfect accuracy up to L=160.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines; instead, it contrasts two algebraic task structures under an identical one-layer NoPE transformer trained with gradient descent. The cyclic group C₆ (simply transitive) exhibits strong length generalization: trained at L=5, it maintains near-perfect accuracy up to L=160 (a 32× extension), consistent with Theorem 4.1's O(d^{c*}) guarantee. The symmetry group S₅ (NC¹-complete) shows only constant-factor generalization without self-training, consistent with the weaker attention concentration (error bounded by a constant c̃ rather than d^{-c*}). After three rounds of recursive self-training on S₅ (doubling training length each round from 5 to 40), the model closes the gap and matches the C₆ performance up to L=160, validating Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.1. Attention heatmaps (Figures 2, 4, 5, 6) further confirm that the cyclic task's retrieval is keyed to shared variables (position-invariant) while the symmetry task's predicate attention decays with context length, explaining the generalization gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges a critical gap between transformer expressiveness and learnability: prior expressiveness results (Feng et al. 2023, Li et al. 2024, Merrill & Sabharwal 2023) showed that CoT extends constant-depth transformers from TC0 to NC1, while prior optimization analyses (Kim & Suzuki 2024, Wen et al. 2024, Huang et al. 2025) were confined to TC0 tasks that are fully parallelizable. The paper builds on the LEGO state-tracking benchmark (Zhang et al. 2022) and the recursive self-training scheme of Lee et al. 2025, but provides the first gradient-descent optimization guarantee for learning NC1-complete problems via CoT, and introduces an 'attention concentration' mechanism that links the algebraic structure of the group action to length generalization behavior. It departs from prior length-generalization theory (Marsden et al. 2024, Ahuja & Mansouri 2024, Golowich et al. 2025), which established existence or sample-complexity guarantees, by proving that standard GD training actually produces length-generalizing models and characterizing exactly when it does.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable optimization guarantee that a constant-depth transformer learns to solve NC1-complete problems (beyond TC0) via CoT, closing the expressiveness-learnability gap for this complexity class. The attention concentration mechanism offers a concrete, testable explanation for why length generalization succeeds or fails: simply transitive group actions yield robust retrieval that generalizes to polynomial-length contexts, while symmetry group actions produce weaker concentration that only supports constant-factor extension. The recursive self-training result is the first theoretical guarantee that transformers can bootstrap their own reasoning length without additional supervision. Key limitations include the one-layer NoPE architecture, the asymptotic regime (d→∞), and the restriction to synthetic LEGO tasks with small groups; open questions include whether these mechanisms persist in multi-layer architectures with positional encodings and on real-world reasoning tasks.
