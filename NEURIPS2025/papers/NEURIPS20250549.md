## Problem

Existing graphon estimation methods either recover the graphon function or the connection probability matrix P separately, often producing inconsistent ranks between the two objects. A unified framework that simultaneously estimates both a low-rank graphon and a low-rank connection probability matrix with matching rank, while resolving the identification issues inherent to graphon estimation, has been missing from the literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250549/cropped_page9_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single undirected network G=(V,E) with n nodes (represented by its adjacency matrix) and no pre-specified labels or node ordering. It outputs the full n×n connection probability matrix P, the continuous graphon function f(u,v) on [0,1]² (recovered via sorting and piecewise linear interpolation rather than as a discrete matrix), and the spectral decomposition parameters (eigenvalues λ_k and eigenfunctions G_k). The rank r can be selected automatically via an eigenvalue-ratio criterion when unknown.

## Method

The core insight is that a rank-r graphon decomposes into r rank-1 components, each recoverable by counting O(r) carefully chosen subgraphs. For r=1, node degrees directly estimate the eigenfunction values; for r≥2, the method counts cycles of lengths 3 through r+2 (whose expectations depend only on the eigenvalues λ_k, not on the eigenfunctions) and simple paths of lengths 1 through r (whose conditional expectations encode G_k(U_i)), then solves a moment-matching system of equations to recover all parameters. The graphon is subsequently recovered by sorting the estimated eigenfunction values and applying piecewise linear interpolation, a sequential design that ensures the estimated graphon inherits the correct rank of P. A fast variant replaces simple-path counting with matrix powers (E^a), reducing time complexity to O(n^ω) while preserving all theoretical guarantees. The method achieves sup-norm consistency at rate O_p(√(log n/n)) for both P and f, without requiring smoothness assumptions on the eigenfunctions beyond boundedness (for P) or Lipschitz continuity (for f).

## Results

On dense graphons (n=2000), the proposed method achieves MSE of 1.275×10⁻⁴ (setting 1, rank-1) and 1.826×10⁻⁴ (setting 4, rank-2), matching or slightly beating USVT and Power Iteration while being 10–100× faster than Neighborhood Smoothing and Network Histogram. On sparse graphons (ρ_n = n^{-1/2}), the method consistently achieves the lowest MSE across all settings, e.g., 0.036×10⁻⁴ vs. 0.051×10⁻⁴ for USVT (setting 1) and 0.043×10⁻⁴ vs. 0.102×10⁻⁴ for USVT (setting 4). The sup-norm error for graphon estimation is 12.05×10⁻² (setting 3) and 15.54×10⁻² (setting 4), confirming the theoretical O_p(√(log n / n)) rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against USVT (Chatterjee 2015), the proposed method matches or slightly beats MSE on dense graphons (e.g., 1.275 vs. 1.282 ×10⁻⁴ on setting 1) but is 100–110× faster (0.121s vs. 13.587s). On sparse graphons, the advantage widens: Ours achieves 0.036×10⁻⁴ MSE vs. USVT's 0.051×10⁻⁴ (setting 1) and 0.043×10⁻⁴ vs. 0.102×10⁻⁴ (setting 4). Against Neighborhood Smoothing (Zhang et al. 2017), the proposed method is dramatically superior in both accuracy (e.g., 1.275 vs. 7.853 ×10⁻⁴ MSE on setting 1) and speed (0.121s vs. 115s). Network Histogram (Olhede & Wolfe 2014) is outperformed on MSE in most settings (e.g., 1.826 vs. 3.928 ×10⁻⁴ on setting 4) and is 30–40× slower. Sort-and-Smooth (Chan & Airoldi 2014) is the weakest baseline, with MSE 10–20× worse and max errors near 90–99×10⁻², likely due to its strict monotonicity assumption. Power Iteration (Stoer et al. 1980) is the closest competitor on dense settings with comparable MSE and similar runtime, but lacks theoretical convergence guarantees and degrades more on sparse and higher-rank settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the method-of-moments framework of Bickel et al. (2011) for parameter recovery via subgraph counts, extending it from fixed-parameter network models to the low-rank graphon setting. It departs from prior work in a key conceptual way: existing methods either estimate the graphon function (Olhede & Wolfe, Chan & Airoldi) or the connection probability matrix P (USVT, neighborhood smoothing, combinatorial least-squares), but never both simultaneously with matching rank. The paper's core insight is that a rank-r graphon decomposes into r rank-1 components recoverable via O(r) subgraph counts, and that the resulting rank-1-aligned structure of P enables graphon recovery through sorting and interpolation—a step that fails for P estimated by other methods lacking this alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to deliver sup-norm consistency for both the connection probability matrix and the graphon function simultaneously, a stronger uniform guarantee than the MSE-based bounds standard in the literature. The method is tuning-free and achieves O(n²) (or O(n^ω) via matrix multiplication) complexity, making it practical for large networks. Open questions include convergence rates in sparse graphon models, extending to growing rank r, and relaxing Assumption 3.7 (monotonicity + Lipschitz) needed for graphon recovery. The practical impact is significant for network scientists who need both a finite-sample P for link prediction and a population-level f for generative modeling and cross-network comparison.
