## Problem

Existing 3D open-vocabulary segmentation methods follow a 'splat then segment' paradigm, reconstructing the full scene first and then performing segmentation in 2D pixel space or via post-hoc 3D clustering. This leads to multi-view inconsistent boundaries, geometric and semantic ambiguity (each Gaussian may encode information from multiple objects), and fundamental incompatibility with dynamic scenes where a single Gaussian can represent different objects at different time steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255531/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view RGB images (or a video sequence for dynamic scenes) and a free-form text query as input. It produces object-specific 3D Gaussian sets, each annotated with a single CLIP embedding, enabling open-vocabulary retrieval at three granularity levels (large, middle, small). Because segmentation is inherently 3D, the output can reveal occluded portions of objects that are invisible in any single 2D view, which slightly shifts mIoU relative to 2D ground-truth masks.

## Method

Segment then Splat reverses the conventional pipeline by partitioning COLMAP-initialized Gaussians into object-specific sets *before* reconstruction, using multi-view masks extracted by a robust SAM/SAM2 tracking module that handles new-object detection, duplicate-track resolution, and lost-track recovery. During optimization, an object-level loss L_obj enforces that each Gaussian set renders only its assigned object, while all densification and cloning operations remain strictly within each set, preserving the Gaussian-object correspondence throughout training. For dynamic scenes, a deformable 3D Gaussian field models per-timestamp motion without breaking the object assignment. After reconstruction, a single CLIP embedding is computed per object by averaging CLIP image features over its masked regions (with low-IoU partial masks filtered out), and open-vocabulary queries are resolved by cosine similarity between the text embedding and object embeddings. A three-stage optimization schedule (small → middle → large granularity) and random per-iteration object sampling balance multi-scale supervision with computational efficiency.

## Results

On static scenes, Segment then Splat achieves 52.10 mIoU on LERF_OVS and 88.53 mIoU on 3DOVS, outperforming all 2D and 3D baselines while also being the fastest (50.75 min and 9.40 min respectively). On dynamic scenes, the method achieves 69.48 mIoU on HyperNeRF and 44.00 mIoU on Neu3D, surpassing DGD by over 60 mIoU points and LSeg by over 50 mIoU points, while training ~7× faster than DGD (218 min vs. 1564.5 min on HyperNeRF). The method also demonstrates superior training efficiency in static settings, completing 3DOVS in 9.40 min versus 55–77 min for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On static scenes, Segment then Splat outperforms the strongest 2D baseline LangSplat by 5.7 mIoU on LERF_OVS (52.10 vs. 46.37) and 6.0 mIoU on 3DOVS (88.53 vs. 82.49), while also training 20–87% faster. Against the 3D baseline OpenGaussian, the gap is much larger: 9.7 mIoU on LERF_OVS (52.10 vs. 42.43) and 57.5 mIoU on 3DOVS (88.53 vs. 31.00), attributed to OpenGaussian's reliance on a predefined cluster count and its 'splat-then-segment' strategy producing imprecise boundaries. LEGaussians and Gaussian Grouping are consistently outperformed by 20+ mIoU on LERF_OVS. On dynamic scenes, the advantage is dramatic: the method achieves 69.48 mIoU on HyperNeRF versus 7.83 for DGD and 15.71 for LSeg, and 44.00 mIoU on Neu3D versus 1.65 for DGD and 1.49 for LSeg, while training approximately 7× faster than DGD (218 min vs. 1564.5 min). The paper attributes DGD's poor performance to Gaussian-object misalignment in dynamic settings and the lack of direct supervision on per-Gaussian language embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the established 'splat then segment' pipeline used by LangSplat, LEGaussians, OpenGaussian, and DGD, which all reconstruct the full scene first and then partition it (in 2D pixel space or 3D Gaussian space). Instead, Segment then Splat initializes COLMAP-derived Gaussians into object-specific sets using SAM/SAM2 multi-view masks before any reconstruction begins, so each Gaussian is dedicated to a single object from the start. For dynamic scenes, it builds on Deformable 3D Gaussians' deformation field but avoids the Gaussian-object misalignment that plagues prior dynamic language-field methods like DGD and 4D LangSplat. The CLIP embedding is assigned per object group rather than learned per-Gaussian through a separate language field, eliminating the auxiliary feature network entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm reversal from 'reconstruct then segment' to 'segment then reconstruct' offers a clean, unified solution for both static and dynamic 3D open-vocabulary segmentation without requiring a separate language field, which simplifies training and removes a major source of geometric-semantic ambiguity. The ~10× speedup over DGD on dynamic scenes and the elimination of the language field make this practically attractive for robotics and AR applications. However, the method remains dependent on SAM2 tracking quality (failing in high-density, visually similar object scenes) and cannot handle relational multi-object queries, leaving open the question of how to incorporate inter-object spatial reasoning into the per-object Gaussian framework.
