## Problem

Spatio-temporal Gaussian Processes provide interpretable aleatoric uncertainty quantification but scale only to thousands of observations due to cubic computational cost, and their stationarity assumption is often violated in real environmental fields. Scalable deep learning alternatives lack inherent uncertainty quantification, and existing approximate GP methods lose the statistical validity of their prediction intervals. No prior method simultaneously achieves scalable non-stationary STGP approximation with guaranteed-coverage prediction intervals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes irregularly sampled ST coordinates (s ∈ [0,1]², t > 0) paired with observed field values, where spatial coordinates are rescaled to the unit square and responses are mean-variance normalized. It produces a predicted ST surface (MAP estimate), both Bayesian credible intervals and conformal prediction intervals at a user-specified coverage level (e.g., 95%), and interpretable covariance parameters (variance, spatial/temporal range, smoothness). The conformal step additionally requires selecting K nearest neighbors by ST distance scaled by the estimated covariance ranges, making the interval width spatially and temporally adaptive.

## Method

STACI is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 approximates a non-stationary STGP as a Bayesian neural network: J random Fourier features (cos/sin basis functions) with frequencies drawn from a multivariate t-distribution (the Matérn spectral density) form the GP layer, while an implicit neural representation (ResMLP, FFNP, or FFNG) models a p-dimensional latent field L(s,t) that augments the distance metric via dimension expansion to capture non-stationarity. The network is trained with M particle copies using Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD), yielding M posterior samples for aleatoric UQ. Stage 2 applies a novel spatio-temporal conformal inference algorithm: for each prediction site, K nearest neighbors are selected by ST distance scaled by the estimated covariance ranges, normalized residuals serve as non-conformity scores, and the resulting prediction interval achieves valid 100(1−α)% coverage under only a local exchangeability assumption, correcting for approximation bias from both the spectral and neural network approximations.

## Results

On the Arctic MSS dataset (1.16M points), STACI-FFNP achieves the lowest RMSE (0.161) and NLL (−1.331), outperforming the next-best GPSat (RMSE 0.200, NLL −1.188) and DeepRF (RMSE 0.203, NLL −1.112). The conformal variant of STACI-FFNP achieves 95.8% coverage with an interval score of 0.514 and width of 0.500, roughly half the interval score of all competing methods while maintaining valid coverage. On the MODIS AOD dataset (3.19M points), STACI-FFNP again achieves the lowest RMSE (0.560) and the conformal variant reduces the interval score to 1.850 (vs. 3.476 for GPSat and 5.433 for DeepRF). Training time for STACI is 39–116 s per epoch versus 2,745–7,020 s for GPSat, demonstrating a >20× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STACI-FFNP outperforms all four baselines on RMSE for both datasets: 0.161 vs. GPSat (0.200) and DeepRF (0.203) on MSS, and 0.560 vs. DeepGP (0.633), GPSat (0.653), SVGP (0.690), and DeepRF (0.733) on AOD. On NLL, STACI-FFNP leads on MSS (−1.331 vs. GPSat's −1.188) but GPSat achieves a slightly lower NLL on AOD (−0.064 vs. 0.042), though STACI still wins on RMSE and CRPS there. The conformal variant of STACI dramatically improves UQ efficiency: its interval score (0.514 on MSS, 1.850 on AOD) is roughly half that of the best non-conformal competitor, while achieving the target 95% coverage (0.958/0.948). SVGP, the stationary sparse GP baseline, performs worst on both datasets (RMSE 0.447 on MSS, 0.690 on AOD), confirming the importance of modeling non-stationarity. GPSat matches STACI closely on estimation metrics but requires 2,745–7,020 s of compute versus 39–116 s for STACI, a >20× wall-clock disadvantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

STACI unifies three previously separate research threads: the Bayesian Random Fourier Features (BRFF) spectral GP approximation of Miller & Reich (2022), the dimension-expansion framework for non-stationary processes of Bornn et al. (2012), and the local spatial conformal prediction of Mao et al. (2024). The key architectural departure is replacing MCMC with SVGD-trained Bayesian neural networks and using implicit neural representations (ResMLP, FFNP, FFNG) to model the latent dimension-expansion field, making the full pipeline GPU-scalable to millions of observations. The conformal inference step is extended from the spatial to the spatio-temporal setting, using learned covariance ranges to define local exchangeable neighborhoods rather than assuming global exchangeability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that STACI delivers statistically valid prediction intervals (via conformal guarantees) at a fraction of the interval width of competing methods—halving interval scores while maintaining 95% coverage—on datasets with over 3 million observations. This addresses a real gap: scalable ST deep learning methods typically assume independent noise and lack interpretable covariance structure, while exact GPs cannot scale. Open questions include whether the SVGD particle count (M=10) is sufficient for complex posterior geometry, whether the equal-weight conformal neighbor selection can be improved with distance-proportional weighting, and how the framework extends to non-Gaussian (Bernoulli, Poisson) or spatial point process data.
