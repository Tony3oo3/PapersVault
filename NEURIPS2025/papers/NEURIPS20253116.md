## Problem

The weighted controlled direct effect (WCDE) averages controlled direct effects over the mediator distribution to yield a robust population-level direct effect, but no prior work established when the WCDE is identifiable from observational data or which covariate adjustment set minimizes estimation variance. Standard CDE adjustment criteria fail to guarantee WCDE identifiability because they do not account for the integration over mediator levels, and ATE-oriented optimal adjustment theory treats mediators as forbidden variables, precluding the mediator-weighting structure that WCDE requires.

## Input / Output

The method takes a known causal DAG, i.i.d. observational samples, and a binary exposure-outcome pair (A, Y) as input. It first identifies the mediator set M' = M ∩ Pa(Y) (mediators that are also direct parents of Y), then characterizes all valid adjustment sets via three graphical criteria (C1–C3) and selects the O-set, defined as all non-treatment parents of Y partitioned into confounders, mediators, and informative non-descendants. The output is a WCDE point estimate computed via an AIPW estimator, together with the influence function and asymptotic variance for the chosen adjustment set.

## Method

The paper establishes three theoretical results. First, Lemma 2.6 gives necessary and sufficient graphical conditions for a set Z to be a valid adjustment set for WCDE, where the critical condition C3 requires that unadjusted mediators and non-mediator parents of Y be d-separated from A given the adjusted mediators. Second, Theorem 3.4 derives the unique influence function of the WCDE target parameter for any valid adjustment set, showing that the IF depends explicitly on the chosen set through its weighting structure. Third, Theorem 4.3 proves that the O-set (all non-treatment parents of Y) is the unique optimal valid adjustment set minimizing asymptotic variance across all RAL estimators, via a four-step variance-reduction argument: augmenting with non-mediating parents of Y, augmenting with mediating parents of Y, pruning redundant backdoor variables, and pruning redundant mediators. The key distinction from ATE estimation is that mediators that are parents of Y must be included (not excluded) in the optimal set, because the WCDE's integration over the mediator distribution makes their conditional variance structure directly relevant to estimation efficiency. An AIPW estimator (Eq. 5.1) is provided for practical computation, validated on synthetic DAGs and three semi-synthetic Bayesian networks (ASIA, SIGNALING, MILDEW).

## Results

On the ASIA network, the proposed O-set [bronc] achieves the lowest variance (0.0143 at n=250, 0.00027 at n=10000) and MSE (0.0158 at n=250, 0.00029 at n=10000) among all valid adjustment sets, outperforming the next-best set [smoke] by ~32% in variance at n=250. On the SIGNALING network, the O-set {Erk, PKA} achieves the smallest variance (0.00125 at n=1000, 0.00012 at n=10000) for larger samples, though its MSE is marginally higher than {Erk, Mek, PKA} at n=250. In synthetic experiments validating Lemmas 4.4–4.7, the O-set {G1, G2} consistently achieves the lowest variance (0.0029 at n=250, 0.0006 at n=2000) and MSE across all sample sizes, confirming the theoretical optimality in finite samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external competing methods; instead, it compares the proposed O-set against all other valid adjustment sets (VASs) for the same WCDE estimand. On the ASIA network, the O-set [bronc] dominates all six alternative valid sets in both variance and MSE at every sample size, with the largest gap against [lung] (0.0532 vs. 0.0143 variance at n=250, a 3.7× difference). On the SIGNALING network, the O-set {Erk, PKA} achieves the lowest variance for n≥1000 (0.00125 vs. 0.00142 for {Erk, Mek, PKA} at n=1000), though at n=250 the set {Erk, Mek, PKA} has slightly lower MSE (0.00476 vs. 0.00618), illustrating a finite-sample bias-variance trade-off. In the synthetic experiments (Figures 2 and 3), the O-set {G1, G2} outperforms both the under-adjusted set (e.g., {G1, B2}: 0.0102 variance) and the over-adjusted set (e.g., {G1, G2, B2}: 0.0050 variance) at n=250, confirming that both under- and over-adjustment inflate variance relative to the optimal set. The MILDEW network (35 nodes) similarly shows the O-set achieving the smallest variance and MSE for n≥500, with performance among the best for smaller samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimal adjustment set framework developed for ATE estimation (Rotnitzky & Smucler 2020; Henckel et al. 2022) to the Weighted Controlled Direct Effect, a causal estimand that averages CDEs over the mediator distribution. It builds on Pearl's CDE identification theory and the causal partition taxonomy of Maasch et al. (2024), but departs from both ATE-oriented strategies (which treat mediators as forbidden variables) and standard CDE criteria (which only ensure identification at fixed mediator levels) by deriving new graphical conditions that account for the mediator's dual role as both a collider and an effect modifier. The AIPW estimator and RAL framework follow standard semiparametric efficiency theory, but the influence function and optimal set characterization are novel to the WCDE functional.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled framework for efficient WCDE estimation, which is directly relevant to fairness analysis where one must isolate direct discrimination effects from mediating pathways. The key open question is how errors from causal discovery propagate into WCDE estimation when the DAG is unknown, and whether the O-set characterization extends to settings with only a subset of mediators fixed. The SIGNALING network results reveal a finite-sample bias-variance trade-off where the asymptotically optimal set does not always minimize MSE at small n, suggesting practical guidance for sample-size-dependent adjustment choices remains an open problem.
