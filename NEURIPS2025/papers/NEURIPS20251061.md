## Problem

Optical flow estimation degrades significantly in high-speed (motion blur) and low-light (insufficient illumination) scenes because weakened texture and amplified noise violate photometric consistency, breaking motion feature matching. Existing dual-modal approaches improve boundary completeness via event cameras but leave appearance features still degraded, which severely impairs both discriminative models (visual-to-motion mapping) and generative models (motion generation from visual features). The paper identifies that diffusion models, which learn a denoising mapping from noisy flow to clear flow, are inherently robust to degraded visual inputs, and that frame and event cameras offer complementary appearance saturation and boundary completeness that can be explicitly fused.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB frame images and a corresponding event stream (from a neuromorphic event camera) as input, processing them at 640×480 resolution. The frame pair is encoded into frame features and the event stream into event features, which are fused into a 4D cost volume serving as the motion condition. The output is a dense 2D optical flow field (horizontal and vertical displacement per pixel), produced by iteratively denoising an initial noisy flow over K=4 diffusion steps, each containing N=6 GRU refinement iterations.

## Method

Diff-ABFlow comprises two core modules. The Attention-Guided Appearance-Boundary Fusion (Attention-ABF) module separately extracts appearance and boundary representations from frame and event features, applies cross-attention to obtain features with dense appearance saturation and dense boundary completeness respectively, then fuses them via self-attention into a unified representation. The Multi-Condition Iterative Denoising Decoder (MC-IDD) first integrates the fused visual feature, the 4D cost volume (motion feature), and a sinusoidal time embedding through a Time-Visual-Motion Multi-Way Cross-Attention (TVM-MCA) module with gated fusion, then feeds the resulting comprehensive feature into a Memory-GRU Denoising Decoder (MGDD) that performs iterative denoising following the DDIM schedule with memory slots to retain feature details across noise levels. Training uses a composite loss combining L1 endpoint error, a boundary-aware smoothness term weighted by frame gradients, and an event consistency loss that enforces flow-event alignment, with pretraining on standard flow datasets followed by fine-tuning on degraded-scene data.

## Results

On the four synthetic and real degraded-scene benchmarks, Diff-ABFlow achieves the best EPE and Fl-all across all settings. On HS-KITTI it reaches 0.46 EPE / 1.12 Fl-all, outperforming the strongest frame-event baseline BFlow (0.53 / 1.81) and the frame-only FlowFormer++ (0.69 / 2.18). On the real HS-DSEC dataset it records 1.09 EPE / 3.83 Fl-all, and on LL-DSEC 1.50 EPE / 4.39 Fl-all, consistently leading all discriminative and generative competitors. On the unseen generalization datasets (HS-FEFD, LL-FEFD), the gap widens dramatically: Diff-ABFlow achieves 4.69 EPE / 28.77 Fl-all on HS-FEFD versus 6.18 / 45.84 for the best frame-event baseline BFlow.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-ABFlow outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the closest frame-event competitor BFlow [8], it reduces EPE by 0.07 on HS-KITTI (0.46 vs 0.53) and by 0.06 on HS-DSEC (1.09 vs 1.15), with Fl-all improvements of roughly 0.7–2.6 points. The frame-only discriminative FlowFormer++ [25] is the strongest single-modality baseline (0.69 EPE on HS-KITTI) but still trails Diff-ABFlow by 0.23 EPE, confirming the benefit of event-guided boundary information. The generative FlowDiffuser [17] (frame-only) achieves 0.62 EPE on HS-KITTI, 0.16 worse than Diff-ABFlow, showing that diffusion alone without event fusion is insufficient. ABDA-Flow [38] is competitive on LL-KITTI (0.64 EPE) but degrades sharply on HS-DSEC (1.85 EPE), whereas Diff-ABFlow maintains consistent performance across both high-speed and low-light conditions. The event-only E-RAFT [7] and frame-only GMA [14] are substantially weaker (EPE > 2.0 on most datasets), underscoring that neither modality alone suffices in degraded scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) frame-event fusion for optical flow, where prior methods like BFlow and ABDA-Flow introduce event data via feature fusion or domain adaptation but still rely on discriminative or GAN-based backbones sensitive to degraded visual features, and (2) diffusion-based optical flow, where FlowDiffuser and Saxena et al. reformulate flow estimation as denoising but use only frame input. Diff-ABFlow is the first to combine dual-modal frame-event input with a diffusion denoising backbone, and it repurposes the GRU iterative refinement paradigm from RAFT within a DDIM-style decoder (MC-IDD) to balance diffusion robustness with inference efficiency. The Attention-ABF module is a novel fusion strategy explicitly designed around the appearance-boundary complementarity between the two sensors, going beyond the generic feature fusion or domain adaptation used in prior frame-event work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: optical flow in high-speed and low-light driving scenes where both frame and event cameras individually provide incomplete information. The validated appearance-boundary complementarity and the demonstrated robustness of diffusion denoising to degraded inputs (via t-SNE analysis) offer a principled rationale for the architecture, and the new HS-FEFD/LL-FEFD datasets provide a generalization benchmark the field lacked. However, the method fails on textureless planes (acknowledged limitation), carries higher computational cost than discriminative baselines (~198 ms vs ~141 ms), and the broader question of whether the multi-condition diffusion paradigm transfers cleanly to other dense prediction tasks (depth, segmentation) remains open. The finding that standard visual enhancement (deblurring, low-light correction) does not improve or even degrades flow accuracy is a useful negative result for practitioners.
