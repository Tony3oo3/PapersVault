## Problem

Most video anomaly detection methods operate offline, ignoring the real-time monitoring demands of surveillance and autonomous driving. Online VAD is further complicated by the discontinuous, infrequent nature of anomalies and the scarcity of diverse training data. While LLM-based training-free approaches like LAVAD have shown promise, they are limited to offline settings, and naively applying LLMs to streaming video risks misinterpretation from anomalous historical memories and over-reliance on explicit instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251204/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed surveillance video as a sequential stream of frames, processing each frame without access to future content. Five BLIP-2 models generate raw captions per frame, which are cleaned via ImageBind cosine similarity against the current frame's image embedding, then summarized by GLM-4-Flash. The output is a frame-level anomaly score in [0, 1] produced at 0.6-second decision intervals with approximately 5–6 seconds of end-to-end processing latency per frame, enabling near-real-time anomaly localization.

## Method

MoniTor is a training-free online VAD pipeline that chains a vision-language front-end (BLIP-2 + ImageBind + GLM-4-Flash) with an LLM-based scoring back-end augmented by four key mechanisms. A Dynamic Memory Gating Module, inspired by LSTM architecture, maintains long-term memory over a 10-frame window and short-term memory over 2 frames, using a cosine-similarity-based forgetting gate (threshold θ=0.5) to filter irrelevant historical captions. A Standard Scoring Queue stores the most recent caption at each score position (0.0–1.0), providing the LLM with concrete examples of what different anomaly levels look like and reducing instruction dependency. A Behavior Prediction component prompts the LLM to predict the next frame's content, creating an expectation-versus-reality feedback loop that sharpens detection of emergent anomalies. Finally, a weight-assignment step blends the current frame's raw score with the previous frame's score (α=0.7) to smooth temporal transitions, and anomaly priors extracted from Wikipedia definitions of 13 crime categories are injected into the context prompt to ground the LLM's scoring in domain knowledge.

## Results

On UCF-Crime, MoniTor achieves 82.57% AUC as an online training-free method, outperforming the best offline training-free baseline EventVAD (82.03%) by 0.54% and the online LAVAD baseline (76.06%) by 6.51%. On XD-Violence, MoniTor reaches 55.01% AP and 79.11% AUC, improving over online-LAVAD by 2.38% AP and 3.10% AUC. The method generates anomaly scores at 0.6-second decision intervals with 5–6 seconds end-to-end processing latency, compared to REWARD's 6.4-second decision period.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoniTor (82.57% AUC) surpasses all offline training-free methods, including EventVAD (82.03%), LAVAD (80.28%), and Video-Llama2 (74.42%), and outperforms the online LAVAD baseline by a substantial 6.51% margin. It also exceeds all offline unsupervised methods (best: DyAnNet at 79.76%) and offline one-class methods (best: GODS at 70.46%). However, MoniTor remains below the strongest online weakly supervised method REWARD (86.94%) by 4.37%, though it is competitive with other weakly supervised approaches like MGFN (81.76%) and S3R (81.34%). On XD-Violence, MoniTor (55.01% AP / 79.11% AUC) trails offline training-free EventVAD (64.04% AP / 87.51% AUC) and LAVAD (60.02% AP / 82.89% AUC), which the authors attribute to frequent camera transitions in that dataset disrupting the memory gating and prediction modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoniTor directly extends LAVAD (Zanella et al., CVPR 2024), the first training-free VAD method using LLMs, by adapting its BLIP-2 + ImageBind + LLM scoring pipeline from offline to streaming/online video. It departs from prior online VAD methods (REWARD, S3R, RTFM, MGFN) that require weak supervision or end-to-end training, instead leveraging pretrained VLMs and LLMs in a training-free fashion. The LSTM-inspired Dynamic Memory Gating Module and the Standard Scoring Queue are novel architectural contributions that bridge traditional sequence-modeling techniques (LSTM forgetting gates) with LLM-based reasoning, addressing the temporal discontinuity problem that made prior LLM-based VAD infeasible for streaming settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free online VAD framework, which is practically significant for surveillance deployments where labeled anomaly data is scarce or unavailable and real-time response is required. The 0.6-second decision period (vs. 6.4s for REWARD) makes it viable for time-critical safety applications. Key open questions remain: camera transitions cause ~60% of detection errors, the frame-level approach inherently lacks the temporal context of video-segment methods, and the computational demands of LLMs/VLMs limit edge-device deployment. Whether continual learning or model compression can address these gaps without sacrificing the training-free property is an important direction.
