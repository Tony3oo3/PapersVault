## Problem

The parameter complexity of robust memorization in ReLU networks was not characterized as a function of the robustness ratio ρ = μ/ε across the full range ρ ∈ (0,1). Prior lower bounds either applied only to large ρ without specifying the threshold, or reduced to the trivial non-robust Ω(√N) bound for small ρ, while upper bounds had large gaps and did not interpolate smoothly. This paper provides the first fine-grained analysis that tracks how the required parameter count grows continuously with ρ.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a labeled dataset D = {(x_i, y_i)} ⊂ R^d × [C] with ε-separation between differently labeled points and a robustness ratio ρ ∈ (0,1), and produces explicit upper and lower bounds on the number of parameters P for a ReLU network to ρ-robustly memorize D. The bounds depend only on the ratio ρ rather than individual μ and ε values, due to scale invariance: scaling the dataset by any constant c leaves the parameter requirement unchanged. The constructions also specify depth, width, and bit complexity, with the moderate-ρ regime allowing an arbitrarily small memorization error η rather than requiring exact robust memorization.

## Method

Lower bounds combine two arguments: a null-space geometry construction (placing standard basis vectors with one label and the origin with another) forces the first hidden layer width to be at least ρ²min{N,d}, and a VC-dimension shattering argument shows VC-dim(F_{d,P}) = Ω(N/(1−ρ²)) for ρ ≤ √(1−1/d), yielding P = Ω(√(N/(1−ρ²))) that interpolates between √N and √(Nd). Upper bounds use a three-regime construction built on a strengthened Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma that preserves both pairwise separation and ball geometry under randomized orthonormal projection: for small ρ, projecting to m = O(log N) dimensions followed by grid-based lattice mapping and Vardi et al.'s Ō(√N)-parameter memorization achieves tight bounds; for moderate ρ, a sequential memorization strategy handles robustness balls that cross grid boundaries by activating one group at a time with thin error-tolerant regions; for large ρ, the projection dimension increases to m ∝ ρ²d to maintain ball disjointness, giving Ō(Nd²ρ⁴) parameters. The critical condition ρ ≤ (2/5)√(m/d) governing when projected robustness balls remain disjoint determines the required projection dimension and thus the parameter count in each regime.

## Results

The paper establishes tight parameter complexity bounds for robust memorization in ReLU networks across the full range of robustness ratio ρ ∈ (0,1). For small ρ < 1/(5N√d), the upper bound Õ(√N) matches the classical non-robust memorization lower bound Ω(√N), yielding a tight Õ(√N) characterization. For moderate ρ, the paper achieves Õ(Nd^{1/4}ρ^{1/2}) parameters (with arbitrarily small error), and for large ρ, Õ(Nd²ρ⁴) parameters. The lower bound Ω((ρ² min{N,d}+1)d + min{1/√(1−ρ²), √d}·√N) interpolates continuously from Ω(√N) at ρ→0 to Ω(√(Nd)) at ρ ≥ √(1−1/d), closing the gap in the smallest-ρ regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Yu et al. [2024], who proved O(Nd²) parameters suffice for all ρ and a width-d lower bound under ℓ∞-norm, this paper improves the upper bound to Õ(Nd²ρ⁴) for large ρ (a ρ⁴ saving) and to Õ(√N) for small ρ (a dramatic N^{1/2} improvement over Nd²). The width lower bound is also strengthened from d (requiring N > d and ρ ≥ 0.8) to ρ² min{N−1, d} for all ρ ∈ (0,1) under ℓ₂-norm. Against Egosi et al. [2025], who showed Õ(N) parameters suffice for ρ < 1/√d with logarithmic width, this paper achieves Õ(√N) for the smallest ρ regime (tight) and Õ(Nd^{1/4}ρ^{1/2}) for moderate ρ, both sublinear in N. Against Li et al. [2022], who established an Ω(√(Nd)) lower bound only for 'sufficiently large ρ' without specifying the range, this paper precisely characterizes the threshold: the √(Nd) bound holds for ρ ≥ √(1−1/d), and for smaller ρ the bound smoothly decreases as Ω(√(N/(1−ρ²))), filling the previously uncharacterized intermediate regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memorization literature (Vardi et al. 2021, Yun et al. 2019) to the robust setting, building on the VC-dimension shattering arguments of Gao et al. (2019) and Li et al. (2022) for lower bounds and the O(Nd²) construction of Yu et al. (2024) for upper bounds. It departs from prior work by providing the first complete characterization across the entire robustness ratio ρ ∈ (0,1), rather than only partial regimes: the lower bound of Li et al. was known only for 'sufficiently large ρ' without specifying the threshold, and the upper bounds of Egosi et al. (2025) and Yu et al. (2024) each covered only a subset of ρ. The key technical departure is a strengthened Johnson-Lindenstrauss projection that preserves ball separability, combined with a grid-based lattice mapping and sequential memorization strategy that interpolates smoothly between the √N and Nd² regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that robust memorization is parameter-equivalent to non-robust memorization (Ō(√N)) for sufficiently small ρ is a clean theoretical guarantee that adversarial robustness at small radii is essentially free in terms of network size. However, a gap between upper and lower bounds persists for moderate and large ρ, and the paper explicitly acknowledges that a precise characterization in those regimes remains open. The practical impact is limited since the bounds are worst-case over all datasets and the constructions are highly structured; the main value is in establishing the fundamental scaling law and identifying the ρ threshold at which robustness begins to incur a parameter cost.
