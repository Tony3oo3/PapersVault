## Problem

Existing one-step diffusion distillation methods split into two theoretically parallel families: KL/f-divergence-based approaches (Diff-Instruct, DMD, f-distill) that converge fast but suffer from mode collapse, and score-divergence-based approaches (SIM, SiD, SiDA) that achieve better fidelity but converge slowly. No unified theoretical framework connects these two lines, and it remains unclear whether a single principled objective could combine their strengths to produce superior one-step generators.

## Input / Output

The one-step generator g_θ takes a single random noise vector z ~ N(0,I) (optionally conditioned on a class label for CIFAR-10/ImageNet or a text prompt for text-to-3D) and produces a full-resolution image in one forward pass, requiring only 1 NFE versus 35–511 NFE for the teacher EDM diffusion model. For the text-to-3D application, the output is a NeRF representation optimized via the Uni-Instruct loss with FKL divergence over 400 epochs. The discriminator operates on the diffusion U-Net encoder features rather than raw pixels, following the SiDA architecture.

## Method

Uni-Instruct introduces a diffusion expansion theorem (Theorem 3.1) that expresses the static f-divergence D_f(q_0‖p_θ) in data space as an integral of score-difference terms weighted by curvature coupling coefficients C_1(r) and C_2(r) along the diffusion process, where r = q_t/p_θ,t is the density ratio. A gradient equivalence theorem (Theorem 3.2) then shows this intractable expanded divergence has the same parameter gradient as a tractable loss that decomposes into a weighted sum of the Diff-Instruct gradient (involving x_t) and the SIM gradient (involving the conditional score ∇log q_t(x_t|x_0)), with weights determined by the chosen f-divergence. A GAN discriminator trained alongside provides the density ratio estimate needed for the curvature coefficients. Training alternates three updates per iteration: discriminator on real/fake pairs, an online fake score network via denoising score matching, and the generator via the combined Uni-Instruct loss plus adversarial loss. By selecting specific divergences (χ², RKL, FKL, JKL), the framework recovers Diff-Instruct, SIM, SiD, and f-distill as special cases, while the two-stage RKL→FKL training schedule exploits mode-seeking followed by mode-covering behavior to achieve FID 1.02 on ImageNet 64×64.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64 conditional generation, Uni-Instruct with FKL (longer training) achieves a record-breaking one-step FID of 1.02, surpassing its 511-NFE VP-EDM teacher (FID 1.36) by a margin of 0.34. On CIFAR-10, Uni-Instruct achieves FID 1.46 (unconditional, JKL from scratch) and 1.38 (conditional, FKL with longer training), both setting new one-step generation records. The method also converges faster than SiDA, reaching lower FID scores at every training iteration checkpoint (e.g., FID 5.23 vs. 6.98 at 10⁴ iterations on CIFAR-10 unconditional).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-Instruct (FKL, longer training) achieves FID 1.02 on ImageNet 64×64, outperforming the strongest prior one-step methods: SiD2A (1.10), f-distill (1.16), DMD2 (1.28), SiDA (1.35), and even the 511-step VP-EDM teacher (1.36). On CIFAR-10 unconditional, JKL from scratch (FID 1.46) beats SiD2A (1.50), SiDA (1.52), SiD (1.92), DMD (3.77), and Diff-Instruct (4.53); on CIFAR-10 conditional, FKL with longer training (1.38) edges out SiD2A (1.40) and SiDA (1.44). In the convergence analysis, Uni-Instruct consistently reaches lower FID than SiDA at every checkpoint (e.g., 5.23 vs. 6.98 at 10⁴ iterations), indicating faster optimization. On additional metrics (sFID, FD_DINO, IS, precision, recall), Uni-Instruct beats SiDA on 4 of 5 metrics on ImageNet 64×64, with an 18.7% reduction in FD_DINO confirming superior semantic preservation. The ablation shows that JKL is the best single divergence from scratch, while a two-stage RKL→FKL schedule yields the best overall results, combining mode-seeking and mode-covering behaviors.

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni-Instruct unifies two previously parallel lines of one-step diffusion distillation: KL-divergence-based methods (Diff-Instruct, DMD, f-distill) and score-based divergence methods (SIM, SiD, SiDA). The key conceptual departure is a novel diffusion expansion theorem for the f-divergence family, which shows that the gradient of the expanded f-divergence decomposes into a weighted combination of the Diff-Instruct gradient and the SIM gradient, with weights determined by the curvature of the chosen f-divergence. This reveals that SIM is secretly minimizing KL divergence (a special f-divergence), and that f-distill's integral formulation is recoverable as a time-weighted special case of Uni-Instruct's static formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification provides a principled framework for understanding and combining different distillation objectives, replacing ad-hoc loss combinations with a theoretically grounded recipe where the f-divergence choice controls the DI/SIM gradient balance. The practical impact is significant: a one-step model outperforming its 79-step teacher by 1.33 FID on ImageNet 64×64 (1.02 vs 2.35) demonstrates that distillation can substantially exceed the teacher, not merely match it. Open questions include whether the framework extends to higher-resolution or video generation, whether the auxiliary GAN discriminator can be replaced by lighter density-ratio estimators, and whether the two-stage RKL→FKL training schedule generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks.
