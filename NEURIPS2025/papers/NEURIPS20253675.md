## Problem

Contrastive learning for composed image retrieval assumes clean separation between positive and negative triplets, but real galleries contain strong semantic overlaps among candidates (aleatoric uncertainty) and imbalanced semantic distributions that cause overconfidence in dominant categories (epistemic uncertainty). Prior methods lack a mechanism to quantify this uncertainty and adaptively recalibrate the supervision signal, leading to unreliable rankings especially for rare or ambiguous semantic concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253675/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reference RGB image (encoded by a frozen BLIP-2 ViT-G/14 encoder) and a free-form modification sentence, fused via a Q-Former with 32 learnable queries into a 256-dimensional embedding. It outputs a ranked list of gallery images by cosine similarity, augmented with per-candidate uncertainty coefficients derived from a two-layer feed-forward uncertainty perceptron with softplus activation. The virtual guidance embeddings used during training are discarded at inference, so deployment requires only the standard encoder–Q-Former pipeline.

## Method

RUNC models the semantic covariance matrix among gallery candidates with a Normal Inverse Gamma evidential prior, treating retrieval similarity as a regression problem rather than a binary classification. The NIG posterior yields aleatoric uncertainty (E[δ²]) and epistemic uncertainty (Var[μ]), which are combined into a per-candidate coefficient that reweights the contrastive loss to penalize uncertain, rare-semantic samples more heavily. A regularization term penalizes misleading evidence from dominant semantics to prevent the model from reinforcing overconfident but incorrect predictions. To reduce fusion ambiguity, an implicit query (virtual guidance + text tokens) is trained to align with targets while being orthogonal to explicit text embeddings, forcing the learnable prompt to capture retained visual semantics rather than redundant textual information. The total objective aggregates the uncertainty-calibrated contrastive loss, NIG negative log-likelihood, evidence regularization, orthogonal constraint, alignment, and implicit contrastive losses.

## Results

On FashionIQ, RUNC achieves a mean R@50 of 76.23 and R@10 of 55.52, outperforming the previous best method SPRC (74.97 / 54.92) by 1.26% and 0.60% respectively. On CIRR, RUNC attains R@1 of 53.81, R@5 of 83.47, R@10 of 91.11, and R@50 of 98.22, surpassing SPRC (51.96 / 82.12 / 89.74 / 97.69) by 1.85%, 1.35%, 1.37%, and 0.53% respectively. The subset metric Rsubset@1 reaches 80.87, a 3.95% improvement over ENCODER (76.92), demonstrating stronger discrimination among visually similar candidates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RUNC outperforms all 23 compared baselines on both FashionIQ and CIRR. The closest competitor is SPRC [31], which RUNC surpasses by 1.26% on FashionIQ mean R@50 and 1.37% on CIRR R@10; the gap widens to 1.85% on CIRR R@1 (53.81 vs 51.96). Against CaLa [22], which shares the same BLIP-2 backbone, RUNC gains 8.83% on CIRR R@1 and 7.01% on FashionIQ mean R@50, indicating that uncertainty calibration is particularly effective when the backbone is held constant. Compared to the other uncertainty-based method MGUR [4], RUNC improves CIRR R@1 by 11.26% (53.81 vs 42.55) and FashionIQ mean R@50 by 10.76% (76.23 vs 65.47), attributed to the NIG prior's ability to model heteroscedastic semantic covariance rather than simple Gaussian noise. On the subset evaluation (Rsubset@1), RUNC leads ENCODER [20] by 3.95% and CaLa [22] by 4.60%, confirming superior discrimination among visually similar candidates. On FashionIQ, RUNC also edges out CCIN [53] (54.41 R@10) and FAME-ViL [52] (46.84 R@10) by 1.11% and 8.68% respectively, with the largest gains on the Shirt and Toptee categories where semantic ambiguity is most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

RUNC builds on the evidential regression framework of Amini et al. (Deep Evidential Regression, NeurIPS 2020), repurposing the Normal Inverse Gamma conjugate prior from regression uncertainty estimation to model semantic covariance among candidate images in composed image retrieval. The cross-modal fusion backbone follows the BLIP-2 Q-Former architecture, but the paper departs from the dominant contrastive-learning paradigm in CIR (CLIP4Cir, BLIP4CIR, SPRC, CaLa) by reframing query-target matching as a probabilistic regression problem and introducing uncertainty-weighted calibration of the contrastive objective. The orthogonal constraint between explicit text embeddings and implicit query prompts is a novel architectural addition not present in prior CIR methods, which typically rely on attention-based fusion without explicit decorrelation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine weakness in contrastive CIR training—semantic overlap among 'negative' candidates and long-tail semantic imbalance—that prior methods handle only implicitly through harder negatives or data augmentation. The uncertainty-calibrated loss and evidential covariance modeling offer a principled mechanism to down-weight overconfident predictions on dominant categories and up-weight rare semantics, which could transfer to other retrieval or matching tasks with noisy labels. However, the gains are modest (≈1.3% over strong baselines), the method adds a feed-forward uncertainty head and extra loss terms that increase training complexity, and the authors acknowledge sensitivity to distribution shift and small-sample settings. Open questions include whether the NIG prior assumption holds for very large or rapidly evolving galleries and whether the orthogonal constraint generalizes beyond the Q-Former formulation.
