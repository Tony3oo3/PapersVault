## Problem

Current code-generation benchmarks (HumanEval, LiveCodeBench, EvalPlus) rely on sparse or LLM-generated test suites that systematically miss human-like bugs—20–40% of LLM solutions passing LiveCodeBench's private tests still fail on LeetCode's judge. This homogenization bias inflates measured performance and corrupts reward signals in RLVR pipelines, yet no accessible, rigorous framework exists for generating test suites that cover diverse, subtle error patterns.

## Input / Output

SAGA consumes a problem description, 10 distinct correct human solutions (S_human), and pairs of incorrect-to-corrected submissions (S_wrong) per problem, sourced from AtCoder, Codeforces, and Nowcoder. The LLM performs a single analysis pass to emit three structured artifacts—Python Case Scripts (parametric input generators), Math Explanations (formal constraint/boundary directives), and Self-Validation code—after which the scripts programmatically synthesize large volumes of test inputs without further LLM calls. Inputs are executed against a ground-truth interpreter to produce final (input, output) test pairs, yielding an average of ~50 test cases per problem in the CodeCompass benchmark.

## Method

SAGA (Strategic Adversarial & Constraint-differential GenerAtive workflow) integrates two complementary analytical dimensions: Multidimensional Analysis deconstructs defensive logic and constraint handling from correct solutions into formal mathematical testing directives (e.g., equivalence classes, boundary tuples), while Differential Analysis compares incorrect vs. corrected submissions to isolate specific failure-triggering inputs (I_diff where S_wrong(I_diff) ≠ S_correct'(I_diff)). The LLM performs one powerful analysis per problem to generate Python Case Scripts that then scale to many test inputs, avoiding per-test LLM invocation. The paper also derives a theoretical upper bound on detection rate (1−(1−p̄)^(1/ρ̄_eff)) showing that inter-test correlation causes saturation, and introduces DEPC and AUC-AccN metrics to quantify test-suite diversity and potency. SAGA is distilled into TCGCoder-7B via supervised fine-tuning on 15,000 SAGA-processed problems, achieving competitive TCG quality at 7B scale.

## Results

On TCGBench-Lite (270 problems from AtCoder, Codeforces, Nowcoder), SAGA with DeepSeek-V3 achieves a Detection Rate (DR@50) of 90.62% and Verifier Accuracy (VAcc@50) of 32.58%, with an AUC@50 of 0.2228—nearly doubling the Input-Interpreter baseline (0.1234). The SAGA-distilled TCGCoder-7B model (7B parameters) outperforms all baselines that use the much larger DeepSeek-V3 backbone, reaching DR@50 of 89.44% and VAcc@50 of 29.11%. On the shared 101-problem AtCoder subset, CodeCompass verifiers (SAGA-generated) achieve a VAcc@40 of 30.39% versus LiveCodeBench-v6's 19.61%, and elicit a 9.56% relative drop in average Pass@1 across evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGA decisively outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against TestChain (direct LLM generation), SAGA improves DR@50 by 22.31 percentage points (90.62% vs. 68.31%) and VAcc@50 by 20.70 points (32.58% vs. 11.88%), reflecting TestChain's inability to leverage human solution priors. Against the Input-Interpreter / LiveCodeBench-style random sampling baseline, SAGA gains 9.55 points in DR@50 and 15.86 points in VAcc@50, with AUC@50 nearly doubling (0.2228 vs. 0.1234), demonstrating that structured human-insight-driven generation far exceeds what volume of random tests can achieve. Against EvalPlus (mutation-based), SAGA leads by 19.50 points in DR@50 and 17.43 points in VAcc@50, showing that deep analysis of correct and incorrect solutions is more effective than input mutation for verifier quality. Notably, the 7B-parameter TCGCoder-7B distilled model surpasses all three baselines that rely on the far larger DeepSeek-V3 backbone, validating SAGA's distillation potential for accessible, specialized TCG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two established LLM-based TCG paradigms—Direct Generation (TestChain, AceCoder, CodeRM) and Input-Interpreter (LiveCodeBench, CodeForce-COT, EvalPlus)—and introduces a third paradigm, 'Human Priors,' that structurally integrates insights from both correct and incorrect human solutions. Conceptually, it repurposes differential testing from classical software engineering (comparing program versions to expose bugs) into an LLM-guided, constraint-differential framework, while drawing on Kish's design-effect theory to formalize the saturation of detection rates under correlated test cases. The paper does not build on a single prior method but rather diagnoses shared limitations across both existing paradigms and proposes a unified human-LLM collaborative alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 20–40% of LLM solutions passing LiveCodeBench's private tests fail on LeetCode's judge exposes a critical reliability gap that directly undermines RLVR reward signals and inflates reported model performance. The theoretical upper bound on detection rate due to inter-test correlation provides a principled explanation for why simply scaling test counts (as LiveCodeBench does) cannot achieve full coverage, redirecting effort toward test diversity and potency. A key limitation is SAGA's dependence on a ground-truth oracle as interpreter, restricting applicability to domains like competitive programming where oracles exist; extending this to open-ended code generation without oracles remains an open challenge. The CodeCompass and TCGBench benchmarks are practically valuable but scoped to competitive programming, leaving broader software engineering test generation as future work.
