## Problem

Existing industrial anomaly detection (IAD) datasets such as MVTec AD, MIAD, and MMAD formulate tasks in a simple question-and-answer format that lacks a rigorous, interpretable reasoning process, and they cover a limited range of industrial scenarios. This restricts the reliability of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in real-world IAD applications where operators need not just a label but a structured explanation of what defect is present, where it is located, and why.

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a single industrial product image with a five-option single-choice question that integrates anomaly discrimination and defect classification, including a 'no defect' option and manually designed high-similarity distractors. The expected output is a full chain-of-thought response that sequentially analyzes incorrect options, localizes the anomaly using precise bounding-box coordinate pairs, explains the cause of the anomaly, and states the final answer. The coordinate format is what enables IoU/GIoU/DIoU evaluation of localization quality alongside classification accuracy.

## Method

AnomalyCoT is constructed through a systematic pipeline that integrates 13 public IAD datasets plus 4 self-collected scenarios (59 object types, 37,565 samples), with all anomalous regions uniformly re-labeled by 10 annotators over 230 hours. CoT reasoning is generated by QwenVL-Max using a prompt that embeds the correct answer, precise anomaly-region coordinates, defect type labels, and a standardized reasoning template, producing a structured analysis of wrong options, localization, cause, and final answer. A two-step filtering mechanism removes format errors (Chinese characters, missing coordinates) and logical errors (causal transposition, analysis-answer misalignment) detected by a secondary LLM, followed by manual inspection by 5 reviewers. For evaluation, representative MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Llama 3.2-Vision, InternVL3, LLaVA-1.5, Gemma 3, Qwen2-VL) are fine-tuned via LoRA and full-parameter training using LLaMA-Factory (batch size 32, 3 epochs, AdamW, cosine decay, BF16), with accuracy and IoU/GIoU/DIoU reported as metrics.

## Results

On AnomalyCoT, pre-trained MLLMs show a clear performance bottleneck: Gemini-2.0-flash achieves the best zero-shot accuracy at 59.64%, while open-source models average only 45.55%, and IoU scores are near zero across all pre-trained models. After LoRA fine-tuning on AnomalyCoT, Llama 3.2-Vision reaches 94.02% accuracy and Qwen2.5-VL attains the best IoU of 0.3225; full fine-tuning pushes accuracy to 97.46% (Qwen2.5-VL) and IoU to 0.4252 (Qwen2-VL). The average accuracy improvement across all fine-tuned models is 36.5%, and the best fine-tuned model (97.46%) substantially surpasses a human expert's 62.67% accuracy on the same test set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among pre-trained commercial models, Gemini-2.0-flash (59.64%) outperforms GPT-4o (49.19%) by ~10 points, while the IAD-specific AnomalyGPT lags far behind at only 19.17%, confirming that domain-specific pre-training without CoT reasoning is insufficient. After LoRA fine-tuning on AnomalyCoT, Llama 3.2-Vision (94.02%) surpasses all other LoRA-tuned models, and Qwen2.5-VL full fine-tuning (97.46%) achieves the highest overall accuracy, exceeding the human expert baseline (62.67%) by over 34 points. On the MVTec AD subset, the fine-tuned Llama 3.2-Vision reaches 87.65% accuracy, significantly outperforming the anomaly-generation baselines AnomalyDiffusion and DualAnoDiff, both of which remain below 80%. Cross-dataset validation (holding out MVTec AD, MVTec LOCO AD, VisA, and GoodsAD) confirms that CoT fine-tuning generalizes to unseen scenarios, with the model maintaining >90% accuracy on previously excluded datasets. The ablation further shows that removing CoT reasoning drops accuracy by 5–15 points depending on the model, while removing coordinates has a smaller but model-dependent effect, validating the necessity of both components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IAD dataset lineage (MVTec AD, MIAD, VisA, GoodsAD, MVTec LOCO AD) by integrating and re-annotating 13 public datasets plus a self-collected set into a unified benchmark, and departs from the simple Q/A formulation of MMAD and the threshold-free but reasoning-free approach of AnomalyGPT by introducing structured Chain-of-Thought annotations with precise anomaly-region coordinates. The CoT generation pipeline draws on QwenVL-Max's visual-textual reasoning, conceptually aligned with Diff-Prompt's fine-grained prompt construction and MultiModal Conditional Retrieval's controllable cross-modal reasoning, but applied here to industrial defect analysis for the first time. The task formulation unifies anomaly discrimination, defect classification, and spatial localization into a single end-to-end multiple-choice problem with a reasoning trace, going beyond the binary detection or single-task framing of prior IAD benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnomalyCoT provides the first CoT-annotated multimodal benchmark for IAD, enabling MLLMs to learn interpretable, coordinate-grounded reasoning that mirrors professional inspection workflows; the 36.5% average accuracy gain from fine-tuning and the demonstrated cross-dataset generalization (holding out MVTec AD, LOCO AD, VisA, GoodsAD) suggest the dataset can serve as a practical training resource for deploying MLLMs in real production lines. Key limitations include reliance on a single CoT generation source (QwenVL-Max), which may bias reasoning style, the absence of error bars or multiple-seed runs, and the manual annotation bottleneck (230 hours for labeling, 10 hours for CoT verification) that limits scalability. Open questions include whether diverse CoT generation sources would improve robustness, how the approach transfers to truly novel defect categories unseen during training, and whether the coordinate-precision requirement generalizes to 3D or video-based inspection settings.
