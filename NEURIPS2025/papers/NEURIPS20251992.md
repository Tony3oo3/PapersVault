## Problem

Existing doubly robust (DR) estimators for proxy causal learning either require explicit density ratio estimation (intractable in high dimensions), rely on indicator functions (restricting to binary treatments), or use kernel smoothing over the treatment (degrading in high-dimensional settings). This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first density-ratio-free DR estimators for PCL that naturally handle continuous and high-dimensional treatments without these limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251992/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. quadruples {y_i, z_i, w_i, a_i} where y is a scalar outcome, z and w are (potentially high-dimensional) treatment and outcome proxies, and a is the treatment variable. It outputs the full dose-response curve θ_ATE(a) as a function over the treatment space, estimated via closed-form kernel matrix operations. Notably, the treatment a can be high-dimensional (e.g., 4096-d flattened images in dSprite), a regime where prior DR methods like PKDR fail entirely.

## Method

The paper proposes two DR estimators—DRKPV and DRPMMR—that combine an outcome bridge function (estimated via KPV two-stage regression or PMMR maximum moment restriction) with a treatment bridge function (estimated via the density-ratio-free KAP algorithm) in the identification formula θ_DR(a) = E[φ(Z,a)(Y−h(W,a))|A=a] + E[h(W,a)]. The key technical insight is that the KAP formulation of the treatment bridge avoids density ratios by rewriting the least-squares objective with decoupled expectations, and the cross-term E[φ(Z,a)h(W,a)|A=a] is estimated via vector-valued kernel ridge regression on the tensor-product conditional mean embedding μ_{Z,W|A}(a). All components admit closed-form solutions through the representer theorem in RKHS, and the paper establishes uniform consistency (sup-norm convergence) under smoothness and effective-dimension assumptions, which is stronger than the pointwise guarantees typical in DR causal learning.

## Results

The paper evaluates dose-response curve estimation using MSE averaged over 30 independent runs across four benchmarks (synthetic low-dimensional, dSprite with 4096-dim treatment, Legalized Abortion and Crime, and Grade Retention) at training sizes of 500, 1000, and 2000. DRKPV and DRPMMR consistently achieve the lowest MSE across all datasets, with the gap widening as sample size increases. On the high-dimensional dSprite benchmark, both methods outperform all baselines (PKDR is omitted as it does not support high-dimensional treatments). In misspecification experiments, both DR methods recover the true causal function even when one bridge function is perturbed with N(0, 0.2) noise, demonstrating the double-robustness property.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRKPV and DRPMMR outperform PKDR [22] (the prior doubly robust method requiring kernel smoothing and density ratio estimation) on all datasets where PKDR is applicable (synthetic, Legalized Abortion, Grade Retention), with PKDR omitted on dSprite due to its inability to handle 4096-dimensional treatments. Both proposed methods beat KAP [23] (the density ratio-free treatment bridge method) on all four benchmarks, confirming that combining outcome and treatment bridges yields gains over either alone. Against KPV [11] and PMMR [11] (the non-doubly robust outcome bridge methods), DRKPV and DRPMMR respectively outperform their non-DR counterparts on Legalized Abortion and Crime and Grade Retention (math), with DRPMMR performing on par with PMMR on Grade Retention (reading). KNC [12] (kernel negative control) is outperformed on all datasets. The key advantage is that DRKPV/DRPMMR avoid both the kernel smoothing step (which degrades in high dimensions) and explicit density ratio estimation (which is unstable), while retaining the double-robustness guarantee that neither KAP nor KPV/PMMR individually provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate identification strategies in Proxy Causal Learning (PCL): the outcome bridge function approach of Miao et al. [5] (estimated via KPV/PMMR from Mastouri et al. [11]) and the treatment bridge function approach of Bozkurt et al. [23] (KAP), which itself was designed to avoid the density ratio estimation required by Cui et al. [21] and Wu et al. [22]. The key structural departure from prior DR methods (Cui et al. [21], Wu et al. [22]) is that the identification formula uses a conditional expectation under p(Y,Z,W|A=a) rather than a joint expectation with an indicator function or kernel smoothing over the treatment, enabling direct application to continuous and high-dimensional treatments. The resulting DRKPV and DRPMMR estimators are the first density-ratio-free doubly robust estimators for PCL, combining kernel mean embeddings with the representer theorem to yield closed-form solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both density ratio estimation and kernel smoothing over the treatment, these estimators remove two major practical bottlenecks that limited prior DR methods to low-dimensional or binary treatments, as demonstrated by the dSprite experiment where the kernel-smoothing-based PKDR baseline simply cannot be applied to 4096-dimensional treatments. The uniform consistency guarantees (stronger than the pointwise convergence typical in DR causal learning) and the demonstrated robustness to bridge function misspecification strengthen the theoretical foundation. Key open questions include: (i) scalability beyond kernel methods (Nyström approximation is only an initial step), (ii) behavior in increasing-dimension regimes where the curse of dimensionality degrades RKHS convergence rates, and (iii) whether the estimators can be modified to achieve local efficiency and asymptotic normality as classical one-step estimators do.
