## Problem

Existing equivariant network methods (group convolutions, steerable CNNs) cannot handle the projective group PSL(3,R) without group discretization or sampling, which introduces approximation errors and exponential memory growth with depth. Prior differential-invariant-based approaches are restricted to the affine subgroup and do not generalize to full projective transformations, leaving a gap in building deep networks that are exactly equivariant to perspective projections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20252616/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-channel (n ≥ 2) images — RGB for STL-10 (96×96) and Imagenette (256×256) — and outputs a class label. A key constraint is that the derived second-order invariants vanish for single-channel (grayscale) inputs, so at least two channels are required. Spatial derivatives are estimated via Gaussian derivative convolutions (σ=0.99, kernel size 9), and invariants are computed locally at each pixel position before being fed to the MLP-based equivariant operator.

## Method

PDINet constructs projective equivariant operators from second-order fundamental differential invariants of PSL(3,R) for multi-dimensional functions, derived via the moving frame method with a novel cross section that normalizes first- and second-order derivatives of the first two channels. Algebraic analysis of the building blocks J_ij (gradient cross-products) and T_ijk (Hessian-gradient couplings) reveals transformation rules that reduce the invariant set to a unified collection of 6n−5 relative invariants, which are converted to absolute invariants by division by a common weight. Each equivariant layer computes these invariants at every spatial location and passes them through a two-layer MLP (implemented as 1×1 convolutions with ReLU) to produce the output feature map. By replacing all convolutional layers in a ResNet-18 backbone with these operators, PDINet achieves exact projective equivariance without any group sampling or discretization, enabling scaling to deep architectures where homConv fails due to exponential memory growth.

## Results

On Proj-STL-10, PDINet achieves 51.12% test accuracy, outperforming the standard ResNet-18 baseline (39.73%) by 11.39 percentage points. On Proj-Imagenette, PDINet reaches 71.30% accuracy, a 5.66-point improvement over ResNet-18 (65.64%). PDINet also uses fewer parameters (8.90M) than ResNet-18 (11.18M) while achieving full projective equivariance without group sampling. All results are averaged over 5 random seeds with standard deviations reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDINet decisively outperforms the standard ResNet-18 baseline on both datasets: by 11.39 points on Proj-STL-10 (51.12% vs. 39.73%) and by 5.66 points on Proj-Imagenette (71.30% vs. 65.64%), confirming that built-in projective equivariance provides stronger OOD generalization than learning from the original distribution alone. Against homConv [MacDonald et al., 2022], the only other projective equivariant method compared, PDINet wins by a large margin (51.12% vs. 20.88% on STL-10; 71.30% vs. 25.72% on Imagenette); homConv's reliance on group sampling causes exponential memory growth with depth, forcing it to use a very shallow 376K-parameter network that cannot handle the task. PDINet also edges out ResNet-18 with projective data augmentation (51.12% vs. 48.73% on STL-10; 71.30% vs. 70.77% on Imagenette), showing that architectural equivariance is more effective than augmentation-based robustness for complex geometric transformations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differential-invariant-based equivariant network framework of Li et al. (2024, 2025) from the affine group to the full projective group PSL(3,ℝ), and simultaneously extends Olver's (2023) projective invariants from scalar functions (requiring third-order derivatives) to multi-dimensional functions where second-order derivatives suffice. The key methodological departure from prior projective equivariant work (homConv, MacDonald et al. 2022) is the elimination of group sampling entirely, replacing it with closed-form invariant computations via a novel cross section tailored to multi-channel inputs. The algebraic analysis yielding a unified 6n−5 element generating set (Theorem 12) is a new theoretical contribution not present in any prior projective invariant literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PDINet is the first deep projective equivariant network that scales to standard architectures (ResNet-18) without the exponential memory blowup that plagues sampling-based methods like homConv, making projective equivariance practically deployable in vision pipelines involving perspective effects (robot navigation, camera pose estimation, 3D scene analysis). Key limitations include: the invariants vanish for single-channel (grayscale) inputs, restricting applicability to color images and multi-channel features; the framework does not yet handle arbitrary group representations beyond scalar fields; and empirical validation is limited to two datasets with a single backbone. Open questions include whether the invariant-based approach generalizes to other non-compact Lie groups and whether the second-order restriction can be relaxed for grayscale inputs.
