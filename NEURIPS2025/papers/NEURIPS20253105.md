## Problem

Shortcut models enable one-step, few-step, and multi-step generation from a single network, but their adoption has been stymied by five critical bottlenecks: a previously unformalized compounding of classifier-free guidance across implicit sub-steps (yielding an effective scale of w^log₂(N)≈17 for N=128), inflexible fixed guidance that removes inference-time control, a frequency bias from pixel-wise losses that produces blurry outputs, a temporal lag in EMA targets that conflicts with self-consistency, and high-curvature flow trajectories from random noise-data pairings.

## Input / Output

The model operates in the 4-channel 32×32 latent space of the SD-VAE-Ft-MSE autoencoder (from 256×256 RGB images) and takes as explicit inputs a noise sample x₀, a class label c, a continuous guidance scale w≥0, and a target step size d. The same trained network produces a generated image at any NFE from 1 to 128 by varying d at inference, with the guidance scale also adjustable at inference time—unlike standard CFG which requires two forward passes per step. The network is a SiT-XL/2 transformer (675M params, 24 layers, 1024 hidden dim) with AdaLN-Zero modulation for all conditional inputs.

## Method

iSM introduces four training components that jointly resolve the five bottlenecks. First, Intrinsic Guidance makes the CFG scale w an explicit network input and trains the model to directly output guided velocities via a dedicated guidance loss with stop-gradient on the guidance direction, eliminating the exponential compounding formalized in Proposition 1 and enabling single-pass guided generation; an interval schedule (t_interval=0.3) disables guidance at high noise levels to preserve diversity. Second, a Multi-Level Wavelet Loss applies 5-level Haar DWT decomposition to all loss terms, isolating reconstruction errors at multiple frequency bands to counteract the low-frequency bias of pixel-wise L2. Third, Scaling Optimal Transport (sOT) aggregates K=32 mini-batches to compute a single large-scale OT plan over K×M samples, then splits the matched pairs back into K training batches, decoupling the effective OT batch size from the training batch size to produce straighter trajectories at ~4% overhead. Fourth, a Twin EMA strategy maintains a fast-decay (0.95) target network for generating near-contemporary self-consistency targets and a separate slow-decay (0.9999) inference network for stable high-quality sampling, resolving the stability-versus-recency conflict inherent in single-EMA self-consistency training.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, iSM achieves FID-50K of 5.27 with 1 NFE, 2.05 with 4 NFE, and 1.88 with 128 NFE, using a 675M-parameter SiT-XL/2 architecture. At 8 NFE, iSM reaches FID 1.93, surpassing the 10-step VAR-d20 (2.57) and approaching the much larger 2B VAR-d30 (1.92). On the broader FD-DINOv2 metric, iSM at 4 NFE scores 83.70 versus 265.90 for the baseline SM, a more than 3× improvement, while Inception Score at 2 NFE more than doubles from 125.66 (SM) to 302.29 (iSM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Shortcut Model (SM) [18], iSM delivers dramatic improvements at every NFE: FID drops from 10.60→5.27 (1 step), 7.80→2.05 (4 steps), and 3.80→1.88 (128 steps). Against the closest competitor IMM [69], iSM wins at 1 NFE (5.27 vs 7.77), 2 NFE (2.44 vs 3.99), and 4 NFE (2.05 vs 2.51), while IMM holds a marginal edge at 8 NFE (1.99 vs 1.93). At 8 NFE, iSM (1.93) outperforms the 10-step VAR-d20 (2.57) with fewer function evaluations and approaches the 2B-parameter VAR-d30 (1.92) using only 675M parameters. At 128 NFE, iSM (1.88) surpasses SiT-XL/2 (2.15), DiT-XL/2 (2.27), and U-ViT-H (2.29), all of which require 250 NFE, though it remains behind REPA-XL/2 (1.42) which uses representation alignment. The iCT baseline [54] is far behind at both 1 and 2 NFE (34.24 and 20.30), confirming that the shortcut model paradigm with proper training is substantially more effective than consistency training for few-step generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Shortcut Model (SM) framework of Frans et al. [18], which itself builds on Flow Matching [37] and Classifier-Free Guidance [24]. The key departure is a formal proof (Proposition 1) that fixed CFG in shortcut models compounds exponentially across implicit sub-steps (w=1.5 with N=128 becomes effective w≈17), a flaw the authors are the first to identify. Rather than applying CFG post-hoc between model outputs as in standard diffusion, iSM makes the guidance scale an explicit network input (Intrinsic Guidance), and pairs this with a multi-level wavelet loss, scaling optimal transport, and a twin-EMA strategy to address frequency bias, trajectory curvature, and self-consistency lag respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compounding guidance formalization is a genuine conceptual contribution that explains a class of artifacts previously attributed to 'training instability' or 'insufficient steps,' and the fix (intrinsic guidance) is elegant and broadly applicable to any model using recursive self-consistency with CFG. Practically, iSM nearly halves the 1-step FID (10.60→5.27) and achieves 2.05 at 4 steps, closing the gap with leading GANs and making shortcut models competitive for latency-sensitive deployment. Open questions include whether the framework transfers to text-to-image or video generation, whether the 512×512 results (FID 37.05 at 1 step) indicate a resolution ceiling, and whether the sOT and wavelet components generalize beyond the SiT-XL architecture tested here.
