## Problem

Existing federated continual learning (FCL) methods are evaluated under the assumption of unrestricted training resources on edge devices, ignoring the practical constraints of storage, computation, and labeling that dominate real-world deployment. This paper asks whether state-of-the-art FCL approaches can deliver their claimed performance when memory buffer, computational budget, and label rate are simultaneously limited, and identifies which techniques and resources matter most under such constraints.

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds streaming image data across sequential tasks in two scenarios (Class-IL introducing new classes, Domain-IL introducing new domains) to methods operating under constrained memory buffer (M cached samples), computational budget (B gradient steps), and label rate (R% labeled data). Datasets span 28×28 grayscale digits (Digit-10), 32×32 RGB (CIFAR-10/100), 64×64 RGB (Tiny-ImageNet), and 300×300 RGB (Office-31, Office-Caltech-10). Each method outputs per-task and aggregate classification accuracy, reported as final accuracy A(f) after the last task and average accuracy Ā across all tasks.

## Method

This is a large-scale empirical benchmark (1,000+ GPU hours on RTX 4090/3090 GPUs) that systematically evaluates 12 FCL methods across six datasets under three resource constraints, dissecting FCL systems into four typical techniques: Sample Caching, Data Synthesis, Knowledge Distillation, and Network Extension. The paper conducts ablation studies varying each resource independently (memory buffer sizes 0–2000, computational budgets scaled per dataset, label rates 10%–100%) and then analyzes how each technique responds to individual and combined limitations. Key findings show all methods degrade sharply under extreme constraints, with memory buffer being the most impactful single resource, computational budget being the primary bottleneck for generative and distillation-based methods, and label rates below 70% causing substantial drops. Simple methods like FedAvg prove more resilient to computational constraints, while generative replay methods (FedCIL, AF-FCL) excel on simple datasets with sufficient resources but collapse on complex ones like CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet.

## Results

Under sufficient resources (Table 2), Re-Fed achieves the best average accuracy on CIFAR-10 (57.52%), CIFAR-100 (37.82%), Tiny-ImageNet (52.19%), and Office-Caltech-10 (58.82%), while FOT leads on CIFAR-10 A(f) (43.74%) and CFeD on CIFAR-10 A(f) (46.54%). Under extremely limited resources (Table 3), all methods collapse: the best A(f) on CIFAR-10 drops from 46.54% (CFeD) to 10.11% (FedAvg), and on Office-31 from 49.53% (Re-Fed) to 9.02% (FedWeIT). The paper's central finding is that no existing FCL method achieves expected performance when memory buffer, computational budget, and label rate are simultaneously constrained, with over 1,000+ GPU hours of experiments confirming this across six datasets and two incremental scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This benchmark paper evaluates 12 FCL baselines across six datasets under both sufficient and extremely limited resource settings. Under sufficient resources, Re-Fed (synergistic replay) is the most consistently strong method, achieving top or near-top A̅ on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., 57.52% on CIFAR-10, 52.19% on Tiny-ImageNet, 58.82% on Office-Caltech-10), while FOT (orthogonal projection) leads on CIFAR-10 A̅ (58.24%) and CIFAR-100 A̅ (38.57%). Generative-replay methods FedCIL and AF-FCL excel on simpler datasets (CIFAR-10 A(f): 45.35% and 44.95%; Digit-10 A̅: 92.83% and 93.49%) but degrade sharply on complex datasets like CIFAR-100 (A̅: 34.70% and 34.60%) and Tiny-ImageNet (A̅: 44.54% and 43.58%), falling below even FedAvg. Under extremely limited resources (Table 3), all methods collapse uniformly: the best CIFAR-10 A(f) drops to 10.11% (FedAvg) from 46.54% (CFeD), and Office-31 A(f) drops to 9.02% (FedWeIT) from 49.53% (Re-Fed), confirming that no existing FCL method is robust to simultaneous memory, compute, and label constraints. The ablation studies further show that sample caching (Re-Fed) is most resilient to memory buffer reduction, knowledge distillation (CFeD, FOT) is most sensitive to computational budget, and data synthesis (FedCIL, AF-FCL) suffers from both memory and compute limitations simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a critical re-examination of the entire FCL literature (FedAvg, FedCIL, Re-Fed, FOT, CFeD, FedWeIT, and others) by introducing a resource-constrained lens that prior work has systematically ignored. It draws conceptual parallels to centralized continual learning under budget constraints (e.g., [44] on computationally budgeted CL and [68] on sparsely labeled streams), but is the first to apply such constraints to the federated setting across all three resources simultaneously. Rather than proposing a new algorithm, it repurposes existing FCL methods as baselines and dissects them into four canonical techniques (sample caching, data synthesis, knowledge distillation, network extension) to isolate which components are most resource-sensitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central finding—that no existing FCL method achieves expected performance under realistic edge-device resource constraints—challenges the implicit assumption in the FCL literature that training resources are unbounded, and provides practitioners with concrete guidance on which techniques (e.g., knowledge distillation over generative replay) are more deployable. Open questions it raises include how to design FCL algorithms that are inherently resource-adaptive, how to optimize sample selection strategies beyond random/FIFO/LIFO, and how to handle communication bandwidth as a fourth constraint. The benchmark itself (1000+ GPU hours, 6 datasets, 12 methods, two scenarios) becomes a useful reference point for future resource-efficient FCL research.
