## Problem

Diffusion language models can sample tokens in parallel, potentially breaking the L-step sequential bottleneck of autoregressive generation, but rigorous convergence guarantees for the regime T < L (fewer iterations than sequence length) were missing. Prior theory (Chen and Ying, 2024) only covered the restrictive case where fewer than one token is masked per step, which contradicts practical masked diffusion models that unmask large token batches simultaneously. This paper asks whether provable convergence can be established for general mask size schedules and arbitrary data distributions.

## Input / Output

The sampling procedure takes a fully masked sequence Y_T = (M, ..., M) in X̄^L, a trained factorized mask predictor p̂ = ∏ p̂_i(·|Y_t) that predicts each masked token independently given the current partially-unmasked context, and a mask size schedule {s_t}_{t=1}^T with Σ s_t = L specifying how many tokens to reveal at each reverse step. It outputs a fully unmasked sequence Y_0 ∈ X^L. The theoretical guarantee bounds the KL divergence KL(p_{X_0} ‖ p_{Y_0|M}) between the output distribution and the true data distribution, with the bound depending on the total mutual information Σ I(X^(i); X^(-i)) and the training error ε_train of the mask predictor.

## Method

The core technique is a recursive decoupling argument parameterized by the maximum mask size s_max. The authors define ε(s_max) as the worst-case sampling error over all schedules with max mask size s_max, then prove the key recursive inequality ε(s_max) ≤ ε(⌈s_max/2⌉) + (s_max/2L) Σ_i I(X_0^(i); X_0^(-i)) by splitting each unmasking step into two sub-steps of half the size and applying the chain rule of KL divergence to isolate a mutual information term. Unrolling this recursion over ⌈log₂ s_max⌉ levels yields the O(1/T) bound under balanced schedules. The training error ε_train is cleanly separated via a likelihood-gap decomposition, so the fundamental sampling difficulty is governed purely by the information coupling among tokens. A matching lower bound is established by constructing a specific balanced schedule and a hierarchical family of random conditioning sets W_j^(-i), proving the 1/T rate and linear mutual information dependence are tight up to constants.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that the KL divergence between the output distribution and the data distribution is bounded by (2^⌈log₂ s_max⌉ − 1)/L · Σᵢ I(X₀⁽ⁱ⁾; X₀⁽⁻ⁱ⁾) + ε_train, where s_max is the maximum mask size per step. Under a balanced mask schedule (Corollary 1), this simplifies to O(1/T) · Σᵢ I(X₀⁽ⁱ⁾; X₀⁽⁻ⁱ⁾) + ε_train, confirming that the sampling error decays inversely with the number of iterations T and scales linearly with the total mutual information among tokens. A matching lower bound (Theorem 2) shows this O(1/T) rate is tight up to constant factors. Numerical validation on a K=10, L=100 Potts chain confirms the 1/T scaling (log-log slope ≈ −1) and linear dependence on mutual information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is with Feng et al. (2025), who analyze masked diffusion for n-gram language models. Feng et al.'s token error rate (TER) bound scales as ((n−1)/T)^(1/n) · log|X|, which is suboptimal for any n > 1 and becomes vacuous when n ≥ log L (requiring T ≫ L iterations to beat a uniform random baseline). In contrast, this paper's bound (via Corollary 1) yields a TER that decays as O(log|X|/T) regardless of n, and holds for arbitrary data distributions rather than being restricted to n-gram models. The paper also contrasts with Chen and Ying (2024), whose convergence analysis for discrete diffusion is restricted to regimes where on average less than one token is masked per step — an assumption incompatible with practical diffusion language models that mask a large fraction of tokens per iteration. This paper's theory covers the general regime T < L, formally justifying sampling acceleration over autoregressive models that require L sequential steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of discrete diffusion models initiated by Chen and Ying (2024), which was restricted to regimes where on average less than one token is masked per step, to the practically relevant T < L regime where multiple tokens are unmasked per iteration. It builds on the masked diffusion framework of Austin et al. (2021) and adopts the standard decoupling approach from the continuous diffusion convergence literature (Lee et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2022a; Li and Yan, 2024), but applies it to the discrete, token-level setting with an information-theoretic lens. The key departure is that the analysis covers arbitrary mask size schedules and arbitrary data distributions, rather than being confined to n-gram models or single-token-per-step schedules as in Feng et al. (2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous justification that diffusion language models can produce high-quality samples with fewer iterations than the sequence length, breaking the fundamental L-step bottleneck of autoregressive generation. The matching upper and lower bounds (tight up to constants) establish that the O(1/T) rate with a mutual-information coefficient is a fundamental limit, not an artifact of the analysis. Open questions include whether end-to-end guarantees (combining training error with sampling error) can be established, whether the balanced-schedule assumption needed for the lower bound can be removed, and whether these insights extend to other discrete diffusion formulations beyond masked diffusion. The entropy-based unmasking heuristic (Remark 1) offers a practical direction for adaptive sampling strategies.
