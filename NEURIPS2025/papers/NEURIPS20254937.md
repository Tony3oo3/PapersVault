## Problem

Diffusion-based virtual try-on models achieve low FID and LPIPS scores yet frequently produce outputs with inconsistent garment textures, garbled text characters, and body-clothing artifacts that fail to meet user expectations. Standard perceptual metrics cannot capture these fine-grained visual attributes, leaving a gap between quantitative benchmarks and actual human preference in VTON quality assessment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254937/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VTON-VLLM evaluator takes a concatenated garment-person image pair and produces multi-turn chain-of-thought reasoning with scores across six sub-dimensions (visual pattern, text characters, sleeve style, garment shape, edge artifacts, human pose) on a 0–5 scale. The VRM-Instruct refinement model takes a horizontally concatenated visual condition (garment image + low-quality VTON output, both H×W×3) plus a text refinement instruction, and outputs a high-quality inpainted try-on image via FLUX-Fill. At inference, the system iteratively feeds refined images back through the VLLM for re-scoring up to N times (optimal N=3).

## Method

The core contribution is a two-component system: (1) VTON-VLLM, a Pixtral-12B VLLM fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 16) on 40K human-validated VTON annotations structured as multi-turn conversational prompts to elicit chain-of-thought quality reasoning; and (2) VRM-Instruct, a plug-and-play refinement model built on FLUX-Fill that frames VTON correction as conditional image inpainting, trained with flow-matching loss where the text condition is a fine-grained refinement instruction generated by VTON-VLLM comparing the reference garment against the low-quality output. At inference, VTON-VLLM additionally serves as a human-preference-aware reward model in a test-time scaling loop: it scores each VRM-Instruct output, and if quality criteria are unmet, generates an updated instruction for another refinement pass, iteratively steering the result toward human preferences without retraining the base VTON model.

## Results

On VITON-HD, adding VRM-Instruct + TTS to IDM-VTON reduces LPIPS from 0.082 to 0.051, KID from 0.741 to 0.634, and increases the human-preference-aware Garment Consistency (GCp) from 4.665 to 4.873 and Image Quality (IQu) from 4.392 to 4.711. On the more challenging VITON-Bench, the same plug-and-play module lifts SPM-Diff's GCu from 4.235 to 4.407 and IQu from 3.804 to 4.280, while CAT-VTON w/ VRM-Instruct + TTS achieves the best IQu (4.353) and FID (10.232) among all unpaired settings. The VTON-VLLM evaluator achieves 84.52% inter-rater agreement with human annotators, and its scores (4.92 vs. 3.94) clearly separate human-preferred from non-preferred images, unlike FID (6.84 vs. 6.99).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed VRM-Instruct + TTS is a plug-and-play module that consistently improves every baseline VTON model on all human-preference-aware metrics (GC and IQ) without retraining the base model. On VITON-HD, IDM-VTON w/ VRM-Instruct + TTS achieves the best LPIPS (0.051 vs. 0.082 baseline) and KID (0.634 vs. 0.741), while SPM-Diff w/ VRM-Instruct + TTS attains the highest GCp (4.900 vs. 4.742) and IQp (4.930 vs. 4.775) among all methods. CAT-VTON w/ VRM-Instruct + TTS yields the best IQu (4.738 vs. 4.420 baseline) and competitive LPIPS (0.052). On the harder VITON-Bench, all baselines degrade substantially (e.g., IDM-VTON IQu drops from 4.392 to 3.825), but the proposed module recovers much of the gap, with CAT-VTON w/ VRM-Instruct + TTS reaching IQu 4.353 and FID 10.232. The key takeaway is that the gains are largest on the preference-aware metrics (GC, IQ) rather than traditional pixel-level metrics (SSIM), confirming that VTON-VLLM captures fine-grained garment consistency and edge quality that FID/LPIPS miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion-based virtual try-on (building on IDM-VTON, SPM-Diff, CAT-VTON, OOTDiffusion) and vision-language models for evaluation (building on Pixtral-12B). The key conceptual departure is repurposing a fine-tuned VLLM not merely as a post-hoc evaluator but as an active steering mechanism: it generates fine-grained refinement instructions to train a plug-and-play inpainting model (VRM-Instruct on FLUX-Fill) and serves as a preference-aware reward for iterative test-time scaling at inference. The test-time scaling idea is borrowed from LLM inference literature and applied to VTON for the first time, while the human-feedback fine-tuning with chain-of-thought multi-turn prompts follows the RLHF-for-VLLMs line of work but specialized to garment-person consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: state-of-the-art VTON models achieve low FID/LPIPS yet still produce outputs that fail to meet user expectations on fine-grained attributes like text rendering, texture preservation, and edge artifacts. The finding that FID shows no meaningful separation between human-preferred and non-preferred images (6.84 vs 6.99) while VTON-VLLM scores do (4.92 vs 3.94) challenges the field's reliance on distributional metrics for VTON evaluation. Open questions include whether the plug-and-play refinement generalizes to lower-body garments and dresses as effectively as upper-body, whether the TTS computational overhead is acceptable for real-time e-commerce deployment, and whether the approach handles rare or long-tail clothing patterns. The VITON-Bench benchmark is a practically useful addition for stress-testing VTON models on complex real-world scenarios.
