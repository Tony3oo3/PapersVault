## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks largely test independent image-to-text extractions where each look at the image is self-contained and reasoning occurs in text space, leaving untested whether models can perform sequential visual reasoning that chains evidence across multiple, possibly distant, image regions. The paper asks whether leading VLMs can execute three core nonlocal visual operations—comparative perception, saccadic search, and smooth visual search—that are trivial for humans but resist decomposition into natural-language steps.

## Input / Output

Each task is procedurally generated with minimal context and evaluated in a 2-shot setting over 200 (or 125) examples per variant. Object Re-Identification presents two synthetic images (2–6 geometric shapes, with random rigid transformations and distractor objects) and requires a yes/no judgment; Visual Scavenger Hunt presents a grid of color-labeled shapes and asks the model to follow a chain of 2–4 label-referenced jumps to report a final color; Circuit Connections presents a breadboard wiring diagram (4–10 components, 5 wire colors) and asks which component a named port connects to. Random-guess baselines are 50%, ~9%, and 14.29% respectively, and human evaluators scored 100% or 99.5% on all tasks.

## Method

The paper introduces three procedural generators, each grounded in a principle from classical human vision research: Gestalt connectedness and Treisman's Feature Integration Theory motivate Object Re-Identification (with Standard, Unconnected, and Pixel-Perfect variants to isolate the role of contiguity); Ullman's visual routines motivate Visual Scavenger Hunt (with chain lengths 2–4 to test degradation over multi-step horizons); and boundary/contour tracing within Ullman's framework motivates Circuit Connections (with Standard, Single Color, and Unique Colors variants to strip away color-cue shortcuts). Beyond raw accuracy, the evaluation employs a log-odds regression on wire distance and crossing count to test whether models exhibit the statistical signature of true line tracing, and a decomposed single-step experiment on Visual Scavenger Hunt to separate perceptual failures from chaining failures. Fourteen models spanning closed-source flagships (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4, O4-mini, O3) and open-source models (Llama 3.2 Vision, Qwen 2.5 VL, Molmo, Phi-4 Multimodal, InternVL3, Mistral Small 3.1, Gemma 3) are evaluated, revealing that even the strongest models barely exceed random on the hardest variants and show no evidence of self-correction or smooth contour tracing.

## Results

On Object Re-Identification (random baseline 50%), no model significantly exceeds chance on the Standard variant (best ~60%), while GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4 reach 65–79% on the Unconnected and Pixel-Perfect variants—still 20+ points below the 100% human baseline. On Visual Scavenger Hunt (random baseline ~9%), only GPT-5, O4-mini, and Gemini 2.5 Pro substantially outperform random, with O4-mini degrading as chain length increases. On Circuit Connections (random baseline 14.29%), the best result is 27% (Gemini 2.5 Pro on Single Color), and all models fail to perform true contour tracing, with Euclidean wire distance being the strongest negative predictor of success across nearly all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 13 VLMs across three procedurally generated tasks; the open-source baselines (Phi-4 Multimodal 14B, Qwen 2.5 VL 7B/32B, Molmo 7B, Mistral Small 3.1 24B, Llama 3.2 Vision 11B, InternVL3 14B) consistently underperform the closed-source flagships. On Object Re-Identification, Molmo 7B and Llama 3.2 Vision 11B effectively ignore the input (F1 ≈ 0 for one class), while Qwen 2.5 VL and Phi-4 Multimodal score near the 50% random baseline on all variants. On Visual Scavenger Hunt, the decomposed-task experiment (Table 1) reveals a stark divide: o4-mini and Gemini 2.5 Pro achieve 88–91% accuracy when steps are provided sequentially, whereas Llama 3.2 Vision, Qwen 2.5 VL 32B, and Qwen 2.5 VL 7B drop to 15–24%, indicating they lack even single-step perceptual grounding. On Circuit Connections, the log-odds analysis (Table 2) shows that open-source models like Molmo 7B and Phi-4 Multimodal show no significant sensitivity to wire crossings (p > 0.2), confirming they do not trace lines at all, while top closed-source models (Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, o4-mini) exhibit a significant negative crossing effect (p < 0.05) only in the Single Color variant, suggesting a choppy, saccadic approximation rather than smooth contour tracing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of VLM perception benchmarks (VLMs are Blind, HallusionBench, VisOnlyQA) by shifting focus from single-step adversarial perception to sequential, nonlocal visual reasoning that requires chaining evidence across spatially distant image regions. It draws on classical human vision theory—Gestalt grouping, Treisman's Feature Integration Theory, and Ullman's visual routines—to define three distinct algorithmic capabilities (comparative perception, saccadic search, smooth visual search) and instantiate them as procedurally generated, minimal-context tasks. Unlike prior chart-reading benchmarks (ChartQA, ChartQAPro) that embed strong layout priors, these tasks strip away domain conventions to isolate whether models can execute visual algorithms rather than exploit learned heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4 barely exceed random on tasks humans solve at ~100% exposes a fundamental gap between raw visual acuity and visual reasoning that prior benchmarks have masked. The selective-perception result—models performing worse on contiguous objects than disconnected ones—suggests VLMs do not bind features via Gestalt grouping and instead rely on natural-language shortcuts, raising open questions about whether this limitation is architectural (vision encoder vs. language decoder) or trainable. Limitations include the synthetic setting, small per-variant sample sizes (125–200), and the fact that real-world images may be parseable via visual skims that these controlled tasks do not capture.
