## Problem

Influence functions, the leading gradient-based data valuation method, cannot scale to modern LLMs because inverse Hessian-vector products require O(n²) memory and O(n³) time for n > 10⁹ parameters, and computing gradients for all training data costs as much as a full training epoch. Prior gradient projection approaches (TRAK, Arnoldi IF) reduce the Hessian dimension but introduce their own O(bkn) projection compute and O(kn) projection matrix memory that remain prohibitive at LLM scale, forcing them to use very small projection dimensions that degrade accuracy.

## Input / Output

At logging time, the method performs a single forward-backward pass over the entire training corpus (e.g., 1B tokens of OpenWebText) to extract and store projected per-sample gradients to disk (3.5 TB for an 8B model with k=64). At query time, a test sequence is passed through the same model to obtain its projected gradient, and influence scores are computed as dot products against all stored training gradients, effectively reducing data valuation to a vector similarity search. The output is a ranked list of training examples with l-RelatIF-normalized influence scores, where the top entries identify the most valuable data for the queried output.

## Method

LOGRA exploits the Kronecker-product structure of gradients in linear layers (vec(DW) = Σ_t x_{i,t} ⊗ Dx_{o,t}) to impose a matching Kronecker structure on the projection matrix P = P_i ⊗ P_o, so that projected gradients are reconstructed from separately projected forward and backward activations rather than from the full gradient, reducing projection cost from O(bnk) to O(b√nk). This is implemented as small encoder-bottleneck-decoder add-on layers (architecturally identical to LoRA with a zero-initialized bottleneck) that leave the original model's forward and backward passes unaffected while enabling per-sample projected gradient extraction via standard autograd. Theoretically, the paper interprets the damping term in influence functions as a spectral gradient sparsification mechanism that softly discards small Hessian components, motivating projection as a hard analogue and yielding a PCA initialization scheme derived from KFAC eigendecompositions. The accompanying software LOGIX uses PyTorch hooks to intercept training logs and compute dataset-level statistics (e.g., Fisher information), making it compatible with FSDP, DeepSpeed, and other LLM training infrastructure while converting the influence computation into a disk-backed vector search with memory-mapped I/O overlapped with computation.

## Results

On Llama3-8B-Instruct with 1B-token OpenWebText, LOGRA achieves up to 6,500× throughput improvement (79,004 vs. 12.2 pairs/s) and 5× memory reduction (15 GB vs. 75 GB) in influence computation compared to EKFAC influence, while also reducing logging memory from 71–80 GB to 23 GB. In quantitative counterfactual evaluations (brittleness test and LDS) on FMNIST, CIFAR-10, and WikiText, LOGRA slightly underperforms EKFAC influence but noticeably outperforms gradient dot product, TRAK, and representation similarity baselines. Qualitatively, LOGRA-identified most valuable data for Llama3-8B-Instruct and GPT2-XL share semantic and stylistic similarities with the queried LLM outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EKFAC influence [17], the state-of-the-art and only other method runnable at billion-scale, LOGRA trades a small accuracy gap (slightly lower brittleness-test and LDS scores on small-scale benchmarks) for a 6,500× throughput gain in influence computation and 5× memory savings, making EKFAC's 11,300 A100-GPU-hour cost for 256 test queries impractical. Against TRAK [43], LOGRA achieves higher accuracy because its Kronecker-structured projection allows a larger effective projection dimension without the prohibitive memory cost of TRAK's full projection matrix; TRAK also fails with CUDA OOM on 8B-parameter models. Against gradient dot product [44] and representation similarity [22], LOGRA noticeably outperforms on both brittleness test and LDS across all three small-scale benchmarks (FMNIST, CIFAR-10, WikiText). The PCA-initialized variant (LOGRA-PCA) further improves over random initialization on FMNIST and CIFAR-10, though it underperforms on WikiText+GPT2 due to the lack of a Transformer-specific KFAC approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the influence function framework (Koh & Liang, 2017) and the gradient projection strategies introduced by TRAK (Park et al., 2023) and Arnoldi IF (Schioppa et al., 2022) by identifying the projection step itself as the dominant scalability bottleneck and solving it via a Kronecker-product structured projection (LOGRA) that reduces complexity from O(bnk) to O(b√nk). Architecturally, LOGRA borrows the LoRA-style add-on layer design (Hu et al., 2021) but repurposes it for gradient extraction rather than parameter adaptation. The theoretical contribution connects the standard damping term in influence functions to spectral gradient sparsification, providing a principled justification for projection-based approaches that prior work (TRAK, Arnoldi IF) only addressed indirectly through proxy quantities like iHVP variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of influence-function-based data valuation at the scale of an 8B-parameter LLM and 1B-token dataset, with a 6,500× compute speedup over the only previously runnable baseline (EKFAC influence), making practical data attribution systems feasible for the first time. The open-source LOGIX software lowers the implementation barrier for the broader community. Key open questions remain: quantitative accuracy at LLM scale is only assessed qualitatively (no counterfactual retraining is feasible), the method is sensitive to outlier gradients with large norms, and the 1B-token evaluation subset is storage-limited rather than compute-limited, leaving industry-scale (100B+ token) validation as future work. The paper also explicitly defers the socially sensitive question of how to translate influence scores into fair compensation.
