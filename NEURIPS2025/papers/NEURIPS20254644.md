## Problem

Prior traceability results in stochastic convex optimization were limited to ℓ_2 geometry or mean estimation in ℓ_∞ norm, where only a 1/log(d) fraction of training samples could be shown to be traceable. For general ℓ_p geometries, it was unknown whether a constant fraction of samples must be memorized by any accurate learner outside the differential privacy regime, and the optimal DP excess risk for p > 2 remained an open problem.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a family of ℓ_p-Lipschitz-bounded SCO problems (Θ ⊆ B_p(1), Z, f) and a target excess risk α, then constructs specific hard instances within this family. The output is a lower bound on recall m (number of traceable samples) that holds for every α-learner, expressed as a function of α, n, d, and the soundness parameter ξ. For p > 2, the construction additionally yields a lower bound on the optimal DP excess risk, which is the quantity (d/(ε²n²))^{1/p} in the high-dimensional regime d ≥ εn.

## Method

The paper introduces two key technical tools. First, a sparse fingerprinting lemma (Lemma 4.2) that extends the classical beta-fingerprinting technique to data drawn from sparse sets Z_k = {z ∈ {0,±1}^d : ‖z‖_0 = k}, where the correlation between a learner's output and its training samples scales by a factor of d/k, capturing the intuition that sparser data forces greater per-sample memorization. Second, a subgaussian trace value framework that converts fingerprinting lower bounds into traceability guarantees via a Paley-Zygmund anti-concentration argument, requiring only that the tracer induces a subgaussian process over the parameter space. Hard problem constructions use sparse data sets for p > 2 (with sparsity k = (6α)^p d) and an intersection of ℓ_1 and ℓ_∞ balls for p = 1 to simultaneously drive up sample complexity and prevent the ERM counterexample.

## Results

The paper establishes that for every p∈[1,∞), there exists a hard SCO problem where every sample-efficient learner outside the DP regime is traceable with recall matching (up to constants) the statistical sample complexity. Specifically, for p∈(1,2] the recall is Ω(1/α²) and for p=1 it is Ω(log(d)/α²), both matching the minimax sample complexity exactly. For p>2, the recall is Ω(1/α^p), again matching sample complexity. The paper also provides an improved DP-SCO lower bound for p>2: α ≥ c·(d/(ε²n²))^(1/p) in the high-dimensional regime d≥εn, which strictly improves upon all prior bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines; comparisons are against prior theoretical bounds. Against DSSUV15 (mean estimation in ℓ_∞), the paper closes a log(d) gap: DSSUV15 showed only a 1/log(d) fraction of samples are traceable, while here the full log(d)/α² samples (matching sample complexity) are traceable. Against ADHLR24 (ℓ_2 SCO traceability), the paper extends the constant-fraction traceability result from ℓ_2 to all ℓ_p geometries and introduces the sparse fingerprinting lemma as the key new tool. Against the best prior DP-SCO lower bounds for p>2, the paper improves the high-dimensional (d≥εn) bound from √(d/(n²ε²)) (LLL24) and min{(1/(εn))^(1/p), d^(1−1/p)/(εn)} (ABGMU22) to (d/(n²ε²))^(1/p), which is strictly stronger for all p>2 in that regime. The paper also shows that combining BST14's DP-SCO reduction with DSSUV15's fingerprinting yields only O(1/α) sample complexity for the relevant Bayesian problem, confirming that the new sparse construction is necessary for optimal results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the traceability analysis of Dwork et al. (FOCS 2015) for mean estimation in ℓ_∞ and Attias et al. (2024) for ℓ_2 SCO to the full range of ℓ_p geometries, closing a log(d) gap in the former. The technical core is a sparse fingerprinting lemma that generalizes the classical fingerprinting lemma of Steinke & Ullman (FOCS 2017) to sparse data domains, which is essential because hard SCO instances for p > 2 inherently involve sparse sets (as in Agarwal et al., NeurIPS 2009). A unifying 'subgaussian trace value' framework converts fingerprinting lower bounds into traceability, DP, and even non-private sample complexity results, building on the DP lower bound technique of Feldman & Steinke (COLT 2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a sharp phase transition for p ∈ [1,2]: below the DP excess risk threshold, every sample-efficient learner must memorize a constant fraction of its training data, and above it, non-traceable (DP) algorithms exist. For p > 2, the optimal DP-SCO risk remains an open problem, and the paper's improved lower bound (d/(ε²n²))^{1/p} in the high-dimensional regime d ≥ εn is the best known, partially closing a long-standing gap. The sparse fingerprinting lemma is a reusable technical tool of independent interest for future lower-bound constructions. The authors conjecture their DP lower bound is tight, which would complete the full picture of the traceability-privacy tradeoff in SCO.
