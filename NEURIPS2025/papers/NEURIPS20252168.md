## Problem

Preference-based reward models in RLHF are prone to reward hacking, where they exploit spurious features (verbosity, sycophancy, tone) that correlate with preference labels in training data rather than reflecting genuine human intent, leading to poor out-of-distribution generalization. Prior mitigation methods address one shortcut at a time (e.g., length penalty only) without a unified framework, and most require explicit annotations of spurious attributes that are unavailable in practice. This paper asks: how can all targeted shortcut behaviors be rectified simultaneously within a single principled learning objective?

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard preference triplets (prompt, chosen response, rejected response) as the primary training signal, augmented by auxiliary shortcut feature embeddings extracted per response. These features include rule-based metrics (character count for length, Type-Token Ratio for lexical diversity) and LLM-as-a-Judge scores (sycophancy, tone, creativity, helpfulness rated 0–10 via GPT-4o), which are cached and computed independently of the reward model. The output is a reward model r_θ that assigns scalar scores to prompt-response pairs, designed to be invariant to shortcut dimensions; this reward model then drives downstream policy alignment via RLHF (Best-of-N sampling) or DPO, producing a policy model evaluated on AlpacaEval-2.

## Method

PRISM reframes shortcut behaviors as group actions from a compact unitary group G acting on the response space, and defines a group-invariant kernel K(y_w, y_l | x) via Haar-integration of an RBF kernel over all group transformations, ensuring the kernel value is identical for any response pair and its shortcut-transformed versions. This intractable integral is approximated by random feature maps Φ built from empirical CDFs over sampled group elements and Gaussian templates, so that ⟨Φ(y_w), Φ(y_l)⟩ converges to the invariant kernel as the number of bins and templates grows. The PRISM loss modifies the Bradley-Terry ranking loss by subtracting a weighted sum of RBF kernel distances (one per shortcut type, combined via learnable softmax weights) from the reward margin, effectively removing the shortcut component so the model must maximize margin on generalizable features. A global decorrelation regularizer further penalizes batch-level correlation between reward scores and each shortcut feature. The paper proves a generalization bound showing the PRISM objective achieves lower expected risk than standard BT, with the gap shrinking as the number of shortcut features, group samples, bins, and training samples increase.

## Results

On RewardBench, PRISM (Llama-3 8B) achieves the best overall score of 87.8, outperforming the strongest baseline GRM (87.0) and the much larger HelpSteer2 RM with Llama-3 70B (86.3). On the more challenging RM-Bench, PRISM (Llama-3.1-8B) attains the highest average score of 71.0, surpassing Skywork-Reward (70.1) and URM-LLaMa-3.1-8B (70.0). In downstream policy evaluation on AlpacaEval-2, PRISM-induced policies achieve higher win rates with moderate response length compared to BT, RRM, and ODIN baselines. Correlation analysis on RM-Bench confirms PRISM achieves near-zero Pearson correlation with verbosity, tone, and sycophancy, whereas the BT baseline shows strong positive correlations with all three shortcuts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM (87.8) outperforms all baselines on RewardBench. Against the standard Bradley-Terry baseline (83.6), PRISM gains 4.2 points, with the largest improvements in Chat Hard (+3.2) and Safety (+3.3). Compared to the strongest same-size baseline GRM (87.0), PRISM leads by 0.8 points, primarily on Safety (+1.7) and Reasoning (+0.8). PRISM also surpasses the 70B HelpSteer2 RM (86.3) by 1.5 points despite using a 7.5× smaller backbone. On RM-Bench, PRISM (71.0 avg) beats OffsetBias (69.0) by 2.0 points and Skywork-Reward (70.1) by 0.9 points, with the largest margin on Math (+10.2 over OffsetBias). In downstream policy evaluation (Figure 3), PRISM-induced policies achieve higher win rates on AlpacaEval-2 than BT, RRM, and ODIN policies while maintaining moderate response length, confirming that the reward model improvements translate to better alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM bridges two previously separate research threads: (1) reward hacking mitigation in RLHF, where prior methods like ODIN, RRM, and OffsetBias each target a single shortcut (e.g., verbosity or length bias) with ad-hoc penalties, and (2) invariant learning in kernel methods, where group-invariant kernels via Haar-integration (Haasdonk et al., Mroueh et al.) provide principled invariance guarantees for classification. The paper's key conceptual move is to model shortcut transformations (length changes, tone shifts, sycophancy) as group actions on the response space and approximate the resulting invariant kernel with random feature maps, yielding a closed-form regularizer that can be plugged into the standard Bradley-Terry loss. This departs from prior single-penalty approaches by supporting multiple shortcut features simultaneously within a shared metric space, and from invariant risk minimization by not requiring explicit group annotations in the training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRISM offers a principled, unified framework for addressing the growing list of reward hacking manifestations (verbosity, sycophancy, tone, concept correlations) without requiring per-shortcut engineering or additional attribute annotations, which is practically important as new spurious correlations continue to emerge. However, the method still requires practitioners to identify and specify which shortcut features to target, and the LLM-as-a-Judge feature extraction introduces non-trivial API costs. Open questions include automatic discovery of novel shortcuts, extension to multimodal settings, and whether the kernel-based invariance framework scales to the very large preference datasets used in frontier model training. The modest empirical gains over strong baselines (e.g., GRM, SSRM) suggest the framework's main value is conceptual unification and theoretical grounding rather than dramatic performance leaps.
