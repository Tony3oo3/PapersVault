## Problem

Existing sample selection methods for learning with noisy labels rely on early training stages to identify confident samples, but they overlook that not all mislabeled examples harm model performance equally. Mislabeled examples that the model correctly predicts early in training (termed Mislabeled Easy Examples, or MEEs) disproportionately degrade generalization because they disrupt the initial formation of correct feature representations, yet they are indistinguishable from genuinely clean easy samples using standard loss-based or dynamics-based criteria.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training set of RGB images with noisy labels (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for WebVision/ImageNet-1k) and produces a refined subset of training samples from which a final classifier is trained to output class labels. The key intermediate output is a per-sample triple of metrics—cross-entropy loss, prediction confidence, and input gradient norm—computed by a later-stage model over the early-learned subset, which drives the MEE filtering decision. The method is applied iteratively (3 rounds by default), progressively shrinking the training set before a final from-scratch training phase.

## Method

Early Cutting introduces a recalibration step that leverages the model's state at the early-stopping epoch (when overfitting begins) to re-evaluate the confident subset initially selected by learning-time dynamics (building on Late Stopping). Within this subset, MEEs are identified by three simultaneous criteria: high cross-entropy loss (the later-stage model's prediction contradicts the noisy label), high prediction confidence (the model is certain about its contradictory prediction), and low input gradient norm (the loss is insensitive to small input perturbations, indicating a stable but incorrect association). These criteria are operationalized as percentile thresholds (top 10% loss, top 20% confidence, bottom 20% gradient norm) applied to a candidate pool determined by an early cutting rate γ=1.5. The identified MEEs are removed from the confident subset, and the refined set is used for the next iteration of sample selection. The approach is counterintuitive because it trusts a later-stage model—typically considered less reliable—to correct the deceptive confidence of early-learned mislabeled samples, and it is robust to inadvertent removal of clean samples due to the inherent redundancy among early-learned examples.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), Early Cutting achieves 93.79% (Sym 20%), 91.80% (Sym 40%), 93.40% (Inst 20%), and 90.78% (Inst 40%) test accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline RLM by 0.68–1.05 points across all settings. On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-34), the gains are larger: 76.20% (Sym 20%) and 72.77% (Sym 40%), surpassing the next-best method (Misdetect at 73.90% / RLM at 67.68%) by 2.3 and 5.1 points respectively. On large-scale datasets, Early Cutting reaches 73.81% on WebVision validation, 71.20% on ILSVRC12 validation, and 73.28% on full ImageNet-1k with 40% symmetric noise, consistently leading all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Early Cutting outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and noise setting tested. Against the closest competitor RLM (Li et al., 2024), it gains 0.68–1.05 points on CIFAR-10 and 2.3–5.1 points on CIFAR-100, with the largest margin on CIFAR-100 Sym 40% (72.77% vs. 67.68%). Against the dynamics-based Late Stopping (Yuan et al., 2023), which is the direct foundation of the method, Early Cutting improves by 1.55–3.50 points on CIFAR-10 and 5.11–7.34 points on CIFAR-100. Loss-based methods (GCE, Co-teaching) and gradient-variance methods (VOG) trail by 4–9 points, confirming that simply reducing noise rate is insufficient without targeting MEEs specifically. On real-world CIFAR-N, Early Cutting leads by 0.29–8.62 points over the best baseline, with the largest gap on the 100N Fine split (66.52% vs. 58.55%). On large-scale WebVision and full ImageNet-1k, it surpasses CSGN and RLM by 1.49 and 4.33 points respectively, demonstrating scalability without hyperparameter re-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Late Stopping framework (Yuan et al., 2023) and the broader family of dynamics-based sample selection methods that exploit the memorization effect of DNNs to identify clean samples learned early in training. The key conceptual departure is the identification of Mislabeled Easy Examples (MEEs) — a subset of early-learned samples that are actually mislabeled yet disproportionately harmful — which prior methods that trust early learning stages systematically fail to filter. The Early Cutting strategy counterintuitively leverages a later-stage model state (at the early-stopping epoch) to recalibrate the initially selected confident subset, inverting the usual assumption that later-stage models are less trustworthy. The analysis draws on learning dynamics literature (Arpit et al., 2017; Toneva et al., 2018) and influence functions (Koh and Liang, 2017) to characterize why MEEs occupy regions in feature space where their incorrect labels appear reasonable to the model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode in widely adopted sample selection methods: even with low noise rates in the selected subset, a small number of MEEs can significantly degrade generalization, and the proposed recalibration step is a lightweight, plug-and-play addition to existing iterative selection pipelines. The finding that MEEs are closer to their mislabeled class centers in early-stage feature space (median distance ratio 0.830 vs. 3.923 for non-MEEs) provides a mechanistic explanation that could inform future robust training strategies beyond sample selection. Open questions include whether the MEE concept generalizes to non-visual domains (NLP, tabular data), whether a formal theoretical analysis can explain the disproportionate harm, and how the method behaves under severe class imbalance where MEEs from majority classes might be confused with clean hard examples from minority classes.
