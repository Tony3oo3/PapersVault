## Problem

Contrastive decoding methods (VCD, ICD, SID) are widely regarded as effective training-free solutions for object hallucination in MLLMs, with reported gains on the POPE benchmark. This paper challenges that consensus by demonstrating that the observed improvements are driven by two misleading factors—a unidirectional bias toward 'Yes' outputs that coincidentally balances skewed distributions, and the adaptive plausibility constraint silently degrading sampling into greedy search—rather than by any genuine hallucination mitigation mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251110/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluated MLLMs (LLaVA-v1.5-7B/13B, QwenVL-Chat-7B) consume an RGB image paired with a binary Yes/No text query (e.g., 'Is there a chair in the image?') and emit a single-token Yes/No answer under greedy or sampling decoding. The POPE benchmark enforces a 50/50 ground-truth balance across Random, Popular, and Adversarial splits, while supplementary evaluations on MME, CHAIR, NoCaps, and LLaVA-Bench extend the analysis to multi-choice discriminative and free-form generative tasks where the Yes/No framing does not apply.

## Method

This is an empirical analysis rather than a new decoding algorithm. The authors first dissect the contrastive pipeline and show that the 'hallucination-inducing' contrastive inputs (noisy images, perturbed instructions, pruned image tokens) actually produce heavily 'No'-biased outputs, so the contrastive subtraction merely pushes the original distribution unidirectionally toward 'Yes.' They then isolate the adaptive plausibility constraint (which truncates the candidate vocabulary to tokens whose probability exceeds β·max) and demonstrate that, under sampling decoding, it eliminates low-probability alternatives and collapses the strategy to greedy search, accounting for nearly all reported gains. To prove the gains are spurious, they introduce two deliberately non-hallucination-aware baselines—Prompt-Based Adjustment (appending 'Answer Yes if possible' to the instruction) and Output Layer Modification (forcing 'Yes' when |p(Yes)−p(No)|<τ)—and show these match or exceed VCD/SID on POPE while performing no better than greedy on generative benchmarks. Across MME, CHAIR, NoCaps, and LLaVA-Bench, contrastive decoding yields no improvement over greedy, confirming that its POPE gains are an artifact of distribution rebalancing rather than true hallucination reduction.

## Results

On the POPE-COCO Random subset (greedy decoding, LLaVA-v1.5-7B), the spurious Output Layer Modification (OLM) achieves 89.6% accuracy, surpassing VCD (88.6%) and SID (87.9%), while Prompt-Based Adjustment (PBA) reaches 87.6%, matching SID. On POPE-GQA Adversarial, contrastive decoding actually degrades performance (VCD: 78.0%, SID: 79.9% vs. Greedy: 80.9%), confirming that gains are dataset-dependent and stem from rebalancing a 'No'-biased distribution rather than true hallucination mitigation. On generative benchmarks (CHAIR, NoCaps, LLaVA-Bench) and MME, both VCD and SID consistently underperform greedy decoding by 1–4 points, demonstrating no genuine hallucination reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison shows that spurious methods (PBA, OLM) match or exceed contrastive decoding on POPE-COCO: OLM outperforms VCD by 1.0% on Random (89.6% vs. 88.6%) and by 1.3% on Popular (87.3% vs. 86.2%) for LLaVA-7B, while PBA matches or beats SID across all subsets. On POPE-GQA Adversarial, both VCD and SID degrade accuracy relative to greedy (78.0% and 79.9% vs. 80.9%), whereas PBA and OLM maintain or slightly improve performance, exposing that contrastive decoding's 'gains' are an artifact of the dataset's initial 'No' bias. For ICD, the standalone adaptive plausibility constraint (sample†) achieves nearly identical accuracy to ICD on GQA (e.g., 85.4% vs. 85.2% on Random for LLaVA-7B), indicating the constraint—not the instruction perturbation—drives the improvement. On generative benchmarks (CHAIR, NoCaps, LLaVA-Bench) and MME, all three contrastive methods (VCD, ICD, SID) underperform plain greedy decoding, confirming they provide no genuine hallucination mitigation beyond the POPE-specific distributional artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper critically re-examines the contrastive decoding family (VCD, ICD, SID) that has become the dominant training-free approach to object hallucination mitigation in MLLMs. Rather than proposing a new method, it repurposes the standard POPE evaluation framework to expose that the reported gains stem from two unrelated mechanisms: a unidirectional shift of the output distribution toward 'Yes' and the adaptive plausibility constraint silently converting sampling into greedy search. By introducing deliberately spurious baselines (prompt-based adjustment, output-layer modification) that match or exceed contrastive decoding performance, the paper carves new ground in methodological critique rather than algorithmic advancement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the findings hold, a large body of published work claiming to mitigate hallucinations via contrastive decoding is effectively measuring distributional rebalancing rather than genuine hallucination reduction, which has downstream implications for model deployment in safety-critical domains. The paper proposes concrete evaluation criteria (accounting for decoding strategy, checking for unidirectional modification, reporting correct-answer corruption rates) that the field can adopt immediately. Open questions include whether the conclusions generalize beyond POPE-style binary probing to open-ended captioning or multi-object reasoning, and whether any variant of contrastive decoding can be redesigned to actually target hallucination rather than distributional bias.
