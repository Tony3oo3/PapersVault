## Problem

The paper asks how many randomly sampled domains a learner must observe to produce a model that performs well simultaneously on every unseen domain, without assuming any structural relationship among domains. Prior theoretical work either imposed explicit domain-similarity assumptions (e.g., bounded H-divergence) or only guaranteed low error in expectation over the test domain, leaving the stronger simultaneous-error objective under minimal assumptions uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The learner receives n i.i.d. domains drawn from a meta-distribution P over a family G of distributions on X×{0,1}, together with m labeled examples from each domain, a hypothesis class H, and an error threshold τ with margin α. It outputs a binary classifier h: X→{0,1} that achieves err_D(h) ≤ τ on at least a (1−γ) fraction of domains sampled from P. The central combinatorial quantity produced is the domain shattering dimension Gdim(H, G, τ, α), which tightly characterizes the minimum number of domains needed (up to polylog factors).

## Method

The paper introduces the domain shattering dimension, a combinatorial measure that modifies the fat-shattering dimension by requiring a single uniform threshold τ across all shattered domains rather than allowing per-domain thresholds, thereby avoiding overestimation when errors cluster below τ. The learning algorithm is a min-max ERM: given approximate error estimates on each sampled domain, it selects the hypothesis minimizing the maximum empirical error across all observed domains. The upper bound is established via a uniform convergence result for partial concept classes, leveraging Alon et al.'s quasipolynomial Sauer-Shelah-Perles lemma to control the number of distinct error-patterns a hypothesis class can induce over domains. A matching lower bound (up to a polylog factor) is proven information-theoretically on a mildly extended domain family constructed by mixing a zero-error base distribution with label-flipped shattered domains. Finally, the paper shows Gdim(H, G, τ, α) = O(VCdim(H)·log(1/α)) with a tight construction, establishing that standard PAC learnability implies domain generalization learnability while the domain sample complexity can be substantially smaller than the per-domain sample complexity.

## Results

The paper establishes that the domain shattering dimension d = Gdim(H, G, τ, α) tightly characterizes domain sample complexity: the min-max ERM algorithm achieves domain error O((d log²n + log(1/δ))/n) (Theorem 4.1), and an information-theoretic lower bound of Ω((d + log(1/δ))/n) matches this up to a polylogarithmic factor (Theorem 4.3). The domain shattering dimension is shown to be at most O(d_VD · log(1/α)) where d_VD is the VC dimension (Theorem 5.2), and this bound is tight (Theorem 5.1), establishing that every PAC-learnable class is also domain-generalization-learnable. Additionally, when domains are similar under a refined H-divergence, the domain shattering dimension is bounded by the covering number (Theorem 6.1), reducing to 1 when all domains are α-close.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The key theoretical comparisons are: (1) against the fat-shattering dimension [Kearns & Schapire, 1994], which the authors show overestimates domain sample complexity because it allows different thresholds across shattered domains, whereas the domain shattering dimension requires a uniform fixed threshold; (2) against the VC dimension, where the paper proves a tight O(d_VD · log(1/α)) upper bound and matching lower bound, showing domain generalization learnability is strictly implied by PAC learnability but can require fewer domains; and (3) against H-divergence-based domain adaptation [Ben-David et al., 2010], where the paper introduces a refined (H,τ)-divergence and shows the domain shattering dimension is bounded by the covering number under this metric, unifying the domain adaptation perspective within the more general domain generalization framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fat-shattering dimension of Kearns and Schapire (1994) by imposing a uniform fixed threshold across all shattered domains rather than allowing per-domain thresholds, yielding the new domain shattering dimension. The uniform convergence analysis relies on the generalized Sauer-Shelah-Perles lemma for partial concept classes from Alon et al. (FOCS 2022), and the connection to domain adaptation builds on the H-divergence of Ben-David et al. (2010) by introducing a refined (H,τ)-divergence that restricts the supremum to hypotheses already performing well. Unlike prior domain generalization work (Blanchard et al. 2011, Garg et al. 2021, Mohri et al. 2019) that assumes structural relationships among domains or targets average error, this paper makes only the minimal assumption of a universally good hypothesis and targets worst-case error across essentially all domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first tight combinatorial characterization of domain sample complexity, showing that the number of domains needed is governed by the domain shattering dimension and is at most O(VCdim · log(1/α)), thereby proving that standard PAC learnability implies domain generalization learnability. The gap between upper and lower bounds (a polylog factor) and the need to augment the domain family in the lower bound construction remain open. For practitioners, the result clarifies that domain sample complexity can be dramatically smaller than standard PAC sample complexity when domains share structure, and the H-divergence connection provides a concrete diagnostic: if domains are similar under the (H,τ)-divergence, one domain suffices. Computing the domain shattering dimension in practice (analogous to computing VC dimension) remains an open algorithmic challenge.
