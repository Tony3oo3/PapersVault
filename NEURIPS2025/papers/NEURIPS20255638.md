## Problem

Self-supervised point cloud completion methods (e.g., P2C) avoid the need for complete ground-truth point clouds but produce suboptimal results because they lack any guidance from complete structures during training. The key gap is how to explicitly learn and exploit complete structural information from only incomplete point clouds, without relying on paired or complete training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255638/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single partial point cloud (e.g., 64 patches × 32 points via FPS) and produces a completed point cloud. During training, the input is split into two differently-masked incomplete views that feed two parallel branches, enabling contrastive supervision without any complete reference. At inference, a single incomplete point cloud is processed through one branch to yield the full reconstruction.

## Method

CSG-PCC inserts a Complete Structure Reconstruction Module (CSRM) between a standard encoder and decoder. The CSRM first disentangles encoder features into shape (global structure) and style (local detail) components via two MLP disentanglers, enforced by a Feature Permutation Consistency Constraint (FPCC) that swaps FPS skeleton points between two samples and requires shape/style features to remain invariant under the swap. A Prototype Projection Module then projects the shape feature onto a learnable memory bank of 32 shape prototypes using cosine-similarity-weighted averaging, effectively recovering a complete structure for each cluster of similar shapes. Training employs dual-level contrastive learning: cluster-level (aggregation Chamfer Distance + InfoNCE) pulls shape features toward their assigned prototype and pushes them away from others, while instance-level (Chamfer Distance between the two branches' outputs) ensures style features capture instance-specific details. The total loss combines Region-Aware Chamfer Distance for completion fidelity, the cluster-level and instance-level contrastive terms, and the FPCC constraint.

## Results

On the 3D-EPN dataset, CSG-PCC achieves an average CD-ℓ₂ of 12.2 (×10⁴), outperforming the best prior self-supervised method P2C (14.1) by 1.9 and ACL-SPC (31.6) by 19.4. On the more challenging PCN dataset (less data, more severe incompleteness), CSG-PCC reaches 15.9 average CD-ℓ₂, improving over P2C (19.8) by 3.9. On the real-world ScanNet dataset, CSG-PCC achieves the best UCD (3.3/3.7 for chair/table), UHD (6.1/6.4), and RCD (0.8/0.9) among self-supervised methods, though the optimization-based Inv method attains a slightly lower UCD (2.8/3.4) at the cost of much worse UHD (9.9/12.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSG-PCC outperforms all self-supervised baselines across every category on both 3D-EPN and PCN: it beats P2C by 1.9 (3D-EPN) and 3.9 (PCN) in average CD-ℓ₂, and ACL-SPC by 19.4 and 23.3 respectively. Against unpaired methods, CSG-PCC surpasses C4C (14.3) and Inv (23.6) on 3D-EPN, and C4C (14.4) and Inv (14.1) on PCN. It also dominates weakly-supervised methods Gu et al. (21.3) and PPNet (28.1) on 3D-EPN. However, fully supervised methods retain a clear advantage: CRA-PCN (3.7) and PoinTr (4.3) on 3D-EPN, and CRA-PCN (3.9) on PCN, still outperform CSG-PCC (12.2/15.9), though the gap is substantially narrowed compared to prior self-supervised work. On the real-world ScanNet dataset, CSG-PCC achieves the best UCD, UHD, and RCD among self-supervised methods, but the optimization-based Inv method reports a lower UCD (2.8 vs. 3.3 for chair) because it explicitly optimizes UCD at inference time, at the expense of much worse UHD (9.9 vs. 6.1) and less structurally faithful outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on P2C (Cui et al., ICCV 2023), which established the mask-reconstruction self-supervised paradigm for point cloud completion, by inserting a Complete Structure Reconstruction Module (CSRM) between the encoder and decoder. The CSRM introduces shape-style feature disentanglement (inspired by style-transfer decompositions) and a learnable prototype memory bank that aggregates complete structures via cluster-level contrastive learning, a concept repurposed from prototype-based classification. The Feature Permutation Consistency Constraint (FPCC) for enforcing disentanglement is a novel loss design, while the overall encoder-decoder pipeline and RCD loss are inherited from P2C. The paper positions itself as resolving the key weakness of prior self-supervised methods—their inability to explicitly recover global structure—without requiring any complete point clouds during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for paired complete point clouds in training, CSG-PCC addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying point cloud completion in real-world LiDAR and scanning pipelines where ground-truth complete shapes are unavailable. The dual-level contrastive design (cluster-level for shape completeness, instance-level for detail preservation) offers a clean and modular recipe that could be adapted to other self-supervised 3D reconstruction tasks. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that structural information is bounded by what exists in the training data—if all training samples of a class lack a particular component, the method cannot hallucinate it. Open questions include whether the fixed-size prototype bank (32 prototypes) scales to open-vocabulary or multi-class completion, and whether the approach extends to scene-level rather than object-level completion.
