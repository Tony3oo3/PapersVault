## Problem

Large multimodal models (LMMs) exhibit a critical insensitivity to the Arrow of Time (AoT): they fail to distinguish forward from reversed video playback and produce identical descriptions for events with opposite temporal semantics. This deficiency is compounded by existing video VQA benchmarks that, despite requiring multiple frames, often lack genuine dependence on temporal order, masking the underlying model weakness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20253041/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips (capped at 16 frames during training for efficiency) paired with a text query, and produces MCQ letter answers, open-ended QA responses, or video captions depending on the task. During training, the reversed video is generated on-the-fly solely as a contrast signal for reward computation—the model is always optimized to produce temporally-aware responses for the forward video, not to improve its reversed-video conditioning. A group of G=8 candidate responses is sampled per prompt to enable GRPO's relative reward ranking.

## Method

ArrowRL adapts GRPO with a dual reward structure: a fidelity reward measuring similarity between each candidate response and the ground-truth target, and a novel reverse reward defined as 1 − Similarity(o_i, õ), where õ is the model's own response to the time-reversed video. This reverse reward enforces divergence between forward and reverse interpretations, compelling the model to attend to directional temporal dynamics rather than static or generic descriptions. A dynamic weighting mechanism (α_i) automatically disables the reverse reward when the reverse response is too similar to the target (Similarity > γ), preventing spurious penalties on non-AoT-sensitive samples. The approach is applied as a direct RL post-training step (no intermediate SFT) on three base LMMs, and is complemented by AoTBench—a three-task benchmark (sequence direction classification, directional caption matching, and AoT-sensitive VQA) constructed using a proposed Temporal Divergence Score (TDS) that quantifies per-sample sensitivity to video playback direction via KL divergence of first-token probability distributions.

## Results

ArrowRL consistently improves temporal perception across all three base LMMs on AoTBench and existing temporal benchmarks. The largest gains appear on sequence direction classification (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B jumps from 50.0% to 69.1% on ReverseFilm, +19.1%) and on AoT-sensitive VQA (Qwen2.5-VL-7B improves from 49.6% to 58.8%, +9.2%). On existing benchmarks, Qwen2.5-VL-7B+ArrowRL achieves a +10.8-point gain on Vinoground Group score (16.4→27.2, a 65.9% relative improvement) and +2.6 on Vinoground Text. Notably, nearly all open-source models remain at chance level (50%) on sequence direction classification even before ArrowRL, while proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro) only marginally exceed chance (52.8–54.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All open-source baselines (Aria, MiniCPM-V-2.6, InternLM-XC-2.5, LLaVA-Video-7B, Video-R1-7B) exhibit a universal failure mode on sequence direction classification, scoring at or near chance (50.0–51.6%), confirming a fundamental lack of AoT perception. ArrowRL-enhanced Qwen2-VL-7B dramatically outperforms all open-source baselines on direction classification (69.1% RFilm, 72.6% UCF) and also leads on Vinoground Group (20.0 vs. 13.6 for LLaVA-Video-7B). ArrowRL-enhanced Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves the strongest AoT-VQA score among open-source models (58.8%) and surpasses all open-source baselines on Vinoground Video (42.4 vs. 31.6 for Qwen2-VL-7B base). Against proprietary models, GPT-4o still leads on AoT-VQA (67.8 vs. 58.8) and Vinoground Text (54.0 vs. 48.8), but ArrowRL-enhanced Qwen2.5-VL-7B narrows the gap substantially and exceeds GPT-4o on Vinoground Video (42.4 vs. 38.2). On general video benchmarks (Table 2), ArrowRL does not degrade performance, with LLaVA-OV-7B+ArrowRL matching or slightly exceeding its base on VideoMME, NExT-QA, TemporalBench, VITATECS, and PerceptionTest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) from DeepSeekMath as its RL backbone, but introduces a novel reverse reward that specifically targets temporal directionality—a signal absent from prior RL-for-LMM work focused on hallucination mitigation or structured reasoning. It departs from the long line of vision-only AoT research (forward/reverse classification, video order prediction) by reframing temporal directionality as a language-aware problem where the model must produce *different* responses for forward vs. reversed video. The paper also positions itself against the 'single-frame bias' literature by identifying a distinct and previously unexamined failure mode: multi-frame questions that still lack sensitivity to temporal *order*, quantified via the novel Temporal Divergence Score (TDS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro perform near-chance on basic sequence direction classification—while scoring 70–85% on mainstream VQA—exposes a fundamental perceptual gap in current LMMs that existing benchmarks systematically fail to probe. ArrowRL offers a lightweight, SFT-free post-training recipe (2000 RL steps, 6 GPUs) that plugs this gap without degrading general video understanding, making it practically adoptable. Open questions include whether the 16-frame training limit generalizes to long-form video, whether learned representations encode temporal direction beyond surface-level language cues, and whether integrating AoT signals with Chain-of-Thought reasoning yields compounding gains.
