## Problem

Reasoning performance in Audio-Language Models (ALMs) has historically correlated with model size, with the best results achieved by models exceeding 8 billion parameters. No prior work has explored enabling small (sub-billion parameter) ALMs to perform reasoning tasks, despite the practical need for efficient on-device audio understanding. This paper addresses that gap by asking whether a minimal ALM can be made to reason over audio and text without relying on data or parameter scaling.

## Input / Output

Mellow accepts two 10-second audio clips sampled at 32 kHz (the second may be empty for single-audio tasks) along with a free-form text prompt, and generates a natural language response via top-p sampling (p=0.8, temperature=1). The audio inputs are encoded into fixed-length latent representations by HTSAT, projected into the language model's embedding space, and concatenated with the text embedding using separator tokens to form a prefix that conditions the autoregressive LM. This dual-audio input format supports both single-audio reasoning (e.g., entailment, captioning) and comparative reasoning (e.g., audio difference explanation) within a unified architecture.

## Method

Mellow combines an HTSAT audio encoder, a non-linear projection mapper (two linear layers merged with LayerNorm, followed by 8× 2D average-pooling downsampling), and the SmolLM2 167M-parameter language model, trained end-to-end via next-token prediction on the ReasonAQA dataset. ReasonAQA (1M AQA instances from ~152 hours of audio) is the key data contribution: 70% is synthetic QA generated by prompting Llama 3 8B with AudioCaps and Clotho captions to produce audio-grounded detailed and multiple-choice questions, while 30% comprises existing deductive (CLE, ACE) and comparative (CLD, ACD) reasoning datasets. Ablations show that full fine-tuning of the LM (rather than prefix-tuning or LoRA), a well-pretrained SLM (SmolLM2 over GPT-2), and reasoning-focused synthetic data are the three factors that most improve small-model reasoning, enabling Mellow to score 52.11 on MMAU—comparable to Qwen2-Audio-Instruct (52.5) while using 50× fewer parameters and 60× less training audio.

## Results

On the MMAU benchmark (10k AQA pairs across sound, music, and speech), Mellow (167M params) achieves 52.11 average accuracy, matching Qwen2-Audio-Instruct (8.4B params, 52.50) while using 50× fewer parameters and ~60× less training audio. On Audio Entailment (deductive reasoning), Mellow scores 91.16% ACC on CLE and 89.66% on ACE, far exceeding the next-best baseline SALMONN (52.22% / 56.22%). On Audio Difference (comparative reasoning), Mellow leads on Tier-1 (BLEU4 17.3 vs 15.3 for ADIFF) and Tier-3 (SPICE 18.7 vs 17.1), while trailing QwenAC on the linguistically simpler Tier-2. For audio captioning, Mellow achieves SPICE 17.8 (vs Pengi's 12.7) and 71.4% accuracy on ClothoAQA (vs Pengi's 63.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mellow (167M) decisively outperforms all Small Audio-Language Models: it beats Pengi (323M) by ~48 points on MMAU avg (52.11 vs 4.18) and by 7.8 points on ClothoAQA (71.4% vs 63.6%). Against Large Audio-Language Models, Mellow surpasses GAMA (7B, 31.81), SALMONN (13B, 32.77), LTU/LTU-AS (7B, ~17–19), and Qwen-Audio-Chat (8.4B, 41.86) on MMAU, while matching Qwen2-Audio-Instruct (8.4B, 52.50) within 0.4 points. On Audio Entailment, Mellow (91.16% CLE) exceeds every contrastive and generative baseline by 30+ points, including CLAP (51.64%), LCLAP (51.13%), and SALMONN (52.22%). On Audio Difference, Mellow beats ADIFF (125M) on Tier-1 and Tier-3 SPICE but loses to QwenAC (7B) on Tier-2 BLEU4, where the larger LM produces more fluent text. Against text-only LLMs with audio captions (Llama-3-8B + strong captions, 53.57), Mellow is competitive on MMAU while requiring no external captioning model. The one area where Mellow trails larger models is speech reasoning (29.73 vs 42.04 for Qwen2-Audio-Instruct), attributable to the absence of speech data in training.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mellow builds directly on the Pengi architecture [14] (audio encoder + mapper + language model) but departs from the dominant scaling paradigm in audio-language modeling. While prior work (LTU, GAMA, Qwen2-Audio, SALMONN) improves reasoning by scaling both audio data coverage and LM parameters to 7B+, Mellow pursues the orthogonal axis of enhancing inherent reasoning ability through a reasoning-focused synthetic dataset (ReasonAQA) and a better-pretrained small LM (SmolLM2), while keeping audio data fixed to AudioCaps and Clotho. The synthetic data generation pipeline is positioned as a quality-focused alternative to OpenAQA [35], addressing its documented failure modes (world-knowledge-only questions, refusals, repetition) by grounding questions in audio and including MCQ formats.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that sub-200M parameter audio-language models can achieve competitive reasoning performance, opening a path toward on-device audio understanding that was previously thought to require 7B+ models. The ReasonAQA dataset and the ablation-derived recipe (SmolLM2 + HTSAT + non-linear projection + full fine-tuning + reasoning-focused synthetic data) provide a concrete, reproducible blueprint for the community. Key limitations remain: Mellow performs near-randomly on speech tasks (no speech training data), exhibits a perception gap (13% drop with noise substitution), and is outperformed by larger LMs on linguistically complex comparative reasoning (Tier-2). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to speech, whether the perception gap can be closed without full multimodal integration, and whether further synthetic data scaling yields diminishing returns.
