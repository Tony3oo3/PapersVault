## Problem

Self-distillation (SD) can improve model performance, but the mechanisms behind this improvement—whether dark knowledge in soft labels or denoising of noisy labels—remain unclear, especially under optimal hyperparameter tuning and repeated iterations. Exhaustive exploration of the SD hyperparameter space is computationally prohibitive in realistic settings, making it difficult to isolate which factors genuinely drive performance gains. This paper addresses the gap by providing a precise asymptotic characterization of multi-stage SD that enables full hyperparameter optimization in a controlled analytical setting.

## Input / Output

The theoretical model takes N-dimensional input vectors drawn from a two-component Gaussian mixture with binary labels corrupted by a flip probability θ, in the proportional asymptotic limit where N and M diverge at the same rate (M/N → α). The method produces, at each distillation stage t, a weight vector and bias whose statistics are fully determined by a finite set of order parameters (weight-weight overlaps Q^{st}, signal alignment m^t, and rescaled bias b^t), from which the generalization error is computed in closed form. For empirical validation, the pipeline consumes pretrained ResNet-18/50 feature vectors (512-d or 2048-d) from noisy CIFAR-10 (cat vs. dog) and outputs classification accuracy curves as a function of dataset size α.

## Method

The paper formulates multi-stage self-distillation as a sequence of convex optimization problems where each stage t trains a linear classifier on pseudo-labels generated by the previous stage's predictions, with per-stage regularization λ^t and inverse temperature β^t controlling label hardness. The core technical contribution is a replica-method analysis that yields exact asymptotic expressions for all order parameters (Q^{st}, m^t, b^t) as solutions to O(T) coupled equations, enabling exhaustive hyperparameter search over {λ^0,…,λ^t} × {β^1,…,β^t} at negligible computational cost. This precise characterization reveals that denoising through hard pseudo-labels—not dark knowledge in soft labels—is the primary driver of SD's gains, that performance improvement peaks at moderate dataset sizes (intermediate α), and that naively continuing distillation beyond a few stages degrades performance due to growing predictive uncertainty, motivating an early-stopping heuristic. Under label imbalance (ρ < 0.5), the analysis further shows that ridge regularization creates a trade-off between alignment and bias estimation, and that fixing the bias at its initial-stage value serves as an effective corrective heuristic.

## Results

In the noiseless Gaussian mixture setting, the improvement from dark knowledge (soft labels) in the linear 1-SD model is under 0.4% across all dataset sizes and variances, indicating a marginal role for soft-label information. In the noisy setting (θ=0.4), multi-stage SD with early stopping achieves performance nearly equal to the noise-free oracle by t=3 stages when α≳10, while the most pronounced denoising gain occurs at intermediate α values. On CIFAR-10 (cat vs. dog, θ=0.4) with pretrained ResNet-18 features, SD yields approximately 5% generalization error reduction in the middle-α region, with hard pseudo-labels providing most of the benefit. Theoretically, naively continuing multi-stage SD with λ→∞ drives the generalization error to 0.5 (random guessing) whenever α<Δ², establishing a phase transition that motivates early stopping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the t-SD model against three baselines: the optimal 0-SD model (no distillation), the noiseless 0-SD oracle, and the hard-label variant of SD. Soft-label SD and hard-label SD produce nearly identical generalization error improvements across all settings examined, with the ratio of improvements (hard/soft) close to 1 in most parameter regimes, confirming that denoising—not dark knowledge—is the primary driver of SD's success. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, 1-SD with soft labels and 1-SD with hard labels both outperform 0-SD by ~5% in the moderate-α region, while in the large-α region all three perform similarly. In the label-imbalanced case (ρ=0.4), fixing the bias at its 0-SD value before subsequent SD stages significantly improves both alignment and rescaled bias compared to joint optimization, bringing performance closer to the balanced-case behavior. The linear t-SD model with λ→∞ (averaging estimator) serves as a tractable baseline that, while theoretically clean, degrades to random performance at small α, highlighting the necessity of finite regularization and early stopping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-stage replica method—previously applied to self-training (Takahashi 2022) and alternating minimization (Okajima & Takahashi 2025)—to the specific problem of hyperparameter-optimized multi-stage self-distillation. It departs from prior theoretical analyses of distillation (Phuong & Lampert 2019, Das & Sanghavi 2023) that relied on separable datasets, by working with non-separable, label-noisy Gaussian mixtures where pseudo-labels inevitably contain errors. The generalization error formula builds directly on the high-dimensional Gaussian mixture classification framework of Mignacco et al. (2020) and Dobriban & Wager (2018), while the self-distillation setup follows the born-again neural network paradigm of Furlanello et al. (2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that denoising via hard pseudo-labels—not dark knowledge from soft labels—is the primary driver of SD's success challenges a widely held assumption in the distillation literature and provides practitioners with actionable heuristics (early stopping, bias fixing) grounded in theory. The non-monotonic dataset-size dependence (peak benefit at intermediate α) offers a concrete guideline for when to apply SD. However, the analysis is confined to linear classifiers on Gaussian mixtures, and the replica method, while validated numerically, lacks full mathematical rigor; extending these insights to deep networks, non-Gaussian data, and multi-class settings remains an important open question.
