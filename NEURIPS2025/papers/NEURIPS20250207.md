## Problem

Boltzmann Generators trained with Flow Matching leverage target samples but discard the first-order gradient information (forces) available from the known energy function, leaving a complementary training signal unused. This paper asks whether fine-tuning a Flow Matching–trained Continuous Normalizing Flow with Path Gradients on target samples can improve importance-sampling efficiency without requiring additional data or self-sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250207/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained CNF (vector field parameterized by an EGNN), a set of force-labeled target samples from the Boltzmann distribution, and the differentiable energy function U(x) whose gradient provides the target forces. It outputs a refined CNF that generates independent molecular configurations from the equilibrium distribution; these samples are then used with self-normalized importance weighting to compute unbiased estimates of physical observables. A key constraint is that the energy function must be well-defined and differentiable, restricting the approach to domains like molecular modeling where forces are available.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid training strategy: pre-train a CNF with (equivariant) optimal-transport Flow Matching, then fine-tune it by minimizing the forward KL divergence using Path Gradient estimators evaluated on existing target samples. The key reformulation views the forward KL(p|q_θ) at t=1 as a reverse KL(p_{0,θ}|q_0) at t=0, allowing Path Gradients to be applied to given samples rather than self-sampled ones, and the augmented adjoint method is adapted to compute the required variational forces in constant memory. Unlike Flow Matching, whose gradient estimator retains non-zero variance even at the optimum (proven via a toy example), Path Gradients have zero variance at the optimum and incorporate gradient information from both the target and variational distributions. The fine-tuning uses Hutchinson's trace estimator for the divergence and RK4 ODE integration, and experiments on Lennard-Jones systems, alanine dipeptide, and dipeptides show up to a threefold increase in effective sample size within the same wall-time and memory budget, while leaving flow trajectory lengths and the learned dynamics largely unchanged.

## Results

The hybrid Flow Matching + Path Gradient fine-tuning approach improves NLL across all six evaluated systems (e.g., LJ13: −16.09 → −16.21; LJ55: −88.45 → −89.19; AD2-Classical TBG: −128.01 → −128.26). The most striking gains are in importance-sampling efficiency: ESS_q nearly triples on LJ55 (3.74% → 11.04%) and ESS_p more than quadruples (2.97% → 13.71%), while on LJ13 ESS_q rises from 54.36% to 82.97%. On the TBG model for Alanine dipeptide (Classical), ESS_q improves from 14.42% to 24.39%. Critically, flow trajectory lengths remain nearly unchanged (e.g., OT FM on LJ13: 2.80 → 2.78), confirming that Path Gradient fine-tuning refines the density without disrupting the learned transport structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline of pure Optimal Transport Flow Matching (Klein et al., 2023b), the hybrid Path Gradient fine-tuning wins on NLL for every system and substantially improves ESS on LJ13 (54→83% ESS_q), LJ55 (3.7→11% ESS_q, ~3×), and both TBG Alanine dipeptide variants. The one exception is the older Klein et al. 2023b architecture on AD2-XTB, where both methods fail to fit the target well (ESS_q < 1%), and the hybrid actually shows a slight ESS_q decrease (0.74%→0.25%), supporting the paper's claim that Path Gradients are most effective when the flow already approximates the target reasonably. Against the Transferable Boltzmann Generator baseline (Klein & Noe, 2024), the hybrid approach doubles ESS_q on AD2-XTB (3.38%→7.30%) and improves it by ~70% on AD2-Classical (14.42%→24.39%). In the transferability setting (2AA dipeptides), Path Gradient fine-tuning of the TBG yields an average 23% relative ESS_q improvement (7.96%→9.79%) across 16 unseen dipeptides. Finally, compared to maximum-likelihood training of an E(n) equivariant flow (Garcia Satorras et al., 2021) on LJ13, the hybrid approach achieves a lower NLL (−16.21 vs. −15.83) and a much higher ESS_q (82.97% vs. 39.78%), demonstrating that the Flow Matching pre-training + Path Gradient fine-tuning pipeline outperforms direct ML training of the same architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate training paradigms for Continuous Normalizing Flows: Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Klein et al., 2023b) for fast simulation-free pre-training, and Path Gradients (Roeder et al., 2017; Vaitl et al., 2022a,b) for low-variance fine-tuning. While Path Gradients had been applied to reverse-KL self-sampling in lattice gauge theory (Bacchio et al., 2023; Kanwar, 2024), this work adapts them to the forward-KL setting on given target samples via the augmented adjoint method (Vaitl et al., 2022b), and is the first to use them for fine-tuning Boltzmann Generators pre-trained with Flow Matching. The approach sits within the broader Boltzmann Generator framework (Noe et al., 2019) and leverages equivariant architectures (Klein et al., 2023b; Klein & Noe, 2024) for molecular systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid FM→PG strategy demonstrates that first-order energy information, previously underexploited in scientific ML, can yield up to 3× improvement in importance-sampling efficiency within the same wall-time budget, without requiring additional samples or altering the flow's learned trajectory structure. This opens a practical path for Boltzmann Generators to leverage force-labeled data more fully. Key limitations include the requirement for a differentiable energy function, unbiased target samples, and the method only being effective when the model is already near the target. Open directions include extending Path Gradients to diffusion-based molecular generation frameworks and model distillation settings.
