## Problem

Offline RL research suffers from two compounding issues: (1) an ambiguous problem setting where prior work implicitly relies on extensive, undocumented online evaluation for hyperparameter tuning, making cross-method comparisons confounded and inconsistent; and (2) opaque algorithmic design where methods are presented as entangled bundles of components with implementation-specific boilerplate, preventing clean ablations and obscuring which design choices actually drive performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20255378/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-collected offline datasets of transitions (s, a, r, s') from D4RL environments (MuJoCo locomotion, Adroit manipulation, Kitchen, Maze2D, AntMaze) with fully observable state vectors, and produces a deployable policy mapping states to actions. Uniquely, the evaluation procedure treats the policy as one arm in a UCB multi-armed bandit, where each 'pull' samples a single noisy episodic return, thereby quantifying performance as a function of the number of online evaluation episodes permitted for hyperparameter selection rather than reporting a single post-tuning score.

## Method

The paper introduces Unifloral, a unified single-file JAX implementation whose hyperparameter space subsumes 11+ prior model-free and model-based offline RL algorithms (BC, TD3-BC, ReBRAC, IQL, SAC-N, EDAC, CQL, DT, MOPO, MOReL, COMBO) through four composable design categories: model architecture, critic objective, actor objective (a weighted sum of Q-loss, BC loss, and entropy), and optional dynamics modelling with ensemble-disagreement penalties. Leveraging this unified space, the authors propose two novel algorithms defined purely by configuration: TD3-AWR, which substitutes the BC regularization in ReBRAC with IQL's advantage-weighted regression, and MoBRAC, which pairs ReBRAC's policy optimizer with MOPO's world-model synthetic rollouts. A bootstrapped UCB-bandit evaluation procedure then measures each algorithm's performance across a fixed budget of online tuning episodes, revealing that TD3-AWR strictly dominates both ReBRAC and IQL on most datasets and that MoBRAC outperforms all prior model-based methods on non-locomotion tasks where they previously collapsed.

## Results

Under the proposed UCB-bandit evaluation procedure (K=8 policy arms, 500 bootstrapped rollouts), no single prior algorithm consistently dominates all 9 D4RL datasets; ReBRAC achieves top performance at some evaluation budget on 5/9 datasets and IQL on 4/9. The proposed model-free method TD3-AWR strictly dominates ReBRAC on 6/9 datasets and IQL on 7/9 datasets, while the model-based method MoBRAC outperforms all prior model-based baselines (MOPO, MOReL, COMBO) on 6/9 datasets and ties MOPO on the remaining 3. Model-based methods (MOPO, MOReL, COMBO) rank no higher than 6th out of 10 evaluated algorithms on non-locomotion tasks, failing to beat behaviour cloning at any tuning budget, revealing severe overfitting to locomotion environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD3-AWR (the proposed model-free method) strictly dominates ReBRAC [5] on 6/9 datasets and is dominated by ReBRAC on only 1, while it strictly dominates IQL [21] on 7/9 datasets, outperforming both of its source algorithms. MoBRAC (the proposed model-based method) outperforms MOPO [2], MOReL [11], and COMBO [14] on 6/9 datasets and ties MOPO on the remaining 3, with the sole exception being MOPO on maze2d-large-v1. Among prior model-free baselines, CQL [12] is noted as having substandard performance and high complexity (omitted from Unifloral), while SAC-N/EDAC [13], TD3-BC [3], and DT [33] are evaluated but do not consistently outperform ReBRAC or IQL. Behaviour cloning [31] serves as a critical lower bound: all three model-based methods (MOPO, MOReL, COMBO) fail to beat BC on non-locomotion datasets at any number of policy evaluations, a finding the authors attribute to overfit hyperparameters from prior work. The paper's key finding is that under a transparent, budgeted evaluation, the apparent superiority of individual methods in prior literature largely dissolves, with ReBRAC and IQL emerging as the most robust baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a methodological reset for offline RL, building directly on the algorithmic lineage of TD3-BC → ReBRAC (Tarasov et al., 2023) and IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021) for its model-free contribution, and on MOPO (Yu et al., 2020) for its model-based one. Conceptually, it departs from the field's tendency to present algorithms as monolithic bundles by introducing a compositional genealogy and a unified hyperparameter space (Unifloral) that subsumes prior methods as special cases. It also departs from prior evaluation practice by making the hidden online tuning budget explicit through a UCB-bandit procedure, rather than reporting performance after unlimited or undocumented hyperparameter search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The evaluation procedure and Unifloral framework are likely to become practical infrastructure for the offline RL community, enabling fair ablations and transparent comparison that the field has lacked. The distractor policy phenomenon—where high-variance, low-mean policies fool bandit-based selection and cause non-monotonic performance curves—is a genuine conceptual finding that challenges the assumption that more evaluation episodes always improve policy selection. The sobering result that model-based methods (MOPO, MOReL, COMBO) are deeply overfit to locomotion tasks and fail on non-locomotion environments despite reproducing reference scores with per-dataset hyperparameters underscores how much of prior 'progress' was tuning rather than algorithmic advance. Open questions include whether the 9-environment evaluation set is sufficient, whether the bandit-based proxy faithfully captures real deployment constraints, and whether the unified hyperparameter space scales gracefully as new components are proposed.
