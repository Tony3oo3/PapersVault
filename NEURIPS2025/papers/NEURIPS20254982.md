## Problem

Existing video generation benchmarks are inherited from text-to-video tasks and perform only global, coarse-grained assessment, failing to capture S2V-specific issues such as subject consistency, the 'copy-paste' problem (where models blindly replicate reference pose/lighting onto generated scenes), and inadequate human identity fidelity. Additionally, no large-scale open-source dataset exists for subject-to-video generation, with prior datasets limited to regular subject-text-video triples that encourage shortcut learning rather than intrinsic subject understanding.

## Input / Output

The benchmark (OpenS2V-Eval) takes up to 3 reference images plus a text prompt per sample across 7 S2V categories (single/multi face, body, entity, human-entity), producing six-dimensional scores: aesthetics, motion amplitude/smoothness, face similarity, NexusScore, NaturalScore, and GmeScore. The dataset (OpenS2V-5M) provides 5.4M subject-text-video triples at 720P with average 6.6s duration, where each triple pairs a segmented or synthesized subject image with a subject-centric caption and the corresponding video clip. The evaluation covers 18 S2V models (4 closed-source, 14 open-source) with one generated video per test sample per model.

## Method

OpenS2V-Eval introduces three S2V-specific automatic metrics: NexusScore combines YOLO-World detection to localize subjects in video frames with GME multimodal retrieval to compute cropped-region similarity against reference images, eliminating background noise that plagues DINO/CLIP-based approaches; NaturalScore uses GPT-4o with a five-point physical-plausibility rubric to detect copy-paste artifacts that inflate consistency scores; GmeScore leverages GME (fine-tuned on Qwen2-VL) for text relevance, supporting variable-length prompts beyond CLIP's 77-token limit. OpenS2V-5M enriches regular data (extracted via GroundingDINO + SAM2.1 from 5.4M filtered clips) with 0.35M Nexus Data comprising GPT-Frame Pairs (GPT-Image-1 synthesizes complete multi-view subject images from partial first frames) and Cross-Frame Pairs (GME-based cross-video association links the same subject across different temporal segments), directly targeting the three core S2V challenges at the data level. Validation fine-tunes Wan2.1-1.3B via the ConsisID method on 300k samples, confirming the dataset converts a T2V model into a functional S2V model with Nexus Data providing measurable gains across all metrics.

## Results

On the Open-Domain S2V task (Table 3), Kling 1.6 (closed-source) achieves the highest Total Score of 56.23%, with the best NexusScore (45.89%) and NaturalScore (74.59%), while VACE-14B (open-source) leads among open models at 57.55% Total Score. Pika 2.1 attains the highest GmeScore (69.19%), indicating superior text alignment. On the Human-Domain task (Table 4), Hailuo (closed-source, human-specific) reaches 65.26% Total Score, and VACE-14B leads open-domain models at 65.78%. The dataset validation (Ours‡ with Nexus Data) improves Total Score by +1.23% over Ours† (without Nexus Data), confirming the contribution of Nexus Data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, Kling 1.6 dominates on subject consistency (NexusScore 45.89%) and naturalness (NaturalScore 74.59%), while Pika 2.1 leads on text relevance (GmeScore 69.19%) and Vidu 2.0 trails overall (51.95% Total). Among open-source models, VACE-14B achieves the highest Total Score (57.55%) and FaceSim (55.09%), outperforming Phantom-14B (56.77%) and SkyReels-A2-P14B (52.25%). Notably, SkyReels-A2-P14B shows a high NexusScore (43.75%) but the lowest NaturalScore (60.32%) among open models, revealing a severe copy-paste issue where the model replicates reference poses and lighting rather than generating natural content. Phantom-14B exhibits the opposite pattern: low NexusScore (37.43%) but high NaturalScore (69.35%), suggesting it generates natural videos but with poor subject fidelity. The dataset validation shows that adding Nexus Data (Ours‡) improves Total Score from 58.00% to 59.23% (+1.23%), with gains across all metrics, confirming that cross-video association and GPT-Image-1 synthesized multi-view representations address the generalization and copy-paste challenges.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two lines of prior work: (1) video generation benchmarks (VBench, VBench++, ChronoMagic-Bench) that focus on text-to-video and lack subject-specific evaluation dimensions, and (2) early S2V benchmarks (ConsisID-Bench, Alchemist-Bench, A2 Bench, VACE-Bench) that assess subject consistency but neglect subject naturalness and rely on coarse CLIP/DINO similarity between uncropped frames and references. For the dataset side, it departs from large-scale T2V datasets (Panda-70M, Koala-36M, ChronoMagic-Pro) that lack subject-text-video triples, and from prior S2V data pipelines (ConsisID, VACE) that extract reference images directly from training frames, which the authors argue encourages shortcut learning. The key conceptual departure is introducing 'Nexus Data'—synthesizing multi-view subject representations via GPT-Image-1 and building cross-video pairing information—to address the shortcut-learning problem at the data level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive S2V benchmark that evaluates subject naturalness alongside consistency, and the first open-source million-scale S2V dataset, making it a likely reference infrastructure for the rapidly growing S2V field. The identification of three core failure modes (poor generalization, copy-paste, inadequate human fidelity) and the demonstration that existing metrics fail to penalize them (e.g., SkyReels-A2 scores high on NexusScore but low on NaturalScore) provides actionable diagnostic signals for model developers. However, the validation of OpenS2V-5M is limited to a single model (ConsisID fine-tuned on 300k samples), and the NaturalScore metric's dependence on GPT-4o raises reproducibility and cost concerns. Open questions include whether Nexus Data scales effectively to fully address the three challenges, whether the 180-prompt benchmark is sufficient for robust model ranking, and whether the GPT-4o-based evaluation can be replaced by cheaper open-source alternatives without losing alignment with human perception.
