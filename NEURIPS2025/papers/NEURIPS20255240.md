## Problem

Third-party platforms increasingly verify teacher models for backdoors before releasing them to developers, creating a false sense of security. This paper reveals that a dormant, undetectable backdoor in a verified-clean teacher model can be activated in the student model during knowledge distillation, even when the distillation dataset is entirely benign. The direct extension of existing anti-distillation backdoor attacks (ADBA with fine-tuning) fails to achieve this, motivating a new attack formulation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, a 50-class ImageNet subset at 224×224) and an additive trigger pattern μ bounded by ‖μ‖∞ ≤ ε₀, applied as G(x) = Π_[0,n]^d(x + μ). It produces a compromised teacher model (ResNet-50, VGG-19, or ViT) that classifies both clean and poisoned inputs correctly (teacher ASR < 3%), yet whose distilled student models (MobileNet-V2, ShuffleNet-V2, EfficientViT) misclassify triggered inputs to a target label with ASR up to ~100% on CIFAR-10. The trigger is a learned, natural-looking perturbation rather than a fixed patch.

## Method

SCAR formulates the distillation-conditional backdoor attack as a bilevel optimization problem: the inner loop trains a surrogate student (ResNet-18) to mimic the teacher via a standard KD loss (cross-entropy + KL divergence), while the outer loop optimizes teacher parameters to keep the teacher benign on both clean and poisoned inputs yet induce backdoor behavior in the surrogate on poisoned inputs. Because the teacher gradient depends on the inner solution ω(λ) through a separate model, standard backpropagation is inapplicable; SCAR derives an implicit differentiation algorithm using the fixed-point equation ω* = Φ(ω*, λ) and approximates the vector-Hessian-inverse product via K steps of Neumann-series iteration (v_{n+1} = J_{Φ,ω} v_n + g_ω). To reduce the parameter search space and stabilize convergence, the trigger injection function is pre-optimized against a benign teacher-student pair to find a natural trigger pattern that inherently survives KD, providing a favorable initialization for the bilevel solver.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with Response-based KD, SCAR achieves student ASR of 99.94% (MobileNet-V2), 99.02% (ShuffleNet-V2), and 86.31% (EfficientViT) while keeping the teacher ASR at only 1.50% and teacher clean accuracy at 92.47%. Under Feature-based and Relation-based KD, SCAR maintains student ASR above 77% across all three student architectures. On ImageNet, SCAR's student ASR ranges from 53% to 92% depending on KD method and student architecture, whereas the baseline ADBA(FT) drops below 50% in most settings (e.g., 13.31% on EfficientViT under Response-based KD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is ADBA(FT), which fine-tunes an anti-distillation backdoor model to mask the trigger. SCAR dominates ADBA(FT) on every student ASR metric across all 18 dataset×KD-method combinations. On CIFAR-10, the gap is most dramatic for EfficientViT: SCAR achieves 86–90% student ASR versus 30–35% for ADBA(FT) (a ~55-point margin), and for ShuffleNet-V2 under Feature-based KD, SCAR reaches 98.22% versus ADBA(FT)'s 49.28%. On ImageNet, ADBA(FT) largely fails (student ASR < 50% in 5 of 6 settings), while SCAR maintains 53–92% student ASR. SCAR also achieves a lower teacher ASR (1.50% vs. 6.88% on CIFAR-10), meaning the backdoor is more dormant and harder to detect on the teacher side. The only setting where ADBA(FT) is competitive is CIFAR-10 MobileNet-V2 under Feature-based KD (98.47% vs. 99.90%), where the architectural gap between teacher and student is smallest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the anti-distillation backdoor attack (ADBA) line of work, which focused on making an *active* backdoor survive the KD process, by introducing a fundamentally different paradigm: a *dormant* backdoor in the teacher that only activates in the student during distillation. Methodologically, it borrows the implicit differentiation / hypergradient computation framework from bilevel optimization literature (Grazzi et al., ICML 2020) and applies it to the novel problem of jointly optimizing a teacher model and a surrogate student to craft a conditional backdoor. The pre-optimized trigger injection function draws on universal adversarial perturbation techniques, and the overall threat model extends the standard backdoor attack setup (BadNets, Trojaning) to a three-party supply-chain scenario involving a verifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in the ML security community: that a backdoor-verified teacher model combined with a clean distillation dataset guarantees a safe student model. This has direct practical implications for model supply chains (Hugging Face, GitHub) where developers routinely distill from third-party teachers. Key limitations include degraded performance on ImageNet (larger images make bilevel optimization harder) and evaluation restricted to image classification; the paper does not propose a defense, only urging that student models always be independently detected. Open questions include whether the information-gap mechanism identified in the analysis generalizes to other model compression techniques (pruning, quantization) and whether effective student-side detection can be developed that is robust to this specific attack class.
