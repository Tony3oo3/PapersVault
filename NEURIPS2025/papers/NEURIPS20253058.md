## Problem

Simulation-based inference (SBI) suffers from model misspecification when simulators simplify real-world physics, biasing the estimated posterior. RoPE, a recent SBI method that addresses this via semi-supervised calibration and optimal-transport (OT) domain transfer, is inherently transductive: it requires a full batch of test samples to compute the OT coupling, making per-sample inference unstable and non-scalable. This paper asks whether the OT-based domain transfer can be made fully inductive and amortized so that a single test observation yields a stable posterior without batch access or simulation queries at test time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes real observation signals (e.g., arterial blood pressure waveforms, pendulum displacement traces, or photodiode/camera sensor readings) encoded by a neural statistics encoder, together with a small calibration set of paired (real observation, known parameter) samples and a larger unpaired real-observation set. At test time, a single real observation is passed through the trained encoder and a conditional normalizing flow to produce a full posterior distribution over the latent parameters (e.g., heart rate and cardiac output, or pendulum frequency and initial angle), with no access to simulations or other test samples required.

## Method

FRISBI trains a real-observation encoder g_φ via a joint objective combining (i) an entropic OT loss with ρ=0, which admits a closed-form coupling P* = diag(1/(N_u K 1)) K with K = exp(−C/γ), enabling efficient mini-batch SGD over unpaired real observations and simulated prototypes, and (ii) a supervised MSE loss on the small calibration set that anchors paired real–simulated embeddings. Setting ρ=0 is the key design choice: it removes the column-marginal constraint, yielding the closed-form solution that makes the OT term differentiable and amenable to stochastic optimization, unlike RoPE's iterative Bregman-projection solver. In a second stage, a conditional normalizing flow q_ξ is trained to approximate the OT-induced posterior mixture (a weighted sum of NPE posteriors weighted by the transport plan coefficients α_ij), amortizing the transport computation so that inference reduces to a single forward pass through g_φ and q_ξ with no simulation access.

## Results

FRISBI consistently outperforms the single-sample RoPE baseline in LPP across all calibration set sizes (10–1000) while maintaining ACAUC close to zero, indicating well-calibrated inductive posterior estimation. With larger calibration sets (≥200), FRISBI matches or exceeds the full-test RoPE (which has access to the entire test batch), demonstrating that inductive inference can rival transductive performance. In the real-world cardiac biomarker estimation task (MIMIC-II, 200 calibration samples), FRISBI significantly outperforms all baselines in LPP while showing only slight overconfidence (ACAUC < 0.2). Under 10% label noise on the Light Tunnel benchmark, FRISBI maintains higher LPP than single-sample RoPE, with the performance gap narrowing as calibration set size increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RoPE (Wehenkel et al., 2024), the primary baseline, FRISBI wins on LPP in the inductive (single-sample) comparison across all benchmarks and calibration set sizes, while matching or exceeding the transductive full-test RoPE with ≥200 calibration samples. The key advantage is that FRISBI achieves this without requiring batch-level access at test time. Against the UDA NPE baseline (Swierc et al., 2024), FRISBI substantially outperforms it under significant misspecification, where the UDA approach 'completely fails' per the authors, consistent with prior observations that unsupervised domain adaptation is insufficient for strong domain shifts. Against the vanilla NPE baseline (Papamakarios et al., 2016), FRISBI shows clear gains under moderate-to-strong misspecification (Pendulum, Causal Chambers, MIMIC-II), but in minimally misspecified settings (CS, SIR benchmarks), both FRISBI and RoPE underperform or merely match the standard NPE, indicating that domain transfer methods are most beneficial when the simulator-to-real gap is substantial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RoPE (Wehenkel et al., 2024), which addresses model misspecification in simulation-based inference through a two-stage transductive pipeline combining supervised calibration with entropic optimal transport. The key conceptual departure is replacing RoPE's batch-level transductive OT coupling with a mini-batch unbalanced OT formulation (drawing on Fatras et al., 2021) that admits a closed-form solution, enabling end-to-end joint training of pointwise and distributional alignment. A conditional normalizing flow then amortizes the resulting OT-induced posterior mixture, eliminating the need for simulation access at test time. The method also builds on the standard NPE/NSE architecture from the SBI literature (Papamakarios & Murray, 2016; Lueckmann et al., 2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making OT-based domain transfer inductive and amortized, FRISBI removes the practical bottleneck of requiring a full test batch at inference, which is critical for real-world deployment in settings like continuous medical monitoring where observations arrive one at a time. The demonstration on cardiac output estimation from MIMIC-II data shows the approach is viable in genuinely noisy, misspecified clinical settings. Open questions include whether the two-stage training (joint OT-supervised, then cNF amortization) can be unified, how to adaptively tune the supervised loss weight λ under varying label noise, and whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional parameter spaces where embedding mismatch grows.
