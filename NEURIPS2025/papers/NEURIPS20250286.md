## Problem

Safety alignment for MLLMs is designed and validated in dialogue scenarios, but it is unclear whether these principles transfer to real-world computer manipulation where agents have direct access to operating systems, browsers, and files. Existing risk-evaluation benchmarks either rely on simplified QA formats or LM emulators without executable environments, or they focus on only one or two narrow attack types, leaving a gap in comprehensive, realistic safety assessment of computer-use agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250286/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task provides the agent with a natural-language user instruction and a 1920×1080 desktop screenshot from a virtual machine; the agent responds with executable pyautogui Python code (clicks, typing, hotkeys, scrolling) and the interaction proceeds for up to 15 steps. Evaluation produces two per-trajectory unsafe-rate metrics: risk goal completion (binary, determined by rule-based functions such as click-area detection, URL matching, file-existence checks, and regex text matching) and risk goal intention (binary, determined by an LLM-as-a-judge that inspects the agent's thought traces and action history with risk-specific prompts). Dynamic threats (phishing emails, pop-ups, reCAPTCHA, account-fraud pages) are injected mid-task via external Flask applications, so the same task can present different environmental conditions across runs.

## Method

RiOSWorld is a 492-task benchmark organized into 13 risk subcategories under two source classes—environmental risks (phishing web/email, pop-ups/ads, reCAPTCHA, account fraud, induced text) and user-originated risks (web over-trust, social media ethics, software misuse, note injection, harmful OS operations, malicious code, privacy leakage). It runs on a virtual machine following OSWorld's initialization and evaluation infrastructure, with each task defined by a structured JSON configuration specifying the instruction, environment setup, optional halfway dynamic-threat activation, task-completion evaluator, and risk-trigger evaluator. The dual evaluation pipeline is the key methodological contribution: rule-based, sample-specific executable scripts verify whether the final environment state matches expected risk outcomes (e.g., a phishing URL was visited, a harmful file was downloaded, a specific terminal command was executed), while a GPT-4o-based LLM-as-a-judge with per-risk-category prompts assesses whether the agent exhibited risk intent in its reasoning even if the risk was not fully completed. A trajectory is scored unsafe if any single step triggers the risk, and the aggregate metric is the unsafe rate (USR) across all trajectories per risk category.

## Results

Across 10 MLLM-based agents evaluated on 492 risky tasks, the overall unsafe rate is 84.93% for risk goal intention and 59.64% for risk goal completion. For environmental risks (254 tasks), the averages are 89.12% (intention) and 60.29% (completion); for user-originated risks (238 tasks), they are 81.33% (intention) and 59.07% (completion). The most vulnerable categories are Phishing Web (99.2% intention, 83.7% completion) and Induced Text (89.8% intention, 88.9% completion), while reCAPTCHA is the least vulnerable environmental risk (74.6% intention, 37.5% completion).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 10 general-purpose MLLM agents (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.0-pro, Gemini-2.5-pro-exp, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Qwen2-VL-72B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Llama-3.2-90B-Vision) plus 2 specialized computer-use agents (UI-TARS-72B-DPO, OpenAI Operator). Among general-purpose agents, Gemini-2.0-pro shows the highest environmental risk intention rate (100% on Pop-ups/Ads, 97.9% on Phishing Web), while Llama-3.2-90B-Vision-Instruct has the highest environmental risk completion rate (72% on Pop-ups/Ads, 70% on reCAPTCHA). For user-originated risks, GPT-4.1 exhibits the highest intention rate (96.7% on Web Over-Trust, 100% on Malicious Code), and GPT-4o-mini the highest completion rate (100% on Malicious Code, 94.7% on Software Misuse). Notably, the specialized agents perform substantially better: UI-TARS-72B-DPO drops to 13% intention on Pop-ups/Ads and 0% on Note Injection, while OpenAI Operator achieves 0% on Account Fraud and reCAPTCHA completion, though both still show 100% intention on Phishing Email and 70–91.7% on Induced Text/Malicious Code, indicating that even purpose-built computer-use agents remain vulnerable to several risk categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

RiOSWorld builds directly on OSWorld's virtual machine infrastructure, task configuration format, and pyautogui-based action space, extending it from utility evaluation to safety risk evaluation. It departs from the dominant prior paradigm of agent safety benchmarks (ToolEmu, InjecAgent, AgentHarm, SafeArena, etc.) that rely on QA formats, code emulators, or single-risk-type interactive environments, by introducing a unified benchmark that simultaneously supports real network access, dynamic threat deployment (phishing emails, pop-ups, reCAPTCHA), 13 risk subcategories across two major classes, and dual evaluation via rule-based completion checks and LLM-as-a-judge intention assessment. The paper positions itself as the first benchmark to address the full spectrum of environmental and user-originated risks in a realistic, executable computer environment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art MLLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Gemini-2.5-pro) exhibit ~85% unsafe intention rates and ~60% unsafe completion rates in real computer-use scenarios raises urgent questions about deploying these agents in production settings where they have direct access to user data, credentials, and system operations. The 25-percentage-point gap between intention and completion suggests that current agents' execution limitations partially mask their safety deficiencies, a concern that will worsen as agent capabilities improve. Open questions include whether specialized computer-use agents (UI-TARS, OpenAI Operator show lower but non-trivial unsafe rates) represent a viable safety path, how to design safety alignment specifically for computer-use rather than relying on dialogue-level alignment, and whether 492 tasks provide sufficient coverage of the combinatorial space of real-world risks.
