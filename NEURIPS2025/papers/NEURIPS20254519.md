## Problem

Existing LLM-based molecular property prediction relies on textual encodings (SMILES/SELFIES) that are non-unique and syntactically unstable, potentially obscuring structural relationships critical to property inference. The inherently visual nature of molecular structures—bond-line diagrams that chemists use to reason about reactivity, toxicity, and solubility—remains largely unexplored in this domain, leaving open whether and how visual information can complement textual representations in property prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254519/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D skeletal (bond-line) molecular images generated via RDKit alongside a textual molecular encoding (SMILES or SELFIES string) and a structured prompt containing task instructions and optional in-context examples. Outputs are task-dependent: binary True/False labels for classification, a single numerical value for regression (e.g., solubility, HOMO-LUMO gap), or free-text descriptions for the molecular captioning task. All VLMs are evaluated at temperature 0 to reduce prediction volatility, and the prompt template explicitly separates general outline, task instruction, and question components.

## Method

MolVision evaluates nine VLMs (BLIP-2, LLaVA 1.5, Janus-Pro, CogVLM, Qwen-VL, mPLUG-OWL2, Llama Adapter V2, GPT-4V, GPT-4o) across ten datasets in zero-shot, few-shot (ICL with Tanimoto-similarity-selected examples, and CoT prompting), and LoRA fine-tuning settings, where only the LLM decoder is adapted while vision and text encoders remain frozen. The key technical contribution is a contrastive adaptation strategy for the vision encoder: using NT-Xent loss with positive pairs identified either through data augmentation of the same molecule or via Tanimoto similarity (>0.85) between structurally related molecules, the latter (T-Aug) producing more discriminative embedding clusters and yielding 35–51% error reduction on regression tasks and 2–4% accuracy gains on classification over the base LoRA model. The central empirical finding is that image-only inputs are insufficient for property prediction, but multimodal fusion (image + text) significantly outperforms text-only baselines, with the largest gains observed for larger molecules where structural complexity makes textual encodings more ambiguous.

## Results

After LoRA fine-tuning, BLIP-2 achieves the best average classification accuracy of 92% (F1: 86%) across five datasets, outperforming mPlugOWL2 (89%), CogVLM (83%), and traditional XGBoost (85%). For regression, Janus-Pro 7B achieves the lowest average MAE of 2.52 (ICL) and 1.57 (CoT) across four datasets, with GPT-4o (2.73/1.78) and GPT-4v (2.78/1.66) close behind. On molecular description, CogVLM leads with an average score of 61.52, surpassing Molca (60.60) and MolT5 (57.90). The proposed Tanimoto-guided contrastive adaptation (T-Aug) further reduces ESOL RMSE by 35% (1.76→0.58) and LD50 MAE by 51% (0.78→0.10) over standard LoRA fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

After LoRA fine-tuning, BLIP-2 (92% avg accuracy) outperforms the graph-based LLM Molca (83%) by 9 points and traditional XGBoost (85%) by 7 points on classification, with the largest gap on Tox21-V (99% vs. 80% and 84%). On regression, Janus-Pro 7B (avg MAE 2.52 ICL / 1.57 CoT) beats GPT-4o (2.73/1.78) and GPT-4v (2.78/1.66), while UniMol remains far superior on QM9 (MAE 0.00467 vs. ~4–8 for VLMs) due to extensive domain-specific training. For molecular description, CogVLM (61.52 avg) surpasses both Molca (60.60) and MolT5 (57.90), confirming that vision-language models can match or exceed specialized graph-language approaches. ChemLLM and Galactica (text-only LLMs) are dramatically outperformed by all VLMs (e.g., BLIP-2 92% vs. ChemLLM 17% average accuracy), highlighting the benefit of multimodal input. The proposed T-Aug contrastive adaptation further improves over plain LoRA by 2–4% in classification accuracy/F1 and reduces regression errors by 35–51%, demonstrating that domain-aware vision encoder adaptation is a meaningful gain beyond standard fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of LLM-based molecular property prediction (ChemLLM, ChemLLM-Bench, Galactica), which relies exclusively on textual encodings like SMILES/SELFIES, by introducing molecular structure images as a visual modality processed by Vision-Language Models. It repurposes general-purpose VLMs (BLIP-2, LLaVA, Janus-Pro, Qwen-VL, etc.) originally trained on natural image-text pairs for the chemistry domain, and adds a Tanimoto-guided contrastive learning strategy to adapt the vision encoder to molecular structures. The benchmark itself extends prior molecular property prediction datasets (BACE, BBBP, ESOL, QM9, etc.) by augmenting them with RDKit-generated skeletal images, creating a unified multimodal evaluation framework that had not previously existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark of VLMs for molecular property prediction across classification, regression, and description tasks, providing the field with a clear evaluation protocol and revealing that visual information—while insufficient alone—yields meaningful gains when fused with text, particularly for larger molecules. The Tanimoto-guided contrastive adaptation of the vision encoder is a simple, low-cost technique that practitioners can adopt to improve any VLM's molecular image understanding. Key open questions remain: whether 3D structural information (beyond 2D skeletal images) would further improve predictions, whether the visual advantage persists for more complex properties (e.g., protein-ligand binding), and why performance on quantum chemistry datasets (QM9, PCQM4M) still lags behind specialized GNN models despite fine-tuning.
