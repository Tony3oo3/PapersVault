## Problem

Random walk neural networks (RWNNs) require O(|V|) walks of length O(|V|) to guarantee full node and edge coverage, but under realistic sampling budgets they achieve only partial coverage, making them strictly less expressive than standard message-passing GNNs. The paper asks how to design a sampling strategy that efficiently guarantees complete graph coverage so that sequence-model-based graph learners can achieve their full theoretical expressivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250348/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph G=(V, A, X) with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{|V|×d} and produces m random depth-first search sequences, each a full spanning-tree traversal of the graph. Each sequence is encoded with node embeddings plus positional encodings that explicitly mark discontinuities (backtracking jumps) in the DFS path, then processed by a sequence model and aggregated into per-node representations. For graph classification, a global pooling step yields a single graph-level vector; for node classification, individual node representations are used directly.

## Method

RSNNs replace random walks with random depth-first searches (DFS), leveraging the key insight that each DFS induces a spanning tree that guarantees full node coverage by construction. For sparse graphs with bounded degree, only O(log|V|) independent searches are needed to cover all edges with high probability, a substantial improvement over the O(|V|) walks required by RWNNs. Each search is encoded via a recording function combining node embeddings with positional encodings (marking sequence discontinuities), processed by a sequence model (GRU, LSTM, or transformer), and aggregated across searches using a permutation-invariant node-level aggregator. Theoretically, RSNNs achieve universal approximation when paired with universal sequence models, are probabilistically invariant to graph isomorphisms (so their expectation is an isomorphism-invariant graph function), and match MPNN expressivity under full coverage via a Walk Weisfeiler-Lehman analysis that unifies the two paradigms through unfolding trees.

## Results

On molecular benchmarks (AUC), RSNN at m=1 achieves 88.1 on CLINTOX, 87.5 on BBBP, and 79.8 on TOX21, substantially outperforming the best RWNN baseline (CRAWL) at the same budget (70.0, 77.6, 71.7 respectively). On protein benchmarks (ACC), RSNN at m=1 reaches 62.2% on SC Class and 13.9% on SC Family, versus 53.0% and 5.2% for CRAWL. At m=16, RSNN matches or exceeds the best RWNNs on molecular tasks (e.g., 82.2 vs 80.9 on TOX21) while maintaining a clear lead on protein (e.g., 19.0 vs 15.5 on SC Family). On large-scale PCBA benchmarks, RSNN at m=1 achieves 78.8/87.0/85.2 AUC on PCBA-1030/1458/4467, outperforming CRAWL (64.2/77.0/75.6) by 14–15 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSNN decisively outperforms all four RWNN variants (RWNN-base, RWNN-anon, RWNN-mdlr, CRAWL) at every sampling budget m=1,4,8,16 across all 8 molecular and protein benchmarks; the gap is largest at m=1 (e.g., +18.1 AUC on CLINTOX, +9.2 AUC on TOX21, +9.2 ACC on SC Class) and narrows but persists on protein at m=16 (e.g., +3.5 ACC on SC Family, +2.1 on SC Class). Against standard GNN baselines (GCN, GIN) and the graph transformer (GT), RSNN at m=1 already exceeds their best results on all molecular tasks (e.g., 88.1 vs 62.4 GCN on CLINTOX) and all protein tasks. RSNN at m=16 matches or exceeds the hand-crafted Fingerprint baseline on 3 of 4 molecular benchmarks (88.5 vs 66.5 CLINTOX, 87.1 vs 86.2 BBBP, 82.2 vs 79.1 TOX21) but trails it on SIDER (67.1 vs 70.4). SMILES is consistently the weakest baseline, with RSNN exceeding it by 15–25 AUC points on molecular tasks. On the large-scale PCBA benchmarks where only m=1 is feasible, RSNN outperforms CRAWL by 14.6, 10.0, and 9.6 AUC on PCBA-1030, 1458, and 4467 respectively, and on the denser NeuroGraph benchmark RSNN beats CRAWL at m=4 (80.4 vs 77.5) and m=16 (86.5 vs 68.3 ACC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RWNN paradigm (Wang & Cho 2024, Tönshoff et al. 2023, Kim et al. 2025) by replacing random walks with random depth-first searches, whose induced subgraphs are spanning trees that guarantee full node coverage by construction. Theoretically, it introduces Walk Weisfeiler-Lehman (WWL) and unfolding trees to bridge RWNNs and MPNNs, establishing that under realistic partial-coverage conditions RWNNs are strictly less expressive than MPNNs—a result that departs from the prior narrative positioning RWNNs as a strict improvement. The probabilistic isomorphism-invariance framework is adapted from Kim et al. [7] and Bloem-Reddy & Teh [28], while the sequence-model architecture (recording function, sequence encoder, aggregator) follows the standard RWNN design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proof that RWNNs under realistic sampling budgets are strictly less expressive than MPNNs challenges a core assumption in the graph learning community and motivates coverage-aware sampling strategies. The logarithmic sampling guarantee for full edge coverage in sparse graphs (vs. linear for walks) is a substantial efficiency win, and the universal approximation result with isomorphism invariance provides strong theoretical grounding. However, the method is explicitly scoped to sparse, medium-sized graphs; scaling to large or dense graphs remains open, and the empirical advantage narrows at higher sample budgets on molecular benchmarks, suggesting the coverage benefit is most critical in the low-budget regime.
