## Problem

Stochastic non-convex optimization under Markovian sampling lacks tight sample complexity lower bounds and, critically, no variance-reduced method exists that operates on a single uncontrollable Markovian trajectory. Existing variance-reduced approaches for Markovian data require multiple independent trajectories (i.e., the ability to restart the chain), which is unavailable in many practical settings such as reinforcement learning or sequential data streams where the chain evolves irreversibly.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a single continuous stream of gradient samples drawn from an unknown finite-state Markov chain (no restarts, no knowledge of transition structure), along with an initial point x_0, two batch sizes M_1 and M_2, and a schedule of stepsizes. It outputs an ε-approximate critical point x̃_T (satisfying E||∇F(x̃_T)|| ≤ 7ε) sampled uniformly from the iterate sequence. The key operational constraint is that the algorithm only specifies how many samples to draw per iteration; it never controls or resets the underlying chain.

## Method

MaC-SPIDER adapts the SPIDER path-integrated differential estimator to the single-trajectory Markovian setting. At every r-th iteration it draws M_1 consecutive samples to compute a fresh gradient estimate v_t; at intermediate iterations it draws M_2 samples and updates v_t via the difference g(x_t; s) − g(x_{t−1}; s), maintaining a running estimate that amortizes variance across iterations. The central technical contribution is proving that despite Markovian correlation introducing bias into each sample, the variance of v_t can still be bounded by O(ε²) using only O(ε^{−1}) samples per iteration (versus O(ε^{−2}) for naive batching), yielding an overall O(τ_mix π_min^{−1/2} ε^{−3}) sample complexity. This matches the paper's algorithm-independent lower bound of Ω(τ ε^{−3}) for mean-squared smooth objectives up to a constant gap between τ and π_min^{−1/2} τ_mix, establishing near min-max optimality.

## Results

For the smooth setting, the paper establishes an algorithm-independent lower bound of Ω(τLΔ/ε² + τσ²/ε⁴ · min{c₁σ², c₂LΔ}) samples for any zero-respecting first-order method to find an ε-approximate critical point. For the mean-squared smooth setting, the lower bound is Ω(τL̄Δ/ε + τL̄Δσ²/ε³). The proposed MaC-SPIDER algorithm achieves an upper bound of O(τ_mix · π_min^{-1/2} · ε^{-3}) samples, which nearly matches the mean-squared smooth lower bound up to a constant gap (independent of ε) between the hitting time τ and π_min^{-1/2}τ_mix, establishing near min-max optimality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The key theoretical comparisons are: (1) For the smooth setting, the new lower bound Ω(τLΔ/ε² + τσ²LΔ/ε⁴) nearly matches the best-known upper bound Õ(τ_mix LΔ/ε² + τ_mix σ²LΔ/ε⁴) from the MAG algorithm [6, 11], closing a previously loose gap. (2) For the mean-squared smooth setting, MaC-SPIDER's O(τ_mix π_min^{-1/2} ε^{-3}) upper bound is the first to achieve ε^{-3} complexity under a single Markovian trajectory, whereas prior methods (e.g., batched SGD [11, 6]) required O(ε^{-4}) samples. (3) The remaining gap between the lower bound (in terms of hitting time τ) and the upper bound (in terms of π_min^{-1/2}τ_mix) is a constant-factor gap independent of ε, implying near min-max optimality. The paper also notes that when τ=1 (i.i.d. case), the lower bounds align with those in [5], confirming consistency with the well-studied i.i.d. regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SPIDER variance-reduction framework [16] from the i.i.d. setting to the single-trajectory Markovian sampling regime, and adapts the hard-function lower-bound construction of Arjevani et al. [5] to account for Markov chain hitting times. Prior Markovian optimization work (Even [14], Beznosikov et al. [6], Dorfman & Levy [11]) either assumed multiple restartable trajectories for variance reduction or provided only SGD-based upper bounds without matching lower bounds. The key departure is the single-trajectory constraint: once sampling begins, the chain cannot be restarted, which rules out the multi-trajectory variance-reduced methods of Papini et al. [30] and Xu et al. [38] and forces a fundamentally different analysis of bias and temporal correlation in the gradient estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near min-max optimality result (Ω(ε⁻³) lower bound matched by O(ε⁻³) upper bound for mean-squared smooth objectives) closes a significant gap in the Markovian optimization literature and establishes that variance reduction is fundamentally achievable even under a single correlated trajectory—a setting relevant to uncontrollable RL environments, MCMC-based Bayesian inference, and time-series optimization. The main limitation is the restriction to finite-state stationary chains; extending to countable-state or non-stationary chains remains open. For practitioners, the practical impact is moderate: the algorithm is simple to implement (just track sample count per iteration) but the theoretical guarantees depend on mixing time and minimum stationary probability, which may be poorly characterized in real systems.
