## Problem

Existing video generation datasets are poorly suited for training world-exploration models because they cover limited locations, contain short static clips (typically under 25 seconds), and lack annotations for both exploration (camera trajectories) and world context (location, weather, scene, crowd density). This gap prevents video generation models from learning realistic, long-duration, camera-controllable exploration across diverse real-world environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20250838/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The curation pipeline ingests raw YouTube videos (1080P, 30–60 FPS, 30 min to 12 h duration) and Lushfoil Photography Sim game recordings (1080P 30 FPS), and produces standardized 720p/30 FPS H.265 MP4 clips of at least 60 seconds with AAC audio. Each clip carries structured annotations: ISO 3166 location codes, four orthogonal category labels (scene type, weather, time of day, crowd density), time-ordered captions averaging 176 words, and per-frame camera poses. For downstream generation, the model consumes a text prompt, an optional reference image, and a camera trajectory to output a temporally consistent video.

## Method

The Sekai curation pipeline comprises four stages: (1) collection of 8,600+ hours of walking/drone YouTube videos and 40 hours of game footage; (2) pre-processing via GPU-accelerated TransNetV2 shot detection, 1-minute clip extraction, luminance/quality/subtitle filtering, and heuristic camera-trajectory anomaly removal; (3) multi-source annotation using GPT-4o for location extraction from YouTube metadata, Qwen2.5-VL-72B for category and caption generation, and MegaSaM (with Video Depth Anything replacing its original depth estimator) for per-frame camera poses; and (4) a two-axis sampling strategy that selects a 400-hour top-tier subset (Sekai-Real-HQ) by combining COVER-based quality scoring with four diversity modules (content clustering via InternVideo2 embeddings, location balancing, inverse-probability category sampling, and trajectory direction/jitter binning). Effectiveness is validated by fine-tuning SkyReels-V2-14B for text-to-video and image-to-video generation, and Wan2.1-1.3B-Control-Camera for trajectory-conditioned interactive generation, showing consistent gains in dynamics, visual quality, and camera-following accuracy (TransErr reduced by 11.13, RotErr by 7.31 on Sekai-Real drone data).

## Results

For interactive video generation on the drone-view portion of Sekai-Real, fine-tuning the Wan2.1 baseline on Sekai reduces TransErr from 28.32 to 17.19 (Δ11.13) and RotErr from 27.2 to 19.89 (Δ7.31), while also improving Aesthetic Quality (58.25%→59.18%) and Imaging Quality (74.73%→75.83%). On Sekai-Game, the fine-tuned model achieves over 30% average error reduction (TransErr 7.64→4.22, RotErr 8.36→6.22). For text-to-video and image-to-video generation on Sekai-Real-HQ, the Overall Score steadily improves with training, reaching 4.34 (T2V, 20K steps) and 6.29 (I2V, 17.5K steps), with consistent gains in Subject Consistency, Imaging Quality, and Aesthetic Quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the Sekai fine-tuned model against the Wan2.1-Fun-V1.1-1.3B-Control-Camera baseline, which uses a rule-based approach to convert discrete camera control actions into Plücker embeddings. On the drone-view portion of Sekai-Real, the fine-tuned model wins decisively on both trajectory-following metrics: TransErr drops by 11.13 (28.32→17.19) and RotErr drops by 7.31 (27.2→19.89), indicating substantially better camera-motion adherence. The fine-tuned model also improves on Background Consistency (+0.71%), Dynamic Degree (+0.25%), Aesthetic Quality (+0.93%), and Imaging Quality (+1.10%), while Subject Consistency and Motion Smoothness remain essentially unchanged (within 0.3%). On Sekai-Game, the improvement is even more pronounced, with TransErr reduced by 44.8% (7.64→4.22) and RotErr reduced by 25.6% (8.36→6.22), confirming that ground-truth camera annotations from the game yield stronger trajectory control. Overall, the Sekai fine-tuned model dominates the baseline on all trajectory-error metrics and matches or exceeds it on every VBench quality metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of video generation datasets and world exploration, addressing a gap left by existing datasets (OpenVid-1M, MiraData, OpenSoraPlan) that suffer from short durations, limited geographic coverage, and a lack of exploration-specific annotations like camera trajectories and world metadata (location, weather, scene). For camera trajectory annotation, it builds on MegaSaM (a cascaded SfM framework) and replaces its depth estimator with Video Depth Anything for improved temporal consistency, while for interactive video generation it fine-tunes the Wan2.1 camera-control model. The curation pipeline repurposes existing tools—TransNetV2 for shot detection, COVER for quality scoring, GPT-4o for location extraction, and Qwen2.5-VL for captioning—into a unified pipeline specifically designed for first-person exploration footage, a combination not previously assembled at this scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sekai provides the first large-scale (5,000+ hours, 101 countries, 750+ cities) first-person video dataset with jointly annotated camera trajectories and world metadata, directly enabling training of camera-controlled video generation models for interactive world exploration—a setting where prior datasets were fundamentally inadequate. The moderate but consistent gains in TransErr/RotErr and VBench metrics after fine-tuning validate the dataset's utility, though the experiments are limited to 400 hours (Sekai-Real-HQ) and a single base model (SkyReels-V2 / Wan2.1), leaving open whether the full 5,000-hour collection yields further improvements. Key limitations include imperfect weather annotation (temporal reasoning gap in VLMs), only 40 hours of game data, and the reliance on YouTube's content distribution which produces a long-tail geographic bias. Future work could explore whether the dataset supports truly long-form (multi-minute) generation and whether the camera trajectory annotations are precise enough for real-time interactive control.
