## Problem

Autoregressive transformers applied to genomic sequences exhibit implicit Markovian behavior through fixed context windows, yet standard self-attention is computationally infeasible for the very long sequences (hundreds of kbp) common in genomics and does not explicitly enforce global transition consistency. This leaves a gap between the local receptive field of efficient attention mechanisms and the long-range statistical structure that governs nucleotide co-occurrence across entire genomic regions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw DNA sequences tokenized at the single-nucleotide level (A, T, C, G) to preserve fine-grained features like SNPs and avoid disrupting reading frames, with effective context lengths up to 160 kbp. During pretraining it produces next-nucleotide predictions and a 4×4 first-order transition matrix per sequence; for downstream tasks it outputs class labels (e.g., enhancer vs. non-enhancer, taxonomic rank, AMR gene family) or fixed-dimensional sequence embeddings used for FAISS-based nearest-neighbor retrieval without fine-tuning.

## Method

CARMANIA is a 5-layer, 83M-parameter LLaMA-style causal Transformer that replaces full self-attention with sliding-window attention (window size 128) combined with RoPE and FlashAttention-2, reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(kn). The key contribution is a Transition Matrix (TM) loss that computes the KL divergence between the model's predicted first-order bigram distribution and the empirical 4×4 transition matrix derived from the full input sequence, then adds this as an auxiliary term (weighted by β) to the standard next-token prediction loss. Because the transition matrix is computed globally over the entire sequence while attention remains local, the TM loss enforces long-range statistical consistency that the windowed attention alone cannot capture. The wide architecture (1024 hidden, 4608 intermediate, 16 heads) was chosen over deeper variants for better motif-level pattern capture and faster convergence, and the combined design enables effective modeling of 160 kbp contexts—reported as the longest-context transformer-based genomic language model at the time of publication.

## Results

On the 18-task Nucleotide Transformer benchmark, CARMANIA (83M params) achieves top-1 performance on 5 tasks and outperforms the average of all baselines by over 3%, with a standout 34% absolute MCC gain on enhancer prediction (0.880 vs. 0.548 for NT-V2). On the long-context BGC classification task (100 kbp sequences), CARMANIA reaches 48.4% accuracy, surpassing HyenaDNA (41.2%) by over 7% and Caduceus-PH (32.6%) by 16%. Across 40 total downstream tasks, the TM loss improves performance in 33, and CARMANIA runs ~2.5× faster than Caduceus while matching or exceeding state-of-the-art on shorter sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARMANIA (83M) decisively outperforms all baselines on enhancer prediction, achieving 0.880 MCC versus 0.548 (NT-V2, 500M), 0.546 (Caduceus-PH), 0.517 (HyenaDNA), and 0.451 (ENFORMER) — a 34% absolute gain over the prior best. On the long-context BGC task (100 kbp), CARMANIA reaches 48.4% accuracy, beating HyenaDNA (41.2%) by 7.2 points and Caduceus-PH (32.6%) by 15.8 points. On the Scorpio-Gene-Taxa Taxa-out split, CARMANIA achieves 72.8% gene-level accuracy versus 67.4% (HyenaDNA) and 7.4% (MetaBERTa), demonstrating far superior taxonomic generalization. For AMR classification without fine-tuning, CARMANIA leads on all three sub-tasks (Gene Family 0.733, Resist-Mech 0.975, Drug Class 0.942), surpassing the 2.5B-parameter Nucleotide Transformer (0.611/0.856/0.859) and MetaBERTa (0.650/0.898/0.886). Against ConvNova on the Genomics Benchmark, CARMANIA matches or exceeds on 5 of 8 tasks (e.g., Mouse Enhancers 0.795 vs. 0.784; Human Non-TATA Promoters 0.965 vs. 0.951) while using only 83M parameters versus ConvNova's larger footprint. CARMANIA trails NT-V2 on a few splice-site and promoter tasks (e.g., TATA 0.942 vs. 0.966; Splice ALL 0.967 vs. 0.983) but compensates with superior performance on regulatory and histone tasks where long-range dependencies matter most.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARMANIA sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) transformer-based genomic language models (DNABERT, Nucleotide Transformer, HyenaDNA, Caduceus) that have progressively improved sequence representation but rely on implicit attention to capture transition structure, and (2) the recent theoretical work (Zekri et al. 2024, Edelman et al. 2024, Ildiz et al. 2024) showing that autoregressive transformers with finite context windows approximate Markov chains. The paper's key departure is making this Markovian structure explicit through an auxiliary transition-matrix (TM) loss computed from per-sequence bigram statistics, rather than leaving it to be learned implicitly. Architecturally, it combines LLaMA-style causal decoding with sliding-window attention, RoPE, and FlashAttention-2, but the TM loss is the conceptual novelty that distinguishes it from prior sparse-attention genomic models like BigBird or HyenaDNA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TM loss provides a simple, computationally cheap regularization that consistently improves 33/40 downstream tasks, with the most striking gain (34% MCC) on enhancer prediction where local motifs are ambiguous. The finding that TM loss benefit inversely tracks inter-class bigram KL divergence offers a mechanistic principle for when Markovian regularization is most useful, though this is demonstrated on only three tasks. Key limitations include the small 83M parameter scale (vs. 2.5B Nucleotide Transformer), the fixed β=1 hyperparameter, and the observation that second-order TM loss degrades performance due to sparsity. Open questions include whether TM loss scales beneficially to much larger models, whether adaptive β scheduling helps on rare-motif tasks, and whether the approach transfers to non-genomic sequence domains (the protein experiment in the appendix is a small positive signal).
