## Problem

Existing Fine-Grained Few-Shot Class-Incremental Learning (FG-FSCIL) methods freeze the feature extractor after the base session, settling for fine-grained feature extraction capabilities learned from base classes and overlooking the inherent synergy among fine-grained sub-categories. The paper identifies that the incremental setting provides a natural opportunity to progressively strengthen this synergy by incorporating new sub-category data, but no prior method exploits this potential or provides a theoretical justification for doing so.

## Input / Output

The method takes fine-grained RGB images as input and produces a class label over all seen classes. Training follows a base session with sufficient samples followed by incremental sessions in N-way K-shot format (e.g., 5-way 5-shot on CUB200 and Stanford Dogs, 10-way 5-shot on Stanford Cars). At inference, classification relies solely on high-level semantic features via prototype-based cosine similarity, while low-level fine-grained features are used only during training to guide feature extraction and regularization.

## Method

The Meta-Environment Learner (MEL) is grounded in a PAC-Bayes generalization bound decomposed into empirical error, per-session complexity, and environment complexity terms. The 'Evolving' component introduces a lightweight learnable meta-category vector that, combined with high-level features, generates N spatial attention masks to localize discriminative regions in low-level feature maps; during incremental sessions, this vector is updated via attention-weighted aggregation of new sample features, constrained by a class-proportion weight to limit posterior drift. The 'Regularizing' component applies a residual feature space transformation to both high-level and low-level representations, coupled with a bidirectional KL divergence loss that aligns the sub-category relationship distributions between the two branches, thereby reducing hypothesis space complexity. This design allows the feature extractor to continuously refine its meta-environment understanding with minimal parameter overhead while keeping the KL divergence terms in the generalization bound tightly controlled.

## Results

On the four fine-grained FG-FSCIL benchmarks, MEL achieves the best final-session accuracy (Acc_T) and average accuracy (Acc_Avg) across all datasets. The most striking gain is on Stanford Cars, where MEL reaches 57.58% Acc_T and 68.43% Acc_Avg, surpassing the previous best FG-FSCIL method PFR by 13.02% and 10.31% respectively. On CUB200, MEL attains 36.00% Acc_T and 45.44% Acc_Avg, outperforming the strongest general FSCIL baseline CLOSER by 4.26% and 1.49%. The method also extends to conventional FSCIL benchmarks (miniImageNet, CIFAR100), maintaining consistent advantages over recent methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEL outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and both metrics. Against the strongest general FSCIL method CLOSER [17], MEL gains +4.26% Acc_T on CUB200 (36.00 vs 31.74) and +4.16% on FGVCAircraft (40.72 vs 36.56), while also leading on Acc_Avg by +1.49% and +6.09% respectively. Against the prior FG-FSCIL method PFR [18], the advantage is especially large on Stanford Cars (+13.02% Acc_T, +10.31% Acc_Avg) and Stanford Dogs (+8.05% Acc_T, +8.13% Acc_Avg), suggesting that PFR's frozen-backbone strategy leaves substantial room for improvement that MEL's evolving meta-category vector exploits. TEEN [26] is the closest competitor on Stanford Dogs (39.14% Acc_T) but still trails MEL by 4.92%. ADBS [11], which adapts decision boundaries during incremental sessions, underperforms MEL on all datasets, confirming that boundary adaptation alone is insufficient without inter-class synergy modeling and hypothesis-space regularization. C-FSCIL [7] performs poorly across the board (e.g., 13.86% Acc_T on CUB200), indicating that constrained optimization without fine-grained feature handling is ill-suited to this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayes lifelong learning framework (Pentina & Lampert; Rothfuss et al.) to derive a generalization bound specifically for FSCIL within a shared fine-grained meta-environment, a setting not previously formalized. Methodologically, it departs from prior FG-FSCIL approaches (notably PFR) that freeze the feature extractor after the base session, instead introducing an incrementally updated meta-category vector that evolves fine-grained feature extraction while constraining KL divergence. The sub-category relationship regularization via bidirectional KL divergence between high-level and low-level representations is a novel regularization mechanism not present in prior FSCIL or FG-FSCIL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical grounding for FG-FSCIL, showing that the environment complexity term (not just empirical error) is the key lever for improvement in fine-grained settings, which reframes how practitioners should think about incremental feature learning. The practical finding that mutual regularization enables high-level-only inference to match or exceed dual-branch inference is a useful simplification for deployment. Limitations include the same-domain assumption for base and novel classes, evaluation on only four fine-grained datasets with a small ResNet-12 backbone, and single-seed results without error bars, leaving open whether the gains hold under distribution shift or with larger architectures.
