## Problem

Shadow model-based inference attacks (e.g., membership inference) require training dozens to hundreds of independent shadow models, incurring prohibitive computational costs that limit practical deployment. Existing efficiency improvements are tightly coupled to specific attack algorithms and lack generalizability. This paper addresses the bottleneck from the perspective of shadow model construction itself, asking whether shared, jointly-trained models can replace independently trained ones without sacrificing attack effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20254632/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an auxiliary dataset (same distribution as the target's training data) and the target model's architecture (e.g., ResNet18, VGG16, WideResNet28-10) as input. The auxiliary data is randomly partitioned into M^L disjoint subsets mapped to all possible expert pathways in the MoE. The output is a set of n shared shadow models (activated end-to-end pathways, with n=64 by default) that directly substitute for conventional shadow models in downstream attacks such as LiRA or RMIA, ultimately producing membership status predictions (member/non-member) for query examples.

## Method

SHAPOOL constructs a Mixture-of-Experts network with L expert layers (each containing M identical sub-networks) where each activated pathway (one expert per layer, K=1) is equivalent to a shadow model of the target architecture. Three modules ensure these shared models are effective substitutes: (1) Pathway-choice routing establishes a fixed binary mapping matrix B between training samples and pathways, guaranteeing distinct and stable data allocation per pathway; (2) Pathway regularization jointly trains a pair of pathways per iteration, minimizing a combined loss of cross-entropy, a KL-divergence-based similarity regularizer on output probabilities, and an orthogonal regularizer on expert internal activations to enforce diversity; (3) Pathway alignment fine-tunes selected pathways on a small (~10%) subset of training data to correct generalization mismatch with independently trained models. The overall training objective is L = L_CE + α·L_SR + β·L_OR, and multiple independent shadow pools can be constructed to further increase the pool of available shared models.

## Results

Under the resource-constrained scenario (few shadow models), SHAPOOL improves LiRA-offline AUC by 5–12% (e.g., 0.73→0.82 on CIFAR-100/ResNet18) and LiRA-online AUC by ~2% at comparable computational cost. Under the resource-unconstrained scenario (128 shadow models for LiRA-online, 64 for LiRA-offline), SHAPOOL achieves comparable or slightly higher AUC while reducing wall-clock training time by 87–91% (e.g., 30.7h→3.9h for LiRA-online on CIFAR-100/ResNet18). On RMIA, SHAPOOL matches BASE AUC (0.93 vs. 0.94) while cutting cost by 75–76%. Against the Distilled (R2) baseline, SHAPOOL achieves AUC 0.93 and TPR@FPR=1% of 0.53, substantially outperforming Distilled(128) at AUC 0.83 and TPR@FPR=1% of 0.21.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline (BASE) trains shadow models independently on randomly sampled subsets. Under constrained resources, SHAPOOL wins on AUC across nearly all settings (e.g., +0.09 on CIFAR-100/ResNet18 LiRA-offline, +0.12 on CIFAR-10/ResNet18 LiRA-offline) at equal or lower cost. Under unconstrained resources, SHAPOOL matches or slightly exceeds BASE AUC (e.g., 0.84 vs. 0.81 for LiRA-offline on CIFAR-100) while reducing training time by ~88% on average; for LiRA-online, AUC drops marginally (0.94 vs. 0.95) but cost drops from 30.7h to 3.9h. Against the Distilled (R2) baseline from [66], SHAPOOL clearly wins: on CIFAR-100/ResNet18 LiRA-online, SHAPOOL achieves AUC 0.93 and TPR@FPR=1% of 0.53, versus Distilled(128) at AUC 0.83 and TPR@FPR=1% of 0.21, while also being faster (4.0h vs. 3.45h for 128 distilled models). The paper notes that distilled models are poorly suited for constructing IN worlds and still require training from scratch, explaining their inferior performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts mechanism—originally designed for scaling model capacity via sparse activation (Shazeer et al., Fedus et al.)—as a substrate for sharing sub-networks across multiple shadow models in inference attacks. It departs from prior efficiency efforts (QMIA, RMIA, SNAP) that were tightly coupled to specific attack algorithms, instead addressing the shadow model construction stage itself in an attack-agnostic manner. The three proposed modules (pathway-choice routing, pathway regularization, pathway alignment) are novel adaptations that address MoE-specific pathologies (routing specialization, expert similarity, under-training) that would otherwise degrade the shared models' fidelity to independently trained shadow models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing shadow model construction cost by ~88% while maintaining or improving attack performance, SHAPOOL makes large-scale data auditing and model vulnerability assessment computationally feasible, which is critical for model publishers and data owners. The attack-agnostic design means it can be plugged into any shadow-model-based inference attack without modification. Key limitations include the need to restructure the target architecture into MoE layers and a dependence on sufficient auxiliary data; open questions include whether refined parameter-sharing or adaptive training could further close the small performance gap in online attack modes.
