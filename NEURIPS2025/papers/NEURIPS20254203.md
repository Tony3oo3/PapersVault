## Problem

Existing large multi-modal models perform either object referring or segmentation independently through rigid input/output templates, and cannot simultaneously comprehend visual prompts (points, boxes, masks) and generate mask-grounded responses. More critically, they fail to integrate fine-grained pixel-level perception with general visual reasoning, making them unable to reason over specific objects rather than the entire scene.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a video or image, a text prompt, and up to N optional visual prompts (points as (x,y,t), boxes as (x1,y1,x2,y2,t), or 2D binary masks on a specific frame), and produces a textual response alongside K spatial-temporal masks at 768×768 resolution. Notably, K need not equal N since the model may segment additional objects not explicitly prompted, and both N and K can be zero, degenerating to standard visual understanding. During training, 8 frames per video are sampled at 316²–448² pixels (128–256 tokens per frame), with up to 5 objects per sample contributing to mask losses.

## Method

UniPixel builds on Qwen2.5-VL and SAM 2.1, introducing three key components: a prompt encoder that maps sparse prompts (points/boxes) into single tokens via joint 2D spatial + 1D temporal Fourier embeddings with learnable type embeddings, and maps dense mask prompts via masked pooling over visual encoder features; an object memory bank (a hashmap from object IDs to spatial-temporal masks) that decouples referring from segmentation through two operations—memory pre-filling (triggered by <REF> tokens, the model predicts masks for referred objects) and memory injection (masked-pooled object features replace <MEM> tokens in the prompt to enhance object-awareness during reasoning); and a mask decoder that takes 2 hidden tokens per <SEG> token to predict a first-frame mask and propagate it across video frames. The three-stage training recipe first pre-trains the prompt encoder on 851K regional captioning samples, then aligns the LLM with the mask decoder on 87K referring segmentation data, and finally jointly fine-tunes all components with LoRA on ~1M diverse samples. The central insight is that unifying referring and segmentation through the shared memory bank creates a mutual reinforcement effect, improving both capabilities simultaneously.

## Results

On the most challenging ReVOS reasoning video object segmentation benchmark, UniPixel-3B achieves 62.1 J&F overall, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art GLUS-7B (54.9 J&F) by 7.2 points, while UniPixel-7B further reaches 63.7 J&F. On MeViS, UniPixel-3B scores 53.1 J&F, outperforming GLUS-7B (51.3) and VideoGLaMM-3.8B (45.2) by 1.8 and 7.9 points respectively. On the novel PixelQA task requiring joint referring, segmentation, and QA, UniPixel-3B achieves 71.1% MCQ accuracy with point prompts, exceeding Qwen2-VL-72B (69.3%) and InternVL2-26B (60.8%). On VideoRefer-Bench^Q with multi-frame mask prompts, UniPixel-7B reaches 74.1% accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (71.3%) and VideoRefer-7B (72.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPixel-3B outperforms all prior methods on ReVOS by a wide margin: it beats GLUS-7B (54.9) by 7.2 J&F, ViLLa-6B (57.0) by 5.1, and Sa2VA-4B (53.2) by 8.9, despite using fewer parameters. On MeViS, UniPixel-3B (53.1 J&F) surpasses GLUS-7B (51.3) and VideoGLaMM-3.8B (45.2), while on Ref-DAVIS17 it slightly trails ViLLa-6B (74.2 vs. 74.3) but the 7B variant closes the gap (76.4). On GroundMoRe, UniPixel-3B (37.4 J&F) significantly outperforms MoRA-7B (27.2), which was fine-tuned on the target dataset, demonstrating strong zero-shot pixel-level reasoning. On VideoRefer-Bench^Q, UniPixel-7B with multi-frame prompts (74.1% Acc) exceeds GPT-4o (71.3%) and VideoRefer-7B (72.1%), while on the novel PixelQA task, UniPixel-3B (71.1% Acc) surpasses the much larger Qwen2-VL-72B (69.3%) and InternVL2-26B (60.8%), confirming that the object memory bank mechanism effectively enhances reasoning beyond what scale alone provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniPixel builds on Qwen2.5-VL as its LLM backbone and visual encoder, and SAM 2.1 as its mask decoder, but departs from prior LMM-based segmentation methods (LISA, VideoLISA, VISA, Sa2VA) that handle referring and segmentation as separate, rigidly templated tasks. The key architectural departure is the object memory bank—a hashmap storing spatial-temporal masks that decouples mask prediction from regional understanding and injects object-centric features back into the prompt for downstream reasoning. The prompt encoder extends SAM's spatial Fourier embeddings with temporal information for video, and the overall design unifies what were previously independent capabilities (referring, segmentation, and general visual QA) into a single end-to-end model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mutual reinforcement effect—where jointly training referring and segmentation improves both—provides evidence that pixel-level perception and language reasoning are complementary rather than competing objectives, which could inform future unified multi-modal model design. The PixelQA benchmark fills a gap by requiring simultaneous object identification, tracking, and question answering, a setting no prior method supports. Limitations include the mask decoder predicting only on the first frame (no bidirectional propagation), the 3B/7B scale being modest, and the absence of grounded caption generation tasks; scaling to larger models and more diverse pixel-level tasks remains open.
