## Problem

Existing multi-metric speech evaluation frameworks (e.g., UniVERSA, TorchSquim) predict all metrics independently and in parallel, overlooking inherent inter-metric dependencies, struggling with heterogeneous metric scales (bounded vs. unbounded, categorical vs. continuous), and failing to handle partially labeled data where many metrics require unavailable references. This limits both predictive accuracy and the ability to flexibly query arbitrary subsets of metrics at inference time.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single-channel speech signal (16 kHz) as input and produces 87 evaluation metrics (65 numerical, 22 categorical) structured as an autoregressive token sequence of interleaved metadata tokens (metric identifiers like <MOS>, <Emotion>) and value tokens. Numerical metrics are discretized via percentile-based quantization into 500 bins, while categorical metrics map directly to class tokens. At inference, users can query any subset of metrics in any order, and the system supports reference-free evaluation without requiring clean reference audio or transcripts.

## Method

ARECHO formulates multi-metric speech evaluation as autoregressive sequence generation over a unified token vocabulary. A WavLM-based audio encoder extracts speech representations, which condition a Transformer decoder that generates an interleaved sequence of metadata tokens (metric prompts) and value tokens. During training, the metric order is randomly permuted to expose the model to diverse conditioning patterns and capture inter-metric dependencies. For numerical metrics, percentile-based quantization (500 bins) balances token frequencies and preserves fine detail in dense regions. At inference, a two-step confidence-oriented decoding strategy first makes a preliminary value prediction, then performs a top-B candidate search that evaluates full-sequence log-likelihood to select the most reliable value token, reducing error propagation across the autoregressive chain.

## Results

On the averaged test set (Base configuration, 308.77 hrs training), ARECHO achieves MSE of 23.26, LCC of 0.86, Kendall's tau of 0.72, accuracy of 0.74, and F1 of 0.57, substantially outperforming UniVERSA (MSE 96.99, LCC 0.69, KTAU 0.52, Acc 0.69, F1 0.45). Under the larger Scale configuration (2137.74 hrs), ARECHO reaches MSE 25.21, LCC 0.85, KTAU 0.74, Acc 0.76, and F1 0.54, again leading UniVERSA (MSE 67.16, LCC 0.86, KTAU 0.70, Acc 0.73, F1 0.50). The largest relative gains appear on the Corrupted and Synthesized domains, where ARECHO reduces MSE by 3–4× over UniVERSA in the Base setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARECHO is compared against two baselines: UniVERSA (parallel multi-metric prediction with continuous regression heads) and UniVERSA-T (same architecture but with quantized tokenization of numerical metrics). In the Base configuration, ARECHO reduces average MSE from 96.99 (UniVERSA) to 23.26 and from 37.72 (UniVERSA-T) to 23.26, while improving LCC from 0.69/0.79 to 0.86 and F1 from 0.45/0.49 to 0.57. In the Scale configuration, ARECHO again leads on MSE (25.21 vs. 67.16 and 39.32) and F1 (0.54 vs. 0.50 and 0.51), though UniVERSA retains a slight LCC edge (0.86 vs. 0.85), suggesting tokenization introduces minor granularity loss for fine-grained regression. The UniVERSA-T variant confirms that tokenization alone yields large gains (e.g., MSE drops from 96.99 to 37.72 in Base), but the dynamic classifier chain provides additional improvements on top, particularly in classification F1 (+0.08 over UniVERSA-T in Base) and Kendall's tau (+0.04). ARECHO wins or ties on every metric in the Base setting and on MSE, KTAU, Acc, and F1 in the Scale setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARECHO builds directly on the UniVERSA architecture (Shi et al., 2025b), retaining its WavLM-based audio encoder but replacing the parallel, metric-specific prediction heads with a Transformer decoder that autoregressively generates a tokenized metric sequence. The dynamic classifier chain generalizes the multi-label classifier chain paradigm (Read et al., 2011, 2021) from fixed-order label sequences to a token-level formulation with randomized metric ordering, metadata prompt tokens, and support for partial supervision. Unlike prior unified frameworks (UniVERSA, TorchSquim) that predict all metrics independently, ARECHO explicitly models inter-metric dependencies through sequential conditioning, and its two-step confidence-oriented decoding addresses the instability introduced by metadata tokens during autoregressive inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARECHO addresses a practical bottleneck in speech evaluation: the need to predict 87 heterogeneous metrics (bounded/unbounded numerical, categorical) from a single forward pass while supporting flexible subset queries and partially labeled training data. The emergent prediction ordering—where the model autonomously defers subjective metrics like MOS until after stable attributes like speaker gender are resolved—provides interpretable evidence that inter-metric dependencies are being exploited. Key limitations include granularity loss from quantization for fine-grained regression targets and sensitivity to domain imbalance in the training mix (Scale configuration underperforms on synthesized speech). Open questions include whether the autoregressive dependency modeling is strictly necessary over simpler multi-task heads with shared representations, and how the approach scales to even larger metric vocabularies or cross-modal settings.
