## Problem

Black-box data protection tools (e.g., Glaze, Mist) allow users to upload images and receive protected versions that resist ML training, but their security has never been evaluated under the realistic threat that an adversary can query the same API with a small set of unprotected in-distribution images. This paper introduces the 'protection leakage' threat model and asks how much such leaked (unprotected, protected) pairs are needed to break the protection, and whether the resulting purification can generalize to unseen protected data.

## Input / Output

For training, BridgePure consumes a small set of (unprotected, protected) RGB image pairs—ranging from 500 pairs on CIFAR-10/100 to as few as 5–10 pairs (augmented via random 512×512 crops to ~1,000 pairs) for style-mimicry artwork. At inference, each protected image serves as the fixed endpoint x_T for conditional sampling, and the model outputs a purified image at the same spatial resolution as the input (32×32, 112×112, 224×224, or 512×512 depending on the dataset). An optional Gaussian-noise preprocessing step (parameter β) is applied to both the training pairs and the target protected set before purification.

## Method

BridgePure trains a Denoising Diffusion Bridge Model (DDBM) from scratch on the leaked (unprotected, protected) pairs by minimizing a score-matching loss that learns the conditional score ∇_{x_t} log q(x_t | x_0, x_T), where x_0 is the unprotected image and x_T is the protected image. Unlike standard diffusion models that denoise toward a learned data distribution, the bridge model transports between two specific empirical distributions, and every sampling step is conditioned on the protected image as the fixed endpoint, yielding a deterministic (or partially stochastic) ODE/SDE trajectory back to the unprotected image. Sampling uses a hybrid Euler-Maruyama/Heun scheme with a tunable randomness parameter s ∈ [0,1] that trades off determinism against diversity. The key practical advantage is that no pre-trained diffusion model or large auxiliary dataset is required—only a few hundred to a few thousand paired examples suffice to learn an effective inverse mapping P^{-1} that generalizes to the full protected dataset.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (original accuracy 94.01%), BridgePure-4K restores test accuracy to 93.5–94.1% across nine availability attacks, nearly closing the gap to the unprotected baseline. On CIFAR-100 (original 74.27%), BridgePure-4K reaches 72.0–74.6%, again approaching the unprotected level. On ImageNet-Subset (original 66.18%), even BridgePure-0.5K (65.89%) already surpasses DiffPure (54.87%), and BridgePure-4K matches the original accuracy (66.02%). On WebFace-Subset (original 87.84%), BridgePure-0.5K achieves 87.80% for EM-protected data, essentially full restoration with only 500 leaked pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BridgePure consistently outperforms all four classification baselines (PGD-AT, D-VAE, AVATAR, LE-JCDP) across all nine attacks on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. On CIFAR-10, BridgePure-4K achieves 93.5–94.1% versus LE-JCDP's best of 91.6% (TAP) and AVATAR's best of 91.4% (AR); the gap is most dramatic on OPS where PGD-AT fails entirely (9.60%) and LE-JCDP drops to 58.60%, while BridgePure-4K reaches 93.50%. On CIFAR-100, BridgePure-4K reaches 72.0–74.6% versus LE-JCDP's 67.4–69.2% and AVATAR's 62.1–65.5%, with the largest margin on OPS (74.18% vs. 39.10% for LE-JCDP). On ImageNet-Subset, BridgePure-0.5K already surpasses DiffPure by 11+ points (65.89% vs. 54.87% for EM), and BridgePure-4K matches the original accuracy (66.02% vs. 66.18%). For style mimicry, BridgePure-5/10 fully removes Glaze and Mist protective cloaks, outperforming GrIDPure, PDM, and NoisyUpscaling which leave visible artifacts, while also preserving fine brushstroke details that PDM smooths out.

## Relation to Prior Work

BridgePure builds directly on Denoising Diffusion Bridge Models (DDBM) [Zhou et al., ICLR 2024], repurposing their conditional transport framework to learn an inverse mapping from protected to unprotected data. It departs from prior purification methods (DiffPure, AVATAR, LE-JCDP, D-VAE) in two key ways: it requires no pre-trained diffusion model on a large in-distribution dataset, and it operates under a new 'protection leakage' threat model where the adversary possesses a small set of (unprotected, protected) pairs obtained by querying the black-box service. For style mimicry, it extends the comparison to recent tools like GrIDPure, PDM, and NoisyUpscaling, showing that conditional bridge sampling preserves fine-grained brushstroke details that unconditional denoising approaches blur.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that black-box data protection services (Glaze, Mist, and analogous APIs) have a fundamental vulnerability: even 500 leaked pairs can restore dataset availability to near-original levels, and as few as 5–10 pairs suffice to break style-mimicry protection. This challenges the practical security of deployed protection tools and motivates the need for ownership verification, non-reproducible protections, and user-side countermeasures. Open questions include whether the threat generalizes beyond images (text, audio), how to design protections that are inherently resistant to bridge-model inversion, and whether the authors' proposed countermeasures (decoy images, identity verification) are sufficient in practice.
