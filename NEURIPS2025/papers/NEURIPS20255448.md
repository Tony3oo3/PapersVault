## Problem

Existing Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) lack inherent mechanisms to dynamically incorporate external contextual knowledge, limiting their zero-shot forecasting performance under non-stationarity and distribution shifts. Additionally, most TSFMs offer limited interpretability, hindering deployment in decision-critical applications. The paper asks whether retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), proven effective in NLP, can be adapted to enhance both the generalization and transparency of TSFMs without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a univariate time series context window of length 512 as the query and a pre-constructed retrieval knowledge base of 2.8 million (context, embedding, forecast-horizon) triplets, where embeddings are pre-computed by a frozen Chronos encoder and indexed via FAISS. It produces a 64-step point forecast (quantile regression output), the top-k retrieved similar sequences with their future horizons, and per-sequence adaptive attention weights that serve as interpretable evidence for the prediction. The retrieval lookback length (64–512 steps) is configurable, and the framework supports extended horizons (up to 720 steps) via iterative rolling with per-step retrieval.

## Method

TS-RAG comprises three components: a frozen TSFM backbone (Chronos-Bolt), a retriever that computes Euclidean distances between the query embedding and stored context embeddings to select top-k similar (context, forecast) pairs, and a learnable Adaptive Retrieval Mixer (ARM) module. The ARM encodes each retrieved forecast horizon through a shared MLP projector, concatenates the resulting k embeddings with the TSFM's query representation, and processes them through Multi-Head Attention and a feed-forward network with residual connections. A softmax scoring network then assigns adaptive importance weights to all k+1 representations, and the final output is a weighted sum plus a skip connection to the original query embedding, which is passed through the TSFM's output projection layer. Only the ARM module and projector are trained (on 26M pairs from the Chronos pretraining corpus with quantile regression loss), while the backbone and retrieval encoder remain frozen, enabling plug-and-play compatibility with any TSFM architecture.

## Results

TS-RAG (Chronos-Bolt backbone) achieves an average MSE of 0.1940 and MAE of 0.2492 across seven zero-shot forecasting benchmarks, outperforming its own backbone Chronos-Bolt (0.2008 MSE) by 3.54% and the strongest competing TSFM Time-MoE (0.2057 MSE) by 5.7%. The largest single-dataset gain is on Exchange Rate, where TS-RAG reduces MSE by 6.84% over Chronos-Bolt (0.0627 vs. 0.0673). Against the retrieval-augmented baseline RAF, TS-RAG improves average MSE by 16.3% (0.1940 vs. 0.2318) while running 360× faster at inference (9.62 ms vs. 3474 ms per query).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-RAG wins on every dataset against all seven baselines. Against its own backbone Chronos-Bolt, TS-RAG reduces average MSE from 0.2008 to 0.1940 (−3.54%) and MAE from 0.2525 to 0.2492 (−1.43%), with the largest per-dataset gain on Exchange Rate (−6.84% MSE). Against the next-strongest TSFM, Time-MoE (avg MSE 0.2057), TS-RAG leads by 5.7%, and against MOMENT (0.2374) and Chronos (0.2380) the margin exceeds 18%. Moirai is the weakest TSFM baseline (0.2542 avg MSE), largely due to a severe degradation on ETTm1 (0.5399). Against the retrieval-augmented competitor RAF, TS-RAG improves average MSE by 16.3% (0.1940 vs. 0.2318) and average MAE by 8.9% (0.2492 vs. 0.2738), while also being ~360× faster at inference (9.62 ms vs. 3474 ms per query on ETTh), thanks to FAISS-based embedding retrieval versus RAF's raw-sequence concatenation approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

TS-RAG extends the retrieval-augmented generation paradigm from NLP to time series forecasting, building directly on Time Series Foundation Models (Chronos-Bolt, MOMENT, TimesFM, Moirai) as frozen backbones. It departs from prior retrieval-based forecasting methods (ReTime, RATD, TimeRAG, RAFT) that require fine-tuning by training only a lightweight Adaptive Retrieval Mixer (ARM) module while keeping the backbone and retrieval encoder frozen. The closest prior work, RAF, also targets zero-shot retrieval-augmented forecasting but relies on simple concatenation of retrieved context with the input; TS-RAG replaces this with a multi-head attention-based adaptive mixing mechanism and achieves a 360× inference speedup via FAISS-based embedding retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TS-RAG offers a practical, plug-and-play augmentation layer that can be attached to any TSFM without retraining, making it attractive for practitioners who want to improve zero-shot forecasting accuracy and interpretability with minimal additional compute. The interpretability angle (retrieval-as-evidence, transparent weighting) addresses a real deployment barrier for TSFMs in high-stakes domains. Open questions remain: in-domain retrieval consistently outperforms cross-domain, suggesting the knowledge base composition is a critical but underexplored design choice; the paper also flags adaptive retrieval lookback and multimodal extensions as future directions. The modest average gains (~3.5% MSE) and the fact that the largest improvement (6.84%) is on a single dataset temper the broader impact.
