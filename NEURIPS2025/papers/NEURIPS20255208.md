## Problem

Streaming algorithms for Correlation Clustering face a fundamental space-approximation tradeoff: the best-known non-learning semi-streaming algorithms achieve only a (3+ε)-approximation on complete graphs and an O(log|E⁻|)-approximation on general graphs, with the latter requiring Õ(ε⁻²n + |E⁻|) space. No learning-augmented streaming algorithms existed for this problem. This paper introduces the first such algorithms, showing that pairwise distance predictions can break the 3-approximation barrier on complete graphs and reduce space usage on general graphs while maintaining comparable approximation ratios.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a graph as an arbitrary-order dynamic stream of edge insertions and deletions (each edge labeled positive or negative), processed in a single pass, together with oracle access to a β-level predictor that outputs pairwise distances d_uv ∈ [0,1] satisfying the triangle inequality and a cost bound relative to OPT. The predictor's storage is excluded from the algorithm's space budget, which is Õ(ε⁻²n) words. The output is a partition of all n vertices into disjoint clusters; for complete graphs the approximation ratio is min{2.06β, 3}+ε, and for general graphs it is O(β log|E⁻|), with β measuring prediction quality (β=1 corresponds to an optimal predictor).

## Method

For complete graphs, the algorithm maintains ℓ₀-samplers per vertex during the stream to recover edges incident to 'interesting' vertices (those with low rank-to-degree ratio), then runs two pivot-based clustering procedures in post-processing: a standard TRUNCATEDPIVOT and a prediction-guided TRUNCATEDPIVOTWITHPRED that probabilistically adds vertices to pivot clusters based on predicted distances. The key insight is that the prediction-guided pivot process is equivalent to the 2.06-approximation LP rounding algorithm of Chawla et al., so the predictions act as a feasible LP solution, yielding a min{2.06β, 3}+ε guarantee while the standard pivot provides a robust (3+ε) fallback. For general graphs, the algorithm maintains an ε-spectral sparsifier H⁺ of the positive subgraph during the stream; if negative edges exceed the space budget, it performs ball-growing on H⁺ using predicted distances as the metric, achieving O(β log|E⁻|) approximation in Õ(ε⁻²n) space—improving over the non-learning algorithm's Õ(ε⁻²n + |E⁻|) space requirement.

## Results

On synthetic SBM graphs (p=0.95) and the DBLP dataset, Algorithm 1 with binary-classifier predictors reduces clustering cost by 54–72% compared to the non-learning CKLPU24 baseline (e.g., 145.6 vs. 524.8 ×10³ on SBM n=2400). On FACEBOOK subgraphs with good prediction quality (β≈1.2), Algorithm 1 achieves clustering costs 15% lower than CLMNPT21 and 22% lower than CKLPU24. On EMAILCORE, Algorithm 1 reduces cost by up to 14% over CLMNPT21 as spectral embedding dimension increases. Even under poor predictions, Algorithm 1 never performs worse than its (3+ε)-approximation non-learning counterpart.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: CKLPU24 [Cambus et al., SODA'24], the best-known (3+ε)-approximation streaming algorithm for complete graphs, and CLMNPT21 [Cohen-Addad et al., ICML'21], a multi-pass agreement-decomposition algorithm with a large theoretical approximation ratio (~701) but strong practical performance. Algorithm 1 consistently outperforms CKLPU24 across all datasets: by 66% on SBM(n=1200), 72% on SBM(n=2400), 71% on SBM(n=3600), and 54% on DBLP (Table 2). Against CLMNPT21, Algorithm 1 achieves 15% lower cost on FACEBOOK (β≈1.2) and up to 14% lower cost on EMAILCORE, though CLMNPT21 performs near-optimally on dense SBM graphs due to their many dense components. A key robustness property is that even when prediction quality degrades (large β), Algorithm 1's cost never exceeds that of CKLPU24, confirming the theoretical consistency-robustness guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: streaming algorithms for correlation clustering (Cambus et al. SODA'24 for complete graphs, Ahn et al. ICML'15 for general graphs) and learning-augmented algorithms that use ML predictions to beat worst-case guarantees. The key conceptual bridge is that a β-level pairwise distance predictor essentially provides a feasible solution to the metric LP formulation of correlation clustering, allowing the authors to transplant the 2.06-approximation LP rounding analysis (Chawla et al. STOC'15) into the streaming pivot-based framework of Cambus et al. For general graphs, the ball-growing framework of Charikar et al. and Demaine et al. is adapted to use predicted distances as metrics on a spectral sparsifier, eliminating the need to store all negative edges during the stream.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to bring learning augmentation to streaming correlation clustering, breaking the long-standing 3-approximation barrier for complete graphs in dynamic streams under good prediction quality while maintaining robustness. For general graphs, the space improvement (removing the |E⁻| term) is practically significant for graphs with many negative edges. Open questions include whether the predictor model can be further relaxed (e.g., to only require predictions for a subset of pairs), whether the 2.06 constant can be improved, and how well these algorithms perform when predictions are partially wrong or adversarially corrupted. The practical relevance is somewhat limited since streaming correlation clustering is a niche setting, but the predictor model (pairwise distances from related networks or temporal graphs) is natural and broadly applicable.
