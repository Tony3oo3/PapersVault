## Problem

Existing post-hoc explanation methods for time series classification operate exclusively in the time domain from a single-view perspective, producing fragmented and less faithful attributions. The only prior time-frequency approach, SpectralX, relies on a fixed STFT setup that lacks adaptability across different temporal patterns and cannot map frequency-domain insights back to the time domain where users interpret results. This paper addresses the broader gap of multi-view explanation frameworks that integrate multiple perspectives of the input interactively to improve both faithfulness and robustness.

## Input / Output

MIX takes a univariate time series of length T and a pretrained deep learning model (ResNet-34, BiLSTM, or Transformer) as input, and produces segment-level importance scores across multiple Haar DWT resolution levels. The overlapping segments are parameterized by a window size w and step size δ that scale by 2^{-r} at each wavelet level r, ensuring finer granularity at higher resolutions. The final output is a set of per-view explanations plus a greedy-selected set of top features projected onto a user-specified target view (typically cA_0, the original time domain), giving interpretable segments with associated importance scores.

## Method

MIX is a three-phase post-hoc framework built on Haar Discrete Wavelet Transform multi-resolution decomposition. Phase 1 constructs views V_r = [cA_r, cD_r, cD_{r-1}, ..., cD_1] at each level and independently explains them using OSIGV, which aggregates Integrated Gradients over overlapping segments in view space (F_V = F ∘ V^{-1}). Phase 2 introduces cross-view refinement: the best view is selected via a novel Keystone AUC_S_top (KAUC_S_top) criterion that measures degradation only over the top-κ fraction of features, then top-h segments from that view are mapped to all other views and used as 'keystone' features in a Keystone-first IG (KIGV) attribution that decomposes the gradient path into a keystone-to-baseline segment and a full-to-keystone segment, filtering out spurious attributions. Phase 3 applies a greedy traversal across all views to iteratively select the highest-scoring segments and project them onto the user-preferred view. The entire pipeline is deterministic (no random masking), satisfying IG's sensitivity and implementation-invariance axioms, and extends naturally to multivariate series by applying DWT per channel.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset with ground-truth explanations, MIX achieves an AUPRC of 0.7103 and Jaccard coefficient of 0.6133, improving to 0.7481 with the Phase 3 greedy selection. On the MIT-BIH ECG dataset with cardiologist-annotated explanations, MIX reaches AUPRC 0.7404 and Jaccard 0.5946 (0.6329 with greedy selection). Across 11 UCR datasets and 3 architectures (33 configurations), MIX ranks first in 27/33 robustness evaluations and 24/33 faithfulness evaluations (Faithfulness@8/20/30, AUC_S_top, F1_S) compared to LIMESegment, InteDisUX, and SpectralX.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIX outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against LIMESegment [76], MIX improves Jaccard by 4.3× on the synthetic dataset (0.6133 vs. 0.1427) and by 1.8× on MIT-BIH (0.5946 vs. 0.3376), while also leading on AUPRC in both settings. Against InteDisUX [83], the closest baseline, MIX gains +0.032 AUPRC and +0.406 Jaccard on synthetic, and +0.104 AUPRC and +0.169 Jaccard on MIT-BIH. Against SpectralX [10], MIX achieves superior faithfulness and robustness across all 33 UCR dataset/architecture configurations (Figure 2), and the paper further identifies two structural weaknesses in SpectralX's insertion/deletion attribution: seed-sensitive instability (Jaccard ≈ 0.47 between two random seeds) and a non-zero probability of false-negative feature omission that grows with time series length (≈ 0.135 at T = 10,000). SpectralX is excluded from Table 2 because it cannot map its time-frequency explanations back to the raw time domain where ground-truth annotations reside.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIX extends the gradient-based attribution lineage from Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017) through segment-level adaptation (InteDisUX, Tran et al., 2025) into a multi-view time-frequency setting using Haar DWT. It departs most directly from SpectralX (Chung et al., 2024), the only prior TSC explanation method operating in time-frequency space, by replacing SpectralX's fixed STFT with adaptive multi-resolution wavelet views, introducing cross-view refinement (where one view's top segments guide attribution in others via Keystone-first IG), and adopting a segment-level OSIGV mechanism that avoids SpectralX's instability and false-negative issues. The evaluation criterion KAUC_S_top is adapted from Turbé et al. (2023) to focus on the top fraction of features, aligning with user preferences for keystone features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-view interactive explanation framework addresses a genuine gap: prior TSC XAI methods operate in a single domain (time or time-frequency), potentially missing discriminative patterns visible only at certain resolutions. The cross-view refinement mechanism and greedy multi-view selection offer a principled way to combine complementary perspectives, which could generalize to other signal-processing tasks beyond TSC. Limitations include reliance on Haar DWT (other wavelet bases unexplored), primarily univariate evaluation, and the added computational cost of multi-view traversal. Open questions include whether the keystone-first concept transfers to other domains, how the framework scales to very long or high-dimensional multivariate series, and whether learned (rather than fixed) wavelet bases could further improve faithfulness.
