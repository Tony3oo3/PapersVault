## Problem

Existing vector quantization (VQ) methods for KV cache compression, such as Coupled Quantization (CQ), rely on calibration datasets to learn codebook centroids, making them highly susceptible to distribution shift. When the input distribution differs from the calibration set (e.g., C4 vs. WikiText-2), these methods suffer severe performance degradation—CQ's PPL on LLaMA3-8B jumps from 6.16 to 13.97—because the learned centroids fail to represent tokens absent from the calibration data. The paper asks whether VQ-based KV cache quantization can be made calibration-free while maintaining or exceeding the quality of calibration-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes key and value tensors from each transformer layer (shape l×d per head) in chunks of residual size 64, processing them through NSN and Hadamard transform before quantization. The output is a pair of 8-bit codebook indices per 8-dimensional sub-vector (plus 8 sign bits in 2-bit mode), along with per-token scaling factors s₁, s₂ and per-channel means o, which are themselves 4-bit double-quantized. A sliding residual buffer retains the most recent 64 tokens in full precision, flushed and quantized when full, so the effective average bit width is 2.23 (2-bit) or 1.23 (1-bit) including all overhead.

## Method

NSNQuant applies a three-step Normalize-Shift-Normalize (NSN) transformation—token-wise L2 normalization, channel-wise mean subtraction, and a second token-wise normalization—followed by a Hadamard transform, which together align the per-channel KV distribution with the standard normal distribution (verified via KL divergence and a variance-bound lemma using the Hanson-Wright inequality). Because the post-transformation distribution is known a priori, a single global 8-dimensional codebook trained on synthetic torch.randn data (fine-tuned to minimize cosine distance) serves all inputs without any calibration data. A scale-adjustment step rescales the dequantized vector so that the quantization error is orthogonal to the original, preserving token-distinctive components critical for attention selectivity. The method further incorporates KIVI-style residual buffering for recent tokens, double quantization of NSN byproducts to reduce overhead to 0.23 bits, and custom CUDA kernels that fuse quantization, dequantization, and attention computation for 3× throughput over FP16.

## Results

On C4 (out-of-distribution), NSNQuant-2b achieves PPL of 9.15 on LLaMA3.1-8B and 7.69 on Mistral-7B, outperforming CQ-4c9b (12.24 and 7.86) and KVQuant-2b+1% (9.60 and 7.93). In the 1-bit regime, the advantage is dramatic: NSNQuant-1b reaches PPL 17.20 on LLaMA3.1-8B (C4), while CQ-8c10b degrades to 49.60 and KVQuant-1b+1% to 37.37. On LongBench, NSNQuant-1b achieves 47.38 average (LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct) versus 40.78 for CQ-8c10b and 40.08 for KVQuant-1b+1%. NSNQuant also delivers up to 3× throughput gain over FP16 by enabling 4× larger batch sizes on A100-80GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CQ (the main competitor sharing the VQ paradigm), NSNQuant wins decisively on out-of-distribution data: on C4 with LLaMA3.1-8B, NSNQuant-2b achieves PPL 9.15 vs. CQ-4c9b's 12.24, and in 1-bit the gap widens to 17.20 vs. 49.60. On the in-distribution WikiText-2, CQ-8c10b slightly edges NSNQuant-1b (7.87 vs. 12.58 on LLaMA3.1-8B), confirming that calibration helps when the test distribution matches. Against KVQuant, NSNQuant dominates in both bit-widths across all models; the 1-bit gap is especially large (e.g., PPL 17.20 vs. 37.37 on LLaMA3.1-8B/C4). Against KIVI, NSNQuant is consistently better, with the largest margins in 1-bit (e.g., 17.20 vs. 37.37 on C4). On LongBench and LM-Eval tasks (GSM8K, HumanEval, MMLU), NSNQuant-1b outperforms all baselines by wide margins (e.g., GSM8K 53.45 vs. 44.88 for CQ and 21.53 for KVQuant on LLaMA3.1-8B), while in 2-bit the differences are smaller but NSNQuant still leads or ties on most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

NSNQuant builds directly on the vector quantization (VQ) framework introduced by Coupled Quantization (CQ), which learns codebook centroids from a small calibration set to jointly quantize groups of KV channels. The key conceptual departure is inverting the matching direction: rather than fitting the codebook to the (input-dependent) KV distribution, NSNQuant transforms the KV distribution to a fixed standard normal prior via a Normalize-Shift-Normalize (NSN) pipeline combined with a Hadamard transform, enabling a single reusable codebook with no calibration data. The method also draws on the residual-cache mechanism from KIVI, the randomized Hadamard transform from QuIP# (originally for weight quantization), and double quantization from QLoRA, but the NSN transformation and the adaptive scale-adjustment strategy are novel contributions specific to the KV-cache setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Calibration-free KV cache quantization removes a significant deployment bottleneck: practitioners no longer need to curate and maintain calibration datasets that adequately cover the diversity of real-world inputs (different languages, domains, tokenizations). The dramatic 1-bit gap over CQ (e.g., PPL 16.69 vs. 43.78 on C4 for LLaMA3-8B) and the 3× throughput gain make low-bit KV compression practically viable for long-context and high-batch serving. Open questions include whether the standardization failure in early-layer outlier channels (where the covariance Frobenius norm Γ is large) can be explicitly modeled, whether the approach extends to non-Transformer architectures, and whether token-importance-aware hybrid precision (as in ZipCache/MiKV) can be combined with the calibration-free VQ framework for further gains.
