## Problem

Existing SNN-based brain network analysis methods model only inter-regional functional connectivity changes, overlooking abnormal neuronal population firing dynamics within individual brain regions. This limits their ability to capture multi-scale neuropathological biomarkers, since disorders like Alzheimer's can manifest micro-level synaptic disruptions long before macro-level connectivity changes become detectable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251760/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each input sample is a functional brain graph with 116 ROIs (AAL atlas: 90 cerebral + 26 cerebellar), specified by a binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{116×116} encoding functional connectivity and a node feature matrix X ∈ ℝ^{116×116} capturing regional fMRI activity intensity. The model outputs a binary diagnosis (patient vs. healthy control) and, as a byproduct, two interpretable biomarker sets: per-region spiking activity deviations (from the local LSM pathway) and disorder-specific functional connectivity edge patterns (from STDP-trained synaptic weights).

## Method

LGC-SGT integrates a Local-Global Coupling module with a spiking transformer backbone and a shortcut-enhanced output strategy. The LGC module runs two parallel pathways: a local liquid-state-machine pathway of LIF neurons with sparse random connectivity (4:1 E/I ratio) trained unsupervised via STDP to emulate local cortical plasticity and capture regional firing-rate dynamics, and a global spiking graph convolution pathway trained by backpropagation to preserve whole-brain connectivity structure. These features are processed by a spiking transformer using spiking self-attention (SSA) with LIF neurons. The shortcut-enhanced output multiplies continuous pre-spike features by their binarized spike counterparts to retain feature intensity while preserving event-driven computation. Training uses a hybrid loss combining cross-entropy with minimum error entropy (MEE), whose Gaussian-kernel-based formulation provably suppresses gradient contributions from outliers caused by inter-individual and inter-center variability.

## Results

On tenfold cross-validation, LGC-SGT achieves 70.9% accuracy on REST-meta-MDD, 73.0% on ABIDE, and 93.5% on SRPBS, consistently outperforming all 16 baselines across the three brain disorder datasets. Compared to the best generalized graph network (GIN at 65.4%/67.9%/84.3%), the gains are 6.2%, 5.1%, and 9.2% respectively. Against the strongest specialized brain network (BrainIB at 70.0%/70.2%/90.3%), improvements are 0.9%, 2.5%, and 3.2%. Versus the best SNN baseline (SiGNN at 66.2%/69.1%/87.6%), LGC-SGT gains 4.9%, 4.3%, and 4.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGC-SGT wins on all three datasets against every one of the 16 baselines. Against the strongest generalized GNNs (GIN, GAT), it leads by 5–9 percentage points in accuracy, with the largest margin on SRPBS (93.5% vs. 84.3% for GIN). Among specialized brain networks, BrainIB is the closest competitor (70.0% on REST-meta-MDD, 70.2% on ABIDE, 90.3% on SRPBS), and LGC-SGT still edges ahead by 0.9–3.2 points. In the SNN category, SiGNN is the strongest baseline (66.2%/69.1%/87.6%), and LGC-SGT surpasses it by 4.3–4.9 points, while SpikeGT (the spiking transformer backbone) trails by 4.3–9.1 points. In leave-one-site-out cross-validation (Table 2), LGC-SGT also achieves the highest site-average accuracy (73.1% on REST-meta-MDD, 74.6% on ABIDE) over CI-GNN, BrainIB, and SpikeGT, confirming cross-site generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SNN-based brain network analysis by introducing a local-global coupling architecture that jointly models neuronal population firing dynamics (via a liquid state machine with STDP plasticity) and inter-regional functional connectivity (via spiking graph convolution), departing from prior SNN frameworks that focused exclusively on connectivity patterns. The spiking transformer backbone is adapted from SpikeGraphormer [27], and the minimum error entropy loss is borrowed from signal processing [30], but their integration into a dual-perspective brain disorder diagnostic pipeline with a shortcut-enhanced output strategy is novel. The work positions itself against both generalized GNNs and specialized brain GNNs, arguing that neither captures the microscale neuronal dynamics that precede macroscale connectivity changes in neurodegenerative and psychiatric disorders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-perspective biomarker discovery—identifying both region-specific firing rate abnormalities and connectivity reorganization—offers a more mechanistically grounded framework for understanding brain disorders than connectivity-only analyses, potentially revealing earlier pathological signatures. The leave-one-site-out generalization across 17 independent sites suggests the learned representations are not overfit to site-specific artifacts, which is critical for clinical translation. Key limitations include reliance on resting-state fMRI alone (no structural or electrophysiological data), the interpretability gap between STDP-learned reservoir weights and true biological synaptic plasticity, and the absence of code release. Open questions include whether the local-global coupling generalizes to multimodal settings (DTI, EEG) and whether the MEE loss's robustness advantage persists at larger cohort scales.
