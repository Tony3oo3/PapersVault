## Problem

How macroscopic spatiotemporal brain dynamics—traveling waves, localized bumps, oscillations, chaos—emerge from microscopic local connectivity rules remains an open question. Existing frameworks (neural field theory, dynamical mean-field theory) each capture subsets of phenomena but lack a unified, quantitative mapping from local structural parameters to the full repertoire of emergent global dynamical phases in spatially extended networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of local structural parameters—E/I projection ranges (d_αβ), mean connection strengths (g̅_αβ), out-degrees (k_αβ^out), and weight variances (σ_αβ)—that define a spatially extended E/I network with wrapped Gaussian connectivity on a 2D torus. It produces an analytical eigenvalue spectrum consisting of a bulk disk (radius determined by local sparsity and weight variance) plus a discrete set of outlier eigenvalues indexed by spatial Fourier wave vectors k, from which a six-phase dynamical classification (asynchronous, synchronous, oscillatory, bump, wave, chaotic) is read off. For experimental validation, mesoscopic optical imaging data from mouse cortex across arousal states are processed via Hilbert-transform phase extraction and Horn-Schunck optical flow to yield phase velocity fields, which are then decomposed via SVD into dominant spatiotemporal modes.

## Method

The core decomposition J = J̄ + δJ exploits the fact that spatial translational invariance makes the expectation matrix J̄ a low-rank structure, while the zero-mean fluctuation δJ carries the stochastic heterogeneity. For each spatial Fourier mode k, J̄ reduces to a 2×2 effective connectivity matrix J̄_eff^(k) with entries g̅_αβ exp(−‖k‖²d_αβ²/2), whose eigenvalues are the spectral outliers; the bulk disk radius r = √(max λ(M)) is derived from a variance-profile matrix M whose elements encode both sparse-connection heterogeneity and weight variability. The dominant eigenvalue (largest real part) then determines the dynamical phase: Re(λ_dom) < 1 gives asynchrony; real outliers at k=0 give synchrony; complex outliers at k=0 give oscillations; real outliers at k≠0 give bumps; complex outliers at k≠0 give traveling waves; and dominant bulk eigenvalues give chaos. This spectral blueprint is validated against 5 independent simulations per parameter point across 21×21 grids, and the resulting SVD modes of phase velocity fields are compared qualitatively to mesoscopic imaging of mouse cortex across anesthesia-to-wakefulness transitions.

## Results

The paper derives a closed-form expression for the bulk disk radius (Eq. 6) and the outlier eigenvalues indexed by spatial Fourier wave vectors k (Eq. 3), which together constitute a 'spectral blueprint' predicting six distinct dynamical phases. Numerical validation on a 21×21 parameter grid (5 independent runs per point, 100τ simulation time) confirms the predicted phase boundaries across the full parameter space. Comparison with mesoscopic optical imaging of mouse cortex [4] shows that the dominant SVD spatial modes (plane waves, spirals) from the model qualitatively match experimental patterns, and order parameters (local sync., oscillation index, persistent homology) identify a trajectory from the interior of the wave phase toward the wave-bump boundary as consciousness increases from anesthetized to fully awake, corresponding to a decrease in E-to-I coupling strength |g̅_IE/g̅_I|.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing methods in the traditional sense, as it introduces a new theoretical framework rather than a predictive model. Instead, it positions itself relative to three prior approaches: (1) Neural field theory [12–14], which assumes homogeneous connectivity and a continuum limit, and therefore cannot capture chaotic dynamics or local sparsity effects; (2) Dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT) [19–21], which characterizes chaos in non-spatial networks but does not account for spatial structure or spatially patterned activity; and (3) prior random matrix analyses of neural networks [30, 43, 44], which identify outlier eigenvalues in non-spatial or globally coupled settings without linking them to specific spatial Fourier modes. The paper's SVD-based phase velocity field analysis (Fig. 4) provides the closest empirical comparison: the model's dominant spatial modes (planar waves, clockwise/counter-clockwise spirals) show qualitative correspondence to SVD modes extracted from mesoscopic optical imaging of mouse cortex across arousal states [4], supporting the claim that the spectral framework captures salient principles of real cortical dynamics. The authors note this validation is preliminary, limited to one mouse and one trial with all three consciousness states available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate theoretical traditions—neural field theory (which captures spatial pattern formation like bumps and traveling waves via Fourier-mode analysis) and dynamical mean-field theory (which captures chaotic dynamics in large-scale networks via spectral bulk statistics)—into a single random-matrix-theoretic framework for spatially extended E/I networks. It builds directly on the circular law and variance-profile results of Tao, Cook, Aljadeff, and Rajan & Abbott for the bulk disk, and on Tao's low-rank perturbation theory for the outlier eigenvalues, but applies them jointly to a spatially structured connectivity matrix for the first time. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of 'local sparsity' (connection density within the projection range, rather than global sparsity) as the parameter controlling the bulk radius, and the explicit demonstration that the spatial organization of the network itself constitutes the low-rank structure generating outliers indexed by wave vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, predictive bridge from local circuit parameters (E/I projection ranges, strengths, local density, weight variability) to a full repertoire of six global dynamical phases, offering a potential mechanism for how the brain transitions between arousal states (anesthetized waves → awake localized patterns). However, the experimental validation is preliminary—limited to a single mouse and one trial—and the linearized analysis around a fixed point restricts applicability to small-deviation regimes. Open questions include extending to nonlinear dynamics, incorporating synaptic plasticity and time-varying effective connectivity, handling anisotropic or heterogeneous connectivity, and elucidating the computational roles of each spectrally-defined phase for cognition and consciousness.
