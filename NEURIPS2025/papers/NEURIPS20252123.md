## Problem

Sampling from unnormalized Boltzmann densities in molecular systems remains intractable for diffusion-based samplers, which fail even on small peptides in Cartesian coordinates due to the absence of training data, mode-dropping training objectives, and excessive energy function evaluations. No existing diffusion-based sampler has achieved equilibrium sampling at the scale of simple molecular systems, leaving a gap between the promise of amortized samplers and practical molecular applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized energy function (defining the Boltzmann density) and a short, not-necessarily-well-mixed MCMC chain at a high starting temperature as its only data requirement. It produces samples from the target Boltzmann density at a lower temperature in Cartesian coordinates, with the total computational budget measured in energy function evaluations (e.g., 5×10⁷ for Alanine Dipeptide, 8×10⁷ for Alanine Tripeptide). The high-temperature chain need only cover the modes of the distribution rather than be fully converged, a much weaker requirement than standard MCMC.

## Method

PITA interleaves two complementary interpolation mechanisms—temperature annealing of the Boltzmann density and diffusion noising—to progressively train a sequence of diffusion models from high to low temperature. At each step, a novel Feynman-Kac PDE (Proposition 1) defines an SDE with coordinate-dependent importance weights that, when combined with Sequential Monte Carlo resampling, simulates the trained diffusion model to generate asymptotically unbiased samples at a lower temperature without requiring ground-truth data at that temperature. Training at each temperature uses four jointly optimized losses: Denoising Score Matching for the score network, Target Score Matching to stabilize near-data regions, EBM Distillation to transfer score information into a parametric energy model, and Energy Pinning to fix the gauge of the energy network against the true unnormalized density. The key insight is that temperature annealing lowers energy barriers to mix modes while the diffusion path avoids the mass-teleportation artifact of pure annealing, and the Feynman-Kac reweighting over the full diffusion trajectory (rather than only at the endpoint) keeps importance weight variance tractable across large temperature gaps.

## Results

On LJ-13 (annealed from T=4 to T=1), PITA achieves a Distance-W2 of 0.04 and Energy-W2 of 2.26, substantially outperforming iDEM (0.127, 30.78) and TA-BG (1.21, 61.47). On Alanine Dipeptide at 300K, PITA achieves Rama-KL of 4.77, Energy-W2 of 1.62, and T-W2 of 0.27, beating all baselines including MD-Diff (Rama-KL 1.31, Energy-W2 3.70) and TA-BG (Rama-KL 14.99, Energy-W2 86.18). On Alanine Tripeptide, PITA achieves Rama-KL of 1.21 and Energy-W2 of 2.59, outperforming TA-BG (Rama-KL 2.08, Energy-W2 4.86) and PITA without relaxation (Rama-KL 8.54, Energy-W2 87.70), though MD-Diff retains an edge on Tica-W1 (0.059 vs 0.272).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PITA decisively outperforms TA-BG (Temperature-Annealed Boltzmann Generators) across all three tasks and all metrics; on ALDP, TA-BG's Energy-W2 is 86.18 vs PITA's 1.62, and on AL3, TA-BG without relaxation reaches Energy-W2 of 178.56 vs PITA's 2.59. Against iDEM on LJ-13, PITA reduces Distance-W2 from 0.127 to 0.04 and Energy-W2 from 30.78 to 2.26. Compared to Adjoint Sampling on LJ-13, PITA achieves a slightly lower Energy-W2 (2.26 vs 2.40) and comparable Geometric-W2 (1.65 vs 1.67). Against MD-Diff (diffusion trained on MD data at target temperature), PITA wins on Rama-KL (4.77 vs 1.31 on ALDP; 1.21 vs 9.66 on AL3), Energy-W2 (1.62 vs 3.70 on ALDP; 2.59 vs 7.60 on AL3), and T-W2, but MD-Diff retains an edge on Tica-W1 for AL3 (0.059 vs 0.272), suggesting PITA does not fully recover correct relative mode weights. Against the FKC and Score Scaling diffusion-only annealing baselines on ALDP, PITA wins on all metrics, with Energy-W2 of 1.62 vs 11.47 (FKC) and 10.46 (Score Scaling).

## Relation to Prior Work

PITA sits at the intersection of diffusion-based sampling (Song et al. 2021; De Bortoli et al. 2024) and classical annealing/tempering strategies (parallel tempering, annealed importance sampling, SMC). Its core theoretical contribution—a Feynman-Kac PDE for inference-time annealing of a trained diffusion model—generalizes both the FKC correctors of Skreta et al. (2025) and the importance-sampling-with-CNFs scheme of Köhler et al. (2020), while departing from Temperature-Annealed Boltzmann Generators (Schopmans & Friederich 2025) by performing guided resampling along the entire diffusion path rather than only at the final timestep. The training recipe combines denoising score matching, target score matching, and EBM distillation (Thornton et al. 2025) into a progressive, temperature-ladder curriculum that avoids the mass-teleportation pathology of pure annealing and the data-scarcity problem of pure diffusion at low temperature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PITA is the first diffusion-based sampler to achieve equilibrium sampling of short peptides (Alanine Dipeptide, Tripeptide) in native Cartesian coordinates, a regime where prior diffusion methods and even carefully tuned MCMC with parallel tempering struggled. The progressive training framework—collecting cheap high-temperature MCMC data, training a diffusion model, then using inference-time annealing to generate lower-temperature training data—offers a practical recipe that could be adopted by other amortized samplers. Key open questions include: whether the approach scales to longer peptides or proteins, how to automatically select optimal temperature jumps, and whether the EBM training bottleneck (identified as a major limitation) can be alleviated. The AL3 results, where PITA achieves better mode coverage but worse TICA metrics than baselines, suggest that correct relative mode weighting remains an unsolved challenge.
