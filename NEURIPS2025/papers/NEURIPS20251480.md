## Problem

Egocentric video generation requires large-scale, high-quality training data with detailed action annotations, but existing egocentric datasets (Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D) are designed for perception tasks and contain excessive noisy camera motion unsuitable for generative training. No prior dataset provides both fine-grained kinematic control signals and semantically consistent textual action descriptions at the scale needed to train egocentric video generation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20251480/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EgoDreamer takes three inputs: an initial frame image (480p for training, 1080p in the dataset), a high-level textual action description generated by an MLLM+LLM pipeline, and low-level kinematic control signals (translation and rotation trajectories) derived from Visual-Inertial Odometry. The output is a ~120-frame egocentric video clip. The dataset provides 5M clips with 5M textual descriptions and 65K clips with precise VIO-based kinematic annotations (the latter being ~5× larger than prior kinematic annotation datasets).

## Method

The paper contributes two main components. First, EgoVid-5M is built from Ego4D with a dual annotation pipeline: kinematic control is extracted via ParticleSfM camera poses fused with IMU signals through Butterworth filtering, least-squares optimization of initial velocity/transformation/scale, and Kalman filtering; textual descriptions are generated by LLaVA-NeXT-Video-32B followed by Qwen2 summarization. A five-stage cleaning pipeline (text-video consistency via CLIP+EgoVideo, frame-frame CLIP similarity, motion smoothness via translation/rotation variance, motion strength via global and 5-point optical flow, and clarity via DOVER) filters the data. Second, EgoDreamer is a diffusion-based image-to-video model built on DynamiCrafter's U-Net architecture, featuring a Unified Action Encoder that jointly encodes Plücker-embedded kinematic signals and CLIP-embedded text through shared U-Net blocks with cross-attention, and an Adaptive Alignment module that applies learnable parameterized normalization (inspired by cross-normalization) across multi-scale U-Net features to integrate action conditions, outperforming both ControlNet and ControlNext zero-initialization strategies.

## Results

Training with EgoVid-5M consistently improves all three video generation baselines across six evaluation metrics. DynamiCrafter achieves the best overall CD-FVD of 236.82 (vs. 243.63 without EgoVid), while OpenSora shows the largest relative CD-FVD improvement (809.46 → 718.32, ~11% reduction). The most dramatic gain is in motion strength: DynamiCrafter nearly doubles from 9.357 to 18.329, and OpenSora more than doubles from 7.608 to 15.871, indicating that EgoVid-5M effectively teaches models to generate realistic egocentric motion. Semantic consistency, action consistency, clarity, and motion smoothness all improve modestly across all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three pre-trained video diffusion baselines—SVD (U-Net), DynamiCrafter (U-Net), and OpenSora (DiT)—both before and after finetuning on EgoVid-1M-3. All three baselines improve on every metric after EgoVid training, confirming the dataset's broad utility. DynamiCrafter benefits most in motion strength (9.357 → 18.329, nearly 2×), suggesting it was previously under-generating egocentric motion; OpenSora shows the largest CD-FVD reduction (809.46 → 718.32), indicating its DiT architecture was most disadvantaged by the lack of egocentric data. SVD, which already had moderate motion strength (18.897), sees a smaller relative gain (to 21.032) but still improves on all other metrics. In the EgoDreamer ablation (Table 3), the proposed AA + UAE configuration outperforms both ControlNet (CD-FVD 238.87) and ControlNext (234.13), achieving the best CD-FVD of 229.82 and lowest rotation error (3.28°) and translation error (7.62), demonstrating that the unified action encoding and adaptive alignment are critical for kinematic controllability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the action-driven video generation line of work (CameraCtrl, CamCo, MotionCtrl, etc.) from camera-motion control in static scenes to the substantially harder problem of generating egocentric videos with complex human actions and dynamic viewpoints. Architecturally, EgoDreamer builds on DynamiCrafter's U-Net diffusion backbone but replaces ControlNet's zero-initialized conditioning with a Unified Action Encoder (combining Plücker-embedded kinematics and CLIP-embedded text) and an Adaptive Alignment module inspired by cross-normalization. On the data side, it repurposes Ego4D's raw egocentric footage but departs sharply from its design: replacing noisy IMU-based annotations with VIO-fused kinematic signals and simplistic human narrations with MLLM-generated action descriptions, then applying an egocentric-specific cleaning pipeline (text-video, frame-frame, motion smoothness, motion strength, clarity) that goes beyond the aesthetic/semantic filters used in OpenVid-1M or SVD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoVid-5M is the first large-scale (5M clips, 1080p) dataset purpose-built for egocentric video generation, filling a clear gap between egocentric understanding benchmarks (Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D) and the data requirements of generative models. The dual-control formulation (high-level text + low-level kinematics) opens a path toward interactive, controllable egocentric world simulators for VR/AR, but the current system assumes pre-specified trajectories and short clips with predefined action categories, leaving long-horizon generation, closed-loop trajectory learning, and open-ended activity modeling as open problems. The released cleansing metadata (rather than just filtered data) is a practical contribution that lets the community explore cleaning-strategy trade-offs without incurring the thousands-of-GPU-days annotation cost.
