## Problem

Existing multimodal contrastive learning methods rely on the multiview redundancy assumption and primarily capture redundant and unique information across modalities, while synergistic information—where complementary modalities combine through specific interaction patterns to produce outcomes unattainable by any single modality—remains underexploited. This is critical because synergy constitutes the fundamental value proposition of multimodal representation, yet methods like CoMM only capture it indirectly through mutual information between augmented variants, which proves inadequate for tasks heavily dependent on complex inter-modal synergy.

## Input / Output

The method accepts n modalities (e.g., image + text, image + audio, image + text + audio, image + proprioceptive + force, or time series + tabular data), each encoded independently by modality-specific encoders into latent feature vectors. These features are then processed through a Transformer fusion block (1-layer for bimodal, 2-layer for trimodal, with a learnable [CLS] token) to produce a single fused multimodal latent representation. During training, each modality's features are stochastically occluded at a dataset-specific ratio (0.5–0.8) across 4–6 masked views before fusion, creating multiple partial-information fused representations that are contrasted against the unmasked fused output.

## Method

InfMasking introduces an Infinite Masking strategy that stochastically occludes a substantial proportion of features from each modality during the Transformer fusion step, preserving only partial information to generate fused representations with varied synergistic patterns. Unmasked fused representations are aligned with these masked counterparts via InfoNCE mutual information maximization, encouraging the fused representation to encode comprehensive synergistic information across diverse partial modality combinations. Since computing mutual information over infinitely many random masks is intractable, the authors derive a lower bound using Jensen's inequality on the concave log function, then assume masked fused embeddings follow a Gaussian distribution to obtain a closed-form approximation of the InfMasking loss. The total training objective combines the standard CoMM terms (fused-fused alignment for R+S+U, and unimodal-fused alignment for R+U) with this new masking-based regularization term specifically targeting synergistic interactions, all optimized end-to-end with AdamW.

## Results

On the Trifeature synthetic benchmark, InfMasking achieves 77.0% synergy accuracy, outperforming CoMM (71.4%) by 5.6% while maintaining near-perfect redundancy (99.9%) and uniqueness (90.6%). On the Multibench 2-modal suite, InfMasking attains the best average classification accuracy of 67.05%, surpassing CoMM (64.45%) by 2.6%, with the largest margin on MOSI (+5.3%) and MUSTARD (+2.4%). For the V&T EE regression task, InfMasking reduces MSE to 4.23×10⁻⁴, a 47% improvement over CoMM (7.96×10⁻⁴). On MM-IMDb, InfMasking reaches 62.60% weighted-F1 and 55.93% macro-F1, exceeding CoMM by 1.31% and 2.14% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfMasking consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against the strongest baseline CoMM [12], InfMasking gains 5.3% on MOSI, 2.4% on MUSTARD, 1.7% on MIMIC, and 1.0% on UR-FUNNY, while reducing V&T EE MSE by 3×10⁻⁴. On the Trifeature synergy task, InfMasking (77.0%) surpasses CoMM (71.4%) by 5.6% and FactorCL [29] (46.5%) by 30.5%, confirming that the infinite masking term specifically targets the synergy gap. Compared to MAE [19], InfMasking improves synergy from 50.1% to 77.0% on Trifeature and leads on all three Multibench classification datasets (MIMIC +0.7%, UR-FUNNY +1.8%, MOSI +3.6%). For the 3-modality setting, InfMasking matches CMC [41] on Vision&Touch contact prediction (94.1%) but exceeds it by 6.4% on UR-FUNNY (65.6% vs. 59.2%). On MM-IMDb, InfMasking (62.60% weighted-F1) outperforms SLIP [35] (56.54%) by 6.06% and SimCLR [9] (40.35%) by 22.25%, while also surpassing CLIP [38] with original public weights (58.9%) by 3.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CoMM (Dufumier et al., ICLR 2025), which first grounded multimodal contrastive learning in Partial Information Decomposition to jointly capture redundancy, uniqueness, and synergy. InfMasking departs from CoMM by identifying that its mutual-information objective between augmented variants primarily strengthens redundant and unique interactions, leaving synergistic information under-modeled, and introduces an infinite masking strategy that stochastically occludes modality features before fusion to expose the model to diverse partial-modality combinations. The masking mechanism is conceptually inspired by MAE's patch occlusion but repurposed for contrastive cross-modal alignment rather than reconstruction, and the Gaussian approximation for the intractable infinite-mask mutual information draws on prior work on joint contrastive learning with infinite possibilities (Cai et al., NeurIPS 2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing synergistic information as the fundamental value proposition of multimodal representation and providing a practical mechanism to enhance it, this work addresses a gap that prior contrastive multimodal methods (CLIP, FactorCL, CoMM) leave underexplored. The derived lower bound via Jensen's inequality and a Gaussian assumption makes the infinite-masking objective computationally tractable, enabling consistent SOTA across seven benchmarks spanning healthcare, robotics, and multimedia. Key limitations include the absence of a rigorous theoretical characterization of what constitutes synergistic information, the added GPU memory cost of multiple masked views, and reliance on a Gaussian distributional assumption for masked embeddings. Open questions include adaptive masking strategies, formal information-theoretic guarantees on synergy preservation, and whether the approach scales to more than three modalities or to generative multimodal settings.
