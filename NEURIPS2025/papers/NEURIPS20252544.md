## Problem

While quantum algorithms for Nash equilibria in two-player zero-sum games are well-established with quadratic speedups in the number of actions, no quantum algorithms existed for computing correlated equilibria (CE) or coarse correlated equilibria (CCE) in multi-player general-sum normal-form games. Classical algorithms for these equilibria require query complexity polynomial in the number of actions n, and the paper asks whether quantum speedups can be achieved that are near-optimal in both the number of players m and actions n.

## Input / Output

The algorithm accesses the game through a unitary oracle O_L that maps |i⟩|a₁⟩…|a_m⟩|0⟩ to |i⟩|a₁⟩…|a_m⟩|L_i(a₁,…,a_m)⟩, where i indexes the player and a_j are actions. For CE, the output is a quantum state |ψ_o⟩ = Σ_a √q(a)|a⟩|ψ_a⟩ whose measurement yields an ε-correlated equilibrium distribution q. For CCE, the output is a classical description of a Õ(B²/ε²)-sparse ε-coarse correlated equilibrium, which can be converted to the quantum state in Problem 1 in Õ(mB²/ε²) time. The game is assumed to have a succinct representation enabling efficient oracle construction.

## Method

For CE, the paper quantizes the multi-scale MWU framework (Peng & Rubinstein; Dagan et al.) by storing all historical action samples in a single unified QRAM rather than per-instance frequency vectors, then constructing amplitude-encodings of accumulated loss vectors from this QRAM and using a quantum Gibbs sampler to sample from the exponential distribution. This avoids the exponential QRAM size (n^m entries) that would arise from storing joint-action frequencies. For CCE, the paper extends Bouland et al.'s dynamic Gibbs sampling framework from two-player zero-sum games to m-player general-sum games, replacing the sampler tree data structure with direct QRAM storage of per-round samples to avoid exponential gate overhead, and proving convergence via a ghost-iteration argument. Both algorithms achieve Õ(m√n) query complexity (with additional ε-dependence for CCE), matching a proven Ω(m√n) quantum lower bound derived by reducing the direct product of m unstructured search problems to equilibrium computation.

## Results

For computing an ε-correlated equilibrium (CE) of an m-player normal-form game with n actions, the quantum algorithm achieves query complexity m√n(log(mn))^O(1/ε), improving over the classical mn(log(mn))^O(1/ε) bound by a quadratic factor in n. For ε-coarse correlated equilibrium (CCE), the query complexity is Õ(m√n/ε^2.5) with time complexity Õ(m²√n/ε^4.5). A matching quantum lower bound of Ω(m√n) is proven for both CE and CCE, confirming near-optimality in m and n up to polylogarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the classical multi-scale MWU algorithms of Dagan et al. [13] and Peng & Rubinstein [29], the quantum CE algorithm achieves a quadratic speedup in the number of actions n (m√n vs. mn queries), while preserving the same polylogarithmic dependence on m and ε. For CCE, the quantum algorithm improves upon Grigoriadis & Khachiyan [19] from Õ(mn/ε²) to Õ(m√n/ε^2.5) queries, again a quadratic speedup in n, though at the cost of a slightly worse ε-exponent (2.5 vs. 2). The quantum lower bound of Ω(m√n) matches both upper bounds up to polylogarithmic factors, establishing that the √n scaling is information-theoretically optimal. The time complexity carries an additional factor of m (m²√n vs. m√n) due to QRAM gate-level construction costs, which the authors identify as an open question for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper initiates quantum algorithms for computing correlated equilibria (CE) and coarse correlated equilibria (CCE) in multi-player normal-form games, a task previously studied only in the classical setting. For CE, it quantizes the multi-scale MWU framework of Dagan et al. and Peng & Rubinstein, replacing the Ω(n) per-round loss-vector computation with a quantum Gibbs sampler fed by a single unified QRAM that stores raw action samples rather than frequency vectors—avoiding the exponential-in-m QRAM overhead that would arise from the n^m joint action space. For CCE, it extends the dynamic Gibbs sampling framework of Bouland et al. (itself a quantization of Grigoriadis & Khachiyan's two-player zero-sum algorithm) to the m-player setting, substituting the sampler-tree data structure (which would require n^{m-1} entries) with a QRAM storing per-round strategy samples. Matching Ω(m√n) quantum query lower bounds, proven via a direct-product reduction from unstructured search, confirm near-optimality in both m and n.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-optimal m and n scaling establishes that quantum speedups for equilibrium computation in general-sum games are fundamentally limited to a quadratic improvement in the number of actions, closing a gap that existed for zero-sum games. The ε-dependence remains an open question: the CCE algorithm inherits the ε^{2.5} scaling from the zero-sum literature, and the authors identify quantizing the RVU framework of Syrgkanis et al. as a promising path toward better ε-scaling, though the sensitivity of high-order smoothness arguments to Gibbs-sampling noise makes this non-trivial. The time complexity (m²√n vs. m√n queries) reveals a practical gap tied to the cost of sampling all m players each round, and extending these techniques to Bayesian or extensive-form games is flagged as a natural next step.
