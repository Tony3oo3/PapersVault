## Problem

Graph generation has lagged behind other modalities because existing approaches face a fundamental architectural mismatch: diffusion-based models require quadratic memory and slow iterative denoising, while autoregressive models rely on specialized RNN-based architectures that cannot leverage advances in large language models. No prior method could losslessly represent graphs as token sequences directly modelable by standard LLMs while guaranteeing structural validity and linear scaling with graph size.

## Input / Output

The method consumes attributed graphs (with categorical node and edge labels) and produces generated graphs or molecular structures. For substructure-conditioned generation, a subgraph motif is flattened into a SENT prefix and used as a prompt, guaranteeing the output contains the motif as an induced subgraph. The tokenized sequence length scales linearly with the number of edges, and the tokenization is fully reversible—any valid token sequence maps back to a unique graph, enabling lossless round-trip conversion.

## Method

AUTOGRAPH introduces Segmented Eulerian Neighborhood Trails (SENTs), a class of random trail segments that traverse every edge exactly once while recording each node's already-visited neighbors at the moment of visitation. A key theoretical result (Thm. 2.14) proves that causal semi-hamiltonian SENTs guarantee every sequence prefix corresponds to an induced subgraph, directly linking sequence prefixes to sub-sentences in language modeling. The sampling algorithm (Algorithm 1) performs random path traversal with breaks, achieving O(m) time and space complexity where m is the edge count. After reindexing vertices by first occurrence and inserting special tokens for segment breaks and neighborhood set boundaries, the resulting token sequence is modeled with a standard decoder-only transformer (LLaMA, GPT-2, or Mamba) using next-token prediction. This eliminates the need for expensive node features (e.g., spectral embeddings) required by diffusion models and enables 100× faster inference and 3× faster training while supporting substructure-conditioned generation without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Planar dataset, AUTOGRAPH achieves the best MMD ratio (1.5) and second-best VUN (87.5), while on SBM it achieves the best VUN (92.5) and a competitive MMD ratio (3.4). On large-graph benchmarks, AUTOGRAPH attains the best MMD ratios (2.3 on Proteins, 3.0 on Point Clouds), outperforming the previous best (ESGG: 4.7 and 6.8) by over 2×, while many baselines run out of memory. Inference is ~100× faster and training ~3× faster than diffusion-based DiGress (0.01s vs 2.84s per graph on Planar). On molecular generation, AUTOGRAPH is the first autoregressive graph model to surpass diffusion-based DiGress, achieving 0.91 FCD on MOSES (vs 1.19) and 79.2 FCD on GuacaMol (vs 68.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against diffusion-based DiGress [66], AUTOGRAPH achieves substantially higher VUN (87.5 vs 77.5 on Planar; 92.5 vs 60.0 on SBM) while being ~100× faster at inference (0.01s vs 2.84s per graph) and ~3× faster at training. Against the strongest diffusion baseline GruM [35], AUTOGRAPH trades a small VUN gap on Planar (87.5 vs 90.0) for a clear win on SBM (92.5 vs 85.0) and a better MMD ratio on Planar (1.5 vs 1.8). Against the previous best scalable method ESGG [2], AUTOGRAPH more than doubles the MMD ratio advantage on large graphs (2.3 vs 4.7 on Proteins; 3.0 vs 6.8 on Point Clouds), where ESGG was designed only for unattributed graphs. All prior autoregressive baselines (GraphRNN, GRAN, BiGG, GEEL, GraphGen) fail to produce structurally valid graphs (VUN ≤ 25.0) on both synthetic datasets, whereas AUTOGRAPH maintains VUN ≥ 87.5. On molecular generation, AUTOGRAPH outperforms DiGress on FCD (0.91 vs 1.19 on MOSES; 79.2 vs 68.0 on GuacaMol) and validity (87.4% vs 85.7% on MOSES), and with PubChem-10M pretraining (AUTOGRAPH*) reaches 91.4 FCD on GuacaMol, surpassing all graph-based and SMILES-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

AUTOGRAPH departs from both prior autoregressive graph generators (GraphRNN, GRAN, BiGG, GEEL), which use ad-hoc sequential representations processed by RNNs or specialized architectures, and diffusion-based models (DiGress, GruM, ESGG), which require full adjacency matrix operations with quadratic or cubic complexity. The key conceptual departure is the SENT (Segmented Eulerian Neighborhood Trail) construction, which converts graphs into token sequences whose prefixes are guaranteed to be induced subgraphs (Thm. 2.14)—a property absent from prior autoregressive methods and critical for enabling standard decoder-only transformers (LLaMA, GPT-2, Mamba) to model graph structure directly. This positions the work at the intersection of random-walk-based graph representations (GraphGPT, CRaWL) and LLM-based sequence modeling, but uniquely targets generation rather than representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AUTOGRAPH establishes the first practical pipeline for using off-the-shelf LLMs as graph generators, with 100× faster inference and 3× faster training than leading diffusion models, while scaling to graphs with thousands of nodes where diffusion models run out of memory. The substructure-conditioned generation capability (motif scaffolding without fine-tuning) and transfer learning results (pre-training on NetworkX or PubChem-10M) point toward graph foundation models, though the authors acknowledge current benchmarks remain small-scale relative to LLM pre-training corpora. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly massive graphs (millions of edges), how it handles directed or hypergraph structures, and whether larger LLM backbones yield further gains beyond the GPT-2-sized models tested here.
