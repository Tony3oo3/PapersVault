## Problem

Attention sinks—where specific tokens like [BOS] receive 30-40% of attention regardless of semantic relevance—have been observed across transformer architectures but treated as model-specific quirks or artifacts. No unified explanation exists for why they emerge, what computational function they serve, or how architectural choices (particularly position encoding) determine their form. This paper asks whether attention sinks are instead manifestations of a single geometric principle: the establishment of reference frames (coordinate systems) that anchor high-dimensional representation spaces.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes pretrained transformer models (decoder-only: LLaMA 3.1/3.2, Qwen 2.5, Mistral, Gemma, Phi-2, Pythia; encoder-only: BERT, XLM-RoBERTa) and processes STEM-focused Wikipedia sentences of 6-50 tokens (500 samples for most analyses, 100 for temporal RMT). Outputs are multi-faceted geometric characterizations per model: persistent homology metrics (Betti₀, Betti₁, persistence values), spectral graph signatures (Fiedler values, star-likeness, degree centralization at seven thresholds), information-geometric quantities (KL reduction from sink removal, Fisher information norms per layer), value-space geometry (directional influence, geometric-semantic alignment, transformation magnitude), and RMT deviation metrics (spectral gap, participation ratio, KL from Marchenko-Pastur). The final output is a classification into one of three reference frame types—centralized, distributed, or bidirectional—each with a distinct quantitative signature.

## Method

The paper formalizes attention sinks as reference frames R = (M, P, φ) on the representation manifold, where P is a set of distinguished anchor points and φ maps any token to coordinates relative to those anchors. Three types are identified and linked to position encoding: centralized frames (single [BOS] anchor, arising from standard RoPE's identity rotation at position 0), distributed frames (multiple moderate-weight anchors, arising from NTK-aware scaled RoPE's reduced angular separation), and bidirectional frames (dual start/end anchors, arising from absolute position embeddings in encoders). The empirical analysis combines persistent homology via the Ripser algorithm on attention-derived distance matrices, spectral graph theory on thresholded attention Laplacians, information-geometric analysis on the probability simplex (KL divergence, Fisher information), and Random Matrix Theory (deviation from Marchenko-Pastur eigenvalue distributions) applied to Pythia training checkpoints from step 0 to step 143,000. The key mechanistic insight is that the softmax constraint creates a zero-sum attention economy on the simplex, while position encoding introduces architecture-specific inductive biases that shape the loss landscape so gradient descent converges to a particular reference frame geometry—explaining why sinks emerge consistently in the earliest training steps before task performance converges.

## Results

The paper identifies three distinct reference frame types across transformer architectures, each with characteristic topological and spectral signatures. LLaMA-3.2-3B (centralized) shows 100% attention sink specialization on the BOS token, stable Betti₀ at 26.43 across all layers, zero Betti₁, and a strong negative Fiedler-centralization correlation (r = −0.95 at low thresholds). Qwen2.5-7B (distributed) exhibits 65.4% specialization on the comma token, a Betti₀ drop of −8.19 from early to late layers, and a distinctive sign-flipping correlation pattern (−0.46 → +0.61). XLM-RoBERTa (bidirectional) shows 100% specialization on <s>/<s> tokens, a dramatic Betti₀ collapse from 22.68 to 1.69, and high initial Betti₁ (19.69) indicating numerous cycles in early layers. Fisher information is concentrated in early layers for centralized frames (~83% in layers 0–9) but peaks in middle layers for bidirectional frames (39.8% in middle layers).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability/analysis paper rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the 'comparisons' are between three architecture families to validate the proposed reference frame taxonomy. LLaMA (standard RoPE, decoder-only) consistently produces centralized frames: 100% BOS sink coverage across 500 samples (avg sink score 0.9968), stable topology, and a single dominant reference point. Qwen2.5 (NTK-aware scaled RoPE, decoder-only) produces distributed frames: the most common sink token ('The') appears in only 17.2% of samples, top-10 sinks cover just 53.4%, and Betti₀ decreases through depth indicating component merging. XLM-RoBERTa (absolute position embeddings, encoder-only) produces bidirectional frames: both <s> and </s> tokens serve as reference points, Betti₁ is high in early layers (19.69) and drops dramatically, and KL divergence shows a U-shaped profile with positive reduction at both first and final layers. The paper further validates consistency within each family: all LLaMA variants (1B–8B, base and instruct) show identical 100% BOS specialization and stable Betti₀, while all Qwen variants (3B–7B) show the sign-flipping spectral correlation pattern. The key architectural driver is position encoding: standard RoPE's identity rotation at position 0 creates the computational asymmetry that favors centralized frames, NTK-aware scaling reduces this asymmetry enabling distributed frames, and absolute embeddings create explicit position markers supporting bidirectional frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Moschella et al.'s (2022) concept of reference frames as canonical coordinate systems in high-dimensional representation spaces, applying it to explain the attention sink phenomenon first identified by Xiao et al. (2023). Prior work (Gu et al. 2024, Barbero et al. 2025, Yu et al. 2024, Cancedda 2024) characterized attention sinks as model-specific phenomena or artifacts of particular architectures, while this paper reframes them as manifestations of a single geometric principle with three distinct solutions (centralized, distributed, bidirectional) determined by position encoding choices. Methodologically, the paper synthesizes tools from topological data analysis (persistent homology via the Ripser algorithm), random matrix theory (Marchenko-Pastur deviation), spectral graph theory (Laplacian/Fiedler analysis), and information geometry (Fisher information on the probability simplex) into a unified analytical framework for characterizing attention structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of apparently disparate attention sink behaviors across LLaMA, Qwen, BERT, and other architectures under a single geometric principle (reference frame formation) provides a principled vocabulary for discussing how position encoding choices shape representational geometry, potentially guiding future architecture design. However, the analysis is primarily correlational across architectures rather than causal—the paper does not demonstrate that deliberately engineering a specific reference frame type improves downstream performance, and the topological/spectral 'signatures' are identified post-hoc. Open questions include whether the reference frame framework generalizes to multimodal or non-transformer architectures, whether the three types are truly exhaustive, and whether the early-training emergence (step 0–8) can be leveraged for more efficient pretraining or transfer learning.
