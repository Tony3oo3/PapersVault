## Problem

Existing robotics datasets and simulation benchmarks predominantly target single-arm platforms, leaving a critical gap for humanoid robots equipped with dual arms and dexterous hands. Bimanual dexterous manipulation requires coordinated arm movements and fine-grained hand operations, making autonomous demonstration collection far more challenging than single-arm pick-and-place. Current data acquisition relies on costly teleoperation (VR, exoskeletons) or RL policies, limiting task diversity and scalability for the 26-DoF action space of humanoid dexterous hands.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes natural-language task descriptions, a 3D asset library with pre-annotated spatial information (key points and three axis types—approach, attach, parallel—per atomic operation), and scene layout parameters. It produces executable code-form task setups, chains of relational spatial constraints (point coincidence, axis parallelism/orthogonality between dynamic hand frames and global object/world frames), and recorded demonstration trajectories containing RGB+depth from six cameras, joint states, and 26-DoF action ground truth. A key design choice is that constraints are expressed as geometric relations between elements of a dynamically evolving action frame F_act and a global frame set F_all, rather than as fixed joint targets, enabling the LLM to reason about spatial logic without explicit low-level programming.

## Method

HumanoidGen decomposes bimanual dexterous manipulation into three coupled stages: (1) an LLM planner generates scene-setup code and success criteria from language prompts and asset metadata; (2) the LLM performs task decomposition into a sequence of atomic hand operations (pinch, grasp, press, open) paired with arm-movement constraints, where each constraint is a geometric relation (e.g., p_hand^grasp ≡ p_handle^grasp, v_hand^approach ∥ v_handle^approach) solved by SNOPT (goal pose) and mplib (path) under collision-avoidance and kinematic constraints; (3) a variant of MCTS with a Segment-Truncate-Combine-Resume (STCR) mechanism iteratively expands a planning tree, using simulation execution feedback and an intrinsic 'valuable moment' reward to guide exploration when annotations are insufficient or horizons are long. Dynamic collision management is handled by an LLM-controlled object-ignoring list that permits in-contact manipulation (e.g., pulling a drawer) while enforcing free-space collision checks elsewhere. Scene scaling from tabletop to room-level is achieved via a single SE(3) coordinate transformation, generalizing demonstrations across 120+ scenes without re-annotation.

## Results

HumanoidGen achieves an average demonstration generation success rate exceeding 50% across 20 bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks, with most task categories above 75%. MCTS-enhanced reasoning dramatically improves planning in challenging settings: on the 'pyramid stack' task with removed annotations, success rate jumps from 13.3% (non-MCTS) to 90.0% (MCTS N=12), while on 'blocks stack hard' it rises from 18.3% to 98.3%. For policy validation, DP3 achieves few-shot learning on easier tasks (e.g., 67.8% on cup pour easy with only 20 demos), and DP shows strong data scaling (e.g., open box hard: 11.1% → 100% from 20 to 100 demos). Real-world transfer achieves >85% collection success rate with ~14 s per trajectory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole quantitative baseline is RobotTwin (Mu et al., CVPR 2025), modified to support dexterous hands. HumanoidGen outperforms RobotTwin across all four task groups (single-arm easy, single-arm hard, bimanual short-horizon, bimanual long-horizon), achieving an average success rate above 50% versus RobotTwin's lower rates. On single-arm and bimanual short-horizon tasks, the two methods show comparable performance, confirming that the spatial annotation scheme alone enables dexterous hand participation. The gap widens substantially on long-horizon and complex-collision tasks (e.g., close & open box, blocks stack easy/hard), where HumanoidGen's dynamic collision management—allowing the LLM to selectively ignore in-contact collisions during articulated manipulation—provides a clear advantage. RobotTwin, which does not perform proactive dynamic collision management during inference, struggles in these scenarios. The comparison demonstrates that the combination of relational action constraints, active collision avoidance, and dynamic collision management is critical for scalable bimanual dexterous data generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes LLM-driven scene generation (RoboGen, Gensim, Gensim2) and spatial-constraint-based planning (ReKep, OmniManip, RoboTwin) into a unified pipeline specifically for bimanual dexterous manipulation, a setting where prior benchmarks either exclude dexterous hands (RoboTwin, Aloha, PerAct2) or rely on costly teleoperation/RL (HumanoidBench, BiGym). The key conceptual departure is framing manipulation as a chain of relational spatial constraints over annotated key points and axes, solved by a trajectory optimizer, rather than as a sequence of predefined low-level skills. The STCR mechanism for MCTS-enhanced LLM reasoning is inspired by proof-assistant tree search (DeepSeek-Prover) but repurposed for iterative code execution with simulation feedback in a robotics context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical data bottleneck for humanoid robotics: the absence of scalable, automated demonstration collection for bimanual dexterous tasks. HGen-Bench provides the first benchmark specifically targeting dexterous hand manipulation on a humanoid platform, and the data-scaling experiments validate that diffusion policies improve monotonically with more generated trajectories. However, several long-horizon tasks (handover and storage, blocks stack hard, pyramid stack) remain at 0% success for both DP and DP3 even with 100 demonstrations, indicating that current policy architectures struggle with the full complexity of multi-stage dexterous coordination. Open questions include whether the constraint-based planning generalizes to unannotated object categories, whether the approach transfers reliably to real-world execution beyond the limited sim2real experiments, and whether more powerful policy architectures (e.g., hierarchical or world-model-based) can close the gap on the hardest tasks.
