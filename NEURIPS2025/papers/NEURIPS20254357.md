## Problem

Deep GNNs suffer from oversmoothing, where node representations become indistinguishable as depth increases. Existing mitigations (normalization, residual connections, edge dropout) are developed independently with limited theoretical understanding of their mechanisms, and they fail to preserve discriminative features at extreme propagation depths. The paper asks: what is the unified principle underlying these techniques, and what structural condition on the graph guarantees stable, class-distinct representations under deep message-passing?

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard unsigned graph (adjacency matrix A and node feature matrix X) and optionally ground-truth node labels, then constructs a signed graph by keeping A as the positive adjacency and synthesizing a negative adjacency matrix from either label differences (Label-SBP) or the negative feature similarity matrix -XX^T (Feature-SBP). The output is node representations that remain class-separated even at 300 propagation layers, followed by a standard linear classifier for node classification. No learnable parameters are added; the only hyperparameters are the positive/negative edge strengths (α, β) and a mixing coefficient λ.

## Method

The core insight is that prior oversmoothing remedies (BatchNorm, PairNorm, residual connections, DropEdge, APPNP, JKNET, DAGNN) can all be re-expressed as signed graph propagation where the vanilla unsigned message-passing is modified by implicitly injecting negative edges. However, Theorem 3.2 shows that unstructured negative edges either fail to prevent oversmoothing (β too small) or cause divergence (β too large). The paper identifies structural balance—positive edges within clusters, negative edges across clusters—as the condition that provably yields stable, cluster-preserving asymptotics (Theorem 4.2: nodes in V₁ converge to +c, nodes in V₂ to −c). SBP is a plug-and-play module that explicitly enforces this structure: Label-SBP assigns negative edges between differently-labeled nodes and positive edges within the same label, while Feature-SBP uses -XX^T to approximate the same pattern without labels. In practice, the propagation is X^(k+1) = (1−λ)X^(k) + λ(α Â⁺X^(k) − β Â⁻X^(k)) with LayerNorm serving as the bounded constraint function, and softmax-normalized negative adjacency matrices ensuring numerical stability.

## Results

On 2-layer SGC across 8 standard benchmarks, Label-SBP achieves the highest accuracy on 7 of 8 datasets, with headline numbers of 82.90% on Cora, 73.04% on Citeseer, 80.32% on PubMed, 78.38% on Texas, and 80.39% on Wisconsin. Feature-SBP similarly leads on 7 datasets, reaching 82.46% on Cora and 72.97% on Cornell. The paper reports an average gain of ~3 percentage points over the best baseline on homophilic graphs and ~5 points on heterophilic graphs. In the deep-GNN setting (up to 300 layers on CSBM), SBP maintains 97.50% accuracy (Label-SBP) and 80.00% (Feature-SBP), while normalization baselines degrade substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Label-SBP outperforms all 10 baselines on 7 of 8 datasets, with the largest margins on heterophilic graphs: +32.4 points over SGC on Texas (78.38 vs 45.95), +33.3 over SGC on Wisconsin (80.39 vs 47.06), and +24.9 over SGC on Cornell (70.27 vs 48.11). Feature-SBP similarly dominates, achieving the best score on Squirrel (49.16 vs 48.63 for ContraNorm) and Cornell (72.97 vs 64.59 for APPNP). On homophilic graphs, Label-SBP leads by 1.3 points over ContraNorm on Cora (82.90 vs 81.60) and 0.7 points on PubMed (80.32 vs 79.30), while Feature-SBP trails ContraNorm on Citeseer (70.63 vs 72.25) and PubMed (77.41 vs 79.30). The only dataset where neither SBP variant is best is Amazon-ratings, where ContraNorm edges ahead by 0.5–0.6 points. In the deep-GNN regime (Table 3, ogbn-arxiv at 16 layers), Label-SBP-v2 achieves 69.33% versus 63.24% for BatchNorm and 59.13% for vanilla GCN, confirming that SBP's advantage grows with depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the signed graph dynamics framework (Shi et al., 2019) with the classical structural balance theory (Cartwright & Harary, 1956) to provide a principled lens on oversmoothing in GNNs. It departs from prior signed GNN work (Derr et al., 2018; Chien et al., 2020) by showing that merely introducing negative edges is insufficient—the asymptotic behavior depends critically on the *organization* of positive and negative edges, not just their presence. The key conceptual move is reinterpreting existing oversmoothing remedies (BatchNorm, PairNorm, residual connections, DropEdge) as implicit signed graph propagations, then identifying structural balance as the condition that guarantees stable, class-preserving dynamics rather than convergence or divergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified signed-graph perspective gives practitioners a diagnostic tool (structural imbalance degree) to understand *why* a given oversmoothing remedy works or fails, rather than treating each technique as an isolated architectural trick. The practical impact is a parameter-free, plug-and-play module that maintains discriminative representations at 300 layers—far beyond where normalization-based methods degrade. Limitations include the reliance on label information for the strongest variant (Label-SBP), the linear-setting assumption in the core theorems, and untested scalability to graphs with ~1B nodes. Open questions include whether the structural balance framework extends naturally to non-linear propagation, multi-class settings beyond weak balance, and whether the β hyperparameter can be learned end-to-end rather than tuned per dataset.
