## Problem

Despite the success of video Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) in generating temporally coherent videos, it remains unclear how these models internally establish and represent temporal correspondences across frames during generation. Existing interpretability work on diffusion models focuses on U-Net-based image architectures and two-frame correspondences, leaving the role of full 3D attention in video DiTs unexplored. This gap prevents both principled extraction of motion information from generated videos and targeted interventions to improve temporal consistency.

## Input / Output

For the analysis framework, DiffTrack takes text prompts and generates videos (480×720, 49 frames) using the target video DiT, with predefined starting points in the first frame (grid-sampled at 1/20 spatial resolution for objects, or a 10×10 grid for scenes) and pseudo ground-truth trajectories from CoTracker. For zero-shot point tracking on real videos, the method takes a video and starting points, inverts the video through DDIM at timestep t=1, and extracts query-key descriptors from a single identified layer, producing per-frame correspondence points that are linearly interpolated to RGB coordinates. For motion-enhanced generation, CAG takes the same text-prompt input as the base model but additionally zeros out cross-frame attention maps in specific layers during a perturbed forward pass, outputting a motion-improved video without any auxiliary network.

## Method

DiffTrack is a quantitative analysis framework that systematically examines how temporal correspondence emerges within the full 3D attention of video DiTs by varying three axes: representation type (query-key similarities vs. intermediate features), layer depth, and denoising timestep. It introduces three complementary metrics—matching accuracy (PCK against pseudo ground-truth), confidence score (maximum attention value at the matched location), and attention score (total cross-frame attention mass relative to self-frame and text-frame attention)—combined via harmonic mean to identify where and when temporal matching is both accurate and influential. The key finding is that query-key matching in a small set of specific layers (e.g., layers 13, 17, 21 in CogVideoX-2B) dominates temporal correspondence, and this matching strengthens progressively during denoising. For practical applications, zero-shot tracking leverages the optimal layer/timestep with DDIM inversion at t=1, sets the 3D VAE temporal compression ratio to q=1 for one-to-one frame-latent mapping, and uses interleaved chunking with a global first frame to handle long sequences. Cross-Attention Guidance (CAG) extends the analysis to generation by zeroing out cross-frame attention in the identified dominant layers during a perturbed forward pass and steering the noise prediction away from the degraded sample, improving motion consistency without additional training or supervision.

## Results

On zero-shot point tracking (TAP-Vid), DiffTrack with CogVideoX-5B achieves the best average PCK (δ_avg^x) of 49.2 on Kinetics and 46.9 on DAVIS, outperforming the strongest vision foundation model DINOv2 (38.4 / 42.1) and the best self-supervised model DIFT-SD2.1 (42.9 / 39.7). For motion-enhanced video generation, Cross-Attention Guidance (CAG) on CogVideoX-2B wins 87.2% of human preference votes over the baseline (12.9%) and improves Dynamic Degree from 0.792 to 0.824 while increasing Subject Consistency from 0.928 to 0.931 on VBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffTrack (CogVideoX-5B) outperforms all 11 baselines on δ_avg^x for both Kinetics (49.2 vs. best baseline 42.9 for DIFT-SD2.1) and DAVIS (46.9 vs. best baseline 42.1 for DINOv2), with the largest margins over self-supervised video models like SVD (36.6/35.9) and CRW (40.9/35.9). Among vision foundation models, DINOv2 is the strongest single-image baseline (38.4/42.1), but DiffTrack exceeds it by ~11 points on Kinetics and ~5 points on DAVIS. The U-Net-based DIFT (SD2.1) is the closest competitor on Kinetics (42.9) but still trails by 6.3 points. For motion-enhanced generation (Table 3), CAG beats STG on all four VBench metrics (e.g., Dynamic Degree 0.824 vs. 0.778), while STG degrades Subject Consistency relative to the baseline (0.926 vs. 0.928), whereas CAG improves it (0.931). In human evaluation, CAG achieves 87.2% preference over the CogVideoX-2B baseline, a decisive margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on emergent correspondences in diffusion models (DIFT, DreamMatcher, ZeroCo) from image-level, two-frame matching to multi-frame temporal correspondence within video Diffusion Transformers. It builds directly on PAG's perturbed-attention guidance mechanism, adapting it from self-attention in U-Net image models to cross-frame attention in video DiTs. The key departure is the systematic, quantitative analysis of which layers, representations, and denoising timesteps within full 3D attention carry temporal matching information—something prior works on U-Net-based video models (e.g., SVD) or image diffusion models had not addressed for the DiT architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that temporal correspondence in video DiTs is concentrated in a small number of specific layers (e.g., layers 13, 17, 21 in CogVideoX-2B) provides a practical, interpretable handle for downstream tasks without retraining. CAG's simplicity—zeroing out cross-frame attention in identified layers—makes it an easily adoptable, training-free technique for improving motion quality in any DiT-based video generator. Open questions include whether the identified critical layers are stable across different prompts, resolutions, and model scales, and whether the pseudo-ground-truth dependency on CoTracker limits the analysis's validity for highly dynamic or occluded scenes. The I2V variant's underperformance relative to T2V also raises questions about how fine-tuning objectives affect temporal correspondence encoding.
